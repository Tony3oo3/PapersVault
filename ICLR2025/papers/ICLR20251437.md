## Problem

While the k-WL hierarchy provides a systematic but coarse and uninterpretable measure of GNN expressiveness, and prior logical expressivity analyses (e.g., Barceló et al. 2020 for MPNNs) cover only individual model families, no unified framework exists to determine the exact logical expressivity of arbitrary GNN architectures. This paper addresses the gap by asking: given any GNN built from aggregation and combination operations, what is the precise set of first-order logic formulas it captures, and how can this set be used to derive homomorphism expressivity, WL bounds, and expressivity comparisons?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251437/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a GNN architecture whose layers can be decomposed into a finite sequence of aggregation (AGG) and combination (COM) operations over node-tuple representations (the GACNN form), along with a specification of the generalized neighbor relation. It produces a recursive grammar defining the equivalent logic set Φ over First-Order Logic with Counting (FOC), from which one can mechanically extract the homomorphism expressivity (a set of subgraphs F), the tightest k-WL upper bound (by counting maximum variables in Φ), and pairwise expressivity inclusions between GNN classes. The framework requires binary-output GNNs and assumes isomorphism-invariant computation.

## Method

The paper introduces the General Aggregate-Combine Neural Network (GACNN) abstraction, which decomposes any GNN layer into a computation DAG of AGG and COM nodes, each with at most two children. Theorem 3 then provides a recursive correspondence: each AGG node maps to a counting-existential formula ∃^{≥N}v(φ_j(v) ∧ 1_{v∈N_i(u)}), each COM node maps to a conjunction or disjunction of child formulas, and each INIT node maps to the atp (all-tuples-property) predicate. Proposition 5 closes the recursion over arbitrary layer depth by taking the union over all L, yielding a layer-independent grammar. From this logic set, homomorphism expressivity is obtained by stripping negation and ∃^{≥N} (N≥2) and mapping each remaining formula's variables bijectively to nodes of a subgraph F (Theorem 8), while WL upper bounds follow directly from the maximum variable count in the grammar via the Cai-Furer-Immerman equivalence (Proposition 9). The framework is validated empirically by training MPNNs, Subgraph GNNs, 2-FGNNs, and NBFNet on synthetic graphs labeled by specific FOC formulas, confirming that each model succeeds exactly on the formulas within its predicted logic set.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with empirical validation on synthetic Erdős-Rényi graphs (500 nodes) confirming the predicted expressivity hierarchy. On node classification, MPNNs achieve 1.000 on φ₁ (2-hop color pattern) but fail on φ₂ (3-clique detection, ~0.86) and φ₃ (adjacent-to-3-clique, ~0.87); Subgraph GNNs (weak) reach 1.000 on φ₂ but still fail on φ₃ (~0.90); 2-FGNNs achieve 1.000 on all three with the predicted number of layers. On link prediction, MPNNs drop to 0.586/0.328 on φ₂ (connected-via-blue-node) while NBFNet and 2-FGNNs achieve 1.000. On separation power, only SEAL distinguishes the Shrikhande and Rook's 4×4 strongly regular graphs, consistent with its 4-WL bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper validates its theoretical expressivity hierarchy (MPNN = 1-WL < Subgraph GNN (weak) = NBFNet < Subgraph GNN (strong) < Local 2-FGNN < 2-FGNN = 3-WL < SEAL < 4-WL) through targeted synthetic experiments. MPNNs (Xu et al., 2018) serve as the weakest baseline: they perfectly learn φ₁ with 2 layers but plateau at ~0.86 on φ₂ and ~0.87 on φ₃, confirming their 1-WL limitation. Subgraph GNNs (Bevilacqua et al., 2021) extend reach to φ₂ (1.000 with 2 layers) but cannot express φ₃, matching the theoretical prediction that they are bounded by 3-WL. 2-FGNNs (Morris et al., 2018) achieve 1.000 on all three node-classification formulas with the exact predicted number of layers (1, 2, 3 respectively), confirming their 3-WL equivalence. For link prediction, NBFNet (Zhu et al., 2021) achieves 1.000 on φ₂ where MPNNs drop to 0.586, validating the single-source aggregation pattern. SEAL (Zhang et al., 2020) is the only model that separates the Shrikhande and Rook's 4×4 graphs in Table 3, consistent with its 4-WL upper bound, while 2-FGNNs (3-WL) fail on this pair.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the logical expressivity analysis of Barceló et al. (2020), which was restricted to MPNNs, into a unified framework (GACNNs) applicable to arbitrary GNN architectures built from aggregation and combination operations. It also extends the homomorphism expressivity results of Zhang et al. (2024) from specific models to a general construction procedure, while solving an open conjecture from that work. The recursive grammar for equivalent logic sets follows the graded modal logic tradition of de Rijke (1996), and the WL upper-bound derivation relies on the FOC–WL equivalence of Cai et al. (1992). The key conceptual departure is treating all GNN layers as instances of a single two-operation (AGG/COM) computation graph, enabling a single theorem (Theorem 3) to subsume previously separate analyses of MPNNs, Subgraph GNNs, NBFNet, SEAL, 2-FGNNs, and others.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a systematic, recipe-like method for determining the logical expressivity, homomorphism expressivity, and WL upper bound of any new GNN architecture that fits the GACNN paradigm, reducing what was previously a case-by-case analysis to a mechanical procedure. This is practically useful for the GNN theory community as new architectures continue to proliferate. However, the framework is limited to aggregation-combination networks and does not cover attention-based or distance-injecting models (e.g., Graphormer-GD), and the empirical validation is confined to small synthetic graphs, leaving open whether the theoretical expressivity distinctions translate to meaningful performance gaps on real-world benchmarks.
