## Problem

Existing hypergraph neural networks (HNNs) require computationally intensive training because their message passing modules make loss computation interdependent across connected nodes, limiting practical deployment on large-scale hypergraphs. No prior method decouples the processing of hypergraph structural information from the model training stage, leaving a gap between the expressiveness of HNNs and their training efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes d-dimensional node features X_V ∈ R^{n×d} and a binary hypergraph incidence matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{n×m} as input. During preprocessing, the TF-MP-Module computes a fixed propagation operator S ∈ R^{n×n} that encodes L-hop neighbourhood information via a weighted clique expansion; at training time, only the task-specific module (e.g., an MLP classifier) is optimised. Outputs are node class logits for classification or per-hyperedge probability scores for hyperlink prediction, with the structural propagation entirely precomputed and frozen.

## Method

TF-HNN decouples hypergraph structure processing from training by designing a training-free message passing module (TF-MP-Module) in two stages: first, learnable parameters in the feature aggregation functions of four SOTA HNNs (UniGCNII, Deep-HGNN, AllDeepSets, ED-HNN) are replaced with identity matrices; second, non-linear activations are removed and L propagation layers are consolidated into a single matrix multiplication X̂ = S·X. Remarkably, this two-stage simplification unifies all four architectures into one formula S = (1−α)^L W^L + α Σ_{l=0}^{L−1}(1−α)^l W^l, where W is a hyperedge-size-weighted clique expansion adjacency matrix and α controls the trade-off between retaining node-specific information and aggregating neighbourhood features. The edge weights are defined as W_{ij} = Σ_k δ(v_i,v_j,e_k)/D_{H_kk^E}, making the weighted clique expansion positively correlated with the probability of connected nodes sharing the same label under homophily. Theoretically, TF-HNN achieves O(M) training complexity (only the task-specific module), utilises the same L-hop neighbourhood information as existing HNNs, and remains robust against oversmoothing as L→∞ due to the α-regularised retention of input features.

## Results

On node classification across six hypergraph benchmarks, TF-HNN achieves the best average accuracy of 82.50%, outperforming the strongest baseline Deep-HGNN (81.38%) and ED-HNN (80.30%). TF-HNN wins on 4 of 6 datasets (Cora-CA: 86.54%, Congress: 95.09%, House: 76.29%, Senate: 70.42%). On the large-scale Trivago benchmark, TF-HNN reaches 90.3% node classification accuracy—approximately 10% above the best baseline (Deep-HGNN at 84.06%)—while requiring only ~13.9 s of training time versus ~3352 s for Deep-HGNN (roughly 1% of the training time). For hyperlink prediction, TF-HNN achieves the best average AUC of 85.39% versus ED-HNN's 84.64%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TF-HNN outperforms all nine HNN baselines on average node classification accuracy (82.50% vs. 81.38% for Deep-HGNN, the strongest competitor), winning outright on Cora-CA, Congress, House, and Senate. On DBLP-CA and Citeseer, PhenomNN narrowly edges TF-HNN by 0.11 and 0.28 percentage points respectively, though TF-HNN remains within the top two on both. The training-efficiency gap is dramatic: TF-HNN requires only the task-specific module (an MLP) to train, while baselines like Deep-HGNN and ED-HNN must jointly optimize their message-passing parameters, resulting in TF-HNN using roughly 1% of Deep-HGNN's training time on Trivago while simultaneously improving accuracy by ~10%. For hyperlink prediction, TF-HNN achieves the best average AUC (85.39%) over ED-HNN (84.64%) and Deep-HGNN (83.88%), again with substantially lower training time. The comparison with graph-based training-free methods (SGC and SIGN applied to clique expansions) confirms that the hypergraph-specific TF-MP-Module is necessary: SGC with CE lags by up to 23 points on Senate, and SIGN with CE, while closer, still underperforms TF-HNN on all four tested datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free message passing paradigm from the graph domain (SGC, SIGN, APPNP) to hypergraphs, where no prior work had achieved a fully training-free structural propagation module. It builds on four state-of-the-art HNNs—UniGCNII, Deep-HGNN, AllDeepSets, and ED-HNN—by identifying a shared feature aggregation function as the core operator across all of them, then stripping away learnable parameters and non-linearities to yield a single unified propagation operator. The key departure from prior hypergraph work is the use of a hyperedge-size-based edge weight on the clique expansion rather than relying on incidence tensors or k-uniform assumptions, which makes the approach applicable to general (non-uniform) hypergraphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training efficiency is a critical bottleneck for deploying HNNs on large-scale hypergraphs, and this paper demonstrates that the structural information can be fully precomputed, reducing training complexity to that of a simple classifier. The 10% accuracy gain over the best baseline with 1% of its training time on Trivago (172K nodes) suggests that learnable message passing may be unnecessary overhead for hypergraph tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to hypergraph-level tasks (explicitly noted as a limitation), whether the hyperedge-size-based weighting generalizes to weighted or directed hypergraphs, and whether automated hyperparameter search for α and L can further simplify deployment.
