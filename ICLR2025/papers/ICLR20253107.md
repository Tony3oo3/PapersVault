## Problem

Existing dataset ownership verification (DOV) methods rely on decision boundaries or watermarking, making them inapplicable to the increasingly popular self-supervised contrastive pre-trained models that lack well-defined decision boundaries. This paper presents the first DOV method specifically designed for contrastive pre-trained encoders, enabling dataset owners to verify whether a black-box model was illicitly pre-trained on their public dataset without modifying the data or requiring access to training details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253107/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The defender queries a black-box suspicious encoder via an Encoder-as-a-Service API (retrieving only feature vectors) and a self-trained shadow encoder, feeding them multi-scale augmented crops (global range 0.4–1.0, local range 0.05–0.4) of images sampled from the public dataset and a private dataset. Only a small fraction of the dataset is needed (e.g., 0.1% of ImageNet, with k_pub = k_pvt = 32 per sampling round over K = 50 rounds). The output is a p-value from a one-tailed paired T-test: p < 0.05 rejects the null hypothesis and concludes the suspect stole the dataset.

## Method

The core mechanism is the 'contrastive relationship gap,' which exploits two empirical observations: (1) contrastive pre-trained encoders produce more similar representations for augmentations of the same seen sample than for unseen samples (unary relationship), and (2) pairwise similarity between seen samples remains stable across augmentations, unlike unseen samples (binary relationship). The method computes these relationships at multiple scales using global and local random cropping, then measures the gap between the suspicious encoder and a shadow encoder (trained without the defender's data) across six similarity metrics (S_U^{gg}, S_U^{ll}, S_U^{gl}, S_B^{gg}, S_B^{gl}, S_B^{ll}). A one-tailed paired T-test on the aggregated gaps across K sampling rounds determines ownership. Unlike watermark-based approaches, no dataset modification is needed; unlike dataset inference methods, only a small subset is required rather than the full training set.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 and ImageNette, the proposed method achieves perfect sensitivity (1.0), specificity (1.0), and AUROC (1.0), while DI4SSL achieves only 0.2/0.3 sensitivity and EncoderMI achieves 0.8/0.15 sensitivity with 0.2/0.9 specificity. On ImageNet, the method correctly rejects the null hypothesis (p < 0.05) for all 10 pre-trained encoders with p-values ranging from 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁴, using only 0.1% of the training data. The method also completes a single verification in 293 seconds versus 10,014 seconds for DI4SSL on ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms both baselines across all evaluated datasets. DI4SSL (Dziedzic et al., NeurIPS 2022) suffers from very low sensitivity (0.2 on CIFAR-10, 0.3 on ImageNette) because it requires inferring the entire training set, making it prohibitively slow (10,014s vs. 293s on ImageNet) and unable to detect theft in most scenarios (p-values of 0.15–0.99 on ImageNet, all above the 0.05 threshold). EncoderMI (Liu et al., CCS 2021) has the opposite problem: it produces extreme false positives (specificity of 0.2 on CIFAR-10, p-values of 10⁻⁴³ to 10⁻⁷⁴ on legal scenarios) because it directly models high-dimensional representations without extracting the discriminative contrastive relationship signal. The watermark-based DOV-CTRL (Li et al., ICCV 2023) also generates false positives on innocent suspects (e.g., p = 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻⁴⁹ on SVHN/ImageWoof when the suspect is legal), whereas the proposed method correctly identifies all legal scenarios with p > 0.05. The proposed method's advantage stems from extracting the contrastive relationship gap—a compact, task-specific signal—rather than operating on raw high-dimensional embeddings or requiring dataset modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends dataset ownership verification (DOV) from the supervised-model setting—where prior methods rely on decision-boundary distances or injected watermarks—to contrastive pre-trained encoders, where no such boundaries exist. It repurposes the inherent training objective of contrastive learning (maximizing intra-sample augmentation similarity) as a verification signal, formalized as the 'contrastive relationship gap,' rather than requiring watermark injection or white-box access. The black-box EaaS formulation departs from Proof-of-Learning/Training-Data approaches that demand the suspect disclose training records, and from DI4SSL/EncoderMI which either require full-dataset inference or train a separate inferencer on high-dimensional representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As contrastive and self-supervised pre-training becomes the dominant paradigm for foundation models, protecting the unlabeled datasets that underpin them is increasingly urgent, yet no prior DOV method addressed this setting. The method's practical advantages—operating on only 0.1% of ImageNet, surviving DP-SGD and downstream fine-tuning, and requiring no dataset modification—make it deployable in real-world scenarios. Key open questions include extending the approach to Masked Image Modeling (where the paper shows it fails), adapting it to non-visual modalities, and whether the contrastive relationship gap can be further obfuscated by more aggressive adversarial training.
