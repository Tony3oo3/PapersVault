## Problem

Modern attention-free sequence models (Mamba, RWKV, Griffin, RetNet, HGRN) lack the interpretability and explainability tools that Transformers enjoy through their explicit attention matrices. The only prior implicit-attention formulation for Mamba (Ali et al., 2024) captures solely the S6 recurrent layer, ignoring critical sub-components such as Conv1D, gate branches, and nonlinear activations, resulting in less faithful explanations. No unified framework exists to express these diverse architectures on a common attention-matrix footing for systematic comparison and downstream XAI use.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained sequence models (Mamba 1.3B/2.8B, RWKV 430M, Griffin, RetNet, HGRN) given either text token sequences (e.g., IMDB reviews, ARC-E prompts) or image patch sequences (e.g., 16×16 ViM patches from ImageNet). It produces an L×L data-dependent implicit attention matrix per layer and per channel, which is then consumed by standard XAI pipelines (raw attention, attention rollout, attribution) to yield pixel- or word-level explanation maps. For Mamba-2, the matrix additionally encodes GroupRMSNorm statistics as a per-head diagonal scaling, and for multi-head variants the formulation broadcasts across heads.

## Method

The core contribution is a set of algebraic manipulations that express the entire token-mixing block of each architecture as a single data-controlled linear operator Y = Hx, where H is an L×L implicit attention matrix. The decomposition proceeds by: (i) converting element-wise gating (SiLU, GELU, sigmoid) into diagonal matrix multiplication, (ii) unrolling the linear recurrent rule (S6 for Mamba, RG-LRU for Griffin, WKV for RWKV) into a lower-triangular matrix whose entries are products of per-step decay and input-dependent coefficients, (iii) representing causal Conv1D as a banded convolution matrix, and (iv) fusing cascaded linear projections and omitting token-independent operators. For Mamba, the final matrix is H = W'_x · α̂ · Z_x' · M (gate × S6 × activation × conv); Mamba-2 prepends a GroupRMSNorm diagonal; Griffin yields H = diag(GELU(Linear'(x))) · α̃ · M; and RWKV gives H = diag(σ(r)) · α̂. This holistic composition—incorporating six or more sub-layers for Mamba-2—produces sharper, more faithful attention maps than the S6-only baseline, as validated by perturbation AUC, segmentation mIoU, and AMPLIFY-based ICL accuracy improvements.

## Results

On ImageNet-Segmentation, the proposed Mamba-Attr (Ours) achieves 79.60% pixel accuracy, 86.40% mAP, and 62.51% mIoU, surpassing the previous Mamba-specific method (Ali et al., 2024) by +4.88 mIoU and the Transformer-Attr baseline (Chefer et al., 2021b) by +1.88 mIoU. In vision perturbation tests on ImageNet, the method reduces positive-perturbation AUC from 16.619 to 11.350 (Attribution) and increases negative-perturbation AUC from 39.632 to 51.310, outperforming both the prior Mamba formulation and ViT counterparts. For NLP (ARC-E, zero-shot), the method improves activation AUAC by ≥2.2% and pruning AU-MSE by >10% over Ali et al. (2024) on both 1.3B and 2.8B Mamba models. In the AMPLIFY in-context learning task, the method yields an average +1.2 accuracy point gain over the Ali et al. attribution baseline across Snarks, CommonsenseQA, and Formal Fallacies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ali et al. (2024), the prior Mamba-specific implicit attention formulation, the proposed method wins on every reported metric: segmentation mIoU improves from 54.24% to 62.51% (+8.27), positive-perturbation AUC drops from 16.619 to 11.350 (Attribution), and AMPLIFY Snarks accuracy rises from 53.11% to 56.15% (1.3B). Versus the Transformer-Attr baseline (Chefer et al., 2021b) applied to DeiT-S, the method achieves higher mIoU (62.51% vs. 60.63%) and mAP (86.40% vs. 84.85%) on ViM-S, demonstrating that the unified formulation transfers Transformer explainability techniques to Mamba with a performance edge. The Attn-Rollout method (Abnar & Zuidema, 2020) is outperformed on ViM-S (58.48% vs. 47.85% mIoU for DeiT-S), and the LRP-based MambaLRP (Jafari et al., 2024) is surpassed by a wide margin (62.51% vs. 49.98% mIoU). The one comparison where the method does not lead is weakly supervised semantic segmentation on Pascal VOC 2012, where Mamba-Attr (52.11% mIoU) falls short of the multi-phase Toco method (Ru et al., 2023) at 61.10%, though it substantially exceeds the plain CAM baseline (35.99%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit attention formulation of Ali et al. (2024), which showed that the S6 selective state-space layer alone can be written as a data-dependent linear operator, by incorporating the full Mamba block (Conv1D, SiLU activations, gate branch, and linear projections) into a single attention matrix. It also generalizes this idea beyond Mamba to RWKV, Griffin, HGRN, and RetNet, unifying a family of gated-linear RNNs under one algebraic framework. Conceptually, it is related to Hyena (Poli et al., 2023), which composed non-attention operators to replicate attention, but here the direction is reversed: existing architectures are shown to already be implicit attention layers. The paper also bridges to the Transformer XAI literature (Chefer et al., Abnar & Zuidema) by demonstrating that standard attention-based explanation methods transfer directly to these non-Transformer models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified implicit attention representation gives practitioners a practical, off-the-shelf tool for applying the rich ecosystem of Transformer explainability techniques (attention rollout, attribution, perturbation-based faithfulness tests) to the rapidly growing family of attention-free sequence models, which previously lacked such interpretability infrastructure. The ablation revealing that Conv1D is the most critical sub-component for faithful attention maps (mIoU drops from 62.5 to 50.6 when removed) while SiLU has negligible impact offers a concrete design insight for future architectures. Open questions include whether the framework extends cleanly to higher-order tensor models like S5, whether the implicit attention view can inform architecture design (e.g., choosing inductive biases via sub-layer selection), and whether the per-channel attention matrices capture the same semantic structure as explicit multi-head attention.
