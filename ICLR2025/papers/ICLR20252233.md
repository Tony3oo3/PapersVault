## Problem

Pre-training data selection for language models has largely relied on manually crafted heuristics—deduplication, n-gram matching, or single-checkpoint influence scores—that are agnostic to the highly dynamic nature of LM training. No prior work provides a rigorous, theory-driven characterization of how individual data points should be weighted to optimize the full training trajectory toward a downstream objective, leaving the field to ad-hoc trial-and-error.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large pre-training corpus (125B tokens from CommonCrawl/Redpajama) and a small downstream objective dataset (LIMA, 1,030 instruction-response pairs) whose loss defines the optimization target. It produces a per-instance quality score vector over the full corpus, computed first on a 160K-instance proxy subset and then transferred via a 125M-parameter data scorer, followed by a Gumbel-Top-K selection that yields a 50B-token high-quality corpus (40% selection ratio) for pre-training LMs of arbitrary size.

## Method

The paper formulates data selection as a discrete optimal control problem: model parameters are state variables evolving via gradient descent, data quality scores (a simplex vector) are time-invariant control variables, and the cumulative downstream loss (AUC) is the cost to minimize. Applying Pontryagin's Maximum Principle with a Lagrange multiplier for the time-invariance constraint yields Theorem 2.1, whose key object is a co-state vector λ_t that back-propagates downstream loss gradients through the Hessian of the training loss, encoding long-range training dynamics from future to current steps. The PDS framework solves these conditions in practice via a bilevel algorithm (Algorithm 1) on a small proxy LM (160M params, 100 inner-loop steps from multiple checkpoints), then trains a 125M Fairseq-Dense data scorer to generalize the quality scores to the full corpus, and finally applies Gumbel-Top-K sampling to select the pre-training subset. This offline, theory-grounded approach differs from prior methods by incorporating multi-step training dynamics rather than single-step gradients, and by operating before training begins so the selected corpus can be reused across model sizes without additional overhead.

## Results

On the OLMo evaluation suite (9 downstream tasks), PDS achieves 48.2% average accuracy at 470M and 51.0% at 1B, outperforming all baselines by 1.2–1.7 points. On MMLU, PDS-trained models achieve substantially lower perplexity on ground-truth answers (27.1 vs. 31.1–34.8 at 470M; 20.5 vs. 23.6–26.1 at 1B) while also attaining the highest 0-shot accuracy (28.9% and 31.4%). PDS delivers a ~2× speedup in pre-training computation for the 1.7B model and reduces data demand by 1.8× in data-constrained settings, with benefits extrapolated to ~400B models via Scaling Laws.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDS outperforms all three offline data-selection baselines on average OLMo accuracy at both 470M and 1B: it beats RHO-Loss by 1.2 and 2.1 points, DSIR by 1.5 and 1.4 points, and IF-Score by 1.6 and 2.0 points, respectively. On MMLU perplexity (lower is better), PDS achieves 27.1 at 470M and 20.5 at 1B, substantially outperforming IF-Score (31.1 / 23.6), RHO-Loss (33.0 / 24.9), and DSIR (34.0 / 25.3), indicating that PDS-selected data yields better-calibrated language modeling. On MMLU 0-shot accuracy, PDS leads at both sizes (28.9% / 31.4%) though the margin over Conventional is modest (1.3 / 1.7 points). The paper also reports a ~2× training-computation speedup for the 1.7B model relative to conventional pre-training, and in data-constrained settings PDS (r=0.25, 4 epochs) matches the performance that would require 1.8× more tokens under conventional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior data selection methods for LMs (deduplication, n-gram matching, influence functions) by formulating the problem through Pontryagin's Maximum Principle from optimal control theory, treating the LM's training dynamics as the controlled system rather than the forward pass as in prior optimal-control-for-deep-learning work (Benning et al., 2019; Li et al., 2017). The key conceptual departure is that the control variables are time-invariant data quality scores (offline selection) rather than time-varying per-step controls, which the authors handle via Lagrange multipliers to derive a tractable equation system. The practical architecture—solving PMP conditions on a small proxy model, then transferring scores via a learned data scorer—builds on the observation that data quality signals are transferable across model sizes (Xie et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024), but applies it in a principled way grounded in the co-state vector's aggregation of long-range training dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical characterization of how individual data points should be weighted during LM pre-training, replacing ad-hoc heuristics with necessary optimality conditions. The 2× pre-training speedup and 1.8× data reduction are practically significant as the community faces data exhaustion, and the offline nature means PDS can be run once and reused across arbitrary model configurations. However, open questions remain: the data-dependence extension (Appendix E) with l0-norm constraints is acknowledged as computationally intractable, the proxy-to-large transfer relies on a 125M scorer that may not capture all quality signals for very different architectures, and the scaling-law extrapolation to 400B models is indirect rather than empirically verified.
