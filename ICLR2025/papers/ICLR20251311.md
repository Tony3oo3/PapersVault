## Problem

Multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) have achieved strong 2D visual grounding and reasoning, but they cannot perceive or reason in 3-dimensional space (view coordinates), which is how humans actually perceive the world. The key open question is whether 3D grounding capability can be induced in a generalist MLLM through pure data scaling alone, without any 3D-specific architectural components or training objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251311/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CUBE-LLM takes a single RGB image (336×336 or 672×672) and a free-form text prompt as primary input, and can optionally accept specialist 3D box predictions (e.g., top-30 CenterPoint outputs from LiDAR) or a 2D bounding box as a visual prompt. The model outputs spatial predictions as autoregressive text token sequences: a 3D box is emitted in the fixed order [x̂, ŷ, z, w, h, l, r₁, r₂, r₃], where each numeric value is encoded as 3 text tokens (3 decimal places), naturally encoding the 2D-to-3D structure. It also supports versatile output formats including 2D boxes, 2D points, scalar depth, and free-text answers, all within a multi-turn conversation.

## Method

CUBE-LLM is built on LLaVA-1.5 with the CLIP encoder replaced by DINOv2, and is trained entirely as a single next-token prediction task on the newly constructed LV3D dataset (9.6M images, 40.9M QA pairs) that unifies 16 existing 2D and 3D datasets under a common multi-turn question-answering format. The core insight is that 3D understanding emerges from careful data curation rather than architectural changes: (1) all 3D labels are standardized to a virtual camera (Omni3D-style) and decomposed into sub-tasks (2D point, depth, size, orientation) to create versatile I/O formats; (2) visual chain-of-thought samples interleave easy-to-hard questions about the same object (e.g., 2D box → 3D box) to exploit the autoregressive structure and induce 2D-to-3D generalization; (3) specialist prompting is trained by mixing ground-truth candidate boxes into the system prompt, enabling test-time adaptation to any external detector. Training proceeds in two stages: low-resolution (336×336) pretraining with a frozen visual encoder, followed by high-resolution (672×672) full fine-tuning on the complete LV3D dataset.

## Results

On the Talk2Car 3D grounding benchmark, CUBE-LLM with LiDAR specialist prompting (CenterPoint) achieves 71.4 BEV AP_A and 64.1 3D AP_A, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art MSSG (50.1 / 45.4) by 21.3 and 18.7 points respectively. On DriveLM-Grounding, CUBE-LLM with full LV3D pretraining reaches 66.0 BEV AP_A, nearly doubling the LLaVA-1.5 baseline (33.2). For complex driving reasoning (DriveLM-QA), CUBE-LLM achieves an overall score of 50.1, improving over the DriveLM baseline (32.4) by 17.7 points. On refCOCO/+/g, CUBE-LLM (7B) attains the best generalist average of 87.0, and maintains competitive scores on VQAv2 (78.3), GQA (62.4), and POPE (87.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Talk2Car 2D grounding, camera-only CUBE-LLM (79.2 AP₀.₅) outperforms all 2D specialist baselines including FA (73.5), Stacked VLBert (71.0), CMRT (69.1), ViLBERT (68.9), and VL-BERT (63.1). For 3D grounding, CUBE-LLM with LiDAR prompting (71.4 BEV AP_A) decisively beats the camera+LiDAR specialist MSSG (50.1) by 21.3 points and Talk2Car-3D (30.6) by 40.8 points. On DriveLM-QA, CUBE-LLM (50.1 overall) surpasses both the DriveLM baseline (32.4) and LLaVA-1.5 (36.1), with the largest gain in the Match metric (39.0 vs. 28.3 and 26.1). On refCOCO/+/g, CUBE-LLM 7B (87.0 avg) outperforms all generalist MLLMs—Qwen-VL (85.7), Ferret (83.9), MiniGPT-v2 (83.8), Shikra (82.9)—and all specialist models except GroundingDINO (86.6). On general MLLM benchmarks (VQAv2, GQA, VizWiz, SQA, POPE), CUBE-LLM matches or slightly exceeds LLaVA-1.5 (e.g., GQA 62.4 vs. 62.0, POPE 87.1 vs. 85.9), confirming that 3D capability is additive rather than a trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

CUBE-LLM extends the LLaVA-1.5 instruction-tuning paradigm by replacing the CLIP visual encoder with DINOv2 and introducing a unified 2D+3D pretraining dataset (LV3D) that reformats diverse grounding and detection labels into multi-turn QA conversations. Conceptually, it departs from prior 3D grounding work (ScanRefer, ReferIt3D, Uni3DL) that relies on point-cloud inputs and specialist architectures, instead showing that an RGB-only MLLM can learn 3D spatial reasoning through data scaling alone. The visual chain-of-thought inference strategy (predict 2D box, then condition on it to predict 3D box) and the specialist-prompting mechanism (injecting CenterPoint predictions as candidate boxes) draw analogies from LLM chain-of-thought and Set-of-Mark prompting, respectively, but apply them to the 3D grounding setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core finding that no 3D-specific architecture or loss is needed—only careful data curation and task decomposition—challenges the prevailing assumption that 3D perception requires dedicated inductive bias, and the LV3D dataset (9.6M images, 40.9M QA pairs) provides a reusable resource for the community. Practical limitations remain: CUBE-LLM handles only single RGB frames (no video for motion reasoning), cannot scale beyond 672×672 resolution without token resampling, and its largest gains on Talk2Car depend on specialist LiDAR prompts rather than pure camera input. Open questions include whether the data-scaling recipe generalizes to novel 3D domains (e.g., robotics, AR) and whether the 2D→3D chain-of-thought can be extended to multi-view or sequential reasoning.
