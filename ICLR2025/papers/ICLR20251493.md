## Problem

Diffusion models require many sequential denoising steps, making inference slow. Existing parallel sampling methods (ParaDiGMS, ParaTAA) parallelize all timesteps simultaneously using dense triangular nonlinear equation systems, which becomes computationally prohibitive for large models and accumulates errors across all prior timesteps into the final sample. No prior approach offers a hierarchical strategy that balances parallel and sequential computation to work efficiently under constrained GPU resources.

## Input / Output

ParaSolver is a training-free wrapper around pre-trained diffusion models, taking standard random noise (and optionally a text prompt for text-to-image) and producing a generated image. It operates on latent-space models (Stable Diffusion v2, 768×768 pixels, 4×96×96 latent) and pixel-space models (LSUN Church, 256×256). The method is parameterized by a subinterval count N, a sliding window size p, a preconditioning step count M, and a tolerance δ, all of which trade off speed against sample fidelity.

## Method

ParaSolver reformulates the sequential diffusion integral as a system of banded nonlinear equations (Definition 1), where each equation links only adjacent timesteps rather than all prior ones as in the triangular formulation of ParaTAA. The key structural insight is that the Jacobian of this banded system has a unit-diagonal block structure (Eq. 10), allowing the diagonal to be computed exactly and the off-diagonal Jacobian terms to be replaced by identity matrices without harming convergence (Proposition 4). This yields a simple, Jacobian-free parallel update rule (Eq. 13) that can be applied within a sliding window of p timesteps, with the window advancing as local states converge. A novel initialization scheme uses M sequential preconditioning steps followed by a single score-network evaluation to estimate the clean sample, then back-propagates through the reverse process to populate all remaining initial guesses at negligible cost. The hierarchical design means N=1 recovers pure sequential sampling and N=T recovers full parallelism, with intermediate values (e.g., N=15) achieving the best wall-clock speedup by keeping the nonlinear system small and sparse.

## Results

ParaSolver achieves up to 8.1× wall-clock speedup on Stable Diffusion-v2 (DDPM 1000 steps: 15.9s vs. 128.0s) and up to 12.1× on LSUN Church (DDPM 1000 steps: 4.1s vs. 50.0s), while maintaining FID and CLIP scores comparable to or slightly better than the sequential baselines. Across all tested configurations (DDPM, DDIM, DPMSolver at 25–1000 steps), ParaSolver consistently outperforms ParaDiGMS in both wall-clock time and NFE, reducing iteration counts by 2.5×–31× relative to sequential sampling. The method requires fewer NFEs than ParaDiGMS (e.g., 1065 vs. 2024 for DDPM 1000 steps on Stable Diffusion-v2), indicating more efficient parallel refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParaSolver dominates ParaDiGMS (Shih et al., 2024) across every configuration tested: on Stable Diffusion-v2 it achieves 8.1× vs. 3.9× speedup for DDPM-1000, 3.3× vs. 1.9× for DDIM-50, and 3.1× vs. 1.8× for DPMSolver-50, while also halving the NFE (e.g., 1065 vs. 2024 for DDPM-1000). On LSUN Church, the gap widens to 12.1× vs. 4.6× for DDPM-1000. Against the sequential baselines (DDPM, DDIM, DPMSolver), ParaSolver matches or marginally improves FID (e.g., 55.3 vs. 55.9 for DDPM-1000; 61.9 vs. 62.9 for DDIM-25) while reducing wall-clock time by 2–12×. The paper does not directly compare against ParaTAA (Tang et al., 2024) as it was not available in the Diffusers library at the time of writing, though the authors note ParaTAA can be incorporated into their framework. With 8 GPUs, ParaSolver scales to 12.1× speedup while ParaDiGMS plateaus at 4.6×, and even on a single GPU ParaSolver achieves 2.5× vs. ParaDiGMS's 1.4×, demonstrating the advantage of the sparse banded structure over the dense triangular one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parallel sampling paradigm for diffusion models introduced by ParaDiGMS (Shih et al., 2024) and ParaTAA (Tang et al., 2024), which both frame sequential denoising as solving a system of nonlinear equations over all timesteps simultaneously. The key departure is reformulating the system with a banded (sparse) structure rather than a dense triangular one, enabling hierarchical sampling where each subproblem involves only a local neighborhood of timesteps. The authors further exploit the unit-diagonal structure of the resulting Jacobian to derive a simplified update rule that avoids expensive Jacobian computation, and they provide a principled initialization strategy using a few reverse-process steps. The framework is presented as a strict generalization: N=1 recovers sequential sampling and N=T recovers the full parallel approach of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.1× wall-clock speedup without quality degradation is practically significant for interactive and real-time generative applications, and the method's compatibility with existing fast solvers (DDIM, DPMSolver) makes it a drop-in acceleration layer. The banded structure's sparsity means ParaSolver scales better under constrained GPU memory (2.5× on a single GPU vs. 1.4× for ParaDiGMS), which is relevant for deployment on consumer hardware. Open questions include the optimal choice of subinterval count N (which trades off parallelism against error accumulation), whether preconditioning steps can themselves be parallelized, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D diffusion models where the sequential dimension is much longer.
