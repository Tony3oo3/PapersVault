## Problem

Existing training-free ODE solvers for diffusion models suffer from significantly amplified discretization errors when the number of function evaluations (NFE) drops below 10, making it extremely difficult to achieve high-quality sampling in very few steps. While higher-order solvers attempt to address this by estimating the noise network multiple times per step, they still fail to converge at low NFE. The paper asks whether one can reduce unnecessary NFE in existing solvers while simultaneously correcting their inherent discretization errors, without any additional training.

## Input / Output

PFDiff is a plug-and-play wrapper around any existing p-order ODE solver (e.g., DDIM, DPM-Solver-1/2/3) applied to a frozen pre-trained diffusion model. It takes a standard Gaussian noise sample x_T and, for conditional generation, a text prompt or class label, then produces a generated image at the target resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for CelebA/ImageNet-64, 256×256 for LSUN/ImageNet-256, 512×512 latent for Stable Diffusion). The method requires no architectural modification or retraining; it only modifies how the solver's score evaluations are scheduled across timesteps, completing two state updates per single noise-network call (1 NFE per iteration for first-order solvers).

## Method

PFDiff introduces a two-stage timestep-skipping update: (1) it reuses the noise-network output cached from the previous timestep (the 'past score') to advance the current state to a 'springboard' intermediate state, and (2) it evaluates the noise network once at the springboard to obtain a 'future score,' which then drives a larger leapfrog update of the original current state over a k-step interval. This foresight mechanism is inspired by Nesterov momentum in SGD, exploiting the observed similarity between DPM sampling trajectories and gradient descent. Proposition 3.1 proves that evaluating the score at a future time point ε ∈ (t_{i-1}, t_i) strictly reduces the magnitude of neglected higher-order Taylor terms compared to evaluating at the current point, thereby correcting the discretization error of first-order ODE solvers. The method is parameterized as PFDiff-k_h (k=1,2,3; h≤k), where k controls the skip length and h selects which intermediate springboard to use, yielding six configurations that approximate the optimal intermediate score without increasing computational cost.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64 with classifier guidance (s=1.0), PFDiff+DDIM achieves 16.46 FID at 4 NFE, an 88.14% improvement over DDIM's 138.81 FID at the same NFE. On MS-COCO2014 with Stable Diffusion (s=7.5), PFDiff+DDIM reaches 13.06 FID at 10 NFE, surpassing the previous SOTA DPM-Solver-v3(2M) (15.32 FID at 10 NFE) by 14.25%. On CIFAR-10 (discrete), PFDiff+DDIM achieves 4.10 FID at 15 NFE, nearly matching DDIM's 4.04 FID at 1000 NFE. On CIFAR-10 (continuous), PFDiff+DPM-Solver-3 reduces FID from 233.56 to 5.67 at 9 NFE (97.57% improvement), resolving convergence failures of higher-order solvers at low NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFDiff consistently outperforms all compared baselines across every dataset and NFE setting tested. Against DDIM (its own baseline solver), PFDiff achieves dramatic gains: 16.46 vs 138.81 FID at 4 NFE on ImageNet 64×64, and 13.06 vs 16.78 FID at 10 NFE on MS-COCO2014. Against higher-order solvers DPM-Solver-2/3 and DPM-Solver++(2M), PFDiff wins by a wide margin at NFE≤10 (e.g., 13.06 vs 15.99/16.11/15.93 at 10 NFE on COCO), while the higher-order solvers sometimes fail to converge entirely at very low NFE (e.g., DPM-Solver-3 on ImageNet 64×64 reaches 383.71 FID at 4 NFE). Against the SOTA training-free method DPM-Solver-v3(2M), PFDiff achieves 13.06 vs 15.32 FID at 10 NFE on COCO (s=7.5), a 14.25% relative improvement. Against UniPC, PFDiff leads by 3.09 FID at 10 NFE on COCO. Against AutoDiffusion (which requires additional search costs), PFDiff achieves 16.46 vs 17.86 FID at 4 NFE on ImageNet 64×64 without any search overhead. Against Analytic-DDIM, PFDiff+DDIM outperforms at NFE<10 on CIFAR-10 (22.38 vs 289.84 at 4 NFE), though Analytic-DDIM retains a slight edge at higher NFE due to its variance correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

PFDiff positions itself as an orthogonal, training-free timestep-skipping layer that can be stacked on top of any existing p-order ODE solver (DDIM, DPM-Solver, etc.), rather than proposing a new solver from scratch. Conceptually, it draws on two prior threads: (1) the observation that noise-network outputs at adjacent timesteps are highly similar (motivating the 'springboard' from past scores), and (2) Nesterov momentum's foresight update mechanism, repurposed here to compute a future score that corrects first-order discretization errors. This departs from prior orthogonal acceleration methods (AutoDiffusion's search-based step selection, DeepCache's feature caching) by requiring no additional search cost and by providing a theoretical error-correction guarantee (Proposition 3.1) that those methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PFDiff offers a practical, drop-in acceleration that reduces NFE by roughly 2× while simultaneously improving sample quality at very low step counts (e.g., 16.46 FID at 4 NFE vs. 138.81 for DDIM on ImageNet 64×64), making near-real-time generation from pre-trained diffusion models more accessible without retraining. The theoretical result that future scores reduce higher-order Taylor remainder terms provides a principled explanation for why the method corrects first-order solver errors rather than merely trading quality for speed. Open questions include whether the six k-h configurations can be selected automatically without manual tuning, whether the approach extends to video or 3D diffusion models where trajectory geometry may differ, and how it interacts with emerging consistency-model or flow-matching paradigms that change the underlying ODE structure.
