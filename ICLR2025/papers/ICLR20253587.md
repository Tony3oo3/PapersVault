## Problem

MQUAKE, the sole public benchmark for multi-hop knowledge editing (MHKE), contains up to 33% or 76% corrupted questions and ground-truth labels due to intra-contamination between edited and unedited cases, inner contamination among edited cases, conflicting edits, missing information in generated instructions, and duplicated cases. These errors systematically distort the reported performance of all prior MHKE methods, making it impossible to reliably compare or advance the field without a corrected evaluation backbone.

## Input / Output

GWalk takes a natural-language multi-hop question (a chain of 2–4 single-hop subquestions derived from Wikidata RDF triples) and a knowledge graph of edited facts stored as structured (subject, relation, object) triples. It sequentially decomposes the question into subquestions, queries the edited-fact KG via embedding-similarity retrieval on entities and relations at each hop, and outputs the final answer. The MQUAKE-REMASTERED datasets additionally provide a dynamic masking API that, given a case_id and an edited flag, returns a contamination-free set of triples for building the editing knowledge bank, enabling evaluation of both edited and unedited cases simultaneously.

## Method

The paper's core technical contributions are threefold. First, a comprehensive audit identifies five distinct error types in MQUAKE with quantitative statistics across all editing-intensity settings. Second, MQUAKE-REMASTERED fixes these errors via hard corrections (resolving conflicting edits, rewriting incomplete instructions with Llama-3.1-405B, removing duplicates) and a dynamic masking mechanism that removes at most N conflicting triples per case at inference time, preserving full dataset capacity without sacrificing any cases. Third, GWalk is a minimally invasive memory-based editing method that stores edited facts as structured triples in a knowledge graph rather than as natural-language descriptions, leveraging graph topology for precise entity-and-relation retrieval at each hop. This avoids the unintended retrievals and hallucinations that plague NL-embedding-based methods like MeLLo, while requiring no external unedited KG, no dataset-specific training splits, and no parameter updates—yet it achieves beyond-SOTA performance across all evaluated models and editing intensities.

## Results

On MQUAKE-REMASTERED-CF-3K with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, GWalk achieves 76.83% total accuracy at 100-edit (69% edited, 77.1% unedited) and 75.03% at 1000-edit, far surpassing MeLLo (<1%) and DeepEdit (15.50% and 11.87% respectively). On MQUAKE-REMASTERED-T with the same model, GWalk reaches 84.01% at full-edit, compared to MeLLo's 16.58% and DeepEdit's 34.71%. On the original (unfixed) MQUAKE-CF-3K, GWalk scores only 36.07% versus 57.37% on the remastered version, demonstrating that dataset errors suppress true method performance by up to 21 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GWalk decisively outperforms all compared baselines across every model and dataset setting. Against MeLLo (the original MQUAKE method), GWalk leads by over 70 points on Llama-3.1-8B (76.83 vs. <1 at 100-edit CF-3K) and by ~42 points on Vicuna-7B (60.90 vs. 18.00). DeepEdit, the strongest parameter-based baseline, is also far behind: 15.50% vs. 76.83% on Llama-3.1-8B at 100-edit. ICE and IKE (in-context learning methods) consistently hit OOM or <1% accuracy as the number of edited facts grows, making them non-competitive at scale. On MQUAKE-REMASTERED-CF-6334 (for parameter-based methods), GWalk achieves 57.55% (Vicuna-7B, 100-edit) versus PokeMQA's 21.77% (6344-edit only) and MeLLo's 19.16%. On RippleEdit (single-edit), GWalk matches or exceeds ICE (85.7 vs. 85.1 on Popular C1) while substantially outperforming ROME (85.7 vs. 37.4) and MeLLo (85.7 vs. 45.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MQUAKE (Zhong et al., 2023), the founding benchmark for multi-hop knowledge editing (MHKE), and the suite of methods evaluated upon it (MeLLo, PokeMQA, RAE, DeepEdit, ICE, IKE, ROME, MEND). It departs from prior partial error identifications by GMeLLo (missing instructions) and DeepEdit (inner contamination in one setting) by providing the first comprehensive audit covering five distinct error types across all MQUAKE variants. The proposed GWalk method positions itself against the RAG-based editing paradigm (MeLLo) by replacing natural-language embedding retrieval with structured knowledge-graph topology queries, and explicitly contrasts with methods that exploit MQUAKE-specific idiosyncrasies (e.g., using Wikidata as an external KG or training on MQUAKE-CF's non-3K subset).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since MQUAKE is the sole publicly available benchmark for MHKE, the discovery that 33–76% of its cases are contaminated or corrupted means that a large body of published editing-method results may be unreliable, making this audit practically critical for the field. The observation that several SOTA methods appear to overfit MQUAKE's dataset-specific structure (37 relation types, Wikidata provenance, overlapping train/test splits) raises important methodological questions about how to evaluate editing methods faithfully. Open questions include how MHKE methods perform on non-triple-representable knowledge, whether the KG-based retrieval advantage of GWalk generalizes beyond Wikidata-derived datasets, and how to handle the operational cost of maintaining unified edited/unedited knowledge graphs at scale.
