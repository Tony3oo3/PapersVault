## Problem

Statement autoformalization suffers from two critical gaps: (1) the absence of a faithful, universal automated evaluation metric that aligns with human intuition—existing proxies like BLEU, typecheck pass rate, and LLM graders are fragile, non-deterministic, or too weak; and (2) the agnosia of contextual information, where autoformalizers ignore previously formalized definitions and theorems in formal libraries, causing severe hallucination of identifiers and syntax, especially in out-of-distribution settings.

## Input / Output

For autoformalization, the method takes an informal mathematical statement and a formal library (Mathlib 4, 243,797 objects) as input and produces a formal Lean 4 statement as output; the retrieval sub-module encodes the informal statement into a dense embedding and retrieves top-k formal objects by cosine similarity against precomputed library embeddings. For the BEq evaluation metric, the input is a pair of syntactically valid Lean 4 formal statements, and the output is a binary equivalence judgment determined by bidirectional symbolic verification in the Lean kernel. Training data is synthesized by topologically informalizing the entire Mathlib 4 library, yielding informal-formal parallel pairs where each object's informalization is conditioned on its dependencies' informalizations.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary contributions. First, BEq (Bidirectional Extended Definitional Equivalence) extends Lean 4's definitional equality by equipping it with a restricted set of transformation primitives (tactics such as exact, rw, intro, cases', constructor) and a neural transformation function (a 20B LLM prompted to generate tactic sequences); two statements are BEq if each can be symbolically transformed to be definitionally equal to the other under these restricted tactics, verified by the Lean kernel. Second, RAutoformalizer introduces a dependency retrieval paradigm: it parses the full dependency graph of Mathlib 4, topologically informalizes all formal objects bottom-up using a 10-shot prompted LLM, fine-tunes a BGE-M3 dense retriever on the resulting parallel data with hard-negative mining, and conditions an InternLM2-Math-Base-7B autoformalizer on the informal statement plus top-5 retrieved dependencies to generate formal statements. A novel OOD benchmark, Con-NF (961 theorem pairs from the New Foundations library), is constructed to evaluate research-level generalization beyond Mathlib 4.

## Results

On the in-distribution ProofNet benchmark, RAutoformalizer achieves 18.18% BEq@8, outperforming the strongest baseline (GPT-4o ICL at 12.83%) by 5.35 percentage points. On the out-of-distribution Con-NF benchmark, RAutoformalizer reaches 16.86% BEq@8, a 3.7× improvement over the best baseline (MMA(Lean) at 4.58%). The proposed BEq metric achieves 100% precision and 90.50% accuracy on the 200-pair Human Equivalence Benchmark, surpassing the best LLM-grader baseline (DeepSeek-V2.5 majority voting) which reaches only 70.59% precision and 82.50% accuracy. The oracle dependency retrieval (RA+R) further demonstrates substantial headroom, reaching 31.28% BEq@8 on ProofNet and 55.36% on Con-NF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAutoformalizer outperforms all nine baselines on both benchmarks. On ProofNet, it beats GPT-4o ICL (12.83% → 18.18% BEq@8) and Lean-Workbook fine-tuning (9.89% → 18.18%), with the non-retrieval ablation (RA−R, 16.58%) already surpassing all baselines, indicating the quality of topological informalization data. On the OOD Con-NF benchmark, the advantage is dramatic: RAutoformalizer achieves 16.86% BEq@8 versus 4.58% for MMA(Lean) and 4.27% for DeepSeek-V2.5 ICL, a roughly 3.7× improvement. For the BEq evaluation metric itself, it achieves 100% precision (vs. 70.59% for DeepSeek-V2.5 majority voting and 62.96% for BLEU threshold) and 90.50% accuracy (vs. 82.50% for the best LLM grader), though recall is lower (72.86% vs. 85.71%) due to the strictness of formal verification. For dependency retrieval, the fine-tuned retriever with topological informalization achieves 35.52% Recall@5 on ProofNet and 24.32% on Con-NF, a >10× improvement over pretrained BGE-M3 (1.93% / 5.66%) and >200× over BM25 (0.16% / 4.41%), confirming that dependency retrieval is a fundamentally different task from semantic similarity search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant autoformalization paradigm (prompt or fine-tune an LLM to directly generate formal statements) by identifying two structural gaps: the absence of a faithful automated evaluation metric and the agnosia of library context. For evaluation, it extends definitional equality from dependent type theory (Lean 4) with a restricted neural transformation function, going beyond prior proxy metrics (BLEU, typecheck, LLM graders) and the SMT-solver approach of Murphy et al. (2024) which was restricted to Euclidean geometry. For context, it introduces dependency retrieval as a formal sub-task and a retrieval-augmented generation paradigm (RAutoformalizer), building on dense retrieval (BGE-M3) and topological sorting of formal library dependencies, rather than the in-context learning or rejection-sampling approaches of prior work (Wu et al. 2022, Poiroux et al. 2024, Ying et al. 2024a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Con-NF benchmark (961 research-level theorem pairs from New Foundations) fills a critical gap in evaluating OOD generalization beyond undergraduate-level Mathlib problems, and BEq offers a principled, universal automated evaluation that the field has lacked. The finding that dependency retrieval is fundamentally distinct from semantic similarity retrieval (10× recall gap over pretrained embeddings) challenges the implicit assumption that general-purpose retrieval models can handle formal library navigation. Key open questions include: whether the large oracle-to-retriever gap (55% vs 17% BEq@8 on Con-NF) can be closed with multi-vector or reranking approaches, whether BEq can be extended to proof-level equivalence, and whether the topological informalization strategy generalizes to other formal libraries beyond Lean 4.
