## Problem

Most multimodal generative models that jointly understand and generate images and text rely on separately pretrained, frozen, lossy image encoders/decoders (e.g., VQ-VAEs or VAEs) that are agnostic to the downstream task and limit model performance. No prior model has demonstrated the ability to train an autoregressive transformer end-to-end from raw pixels while simultaneously producing high-fidelity images and competitive log-likelihood bounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253097/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 256×256 RGB images (central crop, aspect-ratio preserved) and text (sentencepiece tokens, max 64 tokens) in either image-then-text or text-then-image order, with loss applied only to the second modality. Images are converted into 256 soft tokens of dimension 128 via an invertible normalizing flow, which the autoregressive transformer models with a 1024-component GMM head; text tokens use a standard categorical head. At inference, the same flow inverts the generated soft tokens back to pixels, so no separate decoder is needed, and the model can also output explicit NLL scores for raw images.

## Method

JetFormer couples a transformer-based normalizing flow (32 affine coupling blocks with ViT sub-networks, ~450–650M params) with a Gemma-style decoder-only autoregressive transformer (350M–1.3B params) trained end-to-end to maximize the joint log-likelihood of raw pixels and text. The flow losslessly maps each image patch into a soft-token embedding; the autoregressive model then predicts these embeddings sequentially using a GMM prediction head, while a subset of redundant latent channels is factored out and modeled with a simple Gaussian prior to reduce computational burden. A key training innovation is an RGB noise curriculum: additive Gaussian noise (σ₀=64) is injected into input images and cosine-decayed to zero over training, steering the model to prioritize high-level structure early without affecting the final NLL. Classifier-free guidance via rejection sampling over the GMM is used at inference to improve sample quality, and the entire system—flow, transformer, and GMM head—is optimized jointly with a single NLL objective, requiring no auxiliary perceptual or GAN losses.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet-256 generation, JetFormer-L achieves FID 6.64, Precision 0.69, Recall 0.56, and NLL 3.05 bits/dim, competitive with VQ-VAE- and VAE-based baselines while being the only method without any pretrained components. For text-to-image generation on COCO (256×256), JetFormer-L achieves zero-shot FID-30k of 18.63 and fine-tuned FID of 13.07, with NLL 3.85. For image understanding, JetFormer-L (T&I) reaches CIDEr 119.8 on COCO captioning and 70.0% VQAv2 accuracy, surpassing CLIP L/14 (118.2, 67.9%) and CapPa L/14 (118.7, 68.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On text-to-image generation, JetFormer-L (FID 18.63 zero-shot) outperforms DALL-E (27.50), CogView (27.10), CogView2 (24.00), and ARGVLT (16.93), but trails MAGVLT (12.08), Make-A-Scene (11.84), LDM-KL-8-G (12.63), GLIDE (12.24), and DALL-E-2 (10.39); however, JetFormer is the only method that does not rely on any pretrained VQ-VAE, VAE, or super-resolution model. On class-conditional ImageNet-256, JetFormer-L (FID 6.64) beats BigGAN-deep (6.95) but lags behind VQ-VAE-based methods like ViT-VQGAN (3.04) and LDM-4-G (3.60), though it achieves the highest recall (0.56) among all compared methods, suggesting less mode collapse. For image understanding, JetFormer-L (T&I) surpasses CLIP L/14 (CIDEr 119.8 vs. 118.2; VQAv2 70.0% vs. 67.9%) and CapPa L/14 (119.8 vs. 118.7; 70.0% vs. 68.6%), and substantially outperforms VQ-VAE-based generative models MAGVLT (CIDEr 110.7, VQAv2 65.7%) and ARGVLT (CIDEr 94.7). The paper also shows that replacing the end-to-end normalizing flow with a frozen pretrained VAE (2-stage) degrades T2I FID from 30.6 to 95.4, confirming the benefit of joint training.

## Relation to Prior Work

JetFormer combines three threads: the normalizing flow architecture from Jet (Kolesnikov et al., 2024) for lossless image-to-latent mapping, the GMM prediction head and CFG rejection sampling from GIVT (Tschannen et al., 2024) for modeling continuous soft tokens, and the standard decoder-only transformer for autoregressive sequence modeling. The key departure from the dominant VQ-VAE/VAE-based paradigm (DALL-E, CogView, MAGVLT, LDM) is eliminating all pretrained, frozen modality-specific encoders: the normalizing flow is trained end-to-end with the transformer and serves as both image encoder (for understanding) and decoder (for generation) via its invertibility. The noise curriculum, while inspired by diffusion model training schedules, is technically a simple data augmentation that does not condition the model on noise level or perform progressive denoising at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JetFormer is the first model to simultaneously produce high-fidelity images and explicit log-likelihood bounds on raw pixels without any pretrained components, offering a clean end-to-end formulation where the representation is fully tailored to the joint task. The noise curriculum finding—that a simple training-time augmentation can dramatically improve perceptual quality (FID 44.71→7.84) while barely affecting NLL—provides a practical recipe for likelihood-based image models. However, visual quality still lags behind SOTA diffusion models leveraging pretrained latent representations, and the full end-to-end training increases computational cost; whether the approach can close this gap at scale remains an open question.
