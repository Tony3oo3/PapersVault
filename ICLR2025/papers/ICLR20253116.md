## Problem

When pre-trained reward models (e.g., segmentation or depth networks) are used to enforce condition–output alignment in conditional diffusion training, they produce systematically inaccurate feedback on newly generated images due to the distribution shift introduced by diffusion noise. Naively backpropagating these misleading rewards degrades both controllability and generation quality, yet prior reward-based methods (e.g., ControlNet++) apply uniform supervision regardless of feedback reliability.

## Input / Output

The method operates at 512×512 resolution in the SD1.5 latent space and consumes a triplet: a source RGB image, a natural-language text prompt, and a pixel-aligned conditional control (segmentation mask, Hed/LineArt edge map, or depth map). It produces a single generated image that should simultaneously satisfy the text description and the spatial condition. During training, the same condition is fed through two independent diffusion forward passes at different timesteps to yield two intermediate reconstructions, which are decoded and scored by a frozen reward model to estimate per-pixel uncertainty before the consistency loss is computed.

## Method

Ctrl-U introduces a two-phase uncertainty-aware reward modeling framework on top of a fine-tuned ControlNet. In the uncertainty estimation phase, the identical condition is propagated through the diffusion UNet twice with different noise timesteps (|t₁−t₂|=1) and resampled Gaussian noise; the two decoded images are passed through a frozen reward model, and the pixel-wise KL-divergence (segmentation) or ℓ₁ distance (edge/depth) between the two extracted conditions serves as a parameter-free uncertainty indicator. In the uncertainty regularization phase, the standard per-pixel consistency loss is divided by exp(U) and augmented with a λ·U penalty, so that low-uncertainty (reliable) rewards receive amplified gradient signal while high-uncertainty (unreliable) rewards are down-weighted, preventing the model from overfitting to spurious reward feedback. A timestep threshold t_thre=400 gates the reward loss so that only low-noise steps contribute to consistency training, and the total objective combines the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss with the uncertainty-regularized reward loss weighted by μ₀=0.1.

## Results

On the primary controllability benchmark (Table 1), Ctrl-U achieves 46.49 mIoU on ADE20K segmentation, 49.91 mIoU on COCO-Stuff segmentation, 0.8401 SSIM on Hed edge, 0.8488 SSIM on LineArt edge, and 25.86 RMSE on depth maps, outperforming the prior SOTA ControlNet++ by 6.53% (relative) on ADE20K and 8.65% on depth. On image quality (FID, Table 2), Ctrl-U achieves 28.61 on ADE20K and 15.79 on COCO-Stuff, improving over ControlNet++ by 2.98 and 3.50 points respectively. CLIP-Score (Table 3) remains comparable to ControlNet++ across most conditions, confirming that the uncertainty-aware reward modeling does not degrade text-to-image alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ctrl-U outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor ControlNet++ (Li et al., ECCV 2024), Ctrl-U gains +2.85 mIoU on ADE20K (46.49 vs 43.64), +15.35 mIoU on COCO-Stuff (49.91 vs 34.56), +0.030 SSIM on Hed edge, +0.009 SSIM on LineArt, and reduces depth RMSE by 2.46 (25.86 vs 28.32). Against ControlNet (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023), the gains are even larger: +13.94 mIoU on ADE20K and +10.04 RMSE reduction on depth. T2I-Adapter (Mou et al., AAAI 2024) and GLIGEN (Li et al., CVPR 2023) trail substantially, with T2I-Adapter scoring only 12.61 mIoU on ADE20K and GLIGEN 23.78. Uni-ControlNet (Zhao et al., NeurIPS 2023) and UniControl (Qin et al., 2023) also fall well behind, achieving 19.39 and 25.44 mIoU respectively on ADE20K. On FID (image quality), Ctrl-U leads all baselines by 1.88–3.50 points, and on CLIP-Score it matches or slightly trails ControlNet++ by ≤0.4 points, indicating no meaningful loss in text controllability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward-based post-training paradigm introduced by ControlNet++ (Li et al., 2024a), which uses a pre-trained reward model to enforce condition-image alignment during diffusion fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that the reward model suffers from distribution shift on newly generated data, producing systematically inaccurate feedback that corrupts gradient backpropagation. Rather than introducing auxiliary uncertainty networks (as in Kendall & Gal, 2017; Dou et al., 2022), the paper estimates reward uncertainty parametrically via prediction variance across two diffusion forwards at slightly different timesteps, then uses this variance to adaptively reweight the consistency loss. This positions the work at the intersection of conditional diffusion generation and uncertainty-aware learning, applying a lightweight variance-based uncertainty signal to a setting where prior methods treated reward feedback as unconditionally reliable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practical and previously underappreciated failure mode in reward-based diffusion fine-tuning: even at t=0, the reward model's predictions on generated images deviate from ground truth, and this error grows with timestep, injecting incorrect gradients throughout training. The proposed fix is parameter-free and computationally cheap (one extra forward pass), making it easy to adopt in existing ControlNet-style pipelines. However, the approach is validated only on SD1.5 with four conditional types, and the two-forward uncertainty estimation assumes visual similarity between the two generations—a condition that breaks at high timesteps and necessitates the ad-hoc threshold t_thre. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to SDXL or newer architectures, to multi-reward or preference-based fine-tuning (e.g., DPO for diffusion), and whether the KL-divergence-based uncertainty is optimal compared to learned or ensemble-based alternatives.
