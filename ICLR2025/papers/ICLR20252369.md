## Problem

Autoregressive visual generation relies on Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) to achieve high sample quality, but CFG introduces design inconsistencies with language modeling: it doubles sampling cost by requiring two model inferences per token and forces random condition masking during training. The paper asks whether guided sampling can be entirely eliminated by instead fine-tuning pretrained AR models to directly fit the same target distribution that CFG achieves at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label (or text caption) as a conditioning signal and produces a synthetic image via standard autoregressive next-token decoding over discrete VQGAN tokens. Experiments use ImageNet at 256×256 (VAR) or 384×384 (LlamaGen, resized to 256×256 for evaluation). The fine-tuning phase requires only the original pretraining dataset with no additional annotations, and the resulting model uses a single forward pass per token at inference—halving the sampling cost compared to CFG.

## Method

CCA derives a contrastive fine-tuning loss by recognizing that the CFG target distribution differs from the pretrained conditional model by a 'conditional residual' log p(x|c)/p(x), which is learnable via Noise Contrastive Estimation. The key design choice, borrowed from DPO-style LLM alignment, is to parameterize this residual as the log-ratio between the target model p_θ^sample(x|c) and the frozen pretrained model p_φ(x|c), eliminating the need for a separate unconditional or classifier network. In practice, a batch of K image-condition pairs is split into positives (original pairings) and negatives (shuffled conditions), and the loss maximizes relative likelihood for positives while minimizing it for negatives, weighted by hyperparameters β (analogous to CFG scale) and λ (negative-sample weight). One epoch of fine-tuning (~1% of pretraining cost) suffices to match CFG-level quality, and adjusting λ reproduces the diversity-fidelity trade-off that CFG achieves via its guidance scale.

## Results

On the ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional benchmark, CCA fine-tuning (1 epoch, ~1% of pretraining cost) reduces FID from 9.38 to 2.69 for LlamaGen-3B and from 5.25 to 2.54 for VAR-d30 in guidance-free sampling, while boosting IS from 112.9 to 276.8 and 175.6 to 264.2 respectively. These guidance-free numbers are competitive with CFG-enhanced results (FID 2.18/IS 263.3 for LlamaGen-3B, FID 1.92/IS 323.1 for VAR-d30) while halving the per-token inference cost. CCA also achieves a record FID of 2.54 among all guidance-free AR visual models, surpassing prior AR baselines like MAGE (FID 6.93) and RQ-Transformer (FID 7.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCA guidance-free sampling (FID 2.54 for VAR-d30, 2.69 for LlamaGen-3B) approaches but does not fully match CFG-enhanced performance (FID 1.92 and 2.18 respectively), yet eliminates the 2× inference cost of dual-model sampling. Against prior AR baselines, CCA-enhanced models substantially outperform MAGE (FID 6.93), VQGAN (FID 15.78), and RQ-Transformer (FID 7.55) in guidance-free settings, and even surpass their CFG-enhanced counterparts in FID. Compared to LLM alignment methods applied to visual generation, CCA dramatically outperforms DPO (FID 3.43 vs. 61.69 for LlamaGen-L, where DPO causes training collapse) and Unlearning (FID 3.43 vs. 12.22), confirming that the NCE-based contrastive formulation with a frozen reference model is essential. Among all model families on ImageNet 256×256, CCA-enhanced AR models achieve FID scores competitive with top diffusion (DiT-XL/2: 2.27) and mask (MDTv2: 1.58) models while using a simpler single-model sampling pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCA sits at the intersection of two previously independent research threads: Noise Contrastive Estimation (Gutmann & Hyvärinen, 2012) from the statistical learning literature, and Classifier-Free Guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) from visual generation. The paper's key conceptual move is to show that both target the same sampling distribution (Eq. 6) and that the conditional residual log p(x|c)/p(x) can be parameterized either by a separate guidance network (as in CFG) or by the log-ratio between a fine-tuned model and a frozen pretrained model (as in DPO-style alignment, Rafailov et al., 2023). This unification reframes visual guidance as a special case of contrastive alignment, borrowing the parameterization trick from LLM alignment to avoid training an auxiliary unconditional model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is halving AR visual generation inference cost by eliminating the need for a second model forward pass, while the theoretical contribution bridges language-model alignment and visual-guidance into a single framework. However, the evaluation is limited to class-conditional ImageNet 256×256; whether CCA transfers to text-to-image generation (where conditions are high-dimensional and harder to mismatch) remains an open question. The absolute FID still trails CFG by a small margin, and the per-model tuning of λ suggests the method is not yet fully plug-and-play. A natural next step is extending CCA to diffusion-based AR hybrids and text-conditioned settings.
