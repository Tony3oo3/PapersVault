## Problem

Standard VAE-based Bayesian optimisation learns an unconditional latent space that ignores the target quantity values and any available auxiliary covariates, producing a manifold that is poorly structured for the GP surrogate model used in BO. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a conditional generative model that explicitly conditions the latent prior on partially observed target values and additional covariates, yielding a latent space inherently suited for GP-based optimisation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251776/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional observations (52×52 pixel images, SMILES strings for molecules, or parse trees for mathematical expressions), a partially observed scalar target quantity (e.g., RMSE, penalised logP, or log-MSE regret, typically available for only 1–20% of the dataset), and optional auxiliary covariates (molecular properties like molecular weight and hydrogen bond counts, expression element frequencies, or geometric transformations). It outputs a single optimised high-dimensional data point that maximises or minimises the target quantity, along with its evaluated target value. Missing values in both the high-dimensional observations and the auxiliary covariates are handled natively within the probabilistic framework rather than requiring imputation or zero-filling.

## Method

The core contribution is a GP prior VAE in which the standard i.i.d. Gaussian latent prior is replaced by a GP prior conditioned on auxiliary covariates, including the partially observed target quantity itself. The GP prior uses an additive kernel decomposition (e.g., squared-exponential over the target plus application-specific kernels over each auxiliary covariate), so each latent dimension is governed by an independent GP whose covariance encodes smooth correlations across samples. To handle the fact that the target quantity is only observed for a small subset of data, the ELBO is extended with a factorisable variational approximation over the missing covariates, and a low-rank inducing-point approximation reduces the O(N³) KL computation to a mini-batch-compatible form. BO is then performed in the learned latent space using a GP surrogate and expected-improvement acquisition, with the GP prior VAE periodically retrained on the augmented dataset (every ν=10 BO steps) so that the latent manifold adapts as new target values are observed. Unlike weighted-retraining approaches, the model fitting remains unbiased toward high objective values; it is the acquisition function that drives exploitation, while the GP prior ensures the latent space remains smooth and well-structured for the surrogate.

## Results

On the GP predictive log-likelihood evaluation (Table 2), the proposed method achieves -1.72±0.08 on expression reconstruction and -1.37±0.27 on molecular discovery, outperforming all baselines. On the synthetic MNIST-based experiment (5000 instances, 500 observed), the method reduces RMSE significantly below the LSO baseline, with further gains when auxiliary covariates (rotation, x-shift, y-shift) are included in the additive GP prior kernel. On ZINC-250K molecule optimisation (450 BO steps, 1% observed penalised logP), the method identifies candidate molecules with higher penalised logP than LSO, T-LBO, and LOL-BO, and including all 7 auxiliary covariates in the additive kernel yields the best performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed GP prior VAE method outperforms all three baselines on the GP predictive log-likelihood metric: it beats LSO (Tripp et al., 2020) by 1.38 nats on expression reconstruction (-1.72 vs. -3.10) and by 0.64 nats on molecular discovery (-1.37 vs. -2.01), indicating substantially better latent-space structure for GP regression. Against T-LBO (Grosnit et al., 2021), the gains are 0.13 nats (expression) and 0.12 nats (molecular), and against LOL-BO (Maus et al., 2022) the margins are 0.03 nats and 0.02 nats respectively — smaller but consistent wins. On the synthetic MNIST experiment, the method outperforms LSO in RMSE even when conditioning only on the partially observed target (Θ2), with further improvement when auxiliary covariates are added (Θ1, Θ3). On ZINC-250K, the method achieves the highest mean penalised logP over 10 repetitions, and notably, when only 0.1% of target values are observed, the variant using only the 5 RDKit-derived covariates (without the target) performs best, demonstrating robustness in extremely low-label regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GP prior VAE framework (Casale et al., 2018) by conditioning the GP prior on the partially observed target quantity of interest and additional auxiliary covariates, thereby using the generative model itself to structure the latent space for Bayesian optimisation. It departs from standard VAE BO (Tripp et al., 2020), which learns an unconditional latent manifold, and from metric-learning-based approaches (Grosnit et al., 2021) that inject label guidance via explicit triplet/contrastive losses, by instead relying on the smoothness and correlation structure of the GP prior to produce a BO-friendly embedding. The handling of missing covariates follows the conditional VAE formulation of Ramchandran et al. (2024), adapted here to the GP prior setting with an additive kernel over heterogeneous covariates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

High-dimensional BO is a practically important problem in molecular discovery, neural architecture search, and chemical synthesis, and this paper offers a principled way to incorporate domain-specific auxiliary information (molecular properties, expression counts) directly into the latent space construction without ad-hoc loss engineering. The finding that the method can learn useful latent representations even when the target quantity is observed for only 0.1% of data suggests robustness in extremely label-scarce regimes. Open questions include how the method scales to very large latent dimensions or multi-objective settings, whether the GP prior kernel choice can be automated, and whether the periodic retraining strategy is optimal compared to continuous adaptation.
