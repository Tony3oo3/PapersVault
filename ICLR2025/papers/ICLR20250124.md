## Problem

Off-policy learning in contextual bandits suffers from high-variance policy gradient estimation when target rewards are only partially observed due to missing data, delays, censoring, or multi-stage processes. Existing approaches face a dilemma: using only the sparse target reward yields high variance, while relying solely on densely observed secondary rewards (e.g., clicks, dwell time) introduces substantial bias from reward misalignment. This paper formulates a general OPL framework with partially-observed rewards and proposes a principled method to leverage both reward types simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes logged bandit data as tuples (x, a, o, s, r), where x is a context vector, a is a discrete action, o ∈ {0,1} indicates whether the target reward r is observed, and s ∈ ℝ^{d_s} is a multi-dimensional secondary reward vector that is always observed. It also requires the logging policy π₀ and the conditional observation probability p(o|x) (estimable via a supervised classifier if unknown). The output is a parameterized policy π_θ trained to maximize a combined policy value V_c(π;β) = (1−β)V_r(π) + βV_s(π), where β controls the trade-off between target and secondary reward optimization.

## Method

HyPeR introduces a three-term policy gradient estimator for the target reward that decomposes the gradient into a model-based expectation, a secondary-reward correction term (always available), and a target-reward correction term (used only when o=1, weighted by 1/p(o|x)). This estimator is proven unbiased under full support and achieves variance reduction over standard r-DR whenever the conditional q-function q(x,a,s) is better estimated than the marginal q(x,a). The full HyPeR estimator (Eq. 11) mixes this target-reward PG with a standard DR estimator for the secondary reward via a tunable weight γ. A key insight is that setting γ ≠ β (the true objective weight) can improve finite-sample performance by exploiting the bias-variance trade-off: shifting weight toward the lower-variance secondary-reward gradient at the cost of mild bias. The optimal γ is selected via a bi-level optimization using bootstrapped training sets (to preserve data size) and a held-out validation set for policy value estimation.

## Results

The paper evaluates HyPeR on synthetic data (n=2000, |A|=10, 5-dim secondary rewards) and the KuaiRec dataset (1,411 users, 3,317 videos, |A|=100), reporting relative policy values where 0 = uniform random policy and 1 = optimal policy. HyPeR(γ=β) achieves the highest combined policy value across all experimental settings (varying observation probability p(o|x), data size n, target-secondary correlation λ, and weight β). HyPeR(Tuned γ*) consistently outperforms HyPeR(γ=β) and all other feasible baselines, with the advantage most pronounced under sparse target reward observation (p(o|x)=0.2) and small training data sizes. On KuaiRec, HyPeR methods outperform r-DR and s-DR baselines by a wide margin across all evaluated policy values (combined, target, and secondary).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyPeR is compared against six main baselines: r-IPS, r-DR (using only partially-observed target rewards), s-IPS, s-DR (using only fully-observed secondary rewards as surrogates), HyPeR(γ=0) (target-reward component only), and regression-based methods r-DM and s-DM. HyPeR(γ=β) outperforms all baselines on both combined and target policy values in every experimental setting. Critically, HyPeR(γ=0) — which uses secondary rewards only to reduce variance in the target-reward gradient — consistently beats r-DR, confirming the variance-reduction mechanism in Theorem 2. The s-IPS and s-DR baselines, which rely solely on secondary rewards, suffer from substantial bias when secondary rewards are imperfectly correlated with the target reward (smaller λ), while r-IPS and r-DR suffer from high variance when target rewards are sparsely observed. The data-driven weight tuning (HyPeR Tuned γ*) further improves over the fixed-weight HyPeR(γ=β), demonstrating that intentionally using γ≠β (an 'incorrect' weight) yields better finite-sample performance by exploiting the bias-variance trade-off. On KuaiRec, the secondary rewards are deliberately designed to be only moderately correlated with the target reward (watch ratio), making the problem non-trivial and confirming that the hybrid approach is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard off-policy learning (OPL) framework for contextual bandits—built on the IPS and DR estimators of Dudik et al. (2014) and the policy-value formulation of Swaminathan & Joachims (2015)—to the setting where target rewards are only partially observed. Rather than treating partial rewards as a nuisance to be handled by importance-weighting the observed subset (r-IPS, r-DR), HyPeR introduces a hybrid gradient estimator that decomposes the target-reward gradient into a fully-observed secondary-reward component and a partially-observed residual, thereby reducing variance while preserving unbiasedness. The strategic tuning of the mixture weight γ away from the true objective weight β is a novel idea that departs from the standard practice of matching estimator weights to objective weights, and the paper positions this as distinct from multi-objective OPL work (Wang et al., 2022a; Alizadeh et al., 2023) where rewards are unrelated rather than one serving as a variance-reduction proxy for the other.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a highly prevalent practical bottleneck—partial reward observation due to delay, censoring, or multi-stage funnels—that plagues OPL in e-commerce, healthcare, and advertising, and provides a clean theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2) that the hybrid estimator reduces variance relative to r-DR whenever the conditional q-function given secondary rewards is a better predictor. The counterintuitive finding that deliberately using a mismatched weight γ ≠ β improves finite-sample policy value is a useful practical insight for practitioners tuning OPL pipelines. Key limitations include the restriction to contextual bandits (not full MDPs/RL), reliance on a single real-world dataset (KuaiRec) with a synthetic logging policy, and the assumption that the observation probability p(o|x) is estimable; extending the framework to offline RL and validating in deployed production systems are natural next steps.
