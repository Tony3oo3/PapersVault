## Problem

Existing PDE discovery methods (PDE-FIND, SINDy, genetic programming, RL-based approaches) recover mathematical expressions from data but ignore the physical hypotheses and conservation laws that give those expressions meaning, making them fragile to noise and uninterpretable to domain scientists. No prior work formulates the task of selecting which physical hypotheses (e.g., Newtonian vs. non-Newtonian, compressible vs. incompressible) best explain observed PDE data, nor provides a benchmark for this interpretation task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatiotemporal observational fields (velocity, density, pressure, temperature) sampled on 2D spatial grids (100×100 or 50×100) over time, together with a hand-crafted decision forest encoding 16 decision points across 38 mutually exclusive/hierarchical physics hypotheses, and the unchangeable conservation laws (mass, momentum, energy). It outputs a binary decision vector identifying the active hypotheses, estimated physical parameters (e.g., viscosity μ, thermal conductivity k, specific heat C_p), and—when the 'new expression' hypothesis is selected—a discovered constitutive relation (e.g., μ as a function of shear rate γ) with fitted coefficients.

## Method

The task is formulated as a bi-level mixed-integer program: the outer level selects a hypothesis vector h and physics parameters θ_h, while the inner level (triggered only when a 'discover new expression' hypothesis is active) performs sparse symbolic regression over a function library to find a constitutive expression. Hypothesis dependencies (mutual exclusiveness, conditional branching) are encoded as a decision forest rather than optimization constraints, so a root-to-leaf path uniquely determines a valid PDE system via symbolic derivation from conservation laws. The outer search uses HSTS, an MCTS variant with a risk-seeking objective that averages only the top-ε fraction of simulation rewards (rather than the full expectation) to prioritize best-case hypothesis configurations, combined with log-normalized rewards for numerical stability. The inner search uses STOP, a sequential backward-elimination procedure that iteratively solves an L2-regularized least-squares subproblem and deactivates basis functions whose coefficients fall below a threshold. PDE parameter estimation at each node is performed by discretizing derivatives with centered finite differences and solving the resulting nonlinear program with SLSQP.

## Results

On the Fluid Mechanics benchmark (3 scenarios), HSTS achieves perfect recall and precision (1.00/1.00) for PDE term recovery in S1 and S2, and 1.00/0.93 in S3, while PDE-FIND drops to 0.20–0.88 recall and 0.01–0.04 precision with 10–20× higher expression complexity. For hypothesis selection, HSTS matches exhaustive search (ES) on top-1 recall/precision (1.00 on S1, S2; 0.91 on S3) while running 1–2 orders of magnitude faster (e.g., 0.14 h vs. 16.68 h on S1). On the Laser Fusion dataset, HSTS identifies the correct hypothesis combination with 1.00 recall/precision in 8.67 h, whereas ES requires 23.21 h and ES-TL fails to find the correct answer within the same time budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PDE-FIND (Rudy et al., 2017), HSTS wins decisively on every metric: recall is 1.00 vs. 0.20–0.88, precision is 0.93–1.00 vs. 0.01–0.04, and expression complexity is 10–20× lower (109–360 vs. 1377–2176 terms). On extrapolation (test MSR), PDE-FIND degrades by 2–3 orders of magnitude (e.g., 1.48×10⁻¹ on S1 vs. HSTS's 4.83×10⁻⁴), while HSTS stays within 10% of ground-truth PDE residuals. DISCOVER (Du et al., 2023) and SGA-PDE (Chen et al., 2022) perform far worse, with train MSR of 0.12–0.14 and 24–29 respectively, versus HSTS's 2.8×10⁻⁴–9.9×10⁻³. Against exhaustive search (ES), HSTS matches top-1 recall/precision on all scenarios while reducing wall-time by ~100× on S1 (0.14 h vs. 16.68 h) and ~6× on S3 (3.12 h vs. 21.11 h). The SINDy+GPT-4o pipeline (pipelined baseline) fails to correctly identify physics properties (e.g., misclassifies is_turbulent in S1/S2), whereas HSTS gets all decisions correct; GPT-4o only succeeds when fed ground-truth PDE expressions, confirming the advantage of the integrated approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant PDE Discovery paradigm (PDE-FIND, SINDy, DISCOVER, SGA-PDE) that treats equation recovery as unconstrained symbolic regression, arguing that such methods produce mathematically valid but physically meaningless expressions. It builds on MCTS for the outer hypothesis-selection loop (HSTS) and on STRidge's iterative ridge-regression-plus-thresholding mechanism for the inner sparse expression discovery (STOP), but restructures both into a bi-level mixed-integer program where physics hypotheses constrain the search space. The decision-forest representation of hypothesis dependencies is a new organizational idea not present in prior PDE discovery or solving work, and the paper explicitly positions 'PDE Interpretation' as a third task distinct from both PDE Solving (PINN, DeepBSDE) and PDE Discovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing PDE recovery as hypothesis selection within a physics-theory framework, the paper provides the first benchmark and algorithm for a task that aligns more closely with what practicing scientists actually need—interpreting data through known physical laws rather than rediscovering expressions from scratch. The laser fusion application demonstrates real-world relevance where certain physical quantities (magnetic fields, REB divergence) are impossible to measure directly. Key limitations include the simplified decision-forest structure (no cycles or cross-decision dependencies), limited baseline coverage for the new task, and scalability concerns for high-dimensional or highly complex PDE systems. Open questions include applying the framework to genuinely noisy experimental data, extending to decision graphs, and validating on the ongoing double-cone collision ignition experiments the authors mention.
