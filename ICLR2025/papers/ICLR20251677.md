## Problem

Standard GNN message passing on undirected graphs mimics diffusion, causing node features to homogenize and lose discriminative power as depth increases. Existing directed-graph GNNs use discrete edge directions (fully one-way or fully bidirectional), but the optimal information flow between two nodes may require a continuous, intermediate direction. Moreover, the commonly used magnetic Laplacian for directed graphs mixes in-neighbor and out-neighbor messages into both real and imaginary components, preventing a GNN from cleanly separating them.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed graph structure (adjacency matrix) paired with multiple realizations of node features and corresponding targets (graph ensemble setting), such as gene expression snapshots on a gene regulatory network or daily traffic counts on a web link graph. It produces per-node regression predictions (or classification scores) and, as a byproduct, learned continuous edge directions parameterized as phase angles θ_ij ∈ [0, π/2] for every edge, where θ=0 is purely directed j→i, θ=π/4 is undirected, and θ=π/2 is purely directed i→j. The edge directions are differentiable parameters optimized jointly with all GNN weights via gradient descent.

## Method

CoED GNN assigns each edge a learnable phase angle θ_ij and constructs a complex-valued fuzzy Laplacian L_F whose (i,j) entry is exp(iθ_ij) when an edge exists. The real part of L_F encodes in-neighbor edges scaled by cos(θ_ij) and the imaginary part encodes out-neighbor edges scaled by sin(θ_ij), guaranteeing that in- and out-neighbor messages are cleanly separated by construction. At each layer, the architecture computes in- and out-neighbor aggregated messages via asymmetrically normalized propagation matrices P_← and P_→, applies separate learnable weight matrices to each directional message and to the self-features, sums them with a bias, and passes through a nonlinear activation. Both the phase matrix Θ and all weight matrices are updated end-to-end via gradient-based optimization, allowing the model to discover the optimal continuous edge directions that enable long-range directional information flow. The authors prove that this fuzzy Laplacian is more expressive than the magnetic Laplacian and that CoED GNN matches the expressivity of a weak Weisfeiler-Leman isomorphism test for directed graphs with fuzzy edges.

## Results

On real graph ensemble datasets (Table 3), CoED achieves the lowest test loss across all three benchmarks: 3.56×10¹ on Perturb-seq (vs. 3.85×10¹ for GAT), 5.76×10⁻¹ on WikiMath web traffic (vs. 6.00×10⁻¹ for GAT), and 2.91×10⁻³ on the power grid OPF task (vs. 3.28×10⁻³ for DirGAT and 28.56×10⁻³ for GCN). On synthetic datasets (Table 2), CoED reduces test loss by 5–14× over the next-best method: 1.36×10⁻³ on the triangular lattice (vs. 7.54×10⁻³ for FLODE) and 5.02×10⁻³ on the GRN dynamics (vs. 70.31×10⁻³ for FLODE). On the 11-dataset node classification benchmark (Table 1, no edge direction learning), CoED ranks in the top three on most datasets, achieving 92.17% on Roman-Empire and 47.27% on Chameleon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoED outperforms all 12 baselines on the graph ensemble regression tasks (Tables 2–3), which are the paper's primary contribution. Against GCN and SAGE (undirected message passing), CoED gains are largest on directed graphs: on the power grid, CoED (2.91×10⁻³) beats GCN (28.56×10⁻³) by nearly 10×. Against attention-based models GAT and GraphGPS, CoED wins on all three real datasets and both synthetic datasets, with the gap widening on directed structures (e.g., 5.02 vs. 12.07×10⁻³ on GRN for CoED vs. GAT). Against directionality-aware models MagNet and DirGNN/DirGCN/DirGAT, CoED consistently achieves lower loss, with the advantage most pronounced on the power grid (2.91 vs. 3.28×10⁻³ for DirGAT) and the synthetic GRN (5.02 vs. 43.42×10⁻³ for MagNet). Against higher-order models FLODE and DRew, CoED achieves 5–14× lower test loss on synthetic data (1.36 vs. 7.54×10⁻³ on lattice; 5.02 vs. 70.31×10⁻³ on GRN). On the node classification benchmark (Table 1, no edge learning), CoED is competitive but not uniformly best: it leads on Roman-Empire (92.17%) and Chameleon (47.27%) but trails DirGNN on SNAP-Patents (74.67 vs. 73.95%) and Co-GNN on AM-Photo (95.83 vs. 97.44%), confirming that the edge-direction learning advantage is specific to the graph ensemble setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of directed-graph GNNs initiated by DirGNN (Rossi et al., 2024), which first showed that separately processing in-neighbor and out-neighbor messages improves performance on heterophilic graphs. CoED generalizes this by replacing discrete edge directions with continuous 'fuzzy' directions parameterized by phase angles, and introduces a novel complex-valued fuzzy Laplacian that provably separates in- and out-neighbor messages more cleanly than the magnetic Laplacian used by MagNet. Conceptually, the paper departs from attention-based methods (GAT, GraphGPS) by treating edge directions as independent learnable parameters optimized end-to-end, rather than as functions of node features that change with each forward pass. The graph ensemble framing—fixed topology with multiple feature realizations—positions the method for domains like gene regulatory networks, web traffic, and power grids where this structure is natural.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous edge direction learning offers a principled alternative to attention for controlling information flow on graphs, with the practical advantage that directions are feature-independent and thus stable across different feature realizations on the same graph. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that learning edge directions can overfit on standard node classification benchmarks where the graph itself is split for train/test, making the method most impactful specifically in the graph ensemble setting. Open questions include how the approach scales to very large graphs (the Laplacian is dense in general), whether it can be extended to dynamic or evolving graph structures, and whether the expressivity advantage over the magnetic Laplacian translates to gains beyond the graph ensemble regime. The theoretical contribution—matching a generalized WL test for fuzzy directed graphs—provides a clear expressivity ceiling but leaves open whether this ceiling is practically reachable at depth.
