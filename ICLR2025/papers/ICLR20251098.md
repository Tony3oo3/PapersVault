## Problem

DPO and its variants suffer from training instability and hyperparameter sensitivity, particularly on mathematical datasets where highly similar preference pairs make the pairwise contrastive constraint ineffective. The paper identifies the root cause as the unidirectional negative feedback in the log-likelihood loss: the gradient naturally dampens when increasing preferred-sample likelihood but amplifies when decreasing dispreferred-sample likelihood, leading to model collapse. Existing fixes (NLL regularization, pair filtering) add hyperparameters or discard data without addressing this fundamental asymmetry.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts paired with a single preferred or dispreferred response (no pairwise matching required, similar to KTO) along with a frozen reference model π_ref. It produces an aligned policy model π_θ that generates text responses. The loss is a length-normalized cross-entropy over the full vocabulary at each token position, where the target distribution is dynamically computed from the ratio of policy to reference model probabilities, making it a pointwise (non-contrastive) objective that operates on individual samples rather than pairs.

## Method

BNF replaces the standard one-hot target in cross-entropy with a Dynamic Target Distribution (DTD): for the target token y_i, the target probability is clamped at min(π_θ(y_i|x,y_<i)/π_ref(y_i|x,y_<i), 1), and the remaining mass is distributed proportionally to the policy model's probabilities over all other tokens. This design ensures the gradient magnitude |∂L/∂z| peaks exactly at the reference model's likelihood and decreases linearly in both directions—whether the policy likelihood rises above or falls below π_ref—establishing a true bidirectional negative feedback. The label(y) ∈ {+1, −1} sign flips the loss direction for preferred vs. dispreferred samples, while the DTD automatically attenuates the gradient for tokens whose likelihood has already moved far from the reference, preventing the runaway decrease that causes DPO collapse. Because the feedback is built into the loss geometry itself, BNF requires no pairwise contrastive term, no scaling factor β, no margin γ, and no NLL regularization weight λ, reducing the alignment pipeline to a single cross-entropy-style forward pass with a reference model.

## Results

On Gemma-2-9B-it, BNF achieves the best Wild-Bench Elo of 1186, surpassing DPO (1181), SimPO (1181), and even larger models like Gemma-2-27B-it (1183) and Nemotron-4-340B-it (1184). Across all three base models (Meta-Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B), BNF achieves the best average rank (3.13, 3.25, 1.50 respectively), indicating the best overall balance between QA performance and reasoning preservation. On Meta-Llama-3, BNF scores 1153 Elo on Wild-Bench (2nd to DPO's 1155) while maintaining GSM8K accuracy of 77.0% vs. SimPO's 56.6%, demonstrating significantly lower alignment tax.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BNF achieves the best average rank across all three base models, indicating the strongest overall balance between QA and reasoning. On QA benchmarks, BNF is competitive with the best baselines: on Gemma-2-9B-it it outperforms both DPO and SimPO (Elo 1186 vs. 1181), while on Meta-Llama-3 it trails DPO by only 2 Elo points (1153 vs. 1155). The key advantage of BNF is its dramatically lower alignment tax: on Meta-Llama-3, SimPO drops GSM8K from 78.5% to 56.6% (−21.9 pp) and MMLU from 62.4% to 55.0% (−7.4 pp), whereas BNF only drops GSM8K to 77.0% (−1.5 pp) and MMLU to 61.4% (−1.0 pp). Compared to KTO, which preserves reasoning best (GSM8K 78.8%, MMLU 63.7%), BNF sacrifices minimal reasoning (GSM8K 77.0%, MMLU 61.4%) while gaining substantially on QA (Elo 1153 vs. 1139, Arena-Hard LC 54.8% vs. 45.4%). Against SLiC-HF and CPO, BNF dominates on both QA (Elo 1153 vs. 1142/1136) and reasoning (GSM8K 77.0% vs. 66.6%/79.2%), confirming that NLL-regularized DPO variants remain inferior to the bidirectional feedback approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and its variants (SimPO, IPO, KTO, SLiC-HF, CPO), which all introduce pairwise contrastive losses to prevent excessive decrease in dispreferred-sample likelihood. The key conceptual departure is a mechanistic diagnosis: the authors identify the root cause of DPO's instability as the *unidirectional* likelihood-derivative negative feedback inherent in the log-likelihood loss, rather than treating it as a hyperparameter-tuning problem. Their BNF loss replaces the contrastive mechanism with a Dynamic Target Distribution that creates *bidirectional* negative feedback, reducing preference optimization to a length-normalized cross-entropy loss with no extra hyperparameters and no pairwise data requirement—positioning it closer to KTO's non-pairwise formulation but with a fundamentally different stabilization mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean theoretical explanation for a well-known practical failure mode (DPO collapse on math datasets) and a simple fix that eliminates the need for scaling factors, margins, or regularization weights that plague DPO-series tuning. The finding that BNF preserves reasoning ability (lowest alignment tax) while matching top QA performance across three model families is practically valuable for alignment pipelines. However, open questions remain: all experiments are limited to 7B–9B models, the non-pairwise evaluation uses simulated masking rather than genuinely unpaired data, and the authors acknowledge that combining BNF with contrastive terms could further improve performance—suggesting the current formulation may not be the final word.
