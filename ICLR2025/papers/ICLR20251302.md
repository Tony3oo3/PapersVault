## Problem

As cloud-hosted LLMs undergo periodic updates, downstream users who have already invested in fine-tuning older versions for domain-specific tasks face the impractical burden of re-fine-tuning on every new release, especially when compute resources are limited and fine-tuning datasets are restricted (e.g., healthcare, finance). The paper asks: how can personalized knowledge captured in an earlier fine-tuned model be transferred to any evolving LLM without retraining?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251302/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three components: the original pretrained LLM θ, the evolved LLM θ' (obtained via continued pretraining with either LoRA or full-weight updates), and a LoRA adapter Δθ_i extracted from fine-tuning θ on a specific downstream task. The output is a single merged model θ' + Δθ_i that requires zero trainable parameters at inference time and is evaluated zero-shot on seven downstream tasks (BoolQ, SST-2, MRPC, RTE, WinoGrande, WNLI, GSM8K). The LoRA patch is a low-rank matrix pair (A ∈ R^{r×d}, B ∈ R^{d×r}, r=8) applied to all attention and feed-forward layers, and the merge is a simple additive operation on the weight matrices.

## Method

PORTLLM's core insight is that the residual R = Δθ_i' − Δθ_i (the difference between the ideal new-task patch and the old-task patch) is negligible relative to the naive update θ' + Δθ_i, because both patches are low-rank (r≈8) while the pretrained model is effectively full-rank. The paper provides a formal proof (Lemma 1) bounding the error ratio ‖R‖²_F / ‖θ'+Δθ_i‖²_F by (r₂/r₀)·ν, where r₂ ≪ r₀, and empirically confirms this with σ_max ratios of ~66× and Frobenius norm ratios of ~136×. Practically, the user fine-tunes the old model θ once with LoRA to obtain Δθ_i, then when a new model θ' is released, simply adds Δθ_i to θ' in a single merge operation—no gradient steps, no training data access, and no additional compute beyond loading the model. This yields comparable or superior zero-shot performance to full LoRA fine-tuning of θ' while reducing GPU memory by up to 12.2× and eliminating all trainable parameters.

## Results

PORTLLM (θ′ + Δθᵢ) achieves zero-shot performance comparable to LoRA fine-tuning across all seven downstream tasks while requiring zero trainable parameters. On Mistral-7B with OpenOrca continued pretraining, PORTLLM reaches 90.24% on BoolQ, 96.10% on SST-2, 88.73/92.10 (Acc/F1) on MRPC, 89.17% on RTE, 85.01% on WinoGrande, 83.10% on WNLI, and 41.32% on GSM8K. In efficiency terms, the merge operation uses 28.71 GB GPU memory (vs. 350.61 GB for LoRA fine-tuning, a 12.2× reduction) and 0.0083 GPU hours (vs. 40.65, a 4897× reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PORTLLM is compared against four baselines: the pretrained model θ, the continued-pretrained model θ′, the LoRA fine-tuned model θᵢ, and the LoRA fine-tuned updated model θᵢ′. Against the updated model θ′, PORTLLM wins on all seven tasks with gains ranging from +2.78% (BoolQ) to +26.16% (GSM8K), confirming that the model patch successfully transfers task-specific knowledge. Against the fine-tuned model θᵢ, PORTLLM is within 0.77% on most tasks but actually outperforms it on SST-2 (+0.11%), RTE (+1.44%), and GSM8K (+7.28%), suggesting the patch can leverage continued-pretraining benefits. Against the fine-tuned updated model θᵢ′ (the strongest baseline requiring full re-fine-tuning), PORTLLM is within 1.04% on most tasks and outperforms it on WNLI (+1.02%) and GSM8K (+6.37%), all while being completely training-free. The key practical advantage is that PORTLLM requires zero trainable parameters and a simple merge operation, eliminating the need for repeated fine-tuning at each model update.

## Relation to Prior Work

PORTLLM builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), repurposing its low-rank adapter structure not as a training mechanism but as a portable, training-free knowledge carrier across model versions. While prior PEFT work (adapters, prompt tuning, Q-LoRA) focuses on reducing fine-tuning cost for a single model, and model merging literature addresses combining weights from independently trained models, this paper is the first to formalize the problem of transferring a fine-tuned LoRA patch to an evolved base model and provide a theoretical justification (rank-disparity argument) for why the residual is negligible. It sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning, model merging, and continual adaptation, but departs from all three by requiring zero training at the transfer step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: organizations that invest in domain-specific fine-tuning (healthcare, legal, finance) can avoid costly re-fine-tuning at every LLM release, with up to 12.2× GPU memory reduction and ~4900× GPU hours savings. However, the framework's generality is still bounded: it is validated on 7–9B models, assumes the personalization rank is much smaller than the continued-pretraining rank, and cross-architecture portability (e.g., Llama→Mistral) is explicitly left as future work. Open questions include whether the approach degrades under very different pretraining distributions, whether it extends to non-LoRA PEFT methods (adapters, prefix-tuning), and how it behaves at the 70B+ scale where rank assumptions may shift.
