## Problem

Causal Representation Learning (CRL) methods like discrepancy-VAE can identify latent causal factors in Perturb-seq data with strong identifiability guarantees, but the resulting latent factors are opaque and cannot be reconciled with known biological processes. Prior attempts to add interpretability—post-hoc pathway enrichment or arbitrary gene selection—fail to integrate biological knowledge into the learning process itself, leaving models that are either interpretable or principled but not both.

## Input / Output

The model consumes single-cell gene expression profiles (5,000 most variable genes), binary perturbation labels (single- and double-gene CRISPR knock-outs), and a curated set of Gene Ontology biological process gene sets (filtered to <30 genes, non-redundant, with ≥5 genes present in the data). It produces latent causal factors (pathway archetypes) each expressed as a linear combination of pathway activities, a directed acyclic causal graph with learned edge weights, and predicted gene expression distributions for unseen double-gene perturbations. The encoder's first layer outputs per-pathway activity scores α, and the second layer outputs meta-pathway activities Z that serve as exogenous variables for the causal latent factors U.

## Method

The core contribution is the SENA-δ encoder, a two-layer masked MLP that replaces the fully-connected MLP encoder in discrepancy-VAE. The first (SENA) layer computes pathway activity levels as α = σ((W ⊙ M)ᵀx), where M is a binary mask encoding gene-to-BP membership and a tunable hyperparameter λ allows non-member genes to contribute residual signal, balancing strict interpretability against representational capacity. The second layer generates VAE parameters μ_j = αᵀδ_j^(μ) and σ²_j = αᵀδ_j^(σ), so each latent factor is a learned linear combination of pathway activities (a 'meta-pathway activity'), reconciling the biological reality that a single gene KO perturbs multiple pathways with the CRL identifiability requirement that each intervention targets exactly one latent factor. The SENA-δ encoder is dropped into the discrepancy-VAE architecture with no other modifications; the encoder and polynomial decoder train on unperturbed cells while perturbed samples drive the MMD-based discrepancy loss. Ablation studies show λ ∈ {0, 0.1} preserves biologically meaningful latent factors (measured via differential activation and Hits@100) while maintaining reconstruction and generative performance comparable to the unmasked baseline.

## Results

On the Norman2019 dataset (131 unseen double-gene perturbations), SENA-discrepancy-VAE with λ=0.1 achieves MMD of 1.585±0.011 at 105 latent dimensions, slightly outperforming the original discrepancy-VAE (1.598±0.012), and also improves MSE (0.02134 vs. 0.02152) and KLD (0.00019 vs. 0.00022). At 35 latent dimensions, SENA-δ λ=0.1 further reduces MMD to 1.950 vs. 1.990 for the MLP encoder. GEARS, a state-of-the-art non-causal perturbation predictor, performs substantially worse with MMD values of 12.6–15.3 across all latent dimensions, roughly 8× higher than either discrepancy-VAE variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original discrepancy-VAE (MLP encoder), SENA-discrepancy-VAE with λ=0.1 achieves comparable or slightly better MMD and MSE on unseen double perturbations at 105 and 35 latent dimensions (e.g., MMD 1.585 vs. 1.598 at d=105; 1.950 vs. 1.990 at d=35), while consistently improving the variational KLD loss (0.00019 vs. 0.00022 at d=105) and the causal graph sparsity L1 (0.052 vs. 0.061 at d=105). At lower latent dimensions (d=10, 5), the original MLP encoder retains a small MMD advantage, and the fully masked variant (λ=0) degrades noticeably (MMD 1.746 vs. 1.598 at d=105), indicating a reconstruction–interpretability trade-off. Against GEARS, both discrepancy-VAE variants dominate by a wide margin on MMD (≈1.6–2.9 vs. ≈12.6–15.3), though GEARS does not provide a causal graph or generative model. On the Wessels2023 dataset (Appendix V), SENA-discrepancy-VAE matches the original MLP only at higher λ values (λ=0.5), suggesting that the more homogeneous perturbation effects in that dataset make the interpretability constraint harder to satisfy without sacrificing predictive accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrepancy-VAE framework (Zhang et al., 2024) by replacing its fully-connected MLP encoder with a pathway-masked encoder (SENA-δ) that incorporates Gene Ontology biological processes as architectural prior knowledge. It departs from prior CRL applications to Perturb-seq data (Lopez et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024), which produced latent factors that could not be reconciled with known biology, and from prior interpretable VAE approaches for single-cell data (Seninge et al., 2021; Gut et al., 2021; Lotfollahi et al., 2023), which lacked causal identifiability guarantees. The SENA layer itself follows the NetActivity architecture (Ruiz-Arenas et al., 2024), which maps gene expressions to pathway activity scores through a masked linear layer, here repurposed as the first layer of a causal encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to simultaneously achieve principled causal identifiability and biological interpretability in CRL for Perturb-seq data, opening a path toward causal models whose latent factors can be directly inspected and validated against known pathway biology. A key limitation is the inherited assumption that each intervention targets a single latent factor, which forces overlapping biological processes into the same factor and limits fine-grained disentanglement; the authors explicitly flag relaxing this as future work. The Wessels2023 results also reveal that interpretability comes at a predictive cost when perturbations produce similar transcriptomic effects, suggesting the approach is most powerful when perturbations are mechanistically diverse. Broader implications include the potential to use pathway-level causal graphs as hypotheses for experimental validation in drug discovery and disease mechanism studies.
