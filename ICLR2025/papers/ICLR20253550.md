## Problem

FP8 training of LLMs was previously validated only up to 100 billion tokens, leaving open whether it remains stable at the multi-trillion-token scale required for real-world pretraining. When scaled to 2 trillion tokens, FP8 training exhibits a dramatic loss divergence around 200B tokens caused by outlier amplification in the SwiGLU activation — a phenomenon invisible in shorter runs and previously unexplained.

## Input / Output

The method trains a 7B-parameter Llama2 decoder-only Transformer on the Red Pajama text corpus (up to 2T tokens) using FP8 precision: E4M3 for forward-pass weights/activations and E5M2 for backward-pass gradients, with delayed per-tensor scaling. The per-channel Smooth-SwiGLU scaling factors are computed in parallel during training and can be absorbed into adjacent linear-layer weights at inference, yielding zero additional inference cost. Both Adam optimizer moments are stored in FP8 (E4M3 for the first moment, E5M2 for the second), reducing optimizer memory by ~30% compared to FP32.

## Method

The paper identifies that L2 regularization drives the two SwiGLU weight vectors (w₁, w₂) to align (w₁→±w₂) once input magnitudes exceed a threshold, making the activation quadratic in the input and amplifying outliers beyond FP8's limited dynamic range. To fix this, Smooth-SwiGLU applies a per-channel scaling factor sᵢ to the linear branch before quantization and rescales by sᵢ⁻¹ after the final linear layer, preserving the exact function while keeping intermediate values within FP8 range. Additionally, the paper demonstrates the first successful FP8 quantization of both Adam moments, showing that the second moment specifically requires the wider E5M2 range due to the inverse-square-root operation in the update rule. Together, these changes enable stable 2T-token FP8 training on 256 Intel Gaudi2 accelerators with ~34% throughput improvement over BF16 and on-par zero-shot performance.

## Results

On zero-shot downstream tasks, the proposed FP8 method (Smooth-SwiGLU + FP8 Adam moments) achieves on-par or slightly better accuracy than the BF16 Llama2 7B baseline: 62.1% on Lambada (vs. 61.98%), 68.37% on HellaSwag (vs. 68.3%), 65.43% on Winogrande (vs. 64.25%), and 37.8% on Arc-C (vs. 37.37%), with Wikitext perplexity of 5.55 (vs. 5.59). In terms of throughput on Intel Gaudi2, the Smooth-SwiGLU configuration delivers a ~34% speedup (16.89 vs. 12.65 samples/sec, 417 vs. 311 TFLOPS) over BF16. The FP8 optimizer further reduces per-device memory from 63.25 GB to 44.08 GB (~30% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the BF16 Llama2 7B model trained on the same Red Pajama dataset (2T tokens) with identical hyperparameters. The proposed FP8(2) configuration (Smooth-SwiGLU + FP8 Adam moments) matches or marginally exceeds the BF16 baseline on every accuracy metric: +0.12 pp on Lambada, +0.07 pp on HellaSwag, +1.18 pp on Winogrande, and +0.43 pp on Arc-C, while achieving a slightly lower Wikitext perplexity (5.55 vs. 5.59). The Lambada perplexity is marginally higher (5.90 vs. 5.75), but this is within noise. Critically, the FP8(2) method delivers a ~34% throughput improvement (16.89 vs. 12.65 samples/sec) and ~30% memory reduction (44.08 vs. 63.25 GB/HPU) over the BF16 baseline, making it strictly superior in efficiency while maintaining quality parity. The intermediate FP8(1) variant (disabling quantization only on SwiGLU output) is slightly worse than BF16 on Lambada and HellaSwag but still converges, confirming that the SwiGLU outlier amplification is the sole source of instability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Peng et al. (2023), who first demonstrated FP8 LLM training up to 100B tokens, by scaling to 2T tokens (20×) and revealing instabilities invisible at the shorter scale. It departs from prior FP8 work by providing a theoretical explanation for the instability—proving that SwiGLU's two weight vectors must align under L2 regularization when inputs are large—and by introducing Smooth-SwiGLU, a per-channel rescaling of the SwiGLU activation (Shazeer, 2020) that preserves function behavior while preventing outlier overflow. Additionally, it extends the optimizer quantization line by quantizing both Adam moments to FP8 (E4M3 for the first, E5M2 for the second), whereas Peng et al. retained the second moment in FP16.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies a previously unknown, scale-dependent failure mode in FP8 training that would silently corrupt any large-scale run using SwiGLU-based architectures (Llama, PaLM, etc.), making it critical for practitioners planning multi-trillion-token training. The Smooth-SwiGLU fix is architecturally trivial (a per-channel scalar absorbed into adjacent linear layers at inference) and delivers ~34% throughput gain with on-par quality, making it highly adoptable. Open questions include whether the weight-alignment phenomenon generalizes to other GLU variants or non-SwiGLU activations, whether it interacts with other regularization schemes, and whether the single-model (Llama2-7B), single-dataset (Red Pajama) validation is sufficient to guarantee stability at even larger scales or on different hardware.
