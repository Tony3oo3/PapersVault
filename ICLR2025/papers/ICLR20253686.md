## Problem

Diffusion-based inpainting models are slow because they must degrade the known condition to the same noise level as the generation region, leaving early steps semantically uninformative and forcing expensive resampling or correction mechanisms to realign the trajectory. This paper asks whether the clean condition can be exploited from the very first diffusion step without requiring a dedicated inpainting architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB image and a binary mask of arbitrary shape and size, and produces a complete inpainted image. The known region (mask=1) is fed as a noise-free condition starting at diffusion step 0, while the unknown region (mask=0) begins at maximum noise and is progressively denoised. Evaluation covers six mask protocols (Wide, Narrow, Super-Resolve 2x, Alternating Lines, Half, Expand) across CelebA-HQ, ImageNet1K, and Places2.

## Method

TD-Paint converts the scalar diffusion timestep t into a spatial tensor τ ∈ {0,…,T}^{h×w}, assigning t=0 to known pixels and t=current step to unknown pixels, so that the forward noising and reverse denoising are applied pixel-wise with heterogeneous noise levels. During training, a patch-based strategy samples random patch sizes (powers of two) and proportions to create diverse known/unknown splits, and the U-Net is trained with the standard MSE noise-prediction loss on the pixel-wise noised input. The U-Net's time conditioning is modified to compute a per-pixel sinusoidal embedding Γ_{i,j} and apply scale-shift normalization spatially, requiring no other architectural change. At generation, the clean known region is directly concatenated with the current noisy unknown region at each step (Eq. 9), eliminating RePaint's forward-reverse resampling loop and reducing inference from ~4750 steps to 250 steps while improving LPIPS by 20–30% over RePaint-20.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, TD-Paint achieves LPIPS of 0.055 (Wide), 0.028 (Narrow), 0.017 (Super-Resolve 2x), and 0.010 (Altern. Lines), outperforming RePaint-20 by 18–22% and MCG by 21–79% on these masks. On the challenging Half and Expand masks, TD-Paint achieves the best KID (0.00044 and 0.00710 respectively) across all datasets. In terms of efficiency, TD-Paint completes inpainting in 30.67 s (250 NBS) versus 189.62 s for RePaint-20 (4750 NBS) and 184.59 s for MCG (1000 NBS), a ~6× speedup while delivering superior LPIPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD-Paint outperforms all pixel-space diffusion baselines (RePaint-20, MCG, CoPaint) on LPIPS across every mask type on all three datasets, while running ~6× faster than RePaint-20 and MCG. Against latent-space models, TD-Paint dominates on fine-grained masks (Super-Resolve 2x, Altern. Lines) where LDM and ControlNet collapse (LPIPS > 0.5), but BLD achieves lower LPIPS on Wide/Narrow masks (0.014/0.017 vs. 0.055/0.028) at the cost of poor fine-grained performance. Compared to the CNN-based LaMa, TD-Paint matches on Wide/Narrow but wins by a wide margin on Super-Resolve 2x (0.017 vs. 0.219) and Altern. Lines (0.010 vs. 0.110). Against the transformer-based MAT, TD-Paint similarly dominates on fine-grained masks (0.017 vs. 0.509 on Super-Res 2x). On the hardest Half and Expand masks, TD-Paint achieves the best KID (0.00044 / 0.00710 on CelebA-HQ) among all methods, confirming superior distributional fidelity when large image portions are missing.

## Relation to Prior Work

TD-Paint extends the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and directly departs from RePaint (Lugmayr et al., 2022), which degrades the known condition to the same noise level as the generation region and relies on expensive resampling to synchronize semantics. The key conceptual shift is that TD-Paint keeps the condition clean from the first step and instead assigns a per-pixel time map τ, so the U-Net's existing time-embedding mechanism naturally encodes different noise levels for known vs. unknown regions. This eliminates both RePaint's resampling loop and MCG's correction term, while also avoiding the dedicated inpainting architectures required by latent-space methods like LDM and ControlNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× speedup over the strongest pixel-space diffusion baselines (RePaint-20, MCG, CoPaint) while matching or exceeding their quality makes diffusion inpainting far more practical for real-time or resource-constrained settings, and the fact that no architectural modification is needed lowers the adoption barrier significantly. Limitations include the requirement for fine-tuning (no zero-shot capability), operation in pixel space at 256×256 resolution, and the fact that foundation-model-based latent approaches (BLD) still outperform on some mask types. Open questions include whether pixel-wise time conditioning transfers to latent-space diffusion, scales to higher resolutions, or generalizes to other conditional generation tasks beyond inpainting.
