## Problem

SAM's interactive segmentation framework requires manual prompts (points, boxes, or masks), preventing fully automated image manipulation detection and localization. Additionally, prior work shows that RGB-only features generalize poorly across datasets, as models trained on one dataset (e.g., CASIAv2) degrade significantly on others (e.g., COVER). This paper is the first to adapt SAM to IMD by learning prompts automatically and incorporating semantic-agnostic noise views to improve cross-dataset generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252746/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 1024×1024 RGB image as input and internally derives three additional semantic-agnostic views (SRM, Bayer, and Noiseprint noise maps) from it. It produces two outputs: a pixel-level manipulation localization mask (per-pixel probability map) and an image-level binary manipulation label, where the latter is obtained by aggregating pixel responses above an Otsu-derived threshold. The SAM image encoder is frozen throughout, so only the lightweight FCN segmenters, CFP, PMM, and SAM's prompt/mask decoder components are trained.

## Method

IMDPrompter processes four views (RGB, SRM, Bayer, Noiseprint) through identical MobileNet-based FCN segmenters to produce per-view mask probability maps. An Optimal Prompt Selection (OPS) module picks the best view (or ensemble) by minimizing segmentation loss against ground truth, then converts the selected probability map into mask and bounding-box prompts fed into SAM's prompt encoder. A Cross-View Prompt Consistency (CPC) loss aligns all views' predictions toward the optimal prompt, while a Cross-View Feature Perception (CFP) module fuses multi-view features with SAM's frozen image-encoder features via attention-weighted concatenation. A Prompt Mixing Module (PMM) concatenates the CFP output with the optimal prompt encoding through an MLP to form the final prompt embedding for SAM's mask decoder. Training combines Focal Loss on all four view predictions, CPC loss, SAM decoder segmentation loss, and an image-level BCE loss with adaptive Otsu-based pooling; at inference, OPS defaults to the ensemble prediction since no ground truth is available.

## Results

On pixel-level manipulation localization, IMDPrompter achieves a mean P-F1 of 81.36% under best-threshold evaluation and 59.78% under a fixed 0.5 threshold across five datasets (CASIA, COVER, Columbia, IMD2020, NIST16), outperforming the previous best method MVSS-Net (75.48% / 41.90%) by +5.88 and +17.88 points respectively. On image-level detection, IMDPrompter attains a mean AUC of 0.857 and mean F1 of 76.23%, surpassing MVSS-Net (0.826 / 53.98%) by +0.031 AUC and +22.25 F1. The largest gains appear on out-of-distribution datasets: +26.2 P-F1 on COVER and +28.7 on Columbia (best threshold) over the prior best.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMDPrompter outperforms all 16 compared baselines on nearly every dataset and metric. Against the strongest prior method MVSS-Net, it gains +5.88 P-F1 (best threshold) and +17.88 P-F1 (fixed 0.5 threshold) on average, with the largest single-dataset improvement being +26.2 on COVER (83.5 vs 82.4 best threshold; 63.6 vs 45.3 fixed). TruFor, the most recent SOTA, reports strong results on CASIA (83.5) and Columbia (82.1) but lacks IMD2020 and NIST16 evaluations; IMDPrompter surpasses it on all overlapping datasets (85.1 CASIA, 83.5 COVER, 87.3 Columbia) and additionally reports 76.3 on IMD and 74.6 on NIST. On image-level detection, IMDPrompter's mean F1 of 76.23% exceeds MVSS-Net's 53.98% by 22.25 points, with particularly large specificity gains (e.g., 90.2% vs 100.0% on Columbia, 55.2% vs 14.0% on COVER) that reduce false positives. Compared to SAM-based segmentation methods (MedSAM, AutoSAM, SAMed), IMDPrompter achieves substantially higher C-F1 scores (e.g., 66.8 vs 24.0 on COVER; 90.3 vs 33.0 on Columbia), confirming that cross-view noise features are essential beyond SAM's semantic priors alone. Under quality degradation (JPEG compression, Gaussian blur), IMDPrompter retains 72.5–74.6 P-F1 at JPEG-50 and 58.1–66.6 under 29-pixel Gaussian blur, consistently outperforming MVSS-Net by 10–20 points in the most severe settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SAM's image encoder, prompt encoder, and mask decoder as a frozen backbone, repurposing a general-purpose segmentation model for the specialized task of image manipulation detection. It departs from the multi-view fusion lineage of MVSS-Net and TruFor (which concatenate or attention-fuse RGB and noise features in a single CNN) by instead using four views (RGB, SRM, Bayer, Noiseprint) to *generate prompts* for SAM, with an Optimal Prompt Selection module and Cross-View Prompt Consistency loss replacing simple feature averaging. The automated prompt learning paradigm is conceptually inspired by LLM prompt optimization (Battle & Gollapudi, 2024), transplanted into the visual segmentation domain for the first time in the IMD context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first work to adapt SAM to image manipulation detection, it opens a pathway for leveraging large-scale pre-trained segmentation priors in forensic tasks, potentially extending to newer models like SAM 2 and SEEM. The finding that the Noiseprint view is selected as the optimal prompt 46.3% of the time versus only 13.9% for RGB challenges the intuition that semantic content should dominate manipulation cues, suggesting that camera-sensor noise fingerprints are the most transferable signal across domains. Key limitations include the inability to detect fully AI-generated images and the requirement for complete pixel-level supervision; future work on weakly supervised or semi-supervised prompt learning, and on generative-manipulation detection, remains open.
