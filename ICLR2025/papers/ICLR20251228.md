## Problem

Diffusion models frequently fail to adhere to positional instructions in prompts, yet the role of initial Gaussian noise in determining where objects appear has remained largely unexplored. This paper asks: can we identify specific regions in the initial noise that causally drive object placement, and can we detect them before running the diffusion process?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251228/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The trigger patch detector takes a 4×64×64 Gaussian noise latent (the initial noise fed to Stable Diffusion V2) and outputs bounding boxes marking trigger patch locations in that latent space. For the generation applications, the method either rejects and resamples noise regions flagged by the detector (to purify noise for diversity) or resamples until a detected trigger patch aligns with the prompt's positional instruction (for controlled placement). Trigger patches are 24×24 latent-space regions, and the detector is trained on 20,000 noise samples annotated with bounding boxes extracted from 500,000 generated images across 5 COCO classes and 25 prompts.

## Method

The paper identifies 'trigger patches'—outlier regions in Gaussian noise that consistently induce object generation at their location regardless of prompt content. A posterior metric called trigger entropy (variance of detected bounding-box centers across 25 prompts per noise) quantifies patch effectiveness, and an energy-based two-sample test confirms trigger patches follow a distribution significantly different from random noise patches (p-value 0.0 vs. 0.938 for random pairs). To detect patches without running diffusion, the authors train an RTMDet-based detector (ResNet-101 with 4× width expansion, Generalized Focal Loss) on 4-channel noise input, achieving mAP50 of 0.333 versus 0.201 for a shuffled-annotation baseline, confirming the model learns genuine noise-to-position mappings rather than dataset bias. The detector is prompt-agnostic (Class-Specific variant drops to mAP50 0.091) and generalizes across schedulers, aspect ratios, SDXL, LoRA adapters, and SD3/DiT. Two applications follow: reject-sampling to remove trigger patches boosts positional entropy from 135.97 to 171.84, and aligning trigger patches with prompt instructions raises guidance success rate from 57.08% to 83.64%.

## Results

The trigger patch detector achieves an mAP50 of 0.333 on the validation set, surpassing the shuffled-annotation baseline (0.201) by 0.124, confirming it learns genuine noise-to-position mappings. In the prompt-following application, reject-sampling with the detector boosts the Guidance Success Rate (GSR) from 57.08% (control) to 83.64%, a 26.56-point improvement. In the location-diversity application, removing trigger patches raises positional entropy from 135.97 (control) to 171.84, approaching the pure-random upper bound of 170.64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method (trigger-patch reject sampling) outperforms all baselines except Attention Refocusing on the GSR metric: it beats Control (57.08%) by 26.56 points, Attend-and-Excite (64.52%) by 19.12 points, Structured Diffusion (77.41%) by 6.23 points, and Random (61.08%) by 22.56 points. Attention Refocusing (89.83%) remains the strongest baseline, exceeding the proposed method by 6.19 points, but requires GPT-4 for layout generation and takes ~15 s per image versus ~5 s for the proposed approach. On the location-diversity task (Table 5), the method achieves an entropy of 171.84, surpassing Initno (139.89), Attend-and-Excite (145.16), Attention Refocusing (102.10), and Structured Diffusion (133.92), and marginally exceeding the Random baseline (170.64). For multi-object generation (Table 12), the method attains the best CLIP-Full (0.360), CLIP-Min (0.264), and PickScore (0.557) among all compared approaches including Composable Diffusion, Structure Diffusion, Attend-and-Excite, and Divide-and-Bind.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work studying the role of initial noise in diffusion models (Mao et al. 2023, Sun et al. 2024, Guo et al. 2024), which had qualitatively suggested positional information in noise through cross-attention analysis. It departs from that cross-attention-centric perspective by showing that specific noise patches are statistical outliers that can be identified directly from the noise without running the diffusion process, and repurposes an object detector (RTMDet) to perform detection in the noise space. The broader task of positional control in text-to-image generation (Attend-and-Excite, Structured Diffusion, Attention Refocusing) is addressed here from a fundamentally different angle: manipulating the input noise rather than intervening on attention maps during generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that trigger patches are universal, prompt-agnostic outliers provides a mechanistic explanation for the well-known positional bias in diffusion models and opens a model-agnostic, prompt-agnostic control lever that operates entirely before generation. However, the evidence is constrained to five COCO classes and 20K noises, the detector achieves only mAP50 of 0.333, and the hand-crafted patch results (shifted Gaussian, sine function) cause visible image distortion at high effectiveness. Open questions include whether trigger patches generalize to open-vocabulary scenes, whether the phenomenon persists in flow-matching or autoregressive architectures, and whether the modest detector performance can be improved with larger-scale training to enable reliable real-time deployment.
