## Problem

MLLM training data expansion currently relies on fixed heuristics, random thresholds, or narrow evaluation dimensions that do not adapt to the model's evolving state. As datasets grow through version iterations, the definition of 'high-quality' data shifts with the existing distribution and model performance, yet no systematic framework exists to dynamically evaluate, select, and generate targeted data that breaks the scaling law at moderate scales (≤260k).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a base MLLM checkpoint (LLaVA-1.5 with SigLIP encoder and Llama3.1-8B), an initial 10k-sample instruction dataset, and candidate batches of 1k image-text pairs drawn from a ~3.5M-sample pool (ShareGPT4V, LLaVA-1.5/NeXT, ALLaVA, Cambrian-10M) or generated from unlabeled visual cues. It produces an expanded training set up to 260k samples, a learned MLLM-Scorer that outputs binary quality judgments, dynamic quality distribution rules (π*) expressed as interpretable 50+ dimensional vectors, and newly generated visual instructions targeting identified distribution gaps. The evaluation itself requires no backward passes—only forward inference through the model zoo—making it roughly 1/50 the cost of one training step.

## Method

ZOOPROBE formalizes dataset expansion as A* search on a tree of candidate batches, where the quality function Q(d_j; M, s) = r(d_j; M) + γ·h(d_j; π*(s)) combines a reward-so-far term (benchmark performance plus convergence speed) with a heuristic estimating future training quality. The heuristic is grounded in a 50+ dimensional description vector Π = concat{Π_meta, Π_intrinsic} extracted by a small-scale model zoo (GTE-Qwen2 for textual meta-properties, E5-V for multimodal meta-properties, CLIP+SAM for object detection, Depth Anything V2 for depth, plus intrinsic attributes like resolution and token length). Quality rules π* are explored dynamically every 50 expansion steps by perturbing a uniform distribution, fine-tuning on 10k probe sets (full-parameter for <200k params, LoRA otherwise), and selecting via τ-softmax over probe rewards, with LEEP transferability as a cheap proxy. The evolving stage uses identified rules to construct macro task prompts that guide an additional MLLM to generate targeted instructions filling distribution gaps, closing the E3 loop without manual prompt engineering.

## Results

ZOOPROBE significantly outperforms random sampling across all six benchmarks (MMMU, MMStar, RealWorldQA, AI2D, ScienceQA, SEEDBench) for both LLaVA and Wings architectures, breaking the standard scaling law from 10k to 260k training samples. Notably, at 260k scale (roughly one-third of LLaVA-1.5's full training volume), ZOOPROBE achieves near-equivalent performance on AI2D, demonstrating substantial data efficiency. The learned MLLM-Scorer, introduced after the 110k mark, performs at a comparable level to the full ZOOPROBE pipeline, validating its effectiveness as a lightweight quality estimator. Ablations confirm that dynamic quality rules consistently outperform both fixed diverse-distribution strategies and opposite (anti-quality) rules across all scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is random sampling from the same candidate pool (ShareGPT4V, LLaVA-1.5-finetune, LLaVA-NeXT, ALLaVA-finetune, Cambrian-10M, filtered to ~3.5M samples). ZOOPROBE wins decisively over random sampling on every benchmark for both LLaVA and Wings, with the gap widening as data scale increases beyond 110k. In ablation comparisons, a fixed diverse-distribution rule (uniform KL-divergence-based sampling) outperforms random sampling but remains consistently below ZOOPROBE's dynamic rule, confirming that data quality requirements shift with model state. An 'opposite quality' rule (deliberately selecting low-quality data) causes clear performance degradation, validating the directionality of the heuristic. Partial evaluation (half or quarter of the 50+ dimensions) still beats random sampling, though with some performance decline, indicating robustness to resource constraints. The paper does not include head-to-head comparisons against other named data-selection methods (e.g., DoReMi, DoGE, AlpaGasus), limiting direct cross-method benchmarking.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZOOPROBE synthesizes several threads of prior work into a unified data-engine loop: (1) data pruning and selection via intrinsic metrics (Cao et al., Lu et al.), (2) data-mixture optimization via surrogate models (DoReMi, DoGE), and (3) dynamic, model-state-dependent quality rules (Biderman et al., Swayamdipta et al.). It departs from these by formalizing dataset expansion as an A* search over a tree of candidate batches, where the heuristic is a KL-divergence-based diversity estimate conditioned on a dynamically explored quality rule π* rather than a fixed threshold. The model-zoo evaluation (50+ dimensions via CLIP, SAM, E5-V, GTE-Qwen2, Depth Anything V2) extends prior single-scorer approaches to a multi-perspective, interpretable description vector, and the E3 loop closes the cycle by feeding discovered rules back into targeted data generation via macro-task prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The E3 loop offers a practical, automatable pipeline for MLLM data engineering that reduces reliance on manual prompt engineering and fixed heuristics, and the learned MLLM-Scorer provides a lightweight quality proxy for future iterations. However, the 'breaking the scaling law' claim is confined to the 10k–260k regime and is benchmarked only against random sampling, not against other data-selection or mixture-optimization methods, leaving open whether the advantage persists at the multi-million-sample scales where MLLMs are actually trained. The paper also flags safety of generated data, transferability across model scales, and the cost of the probe-based exploration (≈1/20 of a training step) as unresolved practical concerns.
