## Problem

Diffusion and flow-matching models for time series rely on simple isotropic Gaussian priors that bear little resemblance to the temporal structure of real data, forcing the generative process to learn long, complex transformation paths. Conditional Flow Matching (CFM), which supports arbitrary priors and straighter probability paths via optimal transport, had not yet been applied to time series forecasting. The paper asks whether incorporating domain-informed priors—specifically Gaussian Processes—can simplify the generative problem and improve both unconditional synthesis and conditional probabilistic forecasting.

## Input / Output

The conditional model receives the observed past segment y^p (context length equal to prediction length, e.g., 24 steps for hourly data) plus a binary observation mask indicating which timesteps are observed, and produces 100 stochastic forecast samples from which a full predictive distribution is estimated. The unconditional model takes no conditioning input during training but, at inference, accepts y^p to drive Langevin-based prior sampling and guidance. All series are normalized by the global training-set mean, and the model operates on two orthogonal time axes: the flow-matching parameter t ∈ [0,1] governing the generative path, and the temporal index τ indexing positions within the series.

## Method

TSFlow replaces the standard isotropic Gaussian prior in CFM with a Gaussian Process prior GP(0, K) using one of three kernels (squared exponential, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck, or periodic), chosen to match the data's temporal character. During training, mini-batch optimal transport pairs GP prior samples with data samples, and the network (a DiffWave-style architecture with three S4 residual blocks, ~176K parameters) regresses the conditional vector field along the resulting probability paths. For conditional forecasting, the prior is further conditioned on y^p via Gaussian Process Regression: the past component is fixed to the observation (delta function) and the future component is a closed-form Gaussian with mean and covariance from the kernel, so the model is trained directly on the conditional prior q_0(x_0 | y^p). An alternative inference-only route conditions an unconditionally trained model by running Langevin dynamics on the prior score (using an asymmetric Laplace guidance term) and modifying the ODE vector field with a scaled guidance score, eliminating the need for separate conditional training.

## Results

On probabilistic forecasting (CRPS, lower is better), TSFlow-Cond. achieves the best score on 6 out of 8 datasets, with up to 14% improvement over the second-best method. The conditional TSFlow variants (using GP regression priors) outperform or match all four diffusion-based baselines (CSDI, SSSD, Biloš et al., TSDiff) on 7/8 datasets while requiring fewer NFEs (4 vs. 100 for TSDiff). For unconditional generation (2-Wasserstein distance), TSFlow with GP priors at 4 NFEs is competitive with the isotropic prior at 16 NFEs, and consistently outperforms TimeVAE and TSDiff across most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSFlow-Cond. (with GP regression priors) achieves the best CRPS on 6/8 datasets, beating the strongest diffusion-based baseline SSSD on KDDCup (0.278 vs. 0.274), Solar (0.339 vs. 0.384), and Traffic (0.082 vs. 0.097), and outperforming TSDiff on all 8 datasets. Against neural forecasting baselines, TSFlow-Cond. beats DeepAR on 7/8 datasets (e.g., Traffic: 0.082 vs. 0.103; KDDCup: 0.278 vs. 0.362) and WaveNet on 7/8 (Traffic: 0.082 vs. 0.099). The unconditional TSFlow variant (using conditional prior sampling + guidance at inference) remains competitive with TSDiff and SSSD but is consistently 5–10% worse than the conditionally trained version, confirming that direct conditional training is more effective. On the Exchange dataset, TSFlow ties with AutoARIMA and AutoETS at CRPS 0.008, and on Wiki2000, TSFlow-Cond.(OU) at 0.207 slightly beats WaveNet (0.207) and PatchTST (0.209). For unconditional generation (Wasserstein-2), TSFlow with GP priors at 4 NFEs matches or beats the isotropic prior at 16 NFEs on 6/8 datasets, and outperforms TimeVAE by 20–40% across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSFlow extends the Conditional Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and its mini-batch optimal transport generalization (Tong et al., 2023) to time series by replacing the standard isotropic Gaussian prior with Gaussian Process priors that encode temporal structure. This departs from prior diffusion-based time series models (CSDI, SSSD, TSDiff, Biloš et al.) that all rely on fixed isotropic priors, and from functional flow matching (Kerrigan et al., 2023) which operates in infinite-dimensional GP spaces rather than parameterizing the joint data-prior distribution directly. The conditional prior sampling via Langevin dynamics and the use of GP regression for the conditional prior in forecasting are novel combinations that bridge classical GP regression with modern flow-based generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple, unfitted GP prior can meaningfully reduce the complexity of the generative transformation for time series, achieving competitive or state-of-the-art CRPS on 6/8 datasets with only 4 NFEs versus 16+ for diffusion baselines. This suggests that domain-informed priors are an underexploited lever in flow matching beyond image generation. Key limitations include restriction to univariate series, the need for per-dataset kernel selection, and the modest model scale (~176K parameters), leaving open whether the approach scales to high-dimensional multivariate forecasting or benefits from more complex data-dependent priors.
