## Problem

Existing self-supervised adversarial training methods rely on fixed, handcrafted PGD attack parameters (e.g., ε=8, 10 iterations, step size 2) that do not adapt to the model's evolving learning dynamics or account for instance-specific vulnerability. This one-size-fits-all approach limits adversarial robustness and prevents proper alignment between clean and adversarial data distributions in representation space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250298/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 96×96 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and produces robust contrastive feature embeddings from a ResNet-18 encoder. The strategy network outputs discrete attack parameters (PGD step size from {1,...,6}, iterations from {3,...,14}, perturbation ε from {3,...,15}) sampled per-instance from a learned conditional distribution p(a|x;θ). After pretraining, a linear or full-finetuned classifier is attached to produce class labels, evaluated under Auto Attack, PGD-20, and standard accuracy metrics.

## Method

ASTrA introduces a learnable strategy network (also ResNet-18) that autonomously selects per-instance PGD attack parameters via a REINFORCE policy gradient, bypassing the non-differentiability of adversarial example generation. The strategy network's reward combines adversarial contrastive loss (maximized) and clean contrastive loss (minimized), driving an exploration-exploitation process within a single training episode where the network initially samples broadly across perturbation values and converges to dataset-specific optima (e.g., ε=7,11,13 on CIFAR-10). The target network is trained with a composite objective of clean, adversarial, and a novel mixed contrastive loss that directly pairs each clean view with its adversarial counterpart to align their distributions in representation space, mitigating robust overfitting. The strategy network is updated every 10 target-network steps at learning rate 0.1, creating a synchronized co-evolving system that requires no labels, no predefined attack schedules, and no additional hyperparameters beyond the contrastive loss weights.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (SLF protocol, ResNet-18), ASTrA achieves 46.40% Auto-Attack accuracy (AA), 54.02% Robust Accuracy (RA), and 80.54% Standard Accuracy (SA), outperforming the strongest prior self-AT method DeACL (45.31% AA) and the supervised AT baseline (46.23% AA). On CIFAR-100, ASTrA reaches 21.34% AA and 53.20% SA, and on STL-10 it achieves 47.62% AA and 78.00% SA. The extended-pretraining variant ASTrA++ (2000 epochs) further improves to 46.92% AA on CIFAR-10, surpassing supervised AT for the first time in a self-supervised setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASTrA outperforms all six self-AT baselines on AA across all three datasets. On CIFAR-10, it improves over ACL by 8.78% AA (46.40 vs. 37.62), over DeACL by 1.09% (46.40 vs. 45.31), over DYNACL by 1.36% (46.40 vs. 45.04), and over DYNACL-AIR by 1.23% (46.40 vs. 45.17). ASTrA also marginally surpasses the supervised AT baseline on CIFAR-10 AA (46.40 vs. 46.23), and ASTrA++ clearly exceeds it (46.92 vs. 46.23). On CIFAR-100, ASTrA leads ACL by 5.66% AA and DeACL by 1.00% AA; on STL-10 it leads DYNACL-AIR by 0.04% AA and DYNACL by 1.03% AA. In the AFF protocol (Table 2), ASTrA achieves 50.84% AA on CIFAR-10, beating the supervised baseline (48.96%) by 1.88% and DYNACL-AIR (50.60%) by 0.24%. As a plug-and-play module, ASTrA boosts RoCL's AA by 1.22% and DYNACL's AA by 1.38% on CIFAR-10 AFF.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASTrA extends the self-supervised adversarial training (self-AT) lineage initiated by ACL (Jiang et al., 2020) and RoCL (Kim et al., 2020), which established contrastive learning as the foundation for label-free adversarial robustness. The key departure is replacing fixed, handcrafted PGD attack parameters with a learnable strategy network optimized via REINFORCE policy gradients, drawing from adaptive attack strategies explored in supervised AT (LAS-AT, Yao et al.) but adapting them to the label-free self-supervised setting where reward must be derived from contrastive losses rather than classification accuracy. The mixed contrastive loss generalizes RoCL's distribution alignment by pairing each clean view with its own adversarial counterpart, creating a more structured alignment objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASTrA demonstrates that adaptive, instance-level attack strategies learned through reinforcement learning can substantially close the gap between self-AT and supervised AT (exceeding supervised vanilla AT on CIFAR10 and STL10 in AFF), suggesting that the fixed-attack assumption in prior self-AT methods was a significant bottleneck. The plug-and-play nature (consistent gains when grafted onto RoCL and DYNACL) makes it practically useful for the broader self-AT community. Open questions include whether the handcrafted bin discretization of attack parameters can be made fully adaptive, whether the approach scales to larger architectures (ViT, larger ResNets) and full ImageNet, and whether the REINFORCE-based optimization can be replaced with lower-variance policy gradient methods for more stable training.
