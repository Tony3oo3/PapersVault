## Problem

Sigmoid attention has been proposed as a simpler, element-wise alternative to softmax attention in transformers, but prior attempts (e.g., Wortsman et al., 2023a) failed to match softmax performance across domains. The core obstacle—large initial attention norms destabilizing early training—was not identified or addressed, and no theoretical guarantees (universal approximation, regularity) existed for sigmoid-based transformers. This paper unifies prior art, pinpoints the norm-stabilization issue, and provides both theory and best practices to make sigmoid attention a viable drop-in replacement.

## Input / Output

SigmoidAttn operates on the same inputs as standard self-attention: a sequence of n token embeddings X ∈ R^{n×d} (text tokens, 16×16 image patches for ViT, or audio encoder features), producing a sequence of n output vectors of dimension d_v as a weighted sum of values. The key structural difference is that attention weights are computed element-wise via σ(QK^T/√d_qk + b) rather than as row-wise softmax, eliminating the row-reduction step. The bias b = −log(n) makes the attention well-defined in the infinite-sequence limit, where the output converges to an integral over the empirical measure of the input sequence.

## Method

The paper replaces the row-wise softmax in self-attention with an element-wise sigmoid activation σ(u) = (1+e^{−(u+b)})^{−1} applied to QK^T/√d_qk, with a sequence-length-dependent bias b = −log(n) that keeps attention norms bounded as n grows. The critical practical insight is that large initial attention Frobenius norms ‖σ(QK^T/√d_qk)V‖ cause training instability; this is mitigated via LayerScale (initialized at 10^{-4}), QK-norm, and either ALiBi positional embeddings or a negative bias offset proportional to −ln n. FlashSigmoid exploits the element-wise nature of sigmoid to remove softmax normalization and intermediate variable tracking from the kernel, yielding a 17% inference speedup over FlashAttention-2 on H100 GPUs. Theoretically, the paper proves universal approximation for sigmoid transformers (requiring 4 heads vs. 2 for softmax) and shows the Jacobian spectral norm scales as R² rather than R²·exp(cR²) for softmax, indicating improved regularity.

## Results

At 1B parameters with 2K context length, SigmoidAttn matches SoftmaxAttn on average English benchmark score (49.5% vs 49.4%) while achieving a 1.12× training speedup (0.34s vs 0.38s per step). At 7B parameters with 4K context and hybrid-norm, SigmoidAttn slightly outperforms SoftmaxAttn (59.6% vs 59.0% average), with gains on ARC-Challenge (40.5% vs 39.9%) and TriviaQA (39.5% vs 37.7%), while delivering a 1.13× speedup. On ASR (LibriSpeech 960h), SigmoidAttn achieves 2.4% WER on test-clean vs 2.3% for SoftmaxAttn, and on ImageNet ViT-B/16 classification, SigmoidAttn matches SoftmaxAttn top-1 accuracy. FlashSigmoid provides a 17.39% inference kernel speedup and 6.53% training kernel speedup over FlashAttention2 on H100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SoftmaxAttn (Vaswani et al., 2017), SigmoidAttn matches or slightly exceeds performance across all three domains: on 7B LLM benchmarks it achieves 59.6% vs 59.0% average with a 1.13× speedup; on LibriSpeech ASR it achieves 2.4% vs 2.3% WER on test-clean (within noise); and on ImageNet ViT-B/16 it matches top-1 accuracy. Against Wortsman et al. (2023a), who proposed n^{−α} normalization for non-softmax activations, the paper shows that their formulation underperforms without proper bias initialization (b=−log n) and LayerScale (Fig. 10a), whereas SigmoidAttn with these stabilizers matches SoftmaxAttn. Against Hua et al. (2022) ReLU² attention, SigmoidAttn with LayerScale and QK norm performs comparably, while ReLU² underperforms without these stabilizers (Fig. 10c). The key practical advantage over all baselines is the FlashSigmoid kernel, which eliminates the row-wise softmax reduction and achieves 17.39% faster inference kernels than FlashAttention2 on H100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on replacing softmax in attention with alternative activations (ReLU, ReLU², linear, cosine) by providing a comprehensive theoretical and empirical treatment of sigmoid attention. It builds directly on FlashAttention-2's IO-aware kernel design to produce FlashSigmoid, and adapts the universal approximation proof from Yun et al. (2020) to the sigmoid case. Crucially, it departs from Wortsman et al. (2023a), who proposed n^{-α} normalization for non-softmax activations, by showing that a learnable bias b = -log(n) combined with LayerScale is the correct stabilization mechanism, and identifies large initial attention norms as the root cause of prior sigmoid attention failures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes sigmoid attention as a validated, drop-in softmax replacement across language, vision, and speech with a 17% inference kernel speedup, providing practitioners with a concrete recipe (bias initialization, LayerScale, QK norm, hybrid-norm for long sequences) rather than just a theoretical possibility. Open questions remain: whether the R² Lipschitz bound is tight, whether stability issues at 1B/4K without hybrid-norm indicate a fundamental limitation, and whether the approach generalizes to emerging modalities or architectures beyond standard transformers. The FlashSigmoid implementation also raises the question of how these gains compose with distributed training and inference at scale.
