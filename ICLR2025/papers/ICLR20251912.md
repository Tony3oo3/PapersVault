## Problem

Pre-trained continuous-control policies (from RL or IL) often need to be adapted to new requirements—such as safety constraints or behavior preferences—without access to the original training reward or parameters. Existing policy customization solutions for continuous action spaces, like Residual-SAC, still require additional training steps, making them expensive and impractical for truly online deployment. This paper asks whether a purely online planning algorithm can customize a prior policy at execution time with zero additional policy training.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the prior policy's action distribution (not its underlying reward or critic), a learned dynamics model, and a user-specified add-on reward function. At each control step, it takes the current state observation and produces a receding-horizon action sequence (horizon T), of which only the first action is executed before replanning. The dynamics model is trained offline on rollouts from the prior policy (with exploration noise) and can optionally be fine-tuned online with the customized planner's own trajectories (few-shot variant).

## Method

Residual-MPPI integrates Residual Q-Learning into the Model Predictive Path Integral framework by defining the MPPI evaluation function as the sum of the add-on reward and the log-likelihood of the prior policy (weighted by ω′), which theoretically solves the augmented MDP without knowledge of the original reward. The key theoretical result (Proposition 1) establishes that MPPI's optimal action-sequence distribution is equivalent to the maximum-entropy policy distribution, validating MPPI as a solver for the customization objective. The nominal action sequence is initialized by rolling out the prior policy, and K Gaussian noise sequences are sampled around it; importance-sampling weights computed from the augmented evaluation function update the nominal sequence. A learned dynamics model (trained with multi-step prediction error and exploration noise) enables trajectory rollouts, and an optional online fine-tuning loop (few-shot variant) iteratively refines the dynamics using the customized planner's own data to improve accuracy in out-of-distribution states.

## Results

In MuJoCo zero-shot settings, Residual-MPPI achieves total rewards of 1936.2 (HalfCheetah), -60.0 (Swimmer), 7363.0 (Hopper), and 6846.7 (Ant), substantially outperforming the prior policy (1000.7, -245.2, 7252.7, 6333.7) while approaching the Fulltask-SAC upper bound (2149.9, -4.2, 7825.3, 7408.6) with only 2K steps of dynamics training data. In the Gran Turismo Sport racing environment, Few-shot Residual-MPPI reduces off-course steps from 93.1 (GT Sophy 1.0) to 4.4 with only a marginal lap time increase (122.93s vs 117.77s), while all MPPI baselines (Full-MPPI, Guided-MPPI, Greedy-MPPI) and Residual-SAC at 2K laps fail to complete a lap entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Residual-SAC (Li et al., 2024), Residual-MPPI achieves comparable total rewards in MuJoCo (e.g., 1936.2 vs 2184.5 in HalfCheetah, 7363.0 vs 7682.5 in Hopper) using only 2K steps of dynamics training data versus 4M steps of online RL, demonstrating a ~2000× sample-efficiency advantage. In GTS, Residual-SAC requires 80K laps to achieve 0.87 off-course steps at 130.00s lap time, while Few-shot Residual-MPPI achieves 4.43 off-course steps at 122.93s with only ~2K laps of data; at 2K laps, Residual-SAC cannot even finish a lap. Against Fulltask-SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), which serves as an oracle upper bound trained from scratch on the full reward, Residual-MPPI closes most of the gap (e.g., 6846.7 vs 7408.6 in Ant, 7363.0 vs 7825.3 in Hopper) without any access to the original reward function. Residual-MPPI also outperforms all MPPI variants with privileged information: Full-MPPI (no prior) fails catastrophically in all environments, Guided-MPPI (full reward + prior samples) underperforms due to limited long-horizon reasoning, and Valued-MPPI (prior Q-function as terminal value) achieves better basic-task scores but suboptimal full-task performance. The key differentiator is the log π term, which implicitly encodes the original reward and provides long-horizon value information that no finite-horizon planner with explicit rewards can replicate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Residual Q-Learning (RQL) framework of Li et al. (2024), which introduced the policy customization problem and solved it for discrete actions via MCTS and for continuous actions via offline SAC-based training. The key departure is replacing the SAC training step with an online MPPI planner, requiring only access to the prior policy's log-likelihood and a dynamics model. The theoretical bridge connecting MPPI's optimal distribution to maximum-entropy policies (Proposition 1) is drawn from Bhardwaj et al. (2020) and Haarnoja et al. (2017), repurposed here to justify using log π as a reward term that implicitly encodes the unknown prior reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: deploying a pre-trained policy (e.g., a racing agent or robot controller) and then needing to add safety constraints or behavioral preferences without retraining is a common real-world requirement that this paper addresses with a training-free online planner. The demonstration on GT Sophy 1.0 in Gran Turismo Sport—reducing off-course steps from 93 to 4–9 with only ~2,000 laps of dynamics data versus 80,000+ for Residual-SAC—shows the approach scales to complex, high-dimensional tasks. Open questions include the dependence on an accurate dynamics model (a key bottleneck for sim-to-real transfer), the absence of a terminal value estimator limiting long-horizon planning, and whether the method extends to non-maximum-entropy priors or stochastic dynamics.
