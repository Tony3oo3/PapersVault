## Problem

While training scaling laws for LLMs are well-established, the optimal allocation of inference-time compute between model size and inference strategy (sampling, tree search, voting) remains largely unexplored. This paper formulates compute-optimal inference as an explicit optimization problem: given a fixed FLOPs budget per question, how should one choose model size and inference algorithm to maximize problem-solving accuracy?

## Input / Output

The method takes a mathematical problem (MATH or GSM8K) as input, along with a policy LLM (Llemma-7B/34B, Pythia, Mistral-7B, or Llama3-8B) and a process reward model (Llemma-34B fine-tuned on Math-Shepherd) that assigns scalar scores to intermediate solution steps. Inference compute is modulated by the number of generated tokens, and the output is a single selected answer aggregated from multiple candidate solutions via weighted majority voting, best-of-n, or majority voting. The key experimental axis is the FLOPs-per-question budget, with each data point corresponding to 2^i samples for integer i.

## Method

The core contribution is REBASE (REward BAlanced SEarch), a tree search algorithm that replaces MCTS's costly rollouts with direct process-reward-model scoring of intermediate nodes. At each depth, REBASE assigns PRM rewards to all nodes, then allocates the remaining expansion budget across nodes proportionally to their softmax-normalized reward scores (controlled by a balance temperature T_b=0.1), generating children only for the most promising nodes without any explicit rollout. The paper also proves that sampling-based majority/weighted voting converges exponentially to a limit determined solely by the model's marginal answer distribution, establishing fundamental diminishing returns for simple sampling. Empirically, REBASE with smaller models (e.g., Llemma-7B) achieves Pareto-optimal cost-performance trade-offs, consistently outperforming both sampling-based methods and MCTS across all compute budgets, with Llemma-7B+REBASE matching or exceeding Llemma-34B at roughly 2× less FLOPs.

## Results

REBASE achieves Pareto-optimal cost-performance trade-offs across all tested models and tasks. On MATH500, REBASE with Llemma-7B reaches 46.8% accuracy using only 32 samples (1.48×10¹⁴ FLOPs), outperforming sampling with 256 samples (10.0×10¹⁴ FLOPs) at 45.5%—a 7× compute reduction with higher accuracy. Llemma-7B with REBASE requires roughly 2× less total FLOPs than Llemma-34B to achieve comparable accuracy across all inference strategies. On GSM8K, REBASE with Llama3-8B achieves 90.2% accuracy with 128 samples, surpassing the best sampling result of 89.7% with 256 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REBASE consistently outperforms all three baselines across every model, task, and compute budget tested. Against self-consistency (majority voting, Wang et al. 2023), REBASE+MV achieves 46.1% vs. 41.0% on MATH500 with Llemma-7B (32 samples), a 5.1-point gain at comparable FLOPs. Against step-aware weighted majority voting (Li et al. 2023), REBASE+WV reaches 46.8% vs. 43.5% on MATH500 with Llemma-7B, and 49.2% vs. 46.7% with Llemma-34B. Against MCTS (Zhou et al. 2024, LATS), REBASE is strictly superior: MCTS underperforms even plain sampling at every compute budget due to its costly rollouts, while REBASE uses the PRM directly for node expansion without rollouts. Notably, Llemma-7B with REBASE outperforms Llemma-34B with standard majority voting across all compute budgets, demonstrating that a smaller model with an advanced inference algorithm can beat a larger model with a simpler one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training scaling laws literature (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) to the inference regime, formulating compute-optimal inference as a joint optimization over model size and inference strategy under a fixed FLOPs budget. Methodologically, REBASE inherits the exploitation and pruning structure of MCTS (Silver et al., 2016) but replaces costly rollouts with a process reward model (trained on Math-Shepherd, Wang et al., 2024) to drive node expansion, yielding a tree search whose compute cost is comparable to simple sampling. The paper positions itself against prior inference strategies—majority voting (Wang et al., 2023a), weighted voting (Li et al., 2023), and MCTS variants (Zhou et al., 2024)—by being the first to systematically characterize their cost-performance trade-offs and prove convergence bounds for voting-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication—that a 7B model with REBASE can match or exceed a 34B model at 2× less inference FLOPs—offers a concrete deployment strategy for resource-constrained settings, shifting the optimization target from model scale to inference algorithm design. The negative result that MCTS underperforms even simple sampling at matched compute budgets challenges the widespread assumption that tree search is always beneficial for LLM reasoning. Open questions include whether the convergence bounds extend to tree-search policies (the paper hypothesizes but does not prove a higher saturation ceiling for REBASE), whether findings generalize beyond mathematical reasoning to open-ended generation, and how the optimal model-size/inference-strategy trade-off shifts as base models grow to hundreds of billions of parameters.
