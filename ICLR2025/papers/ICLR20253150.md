## Problem

Existing regeneration attacks for image watermark removal (Regen, Rinse) start from a noised version of the watermarked image's latent representation, which retains residual watermark structure for high-perturbation methods like StegaStamp and TreeRing. Increasing noising steps to fully disrupt the watermark severely degrades image quality and semantic consistency, creating an unresolved trade-off between removal effectiveness and visual fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single watermarked RGB image as input (a no-box attack needing no knowledge of the watermark scheme, detector, or generative model). For CtrlRegen+, an additional noising step parameter (t* ∈ [0, T]) controls the degree of watermark destruction, with higher values approaching pure Gaussian noise. The output is a regenerated watermark-free image at the same resolution, conditioned on DINOv2 image embeddings and Canny edge maps extracted from the input.

## Method

CtrlRegen regenerates the image from clean Gaussian noise (or a partially noised latent in CtrlRegen+) using a controllable Stable Diffusion-v1.5 backbone, guided by two trained control modules. A semantic control adapter (DINOv2-giant encoder → trainable projection → decoupled cross-attention layers inserted into the U-Net, following IP-Adapter design) preserves coarse semantic content, while a spatial control network (ControlNet-style architecture fed with Canny edge maps) enforces fine-grained layout and structural consistency. Training proceeds in two stages: first the projection and cross-attention layers are trained with frozen SD and DINOv2 parameters, then the spatial control network is fine-tuned with both conditions active. The key insight is that starting from clean noise inherently contains no watermark structure, so the control mechanisms need only preserve visual consistency rather than also suppress residual watermark signals.

## Results

CtrlRegen reduces the watermark detection TPR@1%FPR of StegaStamp from 1.00 to 0.01 and TreeRing from 0.99 to 0.12, while the uncontrolled Regen baseline only achieves 0.99 and 0.87 respectively. Across all six watermarking methods evaluated, CtrlRegen achieves the lowest post-attack TPR@1%FPR (ranging from 0.00 to 0.12) and the best CLIP-FID scores (e.g., 1.63 for TreeRing vs. 2.84 for Regen and 5.83 for Rinse). On no-reference quality metrics, CtrlRegen consistently outperforms both baselines, achieving Q-Align scores of 3.62–4.17 and LIQE scores of 3.15–4.34, compared to 2.11–3.69 and 1.93–3.96 for the baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CtrlRegen against two uncontrolled regeneration baselines: Regen (single noising-denoising pass, 70 steps) and Rinse (two iterative Regen passes). On high-perturbation watermarks, the advantage is dramatic: for StegaStamp, CtrlRegen reduces TPR@1%FPR to 0.01 versus 0.99 (Regen) and 0.94 (Rinse), and for TreeRing to 0.12 versus 0.87 and 0.61. On low-perturbation watermarks (DwtDctSvd, RivaGAN, SSL, StableSignature), all three methods achieve near-complete removal, but CtrlRegen still attains the lowest residual TPR@1%FPR (0.00–0.06) and the best CLIP-FID scores. On image quality, CtrlRegen consistently outperforms both baselines on Q-Align and LIQE across all six watermarks (e.g., LIQE 4.34 vs. 3.96/3.29 for TreeRing), while its PSNR is lower than the baselines by roughly 4–7 dB — a gap the authors attribute to the controlled regeneration producing content-harmonious changes rather than pixel-level preservation. Overall, CtrlRegen wins on every watermark removal and quality metric, with the largest margins on the high-perturbation watermarks where the baselines fail most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the uncontrolled regeneration attack (Zhao et al., 2023a; An et al., 2024) by replacing the 'partially noised latent → uncontrolled denoise' pipeline with a 'clean Gaussian noise → controllable denoise' paradigm. The controllability is achieved by repurposing two established conditioning mechanisms: IP-Adapter-style cross-attention with DINOv2 image embeddings for semantic alignment, and ControlNet-style edge-conditioned convolutional injection for spatial layout preservation. The key conceptual departure is that the starting point contains zero watermark information, so removal is guaranteed in principle; the challenge shifts entirely to maintaining visual consistency through learned control signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing StegaStamp detection from TPR 1.00 to 0.01 and TreeRing from 0.99 to 0.12 while outperforming uncontrolled baselines on image quality metrics, the paper demonstrates that even high-perturbation, 'robust' watermarks are vulnerable to a black-box regeneration attack requiring only a single watermarked image. This raises urgent questions about whether any current watermarking scheme can survive full regeneration from clean noise, and motivates the development of watermarks that are invariant to semantic-level re-synthesis rather than merely pixel-level perturbations. A noted limitation is that consistency is bounded by the SD-v1.5 backbone; the authors flag SD-v3 and stronger control mechanisms as future directions.
