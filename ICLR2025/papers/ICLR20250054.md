## Problem

Empirical studies show that MAE-trained ViTs learn diverse, local attention patterns while CL-trained ViTs collapse to uniform global attention, yet no theoretical framework explains this divergence for the dominant transformer architecture. Prior SSL theory focused on linear models, CNNs, or MLPs, and no end-to-end convergence guarantee existed for ViTs trained with self-supervised objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250054/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on images represented as P patches of dimension d (X ∈ R^{d×P}), with fixed orthogonal positional encodings added to each patch. For MAE, a random subset of patches (ratio γ) is zeroed out and the ViT must reconstruct the masked patches; for CL, two independently masked views of the same image form a positive pair against a batch of negatives. The key analytical output is not a single prediction but the learned attention pattern—specifically the feature-position (FP) and position-position (PP) correlations that determine which spatial area each query patch attends to.

## Method

The paper models visual data as K clusters, each partitioned into one large global area and several small local areas, with an information gap Δ quantifying the size imbalance. It analyzes one-layer softmax ViTs (single attention head, Q = W^Q W^K, identity value matrix) trained by gradient descent from zero initialization under both MAE and CL losses. The core technical tool is tracking the GD dynamics of two types of attention correlations: FP correlations (e_p^T Q v_{k,m}) and PP correlations (e_p^T Q e_q). The proof proceeds via phase decomposition: for MAE with positive Δ, local-area patches first decouple from the global feature (Phase I) before growing their target local FP correlation (Phase II), while global-area patches learn in a single phase; for CL, the dominance of global patches at initialization causes all patches to exclusively learn global FP correlations, yielding attention collapse. The results provide the first global convergence guarantees for ViTs under SSL objectives and formally characterize the distinct attention patterns each method converges to.

## Results

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so the main results are convergence guarantees and attention pattern characterizations rather than standard benchmark metrics. Theorem 4.1 proves that MAE-trained ViTs converge to near-optimal reconstruction loss (within ε) in O((1/η)·log(P)·P^{max{2(U/L−1),1}(1−κ_s)} + (1/(ηε))·log(P/ε)) iterations, with each masked patch attending almost entirely to unmasked patches in its own area. Theorem 4.4 shows that CL-trained ViTs converge to a collapsed global attention pattern where all patches focus on the global area, even under mild feature imbalance (Δ ≥ Ω(1)). The empirical validation (Figure 3) introduces a novel attention diversity metric on ImageNet 1K, confirming that MAE exhibits significantly higher attention diversity than MoCo, DINO, or DeiT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MAE against three baselines using a novel attention diversity metric on ViT-B/16 pretrained on ImageNet 1K (Figure 3, box plots over 152 images, 12 attention heads in the last layer). MAE consistently shows the highest attention diversity, meaning its query patches attend to distinct, localized areas depending on their spatial position. MoCo v3 (contrastive learning) and DINO (non-contrastive self-supervised) both exhibit markedly lower diversity, with attention collapsing toward a common global region regardless of query patch location. DeiT (supervised) also shows low diversity comparable to the discriminative SSL methods. These empirical results directly validate the theoretical predictions: MAE learns diverse feature-position correlations across all areas (Theorem 4.2), while CL collapses to global FP correlations only (Theorem 4.4), even when the information gap Δ is small.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical study of self-supervised learning from linear models, CNNs, and MLPs to the dominant transformer architecture, filling a gap explicitly identified in the literature. It builds on Jelassi et al. (2022) for spatially structured data distributions in ViTs and on the MAE (He et al., 2022) and CL (Chen et al., 2020) formulations, but departs from prior SSL theory by introducing a feature-position correlation framework that captures how attention patterns emerge during gradient descent training. Unlike prior transformer theory focused on in-context learning or supervised classification, this work is the first to provide end-to-end convergence guarantees for ViTs trained under self-supervised objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical explanation for why MAE and CL produce qualitatively different attention patterns in ViTs, validating empirical observations from Park et al. (2023) and Xie et al. (2023) with precise conditions on the information gap Δ. Key limitations include the one-layer, single-head architecture, a highly structured data distribution with orthogonal features, and the restriction to specific regimes of Δ (excluding the near-zero gap). Open questions include whether these dynamics persist in multi-layer, multi-head ViTs, under less structured data, and whether the two-phase decoupling mechanism for local areas under positive Δ has practical implications for training schedules or masking strategies.
