## Problem

Shampoo, a higher-order preconditioning optimizer, outperforms Adam in large-scale training but introduces additional hyperparameters and computational overhead from frequent eigendecompositions. When the eigendecomposition is computed less frequently to reduce cost, Shampoo's performance degrades sharply because its adaptivity is limited to the infrequent L/R updates. The paper asks whether a simpler, more robust algorithm can retain Shampoo's benefits while reducing both hyperparameter count and sensitivity to preconditioning frequency.

## Input / Output

For each 2D weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n}, SOAP consumes the per-batch gradient G ∈ R^{m×n} and maintains four per-layer state matrices: L ∈ R^{m×m}, R ∈ R^{n×n} (exponential moving averages of GG^T and G^T G), and M, V ∈ R^{m×n} (Adam's first- and second-moment estimates in the rotated basis). The eigenvector matrices Q_L ∈ R^{m×m} and Q_R ∈ R^{n×n} are recomputed every f steps via one power-iteration step plus QR decomposition. For 1D layers SOAP falls back to standard AdamW, and for very large dimensions (e.g., embedding/output layers) the rotation is fixed to identity, recovering Adam on that side.

## Method

The paper first proves that Shampoo with exponent 1/2 and a scalar trace correction is exactly equivalent to running Adafactor in the eigenbasis of Shampoo's Kronecker-factored preconditioner (Claim 1). Building on this, SOAP replaces Adafactor with full AdamW: at each step it rotates the gradient into the current eigenbasis (G' = Q_L^T G Q_R), updates Adam's momentum and second-moment estimates in that rotated space, applies the adaptive update, and rotates back to the original coordinates. The eigenvectors Q_L, Q_R are refreshed only every f steps using a single power-iteration step followed by QR decomposition (cheaper than torch.linalg.eigh), while the second-moment V is updated every step in the slowly changing basis. This design introduces only one new hyperparameter over AdamW (the preconditioning frequency f) and, because the second-moment estimates adapt continuously rather than only at eigendecomposition steps, SOAP degrades much more gracefully as f increases. On 360m and 660m language model pre-training (C4, OLMo architecture), SOAP achieves ≥40% fewer iterations and ≥35% less wall-clock time than AdamW, and ~20% improvements over DistributedShampoo at a 2M token batch size.

## Results

On C4 language model pre-training (360m and 660m models, 2m token batch size, preconditioning frequency 10), SOAP achieves a ≥40% reduction in training iterations and ≥35% reduction in wall-clock time compared to AdamW, and approximately 20% reduction in both metrics compared to DistributedShampoo. At a smaller 256k batch size (360m model), SOAP still reduces iterations by ≥25% vs. AdamW and ~10% vs. Shampoo, while also improving the critical batch size (closer to ideal linear scaling). SOAP is notably more robust to increasing preconditioning frequency, with performance degrading significantly slower than Shampoo at frequencies above 10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOAP outperforms AdamW across all tested configurations: at 2m batch size it reduces iterations by ≥40% and wall-clock time by ≥35%, and at 256k batch size it still achieves ≥25% fewer iterations. Against DistributedShampoo (the winning Algoperf benchmark optimizer), SOAP provides approximately 20% fewer iterations and wall-clock time at 2m batch size, and ~10% improvements at 256k batch size. SOAP also demonstrates superior robustness to preconditioning frequency: while both SOAP and Shampoo outperform AdamW at all frequencies tested (1–100), SOAP's performance degrades significantly more slowly than Shampoo's at higher frequencies. In a direct comparison with GaLore (Appendix D, 210m model), SOAP's design choices prove necessary — GaLore achieves a final loss of 3.12 versus Shampoo's 3.10, while SOAP outperforms both. SOAP also improves the critical batch size relative to AdamW, tracking closer to ideal linear scaling as batch size increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOAP is built directly on the E-KFAC paradigm (George et al., 2018) of running a first-order method in the eigenbasis of a second-order preconditioner, applied here to Shampoo. The key theoretical starting point is Morwani et al. (2024), who showed Shampoo with power 1/2 is the optimal Kronecker approximation of Adagrad; this paper formalizes the further equivalence that this variant is exactly Adafactor in Shampoo's eigenbasis. Anil et al. (2020) described an essentially equivalent algorithm in their Appendix B but reported unpublished negative results, while GaLore (Zhao et al., 2024a) shares the full-rank projection structure but differs in how momentum and the rotation are maintained. SOAP departs from these by using AdamW (rather than Adafactor) as the inner optimizer and by updating the second-moment estimate at every step in the slowly-rotating eigenbasis, which is the key mechanism that stabilizes performance at high preconditioning frequencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A ≥40% reduction in training iterations and ≥35% reduction in wall-clock time over AdamW, with only one additional hyperparameter (preconditioning frequency), is a practically significant efficiency gain for LLM pretraining that could be widely adopted. The robustness to preconditioning frequency (degrading much more slowly than Shampoo) makes the method more forgiving in practice. However, all experiments are at 360m–660m parameter scale with 2m-token batch sizes, well below the 7B+ scale of current frontier models, so generalization to larger runs remains an open question. Future work on distributed implementations, lower-precision preconditioners, and validation at scale will determine whether these gains persist in production settings.
