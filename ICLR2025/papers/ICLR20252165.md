## Problem

Previous trajectory-level equivariant diffusion policies for robot manipulation require every Markov transition in the denoising chain to be SE(3)-equivariant, making training significantly harder and more computationally expensive than invariant alternatives. Additionally, these models define the diffusion process in Euclidean space rather than on the SE(3) manifold, limiting expressiveness for multimodal action distributions. The broader challenge is achieving spatial generalization to unseen object poses with minimal demonstrations in imitation learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a colored point cloud P ∈ R^{N×6} (coordinates + colors) of the target object as observation, segmented from the scene using SAM2 in real-world settings. It outputs a full manipulation trajectory as a sequence of T_p SE(3) transformations (4×4 matrices) representing 6D end-effector poses, which are then executed via inverse kinematics. The action space is defined directly in SE(3) rather than joint space, and the model operates on the SE(3) manifold for both the diffused variable and the noise, with the final noisy state drawn from a Gaussian centered at the identity transformation.

## Method

ET-SEED's key theoretical contribution is Proposition 1, which proves that an equivariant marginal distribution p(x⁰|c) is guaranteed if only one transition in the Markov chain is equivariant (p₂-like), while the remaining K−1 transitions can be merely invariant (p₁-like). This drastically reduces training difficulty since invariant networks converge faster and require less computation than equivariant ones (validated empirically in Appendix D). Architecturally, the model uses two SE(3) Transformer backbones: an invariant backbone E_inv for the first K−1 denoising steps and an equivariant backbone E_equiv for the final step. The equivariant backbone achieves translation equivariance via a mass-center-plus-rotated-offset formulation (t = M + R·offset) and rotation equivariance through type-1 features with Schmidt orthogonalization. The diffusion process is defined on the SE(3) manifold using an interpolation-based noise schedule H^k = Exp(γ√(1−ᾱ_t)ε)·F(√ᾱ_t; H⁰, I), analogous to DDPM but operating on the group rather than Euclidean space.

## Results

ET-SEED achieves the highest success rates across 5 of 6 simulation tasks in the New Poses (NP) generalization setting, with particularly large margins on long-horizon and deformable tasks: Calligraphy NP50 (54% vs. 40% for EquiBot, 3% for DP3), Fold Garment NP50 (69% vs. 60% vs. 8%), and Open Bottle Cap NP50 (82% vs. 77% vs. 14%). In the geodesic distance metric (lower is better), ET-SEED is best on all 6 tasks, e.g., 0.082 for Rotate Triangle NP50 vs. 0.107 (EquiBot) and 1.627 (DP3). In real-world experiments with only 20 demonstrations, ET-SEED achieves 0.8 success on Open Bottle Cap and 0.6 on both Open Door and Fold Garment, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ET-SEED is compared against three baselines: DP3 (a standard 3D diffusion policy), DP3+Aug (DP3 with SE(3) data augmentation), and EquiBot (a SIM(3)-equivariant diffusion policy requiring equivariance at every denoising step). Against DP3 and DP3+Aug, ET-SEED wins decisively on all NP (unseen pose) settings across all six tasks, with gaps of 10–50+ percentage points, confirming that data augmentation alone cannot substitute for structural equivariance. Against EquiBot, ET-SEED wins on 5 of 6 NP50 success-rate metrics (e.g., Calligraphy 54% vs. 40%, Fold Garment 69% vs. 60%, Open Bottle Cap 82% vs. 77%) and on all geodesic-distance metrics, with the sole exception being Fling Garment NP50 where EquiBot edges ahead (64% vs. 62%). The advantage over EquiBot is most pronounced with only 25 demonstrations, where ET-SEED's reduced training complexity (one equivariant step vs. K equivariant steps) translates into substantially better convergence. In real-world experiments with 20 demonstrations, ET-SEED outperforms all baselines on all four tasks, achieving 0.8 success on Open Bottle Cap versus 0.6 (EquiBot) and 0.2 (DP3/DP3+Aug).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of equivariant neural networks (SE(3) Transformers, Fuchs et al. 2020), diffusion-based policy learning (Diffusion Policy, Chi et al. 2023), and SE(3) manifold diffusion (Jiang et al. 2024). Its most direct predecessor is EquiBot (Yang et al. 2024a), which requires every Markov transition in the diffusion process to be SE(3)-equivariant. The key departure is a theoretical result (Proposition 1) showing that only a single equivariant transition is necessary for end-to-end equivariance, with all other steps being merely invariant — a substantially weaker and cheaper constraint. The diffusion formulation is also moved from Euclidean space onto the SE(3) manifold, following Urain et al. (2023) and Jiang et al. (2024), to better capture multimodal action distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'one equivariant step suffices' theorem is broadly applicable to any equivariant diffusion model, not just robotic manipulation, and could simplify training of equivariant generative models more generally. Practically, ET-SEED demonstrates that 20 demonstrations suffice for spatial generalization on real-robot tasks, which is meaningful for deployment where data collection is expensive. However, the method is limited to 6D end-effector pose actions (ruling out dexterous manipulation), requires pre-segmented object point clouds, and the absolute real-world success rates (0.4–0.8) leave room for improvement. Open questions include whether the single-equivariant-step principle extends to other symmetry groups (e.g., SIM(3), conformal) and whether the approach can handle higher-dimensional action spaces.
