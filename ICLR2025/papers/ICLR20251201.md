## Problem

Attribute-based few-shot adaptation methods for VLMs improve out-of-distribution generalization, but the attribute pools they use contain a small subset of spuriously correlated attributes (e.g., 'trees' for 'mountain bike') that VLMs disproportionately rely on for predictions, undermining generalization. No prior work provided an automated, scalable mechanism to identify and mitigate these spurious attributes in open-world settings without human supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (512×512 for diffusion-generated pseudo categories) paired with text prompts consisting of a category name concatenated with visual attributes, and produces a class label via standard CLIP-style similarity scoring. A one-time preprocessing step queries GPT-4V with ~16 training images per category to identify spurious attributes, and Stable Diffusion (or LAION-5B retrieval) generates 16 synthetic images per spurious attribute to form pseudo categories. The method is plug-and-play: it wraps around any existing PEFT backbone (CoCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC, CPL, etc.) without modifying their architecture.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary components. SAP (Spurious Attribute Probing) uses a MLLM with chain-of-thought prompts to partition each category's attribute pool into core and non-core attributes, then trains a Concept Bottleneck Model to measure each non-core attribute's influence weight on predictions; attributes whose weight exceeds an adaptive per-category threshold (the minimum core-attribute weight) are flagged as spurious. SAS (Spurious Attribute Shielding) constructs pseudo categories from the identified spurious attributes using Stable Diffusion (with LLM-generated diverse prompts) or LAION-5B retrieval, then adds an auxiliary cross-entropy loss that forces the VLM to discriminate between the real category and its pseudo counterparts, thereby reducing reliance on spurious visual features. The total loss is L_ce + λ·L_pse, and a selective optimization trick limits the auxiliary loss to only the ~10% of categories most affected by spurious attributes to reduce training overhead.

## Results

On base-to-new generalization across 11 datasets, integrating SAP and SAS into existing PEFT methods yields an average improvement of over 2% in out-of-distribution (new category) accuracy while preserving in-distribution performance. On the adversarial counter-group evaluation (where spurious attributes are absent from test images), SAS bridges the accuracy gap by up to ~6% (e.g., CPL on FGVCAircraft: 27.65% → 32.12%). On group robustness benchmarks, SAS achieves state-of-the-art worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds (89.7%), CelebA (87.4%), and BREEDS (87.8%), outperforming dedicated debiasing methods like C-Adapter and CFR. The method generalizes across 4 VLMs (CLIP, BLIP, EVA-CLIP, SigLIP) and extends to video classification with consistent gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAS consistently outperforms all 11 PEFT baselines it is integrated with (CoCoOp, TCP, KgCoOp, LASP, PromptSRC, MaPLe, CPL, ArGue, MAP, CLIP-Adapter, Tip-Adapter), adding 1–2% on standard test accuracy and up to 6% on the harder counter group where spurious cues are absent. Against dedicated spurious-correlation mitigation methods (Table 11), SAS achieves the best worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds (89.7% vs. C-Adapter 86.9%, CFR 88.2%, DISC 88.7%), CelebA (87.4% vs. C-Adapter 84.6%, CFR 84.7%), and BREEDS (87.8% vs. C-Adapter 80.0%, CFR 85.0%), while remaining competitive on CIFAR-10.02 (88.5% vs. CFR 89.1%). SAP specifically improves attribute-based baselines: CPL's new-category accuracy rises from 65.30% to 67.66% and ArGue's from 66.07% to 67.69% after filtering spurious attributes, without sacrificing base-category accuracy. On recently proposed methods MMA and DMN (Table 18), SAS still yields consistent gains (e.g., DMN on FGVCAircraft: 32.60% → 35.38%), confirming its plug-and-play nature. The method also transfers to video (ViFi-CLIP), where PromptSRC+SAS improves K-400 accuracy from 68.31% to 70.23% and SSv2 from 17.22% to 18.95%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attribute-based PEFT line (CPL, ArGue, MAP) by revealing that a small subset of generated attributes are spuriously correlated with the target category rather than intrinsically descriptive, and that VLMs disproportionately rely on these 'black sheep' for predictions. It builds directly on Concept Bottleneck Models (Koh et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2023) as the interpretive mechanism for quantifying attribute influence, and on MLLMs (GPT-4V) for open-world attribute identification without human supervision. The SAS module departs from prior spurious-correlation mitigation (C-Adapter, DISC, CFR) by explicitly constructing pseudo-categories from identified spurious attributes via diffusion-generated or retrieved images, turning the known-attribute setting into a concrete auxiliary training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fewer than 7% of attributes in a pool can dominate VLM decision-making (occupying 2 of the top-3 CBM weights) challenges the working assumption that attribute-based prompting improves generalization through richer semantic description; instead, the gains may partly come from exploiting background shortcuts. The plug-and-play nature of SAS across PEFT methods and VLMs (CLIP, BLIP, EVA-CLIP, SigLIP) makes it practically deployable, though it requires GPT-4V access and Stable Diffusion inference as one-time costs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-semantic spurious cues (e.g., lighting, camera artifacts), whether it extends to language reasoning or retrieval tasks, and whether the adaptive threshold strategy remains reliable at very large scale (e.g., 10k+ categories).
