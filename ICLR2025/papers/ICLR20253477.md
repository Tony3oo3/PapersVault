## Problem

The robustness of Diffusion-Based Purification (DBP) is conventionally attributed to the forward diffusion process reducing the distribution gap between clean and adversarial images, but this explanation has not been empirically validated and cannot account for observed robustness drops when stochasticity is reduced. The paper asks whether the intrinsic stochasticity in the DBP process—rather than purification—is the primary driver of robustness, and whether DBP models genuinely possess the ability to purify adversarial perturbations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253477/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The DBP pipeline takes an adversarial RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) and a ground-truth class label, applies forward diffusion to a predefined timestep t* (0.1×T), then runs the reverse denoising process to produce a purified image that is fed to a downstream classifier (e.g., WRN-28-10, ResNet-50, ViT-B). During ADDT training, the input additionally includes an adversarial perturbation δ generated by CGPO, which is mapped through RBGM to approximate Gaussian noise before being blended into the noisy input x_t'. The output of the full pipeline is a purified image and its predicted class label.

## Method

The paper introduces a Deterministic White-box (DW-box) attack setting where the attacker knows all stochastic noise variables (forward ε_f and reverse {ε_t}), making the diffusion process deterministic from the attacker's perspective, and shows that DBP models lose most robustness under this setting, revealing that stochasticity—not purification—is the primary defense mechanism. To strengthen the purification component, the authors propose Adversarial Denoising Diffusion Training (ADDT), an iterative two-step procedure: (1) Classifier-Guided Perturbation Optimization (CGPO) generates adversarial perturbations by maximizing classification error through a frozen diffusion model and classifier using PGD with BPDA for non-differentiable operations, and (2) the diffusion model is trained to reconstruct the clean image from the perturbed noisy input, effectively learning to remove both Gaussian noise and adversarial perturbations. To make adversarial perturbations compatible with the Gaussian noise assumption of diffusion models, Rank-Based Gaussian Mapping (RBGM) is applied: it sorts both the perturbation and a sampled Gaussian tensor, then maps perturbation elements to Gaussian values by rank, preserving structural information while enforcing Gaussian-like statistics, and blends the result with additional Gaussian noise via a timestep-dependent coefficient λ_t.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PGD20+EoT10 attacks, ADDT consistently improves robust accuracy across all diffusion backbones: DiffPure+ADDT reaches 62.11% ℓ∞ robust accuracy (vs. 55.96% for vanilla DiffPure), DP_EDM+ADDT achieves 66.41% ℓ∞ and 79.16% ℓ2, and DP_DDPM+ADDT reaches 51.46% ℓ∞ (vs. 47.27% vanilla). On CIFAR-100, DP_DDPM+ADDT improves ℓ∞ robust accuracy from 20.41% to 23.73% at 100 NFEs. On Tiny-ImageNet and ImageNet-1k, ADDT yields smaller but consistent gains (e.g., 19.14%→20.61% ℓ∞ on Tiny-ImageNet with DP_EDM; 46.92%→48.02% on ImageNet-1k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADDT is applied as a fine-tuning procedure on top of existing DBP methods, so the primary comparison is each method with vs. without ADDT. DiffPure+ADDT (62.11% ℓ∞) outperforms the strongest standalone baselines COUP (50.78%) and GDMP-MSE (40.97%) by 11.3 and 21.1 points respectively on CIFAR-10. DP_EDM+ADDT achieves the highest overall robust accuracy (66.41% ℓ∞, 79.16% ℓ2), surpassing DiffPure+ADDT by 4.3 points on ℓ∞. On CIFAR-100, DP_DDPM+ADDT improves ℓ∞ robust accuracy by 3.3 points over vanilla (23.73% vs. 20.41%) and ℓ2 by 3.8 points (41.70% vs. 37.89%). On Tiny-ImageNet, gains are more modest (e.g., DP_EDM: 19.14%→20.61% ℓ∞), and on ImageNet-1k the improvement is only ~1 point (46.92%→48.02%), suggesting that scaling ADDT effectively requires greater model capacity and data volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DiffPure framework (Nie et al., 2022) and the DDPM/DDIM training formulations (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2020), but departs sharply from the prevailing explanation of DBP robustness. Prior work attributed robustness to the forward diffusion process reducing the KL divergence between clean and adversarial distributions (the 'wash-out' effect); this paper instead argues that stochasticity throughout both forward and reverse processes is the primary defense mechanism, demonstrated via a novel Deterministic White-box (DW-box) attack setting. The proposed ADDT method draws on adversarial training (Madry et al., 2018) and EoT-based adaptive attacks (Athalye et al., 2018b), while RBGM is a new technique to reconcile adversarial perturbations with the Gaussian noise assumption inherent in diffusion training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DBP robustness is predominantly stochasticity-driven rather than purification-driven reframes the design space for future defenses: practitioners can now target two complementary axes—improving purification through adversarial training (ADDT) and increasing gradient variance to resist EoT attacks. The DW-box setting and the identified AutoAttack bug (which overestimated DBP robustness by up to 10 points) are practically important corrections for the field's evaluation methodology. However, ADDT's gains are modest at scale (ImageNet-1k shows only ~1 point improvement), suggesting that adequate model capacity and data volume remain bottlenecks, and the fundamental tension between stochasticity-based and purification-based robustness is not yet resolved.
