## Problem

Integrating small-scale experiences (e.g., recent interactions, factual passages) into LLMs requires balancing two properties: efficacy (accurately answering questions about newly injected context) and retention (recalling previously injected contexts after subsequent updates). Existing parameter-based methods (continual learning, model editing, knowledge distillation) struggle with rapid updates and complex multi-faceted experiences, while external-storage methods (RAG, memory modules) incur growing storage overhead. The paper asks whether a model can internalize arbitrary contexts into its own parameters with near-optimal efficacy and robust long-term retention at zero additional storage cost.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes a text context (a passage, conversation, or document) and a constructed Target Sentence Set comprising ~50 diverse question-answer pairs generated by an instruct model (gpt-4o-mini or the corresponding instruct variant) plus random sentences sampled from SlimPajama as a regularization set. At inference time, the updated model receives only a question prompt (no context) and produces a text answer, with the injected knowledge fully encoded in the model weights. For sequential injection, LoRA adapters are merged into the base model after each step, so the model accumulates knowledge across up to 20+ contexts without retaining any external store.

## Method

SELF-PARAM defines a training objective that minimizes the KL divergence between the original model's token distribution conditioned on (context + sentence) and the target model's distribution conditioned on the sentence alone, effectively distilling the context-conditioned behavior into the target model's parameters. The key mechanism is constructing a diverse Target Sentence Set: an instruct model generates multiple question-answer pairs per context to cover varied query formulations, while SlimPajama sentences act as a regularizer preserving general capabilities. The target model is updated via LoRA (rank 8, targeting attention and MLP projections) or direct MLP-layer training, with the KL divergence computed over the full vocabulary at each token position. This differs from standard next-word-prediction fine-tuning because the KL objective aligns the target model's conditional distribution with the context-aware original model across diverse phrasings, preventing overfitting to a single question format and enabling the model to both absorb and dynamically apply the injected knowledge.

## Results

On the Batch Context Injection task (QA-F1), SELF-PARAM achieves 0.5082 (OpenLLaMA-3B-v2, 100 contexts), 0.4521 (Mistral-7B, 100), and 0.4368 (Llama3-8B, 100), closely approaching the upper bound of Base,C+Q (0.5043, 0.5461, 0.5368) while requiring zero additional storage. On Conversational Recommendation (Recall@1), SELF-PARAM reaches 0.0395 on INSPIRED (r₃) and 0.0360 on REDIAL (r₃), outperforming the base model (0.0356, 0.0333) and all retrieval-based baselines. In Sequential Injection, SELF-PARAM retains a QA-F1 of ~0.3 after 20 sequential injections, compared to ~0.14 for the unmodified base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SELF-PARAM substantially outperforms all three external-storage baselines across every model and context size. Against MemoryLLM-8B (Wang et al., ICML 2024), SELF-PARAM achieves 0.4368 vs. 0.1435 QA-F1 on Llama3-8B with 100 contexts (a 3× margin), and 0.4221 vs. 0.0841 with 500 contexts. Against DPR (Karpukhin et al., EMNLP 2020), SELF-PARAM scores 0.4368 vs. 0.2184 (Llama3-8B, 100 contexts) and 0.5082 vs. 0.2795 (OpenLLaMA-3B, 100 contexts), roughly doubling the retrieval-based performance. Against RAPTOR (Sarthi et al., ICLR 2024), the gap is even wider: 0.4368 vs. 0.2000 (Llama3-8B, 100) and 0.5082 vs. 0.1344 (OpenLLaMA-3B, 100). On Conversational Recommendation, SELF-PARAM (Recall@1 = 0.0395 on INSPIRED r₃) beats DPR (0.0277), BM25 (0.0198), and RAPTOR (0.0356), while fine-tuning baselines FT(C) and FT(S) collapse to zero across all scenarios. Critically, SELF-PARAM achieves these gains with zero additional storage (S=0), whereas all retrieval and memory-augmented baselines incur O(n) or O(1) storage overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

SELF-PARAM carves new ground at the intersection of model editing (ROME, MEMIT, MEND), continual learning, and knowledge distillation, but departs from all three by introducing a KL-divergence-based training objective where the 'teacher' is the same model with context appended rather than a separate larger model or a fixed factual statement. Unlike model editing methods that handle simple factual updates, or continual learning that relies on NWP loss over compiled corpora, SELF-PARAM uses diverse QA pairs generated by an instruct model to minimize the distributional gap between context-conditioned and context-free predictions. The 'self-updatable' framing distinguishes it from standard knowledge distillation (Choi et al., Padmanabhan et al.) by eliminating the need for a stronger teacher model, as confirmed by the ablation using the corresponding instruct model instead of gpt-4o-mini.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-storage requirement with near-optimal efficacy (approaching the Base, C+Q upper bound in batch injection) offers a practical path for deploying LLMs in dynamic environments where external memory modules are infeasible. However, sequential injection reveals meaningful degradation (QA-F1 dropping from ~0.5 to ~0.3 over 20 steps), and the ~2× computational overhead relative to fine-tuning limits rapid-update scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models, how it handles genuinely novel or adversarial contexts, and whether the KL objective can be made more robust to the hallucination risk the authors acknowledge. The complete failure of NWP-based fine-tuning (FT(C) scoring 0.0000 in conversational recommendation) is a useful negative result for the continual learning community.
