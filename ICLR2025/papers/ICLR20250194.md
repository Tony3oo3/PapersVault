## Problem

Existing temporal knowledge evaluation for LLMs relies on fixed single time-point snapshots, failing to capture how knowledge evolves across time spans. This limitation is especially acute in domains with gradual or object-level changes (e.g., biomedical research updates, amended legal regulations) where a model must track which specific object attribute was correct at each point in a continuous timeline, not just at one isolated timestamp.

## Input / Output

The evaluation pipeline consumes (s, r, o, t) quadruplets from CHROKNOWBENCH across five domains, paired with 4 few-shot exemplars sampled per relation type, and is scored via three elicitation templates: free-form Generation, 4-option MCQA, and True/False. For CHROKNOWPROMPT, the input additionally includes the most representative objects from adjacent years (previous and next spans) appended as in-context demonstrations around the target year. Outputs are categorized into Correct, Partial Correct, or Incorrect using rapidfuzz token_set_ratio (threshold 70) for fuzzy string matching, with CHROKNOWPROMPT further upgrading Partial Correct to Chrono-Correct when the iteratively refined candidate matches the ground-truth object.

## Method

CHROKNOWBENCH constructs a chronologically accumulated knowledge benchmark by tracking object-level changes (e.g., a player's team, a drug's indication) while keeping subject and relation fixed, sourced from Wikidata, UMLS, CFR, CSKG, and Math-KG, and partitioned into dynamic (object changed within the time frame) and static (no change) subsets. CHROKNOWLEDGE adapts the SliCK sampling framework by appending a temporal component t to the triplet, merging MaybeKnown/WeaklyKnown into Partial Correct, and evaluating under both greedy (τ=0) and temperature (τ=0.7) decoding across 5 few-shot exemplar combinations. CHROKNOWPROMPT is a non-parametric chronological prompting algorithm that, for a target year t_n where the model gives a Partial Correct answer, systematically traverses preceding years (t_{n-1}, t_{n-2}, …) and subsequent years (t_{n+1}, t_{n+2}, …) within a predefined span, appending each year's majority-voted correct object as an in-context exemplar above or below the target prompt. At each step the model generates or verifies-and-refines a candidate answer, and after completing both backward and forward traversals, the final candidate is checked against the ground-truth object; a match reclassifies the knowledge as Chrono-Correct. The approach requires no parameter updates, making it applicable to both open-source and proprietary LLMs, and achieves the largest gains (11.5% average) in the biomedical domain where object changes are gradual and the temporal context is most informative.

## Results

On CHROKNOWBENCH, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct achieves the highest dynamic general-domain scores (28.7% Correct in 2022), while GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-1.5-flash lead in biomedical (51.9% and 49.0% respectively). All models exhibit a clear performance drop in recent years (2022–2023), reflecting pretraining data cutoffs, with dynamic knowledge showing steeper declines than static. CHROKNOWPROMPT yields an average 11.5% increase in the Known category for unchanged objects in the biomedical domain, 2.8% in general, and 3.1% in legal, but only achieves 10–30% of the unchanged-object improvement on changed objects in dynamic datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare CHROKNOWPROMPT against external prompting or editing baselines; instead, it benchmarks 11 LLMs (9 open-source, 2 proprietary) against each other on CHROKNOWBENCH. Among open-source models, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct consistently outperforms smaller models (e.g., 28.7% vs. 9.6% for Llama-3.1-8B on dynamic general knowledge in 2022), while proprietary GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-1.5-flash show competitive but lower scores than the 70B open-source model in the general domain. Template-wise, MCQA and TF consistently outperform Generation in recent years, with the gap widening over time (e.g., in biomedical, MCQA maintains ~50% while Generation drops below 30% by 2023). CHROKNOWPROMPT's total-span traversal nearly doubles the biomedical improvement over previous-span-only (12.0% vs. 6.7%), and the method is ineffective on chat models (Pythia-Chat-Base-7B shows ~0% gain), indicating that instruction-tuning is a prerequisite for the temporal prompting to work. The legal domain remains the hardest: even the best model (GPT-4o-mini) achieves only 3.2% on dynamic legal knowledge, and ChroKnowPrompt yields negligible gains (+0.0 to +1.9) on changed legal objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SliCK sampling-based knowledge categorization framework (Gekhman et al., 2024) by appending a temporal component to the subject-relation-object triplet and reclassifying categories into Correct, Partial Correct, and Incorrect. It departs from prior temporal knowledge evaluation work (TimeQA, TemporalWiki, CarpeDiem, DyKnow, Set the Clock) that typically assesses knowledge at single fixed timestamps, instead tracking object-level evolution across multi-year time frames and distinguishing dynamic from static temporal states. The CHROKNOWPROMPT method draws on non-parametric knowledge editing techniques (MeLLo, IKE) but repurposes them for chronological traversal rather than single-fact correction, and contrasts with TKGs by prioritizing individual knowledge element trajectories over event-snapshot aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHROKNOWBENCH provides the first multi-domain benchmark that explicitly separates time-variant from time-invariant knowledge and tracks object-level temporal evolution, filling a gap where prior evaluations treated temporal knowledge as a single-snapshot problem. The finding that LLMs predominantly exhibit 'Partial Known' rather than complete temporal recall, and that CHROKNOWPROMPT recovers only 10–30% of changed-object performance compared to unchanged objects, highlights a fundamental limitation of prompting-based approaches for genuinely evolving knowledge. Open questions include whether parametric editing techniques (ROME, MEMIT, LoRA-based) can complement prompting for dynamic knowledge, and how to handle unstructured, context-rich domains like legal text where current methods struggle most.
