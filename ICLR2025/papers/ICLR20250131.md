## Problem

Most deep clustering methods assume balanced or roughly balanced class distributions, yet real-world datasets follow long-tailed (Zipf) distributions that cause severe performance degradation. Without supervision, distinguishing head-class from tail-class samples is nearly impossible, and existing long-tailed learning techniques (re-sampling, re-weighting, logit adjustment) are all label-dependent, leaving long-tailed deep clustering largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images drawn from a long-tailed distribution (e.g., CIFAR-10/20 at 32×32, STL-10 at 96×96, Tiny ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet-LT at 224×224) with imbalance ratios ranging from 5 to 100. Each image is augmented twice and passed through a ResNet-18 (or ResNet-50 for ImageNet-LT) backbone to produce 2N ℓ₂-normalized representations per batch. The final output is a set of learned representations that are clustered post-hoc via K-means (averaged over 10 random seeds), yielding cluster assignments evaluated by ACC, CAA, NMI, and ARI.

## Method

ConMix extends Manifold Mixup to contrastive deep clustering by performing multi-sample linear interpolation at the representation level rather than the input level. Within each batch, original representations are randomly tagged into M groups (uniform distribution), and each group's representations are averaged and ℓ₂-normalized to synthesize a new representation; the same tagging is applied to both augmentation branches so that synthesized pairs remain positive. Unlike prior work that uses mixed features only as hard negatives, ConMix feeds the synthesized representations directly into the NT-Xent contrastive loss as both positives and negatives, effectively averaging over multiple samples per contrastive pair. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 1) that this multi-sample mixing implicitly reduces the loss contribution of head-class samples and increases that of tail-class samples, achieving loss re-balancing without any label information. Training follows the SimCLR pipeline (ResNet-18, SGD lr=0.5, cosine decay, τ=0.2) with an optional 200-epoch SDCLR warmup before switching to ConMix for the remaining 800 epochs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with imbalance ratio 5, ConMix achieves 61.6% ACC, 65.4% CAA, 59.8% NMI, and 45.6% ARI, outperforming the strongest baseline IDFD (56.7% ACC, 63.0% CAA) by 4.9 and 2.4 points respectively. On CIFAR-20 (IR=5), ConMix reaches 42.8% ACC and 43.9% NMI, surpassing ProPos (40.7% ACC, 42.6% NMI). On STL-10 (IR=5), ConMix attains 48.9% ACC and 48.8% NMI, exceeding DMICC (45.9% ACC, 45.3% NMI) by 3.0 and 3.5 points. The advantage persists at IR=10 and even at IR=100, where ConMix achieves 53.4% NMI and 33.5% ARI on CIFAR-10 versus the next-best BYOL at 43.1% NMI and 25.3% ARI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConMix comprehensively outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric at both IR=5 and IR=10. The closest competitor is IDFD on CIFAR-10 (IR=5), where ConMix leads by 4.9 points in ACC (61.6 vs 56.7) and 8.0 in ARI (45.6 vs 37.1); on CIFAR-20, ProPos is the nearest rival with ConMix ahead by 2.1 ACC and 1.3 NMI. General self-supervised methods (SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL) and the long-tailed self-supervised method SDCLR are consistently outperformed by 10–20+ points in NMI and ARI, confirming that generic representation learning is insufficient under long-tailed conditions. Deep clustering methods that assume balanced data (CC, CoNR) suffer severe degradation on long-tailed distributions—CC drops to 16.0% ACC on CIFAR-20 and CoNR to 23.8%—while ConMix maintains competitive performance. On the extreme IR=100 setting, ConMix's NMI advantage over BYOL widens to 10.3 points (53.4 vs 43.1), and on Tiny ImageNet (200 classes, IR=10) ConMix still leads all methods in ACC (16.5%) and NMI (34.9%). On ImageNet-LT, ConMix achieves 15.4% ACC and 51.6% NMI, surpassing all deep clustering baselines (IDFD 4.42%, CoNR 6.38%, DMICC 5.24%) and matching or exceeding the best self-supervised baseline BYOL (14.8% ACC, 50.6% NMI).

## Relation to Prior Work

ConMix sits at the intersection of three well-developed threads: contrastive representation learning (SimCLR, SDCLR), feature-space interpolation (Manifold Mixup), and long-tailed learning (re-weighting, logit adjustment). It departs from prior long-tailed methods by being entirely label-free, and from prior mixup methods by operating at the representation level with multi-sample averaging rather than pairwise input-level blending. The closest prior work, P²OT (Zhang et al., 2024), addresses imbalanced clustering but relies on a pre-trained DINO backbone and fine-tunes only the last ViT block, whereas ConMix trains a ResNet from scratch, making the long-tailed bias more severe and the correction more necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-tailed deep clustering from scratch is, as the authors note, almost uncharted, and this paper provides the first principled method with a theoretical guarantee (implicit loss re-balancing) for the setting. The practical impact is currently limited: performance still lags well behind supervised long-tailed recognition, and experiments are confined to small-scale datasets (CIFAR, STL-10, Tiny ImageNet) with at most 200 classes. Open questions include whether the multi-sample mixup strategy transfers to other modalities or to even more extreme imbalance ratios, and whether combining ConMix with stronger clustering objectives (as their ConMix+ProPos variant suggests) can close the gap to supervised methods.
