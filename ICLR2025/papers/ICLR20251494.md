## Problem

Controllable image generation, signal estimation, and joint generation are typically addressed by separate specialized methods (ControlNet, Marigold, LDM3D), each requiring distinct architectures or training procedures. Prior unified attempts like JointNet and UniGD introduce significant complexity through multi-stage training, inpainting-mask inputs, or architectural modifications that hinder adoption. The paper asks whether a single, simple diffusion model trained in one stage with minimal added parameters can handle all these tasks through flexible inference-time sampling alone.

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of correlated images (e.g., RGB + depth, RGB + edge, RGB + pose, or two frames of the same identity) encoded into the VAE latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.5 at 512×512 resolution, along with a text prompt. The key design choice is that the two inputs are fed as a batch into the same UNet with independently sampled noise levels, so at inference one branch can remain clean (t=0) while the other denoises from pure noise. Depending on the sampling schedule, the output is a generated image, an estimated condition map (depth/edge/pose), or a jointly generated image-condition pair; multiple UniCon models can also be combined for multi-signal conditioning.

## Method

UniCon adapts a pretrained latent diffusion model by adding joint cross-attention modules in parallel to the self-attention layers of the UNet transformer blocks, where features from the image branch and condition branch attend to each other bidirectionally with separate projection matrices. LoRA adapters (rank 64) are applied to the condition branch (for non-natural-image modalities like depth or edges) and to the joint cross-attention projections, adding only ~15% parameters while keeping the base model frozen. The critical training innovation, inspired by Diffusion Forcing, is to independently sample diffusion timesteps for the image and condition inputs (Eq. 6), so the model learns to denoise at any combination of noise levels. At inference, this disentangled training enables diverse behaviors without explicit masks: setting the condition timestep to 0 yields clean-condition generation, adding noise to the condition enables coarse/rough control, denoising the condition branch yields estimation, and denoising both jointly produces paired outputs. A guidance term (Eq. 7) further supports partial conditioning by steering noisy latents toward reconstructing masked condition regions.

## Results

On conditional generation (Table 1), UniCon achieves FID-6K of 13.21 (depth), 12.80 (softedge), and 17.51 (pose), outperforming ControlNet (13.68, 13.46, 18.61) and substantially beating Readout-Guidance (18.72, 18.43, 21.07) across all three spatially-aligned conditions. For condition fidelity, UniCon reaches 9.26% AbsRel (depth), 2.28 EMSE (softedge), and 61.97 PCK@0.1 (pose), again surpassing both baselines. On zero-shot depth estimation (Table 2), UniCon achieves 7.9 AbsRel / 93.9% δ1 on NYUv2 and 9.2 / 91.9% on ScanNet, outperforming JointNet and DPT on NYUv2 but trailing Marigold (6.0 / 95.9% on NYUv2). On the more diverse DA-2K benchmark, UniCon-Depth reaches 90.5% accuracy, surpassing Marigold (86.8%), Geowizard (88.1%), and ZoeDepth (89.1%), though below its own annotator Depth Anything V2 (97.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCon outperforms ControlNet on every metric in Table 1: FID-6K improves by 0.47 (depth), 0.66 (softedge), and 1.10 (pose), while condition fidelity gains are 0.59% AbsRel, 0.02 EMSE, and 4.43 PCK points respectively. Against Readout-Guidance, the margins are much larger (e.g., 5.51 FID and 13.93% AbsRel on depth), confirming that the joint-distribution formulation captures image-condition correlation more effectively than feature-readout guidance. For depth estimation, UniCon beats JointNet by a wide margin (7.9 vs 13.7 AbsRel on NYUv2; 9.2 vs 14.7 on ScanNet) and surpasses DPT on NYUv2 (7.9 vs 9.8) but trails it on ScanNet (9.2 vs 8.2). UniCon also outperforms MiDaS on both NYUv2 and ScanNet. The main loss is to Marigold on NYUv2/ScanNet depth estimation (7.9 vs 6.0 AbsRel), which fine-tunes the entire diffusion model solely for depth; however, UniCon reverses the result on the more diverse DA-2K benchmark (90.5% vs 86.8%), suggesting better generalization. UniCon also beats Geowizard (88.1%), Depth Anything V1 (88.5%), and ZoeDepth (89.1%) on DA-2K, while remaining below Depth Anything V2 (97.1%), which serves as its own training annotator. In the user study (Fig. 5), UniCon achieves comparable human preference to specialized methods (ControlNet, Readout-Guidance, IP-Adapter-Face), and qualitative comparisons (Figs. 3, 13) show UniCon producing fewer over-saturated or misaligned outputs than ControlNet and Uni-ControlNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two key ideas: latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) as the base architecture, and the Diffusion Forcing paradigm of training with independent noise levels per input, which the authors repurpose from sequence modeling to image-condition pairs. Conceptually, it departs from the ControlNet lineage (feature residual, input concatenation) by using a lighter joint cross-attention mechanism that supports non-spatially-aligned conditions, and from prior unified models like JointNet and LDM3D by eliminating the need for explicit inpainting masks or multi-stage training. The LoRA-based parameterization positions it as a plug-in adapter rather than a full model fine-tune, distinguishing it from Marigold's approach of fine-tuning the entire diffusion model for a single estimation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single model trained in ~13 hours with only 15% additional parameters can handle controllable generation, estimation, joint generation, and coarse conditioning through different inference-time sampling schedules, reducing the need to maintain separate specialized models. However, the inference speed penalty (5.02 vs. 10.82 iter/s compared to ControlNet) and the instability on loosely correlated pairs (identity, appearance) limit immediate deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex multi-modal conditions, whether the inference overhead can be resolved through LoRA weight fusion or LCM integration, and whether the joint distribution formulation generalizes beyond the image-condition pairs tested here.
