## Problem

Existing methods for combining multiple diffusion models rely on static parameter merging (e.g., Weighted Merging, MBW, autoMBW), which assigns fixed weights regardless of the generation state. However, the denoising capabilities of different models dynamically shift across prompts, initial noises, denoising steps, and spatial locations, meaning no single static weighting can capture each model's strengths. This paper asks whether a dynamic, state-aware ensembling strategy can outperform both individual models and static merging approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes N pre-trained U-Net denoisers (same architecture, e.g., SDv1.5 with 12 down-sampling, 1 middle, and 12 up-sampling blocks), a text prompt, and sampled Gaussian noise as input, and produces a single generated image (256×256 for COCO 2017 evaluation, 512×512 for Draw Bench). At each denoising step and each U-Net block, the SABW aggregator consumes the intermediate feature maps from all N denoisers and outputs a single unified feature map of identical shape, so the ensembled pipeline is drop-in compatible with standard diffusion scheders (DDIM, DPM-Solver) and Classifier-Free Guidance.

## Method

The core contribution is the Spatial-Aware Block-Wise (SABW) feature aggregator, a lightweight module (~50M trainable parameters) inserted between each pair of corresponding U-Net blocks across N frozen denoisers. For each block, SABW concatenates the N output feature maps, applies a ResNet layer to inject the timestep embedding and a Transformer layer with cross-attention to incorporate the text prompt, then projects through a zero-initialized convolution to produce a per-model spatial attention map (softmax over N channels at each spatial location). The final block output is a weighted sum of the N model features using these spatially-varying attention weights, enabling each model to dominate at the spatial positions and timesteps where it is strongest. Training uses the standard denoising loss with only SABW parameters optimized (AdamW, lr=1e-4) on 10K JourneyDB samples for 10 epochs, while all base model weights remain frozen.

## Results

On COCO 2017 (Group I), AFA achieves a FID of 9.76, substantially outperforming the best base model (12.32) and the best baseline MagicFusion (10.53). On Draw Bench Prompts (Group I), AFA reaches an Image Reward (IR) of 0.4388, surpassing the best base model (0.3922) and all baselines. For Group II, AFA achieves FID 10.27 and IR 0.4892, again leading all base models and baselines across every reported metric (FID, IS, CLIP-I, CLIP-T, AES, PS, HPSv2, IR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFA outperforms all four baselines (Wtd. Merging, MBW, autoMBW, and MagicFusion) on every metric in both model groups. Against MagicFusion — the only other ensembling-based method — AFA improves FID from 10.53 to 9.76 (Group I) and from 11.63 to 10.27 (Group II), while boosting IR from 0.3317 to 0.4388 and from 0.4194 to 0.4892 respectively. The merging-based baselines are inconsistent: for example, Wtd. Merging achieves IR 0.3909 in Group I (below the best base model's 0.3922) yet reaches IR 0.4387 in Group II (above the best base model's 0.4238), illustrating the limitation of fixed parameter weights. autoMBW performs worst among all baselines in Group I (FID 13.35, IR 0.3672), even falling below the base models, likely due to its reliance on an aesthetic classifier for weight selection. AFA's advantage is most pronounced on IR (human preference), where it gains approximately 0.05–0.07 over the best base model and 0.04–0.09 over the strongest baseline, indicating that dynamic feature-level aggregation better captures complementary strengths across models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on combining multiple pre-trained diffusion models, departing from static parameter-merging strategies (Weighted Merging, MBW, autoMBW) that assign fixed weights to all U-Net blocks, and from MagicFusion which fuses only the final predicted noises. The key conceptual departure is the observation that denoising capabilities of individual models shift dynamically across prompts, timesteps, and spatial locations, motivating a spatially-aware, block-wise feature aggregator (SABW) conditioned on all of these states. The approach also contrasts with Mixture-of-Experts formulations for diffusion (eDiff-I, ERNIE-ViLG 2.0, MEME) that route to a single expert per timestep, whereas AFA blends all models' intermediate features at every block with learned spatial attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AFA offers a practical, low-parameter (~50M trainable) recipe for combining existing open-source SD models without retraining them, and its notable robustness to reduced inference steps (maintaining quality at 20 steps where base models degrade sharply) provides a genuine efficiency lever for deployment. Open questions remain: ensembling models with different architectures is explicitly out of scope, distillation to very few steps (LCM) failed under frozen denoisers, and gains on already-strong larger models (SDXL, FLUX) are marginal. The work also raises the broader question of whether dynamic feature-level fusion can be made as computationally cheap as static merging while preserving its quality advantages.
