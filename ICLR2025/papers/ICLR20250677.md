## Problem

While prior work established that neural networks efficiently learn low-dimensional projections in standard regression, the implications of feature learning under adversarial perturbations remained unexplored. This paper asks whether neural networks can maintain their statistical adaptivity to low-dimensional structure when trained for ℓ₂-robustness, and whether robust learning incurs additional sample complexity beyond standard learning in high dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250677/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples (x, y) where x ∈ ℝ^d follows a multi-index model E[y|x] = g(Ux) with k ≪ d orthonormal target directions, and produces a two-layer ReLU network f(x; a, W, b) = a^T σ(Wx + b) whose population adversarial risk under ℓ₂-bounded perturbations of budget ε is within ε of the Bayes optimal. The adversary budget ε is assumed constant-order relative to d, and the key structural assumption is that the multi-index coordinates Ux are statistically independent from the orthogonal complement x̃Ux.

## Method

The central result (Theorem 1) proves that under ℓ₂ perturbations and squared loss, the Bayes optimal robust predictor has the form h(Ux) — the same low-dimensional projection as in standard learning — so robust learning reduces to (i) recovering the target subspace U via a standard feature learning oracle (e.g., gradient-based training of the first layer with a standard loss), then (ii) fixing W and b and training only the second-layer weights a by minimizing the empirical adversarial risk. This two-phase procedure (Algorithm 1) yields sample and neuron complexities for the robust phase that are independent of the ambient dimension d, making adversarially robust learning statistically as easy as standard learning. Concrete oracle implementations are provided via gradient-based algorithms from Lee et al. (2024) for single-index polynomials and Damian et al. (2022) for multi-index polynomials, giving end-to-end guarantees with total sample complexity Õ(d/ε̃²) for single-index and Õ(d² + d/ε̃^O(k)) for multi-index models.

## Results

The central theoretical result is that adversarially robust learning of multi-index models is statistically as easy as standard learning: the additional number of samples needed for robustness is independent of the ambient dimension d. Specifically, for Gaussian single-index polynomial models (Corollary 9), the total sample complexity is Õ(d/ε̃²) for fixed-degree polynomial targets and Õ(d/ε̃^O(1)) for pseudo-Lipschitz targets. For multi-index polynomial models (Corollary 11), the total sample complexity is Õ(d² + d/ε̃²) for polynomial targets and Õ(d² + d/ε̃^O(k)) for pseudo-Lipschitz targets, where the d² term comes from the feature learning phase. Numerically, initializing the first layer from the target direction u (or from standard training) yields substantially lower adversarial test error than full adversarial training from random initialization across ReLU, tanh, and He2 link functions (Figure 1).

| Setting | Function Class | Total Sample Complexity | Adversary Budget |

*Key: ε̃ = ε ∧ (ε²/AR*). The d-dependent term comes from the feature learning phase (recovering U); the remaining term is independent of d and comes from the robust second-layer training phase.*

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily theoretical and does not benchmark against competing robust learning methods on standard datasets. The main comparison is between adversarial training strategies: (1) full adversarial training of all layers from random initialization, (2) standard training first (ε=0) followed by adversarial training of the second layer, and (3) fixing the first layer to the true target direction u. In the numerical experiments (Figure 1, d=100, N=100, ε=1), initializing from u or from standard training consistently achieves lower adversarial test error than full adversarial training from random initialization, across ReLU, tanh, and He2 link functions. The theoretical contribution establishes that the standard-training-then-robust-tuning procedure achieves the same sample complexity as standard learning (the robustness overhead is dimension-free), which is the key comparison to the 'naive' adversarial training approach that would naively incur d-dependent complexity. The gap between single-index (d) and multi-index (d²) sample complexity is noted as an open question related to the efficiency of recovering multiple hidden directions simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed theoretical threads: (1) feature learning in two-layer neural networks for multi-index models (Damian et al. 2022; Lee et al. 2024; Oko et al. 2024; Ba et al. 2022), which shows gradient-based training recovers low-dimensional target projections, and (2) adversarial robustness theory (Madry et al. 2018; Javanmard et al. 2020; Schmidt et al. 2018), which studies the statistical and computational costs of robust learning. The key departure is showing that these threads are compatible: the same target subspace U that enables dimension-independent standard learning also provides the Bayes-optimal projection for ℓ2-robust learning, so a two-phase procedure (standard feature learning + robust second-layer training) inherits the dimension-free sample complexity of standard learning. Prior robustness work (e.g., Javanmard & Mehrabi 2024) noted that low-dimensional structure mitigates the robust/standard tradeoff, but focused on population-level analysis with known structure; this paper provides finite-sample, algorithmic guarantees via explicit neural network oracles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that adversarially robust learning of multi-index models requires no additional dimension-dependent samples beyond standard learning—reframes robustness as a second-layer tuning problem rather than a fundamentally harder representation-learning task. This provides a clean theoretical justification for the practical heuristic of standard-training first, then adversarially fine-tuning. Key limitations include the statistical independence assumption (Assumption 1), restriction to ℓ2 perturbations, and primarily Gaussian data; the authors explicitly flag ℓ∞ attacks, the behavior of fully adversarial multi-layer training, and sharper ε-dependence as open problems. For the theory community, the paper establishes a template for combining feature learning oracles with robust optimization, and the DFL/SFL oracle abstractions are reusable for other robustness settings.
