## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods either assume a closed label space (no unknown classes) or operate on a single modality, leaving the combined challenge of multimodal open-set test-time adaptation (MM-OSTTA) unaddressed. In real-world scenarios like autonomous driving, a pretrained multimodal model must adapt online to a target domain that simultaneously exhibits distribution shifts and contains previously unseen classes, a setting where standard entropy-minimization approaches like Tent actively degrade unknown-class detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252792/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled multimodal test batches online: for action recognition, video clips, audio clips, and optical flow frames; for 3D semantic segmentation, LiDAR point clouds and camera images. For each sample it produces a class prediction over the known source classes plus a Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP) score used with a threshold to flag unknown-class samples; for segmentation, per-point class labels and an unknown-detection score. Adaptation updates only the last feature-encoder layer per modality and the final classifier (or batch-norm parameters for segmentation), keeping the bulk of the pretrained network frozen.

## Method

AEO is built on the observation that the entropy gap between known and unknown target samples strongly correlates with open-set detection quality (lower FPR95). The first component, UAE, computes a per-sample adaptive weight W_ada = Tanh(β·(H(p̂)−α)) that automatically switches the entropy objective: samples above the entropy threshold α have their entropy maximized (likely unknown), samples below have it minimized (likely known), and samples near the threshold receive near-zero weight to avoid corrupting adaptation. The second component, AMP, extends this to each modality's individual predictions and adds an adaptive discrepancy term that maximizes inter-modality prediction disagreement for high-entropy (unknown) samples while enforcing agreement for low-entropy (known) samples, thereby amplifying the final fused prediction's uncertainty for unknowns. A negative-entropy diversity loss L_Div over accumulated batch predictions prevents collapse. The total loss L_AEO = L_AdaEnt + γ₁(L_AdaEnt* + L_AdaDis) + γ₂ L_Div is optimized with Adam on each incoming mini-batch, requiring no labels and no source-data access.

## Results

On EPIC-Kitchens (video+audio, 6 domain shifts), AEO achieves a mean H-score of 56.18, improving over the Source baseline (34.11) by 22.07% and the best baseline READ (37.66) by 18.52 points. On the HAC dataset (video+audio), AEO reaches a mean H-score of 48.31 vs. 34.61 for Source and 42.71 for READ. On the Kinetics-100-C corruption benchmark (severity level 5), AEO attains a mean H-score of 60.07, a 22.41% improvement over Source. For 3D semantic segmentation on nuScenes, AEO achieves H-scores of 55.51 (Day→Night) and 63.87 (USA→Singapore), outperforming all baselines including MM-TTA and READ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AEO outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest unimodal open-set TTA method UniEnt, AEO improves H-score by 11.33 points on EPIC-Kitchens (56.18 vs. 44.85) and 5.60 points on HAC (48.31 vs. 37.48), with the gap driven primarily by a much lower FPR95 (48.65 vs. 66.07 on EPIC-Kitchens). Against the SOTA multimodal TTA method READ, AEO gains 18.52 H-score points on EPIC-Kitchens and 1.32 points on Kinetics-100-C (60.07 vs. 58.75), while also achieving superior AUROC (85.58 vs. 69.86 on EPIC-Kitchens). The unimodal TTA baselines Tent and SAR actually degrade performance below the Source baseline in the open-set multimodal setting (H-score drops to 16.52 and 22.48 respectively on EPIC-Kitchens), confirming that naive entropy minimization is harmful when unknown classes are present. On the nuScenes 3D segmentation task, AEO surpasses MM-TTA by 4.52 H-score points (Day→Night) and 3.00 points (USA→Singapore), while also reducing FPR95 by up to 8.22% over Source. In long-term and continual settings, AEO is the only method that maintains or improves H-score over multiple adaptation rounds, whereas Tent, SAR, and OSTTA suffer severe degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out the intersection of two existing research threads—open-set test-time adaptation (OSTTA, UniEnt) and multimodal test-time adaptation (READ, MM-TTA)—that have previously been studied in isolation. It departs from unimodal open-set TTA by showing that their core assumptions (e.g., confidence-based filtering in OSTTA, embedding-space quality in UniEnt) do not transfer to multimodal settings, and from closed-set multimodal TTA by introducing the challenge of unknown classes. The AEO framework repurposes the entropy-minimization paradigm from Tent but inverts it adaptively per-sample via a tanh-gated weight, and extends the optimization to modality-level prediction discrepancies, a mechanism not present in prior unimodal or multimodal TTA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing MM-OSTTA and providing a multi-modality, multi-task benchmark, the paper gives the community a concrete testbed for evaluating adaptation methods under the realistic assumption that target domains contain unseen categories. The demonstration that AEO remains stable in long-term and continual settings (where most baselines collapse) is practically important for deployed perception systems in autonomous driving and robotics. Open questions include whether the entropy-difference objective generalizes to more than two modalities in a principled way, how the method behaves when the unknown-class ratio is extreme or unknown a priori, and whether the approach extends to transformer-based multimodal backbones beyond the CNN architectures tested here.
