## Problem

Conventional Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neurons in spiking neural networks suffer from rapid membrane potential decay that impairs long-term dependency capture and prevents effective processing of multi-timescale temporal dynamics. Existing dual-compartment models (e.g., TC-LIF, LM-H) lack a clear mechanistic separation of frequency components between compartments and provide no theoretical stability or frequency-response guarantees, limiting their effectiveness in complex forecasting tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251006/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×C} (T historical timesteps, C variables) and produces a forecast Y ∈ R^{L×C} for the next L steps. The raw input is converted into a spike train S ∈ R^{T_s×T×C} via a convolutional layer, batch normalization, and the TS-LIF neuron, where T_s timesteps are aligned with the history length. The final output integrates both dendritic and somatic spike streams through a learnable weighted sum, allowing the network to exploit complementary low- and high-frequency representations.

## Method

TS-LIF introduces a dual-compartment spiking neuron where the dendritic compartment (α₁ ≈ 1) functions as a low-pass filter capturing slow trends, while the somatic compartment (α₂ ≈ 0) acts as a high-pass filter tracking rapid fluctuations. Two key mechanisms differentiate it from prior dual-compartment models: (1) a direct somatic current injection term (1−α₂)c[t] that bypasses the dendritic pathway to prevent information loss, and (2) dendritic spike generation s_d that enables multi-scale feature extraction beyond the somatic output alone. The combined spike output s_mix = κs_d + (1−κ)s_s uses a learnable mixing coefficient per channel. The authors derive closed-form eigenvalue stability conditions (|λ| < 1 for both eigenvalues of the system matrix) and Z-transform transfer functions H_d(z), H_s(z) that analytically confirm the frequency-separation behavior. The neuron is plugged into TCN, GRU, and iTransformer backbones and trained end-to-end with standard supervised loss on four benchmark forecasting datasets.

## Results

On four benchmark time series forecasting datasets (Metr-la, Pems-bay, Solar, Electricity) with prediction horizons of 6, 24, 48, and 96 steps, TS-former (iTransformer + TS-LIF) achieves the best average R² of 0.783 and RSE of 0.441, with an average rank of 3.5/3.3 across all 16 configurations. TS-GRU achieves average R² of 0.778 and RSE of 0.460, while TS-TCN reaches R² of 0.777 and RSE of 0.473. The most notable gains appear in long-horizon forecasting: on the Electricity dataset at 96-step prediction, TS-GRU reduces RSE by 43.6% and TS-former by 25.0% compared to their LIF-based counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-LIF consistently outperforms its direct LIF-based counterparts across all three backbone architectures: TS-former beats iSpikeformer by +0.008 R² and −0.035 RSE on average, TS-GRU beats Spike-GRU by +0.019 R² and −0.057 RSE, and TS-TCN beats Spike-TCN by +0.007 R² and −0.045 RSE. Against ANN baselines, TS-former surpasses iTransformer (R² 0.783 vs 0.776; RSE 0.441 vs 0.480) and Autoformer (R² 0.783 vs 0.753; RSE 0.441 vs 0.569), while TS-GRU substantially outperforms the vanilla GRU (R² 0.778 vs 0.733; RSE 0.460 vs 0.573). In the SOTA comparison (Appendix Table 7), TS-former achieves the best R² on Metr-la at all prediction lengths (e.g., 0.620 vs SparseTSF's 0.576 and SAMformer's 0.549 at length 24), though SparseTSF retains a slight edge on Electricity at length 24 (R² 0.991 vs 0.985). Among competing dual-compartment LIF variants (Appendix Table 8), TS-LIF outperforms TC-LIF, LM-H, and CLIF in the GRU backbone on both Metr-la and Electricity across all prediction lengths, with the largest margin on Electricity at length 6 (R² 0.991 vs 0.978 for TC-LIF). The delayed XOR experiment further confirms TS-LIF's long-term memory advantage, achieving 99.4% accuracy at 10-step delay versus 74.8% for standard LIF and 50% for ReLU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-compartment spiking neuron lineage initiated by TC-LIF (Zhang et al., AAAI 2024) and LM-H (Hao et al., 2024), which introduced dendritic-somatic separation for long-term sequential modeling but lacked explicit frequency-domain differentiation between compartments. TS-LIF departs from these predecessors by adding two key mechanisms: direct somatic current injection (a shortcut that prevents high-frequency information loss during dendritic-to-somatic transmission) and dendritic spike generation, enabling each compartment to act as a distinct low-pass or high-pass filter. The forecasting framework itself is built on the SNN-based TCN/GRU/Transformer architectures from Lv et al. (ICML 2024), into which TS-LIF is plugged as a drop-in neuron replacement. Unlike prior multi-compartment models, TS-LIF provides a formal stability proof and Z-transform-derived transfer functions that explicitly characterize the frequency response of each compartment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that biologically motivated compartmentalization with explicit frequency separation can substantially improve SNN-based time series forecasting, particularly for architectures (GRU) that are inherently weaker at capturing long-range dependencies, where TS-LIF yields ~50% RSE reduction under missing data. However, the practical impact is tempered by several limitations: the model does not address cross-variable correlations in multivariate settings, the delayed XOR experiment shows accuracy collapsing to near-chance at 50-step delays, and the energy savings (60–75%) are typical of SNNs generally rather than unique to TS-LIF. Open questions include whether the dual-compartment frequency decomposition generalizes to other time series tasks (anomaly detection, generation, classification) and whether SNN-specific mechanisms can efficiently model inter-variable dependencies without resorting to full attention.
