## Problem

As VLMs are increasingly used to evaluate other VLMs, the reliability of these model-based judges remains underexplored. A natural remedy—aggregating judgments from multiple VLMs via collective intelligence—has not been tested when the judge pool mixes reliable and unreliable models, leaving open whether such aggregation actually improves or degrades evaluation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253193/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes video-question pairs from CVRR-ES (2,400 open-ended QA pairs across 11 visual dimensions, videos averaging 22.3 s) along with candidate VLM responses and reference answers. Each judge produces a 1–5 integer rating with reasoning; the system then computes Weighted Cohen's Kappa (quadratic weighting) between each judge and the LLM Agent-Debate consensus to quantify per-dimension reliability, and the final GPT-4o judge outputs a consolidated assessment that integrates the video, question, candidate response, and all individual VLM reviews.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-stage evaluation framework: (1) individual VLM judges (Video-LLaVA, LLaMA-VID, GPT-4o mini, InternVL2, GPT-4o) independently score candidate responses; (2) a reference-guided LLM Agent-Debate (GPT-4o + GPT-3.5 in text-only mode, multiple debate rounds) produces a consensus rating serving as the reliability ground truth; (3) a collective thought stage where GPT-4o aggregates all VLM reviews plus the video-question-response tuple into a final assessment, and a Mixture-of-Judges variant that dynamically selects the top-k most reliable judges per visual dimension using a Weighted Cohen's Kappa threshold. The key empirical finding is that indiscriminate aggregation of heterogeneous judges does not improve—and can degrade—agreement with the reference, because unreliable judges inject noise that swamps the signal. An ablation fine-tuning Video-LLaVA further shows that improving a model's video comprehension alone is insufficient to make it a reliable judge, as its rating distribution remains skewed high and its Kappa scores stay far below GPT-4o's.

## Results

On the CVRR-ES benchmark (2,400 QA pairs, 11 visual dimensions), GPT-4o as a sole VLM judge achieves the highest Weighted Cohen's Kappa agreement with the reference Agent-Debate method, averaging 33.11–60.38% across dimensions (substantial agreement). In stark contrast, Video-LLaVA and LLaMA-VID as judges show near-zero or negative Kappa (−4.15 to 3.70%), indicating no meaningful agreement beyond chance. The Collective Thought approach (aggregating all five VLM judges) yields average Kappa of only 2.72–42.53%, which is lower than GPT-4o used alone, and the Mixture of Judges strategy (selecting top-reliability judges per dimension) reaches 2.72–60.70%—still not surpassing GPT-4o's solo performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five individual VLM judges plus two aggregation strategies against a reference-guided LLM Agent-Debate method. GPT-4o as a sole judge dominates all baselines: it achieves 60.38% Kappa on Social Context and 55.18% on Visual Context, while Video-LLaVA (−1.83 to 2.81%) and LLaMA-VID (−4.15 to 3.70%) show essentially no agreement with the reference. The Collective Thought approach, which pools reviews from all five VLMs (including unreliable ones) and passes them to GPT-4o for a final assessment, actually degrades performance—its average Kappa (2.72–42.53%) falls below GPT-4o's solo scores (23.09–60.38%). The Mixture of Judges strategy, which dynamically selects only high-Kappa judges per dimension, improves slightly over Collective Thought (up to 60.70% on one dimension) but still does not consistently outperform GPT-4o alone. On the secondary VideoChatGPT dataset (1,000 samples), the same pattern holds: GPT-4o achieves 44.42% average Kappa versus 1.72% for Video-LLaVA and 25.66% for InternVL2, confirming that less capable VLMs systematically overrate candidates and are unreliable as judges regardless of dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-judge paradigm (Zheng et al., Kocmi & Federmann) into the video domain, where prior evaluation relied on human experts or surface-level metrics like BLEU/ROUGE. It repurposes the collective intelligence / wisdom-of-crowds principle from social psychology as a concrete aggregation strategy for VLM judges, and introduces a reference-guided multi-agent debate as a reliability anchor. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first systematic empirical study of VLMs judging VLMs, departing from prior work that assumed model-as-judge reliability without testing it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GPT-4o alone outperforms GPT-4o paired with a group of less reliable judges directly challenges the default assumption in evaluation pipelines that aggregation improves reliability. This has immediate practical implications for anyone building VLM evaluation or RLHF reward-signal systems: indiscriminate ensembling can degrade rather than improve judgment quality. Open questions include whether iterative multi-round debate among VLM agents (rather than one-shot aggregation) can recover the collective benefit, and whether the Agent-Debate baseline's dependence on reference answers limits its use as a gold standard in open-ended settings.
