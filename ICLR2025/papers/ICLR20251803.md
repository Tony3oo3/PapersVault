## Problem

Existing data pruning methods face a fundamental trade-off: score-based approaches select redundant high-scoring samples while ignoring diversity, geometry-based methods maximize spread but discard informative samples, and hybrid methods like D²-Pruning rely on greedy message passing that gets trapped in local optima. No prior method formulates coreset selection as a globally optimized problem that simultaneously accounts for individual sample informativeness and pairwise information overlap without heuristic sequential decisions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a training dataset of N samples along with two precomputed quantities: a sample-wise information vector I ∈ ℝ^N (from supervised scores like EL2N/loss/gradient norm, or unsupervised SSP scores with DINO features; for LLMs, LESS influence scores with gradient features) and an N×N pairwise feature similarity matrix K (inner product of encoder features). The output is a binary selection vector identifying the top-p samples by selection probability, where p is the target coreset budget. The similarity matrix is sparsified to retain only top-k nearest-neighbor entries (k=5), and the dataset can be partitioned into d random subsets processed in parallel, reducing per-subset complexity to O(Nk/d²).

## Method

InfoMax formulates coreset selection as a discrete quadratic programming problem: maximize the sum of individual sample information minus a weighted pairwise redundancy term (α·K), subject to a cardinality constraint of exactly p selected samples. The key theoretical contribution is proving that under the Graph Cut Conditional Gain (GCCG) instantiation from submodular information measures, this quadratic objective is exactly equivalent to the information-theoretic set-level information maximization problem, unifying score-based and diversity-based methods as degenerate special cases (α→0 and α→∞ respectively). To solve the combinatorial problem at scale, InfoMax applies a continuous relaxation (binary → [0,1]^N) and derives an iterative Softmax update rule via the proximal gradient method: X^{t+1} = Softmax(p·I − 2pα·KX^t), which converges at rate O(1/T). Scalability to millions of samples is achieved through sparsification of K to top-k neighbors and dataset partitioning into d parallel subsets, enabling processing of 12M samples in ~37 minutes.

## Results

On image classification, InfoMax achieves 89.1% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 58.7% on CIFAR-100, and 59.0% on ImageNet-1K at a 10% selection rate, outperforming the strongest baseline D²-Pruning by 2.0%, 1.8%, and 3.4% respectively. In vision-language pre-training on CC12M (10% selection), InfoMax surpasses D²-Pruning by 1.5–2.7% across four downstream CLIP tasks (zero-shot ImageNet, linear probing, I2T/T2I Flickr30K retrieval). For LLM instruction tuning (5% selection), InfoMax achieves 47.2% on BBH versus 41.7% for D²-Pruning (a 5.5-point gain) and 51.8% on MMLU versus ~49.8% for D²-Pruning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoMax consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every task and selection rate. Against score-based methods (EL2N, Entropy, Forgetting, SSP, Margin), the gap widens dramatically at high pruning rates: at 10% selection on CIFAR-10, InfoMax (89.1%) exceeds EL2N (30.3%) by 58.8 points and Forgetting (46.3%) by 42.8 points, confirming that pure score ranking collapses without diversity. Against geometry-based K-center (Sener & Savarese), InfoMax gains 20.7 points on CIFAR-10 at 10% (89.1% vs 68.4%), showing that diversity alone without importance scoring is insufficient. Among hybrid methods, InfoMax beats CCS (Zheng et al.) by 1.4–1.7% and D²-Pruning (Maharana et al.) by 1.8–3.4% on CIFAR-100/ImageNet at 10% selection, with the advantage growing as the pruning ratio increases. In multi-modality pre-training, InfoMax surpasses the widely-used Clustering+CLIP baseline (Li et al.) by 2.1–4.0% and D²-Pruning over CLIP scores by 1.0–2.7% on four downstream tasks at 10% selection. For LLM instruction tuning, InfoMax outperforms LESS (Xia et al.) by 1.1–1.5 points on TYDIQA and D²-Pruning by up to 5.5 points on BBH at 5% selection, demonstrating that the quadratic formulation avoids the greedy suboptimality inherent in D²-Pruning's iterative message-passing.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoMax builds on the submodular information measure framework (Wei et al., 2015; Kothawade et al., 2021), specifically the Graph Cut Conditional Gain instantiation, to provide a theoretical justification for formulating coreset selection as a discrete quadratic programming problem. It departs from the dominant greedy paradigm in hybrid pruning methods (notably D2-Pruning's iterative message-passing on a sample graph) by instead solving the selection problem holistically via a continuous relaxation and a proximal-gradient Softmax solver derived from mean-field inference (Baqué et al., 2016; Tan et al., 2021). The method is agnostic to the choice of intra-sample score (EL2N, SSP, LESS, CLIP score) and pairwise similarity, positioning it as a general-purpose solver layer atop existing scoring and feature-extraction pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing greedy per-sample selection with a holistic quadratic optimization, InfoMax consistently outperforms the prior SOTA (D2-Pruning) across image classification, vision-language pre-training on 12M pairs, and LLM instruction tuning, with the largest gains (≈5.5% on BBH) at aggressive 5% selection rates where greedy methods degrade most. The sparsification and partitioning strategies make the approach practical at 12M scale (37 minutes), though the authors acknowledge that billion-scale validation remains open. A key open question is whether the pairwise redundancy model (captured by the quadratic term) is sufficient or whether higher-order information overlap among selected samples would further improve selection quality at very low budgets.
