## Problem

Vision Transformers (ViT) removed the spatial hierarchy of ConvNets but retained the locality inductive bias through patchification (e.g., 16×16 patches) and position embeddings. It remained unclear whether locality is truly fundamental for vision or merely a useful heuristic. This paper asks whether a Transformer can operate directly on individual pixels with zero 2D grid priors and still achieve competitive or better performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252152/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and treats them as unordered sets of individual pixels (e.g., 1024 tokens for 32×32 CIFAR-100, 50,176 tokens for 224×224 ImageNet), with no assumption of spatial adjacency. Outputs span three task types: class labels for supervised and self-supervised classification, depth maps for regression on NYU-v2 (48×64 inputs), and 256×256 generated images via a DiT operating on 32×32×4 VQGAN latent tokens. The sequence length equals H×W, making it substantially longer than standard patch-based ViTs.

## Method

The core modification is replacing ViT's patch projection (e.g., 16×16 or 2×2) with a 1×1 linear projection from R³ to R^d per pixel, combined with learnable position embeddings that encode no 2D grid structure, yielding a permutation-equivariant, locality-free Transformer. This degenerated-patch variant is evaluated across three case studies: supervised ViT classification, MAE self-supervised pre-training followed by fine-tuning, and DiT-based image generation on VQGAN latent space. The key finding is that removing locality consistently improves quality over patch-based baselines (e.g., ViT-S/1 outperforms ViT-S/2 by 1.3% Acc@1 on CIFAR-100; DiT-L/1 achieves lower FID than DiT-L/2). Ablations further show that patchification imposes a stronger locality prior than position embedding type, and that pixel permutation within patches causes far more performance degradation than removing position embeddings entirely.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, the locality-free ViT-S/1 achieves 86.4% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the patch-based ViT-S/2 (83.7%) by 2.7 points. On ImageNet (28×28 inputs), ViT-L/1 reaches 76.9% top-1 vs. 75.6% for ViT-L/2, with consistent gains across all model sizes. For image generation on ImageNet, DiT-L/1 achieves 4.05 FID and 232.95 IS, surpassing DiT-L/2 (4.16 FID, 210.18 IS). On NYU-v2 depth estimation, ViT-S/1 reduces RMSE from 0.80 to 0.72 compared to ViT-S/2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard ViT with 2×2 patches (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021), the locality-free pixel variant (ViT/1) consistently wins across all model sizes and datasets: +1.5% Acc@1 on CIFAR-100 (ViT-T), +1.2–1.3% on ImageNet (ViT-S through ViT-L), +0.5% on Oxford-102-Flowers, and −0.08 RMSE on NYU-v2. Against DiT-L/2 (Peebles & Xie, 2023), the pixel-based DiT-L/1 achieves lower FID (4.05 vs. 4.16), lower sFID (4.66 vs. 4.97), and higher IS (232.95 vs. 210.18) with matched precision/recall, and the gap widens with extended training (2.68 vs. 2.89 FID at 1400 epochs). The paper also notes that Shen et al. (2023) reports only 72.6% Acc@1 with a larger ViT-B/2 on CIFAR-100, whereas the authors achieve 80%+ with smaller models, underscoring that the locality-free design is not merely matching but substantially exceeding well-optimized patch-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequence-length scaling literature (Beyer et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2022) to its logical extreme by eliminating patchification entirely, treating each pixel as a token. It departs from the ViT paradigm (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) that cemented 16×16 patches as standard, and from iGPT (Chen et al., 2020a) which also operated on pixels but underperformed due to insufficient scale and resolution. The DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and MAE (He et al., 2022) serve as the generative and self-supervised backbones, while the pixel-permutation ablation in Section 5 draws on Brendel & Bethge (2019) to isolate locality from location equivariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that locality is not a necessary inductive bias for vision challenges a foundational design assumption and reframes patchification as a purely efficiency-driven heuristic rather than a representational necessity. However, the practical impact is currently limited by quadratic attention cost, and the ImageNet classification results rely on low-resolution inputs (28×28 or 64×64), leaving open whether the advantage persists at full 224×224 resolution with sufficient compute. The pixel-permutation experiments reveal that location equivariance (weight sharing) remains critical even after locality is removed, suggesting the next generation of architectures must preserve this bias while exploring locality-free tokenization. Open questions include whether efficient attention mechanisms (FlashAttention, Ring Attention) can make pixel-level Transformers practical at scale, and whether the advantage generalizes to higher-resolution or video settings.
