## Problem

Rate-distortion-optimized compression for 3D Gaussian Splatting requires entropy coding of sparse, unorganized Gaussian primitives in 3D space, a problem that remains largely unexplored. Early attempts borrowing the hyperprior framework from learned image compression assume factorial (independent) hyperprior components, failing to exploit the inter-correlation (spatial) and intra-correlation (within-primitive) inherent in Gaussian attributes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250946/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes ScaffoldGS anchor points (3D positions, 50-dim latent features, K offsets, and 6-dim scaling factors) and produces a compressed bitstream containing PCA-oriented multi-scale triplanes, entropy-coded anchor attributes, a binary mask, and MLP network weights (positions in 16-bit float, weights in 32-bit float). The decoder reconstructs Gaussian primitives from the bitstream and renders 2D RGB images at arbitrary camera viewpoints via the ScaffoldGS rasterization pipeline.

## Method

CAT-3DGS projects unorganized anchor points onto multi-scale triplanes oriented by PCA of their 3D positions, converting the sparse point cloud into ordered 2D grids that capture spatial correlation. A spatial autoregressive model (SARM) then entropy-codes the triplane hyperprior by exploiting this inter-correlation, unlike prior factorial hyperpriors. For the anchor latent features, a channel-wise autoregressive model (CARM) with uneven slice partition (5, 10, 15, 20 channels) exploits intra-correlation among feature components, reducing feature bitstream size by ~40%. A view frequency-aware masking mechanism weights each Gaussian primitive by its rendering frequency during training, making it harder to mask frequently-used primitives and thus preserving rendering quality at low bit rates. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a rate-distortion objective plus a mask regularization loss.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, CAT-3DGS (high-rate) achieves 27.77 dB PSNR at 12.35 MB, matching HAC's PSNR (27.77 dB) while reducing file size by 43% (vs. 21.87 MB). Compared to vanilla 3DGS (744.7 MB, 27.49 dB) and ScaffoldGS (253.9 MB, 27.50 dB), CAT-3DGS achieves 78× and 26× rate reductions respectively while delivering 0.16–0.27 dB higher PSNR. Across all four datasets (Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks & Temples, Deep Blending, BungeeNeRF), CAT-3DGS consistently achieves the best rate-distortion trade-off among all rate-distortion-optimized methods, with the largest gains at low bit rates where view frequency-aware masking prunes less critical anchors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest rate-distortion-optimized baseline HAC, CAT-3DGS matches or exceeds PSNR while substantially reducing file size: on Mip-NeRF 360 (high-rate), both achieve 27.77 dB PSNR but CAT-3DGS uses 12.35 MB vs. HAC's 21.87 MB (43% smaller); on Tanks & Temples, CAT-3DGS achieves 24.41 dB at 6.93 MB vs. HAC's 24.40 dB at 11.24 MB (38% smaller); on Deep Blending, CAT-3DGS reaches 30.29 dB at 3.56 MB vs. HAC's 30.34 dB at 6.35 MB (44% smaller at a negligible 0.05 dB cost). Against RDO-Gaussian, CAT-3DGS achieves 0.72 dB higher PSNR at 47% smaller size on Mip-NeRF 360 (27.77 vs. 27.05 dB; 12.35 vs. 23.46 MB). Against CompGS, CAT-3DGS delivers 0.51 dB higher PSNR at 25% smaller size on Mip-NeRF 360 (27.77 vs. 27.26 dB; 12.35 vs. 16.5 MB). Against the non-entropy-coded compact representations (EAGLES, LightGaussian, Compact3DGS, Compressed3D, Trimming the fat), CAT-3DGS achieves comparable or higher PSNR at 2–5× smaller file sizes, demonstrating the benefit of joint rate-distortion-optimized entropy coding. ContextGS, a concurrent work, is shown in Figure 4 to be outperformed by CAT-3DGS across the rate-distortion curve, with the gap attributed to CAT-3DGS's triplane-based spatial autoregressive hyperprior and channel-wise autoregressive feature coding.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAT-3DGS builds directly on ScaffoldGS's anchor-based representation and borrows the triplane concept from K-Planes, but repurposes it as a hyperprior for entropy coding rather than as a scene representation. It departs from concurrent rate-distortion-optimized 3DGS compression methods (HAC, ContextGS, CompGS) in two key ways: (1) replacing factorial hash-grid or per-anchor hyperpriors with PCA-guided multi-scale triplanes that preserve spatial structure, enabling spatial autoregressive coding of the hyperprior itself; and (2) introducing channel-wise autoregressive coding to exploit intra-correlation within individual Gaussian primitive features, which no prior 3DGS compression method attempts. The hyperprior framework itself is inherited from learned image compression (Ballé et al., 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first methods to simultaneously exploit both inter-primitive (spatial) and intra-primitive (channel-wise) correlations for 3DGS compression, establishing a more complete coding framework for the emerging rate-distortion-optimized 3DGS compression task. The finding that triplanes outperform hash grids by ~18% BD-rate because they preserve spatial structure (unlike pseudo-random hash mappings) offers a design principle applicable to other structured 3D representations. Limitations include higher decoding latency due to autoregressive models (though rendering throughput is actually improved via masking), and the view frequency-aware masking assumes training and test view distributions are similar. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large or dynamic scenes, and whether the autoregressive decoding bottleneck can be further mitigated through parallelization.
