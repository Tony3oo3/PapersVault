## Problem

No existing machine learning framework can simultaneously address both protein-ligand docking (predictive) and pocket-aware 3D drug design (generative), despite their inherent duality where improved docking directly benefits drug design. Additionally, modeling 3D information is difficult because protein/ligand structures contain discrete atomic information while spatial coordinates are continuous values, and ground-truth complex data remains scarce (fewer than 20,000 in PDBbind).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250988/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For docking, the model takes a protein pocket (atom types + normalized 3D coordinates) and a ligand SMILES string as input, and autoregressively generates the ligand's 3D binding pose coordinates. For drug design, it takes only the pocket and generates both the ligand SMILES and its 3D coordinates. The key format is a parallel sequence: a discrete token sequence (atom types or SMILES tokens) aligned element-wise with a continuous numerical sequence (x, y, z coordinates), where non-coordinate positions are padded with 1.0. All coordinates are normalized by translating the center of mass to the origin and scaling by a factor q=5 to ensure numerical stability.

## Method

3DMolFormer is a dual-channel autoregressive transformer (GPT-2 small, 92M parameters) that processes the parallel sequence format: at input, each token embedding is multiplied by its corresponding numerical value before positional encoding; at output, a standard token prediction head runs in parallel with a numerical head that predicts the next floating-point coordinate. The model is pre-trained on 228M mixed samples (3.2M pockets, 209M conformations, 167K complexes) using a composite cross-entropy + MSE loss, then fine-tuned separately for each task. For docking, supervised fine-tuning minimizes MSE on ligand coordinates only. For drug design, RL fine-tuning uses a regularized MLE loss (Eq. 4) with a composite reward (Vina Dock + QED + SA), and critically exploits task duality by using the docking-finetuned weights to generate 3D coordinates while the RL agent generates SMILES tokens, decoupling the two generation stages.

## Results

On protein-ligand docking (CASF-2016, 285 test samples), 3DMolFormer achieves an average RMSD of 1.29 Å, outperforming Uni-Mol (1.62 Å) and Smina (1.84 Å), with 96.4% of predictions below 3.0 Å and 98.8% below 5.0 Å. On pocket-aware 3D drug design (100 CrossDocked2020 pockets), 3DMolFormer achieves a Vina Dock score of −9.48 (vs. −8.39 for DecompDiff, the previous best) and an 85.3% multi-objective Success Rate (Vina Dock < −8.18, QED > 0.25, SA > 0.59), far exceeding the dataset reference of 25.0% and the next-best method (DecompDiff at 24.5%). The model also achieves a mean Delta Score of 0.716 (vs. 0.437 for Pocket2Mol) and a Clash Score of 3.25 (vs. 4.59 for reference), indicating higher binding specificity and physical plausibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On docking (CASF-2016), 3DMolFormer outperforms all five search-based baselines (AutoDock4, AutoDock Vina, Vinardo, Smina) and the deep-learning baseline Uni-Mol on average RMSD (1.29 vs. 1.62 for Uni-Mol, 1.84 for Smina) and on the %<2.0Å, %<3.0Å, and %<5.0Å thresholds, with the largest gap over Uni-Mol at 96.4% vs. 87.0% for %<3.0Å. The sole exception is %<1.0Å, where Smina (47.4%) edges out 3DMolFormer (43.8%), suggesting search-based methods still capture some ultra-precise poses. On drug design (CrossDocked2020), 3DMolFormer dominates on Vina Dock (−9.48 vs. −8.39 for DecompDiff, −7.80 for TargetDiff, −7.15 for Pocket2Mol), SA (0.78 vs. 0.74 for Pocket2Mol), and Success Rate (85.3% vs. 24.5% for DecompDiff), while Pocket2Mol retains a slight QED advantage (0.56 vs. 0.49). On the PoseBusters benchmark, 3DMolFormer in pocket-aware mode achieves 81.5% %<2.0Å, surpassing Uni-Mol pocket-aware (74.8%) and all blind-docking baselines including AlphaFold3 (76.3%) and Chai-1 (77.1%). Ablation results confirm that removing pre-training drops docking %<3.0Å from 96.4% to 78.1%, and removing RL fine-tuning collapses drug design Success Rate from 85.3% to 2.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

3DMolFormer builds on the GPT-2 autoregressive transformer architecture and the xVal technique of encoding continuous values by multiplying them with token embeddings, repurposing both for 3D molecular data. It departs from the dominant equivariant deep learning paradigm (Uni-Mol, Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff, DecompDiff) by using a non-equivariant dual-channel transformer that handles discrete tokens and continuous coordinates in parallel sequences, and from token-based 3D coordinate representations (XYZ-transformer, BindGPT, Token-Mol) by preserving coordinate continuity. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the predictive-generative duality between docking and drug design within a single framework, using docking-finetuned weights to generate 3D coordinates during the RL-based drug design process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify both core SBDD tasks (docking and pocket-aware 3D drug design) and explicitly leverage their duality, which prior work treated as separate problems. The 85.3% multi-objective success rate (vs. 24.5% for the prior best) and the implicit acquisition of SE(3)-equivariance through data augmentation rather than architectural constraints are practically significant. However, the framework does not model protein flexibility, dynamic binding processes, or environmental factors (temperature, pH), which remain core challenges in computational SBDD. Future work on multi-objective RL and incorporating protein conformational changes could further close the gap to experimental accuracy.
