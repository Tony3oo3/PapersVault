## Problem

Existing zeroth-order (ZO) fine-tuning methods like MeZO approximate gradients using full-rank Gaussian perturbation matrices, which fundamentally mismatch the low-rank structure of true first-order gradients observed during LLM fine-tuning. This rank discrepancy degrades performance, and the full-rank nature of ZO gradients makes momentum-based variants prohibitively memory-expensive, negating the memory savings that motivate ZO optimization in the first place.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (RoBERTa-large, OPT-13B/30B/66B, LLaMA) and a standard loss function evaluated via forward passes only—no backpropagation or activation storage is required. It produces the full fine-tuned model weights (not low-rank adapters), with the only additional stored state being small rank-r matrices U_ℓ ∈ R^{m_ℓ×r} and V_ℓ ∈ R^{n_ℓ×r} per layer (or their random seeds), making the memory overhead negligible relative to the model size. The lazy sampling interval ν and rank r are the key hyperparameters controlling the trade-off between subspace exploration and stability.

## Method

LOZO replaces the full-rank Gaussian perturbation in standard ZO-SGD with a low-rank product UV^T (where U ∈ R^{m×r}, V ∈ R^{n×r}, r ≪ min(m,n)), yielding a gradient estimator that is inherently low-rank and structurally aligned with true FO gradients. A lazy sampling strategy fixes V for ν consecutive iterations while resampling U each step, allowing the algorithm to thoroughly explore a single low-rank subspace before transitioning—this prevents abrupt parameter changes and ensures the cumulative gradient over ν steps remains low-rank. The momentum variant LOZO-M exploits the fixed-V structure to store momentum as a compact m×r matrix N^t rather than a full m×n gradient, projecting N onto the new subspace only when V is resampled. Theoretically, LOZO is shown to be equivalent to a ZO subspace optimization method that alternates between random low-rank subspaces, yielding an O(1/√T) convergence guarantee under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions.

## Results

On OPT-13B (1000 examples), LOZO achieves 91.7% on SST-2, 70.4% on RTE, 89.0% on COPA, and 84.9% on SQuAD, outperforming MeZO (91.3%, 68.2%, 88.0%, 81.8%) and MeZO-LoRA (89.6%, 67.9%, 84.0%, 82.1%) on most tasks while approaching full fine-tuning (FT) performance (91.8%, 70.9%, 79.0%, 84.9%). On RoBERTa-large, LOZO-M achieves 62.7% on MNLI and 74.0% on SNLI with only 2.84 GB memory, compared to MeZO-Adam's 62.6% and 72.7% at 7.42 GB. On OPT-66B, LOZO reaches 92.5% on SST-2 and 74.0% on RTE, surpassing MeZO (92.0%, 71.5%) and ICL (89.3%, 65.3%). LOZO also converges faster, requiring roughly half the training epochs of MeZO on OPT-66B/WiC to reach the same loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeZO, LOZO wins on 9 out of 11 OPT-13B tasks, with the largest gains on MultiRC (+3.6), SQuAD (+3.1), and RTE (+2.2); the only losses are on DROP (−0.6) and a tie on ReCoRD. Against MeZO-LoRA, LOZO wins on 8 of 11 tasks, with particularly large margins on COPA (+5.0), MultiRC (+1.7), and SQuAD (+2.8), but loses on BoolQ (−1.6), ReCoRD (−0.2), and DROP (−0.6). Compared to full fine-tuning (FT), LOZO is within 1% on SST-2, RTE, WSC, and SQuAD, and actually exceeds FT on COPA (+10.0) and ReCoRD (+7.2), though it trails on CB (−14.5), WiC (−9.3), and MultiRC (−8.1). On RoBERTa-large, LOZO-M matches or exceeds MeZO-Adam on MNLI (62.7 vs 62.6) and SNLI (74.0 vs 72.7) while using 62% less memory (2.84 GB vs 7.42 GB). The paper also notes that LOZO converges roughly 2× faster than MeZO on OPT-66B/WiC, requiring only half the training epochs to reach the same loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MeZO line of work (Malladi et al., 2023), which adapted classical ZO-SGD (Ghadimi & Lan, 2013) and Spall's simultaneous perturbation (1992) to memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning. The key departure is replacing the full-rank Gaussian perturbation matrix in RGE with a low-rank product UV^T, motivated by the well-documented low-rank structure of true gradients during LLM fine-tuning. The lazy sampling strategy—fixing V for ν iterations—introduces a subspace-optimization interpretation that provides convergence guarantees, and the momentum variant (LOZO-M) exploits the fixed-V structure to store only a small N matrix rather than a full-rank momentum, a trick not available in standard ZO-SGD-M.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners constrained by GPU memory (especially in long-context fine-tuning), LOZO offers a practical path to near-FO performance at roughly the same memory cost as MeZO, with the added benefit of cheap momentum. The subspace-optimization equivalence gives theoretical grounding that LOZO directly optimizes the full parameter X (unlike MeZO-LoRA, which only optimizes adapters), explaining its consistent empirical edge. Open questions remain: integrating LOZO with adaptive optimizers like Adam without incurring full-rank memory overhead, and understanding why minor loss fluctuations appear late in training under the lazy sampling schedule.
