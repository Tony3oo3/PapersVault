## Problem

Existing LLM self-training methods (STaR, ReST, ReST^EM) only generate step-by-step solutions, training the model to memorize task-specific reasoning rather than capturing the abstract meta-knowledge needed for generalization across similar problems. This leaves open the fundamental question of what essential information self-generated synthetic data should encapsulate to enable robust transfer on challenging reasoning benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251883/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language problem as input and produces a two-round structured output: first an anticipatory plan (a short, high-level abstraction of the solution path that explicitly excludes problem-specific details like calculations or the correct answer), followed by a detailed step-by-step solution that must be consistent with both the plan and the original problem. During data generation, the plan is iteratively refined via self-reflection until it successfully guides the LLM to a correct solution, with a maximum of 4 reflection trials per problem.

## Method

LEPA is a self-training algorithm that iterates between a data generation phase and a model optimization phase. In data generation, the LLM is prompted to first produce an anticipatory plan (constrained to be general meta-knowledge applicable to similar problems, with no problem-specific information), then generate a solution conditioned on both the plan and the problem; if the solution is incorrect, a self-reflection step analyzes the failure and produces a refined plan that is re-tested, repeating until correctness or a trial limit. In model optimization, supervised fine-tuning minimizes the negative log-likelihood of jointly predicting the refined plan and the corresponding solution, formatted as a two-round conversation. The key differentiator from baselines is that the anticipatory plan serves as an abstract solution-generation blueprint that reduces cognitive load, enables generalizable meta-knowledge transfer, and—by excluding the correct answer from the plan—prevents the information-bypassing failure mode that plagues STaR-style correction.

## Results

LEPA achieves an average test accuracy of 73.9% across four reasoning benchmarks (Hellaswag, Hendrycks MATH, BoolQ, PIQA), outperforming the best baseline (ReST^EM at 70.8%) by 3.1 percentage points. On the most challenging benchmark, Hendrycks MATH, LEPA reaches 30.2% accuracy versus 28.2% for ReST and 25.9% for STaR. The largest gain is on Hellaswag, where LEPA scores 91.2% versus 86.4% for ReST^EM (+4.8%). Even without self-training, the LEPA prompt (Plan+CoT) improves the initial Llama 3 8B Instruct over zero-shot CoT on three of four benchmarks, suggesting the anticipatory plan structure itself provides a reasoning benefit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEPA consistently outperforms all three self-training baselines (ReST, ReST^EM, STaR) across all four benchmarks. Against ReST (rejection sampling with iterative fine-tuning), LEPA gains +4.9% on Hellaswag (91.2% vs. 86.3%), +2.0% on Hendrycks MATH (30.2% vs. 28.2%), +3.9% on BoolQ (88.4% vs. 84.5%), and +4.5% on PIQA (85.9% vs. 81.4%). Against ReST^EM (rejection sampling fine-tuning from the initial LLM each iteration), LEPA leads by +4.8%, +3.0%, +2.1%, and +2.4% respectively. Against STaR (self-correction with correct answers), LEPA wins by +5.5%, +4.3%, +2.6%, and +1.7%. The zero-shot CoT prompt (Kojima et al.) serves as a non-self-training reference; LEPA's self-training lifts performance by 13–31 points over this baseline depending on the task. Notably, STaR shows the largest degradation on Hendrycks MATH (25.9%), attributed to false-positive solutions where incorrect rationales coincidentally produce correct final answers.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEPA extends the self-training paradigm (STaR, ReST, ReST^EM) by introducing anticipatory plans as an intermediate abstraction layer between the problem and the step-by-step solution, drawing on Plan-and-Solve Prompting's plan-before-answering idea but embedding it within an iterative self-training loop with self-reflection. Unlike prior self-training methods that only generate and train on step-by-step solutions, LEPA frames the generated data as (plan, solution) pairs where the plan is explicitly constrained to be problem-agnostic meta-knowledge. The self-reflection mechanism for plan optimization departs from STaR's answer-conditioned correction by isolating the correct answer from the plan, preventing the information-bypassing failure mode identified in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEPA addresses a fundamental but underexplored question in LLM self-training—what information should self-generated data encapsulate—by arguing that abstract, generalizable meta-knowledge (plans) is more valuable than task-specific solution traces. The practical impact is modest (1.7–4.8% over best baselines on 8B models), and the approach has not been validated on larger or more advanced LLMs. Open questions include whether the gains persist at scale, how to automatically determine when planning is beneficial versus wasteful, and whether RL-based optimization (briefly explored with REINFORCE) can unlock substantially larger improvements.
