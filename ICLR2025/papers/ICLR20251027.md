## Problem

Existing interpretable GNNs either focus on local subgraphs (missing long-range dependencies) or reduce graphs to a single fixed granularity via global coarsening (e.g., GIP), failing to capture the multi-level structural relationships that real-world graph tasks inherently exhibit. This paper addresses the overlooked challenge of multi-granular interpretability: how to simultaneously explain model decisions across varying levels of graph abstraction, from fine-grained local substructures to coarse-grained global patterns.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with node feature matrix X and adjacency matrix A as input and produces a class label along with a sequence of selected root-to-leaf paths through a hierarchical tree, where each path node at a given depth corresponds to a coarsened graph at a specific granularity level. The interpretability output is structured as a tree traversal: at each of L layers, the model selects one of M perturbed child nodes, yielding a path sequence {k̂¹, k̂², …, k̂ᴸ} that encodes which structural variant was most informative at each granularity. The final embedding is taken from the leaf node of the selected path and passed through a scoring function for classification.

## Method

TIF transforms a plain GNN into a hierarchical tree with three cooperating modules. The hierarchical graph coarsening module iteratively applies GCN layers followed by soft pooling (MLP + softmax to produce clustering assignment matrices S), generating progressively coarser graphs that form the tree's depth. The learnable graph perturbation module adds M learnable perturbation matrices per parent node to the clustering assignment, creating diverse child branches (tree breadth) regularized by similarity (staying close to the original) and diversity (differing across branches) losses. The adaptive routing module assigns an MLP-based router to each non-leaf node that scores and selects the most informative child path, with entropy regularization encouraging exploration of multiple paths. The total loss combines cross-entropy classification, edge-preservation (link) loss, perturbation regularization, and entropy regularization, and the selected root-to-leaf path sequence directly constitutes the multi-granular explanation.

## Results

On prediction performance, TIF achieves the best accuracy on 5 of 8 datasets: D&D (84.19%), MUTAG (94.44%), GraphCycle (84.77%), GraphFive (64.35%), and MultipleCycle (69.04%), while remaining competitive on ENZYMES (58.66%) and COLLAB (77.29%). On explanation accuracy, TIF leads on D&D (89.11%), PROTEINS (87.62%), GraphCycle (93.12%), and MultipleCycle (86.95%), and is within ~1% of the best on the remaining datasets. TIF also demonstrates superior path consistency and balanced path importance compared to the Bi-Tree ablation, confirming the benefit of the multi-branch tree structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest global-based competitor GIP, TIF wins on 5 of 8 datasets in accuracy (D&D: 84.19 vs 79.32; MUTAG: 94.44 vs 91.21; GraphCycle: 84.77 vs 82.15; GraphFive: 64.35 vs 60.38; MultipleCycle: 69.04 vs 68.72) but trails slightly on ENZYMES (58.66 vs 60.61) and COLLAB (77.29 vs 77.49). Against subgraph-based interpretable GNNs (ProtGNN, KerGNN, π-GNN, GIB, GSAT, CAL), TIF achieves higher accuracy on 6 of 8 datasets and higher F1 on 4, with the largest margins on D&D (+5.07 over GSAT) and GraphFive (+4.77 over GIB). Against standard GNNs (GCN, DGCNN, DiffPool, RWNN, GraphSAGE), TIF matches or surpasses on most datasets, with notable gains on D&D (+2.63 over DiffPool) and GraphCycle (+3.36 over DGCNN). On explanation accuracy (Table 2), TIF leads on D&D (89.11), PROTEINS (87.62), GraphCycle (93.12), and MultipleCycle (86.95), and is within 1–2% of GIP on the remaining datasets. The Bi-Tree ablation (simplified binary tree with linear routers) consistently shows lower path consistency and more skewed path importance than TIF, confirming the value of the multi-branch perturbation and MLP-based adaptive routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIF builds directly on GIP's (Wang et al., 2024) learnable soft graph coarsening mechanism and DiffPool's (Ying et al., 2018) hierarchical pooling equations, but departs from both by organizing coarsened graphs into a multi-branch tree rather than a single linear hierarchy. It also repurposes the Neural Decision Tree (NDT) concept from the CNN literature (Wan et al., 2021; Tanno et al., 2019) and transplants it into the GNN setting, using graph coarsening for depth and learnable perturbation for breadth. The key conceptual departure from prior interpretable GNNs (subgraph-based methods like GNNExplainer/ProtGNN, and global-based methods like GIP) is the explicit multi-granular framing: rather than explaining at one fixed level (local subgraph or single coarsened graph), TIF produces a sequence of explanations at progressively coarser granularities along a root-to-leaf path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-granular interpretability framing is genuinely useful for domains like protein classification where relevant structure spans functional groups, amino acids, and whole domains simultaneously, and the tree structure provides a natural, human-readable format for such layered explanations. However, the evaluation methodology is somewhat circular—explanation accuracy is measured by feeding generated explanations back into a trained GNN, which limits confidence in the interpretability claims. The paper is also limited to relatively small graphs (up to ~376 average nodes) and simple synthetic datasets, leaving open whether the approach scales to large-scale graphs without prohibitive computational cost, a limitation the authors acknowledge for future work.
