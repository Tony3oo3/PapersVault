## Problem

Previous work established that satisfiability for basic, linear, and polynomial languages across Pearl's Causal Hierarchy has uniform complexity (NP or ∃R-complete) when marginalization is expressed by explicit expansion. However, the exact complexity when the compact summation operator Σ is used for marginalization was largely unknown: the basic and linear cases with Σ were unexplored at all three PCH levels, and the counterfactual polynomial case with Σ was left open by van der Zander et al. (2023). This paper resolves both gaps, revealing that marginalization via Σ induces a strictly increasing complexity ladder across the hierarchy.

## Input / Output

The input is a formula in a PCH language: a Boolean combination of (in)equalities of terms (basic, linear, or polynomial) at a specified layer (probabilistic, interventional, or counterfactual), where terms may include the unary summation operator Σ over a dummy variable ranging over the domain. The output is a yes/no decision on whether there exists a Structural Causal Model satisfying the formula (satisfiability) or whether it holds for all SCMs (validity). The summation operator is the critical design choice: it allows polynomial-size encoding of marginalization that would otherwise require exponential expansion, and it is precisely what drives the complexity jump from NP to NP^PP/PSPACE/NEXP for basic and linear languages.

## Method

The paper proves exact completeness by reducing canonical problems matched to each complexity level: E-MajSat (NP^PP-complete) for the probabilistic layer, QBF (PSPACE-complete) for the interventional layer, and Schönfinkel-Bernays sentences (NEXP-complete) for the counterfactual layer. The key mechanism is that Σ combined with Boolean events in P(δ) enables counting satisfying assignments (yielding PP-hardness), encoding quantifier alternation via causal ordering and interventions (yielding PSPACE-hardness), and encoding first-order relations via counterfactual comparisons of multiple interventions (yielding NEXP-hardness). Upper bounds use a polynomial-size model property (at most m nonzero probability entries for m constraints), PP oracles for evaluating sums, and for the polynomial case, the NEXP_real machine model characterization of succ-∃R combined with the ∃R algorithm of Mossé et al. (2022) applied to the exponentially expanded instance, with Tseitin's trick handling conditional probabilities and subtractions.

## Results

The paper establishes the first strictly increasing complexity across Pearl's Causal Hierarchy: for basic and linear languages with the marginalization (summation) operator, satisfiability is NP^PP-complete at the probabilistic layer, PSPACE-complete at the interventional layer, and NEXP-complete at the counterfactual layer. For polynomial languages with marginalization, satisfiability is succ-∃R-complete at all three layers, resolving an open problem from van der Zander et al. (2023) regarding the counterfactual level. Without marginalization, all three layers share the same complexity (NP for basic/linear, ∃R for polynomial), so the summation operator is what drives the complexity separation.

|---|---|---|---|

| linear | NP | NP | NP |

| basic + marginalization | NP^PP | PSPACE | NEXP |
| linear + marginalization | NP^PP | PSPACE | NEXP |
| polynomial + marginalization | succ-∃R | succ-∃R | succ-∃R |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is with prior complexity results that showed no separation across PCH levels. Fagin et al. (1990) proved NP-completeness for basic/linear satisfiability at the probabilistic layer, and Mossé et al. (2022) extended this to show NP-completeness at all three layers for basic/linear and ∃R-completeness for polynomial terms — all without the summation operator. The key finding is that adding the marginalization operator Σ breaks this symmetry: basic/linear satisfiability jumps from NP to NP^PP (probabilistic), PSPACE (interventional), and NEXP (counterfactual), a strictly increasing hierarchy under standard complexity assumptions. For polynomial terms with marginalization, van der Zander et al. (2023) had shown succ-∃R-completeness for the probabilistic and interventional layers but left the counterfactual case open; this paper closes that gap by proving succ-∃R-completeness for the counterfactual layer as well, showing that the most expressive polynomial language does not exhibit the same strict separation as the linear language.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on satisfiability complexity in Pearl's Causal Hierarchy initiated by Fagin, Halpern, and Megiddo (1990) for the probabilistic layer and extended by Mossé et al. (2022) to all three layers for polynomial terms. The key departure is the systematic study of basic and linear terms combined with the summation (marginalization) operator, a combination not previously analyzed, and the resolution of the open counterfactual case for polynomial terms with marginalization left by van der Zander et al. (2023). The proof techniques draw on the NEXP_real machine model of Bläser et al. (2024) and the ∃R = NP_real classification of Erickson et al. (2022) to handle the polynomial-with-summation case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate a strictly increasing complexity hierarchy across Pearl's Causal Hierarchy (NP^PP → PSPACE → NEXP), aligning computational difficulty with the well-known strict increase in expressive power across the three reasoning levels. The result has direct implications for algorithm design: under standard complexity assumptions, polynomial-time or NP-level solvers are provably insufficient for interventional and counterfactual satisfiability with marginalization, justifying the use of exponential-time or heuristic approaches. Open questions remain about how complexity changes when the causal graph is given explicitly in the input rather than encoded in the formula, and about restricting to polynomially-sized models for the higher layers.
