## Problem

Existing molecular representation learning methods rely solely on molecular structure or align molecules with a single biological modality (cell morphology or gene expression) via encoder-based contrastive losses, without removing redundant structural information that harms generalization. Prior multi-modal approaches like CLOOME and InfoCORE also ignore genetic perturbations that directly link gene expression and cell morphology, leaving a gap in capturing the full cellular response context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250310/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a molecular graph (atoms and bonds, typically 18–74 atoms) as input and produces a 300-dimensional latent representation via a five-layer GIN encoder. This representation is decoded by separate MLP decoders to reconstruct three types of targets: Morgan fingerprint vectors, CellProfiler morphology features from Cell Painting images, and L1000 gene expression profiles over 978 landmark genes. Downstream, the frozen representation feeds into task-specific MLPs for property prediction (AUC/MAE) or is used to compute likelihood scores against candidate morphology profiles for zero-shot retrieval.

## Method

InfoAlign constructs a cellular context graph with ~277K nodes (molecules, genes, gene expressions, cell morphology) and ~366K weighted edges derived from chemical perturbations, genetic perturbations, gene-gene interactions, and cosine similarity thresholds. For each molecule in a training batch, random walks of length L=4 extract a neighborhood path, and the cumulative product of edge weights along the path determines the importance of each target node. The encoder (GIN) is regularized toward a Gaussian prior via KL divergence (minimality), while multiple MLP decoders reconstruct the features of nodes along the walk path (sufficiency), with the loss weighted by path similarity. The key theoretical contribution shows that this decoder-based alignment yields a tighter mutual information lower bound than the encoder-based InfoNCE loss used in CLIP-style methods, because it is not upper-bounded by log K where K is the number of negative samples.

## Results

InfoAlign achieves the best average AUC across all four evaluation datasets: 81.3±0.6 on ChEMBL2K (41 tasks), 70.0±0.1 on Broad6K (32 tasks), 66.4±1.1 on ToxCast (617 tasks), and 49.4±0.2 MAE×100 on Biogen3K (6 regression tasks). Compared to the second-best method, InfoAlign improves by 2.5%–6.4% on average, with the largest gains on Broad6K (+2.7% over JOAO) and ChEMBL2K (+2.0% over InfoCORE-GE). On zero-shot molecule-morphology retrieval, InfoAlign achieves 1.3% NDCG@1 and 12.5% HIT@10 on ChEMBL2K, outperforming InfoCORE (0% and 11.3%) and CLOOME (0% and 6.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoAlign outperforms all 27 baselines across all four datasets. Among structure-only methods, the strongest GNN baseline (JOAO on Broad6K at 67.3 AUC; ContextPred on ChEMBL2K at 77.0 AUC) is surpassed by 2.7% and 4.3% respectively, while the best chemical language model (MolT5 at 69.9 on ChEMBL2K) trails by 11.4%. Among multi-modal alignment baselines, InfoCORE-GE is the closest competitor on ChEMBL2K (79.3 vs. 81.3 AUC) and ToxCast (65.3 vs. 66.4 AUC), but InfoAlign leads by 9.8 AUC on Broad6K (70.0 vs. 60.2) and 19.0 MAE×100 on Biogen3K (49.4 vs. 69.9), indicating InfoCORE suffers from negative transfer when not all modalities are jointly aligned. CLOOME, which aligns only molecule–morphology, is consistently the weakest multi-modal baseline (66.7 on ChEMBL2K, 61.7 on Broad6K). Single-modal cell morphology and gene expression features (MLP/RF/GP) perform substantially worse than molecular structure alone on most tasks, confirming that structure is the dominant signal for binding-affinity prediction, yet InfoAlign's integration of all three modalities still yields the best overall representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoAlign extends the information bottleneck framework (Tishby et al., 2000; Alemi et al., 2016) and its variational bounds (Poole et al., 2019) to molecular representation learning, departing from the dominant encoder-based contrastive paradigm (CLIP/InfoNCE) used by prior multi-modal methods like CLOOME and InfoCORE. Where those methods align molecular encoders with single biological modalities (morphology or gene expression) via pairwise contrastive losses, InfoAlign introduces a context graph integrating molecules, cell morphology, and gene expression as heterogeneous nodes, then uses random walks to identify multi-modal neighborhoods and decoder-based reconstruction to enforce sufficiency. The key conceptual departure is replacing encoder-encoder contrastive alignment with a single encoder plus multiple modality-specific decoders, justified theoretically by showing the resulting MI lower bound is tighter than the InfoNCE-based bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides evidence that molecular structure alone is insufficient for a meaningful fraction of bioactivity prediction tasks (~20% on ChEMBL2K), and that integrating cellular response data through a principled information-theoretic framework yields consistently better representations across 685 tasks. However, the context graph is built from a fixed set of perturbation datasets (JUMP, L1000, Hetionet), limiting scalability to new cell types or organisms, and the moderate improvement margins (2.5–6.4%) suggest diminishing returns from further multi-modal integration without fundamentally new data sources. Open questions include whether the context graph construction generalizes beyond human cell culture, whether the specific biological edge criteria (gene-gene interactions, cosine similarity thresholds) are critical or replaceable by learned similarity, and whether the decoder-based alignment advantage persists at much larger scale where InfoNCE's log-K bound becomes less restrictive.
