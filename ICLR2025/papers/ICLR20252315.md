## Problem

Image-text contrastive models like CLIP and SigLIP produce strong global representations but lack spatial awareness, performing poorly on dense prediction tasks such as semantic segmentation and depth estimation. Meanwhile, self-supervised models like DINOv2 excel at dense tasks but cannot process text. This paper closes the gap by building a single image-text model whose frozen features are competitive with self-supervised approaches on dense tasks while retaining full multimodal capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252315/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at 224×224 for the global crop and 98×98 for six local crops, plus a masked version) along with two text inputs: the original noisy web caption and a synthetic caption generated by PaliGemma. It produces two distinct global [CLS] embeddings — an object-centric one (e^g) aligned with the noisy caption and a spatially-aware one (ê^g) aligned with the synthetic caption — plus dense patch embeddings for every 14×14 region. At inference, the user selects which global token to use depending on whether the downstream task is object-centric or spatially aware.

## Method

TIPS introduces a dual-embedding architecture with two [CLS] tokens in the ViT, each contrastively aligned (InfoNCE) with a different caption type: the noisy web caption for fine-grained object semantics and a PaliGemma-generated synthetic caption that explicitly describes spatial relationships. On top of this, the method simultaneously applies a DINO-style self-distillation loss (matching local crop [CLS] tokens to an EMA teacher's global [CLS] token) and an iBOT-style masked image modeling loss (recovering masked patch tokens from visible context via prototype scores), both adapted to the weakly-supervised setting with simplified augmentations (crops and flips only) and random 75% masking. The total loss is L_total = ½(L_CLIP + L̂_CLIP) + α·L_distill + β·L_mask. The model is scaled to ViT-g (1.1B image encoder, 12-layer text encoder, SwiGLU FFN) and trained on 117M curated WebLI image-text pairs, with an optional high-resolution fine-tuning stage at 448×448.

## Results

TIPS-g/14 HR achieves 83.6 mIoU on PASCAL VOC and 49.9 mIoU on ADE20k for semantic segmentation, 0.353 RMSE on NYUv2 and 0.058 RMSE on NAVI for depth estimation, and 83.3% KNN / 86.2% linear probe accuracy on ImageNet-1K. On multimodal retrieval, TIPS reaches 93.0 R@1 on Flickr I→T, 84.5 R@1 on Flickr T→I, and 57.2 R@1 on DOCCI I→T. Across all 16 evaluation settings (8 tasks), TIPS achieves the best or second-best result in 13 cases, matching or surpassing DINOv2 on dense prediction while also enabling text-aligned tasks that self-supervised methods cannot perform.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest self-supervised baseline DINOv2-g/14, TIPS-g/14 HR wins on segmentation (83.6 vs 83.0 PASCAL, 49.9 vs 49.0 ADE20k) and fine-grained retrieval (68.2 vs 62.2 UnED R@1), while DINOv2 retains a small edge on depth (0.344 vs 0.353 NYUv2 RMSE) and normals (20.5 vs 21.9 NYUv2). Against image-text baselines, TIPS substantially outperforms CLIP-L on all six dense evaluations (e.g., 83.6 vs 74.5 PASCAL segmentation, 0.353 vs 0.553 NYUv2 depth) and also surpasses SigLIP-SO/14 on dense tasks (83.6 vs 67.8 PASCAL), though SigLIP edges out TIPS on UnED retrieval (70.8 vs 68.2). On multimodal retrieval (Table 3), TIPS leads all image-text methods on I→T and T→I across COCO, Flickr, and DOCCI (e.g., 93.0 vs 91.0 Flickr I→T vs SigLIP; 84.5 vs 75.3 Flickr T→I), but trails SigLIP (83.2) and SILC (83.7) on ImageNet zero-shot classification (79.9). The supervised JFT-3B-g/14 model, despite 1.8B parameters, underperforms TIPS on dense tasks (70.7 vs 83.6 PASCAL segmentation), confirming that contrastive image-text training with spatial-awareness objectives can rival or exceed large-scale supervised pretraining for dense prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIPS builds directly on the CLIP contrastive image-text framework (Radford et al., 2021) and adapts self-supervised techniques from DINO (Caron et al., 2021) and iBOT (Zhou et al., 2022) into a unified training objective. The key departure from prior work is the simultaneous combination of contrastive image-text learning with both self-distillation and masked image modeling, whereas MaskCLIP (Dong et al., 2023) and SILC (Naeem et al., 2024) each incorporated only one of these self-supervised components. Additionally, the dual-embedding design—using two [CLS] tokens trained against noisy web captions and synthetic PaliGemma captions respectively—goes beyond the sampling or multi-text caption combination strategies proposed by LaCLIP (Fan et al., 2023), enabling the model to learn complementary object-centric and spatially-aware global representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIPS closes a significant gap in the field by demonstrating that image-text models can rival self-supervised approaches (DINOv2) on dense prediction tasks while retaining text alignment capabilities, offering a single general-purpose encoder for both vision-only and multimodal applications. The finding that synthetic captions from a generative model substantially improve dense understanding (10+ points on segmentation) reveals that the quality and spatial richness of textual supervision matters far more for dense tasks than previously appreciated. Limitations include weaker zero-shot classification compared to SigLIP (79.7 vs. 83.2) and still slightly trailing DINOv2 on some depth metrics, suggesting the trade-off between spatial coherence and fine-grained discriminability is not fully resolved. Open questions include whether the dual-embedding design can be simplified, whether the approach benefits from even larger scale, and whether the synthetic caption strategy generalizes to other modalities or languages.
