## Problem

Size transferability analyses for GNNs have been almost exclusively limited to message-passing networks (MPNNs), whose expressivity is capped at the 1-WL test. Higher-order GNNs such as Invariant Graph Networks (IGNs) are far more powerful, but their behavior in the graphon limit—particularly continuity, expressivity, and convergence under sampling—remains poorly understood. Prior work by Cai & Wang (2022) introduced a restricted model class (IGN-small) to recover convergence but left open whether this restriction sacrifices expressivity, and relied on a nonstandard partition norm over graphon diagonals.

## Input / Output

The method operates on graphon-signals (W, f) ∈ W₀ × L_r^∞[0,1], where W is a bounded symmetric measurable kernel on [0,1]² and f is a scalar-valued signal bounded by r. IWNs map such inputs to a scalar (invariant output), while EWNs produce tensor-valued outputs in L²[0,1]^k. The analytical framework works simultaneously with L^p distances (p ∈ [1,∞]) and the cut distance δ_□, with the key distinction that IWNs are continuous in L^p but discontinuous in δ_□. Signal-weighted homomorphism densities t((F,d),(W,f)) serve as the fundamental invariants, where F is a (multi)graph pattern and d ∈ ℕ₀^{v(F)} specifies per-node signal exponents.

## Method

The paper introduces Invariant Graphon Networks (IWNs) by extending IGN linear equivariant layers from finite sets to the atomless measure space [0,1], restricting to bounded operators on L²[0,1]^k. This yields the Rook subalgebra of the partition algebra with dimension Σ_s s!·C(k,s)·C(ℓ,s) ≤ bell(k+ℓ), indexed by partitions where no block contains two input or two output indices—corresponding to operations that integrate over axes without selecting diagonals. The authors prove IWNs of order k are at least as expressive as the k-WL test for graphon-signals (via signal-weighted homomorphism densities of bounded-treewidth patterns) and universally approximate continuous functions on compact subsets in L^p distances. Crucially, they show that while IWNs are necessarily discontinuous w.r.t. cut distance (a property inherent to k-WL's use of multigraph homomorphism densities), transferability still holds: any IWN output converges in expectation between independently sampled graphs of growing sizes, with a quantitative rate of O((log n)^{-1/2}) for a universal class of 2-layer IWNs with real-analytic nonlinearities.

## Results

This is a theoretical paper whose main contributions are expressivity and transferability guarantees rather than benchmark accuracy numbers. The key quantitative results include: (1) the dimension of linear equivariant layers on [0,1] is dim LE_{k→ℓ} = Σ_{s=0}^{min(k,ℓ)} s!·C(k,s)·C(ℓ,s) ≤ bell(k+ℓ), showing IWNs use a strict subset of the full IGN basis; (2) IWNs of order k are at least as expressive as the k-WL test and universally approximate continuous functions on compact subsets of graphon-signals in δ_p distances; (3) the transferability rate for 2-layer IWNs with real-analytic nonlinearity is E|N(G_n) − N(G_m)| ≤ M·((log n)^{−1/2} + (log m)^{−1/2}). The proof-of-concept experiment on toy graphons confirms that while MPNN output errors decrease with graph size, 2-IWN errors stabilize (not vanish) yet prediction interval widths contract similarly for both architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's empirical comparison (Figures 2–3) contrasts a standard MPNN (GCN-style) against a 2-IWN on sampled graphs from fixed graphons. For convergence to the graphon limit, the MPNN wins: its absolute output error N(W) − N(G_n(W)) decreases monotonically with n due to δ□-continuity, whereas the 2-IWN's error does not vanish (reflecting the inherent cut-distance discontinuity of higher-order models). However, for the practically relevant transferability question—whether N(G_n) ≈ N(G_m) for large n, m—the two architectures perform comparably: the 90% prediction interval widths of N(G_n(W)) contract with increasing n for both models, with only minor differences visible between MPNN and 2-IWN. This validates Theorem 5.2, showing that despite the discontinuity, IWNs retain transferability properties on par with MPNNs. The theoretical comparison with Cai & Wang's IGN-small (2022) shows that IWNs, as a strict subset of IGN-small, retain the same expressivity (k-WL and universal approximation), resolving an open question from that work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IGN/EGN framework of Maron et al. (2019b) to the graphon-signal space by restricting linear equivariant layers to bounded operators, yielding Invariant Graphon Networks (IWNs). It builds directly on Cai & Wang (2022), who introduced IGN-small to fix convergence issues of IGNs on sampled graphs, but departs from their use of partition norms (diagonals of graphons) in favor of standard L^p and cut distances, which the authors argue align better with the broader graphon literature. The k-WL characterization for graphons from Böker (2023) is extended to graphon-signals via a new tool—signal-weighted homomorphism densities—and the graphon-signal space framework of Levie (2023) provides the compactness and sampling infrastructure. The key conceptual departure is showing that IWNs, a strict subset of IGN-small, retain the full expressivity of the discrete IGN basis in the graphon limit, resolving an open question from Cai & Wang.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of continuity, expressivity, and transferability for higher-order GNNs on graphons, filling a gap where prior transferability analyses were almost exclusively limited to MPNNs. The finding that typical higher-order GNNs are discontinuous w.r.t. cut distance—yet still transferable—challenges the standard triangle-inequality argument used for MPNNs and opens a new line of analysis for size generalization of expressive architectures. Open questions include deriving general quantitative transferability bounds beyond the restricted 2-layer IWN setting, extending to spectral GNNs and sparse graph limits, and understanding whether real-world graph distributions are confined to the compact subsets where L^p-continuity holds.
