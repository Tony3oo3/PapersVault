## Problem

Accelerated diffusion sampling (using far fewer steps than training) degrades image quality due to accumulating prediction errors, but the geometric mechanism behind this degradation—exposure bias—remains underexplored. While prior work identified exposure bias in diffusion models, no training-free method existed to correct the resulting manifold deviation during inference without retraining or extensive hyperparameter tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253484/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen pretrained diffusion model (SDXL at 1024×1024, LDM-4 at 256×256, or Stable Diffusion 3) conditioned on a text prompt or class label. Before accelerated sampling, a lightweight pre-computation pass (20–64 samples at 1000 steps) collects a reference pixel-variance schedule v_t for the denoising observation at each timestep. During inference, the method modifies only the DDIM (or DPM-Solver++) update equations in-place, producing a single generated image with no architectural changes or additional network parameters.

## Method

Under the manifold hypothesis, noisy data at timestep t concentrates on a manifold M_t, and the denoising observation x̂_0^(t) concentrates on a related manifold M_0^(t). The key insight is that the noise-prediction error is amplified by a large scaling factor d_t in the denoising observation (hundreds to thousands of times larger than the corresponding factor n_t on the noisy data), making x̂_0^(t) the most sensitive signal for detecting and correcting manifold deviation. MCDO corrects this by scaling the pixel variance of x̂_0^(t) to match a pre-computed reference v_t, then inverting the denoising equation to recover a corrected noise estimate, which is fed back into the standard DDIM update to produce x_{t-k}. The correction is applied for timesteps above a threshold t_thre (often 0), making the method training-free, nearly hyperparameter-free, and a simple plug-and-play module compatible with DDIM, DPM-Solver++, and rectified-flow samplers.

## Results

On MS-COCO with SDXL at 10 sampling steps, DDIM-MCDO achieves a FID of 15.60 and CLIP score of 31.75, reducing FID by 2.57 over the DDIM baseline (18.17). The improvement grows substantially as steps decrease: on CelebA-HQ at 5 steps, FID drops from 54.99 (DDIM) to 30.76 (DDIM-MCDO), and on ImageNet-256 at 5 steps, FID drops from 16.81 to 8.30. The method is training-free and requires only a single threshold hyperparameter (t_thre), which works well at 0 across all experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDIM-MCDO outperforms all three training-free baselines across every dataset and step count tested. Against DDIM (the base sampler), the FID reduction is 2.57 at 10 steps on MS-COCO, 24.23 at 5 steps on CelebA-HQ, and 8.51 at 5 steps on ImageNet-256. Against TS-DDIM (Li et al., 2024a), which shifts timesteps to match pixel variance, MCDO achieves substantially lower FID (15.60 vs. 23.82 on MS-COCO at 10 steps; 30.76 vs. 50.69 on CelebA-HQ at 5 steps). Against LDM-ES (Ning et al., 2024), which scales noise predictions by a uniform factor λ, MCDO is consistently better and notably more robust to hyperparameter sensitivity (LDM-ES shows a 6.66 FID swing between λ=0.999 and λ=1.001 at 20 steps, while MCDO requires no such tuning). When combined with LDM-ES (DDIM-MCDO†), the two methods are complementary, yielding further gains (e.g., FID 27.39 vs. 30.76 at 5 steps on CelebA-HQ). MCDO also improves over DPM-Solver++(2M) in the appendix (FID 14.88 vs. 15.10 at 10 steps on MS-COCO), confirming compatibility with high-order solvers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the manifold-constraint framework of Chung et al. (2022) and the radius-based manifold proximity analysis of He et al. (2024) to the specific problem of exposure bias in accelerated diffusion sampling. It departs from prior exposure-bias mitigations (TS-DDIM by Li et al. 2024a, which shifts timesteps; LDM-ES by Ning et al. 2024, which scales noise predictions) by instead constraining the denoising observation x̂₀⁽ᵗ⁾ to its own manifold, correcting the noise estimation indirectly while preserving the noisy input x_t. The geometric derivation linking pixel variance to manifold distance (Eq. 16) unifies and generalizes the timestep-shifting heuristic of Li et al. (2024a) into a principled correction rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is training-free, requires only a single pre-computed variance schedule v_t (from ~20 samples), and needs essentially no hyperparameter tuning (t_thre=0 works well), making it a practical plug-and-play module for any DDIM-based pipeline. Its consistent gains across SDXL, LDM-4, and Stable Diffusion 3, as well as across DDIM and DPM-Solver++, suggest broad applicability. Open questions include whether the manifold assumption holds for video/audio generation, whether the pixel-variance proxy for L² norm degrades at extreme resolutions, and whether the fine-grained variant (MCDO+) can be made to outperform the scalar version. The paper does not address direction errors in noise prediction explicitly, only magnitude, which may limit gains in certain regimes.
