## Problem

Existing diffusion-based dense prediction methods (e.g., Marigold) directly inherit the standard image-generation diffusion formulation—noise prediction and multi-step iterative denoising—without analyzing whether it is suitable for dense prediction. This paper identifies that noise prediction introduces harmful variance at initial denoising steps that propagates and magnifies through the process, that multi-step diffusion is unnecessarily computation-intensive and hard to optimize with limited data, and that fine-tuning causes catastrophic forgetting of detail generation capability.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image encoded to latent space via a frozen VAE encoder, optionally concatenated with a Gaussian noise latent (for the stochastic Lotus-G variant), and produces a dense annotation map (depth in disparity space d=1/d′, or surface normal) decoded back to pixel space by the VAE decoder. Training resolutions are 576×768 (Hypersim) and 352×1216 (Virtual KITTI). Multiple stochastic inferences with different noise initializations can be aggregated to yield per-pixel uncertainty maps, highlighting naturally ambiguous regions such as sky and object edges.

## Method

Lotus fine-tunes Stable Diffusion V2 (without text conditioning) with three key modifications to the standard diffusion formulation. First, it replaces ε-prediction with x0-prediction, so the U-Net denoiser directly predicts the clean annotation latent rather than the added noise, eliminating the 1/√ᾱτ amplification factor that causes large variance at high-noise steps. Second, it collapses the multi-step denoising process into a single step at t=T, reducing the optimization space, preventing error propagation across steps, and dramatically accelerating both training and inference. Third, it introduces a detail preserver: a one-dimensional task switcher (encoded positionally and added to time embeddings) that lets the same U-Net either generate the dense annotation (s_y) or reconstruct the input image (s_x), with both losses summed during training to prevent catastrophic forgetting of fine-grained detail generation. The result is a model trained on only 59K synthetic samples that achieves competitive zero-shot depth and normal estimation while being hundreds of times faster than iterative diffusion baselines.

## Results

On zero-shot affine-invariant depth estimation, Lotus-G (59K training samples) achieves AbsRel of 5.4 on NYUv2, 8.5 on KITTI, 5.9 on ETH3D, 5.9 on ScanNet, and 22.9 on DIODE, attaining the best overall rank among all generative methods. Lotus-D achieves comparable results (AbsRel 5.1/8.1/6.1/5.5/22.8) while being deterministic. For surface normal estimation, both Lotus-G and Lotus-D outperform all discriminative and generative baselines in average ranking, with Lotus-D achieving 16.2° mean angular error on NYUv2 and 14.7° on ScanNet. Notably, Lotus uses only 59K training images versus 62.6M for DepthAnything, and is hundreds of times faster than Marigold at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among generative diffusion-based methods, Lotus-G outperforms Marigold (AbsRel 5.5→5.4 on NYUv2, 9.9→8.5 on KITTI, 30.8→22.9 on DIODE) and GeoWizard (14.4→8.5 on KITTI, 33.5→22.9 on DIODE) across nearly all benchmarks, while also being hundreds of times faster at inference. Lotus-D matches or slightly exceeds Diffusion-E2E-FT (5.4→5.1 on NYUv2, 9.6→8.1 on KITTI, 30.3→22.8 on DIODE) with fewer training samples (59K vs 74K). Against the strongest discriminative baseline DepthAnything (62.6M images), Lotus-D is slightly inferior on NYUv2 (5.1 vs 4.3) and ScanNet (5.5 vs 4.3) but substantially better on DIODE (22.8 vs 26.0) and ETH3D (6.1 vs 12.7), using ~1000× less training data. For surface normal estimation, both Lotus variants outperform DSINE (16.4°→16.2° on NYUv2, 16.2°→14.7° on ScanNet) and Diffusion-E2E-FT (16.5°→16.2° on NYUv2, 33.5°→32.3° on Sintel) in average ranking, with Lotus-G achieving the best OASIS score (22.7° vs 23.2° for Diffusion-E2E-FT and 24.4° for DSINE).

## Relation to Prior Work

Lotus builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture (Rombach et al., 2022) and follows the image-conditioned annotation generation formulation introduced by Marigold (Ke et al., 2024) and GeoWizard (Fu et al., 2024). However, it departs from these prior works by systematically arguing that the standard diffusion formulation—ε-prediction combined with multi-step denoising—is fundamentally mismatched to dense prediction, and replaces both with x0-prediction and a single-step procedure. Concurrent works like GenPercept (Xu et al., 2024) and Diffusion-E2E-FT (Garcia et al., 2024) also explored single-step or modified diffusion, but Lotus provides a more principled analysis of *why* the standard formulation fails and adds the detail preserver mechanism to combat catastrophic forgetting in fine-grained regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Lotus achieves competitive zero-shot depth and normal estimation with only 59K training samples (vs. 62.6M for DepthAnything) and is hundreds of times faster than multi-step methods like Marigold, making diffusion-based dense prediction viable for real-time applications. The finding that single-step diffusion outperforms multi-step across all data scales challenges the prevailing assumption that iterative denoising is necessary, potentially simplifying the design space for future generative perception models. Open questions include whether the single-step formulation generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (segmentation, optical flow), whether the stochastic Lotus-G variant yields calibrated uncertainty estimates in practice, and whether the approach can be extended to metric (rather than affine-invariant) depth without the data scaling that discriminative methods require.
