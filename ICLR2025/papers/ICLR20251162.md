## Problem

Current offline-to-online RL fine-tuning methods require continued training on the offline dataset for stability, but this is computationally expensive for large datasets and limits asymptotic performance. The paper identifies that without offline data retention, existing algorithms (CQL, IQL, CalQL) suffer a 'downward spiral' of Q-value divergence caused by distribution shift between offline and online state-action pairs, leading to catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained initializations. The central question is whether one can fine-tune from offline RL initializations while completely discarding the offline dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251162/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

WSRL takes a pre-trained policy π_ψ^pre and Q-function Q_θ^pre (typically from CalQL, though CQL and IQL also work) and online environment interactions as its only inputs; the offline dataset D_off is discarded after pre-training. The warmup phase uses K=5000 steps of the frozen pre-trained policy to seed the replay buffer before any gradient updates begin. The output is a fine-tuned actor-critic policy and Q-function, with the critic updated via standard TD loss and the actor via reparameterization-based policy gradient with entropy regularization (SAC).

## Method

WSRL's key mechanism is a warmup phase where the frozen pre-trained policy collects a small number of online rollouts (K=5000 steps) to seed the replay buffer, bridging the distribution mismatch between offline and online data and preventing the Q-value 'downward spiral' that causes forgetting. After warmup, the algorithm switches to standard non-pessimistic online SAC with a high updates-to-data ratio (UTD=4), an ensemble of 10 Q-networks (min over 2 random subsamples), and layer normalization in both actor and critic. This design is critical: using a pessimistic offline RL algorithm (e.g., CalQL) for fine-tuning instead of SAC significantly degrades performance, and seeding the buffer with random actions or offline dataset transitions is less effective than using the pre-trained policy's online rollouts. The approach is agnostic to the offline RL pre-training algorithm and works across Antmaze, Kitchen, Adroit, and MuJoCo locomotion benchmarks.

## Results

WSRL is evaluated across 7+ tasks spanning D4RL Antmaze (8 mazes), Kitchen (3 variants), Adroit (3 tasks), and MuJoCo Locomotion (9 environments) in the no-retention fine-tuning setting. In all settings, WSRL achieves the highest asymptotic success rate/return and fastest convergence, fine-tuning to near-optimal performance within ~100k–500k online steps. CQL and IQL collapse to ~0% success rate on kitchen-partial and never recover, while CalQL partially recovers but fails to exceed its pre-trained performance. WSRL also outperforms methods that *do* retain offline data (e.g., CalQL with 50% offline sampling, RLPD with 50/50 buffer mixing), demonstrating that discarding offline data entirely is not only feasible but superior when combined with the warmup phase and a non-pessimistic online algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CQL and IQL (both pessimistic/behavioral-constraint offline RL methods), WSRL wins decisively: both baselines suffer catastrophic forgetting in the no-retention setting, dropping to near-zero success rate on kitchen-partial and failing to recover over 1M+ fine-tuning steps, while WSRL recovers within ~20k steps and reaches substantially higher asymptotic performance. CalQL, designed specifically for calibrated fine-tuning, partially recovers from its initial dip but plateaus below its pre-trained performance and is significantly outperformed by WSRL in both speed and final return. JSRL (Jump-Start RL), the most competitive baseline, matches WSRL on Adroit tasks but is significantly worse on Antmaze and Kitchen, likely because it discards the pre-trained Q-function that WSRL leverages. RLPD/SAC(fast), which learns from scratch with offline data in the replay buffer, fails completely in exploration-heavy environments (Antmaze, Kitchen) and only improves slowly in MuJoCo locomotion, whereas WSRL outperforms it across the board by effectively utilizing the pre-trained initialization. SO2, which balances reward maximization with pessimistic pre-training via perturbed value updates, is also outperformed by WSRL in the no-retention setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline-to-online RL fine-tuning line of work (CQL, IQL, CalQL, SO2) by identifying and removing the requirement to retain offline data during fine-tuning. It builds on SAC as the online fine-tuning algorithm, CalQL for calibrated Q-function initialization, and RLPD's high-UTD ensemble design, but departs from all prior offline-to-online methods by showing they fail catastrophically without offline data and proposing a simple warmup phase (collecting a few rollouts from the frozen pre-trained policy) to bridge the distribution gap before switching to standard online RL. The paper also contrasts with RLPD, which uses offline data in the replay buffer from scratch without pre-training, showing that proper pre-training plus warmup outperforms this approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant for scalable RL: if offline data can be discarded after pre-training, the expensive and slow continued training on large diverse datasets becomes unnecessary, and the pessimism constraints that limit performance improvement are removed. The mechanistic diagnosis of the 'downward spiral' (Q-value divergence due to distribution shift at the onset of fine-tuning) provides a clear explanation for why prior methods fail and validates the warmup solution. Limitations include evaluation only on D4RL benchmarks in single-environment, single-task settings; it remains open whether the approach generalizes to multi-task, non-stationary, or real-world robotics settings, and whether the fixed 5K warmup length is sufficient across all domains.
