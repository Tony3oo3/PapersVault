## Problem

Schrödinger Bridge (SB) methods for transporting between arbitrary distributions have been formulated almost exclusively in continuous spaces using Brownian motion, leaving discrete domains such as graphs unaddressed. Moreover, diffusion bridge models require a pre-specified joint coupling between source and target distributions, which is often unavailable or ill-defined for discrete data. No prior framework solves the SB problem in high-dimensional discrete state spaces with a convergence guarantee.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source molecule graph and a target molecule graph (or distributions thereof), where each graph is represented as categorical node features (atomic types) and edge features (bond types). Because graphs are permutation-invariant, the method must first solve a graph permutation matching problem (formulated as a quadratic assignment problem and approximated via max-pooling plus the Hungarian algorithm) before computing reference-process likelihoods or constructing reciprocal bridges. The output is a transformed molecule graph that shifts toward the target property while minimizing structural edits, evaluated on unpaired data where no ground-truth correspondence between source and target molecules exists.

## Method

DDSBM extends Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) from continuous diffusion processes to discrete state spaces by using continuous-time Markov chains (CTMCs) as the reference process. The algorithm alternates between reciprocal projection (constructing a mixture of pinned-down bridges via Doob's h-transform from the current coupling) and Markov projection (finding the closest Markov process in reverse KL divergence), with the Markov projection approximated by neural networks that parameterize the CTMC transition rate matrix. For graphs, the reference process is defined as independent categorical jumps on each node and edge with a monotonically decreasing signal-to-noise ratio, making the induced cost function equivalent to the graph edit distance. The paper proves convergence of the iterative projection sequence to the SB solution (Theorem 3.3) and introduces a time-reversed Markov projection step to correct accumulated marginal drift from neural network approximation, alternating forward and backward generator learning across iterations.

## Results

On ZINC250K, DDSBM achieves the lowest NLL (160.46 vs. 288.57 for DBM, 344.46 for HierG2G, 355.03 for AtomG2G), indicating minimal structural change during graph transformation. It also attains the best FCD (0.833 vs. 1.046 for DBM), NSPDK (7.30e-4), QED MAD (0.120), and SAscore MAD (0.402), while maintaining 100% uniqueness. On the more challenging Polymer dataset, DDSBM dramatically outperforms DBM in validity (97.4% vs. 43.4%), NLL (212.05 vs. 580.42), and GAP W1 (0.127 vs. 0.249), confirming its advantage in preserving molecular structure while shifting the target property.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDSBM outperforms all three baselines on the key structural-fidelity metrics: NLL is reduced by 44% over DBM (160.46 vs. 288.57) and by 53–55% over the latent-based models (344.46, 355.03), confirming that the iterative coupling refinement yields substantially less destructive graph edits. On FCD, DDSBM (0.833) beats DBM (1.046) by ~20% and the latent models (5.0–5.7) by over 6×, showing superior alignment with the target distribution. For molecular property preservation, DDSBM achieves the lowest QED MAD (0.120) and SAscore MAD (0.402), roughly 15–35% better than DBM and 30–40% better than the latent baselines, despite being trained without explicit property supervision. The only metric where a baseline wins is LogP W₁, where HierG2G (0.113) slightly edges out DDSBM (0.139), though both are far below the reference (2.007). On the Polymer dataset, DDSBM's advantage is even more pronounced: validity jumps from 43.4% (DBM) to 97.4%, and NLL drops from 580.4 to 212.0, highlighting that the iterative Markovian fitting is critical when the structure–property relationship is highly non-linear.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF) procedure from Peluchetti (2023a) and Shi et al. (2024), originally formulated for continuous diffusion processes, to the discrete setting of continuous-time Markov chains over finite state spaces, proving convergence to the Schrödinger Bridge solution. For the graph domain, it adopts the discrete diffusion transition rates and categorical priors from DiGress (Vignac et al., 2022) as the reference process, then connects the resulting entropy-regularized optimal transport cost to graph edit distance. Unlike prior diffusion bridge models that require a fixed initial coupling, DDSBM dynamically refines the coupling through alternating reciprocal and Markov projections, and unlike prior SB methods restricted to continuous spaces, it operates natively on discrete graph structures with permutation-invariance handled via graph matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to solve the Schrödinger Bridge problem in high-dimensional discrete state spaces, opening a path for SB-based generative modeling on graphs, text, and other discrete data. The identification of the SB cost with graph edit distance provides a principled interpretation of why minimal structural change emerges naturally, though the NP-hardness of GED limits direct algorithmic exploitation. A key limitation is the computational cost of iterative sampling from the learned Markov process during IMF, which is more expensive than single-pass bridge matching. Open questions include whether the iterative refinement can be accelerated, whether the GED connection enables more efficient coupling initialization, and whether the framework extends to other discrete domains beyond molecular graphs.
