## Problem

Existing file selection methods for LLM pretraining (DSIR, QuRating, Heuristic classification) rely on domain-similarity criteria that induce dimensional collapse in the feature embedding space: they improve performance on domain-specific tasks like reading comprehension but cause severe degradation on general tasks requiring physical-world knowledge. No prior method effectively prevents this collapse while preserving the diversity needed for broad generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text corpus of ~590M files (SlimPajama), a pre-trained feature extractor (Contriever, ~110M parameters) to compute normalized embeddings, a selection budget S (number of files to retain), and a batch scale b (default 1024) that controls the granularity of the greedy selection. Unlike per-sample scoring methods, the selection criterion evaluates the covariance matrix of the entire selected subset, so the output is a jointly optimized set of files rather than independently ranked items. The selected subset (e.g., 1.5% of the corpus, ~9B tokens) is then used directly as the pretraining corpus for TinyLlama models.

## Method

DiSF identifies that dimensional collapse corresponds to dominant top eigenvalues in the feature covariance matrix, and proves (Lemma 1) that minimizing the Frobenius norm of this matrix is equivalent to enforcing eigenvalue uniformity. The selection objective is reformulated as a non-negative proxy function F_M^DiSF(U) = exp(−‖C(U,M)‖_F) over subsets U, and solved via a classical greedy algorithm that iteratively adds the file most reducing the Frobenius norm, operating at batch scale to keep complexity at O(|S|²bd²). The paper provides a theoretical guarantee by proving the proxy function is at least e^(−2μ)·(e^(2μ−ε)−1)/(e^(2μ)−1)-weakly submodular under bounded gain-difference and bounded average-utility assumptions, yielding a (1−e^(−γ))-approximation via the greedy procedure. This distinguishes DiSF from D4 (feature-distance deduplication) and INGENIOUS (facility location), which reduce redundancy but do not directly optimize eigenvalue uniformity.

## Results

On the 1.1B TinyLlama with 50B training budget and 1.5% selection budget (≈9B tokens from 590M SlimPajama files), DiSF achieves 45.2 average across seven commonsense reasoning tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline QuRating-A (44.8) by 0.4% and DSIR (41.8) by 3.4%. On problem-solving tasks, DiSF reaches 25.4 on MMLU (5-shot) and 21.2 on BBH (3-shot), surpassing DSIR by 2.5% and 2.7% respectively. In terms of efficiency, DiSF with 1.5% of files matches or exceeds Full Data pre-training at 50B tokens using only ~20% of the pre-trained tokens (5× data efficiency) and requires 27B–40B tokens to reach Full Data's 50B performance (1.5× training efficiency).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiSF consistently outperforms all five baselines on average commonsense performance across all model sizes (120M, 560M, 1.1B) and both training budgets (10B, 50B). Against DSIR, the gap is largest (3.4% on 1.1B/50B), as DSIR's Wikipedia/Books-targeted selection causes severe dimensional collapse, dropping HellaSwag to 30.4 and PIQA to 64.2 versus DiSF's 37.3 and 69.0. QuRating-A (uniform across all styles) is the closest competitor at 44.8, but DiSF still leads by 0.4% and shows a clear advantage on BoolQ (62.0 vs. 60.1) and HellaSwag (37.3 vs. 36.5). D4 (44.2) and INGENIOUS (44.1) partially mitigate collapse through deduplication and facility-location selection but remain 1.0–1.1% below DiSF, confirming that decorrelation-based selection more effectively uniformizes feature dimensions. Doremi (44.5), which reweights domain mixtures, is competitive on WinoGrande (54.3) but trails DiSF on BoolQ (58.1 vs. 62.0) and HellaSwag (36.6 vs. 37.3). On problem-solving tasks (MMLU, BBH), DiSF leads all baselines by 0.9–2.7%, with the largest margin over DSIR (2.5% on MMLU, 2.7% on BBH).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two research threads: (1) domain-similarity-based file selection for LLM pre-training (Heuristic classification, DSIR, QuRating), which the authors diagnose as suffering from dimensional collapse in the feature space, and (2) diversity-aware selection via submodular optimization (D4's deduplication + prototypicality, INGENIOUS's facility location). DiSF departs from both by directly minimizing the Frobenius norm of the feature covariance matrix to enforce eigenvalue uniformity, drawing on the dimensional-collapse literature from contrastive representation learning (VICReg, Barlow Twins) and framing the selection as a γ-weakly submodular maximization problem solvable by greedy search. Unlike D4 and INGENIOUS, which use indirect diversity proxies, DiSF's criterion is explicitly tied to the spectral structure of the selected set's covariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of dimensional collapse as the concrete mechanism behind the 'diversity dilemma' in data selection provides a useful diagnostic lens for the field, and the 5× data efficiency result (1.5% of SlimPajama files outperforming full-data pre-training at 50B tokens) is practically significant for compute-constrained settings. However, all experiments are conducted on models up to 1.1B parameters and a single corpus (SlimPajama), leaving open whether the approach and its efficiency gains transfer to the 7B+ scale where most production LLMs are trained. The theoretical guarantee relies on bounded-gain and bounded-utility assumptions whose validity at scale is unverified, and the choice of Contriever as the feature extractor—while ablated—may interact with model scale in ways not yet explored.
