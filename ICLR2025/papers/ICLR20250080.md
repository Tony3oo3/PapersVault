## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods fail under multimodal wild TTA, where the target domain simultaneously contains weak OOD samples (single modality corrupted) and strong OOD samples (multiple modalities corrupted or missing). The abrupt distribution gap between the source domain and strong OOD data destroys the prior knowledge in the source model, causing performance to degrade below the unadapted baseline. Prior multimodal TTA work (e.g., READ) only addresses weak OOD under a mild single-shift setting, leaving the mixed, multi-shift scenario unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired video clip and audio clip (10-second segments) as input and produces a class label (action class for Kinetics50, sound class for VGGSound). Missing modalities are replaced with zero vectors to preserve input dimensions. During adaptation, only the affine parameters of normalization layers are updated, and the model processes samples in batches of 16 (Kinetics50) or 64 (VGGSound) over a fixed number of iterations, with the sample selection window dynamically expanding across iterations.

## Method

SuMi introduces three coupled mechanisms for stable multimodal TTA. First, IQR smoothing dynamically selects adaptation samples by computing the interquartile range of concatenated unimodal feature representations and expanding the acceptance window linearly over iterations (f(t)=t/iter), so that weak OOD samples are adapted first and strong OOD samples are gradually included, preventing catastrophic prior-knowledge destruction. Second, unimodal assistance filters the IQR-selected pool by requiring low multimodal entropy (high prediction certainty) while simultaneously requiring sufficiently high weighted unimodal entropy (Ent(x^u1)+μ·Ent(x^u2) ≥ γ_u), ensuring selected samples carry rich cross-modal information rather than being trivially predictable from a single modality. Third, mutual information sharing minimizes the KL divergence between each modality's softmax distribution and a blend of its complementary modality distribution and the fused multimodal distribution, aligning cross-modal predictions; for strong OOD adaptation this loss is applied only in the first t₀ iterations to avoid contamination by heavily corrupted samples. The overall objective weights the combined entropy-plus-MIS loss by an inverse-exponential confidence factor and an indicator that the sample passes both the IQR and unimodal-assistance gates.

## Results

On Kinetics50-C with corrupted video modality (severity 5), SuMi achieves 63.9% average accuracy, outperforming the best baseline READ (63.5%) and the source model (60.2%). On VGGSound-C with corrupted video modality, SuMi reaches 57.3% average accuracy versus 56.4% for READ and 56.1% for the source model. Most critically, on VGGSound-C strong OOD scenarios (missing/corrupted modalities), SuMi achieves 19.7% average accuracy while most baselines collapse below the source model (15.6%), with only EATA (17.4%) remaining competitive. SuMi consistently leads across all four corruption categories (Noise, Blur, Weather, Digital) and all severity levels tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuMi outperforms all seven baselines on the average accuracy across both datasets. On Kinetics50-C (video corruption), SuMi (63.9%) beats READ (63.5%), EATA (61.9%), SoTTA (60.3%), SAR (60.1%), DeYO (60.0%), CEMA (59.9%), and Tent (59.8%), with the largest margin over Tent (+4.1%). On VGGSound-C (video corruption), SuMi (57.3%) leads READ (56.4%) and EATA (56.5%) by roughly 0.8–1.0%. The most dramatic separation appears on VGGSound-C strong OOD (missing/corrupted modalities): SuMi achieves 19.7% average while Tent (5.1%), SAR (5.7%), CEMA (5.7%), DeYO (6.2%), and SoTTA (7.1%) all perform far worse than the source model (15.6%), and even READ (14.5%) degrades below source. Only EATA (17.4%) remains above source on strong OOD, but SuMi still leads by 2.3 points. On the 'Mix' corruption (one modality missing + other corrupted), SuMi reaches 6.7% versus 5.8% for EATA and 4.5% for READ, confirming its advantage in the most extreme failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the entropy-minimization TTA lineage (Tent → EATA → SAR/SoTTA/DeYO/CEMA) from unimodal to multimodal settings, and further from single-domain (mild) TTA to wild TTA where the target stream mixes weak OOD (one modality corrupted) and strong OOD (multiple modalities corrupted or missing) samples. The closest prior work, READ (Yang et al., 2024), addresses multimodal reliability bias but only for weak OOD under a single corruption type; SuMi departs by introducing IQR-based gradual sample admission, a unimodal-entropy-assisted selection criterion, and a mutual information sharing loss to handle the abrupt distribution gaps that cause existing methods to underperform the frozen source model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that standard TTA methods can actively degrade below the source model on strong OOD multimodal data, a failure mode that is practically critical for deployed audio-visual systems facing mixed real-world corruptions. The IQR smoothing mechanism and the counterintuitive finding that slightly elevated unimodal entropy (rather than minimal) identifies more informative samples offer reusable design principles for any multi-source adaptation pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two modalities and entropy minimization to other adaptation objectives (e.g., self-supervised or contrastive losses), and whether the linear smoothing schedule is optimal for very long adaptation streams.
