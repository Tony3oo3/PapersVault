## Problem

Evaluating personalized text-to-image generation models is difficult because traditional automated metrics like DINO and CLIP measure image similarity rather than human-perceived concept preservation, leading to scores that significantly diverge from human judgments. Meanwhile, crowd-sourced human evaluation is expensive, non-standardized, and hard to scale. No existing benchmark combines comprehensive dataset diversity with a human-aligned automated evaluation protocol for personalized T2I generation.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance consists of a reference image (single image, cropped to centralize the subject), a text prompt (one of 9 per reference, spanning photorealistic, non-photorealistic, and imaginative difficulty levels), and the generated image produced by a personalized T2I model. The GPT-4o evaluator outputs two integer scores on a 0–4 scale: a concept preservation score (assessing shape, color, texture, and facial features) and a prompt following score (assessing relevance, accuracy, completeness, and context). The full benchmark comprises 150 reference images and 1,350 prompts across four categories (animals, humans, objects, styles), with ground-truth scores from 7 human annotators per instance.

## Method

DREAMBENCH++ uses GPT-4o as an automated evaluator through a carefully designed two-step prompting pipeline: (1) an internal thinking step where GPT is asked to confirm task understanding and summarize the evaluation criteria (inspired by Self-Align), and (2) a summary-and-planning step that functions as a generalized chain-of-thought before scoring. The evaluation instructions specify task description, scoring criteria tailored to each axis (concept preservation vs. prompt following), a 0–4 integer rating range, and output format. A rating scheme is chosen over pairwise comparison to avoid positional bias in GPT and to keep evaluation cost linear rather than quadratic in the number of methods. The dataset is constructed by generating ~300 keywords via GPT-4o and human annotators, collecting and filtering images from Unsplash/Rawpixel/Google using SAM for subject-region validation, and generating 9 prompts per image with GPT-4o. Human alignment is validated via Krippendorff's alpha and Pearson correlation against 7-annotator ground truth, showing 83.31% and 98.71% consistency for concept preservation and prompt following respectively—substantially outperforming DINO and CLIP baselines.

## Results

DREAMBENCH++ (GPT-4o) achieves 83.31% and 98.71% Pearson correlation with human evaluation on concept preservation and prompt following, respectively—surpassing DINO (50.72%) and CLIP (60.98%) by +32.59% and +37.23%. Among the 7 benchmarked models, DreamBooth LoRA (SDXL) attains the best CP×PF product score (0.517), while IP-Adapter-Plus ViT-H achieves the highest concept preservation (0.833 GPT) but the lowest prompt following (0.413 GPT), illustrating a clear trade-off. The human subject category is the most challenging, with an average concept preservation score of only 0.482, roughly 0.2 lower than the animal category.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks seven personalized T2I models: three fine-tuning-based (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, DreamBooth LoRA) and four encoder-based (BLIP-Diffusion, Emu2, IP-Adapter-Plus ViT-H, IP-Adapter ViT-G). DreamBooth LoRA (SDXL) achieves the best overall balance with a CP×PF product of 0.517, outperforming DreamBooth (0.356) and Textual Inversion (0.236) on both axes. IP-Adapter-Plus ViT-H dominates concept preservation (0.833 GPT, 0.755 Human) but trails significantly in prompt following (0.413 GPT vs. 0.865 for DreamBooth LoRA), yielding the worst CP×PF product (0.344) and the highest CP/PF over-fitting ratio (2.02). Emu2 (SDXL) shows the closest GPT-to-human alignment on concept preservation (0.733 Pearson) but ranks mid-table overall. Critically, DINO and CLIP image-similarity scores diverge substantially from human judgments (e.g., DINO gives IP-Adapter-Plus 0.834 vs. human 0.755, while GPT gives 0.833), confirming that traditional metrics are unreliable for personalized generation evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DreamBench evaluation framework (Ruiz et al., 2023) by scaling it from 30 subjects/25 prompts to 150 images/1,350 prompts with significantly greater diversity, and departs from the standard practice of using DINO/CLIP similarity scores as evaluation metrics. It adapts the MLLM-as-evaluator paradigm demonstrated by Wu et al. (2024) for text-to-3D generation to the more challenging domain of personalized image generation, where subtle concept-preservation differences are harder to assess. The prompting methodology draws on Self-Align (Sun et al., 2023) for internal thinking and chain-of-thought reasoning, but the key conceptual departure is the systematic demonstration that traditional image-similarity metrics (DINO, CLIP) are fundamentally misaligned with human judgment for concept preservation, motivating the shift to GPT-4o-based scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DREAMBENCH++ provides the first scalable, automated, and human-aligned benchmark for personalized image generation, removing the need for expensive crowd-sourced evaluation while achieving 83–99% consistency with human raters. The finding that concept preservation and prompt following exhibit a negative correlation (Pareto trade-off) gives the community a clearer optimization target, and the category-level breakdown (animals vs. humans vs. objects vs. styles) reveals that human faces remain the hardest case. Key limitations include heavy dependence on GPT-4o's evolving capabilities, the counterintuitive finding that in-context learning degrades alignment, and the fact that the benchmark's validity is tied to the quality of its carefully designed prompts. As MLLMs improve (GPT-4o-2024-08-06 already shows 84.23% vs. 79.47%), the benchmark's ceiling will rise, making it a living evaluation standard rather than a static one.
