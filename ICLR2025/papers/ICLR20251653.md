## Problem

Native mesh generation (modeling triangle sequences directly) produces compact, artist-friendly meshes, but existing methods like MeshGPT and PolyGen are confined to simple topologies and small datasets due to the combinatorial complexity of mesh structure. Scaling native mesh generation to large, diverse datasets like Objaverse-XL remains an open challenge because directly modeling full mesh sequences leads to trivial outputs (e.g., cubes).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251653/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in three modes: unconditional generation from scratch, conditional generation given pivot vertices extracted from a reference mesh, and point-cloud-conditioned generation. Meshes are represented as ordered sequences of triangle faces with 7-bit uniform-quantized 3D vertex coordinates, yielding a token sequence of length 0.1v + 6n (where v is vertex count and n is face count), more compact than MeshGPT's 6n or MeshXL's 9n. The output is a complete 3D triangle mesh with compact face counts and sharp geometric detail, suitable for direct use in rendering pipelines without post-processing.

## Method

PivotMesh combines a Transformer-based mesh autoencoder with a pivot-vertex-guided auto-regressive generator. The autoencoder uses a Transformer encoder (with a single-layer GNN replacing vanilla positional encoding for permutation invariance) and a hierarchical decoder that first reconstructs face-level embeddings then expands them to vertex-level embeddings via an MLP, improving surface continuity. Meshes are tokenized via residual vector quantization (r=2, codebook size 16384). The generator is a 24-layer auto-regressive Transformer that learns the joint distribution of pivot vertex tokens and full mesh tokens in a coarse-to-fine manner: it first generates pivot vertices (top-degree vertices, selected at 15% ratio with 5% random dropping to prevent overfitting, yielding ~10% of total vertices) as a coarse geometric scaffold, then conditions the full mesh token sequence on these pivots. This decomposition reduces the effective modeling difficulty and enables the model to handle complex topologies across large-scale datasets.

## Results

On Objaverse, PivotMesh achieves 46.48% COV, 16.66×10⁻³ MMD, and 58.55% 1-NNA, outperforming MeshGPT (35.03% COV, 17.30 MMD, 63.86% 1-NNA) and PolyGen (23.86% COV, 24.01 MMD, 84.07% 1-NNA). On the larger Objaverse-XL dataset, PivotMesh maintains its lead with 45.30% COV, 14.33 MMD, and 57.75% 1-NNA. On ShapeNet (mixed categories), PivotMesh reaches 50.42% COV and 11.03 MMD, a substantial improvement over MeshGPT (43.89% COV, 11.48 MMD) and PolyGen (33.91% COV, 13.47 MMD). Note that for 1-NNA, 50% is optimal, so PivotMesh's values are closest to the ideal across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PivotMesh consistently outperforms both PolyGen and MeshGPT across all three metrics (COV, MMD, 1-NNA) on ShapeNet, Objaverse, and Objaverse-XL. Against MeshGPT (the closest competitor), PivotMesh gains approximately 11 percentage points in COV on Objaverse (46.48% vs. 35.03%) and reduces MMD from 17.30 to 16.66, while also being closer to the optimal 50% on 1-NNA (58.55% vs. 63.86%). Against PolyGen, the margin is even larger, with PivotMesh nearly doubling COV on Objaverse (46.48% vs. 23.86%). For concurrent works MeshAnything and MeshAnything v2, the paper provides only visual comparisons under point-cloud conditioning (Figures 6 and 18), where PivotMesh demonstrates superior handling of complex geometry and topology. Compared to InstantMesh (a neural-representation-based method), PivotMesh produces compact, sharp meshes with far fewer faces for similar shapes, avoiding the dense over-smoothed outputs typical of marching-cubes extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

PivotMesh directly extends MeshGPT (Siddiqui et al., 2023), the most relevant prior work on autoregressive native mesh generation, by replacing its GNN-CNN autoencoder with a Transformer-based hierarchical decoder (face-level → vertex-level) and introducing pivot vertex guidance for coarse-to-fine generation. It also builds on the broader native mesh generation line (PolyGen, PolyDiff) that departs from neural-representation-based 3D generation (SDF, triplanes, FlexiCubes) to produce compact, human-like meshes. The paper positions itself alongside concurrent works (MeshXL, MeshAnything, MeshAnything v2) but differentiates through its hierarchical decoding strategy and the degree-based pivot vertex selection mechanism that enables modeling of complex topology at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PivotMesh represents the first credible attempt to scale native mesh generation to large-scale datasets (Objaverse-XL with ~400k meshes), addressing the long-standing limitation that prior methods were confined to simple geometry. The pivot vertex concept—a simple degree-based selection of high-connectivity vertices as a coarse structural guide—offers a practical and interpretable mechanism for handling complex topology that could be adopted in future mesh generation systems. Key open questions remain: the model's controllability is limited (same pivot vertices can yield diverse meshes), data and parameter scale are still constrained by compute, and integrating richer conditioning signals (images, text) is left for future work. The paper also highlights that the gap between native mesh generation and neural-representation-based methods (e.g., InstantMesh) in terms of geometric fidelity and compactness is narrowing.
