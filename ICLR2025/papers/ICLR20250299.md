## Problem

Existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods (both NeRF-based and 3DGS-based) apply uniform temporal modeling to all scene primitives, incurring unnecessary computational cost and storage for the majority of static points while also causing blurring in dynamic regions due to static-point interference. This results in long training times (hours to days), large model sizes (hundreds of MB to GBs), and suboptimal rendering quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250299/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (18–34 synchronized cameras at 25–30 FPS, resolutions from 1280×720 to 2704×2028) along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses. It produces a compact 4D scene representation (30 MB for the Lite variant, 120 MB for full) that renders photorealistic novel-view images at arbitrary viewpoints and timestamps in real time (~125 FPS on an RTX 3090). The canonical point cloud is initialized from first-frame SfM points, and temporal supervision is derived from per-pixel temporal standard deviation computed across the full video sequence.

## Method

Swift4D introduces a three-stage divide-and-conquer pipeline: (1) train a canonical-space 3DGS from first-frame images for 5000 iterations; (2) optimize a per-Gaussian learnable dynamic parameter d (initialized to 0) via binary cross-entropy against 2D temporal-variance masks, using alpha-composited sigmoid rendering to propagate gradients through occlusion; (3) freeze d and jointly optimize Gaussian attributes with a compact 4D multi-resolution hash encoder (8 MB, 16 resolution levels) and five shallow MLP heads that predict per-timestep deformations (Δμ, Δs, Δq, Δσ, Δsh) applied only to dynamic points (d > 7.0). Static points remain temporally invariant and are simply composited with deformed dynamic points at render time. A temporal importance pruning strategy replaces the standard opacity-reset mechanism to remove floaters without coupling canonical and deformation spaces, and the entire training completes in ~25 minutes.

## Results

On the N3DV dataset, Swift4D achieves the best rendering quality with PSNR 32.23, DSSIM 0.014, and LPIPS 0.043, while training in only 25 minutes with 120 MB storage and 125 FPS rendering. The Lite variant (Swift4DLite) further reduces storage to 30 MB and training to 20 minutes while maintaining competitive quality (PSNR 31.79). On the MeetRoom dataset, Swift4D reaches PSNR 32.05 in 0.3 hours with 40 MB storage, outperforming 3DGStream (PSNR 30.79, 1230 MB) by a wide margin in both quality and compactness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Swift4D outperforms the two strongest prior methods on N3DV—SpaceTimeGS (PSNR 32.05, 10 h) and Real-Time4DGS (PSNR 32.01, 9 h)—by 0.18 and 0.22 dB respectively, while training approximately 20× faster (25 min vs. 9–10 h) and using far less storage (120 MB vs. 200 MB and >1000 MB). Against 4DGS (PSNR 31.02, 50 min), Swift4D gains 1.21 dB in PSNR with half the training time. On MeetRoom, Swift4D (PSNR 32.05, 40 MB) surpasses 3DGStream (30.79, 1230 MB) by 1.26 dB while requiring 30× less storage. On the challenging Basketball court dataset, Swift4D (PSNR 29.03) beats 4DGS (27.87) by 1.16 dB. On ENeRF, Swift4D (PSNR 26.12, LPIPS 0.070) outperforms 4k4d (25.815, LPIPS 0.147) in both PSNR and perceptual quality. The only trade-off is that 3DGStream achieves higher FPS (215 vs. 125), though at the cost of 19× more storage.

## Relation to Prior Work

Swift4D builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its rendering primitive and adapts Instant NGP's multi-resolution hash encoding to a 4D (x,y,z,t) setting for temporal deformation. Its key conceptual departure from prior dynamic 3DGS methods (4DGS, SpaceTimeGS, RTGS) is a divide-and-conquer strategy: rather than applying temporal deformation uniformly to all Gaussians, it learns a per-point dynamic parameter via pixel-level temporal variance supervision, then restricts the 4D hash deformation to only the dynamic subset. This contrasts with earlier attempts at dynamic-static separation (e.g., S4D) that relied on external segmentation models, and with NeRF-based deformation-field methods that map all points through a canonical-to-deformation transform.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 20× faster training than prior SOTA dynamic 3DGS methods with a minimum 30MB model size (vs. 200MB+ for SpaceTimeGS or >1000MB for RTGS), making high-quality 4D reconstruction accessible on consumer hardware. The dynamic-static decomposition is framed as a plug-and-play module applicable to other dynamic reconstruction pipelines, and the finding that uniform temporal treatment of all points actively degrades quality (causing blurring) provides a useful design principle. Open questions include extending to monocular inputs, handling human-centric scenes, and whether the pixel-level variance supervision generalizes to scenes with large global camera motion or non-rigid deformations beyond the tested multi-view setups.
