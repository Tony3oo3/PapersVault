## Problem

Existing Shapley-value-based explanation methods for transformers (e.g., ViT-Shapley) require full fine-tuning of the black-box model into a surrogate and then training a separate heavy-weight explainer, at least doubling training memory, inference cost, and storage. This makes faithful, theoretically grounded self-interpretability impractical for large models, while alternative post-hoc methods (KernelSHAP, gradient-based) either scale poorly or lack the axiomatic guarantees of Shapley values. The paper asks whether a black-box transformer can be made self-interpretable with Shapley-value explanations without altering its original prediction parameters or incurring the cost of a second model.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard 224×224 RGB images (for ViT) or tokenized text sequences (for BERT) and, in a single forward pass, simultaneously outputs class logits from the frozen main branch and a vector of per-token/per-patch Shapley value attributions from the side branch. The side network's hidden dimensions are scaled by a reduction factor r (typically 4 or 8, e.g., 768→96 for ViT-base with r=8), and the explainer head consists of three additional FC layers with GeLU activations. The Shapley output is constrained to satisfy the efficiency axiom via an additive normalization that redistributes the residual between the unconstrained prediction and the required total attribution.

## Method

AutoGnothi attaches two lightweight Ladder Side-Tuning (LST) branches to a frozen pre-trained transformer: a surrogate side network trained to match the original model's output distribution on randomly masked inputs (via KL divergence), and an explainer side network trained to regress Shapley values using the surrogate's masked predictions under the Shapley kernel distribution. Because only the side branches are trained (with gradients never backpropagating through the frozen backbone), the original classifier's parameters and predictions remain exactly unchanged, avoiding the 'Theseus's Paradox' of full fine-tuning. The paper proves convergence guarantees for both stages (Theorems 1 and 2), showing the surrogate's KL divergence decays geometrically and the explainer's Shapley estimation error is bounded by the regression loss scaled by the harmonic number H_{d-1}. At inference, the merged single model produces predictions and explanations in one pass, reducing FLOPs by ~54%, inference time by up to 44%, and total parameters by ~54% compared to running a separate classifier and explainer, while achieving comparable or superior insertion/deletion explanation quality with 97–98% fewer trainable parameters.

## Results

On ViT-base/ImageNette, AutoGnothi achieves the best insertion score (0.9874±0.0265) and best deletion score (0.7954±0.2284) among all 17 compared explanation methods, outperforming the closest Shapley-based baseline ViT-Shapley (Covert et al.) which scores 0.9839 insertion and 0.8121 deletion. In terms of efficiency, AutoGnothi reduces trainable parameters by 97–98% for both surrogate and explainer networks, cuts training GPU memory by 72–77%, and reduces end-to-end inference FLOPs by 54% and inference time by up to 44% compared to the two-model ViT-Shapley pipeline. On the Yelp Review Polarity text task (BERT-base), AutoGnothi achieves 0.9588 insertion and 0.1004 deletion, beating ViT-Shapley's 0.1725 deletion by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoGnothi outperforms all 12 non-Shapley baselines (attention rollout, GradCAM, Vanilla gradients, IntGrad, SmoothGrad, VarGrad, LRP, Leave-one-out, RISE) on both insertion and deletion metrics, with the largest gaps over gradient-based methods (e.g., 0.9874 vs. 0.9447 insertion for GradCAM-Attn). Against the primary Shapley-based competitor ViT-Shapley (Covert et al.), AutoGnothi wins on insertion (0.9874 vs. 0.9839) and deletion (0.7954 vs. 0.8121, where lower is better) on ImageNette, while on Yelp Review Polarity it trades a small insertion loss (0.9588 vs. 0.9620) for a substantial deletion improvement (0.1004 vs. 0.1725). The efficiency advantage is decisive: AutoGnothi uses only 2.23M trainable parameters for the ViT-base surrogate (vs. 85.81M for full fine-tuning) and 2.42M for the explainer (vs. 104.72M), while requiring a single forward pass instead of two separate inferences. The paper also shows that full fine-tuning alternatives (Duo pipeline) suffer a 4.0% drop in prediction accuracy and worse explanation scores due to gradient conflicts between prediction and explanation tasks, whereas AutoGnothi's frozen-backbone design avoids this entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoGnothi repurposes the Ladder Side-Tuning (LST) architecture from parameter-efficient transfer learning and applies it to the two-stage Shapley value explanation pipeline established by ViT-Shapley (Covert et al., 2022) and FastSHAP (Jethani et al., 2021). Rather than fully fine-tuning a surrogate model and then training a separate explainer—as ViT-Shapley does—AutoGnothi freezes the backbone and trains only lightweight additive side branches for both the surrogate and explainer, collapsing two inference passes into one. The paper also deliberately departs from the classical PETL assumption that full fine-tuning is the optimal baseline, arguing (with empirical evidence) that for the dual objective of prediction plus explanation, joint full fine-tuning introduces gradient conflicts that degrade both tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing trainable parameters by 97–98% and enabling single-pass inference for both prediction and Shapley-value explanation, AutoGnothi makes theoretically grounded interpretability practical for large transformer deployments where memory and latency are binding constraints. The theoretical convergence guarantees (Theorems 1 and 2) provide a principled bound on surrogate and explainer quality that prior side-tuning work in XAI lacked. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative models or multi-modal transformers, whether the side-network reduction factor generalizes cleanly across architectures, and whether the modest explanation-quality gap versus full ViT-Shapley (e.g., 0.9874 vs. 0.9839 insertion on ImageNette) persists at ImageNet scale. The paper's evaluation is limited to relatively small datasets (ImageNette, MURA, Oxford-IIIT Pets, Yelp Polarity), so claims about scalability to production-scale models remain untested.
