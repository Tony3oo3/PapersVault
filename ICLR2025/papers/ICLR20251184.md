## Problem

While LLMs are known to hallucinate, produce toxic content, and propagate misinformation, a distinct and underexplored failure mode is that they systematically narrow the diversity of their outputs relative to the human-written data they were trained on. This paper formalizes this phenomenon as 'generative monoculture'—a distributional shift where the probability mass of task-specific attributes (sentiment, algorithms, topics) collapses toward a narrow region—and asks how prevalent it is, what training procedures cause it, and whether standard inference-time interventions can mitigate it.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a task prompt (e.g., a book title or competitive programming problem), a curated source dataset of human-written responses (742 books × 10 Goodreads reviews; 100 Codeforces level-A problems × 20 correct solutions), a target LLM, and sampling parameters (temperature, top-p, prompt template). It produces a suite of dispersion metrics per attribute—distribution of conditional means, entropy, standard deviation, mean pairwise Jaccard/cosine/fingerprint similarity—computed separately on source and generated sets, enabling direct comparison of breadth. For code, correctness is enforced before diversity metrics are computed, and attribute extraction (complexity, algorithms, tags) is performed by GPT-3.5 with human-verified accuracy (mean score ≥ 2.5/3).

## Method

The paper introduces a three-stage measurement paradigm: (1) data curation, where source datasets are filtered to approximate likely LLM training data and generated datasets are produced by sampling each prompt multiple times under controlled parameters; (2) attribute extraction, using task-specific extractors (a fine-tuned SST-2 sentiment classifier, BERTopic for themes, GPT-3.5 for code complexity/algorithms/tags, COPYDETECT for structural fingerprints); and (3) metric calculation, applying dispersion measures matched to attribute type (entropy for categorical, standard deviation for continuous, mean pairwise similarity for set/embedding attributes). The key empirical finding is that RLHF alignment is the primary driver of monoculture: the pre-trained Llama-2 baseline shows near-source diversity, while Llama-2-chat (RLHF) and Vicuna-13b (SFT) show progressively narrower distributions, with RLHF causing the largest collapse. Naive mitigations—raising temperature to 1.5, widening top-p, decaying temperature schedules, and persona-based prompting—reduce the gap only marginally while degrading generation quality (valid-generation fraction drops to ~0.4 at T=1.5, p=1.0).

## Results

For book reviews, generative monoculture is severe: the source (Goodreads) has only 2.8% of books with mean sentiment in [0.95, 1.00], while Llama-2-chat reaches 82.1%, Vicuna-13b 44.7%, and both GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 hit 100% under the default prompt. For code generation, LLM solutions are over 2× more accurate than human solutions and exhibit lower asymptotic complexity and runtime, yet show extremely high pairwise plagiarism scores (near 1.0) compared to zero for human-written solutions. Increasing sampling randomness (T=1.5, p=1.0) reduces the most-positive sentiment range from 82.1% to 58.1% for Llama-2-chat but degrades generation quality sharply (valid fraction drops to 0.394). Temperature decay and persona-based prompting also fail to close the diversity gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against baselines; instead, it compares LLM outputs against human-written source data (Goodreads reviews, CodeContests solutions) as the diversity reference. The key model comparison is between pre-trained Llama-2 (5.9% in top sentiment range), SFT-tuned Vicuna-13b (44.7%), and RLHF-tuned Llama-2-chat (82.1%), showing that RLHF causes the largest diversity loss. Proprietary models (GPT-3.5, GPT-4) exhibit even more extreme monoculture (100% in top sentiment range) than open-source Llama-family models. Among mitigation strategies, increasing temperature and top-p, persona prompting, and temperature decay all reduce the gap only marginally (e.g., persona prompt reduces top-range from 82.1% to 58.1%) while degrading generation quality at high randomness. For code, GPT-4 outperforms Claude-3-Sonnet in accuracy and efficiency, but both show the same monoculture pattern of high pairwise similarity and narrow algorithm coverage relative to human solutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formally defining 'generative monoculture' as a distribution shift from source (training) data to LLM-generated data, distinguishing it from algorithmic monoculture (Kleinberg & Raghavan, 2021), which concerns multiple models producing correlated decisions, and from model collapse (Shumailov et al., 2023), which involves iterative training on synthetic data. Prior diversity work (Santurkar et al., 2023; Padmakumar & He, 2023) examined opinion representativeness or human-LLM co-writing effects but did not frame the phenomenon as a measurable narrowing relative to the model's own training distribution. The paper repurposes standard dispersion metrics (entropy, Jaccard, cosine similarity, Winnowing fingerprints) within a new general measurement paradigm, and positions its findings alongside recent alignment-diversity tradeoff work (Kirk et al., 2024; Sorensen et al., 2024) while extending the scope beyond opinion diversity to arbitrary task-specific attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs are deployed in education, search, and software engineering, the documented severity of generative monoculture—particularly the near-total positivity bias in GPT models and the extremely high code plagiarism scores—raises concrete risks of information loss, reduced intellectual diversity, and correlated security vulnerabilities across codebases. The finding that RLHF is the primary driver (with pre-trained Llama-2 much closer to source diversity than its aligned counterpart) points to a specific and actionable tension in alignment research: current preference optimization systematically sacrifices diversity. The negative result that all tested mitigations (temperature, top-p, persona prompting, temperature decay) fail to close the gap while degrading quality at high randomness is practically important, as it rules out the most obvious fixes and motivates the need for new fine-tuning paradigms that explicitly preserve distributional breadth. Open questions include how to quantify and enforce diversity objectives during alignment without reintroducing toxicity or incoherence, and whether the phenomenon persists or worsens as models scale.
