## Problem

Classical distance-based methods for visualizing phylogenetic tree collections (e.g., MDS with Robinson-Foulds or path-difference metrics) are sensitive to metric choice, suffer from poor resolution where distinct tree groups overlap, and cannot map arbitrary points in the visualization back to valid trees. Existing high-resolution density estimators like ARTree and SBN-EM model tree topology distributions well but do not support representation learning or interpretable visualization. No prior framework simultaneously provides both a learnable low-dimensional representation of tree topologies and a probabilistic generative model over them.

## Input / Output

The method takes a weighted collection of unrooted bifurcating tree topologies sharing the same N leaf nodes (ordered lexicographically by species name), where weights typically reflect frequencies from MCMC posterior samples or bootstrap replicates. Each tree is first converted to an integer encoding vector of length N−3 via a linear-time decomposition/reconstruction algorithm. The model outputs a d-dimensional latent vector per tree (serving as its representation), a factorized discrete probability over the encoding vector (enabling sampling of new topologies from any latent point), and per-tree probability estimates for density evaluation.

## Method

PhyloVAE encodes each tree topology into an integer vector s(τ) = (s₃, …, s_{N−1}) via a linear-time algorithm that decomposes the tree by sequentially removing pendant edges and records edge decisions, then reconstructs to assign consistent node indices. The generative model p_θ(τ|z) factorizes as a product of independent discrete distributions over each s_n given a latent z, with logits produced by a single MLP and masked to valid indices—making it non-autoregressive and parallelizable, in contrast to ARTree's sequential conditional model. The inference model q_φ(z|τ) computes topological node embeddings by minimizing Dirichlet energy, passes them through two rounds of gated GRU message passing, sum-pools to a graph embedding, and outputs a diagonal Gaussian via two MLPs. Training maximizes the IWAE multi-sample lower bound (K=32 particles) using the reparameterization trick, jointly optimizing generative and inference parameters. This non-autoregressive design yields substantially faster training and generation than ARTree while achieving comparable KL divergence to ground truth on benchmark data sets.

## Results

On the eight benchmark sequence sets (DS1–DS8, 27–64 leaves), PhyloVAE with latent dimension d=10 achieves KL divergence to ground truth that is comparable to or better than the autoregressive ARTree baseline: it outperforms ARTree on DS3 (0.0477 vs. 0.0548), DS5 (0.5744 vs. 0.6266), and DS6 (0.2207 vs. 0.2360), while remaining competitive on the remaining sets. PhyloVAE with d=2 also performs reasonably well, e.g., 0.0529 on DS3 and 0.0100 on DS2, validating the low-dimensional representations used for visualization. Crucially, PhyloVAE's non-autoregressive generative model yields substantially faster training and generation than ARTree (Figure 5), while additionally providing a 2D latent space representation for tree topologies that no baseline method offers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyloVAE is compared against three baselines: SBN-EM and SBN-EM-α (Zhang & Matsen IV, 2018), which are Bayesian-network-based tree probability estimators, and ARTree (Xie & Zhang, 2023), a deep autoregressive model. Against SBN-EM and SBN-EM-α, PhyloVAE(d=10) achieves lower KL divergence on all eight data sets, with the largest margins on DS5 (0.5744 vs. 0.8599/0.8218) and DS6 (0.2207 vs. 0.3016/0.2786). Against ARTree, PhyloVAE(d=10) wins on three of eight sets (DS3, DS5, DS6) but trails on the remaining five, most notably on DS1 (0.0189 vs. 0.0045) and DS7 (0.0370 vs. 0.0191); the paper characterizes the overall comparison as 'comparable (if not better).' The decisive practical advantage of PhyloVAE over ARTree is speed: the non-autoregressive factorized generative model (Eq. 5) enables parallel computation of all edge-decision probabilities, yielding significantly reduced training and generation times (Figure 5) even with K=32 IWAE particles. Additionally, PhyloVAE uniquely provides a low-dimensional latent space representation of tree topologies (via the inference model's mean μ_τ), enabling direct visualization and pairwise distance computation that neither SBN-EM nor ARTree supports.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhyloVAE sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work on phylogenetic tree topologies: (1) density estimation methods like SBN-EM and ARTree that model the probability distribution over trees but offer no interpretable representation, and (2) distance-based visualization methods (MDS with Robinson-Foulds or path-difference metrics) that provide low-dimensional views but cannot map arbitrary points back to trees. The key architectural departure from ARTree is replacing the autoregressive factorization Q(τ)=∏Q(eₙ|e<ₙ) with a non-autoregressive, conditionally independent generative model p(τ|z)=∏p(sₙ|z) enabled by a latent variable, while retaining ARTree's linear-time encoding of tree topologies into integer vectors. The inference model reuses the Dirichlet-energy-based topological node embeddings and gated GNN message passing from Zhang (2023), but repurposes them for representation learning rather than direct phylogenetic inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhyloVAE is the first framework to simultaneously provide a probabilistic generative model and a low-dimensional latent representation for phylogenetic tree topologies, enabling both high-resolution density estimation and interpretable visualization (including mapping arbitrary latent-space points to valid trees, which MDS cannot do). The non-autoregressive design yields substantial speedups over ARTree in both training and generation while matching approximation accuracy, making it practical for large posterior samples. Limitations include the requirement of a fixed, pre-ordered leaf set (no handling of variable taxa), and the method is not designed for phylogenetic inference from sequence data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to trees with different leaf sets, integration with branch-length modeling, and application to downstream tasks like phylogenetic placement.
