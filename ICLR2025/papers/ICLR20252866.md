## Problem

Existing certification methods for neural networks provide provable guarantees on prediction invariance under adversarial perturbations, but they leave the calibration of confidence scores unprotected. The paper demonstrates that an attacker can severely degrade model calibration (ECE, Brier score) while leaving accuracy unchanged, even on adversarially trained models, creating a vulnerability in safety-critical systems that rely on reliable confidence estimates for decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252866/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a Gaussian smoothing classifier that provides per-sample certified radius R_n (prediction invariance, C1) and lower/upper confidence bounds (l, u) (C2) at a given adversarial budget R. It produces a closed-form certified Brier score (CBS) as a tight upper bound, an approximate certified calibration error (ACCE) obtained by solving a mixed-integer program over N samples and M bins via ADMM (converging in under 3000 steps, linear complexity O(NM)), and adversarial perturbations γ* for training. The ACCE output is a worst-case ECE value across all feasible confidence assignments within the certified bounds, not a per-sample quantity.

## Method

The paper introduces certified calibration through three components. First, a closed-form certified Brier score (Theorem 3.3) that maximizes the top-label Brier score over the confidence interval [l, u] per sample, yielding a tight bound since predictions are certified invariant. Second, the certified calibration error is reformulated as a mixed-integer program over binary bin-assignment variables a ∈ {0,1}^{NM} and continuous bin-specific confidences z ∈ [0,1]^{NM}, with constraints ensuring unique assignment, valid bin boundaries, and confidence certificates; this is solved with ADMM using split variables for the binary constraint (box ∩ sphere reformulation). Third, adversarial calibration training (ACT) extends SmoothAdv by replacing the cross-entropy adversary with a calibration adversary: Brier-ACT uses the Brier score directly, while ACCE-ACT reformulates the MIP w.r.t. perturbations γ (replacing z with a function h of the model output) and solves it jointly with PGD updates on γ and ADMM updates on the binning variables, yielding a new training objective that improves certified calibration at larger radii without sacrificing certified accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ACCE-ACT reduces the approximate certified calibration error (ACCE) from 56.36 to 47.08 (−12%) and the certified Brier score (CBS) from 36.25 to 24.87 (−31%) at certified radius R=1.0, while maintaining or improving certified accuracy. At R=0.5, Brier-ACT reduces ACCE from 49.30 to 41.32 (−16%) and CBS from 28.88 to 19.38 (−33%). For ACCE approximation, ADMM uniformly yields tighter (higher) bounds than the dECE gradient-ascent baseline, with differences up to ~0.2 in ACCE on ImageNet. The certified Brier score increases with radius, with small-σ models suffering ~100% increase at ε=0.25 while strongly smoothed models increase by <50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SmoothAdv (AT) baseline of Salman et al. (2019), both ACT flavors improve certified calibration without harming certified accuracy: at R=1.0, ACCE-ACT reduces ACCE by 12% (56.36→47.08) and CBS by 31% (36.25→24.87), while Brier-ACT reduces ACCE by 7.5% and CBS by 10.5% at R=0.5. ACCE-ACT outperforms Brier-ACT at R=1.0 on both metrics, while Brier-ACT is slightly better at smaller radii (R=0.05–0.5). Against the dECE gradient-ascent baseline of Bohdal et al. (2023) for approximating the certified calibration error, ADMM uniformly yields higher (tighter) ACCE bounds across all radii and smoothing parameters, with differences up to approximately 0.2 on ImageNet, indicating that solving the MIP is substantially more effective than differentiable calibration error maximization. The paper also shows that the Brier-confidence baseline (using the closed-form CBS-optimal confidences to compute ECE) is consistently lower than ADMM's ACCE, confirming that the Brier score bound does not tightly bound the ECE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the randomized smoothing certification framework (Cohen et al., 2019) and its confidence-bounding extension (Kumar et al., 2020) to a new object: calibration metrics. While prior certification work guarantees prediction invariance (C1) and per-sample confidence bounds (C2), no existing method provides worst-case guarantees on system-level calibration (ECE or Brier score) under adversarial perturbations. The paper also builds on the ACE attacks of Galil and El-Yaniv (2021) by generalizing them into a parameterized family (η,ω)-ACE that targets calibration rather than individual confidence scores, and on SmoothAdv (Salman et al., 2019) by replacing cross-entropy adversaries with calibration adversaries in the training loop. The MIP reformulation of CCE and its ADMM solver are novel contributions not present in prior calibration or certification literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that certifying accuracy is insufficient for safety-critical deployment: an attacker can increase ECE by over 12× on ImageNet while preserving all predictions, exposing a blind spot in current certified ML systems. The practical impact is tempered by the approximate nature of ACCE (ADMM-based) and the weak ACT improvements on ImageNet (1–2%), suggesting the approach is most useful at moderate radii on smaller datasets. Open questions include whether the CCE–CBS convergence observed at large radii holds generally, whether tighter certificates (e.g., CDF) substantially improve ACCE quality, and whether ACT can be made to scale effectively to ImageNet-scale models. The framework also raises the broader question of which other system-level properties (beyond calibration) should be certified in safety-critical ML.
