## Problem

State-of-the-art retrieval models are limited to narrow scenarios: text-only retrievers handle only text-to-text tasks, while multimodal retrievers assume homogeneous document formats and specific task types. No existing model accommodates the full spectrum of universal multimodal retrieval, where queries and documents can each be text, image, or interleaved text-image across diverse retrieval tasks. Additionally, when MLLMs are fine-tuned as retrievers, they exhibit a modality bias that causes them to retrieve text documents instead of images in cross-modal tasks, undermining their multimodal capability.

## Input / Output

The retriever encodes a multimodal query (text, image, or interleaved text-image) concatenated with a task-specific instruction into a single embedding vector, then performs k-nearest-neighbor search over a unified pool of 5.6M heterogeneous documents spanning 10 datasets. Images are tokenized into 576 global image tokens (24×24 patches) and processed through a CLIP image encoder and vision-language MLP projector before entering the LLM. For the reranking stage, the model takes a query-candidate pair and outputs a scalar relevance score computed as the softmax probability of the 'True' token from a binary True/False prompt.

## Method

The paper fine-tunes LLaVa-Next (Mistral 7B backbone) as a bi-encoder retriever using contrastive learning with task-specific instructions, but introduces modality-aware hard negative mining to counteract the MLLM's tendency to retrieve text over images in cross-modal tasks. Two types of hard negatives are mined from a random-negative-trained model: (1) candidates with the wrong modality that rank above the positive, and (2) candidates with the correct modality but unsatisfactory content that rank below position 45; these are sampled with equal probability during training. To preserve strong text-to-text retrieval, the model undergoes continuous fine-tuning on 11 public text retrieval datasets (MS MARCO, HotpotQA, FEVER, etc.) with hard negatives mined by NV-Embed-v1, uniformly mixed with multimodal data. The final model, MM-Embed, is initialized from NV-Embed-v1 (replacing the LLM component of LLaVa-Next) and achieves SOTA on M-BEIR while surpassing NV-Embed-v1 on MTEB text retrieval. Separately, the paper demonstrates that zero-shot prompting of the un-fine-tuned LLaVa-Next as a reranker over top-10 candidates significantly boosts accuracy on challenging interleaved text-image query tasks (e.g., +7 mAP@5 on CIRCO).

## Results

On the M-BEIR universal multimodal retrieval benchmark (16 tasks, 10 datasets, 5.6M candidate pool), MM-Embed achieves 52.7 average R@5, surpassing the best CLIP-based baseline CLIP_SF (47.4 with random negatives, 48.3 with hard negatives) and the best MLLM-based retriever M^hard(NVEmb) (52.3). On the MTEB text retrieval benchmark (15 datasets), MM-Embed scores 60.3 nDCG@10, exceeding the original NV-Embed-v1 (59.4) while retaining multimodal capability. On the CIRCO composed image retrieval benchmark, zero-shot MLLM reranking boosts mAP@5 by over 7 points (e.g., 32.4 → 40.9 for M^hard(NVEmb)), outperforming the prior SOTA MagicLens (24.9 → 32.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP_SF (Wei et al., 2023), the prior SOTA for universal multimodal retrieval, MM-Embed improves M-BEIR average R@5 from 47.4 (random negatives) / 48.3 (hard negatives) to 52.7, with the largest gains on multi-modal query tasks (47.0 vs. 40.1/39.5). Against MagicLens (Zhang et al., 2024) and E5-V (Jiang et al., 2024), which are strong on specific composed-image-retrieval tasks, MM-Embed achieves 52.7 vs. 5.8 and 11.5 on M-BEIR average, and 32.3 vs. 24.9 and 19.1 on CIRCO mAP@5, demonstrating that a single universal retriever can match or exceed task-specific models. Against NV-Embed-v1 (Lee et al., 2024), the text-only retriever used as the LLM backbone, MM-Embed surpasses it on MTEB text retrieval (60.3 vs. 59.4 nDCG@10) while simultaneously achieving state-of-the-art multimodal retrieval, confirming that the continuous fine-tuning strategy preserves and even enhances text capability. Notably, modality-aware hard negative mining is critical: without it, M^rand(NVEmb) scores only 47.2 on M-BEIR and exhibits severe modality bias (42.1% modality accuracy on text-to-image retrieval), whereas M^hard(NVEmb) reaches 52.3 with 100% modality accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal multimodal retrieval framework introduced by Wei et al. (2023, UniIR/M-BEIR), which was limited to CLIP-based retrievers, by being the first to fine-tune MLLMs (LLaVA-Next with Mistral-7B) as bi-encoder retrievers for this task. It adopts the instruction-aware retrieval paradigm from Asai et al. (2023) to handle diverse search intents, and initializes from NV-Embed-v1 (Lee et al., 2024) to preserve strong text-to-text retrieval capability that prior multimodal retrieval work ignored. The key methodological departure is modality-aware hard negative mining, which addresses a modality bias unique to MLLM-based retrievers that does not manifest in CLIP-based systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MM-Embed is the first model to simultaneously achieve SOTA on the M-BEIR universal multimodal retrieval benchmark and top-5 on MTEB text retrieval, bridging a gap where prior approaches excelled in one modality at the expense of the other. The finding that zero-shot MLLM rerankers (simple True/False prompts) can boost mAP@5 by 7+ points on composed image retrieval without any fine-tuning suggests a practical two-stage pipeline for challenging multimodal queries. Open questions include distilling the 7B MLLM retriever into smaller CLIP/BLIP models for deployment efficiency (current storage is 86GB vs. 16GB for CLIP), extending to audio/video modalities, and developing better curriculum learning strategies to avoid the trade-off between multimodal and text retrieval capabilities.
