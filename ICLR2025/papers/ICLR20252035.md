## Problem

LLM training and inference face unsustainable computational costs, with model sizes doubling every 3.4 months and training budgets escalating from $64M (GPT-4) to $191M (Gemini Ultra), while Moore's Law decelerates. No prior work has successfully mapped existing neural network architectures onto dynamical system (DS) machines; previous DS approaches ignored network structure and directly fit data to energy landscapes, which is infeasible for complex NLP tasks. This paper asks whether well-researched Transformer architectures can be repurposed to guide the energy landscape of DS-machines, enabling them to execute LLM computations through Natural Annealing.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text token embeddings as input to the DS-machine, where each token's features are encoded as voltages on nano-scale capacitors (spins). The forward pass produces next-token logits as the stabilized spin voltages at the output layer after Natural Annealing converges to the Hamiltonian's minimum. During ECL training, the ground-truth output is mapped onto output spins, and the physical current difference between incoming and reference currents at each node serves as the gradient signal, eliminating the need for digital output readout.

## Method

DS-LLM maps each Transformer component onto the DS-machine's Hamiltonian by constructing a target function whose minimum corresponds to the desired computation. For linear layers, the minimization of ||Y_DS − X_DS W^T||² reduces to the Hamiltonian form with spins split into input (x) and output (y) groups, coupling parameters J set to 2w_ij, and self-reaction h=1 for outputs. The attention mechanism is decomposed into: (a) Query-Key multiplication, where feature matrices are mapped onto programmable resistors via spin-to-control-port connections; (b) exponential approximation via 3rd-order Taylor expansion; and (c) weighted-sum, mapped analogously to the linear layer. For training, the Electric-Current-Loss (ECL) method exploits the fact that at convergence, the current imbalance I_L^i = I_in^i − I_R^i equals the softmax cross-entropy gradient, enabling hardware-native gradient descent by feeding the loss current back to programmable resistor control ports. The paper proves mathematical equivalence between DS-LLM and traditional LLMs for both inference and training, and validates the approach on models from GPT-2-124M to LLaMA-2-7B, reporting ~5300× training speedup and ~240× inference speedup with ~230,000× and ~6400× energy reduction respectively.

## Results

Across models from GPT-2-124M to LLaMA-2-7B, DS-LLM achieves an average 5.3×10³ training speedup and 2.4×10² inference speedup over GPU baselines, with energy reductions of 2.3×10⁵ (training) and 6.4×10³ (inference). Accuracy on five GLUE datasets is maintained within ~1% of the original LLMs, with some DS-LLM variants even slightly outperforming their GPU counterparts (e.g., GPT2-DS scores 76.72% on MRPC vs. 75.00% for GPT2). For Llama2-7B inference, DS-LLM reaches 2.80×10⁵ tokens/s and 1.95×10¹⁰ tokens/KWh, compared to 398 tokens/s and 3.82×10⁶ tokens/KWh on A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-2, OPT, and Llama2-7B GPU baselines, DS-LLM matches or marginally exceeds accuracy on all five GLUE tasks (e.g., Llama2-7B-DS scores 89.47% on MRPC vs. 90.01% for the original; OPT-2.7B-DS-ECL actually beats the original on SST2 at 94.25% vs. 94.08%) while delivering 167×–489× training throughput and 704×–537× inference token-rate improvements. Compared to the low-precision CPU inference baseline (Shen et al., 2023) on Llama2-7B, DS-LLM achieves 3.03×10⁴ tokens/s versus 45.4 tokens/s (~667× faster) and 4.04×10¹⁰ tokens/KWh versus 1.63×10⁶ tokens/KWh (~24,800× more energy-efficient). Against Cambricon-LLM (Yu et al., 2024), the current SOTA edge accelerator for LLM inference, DS-LLM is ~8,500× faster in token generation rate (3.03×10⁴ vs. 3.55 tokens/s) and ~11,200× more energy-efficient (4.04×10¹⁰ vs. 3.60×10⁶ tokens/KWh). The ECL online training variant (DS-LLM-ECL) shows a small accuracy drop of 0.2–1.0% relative to offline-trained DS-LLM, confirming feasibility of on-device training without sacrificing quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DS-machine line of work initiated by NP-GL (Wu et al., 2024), which applied Natural Annealing to graph learning by directly fitting data to an energy landscape while ignoring neural network architecture. DS-LLM departs from that approach by preserving the full Transformer computational structure—mapping each layer's operations (matrix multiplication, exponential, weighted sum) onto the Hamiltonian of a CMOS-based DS-machine—and introduces an Electric-Current-Loss (ECL) training method that exploits physical currents as hardware-native gradients. The work also positions itself against other alternative computing paradigms (quantum, optical, computing-in-memory), arguing that DS-machines offer a near-term, CMOS-compatible path where those technologies face maturity barriers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the simulation-based speedup and energy-reduction claims (up to ~5,000× training, ~240× inference) translate to fabricated hardware, DS-LLM could fundamentally alter the economics of LLM scaling. However, all performance numbers come from FEA and Cadence 45 nm simulations with no physical chip validation, the ECL training method currently supports only single-layer DS-machines, and the exponential is approximated by a 3rd-order Taylor expansion. Key open questions include real-hardware noise and non-ideality effects at scale, multi-chip communication overhead for models beyond 7B, and whether the analog precision ceiling (ADC/DAC bit-depth) constrains accuracy for larger or more sensitive models.
