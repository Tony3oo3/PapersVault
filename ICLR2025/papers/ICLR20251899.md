## Problem

Existing RAG methods either rely on text-based vector retrieval that misses structured entity relationships, or on KG-based retrieval that suffers from inherent knowledge incompleteness. Hybrid RAG approaches like CoK and GraphRAG aggregate results from both sources but do not use one knowledge source to improve retrieval on the other, leaving them unable to perform the iterative, in-depth multi-hop reasoning that complex questions demand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251899/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question, a knowledge graph (e.g., Wikidata), and a document database (e.g., Wikipedia) as inputs. At each iteration it consumes the current set of topic entities and their triple paths to drive both graph exploration and document retrieval, and produces either a final answer or a set of intermediate 'clues' that re-formulate the query for the next round. The framework is training-free and plug-and-play: it requires no fine-tuning of the LLM or retrieval model, and can be applied to any KG–document pair, with entities extractable from documents alone if no pre-built KG exists.

## Method

ToG-2 introduces a tight-coupling KG × Text RAG paradigm that iteratively alternates between knowledge-guided graph search and knowledge-guided context retrieval. In graph search, the LLM prunes relations from the current topic entities on the KG (via a single combined prompt for efficiency), then discovers connected candidate entities; in context retrieval, a dense retrieval model (BGE-embedding) scores document chunks for each candidate entity using a triple-sentence-augmented query, and the top-scoring chunks are used to prune entities back to a fixed width W for the next graph exploration. This bidirectional coupling—KG structure guides which documents to fetch, and document relevance scores guide which graph paths to retain—is the key distinction from loose-coupling hybrids that merely concatenate retrieval results. After each iteration, the LLM evaluates accumulated triple paths and context chunks to either produce an answer or emit clues that re-formulate the search, continuing up to a maximum depth D (default 3) with a width W (default 3).

## Results

With GPT-3.5-turbo as the backbone, ToG-2 achieves state-of-the-art performance on 6 out of 7 knowledge-intensive datasets: 81.1% EM on WebQSP, 42.9% EM on AdvHotpotQA, 54.1% EM on QALD-10-en, 91.0% EM on Zero-Shot RE, 63.1% Acc. on FEVER, and 93.5% Acc. on Creak. On the domain-specific ToG-FinQA dataset (Chinese financial statements), ToG-2 reaches 34.0% EM, substantially outperforming all baselines. ToG-2 also elevates smaller models (e.g., Llama-3-8B from 20.8% to 34.7% on AdvHotpotQA, a 66.8% relative gain) to the level of GPT-3.5-turbo's direct reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToG-2 outperforms CoT (Wei et al., 2022) and CoT-SC (Wang et al., 2023) on all six open-source datasets, with the largest margins on AdvHotpotQA (42.9% vs. 30.8%/34.4%) and Zero-Shot RE (91.0% vs. 28.8%/45.4%), demonstrating that iterative KG-guided retrieval far exceeds pure prompting strategies for multi-hop reasoning. Against the KG-based ToG (Sun et al., 2024), ToG-2 achieves a substantial +16.6% gain on AdvHotpotQA (42.9% vs. 26.3%) and +4.9% on WebQSP, while ToG retains a marginal 0.3% edge on Creak (93.8% vs. 93.5%), a dataset where facts are verifiable from Wikidata triples alone. Compared to the hybrid CoK (Li et al., 2024), ToG-2 wins on five of six datasets, most notably Zero-Shot RE (91.0% vs. 75.5%) and AdvHotpotQA (42.9% vs. 35.4%), though CoK slightly leads on FEVER (63.5% vs. 63.1%), which the authors attribute to FEVER's predominantly single-hop nature. On the domain-specific ToG-FinQA, ToG-2 (34.0%) dramatically outperforms GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024) at 6.2%, highlighting the advantage of tight-coupling over loose-coupling hybrid retrieval for unseen, knowledge-intensive domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

ToG-2 directly extends Think-on-Graph (ToG, Sun et al. 2024), which performs multi-hop entity exploration over knowledge graphs, by introducing a tight-coupling mechanism where KG structure guides document retrieval and document context in turn refines graph exploration. This departs from prior hybrid RAG methods (CoK, GraphRAG, HybridRAG) that aggregate structured and unstructured knowledge in a loose-coupling fashion without allowing one source to improve retrieval on the other. The iterative feedback loop—where context-based entity pruning selects the next round's graph search targets and KG-guided relation pruning scopes document retrieval—represents a conceptual shift from parallel retrieval to sequential, mutually-informative retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of ToG-2 makes it immediately deployable across LLMs and knowledge sources, and the demonstrated ability to elevate 7-13B parameter models to GPT-3.5-level reasoning has practical implications for cost-sensitive deployments. The new ToG-FinQA dataset addresses a real gap in evaluating RAG on domain-specific, pre-training-unseen knowledge. Open questions include how the method scales to very large or noisy KGs (the 30% completeness experiment shows significant degradation), whether the tight-coupling advantage persists with stronger retrieval models, and whether the EM-based evaluation underestimates true performance given the observed false negatives.
