## Problem

Existing symmetry-based methods for zero-shot coordination in multi-agent settings require a priori knowledge of Dec-POMDP symmetries (e.g., color permutations in Hanabi), which are computationally infeasible to discover automatically in large-scale environments. Moreover, Dec-POMDP symmetries only capture relabeling of actions and observations, missing broader coordination equivalences such as distinct but equally optimal communication protocols that are incompatible in cross-play. This paper introduces expected return symmetries—a strictly broader symmetry group learnable without privileged environment information—to address both limitations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed pool of self-play optimal Boltzmann-exploratory policies (each trained with a different random seed) and Dec-POMDP environment interactions, requiring no ground-truth symmetries, dynamics model, or privileged access. It produces a set of learned transformation functions (neural networks over observation spaces paired with fixed action transpositions) that approximate expected return symmetries, which are then used to train OP-constrained joint policies. The final output is a deployed policy per agent, evaluated by within-population cross-play scores. A key constraint is that transformations are restricted to bijections on action and observation spaces, with the state transformation fixed to identity.

## Method

The paper defines the group of expected return symmetries (ER symmetries) as the set of transformations in Ψ that map self-play optimal Boltzmann-exploratory policies to other self-play optimal Boltzmann-exploratory policies, provably containing Dec-POMDP symmetries as a subgroup. To learn these symmetries, the authors optimize a policy-gradient objective that maximizes the expected return of the transformed policy over a pool of frozen optimal policies, with the state map fixed to identity and action maps restricted to fixed transpositions searched over a combinatorial space. Three algorithmic variants are provided: a basic policy-gradient search (Eq. 9), a regularized version that enforces approximate compositionality and invertibility via additional loss terms (Eq. 10), and a cross-play maximization approach that directly optimizes XP between policy pairs (Eq. 11). The learned symmetries are then plugged into the Other-Play objective, which trains each agent to be compatible with randomly sampled members of its own equivalence class under the learned group, thereby preventing mutually incompatible symmetry breaking at test time without any a priori symmetry knowledge.

## Results

On Hanabi, the proposed OP^Φ^ER method achieves a mean cross-play (XP) score of 21.64±0.07, substantially outperforming the IPPO+OP^Φ^MDP baseline (8.61±0.17) and the IPPO self-play baseline (4.02±0.17), while maintaining near-optimal self-play scores (23.74 vs. 24.04). On Overcooked V2, the ER symmetry population achieves a mean XP of 15.8 compared to 6.74 for the IPPO self-play baseline, significantly reducing the SP-XP gap. In the Cat/Dog environment, ER symmetries enable optimal grounded communication where no non-trivial Dec-POMDP symmetries exist, whereas cognitive hierarchy methods fail entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) IPPO self-play (Yu et al., 2022) and (2) IPPO+OP^Φ^MDP, which applies Other-Play with all 120 known Dec-POMDP symmetries (Hu et al., 2020). On Hanabi, OP^Φ^ER achieves an XP of 21.64±0.07, a 2.5× improvement over OP^Φ^MDP (8.61±0.17) and a 5.4× improvement over IPPO self-play (4.02±0.17), while sacrificing only 0.3 points of self-play return. On Overcooked V2, the ER symmetry population achieves mean XP of 15.8 versus 6.74 for the IPPO baseline, with the bimodal XP distribution of the baseline being largely eliminated. Notably, the ER symmetry method outperforms even the Dec-POMDP symmetry baseline that has privileged access to all 120 environment symmetries, demonstrating that learned ER symmetries capture coordination-relevant equivalences beyond mere relabeling. In the Cat/Dog environment, where no non-trivial Dec-POMDP symmetries exist (so OP^Φ^MDP reduces to self-play), ER symmetries still successfully identify the two incompatible optimal policies and select the grounded communication strategy, whereas cognitive hierarchy methods converge to suboptimal 'bailing'.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Other-Play (OP) framework of Hu et al. (2020), which used Dec-POMDP symmetries to prevent mutually incompatible symmetry breaking in zero-shot coordination. The key departure is defining a strictly broader group—expected return symmetries—that captures equivalences between self-play-optimal policies beyond mere relabeling of actions and observations, and can be learned entirely model-free via policy gradient optimization. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of symmetry-based RL (Van der Pol et al., 2020; Muglich et al., 2022a) and value-based abstraction (Rezaei-Shoshtari et al., 2022; Grimm et al., 2021), but differs by acting on the policy space rather than the state space and requiring no access to environment dynamics or ground-truth symmetries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first symmetry-based ZSC method that requires no a priori environment information, making it applicable in settings where Dec-POMDP symmetries are trivial or unknown (e.g., the cat/dog game) and where competing approaches like off-belief learning or cognitive hierarchies fail. A key limitation is the restriction to bijections on action and observation spaces, which the authors acknowledge is insufficient in some environments (e.g., the two-lever game). Open questions include whether broader transformation classes (beyond bijections) can be learned, whether the approach extends to ad-hoc teamwork or single-agent settings, and how sensitive the method is to the quality and diversity of the pre-trained policy pool used for symmetry discovery.
