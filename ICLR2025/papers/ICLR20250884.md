## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models struggle to maintain consistent subject identity across multiple generated images for storytelling and animation applications. Existing approaches require extensive training, fine-tuning, or complex architectural modifications, and often sacrifice text-image alignment in the process. The paper identifies an overlooked property of language models—context consistency—where identity information is implicitly maintained through contextual understanding when multiple scene descriptions appear within a single prompt.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250884/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single consolidated text prompt that concatenates an identity prompt (describing the subject's attributes) with N frame prompts (each describing a different scenario) into one long sentence, rather than using separate prompts per frame as in prior work. It produces N identity-consistent 1024×1024 images, each aligned with its corresponding frame description, using the SDXL latent diffusion model. The approach is entirely training-free and requires no reference images, unlike methods such as IP-Adapter or PhotoMaker that need an additional input image.

## Method

The core insight is that when all frame descriptions are concatenated into a single prompt, the CLIP text encoder's self-attention mechanism naturally preserves identity consistency across frames, as evidenced by tighter clustering of frame prompt embeddings in text space compared to the multi-prompt setup. To ensure each frame is individually expressed while maintaining identity, the method applies Singular-Value Reweighting (SVR): it performs SVD on the current frame's token embedding plus the EOT token to amplify its semantics (SVR+), then iteratively applies SVD on each other frame's embedding to suppress them (SVR-). Additionally, Identity-Preserving Cross-Attention (IPCA) zeros out the key and value features corresponding to frame prompts in the cross-attention layers, retaining only the identity prompt's features, and concatenates these filtered features with the original ones to reinforce subject consistency. The entire pipeline operates at inference time without modifying any model parameters, making it compatible with any SDXL-based diffusion model.

## Results

On the ConsiStory+ benchmark (200 prompt sets, up to 1500 images), 1Prompt1Story achieves a CLIP-T score of 0.8942, the highest among all training-free methods and close to vanilla SDXL (0.9074). For identity consistency, it scores 0.9117 on CLIP-I and 0.1993 on DreamSim (lower is better), ranking second only to IP-Adapter (0.9429 / 0.1462) among all methods. The method requires 18.70 GB memory and 23.2 s inference time per image, comparable to other training-free baselines like ConsiStory (34.55 GB, 34.6 s) and StoryDiffusion (45.61 GB, 25.7 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free methods, 1Prompt1Story outperforms ConsiStory (CLIP-T 0.8769, DreamSim 0.3188) and StoryDiffusion (CLIP-T 0.8877, DreamSim 0.3212) on both prompt alignment and identity consistency, while also using less memory (18.70 GB vs. 34.55/45.61 GB). Against training-based methods, it surpasses PhotoMaker (CLIP-T 0.8651, DreamSim 0.3996), The Chosen One (CLIP-T 0.7614, DreamSim 0.4929), and Textual Inversion (CLIP-T 0.8378, DreamSim 0.4268) on all three metrics. The only method that beats 1Prompt1Story on identity metrics is IP-Adapter (CLIP-I 0.9429, DreamSim 0.1462), but the authors note this advantage stems from IP-Adapter generating repetitive poses and backgrounds rather than true identity preservation; in a user study, 1Prompt1Story was preferred by 48.6% of participants versus 8.6% for IP-Adapter. On prompt alignment (CLIP-T), 1Prompt1Story (0.8942) also exceeds IP-Adapter (0.8458) by nearly 5 points, indicating better text-image correspondence per frame.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in consistent T2I generation, which relies on training-based personalization (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, PhotoMaker), reference-image conditioning (IP-Adapter, BLIP-Diffusion), or complex architectural modifications (StoryDiffusion's shared self-attention, ConsiStory's iterative clustering). Instead, it repurposes the self-attention mechanism already present in the CLIP text encoder, observing that concatenating all frame prompts into a single input inherently preserves identity through what the authors term 'context consistency'—a property of Transformer-based language models that prior consistent-T2I work had overlooked. The two refinement techniques (Singular-Value Reweighting and Identity-Preserving Cross-Attention) operate purely on text embeddings and cross-attention maps at inference time, requiring no parameter updates or new modules, positioning the method as a lightweight, model-agnostic post-hoc intervention on any CLIP-conditioned diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, reference-image-free nature of 1Prompt1Story makes it immediately applicable to any community SDXL-based model without fine-tuning, lowering the barrier for consistent character generation in animation and storytelling pipelines. However, the method requires all frame prompts to be known in advance and is bounded by the text encoder's maximum token length (mitigated only partially by a sliding-window trick that introduces identity drift over very long stories). The modest quantitative gains over StoryDiffusion (e.g., DreamSim 0.1993 vs. 0.3212, but CLIP-T 0.8942 vs. 0.8877) suggest the context-consistency insight, while elegant, does not yet fully close the gap with methods that explicitly share attention across frames. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video diffusion models, multi-subject scenes with dynamic subject entry/exit, and whether the identity drift in long stories can be addressed without reverting to training-based solutions.
