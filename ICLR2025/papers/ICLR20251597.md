## Problem

Episodic RL (ERL) methods predict entire action trajectories rather than per-step actions, enabling smooth exploration and capturing temporal correlations, but they are almost universally constrained to on-policy training because standard TD-based critics cannot evaluate the value of multi-step action sequences. This limitation prevents ERL from leveraging the sample efficiency gains of off-policy architectures like SAC, leaving a significant gap in data efficiency for tasks with challenging exploration.

## Input / Output

The policy network takes a single initial observation (robot state including position and velocity, plus task objective) and outputs a Gaussian distribution over ProDMP parameter vectors (typically 20–50 dimensions), from which a full action trajectory of T timesteps is generated in closed form. The Transformer critic consumes segments of length L (randomly sampled per update) consisting of one starting-state token followed by L action tokens, and produces L+1 output values: a state value V(s₀) and Q-values for progressively longer action prefixes. A causal mask ensures each action token only attends to past tokens, and the segment length L is randomized across update iterations to regularize the critic.

## Method

TOP-ERL introduces a decoder-only Transformer as a sequence-value critic that estimates Q(s₀, a₀, …, a_{N-1}) for action sequences of varying length N within a segment, trained with N-step squared TD error targets computed from the replay buffer. Because the action sequences are fixed (read from the buffer) rather than sampled from the current policy, the N-step return targets require no importance sampling ratios, avoiding the variance explosion that plagues off-policy N-step returns. For the V-function target, a new MP parameter vector is sampled from the current policy and its trajectory is generated via ProDMPs with the segment's starting state enforced as the initial condition, ensuring state-action consistency in the target computation. The policy is updated via a SAC-style reparameterization trick that maximizes the expected Q-value over all prefix lengths within each segment, with a Trust Region Projection Layer enforcing KL-bounded updates on the full-covariance Gaussian over MP parameters. Random segment lengths (rather than fixed K=25 as in TCE) serve as a critical regularizer, allowing the Transformer to attend to multiple time horizons and yielding substantially higher asymptotic performance.

## Results

On MetaWorld MT50, TOP-ERL achieves a 98% success rate after 10 million samples, matching TCE's asymptotic performance but reaching it in half the samples (20M for TCE), while SAC plateaus at 85% after the same 10M budget. On the dense-reward Box Pushing task, TOP-ERL reaches 80% success at 10M samples versus 60% for TCE at 50M samples; on the sparse-reward variant, TOP-ERL achieves 70% at 14M samples while TCE and gSDE require 50M samples for only 60%. Step-based methods (SAC, PPO, PINK) fail entirely on both Box Pushing variants, and GTrXL fails on the sparse-reward setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOP-ERL outperforms all seven baselines on the challenging exploration tasks (Hopper Jump, Box Pushing dense/sparse), where step-based methods (SAC, PPO, PINK) fail entirely and GTrXL collapses on sparse rewards. Against the strongest ERL baseline TCE, TOP-ERL achieves the same asymptotic MetaWorld MT50 success (98%) in half the samples (10M vs. 20M) and substantially outperforms it on Box Pushing (80% vs. 60% dense; 70% vs. 60% sparse), demonstrating a 4–5× sample-efficiency gain. Among step-based methods, gSDE is the closest competitor on Hopper Jump and sparse Box Pushing (60% at 50M), but still trails TOP-ERL by 10 points and requires 3.5× more samples. BBRL (both standard and full-covariance variants) shows similar learning dynamics to TCE but converges more slowly, confirming that the off-policy Transformer critic is the key differentiator rather than the MP representation alone. GTrXL, the only Transformer-based baseline, achieves moderate performance on dense-reward tasks but fails on sparse rewards, highlighting that TOP-ERL's advantage comes from its N-step return critic design rather than the Transformer architecture per se.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOP-ERL extends the episodic RL (ERL) lineage—particularly BBRL and TCE, which use movement primitives for trajectory-level policy search but are confined to on-policy black-box optimization—by introducing an off-policy update mechanism. It adapts the SAC-style reparameterization trick and critic-guided policy updates to the ERL setting, while the Transformer-based critic for action-sequence value estimation is novel in the online model-free RL context (prior Transformer-in-RL work focused on offline RL or POMDP memory). The trajectory generation relies on ProDMPs, which provide closed-form solutions enabling efficient initial-condition enforcement for target computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TOP-ERL is the first off-policy ERL algorithm capable of handling contextual tasks, and it substantially improves sample efficiency over prior ERL methods (e.g., 80% success at 10M samples vs. TCE's 60% at 50M on Box Pushing). A key open limitation is that trajectories are generated only at episode start, precluding mid-episode replanning for dynamic or changing targets; the authors also note the method is not designed for POMDPs. The finding that a single V-target network suffices without double-Q or clipping—attributed to N-step returns reducing bias—challenges standard off-policy RL practice and could inform simpler critic designs in related settings.
