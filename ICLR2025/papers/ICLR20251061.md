## Problem

In neural language models, next-token prediction (NTP) is computed as a softmax over dot products between a context-encoded query and fixed vocabulary key embeddings. The paper identifies that this fixed key space introduces spurious correlations: tokens that are distributionally similar to top-ranked tokens (e.g., all capitalized letters A–Z) are over-ranked by NTP regardless of whether they lead to correct decoding paths, hurting sampling diversity and making correct long-tail answers nearly unreachable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a knowledge-probing question like 'Show me some black and white animals') plus a growing list of previously explored first-tokens and their decoded answers. It iteratively produces new query representations that are pushed away from the explored key clusters, yielding new top-ranked first-tokens and their greedy-decoded answers. The number of iterations (#Query) and tokens probed per iteration (#Key) are tunable hyperparameters whose product equals the total number of probed paths.

## Method

In-Context Navigation (ICN) mitigates spurious key correlation by explicitly appending previously explored answers to the prompt with an instruction to generate something different (e.g., 'A computer scientist other than Alan Turing is'). This causes the LM to encode a new query representation with low dot products against the explored keys, effectively navigating the query away from those clusters in the key space. The process is iterative: each round decodes the top-#Key first-tokens, appends them to the context, and re-encodes to obtain the next query, with already-probed tokens skipped in subsequent rounds. The paper validates ICN's navigation ability via query-key similarity metrics and shows it outperforms rephrasing and cluster-based reranking baselines on knowledge probing, open-ended generation (improving n-gram diversity and ZeroGen classifier accuracy), and chain-of-thought self-consistency (improving reasoning accuracy on GSM8K, SVAMP, and AQuA). Additionally, the paper demonstrates that the key space is essentially frozen after pre-training (unchanged through SFT and RLHF), meaning fine-tuning only adjusts the query encoder and can inadvertently generalize learned knowledge to spurious same-cluster tokens rather than to other correct tokens.

## Results

On the 12-category knowledge probing benchmark (ProbeSet), ICN with #Query=10, #Key=5 achieves substantial MAP@50 improvements over vanilla decoding across all categories, e.g., 82.23 vs. 73.82 on Sports League, 97.60 vs. 87.63 on Company, and 96.38 vs. 91.57 on City. In open-ended generation, ICN improves unique n-gram diversity (UNG) by 2–3 points and ZeroGen accuracy by 1–2 points over top-token probing (e.g., 64.74 UNG / 86.33 ZGN on SST-2 vs. 61.29 / 84.90). For chain-of-thought self-consistency, ICN yields 78.09% on GSM8K, 85.10% on SVAMP, and 58.66% on AQuA, outperforming both random sampling and top-token probing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICN consistently and substantially outperforms the vanilla top-50 decoding baseline across all 12 knowledge-probing categories, with MAP gains ranging from +2.0 (Astronomical: 78.02 vs. 75.98) to +10.0 (Company: 97.60 vs. 87.63) and +8.4 (Sports League: 82.23 vs. 73.82). The rephrasing baseline (rephrasing the prompt 10 times and probing top-5 keys each) shows negligible improvement over vanilla, sometimes even slightly worse, confirming that simple prompt perturbation cannot navigate the query away from explored key clusters. The reranking baseline (penalizing tokens by in-cluster rank) yields small but consistent gains (+0.5 to +1.0 MAP) over vanilla, validating the spurious-correlation hypothesis, yet remains 5–10 MAP points below ICN, underscoring the importance of generating multiple navigated queries rather than post-hoc rescoring. For chain-of-thought self-consistency, ICN (78.09% GSM8K, 85.10% SVAMP, 58.66% AQuA) beats both the random-sampling self-consistency baseline of Wang et al. (2022) (76.50/83.10/55.51) and the top-first-token probing strategy of Wang & Zhou (2024) (77.56/84.20/57.09), though the margin is smaller than in knowledge probing, suggesting that longer generations partially dilute the first-token navigation benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions NTP as a dense retrieval problem (query-key matching) and builds on prior identifications of NTP limitations such as the softmax bottleneck (Yang et al., 2018), probability bounds for rare words (Demeter et al., 2020), and multi-mode distribution failures (Chang & McCallum, 2022). However, it departs from these works by specifically isolating and quantifying *spurious correlation*—the phenomenon where context-agnostic key-space similarity (e.g., all capital letters clustering together) inflates the NTP rank of semantically irrelevant tokens. The proposed In-Context Navigation (ICN) method repurposes the LM's own instruction-following capability as a navigation mechanism, distinguishing it from prior decoding strategies (nucleus sampling, contrastive decoding) that operate on the probability distribution rather than on the query representation itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the vocabulary key space is essentially frozen during SFT/RLHF, and that fine-tuning a correct token preferentially boosts same-cluster tokens over other correct tokens, raises a concrete concern about the generalization semantics of NTP training—knowledge may propagate along spurious embedding geometry rather than semantic correctness. ICN offers a practical, training-free mitigation that improves probing, open-ended generation, and CoT self-consistency, but it requires multiple forward passes and depends on the LM's instruction-following quality. Open questions include whether contextualized key representations or reranking stages can address the root cause, and whether the spurious-correlation effect persists under alternative architectures (e.g., mixture-of-experts output heads).
