## Problem

Learning to forecast high-dimensional time-series (e.g., image sequences) across diverse dynamical systems requires adapting a latent dynamics function to each system, but real-world streams present non-stationary task distributions with unknown boundaries and identifiers. Existing meta-learning assumes a stationary, labeled task distribution, and prior continual meta-learning (CML) work is limited to image classification or low-dimensional regression with gradient-based (MAML) meta-learners that generalize poorly to latent dynamics forecasting. No framework existed to continually learn what the current system is, how to adapt to it, and when a shift has occurred, all without task labels.

## Input / Output

The model receives 3 initial frames of 32×32 grayscale images (from bouncing-ball, pendulum, mass-spring, or two-body Hamiltonian simulations) and forecasts 20 timesteps ahead. At test time, a context set of k=15 sequences from the current task is encoded into a task embedding, which a hypernetwork maps to task-specific Neural ODE parameters; the encoder then processes the 3-frame input, the dynamics function integrates forward, and the decoder emits the 20-frame forecast. The continual component maintains a fixed-size reservoir (4500 samples, ~25% of total data) of past sequences with inferred task labels or cluster assignments to enable bi-level meta-optimization.

## Method

CoSFan combines a feed-forward what-how meta-model with a when mechanism for continual slow adaptation. The what component uses a context encoder to aggregate k context sequences into a shared embedding c(T_j); the how component is a hypernetwork that maps this embedding directly to task-specific parameters of a Neural ODE dynamics function, eliminating gradient-based inner-loop adaptation at test time. For the when component, a loss-threshold mechanism detects task boundaries, after which either Task-Aware Reservoir Sampling (assigning pseudo-labels via a boundary counter) or Task-Relational Experience Replay (fitting a Bayesian GMM on context embeddings to discover task clusters and relations) re-balances the reservoir and enables correct context-query pairing for bi-level meta-optimization. A cluster embedding regularization term (MSE between meta-embeddings and their nearest GMM component mean) further structures the embedding space. The feed-forward hypernetwork provides a stronger algorithmic prior than MAML for this domain, achieving faster adaptation (single forward pass vs. sequential gradient steps) and better predictive performance when paired with task-aware or task-relational replay.

## Results

On gravity-6, CoSFan (Feed-Forward + Task-Aware Reservoir) achieves the best MSE of 0.0114 (LP) and 0.0120 (RP), with near-zero interference (BTI = −0.0006), outperforming MAML[5-Step] + Task-Aware (MSE 0.0156 LP / 0.0156 RP) by ~27% in learning performance. On mixed-physics, the same configuration achieves MSE 0.0141 (LP) and 0.0154 (RP), again the best among all meta-model and continual-strategy combinations. In terms of adaptation efficiency, the feed-forward approach requires only 0.0017 s to adapt across 12 tasks (TTA-12), compared to 0.7475 s for MAML[5-Step] + Task-Aware — a 440× speedup — while also reducing total training time from 302.8 min to 85.2 min.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSFan (Feed-Forward + Task-Aware) decisively outperforms all MAML-based variants: on gravity-6 it reduces LP MSE from 0.0129–0.0165 (MAML) to 0.0114, and RP MSE from 0.0156–0.0199 to 0.0120, while eliminating catastrophic forgetting (BTI ≈ 0 vs. −0.006 to −0.007 for MAML + Task-Agnostic). Against the non-meta baselines (DKF, VRNN, RGNRes) evaluated in Section 5.2, CoSFan with Task-Aware reservoir achieves DST of 1.67 (LP) / 1.72 (RP) on gravity-6, compared to RGNRes's 3.82 / 7.48 and DKF's 7.98 / 7.13, demonstrating that both the meta-learning and continual components are necessary. BGD-based weight regularization (He et al., 2019) performs worst among all continual strategies, with MSE ~2× higher than reservoir-based approaches on both datasets. Task-Agnostic reservoir sampling (Riemer et al., 2018; Joseph & Gu, 2021) shows better LP than Task-Aware for MAML but suffers severe forgetting (BTI = −0.007), whereas Task-Aware achieves near-zero interference. Notably, in the Task-Agnostic setting the feed-forward meta-model performs similarly or worse than MAML, confirming that the task-aware pairing is essential to unlock the full benefit of the hyper-network's non-linear task embedding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-learning framework for latent dynamics forecasting (Jiang et al., 2023) into a continual, non-stationary setting where task boundaries and identifiers are unknown. It departs from the dominant MAML-based meta-learners in prior CML work (Riemer et al., 2018; Joseph & Gu, 2021; He et al., 2019) by adopting a feed-forward hypernetwork as the algorithmic prior, and introduces a GMM-based task-relational experience replay mechanism that goes beyond standard reservoir sampling. The task boundary detection mechanism is borrowed from OSAKA (Caccia et al., 2020), while the hypernetwork conditioning follows Ha et al. (2016) and the multiplicative interaction rationale from Jayakumar et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a clear gap by demonstrating that CML techniques—previously limited to image classification and low-dimensional regression—can be effectively applied to high-dimensional latent dynamics forecasting, with the feed-forward meta-model and task-aware/relational replay providing a practical recipe for non-stationary forecasting. The task-relational GMM approach offers a principled solution to the rare-task problem in continual learning, maintaining balanced representation even under extreme imbalance. However, evaluation is confined to synthetic physics simulations (bouncing balls, Hamiltonian systems), the local stationarity assumption limits applicability, and the boundary detection mechanism degrades under gradual task shifts—leaving open questions about real-world deployment and extension to meta-RL or other sequential decision-making domains.
