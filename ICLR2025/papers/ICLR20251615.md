## Problem

Training LLMs requires compute proportional to the number of tokens D in the training data (C ≈ 6ND), and since each token carries minimal information, only a small fraction of model parameters are effectively updated per step. While reducing model size N via model growth is well-studied, reducing the number of text units D by increasing per-unit information density remains largely unexplored, despite being intuitively more promising.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text token sequences (e.g., from the Pile dataset) and a standard LLaMA-style Transformer architecture. During patch-level training, every K consecutive token embeddings are averaged into a single patch embedding, shortening the input sequence by a factor of K while maintaining the same effective context length (KT tokens compressed to T patches). The final output is a token-level language model whose parameters are initialized from the patch-level model and fine-tuned on remaining data, so inference proceeds entirely at the token level with no architectural changes.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-stage training procedure: (1) patch-level training, where K consecutive tokens are compressed into a single patch via embedding averaging (no extra parameters), and the model is trained with a next-patch prediction objective using a single output head that computes cross-entropy loss over all K tokens in the next patch simultaneously; (2) token-level training, where the patch-level parameters initialize a standard token-level model that continues training on the remaining (1−λ) fraction of data. The overall training cost is reduced to λ/K + 1−λ times the original (e.g., 0.5× with K=4, λ=2/3). A critical design constraint is that at least one end (input or output) of the patch-level model must remain architecturally identical to the token-level model to serve as a representation anchor for smooth knowledge transfer; adding linear projections at both ends degrades final performance despite lowering patch-level loss.

## Results

With patch size K=4 and λ=2/3, patch-level training reduces overall training costs to 0.5× while maintaining or slightly improving model performance across all four model sizes (370M–2.7B). On the 370M model, perplexity on WikiText-103 drops from 10.9 to 10.7 and average zero-shot accuracy rises from 42.2% to 42.5%. For the 2.7B model, PPL is essentially unchanged (7.1 vs. 7.2) and average zero-shot accuracy improves from 53.1% to 53.5%. The approach also generalizes to multi-epoch training (60B tokens, 6 epochs), where patch-level training at λ=1/2 achieves PPL 10.4 vs. 11.0 for the baseline at 0.625× cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard token-level (next-token) training at the same model size and total compute budget. Patch-level training at λ=2/3 (0.5× cost) matches or slightly exceeds the baseline on perplexity for the 370M (10.7 vs. 10.9), 780M (9.1 vs. 9.2), and 1.3B (8.2 vs. 8.2) models, with a negligible 0.1 PPL increase at 2.7B (7.2 vs. 7.1). Average zero-shot accuracy improves by 0.3–0.9 points across all sizes (e.g., 42.5 vs. 42.2 at 370M; 53.5 vs. 53.1 at 2.7B), with the largest gains on HellaSwag and ARC-C. At the more aggressive λ=4/5 (0.4× cost), performance degrades slightly (PPL 11.0, Avg 42.1 at 370M) but still remains close to the full-cost baseline. The paper does not directly compare against other efficiency methods (e.g., model growth, progressive training) in the main experiments, positioning patch-level training as a complementary, architecture-agnostic strategy rather than a direct competitor to those approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multi-token prediction mechanism from speculative decoding (Cai et al., 2024; Leviathan et al., 2023) for training efficiency rather than inference acceleration, and draws a conceptual analogy to Vision Transformers' patch-based processing (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021). It explicitly departs from model growth (Gong et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2022), which reduces training cost by shrinking parameters, by instead reducing the number of text units while preserving the full parameter set. The two-stage patch-to-token transfer mirrors the knowledge-transfer philosophy of model growth but applied to the data axis, and the single-head multi-token prediction design deliberately avoids the extra parameters that multi-head speculative decoders introduce, to keep the patch-level and token-level architectures aligned for smooth parameter transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 50% reduction in LLM pre-training compute at matched (or slightly better) performance is a practically significant efficiency gain that could lower the barrier to training competitive models. However, the evidence is confined to 370M–2.7B parameters and 360B tokens—well below frontier scale—and the performance advantage of patch-level training visibly shrinks as model size grows (Table 3), raising open questions about whether the benefit persists or reverses at 70B+ scale. The authors themselves note the absence of a general scaling law incorporating patch size K and data fraction λ, and the technique's applicability to other modalities (vision, speech, video) remains unexplored. Multi-epoch training shows a promising but underdeveloped direction where patch-level initialization may act as a regularizer.
