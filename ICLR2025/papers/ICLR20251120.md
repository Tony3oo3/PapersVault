## Problem

Diffusion-based inverse problem solvers rely on approximations for the intractable measurement likelihood (since the diffusion prior is trained unconditionally), which pushes the reverse diffusion process off the data manifold and introduces artifacts in reconstructions. These methods also exhibit poor robustness to the measurement gradient step size and measurement noise, and suffer from high worst-case failure rates, limiting their practical reliability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251120/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy measurements y = A(x*) + n, a known measurement operator A, and a pretrained unconditional diffusion model (pixel-based at 256×256 or latent at 64×64 for LDM-VQ-4 / Stable Diffusion v1.5), and produces a reconstructed image x_0. It operates as a drop-in projection module inserted into the gradient update step of existing solvers (PSLD, ReSample, DPS, DAPS), requiring no retraining of the diffusion model. The SVD is computed on the diffusion state reshaped into its 2D image matrix form at each guidance iteration.

## Method

DiffStateGrad projects the measurement gradient onto a low-rank subspace defined by the SVD of the current diffusion state z_t (or z_{0|t} for methods that apply guidance to the clean estimate), using an adaptive rank r determined by a variance retention threshold τ (default 0.99). The projection takes the form G_t → U_r U_r^T G_t V_r^T V_r, filtering out gradient components orthogonal to the local manifold structure. The key insight is that the subspace must be defined by the state being updated—not by a random matrix or the gradient's own low-rank structure—as confirmed by ablation experiments. A theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) shows that for sufficiently small step sizes, the projected update stays strictly closer to the data manifold than the unprojected update. The module is applied every iteration for PSLD/DPS/DAPS and every 5 gradient steps for ReSample, adding only a few seconds of runtime overhead.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256, DiffStateGrad consistently improves all four base methods across linear and nonlinear inverse problems. The most dramatic gains appear in nonlinear tasks: for phase retrieval, DiffStateGrad-ReSample improves PSNR from 27.61 (std 8.07) to 31.19 (std 4.33), and DiffStateGrad-DAPS improves PSNR from 30.52 to 32.25 while halving the standard deviation. On linear tasks, DiffStateGrad-PSLD reduces LPIPS on random inpainting from 0.246 to 0.165 and DiffStateGrad-DAPS from 0.130 to 0.099. Under a large MG step size, PSLD's LPIPS on random inpainting degrades to 0.463, while DiffStateGrad-PSLD maintains 0.165, demonstrating substantial robustness gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffStateGrad-DAPS outperforms DAPS on every linear task (e.g., random inpainting LPIPS 0.099 vs 0.130, PSNR 32.04 vs 30.79) and also surpasses MPGD-AE, which is the closest prior projection-based method (random inpainting LPIPS 0.099 vs 0.172). DiffStateGrad-DPS dominates MCG by a wide margin (random inpainting LPIPS 0.107 vs 0.286; Gaussian deblur PSNR 26.29 vs 6.72), confirming that projecting onto the diffusion-state subspace is far more effective than projecting onto the clean-data manifold. DiffStateGrad-PSLD achieves the best LPIPS among all latent methods on box inpainting (0.092) and random inpainting (0.165), while DiffStateGrad-ReSample improves ReSample on all five linear tasks. On nonlinear tasks, DiffStateGrad-ReSample nearly halves the PSNR standard deviation for phase retrieval (4.33 vs 8.07) and reduces the failure rate (PSNR < 20) from 26% to 4%, while DiffStateGrad-DAPS improves phase retrieval PSNR from 30.52 to 32.25 with lower variance. DDNM and DDRM, which use null-space or denoiser-based guidance without explicit projection, are consistently outperformed by all DiffStateGrad variants on LPIPS across tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based inverse problem solving framework (DPS, PSLD, ReSample, DAPS) by inserting an SVD-based subspace projection into the measurement gradient update. It departs conceptually from prior manifold-constraint methods (MCG, MPGD) that project onto the clean data manifold M_0, instead projecting onto the noisy intermediate state manifold M_t where the gradient is actually applied. The key positioning is that the subspace should be defined by the diffusion state being updated rather than by random matrices or the measurement gradient's own low-rank structure, a distinction validated by ablation (Table 1). As a modular plug-in, DiffStateGrad is applicable to both pixel-based and latent solvers regardless of whether guidance is applied to x_t or x_{0|t}.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical pain point in diffusion-based inverse solvers: sensitivity to measurement gradient step size and noise, which limits real-world deployment. The significant reduction in worst-case failure rates (e.g., 26%→4% on phase retrieval) and the robustness to large step sizes make diffusion solvers more reliable without requiring per-task hyperparameter tuning. A stated limitation is that DiffStateGrad reinforces the learned prior's structure, potentially introducing prior biases and making it unsuitable for tasks where the prior is poorly matched (e.g., black hole imaging). Open directions include combining DiffStateGrad with initialization strategies and extending the analysis beyond the low-rank SVD approximation to exact tangent-space projections.
