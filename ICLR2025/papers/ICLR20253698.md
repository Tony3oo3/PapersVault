## Problem

While 1-bit binarization of LLMs has been explored (e.g., BiLLM, PB-LLM), these models still require substantial memory for billion-parameter architectures, limiting deployment on resource-constrained devices. The paper identifies that even in fully binarized LLMs, a significant fraction of weights are redundant—randomly flipping non-salient binary weights causes minimal performance degradation—yet no prior method had exploited this redundancy to push compression below 1-bit precision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253698/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices (FP32/FP16) and a calibration dataset (C4, processed in blocks of size 128) to produce sub-1-bit structured binarized weights encoded in a 2-bit integer format (4 bits for N:M index + 2 bits for sign) per 4-element group. The output preserves the N:M sparsity pattern (e.g., 2:4, 4:8, 5:8, 6:8) compatible with NVIDIA Sparse Tensor Cores, with an additional 2-bit overhead per block for trisection region identification. The overall bit-width ranges from 0.55 to 0.85 bits per weight depending on the N:M ratio and salient weight proportion.

## Method

STBLLM applies a three-stage post-training pipeline: (1) an N:M structured pruning step using a novel Standardized Importance (SI) metric that multiplies the L1-normalized weight magnitude (across both input and output dimensions) by the input feature L2 norm, then standardizes to mitigate extreme-value distortion; (2) a Hessian-based partition of remaining weights into salient and non-salient sets, where salient weights receive 2-bit residual approximation (two binary tensors with separate scaling factors) and non-salient weights undergo a trisection search that finds optimal breakpoints p₁* and p₂* to split them into sparse, intermediate, and dense regions, each binarized with its own channel-wise scale; (3) block-wise output-based compensation (OBC) to propagate quantization errors forward. An adaptive layer-wise N:M assignment allocates less aggressive sparsity to high-importance layers (by L2 norm ratio) and more to low-importance ones, maintaining the target overall compression ratio. A specialized CUDA kernel encodes the 2:4 sparse 1-bit pattern into a 6-bit-per-group representation for direct Sparse Tensor Core execution, achieving 17.85× speedup over a 2-bit baseline.

## Results

On Wikitext2 perplexity, STBLLM achieves 11.07 at 0.55 bits per weight on LLaMA-1-65B, surpassing PB-LLM (12.53 at 1.7 bits) and BiLLM (11.57 at 0.7 bits). On LLaMA-1-7B under the most extreme 4:8 setting, STBLLM reduces perplexity from 688.73 (BiLLM) to 31.72, a >20× improvement. On zero-shot tasks, STBLLM(4:8) on LLaMA-1-30B achieves 51.78% average accuracy versus 43.72% for BiLLM(4:8). The specialized CUDA kernel delivers up to 17.85× speedup over ABQ-LLM's 2-bit implementation at sequence length 8192, reaching 263.45 TFLOPS (79.74% of RTX4090 sparse tensor core peak).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BiLLM (the primary 1-bit PTQ baseline), STBLLM wins decisively across all N:M settings and model sizes: at 4:8 on LLaMA-1-7B, perplexity drops from 688.73 to 31.72 (>20×), and at 6:8 on LLaMA-1-65B, from 9.09 to 6.43. STBLLM at 0.55 bits on LLaMA-1-65B (11.07) also beats PB-LLM at 1.7 bits (12.53), demonstrating that sub-1-bit structured binarization can outperform higher-bit partial binarization. GPTQ and RTN are non-competitive at 1-bit (perplexity in the thousands to millions), confirming that naive quantization fails at extreme bit-widths. In the pruning-metric ablation, the proposed SI metric outperforms Wanda (207.32 → 31.72 on LLaMA-1-7B) and is comparable to SparseGPT (32.82) while avoiding expensive second-order computation. On hardware, the STBLLM CUDA kernel achieves 17.85× speedup over ABQ-LLM's 2-bit kernel at sequence length 8192, reaching 263.45 TFLOPS. On zero-shot tasks (Table 4), STBLLM(6:8) on LLaMA-1-30B reaches 60.10% average accuracy vs. BiLLM(6:8) at 50.32%, a ~10-point gap that narrows but persists at 4:8 (51.78% vs. 43.72%).

## Relation to Prior Work

STBLLM builds directly on BiLLM's residual approximation framework for 1-bit PTQ of LLMs, but departs from it by introducing N:M structured sparsity (informed by DominoSearch, Wanda, and SparseGPT) to push compression below 1-bit. The Standardized Importance (SI) metric replaces the Hessian-based importance scoring used in GPTQ, SparseGPT, and BiLLM with a gradient-free combination of standardized weight magnitude and input feature norm, addressing the computational cost and sensitivity to extreme values of second-order methods. The Non-Salient Aware Quantization with trisection search generalizes BiLLM's bell-shaped distribution splitting into a three-region (sparse/intermediate/dense) partitioning with per-region binarization, and the specialized CUDA kernel leverages NVIDIA Ampere Sparse Tensor Cores for 2:4 sparsity acceleration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STBLLM is the first method to achieve sub-1-bit LLM compression (0.55 bits/weight) with usable perplexity, opening a path toward deploying large models on extremely memory-constrained edge devices. The 17.85× speedup over 2-bit ABQ-LLM via Sparse Tensor Cores makes the approach practically deployable rather than purely academic. Key limitations include the lack of support for MoE and Mamba-based architectures, and the notably degraded performance on LLaMA-3-8B at 4:8 (253.76 ppl), suggesting the method's effectiveness may depend on model architecture and training recipe. Open questions include whether the sub-1-bit regime can be pushed further, how the approach interacts with emerging model families, and whether the layer-wise N:M assignment strategy generalizes beyond the L2-norm heuristic used here.
