## Problem

State-space models offer linear-time decoding as an alternative to Transformers' quadratic attention, but their recurrent update rules (the choice of A_t, B_t, and the multiplication operator) remain ad hoc and artisanal, lacking a principled design framework. This paper asks whether SSM design can be grounded in a specific online learning objective, so that the state transition dynamics are derived rather than hand-engineered.

## Input / Output

Longhorn consumes a sequence of input tokens x_t ∈ R^d (preprocessed via a linear projection followed by a Conv1d, as in Mamba) and produces next-token predictions through a state-dependent linear readout o_t = S_t q_t fed into a gated linear unit. The internal memory state S_t is a d×m matrix (m is the key dimension, typically 16 in Mamba-style configs) that compresses all prior context. For the vision variant (ViL), the same recurrence is applied bidirectionally over image patch tokens to produce a class label on ImageNet.

## Method

Longhorn frames the SSM recurrence as solving an online associative recall objective: at each step, find S_t that minimizes ‖S − S_{t−1}‖_F² + ‖S k_t − x_t‖²_{diag(β_t)}, i.e., update the state matrix to best predict x_t from key k_t while staying close to the previous state. The closed-form solution (via the Sherman-Morrison formula) yields S_t = (1_{d×m} − ε_t ⊗ k_t^⊙2) ⊙ S_{t−1} + (ε_t ⊙ x_t) ⊗ k_t with ε_t = β_t / (1 + β_t k_t^T k_t), where β_t = Sigmoid(W_β x_t) is input-dependent. Crucially, the forgetting factor (1 − ε_t ⊗ k_t^⊙2) is derived from the key vector itself, eliminating the need for a separately parameterized forget gate and saving O(d×m) parameters per layer. The architecture otherwise follows Mamba's block structure exactly, making Longhorn a drop-in replacement with an efficient parallel-scan CUDA kernel for training and a recurrent form for linear-time inference.

## Results

On the 1.3B-parameter SlimPajama language modeling benchmark, Longhorn achieves an average accuracy of 52.35 across eight downstream tasks, outperforming Mamba (52.25) and GLA (51.07) while using fewer parameters (64K state size vs. Mamba's 64K and GLA's 512K). Longhorn achieves a 1.8× improvement in sample efficiency over Mamba, reaching the same average perplexity with roughly half the training tokens. On the MQAR synthetic benchmark, Longhorn is the only model to achieve near-perfect recall at sequence length 512 with a hidden dimension of just 64. Additionally, Longhorn trained on 2048 context length successfully extrapolates to 32K (16×) at inference with minimal perplexity degradation, whereas DeltaNet and LLaMA fail to extrapolate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba (the primary SSM baseline), Longhorn wins on sample efficiency (1.8× fewer tokens to reach the same perplexity), on the MQAR benchmark (near-perfect recall at length 512 vs. Mamba's degraded recall), and on real-world recall tasks (36.0/45.0 avg vs. 34.8/41.8 for vanilla/JRT-Prompt). On the 1.3B downstream average, Longhorn edges out Mamba (52.35 vs. 52.25) despite having fewer parameters since it eliminates the separate forget gate. Against GLA, Longhorn achieves a 1.28-point average improvement (52.35 vs. 51.07) while using 8× less state memory (64K vs. 512K). Against the LLaMA Transformer baseline, Longhorn trails slightly on the 1.3B average (52.35 vs. 53.03) but matches or beats it on OpenWebText validation loss at both 120M (3.225 vs. 3.247) and 350M (2.888 vs. 2.891) scales, and crucially extrapolates to 16× training context length where LLaMA fails. On vision (ImageNet), Vision Longhorn slightly outperforms Vision Mamba (76.4 vs. 76.1 Tiny; 80.7 vs. 80.5 Small). Against DeltaNet, Longhorn's key advantage is stable length extrapolation to 32K context, whereas DeltaNet's gradient-step-based update with eigenvalues >1 causes instability beyond training length.

## Relation to Prior Work

Longhorn is architecturally a drop-in replacement for Mamba's SSM block, inheriting its overall transformer-like stack and training infrastructure. Conceptually, it departs from the ad hoc design of (A_t, B_t, *) operators in prior SSMs by deriving the recurrence from the closed-form solution of an online associative recall objective, connecting to the implicit online learning framework of Kulis & Bartlett (2010) and the fast-weight programmer lineage of Schlag et al. (2021). It distinguishes itself from the concurrent DeltaNet work (Yang et al., 2024), which uses an unstable gradient-step update requiring key normalization, by providing a provably stable closed-form update with a diagonal approximation that preserves parallel scan efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled design principle for SSMs—choosing the online learning objective determines the recurrence—which could guide future architecture development beyond the single associative recall formulation explored here. The 16× length extrapolation (2048→32K) without perplexity degradation is practically significant for long-context applications where Transformers fail and other SSMs like DeltaNet also struggle. Open questions include whether other online learning objectives (e.g., online classification, online bandits) yield complementary SSM variants, whether the approach scales beyond 1.3B parameters, and whether combining Longhorn with sliding-window attention (as shown beneficial for Mamba) further closes the gap with Transformers.
