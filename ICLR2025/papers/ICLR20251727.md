## Problem

Existing LLM-based content moderators classify queries and responses in binary (safe/unsafe) terms, which is inadequate for platforms with varying safety thresholds and cannot distinguish between a mildly concerning response and a highly dangerous one. This binary framing also limits the utility of moderation datasets, since annotation guidelines are not standardized and a response labeled safe in one dataset may be unsafe in another. The paper addresses this gap by introducing per-topic severity rubrics and training a moderator that predicts both binary safety labels and fine-grained severity levels.

## Input / Output

The moderator accepts a user query and/or an LLM response as text input, formatted within a LlamaGuard3-style prompt template. For binary tasks, the model outputs 'safe' or 'unsafe' as the first generated token followed by the harm category (one of 11 topics) if unsafe. For severity classification, the prompt includes the full 7-dimension rubric and topic-specific level definitions, and the model outputs a level from 0 (safe) to 4 (extreme). The severity prompt is notably longer than the binary prompt, embedding the complete rubric text inline before the conversation.

## Method

The core contribution is a controlled data generation framework that produces responses spanning a severity spectrum. The authors first curate small seed sets (~300 examples per level) through human-in-the-loop rewriting and expert auditing, then fine-tune specialized LLM generators (Llama3-8B, Llama3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) on each seed set to generate responses at scale with the correct severity characteristics. An iterative model committee refinement step then replaces easier training responses with 'hard' ones identified by a weak moderator in consultation with WildGuard and LlamaGuard3, increasing dataset difficulty without changing example count. The final BingoGuard-8B is trained via supervised fine-tuning on Llama3.1-8B-Base with a multi-task objective combining query classification, response classification, and severity level classification, where the authors show that multi-task training significantly improves severity prediction over a severity-only model.

## Results

BingoGuard-llama3.1-8B achieves state-of-the-art performance across 9 public moderation benchmarks, outperforming the second-best model by 2.1% on query classification (F-1) and 1.9% on response classification (F-1). On its own BingoGuardTest, it reaches 79.4% detection accuracy for binary response classification (vs. 76.5% for GPT-4o) and 78.4 macro-F1 for severity level classification (vs. 54.3 for 5-shot GPT-4o). The model also improves over the best public baseline WildGuard by 4.3% on query classification and 2.6% on response classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BingoGuard-llama3.1-8B outperforms all five baselines on the majority of benchmarks. Against WildGuard-7B (the strongest public open-source moderator), BingoGuard gains 4.3 F-1 on query classification (e.g., 75.7 vs. 70.8 on ToxicChat) and 2.6 F-1 on response classification (e.g., 80.1 vs. 75.4 on WildGuardTestResponse). Against GPT-4o, BingoGuard wins on 7 of 9 benchmarks, with the largest margin on ToxicChat (+7.6) and WildGuardTestResponse (+7.0), though GPT-4o edges ahead on OpenAI Moderation (70.4 vs. 77.9 is actually BingoGuard winning; GPT-4o leads only on Aegis at 83.2 vs. 90.4 — correction: BingoGuard wins there too). Against LlamaGuard3-8B (same base model, different training data), BingoGuard improves by 24.8 F-1 on ToxicChat and 15.9 on WildGuardTestResponse, demonstrating the impact of the severity-aware training data. On BingoGuardTest severity classification, BingoGuard-llama3.1-8B (78.4 macro-F1) surpasses 5-shot GPT-4o (54.3) by 24.1 points, and multi-task training with binary classification boosts severity F-1 by 9.1 points over the severity-only variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based content moderation line (LlamaGuard, WildGuard, ShieldGemma, MD-Judge) from binary safe/unsafe classification to multi-level severity assessment, introducing per-topic rubrics across 11 harmful categories. For data generation, it departs from prior approaches that rely on jailbreaking or GPT-4-as-judge filtering, instead proposing fine-tuned specialized generators per severity level (motivated by Qi et al.'s finding that few examples compromise safety alignment) combined with an iterative model-committee refinement loop inspired by alignment work. The severity rubrics draw on industrial guidelines (Microsoft Azure, Salesforce Trust Layer) and are structured around seven expert-defined dimensions, positioning the work as a principled generalization of the binary framing that prior moderators adopt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that binary classifiers' predictive probability for 'unsafe' is only weakly correlated with actual severity levels challenges the implicit assumption that confidence scores can serve as risk proxies, suggesting platforms need explicit severity outputs for calibrated content filtering. The public BingoGuardTrain (54.9K examples) and BingoGuardTest (988 expert-labeled examples) datasets fill a gap in severity-annotated moderation data, enabling future work to refine rubrics and train more nuanced moderators. Limitations include single-language scope, policy-specific rubrics that may not transfer across jurisdictions, and moderate inter-annotator agreement (Fleiss Kappa 0.53), leaving open whether the 5-level granularity is the right resolution and whether the framework generalizes to multilingual or domain-specific settings.
