## Problem

Offline multi-agent reinforcement learning suffers from data scarcity, and while diffusion-based data synthesis has shown promise in single-agent settings, extending it to multi-agent systems is non-trivial. Existing synthesis methods treat each agent independently, ignoring inter-agent interactions that produce infeasible or distorted transitions, and are restricted to continuous action spaces, excluding discrete environments like SMAC. Additionally, synthetic data quality is bottlenecked by the original dataset's lack of high-value transitions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline multi-agent dataset of joint transitions x = (o, a, o', r) shaped as (|D_ori|, n, d_x), where n is the number of agents and d_x is the per-agent transition dimension. It outputs a synthetic dataset of 5M joint transitions (configurable) that is directly compatible with any offline MARL algorithm (MA-ICQ, MA-CQL, OMAR, MA-BCQ) without modifying the policy learning procedure. For discrete action spaces, a bit action module maps M discrete actions to a binary vector of length ⌈log₂(M)⌉, which is concatenated with continuous variables during diffusion and thresholded back to discrete actions after sampling.

## Method

INS employs an Elucidated Diffusion Model (EDM) with a Transformer encoder whose attention mechanism replaces standard Softmax with Sparsemax along the agent dimension, producing zero-weight attention for irrelevant agents and focusing denoising on critical inter-agent interactions. Training jointly optimizes the denoiser (L₂ score-matching loss) and a state value estimator (TD loss) on the original dataset. During synthesis, B candidate transitions are generated via K-step diffusion sampling, then a value-based select mechanism (Softmax or Top-K) retains the top-η proportion by estimated value, balancing dataset quality against diversity. The bit action module enables seamless handling of both discrete and continuous action spaces by encoding discrete actions as binary bit vectors within the continuous diffusion space, reducing dimensionality from M to ⌈log₂(M)⌉.

## Results

On MPE (continuous action space), INS achieves total normalized scores of 573.0 (MA-ICQ), 529.1 (MA-CQL), and 725.9 (OMAR), outperforming the original datasets (550.0, 520.6, 708.0) and MA-SynthER (561.9, 510.3, 689.3). On SMAC (discrete action space), INS reaches total scores of 143.3 (MA-ICQ), 120.0 (MA-CQL), and 35.8 (MA-BCQ), again surpassing both original (133.6, 109.4, 33.5) and MA-SynthER (137.8, 105.9, 34.3). Notably, INS can synthesize high-quality data using only 10% of the original dataset while maintaining performance comparable to the full dataset (e.g., Spread: 102.3 vs. 102.8 original).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is MA-SynthER, a multi-agent adaptation of SynthER (Lu et al., 2024) that synthesizes transitions per-agent independently using an MLP-Mixer diffusion model without modeling inter-agent interactions. INS consistently outperforms MA-SynthER across all 21 dataset-algorithm combinations in Table 1, with the largest gap on MA-CQL in SMAC (120.0 vs. 105.9 total score, a 13.3% improvement). Critically, MA-SynthER actually degrades performance below the original dataset in several settings (e.g., MA-CQL on MPE: 510.3 vs. 520.6 original; OMAR on MPE: 689.3 vs. 708.0 original), confirming that ignoring inter-agent interactions distorts synthetic data. Two additional baselines—Additive Noise (Laskin et al., 2020) and VAE Augmented—were evaluated only in the preliminary study (Figure 1) and excluded from main experiments due to consistently poor performance, yielding results comparable to or worse than the original dataset. INS also outperforms the original dataset in most settings, with the most substantial gains on medium-quality and random datasets where the diffusion model can fill coverage gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent diffusion-based data synthesis paradigm of SynthER (Lu et al., 2024) to the multi-agent setting, addressing the critical gap that inter-agent interactions were previously ignored during synthesis. It adopts the EDM architecture (Karras et al., 2022) for the diffusion backbone and repurposes Sparsemax (Martins & Astudillo, 2016) from its original role in attention-based NLP/decision-making into a generative denoising context, where it learns interaction patterns from noise rather than extracting them from observed data. The bit action module, inspired by Bit Diffusion (Chen et al., 2023), bridges the continuous-discrete gap that limited prior synthesis methods to continuous action spaces. Conceptually, INS positions itself distinctly from diffusion-based MARL planners like MADiff and DOM2, which use diffusion for action selection or trajectory planning, by treating diffusion purely as a data generator orthogonal to policy learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the 10%-data result: if real-world multi-agent data collection is expensive or dangerous (autonomous driving, robotics), being able to synthesize a full training set from a small seed dataset is a meaningful step toward scalable offline MARL. The finding that ignoring inter-agent interactions (MA-SynthER) can degrade performance below the original dataset provides a clear warning against naively applying single-agent synthesis to multi-agent problems. Open questions remain around variable agent counts, computational scaling to large agent populations, and whether guided diffusion could inject domain-specific constraints (e.g., safety) into the synthetic data. The method's orthogonality to downstream algorithms is a strength, but the evaluation is confined to MPE and SMAC, leaving open whether the approach transfers to more complex or partially observable real-world multi-agent domains.
