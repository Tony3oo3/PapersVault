## Problem

Existing weather models are task-specific, requiring separate architectures for forecasting, super-resolution, image translation, and post-processing, and they cannot generalize across the diverse data modalities (satellite, radar, time-series) produced by Earth observation systems. Prior weather foundation models like ClimaX and Aurora focus narrowly on forecasting or downscaling and do not unify multi-modal, multi-task weather understanding within a single model. The paper asks whether a universal foundation model can handle a wide range of weather understanding tasks and data modalities through in-context learning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a visual prompting QA tuple (P_in, P_target, X_in) where the prompt pair encodes the task identity and X_in is the query; all images are resized to 256×256 and concatenated into a single N×256×256 tensor. Three prompt formats handle different modalities: single-modal (one variable, e.g., LR→HR), cross-modal (multiple channels, e.g., IR-069+IR-107→VIL), and time-series (sequence of t frames→future frames). For ERA5 forecasting, 48 atmospheric variables are aggregated via a cross-attention module into a single vector before entering the ViT, and the model predicts all target variables across all lead times (6h–168h) with one unified model rather than per-lead-time fine-tuned models.

## Method

WeatherGFM unifies all weather tasks as a projection τ: X_S → X_T and frames inference as a visual prompting question-answering problem: X_target = F_τ(P_in, P_target, X_in; θ). Training uses a mixed-modal mask modeling (MMIM) objective where random block-wise masks (75% ratio) are applied to both the prompt target P_target and the ground-truth X_target, while P_in and X_in remain fully visible; the model is trained with MSE loss to reconstruct the masked regions. The backbone is a standard ViT with task-specific patch embedding layers (handling variable channel counts C) followed by an MLP alignment layer that projects all task embeddings into a shared D-dimensional space. At inference, the target is fully masked and the model generates the complete output in a single forward pass, enabling zero-shot task switching by simply changing the prompt pair without any parameter updates.

## Results

WeatherGFM achieves competitive or superior RMSE across 12 weather understanding tasks using a single unified model. On radar extrapolation, it reaches RMSE 0.467 (vs. 0.490 for single-task ViT and 0.815 for UNet); on satellite spatial SR, RMSE 0.042 (vs. 0.047 ViT, 0.932 UNet). On ERA5 medium-range forecasting (1.4°), WeatherGFM outperforms ClimaX at 120h (T2M RMSE 1.68 vs. 1.83; U10 RMSE 2.61 vs. 2.79) and 168h (T2M RMSE 1.76 vs. 2.17; U10 RMSE 3.11 vs. 3.35), and achieves the highest average ACC of 0.848 (vs. 0.836 IFS, 0.824 ClimaX). The model also generalizes to unseen OOD tasks without fine-tuning, e.g., IR107 satellite extrapolation (RMSE 0.389 vs. 0.413 for trained ViT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the UNet single-task baseline (Trebing et al., 2021), WeatherGFM wins decisively on every task, with RMSE reductions of 50–95% (e.g., 0.042 vs. 0.932 on satellite spatial SR; 0.347 vs. 1.033 on satellite extrapolation). Against the single-task ViT baseline (Nguyen et al., 2023), WeatherGFM outperforms on most tasks—radar extrapolation (0.467 vs. 0.490), satellite extrapolation (0.347 vs. 0.408), GOES2Radar (0.436 vs. 0.445), and GOES2POES-NO₂ (0.302 vs. 0.549)—but trails on Deblur (0.264 vs. 0.163) and GOES-IR2GOES-IR (0.310 vs. 0.257), suggesting that the unified multi-task training introduces some interference for certain satellite-to-satellite translation tasks. On ERA5 medium-range forecasting, WeatherGFM surpasses ClimaX (Nguyen et al., 2023) at longer lead times (120h and 168h for both T2M and U10) and achieves a higher average ACC (0.848 vs. 0.824), while also exceeding the operational IFS model at 168h (T2M RMSE 1.76 vs. 2.23; U10 RMSE 3.11 vs. 3.81). Notably, ClimaX requires a separate fine-tuned model per lead time, whereas WeatherGFM handles all lead times with a single model. On OOD tasks (Table 13), WeatherGFM without any fine-tuning matches or beats the trained ViT on IR107 satellite extrapolation (RMSE 0.389 vs. 0.413) but underperforms on 15-min temporal SR (0.272 vs. 0.218), indicating that generalization is strongest for tasks close to the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeatherGFM directly extends the visual prompting question-answering paradigm from PromptGIP (Liu et al., 2023) and Painter (Wang et al., 2023a) into the weather domain, adapting the in-context learning framework that unifies low-level vision tasks to weather understanding. For multi-variable atmospheric inputs (ERA5), it borrows the cross-attention aggregation strategy from ClimaX (Nguyen et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure from prior weather foundation models (ClimaX, Aurora, Pangu-Weather, FourCastNet) is that those systems are task-specific (primarily forecasting or downscaling) and require fine-tuning per task, whereas WeatherGFM treats all 12 weather tasks as instances of a single visual QA problem solvable via prompt selection alone, without parameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that in-context learning can unify diverse weather understanding tasks (forecasting, super-resolution, image translation, post-processing) across multiple data modalities within a single model, suggesting a path toward generalist Earth-system models. The finding that the unified model outperforms single-task baselines challenges the assumption that task specialization is necessary for peak weather model performance. However, significant open questions remain: the data scale is still limited by the high cost of weather observations (unlike low-cost internet images), OOD generalization is partial (failing on multi-modal satellite spatial SR), and the scaling law exploration is preliminary with only 110M–330M parameters. Whether this paradigm scales to truly large-scale, high-resolution global weather modeling remains to be shown.
