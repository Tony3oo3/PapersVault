## Problem

Existing neural combinatorial optimization solvers are tailored to specific problem types (TSP, CVRP, SAT), and no unified framework exists for learning across diverse CO problems in a single model. While polynomial-time reductions show that many CO problems map to a general TSP with arbitrary distance matrices, this matrix-encoded general TSP — with non-metric, asymmetric, or discrete distances and no explicit node coordinates — remains largely unexplored in the neural solver literature, posing new challenges for effective instance encoding at variable scales.

## Input / Output

The method takes an N×N cost/distance matrix as the sole input, where entries can be arbitrary positive reals (non-metric, asymmetric) or binary (discrete), with no 2D coordinates available. The output is a TSP tour (a node permutation), which is then transformed back into the solution of the original CO problem (e.g., a Hamiltonian cycle or a satisfying assignment) via problem-specific inverse mappings. The framework handles variable node counts N without pre-setting a maximum size, and the same trained solver processes instances from multiple problem distributions (ATSP, 2DTSP, DHCP, 3SAT) and scales (N≈20, 50, 100) interchangeably.

## Method

UniCO is a three-step pipeline: reduce a CO instance to a general TSP cost matrix, solve it with a trained neural TSP solver, and transform the resulting tour back to the original problem's solution. Two novel solvers are proposed as pluggable backbones. MatPOENet is a Transformer-based autoregressive model trained via POMO reinforcement learning, whose key innovation is the Pseudo One-hot Embedding (POE): a size-agnostic node embedding generated by running a nearest-neighbor heuristic to obtain a rough tour, then constructing position-based vectors via a rotationally shifted impulse function f(x)=1/cosh(100x), replacing MatNet's fixed one-hot embedding and enabling arbitrary N. MatDIFFNet is a supervised diffusion-based generative model that introduces a mix-noised reference map (fusing the distance matrix with the noised label matrix through an MLP) to replace coordinate-based node features, and a dual-feature convolution that maintains separate 'from' and 'to' node representations with distinct learnable parameters to handle matrix asymmetry. Both solvers are trained in a multi-task, multi-scale regime where instances from all four problem distributions are mixed in each batch, and the framework is also compatible with existing backbones like DIMES.

## Results

On the unified general TSP benchmark (4 problem types × 3 scales), MatPOENet*-8x (multi-scale trained) achieves an average tour length (Avg.L) of 1.8828 at N≈50 and 2.5420 at N≈100, beating LKH-500 (1.9329 / 2.7594) and even LKH-10000 (1.8821 / 2.5848) on the overall Avg.L metric. On the decisive HCP and 3SAT tasks, MatPOENet*-8x reaches 97.20% and 88.44% found rate at N≈50, and 99.88% and 70.92% at N≈100, substantially outperforming vanilla MatNet (30.84% / 30.52% at N≈50). MatDIFFNet-2OPT is competitive on 3SAT (98.64% FR at N≈50) but lags on ATSP and 2DTSP tour length. The average optimality gap to Gurobi is 3.88% (N≈50) and 9.01% (N≈100) for MatPOENet*-8x.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strong LKH heuristic, MatPOENet*-8x achieves a lower average tour length than LKH-500 at both N≈50 (1.8828 vs. 1.9329) and N≈100 (2.5420 vs. 2.7594), and even beats LKH-10000 at N≈100 (2.5420 vs. 2.5848) while being ~8.7× faster (15m35s vs. 2h15m per Table 3). Compared to vanilla MatNet (Kwon et al., 2021), MatPOENet-8x reduces Avg.L from 2.0513 to 1.9082 at N≈50 and from 2.7904 to 2.5717 at N≈100, with dramatically higher found rates on DHCP (98.28% vs. 71.52%) and 3SAT (77.60% vs. 73.08%). Against DIMES (Qiu et al., 2022) trained under the same UniCO framework, MatPOENet-8x substantially outperforms on all four problem types (e.g., ATSP 1.6417 vs. 1.6920, 3SAT FR 77.60% vs. 20.44% at N≈50). GLOP (Ye et al., 2024b), which uses vanilla MatNet as a local reviser, performs poorly on the discrete-space problems (DHCP FR 0.84%, 3SAT FR 0.76% at N≈50), whereas MatPOENet handles both continuous and discrete distance matrices uniformly. MatDIFFNet-2OPT (the paper's diffusion-based solver) is competitive on 3SAT (95.64% FR at N≈50, close to Gurobi's 100%) but trails MatPOENet on ATSP and 2DTSP tour length, reflecting the inherent trade-off between generative diversity and sequential precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MatNet (Kwon et al., 2021), the first neural solver for matrix-encoded asymmetric TSP, by replacing its fixed-size one-hot node embeddings with a size-agnostic Pseudo One-hot Embedding (POE), and by extending diffusion-based TSP solvers (DIFUSCO, T2T) to handle arbitrary distance matrices via a mix-noised reference map. Conceptually, it departs from prior 'multi-task' CO solvers (MTCO, MAB-MTL, MVMoE) that either require problem-specific headers/decoders or are confined to a single problem family (e.g., VRP variants), instead unifying diverse problems through polynomial-time reduction to a single general TSP matrix formulation. The DIMES meta-RL solver and GLOP divide-and-conquer framework are incorporated as compatible backbones, positioning UniCO as an orthogonal layer that can wrap existing solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: neural CO solvers have been overwhelmingly tailored to 2D Euclidean TSP with explicit coordinates, while the broader domain of non-metric, asymmetric, or discrete distance matrices without coordinates remains largely unexplored. The observation that LKH's alpha-measure (relying on minimum 1-tree computation) degrades on discrete instances like HCP and SAT provides a concrete motivation for neural approaches in these regimes. However, significant gaps remain versus exact solvers (Gurobi) on decisive tasks, MatDIFFNet's scalability is limited, and the set of reducible problems is still narrow. Open questions include whether the reduction-to-general-TSP paradigm can absorb MILP or other non-graph CO problems, and whether the POE mechanism generalizes to other sequential decision architectures beyond Transformer.
