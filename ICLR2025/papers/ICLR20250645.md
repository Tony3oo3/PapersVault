## Problem

Current brain decoding frameworks are subject-specific, requiring tens of hours of fMRI per individual and failing to generalise to unseen participants. A key obstacle is that most models treat adjacent cortical locations as spatially independent, ignoring the well-documented spatial autocorrelation of cortical signals that is behaviourally meaningful. This limits the utility of decoding models for neurofeedback and precision cognitive simulation, where pooling experiences across individuals is essential.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 7T cortical surface fMRI (resampled from 59,292 native vertices to a 40,962-vertex I6 icosphere, then patched into 1,280 triangular patches of 45 vertices), paired with 3-second movie clips (16 video frames and 3 fMRI frames sampled with a 6-second temporal lag to account for the haemodynamic response). At inference, the model produces CLIP-space embeddings enabling top-K retrieval of video or audio clips from fMRI (or vice versa), and a two-stage diffusion pipeline (VDVAE + Versatile Diffusion) generates high-resolution video frame reconstructions from fMRI embeddings of unseen subjects.

## Method

SIM combines a Surface Vision Transformer (SiT-small, 12 layers, 6 heads, D=384) with tri-modal CLIP contrastive alignment. The SiT encoder is first pre-trained via a video surface masked autoencoder (vsMAE) that reconstructs masked fMRI frames using 50% tube masking, learning spatio-temporal cortical dynamics. The pre-trained encoder is then fine-tuned alongside frozen videoMAE and wav2vec2.0 encoders through a symmetric six-way CLIP loss that aligns fMRI, video, and audio embeddings into a shared space via trainable multimodal mappers. The key architectural insight is that patching the cortical surface with a regular icosahedral grid and modelling long-range spatial interactions through self-attention captures generalisable patterns of cortical functional topography, enabling the model to decode stimuli from subjects never seen during training. Tri-modal alignment provides complementary information such that decoding between any pair of modalities (e.g., fMRI→video, fMRI→audio, video→fMRI) outperforms bimodal baselines.

## Results

On the HCP 7T movie-watching dataset (174 subjects), SIM achieves 76.8±2.6% top-1 and 94.2±1.5% top-10 accuracy for fMRI→video retrieval, and 56.6±2.5% top-1 and 86.9±2.0% top-10 for fMRI→audio retrieval, using hard-negative sampling and generalising to unseen subjects. Tri-modal CLIP alignment (fMRI + video + audio) consistently outperforms bi-modal alignment, with the largest gain in audio retrieval (56.6% vs. 19.9% top-1). The model also generalises to new movie scenes (Experiment 2: 19.6% top-1 soft-negative) and to both new scenes and new subjects simultaneously (Experiment 3: 19.2% top-1 soft-negative), far exceeding the Ridge regression baseline (~5% top-1 in both cases).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SIM against two baselines: a random-guessing baseline and a Ridge regression model (inspired by Ozcelik & VanRullen, 2023), which benefits from the MSMAll functional alignment already applied to all subjects. For fMRI→video retrieval, SIM with tri-modal CLIP achieves 76.8% top-1 accuracy versus 15.6% for Ridge (a 61.2 percentage-point improvement) and 3.7% for random. For fMRI→audio retrieval, the gap is even more dramatic: SIM reaches 56.6% top-1 versus only 3.2% for Ridge, a 53.4-point margin. The Ridge baseline is particularly weak for audio (3.2% top-1, nearly at chance level of 3.1%), suggesting that linear regression cannot capture the non-linear spatial patterns needed for cross-subject audio decoding. Within the SiT framework, adding the audio modality to the CLIP training (fMRI+V+A vs. fMRI+V) improves video top-1 by 12.1 points (76.8 vs. 64.7) and audio top-1 by 36.7 points (56.6 vs. 19.9), demonstrating that joint tri-modal alignment provides complementary information that benefits all retrieval directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Surface Vision Transformer (SiT) architecture (Dahan et al., 2022) and its video-surface masked autoencoder pre-training (Dahan et al., 2024) into a tri-modal CLIP alignment framework for cross-subject fMRI decoding. It departs from prior brain-decoding work (Benchetrit et al., 2023; Scotti et al., 2024; Ozcelik & VanRullen, 2023) that is typically subject-specific or requires costly per-subject alignment steps (hyperalignment, ≥1 hour of test data), by instead encoding cortical spatial autocorrelation through surface self-attention to achieve generalisation without any per-subject calibration. The reconstruction pipeline adapts the two-stage VDVAE + Versatile Diffusion approach of Ozcelik & VanRullen (2023), while the audio and video encoders (wav2vec2.0, videoMAE) are used as frozen feature extractors in a novel three-way contrastive alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to decode movie stimuli from the brain activity of entirely unseen subjects using only ~15 minutes of fMRI per participant (versus tens of hours in competing frameworks) is a meaningful step toward personalised brain simulation and neurofeedback applications. However, several limitations temper the impact: the evaluation is confined to a single dataset (HCP 7T movie-watching) with a restricted stimulus set, the 3-second clip length limits modelling of complex temporal processes like memory, subcortical structures are ignored, and the SiT architecture's quadratic attention scaling constrains resolution increases. Open questions include whether self-attention patterns vary meaningfully across individuals to predict cognitive traits, whether the approach generalises to entirely different stimulus domains, and how to incorporate subcortical and longer-timescale dynamics.
