## Problem

Video-LLMs are constrained by token length limits, forcing them to process only a subset of frames from a video. Existing frame selection strategies—uniform sampling and individual text-frame retrieval—fail to capture information density variations and the multi-frame temporal reasoning required by complex video understanding tasks, because they treat frames independently rather than as an informative combination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253033/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes M uniformly sampled candidate frames (e.g., 128 at inference) and a text query as input, and outputs a selected combination of T frames (e.g., 8) that are fed into a frozen Video-LLM backbone. The module is plug-and-play: it reuses the visual encoder, projector, and bottom transformer layers of the host Video-LLM (VILA-8B/40B or LLaVA-OneVision-7B), adding only two lightweight FFN heads (~0.2% extra parameters). During training, smaller combinations are used (M=16, T=2 or M=32, T=4), while inference generalizes to larger M and T values.

## Method

FRAME-VOYAGER formulates frame selection as a ranking task over frame combinations rather than a combinatorial search. A human-free data pipeline uses a pre-trained Video-LLM (VILA-8B) to compute language modeling losses for all C(M,T) frame combinations given a query, then ranks combinations by ascending loss—lower loss indicates a more informative combination. Training uses a Bradley-Terry-style reward ranking loss: K ranked combinations are sampled, and all C(K,2) pairs are used to push the model's predicted reward for the lower-loss combination above that of the higher-loss one. The architecture concatenates frame features (from the visual encoder + projector, token-averaged) with query embeddings, processes them through the LLM's bottom 1–2 transformer layers to capture query-frame and frame-to-frame interactions, then computes each frame's reward as the cosine similarity between a pooled query feature and the frame feature via two FFN heads; the combination reward is the mean of its constituent frame rewards. At inference, the top-T frames by individual reward are selected while preserving temporal order, with no parameter updates required.

## Results

On Video-MME (without subtitles), VILA-8B + FRAME-VOYAGER achieves 50.5% overall accuracy, a +3.0% gain over uniform sampling (47.5%), with a notable +3.6% improvement on long videos (43.9% vs 40.3%). LLaVA-OneVision-7B + FRAME-VOYAGER reaches 57.5% on Video-MME, surpassing the 34B LLaVA-NeXT-Video (52.0%) by 5.5 points while using only 8 frames. On MLVU, ANQA, and NextQA, FRAME-VOYAGER consistently improves over its respective backbone: VILA-8B gains +3.5% (MLVU), +2.0% (ANQA), and +5.2% (NextQA); VILA-34B gains +3.3%, +1.1%, and +4.4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRAME-VOYAGER consistently outperforms all compared baselines across the four main benchmarks. On Video-MME, VILA-8B + FRAME-VOYAGER (50.5%) matches LongVILA-256 (50.5%) while using only 8 frames versus 256, and surpasses LLaVA-NeXT-QW2 (49.5%) and VideoLLaMA2 (47.9%). LLaVA-OneVision-7B + FRAME-VOYAGER (57.5%) exceeds the much larger 34B LLaVA-NeXT-Video (52.0%) by 5.5 points and VITA 8×7B (55.8%) by 1.7 points. VILA-34B + FRAME-VOYAGER (60.0%) leads all 34B+ models including LLaVA-NeXT-Video (52.0%) and VITA (55.8%). In the frame-selection ablation (Table 2), FRAME-VOYAGER (50.5%) outperforms the best individual frame-text matching method SeViLA (49.3%) and all rule-based SBD methods (45.7–47.8%), confirming that combinational selection with query awareness is superior to per-frame retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frame selection problem for Video-LLMs from individual frame-query matching (CLIP, SigLIP, VILA-Embedding) to a combinatorial formulation where T frames are selected as a joint subset. It borrows the Bradley-Terry reward ranking loss from RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) and repurposes it to train a lightweight module that scores frame combinations, using a pre-trained Video-LLM's prediction loss as an automatic supervision signal. The approach departs from prior work that either samples frames uniformly or retrieves them independently via semantic similarity, explicitly modeling frame-to-frame interactions through the bottom layers of the LLM backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FRAME-VOYAGER offers a practical plug-and-play solution to the token-length bottleneck in Video-LLMs, with consistent 3–4% gains across VILA-8B, VILA-40B, and LLaVA-OneVision-7B on four benchmarks, and it outperforms LongVILA (128–256 frames) using only 8 frames. Key limitations include reliance on a single reference model (VILA-8B) for data labeling, small training combination sizes (C(16,2), C(32,4)) that may not scale to very long videos, and the plug-in design that leaves the backbone frozen. Open questions include whether integrating frame-combination learning into pre-training would yield stronger gains, and whether the approach generalizes to tasks with ambiguous referents (e.g., EgoSchema's 'camera wearer' questions) where it shows only marginal improvement.
