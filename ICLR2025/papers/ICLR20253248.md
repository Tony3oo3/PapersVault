## Problem

Existing correspondence-based category-level pose estimation methods rely on point-based representations where NOCS coordinates are inherently shape-dependent, causing semantically similar points across different object shapes to map to distinct canonical coordinates. This semantic incoherence forces the correspondence predictor to simultaneously account for pose and large intra-class shape variation, resulting in degraded generalization and large NOCS angle errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253248/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a cropped RGB image (resized to 224×224 for DINOv2 feature extraction) and a partially observed point cloud of N=2048 points back-projected from a segmented depth image, with instance masks provided by an offline Mask R-CNN. Translation and size are predicted separately via a lightweight PointNet++ branch, while the rotation is estimated from the normalized (centralized and scale-normalized) point cloud. The final output is the full 6D pose {R, t} ∈ SE(3) and 3D size s ∈ R³.

## Method

SpherePose projects observed point-wise features onto HEALPix spherical grids (M=768 area-uniform anchors) to create shape-independent spherical representations, then learns the transformation between spherical anchors and spherical NOCS coordinates via a correspondence-based paradigm. Three core designs enable precise correspondence prediction: (1) SO(3)-invariant point-wise feature extraction combining frozen DINOv2 image features, RGB values, radius values, and a proposed ColorPointNet++ (a PointNet++ variant that replaces XYZ coordinates with RGB to ensure invariance); (2) a 6-layer Transformer encoder with learnable position embeddings that propagates features among spherical anchors to handle occlusion-induced missing data; and (3) a hyperbolic correspondence loss (arcosh(1 + L² distance)) that amplifies gradients near zero to distinguish subtle correspondence errors. Rotation is recovered via the Umeyama algorithm with RANSAC outlier removal from the predicted spherical correspondences.

## Results

On REAL275, SpherePose achieves 58.2% on 5°2cm and 88.2% on 10°5cm, outperforming the best correspondence-based method AG-Pose (54.7% / 83.1%) and the best direct regression method SecondPose (56.2% / 86.0%). On the more challenging HouseCat6D dataset, SpherePose reaches 19.3% on 5°2cm and 55.3% on 10°5cm, surpassing AG-Pose by 7.8% and 19.5% respectively. On CAMERA25, SpherePose attains 78.3% on 5°2cm and 92.3% on 10°5cm, edging out AG-Pose (77.8% / 91.6%). The mean NOCS correspondence error drops to 4.52° (angle) and 3.89 cm (distance), compared to 10.36° / 5.88 cm for AG-Pose and 16.62° / 10.50 cm for DPDN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpherePose outperforms all 14 baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest correspondence-based competitor AG-Pose (Lin et al., 2024), SpherePose gains +3.5% on 5°2cm and +5.1% on 10°5cm on REAL275, +7.8% on 5°2cm and +19.5% on 10°5cm on HouseCat6D, and +0.5% on 5°2cm on CAMERA25. Against the strongest direct regression method SecondPose (Chen et al., 2024), SpherePose leads by +2.0% on 5°2cm on REAL275 and by +8.3% on 5°2cm on HouseCat6D, while also using substantially fewer parameters (26.5M vs. 60.2M) and running faster (25.3 vs. 14.3 FPS). The per-category analysis reveals the largest gains on camera and mug categories, which exhibit high intra-class shape variation, confirming that the spherical representation effectively resolves the semantic incoherence problem of point-based NOCS coordinates. On the photometrically challenging HouseCat6D dataset (glass, cutlery, diverse viewpoints), SpherePose achieves the largest absolute margins over all baselines, with a ~20% improvement on 10°5cm over both SecondPose and AG-Pose.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work in category-level object pose estimation: correspondence-based methods (NOCS, DPDN, AG-Pose) that establish point-to-NOCS correspondences and solve pose via Umeyama, and spherical-representation methods (VI-Net, DualPoseNet, SecondPose) that project features onto the sphere. The key departure is combining these: rather than using the sphere for direct regression (as VI-Net and SecondPose do) or using point-based representations for correspondence (as DPDN and AG-Pose do), SpherePose uses the sphere as a shape-independent proxy shape within the correspondence-based paradigm, with HEALPix area-uniform grids replacing the equirectangular grids used by prior spherical methods. The SO(3)-invariant ColorPointNet++ is a minimal modification of PointNet++ that swaps XYZ coordinates for RGB values, and the hyperbolic correspondence loss draws on hyperbolic distance computation from point cloud completion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semantic incoherence problem—where points with similar semantics map to different NOCS coordinates across shape variants—is a fundamental limitation of all point-based correspondence methods, and the sphere-as-proxy-shape framing offers a clean, principled resolution that reduces the correspondence task to pure pose estimation. The per-category results confirm the benefit is concentrated in categories with large intra-class shape variation (camera, mug), validating the motivation. However, the quadratic attention complexity caps the spherical grid at 768 cells (~53.7°² each), limiting spatial resolution; the authors suggest linear attention as a path forward. The method also still depends on an external Mask R-CNN for instance segmentation and a separate PointNet++ for translation/size, leaving the pipeline multi-stage.
