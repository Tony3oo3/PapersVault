## Problem

Existing graph domain adaptation (GDA) methods align source and target graphs primarily through topological structure (adjacency matrices, degree distributions, hierarchical pooling) while neglecting node attribute alignment. This paper demonstrates both empirically and via a PAC-Bayes generalization bound that attribute divergence between domains is often larger than topology divergence, making it a critical but unaddressed source of distribution shift in cross-network node classification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled source graph (node feature matrix X^S ∈ R^{N_S×d}, adjacency matrix A^S, and class labels Y^S) and an unlabeled target graph (X^T ∈ R^{N_T×d}, A^T) as input. From the feature matrices, it constructs a kNN-based feature graph (attribute view) via cosine similarity, so each graph is processed in two parallel views: the original topology graph and the derived attribute graph. The output is predicted class labels for all target graph nodes, evaluated by classification accuracy across cross-network transfer tasks (e.g., USA→Brazil airports, ACM→DBLP citations).

## Method

GAA processes each graph through two parallel GCN branches: one on the original adjacency matrix (topology view) and one on a kNN feature graph built from node attributes (attribute view). An attention-based module maps each view's embeddings into query/key/value spaces to produce learnable attribute embeddings, which are then refined by a cross-view similarity matrix (cosine similarity between topology and attribute embeddings) applied via Hadamard product to integrate structural and attribute information. The alignment loss L_A minimizes MSE between source and target embeddings in both views simultaneously, directly corresponding to the two terms in the PAC-Bayes bound: topology divergence ‖(A^S X^S)_i − (A^T X^T)_j‖² and attribute divergence ‖X_i^S − X_j^T‖². Training combines this alignment loss with source cross-entropy, an adversarial domain classifier (via GRL) on topology-view embeddings, and target entropy regularization, all optimized jointly by backpropagation.

## Results

On the Citation, Blog, and Social network benchmarks (Table 3), GAA achieves the best accuracy across all 8 cross-network node classification tasks, with headline values of 0.789 (A→D), 0.824 (A→C), 0.798 (D→C), and 0.681 (B1→B2). The paper reports a maximum average improvement of 1.80% in accuracy over the strongest SOTA baseline. On the Airport dataset (Table 2), GAA leads on 6 of 8 tasks (e.g., U→B: 0.704, E→B: 0.691, DE→EN: 0.779), and on the large-scale MAG dataset (Table 4), GAA outperforms all baselines on all 10 tasks (e.g., US→JP: 0.492, CN→US: 0.453).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAA outperforms all 12 baselines on every task in Table 3 (Citation, Blog, Social). Against the strongest topology-focused competitors, GAA beats JHGDA by 3.4% on A→D (0.789 vs 0.755) and 1.0% on A→C (0.824 vs 0.814), and beats SpecReg by 2.7% on A→D (0.789 vs 0.762) and a substantial 10.0% on D→A (0.754 vs 0.654). Against the most recent pairwise-alignment method PA, GAA leads by 3.7% on A→D (0.789 vs 0.752) and 2.0% on A→C (0.824 vs 0.804). On the Blog social networks, GAA achieves 0.681 (B1→B2) and 0.679 (B2→B1), surpassing the next-best PA (0.662/0.654) by ~2–3%, while ASN is the only baseline that exceeds GAA on B1→B2 (0.732), suggesting ASN's domain-invariant separation is particularly effective for that specific pair. On the large MAG dataset (Table 4), GAA leads all baselines on all 10 tasks, with the largest margin over PA on US→JP (0.492 vs 0.474) and CN→US (0.453 vs 0.452), while several baselines (UDAGCN, EGI, JHGDA) fail entirely with OOM errors due to the dataset's scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph domain adaptation (GDA) line of work initiated by Shen et al. (2019, 2020a, 2020b) and Wu et al. (2020), which progressively incorporated topological structure into cross-network alignment. It departs from the dominant topology-only paradigm (UDAGCN, JHGDA, SpecReg, GRADE-N) by arguing that node attribute divergence is equally or more critical than structural divergence, supported by a PAC-Bayes generalization bound (building on Ma et al., 2021) that explicitly decomposes domain discrepancy into topology and attribute terms. Methodologically, GAA introduces a kNN-based 'feature graph' as a second alignment channel alongside the original topology graph, combined with attention-based attribute embedding and cross-view similarity matrix refinement — a dual-view design not present in prior GDA methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that attribute shift exceeds topology shift across multiple GDA benchmarks provides a concrete motivation for practitioners to consider feature-space alignment in addition to structural alignment, and the dual-view framework is a straightforward extension that could be incorporated into existing GDA pipelines. However, the theoretical bound is quite loose (double sums over all source-target node pairs with O(·) constants), the average improvement over the best baseline is modest (~1.8%), and several strong baselines (UDAGCN, EGI, JHGDA) fail with OOM on the large MAG dataset, limiting the scalability claim. Open questions include whether the kNN feature graph construction is optimal, whether the approach extends to link-level or graph-level transfer, and whether the attribute-dominance finding holds under more controlled or adversarial distribution shifts.
