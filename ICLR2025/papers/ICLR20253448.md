## Problem

Despite extensive experimental evidence showing that lower mammals (rodents) exhibit disordered salt-and-pepper orientation maps while higher mammals (cats, primates) form continuous pinwheel structures in V1, the spiking mechanisms underlying the emergence of these distinct topologies and their functional roles remain poorly understood. Existing computational models—self-organizing maps, rate-based models, and prior SNNs—either lack biological realism or fail to reproduce pinwheel structures, with no previous spiking network with plasticity successfully generating them.

## Input / Output

The SESNN takes 160 whitened natural images (20 originals rotated/flipped in 90° increments) as input, with each of 4900 E-neurons perceiving a 16×16 pixel patch from an overlapping visual field (overlap parameter ε ranging 10–15 pixels) presented for 100 ms per trial. The primary output is a spatially organized orientation preference map on a 2D cortical lattice (70×70 E-neurons, 35×35 I-neurons with periodic boundaries), from which spike sequences are extracted. When deployed as a front-end encoder for a 5-layer convolutional SNN, these spike sequences produce FashionMNIST class labels.

## Method

The SESNN is a recurrent network of leaky integrate-and-fire neurons (4900 E, 1225 I, 4:1 ratio) trained with two distinct Hebbian-like rules: the Hebbian Oja variant (HO) governs E-E and feedforward connections, learning principal components of input patterns with a normalization term to constrain weights to [0,1], while the Correlation Measuring (CM) rule governs all E-I, I-E, and I-I lateral connections, capturing activity covariance without normalization to enable stronger inhibitory weights. Initial synaptic weights follow a Gaussian decay with Euclidean distance (σ_EE=3.5, σ_EI=2.9, σ_IE=2.6, σ_II=2.1), calibrated to anatomical axon/dendrite lengths. The critical finding is that the visual field overlap parameter ε acts as a bifurcation threshold: below ε=10 pixels the network produces salt-and-pepper organizations, while above it, Hebbian plasticity strengthens local E-E connections among neurons with correlated inputs, forming iso-orientation domains whose swirling boundaries create pinwheels. The CM rule via E-I-E loops then suppresses predictable excitatory responses, enforcing orientation diversity at pinwheel centers and driving sparse coding, while the HO rule maintains local continuity within domains.

## Results

The SESNN model reproduces macaque V1 pinwheel metrics with close fidelity: pinwheel density of 3.175±0.397 pinwheels/mm² (vs. ~3.327 in macaques), NNPD of 0.277±0.043 mm (vs. ~0.242 mm), and hypercolumn size of 0.839±0.054 mm (vs. ~0.760 mm). On FashionMNIST classification, the pinwheel-organized encoder achieves 90.09% overall accuracy versus 88.87% for a Poisson spike encoder. Under Gaussian noise, the pinwheel encoder retains 82.91% accuracy at noise level 0.5, while the Poisson encoder drops to 18.79%, demonstrating substantially superior noise robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the SNN model of Srinivasa & Jiang (2013), which the paper identifies as the closest prior spiking network with plasticity. The SESNN outperforms this baseline on pinwheel density (3.175 vs. ~2.941 pinwheels/mm²) and, critically, produces well-defined NNPD and hypercolumn size metrics where the baseline yields no meaningful values (N/A), indicating the baseline fails to generate proper pinwheel topology. On the FashionMNIST classification task, the pinwheel-organized SESNN encoder achieves 90.09% overall accuracy, outperforming the Poisson spike encoder (88.87%) by 1.22 percentage points. The advantage becomes dramatic under noise: at noise level 0.5, the pinwheel encoder maintains 82.91% accuracy while the Poisson encoder collapses to 18.79%, and at noise 0.7 the gap widens to 43.60% vs. 10.89%. The salt-and-pepper encoder (representing rodent-like organization) also degrades faster than pinwheels under high noise (67.39% at noise 0.5 vs. 82.91%), confirming that pinwheel topology confers a robustness advantage over both random and disorganized spatial arrangements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of self-organizing spiking neural network models for V1 orientation maps, building directly on the Hebbian Oja rule (Oja, 1982) for E-E connections and the Correlation Measuring rule (Zylberberg et al., 2011; King et al., 2013) for E-I connections, combined with LIF dynamics and adaptive thresholds from Stevens et al. (2013). It explicitly positions itself against the Srinivasa & Jiang (2013) SNN baseline, which the authors note produced 'line discontinuity instead of discrete point discontinuity' and thus failed to reproduce true pinwheel singularities. The key departure is the integration of species-specific anatomical parameters (retinotopy, neuronal density, axon/dendrite lengths) into a unified self-evolving framework, with visual field overlap identified as the critical control parameter separating salt-and-pepper from pinwheel topologies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first spiking-network mechanistic account of why higher mammals develop pinwheel structures while rodents retain salt-and-pepper organizations, linking a single anatomical variable (visual field overlap) to a discrete topological transition. The demonstration that pinwheels achieve lower wiring cost, higher information-cost efficiency, and greater noise robustness gives a functional rationale for the evolutionary pressure toward pinwheel organization. However, the practical validation on FashionMNIST is limited in scope (10-class, small dataset), and the model's 2D lattice architecture, fixed E-I ratio, and single cortical layer simplify the true 3D, multi-layer cortical circuitry. Open questions include whether the overlap-threshold mechanism generalizes to 3D cortical geometry, whether the specific HO/CM rule combination is necessary or whether other plasticity rules could produce the same topologies, and whether the coding advantages persist on ecologically richer visual tasks.
