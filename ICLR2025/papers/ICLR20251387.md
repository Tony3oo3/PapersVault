## Problem

Existing visual assistants either handle a narrow set of tasks through diffusion-based editing (e.g., InstructPix2Pix, InstructDiffusion) or rely on pixel-based in-context prompting without the ability to follow human language instructions (e.g., Painter, LVM). No prior system unifies the full spectrum of vision tasks—generation, restoration, grounding, dense prediction, editing, inpainting/outpainting, and controllable generation—under a single framework that accepts free-form, open-language instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251387/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PixWizard takes an input RGB image (or a fully white/black blank canvas to simulate text-to-image generation) together with a free-form natural-language instruction, and produces a single output image at any resolution and aspect ratio. For tasks whose natural output is not an image (e.g., per-pixel depth values, semantic class labels, bounding-box coordinates), the model first generates a color-coded visualization that is then decoded via post-processing (channel averaging, nearest-color lookup, or coordinate extraction). The dynamic partitioning and padding scheme supports resolutions up to 1024×1024 with arbitrary aspect ratios, using NTK-aware scaled RoPE for resolution extrapolation at inference.

## Method

PixWizard is initialized from the pretrained Lumina-Next-T2I flow-based DiT and trained with a Conditional Flow Matching objective that predicts a velocity field conditioned on both an image and a text instruction. Two complementary guidance mechanisms fuse input-image information: a structure-aware path concatenates the SDXL-VAE-encoded image latent with the noise latent along the channel dimension (zero-initialized patch-embedder channels), and a semantic-aware path injects CLIP L/14-336 image embeddings through zero-initialized gated cross-attention layers. A Task-Aware Dynamic Sampler, inspired by DynamicViT, scores each semantic token with a lightweight MLP that also receives a task embedding (derived from CLIP text features via an MLP), then retains only the top-K tokens per layer using a Multi-Hot Gumbel-Softmax relaxation for differentiable top-K selection. Training follows a two-stage protocol: stage 1 upsamples small-dataset tasks to ~20k samples each for 4 epochs, and stage 2 trains on the full 30M-sample Omni Pixel-to-Pixel Instruction-Tuning Dataset with manual per-task sampling weights and 1:1 text-to-image mixing. Classifier-free guidance is applied with separate weights for the image and text conditions, allowing independent control over structural fidelity and instruction adherence.

## Results

On the multi-task benchmark (Table 1), PixWizard achieves 0.287 RMSE on NYUv2 depth estimation (a 10% improvement over Unified-IO's 0.387), 38.67 PSNR on SIDD denoising (outperforming InstructDiffusion's 34.26), 31.43 PSNR on Rain100L deraining (vs. InstructDiffusion's 19.82), and 46.44 cIoU on RefCOCO grounding (vs. InstructDiffusion's 41.64). On image editing (Table 2), PixWizard achieves the best L1 distance (0.069 on Emu Edit, 0.063 on MagicBrush) and competitive CLIP/DINO scores. For generation (Table 3), PixWizard attains FID 7.54 on outpainting, FID 9.27 on inpainting, and FID 9.56 on zero-shot COCO text-to-image, while also achieving the highest F1 (35.46) for canny-conditioned generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unified generalist models, PixWizard consistently outperforms InstructDiffusion across all shared tasks: +4.4 PSNR on SIDD denoising (38.67 vs. 34.26), +11.6 PSNR on Rain100L deraining (31.43 vs. 19.82), and +4.8 cIoU on RefCOCO grounding (46.44 vs. 41.64). Compared to Painter and InstructCV, PixWizard achieves comparable depth estimation (0.287 vs. 0.288/0.297 RMSE on NYUv2) but trails significantly on semantic segmentation (32.76 vs. 49.90/47.23 mIoU) and deraining (31.43 vs. 29.87 PSNR, where Painter is close but InstructCV does not support the task). Against task-specific models, PixWizard remains competitive on denoising (38.67 PSNR vs. PromptIR's 39.52) and surface normal estimation (19.65° vs. InvPT's 19.04°), but is outperformed by specialized methods on segmentation (32.76 vs. Mask DINO's 60.80 mIoU), deraining (31.43 vs. PromptIR's 36.37 PSNR), and grounding (46.44 vs. ReLA's 73.21 cIoU). On image editing, PixWizard achieves the best L1 distance on both Emu Edit (0.069) and MagicBrush (0.063), outperforming InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, and UltraEdit, while remaining close to EmuEdit on CLIP direction similarity. For generation tasks, PixWizard surpasses ControlNet-SD1.5 on canny-conditioned F1 (35.46 vs. 34.65) and depth-conditioned RMSE (33.83 vs. 35.90), outperforms LaMa and LDM-4 on inpainting FID (9.27 vs. 12.0/9.39), and beats DeepFill v2 and MaskGIT on outpainting FID (7.54 vs. 11.51/7.80).

## Relation to Prior Work

PixWizard sits at the intersection of two prior research lines: instruction-tuned diffusion models for image editing (InstructPix2Pix, InstructDiffusion, InstructCV) and in-context visual generalists using pixel prompts (Painter, PromptDiffusion, LVM). It departs from both by adopting a flow-based Diffusion Transformer (initialized from Lumina-Next-T2I) rather than a U-Net, and by replacing pixel-based prompts with free-form natural language instructions. The architecture introduces structure-aware guidance (channel-concatenated VAE latents, following InstructPix2Pix) combined with semantic-aware guidance (CLIP image embeddings via zero-initialized cross-attention), and adapts DynamicViT's token sparsification into a Task-Aware Dynamic Sampler using Multi-Hot Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable top-K selection. The 'drawing' strategy—feeding a blank white/black image to unify text-to-image within the image-to-image framework—is a practical but novel unification trick not seen in prior instruction-tuned diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PixWizard demonstrates that a single DiT-based model trained on 30M multi-task instances can approach task-specific performance across seven diverse vision domains (restoration, grounding, dense prediction, editing, generation, inpainting, outpainting) while remaining controllable via open-language instructions—a capability absent from prior pixel-prompt generalists. Key limitations include the lack of multi-image input support, a persistent gap versus specialized models on segmentation and grounding, and dependence on the quality of the text encoder and foundation model. The authors note that the proposed modules (task embedder, dual guidance, dynamic sampler) are architecture-agnostic and transferable to stronger backbones like SD3 or FLUX, positioning this as a stepping stone toward a unified 'GPT-4 moment' for vision. The 30M Omni Pixel-to-Pixel Instruction-Tuning Dataset itself is a significant community resource for multi-task visual instruction tuning.
