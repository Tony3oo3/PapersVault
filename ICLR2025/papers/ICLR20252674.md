## Problem

Existing referring expression comprehension (REC) models struggle to generalize to unseen expressions due to insufficient image-expression training pairs, while current referring expression generation (REG) methods based on CNN-LSTM or Transformer-LSTM frameworks are deterministic, limited in capacity, and fail to effectively bridge the visual-textual domain gap. This paper introduces diffusion models to REG for the first time, generating diverse synonymous expressions that augment REC training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252674/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (H×W×3) and a cropped target object patch (H_o×W_o×3) as visual input, processed by a ViT/B-32 encoder into visual token sequences. The ground-truth expression is encoded by BERT into L text tokens, then mapped to continuous embeddings via an E_MB function for the diffusion process. The output is a generated referring expression (max sequence length 128, embedding dimension 128); multiple diverse expressions are sampled per target object, and the top 30% by Meteor score are selected to augment REC training sets.

## Method

VIE-DM is a conditional text diffusion model with a Vision-Text Condition (VTC) module and a 12-layer transformer decoder. At each denoising step, the VTC module applies cross-attention to condition noisy text features on both the full-image tokens and target-object tokens, then uses a token selection strategy that computes cosine similarity between image-conditioned and object-conditioned features to retain only the most relevant image tokens, reducing irrelevant visual noise. The model is trained with a standard DDPM noise-prediction objective (2000 steps, square-root schedule) augmented with classifier-free guidance (γ=3.0, 20% unconditional samples) to balance diversity and accuracy. At inference, expressions are decoded via rounding and refined with Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding over 10 candidates scored by Meteor. The resulting diverse image-expression pairs are integrated into REC training sets, consistently improving six transformer-based REC methods across five benchmarks.

## Results

On the primary REG task, VIE-DM with ResNet101 achieves Meteor 0.445 / CIDEr 1.207 on RefCOCO testA, 0.472 / 2.014 on testB, 0.453 / 1.543 on RefCOCO+ testA, 0.491 / 1.663 on RefCOCO+ testB, and 0.735 / 1.558 on RefCOCOg val-g, outperforming all prior REG methods by large margins (e.g., +0.135 Meteor over the best CNN-LSTM baseline on RefCOCO testA). For the downstream REC task, augmenting training data with VIE-DM-generated expressions consistently improves six transformer-based REC models across five datasets; for example, MDETR+VIE-DM reaches 92.15 mAP@1 on RefCOCO testA (vs. 89.58 without augmentation), and OFA-base+VIE-DM reaches 91.75 (vs. 90.67). In diversity metrics, VIE-DM achieves Div-1 of 0.526 and Div-2 of 0.517 on RefCOCO testA, substantially exceeding the 0.327–0.385 range of prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIE-DM outperforms all ten REG baselines on every reported metric across RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg. Against the strongest CNN-LSTM baseline (Tanaka 2019, ResNet101), VIE-DM gains +0.135 Meteor and +0.348 CIDEr on RefCOCO testA, and +0.571 Meteor on RefCOCOg val-g. Against the transformer-based PFOS (Sun 2023), VIE-DM leads by +0.142 Meteor on RefCOCO testA and +0.579 Meteor on RefCOCOg val-g. Against the LLM-based MiniGPT-v2 (Chen 2023, 7B parameters), VIE-DM (1.2B parameters) still wins by +0.028 Meteor on RefCOCO testA and +0.123 Meteor on RefCOCOg val-g, while using roughly 6× fewer parameters. For the REC task, augmenting with VIE-DM-generated expressions improves all six transformer-based REC baselines (TransVG, M-DGT, VLTVG, QRNet, MDETR, OFA-base) on all five datasets; the largest gains appear for MDETR (+2.57 mAP on RefCOCO testA) and TransVG (+1.86 mAP on RefCOCO testA). In diversity comparisons (Table 3), VIE-DM achieves Div-1/Div-2 scores roughly 1.5–2× higher than the best prior method (SLRVM), while maintaining lower mBLEU-4 (indicating greater lexical variety). The only scenario where VIE-DM does not strictly dominate is RefCOCO+ testB Meteor, where VIE-DM w/o CFG (0.411) slightly exceeds full VIE-DM (0.408), though the CIDEr gap remains in VIE-DM's favor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the first diffusion model for referring expression generation (REG), departing from the dominant CNN-LSTM and Transformer-LSTM frameworks (Mao et al. 2016; Yu et al. 2017; Liu et al. 2020; Tanaka et al. 2019; Sun et al. 2023) that the authors argue fail to bridge the visual-textual domain gap and produce deterministic, low-diversity outputs. The method builds directly on continuous-space text diffusion (Diffusion-LM, Li et al. 2022; DiffusER, Reid et al. 2023) and the DDPM training objective (Ho et al. 2020), adapting them with a novel vision-text condition (VTC) module that injects image and target-object features via cross-attention at every denoising step, plus a token selection mechanism to focus on object-relevant visual tokens. Classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021) and Minimum Bayes Risk decoding are incorporated to balance accuracy and diversity. The paper also positions itself within the REC literature by demonstrating that generated expressions serve as effective data augmentation for transformer-based REC models (TransVG, MDETR, QRNet, etc.), complementing prior augmentation attempts like SelfEQ (He et al. 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck in REC: limited training pairs hinder generalization to unseen expressions, and the demonstrated ~2–4 mAP gains across six REC methods on five datasets show that diffusion-generated expressions are a broadly applicable augmentation strategy. The VTC module's token selection design offers a reusable pattern for conditioning text diffusion on spatially localized visual evidence, potentially transferable to other region-specific generation tasks. Limitations include the 1.2B parameter count, 86-hour training cost, and residual generation errors (e.g., confusing a striped shirt with a zebra), which the authors mitigate via Meteor-score filtering but do not fully eliminate. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video referring expressions, whether the 30% augmentation ceiling reflects a fundamental quality limit or a filtering strategy issue, and whether the method's gains persist when the REC backbone is itself a large multimodal model.
