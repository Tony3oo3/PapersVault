## Problem

Existing RAG training methods face a trade-off: Label-Supervised Tuning (LST) provides only sparse binary labels per document, limiting the model's ability to understand *why* a document is relevant, while Chain-Supervised Tuning (CST) requires expensive stepwise reasoning annotations that are costly to collect and difficult to generalize from. This paper addresses how to train LLMs to perform robust multi-step document assessment in RAG without relying on extensive reasoning chain annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251865/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a question and T=5 retrieved documents, then generates a sequential reasoning chain where each step assesses one document as relevant or irrelevant with an explanation, followed by a final answer. Training requires only a small SFT set S_CS (generated by Llama3-8B-Instruct to teach self-correction meta-skills) and a larger label-supervised set S_LS from KILT with binary relevance labels—no stepwise reasoning annotations are needed for the main training phase.

## Method

Backtracking Correction reformulates RAG as a multi-step decision-making process and trains the model by starting from the final state and iteratively backtracking to earlier states. At each state t, the model is fed a fully correct prefix s_{t-1}^+ to isolate errors to the current step, then samples its own on-policy errors o_t and generates corrected outputs o_t^+ using gold labels as feedback. A self-play RL objective (Equation 13) optimizes the model to distinguish corrected from incorrect chains, producing a DPO-like loss that requires no pairwise annotations—only self-generated errors and self-corrections. The backtracking schedule creates an easy-to-hard progression: as the target state moves earlier, the context space grows while the decision space shrinks, making each optimization step progressively more tractable. The reference model and training data are dynamic across iterations (unlike static DPO), and the KL-regularized optimal policy is derived from reward-weighted regression theory.

## Results

On four open-domain QA benchmarks (PopQA, TriviaQA, WebQuestions, SQuAD), Backtracking Correction with LLaMA3-8B-base achieves exact match scores of 59.3, 70.1, 49.3, and 44.9 respectively, outperforming all open-source RAG baselines including Self-RAG-7B (53.8/62.6/32.4/26.5) and REAR (52.9/71.6/38.4/43.8). With LLaMA2-7B-base, BC scores 58.4/68.1/47.6/44.5, still surpassing RobustLM-13B (49.1/62.0/27.3/27.4) and LLaMA2-7B-chat with retrieval (54.0/63.1/45.3/37.2) across all datasets. Notably, BC with LLaMA3-8B exceeds GPT-4 on PopQA (59.3 vs. 38.7) and SQuAD (44.9 vs. 35.1) despite being a 7B/8B open-source model, though it trails GPT-4 on TriviaQA (70.1 vs. 83.4) and WebQuestions (49.3 vs. 61.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Backtracking Correction (BC) with LLaMA3-8B-base decisively outperforms all open-source RAG baselines: it beats Self-RAG-7B by 5.5/7.5/16.9/18.4 points on PopQA/TriviaQA/WebQuestions/SQuAD, and REAR by 6.4/−1.5/10.9/1.1 points (losing only narrowly on TriviaQA). Against RobustLM-13B, BC wins on all four datasets by 10.2/8.1/22.0/17.5 points. Compared to proprietary models, BC with LLaMA3-8B surpasses GPT-4 on PopQA (59.3 vs. 38.7) and SQuAD (44.9 vs. 35.1) but trails on TriviaQA (70.1 vs. 83.4) and WebQuestions (49.3 vs. 61.6), reflecting GPT-4's stronger parametric knowledge. Against instruction-tuned open-source models with retrieval (Mistral-7B, LLaMA2-7B-chat), BC with LLaMA3-8B wins on PopQA (59.3 vs. 59.1) and SQuAD (44.9 vs. 42.0) but is slightly behind on TriviaQA (70.1 vs. 71.2) and WebQuestions (49.3 vs. 57.6). The key advantage highlighted is that BC achieves these results without requiring chain-of-thought annotations from proprietary LLMs or additional training data beyond the original KILT dataset, unlike Self-RAG, REAR, and RAFT which depend on expensive external annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RL-based fine-tuning framework of ReFT (Trung et al., 2024) and the stepwise preference optimization idea from Step-DPO (Lai et al., 2024), but departs from both by introducing a backtracking algorithm that optimizes reasoning chains from the final state backward. The self-play formulation (Player A distinguishing corrected vs. uncorrected chains, Player B generating corrections) draws on the self-play RL literature (DiGiovanni & Zell, 2021) and the KL-regularized optimal policy derivation from Peters & Schaal (2007) and Peng et al. (2019), ultimately yielding a DPO-like loss that requires no pairwise annotations. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against two existing paradigms for RAG robustness: Label-Supervised Tuning (SELF-RAG, REAR, FILCO) which provides sparse binary supervision, and Chain-Supervised Tuning (Chain-of-Note, RAFT) which requires expensive stepwise CoT annotations, arguing that both are suboptimal for learning multi-step document assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for costly chain-of-thought annotations while still achieving competitive RAG performance, making robust retrieval-augmented generation more accessible. The backtracking strategy (easy-to-hard progression by shrinking the decision space) offers a general principle for training multi-step reasoning that could extend beyond RAG to math or code, though the authors acknowledge this extension may be more challenging. Key limitations include evaluation only on 7B/8B models and four open-domain QA benchmarks, and the method still requires a small SFT dataset to bootstrap self-correction ability. An open question is whether the self-play mechanism generalizes to settings where gold labels are unavailable or where the reasoning chain is substantially longer than five steps.
