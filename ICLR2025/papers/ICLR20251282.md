## Problem

CNN-based architectures have been largely overshadowed by Transformer and MLP models in time series analysis, with existing convolutional approaches (TimesNet, MICN, ModernTCN) predominantly modeling features within a single time window while neglecting the comprehensive integration of global, local, and cross-variable interactions. The paper asks whether a CNN can be redesigned to capture the full 3D variation structure of time series—spanning intra-patch, inter-patch, and cross-variable dependencies—while retaining the computational efficiency that makes convolution attractive.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series look-back window X ∈ R^{L×C} and reshapes it into a 3D tensor X_emb ∈ R^{N×2×(P/2)×C_m} via patching and odd-even splitting, where N = L/P patches each of length P are further split into odd and even index subsequences. The output is task-dependent: forecast sequences (R^{T1×C_o}), imputed values (R^{T2×C_o}), a single class label (R^{1×C}), or per-timestep anomaly scores, all produced by a linear head applied to the reshaped 3D representation. The odd-even split is a distinctive design choice that creates an explicit second spatial dimension within each patch, enabling 2D convolution to simultaneously model temporal and cross-variable structure.

## Method

TVNet introduces a 3D-Embedding module that converts a 1D time series into a 3D tensor by (1) linearly projecting features to dimension C_m, (2) applying Conv1D with kernel P to segment into N patches, and (3) performing an odd-even split along the patch-length axis to yield a 4D tensor of shape N×2×(P/2)×C_m. The core 3D-Block employs dynamic convolution where each patch i receives a time-varying weight α_i = 1 + F(v_intra) + F(v_inter), generated by adaptive 3D average pooling (for intra-patch features) followed by 1D average pooling (for inter-patch features), each passed through a Conv1D channel-mixing layer. The effective per-patch weight is W_i = α_i · W_b, decomposing into a shared base weight and a patch-specific scalar, so that at initialization the network reduces to standard convolution. Multiple 3D-blocks are stacked residually, and the final 3D representation is flattened and passed through a task-specific linear head. The paper provides a theoretical argument (Theorem B.1) showing the dynamic weight model achieves strictly lower error than a fixed-weight model under optimal parameterization, and reports O(LC_m²) time complexity with O(C_m²) space complexity independent of sequence length.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (Table 1), TVNet achieves the best average MSE/MAE on 6 of 9 datasets, including ETTm1 (0.348/0.379), ETTm2 (0.251/0.311), ETTh1 (0.407/0.421), Weather (0.221/0.261), Exchange (0.298/0.367), and ILI (1.406/0.766). On short-term forecasting (M4), TVNet attains the best SMAPE (11.671), MASE (1.536), and OWA (0.832). For imputation, TVNet achieves the lowest MSE and MAE across all six datasets (e.g., ETTm1: 0.018/0.088; Electricity: 0.079/0.194). In classification, TVNet reaches 74.6% average accuracy, and in anomaly detection it achieves an 86.8 F1-score, both competitive with or exceeding prior state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TVNet outperforms all Transformer-based baselines (iTransformer, PatchTST, Crossformer, FEDformer) on the majority of long-term forecasting datasets, with particularly large margins on ILI (MSE 1.406 vs. 2.141 for iTransformer) and Exchange (0.298 vs. 0.360). Against the strongest CNN baseline ModernTCN, TVNet wins on 7 of 9 datasets, losing only on Electricity (0.165 vs. 0.156 MSE) and ETTh2 (0.324 vs. 0.322). Compared to MLP-based models (DLinear, RLinear, MTS-Mixer), TVNet achieves lower MSE on 7–8 of 9 datasets, demonstrating that the 3D dynamic convolution captures cross-variable and inter-patch dependencies that linear models miss. On short-term M4 forecasting, TVNet leads all 12 baselines on all three metrics (SMAPE 11.671, MASE 1.536, OWA 0.832). For imputation, TVNet dominates every dataset, with the gap widening as missingness increases (e.g., Electricity MSE 0.079 vs. 0.091 for PatchTST). In classification, TVNet's 74.6% average accuracy surpasses Flowformer, Rocket, and LSTNet; in anomaly detection, its 86.8 F1 is competitive with Anomaly Transformer. Notably, TVNet achieves these results with O(LC_m²) time complexity and O(C_m² + LC_m) space complexity, substantially lower than the O(L²) of standard Transformers, while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

TVNet extends the 2D temporal reshaping idea from TimesNet (1D→2D) to a 3D formulation by adding an odd-even split dimension (borrowed from SCINet), creating three explicit axes: inter-patch, intra-patch, and cross-variable. It departs from static convolution by introducing per-patch dynamic weights inspired by video-domain dynamic convolution (TADA, Dynamic Filter Networks), where a shared base kernel is modulated by a content-dependent scalar. Conceptually, the paper positions CNNs as a viable alternative to the dominant Transformer and MLP paradigms in time series, arguing that the 3D perspective plus dynamic weighting recovers the representational capacity that made attention-based models attractive, while retaining convolutional efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering a single CNN architecture that is competitive across five heterogeneous time series tasks (long/short-term forecasting, imputation, classification, anomaly detection) with O(L·C_m²) time complexity, avoiding the quadratic cost of attention. This is relevant for deployment settings where memory and latency matter. However, the performance margins over the strongest baselines (ModernTCN, RLinear) are often marginal (e.g., 0.001–0.005 MSE on several ETT datasets), and the paper does not explore very long sequences (>2000) or pre-training regimes where the efficiency advantage would be most pronounced. Open questions include whether the 3D-embedding generalizes to irregularly sampled or hierarchical time series, and whether the dynamic weight mechanism scales to thousands of patches without degrading.
