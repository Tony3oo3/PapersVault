## Problem

Achieving optimal Õ(√T) strong regret and strong constraint violation in constrained MDPs (CMDPs) via efficient policy optimization remained an open problem. Efroni et al. (2020) attained these bounds but required solving linear programs over occupancy measures, which is computationally prohibitive in practice. Müller et al. (2024) proposed the first primal-dual policy optimization method for strong metrics but only achieved a suboptimal Õ(T^0.93) rate, leaving a significant gap to the optimal √T order.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in a finite-horizon episodic CMDP with bandit feedback: at each episode the learner only observes rewards and costs for the state-action pairs actually visited along the trajectory, not the full reward/cost vectors. The method requires as hyperparameters the number of episodes T, a confidence parameter δ, and the Slater feasibility margin ρ (the maximum constraint slack over all policies). It outputs a policy π_t for each episode and guarantees Õ(√T) cumulative strong regret and strong violation with high probability, without ever optimizing over the occupancy measure space.

## Method

CPD-PO implements a primal-dual scheme where the primal regret minimizer is PO-DB (policy optimization with dilated bonuses), a state-of-the-art algorithm for adversarial MDPs under bandit feedback that performs state-by-state optimization updates. The key novelty lies in the dual update: instead of gradient descent or multiplicative weights (as in Efroni et al. or Müller et al.), CPD-PO uses a UCB-like binary choice, setting each Lagrange multiplier to either 0 or (L+1)/ρ depending on whether the pessimistic cost estimate under the current policy violates the corresponding threshold. This binary dual update ensures that the Lagrangian value of any policy is upper-bounded by the constrained optimum (up to confidence terms), which collapses the standard Lagrangian regret into the strong regret definition. The primal is fed a Lagrangian loss constructed from optimistic reward estimates and pessimistic cost estimates, scaled to the positive-loss range expected by PO-DB. Unlike Müller et al., no regularization of the Lagrangian game is needed, and unlike Efroni et al., no optimization over occupancy measures is performed, making the algorithm fully policy-based and computationally efficient.

## Results

The paper provides theoretical guarantees for the CPD-PO algorithm in episodic CMDPs with bandit feedback. Theorem 1 establishes a strong regret bound of Õ(L³m/ρ · |X|√(|A|T) + L⁵m/ρ), and Theorem 2 establishes a strong constraint violation bound of Õ(L³m|X|√(|A|T) + L⁵m), both holding with probability at least 1−O(δ). These are optimal Õ(√T) bounds in the number of episodes T, matching the information-theoretic lower bound for this problem class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main theoretical comparison is with Müller et al. (2024), the prior state-of-the-art primal-dual policy optimization method for strong regret/violation in CMDPs. CPD-PO achieves the optimal Õ(√T) rate in T, improving upon Müller et al.'s suboptimal Õ(T^0.93) bound. Compared to Efroni et al. (2020), which also achieves Õ(√T) strong regret/violation, CPD-PO matches the rate but does so via an efficient policy optimization approach (state-by-state updates via PO-DB) rather than solving linear programs over the occupancy measure space, which is computationally intractable in practice. The paper further notes that its bound is not worse than Müller et al. (2024) with respect to the dependency on the Slater parameter ρ, the number of constraints m, and the horizon L, and that the violation bound does not depend on ρ at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the primal-dual framework for CMDPs introduced by Efroni et al. (2020), who first achieved optimal O~(√T) strong regret/violation via linear programming over occupancy measures, and whose primal-dual variants only attained weak (cancellation-allowing) guarantees. It also extends the recent work of Muller et al. (2024), who first achieved sublinear strong regret/violation via primal-dual policy optimization but only at a suboptimal O~(T^0.93) rate, relying on a regularized Lagrangian to ensure strict concavity/convexity. The key departure is the use of a UCB-like binary dual update (choosing between 0 and (L+1)/ρ) paired with PO-DB (Luo et al., 2021) as the primal, eliminating both the need for regularization and any optimization over occupancy measures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result closes a significant open problem in constrained online learning: whether optimal √T strong regret and violation bounds are achievable through efficient policy optimization rather than computationally expensive occupancy-measure-based methods. The practical implication is that constrained RL agents (autonomous driving, ad budgeting, recommendation) can now use a fully model-free, state-by-state policy optimization algorithm with provably optimal guarantees. Limitations include the reliance on Slater's condition (a strict feasibility margin ρ > 0), the episodic finite-horizon setting, and the absence of empirical validation; extending to non-episodic or non-stationary settings, or removing the Slater assumption, remain open.
