## Problem

Offline safe RL methods face two intertwined challenges: (1) policies trained on offline data encounter OOD states and actions during deployment, causing safety violations or excessive conservatism, and (2) most existing approaches are locked to a single fixed constraint threshold and cannot adapt to varying or unseen cost budgets at deployment time. No prior value-based method achieves zero-shot adaptation to different constraint thresholds while simultaneously handling distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

The policy π(a|s,κ) takes the current state s and a dynamic cost budget κ as input and outputs an action. The cost budget is initialized arbitrarily at the start of each rollout and updated autoregressively as κ_{t+1} = κ_t − c_t based on incurred costs, so the agent must adjust its behavior as the budget depletes. During training, the CVAE and classifier are conditioned on κ to generate and classify state-action pairs, while the critics and actor are all parameterized as functions of (s, a, κ), enabling a single trained policy to satisfy any cost budget at evaluation without retraining.

## Method

CCAC reinterprets the offline dataset as generated by a constraint-conditioned behavior policy, where the cost budget κ serves as the conditioning variable that explains behavioral variation. A constraint-conditioned VAE (CVAE) learns the joint state-action distribution p(s,a|z,κ) to generate diverse augmented samples, and a constraint-conditioned binary classifier h(s,a|κ) identifies which generated pairs are OOD (i.e., would exceed the cost budget). The cost critic is trained via a primal-dual objective that minimizes Bellman error on dataset samples while enforcing E_{s,a~ν}[Q_c(s,a|κ)] ≥ ε, where ν is the OOD distribution, thereby overestimating Q_c for unsafe pairs; a theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) guarantees this overestimation exceeds the threshold under appropriate Lagrangian multipliers. The reward critic uses standard Bellman updates, and the actor maximizes reward subject to per-κ cost constraints solved via another primal-dual formulation. The key insight is that overestimating Q_c for OOD pairs achieves the same policy-regularization effect as CQL's underestimation of Q_r, but in a constraint-aware manner that naturally avoids high-cost actions and adapts to varying budgets.

## Results

On the DSRL benchmark (9 tasks across 6 robot types), CCAC achieves the highest average normalized reward (0.91±0.11) among all safe methods while maintaining a normalized cost of 0.39±0.94 (threshold = 1). CCAC is the only method that is simultaneously safe (cost ≤ 1) and achieves high rewards across all 9 tasks. In the zero-shot adaptation experiment (Table 2), where safe trajectories are removed from training data, CCAC still achieves the best average reward (0.85) among safe agents with a cost of 0.25, while all other baselines either violate constraints or become overly conservative.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCAC outperforms all 10 baselines on the joint objective of safety and reward. Among value-based baselines, BCQ-Lag and BEAR-Lag achieve high rewards (avg 0.89–0.90) but are catastrophically unsafe (avg cost 14.4–16.7, far exceeding the threshold of 1); CPQ and COptiDICE are similarly unreliable (avg cost 7.4–14.4). CQL-Saute achieves moderate rewards (0.55) but remains unsafe (avg cost 2.29). VOCE is safe but extremely conservative, achieving only 0.32 average reward. Among sequence-modeling baselines, CDT achieves competitive rewards (0.90 avg) but is unsafe (avg cost 1.84) and cannot guarantee constraint satisfaction; TREBI (avg reward 0.60, cost 3.42) and FISOR (avg reward 0.51, cost 0.27) are both safe or near-safe but sacrifice substantial reward. CCAC uniquely combines the highest average reward (0.91) with a safe average cost (0.39) across all 9 tasks, and is the only method that achieves zero-shot adaptation to varying and unseen cost budgets without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCAC builds on the conditional VAE framework (Kingma & Welling, 2013; Sohn et al., 2015) and PID Lagrangian optimization (Stooke et al., 2020), but departs from prior offline safe RL methods in two key ways. First, it reframes the offline dataset through a constraint-conditioned lens, treating the cost budget κ as a conditioning variable for both the state-action distribution and the OOD definition, rather than using a fixed threshold as in CQL, CPQ, or COptiDICE. Second, it achieves policy regularization by overestimating the cost critic Q_c on OOD pairs (identified via a CVAE-generated distribution and a binary classifier) rather than underestimating the reward critic Q_r as in CQL or IQL, leveraging the dual relationship between Q_r and Q_c (Paternain et al., 2019). This positions CCAC as the first value-based OSRL method to support zero-shot adaptation to varying or unseen constraint thresholds, complementing sequence-modeling approaches like CDT and FISOR that handle varying thresholds but rely on transformer or diffusion architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to deploy a single trained policy across varying safety requirements without retraining has direct practical relevance for safety-critical systems (e.g., autonomous vehicles adjusting to different speed limits). The constraint-conditioned OOD definition—where OOD means exceeding the current cost budget rather than being unseen in the dataset—offers a principled way to incorporate safety into distribution-shift mitigation, which prior methods handle separately. A key limitation is that the CVAE-based data generation may struggle as state-action dimensions grow, and the method is evaluated only on low-dimensional continuous control benchmarks (DSRL). Open questions include whether the approach extends to high-dimensional or discrete action spaces, whether offline-to-online finetuning can further improve performance on sub-optimal datasets, and whether the constraint-conditioned perspective generalizes beyond cost-based constraints to more complex safety specifications.
