## Problem

Existing scaling laws such as Chinchilla predict final pretraining loss as a function of model size and token count but sidestep the learning rate schedule entirely, fitting parameters on a single fixed schedule family (e.g., cosine) and failing to generalize to other schedules or to intermediate training steps. This leaves practitioners without a quantitative tool to predict how different LR decay shapes affect the loss trajectory or to search for better schedules without expensive full training runs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full post-warmup LR schedule E = {η₁,…,η_T} (a T-dimensional vector of learning rates) along with the warmup LR sum S_W, and produces the predicted loss L(t) at every step t from 1 to T. Unlike Chinchilla-style laws that only estimate the final loss, MPL predicts the entire loss curve, enabling both intermediate-step monitoring and schedule optimization. For schedule search, the method additionally takes the fitted parameters Θ = {L₀, A, B, C, α, β, γ} and outputs an optimized T-dimensional LR vector by minimizing the predicted final loss under monotonicity constraints (0 ≤ η_t ≤ η_{t-1}).

## Method

The Multi-Power Law (MPL) decomposes the loss curve into two components via a 'learning rate sum matching' principle: (1) a Chinchilla-like power law L₀ + A·(S₁(t)+S_W)^{-α} that captures the loss of an equivalent constant-LR run with the same cumulative LR sum, and (2) a loss reduction term LD(t) = Σ_k B(η_{k-1}−η_k)·G(η_k^{−γ} S_k(t)) that accounts for the additional loss decrease induced by LR decay, where G(x) = 1−(Cx+1)^{−β} is a saturating power-law function. The LD term is derived bottom-up: the authors first analyze two-stage schedules to identify the power-law shape of loss reduction, then extend to multi-stage schedules to confirm weak dependence on the LR prefix, and finally sum over all incremental LR reductions for general schedules. Only 2–3 short training runs (e.g., a constant and a cosine schedule) are needed to fit all seven parameters, after which the law generalizes to unseen schedules of different shapes, discontinuities, and horizons up to 3× the training length. A theoretical analysis under quadratic loss with power-law Hessian and noise spectra shows the MPL form emerges naturally, and the fitted law can be optimized via Adam over the schedule vector to discover a WSD-like schedule with sqrt-cube decay that outperforms both cosine and grid-searched WSD variants.

## Results

On loss curve prediction across 25M, 100M, and 400M Llama-2 models, MPL achieves R² of 0.9975, 0.9982, and 0.9971 respectively, outperforming the Momentum Law (0.9904, 0.9959, 0.9962) on all model sizes. MPL reduces worst-case prediction error by 15–30% over the Momentum Law (e.g., 0.0040 vs 0.0047 at 25M; 0.0058 vs 0.0094 at 100M). When used to optimize LR schedules, the resulting schedule improves downstream performance over cosine on 5 of 6 benchmarks, with the largest gain on LAMBADA (+2.17%) and C3 (+1.86%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPL outperforms the Momentum Law (MTL) on every metric across all three model sizes, with the largest gap at 100M (WorstE 0.0058 vs 0.0094, a 38% reduction). Against the Chinchilla Data Scaling Law (CDSL), MPL reduces final-loss prediction error to less than 1/3 while requiring only ~1/5 the compute budget, since MPL leverages entire loss curves from just 2–3 training runs rather than final losses from 6+ runs. For schedule optimization, the MPL-induced schedule outperforms the standard cosine schedule by a loss margin exceeding 0.02 and improves downstream accuracy on 5 of 6 benchmarks (LAMBADA +2.17%, HellaSwag +0.62%, ARC-E +0.53%, C3 +1.86%, RTE +1.08%; PIQA drops marginally by 0.06%). The optimized schedule also beats grid-searched WSD and WSDLD variants (Hu et al., 2024) on final pretraining loss, with the key difference being a power-law decay (η ∝ (1−τ)^1.5) to below 1/20 of peak LR rather than linear/exponential decay to 1/10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chinchilla scaling law (Hoffmann et al., 2022) by replacing the step count with the cumulative sum of learning rates and adding a loss-reduction correction term that captures the effect of LR decay. It departs from prior scaling laws, which fit parameters on a fixed schedule family (e.g., cosine only) and predict only the final loss, by formulating a schedule-aware, full-curve prediction problem. The concurrent Momentum Law (Tissue et al., 2024) also incorporates LR into scaling laws but models loss reduction with an exponential kernel; this paper shows a power-law kernel fits better and yields non-collapsed optimal schedules. The theoretical analysis connects to the kernel-regression and quadratic-loss literature (Bordelon, Canatar, Pehlevan, et al.), showing MPL emerges under power-law spectral assumptions on the Hessian and noise covariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Practically, MPL reduces the number of training runs needed for loss prediction from 10+ (Chinchilla-style) to 2–3, and enables gradient-based optimization over the entire LR schedule, producing a schedule that beats both cosine and grid-searched WSD variants. The discovery that the optimal decay follows a power law (exponent ≈1.5, 'sqrt-cube') rather than the linear or exponential decay in WSD offers a simple, actionable refinement (WSDSC) for practitioners. Limitations include slight prediction deviations at long horizons or high peak LRs, and the theoretical analysis is restricted to quadratic losses with specific spectral structures; extending to the river-valley landscape conjectured for LLMs remains open. Broader questions include whether the law generalizes across varying peak LRs, batch sizes, and architectures without refitting.
