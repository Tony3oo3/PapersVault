## Problem

The mechanistic understanding of why LLMs hallucinate or refuse to answer questions about entities they lack knowledge of remains limited. Prior work on factual recall focused on how known facts are retrieved, leaving the decision boundary between hallucination and knowledge refusal unexplored. This paper asks whether models maintain internal linear representations encoding their own knowledge (or lack thereof) about specific entities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252814/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes entity-attribute prompts (e.g., 'When was the player LeBron James born?') and examines the residual stream at the final token of the entity name across all layers of Gemma 2 2B/9B and Llama 3.1 8B. It produces SAE latent activations that separate known from unknown entities, steered residual streams that causally flip refusal to hallucination (or vice versa), attention score maps showing how entity recognition directions modulate attribute extraction heads, and uncertainty latents at the model token that discriminate correct from incorrect answers with 73.2 AUROC.

## Method

The core approach uses pre-trained Sparse Autoencoders (Gemma Scope / LlamaScope with JumpReLU architecture) to decompose residual stream representations into sparse, interpretable features. Entity recognition latents are identified by computing separation scores (fraction of activation on known vs. unknown entities) across four entity types (players, movies, cities, songs), selecting latents with the highest minimum separation score across all types, which concentrate in middle layers. Causal relevance is established through activation steering (adding α·d_j to the residual stream at the entity's final token) and weight orthogonalization (projecting the direction out of all output matrices to remove its influence). Mechanistic analysis via activation patching in a denoising setup reveals that unknown entity latents suppress attention in attribute extraction heads (e.g., L18H5, L20H3), disrupting the factual recall circuit. A separate t-statistic analysis on residual stream activations at the model token identifies uncertainty latents whose top tokens include 'unknown,' 'undisclosed,' and 'TBD,' confirming their association with model uncertainty.

## Results

Steering with the top unknown entity latent in Gemma 2 2B induces nearly 100% knowledge refusal across all entity types (players, movies, cities, songs), while the orthogonalized model (with this direction removed) shows a large reduction in refusal. The discovered 'uncertainty' latent in Gemma 2B IT achieves 73.2 AUROC and F1 of 72 for discriminating correct vs. incorrect answers at the model token. Cross-model validation on 283 song titles released after the knowledge cutoff (August 2024) confirms the latents generalize: unknown entity latents activate at 53–76% frequency while known entity latents activate at only 6–22% across Gemma 2 2B, Gemma 2 9B, and Llama 3.1 8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing hallucination-detection or interpretability methods; instead, it uses random SAE latents and random unit vectors as null baselines for steering experiments. Steering with the top known entity latent produces statistically significant increases in attention to entity tokens compared to 10 random SAE latents in 10/10 (Gemma 2 2B), 10/10 (Gemma 2 9B), and 7/10 (Llama 3.1 8B) cases, while the top unknown entity latent produces significant decreases in 9/10, 1/10, and 10/10 cases respectively. The orthogonalized model (weight projection removing the unknown entity direction) serves as a causal ablation, showing that refusal rates drop substantially when this single direction is eliminated, confirming its necessity. Random latent steering at the same layer and coefficient produces refusal rates close to the unmodified model, ruling out generic activation-magnitude effects. The token likelihood hypothesis is explicitly tested and rejected: correlations between latent activations and next-token prediction probabilities are negligible across all models, and the latents fire on only ~0.5–0.6% of tokens in a broad FineWeb corpus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the factual recall circuit literature (Nanda et al. 2023; Geva et al. 2023; Meng et al. 2022a) by identifying the upstream gating mechanism—entity recognition directions—that determines whether the recall circuit fires or is suppressed, thereby explaining hallucinations and refusals rather than just successful recall. It builds directly on Arditi et al. (2024), who showed safety refusal is mediated by a single direction, by demonstrating that knowledge-specific refusal is similarly steerable via SAE-discovered latents. The methodological backbone is the SAE interpretability toolkit (Bricken et al. 2023; Huben et al. 2024; Lieberum et al. 2024), applied here with activation steering (Turner et al. 2023) and activation patching to establish causal claims about the discovered directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SAEs trained on the base model causally control the chat model's knowledge refusal provides strong evidence that instruction tuning repurposes pre-existing representational mechanisms rather than building new circuits, with implications for how we think about alignment and finetuning. The uncertainty latent achieving 73.2 AUROC for error prediction opens a path toward practical hallucination detection using model internals, though the effect size is moderate. Key limitations include the narrow entity scope (four types from Wikidata), the binary known/unknown classification that may miss partial knowledge, and the fact that the 'knowledge awareness' framing remains correlational in the base model—causal evidence is only established for the chat model's refusal behavior. Open questions include whether these directions generalize to non-factual hallucinations, whether they can be leveraged for reliable hallucination mitigation at deployment, and whether the hierarchical layer organization (specialized early, generalized middle) holds across architectures.
