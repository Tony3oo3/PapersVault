## Problem

Modern document retrieval pipelines depend on brittle multi-step text extraction (OCR, layout detection, chunking, optional captioning) that discards or poorly encodes visual information such as figures, tables, and page layouts. No existing benchmark evaluates retrieval systems on visually rich documents in realistic industrial settings, and no method directly embeds document page images for retrieval without a prior parsing pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw document page image (no OCR or layout parsing required) and a short text query as input. It produces a multi-vector embedding per page (~1024 image-patch vectors plus 6 text-token vectors, each projected to D=128 dimensions, totaling ~257.5 KB per page) and a scalar relevance score computed via the late interaction MaxSim operator between query token embeddings and document patch embeddings.

## Method

ColPali extends PaliGemma-3B (SigLIP vision encoder + Gemma-2B LLM) by adding a linear projection layer that maps every output token embedding—whether originating from an image patch or a text token—into a shared 128-dimensional space. The key insight is that routing image patch embeddings through the LLM aligns them with text-token representations, enabling ColBERT-style late interaction: for each query token, the maximum dot product with all document patch vectors is computed, and these are summed to yield the relevance score. Training uses a pairwise cross-entropy contrastive loss over in-batch hardest negatives, with LoRA (r=32, α=32) applied to the LLM layers and projection head, on 118K query-page pairs. Five <unused0> tokens are appended to queries as a differentiable soft expansion mechanism, and the entire pipeline is end-to-end trainable from raw page images without any intermediate text extraction step.

## Results

ColPali achieves an average nDCG@5 of 81.3 across the 10 ViDoRe retrieval tasks, outperforming the strongest text-based pipeline (Unstructured + Captioning + BGE-M3, 67.0) by 14.3 points and the best contrastive VLM (SigLIP Vanilla, 51.4) by 29.9 points. The gains are most pronounced on visually complex tasks: +32.4 on TAT-DQA (financial tables), +29.5 on Shift (French environmental reports), and +25.0 on AI (scientific documents). ColPali also indexes pages 18× faster than the full Unstructured pipeline (0.39 s vs. 7.22 s per page) while maintaining ~30 ms query latency on a single NVIDIA L4 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ColPali decisively outperforms all five baseline families. Against BM25 (Robertson et al., 1994), the gap is largest on visually rich tasks where text extraction fails entirely (e.g., ArxivQA: 79.1 vs. 31.6 with OCR, 40.1 with captioning). Against BGE-M3 (Chen et al., 2024), ColPali leads by 14.3 nDCG@5 on average (81.3 vs. 67.0 for the best Unstructured+Captioning configuration), with the margin widening to +32.4 on TAT-DQA and +29.5 on Shift. Among contrastive VLMs, SigLIP Vanilla (Zhai et al., 2023) is the strongest at 51.4 average, but ColPali surpasses it by 29.9 points; Jina-CLIP (Koukounas et al., 2024) and Nomic-vision (Nomic, 2024) trail far behind at 17.7 and 12.9 respectively, confirming that single-vector contrastive embeddings are poorly suited for document retrieval. Notably, ColPali also wins on text-centric practical tasks (AI: 96.2, Energy: 91.0, Healthcare: 94.4), showing the advantage is not limited to visually complex documents.

## Relation to Prior Work

ColPali extends ColBERT's late interaction mechanism from text-only retrieval to the vision-language domain, using PaliGemma (SigLIP vision encoder + Gemma-2B language model) as the backbone to produce multi-vector embeddings directly from page images. It departs from the standard industrial document retrieval pipeline (OCR → layout detection → chunking → text embedding) by eliminating all text extraction steps, instead leveraging the VLM's native alignment between image patch tokens and text token embeddings. The ViDoRe benchmark fills a gap between text retrieval benchmarks (MTEB, BEIR) that operate at passage level and vision-language benchmarks (COCO, DUE) that don't target practical document retrieval, introducing page-level evaluation across diverse domains, languages, and visual modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ColPali demonstrates that a single VLM forward pass on a page image can outperform multi-step pipelines (OCR + layout + chunking + captioning + embedding) by ~14 nDCG@5 points while being dramatically simpler and faster to index, potentially reshaping how document retrieval is deployed in RAG and search systems. Key open questions include whether the 257.5 KB per-page storage footprint is acceptable at scale (mitigable via token pooling and quantization), how the approach generalizes to very long documents or non-English corpora beyond zero-shot, and whether combining visual retrieval with visually grounded QA can yield fully end-to-end RAG systems operating purely on image features. The paper also highlights that confidence estimation and abstention mechanisms will be critical for industrial reliability.
