## Problem

Generating human reactions to observed actions in real-time, unconstrained settings (e.g., human-robot interaction) is difficult because no text prompts are available to disambiguate the action's intent, and multi-person motion requires jointly encoding egocentric pose and absolute spatial relationships that existing single-person representations cannot capture. Prior methods either rely on external prompts or directly predict reactions without explicitly reasoning about action semantics, leading to unstable and semantically incoherent outputs.

## Input / Output

The model receives a multi-person action motion sequence (22-joint 3D skeleton with positions, velocities, and rotations at 30 FPS) incrementally at each timestep, with no text prompt or future frames available. It produces a corresponding reaction motion sequence in the same token format, along with intermediate natural-language outputs: an action description and a reasoned reaction description that serve as semantic conditioning. The model reacts in real-time at every timestep and periodically re-thinks (every N_r tokens) to update its inferred prompts, keeping inference under 50 ms per step.

## Method

TTR builds on Flan-T5-base with an extended vocabulary and a decoupled motion tokenizer: a VQ-VAE encodes normalized egocentric pose sequences into discrete pose tokens, while a separate space tokenizer binarizes absolute 2D position and body orientation into discrete space tokens prepended as prefixes. The LLM is first pre-trained non-causally on three task families (motion-to-text, pose-to-space, and motion-to-motion completion) using both Inter-X and HumanML3D data, then fine-tuned causally on two tasks: a thinking task that infers action intent and reasons a reaction description from partial action tokens, and a reacting task that generates reaction tokens conditioned on both the action and the inferred description (using teacher forcing early, then self-generated captions). During inference, the model alternates between reacting at every timestep and re-thinking at a fixed interval (N_r=4 tokens) to correct accumulated errors, achieving an FID of 1.942 versus 3.988 for the prior SOTA ReGenNet.

## Results

On the Inter-X dataset, TTR achieves a FID of 1.942, nearly halving the FID of the strongest baseline ReGenNet (3.988). TTR also leads on all ranking metrics (Top-1: 0.423, Top-2: 0.599, Top-3: 0.693) and accuracy (0.318), as well as multimodal distance (5.643). The only metric where TTR does not lead is diversity (2.629), where ReGenNet (2.502) and InterGen (2.284) are closer to the real-data value of 2.498.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTR outperforms all four baselines on FID, ranking (Top-1/2/3), accuracy, and multimodal distance. Against ReGenNet (the strongest baseline), TTR reduces FID from 3.988 to 1.942 and improves Top-1 from 0.384 to 0.423, but ReGenNet has a slightly better diversity score (2.502 vs. 2.629, closer to the real value of 2.498). Against InterGen, TTR improves FID from 5.506 to 1.942 and Top-1 from 0.326 to 0.423, though InterGen achieves the best diversity (2.284). Against MotionGPT, TTR nearly doubles FID improvement (5.823 → 1.942) and gains substantial ranking gains, with diversity nearly tied (2.629 vs. 2.615). Against InterFormer, TTR dominates across all metrics, with FID dropping from 10.468 to 1.942. The paper attributes TTR's diversity disadvantage to the periodic re-thinking mechanism, which constrains generation toward inferred semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based motion generation paradigm established by MotionGPT (Jiang et al., 2023), which encodes 3D motion into discrete VQ-VAE tokens for autoregressive language modeling, to the multi-person action-to-reaction setting. It departs from ReGenNet (Xu et al., 2024b), the closest prior work on unconstrained online reaction generation, by inserting an explicit semantic reasoning step (inferring action intent and reaction description) between observation and generation, rather than directly mapping action tokens to reaction tokens. The unified space-pose tokenizer builds on InterGen's (Liang et al., 2024) global motion representation but decouples absolute spatial features from egocentric pose to enable a single discrete token vocabulary for both persons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The think-then-react paradigm offers a practical recipe for real-time human-robot interaction where no text prompts are available, and the periodic re-thinking mechanism provides a lightweight error-correction strategy for autoregressive generation. A notable limitation is that single-person motion data (HumanML3D) yields only marginal gains despite the unified tokenizer, as t-SNE analysis reveals minimal pose overlap between single- and multi-person motions — suggesting that scaling multi-person interaction data remains the key bottleneck. Open questions include whether the thinking step generalizes to more than two persons, whether larger LLM backbones further improve semantic reasoning, and how to better bridge the single-to-multi-person motion gap.
