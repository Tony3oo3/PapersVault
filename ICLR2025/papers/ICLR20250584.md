## Problem

Asymptotically stable imitation policies guarantee eventual convergence to a target but ignore transient behavior, causing the robot to deviate significantly from expert trajectories before settling. Contractive dynamical systems provide the stronger guarantee of exponential trajectory convergence, but existing learning methods either impose computationally expensive constraints or sacrifice expressiveness. This paper addresses the gap by learning expressive contractive policies through unconstrained optimization while providing rigorous out-of-sample recovery bounds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only robot state measurements (joint or task-space configuration) without requiring velocity or time-derivative data, distinguishing it from most prior dynamical-system imitation approaches. It produces a continuous-time planned state trajectory via a differentiable ODE rollout over a chosen horizon H, with the initial latent state set through the pseudoinverse of the output map so that the generated trajectory starts at the given initial state. The latent space dimension N_z is decoupled from the state dimension N_y, allowing both lower-dimensional compression for high-dimensional tasks and higher-dimensional embedding for low-dimensional ones.

## Method

SCDS parameterizes the policy as a continuous-time autonomous DS with three components: (i) latent dynamics modeled by a recurrent equilibrium network (REN) that is contractive by construction for any parameter choice, with an adjustable contraction rate γ; (ii) an output transformation consisting of a learnable linear projection followed by K RealNVP-style coupling layers (bijective maps) that provably preserve the contraction property; and (iii) an initial condition computed via the pseudoinverse of the projection applied to the inverse coupling layers. Training solves the IVP differentiably using Neural ODE with batched multiple shooting, minimizes a soft-DTW loss weighted by inverse-squared distance to each demonstration's initial state, and performs unconstrained gradient descent since contractivity holds for all θ. The paper further derives a closed-form upper bound on worst-case OOS loss that decomposes into a precomputable in-sample term and a contraction-dependent uncertainty term, and shows empirically that the coupling layers provide a more efficient route to expressiveness than deepening the REN's implicit layer.

## Results

On out-of-sample (OOS) rollouts, SCDS achieves 0.32±0.15 MSE on LASA-2D, 1.09±0.21 on LASA-4D, 0.89±0.26 on Robomimic-6D, and 2.68±0.65 on Robomimic-14D, reducing the best baseline error by up to a factor of 2.5. On in-sample rollouts, SCDS matches or slightly outperforms baselines (e.g., 0.65±0.05 soft-DTW on LASA-2D vs. 0.72±0.14 for SNDS). The theoretical MSE upper bound (Corollary 4.1.1) is consistently above the observed OOS loss but remains relatively tight (e.g., 0.49±0.07 bound vs. 0.32±0.15 observed on LASA-2D OOS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCDS decisively outperforms all three baselines on OOS recovery across every dataset. Against SNDS (globally stable neural DS), SCDS reduces OOS MSE by 8.5× on LASA-2D (0.32 vs. 2.73) and 2.6× on Robomimic-14D (2.68 vs. 6.88), while also achieving lower soft-DTW in all settings. Against SDS-EF (diffeomorphic stable DS), SCDS cuts OOS MSE by 5.6× on LASA-2D (0.32 vs. 1.78) and 4.1× on Robomimic-14D (2.68 vs. 11.10). Against BC (behavioral cloning, no stability guarantee), the gap is largest: BC's OOS MSE reaches 44.98 on Robomimic-14D versus SCDS's 2.68, and BC trajectories visibly diverge from the target. On in-sample performance, SCDS matches or slightly beats SNDS and SDS-EF on soft-DTW (e.g., 0.65 vs. 0.72 on LASA-2D) and achieves the best MSE on Robomimic-14D (1.68 vs. 1.75 for BC), confirming that the contractive structure does not sacrifice imitation accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two existing architectural ingredients—recurrent equilibrium networks (RENs) for contractive-by-design continuous-time ODEs (Martinelli et al., 2023) and RealNVP-style coupling layers for bijective output transformations (Papamakarios et al., 2021)—into a unified imitation learning policy. It departs from prior contractive imitation methods (Ravichandar et al., 2017; Blocher et al., 2017; Sindhwani et al., 2018) that relied on computationally expensive constrained optimization, and from the recent unconstrained neural contractive DS of Mohammadi et al. (2024) which required second-order ODEs and was limited to lower-dimensional latent spaces. The key conceptual departure is the state-only formulation (no expert velocity needed) combined with a latent space that can be either higher- or lower-dimensional than the observation space, plus a weighted trajectory-space loss that avoids the counterintuitive nonzero-loss-at-perfect-match problem of uniform weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practically deployable framework for robotic imitation with a stronger reliability certificate (contractivity) than the asymptotic stability used by most prior stable-DS methods, guaranteeing bounded transient deviation from expert trajectories rather than merely eventual convergence. The theoretical upper bound on OOS loss (Theorem 4.1) gives practitioners a concrete, computable safety margin at deployment time. Limitations include the computational cost of continuous ODE integration over long horizons, the trade-off between contraction rate and expressiveness, and the inability to compare against the most recent unconstrained contractive baseline (Mohammadi et al., 2024) due to lack of released code. Open questions include extending to long-horizon and stochastic policies, and whether the coupling-layer expressiveness trick generalizes beyond the tested 2D–14D state spaces.
