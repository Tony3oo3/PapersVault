## Problem

Diffusion model inversion suffers from faithfulness and editability challenges due to nonlinear drift and diffusion, and existing remedies require costly latent optimization, prompt tuning, or complex attention processors. Rectified flow models (e.g., Flux) offer a promising alternative with straighter sample paths, yet their inversion and editing capabilities remain largely unexplored, particularly given the architectural differences (MM-DiT vs. UNet) that prevent direct transfer of prior DM inversion methods.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image (a clean photo, a stroke painting, or any corrupted image) and a text prompt specifying desired edits, and produces an edited or generated RGB image. During inversion, the Flux model is queried with a null prompt to compute the unconditional vector field, while the conditional vector field is computed analytically from the LQR solution. The method introduces a single tunable hyper-parameter (controller guidance η_t with a time-varying schedule defined by starting time, stopping time, and strength) on top of the standard Flux pipeline (28 inference steps, classifier-free guidance 3.5), requiring no additional training, latent optimization, or attention manipulation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a pair of controlled ODEs that interpolate between two competing vector fields. The forward (inversion) ODE (8) blends the unconditional RF vector field u_t(Y_t) — which maps a clean image to typical noise but produces atypical noise for corrupted inputs — with a conditional vector field u_t(Y_t|y₁) = (y₁ − Y_t)/(1−t) derived as the optimal controller of a Linear Quadratic Regulator problem, using a guidance parameter γ ∈ [0,1]. The reverse (generation) ODE (15) similarly blends the unconditional RF vector field v_t(X_t) with a conditional field v_t(X_t|y₀) = (y₀ − X_t)/(1−t) that steers the flow toward the reference image, controlled by η ∈ [0,1]. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) proves that the controlled forward ODE is density-equivalent to a rectified SDE whose diffusion coefficient scales with (1−γ), providing a stochastic interpretation that explains the faithfulness–typicality trade-off. The reverse SDE (Theorem 3.5) yields a practical stochastic sampler for Flux by expressing the score function ∇log p_t explicitly in terms of the learned vector field via Tweedie's formula, eliminating the need for a separate score network.

## Results

On LSUN-Bedroom stroke-to-image generation, the method achieves L2=82.65 and KID=0.025, outperforming the best optimization-free baseline SDEdit-SD1.5 (L2=86.72, KID=0.029) by 4.7% in faithfulness and 13.8% in realism, and surpassing NTI+P2P (KID=0.234) by 89% in realism. On SFHQ face editing ('wearing glasses'), the method achieves CLIP-I=0.900 and DINO=0.951, outperforming optimization-free baselines by 26.4% and 6.6% respectively, while running 54% faster than NTI+P2P (39s vs 85s). A large-scale user study (9,000 comparisons, 126 participants) confirms the method is preferred over all baselines by at least 59.67%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDEdit (the strongest optimization-free baseline), the method wins on both faithfulness (L2: 82.65 vs 86.72 on Bedroom) and realism (KID: 0.025 vs 0.029), with a 13.8% KID improvement. Against NTI (optimization-based), the method achieves comparable L2 (82.65 vs 82.77) but is 73% more realistic (KID: 0.025 vs 0.095) without requiring per-step optimization. Against NTI+P2P, the method is 89% more realistic (KID: 0.025 vs 0.234) though NTI+P2P achieves much lower L2 (46.46) by over-fitting to the corrupted stroke input at the cost of photorealism. Against DDIM inversion, the method is 77.9% more realistic (KID: 0.025 vs 0.113) and 5.9% more faithful. On SFHQ face editing, the method matches NTI+P2P on face reconstruction (0.442 vs 0.443) and DINO (0.951 vs 0.953) while achieving 6.7% higher CLIP-I (0.900 vs 0.845) and running 54% faster (39s vs 85s); it outperforms all optimization-free baselines (SDEdit, DDIM) on DINO and CLIP-I but has higher face reconstruction error than NTI (0.442 vs 0.707), reflecting a faithfulness–editability trade-off controlled by the η parameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image inversion and editing literature (SDEdit, DDIM inversion, NTI, P2P) from diffusion models to rectified flow models like Flux and SD3, which use fundamentally different architectures (MM-DiT with T5 conditioning vs. UNet with CLIP cross-attention). The key conceptual departure is formulating inversion as a Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) optimal control problem, yielding a controlled ODE that interpolates between the unconditional RF vector field and a conditional vector field, and proving this is equivalent to a rectified SDE. This draws on the Fokker-Planck/continuity equation duality between ODEs and SDEs, a connection previously exploited in stochastic interpolant frameworks (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023) but not previously applied to controlled inversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As rectified flow models (Flux, SD3) become the dominant architecture for text-to-image generation, the absence of an efficient inversion method was a practical gap; this paper fills it with a training-free, optimization-free approach that requires only a single tunable parameter (controller guidance η). The counterfactual sampling interpretation (noise abduction à la Pearl et al., 2016) opens a conceptual bridge between generative model inversion and causal inference, suggesting applications beyond image editing. Open questions include whether the SDE formulation provides practical sampling benefits over the ODE in real Flux pipelines, whether the approach extends to other flow-based architectures (e.g., video generation), and how the method behaves with more complex multi-step editing sequences.
