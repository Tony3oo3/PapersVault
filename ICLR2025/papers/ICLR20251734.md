## Problem

Every time a base LLM is updated (e.g., Llama3 → Llama3.1), the entire post-training pipeline—multi-round SFT, DPO, and RL—must be redone from scratch, incurring substantial data, compute, and engineering costs. No existing method allows the post-trained capabilities of an older model to be transferred to a new base model without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251734/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires three homologous checkpoints (same architecture, layer count, tokenizer): the original base model Θ_base, the post-trained model Θ_post derived from it, and the newly updated base model Θ_base'. It produces a single model checkpoint Θ_ParamΔ = Θ_base' + (Θ_post − Θ_base) that approximates a fully post-trained model. No training data, reward models, or gradient steps are needed at inference time; the operation is a single element-wise addition of weight tensors.

## Method

Param Δ computes the parameter difference ΔΘ = Θ_post − Θ_base between an existing post-trained checkpoint and its base, hypothesizing that this delta encodes the data-specific knowledge and capabilities acquired during post-training (SFT, DPO, RL). The delta is then added directly to an updated base model of identical architecture, yielding a post-trained model at zero training cost. The paper validates the approach through orthogonality analysis showing that parameter deltas from different post-training regimes occupy near-orthogonal subspaces, and through a quantitative transfer-efficiency study (γ ≈ 0.98) confirming near-lossless knowledge transfer. Four application scenarios are demonstrated: general-purpose post-training transfer, task-specific domain adaptation, continual-pretraining augmentation, and multi-source delta combination with scaling factors α and β. Unlike prior model-merging work (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging) that targets simple task finetunings, Param Δ transfers the full multi-stage post-training signal using the base checkpoint as an explicit anchoring point.

## Results

The headline result is that the 70B Param Δ model (Llama3.1-base + ΔΘ from Llama3-inst) attains approximately 95% of Llama3.1-inst's average performance across 12 benchmarks with zero additional training. For the 8B model, Param Δ achieves MMLU 68.6 (vs. 69.4 reference), IFEval 72.3 (surpassing the 71.5 reference), and HumanEval 64.6 (vs. 69.5). For the 70B model, Param Δ reaches MMLU 81.7 (vs. 83.4), IFEval 83.9 (vs. 87.7), and GSM8K 92.3 (vs. 95.4). The quantitative transfer analysis (Section 5.1) shows a regression coefficient γ ≈ 0.98 between hypothetical and actual Param Δ performance, indicating near-lossless knowledge transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Llama3.1-inst reference (Dubey et al., 2024), the 70B Param Δ model closes most of the gap: it matches or exceeds the reference on IFEval (83.9 vs. 87.7 is close), ARC Challenge (94.3 vs. 94.7), and BFCL (77.7 vs. 77.9), while trailing by 1.7–16.5 points on MMLU, MATH, and API-Bank. The 8B Param Δ model actually surpasses Llama3.1-inst on IFEval (72.3 vs. 71.5) and MBPP (73.3 vs. 70.4), attributed to the stronger Llama3.1 base. Compared to the original Llama3-inst, Param Δ consistently wins because it inherits the upgraded Llama3.1 base (e.g., 70B MMLU: 81.7 vs. 82.0 Llama3-inst, but 81.7 vs. 83.4 Llama3.1-inst). For the medical domain (Scenario 2), Med-Param Δ matches Bio-Medical-Llama (Con, 2024) on medical benchmarks (e.g., Medical Genetics 96.0 vs. 95.0) while substantially outperforming it on general tasks (IFEval 75.4 vs. 52.3, MMLU 67.7 vs. 66.9), demonstrating that domain knowledge transfers intact while the upgraded base recovers general capabilities lost during domain fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model merging and weight-averaging literature (Task Arithmetic, Model Soups, TIES-Merging, DARE) by using the pretrained base checkpoint as an explicit anchor for parameter-delta operations, rather than directly combining two post-trained checkpoints. While Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022) demonstrated that simple task finetunings could be transferred via weight arithmetic, this paper generalizes the approach to replicate the entire multi-stage post-training pipeline (multi-round SFT, DPO, and RL) in a single weight-addition step. The orthogonality analysis of parameter deltas across different post-training datasets connects to the broader parameter-space geometry literature (Nagarajan & Kolter, 2019; Plappert et al., 2017), providing empirical grounding for why the transfer works.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For the open-weight community where base models are updated nearly monthly, this zero-cost method eliminates the need to re-run expensive post-training (data curation, multi-round SFT, DPO/RL) after each base model release, potentially saving weeks of GPU time and thousands of dollars per model update. The main limitation is the requirement for identical architecture and tokenizer, which restricts cross-family transfer; additionally, the performance gap widens on harder reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MATH drops from 49.7 to 28.9 for 8B), suggesting that the most complex post-training signals are least transferable. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to fundamentally different post-training paradigms (e.g., long-chain-of-thought RL as in DeepSeek-R1), whether iterative delta accumulation degrades over multiple base-model updates, and whether the orthogonality property holds at larger scales or with more diverse training data.
