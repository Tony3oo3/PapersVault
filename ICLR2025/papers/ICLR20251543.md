## Problem

Existing information-theoretic generalization bounds for SGD fail to capture the well-known flatness bias: when flatness improves (e.g., by decreasing batch size), actual generalization error decreases, yet bounds like Wang & Mao (2022) increase, misaligning with true generalization. This stems from the trajectory term relying on gradient variance as an implicit proxy for flatness rather than explicitly using Hessians. Additionally, the non-isotropic bound of Neu et al. (2021) is suboptimal because its covariance optimization is shared across all output-weight instances, and is prone to severe overfitting when estimated from the limited number of trained models available in practice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full SGD training trajectory (initial and terminal weights, per-step gradients), the training set S, and empirical and population Hessians at the terminal weight to compute the omniscient perturbation ΔG and evaluate the bound. In experiments, 6 independently trained ResNet-18 models per hyperparameter setting are required to estimate the output weight covariance, and population statistics (Hessians, gradients) are approximated via second-order Taylor expansion on a held-out validation set. The output is a scalar generalization error upper bound decomposed into a trajectory term, a penalty term for the omniscient trajectory, and a flatness term for the SGLD-like trajectory, all explicitly dependent on Hessian structure.

## Method

The core technique is the 'omniscient trajectory,' which constructs an auxiliary perturbation ΔG as a deterministic function of all random variables in the training process (training data, internal randomness, all gradients, and all weights), rather than a fixed covariance shared across instances. This implements the principle that moving optimization inside the expectation (E_W[inf_Σ f(W;Σ)] vs. inf_Σ E_W[f(W;Σ)]) makes the bound instance-specific, tighter, and free of estimation overfitting. Concretely, the original SGD trajectory is first perturbed by ΔG to form the omniscient trajectory, then an SGLD-like trajectory with isotropic Gaussian noise is built on top for mutual information bounding. The optimization of ΔG reduces to a convex problem whose closed-form solution involves the empirical and population Hessians at the terminal weight, yielding a bound where the deviation norm is weighted by Hessian-induced geometry: components along flat directions contribute little while sharp directions are penalized. The resulting bound shows generalization improves when the output weight covariance aligns with flatness—variance is low along sharp directions and high along flat directions—providing a more explicit and numerically tighter characterization than prior IT bounds.

## Results

On ResNet-18 trained on CIFAR-10, the proposed omniscient-trajectory bound (Theorem 2, λ=10⁹) correctly captures the trend of actual generalization error under varied batch size and learning rate, whereas Wang & Mao (2022)'s bound increases as batch size decreases while the true error decreases. The bound is numerically much tighter, being only a few percentages looser than the true generalization gap across hyperparameters (training data usage, label noise, width, depth, weight decay). For Gradient Descent on convex-Lipschitz-Bounded (CLB) problems, the omniscient trajectory technique improves representative information-theoretic bounds from an Ω(1) lower-bound rate to an O(1/√n) minimax excess risk rate, recovering the Bassily et al. (2020) stability-based bound up to a constant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: Wang & Mao (2022) (Proposition 1) and Neu et al. (2021) Proposition 2 (isotropic version). Against Wang & Mao (2022), the key finding is qualitative: their trajectory term (based on gradient variance) misaligns with true generalization under varied flatness (Figure 1a shows the bound increasing as batch size decreases while actual error decreases), while the proposed bound's penalty term (based on Hessians) correctly tracks the generalization trend (Figure 3). Against Neu et al. (2021) Proposition 2, the paper's bound is numerically tighter across all varied hyperparameters in Figure 4 (training data usage, label noise, width, depth, weight decay), with the main improvement coming from the trajectory term after the omniscient perturbation leverages flatness. The paper also notes that some existing bounds fail to capture improved generalization under stronger weight decay, while the proposed bound with large λ does. The O(1/√n) rate for GD on CLB problems (Theorem 3) directly addresses the Ω(1) limitation identified by Haghifam et al. (2023) for PAC-Bayesian, (C)MI, and Gaussian-perturbed bounds, providing a simpler alternative to Wang & Mao (2024a)'s stability-augmented CMI approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the auxiliary trajectory framework for information-theoretic generalization bounds of SGD, building directly on Neu et al. (2021) and Wang & Mao (2022), who introduced SGLD-like perturbed trajectories to bound mutual information. The key departure is the 'omniscient trajectory,' which makes the auxiliary perturbation a function of all random variables in the training process (training data, gradients, weights) rather than an independent Gaussian noise, effectively moving the covariance optimization inside the expectation over output weights. This is motivated by the observation that existing bounds' trajectory terms rely on gradient variance—an implicit flatness measure—rather than Hessians, causing misalignment with actual generalization under varied flatness. The paper also connects to the flatness literature (Jastrzebski et al., Keskar et al.) and addresses the Ω(1) lower-bound limitation for IT bounds on convex-Lipschitz-Bounded problems identified by Haghifam et al. (2023) and Attias et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The omniscient trajectory technique provides a principled way to make IT bounds explicitly leverage flatness, yielding bounds that correctly track generalization under varied batch size and learning rate—something prior bounds fail to do. The O(1/√n) minimax rate for GD on CLB problems is a significant theoretical advance, addressing a recently highlighted fundamental limitation and implying a bypass of the memorization-generalization trade-off. However, the bound's reliance on population gradients and Hessians prevents it from being self-certifying, and the information-theoretic components largely vanish in both the CLB analysis and experiments, suggesting the technique's practical utility may be narrower than its theoretical elegance implies. Open questions include whether the population-statistics dependence can be removed and whether the alignment insight generalizes beyond the tested ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 setting.
