## Problem

Existing diffusion-based time series generation models applied to fMRI treat each brain region independently and ignore the scale-free, multifractal dynamics that characterize real brain signals. This neglect of inter-regional functional connectivity and fractal temporal structure causes generated fMRI to lack physiological realism, limiting their utility for reducing acquisition time or augmenting clinical and neuroscience datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252033/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a retrospective fMRI window of L=64 timesteps across N=82 ROIs (parcellated via AAL atlas from HCP data with T=1200 total volumes) and a precomputed Pearson-correlation functional connectivity matrix as input. It produces generated fMRI time series for a future window of 32, 64, or 96 timesteps across all ROIs, with the generation operating hierarchically across R=5 spatial aggregation levels derived from k-hop neighborhood expansions of the connectivity graph.

## Method

PDH-Diffusion integrates three modules: (1) a Hypergraph-based Hierarchical Signals Generator that builds multi-scale hypergraphs from the rsFC matrix using k-hop neighbor grouping and propagates messages via two-stage vertex-hyperedge updates to produce fMRI signals aggregated at R different spatial ranges; (2) a Dynamics Properties Guiding Module that predicts the generalized Hurst exponent and multifractal spectrum of future signals from an RNN historical embedding, trained with MSE and KL-divergence losses, then projects these into a conditioning vector via an MLP; (3) a Cross-brain Region Guiding Progressive Diffusion Model that generates signals from coarsest to finest spatial scale, where each level's broader regional signal conditions the next finer level through a shared-variance-schedule diffusion process. The final training objective combines weighted diffusion noise-prediction losses across all R levels with the fractal prediction loss, and the denoiser receives a unified condition vector combining historical, fractal, and cross-region components.

## Results

On the HCP dataset (86 ROIs, context length 64), PDH-Diffusion achieves the best MAE, RMSE, and MAPE across all three prediction horizons. At T_pred=32, it reaches MAE 28.56, RMSE 38.70, and MAPE 0.2744%; at T_pred=96, MAE 29.73, RMSE 39.25, and MAPE 0.2803%. The advantage over the strongest non-diffusion baseline (U-Mixer) is modest (≈0.8 MAE at T_pred=32, narrowing to ≈0.1 at T_pred=96), while the gap over diffusion baselines (TimeGrad, SSSD) is substantial (≈5.9–7.9 MAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDH-Diffusion outperforms all seven baselines on every metric (MAE, RMSE, MAPE) across all three prediction lengths. Among the five time-series forecasting baselines, U-Mixer is the closest competitor, with PDH-Diffusion leading by 0.80 MAE at T_pred=32 (28.56 vs. 29.36) but only 0.10 MAE at T_pred=96 (29.73 vs. 29.83), suggesting the advantage narrows for longer horizons. Flowformer is the second-best non-diffusion method, trailing PDH-Diffusion by roughly 1.2–2.0 MAE. The two diffusion-based baselines—TimeGrad and SSSD—perform substantially worse, with MAE values 5.9–7.9 higher than PDH-Diffusion, indicating that generic diffusion architectures without brain-specific physiological priors are poorly suited to fMRI generation. iTransformer and MSGNet occupy the middle range, while TimesNet is the weakest non-diffusion baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based time series generation (TimeGrad, SSSD) by injecting two brain-specific physiological priors into the denoising process: hierarchical regional interactions modeled via hypergraphs built on resting-state functional connectivity (drawing on HGNN+ message propagation), and multifractal dynamics (generalized Hurst exponent and multifractal spectrum via MFDFA) used as conditioning signals. The progressive multi-scale generation with share-ratio variance schedules adapts the multi-granularity diffusion idea from MG-TSD (Fan et al., 2024) to the brain-specific setting where broader regional trends naturally condition finer local signals. Unlike prior task-agnostic diffusion forecasters that treat each ROI independently, PDH-Diffusion explicitly encodes inter-ROI dependencies and scale-invariant fractal structure as first-class constraints on the generative process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If generated fMRI signals can faithfully reproduce physiological dynamics, this could substantially reduce the 60-minute HCP-style acquisition burden and mitigate motion-artifact issues in vulnerable populations (infants, elderly), with downstream benefits for clinical diagnostics and training data augmentation for neuroscience ML. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (HCP, 86 ROIs) and a single forecasting horizon set, and no clinical validation or downstream diagnostic task is demonstrated. Open questions include whether the multifractal conditioning generalizes across different parcellation schemes, task states, or pathologies, and whether the generated signals preserve the subtle connectivity patterns needed for disease detection rather than merely matching aggregate statistics.
