## Problem

Existing dense Neural SLAM methods (NeRF-based and Gaussian Splatting-based) are trained and evaluated almost exclusively on noise-free synthetic data, yet real-world deployment involves sensor noise, rapid motion, depth missing, and multi-sensor desynchronization that cause sharp performance degradation. No comprehensive benchmark systematically exposes these vulnerabilities, and no prior method can produce noise-free photorealistic 3D reconstruction from noisy, fast-motion video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251641/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CorrGS consumes noisy RGB-D video frames (1200×680) at 10× the standard Replica trajectory speed for sparse-view evaluation, and real-world sequences from a RealSense D435i with dynamic illumination and depth noise. It outputs an ego-motion trajectory (evaluated via ATE), a 3D Gaussian Splatting map maintained to be noise-free, and photorealistic RGB-D renderings. The Robust-Ego3D benchmark provides 1,000 synthesized videos across 124 customizable perturbation settings (16 RGB corruptions, 4 depth perturbations, motion deviations, and RGB-D desynchronization) with precise 3D mesh and trajectory ground truth.

## Method

CorrGS builds on SplaTAM's Gaussian Splatting SLAM framework but introduces two correspondence-guided mechanisms. First, Correspondence-guided Pose Learning (CPL) uses LoFTR to extract 2D visual matches between rendered and observed RGB frames, lifts them to 3D via depth, and solves a soft-L1 relative pose optimization to produce a high-quality pose initialization before differentiable rendering-based refinement; a Pose Quality Verification step rejects initializations that worsen rendering loss. Second, Correspondence-guided Appearance Restoration Learning (CARL) trains a lightweight linear restoration model online (Adam, 100 iterations, lr=0.2) to map noisy observed colors to clean colors rendered from the maintained noise-free map, then applies the restored image to both tracking and mapping for a second round of CPL. Together, CPL reduces gradient magnitude and improves Hessian conditioning for stable pose optimization under fast motion, while CARL denoises color inputs to enable noise-free 3D reconstruction, adding only ~0.1 s overhead per frame pair.

## Results

On sparse-view video (10× faster than Replica), CorrGS-L achieves an average ATE of 0.45 cm, outperforming SplaTAM-L (10.9 cm), Hero-SLAM-L (14.08 cm), and CO-SLAM-L (25.08 cm) by 24×, 31×, and 56× respectively. Under noisy illumination (partial brightness reduction), the full CorrGS (CARL-T&M) recovers tracking on all 8/8 sequences with 0.54 cm ATE and 35.48 dB restored RGB PSNR, compared to baseline failure on 7/8 sequences. On the Robust-Ego3D benchmark (124 perturbation settings, 1,000 videos), no single model demonstrates consistent robustness: CO-SLAM is most resilient to RGB imaging perturbations (0.0104 m mean ATE, static), while GO-SLAM is most vulnerable to environmental effects (0.627 m under Fog).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CorrGS-L decisively outperforms all neural SLAM baselines on sparse-view video: it achieves 0.45 cm average ATE versus SplaTAM-L (10.9 cm, 24× worse), Hero-SLAM-L (14.08 cm, 31× worse), and CO-SLAM-L (25.08 cm, 56× worse). GO-SLAM, which uses loop closure and global optimization, is the most robust to fast motion among baselines but still fails on sequence O-2 and achieves only 0.43–20.02 cm ATE across sequences. On the Robust-Ego3D RGB perturbation benchmark, CO-SLAM is the most robust to imaging corruptions (0.0104 m mean ATE, static mode), while GO-SLAM is highly vulnerable to environmental effects like Fog (0.627 m) and Snow (0.192 m). SplaTAM, the direct architectural baseline that CorrGS extends, degrades severely under fast motion (262.81 cm avg ATE without extra iterations) and depth noise, whereas CorrGS's correspondence-guided pose learning (CPL) and appearance restoration (CARL) address both failure modes. ORB-SLAM3, included as a non-neural reference, is more resilient to rapid motion than pure neural methods but produces only sparse maps, motivating CorrGS's hybrid correspondence + Gaussian Splatting design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dense Neural SLAM line (iMAP, Nice-SLAM, CO-SLAM, GO-SLAM, SplaTAM) by systematically studying and addressing robustness under realistic sensor noise, a dimension prior work largely ignored. CorrGS specifically builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) and its SLAM adaptation (SplaTAM, Keetha et al., 2024), but departs from the pure differentiable-optimization paradigm by injecting correspondence-guided pose initialization (inspired by classical feature-matching SLAM like ORB-SLAM3) and an online appearance restoration module. The Robust-Ego3D benchmark extends the robustness-evaluation tradition from image classification (Hendrycks & Dietterich, 2019) and 2D/3D detection (Michaelis et al., 2019) into the temporal, multi-sensor domain of RGB-D SLAM, covering motion, imaging, depth, and synchronization perturbations simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that no existing dense Neural SLAM model is robust across all perturbation categories, and that a seemingly beneficial input (depth) can actively degrade performance under depth noise—a finding that challenges the default assumption of always fusing all sensor modalities. The fragility of the linear-motion assumption in differentiable pose optimization (SplaTAM, CO-SLAM) is a practical warning for deploying these methods on dynamic platforms. Limitations include evaluation restricted to indoor Replica scenes, CARL tested only on illumination changes, and sparse-view settings; open questions include adaptive perturbation selection, outdoor/cross-domain generalization, and extending restoration to depth channels. The 124-setting, 1,000-video Robust-Ego3D benchmark is likely to become a standard stress-test for future SLAM methods.
