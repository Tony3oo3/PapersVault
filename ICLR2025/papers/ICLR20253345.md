## Problem

Existing hierarchical language-conditioned skill learning methods (LISA, LCSD, SkillDiffuser) train the high-level and low-level policies jointly in an end-to-end manner, entangling the learning of the latent plan space with the language-conditioned policy. This coupling causes training instability and index collapse in the discrete codebook, requiring additional stabilization techniques. The paper asks whether decoupling these two learning processes can yield a more robust and interpretable skill abstraction framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural language instruction (encoded by a frozen DistilBERT), a history trajectory of state-action pairs, and the current observation (15-dim state or 64×64 RGB image in LOReL; 30-dim state or 224×224 RGB image in Kitchen). It produces a discrete latent plan index (from a codebook of 20 vectors of dimension 16) every H timesteps via the high-level policy, which the low-level policy then uses to generate H consecutive actions. The trajectory encoder separately maps each H-step trajectory segment to a codebook entry, and the full sequence of latent plans over a trajectory is aligned to the instruction via a CLIP-based contrastive loss.

## Method

LADS introduces a trajectory encoder that, together with the low-level policy, forms a VQ-VAE to learn a discrete latent plan space in a variational manner, decoupled from the high-level policy. The high-level policy (a causal transformer) is modeled as a categorical distribution over the codebook indices rather than a regressive latent vector, trained with cross-entropy to capture the dataset's multi-modality (e.g., multiple valid sub-task orderings for a composite instruction). The KL divergence between the trajectory encoder and the high-level policy is split via stop-gradient into two independent objectives: a cross-entropy loss that trains the high-level policy, and a semantic regularization loss that aligns the full sequence of latent plans with the CLIP text embedding of the instruction using an InfoNCE contrastive objective. This decoupling prevents the high-level policy from destabilizing the codebook during training, and the semantic regularization ensures the discrete latent space encodes task-relevant information rather than overfitting to sub-optimal trajectory patterns.

## Results

On LOReL (state), LADS achieves 68.3% success rate on atomic seen instructions, 56.9% on atomic rephrased, and 30.0% on composite instructions, outperforming all baselines. On LOReL (image), LADS reaches 52.5% (atomic seen), 36.2% (atomic rephrased), and 24.0% (composite). On Kitchen (state), LADS completes 2.25/4 sub-tasks on seen and 2.23/4 on unseen instructions, surpassing the next-best VAE baseline (1.93 and 1.79 respectively). On Kitchen (image), LADS achieves 1.61/4 (seen) and 1.44/4 (unseen), again leading all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LADS outperforms all four baselines across both environments and all instruction categories. Against LISA (Garg et al., 2022), LADS gains +29.7 points on LOReL (state) atomic seen (68.3 vs. 38.6) and +16.6 on composite (30.0 vs. 13.4), demonstrating that decoupling the high-level and low-level policy training resolves the index collapse problem that plagues LISA's end-to-end approach. Against Lang DT (the non-hierarchical Decision Transformer baseline from the same paper), LADS improves by +40.5 points on LOReL (state) atomic seen and +17.5 on composite, confirming the benefit of hierarchical skill decomposition. Against LCSD (Ju et al., 2024), LADS leads by +8.1 points on LOReL (state) atomic seen (68.3 vs. 60.2) and by a wide margin on composite instructions (30.0 vs. not reported, as LCSD does not support composition). Against SkillDiffuser (Liang et al., 2024), LADS achieves +33.8 points on LOReL (state) atomic seen (68.3 vs. 18.7) and +18.2 on composite (30.0 vs. 11.8), showing that VQ-VAE-based discrete skill abstractions with semantic regularization substantially outperform diffusion-based low-level policies for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

LADS builds directly on LISA (Garg et al., 2022), which introduced end-to-end hierarchical skill learning with VQ-VAE discretization from language-annotated offline data, and on task-agnostic variational skill learning (Pertsch et al., 2021; Rosete-Beas et al., 2023). The key departure is decoupling the latent plan space learning (via a trajectory encoder trained with VQ-VAE) from the language-conditioned high-level policy, which prior methods like LISA, LCSD, and SkillDiffuser train jointly and which causes index collapse. Additionally, LADS introduces a categorical prediction head over the codebook (rather than regressing a latent vector) and a CLIP-based contrastive alignment loss to impose semantic structure on the discrete codes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling strategy addresses a practical and well-documented training instability in hierarchical skill learning, and the ablation showing that removing semantic regularization collapses performance (52.5% → 6.2% success rate) underscores that discrete latent spaces without language grounding are essentially uninformative. However, the method is validated only on two small simulated environments (LOReL, Kitchen) with a fixed skill horizon H that requires manual tuning, and the paper itself identifies learning variable-length skills as an open challenge. Broader impact would require validation on real robotic platforms and more diverse task suites.
