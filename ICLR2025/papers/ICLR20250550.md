## Problem

Standard anomaly detection methods treat any deviation from training data as anomalous, but in practice users often need to focus on specific attributes (e.g., object shape) while ignoring nuisance attributes (e.g., color, background). Existing approaches either require extensive data augmentation, labeled nuisance attributes, or express the entire normality boundary in language prompts, making it difficult to capture complex visual normality while selectively controlling which attributes matter.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250550/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (normal reference set and test set), a list of text prompts containing concept values for the target attribute (e.g., 'a photo of a person with brown hair'), and a single hyperparameter d (number of PCA components, typically 8–32 for guiding, 32–384 for ignoring). It outputs a per-image anomaly score computed as the mean cosine similarity to the k nearest normal features in the transformed space, which can be thresholded into a binary normal/anomalous label. For the industrial variant (WinCLIP+LAFT), it additionally produces pixel-level anomaly localization maps. No additional training data, labels, or fine-tuning are required beyond the pre-trained CLIP model.

## Method

LAFT constructs a 'concept subspace' in CLIP's shared image-text embedding space by encoding text prompts containing attribute values, computing pairwise differences between the resulting text embeddings, and applying PCA to extract d robust concept axes that capture the direction of the target attribute. Image features are then transformed via projection onto this subspace (to guide/focus on the attribute) or via orthogonal projection away from it (to ignore the attribute), effectively isolating or removing the attribute's contribution from the feature vector. For anomaly scoring, LAFT AD applies kNN (k=30) on the transformed features using cosine similarity, while WinCLIP+LAFT integrates the same projection into WinCLIP's multi-scale window, image, and text embeddings for industrial anomaly detection. The entire pipeline is training-free, relying solely on CLIP's pre-trained encoders, and the only tunable hyperparameter is the number of PCA components d.

## Results

On Colored MNIST (Number attribute), LAFT AD achieves 98.5% AUROC, 98.4% AUPRC, and 6.9% FPR95 in the guide setting, substantially outperforming the best language-only baseline ZOE (91.2% AUROC) and the best image+language baseline InCTRL (94.0% AUROC). On Waterbirds (Bird attribute), LAFT AD reaches 95.6% AUROC and 20.6% FPR95, surpassing ZOE (92.2% AUROC) and WinCLIP+ (91.8% AUROC). On CelebA, LAFT AD achieves 98.1% AUROC for Eyeglasses detection, far exceeding all baselines (best: InCTRL at 87.8%). For industrial anomaly detection on MVTec AD (K=8), WinCLIP+LAFT-C achieves 96.5% AUROC, outperforming WinCLIP (95.7%) and InCTRL (93.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAFT AD consistently outperforms all five baselines across both semantic anomaly detection datasets. Against language-only methods, LAFT AD surpasses ZOE by 7.3 AUROC points on Colored MNIST (98.5 vs. 91.2) and by 3.4 points on Waterbirds (95.6 vs. 92.2); MCM and CLIPN perform substantially worse (62.9–73.2 AUROC on MNIST), confirming that image-text similarity scoring alone is insufficient. Against image+language methods, LAFT AD beats WinCLIP+ by 5.9 AUROC on MNIST (98.5 vs. 92.6) and by 3.8 on Waterbirds (95.6 vs. 91.8), and outperforms InCTRL by 4.5 AUROC on MNIST (98.5 vs. 94.0) and by 12.0 on Waterbirds (95.6 vs. 83.6). On CelebA Eyeglasses, LAFT AD (98.1 AUROC) dramatically exceeds all baselines, with the closest competitor InCTRL at only 87.8. For industrial anomaly detection, WinCLIP+LAFT-C improves over WinCLIP by 0.8 AUROC on MVTec AD at K=8 (96.5 vs. 95.7) and by 5.1 at K=0 (80.6 vs. 75.5 on VisA), while matching or slightly exceeding InCTRL in most few-shot settings without requiring dataset-specific adapter pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAFT builds directly on CLIP's shared image-text embedding space and the word-embedding arithmetic principle (Mikolov, 2013), repurposing pairwise text-encoder differences as 'concept axes' for projecting or removing visual feature components. It departs from prior CLIP-based anomaly detection methods (MCM, ZOE, CLIPN, InCTRL) that score anomalies via image-text similarity, instead using image-image similarity (kNN) on transformed features with language only refining the normality boundary. For industrial anomaly detection, it extends WinCLIP by applying the same projection to multi-scale window and image embeddings, avoiding the adapter-layer pre-training required by InCTRL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, prompt-driven control over which image attributes define normality offers a practical and interpretable lever for domain experts who know what to ignore (e.g., lighting, background) without collecting additional data or retraining. Key limitations include the heuristic selection of the PCA component count d (no automatic criterion in the unsupervised setting) and the finding that suppressing one attribute does not reliably improve detection of another in real-world, multi-attribute datasets. Open questions include whether concept axes can be composed for multi-attribute guidance, whether d can be selected automatically, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D anomaly detection.
