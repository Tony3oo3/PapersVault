## Problem

Neural dynamics models enable data-driven prediction of scene evolution for robotic manipulation, but their non-linearity makes long-horizon motion planning extremely challenging. Sampling-based planners (CEM, MPPI) degrade in high-dimensional, contact-rich settings, while MIP-based verifiers cannot scale to large neural networks or long planning horizons. The paper addresses this gap by asking whether a branch-and-bound framework, adapted from neural network verification, can systematically explore the action space to find high-quality trajectories over large neural dynamics models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251438/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The planner consumes a pre-trained neural dynamics model (MLP or GNN) that maps (state, action) → next state, a box-constrained action space with per-step dimension k, a planning horizon H (up to 20 steps, giving total input dimension d = kH), and a cost function that may include non-smooth obstacle penalties. The dynamics model is unrolled H times to form a single scalar objective f(u) over the flattened action vector. The output is a near-optimal action sequence u* and the corresponding predicted state-action trajectory, which can be executed directly or tracked via MPC in real-world deployment.

## Method

BaB-ND constructs a search tree over the action space using three GPU-parallelized components. Branching selects subdomains to split via a hybrid heuristic: the top-n₁ by best-found upper bound (exploitation) plus n−n₁ sampled by softmax over normalized lower bounds (exploration), then splits along the dimension maximizing (input range × distribution bias of top-w% samples). Bounding adapts CROWN with two key modifications: propagation early-stop halts backward bound propagation at intermediate layers (concretizing with empirical bounds from CEM samples) to avoid the quadratic relaxation error of full unrolling, and search-integrated bounding replaces recursive pre-activation bound computation with min/max statistics over sampled trajectories. Searching uses CEM within each subdomain, recording intermediate-layer outputs to feed back into the next bounding round. Unlike NN verification, the lower bound need not be strictly sound—only tight enough to prune unpromising regions—enabling these relaxations while still converging to high-quality feasible solutions.

## Results

On the synthetic non-convex optimization problem (f(u) = Σ 5u_i² + cos(50u_i)), BaB-ND achieves near-optimality (f* ≈ −1.9803d) at d=100 dimensions, while all sampling-based baselines converge to sub-optimal values. On the scalability benchmark (Pushing with Obstacles, H=20, 530.2K-parameter model), BaB-ND achieves a final objective of 34.70, outperforming CEM (38.87), MPPI (45.63), and GD (60.26). In the MIP scalability comparison across 36 settings, MIP finds optimal solutions for only 6, sub-optimal for 3, and fails on the remaining 27 within 300 seconds, while BaB-ND handles all settings including H=20 with 500K+ parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BaB-ND consistently outperforms all three baselines (GD, MPPI, CEM) on the four manipulation tasks in both open-loop planning quality and real-world closed-loop execution. Against CEM (the strongest sampling baseline), BaB-ND achieves lower objectives on Pushing with Obstacles, Object Merging, and Object Sorting, with the gap widening as model size and horizon increase (e.g., 34.70 vs. 38.87 at 530.2K/H=20). Against MPPI, BaB-ND shows a particularly large advantage on Object Sorting (where MPPI struggles with discrete actions) and at larger scales. Against MIP (Liu et al., 2023b), BaB-ND is the only method that scales to large neural dynamics models: MIP fails to produce any solution in 27 of 36 tested configurations within 300 seconds, while BaB-ND completes all settings. On Rope Routing, BaB-ND achieves comparable open-loop performance to MPPI and CEM but avoids the horizontal-routing failure mode that causes GD to get stuck.

## Relation to Prior Work

BaB-ND adapts the branch-and-bound + bound-propagation framework from neural network verification (CROWN, α,β-CROWN, Bunel et al.) to the fundamentally different goal of model-based planning: instead of proving a sound lower bound, it must find a concrete near-optimal action sequence. This requires new branching heuristics that balance exploitation and exploration, a propagation early-stop mechanism to avoid the excessively loose bounds that arise from unrolling a dynamics model over long horizons, and a search-integrated bounding step that reuses empirical intermediate-layer bounds from CEM sampling. The paper positions itself between sampling-based planners (CEM, MPPI), which are flexible but degrade in high dimensions, and MIP-based approaches (Liu et al., 2023b), which are globally optimal but intractable for large networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical path to long-horizon planning over large neural dynamics models (500K+ parameters, H=20) where MIP completely fails, validated on real hardware across contact-rich tasks including deformable rope routing and multi-object sorting with GNNs. Key open questions include: how performance degrades when the learned dynamics model has significant prediction error (the paper acknowledges this but does not address it), whether the branching heuristics generalize beyond the tested task families, and whether the reliance on CEM for the searching component limits the achievable solution quality. The insight that *sound* lower bounds are unnecessary for planning—only tight estimates—opens the door to further relaxations and approximations in the bounding step.
