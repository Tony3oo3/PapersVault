## Problem

While flat minima in supervised learning are well-established as indicators of generalization, the analogous concept of flat reward landscapes in RL policy parameter space has received limited attention and lacks a formal theoretical connection to robustness. Prior empirical studies showed that flatter reward surfaces correlate with better robustness, but no rigorous link between reward flatness and action, transition, or reward robustness had been established.

## Input / Output

The method takes state observation vectors from continuous-control MuJoCo environments (HalfCheetah-v3, Hopper-v3, Walker2d-v3) or discrete-action Gym environments (CartPole-v1, LunarLander-v2) and outputs actions sampled from a Gaussian policy (mean and log-std parameters) or a softmax distribution over discrete actions. Notably, training occurs entirely in the nominal environment without explicit uncertainty sets or adversarial agents; robustness emerges implicitly from the flatness of the reward surface in parameter space.

## Method

The paper defines E-flat reward maxima (reward remains constant under parameter perturbations of norm ≤ E) and proves via Proposition 1 that such a maximum yields a Δ-action robust policy with Δ* ≤ ||J(θ*)||·E + O(E²), where J is the Jacobian of the policy's mean action. Practically, this is achieved by SAM+PPO, which replaces standard PPO's gradient ascent with a min-max objective: the inner max identifies the worst-case parameter perturbation ε within an ℓ₂ ball of radius ρ, and the outer min updates parameters using the gradient at the perturbed point. This doubles the forward/backward passes per step but requires no adversarial training, explicit uncertainty modeling, or environment modifications. The paper further argues (Remark 1.2) that reward flatness extends to robustness against transition probability and reward function perturbations, validated empirically across mass scaling, friction changes, and reward noise.

## Results

SAM+PPO achieves the highest nominal returns across all three MuJoCo environments (e.g., 6523 on HalfCheetah-v3, 3766 on Hopper-v3, 5129 on Walker2d-v3 under action noise σ=0.2) and exhibits the smallest performance degradation under all perturbation types. Under mass scaling (×1.2), SAM+PPO actually *improves* on Hopper-v3 (+9) and Walker2d-v3 (+79) relative to nominal, while PPO degrades by −1659 and −4175 respectively. Under friction reduction (0.88), SAM+PPO's degradation is minimal (−23 on HalfCheetah, +61 on Walker2d) compared to PPO's −1495 on Hopper. Flatness metrics confirm SAM+PPO converges to substantially flatter reward maxima (e.g., λ_max drops from 15192.95 to 275.93 on HalfCheetah-v3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM+PPO outperforms both RNAC and RARL on the majority of perturbation scenarios. Against RNAC, SAM+PPO consistently achieves lower degradation: under action noise σ=0.2, SAM+PPO degrades by −1574/−1454/−3096 versus RNAC's −3470/−2124/−3495 on HalfCheetah/Hopper/Walker2d; under mass scaling, RNAC shows only marginal gains over PPO (e.g., −641 vs −972 on HalfCheetah) while SAM+PPO achieves near-zero or positive change (−352, +9, +79). Against RARL, the comparison is closer: RARL matches or slightly beats SAM+PPO on mass scaling for Hopper (−58 vs +9) and Walker2d (−50 vs +79), and on friction for HalfCheetah (−44 vs −23), but SAM+PPO wins decisively on action noise (−1574 vs −1584 on HalfCheetah is a near-tie, but −1454 vs −1174 on Hopper favors RARL slightly) and on friction for Hopper (−772 vs −975). Computationally, SAM+PPO is far cheaper than RARL (×1.08 vs ×4.41 training time) and RNAC (×1.08 vs ×1.43), while achieving superior robustness, making it the most cost-effective robust RL method evaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM (Foret et al., 2021) for finding flat minima and on Lee et al. (2024), who first applied SAM to policy gradient methods, but departs from both by providing the first formal theoretical link (Proposition 1) between flat reward maxima in policy parameter space and action robustness, rather than relying on empirical evidence alone. It also extends the analysis beyond the action-robust MDP formulation of Tessler et al. (2019) to show how reward flatness implicitly confers robustness against transition probability and reward function perturbations. The paper explicitly positions itself against the 'naive transfer' of supervised-learning flatness intuitions to RL, arguing that prior work (Lee et al. 2024; Sullivan et al. 2022) lacked a rigorous bridge and did not scrutinize flatness across the full set of RL perturbation axes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a minimal modification to standard PPO (adding SAM's perturbation step) yields robustness comparable to or better than dedicated RRL methods like RARL and RNAC, while training only in the nominal environment and at lower computational cost (×0.85 total steps vs. PPO, ×4.41 less time than RARL). This suggests that robustness can be obtained 'for free' through optimization geometry rather than explicit adversarial training or uncertainty modeling, which is attractive for real-world deployment where uncertainty sets are hard to specify. Limitations include the Gaussian-policy assumption in the proof, the reliance on first-order Taylor expansion (so the Jacobian bound is heuristic rather than guaranteed), and evaluation restricted to MuJoCo continuous control plus two discrete Gym environments. Open questions include whether the flatness-robustness link holds for non-Gaussian or discrete-action policies at scale, how it interacts with other regularization or ensemble-based robustness techniques, and whether the benefit persists in high-dimensional or partially observable settings.
