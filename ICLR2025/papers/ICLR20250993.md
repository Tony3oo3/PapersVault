## Problem

Existing utilitarian algorithm configuration procedures like UP can only search finite, relatively small sets of configurations, making them inapplicable to algorithms with continuous or uncountable parameters. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a procedure that can efficiently search infinite parameter spaces while retaining the adaptive, anytime optimality guarantees of prior utilitarian methods.

## Input / Output

COUP takes a distribution over (potentially uncountably infinite) configurations, a stream of input instances, a user-specified utility function over runtime, and sequences of phase parameters (ε_p, γ_p) that control the rates of exploring new instances versus new configurations. It is anytime in both ε and γ: at the end of each phase it returns a configuration guaranteed to be (ε_p, γ_p)-optimal, where γ_p quantifies the fraction of the configuration space excluded from the optimality target. The procedure can be terminated at any point, with the guarantee improving as more phases complete.

## Method

COUP is built on the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) bandit procedure rather than the Successive Elimination (SE) procedure underlying UP. In the finite case (OUP), this means the algorithm simply stops running suboptimal configurations once they no longer look promising, rather than continuing to sample them until they can be explicitly eliminated, yielding a strictly tighter sample-complexity bound. The infinite-space version (COUP) runs in successive phases: each phase samples additional configurations from the distribution (shrinking γ_p), reuses all prior runtime samples to update confidence bounds, and runs OUP-style UCB iterations until the gap between the best upper and lower confidence bounds falls below ε_p. A captime doubling mechanism with a refined balancing condition handles unbounded runtimes, and the phased design ensures the total failure probability is controlled across all phases via carefully scaled Hoeffding-based confidence widths.

## Results

OUP proves a meaningful ε-optimality guarantee in an order of magnitude or less total runtime compared to UP, and consistently outperforms a naive Hoeffding-based procedure regardless of captime choice. COUP's exploration of the infinite configuration space costs only a small multiplicative factor over OUP's finite-space runtime, confirming that the continuous search overhead is modest. In head-to-head comparisons against five other configuration procedures (Successive Halving, Hyperband, AC-Band, ICAR, SPC), COUP and OUP eventually narrow in on the optimal configuration for every seed, while non-anytime baselines suffer from sensitive budget-parameter choices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UP (the prior utilitarian procedure built on Successive Elimination), OUP wins decisively: it stops running suboptimal configurations as soon as they no longer look promising rather than continuing until explicit elimination, yielding an order-of-magnitude speedup in proving a given ε. Against the naive Hoeffding procedure, OUP is consistently faster across all captime settings, whereas the naive method can only beat UP when its captime parameter is well-tuned. In the broader comparison (Figure 5), COUP and OUP outperform Successive Halving, AC-Band, and ICAR on the percentage-gap-to-best metric across all three datasets (minisat, cplex_rcw, cplex_region). Hyperband is competitive on cplex_rcw, finding a good configuration faster than COUP initially, but still leaves a gap to the best configuration while COUP and OUP continue to improve. SPC is the closest competitor among the non-utilitarian methods, but COUP and OUP avoid the 'luck of the draw' sensitivity to budget parameters that plagues the non-anytime baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

COUP extends the utilitarian algorithm configuration framework introduced by Graham et al. (2023b) (UP), which was limited to finite configuration spaces and built on the Successive Elimination (SE) bandit procedure. The key conceptual departure is replacing SE with the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) procedure, which naturally ignores suboptimal arms rather than explicitly eliminating them, enabling seamless extension to infinite and continuous parameter spaces. The paper also draws on the broader algorithm configuration literature (LeapsAndBounds, CapsAndRuns, ICAR, SPC, AC-Band, Hyperband) and the multi-armed bandit theory of Even-Dar et al. (2002) and Lai (1987), while introducing a new (ε, γ)-optimality notion that quantifies both approximation quality and the fraction of unexplored configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COUP is the first utilitarian configuration procedure that can search infinite or continuous parameter spaces while maintaining provable near-optimality guarantees, removing a major practical limitation of prior work. The order-of-magnitude speedup over UP even on finite spaces, combined with the anytime property in both ε and γ, makes it a more practical and flexible tool for algorithm configuration. Open questions include how to help end users specify appropriate utility functions, whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional continuous spaces, and whether the theoretical guarantees remain tight in practice for complex algorithm families.
