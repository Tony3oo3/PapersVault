## Problem

Training effective device-control agents via RL typically requires costly on-policy interaction (policy gradients with Monte-Carlo returns), which is impractical in real-world settings like mobile device control where each interaction incurs a cost. The paper addresses how to train a VLM-based state-action Q-value function entirely from offline data and use it to extract a strong policy without any environment interaction, overcoming the instability and compute inefficiency of running TD-learning on billion-parameter VLMs end-to-end.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251188/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes phone screenshots (RGB images), a natural-language task instruction, and the full interaction history (previous screenshots and actions) as input to the VLM backbone. The Q-function outputs a scalar value for a given state-action pair, while the policy outputs a token sequence representing a pixel-level command (e.g., 'click (0.8, 0.2)' or 'type text'). The VLM only sees the current screenshot (with a cursor overlay for click actions) plus a text prompt; the next screenshot is used solely to compute the binary fine-tuning label, not as a direct input to the Q-function.

## Method

Digi-Q operates in three stages: (1) representation fine-tuning, where a VLM (LLaVA-1.5) is fine-tuned with a binary cross-entropy objective to predict whether an action causes a significant visual change (ℓ₂ image distance above a threshold), priming the VLM to attend to actionable scene features; (2) TD-learning on frozen VLM intermediate-layer representations, where a small MLP Q-function and V-function are trained via temporal-difference backups (only ~1% of VLM parameters are updated), avoiding the pathologies of end-to-end TD-learning on large models; (3) Best-of-N policy extraction, where N=16 actions are sampled from the behavior policy at each state, ranked by the learned Q-function, and the actor is trained via supervised log-likelihood to imitate the highest-Q action (conditioned on positive advantage), avoiding the unstable 'negative gradient' of REINFORCE while being less conservative than AWR.

## Results

On the AitW General test set, Digi-Q (offline) achieves 59.0% success rate, and on AitW Web Shopping test set it achieves 47.6%, representing a 21.2% relative improvement over the prior best offline method (DigiRL: 54.5% and 43.8% respectively). Digi-Q also achieves a 31.5% relative improvement over Filtered-BC and a 53.5% absolute improvement over the best prompting-based approach (AppAgent + GPT-4V at 17.7% on General). Notably, Digi-Q's offline performance roughly matches DigiRL when given online interaction (71.2% / 58.0%), demonstrating that the Q-function enables effective policy improvement without environment rollouts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Digi-Q outperforms all prompting-based baselines by a wide margin: Set-of-Marks with GPT-4V achieves only 13.5% on AitW General test, and AppAgent with GPT-4V reaches 17.7%, compared to Digi-Q's 59.0%. Among supervised training baselines, CogAgent (18B) scores 25.0% on General and 38.5% on Web Shopping, while AutoUI (1B) scores 22.9% and 25.0% respectively — both substantially below Digi-Q. The most direct comparison is with DigiRL (offline), the prior state-of-the-art offline RL method: Digi-Q improves from 54.5% to 59.0% on General and from 43.8% to 47.6% on Web Shopping (21.2% relative gain). In the policy extraction ablation (Table 3), Digi-Q's Best-of-N approach (58.0%) substantially outperforms both REINFORCE (37.5%, high variance due to negative gradient) and AWR (19.4%, overly conservative), confirming that the Q-function-based reranking is the key driver of improvement. Digi-Q's offline performance (59.0% / 47.6%) approaches but does not fully match DigiRL with online interaction (71.2% / 58.0%), though the gap narrows considerably, validating the paper's claim that a well-trained Q-function can substitute for costly on-policy rollouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

Digi-Q builds on the ArCHer hierarchical actor-critic framework (Zhou et al., 2024b) for turn-level value functions and token-level policies, but departs substantially in two key ways: it trains the Q-function on frozen VLM intermediate representations (after a novel representation fine-tuning phase) rather than end-to-end, and it replaces REINFORCE/AWR policy extraction with a Best-of-N reranking objective. The representation fine-tuning step—training the VLM via binary classification on whether an action causes a visual state change—is a new ingredient not present in prior VLM-based RL work (e.g., Chen et al., 2024, which uses handcrafted prompts instead). The paper positions itself as the first to scale state-action Q-value functions with Bellman backups to realistic VLM-based device-control agents, filling a gap left by prior work that relied on on-policy policy gradients or Monte-Carlo state-only value functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that Digi-Q achieves offline performance matching online RL (DigiRL with interaction) without any environment interaction, which is critical in settings where rollouts are costly or infeasible (privacy, real-world devices, human interaction). The finding that off-the-shelf VLM representations lack actionable information and require a targeted fine-tuning phase to support Q-function learning is a useful diagnostic for the broader community building VLM-based agents. Open questions include whether the approach extends to online self-improvement loops where the policy drifts from the data distribution, and whether the representation fine-tuning objective generalizes beyond pixel-based device control to other agentic domains.
