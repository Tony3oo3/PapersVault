## Problem

Standard permutation equivariant denoisers in graph diffusion models face a fundamental limitation for graph-to-graph translation: when the noisy input carries no structural information, equivariance forces the model to output identical marginal distributions for all nodes and edges, making it impossible to copy or translate graph structure. The paper formalizes this with a theorem showing the optimal equivariant denoiser converges to the empirical label distribution, and demonstrates that this symmetry bottleneck causes near-zero performance on retrosynthesis (5% top-1 accuracy) despite graph diffusion models being well-suited to the task.

## Input / Output

The model takes a product molecule graph Y (node and edge labels in one-hot format) plus a binary node mapping matrix P^{Y→X} ∈ {0,1}^{N_X×N_Y} that identifies atom-mapped correspondences between product and reactant nodes. The output is a reactant molecule graph X generated via iterative denoising from an all-absorbing-state (⊥) prior over T=100 steps. The node mapping need not be complete or perfect; the model benefits even from partially correct mappings, and at inference time the mapping can be assigned arbitrarily since the aligned equivariance theorem guarantees the same marginal distribution over targets.

## Method

The core contribution is aligned permutation equivariance: the denoiser is constrained to be equivariant only under joint permutations of X, Y, and the node mapping P^{Y→X} that preserve the matching, rather than under arbitrary independent permutations of X. Three concrete alignment mechanisms are proposed and can be combined: (1) node-mapped positional encodings using Laplacian eigenvectors assigned to matched node pairs, (2) a learnable skip connection that adds λ·P^{Y→X}Y directly to the denoiser logits, and (3) input alignment that concatenates P^{Y→X}Y features onto X before the Graph Transformer processes them. The paper proves that aligned denoisers induce aligned permutation invariant generative distributions (Theorem 2), ensuring valid generalization across node orderings. A novel post-training conditioning method is also derived for discrete diffusion, enabling property-guided generation (e.g., atom economy) and interactive inpainting of partial reaction structures.

## Results

On USPTO-50k retrosynthesis, the best aligned model (DiffAlign-PE+skip) achieves 54.7% top-1 accuracy and 0.639 MRR, dramatically outperforming the unaligned equivariant baseline (4.1% top-1, 0.056 MRR). Among template-free methods, DiffAlign-PE+skip is the strongest, surpassing Retroformer (53.2%) and DualTF_aug (53.6%) on top-1. The model also outperforms all non-pretrained baselines on top-1 and matches or exceeds template-based methods (GLN 52.5%, LocalRetro 52.6%) across all top-k metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffAlign-PE+skip (54.7% top-1) is the best template-free method, beating Retroformer (53.2%), DualTF_aug (53.6%), Graph2SMILES (52.9%), and RetroBridge (50.3%, though evaluated with a relaxed metric). It also surpasses all synthon-based methods (GraphRetro 53.7%, RetroDiff 52.6%, MEGAN 48.0%, G2G 48.9%) and template-based methods (GLN 52.5%, LocalRetro 52.6%) on top-1. The only methods that outperform it are the two pre-trained models (PMSR 62.0%, RSMILES 56.3%), which leverage much larger training data. The unaligned equivariant baseline collapses to 4.1% top-1, confirming the theoretical prediction that permutation equivariance prevents graph copying. Among the paper's own ablations, combining positional encodings with skip connections (54.7%) outperforms input alignment alone (44.1%) and PE alone (49.0%), with the PE+skip model also achieving the highest MRR (0.639).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DiGress framework (Vignac et al., 2023) and absorbing-state discrete diffusion (Austin et al., 2021) for graph generation, but identifies a fundamental theoretical limitation: permutation equivariance—the standard inductive bias in graph diffusion—provably prevents the denoiser from performing even the simplest graph-copying task (Theorem 1). The proposed solution, aligned permutation equivariance, connects to the broader symmetry-breaking literature (Smidt et al., 2021; Xie & Smidt, 2024) and to Yan et al.'s (2023) empirical observation that relaxing equivariance with positional encodings helps, but provides the first principled theoretical justification and multiple concrete alignment mechanisms. The post-training conditioning method extends the classifier-guidance paradigm (Chung et al., 2023; Ho et al., 2022) to discrete state spaces, and the alignment technique is conceptually similar to what Igashov et al. (2024) implement in RetroBridge without formal equivariance analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that permutation equivariant denoisers converge to marginal distributions is a fundamental insight that explains why standard graph diffusion models underperform on conditional generation tasks, and the aligned equivariance framework provides a principled fix that preserves the benefits of equivariance where it matters (unmatched subgraphs) while enabling symmetry breaking where it doesn't. The 13× improvement from alignment (4.1% → 54.7% top-1) is well-evidenced across multiple alignment variants and ablations, and the resulting SOTA-matching performance among template-free methods makes graph diffusion viable for retrosynthesis. Key limitations include the requirement for at least partial node-mapping information between input and target graphs, and the computational cost of iterative denoising; open questions include whether the aligned equivariance framework generalizes to other conditional generation settings (e.g., 3D molecular generation) and whether accelerated sampling methods can reduce the inference cost.
