## Problem

Existing 3D occupancy world models (e.g., OccWorld, OccLLaMA) rely on expensive 3D occupancy labels and an indirect architecture—frozen occupancy encoder → discrete tokenizer → autoregressive decoder—that suffers from information loss. While self-supervised 3D occupancy prediction using 2D labels has been explored, no prior work extends this to 4D occupancy forecasting, leaving a gap in how to train world models that forecast future scenes without costly 3D annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252254/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes N multi-view camera images from k past frames (plus optional ego-state: speed, acceleration, historical trajectory) and produces a 200×200×16 voxel grid at 0.4 m resolution with 18 semantic classes for both current and f future frames, along with a predicted ego trajectory. Internally, the occupancy network (BEVStereo backbone) extracts volume features of shape X×Y×Z×D, which the forecasting module propagates forward in feature space rather than token space, preserving continuous geometric structure for downstream rendering-based supervision.

## Method

PreWorld introduces a two-stage training paradigm: (1) a self-supervised pre-training stage where an attribute projection head (several MLPs) maps volume features into density, semantic, and RGB fields, which are then supervised via NeRF-style volume rendering against 2D depth, semantic, and RGB labels across current and future frames; and (2) a fully-supervised fine-tuning stage where the projection head is removed and a standard occupancy head is trained with 3D occupancy labels (focal + Lovász-softmax + scene-class affinity losses). The key architectural contribution is a state-conditioned forecasting module consisting of just two MLPs that directly predict future volume features from current ones, optionally conditioned on ego state. This design avoids the encode-to-tokens-and-decode pipeline of prior work, enabling simultaneous end-to-end optimization of the occupancy network and forecasting module, and—critically—preserving continuous volume features so that 2D rendering supervision can be applied to future frames as well as the current one.

## Results

On the Occ3D-nuScenes benchmark, PreWorld achieves an mIoU of 34.69% for 3D occupancy prediction (with 2D+3D supervision), surpassing the previous SOTA OccFlowNet (33.86%) and SparseOcc (30.90%). For 4D occupancy forecasting, PreWorld sets a new SOTA with an average mIoU of 9.55% over 1–3 seconds, outperforming OccLLaMA-F (8.66%) and OccWorld-D (8.62%). For motion planning with ego-state, PreWorld achieves an average L2 error of 0.31 m and collision rate of 0.53%, matching or exceeding dedicated planning methods like UniAD (1.03 m L2) and VAD (1.22 m L2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreWorld outperforms all 2D-supervised baselines by a wide margin: mIoU 34.69 vs. OccFlowNet 28.42 (2D-only), RenderOcc 23.93, SelfOcc 9.30, and OccNeRF 9.53. Among 3D-supervised methods, PreWorld (3D-only, 33.95) already surpasses SparseOcc (30.90), CTF-Occ (28.53), TPVFormer (27.83), and BEVFormer (26.88), and the 2D pre-training stage adds a further +0.74 mIoU. For 4D occupancy forecasting, PreWorld (avg mIoU 9.55 with 2D+3D) beats OccLLaMA-F (8.66) and OccWorld-D (8.62), while the self-supervised variant PreWorld-S (avg mIoU 3.78) already exceeds OccWorld-S (0.26) by over 1300%. On motion planning, PreWorld with ego-state (L2 0.31 m, collision 0.53%) matches OccLLaMA-F (L2 1.20 m, collision 0.70%) and substantially outperforms ST-P3 (L2 2.11 m) and OccNet (L2 2.14 m), though UniAD (L2 1.03 m) uses additional map/box/motion/track supervision. The 2D pre-training stage consistently improves performance across all three tasks, with the largest relative gains observed when 3D fine-tuning data is limited (e.g., +6.36 mIoU when fine-tuning on only 150 scenes).

## Relation to Prior Work

PreWorld extends the volume-rendering-based 2D supervision paradigm from RenderOcc (Pan et al., 2024) to the temporal forecasting setting, while departing fundamentally from the indirect architecture used by OccWorld and OccLLaMA, which freeze a 3D occupancy model and rely on a tokenizer–autoregressive–decoder pipeline. The key conceptual departure is replacing this multi-stage encoding-decoding chain with a simple state-conditioned forecasting module (two MLPs) that operates directly on volume features, enabling end-to-end joint optimization of the occupancy network and forecasting head. The two-stage training paradigm—self-supervised pre-training with 2D labels followed by supervised fine-tuning with 3D occupancy labels—bridges the gap between self-supervised occupancy prediction (SelfOcc, OccNeRF) and fully-supervised world models, a combination not previously explored for 4D forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing the annotation bottleneck: 2D labels are far cheaper to obtain than dense 3D occupancy annotations, and the scalability experiments show that pre-training on a larger 2D-labeled dataset can compensate for limited 3D-labeled fine-tuning data (450 scenes with pre-training ≈ 700 scenes without). The finding that a two-MLP forecasting module outperforms complex autoregressive token-based architectures when jointly optimized challenges the prevailing design assumption in occupancy world models. Open questions include the 'shortcut' effect where ego-state input may bypass the learned occupancy forecast during planning, whether the approach generalizes beyond nuScenes-scale data, and whether fully self-supervised 4D forecasting (PreWorld-S, still at only ~3.8 mIoU) can reach practical utility without any 3D labels.
