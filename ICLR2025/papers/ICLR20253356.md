## Problem

Existing slot-based object-centric learning methods face a fundamental trade-off when using diffusion models as decoders: leveraging pretrained diffusion models introduces a text-centric conditioning bias in cross-attention layers that hinders slot-based generation, while training from scratch sacrifices the generative power needed for complex real-world images. Additionally, the part-whole hierarchy problem—deciding whether to segment an object as a whole or into parts—remains poorly addressed without external supervision, limiting compositional generation on datasets like COCO.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (H×W×3) and processes it through a DINOv2 ViT-B backbone (patch size 14) to produce flattened visual features, from which slot attention extracts N slot vectors (N×d). Outputs include per-slot segmentation masks (h·w × N attention maps), a reconstructed image generated by the conditioned diffusion model, and compositional edits (object removal, replacement, or addition) achieved by modifying the slot set. A register token, computed by mean-pooling the slots or backbone features, is additionally fed to the original text cross-attention layers to encode global scene context.

## Method

SlotAdapt introduces adapter cross-attention layers after each existing cross-attention layer in all downsampling and upsampling blocks of a frozen Stable Diffusion UNet, allowing slots to condition the diffusion model without being constrained to the text embedding space. A register token (mean-pooled slots or features) is routed through the original text cross-attention layers to capture background and global context, freeing individual slots to focus on specific objects. A guidance loss (binary cross-entropy) is applied between the slot attention mask and the transpose of the adapter cross-attention mask from the third upsampling block, jointly aligning both attention maps with object boundaries without external supervision. Only the adapter layers and slot attention are trained, minimizing a noise-prediction reconstruction loss plus a weighted guidance term, while the pretrained diffusion model remains frozen.

## Results

On COCO, SlotAdapt with guidance achieves FG-ARI 41.4, instance-level mBO 35.1, and class-level mBO 39.2, outperforming all baselines by a notable margin (e.g., ~10% improvement in mBO^i over the next best method). On VOC, it reaches FG-ARI 29.6 and mBO^i 51.5, again leading all competitors. For generation quality, SlotAdapt achieves FID 10.86 and KID×1000 0.39 for reconstruction, and FID 40.57 and KID×1000 34.38 for compositional generation, substantially outperforming LSD (FID 167.2) and SlotDiffusion (FID 64.2) on compositional tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlotAdapt outperforms all five baselines on nearly every metric across both VOC and COCO. Against Slot Attention (SA+DINOViT) and SLATE+DINOViT, the gains are large and consistent (e.g., FG-ARI 29.6 vs. 12.3 and 15.6 on VOC). Against DINOSAUR, the strongest non-diffusion baseline, SlotAdapt leads by 6.4 FG-ARI points on VOC and 7.1 on COCO, with particularly strong instance-level mBO improvements (51.5 vs. 43.6 on VOC; 35.1 vs. 32.3 on COCO). Against LSD, SlotAdapt wins on all metrics on both datasets, with the largest gap on COCO mBO^i (35.1 vs. 27.0). The closest competitor is SlotDiffusion, which matches or slightly exceeds SlotAdapt on VOC mBO^c (55.3 vs. 51.9) and mBO^i (50.4 vs. 51.5), but SlotAdapt clearly dominates on COCO across all three metrics (FG-ARI 41.4 vs. 37.2, mBO^i 35.1 vs. 31.0, mBO^c 39.2 vs. 35.0), demonstrating that the adapter-based conditioning and guidance loss are especially effective on the more complex COCO scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SlotAdapt sits at the intersection of slot-based object-centric learning and latent diffusion models, addressing a specific gap left by prior OCL+diffusion methods (LSD, SlotDiffusion, GLASS). Those methods either force slot representations through text-trained cross-attention layers (introducing a text-centric bias) or retrain the diffusion model from scratch (sacrificing generative quality). SlotAdapt departs from both by inserting lightweight adapter cross-attention layers—inspired by T2I-Adapter—dedicated to slot conditioning, while repurposing the original text cross-attention for a pooled 'register' token that captures scene-level context. The mutual attention-guidance loss, which aligns slot attention masks with adapter cross-attention masks via binary cross-entropy, is a novel self-supervisory signal not present in prior OCL or diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate successful compositional generation and editing on COCO (80 object classes, complex real-world scenes), a setting where prior slot-based methods have struggled. The adapter-based conditioning strategy is a broadly applicable technique for any task requiring non-text conditioning of pretrained diffusion models, and the frozen-backbone training protocol keeps computational costs manageable. Open questions remain: the method still exhibits under/over-segmentation, reconstructed images can drift slightly from inputs, and the reliance on real-world-pretrained diffusion models limits transfer to synthetic domains. Practical deployment for user-guided editing (associating prompts with slots) is identified as future work.
