## Problem

Online k-MEANS clustering with recourse (consistency) has a gap between known upper and lower bounds: prior work achieves O(1)-competitive ratio with O(k poly log n) consistency for k-MEDIAN, but it is unclear whether k-MEANS admits the same, and no algorithm achieves a (1+ε)-competitive ratio with near-optimal consistency. Additionally, existing consistent algorithms are purpose-built and do not provide a general framework to convert any offline clustering algorithm into a consistent online one.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a stream of n points in [Δ]^d (where Δ is the aspect ratio), a cluster count k, and a black-box offline α-approximate clustering algorithm running in T(n) time. It outputs a sequence of center sets C_1, …, C_n (each |C_i| ≤ k) that are (1+ε)α²-competitive against the optimal offline solution for every prefix, with total consistency (sum of new centers added) bounded by O(αdk·poly(ε^{-z} log(nΔ))). The algorithm operates in phases over a consistent ε-coreset of size Õ_ε(k), restarting the bounded-input subroutine O(log(nΔ)) times as the coreset is rebuilt.

## Method

The method composes two components: (1) a consistent online ε-coreset (adapted from Woodruff et al., 2023) that maintains a weighted point set of size Õ_ε(k) for every prefix, reducing the problem to bounded-length input; and (2) a phase-based algorithm for bounded input that, in each phase, first deletes the maximum number of centers while preserving a (1+O(ε))α-approximation, then greedily adds incoming points as centers until k is reached, and finally swaps O(ε^{-O(z)}·ℓ) centers identified via ε-well-separated pairs between the current solution and an α-approximate solution for the phase-end point set. The swap count is bounded using a key lemma showing that if two center sets form t well-separated pairs, then Ω(k−t) centers can be removed with only ε·(cost_U + cost_V) additional cost. Centers are then made 'bounded robust' via a backward-induction robust center sequence construction (generalized from Fichtenberger et al., 2021 to (1+ε)-approximation and general (k,z)-clustering), ensuring inductive stability across phases. This yields the first (1+ε)-competitive online clustering algorithm with O(k poly log n) consistency, and by plugging in k-MEANS++ as the offline subroutine, an efficient practical algorithm with strong empirical consistency.

## Results

On three real-world datasets (SKIN, SHUTTLE, COVERTYPE) with k=10, the heuristic single-swap variant ('ours-heuristic') achieves the lowest consistency by hundreds of times compared to all baselines while maintaining competitive clustering cost. The theoretically guaranteed variant ('ours-faithful', full Algorithm 1 with k-MEANS++) achieves 3–5× lower consistency than the naive k-MEANS++-on-coreset baseline and roughly 2× lower consistency than Lattanzi & Vassilvitskii (2017), with clustering cost comparable to both baselines. The theoretical guarantee is (1+ε)α²-competitive with Õ(k) consistency, which is the first (1+ε)-competitive online clustering algorithm with near-optimal linear-in-k consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) 'naive' — running k-MEANS++ directly on the consistent coreset (Õ(k²)-consistent in the worst case), and (2) 'LV17' — the consistent k-MEANS algorithm of Lattanzi & Vassilvitskii (2017), which is O(1)-competitive and O(k² log(nΔ))-consistent. The 'ours-faithful' algorithm wins on consistency against both baselines: it achieves 3–5× lower consistency than naive and ~2× lower than LV17, while incurring negligible additional cost (as shown in the cost curves of Figure 2). The 'ours-heuristic' single-swap variant dominates all methods on both consistency and cost, achieving hundreds of times lower consistency, though it lacks worst-case approximation guarantees and may degrade on adversarial or highly imbalanced data. The key practical takeaway is that the full theoretical algorithm (with k-MEANS++ plugged in) delivers near-optimal consistency with minimal overhead to solution quality, making it a drop-in replacement for standard k-MEANS++ in streaming settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the consistent online clustering framework introduced by Lattanzi & Vassilvitskii (2017) and the robust-center/well-separated-pairs machinery of Fichtenberger et al. (2021), extending both from O(1)-competitive k-MEDIAN to (1+ε)-competitive general (k,z)-CLUSTERING (including k-MEANS). The key methodological departure is a general reduction: any α-approximate offline clustering algorithm is turned into a (1+ε)α²-competitive online algorithm with O(k poly log n) consistency, via an ε-coreset (borrowed from Woodruff et al., 2023) that reduces the problem to bounded-size input. The robust center sequence definition and well-separated-pair analysis are strengthened from constant-factor to (1+ε)-factor guarantees, replacing the linear-programming-based analysis of Fichtenberger et al. with a local-search-inspired argument that works for general z.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This closes the consistency-vs-competitive-ratio gap that has persisted since 2017: the first (1+ε)-competitive online clustering algorithm with nearly optimal O(k poly log n) consistency is now available for k-MEANS and general (k,z)-CLUSTERING, matching the lower bound up to poly log factors. The plug-in framework makes the result immediately applicable to any practical offline algorithm (e.g., k-MEANS++), bridging the gap between theoretically optimal but impractical algorithms and efficient heuristics. Open questions include whether the poly log n factor in consistency can be eliminated, whether the (1+ε)α² competitive ratio is tight (the α² loss from composition may be improvable), and whether the heuristic single-swap variant (which dramatically outperforms the faithful implementation on tested datasets) can be given worst-case guarantees.
