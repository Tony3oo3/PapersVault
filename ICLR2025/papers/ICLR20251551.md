## Problem

In Spiking Neural Networks, membrane potentials progressively drift away from the firing threshold as time steps advance, causing surrogate gradients to diminish and producing unbalanced, vanishing gradients during backpropagation through both spatial and temporal dimensions. Existing techniques (residual connections, batch normalization, attention) do not adequately address this temporal gradient imbalance in deep SNNs, limiting their representational capacity and convergence speed.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either static RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) replicated across T time steps, or high-temporal-resolution event streams from DVS cameras (DVS-CIFAR10). It produces a single class label per input. During training, auxiliary classifier heads attached to intermediate network stages generate additional predictions for supervision, but these are removed at inference so no extra computational overhead is incurred.

## Method

DeepTAGE combines two mechanisms. First, Temporal-Aligned Gradient Enhancement (TAGE) computes σ_t = ‖x_t − v_th‖₂ at each time step to quantify how far membrane potentials have drifted from the threshold, then rescales the arctangent-based surrogate gradient by a normalization factor δ(σ_t) = w·(σ_t/σ₁) + (1−w), effectively widening the gradient response for later time steps where potentials are more dispersed. Second, Spatio-Temporal Deep Supervision (STDS) attaches lightweight auxiliary classifiers (three conv layers + GAP + FC) to every intermediate network stage at every time step, applying cross-entropy loss across all (K−1)×T auxiliary outputs plus the T final outputs, thereby injecting gradient signals directly into deep layers and later time steps. The full loss is the mean cross-entropy over all K·T prediction endpoints. The approach requires no architectural modification, no additional control modules, and adds zero inference cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DeepTAGE achieves 95.86% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-18 and only 4 time steps, surpassing the best direct-training baseline IM-Loss (95.49%, ResNet-19, T=6) and the ANN-to-SNN conversion method SlipReLU (94.59%, T=4). On CIFAR-100, it reaches 81.39% (ResNet-19, T=4), outperforming GLIF (77.05%) by 4.34 points and SlipReLU (78.55%, T=128) by 2.84 points. On ImageNet, DeepTAGE attains 68.52% (ResNet-18, T=4), exceeding TET (64.79%, ResNet-34, T=6) by 3.73 points. On the neuromorphic DVS-CIFAR10 dataset, it achieves 81.23% (VGG-11, T=10), beating LSG (77.90%) by 3.33 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepTAGE outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every dataset. On CIFAR-10, it beats the closest direct-training competitor IM-Loss (95.49%) by 0.37 points while using fewer time steps (T=4 vs. T=6) and a lighter backbone (ResNet-18 vs. ResNet-19). On CIFAR-100, the margin is much larger: DeepTAGE (81.39%) surpasses GLIF (77.05%) by 4.34 points and SlipReLU (78.55%) by 2.84 points, notably with only 4 time steps versus SlipReLU's 128. On ImageNet, DeepTAGE (68.52%, ResNet-18, T=4) exceeds TET (64.79%, ResNet-34, T=6) by 3.73 points and Real Spike (63.68%, ResNet-18, T=4) by 4.84 points. On DVS-CIFAR10, it leads LSG (77.90%) by 3.33 points and MPBN (74.40%) by 6.83 points. The consistent wins across static and neuromorphic benchmarks, and across both direct-training and ANN-to-SNN conversion paradigms, indicate that the temporal-aligned gradient enhancement and spatio-temporal deep supervision provide broad, architecture-agnostic improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the surrogate-gradient training paradigm for SNNs (STBP, TET, SLTT) by identifying a specific temporal pathology: membrane potential distributions drift away from the firing threshold as time steps progress, causing the standard arctangent-based surrogate to produce vanishingly small gradients at later steps. Rather than modifying the network architecture (as Sew-ResNet, BNTT, or tdBN do), DeepTAGE rescales the surrogate gradient function itself using a per-time-step normalization factor derived from the L2 deviation of membrane potentials from threshold, and augments this with spatio-temporal deep supervision at intermediate stages and time steps. The approach is conceptually orthogonal to prior normalization and residual-connection strategies, and the authors demonstrate it can be stacked on top of Sew-ResNet, TET, and SLTT baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of DeepTAGE—no architectural changes, no extra inference cost, auxiliary classifiers removable after training—makes it an attractive drop-in improvement for existing SNN pipelines targeting neuromorphic hardware. The consistent gains across static (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet) and event-based (DVS-CIFAR10) benchmarks suggest the temporal gradient imbalance is a general bottleneck, not dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other neuron models (e.g., Izhikevich, adaptive-exponential), whether the hyperparameter w remains stable at much larger time steps (T > 20), and whether the deep supervision overhead becomes prohibitive for very deep or very long SNNs. A notable inconsistency in the paper (Table 1 reports 76.13% on CIFAR-100 while the text and Table 5 report 81.39% for the same configuration) warrants scrutiny before the numbers are taken at face value.
