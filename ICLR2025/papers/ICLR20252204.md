## Problem

Despite extensive study of neural network training in infinite-width limits (NTK or mean-field), no analytically solvable model exists that simultaneously exhibits both kernel and feature learning dynamics at finite width with arbitrary initialization. This gap leaves the fundamental question of how and when feature learning emerges from kernel dynamics unanswered for realistic finite-width networks, and prevents a unified phase diagram connecting initialization, learning rates, and width to the learning regime.

## Input / Output

The method takes a one-hidden-layer linear network (width d, input dimension d₀) trained on data constrained to a 1D subspace, with arbitrary Gaussian or structured initialization and independent layer-wise learning rates η_u, η_w. It produces a closed-form trajectory for all weights as a function of a single scalar ξ(t) = exp(−4t/t_c), from which the NTK evolution, layer alignment angle ζ(t), and weight norms are read off directly. The phase diagram is parameterized by power-law exponents of width, output scale γ, initialization scale, and learning rate, yielding a complete classification into kernel, feature learning, frozen, or unstable regimes.

## Method

The key technical step is a change of variables to generalized coordinates p_i and q_i (linear combinations of the first-layer projection and second-layer weights), which reveals two conservation laws: p_i·q_i is constant and the weight direction orthogonal to the data subspace is frozen. This reduces the d-dimensional coupled ODE system to a single scalar ODE for Δ_i(t), which for the linear case (β=1) integrates to a closed-form solution involving the characteristic time t_c and asymptotic scale factors α_±. From this exact solution, the paper identifies three mechanisms exclusive to the feature learning phase: (1) learning by alignment, where the cosine similarity ζ between layers monotonically increases; (2) learning by disalignment, where ζ monotonically decreases when initialization is too large; and (3) learning by rescaling, where layer norms grow or shrink in concert with alignment. The phase diagram is derived by analyzing which term in t_c⁻² (the target-driven feature learning term vs. the initialization-driven kernel term) dominates under various scaling limits, yielding necessary and sufficient conditions for each regime.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with a two-layer ReLU FCN, converting the Kaiming initialization into the feature learning phase (Kaiming+) improves test accuracy by approximately 5% over the kernel phase (Kaiming−), while converting Xavier into the kernel phase (Xavier−) decreases test accuracy by approximately 10% compared to Xavier+. The weight evolution follows a predicted power-law scaling ‖W−W₀‖/‖W₀‖ ∝ d^(−δ) with δ=0 in the feature learning phase and δ=0.5, 1, 2 for NTK, Xavier−, and Kaiming− respectively, confirmed empirically with measured exponents of 0.47, 1.08, and 1.93. The three feature learning mechanisms (alignment, disalignment, rescaling) are validated across linear, ReLU, sigmoid, swish, and leaky ReLU activations, as well as in 4-layer and 6-layer FCNs on MNIST and ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a single proposed method to benchmark against external baselines; instead, it compares different scaling/parameterization regimes (standard NTK, mean-field, Xavier, Kaiming, lazy) and shows how each can be converted between the kernel and feature learning phases by adjusting γ and η. Empirically on CIFAR-10, Kaiming+ (feature learning) outperforms Kaiming− (kernel) by ~5% test accuracy, and Xavier+ outperforms Xavier− by ~10%, confirming that the trainable kernel in the feature learning phase improves generalization. The standard NTK scaling is always in the kernel phase, while the standard mean-field scaling is frozen (no learning occurs) unless the learning rate is rescaled. The theory also explains why larger initialization scales degrade ResNet-18 performance on CIFAR-10: large initialization drives the model toward disalignment (higher weight norms) rather than alignment, a mechanism absent in the kernel regime. Compared to prior exact-solution works (Saxe et al. 2013; Braun et al. 2022), this paper provides a general solution for arbitrary initialization and width, rather than particular solutions under special initializations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of exact-solution work on linear network dynamics (Saxe et al. 2013; Braun et al. 2022), which previously only solved for special initializations, by providing a general solution valid for arbitrary initialization and any finite width. It departs from the dominant infinite-width NTK/mean-field literature (Jacot et al. 2018; Yang & Hu 2020; Tensor Programs) by working at finite width, where both kernel and feature learning phases coexist and can be distinguished analytically. The model also generalizes the one-hidden-neuron exact solution of Kunin et al. (2024) to arbitrary width, and contrasts with the catapult model (Lewkowycz et al. 2020) by focusing on small-learning-rate scaling rather than large-learning-rate escape from the kernel regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three identified mechanisms (alignment, disalignment, rescaling) provide a mechanistic explanation for why larger initialization degrades performance and why layer alignment matters, validated across ResNet-18, 4- and 6-layer FCNs on MNIST and CIFAR-10. The phase diagrams offer concrete hyperparameter recipes (γ, η) to place any initialization into the desired learning phase, with practical implications for scaling Kaiming-initialized networks. Key limitations include the restriction to 1D data subspaces, a single hidden layer, and deterministic gradient flow (no SGD noise or regularization), leaving open whether the mechanisms and phase boundaries persist in higher-dimensional, deeper, or stochastic settings.
