## Problem

Large-scale deep learning training requires both a Hessian-informed pre-conditioner and a well-tuned learning rate scheduler, but full Hessian methods are infeasible at scale (O(d²)–O(d³) cost) and heuristic schedulers demand extensive manual tuning with multiple hyperparameters. Existing automatic learning-rate methods (D-adaptation, Prodigy, DoG) are either restricted to specific optimizers, lack convergence guarantees in practice, or require storing past iterates. The paper asks whether one can derive a Hessian-informed, automatically adaptive learning rate that is compatible with any base optimizer and computationally negligible.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the method consumes the current model parameters w_t, the mini-batch loss L(w_t), the pre-conditioned gradient g_t^optim from any base optimizer (SGD, AdamW, LoRA, etc.), and two additional forward-pass losses evaluated at w_t ± η_{t-1}·g_t^optim. It outputs a dynamically updated learning rate η_t (blended with the previous rate via a smoothing coefficient γ) and the next parameter vector w_{t+1}. The two extra forward passes are activation-free (torch.no_grad()), so they incur no additional memory cost beyond the base optimizer, and the entire overhead is amortized by computing η* only every Φ iterations.

## Method

GeN derives the optimal learning rate from the second-order Taylor expansion of the loss, yielding the closed form η* = G_t^T g_t^optim / (g_t^optim)^T H_t g_t^optim, which reduces to the classical Newton step when g_t^optim = H_t^{-1}G_t. Rather than computing the Hessian or its vector product (which is unsupported in distributed frameworks like DeepSpeed and FSDP), the method estimates the two scalar quantities G^T g and g^T H g by fitting a quadratic to three loss values at {w_t − η_{t-1}g, w_t, w_t + η_{t-1}g}, requiring only two extra forward passes. A validity gate (A* > 0, b* > 0, R² > 0.99) ensures the quadratic fit is reliable before updating the rate, and a lazy frequency Φ amortizes the forward-pass cost so that GeN runs at >92% the speed of the base optimizer for Φ ≥ 8. The approach is scale-invariant for the SGD variant (no gradient clipping needed) and extends naturally to parameter-efficient fine-tuning, where the frozen-parameter structure further reduces the relative overhead.

## Results

On image classification, GeN-SGD (ResNet50) achieves 96.76% on CIFAR-10, 84.77% on CIFAR-100, and 54.24% on Places365, while GeN-AdamW (ViT-base) reaches 98.68% on CIFAR-10, 92.62% on CIFAR-100, and 66.28% on iNaturalist 2021. On the E2E NLG task, GeN-AdamW with GPT2+LoRA achieves BLEU 67.30, NIST 8.65, and CIDEr 2.28, outperforming all heuristic schedulers on 3 of 5 metrics. On GLUE, GeN matches or slightly exceeds published LoRA/BitFit/FT results across all 7 tasks. For object detection, GeN-SGD with Mask R-CNN achieves AP 0.805 on Penn-Fudan.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeN-SGD consistently outperforms Prodigy and D-adapt SGD across all 7 image classification datasets, with the largest gaps on CIFAR-100 (+4.4% over Prodigy, +3.8% over D-adapt) and Food101 (+1.7% over Prodigy, +1.9% over D-adapt). GeN-AdamW beats Prodigy on 6 of 7 datasets (losing only on CIFAR-10 by 0.24%) and D-adapt AdamW on all 7, with the most dramatic advantage on CIFAR-100 (+4.5% over D-adapt) and Places365 (+2.6% over Prodigy). On the E2E NLG task, GeN-AdamW achieves the best BLEU (67.30 vs. 66.85 for linear decay) and CIDEr (2.28 vs. 2.26), though it trails linear decay on ROUGE-L (67.09 vs. 67.78) and constant LR on METEOR (0.4407 vs. 0.4462). On GLUE, GeN with LoRA, BitFit, and full-parameter training matches or marginally exceeds the published results from Hu et al. and Zaken et al. across all 7 tasks. For recommendation (MovieLens-1M), GeN-Adam at batch size 1024 outperforms the constant-LR baseline at the same batch size (Recall@10: 0.821 vs. 0.796) and matches the constant-LR result at batch size 128 (Recall@10: 0.821 vs. 0.820).

## Relation to Prior Work

GeN generalizes the local quadratic approximation (LQA) of Zhu et al. (2021) and QLABGrad (Fu & Wu, 2024), which derived the same optimal learning rate formula but were restricted to SGD. The key conceptual departure is working with an arbitrary pre-conditioned gradient g^optim rather than the raw gradient, which unifies Newton's method as a sub-case (Proposition 3.3) and makes the approach compatible with Adam, AdamW, LoRA, and any future optimizer. Computationally, GeN replaces the Hessian-vector product (used by ADLER and unsupported in distributed frameworks like DeepSpeed/FSDP) with a few additional forward passes, trading a small amortized cost for universal implementability. The paper also positions itself against parameter-free methods (D-adaptation, Prodigy, DoG, DoWG) that estimate a global quantity like ‖w₀−w*‖, arguing that GeN's local Hessian-informed estimate is more principled for non-convex deep learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Learning rate scheduling remains one of the most labor-intensive and fragile aspects of large-scale training, with state-of-the-art models requiring multi-hyperparameter schedulers tuned per architecture and dataset. GeN offers a drop-in replacement that is optimizer-agnostic, requires no Hessian storage, and adds negligible overhead when amortized (Φ≥8), making it attractive for production pipelines. However, the convergence guarantee is local (quadratic near the minimum under convexity and Lipschitz conditions), and the paper acknowledges that global convergence speed in the non-convex regime remains uncharacterized. Open questions include whether the quadratic approximation stays valid in the large-batch, small-learning-rate regime of frontier LLMs, and how GeN interacts with more complex training dynamics like mixed precision, gradient accumulation, and multi-stage pre-training.
