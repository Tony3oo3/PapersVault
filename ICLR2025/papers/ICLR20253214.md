## Problem

Existing I2I generative model unlearning methods frame the task as a single-objective optimization, yielding one fixed solution that ignores the practical need for flexible trade-offs between unlearning completeness and model utility. This approach also lacks theoretical convergence guarantees and suffers from objective conflict when two competing goals are merged into a single weighted loss. The paper is the first to study controllable unlearning, providing a family of Pareto-optimal solutions parameterized by a user-selectable coefficient ε.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253214/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained I2I model (MAE at 224×224, VQ-GAN or diffusion model at 256×256) together with forget and retain image sets, where unlearning is operationalized as the model's inability to reconstruct a full image from a partially cropped version (e.g., 50% center crop). The forget-set loss f1 is the L2 distance between the unlearned model's output and the original model's output on Gaussian noise samples, while the retain-set loss f2 is the L2 distance between the unlearned and original model outputs on retain images. The output is a continuous family of unlearned model parameters indexed by ε, each guaranteed Pareto optimal within a valid range determined by two boundary solutions found in Phase I.

## Method

The paper reformulates I2I unlearning as an ε-constrained optimization: minimize the retain-set utility loss f2(θ) subject to the forget-set unlearning loss f1(θ) ≤ ε, where Lemma 1 bounds the KL divergence to justify replacing the intractable distributional objective with an L2 surrogate against Gaussian noise. A gradient-based SQP variant solves this by computing the update direction g_t as the solution to a convex QP subproblem, with a control function ψ(θ) substituting for the intractable constraint residual f1(θ)−ε. The framework operates in two phases: Phase I identifies two Pareto-optimal boundary points (maximum and minimum unlearning completeness) by setting ε to the infimum of f1 or f2, establishing the valid ε range; Phase II sweeps ε within this range to trace the Pareto front. Propositions 1 and 2 guarantee Pareto optimality of all solutions within the valid range under convexity assumptions, and a continuous-time convergence analysis shows the exponent δ in ψ(θ) governs the trade-off between convergence speed of f1 and f2.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with 50% center cropping, the proposed method (highest unlearning completeness boundary) achieves the strongest forget-set degradation across all three I2I models: on MAE, IS drops from 21.59 to 12.33 and CLIP from 0.88 to 0.69; on VQ-GAN, IS drops from 23.74 to 0.75 and CLIP from 0.78 to 0.46; on the diffusion model, IS drops from 16.90 to 11.84 and CLIP from 0.89 to 0.55. Retain-set performance shows moderate degradation (e.g., MAE IS R: 21.83→17.47, CLIP R: 0.88→0.75), and the controllable framework (Table 2) recovers retain-set metrics progressively as ε increases, reaching near-original performance at the lowest-completeness boundary (MAE IS R: 22.92, CLIP R: 0.84).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA Composite Loss (Li et al., 2024a), the proposed method achieves strictly better forget-set unlearning on all three models: MAE IS F 12.33 vs 13.96, VQ-GAN IS F 0.75 vs 14.34, and Diffusion IS F 11.84 vs 14.33, while maintaining comparable or better retain-set CLIP scores (MAE 0.75 vs 0.70, VQ-GAN 0.82 vs 0.77, Diffusion 0.81 vs 0.80). Compared to Max Loss (Warnecke et al., 2023), Ours yields substantially stronger unlearning (VQ-GAN IS F: 0.75 vs 19.20) with less retain-set degradation (MAE IS R: 17.47 vs 16.55). Against Retain Label (Kong & Chaudhuri, 2023), Ours dominates on both objectives (MAE IS F: 12.33 vs 20.74; MAE IS R: 17.47 vs 14.14). Versus Noisy Label (Graves et al., 2021), Ours achieves better forget-set separation (VQ-GAN IS F: 0.75 vs 15.95) with similar retain-set utility. The key trade-off is that at the highest-completeness boundary, retain-set FID degrades more than Composite Loss (e.g., VQ-GAN FID R: 139.21 vs 37.92), but the controllable framework recovers this by relaxing ε, achieving near-original retain performance at the lowest-completeness boundary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Li et al. (ICLR 2024), which first formulated I2I generative model unlearning as a single-objective optimization with a weighted composite loss over forget and retain sets. The key departure is reframing the problem as a bi-objective optimization (drawing on Kim & De Weck's Pareto front generation framework) and solving it via ε-constrained optimization, replacing the heuristic weighted sum with a principled constrained formulation. The gradient-based solver and convergence analysis build on the dynamic barrier approach of Gong et al. (NeurIPS 2021), adapting its KKT-function machinery to the unlearning setting with a control function ψ(θ) that handles the unknown feasible range of ε during optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The controllable Pareto front of solutions addresses a practical gap: different stakeholders (regulators, users, service providers) may require different trade-offs between unlearning completeness and model utility, and prior methods offered only a single fixed point. The theoretical Pareto optimality guarantee is a meaningful step beyond the heuristic weighted-sum approach, though the convexity assumptions in Propositions 1 and 2 limit applicability to deep networks where non-convexity is the norm. Open questions include whether the L2 surrogate for high-dimensional KL divergence is sufficient, whether the framework extends cleanly to text-to-image models (the authors suggest it can but do not demonstrate), and how the method behaves when the forget set is large or semantically diverse.
