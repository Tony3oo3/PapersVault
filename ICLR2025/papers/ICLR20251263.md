## Problem

While it is known that language models compress their inputs onto low-dimensional manifolds, the evolution of intrinsic dimension (ID) across layers and its relationship to linguistic processing remained uncharacterized. Specifically, it was unclear whether ID fluctuations over layers correspond to meaningful functional transitions, whether they generalize across architectures, or whether they relate to model quality.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes five pre-trained decoder-only transformers (~7B parameters, 32 layers, 4096 hidden dimension) and feeds them 20-token sequences sampled from three in-distribution corpora (Bookcorpus, The Pile, WikiText-103), using only the last token's hidden state at each layer (the sole representation containing full-sequence information under causal attention). Outputs include per-layer GRIDE ID estimates with explicit scale analysis, directional Information Imbalance (Δ) values between all layer pairs, per-layer probing accuracies on five linguistic tasks, per-layer downstream classification accuracies on sentiment and toxicity, and cross-model Δ heatmaps. Shuffled-token versions of each corpus serve as a control condition.

## Method

The paper estimates the intrinsic dimension of each layer's representational manifold using GRIDE (a nonlinear, scale-aware estimator extending TwoNN), performing an explicit scale analysis per model-corpus combination to select a stable plateau. It then quantifies cross-layer neighborhood reconfiguration using Information Imbalance (Δ), a rank-based, non-commutative measure that captures directional information containment between layers. Across all five models and three corpora, a consistent high-ID peak emerges in intermediate layers (roughly layers 6–20, except OPT at 17–23), which is absent for shuffled text and untrained checkpoints. The peak coincides with: a sharp loss of surface-form information (sentence length, word content), the first attainment of asymptotic accuracy on syntactic and semantic probes, the first viable transfer to downstream classification, and a concentration of low cross-model Δ values indicating shared representational geometry. Earlier peak onset significantly correlates with lower next-token surprisal (Spearman ρ=0.65, p=0.01), linking the geometric phase to overall LM quality.

## Results

Across five ~7B-parameter causal LMs (OPT, Llama-3, Pythia, OLMo, Mistral) and three corpora (Bookcorpus, Pile, WikiText-103), all models exhibit a central high-intrinsic-dimensionality (ID) phase peaking at O(10) (vs. hidden dimension 4096 ≈ O(10³)), typically spanning layers 6–20 (OPT: layers 17–23). The ID peak onset significantly predicts LM quality: Spearman ρ = 0.65 (p = 0.01) between peak onset and next-token surprisal, and a marginal inverse correlation between maximum ID and surprisal (ρ = −0.46, p = 0.09). At the ID peak, surface-form probing accuracy drops while syntactic/semantic probing and downstream transfer (sentiment, toxicity) accuracy converge, and cross-model Δ (Information Imbalance) values are lowest, indicating shared representational geometry across architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary representational similarity measure is Information Imbalance (Δ, Glielmo et al. 2022), which is compared against three alternatives: neighborhood overlap (Doimo et al. 2020), Representational Similarity Analysis (Kriegeskorte et al. 2008), and linear CKA (Kornblith et al. 2019). Of these, only linear CKA produced interpretable results broadly coherent with Δ (Appendix H), confirming that cross-model ID-peak intersections correspond to high-similarity regions. Neighborhood overlap and RSA did not yield interpretable patterns. The key advantage of Δ over CKA is its non-commutativity (Δ(A→B) ≠ Δ(B→A)), which captures directional information containment—for example, Pythia and OPT (the two highest-ID models) directionally contain other models' representations at the peak, while CKA, being symmetric, cannot express this asymmetry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Valeriani et al. (2023), who first characterized ID evolution across layers in vision and protein transformers with a preliminary single-LM analysis, by systematically studying five ~7B decoder-only LMs across three corpora and linking the ID peak to functional linguistic processing. It draws on the broader intrinsic-dimension literature in neural networks (Ansuini et al., 2019; Cai et al., 2021; Cheng et al., 2023) and the manifold hypothesis, using GRIDE for nonlinear ID estimation and Information Imbalance (Glielmo et al., 2022) to quantify directional information containment between layers. A key departure from Valeriani et al. is the finding that the high-ID peak, rather than the low-ID valley between peaks, marks the onset of abstract semantic and syntactic processing in language transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a consistent, cross-model high-ID phase as the locus of core linguistic abstraction provides a geometric blueprint for practical operations like layer selection for fine-tuning, model pruning, and model stitching. The correlation between earlier ID-peak onset and lower surprisal (ρ=0.65, p=0.01) suggests ID profiles could serve as lightweight proxies for model quality. However, the evidence remains correlational; the authors explicitly call for causal ablation studies and dimensionality-constrained training to establish whether the ID peak is necessary for linguistic processing. An open question is why high dimensionality aids generalization in language (expressivity for few-shot abstraction) while low dimensionality aids it in vision (noise robustness and linear separability).
