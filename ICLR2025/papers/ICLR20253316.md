## Problem

While functional localization has identified a handful of broad visuo-semantic categories in the brain (faces, places, bodies), the full spectrum of visual semantics remains largely unexplored, and combining fMRI data across participants to discover new shared categories is hindered by the lack of anatomical alignment and shared stimuli. This paper asks whether a data-driven, cross-participant approach can systematically uncover new and refine existing visuo-semantic representations without requiring spatial warping or identical stimulus sets.

## Input / Output

The method takes single-trial fMRI beta weights (6k–19k voxels per participant, selected by noise ceiling >8%) from the NSD dataset (8 participants viewing 30,000 COCO images) and 512-dimensional CLIP ViT-B/32 image embeddings as input. It produces participant-specific decoded CLIP embeddings, cross-participant SDC clusters with their centroids, and for each cluster a set of positive and negative representative images (most and least associated with the cluster centroid) along with voxel localizations on cortical surfaces. Notably, the negative representative images—those most associated with the negated centroid—provide complementary interpretive signal, e.g., revealing that a face cluster's negative images show people whose faces are obscured.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline: (1) training participant-specific linear decoders that map fMRI responses to CLIP embeddings via InfoNCE contrastive loss (τ=0.03, noise augmentation σ=0.1, 50 random restarts averaged), and (2) applying a novel cross-participant DBSCAN variant to the decoder weight vectors, where each 512-dim weight vector w_i^(k) is treated as a CLIP-space 'brain-decodable concept' for voxel i in participant k. The key DBSCAN modification redefines minPts as minNeighbors, requiring a core point to have neighbors from at least minNeighbors *other* participants within its ε-neighborhood, so that core points inherently identify concepts shared across subjects. A second modification adds a within-participant expansion step (ε_expansion = min(ε+0.05, 0.65)) to grow cluster boundaries per participant. Clusters are organized hierarchically over a grid of ε values (0.8 to 0.4, step 0.001) inspired by HDBSCAN, and interpreted by retrieving images nearest to the cluster centroid and its negation in brain-decoded CLIP space.

## Results

The contrastive decoder outperforms the ridge regression baseline across all top-k accuracy values on held-out NSD test data (Figure 12). Compared to BrainCLIP (Liu et al., 2023), which reports mean top-k accuracy across 4 subjects on 982 test images, the proposed contrastive decoder achieves higher accuracy for all reported k values despite using a slightly larger test set. The primary qualitative results identify multiple Shared Decodable Concepts (SDCs) including faces (cluster 88, ε=0.593), food/color (cluster 114, ε=0.555), legs (cluster 106, ε=0.569), hands (cluster 89, ε=0.593), horizontal/vertical orientation (clusters 60/65, ε≈0.66), numerosity (cluster 111, ε=0.563), and close/far visual perspective (cluster 67, ε=0.654), each with consistent voxel localizations across 4–8 of 8 participants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed contrastive decoder against two baselines. First, against a ridge regression decoder trained on the same NSD data (with λ selected via grid search over {0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1000, 10000, 100000}), the contrastive decoder achieves higher top-k accuracy across all values of k, confirming that the InfoNCE contrastive objective is better suited for mapping brain responses to the high-dimensional CLIP embedding space. Second, against BrainCLIP (Liu et al., 2023), which uses a similar CLIP-based decoding framework but reports results on only 4 subjects (1, 2, 5, 7) with 982 test images, the proposed method achieves higher top-k accuracy for all reported k values while evaluating on all 8 NSD participants with a larger held-out test set. The paper does not report a head-to-head comparison on identical test conditions, so the comparison is approximate but favorable to the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DBSCAN/HDBSCAN clustering lineage by introducing a cross-participant variant that redefines the core-point threshold (minNeighbors) to require representation across multiple subjects, and adds a within-participant expansion step to grow cluster boundaries. It departs from prior fMRI clustering work (Golland et al., 2008; Lashkari et al., 2010; Thomas Yeo et al., 2011) and functional alignment methods (Shared Response Model, Hyperalignment) that require anatomical warping or identical stimulus sequences, by casting each voxel into CLIP's shared multimodal embedding space, thereby sidestepping the alignment problem entirely. The contrastive decoding objective (InfoNCE) extends the BrainCLIP line of work (Liu et al., 2023) by framing the decoder as a linear map from brain responses to a frozen CLIP space rather than a generative reconstruction model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, alignment-free route to discovering and localizing visuo-semantic selectivity across participants, and the negative-image analysis offers a stronger interpretive signal than positive-only decoding (e.g., revealing that FFA activity dips when a face is expected but occluded, or that a putative 'food area' is better characterized as a color-selective region). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond vision to other sensory modalities, whether CLIP's representational biases mask or distort neural selectivity, and whether the hierarchical DBSCAN grid adequately captures concepts in regions of uniform density. The limited participant count (n=8) and single-dataset scope (NSD) constrain generalizability, and the authors note the method complements rather than replaces hypothesis-driven experiments.
