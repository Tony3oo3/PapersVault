## Problem

Discovering causal structures with latent variables from observational data is limited by existing methods that rely on iterative discrete searches (hurting scalability) and restrictive assumptions such as linearity or the requirement that latent variables be deterministic invertible functions of measured variables. No differentiable causal discovery method existed for non-linear latent hierarchical models, which appear in gene regulatory networks, image data, and epidemiology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251145/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes purely observational data—either tabular measured variables (synthetic) or images (MNIST at 28×28, CMNIST downsampled to 14×14, CelebA downsampled to 64×64)—and outputs a block upper-triangular binary adjacency matrix M encoding the causal DAG up to permutation of latent variables, along with hierarchical latent representations organized in layers. The number of latent variables is learned automatically: rows of M are allowed to collapse to zero, so the model need not be pre-specified in the number of latent nodes per layer.

## Method

The theoretical core replaces the linear cross-covariance rank indicator with the rank of the Jacobian of E[y|x], proving that this rank equals the minimum number of latent variables d-separating two measured sets (Theorem 1), under only generalized faithfulness and differentiability—eliminating the prior requirement that latent variables be deterministic functions of measurements. For learning, the method formulates a VAE whose decoder implements the structural equations parameterized by the adjacency matrix M, which is relaxed via Gumbel-softmax to enable gradient-based optimization. The training objective combines the ELBO, an L1 sparsity penalty on M, a MINE-based independence loss on exogenous noise, and a differentiable structural regularizer enforcing the two-pure-children condition per latent variable. The block upper-triangular parameterization of M inherently enforces the equal-path-length condition, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end in a single optimization pass rather than the O(ln²) sequential model training required by prior iterative methods.

## Results

On four synthetic causal graph structures (trees and v-structures with LeakyReLU/Tanh non-linearities), the proposed method achieves a mean F1 score of 0.95–0.97 and Structural Hamming Distance (SHD) of 0.67–1.17, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the CMNIST domain-transfer task, the learned latent representations yield 97.9% test accuracy on the reversed color-digit mapping and 75.3% on the unseen blue-color test set. On CelebA (blonde-hair prediction under reversed gender correlation), the method achieves 0.823 AUC, outperforming Graph VAE (0.500) and Causal VAE (0.729).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all four synthetic-graph baselines on both SHD and F1. Against KONG (NeurIPS 2023), the closest non-linear competitor, the gap is 3.2–7.0 in SHD and 0.16–0.36 in F1; KONG requires training O(ln²) generative models, making it significantly slower. HUANG (NeurIPS 2022) and GIN (NeurIPS 2020) are linear methods that fail to recover non-linear structures, with GIN achieving near-zero F1 (0.00–0.36) across all runs. DeCAMFounder (JRSS-B 2023) only discovers edges among observed variables, yielding zero F1 and the highest SHD (11.8–18.5). On the CMNIST downstream task, the method achieves 97.9% accuracy on the reversed-mapping test set, beating Causal VAE (91.6%) and Graph VAE (66.5%); however, on the unseen blue-color test set, Graph VAE slightly edges out the proposed method (76.6% vs. 75.3%). On CelebA, the method achieves 0.823 AUC, clearly surpassing both Causal VAE (0.729) and Graph VAE (0.500).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent hierarchical causal discovery line initiated by Huang et al. (2022) and Kong et al. (2023) by replacing the linear cross-covariance rank indicator with the Jacobian rank of E[y|x], thereby generalizing identifiability to non-linear structural equations without requiring latent variables to be deterministic invertible functions of measured variables. Methodologically, it departs from the iterative discrete search paradigm used by all prior latent hierarchical methods (which require O(ln²) model trainings) by formulating the problem as a continuous optimization over a Gumbel-softmax-relaxed adjacency matrix within a VAE framework, drawing on the differentiable causal discovery tradition (NOTEARS, DAG-GNN) but extending it to the latent hierarchical setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Jacobian rank characterization (Theorem 1) provides a principled, distribution-level indicator of d-separation that works in the non-linear regime, potentially inspiring broader applications beyond hierarchical models. The differentiable formulation offers a clear scalability advantage over iterative methods, demonstrated by learning a 62-node latent graph on MNIST. However, the structural conditions (two pure children per latent, no measured-variable children, equal path lengths) remain restrictive, and the method's performance on the synthetic benchmarks is validated only on small graphs (7–13 variables). Open questions include whether Condition 3 (differentiability) is truly necessary, whether the approach scales to hundreds of variables, and how it performs when the pure-children condition is only approximately satisfied.
