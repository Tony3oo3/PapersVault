## Problem

Existing personality induction methods for LLMs are either prompt-based (unstable and highly sensitive to prompt design) or training-based (requiring substantial compute and labeled data), and neither provides fine-grained, precise control over nuanced personality traits. This paper addresses the gap by identifying which specific neurons in a model's feed-forward networks govern personality traits and manipulating them at inference time to induce desired traits without any parameter modification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253065/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a situational question (a real-world scenario designed to elicit trait-specific responses) as a text prompt and produces a free-form generated response that exhibits the target personality trait. During neuron identification, the model processes 180,000 such questions from PERSONALITYBENCH, and the activation probability of each neuron is computed as the fraction of generated tokens where that neuron's post-activation value exceeds zero. During inference, the manipulation modifies neuron values in-place within the FFN's GLU computation (specifically the σ(hW₁) ⊙ (hW₃) term) before the final projection W₂, so the output is a standard autoregressive text generation with no architectural change.

## Method

NPTI identifies personality-related neurons by computing the activation probability difference δ = Pr_i^{t+} − Pr_i^{t−} between opposing aspects of a Big Five trait (e.g., extroverted vs. introverted) across 36,000 situational questions per trait; neurons with δ > 10% are labeled positive (P_t⁺) and those with δ < −10% are labeled negative (P_t⁻). To induce a positive shift in trait t, the method amplifies positive neurons by adding γ·a₉₅·f(δ) to their original activation (where a₉₅ is the 95th percentile of that neuron's historical activations and f is a sigmoid-weighted function of |δ|), while simultaneously deactivating negative neurons by clamping their values to min(0, n_ori); the negative shift reverses these operations. This manipulation is applied at inference time across all layers using vLLM, requiring no gradient updates or weight changes, and the weighted sigmoid function ensures that neurons with larger activation differences receive proportionally larger modifications to preserve fluency.

## Results

On LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, NPTI achieves an average personality score of 9.43 (out of 10) with variance 0.49 across the five Big Five traits, matching P² on mean (9.43) while halving its variance (0.49 vs. 0.83). NPTI significantly outperforms PAS (6.93 mean, 1.71 variance) and ActAdd (8.20 mean, 2.10 variance) on all traits. It approaches the supervised fine-tuning upper bound (SFT: 9.61 mean, 0.49 variance) without any parameter training, and in human evaluation achieves the best overall average rank (2.27) among all non-training methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against P² (Jiang et al., 2024), NPTI ties on average mean (9.43) but achieves substantially lower variance (0.49 vs. 0.83), indicating more consistent trait expression; NPTI wins on Conscientiousness, Extroversion, and Neuroticism while P² leads on Openness (9.21 vs. 8.50) and Agreeableness (9.68 vs. 9.64). Against PAS (Zhu et al., 2024), NPTI dominates across all five traits, with an average mean gap of 2.5 points (9.43 vs. 6.93) and variance nearly 3.5× lower (0.49 vs. 1.71). Against ActAdd (Turner et al., 2023), NPTI again leads on every trait, with an average mean advantage of 1.23 points (9.43 vs. 8.20) and variance reduced from 2.10 to 0.49. Compared to the SFT upper bound, NPTI trails by only 0.18 in average mean (9.43 vs. 9.61) while matching its variance exactly (0.49), all without modifying model parameters or requiring training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

NPTI repurposes the neuron-manipulation paradigm established for factual knowledge editing (Dai et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2022) and language switching (Tang et al., 2024) toward the distinct goal of personality trait induction in LLMs. It departs from prior personality-induction work (prompt-based: Jiang et al. 2024; training-based: Pan & Zeng 2023; attention-head probing: Zhu et al. 2024) by operating at the FFN neuron level and, crucially, by identifying neurons through activation *difference* between opposing trait aspects rather than absolute activation magnitude. The generative PERSONALITYBENCH benchmark also breaks from the multiple-choice evaluation paradigm (BFI, MBTI) that dominates prior personality assessment of LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NPTI offers a training-free, parameter-preserving route to fine-grained personality control that matches supervised fine-tuning in quality while avoiding the cost and fragility of prompt engineering. The 180K-instance generative benchmark addresses a real gap in evaluation robustness over multiple-choice formats. Open questions include whether the identified neurons transfer across model sizes or require re-identification per architecture, how the method scales to larger models (tested only up to 9B), and whether combined multi-trait manipulation remains stable over long generations. The observation that conscientiousness+ activation *improves* general reasoning while neuroticism+ degrades it most suggests personality neurons are not fully orthogonal to task-relevant circuits, a tension that future work should resolve.
