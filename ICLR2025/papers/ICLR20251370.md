## Problem

Despite widespread empirical evidence that supervised models learn linearly decodable, interpretable representations (neural analogy-making, the linear representation hypothesis, superposition), no comprehensive theory explains why cross-entropy-based classification recovers ground-truth latent structure. Existing identifiability results from nonlinear ICA apply to self-supervised learning but do not cover the dominant supervised setting, leaving a gap between what is observed in practice and what is theoretically guaranteed.

## Input / Output

The method operates on data samples x generated by an injective generator g from latent variables z on the unit hypersphere S^{d-1}, where z is drawn from a von Mises-Fisher distribution centered at a class-specific cluster vector v_c. In the DIET/PID setting, each sample carries a unique instance label i; in the supervised setting, samples carry a semantic class label c. The model produces a d-dimensional latent representation z̃ = f(x) and a linear, bias-free classification head W whose rows w_i (or w_c) identify the cluster vectors. The encoder f can be an MLP or CNN (ResNet-18/50, ViT-B-16), and the latent dimensionality d is typically 512 or 2048 in experiments.

## Method

The paper proposes a cluster-centric data generating process where semantic classes are represented by unit-norm cluster vectors on a hypersphere, and samples within each class are drawn from a vMF distribution around the corresponding cluster vector. The key theoretical result (Thm. 1) proves that globally minimizing the DIET cross-entropy objective (a parametric instance discrimination task with a linear classification head) identifies both the latent variables and the cluster vectors up to orthogonal or affine linear transformations, depending on normalization constraints. The crucial extension (Thm. 2) shows that standard supervised classification is a special case of DIET where instance labels coincide with class labels, so the same identifiability guarantee applies: the composition h = f∘g is a linear map from S^{d-1} to R^d. The paper further establishes a genealogy (GCL → TCL → DIET → supervised classification) unifying these methods under a single cross-entropy framework, and validates the theory on synthetic data, DisLib disentanglement benchmarks, and ImageNet-X proxy factors.

## Results

On synthetic data matching the cluster-centric DGP (vMF, κ=10, d=5, |C|=100), DIET achieves R² = 98.6% for latent variable recovery and 99.9% for cluster vector recovery, confirming linear identifiability. On the DisLib benchmark, trained CNNs (ResNet18) recover latent factors with Pearson correlations up to 1.00 (category in dSprites, Shapes3D, smallNORB) and 0.83–0.99 for continuous factors (position, scale, orientation), substantially outperforming raw input decoding. On ImageNet-X, linear decoders on pretrained ResNet50 and ViT-b-16 representations predict proxy factors of variation significantly above the 50% chance level, with ResNet50 (d=2048) slightly outperforming ViT (d=768).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing representation learning methods but instead validates its identifiability theory against three baselines: raw input (x), randomly initialized model representations, and two trained architectures (3-layer MLP vs. ResNet18 CNN). Trained models consistently and substantially outperform raw input across all DisLib datasets—for example, on MPI 3D category, the CNN achieves 0.97 Pearson correlation versus 0.03 from the input alone. The CNN generally outperforms the MLP on continuous factors (e.g., 0.99 vs. 0.89 on Shapes3D objSize), attributed to the CNN's inductive bias for images, though the MLP occasionally matches or exceeds the CNN (e.g., 0.98 vs. 0.92 on dSprites scale). On ImageNet-X, both ResNet50 and ViT-b-16 significantly exceed the 50% chance-level baseline (Bonferroni-adjusted p < 0.05/85), with ResNet50 showing slightly higher decoding accuracy, likely due to its larger latent space (d=2048 vs. d=768). Some factors (e.g., 'darker'/'brighter') are decodable even from untrained models or raw input, indicating they are trivially present in pixel space, while shape-related factors require the learned nonlinear representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper constructs a 'genealogy' of cross-entropy-based classification methods, showing that Generalized Contrastive Learning (GCL) → Time-Contrastive Learning (TCL) → DIET (parametric instance discrimination) → supervised classification form a nested hierarchy of special cases. The key conceptual departure from prior identifiability work (Hyvarinen et al., 2019; Zimmermann et al., 2021) is the introduction of a cluster-centric DGP using von Mises-Fisher distributions around class vectors, which replaces the uniform marginal assumption and models the clustered structure of real data. By proving that supervised classification is simply DIET with class labels substituted for instance labels, the paper bridges the previously separate literatures on SSL identifiability and supervised representation learning, showing that the cross-entropy loss alone is sufficient to invert the DGP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a theoretical explanation for the 'unreasonable effectiveness' of supervised learning—why simple cross-entropy classification recovers linearly decodable, transferable representations that underpin phenomena like neural analogy-making and the linear representation hypothesis. Key limitations include the restrictive vMF assumption (which may not hold for non-Euclidean latent structures like object pose on SO(3)), the uniform class label assumption, and empirical validation limited to proxy factors on ImageNet-X rather than true latent variables. Open questions include whether identifiability extends to imbalanced classes, non-vMF conditionals in practice, and whether the results scale to the full complexity of natural image generation without the injective generator assumption.
