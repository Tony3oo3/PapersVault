## Problem

Diffusion-based field models (e.g., DPF) represent data as coordinate-signal pairs and learn to denoise them, but their uniform sampling strategy over sparse subsets of pairs fails to capture fine-grained local structure in high-resolution, dynamic data (e.g., a 5-second 240p video contains ~10M pairs, yet only ~1% can be observed per training step). This leads to blurry outputs and poor long-context generation, while modality-specific architectures cannot be easily transferred across tasks like video, 3D, and game generation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes coordinate-signal pairs where coordinates are modality-specific: 2D spatial for images, 3D spatiotemporal for video, 6D camera pose + intrinsics for 3D views, and 4D action + spatiotemporal for games. Signals live in a 16×-downsampled latent space (not raw pixels), and conditioning is provided via T5-XXL text embeddings (256 tokens), a sliding window of past frames (16 for games, 8 for video), and one-hot action vectors. Output is the denoised latent signal at each coordinate, decoded back to pixel space to form the final frame or view.

## Method

Field-DiT replaces uniform pair sampling with a view-wise sampling algorithm that restricts attention to coordinate-signal pairs belonging to a single or several (n) consecutive views (frames or rendered images), preserving local structure while reducing interaction complexity. To handle the memory cost of high-resolution views, signals are compressed into a 16×-downsampled latent space, and the score network is a decoder-only DiT-XL-sized transformer (28 blocks, 1152 hidden dim, 16 heads) operating on these latent tokens. Global geometry is recovered through two complementary conditioning mechanisms: (1) text embeddings injected via adaptive layer normalization (scale/shift per channel) to enforce cross-view consistency, and (2) autoregressive generation where each new frame is conditioned on a fixed sliding window of past frames, yielding linear complexity in the number of generated frames. The model uses classifier-free guidance (scale 8.5) applied to the first three channels of each denoised token, and the same architecture is reused across all modalities with only the coordinate embedding dimension changed.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (64×64), Field-DiT achieves FID 7.29 and IS 9.31, outperforming the domain-agnostic DPF (FID 15.10) and even the domain-specific DiT (FID 7.53). On CelebV-Text (256×256×128), Field-DiT achieves FVD 42.03, FID 24.33, and CLIPSIM 0.220, substantially outperforming all domain-specific video baselines (best competitor Latte: FVD 67.97, FID 39.69). On ShapeNet-Cars (128×128×251), Field-DiT achieves PSNR 23.9 dB and SSIM 0.90, surpassing pixelNeRF (PSNR 23.2, SSIM 0.90) and ViewFormer (PSNR 19.0, SSIM 0.83), though trailing Zero-1-to-3 on FID (24.36 vs 17.90) and LPIPS (0.118 vs 0.093).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, Field-DiT (FID 7.29, IS 9.31) beats all domain-agnostic field models (DPF: FID 15.10; Functa: FID 31.56; GEM: FID 23.83) and slightly edges out the domain-specific DiT (FID 7.53, IS 8.97). On CelebV-Text video generation, Field-DiT (FVD 42.03, FID 24.33, CLIPSIM 0.220) substantially outperforms every domain-specific video baseline, with the closest competitor Latte (FVD 67.97, FID 39.69) still trailing by ~38% in FVD and ~39% in FID. On ShapeNet-Cars 3D novel view synthesis, Field-DiT achieves the best PSNR (23.9 dB) and SSIM (0.90) among all compared methods, surpassing pixelNeRF (PSNR 23.2) and ViewFormer (PSNR 19.0), but loses to Zero-1-to-3 (FID 17.90, LPIPS 0.093) and EG3D-PTI (FID 20.82) on perceptual quality metrics, which the authors attribute to FID/LPIPS prioritizing 2D visual quality over 3D consistency. For game generation (Super Mario Bros), Field-DiT maintains PSNR above 42 dB across all 100 evaluated frames, while DPF runs out of memory beyond 40% of the sequence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffusion Probabilistic Fields (DPF, Zhuang et al., 2023), which first applied diffusion models to coordinate-signal pair representations of data, and on the broader neural field lineage (SIREN, Functa, GEM) that treats data as functions over metric spaces. It departs from DPF's uniform sampling strategy by introducing view-wise sampling that restricts attention to spatially/temporally coherent subsets of pairs, and adds autoregressive generation with text and past-frame conditioning to recover the global geometry that local sampling sacrifices. Architecturally, it adopts the DiT transformer backbone (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and replaces DPF's context-query pair mechanism with a latent-space decoder, trading some per-pair fidelity for the memory efficiency needed to scale to high-resolution, long-context data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single 675M-parameter architecture, unchanged across modalities, can match or exceed domain-specific models on text-to-video (FVD 42 vs. VDM 81, CogVideo 99), 3D novel-view synthesis (PSNR 23.9, SSIM 0.90), and game next-frame prediction, suggesting a viable path toward truly unified generative models. The released Super Mario Bros game benchmark fills a gap in the literature for action-conditioned long-horizon generation. Key limitations remain: the method only applies to 'view-interpretable' modalities, multi-task training with shared weights is not yet demonstrated (each modality uses separate weights), and the comparison to the very latest video/3D models (e.g., Sora-class systems) is absent, leaving open whether this unified approach scales competitively at much larger model sizes.
