## Problem

Existing multi-view 3D object detection methods (monocular projection and NeRF-based) represent objects via discrete positional data—uniformly sampled voxels or point clouds—which fails to capture the continuous surface geometry that real objects inherently possess. While 3D Gaussian Splatting offers a natural continuous surface representation, it introduces numerous outlier Gaussians around object boundaries that distract point-based detectors, and the underdetermined nature of 2D-supervised reconstruction makes local surface descriptors unreliable. The paper asks how to exploit the surface-closure prior (objects are enclosed by closed surfaces) to improve objectness deduction and proposal refinement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view posed RGB images, which are first reconstructed into 3D Gaussian Splatting via SfM initialization and 30K iterations of 3DGS+SuGaR training; Gaussians outside the room cuboid or with opacity < 0.3 are filtered, yielding ~40K input Gaussians. The network trims these to 1024 candidate Gaussians with 256-dim features via a PointNet++ backbone, then outputs initial and refined 3D oriented bounding boxes (center, dimensions, heading angle) with objectness classification scores. The refined proposals are produced by adding an MLP-predicted 9-dim offset (2 objectness, 1 heading, 6 position/size) to the initial proposals.

## Method

Gaussian-Det reformulates each 3D Gaussian as a surface descriptor (position, surface normal from the smallest-eigenvalue eigenvector of the covariance, and largest cross-section area), then initializes object proposals with a VoteNet-style grouping head. The key contribution is the Closure Inferring Module (CIM), which operates in two stages: (1) Partial Surface Inference adds a variational residual F^V (sampled from N(0,I)) to candidate features via an ELBO-trained VAE to model the underdetermined uncertainty of Gaussian Splatting's local surface estimates; (2) Holistic Surface Closure Coalescence computes the numerical flux Φ̂_k = Σ T·n_i·A_i of a constant vector field over all candidate Gaussians within each proposal, then scales the pooled holistic feature by S_k = exp(−γ_k|Φ̂_k|), where γ_k is a learnable flexibility coefficient—lower |Φ̂_k| (better closure) yields higher S_k, supporting the proposal, while higher |Φ̂_k| suppresses it. The final proposal feature concatenates partial and holistic representations, and a stacked MLP refines the bounding box parameters. Training uses a combined loss of the VAE reconstruction/approximation term plus L2 losses on both initial and refined proposals against ground-truth boxes.

## Results

On 3D-FRONT, Gaussian-Det achieves AP25=96.7 and AP50=77.7, outperforming the strongest point-cloud baseline G-BRNet (AP25=88.2, AP50=71.0) by +8.5% and +6.7% respectively, and surpassing the best NeRF-based method NeRF-MAE (AP25=85.3, AP50=63.0) by +11.4% and +14.7%. On the more challenging real-world ScanNet dataset, Gaussian-Det attains AP25=71.7 and AP50=24.5, leading all baselines by +4.9% and +4.1% on AP. The only metric where Gaussian-Det does not lead is AR25 on ScanNet (87.3 vs. 92.0 for NeRF-MAE), while it matches or exceeds all methods on AR50 (43.0). Inference speed is 33.3 FPS with a 24.0 MB model, roughly 20× faster than NeRF-RPN (1.51 FPS, 312.7 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gaussian-Det outperforms all 13 baselines on every AP metric across both datasets. On 3D-FRONT, the closest competitor is G-BRNet (a point-cloud detector fed with Gaussian centers), which trails by +8.5% on AP25 and +6.7% on AP50; the best NeRF-based method NeRF-MAE (with extra pre-training) lags by +11.4% on AP25. On ScanNet, Gaussian-Det beats the strongest point-cloud baseline G-VoteNet by +4.9% on AP25 and the best NeRF-based method NeRF-RPN by +16.2% on AP25. The sole exception is AR25 on ScanNet, where NeRF-MAE (92.0) and G-FCAF3D (90.2) exceed Gaussian-Det (87.3), suggesting the closure-based refinement slightly trades recall for precision at the loosest IoU threshold. The paper also shows that replacing the holistic closure module with a naive coalescence (setting S_k=1) drops AP25 by 3.2% and AP50 by 2.7%, confirming the flux-based closure prior is the key differentiator over standard point-cloud or NeRF-based detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

Gaussian-Det repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) with SuGaR surface regularization (Guédon & Lepetit, 2024) as a continuous surface representation for multi-view 3D object detection, departing from both discrete volumetric approaches (ImVoxelNet, NeRF-RPN, NeRF-MAE) and point-cloud detectors (VoteNet, BRNet) that treat Gaussians as mere positional data. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the intrinsic surface geometry of Gaussian ellipsoids—normal vectors and cross-sectional areas—to formulate a flux-based closure measurement as an objectness prior, combined with a variational residual to handle the underdetermined nature of Gaussian Splatting's local surface features. The detection backbone (PointNet++ with VoteNet-style proposal head) is standard, but the Closure Inferring Module that coalesces partial surfaces into a holistic closure-aware representation is the novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that Gaussian Splatting is a viable and efficient input modality for 3D detection, achieving 33.3 FPS and 24 MB model size versus 1.51 FPS / 312.7 MB for NeRF-RPN—a 20×+ speedup that makes real-time multi-view 3D detection practical without expensive depth sensors. The surface closure prior (flux measurement) is a simple yet effective geometric signal that addresses a real failure mode of point-based detectors: distraction by Gaussian Splatting outliers. Open questions include handling jointed objects (where combined surfaces also appear closed), severe occlusion, and scaling to outdoor/autonomous-driving scenes; the authors suggest color-based discrimination as a natural next step.
