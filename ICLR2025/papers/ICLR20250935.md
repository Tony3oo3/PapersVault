## Problem

Neural ODEs suffer from a generalization bottleneck: when the latent dynamics of a target system deviate slightly from the training distribution, prediction errors accumulate over time and performance degrades sharply. Existing remedies such as domain randomization or transfer learning improve robustness but sacrifice accuracy on the original nominal task, and large-scale retraining is typically required. The paper asks whether a real-time feedback mechanism—well-established in biological and engineering control systems—can be embedded directly into the neural ODE architecture to correct learned dynamics online without degrading prior accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequentially sampled state measurements (e.g., 12-dimensional quadrotor state [p, v, Θ, ω]ᵀ or 2D position-velocity for the spiral toy problem) at a fixed sampling interval T_s, along with external inputs such as motor thrusts. It produces a corrected latent dynamics estimate f̂_neural(t) at each step and, through a cascaded multi-step prediction scheme, outputs predicted future states over a horizon of N steps. In the MPC application, these predictions feed a receding-horizon optimizer that yields the current optimal 4-dimensional thrust vector. A key structural detail is that the feedback state x̂(t) is maintained as an auxiliary variable updated recursively, so no growing history of past measurements needs to be stored.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-DOF feedback neural network that augments a standard Neural ODE with a real-time correction loop: the learned dynamics f_neural(t) is modified by L(x(t) − x̂(t)) (linear form) or h_neural(x(t) − x̂(t), ξ) (nonlinear neural form), where x̂(t) is an auxiliary estimate propagated by Euler integration of the corrected dynamics. A Lyapunov-based convergence theorem guarantees that the state observation error and its derivative (the dynamics error) converge to bounded sets whose radii shrink with the minimum eigenvalue of the feedback gain L. For multi-step prediction, a cascaded architecture chains one-step feedback corrections with a gain-decay schedule L_i = L ⊙ e^(−βi) to prevent noise amplification across layers. The nonlinear feedback is trained via domain randomization while the neural ODE is frozen, so the original network retains its nominal-task accuracy and only the lightweight feedback component adapts to unseen uncertainties. An alternative training procedure based on optimal-control Hamiltonian gradients (Algorithm 2–3) handles external inputs that the original adjoint method in Chen et al. (2018) does not support.

## Results

On the quadrotor Lissajous trajectory tracking task with 37.6% mass uncertainty, inertia uncertainties, drag coefficient uncertainties, and translational disturbances, the proposed FNN-MPC achieves 0.093 m RMSE, outperforming the best baseline AdapNN-MPC (0.151 m) by 38% and the nominal model Nomi-MPC (0.248 m) by 63%. On the irregular object trajectory prediction task (0.5 s horizon, 9 test trajectories), the feedback neural network achieves the lowest prediction error among all compared methods, including model-based drag fitting and standard neural ODE approaches. The multi-step prediction with gain decay further reduces noise amplification, as validated in the spiral curve ablation (Figure 5g).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed FNN-MPC (0.093 m RMSE) decisively outperforms all five baselines on the quadrotor MPC task. Against the neural ODE baseline (Neural-MPC, 0.167 m), the feedback mechanism provides a 44% RMSE reduction, attributed to real-time correction of latent dynamics under unseen parameter uncertainties and disturbances. The adaptive last-layer approach (AdapNN-MPC, 0.151 m) is the closest competitor but still trails by 38%, as it only adjusts a single weighted vector rather than correcting the full dynamics. The feedforward MLP (MLP-MPC, 0.182 m) underperforms the neural ODE due to its single-step training paradigm, confirming the importance of multi-step prediction. The feedback-enhanced nominal model (FB-MPC, 0.203 m) shows that feedback alone without learned dynamics is insufficient. For the irregular object trajectory prediction, the paper reports the feedback neural network achieves the best 0.5 s prediction accuracy over both the model-based drag-fitting method (Frese et al., 2001) and the standard neural ODE (Chen et al., 2018), though exact numerical values are presented only in Figure 7.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) by embedding a classical control-theoretic feedback loop directly into the network architecture, drawing on Kalman filtering, Luenberger observers, and ESO design principles. It departs from domain randomization (Tobin et al., 2017; Peng et al., 2018), which trades nominal-task accuracy for robustness, and from test-time adaptation methods (Cheng et al., 2019; O'Connell et al., 2022; Richards et al., 2023) that adjust only the last layer, by introducing a two-DOF structure where the feedforward neural ODE is frozen and a separate feedback network (linear or nonlinear) corrects the latent dynamics in real time. The multi-step cascaded prediction with gain decay is a novel practical mechanism not present in prior neural ODE or MPC-with-learned-dynamics work (Torrente et al., 2021; Salzmann et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-DOF formulation offers a clean separation of concerns: the neural ODE preserves accuracy on the nominal task while the feedback loop independently handles generalization, avoiding the accuracy-robustness trade-off inherent in domain randomization. The Lyapunov-based convergence guarantee (Theorem 1) under bounded residuals provides theoretical grounding that purely empirical generalization strategies lack. Key limitations include the need for manual tuning of the linear feedback gain and decay rate, the preliminary (single-toy-example) validation of the nonlinear neural feedback, and the assumption that learning residuals remain bounded. Open directions include bilevel joint optimization of the ODE and feedback parameters, extending to unbounded or non-stationary residuals, and scaling the nonlinear feedback to higher-dimensional control tasks.
