## Problem

LoRA fine-tuning of LLMs degrades their safety alignment even when trained exclusively on benign data, because the low-rank adapters perturb the internal safety features that enable the model to detect and reject harmful prompts. Existing post-hoc alignment methods (e.g., InferAligner, Vaccine, Safe LoRA) can only partially restore safety and introduce additional hyperparameters or require access to base model weights. The paper seeks a PEFT method that preserves safety alignment intrinsically during fine-tuning without external alignment steps.

## Input / Output

During training, SaLoRA consumes a small set of ~300 harmful prompts with their safe responses (70% of AdvBench) to pre-compute the fixed safety module, plus the downstream fine-tuning dataset (e.g., Alpaca or CommonsenseQA-15k) for task-specific adapter initialization and supervised training. At inference, the fine-tuned model takes a standard text prompt and produces a generated text response, with the safety module and residual weights merged into the adapter at save time so that inference cost matches vanilla LoRA.

## Method

SaLoRA augments standard LoRA with two components: (1) a fixed safety module C_S = I − U_C U_C^T, where U_C consists of the top-r_s left singular vectors of W X_h (the original layer's output features on harmful prompts), which projects all adapter updates into the subspace orthogonal to the original safety features; and (2) a task-specific initialization for the trainable adapters A_S and B_S, derived from the SVD of W and the top-r_t singular vectors of W X_t (task-specific features), balancing weight norms to improve convergence. The original weight is re-parameterized as W' = W − C_S B_S A_S so the initial model output is identical to the pre-trained model. During training, only A_S and B_S are updated while C_S and W' remain frozen, and at save time C_S is absorbed into B_S (B'_S = C_S B_S) so the stored adapter has the same size as vanilla LoRA.

## Results

On Llama-2-chat-7B fine-tuned on Alpaca (r=16), SaLoRA reduces the harmful rate from 23.7% (LoRA) to 3.5%, and further to 1.4% when combined with Self-Reminder, compared to 11.7% for LoRA+SR. On Llama-3.1-Instruct-8B (r=16), SaLoRA achieves a 2.9% harmful rate versus 11.7% for LoRA and 10.3% for LoRA+SR. On commonsense reasoning (r=16, Llama-2-chat-7B), SaLoRA attains 62.9% average accuracy, outperforming LoRA (59.2%), DoRA (61.3%), and PiSSA (61.2%), while maintaining a 0.0% harmful rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaLoRA decisively outperforms all three PEFT baselines (LoRA, DoRA, PiSSA) on safety: on Llama-2-chat-7B (r=16), SaLoRA's 3.5% harmful rate is 6.8× lower than LoRA's 23.7% and 9× lower than PiSSA's 31.7%. Against the four post-hoc alignment methods, SaLoRA also wins: it beats LoRA+Self-Reminder (11.7%), LoRA+InferAligner (13.5%), LoRA+Vaccine (20.2%), and Safe LoRA (15.7%) on the same setting. On commonsense reasoning accuracy (r=16), SaLoRA (62.9%) surpasses LoRA (59.2%) by 3.7 points, DoRA (61.3%) by 1.6 points, and PiSSA (61.2%) by 1.7 points, showing that safety preservation does not come at a utility cost. The one notable exception is Mistral-Instruct-v0.3, where SaLoRA (31.7%) still substantially improves over LoRA (82.5%) but does not fully restore the pre-fine-tuning rate of 47.8%, suggesting the model's baseline safety was already weak. When combined with Self-Reminder, SaLoRA achieves 0.0% harmful rate on Llama-3.1-Instruct-8B, the best result across all methods and models tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA family of PEFT methods (LoRA, DoRA, PiSSA) by adding a fixed orthogonal safety projection module and a task-specific SVD-based adapter initialization. It departs from prior safety-preservation approaches—Safe LoRA's projection onto the chat-base weight difference, InferAligner's inference-time feature intervention, and Vaccine's adversarial pre-training—by embedding the safety constraint directly into the adapter architecture at training time, requiring only ~300 harmful prompt-response pairs rather than access to a base model or additional training runs. The theoretical analysis builds on the low-rank safety weight space hypothesis from Wei et al. (2024) and the intrinsic dimensionality literature (Li et al., 2018; Aghajanyan et al., 2021), formalizing why benign fine-tuning gradients necessarily perturb safety regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM fine-tuning services become widely accessible (OpenAI API, HuggingFace), the demonstrated safety degradation from even benign LoRA fine-tuning poses a concrete deployment risk that model owners cannot easily monitor. SaLoRA's practical appeal lies in its minimal overhead (0.12h preprocessing, same trainable parameter count as LoRA) and its compatibility with existing inference pipelines like vLLM. Open questions include whether the fixed safety module generalizes to multi-turn conversations, whether it interacts well with RLHF-based alignment, and whether the approach extends to non-LoRA PEFT methods like adapter or prompt tuning. The doubling of adapter storage cost, while small in absolute terms, may matter for platforms hosting thousands of fine-tuned variants.
