## Problem

Traditional LLM fine-tuning is computationally expensive, static, and prone to overfitting on small datasets, making it impractical to maintain separate models for every task. Existing parameter-efficient methods like LoRA lack algebraic compositionality, require orders of magnitude more parameters, and cannot be dynamically recombined at inference time to handle unseen tasks. The paper asks whether LLMs can be made truly self-adaptive—selectively adjusting their own weights in real time for novel tasks—without full retraining.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method receives a text prompt (or image+text for VLM settings) from an unknown task, plus optionally 3–10 held-out few-shot test examples for the CEM-based adaptation strategy. The first inference pass produces either a discrete task category label (prompt/classification expert) or a continuous adapted singular-value vector z′ (few-shot CEM); the second pass then generates the final text response using the adapted weights. Notably, the few-shot examples are test-time data, not training data, and the adaptation cost is a one-time overhead per task rather than per prompt.

## Method

The core building block is Singular Value Fine-tuning (SVF): for each weight matrix W = UΣVᵀ, SVF learns only a scaling vector z ∈ ℝʳ such that W′ = U(Σ⊗diag(z))Vᵀ, modifying singular values while keeping singular vectors fixed. This yields full-rank expressiveness with min(m,n) parameters per matrix (vs. (m+n)×r′ for LoRA), inherent regularization against overfitting, and algebraic composability of expert vectors. Expert vectors are trained end-to-end with REINFORCE policy gradients plus a KL penalty, directly optimizing task correctness rather than next-token prediction, which allows training on as few as hundreds of examples without requiring solution text. At inference, a two-pass mechanism first dispatches the appropriate expert (via prompt-based classification, an SVF-trained classifier, or CEM search over linear combinations of K expert vectors using few-shot test prompts) and then generates the answer with the adapted weights, yielding monotonic performance gains as more test-time information is available.

## Results

On the self-adaptation benchmark (Table 2), Transformer² with few-shot adaptation achieves the best scores across nearly all settings: 25.47 on MATH, 62.99 on Humaneval, and 82.61 on ARC-Challenge with LLAMA3-8B-INSTRUCT, outperforming the base model (24.54, 60.98, 80.63) and LoRA (24.12, 52.44, 81.06). On MISTRAL-7B-INSTRUCT-V0.3, few-shot adaptation reaches 47.40 on Humaneval (vs. 43.29 base, 37.80 LoRA) and 75.47 on ARC-Challenge (vs. 71.76 base). In the fine-tuning comparison (Table 1), SVF consistently surpasses LoRA with orders of magnitude fewer parameters, e.g., 49.74 vs. 44.66 on GSM8K and 85.14 vs. 81.19 on ARC-Easy for MISTRAL-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), SVF achieves consistent and often substantial gains: on MISTRAL-7B, SVF improves GSM8K by 5.1 points (49.74 vs. 44.66) and ARC-Easy by 4.0 points (85.14 vs. 81.19), while on LLAMA3-70B it recovers a 12-point LoRA collapse on GSM8K (88.32 vs. 77.26). In the self-adaptation setting, LoRA degrades performance on MATH (24.12 vs. 24.54 base) and Humaneval (52.44 vs. 60.98 base) for LLAMA3-8B, whereas Transformer² (Few-shot) improves both. Against IA³ (Liu et al., 2022), SVF wins on GSM8K (79.15 vs. 78.01) and ARC-Easy (89.56 vs. 89.10) for LLAMA3-8B, and in the few-shot adaptation scaling study (Table 8), CEM-based Transformer² outperforms IA³ at every shot count (e.g., 82.61 vs. 82.00 at 10-shot on ARC-Challenge). Against DoRA (Liu et al., 2024), SVF leads on GSM8K (79.15 vs. 78.09) and MBPP-Pro (66.67 vs. 64.65), while DoRA slightly edges SVF on ARC-Easy (89.14 vs. 89.56 is actually SVF winning; DoRA gets 89.14). Overall, SVF and Transformer² dominate or match all three baselines across the majority of task-model combinations, with the largest margins appearing on MISTRAL-7B and in the self-adaptation regime where LoRA's overfitting is most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant low-rank adaptation paradigm (LoRA and its variants) by proposing Singular Value Fine-tuning (SVF), which scales the singular values of pre-existing weight matrices rather than introducing new low-rank matrices. The RL training objective draws on REINFORCE (Williams, 1992), while the few-shot adaptation strategy employs the Cross-Entropy Method (Rubinstein & Kroese, 2004) to search over expert vector combinations. Conceptually, the self-adaptation framework extends MoE-style expert routing from token-level to sample-level module selection, and positions SVF-trained vectors as composable building blocks that prior SVD-based PEFT methods (Lingam et al., Bialazy et al.) did not exploit for dynamic inference-time adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Transformer² offers a practical blueprint for self-adaptive LLMs where expert capabilities can be added incrementally without catastrophic forgetting, and the SVF parameterization's orders-of-magnitude parameter reduction (vs. LoRA) makes maintaining large expert libraries feasible. Key limitations include the dependence of expert capabilities on the base model's latent structure (addressable via model merging) and the one-time CEM overhead scaling with the number of specialized domains. The surprising cross-model transfer of SVF vectors between LLaMA-3-8B and Mistral-7B—contingent on preserving singular value ordering—opens the question of whether task-specific skills can be recycled across architectures of different scales, with implications for democratization.
