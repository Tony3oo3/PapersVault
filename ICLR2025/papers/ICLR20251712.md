## Problem

Subquadratic-time algorithms for Gaussian kernel matrix-vector multiplication were previously known only for non-negative vectors (Backurs et al., 2021), leaving the general case unresolved in the high-dimensional regime. This paper introduces a structural sparsity assumption—validated empirically on transformer attention matrices—that the sum of all but the top-n entries of K is at most a constant times the sum of the top-n entries, and under this assumption provides the first subquadratic-time algorithm for unrestricted vectors.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n key vectors and n query vectors in R^d (defining the Gaussian kernel matrix K with entries K_{ij} = exp(-||q_i - k_j||²/2σ²)), an arbitrary vector x ∈ R^n, and an error parameter ε > 0, and outputs y ∈ R^n satisfying ||Kx - y||_2 ≤ ε||x||_2. The error guarantee is relative to ||x||_2 rather than ||Kx||_2, which is critical because it remains meaningful even when x lies in the nullspace of K. The running time is Õ(dn^1.89/ε²), subquadratic in n and linear in d, under the assumption that ||K||_1 = O(n).

## Method

The algorithm decomposes the computation into three phases. First, x is pre-processed: entries with x_j² ≥ n^γ are handled explicitly (computing their contribution to Kx in O(dn^{2-γ}) time), and entries with x_j² ≤ n^{-4} are rounded to zero. Second, for each query q_i, LSH-based nearest-neighbor search (using the Andoni-Indyk hash family) identifies 'heavy' keys k_j where k(q_i, k_j) ≥ n^{-α}, which are then computed exactly; the sparsity assumption guarantees the total number of heavy keys across all rows is Õ(n^{1+α}). Third, the contribution of 'light' keys is estimated via uniform sub-sampling with probability 1/n, where the key innovation is using a fast Gaussian KDE data structure (Charikar et al., 2020) to estimate the per-row variance ∑x_j²K_{ij}², enabling adaptive per-row sample budgets of 10ns_i/ε² repetitions. Setting γ = 0.109 and α = 1/3 balances the exponents to yield the overall Õ(dn^1.89/ε²) runtime.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1.1) achieves Õ(dn^{1.89}/ε²) time for approximate Gaussian kernel matrix-vector multiplication under the sparsity assumption (Assumption A), handling general vectors x with error guarantee ‖Kx−y‖₂ ≤ ε‖x‖₂. Empirically, the head-to-tail ratio constant c is validated to be at most 4.6 across all BERT layers/heads on SQuAD, 5.15 for GPT-1, and 5.39 for RoBERTa, confirming the assumption holds in practice. A runtime experiment on GloVe embeddings (d=100, sequence lengths 4K–16K) shows the ratio of exact-to-approximate wall-clock time increases with sequence length, confirming subquadratic scaling of the algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary prior work is Backurs et al. (2021), which achieves Õ(n^{1.173}/ε^{3.346}) time but requires x to be non-negative and uses the weaker error guarantee ε‖Kx‖₂. This paper removes the non-negativity restriction entirely and uses the stronger ε‖x‖₂ guarantee, which is independent of whether x lies in the nullspace of K. The paper further shows that even for non-negative x, there are error regimes (e.g., x = 1_n with one all-ones row in K) where Backurs et al. degrades to Ω(n²) time while this paper remains subquadratic. Compared to Zandieh et al. (2023) (KDEFormer), this paper's reduction from attention matrices to Gaussian kernel matrices has better precision guarantees and is independent of the vector x, meaning the reduction need only be performed once per matrix rather than once per matrix-vector pair. The paper does not include a head-to-head benchmark table against these baselines; the comparison is primarily theoretical (complexity guarantees and problem settings) supplemented by the empirical validation of the sparsity assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LSH-based kernel computation framework pioneered by Charikar & Siminelakis (2017) and formalized for matrix-vector products by Backurs et al. (2021), which achieved subquadratic time but was restricted to non-negative vectors with an ε||Kx||₂ error guarantee. The key departure is a new structural modelling assumption (Assumption A) that the ℓ₁ norm of the kernel matrix scales linearly in n rather than quadratically, validated empirically on BERT, RoBERTa, and GPT-1 attention matrices. The algorithm combines LSH-based heavy-key recovery with an adaptive per-row sampling budget estimated via the fast Gaussian KDE data structure of Charikar et al. (2020), and builds on the attention-to-Gaussian-kernel reduction of Zandieh et al. (2023) for the empirical evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first subquadratic-time algorithm for high-dimensional Gaussian kernel matrix-vector multiplication that handles unrestricted (including negative) vectors, removing a key limitation of prior work that was tied to the Perron-Frobenius theorem. The empirical finding that the strong ℓ∞-based sparsity assumption (a uniform gap between head and tail entries) does NOT hold—median ratio of n-th to (n+1)-th largest entries is ~1—challenges a natural modelling choice and motivates the ℓ₁-based assumption instead. A limitation is that the algorithm's subquadratic guarantee (n^1.89) depends on the structural assumption, which while well-validated on current LLMs, may not hold for all kernel matrices or future architectures; the exponent also remains close to 2, limiting practical speedups at moderate n.
