## Problem

General-purpose (algebraic) preconditioners for large sparse linear systems—such as ILU, AMG, and inner-outer GMRES—struggle with ill-conditioned matrices, exhibit unpredictable construction times, and suffer from significant failure rates. No prior work has used a neural network to directly approximate the matrix inverse as a preconditioner without exploiting problem-specific structure beyond the matrix itself, leaving a gap for a robust, general-purpose approach that learns A^{-1} from data.

## Input / Output

The GNN preconditioner M takes a sparse matrix A (interpreted as a graph adjacency matrix) and a right-hand side vector b as node features, and outputs an approximate solution x ≈ A^{-1}b. The matrix A is prescaled by a Gershgorin-based factor γ to normalize entry magnitudes, and the input b is projected onto a sphere of radius √n via a parameter-free scaling operator before entering the network. The trained preconditioner is then plugged into FGMRES, where M is applied at each Arnoldi step to generate search directions, and the final solution is recovered as x = M(u_m).

## Method

The paper proposes a scale-equivariant graph neural network (GNP) built from stacked Res-GCONV layers (GCN with residual connections and Gershgorin-based normalization) that directly approximates A^{-1} as a nonlinear operator. Scale-equivariance is enforced by a parameter-free scaling s(·) = (√n/‖b‖₂)· at the input and its inverse at the output, restricting the effective input space to a sphere and guaranteeing M(αb) = αM(b). Training data is generated by sampling x from a mixture of the bottom eigensubspace of A (obtained via the SVD of the Arnoldi Hessenberg matrix) and the standard normal, then computing b = Ax, which balances trainability against coverage of the difficult low-eigenvalue directions. Because M is nonlinear, the paper uses flexible GMRES (FGMRES) rather than standard preconditioned Krylov methods, and provides an a posteriori convergence analysis bounding the residual in terms of the subspace difference between the nonlinear and a reference linear preconditioner. The GNN is trained with an ℓ₁ residual loss using Adam for 2000 steps with fixed hyperparameters across all 867 evaluation matrices.

## Results

Evaluated on 867 non-SPD real matrices (1K–100K rows, <2M nonzeros) from 50 application areas in SuiteSparse, GNP performs best on a substantial portion of problems under both Iter-AUC and Time-AUC metrics, though ILU and AMG are best on more problems overall. When GNP is the best preconditioner, the residual-norm improvement over the second-best method reaches a factor of 6,780× at the 75th percentile and exceeds 10 orders of magnitude in the best case. GNP is particularly effective for ill-conditioned matrices (condition number ≥ 10¹⁶) and in chemical process simulation, economic, and eigenvalue/model reduction problems. GNP construction time scales nearly linearly with matrix size, while AMG construction can exceed GNP by more than an order of magnitude in the worst case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to ILU, GNP is far more robust (0% vs. 40.14% construction failure rate) and substantially outperforms it on ill-conditioned matrices (cond ≥ 10¹⁶), where ILU's sparsity-pattern-based modeling is less effective; however, ILU is the best preconditioner on a larger overall fraction of problems. Against AMG, GNP again wins on robustness (0% vs. 7.15% construction failures) and offers more predictable construction time (nearly linear in matrix size vs. AMG's highly variable cost, with AMG's worst case exceeding GNP by >10×); AMG still edges out GNP on the total number of problems where it is best. Versus GMRES-as-preconditioner (inner-outer GMRES), GNP achieves faster solution times because GMRES is bottlenecked by the Arnoldi orthogonalization process, even though GMRES requires fewer matrix-vector multiplications per iteration. Against the simple Jacobi preconditioner, GNP is clearly superior in both convergence speed and robustness (Jacobi fails on 6.11% of problems). Overall, GNP does not dominate ILU or AMG on the majority of problems but provides a critical fallback: when traditional preconditioners fail or underperform (especially on ill-conditioned or non-PDE matrices), GNP delivers dramatic residual reductions with guaranteed construction success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends FGMRES (Saad, 1993), originally designed for preconditioners that change at each Arnoldi step, to accommodate a fixed but nonlinear operator M, and builds its architecture on the GCN (Kipf & Welling, 2017) with modifications including Gershgorin-based normalization, residual connections, and a scale-equivariant input/output scaling. It departs from recent neural preconditioner work (Li et al. 2023, Häusner et al. 2023, Trifonov et al. 2024) that uses networks to learn nonzero entries within a predefined sparsity pattern, instead directly approximating the mapping b → x = A⁻¹b as a general-purpose algebraic preconditioner without exploiting PDE structure or function-space properties (unlike PINNs or neural operators). The paper also provides the first a posteriori convergence analysis for FGMRES, filling a theoretical gap since standard Krylov subspace tools do not apply to the non-Krylov subspaces produced by nonlinear preconditioners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GNP offers a robust, predictable-construction-time alternative that is particularly effective for ill-conditioned matrices (condition number ≥ 10¹⁶) and application areas (chemical process simulation, economics, eigenvalue/model reduction) where ILU and AMG struggle or fail entirely. The 0% construction failure rate versus ILU's 40% and AMG's 7% is a practical advantage for automated pipelines. Open questions include extending to SPD matrices (where flexible CG with neural operators is more natural), amortizing construction cost across evolving matrices via continual learning, and scaling to larger problems via multi-GPU/distributed GNN training. The paper's use of fixed hyperparameters across 800+ matrices suggests substantial room for improvement with problem-specific tuning.
