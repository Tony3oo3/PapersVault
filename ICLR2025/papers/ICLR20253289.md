## Problem

Existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data for knowledge-intensive, low-data domains consists almost exclusively of Q&A pairs, causing LLMs to learn only superficial output patterns rather than the underlying correlation and logic between knowledge pieces. This limits the model's ability to recall, reason over, and transfer knowledge during fine-tuning, even with substantial training data. The paper asks whether augmenting SFT data with structured knowledge-graph-derived explanations can close this gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple-choice Q&A pairs (e.g., medical exam questions in six languages) and an external open-source knowledge graph (UMLS) as input. It produces natural-language explanations for each Q&A pair that explicitly trace multi-hop reasoning chains (e.g., 'cancer → decreased resistance → persistent fever'), which are then appended to the original Q&A to form enhanced SFT training data. The final output is a fully fine-tuned LLM (LLaMA-2-7B-chat by default) evaluated on multiple-choice accuracy; during inference, only the answer is required, not the explanation.

## Method

KG-SFT is a three-component pipeline. (1) Extractor performs NER on the question, options, and answer, retrieves neighboring triples from the external KG, ranks them via BM25, and mines three types of multi-hop inference paths (question→question, option→option, question→answer) to form a reasoning subgraph. (2) Generator applies the HITS algorithm to score entities and relations within the subgraph (with boosted initial scores for entities appearing in the Q&A), selects the top-ranked triples, and prompts an LLM (ChatGPT) to produce a fluent explanation grounded in those triples. (3) Detector segments the draft explanation into sentences, feeds each sentence together with matched comparison triples into a DeBERTa NLI model to flag knowledge conflicts, and re-prompts the LLM to regenerate if more than 30% of sentences are flagged. The resulting explanation-augmented Q&A pairs are used for full-parameter SFT, with a higher learning rate (5e-6) than vanilla SFT (1e-6), and two distinct prompt templates distinguish answer-only from answer-plus-explanation instructions.

## Results

KG-SFT achieves an average accuracy of 41.79% across six medical multiple-choice benchmarks (English, Chinese, Russian, Spanish, French, Japanese), outperforming vanilla SFT (33.05%) by 8.74 points and the strongest data-augmentation baseline GPT3Mix (39.34%) by 2.45 points. In low-data settings (5% training data), KG-SFT yields the largest gains, e.g., 21.80% → 58.20% on Russian IgakuQA and 26.02% → 40.00% on English MedQA. On the multi-domain MMLU benchmark, KG-SFT reaches 58.72% average accuracy versus 52.45% for SFT and 54.49% for AugGPT. On 3-hop Meta QA commonsense reasoning, KG-SFT attains 64.5% accuracy, surpassing SFT (55.5%) and GPT3Mix (63.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KG-SFT (41.79% avg) outperforms all twelve baselines across the six-language medical benchmark. Against the strongest quantity-augmentation baselines, KG-SFT beats GPT3Mix (39.34%) by 2.45 points and AugGPT (38.30%) by 3.49 points, while using only 1000 training QA pairs versus their 2000. Against knowledge-graph retrieval-augmented methods, KG-SFT surpasses TOG (29.75%) by 12.04 points, KGR (28.83%) by 12.96 points, and KAPING (30.12%) by 11.67 points, indicating that training-time KG integration is far more effective than inference-time retrieval. Against traditional EDA variants (34.12–37.12% avg), KG-SFT leads by 4.67–7.67 points. On the multi-domain MMLU benchmark, KG-SFT (58.72%) also beats GPT3Mix (53.06%) and AugGPT (54.49%), though it underperforms on Formal Logic (39.61% vs. StructGPT's 45.63%) and Professional Accounting (49.62% vs. AugGPT's 50.87%), domains that rely more on symbolic/numerical reasoning than knowledge-based inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of data augmentation and knowledge-graph-enhanced LLMs, but departs from both lines in a key way. Prior data augmentation methods (EDA, AugGPT, GPT3Mix) focus on scaling the quantity of Q&A pairs, while KG-enhanced methods (ToG, KGR, KAPING) inject structured knowledge at inference time via retrieval or beam search. KG-SFT instead uses KGs at training-data construction time: it extracts reasoning subgraphs, converts them into fluent natural-language explanations via an LLM, and validates them with an NLI-based conflict detector, thereby enriching the *quality* of SFT data rather than its quantity or the inference pipeline. The framework repurposes off-the-shelf components (HITS scoring, BM25 ranking, DeBERTa NLI) in a novel pipeline without introducing new model architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in domain-specific LLM development: high-quality SFT data is scarce and expensive to create, and vanilla Q&A pairs fail to convey the logical structure of underlying knowledge. The consistent 8.7% average accuracy gain in low-data settings across six languages and fifteen domains, combined with the plug-and-play compatibility with existing quantity-augmentation methods, makes this a practically adoptable technique for knowledge-intensive, low-data domains. Limitations include underperformance in domains requiring precise symbolic or numerical reasoning (formal logic, professional accounting), dependence on the availability of a relevant external KG, and some degradation in multilingual transfer for languages underrepresented in the base model's pre-training corpus. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to domains lacking mature KGs and whether the explanation-generation step can be made more efficient at scale.
