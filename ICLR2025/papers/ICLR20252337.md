## Problem

Off-the-shelf LLMs exhibit human-like biases in risky and intertemporal choice, but their training data is massive, undisclosed, and potentially contaminated, making it impossible to determine what actually produces these behavioral correspondences. The paper asks whether a minimal language model trained solely on the arithmetic of expected-value calculations, without any natural language supervision or human choice data, can replicate the distortions that classical behavioral models (prospect theory, hyperbolic discounting) attribute to human cognition.

## Input / Output

The model consumes stylized arithmetic equations encoding EV or PV calculations (e.g., '0.5*100=+50' or '0.8*1+0.8^2*10=+7.2') with a custom 320-token sub-word vocabulary covering digits 0-300, operators, and an <AMB> token for unknown probabilities, at a context length of 26 tokens. After pretraining, the model is frozen and final-layer embeddings (320-dim) are extracted for each option's expected value and for the EV difference between two options; these embeddings serve as features in a logistic regression whose output is the predicted probability of choosing option A, directly comparable to human choice rates.

## Method

Arithmetic-GPT is a small decoder-only transformer (~10M parameters, 8 layers, hidden size 320, 8 heads) trained from scratch via next-token prediction on 1M synthetic equations of the form 'p1*x1 +/- p2*x2 = result'. The critical design choice is the distribution of probabilities and values in the synthetic data: the ecological variant draws probabilities from Beta(0.27, 0.27) and values from a power-law (exponent -0.945), matching real-world frequencies, while the uniform variant uses U[0,1] and U[0,300]. Ablation variants additionally randomize the sign of the right-hand-side answer with 50% probability. The paper shows that only the ecological (non-ablated) distribution yields embeddings whose implicit functions, recovered via 1-D multidimensional scaling, quantitatively replicate prospect theory's probability weighting (gamma=0.58), S-shaped utility (alpha=0.42, beta=0.45, lambda=1.4), and hyperbolic discounting (k=0.08), and that these embeddings outperform prospect theory and hyperbolic discounting in explaining four human choice datasets (choices13k, cpc18, gershman20, agrawal23).

## Results

Arithmetic-GPT pretrained on ecologically valid synthetic data (Beta(0.27,0.27) for probabilities, power-law for values) explains 70.8% of variance in risky choices (choices13k), 65.5% (cpc18), 67.8% in intertemporal choices (gershman20), and 95.5% (agrawal23), all measured as R². Ablating the correct answers from the synthetic equations drops performance by 9–15 percentage points, confirming that accurate arithmetic computation is essential. The model's implicit probability weighting, utility, and discounting functions extracted from embeddings quantitatively match classical behavioral economics parameters (γ≈0.58, α≈0.42, β≈0.45, λ≈1.4, k≈0.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prospect theory, Arithmetic-GPT (eco.) substantially outperforms on both risky-choice datasets: 70.8% vs. 51.5% R² on choices13k and 65.5% vs. 54.2% on cpc18, while also generalizing to intertemporal choice where prospect theory does not apply. Against hyperbolic discounting, the advantage is even more pronounced: 67.8% vs. 53.4% on gershman20 and 95.5% vs. 36.1% on agrawal23. Compared to LLaMA-3-70B-Instruct, Arithmetic-GPT (eco.) dramatically outperforms LLaMA3's text-based log-probability predictions (e.g., 70.8% vs. 14.2% on choices13k) and also beats LLaMA3's text-embedding representations on risky choices (70.8% vs. 66.2%, 65.5% vs. 55.7%), though LLaMA3 (txt emb.) slightly edges out on agrawal23 (98.0% vs. 95.5%). The MLP trained directly on human task features achieves the highest R² on most datasets (82.7%, 97.8%), but uses a different input format (numerical task features rather than arithmetic equations), so it represents an upper bound rather than a fair comparison. Overall, a 10M-parameter model pretrained solely on synthetic arithmetic equations matches or exceeds a 70B-parameter general-purpose LLM on predicting human decision-making, without any exposure to human choice data or natural language.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bourgin et al.'s (2019) idea of pretraining on synthetic data to predict human decisions, but reduces the pretraining task to its most primitive computational component—arithmetic of expected value and present value calculations—rather than data generated from a full psychological theory. It departs from the LLMs-as-cognitive-models literature (Binz & Schulz, 2023; Frank, 2023) by arguing that the specific distribution of training data (ecological vs. uniform) is the critical factor producing human-like biases, not model scale or natural language supervision. The paper positions itself against classical behavioral models (prospect theory, hyperbolic discounting) as a unified computational account that generalizes across both risky and intertemporal choice domains, where those models do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 10M-parameter model pretrained solely on synthetic arithmetic with ecologically valid distributions can outperform prospect theory and hyperbolic discounting—and even compete with LLaMA-3-70B on some datasets—suggests that human-like decision biases may emerge from numerical reasoning under realistic input distributions rather than from language or social training. However, the MLP ceiling trained directly on human data still exceeds Arithmetic-GPT on most datasets, and the data gap (1M equations vs. human experience) remains unresolved. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to other judgment and decision-making tasks, what the minimum model size is for reliable human-likeness, and whether different rationality assumptions in the pretraining task would yield different behavioral signatures.
