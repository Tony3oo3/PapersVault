## Problem

While sharpness phenomena such as early sharpness reduction, progressive sharpening, and the edge of stability (EoS) have been widely observed in neural network training, the specific mechanisms and conditions governing their emergence remain unclear. Prior toy models either restricted analysis to particular parameterizations (e.g., NTP only) or failed to capture the full phenomenology across the entire training trajectory, leaving open why some networks exhibit EoS while others do not, and how initialization and parameterization control these behaviors.

## Input / Output

The UV model is a 2-layer linear network f(x;θ) = (1/√n_eff) v^T U x trained on a single example (x, y) with MSE loss, where the dynamics is fully captured by two scalar variables — the residual Δf and the Hessian trace λ — governed by three effective parameters (η, ‖x‖/√n_eff, y). The analysis produces sharpness trajectories, a complete fixed-point stability classification, a bifurcation diagram of sharpness fluctuations versus learning rate, and an EoS phase diagram in (σ_w², c) space. Predictions are then validated on 3–4 layer ReLU FCNs, CNNs, ResNets, and Transformers trained on CIFAR-10, MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and WikiText-2.

## Method

The paper derives closed-form function-space dynamics for the UV model as two coupled nonlinear update equations in (Δf, λ), then performs a complete fixed-point and linear-stability analysis identifying four fixed points/lines (I–IV) whose positions and stability depend on η, n_eff, and y. The four observed training regimes (early transient, intermediate saturation, progressive sharpening, EoS) are shown to arise from the trajectory's proximity to the saddle fixed point II and the stability of the zero-loss line I. EoS is shown to require a critical learning rate η_c = √n_eff/(‖x‖y) above which all zero-loss fixed points become unstable, trapping training on an EoS manifold (the line segment joining fixed points II and IV) that acts as a dynamical attractor. On this manifold, the loss becomes sub-quadratic and the dynamics reduces to a cubic map, yielding a period-doubling route to chaos as η increases. These theoretical predictions — including the EoS phase diagram, the sharpness–weight-norm correlation, and the bifurcation structure — are validated empirically on realistic architectures and both synthetic and real datasets.

## Results

The UV model analysis yields a critical learning rate η_c = √n_eff/(‖x‖y) as a necessary condition for the edge of stability (EoS), with the bifurcation diagram on the EoS manifold extending up to η ≈ 0.8 (for n_eff=1, y=2, η_c=0.5) before diverging. The phase diagram of EoS in real architectures (3-layer ReLU FCNs on CIFAR-10) confirms that EoS is observed for small initial weight variance σ_w², large interpolating parameter s (toward μP), and large learning rate constant c, with a positive correlation between observing EoS and improved test accuracy. The loss on the EoS manifold takes the sub-quadratic form L(θ) ≈ ½(a‖θ‖² − b)², explaining the period-doubling route to chaos via the cubic map. In real datasets (CIFAR-10), sharpness at EoS exhibits long-range temporal correlations and dense bands rather than the clean period-doubling seen in synthetic data, attributed to power-law structure in the input covariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without traditional method-vs-baseline comparisons. Instead, the key empirical comparisons are between parameterizations and between the UV model's predictions and real-world observations. SP networks with large initialization (σ_w²=2.0) do NOT exhibit EoS even at late training times (loss < 10⁻⁵), with sharpness monotonically decreasing after the early transient, whereas μP networks (or SP with small σ_w²) do exhibit EoS with sharpness oscillating around 2/η. The UV model's prediction that μP networks skip the early sharpness reduction phase is confirmed in real FCNs trained on CIFAR-10. For the route-to-chaos phenomenon, 2-layer linear FCNs trained on 5,000 iid random examples reproduce the clean period-doubling bifurcation diagram of the UV model, while the same architecture trained on CIFAR-10 shows only dense sharpness bands with long-range temporal correlations, indicating that dataset structure (specifically, power-law vs. flat singular value spectra of the input covariance) determines whether the period-doubling route to chaos is visible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the line of work on sharpness dynamics during neural network training (Cohen et al. 2021 on EoS, Lewkowycz et al. 2020 on catapult, Kalra & Barkeshli 2023 on the four training regimes) by providing a single mechanistic framework via fixed-point analysis of the UV model in function space. It departs from Agarwala et al. (2022), who claimed the UV model does not exhibit EoS, by showing that the correct choice of parameterization (μP) and training example (y > 0) is essential. It also generalizes beyond Wang et al. (2022), who restricted to cases with progressive sharpening from initialization, and connects the EoS dynamics to the sub-quadratic loss framework of Ma et al. (2022) and the cubic map analysis of Chen et al. (2023), showing that the EoS manifold naturally gives rise to a cubic map and hence a period-doubling route to chaos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified fixed-point-based explanation for all four training regimes (sharpness reduction, intermediate saturation, progressive sharpening, EoS) and identifies parameterization (μP vs. SP/NTP) as the key determinant of which phenomena appear, with practical implications for learning rate transfer and initialization choices. The period-doubling route to chaos characterization of EoS fluctuations offers a new lens for understanding late-time training dynamics and distinguishes between synthetic and real datasets (the latter showing long-range correlations tied to input covariance structure). Key limitations include the UV model's inability to capture non-monotonic loss decrease at EoS, loss catapult at small effective widths, and long-range correlations in real data, leaving a complete theory of EoS in realistic settings as an open problem.
