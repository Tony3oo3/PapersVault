## Problem

Residual connections and normalization layers are standard design choices for GNNs and are empirically known to mitigate oversmoothing, yet their precise theoretical mechanism and effect on the expressive power of GNNs remained uncharacterized. Previous oversmoothing analyses relied on single node-similarity measures that could only detect complete collapse to a one-dimensional subspace, missing the refined geometry of the system. This paper provides a formal characterization of how these methods provably prevent oversmoothing and what subspace the node representations converge to.

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis operates on linearized message-passing GNNs (identity nonlinearity) where the input is a symmetric non-negative adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} and initial node features X^(0) ∈ R^{n×k} with unit-norm columns. The output is the sequence of node representations X^(t) after t layers, analyzed via their distance to one-dimensional subspaces and convergence to eigenspaces of the message-passing operator. The proposed GraphNormv2 layer takes a feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×k} and produces a normalized version by subtracting a learned projection onto the top-k eigenvectors of the message-passing operator, followed by column-wise scaling and affine transformation.

## Method

The paper establishes three key theoretical results: (a) initial residual connections prevent complete rank collapse by incorporating X^(0) at each layer, and the reachable subspace is exactly the Krylov subspace Kr(A, X^(0)); (b) BatchNorm's column-wise scaling prevents complete collapse, and the system exponentially converges to the top-k eigenspace of the centered operator (I − 11ᵀ/n)A regardless of weight choices; (c) the centering step of normalization layers distorts the graph signal by altering structural eigenvectors associated with the Weisfeiler-Leman quotient graph, dampening or losing relevant information in message-passing. Building on insight (c), the authors propose GraphNormv2, which replaces the naive mean-subtraction centering with a learned projection onto the top-k eigenvectors of the message-passing operator (augmented with one additional vector for backward compatibility with BatchNorm and GraphNorm), preserving the integrity of the graph signal while retaining the beneficial scaling effect. Experiments on GCN, GAT, and GIN across node and graph classification benchmarks confirm the effectiveness of GraphNormv2.

## Results

GraphNormv2 achieves the best or near-best accuracy across all GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, GIN) and tasks. On node classification with GCN, GraphNormv2 reaches 85.8% on Cora, 89.5% on CiteSeer, and 68.3% on ogbn-arxiv. The advantage is most pronounced at 20 layers, where GCN+GraphNormv2 achieves 84.8% on Cora and 57.4% on ogbn-arxiv, substantially outperforming PowerEmbed (48.0% and 55.9%) and GroupNorm (64.4% and 6.0%). On GIN, GraphNormv2 dramatically outperforms all baselines on node classification (86.5% Cora, 94.9% CiteSeer, 68.9% ogbn-arxiv vs. 33–86% for other methods).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphNormv2 consistently outperforms or matches all five baselines, with the largest gains appearing at 20 layers. Against BatchNorm, GraphNormv2 wins on GCN 20-layer Cora (84.8% vs. 84.0%) and ogbn-arxiv (57.4% vs. 56.7%), and on GIN node classification it achieves 68.9% on ogbn-arxiv versus 21.1% for BatchNorm. Against GraphNorm, GraphNormv2 improves GCN 20-layer Cora by 4.3 points (84.8% vs. 80.5%) and GIN ogbn-arxiv by 47.3 points (68.9% vs. 21.6%). PairNorm is the weakest baseline at depth, collapsing to 44.0% on GCN 20-layer ogbn-arxiv and 6.2% on GAT 20-layer ogbn-arxiv, while GraphNormv2 maintains 57.4% and 57.5% respectively. GroupNorm and PowerEmbed both suffer severe degradation at 20 layers (e.g., PowerEmbed drops to 48.0% on GCN Cora and 6.0% on ogbn-arxiv), whereas GraphNormv2 retains competitive accuracy. The one setting where GraphNormv2 does not clearly lead is GAT, where GroupNorm and PowerEmbed achieve marginally higher standard-depth scores (95.3–95.5% CiteSeer vs. 94.8%), which the authors attribute to GAT's asymmetric, time-varying message-passing operator falling outside their theoretical analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the oversmoothing analysis of Oono & Suzuki (2020), Cai et al. (2021), and Wu et al. (2023a,b), which characterized exponential collapse in standard GNNs, by providing the first rigorous treatment of how residual connections and normalization layers alter that dynamics. It builds directly on GraphNorm (Cai et al. 2021) by identifying a fundamental flaw in its centering step and proposing GraphNormv2 with a learned eigenvector-based projection. The analysis also draws on the Weisfeiler-Leman / structural eigenvector framework (Morris et al. 2019; Xu et al. 2019) to characterize the Krylov subspace reachable by residual-connected GNNs, and on batch normalization theory from deep learning (Daneshmand et al. 2020) showing BN avoids rank collapse in linear networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear theoretical gap by showing *why* residual connections and normalization prevent oversmoothing, rather than merely observing it empirically. The finding that the centering step in normalization layers actively distorts the graph signal—pushing structural eigenvectors downward in the spectrum and altering the dominant eigenvector—is a conceptual insight that challenges the assumption that normalization is purely beneficial in GNNs. However, the analysis is restricted to linearized GNNs (with non-linear experiments only as supporting evidence), the empirical gains of GraphNormv2 over existing methods are modest (1–2% in most settings), and the theory does not cover attention-based GNNs with time-varying operators. Open questions include closing the gap for non-linear systems and understanding whether the centering distortion is mitigated by non-linearity in practice.
