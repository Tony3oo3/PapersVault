## Problem

INR-based video compressors like NeRV suffer from two practical limitations: their rendering frame rates are too low for real-time use, and they require fully decoding the entire network before reconstructing any single frame, making stream decoding infeasible. This paper asks whether the fast rendering and partial parameter decoding properties of 3D Gaussian Splatting can be transferred to 2D video compression to overcome these gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250030/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of 1920×1080 video frames (600 frames per video from the UVG dataset) along with pre-computed optical flow from VideoFlow as training inputs. It produces a compressed bitstream containing G-PCC-encoded anchor coordinates, ANS-encoded anchor attributes and binary hash grid, and 8-bit quantized network parameters. At decode time, after receiving common information (metadata, anchor positions, network weights), the remaining per-frame bitstream can be decoded on demand, enabling stream and random access decoding without reconstructing the full model.

## Method

The core contribution is the Toast-like Sliding Window (TSW) orthographic projection, which reinterprets a 2D video as a 3D manifold (xy = spatial, z = time) and renders each frame by selecting all Gaussians within a temporal window [t₁−h, t₁+h], rendering from both sides of the camera plane z=t₁, and averaging the results. Built on the HAC compression framework, GSVC replaces Scaffold-GS's viewpoint-conditioned Gaussian generation with time-aware generation using FiLM layers conditioned on positional encodings of the timestamp and relative depth. A deformation network produces per-Gaussian dynamic offsets, and pre-computed optical flow directly supervises the displacement of shared Gaussians between adjacent frames (Eq. 12), providing explicit motion guidance without an auxiliary flow-estimation network. Post-training compression applies G-PCC lossless coding to anchor coordinates, 8-bit quantization to network weights, and a custom GPU-parallel ANS entropy codec to achieve practical bit rates.

## Results

On the UVG dataset (7 videos, 1920×1080, 3900 frames total), GSVC achieves the best PSNR on 6 out of 7 videos (e.g., 33.19 dB on Bosphorus, 37.90 dB on HoneyBee, 34.22 dB on ShakeNDry) and the best LPIPS on all 7 videos (e.g., 0.1780 on Bosphorus, 0.1571 on HoneyBee). GSVC also achieves the smallest compressed size on most videos (e.g., 1.107 MB on Beauty, 2.050 MB on Bosphorus) while delivering 30–40% higher rendering FPS than NeRV and over 40× higher FPS than VCT. In RD-curve comparisons, GSVC attains comparable PSNR to NeRV and HNeRV but significantly better MS-SSIM and LPIPS, and matches H.265 (veryslow) on MS-SSIM/LPIPS at low bit rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS, Scaffold-GS, and HAC, GSVC (and even their TSW variants) dramatically outperforms on all videos where the original methods fail to converge (HoneyBee, Jockey, ShakeNDry) and achieves substantially higher PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS on the remaining videos (e.g., 33.19 vs. 25.42 PSNR on Bosphorus vs. HAC). Among the TSW variants, HAC_TSW is the strongest baseline, achieving second-best results on most metrics, but GSVC still leads by 1–7 dB PSNR on dynamic videos (e.g., 29.38 vs. 22.76 on Jockey) thanks to time-aware generation and optical-flow-guided deformation. In RD-curve comparisons, GSVC surpasses NeRV and HNeRV on MS-SSIM and LPIPS at all bit rates while matching their PSNR, and reaches comparable MS-SSIM/LPIPS to H.265 (veryslow) in the low-bit-rate region. On practical metrics, GSVC renders 30–40% faster than NeRV (e.g., 83.37 vs. ~60 FPS on Beauty) and over 40× faster than VCT, while DCVC is omitted from speed comparisons due to its sub-1 FPS autoregressive decoding. GSVC also uniquely supports stream decoding (frames become renderable as their bits arrive), whereas NeRV and HNeRV require full network decompression before any frame can be reconstructed. Encoding time is 12.4 ks (3 h 26 min) for GSVC versus 22 ks (6 h 6 min) for NeRV and 13.8–28.2 ks for HNeRV on a typical 600-frame video.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting—a framework designed for 3D scene reconstruction and novel view synthesis—into a 2D video compression tool by introducing the TSW orthographic projection, which treats the time axis as a third spatial dimension and exploits inter-frame redundancy via a sliding window. It extends the HAC entropy-constrained 3DGS compression framework and Scaffold-GS's anchor-based Gaussian generation, replacing viewpoint-conditioned rendering with time-stamp-conditioned rendering augmented by FiLM layers and a deformation network. The work positions itself against INR-based video compressors (NeRV, HNeRV), which suffer from slow rendering and inability to stream-decode, and against prior 3DGS-for-video attempts (Shin et al., 2024) that require explicit foreground/background separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a proof-of-concept that 3DGS's explicit representation, fast rasterization, and partial-parameter decoding can be harnessed for practical video compression, offering stream decoding and ~30% higher rendering speed over INR baselines. However, the per-video training requirement (3+ hours on a single GPU) remains a significant barrier to real-time encoding, and the TSW window size h must be manually tuned per video, limiting generalizability. Open questions include adaptive per-frame window sizing, handling optical flow estimation noise that degrades performance on some sequences, and whether the approach scales to longer or higher-resolution videos without the current per-video optimization cost.
