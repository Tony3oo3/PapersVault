## Problem

Deterministic spatiotemporal precipitation nowcasting models produce increasingly blurry predictions as lead time grows, obscuring the small-scale weather patterns critical for extreme precipitation forecasting. Existing conditional generative postprocessors (e.g., DiffCast, CasCast) require pre-generated (blurry prediction, ground truth) pairs for training, making them cumbersome to deploy and unable to generalize to blur modes arising from new datasets, models, or lead times not seen during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253137/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurry radar prediction (resized to 256×256) from any deterministic spatiotemporal model (TAU, PredRNN, SimVP, EarthFormer) at any lead time as input, and produces a deblurred radar prediction with recovered local weather patterns. It additionally outputs an optimized 9×9 blur kernel that, when convolved with the deblurred output, reconstructs the original blurry prediction, providing a verifiable decomposition of the degradation. The method operates as a pure postprocessing step requiring no retraining of the upstream prediction model.

## Method

PostCast reframes prediction blurriness as an unknown convolution y′ = conv(K_{S,T,M}, y) and solves the inverse problem using a pre-trained unconditional DDPM (initialized from ImageNet, fine-tuned on five precipitation datasets) as a generative prior. At each reverse diffusion step, a zero-shot blur kernel estimation mechanism dynamically optimizes a randomly initialized 9×9 kernel by backpropagating a distance metric between the kernel-applied intermediate clean estimate x̃₀ and the blurry prediction, requiring no supervised (blurry, ground truth) pairs. An auto-scale gradient guidance strategy derives the guidance scale s from an empirical formula involving the current noisy state, the DDPM mean, and the distance metric, eliminating manual tuning across blur modes. The gradient of the distance metric with respect to x̃₀ is injected into the reverse-process mean to steer sampling toward outputs whose simulated blur matches the input prediction, while the gradient with respect to kernel parameters updates the kernel for subsequent steps.

## Results

On out-of-distribution SCWDS CAP30, TAU+PostCast achieves a P16 CSI of 0.269 (HSS 0.345, POD 0.428), outperforming TAU+CasCast (0.224) and TAU+DiffCast (0.166). On MeteoNet, TAU+PostCast reaches P16 CSI of 0.182 (HSS 0.288, POD 0.344) versus 0.128 for CasCast and 0.063 for DiffCast. On in-domain SEVIR at 60-min lead time, TAU+PostCast achieves P16 CSI of 0.144 and HSS of 0.402, while EarthFormer+PostCast on SRAD2018 reaches P16 CSI of 0.404 and HSS of 0.396. PostCast consistently improves CSI at the highest precipitation thresholds across all 8 datasets and 4 spatiotemporal backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PostCast is applied as a postprocessing step on top of four spatiotemporal backbones (TAU, PredRNN, SimVP, EarthFormer) and compared against five standalone or coupled generative baselines: DiffCast (CVPR 2024), CasCast (ICML 2024), DGMR (Nature 2021), STRPM (CVPR 2022), and DGP (TPAMI 2021). On out-of-distribution SCWDS CAP30, TAU+PostCast achieves P16 CSI of 0.269, beating TAU+CasCast (0.224) by 20% and TAU+DiffCast (0.166) by 62%; on MeteoNet the gap widens to 0.182 vs. 0.128 (CasCast) and 0.063 (DiffCast). DiffCast and CasCast, which require joint training with blurry-prediction pairs, degrade substantially out-of-distribution (e.g., DiffCast HSS drops to 0.079 on MeteoNet), whereas PostCast maintains strong HSS (0.288) and POD (0.344) without any retraining. DGMR and STRPM, being fully generative or video-prediction models, achieve the lowest CSI scores across all datasets (e.g., DGMR P16 CSI of 0.160 on CAP30 vs. 0.269 for PostCast). DGP, an unsupervised GAN-based restoration method, is competitive on MeteoNet P16 (0.186) but still trails PostCast on HSS (0.250 vs. 0.288) and POD (0.218 vs. 0.344). The key advantage of PostCast is its zero-shot generalization: a single unconditional DDPM fine-tuned on 5 in-domain datasets transfers to 3 unseen datasets and 4 different backbones without any additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of precipitation nowcasting and diffusion-based image deblurring. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (DiffCast, CasCast, UA-GAN) that trains conditional generative models on pre-generated (blurry prediction, ground truth) pairs, instead reframing blurriness as an unknown convolutional blur kernel acting on clean predictions. By repurposing the unconditional DDPM framework from image synthesis (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) and the blur-simulation idea from deblurring literature (Ren et al., 2023), it eliminates the need for paired training data entirely, replacing supervised conditioning with a zero-shot kernel estimation mechanism and an auto-scale gradient guidance strategy applied during the reverse diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic postprocessing design means PostCast can be plugged behind any existing spatiotemporal predictor (RNN, CNN, Transformer) and generalize zero-shot to new datasets, lead times, and regions without retraining the generative component—a significant practical advantage over coupling methods that require retraining per dataset/model/lead-time combination. However, the 1000-step DDPM sampling is computationally expensive for operational nowcasting, and the method's effectiveness is bounded by the quality of the input blurry prediction (acknowledged as a limitation). Open questions include whether the sampling can be accelerated (e.g., via distillation or few-step solvers) and whether the simple 9×9 kernel formulation suffices for more complex, non-convolutional degradation patterns in next-generation nowcasters.
