## Problem

Existing deep learning-based image watermarking methods, while robust to classical distortions like compression and cropping, are largely undetectable after images are edited by large-scale text-to-image models (e.g., Instruct-Pix2Pix, MagicBrush, UltraEdit). No prior benchmark systematically evaluated watermarking robustness across the full spectrum of T2I-based editing techniques, and no method simultaneously achieved high image quality and strong robustness against such edits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253024/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes an RGB image (trained at 256×256, evaluated at 512×512 via resolution scaling) and a 100-bit binary watermark, producing a watermarked image that is perceptually near-identical to the input. The decoder (ConvNeXt-B + FC layer) takes any image and outputs the 100-bit watermark. Notably, the SDXL-Turbo text prompt is fixed to a null prompt throughout, and the condition adaptor fuses the image and watermark into a single representation before VAE encoding rather than using separate conditional branches. A resolution scaling procedure (residual interpolation) allows all methods to operate at arbitrary resolutions without retraining.

## Method

VINE adapts the one-step pretrained diffusion model SDXL-Turbo as a conditional generative watermark encoder, with a lightweight condition adaptor that fuses the input image and watermark bits before feeding them through the VAE encoder, UNet, and VAE decoder. Two key design choices distinguish it: (1) frequency-domain analysis reveals that T2I-based editing removes mid- and high-frequency patterns while preserving low-frequency content, so blurring distortions (pixelation, defocus, motion, Gaussian, zoom blur) are used as computationally cheap surrogate attacks in the noise layer to push the model toward embedding in low-frequency bands; (2) skip connections with zero-convolution layers are added between the VAE encoder and decoder to preserve perceptual fidelity, since the pretrained VAE's compressive design otherwise degrades reconstruction quality. Training proceeds in three stages: first, the extraction loss is prioritized (α=10) with the UNet and VAE decoder frozen; second, all parameters are unfrozen with balanced losses (MSE in YUV space, LPIPS, GAN adversarial, BCE); third, VINE-R is fine-tuned by backpropagating through Instruct-Pix2Pix via a straight-through estimator for additional robustness. The decoder is a separate ConvNeXt-B network trained jointly with the encoder.

## Results

VINE-R achieves the best overall trade-off among all 11 evaluated methods, reaching 99.66% TPR@0.1%FPR on stochastic regeneration, 99.98% on deterministic regeneration, 97.46% on Instruct-Pix2Pix global editing, 99.96% on UltraEdit local editing, and 93.04% on ControlNet-Inpainting, while maintaining a PSNR of 37.34 dB and SSIM of 0.9934. VINE-B offers superior image quality (PSNR 40.51, SSIM 0.9954, LPIPS 0.0029) with slightly lower robustness (e.g., 91.03% on stochastic regeneration, 96.30% on Pix2Pix). Both methods support 100-bit encoding capacity, matching StegaStamp and TrustMark, while all other baselines are limited to 30–64 bits. The weakest scenario for VINE-R is image-to-video generation (SVD), where TPR@0.1%FPR drops to 36.33%, though this still exceeds all baselines except TrustMark (39.60%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VINE-R dominates all baselines on nearly every editing task. Against StegaStamp (the strongest prior method), VINE-R improves TPR@0.1%FPR from 92.13% to 97.46% on Pix2Pix global editing, from 51.24% to 86.86% on UltraEdit global editing, and from 30.85% to 36.33% on SVD, while also achieving a 7.7 dB higher PSNR (37.34 vs. 29.65) and 0.083 higher SSIM. Against MBRS, VINE-R is far superior on stochastic regeneration (99.66% vs. 6.85%) and MagicBrush (94.58% vs. 7.50%), though MBRS edges ahead on deterministic regeneration (99.53% vs. 99.98% is actually VINE-R winning) and local UltraEdit (88.54% vs. 99.96% is VINE-R winning). TrustMark, despite the best image quality (PSNR 41.27), is catastrophically weak on stochastic regeneration (0.86%) and deterministic regeneration (9.22%), where VINE-R achieves 99.66% and 99.98% respectively. EditGuard, designed for tamper localization rather than robust detection, scores below 10% TPR on nearly all editing tasks, confirming it is not competitive for watermark survival. The only scenario where a baseline slightly outperforms VINE-R is SVD (TrustMark 39.60% vs. VINE-R 36.33%), though both remain low, reflecting the fundamental difficulty of preserving watermarks through video generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust image watermarking line of work (StegaStamp, MBRS, TrustMark) by addressing a new threat model: T2I-model-based image editing that prior methods cannot withstand. It builds on SDXL-Turbo as a one-step generative prior for the encoder and borrows zero-convolution layers from ControlNet for perceptual fidelity, but departs from prior edit-robust attempts (EditGuard, Robust-Wide, JigMark) by using frequency-domain analysis to identify blurring as a computationally cheap surrogate for T2I editing during training, rather than backpropagating through the editing model itself. The W-Bench benchmark also extends the earlier WAVES benchmark by covering four editing categories (regeneration, global editing, local editing, I2V) across seven models, and by evaluating methods applicable to real images rather than only generated ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

W-Bench provides the first systematic, reproducible evaluation of watermarking robustness against the full spectrum of T2I-based editing, and its results (most methods dropping to near-zero TPR@0.1%FPR) make a compelling case that current watermarking is inadequate for real-world copyright protection in the generative-AI era. The frequency-domain insight—that T2I editing selectively erases mid/high-frequency patterns while preserving low-frequency structure—offers a principled design guideline for future watermarking methods. Key limitations remain: I2V generation still defeats even VINE-R (TPR ~36%), the model is substantially larger and slower than lightweight baselines, and the approach has not been tested on drag-based or multi-image editing paradigms. An open question is whether the surrogate-attack strategy generalizes to future, more diverse editing models without retraining.
