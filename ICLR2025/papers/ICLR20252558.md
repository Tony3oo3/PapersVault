## Problem

Exploration in sparse-reward Contextual MDPs (CMDPs) remains difficult because existing episodic intrinsic motivation methods rely on either count-based bonuses (which become uniform and uninformative in large or noisy state spaces) or similarity-based bonuses using metrics like Euclidean distance in inverse-dynamics embeddings or transition likelihoods, neither of which accurately captures how temporally distant a state truly is from previously visited ones. This paper identifies that the choice of similarity metric is the critical bottleneck limiting exploration effectiveness in CMDPs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw state observations (64×64×3 RGB pixels for Crafter and MiniWorld; grid-encoded observations for MiniGrid) processed through a shared CNN backbone, and maintains an episodic memory of CNN-extracted embeddings for all states visited within the current episode. At each timestep it outputs a scalar intrinsic reward equal to the minimum learned temporal distance between the current state embedding and all stored episodic embeddings, which is added to the sparse extrinsic reward before a PPO policy update. The quasimetric network accepts pairs of embeddings and produces a single asymmetric distance value, enabling all pairwise distances to a new state to be computed in a single batched forward pass.

## Method

ETD learns a temporal distance quasimetric via contrastive learning with a specialized energy-function parameterization: f(x,y) = c_ψ(y) − d_φ(x,y), where c_ψ is a potential network and d_φ is a Metric Residual Network (MRN) producing asymmetric distances. Training uses the symmetrized infoNCE loss on pairs (s_t, s_{t+j}) where j ~ Geom(1−γ), and the theoretical result shows that the unique solution recovers the successor distance d_SD(x,y) = log[p_γ(s_f=y|s_0=y) / p_γ(s_f=y|s_0=x)], which provably satisfies positivity, identity, and the triangle inequality (quasimetric properties). The intrinsic reward at time t is b_ETD(s_t) = min_{k<t} d_φ(s_k, s_t), the minimum temporal distance to any previously visited state in the current episode, providing an aggressive novelty signal that drives the agent toward temporally distant regions. The approach is invariant to state representation (mitigating the noisy-TV problem) and requires no world model or pretraining, unlike competing reachability-based methods.

## Results

Results are reported primarily through training curves (Figures 5, 6, 10, 11) rather than a single numerical summary table. On the most challenging MiniGrid task (ObstructedMaze-Full), ETD reaches near-optimal performance within 20 million environment steps, achieving approximately 2× the sample efficiency of the strongest baseline (NovelD). Under Gaussian noise (mean 0, variance 0.1) added to MiniGrid states, NovelD fails completely while ETD maintains strong performance, demonstrating robustness to the 'noisy-TV' problem. On pixel-based Crafter (64×64×3 observations) and 3D first-person MiniWorld-Maze, ETD consistently outperforms or matches DEIR, NovelD, and PPO without intrinsic rewards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETD outperforms all six baselines across the main MiniGrid benchmark (8 tasks). Against NovelD (count-based), ETD achieves roughly 2× sample efficiency on ObstructedMaze-Full and, critically, remains effective under state noise where NovelD's episodic count mechanism breaks down entirely. Against DEIR (discriminative-model-based episodic reward), ETD wins on both clean and noisy MiniGrid, attributed to more accurate state similarity measurement via temporal distance versus DEIR's discriminative representation. Against E3B (inverse-dynamics-based episodic bonus), ETD similarly outperforms, as E3B's Euclidean distance in inverse-dynamics embedding space fails to capture true temporal proximity (visualized in Figure 2). Against EC (reachability-based), ETD wins because EC's likelihood measure captures only local proximity and cannot distinguish similarity between most states. Against NGU, ETD clearly outperforms; NGU's episodic reward is tailored for Atari and performs poorly in CMDP settings. Against RND (global random network distillation), RND is nearly ineffective in CMDPs because global exploration experience does not generalize across different episode environments. On Crafter and MiniWorld, ETD matches or exceeds DEIR and NovelD, confirming scalability to high-dimensional pixel observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the successor distance framework from Myers et al. (ICML 2024), which was designed for goal-conditioned RL, to the broader setting of sparse reward Contextual MDPs where environments differ across episodes. It departs from prior episodic intrinsic motivation methods (NovelD, E3B, DEIR, EC, NGU) by replacing their similarity metrics—Euclidean distance in inverse-dynamics embeddings or reachability likelihood—with a theoretically grounded quasimetric temporal distance learned via contrastive learning with a potential-minus-quasimetric parameterization. The MRN architecture from Liu et al. (AAAI 2023) provides the asymmetric distance backbone, and the infoNCE loss with geometrically-distributed positive samples follows the CMD formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete failure mode of existing episodic bonuses: their similarity metrics (Euclidean distance, reachability) do not properly capture temporal structure, as demonstrated by the SpiralMaze visualization where inverse dynamics and likelihood methods produce incoherent distance maps. The temporal distance's invariance to state representations directly addresses the noisy-TV problem, making ETD robust where count-based methods (NovelD) fail completely under Gaussian noise. Key limitations include the purely episodic nature (no global bonus, limiting applicability to singleton MDPs), the ergodicity assumption for successor distance, and the implicit POMDP transformation from history-dependent rewards. A promising open direction is combining global and episodic temporal distance bonuses.
