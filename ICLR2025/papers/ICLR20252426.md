## Problem

Existing diffusion-based probabilistic forecasting methods for multivariate time series model the entire data distribution uniformly, ignoring that different components (trend, seasonal, residual) exhibit markedly different levels of uncertainty. Case studies in the paper show that point forecasting models handle trend and seasonal components well but struggle with the residual, which carries substantially more uncertainty. This uniform treatment forces the diffusion model to learn a more complex distribution than necessary, degrading both point and probabilistic forecasting accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a historical multivariate time series x_{1:H} ∈ R^{C×H} (lookback H=96, C variables) and produces both a point prediction ŷ and 100 probabilistic forecast samples. The final prediction is the sum of the point forecast ŷ (from the frozen conditioning network) and a sample ŷ_g drawn from the conditional diffusion model, which operates on the residual r = y − ŷ. The conditioning network can optionally receive a decomposed input (trend + seasonal) rather than the raw series, and the diffusion model is conditioned on the encoder representation f_enc(x_h) rather than the raw point prediction, providing richer guidance for denoising.

## Method

D³U decouples time series forecasting into two complementary sub-problems: a pre-trained point forecasting model (e.g., SparseVQ) captures high-certainty components and supplies its encoder output as conditional information, while a conditional DDPM with a patch-based denoising network (PatchDN) models only the distribution of the prediction error (residual). PatchDN segments the residual into patches, embeds them via linear projection with sinusoidal positional encodings, and injects the condition through per-variable AdaLN layers that modulate scale and shift in each Transformer encoder block—enabling fine-grained, channel-independent control over non-stationary multivariate data. The diffusion model is trained with a standard denoising objective on the residual, and at inference time DPM-Solver reduces sampling to fewer than 20 steps. Because the diffusion model only needs to capture the high-uncertainty component rather than the full distribution, it achieves stable probabilistic forecasting with fewer steps and lower sensitivity to the number of diffusion iterations. The framework is plug-and-play: any pre-trained point forecasting model can serve as the conditioning network, and the approach can also be retrofitted onto existing conditional diffusion methods like TMDM.

## Results

On six real-world MTS datasets (ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather, Solar-Energy, Electricity, Traffic), D³U (SparseVQ+PatchDN) achieves a 28% average MSE improvement and 21% MAE improvement over the SOTA probabilistic method TMDM, while also outperforming all other probabilistic baselines (TimeGrad, CSDI, TimeDiff) by large margins. In probabilistic forecasting, D³U achieves a 40% average CRPS improvement over TMDM (e.g., 0.285 vs. 0.429 on ETTm1, 0.232 vs. 0.552 on Traffic). Point forecasting performance is competitive with SOTA point models, matching SparseVQ on ETTm1 (MSE 0.363) and ETTm2 (MSE 0.241), though iTransformer retains a slight edge on high-dimensional datasets like Electricity (0.164 vs. 0.179) and Traffic (0.418 vs. 0.468).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA probabilistic method TMDM, D³U achieves substantial gains: 28% lower MSE (e.g., 0.363 vs. 0.607 on ETTm1, 0.468 vs. 0.721 on Traffic) and 40% lower CRPS (0.285 vs. 0.429 on ETTm1, 0.232 vs. 0.552 on Traffic). All other probabilistic baselines (TimeGrad, CSDI, TimeDiff) are outperformed by wide margins on both MSE and CRPS across all six datasets; TimeDiff, the closest probabilistic competitor, still trails D³U by 2–5× in MSE on most datasets. In point forecasting, D³U matches or slightly exceeds SparseVQ (its conditioning network) on ETTm1 and ETTm2, and is competitive with PatchTST and iTransformer on low-dimensional datasets (ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather), though iTransformer retains a 5–15% MSE advantage on high-dimensional datasets (Electricity: 0.164 vs. 0.179; Traffic: 0.418 vs. 0.468). The key differentiator is that D³U simultaneously delivers near-SOTA point accuracy and the best probabilistic calibration (CRPS) among all compared methods, whereas pure point models lack probabilistic output and prior probabilistic models sacrifice point accuracy for distributional modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based probabilistic time series forecasting (TimeDiff, CSDI, TMDM) by introducing a decoupling principle: a pre-trained point forecasting model handles high-certainty components while a conditional DDPM models only the residual (high-uncertainty) distribution. The closest prior work, TMDM (Li et al., 2024a), also pairs a point forecasting model with a diffusion model but models the full data distribution without separating deterministic from uncertain components. The patch-based denoising network (PatchDN) draws on PatchTST's patching strategy and DiT/FiLM's AdaLN conditioning from computer vision, adapting them to the channel-independent time series setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling approach reduces the complexity of the distribution the diffusion model must learn, enabling stable probabilistic forecasting with fewer sampling steps (50–70 vs. 100) and a plug-and-play integration with any existing point forecasting model. A key open question the paper identifies is that for high-dimensional datasets (Electricity, Traffic), epistemic uncertainty in the point model's prediction error contaminates the residual, inflating prediction intervals and degrading CRPS_sum; better separation of epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty is flagged as future work. The practical finding that encoder representations (f_enc) provide superior conditioning signals compared to the point prediction output (ŷ) is a useful design insight for future conditional generative time series models.
