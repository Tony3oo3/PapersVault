## Problem

Modern LLMs consistently fail at intrinsic self-correction: when asked to review and revise their own responses without external feedback, they either make no meaningful edits or degrade correct answers. Prior training approaches—prompting, supervised fine-tuning on offline correction traces (STaR, Pair-SFT), and naive multi-turn RL—suffer from distribution shift (trained on another policy's mistakes) or behavior collapse (learning to produce a strong first answer and then making superficial or no changes), leaving a significant gap in how to teach a single model to genuinely fix its own errors using only self-generated data.

## Input / Output

The model receives a problem (math or code), its own previously generated attempt, and a self-correction instruction that explicitly does not reveal whether the prior answer was correct—instead asking the model to 'deduce whether a mistake exists' and rewrite if needed. The output is a full revised solution or program. At test time, no oracle reward or answer checker is available; the model must internally determine if its first attempt contains an error. Evaluation uses two sequential attempts (one round of self-correction) with greedy decoding, measuring both per-attempt accuracy and the net self-correction delta between attempts.

## Method

SCoRe is a two-stage on-policy multi-turn RL method using REINFORCE/RLOO policy gradients with KL-divergence regularization. Stage I trains the model to maximize second-attempt reward while applying a strong KL penalty (β₂) that constrains the first-attempt distribution to remain close to the base model, thereby decoupling the two attempts and preventing the collapse where both turns converge to the same response. Stage II then jointly optimizes reward across both attempts, but introduces a reward shaping bonus b(y₂|y₁,y*) = α·(r(y₂,y*) − r(y₁,y*)) on the second attempt that explicitly rewards correctness flips (wrong→right) and penalizes regressions (right→wrong), steering the policy toward genuine self-correction rather than the degenerate 'best first answer, no edits' strategy. The key insight is that without this shaped reward and the decoupling initialization, standard multi-turn RL finds the 'direct' solution (optimize first-attempt accuracy, ignore correction) equally optimal on training data, making the meta-strategy of self-correction invisible to the optimizer.

## Results

On MATH (Gemini 1.5 Flash), SCoRe achieves 60.0% accuracy at the first attempt and 64.4% at the second, yielding a self-correction delta Δ(t1,t2) of +4.4% — the first significantly positive intrinsic self-correction gain reported, improving the base model's Δ by 15.6 percentage points (from −11.2% to +4.4%). On HumanEval (Gemini 1.0 Pro), SCoRe achieves a 12.2% self-correction delta (52.4% → 64.6% accuracy) and 60.6% on the offline repair benchmark MBPP-R, improving the base model by 9.1% in Δ(t1,t2). SCoRe also reduces the rate of correct-to-incorrect flips from 15.8% (base) to 1.4% on MATH, indicating the model learns when *not* to modify a correct answer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCoRe outperforms all three baselines on both MATH and HumanEval. Against Self-Refine (prompting-based), SCoRe achieves a +4.4% self-correction delta on MATH versus −1.0% for Self-Refine, and +12.2% versus −1.2% on HumanEval — Self-Refine actually degrades performance in the intrinsic setting. Against STaR (SFT on successful repair traces), SCoRe's 4.4% delta on MATH is over 10× larger than STaR's 0.4%, and STaR fails to reduce correct-to-incorrect flips as effectively. Against Pair-SFT (SFT on synthetically paired traces), SCoRe achieves 4.4% vs. 1.8% on MATH and, critically, 12.2% vs. −1.8% on HumanEval, where Pair-SFT actually *degrades* the base model's self-correction ability despite performing comparably on the static repair task MBPP-R (59.8% vs. 60.6%). These results underscore that on-policy multi-turn RL with reward shaping is essential for genuine intrinsic self-correction, whereas offline SFT methods suffer from distribution shift and behavior collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCoRe extends the REINFORCE-style RL fine-tuning framework of Ahmadian et al. (2024) to a multi-turn setting using the hierarchical RL structure of Zhou et al. (2024), and formalizes the self-correction problem following Welleck et al. (2023). It departs sharply from the dominant SFT-based self-training paradigm (STaR, Pair-SFT) by demonstrating that both offline SFT and naive on-policy multi-turn RL fail for self-correction due to behavior collapse—the model learns to produce a strong first answer and make superficial edits rather than genuinely correcting mistakes. The key methodological departure is a two-stage RL design with a progress-based reward bonus that explicitly incentivizes correctness flips between turns, rather than merely maximizing final-answer accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve significantly positive intrinsic self-correction (15.6% on MATH, 9.1% on HumanEval) without external feedback, teacher models, or multi-model inference, establishing that RL with appropriate regularization is essential for learning meta-strategies like self-correction. The finding that even on-policy RL collapses without reward shaping challenges the assumption that on-policy training alone suffices for nuanced behavioral learning. Open questions include scaling beyond two turns (performance plateaus), unifying the two training stages, and whether the approach generalizes to settings without an oracle reward function during training.
