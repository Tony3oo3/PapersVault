## Problem

Existing matrix decomposition methods for LLM compression either discard large portions of matrix rank (causing significant accuracy loss) or introduce substantial parameter overheads through additional adapters (as in SliceGPT, which adds up to 10% extra parameters). The fundamental limitation is that prior approaches compress each weight matrix independently, preventing true hidden-dimension reduction without auxiliary structures, and they lack a unified theoretical framework connecting compression to classical matrix approximation with error guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (LLaMA-1/2/3, OPT at various scales) and a small calibration set of 128 text sequences of 2048 tokens (from WikiText-2 or Alpaca) to compute empirical correlation matrices in FP64. It outputs compressed weight matrices with reduced intermediate dimensions within each module—for example, the MLP up matrix W_U ∈ R^{d_h × d_int} becomes W_U S_k ∈ R^{d_h × k} with k < d_int—while preserving the original input/output dimensions of the transformer layer. The entire compression process runs on a single A100 GPU in a few hours without any backward propagation or recovery fine-tuning.

## Method

MoDeGPT partitions each transformer layer into three functional modules based on nonlinearity count: Type-I (MLP up/down with 1 nonlinearity), Type-II (key/query with 2 nonlinearities), and Type-III (value/output with 0 nonlinearities). For each module, it jointly optimizes the pair of weight matrices to minimize a modular reconstruction error over calibration data, with the key structural constraint that matrices inside nonlinear functions are compressed via column selection (W → W S_k) while the paired matrix is freely optimized. This constraint makes the optimization tractable and establishes a one-to-one mapping to classical decompositions: Type-I reduces to Nyström approximation of the activation correlation matrix C_σ, Type-II reduces to CR decomposition of C_K^{1/2} C_Q^{1/2}, and Type-II reduces to SVD of C^{1/2} W_V W_O. A global sparsity allocation strategy using Block Influence scores and entropic regularization (Theorem 4) distributes compression non-uniformly across layers, and the entire pipeline requires only forward passes with closed-form solutions per module.

## Results

On WikiText-2 perplexity, MoDeGPT (no gradient) achieves 6.16 at 20% compression and 7.51 at 30% compression on LLAMA-2 7B, outperforming SliceGPT (7.68 and 10.47) and matching or beating gradient-based LLM Surgeon (6.18 and 7.83). On zero-shot tasks, MoDeGPT-Alpaca retains 62.02% average accuracy at 30% compression on LLAMA-2 7B (vs. 69.00% dense) and 63.96% at 25% on LLAMA-3 8B (vs. 72.75% dense), substantially outperforming SliceGPT-Alpaca (46.17%) and ShortGPT-Alpaca (43.24%) on the latter. On LLAMA-2 70B at 30% compression, MoDeGPT with Alpaca calibration achieves 66.79% average zero-shot accuracy (only 3.23% drop from dense 70.02%). Compression of the 13B model completes in 8h26m on a single A100 GPU (98.5% cost reduction vs. LLM Surgeon's 1d9h26m on 8×H100), and inference throughput increases by up to 46%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoDeGPT outperforms all non-gradient baselines (SliceGPT, ShortGPT, SLEB, Uniform, Magnitude, SVD) at every compression rate tested. Against SliceGPT, the closest non-gradient competitor, MoDeGPT reduces perplexity by 28% at 30% compression on LLAMA-2 7B (7.51 vs. 10.47) and by 30% on 13B (6.10 vs. 8.68); on zero-shot tasks the gap widens to 7+ points average accuracy. Against gradient-based LLM Surgeon, MoDeGPT wins at 20%+ compression on both 7B (6.16 vs. 6.18 at 20%; 7.51 vs. 7.83 at 30%) and 13B (6.10 vs. 6.21 at 30%), while LLM Surgeon retains a slight edge only at 10% compression (5.25 vs. 5.48 on 7B). MoDeGPT decisively beats LLM-Pruner (13.56 vs. 7.51 at 30% on 7B) and K-OBD (15.43 vs. 7.51 at 30% on 7B), both of which require backward propagation. On the large-scale LLAMA-2 70B experiment, MoDeGPT+Alpaca achieves 66.79% average zero-shot accuracy at 30% compression, surpassing SliceGPT (52.65%), ShortGPT (48.06%), and SLEB (59.79%) by 7–14 points. The 30–40% compressed MoDeGPT models also outperform 2:4 SparseGPT (semi-structured) on perplexity, demonstrating that fully structured compression can rival semi-structured methods without gradient-based recovery fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoDeGPT extends the matrix decomposition lineage for LLM compression by elevating the unit of decomposition from individual weight matrices to functional modules (MLP, key-query, value-output pairs). It repurposes three classical approximation algorithms—Nyström, CR decomposition, and SVD—each mapped to a module type based on its nonlinearity count, a categorization not present in prior work. The implementation borrows infrastructure from SliceGPT (correlation matrix estimation via Catcher, layer-norm conversion) and the deterministic ridge leverage score sampling from McCurdy (2018), but departs fundamentally from SliceGPT by reducing intermediate dimensions within modules rather than between blocks, thereby eliminating the adapter overhead that SliceGPT introduces. Unlike gradient-based structured pruning methods (LLM-Pruner, LLM Surgeon, OBD), MoDeGPT requires only forward passes on calibration data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 98% reduction in compression compute cost relative to the best gradient-based method, 46% inference throughput gains, and 90–95% zero-shot retention at 25–30% compression across LLaMA-2/3 and OPT models up to 70B. The theoretical error bounds (Propositions 1–3) provide the first systematic characterization of how nonlinearity count in a module affects compressibility, explaining why Type-II (key-query) is most sensitive. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-sequential or mixture-of-experts architectures, whether the Type-II error bound can be tightened, and whether the SVD-dominated compression time for value-output modules can be reduced via randomized SVD. The paper also notes that key-query dimension reduction may slow generation at compression rates below 10%, an engineering trade-off not yet fully resolved.
