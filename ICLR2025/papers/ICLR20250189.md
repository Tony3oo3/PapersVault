## Problem

No existing deep learning model simultaneously answers three fundamental interpretability questions: predicting class labels ('What?'), simulating concept-level interventions to assess their impact on predictions ('How?'), and generating interpretable counterfactuals that explain how to achieve a different class label ('Why not?'). Concept Bottleneck Models address the first two but cannot produce counterfactuals, while post-hoc counterfactual generators handle 'What?' and 'Why not?' but fail on 'How?' for unstructured data like images, where pixel-level modifications are scattered and semantically incoherent.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an image encoded by a pre-trained backbone (ResNet-18, CLIP ViT-B/16, or CXR-CLIP depending on the dataset) and, for counterfactual generation, a desired target class label y' (sampled uniformly during training, user-specified at test time). Outputs include predicted concept labels (binary), a class label prediction, and a counterfactual concept vector c' with its associated class label y'—all at the interpretable concept level rather than the raw pixel level. The model supports two inference modes: 'best bet' (MAP estimate of the most probable counterfactual) and 'multiverse' (sampling multiple counterfactuals from the posterior for user choice).

## Method

CF-CBMs extend the CBM graphical model x→c→y by introducing two continuous latent variables z and z' that encode factual and counterfactual concept dependencies, respectively, with z' explicitly conditioned on z, the factual concepts c, and the class label y. The generative model factorizes as p(c,y|z)·p(c',y'|z')·p(x|z)·p(z)·p(z'|z,c,y), where concepts are modeled as Bernoulli and class labels as Categorical, both parameterized by shared predictors. Training optimizes an ELBO objective jointly across the concept predictor, task predictor, and counterfactual generator, with an additional posterior/prior distance term (Eq. 5) that constrains counterfactuals to be minimally different from the factual concepts. This end-to-end joint training—rather than post-hoc counterfactual generation—forces the task predictor to rely on fewer decisive concepts (reducing confounding sensitivity) and increases the Causal Concept Effect of interventions, yielding simpler explanations and more responsive concept-level reasoning.

## Results

CF-CBMs achieve counterfactual validity of 99.06% on average across five datasets, outperforming the best post-hoc baseline (VCNet at 96.6%) and the weakest (BayCon at 90.4%). On the complex CUB dataset (118 concepts, 200 classes), CF-CBMs reach 99.0% validity versus 66.2% for BayCon and 78.7% for C-CHVAE. In terms of prediction generalization, CF-CBMs match standard CBMs and black-box models (e.g., 99.7% task accuracy on dSprites, 92.0% on SIIM Pneumothorax vs. 89.4% for CBM). Jointly training the counterfactual generator reduces the Causal Concept Effect of confounding features from 0.17 to 0.02 on modified dSprites, indicating the model relies on fewer, more relevant concepts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CF-CBMs match or slightly exceed standard CBMs (Koh et al., 2020) and Concept Embedding Models (Espinosa Zarlenga et al., 2022) in task and concept accuracy while adding counterfactual generation and task-driven intervention capabilities that neither baseline supports. Against the four post-hoc counterfactual generators adapted to the concept space, CF-CBMs consistently win on validity: +8.7 p.p. over BayCon (Romashov et al., 2022), +7.4 p.p. over C-CHVAE (Pawelczyk et al., 2020), +5.7 p.p. over VAE-CF (Mahajan et al., 2020), and +2.5 p.p. over VCNet (Guyomard et al., 2023) on average. The gap is most pronounced on CUB, where CF-CBMs achieve 99.0% validity versus 66.2% (BayCon) and 78.7% (C-CHVAE), reflecting the difficulty of iterative search in high-dimensional concept spaces. CF-CBMs also generate counterfactuals with lower Δ-Sparsity (fewer unnecessary concept changes) and higher IoU (plausibility) than all baselines, while matching the generation speed of VAE-CF and VCNet (sub-second) and far outperforming the iterative BayCon and C-CHVAE (minutes to hours on CUB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Concept Bottleneck Models (Koh et al., 2020), which answer 'what?' and 'how?' queries, by introducing a variational latent variable component (following Kingma & Welling, 2013) that generates concept-level counterfactuals to also answer 'why not?' queries. It departs from post-hoc counterfactual methods (BayCon, C-CHVAE, VAE-CF, VCNet) that operate at the input level or require iterative search, by integrating counterfactual generation directly into the model architecture via joint training. The paper also builds on the Causal Concept Effect (Goyal et al., 2019) to quantify how joint training reshapes concept importance, and on Concept Embedding Models (Espinosa Zarlenga et al., 2022) as a stronger CBM baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CF-CBMs provide a unified, real-time framework for classification, concept intervention, and counterfactual explanation without post-hoc search, which is practically important for interactive interpretability in domains like medical imaging. The finding that joint training of the counterfactual generator reshapes the model's reliance on concepts (fewer, more causally important ones) suggests a new lever for improving model robustness against confounders. Limitations include the need for concept annotations, no guarantee of counterfactual optimality versus exact methods, and inheritance of standard CBM weaknesses (concept impurities, information bottlenecks). Open questions include whether the joint-training benefit generalizes to very large concept spaces or multi-modal settings, and whether the approach can be extended to continuous-valued concepts.
