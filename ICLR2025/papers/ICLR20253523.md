## Problem

State-of-the-art speculative decoding methods like EAGLE-2 suffer from two architectural limitations: partial observability (the draft model only receives the final layer's activations rather than the full LLM state) and lack of on-policyness (training assumes perfect inputs, but at inference the draft model generates its own less-accurate tokens, causing distribution shift). These issues degrade acceptance rates and limit the practical viability of speculative decoding, especially in client-server deployments where the small model must operate with limited access to the large model.

## Input / Output

The draft model consumes the full KV cache of all L layers of the LLM (shape T×L×2E_kv), token embeddings of the generated sequence, and the most recent verified token. It outputs a sequence of drafted tokens (up to K per cycle) that are verified in a single parallel forward pass by the LLM using tree attention. The LSA layer compresses the multi-layer KV cache into a T×2E_kv representation computed once per drafting cycle, while the SA and CA layers process tokens auto-regressively within each cycle. In the client-server setting, the server sends accepted tokens plus associated activations (quantized to 8-bit and gzip-compressed for the initial prompt), while the client sends draft tree nodes encoded as 3-byte token IDs plus 1-byte tree positions.

## Method

The proposed Mixture of Attentions architecture combines three attention mechanisms in the draft model: (1) Layer Self-Attention (LSA) performs bidirectional self-attention over the layer dimension of the LLM's KV cache, aggregating information across all L layers into a compact representation to mitigate partial observability; (2) a causal Self-Attention (SA) layer on token queries ensures awareness of previously drafted tokens; and (3) a Cross-Attention (CA) layer where queries come from SA outputs and keys/values come from LSA, making the drafter T-step bounded (predicting up to K future tokens in one forward pass) to enable more on-policy training with variable-K masking. A Target Layer Inference (TLI) hyperparameter lets the draft model predict an intermediate layer's activation and reuse the last N frozen decoder layers of the LLM, trading drafting speed against accuracy. Training uses a combined forward-KL and Smooth-L1 loss between predicted and target activations, with no generation from either model during training to keep it lightweight.

## Results

On SpecBench (single-device, Llama3-8B-Instruct), the Mixture of Attentions architecture with TLI=1 achieves a mean speedup of 1.95× and mean acceptance length τ of 4.50, improving over EAGLE-2 (1.78×, τ=3.60) by 9.5% in decoding speed and 25% in acceptance length. In the client-server setting on HumanEval under 5G, Ours (TLI=0) reaches 34.1 tokens/sec with τ=4.30 and only 24.1 calls to M_Large, versus EAGLE-2's 24.3 tokens/sec, τ=2.81, and 36.4 calls. Under 4G, the gap widens to 25.1 vs 13.6 tokens/sec. In the disconnection scenario (Table 4), Ours (TLI=3) achieves 18.01% pass@1 on HumanEval after 25 tokens of disconnection, outperforming EAGLE-2 (16.77%) and all independent models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EAGLE-2 (the primary SOTA baseline), the proposed method wins on both speedup and acceptance length across all six SpecBench datasets: mean speedup improves from 1.78× to 1.95× (+9.5%) and mean τ from 3.60 to 4.50 (+25%) at TLI=1. Against the official EAGLE-2 checkpoint (trained on different data with a fixed system prompt), the gap is even larger at +13.7% speedup and +31% acceptance length. Compared to Glide (Du et al., 2024), the method achieves +12.1% speedup and +20.4% acceptance length. Against independent small models (1.3B and 1.7B), the self-drafting approach dominates by a wide margin: +58.5% speedup over the 1.3B independent model and +23.4% over the 1.7B independent model, despite using fewer trainable parameters (250M vs 650M). In the client-server setting, Ours (TLI=0) achieves 34.1 tok/s under 5G versus EAGLE-2's 24.3 tok/s (+40%), with 33% fewer calls to M_Large (24.1 vs 36.4), and the advantage grows under 4G (25.1 vs 13.6 tok/s, +85%). In the disconnection robustness test, Ours (TLI=3) outperforms EAGLE-2 at every disconnection point (e.g., 11.18% vs 8.07% at B=10, 31.67% vs 27.32% at B=50), confirming that the LSA-enhanced architecture retains more LLM knowledge for offline generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EAGLE-2, the current state-of-the-art self-drafting speculative decoding architecture, and departs from it by introducing a Mixture of Attentions design that addresses two identified architectural limitations: partial observability (mitigated via Layer Self-Attention over the full KV-cache of all LLM layers) and lack of on-policyness during training (addressed via a Cross-Attention layer that simulates the drafting-time information asymmetry). The Target Layer Inference (TLI) mechanism further extends the design space by allowing the drafter to predict intermediate-layer activations and reuse frozen LLM decoder layers, a concept not present in EAGLE or Medusa. The paper also extends the speculative decoding paradigm to a client-server deployment setting, which prior work (e.g., Tandem Transformers) acknowledged as impractical due to communication overhead but did not systematically address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The client-server speculative decoding framework opens a practical path toward serving LLMs on edge devices with offline fallback capability, which is a significant deployment consideration as LLMs move toward consumer hardware. The TLI hyperparameter provides a simple, interpretable trade-off knob between drafting speed and accuracy that could be dynamically adapted to network conditions, though the paper leaves this as future work. Limitations include evaluation on a single LLM family (Llama3-8B) and a single small-model size (250M trainable parameters), and the client-server experiments rely on simulated network profiles rather than real-world deployment. Whether the Mixture of Attentions architecture generalizes to much larger LLMs or substantially different architectures remains an open question.
