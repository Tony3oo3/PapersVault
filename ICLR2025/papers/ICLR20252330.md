## Problem

While the high-level training pipeline for MLLMs (pre-training → SFT) is well-established, the empirical details—optimal data mixtures per capability, the role of a high-resolution continual pre-training stage, and the cross-impact of each SFT data category on other capabilities—remain largely unexplored. Additionally, fine-grained visual referring and grounding is under-explored in open-source MLLMs, with even strong proprietary models relying on set-of-mark prompting to reference image regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252330/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MM1.5 accepts arbitrary-aspect-ratio RGB images (single or multi) at resolutions up to 4 Megapixels (e.g., 2016×2016 square or 6048×672 long), along with text prompts and visual prompts in the form of point coordinates or bounding boxes. Outputs are free-form text that can embed grounded bounding box coordinates inline, enabling the model to both interpret spatial references in the input and produce spatially grounded responses. Dynamic image splitting divides each input into a variable number of sub-images (4–9) plus a global overview image, with index tuples (k, i, j) marking each sub-image's position in the original 2D layout.

## Method

MM1.5 retains the MM1 architecture (CLIP image encoder → C-Abstractor connector → LLM backbone) but introduces a three-stage data-centric training recipe: (i) large-scale pre-training with a rebalanced 50:10:40 ratio of image-text, interleaved, and text-only data (shifting weight toward text-only to boost knowledge benchmarks); (ii) a new high-resolution continual pre-training stage on 45M OCR samples at 1344×1344, which the ablations show is essential for text-rich understanding; and (iii) SFT on a carefully tuned mixture of 80% single-image (37.2% text-rich, 22.5% refer&ground, 11.3% general, 5.6% math, 2.3% code, 1.1% science), 10% multi-image, and 10% text-only data. For high-resolution processing, the model uses dynamic image splitting with (n_min, n_max) = (4, 9), placing the overview image after sub-images so the autoregressive decoder can attend to all local patches. The family spans 1B–30B dense parameters plus 1B-MoE and 3B-MoE variants (64 experts, top-2 gating), demonstrating that careful data curation yields competitive performance even at small scales.

## Results

MM1.5-30B achieves 91.4 ANLS on DocVQA, 67.3 ANLS on InfoVQA, 47.4% on MMMU, 55.6% on MathVista, and 90.1% RefCOCO avg., representing substantial gains over MM1-30B (75.8, 47.3, 44.7, 39.4, and no grounding). At the 3B scale, MM1.5-3B reaches 87.7 DocVQA, 58.5 InfoVQA, 44.4 MathVista, and 85.6 RefCOCO avg., while the 3B-MoE variant further improves MMMU to 42.9 and MathVista to 46.9. The 1B-MoE model achieves 84.8 DocVQA and 41.2 MMMU, outperforming all comparable small-scale models. Across all scales, MM1.5 introduces native visual referring and grounding (RefCOCO, LVIS) and multi-image reasoning (NLVR2, VL-ICL) capabilities absent in MM1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B at the 1B scale, MM1.5-1B wins decisively on DocVQA (81.0 vs. 70.0), InfoVQA (50.5 vs. 41.8), and NLVR2 (79.0 vs. 63.4), while also adding grounding and in-context learning capabilities the baseline lacks. Compared to MiniCPM-V 2.0-3B, MM1.5-3B achieves substantially higher DocVQA (87.7 vs. 71.9) and InfoVQA (58.5 vs. 37.6), and supports visual referring/grounding which MiniCPM-V does not. Against Phi-3-Vision-4B, MM1.5-3B leads on DocVQA (87.7 vs. 83.3), InfoVQA (58.5 vs. 49.0), RefCOCO (85.6 vs. 38.1), and VL-ICL (56.3 vs. 19.5), though Phi-3-Vision retains a small edge on MMMU (40.4 vs. 37.1) and RealWorldQA (59.4 vs. 56.9). Versus InternVL2-2B, MM1.5-3B wins on RefCOCO (85.6 vs. 77.7), LVIS (67.9 vs. 51.1), NLVR2 (83.8 vs. 67.4), and VL-ICL (56.3 vs. 18.5), but trails slightly on MathVista (44.4 vs. 46.0). The MoE variants consistently outperform their dense counterparts: MM1.5-3B-MoE surpasses MM1.5-7B on MMMU (42.9 vs. 41.8), MathVista (46.9 vs. 47.6), SEED-1 (73.3 vs. 73.4), and NLVR2 (86.0 vs. 86.9), while MM1.5-1B-MoE leads MM1.5-1B by 3.8 points on DocVQA and 5.4 on MMMU. Overall, MM1.5 delivers the strongest text-rich and grounding performance at every scale, with the 30B model setting new records on DocVQA (91.4), InfoVQA (67.3), and MathVista (55.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

MM1.5 retains the exact architecture of MM1 (CLIP encoder, C-Abstractor connector, LLM backbone) and the referring/grounding formulation from Ferret, deliberately isolating the data-centric training recipe as the variable of study. Unlike most open-source MLLMs (LLaVA series, InternVL2, Cambrian-1) that focus primarily on SFT data, MM1.5 systematically ablates the full training lifecycle—pre-training data ratios, a new high-resolution continual pre-training stage with OCR data, and SFT mixture composition. The dynamic image splitting follows the AnyRes paradigm from LLaVA-NeXT and InternLM-XComposer-2.5, but with thorough ablations on grid configuration, position indicators, and overview image placement that prior works leave underexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides one of the most comprehensive empirical 'recipes' for building competitive MLLMs, with detailed ablations showing how each data category cross-impacts other capabilities (e.g., text-rich data boosting knowledge scores, refer&ground data causing slight regression elsewhere). The finding that high-quality synthetic captions do not clearly outperform simpler OCR data for continual pre-training challenges a common assumption in the field and suggests further investigation is needed. Open questions include whether the synthetic caption gap closes at larger scales, how to unify video understanding (previewed as MM1.5-Video), and whether the data-centric insights transfer to fundamentally different architectures. The practical impact is significant for teams building small-scale (1B–3B) MLLMs, as the recipe demonstrates competitive performance without requiring frontier-scale compute.
