## Problem

Evaluating LLM-generated subjective judgments (e.g., academic peer reviews) lacks a gold standard reference, making standard NLG metrics inapplicable. While LLM-as-examiner approaches (e.g., GPT-4o Examiner) offer a workaround, they are vulnerable to trivial manipulations such as rephrasing or meaningless elongation that artificially inflate scores. The paper asks whether an accurate, manipulation-resistant, automated evaluation metric can be built for open-ended textual responses without any gold standard.

## Input / Output

The metric takes a candidate response and a peer reference response (which need not be gold standard), plus optionally a task synopsis such as a paper abstract for the GEM-S variant. Before scoring, both responses are preprocessed by an LLM (GPT-4o) into a standardized compressed format of one-line judgments (e.g., 'The reviewer appreciates…'). The evaluation-LM (Llama-3.1 8B or 70B) then computes token-level conditional and marginal log-probabilities to estimate point-wise mutual information, and the final benchmark score is the arithmetic mean of PMI estimates over the dataset.

## Method

GEM estimates Shannon mutual information between candidate and reference responses using a generative LLM's token prediction function: it computes log Pr_LLM[Y=y|X=x] by multiplying per-token conditional probabilities with the candidate response in the prompt, and subtracts log Pr_LLM[Y=y] for the marginal. A preprocessing step rephrases both responses into a fixed compressed format to filter out stylistic and syntactic shortcuts that could confound the LLM's predictions. GEM-S further conditions on a task synopsis (e.g., the paper abstract or a summary of author-stated strengths/weaknesses), measuring only the additional semantic information the candidate reveals beyond superficial content. The theoretical guarantee (Proposition 3.1) shows that under a bounded KL-divergence assumption on the LLM's distribution estimate, a candidate whose information structure Blackwell-dominates another will achieve a higher GEM score in the large-sample limit, linking the metric to the information elicitation literature on peer prediction and f-mutual information.

## Results

On a human-annotated peer-grading dataset, GEM-S achieves a Spearman's ρ of 0.479 (p=7.4e-11) with instructor grades, outperforming all baselines except GPT-4o LMExaminer (ρ=0.537). GEM and GEM-S are the only metrics that show statistically significant score decreases on all three semantic degradations (Sentence Deletion, Deletion & Completion, Abstract-only Review) while simultaneously showing no significant score increases under any manipulation (GPT-4o rephrase, Llama-3.1 rephrase, meaningless elongation). On GRE-bench (ICLR 2023), Claude-3-opus achieves the highest scores (GEM: 25.77, GEM-S(abstract): 11.52, GEM-S(ASSW): 5.89), and within each LLM family, larger parameter sizes consistently yield higher scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM-S (ρ=0.479) outperforms all traditional NLG baselines—BLEU (0.023), ROUGE-L (−0.244), BERTScore (−0.061), and all BARTScore variants (−0.511 to 0.164)—on correlation with human-annotated quality scores, though it trails GPT-4o LMExaminer (0.537) by 0.058. The critical differentiator is manipulation robustness: LMExaminer shows significant score *increases* after GPT-4o rephrase (SMD=+0.187), Llama-3.1 rephrase (+0.104), and meaningless elongation (+0.105), while GEM and GEM-S show no significant increases under any manipulation. On degradation sensitivity, GEM and GEM-S are the only metrics that significantly penalize all three degradations including Abstract-only Review (GEM-S SMD=−0.566), where LMExaminer actually *increases* its score (+0.715). BARTScore-precision shows the worst behavior, with large positive SMDs on degradations (e.g., +0.627 on Sentence Deletion), indicating it rewards information loss. The paper also validates GEM on a Yelp restaurant review dataset, confirming similar patterns of degradation sensitivity and manipulation robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges the information elicitation literature (peer prediction, Blackwell order, f-mutual information from Kong & Schoenebeck 2019 and Lu et al. 2024) with the NLG evaluation problem, repurposing mutual information as a scoring criterion where prior metrics (BARTScore, GPTScore) require gold-standard references. It departs from the LMExaminer paradigm (Bai et al., 2024) by replacing direct LLM-as-judge scoring with an information-theoretic quantity that is provably manipulation-resistant under Blackwell dominance. The preprocessing step to strip stylistic 'shortcuts' and the conditional MI formulation (GEM-S) extend the GPPM/GSPPM mechanisms from Lu et al. (2024) by using mutual information gain rather than raw prediction accuracy as the evaluation signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most NLG metrics require gold-standard references, and LLM-as-examiner approaches are demonstrably gameable (meaningless elongation inflates GPT-4o scores significantly). GRE-bench offers a contamination-resistant benchmark for peer review that updates annually with new open-access papers. Key limitations include the dependence on human-generated reference data (risk of model collapse if all references become LLM-generated) and the strong KL-divergence assumption in the theoretical guarantee. Open questions include whether GEM generalizes to other subjective domains (initial Yelp results are promising) and whether the manipulation resistance holds as preprocessing models improve.
