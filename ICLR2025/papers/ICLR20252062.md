## Problem

Masked Image Modeling (MIM) models like MAE and data2vec 2.0 suffer from a fundamental architectural flaw: their lightweight decoder eventually delegates reconstruction work back to the last encoder blocks, degrading representation quality precisely where downstream tasks need it most. This causes MIM models to perform poorly in low-shot and off-the-shelf settings, while Instance Discrimination (ID) methods that form semantic clusters scale poorly to billion-parameter models. The paper asks whether the strong intermediate representations of MIM models can be leveraged to bridge this gap without retraining from scratch.

## Input / Output

MIM-Refiner takes a pre-trained MIM ViT encoder (e.g., MAE, data2vec 2.0, CrossMAE, dBOT at ViT-L/16, ViT-H/14, or ViT-2B/14 scale) and refines it in-place for 20–30 epochs on the same ImageNet-1K data. Multiple ID heads are attached to the last third of encoder blocks (last 8 for ViT-L, last 12 for ViT-H), each producing a contrastive loss on patch-level features. The refined encoder outputs visual features usable for classification, segmentation, clustering, and transfer; the method does not alter the encoder architecture or add parameters to the backbone itself.

## Method

MIM-Refiner is a sequential MIM→ID refinement that attaches an ensemble of Instance Discrimination heads to multiple intermediate encoder blocks (not just the final block), targeting the blocks where representation quality peaks before the late blocks degrade under reconstruction pressure. The contrastive objective is Nearest Neighbor Alignment (NNA), a variant of NNCLR that retrieves the nearest neighbor from a FIFO queue exclusively for positive alignment while using in-batch samples as negatives without NN-swap, which stabilizes training at billion-parameter scale. Training uses a layer-wise learning rate decay of 0.65 to preserve early-block MIM features while allowing later blocks to form semantic clusters. The key architectural insight is that the asymmetric encoder-decoder design of MIM creates three distinct block regimes (general features, abstraction, reconstruction), and the refinement specifically targets the transition zone where abstraction peaks and reconstruction takes over.

## Results

D2V2-Refined-H/14 sets a new state-of-the-art in linear probing at 84.7% and 1-shot classification at 64.2% on ImageNet-1K, outperforming MAWS-6.5B (63.6%) which was pre-trained on 2000× more data. The method dramatically improves MIM models across scales: MAE-L/16 k-NN jumps from 60.6% to 81.5%, and MAE-2B/14 k-NN from 67.1% to 83.2%. In clustering, D2V2-Refined-L/16 achieves 67.4% k-means ACC, surpassing the previous SOTA of 61.6% (TEMI MSN). Full fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K also improves slightly (e.g., D2V2-H/14: 86.6% → 86.8%), confirming no degradation in the many-shot regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIM-Refiner dramatically outperforms unrefined MIM baselines: MAE-L/16 1-shot improves from 14.3% to 57.8% (MAE-Refined) and D2V2-L/16 from 24.1% to 61.7% (D2V2-Refined), while k-NN jumps from 60.6%/51.8% to 81.5%/81.0% respectively. Against ID methods, D2V2-Refined-L/16 beats iBOT (48.5%→61.7% 1-shot) and Mugs (52.9%→61.7% 1-shot) by 13+ points in 1-shot, and also surpasses them in k-NN (81.0% vs 78.0%/80.4%). The previous sequential MIM→ID method MAE-CT (Lehner et al., 2024) is outperformed by MAE-Refined-H/14 by 10.1 points in 1-shot (59.5% vs 49.4%) and 3.2 points in k-NN (82.3% vs 79.1%). Against DINOv2-g (trained on 142M images), D2V2-Refined-H/14 wins in 1-shot (64.2% vs 60.5%) and k-NN (82.3% vs 83.5% is a narrow loss), but DINOv2-g retains an edge in linear probing (86.5% vs 84.7%). In clustering, D2V2-Refined-L/16 achieves 67.4% ACC, beating TEMI MSN (61.6%) and DINOv2-g (47.7%) by large margins. In full fine-tuning (100% labels), MIM-Refiner provides small but consistent gains (e.g., D2V2-H/14: 86.6%→86.8% IN-1K), confirming no regression in the many-shot regime where MIM models traditionally excel.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIM-Refiner sits at the intersection of two SSL paradigms: Masked Image Modeling (MAE, data2vec 2.0) and Instance Discrimination (iBOT, Mugs, NNCLR). It departs from prior sequential MIM→ID methods (MAE-CT, Layer Grafted Pre-training) in a critical way: those attach a single ID head to the last encoder block, whereas MIM-Refiner attaches an ensemble of ID heads to multiple intermediate blocks where representation quality peaks. The Nearest Neighbor Alignment (NNA) loss is a targeted modification of NNCLR that omits the NN-swap for negative samples, improving training stability at scale. The paper's empirical analysis of distinct block types (reconstruction-oriented vs. abstraction-oriented) provides the mechanistic justification for targeting intermediate layers rather than the final one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIM-Refiner offers a remarkably simple and compute-efficient recipe (20–30 epochs of refinement on existing MIM checkpoints) that converts subpar MIM features into state-of-the-art off-the-shelf representations, enabling ID-style scaling to 2B-parameter models without the compute of training from scratch. The block-type analysis provides a reusable diagnostic for understanding where MIM encoders allocate capacity. Key limitations include the dependence on batch normalization in ID heads (hindering distributed scaling) and evaluation restricted to ImageNet-1K due to compute constraints; whether the approach transfers to web-scale pre-training (ImageNet-21K, LVD-142M) remains an open question that the authors flag as future work.
