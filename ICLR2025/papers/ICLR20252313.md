## Problem

Language models have shown strong generative capabilities in NLP but remain largely unexplored for structure-based drug design (SBDD), where the goal is to generate 3D ligands that bind to specific protein pockets. Existing SBDD methods rely on atom-wise autoregressive or diffusion-based generation (requiring thousands of steps) or complex multi-network fragment pipelines, and no prior work uses LMs to directly generate 3D ligands while preserving geometric structure and incorporating protein context.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D protein pocket (backbone atom coordinates and types) as conditioning input, processed by the pre-trained ESM-IF1 inverse folding model into 512-dim node embeddings (projected to 768-dim via MLP). The output is a 3D ligand molecule, generated autoregressively as a sequence of fragment tokens where each fragment is represented by its canonical SMILES string, SE(3)-invariant spherical coordinates (d, θ, φ) of its center under the molecule frame, and a 3D rotation vector (m_x, m_y, m_z) encoding its orientation relative to the molecule frame. The sequence is decoded back to full 3D atom coordinates via inverse coordinate transformations and bond inference via OpenBabel.

## Method

Frag2Seq establishes a bijective mapping (Theorem 3.4) between 3D molecules and 1D sequences by: (1) fragmenting molecules along rotatable non-ring single bonds with degree > 1, (2) constructing SE(3)-equivariant local coordinate frames for both the whole molecule (from first three non-collinear fragment centers) and each fragment (from first three non-collinear atoms), and (3) extracting SE(3)-invariant spherical coordinates and rotation vectors that are provably invariant under any rigid transformation (Lemma 3.3). The sequence is ordered by canonical SMILES to ensure uniqueness. A GPT-1 architecture (12 layers, 12 heads, 768 hidden dim) is trained with next-token prediction cross-entropy loss, with cross-attention layers inserted after self-attention in each block to condition on ESM-IF1 protein pocket embeddings (queries from ligand tokens, keys/values from protein nodes). This fragment-based autoregressive generation achieves ~300× speedup over atom-based baselines while producing molecules with superior binding affinity (Vina score) and drug-likeness (QED, Lipinski) compared to diffusion and autoregressive SBDD methods.

## Results

On the CrossDocked2020 test set (100 proteins), Frag2Seq achieves the best Vina score (-7.366 ± 1.96), highest high-affinity rate (65.3%), best QED (0.645 ± 0.15), and best Lipinski score (4.989 ± 0.11) among all 10 baselines. It also demonstrates a dramatic efficiency advantage, generating 100 molecules per pocket in 48.8 s on average—roughly 13× faster than the next-fastest method (DiffSBDD at 630 s) and up to ~300× faster than autoregressive baselines like 3D-SBDD (15,986 s). Frag2Seq trails only on SA (0.642 vs. Pocket2Mol's 0.752) and Diversity (0.711 vs. GraphBP's 0.844).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Frag2Seq outperforms all autoregressive baselines (3D-SBDD, Pocket2Mol, GraphBP) on Vina score, QED, and Lipinski, with the largest gap over GraphBP (Vina -7.366 vs. -4.719). Against diffusion-based methods, Frag2Seq beats TargetDiff (-7.366 vs. -7.318), DecompDiff (-7.366 vs. -6.607), and DiffSBDD (-7.366 vs. -7.177) on Vina score while also improving QED by 0.09–0.16 points. Among fragment-based baselines, Frag2Seq surpasses FLAG (-7.366 vs. -6.389) and DrugGPS (-7.366 vs. -6.608) on binding affinity and drug-likeness. The closest LM-based competitor, Lingo3DMol, achieves a competitive Vina score (-7.257) but falls far behind on QED (0.269 vs. 0.645) and Lipinski (3.121 vs. 4.989), indicating Frag2Seq's fragment-and-geometry-aware tokenization better preserves drug-like properties. Frag2Seq's only losses are on SA (0.642 vs. Pocket2Mol's 0.752) and Diversity (0.711 vs. GraphBP's 0.844), suggesting its generated molecules are slightly less synthetically accessible and less diverse than those two baselines. In terms of speed, Frag2Seq (48.8 s) is ~13× faster than DiffSBDD (630 s), ~21× faster than DrugGPS (1008 s), and ~328× faster than 3D-SBDD (15,986 s) for generating 100 molecules per pocket.

## Relation to Prior Work

Frag2Seq carves new ground at the intersection of structure-based drug design (SBDD) and language models. Prior SBDD work falls into three families—autoregressive atom-by-atom generation (3D-SBDD, Pocket2Mol, GraphBP), one-shot diffusion (DiffSBDD, TargetDiff, DecompDiff), and fragment/motif-based pipelines (FLAG, DrugGPS)—while prior chemical language models (MolGPT, MolT5, Lingo3DMol) operate exclusively on 2D SMILES or require a separate coordinate-prediction step. This paper is, to the authors' knowledge, the first to use an LM to directly generate 3D ligands for SBDD, combining SE(3)-equivariant local-frame construction with canonical-SMILES ordering to produce a provably bijective, geometry-preserving tokenization. Protein context is injected via cross-attention with ESM-IF1 inverse-folding embeddings rather than the more common GNN-based pocket encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~300× sampling speedup over atom-based autoregressive and diffusion baselines, combined with the best Vina score and QED/Lipinski among compared methods, makes LM-based SBDD a practically viable alternative for high-throughput ligand generation. The formal bijective-mapping theorem and SE(3)-invariance proofs provide a clean theoretical foundation that could transfer to other 3D molecular or materials generation tasks. Key limitations remain: chemical bonds are inferred post-hoc by OpenBabel rather than generated directly, real-valued coordinates suffer quantization loss, and the model has not been scaled beyond 134M parameters. Open questions include whether incorporating binding-affinity priors (as IPDiff does) into the LM framework would close the remaining gap to the strongest diffusion baselines, and whether the fragment-based tokenization generalizes to larger, more diverse chemical spaces.
