## Problem

Controllable generation via guidance (classifier-based or classifier-free) is a cornerstone of continuous diffusion models but does not directly transfer to discrete data, where gradients with respect to tokens are undefined. Existing discrete diffusion extensions such as absorbing-state (masked) models cannot re-edit already-decoded tokens, limiting their steerability, while uniform noise diffusion models that do allow repeated editing have historically underperformed autoregressive baselines in perplexity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of one-hot discrete tokens (noisy latent z_t over a vocabulary of size N), a scalar noise level t ∈ [0,1], and optionally a class label y for conditional generation. At each denoising step it outputs a factorized categorical distribution over each token position, which is tempered by the guidance strength γ before sampling. For sequences of length L, the per-token distributions are computed independently given the full noisy sequence, enabling parallel decoding of all L positions at each of T steps (e.g., T=512 for 32,768-nucleotide genomes or T=1000 for 32×32 CIFAR-10 images).

## Method

The paper introduces two discrete guidance mechanisms: D-CFG, which combines a conditional and an unconditional denoising network's per-token probabilities raised to powers γ and (1−γ) respectively (a direct discrete analog of classifier-free guidance), and D-CBG, which multiplies a separately trained classifier's probability by the denoising distribution per token with a first-order Taylor approximation to avoid O(L·N) classifier evaluations. To make uniform noise diffusion competitive, the authors derive a continuous-time (T→∞) evidence lower bound for the interpolating discrete diffusion framework with π = 1/N, yielding a closed-form integral objective (Eq. 18) that eliminates the reconstruction and prior terms and tightens the diffusion KL term. Models trained with this bound are called UDLM and achieve state-of-the-art perplexity among uniform noise diffusion models. The key practical advantage of UDLM over absorbing-state (MDLM) is that all tokens remain editable throughout decoding, making the model more amenable to iterative guidance corrections and more robust to fast (small-T) sampling.

## Results

On language modeling, UDLM achieves PPL of 2.02 on QM9 (vocab 40), outperforming MDLM (2.12) and AR (2.19), and 3.15 on Species10 (vocab 12), matching MDLM (3.17). On text8 (BPC), UDLM achieves 1.44, surpassing SEDD Uniform (1.47) and D3PM Uniform (1.61) but trailing absorbing-state methods like GenMD4 (1.34). For guided generation, UDLM with D-CFG at γ=3 achieves F1 of 0.94 on species-specific genome generation, outperforming AR (0.74) and MDLM (0.78), and is the only model where increasing γ improves both F1 and Disc. AUROC simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDLM outperforms all prior uniform-noise diffusion methods: on text8 it achieves 1.44 BPC vs. SEDD Uniform (1.47) and D3PM Uniform (1.61), and on LM1B it reaches 31.28 PPL vs. SEDD Uniform (40.25) and D3PM Uniform (137.9). Against absorbing-state MDLM, UDLM wins on small-vocabulary datasets (QM9: 2.02 vs. 2.12 PPL; Species10: 3.15 vs. 3.17) but trails on larger vocabularies (text8: 2.71 vs. 2.62; LM1B: 31.28 vs. 27.04). For classifier-free guidance on genomic sequences, UDLM with D-CFG at γ=3 achieves F1 0.94 and Disc. AUROC 0.87, while AR degrades to F1 0.74 and MDLM to F1 0.78 at the same γ. In molecule design, UDLM with D-CBG achieves mean QED 0.61 with 994.8 valid sequences and mean ring count 4.84 with 897.2 valid sequences, outperforming AR+FUDGE (QED 0.61 but only 924.4 valid; ring count 4.89 with 281.2 valid), AR+PPLM (QED 0.45; ring count 1.92), and UDLM+NOS (QED 0.47; ring count 3.96). On CIFAR-10, UDLM with D-CFG achieves FID 23.21 and IS 8.66, improving over unguided UDLM (FID 33.65) but trailing MDLM with D-CFG (FID 15.56, IS 9.02). UDLM is notably more robust to fast sampling (smaller T): at T=128 on CIFAR-10, UDLM achieves FID 30.48 while MDLM degrades to FID 64.09.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) discrete diffusion models (D3PM, MDLM, SEDD) and (2) guidance mechanisms for controllable generation (classifier-free guidance, FUDGE, PPLM, NOS). It adapts the continuous-time ELBO tightening from MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024a) and Kingma et al. (2021) to the previously underperforming uniform noise setting, and derives discrete analogs of classifier-free and classifier-based guidance that avoid the non-differentiability problem. The paper also establishes equivalence with the concurrent CTMC-based guidance of Nisonoff et al. (2024), positioning its simpler discrete-time formulation as an accessible alternative to rate-matrix manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that uniform noise diffusion can match or beat absorbing-state diffusion on small-vocabulary domains (genomics, molecules) challenges a widely-held assumption and opens the door to more guidable discrete diffusion models that can iteratively re-edit tokens. The D-CFG and D-CBG mechanisms provide practical, easy-to-implement guidance tools that outperform both AR baselines (FUDGE, PPLM) and prior diffusion guidance (NOS) across three domains. Open questions include whether the uniform-noise advantage persists at larger vocabularies, how these methods scale to very long sequences, and whether the first-order Taylor approximation in D-CBG degrades with increasing sequence length or vocabulary size.
