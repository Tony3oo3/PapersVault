## Problem

Existing multimodal VAEs (MVAEs) using mean-based fusion (PoE, MoE) degrade significantly when views are irregularly missing, producing representations that lack both sufficiency and inter-view consistency. The core gap is that these methods do not explicitly model invariant relationships between views, so they cannot effectively infer missing views or maintain semantic coherence as missing rates increase.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253584/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-view data where each sample retains a random subset of L views (missing rates η ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7}), with views ranging from 28×28 or 64×64 RGB images, text feature vectors (10–1024 dim), to acoustic/seismic signals (50 dim). It produces a k-dimensional consensus variable ω (geometric mean of fused marginal Gaussians) used directly for K-means clustering, plus per-view latent variables z_v^(l) organized in an L×L matrix that drive decoders to reconstruct or generate all L views from the incomplete subset.

## Method

MVP extends MVAEs by introducing inter-view correspondences: nonlinear mappings f_lv transform each view's latent encoding into every other view's representation, populating an L×L matrix Z₀ (diagonal = self-encoding, off-diagonal = cross-view transformation). Two set partitions are defined over the latent variables — a single-view partition (columns, grouping variables by target view) and a complete-view partition (rows, grouping variables that jointly cover all L views) — which enable a valid ELBO factorization. Cyclic permutations (generated via Sattolo's algorithm) reorder columns to produce new complete-view partitions, so the ELBO's reconstruction term alternates between self-view and cross-view generation. The key regularization insight is setting permuted posteriors as informational priors, converting the KL term into a Permutation Divergence — a proven dissimilarity coefficient that enforces homogeneity within each single-view cell (Inter-View Translatability) — while a second term aligns consensus variables across different complete-view combinations (Consensus Concentration). Training uses a warm-up phase with the basic ELBO followed by a convex combination of the basic and permuted ELBOs, with a fresh random cyclic permutation sampled each iteration.

## Results

On five incomplete multi-view clustering benchmarks (Handwritten, CUB, Scene15, Reuters, SensIT Vehicle), MVP achieves the best ACC across all datasets at every missing rate (η = 0.1–0.7). At the mid-range missing rate η = 0.5, MVP reaches 90.76% ACC on Handwritten, 74.20% on CUB, 45.28% on Scene15, 53.67% on Reuters, and 70.21% on SensIT Vehicle. On the PolyMNIST generation task (η = 0.5), MVP attains 83.56% semantic coherence accuracy when generating all five views from a single input view, substantially outperforming the next-best MVAE. On MVShapeNet, MVP maintains the highest average SSIM and category/angle classification accuracy across all incomplete view combinations at both η = 0.1 and η = 0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVP outperforms all eight IMVRL baselines (DCCA, DCCAE, DIMVC, DSIMVC, Completer, CPSPAN, ICMVC, DVIMC) on every dataset at every missing rate. The closest competitor is DVIMC, which MVP surpasses by 5.75% ACC on Handwritten (90.76 vs 85.01) and 18.30% on Reuters (53.67 vs 35.37) at η = 0.5; the gap widens at η = 0.7 where DVIMC drops to 3.15% ARI on Reuters while MVP holds 30.66%. CPSPAN is the second-strongest baseline, trailing MVP by 6.12% on Handwritten and 14.13% on CUB at η = 0.5. ICMVC and Completer degrade severely on CUB (22.90% and 51.90% ACC respectively at η = 0.5), and Completer collapses to 19.43% ACC at η = 0.7. Against six MVAE baselines (mVAE, mmVAE, MoPoE, mmJSD, MVTCAE, MMVAE+) on PolyMNIST, MVP leads in clustering NMI, semantic coherence accuracy (83.56% vs ≤70% for others), and SSIM, with MVTCAE being the only method that maintains competitive performance as missing views increase. On MVShapeNet, MVP consistently achieves the highest SSIM and angle/category classification accuracy, while other MVAEs produce blurry reconstructions or category confusion, particularly MMVAE+ which suffers severe category confusion when sampling missing-view information from auxiliary priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MVAE framework (Wu & Goodman, 2018) by introducing explicit inter-view correspondences as nonlinear mapping channels in the latent space, building on the correspondence concept from Huang et al. (2020) but repurposing it for cross-view generation rather than clustering alignment. It departs from prior MVAE variants (mmVAE, mmJSD, MoPoE, MVTCAE, MMVAE+) that fuse unimodal posteriors into a joint posterior via PoE or MoE; instead, MVP maintains separate unimodal posteriors and uses cyclic permutations to create cross-view reconstruction objectives. The informational prior via cyclic permutations of posteriors (Permutation Divergence) extends the data-dependent prior line (Sutter et al. 2020, 2024; Hwang et al. 2021) by replacing strict alignment with a soft consistency measure that has a formal dissimilarity-coefficient guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The permutation-and-partition framework is not tied to the VAE formulation and could be adapted to non-generative multi-view models, broadening its applicability. The Permutation Divergence provides a theoretically grounded, symmetric regularization that avoids the forward/reverse KL asymmetry issues flagged by Hwang et al. (2021). Open questions include scalability to very large numbers of views (the L×L correspondence matrix grows quadratically), whether the approach benefits from learned rather than random permutations, and how it interacts with more recent generative paradigms like diffusion-based MVAEs. The method's reliance on pre-computed Sattolo permutations also limits its adaptability to dynamically changing missing patterns during inference.
