## Problem

While bilevel optimization algorithms have achieved provable convergence to stationary points or local minima, global optimality remains an open problem because the nested bilevel objective F(u) is distorted by the lower-level solution mapping S(u), destroying benign landscape properties (e.g., PL or convexity) that hold at each level individually. This paper asks: under what conditions can first-order bilevel algorithms be guaranteed to reach the global optimum, and for which practical learning problems do those conditions hold?

## Input / Output

The method consumes two continuously differentiable objectives f(u,v) and g(u,v) (instantiated as least-squares losses on linear or bilinear models), a penalty parameter γ = O(ε^{-0.5}), and structured matrix data (training and validation feature/label matrices X_trn, Y_trn, X_val, Y_val). It outputs a pair (u*, v*) that is an (ε, ε) global solution to the bilevel problem, with an iteration complexity of O(log²(ε⁻¹)) inner-loop steps. The two validated applications use overparameterized linear networks (m ≥ max{N, N'}), so the lower-level problem admits multiple solutions, making the bilevel coupling non-trivial.

## Method

The paper reformulates the bilevel problem as a penalized single-level objective L_γ(u,v) = f(u,v) + γ(g(u,v) − g*(u)), which avoids the landscape distortion caused by the solution mapping S(u). It then defines two sufficient 'benign landscape' conditions on L_γ: a joint PL condition (treating (u,v) as one variable, suited to isomorphic upper/lower levels) and a blockwise PL condition (treating u and v as separate blocks, suited to heterogeneous levels). Under joint PL, the penalty-based bilevel gradient descent (PBGD) with a Jacobi update converges globally at an almost linear rate; under blockwise PL, a Gauss-Seidel variant of PBGD achieves the same guarantee. The key technical insight is that for linear models with least-squares loss, the penalized objective decomposes into strongly convex functions composed with linear mappings, and the PL condition is preserved under additivity in this special structure (Observation 2). The authors validate both conditions along the PBGD trajectory for representation learning (two-layer linear network, joint PL) and data hyper-cleaning (one-layer network with sigmoid reweighting, blockwise PL), using induction and acute matrix perturbation theory to bound the local PL constants uniformly over iterations.

## Results

The paper establishes that PBGD achieves an almost linear convergence rate of O(log²(ε⁻¹)) iterations to reach an (ε, ε) global optimal solution for both representation learning (Theorem 2) and data hyper-cleaning (Theorem 3), under joint PL and blockwise PL conditions respectively. Numerically, PBGD with proper stepsizes converges almost linearly in both tasks, and enlarging the penalty constant γ reduces the target optimality gap ε consistently with theory. In the representation learning experiment (Figure 4), all three tested first-order bilevel methods—PBGD, F²SA, and BOME—converge to the global optimum at an almost linear rate, confirming the generality of the PL-based analysis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PBGD against two state-of-the-art fully first-order bilevel methods: F²SA (Kwon et al., 2023) and BOME (Liu et al., 2022). In the representation learning task (Figure 4c–d), all three methods—PBGD, F²SA, and BOME—successfully converge to the global optimum at an almost linear rate, so no method clearly outperforms the others on this metric. The paper's contribution is primarily theoretical: it is the first to prove global convergence guarantees for these penalty-based first-order methods under PL conditions, whereas prior work only established stationary-point or local-minimum convergence. The ablation study (Figure 4a–b) shows that PBGD is sensitive to stepsize choice: excessively large α or β causes divergence, while small values lead to slow convergence, with the theoretical upper bounds from Theorems 1–3 providing safe operating ranges. The authors note that the empirical equivalence of all three methods suggests their local PL-based analysis framework can be extended to other penalty reformulation-based algorithms beyond PBGD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the penalty-based bilevel gradient descent (PBGD) framework of Shen et al. (2023) and Kwon et al. (2024), which previously provided only stationary-point convergence guarantees, by establishing global convergence under newly defined joint and blockwise Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) conditions. The key conceptual departure is showing that the nested bilevel objective F(u) can have a severely distorted landscape (saddle points, non-PL) even when both levels are individually PL, while the penalty reformulation L_γ(u,v) recovers a benign landscape — a structural insight not present in prior bilevel convergence analyses. The PL condition itself is generalized from the single-variable setting of Karimi et al. (2016) to a two-variable bilevel context, with the blockwise variant reflecting the hierarchical structure of upper/lower-level variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Global optimality is essential in high-stakes bilevel applications (healthcare, robotics, policy-making) where local solutions may be unsafe or suboptimal, yet prior work has only guaranteed convergence to stationary points or local minima. The paper's framework — verifying local, trajectory-dependent PL constants via induction and acute matrix perturbation theory — provides a template for proving global convergence in other bilevel learning problems, though it is currently validated only for linear models with least-squares loss. Key open questions include whether the joint/blockwise PL conditions hold for non-linear architectures, more complex losses (e.g., cross-entropy beyond bounded regions), and whether the results extend to stochastic or distributed bilevel settings. The restriction to overparameterized linear networks limits immediate practical impact but establishes a rigorous foundation for future extensions.
