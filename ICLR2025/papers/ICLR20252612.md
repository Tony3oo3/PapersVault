## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for the inexact stochastic proximal point algorithm (isPPA) require restrictive assumptions such as global Lipschitz smoothness of component functions, strong convexity, or Lipschitz continuity of the regularizer over its entire domain—conditions violated by common regularizers like the elastic net. This paper establishes stability, almost sure convergence, and nonasymptotic last-iterate convergence rates for isPPA under only local Lipschitz continuity of component functions and a quadratic growth condition on the objective, and proves these rates are tight up to constant factors.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stochastic composite optimization problem defined by component functions f(x;s) sampled from a distribution P, a proper closed regularizer r(x), an initial point x₁, a stepsize schedule {α_k}, an accuracy parameter γ, and a minibatch size m. At each iteration it draws a minibatch S_k^{1:m} and approximately solves a proximal subproblem until one of three equivalent stopping criteria (distance-to-prox, function-gap, or subgradient-norm) is met. The output is the last iterate x_K, with convergence measured by the expected squared distance to the optimal solution set and the expected KKT residual ‖x_k − prox_φ(x_k)‖₂.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a convergence analysis that avoids smoothness and strong convexity assumptions by combining a local Lipschitz condition on component functions (Assumption 3) with a quadratic growth condition on the objective (Assumption 4). The key recursive inequality (Lemma B.3) bounds the expected squared distance via a variance term ρ_{f,m,U}·L_F(U)²·α_k² that captures minibatch noise, enabling linear convergence to an O(α₀) neighborhood with constant stepsizes and O(k^{−β}) asymptotic rates with diminishing stepsizes α_k = α₀k^{−β}. The KKT residual rate follows from a geometric lemma showing ‖x − prox_φ(x)‖₂ ≤ 2·dist(x, X*). Tightness is established by explicitly solving the regularized Fréchet mean problem, where the lower bound matches the upper bound up to constants, confirming that the stochastic noise term is irreducible and prevents exact convergence with bounded stepsizes.

## Results

The paper establishes that isPPA with constant stepsizes α₀ converges linearly to an O(α₀) neighborhood of the optimal solution set, while with diminishing stepsizes α_k = α₀k^{-β} it achieves an O(k^{-β}) rate in expected squared distance to the solution set and O(k^{-β/2}) in expected KKT residual. These rates are proven tight up to constant factors via an explicit lower bound on the regularized Fréchet mean problem. Numerical experiments on Lasso and elastic net linear regression (n=10000, d=1000, m=32) confirm the predicted asymptotic rates for β ∈ {0.55, 0.75, 0.9, 1}, with β=1 yielding the best performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical baseline is the deterministic proximal point algorithm (PPA, Rockafellar 1976). On the Fréchet mean problem, deterministic PPA with constant stepsizes converges exactly to the optimal solution, whereas isPPA with the same constant stepsizes converges only to an O(α₀) neighborhood due to stochastic noise from minibatch sampling (Figure 1a vs. 1b). This gap is explained theoretically: the noise term ρ_{f,m,U}L_F(U)² in the convergence bound does not vanish with constant stepsizes. Against prior sPPA analyses, isPPA requires strictly weaker assumptions: local Lipschitz continuity of component functions (vs. global Lipschitz or Lipschitz smoothness), no strong convexity (vs. restricted strong convexity in Ryu & Boyd 2014), and no Lipschitz continuity of the regularizer over the entire domain (a condition violated by the elastic net, which blocks the inexact analysis of Yuan & Li 2023). The lower bound on the Fréchet mean problem (Proposition 3.1) confirms that the O(k^{-β}) rate is tight up to constants, meaning no algorithm in this class can improve the asymptotic rate without additional problem structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic proximal point algorithm (sPPA) lineage from Bertsekas (2011) and Ryu & Boyd (2014) to the inexact setting, building on the stability framework of Asi & Duchi (2019) and the only prior inexact sPPA analysis by Yuan & Li (2023). The key departure is replacing restrictive assumptions—global Lipschitz smoothness of component functions, strong convexity of the objective, and Lipschitz continuity of the regularizer over its entire domain—with local Lipschitz continuity and a quadratic growth condition, while also providing last-iterate (rather than ergodic) convergence rates. The proof techniques draw on Davis & Drusvyatskiy (2019) for the local Lipschitz handling and Bertsekas (2011) for the almost sure convergence argument, with a novel tightness analysis via the Fréchet mean problem that has no direct precedent in the sPPA literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in validating inexact sPPA for the most common regularized regression models (Lasso, elastic net) under assumptions that are actually satisfied in practice, with KKT-residual rates that can serve as computable stopping criteria. The tightness result via the Fréchet mean problem is important: it shows that the derived O(k^{-β}) rates cannot be improved without additional problem structure, setting a clear ceiling for future work. Open questions include whether acceleration or variance reduction techniques can break this barrier, and whether the analysis extends to nonconvex settings where last-iterate rates are more naturally applicable. The contrast with deterministic PPA—where isPPA with constant stepsizes only reaches a neighborhood rather than converging exactly—highlights a fundamental limitation of stochastic proximal methods that practitioners should be aware of.
