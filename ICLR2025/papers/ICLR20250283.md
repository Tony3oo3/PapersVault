## Problem

While the loss landscape of unregularized neural networks has been extensively characterized (e.g., absence of spurious local minima), regularization fundamentally changes the qualitative behavior of the global optimum and its connectivity structure. This paper asks: what is the precise geometric structure of the optimal solution set for regularized ReLU networks, how does its topology change as network width varies, and under what conditions are minimum-norm interpolators unique?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250283/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}, label vector y ∈ R^n, network width m, and regularization strength β > 0 as input. The key outputs are: (1) the optimal solution polytope P* ⊂ R^{2dP} (where P is the number of hyperplane arrangement patterns from X), (2) two critical widths m* and M* that govern phase transitions in the connectivity of the optimal set, and (3) the unique optimal model fit y* ∈ R^n. The polytope is parameterized by nonnegative scalars c_i, d_i ≥ 0 along fixed directions determined by the dual optimum, subject to a single linear equality constraint summing to y*.

## Method

The core technique casts the nonconvex regularized two-layer ReLU network training problem into an equivalent convex cone-constrained group LASSO (valid when m ≥ m*), then leverages the dual problem to characterize the solution set. The dual optimum ν* uniquely determines the direction of each optimal parameter vector (via Proposition C.2), reducing the solution set to a polytope of nonnegative conic combinations along those fixed directions. The 'staircase of connectivity' arises from imposing a cardinality constraint (at most m nonzero components) on this polytope: at m = m* the set is finite (vertices only), at m ≥ m*+1 connected components emerge via a splitting operation, at m = M* an isolated point persists, and at m ≥ min{m*+M*, n+1} the entire set becomes connected. This framework extends to vector-valued networks (via copositive norms) and parallel three-layer networks (via finite first-layer direction sets), and is used to construct explicit counterexamples showing non-uniqueness of minimum-norm interpolators when free skip connections, bias regularization, or unidimensionality are absent.

## Results

The paper establishes that for two-layer ReLU networks with weight decay, the optimal solution set forms a polytope whose vertex directions are uniquely determined by the dual optimum (Theorem 1). The key quantitative finding is the 'staircase of connectivity' (Theorem 2): as network width m increases past critical thresholds m* and M*, the optimal set transitions from a finite set of isolated points (m = m*), to having connected components (m ≥ m*+1), to having isolated points at m = M*, to full connectivity at m ≥ min{m*+M*, n+1}. In the toy example (n=2 data points, β=0.1), m=1 yields a single isolated optimum, m=2 produces a continuum of connected solutions, and m=3 = min{m*+M*, n+1} yields a fully connected optimal set. The paper also constructs explicit datasets (e.g., n=5, d=1) where the minimum-norm interpolator has infinitely many solutions, with optimal cost 60 and a one-parameter family of distinct interpolators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or comparison tables. The results extend and refine prior theoretical characterizations: the optimal polytope characterization builds on Mishkin & Pilanci (2023) by explicitly connecting the polytope vertices to the dual optimum; the staircase of connectivity generalizes the overparametrization-connectivity link from Nguyen (2021) and Simsek et al. (2021) to regularized networks with precise critical widths; and the non-uniqueness results for minimum-norm interpolators contrast with Boursier & Flammarion (2023), who proved uniqueness for unidimensional data with free skip connections and regularized bias — this paper shows that removing the skip connection (NSB) or the bias (SNB) breaks uniqueness, and that uniqueness fails entirely for d > 1. The path-with-non-increasing-loss result (Corollary 2) combines Haeffele & Vidal (2017) and Vidal et al. (2022) with the new connectivity theorem to extend Nguyen's (2019) sublevel-set connectivity to the regularized setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convex reformulation framework of Pilanci & Ergen (2020) and the optimal-set characterization of Mishkin & Pilanci (2023) by explicitly connecting the dual optimum to the polytope structure of the solution set, and by deriving a complete phase-transition ('staircase of connectivity') picture as network width varies. It builds on Ergen & Pilanci (2023) for stationary-point characterization via subsampled convex programs, and on Haeffele & Vidal (2017) / Vidal et al. (2022) for landscape connectivity at large width. A key departure from prior work is the challenge to Boursier & Flammarion (2023): the paper shows that free skip connections are not merely cosmetic but are necessary for uniqueness of minimum-norm interpolators, contradicting the empirical belief that they do not affect solution behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The staircase of connectivity provides a precise, computable phase-transition picture (via critical widths m* and M*) for when the optimal solution set transitions from discrete to connected, which is useful for understanding overparametrization effects in regularized training. The non-uniqueness results for minimum-norm interpolators open the question of what architectural choices (beyond free skip connections) guarantee uniqueness in higher dimensions, and the conjecture that non-unique datasets have measure zero suggests a broader 'generic uniqueness' principle. Limitations include the restriction to ReLU activations, the need for m ≥ m* for the convex reformulation to apply, and the fact that the connectivity results are for the optimal set in parameter space (modulo permutations) rather than for the full loss landscape topology.
