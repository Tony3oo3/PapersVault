## Problem

Existing Role-Playing Agents (RPAs) are confined to the textual modality, unable to simulate humans' multimodal perceptual capabilities where visual and textual information are integrated for richer interaction. No prior framework exists for developing or evaluating agents that role-play characters while grounding dialogues in images, leaving a gap in both data resources and stable evaluation methodology for this multimodal setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252385/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The MRPA receives an RGB image, a structured character profile (five parts: brief introduction, personality, life story, main interpersonal relationships, and catchphrases), the dialogue context D, and optionally a second character's profile for inter-role scenarios. It produces a single in-character utterance (m_n) that responds to or initiates dialogue centered on the image. The model's maximum context length is set to 3072 tokens to accommodate detailed profiles and multi-turn history, and outputs must contain only the character's words without AI-assistant prefixes or explanatory text.

## Method

The framework has three components. First, MMRole-Data is constructed by generating character profiles via GPT-4 (summarized from Wikipedia/Baidu Baike for known characters, two-stage generated for hypothetical real-life characters), collecting 11K images (generic MS-COCO images plus manually annotated character-related images), and generating 14K dialogues across three scenarios (single-turn commentary, multi-turn human-role, and multi-turn inter-role) with rigorous manual filtering. Second, MMRole-Eval defines eight metrics across three dimensions (conversational skills, multimodal understanding, role-playing qualities) and trains a specialized reward model (fine-tuned QWen-VL-Chat, 10 epochs, max length 4096) on 23,520 GPT-4 evaluation trajectories to score MRPAs relative to constructed ground-truth data, yielding a ratio-based score per metric. Third, MMRole-Agent is developed by supervised fine-tuning QWen-VL-Chat (9B) on the full 85K-sample training set using 8×A100 GPUs at learning rate 1e-5 over 3 epochs, jointly training across 72 diverse characters to acquire generalizable multimodal role-playing capabilities.

## Results

On the MMRole-Data test set (294 samples), MMRole-Agent (9B, fine-tuned from QWen-VL-Chat) achieves an overall score of 0.994, a 0.151 improvement over its base model QWen-VL-Chat (0.843). Among >100B-parameter models, Claude 3 Opus leads with 1.157 overall, while among open-source models, LLaVA-NeXT-34B achieves the highest score at 1.027. MMRole-Agent generalizes well, scoring 0.999 on the in-distribution test set and 0.981 on the out-of-distribution test set (only a 0.018 drop), and outperforms all evaluated open-source LMMs except LLaVA-NeXT-34B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMRole-Agent (0.994) substantially outperforms its base model QWen-VL-Chat (0.843) by 0.151 overall, and also surpasses LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B (0.944), Yi-VL-6B (0.829), and InternVL-Chat-V1.5 (0.980) in the smaller-parameter group. However, it falls short of LLaVA-NeXT-34B (1.027) by 0.033, which is the strongest open-source model evaluated. Among the >100B closed-source models, Claude 3 Opus (1.157) leads, followed by GPT-4 Turbo (1.099), QWen-VL-Max (1.028), and Gemini Pro Vision (1.021); MMRole-Agent trails all of these but narrows the gap considerably given its 9B parameter size. Notably, LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B and MMRole-Agent both surpass the much larger Yi-VL-34B (0.884), suggesting that training methodology and data quality matter more than raw model scale. On the out-of-distribution test set, MMRole-Agent retains 0.981 overall (vs. 0.999 in-distribution), demonstrating strong generalization to unseen characters and images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-only Role-Playing Agent (RPA) literature (CharacterGLM, Character-LLM, ChatHaruhi) by introducing the multimodal dimension: dialogues centered around images, requiring the agent to integrate visual and textual understanding while maintaining character consistency. On the model side, it repurposes existing Large Multimodal Models (QWen-VL-Chat, LLaVA, InternVL, Yi-VL) for role-playing for the first time, and adapts the reward-model evaluation paradigm from Vicuna/LLaVA by introducing ground-truth comparison to stabilize scoring. The dataset construction pipeline (GPT-4-generated profiles and dialogues, MS-COCO generic images, manually annotated character-related images) is a novel combination but relies heavily on GPT-4 as the data synthesizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive framework (dataset + evaluation + specialized agent) for multimodal role-playing, providing a reusable benchmark and a baseline that the community can build upon. The finding that personality consistency and tone consistency are the most discriminative and challenging metrics—while fluency is nearly universal across models—points to where future MRPAs should focus. A key limitation is that all training data is GPT-4-synthesized, capping MMRole-Agent below GPT-4 itself; the authors acknowledge this and propose multi-model synthesis as a path forward. Open questions include whether MRPAs can generalize to video/audio modalities, whether the evaluation framework transfers to non-English or culturally diverse character sets, and whether the 85-character scale is sufficient to capture the full diversity of human social interaction.
