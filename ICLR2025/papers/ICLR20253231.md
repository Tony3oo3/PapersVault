## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) built on foundation models provide interpretable predictions but are not inherently more robust to distribution shifts at test time than non-interpretable classifiers. When deployed in the wild, CBMs suffer severe accuracy drops under both low-level shifts (noise, blur) and concept-level shifts (background changes), and no prior method existed for test-time adaptation of concept bottlenecks with a foundation model backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253231/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen foundation model (e.g., CLIP:ViT-L/14, BioMedCLIP), a pre-constructed concept bottleneck (m concept vectors and a linear predictor trained on the source domain), and a stream of unlabeled 224×224×3 test images from the target domain. It produces class logits, per-concept scores, and interpretable concept-to-class weight mappings. Pseudo-labels are generated on-the-fly via an ensemble of the foundation model's zero-shot and linear-probing predictors, and adaptation proceeds online in fixed-size batches without access to the source dataset or any ground-truth labels.

## Method

CONDA is a three-step test-time adaptation framework, each step targeting a specific failure mode of CBMs under distribution shift. (1) Concept-Score Alignment (CSA) adapts the concept vectors C to align class-conditional concept score distributions with the source domain using a Mahalanobis-based intra/inter-class distance loss (inspired by CAFA) plus Frobenius-norm regularization. (2) Linear Probing Adaptation (LPA) updates the label predictor (W, b) via cross-entropy on pseudo-labels with an elastic-net sparsity penalty to preserve interpretability. (3) Residual Concept Bottleneck (RCB) introduces r new concept vectors and a linear predictor as a residual branch, jointly optimized with cross-entropy, a cosine-similarity penalty to avoid redundancy with existing concepts, and a coherency regularization for interpretability. The key design insight is that low-level shifts are best addressed by CSA (aligning concept scores), while concept-level shifts require LPA and RCB (adjusting concept reliance and expanding the concept set), and the full pipeline combines all three to handle arbitrary shifts.

## Results

CONDA improves target-domain accuracy of concept bottleneck models by up to 28.7 percentage points (Waterbirds, Yuksekgonul CBM: AVG 32.03% → 60.69%), bringing interpretable CBM predictions to parity with or above non-interpretable zero-shot and linear-probing baselines. On Metashift, CONDA lifts the Oikarinen CBM from 83.72% to 93.90% AVG (matching zero-shot at 94.65%) and the Yuksekgonul CBM from 84.45% to 93.69%. On low-level shifts (CIFAR-10-C), CONDA + Yuksekgonul reaches 84.38% AVG, matching linear probing (84.11%), while on Camelyon17 (natural shift) CONDA + Oikarinen achieves 93.16% AVG, approaching LP (93.69%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-interpretable zero-shot (ZS) and linear-probing (LP) baselines, CONDA-adapted CBMs match or exceed feature-based predictions in most settings: on Waterbirds, all three CONDA variants surpass both ZS and LP in AVG (e.g., Oikarinen+CONDA 62.71% vs. ZS 61.06%, LP 54.10%), and on Metashift they close the gap to within ~1% of ZS (93.69–93.90% vs. 94.65%). Against the unadapted CBM baselines, CONDA yields the largest gains under concept-level shifts: the Yuksekgonul CBM on Waterbirds improves by +28.7 pp in AVG (32.03→60.69) and +15.2 pp in WG (27.80→43.01), while on Metashift the Oikarinen CBM gains +10.2 pp in AVG (83.72→93.90). Under low-level shifts (CIFAR-10-C), CONDA provides more modest but consistent improvements (e.g., +1.8 pp AVG for Yuksekgonul, +3.3 pp for Oikarinen), bringing CBM accuracy to parity with LP. On the medical Camelyon17 dataset (natural shift), CONDA + Oikarinen reaches 93.16% AVG, approaching LP (93.69%) and exceeding ZS (84.55%) by 8.6 pp. The Yeh et al. CBM, which already achieves strong source-domain accuracy, shows smaller relative gains from CONDA (sometimes slight decreases on CIFAR-100-C), suggesting its learned concepts are already partially robust to the tested shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) for interpretable prediction and Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) for handling distribution shifts at deployment. Prior CBM work (Koh et al. 2020; Yuksekgonul et al. 2023; Yeh et al. 2020; Oikarinen et al. 2023) constructs concept banks that remain static after deployment, while prior TTA methods (TENT, CAFA, Contrastive TTA) adapt non-interpretable deep classifiers. CONDA is positioned as the first framework to adapt a concept bottleneck (both the concept vectors and the label predictor) at test time on top of a frozen foundation model backbone, formalizing three distinct failure modes of CBMs under low-level and concept-level shifts and designing a dedicated adaptation step (CSA, LPA, RCB) for each.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a core theoretical premise of CBMs—that routing predictions through high-level concepts should inherently confer robustness to input perturbations—by showing empirically that unadapted CBMs can be *less* robust than direct feature-based predictions under both low-level and concept-level shifts. The residual concept bottleneck mechanism, which discovers new concepts (e.g., 'feathers,' 'wings,' 'beak' on Waterbirds) to compensate for an incomplete source-domain concept set, offers a practical path toward self-correcting interpretable pipelines. Key limitations include the significant gap to perfect pseudo-labeling (especially on CIFAR-100, where WG accuracy remains low), the restriction to linear CBM predictors, and dependence on the quality of the frozen foundation model. Open directions include more advanced pseudo-labeling that handles both shift types simultaneously, zero-shot robustification of the FM backbone, and extending the framework to non-linear or deeper concept bottlenecks.
