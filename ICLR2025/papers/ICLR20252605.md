## Problem

Scaling diffusion transformers (DiT) beyond ~8B parameters remains underexplored due to training and inference efficiency concerns. Existing sparse MoE approaches for DiT adopt token-choice routing inherited from language models, which assigns uniform computation to every image token, cannot exploit the global sequence context available in diffusion (unlike autoregressive decoding), and requires auxiliary load-balancing losses. The paper asks whether a routing strategy that leverages the full-image context and allocates heterogeneous computation adaptively can scale DiT more effectively.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt, encoded by a frozen 670M CLIP-based text encoder with a T5 tokenizer, and produces a 256×256 RGB image. Internally, the image is represented as a sequence of 128 latent-space patches (2×2 patch size with 0.5 masking ratio) processed by a DiT backbone, while the text is encoded to L tokens of dimension d_y and injected via cross-attention. The largest configuration (EC-DIT-M-64E) has 97.21B total parameters but only 8.27B activated per forward pass, maintaining competitive inference speed on 8×H100 GPUs.

## Method

EC-DiT replaces every even-numbered FFN layer in a modified DiT (with cross-attention modules for text conditioning) with a sparse MoE layer using expert-choice routing. The router input is the concatenation of self-attention output and cross-attention output, so it carries both image and text context; each expert then independently selects the top-C most relevant tokens from the entire sequence (C = S·f_c/E), rather than each token selecting its top-k experts. This yields inherently heterogeneous computation—detailed or textually significant regions are processed by multiple experts while plain background tokens may be skipped—and guarantees perfect load balance by construction, eliminating the need for auxiliary balancing losses. The model is trained end-to-end with a rectified-flow L2 objective using logit-normal timestep sampling, scaled across four backbone sizes (XL through M) with 8–64 experts, reaching 97B total parameters. The key insight is that diffusion's access to the full image sequence at every denoising step makes expert-choice routing a natural fit, enabling the router to learn adaptive, text-aware compute allocation through standard supervised training.

## Results

EC-DIT-M-64E (97.21B total params, 8.27B activated) achieves a state-of-the-art GenEval score of 71.68% at 256×256 resolution, surpassing SD3-Large (68.00%) and even SD3-Large with DPO fine-tuning (71.00%) at 512×512. The smaller EC-DIT-3XL-32E (32.15B total, 5.18B activated) reaches 70.91% GenEval with only 64% of SD3-Large's activation size and 33.8% of DENSE-M's inference time. Across all configurations, EC-DIT introduces less than 30% inference overhead while consistently improving text-image alignment, FID, and CLIP Score over both dense and token-choice MoE baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EC-DIT-M-64E (71.68% GenEval) outperforms SD3-Large (68.00%) by 3.68 points and SD3-Large with DPO fine-tuning (71.00%) by 0.68 points, despite operating at 256×256 resolution versus SD3's 512×512. Against the token-choice MoE baseline (GShard with top-2 routing), EC-DIT consistently wins on both GenEval and DSG across all model sizes; for example, EC-DIT-XXL-32E scores 70.86% GenEval versus GShard-XXL-32E's 69.96%, and EC-DIT-XL-8E (68.62%) rivals GShard-XL-16E (68.00%) with half the experts. Compared to dense models, EC-DIT-3XL-32E (70.91%) surpasses DENSE-3XL (68.92%) by 1.99 points while using only 5.18B activated parameters versus 4.50B total. On DSG scores, EC-DIT-M-64E achieves 76.40 overall, exceeding DENSE-3XL (75.03) and all GShard variants (73.47–75.40). In visual comparisons on PartiPrompts, EC-DIT-M-64E demonstrates superior text-image alignment and lower hallucination rates compared to both SD3-Large (8B activated) and FLUX.1[dev] (12B activated), correctly rendering complex compositional prompts where the baselines fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts expert-choice routing (Zhou et al., 2022), originally proposed for scaling language models, to diffusion transformers for text-to-image generation, departing from the token-choice MoE strategy (GShard, Switch Transformer, ST-MoE) that dominates prior sparse DiT work (DiT-MoE, RAPHAEL, SegMoE). It builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) with cross-attention text conditioning (PixArt-α) and rectified flow training (SD3), but the key conceptual departure is exploiting the global sequence context inherent to diffusion (as opposed to autoregressive decoding) to enable adaptive, heterogeneous compute allocation across image patches. The closest prior work, MoMa (Lin et al., 2024), also uses expert-choice routing but only at 7B parameters, whereas this paper scales to 97B.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that expert-choice routing is a more natural and effective scaling strategy for diffusion transformers than the token-choice default, providing a clear mechanistic explanation (global context availability in diffusion enables sequence-level routing decisions). The 97B-parameter scale and SOTA GenEval at 256×256 establish a new upper bound for sparse diffusion models, though the resolution gap with SD3 (512×512) limits direct practical comparison. Open questions include whether the adaptive routing generalizes to higher resolutions, video generation, and whether incorporating object semantics or compositional relationships (beyond pattern complexity) could further improve alignment. The technique's inherent load balancing (no auxiliary loss) and modest inference overhead (<30%) make it a practical scaling recipe likely to influence future DiT architectures.
