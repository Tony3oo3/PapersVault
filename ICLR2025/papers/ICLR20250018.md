## Problem

Forward-only transformers struggle with planning-heavy tasks such as goal-conditioned navigation and story writing because they learn shortcut strategies (e.g., the 'flawed cheat' on star graphs) that prevent them from developing representations capturing all information needed for future predictions. The paper asks whether modifying the architecture and training objective can systematically overcome this limitation in a domain-independent way.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prefix x_{1:t} and a suffix x_{t+k:T} (which may be empty at inference), processed by separate forward and backward GPT2-style encoders. The two output heads jointly predict the token immediately after the prefix and the token immediately before the suffix. For star graph experiments, the input is the edge list, start node, goal node, and current partial path; for TinyStories, it is a 50-token prefix and 100-token suffix. At inference the backward encoder is replaced by a precomputed empty-suffix latent, so the model uses no more parameters than a standard forward-only transformer.

## Method

The Belief State Transformer (BST) pairs a forward encoder F and a backward encoder B, each GPT2-style, with two output heads T_n and T_p that predict the next token after the prefix and the previous token before the suffix, respectively. Training jointly optimizes both objectives over all O(T²) valid prefix-suffix pairs per sequence, with gradients accumulated over the output head before backpropagating into the encoders for efficiency. The key theoretical result (Theorem 2) shows that an ideal BST's forward encoding is a compact belief state—sufficient to sample the entire future conditional distribution—while forward-only and multi-token predictors provably fail to achieve this (Theorems 3–4). The backward encoder's additional gradients prevent the optimization from collapsing into the parity-hard regime that traps forward-only models, enabling the BST to solve star graphs and outperform Fill-in-the-Middle on goal-conditioned and unconditional story generation.

## Results

On the star graph planning task, the Belief State Transformer achieves ~100% path accuracy on all three graph topologies (G(2,5), G(5,5), G(2,20)), while the forward-only baseline is capped at 1/d (50% for d=2, 20% for d=5) and the multi-token baseline fails on G(5,5) and G(2,20). On TinyStories goal-conditioned generation, GPT4 judges prefer BST outputs 64% of the time over Fill-in-the-Middle (binomial CI [54%, 74%]). In the unconditional setting, BST also outperforms FIM, with the GPT4 judge consistently noting that FIM outputs end abruptly mid-sentence while BST maintains correct grammar.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the forward-only next-token prediction baseline (from Bachmann & Nagarajan, ICML 2024), BST achieves ~100% path accuracy on all star graph sizes while the forward-only model is fundamentally limited to 1/d random-guessing accuracy (50% for G(2,5), 20% for G(5,5)), a gap the paper attributes to the parity-hardness of the problem once the 'flawed cheat' shortcut is learned. Against the multi-token (Teacherless) baseline from the same paper, BST wins decisively: multi-token succeeds only on the smallest graph G(2,5) but fails on G(5,5) and G(2,20), whereas BST solves all three. Against Fill-in-the-Middle (Bavarian et al., 2022), BST wins on both star graph navigation (FIM 'performs poorly') and TinyStories generation, where GPT4 judges prefer BST 64% of the time in the goal-conditioned setting and also in the unconditional setting, with FIM stories exhibiting repetitions, confusing dialogue, and abrupt grammatically incorrect transitions to the suffix.

## Relation to Prior Work

The Belief State Transformer repurposes the standard GPT2 decoder-only architecture by adding a backward encoder and a previous-token prediction head, creating a dual-objective training scheme that is architecturally distinct from Fill-in-the-Middle (Bavarian et al., 2022), which simply concatenates prefix and suffix into a single forward pass. While bidirectional models like BERT and ELMo use past-and-future context for representation learning, BST uses the backward signal specifically to shape the forward encoder's latent into a provably compact belief state during training. The star graph benchmark from Bachmann & Nagarajan (2024) provides the testbed, and the theoretical analysis departs from multi-token prediction work (Gloeckle et al., 2024; DeepSeek-V3) by showing that predicting multiple future tokens is insufficient to guarantee belief-state formation, whereas the backward prediction objective is.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core theoretical result—that an ideal BST provably recovers a full belief state while forward-only and multi-token predictors provably do not—gives a clean information-theoretic justification for the architecture and suggests no more complex objective can extract more information. However, all empirical evidence is at small scale (80M parameters, TinyStories, synthetic star graphs), and the paper explicitly acknowledges that scalability to larger practical models remains an open question. The practical impact hinges on whether the O(T²) training overhead and the architectural modification translate into meaningful gains at the scale of modern LLMs, which is untested here.
