## Problem

Generation performance of pre-trained diffusion models varies significantly across the latent space, but there is no principled, model-agnostic way to characterize or predict this variation from the model internals. The paper asks: for any deep generative model pre-trained on an arbitrary dataset, how does the local geometry of the generator function relate to downstream generation quality, diversity, and memorization?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253216/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained generative model's weights (Stable Diffusion 1.4, DDPM, or DiT) and a latent vector (or noisy latent at a specified diffusion timestep) to produce three scalar descriptors: local scaling ψ (log-volume change), local rank ν (effective manifold dimensionality), and local complexity δ (count of CPWL knots in a neighborhood). For the guidance application, a text prompt and seed are fed into the diffusion sampler with an additional reward-model gradient step at each denoising step, producing a geometry-steered image. Descriptors are computed via randomized SVD (k=120) of the input-output Jacobian, making them tractable for large latent diffusion models without retraining.

## Method

Building on the continuous piecewise-linear (CPWL) generator framework, the paper defines three local geometric descriptors—scaling (log-determinant of the per-region affine slope), rank (exponential of Shannon entropy of the singular value distribution), and complexity (number of non-linearities intersecting an ℓ₁-ball neighborhood)—that are computable from any pre-trained model's weights via a single randomized SVD of the input-output Jacobian. The key empirical finding is that these descriptors correlate with downstream generation properties: higher local scaling corresponds to more visually complex, diverse, and lower-likelihood images; lower scaling and rank with memorized or highly aligned generations; and higher complexity with less smooth, more artifact-prone regions. For practical control, the authors train a lightweight reward model (a 5-bin classifier on local scaling) on 50K–800K precomputed descriptors and inject its gradient into the reverse diffusion process, enabling instance-level guidance that increases or decreases generation diversity and aesthetic quality without modifying the base model.

## Results

The paper establishes that local scaling (ψ) of the Stable Diffusion decoder correlates with visual complexity, generation diversity (Vendi score), and human preference (RAHF aesthetic score) across 50K ImageNet and generated images. A reward model trained on as few as 50K precomputed local scaling samples can increase Vendi diversity and RAHF aesthetic scores across most pre-guidance local scaling bins during guided denoising. Computation cost is negligible: 3,929 s for 1,000 local scaling/rank samples and 113 s for 1,000 local complexity samples on TPUv3, totaling ~54.6 V100-hours for 100K samples versus 79,000 A100-hours for full Stable Diffusion training. Local geometry also discriminates on-manifold from off-manifold latents, with the maximum descriptor difference minimized at t ≈ 0.17T for well-trained DDPMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily an analysis and characterization paper rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so no traditional baselines are benchmarked. The paper's internal comparisons show: (1) on-manifold vs. off-manifold geometry is statistically distinguishable via t-tests on local scaling for Stable Diffusion at lower noise levels (Fig. 4); (2) memorized prompts (Wen et al., 2024) produce denoising trajectories with significantly lower mean local scaling, lower local rank, and higher local complexity than 100 random COCO prompts (Fig. 8-top); (3) higher classifier-free guidance scales (7.5 vs. 5 vs. 3) yield lower local scaling and rank but higher complexity, indicating tighter alignment with the conditioning (Fig. 8-bottom); (4) noise-type corruptions increase local scaling while blurring corruptions decrease it, confirming sensitivity to aesthetic perturbation (Fig. 7). The reward-guidance approach is compared against unguided Stable Diffusion baselines, showing consistent gains in Vendi score and RAHF aesthetic score across all pre-guidance bins, with the largest improvements in the lowest local scaling bins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CPWL generator framework (Balestriero et al., 2020; Humayun et al., 2023) and the geometric descriptors previously applied to GANs and small-scale VAEs (Humayun et al., 2021, 2022a,b) to foundational text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, DiT) for the first time at scale. It builds on the MaGNET density-scaling relationship (Humayun et al., 2022a) and the CPWL complexity notion (Hanin & Rolnick, 2019), while adapting the framework to non-CPWL activations (GeLU) via first-order Taylor approximation. For the reward guidance component, it repurposes the universal guidance framework (Bansal et al., 2023) by replacing direct Hessian computation with a lightweight classification-based reward model trained on precomputed local scaling values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale empirical study connecting local manifold geometry to downstream generation quality (aesthetics, diversity, memorization) in foundational text-to-image models, providing practitioners with interpretable, weight-based diagnostics that require no retraining. The practical demonstration that a simple reward model on local scaling can guide diffusion denoising to increase diversity and sharpness offers a lightweight, instance-level control mechanism complementary to distribution-level guidance. Key open questions include how training dynamics shape the learned geometry (the paper acknowledges this but does not resolve it), whether more efficient Jacobian-free estimators can reduce the 3929s/1000-samples computational cost, and whether the CPWL-based descriptors remain reliable as architectures move further from piecewise-linear activations.
