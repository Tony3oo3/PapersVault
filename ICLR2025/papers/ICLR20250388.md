## Problem

Single-agent self-improvement methods that iteratively finetune a language model on its own generated data quickly plateau after 2–3 rounds because response diversity collapses, causing the model to overfit to a narrow set of reasoning chains. This limits autonomous self-improvement to only modest gains above base performance. The paper asks whether structuring self-improvement as a multiagent society with independent finetuning can sustain diversity and enable consistent gains over many more rounds.

## Input / Output

The system takes natural language reasoning questions (arithmetic expressions, grade-school math word problems, competition-level MATH problems) as input and produces a reasoning chain ending in a final numerical answer. At inference, N generation agents each produce an initial response, then N critic agents iteratively refine responses over M debate rounds using summarized peer outputs, and a majority vote over the final round determines the answer. For finetuning data construction, generation agents are trained only on their first-round responses that match the majority-voted answer, while critic agents are trained on full multi-round response sequences where they either corrected an initially wrong answer or maintained a correct one throughout.

## Method

The core mechanism is a society of N generation agents and N critic agents, all initialized from the same base LLM, that are independently specialized through multiagent debate and per-agent finetuning. In each iteration, generation agents produce initial responses, critic agents evaluate and refine them over M debate rounds, and majority voting yields a pseudo-ground-truth answer. Each generation agent is then finetuned exclusively on its own correct first-round responses, and each critic agent on its own multi-round sequences (a weighted mix of correction cases and maintenance cases), so that different agents see different data subsets and develop distinct reasoning specializations. This independent, role-specific training preserves inter-agent diversity across iterations, preventing the model collapse that limits single-agent self-improvement. A simple mathematical model (Section G) formalizes why training each model only on questions it answers correctly drives monotonic specialization on different topics, ensuring the agent society collectively covers a broader solution space over successive finetuning rounds.

## Results

On the MATH dataset (levels 1–3), multiagent finetuning with a single iteration (L=1) achieves 60.60% accuracy with GPT-3.5, 58.80% with Phi-3, 22.50% with Mistral, and 57.40% with LLaMA-3, outperforming all baselines across every model and dataset. On GSM, the method reaches 85.60% (GPT-3.5), 88.60% (Phi-3), 58.40% (Mistral), and 88.60% (LLaMA-3). Over five iterations of finetuning on MATH, Phi-3 improves from 58.8% to 66.0% and Mistral from 22.5% to 28.2%, whereas single-agent finetuning saturates after one iteration and degrades.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two primary baselines: multiagent Debate (Du et al., 2023) and STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022), both of which are well-established approaches for improving LLM reasoning. Multiagent finetuning outperforms Debate on every dataset and every LLM: on MATH, the gains are +4.87 pp (GPT-3.5: 60.60 vs. 55.73), +5.40 pp (Phi-3: 58.80 vs. 53.40), +4.30 pp (Mistral: 22.50 vs. 18.20), and +5.80 pp (LLaMA-3: 57.40 vs. 51.60). Against STaR, which uses ground-truth labels for data selection and multiple finetuning iterations, the proposed method still wins on MATH by +7.60 pp (GPT-3.5), +7.00 pp (Phi-3), and +4.66 pp (Mistral), despite using only a single finetuning iteration without access to ground truth. The advantage is most pronounced on the harder MATH benchmark and for weaker base models (Mistral), where the gap over Debate reaches 4.3 pp and over STaR reaches 4.7 pp. On the easier Arithmetic task, all methods converge near saturation (98–100%), so the differentiation is minimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: self-improvement through iterative finetuning (STaR, self-play fine-tuning, self-rewarding LMs) and multiagent debate for inference-time reasoning (Du et al., 2023). Prior self-improvement methods finetune a single model on its own generated data and typically plateau after 2–3 rounds due to diversity collapse, while prior multiagent debate methods use multiple agents only at inference without any finetuning. The key departure here is a multiagent finetuning procedure in which N generation agents and N critic agents, all initialized from the same base model, are each finetuned on independent, role-specific subsets of debate-generated data, thereby preserving and even increasing response diversity across iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized bottleneck in LLM self-improvement—diminishing returns after a few finetuning rounds—and shows that a society of specialized agents can sustain gains over 5+ iterations (e.g., Phi-3 MATH accuracy rising from 58.8% to 66.0%), a regime single-agent methods cannot reach. The zero-shot generalization result (agents finetuned on MATH outperforming baselines finetuned on GSM) suggests the learned reasoning skills transfer beyond the training distribution. However, the approach is substantially more expensive than single-model finetuning (multiple full model copies, multi-GPU training, multi-agent inference), and the authors note that weight sharing, distillation of the debate into a single model, and integration with RLHF/DPO remain open directions for making the method more practical at scale.
