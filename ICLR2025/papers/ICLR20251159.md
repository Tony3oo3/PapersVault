## Problem

Existing token pruning strategies for Vision Transformers rely on single-branch attention scores to identify unimportant tokens, but discriminative self-supervised learning uses dual-branch Siamese encoders where cross-branch similarity is the core training signal. Applying conventional single-branch pruning to SSL causes a severe accuracy drop (over 2.4% at 80% keep rate) compared to only ~0.2% in supervised learning, because it can asymmetrically prune semantically matching tokens across branches, breaking the consistency that SSL depends on.

## Input / Output

The method operates on two differently augmented views of the same RGB image (e.g., 224×224 patches), each processed by a separate branch of a Siamese ViT encoder (DeiT-T/S/B). Token pruning is applied at selected transformer blocks (4th, 7th, 10th in DeiT), reducing the token sequence length by a predefined keep rate (e.g., 0.8 or 0.7) at each pruning point. The output is a trained ViT encoder evaluated via linear probing for classification, or fine-tuned for object detection (MS COCO) and semantic segmentation (ADE20K).

## Method

SimPrune introduces two mechanisms tailored to the dual-branch SSL setting. First, it performs cross-branch token matching: for each image token in the online (student) branch, it finds the most similar token in the target (teacher) branch via cosine similarity, forming N token pairs; pruning is then executed at the granularity of these pairs so that semantically corresponding tokens are either both retained or both removed, preserving cross-branch consistency. Second, it employs a difficulty-aware sliding-window strategy over the sorted similarity values: early in training the window retains the most similar pairs (pruning the most dissimilar, i.e., an easier task), and the window shifts linearly across epochs toward retaining more dissimilar pairs (pruning the most similar, i.e., a harder task), gradually increasing training difficulty without extra computation. A many-to-one matching imbalance is resolved by applying a lightweight attention-based prune on the target branch to equalize token counts.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with DeiT-S, SimPrune at a keep rate of 0.8 achieves 62.31% top-1 accuracy (vs. 62.49% DINO baseline) while reducing training FLOPs by 24% and training time by 15%. At a more aggressive keep rate of 0.7, SimPrune reaches 61.90% accuracy, outperforming EViT (58.40%) by 3.5% and ToMe (58.68%) by 3.2%. On downstream tasks, SimPrune achieves 49.7 AP^b on MS COCO object detection and 34.0 mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation, matching the DINO baseline (49.8 / 34.3) at 76% FLOPs. The method also generalizes to fine-grained datasets (Stanford Cars, FGVC Aircraft) and alternative SSL frameworks (MoCo v3, DINOv2) with consistent 20–24% FLOP savings and minimal accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimPrune consistently outperforms all single-branch token reduction baselines. Against EViT (attention-based pruning), SimPrune achieves 3.1% higher average accuracy at keep rate 0.7 across DeiT-T/S/B (e.g., 61.90% vs. 58.40% on DeiT-S), and 1.7 AP^b / 2.8 mIoU higher on MS COCO / ADE20K downstream tasks. Against ToMe (token merging), SimPrune delivers 3.2% higher accuracy at keep rate 0.7 and 2.9 AP^b / 2.3 mIoU higher on downstream tasks. In the extended comparison (Table 7), SimPrune also beats MCTF, PatchSlim, and SelfSlim by up to 3% accuracy at comparable FLOPs. On DINOv2, SimPrune outperforms Expediting ViT by 3.9% and EViT by 2.9% accuracy. SimPrune is also compatible with orthogonal efficiency methods: combining it with ETS or Rotation further reduces training time by 17% and 15% respectively without accuracy loss. The core advantage stems from leveraging cross-branch similarity to prune token pairs jointly, preserving semantic consistency that single-branch methods (EViT, ToMe, MCTF, PatchSlim, SelfSlim, Expediting ViT) inherently overlook.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimPrune repurposes the token pruning paradigm established for supervised ViT training (EViT, DynamicViT, PatchSlim) by identifying that its core assumption—single-branch attention scores as a proxy for token importance—breaks down in the dual-branch Siamese architecture of discriminative SSL. Rather than building on a single prior method, the paper carves new ground by introducing cross-branch cosine similarity as the pruning criterion, ensuring semantic consistency is preserved across augmented views. The difficulty-aware sliding-window strategy draws conceptually from curriculum learning literature (Bengio et al., 2009; Zheng et al., 2019) but applies it to the novel problem of controlling SSL training difficulty via selective token-pair removal rather than data ordering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 24% training FLOPs reduction without accuracy loss is practically significant for deploying SSL on resource-constrained platforms, and the finding that SSL is substantially more sensitive to naive token pruning than supervised learning (2.4% vs. 0.2% accuracy drop) is a useful caution for the broader efficiency-in-SSL community. Limitations include evaluation restricted to discriminative SSL (not MIM-based methods like MAE/BEiT), a single encoder family (DeiT), and an ad-hoc handling of the many-to-one token matching imbalance. Open questions include whether the cross-branch similarity principle generalizes to MIM or hybrid frameworks, whether the sliding-window difficulty schedule is optimal, and whether the approach extends to non-Siamese SSL architectures.
