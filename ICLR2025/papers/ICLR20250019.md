## Problem

Video action recognition models suffer from both background bias (predicting actions from scene context) and foreground bias (relying on subject appearance), yet prior debiasing methods have focused predominantly on background bias and require specialized knowledge such as scene labels, separate critic networks, or off-the-shelf salient frame detectors. Additionally, the existing UCF101 bias evaluation protocol leaks background information through bounding-box masks, providing a shortcut that inflates scores and obscures true debiasing performance.

## Input / Output

At test time, the model takes a 32-frame video clip at 224×224 resolution and outputs a single action class label. During training, the method additionally constructs a 'static clip' by sampling one frame from the motion clip and repeating it 32 times; both clips pass through the same Swin-T encoder, but only the motion clip's prediction is scored against the ground truth at inference. The static clip serves purely as an internal adversarial training signal and is never seen at test time.

## Method

ALBAR pairs each motion clip with a zero-motion static clip (one repeated frame) fed through the same 3D encoder, then applies three complementary losses to the static clip's prediction: (1) an adversarial cross-entropy loss with reversed gradients that penalizes the model for correctly classifying actions from static spatial cues alone, (2) an entropy maximization loss that forces the static-clip prediction to be uniform across all classes, preventing the trivial solution of confidently predicting the wrong class, and (3) a gradient penalty (L2 norm of encoder gradients w.r.t. the static input) that stabilizes training by reducing the encoder's sensitivity to static inputs. The combined objective is L_CE + ω_adv·L_adv + ω_ent·L_ent + ω_gp·L_gp, with the motion clip's standard cross-entropy loss anchoring correct predictions. This design eliminates the need for bias attribute labels, separate critic models, or specialized augmentations, and the authors further propose a corrected UCF101 evaluation protocol using SAMTrack segmentation masks to eliminate background leakage.

## Results

On HMDB51, ALBAR achieves a contrasted accuracy of 53.22%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art StillMix (40.28%) by over 12% absolute. The method also dramatically reduces foreground bias, dropping average SCUFO accuracy from 20.46% (no debiasing) to 1.50%, while improving background robustness (Avg SCUBA) from 43.93% to 53.53%. On the improved UCF101 protocol, ALBAR reaches 26.23% contrasted accuracy vs. StillMix's 24.40%, and on Kinetics400 it achieves 44.74% vs. 36.60%. Downstream, the debiased encoder improves UCF_Crime anomaly detection AUC from 82.39% to 84.91% and THUMOS14 mAP from 54.89% to 55.20%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALBAR outperforms all six compared baselines on the primary contrasted accuracy metric across all three main datasets. On HMDB51, ALBAR (53.22%) surpasses the strongest prior method StillMix (40.28%) by 12.94 points, and beats FAME (29.66%), ActorCutMix (28.12%), BE (27.84%), Mixup (26.09%), VideoMix (23.13%), and SDN (22.88%) by 23–30 points. The advantage is most pronounced on foreground bias (Avg SCUFO): ALBAR reduces it to 1.50% versus StillMix's 13.39% and FAME's 26.92%, indicating a far stronger elimination of static foreground reliance. On the improved UCF101 protocol, ALBAR (26.23%) edges out StillMix (24.40%) by ~2 points, and on Kinetics400 it leads by 8.1 points (44.74% vs. 36.60%). The only trade-off is a slight drop in IID accuracy (72.81% vs. 74.82% for StillMix on HMDB51), which is largely recovered when StillMix augmentation is combined with ALBAR (74.31% IID, 53.68% contrasted accuracy).

## Relation to Prior Work

ALBAR extends the adversarial debiasing lineage (Beutel et al. 2017, Zhang et al. 2018) by eliminating the need for a separate critic network or bias-attribute labels, instead using the same 3D encoder on a static (motionless) clip as the adversarial signal. It departs from prior action-recognition debiasing methods (SDN, BE, ActorCutMix, StillMix) that primarily target background bias through augmentations or spatio-temporal decoupling, by simultaneously addressing foreground bias within a single-encoder framework. The gradient penalty component is adapted from WGAN-GP (Gulrajani et al. 2017) but simplified to directly minimize the gradient norm w.r.t. the static input rather than interpolating between samples. The paper also identifies and corrects a background-leakage flaw in the UCF101 SCUBA/SCUFO protocol (Li et al. 2023) by replacing bounding-box masks with SAMTrack segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's simplicity—requiring no additional models, no attribute annotations, and no specialized augmentations—makes it broadly applicable and easy to integrate into existing pipelines, and it can be stacked with prior augmentations like StillMix for further gains. The identification of background leakage in the UCF101 protocol is a methodological correction that affects all prior work using that benchmark, as inflated SCUBA scores may have masked true background bias. Key open questions include whether the assumption that no static information should be useful is always appropriate (e.g., for actions where appearance is genuinely informative), whether the approach generalizes to demographic biases (skin color, facial hair) that the paper mentions but does not directly evaluate, and whether the single-encoder adversarial formulation scales to longer or untrimmed video settings.
