## Problem

Existing LLM alignment methods (RLHF, DPO, PPO) optimize toward monolithic human values, failing to capture the unique behavioral preferences of individual users. Collecting the millions of behavioral data points needed for per-person alignment is impractical, and training-based approaches like PPO or DPO are too computationally expensive to scale to millions of individuals. The paper introduces 'Personality Alignment'—tailoring LLM responses to match a specific person's trait profile—and asks how to achieve this efficiently with minimal per-user data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250513/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a subject's responses to a structured personality questionnaire (IPIP-NEO-120 for Big Five traits or the 27-item Short Dark Triad for negative traits, each on a 5-point 'Very Accurate' to 'Very Inaccurate' scale) as the personality signal, combined with a text prompt at inference time. It produces a personality-aligned text response whose behavioral tendencies match the target individual. Evaluation uses a behavioral difference score (0–4, lower is better) computed as the mean absolute deviation between the LLM's and the subject's questionnaire answers across all items for each trait dimension.

## Method

Personality Activation Search (PAS) is a training-free, activation-steering method that requires no weight modification or backpropagation. First, linear probes are trained on each attention head's activations (using the subject's questionnaire responses formatted as yes/no classification) to identify the top-K heads most predictive of that individual's personality. The normalized probe weights serve as direction vectors in the residual stream between the attention output and the projection matrix Q. Second, a 1-D search over the scalar α ∈ [0,10] determines the optimal intervention magnitude that minimizes the alignment score. At inference, the model's activations in selected heads are shifted by α·σ along the personality direction, storing only ~1,000 parameters per subject (roughly 1/10,000,000 of total model parameters). This yields 1/6 the optimization time of PPO while achieving superior alignment scores across both Big Five and Dark Triad dimensions.

## Results

On the PAPI dataset, PAS achieves the lowest (best) Aligned Score of 8.89 on Llama-3-8B-Instruct and 9.21 on Llama-3-70B-Instruct, outperforming all white-box and black-box baselines across all eight personality dimensions (Big Five + Dark Triad). The metric is the mean absolute deviation between the LLM's and the target subject's questionnaire responses (range 0–4 per dimension; lower is better). PAS requires only ~1/6 of the optimization time of PPO while delivering superior alignment, and on Llama-3-8B it even surpasses GPT-4o's Few-Shot performance on most Big Five dimensions (e.g., Openness 0.72 vs. 0.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAS outperforms all four baseline families on both Llama-3-8B and Llama-3-70B. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), PAS reduces the composite Aligned Score from 13.27→8.89 (8B) and 12.92→9.21 (70B), a gain of ~4.4 and ~3.7 points respectively, while requiring only 1/6 of PPO's optimization time. Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), PAS improves from 12.97→8.89 (8B) and 12.30→9.21 (70B). Against Personalized-Soups and Prompt-MORL (Jang et al., 2024), PAS leads by 0.77/2.0 points (8B) and 0.83/1.5 points (70B), with the gap widening on Dark Triad dimensions (e.g., Machiavellianism 0.96 vs. 1.08 for Soups on 8B). Against the P2 Personality Prompt (Jiang et al., 2024), PAS is substantially better (8.89 vs. 12.25 on 8B), confirming that activation-level intervention surpasses prompt-based ICL. The only method that beats PAS on composite score is GPT-4o Few-Shot (6.81), but PAS on Llama-3-8B still surpasses GPT-4o on four of five Big Five dimensions (Openness 0.72 vs. 0.96, Extraversion 0.86 vs. 0.81 is the exception) and on Machiavellianism (0.96 vs. 0.80 is the exception), showing that targeted activation steering can close the gap with a much larger proprietary model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-steering paradigm (Turner et al. 2023; Li et al. 2024a) from single-objective interventions (safety, truthfulness) to multi-dimensional, per-individual personality alignment, using probe-based head identification from Burns et al. (2023) to find behaviorally separable directions. It departs from the dominant RLHF/DPO/PPO alignment framework, which treats human values as a monolithic societal construct, by reframing alignment as matching an individual's psychometric profile across Big Five and Dark Triad dimensions. The closest prior work—Jang et al. (2023/2024) Personalized Soups and Jiang et al. (2024) P2 prompts—addresses limited preference personalization (8 combinations of 3 dimensions) or qualitative role-play, whereas this paper formalizes the task with 320K+ real psychometric samples and quantitative evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PAPI dataset (325K real subjects across IPIP-NEO-120/300 and Dark Triad) is a practically important resource that the personality-alignment literature has lacked, enabling reproducible quantitative evaluation beyond small-scale or synthetic setups. The training-free PAS method's 1/6-time efficiency relative to PPO makes per-user alignment computationally feasible at scale, which is critical for real deployment. However, open questions remain: Dark Triad alignment scores are notably weaker (1.6–1.9 vs. 0.7–1.0 for Big Five), suggesting the approach struggles with maladaptive traits; the ethical risks of echo chambers and psychological filter bubbles are acknowledged but not empirically mitigated; and the generalization from multiple-choice questionnaire data to open-ended natural interaction remains partially validated.
