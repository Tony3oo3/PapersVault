## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) suffer from persistent hallucinations in dense image captioning, where generated text deviates from visual content due to over-reliance on pretrained language priors. Two critical gaps exist: (1) no metric finely measures caption quality at the concept level, capturing both hallucinated and omitted elements with granularity across objects, attributes, and relations; and (2) existing hallucination mitigation methods either incur substantial training overhead (RLHF/DPO-based approaches) or add inference cost (contrastive decoding), without addressing the root cause of language bias.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image paired with a text question/prompt and produces a dense, detailed image caption. For evaluation, HalFscore takes a generated caption and a ground-truth dense caption (from 1,000 manually annotated DCI images), parses both into triplet-based concept graphs via GPT-4o, and outputs precision (hallucination degree), recall (completeness), and their F-score aggregate. Perturbation texts are generated by GPT-4o conditioned on the image, question, and correct answer, then inserted as additional input tokens before the question during training without any direct loss computed on them.

## Method

PerturboLLaVA introduces adversarially perturbed text during instruction tuning to reduce the model's dependence on language priors. GPT-4o generates contextually relevant perturbations that align with common world knowledge but conflict with the actual visual content (e.g., stating 'bananas turn yellow as they ripen' before asking about a green banana), and these are inserted before the question in the input sequence. The key insight is that by tempting the model to follow misleading textual cues, the training gradient is redirected toward optimizing p(x_k | x_{<k}^{-p}, I) — the image-grounded prediction — rather than the language-prior-driven p(x_k | x_{<k}^{p}), effectively decoupling the model's output from textual bias. Complementarily, HalFscore constructs concept graphs from captions using a triplet representation ⟨e₁, e₂, r₁₂⟩, where GPT-4o extracts entities and relations, then computes precision and recall by identifying hallucinated and omitted concepts through graph matching, yielding a fine-grained F-score that captures hallucinations at the object, attribute, and relation levels simultaneously.

## Results

PerturboLLaVA achieves the best HalFscore of 52.2 among single-method approaches, outperforming VCD (52.0), OPERA (51.9), and RLAIF-V (51.9) on the LLaVA1.5 baseline. On general multimodal benchmarks, PerturboLLaVA improves over the LLaVA1.5 baseline by +1.6 on MMBench (68.9 vs. 67.3), +0.3 on SEED-Bench (65.6 vs. 65.3), and +1.2 on CCBench (30.6 vs. 29.4), while VCD and RLAIF-V show degradation on these metrics. Combining PerturboLLaVA with OPERA at inference yields the best overall HalFscore of 52.8 and CCBench of 31.0. On CHAIR_s (object-level hallucination), RLAIF-V remains strongest (18.1 vs. 36.1 for PerturboLLaVA), though PerturboLLaVA substantially outperforms OPERA (50.8) and VCD (51.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LLaVA1.5 baseline, PerturboLLaVA improves HalFscore by +3.0 (52.2 vs. 49.2), reduces CHAIR_s by 18.1 points (36.1 vs. 54.2), and boosts all three general benchmarks (MMB +1.6, SEED +0.3, CCBench +1.2). Compared to the decoding strategies OPERA and VCD, PerturboLLaVA wins on HalFscore (52.2 vs. 51.9/52.0) and all general benchmarks, while losing on CHAIR_s (36.1 vs. 50.8/51.2) — though the gap narrows substantially. Against the training-based RLAIF-V, PerturboLLaVA achieves a higher HalFscore (52.2 vs. 51.9) and substantially better general performance (MMB 68.9 vs. 63.7, CCBench 30.6 vs. 27.8), but RLAIF-V dominates on object-level CHAIR metrics (CHAIR_s 18.1 vs. 36.1, CHAIR_i 4.7 vs. 10.4) and HallusionBench (51.3 vs. 47.5), likely due to its use of a 34B reward model. The paper also shows that PerturboLLaVA is complementary to OPERA: combining them at inference yields the best HalFscore (52.8) and CCBench (31.0), confirming that perturbative training and contrastive decoding address different aspects of hallucination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA1.5 visual instruction tuning pipeline, inserting adversarially perturbed text into the SFT stage to counteract the model's inherited language bias. It departs from the two dominant hallucination-mitigation paradigms—training-based methods (RLAIF-V, DPO) that require costly reward models or preference data, and decoding-based strategies (OPERA, VCD) that add inference overhead—by proposing a perturbative training strategy that integrates seamlessly into standard SFT with only ~6% additional training time and zero inference cost. The HalFscore metric extends prior hallucination evaluation (CHAIR, MMHalBench) by constructing a language graph via triplet extraction, enabling fine-grained precision/recall decomposition over objects, attributes, and relations rather than a single holistic score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: a nearly free training-time intervention that reduces hallucinations while simultaneously improving general multimodal benchmarks (MMBench +1.6, CCBench +1.2), and it composes additively with decoding strategies like OPERA. However, the evaluation pipeline (HalFscore construction, perturbation generation, and graph matching) all depend on GPT-4o, creating a circularity concern that limits the metric's independence. The approach is validated only on LLaVA1.5/7B, leaving open whether the perturbative training generalizes to larger or architecturally different MLLMs, and whether the GPT-4o-generated perturbations can be replaced by cheaper or rule-based alternatives for broader adoption.
