## Problem

Conventional visual instruction tuning for Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) supervises only text outputs, leaving the visual outputs corresponding to input image tokens entirely unsupervised. This LLM-centric design causes systematic visual shortcomings—poor fine-grained comprehension and elevated hallucination—because the rich low-level detail inherent in input images is never explicitly leveraged as a training signal. The paper asks whether the input images themselves can serve as direct supervision for visual outputs, and how to formulate this reconstruction in a way that avoids the heavy spatial redundancy of raw pixel values.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250518/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, ROSS takes a single RGB image (336×336 for CLIP or 384×384 for SigLIP) and a text instruction, producing a standard text response. During training, the visual outputs (one per visual token, e.g., 576 for CLIP-336 or 729 for SigLIP-384) are additionally supervised: a frozen KL-16 VAE tokenizer encodes the input image into 729 latent tokens at 432×432 resolution, and a small denoiser is trained to recover clean latent tokens from noisy versions conditioned on those visual outputs. The denoiser's input is a triple (noisy latent tokens z_t, timestep t, visual outputs x_{i≤N}), and its output is the predicted noise ε̂_t.

## Method

ROSS augments the standard next-token-prediction loss with a vision-centric reconstructive term: a small denoiser (a stack of Transformer Encoder blocks, each containing three linear projections for conditions, noisy tokens, and timesteps, plus a self-attention module) is trained to denoise latent visual tokens z_0 produced by a frozen KL-16 continuous VAE, conditioned on the LMM's visual outputs x_{i≤N}. The key design choice is using a per-token denoising objective (predicting Gaussian noise added to latent tokens) rather than directly regressing raw RGB pixels or latent values, which avoids being overwhelmed by spatial redundancy and encourages the model to preserve fine-grained image detail. This 'intrinsic activation' approach requires only a single visual encoder (SigLIP or CLIP) and no additional visual experts, in contrast to extrinsic-assistance methods that aggregate multiple domain-specific models. The total training loss is L_LMM = L_text + L_visual, where L_visual is the expected squared error between predicted and true noise over timesteps and noise samples, and the denoiser adds only ~10% training overhead.

## Results

ROSS-7B (SigLIP + Qwen2-7B-Instruct) achieves 57.1 on HallusionBench, 56.7 on MMVP, 88.3 on POPE, and 79.1 on MMBench-EN, consistently outperforming the open-source state-of-the-art Cambrian-1-8B (which aggregates four visual experts) on 7 out of 9 benchmarks. Notably, ROSS-7B surpasses GPT-4V-1106 on POPE (88.3 vs. 75.4), MMBench-EN (79.1 vs. 75.8), MMVP (56.7 vs. 50.0), GQA (65.5 vs. 36.8), and AI2D (79.3 vs. 78.2), while trailing on HallusionBench (57.1 vs. 65.8) and MMMU (46.6 vs. 53.8). The denoising-based reconstructive supervision adds only ~10% training overhead while delivering substantial gains, particularly on fine-grained and hallucination-sensitive benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest open-source competitor Cambrian-1-8B (which aggregates CLIP, SigLIP, DINOv2, and ConvNeXt as extrinsic visual experts), ROSS-7B with a single SigLIP encoder wins on POPE (88.3 vs. 87.4), HallusionBench (57.1 vs. 48.7, a +8.4 gap), MMBench-EN (79.1 vs. 75.9), MMBench-CN (77.1 vs. 68.9), MMMU (46.6 vs. 42.7), MMVP (56.7 vs. 51.3), GQA (65.5 vs. 64.6), and AI2D (79.3 vs. 73.0), while losing narrowly on SEED-Bench (73.6 vs. 74.7). Against GPT-4V-1106, ROSS-7B surpasses it on POPE (+12.9), MMBench-EN (+3.3), MMVP (+6.7), GQA (+28.7), and AI2D (+1.1), but trails on HallusionBench (57.1 vs. 65.8) and MMMU (46.6 vs. 53.8). Compared to LLaVA-v1.5-7B and LLaVA-v1.6-7B (the base architectures), ROSS-7B improves HallusionBench by +9.6 and +21.3 points respectively, and MMVP by +36.7 and +19.4 points, confirming the reconstructive supervision's impact on fine-grained visual comprehension. On the 13B scale, ROSS-13B outperforms Cambrian-1-13B on POPE, HallusionBench, MMBench-CN, MMMU, MMVP, GQA, and AI2D. On the SpatialBench depth-map transfer task, ROSS with RGB+D inputs achieves 66.1 average (vs. 55.0 for LLaVA RGB-only), while the extrinsic MiDaS-3.0 expert approach actually degrades performance (60.0), and even GPT-4o drops from 67.0 to 65.7 when given depth maps.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROSS builds directly on the LLaVA-v1.5 plug-in architecture (visual encoder → connector → LLM) and its two-stage training recipe, but departs fundamentally from the LLM-centric supervision paradigm by introducing a vision-centric reconstructive loss on visual outputs. The denoising objective adapts the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) to a conditional latent-token recovery task, drawing conceptual inspiration from MAE's insight that spatial redundancy in visual signals makes naive regression uninformative. In contrast to concurrent extrinsic-assistance approaches like Cambrian-1 that aggregate multiple visual experts (CLIP, SigLIP, DINOv2, ConvNext), ROSS pursues intrinsic activation with a single encoder, and differs from generative LMMs (DreamLLM, Sun et al. 2024b) by using input images as reconstruction targets rather than text as generation conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single SigLIP encoder with reconstructive supervision matches or exceeds multi-expert aggregation (Cambrian-1) challenges the assumption that more visual experts are necessary for fine-grained comprehension, offering a simpler and more scalable path for LMM design. The demonstration that high-level LMM features retain sufficient low-level detail for image reconstruction after only 5 epochs of denoiser fine-tuning opens the possibility of equipping comprehension models with naive generation capability without external diffusion models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video understanding, whether the frozen teacher tokenizer (KL-16) becomes a bottleneck at higher resolutions, and whether the ~10% training overhead remains acceptable at larger scales. The method's simplicity (a small denoiser + existing instruction-tuning data) makes it practically attractive for the LMM community.
