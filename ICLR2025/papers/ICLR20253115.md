## Problem

Circuit representation learning models face a fundamental scalability bottleneck: GNNs suffer from over-squashing and over-smoothing on large circuits, while transformer-based approaches like DeepGate3 incur quadratic memory complexity that causes OOM errors on circuits with more than ~50K gates. Existing sub-linear GNNs and linear graph transformers either disregard the causal (topological) structure of AIGs or still fail on practical designs with millions of gates, leaving no viable path to train effective representation models at industrial circuit scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes And-Inverter Graphs (AIGs) derived from circuit netlists via the ABC tool, where each node is a PI (in-degree 0), AND gate (in-degree 2), or NOT gate (in-degree 1), forming a DAG with uniform low-degree structure. It produces disentangled functional and structural embeddings at both gate-level and graph-level (via pooling), which are then used by downstream task heads for truth-table prediction, connection classification, size/depth regression, and pairwise distance estimation. A key operational detail is that during training and inference, the model processes the circuit in topological level order, pushing computed embeddings to CPU-side historical storage and pulling them back to initialize overlapping regions in subsequent mini-batches.

## Method

DeepGate4 partitions a large AIG into overlapping cones (depth k=8, stride δ=6) and encodes them level-by-level in topological order, using an intra-level updating strategy (marking already-computed gates to avoid redundant computation) and an inter-level updating strategy (retrieving historical embeddings from the previous level to preserve long-range receptive fields). Within each cone, a GAT-based sparse transformer replaces the dense multi-head attention of DeepGate3, constructing virtual edges from the partial order relation to enable global aggregation while maintaining linear complexity. The model incorporates structural encodings (logic level and out-degree) into initial embeddings, a gradient-norm-based multi-task loss balancer, and a custom Fused-DG2 CUDA kernel that exploits the AIG's max-in-degree-2 property to eliminate thread waste and skip trivial softmax computations, reducing inference time by 35% and memory by 47% over the unfused variant.

## Results

On the ITC99 benchmark, DeepGate4 achieves an overall loss (L_all) of 3.16±0.076, reducing it by 15.5% compared to the second-best method (DeepGate3† at 3.73±0.148). On the EPFL Random Control benchmark, DeepGate4 achieves L_all of 4.43±0.577, a 31.1% improvement over the next-best (Exphormer† at 6.43±0.577). In terms of efficiency, DeepGate4 uses only 479 MB memory and 2.50 s inference time on ITC99, while DeepGate3† requires 6565 MB and 11.32 s. The Fused-DeepGate4 variant further reduces inference time to 1.46 s and memory to 233 MB on ITC99 (41.4% and 51.4% reductions over DeepGate4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepGate4 outperforms all 11 baselines on both ITC99 and EPFL in overall loss (L_all). Against the closest competitor DeepGate3†, DeepGate4 reduces L_all by 15.5% on ITC99 (3.16 vs. 3.73) and 45.5% on EPFL (4.43 vs. 8.13), while also cutting inference time from 11.3 s to 2.5 s (77.9% reduction) and memory from 6565 MB to 479 MB (92.7% reduction) on ITC99. Among sparse transformer baselines, Exphormer† is the strongest on ITC99 (L_all 4.50) but still trails DeepGate4 by 29.8%; on EPFL, Exphormer† (6.43) is beaten by 31.1%. GNN baselines (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT, PNA, DeepGate2, PolarGate, HOGA-5) all suffer from over-squashing and over-smoothing, yielding L_all values between 5.69 and 26.69, far worse than DeepGate4. Notably, dense transformer baselines (GraphGPS, Exphormer, DAGformer, DeepGate3) all encounter OOM errors without the partitioning strategy, and even with it († variants), they remain inferior in both accuracy and efficiency. On the OpenABC-D benchmark (Appendix A.4), DeepGate4 further reduces L_all by 31.48% over the second-best (GraphSAGE at 0.8004 vs. DeepGate4 at 0.5484) and achieves 79% gate-connection prediction accuracy versus 64.94% for GAT.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepGate4 extends the DeepGate family (DeepGate → DeepGate2 → DeepGate3) by replacing the dense transformer in DeepGate3 with a GAT-based sparse transformer using virtual edges (inspired by DAGformer), and introduces a cone-partitioning strategy with level-ordered updating that achieves sub-linear memory complexity. It departs from prior sub-linear GNNs (GNNAutoScale, SketchGNN, GraphFM) by respecting the topological/causal order of AIGs rather than using random subgraph sampling, and from general GNN acceleration (Fused-GAT) by exploiting the uniform low in-degree (≤2) of AIGs to design a custom CUDA kernel that eliminates thread waste and synchronization overhead. The structural encoding and multi-task loss balancer are incremental additions building on DeepGate2/3's disentangled embedding framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cone-partitioning and updating strategy is the key transferable contribution: it is adaptable to any graph transformer and enables training on circuits with millions of gates that previously caused OOM errors, including for dense models like DeepGate3. This opens the door to applying transformer-based global aggregation to industrial-scale EDA tasks (SAT solving, logic equivalence checking, testability analysis) that were previously limited to GNNs with known over-squashing issues. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond AIGs to other circuit representations (e.g., gate-level netlists with multi-input gates), whether the constant-memory property holds for truly billion-gate designs, and whether the partitioning overhead can be further reduced for real-time inference in design flows.
