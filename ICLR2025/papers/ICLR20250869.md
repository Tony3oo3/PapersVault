## Problem

Existing medical vision-language models (e.g., CLIP, Merlin, BIUD) perform global contrastive learning between entire CT volumes and whole radiology reports, ignoring the local correspondence between specific anatomical regions and their report sentences. This coarse-grained alignment causes the model to attend to diagnostically irrelevant regions, degrading both accuracy and interpretability. Moreover, moving to anatomy-level fine-grained alignment introduces a severe false-negative problem: the abundance of anatomy-level normal samples and the high semantic similarity among samples sharing the same pathology create ambiguous patient-level pairings that mislead contrastive training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D CT volumes (D×H×W) and their paired radiology reports. CT volumes are segmented into 36 major anatomies via TotalSegmentator (104 sub-regions grouped), and reports are decomposed into anatomy-wise descriptions using Qwen 2.5 with a complementary string-matching fallback. At inference, the model produces per-anatomy visual and textual embeddings whose cosine similarity with a disease description yields a zero-shot diagnosis score; the same visual encoder can also be paired with a BERT decoder for whole-report generation. Anatomies truncated by RandomCrop during batching are excluded to preserve visual integrity.

## Method

fVLM replaces global image-report contrast with per-anatomy contrastive pre-training: for each anatomy j, the ViT encoder's tokens within the TotalSegmentator mask are aggregated by a learnable anatomy-specific query token (updated via self-attention and L2-normalized), while the BERT encoder processes the corresponding report sentence. Contrastive loss is computed independently for each anatomy across the mini-batch, with a learnable temperature. To handle false negatives, a dual FNR module (1) labels anatomy pairs as positive when both patients' reports omit that anatomy from the impression section (indicating normality), and (2) bootstraps soft labels from predicted similarities using a co-teaching scheme where two fVLMs trained with different initializations and augmentations alternately correct each other's contrastive targets, preventing error accumulation. The full system is trained on MedVL-CT69K (69,086 patients, 272,124 CT scans), the largest CT vision-language dataset to date.

## Results

On the in-house MedVL-CT69K dataset (54 diseases, 15 anatomies), fVLM achieves a zero-shot average AUC of 81.3% and ACC of 76.2%, surpassing the best zero-shot competitor Merlin (71.9% AUC) by 9.4 points and the vanilla CLIP baseline (68.4% AUC) by 12.9 points. On the public CT-RATE benchmark, fVLM attains 77.8% AUC in zero-shot evaluation, exceeding CT-CLIP by 7.4 points; on Rad-ChestCT it reaches 68.0% AUC, a 4.8-point gain over CT-CLIP. Notably, fVLM's zero-shot AUC (81.3%) also exceeds the best supervised baseline CT-VocabFine (76.7%) by 4.6 points, and in the report-generation task it leads CLIP by 4.2 points on ACC (frozen) and 2.5 points (fine-tuned).

## Baselines & Comparisons

fVLM decisively outperforms all nine baselines on the primary MedVL-CT69K zero-shot evaluation. Against the vanilla CLIP architecture (68.4% AUC), fVLM gains 12.9 AUC points, confirming that anatomy-level fine-grained alignment substantially improves over global image-report contrast. The strongest zero-shot competitors—Merlin (71.9%) and BIUD (71.4%)—are surpassed by 9.4 and 9.9 AUC points respectively, while implicit local-alignment methods (LOVT 69.4%, MGCA 70.1%) show only marginal gains over CLIP, underscoring their limited applicability to 3D CT. On the public CT-RATE benchmark, fVLM (77.8% AUC) beats CT-CLIP (70.4%) by 7.4 points and even exceeds the supervised CT-VocabFine (75.0%) and CT-LiPro (75.1%) fine-tuned models; on Rad-ChestCT the 4.8-point AUC advantage over CT-CLIP (68.0% vs 63.2%) demonstrates external generalizability. In the report-generation task, fVLM leads CLIP by 4.2 ACC points in the frozen-encoder setting and 2.5 points after fine-tuning, while also outperforming Merlin and BIUD across all NLP metrics (BLEU, ROUGE-L, CIDEr, GREEN). The supervised baselines (CT-VocabFine, CT-LiPro) remain competitive on F1 but are outperformed by fVLM's zero-shot AUC by 4.6 and 5.2 points, highlighting the annotation-free advantage of the proposed approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP-style contrastive vision-language pre-training to 3D CT by replacing global image-report alignment with explicit anatomy-level matching, building on prior medical VLP work (BIUD, CT-CLIP, Merlin) that contrasts entire volumes with full reports. It departs from implicit local alignment methods (GLoRIA, LoVT, MGCA) that use cross-attention to learn fine-grained correspondences, arguing that such implicit approaches are intractable for the far greater anatomical complexity of 3D CT versus 2D chest X-rays. The false-negative reduction module and co-teaching label correction represent a novel loss-level contribution that addresses a problem specific to fine-grained medical contrastive learning, where anatomy-level normality inflates semantically similar negatives far beyond what global contrastive learning encounters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MedVL-CT69K dataset (69K patients, 272K scans) is the largest CT vision-language resource to date and will likely become a standard benchmark for the field. The finding that implicit local alignment provides only marginal gains over CLIP in 3D CT—while explicit anatomy-level matching yields a 12.9% AUC improvement—challenges the assumption that cross-attention-based fine-grained alignment generalizes from 2D to 3D medical imaging. Open questions include whether the heavy preprocessing pipeline (TotalSegmentator + LLM-based report decomposition) can be made more efficient, whether anatomy-wise report generation (flagged as future work) can fully exploit the fine-grained representations, and whether the co-teaching strategy generalizes to other modalities or fine-grained contrastive settings beyond CT.
