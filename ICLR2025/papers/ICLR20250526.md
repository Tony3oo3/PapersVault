## Problem

Standard neural networks produce overconfident predictions on unfamiliar or adversarial inputs, and existing Bayesian or ensemble uncertainty methods either require prior specification, incur high computational cost, or discard information through model averaging. The core gap is that classical probability vectors cannot represent epistemic uncertainty—the uncertainty about the probability distribution itself—arising from limited or unrepresentative training data, because they are forced to commit to a single point in the probability simplex.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250526/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images and produces a belief function over a budget of class sets (N singleton classes plus K non-singleton focal sets), rather than a single softmax probability vector. From this belief function, a pignistic probability vector (the centre of mass of the associated credal set) yields the pointwise class prediction, while pignistic entropy and credal set width (the gap between upper and lower probability bounds for the most likely class) serve as complementary uncertainty scores. The ground-truth encoding is multi-label: a training sample with true class c has 1s in every focal set containing c and 0s elsewhere, making the output layer a sigmoid-activated multi-label head.

## Method

RS-NN is a wrapper that replaces the final classification layer of any backbone (ResNet50, WideResNet, ViT, etc.) with a sigmoid head whose outputs correspond to a budget O of class sets. The budget is constructed in a one-time pre-processing step: class features from a pretrained ResNet50 are reduced to 3D via t-SNE, a Gaussian Mixture Model is fitted, and the top K non-singleton sets with the highest ellipse-overlap ratio are selected (K=20 for 10-class datasets, 3000 for ImageNet). Training uses Binary Cross-Entropy over the belief-encoded ground truth plus two regularisation terms (mass non-negativity and mass sum-to-one) to enforce valid belief functions via Möbius inversion. At inference, masses are recovered from predicted beliefs, redistributed to individual classes to form the pignistic probability, and the width of the resulting credal set quantifies epistemic uncertainty without requiring sampling or prior specification.

## Results

RS-NN achieves the highest test accuracy across all five classification benchmarks: 99.71% on MNIST, 93.53% on CIFAR-10, 71.61% on CIFAR-100, and 79.92% Top-1 on ImageNet, while matching the fastest inference time (1.91 ms/sample) of a standard CNN. On out-of-distribution detection, RS-NN attains the best AUROC scores (e.g., 94.91 on CIFAR-10 vs SVHN, 60.38 on ImageNet vs ImageNet-O) and the lowest Expected Calibration Error (0.0484 on CIFAR-10, 0.0029 on MNIST). Inference time is 1.91 ms for RS-NN versus 7.11 ms (LB-BNN), 340 ms (FSVI), and 13,164 ms (Deep Ensembles).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS-NN outperforms all four uncertainty-estimation baselines on every dataset in terms of test accuracy. Against the Bayesian methods, the margins are substantial: RS-NN beats LB-BNN by 3.6 pp on CIFAR-10 (93.53 vs 89.95), 11.7 pp on CIFAR-100 (71.61 vs 59.89), and 7.4 pp on ImageNet Top-1 (79.92 vs 72.48); the gap over FSVI is even wider (13.2 pp on CIFAR-10, 18.3 pp on CIFAR-100, 17.4 pp on ImageNet). Against the ensemble baselines, RS-NN edges out Deep Ensembles (5 models) by 0.8 pp on CIFAR-10 and 1.2 pp on ImageNet Top-1, while being ~6,900× faster at inference (1.91 ms vs 13,164 ms); it surpasses ENN (3 models) by 2.0 pp on CIFAR-10 and 8.1 pp on ImageNet Top-1. On OoD detection (AUROC/AUPRC), RS-NN leads all baselines on every iD-vs-OoD pair, with the largest advantage on ImageNet vs ImageNet-O (AUROC 60.38 vs 55.37 for DE and 54.28 for CNN). RS-NN also achieves the lowest ECE across all datasets, indicating better-calibrated predictions than any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Dempster-Shafer belief function framework (Shafer, 1976) and its random-set generalisation (Molchanov, 2005) as the output representation of a deep neural network, replacing the standard softmax probability vector with a mass assignment over sets of classes. The pignistic transformation (Smets & Kennes, 1994) and credal set geometry (Levi, 1980) are inherited directly from the evidence-theory literature to extract point predictions and quantify epistemic uncertainty, respectively. Conceptually, RS-NN positions itself between Bayesian neural networks (which require prior specification and inference-time sampling) and deep ensembles (which require training multiple models), offering a single-model, prior-free alternative that avoids the information loss of Bayesian Model Averaging. A key departure from prior belief-function classifiers (e.g., Denoeux, 2000; Tong et al., 2021) is the budgeting mechanism that selects a tractable subset of focal sets via GMM clustering, making the approach scalable to 1000-class ImageNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RS-NN provides a practical, architecture-agnostic wrapper for epistemic uncertainty that matches or exceeds the inference speed of a standard CNN while outperforming both Bayesian and ensemble baselines on accuracy, OoD detection, and calibration across five datasets and five large-scale architectures. The ability to plug conformal prediction onto pignistic outputs for distribution-free statistical guarantees is a notable practical advantage for safety-critical deployment. Open questions include whether the manually-set budget size K can be made adaptive, whether the t-SNE/GMM budgeting step generalises to streaming or multi-source data, and whether a parameter-level (rather than output-level) belief function representation is feasible. The paper also notes that the mass regularisation terms (α, β) are a soft constraint, and exploring hard-constraint alternatives (e.g., semantic probabilistic layers) remains future work.
