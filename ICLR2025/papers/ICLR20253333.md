## Problem

State space models (SSMs) exhibit an implicit frequency bias—learning low-frequency components faster than high-frequency ones—but this bias has never been formally characterized for SSMs, nor has it been understood why conventional training fails to alter it. The paper asks: what determines the frequency bias of an SSM, why is it locked in during training, and can it be deliberately tuned to suit a given task?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253333/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a single SISO LTI system Γ=(A,B,C,D) within a larger SSM, where the transfer function G(is) maps input Fourier coefficients to output Fourier coefficients. In experiments, images (CelebA at 1024×256 pixels) are flattened in row-major order so that horizontal stripe noise becomes low-frequency and vertical stripe noise becomes high-frequency; video frames (Moving MNIST) are treated as temporal sequences. The output is always the same-length sequence as the input (denoised pixels, predicted frames, or classification logits), and the skip connection D is removed in denoising experiments to isolate the LTI system's frequency-selective behavior.

## Method

The paper formalizes frequency bias as the total variation of the real-valued transfer function G̃(is) over frequency intervals, showing that HiPPO initialization places eigenvalues of A in the low-frequency region, bounding high-frequency variation as O(1/B) (Corollary 1). It then proves via functional derivatives that the gradient of the loss with respect to each imaginary eigenvalue w_j decays as O(|s|^{-2}) at high frequencies (Theorem 1), explaining why w_j gets trapped in low-frequency local minima and training cannot overcome the innate bias. Two tuning mechanisms are proposed: (1) a 'hard' strategy that scales the HiPPO initialization by a factor α to shift eigenvalues into higher-frequency regions, with scaling laws derived from the bilinear transform to avoid aliasing (Proposition 1); and (2) a 'soft' strategy that multiplies the transfer function by a Sobolev filter (1+|s|)^β, which changes the gradient decay to O(|s|^{-2+β}) (Theorem 2), making w_j sensitive to high-frequency losses and enabling the model to learn or suppress high frequencies during training. Together, α controls which frequencies are learnable while β controls how much weight each frequency receives, and the two are shown to be complementary and interactively tunable.

## Results

On the Long-Range Arena (LRA) benchmark, the S4D model equipped with both tuning strategies (scaled HiPPO initialization and Sobolev-norm-based filter) achieves an average accuracy of 88.26%, the best among all compared SSM variants. It attains 62.75% on ListOps, 89.76% on Text, 92.45% on Retrieval, 90.89% on Image, 95.89% on Pathfinder, and 97.84% on Path-X. In the image-denoising experiment on CelebA, increasing α and β systematically reduces the ratio of low-frequency to high-frequency noise pass rates from 4.463e+07 (α=0.1, β=-1) down to 5.963e-06 (α=100, β=1), demonstrating that the frequency bias can be strengthened, weakened, or even reversed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed S4D with frequency-bias tuning achieves the best average LRA accuracy (88.26%), outperforming S5 (87.46%), Liquid S4 (87.32%), Reg. S4D (86.60%), S4 (86.09%), and the vanilla S4D (84.89%). On five of six LRA tasks (ListOps, Text, Retrieval, Image, Pathfinder), the method achieves the top score; it trails S5 only on Path-X (97.84% vs. 98.58%). Compared to the closest competitor S5, the gains are +0.60 on Text, +1.05 on Retrieval, +2.89 on Image, and +0.56 on Pathfinder, while matching S5 on ListOps (62.75%). Against the vanilla S4D baseline, the tuning strategies yield substantial improvements of +2.28 on ListOps, +3.58 on Text, +2.99 on Retrieval, +2.70 on Image, +2.83 on Pathfinder, and +5.89 on Path-X. The image-denoising experiment further shows that the tuning mechanisms allow continuous control over frequency selectivity, with the low/high pass-rate ratio spanning over 13 orders of magnitude across the (α, β) grid.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral bias literature from general neural networks (Rahaman et al., 2019) to state space models by formalizing frequency bias through the total variation of the LTI transfer function and connecting it to the spectrum of the state matrix A. It builds directly on the HiPPO initialization framework (Gu et al., 2020) by proposing a scaled variant, and adapts the Sobolev-norm-based training idea from Czarnecki et al. (2017) and Yu et al. (2023) to the SSM setting. A key departure from prior SSM training analyses (Liu & Li, 2024b; Smekal et al., 2024) is that this paper treats the state matrix A as trainable rather than fixed, enabling the first theoretical characterization of how training dynamics interact with the frequency bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the imaginary parts of diag(A) are essentially frozen during conventional training (1.43% relative change vs. 1002% for the real parts) reveals that the frequency bias is locked in at initialization and cannot be overcome by standard gradient descent — a non-trivial insight for SSM practitioners. The two tuning mechanisms (scaling α and Sobolev filter β) are simple, interpretable, and composable, and they enable a basic S4D to reach 88.26% on LRA, matching or exceeding more complex architectures like S5 and Liquid S4. Open questions include how to automatically select α and β from input spectral properties, whether the analysis extends to non-diagonal or structured A matrices, and whether the Sobolev filter interacts well with selective (input-dependent) SSM variants like Mamba.
