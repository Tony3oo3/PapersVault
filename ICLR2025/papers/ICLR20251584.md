## Problem

Despite extensive safety alignment efforts for LLMs, the specific mechanisms underlying safety remain poorly understood at a fine-grained level. Prior interpretability work attributes roughly 10% of parameters to safety-related functions, a proportion too large to enable targeted, efficient safety tuning. This paper asks whether a much smaller, precisely identifiable set of neurons is responsible for safety, and whether tuning only those neurons can substantially improve safety without degrading general capabilities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (instruction-tuned or base), a corpus of 200 harmful queries drawn from Zou et al. (2024) for neuron detection, and 50 safety training documents (harmful query–refusal pairs) for tuning. It produces two outputs: a set of identified safety neurons (less than 1% of all parameters, predominantly in self-attention layers of the first ~10–16 layers) and a safety-enhanced model whose harmful score drops from ~65–100 to 2–14 on average across attack methods. The neuron importance is measured as the L2 norm of the intermediate representation change upon deactivating a single neuron, with a fixed threshold ε determining activation.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline: (1) Safety Neuron Detection, which identifies neurons whose deactivation consistently shifts the intermediate representation beyond a threshold across all harmful queries in a corpus, using parallel mask-based computation for FFN layers and an attention-score perturbation approximation for self-attention layers to avoid sequential per-neuron evaluation; and (2) SN-Tune, which fine-tunes exclusively the identified safety neurons (zeroing gradients of all other parameters) on a small safety corpus for one epoch at lr=1e-6, reducing harmful scores by 90%+ while preserving or slightly improving MMLU/GSM8K/ARC accuracy. A variant, RSN-Tune, further excludes safety neurons that overlap with 'foundation neurons' (detected via a Wikipedia corpus) to prevent safety degradation during downstream task fine-tuning. Key empirical findings include that safety neurons are language-specific with <30% cross-lingual overlap, reside predominantly in self-attention (77% in Llama2-7B-Chat), and are concentrated in the first several layers where deactivating them causes near-complete safety breakdown.

## Results

SN-Tune reduces the average harmful score (ASR across Direct, GCG, AutoDAN, PAIR attacks) of instruction-tuned models from 93.5 to 3.0 (Vicuna-13B-v1.5), 65.5 to 2.0 (Llama3-8B-Instruction), and 70.8 to 4.5 (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2), while preserving or slightly improving general capability (e.g., MMLU 65.2→67.3, GSM8K 63.2→69.6 on Llama3-8B-Instruction). On base models, SN-Tune reduces harmful scores from ~100 to 5.3 (Llama2-7B-Base), 13.5 (Llama3-8B-Base), and 13.8 (Mistral-7B-v0.1) in just 2 minutes of training with zero additional parameters. RSN-Tune further improves safety robustness during downstream fine-tuning, reducing Llama2-7B-Chat's post-GSM8K harmful score from 41.0 to 26.0 and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2's from 79.0 to 41.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Circ-Break (Zou et al., 2024), which trains an independent circuit-breaker model to intercept harmful outputs. SN-Tune outperforms Circ-Break on all three instruction-tuned models in average harmful score: 3.0 vs. 5.3 (Vicuna-13B), 2.0 vs. 3.0 (Llama3-8B), and 4.5 vs. 10.0 (Mistral-7B). SN-Tune also matches or slightly exceeds Circ-Break on general capability (e.g., 50.7 vs. 49.7 on Vicuna; 59.0 vs. 56.9 on Mistral). Critically, SN-Tune requires 10–20× less training time (2–4 min vs. 23–43 min) and adds zero extra parameters, whereas Circ-Break introduces 27.5–34.1M additional parameters. On base models (Table 3), the gap widens further: Circ-Break fails to establish meaningful safety (harmful scores remain 87.5–92.5), while SN-Tune achieves 5.3–13.8, making it the only viable option for base-model safety in this comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neuron-level interpretability framework from Zhao et al. (2024b) on multilingual processing to the safety domain, repurposing the parallel neuron detection method and the concept of foundation neurons. It departs from prior safety interpretability work (Li et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2024b) by operating at finer granularity (individual neurons vs. layers or features) and identifying a far smaller parameter subset (<1% vs. ~10%) as responsible for safety. Unlike prior safety alignment approaches that modify optimization, data, or add external structures (Circuit Breaker, Llama Guard), SN-Tune directly tunes the identified safety neurons in-place, while RSN-Tune further builds on the foundation-neuron concept to protect safety during downstream fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extreme sparsity finding (<1% of parameters, concentrated in early self-attention layers) provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why safety alignment is so fragile to minor parameter perturbations, and offers a practical, extremely efficient tuning recipe (2 minutes, 50 documents) that could be widely adopted for rapid safety patching. The language-specificity of safety neurons (<30% cross-lingual overlap) has direct implications for multilingual deployment, suggesting that per-language safety tuning is necessary rather than optional. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 7B–13B transformer models, a threshold-based neuron selection that may miss context-dependent safety circuits, and RSN-Tune's inability to fully restore safety (reducing harmful score from 41 to 26 rather than to 0), leaving open whether a more complete separation of safety from foundation neurons is achievable.
