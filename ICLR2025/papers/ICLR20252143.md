## Problem

Vanilla TopK routers in Mixture-of-Experts models introduce a discrete, non-differentiable training objective that limits both performance and scalability. Existing fully-differentiable MoE alternatives (Soft MoE, SMEAR, Lory) either break token causality or underperform standard TopK routing, leaving a gap for a differentiable router that matches or exceeds TopK quality while preserving autoregressive compatibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252143/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a token sequence of length 1024 (batch size 512) and produces next-token probabilities for self-supervised language modeling. Unlike TopK routing which assigns each token to exactly k experts with softmax-normalized weights, ReMoE's ReLU router outputs non-negative per-expert weights where zeros indicate inactive experts, allowing the number of active experts to vary dynamically per token and per layer while maintaining an average of k active experts across the batch.

## Method

ReMoE replaces the TopK(Softmax(xW), k) routing with a simple ReLU(xW) gate, making the compute flow fully differentiable by eliminating the jump discontinuity at the k-th order statistic. Sparsity is controlled via an adaptive L1 regularization whose coefficient λ_i is updated by a zeroth-order rule that increases λ when average sparsity falls below the target (1−k/E) and decreases it otherwise. A load-balancing refinement multiplies each expert's L1 penalty by its activation ratio f_{l,e}, penalizing over-utilized experts and integrating load balancing into the same regularization term. Training naturally progresses through three stages—dense warm-up, sparsifying transition, and stable sparse operation—requiring only ~100 extra iterations of near-dense computation. The architecture is a drop-in replacement within LLaMA-style transformers (GQA, SwiGLU, RoPE, RMSNorm) implemented in Megatron-LM, supporting all standard parallelism strategies.

## Results

On the primary comparison (N=182M, E=8, k=1, trained on 30B tokens of The Pile), ReMoE achieves 40.03% average zero-shot accuracy across six downstream tasks (ARC-c, ARC-e, BoolQ, HellaSwag, LAMBADA, PIQA, RACE), outperforming the strongest TopK baseline dMoE (39.67%) and the dense model (38.20%). In scalability experiments, ReMoE consistently beats TopK-routed MoE across active parameters (182M–978M), expert counts (4–128), and granularity levels (1–64), with the performance gap widening as E increases. Fine-grained ReMoE at G=32 and G=64 reaches the theoretical upper bound of Dense×8 (41.70% avg) while using far fewer FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMoE outperforms all five baselines on average zero-shot accuracy. Against the strongest TopK baseline dMoE (Gale et al., 2023), ReMoE gains +0.36 points (40.03 vs. 39.67), with the largest per-task advantage on LAMBADA (+2.97) and RACE (+1.05). Against the fully-differentiable Lory (Zhong et al., 2024), ReMoE leads by a substantial +2.33 points (40.03 vs. 37.70), confirming that Lory's expert-merging approach, while differentiable, underperforms even standard TopK routing. ReMoE also beats SparseMixer-v2 (Liu et al., 2024) by +1.64 points and expert-choice routing (Zhou et al., 2022) by +1.50 points. In the scalability study (Section 4.3), ReMoE surpasses dMoE across every configuration of N, E, and G, with the gap growing as E increases from 4 to 128 (e.g., at E=128: 42.12 vs. 41.10 avg), indicating steeper scaling with expert count.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReMoE directly extends the sparsely-gated MoE framework of Shazeer et al. (2017) by replacing the discontinuous TopK+Softmax routing with a ReLU gate, a reformulation that aligns all expert-activation breakpoints at zero to restore full differentiability. The load-balancing mechanism is adapted from the auxiliary loss in Switch Transformers (Fedus et al., 2022), re-expressed as a load-balancing-refined L1 regularization with an adaptive coefficient. Unlike prior differentiable MoE approaches (Soft MoE, SMEAR, Lory) that resort to token or expert merging and either break autoregressive causality or underperform TopK routing, ReMoE preserves the standard per-token expert-selection structure while achieving differentiability through a minimal architectural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a one-line routing change (ReLU instead of TopK+Softmax) consistently outperforms all existing routing methods across model sizes, expert counts, and granularity levels suggests that the field's pursuit of complex differentiable MoE formulations may have been over-engineered. The steeper scaling with expert count E and the emergent Huffman-tree-like dynamic allocation open questions about whether ReLU routing can unlock further gains at the 10B+ parameter scale where current experiments stop. Limitations include evaluation only on LLaMA-architecture models up to ~1B active parameters trained on The Pile, leaving open whether the advantage persists at commercial model scales, with different architectures, or on more diverse training corpora.
