## Problem

Spectral compressive imaging (SCI) reconstruction from a single CASSI measurement is inherently ill-posed, and deterministic methods struggle to recover high-frequency texture details. While diffusion models offer generative capacity to fill in missing details, they cannot be directly applied to multispectral images (MSIs) due to severe data scarcity, the high dimensionality of spectral bands, and the channel mismatch between pre-trained RGB diffusion models and multi-band MSIs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250093/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D CASSI compressed measurement of size H×(W+d(B−1)) along with the known measurement matrix Φ, and produces a full 3D multispectral image of size H×W×B (256×256 spatial, 28 spectral bands in experiments). The pipeline is two-stage: a lightweight predictor (a pre-trained fast transformer or 3-stage deep unfolding network) first generates a rough MSI estimate, which is then refined by the diffusion module. The initial predictor runs only once, offloading most computational work and leaving the diffusion model to focus exclusively on high-frequency texture residuals.

## Method

PSR-SCI introduces a predict-and-subspace-refine pipeline with three key mechanisms. First, a frequency separator (iterative Gaussian low-pass filtering) splits the initial MSI estimate into a PSNR-critical low-frequency component and a sparse high-frequency texture component, so the diffusion model only needs to model the residual. Second, a Unmixing-driven Reversible Spectral Embedding (URSe) module performs hierarchical low-rank decomposition of the high-frequency MSI into a low-dimensional abundance map A and spectral coefficients E, making the data compatible with pre-trained 3-channel RGB diffusion models while preserving reversibility (PSNR 47.39 dB on inverse reconstruction). Third, Stable Diffusion 2.1-base is fine-tuned via an IRControlNet-style parallel encoder on the CAVE dataset to generate the high-frequency subspace image, with a high-dimensional guidance mechanism that back-propagates through the reversible embedding ψ⁻¹ to enforce consistency with both the initial prediction and the original CASSI measurement at every sampling step. The final MSI is recovered by reversing the spectral embedding on the diffusion-refined abundance map and adding back the untouched low-frequency component.

## Results

On the KAIST dataset (10 scenes, 256×256, 28 bands), PSR-SCI-D achieves an average PSNR of 38.14 dB and SSIM of 0.967, outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline DiffSCI (35.28 dB / 0.916) by 2.86 dB and the best deep-unfolding method DAUHST-SP2 (37.61 dB / 0.966) by 0.53 dB. On zero-shot datasets, PSR-SCI-D leads in PSNR on ICVL (37.03), NTIRE (36.44), and Harvard (26.90), while PSR-SCI-SR further improves to 37.25, 36.62, and 28.58 respectively. Inference time for 50 diffusion steps is 8.90 s, roughly 9.5× faster than DiffSCI (84.54 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSR-SCI-D (38.14 dB) surpasses the strongest non-diffusion baseline DAUHST-SP2 (37.61 dB) by 0.53 dB and DAUHST-3stg (37.21 dB) by 0.93 dB, while also outperforming all transformer-based methods (CST-L+ 35.71, MST++ 35.72, MST-L 34.81, HDNet 34.66) by 2.4–3.5 dB. Against the closest diffusion competitor DiffSCI (35.28 dB / 0.916 SSIM), PSR-SCI-D gains 2.86 dB in PSNR and 0.051 in SSIM, and is ~9.5× faster at 50 sampling steps (8.90 s vs. 84.54 s). On zero-shot datasets (ICVL, NTIRE, Harvard), PSR-SCI-D achieves the highest PSNR among all tested methods, beating DPU-9stg (36.56/36.25/27.05) and LADE-10stg (35.89/35.58/28.02) on ICVL/NTIRE/Harvard respectively, confirming stronger generalization. The ablation (Table 3) shows that removing the initial predictor drops PSNR from 38.14 to 33.42 dB, and removing the URSe spectral embedding increases inference time from 12.9 s to 193.2 s, underscoring the contribution of each component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based SCI reconstruction line initiated by DiffSCI (Pan et al., CVPR 2024) by introducing a predict-and-subspace-refine strategy that avoids applying diffusion directly to the full high-dimensional MSI. It builds on Stable Diffusion 2.1 (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as the generative backbone and the IRControlNet architecture (Lin et al., 2023) for conditioning, but departs from both by restricting diffusion to a low-dimensional spectral subspace (via a learnable unmixing-driven reversible embedding) and to high-frequency residuals only. The high-dimensional guidance mechanism, which enforces CASSI measurement consistency during sampling, is a novel contribution not present in prior diffusion-for-SCI work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses two practical bottlenecks in diffusion-based SCI: the scarcity of MSI training data (solved by fine-tuning RGB pre-trained models in a compressed subspace) and the prohibitive inference cost of multi-band diffusion (reduced ~10× over DiffSCI via subspace sampling). The zero-shot generalization results on ICVL, NTIRE, and Harvard suggest that the generative prior is more robust to distribution shift than deterministic E2E or deep-unfolding methods, which overfit to training-domain statistics. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher band counts (beyond 28), whether faster samplers (e.g., DDIM, consistency models) can further reduce the 8.9s/50-step cost, and whether the initial predictor dependency can be relaxed for truly single-network deployment.
