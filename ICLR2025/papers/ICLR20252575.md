## Problem

Neural pruning requires identifying optimal subnetworks under a sparsity constraint, which is a high-dimensional zero-one programming problem. Prior work (Mo et al., 2023) approximates the trajectory of a single optimal subnetwork as sparsity increases, but fails to capture the full population of optimal subnetworks and assumes a global optimum always exists near any given optimum. This paper asks how the entire population of optimal subnetworks evolves under continuous sparsity increments, and whether this evolution can be tracked to avoid solving the intractable target-sparsity problem from scratch.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained dense network θ*, a target sparsity level (equivalently a parameter budget d'), and mini-batches from a training dataset for stochastic gradient estimation. It outputs a binary mask with exactly d' non-zero entries, which can be applied at per-parameter (unstructured) or per-channel/filter/node (structured) granularity. The mask is obtained by averaging n simulated particle trajectories and selecting the top-d' entries, making the output a single deterministic mask rather than a distribution.

## Method

The paper reformulates pruning as expected loss minimization over mask distributions via probabilistic lifting with an RBF kernel regularizer, then derives the Sparsity Evolutionary Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov Equation (SFPK): a closed-form distributional ODE ∂_t π_t = −∇·[T(·;π_t)π_t] that approximates how the optimal mask distribution shifts under an infinitesimal sparsity increment. The transition operator T(m;π_t) decomposes into a sparsity-gradient component (pushing toward higher sparsity) and a projected energy-gradient component (minimizing loss orthogonal to the sparsity direction), scaled by a localization radius. SFPK-pruner simulates the associated McKean-Vlasov process with n interacting mask particles, each updated by T using mini-batch stochastic gradients that simultaneously provide the energy signal and act as a repulsive exploration force. The final mask is the top-d' selection from the element-wise average of all n particle trajectories at the target sparsity.

## Results

In one-shot unstructured pruning without retraining on ImageNet-1K, SFPK-pruner consistently outperforms all baselines across sparsity levels. At 90% sparsity, SFPK achieves 55.48% top-1 accuracy on ResNet-50 (vs. 17.48% for WoodFisher, 49.08% for PSO) and 44.06% on MobileNet-V1 (vs. 12.56% for WoodFisher, 20.53% for PSO). In gradual pruning on ResNet-50 (10 prune-and-retrain loops), SFPK achieves 76.18% at 80% sparsity and 75.24% at 90% sparsity, competitive with WoodFisher (76.76% and 75.21% respectively). On DeiT-T structured channel pruning, SFPK attains 71.6% top-1 accuracy at 51.7% FLOPs, the best among all compared transformer pruning methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFPK-pruner decisively outperforms all baselines in one-shot pruning without retraining on ImageNet-1K: at 90% sparsity it leads WoodFisher by 38.0 points on ResNet-50 (55.48% vs. 17.48%) and by 31.5 points on MobileNet-V1 (44.06% vs. 12.56%), and surpasses PSO by 6.4 and 23.5 points respectively. In gradual pruning on ResNet-50, SFPK is competitive with WoodFisher (76.18% vs. 76.76% at 80%; 75.24% vs. 75.21% at 90%) and outperforms STR, PSO, DNW, and CrAM at both sparsity levels. On MobileNet-V1 gradual pruning, SFPK achieves the best accuracy at 90% sparsity (64.10%) among all methods, edging out WF (63.87%) and PSO (63.64%). For DeiT-T structured channel pruning, SFPK achieves the highest top-1 accuracy (71.6%) among all transformer pruning baselines including X-Pruner (71.1%), UVC (70.6%), and WDPruning (70.3%). Score-based methods (SNIP, SynFlow, GraSP) and magnitude pruning collapse to near-chance accuracy at high sparsity without retraining, while SFPK maintains strong performance due to its distributional evolution framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-trajectory sparsity-indexed ODE framework of Mo et al. (2023, PSO) from tracking one optimal subnetwork to modeling the full population of optimal subnetworks as a probability distribution evolving under continuous sparsity increments. It draws on the probabilistic lifting and convexification technique of Wild et al. (2023) to reformulate pruning as expected-loss minimization over mask distributions, and borrows the Fokker-Planck-Kolmogorov equation structure from thermodynamics (Bogachev et al., 2015) to derive a closed-form distributional transition operator. Unlike Bayesian pruning methods (Louizos et al., Molchanov et al.) that estimate a single posterior over parameters, SFPK tracks a sequence of marginal distributions at progressively increasing sparsity levels via a McKean-Vlasov particle simulation, avoiding the instability of soft sparsity penalties and the computational cost of joint parameter-mask optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SFPK framework provides the first principled, closed-form description of how the population of optimal subnetworks evolves as sparsity increases, bridging a gap between discrete zero-one pruning and continuous distributional dynamics. Practically, SFPK-pruner achieves competitive one-shot and gradual pruning results with modest overhead (~0.3 epochs per pruning shot), and its model-agnostic particle simulation applies uniformly to structured and unstructured settings. Key limitations include the requirement that the target model be 'rational' (well-trained, so the dense mask is near-optimal across sparsity levels), and scalability to very large models remains open. The theoretical convergence guarantee (Proposition 3) and the thermodynamic interpretation offer a foundation for future work on adaptive sparsity schedules and multi-objective pruning.
