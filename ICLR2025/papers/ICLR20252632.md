## Problem

In extremely sparse-reward RL environments, existing reward shaping methods face a fundamental tradeoff: intrinsic-motivation approaches (e.g., RND, count-based exploration) encourage broad exploration but ignore state values, leading to the 'noisy TV' problem, while inherent value-based methods provide directional guidance but struggle during early exploration. No prior method autonomously transitions between these two regimes or does so without requiring additional neural networks for reward estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors (ranging from 2-D MountainCar to 7056-D Atari frames) along with historical trajectories classified as success or failure, stored in two buffers with a configurable retention rate φ (default 0.1) to skip redundant adjacent states. The shaped reward R^S(s) is computed in real-time at each update step using the most recently updated success/failure counts, then added to the environmental reward scaled by λ (default 0.6) before being fed into the SAC Q-network loss. Unlike methods that pre-compute or store shaped rewards, SASR recomputes them dynamically so the reward signal reflects the latest learning progress.

## Method

SASR defines a per-state success rate as the ratio of its appearances in successful trajectories to total occurrences, then models this rate as a Beta(α, β) distribution with α = N_S + 1 and β = N_F + 1, sampling from it (Thompson-sampling-inspired) to produce the shaped reward. As training progresses, increasing counts sharpen the Beta distribution, automatically shifting from high-variance stochastic rewards (exploration) to low-variance reliable rewards (exploitation) without any manual schedule. To estimate N_S and N_F in continuous high-dimensional spaces, the method applies Kernel Density Estimation with a Gaussian kernel, accelerated by Random Fourier Features that reduce the kernel computation to an inner product of M-dimensional randomized cosine features (M=1000 default), yielding O(MDB) per-iteration complexity with no additional trainable networks. The entire mechanism is integrated into SAC's off-policy training loop, where the shaped reward is computed on-the-fly during each gradient update rather than stored in the replay buffer.

## Results

SASR is evaluated on 14 extremely sparse-reward tasks (MuJoCo, Gymnasium Robotics, Atari, and MountainCar) using average episodic return over 100 episodes as the primary metric. SASR achieves the best or near-best return on 12 out of 14 tasks, with particularly large margins on Solaris (42.1 vs. next-best 26.3), AntFar (139.8 vs. 115.7), and RobotPush (167.1 vs. 166.9). The two exceptions are RobotReach, where ReLara leads (187.9 vs. 170.2) due to access to action information, and Montezuma's Revenge, where DRND-online edges ahead (6828.5 vs. 6737.9). Across all tasks, SASR substantially outperforms the unshaped RL baselines (SAC, TD3, PPO), which often fail to reach the goal at all (e.g., TD3 scores 0.0 on RobotReach, RobotPush, and Montezuma).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SASR outperforms all seven reward-shaping baselines on the majority of tasks. Against DRND-online, SASR leads on 12/14 tasks (e.g., AntStand 94.9 vs. 67.3, Solaris 42.1 vs. 21.3) but trails slightly on Montezuma's Revenge (6737.9 vs. 6828.5). Against ReLara, SASR wins on 11/14 tasks with notable margins on AntFar (139.8 vs. 115.7) and Solaris (42.1 vs. 20.3), but loses on RobotReach (170.2 vs. 187.9) where ReLara's access to action information provides an advantage. Against GFA-RFE, ROSA, ExploRS, #Explo, and RND, SASR achieves substantially higher returns on nearly all tasks, with the largest gaps on Solaris (42.1 vs. 26.3 for GFA-RFE) and RobotPush (167.1 vs. 0.0 for ROSA/ExploRS/RND). Against the unshaped RL baselines (SAC, TD3, PPO), SASR dominates: TD3 and PPO score 0.0 on RobotReach, RobotPush, and Montezuma, while SAC reaches at most 156.5 on RobotReach versus SASR's 170.2. The paper attributes SASR's advantage to its self-adaptive Beta-distribution mechanism, which provides stochastic rewards early (encouraging exploration) and deterministic rewards later (enhancing exploitation), unlike novelty-only methods that ignore state value or value-only methods that lack early directional guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

SASR carves a middle path between two established reward-shaping branches: intrinsic-motivation methods (RND, #Explo, curiosity-driven) that reward novelty but ignore state value, and inherent-value methods (ReLara, ROSA, ExploRS) that provide task-aligned guidance but struggle in early exploration. The self-adaptive Beta-distribution mechanism repurposes Thompson sampling (originally for multi-armed bandits) into a reward-shaping context, while KDE with Random Fourier Features—borrowed from kernel-methods literature—provides a network-free way to estimate success/failure densities in continuous state spaces. Unlike prior autonomous shaping methods that require additional neural networks or auxiliary agents, SASR achieves its shaping purely through statistical estimation over stored trajectories, positioning it as a lightweight alternative to the heavier multi-agent or learned-reward approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for additional neural networks or auxiliary agents for reward shaping, reducing both computational overhead and implementation complexity while maintaining strong performance across 14 diverse sparse-reward tasks. The self-adaptive Beta-distribution mechanism offers a principled, parameter-light way to balance exploration and exploitation that naturally evolves with data accumulation, avoiding the hand-tuned schedules common in prior methods. Key limitations include sensitivity to the retention rate hyperparameter, the assumption that success/failure can be cleanly defined per trajectory, and the restriction to sparse-reward settings. Open questions include extending SASR to dense-reward environments, developing adaptive retention strategies, and incorporating action information into the state representation for tasks with stochastic targets.
