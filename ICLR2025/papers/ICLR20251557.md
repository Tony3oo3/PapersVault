## Problem

Estimating conditional average treatment effects (CATE) from observational data is essential for personalized medicine, but patient data is highly sensitive and subject to strict privacy regulations. Existing differential privacy (DP) methods for treatment effect estimation focus almost exclusively on the average treatment effect, and the few CATE-specific DP approaches are restricted to RCT data, binary outcomes, or non-model-agnostic base learners. No framework existed for Neyman-orthogonal CATE estimation under DP from observational data with arbitrary machine learning models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observational data (X, A, Y) split into two disjoint subsets: one for privately estimating nuisance functions (propensity score and outcome regression) and one for the second-stage CATE regression, along with a privacy budget (ε, δ). For finite queries, it outputs a d-dimensional vector of CATE estimates where d is known a priori; for functional queries, it releases the entire CATE function as a perturbed RKHS element that can be queried an unbounded number of times. The noise added is either independent Gaussian per query (finite) or a correlated Gaussian process sample (functional), calibrated to the model's gross-error sensitivity or RKHS norm respectively.

## Method

DP-CATE is an output perturbation framework that adds calibrated noise to the second-stage output of any Neyman-orthogonal CATE meta-learner (e.g., R-learner, DR-learner) while preserving quasi-oracle efficiency. The key insight is that the ξ-smooth sensitivity needed for DP calibration can be upper-bounded by the gross-error sensitivity of the second-stage model's influence function, making the calibration computable without infeasible sensitivity estimation over general function classes. For finite queries, Gaussian noise is scaled by γ(T,D)·5√(2ln(n)ln(2/δ))/(εn); for functional queries, a zero-centered Gaussian process with the same kernel as the RKHS regression is added, with its amplitude derived from a novel bound on the RKHS-norm difference between neighboring-dataset solutions (extending Hall et al., 2013). The framework is model-agnostic for nuisance estimation (any ML model) and requires only that the second stage be a smooth parametric model or a Gaussian kernel ridge regression.

## Results

The paper reports no single headline numeric metric, as the primary evaluation is qualitative: PEHE (precision in estimation of heterogeneous effects) decreases monotonically with increasing privacy budget ε and converges to the non-private learner's error across all settings. On synthetic Dataset 2 (30 confounders), both the finite-query variant (with KR and NN base learners) and the functional-query variant show this convergence pattern over 10 runs. On MIMIC-III (d=1,312 queries) and TCGA (d=2,659 queries), the loss in precision due to DP is described as 'fairly small' relative to the non-private learner, with larger prediction variance at smaller privacy budgets as expected.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly states that no suitable baselines exist for differentially private CATE estimation from observational data using meta-learners, so no head-to-head comparisons are made. The closest prior work (Niu et al., 2022) is restricted to explainable boosting machines and is not model-agnostic; other prior DP treatment-effect methods are limited to ATE estimation, RCT data, or binary outcomes. The paper's experiments therefore focus on demonstrating that DP-CATE's prediction error decreases with the privacy budget and converges to the non-private R-learner's performance, rather than on beating a competing private method. The authors note that their framework is the first to combine Neyman-orthogonality with differential privacy for CATE from observational data, which is why a direct baseline comparison is not possible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Neyman-orthogonal meta-learners for CATE estimation (R-learner, DR-learner) and differential privacy, drawing on the robust-statistics connection between DP and influence functions (Dwork & Lei, 2009; Avella-Medina, 2021) to calibrate output-perturbation noise via gross-error sensitivity. For the functional-query variant, it extends Hall et al. (2013) on DP for functional data by deriving an RKHS-norm sensitivity bound for kernel ridge regression under weighted losses. Prior DP-for-treatment-effects work has been limited to ATE estimation or to CATE under restrictive assumptions (RCT-only data, binary outcomes, or non-model-agnostic base learners), leaving a gap that this paper explicitly fills for observational data with arbitrary ML models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework combining Neyman-orthogonality, differential privacy, and observational-data CATE estimation, which is directly relevant to regulated medical settings (e.g., HIPAA) where patient-level data cannot be shared. The two-use-case design (finite queries vs. releasing the full CATE function) covers both research reporting and clinical decision-support deployment. Key limitations include the requirement for Gaussian kernel regression in the functional variant, the absence of any suitable baseline for empirical comparison, and the observation that the DR-learner's inverse-propensity weighting inflates the noise calibration substantially. Open questions flagged by the authors include quantifying the uncertainty added by privatization and understanding the fairness–privacy tradeoff in causal ML.
