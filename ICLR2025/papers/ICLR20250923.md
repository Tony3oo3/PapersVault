## Problem

Best-of-N (BoN) is an effective alignment-via-inference method that draws N samples from a language model and returns the highest-reward one, but it reduces inference throughput by a factor of N. The paper asks: can we fine-tune a language model to internalize the BoN distribution so that a single sample from the fine-tuned model approximates the BoN output, thereby recovering the N-fold throughput gain while preserving alignment quality?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250923/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the fine-tuned model takes a text prompt and produces a single generated string in one forward pass, with no additional sampling or reward-model calls. During training, the method additionally requires a reward model r: Σ* → ℝ that scores generated strings, and M Monte Carlo samples from the reference model to estimate the strict cumulative distribution function F of reward values. The reward model is a separate classifier (e.g., a sentiment classifier or a pythia-2.8B reward model) that is frozen throughout fine-tuning.

## Method

The paper first derives the exact probability distribution π_bon induced by the BoN algorithm (Proposition 1), showing that π_bon(y) = Σᵢ C(N,i) F(r(y))^(N−i) π_ref(y)^i, where F is the strict CDF of rewards under the reference model. It then proposes minimizing the reverse KL divergence D_KL(π_θ ∥ π_bon) as the training objective, analogous to mean-field variational inference, and calls this vBoN. A key theoretical result (Theorem 2) provides a lower bound L(θ) = (N−1) E[log F(r(y))] − D_KL(π_θ ∥ π_ref), revealing that N plays the role of a regularization parameter: N→1 recovers the reference model (like β→∞ in KL-constrained RL), while N→∞ concentrates on maximum-reward strings (like β→0). The objective is invariant to any monotonic transformation of reward values since it depends only on reward rankings through F. In practice, log F is estimated via M Monte Carlo samples from π_ref (200–250 samples suffice per their KS-test analysis), and the model is optimized with PPO, making the per-step compute cost comparable to standard PPO training.

## Results

On the IMDB sentiment control task, vBoN appears on the Pareto frontier of reward vs. KL divergence 50% of the time, compared to 42% for DPO, 33% for PPO, and 4% for BoNBoN. On the Reddit TL;DR summarization task, vBoN achieves the closest performance to exact BoN among all fine-tuning methods, with a reward of 6.79 vs. BoN's 9.18 and PPO's 4.59 on a representative example (Table 2). vBoN significantly outperforms PPO at lower sampling temperatures (0.25, 0.5) while matching PPO at higher temperatures (0.75, 1.0). The Monte Carlo estimator for log F stabilizes after ~200 samples, with zero rejections in a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test at M=250 across 5000 prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against exact BoN (Stiennon et al., 2020), vBoN achieves the closest approximation among all fine-tuning methods, appearing on the Pareto frontier 50% of the time in the sentiment task and tracking BoN's win-rate and reward curves most closely in summarization, while reducing inference cost by a factor of N. Against PPO (Christiano et al., 2017), vBoN significantly outperforms at lower sampling temperatures (0.25, 0.5) in the summarization task and appears more frequently on the Pareto frontier in sentiment control (50% vs. 33%). Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), vBoN achieves higher average rewards in both tasks, though DPO shows slightly better win rates in the sentiment task (42% vs. 50% Pareto frontier share, with DPO winning on win-rate but losing on reward). Against BoNBoN (Gui et al., 2024), vBoN dominates across temperatures in summarization, as BoNBoN's performance degrades sharply at higher temperatures (0.75, 1.0) while vBoN remains stable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes the distribution induced by the Best-of-N (BoN) alignment-via-inference algorithm (Stiennon et al., 2020) and proposes to approximate it via reverse KL minimization, drawing an explicit analogy to mean-field variational inference. It departs from prior fine-tuning-based alignment methods (RLHF/PPO, DPO) by optimizing a new objective derived from the BoN distribution rather than the KL-constrained RL objective, and from concurrent BoN-approximation work (BoNBoN, BOND) by using a principled variational lower bound rather than IPO-style contrastive losses or iterative distillation. The derived lower bound (Theorem 2) reveals that N in vBoN plays the role of a regularization parameter analogous to β in the KL-constrained RL objective, unifying the two frameworks in the N→1 and N→∞ limits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The N× inference throughput gain is practically significant for deployment, and the monotonic invariance of the vBoN objective to reward rescaling addresses a known sensitivity issue in RL-based alignment. However, experiments are limited to small models (GPT-2, Pythia-2.8B) and relatively simple tasks (sentiment steering, Reddit summarization), leaving open whether the approach scales to frontier LLMs and more complex reward landscapes. The Monte Carlo estimation of the CDF F, while shown to converge with ~200 samples, introduces a preprocessing cost that may be non-trivial for large-scale deployment, and the reliance on PPO for optimization inherits its known hyperparameter sensitivity.
