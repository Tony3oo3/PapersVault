## Problem

Generating images or videos that simultaneously and faithfully depict multiple personalized concepts remains difficult: existing joint-training or weight-merging approaches struggle with semantically related concepts (e.g., cats and dogs), scale poorly beyond three concepts, and often cause concept blending or disappearance. Prior inference-time methods like ConceptWeaver require additional inversion steps and direct manipulation of attention layers, limiting flexibility and increasing inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253622/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-object text prompt (e.g., 'a cat and a dog playing with a ball, mountain background'), a set of individually fine-tuned U-Net models (one per concept, trained via DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, or LoRA on SDXL), and for video, a reference image produced by the image-generation stage. It outputs a 768×768 multi-concept image in ~30 s on a single RTX 3090, or a 16-frame 512×512 video in ~50 s using I2VGen-XL. No additional training or inversion is required at inference time; the only auxiliary model is a text-guided segmentation pipeline (Grounding DINO + SAM) used to extract rectangular region masks at an intermediate timestep.

## Method

TweedieMix splits the DDIM reverse-sampling process into two stages. In the first (content-aware) stage, the base non-fine-tuned model samples with a multi-object prompt up to timestep t_con ≈ 0.8T under CFG++, and a resampling strategy at timestep T repeatedly subtracts single-concept Tweedie-denoised outputs from the multi-concept denoised output to amplify multi-object generation. In the second (fusion) stage, rectangular region masks are extracted from the intermediate denoised image via a segmentation model, and each concept's fine-tuned U-Net produces its own CFG++-guided denoised estimate; these per-region denoised latents are then combined via mask-weighted summation in Tweedie's denoised space rather than in noisy latent space or cross-attention features. This denoised-space mixing keeps all contributions on the same data manifold, enabling stable fusion of heterogeneously fine-tuned models without attention manipulation. For video, residual features from the first frame's U-Net bottleneck (midblock and first upsampling block) are linearly interpolated into subsequent frames at timestep T to preserve concept identity across the 16-frame sequence.

## Results

On the Custom Concept 101 dataset (10 multi-concept combinations, 100 images each), TweedieMix achieves the highest CLIP text-alignment score (0.3816), CLIP image-alignment score (0.8311), and DINOv2 image similarity (0.5950), outperforming the strongest baseline ConceptWeaver (0.3707, 0.8095, 0.5352 respectively). In a 20-participant user study, TweedieMix scores 4.56/5 on text match, 4.71/5 on concept match, and 4.47/5 on realism, compared to ConceptWeaver's 4.14, 4.32, and 4.11. For multi-concept video generation, TweedieMix receives a user preference score of 4.5/5 versus DreamVideo's 1.7/5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TweedieMix outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor ConceptWeaver (Kwon et al., 2024), it gains +0.011 CLIP text sim, +0.022 CLIP image sim, and +0.060 DINO similarity, with user-study advantages of +0.42 (text match), +0.39 (concept match), and +0.36 (realism). The earlier multi-concept method Mix-of-Show (Gu et al., 2023) trails by a wider margin (−0.024 CLIP text sim, −0.047 CLIP image sim, −0.074 DINO), reflecting its reliance on LoRA weight merging that causes concept blending. Single-concept joint-training baselines—Textual Inversion, Custom Diffusion, and Perfusion—perform substantially worse, with Perfusion scoring the lowest on all metrics (CLIP text sim 0.3222, user realism 1.88), indicating that joint training on multiple concepts leads to concept disappearance. For video generation, TweedieMix's training-free feature-injection approach (user score 4.5/5) dramatically surpasses the fine-tuning-based DreamVideo (Wei et al., 2023) at 1.7/5, which fails to preserve multiple custom concepts across frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

TweedieMix builds directly on ConceptWeaver (Kwon et al., 2024), which also splits reverse diffusion sampling into stages for multi-concept fusion, but eliminates ConceptWeaver's reliance on inversion steps and cross-attention manipulation. The method further leverages CFG++ (Chung et al., 2024) to keep the denoised space on-manifold, which the authors argue is essential for stable region-wise mixing of outputs from different fine-tuned U-Nets. Conceptually, the key departure from prior multi-concept methods (Mix-of-Show, ConceptWeaver) is performing concept fusion in the Tweedie denoised image space rather than in noisy latent space or attention features, combined with a resampling strategy at the initial timestep that subtracts single-concept denoised outputs from the multi-concept output to reinforce multi-object generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, inference-only approach removes the need for joint training, weight merging, or inversion steps, making multi-concept generation more accessible and flexible (compatible with LoRA, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, and other fine-tuning paradigms). The extension to video via bottleneck feature injection—rather than expensive video model fine-tuning—opens a practical path to multi-concept video generation. Open questions include how the method scales beyond 4–5 concepts, whether the rectangular mask approximation degrades with highly overlapping or small objects, and whether the resampling strategy generalizes to architectures beyond U-Net-based latent diffusion models.
