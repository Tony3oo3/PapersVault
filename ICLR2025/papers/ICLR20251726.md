## Problem

SAM produces highly unstable segmentation results when given low-quality prompts such as imprecise bounding boxes or very sparse points, a problem largely overlooked in prior work. The paper identifies that SAM's mask decoder suffers from attention drift, where suboptimal prompts mislead the cross-attention module to activate background or partial object features rather than the full target, causing large accuracy and consistency drops.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image together with a prompt that may be a noisy bounding box (with uniform noise scale 0.4 on height, width, and center) or a small set of positive points (1, 3, 5, or 10 randomly sampled from the ground-truth mask). It outputs a single binary segmentation mask per prompt. The key design constraint is that the original SAM image encoder and prompt encoder remain frozen; only the mask decoder's cross-attention sampling is modified, so the pipeline retains SAM's standard two-layer transformer decoder structure and produces masks in the same format.

## Method

Stable-SAM introduces a deformable sampling plugin (DSP) that calibrates SAM's token-to-image cross-attention by predicting per-location offsets and feature amplitudes via a small offset network (1×1 conv → 5×5 depthwise conv with LayerNorm+GELU → 1×1 conv), then resampling image features at the shifted locations using bilinear interpolation before feeding them as keys and values into the unchanged attention module. A dynamic routing plugin (DRP) computes a soft gating vector α from the prompt token via a small MLP, adaptively blending deformable and regular grid sampling so that high-quality prompts rely more on the original attention while low-quality prompts invoke stronger deformable correction. A robust training strategy (RTS) exposes the model to prompts of varying quality (GT boxes, noisy boxes, 1/3/10-point prompts) and synthetic occlusion images during training. The entire plugin adds only 0.08 M learnable parameters and requires just one training epoch for fast adaptation, while keeping SAM's architecture and pretrained weights completely intact.

## Results

On four HQ datasets (DIS, ThinObject-5K, COIFT, HR-SOD), Stable-SAM (1 epoch) achieves 82.3 mIoU / 82.3 ST under noisy box prompts, 76.9 mIoU / 71.1 ST under 1-point prompts, and 84.0 mIoU / 84.9 ST under 3-point prompts, substantially outperforming the SAM baseline (48.8/39.5, 43.3/45.1, 78.7/79.3 respectively). On MS COCO with noisy boxes (IoU 0.5–0.6), Stable-SAM reaches 44.8 mAP versus 27.3 for SAM and 31.9 for HQ-SAM. The method requires only 0.08 M learnable parameters and one training epoch, matching or exceeding methods trained for 12 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stable-SAM (1 epoch) decisively outperforms all baselines on the most challenging noisy-box setting: 82.3 mIoU vs. 48.8 for SAM, 52.4 for SAM 2, 72.4 for HQ-SAM, and 70.8 for PT-SAM (12 epochs). The training-free variants (PA-SAM, CAT-SAM, RobustSAM, SAM 2) all cluster around 51–52 mIoU on noisy boxes, showing that architectural tweaks alone cannot resolve the attention drift problem. Full-model finetuning (FT-SAM) catastrophically degrades to 32.5 mIoU, confirming that destroying SAM's pre-trained weights sacrifices zero-shot generality. Among parameter-efficient finetuning methods (DT-SAM, PT-SAM, LoRA, Adapter), all reach ~70 mIoU on noisy boxes after 12 epochs, yet Stable-SAM surpasses them by ~12 mIoU with a single epoch and only 0.08 M parameters. On the 1-point prompt (most ambiguous), the gap widens further: Stable-SAM achieves 76.9 mIoU while all 12-epoch finetuning baselines remain near 43 mIoU, demonstrating that the deformable sampling mechanism and robust training strategy are critical for handling severe prompt ambiguity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM's mask decoder architecture and borrows the deformable feature-sampling mechanism from Deformable DETR, but departs from both in a key way: rather than replacing SAM's standard cross-attention with a deformable variant (which would require retraining and risk catastrophic forgetting), it inserts a tiny offset network that only adjusts *where* and *how strongly* image features are sampled, leaving all original SAM weights frozen. It also contrasts with the broader SAM-adaptation literature (HQ-SAM, LoRA, Adapter, full finetuning), which modifies model parameters; Stable-SAM's plugin design adds only 0.08 M learnable parameters. The paper further introduces a dynamic routing plugin that conditionally blends deformable and regular sampling based on prompt quality, and a robust training strategy that mixes noisy and ambiguous prompts—neither of which appears in prior SAM adaptation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAM has become a de facto standard for promptable segmentation across medical imaging, remote sensing, robotics, and annotation pipelines, yet the paper demonstrates that its performance collapses (mIoU 79.5 → 48.8) under the imprecise boxes and sparse points that real users routinely provide—a practical gap that prior SAM-improvement work largely ignored. The 0.08 M-parameter plugin that recovers most of this gap while preserving zero-shot generality is attractive for data-scarce deployment, and the dynamic routing mechanism offers a principled way to avoid over-correcting when prompts are already good. Open questions include whether the approach transfers cleanly to other foundation models beyond SAM/SAM 2, whether the proposed stability metric (ST) captures all relevant failure modes, and whether the robust training strategy's synthetic occlusion augmentation generalizes beyond the four HQ evaluation datasets.
