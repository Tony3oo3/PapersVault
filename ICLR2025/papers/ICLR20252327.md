## Problem

Existing visual Needle-in-a-Haystack benchmarks rely on artificial constructs like overlaid text, measuring OCR rather than genuine visual retrieval and reasoning. Long-context LMMs can ingest many images but fail to accurately locate relevant visual information among distractors or integrate evidence across multiple images, a critical gap for real-world applications like photo album search or satellite imagery analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252327/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The VHs benchmark presents a set of COCO images (haystack sizes from 1 to 10,000) alongside a binary yes/no question templated as 'For the image with [anchor], is there [target]?' (single-needle) or 'For all images with [anchor], do all/any of them contain [target]?' (multi-needle). MIRAGE consumes up to 10k RGB images plus a text question, encoding each image through a frozen CLIP encoder (576 patch tokens), compressing to 32 tokens via a Q-Former, filtering via a learned retriever, and producing a free-form text answer. The entire pipeline runs on a single 40GB A100 GPU.

## Method

MIRAGE is a visual-RAG framework built on LLaVA-v1.5-7B with a Llama-v3.1-8B backbone that combines three key components: (1) a Q-Former compressor with 32 learned query vectors that cross-attend to 576 CLIP patch features, achieving an 18× token reduction per image; (2) a query-aware retriever module (transformer blocks + sigmoid) trained jointly with next-token prediction via binary cross-entropy loss (weight 5.0 on positives) to score and filter image relevance before the LLM sees them; and (3) a 1.2M-sample instruction-tuning dataset blending RetVQA, SlideVQA, WebQA, and synthetic multi-image data derived from LLaVA's single-image QA with keyword-clustered distractors. Training proceeds in two stages—pre-training the Q-Former and MLP on captioning data, then fine-tuning the full model (except CLIP) on MIQA data with images shuffled to eliminate positional bias.

## Results

MIRAGE-8.3B achieves 67.6 on RetVQA multi-image QA, more than doubling the performance of GPT-4o (34.6) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (32.2), and setting a new state-of-the-art. On the Visual Haystacks (VHs) single-needle benchmark, MIRAGE is the only method that scales to 10,000 images (51.7% accuracy), outperforming all open-source LMMs beyond 3 images and surpassing both GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro beyond 50 images. On single-image QA, MIRAGE retains competitive performance with minor degradation on VQA-v2 (76.6 vs. LLaVA's 78.5) and GQA (59.1 vs. 62.0) due to its 18× token compression (576→32 tokens/image), while surpassing LLaVA on MMB (69.2 vs. 64.3), MMB-CN (66.9 vs. 58.3), and MM-Vet (33.4 vs. 31.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary multi-image benchmark RetVQA, MIRAGE (67.6) dramatically outperforms all baselines: GPT-4o (34.6), Gemini 1.5 Pro (32.2), LLaVA-v1.5-7B (30.6), and LWM (not reported). On the VHs single-needle challenge, MIRAGE surpasses GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro at haystack sizes above 50 images, and outperforms all open-source models (Qwen2-VL, Phi-3-Vision, InternVL2, mPLUG-Owl3, LongVILA) beyond 3 images; it is the only method evaluated at 10K images. On VHs multi-needle, MIRAGE outperforms all open-source LMMs in most settings and approaches GPT-4o at N=100, though it still trails Gemini 1.5 Pro (which maintains >65% accuracy up to N=100). On single-image QA, MIRAGE slightly underperforms LLaVA-v1.5-7B on VQA-v2 (76.6 vs. 78.5), GQA (59.1 vs. 62.0), and TextVQA (56.2 vs. 58.2) due to token compression, but surpasses it on MMB, MMB-CN, and MM-Vet, and consistently outperforms LWM across all shared benchmarks. Compared to the CLIP+LLaVA retrieval baseline, MIRAGE's co-trained retriever achieves significantly higher recall, translating to better downstream accuracy across all VHs haystack sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH) diagnostic from NLP to the vision domain, but explicitly departs from prior visual NIAH benchmarks (Reid et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024c) that rely on overlaid text or out-of-distribution patches, arguing they test OCR rather than genuine visual retrieval and reasoning. The proposed MIRAGE framework builds on LLaVA-v1.5-7B and adapts RAG principles from NLP (Zhang et al., 2024; Izacard et al., 2023) to the multi-image setting, combining a Q-Former-based token compressor (from BLIP-2) with a query-aware retriever trained jointly with the LLM. It also extends prior MIQA work (Bansal et al., 2020; Penamakuri et al., 2023) from small image worlds (≤30 images) to scales of 10,000+ images, introducing a large-scale instruction-tuning dataset to bridge the training data gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VHs provides the first realistic, vision-centric unit test for long-context visual retrieval and reasoning, revealing that even state-of-the-art LMMs suffer up to 40% performance drops from visual distractors and exhibit significant positional biases—limitations invisible to prior OCR-based NIAH benchmarks. MIRAGE demonstrates that a lightweight visual-RAG approach can scale to 10k images on a single 40GB GPU, setting a practical path toward large-scale photo album search and satellite imagery analysis. Key open questions remain: cross-image reasoning still degrades sharply beyond two images, single-image performance is sacrificed by the compression scheme, and the benchmark is limited to COCO objects with binary yes/no answers, leaving open whether findings generalize to richer, open-domain multi-image reasoning.
