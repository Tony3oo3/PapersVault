## Problem

Existing language-based trajectory generators perform a direct 'interaction-to-trajectory' translation, which produces trajectories with unrealistic driving behaviors (e.g., dangerous speeding during overtaking) and poor generalization to unseen road topologies. The core gap is the absence of an explicit driving-logic layer between the abstract interaction text and concrete motion parameters, causing generated data to lack both realism and diversity needed for training robust prediction models.

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: (1) short text segments describing O vehicle-to-vehicle interactions (e.g., 'Car A overtakes Car B'), (2) a vector map represented as E polylines with D points and U attributes per point, and (3) initial trajectories of N vehicles with S waypoints each carrying x/y, heading, and speed. The output is two-stage: first, a text sequence of driving behaviors per vehicle with natural-language logic explanations (e.g., 'Change Lane: Both the left and right sides have ample road widths...'); second, complete trajectories as S waypoints at 20 Hz for up to 20 seconds. The L2T dataset provides 240K scenarios across six road topologies (straightway, bend, roundabout, cross/T/Y-shaped intersections) with four object classes.

## Method

Traj-LLM (built on Llama-7B) performs a two-stage 'interaction-behavior-trajectory' translation. In Stage 1, a VectorNet-style polyline encoder extracts map and initial-trajectory features, which are fused with text-embedded interaction features via a Random Locality Attention mechanism: top-K softmax attention scores between interaction and trajectory/map features are weighted by randomly sampled N(0,1) scalars, then added back to the interaction feature to enforce spatial consistency while introducing jitter for diversity. A cross-attention regression head autoregressively generates behavior text with logic. In Stage 2, the same Random Locality Attention links behavior features to interaction features, and a second regression head outputs trajectory waypoints as text. Both stages are trained with cross-entropy loss on the L2T dataset, where skilled drivers annotated behaviors and their underlying driving logic for each scenario.

## Results

On the L2T dataset, Traj-LLM achieves the best agent-level realism (AVG = 1.69) and scene-level realism (AVG = 1.12), substantially outperforming the next-best method CTG++ (8.90 and 6.17 respectively). For diversity, Traj-LLM leads on all four metrics: agent-level MD = 14.91, AD = 6.81, FD = 8.71, and scene-level WD = 13.81, compared to BITS (6.80, 3.22, 6.98, 7.27) as the strongest diversity baseline. In downstream trajectory prediction, combining WOMD training data with Traj-LLM-generated data improves MTR mAP from 0.405 to 0.416 and HDGT mAP from 0.272 to 0.294 on WOMD validation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Traj-LLM outperforms all four baselines on both realism and diversity. Against the image-based SNet (Bergamini, ICRA 2021) and TSim (Suo, CVPR 2021), Traj-LLM achieves dramatically lower realism distances (1.69 vs. 10.09 and 14.03 agent AVG) and far higher diversity (WD 13.81 vs. 0.00 and 2.92), since image-based methods produce near-deterministic outputs. Against the rule-based CTG (Zhong, ICRA 2023), Traj-LLM reduces agent realism AVG from 9.30 to 1.69 and nearly doubles scene-level diversity (WD 13.81 vs. 5.70). The closest competitor is the text-based CTG++ (Zhong, CoRL 2023), which Traj-LLM beats on realism (1.69 vs. 8.90 agent AVG) and diversity (WD 13.81 vs. 5.79), though CTG++ retains an edge on the Bypass controllability task (91.8% vs. 81.3% success rate) while Traj-LLM leads on Overtake (80.1% vs. 65.2%) and Yield (83.5% vs. 77.7%), yielding a higher average controllability of 81.6% vs. 78.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of language-conditioned traffic generation (CTG, CTG++, LGen) by inserting an explicit driving-behavior intermediate representation between the text interaction description and the final trajectory, reformulating the problem from 'interaction-trajectory' to 'interaction-behavior-trajectory' translation. It repurposes a general-purpose LLM (Llama-7B) as the backbone for both stages, combined with a custom random locality attention mechanism that grounds interaction features to specific map polylines and vehicle trajectories. Unlike graphical (LGSVL) or programmable (Scenic) generators that require manual or code-based scene construction, Traj-LLM operates purely from short natural-language prompts, and unlike prior LLM-based driving agents (GPT-Driver, LanguageMPC) that focus on high-level decision-making, it targets concrete waypoint-level trajectory synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The L2T dataset (240K annotated scenarios across six road topologies with interaction, behavior, and driving-logic text) is a substantial practical resource for the trajectory-generation community, and the released code lowers the barrier to reproducing language-based data augmentation for prediction models. The cross-dataset experiments (WOMD + L2T) confirm that generated trajectories complement rather than replace real data, but the distribution gap between simulated and real trajectories remains a limitation that constrains how much synthetic data can be used. Open questions include whether the two-stage paradigm generalizes to more complex multi-agent interactions (beyond 5 objects), whether larger or domain-specific LLMs would further close the realism gap, and whether the approach extends to other driving tasks such as planning or end-to-end control.
