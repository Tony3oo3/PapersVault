## Problem

Standard ℓp-sensitivity mechanisms (e.g., Laplace noise) for differentially private counting are suboptimal on partially ordered data because they add noise calibrated to impossible inputs that violate the order constraints. While the K-norm mechanism with the induced norm is provably optimal, it requires sampling from a complex d-dimensional polytope (the poset ball), for which no efficient sampler existed prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a database of length-d binary vectors whose bits satisfy a partial order (specified as a |P|×|P| binary adjacency matrix) and a privacy parameter ε, and outputs a differentially private count vector (the sum of all data points plus calibrated noise). The poset is assumed to have a single root; if not, a root is added and its dimension is discarded from the output via post-processing. The noise vector is sampled from the poset ball, a d-dimensional polytope with Ω(d) constraints, rather than from a standard ℓp ball.

## Method

The core contribution is an O(d²) uniform sampler for the poset ball, achieved by identifying it as a double order polytope and leveraging a triangulation indexed by non-interfering pairs of chains in the Birkhoff lattice. The key technical step is proving a bijection between these non-interfering chain pairs and a new combinatorial object called an extended bipartition (a bipartition of the poset ground set into two subsets, each equipped with a linear extension), for which a recursive uniform sampler is constructed by iteratively peeling off maximal elements and inserting them into valid positions in the linear extensions. This yields an overall O(d²) sampler, which is more than cubic faster than the best general polytope sampler and provably exponentially faster than rejection sampling from any ℓp ball. Experiments on path posets, random posets, and the National Health Interview Survey show error reductions of up to an order of magnitude over the ℓ∞ K-norm baseline.

## Results

The proposed K-norm mechanism for partially ordered data achieves a squared ℓ₂ norm ratio (poset ball / ℓ∞ ball) of 0.460–0.573 on the National Health Interview Survey (1–3 sections, d=4–15), roughly halving the error of the ℓ∞ baseline. On path posets, the improvement reaches over an order of magnitude near d=50, and on random posets the error reduction grows to over 90% at d=40. The sampler runs in O(d²) time, taking less than half a second for all dimensions tested (up to d=50) on a 2-CPU machine.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the ℓ∞ K-norm mechanism (Hardt & Talwar, 2010), which the paper justifies as the strongest ℓₚ baseline via an analytic result (Lemma 4.1) showing ℓ∞ minimizes expected squared ℓ₂ norm among all ℓₚ balls containing the poset ball. The proposed method dominates the ℓ∞ baseline across all experiments: up to 10× error reduction on path posets (d≈50), over 90% reduction on random posets (d=40), and roughly 50% reduction on the real-world NHIS survey. The analytical Gaussian mechanism (Balle & Wang, 2018) for (1,10⁻⁶)-DP is also dominated by the ℓ∞ mechanism for d≤50, so the proposed method implicitly outperforms it as well. Additionally, the paper proves (Theorem 3.17) that rejection sampling the poset ball from any ℓₚ ball requires Ω(2^d) samples in expectation, ruling out a simple rejection-based approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the K-norm mechanism framework of Hardt & Talwar (2010) and the efficient problem-specific instantiations of Joseph & Yu (2024) to a new structured-data setting: counting binary vectors constrained by a partial order. The key technical bridge is identifying the sensitivity space's convex hull as a double order polytope (via Chappell et al., 2017), then constructing a uniform sampler over its triangulation by proving a bijection between non-interfering chain pairs and a new combinatorial object (extended bipartitions). The optimality guarantee inherits from Awan & Slavković (2021), who showed the induced-norm K-norm mechanism is optimal in entropy and conditional variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(d²) sampler is provably exponentially faster than any ℓ_p-ball rejection sampler (Theorem 3.17) and achieves a more than cubic speedup over the best general polytope sampler, making the optimal mechanism practically deployable for real survey structures like NHIS. The exponential rejection-sampling lower bound cleanly rules out a natural alternative approach. A significant open question is extending the result to approximate DP, where the geometric problem shifts to finding the minimum-volume enclosing ellipse; the poset ball's lack of symmetry around (1,…,1) breaks the techniques used by Joseph & Yu (2024) for count and vote, suggesting genuinely new methods are needed.
