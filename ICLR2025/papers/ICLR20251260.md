## Problem

Adversarial training (AT) generates adversarial samples whose distribution shifts across epochs as model parameters update, yet the resulting catastrophic forgetting of previously learned adversarial knowledge has gone unexplored. This paper demonstrates empirically that AT indeed suffers from forgetting across multiple datasets and perturbation norms, and asks whether mitigating this forgetting can improve robust accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) with a fixed perturbation bound ε (8/255 for ℓ∞, 128/255 for ℓ2). During training, adversarial examples are generated on-the-fly via K-step PGD from each natural image, and the model maintains a growing memory buffer of frozen teacher checkpoints (one per stage of m epochs). The output is a robust classifier producing C-class labels, evaluated under white-box (PGD-20, CW-20), black-box, and Auto Attack.

## Method

AMS (Adaptive Multi-teacher Self-distillation) treats every m epochs as a 'stage' and saves the model parameters at each stage boundary as a frozen teacher. During training, the current model is regularized via a weighted sum of KL divergences between its outputs and all previous teachers' outputs on the current adversarial samples, encouraging retention of previously learned adversarial knowledge. To handle the low accuracy of AT models (which means earlier teachers may encode incorrect predictions), a Reweighting-based Loss Correction mechanism scales each teacher's distillation contribution by the softmax probability it assigns to the true class, so misclassified samples receive near-zero weight. The final training objective is L_adv + (λ/Σw_ij)·Σ w_ij·D_KL(h_θj(x′)‖h_θt(x′)), where λ controls the plasticity-stability trade-off. The method is plug-and-play and can be layered onto any existing AT algorithm (PGD, TRADES, S²O, AWP, diffusion-based AT).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ℓ∞, ε=8/255, WideResNet-34-10), TRADES+AMS achieves 54.31% Auto Attack accuracy (vs. 53.00% for TRADES alone) and 58.76% PGD-20 accuracy (vs. 56.68%), while maintaining 85.37% clean accuracy. On CIFAR-100, TRADES+AMS improves AA from 26.90% to 28.25% (+1.35%) and PGD-20 from 32.11% to 33.81%. On TinyImageNet, Wang et al.(2023)(1M)+AMS reaches 31.52% AA accuracy, the highest reported in the comparison. The method also reduces robust overfitting: the gap between best and final checkpoint drops from 3.28% (TRADES) to 0.58% (TRADES+AMS) under PGD-20 on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMS is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement to multiple AT baselines (TRADES, S²O, TRADES+AWP, Wang et al. 2023) and consistently improves robust accuracy across all four datasets. On CIFAR-10, TRADES+AMS gains +1.31% AA over TRADES and +1.99% AA over S²O, while TRADES+AWP+AMS reaches 57.36% AA, surpassing all non-diffusion baselines in the table. On CIFAR-100, the improvement is +1.35% AA (TRADES→TRADES+AMS) and +0.26% AA (Sehwag et al.→+AMS), with clean accuracy also improving by +2.34% in the TRADES case. On SVHN and TinyImageNet, Wang et al.(2023)(1M)+AMS outperforms its base by +1.42% and +0.76% AA respectively, though TinyImageNet clean accuracy drops slightly (−0.63%). Compared to CL methods (Table 8/9), AMS outperforms all replay-based (ER, DER, GCR, DGC-ER) and regularization-based (EWC, Lwf) approaches: e.g., PGD-10+AMS achieves 51.63% AA vs. 50.45% for PGD-10+Lwf and 49.53% for PGD-10+EWC, while also maintaining higher clean accuracy (84.45% vs. 83.84% and 83.21%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a conceptual bridge between adversarial training (AT) and continual learning (CL), repurposing multi-teacher self-distillation (originating from knowledge distillation, Hinton et al. 2015) as a regularizer to mitigate catastrophic forgetting across training stages. It builds directly on the PGD (Madry et al., 2018) and TRADES (Zhang et al., 2019b) frameworks, positioning AMS as a plug-in module rather than a standalone training procedure. The key departure from prior CL-forgetting methods (EWC, Lwf, DER) is the Reweighting-based Loss Correction mechanism, which adaptively down-weights teacher signals from misclassified samples—a necessity arising from the inherently low robust accuracy of AT models that standard CL distillation does not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that forgetting is a general property of AT across datasets and perturbation norms provides a new diagnostic lens for understanding why robust accuracy plateaus, and the plug-in design means practitioners can adopt AMS atop any existing AT pipeline with minimal code changes. However, the practical gains are modest (typically 1–2% robust accuracy), and the method incurs additional memory and compute costs from storing multiple teacher checkpoints. The paper itself flags the connection between forgetting and robust overfitting as an open question, suggesting that a deeper theoretical understanding of why distributional drift in adversarial samples causes forgetting—and whether it is the root cause of overfitting—remains to be established.
