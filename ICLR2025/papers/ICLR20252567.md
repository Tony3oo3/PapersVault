## Problem

Existing neural architectures for SPD matrix estimation either fail to guarantee positive-definiteness of outputs or cannot simultaneously enforce additional structural constraints such as element-wise sparsity. No prior method provides strict guarantees for jointly learning matrices that are both SPD and sparse, a requirement central to sparse precision matrix estimation in Gaussian graphical models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252567/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a p×p empirical covariance matrix S as input, initialized internally as (S + I_p)^{-1}, and produces a p×p sparse SPD precision matrix Θ as output. The SPD guarantee is structural (enforced at every intermediate update via Schur's complement), not post-hoc, and sparsity is imposed directly on the off-diagonal entries through learned soft-thresholding within the update functions. The method also maintains the inverse W = Θ^{-1} throughout, enabling O(p²) per column-row update rather than O(p³).

## Method

SpodNet is an SPD-to-SPD neural layer that sequentially updates each of the p column-row pairs: a learned function f(Θ) sets the new off-diagonal entries (allowing arbitrary values), while a learned function g(Θ) sets the diagonal entry as v + u^T[Θ_{11}]^{-1}u with v > 0, guaranteeing SPD via Schur's complement condition at every step. Three variants of increasing expressivity are proposed—UBG (unrolled Block Graphical-ISTA with learned step-sizes and thresholds), PNP (replacing soft-thresholding with a learned denoiser), and E2E (fully flexible MLP for the column update)—all sharing the same SPD-preserving diagonal mechanism. The inverse W is updated in O(p²) using the Banachiewicz formula on Schur's complement, bringing the full layer cost to O(p³). A stabilization mechanism scales the off-diagonal preactivations by √ζ/√(z^T[Θ_{11}]^{-1}z) to control spectral perturbation and prevent eigenvalue collapse during training.

## Results

On synthetic sparse precision matrix recovery, SpodNet models (UBG, PNP, E2E) significantly outperform traditional estimators (GLasso, Ledoit-Wolf, OAS) in NMSE, with the gap widening in high-dimensional, sample-deficient regimes (p=100, n≤p) where traditional methods reach ~90% NMSE. In the large-sample regime at p=20, all methods achieve ~2.5% NMSE, but UBG attains an F1 score above 0.75 for support recovery, with up to 25% F1 improvement over GLasso. On the real-world Animals dataset (p=33, n=102), UBG achieves a graph modularity of 0.78, surpassing GLasso (0.61) and GLAD (0.72), though slightly below EGFM (0.86) which is specifically designed for clustered graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GLasso, SpodNet models (particularly UBG) achieve substantially lower NMSE in sample-deficient settings (n≤p) and up to 25% higher F1 scores for support recovery; on the Animals dataset, UBG's modularity (0.78) nearly doubles GLasso's (0.61). Against Ledoit-Wolf and OAS, the learning-based SpodNet models dominate in NMSE, especially as dimensionality increases and sparsity decreases, where these shrinkage estimators degrade to ~90% NMSE. Against GLAD, SpodNet provides a decisive advantage: GLAD's sparse output Z is never positive-definite (0% SPD rate in the strongly sparse p=100 setting), while its SPD output Θ is not sparse and performs poorly; SpodNet guarantees both properties simultaneously. Against EGFM, UBG trails in modularity (0.78 vs. 0.86), but EGFM is explicitly designed with a low-rank-plus-diagonal inductive bias for cluster discovery, whereas UBG has no such structural prior, making the comparison somewhat asymmetric.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpodNet sits at the intersection of algorithm unrolling (Gregor & LeCun, 2010; Rolfs et al., 2012) and the Plug-and-Play paradigm (Venkatakrishnan et al., 2013), but departs from both by using Schur's complement as a structural invariant that decouples the SPD guarantee from the off-diagonal update. This contrasts with Riemannian SPD networks (Huang & Van Gool, 2017; Gao et al., 2020), which rely on expensive manifold operations and cannot impose sparsity, and with GLAD (Shrivastava et al., 2020), which unrolls an alternating minimization but produces two separate matrices (one sparse, one SPD) that fail to coincide. The key conceptual departure is that the Schur-complement update allows *any* learned function on the off-diagonal block while provably preserving positive-definiteness, a flexibility absent from prior SPD-preserving layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpodNet is the first architecture to provide strict, simultaneous guarantees of SPD and elementwise sparsity in a single output matrix, filling a gap that has blocked data-driven sparse precision matrix estimation. The framework is generic—any structural constraint expressible on off-diagonal entries can be enforced—suggesting applicability beyond sparsity (e.g., low-rank structure, block patterns). Open questions include the computational cost at high dimensions (O(p³) per layer), the lack of formal convergence or expressivity guarantees, and whether the training-stability scaling trick generalizes to deeper stacks. For practitioners in neuroscience, finance, or graph learning, this offers a drop-in module that avoids the ad-hoc eigenvalue clipping or Riemannian retractions of prior approaches.
