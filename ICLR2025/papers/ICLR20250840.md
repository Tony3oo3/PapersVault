## Problem

Self-supervised learning methods rely on randomly sampled image augmentations to generate training views, yet little research has explored more effective strategies for view selection that could improve representation quality. Existing hard-view approaches either require auxiliary networks, adversarial training components, or hyperparameter tuning, and none have been validated at scale on full ImageNet-1k with modern architectures like Vision Transformers. This paper asks whether a simple, learning-free adversarial view selection strategy can consistently improve SSL pretraining across diverse objectives and architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-10) along with the baseline augmentation distribution T (e.g., RandomResizedCrop + ColorJitter), which is left unchanged. For each image in a minibatch, it samples N augmented views (N=4 for SimSiam/SimCLR/iBOT, N=10 for DINO's 2 global + 8 local heads), forwards all views through the encoder and projector, and outputs a single hardest pair per image for the backward pass. The final output is a pretrained backbone whose frozen features or finetuned weights transfer to classification, detection, and segmentation tasks.

## Method

HVP is a learning-free, model-agnostic strategy that replaces the standard two-view random sampling with an adversarial selection loop: for each image, N views are sampled from the augmentation distribution, all C(N,2) pairs are formed, and the sample-wise loss (e.g., negative cosine similarity for SimSiam) is computed for every pair using the current model state. The pair yielding the highest loss is selected and used for the backward pass, while all other pairs are discarded. This process repeats every training iteration, so the notion of 'hard' evolves as the model's embedding space develops. The approach requires only the ability to compute per-sample losses, introduces no new hyperparameters, and acts as a controlled regularization: bounding N prevents the adversary from becoming too strong (which would cause model collapse), while the stateful selection ensures the model is continuously challenged at the edge of its current discriminative capacity.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, HVP consistently improves linear evaluation accuracy by approximately 1% on average across 100- and 300-epoch pretraining for all four SSL methods (DINO, SimSiam, SimCLR, iBOT). The headline result is a new state-of-the-art of 78.8% top-1 linear evaluation accuracy on DINO ViT-B/16 (400 epochs), surpassing the official DINO baseline of 78.2% by 0.6%. Transfer to downstream tasks (finetuning on CIFAR-10/100, Flowers-102, iNaturalist, Food-101, and COCO object detection/instance segmentation) shows consistent gains of 0.3–4.1% depending on the dataset and protocol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HVP outperforms all four baselines on ImageNet linear evaluation across both 100- and 300-epoch settings. The largest gains appear with SimCLR (+1.96% at 100 ep, +1.80% at 300 ep) and DINO ViT-S/16 (+0.73% at 100 ep, +1.08% at 300 ep), while SimSiam gains are more modest on linear eval (+0.78%/+0.55%) but stronger on k-NN (+1.50%/+1.57%). iBOT shows a small k-NN regression at 100 epochs (−0.18%) but recovers at 300 epochs (+0.24%). On the SOTA DINO ViT-B/16 (400 ep), HVP reaches 78.8% linear eval vs. 78.2% baseline and 76.95% k-NN vs. 76.1%, confirming that the gains scale to larger models and longer training. Transfer to COCO object detection (AP50) and instance segmentation also favors HVP by 0.33–0.60 points over both iBOT and DINO baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

HVP extends the line of work on controlling view hardness in discriminative SSL, which has previously relied on adversarial view generators (Tian et al. 2020b, Tamkin et al. 2021, Shi et al. 2022) or cooperative strategies (Hou et al. 2023) that introduce auxiliary networks or significant pipeline modifications. The closest prior work, Koçyigit et al. (2023), also selects hard views using the current model state but targets pretraining acceleration, requires hyperparameter tuning, and is validated only on smaller ImageNet subsets with ResNets. HVP departs from this lineage by being fully learning-free (no additional models), requiring no hyperparameter tuning, and being the first to validate hard view sampling at full ImageNet-1k scale across both ConvNets and ViTs with multiple SSL objectives (SimSiam, DINO, iBOT, SimCLR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent ~1% improvement across four SSL methods and two architecture families, culminating in a new SOTA on DINO ViT-B/16 (78.8% vs. 78.2%), demonstrates that the choice of which views to optimize on is a meaningful lever that had been underexplored at scale. The finding that a simple, learning-free selection strategy outperforms more complex adversarial generators (which the authors show fail in Appendix G) suggests that the model's own loss landscape is a sufficient signal for difficulty, reducing the need for auxiliary training. Key limitations include the ~2× computational overhead from extra forward passes, and the open question of whether efficiency can be improved (e.g., via alternating steps, low-precision selection, or earlier-layer embeddings) without sacrificing the gains. The failure of manual IoU-based policies (Table 4) to replicate HVP's benefits highlights that the selection must operate on latent embeddings with access to the current model state, a constraint that limits further simplification.
