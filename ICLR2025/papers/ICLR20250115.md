## Problem

Multi-Draft Speculative Decoding (MDSD) accelerates LLM inference by having a small draft model propose multiple candidate tokens that a large target model verifies in parallel, but the optimal acceptance rate for a given draft distribution has never been computed for realistic vocabulary sizes (thousands of tokens) because the underlying optimal transport problem has exponentially many variables. Consequently, the gap between existing verification algorithms (RRS, K-SEQ) and the theoretical upper bound has remained unquantified on real text distributions, making it impossible to assess how suboptimal current methods are.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target model distribution p over vocabulary Σ, a draft model output distribution q, and a number of draft tokens n, and produces a verified next token that exactly follows the target distribution p. The draft tokens are constructed via one of several sampling strategies (with replacement, without replacement, or the proposed greedy method), and the verification algorithm must guarantee lossless output. The optimal acceptance rate α*(p, p_draft) is computed as a scalar summary of the best possible verification performance for a given draft distribution, enabling direct comparison across sampling strategies and model pairs.

## Method

The paper derives a subset selection formulation for the optimal acceptance rate by taking the dual of the optimal transport LP and exploiting total unimodularity of the constraint matrix, reducing the problem to minimizing f(H) = P(H) − Q(H) over subsets H of the vocabulary. For sampling with and without replacement, the paper proves Q is a q-convex function, which enables an O(|Σ| log |Σ|) algorithm: sort vocabulary elements by the ratio q(x)/p(x) in non-increasing order, then perform a linear scan to find the minimum of f(H_i) over prefix sets. The paper also proposes a greedy draft construction where the first n−1 tokens are deterministically set to the top-n−1 tokens of q and only the last token is sampled without replacement, yielding a verification algorithm that provably achieves the optimal acceptance rate. Experiments across four model families (LLaMA, OPT, Vicuna, Qwen2) and three tasks show that sampling without replacement outperforms with replacement, and that existing algorithms (RRS, K-SEQ) still fall 0.5–3.3 percentage points below the theoretical upper bound.

## Results

For the first time, the paper computes the theoretical optimal acceptance rate (α*) for MDSD with vocabulary sizes in the thousands. On LLaMA-68M/LLaMA-7B (Alpaca, T=0.7, n=3), the Greedy draft sampling with its optimal verification achieves 78.4% acceptance rate, compared to 73.0% for the with-replacement upper bound and 75.7% for RRS with without-replacement sampling. Across all four model pairs and three tasks, the Greedy method consistently achieves the highest acceptance rates, with gaps of 1–3% over RRS without replacement and 5–7% over K-SEQ. The paper also quantifies that existing verification algorithms (RRS, K-SEQ) still fall 0.5–3.3% below the theoretical upper bound even for their respective draft sampling strategies.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| With Replacement | RRS | 71.6 | 65.3 | 59.8 |
| With Replacement | K-SEQ | 71.9 | 66.0 | 60.0 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Greedy draft sampling method with its optimal verification algorithm outperforms all baselines across every model pair and task. Compared to RRS (Yang et al. 2024b; Jeon et al. 2024), the Greedy method gains 2.7 percentage points on Alpaca (78.4% vs. 75.7%) and 2.8 points on CNN-DailyMail (73.2% vs. 70.5%) for LLaMA-68M/LLaMA-7B. Against K-SEQ (Sun et al. 2024e), which only applies to with-replacement sampling, the Greedy method achieves a 6.5-point advantage on Alpaca (78.4% vs. 71.9%), demonstrating that the draft sampling strategy itself is a major bottleneck. SpecHub (Sun et al. 2024b) is shown to be theoretically equivalent to the Greedy method when n=2 (both guarantee the top-1 token is included), and experiments in Table 2 confirm no statistically significant difference at n=2; however, for n=4 drafts, the Greedy method extends naturally and achieves 88.8% vs. 80.4% for RRS without replacement on Alpaca at T=0.1. The paper also shows that even the best existing verification algorithm for a given draft strategy (e.g., K-SEQ for with-replacement) still falls 0.6–3.3% below the newly computed theoretical upper bound, indicating room for further improvement in verification design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SPECTR (Sun et al., 2024e), which first formulated the optimal acceptance rate for Multi-Draft Speculative Decoding as an optimal transport problem, and on RRS (Yang et al., 2024b; Jeon et al., 2024) and SpecHub (Sun et al., 2024b) as existing verification algorithms. The key departure is a theoretical reframe: by taking the dual of the transport LP and exploiting total unimodularity of the bipartite incidence structure, the authors reduce the exponentially-sized problem to a subset selection problem over the vocabulary, enabling polynomial-time computation of the optimal acceptance rate for common draft distributions. The proposed greedy draft construction (top n−1 tokens deterministically, last token random) is shown to be equivalent to SpecHub in the n=2 case but generalizes to arbitrary n with a simpler verification algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For the first time, this work quantifies the gap between state-of-the-art verification algorithms (K-SEQ, RRS) and the theoretical optimum at realistic vocabulary sizes (thousands), revealing consistent 1–3 percentage point deficits that had previously been unmeasurable. The finding that a simple greedy draft strategy can outperform sampling without replacement at low temperatures suggests that draft construction, not just verification, is a critical design lever. Open questions include whether verification algorithms can be designed to close the gap to the theoretical bound, and how to combine multi-draft and multi-step improvements (tree-structured MDSD) in a jointly optimal way, which the authors note as future work.
