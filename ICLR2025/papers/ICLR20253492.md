## Problem

Implied volatility smoothing has remained an instance-by-instance numerical optimization process: every time market quotes update, a parametric surface (e.g., SVI) must be re-calibrated from scratch. Classical neural networks cannot be applied directly because the input data—collections of observed volatilities—varies in size and spatial arrangement over time as options expire, new strikes appear, and coordinates shift continuously. This prevents leveraging large historical datasets to train a single general-purpose smoothing model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a variable-size set of observed implied volatilities at arbitrary (time-to-expiry, log-moneyness) coordinates—typically 100–500+ points per surface, arranged in dense z-slices sparsely distributed along the ρ-axis—and produces a smooth surface evaluated at any desired output locations in the domain D = (0.01, 1) × (−1.5, 0.5) in transformed (ρ, z) coordinates. The output must satisfy no-arbitrage shape constraints (monotonicity in expiry and non-negative implied density), and the method is robust to subsampling of inputs, which occurs in practice during outlier removal.

## Method

The paper adapts the Graph Neural Operator (GNO) architecture for interpolation by dropping the pointwise linear transformation in the first layer, enabling hidden states to be computed at arbitrary output locations rather than being restricted to input data points. A Nyström approximation with low-discrepancy subsampling (K=50 neighbors) and truncation along the time-to-expiry axis (ρ̄=0.3) controls computational complexity while preserving the discretization-invariance property. Training uses a Vega-weighted relative-error fitting loss combined with penalty terms enforcing butterfly and calendar no-arbitrage constraints via finite differences on a synthetic grid, plus Laplacian regularization. A single model with ~102K parameters is trained for 500 epochs on 43,442 intraday S&P 500 surfaces (2012–2020) and achieves ~0.5% MAPE on 2021 test data, substantially outperforming SVI (~1–2% MAPE) while producing virtually arbitrage-free surfaces.

## Results

On the 2021 test set (CBOE S&P 500 intraday, 20-min intervals), Operator Deep Smoothing (OpDS) achieves a mean surface MAPE of 0.5% (median 0.5%), compared to SVI's 1.5% (median 1.4%). In terms of bid-ask spread coverage, OpDS yields a mean ⟨δ_spr⟩ of 1.27 (median 1.24) versus SVI's 3.38 (median 3.32), meaning OpDS predictions fall within the bid-ask spread far more often. On the generalization test (Table 1), the single GNO trained only on S&P 500 intraday data produces MAPEs of 0.27% (SPX end-of-day), 1.06% (NDX), 1.63% (DJX), and 0.89% (RUT) in January 2021, with near-zero arbitrage losses across all four indices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVI (the industry-standard parametric benchmark), OpDS substantially outperforms on both primary metrics: mean test MAPE is 0.5% vs. 1.5% (a ~3× improvement), and mean test ⟨δ_spr⟩ is 1.27 vs. 3.38 (a ~2.7× improvement), with the gap widening over time as bid-ask spreads tighten. Against Ackerer et al. (2020) 'Deep Smoothing' (DS), which trains a separate classical neural network per surface, OpDS achieves a median extrapolation-test MAPE of 0.9% versus DS's 1.7% on Jan–Apr 2018 end-of-day SPX data (Table 5), while matching DS on interpolation (0.7% vs. 0.8% median). The paper attributes this extrapolation advantage to the operator-level learning paradigm: a single GNO with ~102k parameters leverages the full 10-year training corpus for every surface, whereas DS requires ~5k parameters per instance and cannot share information across surfaces. OpDS also uses ~3000× fewer total parameters than DS over the full dataset (102k vs. >200M), making it far more scalable to higher-frequency data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the graph neural operator (GNO) architecture from Anandkumar et al. (2020) and the broader kernel integral transform neural operator framework of Kovachki et al. (2023) to the financial engineering task of implied volatility smoothing. The key architectural modification—dropping the pointwise linear transformation in the first layer to enable interpolation at arbitrary output locations—departs from standard GNO implementations designed for PDE solving on fixed or regular meshes. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the long-standing parametric smoothing tradition (SVI/SSVI) and against instance-by-instance neural network approaches (Ackerer et al., 2020; Bergeron et al., 2021), arguing that the dynamic, irregular spatial configuration of option quotes (varying strikes, expiries, and coordinates over time) fundamentally excludes classical fixed-input neural networks and motivates the discretization-invariant operator paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single ~100K-parameter model can replace 60,000+ per-slice SVI calibrations or per-surface neural network trainings, with substantially lower error and strong generalization to unseen indices (NDX, DJX, RUT) without any retraining. This opens the door to leveraging large historical options datasets for smoothing in a way that was previously impractical, and the discretization-invariance property provides robustness to the subsampling that occurs in practice during outlier removal. Limitations include loss of parameter interpretability (a concern for some practitioners), dependence on high-frequency proprietary data for optimal performance, and the fact that the Nyström approximation breaks the theoretical smoothness guarantee of the GNO output. Open directions include combining the operator approach with VAE architectures for dimensionality reduction and extending to higher-dimensional surfaces such as the volatility cube.
