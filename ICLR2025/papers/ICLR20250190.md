## Problem

Existing computational antibody design methods optimize primarily for binding affinity but neglect other biophysical properties critical for developability and clinical safety, such as non-specific binding, self-association, and stability. The common wet-lab strategy of generating diverse candidates and post-filtering is inefficient and has low success rates for satisfying all constraints simultaneously. This paper addresses the gap by formulating antibody design as a constrained multi-objective optimization problem within a generative model.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the antigen structure and the antibody framework regions (both structure as SE(3) elements and sequence as 20 amino acid types), with all six CDRs (H1–H3, L1–L3) masked for generation. It outputs the complete CDR sequences (discrete amino acid assignments) and CDR backbone structures (Cα coordinates and local-frame rotation matrices) jointly. During evaluation, 128 antibodies are generated per antigen, and performance is assessed via Rosetta binding energy, evolutionary plausibility, AAR, RMSD, and constraint-violation percentages for self-association, stability, and non-specific binding.

## Method

AbNovo is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 pre-trains a score-based diffusion model for CDR structure–sequence co-design, using CTMC diffusion for discrete sequences and SE(3) diffusion for structures, with a network comprising a structure-aware language model (ESM-2-based, pre-trained on 2.2M structures), Evoformer trunks, and Invariant Point Attention. Stage 2 fine-tunes the base model via constrained preference optimization: binding affinity metrics (Rosetta energy, evolutionary plausibility) serve as continuous rewards modeled through Noise Contrastive Alignment (NCA) adapted for diffusion models, while biophysical properties (self-association, stability, non-specific binding) are enforced as explicit constraints via a primal-dual Lagrangian scheme with iteratively updated dual variables. The key technical contributions are (i) extending constrained DPO from LLM alignment to score-based diffusion models with rigorous theoretical derivation, (ii) replacing pairwise preferences with continuous rewards collected offline from sampled antibodies, and (iii) incorporating a structure-aware language model to mitigate overfitting from scarce antibody–antigen training data. The training proceeds in iterative rounds where the reference model is updated from the latest policy, and both policy and dual variables are optimized using offline reward/constraint evaluations.

## Results

On the RAbD test set, AbNovo achieves a Rosetta Binding Energy of −12.05, substantially outperforming the best baseline (GeoAb at −1.75) and approaching the native antibody reference (−19.41). AbNovo also attains the lowest constraint violation rates across all biophysical properties: 2.3% self-association, 2.8% stability violation, and 1.7% non-specific binding, compared to 38.5–50.8% self-association and 4.8–94.5% stability violations for baselines. On evolutionary plausibility, AbNovo scores 2.36, marginally better than the native reference (2.38) and all baselines (2.44–2.82). The constrained preference optimization stage improves binding energy from −2.60 (base model) to −12.05 while reducing self-association from 19.9% to 2.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AbNovo outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against DiffAb (Luo et al., 2022), AbNovo improves binding energy from −0.96 to −12.05 and reduces stability violations from 15.6% to 2.8%. Against dyMEAN (Kong et al., 2023), the gains are most dramatic on self-association (50.8% → 2.3%) and stability (94.5% → 2.8%), while binding energy improves from −1.74 to −12.05. Against GeoAb (Lin et al., 2024), AbNovo achieves a 10.3-unit improvement in binding energy (−1.75 → −12.05) and reduces self-association from 38.5% to 2.3%. Against AbX (Zhu et al., 2024), AbNovo reverses a positive (unfavorable) binding energy of 4.79 to −12.05 and cuts non-specific binding from 11.6% to 1.7%. Notably, AbNovo's base model (before preference optimization) already surpasses all baselines on binding energy, and the constrained preference optimization stage provides an additional ~9.5-unit improvement while simultaneously satisfying all biophysical constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

AbNovo extends the score-based SE(3) diffusion framework for antibody CDR co-design (DiffAb, AbX) by integrating a constrained preference optimization stage adapted from LLM alignment. The key methodological departure from the closest prior work, AbDPO, is twofold: (1) replacing pairwise preference data with continuous rewards via Noise Contrastive Alignment, and (2) formulating biophysical properties (self-association, stability, non-specific binding) as hard constraints solved via a primal-dual Lagrangian rather than as additional optimization objectives. The structure-aware language model component draws on ESMFold-style architectures pre-trained on 2.2M PDB structures to address the scarcity of antibody-antigen complex data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in computational antibody design: most prior methods optimize only binding affinity, ignoring developability properties critical for clinical translation. The ablation showing that treating biophysical properties as constraints (rather than multi-objective terms) preserves binding energy while satisfying constraints validates the constrained formulation for this domain. However, the absence of wet-lab validation and reliance on Rosetta energy as a proxy for true binding affinity limit the strength of the claims. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other protein design tasks and whether the constraint thresholds transfer across antigen classes.
