## Problem

Unified lexical tone decoding from intracranial recordings across multiple subjects is hindered by physiological and instrumental heterogeneity (varying electrode configurations, individual neural differences). Existing subject-specific models suffer from poor generalization due to data scarcity in invasive settings, while homogeneous approaches that simply pool data fail to exploit subject-specific neural patterns. The paper addresses how to simultaneously capture shared (homogeneous) and subject-specific (heterogeneous) neural representations to enable a single unified decoder across heterogeneous recordings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250581/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw sEEG signals (downsampled to 1000 Hz, low-pass filtered at 300 Hz, notch-filtered at 50/100/150/200 Hz, padded to 1000 samples) from 4 participants with different channel counts and electrode placements reading 407 Mandarin monosyllabic characters. The heterogeneous input is first mapped to a uniform 64-channel latent space by a stem layer before encoding. The output is a 4-class tone label (Tones 1–4) for decoding, alongside disentangled neural representations: discrete codes from a shared codebook (homogeneous) and per-subject private codebooks (heterogeneous).

## Method

H2DiLR operates in two stages. In the self-supervised H2D stage, per-subject 1D-convolutional VQ encoders produce continuous latent tokens that are discretized via a ranking-based partition: the top-νL tokens most similar to a shared codebook are quantized with it (capturing homogeneity), while the remaining (1−ν)L tokens are quantized with subject-specific private codebooks (capturing heterogeneity). The shared codebook is updated via exponential moving average to avoid cross-subject update conflicts, while private codebooks use a commitment loss. A symmetric transpose-convolutional decoder reconstructs the input, and the straight-through estimator handles non-differentiable quantization. In the supervised ND stage, all encoders and codebooks are frozen, and a lightweight 4-layer transformer (128-dim embedding, 512-dim FFN) with 1D-conv patch embedding performs tone classification on the concatenated H2D representations.

## Results

On the sEEG Mandarin tone decoding task (407 syllables, 4 subjects), H2DiLR with full UPaNT+H2D achieves 43.67±1.78% average top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best heterogeneous baseline (CoST, 37.47%) by 6.2 points and the best homogeneous baseline (BIOT, 37.47%) by 6.2 points. The UPaNT-only variant (40.37%) already surpasses all baselines, and adding H2D disentanglement yields a further 3.3-point gain. On the secondary CHB-MIT seizure prediction task, H2DiLR reaches 93.97% accuracy and 0.10/h FPR, improving over the best baseline (NeuroBERT, 89.36% accuracy) by 4.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H2DiLR (UPaNT+H2D) outperforms all seven baselines across every subject on the tone decoding task. Against the strongest heterogeneous baselines with pre-training—CoST (37.47%) and NeuroBERT (36.94%)—H2DiLR gains 6.2 and 6.7 points respectively, while the no-pre-training heterogeneous methods (SPaRCNet 31.47%, FFCL 32.82%, ST-Transformer 33.35%) trail by 10–12 points. The homogeneous BIOT baseline (37.47%) matches CoST but still falls 6.2 points short of H2DiLR, confirming that merely aggregating multi-subject data without explicit homogeneity-heterogeneity modeling is insufficient. The ablation variant H2DiLR with ν=0 (no shared codebook, 38.98%) shows that the disentanglement mechanism itself contributes an additional 4.7 points over a purely heterogeneous VQ setup. On the CHB-MIT seizure prediction task, H2DiLR similarly dominates all baselines, achieving 93.97% accuracy versus NeuroBERT's 89.36% and CoST's 88.45%, with the lowest false positive rate (0.10/h vs. 0.15–0.22/h).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) for its vector-quantized tokenization mechanism (UPaNT), repurposing the codebook concept to store neural 'articulation codes' rather than image patches. It departs from the dominant subject-specific (heterogeneous) paradigm in prior tone decoding work (Liu et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2023) and from BIOT (Yang et al., 2023), which attempted homogeneous cross-subject learning but only aligned channel count and sampling rate without modeling physiological heterogeneity. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a disentanglement stage (H2D) that splits quantized tokens between a shared codebook (homogeneity) and per-subject private codebooks (heterogeneity), creating a third paradigm between purely heterogeneous and purely homogeneous training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled mechanism for unified neural decoding across subjects with heterogeneous electrode configurations, a practical bottleneck for invasive BCI deployment. The 12.2% average accuracy gain over subject-specific models and the validation on seizure prediction suggest the disentanglement principle is task-agnostic. However, the evidence base is narrow: only four subjects, a single primary dataset, and no evaluation on truly unseen subjects, leaving the most practically critical question—zero-shot generalization to new patients—entirely open. The limited interpretability of learned codes (beyond the Tone 1 clustering observation) and the inability to scale the subject pool due to intracranial recording constraints are acknowledged limitations that will need addressing before clinical translation.
