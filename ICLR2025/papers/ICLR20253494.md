## Problem

Edge-level differential privacy for hierarchical clustering under Dasgupta's objective incurs an Ω(n²/ε) additive error lower bound, rendering private HC trees practically useless on natural graph families. This paper asks whether the weaker weight-level DP model—where graph topology is public but edge weights are sensitive—can yield meaningful privacy-utility tradeoffs, and identifies the minimal structural assumption (unit minimum edge weight) under which this is possible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253494/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a weighted, connected graph G = (V, E, w) on n vertices with every edge weight at least 1, along with a privacy parameter ε > 0, and outputs a balanced hierarchical clustering tree (dendrogram) in polynomial time. The unit-weight constraint is essential: without it, the Ω(n²/ε) additive lower bound persists even under weight-level DP. The algorithm is also robust to minimum weight α ≤ 1, degrading gracefully to O(log^1.5 n / (α·ε)) multiplicative approximation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a three-step input perturbation for balanced sparsest cut: (1) add a deterministic offset of 10 log n / ε to every edge weight to 'amplify' the gap between sparse and non-sparse cuts, (2) add independent Laplace noise Lap(1/ε) to each edge, and (3) run a polynomial-time O(√log n)-approximate balanced sparsest cut algorithm (Charikar–Chatziafratis / Krauthgamer et al.) on the perturbed graph. The unit-weight assumption is critical: it ensures the Laplace noise cannot flip a high-sparsity cut into a low-sparsity one, while the deterministic bump only inflates the true sparsest cut by O(log n / ε). The full HC algorithm recursively applies this private sparsest cut to vertex-induced subgraphs, building the dendrogram top-down; privacy is preserved via the post-processing theorem since all recursive cuts are functions of the single perturbed graph, avoiding composition. This yields an O(log^1.5 n / ε) multiplicative approximation to the optimal Dasgupta cost, matching the polylog(n) 'sweet spot' in the non-private HC literature.

## Results

Theoretically, the paper achieves an O(log^1.5 n / ε) multiplicative approximation for ε-weight DP hierarchical clustering (Theorem 1), and proves a matching Ω(n²/ε) additive error lower bound for weight-DP HC without the unit-weight assumption (Theorem 3). Experimentally, on synthetic SBM/HSBM graphs (n=150, k=5) and real-world datasets (IRIS, WINE, BOSTON), the proposed algorithm consistently produces lower Dasgupta's cost than input perturbation and linkage baselines across all tested ε values (0.01 to 2). In terms of scalability, the algorithm runs in 0.016s at n=10, comparable to the non-private algorithm (0.013s), while the Imola et al. (2023) algorithm exceeds 2 hours at the same size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: input perturbation (Lap(1/ε) noise + recursive sparsest cut), single/average/complete linkage with the same perturbation scheme, the Imola et al. (2023) DP algorithm, and the non-private algorithm. On Dasgupta's cost, the proposed algorithm outperforms input perturbation and all linkage methods by a considerable margin, especially at small ε (e.g., ε=0.01), and in some favorable runs approaches the non-private cost. Against Imola et al. (2023), the proposed algorithm is dramatically faster (polynomial vs. exponential time), completing n=10 in 0.016s versus >2 hours, and achieves a purely multiplicative O(log^1.5 n / ε) approximation rather than the O(n^2.5 √log(1/δ)/ε) additive error of the prior work. The paper also notes that the error is less sensitive to the choice of ε compared to typical DP algorithms, since the algorithm releases a combinatorial structure (HC tree) rather than a numeric value.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP hierarchical clustering framework initiated by Imola et al. (2023), who studied the problem under edge-level DP and proved an Ω(n²/ε) additive lower bound. The key departure is shifting to weight-level DP (where graph topology is public but edge weights are sensitive), combined with a unit-weight assumption that enables a dramatically better O(log^1.5 n / ε) multiplicative approximation via a simple input-perturbation scheme (additive weight bump + Laplace noise + recursive balanced sparsest cut). The algorithm builds on the well-known reduction from balanced sparsest cut to HC (Dasgupta 2016; Charikar & Chatziafratis 2017), while the lower bound adapts the random 5-cycle packing argument of Imola et al. to the weight-level setting by embedding cycles into a complete graph with tiny off-cycle weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a sharp dichotomy: with unit-weight edges, the price of privacy for HC is only polylogarithmic (comparable to the best non-private approximation), but without this assumption the Ω(n²/ε) barrier persists even under the weaker weight-level model. This clarifies exactly which structural property of the input makes private HC tractable, with direct implications for applications like financial networks and music libraries where unit-weight is natural. Open questions include whether the multiplicative gap over the non-private O(√log n) approximation can be closed, whether approximate DP (ε,δ) yields improvements, and whether the balanced sparsest cut lower bound (Ω(1/(ε log²n))) can be tightened.
