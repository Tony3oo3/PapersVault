## Problem

Offline multi-agent RL suffers from a fundamental tension: existing provably-efficient algorithms either require sample complexity that scales exponentially with the number of agents (due to distribution shift in the joint action space) or are computationally infeasible because they must evaluate non-convex objectives over the full joint policy space. The paper asks whether a natural structural assumption on the reward can simultaneously yield sample-efficient and computationally-efficient decentralized algorithms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252223/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of M i.i.d. trajectories per timestep, each containing a public context c, per-agent local states s_i, actions a_i, next local states s_i', and rewards r_i, collected under a known behavior policy ν. The output is a product policy π = ∏_i π_i where each agent's local policy π_i maps only (c, s_i) to a distribution over its own actions, forming an ε-approximate coarse correlated equilibrium. The algorithm is fully decentralized: no agent ever observes other agents' states or actions during policy execution, and the final policy is the uniform mixture over all T iterations of the actor-critic loop.

## Method

The core insight is that reward functions with bounded interaction rank K (decomposable into sub-functions each involving at most K agents) are provably more robust to distribution shift: the worst-case error under a density-ratio shift scales as C_DS^K rather than C_DS^N. The proposed algorithm DR-AC exploits this in two phases: first, it learns per-agent reward functions via least-squares regression restricted to a K-IR function class and per-agent transition models via MLE; second, it runs T iterations of a decentralized actor-critic loop where the critic estimates each agent's single-agent Q-function by Monte-Carlo rollout in the learned model (marginalizing over other agents' policies), and the actor updates via χ²-regularized mirror descent with a Bregman divergence no-regret term. The χ²-regularizer keeps each agent's policy close to the behavior policy, bounding the distribution shift that the K-IR structure can absorb, while the Bregman term guarantees no-regret convergence. Under single-agent all-policy concentrability, the sample complexity is polynomial in N with exponent depending only on K, avoiding the exponential-in-N scaling of prior methods.

## Results

Theoretically, the paper shows that under K-interaction rank (K-IR) reward structure, the sample complexity for learning an approximate CCE scales as $(2N^2)^{K-1}$ rather than exponentially in the number of agents N (Table 1). Specifically, for contextual games the gap is bounded by $C_{\sin}((2N^2)^{K-1}\epsilon)^{1/(3K-1)}$, and for Markov games with decoupled transitions the required sample size is $M \gtrsim C_{\sin}^{3K+2} C_S^K H^{6K+2}(2N^2)^{K-1}\log(NH|\mathcal{R}||\mathcal{P}|/\delta)/\epsilon^{3K+2}$. Empirically, with N=50 agents and a true 2-IR reward, the 2-IR critic architecture achieves the smallest maximum gap (near-equilibrium) by a significant margin over both the joint-action critic and the 1-IR (single-agent) critic, with the 1-IR critic performing worst.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior offline MARL works with provable guarantees: Cui and Du (2022a) and Zhang et al. (2023b, BCEL). Cui and Du require a unilateral concentrability coefficient $C_{\text{uni}}(\pi^\star)$ that can scale exponentially with N, whereas this paper's single-agent concentrability $C_S \cdot C_{\sin}$ is strictly weaker ($C_S C_{\sin} \leq C_{\text{uni}}(\pi^\star)$). Zhang et al.'s BCEL algorithm is centralized, requires solving a non-convex problem in the joint action space, and its concentrability $C_{\text{uni}}'$ can also scale exponentially; this paper shows that using K-IR function classes makes $C_{\text{uni}}' \lesssim C_{\sin}^K$, rendering their guarantee non-vacuous. Empirically, the 2-IR critic (matching the true reward structure) outperforms both the general joint-action critic and the 1-IR single-agent critic (the architecture used by VDN, QMIX, and other popular online MARL methods) on the maximum gap metric. The 1-IR critic, while standard in online cooperative MARL, fails dramatically in the offline setting because it cannot iteratively update its local reward estimates as other agents' policies change.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the policy mirror descent framework (Zhan et al., 2023a) for no-regret learning and the TD3+BC regularization approach (Fujimoto and Gu, 2021) for practical implementation, combining them into a decentralized actor-critic algorithm (DR-AC) for offline MARL. It departs from prior offline MARL work (BCEL by Zhang et al., 2023b; Cui and Du, 2022a), which is centralized and computationally infeasible due to joint-action-space optimization, by introducing the interaction rank (IR) structural assumption that enables decentralized, polynomial-sample-complexity learning. The IR concept also generalizes and formalizes the factored critic literature (VDN, QMIX), showing that VDNs are exactly 1-IR critics and that the appropriate IR level depends on the underlying reward structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first decentralized, computationally efficient offline MARL algorithm with provable guarantees, and the key practical takeaway—that the critic architecture's interaction rank should match the true reward structure—provides a clear design guideline for offline MARL practitioners. The empirical finding that standard 1-IR (VDN-style) critics fail catastrophically in offline MARL, while matching the true IR succeeds, is a useful warning for the community. Open questions include extending to general Markov games without decoupled transitions, validating on more complex and realistic environments, and understanding how to automatically estimate the appropriate IR from data.
