## Problem

Standard DP-SGD relies on decomposable (per-example) losses, but unsupervised pre-training of foundation models uses non-decomposable contrastive losses where each example interacts with all others in the batch. For such losses, the L2 sensitivity of the summed gradient grows linearly with batch size n, making the Gaussian mechanism's noise impractically large and rendering standard DP-SGD ineffective at reducing the training loss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes batches of n positive pairs (x_i, x_i') of 32×32 RGB images (e.g., two augmented views of the same CIFAR-10/100 image) and produces d-dimensional embedding vectors via a CNN encoder Φ_w. The contrastive loss compares each anchor embedding against all n key embeddings in the batch using cosine similarity, so the batch composition directly affects every example's loss. Downstream, the frozen embeddings are fed into a small MLP for k-NN classification or fine-tuning on CIFAR-100's 20 coarse categories.

## Method

Logit-DP decomposes the gradient of the contrastive loss into pairwise similarity gradients ∇_w S(Φ_w(x_i), Φ_w(x_j')) (Lemma 4.1) and shows that, under mild conditions on the loss family, the L2 sensitivity of the summed gradient is bounded by (G₁ + G₂ + (n−1)L)B where B is the per-pair clip norm (Theorem 4.2). For the canonical contrastive loss with cosine similarity, L = O(1/n), so the overall sensitivity is O(1) independent of batch size. Concretely, the algorithm clips each of the n² pairwise similarity gradients to norm B, weights them by the loss's partial derivatives, sums them, and adds Gaussian noise calibrated to the O(1) sensitivity — in contrast to Naive-DP which clips the aggregated batch gradient and inherits O(nB) sensitivity. This is validated on CIFAR-10 pre-training (generic embedding net and ResNet-18 without batch norm) and CIFAR-100 fine-tuning, where Logit-DP approaches non-private accuracy while Naive-DP fails to reduce the loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 pre-training with ResNet18, Logit-DP achieves a relative accuracy of 0.730 and Fβ score of 0.768 (relative to non-private), substantially outperforming Naive-DP (standard DP-SGD) which drops to 0.599 accuracy and 0.685 Fβ. For the smaller generic embedding net, both methods perform comparably (Logit-DP: 0.819 accuracy; Naive-DP: 0.827 accuracy). On CIFAR-100 fine-tuning with frozen embeddings, Logit-DP achieves 1.013 relative accuracy and 0.954 relative precision, while Naive-DP drops to 0.946 accuracy and 0.665 precision, confirming that Logit-DP's advantage grows with model complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Logit-DP against Naive-DP, which applies standard DP-SGD (batch-level gradient clipping) directly to the contrastive loss. On CIFAR-10 pre-training with the small embedding net, the two methods are nearly tied (0.819 vs. 0.827 relative accuracy), but Logit-DP clearly wins on ResNet18, achieving 0.730 relative accuracy versus Naive-DP's 0.599 — a 13.1-point gap in relative accuracy and 8.3 points in Fβ score. The advantage is even more pronounced on CIFAR-100 fine-tuning: Logit-DP reaches 1.013 relative accuracy and 0.954 precision, while Naive-DP falls to 0.946 accuracy and only 0.665 precision, indicating that Naive-DP's pre-trained embeddings are significantly less useful for downstream transfer. The paper also notes that Naive-DP's training loss remains nearly constant regardless of batch size, confirming that the O(n) sensitivity growth fundamentally limits its utility, whereas Logit-DP's O(1) sensitivity allows it to scale effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) to the setting of non-decomposable objective functions, specifically contrastive and spreadout regularizer losses used in unsupervised pre-training. Prior work on private pairwise learning (Huai et al., 2020; Kang et al., 2021) addressed convex or strongly convex losses and required knowledge of the encoder's Lipschitz constant, which is intractable for deep networks. The key departure here is a structural insight: by clipping pairwise similarity gradients rather than per-example gradients, the L2 sensitivity of the summed gradient becomes O(1) in batch size, whereas standard per-example clipping yields O(n) sensitivity that renders DP-SGD impractical for contrastive losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Contrastive learning is the dominant paradigm for pre-training foundation models (CLIP, SimCLR, MoCo), yet privacy-preserving training of such models has been largely infeasible due to the batch-size-dependent sensitivity of non-decomposable losses. This work provides the first DP-SGD variant that materially reduces contrastive loss while maintaining O(1) sensitivity, opening a path toward privately pre-training large embedding models. Key limitations include experiments restricted to CIFAR-10/100 with small models (embedding net, ResNet-18 without batch norm), an acknowledged O(n²) memory bottleneck for the logit gradients that is only partially addressed, and the observation that accuracy still lags the non-private baseline by a meaningful margin (e.g., 0.819 relative accuracy). Whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale pre-training or transformer-based encoders remains an open question.
