## Problem

Gradient Ascent (GA), the standard baseline for LLM unlearning, suffers from unbounded optimization and indiscriminate gradient spread across the entire vocabulary, causing unstable training and catastrophic forgetting of retained knowledge. While LoRA offers parameter efficiency for fine-tuning, naively combining it with GA/GD for unlearning fails to meet forgetting criteria within reasonable epochs and degrades generative performance due to LoRA's strong low-rank regularization. This paper is the first systematic study of LLM unlearning under the LoRA paradigm, addressing both the loss function and adapter initialization to achieve robust, efficient unlearning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (GPT-Neo 125M/1.3B/2.7B, Phi-1.5B, or Llama2-7B), a forget set D_f of memorized sequences (e.g., 32 sequences of 200 tokens from TDEC, or 1–10% of fictitious author profiles in TOFU), and a retain set D_r of auxiliary text (e.g., WikiText) to preserve. Before optimization, FILA computes per-parameter relative Fisher information (F_f / F_r) and performs a row-wise weighted SVD to initialize LoRA adapters, simultaneously updating base weights to W* = W − B*A* so model behavior is unchanged at initialization. The output is an unlearned model whose LoRA adapters (rank 4–32, applied to Q, V, and FFN layers) can be merged into base weights for zero-latency inference.

## Method

LoKU combines two novel components. First, the Inverted Hinge Loss (IHL) replaces GA's unbounded negative cross-entropy with a bounded objective L_IHL = 1 + p(x_t|x_<t) − max_{v≠x_t} p(v|x_<t), which suppresses the unwanted token while boosting only the single next-most-likely token v*, thereby eliminating gradient spread across the full vocabulary and preventing divergence. Second, FILA (Fisher-Initialization of Low-rank Adapters) computes the relative Fisher information F_rel = F_f / F_r for each linear layer to identify parameters disproportionately responsible for generating forget-set content, then initializes LoRA factors A*, B* via a closed-form row-wise weighted low-rank approximation (SVD of diag((F_rel·1)^{1/2}) W). The final training objective minimizes IHL on D_f plus standard next-token prediction loss on D_r, updating only the LoRA parameters via SGD, which jointly achieves stable unlearning with minimal downstream performance degradation.

## Results

On TDEC with GPT-Neo-125M, LoKU (IHL+FILA) using only 1.6% of parameters achieves EL₁₀=0.3% and MA=23.9% while preserving Reasoning accuracy at 42.2% (vs. 43.4% pre-unlearning), Dialogue F1 at 10.1% (vs. 9.4%), and Pile PPL at 24.0 (vs. 17.8%). Full-parameter IHL matches this on Dialogue (10.3) and PPL (18.1) but requires 100% of parameters. On TOFU with Phi-1.5B (10% forget set, rank 4), LoKU retains Retain-set Rouge-L of 0.75 and Probability of 0.85, compared to 0.41 and 0.29 for GD, while achieving a Forget Quality p-value of 1.39e-12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GA (Jang et al., 2023), LoKU dramatically outperforms on all retention metrics: GA with full parameters causes Pile PPL to explode from 17.8 to 577.8 and Dialogue F1 to drop from 9.4 to 2.6, while LoKU keeps PPL at 24.0 and Dialogue at 10.1 using only 1.6% of parameters. Against GD (Liu et al., 2022), LoKU wins decisively under LoRA: GD+LoRA degrades Pile PPL to 129.4 and Dialogue to 2.5, whereas LoKU achieves PPL 24.0 and Dialogue 10.1. On TOFU (Phi-1.5B, 10% forget set), LoKU (IHL+FILA) retains Retain-set Rouge-L of 0.75 and Probability 0.85, substantially outperforming GD (0.41, 0.29), KL (0.01, 0.00), and GA (0.01, 0.00), while DPO (0.67, 0.88) and NPO (0.45, 0.20) show intermediate but still inferior retention. Notably, FILA combined with GD or GA actually worsens performance (e.g., GA+FILA drops Retain Rouge-L to 0.00), confirming that FILA's acceleration benefit is contingent on the bounded IHL objective rather than the unbounded GA/GD losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Gradient Ascent (GA) and Gradient Difference (GD) unlearning baselines established by Jang et al. (2023) and Liu et al. (2022a), and on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) as a parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework. It departs from GA by proposing the Inverted Hinge Loss (IHL), a bounded alternative that focuses gradient updates on the true token and its single most-likely competitor rather than spreading updates across the entire vocabulary. It also departs from standard random LoRA initialization by introducing FILA, which uses relative Fisher information (borrowed from continual learning, Kirkpatrick et al. 2017) and weighted low-rank approximation (inspired by PiSSA, Meng et al. 2024) to pre-assign forget-relevant parameters to adapters. The paper is explicitly framed as the first systematic study of LLM unlearning under the LoRA paradigm, filling a gap between the unlearning and PEFT literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LoKU offers a practical path to LLM unlearning with only ~1–2% trainable parameters, making iterative or sequential deletion requests computationally feasible at scale. A key open question the paper identifies is the Streisand effect: over-unlearning can paradoxically make forgotten data more noticeable, and without oracle reference models (as in real-world GDPR scenarios), determining the optimal stopping point remains unsolved. The paper also leaves open whether the IHL+FILA synergy generalizes to much larger models (beyond 7B) and to unlearning of more semantically distributed knowledge rather than memorized sequences. The finding that FILA accelerates divergence under GD but stabilizes under IHL highlights that initialization and loss design are not independently optimizable, a nuance likely to inform future unlearning method design.
