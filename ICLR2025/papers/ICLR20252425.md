## Problem

The loss landscape of ReLU-like networks is non-differentiable at hyperplanes orthogonal to training inputs, yet no systematic characterization of the resulting stationary points existed. Previous works either restricted analysis to differentiable regions, studied only specific saddle constructions, or relied on stationarity notions (Clarke subdifferential, subgradient) that fail to capture the points where gradient descent actually stalls. Consequently, the saddle-to-saddle training dynamics from vanishing initialization remained incompletely described, with certain saddle escape types left unruled-out.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a parameter configuration P = (W, H) of a one-hidden-layer network along with a finite training set {(x_k, y_k)} and computes one-sided directional derivatives (ODDs) of the empirical squared loss in radial and tangential directions of each input weight vector. The output is a geometric classification: whether P is a local minimum, saddle, or local maximum; which hidden neurons are 'escape neurons' (identified by a first-order tangential condition); and, for network embedding operations, whether stationarity or type-1 local minimality is preserved under unit replication, inactive units, or inactive propagation.

## Method

The paper introduces a routine for computing ODDs by decomposing each input weight perturbation into a radial component (along w_i/||w_i||) and a tangential component (orthogonal to it), exploiting the fact that the loss is piecewise C^∞ and polynomial within each orthant defined by the sign pattern of w_i·x_k. Stationarity is defined via directional stationarity (all ODDs non-negative), which the authors show is strictly more informative for GD than Clarke stationarity. The key structural result (Theorem 4.2) proves that a stationary point without 'escape neurons'—hidden neurons whose tangential derivative vanishes while the underlying gradient vector is non-zero—must be a local minimum, with the converse holding for scalar-output networks. The proof aligns the coordinate system with non-differentiable edges so that Taylor expansion applies within each orthant, showing that loss-decreasing paths from non-minimum stationary points necessarily exploit second-order decrease along escape neuron parameters. For network embedding, the paper derives necessary-and-sufficient conditions (involving tangential derivatives and sign constraints on replication coefficients) under which unit replication preserves stationarity or type-1 local minimality, extending Fukumizu et al. (2019) to the non-differentiable setting.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) establishes that a stationary point of a one-hidden-layer ReLU-like network is a local minimum if and only if it contains no escape neurons (for scalar output). Corollary 4.4 shows that non-minimum stationary points with scalar output exhibit second-order loss decrease (L(P̄+δℓ)−L(P̄)∼Cδ², C<0) along escape paths, extending Kawaguchi's (2016) strict-saddle result to non-differentiable points. The numerical experiment (50 hidden neurons, 5 training samples, 500k GD epochs, step size 0.001, vanishing initialization N(0,(5×10⁻⁶)²)) confirms the theory: the loss curve exhibits exactly 3 plateaus corresponding to 2 saddle escapes and 1 local minimum, with saddle escapes coinciding with amplitude increases of small living neurons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no standard benchmark comparisons. Its contributions are framed as extensions of prior theoretical results: (1) It precludes saddle escape types that Maennel et al. (2018), Boursier et al. (2022), Chistikov et al. (2024), Boursier and Flammarion (2024), and Kumar and Haupt (2024) could not rule out, showing that all saddle escapes must involve parameter changes of escape neurons (Fact 4.7). (2) Corollary 4.6 proves that from vanishing initialization, gradient flow can only approach saddle points with fully zero-parameter neurons, matching exactly the saddle type studied by Kumar and Haupt (2024) and ruling out the 'other types' flagged in their Section 5.2. (3) For network embedding, Proposition 4.8 extends Fukumizu et al. (2019) to the non-differentiable ReLU case, providing necessary-and-sufficient conditions for unit replication to preserve stationarity and type-1 local minimality — conditions that Fukumizu et al. could not establish due to non-differentiability. (4) The directional stationarity notion (Definition 3.5) is shown to be more appropriate for characterizing GD stagnation than Clarke stationarity or the right-hand derivative criterion of Cheridito et al. (2022), as demonstrated by counterexamples in Appendix C.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the saddle-to-saddle dynamics literature for shallow ReLU networks (Maennel et al., 2018; Boursier et al., 2022; Chistikov et al., 2024; Kumar and Haupt, 2024) by precluding saddle escape types that prior works—relying on orthogonality or correlatedness assumptions—could not rule out. It adopts directional stationarity (Li et al., 2020) as the appropriate notion for capturing GD-stalling points, departing from Clarke stationarity and subgradient-based criteria that are less suited to first-order dynamics. The network embedding analysis directly extends Fukumizu et al. (2019) from differentiable to non-differentiable settings, and the strict-saddle characterization generalizes Kawaguchi (2016) to potentially non-differentiable stationary points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'escape neuron' concept provides a clean first-order certificate distinguishing local minima from saddles in non-differentiable ReLU landscapes, filling a gap that prior works left open due to simplifying assumptions. This yields a more complete picture of vanishing-initialization training dynamics and clarifies that saddle escape necessarily involves amplitude growth of small living neurons, ruling out alternative escape mechanisms. Limitations include restriction to one-hidden-layer networks, empirical squared loss, and small-scale numerical validation; extending the ODD/Taylor-expansion framework to deeper architectures and characterizing exact inter-saddle dynamics in the general (non-orthogonal, non-correlated) setting remain open.
