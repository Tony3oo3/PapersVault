## Problem

Visuo-tactile sensors are highly non-standardized, so each sensor produces data with distinct characteristics that hinder cross-sensor knowledge transfer and limit the effective data scale for any single sensor. Prior multi-sensor efforts lacked aligned multi-sensor data and failed to jointly exploit paired multi-modal information (vision, text) as a bridge to narrow sensor gaps, leaving no unified representation space that clusters by object tactile properties rather than by sensor identity.

## Input / Output

The method accepts both static tactile images (single frames) and dynamic tactile video clips (F=3 frames), unifying them into a shared 4-D tensor by replicating images along the time axis, then projecting through a shared patch layer into spatio-temporal tokens. Paired RGB images and GPT-4o-generated text descriptions of tactile attributes are consumed during the alignment stage, with a modality-missing-aware mechanism that aligns only the largest available subset per modality combination within each batch. The output is a sensor-agnostic tactile representation vector usable for material/roughness/hardness classification, grasp success prediction, and real-world manipulation policies.

## Method

AnyTouch is a two-stage multi-level framework built on an OpenCLIP-Large encoder. Stage 1 (masked modeling) randomly masks 75% of spatio-temporal tokens and reconstructs both static images and dynamic videos via a ViT decoder, plus a next-frame prediction task for videos, learning pixel-level tactile deformation details. Stage 2 (semantic alignment) performs modality-missing-aware touch-vision-text contrastive alignment using text as the anchor (with LoRA fine-tuning on the vision encoder and a frozen text encoder), combined with a novel cross-sensor matching task that trains an MLP to distinguish whether two tactile samples come from the same object position captured by different sensors, explicitly clustering same-object representations across sensors. A key architectural choice is the use of universal sensor tokens that randomly replace sensor-specific tokens during training (probability linearly increasing to 0.75), so that at inference the model can process data from unseen sensors while retaining knowledge from all seen sensors.

## Results

On the seen-sensor static perception benchmark (TAG, GelSight), AnyTouch trained on the full multi-sensor dataset achieves 80.82% material classification, 86.74% roughness, and 94.68% hardness accuracy, outperforming all single-sensor and multi-sensor baselines. On the unseen-sensor benchmark (OF 1.0, TACTO simulator), AnyTouch reaches 49.62% material accuracy, surpassing UniTouch (41.3%) and CLIP (41.00%). In the real-world fine-grained pouring task, AnyTouch with dynamic perception achieves a mean error of 1.56 g (fine-tuned) and 8.22 g (frozen encoder), beating T3 (2.33 g / 9.74 g) which was trained on ~3M samples versus AnyTouch's 2.48M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyTouch outperforms CLIP (no tactile pre-training) by a wide margin on every task: +27.9 pp on TAG material, +14.8 pp on Feel grasp, and +8.3 pp on TAG hardness. Against the single-sensor baselines, AnyTouch (full data) beats VIT-LENS-2 by 17.8 pp on TAG material (80.82 vs 63.0) and OmniBind by 13.4 pp (80.82 vs 67.45). The strongest multi-sensor baseline, TLV-Link† trained on five datasets, is surpassed by AnyTouch on TAG material (80.82 vs 74.12) and Feel grasp (80.53 vs 76.97). UniTouch, the prior SOTA cross-sensor model, is clearly outperformed when given identical training data: AnyTouch gains +21.4 pp on TAG material (82.74 vs 61.3) and +4.9 pp on Feel grasp (87.17 vs 82.3); on the unseen TACTO sensor (OF 1.0), AnyTouch leads by 5.2 pp (46.50 vs 41.3). In the real-world pouring task, AnyTouch with dynamic perception reduces the mean pouring error to 1.56 g, beating T3 (2.33 g) despite T3 using ~3M training samples versus AnyTouch's 2.48M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-sensor tactile representation learning line initiated by UniTouch (Yang et al., 2024) and TLV-Link (Cheng et al., 2024) by unifying static and dynamic perception in a single framework, drawing on OmniVore's image-video unification strategy and MAE/VideoMAE for pixel-level reconstruction. It departs from prior multi-sensor work (UniTouch, Touch2Touch) by introducing aligned multi-modal data (TacQuad) as an explicit bridge between sensors, proposing universal sensor tokens that replace sensor-specific ones during training, and adding a novel cross-sensor matching task to cluster representations by object semantics rather than sensor identity. The multi-modal alignment component builds directly on CLIP's contrastive formulation, adapted with a modality-missing-aware mechanism to handle heterogeneous dataset coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The low standardization of visuo-tactile sensors is a persistent practical bottleneck in robotic manipulation, and this paper offers a concrete data-driven pathway (aligned multi-modal dataset + unified training paradigm) to reduce sensor-specific engineering. The TacQuad dataset, with its fine-grained spatio-temporal alignment across four sensor types, is a reusable resource for the community. However, limitations remain: only four sensor types are covered, dynamic perception is validated on a single pouring task, and the TacQuad scale (72k frames) is modest relative to the 2.4M training frames from other sources. Open questions include whether the unified static-dynamic paradigm scales to non-visual tactile modalities (temperature, torque) and whether cross-sensor matching generalizes to truly novel sensor architectures beyond the four seen during training.
