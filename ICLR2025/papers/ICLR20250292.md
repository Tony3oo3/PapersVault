## Problem

Gur-Ari et al. (2018) observed that during SGD training, gradients approximately align with the top-k eigenspace of the loss Hessian (the dominant subspace), suggesting neural networks might be trainable within this tiny subspace. This paper asks whether that alignment is actually functional for learning or merely a byproduct of the optimization dynamics, a question with direct implications for efficient training and understanding why high-dimensional optimization does not suffer from the curse of dimensionality.

## Input / Output

The experiments take standard supervised classification inputs (MNIST-5k, CIFAR10-5k, SST2-1k) and train MLP, CNN, Transformer, VGG11, or ResNet8 architectures under various optimizers (SGD, GD, SGDM, Adam, SAM). The key diagnostic output is the alignment metric χ_k(∇L(θ_t)) — the fraction of the gradient lying in the dominant subspace — tracked alongside Hessian eigenvalue spectra and training loss. The critical experimental output is the training loss trajectory when updates are projected onto (Dom-SGD) or out of (Bulk-SGD) the dominant subspace, revealing whether the aligned component actually drives loss decrease.

## Method

The paper introduces a projection-based diagnostic: Dom-SGD projects each update onto the dominant subspace (P_k(θ_t)g_t), while Bulk-SGD projects onto its orthogonal complement (P_k^⊥(θ_t)g_t). Surprisingly, Dom-SGD halts training loss decrease while Bulk-SGD trains as effectively as unmodified SGD, despite discarding the majority of each update. The authors trace the spurious alignment to stochastic noise interacting with an ill-conditioned-valley-like landscape in the gradient-flow regime (alignment vanishes under full-batch GD), and to the self-stabilization mechanism in the Edge-of-Stability regime. They extend the analysis to SAM (showing Dom-SAM also fails) and to momentum/Adam optimizers, introducing effective learning rates (Dom-LR and Bulk-LR) that reveal momentum and adaptive methods amplify the bulk component, partially explaining their practical success.

## Results

The paper's central finding is qualitative rather than a single accuracy number: Dom-SGD (projecting each update onto the dominant subspace) fails to further decrease training loss across all tested architectures (MLP/MNIST-5k, CNN/CIFAR10-5k, Transformer/SST2-1k), while Bulk-SGD (projecting onto the orthogonal bulk subspace) matches original SGD performance despite removing the majority of the update. The alignment metric χ_k(∇L) reaches ~1 for SGD but drops to ~0 for full-batch GD, confirming stochastic noise as the cause. In Table 1, Adam with momentum amplifies the Bulk-LR to 2.66 (vs. Dom-LR of 0.0004), explaining its superior training speed over plain GD (Bulk-LR = 0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM, the paper shows that SAM updates align with the dominant subspace (χ_10 ≈ 1) once sharpness saturates near the SAM-edge, yet Dom-SAM fails to decrease training loss while Bulk-SAM matches SAM, confirming the alignment is spurious. Against Adam, the paper demonstrates that Adam updates are only partially aligned with the dominant subspace (χ_10 in range 0.05–0.5, decreasing over training), and Dom-Adam fails to reduce loss; Adam's effectiveness is attributed to its amplification of Bulk-LR (2.66 vs. 0.0004 Dom-LR with momentum). Against SGDM (SGD with momentum), updates are partially aligned (χ_10 in range 0.5–0.8), Dom-SGDM fails to decrease loss, and momentum amplifies Bulk-LR from 0.01 (plain GD) to 0.083. In all three comparisons, the projected-onto-dominant-subspace variant consistently fails while the bulk-subspace variant matches or exceeds the original optimizer, establishing that the bulk subspace is where learning actually occurs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly challenges the foundational observation of Gur-Ari et al. (2018) that gradients align with the dominant eigenspace of the loss Hessian during SGD training, showing this alignment is 'spurious' and does not reflect where learning actually occurs. It extends the Edge of Stability framework (Cohen et al., 2021; Damian et al., 2023) and Sharpness-Aware Minimization (Foret et al., 2021) by demonstrating that the same spurious alignment phenomenon holds in those regimes as well, with distinct mechanistic causes (self-stabilization vs. stochastic noise). The paper also connects to the momentum/Adam literature by introducing effective learning rates that reveal these optimizers amplify the bulk subspace component, offering a new explanation for their practical success.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result fundamentally reframes how we interpret gradient alignment observations in deep learning: the dominant subspace, despite capturing most of the gradient norm, is not where progress is made, and the small bulk component drives training. This has practical implications for understanding why momentum and adaptive methods work (they amplify the bulk component) and cautions against subspace-restricted training approaches. Open questions include developing theory beyond the 2D toy model, understanding the sharpness dynamics of Dom-SGD vs. Bulk-SGD, and exploring implications for generalization. The finding also suggests that prior work on training in low-dimensional subspaces (Li et al., 2023; Gressmann et al., 2020) succeeded precisely because those subspaces were not the dominant Hessian eigenspace.
