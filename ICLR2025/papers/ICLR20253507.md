## Problem

While many-shot in-context learning (ICL) with long-context LLMs shows performance gains from scaling examples, it remains unclear whether these gains stem from expanded contextual knowledge or from a small subset of disproportionately influential examples. This ambiguity matters because naively dumping all available examples into the context is computationally expensive and may even degrade performance, yet no principled method existed to identify and exploit the most effective demonstration subsets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled dataset of input-answer pairs (typically 150–200 samples) and a black-box LLM with no gradient access. It produces an optimized set of demonstration tuples of the form {input, model-generated reasoning, final output}, where the reasoning paths are regenerated by the LLM using the optimized subset as in-context seeds. The output is evaluated on a held-out test split, and the method is designed to work within the reinforced ICL setup where intermediate rationales are modifiable but inputs and labels are fixed.

## Method

BRIDGE (Bayesian Refinement and Iterative Demonstration Generation for Examples) alternates between an Optimize step and a Generate step over K outer-loop rounds. In the Optimize step, a budget-controlled Bayesian optimizer with a Gaussian Process surrogate (Matérn 2.5 kernel over example-subset overlap) searches for the best demonstration subset; the key design choice is formulating the search as a bi-objective problem—maximizing validation accuracy while minimizing subset cardinality—solved via random Tchebyshev scalarization with a per-iteration scalar β_t ~ Uniform(0.25, 1) and expected improvement as the acquisition function. In the Generate step, the optimized subset is used as seed demonstrations to prompt the LLM to regenerate reasoning paths over the full training set, expanding the example pool back to the many-shot regime with improved rationales. This iterative few-to-many-to-few cycle progressively refines both the selection and the quality of demonstrations, with the sparsity regularization preventing overfitting to the validation set and discouraging memorization of prior-round examples.

## Results

On 16 BBH tasks with gemini-1.5-pro-001, BRIDGE achieves an average test accuracy of 87.1% at its peak (2G milestone), outperforming reinforced ICL (79.6%) by 7.5 points and iterative reinforced ICL (82.3%) by 4.8 points. On GSM-Hard, BRIDGE reaches 73.3% execution accuracy versus 69.9% for reinforced ICL. On the BIRD text-to-SQL benchmark, CHASE+BRIDGE achieves 62.0% execution accuracy, surpassing the CHASE prompt baseline (60.1%) and LoRA fine-tuning with 256 training samples (58.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against reinforced ICL (Agarwal et al., 2024), BRIDGE consistently wins: by 7.5 points on average BBH accuracy (87.1% vs. 79.6%) and by 3.4 points on GSM-Hard (73.3% vs. 69.9%), confirming that the optimize step is critical beyond simple iterative regeneration. Against the CHASE-SQL prompt (Pourreza et al., 2025) on BIRD, BRIDGE adds 1.9 points of execution accuracy (62.0% vs. 60.1%), with gains concentrated on challenging-level problems (49.0% vs. 40.7%). Against LoRA fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2021) on BIRD, BRIDGE with only 128 unified train/validation samples outperforms LoRA trained on 256 samples (62.0% vs. 58.2%) and 1024 samples (62.0% vs. 60.2%), though LoRA with the full 9428-sample training set (63.8%) still edges out BRIDGE. The ablation in Table 5 further shows that replacing Bayesian optimization with random search in the optimize step degrades performance at every BRIDGE milestone, validating the BO component.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRIDGE builds directly on the reinforced ICL framework of Agarwal et al. (2024), which established that filtering to correctly-predicted examples improves many-shot ICL, by adding an intelligent selection layer (Bayesian optimization over example subsets) and an iterative regeneration loop that uses the selected subset as seed demonstrations. The optimize step draws on combinatorial Bayesian optimization (Daulton et al., 2022) and Gaussian process surrogates, while the generate step echoes the self-improving rationale bootstrapping of STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022) and Self-Instruct (Wang et al., 2023). A key departure from prior few-shot example selection work (Zhao et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2024b) is the explicit demonstration that such selection remains beneficial even in the many-shot regime where all examples fit in context, and the use of Tchebyshev scalarization to jointly optimize performance and sparsity without pre-specifying subset size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that many-shot ICL performance is disproportionately driven by a small subset of influential examples—and that naively scaling examples can plateau or even degrade performance even when the overall trend appears positive—provides practitioners with a principled reason to invest in demonstration curation rather than simply filling context windows. BRIDGE offers a model-agnostic, black-box algorithm that achieves 3–7% improvements over reinforced ICL across Gemini, Claude, and Mistral models on reasoning and code-generation tasks, positioning it as a practical complement to (and in some settings a competitor of) parameter-efficient fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to white-box settings where internal representations could reduce optimization cost, whether sample-dependent demonstration selection at test time could further improve efficiency, and validation on task types beyond reasoning-heavy benchmarks.
