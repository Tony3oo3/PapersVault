## Problem

RanPAC's core hypothesis—that random projection onto a higher-dimensional space makes features more linearly separable—was empirically supported but never theoretically justified, and naively increasing the projection dimension is infeasible because the closed-form classifier requires inverting a d'×d' Gram matrix. Additionally, RanPAC only adapts the backbone to the first task, causing later-task representations to overlap and limiting performance as the task sequence progresses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251049/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-R/CUB-200) and produces a single class label per image. At inference, no task ID is available; instead, a two-step self-improvement process first predicts a prompt via an auxiliary head on the un-prompted backbone, then refines it using the main multi-view classifier's prediction to select the final task-specific prompt. The final label is determined by majority voting across up to 2^K−1 combined-view predictions, where K is the number of atomic views.

## Method

BoostCL theoretically proves that random projection onto a d'-dimensional space increases the Bayes margin of each training instance by a factor of O(√d'), explaining why RP improves both per-task generalization and task separability in CL. To avoid the infeasible inversion of a single huge Gram matrix, the method uses K frozen random projection matrices to create K atomic views of manageable dimension d'_a, and shows (Theorem 4.3) that the optimal linear classifier on any combined 'huge view' decomposes into the sum of its atomic-view classifiers. Diversity among the K views is enforced via an AdaBoost-inspired scheme where each k-th view is trained with sample weights that up-weight misclassified examples from the (k−1)-th view, making the views complementary rather than redundant. At test time, all 2^K−1 possible huge-view predictions are generated and a thresholded majority-voting strategy selects the final label. A task-adaptive backbone assigns each task a distinct prompt (trained with cross-entropy plus contrastive regularization against old-task prototypes), and a self-improvement process uses the main classifier's own prediction to correct potentially wrong initial prompt selections, reducing feature shift between training and inference.

## Results

BoostCL achieves the best Final Average Accuracy (FAA) across all four benchmarks: 96.55% on Split CIFAR-100, 80.42% on Split ImageNet-R, 96.73% on 5-Datasets, and 93.03% on Split CUB-200, outperforming the strongest baselines by 2–4% FAA. The method also attains the lowest Final Forgetting Measure (FFM) on Split CIFAR-100 (1.15) and 5-Datasets (0.30). Notably, even the variant without random projection (BoostCL-m w/o RP; K=13) surpasses RanPAC by ~1% on Split CIFAR-100 and Split ImageNet-R, confirming the multi-view ensemble strategy is the primary driver of gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BoostCL outperforms all nine baselines on every benchmark. On Split CIFAR-100, it leads the closest prompt-based competitor HiDe-Prompt (92.61%) by 3.94% FAA and the CP-based RanPAC (92.20%) by 4.35%. On Split ImageNet-R, the gap over RanPAC (77.90%) is 2.52% and over SLCA (77.00%) is 3.42%. On 5-Datasets, BoostCL exceeds HiDe-Prompt (93.92%) by 2.81% and RanPAC (82.85%) by 13.88%, the latter reflecting RanPAC's weakness under large domain shifts. On Split CUB-200, the advantage over RanPAC (90.30%) is 2.73% and over HiDe-Prompt (86.62%) is 6.41%. The single-view variant BoostCL-s already surpasses all baselines, confirming the task-adaptive prompt backbone is independently effective, while the multi-view ensemble adds a further 1.4–2.5% FAA on top.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RanPAC's class-prototype-based continual learning framework, which projects pre-trained ViT features into a high-dimensional space via random projection and trains a closed-form linear classifier. The key departures are: (1) providing the first theoretical proof that random projection increases instance margin at rate O(√d'), explaining why RanPAC's empirically-motivated hypothesis works; (2) replacing a single high-dimensional view with K smaller atomic views trained sequentially using an adaptive AdaBoost/SAMME principle — the first application of boosting to CL; and (3) integrating task-specific prompts (following HiDE/DualPrompt) with a self-improvement prompt-selection process to address feature shifts from mispredicted prompts. The multi-view decomposition also resolves the computational infeasibility of inverting a d'×d' Gram matrix at very large dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical characterization of RP's margin-increasing effect provides a principled foundation for understanding why high-dimensional projection helps in CL, and the multi-view + boosting strategy offers a practical workaround for the Gram-matrix inversion bottleneck that limits RanPAC-style methods. However, the ensemble classifier incurs 15× storage overhead and increased forward time, and the self-improvement process adds inference latency. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes when downstream tasks are substantially different from the pre-training distribution (where the prompt predictor may fail), and whether the voting strategy over 2^K−1 huge views scales practically as K grows. The finding that the multi-view ensemble without any random projection still outperforms RanPAC suggests the diversity mechanism itself is a significant contributor, potentially opening avenues for ensemble CL methods beyond the RP paradigm.
