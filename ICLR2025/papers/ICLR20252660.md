## Problem

LIF-based spiking neural networks are limited by their scalar membrane potential and deterministic, sequential spike generation via the Heaviside function, which creates a parallel-training bottleneck and restricts their ability to capture long-range dependencies. Prior attempts to combine SSMs with spiking models (e.g., BinaryS4D) still rely on LIF neurons and real-valued inputs, forfeiting the energy efficiency that sparse spike-based computation provides on neuromorphic hardware.

## Input / Output

The model accepts real-valued sequences of varying modalities and lengths—permuted MNIST pixel sequences (784), Speech Command waveforms (16000 samples), IMDB byte-level text (4K), ListOps (2K), and image pixel sequences (1K for CIFAR-10/Pathfinder)—and produces a single class label via a pooling-based decoder. A key design choice is that the real-valued input is first encoded into N parallel binary spike trains (one per neuron) through a linear layer followed by a SpikeSampler, so all internal computation operates on sparse {0,1} sequences rather than floating-point values.

## Method

P-SpikeSSM replaces the LIF neuron with a probabilistic SSM-based neuronal model whose n-dimensional hidden state (initialized with HiPPO-legS matrices) serves as a richer membrane potential. Spike generation is stochastic: a Bernoulli variable S_t is sampled with probability p_s[t] = σ(Ch[t]), and the recurrent dynamics are reformulated as a non-circular convolution over the sparse input spike sequence, enabling fully parallel forward and backward passes without BPTT. A surrogate gradient of E[S_t] = p_s[t] resolves the non-differentiability of the sampling operation during backpropagation. For scalability, the architecture stacks K encoder layers, each containing N P-SpikeSSM neurons followed by a SpikeMixer block (FC + GELU) for inter-neuron communication and a FuseClamp block (residual + batch norm + clamp to [0,1]) that produces the spiking probability for the next layer's SpikeSampler. Because inputs are binary spikes, the dominant per-layer cost reduces to accumulation (ACC) operations scaled by the input firing rate, yielding a reported 36× energy reduction over an isoparametric S4 model on ListOps.

## Results

On the Long Range Arena (LRA) benchmark, P-SpikeSSM achieves 58.20% on ListOps, 81.20% on Text, 88.53% on Retrieval, 82.40% on Image, and 84.80% on Pathfinder, establishing state-of-the-art performance among spiking architectures. On permuted sequential MNIST (psMNIST), the model reaches 98.4% accuracy with just two encoder layers of 400 neurons, surpassing all prior SNN baselines and approaching the non-spiking S4 result of 98.7%. On the Speech Command SC10 dataset, P-SpikeSSM achieves 95.6% accuracy with four encoder layers of 256 neurons, outperforming all listed non-spiking baselines except S4 (98.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest spiking competitor, SpikingLMUFormer, P-SpikeSSM wins decisively on all five LRA tasks (e.g., 58.20 vs. 37.30 on ListOps, 88.53 vs. 79.76 on Retrieval, 82.40 vs. 55.65 on Image). Against BinaryS4D (Stan & Rhodes, 2023), which uses LIF neurons over an SSM but still requires floating-point MACs, P-SpikeSSM wins on ListOps (58.20 vs. 54.80), Retrieval (88.53 vs. 85.30), Image (82.40 vs. 82.00), and Pathfinder (84.80 vs. 82.60), while trailing slightly on Text (81.20 vs. 82.50). Compared to the non-spiking S4 (Original), P-SpikeSSM wins on Text (81.20 vs. 76.02) and Retrieval (88.53 vs. 87.09) but is marginally behind on ListOps (58.20 vs. 58.35), Image (82.40 vs. 87.26), and Pathfinder (84.80 vs. 86.05); against the stronger S4 (Improved), it trails on all tasks but remains within 1–10 points. On psMNIST, P-SpikeSSM (98.4%) surpasses all SNN baselines (DSD-SNN 97.3%, SpikingLMUFormer 97.9%) and the non-spiking Transformer (97.9%), reaching within 0.3% of the non-spiking S4 (98.7%). All transformer-based variants (Transformer, Sparse Transformer, Linformer, Linear Transformer, FLASH-quad, TransnormerT2) are outperformed on every LRA task by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the S4/Mamba line of structured state space models (Gu et al., 2021a; Gu & Dao, 2023) and the HiPPO initialization framework (Gu et al., 2020a), repurposing the SSM as a probabilistic neuronal model that operates on binary spike sequences rather than real-valued inputs. It departs from prior SNN+SSM integrations (Stan & Rhodes, 2023; Du et al., 2024) that appended LIF neurons on top of SSM outputs, arguing that the sequential Heaviside-based spike generation creates a training bottleneck and that the SSM's own temporal dynamics make LIF neurons functionally redundant. The convolutional kernel formulation follows Gu et al. (2021b), but is applied here to sparse spike inputs, enabling accumulation-only computation and fully parallel training via a stochastic SpikeSampler layer with a Bernoulli surrogate gradient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully spiking SSM-based architecture to achieve competitive or SOTA performance on the Long Range Arena benchmark, suggesting that the spiking domain is no longer confined to short-sequence or vision tasks. The 36× arithmetic energy efficiency estimate over non-spiking S4 (ListOps) is compelling for neuromorphic deployment, though the analysis is preliminary (arithmetic-only, no memory I/O, no on-chip validation). Open questions include whether the energy savings materialize on actual hardware like Loihi 2, whether the non-local batch normalization in FuseClamp can be replaced without further accuracy loss, and whether the stochastic sampling approach extends to generative or autoregressive sequence tasks beyond classification.
