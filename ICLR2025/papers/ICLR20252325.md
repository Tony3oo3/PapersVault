## Problem

Off-the-shelf foundation models (DINO, CLIP, ViT) are scalable across datasets but underperform dataset-specific retrieval models, and standard dimensionality reduction (PCA, VAE) is not optimized for cosine-similarity-based retrieval. The paper identifies that the underlying distribution of foundation model embeddings—specifically unequal variance across dimensions—negatively impacts cosine similarity discriminative power, and that existing reduction methods require separate training per embedding size, making them impractical under varying compute and bandwidth constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252325/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes d-dimensional image embeddings produced by a frozen foundation model (DINO-S8/B16, DINOv2-Small/Large, CLIP, or ViT) computed over the reference set R only; no query images or labels are needed during training. AE-SVC outputs a full-dimension latent embedding z in R^d with enforced orthogonality, zero mean, and unit per-dimension variance. (SS)2D then outputs a single adaptive embedding whose first m dimensions (for any m in a predefined set of target sizes) independently preserve retrieval performance, enabling the user to truncate the vector to match available compute, storage, or bandwidth without retraining.

## Method

AE-SVC trains an autoencoder with a reconstruction loss plus three distributional constraints on the latent code: a covariance (orthogonality) loss penalizing off-diagonal terms, a mean-centering loss, and a unit-variance loss. The paper proves that under a scale-invariance relaxation, the variance of the cosine similarity distribution equals the sum of sigma_i^4 divided by (sum of sigma_j^2)^2, whose global minimum occurs when all per-dimension variances are equal—exactly what the variance constraint enforces—thereby maximizing retrieval discriminative power. (SS)2D then performs embedding-level similarity-preserving distillation: it computes a teacher cosine similarity matrix C from the AE-SVC latent codes and, for each target dimension m, minimizes the KL divergence between the student's m-dimensional similarity matrix and C, summing over all m in one training run. This single-shot, nested-dimension objective (inspired by Matryoshka representation learning) yields an adaptive embedding that closely approaches the per-dimension upper bound (SSD) without requiring separate networks per size.

## Results

AE-SVC achieves an average 15.5% improvement in mAP@k over the default off-the-shelf foundation model embeddings (measured via PCA) across four datasets (InShop, SOP, Pittsburgh30K, TokyoVal) and multiple foundation models (DINO, DINOv2, ViT, CLIP). The largest single improvement is 24% on InShop with DINOv2-Large at full embedding size. Applying (SS)2D on top of AE-SVC provides a further up-to-10% improvement at smaller embedding sizes (e.g., 64-128 dimensions), closely approaching the per-dimension SSD upper bound while requiring only a single training run.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AE-SVC consistently outperforms PCA (the default off-the-shelf embedding) across all four datasets and all foundation models, with average gains of 15.5% in mAP@k. Against PCA Whitening and TLDR (a retrieval-specific dimensionality reduction method using only covariance loss), AE-SVC achieves significantly greater improvements, attributed to the additional variance and mean-centering constraints applied at the representation level rather than the decoder output. For (SS)2D, the method outperforms VAE at smaller embedding sizes while closely approaching the per-dimension SSD upper bound, all from a single training run rather than separate networks per dimension. When compared to the dataset-specific SOTA model CosPlace, AE-SVC provides only a 2% improvement (vs. 10% for foundation models), confirming that the distributional constraints are most beneficial for off-the-shelf models whose embedding distributions are less optimized for cosine similarity search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) using off-the-shelf foundation models (DINO, DINOv2, CLIP, ViT) for image retrieval, where prior work either uses them as-is or adapts them with labeled data; (2) distribution constraints from semi-supervised representation learning (Barlow Twins, VICReg), which the authors are the first to apply within an autoencoder specifically for retrieval; and (3) similarity-preserving knowledge distillation and Matryoshka-style adaptive embeddings, which the authors repurpose from network-to-network distillation into embedding-level dimensionality reduction. The key conceptual departure is the observation that the *variance structure* of foundation model embeddings, rather than their content, is the bottleneck for cosine similarity retrieval, and that simple orthogonality/mean-centering/unit-variance constraints on an autoencoder latent space fix this without any labeled data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a lightweight, fully unsupervised autoencoder can boost foundation model retrieval by up to 15.5% and then compress embeddings adaptively (via (SS)₂D) with only ~10% further loss, enabling faster retrieval at smaller embedding sizes without retraining per dimension. A notable limitation is that AE-SVC helps foundation models (~10%) far more than dataset-specific models (~2%), suggesting the distribution pathology is specific to how foundation models are pre-trained; the paper acknowledges the root cause of this discrepancy remains unknown. Open questions include whether the variance-aware property can be baked directly into foundation model training objectives, and whether the theoretical analysis (which relies on a relaxation of the cosine denominator) holds tightly in low-dimensional regimes.
