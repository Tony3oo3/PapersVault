## Problem

Existing theoretical studies of induction heads in transformers rely on Markov chains with a fixed causal structure, which cannot capture the dynamic, context-dependent causal relationships characteristic of natural language. This paper asks: how do transformers learn to select the correct causal structure in-context when multiple structures (e.g., different lags) are possible, and what circuit implements this selection?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253128/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a token sequence of length T over a finite alphabet, generated from an interleaved Markov chain whose lag k is drawn uniformly from a known set K but is unknown at inference time; the first k̂ = max(K) tokens are sampled from the stationary distribution to ensure a fixed number of independent variables. The output is the predicted distribution over the next token s_{T+1}. The key constraint is that the model must infer which lag generated the sequence purely from the observed tokens, making this an in-context model selection problem rather than a fixed-structure prediction task.

## Method

The paper introduces a 3-layer disentangled attention-only transformer that implements a 'selective induction head' circuit. Layer 1 uses an attention matrix encoding log(P*) on the semantic block and a ±λ positional block to extract normalized transition probabilities p̃_{i,k} for each candidate lag k. Layer 2 uses K heads with diagonal attention patterns (attending to every K-th token with head-specific offsets) to aggregate all past transition probabilities into the current token's embedding without mixing overlapping transitions. Layer 3 computes softmax weights proportional to exp(β/(T−k̂) · Σ p̃_{i,k}) for each lag, then copies the token at position i−k*+1 where k* is the argmax lag, and the output layer applies P* to produce the next-token distribution. Theoretically, the paper proves this predictor asymptotically converges to the maximum likelihood estimator, and empirically shows that both standard and disentangled transformers trained from scratch with Adam reproduce the same attention patterns and match the construction's performance.

## Results

The paper's main result (Proposition 1) provides an explicit 3-layer disentangled transformer construction that implements a selective induction head, achieving performance matching the Maximum Likelihood (ML) estimator on interleaved Markov chain tasks with lags K={1,2,3} and K={1,2,3,4,5}. Empirically, both standard and disentangled transformers trained from scratch with Adam (lr=0.001, sequence length 128, alphabet size 5) match the theoretical construction's KL divergence, while 2-layer transformers fail to solve the task entirely. The constructed predictor asymptotically converges to ML as β→∞, and attention maps of trained transformers show remarkable structural similarity to the theoretical construction across all three layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but rather validates its theoretical construction against (i) the Bayes-optimal solution (BMA), (ii) the Maximum Likelihood estimator (ML), and (iii) transformers trained from scratch via gradient descent. The 3-layer construction with K heads in the second layer matches ML performance exactly on all tested lag configurations, confirming that the selective induction head mechanism is sufficient for optimal in-context causal structure selection. Trained standard and disentangled transformers (both with learned embeddings) also match the construction's performance, and their attention maps exhibit the same three-layer structure: transition probability extraction, diagonal aggregation, and selective copying. Notably, when restricted to a single head in the second layer, trained transformers sometimes outperform the theoretical construction, suggesting gradient descent discovers more efficient (but harder to interpret) aggregation strategies. The paper also demonstrates that 2-layer transformers fundamentally cannot solve the task regardless of head count, establishing 3 layers as a necessary depth for this mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Markov chain framework for studying induction heads (Nichani et al., 2024; Edelman et al., 2024) by replacing the fixed causal structure with interleaved Markov chains of different lags, thereby introducing an in-context causal structure selection problem. It builds on the disentangled transformer architecture of Friedman et al. (2023) to enable a fully interpretable 3-layer construction, and positions 'selective induction heads' as a fundamentally new circuit type beyond the standard induction heads of Olsson et al. (2022), where the copying position is determined dynamically rather than fixed by the data's unique structure. The paper also connects to the broader in-context algorithm selection literature (Bai et al., 2024; Yadlowsky et al., 2023) but grounds the selection problem in sequence modelling with explicit causal structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first interpretable construction of a transformer circuit that performs in-context model selection over causal structures, bridging mechanistic interpretability and statistical model selection. The close alignment between the hand-constructed attention maps and those of gradient-trained transformers (both disentangled and standard) lends strong credibility to the selective induction head as a real mechanism. However, the main theoretical guarantee (Claim 1) is only proven for special cases (two lags, independent lags, no normalization), and the empirical validation is confined to small synthetic tasks (alphabet size 5, sequence length 128), leaving open whether the mechanism generalizes to richer settings. The finding that 2 layers provably cannot solve the task while 3 suffice is architecturally informative but the practical relevance to large-scale language modelling remains speculative.
