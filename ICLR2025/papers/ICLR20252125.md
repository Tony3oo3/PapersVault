## Problem

Diffusion-based generative models achieve strong point-wise accuracy in physical field reconstruction but systematically violate governing PDEs and boundary conditions, because the constraint manifold defined by nonlinear PDEs is non-convex and intractable for projection-based enforcement. Directly adding physics-informed losses to end-to-end or score-matching objectives leads to convergence failures due to poor initialization and non-convex optimization landscapes. The paper addresses this by decoupling high-quality field generation from physics compliance into separate training stages.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse or downsampled measurements (e.g., 8× Fourier-space downsampled PIV velocity fields, or 99% randomly masked vorticity/concentration fields) which are first upsampled to the target grid via bi-cubic or Voronoi interpolation before entering the network. Training uses randomly cropped local patches from high-resolution fields (256×256 or 64×64×64) for data efficiency, while inference operates on the full domain. The output is a full-resolution spatiotemporal field (velocity, vorticity, or concentration) that simultaneously matches the observations and satisfies the governing equations, with inference targeting ≤10 NFEs.

## Method

PalSB employs a two-stage training pipeline on a simplified U-Net (residual blocks + channel-wise linear attention). Stage 1 pretrains a Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge (I²SB formulation) that learns the conditional mapping from interpolated low-fidelity input to high-fidelity output via a DDPM-style score-matching objective on the analytic posterior, using patch-based training for scalability. Stage 2 (physics-aligned finetuning) unrolls the multi-step sampling path and backpropagates a combined physics-informed loss (PDE residual) plus a regression loss through the trajectory, truncating gradients after the last B steps to control memory—inspired by RLHF-style reward backpropagation for diffusion models. At inference, boundary-aware sampling pads the input according to the specific boundary condition before interpolation, and early-stop sampling uses smaller step sizes with an early termination to reduce discretization error within a fixed NFE budget.

## Results

Across three 2D physical systems (cylinder flow, Kolmogorov flow, Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion) and two reconstruction tasks (Fourier-space interpolation and real-space interpolation), PalSB achieves the lowest normalized equation residual (nER) in nearly all settings, indicating superior physics compliance. For example, on Kolmogorov flow FI, PalSB reduces nER to 2.40×10⁻² versus 7.99×10⁻² for the non-finetuned SB baseline and 3.91×10⁻¹ for E2E+PF, while maintaining competitive point-wise accuracy (nRMSE 0.081 vs. 0.077 for SB). On the reaction-diffusion system FI task, PalSB achieves an nER of 8.91×10⁻⁸, orders of magnitude below all baselines. The method also extends to a 3D compressible Navier-Stokes case (PDEBench), where PalSB achieves nRMSE of 0.093 (FI) and 0.185 (RI) with minimal degradation under 5% Gaussian noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the FNO-based E2E+PF baseline, PalSB achieves substantially lower nER across all six case-task combinations (e.g., 2.40×10⁻² vs. 3.91×10⁻¹ on Kolmogorov flow FI; 8.91×10⁻⁸ vs. 8.17×10⁻⁵ on RDGS FI), while matching or slightly exceeding point-wise accuracy (nRMSE). The E2E+PF model's physics-informed finetuning fails to converge well on highly nonlinear systems due to non-convex optimization, a limitation the paper attributes to poor initialization. Against PIDM (a DDPM-based method with classifier-free guidance on equation residuals), PalSB dominates on both accuracy and physics compliance: on Kolmogorov flow FI, PalSB achieves nRMSE 0.081 vs. PIDM's 0.512 and nER 2.40×10⁻² vs. 3.45×10⁻¹. PIDM's performance degrades severely on the RI task (nER up to 2.73×10¹ on Kolmogorov flow RI) because SDEdit-based reconstruction from sparsely masked inputs disrupts the diffusion dynamics. The non-finetuned SB variant (same architecture without physics-aligned finetuning) matches PalSB on point-wise accuracy but shows 3–10× higher nER, confirming that the physics-aligned finetuning stage is the key differentiator for physical compliance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the I²SB formulation (Liu et al., 2023b) for diffusion Schrödinger bridges, repurposing it from image-to-image translation to physical field reconstruction from sparse measurements. The physics-aligned finetuning stage adapts the reward-backpropagation-through-sampling-path technique from Prabhudesai et al. (2023) and Clark et al. (2023), originally developed for aligning text-to-image diffusion models with human preferences, to enforce PDE compliance. The key conceptual departure from prior work (PIDM, PINN-based E2E models) is the two-stage decomposition: first learning a high-quality DSB mapping, then optimizing physics-informed losses on full multi-step generated samples rather than single-step denoising predictions, which avoids the non-convex optimization failures that plague direct PINN training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent gap in data-driven field reconstruction: models that achieve high point-wise accuracy often violate governing PDEs, while physics-informed approaches struggle with convergence on highly nonlinear systems. The two-stage PalSB framework provides a practical recipe that decouples data-driven quality from physics compliance, and the boundary-aware and early-stop sampling strategies offer practical efficiency gains (≤10 NFEs). Key limitations include the soft-constraint formulation (residual equation violations remain), validation primarily on 2D systems, and the computational cost of unrolling the sampling path during finetuning. Open questions include whether exact constraint satisfaction is achievable, how the approach scales to 3D and larger domains, and whether the physics-aligned finetuning generalizes to systems without known ground truth for the regression regularization term.
