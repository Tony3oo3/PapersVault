## Problem

Topological deep learning (TDL) models operating on simplicial complexes lack large-scale, intrinsically higher-order datasets for reliable benchmarking. Existing higher-order datasets are either small, derived from simpler graph or time-series data, or annotated in ways that do not require genuine higher-order reasoning, making it impossible to assess whether higher-order structures are truly necessary or whether TDL models can capture topological invariants.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250276/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each input is a purely combinatorial triangulation of a closed connected 2- or 3-manifold (≤10 vertices), stored as a list of top-level simplices with no geometric coordinates or intrinsic vertex/edge signals. Feature vectors are either randomly generated or derived from upper/lower connectivity indices of simplices. Outputs are topological labels: Betti numbers (β₀–β₃), homeomorphism type (e.g., S², T², Klein bottle, S³, S²×S¹), and orientability, all computed with integer coefficients. The dataset contains 43,138 surface triangulations and 250,359 three-manifold triangulations, with labels distributed across genus 0–7 and multiple homeomorphism classes.

## Method

MANTRA is a curated assemblage of all known triangulations of closed connected 2- and 3-manifolds up to 10 vertices, originally compiled by Frank H. Lutz, packaged in raw JSON and PyTorch Geometric formats with precomputed Betti numbers, torsion coefficients, genus, and orientability. The benchmark evaluates 12 architectures—five graph-based (MLP, GCN, GAT, UniMP, TAG) and seven simplicial complex-based (SAN, SCCN, SCCNN, SCN, CellMP, Cellular Transformer, DECT)—on three supervised tasks: Betti number regression, homeomorphism type classification, and orientability detection, using stratified 60/20/20 splits and AUROC/accuracy metrics. A key diagnostic experiment applies one barycentric subdivision to test triangulations and re-evaluates trained models, revealing that all models performing above chance suffer significant performance degradation, indicating they fail to learn topologically invariant representations. The paper concludes that while higher-order models are necessary (they consistently outperform graph baselines on non-trivial topological properties), current TDL architectures are not yet truly topological in the sense of being invariant to combinatorial refinements that preserve the underlying manifold.

## Results

On the full surface dataset (2-M⁰), simplicial complex-based (T) models outperform graph-based (G) models on all non-trivial topological tasks: β₁ AUROC 0.68 vs. 0.50, β₂ AUROC 0.59 vs. 0.50, homeomorphism type AUROC 0.69 vs. 0.47, and orientability AUROC 0.56 vs. 0.50. However, G models trivially achieve 1.00 accuracy on β₀ (connectivity) while T models drop to 0.73. After one barycentric subdivision (2-M_H¹), all models that previously beat random guessing degrade substantially (e.g., T homeomorphism type drops from 0.66 to 0.60, T β₀ drops from 0.57 to 0.21), revealing a lack of topological invariance. The best individual models (CT and SCCN) reach AUROC 0.93 on β₁ and 0.91 on homeomorphism type for full surfaces, but average performance across the T family remains below 0.70 on most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 12 models in two families: five graph-based (MLP, GCN, GAT, UniMP, TAG) and seven simplicial complex-based (SAN, SCCN, SCCNN, SCN, CellMP, CT, DECT). Simplicial complex-based models consistently outperform graph-based ones on all non-trivial topological invariants (β₁, β₂, β₃, homeomorphism type, orientability), while graph-based models trivially achieve perfect β₀ accuracy since all triangulations are connected. Among the T family, CT (Cellular Transformer) and SCCN are the strongest performers, reaching AUROC 0.93 on β₁ and 0.91/0.85 on homeomorphism type for full/known-type surfaces, respectively. DECT is excluded from barycentric subdivision experiments due to its built-in subdivision invariance. All G models (GCN, GAT, UniMP, TAG, MLP) produce near-random AUROC (≈0.50) on β₁ and β₂ for surfaces, confirming that 1-dimensional graph structure is insufficient for 2D/3D topology. After barycentric subdivision, every model that previously exceeded random performance degrades, with the most dramatic drops in β₀ accuracy (T: 0.57→0.21) and homeomorphism type (T: 0.66→0.60), indicating none of the 12 architectures learn remeshing symmetry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by providing the first large-scale, intrinsically higher-order dataset for topological deep learning, departing from prior higher-order datasets (Benson et al. 2018, Tadić et al. 2019, Giusti et al. 2016) that are either annotated on graphs or derived from simpler data via topological liftings. It responds directly to the call in Papamarkou et al. (2024) for non-trivial higher-order benchmarks and supersedes the limited 'Torus' dataset (Eitan et al. 2025) and the lifting-based TopoBenchmarkX (Telyatnikov et al. 2024) by offering triangulations whose topological structure is irreducible to their underlying graphs. The empirical evaluation repurposes existing graph and simplicial complex architectures (GCN, GAT, UniMP, SCCN, CT, DECT, etc.) as baselines rather than introducing new methods, positioning the contribution squarely as a dataset-and-benchmark paper with a diagnostic invariance analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MANTRA fills a critical gap: without intrinsically higher-order data, the TDL community cannot reliably assess whether higher-order models genuinely exploit higher-order structure or merely benefit from richer input representations. The finding that barycentric subdivision degrades performance across all models that beat random guessing directly challenges the 'remeshing symmetry' property that Papamarkou et al. (2024) identified as a core motivation for TDL, opening a concrete research direction on topological invariance. Key limitations temper the conclusions: triangulations are capped at 10 vertices (excluding fine-grained meshes), only 2D and 3D manifolds are covered, training is limited to 6 epochs, and the subdivision analysis covers only one subdivision on 2D data. The paper thus serves as both a practical benchmark and a diagnostic tool, but its findings on invariance should be interpreted as preliminary rather than definitive.
