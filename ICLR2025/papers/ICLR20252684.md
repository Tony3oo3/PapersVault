## Problem

Current uncertainty estimation in natural language generation relies on multinomial sampling to approximate semantic entropy, which is imprecise at the small sample sizes feasible in practice and computationally prohibitive at larger ones. Standard sampling does not account for semantic diversity, so obtaining semantically distinct output sequences occurs only by chance, and the semantic entropy estimator used by the prior state-of-the-art (Kuhn et al., 2023) is theoretically improper. The paper asks how to systematically and efficiently sample output sequences that are both likely and semantically diverse to produce a reliable aleatoric semantic uncertainty estimate.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt and a frozen autoregressive language model (OPT-2.7B to 30B), the method also requires a frozen NLI model (DeBERTa) that serves a dual role: computing token-level attribution and substitution scores via gradient backpropagation through the NLI's contradiction loss, and assigning semantic clusters to generated sequences. Substitutions are restricted to tokens at word boundaries for practicality. The output is a set of N semantically diverse output sequences (default N=10) from which a proper semantic entropy estimate is computed via importance-weighted cluster probability approximation, yielding a scalar uncertainty score per prompt.

## Method

SDLG constructs an empirical proposal distribution for importance sampling by explicitly steering generation toward semantic diversity. For an initial output sequence, it computes three token-level scores using the NLI model: an attribution score (gradient magnitude of the contradiction loss w.r.t. each token embedding, identifying which token most affects semantics), a substitution score (cosine similarity between the embedding difference and the gradient, identifying which replacement best alters semantics), and an importance score (the language model's probability of the candidate replacement token, ensuring coherence). The highest-ranked (initial token, alternative token) pair is substituted, and only the suffix after that position is regenerated by the language model, making each additional sample cheaper than full resampling. The importance sampling correction divides each sample's contribution by the probability the language model assigned to the substituted token, yielding a proper estimator of the semantic cluster probability distribution and hence the semantic entropy. The paper also establishes that the prior estimator (directly averaging log-probabilities of observed clusters) is theoretically invalid and that the correct procedure requires first estimating the cluster distribution before computing its entropy.

## Results

Using AUROC (Rouge-L F1 threshold 0.5, 10 samples) as the evaluation metric, SDLG achieves the highest uncertainty estimation performance across all three datasets and four OPT model sizes (2.7B–30B). On TruthfulQA (longest answers), SDLG reaches 0.920 AUROC (2.7B) and 0.956 (13B), substantially outperforming the prior state-of-the-art SE_MS (0.846 and 0.896 respectively). On CoQA and TriviaQA (shorter answers), the margins narrow but SDLG still leads: 0.768 vs. 0.767 (CoQA, 30B) and 0.845 vs. 0.838 (TriviaQA, 13B). SDLG also requires 15–33% fewer FLOPs than multinomial sampling for the same number of generated sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDLG consistently outperforms all four baseline families across every dataset and model size. Against the prior state-of-the-art SE_MS (Kuhn et al., 2023, improper estimator + multinomial sampling), the gap is largest on TruthfulQA: +0.074 AUROC at 2.7B (0.920 vs. 0.846) and +0.060 at 13B (0.956 vs. 0.896), shrinking to +0.001 on CoQA-30B (0.768 vs. 0.767) and +0.007 on TriviaQA-13B (0.845 vs. 0.838) where answers are shorter. Replacing the improper estimator with the proper one (SE(*)_MS) already yields a large gain over SE_MS on TruthfulQA (e.g., 0.846→0.846 at 2.7B is actually the same column; the proper estimator with MS is 0.846 vs. improper 0.405), confirming the theoretical correction matters. SE(*)_DBS (diverse beam search) is consistently weaker than SE(*)_SDLG by 0.05–0.23 AUROC, showing that token-level diversity heuristics are insufficient for capturing semantic diversity. Token-level baselines (PE, LN-PE, SAR) are uniformly the weakest, with AUROC values often below 0.6 on TruthfulQA, confirming that sequence-level semantic clustering is essential for reliable uncertainty estimation in NLG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Kuhn et al. (2023), which introduced semantic entropy and NLI-based semantic clustering as the state-of-the-art framework for uncertainty estimation in NLG. The key departure is twofold: (1) a theoretical correction showing that the standard Monte Carlo estimator used in Kuhn et al. is improper because it conflates sampling from the output-sequence distribution with sampling from the semantic-cluster distribution, and (2) the introduction of SDLG, an importance-sampling-based proposal distribution that explicitly steers generation toward semantically diverse yet likely alternatives using gradient-based token attribution from an NLI model. The method also draws on diverse beam search and token-level uncertainty measures (PE, LN-PE, SAR) as baselines, positioning itself as a targeted improvement over both token-level and sequence-level sampling strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reliable hallucination detection is a prerequisite for deploying LLMs in safety-critical and high-stakes settings, and this paper provides both a theoretically grounded estimator and a computationally efficient sampling strategy that consistently outperforms prior methods across model scales and QA benchmarks. The finding that the widely-used semantic entropy estimator is technically improper, combined with the 15–33% reduction in FLOPs, makes the practical case for adopting SDLG over the current standard. Open questions include the restrictive single-semantic-cluster assumption, the lack of treatment for epistemic semantic uncertainty, and whether the approach generalizes beyond free-form QA to longer-form generation tasks where the single-token substitution strategy may be less effective.
