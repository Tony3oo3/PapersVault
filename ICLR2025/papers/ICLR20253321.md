## Problem

Controlling high-dimensional musculoskeletal systems (700+ muscle-tendon actuators) in near real-time remains an open challenge: deep reinforcement learning requires days of training per task and is bottlenecked by sequential dynamics computation, while existing MPC methods succeed only in low-dimensional settings. No training-free method had previously achieved stable whole-body musculoskeletal control across diverse tasks such as walking on varied terrain or sports imitation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253321/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full simulation state (joint positions/velocities, actuator activations/lengths, task observations; >1500-dim) and exact MuJoCo physics dynamics, and produces 700-dim neural excitation signals for muscle-tendon units. Unusually, the MPC planner operates over only a 37-dim target posture vector (major joint coordinates) rather than the full action space, and the low-level controller maps this single posture target to 700 actuator controls using Jacobian-derived proportional gains that adapt per timestep based on current morphology.

## Method

MPC² is a hierarchical, training-free controller with two components: (1) a sampling-based MPC (MPPI variant) that plans over a 37-dim target posture z, augmented with 'instant rollouts' that sample from the model's current posture rather than the previous policy distribution, enabling rapid re-stabilization when the state deviates sharply; (2) a morphology-aware proportional controller that computes per-actuator forces from the difference between target and current actuator lengths, with proportional gains set adaptively via the Jacobian matrix columns weighted by joint position errors, so that major muscles for the current motion phase receive higher gain automatically. This reduces the planning dimensionality from H×700 to 37, making sampling-based optimization tractable, and eliminates the need for manual per-actuator gain tuning. The training-free nature enables rapid cost function iteration via Bayesian optimization (100 iterations in ~5 hours), demonstrating automatic behavior synthesis for complex tasks like soccer kicking.

## Results

MPC² achieves stable walking of 9.50±0.19 m on the 700-actuator MS-Human-700 model, substantially outperforming the original MPPI baseline (2.05±0.40 m) and all other MPC variants (0.00 m). Control sequences are generated in approximately 2 minutes (at 20% real-time speed), compared to at least one day required by the DRL baseline DynSyn. MPC² also reduces cumulative muscle activation by over 75% relative to DynSyn during walking, despite no explicit energy regularization in the cost function. Automatic cost function optimization via Bayesian optimization improves human walking speed from 0.79 to 1.24 m/s and ostrich walking speed from 0.90 to 2.08 m/s within ~5 hours of 100 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DRL baseline DynSyn (He et al., 2024), MPC² wins decisively on deployment time: MPC² generates stable standing and walking control sequences within 2 minutes, while DynSyn requires at least one full day of training plus deployment. On energy efficiency, MPC² reduces cumulative muscle activation by over 75% compared to DynSyn during walking, despite lacking an explicit energy penalty term. Against the original MPPI (Williams et al., 2016), MPC² achieves 9.50±0.19 m walking distance versus MPPI's 2.05±0.40 m; MPPI can maintain balance in the Stand task but fails to produce sustained forward locomotion in the 700-dimensional action space. All other MPC baselines tested (Gradient Descent, iLQG, Cross Entropy, Robust Sampling, Sample Gradient) achieve 0 m walking distance, confirming that the hierarchical decomposition into posture planning plus morphology-aware proportional control is essential for high-dimensional musculoskeletal control. Additional DRL baselines (MPO, DEP-RL) also fail to achieve stable standing or walking at the same training budget as DynSyn.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MJPC platform (Howell et al., 2022) and MPPI (Williams et al., 2016) for sampling-based model predictive control, but departs fundamentally from the DRL-dominated paradigm for musculoskeletal control (DynSyn, DEP-RL, MPO) by replacing learned state-feedback policies with a hierarchical model-based planner. The key architectural departure is the decomposition into a 37-dimensional posture-level MPC and a morphology-aware proportional controller that uses Jacobian matrices to adaptively coordinate 700 actuators, eliminating the need for any training. Prior MPC-based approaches were limited to low-dimensional torque-driven humanoids; this work extends the sampling-based MPC paradigm to the high-dimensional, over-actuated regime of muscle-tendon unit models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a training-free, model-based method can achieve stable whole-body musculoskeletal control in 2 minutes (vs. days for DRL) opens a pathway to massively parallel task generation and near-zero-cost reward engineering via Bayesian optimization. However, the approach critically depends on access to exact dynamics (simulation setting), and the 20% real-time speed limit means real-world deployment would require faster hardware or reduced model complexity. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to approximate or learned dynamics, whether the morphology-aware gain design transfers to non-musculoskeletal over-actuated systems, and whether the automatic cost function optimization can handle truly open-ended behavioral objectives beyond quadratic position tracking.
