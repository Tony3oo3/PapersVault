## Problem

Zero-shot and few-shot AI-generated image detection methods lack theoretical grounding and exhibit poor generalization across diverse generative techniques. While Mitchell et al. (2023) established a curvature-based criterion for generated text detection using explicit LLM probabilities, no analogous theoretically grounded approach existed for images, where generative models (diffusion models) only provide implicit probability access via score functions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250722/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a scalar detection score C(x₀) that is thresholded (calibrated on 1,000 real images only) to yield a real/generated classification. In practice, the image is mapped to CLIP embedding space, s=64 spherical perturbations are generated around it, and each perturbation is fed through a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 1.4 model to obtain noise predictions. In the few-shot variant, the scalar score is combined with Cozzolino et al.'s CLIP-based criterion via a lightweight mixture-of-experts classifier (e.g., random forest) trained on 1K labeled samples.

## Method

The core insight is that diffusion-model-generated images converge to stable local maxima of the implicitly learned probability manifold, exhibiting high total-variation curvature and low gradient magnitude. The authors derive a practical criterion C(x₀) that approximates three manifold-bias quantities—curvature κ, gradient D, and statistical bias ⟨b₀, x₀⟩—by sampling spherical perturbations around the input, computing score-function approximations via the diffusion model's noise predictions, and evaluating a weighted inner product in CLIP space. The key mathematical contribution (Claim 1 and Corollaries 2–3) connects the score function to curvature and gradient through the Gauss divergence theorem, showing that a single expectation over the sphere boundary simultaneously captures all three quantities. For the few-shot regime, the zero-shot criterion is plugged into a mixture-of-experts framework alongside Cozzolino et al.'s CLIP-based detector, with a lightweight classifier combining both signals, yielding consistent improvements over either method alone across 20 generative models.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting across 20 generative techniques (~200K images), the proposed method achieves an average AUC of 0.835, AP of 0.832, and accuracy of 0.741, substantially outperforming RIGID (AUC 0.439, accuracy 0.555) and AEROBLADE (AUC 0.444, accuracy 0.464). On the top-10 generative techniques where each method performs best, the proposed method reaches 0.839 accuracy versus 0.666 (RIGID) and 0.569 (AEROBLADE). In the few-shot MoE setting, combining the proposed zero-shot criterion with Cozzolino et al. (2024a) via a lightweight classifier further improves separability over either method alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms both existing zero-shot baselines. Against RIGID (He et al., 2024), which measures representation similarity between an image and its noise-perturbed version, the proposed method achieves 0.835 AUC versus 0.439 — nearly double — and 0.741 accuracy versus 0.555. Against AEROBLADE (Ricker et al., 2024), which uses autoencoder reconstruction error, the gap is even wider: 0.835 vs. 0.444 AUC and 0.741 vs. 0.464 accuracy. Both competitors exhibit high variability across generative techniques (e.g., RIGID drops to near-random AUC on Glide), while the proposed method maintains consistent performance. In the few-shot MoE experiments, the proposed zero-shot criterion combined with Cozzolino et al. (2024a) via a random-forest or SVM classifier outperforms Cozzolino et al. alone and the original Ojha et al. (2023) semi-supervised baseline, demonstrating that the manifold-bias criterion adds complementary discriminative information even when small labeled data is available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probability-curvature detection principle of Mitchell et al. (2023, DetectGPT) from explicit LLM text probabilities to the implicit score functions of diffusion models, providing the first zero-shot diffusion-model analysis for generated image detection. It builds on the score-based generative modeling framework of Song & Ermon (2019) but departs from prior zero-shot detectors (AEROBLADE's autoencoder reconstruction error, RIGID's representation similarity) by grounding the detection criterion in total-variation curvature, gradient magnitude, and statistical bias of the learned probability manifold. The few-shot extension via mixture-of-experts integrates with CLIP-based few-shot methods (Cozzolino et al., Ojha et al.) rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable theoretical gap in zero-shot/few-shot generated-image detection by providing a mathematically grounded manifold-analysis framework, and demonstrates strong average performance (AUC 0.835) across 20 generative models with a computationally efficient pipeline (~2.1s/sample). However, per-technique results reveal significant inconsistency: the method underperforms RIGID on Midjourney (AUC 0.63 vs 0.99) and Stable Diffusion (0.78 vs 0.96), suggesting the cross-model generalization—while empirically strong on average—lacks a comprehensive theoretical explanation. Open questions include why a criterion derived from one diffusion model's score function transfers to GANs and commercial tools, and whether the manifold-bias hypothesis holds as generative architectures diverge further.
