## Problem

Existing molecular self-supervised methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: attribute masking is ill-posed (multiple valid reconstructions exist) and fails to capture quantum mechanical features, while denoising methods rely on Gaussian mixture approximations of the potential energy surface whose parameters (e.g., standard deviation) are unknown and must be tuned empirically. The paper asks whether a well-posed, physics-grounded self-supervised task can directly learn interatomic interactions without such distributional approximations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D molecular structure (atomic positions and attributes) and removes one atom's node entirely while encoding its atomic number into all remaining neighbors' embeddings. The position prediction module outputs two distributions per neighbor: a radius distribution over 128 uniform bins spanning 0.9–5 Å, and a direction distribution over a spherical surface grid (100×100 sampling). In auxiliary-task mode, property labels (global energy or node-wise forces) are additionally encoded into the GNN embeddings via Gaussian or spherical-harmonic projections, and the final output is the target molecular property.

## Method

EMPP completely deletes the masked atom's node from the graph and uses an equivariant GNN backbone (Equiformer or TorchMD-Net) to produce SO(3)-equivariant node embeddings for the remaining atoms. Each neighbor independently predicts the masked atom's relative position by decomposing it into a radius (from type-0 scalar features via softmax) and a direction (from high-degree spherical harmonic features projected onto the sphere via Fourier transform, gridded, and passed through a shared MLP + softmax). The training loss is KL divergence between these predicted distributions and smooth Gaussian/spherical-harmonic target distributions (rather than Dirac deltas), which improves training stability and transferability. Because the node is fully removed, the task is well-posed in most organic molecules (unique equilibrium position determined by force balance), bypassing the Gaussian mixture approximation required by denoising methods. EMPP can be applied as unlabeled pre-training on large equilibrium datasets or as an auxiliary task where property labels are encoded into embeddings to link known quantum properties with atomic positions.

## Results

On QM9 with PCQM4Mv2 pre-training (TorchMD-Net backbone), EMPP(3-Mask) achieves the best results in 7 of 12 tasks, including polarizability (α = 0.035 bohr³), HOMO energy (13.7 meV), LUMO energy (13.4 meV), and total energy (U = 5.34 meV), surpassing the strongest denoising baseline Frad (α = 0.037, HOMO = 15.3 meV). Without any extra data, EMPP as an auxiliary task on Equiformer improves QM9 performance in 11 of 12 tasks (e.g., α drops from 0.046 to 0.039 bohr³, HOMO from 15 to 13 meV) and achieves best-in-class energy/force MAE on most MD17 molecules (e.g., Aspirin energy 5.0 meV, forces 6.2 meV/Å). On GEOM-Drug, EMPP reduces energy MAE by 48% (0.075 → 0.039 kcal/mol) over the Equiformer baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two strongest denoising baselines, EMPP(3-Mask) outperforms Frad on 9 of 12 QM9 tasks (e.g., α: 0.035 vs 0.037 bohr³; HOMO: 13.7 vs 15.3 meV; U: 5.34 vs 5.62 meV) and DP-TorchMD-Net on 10 of 12 tasks, while Frad retains a narrow edge on dipole moment (0.010 vs 0.014 D) and G/H energies. Attribute masking (AttrMask) is consistently the weakest self-supervised baseline, trailing EMPP by 2–3× on most QM9 properties (e.g., HOMO: 31.3 vs 13.7 meV). Among non-pretrained baselines on QM9, EMPP(3-Mask) with Equiformer beats SchNet, DimeNet++, PaiNN, TorchMD-NET, SEGNN, and the Equiformer baseline itself in 11 of 12 tasks. On MD17, EMPP(3-Mask) achieves the lowest energy and force MAE on 6 of 8 molecules compared to SchNet, DimeNet, PaiNN, TorchMD-NET, and Equiformer (e.g., Aspirin: 5.0/6.2 vs Equiformer 5.3/6.6 meV and meV/Å). On GEOM-Drug, EMPP reduces energy MAE from 0.075 to 0.039 kcal/mol over Equiformer, a 48% improvement, confirming effectiveness on non-equilibrium conformations where denoising methods are less applicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMPP carves a distinct niche between two established families of molecular self-supervised learning: attribute masking (AttrMask, GraphMAE) and position denoising (DP-TorchMD-Net, Frad, SE(3)-DDM). It departs from masking by targeting 3D positions rather than atomic attributes, arguing that position prediction is well-posed while attribute reconstruction is not. It departs from denoising by completely removing the masked atom's node from the graph rather than perturbing its coordinates, thereby bypassing the Gaussian mixture distribution approximation that denoising methods require to model local PES minima. The method is built on existing equivariant backbones (Equiformer, TorchMD-Net) and reuses spherical harmonic projection techniques from Symphony (Daigavane et al., 2024), but repurposes them for self-supervised learning rather than molecular generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EMPP offers a principled alternative to both masking and denoising for molecular pre-training, with the practical advantage of not requiring empirical tuning of distribution parameters (e.g., the Gaussian mixture σ that denoising methods depend on). Its dual applicability—as both a standalone pre-training objective on unlabeled data and an auxiliary loss during supervised training—makes it broadly useful for the molecular ML community working with equivariant GNNs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher-order spherical harmonic representations (l > 3), how it behaves on larger and more chemically diverse datasets beyond QM9/MD17, and whether the well-posedness argument holds for systems with significant conformational flexibility where multiple local minima are physically relevant.
