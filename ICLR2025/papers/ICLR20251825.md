## Problem

Discrete diffusion language models suffer from three key limitations: they are restricted to fixed-length generation, cannot leverage KV caching for efficient inference due to bidirectional context, and lag behind autoregressive models in perplexity. This paper identifies that the perplexity gap is not fundamental but stems from high gradient variance in the diffusion training objective, and that fixed-length generation is an architectural constraint rather than an inherent property of diffusion.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of L tokens grouped into B blocks of length L' (e.g., L=1024, L'=4/8/16) and processes them through a single transformer with a block-causal attention mask, where tokens in block b attend to all tokens in blocks 1 through b. During training, each block is independently noised (masked) at a sampled rate while conditioning on clean preceding blocks; during inference, blocks are generated sequentially with KV caching, and within each block tokens are denoised in parallel. Unlike standard diffusion LMs, the output length is not fixed at training time—BD3-LMs can generate sequences up to ~10× longer than the training context by continuing block-by-block until an EOS token or entropy threshold is reached.

## Method

BD3-LMs define an autoregressive factorization over blocks of tokens, where each block's conditional distribution p(x^b | x^{<b}) is modeled by a discrete denoising diffusion process (masked diffusion with absorbing state). A single transformer with a custom block-causal attention mask parameterizes all B block conditionals simultaneously, and an efficient training algorithm computes the full NELBO in two forward passes (or one vectorized pass via concatenating noisy and clean sequences with a specialized 2L×2L attention mask). The key theoretical contribution is showing that the perplexity gap between diffusion and AR at block size 1 is entirely due to gradient variance: the diffusion objective only computes cross-entropy on masked tokens (~50% of tokens on average), inflating variance. To address this, the paper introduces data-driven 'clipped' noise schedules that sample mask rates from U[β,ω] rather than U[0,1], with β and ω optimized via grid search during training to minimize the variance of the NELBO estimator. This reduces training variance by an order of magnitude (e.g., from 1.52 to 0.11 for L'=1) and closes the perplexity gap to AR at block size 1, while also improving perplexity for larger block sizes.

## Results

On LM1B (65B tokens), BD3-LMs with block size L'=4 achieve a test perplexity of ≤28.23, setting a new state-of-the-art among discrete diffusion models and improving over MDLM (≤31.78) by 13%. On OpenWebText (524B tokens), BD3-LMs (L'=4) reach ≤20.73 PPL versus MDLM's ≤22.98. For generative quality, BD3-LMs (L'=4) achieve a generative perplexity of 25.7 under GPT2-Large at L=1024 with only 1K NFEs, outperforming MDLM (46.8) and SSD-LM (37.2 at 40K NFEs). BD3-LMs also generate sequences up to ~10× longer than SEDD (max 9,982 tokens vs. 1,024), supporting arbitrary-length generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BD3-LMs outperform all prior discrete diffusion baselines on both LM1B and OpenWebText. Against D3PM (Austin et al., 2021), BD3-LMs reduce LM1B perplexity from ≤82.34 to ≤28.23, a dramatic improvement. Against SEDD (Lou et al., 2024), BD3-LMs achieve lower perplexity (≤28.23 vs. ≤32.68 on LM1B; ≤20.73 vs. ≤24.10 on OWT) and, critically, support variable-length generation (max 9,982 tokens) where SEDD is capped at the 1,024-token training context. Against MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024a), BD3-LMs improve LM1B PPL by 13% (≤28.23 vs. ≤31.78) and OWT PPL by ~10% (≤20.73 vs. ≤22.98); on generative perplexity at L=1024, BD3-LMs (25.7) substantially outperform MDLM (46.8) with the same 1K NFE budget. Against SSD-LM (Han et al., 2022), BD3-LMs achieve better generative perplexity (25.7 vs. 37.2 at L=1024) using an order of magnitude fewer generation steps (1K vs. 40K NFEs), and provide tractable likelihood estimates that SSD-LM's Gaussian diffusion formulation cannot. Against the autoregressive Transformer-XL baseline (Dai et al., 2019, PPL 23.5), BD3-LMs still trail by ~4.7 PPL points, confirming the remaining gap to AR models, though the gap narrows to ~2.9 PPL on OWT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the D3PM framework (Austin et al., 2021) by applying discrete denoising diffusion within each block of an autoregressive factorization, building on the simplified masked-diffusion NELBO from MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024a), Shi et al. (2024), and Ou et al. (2025). Conceptually, it occupies the 'semi-autoregressive' space previously explored by SSD-LM (Han et al., 2022), which performed Gaussian diffusion over blocks, but replaces it with discrete diffusion to retain tractable likelihood estimates. The key departure from prior diffusion LMs is the block-causal attention structure that enables KV caching and variable-length generation—capabilities absent from standard bidirectional diffusion architectures—and the identification of gradient variance (rather than model capacity or objective mismatch) as the primary driver of the perplexity gap to autoregressive models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Block diffusion resolves the fixed-length generation limitation of discrete diffusion LMs and provides a principled, empirically validated explanation for why diffusion underperforms AR even when objectives are mathematically equivalent: high training variance from partial masking. The data-driven 'clipped' noise schedules are a simple, broadly applicable technique that could improve standard MDLM and D3PM training beyond the block setting. However, the perplexity gap to AR remains substantial (28.23 vs. 22.83 on LM1B), training is ~2× more expensive than standard diffusion, and small block sizes reintroduce sequential generation costs—so the practical tradeoff between parallelism, quality, and efficiency is still an open question for scaling to larger models and longer contexts.
