## Problem

Diffusion models suffer from exposure bias: during training the score network sees ground-truth samples, but during sampling it sees its own imperfect predictions, causing score estimation and discretization errors to accumulate along the trajectory and degrade image quality. While prior work identified this training-sampling discrepancy, no lightweight, plug-in mechanism existed to directly compensate for the bias at each sampling step without retraining the base model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252799/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a frozen pre-trained diffusion model s_θ, a noise schedule, and a lightweight U-shaped prompt prediction network v_φ (3.2M–12.7M parameters depending on resolution 32/64/256). At each sampling step t, v_φ consumes the current biased sample x̂_t and outputs a same-shape correction vector; the corrected sample x_t^anti-bias = v_φ*(x̂_t) + x̂_t is fed to the next DM step. The final output is a generated image (e.g., 32×32, 64×64, or 256×256 RGB) with reduced exposure bias, at a sampling overhead of less than 5% (≈2.4 s extra per 1k images on A100).

## Method

The key theoretical insight is decomposing the true transition kernel q(x_t|x_{t+1}) into the fixed modeling kernel p_θ(x̂_t|x_{t+1}) plus a new anti-bias kernel p(x_t|x̂_t, x_{t+1}) (Eq. 8), so that correcting the biased sample rectifies the sampling trajectory. To train v_φ, the paper simulates exposure bias by perturbing a ground-truth image via the forward SDE to obtain x_t and x_{t+1}, then applying one step of a deterministic sampler (e.g., DDIM) to produce x̂_t, so that Φ(x̂_t, x_t) = x_t − x̂_t serves as the regression target. Training uses an SNR-based time-dependent weighting function to stabilize optimization across noise levels, and the best-performing metric is an InfoNCE contrastive loss whose minimization is proven to upper-bound the KL divergence between the true and modeling kernels. At inference, the optimized v_φ* is invoked T−1 times within the original sampler, adding only a single forward pass per step, and the framework extends naturally to classifier guidance, latent diffusion (operating on z_t instead of x_t), and fast ODE solvers.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the prompt learning framework consistently reduces FID across all tested diffusion models: EDM improves from 2.04 to 1.91, SDE (deep, VE) from 2.20 to 2.10, and DDPM from 3.17 to 2.99, while maintaining the same number of NFEs. On ImageNet 256×256, ADM-U improves from FID 7.49 to 7.29 (IS 127.49→134.95) and ADM from 10.94 to 10.37 (IS 100.98→112.00). On CelebA 64×64, ADM-IP improves from FID 2.21 to 2.15 and sFID from 4.33 to 4.19. The additional sampling overhead is less than 5% (e.g., 33.9s → 36.3s for 1k images at 35 NFEs on A100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms every baseline across all datasets and settings. On CIFAR-10, it reduces FID by 0.13 over EDM (2.04→1.91), 0.10 over SDE deep VE (2.20→2.10), 0.18 over DDPM (3.17→2.99), and 1.31 over NSCNv2 (10.87→9.56). On ImageNet 256, ADM-U improves from FID 7.49 to 7.29 and ADM from 10.94 to 10.37, while on CelebA, ADM-IP drops from 2.21 to 2.15 FID. For fast samplers at low NFEs (where discretization error is largest), gains are most pronounced: DEIS (VE) at 10 NFEs improves from 20.89 to 19.76 FID, and DPM-Solver-3 at 12 NFEs from 6.03 to 5.93. For latent diffusion, LDM-4 improves from FID 10.56 to 10.02 and LDM-8 from 15.51 to 14.03 on ImageNet 256. The method also enhances classifier guidance (ADM-G at scale 10: FID 12.00→10.64, IS 95.41→106.13) and further improves ADM-IP (the first work to address exposure bias) on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, demonstrating that the prompt learning framework is complementary to existing anti-bias strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SDE-based diffusion framework of Song et al. (2021) for its theoretical decomposition of the true Gaussian transition kernel into a fixed modeling kernel plus a learnable anti-bias kernel (Eq. 8). It extends the exposure bias problem first identified by Ning et al. (2023, ADM-IP), which addressed the issue via input perturbation during training, by instead proposing a lightweight prompt prediction model that corrects the sampling trajectory at inference time. Conceptually, it repurposes the prompt tuning paradigm from NLP (Lester et al., Li & Liang) and visual prompting (Jia et al., Bahng et al.) into a novel setting: predicting a per-step correction vector that compensates for accumulated score estimation and discretization errors, rather than conditioning a frozen model on task-specific tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's plug-in nature—requiring only a 3.2M–12.7M parameter model and <5% sampling overhead—makes it broadly applicable across architectures (ADM, EDM, LDM), samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver, DEIS), and settings (classifier guidance, latent diffusion) without retraining the base model, which is practically valuable for the diffusion community. However, it still incurs non-trivial training cost (100k–150k iterations on 8–16 A100s) and the per-step correction is approximate rather than exact, so residual bias remains. Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-to-image generation (acknowledged as future work), whether it composes with consistency models or rectified flow, and whether the SNR-weighted training schedule is optimal for all noise schedules.
