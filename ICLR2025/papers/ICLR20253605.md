## Problem

Scaling autoregressive models in vision has not yielded the same dramatic gains as in language models, and it remains unclear whether power-law scaling laws transfer to text-to-image generation. The paper identifies two underexplored design axes—discrete vs. continuous token representation and raster vs. random generation order—as potential explanations for the performance gap between autoregressive and diffusion-based image generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253605/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (up to 128 SentencePiece tokens) embedded by a frozen T5-XXL encoder plus a 6-block trainable text aligner, and produces a 256×256 RGB image. The image is represented as a sequence of 256 tokens: either 16×16 discrete VQGAN tokens (vocab 8192) or 32×32 continuous VAE tokens grouped into 2×2 patches (16 channels each). Inference for the random-order model uses 64 denoising steps with a cosine schedule, temperature sampling, and classifier-free guidance.

## Method

Fluid is a random-order autoregressive model that predicts image tokens in a randomized sequence using a BERT-like transformer with bidirectional self-attention, rather than a fixed left-to-right raster order with causal attention. Instead of discrete tokens with cross-entropy loss, it operates on continuous tokens from a Stable Diffusion VAE and models each token's distribution via a lightweight 6-layer MLP diffusion head (following Li et al. 2024), eliminating vector quantization information loss. The backbone is a standard decoder-only transformer with self-attention, cross-attention to text embeddings, and MLP blocks; unknown tokens are masked by a learnable token and predicted jointly at each step. The key empirical finding is that this combination—continuous tokens plus random-order bidirectional attention—scales most effectively: validation loss follows a power-law with model size, and FID/GenEval scores improve consistently up to 10.5B parameters, outperforming both raster-order and discrete-token variants at every scale.

## Results

Fluid 10.5B achieves a zero-shot FID of 6.16 on MS-COCO 30K and a GenEval overall score of 0.69, setting a new state-of-the-art among autoregressive text-to-image models. Notably, even the smallest Fluid model (369M parameters) matches the FID of Parti (20B parameters) at 7.23, demonstrating a ~54x parameter efficiency gain. The 3.1B variant reaches a GenEval overall score of 0.70, slightly exceeding the 10.5B model (0.69), while the 10.5B model leads on FID. Generation speed is 1.571 seconds per image per TPU (32 TPU v5, batch size 2048).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fluid 10.5B (FID 6.16) outperforms all diffusion-based baselines on MS-COCO FID-30K, beating RAPHAEL (6.61), Transfusion (6.78), Imagen (7.27), DALL-E 2 (10.39), and LDM (12.64). On GenEval overall, Fluid 10.5B (0.69) surpasses both DALL-E 3 (0.67) and SD3 (0.68), while the 3.1B variant edges ahead at 0.70. Among autoregressive baselines, even the smallest Fluid model (369M) matches Parti's 20B-parameter FID of 7.23, and all Fluid sizes beat Muse (7.88) and Show-o (9.24). The paper highlights that continuous tokens are the key differentiator: discrete-token models (e.g., Parti, Muse) plateau in FID around 1B parameters, whereas Fluid's continuous-token design shows consistent improvement up to 10.5B. On GenEval sub-metrics, Fluid 3.1B leads on Single Object (0.98), Two Object (0.83), and Colors (0.82), while DALL-E 3 retains an edge on Two Object (0.87) and Position (0.43 vs. 0.41).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two recent advances in autoregressive image generation: the diffusion-loss-based continuous tokenization from Li et al. (2024), which eliminates vector quantization, and the BERT-like masked generation paradigm of MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022), which the authors reframe as random-order autoregression. It departs from the dominant discrete-token AR lineage (Parti, Muse, VQGAN-based models) by systematically studying a 2×2 design space—discrete vs. continuous tokens crossed with raster vs. random generation order—across model sizes from 150M to 3B, a controlled scaling study not previously conducted for text-to-image AR models. The work also explicitly connects to the NLP scaling-laws literature (Kaplan et al., 2020), testing whether analogous power-law behavior holds for vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that continuous tokens and random-order generation are the critical design choices for scaling AR text-to-image models, with the 10.5B Fluid model achieving SOTA FID (6.16) and GenEval (0.69) that rival leading diffusion systems like DALL-E 3 and SD3. A key open question is whether the near-linear correlation between validation loss and FID/GenEval (Pearson r ≈ 0.92) persists beyond 10.5B parameters, and whether the random-order approach can be made more inference-efficient (it forgoes KV caching). The finding that generation order primarily affects global compositional structure (GenEval) rather than local perceptual quality (FID) suggests future work could hybridize the two orders or develop efficient bidirectional decoding.
