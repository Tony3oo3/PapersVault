## Problem

INT4 weight-activation quantization for LLMs is blocked by activation outliers that stretch the quantization range and compress effective bits for normal values. Existing smoothing methods either migrate outliers into weights (making 4-bit weight quantization infeasible), rely on offline calibration scales that mismatch runtime activations, or use rotation alone that cannot guarantee smoothness. A training-free approach that simultaneously eliminates both channel-wise and spike outliers for accurate A4W4 inference remains an open challenge.

## Input / Output

The method operates on activation tensors (N×K) and weight matrices (M×K) from linear layers in transformer blocks. Weights are rotated offline with a Hadamard matrix and quantized to INT4 via GPTQ; activations are rotated online at inference time. The output is a dequantized linear layer result computed by a fused GEMM kernel that applies per-group runtime smoothing scales (group size 128, matching the GEMM tile) to the dequantized block product, adding only a scalar multiplication per block over the standard A4W4 pipeline.

## Method

Rotated Runtime Smooth (RRS) combines two complementary mechanisms. First, Runtime Smooth computes channel-wise maximums of activations at runtime (rather than offline calibration), reorders activations and weights by magnitude to cluster outliers, groups them to match the GEMM block size, and divides each group by its maximum—applying the resulting scale to the dequantized GEMM output rather than merging it into weights. Second, a Hadamard rotation is applied to weights offline and activations online, which spreads spike outliers uniformly across channels within a token so that the per-channel smoothing scales become consistent, preventing the 'victim' effect where normal values are pruned by abnormally large scales. The key insight is that rotation converts spike outliers into a consistent large magnitude across channels (serving as natural smoothing scales), while Runtime Smooth then eliminates the residual channel-wise outliers that rotation alone leaves as sub-smooth. The entire pipeline is training-free, plug-and-play, and introduces negligible overhead over a standard A4W4 GEMM kernel.

## Results

Under A4W4KV16 quantization, Rotated Runtime Smooth (RRS) achieves WikiText-2 perplexity of 6.66 on LLaMA3-70B, compared to 57.33 for QuaRot (the prior state-of-the-art) and 2.80 for FP16. Across all evaluated models, RRS consistently improves perplexity by 0.1–0.7 over QuaRot, with the largest gap on LLaMA3-70B (57.33→6.66) and LLaMA3.1-70B (6.26→5.56). On zero-shot Common Sense QA (A4W4KV16), RRS achieves 58.0% average accuracy on LLaMA3-8B vs. 55.0% for QuaRot and 30.4% for SmoothQuant/GPTQ. Under the activation-only A4W16KV16 setting, Runtime Smooth alone achieves 40× perplexity improvement over SmoothQuant on LLaMA3-8B (8e2→11.44).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QuaRot (the primary state-of-the-art rotation-based method), RRS wins on every model tested, with the most dramatic improvement on LLaMA3-70B where QuaRot's perplexity degrades to 57.33 while RRS achieves 6.66 under A4W4KV16. Against SmoothQuant, RRS is overwhelmingly superior: SmoothQuant produces NaN or perplexity >100 on most large models under A4W4, while RRS stays within 0.5–1.0 of FP16. Against GPTQ (weight-only 4-bit), RRS is also dramatically better, as GPTQ does not address activation outliers and yields perplexity >100 on LLaMA3-70B. Against the training-based SpinQuant, RRS (training-free) achieves lower perplexity on all four tested models (e.g., 7.79 vs. 7.99 on LLaMA3-8B), while avoiding 1.5–12 hours of GPU training. Against DuQuant (dual rotation), RRS achieves higher average accuracy on Common Sense QA (57.9% vs. 57.0% on LLaMA3-8B), and notably DuQuant+RS (60.1%) outperforms RRS alone, suggesting the runtime smoothing component is broadly complementary to other rotation schemes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work on activation smoothing for LLM quantization: SmoothQuant's offline channel-wise outlier migration and QuaRot's Hadamard-rotation-based outlier spreading. RRS departs from SmoothQuant by computing smoothing scales at runtime (eliminating the calibration mismatch problem) and by never migrating outliers into weights (preserving weight quantizability at 4 bits). It departs from QuaRot by recognizing that rotation alone leaves channel-wise outliers 'sub-smooth' and spike outliers still problematic, and adds a runtime smoothing step on top of the rotated activations. The combination—rotate first to spread spike outliers, then apply group-wise runtime smoothing to eliminate residual channel-wise outliers—is the core conceptual contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INT4 weight-activation quantization is critical for reducing serving cost of large LLMs, and this paper provides a training-free, plug-and-play solution with negligible kernel overhead that consistently outperforms the prior state-of-the-art (QuaRot) across model families. The dramatic LLaMA3-70B result (57.33 → 6.66 perplexity) highlights that model-specific outlier sensitivity can cause rotation-only methods to fail catastrophically, and that the added runtime smoothing provides robustness. Limitations include the acknowledged vulnerability when multiple spike outlier tokens co-occur in a single activation (Section C.1), and the method is validated only on per-channel symmetric quantization. Open questions include generalization to INT2/INT3, non-Transformer architectures, and whether the rotation + runtime smooth combination can be further optimized for hardware-specific block sizes.
