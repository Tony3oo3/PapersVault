## Problem

Conditional image generation models produce visually compelling images but yield weak latent representations for downstream discriminative tasks, because the class-condition guidance present during generation is absent during discrimination. Prior work on generative representation learning has focused almost exclusively on unconditional models, leaving the representation capabilities of conditional generators largely unexplored. BiGR addresses this by being the first conditional generative model that unifies high-quality generation and strong visual representation within a single framework trained purely through a generative objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253240/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 16×16 sequence of K-bit binary latent codes (K ∈ {16, 20, 24, 32}) produced by a binary autoencoder with 16× downsampling (256×256 → 16×16 tokens), along with a class-condition embedding prepended to the sequence and learnable mask tokens at masked positions. For text-to-image, the class token is replaced by T5-XXL text embeddings (max 120 tokens). Generation outputs a full 256×256 or 512×512 image after iterative unmasking; representation extraction outputs a single global vector obtained by average-pooling intermediate transformer features (layer 10 for L, 15 for XL, 32 for XXL), which are more discriminative than final-layer features.

## Method

BiGR replaces the causal attention of Llama with full bidirectional attention and trains via masked modeling on binary latent codes rather than categorical tokens. A binary transcoder (MLP with adaptive LayerNorm) implements a Bernoulli diffusion denoising process that predicts the XOR residual z^t ⊕ z^0, trained with a weighted binary cross-entropy loss that corrects for 0/1 imbalance within each K-bit code. At inference, an entropy-ordered sampling strategy iteratively unmasks tokens in order of decreasing prediction confidence (1 − binary entropy, perturbed by Gumbel noise), requiring only 20–25 iterations versus hundreds of steps in diffusion models. For representation, average pooling over intermediate features yields a global embedding that linearly separates ImageNet classes at 67.5–69.8% top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming other conditional generators (LlamaGen: 40.5%, MAR: 57.9–60.0%) while achieving competitive FID (2.36–2.71).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256, BiGR-XXL-d32 achieves FID 2.36 and IS 277.2 for generation, while simultaneously reaching 69.8% linear-probe top-1 accuracy for discrimination — substantially outperforming LlamaGen-L (FID 3.81, ACC1 40.5%) and MAR-H (FID 1.55, ACC1 60.0%) on the unified metric. The L-sized variant (BiGR-L-d24) achieves FID 2.71 and ACC1 67.5% with only 336M parameters. Inference takes 0.69s per image (vs. 0.13s for LlamaGen), with the gap attributable to the Bernoulli denoising steps that can be reduced to 10 timesteps for faster generation at minimal quality loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LlamaGen (the closest architectural baseline using Llama + VQGAN + autoregressive modeling), BiGR wins decisively on discriminative performance (ACC1 64.3% vs. 40.5%, a 23.8-point gap) and on FID (3.17 vs. 3.81), while LlamaGen is ~5× faster at inference (0.13s vs. 0.69s). Against MAR (Li et al., 2024), which achieves the best FID among compared models (1.55 for MAR-H), BiGR-XXL-d32 trails on FID (2.36) but surpasses it on linear-probe accuracy (69.8% vs. 60.0%), confirming that masked modeling on binary codes yields stronger representations than autoregressive continuous-token prediction. Against diffusion-based SOTA (SiT-XL/2 SDE, FID 2.06) and VAR (FID 1.92), BiGR is competitive but not state-of-the-art on pure generation quality; however, neither reports discriminative metrics, so BiGR's unified advantage is unchallenged. Against dedicated representation-learning methods (iBOT 81.0%, MAGE 78.9%, BYOL 78.6%), BiGR-XL-d32 (69.8%) trails by 9–11 points, but these methods use specialized contrastive or discriminative losses, whereas BiGR is trained solely with a generative reconstruction objective. Overall, BiGR is the strongest conditional generative model on the joint generation+discrimination axis, at the cost of slightly higher inference latency than pure autoregressive baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiGR sits at the intersection of three research threads: binary latent code modeling (lookup-free quantization, Bernoulli diffusion from Wang et al. 2023), masked image generation (MaskGIT, MAGE), and the Llama transformer backbone. Its key conceptual departure is from prior conditional generative models like LlamaGen and MAR, which optimize generation quality but leave representation capabilities unstudied, and from unconditional unification models like MAGE, which combine generation and discrimination but lack conditional guidance. By replacing autoregressive next-token prediction with bidirectional masked modeling over binary codes, BiGR is positioned as the first conditional generative framework that simultaneously delivers strong FID and strong linear-probe accuracy without any discriminative loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the choice between autoregressive and masked modeling has a dramatic (~24-point) impact on representation quality in conditional generation, a finding that could reshape how the community designs unified generative-representational models. Zero-shot generalization to inpainting, outpainting, editing, and interpolation without architectural changes highlights practical flexibility. Key limitations include the fixed sequence length (requiring retraining for higher resolutions), a large hyperparameter search space for sampling, and the observation that conditional training still slightly degrades representation versus unconditional training. Open questions include whether the representation gap can be closed through better conditioning mechanisms and whether the approach scales effectively to text-to-image at higher resolutions.
