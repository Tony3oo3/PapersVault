## Problem

Speech language models (SpeechLMs) like AudioLM and TWIST operate on high-resolution phonetic tokens at 25–50 Hz, requiring several times more tokens per word than text-based LMs. This high granularity substantially impairs training and inference speed, and it is unclear whether modeling speech at such fine temporal resolution actually helps semantic understanding. The paper asks whether one can learn coarser, syllable-like semantic units from raw speech in a fully unsupervised way that are both controllable in rate and sufficient for competitive spoken language modeling.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes raw 16 kHz speech waveforms and produces discrete syllable-like tokens at a controllable rate of 5.0–8.33 Hz with bitrates as low as 60 bps (vs. 175 bps for TWIST units). The SpeechLM takes a short audio sample (3-second crop) as a prompt and generates a 10-second spoken continuation. Decoding back to audio is done via an interleaved vocoder that jointly predicts SylBoost syllable units and TWIST acoustic units, cascaded into TWIST's vocoder, so the final output is a waveform rather than a token sequence.

## Method

The pipeline has two bootstrapping stages. First, LossPred builds a loss-prediction matrix C by sliding a 1-second mask window across a frozen HuBERT student-teacher pair and recording the cross-entropy loss at each masked position relative to the nearest unmasked token; a min-cut algorithm on C then yields noisy syllable-like boundaries. Second, SylBoost applies noisy-student-teacher distillation (MSE between student features and the mean of teacher features within each pseudo-syllable group) for two iterations, progressively sharpening the latent feature space so that intra-syllable frames cluster tightly and inter-syllable frames separate. An efficient dynamic-programming boundary extraction (replacing the slow min-cut) and a K-Means + Agglomerative Clustering quantization step convert the sharpened features into a fixed vocabulary of 2k–16k discrete units. SyllableLM is then a 90M or 300M-parameter OPT-style autoregressive transformer trained on these coarse tokens over 55k hours of LibriLight, achieving SotA sBLIMP and sWUGGY scores with 30× less training compute and 4× faster inference than comparable TWIST models.

## Results

SyllableLM (90M params, 8.3Hz, 91bps) achieves 72.2 on sWUGGY-All and 62.4 on sBLIMP, outperforming TWIST-300M (70.6 / 56.2) and TWIST-1.3B (71.8 / 57.0) with 30× less GPU compute (70 vs 295 GPU-hours). The 300M SyllableLM reaches 63.7 on sBLIMP, surpassing TWIST-13B (59.2) and Moshi-7B (59.9) on semantic understanding. On unit quality, SylBoost reduces resynthesis WER from 37.3% (SD-HuBERT) to 7.0% at 6.25Hz/81bps, achieving the lowest known bitrate for self-supervised-unit resynthesis. Inference is 4.5× faster than TWIST at the same parameter count (2.88E-5 vs 1.28E-4 RTF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TWIST (all sizes 300M–13B), SyllableLM-90M wins on sBLIMP (62.4 vs 56.2–59.2) and sWUGGY-All (72.2 vs 70.6–73.9), while using 2–140× fewer GPU-hours; the 300M SyllableLM further extends the sBLIMP lead to 63.7 vs TWIST-13B's 59.2. Against AudioLM-300M, SyllableLM-90M matches or exceeds sWUGGY-All (72.2 vs 71.5) with 30× less training compute (70 vs 2.9k GPU-hours), though AudioLM retains a slight edge on sWUGGY-IV (83.7 vs 81.7). Against Moshi-7B, SyllableLM-300M wins on sBLIMP (63.7 vs 59.9) but trails on sWUGGY-All (72.2 vs 74.8) and tSC (75.4 vs 80.9), with Moshi trained on 7M hours vs 55K hours. Against the re-implemented TWIST-Cold-Init (90M, same architecture), SyllableLM-90M improves sWUGGY-All by +2.5, sBLIMP by +6.9, and tSC by +2.4, isolating the benefit of syllable-like units over phone-level TWIST tokens. Against BPE (Shen et al., 2024), SyllableLM-90M leads by +10.4 on sWUGGY-All and +7.9 on sBLIMP, confirming that semantic unit structure matters beyond mere bitrate reduction. On unit-level metrics (Table 5), SylBoost reduces resynthesis WER from 37.3% (SD-HuBERT) to 7.0% at 6.25Hz/81bps, approaching the TWIST tokenizer upper bound of 6.3% at 175bps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on extracting coarser semantic units from SSL speech encoders, building directly on SD-HuBERT (DINO-like distillation) and VG-HuBERT (cross-modal contrastive learning). The key departure is LossPred, which analyzes the MLM loss landscape of a pretrained encoder rather than relying on intermediate feature representations or cross-modal signals to discover syllable-like boundaries. SylBoost then refines these noisy boundaries through iterative student-teacher distillation, and the resulting tokens feed into a standard autoregressive transformer SpeechLM following the GSLM pipeline established by Lakhotia et al. and instantiated by AudioLM and TWIST.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30x training compute reduction and 4x inference speedup from operating at 5–8 Hz rather than 25–50 Hz make SpeechLM scaling substantially more tractable, opening a path toward the kind of compute scaling that has driven text LLM progress. However, the evaluation is confined to English audiobook speech (LibriSpeech/LibriLight), and the authors acknowledge that low-frequency units may discard paralinguistic information (tone, speaker identity) that only becomes salient in more diverse or multi-speaker settings. Open questions include whether hybrid low/high-bitrate tokenizations can recover this lost information, whether the loss-based boundary detection generalizes to non-speech audio or multi-speaker scenarios, and whether the approach transfers to non-English languages.
