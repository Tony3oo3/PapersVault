## Problem

Non-convex machine learning objectives typically violate the standard L-smoothness assumption, motivating the more realistic (L₀,L₁)-smoothness condition where the Hessian norm grows linearly with the gradient norm. In federated learning, convergence guarantees for methods combining local steps, random reshuffling, and partial client participation under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness were either absent or required additional restrictive assumptions such as data homogeneity, bounded variance, or cosine relatedness. This paper closes that gap by providing the first analysis of such methods under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness without any extra assumptions, and further derives the first Polyak-Łojasiewicz convergence results for FL methods under generalized smoothness.

## Input / Output

The methods operate on a distributed finite-sum objective where each of M clients holds N local data points, and workers compute either full gradients or stochastic gradients per data point. The stepsize is parameterized as γ_t = 1/(c₀ + c₁‖∇f(x_t)‖), which couples the step magnitude to the local gradient norm and effectively implements smoothed gradient clipping at the server level. The output is a sequence of iterates with convergence rate bounds of O(L₀δ₀/ε) in the nonconvex case and O(L₀/μ · ln(δ₀/ε)) under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, recovering known rates for standard L-smooth problems when L₁ = 0.

## Method

The paper proposes three algorithms—Clip-LocalGDJ, CLERR, and Clipped RR-CLI—that share a common design principle: clients take small local steps that determine the update direction, while the server takes a larger 'jumping' step in the averaged pseudogradient direction, with (smoothed) gradient clipping applied only to the aggregated pseudogradient at the server level. Clip-LocalGDJ alternates local GD steps with server-side clipped averaging; CLERR adds a full epoch of random reshuffling per client before each synchronization; Clipped RR-CLI further incorporates partial client participation via reshuffling of client cohorts (sampling without replacement) and reshuffling of each client's local data within each meta-epoch. The key technical insight is that clipping the aggregated pseudogradient once per epoch—rather than at every inner step—preserves the bias-reduction benefits of reshuffling while still controlling the effect of large gradients under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness. The analysis handles both asymmetric and symmetric (L₀,L₁)-smoothness, and the PL-condition proofs require a careful two-case argument (gradient 'small' vs. 'big') with induction on boundedness of certain metrics.

## Results

The paper is primarily a theoretical contribution, with experiments serving as illustrative validation rather than a standard benchmark comparison. On the synthetic (L₀,L₁)-smooth problem (sum of shifted fourth-order polynomials), methods with gradient clipping (CLERR, Clip-LocalGDJ, Clipped RR-CLI) consistently outperform non-clipping baselines (SO, Nastya) in function residual convergence. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, the authors find that standard per-step clipping (CSO) already performs well, and their epoch-level clipping (CLERR) does not provide additional gains. No single main results table with quantitative accuracy numbers is provided; all experimental results are presented as convergence plots (function residual vs. epochs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **Nastya** (Malinovsky et al., 2022), CLERR achieves lower function residual on the synthetic (L₀,L₁)-smooth problem, confirming that epoch-level clipping of the aggregated pseudogradient is more effective than the jumping mechanism alone. On CIFAR-10, however, both Nastya and CLERR (jumping) fail to improve over standard methods, and CSO (per-step clipping) achieves the best test accuracy. Against **CELGC** (Liu et al., 2022), Clip-LocalGDJ shows superior convergence on the high-dimensional synthetic problem (d=100), particularly when the starting point is far from the optimum (x₀=(10,…,10)), because server-side clipping yields a better gradient approximation than client-side clipping. Against **CE-FedAvg** (Zhang et al., 2022), Clip-LocalGDJ outperforms it in the local-steps experiment (Figure 3b), and Clipped RR-CLI outperforms CE-FedAvg-PP in the partial-participation experiment (Figure 4), attributed to the use of random reshuffling and end-of-epoch clipping providing a better global gradient estimate. Overall, the proposed methods win on the synthetic (L₀,L₁)-smooth problems but do not demonstrate clear advantages on the CIFAR-10 image classification task where standard clipping already suffices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three well-studied threads: generalized (L0,L1)-smoothness (Zhang et al., 2020b), local SGD / FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2016; Khaled et al., 2019a,b), and Random Reshuffling (Mishchenko et al., 2020; Gürbüzbalaban et al., 2015). Prior FL work under (L0,L1)-smoothness required restrictive additional assumptions—data homogeneity (Liu et al., 2022), bounded variance (Wang et al., 2024), or cosine relatedness (Qian et al., 2021)—and only Crawshaw et al. (2024a) addressed partial participation, but without reshuffling. The paper's core departure is combining local steps, random reshuffling, and partial participation under (L0,L1)-smoothness with no such extra assumptions, using a specific interplay of client/server stepsizes and end-of-epoch gradient clipping, and extending the analysis to the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition for the first time in this FL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results close a notable gap in FL theory by providing the first convergence guarantees for methods combining all three practical techniques (local steps, reshuffling, partial participation) under the more realistic (L0,L1)-smoothness assumption, and the first PL-condition results for FL under generalized smoothness. The tightness of the analysis—recovering known rates when L1=0—gives practitioners confidence that the generalized-smoothness overhead is minimal. However, the ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 experiments reveal an honest limitation: when standard per-step clipping already works well (as in typical DNN training), the theoretical advantages of end-of-epoch clipping and 'jumping' do not translate into practical gains. Open directions include communication compression, incomplete local epochs, and client-drift reduction mechanisms under generalized smoothness.
