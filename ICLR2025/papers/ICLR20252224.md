## Problem

Previous mechanistic interpretability works on one-hidden-layer networks trained on finite group composition produced divergent and incomplete explanations: Chughtai et al. (2023) identified irrep sparsity in neurons, while Stander et al. (2024) identified coset concentration, but neither fully described how neurons combine to compute the group operation. Moreover, no rigorous quantitative evaluation existed to assess the completeness of these interpretations, leaving it unclear whether either explanation was sufficient to account for model behavior.

## Input / Output

The verifier V_irrep takes as input the trained model parameters θ (left/right embeddings, unembedding, bias), the finite group G, and an interpretation string π that labels each neuron with its corresponding irrep ρ, ρ-set partition index q, and the associated vectors (a, b, c). It outputs a real-valued lower bound on the model's global accuracy over all |G|² input pairs, computed in O(m|G|²) time—approximately 3× faster than the O(m|G|³) brute-force forward pass over all inputs. The bound is derived as a compact formal proof: the execution trace of the verifier constitutes a mathematical proof that the model achieves at least the stated accuracy.

## Method

The paper discovers that neurons in trained models implement ρ-set circuits: each neuron's left, right, and unembedding weights are rank-one projections of a finite ρ-set B (a set of vectors in the irrep's representation space that ρ permutes transitively), with a shared projection vector a across all neurons in the same irrep. This yields bi-equivariant circuits of the form f_{ρ,B}(z|x,y) = −Σ_{b,b'∈B} b^T ρ(x^{-1}zy^{-1}) b' ReLU[a^T(b−b')], which are maximized at z = x⋆y. The key verification step exploits bi-equivariance: the idealized model's accuracy requires only a single forward pass (since f(z|x,y) depends only on x^{-1}zy^{-1}), reducing the margin computation from O(m|G|³) to O(m|G|²). The verifier then bounds the logit distance between the original and idealized models via norm-based inequalities (Lemma G.1), and the accuracy bound is the fraction of inputs where the idealized margin exceeds this distance. This ρ-set framework unifies prior observations: projected ρ-sets are simultaneously irrep-sparse and coset-concentrated, and the ρ-set size (not irrep dimensionality) explains the frequency ordering of learned irreps.

## Results

On 100 one-hidden-layer models trained on S₅, the paper's ρ-set verifier V_irrep achieves a mean accuracy bound of 91.7% on the 55% of models where the explanation holds (neither a-bad nor ρ-bad), running in 0.75s versus 2.20s for brute force (3× faster, O(m|G|²) vs O(m|G|³)). The coset-concentration verifier V_coset yields a vacuous 0% bound across all models. The abstract reports a ≥95% accuracy bound for 45% of trained models, and the paper notes that no nontrivial non-vacuous bounds are obtainable using only the explanations from previous works (Chughtai et al. 2023; Stander et al. 2024).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is V_coset, derived from Stander et al.'s (2024) coset concentration explanation. V_irrep decisively outperforms V_coset: it achieves a 91.7% mean accuracy bound on explained models versus 0% for V_coset, which fails to produce any non-vacuous bound across all 100 models because the pessimization over unexplained degrees of freedom swamps the margin. Against the brute-force verifier V_brute, V_irrep trades a small loss in bound tightness (91.7% vs. 100% exact) for a 3× speedup (0.75s vs. 2.20s) and a better asymptotic complexity (O(m|G|²) vs. O(m|G|³)), making it tractable for larger groups. The paper also demonstrates that causal interventions (the verification method used by Stander et al.) are insufficient: replicating the same four interventions on a Z/53Z model (which cannot use cosets, as the cyclic group has no nontrivial subgroups) produces qualitatively identical directional effects, showing these interventions cannot distinguish the coset explanation from alternatives. The ρ-set explanation subsumes both prior observations: projected ρ-sets are simultaneously irrep-sparse (Chughtai et al.) and coset-concentrated (Stander et al.), and the paper proves the converse—that the conjunction of both properties is equivalent to being a projected ρ-set (Lemma G.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends and unifies two prior mechanistic interpretability analyses of one-hidden-layer networks trained on finite group composition: Chughtai et al. (2023) observed that neurons are irrep-sparse (linear combinations of matrix entries of a single irrep), while Stander et al. (2024) observed coset concentration (neurons constant on cosets of subgroups). The authors show these are two projections of a single underlying structure—ρ-sets, finite sets on which an irrep acts by permutation—and that the full circuit is bi-equivariant. The verification methodology (compact proofs of accuracy lower bounds) is adapted from Gross et al. (2024), who applied it to the max-of-four task, and is here used as a quantitative measure of explanation completeness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rigorous, quantitative criterion for judging interpretability claims: if an explanation is truly complete, it should yield non-vacuous, tight accuracy bounds faster than brute force. The coset explanation, despite making correct observations about individual neurons, fails this test entirely (0% bound), while the ρ-set explanation succeeds for ~45% of models. Key limitations include the explanation covering only about half of trained models (with identifiable failure modes: non-constant projection vectors or incomplete ρ-sets), the main-text analysis being restricted to S₅, and the compact proof framework being somewhat informal in its translation from explanation to bound. Open questions include understanding the remaining models, generalizing to other groups and architectures, and whether the compact-proof methodology scales to more complex interpretability claims.
