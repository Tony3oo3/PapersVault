## Problem

Existing LLM detoxification methods either require expensive retraining of billions of parameters or depend on external reward models at inference time, making them impractical when only the decoding LM is accessible. This paper asks whether an LLM's own internal representations already encode enough toxicity signal to enable self-detoxification without any external model or parameter updates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250205/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and the decoding LM's own contextual embeddings (specifically the last-token embedding of the concatenated prompt-partial-response) as input. A pre-computed closed-form linear subspace, learned once from a labeled toxicity dataset (Jigsaw, ~2M comments) using the same LM's embeddings, provides the steering signal. At each autoregressive step, the output is an adjusted probability distribution over the vocabulary that reweights the original logits by a margin term, producing detoxified continuations of up to 20 tokens via nucleus sampling.

## Method

SASA learns a class-conditional Gaussian model over the LM's contextual embeddings for toxic vs. non-toxic prompt-response pairs, yielding a Bayes-optimal linear classifier f_v(c,x) = sign(w_v^T(g(c⊕x) − b_v)) in closed form with no gradient-based training. During decoding, at each token step the method computes the signed margin m^v for every candidate token (how far the hypothetical next context sits from the toxic subspace), then solves a constrained optimization that maximizes expected margin while minimizing KL divergence from the original sampling distribution. The optimal solution is a simple logit reweighting: p = Softmax(logit + β·Softmax(m^v)), where β trades off alignment strength against fluency. Proposition 1 proves this reweighting is the exact optimal policy for the alignment objective, distinguishing SASA from heuristics like RAD that arrive at a similar formula without theoretical grounding. The entire pipeline requires no external model, no LM retraining, and no gradients at inference—only the LM's forward pass and a pre-computed (w_v, b_v) pair.

## Results

On the challenging RealToxicityPrompts split with Llama-2-7b, SASA (β=500) achieves an Avg. Max Toxicity of 0.426 and Toxic Rate of 0.447 at perplexity 7.20, outperforming RAD (0.481 / 0.499 at perplexity 7.33) by 10% in toxicity at comparable fluency. On AttaQ, SASA reduces Avg. Max Toxicity to 0.142 (42% lower than RAD's 0.264) while also achieving lower perplexity (6.3 vs 10.5). On BOLD, SASA reaches 0.023 Avg. Max Toxicity versus RAD's 0.050. On the non-toxic RTP with GPT2-Large, SASA (β=500) attains 0.083 Avg. Max Toxicity, the lowest among all compared methods including RAD (0.114).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SASA outperforms RAD (the state-of-the-art decoding-based method requiring an external reward model) across all three benchmarks: on challenging RTP, SASA achieves 0.426 vs 0.481 Avg. Max Toxicity (10% reduction) at similar perplexity; on AttaQ, SASA achieves 0.142 vs 0.264 (42% reduction) at lower perplexity; on BOLD, SASA achieves 0.023 vs 0.050. Against retraining-based methods (DAPT, PPO, Quark) and other decoding-based methods (PPLM, GeDi, DExperts, Rectification) on non-toxic RTP with GPT2-Large, SASA (0.083) achieves the lowest Avg. Max Toxicity, beating the next-best Rectification (0.180) by more than 2×. Among external-model-free methods on challenging RTP with GPT2-Large, SASA (0.267 at perplexity 15.4) substantially outperforms Self-Debiasing (0.263–0.380 at perplexity 14.3–23.5) and ToxificationReversal (0.773 at perplexity 45.8), with SASA achieving comparable or lower toxicity at 2–3× lower perplexity than ToxificationReversal. The key practical advantage is that SASA requires no external reward model, no gradient computation at inference, and no LM retraining, while providing a theoretical optimality guarantee for its sampling policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SASA sits within the decoding-based detoxification literature (DExperts, GeDi, Rectification, RAD) but departs from all of them by eliminating the need for an external reward model or classifier. Its closest conceptual relative is Self-debiasing (Schick et al., 2021), which also exploits the LM's internal capacity, but SASA formalizes the steering mechanism as a Bayes-optimal linear subspace in the LM's own embedding space and derives the sampling policy as the exact solution to a KL-regularized alignment objective. The paper explicitly notes that RAD arrives at the same re-weighting formula (Eq. 1) without theoretical justification, positioning SASA as providing the missing optimization-theoretic grounding for that family of decoding interventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SASA operates entirely within a frozen LM's inference pipeline, making it applicable in API-only or parameter-locked settings where retraining or attaching a reward model is infeasible. A notable finding is that instruction-tuned models (Llama-3.1-Instruct) benefit disproportionately from SASA (68% toxicity drop vs. 51% for the unaligned Llama-2), suggesting alignment enriches the internal representations that the subspace exploits. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-attribute constraints (e.g., simultaneously reducing toxicity and hallucination), how performance degrades on smaller models whose embeddings may not encode toxicity as cleanly, and whether the linear-subspace assumption holds for more nuanced or culturally variable notions of harm.
