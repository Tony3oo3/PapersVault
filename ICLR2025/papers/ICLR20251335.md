## Problem

Existing in-context RL methods (AD, DPT, IDT) imitate the learning histories of a source RL algorithm and thereby inherit its suboptimal behaviors, since gradient-based algorithms update policies gradually and may repeat suboptimal actions before fully integrating new information. No prior in-context RL framework provides a mechanism for the agent to deviate from the source algorithm's behavior and immediately pursue reward-maximizing actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251335/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a current observation o_t and a learning history h_{t-1} (a sequence of past observation-action-reward tuples) as a single tokenized sequence fed into a Transformer. At test time, rather than directly outputting the policy's predicted action, the agent runs a planning subroutine that samples multiple candidate actions, simulates their consequences using the in-context learned dynamics model, and returns the first action of the highest-return path. The dynamics model jointly predicts the next observation, immediate reward, and return-to-go, all conditioned on the same shared Transformer parameters as the policy.

## Method

DICP introduces a meta-model g_θ that shares the same Transformer parameters θ as the policy f_θ, trained with a combined loss L(θ) = L_Im(θ) + λ·L_Dyn(θ), where L_Dyn predicts reward, next observation, and return-to-go given the current observation, action, and history. At meta-test time, the agent employs Model Predictive Control: it samples L candidate actions per step, uses g_θ to roll out future observations and rewards over a planning horizon, and selects the action from the top-K beam ranked by cumulative predicted return. Because the dynamics model is learned in-context independently of the source algorithm's gradual update rule, planning with it provides an explicit mechanism to override suboptimal imitated behaviors. The framework is architecture-agnostic, plugging the dynamics loss into AD, DPT, or IDT backbones (yielding DICP-AD, DICP-DPT, DICP-IDT) and supporting both discrete (categorical) and continuous (Gaussian) action/observation spaces.

## Results

On Meta-World ML1, DICP-IDT achieves 100% success rate on Reach, Push, Pick-Place, and Sweep, and 87% on Peg-Insert-Side, using only 200K environment steps. This surpasses all prior meta-RL methods, which require 10M–300M steps to reach comparable or lower performance. On the full 50-task ML1 benchmark, DICP-IDT achieves a mean success rate of 80%, compared to 75% for IDT and 68% for AD. On ML10, DICP-IDT reaches 46.9% success rate with 500K steps, outperforming IDT (36.7%) and all gradient-based meta-RL baselines (≤35.8% at 350M steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the in-context model-free counterparts, DICP-IDT dominates IDT on Push (100 vs. 94), Pick-Place (100 vs. 95), Sweep (100 vs. 96), and Peg-Insert-Side (87 vs. 76), while tying on Reach (100). DICP-AD similarly outperforms AD on Sweep (71 vs. 67) and Peg-Insert-Side (83 vs. 73), though it slightly trails on Reach (84 vs. 86) and Push (87 vs. 88). Against gradient-based meta-RL methods (RL², MAML, PEARL), DICP-IDT matches or exceeds their success rates on Reach, Push, and Pick-Place while using 1,500× fewer environment steps (200K vs. 300M). Against offline meta-RL baselines (MACAW, FOCAL, BOReL, IDAQ), DICP-IDT achieves dramatically higher success rates on Sweep (100 vs. 0–59) and Peg-Insert-Side (87 vs. 0–30). Against model-based baselines, DICP-IDT matches MuZero (100 on Reach/Push/Pick-Place) with 50× fewer steps (200K vs. 10M) and beats MoSS on Reach (100 vs. 86) while matching on Push and Pick-Place. On the discrete Darkroom environments, DICP consistently outperforms AD, DPT, and IDT in both sample efficiency and final performance, with beam search (K=10) providing the largest gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context RL line initiated by Algorithm Distillation (Laskin et al., 2023), DPT (Lee et al., 2023a), and IDT (Huang et al., 2024), all of which train Transformers to imitate a source RL algorithm's learning history and directly predict actions at test time. The key departure is the addition of an in-context learned dynamics model (sharing the same Transformer parameters) combined with Model Predictive Control (beam search or greedy search) for action selection, making DICP the first model-based approach within the in-context RL framework. Conceptually, the paper argues that dynamics modeling is independent of the source algorithm's gradual update rule, so planning with the learned dynamics provides a mechanism to escape the suboptimal behaviors that model-free in-context RL inevitably inherits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DICP achieves state-of-the-art success rates on Meta-World ML1 and ML10 while requiring only 200K environment steps versus 10M+ for prior meta-RL methods, a roughly 50× sample-efficiency gap that positions in-context RL as a highly competitive meta-RL paradigm. The framework is agnostic to the choice of source algorithm (validated with both PPO and SAC) and base architecture (AD, DPT, IDT), making it a broadly applicable wrapper. Limitations include the additional inference-time computation for planning (up to ~18 GFLOPs per action) and the reliance on offline learning histories from a single source algorithm. Open directions include adaptive planning horizons, incorporating expert demonstrations, and extending to non-stationary or changing dynamics.
