## Problem

Existing jailbreak defenses for LLMs—prompt engineering, perplexity filtering, and safety fine-tuning—introduce computational overhead, increase inference latency, and lack real-time flexibility, while overly restrictive safety measures degrade utility by refusing benign queries. No prior method enables lightweight, runtime adjustment of the safety-utility balance without modifying input prompts or requiring retraining.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method intercepts the hidden state at the last token position of the input prompt (a d-dimensional vector per selected layer) and shifts it along a precomputed safety direction before the model continues generation. The safety direction and sparsity mask are computed offline via PCA on benign/harmful prompt hidden states and are shared across all inputs, so the only per-query operation is a single element-wise addition with negligible cost. The output is the model's standard text response, with the safety-utility trade-off controlled by a scalar α and sparsity ratio k chosen at deployment time.

## Method

Jailbreak Antidote identifies a safety direction in the model's hidden-state space by performing PCA on last-token hidden states collected from benign and harmful prompts, yielding the first principal component as the direction of maximum benign-harmful variance. A key empirical finding is that this direction is sparsely encoded: its components follow a long-tail distribution, so retaining only the top ~5% of dimensions (via a binary mask) preserves nearly all safety signal. During inference, the hidden state at the last token position is shifted by α times the masked safety direction at selected layers, where α controls the strength of the safety adjustment (positive α increases safety, negative α weakens it). Because the direction and mask are precomputed constants, the per-token overhead is a single vector addition with no additional tokens, no retraining, and no change to the model architecture, enabling real-time tuning of the safety-utility balance across models from 2B to 72B parameters.

## Results

Jailbreak Antidote achieves the highest Defense Success Rate (DSR) across all nine models, reaching 100% on Llama-3-70B-it, 99.4% on Llama-3-8B-it, and 95.5% on Qwen-2-7B-it, while simultaneously maintaining the highest AlpacaEval Win Rate (47.4–53.5%) across all models. Notably, the method preserves utility at or above the 50% baseline Win Rate for every model, whereas competing defenses like Semantic Smooth LLM and Smooth LLM drop Win Rate to as low as 19.9% (Phi-3-mini-it). The method also introduces the shortest runtime per query among all defenses, with inference time multipliers of 0.79×–1.04× relative to the undefended baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Jailbreak Antidote outperforms all six prompt-based defenses (ICL, Paraphrase, Perplexity Filter, Self-Reminder, Semantic Smooth LLM, Smooth LLM) on DSR for 8 out of 9 models, with the largest margins on Llama-3-8B-it (99.4% vs. 88.1% for Semantic Smooth LLM) and Llama-3-70B-it (100% vs. 88.2% for Smooth LLM). Critically, it also achieves the highest Win Rate on every model, whereas prompt-based methods like Semantic Smooth LLM and Smooth LLM degrade utility by 15–30 percentage points below baseline (e.g., Smooth LLM drops to 19.9% Win Rate on Phi-3-mini-it). Against safety-alignment defenses, Jailbreak Antidote applied to Llama-3-8B-it-RR achieves 99.6% DSR (vs. 95.6% for Smooth LLM on the same model) while maintaining 53.5% Win Rate, and on Gemma-2-9B-it-DSA it reaches 83.6% DSR with 48.6% Win Rate, matching the aligned model's baseline utility. The Perplexity Filter is the closest competitor on Win Rate (within 1–2 points) but lags significantly on DSR (e.g., 67.9% vs. 99.4% on Llama-3-8B-it). Overall, Jailbreak Antidote is the only method that simultaneously maximizes both safety and utility across all tested models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends representation engineering (Zou et al., 2023a), which identifies a direction in hidden-state space via PCA and shifts activations along it, by applying the technique specifically to jailbreak defense at inference time. The key conceptual departure is the discovery that safety-related information is sparsely encoded—only the top ~5% of dimensions in the safety direction carry meaningful signal—allowing a masked, targeted adjustment rather than a full-state shift. Unlike prior prompt-engineering defenses (ICL, Self-Reminder) or smoothing methods (SmoothLLM, SemanticSmoothLLM) that add token overhead, and unlike safety fine-tuning (RLHF, Safe RLHF) that is costly and inflexible, this approach operates purely on internal activations with zero additional tokens and a tunable strength parameter α for real-time safety-utility trade-off control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sparsity finding—that 5% of hidden-state dimensions suffice for effective safety adjustment—has practical implications for deployment, enabling lightweight runtime safety controls without retraining or prompt modification. However, the method requires white-box access to internal activations, limiting its applicability to closed-source or API-only models. A notable open concern is the dual-use nature of the mechanism: setting α to negative values converts the defense into a white-box attack, raising questions about how to secure the adjustment pipeline itself against adversarial manipulation. Future work on adaptive attacks that specifically target the sparse safety dimensions, and on extending the approach to other alignment properties (fairness, bias), would strengthen the contribution.
