## Problem

Pre-trained language models are vulnerable to textual backdoor attacks planted during pre-training, which stealthily affect multiple downstream tasks while remaining undetectable on benign inputs. Existing defenses are either empirical (lacking theoretical guarantees) or restricted to the downstream fine-tuning phase, and traditional randomized smoothing methods do not proactively identify the malicious knowledge embedded in model parameters, resulting in poison-agnostic passive defense. This paper addresses the gap of providing certified, post-attack robustness guarantees against pre-training backdoor attacks without access to the original poisoned corpus.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a potentially trigger-perturbed text input x', poisoned pre-trained model parameters θ'_P, and a clean fine-tuning dataset D_F. Perturbations are measured in normalized Damerau-Levenshtein edit distance (allowing insertion, deletion, substitution, and transposition of tokens). The output is a class label y* with a certified robustness radius R_r, guaranteeing prediction consistency for any perturbation up to R_r·L tokens with confidence 1−α, where L is the input length.

## Method

FRS integrates three components: (1) Biphased Model Parameter Smoothing (BMPS) adds Gaussian noise to the top-H layers during both fine-tuning iterations and K independent inference-phase copies, approximating the standard K-model ensemble without K separate training runs; (2) Fuzzed Text Randomization (FTR) employs Monte Carlo Tree Search over a segment tree of the input, scoring nodes by KL-divergence-based deviation of model outputs after Damerau-Levenshtein mutations, to identify vulnerable segments likely containing triggers; (3) a probability-weighted randomization strategy then applies higher mutation probability ω_H to identified vulnerable segments and lower ω_L elsewhere, constrained within a Damerau-Levenshtein distance threshold Λ. The theoretical contribution proves a broader certified robustness radius R_r^new = log(ω_M)/log(ω_H) · R_r^old, where ω_M is the uniform randomization probability in standard smoothing, yielding a provably larger radius since ω_M < ω_H.

## Results

On BERT-base under the RIPPLe_a attack, FRS achieves 82.36% clean accuracy (CA), 73.25% poisoned accuracy (PA), and 45.12% attack success rate (ASR) on SST-2, outperforming the strongest certified baseline TextGuard (74.95% CA, 65.24% PA, 52.62% ASR) by 7.4, 8.0, and 7.5 percentage points respectively. Across all three datasets (SST-2, OffensEval, AG's News) and all three attack methods (RIPPLe_a, LWP, BadPre), FRS consistently achieves the highest CA and PA while attaining the lowest ASR. The certified robustness radius is also broader: FRS achieves an average radius of 34.87% on SST-2 versus 27.51% for TextGuard, and 29.24% on AG's News versus 21.68%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRS outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all three datasets and all three attack methods. Against the strongest empirical defense (Bite), FRS improves PA by 10.9 points and reduces ASR by 12.7 points on SST-2 under RIPPLe_a (73.25% vs. 62.32% PA; 45.12% vs. 57.80% ASR). The training-time defenses BKI and R-Adaptor perform worst overall, with ASR values exceeding 70% on SST-2 under RIPPLe_a, indicating they fail to effectively neutralize pre-training backdoors. The certified baseline TextGuard is the closest competitor but suffers from significantly lower CA (e.g., 74.95% vs. 82.36% on SST-2), attributed to its word-hashing mechanism breaking syntactic and semantic integrity. FRS also achieves a broader certified robustness radius than TextGuard (34.87% vs. 27.51% average on SST-2; 29.24% vs. 21.68% on AG's News), validating the theoretical advantage from Corollary 1. The advantage of FRS over TextGuard is most pronounced on AG's News, where the gap in average robustness radius reaches 34.87% relative improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends randomized smoothing (Cohen et al., 2019), originally developed for certified adversarial robustness in vision, to the textual backdoor attack setting for pre-trained language models. It departs from prior certified text defenses (e.g., TextGuard, interval bound propagation) by incorporating software-verification-inspired fuzzing via Monte Carlo tree search to proactively identify trigger-containing segments, and from prior randomized-smoothing-for-backdoors work (Xie et al., 2021; Weber et al., 2023) by operating in the discrete Damerau-Levenshtein text space under a post-attack setting where poisoned pre-training data is inaccessible. The biphased parameter smoothing replaces the computationally prohibitive K-way data randomization with targeted noise on top-H layers, drawing on the observation that backdoor knowledge concentrates in output-proximal layers (Kurita et al., 2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pre-training-phase backdoor attacks threaten the entire PLM ecosystem since a single poisoned checkpoint can compromise all downstream applications, yet certified defenses for this specific threat model were largely absent before this work. The practical limitation of requiring K=20 model copies at inference and the observed diminishing returns on LLaMA3-8B (where inherent model robustness narrows the defense gap to ~3 percentage points) raise open questions about scalability to future 100B+ models. The paper's own discussion acknowledges that adaptive, architecture-aware defense strategies and complementary techniques (adversarial training, distillation) will be needed to maintain FRS's effectiveness as models scale.
