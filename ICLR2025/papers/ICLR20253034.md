## Problem

Standard vision benchmarks (ImageNet-1K, COCO-80) evaluate models on many-class datasets, yet real-world deployments typically involve only 2-10 classes. This gap makes it impossible to predict few-class performance from many-class results, and no existing benchmark systematically studies how model selection, scaling laws, and dataset difficulty behave in this Few-Class Regime.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes RGB images with class labels organized in an ImageNet-style directory structure; few-class subsets are specified purely through lightweight configuration files (class lists + seed numbers) that index into a single copy of the full dataset, avoiding data duplication. For the similarity component, pairs of images are encoded by the CLIP image encoder or DINOv2 and their cosine similarity is normalized to [0,1]. Outputs include per-subset Top-1 accuracy with standard deviation across 5 random seeds, the SimSS difficulty scalar per dataset, and cross-model rankings at each N_CL.

## Method

Few-Class Arena (FCA) is a benchmark tool built on MMPreTrain that provides three sub-benchmarks: FC-Full (evaluate pre-trained full-class models on randomly sampled few-class subsets), FC-Sub (train and test models exclusively on the same few-class subset), and FC-Sim (compute a dataset difficulty score). The key novelty is SimSS, a Similarity-Based Silhouette Score that replaces the classic Silhouette's distance terms a(i) and b(i) with intra-class similarity S_alpha(i) and a newly defined nearest inter-class similarity S_beta_prime(i), both computed via cosine similarity in the CLIP or DINOv2 latent space. This yields a per-instance score aggregated to class and dataset level that captures both cluster tightness and proximity to the nearest competing class. Experiments across 10 models, 10 datasets, and N_CL from 2 to 1000 (1591 runs) reveal that the standard scaling law breaks for sub-models in the Few-Class Regime, while SimSS achieves Pearson r >= 0.88 with sub-model accuracy, enabling difficulty-aware model selection without exhaustive training.

## Results

Across 1,591 training and testing runs (10 models × 10 datasets × multiple class counts), no single model achieves the best Top-1 accuracy on all datasets — e.g., ResNet50 ranks 1st on Quickdraw345 (69.14%) but 7th on ImageNet1K (76.55%), while EfficientNet V2 leads on ImageNet1K (85.01%) and CIFAR-100 (77.05%). In the Few-Class Regime (N_CL ≤ 10), sub-models trained on the target subset consistently outperform full models: on CIFAR-100 with N_CL=2, ResNet50 sub-model reaches 95.30% vs. 71.30% for the full model. The proposed SimSS difficulty metric achieves Pearson correlation r = 0.90 (CLIP) and r = 0.88 (DINOv2) with sub-model accuracy, versus only r = 0.18–0.26 with full-model accuracy, validating it as a reliable proxy for few-class dataset difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper, so the primary comparisons are between model types (full, sub, fine-tuned) and across architectures rather than against a single prior method. Full models (trained on all classes) show increasing variance and inconsistent scaling in the Few-Class Regime: ResNet50 full-model accuracy drops from 76.55% (1000 classes) to 70.60% (2 classes) on ImageNet with standard deviation growing from 0 to 14.67. Sub-models (trained on the target subset) reverse this trend, reaching 94.80% at N_CL=2 with std of only 2.17, and violate the standard scaling law — larger ResNets do not necessarily outperform smaller ones at few classes. Fine-tuned models (e.g., ResNet50 FT on CIFAR-100, N_CL=2: 93.70%) fall between full and sub-models, confirming that sub-models capture the most discriminative features for the target classes. Across the 10-dataset benchmark, model rankings shift dramatically (ResNet50 goes from 1st on Quickdraw345 to 7th on ImageNet1K), demonstrating that many-class benchmark rankings are unreliable for few-class deployment. SimSS using CLIP or DINOv2 as the similarity backbone correlates with sub-model accuracy at r ≥ 0.88, far exceeding its correlation with full-model accuracy (r ≤ 0.26), establishing it as a practical difficulty estimator for model selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Rousseeuw's Silhouette Score by replacing its distance-based terms with intra-class and nearest inter-class similarity scores (adopted from Cao et al., 2023) to create SimSS, a dataset difficulty metric tailored to the few-class setting. It departs from the dominant paradigm of evaluating vision models on many-class benchmarks (ImageNet-1K, COCO-80) by arguing that real-world applications involve only 2–10 classes, a regime the authors term the 'Few-Class Regime' and claim has been largely unaddressed. The similarity base functions leverage CLIP and DINOv2 as general-purpose visual feature extractors, extending prior difficulty-measurement work (Scheidegger et al., 2021; Mayo et al., 2023) that relied on dataset-specific probe nets or neuroscience-based metrics. The benchmark is integrated into the MMPreTrain framework, positioning it as a practical extension of existing training infrastructure rather than a standalone system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FCA benchmark and SimSS metric offer practitioners a concrete tool to estimate achievable accuracy in few-class deployments without exhaustive training across all candidate models, potentially saving significant compute. The finding that the standard scaling law (larger model → higher accuracy) breaks down for sub-models in the Few-Class Regime challenges a widely held assumption and suggests that model selection strategies optimized for many-class benchmarks may be misleading for few-class applications. Key limitations include reliance on CLIP/DINOv2 as similarity base functions (unclear generalization to texture-only or sketch datasets), evaluation primarily on image classification, and the SimSS correlation demonstrated mainly on ResNet variants of ImageNet. Open questions include whether a universal similarity foundation model exists for all image types and how SimSS interacts with representation-similarity-based efficiency metrics.
