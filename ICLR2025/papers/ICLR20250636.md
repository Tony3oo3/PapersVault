## Problem

Existing methods for solving linear inverse problems with pre-trained flow matching or diffusion priors either require expensive iterative optimization at each sampling step or fail to produce high-quality reconstructions under challenging conditions such as high measurement noise and severe ill-posedness. The core difficulty is that the measurement likelihood q(y|x_t) is intractable because the measurement y depends only on the clean signal x_0, not on the noisy intermediate state x_t, making direct Bayesian posterior sampling impractical.

## Input / Output

The method takes a linear measurement y = Ax* + n (e.g., a 4× or 16× bicubic-downsampled image, a Gaussian/motion-blurred image, or a 90%-masked inpainting observation) with known forward operator A and Gaussian noise level σ_n, along with a pre-trained unconditional flow matching (Rectified Flow) or diffusion (EDM) model. It outputs a 256×256×3 RGB reconstruction. The algorithm is entirely training-free: no fine-tuning of the generative model is required, and the only task-specific hyperparameters are the guidance weight c, the number of conditional gradient steps K, and the variance rescaling factor w.

## Method

FIG's key insight is to interpolate the measurement y in parallel to the forward process of x, producing measurement interpolants y_t = α_t y + w σ_t Aε_x. Because the measurement model is linear, this parallel interpolation guarantees that the conditional likelihood q_t(y_t|x_t) is Gaussian with mean Ax_t and variance α_t²σ_n²I for all t, making the likelihood score ∇_x log q_t(y_t|x_t) = -(1/(2α_t²σ_n²))∇_x‖y_t − Ax_t‖² analytically tractable. Theorem 1 then shows that the conditional velocity field decomposes as v_t(x|y_t) = (ḋα_t/α_t)x − λ_t σ_t s_t(x|y_t), which further splits into the unconditional velocity v_t(x) plus a guidance term λ_t σ_t ∇_x log q_t(y_t|x_t), yielding the reverse-time ODE d x = −v_t(x)dt + λ_t σ_t ∇_x log q_t(y_t|x)dt. Numerically, each timestep performs one Euler step of the unconditional velocity followed by K gradient-descent steps on the measurement data-fidelity term, with a rescaled variance parameter w that balances measurement information against the prior to avoid overfitting. For inpainting, FIG+ additionally estimates x̂_0 via Tweedie's formula and mixes its masked-region values into x_t to inject information where the measurement carries no signal.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ 256×256 with σn=0.05, FIG-Diffusion achieves the best PSNR on 3 of 4 tasks: 29.89 (SR 4×), 29.83 (Gaussian Deblur), and 31.81 (Motion Deblur), while FIG-Flow leads the flow-matching group with 29.41, 26.83, 30.84, and 27.08 PSNR across the four tasks. On the challenging high-noise 4× SR (σn=1.0), FIG achieves 24.47 PSNR, outperforming DPS (22.50) and DMPS (24.06), while OT-ODE collapses to 15.14. FIG also maintains competitive runtime (2.80 s/image at 50 NFEs for FIG-Flow) and low memory (7.7 GB), compared to 28.4 s and 28.7 GB for DDNM+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPS (both flow and diffusion variants), FIG wins on SR 4×, Gaussian Deblur, and Motion Deblur with margins of 0.7–8.9 dB PSNR (e.g., FIG-Flow 26.83 vs. DPS-Flow 22.95 on Gaussian Deblur; FIG-Diffusion 31.81 vs. DPS-Diffusion 30.69 on Motion Deblur), but loses on Inpainting where DPS-Diffusion reaches 28.23 PSNR vs. FIG-Diffusion's 27.36. Against DMPS, FIG-Flow dominates all four tasks; DMPS catastrophically fails on 90% inpainting (12.66 PSNR) and is notably weaker on Motion Deblur (29.81 vs. 30.84). Against OT-ODE, FIG-Flow leads on every task, and the gap widens dramatically under high noise: on 4× SR with σn=1.0, OT-ODE collapses to 15.14 PSNR while FIG maintains 24.47. Against DDNM+, FIG-Diffusion achieves substantially better Gaussian Deblur (29.83 vs. 7.84 PSNR) and SR (29.89 vs. 28.82), though DDNM+ edges out FIG on Inpainting (28.70 vs. 27.36). Against DAPS, FIG-Diffusion wins on SR 4× (29.89 vs. 29.59), Gaussian Deblur (29.83 vs. 29.74), and Motion Deblur (31.81 vs. 31.67), but trails on Inpainting (27.36 vs. 27.85); DAPS also requires 1000 NFEs and 59.6 s per image versus FIG's 100 NFEs and 4.1 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIG builds on the measurement-interpolant idea from Song et al. (2022) and the filtering perspective of Dou & Song (2024), but departs from prior guidance-based methods (DPS, DMPS, OT-ODE, DAPS) by making the measurement likelihood tractable at every timestep through parallel interpolation of y alongside x. The theoretical justification (Theorem 1) draws on the velocity-score decomposition from Ma et al. (2024), showing that the conditional velocity field decomposes into an unconditional part plus a closed-form Gaussian likelihood score. Unlike methods that project onto measurement subspaces or approximate the intractable q(y|x_t) via SVD, FIG's key conceptual move is that adding noise to the measurement is easier than denoising the signal, yielding a simple K-step gradient descent update per timestep.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIG provides a clean, theoretically grounded, and computationally efficient (50 NFEs, ~7.7 GB) algorithm that handles challenging linear inverse problems—high-noise super-resolution and severe ill-posedness—where prior methods degrade significantly. Its task-agnostic design works with any linear forward operator and both flow matching and diffusion priors without retraining. Key limitations are that it is restricted to linear problems (incompatible with latent-space models) and produces approximate rather than exact posterior samples. Open directions include extension to bilinear problems (e.g., blind deblurring) and latent diffusion models, as well as connecting to stochastic optimal control for exact posterior sampling.
