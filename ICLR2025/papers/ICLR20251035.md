## Problem

Existing math word problem benchmarks like GSM8K feature concise descriptions, leaving the impact of longer narrative contexts on LLM mathematical reasoning largely unexplored. This paper investigates whether LLMs exhibit a performance degradation (Context Length Generalizability, CoLeG) when solving MWPs with extended narratives, and proposes targeted solutions for both proprietary and open-source models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251035/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a math word problem with extended narrative context, where E-GSM variants range from ~77 tokens (original GSM8K) to ~386 tokens (round-4 extensions) while preserving the original conditions and their order. The output is a numerical answer accompanied by a step-by-step reasoning chain. For the CoRe prompt, the model first produces an explicit list of extracted conditions and the final goal before generating the reasoning chain; for SFT models, the training format follows a standard instruction-response template with the reasoning path and answer as the target sequence.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary strategies. For proprietary LLMs, CoRe (Condition-Retrieving Instruction) is a prompting technique inspired by cognitive load theory that splits problem solving into two stages: first, the model is instructed to retrieve all relevant conditions and the ultimate objective from the lengthy narrative, then it applies standard 0-shot CoT to solve the problem using only the extracted information. For open-source LLMs, extension is used as an auxiliary SFT task: GPT-4-turbo iteratively extends GSM8K questions over 4 rounds (with quality control via entailment scoring and LLM solvability checks), GPT-3.5-turbo generates 5 CoT reasoning paths per extended question (filtering for correct answers), and the resulting ~65K (question, reasoning, answer) tuples are used for supervised fine-tuning. The paper further shows via informativeness and missing-step metrics that the accuracy gains stem primarily from improved mathematical reasoning pathways rather than enhanced semantic understanding.

## Results

On E-GSM, all LLMs show a consistent accuracy decline from round 0 to round 4 (e.g., Claude-3-opus drops from 94.09% to 80.99% with 0-CoT; LLaMA-2-7B drops from 58.45% to 38.95% with SFT on D0). CoRe prompting improves CoLeG-E by 3.1–4.5% and Acc4 by 2.2–5.3% over 0-CoT across proprietary LLMs (e.g., GPT-3.5-turbo: CoLeG-E 51.97% vs. 46.63%; Acc4 69.76% vs. 64.42%). For open-source LLMs, SFT with extension (D) yields an average +5.9% CoLeG-E and +9.12% CoLeG-R over SFT without extension (D0), with the largest gain on LLaMA-2-7B (+7.87% CoLeG-E, +14.33% CoLeG-R). On generalization benchmarks (MAWPS, SVAMP, GSM-IC), CoRe adds 4.5% on MAWPS and 4.15% on GSM-IC, while extension-finetuned models gain an average +15.28% accuracy across model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoRe consistently outperforms both 0-CoT (Kojima et al., 2022) and Plan-and-Solve (Wang et al., 2023) across all four proprietary LLMs on CoLeG-E, CoLeG-R, and per-round accuracy, with the largest gains in later rounds (e.g., GPT-3.5-turbo Acc4: 69.76% vs. 64.42% for 0-CoT and 63.30% for PS). For open-source LLMs, SFT with extension substantially surpasses SFT without extension (D₀) on every model: LLaMA-2-7B improves CoLeG-R from 66.64% to 80.97%, and Mistral-7B from 75.19% to 83.65%. Compared to specialized math LLMs, SFT with extension on LLaMA-2-7B (CoLeG-E 28.09%) approaches MetaMath-7B (31.74%) despite using only ~65K CoT data versus MetaMath's 400K, and extension further improves MetaMath itself (CoLeG-E 31.74% → 38.01%). Extension-finetuned models also outperform MAmmoTH-7B (CoLeG-E 11.52% → 30.15% with SFT on D) and WizardMath-7B (11.89% → 31.37%), as well as Llemma-7B (2.06% → 33.05%) and DeepSeekMath-7B (42.98% → 51.31%), demonstrating broad generalizability of the extension auxiliary task across diverse math-specialized backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot CoT prompting paradigm (Kojima et al., 2022) by prepending a condition-retrieval step inspired by cognitive load theory, and extends the GSM8K benchmark by constructing E-GSM with iteratively lengthened narratives while preserving conditions and answers. It is conceptually distinct from MetaMath's rewriting-based augmentation (Yu et al., 2023), which alters problem conditions, whereas extension here adds narrative context without changing the mathematical structure. The fine-grained analysis draws on ROSCOE's informativeness and missing-step metrics (Golovneva et al., 2023) to disentangle semantic understanding from reasoning efficacy, and the evaluation protocol builds on prior work showing LLMs are distracted by irrelevant context (Shi et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously under-quantified failure mode—degradation in math reasoning as narrative length increases while difficulty stays constant—and provides two lightweight, practical remedies (CoRe prompting for API models, extension-as-auxiliary-task for open-source fine-tuning) that generalize to other MWP benchmarks. The CoLeG-E and CoLeG-R metrics offer a richer evaluation lens than raw GSM8K accuracy, potentially influencing how the community reports and compares math reasoning capabilities. Open questions include whether extension composes with other augmentation strategies (e.g., MetaMath's multi-perspective rewriting), whether the approach transfers to harder problem sets (MATH, Olympiad-level), and whether the observed reasoning-pathway improvement (vs. unchanged semantic understanding) reflects a fundamental architectural limitation or a fixable training-data gap.
