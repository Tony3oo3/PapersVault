## Problem

Pre-training data selection for LLMs remains an unresolved challenge because existing methods rely on limited heuristics and human intuition (e.g., educational value, writing style) rather than comprehensive, principled criteria. While deduplication and domain mixing are standard, they serve only as a priori or post-hoc steps, and no prior work provides a unified framework that simultaneously captures multiple complementary quality dimensions and domain structure for data selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250351/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DataMan takes a single text document (up to 2,048 tokens, averaging 810) as input and produces a structured annotation: 14 scalar quality ratings on a 1–5 integer scale (Accuracy, Coherence, Language Consistency, Semantic Density, Knowledge Novelty, Topic Focus, Creativity, Professionalism, Style Consistency, Grammatical Diversity, Structural Standardization, Originality, Sensitivity, and Overall Score) plus one domain label from 15 predefined categories (Medicine, Finance, Law, Education, Technology, etc.). The output is generated autoregressively as a single text-generation pass, making it suitable for large-scale parallel annotation of the 447B-token DataPajama corpus.

## Method

The key insight is 'reverse thinking': rather than hand-crafting quality criteria, the authors prompt a Super LLM to analyze documents with anomalously high or low perplexity (top/bottom 2%) and self-identify which textual properties drive those anomalies, iteratively refining 13 individual criteria plus a composite Overall Score. DataMan is a Qwen2-1.5B model fine-tuned via pointwise learning-to-rank (L2R) with text-generation loss on 35,700 Super-LLM-annotated documents, chosen over pairwise rating for cost efficiency (N ratings vs. N×(N−1) comparisons) and better handling of marginal quality differences. For data selection, the authors apply top-k sampling per quality criterion while balancing source and domain distribution probabilities, implicitly steering training toward reward-weighted regression. The annotated 447B-token DataPajama corpus is then used to sample 30B-token subsets for training a 1.3B-parameter Sheared-Llama model from scratch, with the Overall Score l=5 subset outperforming a uniform-sampling baseline trained on 50% more data.

## Results

Training a 1.3B-parameter Sheared-LLaMA on 30B tokens selected by DataMan's Overall Score l=5 achieves an average ICL score of 49.1 across 10 downstream tasks, surpassing the SOTA baseline (Educational Value τ=2.0, 46.7) by 2.4 points and even outperforming a model trained on 50% more data via uniform sampling (44.9). The best individual DataMan criterion (Style Consistency, top-k) reaches 48.7 average ICL with a test perplexity of 10.73. In instruction-following evaluation, the Overall Score l=5 model achieves a 78.5% win rate over the SOTA baseline after SFT on 10K ShareGPT examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DataMan's Overall Score l=5 (49.1 avg ICL) outperforms the SOTA baseline Educational Value τ=2.0 (46.7) by 2.4 points, and all 13 individual DataMan criteria improve ICL by 0.4–4.3% over this baseline. Traditional model-output-based methods (DSIR and perplexity filtering) perform worse than random uniform sampling (42.9 and 41.7 vs. 46.8 avg ICL), indicating they are ineffective for data selection at this scale. Notably, DataMan's Overall Score l=5 surpasses the Uniform+50% data model (44.9) by 4.2 points, demonstrating that quality-ranked selection with 30B tokens beats uniform sampling with 45B tokens. Qurating's criteria mix (45.7) underperforms its individual best criterion (Educational Value, 46.7), suggesting a lack of complementarity among those four criteria, whereas DataMan's 13 criteria are complementary and their composite Overall Score achieves the best result. In instruction-following, all Sample-with-DataMan models beat the SOTA baseline with win rates of 67.1–78.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM data-selection literature (deduplication, heuristic filtering, DSIR, perplexity filtering, and Qurating's four-criteria approach) by replacing human-intuition-driven criteria with 14 quality dimensions derived via 'reverse thinking'—prompting a Super LLM to diagnose causes of perplexity anomalies in top/bottom 2% documents. It departs from Qurating's pairwise rating in favor of pointwise learning-to-rank (L2R), and adds a 15-way domain recognition head, unifying quality scoring and domain mixing into a single 'Data Manager' model fine-tuned from Qwen2-1.5B. The pointwise L2R formulation and NDCG error bound are borrowed from the information-retrieval ranking literature (Liu et al., 2007).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The annotated 447B-token DataPajama dataset and the 14-criteria + 15-domain annotation schema provide a reusable, open resource for the community to explore data-quality-to-performance relationships beyond the four Qurating criteria. The finding that these criteria are largely independent of perplexity (Spearman ρ < 0.55) challenges the common assumption that PPL-based filtering captures the same quality dimensions that drive downstream generalization. Key open questions include whether the 14-criteria framework transfers to larger models (>7B) and non-English corpora, whether the ~80% DataMan annotation accuracy is sufficient at scale, and how to combine criteria optimally rather than via a single Overall Score.
