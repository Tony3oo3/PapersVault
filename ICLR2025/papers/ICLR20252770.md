## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) has become the standard technique for boosting diffusion model generation quality, but it requires either training a separate unconditional model or modifying the training loop with periodic label dropping, which reduces training efficiency and complicates multi-condition setups. Moreover, no clear extension of CFG exists for unconditional generation. This paper addresses both gaps by showing that the unconditional score can be estimated from the conditional model alone at inference time, and by proposing a time-step-based guidance signal that works for any diffusion model.

## Input / Output

Both ICG and TSG are purely inference-time modifications applied to pretrained diffusion models with no retraining or fine-tuning. ICG consumes the actual conditioning signal (text prompt, class label, or image) alongside a randomly sampled independent condition (Gaussian noise matched to the condition scale, or a random class/text token) to form the 'unconditional' branch. TSG requires no external condition at all—it only perturbs the internal time-step embedding with zero-mean Gaussian noise scaled by s·t^α, making it directly applicable to unconditional models. Both methods incur the same sampling cost as CFG (two network forward passes per step) and produce synthetic images at the base model's native resolution.

## Method

ICG's key insight, derived from Bayes' theorem, is that replacing the conditioning vector y with a random vector ŷ independent of the noisy input z_t makes the conditional score ∇log p(z_t|ŷ) equal to the unconditional score ∇log p(z_t), so the conditional model itself can serve as the unconditional branch without any auxiliary training. The ICG update formula is identical in structure to CFG (Eq. 5) but substitutes D(z_t, t, ŷ) for the separately trained D(z_t, t, y_null). TSG generalizes the guidance idea by perturbing the time-step embedding with zero-mean Gaussian noise (t̃_emb = t_emb + s·t^α·n) and using the difference between clean and perturbed outputs as the update direction; the paper shows this is equivalent to a Langevin dynamics step in first-order approximation, where the perturbation compensates for per-step sampling errors. Both methods are architecture-agnostic, require no training changes, and can be combined—ICG handles the conditional-to-unconditional contrast while TSG adds a time-step-based quality signal on top.

## Results

ICG matches or slightly outperforms standard CFG across all tested conditional models: on DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet), ICG achieves FID 5.50 vs CFG's 5.56 with higher precision (0.83 vs 0.81); on Stable Diffusion (MS COCO 2017), ICG reaches FID 20.05 vs CFG's 20.13; on Pose-to-Image, ICG improves FID from 14.61 to 13.46; and on MDM, ICG reduces FID from 0.65 to 0.47. For TSG, unconditional Stable Diffusion improves from FID 69.38 to 56.65, and conditional DiT-XL/2 improves from FID 15.49 to 6.39. Combining ICG and TSG on DiT-XL/2 yields FID 5.76, outperforming either method alone (6.47 for ICG, 9.55 for TSG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2022), ICG achieves equal or better FID on every model tested: a marginal improvement on Stable Diffusion (20.05 vs 20.13) and DiT-XL/2 (5.50 vs 5.56), a substantial 1.15 FID gain on Pose-to-Image (13.46 vs 14.61), and a 0.18 FID gain on MDM (0.47 vs 0.65), while requiring no auxiliary unconditional training. Against SAG (Hong et al., 2022) and PAG (Ahn et al., 2024) on unconditional Stable Diffusion, TSG achieves FID 56.65, outperforming SAG (59.34) by 2.69 FID and PAG (62.85) by 6.20 FID, while also improving precision from 0.42/0.48 to 0.54. The paper further demonstrates that removing the CFG label-dropping objective from DiT training and using ICG at inference yields ~30% faster convergence or ~20% lower FID at the same iteration count, showing the standard CFG training procedure is not only unnecessary but actively detrimental to training efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on classifier-free guidance (CFG) by Ho & Salimans (2022), showing that the unconditional score component in CFG's update rule can be approximated by feeding an independent random condition to the conditional model itself, eliminating the need for label dropping or a separate unconditional branch during training. The theoretical justification rests on Bayes' theorem: when the condition is independent of the input, the conditional score reduces to the unconditional score. The paper also introduces time-step guidance (TSG), which extends CFG-like quality boosting to unconditional models by perturbing the time-step embedding, positioning itself as complementary to attention-based guidance methods like SAG and PAG and to the diversity-focused CADS approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone training conditional diffusion models: the paper demonstrates that removing the CFG training objective (label dropping) and using ICG at inference instead yields ~30% faster convergence or ~20% FID reduction, since all training compute is devoted to the conditional score. TSG fills a genuine gap by providing a CFG-analogous quality boost for unconditional models, where no conditional signal exists to exploit. The main open question the authors identify is whether the 2× forward-pass cost inherent to both ICG and TSG (same as CFG) can be eliminated, and whether the hyperparameters (noise scale, power exponent, layer range for TSG) can be made more principled rather than empirically tuned per model.
