## Problem

Minibatch optimal transport (OT) coupling in Conditional Flow Matching straightens sampling trajectories and reduces the number of ODE integration steps needed for inference, but its optimization is confined to individual minibatches, so local assignments are discarded after each step and never inform future minibatches. This limits the quality of the global data-noise coupling approximation, especially as dataset size grows, resulting in unnecessarily curved trajectories and degraded few-step generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252194/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single standard normal noise sample and numerically integrates the learned vector field ODE to produce a generated image, with the number of function evaluations (NFE) controlling the speed-quality trade-off. Training operates on RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-32), 64×64 (ImageNet-64), or 256×256 in the latent space of a pre-trained autoencoder (FFHQ). A key design detail is that multiple noise caches (e.g., 4 for CIFAR-10, 1 for ImageNet) are stored per data point, and one is randomly selected each training step, which artificially enlarges the effective dataset and allows new noise instances to be used at inference without quality degradation.

## Method

LOOM-CFM (Looking Out Of Minibatch-CFM) replaces the discard-and-resample strategy of prior minibatch OT methods with an iterative global assignment that is preserved and refined across training steps. At each iteration, a minibatch of data points is sampled along with their currently assigned noise samples (rather than fresh random noise), a local optimal transport problem is solved via the Hungarian algorithm, and the resulting permutation is composed into the global assignment, which is then saved for future minibatches. This creates implicit communication of local OT structure across different minibatches, progressively approximating the global OT plan. To prevent overfitting to a fixed noise set, multiple noise instances are cached per data point and one is randomly selected per step. The paper proves finite convergence to a stationary transport plan with no negative alternating cycles of length less than the batch size, and demonstrates that the resulting straighter trajectories yield 41–54% FID reductions at 12 NFE over prior minibatch OT methods on CIFAR-10, ImageNet-32, and ImageNet-64.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, LOOM-CFM (4 caches, midpoint solver) achieves FID 4.60 at 12 NFE, reducing FID by 41% compared to OT-CFM (7.77) at the same budget. On ImageNet-32 and ImageNet-64, LOOM-CFM (1 cache) achieves FID 3.89 and 8.49 at 12 NFE respectively, outperforming MFM^w/BatchOT (7.18 and 18.27) by 46% and 54%. Combined with a single reflow step, LOOM-CFM reaches FID 4.49 at 2 NFE on CIFAR-10, surpassing 3-Rectified Flow + distillation (5.21 at 1 NFE). On FFHQ-256 in latent space, LOOM-CFM achieves FID 5.50 at 8 NFE with an ADM backbone, approaching LDM's FID of 4.98 at 50 NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest minibatch-OT baselines, LOOM-CFM consistently wins: on CIFAR-10 it reduces FID from 7.77 to 4.60 at 12 NFE vs. OT-CFM (Tong et al., 2023), and on ImageNet-64 it cuts FID from 18.27 to 8.49 vs. MFM^w/BatchOT (Pooladian et al., 2023). Against NFDM-OT (Bartosh et al., 2024), which learns an additional forward process, LOOM-CFM wins at 12 NFE on all three datasets (e.g., 3.89 vs. 4.11 on ImageNet-32) but underperforms at 4 NFE (13.47 vs. 6.13 on ImageNet-32), indicating NFDM-OT's explicit straightness constraints help at very low step counts. LOOM-CFM + one reflow step (FID 4.49 at 2 NFE on CIFAR-10) outperforms both 2-Rectified Flow + distill (4.85 at 1 NFE) and 3-Rectified Flow + distill (5.21 at 1 NFE) from Liu et al. (2023), showing that a well-initialized single reflow eliminates the need for multiple reflow iterations. On FFHQ-256, LOOM-CFM with an ADM backbone reaches FID 5.50 at 8 NFE, approaching LDM (Rombach et al., 2022) at FID 4.98 but with 6× fewer function evaluations, and substantially outperforming Latent Flow Matching (Dao et al., 2023) which requires 84–88 NFE for comparable quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOOM-CFM extends the minibatch optimal transport coupling framework introduced by Tong et al. (OT-CFM) and Pooladian et al. (BatchOT/MFM^w) within the Conditional Flow Matching training paradigm. The key departure is that prior methods solve the OT assignment independently within each minibatch and discard the result, whereas LOOM-CFM preserves and iteratively refines these local assignments across minibatches, effectively performing a randomized block-coordinate descent toward the global OT plan. The method also draws on the reflow/rectified flow literature (Liu et al.) to demonstrate that its improved coupling yields a stronger initialization for distillation, and contrasts with approaches like Lee et al. and Bartosh et al. that require training auxiliary encoder networks or imposing explicit curvature constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that LOOM-CFM achieves a 41–54% FID reduction at 12 NFE over minibatch OT baselines with negligible additional compute or storage overhead, making it a drop-in improvement for anyone already using OT-based CFM training. Its compatibility with latent-space training (demonstrated on FFHQ-256) and its role as a superior reflow initialization broaden its applicability to high-resolution synthesis and distillation pipelines. A notable limitation, acknowledged by the authors, is incompatibility with conditional generation due to potential sampling bias from fixed couplings, leaving the extension to class-conditional or text-conditional settings as an open problem. The convergence guarantee to a stationary (not globally optimal) matching also means the method inherits the fundamental approximation limits of all minibatch OT schemes.
