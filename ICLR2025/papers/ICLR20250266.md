## Problem

While language model failures such as hallucinations are well-known to correlate with (epistemic) uncertainty, there is a notable lack of decoding methods that actively account for model uncertainty during text generation. Standard Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding selects outputs by maximizing expected utility under a single model distribution, implicitly assuming that one parameter set is a good substitute for the true data-generating distribution—an assumption that breaks down when weight uncertainty is high.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source input x and a collection of model outputs (or token-level probability distributions) drawn from an approximate posterior q(θ) over model parameters, and produces a single selected output string that maximizes expected utility under the predictive posterior. For selective prediction, it additionally outputs an abstention decision based on the expected Bayes risk score. A key practical property is that the sequence-level estimators require only the ability to sample from models (no access to token- or sequence-level probabilities), making them compatible with black-box LLM APIs.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing the single-model distribution p_θ in MBR's expected risk computation with the predictive posterior p_Θ = E_{θ~q}[p_θ], which marginalizes over a posterior distribution on model parameters. This yields three practical estimators: (1) a sequence-level pooled estimator (Eq. 9) that concatenates all hypotheses from M models and applies MBR over the combined set; (2) a sequence-level per-model estimator (Eq. 10) that computes MBR independently per model and sums utilities across models; and (3) a token-level estimator (Eq. 13) that averages next-token probabilities across models before sampling and applying MBR. The posterior is learned via IVON (a variational method with negligible training overhead) for unimodal posteriors, or via Deep Ensembles (multiple independent training runs) for multimodal posteriors. The approach connects MBR to PAC-Bayes bounds and shows that performance improvements correlate with prediction diversity across the ensembled models, scaling with both ensemble size and hypothesis set size.

## Results

On finetuned Gemma-2B (Table 1), the best uncertainty-aware MBR method (sequence-level Eq. 10 with Deep Ensemble) achieves 21.20 BLEU on IWSLT17 En-De and 15.69 BLEU on WMT18 Tr-En, outperforming the single-model MBR@Mean baseline (19.73 and 15.27 BLEU) and the AdamW-trained MBR baseline (19.93 and 14.75 BLEU). Even the zero-overhead unimodal posterior (Eq. 10) reaches 21.08 BLEU on IWSLT17, a +1.35 BLEU gain over MBR@Mean with no additional training or decoding cost. On from-scratch trained Transformers (Table 2), Deep Ensemble posteriors yield up to +1.43 BLEU (28.99 vs. 27.56 on WMT14 En-De beam search), while unimodal posteriors show smaller but consistent gains. Hallucination metrics (LaBSE) and quality estimation (COMET) improve in parallel, with COMET gains on IWSLT17 indicating >85% probability that humans would prefer the uncertainty-aware system.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares uncertainty-aware MBR against two baselines: MBR trained with AdamW (standard training) and MBR@Mean (single model at the mean of the variational posterior). On finetuning (Table 1), the unimodal posterior with zero training overhead consistently outperforms both baselines across all tasks, e.g., +1.35 BLEU over MBR@Mean on IWSLT17 and +0.71 BLEU over MBR(AdamW) on WMT18. The multimodal Deep Ensemble posterior provides further gains (+0.14–0.35 BLEU over unimodal) but requires 4× training time. On from-scratch training (Table 2), unimodal posteriors do not consistently beat the single-model baseline under matched compute, whereas Deep Ensembles deliver clear improvements of +0.4 to +1.4 BLEU. Between the two sequence-level estimators, Eq. (9) (additive union of hypothesis sets) and Eq. (10) (per-model MBR then sum) perform comparably, with Eq. (9) requiring (|M|·|H|)² utility evaluations versus |M|·|H|² for Eq. (10). For zero-shot LLM ensembling (Table 3), Eq. (9) outperforms Eq. (10) by ~0.6 BLEU on IWSLT17 but at higher computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding, originally from statistical machine translation (Kumar & Byrne, 2002) and revived for neural sequence models (Freitag et al., 2022; Eikema & Aziz, 2020), and Bayesian weight uncertainty estimation via variational methods like IVON (Shen et al., 2024) or Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017). The key conceptual move is replacing the single-model distribution in MBR's expected risk with a predictive posterior over model parameters, yielding a principled framework that subsumes prior ad-hoc ensemble strategies (Kobayashi, 2018; Farinhas et al., 2023; González-Rubio et al., 2011) and connects to PAC-Bayes bounds (Alquier, 2024). The paper also extends MBR into selective prediction (Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2017; Ren et al., 2023) by using expected utility as an abstention criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, low-overhead way to ensemble black-box LLMs (requiring only sampling, no probability access) and to decide when to abstain from generation, which is practically relevant as organizations deploy LLMs in high-stakes settings. A notable open question is whether the modest gains observed with Transformer-scale models (86M–261M params) and Gemma-2B finetuning will persist or grow at the scale of frontier LLMs, where weight uncertainty may be better captured by other methods (Laplace, SWAG) that the authors leave for future work. The diversity–quality trade-off highlighted in the paper suggests that simply adding more ensemble members has diminishing returns, pointing toward better posterior approximations as the next frontier.
