## Problem

Differentiable DAG learning methods such as NOTEARS and DAGMA rely on ad-hoc acyclicity constraints (matrix exponential, log-determinant, truncated geometric series) that suffer from gradient vanishing, particularly for linear SEMs with equal noise variances. No unified theoretical framework existed to explain why these constraints work, how they relate to one another, or how to systematically derive new constraints with improved gradient behavior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an n×d matrix of i.i.d. observational samples from a linear SEM and outputs a d×d weighted adjacency matrix whose support defines the estimated DAG. A Hadamard product (B⊙B) maps the unconstrained real-valued adjacency matrix to a non-negative one before applying the analytic DAG constraint. For the unknown-scale setting, an additional correlation-threshold constraint restricts edges to pairs with |Pearson r| > 0.1, and the true DAG is only identifiable up to its Markov equivalence class.

## Method

The paper proves that any analytic function f(x) = c₀ + Σcᵢxⁱ with all cᵢ > 0 and positive convergence radius r = lim cᵢ/cᵢ₊₁ > 0 yields a valid DAG constraint via tr[f(B̃)] = c₀d, unifying NOTEARS (exponential), DAGMA (log-determinant), and Zhang et al. (geometric series) under a single framework. Crucially, the function class F is closed under differentiation, addition, and multiplication, enabling systematic derivation of higher-order constraints (Order-1 through Order-4) from the base functions f_inv^s(x) = (s−x)⁻¹ and f_log^s(x) = −s·log(s−x). Higher-order constraints have larger gradient norms (reducing gradient vanishing) but greater Hessian spectral radius (increasing non-convexity), a trade-off analyzed via Proposition 7. An efficient O(log t) algorithm evaluates the power series using a doubling recurrence L₂ₜ = Lₜ + (B̃/s)ᵗLₜ, and a path-following optimizer with annealed s and l1 regularization solves the resulting constrained problem.

## Results

On large-scale ER graphs with known scale (500–2000 nodes, Gaussian noise), the proposed higher-order analytic DAG constraints consistently outperform DAGMA. For example, on ER4-Gaussian with 2000 nodes, Order-4 achieves SHD 1574.1±152.2 versus DAGMA's 1922.3±187.7, and on ER2-Gaussian with 500 nodes, Order-4 reaches 29.8±21.0 versus DAGMA's 44.9±33.0. On normalized (unknown-scale) 1000-node ER1 graphs, Order-4 achieves SHD 389.4±16.7, substantially outperforming DAGMA (588.8±18.3), PC (563.9±23.8), and GES (4490.2±62.5). On the Sachs nonlinear dataset, replacing the original GraN-DAG constraint with the proposed Order-1–4 constraints reduces SHD from 15 to 13 (and SHD-CPDAG from 10 to 9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DAGMA (Bello et al., 2022), the previous state-of-the-art continuous DAG learning method. The proposed Order-1 through Order-4 constraints outperform DAGMA on nearly all settings: on ER2-Gaussian (500 nodes), Order-4 reduces SHD from 44.9 to 29.8; on ER4-Gaussian (2000 nodes), from 1922.3 to 1574.1; and on ER3-Gaussian (2000 nodes), from 669.5 to 424.9. The improvement is attributed to a tighter annealing strategy for the convergence parameter s derived from analytic function theory, which allows smaller s values and thus less gradient vanishing. On the unknown-scale (normalized) setting, the proposed Order-4 constraint (SHD 389.4) substantially outperforms both the constraint-based PC algorithm (563.9) and the score-based GES algorithm (4490.2), as well as DAGMA (588.8). On the nonlinear Sachs dataset, plugging the proposed constraints into the GraN-DAG framework reduces SHD from 15 (original) to 13, with the gain attributed to reduced gradient vanishing and numerical truncation errors in single-precision mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the continuous DAG learning constraints from NOTEARS (matrix exponential), DAGMA (log-determinant), and Zhang et al. (geometric series) under a single analytic function framework, showing they all belong to a common function class F defined by positive-coefficient power series with positive convergence radius. Building on the path-following optimization framework of DAGMA and the polynomial DAG constraint characterization of Wei et al., the authors exploit closure properties of F under differentiation, addition, and multiplication to systematically generate a hierarchy of higher-order DAG constraints. The nonlinear extension plugs these constraints into the GraN-DAG framework, and the non-convexity analysis via Hessian spectral radius provides a principled trade-off characterization between gradient vanishing and optimization difficulty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The analytic function framework provides a principled, extensible recipe for designing new DAG constraints: any function in F can be differentiated, summed, or multiplied to yield valid constraints, and the order of differentiation directly controls the severity of gradient vanishing. However, the empirical gains over DAGMA are modest (typically 5-15% SHD reduction on large graphs), and the higher-order constraints that mitigate gradient vanishing simultaneously increase non-convexity, creating a fundamental tension that the paper identifies but does not fully resolve. Open questions include whether the framework extends to latent variable settings, whether the non-convexity trade-off can be broken with adaptive order selection, and whether the approach scales beyond 2000 nodes where even the best methods show significant degradation.
