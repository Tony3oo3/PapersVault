## Problem

During training of diffusion and consistency models, intermediate weight checkpoints are saved but never leveraged—only the final converged checkpoint is deployed. The paper identifies that the metric landscape contains numerous high-quality basins near any point along the optimization trajectory that SGD and its variants cannot reliably reach, and that the standard Exponential Moving Average (EMA) merging strategy, while helpful, is provably suboptimal because its coefficients are fixed heuristically rather than adapted to the actual landscape.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of K saved checkpoints (full model weights or LoRA low-rank matrix pairs) collected at regular intervals within a sliding window (e.g., 40K iterations at 100-step intervals for CM on CIFAR-10, 50K at 200-step intervals for DM). It outputs a single merged weight vector formed as a linear combination of the input checkpoints, which is then used as a drop-in replacement for the final model during generation. The search itself requires only forward-pass inference (no backpropagation), evaluating each candidate combination by sampling 5K–10K images and computing FID, making it GPU-memory-light and applicable even when gradients are unavailable or non-differentiable metrics are desired.

## Method

LCSC formulates checkpoint merging as a constrained optimization: find coefficients α₁,…,α_K (with Σαᵢ=1, negatives allowed) that minimize a generation-quality metric F over the linear combination of K checkpoints. Because the optimal coefficients are intractable to predefine (the paper proves via Theorem 3.3 that no single EMA rate can replicate a 3-way EMA-weight combination), LCSC employs a gradient-free evolutionary search: the population is initialized with EMA coefficient vectors at several different rates, then evolved via tournament selection, uniform crossover, and Gaussian mutation over ~2K iterations. Theoretically, the paper shows EMA reduces the last-iterate O(log N / N) convergence bound to O(1/N) but leaves a residual (1−γ), and that the optimal γ depends on N and the unknown landscape—motivating the flexible search. Two practical use cases are demonstrated: (a) applying LCSC at an early training stage or with reduced batch size to match full-training quality with up to 23× fewer iterations, and (b) applying LCSC to the final-stage checkpoints (or a short fine-tuning of a released model) to improve FID at the same NFE or reduce NFE (e.g., 15→9 for DM) while preserving quality.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with Consistency Distillation (CD), LCSC applied at only 50K training iterations achieves FID 3.10, surpassing the fully trained 800K model (FID 3.66) and yielding a ~14× training speedup. With a reduced batch size of 128, LCSC reaches FID 3.21 versus 3.66 for the standard 512-batch model, achieving a ~23× overall speedup. On ImageNet-64 CD, LCSC at 300K iterations with batch size 256 achieves FID 5.51, outperforming the official 600K/2048-batch model (FID 6.31) for a ~15× speedup. For diffusion models, LCSC reduces the required NFE from 15 to 9 on CIFAR-10 (FID 3.97 vs. 3.96 for EMA* at NFE=15) and from 15 to 12 on ImageNet-64 (FID 17.2 vs. 18.1), enabling 1.7× inference speedup while maintaining or improving generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Consistency Models baseline (Song et al., 2023), LCSC consistently outperforms the released EMA checkpoints: on CIFAR-10 CD, LCSC at 50K iterations (FID 3.10) beats the 800K EMA model (FID 3.66), and on ImageNet-64 CD, LCSC at 300K/256-batch (FID 5.51) beats the official 600K/2048-batch model (FID 6.31). Against the stronger EMA* baseline (grid-searched EMA rate), LCSC still wins on CIFAR-10 CD (3.10 vs. 3.51) and ImageNet-64 CD (5.51 vs. 7.17), confirming that EMA is suboptimal. For diffusion models, LCSC at NFE=9 on CIFAR-10 (FID 3.97) matches the DDIM-based EMA* at NFE=15 (FID 3.96), and on ImageNet-64, LCSC at NFE=12 (FID 17.2) beats the iDDPM-based EMA* at NFE=15 (FID 18.1). Notably, LCSC with 1-step sampling on CIFAR-10 CD (FID 2.44) outperforms the 2-step EMA model (FID 2.93), effectively doubling inference speed while improving quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weight-averaging literature (Ruppert, Polyak & Juditsky, Izmailov et al., Wortsman et al.) from convex optimization and multi-task LLM merging to the specific setting of single-training-trajectory checkpoints in diffusion and consistency models. It builds directly on EMA—the de facto standard in DM/CM training—by proving EMA is a suboptimal special case of linear combination and replacing its heuristic rate with coefficients found via evolutionary search (Real et al., 2019). Unlike prior search-based DM methods (OMS-DPM, AutoDiffusion) that optimize schedules or solvers without touching weights, LCSC operates in the weight space itself, and unlike model-soups or task-arithmetic approaches for LLMs, it targets a single training run rather than merging independently fine-tuned models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 14× training speedup at matched batch size (50K vs 800K iterations on CIFAR-10 CD) and 15× on ImageNet-64 mean that expensive generative model training becomes dramatically cheaper, and the technique is trivially applicable to any existing training pipeline that already saves checkpoints. The theoretical proof that EMA is suboptimal (Thm. 3.3) reframes a long-standing empirical practice as a heuristic that can be systematically improved. Open questions include whether the evolutionary search scales to very large checkpoint sets (e.g., 100K+ iterations for Stable Diffusion-scale models), whether the strong-convexity assumption in the convergence analysis holds for deep networks, and whether the method generalizes beyond image generation to video, 3D, or audio diffusion models.
