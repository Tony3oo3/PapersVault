## Problem

Classic rectified flow trains a velocity model with an MSE objective that can only capture the mean of the multimodal velocity distribution at each space-time location, forcing integration paths to remain non-intersecting and unnecessarily curved. This paper asks whether modeling the full multimodal velocity distribution (rather than just its mean) can produce straighter trajectories that require fewer neural function evaluations during sampling.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a sample from a known source distribution (standard Gaussian for location, standard Gaussian for velocity) and produces a data sample by solving two hierarchically coupled ODEs: first integrating in velocity space to draw a velocity sample, then using that velocity to step in location space. For image generation, inputs are 28×28 grayscale (MNIST) or 32×32 RGB (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-32) noise samples, and outputs are same-resolution generated images. The network additionally consumes the current velocity v_τ and velocity-time τ as inputs alongside location x_t and time t, distinguishing it from standard flow-matching architectures.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical rectified flow formulation that replaces the single location-space ODE of classic rectified flow with hierarchically coupled ODEs operating in different domains (location, velocity, acceleration, etc.). At depth 2 (HRF2), a deep network learns an acceleration field a_θ(x_t, t, v_τ, τ) via flow matching on the ground-truth acceleration x₁ − x₀ − v₀, where v₀ is sampled from a known source velocity distribution. During sampling, the velocity ODE is integrated first (using L Euler steps) to obtain a velocity sample from the modeled multimodal velocity distribution, which then drives a single Euler step in location space; this nested integration yields nearly straight, intersecting trajectories that require only 2–5 location steps versus many more for classic RF at the same NFE budget. The formulation generalizes to arbitrary depth D, where depth 1 recovers classic rectified flow and deeper levels model jerk, snap, etc., with the training objective unified as a single MSE over a D-dimensional functional field of directions. For image experiments, the U-Net architecture is extended with dual pathways (spatial convolutional + temporal linear) to process both (x_t, t) and (v_τ, τ) inputs within each ResNet block.

## Results

On MNIST, HRF2 achieves a FID of 2.574 at 500 NFEs, nearly halving the RF baseline's 5.453. On CIFAR-10, HRF2 reaches 3.706 FID vs. 3.887 for RF at 500 NFEs, and on ImageNet-32, HRF2 achieves 6.503 vs. 6.962. The advantage is most pronounced at low NFE budgets: at just 5 NFEs, HRF2 scores 15.798 FID on MNIST versus 19.187 for RF, and 48.933 vs. 69.233 on ImageNet-32. For density estimation, HRF2 also consistently outperforms RF in bits-per-dimension (e.g., 2.975 vs. 2.980 BPD on CIFAR-10, 3.397 vs. 3.416 on ImageNet-32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classic rectified flow (RF) baseline of Liu et al. (2023), HRF2 consistently achieves lower FID across all three image datasets at every NFE budget, with the largest relative gains at low NFEs (e.g., 42% FID reduction on ImageNet-32 at 5 NFEs: 48.9 vs. 69.2). On MNIST the gap is most dramatic, with HRF2 reaching FID 2.574 at 500 NFEs versus 5.453 for RF — nearly a 2× improvement. On CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-32 the gains are more modest but consistent (FID 3.706 vs. 3.887 and 6.503 vs. 6.962 at 500 NFEs). In the density estimation comparison (Table 1), HRF2 also outperforms RF in bits-per-dimension on all four datasets (e.g., 0.261 vs. 0.275 BPD on 1D 1N→2N). Against OTCFM (Tong et al., 2024) in Appendix H.2, the hierarchical variant HOTCFM2 produces straighter trajectories and lower Wasserstein distances on both 1D and 2D synthetic benchmarks, confirming that the hierarchical formulation is orthogonal to and compatible with optimal-transport coupling strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classic rectified flow (Liu et al., 2023) and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) by replacing the single ODE in location space with a hierarchy of coupled ODEs, where each level models the distribution of the previous level's velocity field rather than just its mean. The formulation is grounded in the theoretical observation (Theorem 1) that the velocity distribution induced by linear interpolation is multimodal and at least as complex as the data distribution itself, which the MSE objective of classic rectified flow cannot capture. The hierarchical construction is shown to be a special case of a general depth-D objective (Eq. 11), and the sampling process is framed as a random differential equation (RDE) rather than a stochastic differential equation, distinguishing it from diffusion models. The approach is orthogonal to path-straightening techniques like OTCFM (Tong et al., 2024) and can be combined with them, as demonstrated in the appendix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that capturing the full velocity distribution enables intersecting, straighter integration paths, reducing the number of neural function evaluations needed for generation — a meaningful efficiency gain for flow-based generative models. However, the empirical improvements are modest: slightly better FID on MNIST and CIFAR-10, on-par on ImageNet-32, and the authors themselves note that only the Euler method and basic grid-searched integration schedules were explored. Open questions include whether better ODE solvers, adaptive integration schedules across hierarchy levels, and deeper hierarchies (beyond depth 3) can yield more substantial gains, and whether the framework scales favorably to higher-resolution image generation or other modalities.
