## Problem

State-of-the-art programmatic reinforcement learning (PRL) methods, such as Hill Climbing, require tens of millions of program-environment interactions to find optimal policies because they perform assumption-free, random-guessing search over the program space. This sample inefficiency makes PRL impractical for real-world deployment. The paper asks whether the programming expertise and common-sense reasoning of large language models can bootstrap the search to dramatically reduce the number of required interactions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language task description (task name, map layout, initial position, goal, and reward structure) combined with a fixed Karel domain prompt and a Pythonic-DSL specification as input to GPT-4. The LLM outputs a Python program constrained to a restricted subset (no variables, no extra functions, no boolean operators), which is then rule-based converted into a Karel DSL program represented as an AST with hard limits: at most 6 root-level statements, 44 total tokens, and recursion depth 4. The final output is a set of executable DSL program policies whose quality is measured by average episodic return over 32 task variants.

## Method

The LLM-GS framework has three components. First, domain and task-aware prompting conveys Karel environment mechanics and the specific task in natural language, activating the LLM's programming and planning abilities without leaking the solution. Second, the Pythonic-DSL strategy instructs the LLM to write a constrained Python program (using pre-defined perception and action functions) and then convert it to Karel DSL, which yields a higher rate of grammatically correct programs than direct DSL generation. Third, Scheduled Hill Climbing (Scheduled HC) initializes its search population with the LLM-generated programs and dynamically grows the neighborhood size k from K_start=32 to K_end=2048 on a logarithmic scale governed by a sinusoidal schedule over the total evaluation budget N, allowing efficient local exploitation early and broader exploration later. The full pipeline evaluates LLM-generated programs, sorts them by return, and runs Scheduled HC from the best candidate until an optimal program (return 1.0) is found or the interaction budget is exhausted.

## Results

The primary metric is sample efficiency, measured as the number of program-environment interactions (programs evaluated) needed to reach an optimal episodic return of 1.0. LLM-GS completely solves all six Karel tasks and achieves the best sample efficiency on nearly all ten tasks. On the hardest task DOORKEY, baselines (LEAPS, HPRL, CEBS, HC) fail to converge even at 1M program evaluations, while LLM-GS converges within approximately 500K. On the two novel tasks (PATHFOLLOW, WALLAVOIDER), LLM-GS also surpasses the best baseline HC using only natural language task descriptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-GS outperforms all four baselines (LEAPS, HPRL, CEBS, and HC) by a large margin on almost all ten Karel/Karel-Hard tasks in terms of sample efficiency (fewer program evaluations to reach optimal return). The most dramatic gap is on DOORKEY, where HC (the prior state-of-the-art) and all other baselines get stuck at ~0.5 episodic return and never converge within 1M evaluations, whereas LLM-GS starts near 0.5 and converges to 1.0 within ~500K evaluations. On the Karel set (STAIRCLIMBER, MAZE, FOURCORNERS, TOPOFF, HARVESTER, CLEANHOUSE), LLM-GS solves all tasks with substantially fewer interactions than any baseline. On Karel-Hard tasks (ONESTROKE, SEEDER, SNAKE), LLM-GS also achieves superior efficiency, though SNAKE remains challenging for all methods (best return ~0.09). The advantage is attributed to LLM-generated initial programs providing a much better starting point for the Scheduled Hill Climbing search compared to the random initialization used by all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the programmatic reinforcement learning (PRL) line of work, which represents RL policies as programs in domain-specific languages (Andre & Russell 2001; Trivedi et al. 2021; Liu et al. 2023; Carvalho et al. 2024). It builds directly on Carvalho et al.'s (2024) Hill Climbing (HC) as the state-of-the-art search-based PRL method, but departs from the purely assumption-free, random-guessing paradigm by using LLMs to generate informed initial programs. The Pythonic-DSL strategy is a novel bridging technique that leverages LLMs' proficiency in general-purpose languages to overcome the DSL language barrier, while the Scheduled Hill Climbing algorithm introduces a dynamic neighborhood schedule that adapts exploration breadth to the remaining interaction budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRL has been impractical for real-world deployment due to its requirement of tens of millions of program-environment interactions; this paper demonstrates that LLM-guided initialization can reduce this by an order of magnitude on the hardest tasks (e.g., DOORKEY goes from non-convergence at 1M to convergence at ~500K). The framework's extensibility—requiring only natural language task descriptions for novel tasks—lowers the barrier for non-programmers to obtain interpretable policies. However, the evaluation is confined to grid-world domains (Karel, Minigrid), relies on a proprietary LLM (GPT-4), and the LLM-only revision approach saturates quickly, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to continuous control, larger state spaces, or open-source models.
