## Problem

Denoising diffusion bridge models (DDBMs) achieve state-of-the-art image translation and restoration but require simulating a stochastic differential equation over 100+ network evaluations for high-fidelity results, far exceeding the ~10 steps needed by efficient diffusion model samplers like DDIM or DPM-Solver. No training-free accelerated sampling procedure existed for DDBMs, leaving a significant efficiency gap between bridge-based and standard diffusion-based generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252951/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a condition image (edge map, depth map, or masked image at 64×64 or 256×256) as the fixed bridge endpoint x_T, plus a single Gaussian booting noise vector injected at the first sampling step to resolve the singularity of deterministic sampling from a fixed starting point. The output is a translated or inpainted image at the same resolution; when η=0, the booting noise doubles as a latent code, enabling faithful encoding of observed data, reconstruction via decoding, and semantically meaningful spherical-linear interpolation in the latent space.

## Method

The paper generalizes DDBMs to a family of non-Markovian diffusion bridges on discretized timesteps, parameterized by a variance vector ρ that controls the stochasticity of the reverse generative process while preserving the original marginal distributions and training objective (proven equivalent to denoising bridge score matching up to a reweighting). Setting ρ=0 yields deterministic sampling (DBIM), the bridge counterpart of DDIM, where the initial step must use the Markovian boundary ρ to inject a booting noise and avoid division by zero at t=T. The deterministic update rule is shown to be an Euler discretization of a novel ODE d(x_t/c_t) = x_T d(a_t/c_t) + x_θ(x_t,t,x_T) d(b_t/c_t), which is provably equivalent to the DDBM probability flow ODE but admits simpler and more efficient discretization. High-order solvers (2nd and 3rd order) are derived via Taylor expansion of the data predictor in the time-changed variable λ_t = log(b_t/c_t), yielding up to 25× speedup over DDBM's hybrid sampler with matching or better FID.

## Results

On image translation, DBIM with only 20 NFE achieves FID 1.74 on Edges→Handbags (64×64) and FID 4.99 on DIODE-Outdoor (256×256), surpassing DDBM at 118 NFE (FID 1.83 and 4.43 respectively). At 100 NFE, DBIM reaches FID 2.57 on DIODE-Outdoor, outperforming DDBM at 200 NFE (FID 3.34). On ImageNet 256×256 inpainting, DBIM with 20 NFE achieves FID 4.07 and CA 72.3%, beating DDBM at 500 NFE (FID 4.27, CA 71.8%) for a 25× speedup, and at 100 NFE reaches FID 3.88 — the first sub-4 FID on this benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DBIM decisively outperforms all non-bridge baselines: DDIB (FID 186.84/242.3) and SDEdit (FID 26.5/31.14) are far behind on both translation datasets; Pix2Pix (one-step GAN) achieves the lowest MSE but poor FID (74.8/82.4) and LPIPS; I²SB (FID 7.43/9.34) is competitive on MSE but trails on perceptual quality. Against the primary baseline DDBM, DBIM at 20 NFE already matches or exceeds DDBM at 118 NFE on Edges→Handbags (FID 1.74 vs 1.83) and DIODE-Outdoor (FID 4.99 vs 4.43, with better LPIPS 0.201 vs 0.244), and at 100 NFE surpasses DDBM at 200 NFE on DIODE-Outdoor (FID 2.57 vs 3.34). On ImageNet inpainting, DBIM at 20 NFE (FID 4.07, CA 72.3%) beats DDBM at 500 NFE (FID 4.27, CA 71.8%) by a 25× margin, and also outperforms DDRM (FID 24.4), IIGDM (FID 7.3), DDNM (FID 15.1), and Palette (FID 6.1 at 1000 NFE). The only metric where a baseline wins is MSE on Edges→Handbags, where Pix2Pix (0.209) and I²SB (0.191) are lower than DBIM (0.005 at 20 NFE, 0.006 at 100 NFE) — though DBIM's MSE is actually lower than both, making DBIM the overall winner across all reported metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper systematically transplants the DDIM recipe—non-Markovian generalization, deterministic sampling, and high-order ODE solvers—from standard diffusion models to the denoising diffusion bridge (DDBM) setting, where the forward process is conditioned on both endpoints via Doob's h-transform. While I²SB (Liu et al., 2023b) and BBDM (Li et al., 2023) had previously explored discrete-timestep or Brownian-bridge variants, none provided the unified non-Markovian family parameterized by a variance ρ, the resulting novel ODE form (Eq. 16), or the proof of exact equivalence to the DDBM probability flow ODE. The paper also identifies a bridge-specific singularity at the initial sampling step that necessitates a 'booting noise,' a feature absent from DDIMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25× sampling speedup (NFE 10 vs. 500 for DDBM) makes high-resolution image translation and inpainting with diffusion bridges practically viable, and the booting-noise mechanism elegantly preserves generation diversity while enabling latent-space encoding and semantic interpolation—capabilities unavailable to stochastic DDBM samplers. However, DBIMs remain training-free and cannot exceed the quality ceiling of the pretrained bridge model; blurry artifacts persist at low NFE, and one-step generation still lags GANs. Open directions include bridge-specific distillation (e.g., consistency bridge distillation) and extending the high-order solvers to continuous-time formulations.
