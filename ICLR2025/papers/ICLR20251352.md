## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for LLMs apply a uniform bit-width across both the prefill and decoding phases and across all generated tokens, ignoring the distinct error resilience of each phase and the progressive fault tolerance that emerges as decoding progresses. This uniform policy causes severe accuracy degradation at 2–3-bit precisions for mobile-scale models and fails to exploit the fact that the memory-bound decoding phase benefits more from bit-width reduction than the compute-bound prefill phase.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained LLM (e.g., Vicuna-7B, MobileLLaMA-1.4B, Phi-1.5, Zephyr-3B) and a text prompt, producing a generated token sequence. During deployment, the learned scheduler additionally ingests the prefilled KV cache (K ∈ R^{T×Dk}, V ∈ R^{T×Dv}) from the last attention block to emit a per-prompt precision-switching schedule S before decoding begins. The variably quantized weight variants are produced offline via Any-Precision LLM's nested quantization, so multiple precisions coexist without additional storage overhead.

## Method

PMPD comprises two complementary techniques. First, phase-aware precision allocation assigns a higher bit-width to the compute-bound prefill phase and a lower bit-width to the memory-bound decoding phase, determined by a calibration search over all (prefill, decode) precision pairs that meet a quality target. Second, progressive mixed-precision decoding gradually lowers the weight precision as the output sequence grows, formalized as a constrained optimization that minimizes the number of high-precision decoding steps subject to a quality floor and a monotone-precision precedence constraint. Two schedulers drive the precision-switching decisions: a prompt-agnostic static scheduler that solves the optimization offline on a task-specific validation set, and a task-agnostic learned scheduler—a lightweight single-head attention module over the prefilled KV cache followed by a one-hidden-layer MLP—trained on C4 to predict the optimal switch point per prompt with negligible runtime overhead.

## Results

On algorithmic performance, PMPD-Static achieves near-lossless quality relative to the high-precision baseline while reducing average bitwidth by up to 33% (e.g., 24.3 Rouge-L at 2.39 bits vs. 24.2 at 3 bits on CNN/DM with Vicuna-7B; 31.0 BLEU at 2.68 bits vs. 31.6 at 3 bits on IWSLT). On hardware, PMPD delivers 3.8–8.0× throughput speedup over fp16 on an LLM-optimized NPU (FlightLLM) and 1.40–12.20× speedup on GPU linear layers (RTX 4090 / A40), with up to 1.41× and 1.54× gains over uniform quantization on GPU and NPU respectively. The Learned scheduler achieves an average 0.47-bit reduction on MT-Bench with no BERTScore drop and at most 1.4 Rouge-L points loss, while outperforming DNS by 13.9–18.4% Rouge-L on open-ended tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DNS (Dense-and-Sparse Quantization, Kim et al., ICML 2024), compared at matched average bitwidth. PMPD-Static consistently outperforms DNS across all three tasks and all four models: on IWSLT (Vicuna-7B), PMPD-Static achieves 31.0 BLEU vs. DNS's 27.6 at the same 2.68-bit average (a 12.3% relative gain, up to 29% in the paper's broader reporting); on CNN/DM (MobileLLaMA), PMPD-Static reaches 17.6 Rouge-L vs. DNS's 17.4, and on Dialogsum (MobileLLaMA) it achieves 17.0 vs. DNS's 14.7 (a 15.6% gain). The advantage of PMPD over DNS grows with smaller models, indicating particular suitability for edge deployment. Against the uniform high-precision baseline (Baseline-h), PMPD-Static matches or exceeds quality while using 9–33% fewer bits; in some cases (e.g., CNN/DM Vicuna-7B, Dialogsum Phi-1.5) it even slightly surpasses the high-precision baseline. The Learned scheduler trades a small quality margin (≤1.4 Rouge-L on MT-Bench) for task-agnosticism, and still outperforms DNS by 13.9–18.4% Rouge-L on open-ended generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Any-Precision LLM (Park et al., 2024) for its nested quantization mechanism, which enables a single model to produce multiple precision variants without additional storage overhead. It departs from the uniform-quantization paradigm (GPTQ, DNS/SqueezeLLM) by introducing two orthogonal axes of precision variation: phase-aware allocation (higher precision for compute-bound prefill, lower for memory-bound decoding) and progressive precision reduction within the decoding phase itself. While prior mixed-precision inference work (HAQ, HAWQ, Bit-mixer, CascadeCNN) explored per-layer or per-sample precision selection for CNN classification, and LLM-PQ (Zhao et al., 2024) performed phase-aware partitioning across heterogeneous servers, this is the first to apply temporal, progressive precision switching within a single-device autoregressive LLM decoding loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge and mobile LLM deployment: achieving 3.8–8.0× NPU throughput and up to 12.2× GPU linear-layer speedup over fp16 while maintaining near-lossless quality opens the door to running 7B-class models on resource-constrained hardware. The key open question is generalizability: the learned scheduler is trained on C4 and evaluated on summarization/translation tasks, and it remains unclear how well the progressive-precision insight transfers to highly structured outputs (code, math) or very long generation sequences where error accumulation may behave differently. Additionally, the NPU evaluation is simulation-based (FlightLLM analytical model), and GPU results are limited to linear-layer kernels rather than full end-to-end inference, so real-world deployment gains may differ.
