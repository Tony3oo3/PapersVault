## Problem

Dense 3D tracking from monocular video—estimating a 3D trajectory for every pixel over long sequences—remains unsolved. Existing point-tracking methods (CoTracker, TAPIR) are limited to sparse pixels, while 3D extensions (SceneTracker, SpatialTracker) rely on cross-track attention that is computationally prohibitive at full resolution, and dense 2D methods (DOT) lack temporal consistency. No prior feed-forward approach can efficiently track all pixels in 3D space over hundreds of frames.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB video (T×H×W×3) and per-frame monocular depth maps (T×H×W) estimated by an off-the-shelf model (UniDepth or ZoeDepth), producing dense 3D trajectories P ∈ R^{T×H×W×4} where each entry (u_t, v_t, d_t, o_t) encodes the 2D position, depth, and binary visibility of the pixel at (u,v) in frame 1 as it appears in frame t. Tracking is performed at H/4 × W/4 reduced resolution and upsampled to full resolution; the model is trained and tested at 384×512 input resolution, and the depth input is treated as a noisy, scale-ambiguous signal rather than a precise measurement.

## Method

DELTA uses a coarse-to-fine pipeline: a spatio-temporal transformer performs iterative dense tracking at 1/4 resolution, followed by an attention-based upsampler that predicts full-resolution trajectories. The key architectural contribution is a joint global-local spatial attention: global attention computes virtual tracks by cross-attending only to a sparse set of anchor tracks (M≈10²) uniformly sampled across the frame, reducing cost from O(TKN) to O(TKM), while local self-attention within small 6×6 patches captures fine-grained spatial relations. The upsampler uses multi-head cross-attention with Alibi-style L1 distance bias between fine and coarse pixels, with weights estimated once per window for temporal consistency. A critical empirical finding is that representing depth in log-space (predicting log(d_t/d_1) rather than d_t−d_1) substantially improves 3D accuracy by making the network scale-invariant to depth map errors. Training uses a patchwise strategy with random crops and anchor tracks to avoid train-test resolution mismatch, supervised with L1 losses on 2D position and inverse depth plus binary cross-entropy on visibility.

## Results

On the CVO-Extended benchmark (48 frames), DELTA achieves an EPE of 3.63/2.67/5.24 (all/vis/occ) and 71.6 IoU, outperforming the previous dense-tracking SOTA DOT (4.98/3.59/7.17, 71.1 IoU) by ~27% in EPE. On the Kubric3D dense 3D tracking benchmark, DELTA reaches AJ 81.4 and APD₃D 88.6 in 0.5 minutes, surpassing DOT-3D (AJ 72.3, APD₃D 77.5) by over 10 points while running 18× faster than SpatialTracker (9 min). On TAP-Vid3D (in-the-wild), DELTA+UniDepth achieves average AJ 13.1 and APD₃D 20.6, beating TAPTR+UniDepth (11.8/18.9) and SpatialTracker+UniDepth (10.0/16.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DELTA decisively outperforms DOT (the prior dense-tracking SOTA) on CVO-Extended, reducing EPE from 4.98 to 3.63 (all) and 3.59 to 2.67 (visible), while matching its IoU (71.6 vs 71.1). Against CoTracker, DELTA improves CVO-Extended EPE from 5.20 to 3.63 (all) and 3.84 to 2.67 (visible), a ~30% reduction. On the Kubric3D dense 3D tracking benchmark, DELTA (AJ 81.4, APD₃D 88.6) surpasses DOT-3D (72.3/77.5) by 9.1 and 11.1 points respectively, and dominates SpatialTracker (42.7/51.6) and SceneTracker (–/65.5) by wide margins while running 10–18× faster. On TAP-Vid3D in-the-wild evaluation, DELTA+UniDepth (AJ 13.1, APD₃D 20.6) beats TAPTR+UniDepth (11.8/18.9), LocoTrack+UniDepth (11.8/19.0), and SpatialTracker+UniDepth (10.0/16.8), though it trails TAPTR slightly on occlusion accuracy (83.0 vs 85.6). On LSFOdyssey, DELTA without domain-specific fine-tuning (AJ 29.4, APD₃D 39.6) underperforms SceneTracker trained on the same domain (57.7 APD₃D), but after one epoch of fine-tuning DELTA surpasses it (50.1/69.7). On 2D TAP-Vid sparse tracking, DELTA is competitive with SoTA sparse trackers (e.g., TAPTRv2, LocoTrack) but does not consistently lead, confirming its primary strength is dense rather than sparse tracking.

## Relation to Prior Work

DELTA extends CoTracker's iterative spatio-temporal transformer from sparse point tracking to dense per-pixel tracking by replacing CoTracker's virtual-track cross-attention with a joint global-local spatial attention that uses sparse anchor tracks for global context and small-patch self-attention for local detail. It adopts RAFT's coarse-to-fine strategy (track at reduced resolution, then upsample) but substitutes RAFT's convolutional upsampler with an Alibi-modified cross-attention upsampler. The 3D tracking formulation follows SceneTracker and SpatialTracker (depth correlation features, visibility prediction), while the local 4D correlation design is borrowed from LocoTrack. The key conceptual departure is making dense 3D tracking computationally tractable via patchwise training with anchor tracks, eliminating the train-test resolution mismatch that plagued prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DELTA is the first feed-forward method to track every pixel in 3D over hundreds of frames at practical speed (8× faster than prior dense 3D trackers), opening the door to applications like dynamic video pose estimation and 4D reconstruction that previously required per-video optimization or were computationally prohibitive. The finding that log-depth representation substantially outperforms Euclidean or inverse depth (a previously overlooked factor) provides a simple, broadly applicable insight for any 3D tracking or scene flow pipeline. Key limitations remain: performance depends on the quality and scale-consistency of off-the-shelf monocular depth, temporal windows are still relatively short (struggling with prolonged occlusions), and the method is best suited for videos under a few hundred frames. Open questions include whether improvements in monocular depth estimation (e.g., DepthCrafter) will close the remaining gap, and whether the architecture can be extended to longer sequences without degrading consistency.
