## Problem

Standard least squares estimation for system identification degrades significantly when only a short single trajectory is available or the model is misspecified, yet no prior work provides non-asymptotic guarantees for regularized LSE under the single-trajectory (non-i.i.d.) setting. Existing data-driven robust alternatives, particularly Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization, suffer from the curse of dimensionality with error rates that slow as state-space dimension grows. This paper fills both gaps by introducing a robust LSE formulation with provable near-optimal rates that are dimension-free.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single trajectory of length T+1 consisting of observed states x_t ∈ R^n and control inputs u_t ∈ R^m, together with a known (possibly nonlinear) feature map φ: R^{n+m} → R^p. It outputs the robust parameter estimate θ̂_T ∈ R^{n×p} and a high-probability identification error bound of order Õ(1/√T). The regularization parameter ε is not set from the theoretical upper bound (which is overly conservative) but calibrated via 3-fold cross-validation over a grid of values scaled as (a·10^b)/√T, making the procedure data-dependent.

## Method

The core formulation is a min-max problem: minimize tr(G(θ)Ω) over θ subject to Ω lying in a Schatten p-norm ball of radius ε around the empirical second-moment matrix Ω̂_T. The key technical contribution is showing this min-max problem is equivalent to a convex semidefinite program (Theorem 1) via dualization and a linearization of the quadratic term θ^Tθ, and further equivalent to standard LSE augmented with a Schatten q-norm regularization term (Corollary 1), where 1/p + 1/q = 1. For the special case q=1 (nuclear norm), the regularization reduces to a squared Frobenius norm on θ, yielding a tractable quadratic program. The non-asymptotic analysis (Theorem 2) establishes persistent excitation via the block martingale small ball condition, bounds the gradient of the regularized objective at the true parameter using the coverage guarantee from Proposition 1, and leverages strong convexity to derive the Õ(1/√T) error rate—avoiding the O(1/T^{2/n}) curse of dimensionality inherent to Wasserstein-based robust optimization.

## Results

On the wind speed prediction task (T=30 training samples, 50-day horizon, 20 datasets), the robust LSE achieves consistent RMSE reductions over the standard LSE (HHT-NAR), with the mean percentage improvement growing as T decreases. On 500 synthetic linear systems (n=5, ρ(A*)=1.0), the robust LSE reduces mean operator-norm identification error relative to standard LSE, with larger gains at smaller T. In online LQ control (Boeing 747, Laplacian, UAV; 500 runs, T=1000), R-OFULQ and R-STABL achieve lower cumulative regret than their unregularized counterparts and ARBMLE. The SDP formulation solves in ~0.05–0.06 s regardless of T (Table 1), confirming sample-size-invariant computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the HHT-NAR LSE baseline of Chen et al. (2024) on wind speed prediction, the robust LSE consistently achieves lower RMSE across all 20 test datasets and all training sizes, with the percentage improvement most pronounced at small T (e.g., T=30). In online LQ control, R-OFULQ (robust version of OFULQ from Abbasi-Yadkori & Szepesvári, 2011) and R-STABL (robust version of STABL from Lale et al., 2022) both achieve lower mean regret than their unregularized counterparts and than ARBMLE (Mete et al., 2022) across the Boeing 747, Laplacian, and UAV benchmarks. The paper also contrasts its approach with the Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization model of Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn (2018), arguing that the Wasserstein model's error rate O(1/T^{2/n}) suffers from the curse of dimensionality, whereas the proposed robust LSE achieves the dimension-free rate Õ(1/√T) even under the more stringent single-trajectory (non-i.i.d.) setting. Across all five comparisons, the proposed method wins on the primary metric (RMSE or regret) without incurring additional asymptotic cost in T.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the non-asymptotic analysis of standard LSE for linear system identification under single trajectories (Simchowitz et al., 2018; Jedra & Proutiere, 2020) and the robust optimization framework of Ben-Tal et al. (2009), but departs from both by formulating a min-max LSE over Schatten p-norm uncertainty sets and proving its equivalence to Schatten q-norm regularization. It extends prior special-case regularizations (squared Frobenius norm in Abbasi-Yadkori & Szepesvári, 2011; Hankel nuclear norm in Sun et al., 2022) to general Schatten norms under the more challenging non-i.i.d. single-trajectory setting. The paper also positions itself against Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization (Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn, 2018), showing its approach avoids the curse of dimensionality that plagues Wasserstein DRO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The equivalence between robust LSE and Schatten-norm regularization provides the first probabilistic justification for regularization in system identification, replacing heuristic parameter choices with a data-dependent scaling of O(1/√T). The dimension-free guarantee is a meaningful departure from Wasserstein DRO, whose error rate degrades as O(1/T^{2/n}) with state-space dimension. Key limitations include the restriction to fully observable systems and the poor scalability of the SDP formulation to high-dimensional problems (mitigated only by restricting to q=1, i.e., quadratic programming). Open questions include extending the framework to partially observable systems and understanding whether the dimension-free advantage persists under more general nonlinear feature maps.
