## Problem

Modern LLMs using SwiGLU activations no longer exhibit the natural ~95% activation sparsity found in ReLU-based models, rendering prior activation-sparsity methods like DejaVu inapplicable. Existing work that reintroduces sparsity (e.g., ReLU replacement + continued pre-training) requires training on hundreds of billions of tokens, making it impractical. The paper asks whether meaningful, model-wide activation sparsity can be achieved in modern LLMs with no training at all.

## Input / Output

At inference time, TEAL consumes a standard input token sequence and the pre-trained model weights, with per-layer magnitude thresholds t_p estimated offline from generic text (C4). The sparsity is input-dependent: each forward pass zeros a different subset of activation entries based on their magnitudes relative to the fixed threshold, so no additional parameters are stored or learned. The output is the same generated text as the unsparsified model, but with 40–50% of weight channels skipped during the memory-bound decoding phase, yielding up to 1.8× wall-clock speedup in single-batch settings.

## Method

TEAL applies magnitude-based input sparsity to every matrix in the model (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o, W_gate, W_up, W_down), motivated by the observation that LLaMA-architecture activations are zero-mean unimodal (Gaussian-like before Attention/MLP, Laplacian-like in intermediate states), so low-magnitude entries contribute little to the output. Per-layer sparsity levels are determined by a block-wise greedy algorithm that incrementally raises the sparsity of the layer causing the least L2 activation error, subject to a uniform block-level sparsity budget. A specialized Triton-based sparse GEMV kernel stores weights in column-major format, selectively loads only non-zero-activation columns, and uses SplitK decomposition with FP16 outer accumulation to realize the speedup. The approach is compatible with weight quantization (8-bit RTN, 4-bit AWQ, 2/3-bit QuIP#), and the paper provides a theoretical error bound under the assumption of independent Gaussian weights and activations.

## Results

TEAL achieves 40–50% model-wide activation sparsity with minimal degradation: on Llama-3-8B, perplexity rises only from 5.87 (baseline) to 6.21 at 40% and 6.67 at 50% sparsity, while the six-task downstream average drops from 68.07 to 66.21 and 63.42 respectively. On A6000, TEAL delivers wall-clock decoding speed-ups of 1.53× at 40% and 1.80× at 50% sparsity (Llama-2-7B), and 1.42×/1.63× for Llama-3-8B. At 25% sparsity, degradation is near-zero across all models (e.g., Llama-2-70B: 3.12→3.13 PPL). TEAL is also compatible with weight quantization (8-bit RTN, 4-bit AWQ, 2/3-bit QuIP#), with errors compounding somewhat independently.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CATS (the primary training-free baseline), TEAL wins decisively at both 25% and 40% sparsity. At 25%, TEAL achieves 5.94 PPL on Llama-3-8B versus CATS's 6.78, and at 40% CATS is completely degenerate (7.6×10⁴ PPL) while TEAL maintains 6.21. The advantage stems from two design choices: TEAL sparsifies all seven matrices per block (not just W_up and W_down), avoiding extreme per-layer sparsity, and it uses input sparsity for W_up rather than CATS's output sparsity, which yields lower activation error. Against MaskLLM (2:4 semi-structured weight sparsity, Appendix A.4), TEAL at 50% achieves 6.673 PPL on Llama-3-8B versus MaskLLM's 8.532, while being entirely training-free (MaskLLM required ~800 H100-hours on 2B tokens). TEAL also delivers a higher single-batch speed-up (1.78× vs. 1.4× on Llama-2-7B), though MaskLLM may be more competitive in high-batch and prefill settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEAL extends the activation sparsity line initiated by DejaVu (Liu et al., 2023), which exploited natural ReLU-induced sparsity in older LLMs, to modern SwiGLU-based architectures where such sparsity is absent. It departs from ReLUfication approaches (Mirzadeh et al., 2023; Song et al., 2024a,b) that require hundreds of billions of tokens of continued pretraining, and from CATS (Lee et al., 2024a), which applies training-free sparsity only to MLP intermediate states via output sparsity. TEAL's key conceptual shift is applying uniform magnitude-based input sparsity to every matrix in the model (attention and MLP alike), motivated by the observation that LLM activations are zero-mean unimodal regardless of architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TEAL provides a drop-in, training-free recipe for 40–50% model-wide sparsity with 1.5–1.8× single-batch decoding speedups, and its demonstrated compatibility with weight quantization (AWQ, QuIP#) opens a combined sparsity+quantization efficiency axis that requires new sparse-quantized kernels. The main limitation is that sparsity degrades with batch size, restricting the method to latency-sensitive, low-batch edge deployments. Open questions include sparsifying the LM Head, developing batched sparsification strategies, and whether continued pretraining can push sparsity beyond the ~65% ceiling observed here.
