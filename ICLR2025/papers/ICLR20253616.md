## Problem

Existing zero-shot constrained generation methods for PDE systems rely on gradient-based soft constraints that are computationally expensive, do not guarantee exact constraint satisfaction, and suffer from gradient imbalances and ill-conditioning in large spatiotemporal domains. Moreover, practical PDE constraints (boundary conditions, initial conditions, conservation laws) occupy measure-zero subsets of the domain, providing far less context than typical computer vision inverse problems. The paper addresses how to guide a pre-trained unconstrained generative model to produce solutions that strictly satisfy hard physical constraints in a zero-shot, gradient-free manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained unconditional Functional Flow Matching model (parameterized by a Fourier Neural Operator), a constraint specification (value constraints like IC/BC or region constraints like conservation laws), and an initial noise function sampled from a Gaussian process or white noise prior. It outputs samples from the conditional distribution over the constrained solution set, with the constraint satisfied exactly at the final timestep. Because the FFM is discretization-invariant, the same pre-trained model can generate at higher resolutions (zero-shot superresolution, e.g., 100×100 → 200×200) without retraining, and the framework handles both 2D (1D PDE + time) and 3D (2D PDE + time) spatiotemporal fields.

## Method

ECI sampling interleaves three stages at each Euler step of the flow ODE solver: (1) Extrapolation — the learned vector field predicts the final solution in one step as u₁ = u_t + (1−t)v_θ(u_t); (2) Correction — an oblique projection (Algorithm 4 for value constraints, Algorithm 5 for region/conservation constraints) enforces the constraint exactly on the extrapolated solution; (3) Interpolation — the corrected solution is linearly interpolated back to the current or next timestep as u_{t'} = (1−t')u₀ + t'û₁, propagating constraint information into the noised intermediate state. This procedure is derived from a variational decomposition of the constraint-conditional probability p(û_t | u_t, G) = E_{u₁}[q(û_t | C(u₁, G))], which simplifies under the deterministic OT-path of FFM. Multiple mixing iterations M can be applied recursively at each timestep to promote information mixing between constrained and unconstrained regions, and a re-sampling interval R controls generation stochasticity by periodically resampling the prior noise u₀. The framework is unified across constraint types, requires no fine-tuning or gradient backpropagation, and guarantees exact constraint satisfaction at t=1 by construction (Proposition 1).

## Results

ECI sampling achieves zero constraint error (CE = 0) across all five generative PDE tasks, matching ProbConserv while outperforming it on MMSE, SMSE, and FPD. On the Stokes problem with IC fixed, ECI attains MMSE 0.090×10⁻² and FPD 0.076, compared to 0.601×10⁻² and 0.202 for DiffusionPDE and 1.015×10⁻² and 0.614 for D-Flow. On the 3D Navier-Stokes equation, ECI achieves MMSE 7.961×10⁻² and FPD 1.131, outperforming D-Flow (2.846×10⁻² MMSE but 0.776×10⁻² CE) and DiffusionPDE (19.141×10⁻² MMSE, 2.295×10⁻² CE). ECI-1 also delivers 440× wall-clock speedup and 310× GPU memory reduction over gradient-based D-Flow on the Stokes problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECI sampling dominates ANP across all metrics and all five PDE systems, with MMSE improvements of 1–7× (e.g., 0.090 vs. 13.750 on Stokes IC) and FPD reductions of 2–50×. Against ProbConserv, ECI ties on constraint error (both achieve CE = 0) but substantially outperforms it on distributional fidelity: MMSE is 22× lower on Darcy Flow (2.314 vs. 56.608) and FPD is 14× lower on Stokes IC (0.076 vs. 13.075), because ProbConserv's single non-iterative correction leaves visible artifacts at constraint boundaries. Versus the gradient-based DiffusionPDE, ECI wins on MMSE, SMSE, CE, and FPD for every dataset (e.g., NS equation: 7.961 vs. 19.141 MMSE; 0 vs. 2.295 CE). Against D-Flow, ECI achieves lower MMSE and zero CE on all tasks, though D-Flow occasionally matches or slightly beats ECI on SMSE for the NS equation (6.840 vs. 5.846) and Darcy Flow (1.728 vs. 2.314 MMSE); however, D-Flow requires 28.8 s/sample and 26.4 GB GPU memory versus ECI-1's 0.065 s/sample and 5.4 GB on Stokes IC. The non-zero-shot CondFFM upper bound consistently outperforms ECI (e.g., 0.121 vs. 0.090 MMSE on Stokes IC), confirming that ECI captures most of the benefit of conditional training without any fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Functional Flow Matching (Kerrigan et al., 2023) as the generative prior and the variational decomposition strategy from DDPM/Repaint (Kawar et al., 2022; Lugmayr et al., 2022) for constrained generation, but departs from both the gradient-based guidance paradigm (DiffusionPDE, D-Flow) and the non-iterative correction approach (ProbConserv, Hansen et al., 2023). The key conceptual contribution is the ECI step—interleaving extrapolation, correction, and interpolation at every flow-matching sampling step—which unifies exact constraint satisfaction (via oblique projection from Hansen et al., 2023) with fine-grained iterative control in a gradient-free manner. This positions ECI sampling as the first method to simultaneously achieve zero-shot, gradient-free, exact-constraint, and iterative-control properties for flow-based generative models on PDE systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine gap in scientific machine learning: existing zero-shot constrained generation methods either fail to guarantee exact constraint satisfaction (gradient-based approaches) or lack iterative control (ProbConserv), both of which are critical for physically consistent PDE simulation. The 440× speedup and 310× memory reduction over D-Flow make hard-constrained generation practical for large 3D spatiotemporal systems where gradient backpropagation through ODE solvers is infeasible. Key limitations include the task-specific sensitivity of mixing iterations M and re-sampling interval R (lacking theoretical guidance), the restriction to linear constraints (value and region), and the absence of formal convergence guarantees for the iterative ECI procedure. Future work on non-linear constraints, theoretical hyperparameter selection, and extension to domains beyond PDEs (supply chain, time series) would strengthen the framework's generality.
