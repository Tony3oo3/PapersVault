## Problem

High-resolution dichotomous image segmentation (DIS) requires simultaneously capturing broad contextual awareness and fine-grained boundary details, a trade-off that existing CNN and Transformer methods handle suboptimally. While diffusion models offer rich global priors and fine detail generation, three fundamental conflicts arise when repurposing them for binary segmentation: the continuous latent space clashes with discrete mask outputs, multi-step inference is prohibitively slow for HR images, and the stochastic generative process conflicts with the deterministic nature of perception tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 1024×1024, encodes it into the VAE latent space (reducing spatial resolution to 1/8), and produces a binary segmentation mask plus an auxiliary edge map decoded back to pixel space via the VAE decoder with channel-averaging post-processing. All denoising operates entirely in the 4-channel latent space, and the mask and edge are jointly predicted in a single forward pass by concatenating them along the batch dimension with discriminative embeddings to distinguish the two streams.

## Method

DiffDIS repurposes the pre-trained U-Net from Stable Diffusion V2.1 (initialized with SD-Turbo's distilled weights for a smoother probability landscape) as a one-step denoiser that maps pure Gaussian noise at t=999 directly to a clean mask/edge latent in a single forward pass, eliminating the multi-step recurrence. An auxiliary edge prediction stream is jointly trained with the mask via Batch-Discriminative Embedding, and a Detail-Balancing Interactive Attention module in the U-Net mid-block performs Mutual Cross-Domain Attention (using mask queries against edge keys/values and vice versa) to align boundary details and reduce stochastic variation. Scale-Wise Conditional Injection injects the RGB latent at three encoder scales through zero-convolution injector heads (adapted from ControlNet), providing multi-granular structural guidance without over-constraining texture. Training minimizes the L2 distance between the one-step denoised latent and the VAE-encoded ground-truth mask/edge latents, with CutMix augmentation to mitigate overfitting on the 3,000-image DIS5K training set.

## Results

On the DIS5K benchmark, DiffDIS achieves state-of-the-art performance across nearly all metrics. On the overall DIS-TE(1-4) test set, it reaches F_β^max = 0.911, F_β^ω = 0.892, E_φ^μ = 0.950, S_m = 0.905, and MAE = 0.028, outperforming the strongest baselines (MVANet, BiRefNet) by 0.3–2.7 points on most metrics. On the DIS-VD validation set, it scores F_β^max = 0.908, F_β^ω = 0.888, E_φ^μ = 0.948, S_m = 0.904, and MAE = 0.029. All results are obtained with a single-step denoising inference (0.33 s per image), orders of magnitude faster than multi-step diffusion approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffDIS outperforms all 11 baselines on F_β^max, F_β^ω, E_φ^μ, and MAE across both DIS-VD and the overall DIS-TE(1-4) test set. The closest competitors are MVANet (F_β^max 0.908, MAE 0.035) and BiRefNet (F_β^max 0.900, MAE 0.034); DiffDIS surpasses them by 0.3 and 1.1 points on F_β^max respectively, and by 0.7 and 0.6 points on MAE. On S_m, BiRefNet edges out DiffDIS by 0.001 on the overall test set (0.906 vs. 0.905) and by 0.001 on DIS-VD (0.905 vs. 0.904), while MVANet leads by 0.001–0.002 on DIS-TE2 and DIS-TE3. The diffusion-based GenPercept baseline trails DiffDIS by 7.1 points on F_β^max and 16 points on MAE, confirming that the one-step denoising and edge-assisted strategy substantially improves over generic diffusion repurposing. Among general segmentation models (BSANet, ISDNet, IFA, PGNet), the gap is even larger, with DiffDIS leading by 10–17 points on F_β^max.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Stable Diffusion U-Net and VAE (Rombach et al., 2022) for high-resolution dichotomous image segmentation, building on the broader trend of applying diffusion models to dense prediction (Amit et al., 2021; Ji et al., 2023; Ke et al., 2024). Its key methodological departure is the use of SD-Turbo's adversarially distilled parameters (Sauer et al., 2023) to enable a single-step denoising paradigm derived from the DDPM reverse process (Ho et al., 2020), rather than the multi-step iterative sampling typical of prior diffusion-based segmentation. The Scale-Wise Conditional Injection adapts ControlNet's zero-convolution injectors (Zhang et al., 2023) for multi-scale RGB conditioning, while the Batch-Discriminative Embedding mechanism is borrowed from GeoWizard (Fu et al., 2024) to jointly predict mask and edge within a single network. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against both traditional discriminative DIS methods (IS-Net, BiRefNet, MVANet) and prior diffusion-based approaches, arguing that the generative prior of large-scale diffusion models naturally resolves the receptive-field-vs-detail trade-off that plagues CNN/Transformer segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe of combining SD-Turbo initialization with fixed one-step denoising (t=999) and an auxiliary edge prediction task offers a straightforward path to fast, high-fidelity binary mask generation at 1024×1024 resolution (0.33 s/inference), which could be adopted for other dense prediction tasks requiring deterministic outputs. The ablation finding that multi-step denoising actually degrades performance relative to one-step (e.g., 0.837 vs. 0.853 F_max for SD V2.1) provides a useful conceptual insight: the stochasticity inherent in iterative sampling is fundamentally at odds with the deterministic nature of binary segmentation. However, evaluation is limited to a single benchmark (DIS5K), and the modest SOTA margins over strong baselines like MVANet (0.908 vs. 0.904 F_max on DIS-VD) leave open whether the diffusion prior provides a genuine representational advantage or primarily benefits from the SD-Turbo initialization. Generalization to other segmentation granularities (multi-class, panoptic) or to different resolution regimes remains unexplored.
