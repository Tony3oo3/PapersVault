## Problem

RL agents in safety-critical environments suffer from primacy bias: early constraint violations impose heavy penalties that disproportionately shape the policy, causing agents to overestimate risk across all states and converge to overly conservative, suboptimal policies. Existing safe RL methods (penalties, action filtering, Lagrangian constraints) mitigate violations but at the cost of severely restricting exploration. The paper addresses the gap of how to explicitly learn state-conditioned safety representations that inform exploration without inducing excessive caution.

## Input / Output

The method takes the same state observations as the underlying RL algorithm (joint states for manipulation/locomotion, LiDAR point clouds for driving and Safety Gym, or raw images) and produces both the policy's action and a learned safety representation. The safety representation is a discrete probability distribution over binned steps-to-cost values (distance to the nearest unsafe state), with a fixed safety horizon H_s (e.g., 40 or 80 steps) split into bins of size 4, yielding a low-dimensional vector appended to the state. For high-dimensional observations like images, the safety vector is concatenated with the feature encoder's output rather than the raw pixels.

## Method

SRPL introduces a 'steps-to-cost' (S2C) neural network that maps a state to a softmax distribution over proximity to cost-inducing states, trained via negative log-likelihood on labels derived from the agent's trajectory experience (number of steps until an unsafe state is encountered, or H_s if none). Crucially, the S2C model is trained on off-policy data from a replay buffer spanning multiple past policies rather than only on-policy rollouts, making the safety representation state-centric and generalizable across tasks. The learned safety distribution is then concatenated to the agent's state (or feature encoding) to form an augmented state, which is fed to any standard safe RL algorithm (CPO, TRPO-PID, SauteRL, CRPO, CSC, CVPO). This state augmentation implicitly guides the policy toward safer exploration without explicit action filtering or additional constraint mechanisms, and the S2C model can be frozen and transferred as a prior to new tasks.

## Results

On SafeMetaDrive, SR-CPO achieves a 0.47 success rate versus CPO's 0.088 (5.3× improvement) while reducing total cost from 2715 to 1997. On AdroitHandPen, SR-CRPO reaches a return of 8800±985 with 4626±447 failures, outperforming CRPO (7893±2576 return, 5752±1146 failures). On Safety Gym PointGoal1, SR-CPO doubles the return from 6.25 to 11.63 while reducing cost-rate from 1.61 to 1.49. Across all four main tasks, SRPL-augmented agents consistently achieve higher task performance and fewer constraint violations during training compared to their vanilla counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CPO, SR-CPO wins on all four tasks: it more than doubles the return on PointGoal1 (11.63 vs 6.25), achieves 5.3× higher success rate on SafeMetaDrive (0.47 vs 0.088), and reduces failures on AdroitHandPen from 6667 to 4797. Against CRPO, SR-CRPO achieves the best return on AdroitHandPen (8800 vs 7893) with fewer failures (4626 vs 5752), the highest success rate on SafeMetaDrive (0.53 vs 0.22), and the best return on PointButton1 (3.12 vs 1.47). Against TRPO-PID, SR-TRPO-PID improves return on AdroitHandPen (5963 vs 4809) and PointGoal1 (7.31 vs 2.96) while reducing failures. Against SauteRL, SR-SauteRL shows modest gains (e.g., 4094 vs 3831 return on AdroitHandPen), with SauteRL's state-augmentation via remaining budget being particularly ineffective when the budget stays at 0 until a violation occurs. For off-policy baselines, SR-CVPO and SR-CSC both reach comparable or higher performance than their vanilla versions in just 2M steps, with SR-CVPO matching SR-CPO's performance while CSC's safety critic filtering leads to conservative suboptimal task performance that SRPL partially mitigates.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRPL carves out a distinct niche within safe RL by departing from the dominant paradigm of restricting exploration (action filtering via safety critics like CSC, or hard constraint enforcement via CPO/CRPO) in favor of learning a state-conditioned safety representation that is augmented into the agent's state. Conceptually, it is closest to SauteRL's state-augmentation approach, but differs fundamentally in what is augmented: rather than encoding the remaining constraint budget (a policy-dependent scalar), SRPL learns a distribution over steps-to-cost from diverse past experience, making it policy-agnostic and transferable. The paper also explicitly connects to the primacy bias literature (Nikishin et al., 2022), arguing that early constraint violations create a self-reinforcing conservatism loop that existing penalty-based or Lagrangian methods fail to break, and positions the S2C model as a mechanism to counteract this by providing the agent with an explicit, learned safety signal independent of its current policy's risk estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value of SRPL lies in its plug-and-play nature: it can augment any existing safe RL algorithm (demonstrated with CPO, TRPO-PID, SauteRL, CRPO, CSC, CVPO) without modifying their core optimization, and the learned safety representations transfer across tasks as a useful prior, reducing sample cost for new deployments. However, the paper acknowledges two important limitations: the S2C model cannot capture long-horizon causal safety (a state far from an unsafe region but on an inescapable path to it is misclassified as safe), and the binary safety framing (safe vs. unsafe) does not accommodate graded notions of risk that real-world systems require. Open questions include whether the distributional safety representation can be extended to handle stochastic or partially observable safety, and whether the approach scales to continuous, high-dimensional safety-relevant state spaces beyond the grid-world and low-dimensional state-vector settings tested here.
