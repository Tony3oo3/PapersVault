## Problem

Existing graph dataset condensation methods produce condensed graphs that are tightly coupled to a single dataset and task, failing when the downstream user needs to transfer the condensed graph to a different dataset or a different task (e.g., a new label set or a different task type). This paper asks whether a condensed graph can be made transferable at the data level, so that models trained on it generalize effectively to unseen datasets and tasks without re-condensation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250666/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph dataset G = (X, A, Y) with N nodes, an adjacency matrix, and node labels, along with k pre-trained self-supervised teacher models (e.g., DGI, GraphCL, InfoGraph). It produces a condensed graph G_s with m ≪ N nodes containing both a hard label Y_s^h (for supervised downstream tasks) and a soft label Y_s^s ∈ R^{m×d} (a d-dimensional continuous vector unifying multiple self-supervised teacher outputs). The dual-label structure is unusual: the hard label supports standard classification fine-tuning, while the soft label enables cross-dataset transfer by encoding universal structural knowledge independent of any specific label set.

## Method

ST-GCond combines three mechanisms. First, a task-disentangled meta-optimization strategy (inspired by MAML) splits the supervised task into t sub-tasks; in each epoch, the condensed graph is fast-adapted to each sub-task via an inner KRR-based update, then jointly optimized via an outer meta-loss over all sub-tasks, encouraging the condensed graph to retain task-agnostic structure. Second, a multi-teacher self-supervised optimization loads k pre-trained self-supervised models, weights their outputs with adaptive coefficients {λ_i}, and matches the weighted teacher distribution to a synthetic soft label Y_s^s using a KRR objective, injecting dataset-agnostic knowledge. Third, a mutual information constraint L_MI = I(Y_s^s; Y_s^h) is added to the joint loss to resolve optimization conflicts between the supervised and self-supervised objectives, with a theorem showing I(Y_s^s; Y_s^h) upper-bounds I(P^T(X,A); Y). The KRR-based condensing objective (using SNTK for node-level and LightGNTK for graph-level) replaces gradient/trajectory matching, enabling efficient continuous-label optimization and avoiding triple-level optimization.

## Results

On the single-dataset/single-task node classification benchmark (Table 1), ST-GCond achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on 14 out of 15 condensation ratios across 5 datasets (Cora, Citeseer, Ogbn-arxiv, Flickr, Reddit), with a maximum improvement of 2.71% over the strongest baseline (GEOM). In the cross-dataset transfer scenario, ST-GCond outperforms all baselines by 2.5%–15.5% on node-level and 4.1%–18.79% on graph-level classification. In cross-task settings (link prediction and node clustering), ST-GCond surpasses the VGAE/ARGA baselines on Cora (AUC 93.3%, AP 94.8%) and Citeseer clustering (NMI 40.8%, F1 62.7%, ARI 41.7%), while existing condensation methods fall below these baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-GCond consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every scenario. Against the strongest single-task baselines (GEOM, SFGC, SGDD, GCond), ST-GCond gains 0.5–2.7% accuracy on node classification at matched condensation ratios, and notably achieves lossless results (exceeding full-dataset GCN accuracy) on Cora, Flickr, and Citeseer. In the cross-dataset setting, the gap widens dramatically: ST-GCond beats GEOM by up to 15.5% on node-level transfer (e.g., 87.5% vs. 74.1% on Reddit) and by up to 18.79% on graph-level transfer (e.g., 73.6% vs. 54.8% ROC-AUC on BACE at 0.01% ratio). The graph-level baselines DosCond and KiDD are particularly outperformed, with ST-GCond achieving 68.6–73.6% ROC-AUC on BACE versus 51.7–55.7% for DosCond and 53.5–54.8% for KiDD. Core-set methods (Random, Herding, K-Center) remain far behind all learned condensation methods, confirming that structural information is critical. In cross-task scenarios (link prediction, clustering), ST-GCond is the only method that matches or exceeds the VGAE/ARGA baselines, while GCond, SGDD, SFGC, and GEOM all degrade below them, validating the task-disentangled meta-optimization component.

## Relation to Prior Work

ST-GCond extends the graph dataset condensation line (GCond, SGDD, SFGC, GEOM for node-level; DosCond, KiDD for graph-level) by explicitly targeting cross-task and cross-dataset transferability, a setting all prior methods ignore. It repurposes MAML's inner-outer loop structure for task-disentangled meta-optimization over the condensed graph, and adopts KRR-based objectives (SNTK, LightGNTK) in place of gradient or trajectory matching to support continuous soft labels from self-supervised teachers. The multi-teacher self-supervised component draws on AutoSSL and WAS for task selection and adaptive weighting, while the mutual information constraint resolves conflicts analogous to those addressed in multi-teacher knowledge distillation (Wu et al., 2022; Fan et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first graph condensation framework explicitly designed so that a single condensed graph can serve as a transferable training resource across unseen datasets and tasks, addressing a practical gap where users with limited compute need to adapt to their own data. The mutual information-guided joint condensation is a useful mechanism for balancing supervised and self-supervised signals, and the KRR-based objective provides a computationally lighter alternative to triple-level gradient matching (1.14–2.17× faster than GCond). Key limitations include the need to re-condense when the condensation ratio changes, the reliance on pre-trained teacher models (adding an offline cost), and the fact that cross-dataset gains are measured against baselines not designed for that scenario, making the true competitive landscape less clear.
