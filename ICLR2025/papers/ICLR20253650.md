## Problem

Existing LLM ensembling methods (DEEPEN, GAC) focus solely on the fusion technique without addressing which models are actually compatible for ensembling, and they align probability distributions across the entire vocabulary at every decoding step, incurring substantial computational overhead. This paper identifies that model performance gap, response style, and vocabulary size are the true determinants of ensembling success, and that the candidate next token almost always resides within a small top-k subset, making full-vocabulary alignment both unnecessary and wasteful.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a set of base LLMs (each with its own tokenizer and vocabulary, e.g., LLaMA3 with 128K vocab and Qwen2 with 152K vocab) and produces a single generated text response via iterative token-by-token decoding. At each step, only the top-k tokens (k=10 by default) from each model are extracted and merged into a union set of roughly 14 tokens, compared to 32K–170K tokens manipulated per step by DEEPEN or GAC. The primary base model (the best-performing one) is designated to emit the final token via greedy argmax over the averaged, softmax-normalized probability vector.

## Method

UNITE (Union Top-k Ensembling) operates in two stages. First, a determine-then-ensemble model selection strategy picks the best-performing model for the target task, then iteratively adds the next-best model only if the performance gap is within 10% and response styles are compatible (text length ratio ≤ 2×). Second, at each decoding step, UNITE extracts the top-k tokens and their probabilities from every base model, constructs a union vocabulary V^u, and aligns each model's distribution onto V^u using three rules: tokens already in both the union and the model's top-k are kept unchanged; tokens in the union but present in the model's full vocabulary are appended with their native probability; tokens absent from the model's vocabulary are tokenized by that model's tokenizer and the first sub-token's probability is used. The aligned distributions are softmax-normalized, averaged across models, and the next token is selected by greedy argmax. This eliminates auxiliary mapping matrices and full-vocabulary alignment, reducing per-step token manipulation to under 0.04% of what DEEPEN or GAC require while adding only ~10 ms latency over a single model.

## Results

On the three-model ensemble (Mistral + DeepSeek + OpenChat, with OpenChat as primary), UNITE achieves an average score of 66.54 across GSM8K, PIQA, MMLU, ARC-C, TriviaQA, and NQ, improving +1.16 over the best individual model (OpenChat at 65.38). On the closely-matched LLaMA3 + Qwen2 pair, UNITE reaches 81.14 average (+2.33 over Qwen2's 78.81), with gains of +3.39 on GSM8K and +4.82 on MMLU. UNITE manipulates only ~14 tokens per decoding step versus 32K–170K for DEEPEN and GAC, with a latency of 87.78 ms/token—only ~10 ms above individual model inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNITE outperforms all three baselines on average across both main experimental settings. Against LLM-BLENDER (output-level, pairwise ranking), UNITE improves the average by 4.48 points (66.54 vs. 62.06); LLM-BLENDER actually degrades performance on 5 of 6 tasks, dropping 3.32 points below the best individual model, largely due to its bias toward longer responses. Against DEEPEN (probability-level, relative representation), UNITE gains 3.45 points on average (66.54 vs. 63.11); DEEPEN suffers severe OOM failures when ensembling models with large vocabularies (e.g., LLaMA3 + Qwen2) and drops 11.06 points on PIQA. Against GAC (probability-level, mapping matrix), UNITE leads by 9.57 points on average (66.54 vs. 56.97); GAC is the weakest baseline, losing over 10 points on GSM8K and NQ when combined with Mistral and OpenChat. On the LLaMA3 + Qwen2 pair (Table 4), UNITE achieves 81.14 average vs. LLM-BLENDER's 77.57 and GAC's 78.40, while DEEPEN is entirely infeasible due to OOM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the probability-level LLM ensembling lineage (DEEPEN, GAC, COOL-FUSION) but departs from them on two axes: (1) it introduces a 'determine-then-ensemble' model selection strategy that treats model compatibility (performance gap <10%, response style alignment) as a prerequisite rather than assuming any pair of models can be ensembled, and (2) it replaces full-vocabulary probability alignment with a top-k union set, arguing that the candidate next token almost always resides in the top-k subset. Conceptually, it repurposes the tokenization-aware mapping idea from GAC but applies it only to a small union set, eliminating the need for large auxiliary mapping matrices. The empirical analysis of performance disparity, vocabulary size, and response style as compatibility factors is a new contribution not addressed by prior ensembling methods, which focused exclusively on the fusion mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: UNITE reduces tokens manipulated per step from ~32K–170K to ~14, with only ~10 ms additional latency over a single model, making probability-level ensembling feasible in latency-sensitive deployment. The model compatibility insights (performance gap threshold, response style matching) provide actionable guidance for practitioners selecting which models to ensemble. However, limitations remain: the 10% performance gap threshold is heuristic, response style comparison is acknowledged as 'relatively subjective,' and experiments are limited to models in the 6B–72B range. Open questions include whether the top-k union approach generalizes to very large model pools (10+ models), whether dynamic per-query model selection could further improve results, and how the method behaves when base models have fundamentally different reasoning paradigms (e.g., chain-of-thought vs. direct answer).
