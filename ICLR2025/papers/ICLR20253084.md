## Problem

Cross-domain offline RL aims to improve target-domain policy learning by leveraging a source-domain dataset, but the two domains may have mismatched transition dynamics. Existing approaches (domain classifiers, contrastive learning, mutual-information filtering) require a large target-domain dataset to function reliably, yet in practice only a few thousand target-domain transitions may be available, making single-domain offline RL struggle and prior cross-domain methods degrade to the base method's performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253084/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two offline datasets: a source-domain dataset (D4RL MuJoCo, up to 1M transitions at medium/medium-replay/medium-expert quality) and a small target-domain dataset (~5000 transitions collected in a dynamics-shifted environment). Transitions are consumed as concatenated (s, a, s') vectors for the OT computation. The output is a target-domain policy (built on IQL) together with per-source-transition deviation scores that determine which source transitions are shared and at what weight during value-function training.

## Method

OTDF has two components grounded in a theoretical performance bound (Theorem 3.1) that decomposes the target-vs-source return gap into source-policy deviation, target-policy deviation, and dynamics-mismatch terms. First, it solves an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem (via Sinkhorn) between the source and target transition distributions to obtain an optimal coupling, from which per-source-transition deviation scores d(u_t) are computed; source transitions below the ξ-th quantile are rejected and the rest are weighted by exp(d̂) (min-max normalized) in the Bellman loss, requiring no neural-network training. Second, a CVAE pretrained on the small target dataset models the target behavior policy, and a logsumexp-based regularization term is added to the policy loss to keep the learned policy within the target-domain support region, directly controlling the target-policy-deviation term in the bound. The full procedure is an add-on module compatible with any offline RL algorithm, instantiated here with IQL.

## Results

On morphology-shift tasks (Table 1), OTDF achieves a total normalized score of 1274.3 across 36 tasks, substantially outperforming the second-best baseline DARA (816.8) and the base method IQL* (798.0), representing a 59.7% improvement over IQL*. On gravity-shift tasks (Table 2), OTDF scores 1160.7 total, exceeding DARA (851.0) by 36.4% and IQL* (825.0) by 40.7%. OTDF wins 23/36 morphology tasks and 26/36 gravity tasks, while the best competing method (DARA) wins far fewer. All evaluations use only ~5000 target-domain transitions, a regime where single-domain offline RL methods struggle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTDF substantially outperforms all five baselines in aggregate: on morphology shifts it scores 1274.3 total normalized score vs. DARA (816.8), IGDF (808.7), SRPO (803.1), IQL* (798.0), and BOSA (646.3); on gravity shifts it scores 1160.7 vs. DARA (851.0), SRPO (825.5), IQL* (825.0), IGDF (803.6), and BOSA (763.2). Against the base method IQL*, OTDF yields a 59.7% total-score increase under morphology shifts and 40.7% under gravity shifts, winning 27/36 and 29/36 tasks respectively. DARA (domain-classifier-based reward penalization) is the strongest competitor but still trails OTDF by 55.8 points (morphology) and 309.7 points (gravity) in total score. BOSA (support-constrained regularization) performs worst overall, likely because it constrains the policy to the mixed dataset support rather than the target domain support specifically. SRPO and IGDF perform comparably to IQL* on many tasks, indicating their reward-modification and contrastive-filtering mechanisms fail to provide meaningful adaptation when only ~5000 target-domain transitions are available. OTDF's advantage is most pronounced on walker2d and ant tasks (e.g., walk-m/medium: 50.5 vs. 27.5 for IGDF; ant-m-e/expert: 86.4 vs. 53.9 for BOSA), where dynamics shifts create larger distributional gaps that OT's alignment handles more effectively than learned classifiers or contrastive objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on IQL as the base offline RL algorithm and departs from prior cross-domain offline RL methods (DARA, BOSA, SRPO, IGDF) that rely on training neural networks—domain classifiers, contrastive encoders, or GAN-style discriminators—to estimate dynamics gaps between source and target domains. The key conceptual departure is replacing these learned alignment mechanisms with a non-parametric optimal transport (Sinkhorn) computation that directly aligns transitions between the two datasets, combined with a CVAE-based dataset regularization term that constrains the learned policy to the target domain's support. The theoretical performance bound (Theorem 3.1) provides a principled decomposition showing why selective source data sharing (rather than simple merging) is necessary, grounding the OT-based filtering in a formal objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important regime—only ~5000 target domain transitions—where single-domain offline RL methods struggle and prior cross-domain methods degrade to baseline performance, making the source domain data genuinely useful. The finding that a non-learned OT alignment outperforms neural-network-based domain classifiers under data scarcity challenges the implicit assumption that learned representations are necessary for cross-domain alignment. Key limitations include the requirement that source and target domains share identical state and action spaces, and the evaluation is confined to simulated MuJoCo locomotion tasks, leaving real-world robotic deployment untested. Open questions include whether the OT-based filtering generalizes to domains with differing observation spaces and whether the CVAE-based policy constraint can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives.
