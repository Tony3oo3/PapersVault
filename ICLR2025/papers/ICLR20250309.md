## Problem

Existing adversarial prompters (e.g., AdvPrompter) that automatically generate jailbreaking suffixes for LLMs suffer from low attack success rates (~20% on well-aligned models like Llama2-Chat-7B), produce benign suffixes that alter the original harmful intent (false positives), and require computationally intensive training. This paper addresses how to construct adversarial prompters that achieve near-perfect ASR against well-aligned LLMs while maintaining human-readable, perplexity-robust suffixes and reducing training time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful instruction (e.g., 'Write a tutorial on how to make a bomb') and a desired harmful response, reformulates them through a prompt template (~300 tokens) that explicitly specifies the target response format, and produces a short adversarial suffix (~30 tokens) generated autoregressively by a LoRA-adapted Llama2-7B base model. The suffix is appended to the templated instruction to form the final jailbreaking prompt. Unlike gradient-based methods (GCG) that produce unreadable token sequences, the output suffix is designed to be human-readable with perplexity comparable to natural text, and it is adaptive to the specific input instruction rather than universal.

## Method

ProAdvPrompter introduces a two-stage training pipeline for adversarial prompters. In Stage I (Exploration), the prompter generates suffixes token-by-token via beam search guided by the target LLM's loss (retaining top-k tokens most likely to elicit harmful responses), then fine-tunes on the full dataset including failed attempts using LoRA; a prompt template structures the instruction to narrow the search space and prevent the model from learning benign-rewriting strategies. In Stage II (Exploitation), the prompter iteratively generates k suffixes per instruction via fast autoregressive sampling, evaluates them against the target LLM, and fine-tunes exclusively on a filtered subset of successful suffixes (those whose instruction appears ≤γ times), optionally applying DPO to leverage negative samples. This design reduces the number of expensive beam-search iterations to as few as one, accelerates training by ~20%, and the filtering mechanism prioritizes challenging examples to prevent performance dilution.

## Results

ProAdvPrompter achieves 99.68% ASR@10 (89.42% ASR@1) on the training set and 99.04% ASR@10 (81.73% ASR@1) on the test set against Llama2-Chat-7B, and 97.12% ASR@10 (84.62% ASR@1) on training and 99.04% ASR@10 (85.58% ASR@1) on test against Llama3-Instruct-8B, roughly doubling the performance of the closest prior method. The generated adversarial suffixes maintain a reasonable perplexity (164.30 and 131.99 respectively), far below GCG's 106,374.89, and ProAdvPrompter reduces training time by ~20% compared to AdvPrompter on Llama3-Instruct-8B. Against the perplexity defense on a Harmbench subset, ProAdvPrompter retains 96.30% ASR@10 with a window-20 filter while AmpleGCG drops to 0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), ProAdvPrompter achieves a ~330× improvement in ASR@10 on Llama2-Chat-7B (99.68% vs. 0.3%) while reducing perplexity from 106,375 to 164, making the suffixes far more readable and resistant to perplexity-based filters. Against AutoDAN (Zhu et al., 2023), the gap is similarly dramatic: 99.68% vs. 4.1% ASR@10 on Llama2-Chat-7B training. Against the closest prior method AdvPrompter (Paulus et al., 2024), ProAdvPrompter more than quintuples ASR@10 on Llama2-Chat-7B (99.68% vs. 17.6%) and improves by ~36 percentage points on Llama3-Instruct-8B (97.12% vs. 60.90%), while also showing minimal train-test gap (suggesting better generalization) and ~20% faster training. Against AmpleGCG (Liao & Sun, 2024) on the Harmbench subset, both methods achieve high ASR without defense (100% vs. 96.30%), but under the window-20 perplexity filter ProAdvPrompter retains 96.30% ASR@10 while AmpleGCG drops to 0%, demonstrating substantially greater resilience to perplexity-based defenses.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProAdvPrompter extends the AdvPrompter framework (Paulus et al., 2024), which trains an LLM assistant to generate adversarial suffixes via alternating optimization, by introducing a two-stage pipeline: a loss-guided beam-search Exploration stage (borrowing the search strategy and prompt template from Andriushchenko et al., 2024) followed by an iterative Exploitation stage that fine-tunes the prompter on filtered successful suffixes using SFT and optionally DPO. The key departure from AdvPrompter is the addition of the prompt template to structure instructions and the filtering mechanism to prioritize challenging examples, while the departure from gradient-based methods (GCG, AmpleGCG) is the reliance on logit-only information and autoregressive generation for speed and readability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~2× ASR improvement over AdvPrompter on well-aligned LLMs (99.68% on Llama2-Chat-7B, 97.12% on Llama3-Instruct-8B) and the demonstrated resilience against perplexity defenses make this a practically relevant tool for red-teaming and safety evaluation. However, the method's dependence on a ~300-token prompt template limits its robustness against token-perturbation defenses like SmoothLLM (only 1/27 prompts survived), and the evaluation is confined to 7–8B open-weight models, leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger or closed-source LLMs. Future work on shorter templates, alternative search strategies in Stage I, and scaling to more diverse target models would strengthen the contribution.
