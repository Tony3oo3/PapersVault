## Problem

Zero-shot Vision-Language Models (VLMs) remain vulnerable to spurious correlations, where predictions rely on irrelevant features (e.g., background color in skin cancer diagnosis). Existing mitigation methods either require target labels (contradicting zero-shot), operate on a single modality (risking text-image misalignment), or depend on LLMs to characterize spurious features (introducing hallucination risk and prompt sensitivity). The paper seeks a label-free, LLM-free, training-free approach that handles both modalities simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen CLIP image encoder and text encoder, an RGB image, a set of spurious text prompts (e.g., 'a photo with a water background'), and target class text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a waterbird'). It outputs a predicted class label via standard zero-shot inner-product scoring. No model parameters are trained or updated; all operations occur in the pre-computed embedding space, and the only external inputs beyond the image are short natural-language prompt strings describing spurious and target features.

## Method

TIE (Text prompt-based Image Embedding translation) is grounded in a theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) showing that the optimal way to maximize worst-group accuracy is to translate image embeddings along the negative spurious-feature direction, preserving the latent-space distribution. Rather than projecting embeddings onto a neutralization hyperplane (as ROBOSHOT does, which distorts distribution and is sensitive to prompt quality), TIE computes a normalized spurious vector from text prompt embeddings and subtracts the mean projection of image embeddings onto that vector. When spurious labels are unavailable, TIE* infers pseudo-spurious labels via zero-shot classification against spurious prompts and applies the corresponding translation per sample. A theoretical comparison (Section 3.4) further shows TIE's worst-group accuracy degrades gracefully with imperfect spurious prompts, whereas ROBOSHOT's performance collapses unless the spurious classifier is near-perfect.

## Results

On the Waterbirds dataset with CLIP ViT-L/14, TIE achieves a worst-group accuracy (WG) of 78.82% with an average accuracy of 84.12% and a gap of only 5.30%, surpassing the previous best method (Ideal words, 64.17% WG) by 14.65 percentage points. On the high-stakes ISIC skin cancer dataset (Biomed CLIP), TIE reaches 65.87% WG with a 4.03% gap, compared to 53.30% WG for ROBOSHOT. On the multi-class FMOW satellite dataset (62 classes, 5 groups), TIE achieves 20.19% WG, the highest among all methods. TIE* (the fully zero-shot variant without spurious labels) achieves comparable but slightly lower WG, e.g., 61.60% on Waterbirds ViT-L/14 vs. 78.82% for TIE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIE consistently outperforms all four baselines on worst-group accuracy for ViT-based backbones across all five datasets. On Waterbirds ViT-L/14, TIE (78.82% WG) beats Ideal words (64.17%) by 14.65 points, Orth-Cali (58.56%) by 20.26 points, Perception CLIP (54.12%) by 24.70 points, and ROBOSHOT (45.17%) by 33.65 points. On the ISIC biomedical dataset, TIE (65.87% WG) surpasses ROBOSHOT (53.30%) by 12.57 points and Perception CLIP (41.55%) by 24.32 points. However, on ResNet-50 backbones, Orth-Cali (64.80% WG on Waterbirds) and Perception CLIP (80.22% WG on CelebA) outperform TIE, which the authors attribute to misalignment between the text-space spurious vector direction and the image-space representation due to architectural differences. TIE* (without spurious labels) narrows the gap to TIE substantially on CelebA (82.61% vs. 82.63% WG on ViT-B/32) but shows a larger gap on Waterbirds (61.60% vs. 78.82% on ViT-L/14), indicating that accurate spurious labels remain important for maximum performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIE departs from the dominant line of zero-shot robustification work (ROBOSHOT, Orth-Cali, Perception CLIP) that either projects image embeddings onto a neutralization hyperplane or modifies text prompts to remove spurious information. Instead, TIE introduces a translation operation in the image embedding space guided by spurious text prompts, grounded in a theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) that identifies the optimal translation direction as the negative spurious feature vector. This preserves the distribution of image embeddings (unlike projection) while maintaining cross-modal alignment (unlike text-only methods), and eliminates the need for LLM-generated spurious descriptions that prior methods like ROBOSHOT and Perception CLIP depend on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIE offers a practically attractive recipe—no training, no labels, no LLMs, no access to internal VLM parameters—for improving worst-group accuracy in zero-shot VLM classification, which is important for high-stakes domains like medical imaging where spurious correlations (e.g., color patches in skin lesions) can be dangerous. The theoretical comparison showing that projection-based methods (ROBOSHOT) degrade sharply under imperfect spurious classifiers while TIE remains stable provides a principled argument for why distribution-preserving operations are preferable. Key limitations remain: TIE* still lags behind TIE due to pseudo-label errors, the method struggles with dominant artifacts like watermarks, and the ResNet-50 results hint at unresolved modality-alignment issues between text and image encoders of different architectures.
