## Problem

Recovering 3D hand-face interactions with deformations from a single image is challenging due to self-occlusions, diverse spatial relationships, and single-view ambiguity. The prior state-of-the-art, Decaf, requires a time-consuming per-image optimization (>15 s) and depends on 3D annotations (contact labels, deformations) that are unavailable for in-the-wild data, limiting both speed and generalization. No prior method achieves end-to-end, real-time recovery of hand-face meshes, contacts, and deformations jointly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251564/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 224×224×3 RGB image and produces a 778-vertex hand mesh, a 5023-vertex face mesh, per-vertex face deformation vectors (5023×3), per-vertex contact probabilities for both hand (778) and face (5023), and animatable FLAME/MANO pose, shape, and expression parameters. The outputs are parametric model parameters obtained via neural inverse kinematics from intermediate non-parametric vertex predictions, making them directly usable for downstream animation. Inference runs at 20 fps on an Nvidia 4090 GPU.

## Method

DICE uses a two-branch Transformer architecture (MeshNet and InteractionNet) fed by a pretrained HRNet-W64 CNN backbone. MeshNet regresses unrefined hand and face vertex positions, which are then passed through a neural inverse-kinematics network (IKNet) to recover FLAME and MANO parameters, while InteractionNet predicts the local face deformation field and per-vertex contact probabilities in a separate branch—exploiting the semantic distinction between global geometry and local, transformation-invariant interaction features. A key training innovation is a weakly-supervised pipeline that augments the studio-collected Decaf dataset with 500 in-the-wild images, using depth supervision from a diffusion-based monocular depth estimator (Marigold) applied to 2D keypoints, plus adversarial discriminators on hand and face parameter spaces trained on RenderMe-360 and FreiHand to enforce pose/shape plausibility without requiring 3D ground truth.

## Results

On the Decaf benchmark, DICE achieves a per-vertex error (PVE) of 8.32 mm, a 7.5% reduction over the optimization-based state-of-the-art Decaf (9.65 mm), while running 200× faster (0.088 s vs. 19.59 s per image). Among all regression-based baselines, DICE attains the best PVE (8.32 vs. 9.27 for FastMETRO, 11.0 for PIXIE-R, 11.8 for METRO) and the highest F-Score for physical plausibility (72.7 vs. 72.0 for PIXIE-R, 66.2 for FastMETRO, 65.2 for METRO). DICE also achieves the best contact estimation F-Score on both face (0.61 vs. 0.57) and hand (0.50 vs. 0.47) over Decaf.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DICE outperforms Decaf (the prior SOTA optimization-based method) on reconstruction accuracy (PVE 8.32 vs. 9.65 mm, a 7.5% improvement) and runs 200× faster (0.088 s vs. 19.59 s), but Decaf retains a higher F-Score for physical plausibility (89.6 vs. 72.7) due to its iterative test-time optimization that adjusts hand-face relative positioning. Among regression-based baselines, DICE achieves the lowest PVE across all competitors: 27% lower than PIXIE-R (8.32 vs. 11.0), 30% lower than METRO* (8.32 vs. 11.8), and 10% lower than FastMETRO* (8.32 vs. 9.27). DICE also leads all regression-based methods on F-Score (72.7), narrowly beating PIXIE-R (72.0) and substantially outperforming METRO* (65.2) and FastMETRO* (66.2). On contact estimation (Table 2), DICE surpasses Decaf in F-Score for both face contacts (0.61 vs. 0.57) and hand contacts (0.50 vs. 0.47), with notably higher recall (0.57 vs. 0.49 for face; 0.45 vs. 0.39 for hand). The ablation in Table 3 confirms that the two-branch architecture (separating mesh from interaction) and the weakly-supervised in-the-wild training each contribute meaningfully to the final performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

DICE builds directly on Decaf (Shimada et al., 2023), the prior state-of-the-art for monocular hand-face interaction and deformation capture, but replaces Decaf's test-time optimization with an end-to-end regression pipeline. Architecturally, it adopts the progressive-downsampling Transformer and feature-query design from METRO (Lin et al., 2021a) and the neural inverse-kinematics paradigm from Hybrik (Li et al., 2021) and Kolotouros et al. (2019b), combining them in a two-branch structure (MeshNet for global geometry, InteractionNet for local deformation and contacts) that is novel to this task. The weakly-supervised training scheme, which leverages Marigold depth priors and adversarial pose priors from RenderMe-360 and FreiHand, departs from Decaf's reliance on studio-collected 3D annotations and represents a key methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 200× speedup (20 fps vs. >15 s) while simultaneously improving reconstruction accuracy makes hand-face interaction capture viable for real-time AR/VR and character animation, a domain where Decaf's optimization-based approach was impractical. The weakly-supervised pipeline—using only 500 in-the-wild images with depth and adversarial priors—offers a practical recipe for extending studio-trained models to diverse real-world conditions without expensive 3D annotation. Open questions remain: the method still fails under extreme occlusion, does not model hand deformations, and the in-the-wild training set is small (500 images); scaling to millions of images and incorporating physics-based simulation as a stronger prior are natural next steps.
