## Problem

Open-vocabulary object detection methods that leverage pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) for base-to-novel category generalization suffer from a fundamental misalignment between image-level VLM pretraining and region-level perception. Existing approaches bridge this gap by relying on additional supervisions—caption datasets or pseudo annotations from self-training—which are coarse, noisy, and often require knowledge of novel categories during training. This paper asks whether a cyclic, self-contained knowledge transfer from VLMs can close the performance gap without any extra training data.

## Input / Output

The detector receives an RGB image and a set of category name text prompts (12 template prompts per class, following CAT-SEG). During inference, the set of active category prompts is dynamically determined per image: either via CLIP zero-shot similarity scoring with a threshold ρ, or via a multimodal LLM (GVT) performing binary yes/no concept-existence queries. The output consists of bounding boxes with class labels assigned by cosine similarity between the student's region embeddings and the teacher's text embeddings (mirroring CLIP's zero-shot classification), rather than by the detector's own classification head logits.

## Method

CCKT-Det builds on Deformable DETR and introduces a cyclic cross-modal knowledge transfer loop with two components. First, semantic priors (CLIP text embeddings of image-relevant categories) are added to the learnable object queries to form image-specific language queries, making the decoder novel-concept aware and reducing the search space. Second, a regional contrastive distillation loss (bidirectional InfoNCE) aligns the student's region embeddings with offline-extracted teacher visual features (object crops encoded by the CLIP image encoder), where positive pairs are established via Hungarian matching rather than diagonal alignment. The cycle—semantic priors enter as query input, visual knowledge is distilled at the output—forces the detector to simultaneously bind text and visual knowledge from the VLM, and breaking either leg of the cycle degrades performance. The matching cost is augmented with VLM-computed similarity scores to counteract the detector's bias toward base categories, and the entire framework requires no additional training data beyond instance-level labels on base classes.

## Results

On OV-COCO, CCKT-Det++ with a SwinB backbone and GVT-based semantic priors achieves 46.0% AP₅₀ᴺ on novel classes and 46.2% AP₅₀ overall, setting a new state-of-the-art without any additional training data. With a lighter ResNet-50 backbone, CCKT-Det++ still reaches 45.3% AP₅₀ᴺ, surpassing all prior methods that rely on extra caption datasets or pseudo annotations. On OV-LVIS, CCKT-Det++ attains 18.2% AP_r on rare classes (ResNet-50, no extra data) and 32.8% AP_r (SwinB + pseudo annotations), outperforming CoDet (29.4%) and CORA+ (28.1%). In the cross-dataset transfer setting, the LVIS-trained CCKT-Det++ achieves 38.5% AP on COCO and 15.2% AP on Objects365, narrowing the gap to fully supervised models to −17% and −40% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCKT-Det++ (SwinB) achieves 46.0% AP₅₀ᴺ on OV-COCO, outperforming every baseline in Table 1: it beats CORA+ (43.1%, which uses COCO Caption) by +2.9%, BARON+ (42.7%) by +3.3%, OV-DQUO (45.6%) by +0.4%, CLIP-Self (44.3%) by +1.7%, DITO (40.2%) by +5.8%, and OV-DETR (29.4%) by +16.6%. Notably, CCKT-Det with SwinB (41.9% AP₅₀ᴺ) closely matches CORA+ (43.1%) without any extra caption data, demonstrating superior data efficiency. On OV-LVIS, CCKT-Det++ with SwinB and pseudo annotations reaches 32.8% AP_r, surpassing CoDet (29.4%) and CORA+ (28.1%), while the no-extra-data variant (18.2% AP_r) remains competitive with ViLD (16.3%) and OV-DETR (17.4%). In the transfer setting (Table 3), CCKT-Det++ outperforms DetPro by +3.6% AP on COCO and +3.1% AP on Objects365, and beats BARON by +2.3% AP on COCO and +1.6% AP on Objects365. The method also surpasses F-VLM by +6.0% AP on COCO transfer, while the gap to fully supervised COCO detection narrows to −17% (vs. −20% for BARON and −24% for F-VLM).

## Relation to Prior Work

CCKT-Det builds on the DETR/Deformable DETR architecture and the ViLD paradigm of distilling region-level knowledge from VLMs into a detector, but departs from both in key ways. Rather than relying on extra caption datasets or pseudo annotations (as in OV-DETR, BARON+, CoDet, CORA+), it introduces a cyclic contrastive transfer: semantic priors are injected into object queries at the input, and a regional contrastive loss aligns the resulting visual embeddings with teacher features at the output, forming a closed loop that forces the detector to bind both text and visual knowledge from the VLM. This replaces ViLD's element-wise L1 distillation with a contrastive objective that leverages all unmatched candidates as negatives, and replaces static object queries with dynamically image-conditioned language queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that open-vocabulary detection can reach state-of-the-art performance without any extra training data (no captions, no pseudo labels), which removes a significant practical barrier and data-curation bottleneck in the field. The finding that performance scales consistently with teacher model strength while the student backbone remains at a moderate size (ResNet-50) offers a practical alternative to the common strategy of scaling the detector backbone itself. Open questions include whether the inference-time dependency on a VLM/MLLM for semantic priors can be eliminated, how the method behaves on extremely large vocabularies beyond LVIS, and whether the cyclic design generalizes to other dense prediction tasks like segmentation.
