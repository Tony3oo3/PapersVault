## Problem

Deep learning-based image registration has achieved strong accuracy, but uncertainty estimation remains largely unexplored: existing approaches apply generic techniques like Monte Carlo dropout at the voxel level without exploiting the spatial structure of deformation fields, and there is no principled mechanism to propagate location-level uncertainties to fitted transformation models or downstream neuroimaging tasks. This paper asks whether a hierarchical, closed-form propagation of per-voxel uncertainties through transformation fitting can both improve registration accuracy and provide meaningful error bars for downstream analyses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single T1-weighted brain MRI scan resampled to 1 mm isotropic resolution and produces per-voxel MNI atlas coordinate predictions (in decimeters) along with per-voxel, per-direction standard deviations. The outputs are structured in three hierarchical levels: (1) per-voxel Gaussian parameters (mean and variance), (2) fitted transformation model parameters (affine, B-spline coefficients, or smoothed Demons field) with a full covariance matrix, and (3) multiple sampled segmentations whose label entropy provides a spatial uncertainty map. The network also predicts a binary brain mask used to exclude non-brain regions from all losses and fitting.

## Method

A U-Net backbone is trained in a supervised coordinate-regression framework (RbR) to predict per-voxel MNI coordinates and their per-direction standard deviations, using a Gaussian log-likelihood loss for aleatoric uncertainty alongside coordinate L2, mask, and optional atlas-segmentation Dice losses. The key technical contribution is a three-level uncertainty propagation: per-voxel Gaussian uncertainties are propagated to transformation model parameters via weighted least squares (weight matrix = inverse variance), yielding a closed-form covariance of the fitted coefficients (e.g., B-spline control points) or smoothed field. This second-level covariance enables uncertainty-aware fitting (uncertain voxels contribute less), sampling from the posterior of the transformation, and extraction of principal modes of variation. Finally, drawing multiple transformation samples and warping an atlas segmentation produces a distribution of labels at each voxel, whose entropy constitutes the third-level uncertainty for downstream tasks. The framework is transformation-agnostic—affine, B-spline, and non-parametric Demons models are all supported without retraining the network.

## Results

On ABIDE and OASIS3, uncertainty-aware fitting of B-spline (10 mm) transformations improves Dice scores from 0.782±0.020 to 0.790±0.019 (ABIDE) and from 0.750±0.034 to 0.772±0.030 (OASIS3). Affine transformations also benefit, improving from 0.718 to 0.730 (ABIDE) and 0.673 to 0.682 (OASIS3). The Demons (3 mm) transformation already achieves 0.799±0.020 (ABIDE) and 0.783±0.029 (OASIS3) without uncertainty weighting, showing no further gain. Aleatoric uncertainty correlates with coordinate prediction error (Spearman 0.601±0.019) while epistemic uncertainty via MC dropout does not (Spearman 0.181±0.017).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is RbR (Gopinath et al., 2024), the coordinate-regression atlas registration method on which this work builds. The proposed approach (RbR + atlas segmentation loss + uncertainty-aware fitting) consistently outperforms the original RbR across all transformation models on both test sets. For example, with Demons (3 mm), the proposed method achieves 0.767±0.024 Dice on ABIDE versus 0.739±0.038 for RbR, and 0.759±0.030 versus 0.728±0.028 on OASIS3. The gap is even larger for B-spline (10 mm): 0.758±0.023 vs. 0.738±0.039 (ABIDE) and 0.738±0.029 vs. 0.713±0.032 (OASIS3). The improvement is attributed to two factors: the addition of the atlas segmentation loss during training (which alone lifts RbR's Demons score from 0.739 to 0.767 on ABIDE) and the uncertainty-weighted fitting at test time (which further improves B-spline and affine fits). The Demons transformation does not benefit from uncertainty weighting at test time because it was already used in the training segmentation loss, leaving little room for additional improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Registration by Regression (RbR) framework (Gopinath et al., 2024) for coordinate-regression-based atlas registration, extending it with an uncertainty-aware fitting pipeline. Conceptually, it bridges two previously disconnected literatures: classical Bayesian registration uncertainty (Simpson et al., 2013; Kybic, 2009; Le Folgoc et al., 2017), which models the transform as a random variable, and modern DL uncertainty estimation (MC dropout, ensembles, aleatoric prediction), which operates at the voxel level. The key departure from prior DL registration work is the explicit hierarchical propagation of per-voxel uncertainties through weighted least-squares fitting into transformation model parameters (B-spline coefficients, smoothed fields) and further into downstream tasks, rather than treating uncertainty as a by-product of network predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MC dropout (epistemic uncertainty) correlates very poorly with actual registration error (Spearman ≈ 0.18) while learned aleatoric uncertainty correlates well (Spearman ≈ 0.60) challenges the common practice of defaulting to MC dropout in medical imaging and provides practitioners with a clear, actionable alternative. The uncertainty-aware fitting yields modest but consistent Dice improvements (e.g., +0.022 on OASIS3 for B-spline), and the hierarchical sampling framework offers a principled path to propagate registration uncertainty into group-level statistics and longitudinal analyses. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to pairwise registration, whether the aleatoric-uncertainty advantage holds across architectures and modalities, and whether the downstream statistical benefits (e.g., in aging or disease studies) are as substantial as the registration-level improvements suggest.
