## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for Local GD (FedAvg) on smooth convex objectives require a learning rate η ≤ 1/K, so increasing the number of local steps K is exactly offset by a smaller step size, leaving the dominating convergence term independent of K. This paper asks whether local steps can provably accelerate Local GD for the specific problem of distributed logistic regression, where empirical evidence suggests they do but prior theory cannot capture the benefit.

## Input / Output

The method operates on M distributed clients, each holding n samples (x_mi, y_mi) with ‖x_mi‖ ≤ 1 and binary labels, assumed to be jointly linearly separable with maximum margin γ. The algorithm takes an initialization vector w̄₀, the number of local steps K, communication rounds R, and learning rates as hyperparameters, and produces a last-iterate weight vector ŵ with a convergence guarantee F(ŵ) ≤ O(1/(γ²KR)) for the Two-Stage variant. Unlike baseline analyses that provide average-iterate guarantees, the Two-Stage result is a last-iterate bound, and the Local Gradient Flow variant requires the restrictive setting of M=2 clients with n=1 data point per client.

## Method

The core contribution is Two-Stage Local GD, which exploits the property of logistic loss that the Hessian norm is bounded by the loss value (‖∇²F(w)‖ ≤ F(w)), so once the loss is sufficiently small, a large learning rate η₂ ≤ 4 can be used without violating smoothness. The algorithm runs a warmup phase with small η₁ to reduce the loss below a threshold, then switches to η₂ = Θ(1) for the remaining rounds, achieving O(1/(γ²KR)) convergence and breaking the η ≤ 1/K barrier. The proof treats each round's aggregate update as a biased gradient step on the global objective, bounding the bias via local Hessian control (Lemma 1, a Gronwall-type argument) and local model drift (Lemma 2). A secondary contribution analyzes Local Gradient Flow (continuous-time local updates) for M=2, n=1 using a novel Lyapunov function built from surrogate losses involving the Lambert W function, yielding a similar Õ(1/KR) rate after a transition time τ.

## Results

The headline result is that Two-Stage Local GD achieves a last-iterate convergence rate of O(1/(γ²KR)) for distributed logistic regression after O(KM/γ⁴) warmup rounds, where K is the number of local steps and R is the number of communication rounds. This is the first guarantee showing that increasing K reduces the dominating term of the convergence rate, in contrast to all prior Local GD analyses where the rate is at best O(1/(γ²R)) independent of K. With the optimal choice K = Θ(γ/√(Mε)), the total communication cost is Õ(M^{1/2}/(γ³ε^{1/2})), improving over the baseline Õ(1/(γ²ε^{3/2})). For Local Gradient Flow (M=2, n=1), the rate is Õ(C^α/ε^{1-α}) where α = 1/√(log(1/(Cε))) → 0 logarithmically, yielding a sub-linear ε-dependence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Two-Stage Local GD against two existing Local GD analyses extended to the no-minimizer setting: Woodworth et al. (2020b) and Koloskova et al. (2020). Both baselines require η ≤ 1/K, so increasing local steps K is exactly offset by a smaller learning rate, leaving the dominating convergence term unchanged at O(1/(γ²R)). In contrast, Two-Stage Local GD uses a large second-stage learning rate η₂ ≤ 4 (independent of K), yielding a rate of O(1/(γ²KR)) that genuinely improves with more local steps. Concretely, for the best-K setting, the paper's method achieves Õ(M^{1/2}/(γ³ε^{1/2})) rounds versus Õ(1/(γ²ε^{3/2})) for Woodworth et al. and Õ(1/(γ²ε) + 1/γ^{3/4}) for Koloskova et al., representing a strict improvement in the ε-dependence. Empirically on MNIST (M=5, n=200) and a synthetic 2-client dataset, the two-stage stepsize shows faster convergence with larger K while maintaining stability, whereas the small stepsize η=1/(KH) required by baselines produces overlapping loss curves across all K values. The paper also confirms that Local SGD outperforms Minibatch SGD on CIFAR-10 with ResNets, consistent with prior empirical observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of Local SGD (FedAvg) by McMahan et al. (2017) to the specific problem of distributed logistic regression, departing from the worst-case framework of Woodworth et al. (2020b) and Koloskova et al. (2020) that requires η ≤ 1/K. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the self-concordance-like property of the logistic loss—its Hessian norm decreases with the loss value—to justify a large stepsize η ≤ 4 independent of K, breaking the η ≤ 1/K constraint that nullifies local steps in prior guarantees. The analysis also draws on Orabona (2024) for handling the absence of a global minimizer, and connects to Wu et al. (2024a,b) on edge-of-stability dynamics for logistic regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable demonstration that local steps accelerate Local GD for a concrete problem, challenging the prevailing worst-case narrative that local updates provide no communication benefit. The insight that loss-landscape structure (vanishing Hessian) rather than client-objective similarity drives the benefit offers a new lens for understanding federated learning. However, the guarantee requires a two-stage warmup (not vanilla Local GD), and the Local Gradient Flow results are limited to M=2 clients with n=1 data point each. The central open question—whether vanilla Local GD with a fixed large stepsize achieves O(1/KR) for logistic regression—remains unresolved, and extending the analysis beyond logistic regression or to stochastic settings is left for future work.
