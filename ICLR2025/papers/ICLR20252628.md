## Problem

Existing visual prompting methods (e.g., Pad Prompting in AutoVP, ILM-VP) restrict tunable parameters to the periphery of resized images, leaving central patches unmodified and treating each patch's prompt independently. This ignores the inductive bias from shared information and positional encoding across patches, limiting the model's ability to capture global context. The paper identifies these limitations through a systematic preliminary study of four alternative VP designs and proposes a unified solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images and resizes them to a fixed 224×224 resolution (no separate padding step). A low-rank visual prompt of shape c×224×224 (factorized as B·A with rank r=4, yielding only ~5K parameters) is added element-wise to the resized image before feeding it into a frozen backbone. The frozen model's output is then passed through a trainable linear probing head to produce class labels; for detection and segmentation variants, the corresponding task heads are adapted instead.

## Method

LoR-VP replaces the conventional pad-based visual prompt with the product of two low-rank matrices B ∈ R^{c×L×r} and A ∈ R^{c×r×L} (r≪L, typically r=4), which is added directly to the full-resolution resized image. This factorization simultaneously introduces shared information along rows and columns (inductive bias across patches) while preserving patch-specific degrees of freedom through independently adjustable coefficients. B is zero-initialized and A is randomly Gaussian-initialized so that B·A equals zero at training start, ensuring the prompted image equals the original image initially. For output transformation, the method uses Linear Probing on the frozen backbone's features rather than label mapping, which scales better to large classifier heads (e.g., 21,841-class Swin-B). Only the ~5K VP parameters and the LP head are trained, reducing tunable parameters by up to 18× compared to AutoVP while achieving 6× faster convergence and 3.1% higher average accuracy across seven architectures and four datasets.

## Results

LoR-VP achieves an average 3.1% accuracy improvement over the SOTA AutoVP across seven network architectures and four datasets, while requiring only 5K visual prompt parameters (18× fewer than AutoVP) and 6× less training time. On ImageNet-1K with ImageNet-21K-pretrained Swin-B, LoR-VP reaches 84.04% top-1 accuracy versus 78.98% for AutoVP and 83.52% for LP. Out-of-distribution generalization improves by an average of 10.6 points over AutoVP across ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-A, and ImageNet-V2. In object detection (Pascal VOC 2007), LoR-VP achieves 77.02 AP₅₀ versus 73.07 for AutoVP; in semantic segmentation (Pascal VOC 2012), it reaches 68.55 mIOU versus 67.42.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoR-VP consistently outperforms all four baselines across every model and dataset combination tested. Against AutoVP (ICLR 2024), the SOTA visual prompting method, LoR-VP gains 3.2% average accuracy on ImageNet-1K/CLIP-pretrained models and 5.06% on ImageNet-21K-pretrained Swin-B, while training 6× faster with 18× fewer VP parameters. Against ILM-VP (CVPR 2023), LoR-VP is dramatically superior: ILM-VP collapses to 26.17% on Tiny-ImageNet with ViT-B/16-P (vs. 84.40% for LoR-VP) and 66.39% on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (vs. 88.64%), largely because ILM's iterative label mapping is computationally prohibitive on large models. Against CLIP-VP (Bahng et al., 2022), LoR-VP achieves higher accuracy with far fewer parameters and simpler implementation (no complex resizing/padding/mask operations). Against VPT (ECCV 2022), which modifies transformer layers rather than input pixels, LoR-VP achieves 88.94% average accuracy across ten diverse datasets versus 87.66% for VPT, confirming that pixel-level low-rank prompting is competitive with internal-layer prompt tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual prompting paradigm introduced by Bahng et al. (CLIP-VP, 2022), which established the input-modification + output-transformation framework for adapting pre-trained vision models. It departs from the dominant 'pad prompting' design (used by ILM-VP and AutoVP) that restricts tunable parameters to the image periphery, instead borrowing the low-rank factorization idea from LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) to construct a full-image prompt via the product of two small matrices. On the output side, it replaces the label-mapping strategies (FLM, ILM, FM) prevalent in prior VP work with simple linear probing, arguing that the latter is more effective and scalable for large classifier heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the dramatic efficiency gains: a rank-4 low-rank prompt uses only ~5K parameters (18× fewer than AutoVP) and trains 6× faster while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy, making visual prompting viable in resource-constrained settings like mobile deployment. The conceptual contribution—that a structured, low-rank inductive bias across rows and columns outperforms unstructured per-patch prompts—challenges the prevailing assumption that more independent parameters yield better adaptation. Open questions include whether the low-rank structure remains optimal for tasks with highly local or asymmetric spatial dependencies, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to video or multi-modal prompting settings.
