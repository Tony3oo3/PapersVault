## Problem

Despite Shampoo's strong empirical performance in large-scale neural network training (outperforming all methods in the Algoperf benchmark and powering Gemini 1.5 Flash and Google Ads CTR models), the theoretical reason its Kronecker product preconditioner works well remained unexplained. Specifically, the relationship between Shampoo's approximation and the optimal Kronecker product approximation of the Gauss-Newton Hessian or Adagrad covariance matrix was unknown, and a common misconception about the role of the square root in Shampoo's update persisted in the literature.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a per-layer basis, taking the gradient matrix G ∈ R^{m×n} (reshaped from the vectorized gradient of a weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n}) and producing an updated weight matrix via W ← W − η L^{−1/4} G R^{−1/4}. The two maintained preconditioners L ∈ R^{m×m} and R ∈ R^{n×n} form a Kronecker product L ⊗ R that approximates the full mn × mn Hessian or Adagrad covariance matrix, reducing memory from O(m²n²) to O(m² + n²). The paper's key insight reframes the output: the square of Shampoo's approximation, E[GG^T] ⊗ E[G^T G], is the meaningful quantity that connects to the optimal Kronecker approximation, not the square-root version E[GG^T]^{1/2} ⊗ E[G^T G]^{1/2} used in the original update.

## Method

The paper proves (Proposition 1) that the square of Shampoo's Kronecker approximation, E[GG^T] ⊗ E[G^T G], is exactly equivalent to a single step of the power iteration algorithm (starting from identity matrices) for computing the optimal Frobenius-norm Kronecker product approximation of H = E[gg^T]. Theoretical justification for why one step suffices comes from two observations: the identity matrix maximizes the worst-case inner product with all PSD matrices (Proposition 2), and non-top singular vectors of the reshaped Hessian are not PSD (Lemma 6), causing trace cancellations that make α₁ dominate other coefficients. When H is itself a Kronecker product (rank-1 reshaped Hessian, as in binomial logistic regression), the approximation becomes exact up to a trace normalization (Corollary 2). Empirically, across MNIST (logistic regression), CIFAR-5M (ResNet18), and ImageNet (ConvNeXt-T), Shampoo² closely tracks the optimal Kronecker approximation while the original Shampoo (with square roots) performs significantly worse. The paper further quantifies how practical shortcuts—averaging gradients over a batch and substituting real labels for sampled labels (empirical Fisher)—degrade the Hessian approximation quality, with the latter effect growing with batch size.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative result is that Shampoo² (the square of Shampoo's Kronecker approximation, i.e., E[GGᵀ] ⊗ E[GᵀG]) achieves cosine similarity close to 1 with the optimal Kronecker product approximation (computed via 5 rounds of power iteration) across MNIST, CIFAR-5M, and ImageNet with logistic regression, ResNet18, and ConvNeXt-T architectures. In contrast, the original Shampoo approximation (with square roots, E[GGᵀ]^{1/2} ⊗ E[GᵀG]^{1/2}) shows significantly lower cosine similarity with the optimal Kronecker approximation. For MNIST-2 (binary logistic regression), Shampoo² achieves perfect correlation (cosine similarity = 1) with the optimal Kronecker approximation, as proved in Corollary 2. At batch size 1, the empirical Fisher closely tracks the optimal Kronecker approximation throughout training, but at batch size 256 the approximation quality degrades due to the non-zero-mean gradient term (Lemma 8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three Kronecker product approximations of the Gauss-Newton Hessian (H_GN) and Adagrad preconditioner (H_Ada): the optimal Kronecker approximation (5 rounds of power iteration), Shampoo², and original Shampoo. Shampoo² closely tracks the optimal Kronecker approximation across all datasets and architectures (Figure 1), while original Shampoo performs significantly worse, confirming that the square root in Shampoo's update is a suboptimal choice for approximating the Hessian. In the ViT/CIFAR-5M experiments (Figure 4, Appendix B), Shampoo² also outperforms K-FAC in capturing the true Hessian structure, though K-FAC shows competitive performance in some cases. The gap between Shampoo² and the optimal Kronecker approximation is relatively larger for the ViT architecture compared to ResNet18 and ConvNeXt-T, suggesting architecture-dependent approximation quality. The paper also shows that using real labels (empirical Fisher) at batch size 1 maintains high approximation quality, but degrades at batch size 256, whereas averaging gradients across the batch has no effect in expectation (Lemma 7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the optimal Kronecker product approximation theory of Van Loan & Pitsianis (1993) and the Shampoo optimizer of Gupta et al. (2018), establishing a precise equivalence between Shampoo's squared preconditioner and one step of power iteration for the optimal Kronecker approximation. It departs from Ren & Goldfarb (2021), who showed a similar correlation under the strong tensor-normal distributional assumption, by proving the result under only a rank-1 (or near-rank-1) second-moment condition, making it applicable to both the Adagrad and Hessian perspectives. The work also extends Koroko et al. (2023) to weight-sharing networks and provides the theoretical foundation that was subsequently used in the design of SOAP (Vyas et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves a long-standing gap in understanding why Shampoo works empirically, showing that its squared form (Shampoo²) is the quantity that actually approximates the optimal Kronecker product, while the original Shampoo with square roots is a strictly worse approximation. The practical impact is already visible in SOAP, which incorporates these insights and outperforms both AdamW and Shampoo on language modeling. Open questions remain about the conditions under which the Hessian is well-approximated by a Kronecker product (ViT architectures show notably larger gaps), and about whether further power iteration steps or alternative initializations could yield meaningfully better approximations in practice.
