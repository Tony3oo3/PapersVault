## Problem

Previous energy-based methods for OOD detection and generalization focus exclusively on the energy gap between in-distribution and out-of-distribution samples, neglecting the energy disparity among in-distribution training samples themselves. This paper shows that the energy distribution across training samples is biased (lower energy for more frequent classes) and that regularizing this intra-distribution energy disparity is both necessary for OOD generalization and orthogonal to existing risk-based regularization approaches like Invariant Risk Minimization.

## Input / Output

The method requires a pre-trained classifier (ResNet-32, ResNeXt-50, or ResNet-152), the full training set with labels, and a validation set drawn from the training set itself (no external data). For imbalanced CIFAR, the validation set is balanced to the minimum per-class count; for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist, it is stratified by shot level (few/median/many). The output is a set of per-sample energy regularization coefficients β_x computed via the influence function, which are then used to finetune the classifier for a small number of epochs (5–30 depending on dataset).

## Method

The paper first proves a theoretical unification: adding an energy regularization term β_x·E_θ(x) to cross-entropy simultaneously performs data re-weighting (scaling the loss by 1−β_x) and margin control (shifting the gradient coefficient of the true-class logit from p(y|x)−1 to p(y|x)−1/(1−β_x)), thereby subsuming both major branches of long-tail recognition methods. For OOD generalization where the distribution shift is implicit, the paper proposes Influence-Aware Energy Regularization (IAER), which computes the per-sample coefficient β_xi = −γ·I_val(x_i)/I_val^max using the influence function of energy regularization on a validation set, where I_val(x_i) = −∇_θ L(x_va,ŷ_va,θ̂)ᵀ H_θ̂⁻¹ ∇_θ E_θ(x_i). A key theoretical result (Remark 4.1) establishes that energy is arbitrary relative to classifier predictions—any prediction can be paired with any energy value—making energy regularization orthogonal to risk-based methods. The influence function is approximated via stochastic estimation (5000 iterations for CIFAR, 1000 for ImageNet-scale models) and the finetuning uses a small learning rate (1e-4 for CIFAR, 0.2 for ImageNet-scale) to avoid disrupting the pre-trained representation.

## Results

On domain generalization benchmarks (DomainBed), ERM+IAER achieves 51.9% on CMNIST, 86.6% on PACS, and 78.5% on VLCS, outperforming the ERM baseline (51.5%, 85.5%, 77.4%) and matching or exceeding IRM, GroupDRO, MLDG, CORAL, and SagNet. On long-tail CIFAR-10 (imbalance ratio 100), IAER boosts ERM from 70.36% to 75.83% and LDAM-DRW from 77.16% to 78.37%. On subpopulation shift (SubpopBench), IAER improves worst-group accuracy substantially, e.g., from 4.6% to 14.3% on CMNIST and from 30.0% to 33.3% on NICO++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On PACS, ERM+IAER (86.6%) outperforms all six baselines, beating the strongest competitor CORAL (86.1%) by 0.5% and ERM (85.5%) by 1.1%. On VLCS, ERM+IAER (78.5%) ties IRM (78.5%) and slightly trails CORAL (78.8%) by 0.3%, while clearly surpassing ERM (77.4%) and GroupDRO (76.7%). On CMNIST, ERM+IAER (51.9%) is competitive with IRM and GroupDRO (both 52.0%) but trails them by 0.1%, while improving over ERM (51.5%) and CORAL (51.2%). In the long-tail setting, IAER consistently improves both ERM and LDAM-DRW pre-trained models, with the largest gain being +5.47% error reduction on long-tailed CIFAR-10 (ratio 100) for ERM. On subpopulation shift, IAER yields the most dramatic improvement on worst-group accuracy (e.g., +9.7 points on CMNIST), while mean accuracy gains are modest (+0.3 to +0.8 points). Overall, IAER is orthogonal to risk-based methods (IRM, GroupDRO) and provides complementary gains, particularly on PACS and worst-group metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based model framework (Grathwohl et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2020) from its traditional use in OOD detection—where energy distinguishes in-distribution from out-of-distribution samples—to regularizing energy differences *among* in-distribution training samples. It provides a theoretical unification showing that two seemingly distinct branches of long-tail recognition (data reweighting and margin control à la LDAM) are both implicit forms of energy regularization, then extends this perspective to OOD generalization via influence functions (Koh & Liang, 2017) to determine per-sample regularization coefficients. The approach is explicitly orthogonal to invariant risk minimization methods (IRM, GroupDRO), which regularize risk rather than energy across domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified energy perspective offers a clean theoretical lens connecting disparate long-tail and subpopulation-shift methods, and the Remark 4.1 (arbitrary energy) formalizes why energy regularization is independent of prediction-based methods. However, the practical impact is limited by the computational cost of influence function approximation (hours per dataset), the need for a validation set whose choice significantly affects performance, and the fact that influence functions are defined for convex losses and may not accurately reflect behavior in non-convex neural networks. Open questions include whether cheaper approximations of the energy influence can capture the same signal, and whether the energy-regularization perspective can be combined with risk-based methods (IRM, GroupDRO) for complementary gains.
