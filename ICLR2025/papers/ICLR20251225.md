## Problem

Parameter sharing (layer tying) is a natural way to reduce LLM deployment cost, but its application to modern pretrained LLMs has yielded limited success. The key gap is the absence of an effective method to convert existing, well-pretrained Transformers into compact parameter-shared models without substantial performance degradation, and the lack of a principled way to relax the strict tying constraint while preserving compactness.

## Input / Output

The method takes the full pretrained weight matrices of a standard Transformer (e.g., Gemma 2B with 18 layers, 2048 hidden dim) and produces a Recursive Transformer that stores only K unique layers (e.g., 9) to be looped L/K times, optionally augmented with per-loop LoRA adapters of configurable rank. The LoRA initialization is computed via truncated SVD on the residual matrices (W_ℓ − W'_shared), so the rank hyperparameter directly controls the trade-off between the fully-tied recursive model and the original full-size model. At inference, the model produces generated text tokens, and the recursive structure additionally enables continuous depth-wise batching throughput metrics.

## Method

The Recursive Transformer converts a pretrained L-layer model into a looped architecture by selecting a block of K layers (using Stepwise, Average, or Lower initialization from the original weights) and repeating it L/K times, then uptraining on SlimPajama for 15–60B tokens with optional forward-KL knowledge distillation from the full-size teacher. The Relaxed Recursive Transformer relaxes strict weight tying by attaching independent LoRA modules to each layer per loop iteration, initialized via truncated SVD on the residual between original and shared weights so that W'x + BAx ≈ Wx at high rank. This creates a continuous Pareto frontier: rank 0 recovers the fully-tied Recursive Transformer, while full rank recovers the vanilla model. A key architectural insight is that the shared layer function across loop iterations enables Continuous Depth-wise Batching—a new serving paradigm where different samples in a batch can simultaneously occupy different loop iterations—combined with early-exiting to achieve theoretical 2–3× throughput gains over vanilla Transformers.

## Results

The recursive Gemma 1B model (converted from Gemma 2B with 2 loop blocks) achieves 51.7% average few-shot accuracy after only 15B tokens of uptraining, outperforming the reduced-size baseline (38.2%) by 13.5 points and surpassing both TinyLlama 1.1B (43.3%) and Pythia 1B (48.8%). With 60B tokens of uptraining plus knowledge distillation, the relaxed Gemma model at LoRA rank 512 reaches 58.4% average accuracy, nearly matching the full-size Gemma 2B (58.6%) while using roughly half the parameters. In terms of inference throughput, continuous depth-wise batching with early exiting yields up to 2.66× speedup over the vanilla Gemma model (2877 vs. 1080 tokens/sec), and the recursive Gemma achieves nearly 4× the throughput of vanilla Pythia 1B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full-size Gemma 2B (61.7% pretrained, 58.6% after 60B-token uptraining), the relaxed recursive Gemma at rank 512 with 60B tokens + KD achieves 58.4%, essentially matching the full-size model while using ~half the parameters. Against TinyLlama 1.1B (43.3% at 105B tokens, 47.1% at 503B tokens), the recursive Gemma 1B (51.7% after only 15B tokens of uptraining) wins by 8.4 and 4.6 points respectively, despite being trained on far fewer tokens. Against Pythia 1B (48.8% after 300B tokens of pretraining), the recursive Gemma 1B again wins by 2.9 points with only 15B tokens of uptraining. The paper also compares against reduced-size models pretrained from scratch on the same 60B-token budget with distillation; the recursive Gemma outperforms these by 10 percentage points (51.7% vs. 38.2%), demonstrating that the initialization from pretrained weights and the looping architecture are both critical. In the throughput comparison, the recursive model with CDB + early exiting (2877 tok/s) surpasses both vanilla Gemma (1080 tok/s) and Gemma with continuous sequence-wise batching (1528 tok/s), achieving 2.66× and 1.88× speedups respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Universal Transformer lineage (Dehghani et al., 2019) and the CYCLE parameter-sharing strategy (Takase & Kiyono, 2023) from small, from-scratch models to the LLM regime, where prior parameter-sharing work has shown limited success. The key methodological departure is the Relaxed Recursive Transformer, which relaxes strict weight tying via layer-specific LoRA modules (Hu et al., 2022) initialized through truncated SVD on residual weight matrices—a technique not present in prior looped-Transformer or LoRA literature. The proposed continuous depth-wise batching inference paradigm generalizes continuous sequence-wise batching (Kwon et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2022) by exploiting the shared layer functions across loop iterations, enabling a new dimension of dynamic scheduling that vanilla Transformers cannot support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: a recursive Gemma 1B uptrained on only 15B tokens outperforms full-size TinyLlama 1.1B (105B tokens) and Pythia 1B (300B tokens), suggesting that source-model quality can substitute for training data volume in compressed architectures. The continuous depth-wise batching with early-exiting offers a theoretically 2–3× throughput gain, though the oracle-exiting assumption and the restriction to 1–2B models limit near-term practical impact. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 7B+ models, how to handle KV-cache computation for early-exited tokens in practice, and whether the SVD-based LoRA initialization generalizes to sparser or MoE architectures.
