## Problem

Standard optimizers applied to LoRA (Adam, Adagrad, RMSProp, even Shampoo) are not transformation invariant: the same weight update Z = AB^T decomposed as A₁B₁^T vs. A₂B₂^T yields different parameter updates, causing one LoRA factor to dominate while the other remains nearly fixed. This mathematical inconsistency leads to inefficient feature learning and sub-optimal solutions in practice. The paper asks whether a principled optimizer can be designed that inherently enforces transformation invariance for LoRA without incurring the heavy overhead of full matrix preconditioners.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the LoRA factor matrices A ∈ R^{m×r} and B ∈ R^{n×r} (with r ≪ min(m,n)) along with their per-step gradients ∇A = ∇Z·B and ∇B = ∇Z^T·A. It produces parameter updates δA and δB that are guaranteed to yield the same composite update δZ regardless of the specific factorization of Z. A key structural detail is that preconditioning is applied only on the short r×r side (one-sided), keeping memory at O(mr + nr + r²) and per-step time at O(mr² + nr² + r³), comparable to Adam when r is small.

## Method

LoRA-RITE achieves transformation invariance by introducing 'unmagnified gradients' that strip the magnitude from standard gradients via polar decomposition: ∇̄A = ∇Z·U_B = ∇A·R_B^†, where B = U_B·R_B. The update rule δA = -η·∇̄A·(∇̄A^T·∇̄A)^(-1/2)·(R_B^T)^† applies Adagrad-style matrix preconditioning on the r×r side, with the (R_B^T)^† factor adjusting magnitude so that δA·B^T is invariant across equivalent factorizations. To incorporate adaptive first and second moments, the algorithm projects accumulated statistics through basis-change matrices P_{A_t} = (U_{B_t})^T·U_{B_{t-1}} at each step, and tracks an 'escaped mass' ρ that compensates for information lost during basis transitions, ensuring the preconditioner monotonically increases. The paper proves that diagonal preconditioners fundamentally cannot achieve transformation invariance, that the method guarantees efficient feature learning (Theorem 1), and provides an O(G·D_A·D_B·T^{-1/2}) regret bound (Theorem 4) that improves over one-sided matrix Adagrad when the two LoRA factors are imbalanced in magnitude.

## Results

On the LLM benchmarking suite (HellaSwag, ArcChallenge, GSM8K, OpenBookQA), LoRA-RITE achieves 58.81% average accuracy with Gemma-2B and 76.91% with Gemma-7B, outperforming Adam (54.33% / 68.71%) by 4.5% and 8.2% respectively. On GSM8K with Gemma-7B, LoRA-RITE reaches 55.50% accuracy, surpassing Adam (48.37%) by 7.1 points and the second-best optimizer Lamb (50.64%) by ~5 points. On the Super-Natural Instructions dataset, LoRA-RITE yields a 4.6% accuracy gain over Adam on Gemma-2B and consistent improvements across classification and generation tasks. Training speed overhead is minimal: LoRA-RITE is only 8% slower than Adam on Gemma-2B and 5% slower on Gemma-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA-RITE consistently outperforms all five baselines across both model sizes and all four benchmarks. Against Adam (the standard default), LoRA-RITE gains 4.5% average accuracy on Gemma-2B and 8.2% on Gemma-7B, with the largest single-task gain being +7.1 points on GSM8K (Gemma-7B). Lamb is the strongest competitor, achieving scalar scale invariance through layer-wise norm normalization, yet LoRA-RITE still leads by 1.6% (Gemma-2B) and 2.2% (Gemma-7B) in average accuracy, suggesting that full transformation invariance beyond scalar scale invariance provides additional benefit. Shampoo, despite being a matrix preconditioner, underperforms Adam on several tasks (e.g., ArcChallenge 52.47% vs 54.78% on Gemma-7B) and is slower in wall-clock time than LoRA-RITE due to its O(m³+n³) preconditioner updates. ScaledAdam and LoRA+ show only marginal differences from Adam, confirming that simply adjusting learning rates or combining Riemannian gradient with element-wise Adam does not recover the full benefit of transformation invariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LoRA parameterization (Hu et al., 2022) and adapts the adaptive preconditioning mechanism of Adagrad (Duchi et al., 2011) to a matrix form operating on the low-rank side. It departs from prior work by proving that diagonal preconditioners (Adam, Adagrad, RMSProp) fundamentally cannot achieve transformation invariance for LoRA, and that existing matrix preconditioners (Shampoo, CASPR) also fail this property while incurring O(m²+n²) overhead. The closest prior approaches—LoRA+ (different learning rates per factor) and ScaledAdam (Riemannian gradient descent combined with Adam)—address related imbalances but sacrifice either simplicity or invariance; LoRA-RITE is positioned as the first adaptive matrix-preconditioning optimizer that provably maintains full transformation invariance at near-Adam cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for the large community doing LoRA fine-tuning: a drop-in optimizer replacement with ~8% overhead that consistently yields 3–7% accuracy gains across models and tasks. The theoretical contribution—showing diagonal preconditioners are fundamentally incompatible with transformation invariance—provides a clean design principle for future PEFT optimizers. Key limitations include the assumption that the loss takes the form f(AB^T), which may not hold for more complex PEFT structures (DoRA, DeepLoRA), and the open question of whether the invariance principle extends to those variants. The paper also acknowledges that LoRA itself has smaller representational power than full fine-tuning, so the optimizer cannot close that gap.
