## Problem

While upper bounds on the generalization error of deep neural networks have been well studied, no minimax lower bound for deep feed-forward networks with non-linear (ReLU) activation functions matching those upper bounds had been established. This left open the fundamental question of whether the correct sample complexity rate is the 'parametric' 1/n (as in classical statistics) or the slower 1/√n, and whether practitioners' intuition that deep learning requires 'many' samples is mathematically justified.

## Input / Output

Theoretically, the framework assumes n i.i.d. samples (x_i, y_i) where x_i follows a fixed Gaussian marginal P_x = N(0, I_d) and y_i = f*(x_i) + u_i with Gaussian noise u_i ~ N(0, σ²), producing a minimax risk lower bound that scales as √(log(d))/n for the squared L₂ loss. Empirically, the method consumes standard RGB images (28×28 for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10) and 8-attribute tabular data (California Housing Prices), incrementally increasing training set size in steps of 200–500 samples and reporting test error as a proxy for generalization error.

## Method

The core technique applies a 'local packing' (rescaling) variant of Fano's inequality to derive a minimax lower bound, requiring a novel lower bound on the packing number of deep ReLU network spaces (Lemma 3.2). This packing bound is established by constructing a subclass of the network space using non-negative weight matrices—where ReLU activations become identity and the deep network reduces to a matrix product—combined with binary vectors of fixed Hamming weight and Gaussian half-space integral calculations. The resulting bound depends on the input dimension only through a log(d) factor and on the sparsity parameter V_F = (v_s/L)^L, matching the rate in existing upper bounds for ℓ₁-regularized networks. Empirically, the paper fits two candidate curves (c₁ + α/√n and c₂ + β/n) to test error across increasing sample sizes using SLSQP optimization, consistently showing the 1/√n curve fits far better (R² > 0.8 vs negative R² for 1/n) across feed-forward and CNN architectures on both classification and regression tasks.

## Results

The paper establishes a minimax lower bound showing that the generalization error of ReLU feed-forward networks scales as √(log(d)/n), confirming the 1/√n rate rather than the parametric 1/n rate. Empirically, the 1/√n curve (c₁ + α/√n) consistently outperforms the 1/n curve (c₂ + β/n) across all four datasets and all architectures tested. For MNIST, the 1/√n curve achieves R² = 0.93 (shallow) and 0.96 (four-layer) versus negative R² (−1.4 and −0.78) for the 1/n curve. For the California Housing Prices regression task, the 1/√n curve achieves R² = 0.84 with MSE = 2.1×10⁻², while the 1/n curve yields R² = 0.21 with MSE = 9.9×10⁻².

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central empirical comparison is between two competing rate hypotheses for generalization error: the parametric 1/n rate (inherited from classical statistics such as least-squares linear regression) and the non-parametric 1/√n rate. The 1/√n curve wins decisively across every dataset and architecture examined. On MNIST, the 1/√n curve achieves R² of 0.93–0.96 while the 1/n curve produces negative R² values (−1.4 and −0.78), indicating it fits worse than a horizontal line. On Fashion-MNIST, the 1/√n curve maintains R² of 0.82–0.88 across shallow, three-layer, and CNN architectures, while the 1/n curve again yields negative R² (−1.6 to −2.1). On CIFAR-10, the 1/√n curve still outperforms (R² = 0.53 shallow, 0.48 CNN) though both curves fit less tightly, likely due to the higher intrinsic complexity of the task. On the CHP regression dataset, the 1/√n curve achieves R² = 0.84 versus 0.21 for 1/n. The consistent superiority of the 1/√n rate across classification and regression, feed-forward and CNN architectures, provides strong empirical support for the theoretical minimax lower bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimax lower bound program for neural networks from shallow architectures with sinusoidal (Klusowski & Barron, 2017) or linear (Du et al., 2018) activations to deep ReLU feed-forward networks, using the local packing/rescaling variant of Fano's inequality from Yang & Barron (1999). The key technical departure is establishing the first lower bound on the packing number of deep ReLU network spaces (Lemma 3.2), which prior work had not achieved. The resulting 1/√n rate matches the upper bound for ℓ₁-regularized networks from Taheri et al. (2021), closing a gap that the authors note had remained open for deep non-linear architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first rigorous mathematical confirmation that deep ReLU networks fundamentally require ~1/ε⁴ samples to achieve error ε², in contrast to ~1/ε² for classical parametric methods like least squares. This has direct implications for data budgeting in large-scale training (e.g., LLMs) and validates the practitioner intuition that deep learning is data-hungry. Limitations include the Gaussian input assumption, the v₀=1 constraint on the first layer, and the ℓ₁-type sparsity structure; whether the 1/√n rate persists without sparsity assumptions or for other activation functions remains open. The empirical extension to CNNs and multiple datasets strengthens the claim but does not constitute a proof for those architectures.
