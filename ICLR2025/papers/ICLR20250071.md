## Problem

Reconstructing high-fidelity, time-consistent meshes from dynamic video remains highly challenging: volumetric representations (NeRFs) lack explicit geometry and memory efficiency, while point-cloud methods (3D Gaussian Splatting) do not produce meshes with cross-frame vertex correspondences. No prior framework recovers meshes with flexible topology changes and consistent vertex tracking across time from dynamic observations, which is essential for downstream tasks like texture editing and physics-based simulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of dynamic RGB video frames along with per-frame camera intrinsics/extrinsics and time labels as input; for real-world videos, a foreground segmentation mask is required. It outputs a temporally consistent 3D mesh sequence where each frame's mesh has a 1-to-1 correspondence to the underlying 3D Gaussians, enabling vertex-level tracking across frames. The mesh supports arbitrary topology changes (e.g., sphere-to-torus), and vertex colors are stored in the canonical space via a time-dependent appearance module, making the output directly usable in physics simulators and for dynamic texture propagation.

## Method

DG-Mesh maintains a set of canonical 3D Gaussians deformed to each timestep by a forward MLP, then extracts a mesh per frame via a differentiable Poisson Surface Reconstruction (DPSR) followed by differentiable Marching Cubes, rendered with Nvdiffrast. The key novelty is Gaussian-Mesh Anchoring: at every 100 training iterations, the deformed Gaussians are re-densified and pruned to enforce a strict 1-to-1 mapping with mesh face centroids, correcting the non-uniform Gaussian distribution that violates DPSR's uniform-sampling assumption. A backward deformation network projects the anchored Gaussians back to the canonical space, and a Cycle-Consistent Deformation loss (L1 between forward deformation and negated backward deformation) keeps the canonical set in sync with additions/removals. Training combines Gaussian splatting rendering loss, mesh rasterization loss, mask loss, Laplacian smoothness regularization, anchoring distance loss, and the cycle-consistency loss over 50k iterations on a single GPU.

## Results

On the DG-Mesh dataset (6 dynamic scenes with ground-truth meshes), DG-Mesh achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (CD) on 5 of 6 scenes (e.g., 0.297 on Horse vs. 0.895 for the best baseline 4DGS; 0.510 on Bird vs. 0.742 for K-Plane) and the lowest Earth Mover Distance (EMD) on all 6 scenes (e.g., 0.047 on Duck vs. 0.055 for K-Plane; 0.125 on Bird vs. 0.131 for K-Plane). DG-Mesh also achieves the highest mesh-rendering PSNR (PSNRm) across all scenes, reaching 32.757 on Duck, 30.865 on Horse, and 33.026 on Girlwalk. On the D-NeRF dataset, DG-Mesh attains the best mesh-rendering PSNR on 7 of 8 objects (e.g., 31.769 on Jumpingjacks vs. 27.078 for HexPlane; 30.400 on Mutant vs. 26.811 for HexPlane), while also providing the only cross-frame vertex correspondences among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DG-Mesh outperforms all 8 baselines on EMD across all 6 DG-Mesh scenes, with the largest margins on Bird (0.125 vs. 0.131 for K-Plane, the closest competitor) and Horse (0.164 vs. 0.177 for SC-GS). On CD, DG-Mesh wins on 5 of 6 scenes; the sole exception is Torus2sphere where 4DGS achieves a marginally lower CD (1.523 vs. 1.572), though DG-Mesh still wins on EMD (0.177 vs. 0.226) and PSNRm (25.703 vs. not reported) for that scene. Among the Gaussian-based baselines, 4DGS and SC-GS are the strongest competitors on CD for Horse (0.895/0.897 vs. DG-Mesh's 0.297), while Deformable-GS and 4D-GS are competitive on EMD for Beagle (0.106/0.161 vs. 0.114). On the D-NeRF dataset (Table 2), DG-Mesh achieves the highest mesh-rendering PSNR on 7 of 8 objects, with particularly large gains on Jumpingjacks (31.769 vs. 27.078 for HexPlane) and Mutant (30.400 vs. 26.811 for HexPlane), while HexPlane edges out DG-Mesh on Lego (22.872 vs. 21.289). Crucially, DG-Mesh is the only method that provides cross-frame vertex correspondences, enabling downstream tasks like dynamic texture editing that are infeasible with the other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm (canonical Gaussians + deformation networks, as in Deformable-GS and 4D-GS) by adding an explicit mesh extraction and tracking layer on top. It leverages the Differentiable Poisson Solver and differentiable Marching Cubes to convert the deformed Gaussian point cloud into a watertight mesh at each timestep, then introduces two novel mechanisms—Gaussian-Mesh Anchoring (mesh-guided densification/pruning to enforce uniform Gaussian distribution) and Cycle-Consistent Deformation (a backward deformation network that keeps the canonical space consistent when Gaussians are added or removed)—to establish 1-to-1 mesh-face-to-Gaussian correspondences and cross-frame vertex tracking. Unlike prior dynamic NeRF or 3DGS methods that focus solely on rendering quality, DG-Mesh explicitly targets mesh geometry fidelity and temporal vertex correspondences, which prior baselines (D-NeRF, K-Plane, HexPlane, TiNeuVox, 4D-GS, Deformable-GS, 4DGS, SC-GS) do not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Meshes with cross-frame correspondences are the standard asset format in 3D engines, enabling downstream tasks like dynamic texture editing, physics-based simulation, and geometry processing that are intractable with implicit or point-cloud representations. The demonstrated ~100× speedup in mesh querying (10.9 FPS vs. 0.10 FPS for Deformable-GS) makes the output practically usable in interactive pipelines. Key limitations remain: the method requires foreground segmentation, struggles when entirely new object parts appear (fundamental correspondence ambiguity), and the 10.9 FPS mesh rendering, while fast, is not yet real-time for high-resolution applications. Open questions include extending to multi-object scenes, handling more extreme topology changes, and reducing the 50k-iteration training cost.
