## Problem

Models trained on synthetic datasets from dataset distillation are highly sensitive to the choice of loss function for soft label utilization, yet no universal loss function exists to handle this robustly. Additionally, cross-optimizer generalization—a previously overlooked issue—reveals that traditional loss functions (e.g., KL divergence) suffer catastrophic performance drops (up to 50%) when the optimizer changes from AdamW to SGD or Adam. Soft labels from teacher models are also inherently suboptimal due to limited teacher accuracy and predominantly intra-class information, while hard labels alone fail on small synthetic datasets.

## Input / Output

GIFT is a plug-and-play module that operates on pre-generated synthetic datasets from existing distillation methods (SRe2L, RDED, DATM, G-VBSM, CDA) without modifying the distillation pipeline. It takes three inputs: synthetic images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K), soft labels from pre-trained teacher models, and hard labels. The only change is replacing the evaluation loss function, incurring negligible additional training time (~1–67 seconds) and zero additional peak memory. It produces a trained classification model and is validated across six architectures (ConvNet, ResNet-18/101, Swin-V2-Tiny, EfficientNet-B0, MobileNet-V2) and three optimizers (SGD, Adam, AdamW).

## Method

GIFT comprises two modules. First, label refinement blends normalized soft labels with normalized label-smoothed hard labels via ỹ ← γ·(y/‖y‖) + (1−γ)·(ỹ/‖ỹ‖) with γ=0.1, correcting erroneous teacher signals and enhancing inter-class dispersion. Second, a cosine similarity loss L = E[1 − (φ_θ(x̃)·ỹ)/(‖φ_θ(x̃)‖·‖ỹ‖)] is derived theoretically by upper-bounding the InfoNCE loss (a proxy for V-information) and exploiting the approximate orthogonality of refined soft labels, which drives the negative-sample term to zero. This normalization-based loss inherently stabilizes training across optimizers because it avoids the small-loss-value regime where Adam's integrated weight decay dominates gradient updates. The approach requires no extra information, no dataset re-synthesis, and adds near-zero computational cost while consistently improving all baseline methods by 0.3–5.0% in accuracy and up to 51.5% in cross-optimizer generalization.

## Results

GIFT is a plug-and-play label utilization method that consistently improves all SOTA dataset distillation baselines without additional computational cost. On CIFAR-100 (IPC=10, ConvNet), SRe2L+GIFT reaches 38.0% top-1 accuracy (↑4.3% over SRe2L's 33.7%), and RDED+GIFT reaches 50.6% (↑2.5% over RDED's 47.5%). On ImageNet-1K (IPC=10, ConvNet), RDED+GIFT achieves 24.0% (↑3.9% over RDED's 20.1%). The most dramatic gains appear in cross-optimizer generalization: on CIFAR-100 with SGD, RDED+GIFT achieves 53.4% versus RDED's 1.9% (↑51.5%), and on ImageNet-1K with SGD, RDED+GIFT reaches 45.8% versus RDED's 0.1% (↑30.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIFT consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and IPC setting tested. Among dataset distillation methods, the largest gains are over SRe2L (up to +4.3% on CIFAR-100 IPC=10) and G-VBSM (up to +3.7% on CIFAR-100 IPC=10), while RDED and DATM see more modest but consistent improvements of +1.0–3.9%. Against CDA at higher IPC (50–200), GIFT adds +5.0% on Tiny-ImageNet IPC=50 and +1.1% on ImageNet-1K IPC=50. In the knowledge distillation comparison (Table 5), GIFT surpasses all four KD baselines: it beats DKD (the strongest KD method) by +1.6% on CIFAR-100 IPC=10 and by +6.4% on ImageNet-1K IPC=50, while outperforming KD, WSLD, and NKD by margins of 7–12% on CIFAR-100. The cross-optimizer results (Table 8) reveal the most striking advantage: RDED+GIFT gains +51.5% under SGD on CIFAR-100 and +30.8% under SGD on ImageNet-1K, whereas the baselines collapse to near-chance accuracy under non-AdamW optimizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a plug-and-play refinement layer on top of existing soft-label dataset distillation methods (SRe2L, RDED, DATM, G-VBSM, CDA), rather than proposing a new distillation pipeline. Theoretically, it builds on the V-information framework from Sun et al. (2024) and the InfoNCE loss (Oord et al., 2018), deriving a cosine similarity loss as a tractable upper bound on V-information. Conceptually, it departs from prior work by treating the *evaluation-time* loss function as a first-class design choice—something the field had largely ignored—rather than focusing on the distillation process itself. The label refinement step (blending teacher soft labels with smoothed hard labels) draws on standard label smoothing (Szegedy et al., 2016) but applies it in a novel context to correct teacher errors and improve inter-class separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most impactful finding is the revelation that distilled datasets are extremely fragile under optimizer changes (e.g., RDED drops from 47.5% to 17.8% on Tiny-ImageNet when switching from AdamW to Adam), exposing a previously unexamined vulnerability in the evaluation protocol for dataset distillation. GIFT's near-zero-cost plug-and-play design makes it immediately adoptable, and the consistent 1–4% gains across scales (up to 30.8% in cross-optimizer settings) are practically meaningful. However, the method does not alter the distillation process itself, so it cannot fix fundamentally poor synthetic images; its ceiling is bounded by the quality of the underlying distilled data. Open questions include whether the cosine similarity loss generalizes to non-classification tasks (e.g., detection, segmentation) and whether the γ=0.1 label refinement ratio remains optimal when teacher models are significantly stronger than the student.
