## Problem

Provable machine unlearning guarantees have been established only for supervised learning settings (linear models, convex losses, Bayesian models, GANs), with no theoretical results for the dominant pre-training and fine-tuning paradigm. This paper provides the first provable unlearning guarantees in that paradigm by studying topic models—simple bag-of-words language models that can be pre-trained self-supervised and adapted to downstream tasks like classification and retrieval.

## Input / Output

The unlearning algorithm consumes a trained topic model (matrices A ∈ R^{n×r} and R ∈ R^{r×r}), a forget set S_f of documents to remove, and precomputed training-set statistics (the word co-occurrence matrix Q, the set of anchor words P, per-word coefficient vectors C_i, and normalization constants p). It outputs a modified topic model (Ã, R̃) that is (ε,δ)-indistinguishable from one retrained without S_f, with a deletion capacity T bounding how many documents can be removed before utility degrades. In the downstream setting, the output is a single matrix B = Aw (the fine-tuned model) rather than A and w separately, and the base model A is left unmodified.

## Method

For pre-training unlearning (Algorithm 1), the method updates the co-occurrence matrix by subtracting forget-set documents, then performs a projected Newton step on each word's coefficient vector C_i (its convex-combination weights over anchor words) to approximate the retrained solution without full retraining; the updated A is reconstructed from C and the Gaussian mechanism is applied to certify (ε,δ)-indistinguishability, yielding a deletion capacity of Õ(m/(r²√(nr))). For downstream unlearning (Algorithm 2), the key insight is that any perturbation to A can be equivalently absorbed into the head w so that the product B = Aw is preserved, meaning the base model never needs modification. Because the ground-truth head w* is sparse (non-zero only on task-relevant topics T_clf), the sensitivity of B to deletions is reduced by a task-dependent factor q = min_{k∈T_clf} Pr[z=k], improving the deletion capacity to Õ(mq/(r√(nr)))—a strict improvement over pre-training whenever the task does not depend on every topic. The entire analysis relies on the p-separability (anchor word) assumption and the robust simplex geometry from Arora et al. (2012a,b).

## Results

The paper provides the first provable deletion capacity guarantees for unlearning in the pre-training and fine-tuning paradigm. For pre-training (Theorem 2), the unlearning algorithm can remove $\tilde{O}(m/(r^2\sqrt{nr}))$ documents while preserving utility, where $m$ is the corpus size, $r$ is the number of topics, and $n$ is the vocabulary size. For downstream task unlearning (Theorem 4), the deletion capacity improves to $\tilde{\Omega}(mq/(r\sqrt{nr}))$, where $q \in [1/r, 1]$ is a task-dependent sparsity parameter — simpler tasks (larger $q$) allow more data to be unlearned. Computationally, unlearning is substantially faster than retraining: the dominant cost drops from $\mathcal{O}(n^2 r + nr^2/\epsilon_0^2)$ (retraining) to $\mathcal{O}(nr^2)$ (unlearning) for topic recovery, and the co-occurrence update costs only $\mathcal{O}(m_U)$ versus $\mathcal{O}(m)$ for retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or dataset evaluations; the 'comparison' is between the proposed unlearning algorithms and full retraining in terms of both computational cost and deletion capacity. The key comparison is between the naive downstream setting (Theorem 3, releasing $A$ and $w$ separately) and the realistic setting (Theorem 4, releasing only $B = Aw$): the naive setting inherits the pre-training deletion capacity $\tilde{O}(m/(r^2\sqrt{nr}))$ and requires retraining the head for each unlearning request, while the realistic setting achieves a strictly larger deletion capacity $\tilde{\Omega}(mq/(r\sqrt{nr}))$ (improved by a factor of $q \cdot r$) and does not modify the base model $A$. The paper also contrasts its guarantees with prior work: Golatkar et al. (2020) provides considerably weaker guarantees on unlearning with respect to probes, while Sekhari et al. (2021) and other certified unlearning works (Guo et al. 2020, Neel et al. 2021) are limited to supervised or convex settings without the pre-training/fine-tuning decomposition. No empirical accuracy or utility numbers are reported, as the contribution is entirely in the form of deletion capacity bounds and runtime analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the (ε,δ)-unlearning framework of Sekhari et al. (2021) from single-stage supervised learning to the pre-training and fine-tuning paradigm, using topic models (Arora et al., 2012a,b) as a tractable testbed. The learning algorithm, anchor-word identification, and robust-simplex analysis are inherited directly from Arora et al. (2012a,b), while the unlearning mechanism adapts the Gaussian mechanism from differential privacy to the non-negative matrix factorization structure of topic models. The key departure from prior unlearning work is the introduction of 'utility-preserving unlearning with deletion capacity' (Definition 4), which couples indistinguishability with a performance-degradation bound, and the use of Newton-step updates to avoid full retraining. The paper also positions itself against Golatkar et al. (2020), noting that their guarantees on unlearning with respect to linear probes are considerably weaker than the task-specific guarantees provided here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable unlearning guarantees in the pre-train/fine-tune pipeline, a setting that mirrors how modern foundation models are actually deployed. The central conceptual finding—that unlearning pre-training data is easier from a fine-tuned model and can be done without modifying the base model—suggests a practical pathway for compliance: organizations can handle deletion requests at the task-head level rather than retraining expensive base models. The main limitation is that topic models are bag-of-words and cannot capture the autoregressive, compositional structure of modern LLMs, so extending these guarantees to transformer-based models remains a significant open problem. Additionally, the p-separability assumption (existence of anchor words) is restrictive and may not hold for real-world corpora, limiting immediate applicability.
