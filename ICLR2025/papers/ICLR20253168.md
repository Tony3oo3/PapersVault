## Problem

Machine unlearning for Vision Language Models under the Right to be Forgotten setting remains largely unexplored: prior VLM unlearning work targets privacy-unrelated concepts (e.g., 'Van Gogh') or classification tasks, and no standardized benchmark exists to evaluate whether VLMs can truly forget facial-identity-linked private information while preserving general visual capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253168/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 1024×1024 synthetic facial images from SFHQ paired with text questions about fictitious personal profiles (names, addresses, health records, criminal histories), producing text answers that are scored across two dimensions: model utility (ROUGE-L, GPT-Eval, Truth Ratio, MME/POPE accuracy on the retain set) and forget quality (Exact Match, KS-Test, Min-K% Prob membership inference, and adversarial privacy extraction via GPT-4o paraphrased questions on the forget set). The two-stage pipeline first fine-tunes a VLM on all 8,000 VQA pairs, then applies unlearning to a 5% forget subset while evaluating retention on the remaining 95%.

## Method

FIUBENCH formalizes VLM unlearning as forgetting image-paired private knowledge rather than visual attributes, then constructs a Fictitious Facial Identity VQA Dataset by selecting 400 diverse synthetic faces via CLIP→UMAP→K-means filtering, randomly pairing each with health/criminal/address records, and generating 20 GPT-4o VQA pairs per identity. The evaluation follows a learn-then-unlearn pipeline inspired by TOFU: Stage I fine-tunes LLaVA-Phi-3B or Llama-3.2-Vision-11B on the full VQA set, and Stage II applies four unlearning strategies (Gradient Ascent, Gradient Difference, KL Minimization, Preference Optimization) to the forget set. A key differentiator is the robust evaluation layer: beyond surface-level Exact Match, the benchmark deploys Min-K% Prob membership inference attacks and adversarial privacy extraction (scoring EM across three GPT-4o paraphrases per question) to detect whether private knowledge persists internally even when the model superficially refuses to answer. Ablations further examine which VLM components (vision encoder, projector, LLM) to fine-tune during unlearning and propose a Knowledge Unlearning variant that operates without exact VQA pairs.

## Results

Across both LLaVA-Phi-mini-3B and Llama-3.2-Vision-11B, no unlearning method achieves a satisfactory balance between forget quality and model utility. Gradient Ascent (GA) achieves the best forget quality (avg. ~93) but catastrophically degrades utility (avg. drops to 42.5 on LLaVA-Phi, 11.7 on Llama-3.2). Preference Optimization (PO) shows a critical divergence: its Exact Match score is very low (6.90 on LLaVA-Phi, 0.30 on Llama-3.2), suggesting surface-level forgetting, yet its MIA score remains high (64.2 and 42.3 respectively), revealing that private knowledge persists internally. The Retain Model (fine-tuned only on the retain set) serves as the ideal upper bound, achieving avg. utility of 88.6/85.3 and avg. forget quality of 93.3/93.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four existing LLM unlearning methods adapted to VLMs, finding that none achieves effective unlearning under the Right to be Forgotten setting. GA (Yao et al., 2023) achieves the highest forget quality (avg. 92.9/93.2) but at the cost of near-total utility destruction (avg. utility drops to 42.5/11.7 vs. 88.6/85.3 for the Retain Model). GD (Liu et al., 2022) offers a moderate trade-off, retaining ~61.6/48.4 avg. utility while achieving 80.7/86.9 forget quality. KL (Yao et al., 2024a) best preserves utility (78.2 on LLaVA-Phi) but fails to forget effectively (avg. forget quality only 36.8, with MIA at 42.1). PO (Maini et al., 2024) is the most revealing comparison: it achieves the lowest EM scores (6.90/0.30), suggesting the model refuses to answer, yet its MIA scores (64.2/42.3) are far above the Retain Model's (12.3/12.2), proving that private knowledge is merely suppressed rather than truly unlearned. This divergence between surface-level refusal and internal knowledge retention is the paper's central empirical finding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fictitious learn-then-unlearn paradigm of TOFU (Maini et al., 2024) from LLMs to Vision Language Models, adapting four LLM unlearning baselines (Gradient Ascent, Gradient Difference, KL Minimization, Preference Optimization) to the VLM setting. It departs from prior VLM unlearning work (MultiDelete, MMUBench) by targeting privacy-sensitive facial identity information under the Right to be Forgotten rather than generic concept removal, and by formalizing VLM unlearning as forgetting image-paired private knowledge while preserving visual attribute recognition. The two-stage pipeline with a controlled fictitious dataset and the inclusion of membership inference attacks and adversarial paraphrase extraction as robustness metrics represent a methodological advance over prior unlearning evaluations that relied solely on surface-level answer matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIUBENCH is the first standardized benchmark for VLM unlearning under the Right to be Forgotten, filling a gap that prevents systematic comparison of unlearning algorithms in the privacy-critical VLM setting. The key open question it surfaces is whether any current method can simultaneously achieve strong forget quality and preserve model utility; all four baselines fail to do so, with gradient-ascent methods sacrificing utility and alignment-based methods failing to truly remove knowledge. A significant limitation is that the benchmark simulates rare knowledge via fine-tuning on a fictitious dataset rather than addressing information acquired during pre-training or in-context learning, which limits direct transferability to real-world deployment scenarios. Future work must address whether unlearning can be made effective at scale without retraining, and whether the privacy-attack evaluation protocol generalizes beyond facial identity to other sensitive modalities.
