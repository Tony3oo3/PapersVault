## Problem

Applying diffusion models to offline cooperative MARL faces a fundamental tension: centralized diffusion models (e.g., MADIFF) capture cooperation but scale poorly with agent count, while independent per-agent diffusion models scale but fail to model inter-agent coordination. The widely used IGM principle, which underpins scalable value-based MARL, is inapplicable to diffusion-based planners that generate future outcomes rather than selecting greedy actions. A general design principle and factorization mechanism for diffusion-based MARL was missing.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives its local observation history τ_i and a scalar return value R as conditioning signal y_i, which guides the diffusion process toward high-return cooperative behaviors. The output is either a sequence of H future observations (DoF-Trajectory, from which actions are derived via a learned inverse dynamics model) or a continuous action vector (DoF-Policy). Critically, during decentralized execution each agent's input complexity is O(1) with respect to the number of agents N, in contrast to MADIFF's O(N), since each agent only conditions on its own observation rather than a noisy global observation vector.

## Method

DoF introduces the Individual-Global-identically-Distributed (IGD) principle, which requires that the product of N individual diffusion distributions equals the joint diffusion distribution, generalizing IGM to generative models. The framework trains a single centralized noise model ε_tot^θ_tot on joint data, then factorizes it via a noise factorization function f (Concat or WConcat) into N independent noise models ε_i^θ_i with disjoint parameters θ_i ⊂ θ_tot, each operating on O(1)-dimensional inputs. A data factorization function h (e.g., weighted concatenation or attention) is applied at the final denoising step to model residual inter-agent dependencies in the generated data. The paper proves that both Concat and WConcat satisfy IGD, and shows that MADIFF does not. Two agent variants are implemented: DoF-Trajectory (built on Decision Diffuser, generating observation sequences) and DoF-Policy (built on DiffusionQL, generating actions directly), both using classifier-free guidance with return R as the condition.

## Results

On the SMAC benchmark (Table 2), DoF achieves the highest average return across most of the 18 map-data combinations. For example, on 3m with Medium data, DoF scores 18.6±1.2 vs. MADIFF (16.4±2.6) and MADT (15.8±0.5); on 3m with Poor data, DoF scores 10.9±1.1 vs. MADIFF (10.3±6.1) and MADT (4.2±0.1). On the heterogeneous 3s5z_vs_3s6z map with Good data, DoF (12.8±0.8) substantially outperforms MADIFF (7.1±1.5). In the scalability experiment (Table 3), DoF successfully handles 64 agents (210.42 reward, 5924 MB GPU) while MADIFF encounters an out-of-memory error at 64 agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoF outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of SMAC map-data combinations. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor MADIFF, DoF wins on 16 out of 18 SMAC settings, with the largest margins on heterogeneous maps (e.g., 3s5z_vs_3s6z Good: 12.8 vs. 7.1) and low-quality data (e.g., 8m Poor: 12.0 vs. 9.8). Against the transformer-based MADT, DoF achieves comparable or better results on most maps, with a notable advantage on Poor-data settings (e.g., 3m Poor: 10.9 vs. 4.2; 8m Poor: 12.0 vs. 4.8). Value-based offline MARL methods (MABCQ, MACQL, MAICQ) are consistently outperformed, especially on Medium and Poor data where their value-function approximation errors compound. On MPE and MA-Mujoco (Appendix Tables 11 and 13), DoF also achieves the best or near-best returns. In the scalability experiment, DoF handles 64 agents with 5924 MB GPU memory and 24.3 s inference time, while MADIFF runs out of memory at 64 agents and requires 21862 MB at 32 agents.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoF extends the IGM principle (Son et al., 2019), originally formulated for value-based MARL, into the IGD principle for diffusion-based MARL, requiring that the product of individual diffusion distributions equals the joint diffusion distribution. Methodologically, it builds on Decision Diffuser (Ajay et al., 2023) for trajectory generation and DiffusionQL (Wang et al., 2023) for action generation, but departs from MADIFF (Zhu et al., 2024) by introducing noise and data factorization functions that decompose a single centralized diffusion model into N independent per-agent models, reducing per-agent input complexity from O(n) to O(1). The theoretical contribution—proving that simple Concat and WConcat factorizations satisfy IGD—provides the first principled design guideline for diffusion-based cooperative MARL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IGD principle fills a gap in the MARL design-principle literature by providing a diffusion-appropriate analogue to IGM, enabling principled factorization of centralized generative models into decentralized ones. The scalability advantage (DoF handles 64 agents where MADIFF hits OOM) is practically significant for large-scale cooperative systems. Open questions include whether more expressive factorization functions (e.g., attention-based) can satisfy IGD while remaining decentralized, whether the framework extends to non-cooperative or mixed-motive settings, and whether the O(1) per-agent complexity advantage persists when agents have heterogeneous observation spaces.
