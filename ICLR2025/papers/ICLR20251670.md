## Problem

While disentanglement and compositional generalization are both essential for learning composable abstractions, prior theoretical results enabling both simultaneously rely on overly restrictive assumptions (e.g., non-interacting objects or additive generators) that cannot model real-world concepts with non-trivial interactions. The paper asks: what general property of concepts, grounded in first principles, provably enables both disentanglement and compositional generalization for flexible generator functions?

## Input / Output

The model takes multi-object scene images (Sprites: 2–4 objects on black backgrounds; CLEVR6: 2–6 objects with reflections and shadows) and produces a set of K latent slot vectors (one per object) along with a full-image reconstruction. The encoder maps the image through a CNN backbone followed by a Transformer with self- and cross-attention, yielding a factorized posterior over slots; the decoder maps slots back to pixels via a cross-attention Transformer where each pixel position queries all slots. Evaluation uses Jacobian-based metrics (J-ARI, JIS) that assign pixels to slots by the L1 norm of slot-wise decoder Jacobians rather than attention scores.

## Method

The paper formalizes the interaction asymmetry principle—parts of the same concept have more complex interactions than parts of different concepts—as block-diagonality conditions on (n+1)-th order partial derivatives of the generator across slots, and proves this enables both disentanglement (Thm. 4.3) and compositional generalization (Thm. 4.4) for n ∈ {0,1,2}, unifying prior results as special cases. Practically, the theory motivates a Transformer-based VAE trained with a three-part loss: reconstruction, a KL term penalizing latent capacity, and a novel cross-attention interaction regularizer (L_interact) that minimizes pairwise products of attention weights A_{l,j}·A_{l,k} for j≠k, pushing each pixel to attend to only one slot. This regularizer exploits the fact that in a cross-attention decoder, inter-slot interactions are mediated entirely by attention weights, making it a scalable proxy for the theoretical constraint of at most n-th order cross-slot interactions without requiring intractable high-order derivative computations.

## Results

On Sprites, the regularized Transformer-VAE (α=0.05, β=0.05) achieves J-ARI of 93.7±0.6 and JIS of 95.0±1.7, outperforming the unregularized Transformer (80.5/57.0) and matching or exceeding Slot Attention + Spatial Broadcast Decoder (89.3/91.4). On CLEVR6, the method reaches J-ARI 96.5±0.4 and JIS 83.8±1.1, surpassing the unregularized Transformer (92.7/54.8) and Slot Attention + Transformer (95.5/63.1), though the SBD baseline achieves higher JIS (95.3) at the cost of failing to model inter-object reflections. The KL regularizer (β) is the dominant factor for disentanglement, with the interaction regularizer (α) providing additional gains on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Slot Attention + Spatial Broadcast Decoder (the strongest object-centric baseline), the proposed method wins on Sprites for both J-ARI (93.7 vs 89.3) and JIS (95.0 vs 91.4), but loses on CLEVR6 JIS (83.8 vs 95.3) and is marginally lower on J-ARI (96.5 vs 97.0). The paper argues this trade-off is favorable: the SBD's near-perfect JIS comes from its restricted architecture that cannot model reflections between objects, whereas the regularized Transformer captures these interactions while still achieving strong disentanglement. Against Slot Attention + Transformer decoder, the proposed method clearly wins on both datasets and both metrics (e.g., CLEVR6 JIS: 83.8 vs 63.1). Against the unregularized Transformer (same architecture, no regularizers), the gains are substantial, particularly on JIS (57.0→95.0 on Sprites; 54.8→83.8 on CLEVR6), confirming that both the KL and interaction regularizers are necessary for effective object disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends two prior theoretical results for provable object disentanglement and compositional generalization: Brady et al. (2023), which assumed non-interacting objects (n=0), and Lachapelle et al. (2023), which assumed additive decoders (n=1). The authors show both are special cases of a more general 'interaction asymmetry' principle formalized via block-diagonality of higher-order derivatives of the generator, and extend the analysis to n=2, permitting non-trivial cross-concept interactions. On the method side, the paper departs from the dominant object-centric learning paradigm (Slot Attention, Spatial Broadcast Decoders) that enforces disentanglement through architectural priors, instead proposing to regularize a flexible Transformer-VAE's cross-attention weights to approximate the theoretical constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interaction asymmetry principle provides a single, interpretable condition that simultaneously guarantees both disentanglement and compositional generalization, resolving a gap where prior results addressed only one or the other, or were restricted to non-interacting objects. However, the empirical validation is limited to synthetic datasets with 2–6 simple geometric objects (Sprites, CLEVR6), and the paper explicitly defers compositional generalization experiments and real-world data to future work. Open questions include whether the attention regularizer scales to natural images, whether the n≤2 theoretical bound is tight for real concepts, and whether the method can handle concept types beyond objects (attributes, events, object-parts).
