## Problem

Existing multimodal in-context learning (ICL) evaluation relies almost exclusively on VQA and image captioning, which the paper shows are poor proxies: models already perform well zero-shot on these tasks, and the apparent ICL gains largely reflect learning answer formatting rather than genuine multimodal reasoning. When LLM-based semantic evaluation replaces exact string matching, the ICL benefit over zero-shot nearly vanishes. The broader capabilities and limitations of multimodal ICL—rule induction, fine-grained perception, interleaved reasoning, long-context handling—remain largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

For image-to-text tasks, the model receives an interleaved sequence of few-shot support examples (image + question + answer pairs) followed by a query image and question, and must produce a text answer such as a synthetic class label, object count, OCR transcription, or arithmetic result. For text-to-image tasks, the input is a text prompt (e.g., a synthetic category name or attribute keyword) and the output is a generated image, evaluated by a VLLM judge. The benchmark is deliberately compact (1.82 GB total, 15,260 train / 1,760 test examples across 10 tasks) to be accessible without prohibitive compute, and includes text-only variants of several tasks to isolate the effect of multimodality.

## Method

VL-ICL Bench is a suite of 10 tasks spanning image-to-text and text-to-image generation, each designed to stress a specific ICL capability: fast concept binding (synthetic names via open-ended MiniImageNet), fine-grained perception and induction (CLEVR counting, TextOCR), rule induction and arithmetic reasoning (Operator Induction and its interleaved variant), relation learning over image pairs (Fast Matching MiniImageNet), and latent-variable induction in generation (CoBSAT, Fast Counting, Fast Attribute Matching). A key design choice is the use of artificial/synthetic labels to eliminate reliance on pre-trained world knowledge, forcing the model to genuinely learn the mapping from the support set. The authors evaluate 16 image-to-text VLLMs (0.5B–80B parameters, including GPT-4V) and 5 text-to-image models, using three summary metrics—zero-shot accuracy, peak accuracy, and ICL efficiency (area under the accuracy-vs-shots curve above the zero-shot baseline). Additional analyses disentangle context-length effects (via SelfExtend), compare image vs. text inputs, investigate emergent ICL thresholds across model sizes (0.5B/7B/72B LLaVA-OneVision), and test whether chain-of-thought prompting or similarity-based demonstration selection can close the gap.

## Results

On VL-ICL Bench's image-to-text tasks, LLaVA-OneVision-72B achieves the best average rank (3.8), with peak accuracies of 98.7% on Fast Open-Ended MiniImageNet, 75.6% on Operator Induction, and 70.3% on Fast Matching MiniImageNet, and an ICL efficiency of 68.4% on MiniImageNet. GPT4V ties for best average rank (3.8), reaching 92.0% peak on Operator Induction and 58.2% on Fast Matching MiniImageNet. For text-to-image tasks, SEED-LLaMA-14B achieves the best average rank (2.5), with peak accuracies of 21.2% (T2I MiniImageNet), 43.8% (CoBSAT), and 51.6% (Fast Counting). No model exceeds 50% peak accuracy on any text-to-image task, and several models (e.g., LLaVA-Next-7B, Otter) show negative ICL efficiency on multiple tasks, indicating performance degrades with more shots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 16 image-to-text VLLMs evaluated, LLaVA-OneVision-72B and GPT4V share the best average rank (3.8), but they excel on different tasks: LLaVA-OneVision-72B dominates on Fast Open-Ended MiniImageNet (98.7% peak vs. GPT4V's 78.0%) and Fast Matching MiniImageNet (70.3% vs. 58.2%), while GPT4V leads on Operator Induction (92.0% vs. 75.6%) and Interleaved Operator Induction (34.0% vs. 47.8% — actually LLaVA-OneVision wins here). Notably, zero-shot performance does not predict ICL ability: LLaVA-Next-7B, a strong zero-shot model on mainstream benchmarks, ranks near the bottom on VL-ICL Bench (avg. rank 13.2) due to its inability to handle multiple images and long token sequences. For text-to-image, SEED-LLaMA-14B (avg. rank 2.5) outperforms GILL (4.8) and Emu2-Gen (2.8) on CoBSAT (43.8% vs. 12.3% and 28.7% peak) and Fast Counting (51.6% vs. 34.2% and 59.2%), though Emu2-Gen edges ahead on T2I MiniImageNet (37.0% vs. 21.2%). A critical finding is that no model achieves uniform excellence: even the best models show negative ICL efficiency on at least one task (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision-72B has only 2.4% efficiency on TextOCR), and several models (LLaVA-Next-7B, Otter, IDEFICS2-8B) exhibit negative efficiency on multiple tasks, meaning more shots actively hurt performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the open-ended MiniImageNet ICL formulation from Tsimpoukelli et al. (2021) and the CoBSAT text-to-image ICL benchmark from Zeng et al. (2024), but departs sharply from the dominant practice of evaluating VLLM ICL via few-shot VQA and captioning. The authors demonstrate that this common evaluation paradigm measures answer-format learning rather than genuine multimodal inference, and instead adopt the ICL community's protocol (Dong et al., 2024; Min et al., 2022) to construct a ten-task suite spanning fast binding, rule induction, fine-grained perception, interleaved reasoning, and long-context challenges. The work also contrasts with trained visual ICL models (Bar et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023), which learn in-context behavior during training rather than exhibiting it emergently from a frozen LLM backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VL-ICL Bench provides the first comprehensive, capability-diverse evaluation of multimodal ICL, revealing that even GPT-4V and LLaVA-OneVision-72B struggle on several tasks and that zero-shot benchmark performance is a poor predictor of ICL ability. Key open questions include how to better expose the underlying LLM's ICL capacity to the multimodal setting, how to handle the context-length bottleneck that limits shot scaling, and whether the emergent size threshold observed at 72B is architecture-specific. A notable limitation is the small number of text-to-image models evaluated (only five), reflecting the immaturity of that model class. The benchmark is compact (1.82 GB total) and designed to be widely adopted as the field's standard for multimodal ICL assessment.
