## Problem

While almost sure convergence of stochastic gradient descent is well understood, the analogous theory for zeroth-order (derivative-free) optimization methods remains largely undeveloped. The Stochastic Three Points (STP) algorithm, a simple direct-search method with stochastic directions, had only convergence-in-expectation guarantees from its introduction by Bergou et al. (2020), leaving open whether individual trajectories converge with probability one and at what rate. This paper provides the first almost sure convergence results for STP across smooth, smooth convex, and smooth strongly convex function classes.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only a function evaluation oracle (no gradient access), an initial point θ¹ ∈ ℝᵈ, a step size sequence {α_t}, and a probability distribution 𝒟 over ℝᵈ for random directions. At each iteration it evaluates f at exactly three points (θᵗ, θᵗ + α_t s_t, θᵗ − α_t s_t) and moves to the best. The output is a sequence of iterates whose gradient norm and function value gap converge to zero, with explicit rates: o(T^{-(1/2−ε)}) for the best gradient iterate on smooth functions, O(d/T) in expectation for the function gap on convex functions, and geometric O((1−μ/(2πdL))^T) for strongly convex functions.

## Method

The STP algorithm is a direct-search method: at each step it draws a random direction s_t ~ 𝒟, evaluates f at three candidate points, and selects the minimizer among them. The core technical contribution is a convergence framework built on a conditional descent inequality (E[f(θ^{t+1})|θᵗ] ≤ f(θᵗ) − μ_𝒟 α_t ‖∇f(θᵗ)‖_𝒟 + Lα_t²/2) combined with two key lemmas: one showing that summability of α_t·‖∇f(θᵗ)‖ implies the best iterate converges at rate o(1/Σα_t), and a Riemann-series-type lemma (Lemma 7) establishing that the final gradient norm vanishes almost surely under the standard Robbins-Monro conditions (Σα_t² < ∞, Σα_t = ∞). For the strongly convex case, the step sizes are made adaptive by approximating directional derivatives via |f(θᵗ + h^{-t}s_t) − f(θᵗ)|/(Lh^{-t}), and the Polyak-Lojasiewicz inequality converts the gradient bound into a geometric contraction of the function gap, yielding the first almost sure geometric convergence rate for a zeroth-order method.

## Results

For smooth (non-convex) functions, the best gradient iterate of STP converges almost surely to zero at rate o(1/T^{1/2−ε}) for any ε∈(0,1/2), and the final gradient iterate converges to zero both a.s. and in expectation. For smooth convex functions, f(θ^T)−f(θ*) converges in expectation at rate O(d/T) and almost surely at rate o(1/T^{1−ε}) for any ε∈(0,1). For smooth strongly convex functions, the function value gap converges in expectation at rate O((1−μ/(2πdL))^T) and almost surely at rate o((1−sμ/(2πdL))^T) for any s∈(0,1), where μ and L are the strong convexity and smoothness parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the numerical experiments (Section 6, d=500), STP is compared against RGF (Nesterov & Spokoiny, 2017) and GLD (Golovin et al., 2020) on a smooth non-convex quadratic objective. STP and RGF show nearly identical convergence of the last gradient norm, with STP being slightly better in both the number of iterations and wall-clock time to reach a given accuracy, consistent with their matching theoretical complexity bounds. STP clearly outperforms GLD on both metrics, and this advantage is amplified by the fact that STP and RGF require only 2 function evaluations per iteration, whereas GLD requires ⌈log₂(R/r)⌉ evaluations per iteration. Theoretically, STP matches RGF's complexity across all three function classes (smooth, convex, strongly convex) in terms of both accuracy ε and dimensionality d, while achieving a linear (rather than quadratic) dependence on d compared to deterministic direct search methods. In the strongly convex case, GLD's complexity is higher than STP's by a factor of log(d) when the monotone transform is the identity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of the Stochastic Three Points (STP) algorithm introduced by Bergou et al. (2020), who provided only in-expectation convergence guarantees for smooth, smooth convex, and smooth strongly convex functions. The authors draw on the probabilistic descent framework of Gratton et al. (2015) for direct search methods and the almost sure convergence techniques developed for SGD (Bertsekas & Tsitsiklis, 2000; Sebbouh et al., 2021; Liu & Yuan, 2022), adapting them to the zeroth-order setting. A key departure from prior work is the establishment of almost sure convergence for the *last* gradient iterate (not just the best iterate) under only smoothness and boundedness, and the first almost sure convergence *rates* for any zeroth-order method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Almost sure convergence guarantees that every single trajectory of the algorithm converges with probability one, which is essential for understanding the behavior of individual runs in practical applications like adversarial attacks, reinforcement learning, and hyperparameter tuning where one cannot average over many runs. The paper also resolves an open question by showing the last iterate converges (both a.s. and in expectation) without requiring convexity or additional structural assumptions. Open questions include whether analogous almost sure results extend to the stochastic (noisy function evaluation) setting, to constrained problems, or to other zeroth-order methods like RGF or GLD, and whether the gap between a.s. and in-expectation rates can be closed entirely (i.e., achieving O rather than o rates almost surely).
