## Problem

Large multimodal models consistently allocate high attention weights to specific visual tokens that are irrelevant to the corresponding text token, yet the underlying cause and significance of this behavior remained unexplored. The paper asks what property drives these irrelevant visual tokens to appear in fixed locations across all text tokens, and whether the attention wasted on them can be repurposed to address the well-documented under-attention to images in LMMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253163/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VAR operates entirely within the transformer decoder's attention mechanism during standard inference, taking the same image-text pairs and producing the same text responses as the base LMM without altering input format, adding inference steps, or requiring a second model. The only model-specific requirement is identifying the sink dimensions (e.g., {1415, 2533} for LLaMA-2-based models, {458, 2570} for Qwen2-VL), which are inherited from the base language model and remain unchanged after multimodal fine-tuning. Three hyperparameters (τ=20, p=0.6, and task-specific ρ) control sink token identification, redistribution fraction, and head selection threshold respectively.

## Method

The paper discovers that irrelevant visual tokens exhibit massive activation in specific hidden state dimensions (sink dimensions) identical to those of text sink tokens like BOS, confirming they are visual analogues of the attention sink phenomenon in language models. Attention knockout experiments validate that masking these visual sink tokens causes negligible performance drop, establishing them as surplus attention that can be recycled. VAR then performs two steps: (1) selecting image-centric heads by computing the visual non-sink ratio (proportion of attention to non-sink visual tokens) and retaining heads above threshold ρ, and (2) redistributing a fraction p of sink token attention to visual non-sink tokens proportionally to their existing attention weights, preserving the softmax normalization. The method is training-free, requires no additional models or inference steps, and is applied across six LMMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B/HD-13B, VILA-13B, Qwen2-VL-7B, InternVL2-8B) with consistent improvements on general vision-language, hallucination, and vision-centric benchmarks.

## Results

VAR consistently improves all six evaluated LMMs across 16 benchmarks spanning general vision-language, visual hallucination, and vision-centric tasks, without any additional training or inference steps. On the general vision-language suite (Table 1), LLaVA-1.5-7B gains +18.3 on MME (1495.5→1513.8), +1.5 on GQA (62.0→63.5), and +2.6 on MM-Vet (31.1→33.7). Notably, LLaVA-1.5-7B+VAR surpasses the larger vanilla LLaVA-1.5-13B on GQA (63.5 vs 63.3), VizWiz (53.7 vs 53.6), MME (1513.8 vs 1501.2), and POPE F1 (86.53 vs 85.90). On vision-centric tasks, MMVP scores improve substantially, e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B jumps from 3.33 to 9.33 and LLaVA-1.5-HD-13B from 36.0 to 39.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates VAR as a plug-and-play enhancement applied to five base LMMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B/HD-13B, VILA-13B, Qwen2-VL-7B, InternVL2-8B), comparing each model against its own VAR-enhanced version. VAR wins on every benchmark for every base model, with the largest relative gains on vision-centric tasks (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B MMVP: 3.33→9.33, a 180% improvement) and hallucination metrics (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B CHAIR_S: 45.0→43.2, POPE F1: 85.90→86.53). A particularly striking cross-model comparison shows that the smaller LLaVA-1.5-7B+VAR outperforms the larger vanilla LLaVA-1.5-13B on GQA (63.5 vs 63.3), VizWiz (53.7 vs 53.6), MME (1513.8 vs 1501.2), and POPE F1 (86.53 vs 85.90), suggesting attention redistribution can partially compensate for model scale. In the VCD case study (Table 7), VAR stacked on top of Visual Contrastive Decoding further improves POPE (87.3→87.6), MME (1580.7→1597.2), and GQA (66.2→67.1), confirming complementarity with existing hallucination-mitigation methods. The ablation in Table 4 shows that applying redistribution to all heads (without image-centric head selection) collapses performance to 0.0, underscoring that head selection is essential rather than optional.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention sink phenomenon from language models (Xiao et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2024a) to the visual modality in large multimodal models, showing that background visual tokens exhibit the same massive-activation-in-fixed-dimensions pattern as text sink tokens like BOS. It builds on Yu et al. (2024) for the proportional attention redistribution formula, and addresses the well-documented problem of insufficient visual attention in LMMs (Chen et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024d) with a training-free, single-model approach that contrasts with visual contrastive decoding (Leng et al., 2024), which requires two models and extra inference steps. The key conceptual departure is identifying that the 'irrelevant' visual tokens receiving high attention are not a bug in the visual encoder but an inherited property of the base LLM's attention sink mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that visual sink tokens (mostly in background regions) can be entirely masked without performance degradation provides a clean mechanistic explanation for a previously puzzling attention pattern, and the surplus attention budget they occupy offers a free resource for improving visual grounding. VAR's training-free, plug-and-play nature makes it immediately applicable to deployed LMMs, and its compatibility with existing methods like VCD suggests broad utility. Open questions remain about how visual tokens are selected as sink tokens during multimodal training, whether the phenomenon generalizes to video or 3D inputs, and whether the raw attention maps used here could be further refined with object-level grounding to yield larger gains.
