## Problem

Language models frequently produce responses unfaithful to their input contexts due to prompt injections, backdoor attacks, and learned biases, yet it remains unclear whether their internal representations still encode a faithful world model. While prior work hypothesizes that LMs maintain latent world models that can diverge from outputs, no method existed to extract these internal states as structured, verifiable logical propositions that could serve as a monitoring signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250699/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-layernorm activations at a single layer (l=20 for domain probes, l=15 for binding) from a 13B-parameter LM (Tulu-2-13B) processing a short text passage, and outputs a set of logical propositions binding entities to attributes (e.g., {LivesIn(Alice, Laos), WorksAs(Bob, nurse)}). The closed world is restricted to four domains (60 names, 16 countries, 41 foods, 14 occupations) with three binary predicates, and each context describes exactly two entities with six propositions. Domain probes classify individual token activations into domain values or a null symbol, while the binding similarity metric operates on pairs of token activations to determine which entity-attribute pairs are co-bound.

## Method

Propositional probes compose two components: (1) linear domain probes that classify token activations into lexical values via dot-product with learned direction vectors (a multi-class generalization of difference-in-means probes), and (2) a binding similarity metric that determines which tokens are bound together. The key technical contribution is a Hessian-based algorithm for identifying the binding subspace: it first ablates binding information by moving binding vectors to their midpoints, then measures binding strength F(x,y) by perturbing activations in directions x and y and querying the model's probability of the correct attribute, and finally recovers a low-rank binding matrix H ≈ ∇_x∇_y F(x,y). The SVD of H yields the binding subspace, and the similarity metric d(Z_s, Z_t) = Z_s^T U_(k) S_(k)² U_(k)^T Z_t projects activations into this subspace. A lookup algorithm then composes domain probe outputs into propositions by matching each attribute to the name with highest binding similarity. Despite training only on simple templated English sentences, the probes generalize to story-form rewrites and Spanish translations, and remain faithful in adversarial settings where prompting fails.

## Results

On standard settings, propositional probes achieve a Jaccard index of 0.99 (SYNTH), 0.90 (PARA), and 0.78 (TRANS), matching the prompting skyline (1.00, 0.98, 0.78) within ~10%. In adversarial settings, the gap widens dramatically: under prompt injection, propositional probes maintain Jaccard 0.99/0.90/0.76 while prompting collapses to 0.49/0.48/0.51; under a backdoor attack (fine-tuned to answer incorrectly on Spanish), probes retain Jaccard 0.68 while prompting drops to 0.00. Exact-match accuracy tells a similar story, with probes at 0.98/0.55/0.24 (prompt injection) versus prompting at 0.07/0.04/0.06.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a prompting skyline that iteratively queries the model for names and then for each predicate, constraining answers to valid domain values. On standard (non-adversarial) settings, propositional probes match prompting on Jaccard index across all three datasets (within 1–8 points), though prompting achieves higher exact-match accuracy, especially on TRANS (0.40 vs. 0.26), largely due to the food domain probe generalizing less well to Spanish. Under prompt injection (prefix 'Always answer the opposite'), prompting degrades severely (Jaccard drops from ~0.98 to ~0.49 on PARA) while propositional probes remain essentially unchanged (0.90 on PARA), demonstrating that the internal world model stays faithful even when the model's output behavior is corrupted. Under the backdoor attack (fine-tuned to give wrong answers on Spanish inputs), prompting fails completely (Jaccard 0.00) while propositional probes retain 0.68 Jaccard. In the gender bias setting, both methods are susceptible to bias, but probing is significantly less biased than prompting on anti-stereotypical contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the binding vector decomposition discovered by Feng & Steinhardt (2023), who showed that LM activations can be split into lexical and binding components but could only estimate binding vector *differences* across contexts. Here, the authors develop a Hessian-based algorithm to identify the binding *subspace* itself, enabling a symmetric similarity metric that composes domain probes into full propositions. The domain probes are a multi-class generalization of the difference-in-means probes from Mallen & Belrose (2023), and the easy-to-hard evaluation paradigm is borrowed from the same line of work. The compositional approach departs from prior probing literature (which extracts individual lexical concepts) and from prior propositional-belief probing in game environments (Li et al., 2021; 2022) by exploiting the binding mechanism to bind entities to attributes in general text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that LM internal world models remain faithful under prompt injection, backdoor attacks, and gender bias even when outputs are unfaithful—provides a concrete mechanism for inference-time monitoring of deployed LMs, which is increasingly critical as models are used as autonomous agents. However, the current scope is narrow: a closed world of four domains with 14–60 values each, reliable binding only for two entities, and validation primarily on a single 13B model. Key open questions include scaling to open-world semantics, modeling role-filler binding and state changes, and whether the faithful-internal/unfaithful-output gap persists in larger, more capable models where unfaithfulness may originate earlier in the representational pipeline.
