## Problem

While sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models have been empirically shown to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in continual learning, no theoretical analysis exists to explain how experts specialize, how the router learns correct task-to-expert assignments, or how MoE affects forgetting and generalization. A critical open question is whether the gating network in CL requires different training dynamics than in standard single-task MoE, and what the explicit performance bounds are compared to a single-expert baseline.

## Input / Output

Each training round presents a task drawn from a pool of N ground-truth linear models, with a dataset consisting of one feature-signal vector (identifying the task) plus s−1 Gaussian noise samples in an overparameterized regime (s < d). The MoE produces a routing decision (top-1 expert index), updates the selected expert's linear weights via minimum-norm GD, and outputs forgetting and overall generalization error metrics. For DNN validation, the same algorithm is applied to image classification on CIFAR-10, MNIST, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet, where the gating network input is a normalized feature vector derived from the task's class identity.

## Method

The paper designs a sparsely-gated MoE with M linear experts, a linear gating network, and top-1 switch routing augmented with uniform random perturbations for exploration. Two key training designs distinguish it from prior MoE work: (1) a novel locality loss that penalizes routing a task to an expert whose model has changed substantially, encouraging experts to specialize in clusters of similar tasks; and (2) an early-termination mechanism that permanently stops updating the gating network once all experts' gate outputs have converged within a threshold, preserving the output gaps needed for correct routing. Theoretically, the paper proves that after O(M) exploration rounds, experts stabilize into task-specific sets and the router selects the correct expert with balanced loads, and it derives explicit upper bounds on expected forgetting and generalization error showing MoE outperforms a single expert—especially when task distributions differ substantially. A central finding is that unlike single-task MoE, continual task arrival necessitates terminating gating network updates; otherwise the router's output gaps collapse and it begins selecting wrong experts, causing oscillatory performance degradation.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, the MoE model with early termination (ET) achieves 32.9% average incremental accuracy with M=10 experts, compared to 17.1% for the single-expert baseline (M=1), a 15.8-point improvement. On Tiny ImageNet, M=8 experts with ET reaches 37.6% versus 17.4% for the single expert. Critically, removing early termination causes performance to collapse below the single-expert baseline (e.g., 9.1% vs. 17.1% on CIFAR-100 with M=10), confirming the theoretical necessity of terminating gating network updates. The paper also shows that adding more experts (M=10 vs. M=8) does not always help: on Tiny ImageNet, M=8 (37.6%) outperforms M=10 (28.3%), consistent with the theory that extra experts delay convergence without improving final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the single-expert continual learning model (M=1) from Lin et al. (2023), which provides the theoretical and empirical reference for forgetting and generalization error in overparameterized linear regression CL. The MoE model with early termination consistently outperforms the single expert across all real datasets: on CIFAR-100, M=10 with ET achieves 32.9% vs. 17.1% for M=1 (+15.8 pp); on Tiny ImageNet, M=8 with ET achieves 37.6% vs. 17.4% (+20.2 pp). However, the comparison is nuanced: without early termination, the MoE model actually underperforms the single expert (e.g., 9.1% vs. 17.1% on CIFAR-100), demonstrating that the gating network's continual update destroys expert specialization and causes the router to select wrong experts. The paper further shows that increasing M beyond the number of task clusters (K) does not improve performance (M=10 < M=8 on Tiny ImageNet), a finding absent from prior single-expert CL theory. The theoretical contribution is that MoE with ET drives expected forgetting to zero as T→∞, whereas the single-expert baseline exhibits persistent oscillatory forgetting proportional to inter-task model gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate theoretical threads: the overparameterized linear regression framework for CL (Evron et al. 2022; Lin et al. 2023), which characterized forgetting for a single expert, and the sparsely-gated MoE architecture (Shazeer et al. 2016; Fedus et al. 2022; Chen et al. 2022), which had been applied empirically to CL (Hihn & Braun 2021; Wang et al. 2022; Doan et al. 2023; Rypeść et al. 2023) without any theoretical grounding. The paper departs from prior MoE theory (Chen et al. 2022; Li et al. 2024) by introducing a novel locality loss for the gating network and, critically, an early-termination mechanism that is unnecessary in single-task MoE but essential under sequential task arrival. It also extends the single-expert CL theory of Lin et al. (2023) to the multi-expert setting, deriving explicit forgetting and generalization bounds for both M > N and M < N regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical characterization of how and why MoE mitigates catastrophic forgetting in CL, providing practitioners with principled guidance on expert count, gating network training duration, and load-balancing design. The finding that the gating network must be frozen after a finite exploration phase (T₁ = O(M/η)) is a non-obvious design constraint absent from all prior MoE-in-CL work, and the counterintuitive result that adding more experts delays convergence without improving performance challenges the common 'more capacity is better' heuristic. Key limitations include the restriction to overparameterized linear models and top-1 routing; extending the theory to top-k routing, non-linear experts, and task-free CL settings remains open. The DNN experiments on CIFAR-10, MNIST, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet provide encouraging but preliminary evidence that the linear-model insights transfer to deep networks.
