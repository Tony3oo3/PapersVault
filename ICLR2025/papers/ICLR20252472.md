## Problem

Existing chain-of-thought (CoT) approaches to reasoning only achieve interpolation within the training length range, failing to extrapolate to longer problem instances (length generalization, LG). The paper identifies the root cause via a theorem showing that infinitely many higher-dimensional functions can reproduce the observed behavior on shorter sequences, making the correct longer-length function fundamentally underdetermined without additional structural bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252472/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single CoT step input S^t as a token sequence and predicts the next step S^{t+1}, iterating until an EOS token is emitted. For (n,r)-consistent schemes, tokens are multi-dimensional (e.g., 11 dimensions for multiplication, 12 for division), where each dimension carries an indicator tag (I, J, K, F, T, etc.) that marks which positions participate in the current computation, and blank/empty tokens are padded to ensure every position can serve as a context center.

## Method

The core contribution is the (n,r)-consistency condition: a problem is (n,r)-consistent if every output position is determined by a consistent context of at most n r-length intervals, meaning the same context always yields the same output regardless of sequence length or position. The paper proves that an n-layer Transformer with alternating local attention (padding mask restricted to r-length intervals, relative position encoding) and global attention (no mask, no position encoding) can extract and match these contexts to achieve perfect LG. The CoT schemes are designed with indicator tags that implicitly encode the computation rules, creating unambiguous local contexts. The proof constructs the model using lookup-table and copy lemmas from Feng et al. (2023), showing the feed-forward layers implement context-to-output mappings while attention layers assemble multi-interval contexts across positions.

## Results

For (n,r)-consistent CoT schemes, the Transformer achieves 100% accuracy on all standard LG test sets: arithmetic in F₇ (up to L∈[3,100)), parity (up to L∈[1,1000)), addition-[2] (up to L∈[1,21)), multiplication-[11] (up to L∈[1,11)), and division-[12] (up to L∈[1,11)). In stress tests with much longer sequences, performance degrades gradually: e.g., multiplication-[11] drops from 100% to 98.9% (L∈[1,16)), 70.0% (L∈[1,21)), and 61.4% (L∈[1,26)), while parity maintains 100% even at L∈[1,1000)). Non-consistent CoT schemes (addition-[1], multiplication-[1]) fail to generalize, confirming the theoretical prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly does not compare against external baselines, stating that 'no existing reported system can solve multiplication and no reported system has even attempted division.' The key internal comparison is between (n,r)-consistent and non-consistent CoT schemes for the same problem: addition-[1] (not (n,r)-consistent) fails to achieve LG, while addition-[2] ((3,3)-consistent) reaches 100% accuracy across all standard test lengths. Similarly, multiplication-[1] (not (n,r)-consistent) shows poor LG accuracy, while multiplication-[11] ((12,3)-consistent) achieves 100% on all standard tests. This directly validates the theoretical claim that (n,r)-consistency is the determining factor for LG success. The authors note that prior work (Duan & Shi 2023, Zhou et al. 2024, Jelassi et al. 2023, He et al. 2024) using position embedding modifications cannot solve addition perfectly or multiplication at all, positioning this work as the first to theoretically guarantee and empirically demonstrate perfect LG for multiplication and division.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CoT-based learnability theory of Feng et al. (2023), Wies et al. (2023), and Malach (2023), which proved that neural networks can learn reasoning from chain-of-thought data but only for fixed-length instances (interpolation). The key departure is addressing length generalization (extrapolation beyond training length), which those results explicitly do not cover. The paper also positions itself against empirical LG approaches (Duan & Shi 2023, Zhou et al. 2024, Jelassi et al. 2023, He et al. 2024) that modify position encodings or attention biases, showing via Theorem 3.1 that such architectural changes are fundamentally insufficient because infinitely many higher-dimensional continuations match any lower-dimensional function. The proof of the main LG guarantee (Theorem 3.6) directly borrows the LOOKUP and COPY lemmas from Feng et al. (2023) to construct the n-layer Transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical framework specifically for length generalization in reasoning, providing a concrete sufficient condition ((n,r)-consistency) and a design principle (use tags to create unambiguous local contexts) for CoT schemes that guarantee LG with Transformers. The empirical achievement of 100% LG on multiplication and division—tasks no prior method has solved—validates the theory. However, significant limitations remain: (n,r)-consistency is sufficient but not necessary, CoT scheme design is currently manual and problem-specific, and performance degrades at extreme lengths due to attention dilution (Table 3 shows drops to 61–77% at the longest test settings). Open questions include automating CoT scheme design, characterizing necessary conditions, and whether the approach extends to more complex or open-ended reasoning tasks.
