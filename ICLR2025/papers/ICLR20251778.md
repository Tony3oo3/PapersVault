## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods suffer from two fundamental issues: untargeted unlearning (e.g., gradient ascent, NPO) attempts to approximate an unpredictable retain-model behavior that may itself involve hallucinations, while targeted unlearning (e.g., IDK fine-tuning, DPO) lacks sufficient regularization to prevent the model from becoming overly ignorant on the retain set. Additionally, prior work relies almost exclusively on ROUGE for evaluation, missing critical dimensions like token diversity, semantic drift, and factual correctness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (Llama2-chat-7B or Llama-3-8B-Instruct), a forget set of question-answer pairs to be unlearned, a retain set (neighbor set) for regularization, and 100 rejection templates for targeted unlearning. It produces modified LLM parameters via fine-tuning. Notably, the ME loss is computed over both the question and answer portions of each forget-set sample (unlike prior work that masks the question), and the AP loss operates on the ratio of rejection-template probability to original-answer probability on the retain set, requiring no reference model.

## Method

For untargeted unlearning, the paper proposes ME+GD, which minimizes the KL divergence between each predicted next-token distribution and a uniform distribution over the vocabulary (equivalent to maximizing prediction entropy), combined with standard gradient descent on the retain set. This aligns the model's behavior on the forget set with that of a randomly initialized model, avoiding the unpredictable and potentially hallucinating target of gradient ascent. For targeted unlearning, the paper proposes IDK+AP, which pairs IDK fine-tuning (relabeling forget-set questions with rejection templates) with a novel Answer Preservation (AP) loss. The AP loss is a preference-optimization-style regularizer that simultaneously pushes down the probability of rejection templates and maintains the probability of original answers on the retain set, with an adaptive gradient weight that strengthens regularization as the model drifts toward ignorance. The paper also introduces three new evaluation metrics—Token Entropy, Cosine Similarity, and Entailment Score—aggregated into Model Utility and Forget Efficacy scores.

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark, ME+GD achieves the best average MU/FE across all three tasks (0.824, 0.839, 0.842 for forget01/05/10), substantially outperforming GA+GD (0.630/0.439/0.747) and NPO+GD (0.626/0.619/0.666). In the real-world unlearning scenario with Llama3, ME+GD achieves MU=0.490 and FE=0.931, the only method maintaining both high utility and efficacy simultaneously, while all baselines drop MU below 0.25. IDK+AP achieves the best MU (0.531) among targeted methods in the real-world setting, compared to 0.000 for IDK+GD and DPO+GD. On downstream tasks, ME+GD preserves the highest average performance (0.529) versus 0.368–0.452 for baselines, with particularly large gaps on TriviaQA (0.485 vs. 0.000–0.248).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NPO (Zhang et al., 2024a), ME+GD dominates on both MU and FE across all TOFU tasks (e.g., MU 0.747 vs. 0.547 on forget05) and in the real-world setting (MU 0.490 vs. 0.214), while also preserving downstream task performance far better (avg. 0.529 vs. 0.391). Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024), IDK+AP achieves MU=0.531 versus 0.000 for DPO+GD/KL in the real-world scenario, showing that DPO's preference optimization causes the model to become completely ignorant on the neighbor set. Against the IDK fine-tuning baseline from TOFU (Maini et al., 2024), IDK+AP dramatically improves MU from 0.000 to 0.531 in the real-world setting and from 0.398 to 0.750 on TOFU forget05, confirming that standard GD regularization is insufficient to prevent excessive ignorance. Notably, NPO-based methods fail catastrophically in continual unlearning (MU drops to zero within a few subtasks) due to reference model drift, whereas ME+GD and IDK+AP maintain stable MU throughout.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a critical re-examination of the LLM unlearning landscape established by TOFU (Maini et al., 2024) and NPO (Zhang et al., 2024a), rather than building on a single prior method. It departs from the dominant 'reverse gradient descent' intuition for untargeted unlearning by arguing that the target behavior (a retain model) is fundamentally unpredictable and hallucination-prone, and instead proposes aligning the unlearned model with a randomly initialized model via entropy maximization. For targeted unlearning, it identifies a specific gap in existing regularization (GD, KL) that fails to counteract the increase in rejection-template probability on the retain set, and introduces an Answer Preservation loss inspired by preference optimization but repurposed as a regularizer. The three new evaluation metrics (token entropy, cosine similarity, entailment score) extend the ROUGE/P/TR suite that prior work relies on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hallucination finding (74% of surrogate retain model outputs judged as hallucinations) raises a practical legal risk that prior unlearning work has overlooked: even 'successful' unlearning may produce plausible-but-wrong answers that could be mistaken for leaked information. The NPO failure in continual unlearning due to reference-model drift is a practically important warning for iterative unlearning pipelines. Open questions remain around how to expand the retain set when it shrinks dramatically in continual scenarios, whether the ME objective generalizes beyond the controlled TOFU-style setting to truly open-ended knowledge, and whether the AP loss's adaptive weighting can be made more robust when the retain set is very small relative to the forget set.
