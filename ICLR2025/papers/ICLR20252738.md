## Problem

ADMM-based and Variational Bayes-based federated learning methods have been developed in parallel for decades but were believed to be fundamentally disconnected: ADMM uses dual variables for synchronization while VB uses posterior distributions with no notion of duality. This paper identifies a precise mathematical bridge showing that ADMM dual variables naturally emerge from VB site parameters, enabling the combination of their complementary strengths.

## Input / Output

Each client k holds local data D_k (tabular rows or RGB images) and a shared model architecture; the server receives dual-variable vectors v_k (and optionally diagonal precision matrices V_k for FedLap-Cov) plus, for FedLap-Func, soft-label predictions over a small set of shared inputs M_k (one point per class, C×C communication cost). The server outputs global model parameters w_g, which are broadcast back to clients. Communication cost is P parameters per round for FedLap, 2P for FedLap-Cov, and P+C² for FedLap-Func, where P is model size and C is the number of classes.

## Method

The key insight is that in Partitioned Variational Inference (PVI) with isotropic Gaussian approximations, the site parameter t̂_k(w) takes the form exp(δv_k^T w), which is exactly the linear dual term in ADMM, yielding a line-by-line correspondence between PVI and ADMM updates (FedLap). Extending to full-covariance Gaussians introduces a second dual variable V_k (a symmetric matrix) that acts as a Newton-like preconditioner in the global update, producing FedLap-Cov where the Euclidean proximal term becomes a Mahalanobis distance and the server update is preconditioned by S_g = δI + ΣV_k. A third variant, FedLap-Func, augments the server objective with function-space losses over a small set of shared inputs (soft labels from clients), improving gradient reconstruction without requiring full data sharing. All three variants are derived by choosing different candidate distribution families Q in PVI and applying Laplace (delta) approximations to decouple mean and covariance updates.

## Results

Across five datasets (UCI Credit, FLamby-Heart, MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10) with both homogeneous and heterogeneous splits, FedLap matches or exceeds FedDyn (the best ADMM baseline) in all settings. FedLap-Cov, which adds learned diagonal covariances as preconditioners, improves over FedDyn by 1.7%–5.9% accuracy on four of five dataset settings (e.g., 80.5% vs. 74.6% on heterogeneous Fashion-MNIST at 50 rounds; 85.2% vs. 83.5% on UCI Credit at 50 rounds). FedLap-Func, which adds function-space soft-label information, further improves early convergence, reaching 76.0% on CIFAR-10 at 10 rounds vs. 74.8% for FedLap. All three proposed methods consistently outperform FedAvg and FedProx.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAvg and FedProx are consistently the weakest baselines across all datasets and heterogeneity levels, trailing FedDyn by 2–10% in most settings. FedLap matches or slightly exceeds FedDyn in every configuration (e.g., 83.5% vs. 83.5% on UCI Credit at 50 rounds; 98.2% vs. 98.0% on heterogeneous MNIST), while requiring one fewer hyperparameter (no local weight-decay). FedLap-Cov clearly outperforms FedDyn on four of five dataset settings, with the largest gap on heterogeneous Fashion-MNIST (80.5% vs. 74.6%, a 5.9% gain at 50 rounds) and UCI Credit (85.2% vs. 83.5%); it is marginally worse only on CIFAR-10 (79.2% vs. 79.4%, within standard deviation). FedLap-Func shows the most pronounced gains in early training rounds, reaching 76.0% on CIFAR-10 at 10 rounds versus 74.8% for FedLap and 72.7% for FedDyn, and 78.1% on heterogeneous FMNIST at 50 rounds versus 77.6% for FedLap. The paper also notes that FedDyn's performance is highly sensitive to its additional weight-decay hyperparameter, whereas FedLap is robust to order-of-magnitude changes in its δ parameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two federated learning paradigms that were previously considered fundamentally disconnected: ADMM-based methods (FedDyn, FedADMM, FedPD) that use primal-dual updates with dual variables, and Variational Bayes methods (Partitioned Variational Inference, Bayesian committee machines) that use posterior distributions and site parameters. The key theoretical contribution is showing that ADMM dual variables naturally emerge from VB site parameters under isotropic Gaussian approximations, yielding a line-by-line correspondence between the two update rules. Building on PVI (Ashman et al., 2022) as the direct foundation, the paper derives FedLap (the isotropic case), then extends it to FedLap-Cov (full covariance, drawing on Laplace approximation and natural gradient literature) and FedLap-Func (function-space regularisation, drawing on knowledge-adaptation priors from Khan & Swaroop, 2021 and Daxberger et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual unification of ADMM and VB opens a principled pathway to combine their complementary strengths: ADMM's convergence guarantees and duality structure with Bayes' uncertainty quantification and flexible regularisation. Practically, FedLap-Cov's covariance-based preconditioning and FedLap-Func's function-space information yield consistent (if modest) improvements over FedDyn across heterogeneous settings, with the added benefit of one fewer hyperparameter to tune. Open questions include convergence rate analysis, extension to partial client participation, differential privacy, and whether the ADMM-VB connection generalises beyond the federated setting to broader distributed optimisation problems. The empirical gains, while consistent, are modest (1.7–5.9% in the best cases), and the methods require assumptions (Gaussian posteriors, shared unlabelled inputs for FedLap-Func) that limit immediate applicability.
