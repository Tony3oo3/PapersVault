## Problem

While hyperparameter transfer across model sizes is well-studied (e.g., muP), the dependence of optimal learning rate on token horizon — the total number of tokens seen during training — has not been systematically investigated. This paper shows that optimal LR decreases significantly as the token horizon increases, a fact that holds even under muP parametrization, and that this dependence follows a reliable power-law scaling relationship enabling zero-overhead LR transfer from short to long training runs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253015/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes model size (parameter count, 50M–2.7B in experiments), token horizon (25B–800B tokens), and batch size (0.5M or 1M tokens) as inputs, and produces a predicted optimal learning rate via the scaling law LR*(N,D) = C·N^(−α)·D^(−β). The fitting procedure requires only the final validation loss at a handful of (LR, horizon) pairs per model; the quadratic fit in log(LR) at each horizon yields the optimal LR, and a subsequent linear fit in log-log space across horizons recovers the scaling constants B and β with R² ≥ 0.96.

## Method

The paper conducts a large-scale ablation (>250 runs) on GPT-3-architecture transformers trained on RefinedWeb, varying LR by factors of {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4} around each model's GPT-3 base LR across token horizons of 25B–800B. At each horizon, a second-degree polynomial in log(LR) is fitted to the final validation losses (R² ≥ 0.995) to locate the optimal LR; the minimizer of this parabola serves as the empirical LR*. These optima are then fit to the power-law LR*(D) = B·D^(−β) via least squares in log-log space, yielding β ≈ 0.32 for models ≥ 760M. A joint scaling law LR*(N,D) = C·N^(−α)·D^(−β) with α ≈ 0.23 is derived by exploiting the parallelism of log-log lines across N and D, validated on a held-out 7B Llama-architecture model (R² = 0.978). The paper further demonstrates that muP parametrization does not eliminate the horizon dependence, and provides a practical rule-of-thumb: LR*(D_long) ≈ LR*(D_short)·(D_short/D_long)^(−0.32), requiring no additional compute beyond standard short-horizon LR sweeps.

## Results

The optimal LR follows a power-law scaling LR*(D) = B·D^(−β) with R² in the range 0.96–0.997 across model sizes from 50M to 2.7B parameters. The joint scaling law LR*(N,D) = C·N^(−α)·D^(−β) with C≈1.55×10⁻³, α≈0.23, β≈0.32 achieves a validation R² of 0.978 on a held-out 7B Llama model. Simulated hyperparameter transfer from 100B to 800B tokens yields a relative error of 10–15%, compared to >250% error from simply reusing the 100B optimal LR. For Llama-1 at 1T tokens, the predicted optimal LR is 1.15×10⁻⁴ versus the 3×10⁻⁴ actually used, an overestimate by a factor of >2.5, with an upper-bound validation loss penalty of ΔL = 0.027.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the 'no transfer' baseline: reusing the optimal LR found at 100B tokens for an 800B-token run produces a relative error exceeding 250%, while the proposed power-law scaling reduces this to 10–15%. Against Llama-1's published LR of 3×10⁻⁴, the scaling law predicts an optimal LR of 1.15×10⁻⁴ at 1T tokens, indicating Llama-1 trained with an LR too large by a factor of >2.5 (upper-bound loss penalty ΔL = 0.027). The paper also tests whether muP parametrization (Yang et al., 2022) eliminates the token-horizon dependence of optimal LR; it does not—the optimal LR still decreases monotonically with horizon under muP, confirming that muP addresses model-size transfer but not horizon transfer. The joint scaling law (Eq. 3) is validated across architectures (GPT-3 and Llama) with R² = 0.978 on the held-out 7B Llama model, demonstrating generalizability beyond the GPT-3 architecture used for fitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hyperparameter transfer framework established by muP (Yang et al., 2022), which addresses LR transfer across model sizes, to the previously unstudied dimension of token horizon. It adopts the power-law fitting methodology and notation from Kaplan et al. (2020) and Hoffmann et al. (2022) for LLM scaling laws, but applies it to the optimal LR as a function of training tokens rather than to validation loss. The work also engages with Everett et al. (2024) on scaling exponents across parameterizations, noting that their fixed-horizon assumption is precisely the limitation this paper addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a zero-overhead rule-of-thumb (Eq. 5) that practitioners can immediately apply when scaling training duration, and the Llama-1 case study demonstrates concrete real-world impact by showing a major model was trained with an LR >2.5× too large. Key limitations include the token horizon ceiling of ~800B tokens (well short of Llama-3's 15T), restriction to a single dataset and optimizer, and the fact that the joint N-D scaling law (Eq. 3) is only validated for models ≥760M. Open questions include interactions with weight decay, LR schedules, multi-epoch training, and whether the exponents hold for MoE or state-space architectures.
