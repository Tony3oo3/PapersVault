## Problem

Particle-in-Cell (PIC) simulations for Laser-Plasma Interaction in inertial confinement fusion require tens of millions of CPU hours even for short 100-picosecond runs, creating a severe bottleneck for fusion research. While diffusion models offer a promising alternative for generating scientific data, two gaps remain unresolved: how to faithfully encode continuous physical parameters so generated data respects underlying physics, and how to eliminate the multi-step denoising overhead to make generation truly competitive with PIC in wall-clock time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252572/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts four continuous scalar parameters—electron temperature (keV), ion temperature (keV), laser intensity (W/m²), and a target simulation time t_as (ps)—and produces a single 2D electric field snapshot with two orthogonal components (E1, E2) at that arbitrary time point. Unlike PIC, which must integrate sequentially from t=0, Diff-PIC can directly generate a snapshot at any t_as without simulating prior time steps, decoupling data dependency across parameter combinations. The training dataset comprises 6,615 OSIRIS PIC simulations, each containing 80 sequential electric field snapshots.

## Method

Diff-PIC is a conditional diffusion model built on a modified U-Net backbone (three down-sampling and three up-sampling blocks with attention) that generates 2D electric field images conditioned on physical parameters. Two key innovations distinguish it: (1) a Physically-Informed Parameter Encoder (PIPE) that encodes each continuous parameter via parallel sinusoidal positional encoding (for smooth interpolation) and polynomial encoding up to 4th degree (for extrapolation), concatenates the 20-dimensional embeddings, and refines them through an MLP, enabling generalization both within and beyond the training parameter range; (2) Rectified Flow Acceleration (RFA) that trains the model with a flow-matching objective to learn straight trajectories from Gaussian noise to the target field, followed by an interactive reflow procedure that straightens the learned paths, reducing generation to a single forward pass. The model is trained in a supervised manner on PIC-generated snapshots, minimizing the squared deviation between the linear noise-to-data interpolation and the predicted velocity field.

## Results

On the OSIRIS PIC simulation dataset (6,615 simulations × 80 snapshots), Diff-PIC achieves a 59.25% average reduction in MAE, 57.77% reduction in RMSE, and 49.21% reduction in FID across all testing sets compared to baselines. For the E1 electric field testing set, Diff-PIC reaches MAE=1.68×10⁻², RMSE=2.29×10⁻², FID=1.62, versus GAN-PIC (MAE=4.73, RMSE=5.84, FID=2.51) and NF-PIC (MAE=4.61, RMSE=5.35, FID=2.42). In extrapolation (20% beyond training range), Diff-PIC maintains ~2% relative absolute error. On a single RTX 4090 GPU, Diff-PIC achieves a ~16,200× speedup over traditional PIC for a 100 ps simulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-PIC is compared against two generative baselines, both equipped with the same Physically-Informed Parameter Encoder (PIPE) for fair comparison. GAN-PIC (StyleGAN2-based) and NF-PIC (Diffusion Normalizing Flow-based) are both substantially outperformed by Diff-PIC on every metric (MAE, RMSE, FID) for both E1 and E2 electric fields in both interpolation and extrapolation settings. In the interpolation testing set, Diff-PIC reduces MAE by ~64% over GAN-PIC and ~63% over NF-PIC on E1, and by ~58% and ~56% respectively on E2. In extrapolation (20% beyond training range), Diff-PIC still leads by ~62% in MAE over GAN-PIC on E1. On speed, Diff-PIC-GPU (16,200×) and GAN-PIC-GPU (15,600×) are comparable, while NF-PIC-GPU is significantly slower (921×) due to its multi-step denoising; Diff-PIC's one-step rectified flow gives it a clear efficiency edge over NF-PIC while matching GAN-PIC's speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-model-for-scientific-simulation line of work (molecular dynamics, galaxy image generation) to the specific domain of Particle-in-Cell (PIC) simulations for laser-plasma interaction in inertial confinement fusion. It builds directly on DDPM for the generative backbone and on rectified flow / InstaFlow to collapse multi-step denoising into a single forward pass, but departs from prior PIC acceleration efforts (GPU parallelization, hybrid CPU-GPU codes) by entirely bypassing the iterative time-stepping paradigm. The Physically-Informed Parameter Encoder (PIPE), which combines sinusoidal positional encoding with polynomial basis functions, is a novel conditioning mechanism not present in prior diffusion-based scientific data generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~16,200× speedup on a single RTX 4090 versus supercomputer PIC runs, combined with 1–2% relative error well below the ~12% threshold deemed acceptable for ICF analysis, makes rapid parameter exploration and data augmentation practically accessible to fusion researchers without HPC access. However, the evaluation is confined to 2D electric fields with only three physical parameters and a single OSIRIS dataset, leaving open whether the approach scales to 3D fields, additional parameters (magnetic fields, density, pressure), or longer simulation times. The authors themselves flag the need to harmonize simulation time t_as with the diffusion timeline t and to extend the condition encoder to broader physical parameter spaces, suggesting the current framework is a proof-of-concept rather than a production-ready surrogate.
