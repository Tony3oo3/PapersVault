## Problem

Decoder-only LLMs, including MoE variants, are trained for autoregressive next-token prediction, so their hidden states are biased toward output prediction rather than capturing full input semantics. This limits their effectiveness as embedding models without additional representation finetuning, raising the question of whether pre-trained LLMs can truly be called generalists given the broad application of embedding tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252521/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sentence as input and produces two complementary embedding vectors: a hidden-state embedding (last token, last layer, dimension d) and a routing-weight embedding (concatenated gating outputs across all L layers for the last token, dimension ΣN^(l)). For similarity-based tasks, the final output is a scalar similarity score computed either by cosine similarity on a concatenated vector or by a weighted sum of separately computed HS and RW cosine similarities. The routing-weight embedding is notably more robust to prompt variation than the hidden-state embedding.

## Method

The key insight is that MoE routing weights (RW), which record how each input is distributed across experts at every layer, capture intermediate reasoning choices that complement the output-biased hidden state (HS). MOEE combines these two signals via two strategies: (1) concatenation, which simply joins e_HS and e_RW into a single vector, and (2) a weighted sum of similarities, where cosine similarity is computed independently on HS and RW embeddings and then combined as sim_final = sim_HS + α·sim_RW. The hyperparameter α is optimized at test time using a gradient-based approach that maximizes Spearman rank correlation between the two similarity streams, requiring no ground-truth labels. The entire pipeline is training-free, operating directly on pre-trained MoE LLMs (DeepSeekMoE-16B, Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B, OLMoE-1B-7B) without any finetuning, and consistently outperforms both standalone HS and RW across six MTEB task categories.

## Results

On the MTEB benchmark (6 task categories, 20 datasets), MOEE(sum) with PromptEOL achieves average scores of 53.28 (DeepSeekMoE-16B), 55.04 (Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B), and 55.16 (OLMoE-1B-7B), all without any fine-tuning. Without prompts, MOEE(sum) improves over standalone HS by 22.45% on DeepSeekMoE (43.30 vs. 35.36 avg). The largest gains appear in STS (e.g., 74.63 Spearman on OLMoE with PromptEOL) and Re-ranking (60.43 MAP on OLMoE), while Clustering sees the most relative improvement (36.46 V-Measure vs. 32.83 for HS on OLMoE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

With PromptEOL, MOEE(sum) on OLMoE-1B-7B (55.16 avg) surpasses all trained baselines including coCondenser-msmarco (54.55), SimCSE-BERT-sup (53.53), and SGPT-125M-nli (51.27), despite requiring zero fine-tuning. On Re-ranking, MOEE(sum) on OLMoE achieves 60.43 MAP, edging out coCondenser (60.08); on Classification, MOEE(sum) on Qwen1.5-MoE reaches 59.57 accuracy, beating SimCSE-BERT-sup (58.98). However, MOEE(sum) still trails SimCSE-BERT-sup on STS (74.63 vs. 79.97 Spearman) and Pair Classification (71.26 vs. 75.82 AP), where supervised contrastive training provides an edge. Without prompts, MOEE(sum) lags behind trained methods (e.g., 43.30 vs. 54.55 for coCondenser on DeepSeekMoE), but the gap narrows substantially with PromptEOL. Across all three MoE models, MOEE(sum) consistently outperforms both standalone HS and RW, with the largest relative gains in Clustering (+22–28% over HS) and STS (+10–15% over HS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes an existing architectural byproduct—the routing weights of MoE LLMs—as a standalone embedding signal, departing from the standard practice of extracting embeddings solely from hidden states (as in PromptEOL and prior training-free LLM embedding work). It builds on the training-free embedding paradigm for decoder-only LLMs but introduces a fundamentally new information source: the per-layer expert gating distributions, which had never been considered as representations. The combination strategy (weighted sum of separate similarities vs. concatenation) is a novel design choice motivated by the observed complementarity between the two signals, rather than a straightforward fusion of existing embedding techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MoE routing weights provide complementary, prompt-robust, high-level semantic information to hidden states opens a new axis for extracting representations from MoE LLMs without any fine-tuning, which is practically valuable given the growing adoption of MoE architectures. However, the approach is inherently limited to MoE models, and the gains, while consistent across three models and six task types, are moderate (roughly 5–10 points over HS alone on MTEB averages). Open questions include whether the routing-weight signal generalizes to non-MoE architectures (e.g., via attention patterns), how it behaves on longer paragraph-level inputs where PromptEOL's single-word compression may degrade, and whether task-adaptive weighting of RW and HS can close the remaining gap to purpose-trained embedding models.
