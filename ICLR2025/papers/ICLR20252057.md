## Problem

No existing single vision-language model unifies fine-grained spatial localization (object detection), temporal reasoning (multi-object tracking), and natural language description in a single end-to-end system. The core obstacle is data scarcity: full captioned-trajectory annotations are extremely rare, while annotations for individual sub-tasks (detection, tracking, captioning) are abundant but disjoint, making it unclear how to train a unified model without full task annotations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video as a sequence of frames (up to 200 frames at 384×384 resolution for VidSTG; 3 sparsely sampled frames for VLN) and produces, for every detected object, a temporally consistent trajectory (bounding boxes linked by a shared identity) together with a single natural-language caption describing that trajectory. In grounding mode, a sentence query is additionally provided as input, and the model selects the trajectory whose caption-likelihood (weighted by detection score) best matches the query, outputting only the corresponding bounding boxes.

## Method

The architecture comprises three jointly trained modules: a per-frame class-agnostic object proposal network (CenterNet on a CLIP-initialized ViTDet-Base backbone), an end-to-end tracking module that uses a two-layer transformer to predict a global M×M association matrix over all object features in the video, and a trajectory captioning module that aggregates features along each identity (via soft weighted averaging or hard uniform sampling of m=6 frames) before autoregressive decoding with a 6-layer text decoder. The key architectural novelty is a differentiable greedy identity-assignment algorithm (Algorithm 1) that extracts trajectories from the association matrix directly on accelerators, enabling backpropagation through the tracking step—something prior trackers treat as non-differentiable post-processing. Training leverages a mixture of four disjoint datasets (COCO, Visual Genome, SMiT, Aug-COCO) that each supervise a different loss term (L_object, L_caption, L_assoc), allowing the full model to achieve zero-shot Dense VOC capability without any full-task annotations, and serving as a strong initialization for downstream finetuning.

## Results

On VidSTG (finetuned), the full model achieves CHOTA 56.9 (DetA 65.8, AssA 70.4, CapA 39.7), and on VLN (finetuned) achieves CHOTA 41.3 (DetA 44.3, AssA 89.5, CapA 17.7). Zero-shot (no task-specific training), the model reaches CHOTA 51.4 on VidSTG and 29.2 on VLN. On the BenSMOT person-tracking-and-captioning benchmark, the model achieves 90.19 HOTA and 0.254 CIDEr, substantially outperforming the prior state-of-the-art (71.98 HOTA, 0.087 CIDEr). For video grounding, the model achieves 61.9 sIoU on VidSTG and 65.1% Rec.&Prec.>0.5 on VLN Location-QA, both setting new state-of-the-art without explicit grounding training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against per-frame captioning + offline tracker baselines on VidSTG (Table 2), the end-to-end model (hard aggregation) achieves CHOTA 54.9, outperforming per-frame captioning with ByteTrack (52.3), FairMOT (51.2), and IoU tracker (49.9), with the largest gain in Association Accuracy (65.9 vs. 60.2 for ByteTrack). On BenSMOT, the full model (90.19 HOTA, 0.254 CIDEr) surpasses BeyondMOT (Li et al., 2024b) by 18.2 HOTA and 0.167 CIDEr; even with a smaller backbone and no pretraining, the end-to-end architecture alone still outperforms BeyondMOT on tracking and captioning. Against OW-VisCap (Choudhuri et al., 2024) on VidSTG, the model achieves CHOTA 56.9 vs. 53.0, with a large AssA advantage (70.4 vs. 54.0) but lower CapA (39.7 vs. 43.9) due to a much smaller language decoder (46M vs. 2.7B params). For spatial grounding on VidSTG, the generative model (61.9 sIoU) edges out dedicated grounding methods STCAT (61.7), TubeDETR (59.0), and STVGBert (47.3), and on VLN Location-QA achieves 65.1% Rec.&Prec.>0.5 vs. GRiT (62.1) and ReferFormer (48.3). The model also achieves competitive zero-shot grounding (54.1 sIoU on VidSTG) without any task-specific training, demonstrating the transferability of the disjoint pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRiT/GIT architecture for dense image object captioning to the video domain by adding an end-to-end tracking module that assigns trajectory identities and aggregates features over time before captioning. The tracking component builds on Global Tracking Transformers (GTR) but introduces a novel differentiable identity-assignment algorithm (Algorithm 1) that operates within the network rather than as a post-processing step, enabling joint training with the captioning head. The training strategy departs from prior work by decomposing the full task into disjoint sub-tasks (detection on COCO, dense captioning on Visual Genome, video captioning on SMiT, tracking on Aug-COCO) and combining them, rather than requiring a single dataset with full trajectory-caption annotations. The paper also repurposes video grounding datasets (VidSTG, VLN) for evaluation and shows that a generative captioning model can perform discriminative grounding by selecting the trajectory with maximum sentence likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes Dense VOC as a concrete, evaluable task with a principled metric (CHOTA) that jointly measures detection, tracking, and captioning accuracy, filling a gap where no single model previously unified spatial, temporal, and linguistic understanding. The disjoint supervision strategy is practically important: it shows that a complex multi-component model can be trained to zero-shot competence without any full-task annotations, leveraging the abundance of single-task datasets. Key limitations include producing only a single caption per trajectory (rather than segmenting multiple actions), relying on repurposed grounding datasets rather than a purpose-built benchmark, and a relatively small 46M-parameter language head that limits captioning quality compared to larger LLM-based approaches. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos, whether multi-segment captioning within a trajectory is feasible, and whether a dedicated Dense VOC dataset with richer annotations would further improve results.
