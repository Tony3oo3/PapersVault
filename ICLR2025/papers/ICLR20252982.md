## Problem

Retrieval-augmented generation systems inevitably receive noisy or irrelevant retrieved documents, yet existing RAG methods handle this noise implicitly by training models to directly predict answers, making the denoising process opaque and hard to verify. Obtaining explicit denoising supervision typically requires costly human annotation, leaving a gap between the need for interpretable noise handling and the practical constraints of supervision acquisition.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question, a set of top-K retrieved Wikipedia documents (5 for most tasks, 10 for multi-hop), and optionally N rationale demonstrations (default 2 for ICL). During rationale generation, the ground-truth answer is also provided to the generator model. The output is a structured denoising rationale that identifies which retrieved documents are relevant, explains how their contents lead to the answer, and states the final answer. For INSTRUCTRAG-FT, the inference input format matches training: retrieved documents prepended to the question, with the model generating the rationale autoregressively.

## Method

INSTRUCTRAG is a two-step self-synthesis framework that leverages an instruction-tuned LM's ability to generate explicit denoising rationales without additional supervision. In step one, given a QA pair and noisy retrieved documents, the rationale generator (M_φ) is prompted to identify relevant documents and articulate how they lead to the ground-truth answer, producing a rationale r that distinguishes useful from noisy content. In step two, these rationales serve as either in-context learning demonstrations (INSTRUCTRAG-ICL, training-free) or supervised fine-tuning targets (INSTRUCTRAG-FT, trainable), where the rationale learner (M_θ) is trained to maximize log p_θ(r|q,D). By default both M_φ and M_θ are the same off-the-shelf Llama-3-Instruct-8B model, making the entire pipeline self-contained. The key distinction from vanilla RAG is that the model must explicitly reason about which documents to trust before answering, rather than implicitly filtering noise during answer prediction.

## Results

INSTRUCTRAG-FT (Llama-3-Instruct 8B) achieves 66.2% on PopQA, 78.5% on TriviaQA, 65.7% on Natural Questions, 57.2% on 2WikiMultiHopQA, and 47.6% exact match on ASQA. INSTRUCTRAG-ICL (Llama-3-Instruct 8B) reaches 64.2%, 76.8%, 62.1%, 50.4%, and 44.7% respectively, while the 70B variant further improves to 65.5%, 81.2%, 66.5%, 57.3%, and 47.8%. The paper reports an average relative improvement of 8.3% over the best baseline across all five knowledge-intensive benchmarks in both training-free and trainable settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against In-Context RALM (Ram et al., 2023), INSTRUCTRAG-ICL wins on every metric: e.g., 64.2% vs. 62.3% on PopQA, 76.8% vs. 71.4% on TriviaQA, and 44.7% vs. 40.0% exact match on ASQA, with the gap widening on citation precision (70.9 vs. 62.1). Against Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2023b), INSTRUCTRAG-FT dominates on all accuracy metrics (66.2 vs. 55.8 on PopQA, 65.7 vs. 42.8 on NQ, 57.2 vs. 32.9 on MultiHopQA, 47.6 vs. 36.9 on ASQA em), though Self-RAG retains a slight edge on ASQA citation precision (69.7 vs. 65.7) and recall (69.7 vs. 70.5). Against RetRobust (Yoran et al., 2024), INSTRUCTRAG-FT leads on PopQA (66.2 vs. 56.5), TriviaQA (78.5 vs. 71.5), NQ (65.7 vs. 54.2), and ASQA em (47.6 vs. 40.5), while RetRobust is competitive on MultiHopQA (54.7 vs. 57.2), likely benefiting from GPT-3-generated intermediate subqueries. Notably, Self-RAG underperforms even vanilla SFT across all benchmarks, which the authors attribute to its reliance on GPT-4-generated reflection tokens that are unavailable under their constrained setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

INSTRUCTRAG repurposes the chain-of-thought rationale generation paradigm from general reasoning into the RAG denoising context, where prior work (Self-RAG, RetRobust) addresses noise through adaptive retrieval or robustness training but requires expensive external supervision from GPT-3/GPT-4. The key conceptual departure is leveraging the instruction-following ability of off-the-shelf LMs to self-synthesize denoising rationales without any additional supervision, positioning the method as orthogonal to retrieval-side improvements (query rewriting, adaptive retrieval) that dominate recent RAG literature. It also connects to the broader self-synthesis and self-improvement thread in LMs (e.g., Magpie, Self-ICL) but applies it specifically to the RAG noise-robustness problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit denoising rationale provides interpretability and verifiability that implicit RAG approaches lack, which is important for high-stakes deployments. The finding that self-synthesized rationales suffice without external supervision lowers the barrier to robust RAG, and the demonstrated OOD generalization (including transfer to code generation) suggests the denoising skill is somewhat task-agnostic. Open questions include whether the approach extends to open-ended generation, how it behaves under extremely long contexts with many retrieved documents, and whether the 89% overall rationale consistency (vs. 98% when relevant docs exist) can be improved with filtering mechanisms.
