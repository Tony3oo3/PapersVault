## Problem

Quantum algorithms for nonconvex optimization have been proposed under idealized noiseless oracle assumptions, but near-term quantum devices introduce noise that can fundamentally alter the optimization landscape. This paper systematically characterizes the robustness of quantum algorithms for finding ε-approximate second-order stationary points (ε-SOSPs) under noisy zeroth- or first-order oracles, identifying precise noise thresholds that separate regimes of poly-logarithmic, polynomial, exponential, or information-theoretically unsolvable query complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a B-bounded, ℓ-smooth, ρ-Hessian Lipschitz target function F: ℝᵈ → ℝ, accessed only through a noisy quantum oracle: either a zeroth-order oracle U_f encoding a function f with ‖F−f‖∞ ≤ ν, or a first-order oracle U_g encoding a gradient ∇f with ‖∇F−∇f‖∞ ≤ ν̃. The quantum oracles support coherent superposition queries, enabling parallel evaluation at multiple points. The output is a point x satisfying ‖∇F(x)‖ ≤ ε and λ_min(∇²F(x)) ≥ −√(ρε), i.e., an ε-SOSP of the true target F rather than the noisy proxy.

## Method

The paper develops three quantum algorithms tailored to increasing noise levels. For tiny noise (ν = Õ(ε¹⁰/d⁵)), it combines Perturbed Accelerated Gradient Descent with Jordan's quantum gradient estimation (phase kickback + QFT on a uniform mesh), achieving Õ(log d/ε^1.75) queries. For small noise (ν = Õ(ε⁶/d⁴)), it uses Perturbed Gradient Descent with the same quantum gradient estimation, yielding Õ(log⁴d/ε²) queries and an exponential speedup over the classical Ω(d/log d) lower bound. For intermediate noise (ν = O(ε^1.5/d)), it applies quantum mean estimation to stochastic gradients obtained via Gaussian smoothing of the noisy oracle, giving Õ(d^2.5/ε^3.5) queries—a polynomial improvement over the classical Õ(d⁴/ε⁵). Complementing these upper bounds, the paper constructs hard instances using a non-informative band around the origin (exploiting concentration of measure) to prove quasi-polynomial Ω(d^log d) lower bounds for both classical and quantum algorithms at higher noise, an information-theoretic impossibility result at ν = Õ(ε^1.5), and an Ω(ε^{-12/7}) lower bound in precision for any randomized classical or quantum algorithm.

## Results

Under Assumption 1.1 (noisy zeroth-order oracle), the paper establishes three quantum upper bounds: Õ(log d/ε^1.75) queries for tiny noise ν = Õ(ε^10/d^5), Õ(log^4 d/ε^2) for small noise ν = Õ(ε^6/d^4), and Õ(d^2.5/ε^3.5) for intermediate noise ν = O(ε^1.5/d). Under Assumption 1.2 (noisy first-order oracle), the quantum algorithm achieves O(d^2/ε^3) queries at noise level ν̃ = Θ(ε/d^0.5). The paper also proves a universal lower bound of Ω(ε^{-12/7}) on query complexity for any randomized classical or quantum algorithm, and a quasi-polynomial lower bound of Ω(d^log d) for both classical and quantum algorithms when noise exceeds certain thresholds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the state-of-the-art classical algorithm of Jin et al. (2018a), which requires Õ(d^4/ε^5) zeroth-order queries at noise level ν = O(ε^1.5/d), the paper's quantum algorithm (Theorem 2.5) achieves Õ(d^2.5/ε^3.5), yielding a polynomial speedup in both d and ε. For the first-order oracle at noise ν̃ = Θ(ε/d^0.5), the quantum algorithm (Theorem 2.6) achieves O(d^2/ε^3) versus the classical O(d^3/ε^4) of Jin et al. (2018a), again a polynomial improvement. In the small-noise regime (ν = Õ(ε^6/d^4)), the quantum algorithm achieves poly-logarithmic query complexity O(log^4 d/ε^2) while the classical lower bound (Theorem 3.3) is Ω(d/log d), establishing an exponential quantum speedup. The paper extends the deterministic classical lower bound of Carmon et al. (2021), Ω(ε^{-12/7}), to all randomized classical and quantum algorithms (Theorem 3.5), showing that noise does not weaken the fundamental ε-dependence lower bound. In the first-order small-noise regime (ν̃ = O(ε/d^0.5+ζ)), both classical and quantum algorithms achieve the same O(log^4 d/ε^2) complexity, so no quantum speedup exists there.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: classical nonconvex optimization with noisy oracles (Jin et al. 2018a, Zhang et al. 2017) and quantum algorithms for nonconvex optimization (Zhang et al. 2021, Liu et al. 2023). Prior quantum nonconvex optimization work assumed noiseless oracles, while classical noisy nonconvex optimization had no quantum counterpart. The paper bridges this gap by combining Jordan's quantum gradient estimation (Jordan 2005; Chakrabarti et al. 2020) with perturbed accelerated gradient descent (Zhang & Li 2021) and quantum mean estimation with Gaussian smoothing (inspired by Jin et al. 2018a), systematically characterizing robustness across noise regimes. The lower bounds extend the hard-instance constructions of Jin et al. (2018a) and Carmon et al. (2021) to the quantum setting via concentration-of-measure arguments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first complete noise-regime taxonomy for quantum nonconvex optimization, identifying precisely where exponential quantum speedups exist (ν ≲ ε^6/d^4), where only polynomial speedups are possible (ν ≈ ε^1.5/d), and where the problem becomes information-theoretically unsolvable (ν ≳ ε^1.5). This is important for near-term quantum device design, as it quantifies the noise tolerance required for quantum speedups to materialize. Key open questions include whether the polynomial-query quantum algorithm (Theorem 2.5) can be improved, whether optimal quantum query complexities can be characterized, and whether results extend to more realistic quantum noise models beyond bounded pointwise error.
