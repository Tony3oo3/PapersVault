## Problem

While theory guarantees that maximally expressive MPNNs can separate all WL-separable graph pairs, it provides no information about the quality of that separation. In practice, embeddings of theoretically separable graphs can be nearly identical under finite precision, and the separation quality of sum-based MPNNs deteriorates rapidly with depth. This paper addresses the missing quantitative analysis of how well-separated embeddings actually are.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251989/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes graphs with node features in a compact set Ω⊂ℝ^d (or multisets of vectors from Ω) and produces a scalar or vector embedding. The stability analysis is conducted with respect to the augmented Wasserstein metric for multisets and the Tree Mover's Distance TMD^(K) for graphs, which is zero if and only if two graphs are not K-WL-separable. The sort-based aggregation requires prior knowledge of the maximum multiset size for augmentation with a fixed point z outside Ω.

## Method

The paper introduces a probabilistic framework called 'Hölder in expectation' that quantifies separation quality of parametric functions by requiring the expected ratio of output distance to input distance (raised to a Hölder exponent) to be uniformly bounded away from zero over all input pairs. Using this framework, the authors prove that ReLU-sum multiset functions achieve a lower-Hölder exponent of (p+1)/p, smooth-activation sums require α≥n (the multiset size), and sort-based functions achieve α=1 (lower-Lipschitz). They then construct two novel MPNNs: SortMPNN, which uses column-wise sorting of projected neighborhood features for aggregation and readout (yielding the first MPNN with both upper and lower Lipschitz guarantees in expectation), and AdaptMPNN, which adapts the ReLU bias to the min-max range of projected features to avoid the range assumption. Adversarial ε-tree examples demonstrate that standard sum-based MPNNs (GIN, GCN, GAT) achieve only 50% accuracy on a binary classification task that SortMPNN and AdaptMPNN solve perfectly.

## Results

On the adversarial ε-Tree binary classification task, SortMPNN and AdaptMPNN achieve 100% accuracy while all sum-based baselines (GIN, GCN, GAT, ReluMPNN, SmoothMPNN) fail completely at 50%. On standard TU graph classification benchmarks, SortMPNN achieves 90.99% on Mutag, 76.46% on Proteins, 66.31% on PTC, 83.55% on NCI1, and 82.75% on NCI109, outperforming GIN on four of five datasets. On LRGB, SortMPNN achieves 0.6940 AP on peptides-func (best) and 0.2464 MAE on peptides-struct (second to GCN's 0.2460). On ZINC12K with ESAN, SortMPNN outperforms GIN in 5 of 8 aggregation scenarios, with the largest gain on DS-GNN(EGO) (0.104 vs 0.126 MAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SortMPNN consistently outperforms GIN (Xu et al., 2019) on TU datasets, gaining +1.6% on Mutag, +1.7% on PTC, +0.85% on NCI1, and +0.55% on NCI109, while being comparable on Proteins (+0.26%). Against GCN (Kipf & Welling, 2017), SortMPNN wins on Mutag (+5.4%), PTC (+2.1%), and NCI1 (+3.35%), and on LRGB peptides-func (0.6940 vs 0.6860 AP). GraphSage (Hamilton et al., 2017) is outperformed on all five TU datasets, with the largest margin on NCI1 (+5.85%). On LRGB, SortMPNN beats GINE (Hu et al., 2020) by +3.2% AP on peptides-func and GatedGCN (Bresson & Laurent, 2018) by +1.75% AP. GAT (Velickovic et al., 2018) is only compared on the ε-Tree task where both fail (0.5 accuracy), but SortMPNN's advantage there is absolute (1.0 vs 0.5). Notably, SortMPNN with width=1 already achieves lower-Lipschitz-in-expectation guarantees, explaining its robustness under reduced parameter budgets where it outperforms GCN on peptides-struct.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MPNN expressiveness analysis of Xu et al. (2019) and Morris et al. (2019), which established that MPNNs can only separate WL-separable graph pairs, by introducing a *quality* dimension to separation via a novel 'Hölder in expectation' framework for parametric functions. It builds on bi-Lipschitz stability results for multiset embeddings (Balan et al., 2022 for sorting; Cahill et al., 2024c for max-filters) and the Tree Mover's Distance metric (Chuang & Jegelka, 2022), but departs from prior work by showing that uniform lower-Hölder is too stringent for neural networks and proposing a probabilistic relaxation. The SortMPNN architecture draws directly on the sort-based multiset embeddings of Balan et al. (2022) and Dym & Gortler (2023), while the Adaptive ReLU variant is a new contribution addressing the bias-range assumption in standard ReLU summation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a critical gap between theoretical separability (WL test) and practical separation quality: standard MPNNs (GIN, GCN, GAT) achieve random-chance accuracy on graphs that are provably separable by 3 WL iterations, while SortMPNN achieves perfect accuracy. This reframes the design criteria for permutation-invariant networks from 'can it separate?' to 'how well does it separate?', with direct implications for architecture selection. Open questions include whether wider ReluMPNNs can recover good Hölder exponents, whether lower-Lipschitz architectures can be built without requiring prior knowledge of maximum multiset size (a limitation of SortMPNN), and whether the analysis extends to other graph metrics beyond TMD. The finding that smooth activations—despite guaranteeing injectivity—have strictly worse stability exponents (α ≥ n) than ReLU (α = 3/2) challenges the common assumption that smoother is better for representation quality.
