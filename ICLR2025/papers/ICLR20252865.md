## Problem

Controlled text generation methods increase the likelihood of satisfying a constraint but cannot guarantee that every generated output strictly meets it. This paper formalizes 'guaranteed generation' — producing outputs that always satisfy a hard constraint while minimally distorting the original model's distribution — and proves (Theorem 1) that no autoregressive model can exactly represent the ideal filtered distribution under P≠NP, establishing that training-time methods alone are insufficient and must be complemented by inference-time sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a base autoregressive LLM (Gemma-2B for unconditional 30-token generation, GPT-2 fine-tuned on ROCStories for conditional 80-token story completion) and a binary constraint predicate b (e.g., 'text contains the string amazing' with AR_a≈0.0023, or 'last sentence joy-score > 0.98' with AR_a≈0.005). It outputs text sequences that are guaranteed to satisfy b, drawn from a distribution g' that approximates the gold filtered distribution g = a(y)b(y)/Z. The constraint can be a simple lexical inclusion or a thresholded real-valued scorer, and the framework handles both unconditional and prefix-conditioned generation.

## Method

GUARD combines a trained autoregressive proposal a' with rejection sampling at inference time: sample y from a' until b(y)=1, then return y. The proposal a' is trained to approximate the gold distribution g (the base model a renormalized over constraint-satisfying outputs) using Distributional Policy Gradient (DPG), which adaptively minimizes KL(g||a') by performing gradient descent with importance-weighted updates. The key theoretical result (Theorem 2) shows KL(g||a') = KL(g||g') − log(AR_a'), meaning a single quantity simultaneously controls both the distributional fidelity of the output and the inference efficiency (acceptance rate). A warm-start variant initializes DPG with constraint-aware prompting (CAP) to bypass the low-acceptance early training phase, yielding a high initial AR that provides abundant gradient signal. Experiments show GUARD improves acceptance rate by ~180× (lexical) and ~60× (sentiment) over the base model while maintaining near-gold distributional closeness, with DPG strictly Pareto-dominating SFT.

## Results

In the lexical constraint scenario (Gemma-2B, 30-token generation, keyword 'amazing'), GUARD with warm-start DPG increases the acceptance rate from 0.0023 (base LLM) to 0.416, an approximately 180× speedup, while achieving KL(g||g') = 0.633 — far below the 6.06 of a naive heuristic sampler. In the sentiment reversal scenario (GPT-2 fine-tuned on ROCStories, positive-ending constraint), GUARD improves the acceptance rate from 0.005 to 0.306 (~60×) while maintaining close proximity to the gold distribution. DPG-based g' matches the gold distribution's diversity (Self-BLEU 2-gram: 0.342 vs. 0.315 for gold), whereas CAP-based g' shows significantly reduced diversity (Self-BLEU 2-gram: 0.438).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GUARD's rejection sampler against two alternative inference-time techniques: Independent Metropolis-Hastings (IMH) and Quasi-Rejection Sampling (QRS). IMH, while theoretically converging to the gold distribution g, converges very slowly in practice, making it an impractical approach for guaranteed generation; each individual IMH move is at least as costly as a full run of GUARD's Algorithm 1. QRS (Eikema et al., 2022) produces outputs that more closely approximate g than plain rejection sampling and still enables tractable measurement of distance from g, but remains more computationally costly than GUARD's Algorithm 1. Within the GUARD framework itself, warm-start DPG strictly Pareto-dominates SFT: for any SFT checkpoint, warm-start DPG achieves both a higher acceptance rate and a lower KL(g||g') simultaneously. CAP (constraint-aware prompting) achieves a high acceptance rate without training but produces distributions that greatly diverge from g, as evidenced by substantially higher Self-BLEU scores and positional bias in both experimental scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Distributional Policy Gradient (DPG) framework for training autoregressive models to match energy-based targets (Parshakova et al., 2019; Go et al., 2023), repurposing it to approximate a 'gold' filtered distribution defined by a base LLM intersected with a binary constraint. It departs from the controlled text generation literature, which conditions on attributes without guaranteeing strict constraint satisfaction, and from HMM-based tractable control methods (Zhang et al., 2023; 2024b) that handle only lexical/finite-state constraints. The impossibility theorem extends Lin et al.'s (2020b) ARM limitations to the filtered-model setting, and the Pythagorean identity for I-projections (Csiszár & Shields, 2004) is applied here to link proposal quality to both sampler efficiency and distributional fidelity in a single KL term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result formally establishes that no standard autoregressive architecture can exactly represent the ideal constrained distribution, closing off the 'just fine-tune' route and motivating the training-plus-inference-time combination that GUARD provides. Theorem 2's clean decomposition (KL(g||a') = KL(g||g') + log(1/AR)) gives practitioners a single, interpretable optimization target that simultaneously governs inference speed and output quality. However, the empirical validation is limited to two relatively simple constraint types (keyword inclusion, sentiment threshold) on small models (Gemma-2B, GPT-2), and the framework's dependence on a well-calibrated binary filter b remains an open practical challenge; scaling to complex, compositional, or soft constraints on frontier-scale LLMs is untested.
