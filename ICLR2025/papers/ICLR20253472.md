## Problem

Discrete diffusion models (DDMs) rely on sequential token refinement, making sampling slow. Parallel samplers like τ-leaping and k-Gillespie accelerate generation by updating multiple tokens simultaneously, but this introduces Compounding Decoding Error (CDE) — a mismatch between the true joint token distribution and the product of independent marginals used during parallel sampling. No principled method existed to allocate the fixed sampling budget across timesteps to minimize this error without incurring extra inference cost or requiring architectural changes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pretrained DDM (its neural network parameters, forward transition kernel type — absorbing, uniform, or Gaussian — and a fixed NFE budget, which must be a power of two for the main hierarchical algorithm) and produces a non-uniform set of intermediate timesteps {T → t₁ → … → t_{N-1} → 0}. The schedule optimization is a one-time offline procedure (seconds to minutes on a single GPU); at inference, the model is simply evaluated at the optimized timesteps with zero additional cost. For NFE values that are not powers of two, a subset-selection heuristic picks the most impactful timesteps from the nearest 2^K schedule.

## Method

JYS defines CDE for a single parallel step as the KL divergence between the true joint distribution and the product of per-token marginals (equivalently, the conditional mutual information between tokens), then proves that the cumulative CDE over a schedule upper-bounds the KL divergence between the true data distribution and the parallel-sampled distribution (Theorem 3.1). To make this bound tractable, the authors derive a KL-Divergence Upper Bound (KLUB) for two CTMCs via Girsanov's theorem, yielding a closed-form expression in terms of rate-matrix ratios at transition events. The schedule is then optimized with a hierarchical breakdown strategy: starting from {T, 0}, each interval is recursively split by finding the breakpoint t that maximizes the KLUB between the two-step and one-step approximations, using golden-section search (the KLUB is empirically unimodal in t). Two key computational tricks make KLUB estimation feasible: (1) replacing the intractable ground-truth path distribution with a coarser single-step approximation, and (2) applying the law of total expectation to replace Monte Carlo counting of transitions with the closed-form rate × Δt approximation, reducing per-evaluation cost to a single forward pass through the denoising network.

## Results

Across all four evaluation domains, JYS consistently outperforms the uniform sampling schedule at the same NFE budget. On CIFAR-10 (Gaussian transition, denoising parameterization), JYS achieves lower FID scores across all NFEs from 32 to 256. On text generation (SEDD-small/medium, absorbing transition, score parameterization), JYS yields lower generative perplexity (measured by GPT-2-large) at all NFEs from 16 to 256. On the Countdown synthetic dataset, JYS produces fewer rule-violating samples, and on Lakh Pianoroll (uniform transition, denoising parameterization), JYS-generated notes have smaller Hellinger distance to the NFE=512 reference at all NFEs from 2 to 64. The optimization itself is lightweight: for NFE=64, schedule optimization takes ~45 seconds on CIFAR-10 and ~5 minutes on text generation with a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the uniform sampling schedule (equal-sized intervals), which JYS outperforms on every dataset and every NFE tested. In Appendix E.4, the authors additionally compare against three heuristic schedules—LogSNR-Cosine, Sigmoid (more steps in the middle), and Logit (more steps at both ends)—and JYS consistently achieves the best performance across all NFE values on both CIFAR-10 and text generation, indicating that simple heuristic re-allocations cannot match the principled KLUB-driven optimization. The paper also notes that JYS is complementary to existing corrector-based methods (predictor-corrector, informed correctors) and can be combined with them for further gains, though no joint experiments are reported. The JYS schedule optimized on SEDD-small transfers directly to SEDD-medium without re-optimization, maintaining its advantage over uniform.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sampling schedule optimization paradigm from continuous diffusion models (Align Your Steps, AdaptiveSchedules) to discrete diffusion models, where the underlying dynamics are CTMCs rather than SDEs/ODEs. The theoretical core builds on the CTMC framework of Campbell et al. (2022) and the change-of-measure results for time-inhomogeneous Markov processes (Ding & Ning, 2021), applying Girsanov's theorem to derive a tractable KL-divergence upper bound (KLUB) on the compounding decoding error introduced by τ-leaping. Unlike predictor-corrector samplers or k-Gillespie, which require additional computation or specialized architectures, JYS operates as a zero-overhead schedule optimization that is orthogonal to and composable with those methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JYS provides a principled, plug-and-play way to improve DDM sampling quality at fixed NFE without any extra inference cost, validated across images, text, and music with three different transition kernels. The hierarchical breakdown strategy with golden-section search makes the optimization practical (seconds to minutes on a single GPU), and the resulting schedule shapes (concentrating steps where mutual information is high) offer interpretable guidance for practitioners. Key limitations include the restriction to NFE = 2^K (mitigated by a subset-selection heuristic) and the single-state-transition assumption in the closed-form KLUB approximation, which is exact only for absorbing kernels. Open questions include principled optimization for arbitrary NFE, extending KLUB to state-dependent CTMCs, and whether the schedule can be co-adapted during training.
