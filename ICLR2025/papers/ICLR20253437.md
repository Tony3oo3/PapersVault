## Problem

Newton's method achieves quadratic convergence but requires inverting the full Hessian at each iteration (O(d³) cost), which is prohibitive in high-dimensional problems. In massively parallel serverless (FaaS) settings where workers cannot communicate with each other, directly inverting the Hessian is infeasible, and existing Hessian sketching methods either require the effective dimension to be known a priori or lack a principled adaptive procedure for selecting the sketching dimension. This paper addresses the gap by providing a fully adaptive, data-driven debiasing scheme grounded in the Marchenko-Pastur law that requires only sketched measurements of the Hessian.

## Input / Output

Each worker independently samples a random dense sketching matrix S ∈ R^{m×d} (i.i.d. Gaussian, Rademacher, or sparse Rademacher entries) and computes the sketched Hessian SHS^T, which may be obtained via automatic differentiation even when F is only available as a program oracle. The adaptive procedure (Algorithm 1) selects m by doubling from an initial m₀ and testing a single scalar condition on the empirical Stieltjes transform at z₀ = −5λ/12; Algorithm 2 then finds the debiasing parameter λ̂ via binary search on the same transform. The server averages q independent sketched inverse Hessian estimates to form the Newton direction, and the final output is the optimized parameter θ with a non-asymptotic error bound that is dimension-free (independent of the Hessian's condition number).

## Method

The key insight is that the Marchenko-Pastur equation for the companion sample covariance SHS^T implies that the sketched inverse Hessian S^T(SHS^T + λ̃I)^{-1}S is asymptotically unbiased for (H+λI)^{-1} precisely when λ̃ = λ(1 − d_H(λ)/m), where d_H(λ) is the effective dimension. Since d_H(λ) is intractable to compute (it requires inverting H), the paper introduces a doubling-based adaptive test: at each candidate m, the worker evaluates the empirical Stieltjes transform at z₀ = −5λ/12 and accepts m if the value exceeds 1/λ, which w.h.p. holds iff m ≥ 2·d_H(λ) and fails iff m < 1.5·d_H(λ). For Gaussian sketches, the paper proves non-asymptotic, dimension-free concentration bounds showing the aggregate error ‖(H+λI)^{1/2} W̄ (H+λI)^{1/2} − I‖ ≤ ε with m = O(1/ε²) and q = O(d log d · log(1/δ)/(mε²)) workers. Combined with a new convergence theorem for approximate Newton on self-concordant objectives (quadratic convergence above the noise floor η, then linear at rate O(η)^t below it), this yields an end-to-end guarantee with total runtime O(d²m·log(1/ε)/log m) in the massively parallel regime, independent of the Hessian's condition number.

## Results

The adaptive sketching procedure (Algorithm 1) achieves a 100% success rate across all tested spectral decay regimes (α = 1, 2/3, 1/2) and all three sketch types (Gaussian, Rademacher, sparse Rademacher), consistently selecting m within the theoretical guarantee [1.5·d_H(λ), 4·d_H(λ)]. In end-to-end optimization on UCI datasets, the proposed debiased parallel Newton method converges in ≤5 iterations on 'eunite2001', 'bodyfat', and 'pyrim' where all five baseline distributed Newton methods require ≥20 iterations. For logistic regression, the method halves the iteration count compared to the second-best baseline on 'german_numer', 'ionosphere', and 'sonar', while DANE diverges entirely on 'german_numer' and 'fourclass'.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five distributed Newton methods: GIANT (averages local Hessian inverses), Determinantal Averaging (weights by local determinants), Optimal Shrinkage (Zhang & Pilanci 2023, requires known effective dimension), DiSCO (uses only the first worker's Hessian), and DANE (solves local subproblems). The proposed debiased Hessian sketching method outperforms all five baselines in convergence speed on nearly every dataset for both ridge and logistic regression, with the largest gains (4× fewer iterations) on 'eunite2001', 'bodyfat', and 'pyrim'. The method also exhibits the smallest variance across Monte-Carlo trials, indicating superior robustness. For logistic regression, DANE diverges on 'german_numer' and 'fourclass', and Determinantal Averaging produces NaN values on 'ionosphere' and 'sonar' due to numerical instability in determinant calculations. On 'segment' and 'mg' where baselines already converge within ~10 steps, the proposed method still provides a modest but consistent improvement. The key advantage is that the proposed method requires no knowledge of the effective dimension d_H(λ) (unlike Optimal Shrinkage) and operates under a fundamentally different paradigm (centralized data access with parallel sketching vs. data splitting among workers).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on debiasing sketched Hessian inverses for distributed Newton methods (Derezinski et al., 2020; Zhang & Pilanci, 2023), which previously required the effective dimension d_H(λ) to be known a priori. The key conceptual departure is an adaptive, data-driven procedure for selecting the sketching dimension m and debiasing parameter λ̃ using only sketched measurements, grounded in the Marchenko-Pastur law and its deterministic equivalents from random matrix theory. The paper also departs from the federated learning framing of prior distributed Newton work by assuming all workers have full data access (a serverless/FaaS star topology), shifting the goal from minimizing data transfer to minimizing deployment rounds and per-worker compute. The non-asymptotic guarantees for Gaussian sketches build on concentration inequalities and the orthogonal invariance of the Gaussian distribution, extending the asymptotic RMT framework of Knowles & Yin (2017) and LeJeune et al. (2022) to finite-sample, dimension-free bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive sketching dimension selection fills a practical gap that was 'left largely unaddressed' in the Hessian sketching literature, making the debiasing framework directly deployable without expensive a priori spectral analysis. The dimension-free error bounds (independent of the Hessian's condition number) are a meaningful theoretical advance, as they guarantee robustness for ill-conditioned objectives where first-order methods typically degrade. The convergence analysis for approximate Newton with self-concordant objectives (Theorem 4.1) is noted by the authors as potentially of independent interest beyond the parallel setting. Limitations include the requirement for i.i.d. dense sketches (non-asymptotic results are Gaussian-specific), the assumption of full data access at each worker (ruling out federated settings), and the fact that the practical speedup over exact Newton is most pronounced when the effective dimension is much smaller than d.
