## Problem

Robustified ANNs have demonstrated strong alignment with human ventral stream processing, enabling prediction of neural activity and modulation of human percepts, but their potential to *improve* human visual learning has remained unexplored. Specifically, no prior work has used model-based difficulty estimation and category-specific image enhancement to design curricula that accelerate novice human learning of unfamiliar image categories, particularly in clinically challenging domains like histology and dermoscopy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251518/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled RGB images (resized to 224×224×3 with center-crop, grayscale converted to RGB) from a target classification task and a pre-trained robustified ANN (adversarially trained ResNet-50 or XCiT). It produces three outputs: (1) a per-image difficulty score computed as the class-specific percentile rank of the ground truth logit, (2) enhanced images generated by projected gradient ascent on the ground truth logit within an ℓ₂ pixel budget ε, and (3) a structured training curriculum that sequences images by difficulty and applies tapering enhancement magnitudes across training blocks. The final evaluation is on unmodified, randomly selected held-out test images, ensuring the gains reflect genuine learning rather than perturbation memorization.

## Method

L-WISE combines two model-based mechanisms applied during the training phase of a human image categorization task. First, Difficulty Selection (DS) restricts image sampling to those below a stepwise-increasing ground truth logit percentile threshold (starting at d₁=0.1, incrementing by 0.15 per block over 6 blocks), implementing an easy-to-hard curriculum. Second, Enhancement Tapering (ET) generates perturbations via projected gradient ascent that maximize the ground truth logit (and optionally minimize competing class logits with weight α=1 for fine-grained tasks), with the ℓ₂ pixel budget ε halving per block from ε=8 to 0 over the same 6 blocks. The key insight is that the robustified model's ground truth logit serves a dual role: as a state-of-the-art predictor of human recognition difficulty (AUC=0.72, outperforming c-score, prediction depth, and adversarial robustness) and as an optimization target whose maximization produces semantically meaningful perturbations that amplify category-relevant features for human viewers. The approach is validated across three domains—4-way moth species, 4-way dermoscopy, and 2-way histology—yielding 33–72% accuracy gains above chance and 20–23% training time reductions relative to control participants.

## Results

L-WISE increases the test-time accuracy margin above chance by 57.6% on a 4-way moth species task, 72.3% on a 4-way dermoscopy task, and 33.1% on a binary histology task, while simultaneously reducing training duration by 20–23% with a fixed number of trials. The ground truth logit from a robustified ResNet-50 predicts human categorization error with AUC = 0.72 (10-fold CV), outperforming prior state-of-the-art difficulty metrics. On a 16-way ImageNet animal classification task, logit-maximization enhancement with ε = 20 raises mean human accuracy from 0.75 (unmodified) to 0.84, whereas off-the-shelf enhancement methods (CLAHE, MSRCR, Lightroom Auto) produce no significant accuracy change despite inducing larger pixel-level perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For image enhancement, L-WISE's logit-maximization perturbations significantly outperform three off-the-shelf baselines—CLAHE, MSRCR, and Adobe Lightroom Auto—all of which induced larger average ℓ₂-norm pixel changes yet produced no statistically significant increase in human categorization accuracy (Fig. 1B). For difficulty prediction, the robustified ResNet-50 ground truth logit (AUC = 0.72) outperforms the c-score (Jiang et al., 2021), prediction depth (Baldock et al., 2021), and image-level adversarial robustness, and even outperforms all three metrics combined into a single logistic regression model (Fig. S6). In the learning-augmentation comparison, L-WISE (0.60 mean accuracy, 11.1 min training) beats the control group (0.47, 14.0 min) on the moth task and (0.47, 10.5 min) beats control (0.38, 13.5 min) on dermoscopy; the individual components ET and DS each contribute significantly, but their combination in L-WISE yields the best accuracy and the shortest training time. Notably, ET (shuffled) underperforms ET, confirming that the easy-to-hard curriculum ordering matters for enhancement, while DS (shuffled) matches or exceeds DS, suggesting that simply presenting easier images on average is the primary driver of the selection benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes adversarially trained (robustified) ANNs—originally developed for adversarial robustness (Madry et al., 2018) and subsequently shown to modulate human percepts in a disruptive direction (Gaziv et al., 2023)—by reversing the perturbation objective to *enhance* ground-truth category perception rather than disrupt it. It also departs from the machine teaching literature (STRICT, EXPLAIN, MaxGrad), which focuses on selecting optimal teaching sets, by introducing category-specific image enhancement as a complementary mechanism. The difficulty-prediction component supersedes prior state-of-the-art metrics (c-score, prediction depth) with a simpler ground-truth logit score from the same robustified model, positioning the logit as a unified tool for both difficulty estimation and perturbation generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 33–72% test-accuracy gains and 20–23% training-time reductions across natural, dermoscopy, and histology domains suggest a practical pathway for accelerating expert visual training in medicine, where perceptual learning is bottlenecked. However, the ablation reveals that the full L-WISE combination does not significantly outperform its individual components in accuracy, and that shuffling the difficulty schedule (DS-shuffled) actually outperforms the intended easy-to-hard ordering—challenging the curriculum-learning assumption that ordering matters. Critical open questions include the homogenizing effect of high-ε enhancements (e.g., benign moles becoming uniform blobs), potential skin-tone bias in dermoscopy perturbations, and whether fixed schedules can be replaced by adaptive, learner-responsive scheduling before clinical deployment.
