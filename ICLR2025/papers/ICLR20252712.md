## Problem

Existing analysis-by-synthesis 3D facial reconstruction methods struggle to capture fine-grained expressions (irregular mouth shapes, exaggerated or asymmetrical movements) because differentiable renderers produce overly smoothed pseudo-images and cannot model complex lighting, shadows, or occlusions. SMIRK partially addressed this with a neural renderer but still relies on randomly sampled input pixels, leaving a significant domain gap between synthesized and real images that degrades expression accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 224×224 RGB face image and produces a hybrid representation: explicit FLAME parameters (shape β∈R³⁰⁰, expression Ψ, pose θ) for 3D mesh reconstruction, plus a compact multi-scale appearance token T (4 sub-tokens of 256 dimensions each) encoding identity, texture, and illumination. The token-guided synthesizer then generates a high-fidelity reconstructed facial image aligned to the mesh geometry. The tokens are interpretable and transferable, enabling downstream tasks like identity swapping and expression transfer without retraining.

## Method

TEASER introduces a Multi-scale Facial Appearance Tokenizer (MFAT) built on MobileNet-V3 that extracts features at 4 encoder stages, projects each to a 256-dim vector via average pooling and a fully connected layer, and concatenates them into a single appearance token capturing both high-level semantics and fine-grained texture. A Token-guided Face Synthesizer (TFS), a U-Net conditioned on the rendered mesh image and background, injects the token through two mechanisms: AdaIN for global style modulation and a ControlNet-style zero-convolution token decoder for high-frequency detail injection. Training is self-supervised via a token cycle loss (augmenting expression parameters and requiring the token to remain invariant), a pose-dependent landmark loss that masks invisible landmarks based on yaw angle, and a region loss focusing on eye and mouth areas. The two-stage training alternates between updating geometry/token encoders and the TFS to prevent the renderer from compensating for encoder errors.

## Results

On the LRS3 test set, TEASER achieves LPIPS 0.077, FID 19.41, CSIM 0.804, PSNR 30.67, AED 0.114, and APD 0.044, outperforming SMIRK (LPIPS 0.109, FID 25.39) and 3DDFA-V3 (LPIPS 0.181, FID 56.18) across all image-level metrics. On HDTF, the advantage is even more pronounced: TEASER reaches FID 30.19 and PSNR 28.35 versus 3DDFA-V3's FID 104.90 and PSNR 12.87. For video reconstruction, TEASER reduces warp-error to 0.801 and flicker to 1.167 on LRS3 (vs. SMIRK's 1.245 and 1.588), demonstrating superior temporal consistency. On 3D geometry benchmarks (NoW, Stirling, FaceWarehouse), TEASER achieves the best median error (0.92 mm on NoW, 1.00 mm on Stirling, 2.78 mm on FaceWarehouse).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEASER consistently outperforms SMIRK (CVPR 2024) on every metric across both LRS3 and HDTF, with the largest gains in FID (19.41 vs. 25.39 on LRS3) and video warp-error (0.801 vs. 1.245), attributed to the token-based guidance replacing SMIRK's randomly sampled pixels. Against 3DDFA-V3 (CVPR 2024), TEASER shows a dramatic margin on HDTF (FID 30.19 vs. 104.90; PSNR 28.35 vs. 12.87), reflecting 3DDFA-V3's reliance on the BFM model rather than FLAME. On 3D geometry benchmarks (Table A-1), TEASER achieves the best median error on NoW (0.92 mm), Stirling (1.00 mm), and FaceWarehouse (2.78 mm), surpassing SMIRK (0.99/1.01/2.87) and DECA (1.23/1.03/3.09). Visually (Fig. 3), TEASER and SMIRK both outperform DECA, EMOCA, and Deep3DFace in capturing vivid expressions, but TEASER produces more precise mouth shapes, eye openness, and lip closure details. The only metric where a baseline is competitive is HDTF flicker, where 3DDFA-V3 (0.870) slightly edges TEASER (0.662) — though TEASER still wins on all other HDTF metrics by large margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEASER is a direct evolution of SMIRK (Retsinas et al., CVPR 2024), which replaced the traditional differentiable renderer in the analysis-by-synthesis pipeline with a neural image generator. The key conceptual departure is replacing SMIRK's randomly sampled input pixels with a learned multi-scale appearance tokenizer (MFAT) that produces compact, interpretable tokens, combined with a ControlNet-inspired zero-convolution decoder for high-frequency detail injection. The broader lineage traces through the FLAME-based analysis-by-synthesis family (DECA, EMOCA, 3DDFA-v3), and the pose-dependent landmark loss departs from standard dynamic landmark marching by introducing yaw-angle-conditioned visibility masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent weakness in single-image 3D face reconstruction—accurate capture of fine-grained expressions (irregular mouth shapes, asymmetrical movements, subtle gaze shifts)—and demonstrates that a well-designed implicit appearance token can provide stronger photometric supervision than pixel sampling or PCA texture coefficients. The interpretability of the tokens (identity clustering via t-SNE, clean identity/expression disentanglement) opens practical downstream applications in face animation, expression transfer, and identity swapping without requiring separate models. However, the method remains constrained by the FLAME parametric model, cannot handle strong occlusions (hands, microphones, bangs), and the overall framework is still analysis-by-synthesis, leaving open whether the token-guided rendering paradigm can be extended to non-parametric or generative 3D representations.
