## Problem

Prior 4D generation methods rely on separately trained video and multi-view generative models, producing outputs that are inconsistent across both the temporal (frame) and spatial (view) axes, and depend on slow SDS-based optimization that takes hours per object. Additionally, no large-scale dataset of dynamic 3D objects exists to train a unified generative model that jointly reasons over motion and viewpoint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251197/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular input video of F frames and a user-specified camera trajectory of V views (each defined by elevation and azimuth angles), and produces a V×F grid of novel-view images at 576×576 resolution. The first frame's multi-views are pre-generated by SV3D and serve as conditioning; the model generates 8 views × 5 frames per inference pass (~40 s), with a mixed sampling scheme extending to arbitrary-length videos. The generated novel-view videos are then used to optimize a dynamic NeRF (canonical hash grid + deformation MLP) in approximately 15–20 minutes per object.

## Method

SV4D is a unified latent video diffusion model built by combining SVD (for temporal priors) and SV3D (for multi-view priors) into a single UNet with three attention types per layer: spatial attention (within each image), view attention (across views at the same frame, cross-attended to input-video CLIP embeddings), and frame attention (across frames at the same view, cross-attended to the reference multi-views of the first frame). A mixed sampling scheme first generates sparse anchor frames via interleaved sampling, then densely fills intermediate frames using the anchors as new conditioning, alternating forward/backward conditioning to ensure smooth transitions. The model is trained on the newly curated ObjaverseDy dataset (filtered and re-rendered dynamic objects from Objaverse) using progressive training on static then dynamic camera orbits. For 4D reconstruction, the generated novel-view videos serve as pseudo ground-truths to optimize a dynamic NeRF (Instant-NGP hash grid + time-conditioned deformation MLP) with photometric (MSE, LPIPS, mask) and geometric (normal, depth smoothness) losses, eliminating the need for SDS.

## Results

On the ObjaverseDy dataset, SV4D achieves the best 4D generation quality across all metrics: LPIPS 0.131, CLIP-S 0.905, FVD-F 659.66, FVD-V 368.53, FVD-Diag 525.65, and FV4D 614.35, outperforming the closest baseline DreamGaussian4D (FVD-F 697.80, FV4D 638.15) and Consistent4D (FVD-F 880.54, FV4D 871.95). For novel view video synthesis on ObjaverseDy, SV4D achieves FVD-F 585.09 and FV4D 470.46, a 31.5% reduction in FVD-F over SV3D (729.67) and 14.2% over STAG4D (652.43). On Consistent4D, SV4D reduces FVD-F to 732.40 for 4D outputs (vs. 1133.93 for Consistent4D) and 677.68 for novel view synthesis (vs. 989.53 for SV3D). User studies confirm these gains, with SV4D preferred 73.3% of the time for video synthesis and 60% for 4D outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SV4D outperforms all nine baselines on the primary 4D generation metrics. Against Consistent4D [24], SV4D reduces FVD-F by 25.1% (659.66 vs. 880.54) and FV4D by 29.5% (614.35 vs. 871.95) on ObjaverseDy, while also achieving lower LPIPS (0.131 vs. 0.165). Against STAG4D [88], SV4D improves FVD-F by 29.0% and FV4D by 38.7%, with a notable CLIP-S gap (0.905 vs. 0.860). Against DreamGaussian4D [55], the closest competitor, SV4D wins on every metric with margins of 5.5% on FVD-F and 3.7% on FV4D. For novel view video synthesis, SV4D beats SV3D [72] by 19.8% on FVD-F (585.09 vs. 729.67) and 31.9% on FV4D (470.46 vs. 690.49), though SV3D has a marginally lower LPIPS (0.131 vs. 0.136). Diffusion² [82] is the weakest baseline, trailing SV4D by 44.2% on FVD-F and 64.3% on FV4D. On Consistent4D, SV4D also dominates: 35.4% lower FVD-F than Consistent4D (732.40 vs. 1133.93) and 26.2% lower than STAG4D (732.40 vs. 992.21). 4Diffusion [89] and Efficient4D [48] are only compared on Consistent4D with limited metrics (no FVD-F reported for 4Diffusion due to frame count mismatch), while 4DGen [85] and GaussianFlow [14] are compared only on LPIPS and CLIP-S where SV4D also leads.

## Relation to Prior Work

SV4D unifies two previously separate lines of work—video diffusion (SVD) for temporal consistency and multi-view diffusion (SV3D) for spatial consistency—into a single latent diffusion model with dedicated view and frame attention blocks. This departs from prior 4D generation methods (DreamFusion, Consistent4D, STAG4D, 4D-fy) that rely on SDS-based optimization to distill priors from independently trained video and multi-view models, and from concurrent works (Diffusion², 4Diffusion, Vivid-ZOO) that jointly sample but still compose separate generators. The 4D reconstruction step follows the D-NeRF + Instant-NGP paradigm but replaces SDS with direct photometric optimization on the generated novel view videos, and the curated ObjaverseDy dataset addresses the data scarcity that has limited prior training-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By training a single model on 4D data and eliminating SDS, SV4D reduces 4D generation time from hours to roughly 15–20 minutes while achieving state-of-the-art multi-frame and multi-view consistency, making dynamic 3D content creation more practical for applications like gaming and AR/VR. Key limitations include the reliance on synthetic training data (ObjaverseDy) with uncertain generalization to complex real-world scenes, the fixed 8-view × 5-frame inference window that necessitates the mixed sampling heuristic, and the still-required per-object optimization step that prevents fully feed-forward generation. Open questions include whether the unified attention design scales to longer videos or multi-object scenes, and whether the approach can be extended to text-conditioned 4D generation without a reference video.
