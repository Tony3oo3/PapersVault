## Problem

Despite the rapid proliferation of 3D Neural Graphics Representations (NeRF, 3DGS, and variants) shared on online platforms, no framework exists for accurately retrieving stored NGRs from large data stores using text queries. Existing NGR semantic understanding methods operate scene-by-scene, require architecture-specific modifications, and cannot scale to hundreds of scenes as a general retrieval solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251142/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a pre-trained NGR in any format (NeRF, 3DGS, etc.) stored without its original training images or camera poses, along with a user text query (object label or free-form sentence). It renders RGB images from the NGR, generates visual embeddings via a pre-trained VLM, and stores them in a FAISS IVF1024 index. At query time, the text is encoded by the same VLM's text encoder, and retrieval returns the scene with the highest cosine similarity, optionally accompanied by a rendered image. The entire scene analysis (noise detection, camera navigation, embedding generation) takes under 20 seconds per scene, and individual queries resolve in ~1 ms.

## Method

Retri3D renders RGB images from any pre-trained NGR and uses a zero-shot VLM (XDecoder or OpenCLIP) to generate compact visual embeddings, avoiding architecture-specific semantic field training. The key technical contribution is Neural Graphics Noise Analysis: pixel-wise activation features from the VLM's visual encoder are shown to cluster tightly for noise/floater artifacts across scenes, enabling a multivariate Gaussian (fitted on random-viewpoint renderings) to score pixel cleanliness via Mahalanobis distance. The Smart Camera Movement Module (SCMM) iteratively navigates the camera toward cleaner viewpoints by applying linear-decay edge padding and Gaussian blur to the clean-score map, then finding the maximum-sum submatrix to determine the next viewing direction. SCMM operates on low-resolution renderings for efficiency, then performs high-resolution rendering only at the discovered clean viewpoints for final embedding generation. This pipeline is fully general across NGR formats, requires no fine-tuning of the VLM, and achieves retrieval accuracy within a few percent of the oracle training-viewpoint baseline while being orders of magnitude faster and more storage-efficient than LERF or LangSplat baselines.

## Results

On the LERF dataset (13 scenes) with 100 rendered images, Retri3D achieves 80.02% P@1 retrieval accuracy with SCMM-selected viewpoints (84.95% with training viewpoints), substantially outperforming LangSplat (79.83%) and LERF (26.24%) for object-label queries, and 70.48% vs. 51.92% (LangSplat) and 36.89% (LERF) for LLaVA caption queries. On ScanNet++ (280 scenes), SCMM achieves 64.18% P@1 at 100 images versus 29.04% for random viewpoints. In terms of efficiency, Retri3D requires only 20.58 MB of embedding storage and 5×10⁻⁵ s per query, compared to 18.78 GB / 10⁻³ s for LangSplat and 225.4 GB / 17 s for LERF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LangSplat, Retri3D (SCMM) achieves 80.02% P@1 at 100 images for object labels, edging out LangSplat's 79.83% by 0.19 points without requiring training poses; with training poses available, the gap widens to 5.12 points (84.95% vs. 79.83%). For LLaVA caption queries, Retri3D (SCMM) leads LangSplat by ~18.6 points (70.48% vs. 51.92%), as LangSplat's object-segmented embeddings are poorly matched to whole-image sentence descriptions. Against LERF, Retri3D dominates by a large margin: 80.02% vs. 26.24% (object labels) and 70.48% vs. 36.89% (LLaVA captions) at 100 images, since LERF's multi-scale CLIP embeddings are too uniform to distinguish objects across scenes. In terms of storage and speed, Retri3D is ~913× smaller than LangSplat (20.58 MB vs. 18.78 GB) and ~10,957× smaller than LERF (20.58 MB vs. 225.4 GB), with retrieval 20× faster than LangSplat and 340,000× faster than LERF. For the noise-analysis comparison, Retri3D's Neural Graphics Noise Analysis achieves lower (better) AUSE-RGB than Bayes' Rays across all four ScanNet scenes (e.g., 0.18 vs. 0.38 on scene 001), while Bayes' Rays achieves lower AUSE-Depth, reflecting that each method's uncertainty aligns better with its native signal (RGB vs. depth).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of NGR representations and retrieval, repurposing pre-trained VLMs (XDecoder, OpenCLIP) as a general-purpose semantic encoder that bypasses the need for NGR-specific architectural modifications. It departs from the LERF/LangSplat line of work, which injects visual embeddings into the NGR training process, by instead rendering RGB images and applying off-the-shelf VLMs zero-shot. The Neural Graphics Noise Analysis technique is conceptually novel: it leverages the observation that VLM activation features naturally cluster rendering artifacts (floaters, noise) into a tight distribution separable from clean pixels, a property not previously exploited. The Smart Camera Movement Module draws on active view selection ideas from NeRF uncertainty estimation (e.g., Bayes' Rays) but operates purely on rendered RGB without access to internal NGR parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As NGR assets proliferate on platforms like Polycam and Luma AI, the inability to search or retrieve them is a practical bottleneck that this work directly addresses. The key open question is scalability: while the paper demonstrates 280 scenes with ~1ms lookup, the noise Gaussian model and SCMM heuristics may not generalize to millions of diverse scenes or to dynamic/outdoor environments. The finding that noise features are consistent across NGR types (NeRF vs. 3DGS) and across scenes suggests the technique could be repurposed for other NGR quality-assessment tasks beyond retrieval. A limitation is that the approach still requires rendering, which introduces a dependency on the NGR's rendering pipeline and may not capture purely geometric properties.
