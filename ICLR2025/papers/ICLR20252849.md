## Problem

Standard LLM fine-tuning (SFT or RL) is agnostic to the inference-time strategy, yet the optimal training objective depends critically on how the model will be used at test time. For Best-of-N (BoN) inference—where N candidates are sampled and a verifier selects the best—conventional training fails to account for the non-differentiable argmax selection, leading to suboptimal policies that do not exploit the exploration-exploitation trade-off inherent in BoN sampling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts paired with either expert demonstrations (SFT setting) or binary environment rewards (RL setting), along with a verifier score function r(x,y) used for candidate selection during both training and inference. The output is a fine-tuned base policy π that is explicitly optimized so that when wrapped in the BoN inference procedure (sample N candidates at temperature T, select argmax by verifier), it achieves higher BoN accuracy or pass@N than a conventionally trained policy. A key design detail is that the training sample count N′ and temperature T′ can differ from evaluation settings, and the learned policy generalizes across different (N, T) configurations at test time.

## Method

The paper derives a variational approximation of the BoN policy as an exponential-twisting (energy-based) distribution π_bon(y|x) ∝ π(y|x)·exp(λ_N·Q_π(x,y)), where Q_π is the expected win-rate over the base policy and λ_N is a 1D-search parameter monotonically increasing in N. This circumvents the non-differentiable argmax and yields closed-form gradients for both SFT and RL objectives. For SFT, the gradient decomposes into a standard likelihood term plus an inference-awareness term that regularizes the policy to increase data win-rate while limiting overfitting to BoN samples. For RL, a REINFORCE-style gradient draws samples from the BoN policy rather than the base policy; under binary reward, this simplifies to an asymmetric weighting scheme (BoN-RLB) where positive samples on hard problems (high P_fail) receive exponentially larger weight than negative samples, inducing implicit exploration. A positive-only variant (BoN-RLB(P)) uses a change of measure to eliminate negative examples entirely. The paper also establishes a co-scaling law between optimal temperature T* and sample size N*, showing that harder problems require higher T* and correspondingly larger N*, which directly informs the training hyperparameter choices.

## Results

On Hendrycks MATH with Gemma 2B, BoN-RL-V (N'=32) improves Bo-32 accuracy from 26.8% (base) to 30.8%, while BoN-RL-S improves pass@32 from 60.0% to 67.0%. On HumanEval, BoN-RLB(P) improves pass@16 from 61.6% to 67.1%. Training with N'=32 also generalizes to N=1, improving direct accuracy from 22% to 26%, and the gains transfer to held-out benchmarks (Functional MATH, MathOdyssey) and different evaluation temperatures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022), which performs self-training on correctly generated BoN responses, the proposed BoN-aware methods consistently outperform: BoN-RL-S achieves 67.0% pass@32 on MATH versus STaR's 55.0%, and BoN-RLB/BoN-RLB(P) reach 60.0% pass@32. The paper attributes STaR's inferiority to its failure to (i) use negative samples for implicit exploration, (ii) re-weight examples by difficulty, and (iii) account for the importance-sampling gap between the base and BoN policies. Against inference-unaware RL with verifier feedback (RL-V, based on Lee et al., 2023), BoN-RL-V substantially outperforms on BoN accuracy (30.8% vs. a lower baseline), while the inference-unaware RL-V suffers from reward hacking. Standard RL with N'=1 degrades pass@N performance (e.g., pass@16 drops to 59.8% on HumanEval), confirming that optimizing for a single-sample reward without accounting for the BoN inference strategy is counterproductive for multi-sample evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Best-of-N inference strategy (Cobbe et al., 2021; Brown et al., 2024) and the variational approximation of BoN policies (Gui et al., 2024), but departs from prior work by explicitly incorporating the inference strategy into the training objective rather than treating it as a post-hoc design choice. It extends standard SFT and REINFORCE-style RL by replacing the base policy with the BoN policy in the optimization, connecting to energy-based policy gradients (Rafailov et al., 2024) and the STaR self-training paradigm (Zelikman et al., 2022). The co-scaling analysis of temperature and sample size builds on empirical observations from inference-time compute scaling (Snell et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024) but formalizes the exploration-exploitation tradeoff in a way that directly informs algorithm design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a principled framework for aligning LLM training with a specific inference strategy, showing that this alignment yields consistent gains over inference-unaware fine-tuning across math and code benchmarks. The co-scaling law (power-law in N with temperature-dependent parameters) provides practical guidance for jointly tuning inference hyperparameters. However, the gains are moderate (e.g., 26.8%→30.8% on Bo32 MATH), experiments are limited to 2B/9B Gemma models, and the framework is restricted to BoN rather than more complex inference algorithms (MCTS, multi-turn revision). Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether it generalizes to non-binary rewards, and how it interacts with more sophisticated inference-time strategies.
