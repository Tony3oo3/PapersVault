## Problem

Query-aware dynamic token sparsity methods (e.g., Quest, DoubleSparse) select critical KV cache tokens per attention computation, but this per-query selection introduces substantial overhead—DS spends nearly 50% of its token-sparse attention runtime on selection alone. No prior work exploits the fact that critical token indices are highly similar across neighboring layers, heads, and adjacent queries, leaving a large source of redundant selection computation unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt that is first processed through a dense prefilling phase to build the full KV cache, then enters an autoregressive decoding phase where only a subset of critical KV tokens (N_c out of n) are loaded per attention computation. During prefilling, the system additionally computes critical token indices for all layers and heads and runs a greedy algorithm to produce a per-batch sharing configuration (which layers/heads share indices with which). During decoding, attention blocks that are assigned to share simply load precomputed indices from a small O(N_c) integer cache rather than recomputing them, and the final output is the standard generated token sequence.

## Method

HShare introduces a three-level hierarchical sharing framework that reuses critical KV cache token indices across layers, heads, and adjacent queries. The key empirical insight is that the set of critical tokens (identified via sink tokens, a recent window, and top-z middle tokens selected using 4-bit quantized heavy-channel approximate attention weights) exhibits high pairwise similarity (measured by set overlap) across neighboring layers, heads within a layer, and consecutive queries. A greedy algorithm (Algorithm 1) computes a similarity matrix over all layer (or head) index sets after prefilling, then iteratively pairs the most similar layer/head and assigns the higher-indexed one to reuse the lower-indexed one's indices, producing a per-batch sharing configuration applied throughout decoding. At the query level, every a consecutive queries share a single critical token set. The total sharing ratio is the product of independent layer, head, and query ratios (Eq. 4), and the configuration is recomputed online per batch after prefilling at negligible cost, reducing the number of critical-token selections from L×H×n to a fraction determined by the chosen ratios.

## Results

On GSM8K (flexible/strict) and COQA, HShare (7/8-3/4-1/2) achieves 0.7536/0.7468 and 0.7105 on LLaMA3-70b, 0.1835/0.1721 and 0.5982 on LLaMA2-7b-chat, and 0.3287/0.3233 and 0.6667 on Mistral-7b, matching or slightly exceeding DoubleSparse (DS) while using only 33.6% of DS's selection complexity. On LongBench (16 English datasets), HShare achieves an average score of 32.13 vs. 32.11 for DS and 32.90 for the original dense model. In efficiency, HShare delivers up to 8.6× self-attention latency reduction over FlashAttention-2 and up to 2.7× end-to-end throughput improvement over GPT-fast.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HShare clearly outperforms the two KV-cache eviction baselines (StreamingLLM and H2O) across all three models and both benchmarks; for example, on LLaMA3-70b GSM8K, HShare (0.7536) beats H2O (0.5815) by 17 points and StreamingLLM (0.3912) by 36 points. Against the query-aware dynamic sparsity baselines, HShare (7/8-3/4-1/2) matches or slightly exceeds DS on GSM8K for all three models (e.g., 0.7536 vs. 0.7415 on LLaMA3-70b) while requiring only 33.6% of DS's selection complexity (0.021T vs. 0.0625T), and substantially outperforms Quest (e.g., 0.7536 vs. 0.4602 on LLaMA3-70b GSM8K). On LongBench, HShare (32.13 avg) edges out DS (32.11) and significantly surpasses Quest (31.35) and H2O (31.38). In efficiency, HShare achieves up to 8.6× lower self-attention latency than FlashAttention-2 and up to 2.7× higher end-to-end throughput than GPT-fast; at BS=16, Seqlen=4k, HShare is 17.48% faster than DS in throughput. The only case where HShare trails a baseline is COQA on Mistral-7b, where DS scores 0.6693 vs. HShare's 0.6667, a negligible 0.26-point gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

HShare extends the query-aware token sparsity paradigm introduced by Quest and DoubleSparse, which dynamically select critical KV cache tokens per attention block but incur significant per-query selection overhead. The key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that critical token indices exhibit substantial similarity across layers, heads, and adjacent queries, enabling a hierarchical sharing mechanism that reuses indices rather than recomputing them. Unlike KV cache eviction methods (StreamingLLM, H2O) that discard historical information, HShare retains the full KV cache and only selectively loads critical portions, combining the accuracy benefits of query-aware sparsity with the efficiency of fixed-pattern approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.6× self-attention speedup and 2.7× end-to-end throughput improvement are practically significant for long-context LLM serving, and the hierarchical sharing concept is orthogonal to other optimizations like quantization and speculative decoding, making it composable. However, the sharing ratio selection remains heuristic and task-dependent, and the paper acknowledges that dynamically determining optimal sharing configurations per task is left as future work. The approach's reliance on prefill-phase similarity computation also means it adds a small one-time cost that may not be negligible for very short sequences or streaming scenarios.
