## Problem

In contrastive training of text embeddings, the temperature parameter in InfoNCE creates an unresolved trade-off: lower temperatures improve retrieval accuracy but degrade post-training compression, while higher temperatures do the reverse. No prior work connected this trade-off to the intrinsic dimensionality of the embedding space or proposed a principled mechanism to simultaneously optimize for both absolute performance and compressibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250389/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts text passages or queries (up to 1024 tokens, tokenized with a 100k tiktoken vocabulary) and produces 1024-dimensional dense embeddings from a 356M-parameter CodeSage transformer. The critical output flexibility is that a single trained model yields usable representations at multiple compression levels without retraining: full 1024-dim at 32-bit precision, truncated to 256 or 512 dimensions via random feature selection, or binarized to 1-bit per dimension (32× size reduction) using the sign function with Hamming similarity for retrieval.

## Method

The paper's central finding is that the temperature τ in InfoNCE directly governs the intrinsic dimensionality of the learned embedding space: lower τ produces higher intrinsic dimensionality (measured via PCA, e.g., 654 vs. 430 principal components needed for 95% variance at τ=0.04 vs. 0.3), which in turn makes embeddings harder to compress. To resolve the retrieval-versus-compression trade-off, the paper introduces temperature aggregation (summing InfoNCE losses at multiple τ values, Eq. 6) and temperature specialization (assigning a small τ at the 256-dim MRL level for retrieval quality and a larger τ at 1024-dim for compressibility, Eq. 8), both combined with Matryoshka Representation Learning. The best configuration (TempSpecMRL with binary re-ranking) matches the best single-temperature retrieval score while achieving 99% quality retention at 32× size reduction, effectively decoupling the two objectives that were previously in conflict.

## Results

The proposed temperature aggregation methods (TempAgg, TempAggMRL, TempSpecMRL) achieve near-best retrieval performance (46.0–46.6 nDCG@10 on MTEB) while substantially improving compression quality retention over single-temperature baselines. Specifically, TempSpecMRL reaches 99.0% quality retention with binarization + re-ranking (32× size reduction) and 97.1% retention when truncating to 256 dimensions (4× reduction), compared to 97.6% and 93.2% respectively for the best single-temperature retrieval model (τ=0.04). Binarization alone at τ=0.1 achieves 32× compression with 97.8% retention, outperforming 4× truncation (96.4% retention) at the same temperature. Intrinsic dimensionality drops from 654 (τ=0.04) to 430 (τ=0.3), confirming the link between lower dimensionality and better compressibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three temperature aggregation/specialization methods against two single-temperature InfoNCE baselines (τ=0.04 for best absolute retrieval, τ=0.1 for best compressed retrieval) and against MRL (Matryoshka Representation Learning) as a training-aware compression method. TempAggMRL nearly matches the best retrieval score (46.6 vs. 46.7 for τ=0.04) while improving 256-dim retention from 93.2% to 96.9% and binary retention from 95.3% to 97.1%, effectively resolving the retrieval-vs-compression trade-off. TempSpecMRL achieves the best quality retention (97.1% at 256, 99.0% binary re-rank) at a small cost in absolute retrieval (46.0 vs. 46.7). MRL training consistently improves over plain InfoNCE at all temperatures: it raises full-size retrieval by ~0.5 points, improves 256-dim retention by 2–3 points, and slightly boosts binary retention (e.g., 95.3→96.1 at τ=0.04). MRL also reduces intrinsic dimensionality across all temperatures (e.g., 654→626 at τ=0.04), further supporting the intrinsic-dimensionality–compressibility correlation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the temperature analysis originally developed for vision contrastive learning (Wang & Liu, 2020; Kukleva et al., 2023) to text embeddings, building directly on InfoNCE as the standard contrastive loss and on Matryoshka Representation Learning (Kusupati et al., 2024) as a compression-aware training objective. Its key conceptual departure is establishing an empirical link between the temperature hyperparameter, the intrinsic dimensionality of the resulting embedding space (measured via PCA), and post-training compression quality—a connection not previously drawn in either the vision or text embedding literature. The proposed temperature aggregation and specialization losses (Eqs. 6–8) combine these two threads into a unified training recipe that simultaneously optimizes for full-size retrieval and compressed representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single hyperparameter (temperature) controls both absolute retrieval quality and compressibility provides practitioners with a concrete, interpretable knob for navigating the performance-vs-storage trade-off in retrieval systems, where embedding storage costs are a major practical concern. The temperature aggregation methods achieve near-best retrieval while retaining 99% quality at 32× compression (binarization + re-ranking), which is directly deployable. Open questions include the mechanistic explanation for why higher temperature reduces intrinsic dimensionality, whether the findings generalize beyond the single CodeSage architecture and English-centric evaluation used here, and why binarization consistently outperforms truncation as a compression strategy—a question the authors flag for future work.
