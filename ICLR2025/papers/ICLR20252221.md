## Problem

Existing ΔΔG predictors for protein complexes rely on computationally heavy IPA-style backbones (as in AlphaFold2), making high-throughput mutation screening impractical, and the enormous combinatorial mutation space (20^N) remains largely unexplored for identifying accessible evolutionary regions. Prior antibody optimization methods focus on generating mutations via deep generative models, leaving the complementary problem of efficiently selecting and explaining favorable mutations under-explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252221/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a wild-type protein complex 3D structure and a mutation set (residue positions + target amino acid types) as input, encoding each residue with E(3)-invariant node features (type, dihedral/torsion angles, mutation flag) and pairwise features (relative sequential/spatial distances, backbone-atom direction encodings in local coordinate frames). Sequences/structures are cropped into patches of 32 residues centered on a seed residue. Outputs include a scalar ΔΔG score, per-site mutation importance probabilities, per-site 20-dimensional amino acid preference distributions, and a ranked list of candidate antibodies with their predicted ΔΔG scores.

## Method

Light-DDG replaces the heavy IPA-style backbone with a lightweight 4-layer, 4-head Transformer (128 hidden dim) and is trained in two stages: supervised pre-training on SKEMPI-Aug (670k mutations annotated by Prompt-DDG via K-fold cross-augmentation to prevent data leakage), followed by fine-tuning on SKEMPI v2.0 with a joint loss combining ground-truth ΔΔG regression and knowledge distillation from Prompt-DDG. For explanation, a Mutation Explainer computes approximate Shapley values via an iterative coarse-to-fine algorithm that progressively concentrates sampling on promising residue sites and mutations, reducing the combinatorial cost of exact Shapley computation. For optimization, the learned site-importance and per-site preference distributions guide stochastic sampling of 10,000 candidate mutations, which are rapidly scored by Light-DDG to identify the best variants—requiring no generative model or additional functional annotations.

## Results

On SKEMPI v2.0 (3-fold cross-validation), Light-DDG achieves a per-structure Pearson of 0.5440 and per-structure Spearman of 0.5004, improving over the previous SOTA Prompt-DDG by 15.45% and 17.55% respectively. Overall metrics also improve substantially: Pearson 0.7429 (+9.70%), Spearman 0.6767 (+14.50%), RMSE 1.3837 (−9.01%), MAE 0.9697 (−9.96%), and AUROC 0.7935 (+4.85%). For antibody optimization on SARS-CoV-2, Uni-Anti (Directed) achieves a best ΔΔG of −2.872 kcal/mol for joint CDR-H1/2/3 optimization, outperforming the best generative baseline dyMEAN (−1.942) by nearly 1 kcal/mol. The method also delivers 89.7× inference acceleration over Prompt-DDG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Light-DDG outperforms all 18 baselines on all 7 evaluation metrics, with the largest margins on the per-structure metrics where it surpasses Prompt-DDG (the teacher) by 15.45% (Pearson) and 17.55% (Spearman). Among pre-training-based methods, ProMIM and Prompt-DDG are the closest competitors on overall metrics, but Light-DDG still leads by 9.70% on overall Pearson and 14.50% on overall Spearman. For antibody optimization on SARS-CoV-2 (Table 6), Uni-Anti's directed mutation strategy achieves −2.872 kcal/mol for joint CDR-H1/2/3, beating dyMEAN (−1.942), DiffAb (−1.828), MEAN (−1.653), and RefineGNN (−1.310) by 0.9–1.6 kcal/mol. In the antibody screening task (Table 5), Uni-Anti achieves the best average rank of 2.40 across five known favorable mutations, compared to 4.00 for Prompt-DDG and 3.40 for RDE-Network. On the broader SAbDab benchmark (500 antibodies, CDR-H3), Uni-Anti reaches −3.87 kcal/mol versus −3.05 for dyMEAN and −2.84 for DiffAb, confirming consistent gains across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in ΔΔG prediction that relies on heavy IPA-style backbones (as in AlphaFold2) with unsupervised pre-training on PDB-REDO, instead proposing a lightweight 4-layer Transformer trained via supervised pre-training on a newly constructed augmented dataset (SKEMPI-Aug) and fine-tuned with knowledge distillation from Prompt-DDG. It also shifts the antibody optimization pipeline from generation-centric approaches (RefineGNN, MEAN, dyMEAN) that require deep generative models and Iterative Target Augmentation, to a selection-centric framework where a fast ΔΔG predictor directly serves as both optimizer and explainer. The Mutation Explainer repurposes Shapley value computation (Shapley et al., 1953) for the novel purpose of estimating per-residue, per-mutation marginal contributions in a coarse-to-fine iterative scheme, reducing the combinatorial explosion of exact Shapley computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 89.7× inference acceleration with simultaneous performance gains over the teacher makes high-throughput mutation screening practically feasible, and the released SKEMPI-Aug dataset (670k annotated mutations) provides a valuable supervised pre-training resource for the community. The conceptual finding that architectural simplification can be compensated by data augmentation plus distillation challenges the assumption that ΔΔG prediction requires coordinate-encoding backbones. Key limitations include the absence of wet-lab validation, reliance on ΔΔG as the sole evolutionary prior, and the fact that the Mutation Explainer's Shapley estimates are approximations whose accuracy degrades with larger mutation spaces. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-antibody proteins, how it combines with generative models for controllable design, and whether other priors (e.g., kinetic parameters) can be integrated.
