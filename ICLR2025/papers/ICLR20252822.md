## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) research has focused primarily on stable optimization to prevent model collapse under distribution shifts, but has largely neglected enhancing the model's representational capacity during adaptation. The paper identifies that TTA models exhibit linear mode connectivity—analogous to fine-tuned models—yet this property has not been exploited to enrich representations at test time, leaving a gap between offline domain-generalization techniques (weight averaging, high-dropout fine-tuning) and online TTA.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled out-of-distribution RGB test images in online batches (ranging from batch size 1 to 64) and produces a single class prediction per image via the ensemble network f_e. Only the affine parameters of normalization layers are updated during adaptation, while the backbone remains frozen. The ensemble network f_e requires only one additional feedforward pass per sample compared to the adapter f_a, keeping inference cost to a single model forward pass at deployment.

## Method

TTE builds a teacher-student framework where an adapter network f_a is adapted online and an ensemble network f_e is maintained via an adaptive exponential moving average whose momentum is modulated by the reverse KL divergence between f_e and f_a (lower momentum when representations diverge, actively incorporating new information). Dropout is applied to the penultimate layer of f_a to encourage diverse collaboration among existing representations, which is then transferred to f_e through weight-space averaging. To prevent model collapse from destabilizing the ensemble, TTE introduces a de-biased distillation objective that tracks accumulated prediction bias via EMA of first-order batch statistics and subtracts a bias-proportional correction from f_e's outputs before computing the reverse KL divergence. The combined objective L_tte = L_tta + L_rkl is integrated seamlessly into existing TTA methods (Tent, SAR, DeYO) by simply adding the distillation term to their original loss, requiring only a few additional lines of implementation.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (level 5, Label Shifts), TTE integrated with DeYO achieves 50.7% average accuracy on ResNet50-GN (+9.9% over DeYO alone) and 65.2% on ViTBase (+3.9%). Under the more extreme Batch Size 1 setting, DeYO+TTE reaches 50.5% (ResNet50-GN, +8.3%) and 65.2% (ViTBase, +2.1%). In the most challenging continual TTA with non-i.i.d. distributions, TTE(w. DeYO) achieves 49.7% on ResNet50-GN and 61.6% on ViTBase, while all other baselines collapse to near-zero accuracy in later adaptation stages. On natural distribution shifts (ImageNet-S/R/V2), TTE consistently outperforms DeYO by +0.6% to +1.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTE is integrated into three representative TTA baselines (Tent, SAR, DeYO) and compared against CoTTA for continual adaptation. Against Tent, TTE delivers the largest absolute gains: +19.4% on ResNet50-GN and +12.6% on ViTBase (Label Shifts), and +24.1% / +12.7% under Batch Size 1, largely because Tent is highly prone to model collapse (e.g., 1.0% on Shot noise for ViTBase) which TTE's de-biased distillation prevents. Against SAR, TTE provides more modest but consistent improvements (+7.2% ResNet, +3.3% ViT in Label Shifts); notably, Tent+TTE even surpasses SAR+TTE in Batch Size 1 (48.7% vs 38.5% on ResNet), suggesting sharpness-aware optimization offers limited benefit at very small batch sizes. Against DeYO (the strongest baseline), TTE adds +9.9% (ResNet) and +3.9% (ViT) in Label Shifts, and +8.3% / +2.1% in Batch Size 1. Against CoTTA in continual TTA with non-i.i.d. distributions, TTE(w. DeYO) achieves 49.7% (ResNet) and 61.6% (ViT) average accuracy, while CoTTA collapses to 2.2% and 3.4% respectively, demonstrating that the ensemble + robust distillation framework is far more stable under sequential distribution shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: test-time adaptation (TTA) for online distribution-shift handling and robust fine-tuning under linear approximation for offline domain generalization. It empirically demonstrates that TTA models exhibit linear mode connectivity (a property previously established for fine-tuned models by Wortsman et al. and Rame et al.), enabling the transfer of weight-space ensemble and high-dropout strategies from the offline setting into the online TTA loop. Unlike prior teacher-student TTA frameworks (CoTTA, ROTA) that rely on dense augmentations and near-constant high momentum (0.999), TTE uses adaptive momentum modulated by KL divergence and requires no augmentation, reducing computational overhead. The de-biased reverse-KL distillation objective departs from standard entropy minimization by explicitly regularizing against accumulated class bias, addressing model collapse from a representation-diversity angle rather than the optimization-stability angle taken by SAR and DeYO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TTE offers a plug-and-play, computationally lightweight module (one extra forward pass) that consistently improves multiple existing TTA methods across four datasets and four challenging scenarios, making it broadly applicable to future TTA developments. The empirical confirmation of linear mode connectivity in TTA provides a theoretical justification for weight-space ensembling in the online setting, potentially simplifying future adaptation designs. However, gains on intrinsic distribution shifts (ImageNet-V2) are negligible, and the de-biasing scheme shows sensitivity to true-biased scenarios (consecutive same-class samples), leaving open questions about robustness in highly skewed streaming settings. The dramatic continual-TTA results (near-zero → 50–60% accuracy) are partly attributable to baseline collapse, so the practical ceiling of TTE in stable regimes remains to be established.
