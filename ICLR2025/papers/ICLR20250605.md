## Problem

Single-particle cryo-EM images contain rich information about conformational heterogeneity, but existing heterogeneous reconstruction methods either recover only discrete consensus states or, when using per-residue deformation with deep learning, get trapped in local minima due to the extremely low signal-to-noise ratio and the fact that only 2D projections are observed. This limits the ability to recover the full continuous distribution of protein conformations from real experimental data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250605/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of noisy 2D cryo-EM particle images, a nominal 3D protein structure (typically from AlphaFold, optionally fitted into a consensus reconstruction via backprojection), and per-image pose estimates (rotation matrix R and translation vector t) obtained from standard tools like RELION or cryoSPARC. It outputs, for each image, a full 3D atomic structure (Cα coordinates), a learned segment decomposition of the protein chain (GMM parameters shared globally), and per-segment rigid body transformations (quaternion rotation + translation) that are specific to each image's conformation.

## Method

CryoSPHERE is a VAE that jointly learns a protein segmentation and per-image conformational deformations. A Gaussian mixture model with N_segm components is fitted on the 1D residue index axis to partition the amino-acid chain into segments, with a temperature-annealed soft assignment ensuring near-hard partitioning while remaining differentiable. The decoder maps a per-image latent variable z_i to one rigid body transformation (axis-angle rotation + translation) per segment, and each residue's coordinates are updated by weighted contributions from all segments proportional to its GMM membership. The encoder is a standard neural network producing a Gaussian posterior over z_i, and training minimizes an ELBO combining a correlation-based image reconstruction loss (accounting for unknown preprocessing) with KL divergences on both the local latent and the global GMM parameters. The key design choice—constraining motion to rigid body movements of learned segments rather than per-residue deformations—reduces the effective dimensionality, provides strong structural priors that resist noise, and yields interpretable domain-level motions.

## Results

On the synthetic MD dataset (bacterial phytochrome, SNR 0.001), cryoSPHERE achieves a correlation of 0.74 between predicted and ground-truth inter-domain distances, demonstrating per-image conformational recovery at extremely low SNR. In the FSC comparison (Figure 4), cryoSPHERE outperforms both cryoDRGN and cryoStar at both the 0.5 and 0.143 resolution cutoffs. On the real EMPIAR-12093 Pfr dataset (highest noise and dynamics), cryoSPHERE recovers the biologically expected 'scissoring' motion with zero structural clashes, while cryoStar produces disordered, clashing conformations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against cryoDRGN (volume-based VAE), cryoSPHERE achieves superior FSC at both 0.5 and 0.143 cutoffs on the MD dataset; the authors attribute this to (i) fitting the AlphaFold base structure into a consensus map (boosting 0.5 cutoff) and (ii) acting at the structure level rather than density level (boosting 0.143 cutoff), since cryoDRGN underestimates the protein opening and produces noisy volumes. Against cryoStar (per-residue deformation VAE), cryoSPHERE wins on FSC at the 0.143 cutoff, which the authors attribute to rigid-body segment motion being more noise-resistant than independent per-residue translations. The most dramatic separation appears on EMPIAR-12093 Pfr: cryoStar fails entirely, producing a disordered upper domain with random motion and numerous structural clashes (Figure 7), while cryoSPHERE recovers the expected scissoring conformational change with zero clashes. On the Pr state of EMPIAR-12093, both cryoSPHERE and cryoStar agree on the upper-domain rotation, indicating the methods are comparable when noise and dynamics are moderate. Overall, cryoSPHERE dominates in the high-noise, high-dynamics regime where structural constraints from rigid-body segmentation provide the strongest regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CryoSPHERE extends the structure-based cryo-EM reconstruction line (AtomVAE, CryoFold, cryoStar) by replacing per-residue deformation with learned rigid-body transformations on GMM-defined segments, reducing the effective dimensionality of the motion space. It departs from e2gmm, which also uses a GMM but in 3D space and requires user-defined segmentation, by learning both the segmentation and the rigid-body motion end-to-end via a VAE. The volume-based methods (cryoDRGN, 3DFlex, CryoAI) are positioned as complementary rather than competing, since they lack the structural priors that cryoSPHERE exploits, and the paper demonstrates a debiasing pipeline that combines cryoSPHERE's latent space with volume reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By constraining motion to rigid-body transformations on learned segments, cryoSPHERE achieves meaningful single-particle conformational recovery at extremely low SNR (0.001) where per-residue methods fail, and it recovers per-image conformations (correlation 0.73–0.74 with ground truth) that prior structure-based methods like AtomVAE could not. Key limitations remain: volume-based debiasing breaks down for the most dynamic states (Pfr phytochrome), compositional heterogeneity is undetected, and the method requires a plausible nominal structure from AlphaFold or similar. Open questions include whether learned segments correlate with 'omics'-scale domain annotations and whether the framework can predict rare intermediate states and their kinetics.
