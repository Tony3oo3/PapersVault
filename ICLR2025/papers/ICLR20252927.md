## Problem

Existing open-source LMMs are largely confined to single-image tasks, and multi-image, video, and 3D scenarios are addressed by separate task-specific models, preventing cross-scenario generalization and emerging capabilities. No unified framework exists that handles all these interleaved visual settings within a single model while preserving single-image performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252927/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an interleaved sequence of image tokens and text, where images are encoded by SigLIP at 384×384 resolution; for single-image tasks, high-resolution images are split into multiple low-resolution patches (any-resolution design from LLaVA-NeXT), and for video, frames are sampled (10 frames during training, up to 16 at inference). Two inference formats are supported: 'in-the-front' (all image tokens prepended before the prompt) and 'interleaved' (image tokens placed at their original <image> positions in the text). Output is free-form text, covering VQA answers, captions, edit instructions, video descriptions, and 3D scene reasoning.

## Method

The core insight is treating the image-text interleaved format as a universal data template that unifies multi-image, multi-frame (video), multi-view (3D), and multi-patch (single-image) scenarios into one training pipeline. The authors build M4-Instruct (1,177.6K samples, 14 tasks, 41 datasets) and train a LLaVA-NeXT architecture (SigLIP 400M encoder + 2-layer MLP projector + Qwen 1.5 LLM at 0.5B/7B/14B) via three key techniques: (1) continuing from a pre-fine-tuned single-image checkpoint rather than from stage-1 pre-training, (2) mixing both in-the-front and interleaved token layouts during training for format robustness, and (3) jointly training across all four data scenarios so that complementary semantics transfer between tasks. A DPO stage on video data further boosts video performance to surpass the 34B LLaVA-NeXT-Video baseline.

## Results

On the proposed LLaVA-Interleave Bench (multi-image), LLaVA-Interleave 7B achieves 58.6 in-domain average and 42.8 out-domain average, surpassing the previous best open-source model Mantis (7B) by +19.0 and +3.5 points respectively, while GPT-4V remains stronger on out-domain (57.8). On video benchmarks, LLaVA-Interleave 7B(DPO) attains 3.90 on VDD and 4.12 on VideoChat-GPT CO, matching or exceeding the 34B LLaVA-NeXT-Video-DPO. On 3D benchmarks, LLaVA-Interleave 14B reaches 59.2 average, outperforming GPT-4V (34.6) and 3D-LLM (22.9) by large margins using only multi-view images without point clouds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest open-source multi-image baseline Mantis (7B), LLaVA-Interleave 7B improves in-domain average from 39.6 to 58.6 (+19.0) and out-domain from 39.3 to 42.8 (+3.5), with particularly large gains on Spot the Difference (17.6→37.1), Multi-image VQA (52.5→87.5), and Puzzle (25.7→48.7). Compared to GPT-4V, LLaVA-Interleave 14B surpasses it on in-domain (62.3 vs 39.2) but trails on out-domain (44.3 vs 57.8), with GPT-4V leading on MathVerse (60.3 vs 33.4) and SciVerse (66.9 vs 32.7). On video, LLaVA-Interleave 7B(DPO) matches the 34B LLaVA-NeXT-Video-DPO on VDD (3.90 vs 3.84) and VideoChat-GPT CO (4.12 vs 4.12) while using a much smaller model, and outperforms VideoChat2 (7B) on NExT-QA (78.2 vs 68.6). On 3D, LLaVA-Interleave 14B (59.2 avg) dramatically outperforms 3D-LLM (22.9), Point-Bind&LLM (22.5), and even GPT-4V (34.6), despite not using point cloud inputs. For single-image tasks, LLaVA-Interleave 7B maintains or slightly improves upon LLaVA-NeXT-Image 7B (73.3 vs 72.3 avg), confirming that multi-scenario training does not degrade core capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA-NeXT architecture (vision encoder + projector + LLM) by introducing an interleaved image-text data format as a universal template that unifies multi-image, video (multi-frame), 3D (multi-view), and single-image (multi-patch) scenarios under one training pipeline. Prior work treated these as separate problems requiring dedicated models—Mantis for multi-image instruction tuning, LLaMA-VID and LLaVA-NeXT-Video for video, and 3D-LLM/Point-LLM for 3D—whereas LLaVA-Interleave argues that the interleaved format naturally subsumes all four as special cases. The training strategy of continuing from a single-image fine-tuned checkpoint (rather than from pre-training) and mixing 'in-the-front' and 'interleaved' token placements during training are practical departures from prior multi-image tuning recipes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The M4-Instruct dataset (1.17M samples across 41 datasets) and LLaVA-Interleave Bench provide the community with the first comprehensive training and evaluation resources for unified multi-scenario LMMs, reducing the need to train separate models per modality. The demonstrated cross-task transfer (e.g., single-image humor analysis extending to multi-image, or image Twitter-post generation extending to video) suggests that joint multi-scenario training yields compositional generalization, though these are shown only qualitatively. Open questions include whether the unification approach scales to even more modalities (audio, point clouds), whether the slight performance degradation on non-video tasks after DPO (Table 14) is a fundamental trade-off, and whether the 3D results generalize beyond the relatively small in-domain benchmarks used.
