## Problem

Existing training-free methods for consistent multi-image generation rely on cross-frame self-attention, which works for single subjects but suffers from inter-character attention leakage when scaling to multiple characters—tokens from one subject attend to another, causing a dog to appear like a duck. Additionally, even when general appearance consistency is achieved, fine-grain details such as faces, ears, and wing colors remain visibly inconsistent across storyboard frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251746/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt describing a story (e.g., 'a cute bear and lion meeting, talking, playing on a beach') and a number of desired storyboard frames B. A multi-modal LLM decomposes this into per-frame textual descriptions and per-character bounding-box masks, which are then fed into a region-based diffusion sampler. The output is a sequence of B RGB images in which the same characters maintain both identity and fine-grain appearance across all frames, generated in ~8.7 seconds on a single H100 GPU without any per-subject training or optimization.

## Method

StoryBooth combines three training-free components. First, multi-modal chain-of-thought reasoning localizes each character's bounding-box region in every storyboard frame, providing the spatial layout needed for bounded attention. Second, a bounded cross-frame self-attention layer modifies the standard attention mask so that each token attends only to tokens belonging to the same subject across all B frames (concatenated into a single BN×BN attention matrix), with a random dropout term (β_d) that stochastically allows attention to global/background tokens to preserve image quality. Third, a cross-frame token-merging layer uses the resulting attention map to identify the most similar token for each source token in other frames and blends them (O_merge = (1−α)O_src + αO_target), enforcing hard fine-grain consistency; an early negative-α unmerging step during the first 50 diffusion timesteps pushes features apart to recover pose variance.

## Results

On the overall evaluation, StoryBooth achieves the best text-to-image alignment (VQAScore 0.706) and competitive character consistency (DreamSim 0.811) across single- and multi-subject storyboard generation. The gains are most pronounced in the multi-subject setting, where StoryBooth reaches 0.633 T2I and 0.846 CC, substantially outperforming the next-best methods (ConsiStory: 0.388/0.785; StoryDiffusion: 0.407/0.779). Inference time is 8.7 seconds per storyboard on a single H100 GPU, roughly 30× faster than optimization-based methods (DreamBooth-LoRA: 312 s; Textual Inversion: 455 s) while remaining close to the fastest training-free baseline StoryDiffusion (6.5 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StoryBooth outperforms all seven baselines on overall T2I alignment (0.706 vs. 0.583–0.647 for the next-best) and achieves the best multi-subject character consistency (0.846), a clear margin over ConsiStory (0.785) and StoryDiffusion (0.779). Against the closest training-free competitor StoryDiffusion, StoryBooth gains +0.108 T2I and +0.057 CC overall, with the largest gap in the multi-subject T2I score (0.633 vs. 0.407), confirming that bounded cross-frame attention effectively reduces inter-character leakage. IP-Adapter is the only method that marginally edges out StoryBooth on overall CC (0.812 vs. 0.811), but it lags significantly on T2I (0.607 vs. 0.706) and suffers from reduced prompt alignment in multi-character scenes. Compared to optimization-based methods (DreamBooth-LoRA, Textual Inversion), StoryBooth improves both T2I and CC while being 30–50× faster. In the single-subject setting, StoryBooth is competitive but not dominant: StoryDiffusion achieves slightly higher T2I (0.788 vs. 0.781) and ConsiStory slightly higher CC (0.791 vs. 0.775), indicating the bounded-attention and token-merging mechanisms are most beneficial when multiple characters are present. A human user study (Table 2) corroborates these findings, with StoryBooth preferred over StoryDiffusion in 46.4% of T2I comparisons and 58.3% of consistency comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free consistent generation line initiated by StoryDiffusion and ConsiStory, which use unbounded cross-frame self-attention to enforce subject consistency across storyboard frames. The key departure is a diagnostic analysis showing that this unbounded attention causes inter-character leakage when multiple subjects are present, and a solution that combines region-based planning (from Yang et al., 2024a) with a bounded cross-frame self-attention mask (inspired by within-image bounded attention in Dahary et al., 2024) plus a cross-frame token-merging layer adapted from ViT token merging (Bolya et al., 2022). Unlike optimization-based personalization methods (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) or encoder-based approaches (IP-Adapter, BLIP-Diffusion), StoryBooth requires no per-subject training or inference-time optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode (self-attention leakage) that explains why existing training-free consistency methods degrade with multiple characters, and offers a simple, training-free fix that is ~30× faster than optimization-based alternatives. This makes multi-character storyboard generation practical for applications like visual storytelling and multi-shot video anchor generation without expensive per-subject fine-tuning. Open questions include scalability beyond two characters, handling occluded or overlapping subjects where region masks become ambiguous, and whether the bounded-attention + token-merging recipe transfers to video diffusion models for temporally consistent multi-character video generation.
