## Problem

For constant step-size SGD with Polyak-Ruppert averaging on strongly convex problems, the best known non-asymptotic root-MSE bound has a second-order term of order O(n^{-2/3}), which is suboptimal. While the Root-SGD algorithm of Li et al. (2022) achieves the best known O(n^{-3/4}) rate, it relies on a complex two-timescale scheme with multiple restarts. This paper asks whether a simpler modification—Richardson-Romberg extrapolation—can match this rate while also identifying the precise leading term of the MSE that aligns with the minimax-optimal asymptotic covariance matrix.

## Input / Output

The method requires a strongly convex, 4-times continuously differentiable objective f with bounded third and fourth derivatives, an unbiased stochastic gradient oracle ∇F(θ,ξ), and a known total iteration budget n (since the step size γ = n^{-1/2} must be set a priori). It produces the Richardson-Romberg estimator θ̄ₙ^(RR) = 2θ̄ₙ^(γ) − θ̄ₙ^(2γ), constructed by running two parallel SGD chains with step sizes γ and 2γ driven by the same noise sequence, and provides explicit non-asymptotic root-MSE and p-th moment error bounds (p ≥ 2) with a sharp leading term √(Tr Σ_ε^⋆)/√n and a second-order term of order n^{-3/4}.

## Method

The analysis treats constant step-size SGD iterates as a time-homogeneous Markov chain and establishes geometric ergodicity with respect to a non-standard weighted Wasserstein semimetric whose cost function c(θ,θ′) = ‖θ−θ′‖(‖θ−θ⋆‖+‖θ′−θ⋆‖+2√2 τ₂√γ/√μ) scales linearly in γ, yielding tighter remainder bounds than the standard p-Wasserstein distance. The Richardson-Romberg estimator θ̄ₙ^(RR) = 2θ̄ₙ^(γ) − θ̄ₙ^(2γ) cancels the O(γ) bias in the stationary mean (the term γΔ₁ from the Taylor expansion of ∇f around θ⋆), reducing the second-order error from O(n^{-2/3}) to O(n^{-3/4}). The proof combines a summation-by-parts representation of H⋆(θ̄ₙ−θ⋆), a fourth-order Taylor expansion isolating the cubic remainder G(θ), and a novel Rosenthal-type inequality for additive functionals of the geometrically ergodic Markov chain that exploits the γ-linear scaling of the cost function. Using the same noise sequence for both chains is critical: it preserves the leading variance term W = n^{-1}Σε_{k+1}(θ⋆) so that RR extrapolation only inflates second-order variance components, keeping the leading MSE term at the minimax-optimal rate.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Corollary 7) establishes that the Richardson-Romberg estimator θ̄ₙ^(RR) with constant step size γ = n^{-1/2} achieves a root-MSE bound of √(Tr Σ_ε^*)/√n + C(L,μ)/n^{3/4} + R₄, where the leading term matches the minimax-optimal asymptotic covariance and the second-order term O(n^{-3/4}) is the best known rate among first-order methods. Theorem 9 extends this to p-th moment bounds for p ≥ 2, yielding E^{1/p}[‖H*(θ̄ₙ^(RR) − θ*)‖^p] ≤ c₁√(Tr Σ_ε^*)·p^{1/2}/√n + C(L,μ,p)/n^{3/4} + R₅. Numerical experiments on f(θ) = θ² + cos θ with Gaussian noise confirm the predicted O(n^{-3/4}) scaling of the second-order term in the root-MSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Richardson-Romberg SGD (RR-SGD) matches the best-known second-order rate O(n^{-3/4}) (δ = 1/4) previously achieved only by the more complex Root-SGD algorithm of Li et al. (2022), which requires a two-timescale stochastic approximation scheme with multiple restarts. In contrast, RR-SGD uses a simple constant step size γ = n^{-1/2} combined with Polyak-Ruppert averaging and a single Richardson-Romberg correction (θ̄ₙ^(RR) = 2θ̄ₙ^(γ) − θ̄ₙ^(2γ)), making it substantially simpler to implement. Compared to standard Polyak-Ruppert averaged SGD (which achieves only δ = 1/6 with γ = n^{-2/3}), RR-SGD improves the second-order rate from O(n^{-2/3}) to O(n^{-3/4}) at the cost of running two parallel chains with the same noise sequence. The paper notes that its assumptions are slightly stronger than those of Li et al. (2022): it requires f to be 4-times continuously differentiable with bounded 3rd and 4th derivatives (A2), whereas Root-SGD assumes only Lipschitz continuity of the Hessian. The leading term √(Tr Σ_ε^*)/√n is explicitly identified and matches the minimax-optimal CLT covariance, a feature not present in the earlier RR-based analyses of Huo et al. (2023) or Zhang & Xie (2024).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) non-asymptotic analysis of Polyak-Ruppert averaged SGD (Moulines & Bach 2011; Gadat & Panloup 2023), which previously yielded a second-order error rate of O(n^{-2/3}) or O(n^{-5/4}); (2) the Markov chain framework for constant step-size SGD (Dieuleveut et al. 2020), which provides the geometric ergodicity and moment bounds underpinning the analysis; and (3) Richardson-Romberg extrapolation from numerical SDE simulation (Talay & Tubaro 1990), repurposed here to cancel the leading O(γ) bias in the stationary distribution. The key departure from Li et al. (2022) Root-SGD is that the same best-known δ=1/4 rate is achieved via a simple two-chain extrapolation rather than a two-timescale algorithm with multiple restarts, and the analysis is extended to p-th moment bounds (p≥2) for the first time in the nonlinear strongly convex setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result demonstrates that the best-known non-asymptotic rate for first-order methods (δ=1/4) is accessible through a minimal modification of standard SGD, removing the need for the algorithmic complexity of Root-SGD. The extension to p-th moment bounds with the same O(n^{-3/4}) second-order term, combined with a sharp leading term matching the minimax-optimal covariance matrix, provides a complete finite-sample picture previously available only for linear stochastic approximation. Open questions include whether the 4th-order smoothness assumption (bounded 3rd and 4th derivatives) can be relaxed, whether the analysis extends to dependent (Markovian) noise, and whether the strong convexity requirement can be dropped in favor of Polyak-Łojasiewicz conditions.
