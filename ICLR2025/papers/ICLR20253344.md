## Problem

Test-time adaptation (TTA) for LiDAR-based 3D object detection remains unexplored, and existing mean-teacher TTA methods treat all historical checkpoints uniformly via exponential moving average, failing to selectively leverage diverse long-term knowledge from different adaptation stages. Additionally, prior domain shift studies have not addressed the challenging 'cross-corruption' scenario where cross-dataset gaps (e.g., different LiDAR beam counts, object scales) and sensor/weather corruptions co-occur simultaneously in real-world deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of LiDAR point clouds (batch size 8) at test time, with the 3D detector (SECOND, PV-RCNN, or DSVT) pretrained on a source dataset and no ground-truth labels available during adaptation. It outputs 3D bounding boxes (center coordinates, dimensions, orientation) evaluated via KITTI AP_BEV and AP_3D metrics at easy/moderate/hard difficulty. Adaptation runs in a single online pass with a fixed model bank of K=5 checkpoints, updated every L=112 batches, requiring only ~17.4 GB GPU memory.

## Method

MOS maintains a bank of K historical checkpoints and, for each incoming test batch, assembles them into a 'super model' via weighted parameter averaging, where the weights (synergy weights) are computed as the inverse of a generalized Gram matrix. Each Gram matrix entry is the product of two tailored similarity functions: a feature-level similarity measuring linear independence via the rank (approximated by nuclear norm) of concatenated BEV feature maps, and an output-level similarity measuring prediction discrepancy via the inverse of Hungarian matching cost on 3D box sets. The super model generates pseudo labels that supervise a single-iteration self-training step for the current model, while a dynamic bank update periodically evicts the checkpoint with the lowest mean synergy weight and inserts the newly trained model. This mechanism selectively amplifies checkpoints holding unique knowledge (e.g., rare object classes) and suppresses redundant ones, outperforming uniform EMA aggregation by 39.8% in moderate AP_3D and reducing memory by 85% compared to assembling 20 checkpoints.

## Results

On the cross-dataset Waymo→KITTI task, MOS achieves 81.90/64.16 AP_BEV/AP_3D (moderate), closing 91.12%/79.79% of the gap to the fully supervised Oracle (83.29/73.45) and outperforming the multi-epoch UDA method ST3D (61.83 AP_3D). On nuScenes→KITTI, MOS reaches 71.13/51.11 AP_BEV/AP_3D, a 7.4% AP_3D gain over the best TTA baseline CoTTA. Across eight corruption types (KITTI→KITTI-C, heavy level), MOS achieves a mean AP_3D of 74.16/61.82/57.48 (easy/moderate/hard), surpassing all baselines by 3.73–5.56%. In the most challenging cross-corruption scenario (Waymo→KITTI-C), MOS surpasses the best TTA baseline by 67.3% and direct inference by 161.5% in AP_3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOS decisively outperforms all four TTA baselines (Tent, CoTTA, SAR, MemCLR) across every evaluated shift type. On Waymo→KITTI, MOS achieves 64.16 AP_3D versus CoTTA's 35.34 (+81.5%), Tent's 30.12, SAR's 30.39, and MemCLR's 29.83. Against the multi-epoch UDA method ST3D (61.83 AP_3D), MOS still wins by 2.33 points while requiring only a single-pass online adaptation rather than multiple training epochs. The weakly supervised SN method (59.20 AP_3D) is also surpassed by MOS. On the corruption benchmark (KITTI→KITTI-C), MOS leads CoTTA by 3.11 AP_3D at moderate difficulty (61.82 vs. 58.71), with the largest margin on incomplete echoes (+23.98% at hard level). In the hybrid cross-corruption setting (Waymo→KITTI-C), all TTA baselines degrade severely (e.g., 3.51 AP_3D for incomplete echoes without adaptation), while MOS maintains substantially higher performance, confirming that mean-teacher-based methods fail to retain diverse long-term knowledge under compound domain shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) paradigm—well established for image classification (Tent, CoTTA, SAR) and 2D object detection (MemCLR)—to the previously unexplored domain of LiDAR-based 3D object detection. It departs from the standard mean-teacher/EMA framework by replacing uniform checkpoint averaging with an inverse generalized Gram matrix that computes data-dependent synergy weights, drawing on model-soup and Fisher-weighted averaging ideas but adapting them to the label-free, streaming TTA setting. The pseudo-labeling self-training loop follows ST3D/ST3D++ for 3D detection, while the cross-corruption evaluation scenario (cross-dataset shift + sensor/weather corruption co-occurring) is a new benchmark formulation not present in prior UDA or TTA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of TTA for 3D LiDAR detection, and the cross-corruption benchmark (Waymo→KITTI-C) fills a practical gap by modeling the hybrid shifts that real deployments actually face. The finding that 3 diverse checkpoints outperform 20 recent ones by 18.4% in AP_3D while using 85% less memory challenges the common assumption that recency dominates in continual adaptation. The main limitation is computational: K models must each infer every test batch, making the approach expensive for real-time deployment; the authors acknowledge that storing and assembling only a subset of parameters (e.g., selected layers) is a natural next step. Whether the synergy-weight formulation generalizes beyond SECOND/PV-RCNN-style voxel detectors to transformer-based 3D detectors remains open.
