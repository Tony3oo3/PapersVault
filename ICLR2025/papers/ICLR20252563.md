## Problem

Previous identifiable representation learning methods for dynamical systems (e.g., LEAP, TDRL) can recover latent states but cannot identify the underlying nonlinear transition function, relying instead on linear approximations or inverse-transition estimation. This limits their ability to reliably predict complex future behavior, since without a provably identifiable forward dynamics model, predictions degrade rapidly over longer horizons. This paper is the first to establish identifiability guarantees for the transition function itself, not just the latent states.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional observation sequences (video frames or motion-capture joint angles) together with a discrete regime label that serves as the auxiliary variable u modulating the noise distribution. At inference, an initial-condition encoder processes the first T_ic frames to sample z_0, after which a sequential RNN+MLP encoder infers process noise s_t at each step conditioned on all past embeddings and the previous latent state, and the learned transition function propagates z_t forward. Outputs include per-step latent state estimates, process noise estimates, and the learned forward transition function f(z_{t-1}, s_t) that enables multi-step future prediction in both latent and observation space.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is an augmented dynamics framework in which both the transition function f and emission function g are augmented with previous time steps to form bijective maps, enabling identifiability of the transition function via a two-step argument: first identifying latent states (Theorem 1, extending Yao et al. 2021), then identifying the process noise s_t through a sufficient-variability assumption on the noise prior (Theorem 2, the main result). The key assumptions are (i) conditionally independent process noise whose distribution is modulated by an auxiliary variable u (regime label or previous noise), and (ii) bijectivity of the augmented transition function. Practically, the model is a VAE where the noise prior p(s|u) is factorized into independent 1D neural spline flows (enforcing conditional independence and allowing multi-modal distributions), the transition function is decomposed into K separate MLPs (one per latent dimension, enforcing the single-noise-per-state assumption), and the noise prior deliberately does not depend on the previous state to preserve identifiability. Training maximizes a β-ELBO with a standard Gaussian prior on z_0 and the conditional flow prior on s_t, using reparameterized sampling through the sequential forward pass.

## Results

On the MOCAP-SINGLE dataset (75-step input, 150-step prediction horizon), the method achieves TEST-MSE of 3.82±0.40, outperforming Latent SDE (4.03±0.20), Latent NODE (5.98±0.28), and NODE (22.49±0.88). On MOCAP-MULTI and MOCAP-SHIFT (multi-subject, non-i.i.d. settings), the method achieves 19.6±1.2 and 36.0±1.9 TEST-MSE respectively, dramatically outperforming MONODE (57.7±9.8 and 58.0±10.7) and LEAP-LIN (57.9±10.5 and 51.1±2.8). On the cartpole dataset, the method achieves MCC[z_input] of 0.95±0.02 and MCC[x_future[8]] of 0.91±0.02, the highest among all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 10 baselines across all datasets. On MOCAP-SINGLE, it beats the closest competitor Latent SDE (Li et al., 2020) by 0.21 MSE (3.82 vs. 4.03) and NODE (Chen et al., 2018) by a factor of ~6× (3.82 vs. 22.49). On the multi-subject MOCAP-MULTI and MOCAP-SHIFT datasets, the advantage is most dramatic: the method achieves 19.6 and 36.0 MSE versus MONODE (Auzina et al., 2024) at 57.7 and 58.0, a ~3× improvement, and versus LEAP-LIN (Yao et al., 2021) at 57.9 and 51.1. On cartpole, the method achieves MCC[z_input] of 0.95 vs. CRU (Schirmer et al., 2022) at 0.85 and LEAP-LIN at 0.81, and MCC[x_future[8]] of 0.91 vs. LEAP-LIN at 0.78. Notably, LEAP-NP and TDRL (Yao et al., 2021; 2022) cannot produce future predictions at all since they only approximate the inverse transition function, making them N/A on all MSE and future MCC metrics. The method also wins on the synthetic dataset with MCC[z_input] of 0.94 vs. LEAP-NP at 0.89, and MCC[s_input] of 0.64 vs. TDRL at 0.42, confirming superior recovery of both latent states and process noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiable representation learning line (LEAP, TDRL) that recovers latent states in dynamical systems by proving identifiability of the transition function itself, not just the states. It adapts the augmented dynamics framework from Independent Innovation Analysis (Morioka et al., 2021), which operated in observation space, into the latent space, and borrows the noise-to-state inference structure from Franceschi et al. (2020). The key theoretical departure is the bijectivity assumption on the augmented transition function combined with sufficient variability in process noise, which together enable recovery of the dynamics up to element-wise transformations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework with a provable identifiability guarantee for the transition function in nonlinear latent dynamical systems, closing a gap where prior methods could identify states but not the dynamics governing them. The practical consequence is substantially better long-horizon prediction and sample-efficient domain adaptation (50 sequences suffice to match a full-data baseline). Key limitations include the bijectivity requirement (ruling out intrinsically stochastic transitions) and the need for sufficient variability in noise across regimes; the authors note that understanding performance under assumption violations and demonstrating gains in downstream tasks like model-based policy learning remain open.
