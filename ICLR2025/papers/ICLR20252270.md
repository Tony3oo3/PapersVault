## Problem

When LLMs outperform humans on certain tasks, aligning strong student models requires weak-to-strong alignment using noisy supervision from weaker teachers. Existing strong-to-weak methods assume teachers are stronger and ignore iterative improvement of teacher quality, while self-alignment methods collapse when repeatedly trained on self-generated familiar data. The paper asks: how can we continually improve alignment of strong students through contrastive preference optimization without collapse?

## Input / Output

The framework takes a held-out question set Q_w2s from alignment datasets (HH-RLHF, PKU-SafeRLHF), K weak teacher models (Llama2-7b, Mistral-7b, Llama3-8b) initialized on positive behavioral data, and a strong student (Llama2-70b) initialized on weak teacher labels. At each iteration, it consumes text prompts to generate contrastive preference pairs — unfamiliar positive responses (from other agents, filtered by perplexity for the student) and familiar negative responses (from dedicated negative agents fine-tuned on negative behavioral data) — and outputs updated model weights for both the strong student and all weak teachers via DPO optimization.

## Method

MACPO is a multi-agent iterative framework that alternates between reinforcing unfamiliar positive behaviors and penalizing familiar negative ones using DPO. Two complementary strategies drive each iteration: (1) mutual positive behavior augmentation, where weak teachers generate candidate responses that are filtered by the strong student's perplexity to select high-quality unfamiliar positives for the student, while the student's own outputs serve as unfamiliar positives for the teachers; and (2) hard negative behavior construction, where separate negative agents (initialized on negative behavioral data) generate self-induced negative responses that are harder to distinguish and thus more effective as penalty targets. Both the strong student and all K weak teachers are iteratively updated with a combined DPO + SFT loss (γ=0.2) on these contrastive pairs, while negative agents remain frozen after initialization. The key insight is that cross-agent positive behavior prevents the model collapse seen in self-alignment, and self-induced negative behavior provides harder negatives than externally sourced ones.

## Results

On the HH-RLHF and PKU-SafeRLHF datasets, MACPO (iter3) achieves an average reward model score of 67.85, substantially outperforming the best self-alignment baseline Self-rewarding (iter2) at 56.57 and the best weak-to-strong baseline Confident loss at 49.07. MACPO improves from 59.47 (iter1) to 67.85 (iter3) across three iterations without collapse, while SPIN degrades after iter1 (51.62→46.02) and Self-rewarding degrades after iter2 (56.57→55.62). On GPT-4 pairwise evaluation, MACPO wins 87–91% of comparisons against the strongest baselines in each category (RLCD, Self-rewarding, Confident loss) across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACPO outperforms all five baseline categories. Against strong-to-weak methods, MACPO (iter3, avg 67.85) beats RLAIF (53.61) by +14.24 and RLCD (55.26) by +12.59 on average reward model score; on GPT-4 pairwise evaluation, MACPO wins 87% vs RLAIF and 69% vs RLCD on HH-Helpful. Against self-alignment methods, MACPO surpasses SPIN (best iter1: 51.62) by +16.23 and Self-rewarding (best iter2: 56.57) by +11.28, and critically avoids the collapse that causes SPIN to drop from 51.62 to 46.02 and Self-rewarding from 56.57 to 55.62 after additional iterations. Against weak-to-strong baselines, MACPO improves over Naive SFT (49.41) by +18.44 and Confident loss (49.07) by +18.78, with GPT-4 win rates of 89% and 87% respectively on HH-Helpful. Human evaluation confirms these gaps: MACPO wins 91% vs Confident loss and 80% vs Self-rewarding on HH-Helpful, all with p<0.05.

## Relation to Prior Work

MACPO builds directly on DPO as its optimization backbone but repositions it within a multi-agent iterative training loop that departs from both strong-to-weak alignment (RLAIF, RLCD) and self-alignment (SPIN, Self-rewarding). The weak-to-strong generalization framework of Burns et al. (2023) is the closest prior work, but that method only reinforces positive behavior from frozen weak teachers via a confidence loss for classification tasks; MACPO extends this to generative alignment by iteratively improving both teachers and students and by explicitly penalizing familiar negative behavior. The multi-agent framing draws on LLM-based multi-agent systems literature, but unlike prior work that employs multiple agents at inference time, MACPO co-optimizes all agents during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs increasingly outperform human annotators on specific tasks, the weak-to-strong alignment setting becomes practically unavoidable, yet prior methods either assume stronger teachers or suffer from model collapse when iterating on self-generated data. The finding that mutual learning between heterogeneous agents (7B teachers, 70B student) simultaneously improves all parties and that scaling the number of weak teachers extends the number of stable iterations provides a concrete recipe for alignment in regimes where no single strong supervisor exists. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to helpfulness/harmlessness, reliance on fine-tuning on negative data as the sole mechanism for inducing negative behavior, and the use of relatively weak baselines (Naive SFT, Confident loss) for the weak-to-strong category, leaving open whether the gains persist against stronger iterative baselines.
