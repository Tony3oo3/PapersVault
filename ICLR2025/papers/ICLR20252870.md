## Problem

Existing knowledge editing methods for LLMs suffer from a previously uncharacterized failure mode on complex tasks like multi-hop reasoning: edited models assign abnormally high probabilities to the edit target even when it is an implausible answer. The paper identifies this as 'Editing Overfit,' attributing it to the prevailing editing paradigm's overemphasis on the direct p(s,r)→o* correspondence with limited edit samples, and shows that standard overfitting mitigations (norm constraints, batch editing, data augmentation) are largely ineffective.

## Input / Output

LTI takes an edit sample (s, r, o, o*), the base LLM, and a target MLP layer l, and produces a modified recall vector v* = v_s^l + h that replaces the standard ROME/MEMIT recall vector in the rank-one weight update. Unusually, the method requires a forward pass of the *unedited* model with the new knowledge prepended as an in-context prefix (e.g., 'Imagine that Microsoft is founded by Steve Jobs') to generate target subject representations and output distributions that serve as constraint targets during optimization.

## Method

LTI (Learn the Inference) is a plug-and-play strategy that replaces the standard recall vector computation in locate-then-edit methods with a constrained optimization over a learnable offset h. It introduces three joint losses: (1) a Subject Representation Constraint aligning the subject's last-token representation at a deeper layer m > l between the edited model and the context-guided unedited model via KL divergence; (2) an Output Distribution Constraint aligning the full output token distributions at the final position; and (3) a New Knowledge Constraint ensuring the edit target o* is predicted under random prefixes. The key insight is that by constraining intermediate representations to match those produced by in-context learning, the edited model learns to *recall* new knowledge through the same multi-step mechanism as unedited LLMs rather than memorizing a flat input-output mapping. The strategy is model-agnostic and can be combined with ROME, MEMIT, FT, or MEND.

## Results

On GPT-J, ROME-LTI reduces the Direct Probability (DP) on Multi-hop Reasoning from 41.03% (ROME) to 11.12%, while improving the Editing Overfit Score (EOS) from 34.91 to 69.83. Similarly, MEMIT-LTI reduces DP from 25.80% to 16.35% and improves EOS from 50.49 to 61.44. On GPT-2 XL, ROME-LTI cuts Multi-hop DP from 23.32% to 10.08% (EOS: 46.11→65.94), and MEMIT-LTI reduces DP from 16.75% to 8.05% (EOS: 57.06→72.02). Efficacy scores remain at 100% for both LTI variants, confirming that overfitting mitigation does not compromise edit success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROME-LTI substantially outperforms ROME on all four overfit tasks: Multi-hop DP drops from 41.03% to 11.12% and EOS rises from 34.91 to 69.83 on GPT-J; Subject Specificity EOS improves from 6.77 to 28.38. MEMIT-LTI similarly improves over MEMIT, reducing Multi-hop DP from 25.80% to 16.35% and raising EOS from 50.49 to 61.44. FT exhibits the most severe overfitting (Multi-hop DP=42.51%, Prefix Distraction EOS=4.58%), while FT-L and MEND show milder overfitting but at the cost of significantly lower Paraphrase scores (45.44% and 54.17% vs. 99.27% for ROME), suggesting their edits are partially unsuccessful. LTI maintains 100% Efficacy while achieving these overfitting reductions, and slightly reduces Paraphrase AMS (99.27→96.27 for ROME-LTI), a trade-off the authors deem acceptable given the large gains on complex reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the locate-then-edit paradigm (ROME, MEMIT) and the interpretability literature on LLM knowledge recall (Geva et al., 2023; Meng et al., 2022a), which established the two-step mechanism of subject aggregation in shallow layers and extraction in deeper layers. It departs from the standard editing objective—maximizing P(o*|p(s,r))—by reframing the problem as one of learning the inference process rather than the input-output mapping, drawing on in-context learning as a behavioral template. The EVOKE benchmark extends COUNTERFACT and COUNTERFACTPLUS with overfit-specific tasks (Prefix Distraction, Subject/Relation Specificity), and the LTI strategy is positioned as a plug-and-play module composable with any existing editing method (FT, MEND, ROME, MEMIT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unifying diagnosis—Editing Overfit—for the well-observed but previously unexplained failure of all parameter-modifying editing methods on complex reasoning tasks, and shows that standard overfitting mitigations (norm constraints, batch editing, data augmentation) are largely ineffective in this setting. LTI offers a practical, model-agnostic fix, but its reliance on the base model's in-context learning capability and its design for structured (s,r,o,o*) triples limit applicability to non-structured or poorly-ICL-capable models. Open questions include whether the multi-stage constraint framework generalizes to unstructured knowledge editing, whether it transfers to larger or differently-architected models, and whether the slight Paraphrase degradation is acceptable in safety-critical editing scenarios.
