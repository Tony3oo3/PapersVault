## Problem

Existing online conformal inference methods (ACI, OGD, PID) update prediction set thresholds using only a binary miscoverage indicator, treating a barely-missed prediction identically to a wildly-wrong one. This ignores the magnitude of the revealed error (the distance between the non-conformity score and the current threshold), causing slow correction after distribution shifts and unnecessarily wide prediction sets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252942/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the method receives a sequential observation X_t and a pre-trained base predictor f̂_t, then computes a non-conformity score s_t = S_t(X_t, Y_t) once the label is revealed. It outputs an asymmetric conformal prediction set [Ŷ_t − q_t^l, Ŷ_t + q_t^u] where upper and lower thresholds are each calibrated at α/2. The threshold q_t is updated online via a partially smoothed gradient step that incorporates both the binary miscoverage indicator and a continuous error-magnitude term.

## Method

ECI modifies the standard OGD update on the quantile loss by adding an Error Quantification (EQ) term (s_t − q_t)∇f(s_t − q_t), where f is a sigmoid smoothing function. The key design choice is to keep the actual miscoverage indicator err_t in the update (rather than replacing it with f(s_t − q_t) as in fully smoothed variants), which avoids the smoothing bias while still gaining continuous feedback about error magnitude. The update rule becomes q_{t+1} = q_t + η[err_t − α + (s_t − q_t)∇f(s_t − q_t)]. Two variants are proposed: ECI-cutoff, which suppresses the EQ term when |s_t − q_t| is below an adaptive threshold to prevent over-compensation, and ECI-integral, which aggregates weighted past errors for more stable coverage. Theoretically, ECI achieves a finite-sample coverage guarantee under fixed learning rates (every miscoverage step is followed by at least N−1 coverage steps) and a distribution-free miscoverage bound under arbitrary adaptive learning rates, requiring only bounded scores and a bounded smooth function.

## Results

Across four real-world datasets (Amazon stock, Google stock, electricity demand, Delhi temperature) and three base predictors (Prophet, AR, Theta), ECI and its variants maintain coverage at the target 90% level while producing the tightest prediction sets. On Amazon stock with the Prophet model, ECI-integral achieves an average set width of 42.01 versus 55.15 for OGD, 52.56 for PID, and ∞ for ACI, while maintaining 89.8% coverage. With the AR model on Google stock, ECI-cutoff achieves an average width of 19.84 compared to 33.76 for OGD and 64.88 for PID. ECI-cutoff and ECI-integral consistently achieve the shortest median widths across all dataset–model combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECI variants dominate on set width across nearly all dataset–model combinations. Against ACI, the advantage is stark: ACI produces infinite average widths (due to unbounded prediction sets) on every dataset, while ECI-integral keeps widths finite and shortest. Against OGD and SF-OGD, ECI-cutoff and ECI-integral reduce average width by roughly 15–25% (e.g., 42.01 vs. 55.15 on Amazon/Prophet; 19.84 vs. 33.76 on Google/AR) while maintaining comparable coverage. Against decay-OGD, ECI is substantially tighter (42.01 vs. 97.22 on Amazon/Prophet). Against Conformal PID, ECI wins decisively with AR and Theta base models (e.g., 17.12 vs. 59.22 on Amazon/AR), but PID's scorecaster gives it an edge with the weaker Prophet model on electricity demand (0.207 vs. 0.384); the authors show that adding a scorecaster to ECI (ECI+scorecaster) recovers and surpasses PID (0.193 vs. 0.207). Overall, ECI-cutoff achieves the shortest average width in the majority of settings, with ECI-integral a close second.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online conformal inference framework initiated by ACI (Gibbs & Candès, 2021) and its OGD-based variants, which update prediction set thresholds using only binary miscoverage feedback. The key departure is the introduction of a continuous error-quantification (EQ) term derived from partially smoothing the quantile loss, providing magnitude-aware feedback that accelerates adaptation to distribution shifts. The method is conceptually distinct from Conformal PID (Angelopoulos et al., 2023b), which relies on integration and a trained scorecaster; ECI replaces both with a single smooth gradient term, yielding tighter sets without requiring an auxiliary model. Theoretically, the paper builds on the distribution-free regret analysis of online learning but provides a novel finite-window coverage guarantee (Theorem 1) that every miscoverage is followed by at least N−1 coverage steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EQ term addresses a practical limitation of all prior online conformal methods: binary feedback causes slow correction after large miscoverages, leading to unnecessarily wide prediction sets during distribution shifts. The distribution-free guarantees (no exchangeability or mixing assumptions) make the approach broadly applicable to real-world time series with arbitrary dependence. However, the improvements over strong baselines like OGD and PID are modest in most settings, and the method still requires bounded non-conformity scores (Assumption 1). Open questions include whether the EQ mechanism extends naturally to multivariate or structured prediction tasks, and whether the partially-smoothed design can be generalized beyond the Sigmoid choice without the bias issues of full smoothing.
