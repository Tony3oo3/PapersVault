## Problem

Existing extrapolative protein design methods learn gradient directions from noisy pairwise rankings, which may be insufficient to capture the fitness landscape beyond the training region. The paper hypothesizes that pairwise models fail to capture three-way relations (how two alternatives fare relative to a seed), leading to poor extrapolation. The gap addressed is the lack of a mechanism to distill higher-order (triplet) ranking information directly into a generative model to improve extrapolation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a seed protein sequence from the training fitness region and iteratively edits it over T=10 rounds, sampling N candidate sequences per seed per iteration using top-k (k=10) and top-p (p=0.95) sampling with temperature 0.7 (without scorer) or 1.0 (with scorer). At each iteration, M sequences (10,000 for AAV, 2,000 for GFP) are retained as seeds for the next round. The final output is a pool of M candidate sequences whose fitness is evaluated in the extrapolation region (beyond y_max of training), with an optional scorer function used to select the best sequence per seed at each iteration.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline: first, a Prot-T5-XL local editor is fine-tuned via MLE on pairs (seed, improved sequence) to learn first-order gradient directions; second, the pairwise model is aligned using hard triplets via EXO (reverse KL offline preference optimization). Preference triplets (prompt, desired, undesired) are constructed following two principles—'Don't go backward' (f_s(x_l) < f_s(prompt) < f_s(x_w)) and 'Don't get stuck at the same fitness' (f_s(x_l) ≈ f_s(prompt) < f_s(x_w))—and are ranked by a model-aware hardness score S = log P_pair(x_l|prompt) − log P_pair(x_w|prompt), selecting the top 100K hardest examples where the pairwise model prefers the undesired over the desired response. The EXO loss minimizes the reverse KL between an empirical softmax over the log-likelihood ratio (P_θ/P_pair) and a near-deterministic target that assigns probability 1−ε to the desired response, effectively performing mode-seeking distribution matching rather than DPO's mean-seeking forward KL. This model-dependent data distillation focuses learning on the exact regions of sequence space where the pairwise model's gradient estimate is wrong, analogous to correcting harmful generations in LLMs.

## Results

On the medium-difficulty AAV dataset, EXO achieves 85.07% extrapolation (vs. 3.49% for Align-plm, the best baseline) and a top-100 fitness of 13.18 (vs. 9.43 for ICE). On medium-difficulty GFP, EXO reaches 92.92% extrapolation and top-100 fitness of 4.04 (vs. 0.00% and 2.12 for Align-plm). On hard-difficulty AAV, EXO achieves 52.75% extrapolation and fitness of 10.95, outperforming Align-plm (20.76%, 9.01). The one exception is hard-difficulty GFP, where Align-plm achieves higher extrapolation (54.45% vs. 24.27%), though EXO still attains the best top-100 fitness (2.81 vs. 2.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against non-scorer baselines, EXO dominates: it outperforms unconditional Prot-T5-XL sampling (1.64% extrapolation on AAV-hard) by 31×, ICE (5.58%) by ~9×, and Align-plm (20.76%) by 2.5× on AAV-hard, with even larger margins on medium splits (85.07% vs. 3.49% for Align-plm on AAV-medium). The sole exception is GFP-hard extrapolation, where Align-plm (54.45%) exceeds EXO (24.27%), though EXO still leads on top-100 fitness (2.81 vs. 2.55). Among scorer-based methods, EXO+scorer surpasses LatProtRL (64.82% → 84.04% on AAV-hard) and BiGGS (16.80% → 84.04%), while BiGGS retains an edge on GFP-hard extrapolation (99.53% vs. 50.91%) but EXO+scorer matches its top-100 fitness (3.79 vs. 3.83) and achieves closer distance to ground truth (1.73 vs. 3.48 mutations). In the preference-learning algorithm comparison (Table 6), EXO outperforms DPO (74.39% on AAV-medium), AOT (50.78% on AAV-hard), IRPO (47.26% on GFP-medium), IPO, NCA, and SPPO on 3 of 4 datasets; IRPO narrowly wins on GFP-hard (55.15% vs. 24.27%). Overall, EXO is state-of-the-art on 3 of 4 datasets when compared against all scorer-based and non-scorer baselines combined.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the edit-based extrapolative protein design line (ICE, Align-PLM) by arguing that pairwise ranking is insufficient to capture the fitness gradient and that higher-order triplet relations are needed. It draws directly from the LLM preference-alignment literature, specifically adopting EXO's reverse-KL offline optimization objective (Ji et al., 2024) and the DPO/IPO family of methods, and repurposes them for protein sequence generation. The key conceptual departure is the 'data distillation' step: rather than training on all available triplets, the method identifies model-dependent hard triplets where the pairwise local editor makes its worst mistakes, mirroring the curriculum-learning intuition from LLM alignment. This positions the work at the intersection of protein language modeling and offline preference optimization, a combination not previously explored in the protein design literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Extrapolative protein design is critical for engineering therapeutics (e.g., AAV capsids for gene therapy, GFP variants for imaging), and the demonstrated 17× improvement in extrapolation percentage on AAV-medium over the best pair-based baseline is practically significant. However, the method underperforms Align-PLM on the GFP hard split (24.27% vs 54.45% extrapolation), revealing that triplet distillation does not uniformly resolve the extrapolation challenge and that dataset-specific fitness landscape structure matters. Open questions include whether higher-order relations (quadruples via Plackett-Luce) yield further gains, whether the approach generalizes beyond the two well-studied proteins tested, and whether wet-lab validation confirms the in-silico gains, since the scorer's own accuracy degrades in the extrapolation region as the authors acknowledge.
