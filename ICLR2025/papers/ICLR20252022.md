## Problem

Protein language models achieve strong performance on structure and function prediction but remain opaque black boxes that domain experts cannot control, interpret, or debug. Existing conditional pLMs that append property tags can selectively ignore those tags during generation and offer no mechanism to understand which concepts drive predictions or to identify learned spurious correlations. This limits their use in high-stakes applications like drug discovery where precise, verifiable control over biophysical properties is essential.

## Input / Output

The model consumes masked protein sequences (amino acid tokens with [CLS]/[EOS] delimiters, truncated to 512 residues) paired with 718 predefined concept annotations—14 Biopython-computed biophysical properties (GRAVY, charge, aromaticity, etc.) and 704 SWISS-PROT biological annotations (organism, molecular function, cellular component). During intervention, the user sets target concept values and the model outputs a regenerated protein sequence; the linear decoder additionally exposes a per-concept, per-amino-acid weight matrix that directly quantifies each concept's contribution to every token prediction.

## Method

CB-pLM inserts a concept bottleneck module between a standard BERT encoder and a linear decoder: the [CLS] hidden state is mapped to 718 concept predictions (each scaled by a learnable embedding), an orthogonality network produces a complementary 'unknown' embedding, and a single linear layer concatenates both to predict masked tokens. Training minimizes a weighted sum of masked language modeling loss, concept MSE loss (with missing-value masking), and an orthogonality loss penalizing cosine similarity between known and unknown embeddings, using independent training to prevent concept leakage. For interventions, a single-step integrated gradients approximation (gradient × (input − mask)) identifies the top-5% tokens most influential on a target concept, which are then masked and regenerated under the user-specified concept value. This architecture yields 3× larger concept shifts than conditional baselines, near-perfect control accuracy, and immediate debuggability via linear weight inspection.

## Results

On the Mason antibody dataset, CB-pLM achieves perplexity of 2.48 (650M) and 2.50 (3B), outperforming ESM2 (3.41 and 3.01 respectively) while not requiring conditioning tags at inference. For single-concept control, CB-pLM achieves near-perfect intervention accuracy (outperforming other conditional models by >15%) and a 3× larger average change in the target concept value. For multi-concept control (GRAVY + charge at pH 7), CB-pLM reaches 94% joint control accuracy, exceeding the second-best model by 34 percentage points. In the Siltuximab GRAVY-reduction case study, CB-pLM produces the second-best concept distribution shift (behind LaMBO-2) while preserving protein naturalness as validated by TAP metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard masked language models (LBSTER, ESM2), CB-pLM matches or exceeds perplexity at scale: at 650M and 3B parameters, CB-pLM (2.48, 2.50) substantially outperforms ESM2 (3.41, 3.01), and at 24M it beats LBSTER (4.29 vs. 8.20), though at 150M LBSTER retains a slight edge (2.97 vs. 3.20). Against conditional baselines C-pLM and CC-pLM (which receive ground-truth concept tags as input), CB-pLM achieves comparable perplexity without any tag input, and decisively wins on control: it achieves 3× larger concept-value shifts and ~16% higher intervention accuracy across 14 biophysical concepts. In the Siltuximab redesign task, CB-pLM ranks second to LaMBO-2 (explicitly trained for GRAVY optimization) in GRAVY distribution shift while maintaining protein naturalness, outperforming PropEn, WJS+ESM2, and the Hydrophilic Resample baseline. The key trade-off is that at smaller scales (24M–150M), tag-based conditional models achieve slightly lower perplexity, but CB-pLM's architectural constraints (orthogonality loss, linear decoder) do not degrade performance at scale and unlock control, interpretability, and debugging capabilities unavailable to all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Concept Bottleneck Generative Models (CBGMs, Ismail et al. 2023) from image generation to masked protein language models, and builds on the original Concept Bottleneck Model architecture (Koh et al. 2020) for the CB-module and independent training scheme. It departs from prior conditional protein language models (C-pLM, CC-pLM) that steer generation via input tags by instead forcing concepts through an internal bottleneck layer with an orthogonality loss, making concepts a necessary intermediate rather than an optional input. The approach also contrasts with SAE-based mechanistic interpretability (Bricken et al. 2023; Templeton et al. 2024), which requires post-hoc feature discovery, whereas CB-pLM encodes concepts by design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that interpretability constraints (a concept bottleneck layer with 700+ concepts) do not degrade pre-training perplexity or downstream performance, addressing a key barrier to deploying interpretable models in high-stakes drug discovery. The 3× control improvement over conditional baselines and the ability to match SOTA single-concept-optimized models (LaMBO-2) with a general-purpose model trained on 700+ concepts suggest a practical path toward trustworthy, debuggable protein design tools. Open questions include whether the architecture generalizes to unseen concept combinations on real proteins (demonstrated only on Color-MNIST), how well disentanglement holds at scale, and whether the unknown embedding can be further reduced without performance loss.
