## Problem

Existing 3D affordance detection methods rely on fixed, predefined label sets and short-text classification paradigms, limiting their ability to generalize to unseen affordance types or understand complex natural language instructions in open-world scenes. Additionally, the scarcity of 3D affordance datasets constrains effective training of large-scale models that could leverage rich world knowledge for reasoning-based detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252590/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N points in R^{n×3}, either full-view or partial-view) and a free-form query reasoning instruction (e.g., 'Where should I grip to pour from this mug?') as input. It produces a binary per-point affordance mask (M_a ∈ R^N) delineating the functional region relevant to the instruction. The LLM first generates a text response containing a special <AFF> token; the mask is then decoded from that token's hidden embedding combined with dense point features, rather than being predicted directly by a segmentation head.

## Method

3D-ADLLM introduces an 'embedding-as-affordance' paradigm: a new <AFF> token is added to the LLM vocabulary (Phi-3.5-mini, fine-tuned with LoRA), and when the model generates this token, its last-layer hidden state is projected and fed into a two-layer Affordance Decoder (AFD) that cross-attends with dense point features from a Point Transformer backbone to produce the segmentation mask. A Point-BERT encoder (pre-trained with ULIP-2) converts the point cloud into a sequence of point tokens that are projected into the LLM's token space, enabling joint text-and-point reasoning. Training follows a multi-stage strategy: first, a novel Referring Object Part Segmentation (ROPS) pre-training task on PartNet equips the decoder with general object-part segmentation knowledge; second, fine-tuning on the IRAS task (42K instruction-mask pairs from 3D AffordanceNet) instills reasoning-based affordance detection. A sample unbalanced loss factor (ω_i = (max(c)/c_i)^{1/4}) reweights per-class mask losses to handle the large variation in affordance region sizes (5%–40% of the point cloud).

## Results

On the zero-shot open-vocabulary affordance detection task (3D AffordanceNet), 3D-ADLLM with Phi-3.5-mini achieves 30.43 mIoU^c and 47.78 mAcc^c on full-view, and 27.25 mIoU^c and 39.04 mAcc^c on partial-view, outperforming the runner-up LASO by 8.02% and 7.19% in mIoU^c respectively. On the out-of-distribution AffordPose dataset, 3D-ADLLM reaches 36.33 mIoU^i and 36.33 mAP50^i, surpassing LASO (34.49 mIoU^i, 8.40 mAP50^i) by a large margin. The method also improves over OpenAD by 16.9% (full-view) and 15.96% (partial-view) in mIoU across 18 affordance types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-ADLLM (Phi) decisively outperforms all ten baselines on every metric in both full-view and partial-view settings. Against the strongest prior method LASO (Li et al., 2024), it gains +8.02 mIoU^c (30.43 vs 22.41) and +17.56 mAcc^c (47.78 vs 30.22) on full-view, and +7.19 mIoU^c (27.25 vs 20.06) on partial-view. Compared to OpenAD (Nguyen et al., 2023), which relies on fixed affordance labels, 3D-ADLLM improves mIoU^c by 16.90 points (full) and 15.96 points (partial), demonstrating the benefit of language-driven reasoning over label-based classification. IAGNet (Yang et al., 2023) trails by 14.27 mIoU^c on full-view, while the zero-shot point-cloud classifiers (ZSLPC, TZSLPC, 3DGenZ) remain far behind at under 10 mIoU^c. ShapeLLM (Qi et al., 2024), tested without fine-tuning, performs worst (0.88 mIoU^c), confirming that generic 3D LLM grounding does not transfer to fine-grained affordance segmentation. On the OOD AffordPose benchmark, 3D-ADLLM leads LASO by 1.84 mIoU^i and 27.93 mAP50^i, indicating stronger generalization to unseen affordance-object pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established 3D affordance detection paradigm (3D AffordanceNet, OpenAD, LASO, IAGNet), which formulates the problem as fixed-label semantic segmentation or CLIP-based phrase matching, by reformulating it as Instruction Reasoning Affordance Segmentation (IRAS) driven by free-form natural language queries. It draws on the 3D LMM line of work (3D-LLM, ShapeLLM, Chat-3D) for the point-cloud-to-LLM interface, but introduces a key architectural departure: a special <AFF> token whose hidden embedding is decoded into a segmentation mask, an idea adapted from LISA's 2D reasoning-segmentation approach. The multi-stage training strategy with a novel ROPS pre-training task on PartNet is a transfer-learning mechanism not present in prior affordance or 3D LMM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By injecting LLM world knowledge and reasoning into 3D affordance perception, this work opens a path toward robots that can interpret complex, context-dependent interaction instructions without requiring new labels or retraining for each affordance type. The ~8% mIoU gain over the prior SOTA (LASO) and strong out-of-distribution results on AffordPose suggest practical value for open-world manipulation. However, the approach remains constrained by the small 3D AffordanceNet fine-tuning set (42K samples), the reliance on a single-object point cloud input (no multi-object scene reasoning), and the untested scalability of the <AFF> token mechanism to more complex or dynamic scenes. Open questions include whether the ROPS pre-training stage generalizes beyond PartNet's 24 categories and whether the framework can handle temporal or multi-step interaction reasoning.
