## Problem

Generalizable deep image denoising models must handle noise types unseen during training, but the current state-of-the-art Masked Training (MT) method, while improving cross-noise generalization, produces over-smoothed outputs that lose high-frequency details and degrade PSNR. Prior work has analyzed feature statistics to diagnose generalization failures but has not directly manipulated them as a training-time intervention to improve robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251866/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a noisy RGB image of arbitrary spatial resolution (H×W×3) and outputs a denoised RGB image via residual learning: the network predicts a residual R∈R^{H×W×3} that is added to the input. Training is performed exclusively on Gaussian noise (σ=15) using 128×128 patches, yet the model is evaluated on seven unseen noise types (speckle, salt-and-pepper, Poisson, ISP, mixture, Monte Carlo rendering, and smartphone sensor noise) at multiple intensity levels across four benchmark test sets.

## Method

RNINet is a streamlined 4-level CNN encoder-decoder (channels [64,128,256,512]) whose key innovation is a noise injection block placed at each encoder level. Each block computes channel-wise mean μ and standard deviation σ of downsampled features, Z-score normalizes them, then injects random tensors ε₁,ε₂~N(0,1) into the statistics via a reparameterization formulation (μⁿ=μ+ε₁·V(μ), σⁿ=σ+ε₂·V(σ)) before reconstructing noised features. This makes the noise injection differentiable, allowing the network to learn a nonlinear mapping from noised feature statistics to clean outputs. The theoretical justification invokes the Lindeberg-Feller Central Limit Theorem: since features from images corrupted by different noise types collectively form a normal distribution, injecting Gaussian noise into feature statistics effectively simulates the statistical signatures of unseen noise domains, enhancing generalization without architectural complexity.

## Results

Trained exclusively on Gaussian noise (σ=15), RNINet generalizes to seven unseen noise types. On the McMaster benchmark, RNINet achieves 32.20 dB PSNR on speckle noise (σ²=0.02), outperforming MT by 1.72 dB and DRUNet by 2.35 dB. In-distribution (Gaussian σ=15), RNINet surpasses MT by 2.46–3.46 dB PSNR across four test sets (e.g., 34.05 vs 30.85 on McMaster). RNINet also achieves 10× faster inference (0.10 s vs 1.04 s per image) with lower MACs (44.7G vs 51.6G) and GPU memory (0.62G vs 0.90G) compared to MT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SOTA generalizable method MT (Chen et al., 2023), RNINet wins on moderate-to-mild noise levels across all noise types (e.g., +1.72 dB on speckle σ²=0.02, +2.28 dB on salt&pepper d=0.004, +0.91 dB on Poisson α=2, +2.39 dB on mixture L2), but MT retains a slight edge under extreme noise (e.g., speckle σ²=0.04: MT 28.66 vs RNINet 28.35 dB) due to its stronger smoothing. On in-distribution Gaussian denoising, RNINet dominates MT by 2.46–3.46 dB PSNR, confirming that MT's masking causes over-smoothing even on its training distribution. Against non-generalizable baselines (DRUNet, SwinIR, Restormer, CODE), RNINet leads by 1.5–3.5 dB on unseen noise types, while these methods degrade sharply when tested outside their training noise. On real-world smartphone noise (SIDD), RNINet achieves 28.90 dB PSNR, edging out MT (28.66) and substantially outperforming DRUNet (28.09) and SwinIR (27.62). On zero-shot real-world denoising (PolyU, Nam), RNINet surpasses both MT and the LAN zero-shot denoisers by 3.6–4.6 dB PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly responds to Masked Training (MT, Chen et al. 2023), the current SOTA for generalizable deep image denoising, by replacing MT's complex masked SwinIR transformer with a streamlined CNN encoder-decoder. The key conceptual departure is the repurposing of the VAE reparameterization trick: rather than merely analyzing feature statistics (mean, variance) as prior generalization studies (Liu et al. 2021, 2023) do, RNINet actively injects random noise tensors into those statistics during training, grounded in a Lindeberg-Feller CLT argument that features from diverse noise types collectively form a normal distribution. This positions the work at the intersection of feature-statistics analysis in neural networks and stochastic training techniques, carving a path that neither the masked-training line nor the feature-statistics-analysis line had previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup and substantially lower GPU memory (0.62G vs 0.90G) make generalizable denoising far more deployable in real-world pipelines, while the simpler architecture lowers the barrier to adoption. A notable limitation is that MT still outperforms RNINet under super-high noise intensities (e.g., Poisson with scale factor 3), suggesting the noise-injection strategy provides moderate rather than extreme smoothing. An open question the authors flag is the optimal combination of noise types during training: their preliminary experiment (Gaussian→Poisson) showed overall degradation, indicating that multi-noise training is non-trivial and warrants further investigation.
