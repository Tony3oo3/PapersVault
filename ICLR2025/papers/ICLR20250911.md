## Problem

Steering Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) to optimize a downstream reward is fundamentally harder than for autoregressive models: diffusion models lack closed-form likelihoods, and discrete spaces have no conventional gradient operator for guidance. Existing approaches either require costly full-trajectory simulations (e.g., RTB) or optimize loose lower bounds that lead to unstable training, leaving no scalable and rigorous method for aligning MDMs with arbitrary reward functions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained MDM (providing its denoising posterior p_t^pre(x₀|x_t)), a scalar reward function R(x₀) that need not be differentiable, and optionally a replay buffer of clean or partially masked sequences. It outputs a fine-tuned MDM parameterized by μ_θ(x_t, t) that serves as an amortized sampler from the reward-induced posterior π₀(x₀) ∝ p₀^pre(x₀)R(x₀). Sampling from the fine-tuned model at inference requires only a single model call per denoising step (1 model call/inf. step), matching the base MDM's cost, and the training step is simulation-free—no full forward corruption trajectory is needed.

## Method

DDPP casts reward-guided steering as Bayesian posterior sampling: it defines a forward masking process on the target posterior π₀ and learns a second MDM to approximate the corresponding reverse process. The key insight is that the denoising reward-induced posterior π_t(x₀|x_t) decomposes as p_t^pre(x₀|x_t)·R(x₀)/Z_{π_t}(x_t), so matching it reduces to a log-ratio regression between the fine-tuned model's denoiser and the pre-trained model's denoiser plus the log-reward. Three simulation-free variants handle the intractable log partition function: DDPP-IS uses Monte Carlo importance sampling with the fine-tuned model as proposal, DDPP-LB learns a lower-bound estimate via an auxiliary prediction head (cheapest, 1 model call/train step), and DDPP-KL minimizes a reverse KL using the REINMAX discrete gradient estimator (requires a differentiable reward). The single-step formulation (Eq. 11) avoids unrolling sub-trajectories by matching only at the predicted endpoint x₀, making it applicable to sequences of moderate length without discretization error from trajectory unrolling.

## Results

On binarized MNIST (even-digit generation), DDPP-KL achieves the best log reward (-3.13) and FLD (31.75) while maintaining BPD (0.129) comparable to the base model (0.130). On protein design, DDPP-LB generates sequences with 43.6% β-sheet content (vs. 11.1% base, 31.9% RTB) while achieving the highest pLDDT (0.897) and pTM (0.806), with wet-lab validation confirming transient expression of 4/6 designed constructs. On text generation, DDPP-LB achieves log R of 205.76 on Tinystories and 152.08 on Amazon reviews, substantially outperforming SVDD (146.95 / -27.48) and RTB (107.83 / -35.22) while maintaining competitive Gen PPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDPP consistently outperforms RTB across all domains: on MNIST, DDPP-KL improves log R from -18.66 to -3.13 and FLD from 45.97 to 31.75; on protein design, DDPP-LB achieves 43.6% β-sheet vs. RTB's 31.9% with higher pLDDT (0.897 vs. 0.806); on text, DDPP-LB surpasses RTB by ~98 log R points on Tinystories. Against SVDD, DDPP wins decisively on protein (β-sheet 0.436 vs. 0.114; pLDDT 0.897 vs. 0.484) and text (log R 205.76 vs. 146.95 on Tinystories), though SVDD achieves a slightly higher log R on MNIST (-0.03 vs. -3.13) at the cost of worse FLD. Discrete guidance (Nisonoff et al.) is only applicable with differentiable rewards and degrades sample quality sharply at higher scales (FLD 72.19 at scale 100), whereas DDPP-KL maintains FLD of 31.75. Notably, DDPP-LB exceeds the Best-of-10 upper bound on protein β-sheet content (0.436 vs. 0.280) and Tinystories log R (205.76 vs. 93.25), indicating the amortized sampler captures reward structure more effectively than finite-sample rejection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the simplified masked diffusion model (MDM) framework (Sahoo et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024) and the trajectory-matching ideas from GFlowNets/Relative Trajectory Balance (Venkatraman et al., 2024), but reframes the steering problem as Bayesian posterior sampling rather than reinforcement learning. The key departure is that DDPP exploits the denoising posterior parametrization inherent to MDMs to construct simulation-free matching objectives at individual noise levels, avoiding the full trajectory simulation required by RTB. The DDPP-KL variant additionally leverages the REINMAX discrete gradient estimator (Liu et al., 2024) to bypass log-partition function estimation entirely when the reward is differentiable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDPP provides the first scalable, simulation-free method to steer MDMs with general (including non-differentiable) reward functions, filling a gap that has prevented MDMs from matching the controllability of autoregressive models in practical applications. The wet-lab validation of reward-optimized protein sequences adds biological credibility beyond in-silico metrics. Open questions include how to balance the DDPP-LB log-partition function head optimization, the choice of sub-trajectory step size γ, and whether these methods scale to much larger pre-trained MDMs (e.g., billion-parameter language models) where the current experiments use relatively small models.
