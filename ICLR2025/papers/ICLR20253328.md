## Problem

Score function estimators (REINFORCE-style) have been dismissed for k-subset sampling due to their high variance, leading the field to rely on approximate pathwise gradients or relaxed sampling that are biased or produce non-discrete samples. This paper revisits score function estimators for k-subset sampling, asking whether they can compete with state-of-the-art pathwise and relaxed methods despite their weaker assumptions, while offering the complementary benefits of unbiasedness and applicability to non-differentiable downstream functions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a vector of subset parameters θ ∈ [0,1]^n, a target subset size k, and a downstream function f (which may be non-differentiable or a black-box). In the forward pass, it produces exact k-hot binary subset samples via Gumbel top-k sampling (used as an approximation to conditional Poisson sampling). In the backward pass, it produces an unbiased Monte-Carlo gradient estimate of ∇_θ E_{p_{θ,k}(z)}[f(z)] using N samples, where the Poisson binomial normalization term is computed once per θ via FFT and reused across all N samples for the control variate.

## Method

The score function for the conditional Poisson k-subset distribution decomposes into a Bernoulli term (trivial) and a Poisson binomial normalization term. The key technical contribution is computing the Poisson binomial PMF efficiently in O(n log n) time using a discrete Fourier transform (following Fernandez & Williams, 2010), with the gradient obtained via automatic differentiation. To address the high variance of the vanilla REINFORCE estimator, the paper employs multi-sample control variates: N samples are drawn, and each sample's f-value is centered by the mean of the remaining N−1 samples, yielding an unbiased variance-reduced gradient. The forward pass uses Gumbel top-k sampling as a practical approximation to exact conditional Poisson sampling, trading a small bias for computational simplicity. The resulting estimator requires no tuning hyperparameters (unlike relaxation temperature in Gumbel-Softmax variants) and does not assume differentiability of f, making it applicable to reinforcement learning and black-box optimization settings.

## Results

On the VAE benchmark (d=20, n=20, k=10), SFESS+VR achieves 68.83 BCE+KL on MNIST and 218.39 on Fashion-MNIST, closely matching the state-of-the-art SIMPLE (67.96 and 218.82) and outperforming STGS (73.90 and 225.06). On feature selection (n=784, k=50), SFESS+VR reaches 0.132 BCE on MNIST, slightly beating SIMPLE (0.133) and STGS (0.146). On stochastic k-NN, SFESS+VR achieves 94.9% accuracy on MNIST and 86.9% on Fashion-MNIST, within 0.1–0.4% of the best baselines. Without variance reduction, vanilla SFESS fails dramatically on the VAE task (205.72 vs. ~68–97 for all other methods), underscoring the critical role of the control variate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFESS+VR is most competitive with SIMPLE (Ahmed et al., 2023), the state-of-the-art pathwise gradient estimator: on the VAE task (d=20) it trails by only 0.87 on MNIST (68.83 vs. 67.96) and actually edges out SIMPLE by 0.43 on Fashion-MNIST (218.39 vs. 218.82). On feature selection (k=50, MNIST), SFESS+VR slightly beats SIMPLE (0.132 vs. 0.133 BCE), while on k-NN it matches SIMPLE within 0.1% accuracy on both datasets. Against the relaxed-sampling baselines GS and STGS (Xie & Ermon, 2019), SFESS+VR consistently wins by a wider margin—e.g., 5.07 lower BCE+KL on the VAE (MNIST) and 0.014 lower BCE on feature selection (k=50, MNIST). I-MLE (Niepert et al., 2021) is the weakest baseline across all three tasks, with SFESS+VR outperforming it by 15.72 on VAE (MNIST) and 0.052 on feature selection (k=50, MNIST). The key differentiator is that SFESS+VR produces exact k-hot samples and unbiased gradients without requiring a differentiable downstream function, properties none of the compared baselines simultaneously offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits score function (REINFORCE-style) estimators for k-subset sampling, a direction explicitly dismissed in prior work (Xie & Ermon 2019; Niepert et al. 2021; Ahmed et al. 2023) as too high-variance to be useful. It builds on the classical score function estimator (Williams 1992), the FFT-based closed-form Poisson binomial PMF (Fernandez & Williams 2010) for efficient score computation, Gumbel top-k sampling (Kool et al. 2019b) for the forward pass, and multi-sample control variates (Mnih & Rezende 2016; Kool et al. 2019a) for variance reduction. The key conceptual departure from the dominant pathwise-gradient and relaxed-sampling paradigms (GS, STGS, SIMPLE, NCPSS) is that SFESS requires no differentiable downstream function and produces exact k-hot samples with unbiased gradients, at the cost of needing multiple f evaluations per step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that score function estimators can match state-of-the-art pathwise and relaxed methods on feature selection, VAE, and stochastic k-NN tasks, this work reopens a previously closed avenue for k-subset sampling and extends its applicability to non-differentiable or black-box objectives (e.g., reinforcement learning, combinatorial losses). The main practical limitation is the need for multiple samples for variance reduction, which multiplies the cost of evaluating f, and the use of Gumbel top-k as an approximate proxy for true conditional Poisson sampling introduces a small bias. Open questions include whether combining score function and pathwise estimators (as done for Bernoulli/categorical variables) could further reduce variance, and whether the approach scales to much larger n and k where the FFT computation and multiple f evaluations become prohibitive.
