## Problem

Existing multi-modal 3D scene understanding methods (e.g., LangSplat, LEGaussians) naively reuse the color opacity parameter from 3DGS to α-blend language features during rasterization, ignoring that opacity encodes visual translucence rather than semantic relevance. This causes poor semantic rendering of translucent or reflective objects (glass, metal) and allows the richer color modality to dominate training, leading to over-fitting where different objects sharing similar colors receive identical semantic representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB images (124–297 per scene) and a text query at inference time. Ground-truth 2D language maps are pre-extracted per image using SAM ViT-H for segmentation masks and OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 for dense language embeddings, followed by autoencoder-based dimension reduction. At test time, the trained 3D Gaussian model renders a per-pixel 2D language embedding from any novel camera pose, which is scored against the text encoder's query embedding to produce an open-vocabulary segmentation or localization map.

## Method

The core contribution is a cross-modal rasterizer that (1) fuses per-Gaussian color and semantic features via a self-attention module before rasterization, and (2) introduces a learnable smoothed semantic indicator l^i ∈ [0,1] that replaces color opacity in the α-blending of language features, allowing the model to keep semantic contributions at full strength for tangible objects while suppressing intangible lighting-effect Gaussians. A second contribution is a camera-view blending augmentation: two training views are paired, their poses interpolated via Slerp (rotation) and Lerp (translation) with a Beta(0.2, 0.2)-sampled ratio k, and the blended 2D language map (k·H₁ + (1−k)·H₂) is used as a regularization target weighted by SSIM between the source images. Training jointly minimizes MSE on rendered color and semantic maps plus the view-blending loss, with all spatial Gaussian parameters (position, covariance) shared across modalities while only features and opacity/indicator are disentangled.

## Results

On the LERF dataset, the proposed method achieves 62.0 mIoU (average across ramen, teatime, figurines, kitchen), outperforming the second-best LangSplat (51.4) by 10.6 points. On 3D-OVS, it reaches 97.1 mIoU versus 93.4 for LangSplat and 90.6 for GOI. On Mip-NeRF 360, it scores 89.8 mIoU, surpassing GOI (86.5) and LangSplat (55.5). For 3D object localization on LERF, it achieves 96.0% accuracy, exceeding FMGS (91.5%) and GOI (84.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all six baselines across all three datasets. On LERF mIoU, it beats LangSplat (51.4) by 10.6 points, GOI (50.6) by 11.4, LEGaussians (46.9) by 15.1, GS-Grouping (46.3) by 15.7, and Feature-3DGS (45.7) by 16.3. On 3D-OVS mIoU, the margin over LangSplat narrows to 3.7 points (97.1 vs 93.4), but it still leads GOI by 6.5, LEGaussians by 8.6, and FMGS by 9.8. On Mip-NeRF 360, the advantage over GOI is 3.3 points (89.8 vs 86.5), while the gap over LangSplat is a dramatic 34.3 points. For localization accuracy on LERF, it surpasses FMGS (91.5%) by 4.5 points and GOI (84.4%) by 11.6 points. The gains are particularly pronounced on scenes with translucent or reflective objects (e.g., ramen scene glass: 78.6 vs 64.1 mIoU with/without the semantic indicator), confirming that the cross-modal rasterizer and semantic indicator specifically address the failure modes of prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based open-vocabulary scene understanding line initiated by LangSplat and LEGaussians, which naively reuse the color opacity parameter to α-blend language features during rasterization. The key departure is a cross-modal rasterizer that (1) fuses color and semantic features via self-attention before rasterization, and (2) introduces an independent learnable 'smoothed semantic indicator' to replace color opacity for the language modality, acknowledging that translucency and light transport are visual phenomena with no semantic analogue. A camera-view blending regularization (Slerp/Lerp pose interpolation + mixup of 2D language maps) further distinguishes the approach from prior single-view supervision schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semantic indicator addresses a concrete failure mode—mis-segmentation of translucent and reflective objects (glass, stainless steel)—that prior 3DGS-semantic methods cannot handle, with per-category ablations showing 14–15% mIoU gains on such objects versus ~3.6% on opaque ones. The camera-view blending technique offers a lightweight, online regularization that outperforms offline 3DGS-rendered augmentation, suggesting a general recipe for multi-modal 3D representation training. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether the self-attention fusion generalizes to higher-dimensional or multi-foundation-model semantic features, and whether the single-phase joint training (vs. two-phase color-then-semantic) remains advantageous at larger scene scales.
