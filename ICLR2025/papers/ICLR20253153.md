## Problem

Existing hyperbolic vision-language models like MERU capture only the inter-modal hierarchy (text is semantically broader than image) but ignore the intra-modal compositional structure: an image is a composition of object regions, and a caption is a composition of noun phrases. The paper asks how to jointly enforce both inter-modal and intra-modal hierarchical orderings in a shared hyperbolic embedding space to achieve scene-level and region-level understanding simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253153/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images resized to 224×224, full text captions, and offline-extracted bounding boxes (minimum 32×32 pixels, ≥0.65 CLIP confidence) paired with their corresponding noun phrases or referring expressions extracted via spaCy dependency parsing and localized by GLIP. This effectively doubles the training data: each image-text pair yields additional box-image and box-text pairs. The output is a shared hyperbolic embedding in the Lorentz model (curvature κ learned, initialized at 1.0) from which zero-shot classification, retrieval, detection, and hierarchical matching are performed via Lorentzian distance.

## Method

HyCoCLIP introduces Compositional Entailment Learning with two loss components operating in the Lorentz hyperbolic model. The Hierarchical Compositional Contrastive (hCC) loss performs four-way contrastive alignment: full images are contrasted against full captions (specific-to-specific), while image boxes are contrasted against full captions and text boxes against full images (general-to-specific), deliberately avoiding box-to-box negatives that would be semantically ambiguous. The Hierarchical Compositional Entailment (hCE) loss enforces partial ordering via entailment cones with a key modification: a half-aperture threshold η (η_inter=0.7, η_intra=1.2) relaxes the cone width so specific concepts can be placed at varying radial distances from their general parents, rather than being pushed only to the cone boundary. The four entailment relations enforced are: text box entails image box (inter-modal), full text entails full image (inter-modal), full image entails image box (intra-modal), and full text entails text box (intra-modal). The total loss is hC = hCC + 0.1·hCE, trained with ViT-S/16 or ViT-B/16 vision encoders and a 12-layer CLIP text encoder on 20.5M grounded GRIT pairs.

## Results

On the GRIT pre-training set (20.5M pairs + 35.9M boxes) with ViT-B/16, HyCoCLIP achieves 45.8% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy, outperforming CLIP (40.6%) and MERU (40.1%) by 5.2 and 5.7 points respectively. Across 16 classification benchmarks, HyCoCLIP wins on 15 of 16 datasets, with particularly large gains on fine-grained tasks (Food-101: 59.2% vs 48.6%/51.5%; CUB: 16.4% vs 10.0%/11.9%). On zero-shot object detection (COCO 17 novel categories), HyCoCLIP reaches 68.5 AP, surpassing RegionCLIP (65.2), MERU (55.8), and CLIP (51.2). Hierarchical classification metrics (TIE, LCA, Jaccard, PH, RH) show consistent improvements, e.g. TIE drops from 3.60 (CLIP) and 3.63 (MERU) to 3.17 for HyCoCLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), HyCoCLIP wins on 15 of 16 classification datasets with the ViT-B/16 backbone on GRIT, with the largest margins on CIFAR-10 (+9.9 pts), Food-101 (+10.6 pts), and CUB (+6.4 pts); the sole loss is on EuroSAT (35.8 vs 37.1). Against MERU (Desai et al., 2023), HyCoCLIP similarly dominates on 15/16 datasets, with ImageNet improving from 40.1% to 45.8% and Food-101 from 51.5% to 59.2%; MERU's only advantage is on EuroSAT (31.7 vs 35.8 is actually a HyCoCLIP win, so MERU has no clear win on ViT-B/16 GRIT). On zero-shot retrieval, HyCoCLIP leads on image retrieval (COCO R@5: 58.4 vs 57.4 for both baselines) but is slightly behind MERU on text retrieval (COCO R@5: 72.0 vs 72.3). Against RegionCLIP (Zhong et al., 2022) on zero-shot object detection, HyCoCLIP achieves 68.5 AP vs 65.2 AP, and also substantially outperforms it on classification (41.7 vs 40.6 ImageNet) and scene understanding (59.8 vs 52.5 VL-CO). Notably, naively adding box pairs to CLIP or MERU training (without the compositional entailment objective) degrades or fails to improve performance, confirming that the hierarchical loss structure is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MERU (Desai et al., 2023), which introduced hyperbolic contrastive learning with inter-modal entailment (text entails image), by adding intra-modal compositional hierarchies: whole images entail their object boxes, and whole captions entail their noun phrases. The entailment cone formulation is inherited from Le et al. (2019) and Ganea et al. (2018a), while the grounding pipeline (GLIP + spaCy) follows Peng et al. (2023). Unlike RegionCLIP (Zhong et al., 2022), which learns regional representations purely through contrastive learning and distillation, HyCoCLIP unifies inter-modal and intra-modal ordering within a single hyperbolic objective, and unlike Ge et al. (2023), which models object-scene hierarchies in vision only, it jointly enforces hierarchies across both modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that naively augmenting CLIP or MERU training with box-level samples actually degrades performance, showing that compositional structure requires explicit hierarchical inductive bias (entailment cones) to be useful rather than merely more data. This provides a principled recipe for incorporating regional and phrase-level information into vision-language pretraining without the pitfalls of unstructured data augmentation. Key limitations include the dependency on external grounding models (GLIP) for box generation, the increased training data volume (~77% more samples), and the observation that the hierarchical structure may not be optimal for large-scale retrieval tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViT-L/H), whether learned grounding could replace the GLIP pipeline, and whether the inter/intra-modal η thresholds generalize across domains.
