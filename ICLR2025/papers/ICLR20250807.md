## Problem

Causal discovery algorithms depend on unverifiable assumptions (i.i.d., faithfulness, causal sufficiency, no measurement error) that are routinely violated in real-world data, yet prior benchmarks either excluded differentiable methods from misspecification testing or covered only a narrow set of violations. This paper is the first to systematically evaluate differentiable causal discovery across eight distinct assumption-violation scenarios, filling a critical gap in the robustness literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250807/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds n×d observational data matrices (n=2000, d∈{10,20,50}) generated from linear or nonlinear SEMs over ER, SF, and GRP graphs, with each of eight violation scenarios perturbing the data-generating process (e.g., adding latent confounders, injecting measurement noise, standardizing variables, dropping MCAR rows, or swapping linear/nonlinear mechanisms). Algorithms output either a fully oriented DAG or a CPDAG, evaluated via Structural Hamming Distance (SHD) and Structural Intervention Distance (SID) against the ground-truth graph, with MEC outputs scored favorably by assuming correct edge directions.

## Method

The paper conducts over 70,000 experiments across 12 algorithms (PC, GES, DirectLiNGAM, CAM, Var/R²-SortnRegress, NOTEARS, GOLEM, NoCurl, DAGMA, NOTEARS-MLP, GraN-DAG) under eight misspecified scenarios, tuning hyperparameters per dataset for fair comparison. The key empirical finding is that differentiable methods achieve optimal or competitive SHD/SID in all scenarios except scale variation, where their performance degrades significantly. Theoretically, the authors invoke Loh & Buhlmann (2014) theorems on noise-ratio-dependent identifiability: under measurement error and unfaithful path cancellation the effective noise ratio r increases above 1, breaking the equal-variance guarantee that underpins least-squares-based differentiable methods, while under MCAR missingness the noise ratio remains unchanged, explaining the observed robustness. A dedicated MLP-setting comparison shows NOTEARS-MLP outperforms CAM across nearly all violations, reinforcing the practical advantage of differentiable approaches when the true mechanism is unknown.

## Results

Across 12 causal discovery algorithms evaluated on 2,400+ synthetic datasets (ER/SF/GRP graphs, 10–50 nodes) under 8 model assumption violations, differentiable methods (NOTEARS, GOLEM, NoCurl, DAGMA) achieve near-perfect recovery in the linear vanilla setting (SHD ≈ 1.2–2.0, SID ≈ 0.4–5.1) and maintain competitive performance in confounded (SHD 6.7–9.1), measurement error (SHD 11.8–17.8), heterogeneous (SHD 5.5–6.6), unfaithful (SHD 2.1–2.7), and missing (SHD 1.5–3.1) scenarios. The sole exception is scale-variant data, where linear differentiable methods degrade sharply (SHD 17.5–27.2) and fall below PC (12.4) and GES (13.8). In the nonlinear setting, NOTEARS-MLP and DAGMA outperform CAM under mechanism violation (SHD 5.9 and 3.3 vs. CAM's 17.8), while CAM retains an edge in scale-variant data (SHD 4.7 vs. 10.0–16.1 for differentiable methods).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the linear vanilla setting, all four linear differentiable methods (NOTEARS, GOLEM, NoCurl, DAGMA) achieve SHD of 1.2–2.0, dramatically outperforming PC (12.4), GES (13.8), DirectLiNGAM (19.6), and both SortnRegress variants (11.2–20.2). Under latent confounding, GOLEM achieves the best SHD (6.7) and NoCurl the best SID (5.4), while PC degrades to 18.1 SHD and GES to 25.9. Under measurement error, NoCurl (SHD 11.8) and NOTEARS (12.5) remain competitive with DirectLiNGAM (17.6) and Var-SortnRegress (19.6), though all methods degrade from vanilla. The critical failure mode is scale-variant data: NOTEARS (SHD 18.0), GOLEM (17.5), NoCurl (27.2), and DAGMA (17.9) all fall below PC (12.4) and GES (13.8), confirming Reisach et al.'s (2021) observation. In the nonlinear setting, CAM dominates the vanilla GP mechanism (SHD 4.7 vs. NOTEARS-MLP's 12.4), but under mechanism violation NOTEARS-MLP (SHD 5.9) and DAGMA (SHD 3.3) substantially outperform CAM (17.8), demonstrating that differentiable methods are more robust when the true mechanism is unknown. Runtime analysis shows DAGMA (2.4 s) and NOTEARS-MLP (4.7 s) are among the fastest methods, while GOLEM (130 s) and GraN-DAG (119 s) are significantly slower at 10 nodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal discovery benchmarking lineage initiated by Heinze-Deml et al. (2018) and Montagna et al. (2023), which evaluated constraint-based, score-based, and functional causal model methods under assumption violations, by being the first to systematically assess differentiable (gradient-based) causal discovery methods—NOTEARS, GOLEM, NoCurl, DAGMA, NOTEARS-MLP, and GraN-DAG—across the same and additional misspecified scenarios. It also builds on the theoretical framework of Loh & Buhlmann (2014) on noise-ratio conditions for DAG identifiability to explain why differentiable methods degrade under measurement error and unfaithful path cancellation. The paper departs from prior benchmarks by covering the most extensive set of eight violation types (confounding, measurement error, autoregressive, heterogeneous, unfaithful, scale-variant, missing, mechanism violation) and by including a dedicated MLP-parameterized comparison to fairly evaluate CAM against differentiable methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark provides practitioners with the first comprehensive evidence that differentiable causal discovery methods are robust across most real-world assumption violations (except scale variation), which directly informs method selection for applied causal inference where assumptions are unverifiable. The theoretical explanation via noise-ratio analysis (Theorems 7 and 9 from Loh & Buhlmann) clarifies the mechanism behind performance degradation, offering a principled diagnostic rather than purely empirical observation. Open questions remain: nonlinear differentiable methods still struggle with scale variation (though Deng et al., 2024 suggest a fix via loss function design), the study is limited to non-temporal settings, and validation on truly real-world data (beyond the Sachs dataset and semi-synthetic experiments) is still needed. The paper also highlights that CAM's apparent superiority in the nonlinear GP setting is an artifact of distributional alignment, not a fundamental advantage.
