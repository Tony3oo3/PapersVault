## Problem

Generating high-quality 4D content from monocular videos requires simultaneously maintaining spatial consistency across viewpoints and temporal consistency across timesteps. Existing SDS-based methods produce low-quality motions and over-saturated appearances, while recent multiview video diffusion approaches demand large 4D training datasets and extensive compute. The core gap is a training-free mechanism to enforce temporal coherence in multiview diffusion outputs without retraining the generative model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252833/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video (32 frames, ~2 seconds, stationary camera) of a dynamic foreground object, which can be either real or synthesized by a text/image-to-video model. It produces a dynamic 3D Gaussian field (a static 3DGS with 512 keypoint-controlled deformation points) renderable at arbitrary viewpoints and timesteps via splatting. Intermediate supervision signals include K=6 multiview images at 512×512, predicted normal maps, and rendered 2D optical flow maps used as geometric constraints during training.

## Method

MVTokenFlow operates in three stages: (1) per-frame multiview generation with Era3D, augmented by enlarged self-attention that shares keys/values across timesteps for the same viewpoint to provide free temporal coherence; (2) coarse dynamic 3DGS reconstruction supervised by rendering, mask, D-SSIM, ARAP, normal-map, and 2D-flow losses, where the flow loss aligns rendered Gaussian offsets with RAFT-estimated optical flow on the front view; (3) a regeneration-and-refinement loop where 2D flows rendered from the coarse field guide token propagation between keyframes during denoising (Eq. 1: linearly interpolating warped self-attention features from bracketing keyframes), forcing associated pixels to share similar tokens and thereby improving temporal consistency across all viewpoints. The regenerated multiview videos then refine the 4D field for 15K additional iterations. The key insight is that a temporally coherent video shares similar diffusion tokens across frames, and 2D flows from a coarse 3D reconstruction can identify which pixels correspond, enabling token reuse without retraining the diffusion model.

## Results

On the Consistent4D dataset, MVTokenFlow achieves the best performance across all metrics: LPIPS 0.04, PSNR 31.27, SSIM 0.97, and CLIP 0.91 for view synthesis and spatial consistency. For novel view video synthesis (Table 2), the method further achieves LPIPS 0.1216, CLIP 0.948, and FVD 846.32, outperforming all baselines. The most notable improvement is in LPIPS (perceptual quality), where MVTokenFlow reduces the score from 0.06 (STAG4D) to 0.04, indicating significantly more realistic generated content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVTokenFlow outperforms all three baselines on every metric in the main comparison (Table 1). Against Consistent4D, the gains are substantial: PSNR improves from 23.97 to 31.27 (+7.3 dB) and LPIPS drops from 0.09 to 0.04, reflecting the limitations of SDS-based NeRF approaches in producing sharp, temporally coherent results. Against SC4D, MVTokenFlow leads by 1.77 dB in PSNR and 0.04 in LPIPS, with the gap attributed to SC4D's weaker temporal consistency from simply distilling video diffusion models. Against the strongest baseline STAG4D, MVTokenFlow wins on LPIPS (0.04 vs 0.06), PSNR (31.27 vs 30.79), and SSIM (0.97 vs 0.94), while tying on CLIP (0.91). In the novel view video synthesis evaluation (Table 2), MVTokenFlow also dominates: FVD 846.32 vs 972.73 (STAG4D) and 1072.94 (Consistent4D), and CLIP 0.948 vs 0.885 (STAG4D), confirming superior temporal coherence under unseen viewpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multiview diffusion for 3D generation (Era3D, SyncDreamer) and token-based video editing techniques (TokenFlow, VidTome), repurposing the latter to solve the temporal consistency problem in multiview video generation. It departs from the dominant SDS-based 4D generation pipeline (Consistent4D, SC4D, STAG4D) by replacing score distillation with explicit multiview diffusion plus a two-stage coarse-to-fine reconstruction using dynamic 3D Gaussians (SC-GS). The key conceptual move is using rendered 2D flows from a coarse 3D reconstruction to guide token propagation across timesteps in the diffusion process, bridging the spatial consistency of multiview diffusion with the temporal redundancy of coherent video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical recipe for improving temporal consistency in multiview generation without requiring 4D training data or retraining the diffusion model, which is valuable given the scarcity of 4D datasets. However, the method inherits Era3D's limitations on complex objects and uncommon viewpoints, and the 120-minute training time (comparable to SDS-based methods) limits its efficiency advantage over 3DGS-only approaches. An open question is whether the token propagation strategy generalizes to longer sequences or more viewpoints, and whether the two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline can be made more efficient by reducing the number of regeneration passes.
