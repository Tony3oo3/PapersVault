## Problem

Existing multi-modal generative models either quantize continuous data like images into discrete tokens (sacrificing information) or bolt separate architectures together for each modality. No single unified model could simultaneously generate high-quality text and images without an information bottleneck, leaving a gap between the state of the art in discrete sequence modeling (next-token prediction) and continuous media generation (diffusion).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250235/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single interleaved sequence of up to 4096 elements, where text tokens are discrete integers from the Llama 2 vocabulary and image patches are continuous 8-dimensional vectors (a 256×256 image becomes 256 patches of 2×2 latent pixels after VAE encoding and patchification). Image segments are delimited by BOI/EOI tokens. At inference, the model produces arbitrary mixtures of text and images: text is sampled autoregressively, while images are generated by 250-step diffusion denoising in the VAE latent space and decoded back to 256×256 RGB pixels. After fine-tuning on 8k editing examples, the model also accepts an input image plus a text instruction and outputs an edited image.

## Method

Transfusion trains a single transformer with two simultaneous objectives: standard next-token prediction (cross-entropy) on text tokens and denoising diffusion (MSE on predicted noise) on image patches, combined as L = L_LM + λ·L_DDPM with λ=5. The key architectural insight is a hybrid attention mask that applies causal attention across the full sequence but allows bidirectional attention among patches within the same image, letting each patch attend to all others in its image while only seeing preceding text. Modality-specific lightweight layers handle the interface: text uses standard embedding matrices, while images use either a simple linear layer or U-Net down/up blocks to compress and expand k×k patch windows into/from the transformer's hidden dimension. At inference, decoding switches modes at BOI/EOI boundaries—sampling tokens in LM mode and iteratively denoising a noise vector in diffusion mode—enabling generation of any text-image mixture from one forward pass.

## Results

The 7B Transfusion model (7.3B params, 2T multi-modal tokens) achieves a COCO FID of 6.78 and GenEval score of 0.63 for image generation, while simultaneously reaching 66.1% Llama 2 Eval Suite accuracy for text generation — matching Llama 1 (66.1%) and approaching Llama 2 (66.3%). In the controlled comparison against Chameleon at the same 7B scale, Transfusion achieves FID 16.8 vs. 29.6, CIDEr 27.2 vs. 18.0, and C4 PPL 7.72 vs. 8.41, with parity FLOP ratios as low as 0.029× for FID (i.e., Transfusion reaches Chameleon's image quality at ~34× less compute).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Chameleon (the primary controlled baseline), Transfusion wins on every benchmark: FID 16.8 vs. 29.6, CIDEr 27.2 vs. 18.0, C4 PPL 7.72 vs. 8.41, and Llama Acc 61.5 vs. 59.1, reaching FID parity at only 2.9% of Chameleon's FLOPs and text parity at ~50–60% of Chameleon's FLOPs. For image generation, Transfusion (FID 6.78, GenEval 0.63) outperforms DALL-E 2 (FID 10.39, GenEval 0.52), SDXL (GenEval 0.55), SD 2.1 (GenEval 0.50), Imagen (FID 7.27), and Parti (FID 7.23), and edges out DeepFloyd on GenEval (0.63 vs. 0.61) while matching it on FID (6.78 vs. 6.66). The only model Transfusion trails is SD 3 (GenEval 0.68), which benefited from synthetic backtranslated captions not used in Transfusion's training. On text generation, Transfusion matches Llama 1 exactly (66.1%) and is within 0.2 points of Llama 2 (66.3%), despite being a unified multi-modal model rather than a text-only LM.

## Relation to Prior Work

Transfusion sits at the intersection of two dominant paradigms: autoregressive language modeling (Llama, GPT) for discrete tokens and denoising diffusion (DDPM, LDM) for continuous data. Its closest conceptual predecessor is Chameleon, which unifies text and image generation in a single transformer by quantizing images into discrete tokens via VQ-VAE and applying a single LM loss over the combined sequence. Transfusion departs from this by keeping images in continuous latent space and applying a separate diffusion (MSE) loss to image patches while retaining cross-entropy for text, combined with a hybrid attention mask (causal across the sequence, bidirectional within each image). The architecture also draws on latent diffusion's VAE-based patchification and U-Net inductive biases, but integrates them as lightweight modality-specific I/O layers around a single shared transformer rather than as a separate diffusion backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Transfusion provides a practical, scalable recipe for truly unified multi-modal generation that avoids the information bottleneck of image quantization, and the controlled experiments show it dominates the discretization approach (Chameleon) in compute efficiency across every modality pairing—most dramatically in text-to-image FID (34× fewer FLOPs to parity). The 7B model trained on 2T tokens reaches GenEval 0.63 (surpassing SDXL and DALL-E 2) while simultaneously matching Llama-level text performance, demonstrating that the two objectives can coexist without significant parameter-sharing cost. Open questions include whether the recipe extends to video and audio, whether flow matching could replace diffusion for further efficiency, and why the text performance cost of multi-modal training is smaller with diffusion than with discrete image tokens—a phenomenon the paper identifies but does not fully explain.
