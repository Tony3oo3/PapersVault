## Problem

Existing hybrid latent refinement (HLR) methods, designed for in-domain inference improvement, suffer from significant rate cost degradation when applied to cross-domain image compression (e.g., natural-to-screen-content). The paper identifies the root cause as an underlying mismatch between the refined Gaussian conditional p(y|z) and hyperprior p(z) distributions, which triggers deteriorated joint probability approximation of the marginal distribution and increased bit consumption — a failure mode overlooked by prior two-step TTA-IC frameworks that adopt vanilla HLR as an inseparable component.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single cross-domain RGB image (screen content, pixel-style gaming, or painting, at resolutions ranging from ~492×640 to ~915×1627) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image. Crucially, it operates entirely at test time without modifying any pretrained model parameters: it iteratively refines the latent variable y and side information z over 2000 Adam steps (lr=1e-3), using T=20 Monte Carlo dropout samples to estimate the posterior p(z|y) on-the-fly. The refined latents are then entropy-coded and decoded as usual, making the method directly pluggable into existing two-step TTA-IC pipelines (latent refinement + decoder adaptation).

## Method

The paper first provides a marginalization-approximation analysis (Propositions 1–2, Corollary 1) showing that in cross-domain settings the extra rate consumption ΔH exceeds the in-domain bound ΔH*, and that vanilla HLR's joint optimization of y and z can actually worsen the joint probability approximation (ΔH^M > ΔH^0 > ΔH*) due to the mismatch between the source-trained entropy bottleneck and the refined Gaussian conditional. To remedy this, a distribution regularization term β·(−log p(z_t^m | y_t^m)) is added to the HLR rate-distortion objective, which empirically minimizes the extra marginalization rate and encourages the deteriorated joint probability to approach the initial or optimal one. The posterior p(z|y) is estimated via dropout variational inference (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016): the pretrained hyper-analysis network h_a is treated as a probabilistic model by applying Bernoulli dropout (p=0.5) across T=20 Monte Carlo forward passes, yielding a fully factorized Gaussian N(μ̂, σ̂²) without introducing any new network layers. This Bayesian approximation is plug-and-play — it degrades to the deterministic network when dropout probability is 1 — and the resulting method achieves consistent BD-rate gains over BLR and HLR across six in- and cross-domain datasets while integrating into SOTA TTA-IC frameworks (Tsubota et al., Shen et al., Lv et al.) with incremental benefits.

## Results

On the AR-CM backbone, the proposed method achieves an average BD-rate savings of −27.00% across six datasets, outperforming HLR (−21.36%) and BLR (−21.41%). On the Hyperprior backbone, the average BD-rate savings is −20.78%, again surpassing HLR (−14.87%) and BLR (−15.41%). The most dramatic gains appear on cross-domain tasks: e.g., on Pixel (gaming images) with AR-CM, the proposed method reaches −35.89% BD-rate vs. −31.76% for HLR and −26.22% for BLR. On the in-domain Kodak dataset, the method also leads with −16.11% (AR-CM) and −14.82% (Hyperprior), confirming consistent improvement over both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BLR (Campos et al., 2019), the proposed method wins on every dataset and backbone, with the largest margins on cross-domain tasks (e.g., +7.56% BD-rate on SIQAD with AR-CM, +7.79% on SCID with Hyperprior). Against HLR (Yang et al., 2020), the proposed method also dominates across all cross-domain datasets; notably, HLR actually underperforms BLR on SCID (−15.11% vs. −18.31%) and CCT (−23.08% vs. −25.33%) with AR-CM, confirming the paper's theoretical claim that vanilla HLR's joint probability approximation deteriorates under domain shift. When plugged into SOTA TTA-IC frameworks, the proposed method consistently improves over the original latent refinement stage: replacing HLR in Tsubota et al. (2023) lifts the average BD-rate from −10.02% to −12.01% (Stage 1) and from −14.89% to −16.95% (Stage 2 with adaptor); replacing BLR in Shen et al. (2023) improves the average from −4.74% to −12.01% (Stage 1) and from −10.94% to −15.26% (Stage 2). Similarly, replacing HLR in Lv et al. (2023) raises the average from −9.66% to −12.01% (Stage 1) and from −13.57% to −16.06% (Stage 2). The method thus demonstrates a clear plug-and-play advantage over all five baselines without any model architecture modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid latent refinement (HLR) framework of Yang et al. (2020), which jointly optimizes latent variables y and side information z for in-domain inference improvement, by diagnosing why HLR degrades in cross-domain compression and proposing a distribution regularization fix. The regularization term is implemented via dropout variational inference (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) to estimate the posterior p(z|y) without modifying any model architecture, distinguishing it from bits-back coding which assumes in-domain optimal joint probabilities. The method is positioned as a plug-and-play replacement for the latent refinement stage in existing SOTA TTA-IC pipelines (Tsubota et al. 2023, Shen et al. 2023, Lv et al. 2023), which previously relied on vanilla HLR or BLR as an inseparable ingredient without cross-domain tailoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously overlooked failure mode: HLR, widely adopted as the first stage in SOTA TTA-IC methods, actually increases rate consumption in cross-domain scenarios due to a mismatch between the refined Gaussian conditional and the static hyperprior. The plug-and-play nature of the proposed distribution regularization means existing TTA-IC systems can gain consistent BD-rate improvements (roughly 2–6% over HLR across five cross-domain datasets) without architectural changes. Limitations include a minor increase in adaptation time (~4% over HLR) due to Monte Carlo dropout sampling, and the method is validated primarily on screen-content and stylized images; generalization to more extreme distribution shifts (e.g., medical imaging) is only qualitatively explored. Open questions include whether more efficient posterior estimation (beyond DVI) could reduce the adaptation overhead, and whether the theoretical framework extends to autoregressive or flow-based entropy models.
