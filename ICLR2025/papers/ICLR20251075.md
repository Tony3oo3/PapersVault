## Problem

Existing tracking-by-propagation methods only propagate query information across adjacent frames, failing to capture long-range temporal dependencies within tracklets, interdependencies among tracklets, and temporal occlusions. In complex scenarios like coordinated dance, team sports, or animal flocks where objects move synchronously and frequently occlude each other, this limitation prevents accurate trajectory prediction through occlusions and across long time horizons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251075/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video frame sequence (RGB images resized to 800 on the shorter side, max 1536) and produces per-frame bounding boxes with persistent track IDs. During training, 10-frame clips are sampled at random intervals, but gradients are backpropagated only on the last 5 frames to save memory. At inference, the model processes frames sequentially and can track indefinitely long sequences, with track queries dropped only after N_miss consecutive low-confidence frames (35–50 depending on dataset).

## Method

SambaMOTR integrates a novel set-of-sequences model (Samba) into a tracking-by-propagation framework built on Deformable-DETR with ResNet-50. Samba independently compresses each tracklet's history into a hidden state via selective state-space models (Mamba-style SSMs), then synchronizes these hidden states across all active tracklets at every time step using stacked multi-head self-attention blocks, enabling tracklets to exchange information and model joint dynamics. A MaskObs mechanism masks uncertain observations from the SSM state update during occlusions, forcing the model to rely solely on long-term memory and inter-tracklet interactions to predict the next query. The model autoregressively predicts residual updates to track queries, and an efficient training recipe (10-frame sequences with gradients on only the last 5) enables learning stronger long-range dependencies without increasing GPU memory.

## Results

SambaMOTR achieves state-of-the-art performance on all three benchmarks: 67.2 HOTA / 57.5 AssA on DanceTrack, 69.6 HOTA / 73.6 AssA on BFT, and 69.8 HOTA / 59.4 AssA on SportsMOT. The largest gains are on DanceTrack, where SambaMOTR improves HOTA by +3.8 and AssA by +5.2 over the previous best tracking-by-propagation method MeMOTR (63.4 HOTA, 52.3 AssA). On BFT, SambaMOTR surpasses the best tracking-by-detection method OC-SORT by +2.8 HOTA and +4.9 AssA, and outperforms TrackFormer by +6.3 HOTA. On SportsMOT, SambaMOTR achieves the best overall HOTA (69.8) despite using a weaker detector (Deformable-DETR) than OC-SORT's YOLOX-X.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SambaMOTR outperforms all 14 baselines across all three datasets. On DanceTrack, it beats the closest tracking-by-propagation competitor MeMOTR by +3.8 HOTA and +5.2 AssA, and surpasses the best tracking-by-detection method C-BIoU (YOLOX-X) by +6.6 HOTA despite using a weaker detector. On BFT, SambaMOTR achieves +2.8 HOTA over OC-SORT (the best tracking-by-detection method) and a striking +6.3 HOTA over TrackFormer, the prior best tracking-by-propagation method. On SportsMOT, SambaMOTR edges out MeMOTR by +1.0 HOTA and +1.6 AssA, and surpasses OC-SORT by +1.7 HOTA and +4.6 AssA. Notably, SambaMOTR consistently achieves the highest AssA across all datasets, confirming that its synchronized set-of-sequences modeling and MaskObs strategy primarily improve association accuracy rather than detection. The gap over MOTR (the original tracking-by-propagation baseline) is especially large: +17.3 AssA on DanceTrack, demonstrating the value of long-range memory and tracklet interaction modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

SambaMOTR extends the tracking-by-propagation paradigm (MOTR, TrackFormer) by replacing the frame-to-frame query propagation with a set-of-sequences model built on selective state-space models (Mamba). Where MeMOTR attempted long-range memory via an exponential moving average and processed each tracklet independently, Samba compresses each tracklet's full history into a recurrent hidden state and then synchronizes these states across tracklets via self-attention, explicitly modeling inter-tracklet interactions. The MaskObs technique for occlusion handling departs from prior methods that simply freeze track queries, instead allowing the SSM to continue updating from memory and cross-tracklet context even when observations are unreliable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first tracking-by-propagation method to jointly and principledly address long-range temporal dependencies, tracklet interdependencies, and temporal occlusions without handcrafted heuristics, achieving new SOTA on three challenging benchmarks (DanceTrack, BFT, SportsMOT). The linear-time complexity in sequence length enables tracking over indefinitely long horizons, a practical advantage over quadratic-complexity memory approaches. Key limitations remain: the self-attention synchronization introduces quadratic complexity in the number of tracklets, and the tracking-by-propagation paradigm still drops objects after a fixed number of missed frames, leaving long-term re-identification as an open problem for scenarios like sports where players leave the frame for seconds.
