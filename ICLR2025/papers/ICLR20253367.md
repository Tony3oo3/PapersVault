## Problem

Mask2Former-style universal segmentation architectures allocate over 50% of their total compute to the transformer encoder, which maintains a full-length multi-scale token sequence at every layer. Existing efficient DETR-style encoders (Lite-DETR, RT-DETR) are designed for detection and either discard multi-scale features or cannot reduce the pixel-embedding overhead, leading to significant accuracy drops (~11%) when adapted to segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253367/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB image (800×1333 for COCO, 1024×2048 for Cityscapes) and produces a unified set of outputs: a panoptic segmentation map, per-pixel semantic masks, instance masks with class labels, and bounding boxes—all from a single model trained exclusively on panoptic annotations. The backbone extracts four multi-scale feature maps at 1/4², 1/8², 1/16², and 1/32² spatial resolutions, which are flattened into a token sequence of length K = HW/32² + HW/16² + HW/8² with channel dimension C=256.

## Method

PRO-SCALE restructures the transformer encoder into three sequential stages that progressively expand the token length: stage 1 processes only the smallest-scale tokens (s4), stage 2 adds s3 tokens, and stage 3 adds s2 tokens, with each stage repeated p1, p2, p3 times respectively. This exploits the observation that small-scale features carry abundant semantics while large-scale features contribute mostly redundant local detail, so they need fewer attention updates. A Light Pixel Embedding (LPE) module replaces the original convolutional pixel-embedding layer with a parameter-free maxpooling (kernel 3, stride 1) + normalization + non-linearity, reducing encoder GFLOPs by ~45%. A Token Re-Calibration (TRC) module applies contrastive attention to enrich s3 and s4 tokens with s2 information via a lightweight channel-reduction + sigmoid gate, recovering segmentation quality lost from skipping s2 in early layers at negligible cost (0.006 GFLOPs). The encoder uses deformable attention for linear complexity, and the full framework achieves ~52% encoder and ~27% overall GFLOPs reduction with no performance drop on COCO.

## Results

On COCO with SWIN-T backbone, PRO-SCALE (3,3,3) achieves 52.82 PQ (panoptic quality), 63.49 mIOU_p, and 42.60 AP_p at 171.70 total GFLOPs, surpassing the original Mask2Former (52.03 PQ at 234.50 GFLOPs) while reducing encoder GFLOPs by 52% (117.00 → 56.18). The most aggressive configuration (1,1,1) cuts encoder GFLOPs by 80.4% (117.00 → 22.89) at a modest 1.24 PQ drop. On Cityscapes with SWIN-T, PRO-SCALE (3,3,3) reaches 63.06 PQ at 392.10 GFLOPs versus M2F's 64.00 PQ at 537.80 GFLOPs, a 27% overall reduction with only 0.94 PQ loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRO-SCALE (3,3,3) outperforms the original Mask2Former on COCO with SWIN-T (52.82 vs. 52.03 PQ) while halving encoder GFLOPs, and beats Lite-M2F (52.70 PQ) with a 52% encoder reduction versus Lite-M2F's 32%. Against RT-M2F, PRO-SCALE gains 11.46 PQ points (52.82 vs. 41.36) at comparable encoder cost, highlighting that RT-DETR's hybrid encoder design is poorly suited to segmentation. Among non-M2F-style models, PRO-SCALE (3,3,3) with Res50 matches ReMaX's 53.50 PQ closely (51.45 PQ) while reporting full mIOU_p and AP_p metrics, and surpasses YOSO (48.40 PQ) and PEM (46.38 PQ) by 3+ PQ points. On Cityscapes, PRO-SCALE (3,3,3) with SWIN-T achieves 63.06 PQ vs. Lite-M2F's 62.29 and RT-M2F's 59.73, confirming consistent gains. Beyond segmentation, PRO-SCALE integrated with DINO achieves 49.4 AP (vs. DINO's 49.0), with MaskCLIP improving from 29.98 to 34.53 PQ on COCO, and MaskDINO maintaining 50.83 Box AP while cutting encoder GFLOPs by 47%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mask2Former (M2F) universal segmentation architecture and its deformable-attention transformer encoder, proposing PRO-SCALE as a drop-in replacement encoder that progressively expands token length across encoder layers rather than maintaining the full multi-scale token sequence at every layer. It departs from prior efficient DETR-style encoders (Lite-DETR, RT-DETR) that were designed for object detection and either discard multi-scale features or lack the ability to reduce pixel-embedding computation, making them poorly suited to segmentation. The Light-Pixel Embedding (LPE) module and Token Re-Calibration (TRC) unit are novel auxiliary components that complement the progressive scaling strategy, with TRC borrowing a contrastive-attention mechanism to inject large-scale context into small-scale tokens without additional transformer layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that over 50% of M2F's compute is consumed by the encoder, and that this can be cut by ~52% with negligible (or even slightly positive) performance impact, is practically significant for deploying universal segmentation models at scale. The progressive token scaling idea is simple, architecture-agnostic, and demonstrated across multiple backbones (Res50, SWIN-T/S/B/L, MViT-T), datasets (COCO, Cityscapes), and even extended to detection (DINO) and open-vocabulary segmentation (MaskCLIP, FCCLIP), suggesting broad applicability. Open questions include whether the strategy generalizes to even deeper encoders or larger token counts, whether the LPE module's simplicity holds under more challenging segmentation scenarios, and whether further gains are possible by combining PRO-SCALE with other efficiency techniques (e.g., quantization, distillation).
