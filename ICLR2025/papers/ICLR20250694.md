## Problem

Characterizing equivariant linear layers for permutation groups has traditionally relied on parameter-sharing, which becomes extremely cumbersome for complex multi-permutation structures like deep weight spaces. More critically, for sets of unaligned symmetric elements (wreath product equivariance), prior characterizations assumed the group acts transitively, a condition that fails for graphs and weight spaces, leaving a large class of non-Siamese equivariant layers unexplored and unused in practice.

## Input / Output

The method operates on k-tuples of elements from a group representation space V (e.g., k adjacency matrices of size n×n, two sets of MLP weights/biases, or two multisets of d-dimensional vectors), where each coordinate can be independently transformed by a group element and the k-tuple can be permuted. The equivariant linear layers map V^k → V^k, and the final network output is task-specific: a k-dimensional anomaly probability vector, a scalar Wasserstein distance, or an alignment permutation. A key structural detail is that the non-Siamese layers aggregate information across all k coordinates via inner products with a basis of the fixed-point subspace V_fixed, producing a shared vector replicated across all k output positions.

## Method

The core technique decomposes the representation space V into irreducible components and applies Schur's lemma to enumerate all equivariant linear maps, replacing the parameter-sharing approach. For the wreath product G≀S_k setting, the key theoretical result (Theorem 5.2) shows that every equivariant layer decomposes into a Siamese part (applying a G-equivariant map independently to each coordinate) plus s² non-Siamese layers, where s is the number of trivial irreducible representations in V. Each non-Siamese layer takes the form (v₁,…,vₖ) ↦ (Σ_ℓ⟨v_ℓ, eᵢ⟩eⱼ, …, Σ_ℓ⟨v_ℓ, eᵢ⟩eⱼ) for basis vectors eᵢ, eⱼ of V_fixed, effectively broadcasting a cross-coordinate aggregate back to every position. The authors implement this as SchurNet and validate it on graph anomaly detection (where Siamese networks fail by design at 10% accuracy vs. 100% for SchurNet), Wasserstein distance estimation, and weight space alignment, showing consistent improvements over Siamese-only and partial non-Siamese baselines.

## Results

On the synthetic graph anomaly detection task, SchurNet achieves 100% accuracy (η=0) and 97.0% accuracy (η=0.1), compared to 10% for Siamese-only networks and 97.5%/92% for the DSS baseline. On Wasserstein distance computation, SchurNet outperforms NProductNet on most datasets, with the largest gains on ModelNet-large (0.0468 vs 0.140 relative error at 2048 points) and RNAseq [300,500] (0.0334 vs 0.292). On weight space alignment, SchurNet achieves lower reconstruction loss than the Siamese baseline on both MNIST (0.251 vs 0.263) and CIFAR-10 (1.782 vs 1.788).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DSS baseline (Maron et al., 2020), which adds a single non-Siamese layer under the transitivity assumption, SchurNet's full set of four non-Siamese layers improves graph anomaly detection accuracy from 97.5% to 100% (η=0) and from 92% to 97.0% (η=0.1). Against NProductNet (Chen & Wang, 2024) on Wasserstein distance estimation, SchurNet wins on 8 out of 12 dataset-size configurations, with particularly large margins on ModelNet-large (0.0468 vs 0.140) and RNAseq [300,500] (0.0334 vs 0.292), but loses on noisy-sphere-6 at both sizes (0.0217 vs 0.015 and 0.0795 vs 0.049) and ties on uniform-256 and RNAseq [20,200]. Against the Siamese DWS alignment network (Navon et al., 2024), SchurNet achieves lower reconstruction loss on both MNIST (0.251 vs 0.263) and CIFAR-10 (1.782 vs 1.788), with the CIFAR-10 accuracy gap being more pronounced (0.0 vs 1.0e-4). Overall, the full irreducible-based characterization consistently matches or exceeds the restricted baselines, with the largest gains appearing in settings where the number of trivial representations (and hence non-Siamese layers) is large.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-derives known equivariant layer characterizations (DeepSets, 2-IGN, DWSNets) using irreducible representations and Schur's lemma rather than the standard parameter-sharing approach, yielding a significantly simpler derivation for deep weight spaces. It then extends the wreath product equivariance characterization from the transitive-group setting of Maron et al. (2020) and Wang et al. (2020) to general finite groups, revealing s² non-Siamese equivariant layers instead of the single one found under transitivity. The methodological lineage connects to the irreducible-representation approach used in rotation-equivariant networks (Thomas et al., 2018; Anderson et al., 2019) and the permutation-group irreducible analysis of Thiede et al. (2020), but applies it to weight spaces and unaligned symmetric sets where prior work relied on parameter sharing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that general (non-transitive) wreath products admit s² non-Siamese equivariant layers—potentially dozens or hundreds in weight-space settings—challenges the implicit assumption in prior work that Siamese plus one DSS layer captures the relevant structure. The irreducible-based methodology reduces implementation bookkeeping (eliminating the need for the large case-by-case tables in Navon et al., 2023) and generalizes naturally to compact infinite groups. Open questions include extending the irreducible decomposition to k-IGN for k > 2, where intermediate features live in n^k-dimensional tensor spaces, and exploring whether the additional non-Siamese layers yield larger gains on more complex or real-world tasks beyond the synthetic and moderate-scale experiments presented here.
