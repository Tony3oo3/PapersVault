## Problem

Fine-tuning aligned LLMs on new domain-specific knowledge risks catastrophic forgetting of pretraining capabilities, and standard approaches like continued pretraining (CPT) or instruction fine-tuning (IFT) do not explicitly guide how the model should process and internalize new content. The paper asks whether the same prompting techniques that steer LLM behavior at inference time can also improve the fine-tuning process itself, effectively teaching LLMs *how* to learn new material.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw domain-specific text sequences (up to 4096 tokens) from corpora like OpenMedText (121K journal articles + 29 textbooks) or 306K financial news articles, and prepends a randomly sampled contextual prompt (~10-30 tokens, adding only ~0.8% to sequence length) to form the training input. The output is updated LLM parameters trained with a standard causal language modeling objective conditioned on the prompt. Prompts are either manually designed from 10 educational-theory templates or generated text-adaptively by GPT-4o-mini per training batch.

## Method

Contextual Fine-Tuning (CFT) generalizes instruction tuning by prepending educational-theory-inspired prompts (e.g., 'Concentrate on understanding the core principles…', 'Critically analyze the upcoming information…') to domain text during training, with the loss computed only on the text tokens conditioned on the prompt (Eq. 3). The key mechanism is that these prompts alter the gradient signal during backpropagation, steering parameter updates toward semantically meaningful representations rather than pure next-token prediction. Synthetic experiments on compositional function classes confirm that CFT gradients align closely with true target-function gradients (inner product approaching 1), while CPT and negative-prompt ablations do not. The approach adds negligible computational overhead (~0.8% more training time) and can be combined with IFT for additive gains, outperforming CPT by 1.85–4.32% on medical/financial benchmarks and 6.71% on Gemini-1.5-Flash.

## Results

On medical benchmarks (MMLU subsets + MedQA), CFT achieves 47.81% average accuracy with Llama-2-7B-Chat (vs. 45.96% for CPT and 45.05% for the base Chat model), and 56.56% with Llama-2-13B-Chat (vs. 54.31% CPT). On financial benchmarks (FiQA, Causal20, MultiFin F1), CFT reaches 67.96% average F1 (7B) and 70.45% (13B), compared to 63.64% and 67.45% for CPT respectively. The relative improvement over CPT (%Δ_CPT^CFT) ranges from 1.85% (7B medical) to 4.32% (7B financial), and scales to 6.71% on Gemini-1.5-Flash for filtered MedQA questions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFT consistently outperforms Continued Pretraining (CPT) across all benchmarks and model scales: +1.85% average on 7B medical, +2.25% on 13B medical, +4.32% on 7B financial, and +3.0% on 13B financial. Against AdaptLLM (Cheng et al., 2024), a domain-specific continued pretraining method that converts corpora into reading-comprehension format, CFT achieves 4.89% higher average accuracy across all seven medical benchmarks (e.g., 48.15% vs. 44.45% on Anatomy, 54.00% vs. 45.00% on Medical Genetics). When combined with Instruction Fine-Tuning, CFT+IFT yields a 2.95% improvement over the base model on medical benchmarks versus only 1.91% for CPT+IFT, and a 36.28% F1 improvement on financial benchmarks versus 8.67% for CPT+IFT. On Gemini-1.5-Flash, CFT outperforms CPT by 6.71% on filtered MedQA questions (43.89% vs. 37.18%), suggesting the benefit amplifies with model scale. Negative contextual prompts (designed to mislead) degrade CFT performance by 2.57% (7B) and 1.36% (13B) on medical benchmarks, confirming that the semantic content of prompts—not merely their presence—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions Contextual Fine-Tuning (CFT) as a generalization of instruction tuning that blends in-context learning with gradient-based learning, drawing on the instruction-tuning paradigm of Wei et al. (2022a) and the few-shot/CoT prompting literature (Brown et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2022c). The key conceptual departure is moving prompts from inference time to training time: rather than using instruction-response pairs or chain-of-thought demonstrations at test time, CFT prepends pedagogically-motivated contextual prompts (grounded in constructivist learning theory, cognitive load theory, and Bloom's taxonomy) to raw domain text during continued pretraining, thereby modifying the gradient signal. It also contrasts with AdaptLLM (Cheng et al., 2024), which converts corpora into reading-comprehension format, and with standard continued pretraining that uses no prompts at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFT offers a near-free modification to domain fine-tuning (≈0.8% increase in training time) that consistently outperforms continued pretraining across medical and financial domains and model scales (7B, 13B, Gemini-1.5-Flash), and the synthetic gradient-alignment experiments provide a mechanistic explanation for why the prompts help. However, the practical gains are modest (1–4% on Llama models), the method has only been validated on high-information-density corpora (journals, textbooks), and the Gemini experiment is constrained by API limits (500 examples × 5000 chars). Open questions include whether CFT generalizes to lower-density data (e.g., Reddit posts), how it interacts with the explanatory text already present in pretraining corpora (analogous to the CoT locality hypothesis of Prystawski et al., 2023), and whether the specific prompt content matters more than the mere presence of additional conditioning tokens.
