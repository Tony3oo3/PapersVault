## Problem

Whether DNNs memorize their training data is a fundamental open question, and the most direct way to verify it is to reconstruct training data from model parameters. However, all prior reconstruction methods (Haim et al., Loo et al., Buzaglo et al.) rely on linear or approximately linear training dynamics characteristic of shallow, wide MLPs in the NTK regime, and fail on practical DNNs like ResNet where gradient directions change substantially throughout training. The key missing ingredient is an accurate characterization of the full non-linear training trajectory, not just the initial and final parameter states.

## Input / Output

The method requires the initial parameters θ₀ (e.g., pre-trained weights) and final parameters θ_f of a fine-tuned model, along with the dataset size and training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, number of epochs). It outputs reconstructed training images at the original resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet/ViT), or reconstructed text sentences in the NLP setting. Notably, the method can operate with unknown hyperparameters by grid-searching the learning rate for a fixed batch size based on early-step loss values, and can handle unknown dataset size by using an oversized dummy set where extra images collapse to low-gradient noise.

## Method

SimuDy formulates reconstruction as an inverse problem: it simulates the full training dynamics by running T epochs × N batches of SGD from θ₀ on a dummy dataset initialized as Gaussian noise, then optimizes the dummy images end-to-end so that the simulated final parameters θ̂_f match the true θ_f. The reconstruction loss is a cosine similarity between the parameter displacement vectors (θ_f − θ₀) and (θ̂_f − θ₀), augmented with a total-variation prior to encourage natural image structure. Gradient clipping is applied during the simulated updates to prevent explosion from noisy initializations and to inject a norm-based prior. The critical distinction from prior work is that the dummy images are differentiated through the entire multi-step training loop, so the optimization captures the non-linear evolution of gradients at every intermediate checkpoint rather than approximating dynamics with a linear combination of initial and final gradients. For ViT architectures, an additional loss term on position-embedding parameter changes is added to enforce correct spatial patch alignment.

## Results

On ResNet-18 fine-tuned on CIFAR-10, SimuDy achieves SSIM scores of 0.1982 (50 images), 0.1998 (60 images), and 0.1197 (120 images), substantially outperforming Loo et al. (0.0774 at 50 images, 0.0482 at 120 images) and Buzaglo et al. (0.0297 at 50 images, 0.0239 at 120 images). On MLPs trained on 100 CIFAR-10 images, SimuDy reaches SSIM 0.3374 versus 0.1384 for Loo et al. and 0.1426 for Buzaglo et al. The method also generalizes to higher resolutions (64×64 Tiny ImageNet), larger architectures (ResNet-50, ViT on ImageNet), and NLP (TinyBERT on CoLA), with SSIM 0.2083 on SVHN and 0.2125 on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimuDy consistently and substantially outperforms all three baselines across every tested setting. Against Loo et al. (2024), which linearly combines initial and final gradients, SimuDy achieves roughly 2–4× higher SSIM on ResNet-18 (e.g., 0.1982 vs. 0.0774 at 50 images; 0.1197 vs. 0.0482 at 120 images) and 2.4× higher on MLPs (0.3374 vs. 0.1384). Against Buzaglo et al. (2024), which relies solely on final-parameter gradients with KKT conditions, the gap is even wider: SimuDy scores 0.1982 vs. 0.0297 on ResNet-18 (50 images) and 0.3374 vs. 0.1426 on MLPs (100 images). Against Haim et al. (2022), the original KKT-based method, SimuDy achieves SSIM 0.5378 in the binary classification setting on a 20-image MLP, far exceeding the baseline. The advantage grows with dataset size and model complexity, as non-linear training dynamics become more pronounced, causing the gradient-based baselines to degrade sharply (Loo et al. drops to ~0.05 SSIM at 120 images) while SimuDy maintains usable reconstructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-data-reconstruction-from-parameters line initiated by Haim et al. (2022, KKT/max-margin for homogeneous networks), Buzaglo et al. (2024, multi-class extension), and Loo et al. (2024, NTK-regime reconstruction with initial+final gradient combination). All prior methods characterize training dynamics through static parameter differences (initial vs. final), which is valid only under the linear-dynamics assumption of wide MLPs in the NTK regime. SimuDy departs fundamentally by simulating the full non-linear training trajectory with a dummy dataset and optimizing that dataset to match the real dynamics, borrowing the cosine-similarity loss and TV prior from the gradient-inversion literature (Geiping et al., 2020) but applying them across an entire multi-step training process rather than a single gradient step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a substantial fraction of training images can be recovered from the parameters of a practical DNN (ResNet), moving the memorization question from theoretical wide-MLP settings to architectures actually deployed in practice. The key open question is scalability: reconstruction quality degrades sharply with dataset size (SSIM drops from ~0.20 at 50 images to ~0.12 at 120), and the method requires storing the full training computation graph, limiting it to small datasets on a single GPU. Future work on more efficient dynamics characterization (e.g., exploiting low-dimensional structure in parameter trajectories) could unlock reconstruction at scale, with direct implications for privacy auditing of deployed models.
