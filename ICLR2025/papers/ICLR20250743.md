## Problem

The standard VLM training recipe fully pre-trains a language model on text before introducing image data in a separate phase, but it remains unclear whether this two-step pipeline is optimal or whether integrating images earlier in pre-training yields better downstream performance. This paper systematically investigates the timing, ratio, and method of introducing visual tokens during pre-training to determine whether and how an integrated approach outperforms the conventional fully-then-partially multimodal strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250743/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (each encoded to 729 tokens via a frozen SigLIP-400M encoder projected through a two-layer MLP) concatenated with text prompts, at a fixed sequence length of 1024 tokens. Image tokens are masked from the autoregressive cross-entropy loss during both pre-training and fine-tuning, so the model only predicts text tokens. Outputs are free-form text responses evaluated 0-shot across vision-language benchmarks (VQAv2, GQA, TextVQA, POPE, RefCOCO, OCID-Ref) and text-only benchmarks (ARC-easy, HellaSwag, PIQA, etc.).

## Method

The paper trains ~300 models at 1.4B parameters (DCLM-1B backbone) by resuming text-only pre-training checkpoints at 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, and 100% completion, then continuing with a mixed image-text dataset (DataComp-DR-1B captions + DCLM text) for 28B tokens under a continued cosine cooldown schedule rather than a re-warmup. Key ablations vary the image-to-text ratio (0–100%), the proportion of instruction-tuning data mixed into pre-training, and the number of LLaVA fine-tuning epochs (1–6). The central finding is that interrupting text pre-training at 80% and adding 10–20% visual tokens during the cooldown phase outperforms the conventional fully-pretrained-then-re-warmup approach by ~2% on average across six vision-language tasks, while preserving text-only performance. Mixing instruction-tuning data into the pre-training stage actively degrades vision performance, and fine-tuning beyond 4 epochs causes overfitting on both modalities.

## Results

For a 1.4B model, introducing visual tokens at 80% of text-only pre-training (with a 10% image / 90% text ratio during the remaining 28B tokens) yields a ~2% average improvement over the conventional approach of fully pre-training the LLM then re-warming with image data. At the 1B scale, 10–20% visual tokens during the cooldown phase is optimal; going below or above this range degrades downstream performance. The 7B-scale validation (Table 3) confirms the trend: adding images during pre-training improves VQAv2 by 1.7 pts (76.39 vs 74.69), RefCOCO by 5.3 pts (0.642 vs 0.589), and OCID by 6.4 pts (0.461 vs 0.397) over a token-matched text-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Prismatic 7B, the proposed 1B model trails by 2.3–9.7 points across vision benchmarks (e.g., VQAv2 76.82 vs 79.05, RefCOCO 61.29 vs 73.62), a gap largely attributable to the 7× parameter difference; on POPE (hallucination) the gap narrows to under 1 point (87.33 vs 88.28). Against PaliGemma 3B, the 1B model is further behind on most tasks (VQAv2 76.82 vs 85.6, TextVQA 49.05 vs 73.2), though it slightly edges out PaliGemma on POPE (87.33 vs 87.0). The more informative internal comparison at 7B scale (Table 3) shows the proposed early-image-introduction strategy consistently outperforms the conventional fully-pretrained-then-re-warm approach: VQAv2 +1.7, GQA +0.5, TextVQA +5.2, RefCOCO +5.3, OCID +6.4, and POPE +0.4 points, with only VizWiz showing a small reversal (10.88 vs 11.69). Overall, the paper's contribution is a training-strategy improvement rather than a new architecture, and the external comparisons serve as sanity checks rather than the primary evidence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage VLM training paradigm (fully pre-train an LLM, then add images in a second phase) followed by LLaVA, Flamingo, BLIP-2/3, IDEFICS, DeepSeek-VL, Qwen-VL, PaliGemma, and Janus. It builds on the DCLM-1B text pre-training recipe and the Prismatic fine-tuning framework, but introduces a key methodological shift: resuming a partially pre-trained LLM checkpoint during its cooldown phase and mixing in image-text data before the text-only schedule completes. The paper positions itself as a systematic empirical ablation of this integrated approach, contrasting it against the re-warmup strategy used by nearly all prior open VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that introducing images at 80% pre-training completion outperforms the standard fully-pretrained-then-add-images pipeline challenges a widely adopted assumption in the VLM community and offers a concrete, actionable recipe (10–20% image ratio at 1B scale, no instruction data in pre-training, 2–4 fine-tuning epochs). However, the evidence at 7B scale is limited to a single token-matched comparison, and the paper does not explore interleaved data, PrefixLM, or higher token multipliers at 1B, leaving open whether the optimal image-text ratio and timing shift at larger scales. The modest 2% average improvement and the scale-dependent ratio behavior (larger fractions preferred at 79M) suggest that VLM scaling laws may differ meaningfully from LLM scaling laws, a question the authors flag but do not resolve.
