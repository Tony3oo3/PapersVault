## Problem

Diffusion models achieve remarkable empirical success on high-dimensional data, yet classical score estimation theory yields sample complexity bounds that scale exponentially with the ambient dimension, revealing a curse of dimensionality. Prior work showing that latent subspace or independence structure can be exploited to avoid this curse required specialized architectures (e.g., linear autoencoders with known latent dimension, or sparsity-constrained networks) that are difficult to satisfy in practice. This paper asks whether a standard single hidden layer network, without any architectural modifications tailored to the latent structure, can provably adapt to hidden low-dimensional structure and achieve sample complexity governed by the intrinsic rather than ambient dimension.

## Input / Output

The method takes n iid samples x_0^i from a target distribution p_0 on R^D (assumed to have either a d-dimensional linear subspace structure with d << D, or K independent variable groups) together with iid Gaussian noise draws for the denoising score matching loss. It outputs a sample from p_ζ (a slightly noise-perturbed version of p_0 at early-stopping time ζ) via the exponential integrator reverse SDE, with a guarantee that KL(p_ζ ∥ Law(ŷ_{T−ζ})) ≤ ε². The score functions are learned separately at N discrete timesteps, each as an ERM over an F_1 norm ball, and the final sample is produced by integrating the learned score field backward from a standard Gaussian.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a statistical analysis of denoising score matching (DSM) over the Barron space F_1 — the infinite-width limit of single hidden layer ReLU networks — showing that the F_1 norm of the true score function decomposes under latent structure so that the required norm bound R_t depends on the intrinsic dimension d (or per-group dimensions d_i) rather than the ambient dimension D. For subspace structure, the score decomposes into a latent score embedded via the unknown orthogonal matrix U (which preserves the F_1 norm by a key embedding fact) plus a linear projection term; for independent components, it is a sum of low-index functions each depending on only d_i coordinates. This structural decomposition, combined with Rademacher complexity bounds over F_1 norm balls and a truncation argument for unbounded data, yields a score estimation rate of Õ_d(1)(poly(D)/n)^{2/(d+5)}. The end-to-end sampling guarantee is obtained by plugging this score error into the exponential integrator analysis of Benton et al. (2024), giving a first-of-its-kind sample complexity bound for learning a diffusion model over single hidden layer networks that adapts to intrinsic dimensionality without specialized architectures.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is an end-to-end sample complexity bound for learning diffusion models over single hidden layer (Barron) networks that depends exponentially only on the intrinsic dimension d, not the ambient dimension D. For latent subspace structure (Theorem 3.3, Corollary 3.4), the score matching risk scales as Õ_d(1)(poly(D)/n)^(2/(d+5)), and the end-to-end sampling guarantee requires n ≥ Õ_d(1) poly(D)/ζ · ε^(-(d+5)) samples to achieve KL(p_ζ ∥ Law(ŷ_{T-ζ})) ≤ ε². For hidden independent components (Theorem 3.7, Corollary 3.8), the rate becomes n ≥ poly(D)/ζ · max_i poly_{d_i}(K) · ε^(-(d_i+5)), where d_i is the dimension of the i-th independent block. Empirically, on synthetic tasks with d=2 latent structure embedded in D up to 64 dimensions, the Barron network maintains stable sample quality while the random feature model becomes unstable at D=32 and D=64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the experiments (Section 4), the Barron single hidden layer network is compared against a random feature model (Rahimi & Recht, 2007) and a two hidden layer network on two synthetic tasks. The Barron model significantly outperforms the random feature model as D increases: at D=32 and D=64, the random feature model's samples become unstable and fall outside the visualization frame, while the Barron model continues to produce reasonable samples, consistent with the theory predicting exponential dependence on d=2 rather than D. The two hidden layer network outperforms the Barron model in both experiments, benefiting from its additional nonlinear layer and more parameters. Theoretically, the paper improves upon Chen et al. (2023b) by removing the need for a specialized linear autoencoder architecture, tightening the n-dependence from n^(-(2-o(1))/(d+5)) to n^(-2/(d+5)), and improving the σ_t dependence from σ_t^(-2) to σ_t^(-4/(d+5)) near t=0. Compared to Oko et al. (2023), the Barron approach avoids computationally intractable bounded sparsity constraints and relaxes technical assumptions (e.g., no requirement for C^∞ density near boundaries), though Oko et al. achieve a sharper W_1 rate of n^(-(3-δ)/(d+4)} under stronger assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed threads: (1) the reduction of diffusion model sampling quality to L2 score estimation error (Chen et al. 2023d; Benton et al. 2024), and (2) the statistical learning theory of single hidden layer networks in the Barron space (Bach 2017). Prior work on adaptive sample complexity for diffusion models under latent structure (Chen et al. 2023b; Oko et al. 2023) required specialized architectures—linear autoencoders with known latent dimension or bounded weight sparsity—to avoid the curse of dimensionality. The key departure here is showing that the Barron F1 norm's low-index structure is sufficient for adaptation to latent subspaces and independent components without any architectural modifications, making the result applicable to the most natural (and practically relevant) single hidden layer network class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end sample complexity bound for learning a diffusion model over single hidden layer networks that adapts to intrinsic dimensionality, closing a gap between the empirical success of diffusion models on structured high-dimensional data and the curse-of-dimensionality barriers in classical distribution recovery. The result suggests that the low-index structure of the Barron space is a sufficient inductive bias for structure discovery, potentially explaining why shallow networks in practice can learn from data concentrated on low-dimensional manifolds. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-linear manifolds, latent diffusion models, whether gradient descent can efficiently learn the low-index structure, and closing the gap between the n^{-2/(d+5)} rate and the minimax-optimal n^{-2/(d+4)} rate. The poly(D) pre-factors in the sample complexity (vs. polylog(D) in Chen et al. 2023b) remain a limitation tied to the absence of uniform boundedness constraints on the score functions.
