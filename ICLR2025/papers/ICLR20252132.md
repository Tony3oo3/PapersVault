## Problem

While prior work established that gradient descent on two-layer ReLU networks converges to non-robust solutions (Vardi et al., 2022; Frei et al., 2024), the specific mechanism driving this non-robustness remained uncharacterized. Min & Vidal (2024) conjectured that hidden-layer weights converge to averages of cluster-center features, but no rigorous finite-time proof existed. This paper identifies and proves this 'feature averaging' implicit bias as a principal factor behind adversarial vulnerability, and shows that fine-grained supervision can eliminate it.

## Input / Output

Theoretically, inputs are data points x = μ_j + ξ ∈ R^d where μ_j are k mutually orthogonal cluster centers (each with norm √d) and ξ ~ N(0, I_d), partitioned into positive and negative classes; the network outputs a scalar binary score f_θ(x) ∈ R. For the fine-grained supervision variant, the output becomes a k-dimensional vector F_θ(x) ∈ R^k (one ReLU-based score per cluster), from which a binary prediction is derived by summing positive-class softmax probabilities minus negative-class probabilities. Empirically, inputs are MNIST (28×28 grayscale) or CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB) images processed through a frozen ResNet18 backbone, with only the final two-layer ReLU head trained.

## Method

The paper proves that gradient descent on a two-layer ReLU network (fixed diagonal second layer with ±1/m weights, logistic loss) trained on multi-cluster binary data converges to a feature-averaging solution: after Ω(η⁻¹) iterations, each hidden neuron's weight aligns with the average of cluster centers in its class, w_{s,r} ≈ λ · Σ_{j∈J_s} μ_j/‖μ_j‖². The proof decomposes each weight into feature and noise components, uses an auto-balance argument to show loss derivatives remain balanced across data points, and proves all same-class neurons activate on all same-class data, reducing the dynamics to a tractable linear system. This averaging renders the network vulnerable to perturbations of radius Ω(√(d/k)) along the direction −Σ_{j∈J_+}μ_j + Σ_{j∈J_-}μ_j, while an Ω(√d)-robust solution provably exists. The paper further shows that providing cluster-level k-class labels instead of binary labels causes GD to learn feature-decoupled weights (each neuron aligns with a single μ_j), yielding a binary classifier with optimal O(√d) robustness radius.

## Results

Theoretically, the paper proves that gradient descent on a two-layer ReLU network converges to a feature-averaging solution with robust radius only Ω(√(d/k)), while an optimal robust network with radius O(√d) exists — a gap of Θ(√k). Empirically, on synthetic data, MNIST, and CIFAR-10, models trained with 10-class (fine-grained) labels consistently achieve higher ℓ₂-robust accuracy than those trained with 2-class (binary) labels across all perturbation radii tested via PGD attacks. In the adversarial training comparison on synthetic data (Appendix B.2), fine-grained supervision (10-class labels) yields slightly better robust accuracy than PGD-based adversarial training at both radius 20 and 40.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical comparison is between standard binary (2-class) training, fine-grained multi-class (10-class) training, and PGD-based adversarial training (Madry et al., 2018). On all three datasets (synthetic, MNIST, CIFAR-10), the 10-class fine-grained model achieves strictly higher robust accuracy than the 2-class model at every perturbation radius, confirming the theoretical prediction that feature decoupling yields optimal O(√d) robustness versus the Ω(√(d/k)) robustness of feature averaging. In the synthetic adversarial training experiment (Figure 11), PGD training at radius 40 improves robustness over the 2-class baseline but remains slightly below the 10-class fine-grained model, suggesting that fine-grained supervision is a more effective robustness mechanism than explicit adversarial training in this setting. The weight correlation matrices (Figure 2) further confirm that 2-class models learn averaged features (off-diagonal correlations) while 10-class models learn decoupled features (near-diagonal correlation matrices), on both synthetic data and CIFAR-10 with a ResNet-18 backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Vardi et al. (2022) and Frei et al. (2024), who showed via KKT-condition analysis that gradient flow on two-layer ReLU networks converges to non-robust solutions under multi-cluster data distributions. The key departure is a shift from characterizing limiting KKT solutions to performing a finite-time analysis of gradient descent dynamics, which reveals the concrete mechanism—termed 'feature averaging'—by which each hidden neuron's weight converges to a weighted average of cluster-center features. This resolves the conjecture of Min & Vidal (2024) that weight vectors align with averaged cluster features, and extends the analysis to show that providing fine-grained (multi-class) supervision breaks the averaging bias and recovers optimal robustness, a remedy not addressed in prior KKT-based work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, feature-level explanation for a long-standing puzzle: why gradient descent, which finds well-generalizing solutions, systematically produces networks vulnerable to small adversarial perturbations. The practical implication—that simply relabeling a binary task as a multi-class task (one class per feature/cluster) can restore near-optimal robustness—is actionable and connects to empirical findings on part-based models and fine-grained annotations. However, the theory is confined to two-layer ReLU networks with orthogonal equinorm clusters and small initialization, and the real-world experiments only train the last two layers of a pre-trained ResNet18, leaving open whether feature averaging persists in deeper, wider networks trained end-to-end and whether the fine-grained-supervision remedy scales to large-scale vision tasks.
