## Problem

State-of-the-art deep clustering methods suffer from severe overconfidence—predicted confidence far exceeds actual prediction accuracy—yet this issue has been entirely overlooked in the clustering literature. Existing calibration techniques are unsuitable for unsupervised clustering: post-hoc methods like Temperature Scaling require a labeled validation set, while regularization-based methods like Label Smoothing uniformly penalize all samples, destroying the high-confidence predictions needed for reliable pseudo-label selection. Additionally, prior self-labeling methods use a fixed global threshold that ignores the model's evolving learning status, selecting too few samples early and too many noisy samples late in training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images at varying resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR, 96×96 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet subsets, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet), applying weak augmentation for pseudo-label generation and strong augmentation for the training target. It produces both a cluster assignment and a calibrated confidence score per sample, with the final prediction taken from the calibration head rather than the clustering head. Within each batch, K-means partitions the feature space into K mini-clusters (K ranges from 40 to 1000 depending on dataset complexity), and the number of selected pseudo-labels per class is determined dynamically by summing calibrated confidences over the top-ranked samples.

## Method

CDC employs a dual-head architecture (clustering head and calibration head, both 3-layer MLPs) on a MoCo-v2 pretrained ResNet-34 backbone. The calibration head is trained with a region-aware loss: K-means partitions batch features into mini-clusters, and the head is penalized to match the average clustering-head prediction within each mini-cluster, which theoretically penalizes confidence only in unreliable regions (crossing decision boundaries) while preserving it in reliable regions (Theorem 1). The clustering head uses the calibration head's confidence to dynamically determine how many pseudo-labels to select per class, replacing the fixed global threshold. A feature prototype-based initialization strategy sets MLP weights to K-means centers of the pretrained features, transferring discriminative ability from the backbone and reducing training variance. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 2) guarantees that the calibration head's ECE is no worse than the clustering head's under mild conditions.

## Results

CDC-Cal achieves the best expected calibration error (ECE) across all six benchmarks: 1.1% on CIFAR-10, 4.9% on CIFAR-20, 0.9% on STL-10, 0.8% on ImageNet-10, 7.7% on ImageNet-Dogs, and 11.0% on Tiny-ImageNet, representing roughly a 5× average improvement over the best competing method. On clustering accuracy (ACC), CDC-Clu reaches 94.9% on CIFAR-10, 61.9% on CIFAR-20, 93.1% on STL-10, 97.2% on ImageNet-10, 79.3% on ImageNet-Dogs, and 34.0% on Tiny-ImageNet, ranking first in 11 of 12 clustering metric cases (ACC, ARI, NMI across six datasets). The method also surpasses the supervised+MoCo-v2 baseline on CIFAR-10 (94.9% vs 94.1%) and STL-10 (93.1% vs 90.5%) in clustering accuracy while achieving far lower ECE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against representation-learning baselines (MoCo-v2, SimSiam, BYOL, ProPos, CoNR), CDC-Clu achieves the highest ACC on all six datasets, e.g., 94.9% vs. 94.3% (ProPos) and 93.2% (CoNR) on CIFAR-10, and 79.3% vs. 79.4% (CoNR) on ImageNet-Dogs; however, these baselines do not report ECE since they lack a confidence head. Among self-labeling methods, CDC-Cal dominates on ECE by a wide margin: 1.1% vs. 6.7% (SCAN-3) and 7.8% (SPICE-3) on CIFAR-10, and 4.9% vs. 39.0% (SCAN-3) and 40.6% (SPICE-3) on CIFAR-20. On clustering accuracy, CDC-Clu outperforms SCAN-3 (94.9% vs. 90.3% on CIFAR-10; 61.9% vs. 51.2% on CIFAR-20) and SPICE-3 (94.9% vs. 91.5% on CIFAR-10; 61.9% vs. 58.4% on CIFAR-20). CDC also surpasses CC (85.2% ACC, 6.2% ECE on CIFAR-10) and SeCu (92.6% ACC, 4.9% ECE) on both metrics. The only setting where a baseline exceeds CDC is the supervised+MoCo-v2 reference on ImageNet-10 (99.9% ACC) and ImageNet-Dogs (99.5% ACC), which uses ground-truth labels unavailable in the unsupervised clustering setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-labeling-based deep clustering paradigm established by SCAN and SPICE by introducing a dual-head architecture (clustering head + calibration head) that explicitly addresses the overconfidence problem previously overlooked in this line of work. It departs from prior calibration methods in supervised learning (Temperature Scaling, Label Smoothing, Focal Loss) by showing they are inapplicable or harmful in the unsupervised clustering setting, and instead proposes a region-aware penalty that selectively reduces confidence only in unreliable regions. The feature prototype-based initialization strategy also departs from the standard random initialization of the clustering head, drawing on the discriminative structure already present in MoCo-v2 pre-trained features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to systematically study and address confidence calibration in deep clustering, filling a gap that becomes critical when clustering outputs feed into downstream decision-making systems (medical diagnosis, autonomous driving). The finding that overconfidence in pseudo-labeling-based deep clustering is worse than in supervised learning challenges the implicit assumption that model confidence reflects true cluster membership probability. Open questions include whether the calibration approach generalizes beyond image clustering to other modalities, whether it remains effective without strong self-supervised pre-training, and whether the K-means-based region partitioning scales efficiently to very large datasets.
