## Problem

Gradient-based token optimization methods like GCG rely on linear approximations of the loss in the embedding space, but in discrete token spaces these approximations are inaccurate, yielding only marginal improvement over random token selection. The paper proves the model-agnostic LLM input generation problem is NP-hard, showing that no generic efficient algorithm exists and that problem-specific structure must be exploited to make optimization tractable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251367/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a malicious prompt (e.g., 'how to make a bomb?') and a target LLM, and produces an adversarial suffix string s of bounded length n over the model's vocabulary such that the concatenated input ⟨x, s⟩ elicits a harmful affirmative response rather than a refusal. Unusually, the method also generates a chain of LoRA fine-tuned model checkpoints (768 total at rank 16) that serve as intermediate optimization targets; these checkpoints are shared across all test queries rather than being per-prompt, and the final output is the suffix that successfully jailbreaks the original base model.

## Method

The core insight is a functional duality: the objective f_p(x) can be lifted to F(p,x) where p (model parameters) is continuous and x (tokens) is discrete, allowing gradient descent over p to smooth the combinatorial search over x. Concretely, the method first fine-tunes the model via LoRA for 64 epochs (learning rate 2e-5) on the test dataset to minimize the jailbreak loss for a fixed initial suffix, saving all intermediate parameter states p_0 through p_t. It then works backwards from the most misaligned checkpoint p_t to the base model p_0, performing greedy random token substitution at each step and warm-starting from the solution found at the next weaker checkpoint. A binary search strategy over the checkpoint chain (Algorithm 2) accelerates this process by jumping to intermediate checkpoints rather than iterating through all 768 states sequentially. The key mechanism is that adjacent checkpoints differ by a single gradient update, so their sublevel sets S_p^a(F) are highly similar, making the warm-started greedy search effective at each step.

## Results

The FH-GR method achieves 76.5% attack success rate (ASR) at 1000 iterations on Llama-3 8B Instruct and 99.5% on Llama-2 7B, substantially outperforming GCG (59.0% and 63.5%) and AutoDAN (19.5% and 61.5%) on these robust models. On weaker models (Mistral-v0.3 and Vicuna-v1.5), all methods saturate near 100% ASR by 500 iterations. The RBO analysis (Table 1) reveals that token gradients provide only marginal ranking quality over random selection (RBO ≈ 0.51 vs. 0.50), explaining why gradient-free FH-GR with random token substitution outperforms gradient-based GCG. The paper reports a 20–30% improvement in ASR over existing methods on safe open-source models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), the primary gradient-based token-search baseline, FH-GR achieves a 17.5-point ASR improvement on Llama-3 8B (76.5% vs. 59.0% at 1000 iterations) and a 36-point improvement on Llama-2 7B (99.5% vs. 63.5%). Notably, FH-GR uses random token substitution rather than gradients, yet still outperforms GCG, which requires 85% more computation per iteration for gradient calculation. Against AutoDAN (Liu et al., 2024a), a prompt-level genetic algorithm, FH-GR dominates on Llama-3 (76.5% vs. 19.5%) and Llama-2 (99.5% vs. 61.5%), with the gap being especially stark on Llama-3 where AutoDAN's limited suffix diversity from the DAN framework proves insufficient. On the weaker Mistral and Vicuna models, all methods converge to near-saturation ASR, making differentiation minimal. The paper also shows that applying the FH framework on top of GCG (FH-GCG) further improves GCG from 59.0% to 77.5% on Llama-3, confirming the homotopy mechanism is orthogonal to the underlying search strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the homotopy optimization framework (Dunlavy & O'Leary, 2005) from continuous parameter spaces to the discrete token space of LLMs by exploiting the functional duality between model parameters and inputs. It departs from the GCG paradigm (Zou et al., 2023), which treats the objective f_p(x) as static and optimizes over tokens via embedding-space gradients, by lifting the problem to F(p,x) and first optimizing over the continuous parameter p via fine-tuning. The approach also draws on adversarial training concepts from image models (FGSM, PGD) but inverts them: rather than training for robustness, the method deliberately misaligns the model to create a sequence of easier subproblems. The NP-hardness proof for model-agnostic LLM input generation is a new theoretical contribution not present in prior jailbreak optimization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that token gradients provide only marginal improvement over random selection (RBO ≈ 0.51 vs 0.50) challenges the foundational assumption underlying GCG and related methods, suggesting that the 85% computational overhead of gradient computation may be unjustified for discrete token optimization. The 20–30% ASR improvement over GCG and AutoDAN on Llama-2 and Llama-3 is practically significant for red-teaming, and the observation that intermediate misaligned checkpoints can facilitate attacks on the base model has broader implications for understanding how alignment training shapes the adversarial landscape. Open questions include whether the method scales to larger models (tested only up to 8B), whether full fine-tuning (vs. LoRA) changes the results, and the limited transferability of attacks across checkpoints for well-aligned models like Llama-2/3, which hints at a deeper structure in how alignment training transforms the adversarial subspace.
