## Problem

Recognizing concepts in satellite imagery depends on spatial scale: large concepts like forests are better detected at low resolution, while fine-grained concepts like swimming pools require high resolution. However, high-resolution imagery is expensive and sparsely available, and prior work either optimizes accuracy without considering acquisition cost or optimizes cost without reasoning about the scale of the target concept. This paper addresses the missing holistic treatment that jointly considers concept scale, underlying geography, and HR acquisition budget.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text search concept, a set of locations each with one LR Sentinel-2 image (10 m GSD) and a corresponding set of K HR images (1 m GSD for NAIP, 5 m GSD for NICFI), and a user-specified budget B for HR image acquisition. It outputs per-location concept presence scores, a ranked list of locations where HR imagery should be acquired (within budget), and a final retrieval ranking. Notably, one LR image tiles into K HR images (K=100 for NAIP, K=4 for NICFI), so the budget is expressed in locations rather than individual images, and the LLM component consumes only the concept name as text to recommend the appropriate resolution.

## Method

The framework combines three components. First, a knowledge-distilled LR model (M_KD^LR) is trained to minimize MSE against HR model scores (Eq. 3), effectively transferring fine-grained detection capability from HR to LR imagery via a cross-resolution teacher-student setup. Second, an LLM is prompted with in-context examples of seen concepts paired with their empirically best-performing modality to infer whether an unseen concept is better suited to LR or HR imagery. Third, a model-disagreement criterion (Eq. 5) uses the KD model as a stand-in for the HR model to rank locations by predicted disagreement between LR and HR scores, enabling budget-constrained HR acquisition without needing HR imagery over the full region. At inference, the LLM routes each concept to LR-only inference or to a hybrid pipeline where the top-B disagreement locations receive HR inference and the remainder are scored by the KD model on LR imagery.

## Results

On the zero-shot GRAFT model (Sentinel-2/NAIP benchmark), the full system achieves mAP@100 = 0.633 and mAP@20 = 0.639, outperforming the all-HR baseline (0.501/0.513) by 26.3% while using only 5,954 sq.km of HR imagery versus 25,163 sq.km (a 76.3% reduction). For the fully-supervised ResNet-50 model, the full system reaches mAP@100 = 0.736 and mAP@20 = 0.796, a 6% improvement over the all-HR baseline (0.695/0.735) with the same 5× HR reduction. The knowledge-distilled LR model alone (no HR at inference) already surpasses the raw LR model by 10.8% mAP@100 in the zero-shot setting (0.534 vs. 0.482).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The full system (LLM + model disagreement + KD) outperforms the all-HR GRAFT baseline by 26.3% mAP@100 (0.633 vs. 0.501) while using 76.3% fewer HR images, and beats the all-LR GRAFT by 31.3% (0.633 vs. 0.482). Against prior cost-aware sampling methods, it dominates: Patchdrop (Uzkent & Ermon, 2020) achieves only 0.445 mAP@100 in the supervised setting, and IS-count night-lights sampling (Meng et al., 2022) scores 0.379 mAP@100 in the zero-shot setting—both far below the proposed 0.633. Among zero-shot baselines, the KD model alone (0.534) already surpasses RemoteCLIP (0.461), CLIP-RSICD (0.393), and OpenCLIP (0.481) without any HR imagery at test time. In the supervised setting, the full system (0.736) exceeds Scale-MAE (0.472), SatMAE (0.351), Cross-Scale MAE (0.412), and the multi-resolution ResNet-50 (0.421) by 16–38 mAP points, confirming that the scale-aware budget allocation strategy is the dominant factor over architecture alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: multi-scale satellite recognition (Reed et al., 2023; Mall et al., 2024), budget-constrained HR image acquisition (Uzkent & Ermon, 2020; Meng et al., 2022), and knowledge distillation / learning with privileged information (Hinton et al., 2015; Vapnik et al., 2015). Prior work on scale addresses accuracy but ignores acquisition cost, while prior work on cost-aware acquisition reasons about geography but not about the physical scale of the target concept. The key conceptual departure is treating the HR-to-LR distillation as cross-modality (different input resolutions) rather than same-input compression, and using an LLM with in-context examples to infer which resolution suits a given concept — a repurposing of LLM semantic reasoning for a remote-sensing decision problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for ecologists, archaeologists, and urban planners who operate under tight imagery budgets: a 5× reduction in HR acquisitions while *improving* accuracy over the HR-only baseline is a compelling efficiency gain. The finding that a simple LLM in-context prompt outperforms using actual OSM concept areas for modality selection suggests that semantic understanding of a concept's visual signature matters beyond its physical footprint, opening questions about how to formalize 'concept scale' more rigorously. Key limitations include the discrete two-resolution assumption (real sensors span a continuum), reliance on noisy OSM annotations, and the absence of sub-image segmentation for targeted HR acquisition.
