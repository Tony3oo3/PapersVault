## Problem

Popular style mimicry protections (Glaze, Mist, Anti-DreamBooth) with millions of downloads claim to shield artists from generative AI style theft via adversarial perturbations, but prior evaluations were non-comprehensive, used outdated purification models, and failed to account for adaptive adversaries. This paper asks whether these protections hold up against simple, off-the-shelf robust mimicry methods that a moderately resourceful forger could deploy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252530/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 18 protected artwork images per artist (center-cropped to 512×512, captioned with BLIP-2) and 10 diverse text prompts, then produces 768×768 style-mimicked images via fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 2.1. Success is measured through an MTurk user study where 5 annotators per comparison judge whether robust mimicry outputs (from protected images) are preferred over baseline outputs (from unprotected images), with 50% success rate indicating indistinguishability. The study evaluates both image quality and stylistic fidelity as independent attributes.

## Method

The paper proposes four robust mimicry methods that combine off-the-shelf image processing to bypass protections: (1) Gaussian noising (σ²=0.05) as a simple preprocessing step, (2) DiffPure using Stable Diffusion XL for adversarial purification, (3) Noisy Upscaling—applying Gaussian noise (σ=0.1) followed by the Stable Diffusion Upscaler, which exploits the upscaler's training on noise-augmented images to simultaneously remove noise and perturbations, and (4) IMPRESS++, a white-box method combining reverse encoder optimization via PGD, negative prompting with controlled strength, and post-generation DiffPure denoising. The key insight is that Noisy Upscaling, the most effective method (median success rate >40% across all protections), requires only black-box access to a fine-tuning API and tools that predated the protections. A unified evaluation across 10 artists (5 historical, 5 contemporary), 15 protection-method scenarios, and a 'best-of-4' selection strategy demonstrates that all protections are easily circumvented, with best-of-4 consistently exceeding 50% success rate.

## Results

The paper evaluates three style-mimicry protections (Glaze, Mist, Anti-DreamBooth) against four robust mimicry methods using a user study with 10 artists and 10 prompts. The key metric is success rate: the percentage of annotators who prefer robust-mimicry outputs over baseline outputs from unprotected art (50% = indistinguishable, 0% = protection fully effective). Noisy Upscaling is the most effective single method, achieving median success rates of 45.0% (Anti-DreamBooth), 39.4% (Glaze), and 42.8% (Mist) on image quality. The 'best-of-4' strategy, which selects the most successful method per prompt, exceeds 50% for all three protections (56.6%, 56.6%, 62.0%), meaning robust mimicry is on average indistinguishable from mimicry on unprotected art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three protections are shown to be easily circumvented by the proposed robust mimicry methods. Noisy Upscaling (a simple two-step preprocessing: Gaussian noise + Stable Diffusion Upscaler) is the strongest single method, reducing Anti-DreamBooth's protection from 11.6% (naive) to 45.0% success rate, Glaze from 22.2% to 39.4%, and Mist from 9.0% to 42.8% on quality. The 'best-of-4' ensemble strategy surpasses the 50% indistinguishability threshold for every protection (56.6% for Anti-DreamBooth, 56.6% for Glaze, 62.0% for Mist), meaning a forger who tries all four methods and picks the best output per prompt produces results that annotators rate as equal to or better than mimicry from fully unprotected art. Notably, even without any robust mimicry intervention, Glaze's protection is already significantly degraded (22.2% naive success rate) simply by using a different off-the-shelf finetuning script, revealing fundamental brittleness. The more complex white-box method IMPRESS++ does not provide significant advantages over the simpler black-box preprocessing methods, suggesting that low-effort techniques are sufficient to defeat these protections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the unlearnable examples literature and adversarial purification, applying both to the specific domain of style mimicry protections for artists. It builds directly on IMPRESS (Cao et al., 2024), the first method designed to circumvent style mimicry protections, but demonstrates that simpler off-the-shelf techniques (Gaussian noising, Noisy Upscaling, DiffPure) achieve comparable or better results without requiring white-box access. The paper departs from prior evaluations by introducing a unified protocol with a user study, a common off-the-shelf finetuning script, and a diverse set of historical and contemporary artists, addressing the non-comparability and reliance on unreliable automated metrics (e.g., CLIP similarity) in earlier protection evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that protections with over 1M downloads and major media coverage (NYT, CNN, Scientific American) provide essentially no security against low-effort attacks has immediate practical consequences for artists who may be publishing work under a false sense of safety. The paper highlights a fundamental first-mover asymmetry: once protected images are online, the forger can adaptively select the best circumvention method, making adversarial perturbation-based protections inherently fragile. Updated versions (Glaze 2.0/2.1, Mist v2) also fail, suggesting the problem is structural rather than a bug in a particular implementation. The open question is whether any technical protection can overcome this asymmetry, or whether the field must pivot to non-technical solutions (legal, platform-level, provenance-based).
