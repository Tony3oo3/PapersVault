## Problem

Extracting temporally extended skills from offline data is critical for efficient RL in long-horizon, sparse-reward settings, but existing approaches either discover skills unsupervised (yielding scattered, semantically meaningless segments) or rely heavily on human annotation. The paper asks how to obtain interpretable, task-relevant temporal segmentations from offline trajectories without extensive manual intervention or fixed-horizon heuristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251788/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline trajectory datasets (state-action-reward sequences) where states can be proprioceptive vectors or pixel observations, plus rendered RGB images of segment boundaries for VLM queries. The VLM (GPT-4o) is queried in a multi-turn dialogue format, receiving the initial and terminal rendered observations of each VQ-segmented sub-trajectory to assign or correct a skill index. The output is a discrete codebook of skill labels assigned to every state, together with a two-level hierarchical policy: a high-level discrete skill selector and a low-level action policy conditioned on both the current observation and the assigned skill.

## Method

VanTA modifies VQ-VAE by replacing the reconstruction decoder with a policy decoder p_φ(a|s,z), so the latent space is shaped for behavior rather than pixel reconstruction. In the first stage, the encoder projects consecutive observations into a shared codebook index, forming initial trajectory segments; these segments are then passed to GPT-4o, which relabels each segment's index based on its visual content, and the codebook is updated via exponential moving average with the VLM-assigned embeddings. This VQ-initialize → VLM-relabel → codebook-update cycle repeats iteratively, progressively merging scattered segments and correcting erroneous assignments until convergence. In the second stage, a high-level discrete DQN learns skill selection over the compact codebook, while a low-level weighted behavior-cloning policy (IQL-style importance weighting) learns actions conditioned on the skill label. Theoretically, the paper shows that VLM-guided skills induce stronger intra-segment temporal autocorrelation, shrinking the effective policy space and tightening the suboptimality bound relative to both flat offline RL and unguided hierarchical baselines.

## Results

VanTA achieves the best or near-best performance across all three benchmark suites. On Franka Kitchen, it scores 69.2 (complete), 71.2 (partial), and 68.5 (mixed), outperforming the strongest baselines (LDCQ at 67.8, 62.3, and IQL at 62.5) by 1.4–6.2 points. On MiniGrid, VanTA leads on 6 of 8 tasks, with the largest margin on KeyCorridorS3R3-mixed (0.61 vs. IQL's 0.51) and KeyCorridorS3R3-complete (0.81 vs. LDCQ's 0.69). On Crafter, VanTA achieves a score of 5.46, more than doubling the next-best baseline (IQL at 2.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VanTA outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of tasks (10 of 12). Against the strongest offline RL baselines, IQL and LDCQ, VanTA gains 1.4–6.2 points on Kitchen and 0.04–0.12 on MiniGrid, with the largest advantage on the more complex KeyCorridorS3R3 tasks (0.81 vs. 0.72 for IQL, 0.61 vs. 0.51 for IQL on mixed). Against goal-conditioned methods (GCSL, WGCSL, RvS), VanTA is consistently superior, particularly on mixed datasets where hindsight relabeling struggles (e.g., KeyCorridorS3R3-mixed: 0.61 vs. 0.42 for RvS). VanTA ties BC on MiniGrid-DoorKey-6x6-complete (both 0.92) and is slightly behind RvS on DoorKey-8x8-complete (0.92 vs. 0.94) and IQL on RedBlueDoors-6x6-complete (0.85 vs. 0.90), but these are the only losses. On Crafter, where GCSL and WGCSL are infeasible due to the open-world visual setting, VanTA nearly doubles IQL's score (5.46 vs. 2.75) and outperforms LDCQ by 5.7×.

## Relation to Prior Work

VanTA sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: temporal abstraction in hierarchical RL (OPAL, DIAYN, GCSL, LDCQ) and knowledge distillation from pretrained VLMs. It builds directly on VQ-VAE's codebook mechanism and IQL's conservative offline policy learning, but departs from prior skill-discovery methods by using a VLM (GPT-4o) as a *discriminative* relabeler of VQ segments rather than as a generative planner or instruction encoder. This contrasts with earlier VLM-for-RL work (Inner Monologue, Video Language Planning) that leverages VLMs for step-by-step planning, and with human-annotated skill extraction (Lee et al., 2019; Fu et al., 2024) that requires manual supervision. The L1 smoothness regularization on latent transitions is borrowed from Gumbsch et al. (2024) for adaptive temporal abstraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in offline RL: unsupervised skill discovery often yields fragmented, semantically meaningless segments that degrade downstream policy learning, while human-annotated skills are expensive and don't scale. By grounding the VQ codebook in VLM world knowledge, VanTA demonstrates that a small amount of external semantic signal can substantially improve data efficiency (28.6% gain at 10% data) and produce interpretable, task-aligned skill decompositions. Key limitations include the dependence on proper VQ initialization (poor initialization leads to high VLM query cost), the inability of VLMs to correct mode collapse, and the manual prompt engineering required per environment. Open questions include reducing VLM query complexity, extending to preference-based and multi-agent offline settings, and whether the approach generalizes to higher-dimensional continuous control beyond the tested benchmarks.
