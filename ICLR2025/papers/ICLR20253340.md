## Problem

Existing video instruction tuning datasets are predominantly descriptive (75% of VideoInstruct-100K questions are caption prompts) and lack reasoning-focused content, while the broader CV community has accumulated large labeled video datasets (action recognition, quality assessment, temporal localization) that cannot be directly converted into instruction tuning data. The paper asks: can any labeled video dataset, regardless of task or label format, be leveraged for video instruction tuning with chain-of-thought reasoning?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253340/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips paired with task-specific labels of heterogeneous types (class names for action recognition, numeric scores for quality assessment, bounding boxes and temporal segments for localization) and produces (video, question, chain-of-thought answer) triplets where the answer must contain the original label. The generated VSTAR-1M dataset spans 675K+ videos from three source datasets, and the final output is both an instruction-tuned LMM and the reusable dataset. Questions are generated by prompting an LLM with the task description and label, while answers are structured as step-by-step rationales culminating in the label.

## Method

Video-STaR runs iterative generate-filter-train cycles initialized from Video-LLaVA. In each cycle, the previous cycle's model first attempts direct Answer Generation (video + question → rationale + answer); a Parser-Verifier then extracts predicted labels from the generated text and compares them to gold labels using task-appropriate metrics (IoU for boxes, BERT embedding similarity for sequences, 5% tolerance). Videos that fail verification enter Label Rationalization, where the model is given the correct label and asked to reason backward to produce a justification. The model is finetuned from the original pretrained checkpoint on the verified dataset each cycle, while the next cycle's data generation uses the newly finetuned model. This design uses existing labels as weak supervision to filter hallucinated answers, progressively improving both data quality and model capability across cycles.

## Results

On TempCompass, Video-STaR improves Video-LLaVA's average score from 45.7% to 51.8% (+6.1%), outperforming all open-source baselines and approaching Gemini-1.5 (66.0%). On adapted downstream tasks, Video-STaR achieves 59.9% accuracy on Kinetics700-QA (vs. 50.0% for Video-LLaVA) and 21.6% on FineDiving-QA (vs. 17.6%), demonstrating strong task adaptation. On zero-shot VQA benchmarks, Video-STaR attains the highest MSVD-QA accuracy (71.3%) and MSRVTT-QA accuracy (58.2%) among open-source models, though gains are modest, suggesting these benchmarks are near their noise ceiling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Video-STaR consistently outperforms the Video-LLaVA base model across all evaluated benchmarks: +6.1% on TempCompass (51.8 vs. 45.7), +9.9% on Kinetics700-QA (59.9 vs. 50.0), and +4.0% on FineDiving-QA (21.6 vs. 17.6). Against Video-LLaVA+ (naive template-based label usage), Video-STaR shows a clear advantage on adapted tasks (e.g., 59.9 vs. 49.5 on Kinetics700-QA), demonstrating that simply converting labels to QA pairs is insufficient. Compared to Vid-LLaVA^Gemini (distillation from Gemini-1.5-pro on 3500 videos per dataset), Video-STaR wins on all three adapted benchmarks (59.9 vs. 49.4 on Kinetics700-QA, 33.0 vs. 29.3 on STAR-bench-QA, 21.6 vs. 16.5 on FineDiving-QA), indicating self-training with full-dataset labels surpasses partial distillation. However, Video-STaR still trails Gemini-1.5-pro substantially on TempCompass (51.8 vs. 66.0), and on zero-shot VQA benchmarks the gap to Gemini is small or nonexistent, suggesting open-source models are approaching the noise floor of those evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

Video-STaR extends the STaR self-training paradigm (Zelikman et al., 2022) from text-only LLMs to video LMMs, adapting the generate-filter-rationalize cycle to a multi-modal setting where a Parser-Verifier component checks whether generated answers contain ground-truth video labels. It departs from prior video instruction tuning work (VideoInstruct-100K, Video-ChatGPT), which generated QA pairs by prompting LLMs with video captions and produced predominantly descriptive questions, by instead leveraging existing labeled video datasets (action recognition, AQA, temporal localization) as weak supervision to produce reasoning-focused instruction data. The base architecture is Video-LLaVA, and the approach is positioned as a complement to, rather than replacement for, existing instruction tuning datasets like VideoInstruct-100K and LLaVA v1.5.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in video LMM research: the scarcity of diverse, reasoning-focused video instruction tuning data. By showing that any labeled video dataset can be converted into instruction tuning data through self-training, Video-STaR opens a path to leveraging the extensive collection of task-specific video benchmarks (action recognition, AQA, TAL) that the CV community has already curated. The VSTAR-1M dataset and the demonstrated ability to adapt LMMs to novel tasks (e.g., Olympic diving scoring) with interpretable rationales have practical value. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-form video, how to reduce hallucinations in the rationalization step, and whether the 5% verification margin is optimal across label types.
