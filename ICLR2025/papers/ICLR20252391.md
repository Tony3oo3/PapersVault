## Problem

Continually adapting multimodal LLMs to a stream of new instruction-tuning datasets is prohibitively costly and suffers from catastrophic forgetting, especially for rare or newly introduced skills. Static importance-based data selection methods fail in this multi-task, evolving-distribution setting because sample importance shifts as the model's knowledge and the dataset pool change over time, and experience replay alone cannot retain skills that appear only once in the stream.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained MLLM (LLaVA-1.5-7B with LoRA adapters) and a growing pool of multimodal instruction-tuning datasets, where each sample is an image-text pair consisting of a CLIP ViT-L/14-encoded image, a text prompt, and a ground-truth answer. At each timestep, Adapt-∞ selects a fixed-budget subset (e.g., 25k samples) from the combined pool of all past and newly arrived datasets, and the output is the updated MLLM weights after training on that subset. The data pool is additionally compressed via permanent pruning to a configurable size (e.g., 100k–500k samples) to bound computational cost over time.

## Method

Adapt-∞ operates in three stages at each timestep. First, it extracts gradient vectors from the middle layers of the current model for every sample in the data pool and performs k-means clustering to form pseudo-task (skill) clusters, since gradients capture skill identity far better than hidden-state outputs. Second, for each cluster it selects the best scoring function from a pool of experts (perplexity, image grounding, EL2N, entropy) by maximizing the entropy of the score distribution within that cluster, then applies CCS uniform-range sampling to pick a skill-balanced subset. Third, LITE-Adapt-∞ adds a permanent pruning step that computes pairwise cosine similarities in hidden-layer-output space within each cluster and removes the most semantically redundant samples, keeping the pool size bounded. A key new scoring function, the Image Grounding score (ratio of text-only perplexity to image-conditioned perplexity), prioritizes samples that genuinely require visual understanding, and the multi-way design ensures no single metric dominates across heterogeneous skill clusters.

## Results

On the lifelong multimodal instruction tuning benchmark (LLaVA-1.5-7B with LoRA, 6 datasets over 6 timesteps), Adapt-∞ achieves 102.3% relative gain and only 0.9% forgetting rate using just 25k training samples per timestep, compared to 68.0% relative gain and 26.0% forgetting for sequential training on full datasets. Scaling the data budget to 50k and 100k samples further improves relative gain to 107.2% and 109.7% respectively, with average accuracy reaching 52.5%. The LITE-Adapt-∞ variant with permanent data pruning retains 99.7% relative gain and 1.3% forgetting while controlling data pool size to 100k samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adapt-∞ (25k) decisively outperforms all score-based selection baselines: EL2N (82.4% RG, 12.2% FR), Entropy (79.6% RG, 15.1% FR), and Perplexity (91.4% RG, 9.6% FR), achieving 102.3% RG and 0.9% FR — a 10.9–22.7 point gain in relative gain and 4.7–14.2 point reduction in forgetting. Among embedding-based pruning methods, SemDeDup (76.4% RG) and Density-based Pruning (78.1% RG) perform worst, while COINCIDE (89.5% RG, 3.9% FR) is the strongest baseline but still trails Adapt-∞ by 12.8 points in relative gain and 3.0 points in forgetting rate. Notably, even simple random selection (95.3% RG, 2.1% FR) outperforms all score-based methods, revealing that static importance scores fail to produce task-balanced subsets from multi-task datasets. The paper's key insight is that gradient-based pseudo-task clustering combined with entropy-selected multi-way scoring within each cluster is what enables Adapt-∞ to exceed 100% relative gain (i.e., forward transfer beyond the best single-timestep performance), a result no baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes continual instruction tuning (CIT) as a data selection problem rather than a model-architecture or regularization problem, departing from prior CIT methods (KPIG, EProj, Fwd-Prompt) that modify the training procedure itself. It builds on CCS (Zheng et al., 2023) for balanced coverage-based sampling, on Yoon et al. (2022) for gradient-based coreset selection using essential layers, and on the perplexity score (Marion et al., 2023) which it extends into the novel Image Grounding score. The key conceptual departure is from conventional continual learning's disjoint-task assumption: LiIT treats the data pool as a growing, overlapping, multi-task corpus where sample importance shifts with model state, necessitating dynamic multi-way selection rather than a single static importance metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that in multi-task instruction tuning, naive score-based data selection is actively harmful (12–15% forgetting) compared to random pruning (2.1%), a finding that challenges the default assumption that informed selection always beats random. Adapt-∞'s gradient-based pseudo-task clustering plus per-cluster best-scoring-function selection achieves 0.9% forgetting and >100% relative gain with only 25k samples, offering a practical recipe for sustainable MLLM adaptation. Limitations include evaluation on a single 7B model (LLaVA 1.5) across five datasets, and the approach's reliance on gradient extraction (92 hours on 8×A100) remains a significant computational overhead, though LITE-Adapt-∞ mitigates this. Open questions include whether the gradient-captures-skills / hidden-states-capture-semantics distinction holds across architectures, and how the method scales to dozens of datasets or truly novel output modalities.
