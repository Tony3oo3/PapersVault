## Problem

Model inversion attacks on 3D point cloud data remain largely unexplored, despite the rich privacy-sensitive information (identity, location, behavior) contained in 3D scenes shared in autonomous driving. The only prior attempt used a simple Point Regression approach that failed because it could not handle the inherent sparsity of voxelized point clouds or the ambiguity between empty voxels and valid points at the origin, leading to massive point over-generation and clustering near (0,0,0).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253562/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes intermediate 3D voxel feature tensors (f_N ∈ R^{C_N × h_N × w_N × d_N}) extracted at various downsampling depths (1–4 layers) from a known voxel-based feature extractor (VoxelBackbone or VoxelResBackbone) and produces a restored 3D point cloud scene. Internally, the model outputs binary voxel occupancy scores at the original voxel resolution (R^{1×H×W×D}); points are then placed at the center coordinates of voxels classified as occupied. The inversion model is trained in a supervised manner using paired (feature, original point cloud) data from KITTI and Waymo, requiring no additional labeling beyond the voxelization of the original scene.

## Method

ConcreTizer replaces the conventional coordinate-regression inversion with two tailored components. First, Voxel Occupancy Classification (VOC) reframes restoration as a binary classification problem—each voxel is labeled empty (0) or occupied (1)—resolving the semantic ambiguity of zero-padded voxels and eliminating origin clustering; a Sigmoid Focal Loss handles the severe class imbalance that grows with network depth. Second, Dispersion-Controlled Supervision (DCS) partitions the feature extractor at each downsampling layer and trains a corresponding inversion block per segment, where each block jointly predicts an occupancy mask (via SF loss) and channel values (via L2 loss), then masks the channel output with the occupancy mask to suppress the progressive spread of non-empty voxels into empty regions. The overall inversion architecture mirrors the feature extractor with upsampling at downsampling positions, and at the final (shallowest) stage only classification is performed, with restored points placed at voxel centers.

## Results

On the most challenging setting (4 downsampling layers / 12th layer depth), ConcreTizer achieves CD of 0.0776 cm and F1 of 0.9160 on KITTI, and CD of 0.1087 cm and F1 of 0.9251 on Waymo, substantially outperforming both baselines. In terms of downstream 3D object detection utility, restored scenes from ConcreTizer retain 75.5–87.0% of original detection AP on KITTI and 62.6–75.7% on Waymo, while Point Regression yields 0% AP (completely unusable) and UltraLiDAR retains only ~70–75% on KITTI but drops to ~35–38% on Waymo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Point Regression (Hwang et al., ICCV 2023), ConcreTizer is overwhelmingly superior: at the deepest layer on KITTI, CD drops from 4.14 to 0.078 cm (~53×) and F1 rises from 0.13 to 0.92, while on Waymo CD drops from 4.93 to 0.11 cm and F1 rises from 0.44 to 0.93. Point Regression produces completely unusable 3D detection results (0% AP across all four detection models on both datasets). Against UltraLiDAR (Xiong et al., CVPR 2023), ConcreTizer wins on CD by 23.4% (KITTI) and 21.1% (Waymo), and on F1 by 12.4% (KITTI) and 4.5% (Waymo) at the deepest layer; however, UltraLiDAR retains a slight HD advantage on KITTI (7.92 vs. 8.12 cm) due to its 2D VQ-VAE design smoothing out sparse-structure artifacts. On 3D object detection, ConcreTizer consistently outperforms UltraLiDAR across all four detection models on both datasets (e.g., 66.25 vs. 58.32 AP for PointPillar on KITTI; 0.4245 vs. 0.2328 APH for PointPillar on Waymo), confirming that ConcreTizer's voxel-level restoration preserves fine geometric detail better than the generative approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on the Point Regression inversion approach from Hwang et al. (ICCV 2023), which was the only prior attempt at 3D point cloud feature inversion and which concluded that restoration was infeasible. The authors identify two specific failures of that approach—zero-padding ambiguity and VoI dispersion through convolutional layers—and replace the regression objective with Voxel Occupancy Classification (VOC) and a block-wise Dispersion-Controlled Supervision (DCS) strategy. Conceptually, the work also draws on 2D model inversion literature (Mahendran & Vedaldi, Dosovitskiy & Brox) for the symmetric decoder architecture, and positions itself against generative LiDAR completion models (UltraLiDAR, Xiong et al. CVPR 2023) as an alternative restoration paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper overturns the working assumption in the AV community that sharing 3D voxel features is inherently privacy-safe, demonstrating that near-faithful scene restoration (and 75–87% of original 3D detection accuracy) is achievable with a simple, well-motivated attack. This has immediate implications for cooperative perception, computation offloading, and federated learning pipelines that rely on feature sharing. Key open questions include: effective defenses that do not degrade detection utility (current perturbations cause steep accuracy drops), extension beyond voxel-based backbones to point-set and graph representations, and whether the known-parameters assumption can be relaxed. The paper's scope is limited to two architectures and two datasets, and the defense analysis is preliminary.
