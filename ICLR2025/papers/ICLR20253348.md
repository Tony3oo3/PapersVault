## Problem

Existing neural acoustic field methods either ignore the critical influence of 3D scene geometry on sound propagation or rely on additional annotations (RGB-D images, surface acoustic coefficients) that are unavailable in real-world settings. Because sound propagates omnidirectionally as a spherical wavefront interacting with the full 3D volume of a room, conditioning on 2D camera views or requiring co-located audio-visual sensors limits both the quality and practical applicability of learned acoustics.

## Input / Output

The method takes microphone and source 6DoF poses (position + orientation), a 128³ voxel grid with 7 channels (averaged color, density, and voxel coordinates) extracted from a trained NeRF, and a normalized time query. It outputs log-magnitude STFTs of binaural RIRs (22.05 kHz, 512 FFT bins for SoundSpaces; 48 kHz, 1024 FFT bins for RAF) which are inverted via Griffin-Lim to waveforms, plus RGB novel views from the NeRF. Crucially, the audio and visual modalities can be rendered independently at spatially distinct positions, and training does not require co-located sensors or equal data amounts per modality.

## Method

NeRAF comprises three modules: a Nerfacto NeRF backbone, a grid sampler, and a Neural Acoustic Field (NAcF). The grid sampler sequentially queries the radiance field with each voxel's center and 18 viewing directions, averaging colors to populate a 7-channel 3D volume that encodes both geometric (density) and appearance (color) priors. A ResNet3D-50 jointly trained with the fields extracts 1,024–2,048 features from this grid, which are concatenated with positionally encoded poses, spherically harmonic encoded directions, and a positionally encoded time query. NAcF then uses two MLP blocks: the first fuses all inputs into a 512-dim acoustic scene representation, and the second (one head per microphone channel) predicts F frequency bins per time query, learning HRTFs for binaural spatialization. Training is cross-modal and supervised, combining the standard NeRF photometric loss with a spectral convergence + spectral loss on log-magnitude STFTs, with NAcF training delayed until the grid has been populated several times.

## Results

On SoundSpaces, NeRAF achieves T60=2.14%, C50=0.38 dB, and EDT=0.010 s, outperforming the previous best (NACF w/T) by 22.4% on C50 and 28.6% on EDT. On the real-world RAF dataset, NeRAF reaches T60=7.47%, C50=0.61 dB, EDT=0.020 s, and STFT error=0.17 dB, improving over the best prior method by 6.7% T60, 14.2% C50, 4.5% EDT, and 52.5% STFT error. Cross-modal learning also boosts novel view synthesis: on complex apartments with only 75 training images, NeRAF improves LPIPS by 12.2% (Apartment 1) and 15.3% (Apartment 2) over Nerfacto alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRAF outperforms all four baselines on both SoundSpaces and RAF. Against NAF (Luo et al., 2022), NeRAF reduces T60 error from 3.18% to 2.14% on SoundSpaces and from 10.08% to 7.47% on RAF, while cutting STFT error by 73% (0.64→0.17 dB). Against INRAS (Su et al., 2022), NeRAF improves C50 from 0.60 to 0.38 dB (SoundSpaces) and STFT error from 0.36 to 0.17 dB (RAF). Against AV-NeRF (Liang et al., 2023a), the closest competitor since it also uses Nerfacto, NeRAF achieves 13.4% lower T60, 33.3% lower C50, and 37.5% lower EDT on SoundSpaces, and 52.5% lower STFT error on RAF. Against NACF (Liang et al., 2023b), which requires pre-acquired RGB-D images and surface acoustic coefficients, NeRAF matches or exceeds its performance (T60 2.14 vs 2.36, C50 0.38 vs 0.50 on SoundSpaces) without any additional annotations, and achieves substantially lower STFT error on RAF (0.17 vs 0.39 dB). NeRAF also demonstrates superior data efficiency: with only 75% of training audio it already outperforms AV-NeRF trained on 100% of data.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeRAF extends the neural acoustic field line of work (NAF, INRAS, AV-NeRF, NACF) by replacing their 2D image-based or pre-annotated conditioning with a 3D voxel grid populated from a NeRF radiance field, providing full volumetric geometric and appearance priors without additional annotations. It departs from AV-NeRF, which renders RGB-D only at the microphone's pose, by capturing scene information beyond the camera's field of view through the grid sampler. The joint cross-modal training of NeRF and NAcF, where both modalities benefit from each other, is a key architectural departure from prior methods that treat audio and vision as independent or one-directionally conditioned tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeRAF provides a practical, Nerfstudio-integrated pipeline for realistic audio-visual scene synthesis that is more data-efficient than prior methods and enables independent rendering of each modality at arbitrary positions. The finding that cross-modal learning improves novel view synthesis in complex scenes with sparse observations (up to 15% LPIPS improvement) suggests a broader principle that acoustic geometry can regularize visual reconstruction. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to static scenes, NeRF still requires per-scene training (limiting multi-scene generalization), and the 3D grid resolution introduces a trade-off between fidelity and computational cost. Future work on dynamic sound sources and generalizable NeRF backbones would significantly broaden applicability.
