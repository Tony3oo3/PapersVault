## Problem

In amortized sequential sampling (e.g., GFlowNets) and entropy-seeking RL, long trajectories make credit assignment and mode discovery increasingly difficult, especially when multiple high-reward states each require many steps to reach. While macro-actions and temporal abstraction have been studied in standard RL, they have not been applied to diversity-seeking amortized samplers, and prior approaches either discover macro-actions offline or enforce compressibility through explicit objectives rather than exploiting the emergent structure in the policy's own trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method consumes action sequences (trajectories) generated by a sampler operating on a discrete MDP, optionally mixed with high-reward trajectories from a prioritized replay buffer, along with the reward function. It produces a dynamically growing library of high-level action chunks (subsequences of atomic actions) that augment the action space, and the augmented sampler then outputs terminal states (objects) from the target distribution. The policy parameterization must handle a nonstationary action space: an LSTM-based action encoder maps each chunk (a variable-length sequence of atomic actions) to a fixed-dimensional embedding, and logits are computed via dot-product with a state-dependent query vector.

## Method

ACTIONPIECE iteratively discovers high-level actions by applying Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) tokenization to a corpus of high-reward trajectories, extracting the most frequent action subsequences as new 'chunk' actions added to the action space. Two variants are proposed: ACTIONPIECE-INCREMENT adds one chunk per round (curriculum-like), while ACTIONPIECE-REPLACE replaces the entire non-atomic action space with the top-M most frequent tokens each round. The key insight is a virtuous cycle: shorter trajectories improve credit assignment, which accelerates learning and exploration, which in turn produces more diverse high-reward trajectories from which better chunks can be extracted. The paper also introduces ShortParse, a fixed backward policy for GFlowNets that assigns probability proportional to exp(λ|τ|) over trajectories, preferring shorter parses and enabling efficient computation via a linear-time recurrence over the token vocabulary.

## Results

On the graph environment (7 nodes), ACTIONPIECE-INCREMENT reduces the ELBO Gap from 0.72±0.39 (ATOMIC) to 0.25±0.14, with JSD improving from 0.38 to 0.36 and L1 distance from 1.44 to 1.39. For mode discovery in FractalGrid, ACTIONPIECE-INCREMENT with GFlowNet unlocks exploration of faraway modes that all ATOMIC samplers fail to reach. On L14_RNA1, GFlowNet with ACTIONPIECE-INCREMENT achieves a mean chunk occurrence of 1.11 and coverage of 0.69 in high-reward objects, substantially outperforming A2C (0.36/0.23) and SAC (0.43/0.29). Transferred chunk libraries from GFlowNet yield the best downstream mode discovery on L14_RNA2 and L14_RNA3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ATOMIC GFlowNet baseline, ACTIONPIECE-INCREMENT consistently accelerates mode discovery across all four environments (FractalGrid, RNA Binding, Bit Sequence, Graph), with the largest gains in FractalGrid where ATOMIC samplers get stuck in the first mode. Compared to A2C and SAC, GFlowNet with ACTIONPIECE-INCREMENT learns chunks that far better capture the latent structure of the target distribution (mean chunk occurrence 1.11 vs. 0.36–0.43), and its chunk libraries transfer more effectively to new tasks (L14_RNA2, L14_RNA3). However, ACTIONPIECE hurts A2C and SAC on RNA binding, likely due to a negative feedback loop where greedy exploitation concentrates chunks around a single mode, reducing diversity. Against MaxEnt-GFlowNet and ShortParse-GFlowNet, the standard GFlowNet with ACTIONPIECE-INCREMENT performs comparably on chunk quality metrics, confirming that the chunking benefit is robust to the choice of backward policy. Option-Critic (a dedicated temporal-abstraction method) serves as a reference but is not directly compared with chunking mechanisms in the main tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: macro-action discovery in RL (Randlov, 1998; Sutton et al., 1999; Durugkar et al., 2016), amortized sampling via GFlowNets (Bengio et al., 2021; Malkin et al., 2022), and subword tokenization (BPE; Gage, 1994). The key departure from prior macro-action work is that ACTIONPIECE constructs the action library online and iteratively during training using BPE, rather than learning a static set offline or using fixed-length n-grams. The paper also introduces ShortParse, a new backward policy for the trajectory balance objective that biases toward compact tokenizations, which is a novel contribution to the GFlowNet training framework itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of hierarchical planning in amortized sequential sampling, showing that online action abstraction can accelerate mode discovery and improve density estimation in GFlowNets. A notable finding is that GFlowNet-based samplers learn substantially better chunks than RL-based methods (A2C, SAC), because the entropy-seeking objective prevents the negative feedback loop where greedy exploitation concentrates chunks around a single mode. Limitations include evaluation only on small synthetic tasks (14-char RNA, 64/128-bit sequences, 7-node graphs), use of a fixed BPE tokenizer, and a single target distribution per run; open questions include task-conditioned libraries, joint modeling of the abstraction library, and scaling to larger or continuous action spaces.
