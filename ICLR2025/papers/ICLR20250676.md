## Problem

Theoretical guarantees for Nesterov acceleration have been restricted to convex or strongly convex objectives, yet deep learning loss landscapes are notoriously non-convex with non-unique minimizers and small negative Hessian eigenvalues near the global minimizer set. This paper closes the gap by proving accelerated convergence under a weaker 'strong aiming' geometric condition that permits a curved manifold of minimizers and bounded negative curvature, matching the geometry observed in overparametrized networks.

## Input / Output

The method requires a C¹-smooth objective f whose set of minimizers forms a C²-submanifold, a unique closest-point projection π defined on a sublevel set, and the μ-strong aiming inequality relating the gradient to the distance from the nearest minimizer. In discrete time, an additional bound on negative Hessian eigenvalues (ε ≤ √(μ/η)) and an affine-linear projection are assumed. The output is an explicit accelerated rate: (1−√(μη))^n in discrete time or e^{−√μ t} in continuous time, with a noise floor σ_a²√(η/μ) in the stochastic additive-noise case.

## Method

The core mechanism is a modified Lyapunov function that separates tangential and normal velocity components relative to the minimizer manifold, since the closest-point projection π(x_t) moves along the manifold as the iterate evolves. A key geometric lemma (Lemma 7) establishes that the projection never moves opposite to the trajectory, enabling control of the tangential drift term. In continuous time, the heavy-ball ODE with damping γ=2√μ yields exponential decay at rate √μ via a Lyapunov function coupling f(x_t)−inf f with a quadratic term in velocity and displacement from π(x_t). In discrete time, the Nesterov scheme is reparametrized as a time-stepping of the heavy-ball system, and the Lyapunov sequence uses distinct coefficients for P v_n (tangential) and P⊥ v_n (normal) to absorb the tangential drift. Stochastic variants (additive and multiplicative noise) are handled via the AGNES scheme with an additional parameter α that adapts to the multiplicative variance, yielding the same accelerated rate with a noise floor.

## Results

The paper proves that Nesterov's accelerated gradient method achieves a convergence rate of (1−√(μη))^n in discrete time under a 'μ-strong aiming condition' that permits non-unique minimizers and negative Hessian eigenvalues up to magnitude √(μ/η). In continuous time (heavy ball ODE), the rate is e^{−√μ t}, compared to e^{−μt} for plain gradient flow. With additive stochastic noise (σ_a), the method converges to a noise floor of σ_a²√(η/μ) at the same accelerated linear rate, improving upon the σ_a²/(2μ) floor of stochastic gradient descent. With combined additive and multiplicative noise, the AGNES variant achieves rate (1−√(μη/(1+σ_m²)))^n with noise floor σ_a²√(η/(μ(1+σ_m²))).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against plain gradient descent: under the same geometric assumptions, GD achieves rate (1−μ/L)^n while Nesterov achieves (1−√(μ/L))^n, yielding a square-root speedup in the condition number. Compared to prior non-convex acceleration results (Hinder et al. 2020; Fu et al. 2023; Hermant et al. 2024), this paper's strong aiming condition is strictly weaker: it does not require convexity, uniqueness of minimizers, or star-shaped sublevel sets, and explicitly accommodates a manifold of minimizers with tangential drift. Against the aiming condition of Liu et al. (2024), which only covers gradient flow, this paper extends the analysis to momentum-based methods (heavy ball ODE, discrete Nesterov, and stochastic variants). The paper also contrasts with Yue et al. (2023), who prove that acceleration is impossible under the PL condition alone, showing that the additional geometric structure of the aiming condition (which implies PL but is strictly stronger) is what enables the accelerated rate. In the stochastic setting, the noise floor σ_a²√(η/μ) for Nesterov is strictly smaller than σ_a²/(2μ) for SGD at the optimal step size η=1/L, demonstrating that momentum's memory of past gradients helps average out additive noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two geometric threads—quasi-strong convexity (Necoara et al., 2019) and the aiming condition (Liu et al., 2024)—into a single 'strong aiming condition' that permits non-unique minimizers and bounded negative Hessian eigenvalues, then proves Nesterov acceleration under this weaker geometry. The continuous-time analysis builds on the heavy ball ODE of Su et al. (2016), while the stochastic discrete-time results extend the AGNES scheme of Gupta et al. (2024) from convex to benignly non-convex settings. Crucially, the paper departs from the quasar-convexity line (Hinder et al., 2020; Hermant et al., 2024) by dropping the uniqueness-of-minimizer requirement, and from the PL-condition line (Yue et al., 2023) by showing that a different geometric weakening (allowing small negative curvature along the minimizer manifold) does preserve acceleration where PL does not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result validates a long-standing heuristic in deep learning—that small negative Hessian eigenvalues tangential to the minimizer manifold do not impede momentum-based acceleration—by providing the first rigorous convergence proof under this geometry. The key open limitation is the affine-linear closest-point projection assumption in the discrete-time theorems, which the authors explicitly flag as the most cumbersome restriction and a priority for future work; relaxing it to general curved manifolds would bring the theory much closer to actual overparametrized network landscapes. The continuous-time results (Theorems 6, 8) are more geometrically general and suggest the discrete-time restriction is technical rather than fundamental, but bridging that gap remains an important open problem for the optimization theory community.
