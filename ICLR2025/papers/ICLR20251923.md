## Problem

Prior global identifiability results for dictionary learning either restricted to complete dictionaries (m ≥ k) or relied on combinatorial arguments requiring factorial sample complexity in the latent dimension. This paper addresses whether global identifiability of an overcomplete dictionary (k > m) can be guaranteed with practical, near-optimal sample complexity, which had remained an open problem in matrix factorization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251923/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{m×n} (or C^{m×n}) with m < k as input and recovers an overcomplete dictionary A ∈ R^{m×k} and sparse coefficient matrix S ∈ R^{k×n}. The dictionary is identifiable up to column permutation and sign flips; the sparse coefficients are additionally identifiable when A guarantees ℓ1 recovery of s-sparse vectors. The key structural constraint is that the latent dimension k exceeds the ambient dimension m, making the factorization inherently non-unique without the proposed identifiability criterion.

## Method

The paper proposes a novel nonconvex formulation that minimizes a weighted sum of ℓ1 norms of the rows of S plus (1/2)log det(AA^T) subject to X = AS, where the volume term generalizes the complete-dictionary determinant criterion to the overcomplete case via det(AA^T). Global identifiability is guaranteed when the cellular hull of the rescaled coefficient matrix is m-strongly scattered in the k-hypercube [−1,1]^k, a geometric condition that reduces to the classical sufficiently scattered condition when m = k. Under the sparse-Gaussian generative model, this condition holds with overwhelming probability when n = O((k²/m)log(k²/m)), matching the O(k log k) bound for complete dictionaries when m = k. The algorithm alternates between a gradient-descent update for A (solving a log-determinant regularized least squares) and a proximal gradient update for S with row-wise soft-thresholding, where the threshold weights are set via Cauchy-Schwarz at each iteration.

## Results

The main contribution is a theoretical guarantee: the overcomplete dictionary A (m×k, m<k) is globally identifiable via the proposed log-determinant + weighted ℓ₁ formulation if the cellular hull of the rescaled coefficient matrix is m-strongly scattered in the k-hypercube. Under the sparse-Gaussian model with s<m nonzeros per column, this condition holds with overwhelming probability when n = O((k²/m)·log(k²/m)), which reduces to O(k log k) in the complete case (m=k). In a synthetic experiment (s=5, m=10, k=20, n=200), the alternating minimization algorithm recovers the dictionary to an estimation error of ‖A_∨ − A_⋆‖_F² ≈ 10⁻⁸ after column matching via the Hungarian algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without head-to-head empirical comparisons against competing algorithms. The comparison is made at the level of identifiability guarantees and sample complexity. The proposed O((k²/m)·log(k²/m)) sample complexity is a sharp generalization of the O(k log k) bound for complete dictionary learning by Hu & Huang (2023a) and Sun & Huang (2024), and represents a factor-of-k improvement over the global optimality guarantees for overcomplete DL by Agarwal et al. (2016) and Rambhatla et al. (2019). It also dramatically improves upon the combinatorial ℓ₀-norm approach of Aharon et al. (2006b) and Hillar & Sommer (2015), which requires n = O((s+1)·C(k,s)) samples — factorial in the latent dimension. Cohen & Gillis (2019) achieved O(k³/(k−s)²) but only for complete dictionaries (m≥k). The key distinction is that this is the first identifiability result for a matrix factorization model where the latent dimension k exceeds the ambient dimension m, with no incoherence or restricted isometry assumptions required beyond ℓ₁-recovery of s-sparse vectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the volume-based global identifiability framework of Hu & Huang (2023a) and Sun & Huang (2024) from complete dictionaries (m ≥ k) to overcomplete dictionaries (m < k), replacing the inapplicable det(A) with det(AA^T) and introducing a new 'm-strongly scattered' geometric condition in the k-hypercube. It departs fundamentally from prior overcomplete DL identifiability results (Aharon et al. 2006b; Hillar & Sommer 2015; Garfinkle & Hillar 2019), which relied on combinatorial arguments requiring factorial sample complexity in the latent dimension k. The l1-norm formulation connects to the compressive sensing literature, but the key novelty is the hybrid criterion combining weighted row-wise l1 norms with a log-volume term, which together yield a tractable global identifiability condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first global identifiability guarantee for a matrix factorization model where the latent dimension exceeds the ambient dimension, resolving a long-standing gap between complete and overcomplete dictionary learning theory. The polynomial sample complexity O((k²/m)log(k²/m)) is a dramatic improvement over the factorial bounds from combinatorial approaches, and the condition is shown to be nearly minimal. However, the algorithmic side is preliminary: the proposed alternating minimization has no convergence guarantee to the global optimum, and empirical validation consists of a single synthetic experiment (10×20 dictionary, 200 samples). Open questions include designing efficient algorithms that provably reach the global solution, extending the analysis to noisy data, and understanding whether the m-strongly scattered condition can be relaxed or verified in practice.
