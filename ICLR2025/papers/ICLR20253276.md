## Problem

Whether LLMs can match or surpass human creativity remains an open question, but existing approaches to quantify writing creativity rely on subjective human rubric evaluations that do not scale to the volume of machine-generated text. This paper asks: can we build an objective, scalable, and computationally tractable measure of linguistic creativity that distinguishes human from machine text without requiring human annotators or model access?

## Input / Output

The method takes a target text (a novel snippet, poem, speech, news article, or theorem proof of ~256+ words) and a massive reference corpus of web documents (RedPajama, ~900M documents / 100B+ tokens). It produces a scalar CREATIVITY INDEX (the area under the L-uniqueness curve across n-gram lengths), the full L-uniqueness curve showing the proportion of words in novel contexts at each minimum n-gram length L, and a binary human/machine classification for zero-shot detection. For human texts that predate the corpus cutoff, a deduplication step removes documents containing copies or quotations of the target text before matching.

## Method

CREATIVITY INDEX quantifies how much of a text can be reconstructed by mixing and matching existing web snippets: for each minimum n-gram length L, L-uniqueness measures the fraction of words not covered by any n-gram (length ≥ L) found in the reference corpus, and the index sums L-uniqueness over all L. To compute this efficiently, DJ SEARCH is a two-pointer dynamic programming algorithm that identifies the longest matching n-gram starting at each position in O(|x|) calls to the matching function f, exploiting the monotonicity that if x[i:i+n] is found then x[i+1:i+n] is also found. The matching function f combines exact verbatim retrieval via Infini-gram (millisecond-scale n-gram lookup) with near-verbatim semantic matching via Word Mover's Distance (WMD) on pre-computed word embedding pairwise distances, which avoids the infeasible cost of computing n-gram-level embeddings for every snippet in the corpus. BM25 pre-filters the corpus to the most topically relevant documents before WMD is applied, and WMD is only invoked when Infini-gram finds no exact match, keeping the overall pipeline tractable at web scale.

## Results

The CREATIVITY INDEX of professional human authors is on average 66.2% higher than that of LLMs across novel writing (52.2% higher, verbatim; 102.5% higher with semantic matches), poetry composition (31.1% higher), and speech drafting (115.3% higher). RLHF alignment reduces LLM CREATIVITY INDEX by an average of 30.1% (verbatim) and 8.9% (semantic), indicating convergence toward preferred human linguistic styles. For zero-shot machine text detection, CREATIVITY INDEX achieves a 30.2% AUROC improvement over DetectGPT and a 3.5% average AUROC improvement over the supervised GhostBuster in five out of six domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CREATIVITY INDEX against two primary baselines for machine text detection: DetectGPT (Mitchell et al., 2023a), a zero-shot method using probability curvature, and GhostBuster (Verma et al., 2024), a supervised detector fine-tuned on thousands of ChatGPT generations. CREATIVITY INDEX decisively outperforms DetectGPT by 30.2% AUROC across all six domains (novel, poetry, speech, news, theorem proof, and one additional), and also beats OpenAI's RoBERTa-based detector by 26.9% AUROC. Against the stronger supervised GhostBuster, CREATIVITY INDEX wins in five out of six domains with an average 3.5% AUROC gain, despite requiring no training data or knowledge of the generating model. For the creativity measurement itself, the paper does not compare against a single competing metric but positions CREATIVITY INDEX as complementary to rubric-based human evaluation (Chakrabarty et al., 2024) and n-gram novelty measures (Merrill et al., 2024), noting that it uniquely combines verbatim and semantic matching across variable n-gram lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two algorithmic components—Infini-gram for fast verbatim n-gram retrieval and Word Mover's Distance for semantic similarity—to construct a novel two-pointer dynamic programming algorithm (DJ SEARCH) that efficiently identifies all n-gram matches between a document and a web corpus. Conceptually, it extends the n-gram novelty measurement line (Nguyen 2024; Merrill et al. 2024) by adding semantic matching and reframing the metric as a text-reconstruction problem, while departing from rubric-based creativity assessment (Chakrabarty et al. 2024; Vaezi & Rezaei 2018) that relies on human evaluators and cannot scale. The paper also repurposes the resulting CREATIVITY INDEX as a zero-shot machine text detection criterion, connecting to the DetectGPT/GhostBuster detection literature in a way that prior creativity metrics had not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that RLHF reduces CREATIVITY INDEX by 30.1% (verbatim) raises important questions about whether alignment trades off originality for preference-conformity, with implications for how we evaluate and deploy aligned models. The practical utility of DJ SEARCH as an out-of-the-box detection tool that beats both zero-shot and supervised baselines is significant for content provenance in the digital age. Key limitations include dependence on the reference corpus (private training data of closed-source models inflates their scores), the focus on high-quality human writers rather than the full distribution, and the fact that n-gram overlap does not conclusively prove memorization of a specific document. Open questions include whether the metric captures non-linguistic dimensions of creativity (conceptual originality, narrative structure) and how it generalizes to multilingual or code settings.
