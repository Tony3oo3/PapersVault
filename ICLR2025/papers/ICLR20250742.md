## Problem

Existing inverse rendering methods face a fundamental trade-off: implicit representations (NeRF, SDF) produce high-quality novel views but cannot output mesh assets usable in graphics engines and lack physically-based constraints on indirect illumination, while mesh-based methods like NVdiffrec-MC suffer from unstable geometry optimization and omit multi-bounce path tracing. No prior framework combines explicit triangular mesh geometry with physically-based multi-bounce path tracing and efficient Monte Carlo sampling for joint decomposition of shape, material, and lighting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics (poses) of a single object under unknown environment lighting. It produces an explicit triangular mesh with refined vertex positions, a spatially-varying PBR material map (diffuse albedo and roughness via a hash-grid MLP, optionally metallic for the OWL dataset), and a 256×512 trainable HDR environment map. The output mesh is directly compatible with modern graphics engines and CAD software, enabling downstream relighting, scene editing, and material editing without additional conversion.

## Method

MIRReS is a two-stage pipeline: Stage 1 trains an InstantNGP radiance field and a NeuS2 SDF to extract a coarse mesh via Marching Cubes; Stage 2 refines the mesh via trainable per-vertex offsets while jointly optimizing materials and lighting through differentiable physically-based rendering. The key rendering component is multi-bounce path tracing (up to 3 bounces) over the explicit mesh using a linear BVH for ray-mesh intersection, where indirect radiance is estimated by recursively tracing secondary rays rather than caching in a radiance field. For direct illumination, the method employs ReSTIR (reservoir sampling with weighted spatiotemporal reuse) to approximate the ideal importance sampling distribution L_env·f_r, dramatically reducing Monte Carlo variance at low sample counts (32 SPP). An EAWT denoiser further suppresses residual noise, and the geometry is stabilized by Laplacian smooth and offset regularization losses alongside the standard L2 rendering losses.

## Results

On the TensoIR synthetic dataset, MIRReS achieves the best scores across all metrics: Chamfer Distance of 0.056 (23.3% lower than the second-best NVD-MC at 0.073), Normal MAE of 3.305° (19.4% lower than TensoIR's 4.100°), Albedo PSNR of 32.35 dB, and Relighting PSNR of 32.36 dB. On the real-world Objects-with-Lighting (OWL) dataset, it reaches 28.83 dB relighting PSNR and 38.22 dB novel view synthesis PSNR, outperforming all baselines by a large margin. The ablation study confirms that both reservoir sampling (+0.58 dB albedo PSNR) and multi-bounce path tracing (+1.80 dB albedo PSNR) contribute additively to the final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIRReS outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric across both datasets. Against TensoIR (implicit TensoRF-based), it gains +3.07 dB albedo PSNR, +3.78 dB relighting PSNR, and reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.083 to 0.056; TensoIR's implicit density field struggles with high-frequency details and bakes shadows into albedo. Against NVdiffrec-MC (mesh-based, single-bounce), the improvement is even larger: +3.47 dB albedo PSNR, +4.55 dB relighting PSNR, and N-MAE drops from 5.050° to 3.305°, as NVD-MC's unstable DMTet geometry produces holes and self-intersections that degrade path tracing. Against GS-IR (3D Gaussian Splatting), MIRReS leads by +2.06 dB albedo PSNR and a substantial +7.99 dB relighting PSNR, since GS-IR's split-sum approximation cannot capture multi-bounce effects. On the real OWL dataset, MIRReS achieves 28.83 dB relighting PSNR vs. TensoIR's 26.38 dB and NVD-MC's 21.11 dB, with GS-IR performing poorly (18.76 dB, SSIM 0.101) due to its inability to handle complex real-world lighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIRReS extends the mesh-based inverse rendering line initiated by NVdiffrec-MC (Hasselgren et al., 2022) by replacing its single-bounce Monte Carlo rendering with physically-based multi-bounce path tracing, and by incorporating ReSTIR-style reservoir sampling (Bitterli et al., 2020) for direct illumination to reduce variance at low sample counts. The two-stage geometry pipeline—extracting a coarse mesh from a neural implicit surface (NeuS2) and refining it with trainable vertex offsets—follows the NeRF2Mesh (Tang et al., 2023) paradigm, deliberately departing from the implicit density-field representations used by TensoIR and NeILF++. The key conceptual departure from prior work is that explicit triangular meshes make recursive multi-bounce path tracing computationally tractable, whereas implicit radiance-field methods must approximate indirect illumination through volume rendering without physical constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing explicit triangular meshes with PBR materials and HDR environment maps, MIRReS outputs assets directly compatible with modern graphics engines and CAD software, addressing a practical gap left by implicit-representation methods. The multi-bounce path tracing component is particularly valuable for scenes with complex self-shadowing and inter-reflections, where prior methods bake lighting artifacts into albedo. However, the method's accuracy is bounded by the quality of the initial coarse mesh from NeuS2, and it does not propagate gradients through non-primary rays, limiting reconstruction fidelity in highly specular or high-frequency-detail regions. Open questions include combining this framework with recent specular-reflection-aware methods and developing efficient gradient computation for deeper bounce paths.
