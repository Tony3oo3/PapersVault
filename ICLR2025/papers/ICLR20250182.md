## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs generate one token at a time, creating a latency bottleneck that limits search, planning, and repeated-sampling applications. Discrete diffusion language models can generate many tokens in parallel but require thousands of decoding steps to match AR quality. The paper addresses how to reduce the number of inference steps by 32–64× without relying on deterministic noise-to-data mappings, which provably cannot exist for absorbing discrete diffusion that always initializes from an all-MASK sequence.

## Input / Output

The model takes a fully masked token sequence (every position replaced with a special MASK token) plus an optional text prompt for conditional generation, and outputs a complete sequence of text tokens in a single forward pass per decoding step. Because the architecture is a non-causal (bidirectional) diffusion transformer, KV caching is unavailable, so each decoding step reprocesses the entire sequence. The distilled student generates at least 32 tokens simultaneously per step, completing a full sequence in as few as 16 forward passes versus 1024 for the teacher.

## Method

Self-Distillation Through Time (SDTT) trains a student denoiser to match the log-probabilities that a teacher denoiser would assign when denoising a MASK token over m/k sampling steps. Concretely, the teacher runs m/k ancestral sampling steps from a noisy input; whenever a MASK token is resolved, the teacher's predicted log-probabilities for that token become the distillation target, and the student is trained (via reverse KLD) to predict those same log-probabilities in a single step. The procedure is applied iteratively over 7 rounds with geometrically increasing step sizes (1024→512→…→16), each round initializing the student from the previous round's weights. The key distinction from continuous-diffusion distillation (e.g., DDIM-based progressive distillation) is that SDTT operates on stochastic sampling targets rather than deterministic trajectories, making it applicable to the absorbing discrete diffusion setting where no unique noise-to-data map exists.

## Results

SDTT reduces the number of decoding steps by a factor of 32–64 while preserving or improving text quality. At the 1.3B parameter scale, the distilled diffusion model generates tokens up to 8× faster than GPT-2 with KV-caching (16 decoding steps, batch size 8, A100 GPU). On LAMBADA, the KLD-distilled student slightly outperforms the teacher in suffix accuracy, and the 169M distilled model (64+ steps) achieves lower generative perplexity than the 863M teacher (1024 steps). Downstream MCQ accuracy is minimally affected by distillation, with the KLD student matching or exceeding the teacher on 5 of 6 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is GPT-2 (Radford et al., 2019), used both as an AR perplexity/latency reference and as the scorer for generative perplexity. On latency, the 1.3B SDTT-distilled diffusion model (16 steps, no KV-caching) is 8× faster than GPT-2 with KV-caching, and the 169M model shows even larger speedups. On generative perplexity, the KLD-distilled student matches GPT-2 with nucleus sampling (p=0.95) in just 32 forward passes, whereas the undistilled teacher requires 1024 steps. On LAMBADA suffix accuracy, the KLD student slightly exceeds the teacher (and thus the AR baseline), suggesting that parallel multi-token decoding after distillation improves last-word prediction. On the six MCQ downstream tasks, the KLD student is within 0.1–1.3 points of the teacher on 5 of 6 tasks, confirming that SDTT preserves natural-language-understanding performance while drastically reducing inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the masked diffusion language modeling (MDLM) framework of Sahoo et al. (2024) and the discrete diffusion language model architecture of Lou et al. (2023), adopting their absorbing diffusion process and diffusion transformer backbone. The distillation strategy is inspired by progressive distillation for continuous diffusion models (Salimans & Ho, 2022), but departs fundamentally from the DDIM-based deterministic mappings that underpin most continuous distillation methods. The authors argue such deterministic maps cannot exist for discrete diffusion language models (which always initialize from fully-masked sequences), and instead propose Self-Distillation Through Time (SDTT), which matches the student's single-step prediction to the teacher's multi-step denoising log-probabilities via a divergence objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDTT offers a practical path to making discrete diffusion language models competitive with autoregressive models on inference latency (8× faster than GPT-2 with KV caching at 1.3B parameters), which is critical for applications requiring large-scale search, planning, or repeated sampling. However, the evidence is somewhat limited: latency measurements use untrained models, the largest quality-evaluated model is only 860M parameters, and the generated text examples in the appendix reveal substantial quality gaps compared to AR models. Open questions include whether the speedup persists at frontier scale, whether activation caching can be incorporated into the non-causal architecture, and whether SDTT transfers to tasks demanding high-fidelity long-form generation rather than short completions.
