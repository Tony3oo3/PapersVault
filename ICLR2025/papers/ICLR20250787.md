## Problem

Random features approximate kernel evaluations via Monte Carlo sampling, but the resulting estimates converge slowly, undermining the efficiency gains that motivate RFs. Existing variance reduction techniques (QMC, structured MC, antithetic variates) are either suboptimal, difficult to design, or lack guarantees in the low-sample, high-dimensional regime where RFs matter most. The paper asks whether a principled, unifying framework can identify the best possible dependencies between random frequencies across different basis functions and input domains, and reveals that variance reduction itself may not be the right objective for certain downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250787/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For Euclidean inputs, the method takes data points in R^d and a kernel lengthscale, then couples the norms of m orthogonal Gaussian frequency vectors (each marginally Chi_d-distributed) to produce lower-variance kernel estimates. For graph inputs, it takes an undirected graph and a termination probability, then couples the lengths of pairs of random walks (each marginally geometrically distributed) via a data-dependent permutation learned on a reference graph. The output is a set of coupled random features yielding an approximate Gram matrix, attention scores, or GP predictive posterior; notably, the GRF coupling is data-dependent (solved per hyperparameter) while the RFF/RLF coupling is data-independent.

## Method

The paper frames RF variance reduction as a multi-marginal optimal transport problem where the cost is the squared kernel estimator and the marginals are fixed by unbiasedness. For RFFs and RLFs, it solves this OT problem exactly at m=2 using c-monotonicity arguments, showing that negative monotone coupling of frequency norms (F_chi_d(ω1)+F_chi_d(ω2)=1) minimizes variance; this extends to arbitrary m via pairwise norm-coupling (PNC), and antithetic sampling is recovered as the optimal coupling when directions are unconstrained. For graph random features, the OT problem over walk lengths is approximated by restricting to permutation densities, reducing the search to a bipartite matching between quantiles of geometric distributions solvable by the Hungarian algorithm in O(n^3). A key empirical finding is that for attention estimation in Performers, the positive monotone coupling (which maximizes pointwise kernel variance) actually reduces the MSE of row-normalised attention scores and improves ImageNet accuracy by +0.8%, demonstrating that the naive variance-reduction OT objective is sometimes the wrong cost function for downstream performance.

## Results

On six UCI datasets, pairwise norm-coupled (PNC) RFFs reduce normalised kernel RMSE by 10–20% over orthogonal independent-norm baselines (e.g., 0.433 vs. 0.535 on Abalone; 0.481 vs. 0.586 on Airfoil), and PNC+antithetic RLFs achieve similar gains (e.g., 0.324 vs. 0.360 on Boston). Surprisingly, on ImageNet with Performers, PNC *decreases* test accuracy (0.620 vs. 0.625 for orthogonal), while positive monotone (PM) coupling—which maximises pointwise kernel variance—*increases* accuracy to 0.633, revealing that naive variance reduction is not always the right objective for attention estimation. For graph random features, σ-coupled GRFs consistently match or beat antithetic termination across all termination probabilities on Cora and other real-world graphs, and unlock >2× improvements in GP predictive RMSE and KL divergence on mesh-graph interpolation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Halton QMC sequences, PNC RFFs and RLFs achieve substantially lower RMSE on all six UCI datasets (e.g., 0.433 vs. 0.991 on Abalone for RFFs), confirming that data-agnostic low-discrepancy sequences are suboptimal in the low-sample, high-dimensional regime. Against orthogonal independent-norm features (Yu et al., 2016), PNC consistently reduces kernel RMSE by 10–20% at no extra computational cost, validating the OT-derived negative monotone coupling. Against antithetic termination for GRFs (Reid et al., 2024c), σ-coupled GRFs match or beat the hard-coded algorithm across all termination probabilities on Cora and other graphs, and the data-dependent coupling adapts to different hyperparameters. The most striking comparison is in Table 2 (Performers on ImageNet): PNC *underperforms* plain orthogonal features (0.620 vs. 0.625 test accuracy), while positive monotone coupling—which solves the 'wrong' OT problem by maximising pointwise variance—*outperforms* both (0.633), demonstrating that variance reduction alone is insufficient for attention estimation where row normalisation couples the kernel estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the variance reduction problem for random features under the lens of optimal transport, building on the random features framework (Rahimi & Recht, 2007), structured Monte Carlo / orthogonal random features (Yu et al., 2016; Rowland et al., 2018), and graph random features (Choromanski, 2023; Reid et al., 2024b). Prior variance reduction techniques—QMC (Halton sequences), common random numbers, antithetic variates, and handcrafted SMC—were applied ad hoc without a unifying optimisation principle; here, the authors cast coupling design as a Kantorovich multi-marginal OT problem and solve it exactly for m=2 (recovering negative monotone coupling) and approximately for discrete spaces via bipartite matching. The work also reinterprets antithetic sampling in Performers (Choromanski et al., 2020) as an OT coupling, providing the first theoretical justification for its effectiveness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous variance-reduction guarantees for norm-coupled random features and the first data-dependent coupling for graph random walks, with practical gains in scalable graph-based GP inference. However, its most important contribution is a conceptual warning: for attention estimation in Performers, maximising pointwise kernel variance (the 'wrong' OT sign) outperforms variance reduction, revealing that the joint covariance structure of kernel estimates—not their individual variances—governs downstream performance after normalisation. This opens the question of what the correct OT cost function should be for each downstream task (spectral properties of K̂, row-normalised attention MSE, etc.), a direction the authors identify as largely unexplored.
