## Problem

Accurate electronic charge density (ECD) prediction is essential for determining total energy and ground-state properties of materials, yet existing DFT datasets either lack charge density files, use only the lower-accuracy PBE functional, or are too small and specialized for systematic benchmarking. No large-scale, mixed-precision dataset combining PBE and HSE functional charge densities has been available to train and evaluate ML models on this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250178/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a crystal structure (atomic species, fractional/Cartesian positions, and three lattice vectors) as a graph and predicts the scalar electronic charge density ρ(r) at each point of a fine FFT grid (e.g., 120×120×180 points, varying per structure). For spin-polarized magnetic materials, both total and spin-resolved charge densities are output. The grid resolution and augmentation occupancies (PAW) are stored alongside the density values in the ECD file format.

## Method

The benchmark uses ChargE3Net, a higher-order SE(3)-equivariant graph neural network that represents features as irreducible tensor representations V^(ℓ,p) and combines them via equivariant tensor products with Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, with charge density probe points inserted as additional graph nodes for node-regression. The key training strategy is two-stage: pre-train on 138,134 PBE-precision structures to learn broad structural representations, then fine-tune on 5,647 HSE-precision structures to capture higher-accuracy electronic structure details. Training runs for 1M steps with 200 random probe points per batch, L1 loss, Adam optimizer, and exponential LR decay. The paper demonstrates that equivariant models outperform invariant baselines (DeepDFT, invChargE3Net) and that increasing the HSE data ratio monotonically reduces MAE from 2.156 eV (0% HSE) to 1.085 eV (100% HSE), while also improving DFT SCF convergence acceleration.

## Results

On the MP-PBE benchmark, ChargE3Net achieves a normalized MAE of 0.523±0.01, substantially outperforming invDeepDFT (1.293±0.03) and DeepDFT (1.212±0.02). On the ECD-PBE-id task (140,646 structures), the equivariant ChargE3Net achieves 0.685±0.03 MAE versus 0.732±0.02 for its invariant counterpart. Fine-tuning the PBE-trained model on HSE data (ECD-PBE_HSE_tuning-id) reduces the cross-functional MAE from 2.156±0.08 (direct PBE→HSE prediction) to 1.085±0.03, and the OOD evaluation on 2,000 GNoME structures yields 0.643 eV MAE, comparable to in-distribution performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Jørgensen & Bhowmik (2022) baselines on MP-PBE, ChargE3Net (0.523) outperforms both invDeepDFT (1.293) and DeepDFT (1.212) by a wide margin, confirming the advantage of higher-order equivariant representations over invariant-only GNNs. Compared to the original ChargeE3Net trained on MP data (Koker et al., 2024, MAE 0.523 on MP-PBE), the ECD-trained model achieves a comparable 0.520 on the ECD-PBE_MP-id split, validating that the larger ECD dataset does not degrade cross-dataset performance. On the wet-experimental band gap task (41 structures), the ECD-PBE_HSE model (MAE 0.65 eV) beats CGCNN (1.45), MEGNet (1.36), CrystalNet (1.19), and CrystalNet-TL (0.70) from Chen et al. (2019, 2022), though at ~10× the inference time (14.4 min vs. ~1.5 s per structure). The equivariant ChargE3Net also outperforms its invariant variant invChargeE3Net on ECD-PBE-id (0.685 vs. 0.732), demonstrating that SE(3) equivariance is critical for charge density prediction on large, diverse crystal datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the charge density prediction task established by DeepDFT (Jørgensen & Bhowmik, 2022) and ChargE3Net (Koker et al., 2024) from single-precision PBE data on the Materials Project (~12,000 entries) to a mixed-precision setting combining 140,646 PBE and 7,147 HSE functional calculations. The key conceptual departure is the transfer learning paradigm—training on large-scale PBE data and fine-tuning on a smaller HSE subset—which addresses the fundamental tension between data scale (PBE) and accuracy (HSE) that prior single-functional datasets could not resolve. The equivariant tensor network architecture (ChargE3Net) is adopted as-is; the contribution is the dataset, the mixed-precision training protocol, and the multi-task benchmark design rather than a new model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ECD dataset represents the largest publicly available collection of DFT charge densities with both PBE and HSE functionals (~130M CPU core hours), filling a critical gap left by MP, OQMD, and NOMAD, which either lack HSE data or provide charge densities from non-self-consistent steps. The demonstrated PBE→HSE fine-tuning pipeline (reducing MAE from 2.156 to 1.085 eV) offers a practical recipe for the community to leverage large PBE databases while approaching HSE-level accuracy at a fraction of the computational cost. Open questions include whether further scaling HSE data continues to improve accuracy (the trend in Table 4 suggests yes but is untested beyond 7,147 entries), how the approach generalizes to strongly correlated or multi-component systems beyond ternary compounds, and whether the OOD performance on GNoME (0.643 eV) holds for truly unprecedented compositions.
