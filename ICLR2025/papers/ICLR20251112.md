## Problem

Current weather nowcasting methods are limited to 2D spatial predictions at specific altitudes, overlooking the vertical dependencies between different cloud layers that are critical for capturing complete storm structure. Extending these methods to 3D volumetric prediction is impractical because raw 3D tensor inputs cause memory and compute costs to scale quadratically with horizontal resolution, making high-resolution 3D forecasting infeasible on available hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251112/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 5 observed 3D radar frames (e.g., 36×384×512 for MOSAIC, 36×512×512 with 6 channels for NEXRAD) and predicts 20 future 3D radar frames. Rather than operating on raw 3D volumes, each frame is re-represented as a fixed set of 49,152 3D Gaussians (position, N radar features, scaling, rotation), so the prediction target becomes the DiffGaussian ΔG_t = G_t − G_0. This Gaussian parameterization decouples memory usage from spatial resolution, scaling linearly only with the number of primitives and enabling 16× higher effective resolution than competing 3D representation methods.

## Method

The framework has two components. First, STC-GS performs a bidirectional reconstruction: a backward pass from frame T to 0 optimizes only Gaussian positions under dual-scale constraints (a pseudo-3D optical flow constraint from RAFT-estimated 2D flows on three orthogonal planes, and a global energy-based distribution constraint via Gaussian-kernel-smoothed radar data), then a forward pass from 0 to T optimizes all Gaussian parameters with reconstruction loss, preserving spatiotemporal coherence without adding or removing Gaussians. Second, GauMamba stacks MambaGRU blocks that combine a selective state-space (Mamba) backbone for efficient long-sequence processing of Gaussian tokens with a GRU memory gate that carries hidden states across timestamps, enabling the model to learn temporal evolution of Gaussian groups while maintaining linear-time complexity. The model is trained with supervised loss on DiffGaussian targets, using teacher forcing for the observed window and autoregressive roll-out for the prediction horizon, with Morton sorting applied to preserve spatial locality in the 1D token sequence.

## Results

On the MOSAIC dataset, GauMamba achieves MAE 0.714, SSIM 0.897, LPIPS 0.157, and LPIPS_Radar 0.741, outperforming all baselines. On NEXRAD, GauMamba reaches MAE 0.003, SSIM 0.900, LPIPS 0.126, and LPIPS_Radar 0.665. In terms of Critical Success Index (CSI-20/CSI-30 at 4×4 pooling), GauMamba improves over the best competing method by 12.1% and 69.0% on MOSAIC, and by 4.8% and 101.1% on NEXRAD. For 3D reconstruction, STC-GS achieves MAE 0.014×10, PSNR 40.26 dB, and SSIM 0.970 on NEXRAD, surpassing 3DGStream, 4DGS, and Deform-3DGS across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GauMamba outperforms all seven baselines on both prediction datasets. Against ConvGRU (Shi et al., 2017), GauMamba reduces MAE by 58.5% on MOSAIC (0.714 vs. 1.728) and by 50% on NEXRAD (0.003 vs. 0.006), while achieving substantially higher SSIM (0.897 vs. 0.621 on MOSAIC). Against PhyDNet (Gu & Thome, 2020), GauMamba improves MAE by 21.6% on MOSAIC and 82.4% on NEXRAD, with CSI-30 more than doubling on NEXRAD (0.179 vs. 0.089). Against SimVP (Gao et al., 2022), GauMamba achieves lower MAE (0.714 vs. 0.890) and dramatically better LPIPS_Radar (0.741 vs. 3.516) on MOSAIC. Against DiffCast (Yu et al., 2024), GauMamba reduces MAE by 62% on MOSAIC (0.714 vs. 1.878) and by 98% on NEXRAD (0.003 vs. 0.157), while DiffCast's diffusion-based approach produces noisy outputs with the worst SSIM (0.355 on MOSAIC, 0.004 on NEXRAD). For reconstruction, STC-GS beats 3DGStream (Sun et al., 2024) by 26% in MAE (0.014 vs. 0.019×10), 4DGS (Wu et al., 2024) by 50% in MAE (0.014 vs. 0.028×10), and Deform-3DGS (Yang et al., 2024) by 52% in MAE (0.014 vs. 0.029×10), while also achieving the best PSNR (40.26 dB) and lowest LPIPS_Radar (0.123).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from RGB scene rendering to radar echo representation, replacing SfM initialization with random sampling from radar volumes and swapping RGB/opacity attributes for multi-channel radar features. For prediction, it extends Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023) with a GRU memory mechanism to form GauMamba, which tracks temporal evolution of Gaussian groups. The key conceptual departure from prior dynamic Gaussian methods (4DGS, Deform-3DGS, 3DGStream) is that radar clouds lack rigid-body or locally-static structure, so the paper introduces a bidirectional reconstruction with dual-scale constraints (3D optical flow + energy-based distribution) to maintain per-Gaussian identity across frames rather than relying on deformation fields or 4D parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to perform 3D volumetric radar sequence prediction, addressing a gap where prior nowcasting methods were limited to 2D slices at fixed altitudes. The Gaussian representation decouples memory cost from spatial resolution (linear in Gaussian count vs. quadratic in pixel resolution), enabling 16× higher-resolution predictions that were infeasible for raw-tensor baselines on 4×A100 GPUs. However, the pipeline is complex (bidirectional reconstruction + flow estimation + energy constraints + Mamba training), the scope is specialized to radar nowcasting, and the authors acknowledge limited exploration of broader dynamic 3D applications. Open questions include whether the STC-GS representation generalizes to other volumetric time-series tasks and whether the reconstruction-to-prediction two-stage pipeline can be unified end-to-end.
