## Problem

While prompt tuning is widely used to teach multimodal models new visual concepts by optimizing a single embedding, it remains unclear whether these learned soft prompts correspond to meaningful semantic directions in the text encoder's embedding space or something more pathological. This paper asks: how do prompt-tuning solutions that encode specific visual concepts (e.g., 'black dog') actually differ from typical discrete prompts, and can they transfer across models and tasks?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251499/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt containing a single learned soft embedding (initialized near a discrete anchor word in the text encoder) and a small set of 8 images per concept, and produces task-specific outputs: 768×768 generated images (Stable Diffusion 2.1), bounding boxes with class labels at 960×960 input resolution (OWL-v2), or class labels at 224×224 (DFN CLIP). The soft embedding is constrained via projected gradient descent to remain within a normalized δ-ball of its anchor word, and transfer between models is performed through a closed-form linear least-squares map learned on >40,000 shared token embeddings.

## Method

The paper conducts a large-scale empirical study optimizing 4,800 prompt embeddings for 40 visual concepts across four datasets and three model families (Stable Diffusion 2.1, OWL-v2, DFN CLIP) under four constraint radii δ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0}. The key finding is 'fracturing of the embedding space': performant solutions exist within an ε-ball of virtually any discrete anchor word, regardless of semantic relevance to the target concept, and performance saturates at δ=0.5. Layer-wise analysis (via truncated text encoders and t-SNE of pooling-token activations) reveals that these solutions target the final transformer blocks to steer representations away from the anchor and toward the target concept, behaving like adversarial perturbations rather than emergent semantic embeddings. A linear transfer function between model embedding spaces confirms that while discrete prompts and randomly perturbed prompts transfer nearly losslessly, prompt-tuning solutions do not, and CKA scores (0.5–0.75) show the underlying embedding spaces are in fact similarly structured—making the non-transferability a property of the adversarial optimization, not of incompatible geometry.

## Results

Across 4,800 optimized prompt embeddings for 40 visual concepts on four datasets (ImageNet, DreamBooth, COCO, PASCAL VOC), in-domain performance saturates at a constraint level of δ=0.5, meaning solutions within a small ε-ball around any discrete prompt achieve near-optimal task performance. Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) scores between the three models' text encoder input embeddings range from 0.506 to 0.759, confirming high structural similarity across models. However, transferred prompt-tuning solutions perform poorly: classification→generation transfer achieves at most 84% of in-domain performance (PASCAL), while detection→classification transfer reaches only 26% (COCO). In contrast, non-tuned discrete prompt embeddings transfer nearly losslessly, and randomly perturbed embeddings (σ=0.01) also transfer well, confirming that the non-transferability stems from the adversarial nature of tuned solutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is an empirical study rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark a proposed method against external baselines. Instead, it compares three internal conditions: (1) in-domain prompt-tuning solutions, (2) transferred prompt-tuning solutions, and (3) non-tuned or randomly perturbed discrete embeddings. The key finding is that non-tuned anchor word embeddings transfer nearly losslessly across all six model-task pairs (Figure 7), and randomly perturbed embeddings (Gaussian, σ=0.01) also transfer robustly (Figure 10), while tuned prompt embeddings lose most of their performance upon transfer. Among tuned transfers, classification→generation is the strongest direction (up to 84% of in-domain on PASCAL), generation→detection is intermediate (up to 28% on ImageNet), and detection→classification is the weakest (up to 26% on COCO). An MLP transfer function (4096 hidden units, ReLU) does not improve over the linear transfer function, with marginally worse performance. The CLIP L-14 classifier used to evaluate generation quality achieves 88.8%–98.8% baseline accuracy across the four datasets, validating the evaluation metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt tuning framework (Lester et al., 2021; Gal et al., 2023) from language modelling and single-task visual generation to a systematic cross-task analysis spanning text-to-image generation, open-vocabulary detection, and zero-shot classification. Rather than proposing a new tuning method, it reframes the question: what do the optimized soft prompts actually represent? The paper draws a direct analogy to adversarial examples (Goodfellow et al., 2015), showing that prompt tuning solutions function as perturbations on the text encoder's embedding space rather than as emergent visual representations. It also connects to prior work on prompt transferability in language models (Passigan et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2023) by extending the transferability question to models with different label modalities (images, bounding boxes, class labels).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that soft prompts are model-specific adversarial perturbations rather than transferable visual representations has direct practical implications: prompts trained for one model or task cannot be reused for another without retraining, undermining a key promise of the prompt-tuning paradigm for reducing ML system costs. The paper provides a useful benchmark (4,800 embeddings, 40 concepts, 4 datasets, 3 models) for evaluating future transfer methods, but does not propose solutions. Open questions include whether regularization or architectural changes could guide prompt tuning toward the semantically meaningful, transferable representations that the CKA analysis shows do exist in the embedding space, and whether the fractured-solution phenomenon persists in next-generation multimodal models.
