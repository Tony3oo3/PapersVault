## Problem

Standard LoRA training updates low-rank factors simultaneously via plain gradient descent, which does not guarantee convergence to a local optimum because the induced projection onto the tangent space is non-orthogonal unless the factors are orthonormal with unit singular values. Existing methods that do guarantee optimality (e.g., DLRT) require multiple sequential gradient tape evaluations per iteration, making them significantly slower than practical baselines like AdaLoRA. GeoLoRA addresses this gap by providing a single-backward-pass training procedure that follows the correct Riemannian gradient flow while maintaining computational parity with standard LoRA-style updates.

## Input / Output

GeoLoRA is applied to pre-trained networks by injecting low-rank adapters of the form USV^T into attention (Q, K, V) and feed-forward weight matrices, with frozen pre-trained weights W_pt. For NLP (DeBERTaV3-base on GLUE), input is tokenized text and output is a classification label or similarity score; for vision (ViT-base-patch16-224 on CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet), input is a 224×224 RGB image and output is a class label; for Stable Diffusion Dreambooth, input is a text prompt and output is a generated image. The adapter rank is not fixed a priori but is determined dynamically per layer via SVD truncation with a relative threshold τ, so the parameter budget adapts automatically during training.

## Method

GeoLoRA reformulates the low-rank dynamics by introducing auxiliary variables K = US and L = VS^T, which eliminates the stiff S^{-1} term in the Riemannian gradient flow while preserving the correct projected gradient direction. Each iteration performs: (1) a single backward pass to obtain ∇_U L, ∇_S L, ∇_V L; (2) parallel gradient steps on S, K, and L; (3) basis augmentation via QR decomposition of [U_0 | K^new] and [V_0 | L^new], doubling the rank to allow adaptive rank growth; (4) assembly of a 2r×2r augmented coefficient matrix; and (5) SVD-based truncation with a relative threshold τ to prune negligible singular values. This design requires only one gradient tape and one small SVD per step, matching the per-iteration cost of AdaLoRA, while provably converging to a stationary point satisfying the local optimality condition P(W*)∇L(W*)=0. Theoretical guarantees include a stochastic descent bound, convergence under Robbins-Monro learning rates, and an error bound showing the low-rank trajectory stays close to the full-rank gradient flow.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with DeBERTaV3-base, GeoLoRA achieves a mean score of 88.47 with only 0.86M trainable parameters, outperforming AdaLoRA (88.30, 1.27M params), LoRA (87.87, 1.33M), DoRA (87.96, 1.33M), and full fine-tuning (87.92, 184M). On ViT-base-patch16-224, GeoLoRA reaches 98.55% on CIFAR-10, 91.63% on CIFAR-100, and 88.09% on Tiny-ImageNet, surpassing AdaLoRA (98.51%, 91.44%, 87.21%) with fewer parameters. On Dreambooth Stable Diffusion, GeoLoRA achieves a validation loss of 0.242 with 2.6M parameters, beating AdaLoRA (0.245, 4.7M) and LoRA (0.275, 3.0M). GeoLoRA also trains ~28% faster than AdaLoRA (17.6 vs 13.75 it/s evaluation, 5.6 vs 4.83 it/s training on GLUE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoLoRA outperforms AdaLoRA on 6 of 8 GLUE tasks (SST-2, CoLA, QNLI, MRPC, STS-B, and overall mean) while using 32% fewer parameters (0.86M vs 1.27M); AdaLoRA retains a narrow edge on MNLI (90.44 vs 90.38) and RTE (86.00 vs 85.93). Against fixed-rank LoRA (87.87 mean) and LoRA+ (87.85), GeoLoRA gains +0.60 and +0.62 mean points respectively, with the largest gap on STS-B (+2.48 over LoRA, +3.51 over LoRA+). DoRA (87.96) is outperformed by GeoLoRA by +0.51 mean, with GeoLoRA notably stronger on SST-2 (+1.68) and MRPC (+0.78). On ViT fine-tuning, GeoLoRA beats AdaLoRA on all three datasets (CIFAR-10: 98.55 vs 98.51; CIFAR-100: 91.63 vs 91.44; Tiny-ImageNet: 88.09 vs 87.21) while using fewer parameters on CIFAR-100 (0.35M vs 0.45M). On Dreambooth, GeoLoRA achieves the lowest validation loss (0.242) with 2.6M parameters, beating AdaLoRA (0.245, 4.7M) and LoRA (0.275, 3.0M). GeoLoRA also trains ~28% faster than AdaLoRA across GLUE benchmarks (5.6 vs 4.83 it/s), and ablations show it is significantly more robust to learning-rate perturbations, where AdaLoRA diverges at λ=8e-3 on CIFAR-100 while GeoLoRA remains stable.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoLoRA bridges two previously separate lines of work: dynamical low-rank approximation theory from numerical PDE solvers (Koch & Lubich, 2007; Ceruti et al., 2023) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning via low-rank adapters (LoRA, AdaLoRA). While prior geometric low-rank training methods (DLRT, Schotthofer et al. 2022; Zangrando et al. 2024) already exploited Riemannian geometry to guarantee convergence to optimal low-rank solutions, they required multiple sequential gradient tape evaluations per step, making them impractical for large-scale PEFT. GeoLoRA's key departure is a reformulation of the projected gradient flow into variables (S, K, L) that can be updated in a single backward pass, matching the computational cost of AdaLoRA while inheriting the convergence and local-optimality guarantees of the geometric approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first PEFT method with provable convergence to a locally optimal low-rank adapter at the same per-iteration cost as AdaLoRA, addressing a gap where prior methods either lacked guarantees (LoRA, AdaLoRA) or were too expensive (DLRT). The robustness to learning rate and initial rank choices reduces the hyperparameter tuning burden that plagues current LoRA variants. Open questions include whether the per-iteration SVD of the 2r×2r coefficient matrix becomes a bottleneck at very high ranks, how the method scales to multi-billion-parameter models with thousands of adapter layers, and whether the global truncation strategy (Proposition 1) can be further optimized for heterogeneous architectures.
