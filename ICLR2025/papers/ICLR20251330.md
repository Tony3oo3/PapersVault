## Problem

Previous analyses of parallel and asynchronous stochastic optimization assumed fixed, equal computation speeds for all workers, failing to capture real-world scenarios such as random outages, time-varying performance, and heterogeneous hardware speeds. This paper asks what the optimal time complexities are when workers can exhibit virtually arbitrary computation behavior over time, and whether known methods remain optimal under such generality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251330/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a smooth nonconvex function f: R^d → R, n workers each characterized by a computation power function v_i: R_+ → R_+ (non-negative, continuous almost everywhere), and stochastic gradient oracles with σ²-bounded variance. The output is an ε-stationary point (E[‖∇f(x̄)‖²] ≤ ε) together with the optimal time complexity, which is expressed as an implicit sequence {t_k} defined recursively by the computation power functions; no closed-form exists in general, but the sequence is computable numerically via bisection since the defining functions are non-decreasing.

## Method

The core contribution is the universal computation model, where worker i computes ⌊∫_{t₀}^{t₁} v_i(τ)dτ⌋ stochastic gradients in interval [t₀, t₁], unifying fixed speeds, periodic fluctuations, random outages, and arbitrary trends under a single formalism. Using a time multiple oracles protocol in which algorithms control both time and oracle selection, the paper proves tight lower bounds for zero-respecting algorithms in both homogeneous and heterogeneous setups, constructing worst-case instances from the function F_T of Carmon et al. (2020) with stochastic oracles that zero out the last non-zero coordinate with probability p. The lower-bound proofs require new concentration arguments to handle the time-varying nature of computation powers, reducing the problem to counting indices where geometric random variables exceed a threshold. In the homogeneous setup, the bound is matched by Rennala SGD, which collects fixed-size batches of S = max{⌈σ²/ε⌉, 1} gradients from all workers in parallel; in the heterogeneous setup, it is matched by Malenia SGD, which uses a harmonic-mean-based stopping criterion that adapts to per-worker computation rates. Both methods achieve optimality without prior knowledge of the computation power functions, making the implicit nature of the bounds purely a theoretical concern.

## Results

The paper establishes tight (matching up to constants) lower and upper bounds on the time complexity of parallel stochastic optimization under a new universal computation model that captures arbitrary, time-varying, and heterogeneous worker speeds. In the homogeneous setup, the optimal time complexity is defined by the implicit sequence $\bar{t}_k$ (Eq. 12), achieved by Rennala SGD; in the heterogeneous setup, it is given by the harmonic-mean sequence $\bar{t}_k$ (Eq. 19), achieved by Malenia SGD. For the special case of fixed computation powers, the homogeneous complexity reduces to $\Theta(\min_{m\in[n]}(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}v_{\pi_i})^{-1}(L\Delta/\varepsilon + L\Delta\sigma^2/(m\varepsilon^2)))$ and the heterogeneous complexity to $\Theta(\max_{i\in[n]}1/v_i + (\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}1/v_i)\sigma^2/(n\varepsilon))$, recovering and generalizing prior results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical benchmarks, so comparisons are between theoretical bounds rather than method accuracies. The lower bounds (Theorems 5.1, 6.2) apply to virtually all synchronous and asynchronous methods, including Minibatch SGD, Asynchronous SGD (Recht et al., 2011), and Picky SGD (Cohen et al., 2021), showing none can beat the stated time complexity under the universal computation model. The upper bounds are matched by Rennala SGD (homogeneous) and Malenia SGD (heterogeneous) from Tyurin & Richtárik (2023), which were previously shown optimal only under the fixed computation model. The key advance over prior work (Mishchenko et al., 2022; Koloskova et al., 2022; Tyurin & Richtárik, 2023) is that the optimality now holds under arbitrary time-varying, heterogeneous, and potentially random computation powers, including random outages and periodic fluctuations, without requiring prior knowledge of the workers' speed profiles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the time complexity analysis of parallel stochastic optimization from the fixed computation model (Tyurin & Richtárik, 2023), where worker speeds are constant, to a new 'universal computation model' where computation powers v_i(t) can be arbitrary non-negative functions of time, capturing irregular speeds, periodic fluctuations, and random outages. The lower bound constructions adapt the worst-case function F_T from Carmon et al. (2020) and the coordinate-zeroing stochastic oracle from Arjevani et al. (2022) to the parallel asynchronous setting, with new concentration arguments to handle time-varying computation. The key positioning is that prior time complexity results (Mishchenko et al., 2022; Koloskova et al., 2022; Tyurin & Richtárik, 2023) all assumed bounded or fixed computation times, and it was unclear whether their optimal methods would remain optimal under realistic heterogeneous and time-varying computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes the problem of determining optimal time complexities for parallel stochastic optimization under virtually arbitrary computation behavior, providing a complete theoretical picture that practitioners can reference when designing distributed training systems with heterogeneous hardware. The fact that Rennala SGD and Malenia SGD achieve optimality without any prior knowledge of the computation power functions is practically significant: no re-tuning is needed when hardware performance changes. Open questions include extending the analysis to correlated computation and gradient randomness, and whether similar tight bounds can be established for accelerated or second-order methods under the universal computation model.
