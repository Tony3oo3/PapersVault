## Problem

Predictive coding networks (PCNs) have been shown to match backpropagation on small-scale tasks, but the field lacks a unified benchmark, a fast open-source library, and a clear understanding of where PCNs fail at scale. Prior works propose ad-hoc tasks and architectures without cross-comparison, and the absence of specialized tooling makes even small hyperparameter searches prohibitively slow, obscuring the fundamental scalability bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251276/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In discriminative mode, the network takes a grayscale or color image (28×28 for MNIST/FashionMNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) and produces a class label; the input is clamped to the bottom layer h_0 and the label to the top layer h_L. In generative mode, the image is clamped to h_L and inference runs downward to yield a compressed latent code at h_0, which is then decoded back into a reconstructed or generated image. For associative memory, corrupted inputs (Gaussian noise σ=0.2 or bottom-half masking) are fed to h_L and the network must recover the original training image.

## Method

The paper introduces PCX, a JAX-based library that implements PCNs as hierarchical Gaussian generative models trained by alternating inference (T gradient steps on latent states h) and learning (one gradient step on weights θ) to minimize variational free energy. It benchmarks six learning algorithms—standard PC with SE/CE loss, incremental PC, Monte Carlo PC with Langevin dynamics, and positive/negative/centered nudging adapted for the first time from the equilibrium propagation literature—across MLP, VGG-5/7/9, and ResNet-18 architectures on five datasets. A key empirical finding is that PC performance peaks at VGG-5 depth and degrades monotonically with additional layers (VGG-5 > VGG-7 > VGG-9 > ResNet-18), the opposite of backpropagation, traced to exponentially growing energy imbalances across layers that suppress gradients in early layers. The paper further identifies an interaction between the AdamW optimizer and hidden-layer width that destabilizes training for wide PCNs, and shows that skip connections can partially rescue very deep architectures (VGG-19 accuracy jumps from 25% to 74% on CIFAR-10).

## Results

On VGG-5 architectures, centered nudging (CN) matches or slightly exceeds backpropagation: 89.47% on CIFAR-10 (vs. BP-SE 89.43%), 67.19% top-1 on CIFAR-100 (vs. 66.28%), and 46.38% top-1 on Tiny ImageNet (vs. 44.90%). Performance degrades with depth: on VGG-7/CIFAR-10, CN drops to 88.40% (BP-SE: 89.91%), and on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10, the best PC method (iPC) reaches only 70.44% versus BP-CE's 92.83%. In generative mode, iPC achieves the lowest reconstruction MSE on CelebA (1.30×10⁻³ vs. PC 2.35×10⁻³ and BP 3.34×10⁻³), and MCPC outperforms a VAE on FID (2.53 vs. 4.19) for MNIST generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against backpropagation (the primary baseline), PC with centered nudging (CN) achieves parity or a slight edge on VGG-5 (within ~1% on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet), but falls behind by 1.5–3% on VGG-7 and by 22–43% on ResNet-18, revealing a clear scalability wall. Incremental PC (iPC, Salvatori et al. 2024) is the best PC variant on small MLPs (98.45% on MNIST) but degrades sharply on deeper networks, reaching only 70.44% on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 versus BP's 92.83%. In generative mode, MCPC (Oliviers et al. 2024) beats a standard VAE (Kingma & Welling 2013) on FID for MNIST (2.53 vs. 4.19) but trails on inception score (7.13 vs. 7.91), while iPC outperforms both PC and BP on CelebA reconstruction MSE (1.30 vs. 2.35 and 3.34 ×10⁻³). PCX is also ~2.5–3× faster per epoch than the prior open-source PC library (Song 2024), e.g., 5.33 s vs. 13.49 s on VGG-5/CIFAR-100 and 54.6 s vs. 137.6 s on VGG-7/Tiny ImageNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the predictive coding (PC) framework originating from Rao & Ballard (1999) and its adaptation for training neural networks (Whittington & Bogacz, 2017), unifying several algorithmic variants—standard PC, incremental PC (Salvatori et al., 2024), Monte Carlo PC with Langevin dynamics (Oliviers et al., 2024), and nudging strategies borrowed from equilibrium propagation (Scellier & Bengio, 2017)—under a single open-source library (PCX) and standardized benchmark. It departs from prior PC work, which typically proposed bespoke tasks and architectures without cross-comparison, by introducing a uniform evaluation protocol spanning MLPs through ResNet-18 on datasets from MNIST to Tiny ImageNet. The first application of nudging algorithms to PC models (previously confined to the Eqprop literature) is a notable methodological bridge between the two communities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most important contribution is a clear, quantified characterization of PC's scalability wall: performance degrades monotonically with depth (VGG5 > VGG7 > VGG9 > ResNet-18), the opposite of BP, with ResNet-18 PC achieving only ~43% on CIFAR-10 versus ~93% for BP. The mechanistic analysis identifies severe inter-layer energy imbalance (up to 6 orders of magnitude) and an unexpected Adam-optimizer/width interaction as key bottlenecks, pointing to concrete future directions (better energy propagation, width-aware optimizers, skip connections). As the first comprehensive PC benchmark with a fast JAX-based library, it provides the infrastructure the field has lacked for reproducible, iterative progress toward the open problem of scaling bioplausible learning to modern architectures.
