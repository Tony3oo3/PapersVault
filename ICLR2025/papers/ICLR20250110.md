## Problem

Decision tree induction is NP-complete, forcing practitioners to choose between fast but suboptimal greedy methods (CART, C4.5) and globally optimal but exponentially scaling exact methods (GOSDT, DL8.5) that are restricted to classification. Genetic programming offers a scalable middle ground, but conventional genetic operators are structurally unguided, lack semantic awareness of the solution space, and are limited to binary arity, resulting in inefficient exploration of the combinatorial tree space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular dataset along with a semantic task context (feature names, types, value ranges, label distribution, and a natural-language task description) and a population of parent trees serialized as nested JSON dictionaries where keys encode splitting conditions (feature name and threshold) and leaf values encode class labels or regression outputs. The LLM is queried via structured prompts containing these serialized trees as few-shot examples, and outputs new trees in the same nested-dictionary format, constrained to binary splits using only <, >, <=, >= operators at a fixed maximum depth (3 or 4 in experiments).

## Method

LLEGO replaces conventional GP genetic operators with LLM-based (GPT-3.5-turbo) semantically-aware operators that exploit the model's encoded domain knowledge as a prior over the tree search space. The fitness-guided crossover samples ν=4 parents via roulette-wheel selection weighted by fitness, computes a target fitness f* = f_max + α(f_max − f_min), and prompts the LLM to generate offspring conditioned on this target, steering search toward high-performing regions. The diversity-guided mutation samples parents uniformly, generates λ′ candidate offspring, and selects λ of them using a temperature-τ-controlled categorical distribution over negative log-probabilities, prioritizing candidates that are unlikely under the current population to maintain diversity. The natural-language representation enables higher arity (ν > 2) than traditional subtree crossover, and the two hyperparameters α and τ provide explicit, tunable control over the exploitation–exploration trade-off across generations.

## Results

On 7 classification datasets at depth 4, LLEGO achieves the best average rank (1.0) across all methods, with balanced accuracy ranging from 0.663 (Compas) to 0.951 (Breast). On 5 regression datasets at depth 4, LLEGO achieves the lowest MSE on 3 of 5 datasets (Cars: 0.099, Cholesterol: 1.322, Wage: 1.067), outperforming both CART and GATree. The performance advantage over baselines is most pronounced at depth 4, where the search space is substantially larger, and LLEGO consistently achieves the lowest generalization gap across all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLEGO outperforms all five baselines on every classification dataset at depth 4, achieving the best balanced accuracy across the board (e.g., 0.751 on Heart vs. 0.713 for CART and 0.676 for GATree; 0.721 on Diabetes vs. 0.704 for CART). Against the GP baseline GATree, LLEGO's advantage is most pronounced on Heart (+7.5 pp) and Diabetes (+4.6 pp), confirming the benefit of semantic priors over unguided structural mutations. The exact optimization methods GOSDT and DL8.5 are competitive at depth 3 but fall behind LLEGO at depth 4, where their exponential complexity limits effective search. On regression, LLEGO wins on 3 of 5 datasets (Cars, Cholesterol, Wage) but is slightly outperformed by CART on Abalone (0.577 vs. 0.561 MSE) and Wine (0.828 vs. 0.807 MSE). Notably, LLEGO is the only method applicable to both classification and regression objectives, whereas GOSDT and DL8.5 are restricted to classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of genetic programming for decision tree induction (Koza, GATree) and the emerging use of LLMs as variation operators in evolutionary search (Meyerson et al., Lehman et al.). It departs from conventional GP operators, which are structurally-based and semantically unaware, by repurposing LLMs as conditional generative models that encode domain knowledge about the tree search space. Unlike prior semantic GP work (Krawiec, Moraglio), which was domain-specific and never applied to tree induction, LLEGO uses LLM in-context learning to implement fitness-guided crossover and diversity-guided mutation with controllable hyperparameters. It also extends prior LLM-as-mutation-operator work (code evolution, prompt optimization) by introducing *guided* rather than unguided variations, conditioning offspring generation on target fitness and using log-probabilities to steer exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Decision trees remain a critical interpretable model class in high-stakes domains (healthcare, finance), and this work demonstrates that LLM-guided evolutionary search can consistently outperform both greedy (CART, C4.5) and exact (GOSDT, DL8.5) methods, particularly in larger search spaces (depth 4). The key open question is the computational cost: LLM inference makes each generation far more expensive than conventional GP, limiting practical adoption unless inference costs drop or the approach is distilled. The paper also raises important questions about bias propagation from LLMs into evolved trees and whether the method generalizes beyond tabular decision trees to other combinatorial optimization problems with natural-language-representable solutions.
