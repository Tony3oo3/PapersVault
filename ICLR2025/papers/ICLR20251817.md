## Problem

Existing prompt learning methods for vision-language models either use simplistic templates like 'a photo of a {class}' or append unstructured LLM-generated descriptions, failing to exploit the inherent hierarchical structure among those descriptions. Moreover, aligning all descriptions with a single CLS token cannot capture attribute-level visual nuances, and LLM-generated descriptions often include irrelevant or contradictory details (e.g., 'steamed' and 'fried' for dumplings) that mislead classification on specific images.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images encoded by a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone and a set of LLM-generated structured text descriptions organized in a per-class Tree of Attributes (concept → attribute → description). At inference, the final class prediction is produced by a weighted fusion (hyperparameter α=0.4) of the global CLS-token logit and the average of per-attribute expert-token logits, yielding a single class label. The text prompts are generated offline via GPT-3.5-turbo in a three-step pipeline (attribute generation, in-context example, full description generation) and are shared across all classes within a dataset.

## Method

TAP distills a structured knowledge graph from LLMs in a top-down 'concept–attribute–description' tree for each class, then learns it bottom-up via three coupled mechanisms. First, learnable visual expert tokens (one per attribute) are inserted into the vision encoder input sequence with deep prompting (zero-initialized layer residuals), replacing the reliance on a single CLS token for attribute-level alignment. Second, a Vision-Conditional Pooling (VCP) layer uses each expert token as an attention query over the embedded descriptions of its attribute, producing an instance-specific attribute-level text embedding that suppresses irrelevant or contradictory descriptions. Third, training minimizes a contrastive loss between each expert token and its corresponding attribute embedding, plus L1/KL/contrastive regularization against the frozen CLIP features; at inference, predictions from the CLS token and all attribute experts are fused via a weighted sum of cosine-similarity logits.

## Results

On base-to-novel generalization across 11 datasets, TAP achieves an average harmonic mean (HM) of 81.04%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art PromptSRC (79.97%) by 1.07% and vanilla CLIP (71.70%) by 9.34%. On cross-dataset transfer (trained on ImageNet, zero-shot on 10 target datasets), TAP achieves 66.56% average target accuracy, outperforming PromptSRC by 0.75% while also improving source ImageNet accuracy by 1.03%. On 16-shot few-shot classification, TAP reaches 83.37% average accuracy, exceeding PromptSRC (82.87%) by 0.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAP outperforms all seven baselines on the primary base-to-novel generalization task, achieving the best average HM (81.04%), Base (84.75%), and Novel (77.63%) simultaneously. The closest competitor, PromptSRC, trails by 1.07% in HM, while LoGoPrompt is 2.01% behind; the gap widens substantially against earlier methods like CoOp (9.38% HM gap) and CoCoOp (5.21% HM gap). On cross-dataset transfer, TAP wins on 8 of 10 target datasets versus PromptSRC and uniquely improves source ImageNet accuracy (72.30%) over all baselines, whereas CoCoOp and PromptSRC sacrifice 0.49% and 0.24% on the source respectively. In 16-shot few-shot classification, TAP leads on 9 of 11 datasets with 83.37% average, narrowly beating PromptSRC (82.87%) and substantially outperforming CoCoOp (74.90%). The largest per-dataset gains appear on fine-grained benchmarks: TAP improves novel-class accuracy by 8.27% on EuroSAT, 5.03% on DTD, and 3.63% on UCF101 over PromptSRC.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAP sits at the intersection of two prompt-learning lineages: (1) learnable continuous prompts for CLIP (CoOp, CoCoOp, PromptSRC) that optimize a small set of tokens but rely on the simplistic 'a photo of a {class}' template, and (2) LLM-generated description prompting (Menon & Vondrick 2023, Roth et al. 2023, Pratt et al. 2023) that enriches text prompts with free-form descriptions but treats them as an unstructured flat set. TAP departs from both by distilling a hierarchical 'concept → attribute → description' knowledge graph from LLMs and aligning each attribute branch with a dedicated learnable visual expert token, while a vision-conditional pooling layer selects instance-relevant descriptions per attribute. The visual expert tokens borrow the deep-prompting mechanism from VPT, and the contrastive training objective follows the CoOp/PromptSRC recipe with added regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured Tree-of-Attribute framing offers a principled way to exploit LLM world knowledge for VLM adaptation, and the vision-conditional pooling mechanism addresses a real misalignment problem (e.g., 'fried' vs. 'steamed' dumplings) that flat description averaging cannot resolve. However, the paper itself acknowledges a key limitation: on fine-grained datasets where classes differ subtly, LLMs generate near-identical descriptions for distinct classes, undermining the approach's core assumption that structured attributes provide discriminative signal. Open questions include whether the tree structure can be learned end-to-end rather than generated offline, whether the method scales to open-vocabulary or video settings, and whether the modest 1% gains over PromptSRC justify the added complexity of multiple expert tokens and per-attribute VCP layers.
