## Problem

Knowledge-guided context optimization (KGCO) methods for prompt tuning of vision-language models enforce consistency between learnable and crafted prompts to prevent catastrophic forgetting, but this introduces a bias toward pre-training knowledge that degrades performance on base (seen) classes. The paper identifies an unresolved contradiction: existing methods cannot simultaneously preserve general knowledge for unseen classes and capture task-specific discriminative knowledge for seen classes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of class names as input. A sequence of 4 learnable prompt tokens is concatenated with each class token and passed through the frozen CLIP text encoder (ViT-B/16 backbone) to produce per-class text embeddings; the image is encoded by the frozen visual encoder. Classification is performed via cosine-similarity contrastive matching between the image embedding and the N_c text embeddings, yielding a single class label. No additional parameters are introduced beyond the 4 prompt tokens.

## Method

DeKg augments the standard KGCO objective (cross-entropy loss plus a Euclidean consistency constraint L_kg between learnable and crafted prompt embeddings) with a new independence regularization L_kd computed via the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC). Specifically, L_kd = (N_c−1)⁻² tr(K H K^clip H), where K is the inner-product kernel matrix over learnable embeddings and K^clip is the fixed kernel matrix over crafted CLIP embeddings; the centering matrix H removes mean bias. Because K^clip is fixed, L_kd depends only on the learnable prompts and introduces zero extra parameters. The total loss is L = L_ce + λL_kg + μL_kd with λ/μ = 3/1. The key insight is that L_kg enforces intra-class consistency (preserving general knowledge) while L_kd enforces inter-class divergence guided by the crafted prompts' structure (capturing task-specific knowledge), making the two constraints complementary. The method is plug-and-play, seamlessly integrating with existing KGCO frameworks such as KgCoOp and TCP.

## Results

On the base-to-new generalization benchmark (16-shot, ViT-B/16, 11 datasets), DeKg_TCP achieves the best harmonic mean of 80.44%, with 84.96% base accuracy and 76.38% new accuracy, outperforming the strongest baselines PromptSRC (H=79.97%) and TCP (H=79.51%). In cross-dataset generalization (trained on ImageNet, tested on 10 target datasets), DeKg_TCP attains the highest average accuracy of 66.64% vs. 66.29% for TCP. In 4-shot few-shot classification, DeKg_TCP reaches 77.06% average accuracy, surpassing TCP (76.72%) and PromptSRC (75.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeKg_TCP outperforms all eight baselines on the harmonic mean in base-to-new generalization. Against the zero-shot CLIP baseline (Radford et al., 2021), DeKg_TCP gains +15.62% on base accuracy (84.96 vs. 69.34) while maintaining a slight edge on new classes (76.38 vs. 74.22). Compared to CoOp (Zhou et al., 2022b), which overfits to base classes, DeKg_TCP improves base by +2.32% and new by +8.38%. Against the knowledge-guided baselines KgCoOp (Yao et al., 2023) and ProGrad (Zhu et al., 2023), which suffer from bias toward pre-training, DeKg_TCP recovers the lost base accuracy (84.96 vs. 80.73/82.48) while also improving new-class accuracy. The closest competitors are PromptSRC (Khattak et al., 2023b) and TCP (Yao et al., 2024); DeKg_TCP beats PromptSRC by +0.70% on base and +0.28% on new, and beats TCP by +0.83% on base and +1.02% on new. In cross-dataset generalization, DeKg_TCP leads TCP by +0.35% average and shows the largest gains on fine-grained datasets like Aircraft (+1.60%). In 4-shot few-shot learning, DeKg_TCP achieves the best average (77.06%), with notable margins over TCP on Flowers (+0.81%) and EuroSAT (+1.73%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge-guided context optimization (KGCO) lineage initiated by KgCoOp (Yao et al., 2023) and TCP (Yao et al., 2024), which added consistency constraints between learnable and crafted prompts to prevent catastrophic forgetting in CLIP-based prompt tuning. The key conceptual departure is identifying that these consistency constraints, while improving new-class generalization, introduce a systematic bias toward pre-trained representations that degrades base-class discrimination. DeKg addresses this by introducing an HSIC-based independence regularization that encourages divergence between learnable and crafted prompts, positioning itself as a complementary term to the existing consistency loss rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of DeKg (zero additional parameters, seamless integration with KgCoOp and TCP) makes it a practical drop-in improvement for the prompt tuning community, and the clean decomposition—consistency for intra-class generalization, independence for inter-class discrimination—offers a useful conceptual framework for understanding the base/new class trade-off. However, the improvements are modest (roughly 1–2% harmonic mean), the evaluation is confined to ViT-B/16 with context length 4 across 11 datasets, and it remains open whether the HSIC regularization generalizes to larger backbones, longer prompts, or non-CLIP VLMs. The paper does not explore whether the independence constraint interacts differently with visual-side prompt tuning (e.g., MaPLe) or with conditional prompt learning.
