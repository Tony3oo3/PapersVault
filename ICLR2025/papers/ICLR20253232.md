## Problem

Previous results on playing against no-regret learning agents in Stackelberg games and contract design established only qualitative U*±o(1) bounds without characterizing the exact magnitude of the o(1) terms, and did not cover Bayesian persuasion or provide a unified framework. This paper asks: what is the largest class of principal-agent problems where no-swap-regret learning caps the principal's utility at U*+o(1), what is the precise dependence on the agent's regret, and can all such problems be analyzed in a single framework?

## Input / Output

The framework takes a generalized principal-agent problem (convex decision space X, finite action set A, signal set S, utility functions u,v linear in x, and an optional convex constraint C on the principal's strategy) together with the agent's contextual regret bound CReg(T) or contextual swap-regret bound CSReg(T). It produces explicit asymmetric bounds: a lower bound of U* − O(√(Reg(T)/T)) achievable by the principal using a fixed strategy, and an upper bound of U* + O(SReg(T)/T) that holds even against adaptive principal strategies. The square-root vs. linear asymmetry is shown to be intrinsic via a tight Bayesian persuasion example.

## Method

The core technique is a reduction from the T-round repeated game with a learning agent to a one-shot generalized principal-agent problem with an approximately-best-responding agent, where the approximation parameter δ equals the agent's average regret Reg(T)/T. A no-regret agent's time-averaged strategy ρ is shown to be a δ-best-response to the principal's fixed strategy, yielding the maxmin objective OBJ^R(δ) as a lower bound; a no-swap-regret agent's strategy is shown to be a δ-best-response to a hypothetical joint-signal strategy, yielding the maxmax objective OBJ^R(δ) as an upper bound. The analysis of these objectives uses a perturbation argument: any δ-best-responding agent strategy can be made exactly best-responding by slightly perturbing the principal's strategy, bounding the utility gap via the inducibility gap G, the utility Lipschitz constant L, and the decision-space diameter. The √δ lower bound for randomized strategies (vs. δ for deterministic) arises because a randomized δ-best-response can place √δ probability mass on √δ-suboptimal actions, and this is shown tight via a two-state Bayesian persuasion instance. Additionally, the paper shows that mean-based learning algorithms (no-regret but not no-swap-regret) are exploitable: the principal can switch signaling schemes mid-game to exploit the agent's reliance on historical averages, achieving utility significantly above U*.

## Results

The paper establishes that in all generalized principal-agent problems where the agent has no private information, the principal's achievable utility against a no-swap-regret learning agent lies in the asymmetric range [U* − Θ(√(SReg(T)/T)), U* + O(SReg(T)/T)]. Specifically, the principal can guarantee at least U* − O(√(Reg(T)/T)) using a fixed strategy (Theorem 3.1 + Theorem 4.1/4.2), while no adaptive strategy can exceed U* + O(SReg(T)/T) (Theorem 3.4 + Theorem 4.1/4.2). The √(δ) lower bound is tight: Example 4.1 constructs a Bayesian persuasion instance where the principal's utility is at most U* − Ω(√δ) for any strategy. For mean-based (no-regret but not no-swap-regret) agents, the principal can exploit the agent to achieve utility significantly above U* (Theorem 3.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper refines the qualitative U* ± o(1) bounds established by Deng et al. (2019) for Stackelberg games and Guruganesh et al. (2024) for contract design by providing explicit quantitative terms: O(√(Reg(T)/T)) for the lower bound and O(SReg(T)/T) for the upper bound. The key advance is generality: these bounds are shown to hold for *all* generalized principal-agent problems where the agent has no private information, including Bayesian persuasion (where the principal is privately informed), which was not covered by prior work. The paper also identifies the largest class of problems where no-swap-regret caps the principal at U* + o(1), answering an open question: the cap holds precisely when the agent lacks private information, and fails when the agent is privately informed (as in auctions, per Braverman et al. 2018, and Bayesian Stackelberg games, per Mansour et al. 2022). Additionally, the paper introduces the maxmax objective and shows that mean-based learning agents (e.g., MWU, EXP-3) are exploitable beyond U*, a phenomenon not captured by prior no-regret analyses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the line of work on principal-agent problems with learning agents, which had been studied separately for specific game classes: Stackelberg games (Deng et al., 2019), contract design (Guruganesh et al., 2024), and auctions (Braverman et al., 2018). It builds on the generalized principal-agent framework of Myerson (1982) and Gan et al. (2024), the no-swap-regret concept of Hart & Mas-Colell (2000), and the contextual multi-armed bandit formulation of Lu et al. (2010), reducing the repeated learning problem to a one-shot approximate-best-response problem. The key departure from prior work is providing tight quantitative bounds (explicit O(√(Reg(T)/T)) and O(SReg(T)/T) terms) rather than the o(1) characterizations in earlier papers, and extending the analysis to the constrained case (Bayesian persuasion) where the principal is privately informed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification shows that the no-swap-regret cap on principal utility at U*+o(1) holds for the entire class of generalized principal-agent problems without agent private information, answering a natural question about the boundary of this phenomenon. The asymmetric tight bounds (√δ worst-case vs. δ best-case) and the demonstration that mean-based learners are exploitable provide a more complete picture of when and how a principal can benefit from agent learning. Open questions include extending to settings where the agent has private information, non-stationary environments, or where both players are learning, and deriving exact upper bounds for no-regret-but-not-no-swap-regret algorithms beyond the mean-based case.
