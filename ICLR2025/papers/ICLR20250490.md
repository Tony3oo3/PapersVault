## Problem

Discrete diffusion language models lack tight, computationally efficient perplexity bounds for evaluation, and the standard score-entropy ratio-matching training objective is suboptimal compared to simpler alternatives. Additionally, existing transition-rate matrices (absorb, uniform) do not allow token refinement after unmasking in the reverse process, limiting their utility for tasks like spelling correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250490/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text sequence of length L over a vocabulary of size V, which is corrupted at a sampled noise level t via a CTMC forward process (token-level transitions). The network outputs an L×V matrix of per-token marginal probabilities (the probability that each position's original token was each vocabulary item), from which conditional ratios are reconstructed analytically for generation. For evaluation, the method produces a perplexity upper bound (J₂) computed in closed form; for the spelling-correction task, it takes character-level text with 5% injected errors and outputs the most likely correction.

## Method

The paper introduces CEDD (Cross-Entropy Discrete Diffusion), which reparametrizes the score network so that the neural network f_θ predicts per-token marginal probabilities p_{0|t}^i(h|x_t) rather than ratios directly; the true conditional ratios p_{t|0}(y^i|h)/p_{t|0}(x_t^i|h) are computed analytically from the matrix exponential and combined with f_θ to reconstruct the full score. Training minimizes a weighted denoising cross-entropy loss L_ll over the clean tokens, which is ~15% faster per step than score-entropy and yields up to 10% lower perplexity. The paper also proves three theorems on KL divergence in CTMCs, culminating in Theorem 4 which gives a tighter perplexity bound J₂ that avoids the intractable K(a) term, and introduces roulette diffusion—a transition-rate matrix interpolating between absorb and uniform dynamics with a closed-form matrix exponential that enables token correction after unmasking.

## Results

On OpenWebText-trained models (L=128, 1024 reverse steps), CEDD* Absorb achieves the best generative perplexity of 143.86 and the lowest perplexity bounds across all five test sets (e.g., LAMBADA 64.60, WikiText2 65.04, PTB 192.99, WikiText103 64.69, 1BW 79.81), outperforming SEDD Absorb (GenPerp 172.35) by approximately 17% in generative perplexity. At L=1024, CEDD* Absorb reaches GenPerp 101.83 and WikiText2 perplexity 41.57, surpassing PLAID (51.80) and D3PM (77.28) on WikiText2 while remaining competitive with GPT-2 (37.68). Training with CEDD is approximately 15% faster per iteration than SEDD due to the simplified cross-entropy loss, and the proposed J₂ perplexity bound is empirically tighter than J₁ in every tested configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CEDD* Absorb decisively outperforms SEDD Absorb (Lou et al., 2024) across all metrics: GenPerp 143.86 vs. 172.35 (~17% lower), LAMBADA 64.60 vs. 70.07, and PTB 192.99 vs. 240.43, with the gap narrowing but persisting at L=1024 (GenPerp 101.83 vs. 105.27). Against Discrete Flow Matching (Gat et al., 2024), CEDD* Absorb edges out DFM k_t=t on GenPerp (143.86 vs. 145.48) and leads on LAMBADA (64.60 vs. 71.90) and WikiText2 (65.04 vs. 71.20), though DFM k_t=t is competitive on PTB (221.15 vs. 192.99 in CEDD*'s favor). CEDD* Absorb also beats the SOTA masked diffusion model CEDDT (Sahoo et al., 2024) on GenPerp (143.86 vs. 154.04) and all five perplexity metrics at L=128, and maintains the advantage at L=1024 (GenPerp 101.83 vs. 108.88). Compared to D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) at L=1024, CEDD* Absorb substantially outperforms on every perplexity metric (e.g., LAMBADA 52.70 vs. 93.47, WikiText2 41.57 vs. 77.28). GPT-2 remains the strongest autoregressive baseline at L=1024 (GenPerp 41.02, WikiText2 37.68), but CEDD* narrows the gap considerably and converges faster in terms of parameter updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CTMC-based discrete diffusion framework of Lou et al. (2024) by providing three new theorems that serve as the discrete analogues of the continuous diffusion results in Song et al. (2021b), and by proposing CEDD — a cross-entropy training objective derived from the L_ll loss of Campbell et al. (2022) — as a replacement for the original score-entropy (SEDD) objective. The roulette diffusion transition-rate matrix is positioned as the only new matrix (besides absorb and uniform) with an analytically tractable exponential, filling a gap in the available forward-process choices. The cross-entropy training strategy is noted as concurrent with Sahoo et al. (2024), Shi et al. (2024), and Ou et al. (2025), which derived the same loss specifically for absorb diffusion; this paper generalizes the result to all CTMC dynamics and adds the theoretical perplexity bound (Theorem 4) that those works lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tighter J2 perplexity bound and the CEDD training strategy offer practical improvements (up to 10% lower perplexity, 15% faster training) for anyone building discrete diffusion language models, while the roulette diffusion matrix opens a path toward token-refinement capabilities useful for downstream tasks like spelling correction. However, the gap with autoregressive models (GPT-2) remains substantial at longer sequence lengths, and the paper acknowledges that noise schedules and time-dependent weighting were chosen heuristically rather than derived from first principles. Open questions include whether the CEDD advantage persists at scale, whether an optimal weighting schedule exists, and whether the Eroulette (time-evolving p_m) generalization yields further gains.
