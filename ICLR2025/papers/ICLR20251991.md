## Problem

The maximum mutual information (MMI) criterion, the fundamental objective in extractive rationalization, suffers from diminishing marginal returns: once most rationale components are identified, the remaining ones contribute only trivially to reducing cross-entropy, making it difficult for the extractor to locate them. This paper identifies this limitation both theoretically (via the non-additivity of mutual information) and empirically, and proposes an alternative objective that avoids the problem entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes text sequences (max length 256), graph structures, or images as input. The extractor produces a binary mask selecting rationale tokens, nodes, or pixels (targeting ~15% sparsity via a soft constraint), and the predictor consumes the masked rationale to produce a class label. Notably, the extractor's training signal comes from the L2 norm of the rationale's intermediate representation through the predictor's encoder, not from the final prediction output, decoupling rationale selection from the model's classification head.

## Method

N2R (Norm to Rationale) replaces the extractor's cross-entropy (MMI) objective with the negative log of the L2 norm of the rationale candidate's representation through the predictor's encoder: min -log(||Enc(Z)||₂). The key insight, borrowed from OOD detection, is that neural networks have low-rank weight matrices whose column vectors span a 'capability subspace'; inputs matching learned directions produce high-norm representations while uninformative inputs (orthogonal to those directions) produce near-zero norms. This norm objective grows approximately linearly with the proportion of true rationale components, avoiding the saturation that plagues cross-entropy. The predictor is still trained with standard cross-entropy, and the two objectives are alternated during training. A combined variant (MMI+N2R) further improves performance by leveraging MMI's stronger early-training gradients and the norm's stronger late-training gradients.

## Results

On the BeerAdvocate benchmark (GRU encoder), N2R achieves F1 scores of 72.7 on Beer-Appearance and 68.7 on Beer-Aroma, outperforming the strongest baseline A2I (67.1 and 67.6) by 8.3% and 1.6% relative improvement, respectively. On the HotelReviews benchmark, N2R reaches F1 of 48.6 (Hotel-Service) and 39.8 (Hotel-Cleanliness), beating A2I (47.1 and 36.5) by 5.1% and 9.0% relative improvement. With a BERT encoder, N2R achieves F1 of 49.9 (Beer-Appearance) and 57.6 (Beer-Aroma), surpassing the best prior method CR (39.0 and 49.1) by 10.9 and 8.5 points. On the graph dataset BA2Motifs (GCN encoder), N2R improves F1 from 36.4 (RNP) to 40.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

N2R consistently outperforms all seven baselines (RNP, Inter_RAT, NIR, A2I, A2R, INVRAT, CR) across all four text classification datasets and the graph dataset. Against the strongest text baseline A2I, N2R gains 8.3% relative F1 on Beer-Appearance (72.7 vs 67.1), 1.6% on Beer-Aroma (68.7 vs 67.6), 5.1% on Hotel-Service (48.6 vs 47.1), and 9.0% on Hotel-Cleanliness (39.8 vs 36.5). Against the BERT-based baseline CR, N2R achieves substantially higher F1 (49.9 vs 39.0 on Beer-Appearance; 57.6 vs 49.1 on Beer-Aroma). Inter_RAT performs notably worse than vanilla RNP on Beer-Appearance (F1 51.4 vs 66.5), while NIR is roughly on par with RNP. On the graph dataset BA2Motifs, N2R improves F1 by 3.8 points over RNP (40.2 vs 36.4). The combined MMI+N2R variant further improves over vanilla MMI by 13.1% (Beer-Appearance) and 13.5% (Hotel-Cleanliness) relative F1, confirming the two objectives complement each other across training stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RNP (Rationalizing Neural Predictions) cooperative game framework of Lei et al. (2016), preserving its extractor-predictor architecture and Gumbel-softmax selection mechanism, but replaces the long-standing MMI (maximum mutual information) objective for the extractor with a norm-based loss derived from OOD detection theory (Kang et al., 2024). All prior rationalization methods—Inter_RAT, NIR, A2R, CR, A2I—treat MMI as the fundamental criterion and add auxiliary regularizers on top; this paper instead argues that MMI itself is the bottleneck due to diminishing marginal returns, and proposes an orthogonal alternative objective. The theoretical grounding (low-rank weight matrices, orthogonality of unlearned inputs in high dimensions) is borrowed from the OOD detection literature and repurposed for interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying a fundamental limitation of the MMI criterion that has underpinned extractive rationalization for years, this work opens a new design space for rationale extraction that is decoupled from the model's final output, potentially enabling explanation of task-agnostic pretrained encoders. The method's simplicity (replacing one loss term) and generality across architectures (GRU, BERT, GCN) and modalities (text, graph, image) make it easy to adopt. Open questions include whether the norm objective generalizes beyond the RNP cooperative game, whether the uniform-informativeness assumption in the theoretical analysis holds in practice, and whether the approach can be extended to more complex explanation tasks (e.g., multi-label, structured outputs). The comparison with LLMs suggests rationalization remains competitive for extractive explanation even in the LLM era.
