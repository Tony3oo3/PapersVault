## Problem

Existing image captioning metrics (BLEU, CIDEr, SPICE, etc.) show poor correlation with human judgment and cannot assess hallucinations or fine-grained comprehensiveness in the hyper-detailed captions that modern VLMs produce. Additionally, traditional caption datasets rely on short, coarse-grained ground-truths that are inadequate for evaluating these models, leaving no reliable benchmark or metric purpose-built for detailed captioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252883/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a free-form captioning prompt and produces a detailed, fine-grained image caption. For evaluation and feedback, both generated and reference captions are decomposed into 'primitive information units' — the smallest self-sufficient, verifiable statements (e.g., 'cat_1 is sitting on the table') — which are then individually checked against the image. The DECAPBENCH benchmark uses 400 images with hyper-detailed, hallucination-free human-annotated captions sourced from ImageInWords, replacing the short COCO-style captions used in prior work.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified framework with three components. DCSCORE evaluates captions by decomposing them into primitive information units, matching generated units against reference units for comprehensiveness, and verifying each unit's factual correctness against the image via GPT-4o, yielding a combined precision-recall F1 that achieves 0.66 PCC with human experts (vs. 0.42 for the prior best). DECAPBENCH provides 400 image-caption pairs with hyper-detailed ground-truths, reaching 0.90 Spearman correlation with VLM Arena Elo ratings on description tasks. FEEDQUILL collects scalable preference data by generating multiple candidate responses via nucleus sampling, decomposing each into verifiable primitive information units, and scoring them with an ensemble of open-source VLMs; two separate reward models are trained — one on precision (fraction of correct units, c_p) and one on richness (number of units, c_r) — and the policy is optimized via PPO with the summed reward r = c_p + 0.5·c_r, reducing hallucinations by 40.5% relative points on mmHal-V while surpassing GPT-4o on detailed captioning.

## Results

FEEDQUILL-7B achieves state-of-the-art detailed image captioning performance, scoring 55.65 on DECAPBENCH (surpassing GPT-4o's 53.44 by 2.21 points) and 100.5 on LLaVA-W (exceeding GPT-4V's 98.0). The proposed DCSCORE metric achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.6605 with human expert judgments, outperforming the next-best metric FLEUR (0.4230) by 0.2375. Across comprehensive benchmarks, FEEDQUILL-7B scores 80.5 on MMBench, 62.4 on MMStar, and 59.3 on MMVet, while reducing hallucinations (mmHal-V) from 2.94 to 3.10 relative to its LLaVA-Onevision-7B base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEEDQUILL-7B surpasses GPT-4o on DECAPBENCH (55.65 vs. 53.44, +2.21) and GPT-4V on LLaVA-W (100.5 vs. 98.0, +2.5), establishing new state-of-the-art in detailed captioning and visual chatting respectively. Among open-source models, FEEDQUILL-7B outperforms InternVL-2-26B (49.59), Claude-3.5-Sonnet (52.37), and all LLaVA-series baselines on DECAPBENCH, with the largest gain over its own base model LLaVA-Onevision-7B (+12.16). On hallucination metrics, FEEDQUILL-7B reduces CHAIR_S from 47.8 to 5.1 and CHAIR_I from 25.3 to 2.6 compared to LLaVA-1.5-7B, and improves mmHal-V from 2.94 to 3.10 over LLaVA-Onevision-7B. However, FEEDQUILL-7B trails GPT-4o on broader reasoning benchmarks such as MMMU (47.9 vs. 69.1), MMVet (59.3 vs. 76.2), and WildVision (59.60 vs. 89.41), reflecting its training focus on captioning rather than general multi-modal reasoning. The DCSCORE metric itself outperforms all 12 compared evaluation metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, SPICE, CLIP-Score, PAC-Score, CAPTURE, CLAIR, FLEUR, GPT4-Eval, FaithScore, RLAIF-V) in human-judgment correlation, with PCC of 0.6605 versus the runner-up FLEUR at 0.4230.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the step-by-step verification paradigm from Lightman et al. (2023), adapting the concept of decomposing responses into independently verifiable units to the vision-language captioning domain. It positions itself against two distinct lineages: (1) image captioning evaluation metrics (BLEU, CIDEr, SPICE, CLIPScore, CLAIR, FLEUR, etc.), which the authors argue are unreliable for modern detailed captions, and (2) preference optimization methods for VLMs (RLHF-V, RLAIF-V, HA-DPO, POVID, CSR, STIC), which rely on human labeling or heuristic perturbation to construct preference pairs. The key conceptual departure is unifying evaluation (DCSCORE) and preference learning (FEEDQUILL) under a single primitive-information-unit decomposition framework, so that the same decomposition-and-verification mechanism serves both as a scoring metric and as an automatic feedback signal for PPO-based alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DECAPBENCH and DCSCORE address a genuine gap: as VLMs produce increasingly detailed captions, the field lacked a reliable evaluation framework that correlates with human judgment, and this paper demonstrates that fine-grained unit-level verification substantially closes that gap (0.66 PCC vs. 0.42 for the best prior metric). The finding that a 7B model trained with FEEDQUILL surpasses GPT-4o on detailed captioning while also reducing hallucinations by 40.5% relative points suggests that targeted preference optimization on captioning can yield specialized capabilities without degrading general VLM performance. Open questions include the scalability and cost of the GPT-4o-dependent verification step in DCSCORE, whether the primitive-unit decomposition generalizes to non-captioning tasks (e.g., visual reasoning or multi-turn dialogue), and whether the approach remains effective as open-source VLMs close the gap with proprietary models.
