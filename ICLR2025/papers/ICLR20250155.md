## Problem

Unlike text, audio data does not inherently contain the instructions used to generate it, making it difficult for audio models to learn free-form instruction following through unsupervised next-token prediction. Additionally, compositional abilities—combining, interpolating between, or negating instructions—are generally unattainable through data alone, as negation pairs and instruction compositions rarely exist in natural datasets.

## Input / Output

The model accepts free-form or template-based text instructions (encoded by a frozen byT5 tokenizer-free language model) along with optional audio contexts (speech prompts, audio events, MIDI tracks) appended via 'given context:<audio>' markers. Audio is represented as a 22 kHz, 80-bin mel-spectrogram scaled to approximately zero mean and unit variance, subsumed by a shallow 3-layer learnable transformer encoder. The model supports both absolute instructions ('synthesize a happy voice') and relative instructions ('increase the happiness of this voice'), enabling dynamic transformation tasks alongside generation.

## Method

Fugatto is a T5-based Transformer with Adaptive Layer Norm and kernel-size-3 convolutions replacing MLPs, trained with the Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM) objective. The training follows a curriculum: starting with template-based instructions on a task subset, then transitioning to an equal mixture of template-based and free-form instructions with multinomial oversampling (β=0.25) adjusted by validation scores. The key dataset contribution is a five-pillar generation strategy—LLM-generated instruction scripts, absolute/relative instruction creation, synthetic captions from audio understanding models, dataset transmutation to uncover implicit relationships, and audio processing tools (Praat, Pedalboard) for synthetic paired data—yielding 20M+ rows (~50,000 hours). The core inference contribution, ComposableART, extends classifier-free guidance to a weighted combination of vector fields across multiple instructions, mel-frame indices, and even distinct models (Equation 2), enabling attribute composition, task composition, model composition, temporal composition, and negation without retraining.

## Results

On the MusicCaps text-to-audio benchmark, Fugatto (γ=1) achieves the best scores across all three metrics: FD 11.52, FAD 1.43, and IS 2.73, outperforming the next-best specialist (Tango-AF&AC-FT-MC: FAD 1.99) and all generalist models. On AudioCaps, Fugatto (γ=1) achieves FAD 1.36 and IS 9.72, beating generalist baselines NExT-GPT (FAD 1.68) and UniAudio (FAD 3.12) but trailing the specialist AudioBox (FAD 1.10). On zero-shot TTS (LibriSpeech Test Clean), Fugatto (γ=3) reaches WER 2.44 and speaker similarity SIM-r 0.62, competitive with UniAudio (WER 2.00, SIM-r 0.71) but below the specialist Natural Speech3 (WER 1.81, SIM-r 0.76). On speech denoising (DNS), Fugatto achieves PESQ-WB 2.77 and STOI 95.70, close to but below the specialist CleanUNet 2 (PESQ-WB 3.26, STOI 97.70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fugatto clearly outperforms all generalist baselines on text-to-audio: on MusicCaps it beats UniAudio (FAD 1.43 vs 3.65) and on AudioCaps it beats both NExT-GPT (FAD 1.36 vs 1.68) and UniAudio (FAD 1.36 vs 3.12). Against specialist models, Fugatto wins on MusicCaps (FAD 1.43 vs Tango's 1.99 and AudioLDM-2's 2.74) and on AudioCaps FAD vs VoiceLDM (1.36 vs 2.50), but loses to AudioBox on AudioCaps (FAD 1.36 vs 1.10, FD 16.73 vs 10.14). On zero-shot TTS, Fugatto (γ=3, WER 2.44, SIM-r 0.62) beats Vall-E (WER 5.90, SIM-r 0.58) and AudioBox (WER 4.80) but trails Natural Speech3 (WER 1.81, SIM-r 0.76) and UniAudio (WER 2.00, SIM-r 0.71). On speech denoising, Fugatto (PESQ-WB 2.77, STOI 95.70) is competitive but below CleanUNet 2 (PESQ-WB 3.26, STOI 97.70). On bandwidth extension (VCTK), Fugatto (LSD-4kHz 1.29) is close to AudioSR (1.25) but slightly worse, and on LSD-8kHz Fugatto (1.25) trails AudioSR (1.08) and NuWave (0.88).

## Relation to Prior Work

Fugatto follows the LLM-inspired generalist paradigm (large-scale data, compute, and a unified training objective) applied to audio, building on the OT-CFM flow-matching objective (Lipman et al., 2022; Tong et al., 2023) and the T5-based transformer architecture with Adaptive Layer Norm. Its key methodological departure is ComposableART, which extends classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) and composable diffusion guidance (Liu et al., 2023; Nie et al., 2021) from single-attribute composition to a generalized framework spanning multiple instructions, temporal frames, and even distinct models (following Karras et al., 2024's velocity-based model composition). Unlike prior audio models (AudioBox, NExT-GPT, UniAudio) that rely on autoregressive or diffusion objectives with rigid task-specific conditioning, Fugatto uses a frozen byT5 text encoder, a shallow mel-spectrogram encoder, and LLM-generated free-form instructions to support a much broader and more flexible task space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fugatto establishes a practical blueprint for building generalist audio foundation models, demonstrating that a single flow-matching transformer can handle TTS, TTA, speech enhancement, source separation, MIDI-to-audio, and compositional generation with competitive or superior metrics to specialist models. ComposableART offers a training-free, inference-time mechanism for composition, interpolation, negation, and temporal control that is broadly applicable to any score-based or flow-matching audio model, potentially replacing the need for task-specific heads or external classifiers. Key open questions remain: whether the mel-spectrogram representation (lacking stereo and low-frequency fidelity) limits ceiling performance, how data mixing and instruction diversity drive emergent capabilities at scale, and how to stabilize and automate the weight selection in ComposableART. The denoising gap versus specialist models and the qualitative-only evaluation of emergent sounds and compositional outputs are notable limitations.
