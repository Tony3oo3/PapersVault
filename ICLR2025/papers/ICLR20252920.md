## Problem

Existing hallucination detection methods for LLMs rely on single-aspect indicators—such as prediction uncertainty, self-consistency, or internal-state eigenvalues—confined to either the output space or the internal representation space, which limits detection accuracy and generalizability. Moreover, the majority of these methods are purely diagnostic and do not provide a mechanism for mitigating hallucinations or calibrating the model's probability distribution, restricting their practical utility in generation tasks.

## Input / Output

The D3T network consumes a vector of six prediction characteristics extracted per token step (max/min/mean probability, entropy-based uncertainty, Top-1 and Top-K margins, pairwise cosine consistency across B=3 sampled responses, and logits norm), while the MLP consumes the token hidden states from the LLM's final layer. The framework outputs a calibrated token probability distribution (Eq. 3) in which the D3T's hallucination probability scales the magnitude of per-token logit adjustments derived from the MLP, and a binary hallucination classification score from the D3T leaf-node classifier. No external hallucination labels are required; training uses only the original LLM cross-entropy loss.

## Method

HADEMIF couples two compact networks—totaling less than 2% of the LLM's parameters—with a logit-calibration step. The D3T (Deep Dynamic Decision Tree) replaces the fixed N-ary structure of DNDT by learning per-feature cut-point counts via a sigmoid-gated ceiling (with straight-through gradients), then applies soft binning (linear layer + softmax) followed by a Kronecker product to route each input to a leaf node, preserving interpretability while reducing redundant branches. A parallel MLP maps token hidden states to a per-vocabulary adjustment vector H_l^s. Calibration modifies each logit as u_l^(v) − H_l^o · log(H_l,v^s), where H_l^o is the D3T's hallucination probability, so that tokens predicted as hallucinated receive stronger probability suppression. For fine-tuning, an alternating optimization loop updates the LLM (via LoRA, rank 8) and the two detection networks in separate gradient steps, all driven by the standard language-modeling loss without any hallucination annotations.

## Results

On the CAT benchmark with Llama2-7B, HADEMIF reduces the average ECE by 51% (from 0.118 to 0.058) and the Brier score by 14% (from 0.206 to 0.178) for phrase- and sentence-level tasks, while improving average acc@50 from 0.451 to 0.541. For paragraph-level tasks, ECE drops by 10% (0.107→0.096) and Brier by 6% (0.221→0.208). The framework introduces less than 2% additional parameters relative to the LLM and is evaluated across eight open-source models ranging from 1.5B to 30B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against model calibration baselines, HADEMIF (with fine-tuning) achieves the lowest average ECE (0.058) and Brier (0.178) while attaining the highest acc@50 (0.541), outperforming LITCAB+Temp Scaling (ECE 0.084, Brier 0.196) and Calibration-Tuning (ECE 0.075, Brier 0.189). Label Smoothing and Temperature Scaling perform poorly, with Label Smoothing often degrading below the original LLM (e.g., acc@50 drops from 0.288 to 0.208 on NQ). Among hallucination-specific methods, Self-Consistency is the strongest baseline (avg ECE 0.462) but is far outperformed by HADEMIF (0.058), a 54% reduction; R-Tuning (avg ECE 0.125) is the best fine-tuning baseline but produces only binary confidence scores, limiting its calibration quality. P(IK) and Verbalization perform worst among hallucination detection baselines (avg ECE 0.225 and 0.462 respectively), confirming that raw LLM self-assessment is insufficient without structured modeling. HADEMIF's advantage is most pronounced on sentence-level tasks (TruthfulQA ECE: 0.058 vs. 0.060 for R-Tuning) and on coverage metrics (cov@40 on TruthfulQA: 0.510 vs. 0.500 for R-Tuning), demonstrating fine-grained calibration that single-score methods cannot achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep Neural Decision Tree (DNDT) framework by Yang et al. (2018) into a dynamic variant (D3T) that learns per-feature cut points during training, replacing the fixed N-ary tree structure. It departs from prior hallucination detection work that relies on single-aspect metrics (uncertainty, self-consistency, or internal-state eigenvalues) confined to either the output or internal space, by jointly modeling both spaces through a compact D3T (on prediction characteristics) and an MLP (on token hidden states). The calibration mechanism, which uses the D3T's hallucination probability to modulate logit adjustment via the MLP's per-token scores, unifies detection and mitigation in a single framework, going beyond prior methods that address only one of these objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's <2% parameter overhead and applicability in both inference and fine-tuning phases make it a practical drop-in for deployed LLMs in reliability-critical domains (legal, financial, medical). The D3T's interpretability provides actionable insight into which prediction characteristics drive hallucination, with the finding that consistency outweighs uncertainty being a useful design signal for future detectors. Key limitations include the white-box requirement (inaccessible hidden states for API-only models) and the computational cost of computing the consistency metric across multiple sampled responses, which the authors flag as a target for future efficiency improvements. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multimodal or reasoning-heavy settings and whether additional metrics (loss gradients, prediction robustness) can further sharpen detection.
