## Problem

Hard-label adversarial attacks, where only top-1 predictions are available, require solving a high-dimensional combinatorial optimization problem. Ray-search methods (OPT, Sign-OPT) reformulate this as continuous optimization over ray directions, but Sign-OPT's sign-based gradient estimation sacrifices accuracy while OPT's finite-difference approach incurs prohibitive query costs. The key gap is how to incorporate transfer-based priors from surrogate models into gradient estimation under the hard-label constraint, where evaluating the objective function g(θ) requires expensive binary search and existing prior-guided methods (e.g., PRGF) only apply to score-based attacks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a benign RGB image (32×32×3 for CIFAR-10; 224×224×3 or 299×299×3 for ImageNet) and interacts with the target model exclusively through hard-label queries (top-1 predictions only, no logits). It additionally requires one or more surrogate models (e.g., ResNet-50, ConViT, SENet-154) whose white-box access is used offline to compute transfer-based gradient priors via the C&W loss at the surrogate's decision boundary. The output is an adversarial example x_adv with minimum l_p norm perturbation (p ∈ {2, ∞}) that causes the target model to output an incorrect (or specified target) label.

## Method

The approach minimizes g(θ), the shortest l_p distance along ray direction θ from the benign image to the adversarial region, using zeroth-order optimization with prior-guided gradient estimators. Transfer-based priors are obtained by computing ∇_θ h(θ, λ₀) on each surrogate model, where h is the negative C&W loss evaluated at the surrogate's decision boundary λ₀, which is provably proportional to ∇g_f(θ). Two estimators are proposed: Prior-Sign-OPT applies sign-based directional derivative estimation uniformly to both priors and random orthonormal vectors (q total queries), while Prior-OPT uses finite-difference (via binary search) for the s prior directions but aggregates the q−s random directions into a single sign-based vector v_⊥, treating priors and random vectors asymmetrically to balance accuracy and query cost. Both estimators approximate the projection of the true gradient onto the subspace spanned by the orthonormalized priors and random vectors (via Gram-Schmidt), and the paper derives closed-form expressions for the expected cosine similarity between each estimator and the true gradient, proving that Prior-OPT outperforms Sign-OPT when the priors' alignment with the true gradient exceeds a threshold of approximately 2s/(πd).

## Results

On ImageNet, Prior-OPT with two surrogate models (IncResV2 & Xception) achieves a mean ℓ₂ distortion of 3.119 at 10K queries for untargeted attacks on Inception-v4, compared to 8.786 for Sign-OPT and 7.115 for the best SQBA variant. For targeted attacks on Inception-v4 at 20K queries, Prior-Sign-OPT with two surrogates reaches 10.769 distortion versus 16.281 for Sign-OPT and 19.522 for HSJA. On ViT, Prior-OPT with two surrogates (ResNet50 & ConViT) achieves 3.609 at 10K queries (untargeted), outperforming Sign-OPT (4.572) and SQBA_ConViT (4.452). The paper also demonstrates consistent gains against adversarially trained (AT) and TRADES defense models on both ImageNet and CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prior-OPT with two surrogate models substantially outperforms Sign-OPT (the direct baseline it extends) across all target models: on Inception-v4 untargeted @10K, distortion drops from 8.786 to 3.119 (a 64% reduction), and on ViT untargeted @10K from 4.572 to 3.609. For targeted attacks, Prior-Sign-OPT with two surrogates achieves the best results, beating Sign-OPT by 34% on Inception-v4 (10.769 vs 16.281 at 20K) and by 9% on ViT (7.544 vs 8.267). Against boundary-search methods, Prior-OPT also dominates: HSJA achieves 11.645 on Inception-v4 untargeted @10K versus 3.119 for Prior-OPT, and TA/G-TA score 11.694/11.448 respectively. The transfer-based baselines SQBA and BBA, which also use surrogate models, are outperformed: SQBA_Xception reaches 7.115 on Inception-v4 untargeted @10K while Prior-OPT achieves 3.119, and BBA_Xception scores 15.282 versus 3.119. The paper notes that Prior-OPT excels in early iterations (lower query budgets) while Prior-Sign-OPT catches up and surpasses it at higher query budgets for targeted attacks, suggesting complementary strengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ray-search framework of OPT (Cheng et al., 2019) and its sign-based variant Sign-OPT (Cheng et al., 2020) by incorporating transfer-based priors from surrogate models into the gradient estimation step. The subspace projection estimator theory from score-based zeroth-order optimization (Meier et al., 2019; Cheng et al., 2021) is adapted to the hard-label setting, where the objective g(θ) can only be evaluated via costly binary search rather than direct function queries. Unlike prior methods that combine transfer and decision-based attacks (BBA, CISA, SQBA), which set prior weights empirically, this work derives theoretically optimal coefficients through Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization and provides the first expected cosine similarity analysis for the Sign-OPT family of estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical analysis establishing that even a single moderately-aligned prior (|α| > ~0.014 for d=3072, q=200) can outperform Sign-OPT provides a principled understanding of when and why transfer-based information helps in hard-label attacks, filling a gap where prior methods lacked convergence or accuracy guarantees. The approach naturally scales to multiple surrogate models and addresses the practical challenge of weighting priors that may deviate significantly from the true gradient. Open questions include the difficulty of targeted attacks (requiring a new target class in the surrogate), the assumption that g(θ) is locally differentiable, and whether the gains persist under stronger defenses or in truly model-agnostic settings without access to any surrogate.
