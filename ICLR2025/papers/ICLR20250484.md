## Problem

Existing certified robust training methods are designed for deterministic DNNs and cannot handle the stochastic outputs of VAEs, where the worst-case loss must be computed over a potentially infinite set of output distributions. The few prior works on certifiably robust VAE training (e.g., Barrett et al., 2022) impose strict architectural constraints such as fixed latent-layer variance and Lipschitz continuity, limiting their applicability. This paper asks whether one can train general VAEs with provable robustness guarantees without such constraints.

## Input / Output

For the wireless task, the input is a vector of uplink channel measurements (10,000 samples collected in a 10m×7m environment) and the output is a downlink channel estimate evaluated by SNR; for vision, inputs are 28×28 MNIST or 32×32 CIFAR-10 images and outputs are reconstructions evaluated by MSE. In both settings, the encoder maps inputs to per-dimension Gaussian parameters (μ, σ) in a latent space (50-dim for wireless, 32/64-dim for vision), and the decoder is deterministic given a latent point. The method additionally produces a certified worst-case error bound at a user-specified probability threshold (1−δ), with the input region defined by an L∞ perturbation budget ε.

## Method

CIVET's key insight is that the worst-case VAE error over all reachable output distributions can be bounded in two steps: (a) selecting a support set S in the latent space that captures at least (1−δ) probability mass for every distribution reachable by the encoder, and (b) bounding the deterministic decoder's worst-case error over S via standard IBP. The support set is restricted to multidimensional boxes whose bounds are computed in closed form (via binary search on the inverse Gaussian CDF) from the IBP-propagated intervals [μ_lb, μ_ub] and σ_ub, making the entire pipeline differentiable with respect to both encoder and decoder parameters. The CIVET loss weights multiple support sets at different probability thresholds (e.g., δ ∈ {0.35, 0.2, 0.05}) to approximate the expected worst-case error without committing to a single target probability. This reduces certified VAE training to a sequence of deterministic IBP passes through the decoder, requiring no Lipschitz constraints or fixed-variance assumptions on the architecture.

## Results

On the FIRE wireless dataset at ε=25%, CIVET achieves a certified SNR of 12.88 dB, dramatically outperforming standard training (−2.35 dB) and PGD adversarial training (3.17 dB), while retaining a baseline SNR of 15.82 dB (vs. 17.79 dB for standard). On MNIST at ε=0.1, CIVET achieves a certified MSE of 0.0089 versus 0.1426 for standard training and 0.0648 for PGD. On CIFAR-10 at ε=8/255, CIVET achieves a certified MSE of 0.0153 versus 0.2098 for standard and 0.0760 for PGD, with a baseline MSE of 0.0062 (vs. 0.0041 standard). Across all datasets and perturbation levels, CIVET consistently provides the tightest certified bounds while incurring only a small degradation in clean (non-adversarial) performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Lipschitz-constrained VAEs of Barrett et al. (2022), CIVET wins on nearly all metrics: on CIFAR-10 at ε=8/255, CIVET achieves a certified MSE of 0.0153 versus 0.0267 for Lipschitz VAEs (a 43% improvement), and a baseline MSE of 0.0062 versus 0.0112 (a 45% improvement). On CIFAR-10 at ε=2/255, CIVET more than halves the certified MSE (0.0055 vs. 0.0105) and baseline MSE (0.0049 vs. 0.0083). The single exception is MNIST (fully connected) at ε=0.3, where Lipschitz VAEs achieve a slightly better certified MSE (0.0486 vs. 0.0507), though CIVET still dominates on baseline (0.0043 vs. 0.0064) and LSA (0.0412 vs. 0.0366 is actually Lipschitz winning on LSA here). Compared to standard and PGD adversarial training, CIVET achieves 10–15× lower certified MSE on vision tasks and 4–6× higher certified SNR on the wireless task, at the cost of a small (typically <10%) degradation in clean accuracy. Unlike Lipschitz VAEs, CIVET imposes no architectural constraints (no fixed variance, no Lipschitz constant, no GroupSort activations) and generalizes to convolutional architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

CIVET extends the certified robust training paradigm from deterministic DNNs (where IBP-based bounding is well-established, e.g., Mirman et al. 2018) to stochastic VAEs by introducing a support-set reduction that converts the infinite-distribution worst-case problem into a finite deterministic decoder bounding problem. It departs from the prior certified VAE work of Barrett et al. (2022), which required Lipschitz-constrained architectures with fixed latent variance and GroupSort activations, by removing all such architectural restrictions. The probabilistic specification framework of Berrada et al. (2021) provides the formal worst-case error definition that CIVET's training objective optimizes, while the IBP machinery handles the deterministic bounding sub-problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general-purpose certified training method for VAEs without architectural constraints, enabling provable robustness guarantees for VAEs deployed in safety-critical wireless and vision systems where prior methods were inapplicable. Key limitations include a ~6× training runtime overhead (296 min vs. 45 min on CIFAR-10), the assumption of Gaussian latent distributions, and the restriction of support sets to axis-aligned boxes, which may be overly conservative in high dimensions. Open questions include whether the support-set idea extends to non-Gaussian or correlated latent spaces, how it scales to deeper/wider VAEs, and whether combining with techniques like SABR can close the remaining gap to adversarial training performance.
