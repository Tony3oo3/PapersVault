## Problem

Existing neural multi-objective combinatorial optimization (MOCO) methods represent problem instances solely as graphs, which limits their ability to capture spatial structure and leads to a persistent optimality gap. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a complementary image modality derived from the same instance, arguing that graph-only encoding is insufficient for comprehensive representation learning in neural MOCO solvers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251216/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a MOCO problem instance in two forms: a standard graph (nodes with z-dimensional feature vectors) and a constructed instance image whose format is problem-specific (e.g., an M-channel binary image for M-objective TSP where node coordinates become black pixels, or a single-channel grayscale image for CVRP/KP where demand/weight values become pixel intensities). A problem-size adaptive resolution (PSAR) strategy sets the image resolution as W=H=⌈10√n/w⌉×w (with w=h=16) to maintain a stable node density of ~0.01 across sizes, enabling out-of-distribution generalization. The model outputs a Pareto-optimal solution set by decomposing the MOCO problem into N scalarized subproblems (via Das-Dennis weight vectors) and autoregressively constructing a solution sequence for each subproblem through a decoder that computes node-selection probabilities.

## Method

The GIMF framework integrates a graph Transformer encoder and a Vision Transformer encoder in a shared encoder-decoder architecture (L=6 total layers). The first L−L′=3 layers process graph node embeddings and image patch embeddings independently via standard multi-head self-attention with instance normalization. The final L′=3 multimodal fusion layers introduce modality-specific bottleneck tokens (n_b=n_b′=10, far fewer than n or n′) that mediate cross-modal interaction through symmetric graph-guided and image-guided multi-head cross-attention, condensing essential information while avoiding the O((n+n′)²) cost of full joint self-attention. Scalable positional encodings via an MLP map normalized patch coordinates to position embeddings, handling variable patch-sequence lengths across problem sizes. The framework is deployed as a plug-in onto two state-of-the-art neural MOCO backbones—CNH (yielding GIMF-C with dual-attention for weight vectors) and PMOCO (yielding GIMF-P with a hypernetwork)—and trained end-to-end with REINFORCE using a POMO-style baseline over multiple optima.

## Results

On Bi-TSP100, GIMF-C achieves HV 0.7040 (gap 0.35% vs. GIMF-C-Aug), outperforming CNH (0.7019, gap 0.65%) and all other neural baselines. On Tri-TSP100, GIMF-C reaches HV 0.4971 (gap 1.00%), a 0.81% improvement over CNH (0.4931, gap 1.79%). With instance augmentation, GIMF-C-Aug attains the best HV across all four problems (e.g., 0.7065 on Bi-TSP100, 0.5021 on Tri-TSP100) while requiring only 21 minutes vs. 6.0 hours for the strong WS-LKH heuristic. On out-of-distribution Bi-TSP200, GIMF-C achieves HV 0.7325 (gap 0.50%), surpassing CNH (0.7292, gap 0.95%) and all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIMF-C consistently outperforms the state-of-the-art CNH across all four problems and all sizes, with the largest gap on Tri-TSP100 (HV 0.4971 vs. 0.4931, a 0.81% improvement). GIMF-P similarly surpasses PMOCO in every setting, and notably GIMF-P even beats PMOCO-Aug on Bi-CVRP100 (gap 0.69% vs. 2.70%). Against the multi-model baselines (DRL-MOA, MDRL, EMNH), GIMF-C maintains superiority on Bi-TSP and Tri-TSP (e.g., 1.00% vs. 3.09% gap on Tri-TSP100) but shows slight underperformance relative to EMNH on Bi-CVRP and Bi-KP. Compared to conventional MOEAs (MOEA/D, NSGA-II, MOGLS, PPLS/D-C), GIMF-C achieves substantially higher HV with orders-of-magnitude less computation time (e.g., 33 s vs. 2.2 h for MOEA/D on Bi-TSP100). The strong problem-specific heuristic WS-LKH still achieves marginally better HV on Bi-TSP100 (0.7090 vs. 0.7040) but requires 6.0 hours vs. 33 seconds, and GIMF-C-Aug closes this gap entirely. On out-of-distribution Bi-TSP200, GIMF-C (0.7325) outperforms all neural baselines and MOEAs, with only WS-LKH (0.7490) achieving a higher HV at 22 hours of computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decomposition-based neural MOCO paradigm (CNH, PMOCO, POMO) by introducing a complementary image modality alongside the standard graph representation. It builds on the Graph Transformer architecture (Kool et al., 2019) for node-level encoding and the Vision Transformer (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) for spatial structure capture, then bridges them via a multimodal fusion mechanism inspired by attention bottlenecks (Nagrani et al., 2021) but modified with modality-specific bottlenecks. The key departure from prior neural MOCO work is the explicit construction of instance images and the PSAR strategy to maintain stable node density across problem sizes, addressing a generalization gap that purely graph-based methods cannot resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GIMF framework provides a generic, plug-in enhancement for existing neural MOCO backbones, consistently closing the optimality gap between learned and heuristic methods across TSP, CVRP, and knapsack problems. The PSAR ablation reveals that without adaptive resolution, the multimodal approach actually degrades out-of-distribution performance below the graph-only baseline, highlighting a non-trivial design constraint for cross-size generalization. Open questions remain about applicability to MOCO problems where meaningful image construction is difficult, the computational overhead of the additional modality, and whether the approach extends to real-world, non-synthetic instances.
