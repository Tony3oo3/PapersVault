## Problem

Existing diffusion-based control methods for complex physical systems rely on synchronous denoising across all physical time steps within a model horizon, creating a fundamental tension: full sampling at every step is computationally prohibitive, while replanning every h steps breaks the closed-loop requirement and degrades performance. This paper addresses how to achieve true closed-loop diffusion control with significantly reduced per-step sampling cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253580/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial spatial-field state (e.g., 128-cell 1D velocity profile or 64×64 2D velocity/smoke field), a scalar control objective J, and the system dynamics G (a PDE solver). At each of N physical time steps, it consumes the real-time measured state u_env,τ as a conditioning input and outputs a single spatial-field control signal w_τ (e.g., an external force field). The model operates over a short horizon H=16 (1D) or H=15 (2D) with T=900 or T=600 diffusion steps, but only T/H=60 or T/H=40 denoising steps are needed per physical step at inference.

## Method

CL-DiffPhyCon introduces an asynchronous denoising framework where different physical time steps within the horizon carry different noise levels (earlier steps are less noisy), formalized via a decomposition theorem (Theorem 1) of an augmented joint distribution into an initializing distribution and per-step transition distributions. Two U-Net diffusion models are trained: a synchronous model ε_φ (standard DDPM loss) that produces the initial noisy horizon, and an asynchronous model ε_θ (component-wise noise prediction loss) that denoises the horizon from T/H to 0, where each component i is at noise level t + i·T/H. At inference, only the first (least noisy) component is extracted as the control signal, fed to the PDE dynamics, and the real feedback state conditions the next step — achieving H/h speedup over DiffPhyCon-h while maintaining closed-loop adaptation. The approach further composes with DDIM for an additional ~5× speedup, and guided sampling via the control objective J is incorporated through an extra gradient term in the reverse SDE.

## Results

On the 1D Burgers' equation control task, CL-DiffPhyCon achieves the best control objective J across all six settings (noise-free, physical constraint, system noise, measurement noise, full/partial observation with partial control), reducing J by 48.6–57.2% compared to the strongest diffusion baseline (DiffPhyCon-1). On the 2D incompressible fluid control task, CL-DiffPhyCon outperforms all baselines in both large-domain and boundary control under fixed and random map evaluation. In terms of efficiency, CL-DiffPhyCon reduces average inference time to 4.55 s (vs. 49.23 s for DiffPhyCon-1 and 6.91 s for RDM on 1D), and combining with DDIM (30 steps) further accelerates inference to 0.83 s on 1D and 26.01 s on 2D, approaching the speed of non-diffusion baselines while retaining superior control performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CL-DiffPhyCon decisively outperforms all five baseline families. Against Behaviour Cloning (BC) and BPPO, which rely on suboptimal training trajectories, CL-DiffPhyCon reduces J by roughly 4–5× (e.g., 0.0096 vs. 0.4708 noise-free on 1D), confirming that diffusion-based global optimization over the horizon is far superior to imitation or behaviour-based RL. Against PID, CL-DiffPhyCon achieves 34× lower J in the noise-free 1D setting (0.0096 vs. 0.3250) and is the only method applicable to the multi-actuator partial-control settings. Against DiffPhyCon (its open-loop predecessor), CL-DiffPhyCon reduces J by 48.6–57.2% while being 10.8× faster than DiffPhyCon-1 (4.55 s vs. 49.23 s) thanks to the asynchronous denoising framework; with DDIM the speedup reaches ~60×. Against RDM (adaptive replanning), CL-DiffPhyCon achieves lower J in every setting (e.g., 0.0096 vs. 0.0296 noise-free) and is ~1.5× faster on 1D (4.55 s vs. 6.91 s) and ~1.7× faster on 2D (144 s vs. 238 s), without requiring thresholding hyperparameters or extra likelihood-estimation computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DiffPhyCon (Wei et al., 2024), which applies diffusion models to open-loop PDE control, by introducing an asynchronous denoising framework that decouples noise levels across physical time steps, enabling true closed-loop control with real-time state feedback. The theoretical derivation (Theorem 1) is grounded in the score-based SDE framework (Song et al., 2021), and the method is distinguished from adaptive replanning approaches like RDM (Zhou et al., 2024) by requiring no extra thresholding hyperparameters or likelihood estimation. While rolling diffusion models (Ruhe et al., 2024) and AR-diffusion (Wu et al., 2023) also assign varying noise levels across frames, those target sequential content generation without external interaction, whereas CL-DiffPhyCon's asynchronous structure is derived from the target control distribution rather than being heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental tension in diffusion-based control: closed-loop operation demands per-step feedback, yet synchronous denoising over the full horizon makes this prohibitively expensive. The asynchronous framework provides a theoretically grounded (not heuristic) solution that simultaneously achieves closed-loop correctness and H/h-fold speedup, with further gains from DDIM. Key limitations include offline-only training (no online adaptation), lack of formal performance bounds under guidance sampling, and evaluation on only two PDE tasks. The authors note potential extension to robot and drone control, suggesting the asynchronous denoising principle could generalize beyond PDE-constrained settings.
