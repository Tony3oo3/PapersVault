## Problem

Inferring the internal topology of an opaque object from external visual observations is a long-standing challenge: traditional methods require intrusive sensors or expensive CT/MRI equipment, while recent 3D vision approaches (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) reconstruct only visually plausible surfaces with no physically meaningful interior. Mesh-based topology optimization, though applicable, demands tedious and error-prone mesh extraction and filling, and produces rough boundaries unsuitable for manufacturing. No prior pipeline combines non-intrusive visual input with a mesh-free, physically grounded interior reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes multi-view photos of the object's exterior and a short video of its dynamic motion (e.g., oscillation period, balancing pose, bouncing trajectory) as the sole input; no ground-truth interior geometry or CT scan is required. The output is a volumetric point cloud where each particle carries a physical parameter (density or Young's modulus) determined by the inferred topology, yielding a mesh-free, 3D-printable representation. Because the method is particle-based end-to-end, the result avoids the mesh-fixing step entirely and produces smoother surfaces than mesh baselines, as validated by a 3D-printed pear prototype.

## Method

TopoGaussian is a four-stage, fully particle-based pipeline: (1) Gaussian Splatting reconstructs the exterior surface point cloud from multi-view images; (2) interior points are filled to form a volumetric point cloud; (3) a topology representation—chosen from per-particle indicator variables, a neural implicit SDF, or a 10-parameter quadratic surface—assigns physical attributes to each particle; (4) a novel particle-based differentiable simulator (supporting rigid Newton-Euler dynamics, soft Saint Venant-Kirchhoff constitutive models via corotated SPH, pneumatic actuation through volume-energy forces, and penalty-based collision) is backpropagated via the adjoint method to compute gradients with respect to topology parameters. Gradient-based optimizers (L-BFGS-B, Adam) then minimize an L2 loss between simulated and reference motion, iteratively adjusting the topology until the inferred interior reproduces the observed dynamics. The key architectural insight is that all stages—reconstruction, representation, simulation, and optimization—operate exclusively on particles, eliminating the mesh bottleneck that plagues prior mesh-based topology optimization pipelines.

## Results

On reconstruction quality (Laplacian of surface normal, lower is better), TopoGaussian achieves an average of 0.314 across six rigid-body examples, compared to 0.749 for PGSR (voxel=0.05), 0.790 for PGSR (voxel=0.2), and 0.964 for Gaussian Surfels (excluding one failure). In terms of volumetric shape generation time, TopoGaussian is 5.26× faster than PGSR (voxel=0.05), 4.81× faster than PGSR (voxel=0.2), and 1.28× faster than Gaussian Surfels on average. The quality advantage translates to 2.33×, 2.50×, and 2.55× smoother surfaces over the three baselines respectively, which the authors validate by 3D-printing the pear example.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two mesh-based Gaussian Splatting SOTA methods: PGSR (Chen et al., 2024) and Gaussian Surfels (Dai et al., 2024), both adapted by replacing their topology representation with the output triangle mesh and chaining into the same simulation-optimization pipeline. TopoGaussian wins decisively on both speed and quality: it is 5.26× and 4.81× faster than PGSR at two voxel resolutions, and 1.28× faster than Gaussian Surfels, while producing 2.33×–2.55× smoother (lower Laplacian) surfaces. Gaussian Surfels additionally fails to produce a visually correct result on the cow example (and on fish-1 in the extended Table 3), whereas TopoGaussian succeeds on all 19 rigid-body examples. The speed advantage stems from avoiding expensive mesh extraction and fixing, and the quality advantage comes from the particle-based representation naturally yielding smoother, 3D-print-friendly boundaries without the artifacts introduced by mesh discretization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: 3D scene reconstruction via Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2023), differentiable simulation for gradient-based design (Du et al., 2021; Geilinger et al., 2020), and topology optimization from structural engineering (Sigmund, 2001; Li et al., 2021). The key departure from prior work is the insistence on a fully particle-based pipeline: while existing differentiable simulators rely on mesh or finite-element discretization (requiring error-prone mesh extraction from Gaussian Splatting output), TopoGaussian builds a particle-native simulator supporting constitutive models, pneumatic actuation, and collision without any mesh. For topology representation, it unifies three options—per-particle density, neural implicit SDF (Park et al., 2019), and quadratic surfaces (Hafner et al., 2024)—within a single optimization framework, whereas prior topology optimization works typically commit to one representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline demonstrates that non-intrusive visual inputs (photos and video) can recover physically meaningful interior structures without CT/MRI, opening applications in reverse engineering, soft robotics design, and 3D-printable manufacturing. The 5.26× speedup and 2.3–2.6× quality improvement over mesh-based baselines, combined with 3D-printable output, make the approach practically relevant. Key limitations remain: the pipeline handles only a single object with two material types, optimization is offline (not real-time), and the comparison excludes dynamic reconstruction methods (Dynamic NeRF, 4D Gaussian) that could in principle provide richer motion priors. Extending to multi-material, multi-object scenes and accelerating to interactive rates are natural next steps.
