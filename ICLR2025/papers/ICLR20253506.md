## Problem

Existing convergence results for Gradient Descent variants on convex (L0,L1)-smooth problems either require an additional L-smoothness assumption or rely on small stepsizes that confine iterates to a compact set where the gradient is bounded. This paper asks how convergence bounds for GD with clipping, Polyak stepsizes, acceleration, and adaptive stepsizes depend purely on L0 and L1, without any standard smoothness constant or exponential dependence on the initial distance to the solution.

## Input / Output

The methods take a convex (or strongly convex) differentiable objective f: R^d → R satisfying symmetric (L0,L1)-smoothness, a starting point x^0, and the smoothness parameters L0, L1 (plus a stepsize η for (L0,L1)-GD and STM). They produce an ε-solution x^N with f(x^N) − f(x*) ≤ ε after a bounded number of iterations. A key feature is that the resulting complexity bounds depend only on L0, L1, and R0 = ‖x^0 − x*‖, and crucially do not depend on the Lipschitz constant L of the gradient (which may be infinite) nor on ‖∇f(x^0)‖ (which can be exponentially larger than R0 under (L0,L1)-smoothness). For the stochastic extensions, the input additionally requires a finite sum of functions sharing a common minimizer (over-parameterization assumption).

## Method

The central technical contribution is a two-phase analysis for (L0,L1)-GD (a smoothed gradient clipping method with stepsize η/(L0+L1‖∇f(x^k)‖)): when ‖∇f(x^k)‖ ≥ L0/L1 (large-gradient phase), the squared distance to the solution decreases by a constant νη/(8L1²) per step, bounding the phase length by O(L1²R0²); when ‖∇f(x^k)‖ < L0/L1 (small-gradient phase), the method behaves as standard GD on a 2L0-smooth convex problem, yielding the O(L0R0²/ε) rate. The same two-phase structure is established for GD-PS (which requires only f(x*) rather than L0, L1), giving matching O(max{L0R0²/ε, L1²R0²}) complexity without L-smoothness. For acceleration, the paper proposes (L0,L1)-STM, an adaptation of the Similar Triangles Method where the GD-type stepsize is proportional to 1/(L0+L1‖∇f(x^{k+1})‖) rather than a fixed smoothness constant, achieving O(√(L0(1+L1R0·exp(L1R0))R0²/ε)) complexity. For Adaptive Gradient Descent, a new potential function incorporating a cumulative step-size term yields a refined O(L0D²/N) rate that avoids the exponential factor (1+L1D·exp(L1D))·exp(√2·L1D) present in the naive application of prior results.

## Results

For convex (L₀,L₁)-smooth optimization, the paper establishes O(max{L₀R₀²/ε, L₁²R₀²}) iteration complexity for both (L₀,L₁)-GD (smoothed gradient clipping) and GD-PS (Polyak stepsizes), where R₀ = ‖x⁰ − x*‖. The accelerated (L₀,L₁)-STM achieves O(√(L₀(1+L₁R₀e^{L₁R₀})R₀²/ε)), and Adaptive Gradient Descent achieves O(max{L₀D²/ε, m²(L₁²D²+L₁⁴D₁⁴)}) where m is a logarithmic factor. Crucially, all bounds are independent of the global Lipschitz constant L and of ‖∇f(x⁰)‖, which can be exponentially larger than R₀ under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Koloskova et al. (2023) and Takezawa et al. (2024), the new (L₀,L₁)-GD and GD-PS bounds eliminate the dependence on the global smoothness constant L, which can be arbitrarily large or even infinite (e.g., for f(x)=‖x‖⁴, L scales as O(1+‖x⁰‖³) while L₀=4 and L₁=3 remain fixed). This means that by moving the starting point x⁰ farther from the solution, the previous bounds degrade without bound while the new bounds remain unchanged. Compared to Li et al. (2024a), the new results remove the dependence on the gradient-norm bound G and on f(x⁰)−f(x*), both of which can be exponentially larger than R₀ under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness (by Lemma 2.1, ‖∇f(x⁰)‖ can scale as L₀R₀e^{L₁R₀}). The paper also identifies a novel two-stage behavior of (L₀,L₁)-GD: an initial phase where the gradient is large (‖∇f‖≥L₀/L₁) and the distance to the solution decreases by a constant per step, followed by a phase where the method behaves as standard GD on a 2L₀-smooth problem. For the accelerated (L₀,L₁)-STM, the worst-case complexity matches that of Nesterov's method from Li et al. (2024a), but without requiring stepsizes that depend on R₀ and f(x⁰)−f(x*).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the (L0,L1)-smoothness framework introduced by Zhang et al. (2020b) for non-convex problems to the convex setting, building on the symmetric (L0,L1)-smoothness formalism and key technical inequalities from Chen et al. (2023). It departs from prior convex analyses by Koloskova et al. (2023), Takezawa et al. (2024), and Li et al. (2024a), which either required an additional L-smoothness assumption or relied on small stepsizes that implicitly bound the gradient norm, leading to exponential dependence on the initial distance R0. The paper adapts the Similar Triangles Method (Gasnikov & Nesterov, 2016) and Adaptive Gradient Descent (Malitsky & Mishchenko, 2020) to the (L0,L1)-smooth convex setting, deriving the first convergence guarantees for these methods under this assumption without L-smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of the L-smoothness dependency and the exponential-in-R0 term from convergence bounds is practically significant: for functions like f(x)=||x||^4, the Lipschitz constant L grows as O(1+||x^0||^3), making prior bounds arbitrarily worse as the starting point moves away from the solution, while the new bounds depend only on L0 and L1. The discovery of a two-stage behavior (constant-distance-decrease phase followed by standard-GD-like phase) provides a new conceptual understanding of how clipping interacts with (L0,L1)-smoothness. Key open questions remain: tight lower bounds for convex (L0,L1)-smooth optimization, optimal methods for this class, and stochastic extensions beyond the restrictive common-minimizer (over-parameterization) assumption.
