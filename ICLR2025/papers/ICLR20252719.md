## Problem

Smaller language models (SLMs, <13B parameters) struggle to generate diverse, high-quality rationales for complex reasoning tasks, and existing self-improvement methods (self-refinement, sampling-based decoding, LLM-as-a-judge) fail to generalize at this scale. Approaches that rely on external large LLMs for supervision face legal, copyright, and ethical restrictions in commercial settings. The paper asks: how can SLMs be trained to generate and refine diverse rationales and select high-quality ones without any external LLM supervision or ground-truth rationale annotations?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252719/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single task instruction (text) as input and produces a rationale (a natural-language reasoning chain) followed by a final answer. At inference, a lightweight DeBERTa controller first selects which of two SLM variants should generate the rationale, then selects which variant should refine it, before the refined rationale is appended to the instruction prompt to elicit the final answer from the instruct model. The rationale is the intermediate artifact whose quality is optimized; the final answer accuracy serves as the end-task evaluation metric.

## Method

COALITION creates two distinct variants of the same SLM by instruction fine-tuning them on separate random splits of a CoT dataset, enabling them to exhibit different reasoning behaviors. During training, each variant generates a rationale for a task instruction, and both variants then refine each other's rationales (self-refinement and cross-refinement), producing a pool of diverse candidates. Each candidate is scored by the IFT model's likelihood of generating the ground-truth answer conditioned on that rationale, and the variants are tuned via DPO to prefer higher-scoring rationales, with a filtration step that discards samples where even the best rationale does not improve over no rationale. At inference, a trained DeBERTa controller dynamically routes each sample to the appropriate variant for generation and refinement, and ablations show that cross-refinement between the two variants consistently outperforms self-refinement by a single model.

## Results

Using Llama3-8B as the backbone, COALITION achieves 81.06% on GSM8K, 77.13% on PIQA, 83.26% on WinoGrande, 63.23% on HellaSwag, and 82.06% on CSQA, outperforming all baselines by up to 5.6% (on CSQA vs. SPIN). The gains are consistent across all three task domains (math, NLI, commonsense) and hold across model families (Phi3, Qwen1.5, Mistral, Llama3) and parameter scales (3.8B–14B). On the open-LLM leaderboard (task-agnostic SRO), COALITION yields uniform improvements of 1–12% across 10 general benchmarks for all tested models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COALITION uniformly outperforms all nine baselines across all five datasets. Against the strongest trainable self-play baseline SPIN (77.01/71.85/79.02/58.22/76.48), COALITION gains +4.05% on GSM8K, +5.28% on PIQA, +4.24% on WinoGrande, +5.01% on HellaSwag, and +5.58% on CSQA. The best prompting-based baseline, Self-Refine (77.26/72.81/79.49/60.48/78.22), is surpassed by 2.75–4.32% across datasets, confirming that trainable cross-variant deliberation beats single-model self-correction. Notably, Self-Rewarding LMs—which relies on sampling-based decoding and LLM-as-a-judge—degrades substantially at 8B scale (72.16 on GSM8K), and COALITION exceeds it by ~9% on GSM8K and WinoGrande, demonstrating that prompt-based quality rating does not generalise to smaller models. Exchange-of-Thought and Tree-of-Thought, which depend on multi-model or tree-search prompting, also underperform COALITION by 5–13% on GSM8K, further supporting the need for a trainable, task-guided selection mechanism rather than fixed prompting strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

COALITION sits at the intersection of preference optimization (DPO), self-play rationale generation, and multi-agent communication, but deliberately departs from each. Unlike distillation-based approaches (Distilling Step-by-Step, Zephyr) that rely on external LLMs for supervision, and unlike prompt-based methods (CoT, ToT, Self-Refine, Exchange-of-Thought) that the paper shows fail to generalize to smaller models, COALITION creates two variants of the same SLM trained on separate data splits to produce diverse rationales through cross-communication. The key methodological departure from prior self-play work (SPIN, Self-Rewarding LMs) is the use of likelihood-of-GT-answer as a task-guided utility score for rationale selection rather than LLM-as-a-judge, combined with a learned controller for variant routing at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important constraint—legal and ethical restrictions on using proprietary LLM outputs to train commercial models—by showing that a single SLM can be improved through internal deliberation between its own variants. The finding that cross-refinement consistently outperforms self-refinement across five datasets and four model families (4B–14B) provides evidence that diversity from distinct training splits is more valuable than sampling-based diversity from a single model. Open questions include whether scaling to more than two variants yields further gains, whether domain-specialized variants could improve performance on specific task types, and whether the approach extends to tasks where ground-truth answers are unavailable during training.
