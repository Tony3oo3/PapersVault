## Problem

The design of activation functions for deep neural networks has historically relied on empirical trial-and-error without theoretical justification, making the search for better activation functions inefficient and aimless. This paper asks three questions: does a best activation function exist, can a principled methodology be provided for searching better ones, and can such a methodology be demonstrated in practice?

## Input / Output

The EAFO methodology takes a data distribution p(y) (assumed Gaussian via the Central Limit Theorem) and the inverse function y(x) of a starting activation function as input, and produces a correction term η(x) that, when added to y(x), yields the inverse of an optimized activation function. In practice, CRReLU is applied as a drop-in replacement in standard vision transformer and CNN architectures consuming 32×32 or 224×224 RGB images, and in GPT-2 (137M parameters) for DPO-based LLM fine-tuning on preference pairs, with a single learnable scalar ε (initialized to 0.01) per activation layer.

## Method

The paper expresses information entropy as a functional H(y(x)) of the inverse activation function, then applies the Euler-Lagrange equation to prove the existence of a Worst Activation Function with Boundary Conditions (WAFBC), which takes the form of a cumulative distribution function and represents a global maximum of the entropy functional. From the Taylor expansion of this functional, the EAFO methodology derives a correction term η(x) = −(p(y(x))·y''(x)/y'(x) + p'(y(x))·y'(x)) that, when added to the inverse activation function, reduces entropy. Applying EAFO to ReLU under a Gaussian input distribution assumption yields CRReLU: f(x) = max(0,x) + εx·exp(−x²/2), where ε is a learnable parameter that enables self-optimization and mitigates the Gaussian assumption. The paper further proves that CRReLU's Lipschitz constant is max(1+ε, 1−0.446ε), recommending ε ∈ (−0.188, 0.084) to maintain Lipschitz continuity superior to GELU.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ViT-Tiny, CRReLU achieves 80.7% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline PReLU (78.0%) by 2.7 points and GELU (70.4%) by 10.3 points. On CIFAR-100 with ViT-Tiny, CRReLU reaches 46.6% versus 43.2% for PReLU and 32.6% for GELU. On ImageNet-1K with ViT-Tiny, CRReLU attains 57.5% top-1 accuracy, surpassing PReLU (56.8%) and GELU (53.9%). In LLM fine-tuning (GPT-2 with DPO), CRReLU consistently outperforms GELU across all penalty coefficients β ∈ {0.1, 1, 2, 5} on evaluation margin reward, accuracy, and loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRReLU decisively outperforms all six baselines (GELU, ELU, PReLU, CELU, SiLU, Mish) on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 across ViT-Tiny, DeiT-Tiny, and TNT-Small, with margins of 2.7–14.3 points over the strongest competitor (PReLU). On ImageNet-1K with ViT-Tiny, CRReLU (57.5%) leads PReLU (56.8%) by 0.7 points and GELU (53.9%) by 3.6 points; however, on DeiT-Tiny, GELU (61.7%) edges out CRReLU (61.6%) by a negligible 0.1 point, which the authors attribute to distillation bias from the GELU-trained teacher model. In the LLM fine-tuning task (GPT-2 + DPO on SHP and HH), CRReLU surpasses GELU on all three evaluation metrics (margin reward, accuracy, loss) at every tested β value, with the largest gap at β=5 (1.847 vs 1.654 margin reward). ELU and CELU consistently underperform all other baselines by 5–15 points, while SiLU and Mish cluster in the middle, trailing PReLU by 2–4 points on most configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the long-standing empirical tradition of activation function design (Leaky ReLU, PReLU, ELU, Swish/SiLU, Mish, GELU) by introducing a theoretical framework grounded in information entropy and calculus of variations. It builds directly on ReLU as the base function, applying a correction term derived from the Taylor expansion of the entropy functional (the EAFO methodology) to produce CRReLU. The key conceptual departure is the proof that a Worst Activation Function with Boundary Conditions (WAFBC) exists as a global maximum of the entropy functional, establishing that no optimal activation function exists—only progressively better ones—thereby reframing activation function search as a descent problem from WAFBC rather than an unconstrained search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EAFO methodology provides the first principled, entropy-based recipe for deriving improved activation functions, potentially replacing ad-hoc empirical search with a systematic procedure. Open questions remain significant: the Gaussian distribution assumption may not hold in transformer attention layers, dynamic optimization of activation during training is computationally prohibitive at scale, and the framework currently requires invertible activation functions. The moderate but consistent gains of CRReLU over GELU across ViT, DeiT, TNT, ConvNeXt, and GPT-2 fine-tuning suggest practical utility, but the theoretical framework's broader applicability (e.g., to KANs or non-invertible functions) remains unexplored.
