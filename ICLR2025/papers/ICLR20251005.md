## Problem

Adam/AdamW requires a per-parameter second-moment vector v, doubling the optimizer memory footprint (e.g., ~56 GB for a 7B model), which forces expensive CPU offloading and model sharding. The paper asks whether Adam's per-parameter learning rates are truly necessary, or whether the near-block-diagonal Hessian structure of neural networks means most of these learning rates are redundant and can be collapsed into far fewer block-wise rates without performance loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251005/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The optimizer consumes standard per-parameter gradients and hyperparameters (β₁, β₂, ε, learning rate, weight decay) identical to AdamW, but additionally requires a parameter partition defined by the Hessian structure of the architecture. For Transformers, this means grouping parameters by heads (Q, K), output neurons (V, attn.proj, MLP), or tokens (embed, output). The output is updated parameters plus a drastically reduced v: instead of one scalar per parameter, v stores one scalar per block (e.g., one per head or per output neuron), cutting ≥99.9% of v elements and halving total optimizer memory from 2× model size to 1× model size.

## Method

Adam-mini replaces Adam's per-parameter second moment v_i with a single block-wise value v_b = (1−β₂)·mean(g_b ⊙ g_b) + β₂·v_b, where g_b is the gradient sub-vector for block b, yielding one learning rate η/√v_b shared across all parameters in that block. The critical design choice is the partition strategy, guided by Principle 1: each parameter block should correspond to the smallest dense sub-block in the Hessian. For Transformers, this yields four partition rules—Q and K by attention heads, V/attn.proj/MLP by output neurons, and embed/output by vocabulary tokens—derived from empirical Hessian visualization showing near-block-diagonal structure with dense sub-blocks of these sizes. The paper provides a linear-algebraic explanation for why this works: Adam's diagonal preconditioner D_Adam is ineffective at reducing the condition number of dense Hessian sub-blocks (measured by the ratio r = κ(D_Adam·H_b)/κ(H_b), which grows as the block becomes denser), so a single well-chosen learning rate per dense block can match or exceed Adam's per-parameter rates. Empirically, Adam-mini matches or slightly beats AdamW on GPT-2 (125M–1.5B) and Llama (20M–13B) pre-training, SFT, and RLHF, while achieving 49.6% higher throughput on 2×A800-80GB GPUs due to the reduced memory enabling larger per-GPU batch sizes and less inter-GPU communication.

## Results

Adam-mini achieves 50% less memory than AdamW across all tested models (e.g., 26.96 GB vs. 53.92 GB for Llama 2-7B) while maintaining on-par or better performance. In scaling law experiments (39M–1B Llama 2 on C4), Adam-mini reaches slightly lower final validation perplexity than AdamW for every model size (e.g., 12.372 vs. 12.452 at 1B). On Llama 2-7B pre-training with 2× A800-80GB GPUs, Adam-mini achieves 49.6% higher throughput (5572 vs. 3726 tokens/s) and saves 33.1% wall-clock time. On RLHF alignment of Llama 2-7B, Adam-mini scores 5.68 on MT-Bench (GPT-4 judge) versus 5.54 for AdamW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW, Adam-mini matches or slightly outperforms: final perplexity is lower at every model size in the scaling law study (Table 4), and MT-Bench scores are higher for both SFT (5.40 vs. 5.37) and RLHF (5.68 vs. 5.54) on Llama 2-7B. Against Adafactor (both the original and the Zhai et al. 2022 variant), Adam-mini consistently achieves lower training loss on Llama 2-20M and Llama 2-1B pre-training even after extensive hyperparameter sweeps (β₂, ε, warm-up steps); Adafactor also suffers training instability at 1B scale. CAME and SM3, the other memory-efficient baselines, similarly underperform Adam-mini on GPT-2 and Llama pre-training (Figures 8, 10). Lion (Chen et al., 2024), despite using the authors' recommended tuning strategy (lr ≈ 10× smaller than AdamW, β₁=0.95, β₂=0.98), still underperforms Adam-mini on Llama 2-20M and encounters loss spikes on GPT-2-125M. LAMB, which retains coordinate-wise learning rates and adds layer-wise rescaling, performs worse than Adam-mini on GPT-2 pre-training and does not save memory over Adam.

## Relation to Prior Work

Adam-mini builds directly on Adam/AdamW by replacing the per-parameter second-order momentum v with a block-wise mean, guided by the near-block-diagonal Hessian structure of neural networks (Collobert, 2004) and the Hessian-block heterogeneity findings for Transformers (Zhang et al., 2024). It departs from prior memory-efficient optimizers like Adafactor and CAME, which reduce v via generic low-rank factorization, by instead leveraging problem-specific Hessian structure to determine where learning rates can be safely consolidated. The paper also positions itself against Lion and LAMB, arguing that its Hessian-based partition principle is more principled and that the PyTorch default partition (used by NovoGrad and BAGM) causes training instability at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% memory reduction (eliminating v entirely) is practically transformative for LLM training, enabling larger batch sizes per GPU and reducing communication overhead, yielding 33% wall-clock savings. The Hessian-based partition principle provides a general design framework applicable beyond Transformers, though the paper acknowledges that the mean(v) learning rate is suboptimal and that eigenvalue-based designs within blocks could yield further gains. Open questions include deriving theoretical lower bounds on the condition number of Adam's preconditioner on dense Hessian blocks, and whether the partition strategy generalizes to novel architectures without manual Hessian analysis.
