## Problem

Existing Schrödinger Bridge Matching frameworks operate at two extremes: fully unsupervised (no pre-aligned pairs, discarding valuable coupling information) or fully supervised (all pairs aligned, infeasible for most applications due to labeling cost). No matching framework can effectively leverage the small fraction of pre-aligned pairs that are typically available in practice, leaving a gap between scalability and the use of partial supervision to guide transport.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252127/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes source and target distribution samples (π₀, π₁) plus a small key-point (KP) set of pre-aligned pairs (<8% of the dataset) whose trajectories are assumed fixed and given as solutions of the static Schrödinger Bridge. For image translation, inputs are 512×1 latent vectors from a pre-trained ALAE autoencoder on 1024×1024 FFHQ images. The output is a parameterized drift field u_t^θ that, when propagated through the SDE dX_t = u_t^θ dt + σ dW_t, induces a coupling producing transported samples or translated images in the target distribution.

## Method

FSBM begins from a static semi-supervised entropic OT problem augmented with a distance-preserving guidance function G that penalizes deviation of non-coupled samples from their relative distance to the nearest KP sample, then derives an equivalent dynamic formulation (Theorem 3.2) adding a state-feedback term |u_t^T ∇G| + (σ²/2)ΔG to the standard SB kinetic energy objective. Training alternates between two stages: (1) optimizing the intermediate path conditioned on fixed endpoints, approximated simulation-free as a Gaussian path with spline-parameterized mean and variance, where the guidance term acts as state feedback steering non-coupled samples along the trajectory of their assigned KP; and (2) matching the parameterized drift to the optimized conditional drift via a Bregman-divergence-based variational gap (Entropic Lagrangian Bridge Matching objective), whose Hamiltonian involves a piecewise function g(·) handling the absolute value in the guidance term. The key insight is that the inner product of the drift with ∇G projects the conditioned drift onto the guidance gradient, creating regions of attraction around each KP that preserve structural and distance information throughout the transport.

## Results

On the FFHQ image translation tasks (1024×1024, ALAE latent space), FSBM achieves the lowest FID across all four directions: 14.78 (Man→Woman), 16.12 (Woman→Man), 15.52 (Old→Young), and 17.14 (Young→Old), outperforming DSBM and LOSBM. In crowd navigation, FSBM maintains W₂ < 1 under all perturbed initial conditions (shifted mean, shifted STD, uniform), whereas GSBM diverges catastrophically (W₂ up to 2.43×10¹⁰). For high-dimensional opinion depolarization (X ∈ ℝ¹⁰⁰), FSBM achieves W₂ = 42.91 and KL = 0.034 in 2h43m, matching GSBM's quality (W₂ = 43.41, KL = 0.034) while requiring roughly half the training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSBM is compared against four baselines across three task families. Against GSBM (Liu et al., 2024) on crowd navigation, FSBM is dramatically more robust: under perturbed mean/STD/uniform initial conditions, GSBM's W₂ explodes to 8.87×10⁵, 2.43×10¹⁰, and 6.65×10⁹ respectively, while FSBM stays below 0.67; on opinion dynamics, FSBM matches GSBM's KL (0.034) with a slightly lower W₂ (42.91 vs 43.41) in half the wall-clock time (2h43m vs 5h29m). Against DSBM (Shi et al., 2023) on image translation, FSBM wins on all four FID metrics (e.g., 14.78 vs 15.90 for Man→Woman) and all four LPIPS metrics (0.44 vs 0.47 for Man→Woman), with only ~180 MB additional VRAM. Against LOSBM (Gushchin et al., 2024), FSBM achieves substantially lower FID (14.78 vs 18.89 for Man→Woman) and LPIPS (0.44 vs 0.70), indicating LOSBM loses input features during translation. Against DeepGSB (Liu et al., 2022) on opinion dynamics, FSBM reduces W₂ from 58.16 to 42.91 and KL from 0.096 to 0.034 while training in less than half the time.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSBM bridges two previously disconnected extremes in Schrödinger Bridge Matching: the fully unsupervised DSBM (Shi et al., 2023), which learns couplings from scratch, and the fully supervised I²SB (Liu et al., 2023), which requires all pairs to be pre-aligned. The method's starting point is the keypoint-guided static entropic OT formulation from Gu et al. (2022, 2023), which uses a small set of aligned pairs to guide transport of unpaired samples, but the paper's core contribution is deriving an equivalent dynamic formulation (Theorem 3.2) and integrating it into the alternating two-stage BM optimization scheme inherited from GSBM (Liu et al., 2024). The variational gap for the coupling optimization step is expressed via Bregman divergence following Neklyudov et al. (2023), extended here to handle the non-standard Lagrangian introduced by the guidance term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSBM addresses a practical gap: most real-world applications (image restoration, protein docking, domain adaptation) have a small number of aligned pairs but not full supervision, and existing matching frameworks discard this information. The demonstration that fewer than 8% aligned pairs yield substantial gains in generalization (especially under distribution shift) and training speed makes the approach broadly applicable. Key limitations include reliance on the quality and mode coverage of the KP set, use of only the single nearest keypoint rather than k-nearest, and the absolute-value relaxation in the dynamic objective that may introduce conservatism. Open directions include adaptive KP sampling that guarantees mode coverage, task-specific guidance functions, and extending the framework to multi-source or time-varying settings.
