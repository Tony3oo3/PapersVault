## Problem

Existing graph-language models for semi-supervised document classification face two coupled challenges: graph generation relies on artificial edge-distribution assumptions that may not reflect real citation network structure, and LM finetuning for text embedding requires extensive annotations that are unavailable in low-supervision regimes. This paper asks whether a natural structural property of real networks—the scale-free degree distribution—can serve as a principled, training-free graph prior that simultaneously enables reliable pseudo-label generation to compensate for label scarcity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw document text sequences (titles and abstracts) along with a small set of labeled samples (m ≪ n, e.g., 50–600 labels out of thousands to hundreds of thousands of nodes). Text is first encoded into hand-crafted feature vectors via traditional encoders (BoW, TF-IDF, or skip-gram) for graph construction, then processed by a finetuned DeBERTa (optionally augmented with GPT-3.5 explanatory responses) to produce dense text embeddings. The output is a predicted class label for every unlabeled document, produced by a final GNN classifier operating on the enriched embeddings and the constructed KNN graph.

## Method

SFGL unifies graph generation and text embedding under a scale-free structural prior. The key empirical finding is that a directed KNN graph built with cosine similarity and an appropriately chosen k (20–25) approximates a power-law degree distribution (α ≈ 2.7–3.3) on citation networks, validated by log-log curve fitting. The pipeline proceeds in stages: (1) encode text into shallow features and construct the KNN graph; (2) train a GNN pseudo-labeler on the graph to propagate labels from the few labeled nodes to all unlabeled nodes; (3) finetune DeBERTa jointly on real and pseudo-labels to obtain semantically enriched embeddings; (4) optionally feed GPT-3.5-generated explanatory responses into the LM finetuning; (5) train a final GNN classifier on the enriched embeddings and KNN graph. An iterative variant re-encodes, re-constructs the graph, and re-generates pseudo-labels using the improved embeddings, creating a mutually reinforcing loop between the GNN and LM components.

## Results

On the four citation-network benchmarks, SFGL+GPT achieves the highest classification accuracy at every labeling rate tested. At the lowest labeling rates, SFGL+GPT reaches 86.4% on PubMed (60 labels), 45.7% on ogbn-arxiv (50 labels), 49.5% on arxiv23 (50 labels), and 63.5% on Cora (70 labels), consistently outperforming the strongest baseline GCN+DeBERTa+GPT by 12.5, 24.3, 16.9, and 14.9 percentage points respectively. Even without GPT, SFGL alone surpasses GCN+DeBERTa by 25.4 points on PubMed and 16.0 points on ogbn-arxiv at the lowest labeling rates. The advantage is most pronounced under extreme label scarcity (≤0.3% labeled), where pure LM baselines (DeBERTa, DeBERTa+GPT) become unreliable or infeasible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCN (Kipf & Welling, 2016), SFGL+GPT wins on all four datasets at every labeling rate, with the largest gaps at low labels (e.g., +18.7 pts on PubMed, +11.9 pts on ogbn-arxiv at 50 labels). Against DeBERTa (He et al., 2021), the advantage is dramatic: DeBERTa is infeasible or near-random on ogbn-arxiv and arxiv23 at low labeling rates, while SFGL+GPT achieves 45.7% and 49.5% respectively. Against GCN+DeBERTa (Zhao et al., 2022), SFGL+GPT outperforms by 15–25 points at the lowest labeling rates, demonstrating that the scale-free KNN graph plus pseudo-labeling is far more effective than simply combining a GCN with LM embeddings. Against GCN+DeBERTa+GPT (He et al., 2024), the closest competitor, SFGL+GPT still leads by 12–25 points at low labels, confirming that the graph-based pseudo-labeler provides complementary supervision that GPT-generated responses alone cannot replicate. At higher labeling rates (e.g., PubMed 500 labels), the gap narrows (SFGL+GPT 91.2% vs. GCN+DeBERTa+GPT 92.8%), suggesting the method's primary value is in the extreme low-supervision regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work in graph-language models: latent graph inference (LGI) models that learn graph structures under artificial edge-distribution assumptions, and language-assisted graph (LAG) models that finetune LMs but require extensive annotations. It departs from both by replacing learned latent graphs with a simple KNN graph justified by the scale-free property of real citation networks, and by replacing label-hungry LM finetuning with a graph-based pseudo-labeler that propagates supervision through the KNN structure. The GPT-integration pipeline and final GNN classification architecture are directly inherited from He et al. (ICLR 2024), positioning SFGL as a structural-prior-driven extension of that framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a trivially constructed KNN graph with cosine similarity can serve as a well-motivated, training-free structural prior for citation networks, eliminating the need for complex latent graph optimization. The finding that real graphs do not consistently outperform KNN graphs under extreme label scarcity challenges the assumption that ground-truth topology is always beneficial. Key limitations include the restriction to citation networks, the approximate (not exact) nature of the scale-free fit, and mild overfitting observed in iterative training; extending the approach to social or biological networks and incorporating the power-law constraint into an explicit optimization objective are natural next steps.
