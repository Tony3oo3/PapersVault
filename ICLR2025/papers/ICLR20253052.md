## Problem

Designing incentives to steer multi-agent systems toward desired outcomes is critical in economics and beyond, but existing work (Zhang et al., 2023) assumes agents are no-regret learners, which is unrealistic for agents with bounded rationality and recency bias. This paper tackles the more general and practically relevant problem of steering Markovian agents—whose policy updates depend only on the current policy and observed rewards—in a finite-horizon, non-episodic setting where the mediator has only one interaction with real agents and faces fundamental uncertainty about the agents' learning dynamics.

## Input / Output

The mediator receives a finite-horizon Markov game G, a finite model class F of candidate learning dynamics (satisfying realizability), the initial joint policy π₁, and access to simulators for every f ∈ F. It produces a history-dependent steering strategy ψ* that, at each step t, maps the full interaction history {π₁, u₁, …, π_t} to a non-negative steering reward vector u_t bounded by U_max, applied element-wise to the game's reward function. In the grid-world variant, the mediator observes only agents' recent pixel-based trajectories rather than exact policy parameters, making the problem partially observable.

## Method

The paper formulates steering as a finite-horizon non-episodic RL problem with a novel objective (Obj. 1) that maximizes the average over F of β·η^goal(π_{T+1}) minus cumulative steering cost, and proves the optimal solution is Pareto optimal and achieves ε-steering gap for sufficiently large β. For small |F|, the problem is recast as a POMDP with hidden state (π_t, f) and solved exactly via a belief-state MDP lift, where the mediator learns a policy over the low-dimensional posterior b_t over models (Alg. 4). For large |F|, a First-Explore-Then-Exploit (FETE) framework is proposed: a strategic exploration phase maximizes the MLE identification success rate (rather than using random noise), followed by an exploitation phase that solves Obj. (1) treating the MLE-identified model as ground truth. Theoretical existence results show that for NPG/PMD dynamics under a general incentive-driven assumption, steering strategies achieving arbitrary ε-gap exist for any feasible initialization, and for identifiable model classes the FETE framework recovers near-optimal performance.

## Results

In the small model set setting (|F|=2, Stag Hunt with NPG), the belief-state strategy ψ*_{Belief} achieves 0.87±0.05 probability of ε-steering gap (ε=0.01) under f_{0.7} with steering cost 10.5±0.4, and 0.99±0.01 under f_{1.0} with cost 6.1±0.3, while ignoring model uncertainty (deploying ψ*_{0.7} or ψ*_{1.0} blindly) leads to failure (0.13 probability) or excessive cost (8.2 vs 5.6). In the large model set setting (|F|=3^10, 10-player cooperative game), FETE achieves near-100% MLE identification accuracy after only 30 exploration steps (vs. 300+ for random exploration), and its steering gap and cost are competitive with the Oracle (which knows f* in advance), while significantly outperforming FETE-RE (the SIAR-MPC adaptation) in steering gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is FETE-RE, an adaptation of SIAR-MPC (Canyakmaz et al., 2024) that replaces FETE's strategic exploration with noise-based random exploration. In the large model set experiment (|F|=3^10, 10-player game), FETE significantly outperforms FETE-RE in steering gap across all three tested model realizations (f_1, f_2, f_3), while both are competitive with the Oracle in steering cost. The exploration efficiency gap is stark: FETE's strategic exploration reaches near-100% MLE accuracy in ~30 steps, whereas FETE-RE's random exploration requires over 300 steps. In the small model set setting, the belief-state approach (ψ*_{Belief}) dominates the naive strategies that ignore model uncertainty: deploying ψ*_{0.7} under f_{1.0} incurs 46% higher cost (8.2 vs 5.6), and deploying ψ*_{1.0} under f_{0.7} fails to achieve the target accuracy (0.13 vs 0.87 probability of ε-steering gap). The Oracle (knowing f* in advance) remains the upper bound, but FETE closes most of the gap, validating the first-explore-then-exploit design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the steering problem introduced by Zhang et al. (2023) from no-regret learners to the broader class of Markovian agents (whose policy updates depend only on current policy and reward, motivated by human recency bias), and shifts from an infinite-horizon adversarial setting to a finite-horizon non-episodic one. It leverages the POMDP-to-belief-MDP lifting (Ibe, 2013) to handle model uncertainty via history-dependent strategies, and grounds its theoretical existence results in Policy Mirror Descent (Xiao, 2022), of which Natural Policy Gradient is a special case. The concurrent work of Canyakmaz et al. (2024) addresses a similar finite-horizon non-episodic steering problem but uses noise-based exploration and regression-based model estimation, whereas this paper employs strategic MLE-based exploration and a principled belief-state formulation for small model classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally model agent steering as a non-episodic RL problem with history-dependent strategies under model uncertainty, providing both existence guarantees and tractable algorithms (belief-state DP for small model classes, FETE for large ones). The grid-world experiment demonstrating that steering is possible using only behavioral observations (trajectories) rather than full policy access has practical relevance for privacy-constrained settings. Open questions include extending to non-Markovian agents, incorporating budget constraints on steering rewards, and identifying objectives that strictly dominate the current Pareto-optimal formulation. The framework could inform real-world incentive design in technology adoption, public goods provision, and collective action problems.
