## Problem

Standard continued pretraining requires 10B–100B+ tokens to adapt a language model to a new domain, making it infeasible for small, niche corpora of only ~1M tokens where each fact appears rarely or only once. Simple paraphrasing of such a corpus fails to provide the diverse knowledge representations that large-scale pretraining naturally offers, so the model cannot effectively acquire parametric knowledge from the source documents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251593/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small source corpus (265 QuALITY articles, ~1.3M tokens) and a prompted LM (GPT-4) as inputs, and produces a ~350× larger synthetic corpus (455M tokens) via the EntiGraph algorithm. The synthetic corpus is then used for continued pretraining of Llama 3 8B Base with RedPajama replay. Evaluation is closed-book: the model must answer 4,609 multiple-choice QA questions and follow open-ended summarization instructions about the source articles without any test-time access to them, isolating parametric knowledge from retrieval.

## Method

EntiGraph is a two-step synthetic data augmentation algorithm that externalizes diversity to the combinatorial structure of a knowledge graph over entities. Step 1 extracts salient entities from each source document via a prompted LM; Step 2 iteratively samples pairs and triplets of entities and prompts the LM to describe their relations in the context of the source document, generating diverse text that explores edges of the underlying knowledge graph. The resulting synthetic corpus is used for standard causal-language-modeling continued pretraining (2 epochs with RedPajama replay) on Llama 3 8B Base. A key ablation shows that replacing GPT-4 with the same-capacity Llama 3.1 8B Instruct as the generator preserves the log-linear scaling trend, ruling out distillation as the source of gains. A theoretical model formalizes EntiGraph as a breadth-first-search process on a random directed graph, yielding a mixture-of-exponential scaling law (linear → log-linear → plateau) that closely fits the empirical accuracy curve up to 455M synthetic tokens.

## Results

On the QuALITY closed-book QA benchmark (4,609 multiple-choice questions), EntiGraph CPT improves Llama 3 8B Base accuracy from 39.49% to 56.22% using 455M synthetic tokens, with log-linear scaling observed across the full token range. In the open-book RAG setting, EntiGraph CPT + RAG achieves 62.60% accuracy versus 60.35% for Llama 3 8B Base + RAG (both with 99.63% Recall@8), while GPT-4 + Oracle RAG reaches 86.09%. The EntiGraph CPT gain of 16.73 percentage points captures approximately 80% of the absolute improvement provided by RAG (20.86 points) in a setting where retrieval recall is nearly perfect.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Llama 3 8B Base (the base model from which EntiGraph CPT is derived), the proposed method wins decisively in both settings: closed-book QA improves from 39.49% to 56.22% (+16.73 pp), and open-book RAG accuracy rises from 60.35% to 62.60% (+2.25 pp), confirming that parametric knowledge from synthetic CPT composes with retrieval. Against GPT-4 + Oracle RAG (86.09%), EntiGraph CPT + RAG (62.60%) trails by 23.5 pp, but GPT-4 is a substantially larger model with a perfect retriever, so this gap is expected. The paper also compares against two internal baselines: Raw CPT (continued pretraining directly on the 1.3M-token source corpus) underperforms even the unmodified Llama 3 8B Base, and Rephrase CPT (iterative paraphrasing) scales poorly, plateauing well below EntiGraph CPT at 38M tokens. These results indicate that the entity-graph-based diversity of EntiGraph is critical—simple rephrasing or raw repetition of a small corpus is insufficient for effective knowledge acquisition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continued pretraining paradigm (Gururangan et al., 2020) from its standard regime of 10B–100B+ token domain corpora down to ~1M token small corpora, introducing synthetic data augmentation as a necessary intermediary step. It departs from simple rephrasing-based augmentation (Maini et al., 2024; Ovadia et al., 2024) by showing that paraphrase diversity is insufficient, and instead externalizes diversity to a combinatorial entity-graph structure. It also distinguishes itself from knowledge editing (Zhu et al., 2020; Meng et al., 2022), which learns atomic (subject, relation, object) tuples, by targeting corpus-level parametric knowledge acquisition, and from synthetic pretraining data generation (Eldan & Li, 2023; Gunasekar et al., 2023), which creates data for training from scratch rather than teaching a pretrained model about a specific small corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As public text data is projected to be exhausted by frontier models around 2028, the ability to extract more knowledge from small, proprietary, or long-tail corpora becomes critical; this paper provides a concrete recipe (synthetic augmentation → continued pretraining → optional RAG) that achieves 80% of RAG's absolute accuracy gain without test-time corpus access. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the QuALITY corpus and single 8B base model, whether it can operate without a strong prompted LM (the ablation with Llama 3.1 8B Instruct is encouraging but limited to 334M tokens), and whether entity-graph-based augmentation can improve data efficiency on standard large-scale pretraining data. The reliance on GPT-4 for synthesis and the risk of hallucination on more complex content remain practical limitations.
