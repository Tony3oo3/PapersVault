## Problem

LAVA, the leading model-agnostic data valuation method based on optimal transport, requires O(N²) memory to compute the full pairwise cost matrix, making it infeasible for datasets beyond ~50K points. This memory bottleneck prevents OT-based data valuation from scaling to the large, web-scraped, noisy datasets (millions of points) that are common in production settings. SAVA addresses this gap by reformulating the OT computation on batches of data points rather than the entire dataset, analogous to how stochastic gradient descent replaces full-batch gradients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250245/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SAVA takes a noisy labeled training set (up to 1M+ RGB images with unreliable labels) and a smaller clean labeled validation set as input, with features extracted by a pre-trained ResNet18. It outputs a scalar valuation score per training point, where higher scores indicate greater dissimilarity to the validation distribution and thus higher likelihood of being noisy, corrupted, or irrelevant. The scores are model-agnostic (no dependency on a specific trained classifier's loss) and are used downstream for corruption detection (ranking top-scoring points), data pruning (removing the highest-scoring fraction), or incremental learning pipelines.

## Method

SAVA decomposes the single large OT problem into a two-level hierarchical OT (HOT): first, it solves Kt×Kv small OT problems between individual training batch Bi and validation batch Bj' (each of size Ni×Nj' ≪ N×N'), computing pairwise labeled-point costs via feature distance plus a weighted OT distance between conditional label distributions; second, it solves one small OT problem across the Kt×Kv batch-level cost matrix to obtain the optimal cross-batch transport plan π*. The per-point valuation score is then the calibrated gradient of the HOT with respect to that point's probability mass, weighted by π* across validation batches. The paper also corrects a theoretical error in LAVA's Theorem 2 on the entropic-regularization trade-off by properly accounting for the non-negativity constraint on the transport plan and fixing the discrete KL divergence formulation, yielding a refined bound on the gradient approximation error that scales with ε.

## Results

On the Clothing1M dataset (1M+ noisy training images, 14-class classification), SAVA achieves 70.0 ± 0.2 test accuracy after pruning low-value data, compared to 67.6 ± 0.2 for training on the full noisy set — a 2.4-point improvement. On CIFAR-10 corruption detection (50K training points), SAVA consistently detects noisy labels, noisy features, backdoor (Trojan square), and poison (frogs) corruptions at high detection rates (fraction of corrupted points in the top N/4 prefix), while LAVA suffers GPU out-of-memory errors at the full 50K dataset size. SAVA scales to datasets two orders of magnitude larger than LAVA without memory issues, using a default batch size of 1024.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAVA matches LAVA's detection performance on small CIFAR-10 subsets (≤10K points) but uniquely scales to the full 50K-point dataset where LAVA hits GPU OOM errors across all four corruption types. Against KNN Shapley, SAVA outperforms on noisy features and poison frogs detection (KNN Shapley struggles after inspecting 25% of ordered data for poison frogs), while both scale similarly on noisy labels and backdoor attacks. TracInCP fails to detect noisy labels better than random selection and struggles with noisy features and backdoor attacks; Influence Functions matches SAVA on noisy labels but is prohibitively expensive on large datasets. On Clothing1M, SAVA (70.0 ± 0.2) outperforms both EL2N and Batch-wise LAVA, demonstrating the benefit of SAVA's optimal-plan-weighted aggregation across validation batches versus Batch-wise LAVA's uniform weighting. Supervised prototypes, a semantic deduplication method that ignores labels, only marginally beats random pruning, confirming that label-aware OT-based valuation is substantially more effective for noisy-label pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAVA is a direct scalable extension of LAVA (Just et al., 2023), which introduced model-agnostic data valuation via the calibrated (sub)gradient of the OT distance between training and validation distributions. The key architectural departure is the adoption of hierarchical optimal transport (Yurochkin et al., 2019; Lee et al., 2019) to decompose a single expensive OT problem over the full dataset into many cheaper batch-level OT subproblems, reducing memory from O(N×N') to O(Ni×Nj'). The ground cost for labeled data follows Alvarez-Melis & Fusi (2020), and the paper also corrects two errors in LAVA's Theorem 2 regarding the dual formulation of entropic regularized OT (missing non-negativity constraint and incorrect discrete KL divergence term).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAVA makes OT-based data valuation feasible for web-scale datasets (demonstrated on 1M+ point Clothing1M), removing the quadratic memory bottleneck that previously restricted LAVA to tens of thousands of points. The theoretical correction to LAVA's entropic regularization analysis is important for anyone building on that result. However, the method remains limited to labeled image datasets (no text or speech ground costs explored), and the use of HOT introduces a looser validation error bound than standard OT. Open questions include whether the batch-level coupling approximation degrades valuation quality in highly imbalanced or multi-modal settings, and whether the approach extends to unlabeled or semi-supervised data valuation.
