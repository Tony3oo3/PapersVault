## Problem

Existing FOL reasoning benchmarks suffer from a fundamental trade-off: template-based datasets (ProntoQA, ProofWriter, RuleTaker) are scalable but lack linguistic diversity and full coverage of all seven FOL connectives, while manually curated datasets (FOLIO) offer richness but are small, expensive to produce, and vulnerable to data contamination. Additionally, most prior datasets either omit intermediate reasoning chains entirely or provide them in a form that requires users to write custom extraction code, limiting their utility for training and transparent evaluation.

## Input / Output

The generation framework takes a randomly sampled subject name (from public human/pet name repositories) and a characteristic keyword (from WordNet) as seeds, and produces a complete FOL reasoning instance comprising a set of premises (facts and rules), a goal statement, two types of logically relevant distractions, and a step-by-step reasoning chain with intermediate conclusions in both symbolic and natural language. The resulting ProverQA benchmark contains 1,500 instances partitioned into three difficulty tiers by reasoning-chain length (easy: 1–2 steps, medium: 3–5 steps, hard: 6–9 steps), with premises shuffled within each problem to prevent pattern exploitation.

## Method

ProverGen is a three-stage neurosymbolic pipeline that alternates between LLM generation and symbolic verification. Stage 1 uses Llama3.1-70B-Instruct to write a short background story conditioned on the name and keyword, establishing linguistic context. Stage 2 constructs the logic skeleton via a top-down reasoning-tree approach: starting from the goal node (set to True), it iteratively samples logical expressions linking the current node to new premise nodes, then invokes the Prover9 symbolic prover to compute the truth values of those premises, guaranteeing logical consistency at every layer; two types of distractions (domain-related but irrelevant, and logically related but leading to Uncertain) are injected to stress-test robustness. Stage 3 translates each symbolic fact and rule into natural language using LLMs, generating both a universal and a specific version of each rule and selecting the one consistent with common sense. This top-down construction (as opposed to the bottom-up premise-to-conclusion approach in prior work) ensures every fact and rule is directly relevant to the conclusion, and the Prover9 verification step eliminates the logical incoherence that plagues purely LLM-generated reasoning chains.

## Results

On ProverQA, even the strongest models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Mistral-Large-Instruct) barely exceed 50% accuracy on the hard subset (6–9 reasoning steps) with CoT prompting (50.00%, 56.40%, 52.20% respectively), while achieving 92–95% on the easy subset. CoT prompting yields an average improvement of 12.86% on easy, 8.43% on medium, but only 3.86% on hard problems. In the finetuning experiment, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct trained on ProverQA data achieves 97.00/90.60/68.20% on ProverQA easy/medium/hard (gains of +21.4/+44.0/+34.6%) and a +7.5% average OOD improvement across ProntoQA, ProofWriter, and FOLIO, substantially outperforming finetuning on ProofWriter (−2.0% OOD avg) or FOLIO (−11.4% OOD avg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProverQA is positioned against three existing FOL reasoning benchmarks: ProntoQA (template-based, Saparov & He 2022), ProofWriter (template-based, Tafjord et al. 2021), and FOLIO (manually curated, Han et al. 2022). In terms of difficulty, ProverQA-hard is substantially more challenging than all three: GPT-4o scores 100% on ProntoQA and 67.33% on ProofWriter with CoT, but only 50.00% on ProverQA-hard, indicating ProverQA avoids the performance saturation seen on simpler benchmarks. In the finetuning comparison (Table 4), ProverQA training data yields a +7.5% average OOD improvement for Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, compared to −2.0% for ProofWriter and −11.4% for FOLIO, demonstrating that ProverQA's diverse, prover-verified reasoning chains transfer more effectively to unseen FOL problems. The paper also shows ProverQA uniquely satisfies all four benchmark criteria (scalability, natural language diversity, symbolic representations, and faithful reasoning chains) simultaneously, whereas each prior dataset fails on at least one dimension (Table 1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: FOL reasoning benchmarks (ProntoQA, ProofWriter, RuleTaker, FOLIO) and symbolic-prover-augmented LLMs (Logic-LM, LINC, SOLA). The key conceptual departure is repurposing the symbolic prover (Prover9) from its traditional role as a *solver* (translating NL to symbolic form, then solving) into a *generator* of logically sound reasoning trees, while LLMs handle natural language diversity. Additionally, the framework adopts a top-down reasoning tree construction (goal → premises) in contrast to the bottom-up approach used by prior template-based datasets, which the authors argue avoids disconnected or contradictory premises.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProverQA addresses a real gap: existing FOL benchmarks are either too simple (template-based, nearly saturated by SOTA models) or too small and contamination-prone (manually curated like FOLIO). The neurosymbolic generation pipeline offers a scalable path to fresh, uncontaminated evaluation data, which is increasingly important as model training corpora grow. The finding that fine-tuning on ProverQA data yields the best OOD transfer (+7.5% avg) while FOLIO fine-tuning actually degrades OOD performance (−11.4%) suggests that reasoning-chain-rich synthetic data may be more transferable than manually curated but chain-free data. Open questions include whether the framework extends beyond FOL to modal or temporal logics, and whether the benchmark's difficulty ceiling will be broken as models improve.
