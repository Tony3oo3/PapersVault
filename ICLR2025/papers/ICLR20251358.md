## Problem

Deep neural networks trained on object classification appear to align similarly well with all three high-level visual streams (ventral, dorsal, lateral), contradicting the well-established functional dissociation among these pathways. This apparent paradox arises because standard alignment metrics (RSA, linear encoding) are rotation-invariant and thus blind to the specific axes of neural tuning that differentiate the streams. The paper asks: what actually distinguishes the representations in each stream, and can a new alignment measure reveal these differences?

## Input / Output

The method consumes fMRI voxel × stimulus response matrices (S×V) from four NSD subjects viewing 10,000 natural images, and DNN feature activations (unit × image) from AlexNet, ResNet-50, and ViT models evaluated on the same stimuli. It produces sparse component response matrices R (S×C) and voxel/unit weight matrices W (C×V) with C=20 components, Image Connectivity Matrices (S×S binary matrices indicating co-assignment to the same dominant component), and alignment scores (Pearson r on upper-triangular ICM entries) comparing brain streams to each model. Behavioral saliency ratings from 125 crowd-sourced subjects provide quantitative validation of component interpretations.

## Method

The core decomposition uses Bayesian NMF with exponential priors on both R and W, inferred via MCMC over N=50 iterations and aggregated through a consensus clustering procedure, yielding sparse, non-negative components that respect biological constraints (no negative responses, no orthogonality requirement). The key novelty is Sparse Component Alignment (SCA): rather than computing pairwise distances in the full neural space as RSA does, SCA constructs a binary Image Connectivity Matrix per NMF iteration where entry (i,j)=1 if stimuli i and j maximally load onto the same component, then averages these matrices across iterations and correlates upper-triangular entries between systems. This design retains sensitivity to the native tuning axes of each representation, making it detectable when two systems share similar component structure but differ in which specific axes are emphasized. Simulations confirm that SCA degrades gracefully with small rotations in the native axes while RSA remains invariant, and behavioral validation against the Meadows dataset shows ICMs capture behaviorally relevant information comparable to full RDMs.

## Results

Using pre-trained AlexNet as the reference model, SCA reveals a stark dissociation in brain-model alignment across visual streams: ventral r=0.187 vs. dorsal r=0.058 and lateral r=0.047, with the latter two approaching untrained-model baselines. In contrast, linear encoding yields more uniform alignment (dorsal r=0.232, lateral r=0.179, ventral r=0.180), and RSA shows a milder ventral preference (ventral r=0.347, lateral r=0.222, dorsal r=0.199). Behavioral alignment (Meadows dataset) confirms that ventral stream and task-optimized models best predict human similarity judgments, with SCA's connectivity matrices capturing similar patterns while maintaining sparser coding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Linear encoding (Yamins et al., 2014) reports near-equal alignment across all three streams (dorsal r=0.232, lateral r=0.179, ventral r=0.180), suggesting DNNs model all pathways comparably well. RSA (Kriegeskorte, 2008) shows a moderate ventral advantage (r=0.347) over dorsal (r=0.199) and lateral (r=0.222), but the gap remains relatively small. SCA, by contrast, reveals a dramatically larger ventral-to-dorsal/lateral gap (r=0.187 vs. 0.058/0.047), with dorsal and lateral alignment dropping to near-untrained levels. The paper argues this discrepancy arises because both linear encoding and RSA are rotation-invariant and thus insensitive to the specific axes of neural tuning that SCA explicitly captures. The Component Matching Score (CMS), which uses the same NMF components but enforces a strict 1-1 mapping, shows only modest similarity across streams, confirming that SCA's signal is not an artifact of the decomposition itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hypothesis-free voxel decomposition line of work (Norman-Haignere et al., 2015; Khosla et al., 2022) from the ventral stream alone to all three high-level visual pathways, using Bayesian NMF to recover sparse, interpretable components. It departs conceptually from the growing body of evidence (Finzi et al., 2024; Conwell et al., 2024) that image-trained DNNs fit dorsal and lateral streams as well as the ventral stream, arguing that this apparent equivalence is an artifact of the rotational invariance inherent in standard alignment measures like RSA and linear encoding. The proposed Sparse Component Alignment (SCA) metric is positioned as a complementary, tuning-sensitive alternative within the population-geometry family of alignment measures, rather than a replacement for existing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SCA reveals a stark ventral-specific alignment pattern—while RSA and linear encoding show near-uniform fit across all three streams—challenges the prevailing narrative that image-trained DNNs are good models of the entire human visual system. This reframing has practical implications: it suggests that video-trained networks, Bayesian social-cognition models, or other architectures may be needed to capture dorsal and lateral computations, and that current benchmarks may overstate the generality of object-recognition DNNs. Key limitations include the small subject count (n=4), reliance on static images (limiting motion-related dorsal/lateral signals), and the somewhat circular design of SCA around the very sparsity structure it aims to detect. Open questions include whether SCA's conclusions hold with dynamic stimuli, larger cohorts, and alternative decomposition methods.
