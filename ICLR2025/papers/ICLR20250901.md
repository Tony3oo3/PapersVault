## Problem

Simulation-based inference (SBI) methods require fully observed data, yet real datasets frequently contain missing values due to instrument limitations, data corruption, or incomplete observations. The paper formalizes the problem of missing data in SBI and shows that naive imputation strategies (e.g., zero-filling or mean-filling with a binary mask) introduce systematic bias in the estimated posterior, with the bias growing as the missingness proportion increases. Prior work either ignores the underlying missingness mechanism (MCAR/MAR/MNAR) or does not jointly learn imputation and inference, leaving a gap in robust, amortized SBI under missing data.

## Input / Output

During training, RISE consumes simulator-generated samples (x, θ) drawn from p(x|θ)p(θ), a binary missingness mask s created under a chosen mechanism (MCAR, MAR, or MNAR), and a missingness proportion ε. At inference time, the method takes a single real-world observation x_obs with its mask and produces both an amortized posterior q_φ(θ | x_obs, x_mis) via a masked autoregressive flow and a predictive distribution over the missing values via a latent neural process decoder. A meta-learning variant (RISE-Meta) additionally amortizes over ε, so a single trained model generalizes to unseen missingness levels without retraining.

## Method

RISE treats the missing values x_mis as latent variables and derives a variational lower bound (analogous to a VAE ELBO) that jointly optimizes an imputation network p̂_φ(x_mis|x_obs) and an NPE inference network q_φ(θ|x_obs, x_mis), yielding the loss ℓ_RISE = −E[log p̂_φ(x_mis|x_obs) + log q_φ(θ|x_obs, x_mis)]. The imputation model is parameterized as a latent neural process: a permutation-invariant encoder maps the observed context set (values + locations) to a latent z̃, and a decoder produces a Gaussian predictive distribution at each missing location, with Monte Carlo sampling over z̃ to approximate the marginal. The missingness mechanism is explicitly incorporated into the latent: for MCAR/MAR only z is needed, while for MNAR the mask s is appended to form z̃=(z,s) so the model accounts for value-dependent censoring. The inference network is a standard NPE-C with MAF, and the joint loss reduces to the standard NPE objective when no values are missing. RISE-Meta extends the training loop by sampling ε from a distribution over missingness proportions at each iteration, enabling a single model to handle arbitrary missingness levels at test time.

## Results

On four SBI benchmarks (GLU, GLM, Ricker, OUP) under MCAR and MNAR missingness at 10%, 25%, and 60%, RISE achieves the lowest C2ST scores in nearly all settings, e.g., 0.93 vs. 0.98 (NPE-NN) and 0.96 (Simformer) on GLU at 60% MCAR, and 0.41 vs. 0.57 (NPE-NN) on Ricker at 60% MNAR. RISE also attains the best (least negative) NLPP in most configurations, such as −6.21 vs. −6.62 (Simformer) on GLU at 60% MCAR. The performance gap over baselines widens with increasing missingness, and the advantage is most pronounced under MNAR where baselines do not model the missingness mechanism. On real-world bioactivity imputation, RISE achieves R² = 0.67 (Adrenergic) and 0.26 (Kinase), outperforming all baselines including Conduit (0.62/0.22) and CNP (0.65/0.24).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RISE outperforms NPE-NN (Lueckmann et al., 2017) on C2ST across all four SBI benchmarks and both missingness types, with the gap widening at 60% missingness (e.g., 0.93 vs. 0.98 on GLU MCAR; 0.41 vs. 0.57 on Ricker MNAR). Against Wang et al. (2024), which uses zero-augmentation with a binary mask, RISE consistently achieves lower C2ST and higher NLPP, confirming that naive constant imputation introduces posterior bias. Simformer (Gloeckler et al., 2024) is competitive at low missingness (10%) but RISE pulls ahead at 25% and 60%, particularly under MNAR where Simformer does not model the missingness mechanism (e.g., NLPP −7.11 vs. −7.56 on GLM at 60% MNAR). On the bioactivity imputation task, RISE achieves R² = 0.67 on Adrenergic and 0.26 on Kinase, beating Conduit (0.62/0.22), CNP (0.65/0.24), DNN (0.60/0.11), CMF (0.59/−0.11), and QSAR (N/A/−0.19), demonstrating that the NP-based imputation component generalizes well to out-of-distribution test data (Kinase contains outliers absent from training).

## Relation to Prior Work

RISE sits at the intersection of three research threads: neural posterior estimation (NPE) for likelihood-free inference, latent neural processes (LNPs) for probabilistic imputation, and the MIWAE-style latent variable factorization for missing data. It departs from prior SBI-under-missing-data work (Wang et al. 2024, Lueckmann et al. 2017a, Gloeckler et al. 2024) by treating imputation as a jointly-learned, amortized component of the inference pipeline rather than a preprocessing step, and by explicitly modeling all three missingness mechanisms (MCAR, MAR, MNAR) through the LNP's latent structure. The variational lower bound (Proposition 2) unifies the imputation and inference objectives into a single differentiable loss, reducing to standard NPE when no data is missing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Missing data is a pervasive practical obstacle in SBI applications (astrophysics, high-energy physics, computational biology), yet prior methods either ignore the missingness mechanism or rely on ad-hoc imputation that the paper formally shows biases the posterior. RISE's joint amortized framework and its meta-learning variant (RISE-Meta) offer a practical path to robust inference without retraining per missingness level. Key limitations remain: RISE inherits NPE's known calibration issues, the NP decoder's Gaussian assumption may limit expressivity for complex imputation distributions, and the interaction between model misspecification and missing data is left as future work.
