## Problem

In one-sided feedback settings (e.g., bank loans where repayment is only observed for approved applicants), the standard value function is undefined because the counterfactual outcome Y(0) does not exist, and the no-unmeasured-confounders assumption is inherently violated since action assignment depends on the unobserved potential outcome. Existing approaches either require costly exploration, are restricted to binary outcomes, or assume all covariates are shadow variables. This paper addresses the gap by providing a principled framework for decision evaluation and learning from purely observational data without NUC.

## Input / Output

The method consumes historical observational data where the outcome Y is only recorded when the action A=1 (e.g., loan approved), along with covariates X partitioned into U (action-relevant) and Z (shadow variables satisfying Z ⊥ A | U, Y(1) and Z ⊥ Y(1) | U). It produces a point estimate of the value function V_1(π) = E[Y(1)π(X)] for a given decision rule, and for learning, it outputs an optimal deterministic decision rule π* via a weighted classification formulation. Unlike standard off-policy evaluation, the input data is structurally incomplete—rejected cases contribute no outcome information—and the shadow variable partition is a required structural assumption rather than a modeling choice.

## Method

The paper defines a novel value function V_1(π) = E[Y(1)π(X)] that accounts for the undefined Y(0), then proves identification under a general action assignment mechanism φ(u,y) = P(A=1|U=u, Y(1)=y) by leveraging shadow variables and a conditional completeness assumption. Using semiparametric theory, it derives the efficient influence function (EIF) and efficiency bound, yielding a doubly-robust-style estimator that combines an IPW term with a conditional-expectation correction. For continuous outcomes, a two-step strategy avoids density estimation by constructing pseudo-outcomes and fitting them via regression (e.g., random forests or GAMs), while binary outcomes use a direct probability model. Finally, Proposition 5.3 transforms the optimal decision rule search into a weighted classification problem where each sample's label is I{ψ̂ > 0} and weight is |ψ̂|, enabling the use of off-the-shelf classifiers (e.g., depth-2 decision trees) for policy learning.

## Results

In the continuous-outcome simulation (Case 1, n=2000), the proposed efficient estimator V̂_eff achieves RMSE of 0.200–0.217 across three decision rules, substantially outperforming V̂_IPW-naive (RMSE 0.706–0.773) and V̂_IPW-eff (RMSE 0.256–0.312). The doubly robust (DR) estimator, while having lower SD (0.108–0.144), suffers from large bias (0.314–0.349) because the no-unmeasured-confounders assumption is violated. In the binary-outcome simulation (Case 2, n=2000), V̂_eff achieves RMSE of 0.0119–0.0163 with near-zero bias (|bias| ≤ 0.0013), again the best among all consistent estimators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: IPW with a naive (non-efficient) η estimator, IPW with the efficient η estimator, and the doubly robust (DR) estimator from Zhang et al. (2012) / Dudik et al. (2014). The proposed V̂_eff dominates all consistent baselines on RMSE and SD: in Case 1 (continuous outcome, n=2000), it reduces RMSE by ~3.4× versus V̂_IPW-naive (0.20 vs. 0.71) and by ~1.2× versus V̂_IPW-eff (0.20 vs. 0.26). The DR estimator, while achieving the lowest SD (0.108–0.144), is severely biased (0.31–0.35) because it relies on the NUC assumption, which is deliberately violated in the simulation; its RMSE (0.35–0.36) is roughly 1.7× that of V̂_eff. In Case 2 (binary outcome), the gaps are smaller but V̂_eff still achieves the lowest RMSE (0.0119–0.0163) and near-zero bias, while DR remains biased (0.017–0.019). In the real-data loan application, the proposed method yields a higher average testing value and lower variability than all three baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the shadow variable (SV) identification framework from Wang et al. (2014) and Miao et al. (2016) to the one-sided feedback setting, where one potential outcome is literally undefined rather than merely unobserved. It departs from prior one-sided feedback work (Jiang et al. 2021; Pacchiano et al. 2021), which was restricted to binary outcomes and online learning, by providing an off-policy evaluation and learning framework for both binary and continuous outcomes from observational data. Relative to the selective/non-random-missing labels literature (Sportisse et al. 2023; Hu et al. 2022), this paper relaxes the requirement that all covariates serve as SVs, allowing the missing mechanism to depend on both covariates and the outcome, and derives the semiparametrically efficient estimator via the EIF rather than a consistent-but-suboptimal one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically critical gap: in domains like lending, hiring, and admissions, outcomes are only observable for one action, and the standard NUC assumption is routinely violated. By providing a semiparametrically efficient estimator that requires only a subset of covariates as SVs and works for continuous outcomes without density estimation, the paper offers a principled tool for decision evaluation and learning where exploration is costly or unethical. Open questions include the practical difficulty of verifying the completeness assumption (Assumption 4.2), the restriction to binary actions, and whether the SV requirement can be further relaxed or automated. The classification-based learning reformulation (Proposition 5.3) is a practical contribution that enables use of off-the-shelf classifiers for policy optimization.
