## Problem

Standard diffusion models rely on a Markovian denoising process where each step only conditions on the immediately preceding noisy sample, preventing the model from leveraging the full generation trajectory. This restriction leads to training and inference inefficiencies, as individual denoising steps are unaware of prior generation context, and forces all information to be compressed into a single corrupted state at each noise level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253411/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes either a text prompt (encoded by a frozen T5-XL encoder and injected via cross-attention) or a class label (injected via AdaLN modulation) as conditioning, and produces a 256×256 RGB image (extendable to 512×512 and 1024×1024 via Matryoshka-DART). Images are represented as sequences of 256 continuous tokens in a pretrained VAE latent space (no quantization), with T=16 noise levels concatenated along the sequence dimension, yielding up to 4096 tokens per forward pass. In multimodal mode (Kaleido-DART), the model jointly outputs discrete text tokens and continuous image tokens in a single autoregressive sequence.

## Method

DART replaces the Markovian forward process of diffusion models with an independent noising process where each noisy sample x_t is conditionally independent given x_0, enabling the generator p_θ(x_{t-1} | x_{t:T}) to attend to the entire denoising trajectory via a chunk-based causal attention mask. The architecture is a standard decoder-only Transformer (RoPE, SwiGLU, no time embeddings) that autoregressively predicts the clean image mean at each noise level, trained with a weighted L2 loss analogous to standard diffusion. Two extensions improve expressiveness: DART-AR adds token-level autoregression within each denoising step (4096 AR steps total), and DART-FM appends a small 3-layer MLP flow-matching network that refines the Gaussian prediction at each step. Proposition 1 establishes a bijection between the non-Markovian schedule {γ_t} and any standard Markovian schedule {ᾱ_t}, allowing direct transfer of existing noise schedules and loss weightings.

## Results

On ImageNet-256 class-conditional generation, DART-FM achieves FID 3.82 with only 16 sampling steps (820M params), outperforming the reproduced 16-step baselines DiT* (FID 19.52), SiT* (FID 6.98), and MAR* (FID 6.37) by large margins. DART-AR reaches FID 3.98, surpassing MAR-AR (FID 4.69) and approaching LDM (FID 3.60 at 250 steps) and RQ-Transformer (FID 3.80 at 68 steps). On text-to-image generation (COCO), DART-FM achieves the best FID of 11.12 among all DART variants. The paper notes DART is trained for only 500K iterations versus 7M for DiT, and uses ~800M parameters versus 2B for VAR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the same 16-step budget, DART-FM (FID 3.82) dramatically outperforms reproduced DiT* (FID 19.52), SiT* (FID 6.98), and MAR* (FID 6.37), demonstrating the advantage of leveraging the full denoising trajectory. DART-AR (FID 3.98) beats the autoregressive baseline MAR-AR (FID 4.69) by 0.71 FID, validating token-level autoregressive denoising over masked modeling. Against full-budget baselines, DART-FM (16 steps) approaches LDM (FID 3.60, 250 steps) and essentially matches RQ-Transformer (FID 3.80, 68 steps, 3.8B params), while DiT at 250 steps (FID 2.27) and VAR (FID 1.73, 2B params) remain stronger but require substantially more compute or parameters. On text-to-image (COCO), DART-FM achieves FID 11.12, outperforming the in-house DiT baseline at both 16 and 250 steps. The paper emphasizes that DART is trained for only 500K iterations versus 7M for DiT, and the largest DART model (~812M) is smaller than VAR (2B) and MAR (943M), suggesting further gains with more training and scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

DART reinterprets diffusion models through the hierarchical VAE lens (Kingma et al., 2021; Kingma & Welling, 2013), identifying the Markovian assumption as the key simplification that can be relaxed without sacrificing the denoising objective. It then implements the resulting non-Markovian generator with a standard autoregressive Transformer (following DiT's VAE-latent patchification and LLaMA-style RoPE/SwiGLU design), bridging the conceptual gap between diffusion and AR generation. This is distinct from concurrent unification efforts (Transfusion, Show-o, Monoformer) that adapt LM architectures to standard Markovian diffusion without modifying the underlying generative process. The DART-FM variant further incorporates flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) as a lightweight refinement on top of the Gaussian denoising prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that the Markovian constraint is not necessary for high-quality generation and that seeing the full denoising trajectory improves efficiency, especially at low step counts—could reshape how future generative architectures are designed. However, practical limitations temper the impact: DART-AR's 238-second inference time (vs. 2.4s for vanilla DART) makes it impractical, and all experiments are at 256×256 with only 500K training steps, leaving open whether the advantage persists at higher resolutions and with full-scale training. The unified text+image generation (Kaleido-DART) and multi-resolution extension (Matryoshka-DART) point toward a single-model paradigm for diverse generation tasks, but these remain preliminary. A key open question is whether the non-Markovian formulation can be combined with long-context architectures to enable video generation without the memory blowup that currently limits T.
