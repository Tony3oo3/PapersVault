## Problem

Existing dexterous grasping policies are tailored to specific robotic hands, and developing a single universal policy that controls diverse dexterous hands—varying in finger count, degrees of freedom, joint limits, and link geometry—remains largely unexplored. The core difficulty is that hand-specific joint positions in both observations and actions cannot be directly aligned across embodiments, and contact-rich grasping further complicates transfer due to differences in hand size and shape.

## Input / Output

The policy takes as input the 6-DoF arm joint positions, the 3D positions of the palm and first four fingertips (deliberately excluding hand-specific joint angles to maintain embodiment-agnostic observations), a 512-point object point cloud (processed by a simplified PointNet), and the previous arm and hand actions. It outputs 6-dimensional arm joint position targets and k-dimensional eigengrasp weights (typically k=5), which are linearly combined with PCA eigenvectors of MANO hand poses to produce a human hand pose, then retargeted to the specific hand's joint targets via a learned neural network. For hands with fewer than four fingers, unused fingertip positions are zero-padded.

## Method

CrossDex unifies the action space using eigengrasps—PCA eigenvectors of 45-dimensional MANO hand poses from the GRAB dataset—so the policy outputs embodiment-agnostic hand poses that are mapped to each robot hand's joint positions through DexPilot-style retargeting. To accelerate training in IsaacGym's 8,192 parallel environments, the iterative optimization-based retargeting is replaced by per-hand 4-layer MLPs (512 hidden dims) trained on retargeting labels, enabling batch computation. The observation space is simplified to only fingertip and palm 3D positions, removing hand-specific joint angles that would hinder cross-embodiment transfer. Training follows a teacher-student framework: state-based PPO policies are learned per object group across all four training hands simultaneously (co-training across embodiments), then distilled into a single vision-based policy via DAgger with behavior cloning. Embodiment randomization (Gaussian noise on the hand-arm mounting joint) further enhances robustness and zero-shot transfer to unseen hands.

## Results

CrossDex achieves an 80.0% average success rate on the four training hands (vision-based policy) and 35.2% on two unseen hands (LEAP Hand and Inspire Hand), outperforming all baselines across every metric. On the training hands, CrossDex's state-based policy reaches 88.5% success, comparable to the best baseline MT-Raw-OA (91.4%). The most striking result is zero-shot generalization: CrossDex's vision-based policy on unseen hands (35.2%) nearly doubles the best baseline MT-Raw-A (21.0%), while MT-Raw-OA and MT-Raw-O collapse to 16.2% and 14.5% respectively. In finetuning experiments (Table 2), CrossDex also leads on all three settings, reaching 87.2% on 5 YCB objects (state), 64.3% on all YCB objects (vision), and 74.0% on 55 unseen GRAB objects (vision).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CrossDex against three multi-task RL baselines that differ in how they handle observations and actions: MT-Raw-OA (full proprioception + raw joint actions + embodiment one-hot labels), MT-Raw-A (unified fingertip/palm observations + raw joint actions), and MT-Raw-O (full proprioception + eigengrasp actions). CrossDex wins on all four evaluation metrics. On training hands, all methods achieve >80% state-based success, but CrossDex's vision-based policy (80.0%) edges out MT-Raw-OA (78.2%) and MT-Raw-O (77.9%). The decisive gap appears on unseen hands: CrossDex's vision-based policy reaches 35.2%, while MT-Raw-A (the closest competitor) manages only 21.0%, and MT-Raw-OA/MT-Raw-O drop to 16.2%/14.5%. The paper attributes this to MT-Raw-OA and MT-Raw-O relying on embodiment one-hot labels during state-based training, which are removed during vision-based distillation, causing a large performance drop. MT-Raw-A, which already uses unified observations, generalizes better than the other two baselines but still lags CrossDex by ~14 percentage points on unseen hands, underscoring the benefit of the eigengrasp action space over raw joint-position actions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the teacher-student RL framework from UniDexGrasp (Xu et al., 2023) and the DexPilot retargeting method (Handa et al., 2020) to a cross-embodiment setting, using eigengrasps (Ciocarlie et al., 2007) of the MANO hand model as a universal action interface. While prior dexterous grasping work (DexPoint, UniDexGrasp, UniDexGrasp++) learns policies for a single specific hand, and cross-embodiment learning has been applied to robot arms and navigation, no prior work has unified control across structurally diverse dexterous hands. The key departure is replacing hand-specific joint actions with low-dimensional human eigengrasp actions and hand-specific proprioception with embodiment-agnostic fingertip/palm positions, drawing an explicit analogy to human teleoperation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CrossDex demonstrates that a single vision-based policy can grasp 45 YCB objects across four different dexterous hands at 80% success and transfer zero-shot to two unseen hands at ~35-39%, offering a practical path toward reducing the per-embodiment cost of deploying dexterous manipulation. The technique of using human eigengrasps plus learned retargeting networks as a universal action space is likely to be adopted by the dexterous manipulation community. Key limitations include simulation-only training with limited real-world validation (a single demo), only four training hands, and restriction to static grasping rather than in-hand reorientation or dynamic handover, leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex manipulation tasks and larger embodiment diversity.
