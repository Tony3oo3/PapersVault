## Problem

Off-policy learning (OPL) for contextual bandits in large action spaces faces a fundamental bias-variance dilemma: policy-based methods (IPS, DR) collapse under excessive variance from importance weighting over millions of actions, while regression-based methods suffer from high bias due to reward model misspecification. No existing OPL method simultaneously controls both bias and variance when the action space is large and structured.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252080/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes logged bandit data D = {(x_i, a_i, r_i)} drawn from a logging policy π_0, along with a pre-trained regression model q̂(x,a) used to form action clusters (via KMeans on averaged estimated rewards). It produces a two-stage policy: a 1st-stage cluster-selection policy π_θ^1st over a compact cluster space C (|C| ≪ |A|) and a 2nd-stage action-selection policy π_ψ^2nd that picks the best action within the sampled cluster. At inference, an incoming context x first yields a cluster sample, then a specific action within that cluster, equivalent to sampling from the overall policy π^overall(a|x).

## Method

POTEC decomposes the overall policy into a 1st-stage cluster-selection policy and a 2nd-stage within-cluster action-selection policy, trained with fundamentally different approaches. The 1st-stage policy is learned via a novel POTEC gradient estimator that applies importance weighting only in the cluster space (w(x,c) = π_θ^1st(c|x)/π_0(c|x)) rather than the full action space, yielding substantially lower variance; its bias is governed by a pairwise regression model f̂ that only needs to preserve relative reward differences within each cluster (local correctness), a much weaker requirement than global reward accuracy. The 2nd-stage policy is constructed directly from the pairwise regression model f̂_ψ, selecting argmax f̂_ψ(x,a) among actions in the sampled cluster. Theoretically, POTEC is unbiased under local correctness and provides a strict generalization of both policy-based (|C|=|A|) and regression-based (|C|=1) approaches, with the number of clusters as a tunable hyperparameter interpolating between the two extremes.

## Results

On synthetic data (|A|=500, |C|=30, n=4,000), POTEC achieves the highest normalized policy value across all training data sizes, with the advantage growing as n increases; baselines (IPS-PG, DR-PG, Reg-based) remain consistently inferior. When the action space expands to |A|≥2,000, baseline methods perform worse than the logging policy, while POTEC (even with heuristically learned clusters) maintains a clear advantage. On the real-world KuaiRec dataset (1,411 users, 3,317 videos), POTEC is the most sample-efficient method, outperforming all baselines particularly at small training sizes, and remains robust even when the ratio of deficient actions grows large, where DR-PG degrades significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POTEC is compared against three baselines: IPS-PG (Swaminathan & Joachims, 2015), DR-PG (Dudik et al., 2014), and a regression-based method. POTEC outperforms all three baselines in every experimental setting tested (varying training data sizes, action space sizes, cluster counts, and deficient action ratios). DR-PG is consistently the strongest baseline but is still substantially outperformed by POTEC, especially in large action spaces (|A|≥2,000) where DR-PG falls below the logging policy value. IPS-PG suffers the most from high variance in large action spaces and is the weakest baseline overall. The regression-based method avoids variance issues but is limited by bias from reward model misspecification, making it inferior to POTEC which only requires local correctness (preserving relative reward differences within clusters) rather than globally accurate reward estimation. On KuaiRec, as the ratio of deficient actions increases, DR-PG degrades noticeably while POTEC maintains its advantage, confirming that the milder full-cluster-support condition (Condition 3.1) is practically beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

POTEC repurposes the conjunct effect model (CEM) from the off-policy evaluation literature (Saito et al., 2023) — which decomposes rewards into cluster and residual effects for OPE — and applies it to the off-policy *learning* problem via a novel two-stage policy decomposition. The first-stage policy gradient estimator inherits the cluster importance weighting idea from MIPS/OffCEM but is adapted for policy optimization, while the second-stage policy leverages pairwise regression to satisfy a local correctness condition. The paper positions itself as the first OPL framework specifically designed for large discrete action spaces, unifying policy-based (IPS/DR) and regression-based approaches as special cases of its two-stage decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POTEC addresses a critical practical gap: existing OPL methods collapse in large action spaces (millions of items in recommendation) due to either excessive variance (policy-based) or bias (regression-based), yet no principled method existed for this regime. The local correctness condition is a meaningful relaxation of full support, and the theoretical variance reduction is clean. However, the reliance on heuristic action clustering (KMeans on estimated rewards) is acknowledged as a limitation, and the method has not been extended beyond contextual bandits to offline RL or LLM settings. Open questions include whether context-dependent or learned clustering can further improve performance and whether the framework transfers to sequential decision-making.
