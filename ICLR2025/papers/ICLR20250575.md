## Problem

While LLM-based chatbots are increasingly deployed as personal assistants, there is no comprehensive benchmark measuring whether they can proactively infer, remember, and apply user preferences across multi-session, long-context conversations. Existing personalization benchmarks focus on single-turn tasks, stylistic attributes, or explicit profile conditioning, leaving unexplored the harder problem of tracking implicit preferences amid unrelated dialogue spanning up to 100k tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250575/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance is a multi-session conversation in which a user preference (stated explicitly in one sentence, inferred from a 2-turn choice-based exchange, or subtly revealed within a 4–8 turn persona-driven dialogue) is followed by unrelated distractor turns drawn from LMSYS-Chat-1M (up to 100k tokens) and a final query constructed so that a generic response would violate the preference. The model produces either a free-form generated response (scored by four independent Claude 3 Sonnet evaluators on binary metrics aggregated into four disjoint error types) or a single selected option from a four-way MCQ, enabling both detailed error analysis and fast automatic scoring.

## Method

PREFEVAL comprises 3,000 manually curated preference-query pairs (1,000 unique pairs × 3 preference forms) across 20 daily-life topics, with unrelated multi-session context turns interleaved between preference disclosure and query to stress long-range retrieval. The evaluation protocol tests five methods—zero-shot, a one-sentence Reminder prompt, Self-Critic (generate-critique-revise), 5-shot Chain-of-Thought, and RAG using SimCSE sentence embeddings to retrieve top-5 relevant exchanges—across 10 LLMs (Claude 3/3.5, Mistral 7B/8×7B, LLaMA 3 8B/70B, GPT-4o, o1-preview, Gemini-1.5-Pro) at context lengths from ~3k to ~103k tokens. A key finding is that a trivial Reminder prompt outperforms more elaborate Self-Critic and CoT strategies, while RAG is most effective for models with weaker intrinsic long-context retrieval. The authors further demonstrate that supervised fine-tuning Mistral-7B on 80% of PREFEVAL topics (with 0, 5, or 10 interleaved context turns) yields significant zero-shot gains on the unseen 20% and improves length generalization, with attention analysis showing up to 4.97% increased attention to preference-related tokens.

## Results

On PREFEVAL, zero-shot preference-following accuracy drops below 10% at just 10 turns (~3k tokens) for most models (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet: 7%, GPT-4o: 7%, Gemini 1.5 Pro: 7%), and falls near zero by 300 turns (~103k tokens). The Reminder prompting method recovers accuracy to 45–100% at 10 turns but still degrades to 2–98% at 300 turns depending on the model. RAG (top-5 retrieval) is the strongest method overall, while fine-tuning Mistral-7B on PREFEVAL surpasses all prompting baselines and generalizes to 70-turn contexts. The classification (MCQ) task shows a 0.73 correlation with generation-task accuracy, validating it as a cheaper proxy metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five methods (Zero-shot, Reminder, Self-Critic, Few-Shot CoT, RAG) across 10 LLMs. RAG (Lewis et al., 2020) consistently achieves the highest preference-following accuracy across most models, confirming that retrieval capability is the bottleneck; however, in Claude 3 Sonnet and Mistral 8x7b, the simple Reminder prompt matches or exceeds RAG, suggesting strong intrinsic long-context retrieval. Self-Critic (Huang et al., ICLR 2024) underperforms Reminder in most settings and, in the classification task, actually degrades below zero-shot because the revision step hallucinates preferences. Among proprietary models, o3 achieves the best zero-shot score (71% at 10 turns) but still drops to 5% at 300 turns; GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro are near-zero (7%) in zero-shot at 10 turns, while Claude 3.5 Sonnet is also at 7% and fails to recover even with Reminder at 300 turns (2%). Fine-tuning Mistral-7B on PREFEVAL (SFT) surpasses all prompting and RAG baselines in zero-shot evaluation and shows improved length generalization, with attention to preference regions increasing by up to 4.97%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM personalization benchmarks and long-context evaluation. Prior personalization benchmarks (LAMP, RPBench, TIMECHARA, RoleLLM) focus on stylistic preferences, character role-playing, or single-turn tasks, while long-context benchmarks (LongBench, ∞Bench, Loogle) test needle-in-a-haystack retrieval. PREFEVAL departs from both by defining preference following as a distinct multi-session, long-context task that requires inference (explicit and implicit), retrieval across distractor turns, and proactive application—rather than simple fact retrieval or style mimicry. The evaluation methods (RAG, self-critique, CoT, reminder prompting) are repurposed from prior work, but the task formulation and error taxonomy are novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PREFEVAL exposes a critical and practically important gap: even frontier models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro) achieve below 10% preference-following accuracy in zero-shot settings at just 10 turns, undermining the viability of current LLMs as personalized assistants. The benchmark provides a standardized, reproducible resource (3,000 pairs, 20 topics, generation + classification tasks) that the field can use to track progress. Key open questions remain: how to make LLMs proactively attend to preferences without explicit prompting, how to handle genuinely evolving or contradictory preference histories, and whether synthetic preference pairs adequately capture the nuance of real user interactions. The finding that fine-tuning on PREFEVAL substantially closes the gap suggests the capability is learnable but not yet internalized by base models.
