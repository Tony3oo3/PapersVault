## Problem

Existing customized image generation methods, whether tuning-based or tuning-free, fail to accurately disentangle subject-essential attributes from subject-irrelevant ones (pose, background, image tone) in single-image settings. This entanglement causes irrelevant factors to infiltrate generation, simultaneously degrading editability (instruction adherence) and ID consistency. The paper tackles this by explicitly separating and enriching subject-essential features in a tuning-free, single-reference-image framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image and a free-form text prompt as input, producing a customized image that preserves the subject's identity while following the textual instruction. No per-subject fine-tuning or multiple reference images are required. The reference image is first augmented (to prevent mere duplication), then encoded at 256×256 for disentanglement and 512×512 for enrichment; inference runs on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with DDIM (50 steps, CFG scale 5.0) in approximately 1.96 seconds on a single A800 GPU.

## Method

DisEnvisioner is a two-stage tuning-free pipeline built on Stable Diffusion v1.5. The DisVisioner stage uses an image tokenizer with spatial-wise cross-attention to aggregate CLIP image features into exactly one subject-essential token and one subject-irrelevant token, enforced as mutually orthogonal sets via softmax over the spatial dimension; queries are initialized with CLIP priors (random vectors for subject, class-name embeddings for irrelevant). The EnVisioner stage then projects the single subject token into four granular tokens via a dedicated projector and injects them through a separate cross-attention branch decoupled from both the text condition and the irrelevant-feature branch, with the irrelevant branch zeroed out (λ_i=0) at inference. Training proceeds in two sequential phases—first the DisVisioner (120K steps, frozen SD, only to_k/to_v trainable), then the EnVisioner (120K steps, DisVisioner frozen, only projectors and new attention layers trainable)—both using the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss with a reconstruction target.

## Results

On the DreamBooth evaluation set (30 subjects, 158 images, 25 editing prompts each), DisEnvisioner achieves the best text-alignment (C-T = 0.315) and lowest internal variance (IV = 0.026), indicating superior editability and resistance to subject-irrelevant factors. It also attains the best mean rank (mRank = 2.0) across all six metrics. Inference time is 1.96 s per image on a single A800 GPU, comparable to other tuning-free methods (BLIP-Diffusion: 1.10 s; IP-Adapter: 1.98 s) but orders of magnitude faster than tuning-based baselines (DreamBooth: 1120 s; DisenBooth: 2420 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisEnvisioner outperforms all five baselines on editability (C-T: 0.315 vs. 0.275–0.303) and on internal variance (IV: 0.026 vs. 0.029–0.041), confirming that its disentanglement mechanism effectively removes subject-irrelevant factors like pose and background. However, it trails IP-Adapter (C-I = 0.883, D-I = 0.912) and DreamBooth (C-I = 0.842, D-I = 0.849) on raw ID-consistency metrics; the authors attribute this to those methods over-replicating the reference image at the cost of editability. Among tuning-free methods, DisEnvisioner beats BLIP-Diffusion (C-T 0.315 vs. 0.295; IV 0.026 vs. 0.029) and ELITE (C-T 0.315 vs. 0.287; IV 0.026 vs. 0.036) on both editability and robustness, while matching IP-Adapter's inference speed (1.96 s vs. 1.98 s). The overall mRank (2.0) is the best among all methods, reflecting a balanced advantage across editability, ID consistency, robustness, and speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisEnvisioner builds on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion and the CLIP-based visual encoding paradigm, but departs sharply from prior tuning-free customization methods (IP-Adapter, PhotoVerse, BLIP-Diffusion, ELITE) that either treat the entire global image feature as subject-essential or rely on implicit multi-layer feature separation. Its core architectural lineage traces to the image tokenizer (Wu et al., 2022) for compact visual token aggregation, repurposed here with spatial-wise cross-attention to explicitly disentangle subject-essential from subject-irrelevant tokens. The two-stage pipeline (disentangle then enrich) echoes the structure of BLIP-Diffusion and ELITE, but replaces their heuristic feature extraction with a dedicated trainable tokenizer and a projector-based enrichment module, while remaining fully tuning-free and single-image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important failure mode in customized generation—irrelevant attributes (pose, background, tone) leaking into the output and degrading both editability and ID consistency—by introducing an explicit disentanglement mechanism that is lightweight and requires no test-time fine-tuning. The novel Internal Variance (IV) metric provides a useful diagnostic for measuring sensitivity to irrelevant factors, a dimension prior work largely ignored. Open questions include whether the 1+1 token disentanglement generalizes to multi-subject scenes or more complex compositions, whether the approach transfers to newer diffusion backbones (SDXL, DiT-based models), and how it performs when the reference image contains occlusions or multiple overlapping subjects.
