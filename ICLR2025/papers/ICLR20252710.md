## Problem

LLMs struggle with long-context reasoning not only due to quadratic attention complexity but also because of the scarcity and cost of annotating long-context instruction data. No open-source instruction-tuning dataset exceeds 100K tokens, creating a significant gap between theoretical context-length claims and practical long-context capabilities. This paper addresses the data bottleneck by proposing a scalable synthetic data generation strategy that is unconstrained by the length of available real-world documents.

## Input / Output

For data generation, the pipeline takes raw long-form documents (books from the Together long books dataset, ~1.4B tokens total) and produces 582,900 hierarchical and diverse QA pairs organized into training samples of 180K, 350K, 650K, and 1M tokens by concatenating 2, 4, 8, and 12 documents respectively. For inference, the trained model takes a long-context document (up to 1M tokens) interleaved with questions and produces answers, with loss computed solely on answer tokens during training (questions and context are masked). The generated QA pairs follow a structured format where each document is followed by its summary, then hierarchical follow-up questions, then diverse specific-detail questions, with cross-document reference questions inserted between documents.

## Method

The core pipeline splits each document into 4K-token small chunks grouped into 12K-token medium sections, generates hierarchical summaries at each level using a short-context LLM (Qwen-2-72B), and then produces QA pairs in a hierarchical order (global summary → medium chunk → small chunk) that alternates between exploiting the current chunk and exploring new ones. To scale beyond single-document length, multiple documents are concatenated with a fixed schedule: N₁=5 hierarchical + 5 diverse QAs after each document, N₂=9 diverse QAs sampled from all previously visited documents, and N₃=3 hierarchical QAs revisiting prior documents with 60% probability. The model is trained via stepwise RoPE scaling (180K → 350K → 650K → 1M tokens) on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct at a 6e-5 learning rate for 1 epoch per stage, using FSDP and DeepSpeed Ulysses sequence parallelism. Ablations confirm that combining hierarchical ordering with diverse question types and fixed question counts (vs. randomized) yields the best long-context performance, and that the approach generalizes across generator model sizes (72B, 7B, and same-size 8B).

## Results

On RULER, the 1M model achieves 62.95% accuracy at 1M tokens and 82.05% at 131K tokens, substantially outperforming zero-shot RoPE scaling (48.81% at 1M) and the base LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (28.93% at 524K). On InfiniteBench (100K context), the 1M model scores 54.80 average, surpassing LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (51.31) and the Gradient AI 1M model (41.04). On LongBench (10K context), the 1M model retains 46.42 average (vs. 48.11 for the base model), and on MMLU it achieves 65.08 (vs. 68.21 for the base), showing minimal regression in general capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two primary baselines: the base LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct model and the Gradient AI 1M-context model (gradientai/Llama-3-8B-Instruct-Gradient-1048k, trained on 1.4B tokens). On RULER, the 1M model dramatically outperforms both: at 1M tokens it reaches 62.95% vs. 48.81% for zero-shot RoPE scaling and 28.93% for the base model at 524K. On InfiniteBench, all four context-length variants (180K–1M) beat both baselines, with the 1M model scoring 54.80 vs. 51.31 (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct) and 41.04 (Gradient AI). On LongBench, the 1M model (46.42) slightly trails the base LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (48.11) but decisively outperforms Gradient AI (35.89). On MMLU, the 1M model (65.08) shows a modest ~3-point drop from the base (68.21) while Gradient AI degrades more severely to 60.48, confirming that the hierarchical synthetic data strategy preserves general capabilities far better than the competing 1M-context training recipe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RoPE scaling literature (Positional Interpolation, NTK-aware scaling) for extending context windows, but departs from prior work by addressing the data bottleneck: most long-context extensions rely on limited natural long-form data or simple concatenation of short sequences. The synthetic data generation pipeline draws on the Self-Instruct/WizardLM lineage of LLM-generated instruction data and the multi-document concatenation idea from Xiong et al. (2023), but introduces a hierarchical ordering strategy (global → medium → small chunks) and a diverse question-type pool that no prior open-source long-context dataset has systematically combined. The stepwise RoPE scaling training schedule (180K → 350K → 650K → 1M) is a practical training recipe rather than a new positional encoding method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open-source, systematically ablated pipeline for generating instruction-tuning data beyond 100K tokens, filling a concrete gap between theoretical context-length claims and available training data. The finding that 7B/8B generator models produce data of comparable quality to a 72B model makes the approach accessible to resource-constrained groups. Open questions include whether the hierarchical strategy generalizes to non-narrative domains (code, structured data), whether the self-evolutionary prompt adaptation the authors envision can close the remaining gap to 72B-quality data, and how the approach interacts with architectural innovations like linear attention or state-space models that may reduce the need for such data-heavy training.
