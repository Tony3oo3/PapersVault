## Problem

Existing backdoor attack research on LLMs (e.g., BadChain, AdvICL) targets in-context learning or general reasoning tasks but fails to address the unique security vulnerabilities introduced when LLMs are fine-tuned for embodied decision-making and augmented with RAG in safety-critical cyberphysical systems. The integration of domain-specific fine-tuning, retrieval-augmented generation, and physical-world grounding creates multiple unexplored attack surfaces that, if exploited, could cause physical harm such as vehicles accelerating into obstacles or robots placing knives on beds.

## Input / Output

The system consumes textual scenario descriptions (generated via rule-based templates for HighwayEnv or GPT-4V for CARLA RGB images), a task-specific system prompt, ICL demonstrations, and optionally retrieved knowledge from a Sentence-BERT-based RAG database. The LLM outputs a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a structured decision: for driving, a discrete action label (e.g., 'Acceleration', 'Turn Left', 'Right Lane'); for robotics, executable Python code with sequential simulator API calls (walk, find, grab, putin) that drives the agent in VirtualHome.

## Method

BALD proposes three backdoor attack mechanisms targeting different pipeline components: (1) BALD-word injects rare trigger words into query prompts, first optimized via ICL as an implicit fine-tuning proxy (leveraging the duality between attention and gradient descent) before being embedded into the model via LoRA fine-tuning; (2) BALD-scene uses Scenic to sample physical scenarios, a VLM to generate natural-language descriptions, and an LLM rewriter with contrastive reasoning templates—distinct formats for backdoor vs. boundary scenarios—to fine-tune the model to misbehave only in specific environmental conditions without any runtime system intrusion; (3) BALD-RAG employs dual triggers (fine-grained scenario descriptions for retrieval and embedded word triggers for activation) to poison the RAG knowledge base. The key empirical finding is that fine-tuning-stage attacks achieve near-100% ASR while ICL-stage attacks (BadChain) drop to 5–48%, and the contrastive reasoning design is critical for simultaneously achieving high attack success rate and low false alarm rate on boundary scenarios.

## Results

BALD-word achieves 100% attack success rate (ASR) across all three models (GPT-3.5, LLaMA2, PaLM2) on both HighwayEnv and nuScenes, while maintaining benign accuracy within 1–3% of the benign fine-tuned baseline. BALD-scene achieves ASR ranging from 36% to 100% (average ~78%) with false alarm rates (FAR) between 2% and 42%, and requires no runtime system intrusion. BALD-RAG achieves 95–100% ASR on HighwayEnv and 100% conditioned ASR on nuScenes when the poisoned knowledge is successfully retrieved. Notably, only 7.5% poison data injection is sufficient to achieve near-100% ASR for word-based attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is BadChain (Xiang et al., 2024), an in-context-learning backdoor attack that poisons ICL demonstrations with malicious examples. BALD-word dominates BadChain by a wide margin: on HighwayEnv, BALD-word achieves 100% ASR versus BadChain's 12.9% (GPT-3.5), 48.4% (LLaMA2), and 5.6% (PaLM2); on nuScenes, the gap is similarly large (100% vs. 22.0%, 26.0%, and 10.0% respectively). BALD-scene also outperforms BadChain on every model–dataset combination, reaching up to 100% ASR (PaLM2 on HighwayEnv) versus BadChain's 5.6%. The paper attributes BadChain's poor performance to the fact that task-specific fine-tuning substantially hardens models against ICL-stage attacks, whereas BALD targets the fine-tuning stage itself. On the robotics (VirtualHome) task, BadChain achieves only 20% ASR while BALD-word and BALD-RAG both reach 100%, further confirming the advantage of fine-tuning-stage attacks over ICL-stage attacks in embodied settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor attack literature on LLMs (BadChain, AdvICL) from the in-context learning stage to the fine-tuning stage, leveraging the theoretical insight that ICL approximates few-shot fine-tuning (Dai et al., 2023) to decouple trigger optimization from model adaptation. It also builds on RAG poisoning (Zou et al., 2024) but departs by using a dual-trigger design (scenario for retrieval + word for activation) rather than injecting factually incorrect knowledge. The key conceptual departure is treating the embodied decision-making pipeline—spanning LLM, RAG database, and physical environment—as a multi-component attack surface, rather than targeting the LLM in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM-based decision-making systems are increasingly deployed in safety-critical embodied agents (autonomous vehicles, home robots), this work demonstrates that the fine-tuning stage—a step widely assumed to improve reliability—actually introduces a severe new vulnerability: only 7.5% poisoned data achieves near-100% attack success, and scenario-based triggers require no runtime system access. The limited effectiveness of inference-stage defenses (benign ICL demonstrations, outlier word removal) against fine-tuning-stage backdoors raises urgent open questions about how to certify or audit third-party fine-tuned models. Limitations include simulation-only evaluation, small datasets, and the lack of established benchmarks for LLM-based embodied decision-making, leaving the real-world transferability of these attacks uncertain.
