## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of transformers via neural ODEs rely on weight-sharing assumptions that predict output clustering at infinite depth, contradicting the flexible next-token prediction behavior of practical autoregressive models. Prior neural ODE transformer implementations either share weights across layers (degrading performance) or fail to fully exploit the adaptive computation inherent in differential equations. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a fully time-dependent weight formulation that avoids clustering while enabling flexible architectural adaptation during fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a standard token sequence and produces next-token predictions, but its continuous-time formulation means the effective number of layers is determined by the ODE solver's step size rather than a fixed architecture. Beyond standard outputs, DIFFEQFORMER generates token-level Lyapunov sensitivity values quantifying how perturbations in the i-th input token propagate to the j-th output token, and exposes the spectral dynamics of QK and OV weight pairs as smooth functions of layer index. A pretrained model can be reconfigured post-hoc by simply changing the solver step size, transforming it into architectures with different depths (e.g., 18→9 or 18→24 layers) without retraining from scratch.

## Method

DIFFEQFORMER formulates each transformer block as a non-autonomous ODE where multi-head attention captures inter-particle interactions and a GeLU feedforward layer acts as convection, with all weights (Q, K, V, W₁, W₂) generated as functions of continuous time t via hypernetworks. Each weight is computed as W(t) = Proj(MLP(Sinusoidal(t))), where the sinusoidal embedding maps time into a 128-dimensional Fourier space, a two-layer SiLU MLP introduces nonlinearity, and a linear projection reshapes to the target weight dimensions. Spectral analysis reveals that eigenvalue magnitudes of QK and OV pairs increase toward the final layers, which the authors demonstrate through trajectory simulation prevents the clustering predicted by weight-sharing theory. For interpretability, the paper derives token-level Lyapunov exponents from the Jacobian of the vector field, capturing both attention-mediated information flow and input-output changes in a single principled quantity. The adaptive fine-tuning capability arises naturally from the ODE formulation: a model pretrained with 18 solver steps can be fine-tuned with 9, 12, or 24 steps, effectively changing its depth while reusing the learned time-dependent weight functions, and remains compatible with standard techniques like LoRA.

## Results

On OpenWebText, DIFFEQFORMER consistently outperforms GPT baselines across all model scales: 22.06 vs 22.60 (small), 17.67 vs 17.85 (medium), 15.43 vs 15.64 (large), and 9.68 vs 10.17 perplexity vs Llama-1B. On WikiText-103, DIFFEQFORMER achieves 21.94 perplexity (medium) versus 23.38 for GPT-medium, while the shared-weight baseline degrades to 32.95. In downstream zero-shot evaluation, DIFFEQFORMER shows statistically significant gains over GPT-large on Lambada OpenAI (38.39 vs 37.24) and Lambada Standard (30.37 vs 29.30), and over Llama-1B on both Lambada benchmarks (43.74 vs 41.84 and 31.55 vs 27.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFEQFORMER outperforms all four baselines on the primary language modeling metric (perplexity). Against GPT (Radford, 2018), it wins at every scale on both OpenWebText and WikiText-103, with the largest margin at medium scale (21.94 vs 23.38 on WikiText-103). Against GPT-3-scale GPT-large (Brown et al., 2020), the improvement is modest but consistent (15.43 vs 15.64 on OpenWebText), and DIFFEQFORMER additionally shows statistically significant downstream gains on reading comprehension tasks. Against Llama-1B (Touvron et al., 2023), DIFFEQFORMER achieves a notable 0.49 perplexity improvement (9.68 vs 10.17) and 2–4% accuracy gains on Lambada benchmarks. Against the shared-weight neural ODE transformer of Zhong et al. (2022), DIFFEQFORMER's time-dependent weights yield a dramatic improvement (22.84 vs 36.27 perplexity at small scale; 21.94 vs 32.95 at medium), confirming that weight-sharing is a major bottleneck for ODE-style transformers in autoregressive settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural ODE formulation of transformers initiated by Lu et al. (2019) and the theoretical particle-system analyses of Geshkovski et al. (2023a,b), but departs from them by replacing the weight-sharing assumption with time-dependent weights generated via hypernetworks with Fourier time embeddings (inspired by diffusion model architectures). While prior neural ODE transformer works (Zhong et al., 2022; Dutta et al., 2021) explored weight-sharing or partial time-dependence, DIFFEQFORMER parameterizes all attention and feed-forward weights as continuous functions of layer index, enabling full non-autonomous ODE dynamics. The spectral analysis framework connects to the gradient-flow and induction-head literature (Elhage et al., 2021; Geshkovski et al., 2023b), while the Lyapunov exponent approach draws on dynamical systems tools previously applied to RNNs (Vogt et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational assumption in transformer theory: that weight-sharing leads to clustering at infinite depth. By demonstrating (via simulation) that learned time-dependent weight dynamics with increasing eigenvalue magnitudes prevent clustering, it suggests that existing theoretical frameworks need revision to accommodate non-autonomous vector fields. The adaptive fine-tuning capability—pretraining at one depth and fine-tuning at another via ODE solver step-size adjustment—is a genuinely novel practical feature with no prior equivalent. Open questions include whether these spectral dynamics persist at much larger scales, whether the clustering-prevention result can be made rigorous, and how the approach scales memory-efficiently to billion-parameter regimes.
