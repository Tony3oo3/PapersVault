## Problem

Traditional antibiotic discovery is prohibitively costly and slow, and while foundation models have shown promise in molecular property prediction, existing approaches fail to fully exploit the complementary information available across multiple molecular representations (SMILES, graphs, fingerprints). The paper addresses this by asking whether a domain-specific, multimodal contrastive learning framework can outperform large-scale general-purpose pretraining for antibiotic-related property prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252248/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single SMILES string as input and internally derives two additional modalities via RDKit: a 2048-bit Morgan fingerprint (radius 2, selected via ablation) and a molecular graph with atom/bond features. During pretraining, the three encoders produce aligned molecular embeddings in a shared vector space; during fine-tuning, a classification head on top of the fused representation yields a binary activity score (e.g., MIC ≤ 8 µg/mL for E. coli). Molecules with SMILES longer than 100 characters or containing elements outside a defined set are excluded from the 1.59M-molecule ChEMBL pretraining corpus.

## Method

CL-MFAP jointly pretrains three encoders via multimodal contrastive learning: a transformer with Rotary Positional Embedding for SMILES, a transformer with a novel Bi-Level Routing Attention (BRA) mechanism for molecular graphs, and an MLP for Morgan fingerprints. The BRA mechanism, adapted from computer vision (BiUNet), operates in two stages—first computing window-to-window attention scores to identify the top-k most relevant spatial regions, then performing fine-grained pixel-to-pixel attention only within those selected windows—thereby focusing capacity on functionally critical substructures like cyclic motifs while filtering peripheral noise. All three modality pairs are aligned using NT-Xent loss with learned per-pair weights, treating different modalities of the same molecule as positives and different molecules as negatives. The model is pretrained unsupervised on 1.6M ChEMBL bioactive molecules and then fine-tuned with a linear probe for downstream binary property prediction, achieving state-of-the-art ROC-AUC on E. coli MIC (0.854) and competitive results across five additional drug-discovery benchmarks.

## Results

CL-MFAP achieves the best ROC-AUC of 0.854±0.037 on the E. coli MIC dataset, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: MolBERT at 0.77). On H. influenzae MIC, CL-MFAP scores 0.874±0.015, narrowly trailing MoLFormer (0.876±0.017). Across all six downstream tasks, CL-MFAP attains the highest Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) score, confirming superior overall performance, though it underperforms on Bioavailability (0.60±0.03) where MolBERT (0.75) and FP-GNN (0.75) lead. The model also achieves the best RePRA scores (S_AD = 0.008, S_IR = 1.317), indicating the highest-quality molecular representations among all evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CL-MFAP decisively outperforms all five baselines on the antibiotic-relevant E. coli MIC task (0.85 vs. 0.71–0.77), with a margin of 0.08–0.14 ROC-AUC over the nearest competitors. Against MoLFormer (trained on 1.1B molecules), CL-MFAP wins on E. coli MIC, PAMPA, and BACE but loses on H. influenzae MIC (0.87 vs. 0.88) and Bioavailability (0.60 vs. 0.72). Against MolBERT (same 1.6M ChEMBL pretraining data), CL-MFAP wins on E. coli MIC and PAMPA but trails on BBBP (0.93 vs. 0.97), Bioavailability (0.60 vs. 0.75), and BACE (0.88 vs. 0.89). Against ChemBERTa-2, MolCLR, and FP-GNN, CL-MFAP consistently leads on both MIC datasets and BACE, while FP-GNN and MolBERT maintain advantages on BBBP and Bioavailability. The MRR analysis confirms CL-MFAP's overall superiority, ranking first across the aggregate of all six tasks despite its weakness on Bioavailability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) transformer-based molecular language models (MoLFormer, ChemBERTa, MolBERT) that encode SMILES strings, (2) contrastive learning for molecular representation (MolCLR, UniCorn) that leverages unlabeled data, and (3) graph transformers for molecular structure (Graphormer). Its primary architectural departure is the first application of Bi-Level Routing Attention (BRA), previously used in computer vision (BiUNet, Dong et al. 2023), to molecular graph encoding, combined with RoPE-based positional encoding for the SMILES pathway. Unlike prior multimodal molecular models (e.g., FP-GNN) that fuse modalities at the feature level for supervised prediction, CL-MFAP uses NT-Xent contrastive loss to align three modality-specific encoders (SMILES transformer, graph transformer with BRA, and Morgan fingerprint MLP) in a shared embedding space, pretrained on a smaller domain-specific ChEMBL dataset rather than billion-scale general corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model's strongest result—a 14-point ROC-AUC gap over MoLFormer on E. coli MIC prediction—demonstrates that domain-specific contrastive pretraining on 1.6M bioactive molecules can outperform general-purpose models trained on 1.1B compounds for antibiotic-relevant tasks, a practically important finding given the antimicrobial resistance crisis. The case study identifying two structurally novel compounds with high functional similarity to known E. coli antibiotics (Levofloxacin, Ciprofloxacin) provides a concrete validation pipeline. However, the model is limited to 2D representations (no 3D geometry), the H. influenzae MIC result is essentially tied with MoLFormer, and the broader antibiotic spectrum (Gram-positive, MDR pathogens) remains untested. Open questions include whether the BRA mechanism generalizes to 3D molecular representations and whether the contrastive framework scales to larger, more diverse bioactivity databases.
