## Problem

State-of-the-art visual reasoning models (OCRA, Slot-Abstractor) rely on object-centric slot attention and relational bottlenecks but fail to generalize learned relations to out-of-distribution objects, because their perceptual segregation cannot reliably capture relations between different parts of objects. Active vision theories suggest that humans factorize scenes into visual ('what') and spatial ('where') contents, with the low-dimensional spatial geometry providing a scaffold for relational reasoning that is agnostic to specific visual details. This paper tests whether explicitly implementing such a what/where factorization through sequential active glimpsing can close the generalization gap.

## Input / Output

The model takes 128×128 images (binary for SVRT/ART, colored 3D renders for CLEVR-ART) and sequentially produces T=15–35 glimpse-content/location pairs, where each glimpse content is a 15×15 (or 21×21) patch extracted by a multi-scale or log-polar sensor centered on a salient location, and each location is a 2D coordinate rescaled to [-1,1]. The downstream architecture consumes both sequences jointly and outputs a binary decision (same/different) or a multiple-choice selection (correct object or object pair), trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy.

## Method

Glimpse-based Active Perception (GAP) first computes a saliency map via error neurons that measure L2 distance between a central 5×5 patch and its 8 surrounding patches (min aggregation). A winner-takes-all mechanism selects the highest-saliency location, and an inhibition-of-return mask (hard or soft) suppresses it for subsequent iterations, yielding a sequence of T glimpse locations. A glimpse sensor (multi-scale: three 15×15 patches at decreasing resolutions; or log-polar: foveal-inspired sampling) extracts the visual content around each location, producing a dual sequence of 'what' (glimpse contents) and 'where' (glimpse locations). The key architectural choice is the Abstractor downstream, whose Relational Cross Attention computes queries and keys from glimpse contents (visual features) but values from glimpse locations (pure spatial coordinates), so that attention is driven by visual relations while the processed information is structural. This explicit disentanglement of what and where, combined with processing only salient regions rather than the full image, yields superior OOD generalization and sample efficiency without any pre-training.

## Results

On the SVRT dataset (23 tasks, 1000 training samples), GAP-Abstractor achieves 95.4% average accuracy across all tasks (93.1% on same-different, 98.5% on spatial relations), outperforming the best prior-art model Slot-Abstractor* (94.7%). On the SVRT #1-OOD benchmark testing generalization to 13 novel shape distributions, GAP-Abstractor reaches 89.6% accuracy versus 72.8% for Slot-Abstractor* and 79.8% for GAMR. On the ART dataset (4 abstract reasoning tasks with only 5 training objects), GAP-Abstractor achieves 97.7%, 96.3%, 98.4%, and 96.8% on SD, RMTS, Dist-3, and ID tasks respectively, surpassing all baselines without any pre-training. On CLEVR-ART with photorealistic 3D objects, GAP-Abstractor matches Slot-Abstractor* (95.9% vs 96.3% RMTS; 93.4% vs 91.6% ID) despite not being pre-trained on test-set objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAP-Abstractor outperforms all five baselines on the primary SVRT benchmark, achieving 95.4% (1000 samples) vs. Slot-Abstractor* (94.7%), OCRA* (92.8%), GAMR (90.8%), Attn-ResNet (83.9%), and ResNet (76.7%). The advantage is most pronounced on same-different tasks, where GAP-Abstractor reaches 93.1% versus 91.9% for Slot-Abstractor* and only 82.1% for GAMR. On the critical OOD generalization test (SVRT #1-OOD), GAP-Abstractor achieves 89.6% accuracy, a 16.8-point margin over Slot-Abstractor* (72.8%) and 10.8 points over GAMR (79.8%), demonstrating that the explicit spatial 'where' pathway enables relation learning agnostic to visual appearance. On ART, GAP-Abstractor leads all baselines by 1.3–31.5 points across four tasks, notably without any pre-training (whereas OCRA* and Slot-Abstractor* rely on pre-trained segmentation). On CLEVR-ART, GAP-Abstractor is marginally below Slot-Abstractor* on RMTS (95.9% vs. 96.3%) but exceeds it on ID (93.4% vs. 91.6%), and in a fairer OOD comparison with novel shapes (pyramid, torus), GAP-Abstractor clearly outperforms Slot-Abstractor which was pre-trained on the original test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on active vision theories (Yarbus, Hayhoe & Ballard) to propose Glimpse-based Active Perception (GAP), which sequentially fixates on salient image regions using a bio-inspired pipeline of error neurons, winner-takes-all selection, and inhibition-of-return. It builds directly on the Abstractor's Relational Cross Attention mechanism (Altabaa et al., 2024) as its downstream architecture, repurposing RCA so that queries/keys derive from visual glimpse contents while values come from purely spatial glimpse locations. The approach departs from slot-attention-based methods (OCRA, Slot-Abstractor) that rely on learned positional embeddings and pre-trained segmentation, instead using raw 2D coordinates of salient regions, and from RL-based glimpsing (Mnih et al., 2014) by replacing learned policies with a deterministic saliency-driven process that avoids the exploration inefficiency of large action spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides computational evidence that explicitly factorizing an image into complementary 'what' (visual content) and 'where' (spatial location) streams, with the where stream stripped of all visual information, is essential for learning relations that generalize to unseen objects — a capability where prior object-centric methods fail. The finding that a simple, non-learned saliency mechanism (error neurons + WTA + IoR) outperforms learned segmentation and RL-based attention for visual reasoning suggests that bio-inspired inductive biases may be more sample-efficient than learned alternatives in low-data regimes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to natural images with complex textures and occlusions, whether more powerful saliency extractors (as preliminarily explored with CNN features and Itti-Koch) can close the remaining gap on realistic scenes, and whether the GAP framework can be integrated into visually grounded LLMs for broader reasoning tasks.
