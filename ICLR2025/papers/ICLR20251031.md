## Problem

Data valuation for generative models remains largely unexplored: existing metric-based methods lack robust performance metrics for generative tasks, influence-based methods incur prohibitive Hessian computation costs and are model-specific, and data-driven methods require labels. No unified, model-agnostic, and training-free approach exists for measuring how much each training image contributes to a set of generated images across diverse generative architectures (GANs, VAEs, diffusion models).

## Input / Output

The method takes a deduplicated training dataset of n images, a well-trained generative model G*, and a fixed set of m generated images X̂ produced by G*. It requires no retraining or gradient access to G*. The output is a scalar value score φ_i for each training sample, computed as the sum of quality-calibrated, similarity-weighted contributions across all generated samples where that training sample ranks among the top-k nearest neighbors. The method operates on RGB images at resolutions ranging from 28×28 (MNIST) to 1024×1024 (FFHQ) and is agnostic to the underlying generative architecture.

## Method

GMVALUATOR reformulates data valuation as a similarity-matching problem with three modules. First, Efficient Similarity Matching (ESM) uses CLIP embeddings and Product Quantization to rapidly recall top-k candidate training samples per generated image (reducing complexity from O(mnC) to O(mQD)), then re-ranks them using perceptual distance metrics (LPIPS or DreamSim) for precision. Second, Image Quality Assessment via MANIQA produces a quality score q_j ∈ [0,1] per generated image, which calibrates contribution scores so that low-quality generations distribute less total credit. Third, the final contribution score is V(x_i, x̂_j) = q_j · exp(−d_ij) / Σ_{i∈P_j} exp(−d_ij) for top-k members and zero otherwise, and the data value φ_i is the sum over all generated samples. A theoretical bound (Theorem 2.4) justifies the approach by showing that minimizing the Wasserstein distance between contributor attribute distributions and generated-data attribute distributions approximates the true K contributors.

## Results

On the Identical Class Test (C1), GMVALUATOR (DreamSim) achieves 88.78% identical-class ratio on MNIST (k=30), 77.94% on CIFAR-10 (k=30), and 77.27% on ImageNet (k=30), substantially outperforming VAE-TracIn (72.00% on MNIST, 6.28% on CIFAR-10). On the Identical Attributes Test (C2) with CelebA, GMVALUATOR (DreamSim) reaches 99.19% for Gender and 97.78% for Hat at k=5. In terms of efficiency (C4), GMVALUATOR (No-Rerank) attributes contributors in 0.250 s per generated sample on MNIST versus 47.945 s for VAE-TracIn (~190× speedup), and completes full data valuation on Noise MNIST in 2,137 s versus 14,543 s for IF4GAN (~7× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VAE-TracIn (Kong & Chaudhuri, NeurIPS 2021), GMVALUATOR (DreamSim) wins decisively on the Identical Class Test: 88.78% vs 72.00% on MNIST (k=30) and 77.94% vs 6.28% on CIFAR-10 (k=30), with VAE-TracIn being infeasible on ImageNet due to prohibitive Hessian-estimation cost. On efficiency, GMVALUATOR (No-Rerank) is roughly 190× faster per generated sample on MNIST (0.250 s vs 47.945 s) and ~88× faster on CIFAR-10 (0.755 s vs 66.178 s). Against IF4GAN (Terashita et al., 2021), GMVALUATOR outperforms on Out-of-Distribution Detection (C3): all 100 noisy MNIST samples are ranked at lower value positions by GMVALUATOR than by IF4GAN. On full data-valuation runtime for Noise MNIST, GMVALUATOR (No-Rerank) is ~7× faster (2,137 s vs 14,543 s), though GMVALUATOR (DreamSim) is slightly slower (17,086 s vs 14,543 s) due to the heavier perceptual re-ranking step, representing a speed–accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing retrieval-style similarity matching (CLIP embeddings + Product Quantization for recall, DreamSim/LPIPS for re-ranking) to solve data valuation in generative models, a setting where prior methods are either model-specific (VAE-TracIn for VAEs, IF4GAN for GANs) or inapplicable (metric-based Shapley/Banzhaf methods lack robust performance metrics for generative models). Conceptually, it departs from the influence-function lineage by arguing that the contribution of a training sample to a generated sample can be captured by perceptual similarity rather than gradient-based parameter influence, and it introduces image quality calibration (MANIQA) to correct for biased credit assignment to low-quality generations. The theoretical grounding (Theorem 2.4) borrows the Lipschitz/Wasserstein framework from Just et al. (2023) but applies it to the generative setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GMVALUATOR provides the first practical, training-free, model-agnostic tool for attributing credit to individual training images in generative models, with direct implications for GDPR compliance, data pricing, and intellectual property auditing in the era of large-scale image generation. The four evaluation criteria (identical class, identical attributes, OOD detection, efficiency) establish a reusable benchmark protocol for future generative-model data valuation work. Key limitations include restriction to vision modalities (no NLP/LLM extension demonstrated), dependence on a pre-trained encoder (CLIP) for the recall phase, and the fact that the method measures contribution to a fixed set of generated samples rather than to the model's overall distributional quality. Extending the approach to text-to-image conditioning, video generation, and language models remains an open direction.
