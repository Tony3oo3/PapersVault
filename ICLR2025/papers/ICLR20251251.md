## Problem

Autoregressive LLM decoding is inherently myopic: at each step the model selects the action with the highest immediate conditional probability without anticipating long-term consequences, leading to irreversible errors in multi-step planning. The paper formalizes this gap from an optimal control perspective, showing that over 60% of Llama-3 reasoning traces on GSM8K and MATH exhibit myopia, and that LLMs struggle to identify mistakes in early intermediate steps. The key question is whether an LLM can proactively avoid erroneous steps without requiring them to occur first.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (math problem, code instruction, or agent task goal), a pretrained LLM, and for agent tasks, environment observations that update after each action. At each decoding step it samples K parallel foresight rollouts of T₀ steps (typically 5–6) from the LLM, computes their joint probabilities, and outputs a single next action (or code line) selected from the re-weighted distribution. A trajectory recycling pool reuses previously sampled rollouts across timesteps to reduce redundant computation, and an optional external reward model (e.g., Math-Shepherd) can substitute for LLM self-evaluation of foresight quality.

## Method

Predictive-Decoding applies Model Predictive Control to LLM generation: instead of optimizing an entire action sequence at once (exponential search space), it solves T sequential sub-problems, each with linear solution space |A|. At each step t, it samples K foresight trajectories (a_t, …, a_{t+T₀}) in parallel, then re-weights the original LLM distribution via an energy-based model formulation using sampling-importance-resampling: p_τ(a_t) ∝ P^LLM(a_t | history) · exp[E[P^LLM(a_t, a_{>t}, s_{>t} | history)] / τ). The next action is sampled from this re-weighted categorical distribution, with temperature τ controlling the trade-off between fitting the LLM's prior and favoring high-foresight-probability actions. The approach is training-free, uses the LLM as both policy and world model, and achieves Pareto superiority over search-based baselines (MCTS, Guided-Decoding) on inference scaling curves by avoiding the exponential growth of iterative planning while retaining global-optimality guarantees.

## Results

On GSM8K (8-shot, PAL format), Predictive-Decoding with Llama3-8B achieves 78.5% accuracy, a 7.2% improvement over autoregressive PAL (71.3%), while using only 161.3×10¹² FLOPS versus 478.6×10¹² for Beam Search. On MATH, it reaches 34.0% vs. 29.7% for PAL. For agent tasks, Llama3.1-70B with Predictive-Decoding achieves 44.0% success on AlfWorld (vs. 19.4% Act, 18.7% ReAct) and 38.3% on PDDL (vs. 35.0% Act). On HumanEval (zero-shot), Llama3-8B reaches 57.3% pass@1 and 76.8% pass@10, outperforming all baselines including Beam Search (53.5%/70.1%) and Self-Infilling (51.8%/75.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Predictive-Decoding consistently outperforms autoregressive PAL (Gao et al., 2023) across all tasks: +7.2% on GSM8K, +4.3% on MATH, +13.0% on HumanEval pass@1, and +24.6% on AlfWorld success rate. Against Beam Search (Freitag & Al-Onaizan, 2017), it matches or slightly exceeds accuracy (78.5% vs. 78.4% on GSM8K) while using roughly 3× less computation. Combined with Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2022), Predictive-Decoding reaches 81.3% GSM8K and 40.3% MATH, surpassing both autoregressive+SC (80.9%/31.8%) and Beam Search+SC (79.5%/39.4%). On coding, it beats Self-Infilling (Zheng et al., 2023) by 5.5% pass@1 on HumanEval (57.3% vs. 51.8%). For agent tasks, it substantially outperforms ReAct (Yao et al., 2022) by 25.3% on AlfWorld success (44.0% vs. 18.7%). With a reward model (Math-Shepherd), Predictive-Decoding+RM achieves 87.9% GSM8K, beating Guided-Decoding+RM (Xie et al., 2024) by 1.4% with 65% of the FLOPS, MCTS+RM (Hao et al., 2023) by 2.4% with 50% FLOPS, Tree-of-Thought+RM (Yao et al., 2024) by 5.9%, and A*Search+RM (Zhuang et al., 2023) by 4.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes LLM autoregressive decoding through the lens of Model Predictive Control (MPC) from classical control theory, identifying the inherent myopia of next-token prediction as a failure to solve the tail subproblem in sequential optimization. It departs from the dominant iterative planning paradigm (Tree-of-Thought, MCTS, Guided-Decoding) by adopting a sequential, non-iterative approach that fixes each action as it progresses, reducing the solution space from exponential to linear per step. The reweighting mechanism draws on sampling-importance-resampling from energy-based models, applied here to rescale LLM generation distributions based on foresight trajectory evaluations. Unlike prior foresight methods (constrained decoding, self-infilling) that operate at the token level with short lookaheads, Predictive-Decoding operates at the action/step level with multi-step foresight, making it applicable to structured reasoning and agent tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnostic (myopic gap) quantifying that ~60% of LLM reasoning is myopic and that the majority of errors on math benchmarks occur in myopic instances, grounding a previously intuitive concern in measurable terms. As a training-free method applicable to any LLM, it offers a practical drop-in improvement that achieves Pareto superiority over search-based baselines (MCTS, Guided-Decoding) while using roughly 50% of the inference FLOPS, addressing the growing concern about compute-optimal inference scaling. Key limitations include restriction to tasks with clear step structure (math, coding, discrete agents), reliance on the LLM as an imperfect world model (hallucination in state prediction), and no exploration of how the method could generate training data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to free-form long-context generation and whether the MPC framing can inform training objectives rather than just inference-time decoding.
