## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of ridgeless (minimum ℓ₂-norm) interpolation least squares estimators in the overparameterized regime assume i.i.d. regression errors with common variance (Ω = σ²I), an assumption that fails for time series, panel, and grouped data where errors exhibit serial or clustered dependence. This paper asks how to characterize the prediction and estimation risks under a general positive-definite error covariance matrix Ω, and why the double descent phenomenon appears empirically robust to the degree of dependence across observations.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a design matrix X ∈ ℝ^{p×n} (p > n) assumed left-spherical (a weaker condition than i.i.d. normal rows), a general error covariance Ω = E[εεᵀ] that may have non-identical diagonal entries and non-zero off-diagonal elements, and a feature covariance Σ = E[x₀x₀ᵀ]. It produces exact finite-sample closed-form expressions for both the Σ-weighted prediction risk and the unweighted estimation risk, each decomposed into a bias term r²(1−n/p) and a variance term that factorizes as (Tr(Ω)/n) times a functional of X's spectral distribution. The key structural output is that only Tr(Ω) — not the individual off-diagonal correlations — enters the variance component.

## Method

The core mechanism exploits left-spherical symmetry of X (invariance under left-multiplication by any orthogonal matrix) to show that the alignment matrix Γ(X) between the right-singular vectors of B = Σ^{1/2}X† and the left-eigenvectors of T = Ω^{1/2} averages to (1/n)J under the Haar measure over O(n), collapsing the variance to (1/n)Tr(Ω)·E_X[Tr((XᵀX)†Σ)] for prediction risk and (1/n)Tr(Ω)·E_X[Tr(Λ⁻¹)]/p for estimation risk. For the bias component, under a random-effects hypothesis (E[ββᵀ] = r²I/p), both risks yield the same bias r²(1−n/p) independent of the error structure. The asymptotic analysis invokes the Silverstein–Bai theorem on Stieltjes transforms of XXᵀ/n to recover the double descent curve, showing that the variance term scales as s*·lim Tr(Ω)/n where s* = Θ(1/(γ−1)) for bounded spectral support, while the bias remains bounded as γ→∞. The results are validated on AR(1) and clustered error structures, confirming that different Ω matrices sharing the same trace produce identical expected variances.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) shows that the expected variance of the prediction risk factorizes as (1/n)·Tr(Ω)·E_X[Tr((X^T X)^† Σ)], meaning only the trace of the error covariance matrix Ω matters, not its off-diagonal correlation structure. This is validated numerically: on ACS 2018 data (n=1,415, p up to 2,182), in-sample and out-of-sample MSE curves are nearly identical across clustered-noise levels c ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75} (Figure 1), and contour plots for AR(1) and clustered errors (Figures 2–3) confirm that all (σ², ρ²) pairs sharing the same Tr(Ω)/n yield identical expected variances. In the high-dimensional asymptotic regime (p/n → γ > 1), the estimation risk converges to r²(1 − 1/γ) + s*·lim Tr(Ω)/n (Corollary 4.8), recovering the double-descent curve with s* = Θ(1/(γ−1)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical extension paper rather than a method-comparison study, so there is no head-to-head benchmark table. The key 'comparison' is against the i.i.d. error assumption (Ω = σ²I) used in Hastie et al. (2022) and Dobriban & Wager (2018): the paper recovers their exact expressions as a special case when Ω is spherical, while showing that for general Ω (AR(1), clustered, or arbitrary positive-definite), the variance component depends only on Tr(Ω)/n. Against Chinot et al. (2022) and Chinot & Lerasle (2023), who make no assumptions on errors at all (allowing adversarial noise) but provide only high-probability upper bounds, this paper gives exact finite-sample characterizations under the weaker (but still non-trivial) left-spherical design assumption. A notable contrast with classical econometrics is highlighted: in the standard asymptotic theory for least squares, the off-diagonal elements of Ω affect the variance-covariance matrix V_LS and must be estimated consistently, whereas here they play no role in the expected prediction or estimation risk of the ridgeless interpolator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on prediction and estimation risk of ridgeless least squares (Dobriban & Wager, 2018; Hastie et al., 2022) by replacing the standard i.i.d. zero-mean common-variance error assumption with a general variance-covariance matrix Ω that permits serial or clustered dependence. It leverages the left-spherical symmetry of the design matrix (weaker than i.i.d. normal) and the Stieltjes transform machinery from Silverstein & Bai (1995) to derive exact finite-sample expressions. The paper is distinct from Chinot et al. (2022) and Chinot & Lerasle (2023), who made no assumptions on errors at all to handle adversarial settings, whereas here the goal is to accommodate realistic time-series and panel-data dependence structures while retaining a tractable characterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that only Tr(Ω) governs the variance component of both prediction and estimation risk, regardless of the off-diagonal correlation structure—provides a clean theoretical justification for why double descent appears robust to clustered or serially dependent noise in practice. This extends the benign-overfitting narrative to time series, panel, and grouped data, which are common in economics and social science applications. A key limitation is the left-spherical design matrix assumption, and the authors note that verifying whether the trace-only result persists under more general (non-spherical) designs remains an open question. The result also stands in sharp contrast to classical econometrics, where off-diagonal elements of Ω do affect the asymptotic variance of the LS estimator, highlighting a fundamental difference between overparameterized and underparameterized regimes.
