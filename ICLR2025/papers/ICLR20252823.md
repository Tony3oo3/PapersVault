## Problem

The original Kolmogorov Superposition Theorem (KST) formulation underlying Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) requires O(d²) inner functions, provides minimal structural insight into the superposition, and has shown mixed empirical performance compared to MLPs. The paper asks which alternative superposition theorem formulation is best suited for practical deep learning, particularly in physics-informed settings where derivative information and low-dimensional function approximation are critical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252823/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ActNet takes low-dimensional coordinate vectors (2D spatial (x,y) or 1D spatiotemporal (x,t)) as input and outputs a scalar PDE solution value. Boundary and initial conditions are enforced exactly by multiplying the raw network output by a constraint-satisfying factor (e.g., (1−x²)(1−y²) for Dirichlet problems), so the network only needs to learn the interior solution. PDE residuals are computed by analytically differentiating the network output, and the derivative of an ActNet is itself another ActNet, ensuring higher-order derivatives do not vanish as they do with ReLU-based MLPs.

## Method

ActNet is built on Laczkovich's (2021) version of KST, which requires only O(d) inner functions (vs. O(d²) for the original KST used in KANs) and is valid over all of ℝᵈ rather than just [0,1]ᵈ. The core ActLayer computes S(Λ ⊙ βB(x)), where B(x) is a sinusoidal basis expansion matrix with mean-zero, variance-one normalization, β ∈ ℝ^{m×N} are trainable basis coefficients, and Λ ∈ ℝ^{m×d} are trainable per-head weights; this is equivalent to a multi-head MLP where each head applies a distinct trainable activation function φ_k(t) = Σⱼ β_kj b_j(t) followed by a 1D linear projection. The paper proves that a composition of just two ActLayers is a universal approximator on any compact set, and that the sinusoidal basis with the specified initialization guarantees activations remain mean-0, variance-1 at every layer regardless of depth or width. Training follows the standard PINN paradigm: minimizing PDE residuals with exact boundary condition enforcement, using Adam with adaptive gradient clipping and optional LBFGS fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Advection equation (c=80), ActNet achieves a relative L2 error of 9.50e-5, outperforming JaxPi (6.88e-4) by ~7× and PirateNet (5.48e-4) by ~6×. On the full Kuramoto-Sivashinsky solution, ActNet reaches 8.53e-2 relative L2 error, beating JaxPi (1.61e-1) and PirateNet (2.11e-1). In ablation studies across Poisson, Helmholtz, and Allen-Cahn PDEs, ActNet outperforms KANs by 1–3 orders of magnitude on relative L2 error (e.g., 6.3e-4 vs 1.8e-1 on Poisson w=16) and is competitive with or superior to Siren and standard MLPs, particularly at high oscillation frequencies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActNet decisively outperforms KANs (Liu et al., 2024) across every benchmark tested, with relative L2 errors lower by 1–3 orders of magnitude (e.g., 6.3e-4 vs 1.8e-1 on Poisson w=16; 5.6e-5 vs 5.3e-4 on Allen-Cahn). Against Siren (Sitzmann et al., 2020), ActNet wins on Poisson and Helmholtz at all frequencies (especially at high w, where the gap widens to ~2 orders of magnitude), but Siren achieves a slightly lower Allen-Cahn error (2.1e-5 vs 5.6e-5) at larger network sizes, though ActNet attains the lowest PDE residual loss in all cases. Against JaxPi's modified MLP (Wang et al., 2023), ActNet improves the Advection result by ~7× (9.50e-5 vs 6.88e-4) and the full Kuramoto-Sivashinsky solution by ~2× (8.53e-2 vs 1.61e-1). Against PirateNet (Wang et al., 2024a), ActNet wins on Advection (9.50e-5 vs 5.48e-4) and full KS (8.53e-2 vs 2.11e-1), but PirateNet narrowly edges out ActNet on the KS first time window (1.23e-5 vs 1.34e-5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the original Kolmogorov Superposition Theorem formulation used in KANs (Liu et al., 2024) by adopting the Laczkovich (2021) variant, which reduces the number of required inner functions from O(d²) to O(d) and operates over all of R^d rather than the unit cube. The ActLayer is positioned as a multi-head MLP with trainable sinusoidal activations, or equivalently a LoRA-style KAN layer, bridging the KST literature with modern attention-inspired architectures. Within the PINN framework, ActNet is compared against both KST-based (KAN) and MLP-based (Siren, JaxPi, PirateNet) approaches, situating itself as a theoretically grounded middle ground between the two families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ActNet offers a principled, scalable alternative to KANs for PDE simulation with a clean universal approximation guarantee at fixed depth (two ActLayers) and a provably stable initialization scheme. The consistent outperformance of KANs by orders of magnitude and competitive results against state-of-the-art MLPs on challenging benchmarks (Advection c=80, Kuramoto-Sivashinsky) suggest that the choice of KST formulation matters more than previously appreciated. Key open questions include whether ActNet's advantages extend to data-driven (non-PINN) settings, higher-dimensional problems, and integration into larger architectures like transformers or U-Nets, as well as whether the ~2-3× computational overhead over MLPs can be further reduced through hardware-level optimizations.
