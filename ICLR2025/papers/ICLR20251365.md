## Problem

Existing context pruning methods for RAG are limited in practical applicability: they either assume a fixed compression ratio or a single relevant sentence per context, rely on inefficient billion-parameter LLMs or autoregressive generation, and are typically trained and tested on a single domain. No prior approach provides a lightweight, query-dependent, sentence-level pruner that dynamically detects the needed pruning amount (from zero to all sentences) and generalizes out-of-the-box across diverse QA domains.

## Input / Output

Provence takes a concatenated query-passage pair as input to a DeBERTa cross-encoder and produces per-token binary inclusion probabilities over the passage tokens, which are naturally clustered at the sentence level due to sentence-level training targets. At inference, a single threshold T binarizes these probabilities, and a 'sentence rounding' step (keep a sentence only if >50% of its tokens are selected) ensures whole-sentence granularity. In the unified variant, the model simultaneously outputs a scalar passage relevance score from the BOS token representation, enabling reranking and pruning in one forward pass.

## Method

Provence formulates context pruning as binary sequence labeling: a DeBERTa-v3 cross-encoder encodes the query-context pair and a lightweight prediction head outputs per-token keep/discard probabilities. Silver training labels are generated by prompting Llama-3-8B-Instruct to answer questions while citing relevant context sentences, producing variable-length selections that teach the model to adapt pruning ratio per context. The key architectural insight is unifying reranking and pruning into a single model with two task heads—a scalar reranking head on the BOS token and a per-token pruning head on passage tokens—trained jointly with a mean-squared-error regularizer that distills scores from the initial reranker to preserve ranking quality. Training uses the full MS MARCO collection (370k queries, 3.2M documents split into variable-length passages of 1–10 sentences) to ensure domain diversity, and the model is initialized from a pretrained DeBERTa-v3 cross-encoder reranker.

## Results

On Natural Questions, Provence (unified with reranker, T=0.1) achieves 72.4 LLM-Eval with 62.2% compression, matching or exceeding the full-context baseline of 71.8. On PopQA, Provence actually *improves* over full context (59.3 vs. 57.8) by filtering noise, while achieving 68.6% compression. Across all seven QA domains, Provence consistently sits on the Pareto front of compression vs. quality, achieving 50–80% compression with negligible or zero performance drop. The unified model preserves reranking performance (e.g., 84.4 R@5 on NQ vs. 83.0 for the base DeBERTa-v3 reranker).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Provence outperforms all five baseline pruners across every evaluated domain at comparable or higher compression rates. Against LLMLingua2 (token-level, query-independent), Provence gains 4–5 LLM-Eval points on NQ (72.4 vs. 67.5) and 6.8 points on PopQA (59.3 vs. 52.5) while compressing more aggressively. Against LongLLMLingua (token-level, query-dependent, LLM-based), Provence leads by 3.9 points on NQ and 4.8 on PopQA at similar compression. Against RECOMP extractive (sentence-level, dual-encoder), Provence wins by 4.2 points on NQ and 3.6 on PopQA; RECOMP abstractive is substantially worse (66.9 on NQ) due to hallucination and autoregressive generation overhead. Against DSLR (sentence-level, cross-encoder, independent sentence scoring), Provence leads by 1.9 points on NQ and 0.6 on PopQA, with a larger gap on HotpotQA (56.7 vs. 50.7). Dense-X retrieval, which reformulates the datastore rather than pruning, underperforms Provence by 9.7 points on NQ and 7.3 on PopQA, confirming that post-retrieval pruning is more effective than changing retrieval granularity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Provence builds on the context pruning task for RAG established by RECOMP, FilCo, COMPACT, and the LLMLingua family, but departs from them in three key ways: it formulates pruning as binary sequence labeling over a cross-encoder (DeBERTa-v3) rather than autoregressive generation or independent sentence scoring; it unifies pruning with reranking in a single model, eliminating the separate pruning step from the RAG pipeline; and it trains on the diverse MS MARCO corpus with silver labels from Llama-3-8B-Instruct to achieve cross-domain robustness. The closest concurrent work, DSLR, also performs extractive sentence-level pruning with a reranker but processes sentences independently, whereas Provence encodes the full query-context pair jointly. The architectural foundation is the DeBERTa-v3 cross-encoder reranker (Lassance & Clinchant, 2023), which Provence extends with a per-token pruning head and a score-distillation regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Provence offers a practically deployable context pruner that is nearly free in execution time (when unified with the reranker), requires only a single threshold hyperparameter, and generalizes across QA domains without retraining — addressing a real gap where prior pruners were either domain-specific, required fixed compression ratios, or were too slow for production RAG pipelines. The finding that a lightweight DeBERTa model matches or exceeds LLM-based pruners (e.g., LongLLMLingua on Llama-2-7B) in both quality and speed is a useful efficiency result for practitioners. Limitations include English-only training, single-passage processing, and QA-specific framing; extending to multi-passage contexts, other tasks, and multilingual settings remains open. The unification of pruning and reranking is a clean architectural insight that could inform future RAG pipeline design more broadly.
