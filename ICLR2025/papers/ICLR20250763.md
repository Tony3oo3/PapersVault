## Problem

Current RAG pipelines are text-based, requiring a parsing stage (OCR, layout recognition, text joining) to extract text from multi-modal documents before retrieval and generation. This parsing inevitably introduces errors and discards visual information—layout, figures, decorative fonts—that plays a crucial role in real-world documents. The paper investigates whether a vision-language model can replace both the retriever and generator in a RAG pipeline, bypassing parsing entirely and preserving all information in its original visual form.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250763/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text query and a corpus of raw document page images (screenshots, not parsed text) as input, and produces a ranked set of retrieved page images followed by a generated text answer. Each page is encoded into a single 2304-dimensional vector by the VLM-based retriever, enabling efficient ANN search with only 4.5 KB per document (versus 256 KB for ColPali's multi-vector representation). For generation, single-image VLMs receive either horizontally concatenated pages or individually scored pages, while multi-image VLMs (MiniCPM-V 2.6, GPT-4o) accept multiple page images directly for cross-page reasoning.

## Method

VisRAG replaces both the retriever and generator in a standard RAG pipeline with VLM-based components. The retriever (VisRAG-Ret) follows a dual-encoder architecture built on MiniCPM-V 2.0, encoding the query as text and each document page as an image, then computing embeddings via position-weighted mean pooling over the final hidden states; it is trained with InfoNCE loss using in-batch negatives. The generator (VisRAG-Gen) feeds retrieved page images directly to a VLM for answer generation, employing page concatenation or weighted selection (combining generation perplexity with normalized retrieval scores) for single-image VLMs, or direct multi-image input for VLMs trained on cross-image reasoning. The key insight is that a unified VLM architecture captures both textual and visual semantics more holistically than separate text and vision encoders, and that directly encoding the page image preserves layout, figures, and decorative text that would be lost or distorted during text extraction.

## Results

In the in-domain retrieval setting, VisRAG-Ret achieves 77.91 MRR@10 on average across six document VQA benchmarks, outperforming ColPali (76.54), MiniCPM(OCR) (74.78), and SigLIP (71.68). In the out-of-domain setting (trained only on synthetic data), VisRAG-Ret reaches 71.49 MRR@10, surpassing MiniCPM(OCR) by 13% and SigLIP by 20%. End-to-end, VisRAG achieves a 40% relative accuracy improvement over TextRAG with MiniCPM-V 2.6 as generator (56.12% vs. ~40% average) and a 20% relative improvement with GPT-4o (54.98% vs. 45.91% for GPT-4o with OCR text).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisRAG-Ret outperforms all text-based and vision-only baselines in both out-of-domain and in-domain settings. Against bge-large (Xiao et al., 2023), a 335M text embedding model, VisRAG-Ret achieves 77.91 vs. 59.13 MRR@10 (off-the-shelf), showing that text embeddings trained on clean corpora struggle with parsed real-world documents. Against NV-Embed-v2 (Lee et al., 2024), a 7.85B text model trained on millions of pairs, VisRAG-Ret (3.43B, trained on ~240K synthetic pairs) achieves 95% of its performance in the out-of-domain setting (71.49 vs. 75.24). Against SigLIP (Zhai et al., 2023), the vision encoder alone, VisRAG-Ret gains 13% (out-of-domain) and 9% (in-domain), demonstrating that the language model component is crucial for semantic understanding. Against ColPali (Faysse et al., 2024), the closest concurrent work, VisRAG-Ret achieves 77.91 vs. 76.54 MRR@10 while using only 4.5KB per document (single 2304-dim vector) versus ColPali's 256KB (1030 × 128-dim vectors), making it far more scalable. For generation, VisRAG-Gen with GPT-4o (54.98% top-3) outperforms GPT-4o with OCR-extracted text (45.91%) by ~9 points, confirming that direct image processing preserves critical visual and layout information lost during parsing.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisRAG extends the dual-encoder dense retrieval paradigm (Karpukhin et al., 2020) and the standard RAG pipeline (Lewis et al., 2020) by replacing the text-based LLM encoder with a VLM that directly encodes document page images, thereby eliminating the parsing stage. It is conceptually adjacent to concurrent multi-vector page-embedding retrievers like ColPali and DSE, but differs by providing a full end-to-end RAG pipeline (retrieval + generation) with a single-vector representation rather than focusing solely on retrieval. The generation stage builds on recent multi-image VLMs (MiniCPM-V 2.6, Qwen2-VL, GPT-4o) and introduces page concatenation and weighted selection as practical mechanisms for single-image VLMs to handle multiple retrieved pages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VisRAG demonstrates that bypassing document parsing with a VLM-based pipeline yields 20–40% end-to-end gains over text-based RAG while using a simpler architecture and far less memory per document (4.5 KB vs. 256 KB for ColPali), making it a practical candidate for scaling to large document corpora. The finding that even a strong VLM used as a parser (MiniCPM-V 2.0 Captioner) underperforms direct VLM encoding confirms that the transcription-to-text step introduces irrecoverable information loss, a result that may reshape how multi-modal document pipelines are designed. Open questions remain around multi-page reasoning (performance degrades beyond top-3 for most VLMs), the optimal retrieval granularity for very long documents, and whether the approach generalizes to non-English or highly stylized layouts.
