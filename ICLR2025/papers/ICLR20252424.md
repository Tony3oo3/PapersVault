## Problem

Existing reasoning segmentation datasets (ReasonSeg, MUSE) predominantly address single-target, object-level reasoning, leaving multi-target scenarios (e.g., multiple objects that could satisfy a command like 'turn on the TV') and part-level reasoning (e.g., specific buttons on a remote) largely unexplored. This limits models' ability to perform the fine-grained, context-aware segmentation needed for practical human-AI interaction.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (resized to 224×224 for CLIP, processed at native resolution by SAM-ViT-H) paired with an implicit, complex text instruction that requires world knowledge or multi-step reasoning rather than direct object naming. The output is a textual response with one [SEG] token appended per target entity (object or part), from which the SAM mask decoder produces a separate pixel-level segmentation mask for each target. The MMR dataset provides 194K such instruction-mask pairs across 57,643 images, with the test set further partitioned into object-only, part-only, and mixed-granularity subsets for granular evaluation.

## Method

M²SA extends the LISA framework (SAM + LLaVA) with two architectural modifications: (1) Early Local Feature Fusion, which extracts fine-grained features (edges, boundaries) from the 8th layer of SAM's vision encoder, aligns their channel dimension via two convolution layers, and sums them with the global semantic features from the final encoder layer to produce richer visual features for the mask decoder; and (2) multiple [SEG] tokens, where each target in the answer text is annotated with its own [SEG] token, and the corresponding token embeddings are projected into SAM's prompt space to generate distinct segmentation prompts per target. Training is end-to-end with LoRA on the MLLM, using a mixed dataset (semantic segmentation, RES, VQA, and MMR in a 2:9:2:6 sampling ratio) and a combined loss of autoregressive cross-entropy for text and weighted BCE + DICE for masks. The MMR dataset itself is constructed by feeding PACO-LVIS images with their object/part bounding-box annotations into GPT-4V via a two-step prompt (global caption generation followed by multi-target Q&A generation), then filtering 222K generated pairs down to 194K through four human inspectors.

## Results

On the proposed MMR benchmark (test, Obj&Part), M²SA-7B achieves 30.9 gIoU / 46.8 cIoU, outperforming GLaMM_tr (30.3/45.0), GSVA-7B_tr (21.2/34.5), and LISA-7B_tr (19.5/27.1). On multi-target referring expression segmentation (Multi-RefCOCO val), M²SA-7B reaches 71.3 cIoU, nearly doubling LISA-7B (34.0) and substantially exceeding GSVA-7B (50.7) and GLaMM (30.8). On the multi-granularity RefCOCOm benchmark, M²SA-Llama2-13B achieves 37.3 mIoU (Obj&Part val), surpassing GLaMM (35.3) and LISA-Llama2-13B (35.2). On unseen-domain generalization (GTAV/Cityscapes), M²SA-7B scores 35.1/41.3 mIoU versus 3.7/6.1 for LISA-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M²SA decisively outperforms LISA (both 7B and 13B) on the MMR benchmark (30.9 vs. 14.5 gIoU test Obj&Part) and on multi-target RefCOCO (71.3 vs. 34.0 cIoU val), where the single [SEG] token in LISA is a clear bottleneck. Against GSVA-7B, M²SA wins on MMR (30.9 vs. 15.5) and Multi-RefCOCO (71.3 vs. 50.7), but GSVA retains a slight edge on single-target RefCOCO val (76.4 vs. 74.0 cIoU). GLaMM is the strongest single-target baseline (79.5 cIoU on RefCOCO val) yet trails M²SA on MMR (13.3 vs. 30.9) and Multi-RefCOCO (30.8 vs. 71.3), confirming that GLaMM's pixel-grounding approach does not transfer well to multi-target or part-level reasoning. Among traditional RES methods (MCN, VLT, CRIS, LAVT, ReLA, X-Decoder, SEEM), M²SA-7B is competitive on single-target RefCOCO (74.0 val cIoU, close to LISA-7B's 74.1) but the paper's emphasis is on the multi-target and multi-granularity settings where these methods are not applicable. On RefCOCOm (multi-granularity RES), M²SA-Llama2-13B surpasses UniRES (37.3 vs. 34.3 Obj&Part val mIoU), LISA-Llama2-13B (37.3 vs. 35.2), and GLaMM (37.3 vs. 35.3), though the margin is modest due to the absence of person-class annotations in MMR. On unseen driving-scene data (GTAV, Cityscapes), M²SA-7B achieves 35.1/41.3 mIoU, dramatically outperforming LISA-7B (3.7/6.1), GSVA-7B (15.7/14.6), and GLaMM (12.6/12.6), demonstrating strong cross-domain generalization for multi-target segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reasoning segmentation paradigm introduced by LISA (Lai et al., 2023), which combined LLMs with SAM to generate pixel-level masks from implicit text queries. While LISA's ReasonSeg dataset and subsequent MUSE (PixelLM) addressed single-target and multi-target object-level reasoning respectively, neither covered part-level granularity in multi-target contexts. M²SA modifies LISA's architecture with two targeted additions—early local feature fusion from SAM's encoder and multiple [SEG] tokens—while leveraging PACO-LVIS annotations and GPT-4V (following LLaVA's data generation paradigm) to construct the 194K-pair MMR dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMR dataset (194K pairs, 75 object + 445 part categories) fills a critical scale and granularity gap in reasoning segmentation benchmarks, making it likely to become a standard evaluation resource. However, part-level segmentation remains extremely challenging across all methods (best gIoU ~13.5 on part-only test), and the absence of the human class limits real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the GPT-4V-generated instructions introduce systematic biases, whether the long-tail performance drop on infrequent object categories can be mitigated, and whether the high computational cost (30+ GB GPU memory) can be reduced for practical deployment.
