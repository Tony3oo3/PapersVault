## Problem

Existing concept erasing methods that fine-tune cross-attention (CA) layers in diffusion models are mathematically equivalent to adding linear weight updates, which the paper proves cannot simultaneously achieve strong erasure of target concepts and preservation of diverse remaining concepts. Specifically, increasing the magnitude of the weight change to shift target concept distributions necessarily raises the upper bound on output variation for remaining concepts drawn from broad distributions, creating an inherent tension that linear updates cannot resolve.

## Input / Output

The method operates on Stable Diffusion v1.4, consuming text prompts and producing 512×512 RGB images via DDIM sampling with 50 steps. The ResAG modules are inserted into the key and value projections of every cross-attention layer in the U-Net, with the attention parameters (U₁, U₂, v) shared across all CA layers while the low-rank erasing matrices (U₃, U₄) are per-projection. Training computes the loss directly on CA layer projection outputs rather than end-to-end diffusion model outputs, eliminating the need for full forward passes through the denoising network.

## Method

The core mechanism is the Residual Attention Gate (ResAG), a nonlinear module that computes an embedding-dependent projection f(E) via self-attention over token-to-token relationships, then applies a sigmoid gate to a low-rank weight update (ΔW = U₄ᵀU₃) so that the erasing change is selectively transmitted only for target concept tokens while suppressed for remaining concept tokens. An attention anchoring loss minimizes the theoretical upper bound on CA output variation (derived in Theorem 1) for a set of anchor concepts, with noise perturbation and Mixup applied to anchor embeddings to prevent overfitting. The RARE strategy iteratively trains learnable residual adversarial embeddings to regenerate erased concepts, then retrains the ResAG to block them, enhancing robustness against prompt attacks. For multiple concept erasure, individual ResAGs are merged at inference by selecting, per token, the ResAG whose gate value is highest, avoiding interference between different target concepts.

## Results

On celebrities erasure (50 targets), CPE achieves a CLIP Score of 20.79 and GCD accuracy of 0.37% for target concepts, while preserving remaining concepts near original levels (e.g., 100 remaining celebrities: CS 34.82 vs. 34.83 original; COCO-30K FID 14.13 vs. 14.04 original). On artistic styles erasure (100 targets), CPE reaches CS 20.67 for targets with remaining celebrity CS of 34.81 (vs. 34.83 original). For explicit content erasure, CPE detects only 40 explicit instances on I2P (vs. 743 for unmodified SD v1.4), and achieves zero attack success rate against both Ring-A-Bell and UnlearnDiff on Van Gogh style erasure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPE outperforms FMN by a large margin on erasure effectiveness (target CS 20.79 vs. 32.38; GCD ACC 0.37% vs. 59.98%) while maintaining comparable or better preservation across all remaining domains. Against ESD-x/u, CPE achieves stronger erasure (CS 20.79 vs. 24.41/21.64) and dramatically better preservation (e.g., remaining celebrity CS 34.82 vs. 26.23/21.95; COCO FID 14.13 vs. 14.40/15.98). UCE achieves the best raw erasure (CS 17.73, ACC 0.09%) but suffers catastrophic forgetting on remaining concepts (COCO FID 97.09 vs. CPE's 14.13; remaining celebrity CS 24.76 vs. 34.82), making it impractical. MACE preserves remaining celebrities well (CS 34.39) but shows weaker erasure (CS 24.60) and degrades on other domains (artistic style CS 27.25 vs. CPE's 29.01). For explicit content erasure, CPE detects only 40 explicit instances on I2P, less than half of RECE (89) and MACE (111). On adversarial robustness, CPE achieves 0.00% ASR against both Ring-A-Bell and UnlearnDiff on Van Gogh style erasure, outperforming RECE (28%/64%) and AdvUnlearn (–/2%), while UCE is highly vulnerable (94% ASR under UnlearnDiff).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept erasure line of work initiated by ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023), whose surrogate-concept alignment loss is directly adapted as CPE's erasing objective, and RECE (Gong et al., 2024), whose iterative erase-then-attack paradigm is mirrored in CPE's RARE strategy. The key conceptual departure is from the linear-only fine-tuning of cross-attention layers (as in UCE, MACE, TIME) to a nonlinear Residual Attention Gate that selectively transmits weight changes only for target concepts. The paper's Theorems 1–2 formalize why this departure is necessary: under a Gaussian mixture model for text embeddings, the expected perturbation to remaining concepts grows with the Frobenius norm of the linear update, creating an irreducible tension that no amount of anchoring loss can fully resolve with purely linear modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPE provides a theoretically grounded, parameter-efficient (<0.01% of SD v1.4 per concept) solution to the long-standing tension between erasure effectiveness and preservation of diverse remaining concepts, with near-zero KID on multiple domains simultaneously. The adversarial robustness (zero ASR against Ring-A-Bell on explicit content) addresses a practical deployment concern that prior methods have been shown vulnerable to. Open questions include whether the Gaussian mixture assumption on text embeddings holds for more diverse or multilingual settings, whether the approach transfers to non-U-Net diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), and whether the per-concept ResAG modules scale gracefully to thousands of erasure targets without the merging heuristic degrading performance.
