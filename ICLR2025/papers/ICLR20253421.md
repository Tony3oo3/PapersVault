## Problem

A widespread belief holds that standard Bayesian Optimization with Gaussian processes is limited to low-dimensional problems (d ≤ 15–20), yet this claim lacked both rigorous empirical evidence and a theoretical explanation for the failure mechanism. This paper identifies the specific cause—gradient vanishing during GP marginal-likelihood training due to improper length-scale initialization—and demonstrates that, once addressed, standard BO can match or surpass dedicated high-dimensional methods.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a black-box objective f: X ⊆ ℝ^d → ℝ where only noisy function evaluations are available (no gradients), with d ranging from 30 to 1,003 across benchmarks. The GP surrogate is trained by maximizing the log marginal likelihood via point estimation (L-BFGS, Adam, or RMSProp), and at each BO iteration the UCB acquisition function (λ=1.5) is maximized to select the next query point. The output is the best-found input vector and its function value, with the GP posterior mean μ(x*) and variance v(x*) available at any point for uncertainty quantification.

## Method

The paper's core insight is that the SE kernel's gradient with respect to each length-scale contains the factor ρ²/e^(ρ²), which falls below machine epsilon (2⁻⁵³) once ρ > 6.58, causing gradient vanishing that prevents effective GP training in high dimensions. A probabilistic tail bound (via Hoeffding's inequality on squared distances under uniform sampling) shows that with any constant ℓ₀, the probability of exceeding this threshold approaches 1 rapidly as d grows (e.g., >0.99 at d≈205 for ℓ₀=0.5). The Matérn-5/2 kernel avoids this because its corresponding factor ρ²/e^(√5·ρ) decays much more slowly, requiring ρ > 21.98 to vanish—roughly 3× higher—making it robust up to d ≈ 1000 under the same initialization. To fix the SE kernel, the authors propose a simple robust initialization ℓ₀ = c√d (c=1), for which they prove the gradient-vanishing probability decreases as exp(−O(d)), fundamentally eliminating the failure mode without additional priors or regularization. Across 12 benchmarks (dimensions 30–1003), standard BO with either the Matérn kernel or SE + robust initialization consistently achieves top-tier results, frequently outperforming structural-assumption-based methods like SaasBO, HESBO, ALEBO, RDUCB, and TURBO.

## Results

Across 12 high-dimensional benchmarks (dimensions 30–1,003), SBO-Matern (RI), SBO-SE (RI), and VBO consistently achieve top-tier optimization performance, often outperforming all other methods by a large margin on Ackley(300,150), DNA(180), and SVM(388). SBO-Matern with the default ℓ₀=0.693 initialization also achieves top-tier results on all benchmarks except Humanoid-Standup (d=1003), while SBO-SE with the same initialization fails entirely for d≥150 due to gradient vanishing. The theoretical analysis shows that under ℓ₀=0.5, the probability of gradient vanishing exceeds 0.99 at d=205 for the SE kernel but not until d=1040 for the Matérn kernel, explaining the empirical gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBO-Matern (RI) and SBO-SE (RI) with the proposed robust initialization (ℓ₀=√d) match or exceed VBO (Hvarfner et al., 2024) across all 12 benchmarks, with VBO showing slightly better runtime efficiency on Rosenbrock(100,100) and DNA(180) but worse ultimate performance on Ackley(150,150), Ackley(300,150), and Humanoid-Standup(1003). All structural-assumption baselines—Tree UCB, RDUCB, HESBO, ALEBO, SaasBO (NUTS and MAP), and TURBO—are consistently outperformed by SBO-Matern (RI) and SBO-SE (RI), particularly on benchmarks with partial variable dependency (Ackley, Hartmann6) and high-dimensional real-world tasks (SVM, DNA, Mopta08). SBO-Matern with the default ℓ₀=0.693 initialization already outperforms all structural baselines on every benchmark except Humanoid-Standup, where the dimension exceeds 1,000 and even the Matérn kernel suffers from gradient vanishing. BNN-based BO methods (Laplace and HMC) perform at or below the worst GP-based baselines, confirming that GP surrogates remain superior in the low-data, high-dimensional regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly challenges the dominant paradigm in high-dimensional BO, where methods like REMBO, HESBO, ALEBO, SaasBO, and RDUCB all impose structural assumptions (low-rank embeddings, additive decomposition, sparse priors) to avoid handling high-dimensional inputs directly. It builds on the standard GP/BO framework (Williams & Rasmussen 2006; Snoek et al. 2012) and the gradient vanishing literature from deep learning (Bengio et al. 1994; Hanin 2018), applying Hoeffding's inequality to derive probabilistic bounds on when GP training fails. The most closely related prior work is VBO (Hvarfner et al. 2024), which also demonstrated that standard BO can succeed in high dimensions by placing a log-normal prior over length-scales; this paper provides the theoretical explanation for *why* the failure occurs and offers a simpler fix (ℓ₀ = c√d) requiring no additional prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single initialization change (ℓ₀ = c√d) eliminates the primary failure mode of standard BO in high dimensions has immediate practical impact: practitioners no longer need complex structural assumptions or additional priors to handle problems with hundreds or thousands of variables. The probabilistic bounds give clear, actionable thresholds (e.g., SE kernel fails around d≈200 with ℓ₀=0.5, Matérn around d≈1000) that explain observed failures. Open questions remain about whether there is a fundamental upper dimensionality limit for GP-based BO, how optimizer choice interacts with initialization robustness, and whether the √d scaling is optimal or merely sufficient. The paper also highlights that the Matérn kernel, despite being less popular in BO practice, is inherently more robust to high-dimensional settings than the SE kernel.
