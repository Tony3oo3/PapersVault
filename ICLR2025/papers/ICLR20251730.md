## Problem

Differentiating through iterative physics solvers during neural network training imposes a severe computational burden, as both forward and backward passes must execute many solver iterations to achieve tight tolerances. While bilevel optimization theory (Pedregosa, 2016) showed that progressive refinement of inner solvers can reduce cost in convex hyperparameter settings, it remained unexplored whether the same principle—and the additional observation that full physics convergence is unnecessary for neural network accuracy—applies to the sparse linear systems arising from discretized PDEs in nonconvex network training.

## Input / Output

The method operates on discretized PDE state vectors (e.g., velocity/pressure fields for Navier-Stokes, scalar fields for heat diffusion) assembled into sparse linear systems via matrix-free finite-difference operators on uniform Cartesian grids. The solver iteration count K is the controlled variable: it starts at a coarse value (K₀=1, or K₀=4 for strongly divergent regimes like Burgers) and is adaptively increased during training. The output is a trained neural emulator (MLP or ResNet) achieving identical validation accuracy to fully converged physics, but with up to 86% fewer cumulative solver iterations and up to 78% wall-clock training time reduction. The method is agnostic to the specific iterative solver (Jacobi, steepest descent, GMRES) and differentiation scheme (unrolled or implicit).

## Method

PRDP combines two complementary savings mechanisms. First, progressive refinement (PR) savings: training begins with very coarse physics (few solver iterations) and the iteration count is increased by ΔK only when the exponentially smoothed validation metric plateaus, following the bilevel optimization insight that early outer iterations tolerate coarse inner solutions. Second, incomplete convergence (IC) savings: refinement stops at K_max, a level significantly below full convergence K_ε, because neural network training's stochastic mini-batching, momentum-based optimizers, and inherently approximative nature do not require fully converged physics gradients. The algorithm monitors two ratios—r (current vs. grace-window validation loss) for detecting plateaus that trigger refinement, and r_c (current vs. pre-refinement checkpoint) for detecting when further refinement yields no improvement—along with a divergence guard for initially unstable regimes. For implicit differentiation, the same iteration count is applied to both the primal solve and the adjoint linear system (A^T λ = ū_h), coupling primal and adjoint accuracy; for unrolled differentiation, the AD engine naturally unrolls the same number of iterations in both passes.

## Results

PRDP achieves identical validation accuracy (nMSE) to fully converged physics while reducing cumulative solver iterations by 59–86% across tested problems. In the 1D heat diffusion emulator, PRDP saves 79% of inner iterations (60% IC + 19% PR); in 2D heat, 86% (72% IC + 14% PR) with a 78% reduction in wall-clock training time; in 3D heat, 81% savings with 24% training time reduction. For the Navier-Stokes neural-hybrid emulator, PRDP reduces total iterations by 81% (65% IC + 16% PR), yielding a 62% reduction in training time. The Poisson inverse problem demonstrates 33–48% PR savings depending on parameter dimensionality and solver choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's baseline is training with fully converged physics (K_ε iterations to reach ε=10⁻⁵ tolerance). PRDP matches the baseline's final validation nMSE in every tested scenario while using substantially fewer solver iterations. The method is agnostic to both the iterative solver (Jacobi, Steepest Descent, GMRES) and the differentiation strategy (unrolled vs. implicit), achieving nearly identical savings under each combination. In the Navier-Stokes neural-hybrid setting, even accounting for the additional cost of running fully converged physics during validation (required because inference uses the converged solver), PRDP remains 57% faster than the fully converged baseline. No competing adaptive-refinement or incomplete-convergence method is directly benchmarked; the paper positions itself as the first to combine both PR and IC savings for sparse linear systems in differentiable physics training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pedregosa's (2016) progressive refinement framework for bilevel optimization from convex hyperparameter tuning to the nonconvex setting of neural network training through differentiable physics. It also draws on incomplete convergence results from deep equilibrium models (Shaban et al., 2019; Fung et al., 2021; Geng et al., 2021) and truncated backpropagation, but applies these ideas to a fundamentally different inner problem: iterative linear solvers for sparse PDE discretizations rather than dense nonlinear root-finding. The key departure is the automatic, adaptive detection of both the refinement schedule and the stopping level (K_max) via validation-metric plateauing, making the approach problem-agnostic without hand-tuned schedules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Differentiable physics training is a major computational bottleneck for neural-hybrid solvers, and PRDP's 62–78% training time reduction (with up to 86% fewer solver iterations) could make previously infeasible settings—long temporal unrollings, 3D high-resolution simulations—practically accessible. The adaptive, problem-agnostic nature of the algorithm (no hand-tuned schedules) broadens its applicability across PDE types, solvers, and differentiation methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unstructured meshes, multi-physics coupling, and whether IC savings can also reduce inference cost for neural-hybrid emulators. The method is limited to iterative linear solvers and cannot apply to purely explicit or spectral methods.
