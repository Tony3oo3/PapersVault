## Problem

Conventional quantum machine learning approaches face two practical barriers for LLM fine-tuning: data encoding into quantum states becomes intractable for large inputs, and inference requires quantum hardware. Meanwhile, existing PEFT methods like LoRA and DoRA still require a non-trivial number of trainable parameters and offer limited flexibility in exploring the parameter space between discrete rank values. This paper asks whether quantum circuits can serve as a parameter generator for PEFT methods, reducing trainable parameters further while keeping inference entirely classical.

## Input / Output

The method takes text sequences (WikiText-2, Penn Treebank) as fine-tuning input and produces a fine-tuned LLM (GPT-2 80M, GPT-2 XL 1.5B, or Gemma-2 2B) that generates text. Unusually, the trainable parameters of the PEFT layer (low-rank matrices A and B for LoRA/DoRA, prefix vectors for PT, or adapter weights for FFA) are not directly optimized but are generated at each training step by a PQC whose measurement probabilities are mapped through a classical MLP. The quantum circuit uses only 4–11 qubits with 8-layer R_Y+CNOT ansatz, and the batched generation scheme splits the target parameter vector into chunks of n_mlp elements to reduce qubit count from ceil(log2(m)) to ceil(log2(ceil(m/n_mlp))).

## Method

QPA replaces direct training of PEFT parameters with a two-stage generation pipeline: a PQC with N qubits and L repetitions of the R_Y+CNOT ansatz produces 2^N basis-state measurement probabilities, and a classical MLP mapping model (with hidden dimensions [32,64,128,128,64,32,n_mlp]) maps each basis state's binary representation and probability to a batch of n_mlp target parameters. Gradients of the LLM loss flow back through the mapping model to the quantum parameters via the chain rule (Eq. 3), enabling end-to-end optimization with AdamW. The key insight is that the exponentially large Hilbert space (2^N) provides an efficient representation for adaptation: only O(polylog(m)) quantum parameters control m classical parameters, and the high-dimensional space enables finer-grained exploration than discrete rank choices in LoRA. Batched parameter generation further reduces qubit requirements by a factor of ceil(log2(n_mlp)), cutting GPU memory for quantum state storage proportionally. Experiments on GPT-2 and Gemma-2 show QPA reduces trainable parameters to 52.06% (GPT-2) and 16.84% (Gemma-2) of standard LoRA while matching or slightly improving perplexity.

## Results

On WikiText-2, QPA LoRA reduces trainable parameters to 52.06% of standard LoRA for GPT-2 (106,264 vs. 204,100 params) while improving perplexity from 1.595 to 1.583 (0.75% gain). For Gemma-2, QPA LoRA achieves 16.84% of LoRA's parameters (173,888 vs. 1,032,192) with a marginal perplexity improvement (1.417 vs. 1.418). Across all four PEFT methods (LoRA, DoRA, PT, FFA), QPA reduces parameter counts by roughly an order of magnitude (e.g., 0.40% → 0.01% for FFA on Gemma-2) while maintaining comparable or improved text-generation perplexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QPA LoRA outperforms standard LoRA on both GPT-2 (PPL 1.583 vs. 1.595) and Gemma-2 (PPL 1.417 vs. 1.418) while using substantially fewer trainable parameters (52.06% and 16.84% respectively). QPA DoRA similarly beats DoRA on both models (GPT-2: 4.955 vs. 5.003; Gemma-2: 5.487 vs. 5.504) with roughly 16× fewer parameters on GPT-2. QPA FFA wins on GPT-2 (PPL 1.689 vs. 1.763) but underperforms FFA on Gemma-2 (1.507 vs. 1.439), though it still uses only 2.5% of FFA's parameters. QPA PT is the weakest comparison: it loses to PT on GPT-2 (2.327 vs. 2.225, a 4.38% perplexity cost) and is marginally worse on Gemma-2 (1.540 vs. 1.530), though it reduces parameters to 18.45% (GPT-2) and 5% (Gemma-2). Overall, QPA's strongest and most consistent gains are in the LoRA and DoRA settings, where the quantum parameter generation most effectively exploits the low-rank structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantum parameter generation framework (Liu et al., 2024b; Liu & Chen, 2024a), which uses parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) coupled with a classical MLP mapping model to generate classical neural network parameters, by redirecting its output from full model weights to the smaller parameter sets of PEFT methods (LoRA, DoRA, Prefix-Tuning, FFA). The batched parameter generation mechanism (chunking m parameters into n_ch groups of n_mlp) is adopted directly from the federated quantum-train work to reduce qubit requirements from O(log m) to O(log(m/n_mlp)). Conceptually, the paper departs from conventional QML—which encodes input data into quantum states and requires quantum hardware at inference—by confining quantum circuits to the training stage only, so that the resulting fine-tuned model is entirely classical. It also departs from prior quantum parameter generation studies (which targeted models under 1M parameters) by scaling the target to a 0.52B-parameter linear layer in Gemma-2, roughly 1785× larger.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QPA demonstrates that quantum circuits can serve as a parameter-compression mechanism for PEFT, reducing trainable parameters by ~48% (GPT-2 LoRA) to ~83% (Gemma-2 LoRA) while maintaining or marginally improving perplexity, and it does so with only 4–11 qubits—well within near-term hardware reach. However, several limitations temper the impact: experiments fine-tune only the final lm_head layer (not the full model), quantum circuits are simulated classically via TorchQuantum rather than run on actual hardware, and QPA incurs roughly 3× the wall-clock time of standard LoRA. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-layer fine-tuning, whether convergence and trainability can be formally characterized, and whether the marginal perplexity gains (0.07–0.75%) persist under realistic noise and finite-shot conditions on real quantum processors.
