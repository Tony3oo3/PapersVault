## Problem

Most prior work on adversarial bandits focuses on designing attacks to force linear regret, while robust defense algorithms for the strong-adversary attack model—where the attacker observes the learner's action before altering the reward—remain largely unexplored. Existing corruption-robust algorithms rely on randomized arm pulling, which is invalid under the attack model since the adversary can adapt to the observed action. This paper is the first to develop robust algorithms with tight regret bounds for stochastic MAB under adversarial attacks, for both known and unknown attack budgets.

## Input / Output

The learner receives only attacked reward observations (not true rewards) after pulling an arm, with the total attack budget C defined as the sum of absolute reward alterations over T rounds. The algorithms take K arms, horizon T, and either a known C or operate without knowledge of C (unknown/adaptive budget case), plus a confidence parameter δ. The output is a per-round arm selection policy together with regret upper bounds expressed in terms of K, T, C, and the reward gap Δ; regret is measured against raw (unattacked) rewards, making it a stronger metric than pseudo-regret.

## Method

The core mechanism is successive elimination with a widened confidence radius: the standard Hoeffding confidence interval is augmented by an additive C/N_k term to account for worst-case attack, enabling deterministic (non-randomized) elimination of suboptimal arms. For known C, SE-WR achieves a gap-dependent bound O(Σ log T/Δ_k + KC), and two stopping-condition variants (SE-WR-Stop) yield gap-independent additive O(√(KT log T) + KC) and multiplicative O(√(KT(log T + C))) bounds. For unknown C, PE-WR applies a multi-phase structure where each phase assumes a halved attack budget, achieving an additive Ō(√(KT) + KC²) bound, while MS-SE-WR runs log₂T instances of SE-WR-Stop with geometrically increasing C inputs and applies CORRAL or EXP3.P model selection to achieve multiplicative Ō(√(KC)·T^{2/3}) or Ō(KC√T) bounds. Matching lower bounds are provided, including a general Ω(KC) bound and class-specific bounds that confirm tightness in both T and C dependence.

## Results

For the known attack budget case, SE-WR achieves a gap-dependent regret of O(Σ_{k≠k*} log T/Δ_k + KC), improving upon the prior O(Σ_{k≠k*} log T/Δ_k + Σ_{k≠k*} C/Δ_k) by removing the 1/Δ_k dependence on C. The gap-independent SE-WR-Stop variants achieve O(√(KT log T) + KC) (additive) and O(√(KT(log T + C))) (multiplicative). For the unknown attack budget case, PE-WR achieves Õ(√(KT) + KC²) (additive) and MS-SE-WR achieves Õ(√(KC)·T^{2/3}) or Õ(KC√T) (multiplicative), all matching corresponding lower bounds up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baseline families: UCB and SE (standard non-robust bandit algorithms), FSAAER and MLAAER (designed for the corruption/medium-adversary model by Lykouris et al., 2018), and BARBAR (designed for unknown corruption by Gupta et al., 2019). Under the strong-adversary attack model, all corruption-based baselines (FSAAER, MLAAER, BARBAR) fail to maintain sublinear regret as the attack budget C grows: MLAAER performs poorly at C=6000 and fails entirely at C=9000, while FSAAER degrades progressively. The proposed SE-WR and SE-WR-Stop (known C) and PE-WR and MS-SE-WR (unknown C) consistently achieve sublinear regret across all tested budgets (C ∈ {0, 3000, 6000, 9000}) with K=10 arms and T=200,000 rounds. For large attack budgets (C=9000), only the multiplicative-bound MS-SE-WR algorithms maintain sublinear regret, confirming the theoretical prediction that multiplicative bounds are necessary when C > Ω(√T). The paper also demonstrates an intrinsic separation: with the same budget, an attack produces K times larger regret impact than a corruption (Θ(KC) vs. Θ(C')), and in the unknown-budget case the C² additive term is tight versus the C' linear term for corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust bandit literature from the corruption model (medium adversary, Lykouris et al. 2018; Gupta et al. 2019) to the harder adversarial attack model (strong adversary), where the attacker observes the learner's action before modifying rewards. It repurposes the SE-WR successive elimination framework from Lykouris et al. (2018) and the phase elimination structure from Bogunovic et al. (2021) for linear bandits, but must abandon the randomized arm-pulling strategies that underpin corruption-robust algorithms since they are ineffective against a strong adversary. The key methodological departure is a tighter analysis of elimination conditions (Lemma 14) that removes the 1/Δ_k dependence on the attack budget C, enabling both additive and multiplicative C-dependence bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a fundamental separation between corruption and attack models: an O(√T) attack budget suffices to cause linear regret, whereas O(T) corruption is needed for the same effect, and the attack-induced regret scales as Θ(KC) versus Θ(C) for corruption. This clarifies why prior corruption-robust algorithms fail under attacks and provides tight matching upper and lower bounds for both known and unknown attack budgets. Open questions include whether the gap between additive (KC²) and multiplicative (KC√T) bounds for unknown C can be closed, whether the results extend to structured bandits (linear, Lipschitz) with matching tightness, and whether verification schemes (Rangi et al., 2022) can bridge the corruption-attack gap.
