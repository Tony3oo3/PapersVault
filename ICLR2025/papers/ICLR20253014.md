## Problem

Token-prediction motion generation models trained with next-token prediction objectives produce multi-agent behaviors that deviate from human preferences, necessitating post-training alignment. However, obtaining preference rankings at scale for complex multi-agent scenes (100+ agents) is prohibitively expensive, and existing Alignment-from-Demonstrations methods adopt an adversarial assumption that treats all model-generated samples as unpreferred, discarding the nuanced preference signal among the model's own generations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253014/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 1 second of past observations as scene context for up to 128 heterogeneous agents at 10 Hz and generates 8-second joint action token sequences (one token per agent per timestep). For preference data construction, 64 rollouts are sampled per scene context and ranked using an OT-based preference distance computed over agent-level occupancy measures in a 5-dimensional feature space (collision status, road boundary distance, minimum clearance, control effort, speed) with L2 cost weights [10,5,2,1,1]. The top 16 and bottom 16 rollouts form 16 pairwise preference comparisons per training example, all derived without human annotation.

## Method

DPA-OMF defines an implicit preference distance via optimal transport: for each agent, it computes the Wasserstein-2 coupling between the rollout's empirical occupancy measure and the expert demonstration's occupancy measure in a semantic feature space, then aggregates agent-level couplings into a scene-level distance. This distance ranks K rollouts from the reference model per scene context, constructing preference pairs (top-16 vs. bottom-16) that capture relative quality among the model's own generations rather than treating all as negative. The reference model is then fine-tuned with a multi-agent extension of contrastive preference learning, which maximizes the log-likelihood ratio of preferred over unpreferred token sequences relative to the frozen reference model. This avoids the heterogeneity bias of adversarial AFD (where positive and negative examples come from different distributions) and the distributional collapse of SFT-on-best-samples, while requiring no reward learning, RL, or additional human annotations.

## Results

On the WOSAC challenge (up to 128 agents, 8-second generation at 10 Hz), DPA-OMF improves the 1M-parameter MotionLM reference model's composite realism from 0.721 to 0.739, while also boosting map compliance from 0.815 to 0.867. The aligned model matches the 35M-parameter Trajeglish on kinematic (0.415), interactive (0.786), and map compliance (0.867) scores, and approaches the 3M-parameter BehaviorGPT on composite realism (0.739 vs. 0.747). All improvements are achieved with zero additional human annotation, using only implicit preference feedback from pre-training expert demonstrations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 35M-parameter Trajeglish, DPA-OMF (1M) matches exactly on kinematic (0.415), interactive (0.786), and map compliance (0.867), while substantially outperforming it on composite realism (0.739 vs. 0.721) and minADE (1.413 vs. 1.544). Against the 3M-parameter BehaviorGPT, DPA-OMF is close on composite realism (0.739 vs. 0.747) and minADE (1.413 vs. 1.415) but trails on kinematic (0.415 vs. 0.433) and interactive (0.786 vs. 0.799). Against the 102M-parameter SMART, DPA-OMF remains behind on composite realism (0.739 vs. 0.761) and kinematic (0.415 vs. 0.479), though it matches on map compliance (0.867 vs. 0.864). Among the same-size baselines, DPA-OMF clearly outperforms DPA-ADEF (0.739 vs. 0.714), SFT-bestADE (0.739 vs. 0.715), and SFT-bestOA (0.739 vs. 0.723) on composite realism, confirming that ranking among the model's own generations is more effective than adversarial or supervised fine-tuning approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM post-training preference alignment paradigm (DPO, contrastive preference learning) to multi-agent motion generation, using MotionLM's autoregressive token-prediction architecture as the base model. Its key departure from prior Alignment-from-Demonstrations (AFD) methods is rejecting the adversarial assumption that all model-generated samples are unpreferred; instead, it ranks the model's own rollouts against each other using an optimal-transport-based occupancy measure distance to expert demonstrations, drawing on inverse reinforcement learning principles. The contrastive preference learning objective from Hejna et al. (2023) is adapted to the multi-agent setting, and the OT distance replaces the need for a learned reward model or adversarial discriminator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that preference alignment can meaningfully improve large-scale multi-agent motion generation (100+ agents) using only implicit feedback from pre-training data, with zero additional human annotation cost. The finding that a 1M-parameter model becomes competitive with 35M–102M-parameter imitation-based models highlights that alignment quality can partially substitute for model scale. Open questions include whether the OT-based preference distance generalizes beyond traffic simulation to other embodied domains (robotics, humanoid locomotion), how feature selection in the occupancy measure affects alignment quality in more complex settings, and whether the observed preference over-optimization (small data degrades performance) persists at larger model scales. The paper's single-dataset, single-architecture evaluation limits confidence in broader applicability.
