## Problem

Standard pixel-wise losses (e.g., Dice) ignore topological errors that can arise from single-pixel mistakes, while existing topology-aware methods either lack strict theoretical guarantees, are restricted to specific structure types (e.g., tubular), or incur high computational costs from persistent homology. This paper seeks a loss that is both computationally efficient and provides provably strict topological guarantees beyond mere homotopy equivalence of prediction and ground truth.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 2D image (RGB or grayscale, e.g., 128×128 crops or 800×800 patches) and produces a per-pixel segmentation mask. During training, the loss operates on the overlay of the network's binarized prediction and the ground truth, forming a 4-class combined image (TP/TN/FP/FN) that is partitioned into superpixels via connected component labeling. Multi-class segmentation is handled by decomposing the problem into multiple binary sub-problems, scaling complexity linearly with the number of classes.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs a combined component graph G(P,G) whose vertices are connected components of the 4-class overlay and whose edges encode spatial adjacency, yielding a bipartite planar graph that fully encodes the topology of both prediction and ground truth. A vertex is deemed 'critical' if it is misclassified and does not have exactly one correctly-predicted foreground neighbor and one correctly-predicted background neighbor; only critical vertices contribute to the loss, which aggregates the mean predicted class score over each critical region. The paper proves that L_CG = 0 if and only if the inclusion maps from the intersection to the prediction and from the prediction to the union are deformation retractions, a strictly stronger guarantee than homotopy equivalence alone. The method runs in O(n·α(n)) time via union-find connected-component labeling, achieving 3–6× speedup over persistent-homology-based losses. A complementary DIU metric, derived via Alexander duality from the kernel and cokernel of the inclusion-induced homology map between intersection and union, provides a sensitive evaluation measure that captures spatial correspondence errors invisible to Betti-number or Betti-matching metrics.

## Results

Topograph achieves the best DIU and Betti Matching (BM) scores across all five datasets (binary: Buildings, Roads, Cremi; multi-class: Platelet, TopCoW), with DIU values of 77.82, 6.52, 21.27, 10.91, and 10.48 respectively. Pixel-wise Dice scores remain competitive with baselines (within ~0.01–0.02), confirming that topological gains do not come at the cost of segmentation accuracy. The method also achieves the best clDice on Platelet (0.843) and TopCoW (0.801), and is within 0.01 of the best on Roads and Cremi. Loss computation is 3–6× faster than PH-based methods (95.94 ms vs. 327–603 ms per loss calculation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Topograph outperforms all four baselines on the two strictest topological metrics (DIU and BM) across all five datasets. Against Betti Matching (Stucki et al., ICML 2023), the closest PH-based competitor, Topograph achieves lower DIU on every dataset (e.g., 10.48 vs. 12.35 on TopCoW; 6.52 vs. 6.73 on Roads) while matching or exceeding BM scores, confirming that enforcing homotopy equivalence between intersection and union captures spatial correspondences that BM's barcode matching alone misses. Against HuTopo (Hu et al., NeurIPS 2019), Topograph consistently wins on DIU and BM; HuTopo's notably low B0 paired with high BM/DIU reveals its known limitation of optimizing feature counts without spatial alignment. ClDice (Shit et al., CVPR 2021) is competitive on tubular datasets (Cremi, Roads, TopCoW) where centerline connectivity is the dominant topological feature, but degrades severely on the blob-like Platelet dataset (DIU 50.16 vs. 10.91 for Topograph), highlighting its lack of generality. The Mosin loss (Mosinska et al., CVPR 2018) is outperformed on DIU and BM across all datasets and shows the highest variance, particularly on TopCoW (DIU 23.53±16.95). In terms of pixel-wise accuracy, Topograph's Dice scores are within 0.01–0.02 of the best baseline on each dataset, confirming that topological gains do not sacrifice segmentation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOPOGRAPH carves new ground by repurposing the classical connected-component graph (a standard tool in image processing) as a topological loss computation substrate, rather than relying on persistent homology barcodes or task-specific skeleton-based losses. Conceptually, it departs from PH-based methods (Hu et al. 2019; Stucki et al. 2023) by encoding the full topological relationship between prediction and ground truth in a single bipartite graph, enabling a loss that enforces homotopy equivalence not just between prediction and label but also between their union and intersection via inclusion maps — a strictly stronger guarantee than Betti Matching provides. It also generalizes task-specific approaches like clDice (Shit et al. 2021), which are limited to tubular structures, into a framework applicable to arbitrary 2D topologies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous achievement of stricter topological guarantees (deformation retractions between intersection, prediction, and union) and 3–6× lower computational cost than PH-based methods makes topology-aware segmentation practical for full-resolution medical and natural images where barcode computation is prohibitive. The DIU metric exposes blind spots in existing evaluation: Betti numbers and even Betti matching can score two predictions identically while DIU distinguishes spatially misaligned topological features, offering a more sensitive diagnostic for practitioners. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to 2D, the binarization step discards sub-threshold topological information (partially mitigated by the threshold-variation heuristic), and extending the framework to 3D cubical complexes or integrating it with full filtrations are open directions that could broaden its applicability.
