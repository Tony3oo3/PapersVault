## Problem

LLMs trained to refuse harmful queries frequently exhibit false refusal, declining safe requests that superficially resemble unsafe ones (e.g., 'how do I kill a Python process?'). Existing mitigation methods are either training-based and inflexible (safety can only be calibrated at training time) or training-free but computationally expensive at inference and imprecise, causing unintended degradation of general model capability. The paper seeks a single intervention that is simultaneously surgical (preserves safety and general capability), cheap (no additional inference cost), and flexible (enables fine-grained calibration of the safety boundary).

## Input / Output

The method requires a pre-trained LLM and small sets of harmful (128), pseudo-harmful (128), and harmless (128) text prompts for vector extraction, plus 32 validation samples from each category to select the most effective candidate vector. The output is a modified model whose output projection weight matrices in the Attention and FNN blocks have been edited to remove the false refusal direction, or equivalently an inference-time activation steering operation applied at a single layer. The modified model accepts standard text prompts and produces text responses with no additional memory or inference-time cost relative to the original model.

## Method

The paper extracts a true refusal vector via diff-in-means between harmful and harmless query activations, and a false refusal vector via diff-in-means between pseudo-harmful and harmless query activations, selecting the most effective candidate at each layer and token position by the drop in refusal score after ablation. The key insight is that these two vectors overlap in activation space, so ablating the raw false refusal vector also removes true refusal behavior; to fix this, the paper orthogonalizes the false refusal vector against the true refusal vector, removing the component along the true refusal direction before ablation. A partial orthogonalization coefficient λ ∈ [0,1] scales the projection removal, enabling fine-grained control: lower λ values modify the false refusal vector less, producing stronger refusal removal and more compliant responses to ambiguous queries. The ablation is implemented by projecting the residual stream onto the orthogonalized false refusal direction and subtracting this projection, which is algebraically equivalent to a one-time edit of the model's output projection weight matrices (W′ = W − rrᵀW), requiring no additional inference cost.

## Results

Across five chat-tuned LLMs (Gemma-7B-IT, Llama3-8B-Inst, Llama2-7B/13B/70B-Chat), ablating the orthogonalized false refusal vector substantially increases compliance on pseudo-harmful queries while preserving safety: e.g., Llama2-7B-Chat's ORB-Hard compliance rises from 14.8% to 65.6% and XSTest-Safe(H) from 13.6% to 42.4%, while harmful-query compliance stays low (1.6%→5.4%). General capability is essentially unchanged, with absolute shifts below 1.0 point on MMLU, ARC-C, and WikiText PPL across all models. The method requires no additional inference-time computation or memory, as the ablation is folded directly into the model's projection weight matrices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SCAN (Cao et al., 2024), the proposed method achieves a lower XSTest-Unsafe compliance rate (0% vs. 6.5%), indicating fewer safety regressions, while matching Self-CD (Shi et al., 2024) on XSTest-Safe (85.2% vs. 85.2%) and substantially outperforming SafeDecoding (Xu et al., 2024) at 85.2% vs. 75.2%. On general capability, the method retains 47.2% MMLU accuracy versus SCAN's 40.5%, with only +0.1 PPL change versus SCAN's +1.56, and requires no extra inference time or memory (SCAN increases both). Compared to the training-based DRO baseline (Zheng et al., 2024), the method achieves 85.2% XSTest-Safe compliance versus DRO's 58.5%, while DRO's lower XSTest-Unsafe rate (1.5%) suggests it over-refuses. The key advantage is that the ablation is prompt-agnostic and folded into weights, unlike SCAN's query-dependent classifier or Self-CD's dual inference, making it strictly cheaper at deployment time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representation-engineing line of work initiated by Zou et al. (2023a) and, more directly, by Arditi et al. (2024), who showed that a single diff-in-means vector can control general refusal behaviour in LLMs. The key departure is the recognition that a single refusal vector conflates true and false refusal, and the introduction of an orthogonalization step (and its partial variant) to disentangle the two, enabling surgical removal of false refusal without compromising safety. Compared to prior training-free mitigations (SCAN, Self-CD) that add inference-time classifiers or dual decoding passes, this method bakes the intervention into the model weights at zero additional cost, and compared to training-based calibrations (DRO, Safe Unlearning) it offers post-hoc, continuous adjustment via the λ parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

False refusal is a practical and growing concern as LLMs are deployed in safety-critical settings where over-refusal degrades user trust and utility; a training-free, model-agnostic, zero-overhead intervention is immediately deployable on existing checkpoints. The partial-orthogonalization dial (λ) is a useful practical tool for operators who need to tune the safety/helpfulness trade-off per deployment context without retraining. Open questions include whether the approach generalises to more diverse or adversarially constructed pseudo-harmful prompts, whether sequence-level (rather than first-token) refusal metrics would yield stronger vectors, and whether the single-vector assumption holds for more complex or multi-turn refusal behaviours.
