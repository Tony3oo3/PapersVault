## Problem

Existing LLM mathematics benchmarks are dominated by problems with exact, closed-form solutions at grade-school through high-school difficulty, leaving a critical gap for graduate-level applied mathematics that requires approximate analytical techniques such as dominant balance, Laplace's method, and asymptotic expansion. These approximation-oriented problems—ubiquitous in real scientific and engineering contexts—demand a combination of mathematical reasoning, computational tool use, and subjective judgment that current benchmarks do not test.

## Input / Output

Each problem is a LaTeX-formatted statement (polynomial, ODE, or integral) accompanied by problem-type-specific format hints instructing the model to output answers in SymPy-convertible expressions, Python lists of roots, or power-law forms enclosed in \boxed{}. Unlike standard math benchmarks with a single exact answer, HARDMATH problems require approximate solutions valid in multiple parameter regimes (e.g., small ε, large ε, very large ε), so the output is a set of regime-specific formulae rather than one closed-form result. Evaluation combines automatic SymPy/numerical equivalence checking of final answers with a novel GPT-4o-based procedural grader that scores intermediate reasoning steps against course-style rubrics.

## Method

HARDMATH is algorithmically generated using SymPy and SciPy: problems are constructed by randomly sampling coefficients, polynomial degrees, integration bounds, and initial conditions, while solutions are produced by systematically navigating all possible dominant-balance cases and verifying self-consistency. Each generated solution is validated against numerically computed ground truths, with problems retained only if the approximate solution achieves less than 10% relative error across evaluation points in every regime. The evaluation protocol tests six LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, o1-mini, Llama3-8b, CodeLlama-13b) under zero-shot and few-shot Chain-of-Thought prompting, scoring via both automatic answer matching and LLM-based procedural grading that awards partial credit for correct intermediate steps. A key design choice is the inclusion of 40 hand-crafted word problems embedding the same mathematics in realistic scientific contexts, plus a preliminary pipeline for auto-generating domain-specific contexts via GPT-4o with a secondary plausibility verifier.

## Results

On HARDMATH-MINI (366 problems), o1-mini with 5-shot CoT prompting achieves the highest overall accuracy of 62.3%, followed by GPT-4 at 43.8%, GPT-3.5 at 24.6%, Llama3-8b at 20.2%, and CodeLlama-13b at 9.79%. ODEs are the hardest problem type for all models (o1-mini: 30.6%, GPT-4: 21.7%), while Nondim is the easiest (o1-mini: 84.5%, GPT-4: 48.6%). On the 40 hand-crafted word problems in applied science contexts, GPT-4 achieves only 28.1% overall accuracy without problem-specific hints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

o1-mini (5-shot CoT) outperforms GPT-4 (5-shot CoT) by 18.5 percentage points overall (62.3% vs. 43.8%), with the largest gap on Nondim (84.5% vs. 48.6%) and ODEs (30.6% vs. 21.7%). GPT-4 substantially outperforms GPT-3.5 (43.8% vs. 24.6%) and both open-source models (Llama3-8b: 20.2%, CodeLlama-13b: 9.79%). Critically, all models perform far below their scores on established benchmarks: GPT-4 drops from 72.2% on MATH and 92.0% on GSM-8K to 43.8% on HARDMATH-MINI; o1-mini drops from 90.0% on MATH-500 to 62.3%; and Llama3-8b drops from 30.0% on MATH to 20.2%. Few-shot CoT prompting consistently improves performance across all models, with o1-mini and GPT-4 showing the largest gains (e.g., o1-mini jumps from 29.8% to 62.3% between 0-shot and 5-shot). On the word-problem extension, GPT-4's accuracy drops further to 28.1% when problem-specific format hints are removed, suggesting that contextual framing adds an additional challenge beyond the core mathematical reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM mathematical reasoning evaluation line (MATH, GSM8K, MATHQA) into a substantially different sub-domain: asymptotic and approximation methods from graduate-level applied mathematics. While prior graduate-level benchmarks (GHOSTS, ARB) exist, they are small, manually curated, and focus on abstract/formal mathematics; HARDMATH departs by algorithmically generating problems centered on the Method of Dominant Balance, Laplace's method, and nondimensionalization—techniques that require subjective judgment and numerical verification rather than closed-form derivation. The evaluation methodology builds on Hendrycks et al. (2021) for automatic answer checking but introduces a novel LLM-based procedural grading protocol to handle the multi-regime, approximate-answer nature of the problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HARDMATH fills a clear gap: no prior dataset offers a scalable, algorithmically generated collection of graduate-level applied math problems requiring approximation reasoning, which is the dominant mode of mathematical work in science and engineering. The finding that even o1-mini (62.3%) and GPT-4 (43.8%) perform far below their scores on MATH or GSM8K suggests current LLMs lack robust approximation reasoning, a capability critical for scientific tool-use. Open questions include whether fine-tuning on the full 1,060-problem HARDMATH set closes the gap, whether the automated word-problem context generation scales reliably, and how error modes evolve as models improve. A limitation is that o1-mini's tendency to omit intermediate steps hampers fine-grained error analysis.
