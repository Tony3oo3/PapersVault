## Problem

Existing bilevel optimization methods for constrained lower-level problems are restricted to linear constraints or constraints depending only on the lower-level variable, and typically require strong convexity of the LL objective or second-order (Hessian) computations. No first-order, single-loop, Hessian-free algorithm exists for the general case where LL constraints couple both upper-level and lower-level variables without imposing strong convexity or full convexity on the constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes the UL objective F(x,y), LL objective f(x,y), LL constraint mapping g(x,y)≤0 (which may couple x and y), and closed convex feasible sets X⊂Rⁿ and Y⊂Rᵐ. It produces an approximate stationary solution (x*, y*) of the original bilevel problem via the penalized single-level reformulation, with a convergence rate of O(1/√K) on the KKT residual. The algorithm introduces an auxiliary multiplier variable z∈[0,r]ᵖ and an auxiliary primal variable θ∈Y, so the actual iterates are (x,y,z,θ,λ), but the final output is the (x,y) pair satisfying the LL optimality condition.

## Method

The core technique is a doubly regularized gap function G_γ(x,y,z) = max_{θ,λ} {L(x,y,λ) − (1/2γ₂)‖λ−z‖² − L(x,θ,z) − (1/2γ₁)‖θ−y‖²} that encodes the LL KKT conditions into a single smooth inequality constraint G_γ(x,y,z)≤0, eliminating the need for projection onto the coupled LL constraint set. The bilevel problem is reformulated as min F(x,y) s.t. G_γ(x,y,z)≤0, then solved via a penalty formulation with an iteratively increasing parameter c_k = c(k+1)^ρ. BiC-GAFFA is a single-loop, first-order, Hessian-free algorithm that at each iteration performs one projected gradient step on an auxiliary variable θ to approximate the inner minimizer, computes λ via a simple projection onto R₊ᵖ, and updates (x,y,z) in parallel using the approximate gradient of the penalized objective. The convergence analysis requires only convexity (not strong convexity) of the LL objective and first-order differentiability of all problem functions, with no coupling convexity assumption on the LL constraints.

## Results

On the sparse group LASSO hyperparameter selection problem (nTr=100, nVal=100, nTest=300), BiC-GAFFA achieves a validation error of 7.3±1.3 and test error of 22.3±3.0, substantially outperforming the next-best method VF-iDCA (14.6±2.6 val, 25.4±3.9 test). On the GAN distribution recovery task, BiC-GAFFA variants (Bi-GAN, Bi-WGAN, Bi-ConGAN) reach EMD<0.5 in 2.0–2.6 seconds, compared to 17–60 seconds for standard GAN, WGAN, WGAN-GP, Con-GAN, and UGAN baselines. On the synthetic coupled-constraint bilevel problem (n=1000, q=3), BiC-GAFFA converges correctly while AiPOD fails entirely and LV-HBA is significantly slower due to costly projection steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiC-GAFFA outperforms all 12 baselines across the evaluated tasks. On the synthetic coupled-constraint problem, AiPOD (Xiao et al., 2023) fails to converge on either q=1 or q=3, while GAM (Xu & Zhu, 2023) and BVFSM (Liu et al., 2023a) converge but much more slowly; LV-HBA (Yao et al., 2024) performs well for q=1 but degrades significantly for q=3 due to the high cost of projecting onto the coupled constraint set, whereas BiC-GAFFA avoids this projection entirely. On the sparse group LASSO task, BiC-GAFFA reduces validation error from 14.6 (VF-iDCA, Gao et al., 2022) to 7.3, and from 19.7 (IGJO, Feng & Simon, 2018) to 7.3, while also improving test error over all baselines. On the GAN distribution recovery benchmark, BiC-GAFFA variants reach EMD<0.5 in approximately 2 seconds, an order-of-magnitude speedup over WGAN (17.2s), WGAN-GP (31.5s), Con-GAN (36.4s), and standard GAN (60.0s), with UGAN (Metz et al., 2016) also failing in 3–4 out of 20 runs. On the SVM and data hyper-cleaning tasks, BiC-GAFFA converges faster than both GAM and LV-HBA while achieving lower validation and test errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the doubly regularized gap function (originally from Nikaido-Isoda-type reformulations for generalized Nash equilibrium problems, Von Heusinger & Kanzow 2009) to the constrained bilevel optimization setting, where it had not previously been applied. It departs from the value-function-based reformulation line (Ye & Zhu 1995; Kwon et al. 2023; Shen & Chen 2023) and the proximal Lagrangian value function approach of Yao et al. (2024) by integrating coupled lower-level constraints directly into a single smooth inequality constraint, thereby eliminating the need for Euclidean projection onto the coupled constraint set. The first-order penalty method framework follows Liu et al. (2023a), Shen & Chen (2023), and Kwon et al. (2024), but the key distinction is that prior constrained BiO methods either restrict to linear LL constraints, require strong convexity of the LL objective, or assume coupling convexity of the constraints—none of which are needed here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing constrained bilevel optimization to a single smooth inequality constraint without requiring strong convexity or coupling convexity, this work opens the door to first-order, Hessian-free algorithms for a substantially broader class of constrained BiO problems (adversarial learning, federated learning, meta-learning with constraints). The extension to minimax lower-level problems suggests a unifying framework. However, the convergence analysis is limited to convex LL problems, the experiments are confined to small-scale synthetic and 2D GAN settings, and the auxiliary sequences (θ, λ) introduce additional memory overhead that may be prohibitive at scale. Open questions include extending the analysis to nonconvex LL objectives, reducing memory footprint, and validating on large-scale real-world constrained BiO applications.
