## Problem

In in-context learning for LLM planning, the standard practice of selecting exemplars by task-description similarity can produce false positives: semantically near-identical task descriptions (e.g., 'put b1 on the table' vs. 'put b100 on the table') can require completely different action sequences and strategies. It remained unclear which contextual signals actually help the model plan correctly, and whether plan-level information could serve as a more reliable selection criterion than surface-level task similarity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a planning task description (in PDDL predicate format or natural language), a pool of exemplar candidates (each pairing a task description with a reference plan), and a model-generated initial plan obtained by a preliminary ICL call with random exemplars. Plans are reduced to action-type sequences by stripping object identifiers; similarity is computed as the squared length of the Longest Common Action Sequence (LCAS) divided by the product of the two sequence lengths. The output is a dynamically-sized, test-example-specific set of exemplars (typically 6–17 on average) that forms the ICL prompt, from which the LLM produces an executable, validator-checkable action plan.

## Method

GRASE-DC is a two-stage exemplar-selection pipeline built around Action Sequence Similarity (AS). In the GRASE stage, the model's initial plan (generated with random exemplars) is converted to an action sequence and used to rank all candidates by AS, replacing the need for Oracle plans. In the DC stage, Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering is applied over the reciprocal-of-AS distance matrix among the top-ranked candidates; a relevance threshold (mean + 1σ) discards weakly related exemplars, and a per-cluster cap (≤3 exemplars) enforces diversity, yielding a dynamic exemplar count per test example without a fixed Top-N hyperparameter. The pipeline is iterative (GRASE-DC*): the newly generated plan re-enters GRASE for a second round of re-sampling and clustering. An optional validator (VAL) gates re-sampling to only failed examples, functioning as rejection sampling. For efficiency, the paper further proposes an MLP regressor over Gecko embeddings (95% of GRASE performance at ~66% FLOPs) and a BPE-Proxy that pre-computes AS against ~200 frequent action sub-sequences (83% performance at ~27% FLOPs), both avoiding the extra LLM prompt that GRASE requires.

## Results

GRASE-DC achieves up to ~11–40 point absolute planning accuracy improvement over random exemplar selection across PDDL tasks (Blocksworld, Minigrid, Tetris, Logistics) and natural language Trip Planning, while requiring 27.3% fewer exemplars on average. The iterative variant GRASE-DC*+VAL further boosts performance by up to 18.9% with 39% fewer exemplars. On the hardest OOD setting (8–20 blocks with 3–7 block exemplars), GRASE-DC delivers a 23.3-point absolute accuracy gain over Random, versus 16.3 points in-distribution. Efficiency approximations (MLP, BPE-Proxy) retain 95% and 83% of GRASE's performance at 66% and 27% FLOPs respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Random baseline (Valmeekam et al., 2023a), GRASE-DC wins decisively on every task: Blocksworld 80% vs. 43%, Tetris 54.7% vs. 5.7%, Logistics 44.3% vs. 20.7%, and OOD Blocksworld 40.7% vs. 16.0%. Against the Task-description similarity baseline (QA-F1, following Khashabi et al., 2020), GRASE-DC is consistently superior; notably, Task similarity is actually *worse* than Random on Blocksworld (41% vs. 43%) and Minigrid (49% vs. 58%), confirming the paper's claim that semantically similar task descriptions can produce drastically different plans. Against the Gecko embedding-based selection (Lee et al., 2024), GRASE achieves 58% vs. 46% best accuracy on Blocksworld (Table 3), demonstrating that plan-level action sequence similarity captures structure that off-the-shelf text embeddings miss. The Oracle-plan AS baseline (Baseline_AS) serves as an upper-bound reference; GRASE with model-generated plans matches or exceeds it at low exemplar counts (e.g., 58% vs. 57% at 4 exemplars on Blocksworld), validating that the model's own plan direction is a useful signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL exemplar selection literature (Rubin et al., 2022; Ye et al., 2023), which has focused on task-description similarity, by arguing that for planning tasks, plan-level action sequence similarity is a fundamentally better signal. It departs from the search-based LLM planning approaches (MCTS, ToT) by showing that a single additional prompt plus clustering over action sequences achieves comparable or better gains at a fraction of the inference cost. The iterative refinement mechanism draws on the STaR/Self-Refine lineage but applies it to exemplar re-selection rather than output correction, and the BPE-Proxy efficiency variant repurposes the classic data-compression algorithm (Gage, 1994) as a similarity proxy over action sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone deploying LLM-based planning agents: a simple, model-agnostic exemplar selection strategy yields 11–40 point accuracy gains with fewer exemplars, and the OOD result (~24 points on harder problems using simpler exemplars) suggests the method can bootstrap capability beyond the training distribution. Open questions include whether the action-sequence representation is optimal (the appendix explores object-centric and execution-based variants with mixed results), whether the approach transfers to open-ended agentic tasks without a formal validator, and whether the MLP/BPE-Proxy approximations close the gap as candidate pools grow to tens of thousands. The paper also leaves the question of how to handle tasks where plans are not easily decomposable into discrete ordered actions.
