## Problem

Existing model-based RL agents for open-world visual control (e.g., DreamerV3) are 'short-sighted,' optimizing policies over only ~15 imagined steps, which is insufficient for tasks requiring navigation across vast state spaces to reach distant targets. The primary challenge is improving exploration efficiency in open worlds where long-horizon payoffs demand the agent to anticipate and plan toward goals that are far from the current observation, rather than relying solely on short-term trial-and-error.

## Input / Output

The agent receives 64×64 RGB image observations and a text task instruction (e.g., 'cut a tree') at each step, and outputs a low-dimensional control action. Additionally, a multimodal U-Net generates a 64×64 affordance map per observation that encodes spatial exploration value relative to the task. The world model further predicts jumpy state transitions annotated with a step interval Δ (number of real environment steps skipped) and a cumulative discounted reward G over that interval, enabling the policy to reason about multi-step outcomes in a single imagination step.

## Method

LS-Imagine builds a dual-branch world model on top of DreamerV3's RSSM: a short-term branch performs standard single-step latent transitions, while a long-term branch simulates 'jumpy' transitions toward task-relevant targets identified by an affordance map. The affordance map is generated by sliding a 15%-sized bounding box across the image, cropping 16 zoomed-in frames to simulate virtual approach, scoring them with MineCLIP against the text instruction, and fusing scores into a smooth spatial map; a Swin-UNet-based multimodal U-Net then learns to predict these maps in real time. A Gaussian-weighted intrinsic reward encourages the agent to center task-relevant regions in its view, and a dynamic kurtosis-based threshold on the affordance map triggers the jump flag. During behavior learning, the actor-critic operates over a mixed imagination sequence where short-term and long-term transitions are interleaved sequentially; the λ-return is modified to account for variable step intervals Δ, and the actor is not updated at jumpy steps (weighted by 1−ĵₜ) since no action is taken during the jump.

## Results

On the MineDojo benchmark, LS-Imagine achieves the highest success rate across all five tasks, with the most dramatic gains on 'Harvest log in plains' (80.63% vs. 53.33% for DreamerV3) and 'Shear sheep' (54.28% vs. 25.13%). It also completes tasks in fewer steps, e.g., 503.35 steps for 'Harvest log in plains' versus 711.22 for DreamerV3. On 'Mine iron ore', LS-Imagine reaches 20.28% success in 1748.55 steps, outperforming DreamerV3 (16.79%, 1789.06 steps) and PTGM (15.14%, 1586.03 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LS-Imagine outperforms all five baselines on success rate across all five MineDojo tasks. Against the closest competitor DreamerV3 (the only other pure RL method using pixels only), LS-Imagine gains +27.3 pp on 'Harvest log in plains', +21.6 pp on 'Harvest water with bucket', +2.8 pp on 'Harvest sand', +29.2 pp on 'Shear sheep', and +3.5 pp on 'Mine iron ore'. Compared to STEVE-1 (a generative text-to-behavior model), LS-Imagine surpasses it by +23.6 pp on 'Harvest log in plains' and +51.3 pp on 'Shear sheep', while STEVE-1 cannot complete 'Mine iron ore' at all. PTGM, which pretrains goal-based policies, is outperformed by LS-Imagine on every task, with the largest gap on 'Harvest water with bucket' (+74.5 pp). Director, a hierarchical latent-space planner, is dominated by LS-Imagine on all tasks, particularly on 'Harvest log in plains' (+72.0 pp) and 'Shear sheep' (+53.0 pp). LS-Imagine also achieves fewer steps per episode on four of five tasks, indicating faster task completion in addition to higher success rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

LS-Imagine is built directly on DreamerV3's RSSM-based world model architecture, extending it with a parallel long-term (jumpy) transition branch alongside the standard short-term single-step dynamics. The method draws on MineCLIP (Fan et al., 2022) for both reward shaping and affordance map annotation, and positions itself against hierarchical MBRL approaches like Director and Dr. Strategy by using affordance-map-guided jumpy transitions rather than learned latent goals. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the use of image zoom-in to simulate virtual exploration and generate goal-conditioned affordance maps without requiring successful trajectories, thereby resolving the chicken-and-egg problem of training long-horizon transition models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation of current MBRL agents in open-world settings—their short-sightedness when optimizing over only ~15 imagined steps—and demonstrates that explicitly modeling long-distance state jumps can substantially improve exploration efficiency. However, the approach is constrained to 3D navigation environments with embodied agents and fixed camera viewpoints, and the authors acknowledge it is unsuitable for 2D environments, fixed-viewpoint settings, or tasks with complex reward structures beyond object approach. Open questions include whether the zoom-in affordance mechanism generalizes beyond MineCLIP's pretraining distribution, how the method scales to tasks requiring multi-step compositional reasoning (beyond the 7-subgoal Tech Tree experiment), and whether the computational overhead of dual-branch world models is justified relative to simpler long-horizon credit assignment techniques.
