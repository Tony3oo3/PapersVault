## Problem

Deterministic diffusion inversion (e.g., DDIM inversion) relies on a linear approximation of an implicit equation, introducing errors that accumulate across time steps and cause poor reconstruction—especially in few-step models (2–4 denoising steps) where gaps between latents are large. Existing iterative fixes (fixed-point iteration, gradient descent) improve accuracy but remain too slow for interactive use, and vanilla Newton-Raphson applied naively converges to out-of-distribution roots that yield poor image quality. The paper asks whether inversion can be solved both accurately and fast enough for real-time editing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-encoded image latent z_0 (e.g., 4×64×64 for SDXL-Turbo) and its corresponding text prompt, and outputs a noise latent z_T in the seed space that, when denoised with the same prompt, reconstructs the input image. It operates entirely in latent space with deterministic schedulers (DDIM or Euler), requiring no additional parameters, training, or prompt optimization. In practice, inversion completes in ~0.4 s on an A100 GPU for 4-step models, and the resulting seed can be fed directly into editing (Prompt-to-Prompt), interpolation, or rare-concept generation pipelines.

## Method

GNRI reformulates each inversion step as finding the root of the residual r(z_t) = z_t − f(z_t), where f encodes the scheduler's implicit equation. To make Newton-Raphson computationally tractable for high-dimensional latents (~16K), the residual is reduced to a scalar via an L1 norm, turning the D×D Jacobian into a D-dimensional gradient vector that can be computed via automatic differentiation. The key insight is that vanilla NR on this non-convex function converges to out-of-distribution roots; GNRI adds a guidance term G(z_t) derived from the known Gaussian transition q(z_t|z_{t-1}) of the diffusion process, which penalizes solutions far from the expected noise mean μ_t. The resulting component-wise update (Eq. 10) requires only 1–2 NR iterations per diffusion step, achieving quadratic-like convergence while staying in-distribution, and the contraction-mapping analysis (Appendix C) confirms the scheme's stability with a Lipschitz constant ρ ≈ 0.50 versus 0.90 for fixed-point iteration.

## Results

On PIE-Bench with SDXL-turbo, GNRI achieves the best background preservation (PSNR 30.22, LPIPS 40.15×10⁻³, SSIM 98.66×10⁻²) and highest CLIP similarity (Whole 26.15, Edited 23.26) among all compared methods, while completing inversion in 0.521 s on a single A100 GPU. On the COCO-2017 reconstruction benchmark, GNRI reaches the highest PSNR among deterministic methods (approaching the VAE upper bound) while reducing inversion-reconstruction time by ×2 vs. DDIM and up to ×40 vs. ExactDPM. For rare-concept generation on ImageNet-1k/LAION-2B, GNRI improves tail-class accuracy from 85.1% (DDIM) to 89.1% and total accuracy from 94.3% to 95.8%, with the fastest initialization time (17 s vs. 240 s for ExactDPM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GNRI outperforms all seven baselines on every quality metric (PSNR, LPIPS, MSE, SSIM, CLIP) while being among the fastest. Against DDIM inversion, GNRI improves PSNR from 18.59 to 30.22 and LPIPS from 177.96 to 40.15 (×4.4 reduction) at a negligible inversion-time cost (0.521 s vs. 0.454 s). Versus the iterative baselines ReNoise (5.41 s) and ExactDPM (15.11 s), GNRI is 10× and 29× faster respectively while achieving higher PSNR (30.22 vs. 27.11 and 24.54). The stochastic EditFriendly method (32.3 s inversion) is 62× slower and still trails GNRI on all preservation metrics. TurboEdit, the closest deterministic competitor, is 2× slower (0.981 s) and lower on PSNR (29.52 vs. 30.22), LPIPS (44.74 vs. 40.15), and CLIP Edited (22.34 vs. 23.26). In the rare-concept generation setting, GNRI's guidance term yields a mean noise likelihood of 0.82 versus 0.34 for Anderson Acceleration (Pan et al.), translating to 7.5 dB higher PSNR (25.8 vs. 18.3) on out-of-distribution samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2021), which introduced deterministic inversion via a linear approximation of the implicit denoising equation. Prior work has attempted to fix the approximation error through fixed-point iterations (AIDI, ReNoise), gradient descent (ExactDPM), or learned invertible layers (EDICT), but these remain either slow or require additional parameters. GNRI departs from this lineage by reframing inversion as a scalar Newton-Raphson root-finding problem (via an L1 norm on the residual) and introducing a distributional guidance term derived from the known Gaussian noise schedule, steering iterations toward in-distribution roots rather than out-of-distribution ones that vanilla NR converges to.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.4-second inversion time on an A100 for few-step models (SDXL-Turbo, Flux.1) opens the door to interactive, real-time image editing with text-to-image models — a capability that was previously infeasible with deterministic inversion. The method is training-free, parameter-free, and scheduler-agnostic, making it broadly applicable. Open questions include the theoretical analysis of NR convergence on non-negative objectives without exact zero crossings (acknowledged in Appendix B), the 4.6% failure rate under prompt-image misalignment, and whether the guidance term can be generalized to incorporate richer domain priors beyond the Gaussian noise schedule.
