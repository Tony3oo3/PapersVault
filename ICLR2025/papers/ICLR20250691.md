## Problem

Neural ODEs are infinite-depth continuous models, yet all existing NTK-based global convergence guarantees apply only to finite-depth networks, leaving it unclear whether the NTK is well-defined or remains strictly positive definite in the continuous limit. The paper asks which properties of the activation function—smoothness and nonlinearity—are necessary and sufficient to ensure well-posed ODE solutions, a well-defined limiting NTK, and global convergence under gradient descent.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single data point x ∈ R^d (normalized to the unit sphere in theory) and outputs a scalar prediction f(x;θ) = (σ_v/√n) v^T φ(h_T), where h_T is the terminal hidden state of an n-dimensional ODE integrated over [0,T]. Parameters U ∈ R^{n×d}, W ∈ R^{n×n}, and v ∈ R^n are randomly initialized from N(0,1) with variance hyperparameters σ_u, σ_w, σ_v. Training uses standard supervised squared loss over N samples, with gradients computed via the adjoint method (solving a backward ODE) rather than discrete backpropagation.

## Method

The paper establishes global convergence of Neural ODEs under gradient descent by proving three linked results. First, Lipschitz continuity of φ guarantees globally unique solutions to both the forward ODE and the backward (adjoint) ODE via Picard-Lindelöf, and no extra regularity is needed for the augmented backward ODE. Second, by approximating the Neural ODE with a sequence of finite-depth ResNets and applying the Moore-Osgood theorem, the authors show the NNGP and NTK converge to deterministic limits as width n→∞, with the key intermediate step being uniform-in-width convergence of the discretized states (rate O(1/L) in depth). Third, they derive an integral-form expression for the limiting NTK K_∞ and prove it is strictly positive definite whenever φ is nonlinear but non-polynomial, using a dual-activation / Hermitian-expansion argument. Under these conditions, the NTK Gram matrix remains SPD throughout training, yielding exponential loss decay at rate (1 − ηλ_0/16)^k for sufficiently wide networks.

## Results

The paper establishes that finite-depth ResNet approximations of Neural ODEs converge to the continuous model at a rate of O(1/L) in both output and gradient (Figure 1), with the NTK approximation error inheriting the same rate. Empirically, the NTK's smallest eigenvalue remains strictly positive for widths exceeding the number of training samples (1000), confirming SPD, while parameter distances stay bounded at O(1) throughout training (Figure 2). Larger widths (2000, 4000) exhibit faster exponential convergence of train and test loss compared to smaller widths (500, 1000). Smooth activations (Softplus) converge measurably faster than non-smooth ones (ReLU), and non-polynomial nonlinearities yield SPD NTKs with faster convergence than polynomial alternatives (Figures 7–8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a traditional method-vs-method benchmark table; comparisons are structured around activation function choices and architectural hyperparameters rather than competing algorithms. Softplus (smooth, non-polynomial) outperforms ReLU (non-smooth, piecewise-linear) in convergence speed, attributed to more accurate gradient computation via the adjoint method (Figure 7). Among smooth activations, non-polynomial nonlinearities (Softplus, GELU) produce SPD limiting NTKs with faster convergence, while quadratic activations also yield SPD NTKs but at a slower rate (Figure 8). Proper ODE scaling (σ_w = O(1/T)) is critical: without it, long-horizon training exhibits early-stage damping from accumulated numerical errors (Figure 3). Adaptive ODE solvers, while accurate, suffer from poor efficiency and instability at large widths (Figure 14), making fixed-step methods preferable in the overparameterized regime studied here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK-based global convergence theory established for finite-depth overparameterized networks (Du et al., 2019a; Allen-Zhu et al., 2019) to the continuous, infinite-depth setting of Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018b). It builds on the NNGP framework (Lee et al., 2018; Yang, 2019) and the dual activation technique (Daniely et al., 2016) for proving strict positive definiteness, but introduces a new mathematical framework using the Moore-Osgood theorem to handle the non-commuting limits of width and depth that arise in infinite-depth models. The adjoint method for gradient computation (Chen et al., 2018b) is retained, but the paper shows that Lipschitz continuity of the activation and its derivative are sufficient conditions for well-posedness without additional regularization, departing from prior work that required stronger assumptions for augmented ODE systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first rigorous global convergence guarantee for Neural ODEs under plain gradient descent in the overparameterized regime, resolving a key open question about whether standard optimization methods can reliably train continuous-time models. The practical implication—that smooth, non-polynomial activations like Softplus are preferable to ReLU for Neural ODE training, and that σ_w should scale as O(1/T) for long horizons—gives practitioners concrete design guidelines. Limitations include the restriction to a single hidden-layer architecture with shared parameters, the requirement of non-polynomial (not merely nonlinear) activations for the SPD proof, and the fact that the convergence rate depends on the smallest eigenvalue of the limiting NTK, which may be small in practice. Open questions include whether these results extend to multi-layer or non-shared-parameter Neural ODEs, and whether the overparameterization requirement can be relaxed.
