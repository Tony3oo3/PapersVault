## Problem

While Heavy Ball with Jacobi polynomial schedules achieves the optimal accelerated rate O(t^{-2ζ}) for non-stochastic gradient descent on quadratic problems with power-law spectrum, direct application of this schedule to stochastic mini-batch SGD consistently leads to divergence. The paper addresses whether first-order methods with auxiliary memory can achieve genuine stochastic acceleration—improving the convergence exponent beyond plain SGD's O(t^{-ζ})—while remaining stable under mini-batch noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252875/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on infinite-dimensional quadratic objectives defined by a Hessian H with eigenvalues λ_k ~ Λk^{-ν} and target coefficients satisfying a power-law source condition with exponent ζ. The algorithm consumes mini-batch samples from a data distribution ρ and is parameterized by a triplet (δ, α_eff, q_0) for the memory-1 case, where δ controls the memory decay, α_eff is a generalized effective learning rate, and q_0 is an additional gradient coefficient. The output is the parameter trajectory w_t and its mean loss L_t, with the key quantity of interest being the asymptotic convergence exponent and constant in L_t ~ C_L t^{-ξ}.

## Method

The paper introduces a general framework for memory-M first-order algorithms (storing M auxiliary velocity vectors) and proves their equivalence to multistep recurrences via characteristic polynomials. It then develops a combinatorial loss expansion in terms of scalar signal propagators V_t and noise propagators U_t, showing that all stationary stable memory-M algorithms retain the plain GD exponent ζ but differ in the constant C_L ∝ α_eff^{-ζ}, where α_eff generalizes the Heavy Ball effective learning rate. For memory-1 algorithms, the authors identify a region in the (δ, α_eff, q_0) parameter space where α_eff → ∞ while maintaining both noiseless and noisy stability, by taking δ → 0 with q_0 > 0 and appropriate spectral conditions. This yields an Accelerated Memory-1 (AM1) algorithm with a power-law schedule (δ_t ~ t^{-δ̄}, α_eff,t ~ t^{ᾱ}) that heuristically and empirically achieves convergence O(t^{-ζ(1+ᾱ)}) up to O(t^{-ζ(2-1/ν)}) in the signal-dominated regime, outperforming plain SGD while remaining stable at small batch sizes.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result is that the proposed Accelerated Memory-1 (AM1) algorithm achieves a convergence rate of O(t^{-ζ(1+ᾱ)}) in the signal-dominated phase (ζ < 2 − 1/ν), with the maximal rate O(t^{-ζ(2−1/ν)}) obtained at ᾱ = 1 − 1/ν, compared to plain SGD's O(t^{-ζ}). Theorem 7 proves that the effective learning rate α_eff can be made arbitrarily large while maintaining both noiseless and noisy stability, enabling the loss constant C_L to be driven to zero. Empirically, on MNIST classification with a shallow ReLU network and on synthetic Gaussian data with ideal power-law spectra, AM1 remains stable for small batch sizes over long training durations, while Jacobi-scheduled Heavy Ball diverges (Fig. 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Jacobi-scheduled Heavy Ball (Brakhage, 1987), which achieves the optimal non-stochastic rate O(t^{-2ζ}), the AM1 algorithm trades some acceleration (achieving O(t^{-ζ(2−1/ν)}) instead) for stochastic stability: Jacobi HB consistently diverges in the mini-batch setting, with increasing batch size only delaying the loss explosion, while AM1 remains stable for small batch sizes and long training (Fig. 1, both Gaussian and MNIST panels). Against plain SGD (Berthier et al., 2020), which establishes the O(t^{-ζ}) rate in the signal-dominated regime, AM1 provides a strict improvement in the convergence exponent, with the dashed theoretical lines in Fig. 1 (t^{-ζ} for SGD vs. t^{-ζ(1+ᾱ)} for AM1) asymptotically matching the experimental loss trajectories. The paper also shows that the phase diagram (signal-dominated vs. noise-dominated) established for plain SGD by Berthier et al. (2020) and Velikanov et al. (2023) extends unchanged to arbitrary memory size M (Theorem 5), with the only algorithm-dependent effect being through the effective learning rate α_eff and batch size |B|.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stationary SGD convergence analysis of Velikanov et al. (2023) and Berthier et al. (2020) from memory-0 (plain SGD) and memory-1 (Heavy Ball) to a general memory-M framework, unifying these methods under a common propagator expansion. It departs from the non-stochastic acceleration literature (Brakhage 1987, Nemirovskiy & Polyak 1984) by showing that Jacobi-scheduled Heavy Ball, which achieves O(t^{-2ζ}) in the noiseless setting, consistently diverges under mini-batch noise, and instead identifies a different mechanism—arbitrarily large effective learning rate within a slightly generalized memory-1 parameterization—that enables stable partial acceleration. The spectrally-expressible (SE) noise approximation from Velikanov et al. (2023) and Bordelon & Pehlevan (2021) is retained as the analytical backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a key open question: whether the non-stochastic acceleration exponent can be recovered in the stochastic mini-batch regime. The answer is partially yes—AM1 achieves O(t^{-ζ(2-1/ν)}) in the signal-dominated phase, strictly better than SGD's O(t^{-ζ}) but short of the non-stochastic O(t^{-2ζ}). However, the accelerated convergence rate for the non-stationary AM1 schedule is only heuristically derived (not rigorously proven), and the method requires knowledge of the spectral exponent ν. Open problems include rigorous proof of the accelerated rate, extension to larger memory sizes M, and applicability beyond the power-law/SE-noise assumptions to practical deep learning training.
