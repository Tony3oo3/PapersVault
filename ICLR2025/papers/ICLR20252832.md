## Problem

Despite RL's practical success in continuous state and action spaces, nearly all theoretical analysis is restricted to finite MDPs, and the manifold hypothesis—widely assumed in RL—has never been theoretically validated. This paper asks: what is the geometric structure of the set of states actually attained by neural policies trained via semi-gradient updates, and can its dimensionality be bounded in terms of the action space rather than the (typically much larger) state space?

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a continuous-time deterministic MDP with control-affine dynamics (smooth g and h_i functions) and a wide two-layer neural policy linearized around its random initialization, producing a probabilistic concentration guarantee: the push-forward of the exponential map over a short time interval δ concentrates around a manifold of dimension at most 2d_a + 1, independent of the nominal state dimension d_s. Empirically, the method consumes evaluation trajectories from DDPG or SAC agents and outputs intrinsic dimension estimates via the Facco et al. (2017) minimal-neighborhood-information algorithm, as well as discounted returns when a CRATE sparsification layer is inserted into the policy and Q networks.

## Method

The core contribution is a proof that, in the infinite-width limit with vanishing learning rates, the locally attainable state set under a linearized wide two-layer policy trained by stochastic semi-gradient updates concentrates around a manifold of dimension ≤ 2d_a + 1. The proof proceeds by (1) expanding the state trajectory via the Lie series of the vector field induced by the policy, (2) showing weak convergence of the parameter dynamics to a random ODE using the Ben Arous et al. (2022) continuous-time SGD framework, and (3) demonstrating that the push-forward of the exponential map is spanned by 2d_a + 1 vectors (the action directions h_j, mean second-order corrections v_j^τ, and a paraboloid term tg + t²g'). For practical application, the authors replace one fully-connected layer in SAC's policy and Q networks with a CRATE sparsification layer that learns orthogonal, low-rank representations across local manifolds, yielding significant improvements in high-DoF DM Control environments (Dog Stand, Dog Walk, Quadruped Walk) with only ~5 lines of code change and two additional hyperparameters.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that the locally attainable states of a wide two-layer neural policy in continuous RL concentrate around a manifold of dimension at most 2d_a + 1, where d_a is the action space dimensionality, independent of the nominal state space dimension d_s. Empirically, the intrinsic dimensionality of attainable states (estimated via the Facco et al. algorithm) remains below the 2d_a + 1 bound across four MuJoCo environments (Walker2D, Cheetah, Reacher, Swimmer). In a fully reachable toy linear system with d_a = 1, the estimated intrinsic dimension stays at or below 3 (= 2d_a + 1), and even drops to 2 (= d_a + 1) due to the truncated Lie series for GeLU activation. Replacing one fully-connected layer in SAC with a CRATE sparsification layer yields significant improvements in high-DOF DM Control environments (Dog Stand, Dog Walk, Quadruped Walk) where vanilla SAC fails to learn.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical comparison is between vanilla SAC and sparse SAC (with a single CRATE sparsification layer replacing one fully-connected layer, α_π=0.5, α_Q=0.4, width 1024) on Ant, Dog Stand, Dog Walk, and Quadruped Walk. Sparse SAC substantially outperforms vanilla SAC in the high-dimensional Dog environments, where vanilla SAC fails to learn at all, while both perform comparably on Ant. A secondary comparison validates the linearised policy model: DDPG with a canonical two-layer NN policy versus DDPG with a linearised (first-layer-only) policy shows that the return gap (J*_n − J^lin_n) approaches zero at large widths (log_2 n > 15) on Cheetah, confirming the theoretical model's validity. The theoretical manifold dimension bound (2d_a + 1) is validated against empirical intrinsic dimension estimates, which consistently fall below the bound in all four MuJoCo tasks tested. The sparsification layer adds no computational overhead since it is a linear transformation of the feed-forward layer, requiring only ~5 lines of code change and two additional hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: the manifold hypothesis in RL (Smart & Kaelbling 2002; Mahadevan 2005; Machado et al. 2017), which posited low-dimensional state structure without proof; differential-geometric control theory (Kalman 1960; Sussmann 1973; Isidori 1985), which characterizes reachable sets under all smooth time-variant controls; and the neural tangent kernel / linearized wide-network framework (Jacot et al. 2018; Lee et al. 2019; Cai et al. 2019b; Wang et al. 2019), which provides an analytically tractable model of neural policies. The key departure from control theory is restricting the policy family to those parameterized by a wide two-layer network trained via semi-gradient updates, rather than all admissible time-variant controls. The continuous-time SGD dynamics framework of Ben Arous et al. (2022) is adapted to track the Lie series statistics of the policy, and the CRATE sparsification layer (Yu et al. 2023a) is repurposed as a practical architectural modification to exploit the proven low-dimensional structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical result proving that the locally attainable state set in continuous RL under neural policies concentrates around a manifold whose dimension is linear in the action space dimension (2d_a + 1), independent of the nominal state dimension. This validates a long-standing but unproven assumption in the RL literature and provides a principled justification for low-dimensional representation learning in control. Key limitations include the restriction to deterministic transitions, the assumption of access to the true Q-function, and the inability to handle the regime where d_s grows with network width n. The practical demonstration that a single sparsification layer (≈5 lines of code) improves SAC in high-DoF DM Control environments where vanilla SAC fails suggests a low-cost path to better high-dimensional control, though the effect size and generality across architectures remain to be established.
