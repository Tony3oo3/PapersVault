## Problem

Unsupervised anomaly detection methods are highly sensitive to hyper-parameter choices, yet tuning them is fundamentally difficult because no labeled anomalies exist to provide validation feedback during the search. Existing approaches either assume a known anomaly ratio, rely on historical labeled datasets for meta-learning, or introduce other forms of supervision, all of which contradict the unsupervised nature of the task. This paper seeks fully unsupervised surrogate metrics that can guide both hyper-parameter optimization and cross-model selection without any anomaly labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled tabular training set (standardized using its own mean and standard deviation) and a pool of candidate UAD models (OCSVM, AutoEncoder, DeepSVDD, DPAD) each with a defined hyperparameter search space. The training set is internally split into a training subset and a validation subset; the validation subset is paired with a synthetic isotropic Gaussian dataset (matched to the training subset's mean and per-feature variance) to compute the NPD score. The output is the best hyperparameter configuration per model plus the single best model, which then produces anomaly scores on held-out test data (50% of normal samples plus all anomalies).

## Method

AutoUAD proposes three surrogate metrics—relative-top-median (RTM), expected-anomaly-gap (EAG), and normalized pseudo discrepancy (NPD)—that approximate expected test AUC/F1 without labels, and integrates them with Bayesian optimization via TPE to sequentially search each model's hyperparameter space. NPD, the most effective metric, computes the squared difference between mean anomaly scores on a real validation subset and a synthetic isotropic Gaussian subset, normalized by their combined variances; the Gaussian is justified by its maximum-entropy property (Theorem 1) and its role as a diverse proxy for potential anomalies. Theoretical analysis shows NPD is upper-bounded by the normal-anomaly score gap (Theorem 2), is translation- and scale-invariant, and yields a Rademacher-complexity-based FPR+FNR bound (Theorem 4). RTM and EAG, while simpler, require an additional threshold hyper-parameter and can overfit complex models, making NPD the preferred choice for deep UAD methods.

## Results

Across 38 benchmark datasets, NPD-guided Bayesian optimization achieves the highest mean AUC among all methods for OCSVM (84.03%), AE (83.58%), and DPAD (80.36%), approaching the oracle upper bound (Max) of 85.75%, 84.86%, and 83.38% respectively. For F1, NPD reaches 60.26% (OCSVM), 59.73% (AE), and 55.67% (DPAD), substantially outperforming Default (54.32%, 56.68%, 48.66%) and Random (48.90%, 57.86%, 46.57%). In the UOMS setting (Table 2), NPD achieves 83.49% AUC and 59.30% F1, significantly outperforming all baselines with p-values of 0.0000 vs. Random.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NPD significantly outperforms EM/MV (Goix 2016) on OCSVM (84.03 vs. 71.41 AUC, +12.6 points) and DPAD (80.36 vs. 72.59 AUC, +7.8 points), while essentially tying on DeepSVDD (74.38 vs. 74.43) and narrowly leading on AE (83.58 vs. 83.08). In the UOMS setting (Table 2), NPD (83.49 AUC) beats ModelCentrality/MC (Lin 2020) by 12.8 points (70.7 AUC) and HITS (Ma 2023) by 10.1 points (73.44 AUC), with p-values of 0.0000 vs. Random for both AUC and F1. The consensus-based baselines MC and HITS underperform because they are designed to select from a model pool via voting rather than serving as a per-model surrogate metric, making them sensitive to the majority of poorly configured models. NPD's advantage is most pronounced on complex deep models (DPAD, AE) where hyperparameter sensitivity is highest, while on DeepSVDD the gap is smaller, suggesting the method's benefit scales with model complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of Bayesian optimization and unsupervised anomaly detection by proposing surrogate metrics (RTM, EAG, NPD) that require no labels, no historical datasets, and no anomaly-ratio assumption—departing from meta-learning approaches (Zhao et al., 2021; Ding et al., 2024) that rely on supervised historical data, and from consensus-based or statistical metrics (Goix, 2016; Ma et al., 2023) designed for transductive outlier detection rather than inductive UAD. The NPD metric repurposes the idea of comparing a model's behavior on real data versus synthetic data (here, an isotropic Gaussian matched to the training statistics) as a proxy for generalization, drawing conceptually on internal validity metrics from clustering (Fan et al., 2022) but applying them in a fundamentally different setting. The optimization framework itself (TPE-based Bayesian optimization) is standard, but its application to UAD with these novel, hyperparameter-free surrogate functions is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a fully unsupervised, hyperparameter-free surrogate (NPD) with theoretical error-rate bounds via Rademacher complexity, this work makes automated UAD practical for real-world deployment where anomaly labels are unavailable and the anomaly ratio is unknown. The consistent outperformance over existing heuristics across 38 datasets and four diverse UAD methods (shallow OCSVM to deep DPAD) suggests the approach is broadly applicable. However, the authors acknowledge that the highest NPD does not always correspond to the best test performance, and the method has not been validated on very large-scale or image/video anomaly detection tasks. Open questions include whether more sophisticated surrogate metrics could close the remaining gap to oracle performance, and whether the isotropic Gaussian assumption in NPD generalizes well to highly structured or multimodal data distributions.
