## Problem

Existing multi-dataset 3D detection frameworks like Uni3D retain dataset-specific normalization and head layers, which inhibits full mutual utilization of cross-dataset features and prevents zero-shot generalization to unseen domains. The paper addresses how to achieve a truly unified (fully parameter-shared) and universal (zero-shot on new datasets) training paradigm for LiDAR-based 3D object detection across KITTI, Waymo, and nuScenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253456/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds (range-aligned to 75.2×75.2 m in X/Y and 2–4 m in Z) together with lightweight dataset-specific prompts: the point range (x₁,y₁,x₂,y₂) for BEV masking and per-instance point distribution statistics for mean-shifted normalization. It outputs oriented 3D bounding boxes with categories limited to vehicle, pedestrian, and cyclist, evaluated via AP/APH (Waymo) or AP_BEV/AP_3D over 40 recall positions (KITTI, nuScenes). Critically, for zero-shot transfer to an unseen dataset, only its point range and distribution statistics are needed as prompts—no retraining or new parameters.

## Method

Uni²Det introduces three multi-stage prompting modules that operate at different levels of a standard 3D detector (PV-RCNN or Voxel-RCNN) while keeping all parameters fully shared across datasets. At voxelization, a mean-shifted batch normalization blends channel-wise mean with instance-level mean via a balancing ratio α, correcting point distribution disparities without dataset-specific branches. At the backbone, a binary BEV mask derived from each dataset's point range is concatenated along the feature dimension before every 2D convolution, providing spatial prior signals so the network ignores out-of-range regions. At the head, object-conditional residual learning generates per-RoI residuals via a stop-gradient function, supervised by an MLP discriminator to encode dataset identity, which are added back to RoI features to compensate for cross-dataset statistical differences in object size. This design enables a single detection head to serve all datasets and supports zero-shot inference on new datasets by simply supplying their intrinsic attributes as prompts.

## Results

On the KITTI-Waymo consolidation (Voxel-RCNN), Uni²Det achieves 75.25/71.48 AP_BEV/AP_3D on KITTI and 73.01/70.57 on Waymo, outperforming Uni3D (74.02/68.57 and 72.15/69.46) and simple data merging (64.78/47.63 and 71.34/68.73). On the Waymo-nuScenes consolidation, Uni²Det reaches 73.27/70.99 mAP on Waymo and 36.20/27.19 on nuScenes, surpassing Uni3D by ~1.4 and ~1.3 mAP points respectively. In zero-shot cross-dataset transfer (Waymo+nuScenes→KITTI), Uni²Det with SN achieves 75.57/64.09 mAP, exceeding Uni3D with single head and SN (73.48/60.51) and data merging with SN (72.43/59.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Uni3D (Zhang et al., 2023), Uni²Det consistently wins across all three dataset consolidations: on KITTI-Waymo it gains +1.23/+2.91 AP_BEV/AP_3D on KITTI and +0.86/+1.11 on Waymo; on Waymo-nuScenes it gains +1.36/+2.11 on Waymo and +1.25/+0.88 on nuScenes. Uni3D also suffers from negative transfer in some cases (e.g., Waymo mAP drops below single-dataset pre-training under Waymo-nuScenes consolidation), whereas Uni²Det always improves with additional data. In zero-shot generalization (Table 1), Uni²Det with SN outperforms the SN baseline (Wang et al., 2020) by +2.49/+3.79 mAP on Waymo→KITTI (PV-RCNN) and +1.51/+2.35 on Waymo+nuScenes→KITTI. In the normalization ablation (Table 5), Uni²Det's mean-shifted batch normalization beats PPT (Wu et al., 2024) by +0.59/+0.47 on KITTI and −0.02/+0.27 on Waymo, confirming that instance-level correction is more effective than PPT's learned prompt-driven normalization for multi-dataset settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-dataset 3D object detection line initiated by Uni3D (Zhang et al., 2023), which shared backbone parameters but retained dataset-specific normalization and detection heads, and by PPT (Wu et al., 2024), which introduced prompt-driven normalization for multi-dataset 3D representation learning. Uni²Det departs from both by replacing all dataset-specific components with a fully shared architecture and instead injecting dataset-specific information through multi-stage prompts derived from easily obtainable intrinsic attributes (point range, point distribution) at the voxelization, backbone, and head stages. The prompt-learning paradigm is repurposed from 2D vision and representation learning into a structured, stage-wise design for LiDAR-based 3D detection, making it plug-and-play compatible with existing detectors like PV-RCNN and Voxel-RCNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to train a single 3D detector across multiple LiDAR datasets and zero-shot transfer to unseen datasets without retraining is practically important for autonomous driving deployment, where LiDAR hardware and geographic conditions vary. The plug-and-play design lowers the barrier for adopting multi-dataset training in existing pipelines. However, the framework assumes an identical category set (vehicle, pedestrian, cyclist) across all datasets, limiting applicability to more diverse or open-vocabulary scenarios, and has not been validated on indoor point cloud datasets. Open questions include whether the prompting strategy scales to larger category spaces, whether it generalizes to camera-based or multi-modal 3D detection, and how it interacts with emerging foundation-model approaches for 3D perception.
