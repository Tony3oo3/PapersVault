## Problem

Neural network classifiers achieve high accuracy but function as opaque black boxes that lack transparency and cannot support interactive reasoning with human users. Existing post-hoc interpretability methods (e.g., Grad-CAM) only visualize decisions after the fact, while causal GNNs quantify cause-effect relationships but do not enable real-time intervention or human-guided classification. No prior framework integrates causal inference directly into the classification architecture to support both interpretable reasoning and test-time do-interventions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input, encoded by a frozen backbone (ResNet50 or CLIP ViT-H-14) into a fixed feature vector. Output is a multi-label probability vector over all concept nodes in the causal graph (e.g., 2,357 nodes for ImageNet: 1,357 prior + 1,000 posterior; 25 nodes for Bird: 16 prior + 9 posterior), where the posterior nodes correspond to the original class labels and prior nodes are WordNet ancestors. During test-time intervention, a user can override prior concept values (set to 0 or 1 with a confidence of ±5), and the model re-propagates through the graph to produce updated posterior predictions.

## Method

NCG constructs a directed acyclic causal graph from WordNet, where dataset labels become posterior concept nodes and their ancestors (within 5 hops) become prior concept nodes. Edge weights are estimated as unbiased average treatment effects using Propensity Score Matching or Doubly Robust Learning, with a propensity score variable L(X) introduced to reduce the dimensionality of the confounding sample variable X (solving the curse of dimensionality in the backdoor adjustment formula). At inference, a concept proposer network generates initial logits, which are then updated via topological-order message passing where each node aggregates weighted contributions from its parents using the causal adjacency matrix. An intervention training procedure randomly fixes a subset of prior concept logits (rate p=0.15) during training to simulate do-interventions, aligning the training distribution with test-time intervention scenarios and enabling the model to answer 'what-if' queries. Learnable per-node scaled weights correct for label imbalance caused by logit accumulation from ancestor nodes.

## Results

On the Bird dataset, NCG(DRL) with CLIP backbone achieves 94.49% accuracy and 94.53% macro-F1, outperforming the CBM baseline (90.67% Acc) by nearly 4 points. On ImageNet, NCG(PSM) with CLIP reaches 84.44% accuracy, surpassing the multi-class CLIP baseline (83.49%) by ~1 point. With test-time intervention, NCG achieves nearly 95% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet, demonstrating the effectiveness of human-AI collaborative reasoning. All NCG variants show statistically significant improvements (p < 0.05) over the multi-class baseline on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM, Koh et al. 2020), which predicts prior concepts through a bottleneck layer before predicting the label. NCG substantially outperforms CBM: on Bird with CLIP, NCG(DRL) achieves 94.49% vs. CBM's 90.67% (a 3.8-point gap), and on ImageNet with CLIP, NCG(PSM) reaches 84.44% vs. CBM's 83.32%. The authors attribute this to NCG's explicit causal graph structure modeling directed relationships among concepts, whereas CBM only separates prior and posterior concepts without encoding their causal dependencies. NCG also consistently beats the simpler multi-class and multi-label baselines (which lack any concept structure), with the largest gains on the smaller Bird dataset where causal structure is more informative. Notably, NCG(PSM) with CLIP is the best-performing configuration on ImageNet (84.44%), while NCG(DRL) excels on Bird (94.49%), suggesting the choice between PSM and DRL depends on dataset scale and complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of structural causal models (Pearl, 2000) and graph-based neural network reasoning, departing from both traditional classification (which treats labels as independent) and causal-driven GNNs (which interpret data but lack interactive intervention). It builds directly on do-calculus and the potential outcome framework (Rosenbaum & Rubin, 1983; Bang & Robins, 2005) to estimate edge-level causal effects via propensity score matching and doubly robust learning, but applies them to a fixed, WordNet-derived concept graph rather than a learned task-level causal graph. The closest prior work is the Concept Bottleneck Model (Koh et al., 2020), which also separates prior and posterior concepts, but NCG explicitly models directed causal relationships among concepts and supports do-interventions during both training and inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a concrete mechanism for human-AI collaboration in classification: users can intervene on prior concepts at test time to steer predictions, achieving ~95% top-1 on ImageNet with CLIP. However, the approach is tightly coupled to WordNet's hierarchical structure, limiting applicability to domains without clear taxonomic hierarchies, and the causal graph is static rather than learned. Open questions include whether the graph topology and edge weights can be jointly learned from data, whether the intervention training strategy (fixed rate, fixed confidence value) generalizes beyond the tested settings, and whether the modest core classification gains (e.g., 83.49→84.44% on ImageNet with CLIP) justify the added complexity over simpler baselines.
