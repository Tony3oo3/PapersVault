## Problem

Existing eigenvector-based positional encodings for graphs cannot simultaneously satisfy four key criteria: expressive power, stability under perturbations, scalability, and genericness. Full eigenvector methods achieve high expressiveness but require O(N³) computation and O(N²) memory, while lightweight variants using only a subset of eigenvectors introduce inductive bias and lose stability (their error bounds depend on the eigengap). The paper asks whether one can learn PEs that are jointly expressive, stable, scalable, and generic.

## Input / Output

The method takes an anonymized graph (node and edge attributes stripped) and produces an N × d_p matrix of node-level positional encodings. For R-PEARL, each node is initialized with M independent 1D random samples (typically 10–100), while B-PEARL uses N standard basis vectors. The generated PEs are then concatenated with any original node/edge attributes and fed into a downstream GNN (e.g., GINE) or Graph Transformer for the final task prediction.

## Method

PEARL's core insight (Proposition 3.1) is that message-passing GNNs with specific aggregation functions (adjacency, Laplacian, normalized variants, random-walk) compute nonlinear MLP mappings of the GSO eigenvectors, with first-layer weights parameterized by eigenvalues and eigenvector projections. To unlock this expressiveness while preserving permutation equivariance, each node is initialized with M unique identifiers (random samples in R-PEARL, basis vectors in B-PEARL) that break structural symmetries; each sample is processed independently by an L-layer GNN Φ, and the M outputs are aggregated via a statistical pooling function ρ (empirical mean or summation) to recover equivariance. This yields O(MN) or O(N²) computational complexity versus O(N³) for full eigenvector methods, with a sample-complexity bound M ≤ 1/(δε²) independent of graph size. The framework is proven basis-universal (Theorem 3.1), strictly more expressive than 1-FWL (Corollary 4.5), capable of counting 3- through 7-node cycles (Corollary 4.4), and stable with bounds independent of the eigengap (Corollary 4.6).

## Results

On REDDIT-B, R-PEARL achieves 93.0±1.3% classification accuracy, outperforming the best eigenvector-based baseline SignNet-8S (92.4±1.1%) and all structure-based methods. On REDDIT-M, R-PEARL reaches 59.4±1.0%, surpassing SignNet-8S (57.8±0.8%) by 1.6 points. Full eigenvector methods (SignNet-full, BasisNet, SPE) are unable to run on either dataset due to O(N²) memory requirements, while R-PEARL operates at O(MN) computational complexity. On ZINC logP regression, B-PEARL achieves the best test MAE of 0.0644±0.001, outperforming SPE (0.0693±0.004) with quadratic rather than cubic complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-PEARL outperforms all baselines on both REDDIT-B and REDDIT-M. Against the strongest eigenvector-based baseline SignNet-8S (8 smallest eigenvectors, O(N³) complexity), R-PEARL gains 0.6 points on REDDIT-B (93.0 vs 92.4) and 1.6 points on REDDIT-M (59.4 vs 57.8), while reducing computational complexity by one to two orders of magnitude. The lightweight SignNet-8L variant (8 largest eigenvectors, O(N) complexity) performs substantially worse at 79.5% and 41.4%, confirming that selecting only a few eigenvectors introduces harmful inductive bias. Full eigenvector methods (SignNet-full, BasisNet, SPE) all fail with out-of-memory errors on both datasets due to their O(N²) memory footprint. On ZINC logP regression, B-PEARL achieves the best MAE (0.0644) and smallest generalization gap (0.0353), beating SPE (0.0693 MAE) with quadratic vs. cubic complexity. On DrugOOD out-of-distribution generalization, B-PEARL improves AUC by 9.5% over BasisNet and 3.8% over SignNet on the Size split, while matching SPE's performance with lower complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEARL builds on the GIN message-passing architecture (Xu et al., 2019) and the spectral positional encoding framework of SPE (Huang et al., 2024), showing that B-PEARL is mathematically equivalent to SPE when the spectral filters are pointwise analytic. The key conceptual departure is the observation that message-passing GNNs are inherently nonlinear functions of GSO eigenvectors (Proposition 3.1), which reframes the PE design problem: rather than computing eigenvectors explicitly at O(N³) cost, one can initialize GNNs with random or basis vectors and use statistical pooling to recover equivariant spectral information at linear or quadratic complexity. This positions PEARL as a unifying bridge between the random-initialization GNN literature (Abboud et al., 2021; Sato et al., 2021) and the eigenvector-based PE literature (SignNet, BasisNet, SPE), resolving the expressiveness-equivariance trade-off that has limited both families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale graph learning: PEARL reduces PE computation from O(N³) to O(MN) or O(N²F²), making eigenvector-quality PEs feasible on graphs with millions of nodes (demonstrated on the 38M-node RelBench dataset). The stability guarantee independent of the eigengap addresses a fundamental weakness of eigenvector-based PEs for out-of-distribution generalization, with concrete improvements on DrugOOD size-shift tasks. Open questions include whether structurally-aware sampling distributions (beyond i.i.d. Gaussians) could further boost expressiveness beyond 2-FWL, and whether the framework extends naturally to directed, temporal, or heterogeneous graphs beyond the limited RelBench evaluation.
