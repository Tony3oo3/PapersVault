## Problem

Training GNNs on large-scale graphs suffers from neighbor explosion: message passing from out-of-batch neighbors (MP_OB) recursively depends on higher-order neighbors, causing exponential computational cost with GNN depth. Existing mini-batch methods face a fundamental trade-off—recursive sampling preserves accuracy but is slow, while one-shot subgraph sampling is fast but drops MP_OB entirely, leading to significant accuracy degradation. The paper asks whether MP_OB can be accurately converted into in-batch message passing (MP_IB) without approximation.

## Input / Output

TOP takes a graph (adjacency matrix and node feature matrix) and a mini-batch of target nodes, and produces node embeddings and class predictions. During training, it operates solely on the subgraph induced by the mini-batch plus a precomputed topological compensation matrix (∂Â_B), so no out-of-batch node features or embeddings are needed at inference time. The compensation is computed once in a pre-processing phase using basic embeddings from a randomly initialized GNN, making the training loop depend only on in-batch data and a small k×k projection matrix.

## Method

The core insight is message invariance: the embeddings of out-of-batch neighbors can be expressed as a linear transformation of in-batch embeddings (H_{N_B^c} ≈ R·H_B), which converts costly MP_OB into fast MP_IB by adding weighted edges (topological compensation) to the induced subgraph. The coefficient matrix R is estimated via linear regression on basic embeddings—concatenated embeddings from a randomly initialized GNN across all layers—which provably identifies 1-WL indistinguishable node pairs and captures the linear dependence structure. A low-rank decomposition (proto-algorithm) makes the estimation efficient, reducing the per-layer cost from O(|B|²d) to O(|B|k² + k²d). TOP achieves an O(ε^{-4}) convergence rate to an ε-stationary point, matching standard SGD and improving over LMC's O(ε^{-6}), while being significantly faster than GAS, CLUSTER, and SAINT on graphs with millions of nodes and billions of edges.

## Results

On the large-scale Ogbn-products dataset (2.4M nodes, 61.9M edges), TOP-GCNII achieves 81.96% test accuracy, outperforming CLUSTER-GCNII (79.62%), GAS-GCNII (79.99%), and NS-SAGE (78.70%), while full-batch GD-GCNII runs out of GPU memory. On the massive Ogbn-papers100M dataset (111M nodes, 1.6B edges), TOP-GCNII reaches 67.21% accuracy, substantially exceeding CLUSTER-GCNII (51.73%) and matching NS-SAGE (67.06%) and SHADOW-GAT (67.08%). TOP's relative approximation errors to full-batch message passing remain below 5% across all tested datasets and GNN architectures (GCN, GCNII, SAGE, GAT, PNA), and it achieves a convergence rate of O(ε^{-4}), matching standard SGD and improving over LMC's O(ε^{-6}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLUSTER-GCN, TOP achieves a 2.34-point accuracy gain on Ogbn-products (81.96 vs 79.62) and a dramatic 15.48-point gain on Ogbn-papers (67.21 vs 51.73), where CLUSTER's lack of out-of-batch message compensation causes severe degradation at low subgraph ratios. Against GAS, TOP wins on Ogbn-products (81.96 vs 79.99) and avoids the OOM failure on Ogbn-papers (67.21 vs OOM), while also being up to 11× faster per epoch due to eliminating the need to pull and push historical embeddings. Against node/layer-wise sampling (NS-SAGE and LABOR), TOP achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy (67.21 vs 67.06 on Ogbn-papers) while being more than 2× faster in wall-clock time and using linear rather than exponential GPU memory with respect to GNN depth. Against precomputing methods SGC and SIGN, TOP substantially outperforms on Ogbn-papers (67.21 vs 63.29 and 66.06 respectively). On small datasets (Ogbn-arxiv, Flickr), TOP matches full-batch GD-GCNII within 0.3 points (72.52 vs 72.83), confirming that the topological compensation introduces negligible approximation error when the subgraph ratio is large.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOP positions itself within the subgraph sampling family (CLUSTER-GCN, GraphSAINT, GAS, LMC) but introduces a fundamentally different compensation mechanism: rather than dropping out-of-batch messages entirely (CLUSTER, SAINT) or approximating them with stale historical embeddings/gradients (GAS, LMC), it learns a linear transformation that converts MP_OB into MP_IB via the novel concept of message invariance. This is orthogonal to the subgraph sampler choice (METIS, random, GraphSAINT, SHADOW), making TOP a drop-in improvement over existing samplers. Theoretically, it improves upon LMC's O(ε^{-6}) convergence to O(ε^{-4}) by eliminating the staleness issue that plagues historical-embedding-based methods, matching the rate of standard SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: TOP achieves up to 11× speedup over GAS on Ogbn-products and handles 100M-node graphs where full-batch methods are infeasible, with <5% approximation error across GCN, GCNII, GAT, SAGE, and PNA backbones. The key open question is whether the linear message-invariance approximation degrades for GNNs with highly non-linear aggregation (e.g., graph transformers with global attention), which the authors acknowledge but do not test. The pre-processing cost (144s on papers100M) is amortized but non-trivial, and the method's reliance on random-init embeddings as a proxy for structural similarity remains an empirical assumption rather than a proven guarantee for arbitrary architectures.
