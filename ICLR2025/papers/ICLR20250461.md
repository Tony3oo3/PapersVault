## Problem

Removal-based faithfulness metrics for XAI explainers suffer from the Out-of-Distribution (OOD) problem, where perturbed inputs no longer follow the training distribution, making model predictions unreliable. While ROAR addresses OOD by retraining on explainer-guided perturbations, it introduces information leakage and requires a separate retrained model per explainer. Additionally, prior metrics are highly sensitive to explanation size (sparsity), leading to inconsistent evaluations when the true explanation size is unknown.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pre-trained classifier, a training set for fine-tuning, and a test set of input samples (images, time series, or text) with their labels, plus an explainer that produces a binary mask M of size s for each input. It outputs two scalar faithfulness scores, FFid+ (measuring prediction drop when removing important features) and FFid- (measuring prediction drop when retaining only important features), each averaged over N stochastic sampling iterations. The key constraint is that the total number of removed elements during evaluation is upper-bounded by β·td (the same bound used during fine-tuning), ensuring evaluation inputs remain in-distribution.

## Method

F-Fidelity combines two mechanisms: (1) an explanation-agnostic fine-tuning step where the classifier is trained on inputs with up to β fraction of elements randomly masked (pixels, tokens, or time steps), making the model robust to partial removal without leaking explainer-specific information; and (2) a controlled stochastic removal during evaluation that modifies the R-Fidelity sampling functions χ+ and χ- by truncating the removal fraction to α+ = min(α_orig+, βtd/s) and α- = min(α_orig-, βtd/(td-s)), so the perturbed inputs stay within the distribution seen during fine-tuning. Unlike ROAR, a single fine-tuned surrogate model serves all explainers, eliminating per-explainer retraining and information leakage. Theoretically, the paper proves that FFid+ as a function of explanation size s is monotonically increasing for s < c₁ (the most influential tier size) and monotonically decreasing beyond max(β/α_orig+ · td, c₁), enabling recovery of the ground-truth explanation size from the metric's shape.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet, F-Fidelity achieves perfect Spearman rank correlation (−1.00) with ground-truth explainer rankings in MoRF vs. GT for both SG-SQ and GradCAM explainers, while Fidelity (−0.68 / +0.81) and ROAR (−0.66 / +0.99) show substantially weaker or even positive (incorrect) correlations. On Tiny-ImageNet, F-Fidelity again achieves −1.00 macro and micro correlations across all settings. On the Boiler time-series dataset, F-Fidelity reaches 0.86 macro LeRF vs. GT correlation versus 0.22 (Fidelity), 0.36 (ROAR), and 0.53 (R-Fidelity), demonstrating the largest margin of improvement. On NLP datasets (SST2, BoolQ), all methods achieve near-perfect correlations (±1.00), reflecting the inherent robustness of NLP models to token masking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F-Fidelity is compared against three baselines: Fidelity (Pope et al., 2019), ROAR (Hooker et al., 2019), and R-Fidelity (Zheng et al., 2024). On image classification (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet), F-Fidelity decisively outperforms Fidelity and ROAR in MoRF vs. GT correlations, achieving −1.00 where Fidelity yields −0.68 (SG-SQ) or even +0.81 (GradCAM, indicating inverted rankings) and ROAR yields −0.66 or +0.99. F-Fidelity ties R-Fidelity on SG-SQ (both −1.00) but clearly outperforms it on GradCAM (−0.60 vs. +0.20 macro; −0.71 vs. +0.09 micro), where R-Fidelity's stochastic removal without fine-tuning is insufficient. On the Boiler time-series dataset, F-Fidelity achieves 0.86 macro LeRF vs. GT correlation, substantially exceeding R-Fidelity (0.53), ROAR (0.36), and Fidelity (0.22). On NLP tasks (SST2, BoolQ), all four methods achieve near-identical near-perfect correlations, indicating that the OOD problem is less severe for NLP models and simpler metrics suffice in that domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the R-Fidelity framework (Zheng et al., ICLR 2024), which introduced stochastic removal to mitigate OOD issues in GNN explanation evaluation, by adding an explanation-agnostic fine-tuning step that makes the classifier robust to the perturbations used during evaluation. It departs from ROAR (Hooker et al., 2019), which retrained a separate model per explainer and suffered from information leakage, by using a single fine-tuned model with random masking that is independent of any specific explainer's output. The foundational Fidelity metric (Pope et al., 2019) provides the removal-based evaluation template that F-Fidelity modifies through its upper-bounded stochastic sampling and robustness-enhancing fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

F-Fidelity provides a unified, modality-agnostic evaluation framework that addresses three simultaneously: OOD sensitivity, information leakage, and explanation-size dependency—issues that prior methods handled only partially. The theoretical result showing that FFid+ as a function of sparsity is piecewise monotone with a direction change at the true explanation size offers a principled way to infer explanation sparsity without ground-truth masks. Limitations include the need for additional fine-tuning compute, the idealized assumptions in the theoretical analysis (Shapley-based explainers, discrete influence tiers), and the controlled experimental setup using artificially degraded explanations rather than comparing genuinely distinct explainers. Open questions include whether alternative perturbation strategies (e.g., Gaussian blur) or limited-data fine-tuning settings would further improve practical applicability.
