## Problem

Standard OT barycenter methods are highly sensitive to outliers and class imbalance in real-world data, yet all existing robust barycenter approaches are restricted to discrete (empirical-measure) settings. This paper is the first to develop a robust barycenter algorithm under the continuous distribution setup, where the learned transport maps must generalize to unseen input points rather than only interpolating between training samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252672/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes empirical samples from K source distributions (only accessible via sampling, not explicit densities), general continuous cost functions c_k (not limited to quadratic Euclidean cost), and ψ-divergence parameters controlling the degree of unbalancedness. It outputs neural-network-parameterized conditional transport maps T_k that map arbitrary input points to the barycenter, plus a scalar m-congruence constant. At inference time, a rejection sampling procedure based on the learned potentials determines which input points are accepted, effectively filtering outliers from the barycenter generation pipeline.

## Method

The core contribution is a semi-dual reformulation of the SUOT barycenter problem as a min-max optimization over m-congruent potentials (satisfying Σλ_k f_k ≡ m) and conditional transport plans, eliminating the need to optimize over the barycenter distribution directly. The m-congruence condition generalizes the standard congruence condition from balanced OT barycenters and is enforced via a specific neural network parametrization of the potentials. Conditional plans are represented as push-forwards of an auxiliary distribution through stochastic neural maps T_k, and the objective is optimized via stochastic gradient descent-ascent with Monte Carlo batch estimation. The ψ-divergence in the dual formulation (replacing the hard marginal constraint with a soft penalty) is what provides robustness: the unbalancedness parameter τ controls how aggressively the model rejects outliers and rebalances class proportions, with smaller τ yielding more robustness and larger τ recovering the standard OT barycenter.

## Results

On the Spiral→Gaussian Mixture benchmark, U-NOTB achieves the best L2 transport map error (0.25 vs. 0.31 for Mini-Batch UOT and 0.65 for UOTM) and the best W2 barycenter distance (0.12 vs. 0.15 and 0.29). On Moon→Spiral, U-NOTB again leads in L2 error (0.52 vs. 0.64 and 0.89) but trails Mini-Batch UOT in W2 distance (0.81 vs. 0.93). Overall, U-NOTB achieves the best score in 3 out of 4 quantitative metrics across the two synthetic datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

U-NOTB outperforms both baselines (UOTM and Mini-Batch UOT) on the L2 transport map error metric across both synthetic datasets, with margins of 0.06 over Mini-Batch UOT and 0.40 over UOTM on Spiral→GM, and 0.12 and 0.37 respectively on Moon→Spiral. On the W2 barycenter distance, U-NOTB wins on Spiral→GM (0.12 vs. 0.15 and 0.29) but loses to Mini-Batch UOT on Moon→Spiral (0.81 vs. 0.93), though it still beats UOTM (0.59). The balanced OT barycenter baseline NOTB (Kolesov et al., 2024a) is used qualitatively in the robustness experiments (Figures 3–4) to demonstrate that U-NOTB with small τ rejects outliers and rebalances class-imbalanced modes, whereas NOTB produces undesirable intermediate modes. The paper also shows that the unbalancedness parameter τ provides a continuous knob: at τ=200 the method closely recovers the balanced OT barycenter, while at τ=1 it maximally rejects outliers (acceptance rate drops from ~63–71% for in-distribution points to ~19% for outlier digit '7' in the shape-color experiment).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous OT barycenter framework of Kolesov et al. (2024a) by replacing balanced OT with semi-unbalanced OT (SUOT), drawing on the semi-dual UOT formulation of Choi et al. (2024a) and the stochastic map parametrization of Korotin et al. (2023a). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an m-congruence condition (generalizing the standard congruence condition) that arises naturally when the barycenter problem involves unbalanced marginals, and the use of ψ-divergences to penalize marginal mismatch. Prior robust barycenter work (Le et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2024) is restricted to discrete empirical measures, making this the first continuous-setup robust barycenter method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap: practitioners working with noisy or imbalanced data in continuous OT barycenter problems (e.g., image aggregation across hospitals) previously had no scalable, theoretically grounded tool. The tunable unbalancedness parameter τ offers practical controllability over robustness, and the rejection-sampling inference procedure enables handling of new inputs at test time. Limitations include the strong convexity assumption in the quality bounds (Theorem 3), the known fake-solutions issue of neural OT solvers, and experiments limited to 2D toy data and a single 32×32 image experiment; scaling to high-dimensional data and more complex cost structures remains open.
