## Problem

Offline RL methods based on temporal difference learning are highly vulnerable to data corruption (noise in states, actions, or rewards), especially when the offline dataset is limited. Prior robust offline RL work has focused almost exclusively on hardening Q-function-based methods, leaving open whether sequence modeling approaches—which bypass the Q function entirely—can handle corruption more effectively. This paper shows that vanilla Decision Transformer is already more robust than temporal difference baselines under corruption, and asks how to further unlock this potential.

## Input / Output

RDT consumes trajectory sequences of length K=20, where each timestep contributes a return-to-go scalar, a state vector, and an action vector, all projected into a shared embedding dimension with learned timestep embeddings. Unlike standard DT which predicts only actions, RDT jointly predicts both the next action and the next reward, providing additional supervision that helps counter corrupted action labels. The model is trained on corrupted trajectories (30% corruption rate, scale ε=1.0) and evaluated by rolling out the learned policy in clean or perturbed environments over 10 trajectories of up to 1000 steps.

## Method

RDT augments Decision Transformer with three complementary robustness techniques targeting model structure, loss function, and data refinement. (1) Embedding dropout randomly zeros dimensions in the projected feature embeddings (after linear projection, before the Transformer) with probability p∈(0.1,0.3), encouraging robust representations without the information loss of masking raw state/action elements. (2) Gaussian weighted learning computes per-sample weights w=exp(−β·δ²) where δ is the detached prediction error, so that samples with anomalously large loss (likely corrupted labels) receive exponentially smaller gradient contributions; separate temperature coefficients β_a and β_r control the blurring for action and reward losses. (3) Iterative data correction tracks running mean and variance of prediction errors during training, then after a warm-up epoch (50th), computes z-scores for each sample and permanently replaces actions or rewards whose z-score exceeds a threshold ζ≈5 with the model's own predictions, effectively purging corrupted labels from the dataset. The key insight is that the supervised sequence-modeling paradigm is inherently more corruption-resistant than bootstrapped Q-learning, and these three lightweight modifications systematically close the remaining gaps in input robustness, label robustness, and data quality.

## Results

Under random data corruption (30% rate, scale 1.0) across 27 task-attack settings on MuJoCo(10%), Kitchen, and Adroit(1%), RDT achieves an overall average normalized score of 57.8, outperforming DT (45.1) by 28.2% and the best temporal-difference baseline RIQL (29.3) by 97%. Under adversarial corruption, RDT reaches 54.3 overall, improving over DT (41.7) by 105% in the action-attack setting. RDT achieves the highest score in 24 out of 27 random-corruption settings and maintains robustness under testing-time observation perturbations (up to scale 0.5) where RORL and DT degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDT (57.8) decisively outperforms DT (45.1) by 28.2% overall under random corruption, winning 24 of 27 individual task-attack settings; the gap widens under adversarial action corruption where RDT (45.3) surpasses DT (22.1) by 105%. Among temporal-difference baselines, RIQL (29.3) is the strongest but still trails RDT by 97%, while CQL (3.8) and UWMSG (3.5) collapse to near-zero on Kitchen and Adroit tasks, confirming that Q-function-based methods are fundamentally fragile under limited-data corruption. DeFog (38.1), the closest sequence-modeling competitor, is outperformed by RDT in all three attack categories, with the largest margin on action corruption (56.3 vs. 38.7). RBC (33.7) slightly beats BC (31.1), validating the Gaussian weighted learning component in isolation, but both remain far below RDT. Under testing-time observation perturbation (scale 0.5), RDT maintains stable performance while RORL (a dedicated test-time robustness method) fails entirely due to training-time data corruption, and DT/RIQL show significant degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Decision Transformer (DT) paradigm for offline RL and departs from the dominant temporal difference learning approach (CQL, IQL, UWMSG, RIQL) that prior robust offline RL work has focused on. While Ye et al. (2024b) and Yang et al. (2024c) develop specialized robustness mechanisms for Q-functions under data corruption, this paper demonstrates that the sequence modeling formulation of DT is inherently more robust to corruption, particularly in limited-data regimes, without any such modifications. RDT then draws on supervised learning robustness techniques (embedding dropout, Gaussian-weighted loss, iterative label correction) to further enhance DT, positioning itself as a bridge between sequence modeling for RL and noise-robust supervised learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that vanilla sequence modeling outperforms purpose-built robust temporal difference methods under data corruption with limited data challenges the field's default assumption that Q-function robustness is the right lever for handling corrupted offline data. This opens a path toward deploying offline RL in real-world settings where sensor noise, human error, or adversarial tampering are inevitable but data collection is expensive. Key limitations include the absence of state correction (left to future work), performance degradation under high corruption rates, and evaluation restricted to MuJoCo/Kitchen/Adroit benchmarks; whether the sequence-modeling advantage persists in continuous high-dimensional control or real-world robotics remains open.
