## Problem

Most test-time adaptation (TTA) methods are designed for classification and rely on entropy minimization or naive full-dimensional feature alignment, neither of which works well for regression. Regression models trained with MSE loss produce features concentrated in a small subspace of the full feature space, causing naive feature alignment to be ineffective or even harmful because it treats degenerated zero-variance dimensions equally with meaningful ones. This paper addresses the lack of an effective, stable TTA method for regression that accounts for this subspace structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained regression model (ResNet-26/50, ViT-B/16, or MLP) and a stream of unlabeled target samples (RGB images or tabular rows), along with pre-computed source statistics: the feature mean, top-K PCA eigenvectors, and corresponding eigenvalues of the source feature covariance. During adaptation, only the affine parameters (γ, β) of batch/layer normalization layers are updated, while all other weights remain frozen. The model outputs a single scalar regression value per input, and the feature used for alignment is the penultimate-layer representation (2048-d for ResNet, 768-d for ViT, 100-d for MLP).

## Method

SSA consists of two components applied during TTA. First, subspace detection computes the PCA of the source feature covariance matrix and selects the top-K eigenvectors (default K=100) that span the subspace where source features actually concentrate, reducing the alignment problem from the full 2048-d space to a much smaller valid subspace. Second, dimension weighting assigns each subspace axis a weight α_d = 1 + |wᵀv_d^s|, where w is the linear regressor weight vector, so that subspace directions more aligned with the output receive stronger alignment pressure. During adaptation, target features are projected into the source subspace, and a weighted symmetric KL divergence between the projected target mini-batch statistics and the source diagonal Gaussian (zero mean, eigenvalue variances) is minimized via gradient descent on BN/LN affine parameters only. This avoids the numerical instability of aligning near-zero-variance dimensions and leverages the central limit theorem to ensure projected features approximate Gaussians, making the KL-based alignment well-posed.

## Results

SSA achieves R²=0.511 on SVHN→MNIST (vs. 0.406 for the unadapted source model), R²=0.731 mean across 13 corruption types on UTKFace (vs. −0.678 for source), R²=0.778 mean on Biwi Kinect gender shift (vs. 0.706 source), and R²=0.639 on California Housing (vs. 0.605 source). On SVHN→MNIST, SSA also reduces RMSE from 2.232 to 2.024 and MAE from 1.608 to 1.209. The method consistently outperforms all classification-derived TTA baselines, several of which diverge or underperform the unadapted source model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSA outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset. On SVHN→MNIST, SSA (R²=0.511) beats the closest baseline Prototype/T3A (0.491) by 2.0 points, while DANN (0.307), TTT (0.288), FR (0.369), and RSD (0.252) all underperform the unadapted source (0.406), and VM diverges catastrophically (R²=−685). On UTKFace with 13 corruptions, SSA achieves a mean R² of 0.731, edging out FR (0.700) and RSD (0.701) by ~3 points, while VM (−2.035) and Prototype (−0.830) diverge entirely. On Biwi Kinect, SSA (0.778) surpasses BN-adapt (0.746) and RSD (partial, ~0.75), whereas FR (−0.357), VM (−0.100), and Prototype (−318) all fail. On California Housing, SSA (0.639) beats FR (0.510) and BN-adapt (0.318), with RSD failing due to numerical instability of SVD. The consistent pattern is that classification-derived TTA methods break the low-dimensional feature subspace learned by regression models, causing degradation or divergence, while SSA's subspace-restricted alignment preserves it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-alignment family of test-time adaptation (TTA) methods—originally developed for classification (Tent, CFA, BUFR, CAFe, CAFA)—to regression, where entropy-based TTA is inapplicable because models output scalars rather than class distributions. The key departure is the observation that regression features (trained with MSE loss) concentrate in a very low-dimensional subspace of the full feature space, making naive full-space alignment unstable and harmful. SSA addresses this by combining PCA-based subspace detection with output-aware dimension weighting, then performing KL-divergence alignment only within the detected subspace, updating only normalization-layer affine parameters as in Tent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap: TTA for regression was largely unexplored, and the finding that standard classification-oriented TTA baselines often degrade regression performance below the unadapted source model is practically important for anyone attempting to transfer TTA techniques to regression pipelines. The method is lightweight (only BN/LN parameters updated, no architecture changes, no source data access at test time) and generalizes across architectures (ResNet, ViT, MLP) and data types (images, tabular). A stated limitation is the covariate-shift assumption (p(y|x) unchanged); extending to concept drift or label shift is left as future work, as is the question of whether the subspace structure persists under more complex distribution shifts.
