## Problem

Existing methods for applying pre-trained LLMs to time series tasks rely on token-level alignment (matching TS patch embeddings to language token embeddings), which the authors argue fails to activate LLMs' true strength: their deep understanding of linguistic logic and structure. Without explicit structural segmentation and logical guidance, lengthy TS-language multimodal inputs remain verbose and semantically incoherent, causing LLMs to underperform even in few-shot and zero-shot settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253265/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series data segmented into patches (sliding window size p, stride s) embedded into the LLM's M-dimensional space, interleaved with language prompt token embeddings. For FSCA, the TS sequence is split into N ordered parts, with each preceding part serving as a demonstration example paired with the prompt, producing N−1 few-shot pairs before the final prediction segment. The DSCA-GNNs module can be inserted at arbitrary layers of a frozen GPT-2 backbone (first 4 layers used), and the fine-grained branch output is decoded into forecasted values (MSE loss) or class labels (cross-entropy loss).

## Method

The core contribution is the Context-Alignment paradigm, implemented via Dual-Scale Context-Alignment GNNs (DSCA-GNNs) that jointly perform structural and logical alignment. Structural alignment uses a fine-grained GNN (G_F, one node per token) and a coarse-grained GNN (G_C, one node per modality block via learnable linear aggregators f_e and f_z), so the LLM perceives the entire TS sequence as a single linguistic component while retaining token-level detail. Logical alignment is encoded as directed edges: in G_C, edges flow from the TS block to the prompt (upstream information) and from the prompt to the next TS block (expected output); in G_F, these are decomposed into token-level edges with weights proportional to cosine similarity and constrained to sum to 1 per target, implicitly treating TS as a whole. A learnable interaction (assignment matrix + weight matrix) transfers macroscopic context from G_C back into G_F. The FSCA instantiation arranges N TS parts with repeated prompts in a few-shot format, and the entire DSCA-GNNs module can be repeatedly inserted at multiple encoder layers to progressively reinforce structural and logical priors.

## Results

On long-term forecasting across 8 datasets (ETT, Weather, ILI, Electricity, Traffic), FSCA achieves the best average MSE, reducing it by 3.1% over the second-best method PatchTST and by 7.3%, 12.2%, and 16.6% over LLM-based baselines S²IP-LLM, Time-LLM, and GPT4TS respectively. In few-shot forecasting (5% training data), FSCA achieves a 6.7% average MSE reduction over S²IP-LLM and a 15.8% gain over PatchTST. In zero-shot cross-domain forecasting, FSCA shows a 13.3% improvement over PatchTST and 18.3–24.3% gains over LLM-based methods. On UEA classification, FSCA reaches 76.4% average accuracy, a 2.4% improvement over the next best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSCA consistently outperforms all three LLM-based baselines (S²IP-LLM, Time-LLM, GPT4TS) across every forecasting setting, with the largest margins in zero-shot (18.3–24.3% MSE reduction) and few-shot (6.7–10.8%) scenarios, confirming that context-level alignment activates LLM capabilities more effectively than token-level alignment. Against the strongest non-LLM baseline PatchTST, FSCA wins on 7 of 8 long-term forecasting datasets (losing only on Weather by a negligible 0.001 MSE) and achieves 13.3% and 15.8% MSE reductions in zero-shot and few-shot settings respectively. Among Transformer-based baselines, FSCA surpasses iTransformer, FEDformer, TimesNet, and ETSformer on nearly all datasets, with particularly large gaps on Traffic (0.386 vs. 0.610–0.624 MSE for FEDformer/TimesNet). On short-term M4 forecasting, FSCA achieves the best SMAPE (11.828), MASE (1.580), and OWA (0.850), edging out S²IP-LLM (12.021/1.612/0.857) and N-BEATS (12.035/1.625/0.869). For UEA classification, FSCA's 76.4% average accuracy surpasses GPT4TS (74.0%) and all classical baselines including ROCKET, LSTNet, and LightTS, demonstrating generality beyond forecasting tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant token-level alignment paradigm (Time-LLM, S2IP-LLM, GPT4TS) that maps TS patch embeddings into LLM embedding space, arguing instead that LLMs' core strength lies in understanding linguistic logic and structure rather than superficial token processing. It builds on PatchTST's patch-based tokenization for TS input and Brown's few-shot prompting technique, but introduces a fundamentally different alignment mechanism: Dual-Scale Context-Alignment GNNs that construct explicit graph structures (dual-scale nodes for hierarchy, directed edges for logical flow) over TS-language multimodal inputs. The paper positions itself as addressing a prerequisite step—activating LLMs' TS comprehension through context-level alignment—before attempting performance enhancement, which prior methods conflate or skip.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that structural and logical context priors are more important than token-level embedding alignment for activating LLMs on TS tasks—challenges a widely adopted design assumption in the LLM-for-TS literature. The particularly strong zero-shot cross-domain results (13–24% MSE reduction over baselines) suggest that explicit logical/structural scaffolding provides transferable inductive bias that raw embedding alignment cannot. However, the approach is validated only on GPT-2 (a ~124M parameter model) and the ETT family for zero-shot, leaving open whether the hand-crafted graph topology and dual-scale GNN design generalize to larger, more capable LLMs or to TS domains with fundamentally different temporal structures. The fixed, prompt-dependent graph construction also limits flexibility for novel task formulations.
