## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient text-video retrieval methods (e.g., VoP, DGL) process each sampled frame independently through CLIP's image encoder, resulting in high inference costs due to the large number of tokens. Meanwhile, token compression methods like ToMe and DiffRate target spatial redundancy within a single image but overlook the substantial temporal redundancy across consecutive video frames. No dedicated compression strategy addresses this cross-frame redundancy in the CLIP-based retrieval setting where frames are encoded separately.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252190/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sampled as F frames (12 for short-video datasets like MSRVTT/LSMDC, 64 for long-video datasets like ActivityNet/DiDeMo) and a text query. Each frame is independently encoded by CLIP's image encoder into patch tokens, and the text is tokenized and encoded by CLIP's text encoder. The output is a cosine similarity score between the aggregated video feature and the text feature, used for bidirectional (text-to-video and video-to-text) retrieval. A distinctive aspect is that the video token sequence is progressively compressed from F×N tokens (e.g., 12×197 for ViT-B/16) down to a single clip of ~127 tokens, a 95% reduction.

## Method

TempMe introduces a Progressive Multi-Granularity (PMG) framework with two stages. The ImgMe Block applies ToMe-style bipartite soft matching to merge a small number of spatially redundant tokens within each frame per layer. The ClipMe Block then performs two novel operations: cross-clip merging, which aggregates adjacent clips by selecting the most similar token pairs across frames (with trainable clip positional embeddings added to preserve temporal information), and intra-clip merging, which further compresses tokens within the newly formed clip at a larger ratio. For 12-frame videos, clips are progressively merged as 12→6→3→1 across layers 9–11, enabling later attention layers to operate on cross-frame tokens in a spatio-temporal domain. LoRA (rank 8) is used for parameter-efficient adaptation of both encoders, yielding only ~0.50M trainable parameters. This progressive merging simultaneously reduces temporal redundancy and enhances temporal modeling, achieving both efficiency gains (51% fewer GFLOPs, 1.8× speedup) and accuracy improvements (4.4% R-Sum over VoP/DGL).

## Results

On MSRVTT text-to-video retrieval with CLIP-ViT-B/32, TempMe achieves R@1=46.1 and R-Sum=198.6 using only 0.50M trainable parameters and 34.8 GFLOPs, outperforming all parameter-efficient baselines. With CLIP-ViT-B/16, TempMe reaches R@1=49.0 and R-Sum=206.7 at 121.4 GFLOPs, reducing output tokens by 95% and GFLOPs by 51% compared to VoP while achieving a 1.8× speedup and 4.4% R-Sum improvement. Under full fine-tuning with ViT-B/16, TempMe gains 7.9% R-Sum over CLIP4Clip while training 1.57× faster and using 75.2% of the GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TempMe dominates all 15 baselines on MSRVTT across both ViT-B/32 and ViT-B/16 backbones. Against the strongest parameter-efficient video retrieval methods, TempMe outperforms VoP (14.10M params, 58.0 GFLOPs, R-Sum 194.8) by 3.8% R-Sum while using 28× fewer parameters and 40% fewer GFLOPs, and beats DGL (0.83M, 67.4 GFLOPs, R-Sum 194.4) by 4.2% R-Sum with 48% lower GFLOPs. Among token compression baselines, TempMe surpasses ToMe (R-Sum 191.4, 40.2 GFLOPs) by 7.2% R-Sum and DiffRate (R-Sum 189.9, 36.8 GFLOPs) by 8.7% R-Sum, while also achieving the lowest GFLOPs (34.8). On the larger ViT-B/16 backbone, TempMe (R-Sum 206.7, 121.4 GFLOPs) exceeds VoP (202.3, 246.2 GFLOPs) by 4.4% R-Sum with 51% fewer GFLOPs and a 1.8× throughput advantage (45.1 vs 25.0 videos/s). Consistent gains are observed on ActivityNet (R-Sum 205.6 vs 191.5 for LoRA), DiDeMo (R-Sum 202.2 vs 188.7), and LSMDC (R-Sum 217.0 vs 207.6), confirming generalization across datasets and video lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

TempMe extends ToMe's bipartite soft-matching token merging from the spatial domain (within a single image) to the temporal domain (across consecutive video frames), introducing a Progressive Multi-Granularity framework that first compresses spatial redundancy per-frame (ImgMe) and then progressively merges temporal redundancy across frames (ClipMe). It departs from existing video token compression methods (STA, TESTA) that assume all frame tokens are processed jointly in a video-specific architecture, whereas TempMe is designed specifically for CLIP's per-frame image encoder paradigm. The method also integrates with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, Prompt, Adapter) to keep trainable parameters minimal (~0.50M), positioning it at the intersection of PEFT and token compression for cross-modal retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical bottleneck for deploying CLIP-based text-video retrieval is inference cost, and this paper identifies temporal redundancy across frames as the dominant yet overlooked source of that cost, showing that 95% of tokens can be eliminated while *improving* accuracy by enabling cross-frame attention. The method generalizes beyond CLIP to video foundation models (UMT) and video QA, suggesting broad applicability. Key limitations include incompatibility with architectures that process all frame tokens simultaneously (e.g., CLIP-ViP), and the text encoder remains unoptimized. An open question is whether the progressive merging strategy can be further accelerated or adapted to streaming/real-time video retrieval settings.
