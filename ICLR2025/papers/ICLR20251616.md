## Problem

Decision Transformers capture long-range dependencies effectively via self-attention but fail to model fine-grained Markovian patterns (local associations between consecutive timesteps) prevalent in many offline RL datasets. Conversely, convolution-based variants like Decision ConvFormer excel at local patterns but degrade on non-Markovian tasks requiring long-term credit assignment (e.g., Maze2d). No existing architecture flexibly handles both dependency types across diverse offline RL datasets.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of K timesteps, each containing a return-to-go scalar, a state vector, and an action vector, with the state optionally augmented by goal-state coordinates for goal-reaching tasks. The embedding dimension is split asymmetrically along the channel axis (ratio ξ) between the two branches rather than fed identically to both. Output is a single next-action vector generated autoregressively, with the model conditioned on a desired RTG value (and goal position) to steer trajectory generation.

## Method

LSDT replaces the standard masked attention block in DT with a Masked Long-Short block containing two parallel branches: a self-attention branch (long-term) and a Dynamic Convolution branch (short-term). The input embedding of dimension D is partitioned along the channel axis into (1−ξ)D for attention and ξD for DynamicConv, allowing flexible allocation of capacity between global and local dependency modeling. DynamicConv generates per-timestep convolution kernels via a linear projection of the current input, reducing parameter count from d²×k to H×k while maintaining linear complexity. For goal-reaching tasks, the method introduces goal-state conditioning by concatenating the goal coordinate vector to each state representation, providing explicit directional information that RTG alone cannot supply in sparse-reward, multi-goal environments. The architecture is plug-and-play: setting ξ=0 recovers standard DT, ξ=100% yields convolution-only DT, and the short-term branch can be swapped with DC's fixed filters to enhance other models (LSDC).

## Results

On the D4RL locomotion benchmark (9 tasks), LSDT achieves an average normalized score of 82.0, matching DC (82.2) and outperforming DT (76.4), IQL (76.5), and CQL (77.6). On the challenging Antmaze-medium-play task, LSDT with goal-state conditioning scores 85.5, dramatically surpassing DT (0.0), DC (1.5), and IQL (71.2). On Maze2d-large, LSDT-gs reaches 116.7, nearly doubling QDT (62.0) and DCh-gs (57.7). On Adroit tasks (Appendix C), LSDT achieves 109.2 on pen-human-v0, outperforming IQL (71.5) and EDAC (52.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSDT consistently outperforms standard DT across all 13 locomotion and Antmaze tasks, with the largest gaps on Antmaze-medium-play (85.5 vs 0.0) and Antmaze-medium-diverse (75.8 vs 0.0), where DT completely fails. Against DC (the strongest DT variant), LSDT matches on locomotion average (82.0 vs 82.2) but dramatically outperforms on non-Markovian Antmaze tasks (85.5 vs 1.5 on medium-play), while DC retains a small edge on Hopper-medium (92.6 vs 87.2) and Hopper-medium-replay (94.2 vs 93.9). Against value-based baselines, LSDT exceeds IQL on 7 of 9 locomotion tasks and on both Antmaze-medium tasks, and surpasses CQL on all Antmaze tasks. On Maze2d (Table 2), LSDT-gs outperforms QDT by 19.9 points on medium (68.4 vs 48.5) and 54.7 points on large (116.7 vs 62.0), while CQL remains competitive only on the simpler umaze. On Adroit (Table 9), LSDT achieves the best score on 6 of 8 tasks, with the largest margin on pen-human-v0 (109.2 vs 71.5 IQL), though it ties with EDAC on the very difficult relocate-cloned-v0 (both near 0). The goal-state conditioning ablation (Table 4) shows DT alone scores 0.0 on Antmaze-medium-play/diverse, while DT-gs reaches 39.0/46.8, confirming that explicit goal information is critical for sparse-reward navigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decision Transformer (DT) lineage by introducing a dual-branch architecture that parallels self-attention (long-range) with Dynamic Convolution (short-range), drawing conceptual inspiration from Branchformer and Conformer in speech/CV. It departs from Decision ConvFormer (DC), which replaces attention entirely with causal convolutions, by retaining both mechanisms in parallel and making their relative contribution tunable via a channel dimension ratio ξ. The goal-state conditioning component repurposes a standard technique from goal-conditioned MLP-based RL (Schaul et al., 2015; Liu et al., 2022) into the sequence-modeling RL setting, where it had been underexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LSDT provides a practical, plug-and-play architecture that consistently improves DT across both Markovian (locomotion) and non-Markovian (Antmaze, Maze2d) offline RL datasets, with the most dramatic gains in goal-reaching tasks where DT previously scored 0. The goal-state conditioning trick—simply concatenating goal coordinates to the state—yields large improvements (e.g., 0→85.5 on Antmaze-medium-play) and is immediately adoptable by any DT-based method. Open questions include whether the dimension ratio ξ can be learned dynamically rather than hand-tuned per task, whether the architecture scales to multi-task or continuous online settings, and whether the DynamicConv overhead can be reduced without sacrificing the local-feature gains.
