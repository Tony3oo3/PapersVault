## Problem

Standard SGD convergence guarantees rely on unbiased gradient estimates, but many practical settings (approximate preconditioning, gradient compression, differential privacy noise) produce gradients whose expectation is only correlated with, not equal to, the true gradient. Existing analyses either assume additive noise that accumulates over iterations, require strong convexity, or cannot handle significant bias at the optimum. This paper provides convergence guarantees for convex optimization under the weaker assumption that the expected oracle output is merely positively correlated with the true gradient, and extends the framework to non-convex functions with a hidden convex structure.

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a convex (or hidden-convex) objective f: R^d → R and a stochastic oracle h(x) whose output satisfies ‖h(x)‖ ≤ H a.s. and whose expectation is correlated with ∇f(x) (e.g., E[h(x)] = A(x)∇f(x) for an SPD matrix A, or more generally ⟨E[h(x)], ∇f(x)⟩ ≥ α‖∇f(x)‖‖E[h(x)]‖). Problem parameters (Lipschitz constant H, smoothness L, minimizer bound R, correlation constants α, β) are assumed known for hyperparameter tuning. The output is a single point x with f(x) − f(x⋆) ≤ ε, achieved in Õ(ε^{-2}) oracle calls for slowly-varying misalignment or Õ(ε^{-3}) for general misalignment and hidden convexity.

## Method

Three algorithmic frameworks are developed. For slowly-varying SPD matrix transformations, the paper uses projected SGD with momentum where the iterate-averaging scheme queries gradients at the running average (not just averaging at the end), giving iterates a natural O(1/t) stability property; the analysis works in the matrix-induced norm ‖·‖_{A_t^{-1}} and shows that ℓ₂-projection with a carefully chosen radius is equivalent to matrix-norm projection, with momentum critically correcting for gradient misalignment. For general misaligned gradients, the algorithm uses mini-batched normalized gradient steps with a novel correction term (−η²x_t/‖x_t‖²) that keeps iterates within a ball without standard projection, analyzed via a potential function Φ_t = (t+k)(f(x_t)−f(x⋆)). For hidden convexity (f(x) = C(P(x)) with convex C and invertible transform P), a multi-scale algorithm performs projected SGD in shrinking balls around iteratively updated centers, leveraging a key lemma that local non-improvement in a small ball implies near-global optimality, yielding an O(N^{-1/3}) rate without requiring access to the Jacobian J(x).

## Results

The paper establishes three main convergence rate guarantees for optimization with misaligned stochastic gradients. For slowly-varying matrix-transformed gradients (Section 3), the algorithm achieves the optimal rate of $\tilde{O}(N^{-1/2})$, i.e., $O(\sqrt{\log N / N})$ (Theorem 3.2). For the more general setting where the expected gradient is merely correlated with the true gradient (Section 4), the rate is $\tilde{O}(N^{-1/3})$, with the explicit bound $\frac{5200}{N^{1/3}} \cdot \log T \cdot \left(\frac{RH}{\alpha^2 \beta} + \frac{LR^2}{\alpha^4}\right)$ (Theorem 4.4). For hidden convexity (Section 5), the algorithm achieves $O(N^{-1/3})$ with bound $O\left(\frac{D_2^{4/3} H \rho^{1/3}}{(\alpha\beta)^{4/3} N^{1/3}}\right)$ (Theorem 5.4). These are purely theoretical guarantees; no empirical experiments are conducted.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's comparisons are entirely theoretical, benchmarking convergence rates against prior analyses. Against Demidovich et al. (2023), which handles a broadly similar biased-gradient setup, this paper improves the non-strongly-convex rate from $O(N^{-1/4})$ to $\tilde{O}(N^{-1/3})$ while removing the strong-convexity requirement that their $O(1/(\mu^2 N))$ result demands. Against Beznosikov et al. (2023) for compressed gradients, this paper relaxes the requirement that noise must vanish when the true gradient vanishes, allowing persistent bias at the optimum. For hidden convexity, the paper improves upon Chen et al. (2024), which achieves $O(N^{-1/4})$ in a matching setting, to $O(N^{-1/3})$, and avoids the assumption in Sakos et al. (2024) that the raw Jacobian $J(x)$ is accessible. The $\tilde{O}(N^{-1/2})$ rate in Section 3 is noted to be unprovable even for error-free gradient oracles (Nesterov et al., 2018), making it a tight result for the slowly-varying preconditioning case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant additive-noise model for biased gradients (Ajalloeian & Stich 2020; Beznosikov et al. 2023; Demidovich et al. 2023) and instead studies a multiplicative/correlation-based misalignment where E[h(x)] is merely correlated with ∇f(x), allowing non-zero bias even at the optimum. The analytical toolkit draws on Cutkosky's (2019) anytime online-to-batch conversion for iterate-averaging and Defazio's (2020) equivalence between primal averaging and SGD with momentum, while the hidden convexity application extends Fatkhullin et al. (2023) by assuming smoothness of the transformation P rather than the convex function C. The connection to online optimization with hints (Dekel et al. 2017; Bhaskara et al. 2020, 2021) is noted but distinguished: here the algorithm never sees the true gradient and cannot self-correct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results have direct practical relevance to preconditioned optimizers (Shampoo, AdaHessian, Sophia), gradient compression in distributed training, and non-convex problems with hidden convex structure (RL, revenue management, neural network training). The finding that momentum corrects for gradient misalignment is notable because standard theoretical analyses of preconditioned methods omit momentum despite its universal use in practice. Key open questions include whether the O(ε^{-3}) rate in the general and hidden-convexity settings can be improved to O(ε^{-2}), whether a single meta-algorithm can subsume all three settings, and whether the bounded-gradient assumption can be relaxed.
