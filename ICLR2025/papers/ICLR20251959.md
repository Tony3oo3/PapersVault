## Problem

LLMs frequently contradict themselves and external knowledge bases when prompted to reason about logical relations (negation, implication, modus ponens), and existing remedies either require external solvers at inference time, are tailored to a single constraint type, or demand large-scale fine-tuning. This paper seeks a principled, constraint-agnostic fine-tuning objective that improves both factuality and self-consistency without any external reasoning component at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251959/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM, a knowledge base of textual facts with ground-truth truth values (e.g., BeliefBank's 1,072 calibration facts), and a set of logical constraints expressed as arbitrary propositional logic formulas over those facts. At training time, each fact is queried via a binary yes/no prompt and the LLM's probability of emitting an affirmative token is extracted; these probabilities are plugged into a compiled circuit to compute the semantic loss. The output is a fine-tuned LLM (via LoRA + 4-bit quantization) that, when prompted with the same binary questions, allocates near-zero probability to truth assignments violating the constraints, with no additional inference-time overhead.

## Method

The core mechanism is the semantic loss (SL), which maximises the exact probability that a logical constraint holds under the LLM's induced distribution over fact truth values — formally a weighted model count (WMC) where weights are the LLM's per-fact probabilities. To make WMC tractable and differentiable, each constraint is compiled once into a smooth, decomposable, deterministic circuit (a sentential decision diagram) using PySDD, taking only ~2.5 ms per formula; the circuit is then evaluated in closed form with the LLM's extracted probabilities, and gradients flow back through the circuit graph to update LLM parameters. The approach is constraint-agnostic: negation (XOR), factual implication, reverse implication, or their conjunction (the SUPER constraint) can all be expressed as a single circuit and optimised simultaneously in one training pass. Training uses LoRA with 4-bit quantisation on LLaMA-2 7B/70B or Macaw-Large, requiring only a single offline fine-tuning session with no external solver at inference time.

## Results

On BeliefBank (test/silver split), LoCo-LLaMA-2-7B(SUPER) achieves an average consistency score of 0.80 across all six metrics (FAC, C-IMP, C-REV, C-NEG, SC-IMP, SC-REV), compared to 0.41 for LLaMA-2-7B zero-shot and 0.55 for LLaMA-2-70B zero-shot. The F-IMP variant excels on implication consistency (C-IMP=0.98, SC-IMP=0.99) and factuality (FAC=0.98) but sacrifices negation consistency (C-NEG=0.01). On the Macaw-Large comparison (RQ1), LoCo-Macaw trained on T1 facts only achieves IMP=0.99 and TOT F1=0.96, outperforming ConCoRD (IMP=0.91, TOT F1=0.91) without any external solver at inference time. On EntailmentBank, LoCo-LLaMA-2-7B(F-IMP) maintains 0.98 consistency across all tree depths (1–5), while the baseline LLaMA-2-7B degrades from 0.87 to 0.63.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ConCoRD (Mitchell et al., 2022), which uses a MaxSAT solver at inference time, LoCo-Macaw trained on T1 facts only achieves IMP=0.99 vs. ConCoRD's 0.91 and TOT F1=0.96 vs. 0.91, while also being faster at inference (2405s vs. 3669s for the full BeliefBank evaluation). Against zero-shot Macaw-Large (Tafjord & Clark, 2021), LoCo-Macaw improves IMP from 0.83 to 0.99 and TOT F1 from 0.81 to 0.96, showing that the semantic loss is far more effective than the pre-trained model's implicit knowledge. Against LLaMA-2 (Touvron et al., 2023), the SUPER variant of LoCo-LLaMA-2-7B (AVG=0.80) surpasses even the much larger LLaMA-2-70B zero-shot (AVG=0.55) by 25 percentage points on average consistency, while standard XENT fine-tuning of LLaMA-2-7B actually degrades performance to AVG=0.35 due to class imbalance. The F-IMP variant achieves near-perfect implication consistency (C-IMP=0.98, SC-IMP=0.99) but at the cost of negation consistency (C-NEG=0.01), illustrating the multi-objective trade-off; the SUPER conjunction balances all constraints and yields the best overall average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the semantic loss (Xu et al., 2018) — previously applied to tabular and image classification — to LLM fine-tuning by compiling propositional logic constraints into sentential decision diagrams (Darwiche, 2011; Vergari et al., 2021) for tractable, differentiable weighted model counting (Chavira & Darwiche, 2008). It departs from the dominant paradigm of enforcing LLM consistency via inference-time external solvers (ConCoRD, Mitchell et al., 2022; maieutic prompting, Jung et al., 2022) or constraint-specific fine-tuning heuristics (Burns et al., 2022 for negation; Kassner et al., 2023 for implications), offering instead a single constraint-agnostic training objective that can combine arbitrary propositional logic formulas simultaneously. The key conceptual move is treating the LLM's token-level truth probabilities as weights in a WMC computation, making the loss agnostic to the specific logical structure being enforced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach provides a principled, inference-time-free mechanism for improving LLM logical consistency, which is practically important for deploying LLMs as reliable knowledge sources. A notable limitation is the reliance on fine-tuning (sensitivity to prompt format) and the conditional independence assumption on fact probabilities, which can bias learned solutions. Open questions include extending to first-order logic and more complex operators, integrating with writable external knowledge bases (à la REFLEX), and whether the independence assumption holds for larger or more entangled knowledge domains. The finding that a fine-tuned 7B model can match or exceed a 70B model on consistency metrics suggests that targeted neuro-symbolic fine-tuning may be a more efficient path to reliability than scaling alone.
