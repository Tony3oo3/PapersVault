## Problem

Existing omnidirectional radiance field methods assume an idealized spherical camera model, ignoring the complex lens distortion inherited from stitching multiple fisheye lenses in real 360-degree cameras. Meanwhile, all prior self-calibration approaches (BARF, L2G-NeRF, CamP) are designed exclusively for perspective cameras and cannot directly handle omnidirectional images, leaving no framework to jointly recover accurate camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and scene geometry from noisy or absent pose priors in 360-degree settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple equirectangular 360-degree images (760×1520 on 360Roam, 1000×2000 on OmniBlender) along with an optional coarse point cloud for 3D Gaussian initialization (from SfM, monocular depth estimation, or random sampling) and optionally noisy SfM camera poses. It outputs a set of 3D Gaussians encoding the omnidirectional radiance field, refined camera poses parameterized as quaternion-plus-translation vectors, a learned per-scene omnidirectional distortion model, and real-time (>60 FPS) novel 360-degree view rendering. Notably, the system can operate entirely from scratch with all cameras initialized at the origin and no pose prior.

## Method

SC-OmniGS builds on 3D Gaussian splatting with a differentiable omnidirectional rasterizer that projects Gaussians onto a unit sphere and unrolls to equirectangular, with analytically derived gradients for camera pose (quaternion and translation) through both the color (spherical harmonics viewing direction) and the 2D Gaussian kernel (projection center and Jacobian) branches. A novel generic omnidirectional camera model, decoupled from the rasterizer, learns per-pixel angle distortion via a frozen spherical grid multiplied by trainable distortion coefficients (Hadamard product with Tanh activation), enabling differentiable undistortion of input images via bicubic grid sampling. Joint optimization minimizes a cosine-weighted spherical photometric loss (MAE + SSIM) that compensates for the non-conformal equirectangular projection's polar stretching, combined with an anisotropy regularizer that penalizes 3D Gaussian scale ratios exceeding a threshold to prevent filamentous kernels near the poles. All parameters—Gaussian positions, covariances, SH coefficients, camera poses, and distortion coefficients—are optimized end-to-end with Adam over 30,000 iterations, with periodic re-initialization of Gaussians from the coarse point cloud at iterations 2000 and 4000 to prune spurious primitives.

## Results

On the real-world 360Roam dataset (8 multi-room scenes), SC-OmniGS achieves 25.30 PSNR / 0.803 SSIM / 0.180 LPIPS on test views without perturbation, outperforming OmniGS (24.21 PSNR) and 3D-GS (20.79 PSNR). Under camera pose perturbation, SC-OmniGS maintains 24.91 PSNR / 0.790 SSIM (SfM init) or 24.34 PSNR / 0.770 SSIM (random init), while all NeRF-based baselines drop to 13.7–20.5 PSNR. On the synthetic OmniBlender dataset, training from scratch with rendered depth initialization, SC-OmniGS reaches 34.37 PSNR / 0.956 SSIM on Barbershop test views, compared to 28.20 PSNR for the best NeRF baseline (L2G-NeRF°) and 18.43 PSNR for OmniGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SC-OmniGS outperforms all five baselines across both datasets and all evaluation settings. Against the non-calibration baselines, SC-OmniGS improves over OmniGS by +1.09 PSNR (25.30 vs 24.21) without perturbation and by a dramatic +9.29 PSNR (24.91 vs 15.62) under perturbation, where OmniGS collapses. Against the self-calibration NeRF baselines under perturbation on 360Roam, SC-OmniGS (SfM init) leads BARF by +4.71 PSNR (24.91 vs 20.20), L2G-NeRF by +4.40 PSNR (24.91 vs 20.51), and CamP by +10.66 PSNR (24.91 vs 14.25). Even with random point initialization under perturbation, SC-OmniGS (24.34 PSNR) still exceeds the best NeRF baseline (L2G-NeRF at 20.51 PSNR) by nearly 4 PSNR. On OmniBlender from scratch, SC-OmniGS (render depth) achieves 34.37 PSNR on Barbershop test, surpassing L2G-NeRF° (28.20), BARF° (27.51), and OmniGS (18.43) by 6–16 PSNR, demonstrating that the omnidirectional pose gradient derivation is critical for stable calibration from zero pose prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

SC-OmniGS extends the self-calibrating radiance field line (BARF, L2G-NeRF, CamP) from perspective cameras to omnidirectional 360-degree cameras, a setting where all prior calibration methods are inapplicable. It builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting for the scene representation and on OmniGS's differentiable omnidirectional rasterizer (splatting Gaussians onto a unit sphere), but departs from both by deriving closed-form camera pose gradients within the omnidirectional splatting procedure and introducing a learnable spherical-grid camera model to rectify stitching distortion. The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating the 360-degree image as a whole sphere for joint optimization rather than decomposing it into cube-map perspective views, which the authors show leads to optimization instability and disconnected faces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to jointly optimize omnidirectional camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and 3D Gaussians, removing the dependency on SfM for 360-degree radiance field reconstruction — a practical bottleneck for consumer-grade 360 cameras whose stitching distortion is not captured by ideal spherical models. The differentiable omnidirectional camera model (a frozen spherical grid with learnable distortion coefficients) is a reusable component for any pipeline processing real 360-degree imagery. A stated limitation is that training from scratch on large multi-room scenes still fails, pointing toward integration with omnidirectional SLAM as a natural next step. The weighted spherical photometric loss and anisotropy regularizer are also transferable to other equirectangular rendering tasks.
