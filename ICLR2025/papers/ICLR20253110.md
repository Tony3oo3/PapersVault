## Problem

Shallow GNNs (fewer than 10 layers) empirically predict near-optimal LP solutions, yet LPs are global optimization problems where one variable's optimum depends on long-range dependencies. Prior theoretical results guaranteeing GNN expressiveness for LPs require depth growing polynomially with problem size, leaving the principle behind shallow GNNs' empirical success unexplained. This paper bridges that gap by connecting GNN depth to distributed computing round complexity and shifting from worst-case to average-case analysis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse binary LP in normalized packing/covering form (max 1ᵀx s.t. Ax ≤ 1, x ≥ 0 with A ∈ {0,1}^{m×n} having O(m+n) non-zero entries and m = Θ(n)) and encodes it as a bipartite graph where left nodes are primal variables, right nodes are dual variables, and edges correspond to non-zero entries of A. It outputs a (1+ε)-approximate primal solution x̂ and dual solution ŷ simultaneously, with the guarantee holding for (1−η) fraction of all instances of a given size. The GNN depth and width remain constant as m and n grow, using only 5-dimensional per-node feature vectors and at most ~2K learnable parameters.

## Method

The core insight is a bidirectional equivalence: any d-round anonymous distributed LP algorithm can be simulated by a d-depth anonymous GNN, and vice versa. Building on this, the authors design a new constant-round distributed algorithm (Algorithm 2) that first identifies γ-dense rows and columns (which are rare on average), puts them to 'sleep,' runs a constant-round packing/covering algorithm (Algorithm 1 from Li et al. 2024b) on the remaining row-sparse, column-sparse subproblem, and then reconstructs the full solution by setting slept variables to fixed values. Unrolling this algorithm yields a constant-depth, constant-width GNN with a mask mechanism (learnable sigmoid-based functions) to handle the sleep/awake distinction and parameterized functions g_θ and b_θ to approximate the algorithm's threshold and scaling operations. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3) proves that for (1−η) fraction of sparse binary LP instances, this GNN achieves (1+ε)-approximation with depth bounded by O(log(γ)/ε²), independent of problem size. Empirically, a 5-layer unrolled GNN with 16-dimensional hidden features achieves ~0.20 relative primal gap and ~0.46 relative dual gap across problem sizes up to 1500, using only ~2K parameters versus ~250K for a comparable GCN.

## Results

On sparse binary LP instances of sizes 100–1500 (with 10×(M+N) non-zero entries), the proposed 5-layer unrolled GNN achieves a test primal relative gap (RP) of ~0.20 and a test dual relative gap (RD) of ~0.45–0.47, compared to GCN's RP of ~0.94–1.0 and RD of ~18.3–18.6. Training loss is comparable between the two methods (~0.008–0.009). The proposed GNN uses at most 2K parameters, roughly 0.8% of the ~250K parameters in the GCN baseline, while maintaining constant depth (L=5) and width (K=16) regardless of problem size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed constant-depth unrolled GNN against a standard GCN baseline (same L=5 layers, 64-dimensional features) on randomly generated sparse binary LP instances. The proposed method decisively outperforms GCN on both primal and dual relative gaps: test RP drops from ~1.0 (GCN) to ~0.20 (proposed), and test RD drops from ~18.3–18.6 (GCN) to ~0.45–0.47 (proposed), representing roughly a 5× improvement on RP and a ~40× improvement on RD. This advantage is achieved while using only 2K parameters versus ~250K for the GCN (a 125× reduction). Training loss is essentially matched between the two methods (~0.008–0.009), indicating the gap is not due to underfitting but rather to the structural inductive bias of the unrolled distributed algorithm. The performance of the proposed method remains stable across all four problem sizes (100 to 1500), consistent with the theoretical guarantee of constant depth independent of instance size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the distributed algorithm for sparse packing/covering LPs by Li et al. (2024b), unrolling it into a GNN architecture, and extends it with a new distributed algorithm (Algorithm 2) that handles average-case instances by 'sleeping' dense rows and columns. It positions itself against the GNN-for-LP literature (Chen et al. 2023, Qian et al. 2024, Li et al. 2024a), which established that GNNs can solve LPs but required depth growing polynomially with problem size. The key conceptual departure is the explicit equivalence between d-round distributed LP algorithms and d-depth GNNs, combined with a shift from worst-case to average-case analysis, drawing on impossibility results from the LOCAL model (Kuhn et al. 2016).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for why shallow GNNs empirically succeed on LPs, resolving a counter-intuitive gap between local information processing and global optimization. However, the practical impact is currently limited: the theoretical depth bound is very large (e.g., 54079 for a 2-approximation on 99% of instances), the results are restricted to sparse binary LPs with m=Θ(n), and the empirical validation uses only small-scale problems (100–1500 variables). Open questions include extending the analysis to general non-negative LPs, tightening the theoretical constants, and validating the constant-depth claim at the scale where GNNs are actually deployed (millions of variables).
