## Problem

Despite expanded training context windows (e.g., 128K for Llama 3.1), open-source LLMs typically achieve effective context lengths below 50% of their training length. The paper identifies the root cause as a left-skewed frequency distribution of relative positions during pretraining and post-training, where distant position indices are severely undertrained, impairing the model's ability to gather information from far-away tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251970/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

STRING operates at inference time on standard long-context text sequences (up to 128K tokens) and produces modified RoPE position indices for queries only, leaving key and value caches unchanged. The method requires no additional training or fine-tuning; it is applied as a drop-in replacement for standard RoPE by adjusting position IDs before the rotary embedding computation. For short inputs shorter than the shift offset S, STRING degenerates to standard RoPE with no behavioral change.

## Method

STRING manipulates the relative position matrix P in three steps: (1) dropping all position indices i ≥ N that fall in the infrequent tail, (2) shifting the remaining well-trained indices from the main diagonal toward the bottom-left corner with offset S = L − N so that distant token pairs are represented by frequently encountered position values, and (3) adding a small local window W (≥32) to the shifted region to preserve fluency among neighboring tokens. The implementation splits attention into two FlashAttention calls: a sliding-window attention around the diagonal (unmodified positions) and a causal self-attention over the bottom-left triangle (shifted query positions), whose outputs are merged via softmax-normalized weighting. This incurs negligible computational overhead—less than 0.3 s/token and <5 GB extra GPU memory on A100—while improving NIAH scores by an average of 18 points across seven models and boosting Llama 3.1 70B and Qwen2 72B by over 10 points on RULER and InfiniteBench.

## Results

On the Needle-in-a-Haystack (4-needle) benchmark, STRING achieves an average score of 85.7% across seven models (2K–128K training lengths), a 17.9-point improvement over the original RoPE (67.8%) and 12.6 points over the best extrapolation baseline DCA (73.1%). On RULER at 128K, STRING boosts Llama3.1 70B from 66.6 to 81.7 average and Qwen2 72B from 53.7 to 84.6, establishing new state-of-the-art for open-source models. On InfiniteBench, Llama3.1 70B with STRING reaches 56.88 average, surpassing GPT-4-128K (55.69), Claude-2 (47.96), and Kimi-chat (43.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STRING consistently outperforms all five training-free extrapolation baselines (NTK-Aware RoPE, YaRN, ReRoPE, Self-Extend, DCA) on the NIAH 4-needle task, achieving 85.7% average versus 73.1% for DCA (the strongest baseline) and 67.8% for original RoPE. On RULER with Llama3.1-8B, STRING is the only method that improves over the baseline (80.0 vs. 77.0), while all extrapolation methods either degrade or barely change performance (e.g., YaRN drops VT from 70.4 to 39.8). At the 70B scale, STRING delivers a 15.1-point gain on Llama3.1 70B (66.6→81.7) and a 30.9-point gain on Qwen2 72B (53.7→84.6), both surpassing GPT-4-128K (81.2). On InfiniteBench, Llama3.1 70B with STRING (56.88) exceeds GPT-4-128K (55.69) by 1.2 points and outperforms Claude-2 (47.96) and Kimi-chat (43.91) by 8.9 and 13.0 points respectively, with the largest single-task gain on Retrieval KV (76.07 vs. 2.22 for baseline Llama3.1 70B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on length extrapolation (NTK-Aware RoPE, YaRN, ReRoPE, Self-Extend, DCA), which all aim to push models beyond their training context length. Instead, STRING reframes the problem as one of *within-training-length* underutilization, identifying the left-skewed frequency distribution of relative positions in the RoPE position matrix as the root cause. The method repurposes sliding-window attention and FlashAttention as implementation vehicles, but the core idea—shifting well-trained position indices to overwrite undertrained ones during inference—is a new diagnostic-driven intervention rather than an architectural or frequency-scaling change. The empirical pretraining study on TinyLlama provides the first direct evidence linking position exposure frequency to effective context length, a connection prior work had not isolated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STRING's training-free, FlashAttention-compatible design means it can be dropped into any existing RoPE-based model (Llama, Qwen, Mistral) with zero additional compute, making it immediately deployable and practically impactful. The diagnostic insight—that the tail of the position frequency distribution is the binding constraint on long-context performance—opens a clear training-side research direction (rebalancing position exposure during pretraining) that the paper explicitly leaves as future work. Limitations include the pretraining study being restricted to ≤4K context lengths, the unknown interaction with multi-stage long-context training curricula (e.g., Llama 3.1's 6-stage approach), and the fact that STRING does not address non-RoPE positional encodings. The finding that most open-source models' effective context is under 50% of their claimed length, and that a simple index reassignment recovers much of the gap, should reshape how the community diagnoses and addresses long-context failures.
