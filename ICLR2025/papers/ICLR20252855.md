## Problem

Existing protein backbone generation methods rely on parametric, linear topology configurations (blueprints) that define secondary structure elements through a fixed set of geometric parameters, limiting coverage of the 3D topology space and preventing flexible, user-driven topology editing. This constrains delicate backbone space navigation needed for tasks like multi-state design and allosteric protein design, where precise control over helix/strand arrangement and connectivity is essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252855/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D curve as topology condition, where α-helices and β-sheets are abstracted to their central axes while loop regions retain original Cα coordinates; the curve is resampled, smoothed, and annotated with SSE labels. For binder design, a target protein structure and identified interface hotspot residues are additionally provided; for motif scaffolding, a fixed motif structure is supplied as a template. The output is a full protein backbone represented as residue frames (Cα translation coordinates in R^{N×3} plus N-Cα-C rotation matrices in SO(3)^N), which can then be refolded via ProteinMPNN + OmegaFold to assess designability.

## Method

ProtPainter operates in two stages: (1) a CurveEncoder (3-layer EGNN + 1D curvature CNN + multi-head attention) predicts SSE annotations from the input curve, which are then used to parametrically generate a 'naive sketch' that upsamples the coarse curve into a full-resolution backbone-length representation; (2) the sketch guides a retraining-free DDPM sampling process by fusing into the denoising steps via a low-pass filtering operation φ that aligns the sketch and generated backbone at matching dimensions, analogous to ILVR's reference-image conditioning in super-resolution. A key mechanism, Helix-Gating, implements a two-phase fusion schedule: in the 'confidential' phase (when the RoseTTAFold-predicted helix percentage is below the sketch's), guidance is scaled down by a factor dependent on the helix-percentage gap; once the threshold is crossed, the 'controllable' phase applies full sketch guidance. The translation part is guided by RoseTTAFold self-conditioning estimates (inherited from RFDiffusion), while rotation follows Brownian motion on SO(3), with the two treated as conditionally independent under the translation condition.

## Results

On the Protein Restoration Task across three datasets (HHH_ems, 1a0b_cluster, GPCR), ProtPainter achieves scTF of 0.767, 0.791, and 0.800 respectively, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: RFDiffusion at 0.693, 0.272, 0.262). In terms of fit designability (FD: scTM>0.5, pLDDT>70, scTF>0.7), ProtPainter reaches 0.654, 0.734, and 0.792, while RFDiffusion achieves only 0.640, 0.050, and 0.000, and all conditional baselines (Chroma, TopoDiff) score near zero. Confident designability (CD: scTM>0.5, pLDDT>70) remains competitive at 0.832, 0.870, and 0.936, slightly below RFDiffusion's 0.980, 0.974, 0.886 on shorter proteins but surpassing it on GPCR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtPainter decisively outperforms all baselines on the novel scTF metric (topology fitness to the curve condition), achieving 0.767–0.800 versus 0.179–0.693 for the next-best method (RFDiffusion, which is only conditioned on sequence length and has no topology signal). On fit designability (FD), ProtPainter achieves 0.654–0.792 while RFDiffusion drops to 0.050 on 1a0b_cluster and 0.000 on GPCR, and all conditional baselines (Chroma with SSE or PointCloud, TopoDiff) score ≤0.160, indicating they cannot simultaneously satisfy topology and designability constraints. RFDiffusion retains a slight edge on confident designability (CD) for shorter proteins (0.980 vs. 0.832 on HHH_ems) and on scTM for HHH_ems (0.910 vs. 0.718), reflecting that unconditional generation can produce structures closer to the original fold when no topology constraint is imposed. Chroma's PointCloud conditions (r=0–4 Å) provide only vague spatial guidance and yield scTF values of 0.290–0.414, far below ProtPainter's curve-based control. TopoDiff, the most relevant topology-conditioned baseline, maps sketches into a VAE latent space but achieves scTF of only 0.179–0.350, suggesting its latent representation cannot faithfully encode the detailed 3D topology specified by curves.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtPainter builds directly on RFDiffusion's DDPM backbone generation framework and its RoseTTAFold-based translational guidance, while adapting the ILVR conditioning mechanism from image super-resolution to align generated backbones to a reference sketch. It departs from prior topology-conditioned methods (TopoDiff, DiffTopo, blueprint-based approaches) that rely on linear SSE sequences or latent-space topology by introducing 3D curves as a more flexible and spatially detailed topology representation. The CurveEncoder, which predicts secondary structure annotations from curves, combines EGNN-based geometric feature extraction with curvature CNNs, drawing on equivariant graph neural network literature. Conceptually, the paper reframes topology-conditioned generation as a super-resolution problem where coarse curves are refined into full backbones, a framing absent from prior protein design work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a new modality for protein design where users can intuitively draw or drag 3D curves to specify topology, enabling flexible navigation of backbone space that parametric topology configurations cannot support. The introduction of scTF and the Protein Restoration Task provides the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating topology-conditioned generation, filling a gap in the field. However, limitations remain: beta-sheet control is only briefly demonstrated, inference takes 10 seconds to 2 minutes per backbone, evaluation is on small clusters of 50 proteins, and the method has not been validated on experimentally designed proteins. Open questions include whether curve-based conditioning scales to larger multi-domain proteins, how well it handles complex beta-sheet topologies, and whether the approach can be integrated into real-time design workflows.
