## Problem

Pretrained masked language models encode conceptual knowledge with hierarchical (lattice) structure, but the mechanism by which this conceptualization emerges from the MLM pretraining objective remains unexplained. Prior work predominantly maps discovered concepts onto human-defined ontologies (e.g., WordNet), illuminating *what* concepts are learned but not *how* they arise, and overlooking latent concepts that have no human-defined name.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained MLM (no fine-tuning), a small set of hand-crafted cloze patterns (each containing two [MASK] slots for an object and an attribute), and domain-specific sets of objects and attributes drawn from the model vocabulary. It produces a probabilistic triadic formal context (a |G|×|M|×|B| tensor of conditional probabilities), which after min-max binarization yields a binary incidence matrix and, via FCA closure, a full concept lattice with extents and intents. The conditional probability rows also serve as dimensionally interpretable conceptual embeddings where each column corresponds to a specific attribute.

## Method

The key insight is that the MLM's masked-token objective implicitly learns a formal context: the conditional probability p_θ(g | b^{·,m}) or p_θ(m | b^{g,·}) under a concept pattern b encodes the object–attribute incidence relation. The paper constructs a probabilistic triadic formal context by batch-evaluating the MLM on partial pattern fillings, reducing the naive O(|G||M||B|) cost to near-linear |B|⌈|G|/β⌉ forward passes. After aggregating across patterns (average or max pooling) and binarizing via min-max normalization with a threshold α, standard FCA closure operators recover all formal concepts and their partial-order lattice. Theoretically, Lemma 1 proves that the formal context is identifiable from i.i.d. pattern fillings in the population limit, and Lemma 2 bounds the gap between the observed conditional MI and the true object–attribute MI by 2H(Z|b), showing that pattern quality (low confounding entropy) is the critical factor for accurate lattice recovery.

## Results

On formal context reconstruction (Table 2), BertLattice achieves MRR of 0.823–0.842 and Hit@1 of 0.721–0.751 on Region-language, compared to 0.094–0.340 MRR for the BertEmb. baseline. On Animal-behavior, BertLattice reaches MRR ≈ 0.19–0.21 and Hit@10 ≈ 0.45–0.54, while BertEmb. yields trivial results (≤ 0.1). For downstream concept classification (Table 3), BertLattice (max.) attains F1 of 0.703–0.709 and mAP of 0.667–0.701 on Region-language, versus 0.207–0.231 F1 for BertEmb.; on Disease-symptom, BioBERT-based BertLattice reaches F1 of 0.601–0.611 versus 0.345 for BioBERT BertEmb.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is BertEmb., a naive method that predicts object–attribute relations by measuring cosine distance between last-layer hidden-state embeddings of objects and attributes. BertLattice (the proposed FCA-based method) dominates BertEmb. across every model size and dataset: on Region-language, BertLattice (max.) with BERT-base achieves MRR 0.842 vs. 0.340 for BertEmb. (a 2.5× improvement), and with BERT-distill the gap widens to 0.831 vs. 0.094 (nearly 9×). On Animal-behavior, BertEmb. produces only trivial scores (≤ 0.1) while BertLattice reaches MRR ≈ 0.20 and Hit@10 up to 0.537. For concept classification, BertLattice (max.) with BERT-large attains F1 0.709 / mAP 0.701 on Region-language versus 0.231 / 0.028 for BertEmb., and on Disease-symptom BioBERT BertLattice (max.) reaches F1 0.611 versus 0.345 for BioBERT BertEmb. The paper does not compare against other FCA or ontology-learning baselines; the comparison is strictly between the conditional-probability-based lattice construction and the embedding-distance baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Formal Concept Analysis (FCA) and its triadic extension (Jaschke et al., 2006) to provide a mathematical formalization of how masked language models implicitly learn concept lattices. It departs from prior work on conceptual knowledge in MLMs (Wu et al., 2023; Peng et al., 2022; Dalvi et al., 2022), which typically probes models for human-defined concepts and maps them to ontologies like WordNet, by instead reconstructing concept lattices directly from conditional probability distributions without any reliance on predefined concepts. The key conceptual move is reframing the MLM's masked-token objective as a formal context learning problem, connecting the distributional hypothesis of word similarity to FCA's object-attribute incidence structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, ontology-free explanation for how conceptualization emerges from MLM pretraining, and demonstrates that the resulting lattices can contain 'latent' concepts (e.g., 'German and French speaking regions') absent from human-defined taxonomies. However, the evaluation is limited to three small single-relational datasets (122–354 objects), and the framework does not yet handle multi-relational data or autoregressive models, which are the dominant architecture today. Open questions include whether the lattice structure persists at scale, how it interacts with the linear representation hypothesis in hidden activations, and whether the discovered latent concepts are genuinely novel or merely rephrasings of known relations.
