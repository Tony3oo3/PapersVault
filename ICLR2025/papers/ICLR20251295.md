## Problem

Continuous sparsification methods like spred, which parameterize weights as m⊙w with weight decay, are mathematically equivalent to LASSO in their optimization objective yet consistently outperform it in practice. The paper identifies the missing explanation: the training dynamics of m⊙w induce an implicit regularization (a mirror flow) that differs fundamentally from explicit L1 penalty, but the mechanism and how to control it were previously uncharacterized. This gap limits the ability to tune sparsity-accuracy tradeoffs, particularly in the high-sparsity regime.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224) and produces class labels, but during training each scalar weight is replaced by a pair (m_i, w_i) whose product m_i⊙w_i is the effective weight, doubling the parameter count. At inference the model reverts to the single sparse vector x = m⊙w. The theoretical analysis also applies to numerical data via diagonal linear networks (n=100 features, d=40 samples) where the output is a continuous regression target.

## Method

PILoT extends the mirror flow framework by showing that the m⊙w parameterization with time-dependent weight decay α_t induces a time-dependent Bregman potential R_{a_t}(x) whose implicit bias transitions from L2 to L1 as a_t = (m₀²−w₀²)exp(−2∫α_s ds) shrinks. Two key design choices differentiate it from spred: (1) an initialization with m₀²−w₀²=β>0 (rather than β=0) that enables sign flips in the gradient flow, and (2) a dynamic regularization schedule that multiplicatively increases α_k when training accuracy improves and the sparsity threshold K is unmet, then decays α after half the training epochs to guarantee convergence. The resulting gradient flow dx_t = −√(x_t²+a_t²)⊙(∇f(x_t)+2α_t·x_t/√(x_t²+a_t²))dt actively scales updates by weight magnitude, sparsifying redundant parameters exponentially faster than LASSO's linear decay. The paper proves convergence to a minimizer under quasi-convexity or the PL-inequality, and optimality (KKT conditions) for underdetermined linear regression.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-50, PILoT achieves 72.67% top-1 accuracy at 94% sparsity, outperforming STR (70.4%) and spred (69.47%) by 2.3 and 3.2 percentage points respectively. At 97% sparsity, PILoT reaches 68.49% versus 67.22% (STR) and 66.12% (spred). On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with ResNet-20/18, PILoT consistently outperforms STR, spred, and LASSO across all sparsity levels, with the largest gains in the high-sparsity regime (>80%). When combined with iterative pruning (WR and LRR) on ImageNet ResNet-18, the m⊙w parameterization further boosts accuracy at all sparsity levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PILoT outperforms spred (Ziyin & Wang, 2023) at every sparsity level on ImageNet, with the gap widening from +0.12 pp at 80% sparsity to +3.2 pp at 94% and +2.4 pp at 97%, confirming that the dynamic L2→L1 transition and sign-flip-capable initialization are critical in the high-sparsity regime. Against STR (Kusupati et al., 2020), PILoT matches performance at ~88% sparsity (74.73% vs 74.73%) but clearly surpasses it at 94% (72.67% vs 70.40%) and 97% (68.49% vs 67.22%), while STR retains a slight edge at 80% (76.19% vs 75.62%). On CIFAR-10/100, PILoT dominates all three baselines (STR, spred, LASSO) across the full sparsity range, with the advantage most pronounced above 80% sparsity. When combined with iterative pruning, PILoT's m⊙w parameterization makes Weight Rewinding (Frankle et al., 2019) competitive with Learning Rate Rewinding (Maene et al., 2021) on ImageNet ResNet-18, and both methods show consistent accuracy gains over their unparameterized baselines at all sparsity levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mirror flow framework of Li et al. (2022) by introducing time-dependent Bregman potentials, showing that the m⊙w reparameterization with weight decay (as in spred, Ziyin & Wang 2023) induces a mirror flow whose implicit bias transitions from L2 to L1 over training. It departs from the spred/LASSO equivalence result by arguing that the objective-level equivalence does not explain spred's empirical superiority; instead, the training dynamics (implicit regularization) are the key differentiator. The method also builds on the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis philosophy and continuous sparsification methods like STR, while the dynamic regularization schedule and sign-flip-capable initialization represent a deliberate departure from spred's balanced initialization and constant weight decay.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for why continuous sparsification with m⊙w outperforms explicit L1 regularization (LASSO), attributing the gap to a controllable implicit bias rather than the optimization objective. The time-dependent Bregman potential framework is of potential independent interest, offering a mechanism to steer implicit regularization from L2 to L1 at a desired speed, which is crucial for entering the rich regime. Limitations include the requirement that α_t → 0 for convergence (ruling out constant regularization), the potentially large constant in the convergence rate, and the fact that guarantees hold primarily for quasi-convex or PL-satisfying losses. Open questions include whether the framework extends to structured sparsity (the paper shows neuron-wise masks do not induce a mirror flow) and whether the dynamic schedule generalizes to non-convex deep network training beyond the diagonal linear network setting.
