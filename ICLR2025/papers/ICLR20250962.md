## Problem

While monocular video generation models have achieved impressive results, generating 3D stereoscopic videos remains under-explored due to the scarcity of stereo training data. Existing novel view synthesis approaches rely on camera pose estimation, which is unstable for generated videos with dynamic content, and lack the generative ability to hallucinate occluded regions. The paper addresses this by proposing a pose-free, training-free framework that leverages an off-the-shelf video diffusion model to produce semantically consistent and temporally smooth stereoscopic videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250962/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt or single image as conditioning and produces a pair of 16-frame monocular video sequences (left and right eye views) forming a 3D stereoscopic video. Internally, it constructs a frame matrix with 8 intermediate camera views evenly distributed along a 0.08 baseline (depth normalized to 1–10 m), and the final output is extracted from the leftmost and rightmost columns. The pipeline runs on a single A6000 GPU in approximately 8 minutes using ~10 GB of RAM, and has been validated on videos generated by Sora, Lumiere, WALT, and Zeroscope.

## Method

The core contribution is a frame matrix representation where rows correspond to camera views at a fixed timestamp and columns correspond to temporal sequences at a fixed view; the video diffusion model is applied to jointly denoise along both the spatial (row) and temporal (column) directions via alternating resampling, ensuring semantic consistency across views and temporal smoothness across frames. A second key mechanism is disocclusion boundary re-injection: at each denoising step, the predicted clean latent is decoded, its unoccluded regions are replaced with warped pixels, and the composite is re-encoded to update the known latent, preventing VAE downsampling from propagating dark disocclusion artifacts into the boundary. The warping stage uses Depth Anything for monocular depth estimation with optical-flow-based temporal smoothing and multi-plane projection to remove isolated pixels and cracks before inpainting. The entire pipeline is training-free and pose-free, requiring only an off-the-shelf video diffusion model (e.g., Zeroscope) and a depth estimator.

## Results

On the primary quantitative evaluation (Table 2), SVG achieves the best scores across all four metrics: CLIP semantic consistency of 96.44, aesthetic score of 5.27, DOVER quality of 0.584, and FVD of 599. In the human perception study (Table 1, 7-point Likert scale, 20 participants, VR headset), SVG scores 5.24 on stereo effect, 5.15 on temporal consistency, 5.12 on image quality, and 5.35 on overall experience, outperforming all baselines with statistically significant differences (Kruskal-Wallis χ² > 13.3, p < 0.001). The method is evaluated on videos generated by Sora, Lumiere, WALT, and Zeroscope, with 16-frame sequences processed in ~8 minutes on an A6000 GPU using ~10 GB RAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVG outperforms all four primary baselines on every metric. Against video inpainting methods, E2FGVI (CLIP 94.34, FVD 638) and ProPainter (CLIP 95.29, FVD 606) produce blurry disoccluded content and lower semantic consistency; SVG leads by 2.1 and 1.15 CLIP points respectively. Against novel view synthesis methods, RoDynRF (CLIP 96.03, DOVER 0.352) and DynIBaR (CLIP 93.24, FVD 1208) suffer from unstable camera pose estimation and lack generative ability for unseen regions; SVG achieves substantially higher DOVER (0.584 vs. 0.352/0.365) and lower FVD (599 vs. 727/1208). Against Deep3D (ECCV 2016), SVG wins decisively on stereo effect (5.29 vs. 2.29, p < 0.001) and overall experience (5.08 vs. 3.68, p < 0.001) but falls slightly short on temporal consistency (5.06 vs. 5.37, p = 0.015). Against single-image view synthesis methods SVM (DOVER 0.215, FVD 784) and AdaMPI (DOVER 0.245, FVD 718), SVG's use of temporal context yields dramatically better quality (DOVER 0.584, FVD 599) and temporal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D-to-3D warping-and-inpainting paradigm from Text2Room (Höllein et al., 2023) into the video domain, replacing the image inpainter with a video diffusion model and introducing a frame matrix representation that jointly denoises along both spatial (camera motion) and temporal (scene motion) directions. The inpainting mechanism builds on RePaint's resampling strategy (Lugmayr et al., 2022) and modifies Blended Latent Diffusion's (Avrahami et al., 2023) single-encode scheme with iterative disocclusion boundary re-injection to combat latent-space feature corruption. Unlike NeRF-based novel view synthesis methods (RoDynRF, DynIBaR) that require accurate camera poses, this approach is explicitly pose-free and training-free, relying solely on monocular depth estimation and the generative prior of an off-the-shelf video diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges a practical gap between the rapid progress in monocular video generation and the still-limited ability to produce immersive stereoscopic 3D content for VR/AR, doing so without requiring stereo training data or camera pose estimation. The frame matrix formulation is a generalizable regularization strategy that could be adopted by future multi-view video generation systems beyond stereoscopy. Key limitations include the restriction to short clips (16 frames due to Zeroscope's architecture), dependence on depth estimation quality for thin structures, and the computational overhead of auxiliary views; open questions include scaling to longer videos via stronger backbones like Sora and improving efficiency of the multi-directional denoising.
