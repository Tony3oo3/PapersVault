## Problem

Despite Mamba's strong empirical performance across diverse domains, a theoretical understanding of how tokens evolve through the depth of a deep selective state space model remains absent. Specifically, it is unknown whether tokens in a pretrained Mamba converge, diverge, or exhibit mixed behavior as the model deepens, and how these dynamical properties affect downstream model performance.

## Input / Output

The method operates on sequences of D-dimensional tokens (text tokens of length L for language modeling, or 2D image patch tokens for vision) and produces same-length output sequences through the S6 layer. The key internal quantity analyzed is the hidden attention tensor P, whose entries are determined by the input-output matrix S_C^T S_B, the step-size function Δ, and the decay parameter a. In the practical reordering refinement, tokens are permuted by a learnable importance score s_l = ⟨K, S_Δ x_{l0}⟩ before entering the S6 layer, with SoftSort providing a differentiable relaxation of the sorting operation.

## Method

The paper derives the continuous-time limit of Mamba's S6 layer by treating the layer index as a time variable, yielding a coupled ODE system for the token sequence. In the one-dimensional case, it proves a dichotomy: if the scalar input-output parameter μ = S_C^T S_B < 0, all tokens converge to zero at rate O(1/√t) (convergence scenario); if μ > 0, all tokens diverge to infinity, either slowly at a logarithmic rate O((ln t)^l) when S_Δ x_{l0} < 0 for all l, or by finite-time blowup when S_Δ x_{l0} > 0 for some l. Crucially, in the slow-divergence regime, tokens at different positions diverge at different rates, meaning they contribute unequally to gradient updates during training. Based on these results, the paper proposes two refinements: (i) constraining S_C^T S_B to have only positive eigenvalues via LDL^T parameterization to exclude the harmful convergence scenario, and (ii) reordering tokens in ascending order of their importance scores before the S6 layer using SoftSort for gradient propagation. Experiments on WikiText-103 (language modeling) and ImageNet-1K (image classification with MambaVision) confirm that excluding negative eigenvalues reduces perplexity and that token reordering improves top-1 accuracy and accelerates convergence.

## Results

On WikiText-103 language modeling, the token reordering technique reduces perplexity from 16.46 to 14.97 for Mamba (1.49 PPL improvement), from 21.61 to 20.72 for H3, and from 16.98 to 13.44 for a converted Transformer. On ImageNet-1K image classification, reordering improves MambaVision-T from 81.90% to 82.02% top-1 accuracy and from 95.86% to 95.87% top-5 accuracy, while also accelerating convergence. The eigenvalue configuration study on WikiText-103 shows that constraining S_C^T S_B to have only positive eigenvalues yields the best perplexity (16.66) compared to mixed (16.82) or negative (17.28) eigenvalues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's token reordering method consistently outperforms all four baselines. Against Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2024), reordering reduces WikiText-103 perplexity from 16.46 to 14.97 (1.49 PPL gain) and improves ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy from 81.90% to 82.02%. Against H3 (Fu et al., 2023), the improvement is smaller at 0.89 PPL (21.61 → 20.72), likely because H3 lacks the S_Δ step-size mechanism and uses a simpler importance score. Against the Transformer baseline (Vaswani et al., 2017, converted to Mamba format), reordering yields the largest gain of 3.54 PPL (16.98 → 13.44), suggesting the reordering is particularly beneficial for architectures where token ordering is less naturally optimized. Against MambaVision-T (Hatamizadeh & Kautz, 2024) on ImageNet-1K, the improvement is modest (0.12% top-1, 0.01% top-5) but the method achieves faster training convergence. The eigenvalue exclusion refinement (removing negative eigenvalues from S_C^T S_B) also consistently improves over the unconstrained baseline, with positive-only eigenvalues achieving 16.66 PPL versus 17.28 for negative-only.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time limit methodology (originally developed for ResNets and neural ODEs, and recently applied to Transformers by Geshkovski et al.) to the selective state space model family, specifically Mamba's S6 layer. While prior theoretical work on Mamba has focused on expressivity (Ali et al., 2024; Muca Cirone et al., 2025; Merrill et al., 2024) or copying capabilities (Jelassi et al., 2024), this is the first to derive and characterize the asymptotic dynamical behavior of tokens in a deep selective SSM. The proposed token reordering technique repurposes SoftSort (Prillo & Eisenschlos, 2020) as a differentiable permutation mechanism, and the eigenvalue-based initialization constraint draws on LDL^T decomposition techniques from matrix analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of when tokens in deep Mamba architectures collapse versus diverge, offering a concrete diagnostic (sign of S_C^T S_B eigenvalues) that practitioners can check during training. However, the theoretical results are restricted to the single-channel (D=1) case, and the authors acknowledge that LayerNorm in the full Mamba architecture breaks the predicted dynamics, limiting direct applicability. The practical improvements are modest (0.12% Top-1 on ImageNet, ~1.5 PPL on WikiText-103), and the higher-dimensional conjecture remains unproven. Open questions include whether the reordering strategy generalizes beyond the simple linear projection used here, and how these dynamics interact with the full Mamba block (convolutions, layer norm, gating).
