## Problem

Composing multiple independently trained LoRAs for multi-concept image generation suffers from 'semantic conflicts' that degrade image quality as the number of LoRAs increases. Existing training-free fusion methods (LoRA Merge, LoRA Switch, LoRA Composite, LoraHub) apply LoRAs with equal contribution at each denoising step, ignoring that different LoRAs operate on fundamentally different frequency spectra. This paper identifies and exploits this frequency-domain disparity to resolve conflicts without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and N pre-trained LoRA modules (e.g., character, clothing, style, background, object) as input, applied at scale 1.4 within the cross-attention layers of a Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net. It produces a single 512×512 multi-concept image via DPM-Solver++ sampling (200 steps for anime, 50 for realistic). The framework is entirely training-free: LoRAs are plugged in at inference time, and the only tunable hyperparameters are the dominant LoRA weight scale w_dom (initialized at N−0.5 with geometric decay) and two caching interval integers c₁=2, c₂=3.

## Method

CMLoRA introduces a Fourier-domain profiling step that measures the change in high-frequency component amplitude (ΔH₀.₂) of each LoRA's contribution across denoising steps, partitioning LoRAs into a high-frequency dominant set H (e.g., Style, Character) and a low-frequency dominant set L (e.g., Background). During inference, a dominant LoRA from H performs full U-Net inference in early steps while transitioning to a dominant LoRA from L in later steps, with its weight decaying as w_dom^i = w_dom^{i−1} − 0.5^i. Non-dominant LoRAs are served via a DeepCache-inspired mechanism that reuses cached U-Net upsampling-block feature maps, with a non-uniform cache interval (c₁ in early/late phases, c₂ in the middle 40–90% phase) chosen to match the empirically observed similarity trend of fused feature maps. This dominant/non-dominant split with frequency-aware scheduling reduces semantic conflicts while cutting MACs by roughly 30–40% compared to full multi-LoRA inference.

## Results

On the ComposLoRA testbed, CMLoRA (Cache D) achieves the highest CLIPScore across all LoRA counts (N=2 to 5), reaching 34.341 at N=5 compared to 33.659 for LoraHub and 33.475 for LoRA Switch. In the MiniCPM-V evaluation, CMLoRA scores 8.393 average across four dimensions (Element Integration, Spatial Consistency, Semantic Accuracy, Aesthetic Quality), substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: Switch at 7.110). The paper reports an average 2.19% CLIPScore improvement and 11.25% MLLM win-rate improvement over LoraHub, LoRA Composite, and LoRA Switch combined.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMLoRA outperforms all four baselines on CLIPScore at N=5: 34.341 vs. Naive (33.809), Merge (32.364), Switch (33.475), Composite (32.582), and LoraHub (33.659). The gap widens with more LoRAs — at N=2, CMLoRA (35.422) is competitive with LoraHub (35.681) and Switch (35.394), but by N=5 it leads by 0.68 points over LoraHub and 0.87 over Switch. In the MiniCPM-V evaluation, CMLoRA's advantage is most pronounced on Semantic Accuracy (7.993 vs. 6.543 for LoraHub, 6.533 for Switch, 6.505 for Composite) and Aesthetic Quality (8.675 vs. 7.740 for LoraHub), while the Naive model scores lowest on Semantic Accuracy (5.127), confirming that multi-LoRA composition is essential. The caching mechanism (Cache D) consistently improves over uniform caching (Cache c=2,3,5) across all base methods, with LoRA Composite benefiting most from caching due to its lack of frequency-domain partitioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LoRA Switch and LoRA Composite frameworks from Zhong et al. (2024), which established training-free multi-LoRA composition by either activating one LoRA per timestep or aggregating all LoRA scores at each step. It departs from these by introducing a Fourier frequency-domain analysis to characterize how different LoRA categories (Character, Style, Background, etc.) contribute distinct frequency components during denoising, and by adapting the DeepCache (Ma et al., 2024) caching mechanism to selectively cache non-dominant LoRA feature maps. The key conceptual departure is treating multi-LoRA composition as a frequency-scheduling problem rather than a weight-merging or simple switching problem, which motivates the dominant/non-dominant LoRA hierarchy with decaying weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-concept image generation (combining character, clothing, style, background, and object LoRAs) is a practical bottleneck for users of diffusion models, and this paper provides a principled explanation for why naive composition degrades with more LoRAs—frequency-domain semantic conflicts. The MLLM-based evaluation pipeline (MiniCPM-V) addresses a real gap in the field where CLIPScore and ImageReward struggle with out-of-distribution concepts. However, limitations remain: the method is evaluated only on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with 22 LoRAs, cannot handle spatial localization of same-category concepts, and the CLIPScore gains (~2%) are modest even though the MLLM win-rate gains (~11%) are more substantial. Open questions include generalization to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), scaling beyond 5 LoRAs, and whether the frequency partitioning holds for LoRAs trained on overlapping semantic categories.
