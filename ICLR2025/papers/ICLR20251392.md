## Problem

Camera-only 4D occupancy forecasting is essential for autonomous driving safety, but the state-of-the-art method Cam4DOcc incurs prohibitive computational costs (6434 GFLOPs, 57 GB GPU memory) that preclude real-time deployment on edge agents with restricted budgets. The paper addresses this efficiency gap while simultaneously improving forecasting accuracy over existing camera-only baselines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251392/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surround-view RGB images at 448×800 resolution spanning 3 temporal frames (2 past + 1 present) and produces 4 future frames of 3D occupancy at 0.2 m voxel resolution over a (512, 512, 40) grid covering ±51.2 m in x/y and −5 to 3 m in z. 2D features from a shared ResNet-FPN encoder are lifted to 3D via depth estimation and voxel pooling, then aligned to the current-frame coordinate system using 6-DoF ego-vehicle poses before entering the forecasting pipeline. The output can be either future occupancy or occupancy flow, decoded by a shared Predictor head.

## Method

OccProphet replaces the heavy 3D convolutional encoder-decoder of Cam4DOcc with a three-stage Observer–Forecaster–Refiner pipeline. The Observer applies an Efficient 4D Aggregation (E4A) module that progressively downsamples the 4D motion-aware feature, performs spatio-temporal interaction via a Tripling-Attention Fusion (TAF) operation that decomposes each 3D feature into scene (1D), height (1D), and BEV (2D) branches with window-based multi-head self-attention, then upsamples with skip connections to recover resolution. The Forecaster extracts a global environmental condition via 3D global average pooling and uses it to generate adaptive linear weights, enabling the network to modulate its prediction strategy based on traffic complexity rather than relying on a single static projection. The Refiner reuses the E4A architecture on the concatenation of historical observations and forecasted future frames to introduce cross-frame interactions that the linear Forecaster lacks. This design reduces parameters by 78%, FLOPs by 69%, and GPU memory by 58% relative to Cam4DOcc while achieving 4–18% relative IoU gains across nuScenes, Lyft-Level5, and nuScenes-Occupancy.

## Results

On the primary task of forecasting inflated GMO, OccProphet achieves IoU_f of 29.15% on nuScenes and 40.11% on Lyft-Level5, surpassing the strongest baseline Cam4DOcc (OCFNet) by 4.18% and 15.92% respectively. On the more challenging fine-grained GMO task (nuScenes-Occupancy), OccProphet reaches IoU_f of 11.98%, a 18.61% relative improvement over Cam4DOcc (10.10%). In terms of efficiency, OccProphet reduces parameters by 78% (82M vs 370M), GPU memory by 58% (24G vs 57G), and FLOPs by 69% (1985G vs 6434G), while achieving 2.6× higher FPS (4.5 vs 1.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OccProphet outperforms all seven baselines across every reported metric and dataset. Against the strongest competitor Cam4DOcc (OCFNet), OccProphet gains +3.06 IoU_c and +1.17 IoU_f on nuScenes, and a much larger +6.97 IoU_c and +5.51 IoU_f on Lyft-Level5, while simultaneously running 2.6× faster with 69% fewer FLOPs. The BEV-based baselines PowerBEV-3D and BEVDet4D trail OccProphet by 6–12 IoU_f points on nuScenes, confirming the advantage of full 3D voxel representation over 2D BEV. The point-cloud prediction pipeline SPC (SurroundDepth + PCPNet + Cylinder3D) performs worst overall (IoU_f ≈ 1–2%), likely due to its lack of shape consistency and reliance on sparse supervision. OpenOccupancy-C, a semantic occupancy prediction method repurposed for forecasting, lags far behind all forecasting-specific methods (IoU_f ≈ 11–14%), highlighting the difficulty of temporal extrapolation. On the fine-grained tasks (Tables 3 and 4), OccProphet's margin over Cam4DOcc widens to 10–19% relative IoU_f, suggesting the Observer-Forecaster-Refiner pipeline is particularly effective at capturing intricate 3D object geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as an efficiency-focused successor to Cam4DOcc, the first camera-only 4D occupancy forecasting benchmark and baseline. Rather than introducing a new task formulation, it repurposes existing architectural ideas—UNet-style downsampling/upsampling, window-based multi-head self-attention (from Swin Transformer), and residual connections—into a novel Observer-Forecaster-Refiner pipeline that explicitly targets reduced computational cost. The tripling-attention fusion (decomposing 3D features into scene, height, and BEV branches) and the conditional forecasting mechanism are new design choices, but each component draws on well-established primitives. The paper also contrasts its 3D voxel-based approach with BEV-based forecasting methods (FIERY, BEVerse, TBP-Former) and LiDAR-based occupancy forecasting (UnO, point cloud forecasting), arguing that camera-only 3D occupancy forecasting is the most practical and generalizable formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 58–78% reduction in parameters, memory, and FLOPs combined with a 2.6× speedup and 4–18% accuracy gain over Cam4DOcc makes camera-only 4D occupancy forecasting substantially more viable for on-vehicle edge deployment, which was the stated motivation. The tripling-attention decomposition and E4A module offer reusable efficiency techniques for other 4D spatio-temporal tasks. However, the paper acknowledges that fine-grained forecasting in dense scenarios remains challenging for both OccProphet and Cam4DOcc, and the evaluation is limited to three datasets with a single primary baseline (Cam4DOcc), leaving open whether the efficiency-accuracy tradeoff generalizes to longer forecasting horizons, more diverse driving conditions, or different backbone architectures.
