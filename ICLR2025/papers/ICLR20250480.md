## Problem

While adversarial robustness and label-noise robustness are well-studied, the vulnerability of classifiers to the same image appearing with different semantics at different resolutions remains largely unexplored. In practice, classifiers routinely process compressed or low-resolution inputs (social media, IoT, surveillance), yet no systematic attack or defense framework addresses this resolution-dependent fragility. This paper introduces the Resolution Attack, a novel paradigm that crafts natural-looking images whose high-resolution and downsampled versions carry entirely different semantics, deceiving both classifiers and human observers.

## Input / Output

The method takes two text prompts (one specifying the high-resolution class C_H and one the low-resolution class C_L) and, for the RAS variant, an additional source image I_s that constrains the structural layout of the output. The output is a 512×512 RGB image that is visually natural at full resolution (classified as C_H) but, when downsampled by a factor of 3 to 64×64, is both visually recognizable and classifier-confirmed as C_L. Unlike traditional adversarial examples, the generated image contains no pixel-level perturbation; the dual semantics are embedded through the image's intrinsic frequency structure, and the transition between resolutions is smooth and coherent.

## Method

The framework is built on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and operates entirely zero-shot. Its core component, the Dual-Stream Generative Denoising Module, runs the U-Net twice per denoising step with separate prompts P_H and P_L, then composes the predicted noises in the frequency domain: the low-frequency component is taken from ε_L (governing coarse structure visible at low resolution) and the high-frequency component from ε_H (governing fine details at full resolution). A Time-Dependent Denoising Strategy further refines this by using only P_L in the first 20 steps (coarse structure phase), both prompts in the middle 260 steps (dual-semantic blending), and only P_H in the last 20 steps (fine-detail phase), aligning with the known coarse-to-fine generation behavior of diffusion models. For the RAS variant, DDIM inversion maps the source image into the initial noise to fix layout, and a Structural Guidance Module (adapting ControlNet-style conditioning) injects depth maps and softedge images from the source to enforce structural alignment, with the choice of which control signal to apply depending on the semantic gap between C_H and C_L.

## Results

The Resolution Attack achieves a Corrective Attack Success Rate (ASRC) of 71.4% on ResNet-50, 68.2% on EfficientNet, 61.6% on VGG19, 60.5% on DenseNet, and 43.6% on InceptionV3 in the labeled setting, demonstrating that all tested classifiers are vulnerable. The RAS variant (with source image) further improves ASRC to 77.1% on ResNet-50 and 75.3% on DenseNet, while maintaining structural fidelity to the source image (SSIM up to 0.727). In the unlabeled setting, low-resolution accuracy (AccL) drops but remains substantial (e.g., 69.3% on DenseNet, 63.2% on ResNet-50), confirming the attack generalizes beyond ImageNet label space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an attack paper without direct comparisons to prior attack methods; instead, it evaluates the vulnerability of five widely-used ImageNet classifiers (ResNet-50, VGG19, InceptionV3, EfficientNet, DenseNet) to the proposed resolution attack. All classifiers are successfully attacked, with ASRC ranging from 43.6% (InceptionV3) to 71.4% (ResNet-50) in the labeled setting. EfficientNet, despite having the highest clean high-resolution accuracy (89.6%), is still attacked at 68.2% ASRC, indicating that stronger classifiers do not confer resolution robustness. InceptionV3 is the most resistant (43.6% ASRC), likely due to its multi-scale architecture, yet remains vulnerable. The RAS variant with source image boosts ASRC by 5–13 percentage points over RA for most classifiers (e.g., ResNet-50: 71.4% → 77.1%), showing that structural guidance from a source image makes the attack more reliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of diffusion-model-based image generation and model robustness, departing from the adversarial-attack literature (which relies on model-specific, pixel-level perturbations) and label-noise robustness. It repurposes Stable Diffusion's U-Net as a zero-shot generator, DDIM inversion to anchor source-image structure, and ControlNet-style conditioning (depth/softedge maps) as a Structural Guidance Module. The core methodological novelty is the Dual-Stream Generative Denoising Module, which decomposes the denoising noise into low-frequency (driven by the low-res prompt) and high-frequency (driven by the high-res prompt) components, combined with a time-dependent strategy that assigns different semantic prompts to early, middle, and late denoising stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a previously unexamined vulnerability: widely deployed classifiers (ResNet, VGG, Inception, EfficientNet, DenseNet, ViT, CLIP, BLIP-2, LLaVA) are not robust to the same image at different resolutions, and a zero-shot diffusion model can exploit this without any target-model access. The face-swapping and facial-camouflage demonstrations underscore real-world security implications for biometric systems. Open questions include how to build defenses (the paper only suggests fine-tuning classifiers on generated images), whether the attack transfers to higher compression ratios or different downscaling kernels, and whether the dual-semantic property degrades as diffusion models improve in fidelity. The moderate attack success rates (43–78% depending on classifier and setting) suggest the vulnerability is real but not yet catastrophic, leaving room for both stronger attacks and effective mitigations.
