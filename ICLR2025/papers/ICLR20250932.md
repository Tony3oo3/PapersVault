## Problem

The long-time asymptotic behavior of SVGD in the finite-particle regime (n < ∞ fixed, k → ∞) remains uncharacterized: existing convergence guarantees only hold for small k (e.g., k < log log n), and SVGD cannot converge to the continuous target π because its iterates are discrete measures with n atoms. This paper asks what SVGD actually converges to as k → ∞ for fixed n, and whether that limit approaches the target as n grows.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a target log-density F: ℝ^d → ℝ (so π ∝ exp(−F)), a positive-definite kernel K in an RKHS, n particles initialized from a distribution with bounded fourth moments, a deterministic step-size sequence (γ_k) satisfying γ_k → 0 and Σγ_k = ∞, and a regularization parameter λ ≥ 0. The output is a sequence of n-particle configurations whose empirical measure μ_k^n = (1/n)Σ δ_{X_k^{i,n}} approximates π; convergence is established in the doubly asymptotic regime (k,n) → (∞,∞) in the Hausdorff-Wasserstein sense, and the method also produces an averaged empirical measure for tighter concentration.

## Method

NSVGD augments each SVGD update with a Langevin regularization term (−λγ_{k+1}∇F + √(2λγ_{k+1})ξ), making the particle dynamics a noisy interacting system. The key theoretical insight is that the piecewise-linear interpolation of the particle trajectories converges, in the doubly asymptotic limit, to the law of a McKean-Vlasov SDE (propagation of chaos), and the limit set L^n consists of stationary measures of this SDE. Because λ > 0 introduces non-degenerate diffusion, the target π becomes the unique stationary distribution of the McKean-Vlasov process (unlike pure SVGD where Dirac masses at critical points of F are also stationary), so L^n → π in probability as n → ∞. The proof proceeds via a KL-divergence Lyapunov function along McKean-Vlasov trajectories, time-shift recurrence of the limiting measures, and the Birkhoff center to identify the unique recurrent set. Under an additional Log-Sobolev Inequality on π, the time-averaging can be removed, yielding convergence of the raw empirical measure itself.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: Theorem 1 establishes that the distributional limit set Lⁿ of NSVGD is well-defined and non-empty for fixed n; Theorem 2 shows that the averaged limit set L̄ⁿ converges in probability to the target π as n→∞; Theorem 3 extends this to the non-averaged empirical measure under an LSI assumption. Empirically, on the variance collapse experiment (sampling from a standard Gaussian), NSVGD maintains a Dimension-Averaged Marginal Variance (DAMV) near 1 (the correct value) as n increases, while SVGD's DAMV collapses toward 0 as the dimension d grows. On the Neal funnel distribution, NSVGD with small λ achieves MMD performance close to SVGD while retaining convergence guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares NSVGD against deterministic SVGD (λ=0) and the Langevin algorithm (K=0, λ=1). On the variance collapse experiment (standard Gaussian target, d/n>1 regime from Ba et al. 2021), SVGD exhibits severe variance collapse with DAMV tending to zero as dimension increases, while NSVGD with λ>0 avoids this collapse entirely and its DAMV converges to 1 (the correct value) as n grows. On the Neal funnel distribution (n=100, γₖ=0.1, d=2), the Langevin algorithm fails to recover the thin part of the distribution (x₁≤2), whereas SVGD successfully does so; NSVGD with small λ replicates SVGD's performance while maintaining theoretical convergence guarantees, and with large λ recovers Langevin-like behavior. The key practical takeaway is that setting λ sufficiently small retains SVGD's superior sampling quality (faster convergence, better mode coverage) while the Langevin regularization term ensures the limit set is unique and coincides with the target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SVGD (Liu & Wang, 2016) by adding a Langevin regularization term at each iteration, creating NSVGD. Prior convergence analyses of SVGD either assumed the population limit (n=∞) or restricted to small iteration counts (k < log log n) in the finite-particle regime, leaving the long-time behavior for fixed n entirely uncharacterized. The proof technique builds on the McKean-Vlasov framework and propagation of chaos results from Bianchi et al. (2024), showing that NSVGD trajectories converge to stationary measures of a nonlinear SDE. The key conceptual departure from pure SVGD is that the Langevin noise ensures the target π is the unique stationary distribution of the associated McKean-Vlasov process, whereas pure SVGD admits Dirac measures at stationary points of F as additional stationary states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to rigorously characterize what SVGD-type algorithms converge to in the practically relevant regime of fixed finite particles and infinite iterations, resolving a question the authors explicitly identify as open. The result that the limits lim_{n→∞} and lim_{k→∞} can be interchanged for NSVGD (but not for pure SVGD) provides a clean theoretical explanation for the empirically observed variance collapse and a minimal fix. Open questions include quantifying convergence rates to the limit set, determining the optimal regularization parameter λ for a given application, and extending the analysis to uniform-in-dimension bounds that would explain the d/n > 1 threshold observed empirically.
