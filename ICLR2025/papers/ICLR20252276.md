## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) are widely regarded as a principled framework that unifies diffusion theory with masked models and outperforms autoregressive models (ARMs) in text generation. This paper challenges both claims: it shows that MDMs' training and sampling are theoretically free from the time variable (the hallmark of diffusion), making them equivalent to simple masked models, and it identifies a hidden numerical bug in 32-bit Gumbel-based categorical sampling that artificially lowers effective temperature and reduces token diversity, rendering previous MDM-vs-ARM comparisons unfair.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully masked token sequence of length L (all positions set to [MASK]) over a discrete vocabulary of size m and produces a fully unmasked text sequence. The proposed First-Hitting Sampler (FHS) decodes exactly L tokens in random order, with each step analytically sampling the next unmasking time and performing a single categorical draw at one randomly chosen masked position. This contrasts with the original MDM sampler, which requires N ≫ L discrete time steps and up to L categorical samplings per step, with many steps producing no token change.

## Method

The paper makes three core contributions. First, it proves the MDM training ELBO can be reformulated as a partition by the number of masked tokens rather than continuous time, showing the time variable is merely a continuous relaxation of the masked ratio and the optimal network is time-independent. Second, it derives the First-Hitting Sampler (FHS), which analytically samples the next unmasking time via inverse transform sampling on order statistics of uniform variables (Proposition 4.1), then uniformly selects a masked position and draws the token from the network prediction—achieving exact theoretical equivalence to the continuous-time reverse Markov process with up to 20× wall-clock speedup. Third, it identifies that 32-bit floating-point precision truncates the Gumbel distribution in categorical sampling, and derives a closed-form expression (Proposition 5.2) showing this truncation shifts class probabilities to amplify high-probability tokens, effectively lowering temperature and reducing token diversity, which unfairly inflates the generative perplexity metric in favor of MDMs over ARMs.

## Results

After fixing the 32-bit Gumbel truncation issue (using 64-bit precision), the true generative perplexity of MDMs (SEDD Absorb, MDLM) is revealed to be approximately 100, significantly lagging behind the autoregressive baseline (<40) on OpenWebText. The First-Hitting Sampler (FHS) achieves up to 20× wall-clock speedup over the original MDM sampling while maintaining equivalent generation quality. The 32-bit truncation artificially lowers Gen PPL to ~31 (vs. ~126 at 64-bit) while reducing sentence entropy from 5.66 to 5.17, creating a misleading impression of superior MDM performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MDMs (SEDD Absorb and MDLM) against a same-architecture autoregressive model (ARM) trained on OpenWebText. Under the original 32-bit sampling, MDMs appear to surpass ARMs in generative perplexity at ~2k sampling steps, but this is entirely an artifact of the truncated Gumbel distribution lowering effective temperature and reducing token diversity. After correcting to 64-bit precision, MDMs' Gen PPL (~100) is substantially worse than ARMs (<40), and entropy returns to a comparable level (~5.60–5.70) across all models. The proposed FHS matches the generation quality of the original MDM sampler while being up to 20× faster in wall-clock time, and avoids the batch-size-dependent NFE degradation of the caching strategy. The temperature trade-off curve (Gen PPL vs. entropy) achievable with the corrected FHS is slightly better than the original MDM sampling but still significantly behind ARMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the recent MDM framework established by SEDD (Lou et al., 2023), MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024), and Shi et al. (2024), which aligned masked diffusion with continuous-time diffusion principles. It departs from this lineage by proving that the continuous-time formulation is theoretically redundant: the ELBO reduces to a discrete partition by masked-token count (recovering the likelihood-weighted objective of order-agnostic autoregressive models, Uria et al. 2014; Hoogeboom et al. 2021a), and the sampling process is equivalent to the token-by-token decoding of classical masked models (MaskGIT, MaskPredict). The First-Hitting Sampler is a new contribution derived from the continuous-time Markov chain structure, and the identification of the 32-bit Gumbel truncation issue is entirely novel, with no prior work having analyzed the numerical precision of categorical sampling in discrete diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing narrative that MDMs represent a genuinely superior paradigm over ARMs for text generation, showing that the reported advantage was an artifact of a floating-point truncation bug that artificially lowered effective temperature. The 20× sampling speedup and the theoretical equivalence to simpler masked models suggest the community may be over-investing in a more complex framework without practical benefit. However, the experiments are limited to GPT-2-scale models on OpenWebText, leaving open whether the conclusions hold at LLM scale, and whether MDMs retain advantages for order-agnostic data (e.g., images, molecules) where ARMs are less natural. The numerical precision finding is broadly applicable to any Gumbel-max-based categorical sampling in 32-bit, including some LLM inference pipelines.
