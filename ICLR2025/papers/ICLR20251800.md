## Problem

Existing video generation methods, predominantly diffusion-based, excel at short-duration content (seconds to a minute) but fail to model complex narratives, maintain character identity across scene changes, or support arbitrary generation lengths. Autoregressive models handle long sequences and complex reasoning well but are inefficient for visual rendering. No prior framework unifies both paradigms to produce movie-length videos with coherent multi-character storylines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251800/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a structured multimodal script per keyframe containing text descriptions (scene elements, plot, character appearances) compressed via sentence-level LongCLIP tokenization, face embeddings extracted by FARL (top 5 characters per frame), and structural identifiers. Optionally, 10 reference frames from the same episode are prepended as visual tokens for few-shot personalization. The output is a sequence of 896×512 keyframe images (2 compressed tokens per frame decoded by a diffusion decoder), which are then rendered into video clips via an image-to-video diffusion model, supporting generation of thousands of keyframes spanning tens of minutes to hours.

## Method

MovieDreamer is a three-stage hierarchical pipeline: (1) a diffusion autoencoder (CLIP ViT-L + 6-block transformer compressor) encodes each keyframe into just 2 continuous tokens, decoded by a fine-tuned SDXL U-Net; (2) a LLaMA-7B autoregressive model, initialized from pretrained weights, predicts these continuous tokens sequentially using a k-mixture GMM head (inspired by GIVT) trained with negative log-likelihood plus L1/L2 losses, conditioned on the multimodal script and historical tokens; (3) an ID-preserving diffusion decoder injects face and description embeddings into cross-attention to recover fine facial details lost in compression, and an SVD-based image-to-video model renders keyframes into clips using an anchor-frame strategy (reusing the CLIP feature of the original image rather than the last generated frame) to prevent error accumulation over long sequences. Anti-overfitting is addressed via 50% dropout, face embedding randomization across frames, token masking, and temporal augmentation, while few-shot in-context learning is enabled by stochastically prepending reference frame tokens during training.

## Results

On the story generation benchmark (100 unseen movies, 1M keyframes), MovieDreamer achieves a CLIP score of 19.584, FID of 2.043, and Inception Score of 9.698, substantially outperforming StoryDiffusion (CLIP 15.232, FID 4.643) and StoryGen (CLIP 13.261, FID 8.557). The proposed long-term consistency (LT) metric reaches 0.814 for the zero-shot model and 0.893 with few-shot references, compared to 0.596 and 0.542 for the two baselines respectively. For full-length video generation, MovieDreamer attains a CLIP-similarity of 0.704 and Aesthetic Score of 6.049, surpassing SEINE (0.688, 5.825), StreamingT2V (0.624, 4.119), and FreeNoise (0.612, 3.194).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MovieDreamer outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric. In story generation (Table 1), it beats StoryDiffusion by +4.35 CLIP, −2.60 FID, and +0.218 LT, and beats StoryGen by +6.32 CLIP, −6.51 FID, and +0.272 LT, demonstrating substantially better narrative alignment, image quality, and character consistency. In full-length video generation (Table 2), MovieDreamer surpasses the closest competitor SEINE by +0.016 CLIP-sim and +0.224 Aesthetic Score, while leading StreamingT2V by +4.22 CLIP and +1.93 Aesthetic, and FreeNoise by +5.19 CLIP and +2.86 Aesthetic. The few-shot variant (Ours-ref) further improves over the zero-shot model on all metrics, confirming the effectiveness of in-context reference frames for personalized generation. Notably, the Aesthetic Score gap is smallest against StoryDiffusion (6.093 vs. 6.134), suggesting that diffusion-based baselines can match raw visual appeal but fail on consistency and narrative coherence where MovieDreamer's autoregressive hierarchy excels.

## Relation to Prior Work

MovieDreamer carves a distinct niche by hierarchically decomposing long-form video generation into an autoregressive narrative-planning stage (initialized from LLaMA-7B, using GMM-based continuous token prediction inspired by GIVT) and a diffusion-based rendering stage (SDXL U-Net + Stable Video Diffusion). It departs from the dominant pure-diffusion paradigm (Sora, SEINE, StreamingT2V) by arguing that autoregressive models are better suited for modeling complex narrative logic and arbitrary-length prediction, while diffusion excels at visual fidelity. The multimodal script conditioning—combining text descriptions, face embeddings (FARL), and sentence-level CLIP tokens—repurposes VLM-style input structuring for generative video, and the in-context few-shot mechanism borrows from LLM prompting paradigms to enable personalized generation without fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to generate ultra-long (tens-of-minutes-to-hours) narrative video with multi-character identity consistency, moving beyond the clip-looping or short-clip-extension approaches that dominate prior long-video work. The hierarchical design is explicitly orthogonal to short-video quality improvements, meaning it can benefit from future advances in image-to-video models without retraining. Key open questions include: object-level (non-character) consistency remains unaddressed, the 128-keyframe context window limits true single-pass generation, the 50k-video training set is small and may constrain generalization, and the method's heavy compute requirements (6×A800 for 3 weeks on the decoder alone, 4×H100 for 3 days on the AR model) limit accessibility. The paper also raises important ethical concerns about misuse for generating convincing long-form deceptive content.
