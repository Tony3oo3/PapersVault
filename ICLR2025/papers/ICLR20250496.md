## Problem

Mainstream actor-critic algorithms for continuous control (SAC, TD3, REDQ, DSAC-T) rely on heuristic exploration mechanisms such as noise perturbation and entropy maximization, lacking a principled, uncertainty-driven exploration strategy. Prior work applying Langevin Monte Carlo (LMC) based Thompson sampling to RL (LMCDQN) is limited to discrete action spaces and cannot handle the multidimensional continuous actions, value overestimation, and low update-to-data ratios that characterize continuous control. LSAC addresses all three of these gaps simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The agent receives state observations as either low-dimensional vectors (MuJoCo, dim 14–56) or stacks of three 84×84 RGB frames (DMC) and outputs a continuous action vector in a box-constrained space [-a_l, a_h]^d. The critic does not produce a scalar Q-value but a full Gaussian distribution over soft state-action returns (mean Q_ψ and standard deviation σ_ψ), and the policy is a diagonal Gaussian parameterized by a Max-Ent objective. A diffusion generator additionally produces synthetic transition tuples (s_M, a_M, r_M, s'_M, d_M) that are mixed with real replay data at a 0.5 ratio during critic updates.

## Method

LSAC replaces the standard Adam-based critic update with an adaptive SGLD (aSGLD) step that performs one noisy gradient descent iteration per update, effectively sampling from the posterior over distributional Q-functions (approximate Thompson sampling). To handle the multimodal posterior, n parallel LMC chains (default n=10) are maintained with different initializations, implementing a simplified parallel tempering scheme without replica exchange; at each actor update, one critic is sampled uniformly from this collection. To boost the update-to-data ratio and inject diversity, a diffusion model trained via score matching on the replay buffer periodically generates synthetic transitions, whose actions are then refined by one step of gradient ascent along ∇_a Q_ψ before being mixed with real data for the critic loss. The distributional critic objective uses a KL divergence between the Bellman-pushed target distribution and the current Gaussian value distribution, with an adaptive gradient scalar ω proportional to the batch variance, which empirically reduces Q overestimation bias compared to a standard critic.

## Results

On MuJoCo (1e6 steps, 10 seeds), LSAC achieves the highest average return on 5 of 6 tasks, with the largest margin on HalfCheetah (17948 vs. 12703 for DSAC-T, +41%) and Ant (7411 vs. 6153, +20%). On DMC (3e6 steps), LSAC outperforms all model-free and model-based baselines on 9 of 12 hard-exploration tasks, including dramatic wins on Reacher Hard (981 vs. 11 for DSAC-T) and Hopper Hop (557 vs. 344 for DSAC-T). The sole MuJoCo loss is Humanoid (8545 vs. 9028 for DSAC-T), a marginal ~5% gap. In maze exploration experiments, LSAC discovers multiple paths to both goals with state coverage comparable to or exceeding DIPO, while all other baselines find at most a single path.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DSAC-T (the strongest model-free baseline), LSAC wins on 5/6 MuJoCo tasks and 9/12 DMC tasks; DSAC-T edges out LSAC only on Humanoid (9028 vs. 8545) and DMC's Quadruped Run (788 vs. 473) and Cartpole Swingup Sparse (751 vs. 610). Against DIPO, LSAC dominates on all 6 MuJoCo tasks (e.g., 17948 vs. 9329 on HalfCheetah) and most DMC tasks, while in maze exploration both find multiple paths but LSAC achieves greater state coverage. Against SAC, TD3, PPO, and TRPO, LSAC wins on all 6 MuJoCo tasks by wide margins (e.g., 17948 vs. 10543 for SAC, 6560 for PPO, 6534 for TRPO on HalfCheetah); on DMC, SAC is competitive only on Walker Walk (968 vs. 964) while the others trail substantially. Against REDQ (ensemble-based), LSAC wins all 6 MuJoCo tasks and is significantly faster in wall-clock time (1128 ms vs. 1623 ms per update on Ant). Against QSM (diffusion-policy-based), LSAC is faster and achieves higher returns on MuJoCo. Against Dreamer (model-based) and DrQ-v2 (visual model-free), LSAC wins 9/12 DMC tasks; Dreamer and DrQ-v2 outperform LSAC on Quadruped Run and Cartpole Swingup Sparse, where pixel-based dynamics modeling provides an advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

LSAC extends the line of LMC-based approximate Thompson sampling in RL (Ishfaq et al., 2024a,b; Dwaracherla & Van Roy, 2020), which was previously restricted to discrete action spaces, to continuous control by replacing greedy action selection with a Max-Ent policy objective and a distributional critic (inspired by DSAC-T). It further departs from diffusion-based RL methods (DIPO, QSM) that parameterize policies as diffusion models; here, diffusion is repurposed solely as a synthetic data generator for critic updates, with Q action gradient refinement constraining synthetic actions to high-value regions. The parallel tempering mechanism for multimodal Q posteriors and the adaptive SGLD sampler (Kim et al., 2022) are integrated in a way not previously seen in actor-critic continuous control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first successful application of LMC-based Thompson sampling to continuous control with continuous action spaces, providing a principled exploration mechanism that complements the heuristic noise-based exploration in SAC/TD3. The combination of distributional critic, parallel tempering, and diffusion synthetic data offers a modular recipe that could be adopted in other off-policy settings. Limitations include sensitivity to the LMC step size and bias factor, computational overhead from parallel critics and periodic diffusion updates, and the simplified parallel tempering (no replica exchange) that may limit exploration in highly multimodal posteriors. Open questions include whether underdamped LMC or other advanced samplers can further improve mixing, and whether the approach scales to high-dimensional pixel-based continuous control without the pixel-to-vector bottleneck.
