## Problem

While VLMs have demonstrated impressive capabilities, their ability to perform abductive reasoning over visual scenes—inferring plausible causes or outcomes from a context image—remains largely unexplored. Prior work evaluated VLMs on single-scene tasks (visual entailment, improbable event detection) or sequential next-frame prediction, but did not test whether models can judge the plausibility of candidate hypothesis images relative to a premise image and explain their reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253099/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 350 triplets of photorealistic AI-generated images (1,050 total) where each triplet contains one premise image and two hypothesis images, annotated with a gold label, gold explanation, reasoning category, and temporal direction/duration. In the triplet setup, the model receives the premise plus both hypotheses (either as separate images or a single combined image) and outputs a hypothesis index with a free-form explanation; in the pairs setup, it receives one premise-hypothesis pair and outputs a plausibility score on a 1–10 Likert scale. A text-only variant feeds the original generation prompts instead of images, isolating textual reasoning from visual interpretation.

## Method

NL-EYE is a carefully curated benchmark of 350 image triplets spanning six reasoning categories (physical, functional, logical, emotional, cultural, social) with temporal annotations (forward/backward/parallel direction; short/long duration). Data creation required three human-intensive stages: 20 annotators wrote textual scene triplets, professional designers iteratively generated photorealistic images using Midjourney and DALL-E 3 with inpainting, image-conditioned prompting, and shared seeds for visual consistency, and the same designers wrote gold explanations and assigned categories. The evaluation framework tests VLMs under two input strategies (separate vs. combined images), two reasoning approaches (vision-based end-to-end vs. text-based using gold or model-generated captions), and two prediction setups (triplet comparative with order-consistency accuracy, and pairs absolute scoring with order-faithfulness accuracy). Explanations are evaluated both by human crowd-workers selecting valid justifications and by an LLM-as-judge comparing against gold references. The key empirical finding is that VLMs perform at or below random baseline (25–60% consistency accuracy) while humans reach 85%, and the bottleneck is visual interpretation rather than textual reasoning, as the same models achieve ~80% when given gold text descriptions.

## Results

On NL-EYE, humans achieve 85% triplet accuracy and 95% explanation quality, while the best VLM (GPT-4o with combined-image input) reaches only 60% triplet accuracy and 44% human-evaluated explanation quality. Most VLMs perform at or below the dumb-pixel baseline (50%), and open-source models (LLaVA 1.6: 14%, Fuyu: 4%) score below the random baseline (25%). In text-only experiments with gold descriptions, the same VLMs jump to 65–81% accuracy, revealing that the bottleneck is visual interpretation rather than textual reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 8+ VLMs and 2 NLI models against human performance. GPT-4o is the strongest VLM, reaching 60% triplet accuracy (combined image) but still trailing humans (85%) by 25 points; all other VLMs score at or below the 50% dumb-pixel baseline. Open-source models LLaVA 1.6 (14%) and Fuyu (4%) fall below even the 25% random baseline. In text-only settings with gold descriptions, GPT-4o (80%), Claude-Opus-3 (81%), and GPT-4 (78%) approach human-level performance, while fine-tuned NLI models BART-L-MNLI (68%) and DeBERTa-v3 (65%) also substantially outperform their vision-based counterparts. When VLMs generate their own image captions (image-to-text pipeline), accuracy drops to 29–40%, confirming that inaccurate visual descriptions—not weak textual reasoning—are the primary failure mode. Across all models, explanation quality lags prediction accuracy by 10–30 points, with even the best VLM (Claude-Sonnet-3.5, separate images) achieving only 50% human-evaluated explanation validity versus 95% for humans.

## Relation to Prior Work

NL-EYE extends the textual abductive NLI task (Bhagavatula et al., 2019) from text to the visual domain, formulating it as an image-to-image plausibility judgment. It departs from prior visual abductive reasoning work (e.g., Sherlock, Hessel et al. 2022) and visual entailment benchmarks (SNLI-VE, WYSIWYR) by requiring multi-image reasoning over a premise and two hypothesis images, and by adding a free-form explanation generation task. The use of synthetic, visually consistent triplets generated via Midjourney and DALL-E distinguishes it from natural-image datasets like Winoground or VCOPA, enabling controlled multi-image consistency that is difficult to achieve with real-world footage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark and well-evidenced gap: humans achieve 85% accuracy while most VLMs perform at or below random (25%), and even the best model (GPT-4o at 60%) is far from human level. The finding that the same models reach ~80% on text-only descriptions but drop to ~40-50% on images localizes the bottleneck to visual encoding rather than textual reasoning, pointing toward concrete architectural improvements. Open questions include whether better image-to-text alignment, visual chain-of-thought, or training on semantic-over-stylized data can close this gap, and whether the order-sensitivity and format-sensitivity observed here will persist as models scale.
