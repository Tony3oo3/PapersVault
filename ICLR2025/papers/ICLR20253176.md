## Problem

Existing representation editing and reading (REAR) methods for reducing LLM hallucinations rely on specialized tools (linear probes, autoencoders) trained on in-domain samples, making them difficult to scale, prone to overfitting, and limited in generalizability across diverse datasets. This paper asks whether a simpler, training-free signal within the model's internal representations can reliably distinguish factual from hallucinated content without any external modules or in-domain calibration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253176/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multiple-choice question with N candidate answer options; each option is prepended with the question text (and optional instructions) and passed through the LLM as a separate forward pass. The output is a single predicted answer label determined by majority voting across selected attention head norms. A one-time calibration step requires only 30 randomly drawn samples from the target dataset's training split to identify which heads to use and their correlation direction, taking under 10 seconds on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

NoVo exploits the L2 norm of attention head context vectors at the final sequence position as a scalar measure of latent truth, motivated by the observation that these norms spike by up to 83% at token positions where factual propositions are complete or relevant factual associations occur. A Norm Selection stage feeds 30 random samples through the model, computes argmax/argmin predictions for every head, and retains only heads whose accuracy exceeds the 85th percentile as 'Voters,' recording each Voter's correlation direction (whether higher or lower norm indicates truth). During inference, each candidate answer produces a Norm Matrix, and the selected Voters cast individual predictions (argmax or argmin per Voter based on their stored direction), with the final answer determined by majority vote via the mode operator. The approach requires no training, no probes, no autoencoders, and no external data, making it a lightweight ensemble of weak learners that scales to arbitrary MCQ datasets. The paper further identifies two functional Voter types—Type-1 (structural, attending to end tokens and punctuation) and Type-2 (associative, attending to specific token relationships)—that are evenly distributed across upper layers and contribute complementary error patterns to the ensemble.

## Results

On TruthfulQA MC1 (the hardest single-correct-answer track), NoVo achieves a new SOTA accuracy of 78.09% on Mistral-7B-Instruct, surpassing GPT-4 (59.0%) by 19 points and the best prior REAR method (RePE, 58.9%) by 19.2 points. Across four 7B models, NoVo yields median gains of 22–31 accuracy points over the log-likelihood baseline. On 20 diverse reasoning, factuality, and NLU datasets, NoVo produces significant gains in over 90% of cases, with the largest improvements on QASC (+34.6 pts on Mistral), CosmosQA (+35.9 pts), and CICERO v2 (+35.0 pts).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoVo decisively outperforms all six REAR baselines on TruthfulQA MC1: on Mistral-7B-Instruct it beats TruthX (56.43%) by 21.7 pts, ITI (55.73%) by 22.4 pts, DoLa (48.83%) by 29.3 pts, ICD (58.13%) by 20.0 pts, and RePE (58.9% on Llama2-Chat) by 19.2 pts; TruthForest (39.30% on Llama2-Chat) trails by 30.8 pts. On the factuality benchmarks (Table 4), NoVo (76.82% on FACTOR-Expert, 72.30% on Natural Questions, 97.81% on TriviaQA) exceeds TruthX, ITI, and ICD by 11–21 points across all three datasets. In the finetuning setting, +NoVo surpasses standard SFT by an average of 1.3 points and the TEAM binary-reformulation variant by 0.7 points across nine datasets. Against PriDe (Zheng et al., 2024), NoVo is competitive on ARC (53.8% vs 53.7% for Llama2-7B) and CSQA (51.0% vs 52.9%), while trailing slightly on MMLU (43.2% vs 45.3%). UnifiedQA-11B and UNICORN-11B are included as reference-only baselines without direct comparison. Overall, NoVo wins or ties on every head-to-head comparison where both methods report numbers, with the largest margins on the hallucination-specific TruthfulQA benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

NoVo builds on the representation engineering and reading (REAR) paradigm, which established that hidden states in LLMs encode truth signals (Burns et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023) and that individual attention heads carry such signals (Li et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024). It also draws on computer vision findings that L2 norms of feature vectors correlate with quality (Kim & Lee, 2017). However, NoVo departs sharply from prior REAR methods by eliminating the need for specialized tools (linear probes, autoencoders), in-domain cross-fold training, or curated templates, instead using only the L2 norm of attention head context vectors at the final sequence position combined with a majority-voting ensemble. This positions NoVo as a lightweight, training-free alternative to methods like DoLa, ITI, TruthForest, TruthX, ICD, and RePE, which all require some form of in-domain adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a trivially computed scalar (attention head L2 norm) combined with naive majority voting can outperform sophisticated probe-based and contrastive-decoding methods by 19+ points on TruthfulQA MC1 challenges the assumption that hallucination mitigation requires complex learned representations. The paper frames a broader, task-agnostic problem: factual hallucinations stem from misalignments between internal hidden states and the language modelling likelihood, with head norms offering a potential universal signal for truth-ranking across candidate spans. Key limitations include restriction to multiple-choice settings (the norm measure is inherently relative), unclear transferability of the DeBERTa fine-tuning results to decoder architectures, and dependence on in-domain samples for Norm Selection. Open questions include whether head norms can be applied to single-span generation, whether the truthful hypersphere structure can be further characterized, and whether more sophisticated ensemble strategies (beyond majority vote) could push accuracy further.
