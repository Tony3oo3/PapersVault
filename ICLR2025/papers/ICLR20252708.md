## Problem

Existing test-time data poisoning (TTDP) studies overestimate the adversarial risk of TTA methods by relying on unrealistic assumptions: white-box access to real-time model weights, direct access to benign users' samples for optimization, and offline attack ordering. No prior work has formulated a threat model that simultaneously enforces grey-box access, no access to other users' data, and online injection, leaving the true adversarial vulnerability of TTA unclear.

## Input / Output

The adversary takes clean test images from its own subset B_ab (e.g., public images from the same distribution), the initial source model weights, and prediction feedback from previously injected poisoned samples to iteratively update a surrogate model. It produces L∞-bounded poisoned samples (budget b=0.3) that are injected into the TTA pipeline alongside benign users' samples, with attack success measured as the classification error rate on the unseen benign subset B_b. The overall attack budget r = |B_a|/(|B_a|+|B_b|) is fixed at 50%, and poisoning is applied either uniformly across the test stream or concentrated in a short burst (non-uniform).

## Method

The paper proposes a grey-box in-distribution attack framework with three key components. First, a surrogate model is maintained and distilled from the online TTA model via symmetric KL-divergence on the adversary's own query feedback, eliminating the need for real-time weight access. Second, the bi-level optimization from DIA is reformulated into a single-level problem by replacing benign samples with the adversary's clean samples and adding a feature consistency regularization (KL-divergence between intermediate-layer Gaussian feature distributions of poisoned and clean samples), justified via PAC learning to ensure attack generalization. Third, two TTA-aware attack objectives are designed: a Notch High Entropy (NHE) attack that zeros out the ground-truth class probability and minimizes cross-entropy against a uniform target over remaining classes, and a Balanced Low Entropy (BLE) attack that uses a Hungarian-algorithm-based label mapping to distribute misclassification across all classes with class-balanced confidence. The combined objective is optimized via 40-step PGD with Lagrangian multipliers for the feature consistency constraint.

## Results

Under the proposed RTTDP protocol (grey-box, no access to benign data, online injection), the NHE Attack achieves the highest average error rate across all three datasets: 36.25% on CIFAR-10-C, 53.65% on CIFAR-100-C, and 68.04% on ImageNet-C (vs. 18.28%, 37.77%, and 60.30% for no attack). The BLE Attack achieves the best average ranking (1.57 on CIFAR-10-C, 2.28 on CIFAR-100-C, 3.00 on ImageNet-C), indicating consistent top-2 performance across TTA methods. Notably, existing poisoning methods (DIA, TePA, MaxCE) show only marginal degradation over no-attack baselines for robust TTA methods like CoTTA and ROID, suggesting the adversarial risk was previously overestimated under unrealistic white-box assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed NHE and BLE attacks substantially outperform all existing poisoning baselines under the RTTDP protocol. On CIFAR-10-C (Uniform), NHE Attack achieves 36.25% average error vs. 21.73% for TePA* and 20.05% for DIA*, while BLE Attack achieves 35.61% vs. 18.71% for MaxCE. The gap is most dramatic on naive self-training methods (TENT, RPL), where NHE reaches 73.86% and 72.40% error vs. 33.78% and 22.36% for TePA*, but shrinks for robust methods like CoTTA (17.54% vs. 16.57%) and ROID (17.00% vs. 18.76%), indicating these methods are genuinely more resistant. Among baselines, TePA* and Unlearnable Examples are the strongest existing attacks, while MaxCE and Adversarial Poisoning perform near the no-attack baseline, confirming that generic adversarial objectives are poorly suited for TTA poisoning. In the adversarial attack comparison (Table 8), even advanced methods like GMSA-AVG (24.79% avg on CIFAR-10-C) and AutoAttack (20.91%) fall well short of the proposed data poisoning objectives (36.28–36.29%), validating that poisoning and adversarial attacks target fundamentally different vulnerabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time data poisoning (TTDP) line initiated by DIA (Wu et al., 2023) and TePA (Cong et al., 2023) by arguing that both rely on unrealistic assumptions—white-box model access, visibility of benign users' samples, and offline attack ordering—and proposes a more constrained grey-box, online protocol (RTTDP). Methodologically, it borrows surrogate model distillation from the adversarial attack literature and PAC learning theory to justify a feature-consistency regularization that replaces the need for benign samples in the outer optimization. The TTA-aware attack objectives (Notch High Entropy and Balanced Low Entropy) are novel formulations that exploit the specific self-training and entropy-minimization mechanisms of TTA methods rather than treating them as static classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central empirical finding—that TTA methods, particularly those using EMA (CoTTA, ROID), are substantially more robust to poisoning under realistic assumptions than prior work suggested—recalibrates the field's threat model and suggests that the adversarial risk was overstated by overly strong attack assumptions. The practical defense recommendations (entropy thresholding, data augmentation, EMA updates) are simple and immediately actionable for TTA practitioners. Open questions include whether the RTTDP protocol and its findings generalize to open-world TTA, multi-modal settings, or larger foundation-model-based TTA pipelines, and whether the surrogate distillation approach remains effective when the online model drifts significantly from the source.
