## Problem

Existing research on emergent abilities in large language models treats models as black boxes, correlating performance with model size or evaluating outputs across benchmarks, but provides no quantitative framework for analyzing the internal structural dynamics that drive emergence. This paper addresses that gap by asking whether the self-organization of neuron interactions during training can be measured structurally and linked to the development of emergent capabilities, drawing an analogy to how brain network microstructure supports cognitive function.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251398/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full weight matrices of a transformer model at a given training checkpoint (Pythia 14M–2.8B, GPT-NeoX architecture) and converts them into a weighted directed Neuron Interaction Network (NIN) where edge weights are the inverse of absolute weight magnitudes (p=−1) and shortest-path distances include a path-length penalty (λ=1, γ=5). Because full NINs contain millions of nodes, analysis is performed on 10 randomly sampled subgraphs of 64 nodes per layer, with results averaged to reduce variance. Outputs include the full multifractal spectrum f(α), the Lipschitz-Hölder exponent α(q), the mass exponent τ(q), and the scalar degree-of-emergence metric E computed as the product of relative heterogeneity change w(t)/w(0) and the log-ratio of regularity α₀(0)/α₀(t).

## Method

NeuroMFA first transforms a standard feedforward NN into a Neuron Interaction Network (NIN), a weighted directed graph where each edge weight ω_ab = |w_ab|^{−1} inverts connection strength into distance, and node proximity is defined via shortest-path distance augmented by a path-length penalty term. For each neuron, a box-growing procedure records the number of neighbors N_{l,i}(r) within radius r, revealing a local power-law N(r) ∼ r^D that establishes fractal scaling. A distortion factor q is then introduced to construct the partition function Z_q(r) = Σ p_{l,i}(r)^q across all neurons, whose log-log slope against r/d yields the mass exponent τ(q). A Legendre transform of τ(q) produces the Lipschitz-Hölder exponent α(q) and the multifractal spectrum f(α), from which two structural metrics are extracted: α₀ (the α at which f(α) peaks, measuring regularity) and w = α_max − α_min (spectrum width, measuring heterogeneity). The degree of emergence E = [w(t)/w(0)] · log[α₀(0)/α₀(t)] combines both dimensions into a single scalar that captures the joint increase in interaction diversity and structural order during training, validated against LAMBADA, SciQ, PIQA, and ARC-easy benchmarks across Pythia checkpoints.

## Results

The paper analyzes Pythia models (14M–2.8B) across 154 training checkpoints using NeuroMFA. Key findings: (1) spectrum width w (heterogeneity) increases steadily with training for all models, stabilizing after ~35,000 epochs for models >1B; (2) for models exceeding 100M parameters, the multifractal spectrum shifts leftward (decreasing α₀, i.e., increasing regularity) from epoch 0 to ~35,000, while the 14M and 31M models show no such shift, indicating insufficient self-organization; (3) the proposed degree-of-emergence metric E correlates positively with benchmark accuracy on LAMBADA, PIQA, SciQ, and ARC-easy, and increases logarithmically with model size at 53,000 epochs (all models show low E at 512 epochs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not directly benchmark NeuroMFA against competing emergence-measurement methods (e.g., the entropy-reduction approach of Chen et al. 2024 or the pre-training-loss perspective of Du et al. 2024). Instead, it validates the structural metric E against standard LLM performance benchmarks (LAMBADA, PIQA, SciQ, ARC-easy, WSC, LogiQA) as proxy indicators of emergent capability. The authors report a strong positive correlation between E and benchmark accuracy across training epochs for Pythia 160M, 1.4B, and 2.8B models (Fig. 6), and show that E tracks the radar-chart performance of 1B and 1.4B models across four cognitive/reasoning benchmarks (Fig. 7). They note that WSC and LogiQA exhibit significant performance oscillations, underscoring the limitations of relying solely on output-based metrics. The paper positions itself as complementary to rather than competitive with existing scaling-law and evaluation-based approaches, arguing that structural analysis provides a model-internal perspective that black-box performance metrics cannot capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends box-covering and box-growing multifractal analysis methods originally developed for complex networks (Song et al., 2005; Evertsz & Mandelbrot, 1992; Salat et al., 2017; Furuya & Yakubo, 2011) to a neuron-level analysis of transformer architectures, drawing an explicit analogy to neuroscience research on how brain network microstructure supports cognitive function (Bullmore & Sporns, 2009; Bassett & Sporns, 2017). It departs from the dominant paradigm in emergent-ability research (Wei et al., 2022a; Srivastava et al., 2022; Schaeffer et al., 2024; Fu et al., 2023), which treats models as black boxes and correlates output performance with model size, by instead proposing a structure-based metric derived from the internal dynamics of neuron interactions during training. The self-organization framework (Goldstein, 1999; Crutchfield, 1994; Turing patterns) provides the theoretical bridge between micro-level neuron interactions and macro-level emergent capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuroMFA offers a principled, interpretable lens into *why* large models develop new capabilities by linking internal structural evolution (increasing heterogeneity and regularity of neuron interactions) to external performance, moving beyond the black-box scaling-law paradigm. However, the empirical validation is limited to Pythia (GPT-NeoX) models across a small set of benchmarks, and the correlation between the degree-of-emergence metric E and task performance, while consistent, has not been tested across diverse architectures, modalities, or training regimes. Open questions include whether the multifractal signatures generalize to non-transformer architectures (e.g., Mamba, state-space models), whether the metric can predict emergence *before* it manifests in downstream performance, and whether the observed stabilization of spectral width around 35,000 epochs reflects a fundamental training limit or a dataset-specific saturation.
