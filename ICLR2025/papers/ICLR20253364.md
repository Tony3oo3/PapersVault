## Problem

Existing vision tokenizers in MLLMs (patch-level continuous/discrete tokens and fixed learnable queries) fragment visual semantic units by dividing images into fixed-size patches, disrupting the integrity of objects and compositional regions. This creates a fundamental misalignment between naturally discrete language tokens and fragmented visual tokens, undermining fine-grained vision-language understanding, generation, and editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253364/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts 384×384 RGB images and text prompts (questions, captions, editing instructions, or referring expressions) and produces text responses, generated/edited images, and segmentation masks. A key distinction is that the number of visual tokens is dynamic rather than fixed—determined per-image by a density-peak clustering stopping criterion—so a simple scene may yield ~20 tokens while a complex one yields more. The visual tokens are continuous (not discrete codebook entries) and each token corresponds to a semantically complete region rather than a fixed spatial patch.

## Method

SeTok groups vision encoder patch embeddings (from SigLIP-SO400M) into a variable number of semantic clusters via a density-peak clustering algorithm adapted from GENESIS-V2, where cluster seeds are selected by combining local density and minimum-distance scores rather than uniform random selection. A token merger with 12 inner-cluster and 8 inter-cluster Transformer layers aggregates features within and across clusters, incorporating 2D rotary position embeddings to preserve spatial information. SeTok is trained jointly with a concept-level image-text contrastive loss (multi-positive, K objects per image) and an image reconstruction loss through a detokenizer. The resulting tokens are concatenated with text tokens and fed into LLaMA-2-7B (SETOKIM), which is trained with a unified autoregressive objective—cross-entropy for text and diffusion loss for vision—followed by instruction tuning, and a lightweight SAM-style mask decoder is attached for referring segmentation.

## Results

SETOKIM (7B) achieves state-of-the-art or competitive results across all major vision-language benchmarks: 86.9 CIDEr on Flickr30K, 78.5% on VQA^v2, 60.2% on OK-VQA, 65.6% on GQA, 89.1 on POPE, 1537.8 on MME, and 45.2 on MM-Vet. For image generation, it attains 8.5 FID on MSCOCO and 6.9 L1 on MagicBrush editing, outperforming all MLLM-based baselines. On referring segmentation, SETOKIM reaches 71.3 on refCOCOg (val/test) and 52.7 cIoU on Reaseg, surpassing LISA (70.6 / 48.4) and PixelLM (70.5 / –). The SeTok tokenizer itself achieves 2.07 rFID and 75.4% Top-1 on ImageNet, matching or exceeding fixed-token VQ models while using a dynamic (average ~25) number of tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest same-size baseline LLaVA-1.5 (7B, patch-level continuous tokens), SETOKIM improves GQA by +3.6% (65.6 vs 62.0), MM-Vet by +12.1 (45.2 vs 33.1), POPE by +3.2 (89.1 vs 85.9), and MME by +27.1 (1537.8 vs 1510.7), while matching VQA^v2 (78.5). Against larger models, SETOKIM (7B) outperforms SEED-X (17B) on GQA (65.6 vs 47.9) and POPE (89.1 vs 84.2), and Chameleon (34B) on Flickr30K (86.9 vs 74.7) and VQA^v2 (78.5 vs 66.0). In image editing (Table 2), SETOKIM achieves the best L1 on MagicBrush (6.9 vs 7.6 for Morph-Token, 8.2 for MGIE) and EVR (14.1 vs 15.3, 16.3), though MGIE edges it on MA5K L1 (13.3 vs 15.7) and CLIPim (91.1 vs 89.6 on MSCOCO). For referring segmentation, SETOKIM leads all baselines on refCOCOg (71.3 vs 70.6 for LISA, 70.5 for PixelLM) and Reaseg cIoU (52.7 vs 48.4 for LISA). The dynamic hard-clustering strategy (avg ~25 tokens, 8.3 TFLOPs) also outperforms fixed 256-patch tokenization (15.7 TFLOPs) on both Flickr30K (86.9 vs 85.1) and OK-VQA (60.2 vs 51.7) while halving compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the iterative seed-selection and mask-assignment procedure from GENESIS-V2 (Engelcke et al., 2021) for scene decomposition, replacing its uniform seed scoring with a density-peaks criterion to dynamically determine cluster centers. The training objective borrows the multi-positive contrastive formulation from GroupViT (Xu et al., 2022) and the diffusion-based autoregressive generation loss from MAR (Li et al., 2024a). Conceptually, the work departs from the dominant paradigm of fixed patch-level tokenization (ViT-style) and fixed-query compression (Q-Former, Perceiver) by proposing that visual tokens should be semantically equivalent to linguistic tokens—i.e., each token should correspond to a complete object or compositional region rather than a spatially fixed square.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental representational mismatch in MLLMs: language tokens are discrete semantic units while standard visual tokenization produces spatially fixed patches that fragment objects. The dynamic, content-adaptive token count (averaging ~25 tokens vs. 256 fixed patches) yields a 48% reduction in TFLOPs while matching or exceeding fixed-token baselines, suggesting that most patch-level redundancy can be eliminated without quality loss. Key limitations include the 7B model scale, 384×384 resolution ceiling, and persistent hallucination; open questions include whether density-peaks clustering generalizes to video or higher-resolution inputs, and whether the approach scales to 65B+ backbones where the LLM's own representational capacity may dominate over tokenizer design.
