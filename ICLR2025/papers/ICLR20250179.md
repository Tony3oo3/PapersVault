## Problem

Existing latent 3D reconstruction methods face a significant domain gap between 2D VAE latent space and 3D radiance field representations: the VAE's compact latent encoding introduces massive high-frequency noise that destroys multi-view geometric consistency, and the non-linear rendering process shifts the latent distribution away from what the VAE decoder expects. As a result, directly lifting 2D latent features into 3D produces degraded, artifact-ridden novel views, and no prior work has demonstrated photorealistic 3D reconstruction entirely in the latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250179/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (512×512) with COLMAP-estimated camera poses, which are encoded into 4-channel latent representations at 1/8 spatial resolution (H'×W'×4) by a fine-tuned VAE encoder. For text-to-3D, a text prompt guides a pre-trained diffusion U-Net operating in the same latent space. The output is a set of 3D Gaussians parameterized in latent space (position, scale, rotation, opacity, and spherical-harmonic latent coefficients), which are rasterized into 2D latent maps and decoded back to full-resolution RGB images by the aligned VAE decoder.

## Method

The framework is a three-stage pipeline. Stage I fine-tunes the VAE encoder with a correspondence-aware loss that enforces L1 consistency between latent pixels linked by the fundamental matrix across view pairs, weighted by normalized absolute pose error, plus a KL regularization term anchoring the fine-tuned latents to the original VAE distribution—this removes high-frequency noise while preserving generative capacity. Stage II builds a Latent Radiance Field (LRF) by representing the scene as 3D Gaussians whose per-point appearance is a view-dependent latent code modeled with degree-3 spherical harmonics, optimized via differentiable rasterization and alpha-blending directly in the 4-channel latent space with an L1+D-SSIM loss. Stage III fine-tunes the VAE decoder on a paired dataset of LRF-rendered latents (from both training and novel views) and their ground-truth images, closing the distribution gap introduced by the non-linear rendering pipeline so that decoded images are photorealistic without any per-scene refinement module.

## Results

On the in-distribution DL3DV-10K dataset, 3DGS-LRF achieves PSNR 22.45, SSIM 0.667, and LPIPS 0.197, outperforming all image-space and latent-space baselines. On out-of-distribution datasets, the method maintains strong performance: PSNR 26.26 on MVImgNet, 20.00 on NeRF-LLFF, and 20.83 on Mip-NeRF360. In the few-shot (3-view) setting on NeRF-LLFF, the method reaches PSNR 15.51 and SSIM 0.379, surpassing 3DGS (13.79) and Mip-Splatting (13.70). Efficiency-wise, the method trains in 3.9 min with 1 GB GPU usage and 96.42 MB storage, while rendering at 180 FPS plus 20 FPS decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 3DGS-LRF outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all four datasets. Against the strongest image-space baseline Mip-Splatting/8, the method gains +1.37 PSNR on MVImgNet (26.26 vs. 24.89), +0.32 on NeRF-LLFF (20.00 vs. 19.68), +0.64 on DL3DV-10K (22.45 vs. 21.81), and −1.55 on Mip-NeRF360 (20.83 vs. 22.38), where Mip-Splatting retains a slight edge in PSNR but the method still wins on SSIM (0.469 vs. 0.502) and LPIPS (0.328 vs. 0.521). Against the closest latent-space baseline 3DGS-VAE, the method improves PSNR by +1.22 (MVImgNet), +0.93 (NeRF-LLFF), +1.88 (DL3DV-10K), and +1.39 (Mip-NeRF360). Latent-NeRF* and Feature-GS are consistently weaker, with Feature-GS lagging by 4–6 PSNR on most datasets. In the 3-view few-shot setting (Table 2), the method achieves PSNR 15.51 vs. 13.79 for 3DGS and 13.70 for Mip-Splatting, a ~1.8 dB margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the radiance field backbone and the VAE from Latent Diffusion Models as the 2D representation, but departs from prior latent 3D reconstruction work (Latent-NeRF, ED-NeRF, Feature 3DGS) by injecting 3D awareness directly into the VAE's 2D latent space via a correspondence-consistency loss, rather than adding per-scene refinement layers or view-independent feature fields. The VAE-RF alignment stage—fine-tuning the decoder on rendered latents to close the distribution gap between radiance field output and VAE input—is a new component not present in prior work. Conceptually, the paper inverts the common direction of distilling 2D features into 3D, instead making the 2D encoder 3D-aware so that standard 3D optimization (3DGS) works natively in latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a radiance field operating entirely in a 2D latent space (64×64) can match or exceed image-space NVS quality, with substantial efficiency gains (1 GB GPU vs 3 GB, ~half the storage). The plug-and-play compatibility with existing Stable Diffusion pipelines—requiring no U-Net fine-tuning—makes the approach immediately useful for text-to-3D generation and latent diffusion model research. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more compact 3D representations, 3D latent diffusion models, and whether the correspondence-aware fine-tuning generalizes to other VAE architectures or higher-dimensional latent spaces beyond the 4-channel LDM VAE.
