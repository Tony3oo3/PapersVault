## Problem

Zero-shot (training-free) NAS methods have primarily been evaluated on generic NAS benchmarks with limited consideration for specific NLU tasks, and prevailing approaches either require gradient computation or rely on single-feature proxies that yield biased evaluations. No prior work had applied zero-shot NAS to the search space of lightweight BERT-based language models for natural language understanding, leaving a gap between efficient architecture search and practical deployment on resource-constrained devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251521/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text minibatch of shape B×N×D (batch size × token length × embedding dimension) passed through a randomly initialized candidate architecture, and produces a single scalar W-PCA score per architecture. The search space is parameterized as m layers, each selecting one of 2 block types (BERT or MobileBERT) and one of n FFN hidden dimensions (multiples of 132), yielding (2×n)^m total combinations. The genetic algorithm outputs an optimal architecture encoding, which is then pretrained via knowledge distillation from a BERT-base teacher and fine-tuned on downstream tasks.

## Method

W-PCA computes, for each FFN layer, the hidden state H = XW₁ + b₁ after the first linear projection, reshapes it to (BN)×D′, centers it, computes the covariance matrix, and performs eigenvalue decomposition to find the minimum number of principal components k whose cumulative eigenvalue ratio exceeds η=0.99. The per-layer score S_f is this k value; the total PCA score S is the sum over all m layers. The final W-PCA metric is the product of the total parameter count w and S, combining model capacity with representational compactness in a single gradient-free scalar. A genetic algorithm (population 50, 40 generations, crossover probability 1, mutation probability 0.1, parameter budget constraint) searches the block-type and FFN-dimension space to maximize W-PCA, requiring only one forward pass per candidate with no backpropagation. The key empirical insight is that PCA values at initialization (epoch 0) strongly correlate with post-training GLUE performance, and multiplying by #Params improves Kendall τ from 0.466 to 0.526 and Spearman ρ from 0.677 to 0.698 over prior zero-shot proxies.

## Results

On the GLUE dev set, W-PCA-Small (15.6M params) achieves an average score of 81.4 with only 0.5 GPU days of search time, surpassing EfficientBERT (80.4, 58 GPU days) by 1.0 points and NAS-BERT-30 (80.3, 96 GPU days) by 1.1 points while reducing search cost by 2–3 orders of magnitude. On the GLUE test set, W-PCA-Small reaches 78.3 average with 54 ms latency, outperforming EfficientBERT++ (78.0, 65 ms) and all manual lightweight models. On SQuAD v1.1, W-PCA-Small achieves 78.4/86.7 EM/F1, exceeding EfficientBERT++ (78.3/86.5) with fewer parameters. In ranking correlation on the FlexiBERT benchmark, W-PCA attains Kendall τ = 0.526 and Spearman ρ = 0.698, exceeding the best prior zero-shot proxy (Head Confidence: τ = 0.306, ρ = 0.364) by 0.220 and 0.334 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

W-PCA-Small outperforms all training-based one-shot NAS baselines on the GLUE dev set: it beats EfficientBERT (80.4) by 1.0, NAS-BERT-30 (80.3) by 1.1, and AutoBERT-Zero-small (80.5) by 0.9 in average score, while reducing search time from 58–1,000 GPU days to just 0.5 GPU days (a 100×–2,000× speedup). Among zero-shot baselines, W-PCA-Small surpasses Head Confidence (80.2) by 1.2, Softmax Confidence (78.9) by 2.5, and Synaptic Diversity (78.3) by 3.1 on GLUE dev average. On the GLUE test set, W-PCA-Small (78.3) edges out EfficientBERT++ (78.0) by 0.3 while using fewer parameters (15.6M vs. 16.0M) and lower latency (54 ms vs. 65 ms). On SQuAD v1.1, W-PCA-Small (78.4/86.7 EM/F1) marginally beats EfficientBERT++ (78.3/86.5) and substantially outperforms TinyBERT-4 (72.7/82.1) and MiniLM-6. In the FlexiBERT ranking correlation benchmark, W-PCA (τ=0.526, ρ=0.698) dominates all gradient-based zero-shot proxies (Synaptic Saliency τ=0.130, Jacobian Cosine τ=0.116, Synaptic Diversity τ=0.021) and the gradient-free Head Confidence (τ=0.306), confirming its superiority as a training-free architecture evaluator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot (training-free) NAS paradigm—previously applied primarily to vision and evaluated on generic NAS benchmarks—into the domain of lightweight NLU models. It draws on SPOS for its supernet-style search space composition and on EfficientBERT's knowledge distillation loss for the post-search training phase, but departs from both by eliminating the supernet training entirely. The PCA-based proxy is inspired by Pan et al.'s use of PCA for Vision Transformer training optimization, repurposed here as an architecture quality indicator at initialization. The paper positions itself as the first to apply zero-shot NAS specifically to NLU tasks, filling a gap where prior zero-shot proxies (Synaptic Saliency, Activation Distance, Jacobian Cosine, attention-confidence metrics) had only been evaluated on ranking benchmarks without task-specific accuracy validation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–3 order-of-magnitude reduction in search time (0.5 GPU-days vs. 58 days for one-shot NAS) makes architecture search for lightweight LMs practically accessible, and the gradient-free, single-forward-pass evaluation is trivially parallelizable. However, the actual accuracy gains over strong one-shot baselines are modest (e.g., 81.4 vs. 80.4 GLUE dev average), and the CLM extension to 1–3B parameter models in the appendix shows only marginal improvements over baselines, raising questions about whether the PCA proxy's advantage persists at scale. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to non-BERT architectures (e.g., decoder-only LLMs with different FFN structures), whether the η=0.99 threshold is robust across tasks, and whether the genetic algorithm's search quality is a bottleneck relative to the proxy's ranking accuracy.
