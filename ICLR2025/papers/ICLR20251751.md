## Problem

Reinforcement learning agents struggle to generalize to unseen environments because they overfit to specific training dynamics, and existing approaches—data augmentation, invariant representation learning, meta-RL, and standard world models—either cannot handle fundamentally different underlying dynamics or produce entangled latent representations that do not decompose into reusable parts. No prior work leverages the compositional structure of language to identify and recombine causal components of the environment, which is how humans naturally generalize from known to novel task configurations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64×64×3 RGB observations, continuous or discrete actions, and discrete language component tokens (e.g., verb and object words) that are encoded as learned token embeddings to condition the latent decomposition. The latent state is partitioned into m disjoint blocks, each of dimension n_ci, where each block is controlled by exactly one language component. Outputs include the disentangled per-component latents, a policy action from a SAC actor conditioned on reward-masked latents and a task embedding, plus reconstructed observations, predicted rewards, and continuation signals decoded through sparse binary masks that select only the relevant latent subset for each decoding head.

## Method

WM3C builds on DreamerV3's RSSM architecture but factorizes the dynamics into per-component representation and transition models, each conditioned on its own language token embedding, so that the latent space is explicitly partitioned into language-controlled blocks. A key theoretical contribution is a block-wise identifiability theorem proving that each component c_i can be uniquely recovered (up to an invertible function) given n_ci+1 distinct values of its controlling language component, under mild smoothness and conditional-independence assumptions—requiring far fewer auxiliary values than dimension-wise ICA methods. To enforce the causal structure in practice, the training objective combines a reconstruction loss, a factorized KL divergence between per-component posterior and prior, mutual information constraints (via MINE) that maximize I(l_i; c_i) while minimizing I(l_i; c_j) for j≠i, and an adaptive L1 sparsity loss on learnable decoding masks that dynamically controls how many latent dimensions each decoder uses. For adaptation to unseen task compositions, only the dynamics-related modules (task encoder, per-component representation/transition models, decoding masks, and policy) are fine-tuned while the visual encoder and other fixed components remain frozen, exploiting the sparsity of causal systems where only inter-component interactions change.

## Results

On the synthetic component identification task, WM3C achieves diagonal R² > 0.9 with off-diagonal values around ~0.1, significantly outperforming all baselines (iVAE, TCL, TDRL, NCTRL, DreamerV3, TD-MPC2) in both accuracy and consistency (minimal standard deviation across three runs). On Meta-World, WM3C (MAE) demonstrates the best data efficiency during multi-task training across 18 tasks (5M steps), with particularly strong gains on tasks like box-close and door-unlock where DreamerV3 struggles. During adaptation to 9 unseen tasks (250K steps), WM3C MAE consistently achieves higher success rates than full-parameter fine-tuned DreamerV3 on 7 of 9 tasks, with the two exceptions involving genuinely novel components (coffee-button, handle-press-side).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WM3C outperforms all seven baselines on the synthetic component identification task (R² metric), with the gap being most pronounced against DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2, which produce entangled representations that do not prioritize distinct latent components. iVAE underperforms because it uses a standard domain index rather than language components, while TDRL fails to recognize composable components tied to language due to its independent noise assumption, and NCTRL's hidden Markov model prior is too simplistic for complex latent structure changes. On Meta-World training, WM3C (both CNN and MAE variants) surpasses DreamerV3 and MT-SAC in average success rate, with the MAE variant providing further improvements through better high-level semantic extraction. On adaptation to unseen tasks, WM3C MAE (fine-tuning only the dynamics module) beats full-parameter fine-tuned DreamerV3 on 7 of 9 tasks, confirming that compositional dynamics adaptation is more sample-efficient than full retraining. However, on the two tasks containing genuinely novel components (coffee-button with unknown object, handle-press-side with unknown verb variant), WM3C does not consistently outperform DreamerV3, suggesting that dynamics-only adaptation has limits when entirely new components are introduced.

## Relation to Prior Work

WM3C builds on the DreamerV3 world model architecture and the causal representation learning literature (nonlinear ICA, temporal causal discovery methods like TDRL and NCTRL), but departs from them in two key ways: it replaces dimension-wise identifiability with block-wise identifiability, and it uses multiple language components as separate auxiliary variables each controlling their own latent block, rather than a single auxiliary variable connecting to all latents. The integration of masked autoencoders for semantic extraction and mutual information constraints (MINE) for enforcing conditional independence are practical additions not present in prior causal RL work. The paper positions itself as the first to demonstrate identifiability of disentangled components separately controlled by different intermittent control signals in a general nonlinear RL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The block-wise identifiability theorem provides a practical theoretical guarantee that composable causal components can be identified with as few as n_{c_i}+1 values per language component, significantly reducing the data requirements compared to prior approaches. The finding that only fine-tuning the dynamics module (rather than the full world model) suffices for adaptation to new task compositions has direct practical implications for efficient transfer in robotic manipulation. However, the framework currently assumes a clean verb-object decomposition, Markov faithfulness, and invertible observation functions, limiting applicability to more complex or ambiguous language instructions. Open questions include scaling to hierarchical causal structures, offline learning, and environments where language components overlap or are ambiguous.
