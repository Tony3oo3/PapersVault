## Problem

Existing LVLM benchmarks rely on images from public datasets, creating a data-leakage risk that inflates scores, and they evaluate only on realistic-style images under clean conditions. Multi-stylized images and noisy scenarios (corruption, print attacks, adversarial attacks) remain largely unexplored for fine-grained perceptual tasks, leaving a gap in understanding model robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252326/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample pairs a 1024×1024 synthesized image with a text question in one of three formats: multi-choice (3–4 options), true-or-false, or free-form captioning. Closed-ended questions yield accuracy scores (random baselines of 25% and 50% respectively), while free-form questions are scored via SentenceTransformer cosine similarity between the model's caption and the original generation prompt. The released benchmark contains 617K QA pairs across 20 perceptual subtasks, 51 artistic styles, 4 image scenarios, and 3 question types, with 289K unique images and 162K unique questions.

## Method

Dysca employs a bottom-up, rule-based generation pipeline that decouples each image prompt into four metadata components (attribute, foreground, style, background), assembles them via pre-designed templates, and synthesizes images with SDXL (or TextDiffusion2 for the OCR subtask). Because the ground-truth content is known from the prompt, QA pairs are generated deterministically without LLM annotation, eliminating hallucinated labels. A four-stage cleaning procedure (manual metadata pruning, CLIP-L-14 filtering at 0.75 threshold, PP-OCRv3 text verification, and top-6-model consensus filtering) removes ~40% of low-quality samples. Four scenarios are constructed by applying corruption (from MMCBench), print attacks (deceptive wrong-option text overlaid on the image), and PGD adversarial perturbations (using InstructBLIP as a white-box proxy). Two novel diagnostic metrics are introduced: sensitivity to question type (SQ), measuring inconsistency between multi-choice and true-or-false performance, and sensitivity to covariate shift (SC), measuring variance across the 51 image styles.

## Results

On Dysca (617K QA pairs, 20 perceptual subtasks), GLM-4V achieves the highest clean-scenario score of 82.09, followed by XComposer-VL (InternLM2-7B) at 79.13, MiniCPM-v2.5 at 78.75, and Gemini-1.5-Pro at 77.79. Under print attacking, mPLUG-Owl-2 shows the greatest robustness (only 1.7% drop), while GPT-4o suffers a 25.8% decline (75.69→56.34) and GLM-4V drops 36.5% (82.09→52.09). In the adversarial attacking scenario, models sharing EVA-CLIP encoders (e.g., XComposer-VL) experience the largest degradation (up to 57.6% drop). All 26 models score near random (~35%) in the blind setting, confirming effective mitigation of data leakage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper validates Dysca's evaluation rankings against three established non-synthetic benchmarks using Spearman (ρ) and Kendall (τ) rank correlation. Against OCRBench, Dysca shows the strongest agreement (ρ=0.90, τ=0.80), and against MMBench it achieves ρ=0.70, τ=0.60, both exceeding the 0.6 significance threshold. Against SeedBench-2, the correlation is weaker (ρ=0.46, τ=0.43) because SeedBench-2 uses only realistic images; restricting Dysca to its realistic-style subset improves this to ρ=0.64, τ=0.62. The paper further demonstrates via KMMD distribution distance that Dysca's image distribution sits between real-image benchmarks (COCO, MME) and stylized benchmarks (MMStar, MME), supporting its representativeness. In terms of benchmark features (Table 1), Dysca uniquely combines automatic annotation, novel synthesized images, all three question types (multi-choice, true-or-false, free-form), and 20 perceptual subtasks at a 617K scale, surpassing all prior benchmarks in coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dysca extends the LVLM evaluation benchmark lineage (MME, MMBench, SEED-Bench, etc.) by replacing real-image selection with a fully synthetic, rule-based generation pipeline built on Stable Diffusion XL. It departs from JourneyDB, the prior synthetic-image benchmark, by adopting a bottom-up, dynamically scalable architecture with rule-based QA generation instead of a fixed top-down set of Midjourney images labeled by ChatGPT, thereby avoiding hallucinated annotations. The multi-scenario evaluation (corruption, print attack, adversarial attack) builds on robustness studies like MMCBench and the Typographic Dataset but generalizes them across 20 perceptual subtasks simultaneously rather than in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating strong Spearman/Kendall correlations with established real-image benchmarks (ρ=0.90 with OCRBench, ρ=0.70 with MMBench), Dysca provides the first systematic validation that large-scale synthetic data can serve as a fair, leakage-free proxy for LVLM evaluation. The benchmark's dynamic, template-driven design means new subtasks, styles, or attack scenarios can be added without manual annotation, lowering the barrier for future evaluation. Open questions remain: the weaker correlation with SeedBench-2 (ρ=0.46) suggests synthetic images may not fully capture distributional nuances of real-world content, and whether the 51-style coverage adequately represents the full diversity of visual inputs LVLMs encounter in deployment.
