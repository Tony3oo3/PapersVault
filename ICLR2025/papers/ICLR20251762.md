## Problem

Rehearsal-based continual learning methods reduce catastrophic forgetting by replaying a small buffer of past samples, but forgetting persists because the model's output distribution for previously seen data can shift dramatically under small parameter updates. The stability of model predictions within a local parameter neighborhood has been largely unexplored: if outputs are locally stable, future updates in any direction are less likely to cause forgetting. STAR addresses this gap by explicitly regularizing the worst-case change in output distribution over a local parameter neighborhood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251762/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

STAR operates on RGB images (32×32 for Split-CIFAR10/100, 84×84 for Split-miniImageNet) in a class-incremental setting with a single shared classification head, producing a class label per image. It requires only a memory buffer of previously seen samples (managed via reservoir sampling) and does not need task boundaries or task labels during training. The method is applied as an additional loss term on top of any existing rehearsal-based CL baseline (ER, ER-ACE, DER++, X-DER-RPC), so the input/output interface of the underlying method is unchanged.

## Method

STAR defines a forgetting surrogate as the KL-divergence between the model's output probability distributions at the current parameters θ_t and future parameters θ_{t+s}, restricted to correctly classified buffer samples. Since future parameters are unavailable, it optimizes the worst-case version of this surrogate over a bounded local parameter neighborhood (‖δ‖₂ ≤ d). The perturbation δ is computed via a single gradient ascent step on the KL-divergence, with layer-wise normalization (scaling each layer's perturbation by ‖θ^(l)‖₂/‖g^(l)‖₂) to account for differing weight scales. The gradient for updating θ is approximated by evaluating the loss gradient at the perturbed point (following AWP/SAM), avoiding Hessian computation. The final training objective is L_CL + λ·L_STAR, making STAR a plug-and-play regularizer that steers parameters toward flat regions of the output-distribution landscape, giving future updates more flexibility without hindering plasticity.

## Results

STAR achieves up to 15.11% absolute improvement in average accuracy over the ER baseline on Split-CIFAR10 (buffer 100: 36.39 → 51.5). When compared against other plug-and-play enhancement methods on the ER-ACE baseline, STAR attains the best average accuracy in 7 out of 9 dataset/buffer configurations (e.g., 60.69% vs. 57.18% for OCM on Split-CIFAR10 buffer 100; 75.44% vs. 72.35% for DualHSIC on Split-CIFAR10 buffer 500). On the DER++ baseline, STAR similarly leads in most settings (e.g., 61.76% vs. 59.25% for OCM on Split-CIFAR10 buffer 100; 76.52% vs. 74.34% for DualHSIC on Split-CIFAR10 buffer 500), with DualHSIC or LiDER narrowly winning on a few Split-CIFAR100 and Split-miniImageNet configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR outperforms all five competing enhancement methods (sSGD, oEWC, OCM, LiDER, DualHSIC) on the ER-ACE baseline in 7 of 9 settings, with the largest margin over OCM on Split-CIFAR10 buffer 100 (60.69% vs. 57.18%, +3.51%) and over DualHSIC on Split-CIFAR10 buffer 500 (75.44% vs. 72.35%, +3.09%). On the DER++ baseline, STAR leads in 6 of 9 settings, with the clearest win over OCM on Split-CIFAR10 buffer 100 (61.76% vs. 59.25%, +2.51%) and over DualHSIC on Split-CIFAR10 buffer 500 (76.52% vs. 74.34%, +2.18%). STAR loses narrowly to DualHSIC on Split-CIFAR100 buffer 200 with ER-ACE (30.38% vs. 30.97%) and to LiDER on Split-miniImageNet buffer 5000 with DER++ (33.36% vs. 35.04%), but these gaps are within 1.7%. sSGD and oEWC are consistently the weakest enhancements, with sSGD actually degrading DER++ performance on several settings (e.g., 16.31% vs. 18.02% on Split-miniImageNet buffer 1000). The paper also demonstrates that STAR improves even the simplest ER baseline by up to 15.11% absolute accuracy (36.39% → 51.5% on Split-CIFAR10 buffer 100), confirming its plug-and-play utility across method complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR adapts the worst-case weight perturbation framework from robustness (AWP, Wu et al. 2020) and generalization (SAM, Foret et al. 2021) to the continual learning setting, specifically repurposing their layer-wise normalized gradient perturbation and the gradient-at-perturbed-point approximation for SGD. Within CL, it departs from prior regularization-based enhancements (EWC, OCM, DualHSIC, LiDER) by targeting the stability of the model's output distribution over correctly classified buffer samples in a local parameter neighborhood, rather than penalizing weight changes, maximizing mutual information, or constraining Lipschitz constants. The paper positions itself as orthogonal to buffer management strategies (reservoir sampling) and task-identification mechanisms, making it a truly plug-and-play add-on to any rehearsal-based baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAR provides a simple, task-agnostic regularization that consistently improves multiple rehearsal-based CL methods by up to ~15% without requiring task boundaries or architectural changes, which is practically valuable for practitioners already using ER, DER++, or X-DER. The worst-case perturbation framing offers a principled connection between local parameter-space geometry and forgetting, suggesting that flatter loss regions around correctly classified samples are a useful inductive bias for CL. However, the computational overhead (~2× training time) and the reliance on a gradient approximation that degrades with more perturbation steps limit immediate adoption, and the evaluation is confined to class-incremental settings on relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10/100, miniImageNet), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or more challenging scenarios like domain-incremental or task-incremental CL.
