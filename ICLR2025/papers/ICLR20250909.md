## Problem

Current Dual-RL methods for offline RL suffer from two critical flaws: semi-gradient updates cause them to learn an action distribution ratio rather than the true state-action visitation distribution ratio, and the learned ratio is regularized by the offline data distribution, preventing it from approaching the optimal discriminator weight. This limits Dual-RL's ability to extract performant policies via weighted behavior cloning, causing it to consistently lag behind Primal-RL baselines despite its in-sample learning advantage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') transitions and iteratively refines it by filtering out zero-weight suboptimal transitions at each iteration. The final output is a unimodal Gaussian policy extracted via weighted behavior cloning on the last filtered subdataset, weighted by the learned state-action visitation distribution ratio. The iterative filtering means the effective dataset shrinks over iterations, and the method uses χ²-divergence as the f-divergence with a single hyperparameter λ controlling the regularization strength.

## Method

IDRL addresses both Dual-RL limitations through a two-stage correction and iterative self-distillation. First, it recovers the true state-action visitation distribution ratio by decomposing the problem: semi-gradient Dual-RL yields the action distribution ratio w*(a|s), which is then corrected via an off-policy evaluation formulation (using Fenchel-Rockafellar duality) that jointly learns auxiliary networks U and W to produce the state-level ratio w*(s), giving the full ratio w*(s,a) = w*(s)·w*(a|s). Second, it iteratively filters the dataset to retain only transitions with non-zero visitation ratio, then re-runs Dual-RL on the refined subdataset, effectively replacing the behavior visitation distribution with the previous iteration's regularized optimal visitation distribution. This curriculum produces a monotonic improvement guarantee (Theorem 4) and a tighter performance bound (Theorem 3) compared to single-pass Dual-RL, progressively approaching the optimal discriminator weight without requiring an external expert dataset.

## Results

On D4RL benchmarks, IDRL achieves the highest normalized scores across nearly all 18 tasks, with particularly large gains on challenging Antmaze navigation (e.g., 99.5 on antmaze-umaze vs. 97.8 for ReBRAC and 94.1 for O-DICE) and Kitchen tasks (80.5 on kitchen-complete vs. 77.2 for ReBRAC). On Mujoco locomotion, IDRL scores 58.4/99.5/92.8 on medium, 58.0/101.7/84.4 on medium-replay, and 98.1/111.0/113.9 on medium-expert (halfcheetah/hopper/walker2d), consistently outperforming both Primal-RL and Dual-RL baselines. On corrupted demonstrations (1–10% expert ratio), IDRL with 3 iterations substantially outperforms naive BC, advantage-weighted BC, and density-weighted approaches, recovering near-optimal policies even when 99% of transitions are random.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDRL outperforms the SOTA Primal-RL method ReBRAC on 16 of 18 tasks, with the largest margins on Antmaze (e.g., +9.0 on antmaze-medium-play: 93.0 vs. 84.0) and halfcheetah-medium-replay (+7.0: 58.0 vs. 51.0). Against the SOTA Dual-RL method O-DICE, IDRL wins on 15 of 18 tasks, again with the biggest gaps on Antmaze navigation (+5.4 on umaze, +7.0 on medium-play) and halfcheetah-medium-replay (+14.0: 58.0 vs. 44.0). IDRL also surpasses CQL, TD3+BC, IQL, SQL, and Diffusion-QL on the vast majority of tasks, confirming that in-sample value learning with correct visitation-ratio estimation is more effective than both action-level regularization and diffusion-based policy extraction. On the corrupted-demonstrations experiment (1–10% expert ratio), IDRL with 3 iterations dramatically outperforms naive BC, advantage-weighted BC, and the density-weighted + advantage-weighted (DW+AW) reweighting approach from Hong et al. (2023b), recovering near-expert performance even when 99% of transitions are random. The one task where IDRL does not clearly lead is antmaze-large-diverse (54.2 vs. 54.6 for O-DICE), where the extremely sparse expert signal limits all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Dual-RL / Distribution Correction Estimation (DICE) framework (Sikchi et al., 2023; Mao et al., 2024a), which reformulates offline RL as maximizing reward over visitation distributions with an f-divergence regularization. The key departure is a diagnostic finding: standard semi-gradient Dual-RL actually learns an action distribution ratio π*(a|s)/μ(a|s) rather than the intended state-action visitation ratio d*(s,a)/d^D(s,a), a distinction the authors formalize in Proposition 1. They correct this via an off-policy evaluation step (Theorem 1) and then propose iterative self-distillation to break the data-regularization barrier, drawing on discriminator-weighted imitation theory (Xu et al., 2022b) for the performance bound. The approach also connects to recent work on corrupted/imbalanced offline datasets (Hong et al., 2023b) but replaces trajectory-level reweighting with transition-level iterative filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification that semi-gradient Dual-RL learns the wrong ratio is a meaningful conceptual correction for the DICE community, and the iterative filtering approach provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism to escape the regularization barrier that plagues all offline RL methods. The method's ability to recover near-optimal policies from heavily corrupted demonstrations (1% expert data) where all prior offline RL methods fail suggests practical relevance for real-world settings where data quality is heterogeneous. Limitations include increased training time from multiple iterations and potential generalization issues with small datasets; extending IDRL to the online setting is identified as a natural next step.
