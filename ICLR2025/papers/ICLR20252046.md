## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently produce hallucinated responses that are syntactically plausible yet visually ungrounded. The paper identifies that these hallucinations are not uniformly distributed across layers but manifest as localized surges in the later layers, where the model 'overthinks' and shifts away from correct visual information already extracted in earlier layers. Existing decoding-based mitigations (VCD, DOLA, OPERA) only correct the final-layer logits without addressing the progressive corruption of hidden states during the forward pass.

## Input / Output

For LVLMs, the method takes an RGB image paired with a text question (e.g., VQA) and produces a text answer; for LLM transfer experiments, it takes a text prompt and produces a text response. Crucially, DAMO operates entirely within a single forward pass—modifying hidden states in-place at each layer—so it requires no additional forward passes, no external memory, and no retraining. Memory consumption is nearly identical to regular decoding (e.g., 14.6 GB for LLaVA1.5), in contrast to VCD (15.6 GB, two forward passes) or OPERA (up to 48.8 GB with beam search).

## Method

DAMO adapts the momentum update from gradient-based optimization to the forward computation of a transformer. At each layer j, the raw activation ∇h_t^{j+1} = f_j(h_t^j) is not added directly to the hidden state but is instead blended into a momentum buffer: v_t^{j+1} = βv_t^j + (1−β)∇h_t^{j+1}, and the hidden state is updated as h_t^{j+1} = h_t^j + v_t^{j+1}, thereby smoothing the update trajectory and resisting abrupt later-layer deviations. An adaptive activation refinement mechanism monitors the cosine similarity between the per-layer early-exit distribution change ∇P^j and its own momentum ∇P̂^j; when this similarity drops below a threshold τ, refinement is triggered, signaling the onset of a hallucination surge. Upon triggering, the momentum coefficient switches from a lower β₁ to a higher β₂, preserving more historical (visually grounded) information and diluting the hallucinatory activations introduced by subsequent layers. The entire procedure is training-free, requires no extra forward passes, and introduces only negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On the MME benchmark, DAMO achieves total scores of 1515.89 (LLaVA1.5), 1520.74 (INF-MLLM1), and 1437.46 (mPLUG-Owl2), outperforming all baselines on two of three models and matching OPERA on LLaVA1.5. On POPE (MSCOCO popular setting, mPLUG-Owl2), DAMO improves accuracy from 80.73% to 82.77% and F1 from 82.52% to 83.80% over regular decoding. On LLaVA-Bench, DAMO achieves an overall score of 78.13, surpassing DOLA (77.17) and significantly outperforming OPERA (64.17). When transferred to LLaMA2-7B, DAMO improves TruthfulQA MC1 to 34.15 and FACTOR Wiki to 57.48, both exceeding Regular and DOLA baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAMO consistently outperforms VCD across all three models on MME, with the largest margin on mPLUG-Owl2 (+38.91 points: 1437.46 vs. 1398.55); VCD also shows accuracy declines on POPE (e.g., −1.80% on LLaVA1.5 MSCOCO popular). Against DOLA, DAMO wins on MME for LLaVA1.5 (+23.78) and mPLUG-Owl2 (+10.42), while DOLA shows zero improvement over Regular on INF-MLLM1 (both 1492.71); on LLaVA-Bench, DAMO (78.13) edges out DOLA (77.17). Compared to OPERA, DAMO achieves a higher total on INF-MLLM1 (+27.59) and mPLUG-Owl2 (+8.66), though OPERA slightly leads on LLaVA1.5 MME (1518.36 vs. 1515.89); however, DAMO dramatically outperforms OPERA on LLaVA-Bench (78.13 vs. 64.17) and uses far less memory (14.6 GB vs. up to 48.8 GB for OPERA on INF-MLLM1). On the LLM transfer experiments, DAMO surpasses both Regular and DOLA on TruthfulQA MC1 (34.15 vs. 33.66/33.29) and FACTOR Wiki (57.48 vs. 56.91/56.51), confirming cross-domain transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAMO repurposes the momentum update rule from gradient-based optimization (Polyak, 1964; Sutskever et al., 2013) and transplants it into the forward computation of transformers, building on the theoretical observation (Ahn et al., 2024) that a transformer's forward pass is conditionally equivalent to gradient descent steps. It departs from the dominant line of hallucination-mitigation decoding methods—VCD, DOLA, IBD, OPERA—which all operate by contrasting or adjusting logits at the final layer, by instead modifying hidden states layer-by-layer during the forward pass using an accumulated activation momentum. The early-exit literature (Teerapittayanon et al., 2016; Schuster et al., 2022) provides the diagnostic tool that reveals the key empirical observation: LVLMs extract correct visual semantics in early layers but 'overthink' and deviate in later layers, motivating the targeted, adaptive refinement strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, low-memory decoding strategy that requires no architectural changes or additional forward passes, DAMO is immediately deployable on existing LVLMs and even transfers to pure LLMs, making it a practical tool for practitioners concerned about hallucination without retraining. The layer-wise diagnostic (75% of hallucinated samples show correct early-layer predictions) offers a useful mechanistic explanation that could inform future training or architecture design. However, the method's effectiveness is sensitive to model-specific hyperparameters (τ, β₁, β₂) that must be tuned per model and task, and the improvements, while consistent, are moderate in absolute terms (e.g., ~24 points on LLaVA1.5 MME, <1 point on LLM transfer benchmarks), leaving open whether the momentum analogy can be made more principled or whether it generalizes to larger, more heterogeneous model families.
