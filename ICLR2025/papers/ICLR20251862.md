## Problem

Existing imitation learning policies for robot manipulation struggle to generalize across spatial and visual variations because they rely on a single input modality (typically RGB images) and a single action representation (per-timestep dense actions). Image-based policies memorize scene-specific details, while point-cloud-based policies lack the resolution for precise manipulation. The underexplored question is how to design a policy that dynamically switches between different input modalities and action representations during execution to exploit the strengths of each for different manipulation phases.

## Input / Output

The waypoint policy consumes a colorized 3D point cloud (downsampled to 1024 points via farthest-point-sampling) and outputs a per-point saliency probability, a 3D translational offset relative to the salient point, plus point-agnostic predictions for rotation (Euler angles), binary gripper state, and next mode. The dense policy consumes a single wrist-camera RGB image (processed by ResNet-18) concatenated with proprioception, and outputs a 7-DoF end-effector action augmented with a mode-prediction dimension. At inference, the active mode determines which policy is queried; waypoints are executed by a linear-interpolation controller over k steps, while dense actions are executed one step at a time.

## Method

SPHINX introduces a hybrid architecture with two specialized policies: a GPT-2-style Transformer (6 layers, 512-dim, no positional embedding) over point-cloud tokens that predicts salient points via a soft probability map (Gaussian-like decay within a 5 cm radius) and regresses the waypoint position as an offset from the salient point rather than an absolute coordinate, and a Diffusion Policy (DDPM with 1-D UNet) over wrist images for precise local manipulation. Both policies predict the next execution mode (waypt/dense/terminate), enabling learned switching between long-range reaching and fine-grained insertion. Training uses a custom web-based data collection interface where demonstrators click salient points in a live point-cloud view and drag a virtual gripper to specify waypoints, eliminating manual teleoperation of long-range motions. Temporal augmentation expands each waypoint specification into multiple training examples by including interpolated controller steps (α=0.2), increasing effective dataset size ~6×. The full waypoint loss is an unweighted sum of cross-entropy (saliency), masked MSE (offset on salient points only), MSE (rotation), BCE (gripper), and NLL (mode).

## Results

SPHINX achieves 86.7% average success across 4 real-world tasks (Drawer, Cup Stack, Train Track, Coffee Making) and 2 simulated tasks (Can, Square), outperforming the next best state-of-the-art IL baseline by 41.1% on average across 440 real-world trials. On waypoint-only tasks, SPHINX achieves 1.6× better performance than the best SoTA image-based or 3D-based diffusion policy (e.g., 93.5% on Can vs. 78% for the best baseline; 86.5% on Square vs. 65.5%). SPHINX also achieves a 1.7× execution speedup over diffusion policy (9.2 s vs. 15.6 s on Cup Stack) and generalizes to unseen viewpoints, visual distractors, and spatial arrangements (e.g., 9/10 on novel viewpoint vs. 0/10 for DP on Cup Stack).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPHINX outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated task. Against Diffusion Policy (DP), the strongest dense-only baseline, SPHINX achieves substantially higher success on all three challenging real-world tasks (Cup Stack, Train Track, Coffee Making) and dominates on OOD generalization (e.g., 9/10 vs. 0/10 on Cup Stack with a novel viewpoint; 9/10 vs. 4/10 on Drawer at unseen heights). Against HYDRA, the hybrid waypoint+dense baseline using only images, SPHINX wins because HYDRA's waypoint head lacks salient-point grounding and point-cloud spatial context, leading to inaccurate long-range predictions. Against fine-tuned OpenVLA, SPHINX wins because OpenVLA cannot use wrist-camera close-ups or mode switching, making it unable to achieve the required precision for insertion and alignment sub-tasks. Against 3D Diffusion Policy (point-cloud-based), SPHINX achieves 1.6× better success on waypoint-only tasks (Can and Square), benefiting from salient-point-anchored offset prediction rather than direct pose regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPHINX builds on the hybrid action space paradigm introduced by HYDRA (Belkhale et al., 2023) and AWE (Shi et al., 2023), which decouple long-range waypoint actions from per-timestep dense actions. It extends this by introducing a hybrid *input* modality as well: point clouds feed the waypoint policy while wrist-camera images feed the dense policy (instantiated as Diffusion Policy, Chi et al., 2023), with salient points serving as the semantic bridge between the two. The salient-point grounding mechanism draws inspiration from per-point action prediction in grasping (Saxena et al., 2008; Sundermeyer et al., 2021) and keypoint-conditioned policies (Sundaresan et al., 2023), but generalizes it to arbitrary manipulation tasks by learning which points are task-relevant rather than relying on segmentation or fixed object models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPHINX demonstrates that combining viewpoint-agnostic 3D inputs with close-up 2D inputs, gated by learned salient points, yields a 41.1% average improvement over the next-best IL baseline and robust generalization to novel viewpoints, visual distractors, spatial rearrangements, and execution speeds—axes where prior IL policies typically fail. The ablation revealing that waypoint prediction *without* salient-point grounding underperforms dense-only diffusion policies is a useful caution: shorter action horizons alone do not confer spatial generalization. Open questions include extending the linear waypoint controller to dynamic, non-quasistatic motions, incorporating tactile or force sensing for the 'last mile' of precise insertion, and scaling the approach beyond a single Franka Panda platform to multi-robot or in-the-wild deployment.
