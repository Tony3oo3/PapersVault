## Problem

Existing speech-driven 3D talking face methods rely on discrete emotion labels applied globally across entire sequences, leaving fine-grained spatiotemporal control of localized muscle movements (e.g., a sudden eyebrow raise mid-sentence) unexplored. Directly conditioning a generative model on speech audio, coarse-grained emotion, and fine-grained action units simultaneously causes condition entanglement that degrades lip synchronization and prevents precise localized control.

## Input / Output

The method consumes speech audio (encoded by a frozen Wav2Vec2 model, interpolated to 25 fps), coarse-grained conditions (talking style from a facial image via CLIP, emotion label as CLIP text embedding, and a scalar intensity factor), and fine-grained conditions structured as triplets of binary AU vectors with start/end frame timestamps. It outputs a sequence of 51-dimensional ARKit blendshape coefficients at 25 fps representing the full 3D facial animation. A lightweight text-based AU detector (CLIP-Adapter + classification head) optionally converts free-form natural language descriptions into the AU triplet format, enabling language-driven fine-grained control.

## Method

Cafe-Talk uses a two-stage diffusion-transformer pipeline: Stage 1 trains a DiT-based generator with speech audio injected via FiLM cross-attention (with a diagonal alignment mask for lip sync) and coarse-grained conditions via AdaLN self-attention; Stage 2 freezes the base model and adds a fine-grained control adapter (FiLM self-attention followed by a zero-initialized convolution) that gradually incorporates AU conditions without disrupting learned lip movements. A swap-label training mechanism replaces the coarse-grained emotion label with a conflicting one during Stage 2, forcing the adapter to learn AU-driven control independent of the emotion label, while a spatiotemporal mask restricts the loss to AU-activated regions. At inference, a mask-based classifier-free guidance technique applies the fine-grained condition only within user-specified temporal windows (preventing leakage to neighboring frames) and uses a guidance scale β to control expression intensity. A CLIP-Adapter-based text detector, trained with binary cross-entropy plus InfoNCE contrastive loss on GPT-generated and AffectNet-sourced text-AU pairs, bridges natural language descriptions to the AU condition space.

## Results

On the MEAD test set, Cafe-Talk achieves the best lip synchronization (LVE 7.21) and dramatically outperforms all baselines in emotion accuracy (59.48% vs. 12.44% for UniTalker and 9.97% for EmoTalk) and expression diversity (119.36 vs. 12.64 and 62.02). On RAVDESS, it attains the lowest LVE (3.93) and highest SyncC (3.95). On the LibriSpeech benchmark, Cafe-Talk achieves the best SyncC (5.55) while maintaining competitive SyncD (9.76), confirming strong generalization to unseen audio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cafe-Talk outperforms all five baselines on lip synchronization (LVE) across both MEAD and RAVDESS, with the largest margins over FaceFormer (16.36→7.21 on MEAD) and EMOTE (14.67→3.93 on RAVDESS). Against the two most directly comparable ARKit-based methods, EmoTalk and UniTalker, Cafe-Talk reduces MEAD LVE from 7.90/10.14 to 7.21 and RAVDESS LVE from 4.31/4.93 to 3.93. The emotion accuracy gap is particularly striking: Cafe-Talk reaches 59.48% on MEAD versus only 9.97% (EmoTalk) and 12.44% (UniTalker), while expression diversity jumps from 62.02/12.64 to 119.36, reflecting the diffusion-based architecture's ability to generate varied expressions. On the LibriSpeech benchmark, Cafe-Talk achieves the highest SyncC (5.55 vs. 4.72 for UniTalker and 3.30 for EmoTalk), though its SyncD (9.76) is marginally worse than UniTalker's (9.70), suggesting a slight trade-off in lip-sync tightness when fine-grained control is active.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cafe-Talk builds on the diffusion-transformer (DiT) architecture for talking face generation established by Ng et al. (2024) and Zhao et al. (2024), and adopts CLIP-based encoding for coarse-grained emotion/style conditions following Xu et al. (2023) and Ma et al. (2023). Its key architectural departure is the two-stage training pipeline with a ControlNet-style zero-convolution adapter (Zhang et al., 2023a) to inject fine-grained AU conditions without disrupting lip sync, combined with a novel swap-label training mechanism that has no direct precedent in prior talking face work. While prior methods (Ma et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2024, Sun et al. 2024) use AUs to refine emotion labels in a temporally consistent manner, Cafe-Talk repositions AUs as truly spatiotemporally localized control signals that can conflict with and override coarse-grained conditions, extending the mask-based classifier-free guidance of Ho & Salimans (2021) to enforce this dominance at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve true spatiotemporal fine-grained control in 3D talking face generation, opening the door to animator-friendly and LLM-agent-driven facial animation where specific muscle movements can be directed at precise moments. The swap-label training mechanism and mask-based CFG are generalizable techniques for disentangling entangled conditions in any multi-condition generative model. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that lower-face AUs in conflict with speech audio still produce inaccurate lip movements, leaving open the question of how to harmonize fine-grained lower-face control with phonetic constraints. The broader implication is toward real-time AI agents that can express nuanced, temporally-varying emotions driven by natural language instructions.
