## Problem

Rank-based measures of correlation such as Chatterjee's coefficient and the FOCI feature selection algorithm are non-parametric and non-differentiable, rendering them inapplicable to gradient-based neural network training. While FOCI provides a strong, model-free feature selection baseline, its step-wise nature limits it to O(p²) complexity and prevents simultaneous optimization over all features, and its discrete rank operations block end-to-end learning with neural networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standardized feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×p}, a response variable Y ∈ R^n, and optionally group attributes X_G or sensitive attributes X_S. In the vec parameterization, it outputs a real-valued weight vector θ ∈ R^p whose magnitudes encode each feature's predictive informativeness (with a cutoff v for binary selection); in the NN parameterization, it outputs a trained network f_θ that maps inputs to learned features. The method operates in three modes: (dF1) maximizing rank-based dependence between Y and f_θ(X) for feature selection, (dF2) adding T_{n,β}(X_G, f_θ(X)) as a regularizer to a standard loss, and (dF3) maximizing conditional dependence T_{n,β}(Y, f_θ(X)|X_S) for fairness.

## Method

The core contribution is a differentiable relaxation T_{n,β} of Azadkia & Chatterjee's conditional dependence estimator T_n, obtained by replacing hard nearest-neighbor rank lookups (r_{N(i)}, r_{M(i)}) with softmax-weighted rank aggregations (r^T S_β, r^T U_β) over pairwise Euclidean distance matrices. A theorem establishes that lim_{n→∞} lim_{β→∞} T_{n,β} = T almost surely, preserving all four natural properties (normalization, independence, complete dependence, scale invariance). The parameterization f_θ can be a simple dot product θ ⊙ X (vec) or a multi-layer perceptron (NN), enabling the method to learn nonlinear and basis transformations of features. Training uses standard gradient descent (Adam) on the differentiable objective, with fixed hyperparameters β=5 (softmax temperature) and v=0.1 (cutoff), reducing the tuning burden to conventional optimization parameters. This differentiability allows simultaneous optimization over all features and integration as a regularizer or conditioning term within arbitrary neural network training pipelines.

## Results

On real-world feature selection (Table 2), vec-(dF1) and NN-(dF1) achieve the lowest logistic test loss on 4 out of 5 UCI datasets (e.g., 2.56 on Spambase vs. 2.92 for FPR/FDR/FWE; 2.11 on QSAR vs. 2.16 for LDA/PCA; 9.61 on Toxicity vs. 10.00 for PCA). On the Waterbirds spurious-correlation benchmark, difFOCI+ERM improves worst-group accuracy from 60.0% (ERM) to 85.7%, and difFOCI+DRO improves it from 76.9% (DRO) to 87.2%, while maintaining ~93–94% average accuracy. In fairness experiments, (dF3) reduces test accuracy on the sensitive attribute by 7–22 percentage points (e.g., from 71.2% to 48.9% on Student data) while preserving or slightly improving target accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FOCI (the non-differentiable original), difFOCI consistently achieves lower test loss on all five UCI feature-selection datasets (e.g., 2.56 vs. 3.04 on Spambase; 2.11 vs. 3.44 on QSAR), confirming that the differentiable relaxation preserves FOCI's strengths while adding the ability to account for feature variability. Compared to scikit-learn feature-selection baselines (GUS, SelectPercentile, FPR, FDR, FWE, SelectKBest), both vec-(dF1) and NN-(dF1) outperform all of them on Spambase, Toxicity, and QSAR, and match the best (K.B. at 0.00) on Breast Cancer. Against dimensionality-reduction methods (UMAP, LDA, PCA), difFOCI wins on Spambase, Toxicity, and QSAR but is slightly behind K.B. on Religious (0.48 vs. 0.48 tie) and matches on Breast Cancer. On the Waterbirds spurious-correlation task, difFOCI+ERM (85.7% worst-group) and difFOCI+DRO (87.2%) substantially outperform plain ERM (60.0%) and DRO (76.9%), and are competitive with or slightly exceed DRO(e.s.+strong l2) (86.0%). Across the six-dataset worst-group-accuracy benchmark (Table 4), difFOCI+ERM/DRO matches or exceeds DRO on MultiNLI (77.6/77.5 vs. 77.0), CelebA (89.3/89.8 vs. 88.8), and MetaShift (83.1/91.7 vs. 86.2), while trailing DRO on CheXpert (54.4/75.3 vs. 73.9) and NICO++ (47.1/46.3 vs. 38.3/43.1 for Mixup), and is broadly competitive with JTT, Mixup, and IRM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper makes the non-parametric, rank-based conditional dependence estimator T_n from Azadkia & Chatterjee (2021, FOCI) differentiable by replacing hard nearest-neighbor lookups with softmax-weighted rank aggregations, yielding difFOCI. The theoretical lineage traces back through Chatterjee's (2020) rank-based correlation coefficient to Dette et al.'s (2013) copula-based regression dependence measure, and ultimately to Rényi correlation. The key departure from prior work is that difFOCI is the first parametric framework to directly optimize a rank-based objective end-to-end, whereas existing rank-based feature learning methods (Kuo & Hsu, 2017; Wojtas & Chen, 2020) rely on indirect two-network architectures. The paper positions itself as bridging a gap between well-studied statistical rank methods and practical deep learning, where non-smoothness previously prevented direct use.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The differentiable relaxation provides a theoretically grounded (Theorem 4: convergence to the original estimator) plug-in objective that can serve as a feature selection criterion, a spurious-correlation regularizer, or a fairness conditioning term within standard neural network training. This unification under a single rank-based framework is practically useful, though the real-world gains over strong baselines (DRO, IRM, Mixup) are competitive rather than dramatically superior. Open questions include scalability of the O(n²) pairwise distance computation to very large batches, whether the fixed softmax temperature β=5 is truly universal, and whether the fairness application (dF3) can be made more principled than the authors' own 'heuristic' characterization. The method also requires group or sensitive attribute labels for its dF2/dF3 variants, limiting applicability in label-free settings.
