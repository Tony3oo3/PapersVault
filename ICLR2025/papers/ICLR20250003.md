## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering but requires storing millions of explicit Gaussian attributes, easily exceeding 1 GB per scene. Existing compression methods either rely on global statistics (quantization, image codecs) that degrade quality when compression is aggressive, or use anchor-based neural representations that add 30%+ rendering overhead. No prior method fully exploits the local spatial coherence of all Gaussian attributes while preserving the original 3DGS rendering pipeline's speed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of RGB images with corresponding camera intrinsics/extrinsics as training input, and additionally generates a dense 1M-point cloud via Nerfacto (a NeRF variant) to initialize Gaussians, replacing the sparse SfM points used by standard 3DGS. The output is a compact representation consisting of a multi-resolution hash grid (16 levels, up to 2^19 feature vectors per level), small MLPs, and per-Gaussian explicit attributes (position, base scale, base color, SH bandwidth), which together compress to 7.9–13.9 MB on Mip-NeRF 360. Upon decoding, the representation reconstructs a standard 3DGS point set so that the original rasterization pipeline runs unmodified, yielding 2.1–2.4× faster rendering than 3DGS.

## Method

The core insight is that 3D Gaussian attributes (opacity, scale, rotation, SH coefficients) exhibit strong local spatial coherence analogous to pixel coherence in images. LocoGS decomposes each Gaussian into explicit attributes (position, base scale γ, base color k⁰, adaptive SH bandwidth b) stored per-primitive, and implicit attributes (opacity, normalized scale, rotation, residual SH coefficients) encoded by a multi-resolution hash grid followed by per-attribute MLPs, so that nearby Gaussians share a compact neural field. During training, a soft movement pruning loss with straight-through estimators removes low-contribution Gaussians, while an adaptive SH bandwidth mechanism assigns each Gaussian a variable maximum SH degree (0–3) to reduce computation on near-Lambertian surfaces. For final compression, positions are losslessly encoded via G-PCC octree coding (using a float-to-int reinterpretation trick to avoid quantization error), remaining explicit attributes are k-bit quantized and entropy-coded after Morton-curve sorting, and hash-grid/MLP parameters are quantized and entropy-coded. This design fully exploits locality for all attributes while keeping the standard 3DGS splatting renderer intact, unlike anchor-based methods that require per-view MLP evaluation during rendering.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, LocoGS achieves PSNR 27.33, SSIM 0.814, and LPIPS 0.219 at only 13.89 MB storage (59.2× compression vs. 3DGS's 822.6 MB) while rendering at 270 FPS (2.1× faster than 3DGS's 127 FPS). The smaller variant (Ours-Small) reaches 7.90 MB (104× compression) at 310 FPS with PSNR 27.04. On Tanks and Temples and Deep Blending, LocoGS outperforms 3DGS in both PSNR (23.84 vs. 23.67; 30.11 vs. 29.48) and rendering speed (311 vs. 175 FPS; 297 vs. 134 FPS) while using 36.7× and 51.8× less storage, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LocoGS outperforms all quantization-based baselines (CompGS, C3DGS, LightGaussian, EAGLES) on both rendering quality and storage size: e.g., on Mip-NeRF 360, LocoGS achieves PSNR 27.33 vs. 27.04–27.10 for these methods while using 13.89 MB vs. 22.93–59.49 MB. Against the image-codec-based SSGS, LocoGS wins on PSNR (27.33 vs. 27.02), SSIM (0.814 vs. 0.800), and storage (13.89 vs. 43.77 MB). Compared to the hash-grid-based Compact-3DGS, LocoGS achieves higher PSNR (27.33 vs. 26.95) and SSIM (0.814 vs. 0.797) at half the storage (13.89 vs. 26.31 MB) and nearly 2× the rendering speed (270 vs. 143 FPS). Against the anchor-based SOTA HAC, LocoGS trades a small PSNR gap (27.33 vs. 27.49) for a 2.4× rendering speed advantage (270 vs. 110 FPS) and 1.2× smaller storage (13.89 vs. 16.95 MB). On Tanks and Temples and Deep Blending, LocoGS surpasses 3DGS in PSNR (23.84 vs. 23.67; 30.11 vs. 29.48) and SSIM while being 36.7× and 51.8× smaller, and outperforms HAC in both PSNR and FPS on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

LocoGS builds directly on 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its rendering backbone and on multi-resolution hash grids (Müller et al., 2022) as the neural field for encoding implicit Gaussian attributes. It departs from prior compression work in two key ways: (1) unlike global-statistic-based methods (CompGS, C3DGS, LightGaussian, EAGLES) that quantize based on global distributions, LocoGS exploits the local spatial coherence of ALL Gaussian attributes (opacity, scale, rotation, residual SH) simultaneously via a hash grid; and (2) unlike anchor-based methods (Scaffold-GS, HAC) that modify the rendering pipeline with per-view MLPs, LocoGS reconstructs a standard explicit 3DGS representation after decompression, preserving the original rasterization pipeline and its speed. The dense initialization from Nerfacto and the adaptive SH bandwidth scheme are borrowed from adjacent NeRF and rate-distortion literature respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LocoGS addresses the practical storage bottleneck of 3DGS (often >1 GB per scene) while simultaneously improving rendering speed over the uncompressed baseline, a combination that prior compression methods have not achieved. The finding that local coherence is strong enough to encode all attributes in a shared hash grid—enabling aggressive pruning without quality loss—suggests a general principle for point-based representations. Key limitations include ~1 hour training time per scene, higher memory usage during training than 3DGS, and an assumption of cube-shaped object-centric scenes that may not generalize to large-scale or non-object-centric environments. Open questions include whether the hash grid approach scales to city-scale scenes and whether training efficiency can be improved without sacrificing the compression ratio.
