## Problem

Diffusion models, including popular text-to-image systems, consistently fail to generate images with a specified topology — they cannot reliably produce a requested number of connected components (0-dim) or enclosed regions/holes (1-dim), as measured by Betti numbers. This limitation blocks their use in applications demanding exact structural control, such as robotics, urban planning, and environmental modeling, where the number of objects or regions must be precise.

## Input / Output

The method takes a scalar topological constraint c (the desired Betti number, ranging 1–10) and optionally a semantic class label (e.g., animal category or shape type) as conditioning inputs. It produces a 256×256 binary segmentation mask containing exactly c topological structures — either c connected components (0-dim, for Shapes and COCO) or c enclosed holes/regions (1-dim, for CREMI and Google Maps). The mask can subsequently be fed to a pretrained ControlNet to render a textured image, but the core output is the topologically correct mask itself.

## Method

TopoDiffusionNet (TDN) extends the ADM diffusion model by injecting the topological constraint c as a condition embedding alongside the timestep embedding in all residual blocks, and by adding a topology-aware loss L_top computed via persistent homology. At each training timestep, the predicted noiseless image x̂₀ᵗ is recovered from the U-Net's noise prediction, and its persistence diagram is computed using the Cubical Ripser library. The diagram is partitioned into the top c dots by persistence (salient structures P) and the remainder (noisy structures Q); L_preserve maximizes the persistence of P while L_denoise minimizes the persistence of Q, both expressed as squared differences between function values at local maxima and saddle points, making the loss fully differentiable. The total training objective is L_simple + λ·L_top (optimal λ = 1e-5), trained with T=1000 DDPM timesteps and a cosine noise scheduler, with 50-step DDIM sampling at inference. This mechanism ensures that at every denoising step the model is steered toward preserving exactly c structures while suppressing spurious ones, rather than relying solely on conditioning.

## Results

TDN achieves 94.78% accuracy on the synthetic Shapes dataset (0-dim), 85.57% on COCO animals (0-dim), 83.18% on Google Maps (1-dim), and 77.85% on CREMI (1-dim), substantially outperforming the ADM-T baseline (75.00%, 66.85%, 54.94%, 53.57% respectively). The F1 scores follow the same pattern, reaching 94.88% (Shapes), 86.13% (COCO), 83.94% (Google Maps), and 79.59% (CREMI). In an additional comparison against the counting-aware CLIP method of Paiss et al., TDN achieves 79.69% accuracy versus 50.18%. All improvements over ADM-T are statistically significant (unpaired t-test, 95% CI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDN decisively outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the text-to-image models Stable Diffusion and DALL-E 3, TDN gains roughly 31 and 26 percentage points in accuracy on Shapes, and 39 and 34 points on COCO, confirming that prompt-based generation cannot reliably enforce exact object counts. Against Attention Refocusing (AR), which uses GPT-4-generated bounding boxes, TDN still leads by ~21 points on Shapes and ~22 on COCO; AR also suffers from fragmented, partitioned outputs at higher object counts. The most direct comparison is with ADM-T (the same diffusion backbone conditioned on the Betti number but without the topological loss), where TDN improves accuracy by ~20 points on Shapes, ~19 on COCO, ~28 on Google Maps, and ~24 on CREMI, demonstrating that the persistent-homology-based L_top is the critical ingredient. In the additional counting comparison (Table 2), TDN achieves 79.69% accuracy versus 50.18% for the counting-aware CLIP method of Paiss et al., a ~30-point margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ADM diffusion model (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) by introducing a topology-aware loss built on persistent homology, a tool from topological data analysis previously applied to GANs only for quality evaluation or soft priors (Wang et al., 2020; Bruel-Gabrielsson et al., 2019). It departs from prior counting/layout methods (Paiss et al., 2023; Phung et al., 2024) that handle only 0-dimensional object counts via bounding boxes, by formulating a unified differentiable objective that enforces exact Betti numbers in both 0-dim (connected components) and 1-dim (holes/regions) simultaneously. The condition injection follows the standard ADM pathway (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021), but the key conceptual departure is using the persistence diagram as a training signal rather than a post-hoc evaluation metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to make diffusion models respect exact topological constraints, opening a path toward topologically faithful generation in domains like robotics, urban planning, and medical imaging where structural correctness is non-negotiable. Limitations include restriction to Betti numbers (no higher-dimensional or more complex topological invariants), generation of masks rather than direct images (requiring a separate ControlNet pass), and evaluation on relatively small or synthetic datasets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumes, more complex topological constraints (e.g., genus, linking numbers), and whether the persistent homology loss can be made more efficient for larger resolutions.
