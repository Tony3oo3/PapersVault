## Problem

Linear bandit algorithms like Thompson Sampling (LinTS) and Greedy show strong empirical performance but lack minimax-optimal frequentist regret guarantees: LinTS requires posterior inflation by a factor of √d to achieve any bound, and Greedy has no theoretical guarantees at all. The paper asks whether one can identify, in a data-driven way, the problem instances where these algorithms fail, and apply targeted course-correction to recover optimal regret while preserving their empirical efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250670/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step the algorithm receives a set of d-dimensional action vectors and a scalar reward, and maintains the running sample covariance matrix V_t and RLS estimate θ̂_t. The key intermediate output is a data-driven regret proxy μ̂_t computed from the eigenvalues of V_t and the confidence ellipsoid geometry, which is compared against a user-set threshold μ to decide whether to take a base-algorithm action (LinTS or Greedy) or an OFUL action. The final outputs are the selected action, a cumulative frequentist regret bound, and the fraction of time spent in each mode.

## Method

The paper introduces POFUL, a unified framework parameterized by inflation ι_t, optimism τ_t, and a feasible pivot θ̃_t that subsumes OFUL, LinTS, TS-Freq, and Greedy as special cases. The core theoretical contribution is a data-driven frequentist regret bound (Theorem 1) expressed in terms of a regret proxy μ_t that depends on an uncertainty ratio α_t = ‖x*_t‖_{V_t^{-1}} / ‖x̃_t‖_{V_t^{-1}}, which is generally intractable because it involves the unknown optimal action. To make the bound computable, Theorem 2 derives a geometric upper bound on α_t by analyzing the projection of the confidence ellipsoid onto the unit hypersphere, using only the eigenvalues of V_t and the RLS estimate. The meta-algorithm TS-MR (and its Greedy variant) then monitors μ̂_t in real time: when μ̂_t exceeds a threshold μ, it switches to an OFUL action to guarantee bounded instantaneous regret; otherwise it follows the base algorithm, achieving minimax-optimal Õ(d√T) frequentist regret while using OFUL actions only in the early or problematic phases.

## Results

TS-MR and Greedy-MR achieve minimax optimal frequentist regret of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\max\{\mu,2\}\, d\sqrt{T})$, matching the OFUL bound while preserving the empirical efficiency of LinTS and Greedy. On the prior-mean-mismatch synthetic example (d=30), LinTS and Greedy incur linear regret, whereas TS-MR and Greedy-MR (with μ=12) achieve sublinear regret by adaptively switching to OFUL actions. On all three real-world datasets (Cardiotocography, JapaneseVowels, Segment), TS-MR and Greedy-MR match the low regret of LinTS and Greedy while OFUL and TS-Freq show substantially higher cumulative regret due to overly conservative exploration. The fraction of OFUL actions in TS-MR and Greedy-MR is concentrated in the early time steps, confirming that course-correction is invoked only when geometrically necessary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OFUL (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011), TS-MR and Greedy-MR achieve comparable or lower regret on all synthetic and real-world instances, while using OFUL actions only in a small early-stage fraction, thus avoiding OFUL's computational overhead (NP-hard optimization) for most of the horizon. Against LinTS (Agrawal & Goyal, 2013), TS-MR matches or improves upon LinTS's empirical regret in easy instances (Examples 1 and 2) and decisively outperforms it in the hard prior-mean-mismatch case (Example 3) where LinTS suffers linear regret. Against TS-Freq (Abeille et al., 2017), TS-MR avoids the $\sqrt{d}$ posterior inflation, resulting in substantially lower cumulative regret across all settings. Against Greedy (Lattimore & Szepesvari, 2017), Greedy-MR outperforms plain Greedy in the contextual bandit embedding (Example 2) where Greedy lacks exploration for some arms, and again dominates in Example 3 where Greedy also incurs linear regret. Overall, the course-corrected algorithms strictly dominate their base counterparts in the hard instances while matching them in the easy ones, and they dominate OFUL/TS-Freq in regret across all tested scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies OFUL, LinTS, TS-Freq, and Greedy under a single POFUL framework, departing from prior optimism-based analyses (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011; Agrawal & Goyal, 2013; Abeille et al., 2017) by not requiring optimistic samples or posterior inflation. The key methodological departure is a real-time geometric analysis of the full d-dimensional confidence ellipsoid, which supersedes the single-scalar thinness coefficient used by Hamidi & Bayati (2020a) to decide when to inflate the posterior. By tracking the ellipsoid's spectral structure, the paper derives a data-driven frequentist regret bound that is computable online, enabling adaptive course-correction without sacrificing the computational efficiency of LinTS or Greedy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges a long-standing gap between the strong empirical performance of LinTS and Greedy and their absence of frequentist regret guarantees, offering a practical mechanism (TS-MR, Greedy-MR) that provably achieves minimax-optimal O(d√T) regret while retaining the low-regret behavior of the base algorithms in typical instances. The geometric analysis of the confidence ellipsoid is potentially of independent interest beyond bandits. Open questions include extending the geometric bounds to discrete action spaces (deferred to the longer version), characterizing the optimal threshold μ more precisely, and whether the course-correction paradigm generalizes to other bandit or RL settings where optimistic sampling is costly or infeasible.
