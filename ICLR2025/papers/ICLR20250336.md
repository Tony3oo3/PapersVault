## Problem

Existing peptide design methods generate all residues simultaneously, ignoring that only a few hot spot residues drive binding affinity while the rest serve as structural scaffolds. Additionally, simultaneous generation struggles to satisfy the rigid geometric constraints imposed by peptide bonds (fixed bond lengths, planar conformations), and practical drug discovery scenarios—where known hot spots must be scaffolded into complete peptides—lack proper benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target protein's binding pocket (residues within 10 Å of the native peptide), the desired peptide length N, and a hot spot count k (typically 1–3). It outputs a complete peptide as a sequence of N residues, each specified by a Cα position (x ∈ R³), a backbone orientation (O ∈ SO(3) defined by N–Cα–C atoms), and an amino acid type. In the scaffold generation variant, k ground-truth hot spot residues are fixed as conditions, and the model must generate the remaining scaffold residues that link them into a valid peptide.

## Method

PepHAR decomposes peptide generation into three stages. First, an energy-based density model (IPA backbone trained via Noise Contrastive Estimation) learns the conditional residue distribution P(c, x, O | T), from which k hot spot residues are sampled using Langevin MCMC on SE(3) with Riemannian updates on SO(3). Second, each hot spot seeds a fragment that is autoregressively extended left or right by sampling dihedral angles (ψ, φ) from von Mises distributions predicted by a directional-masked IPA network, then reconstructing the new residue's backbone via rigid-body transformations (Left/Right operations). Third, a correction stage iteratively optimizes the assembled peptide using a composite objective combining backbone self-consistency (comparing original vs. dihedral-reconstructed structures) and dihedral likelihood, with residue types re-predicted by the density model at each step.

## Results

On the peptide binder design task (Table 1), PepHAR (K=3) achieves the best Secondary Structure Ratio (84.91%) and Binding Site Rate (86.74%), closely matching PepFlow's BSR (86.89%) while substantially outperforming it on Validity (59.31% vs 40.27%) and Novelty (79.11% vs 50.26%). PepHAR (K=3) also attains the highest Diversity (29.58%) among methods with strong structural fidelity, and a competitive RMSD of 2.68 Å (vs. PepFlow's 2.07 Å). On the scaffold generation task (Table 2), PepHAR (K=3) further improves to 2.15 Å RMSD and 88.02% BSR, demonstrating that incorporating known hot-spot residues as priors yields near-native geometries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RFDiffusion, PepHAR (K=3) substantially outperforms on structural fidelity (SSR 84.91% vs 63.86%, BSR 86.74% vs 26.71%) and novelty (79.11% vs 53.74%), but trails on Validity (59.31% vs 66.04%) and Stability (16.62% vs 26.82%), which benefits from RFDiffusion's pre-trained RoseTTAFold weights. Against PepFlow, PepHAR wins decisively on Validity (59.31% vs 40.27%) and Novelty (79.11% vs 50.26%), while PepFlow retains a narrow edge on RMSD (2.07 vs 2.68 Å), Affinity (21.37% vs 20.53%), and Success rate (27.96% vs 25.54%). Against PepGLAD, PepHAR dominates on nearly all metrics—RMSD (2.68 vs 3.83 Å), BSR (86.74% vs 19.34%), Affinity (20.53% vs 10.47%), and Success (25.54% vs 14.05%)—with PepGLAD only leading on Diversity (32.10% vs 29.58%). Against ProteinGenerator, PepHAR is superior on every metric except Validity (59.31% vs 65.88%), with particularly large gaps in SSR (84.91% vs 29.15%) and BSR (86.74% vs 24.62%). Overall, PepHAR's autoregressive dihedral-angle extension provides the best balance of geometric validity, binding-site accuracy, and sequence novelty among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

PepHAR departs from the dominant paradigm in peptide design—simultaneous generation of all residues via diffusion or flow-matching (PepFlow, PepGLAD, RFDiffusion)—by decomposing the problem into a hot-spot sampling stage (energy-based model trained with NCE and sampled via Langevin MCMC), an autoregressive fragment extension stage (modeling dihedral angles with von Mises distributions), and a gradient-based correction stage. The SE(3)-equivariant IPA backbone is shared with AlphaFold and subsequent protein design methods, but the autoregressive, fragment-by-fragment extension with explicit dihedral angle modeling is a conceptual departure from treating residues as independent local frames. The scaffold generation task, where known hot-spot residues are fixed and the model must link them, extends the standard de novo peptide design formulation toward a more realistic drug-discovery workflow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scaffold generation benchmark addresses a practical gap: real peptide drug discovery often starts from known key interaction residues rather than designing from scratch, yet prior generative models were only evaluated on the de novo setting. The three-stage decomposition provides a principled way to enforce peptide bond geometry (planarity, fixed bond lengths) that simultaneous-generation methods can violate, as evidenced by PepFlow's low Valid% (40.27). Open questions include how the method scales to longer peptides or multi-fragment designs beyond K=3 hot spots, whether the correction stage can be replaced by a more principled differentiable objective, and whether the approach generalizes to cyclic or constrained peptides common in therapeutic applications.
