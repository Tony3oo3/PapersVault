## Problem

Standard image autoencoders produce latent spaces that lack underlying 3D geometry, so encoding 3D-consistent views yields latent representations that are themselves 3D-inconsistent, preventing direct NeRF training in the latent space. Prior works resorted to ad-hoc adapter or refinement layers as scene-dependent workarounds, but no universal 3D-aware latent space induced by an autoencoder existed. This paper addresses the root cause by proposing an autoencoder whose latent space inherently preserves 3D-consistency.

## Input / Output

The IG-AE takes a single RGB image (128×128) and produces a 3D-aware latent image of shape 16×16×16 (downscale factor 8, 16 channels), while the latent NeRF pipeline takes posed RGB images of a scene and outputs novel-view latent renderings (16×16×16) that are decoded back to 128×128 RGB. The 8× spatial downscale yields a 64× reduction in per-pixel rendering cost, and the decoder adds ~6.7 ms per image to recover full resolution. Training uses 500 Objaverse scenes (300 views each) for 3D regularization and 40,000 ImageNet images for autoencoder preservation.

## Method

IG-AE regularizes a pre-trained KL-f8-d16 VAE by jointly training it with a set of Tri-Plane latent scenes: the encoder is pushed to produce latents matching 3D-consistent Tri-Plane renderings (L_latent), while the decoder is aligned to reconstruct RGB from those renderings (L_RGB), and AE preservation losses on both synthetic and real data prevent degradation of reconstruction quality. The key insight is that Tri-Planes trained on 3D-inconsistent latents converge to a common coarse geometry while maintaining 3D-consistency, thereby providing the 3D-consistent supervision signal the autoencoder needs. For latent NeRF training, a two-stage pipeline is used: Latent Supervision trains the NeRF on encoded latent images using its native loss, followed by RGB Alignment which fine-tunes both the decoder and the latent scene to minimize pixel-level error against ground-truth RGB, correcting for accumulated errors from the imperfect AE and NeRF. The approach is implemented as an open-source Nerfstudio extension supporting Vanilla-NeRF, Instant-NGP, TensoRF, and K-Planes.

## Results

On ShapeNet datasets (Bags, Hats, Vases), IG-AE consistently outperforms a standard AE across all four NeRF architectures. For example, Vanilla-NeRF with IG-AE achieves 29.57/29.31/33.06 PSNR on Bags/Hats/Vases vs. 28.81/28.75/32.00 for the standard AE, and Instant-NGP improves from 24.29 to 25.90 PSNR on Bags. Latent NVS metrics (Table 6) show even larger gaps: Vanilla-NeRF reaches 50.31 PSNR in latent space with IG-AE vs. 38.36 with a standard AE. Compared to RGB-space NeRFs (Table 8), latent NeRFs trade some NVS quality (e.g., Vanilla-NeRF: 33.60 vs. 40.78 PSNR) for substantial speedups in both training (28 min vs. 637 min) and rendering (19.7 ms vs. 1201 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares latent NeRFs (trained with IG-AE) against their RGB-space counterparts across four Nerfstudio architectures. On NVS quality, all latent methods lose to their RGB versions: Vanilla-NeRF drops from 40.78 to 33.60 PSNR, Instant-NGP from 37.78 to 27.47, TensoRF from 40.65 to 32.04, and K-Planes from 32.98 to 32.36. However, latent training delivers dramatic speedups: Vanilla-NeRF training drops from 637 to 28 min (22.7×) and rendering from 1201 to 19.7 ms (61×); TensoRF training drops from 92 to 20 min (4.6×) and rendering from 101.4 to 10.4 ms (9.7×); K-Planes training drops from 88 to 29 min (3×) and rendering from 63.3 to 11.3 ms (5.6×). Instant-NGP is the exception on training time (6 min RGB vs. 19 min latent), as its hash-grid architecture is already extremely fast in RGB, though it still gains a rendering speedup (11.3→7.3 ms). The authors attribute the NVS quality gap to the loss of high-frequency details when enforcing 3D consistency in the latent space, and note this limits applicability to complex real-world scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeRF line of work (Mildenhall et al., 2020) and the Nerfstudio framework (Tancik et al., 2023) by introducing a 3D-aware latent space for scene representation. Prior latent NeRF approaches (Latent-NeRF, ED-NeRF, Latent-Editor, RLS) all resorted to ad-hoc adapter layers or hybrid RGB+latent training to work around the fundamental incompatibility between image latent spaces and 3D consistency. IG-AE instead addresses this root cause by jointly training an autoencoder with Tri-Plane latent scenes (Chan et al., 2022), using the pre-trained Ostris KL-f8-d16 VAE as its base architecture, thereby producing latent encodings that are inherently 3D-consistent without requiring any RGB supervision during NeRF training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that an image autoencoder can embed a 3D-aware latent space, unlocking a clean pathway for interoperability between NeRFs and latent-based methods (e.g., diffusion models for scene editing or generation) without task-specific adapters. The 20×+ training and rendering speedups (e.g., Vanilla NeRF: 637→28 min) are practically significant. However, the approach is currently limited to object-level synthetic scenes due to high-frequency detail loss in the latent representation, and RGB NVS quality still lags behind standard RGB NeRFs by several PSNR points. Open questions include whether 3D-aware latent spaces can be extended to complex real-world scenes and whether the Tri-Plane supervision strategy generalizes to larger-scale or dynamic scenes.
