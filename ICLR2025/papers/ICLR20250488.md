## Problem

Federated Continual Learning (FCL) has been predominantly studied in offline settings where complete task datasets are stored locally and iterated over multiple times, with most solutions relying on generative replay tailored to vision tasks. The online scenario—where data arrives in mini-batches that can only be processed once and the model must update with high frequency—remains largely unexplored in FCL, yet it better reflects realistic conditions on resource-constrained edge devices. This paper formalizes the online-FCL problem and demonstrates that generative-based approaches are fundamentally ill-suited to this setting.

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, the method consumes image patches (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, variable sizes for medical histology images) arriving as a stream of mini-batches of size 10 that are seen exactly once. For text classification, raw documents are first encoded into 384-dimensional latent vectors via a pre-trained e5-small-v2 sentence embedder, and perturbations for uncertainty estimation are applied as Gaussian noise (N(0, 0.1)) in this latent space. The output is a class label for multi-class classification, with non-overlapping classes across tasks per client.

## Method

The core contribution is an uncertainty-aware memory management strategy that uses Bregman Information (BI)—the variance term from a bias-variance decomposition of cross-entropy loss in logit space—as an epistemic uncertainty estimator to populate a fixed-size, class-balanced replay buffer. BI is computed via test-time augmentation (12 standard augmentations for images, Gaussian noise in embedding space for text), measuring how much the logit predictions vary across perturbations; samples with the lowest BI (most representative of their class's data-generating process) are retained in memory. At each mini-batch, the model trains jointly on the incoming batch and a random replay set drawn from memory, while a burn-in period and a jump parameter q=5 regulate when clients participate in federated parameter averaging. On the server side, class-based parameter averaging ensures equal contribution from over- and under-represented classes, and the new global parameters are smoothed with the previous round's parameters to avoid abrupt shifts. The approach is modality-agnostic (slim ResNet-18 for vision, a 512-unit MLP for text) and requires no generative models, making it applicable to any data type where TTA can be defined.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (5 tasks, M=1000), the proposed BI-based memory management (bottom-k) achieves 35.83% average last accuracy and 19.07% average forgetting, substantially outperforming FedAvg (16.90% / 78.29%), MFCL (16.42% / 55.64%), and FedCIL (16.26% / 49.87%). On the imbalanced biomedical CRC-Tissue dataset, BI reaches 62.33% accuracy with only 7.99% forgetting, compared to 22.26% / 87.39% for FedAvg and 27.62% / 76.34% for MFCL. On text classification (DBPedia), BI achieves 78.68% accuracy and 21.79% forgetting versus 19.57% / 99.02% for FedAvg, demonstrating modality-agnostic effectiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed BI-based memory management dominates all baselines on average last accuracy across every dataset: on CIFAR-10 it more than doubles the accuracy of FedAvg (35.83% vs. 16.90%), MFCL (16.42%), and FedCIL (16.26%), and on CRC-Tissue it nearly triples FedAvg's accuracy (62.33% vs. 22.26%). On forgetting, BI achieves the lowest values on CIFAR-10 (19.07% vs. 49.87–78.29%), CRC-Tissue (7.99% vs. 62.47–87.86%), and all three text datasets (21.79–32.65% vs. 50.22–99.06%). The only exceptions are CIFAR-100 forgetting, where FedCIL is slightly lower (4.99% vs. 6.96%), and KC-Cell forgetting, where FedCIL edges out BI (55.82% vs. 59.05%), though BI still leads on accuracy in both cases. Compared to the memory-based baselines ER and CBR (Tables 1–4), BI with bottom-k selection consistently reduces forgetting by 5–15 percentage points while matching or exceeding their accuracy, confirming that the Bregman Information estimator selects more representative replay samples than random or class-balanced sampling. Generative-based methods (MFCL, FedCIL) are particularly disadvantaged in the online setting because they cannot iterate over full task datasets, resulting in poor synthetic sample quality and performance close to or below standard FL without replay.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends federated continual learning (FCL) to an online setting where data arrives in single-pass mini-batches, a scenario that has been well-studied in online continual learning but largely unexplored in the federated context. Methodologically, it builds on the bias-variance decomposition of cross-entropy loss by Gruber & Buettner (2023) to compute Bregman Information as an epistemic uncertainty estimate, adopts the class-balanced memory update from Chrysakis & Moens (2020), and incorporates parameter smoothing from Shenaj et al. (2023). The key departure is from the dominant generative-replay paradigm in FCL (MFCL, FedCIL), which the authors argue is fundamentally incompatible with the online setting due to its need for repeated passes over complete task datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a realistic gap: in practice, data on edge and medical devices arrives in streams rather than as pre-collected static datasets, making the offline assumption of current FCL methods impractical. The BI-based memory management is modality-agnostic (validated on both vision and text), handles class imbalance in biomedical data, and requires only a small local buffer, making it suitable for resource-constrained devices. Limitations include the TTA overhead for uncertainty estimation, the fact that the BI estimator is only asymptotically unbiased, and the absence of direct online-FCL competitors for comparison. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks (e.g., segmentation, detection) and whether deeper ensembling (beyond TTA) would further improve the uncertainty estimates.
