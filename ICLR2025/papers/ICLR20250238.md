## Problem

Multi-agent reinforcement learning methods suffer from high sample complexity, poor generalization to unseen agents, and limited reasoning capabilities, while existing LLM-based agents have been restricted to collaborative settings. No prior LLM agent could handle the full spectrum of competitive, cooperative, and mixed-motive multi-agent dynamics while inferring and adapting to other agents' hidden strategies online within an episode.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250238/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a partially-observable grid state (5×5 window for dyadic environments, 11×11 for the 8-player Arena) converted from pixel images into a textual map listing entity types and coordinates, plus a memory buffer of past observations, rewards, and interaction inventories. Critically, the opponent's inventory is never directly observed; it must be estimated by the LLM from the reward received and the agent's own inventory. The agent outputs a sequence of atomic actions (step, turn, fire, noop) via A* pathfinding, a natural-language high-level strategy conditioned on the best hypothesis, and a maintained set of scored hypotheses about each other agent's strategy.

## Method

Hypothetical Minds integrates a modular cognitive architecture (perception, memory, hierarchical planning) with a novel Theory of Mind module that generates, evaluates, and refines natural-language hypotheses about other agents' latent strategies. Hypotheses are scored via a Rescorla-Wagner update rule using self-supervised intrinsic rewards: the LLM predicts each opponent's next behavior under each hypothesis, and correct predictions yield positive reward (c=1) while incorrect ones yield negative reward, with learning rate α=0.3. The best hypothesis is selected by MAP estimation and, once its value exceeds a validation threshold (V_thr=0.7), it conditions the high-level plan until its predictive accuracy degrades. The top-k (default 5) highest-valued hypotheses are shown to the LLM during generation to enable iterative refinement, and separate hypothesis streams are maintained per opponent in population-based environments. The validated hypothesis conditions a high-level plan, which the Subgoal module decomposes into 3–6 action function calls executed by a hardcoded A* pathfinding planner.

## Results

Across 30 evaluation scenarios in four Melting Pot environments, Hypothetical Minds (HM) outperforms all LLM-agent baselines on the majority of scenarios in every environment. On Running With Scissors Repeated, HM achieves average rewards of 12.9–63.8 across 9 scenarios, compared to −4.2 to 31.0 for the best LLM baseline (Reflexion) and near-zero for OPRE. On Collaborative Cooking Asymmetric, HM scores 426.8–628.3, exceeding the next-best baseline (PlanReAct at 292.6–455.6) by 20–125 points. On Prisoner's Dilemma, HM is the top LLM agent on 5/10 scenarios but OPRE (a trained MARL method) achieves the highest overall rewards due to superior spatial resource collection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Reflexion, HM wins on 22/30 scenarios with the largest margins in RWS Repeated (e.g., SC7 Paper: 59.6 vs 13.2; SC8 Scissors: 63.8 vs 31.0) and Collaborative Cooking (SC0: 628.3 vs 292.6, a 2.1× improvement). Against ReAct, HM dominates nearly all scenarios, as ReAct frequently produces negative rewards (e.g., RWS SC4: −1.5 vs 48.5; RWS Arena SC6: −10.7 vs 24.9). Against PlanReAct (the closest LLM baseline, differing only in the absence of the ToM module), HM wins 24/30 scenarios, with the most notable gaps in RWS Repeated (SC1 Best Response: 23.2 vs 21.1; SC7: 59.6 vs 13.0) and Collaborative Cooking SC0 (628.3 vs 402.9). Against PPO (trained for 10⁸ steps), HM wins 25/30 scenarios; PPO's key weakness is poor generalization to unseen partner types (Collaborative Cooking SC2 Unhelpful: 0.0 vs 426.8). Against OPRE (hierarchical MARL), the picture is mixed: OPRE achieves near-zero on all RWS scenarios and poor results on Collaborative Cooking, but OPRE wins 7/10 Prisoner's Dilemma scenarios (e.g., SC1 Cooperator: 102.5 vs 78.7) due to more efficient spatial resource collection that ungrounded LLMs struggle with, illustrating a tradeoff between abstract strategic reasoning (LLM advantage) and low-level embodied control (RL advantage).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative agents cognitive architecture (Park et al., 2023) — with its perception, memory, and planning modules — by introducing a Theory of Mind module that generates, evaluates, and refines natural-language hypotheses about other agents' latent strategies using a Rescorla-Wagner update rule and MAP estimation. It departs from prior LLM-agent work (Voyager, SAMA, ProAgent) that was restricted to collaborative settings by addressing competitive, mixed-motive, and collaborative dynamics uniformly. The hypothesis evaluation mechanism draws on cognitive science (Rescorla-Wagner, Bayesian inverse planning) and parallels Tree-of-Thoughts' parallel path evaluation, but scores hypotheses by predictive accuracy over observed trajectories rather than by an external verifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first LLM agent demonstrated across competitive, collaborative, and mixed-motive multi-agent domains, showing that structured hypothesis generation and evaluation (not just generation) are critical for adapting to diverse opponent strategies. Key limitations include the need for human-authored scaffolding and explicit game-rule encoding in prompts, limited spatial reasoning of ungrounded LLMs (causing inefficient navigation), and high per-episode computational cost (~$10 with GPT-4). Open questions include whether agents can autonomously learn scaffolding and game mechanics from environmental feedback, and whether the ToM module's principles transfer to real-world social interactions like adaptive tutoring or personalized dialogue.
