## Problem

Existing methods for inferring continuous dynamics from time-resolved snapshots either assume mass conservation (balanced Schrödinger bridge or OT), thereby producing false transitions when cell growth or death is present, or require prior knowledge of growth/death rates. No computational framework existed for learning the full regularized unbalanced optimal transport (RUOT) problem—which simultaneously captures stochastic diffusion and unbalanced mass changes—directly from data without such priors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unnormalized empirical distributions at T discrete time points (e.g., scRNA-seq cell coordinates at 3–5 time points, or synthetic gene-expression snapshots), where the total mass at each time point may differ due to cell division or death. It outputs three neural-network-parameterized fields: the probability-flow ODE drift v(x,t), the local growth/death rate g(x,t), and the log-density s(x,t), from which the Waddington potential landscape U = −σ²(t) log p(x,t)/2 is directly read off. Because the distributions are unnormalized, the reconstruction loss uses a local mass-matching strategy (nearest-neighbor cardinality) rather than standard balanced OT, and the energy integral is evaluated via Monte Carlo along Neural ODE trajectories weighted by exp(∫g ds).

## Method

DeepRUOT reformulates the RUOT variational problem (Definition 4.1) into a Fisher-information-regularized form (Theorem 4.1) that replaces the SDE drift b with the probability-flow ODE velocity v, eliminating the need to simulate stochastic paths and avoiding costly cross-terms involving ∇log p. Three neural networks parameterize v(x,t), g(x,t), and s(x,t) = ½σ²(t) log p(x,t), and are trained with a composite loss: a Monte Carlo energy loss (kinetic + Fisher + growth penalty) computed along Neural ODE trajectories, a reconstruction loss combining local mass matching and weighted Wasserstein distance, and a physics-informed Fokker-Planck residual. Training proceeds in two stages: a pre-training phase that first fits v and g via reconstruction loss with a hyperparameter schedule (mass-matching first, then transition refinement), then initializes s via conditional flow matching (CFM) on Brownian-bridge interpolants; followed by a joint fine-tuning phase minimizing the full three-term loss. The key architectural insight is that the Fisher regularization form makes the SDE-to-ODE transformation explicit, so the entire problem reduces to learning an ODE velocity field plus a scalar growth field, which is far more tractable than solving the original stochastic control problem.

## Results

On the synthetic gene regulatory network, DeepRUOT achieves the lowest Wasserstein distances across all four time points, with W₁=0.044 and W₂=0.058 at t=1, improving to W₁=0.104 and W₂=0.121 at t=4. On the real mouse hematopoiesis scRNA-seq data, DeepRUOT attains W₁=0.145 and W₂=0.173 at t=1 and W₁=0.132 and W₂=0.167 at t=2, outperforming all five baselines. The method also qualitatively eliminates false transitions (e.g., spurious attraction of quiescent cells) that appear in balanced Schrödinger bridge methods when growth is ignored, and accurately recovers ground-truth growth rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepRUOT outperforms all five baselines on both the synthetic gene regulatory network and the real mouse hematopoiesis scRNA-seq data. The closest competitor is Unbalanced OT (Sha et al., 2024), which captures growth but omits stochastic diffusion; DeepRUOT's advantage over it is modest (e.g., W₁ 0.044 vs. 0.047 at t=1) but consistent, confirming the benefit of incorporating stochasticity. The balanced Schrödinger bridge method SF2M (Tong et al., 2024b) performs substantially worse (W₁=0.174 vs. 0.044 at t=1) and produces qualitatively false transitions by attracting quiescent cells into growth regions to maintain mass balance. Unbalanced SB (Pariset et al., 2023) and uAM (Neklyudov et al., 2023) are significantly inferior, with W₁ values of 0.729 and 0.448 respectively at t=1 on the gene regulatory data. MIOFlow (Huguet et al., 2022), a balanced OT-based method, degrades most rapidly over time (W₁ reaching 0.614 at t=4). On the scRNA-seq data, DeepRUOT also leads at both time points, with the gap over SF2M widening at t=2 (W₁ 0.132 vs. 0.190).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in dynamical optimal transport: the Schrödinger Bridge formulation (capturing stochastic effects) and the unbalanced Benamou-Brenier formulation (capturing growth/death via the WFR metric), into a single Regularized Unbalanced Optimal Transport (RUOT) framework. It departs from prior unbalanced SB methods (Pariset et al., 2023; Schiebinger et al., 2019) by not requiring prior knowledge of growth/death rates or additional lineage information, and from balanced SB solvers (Tong et al., 2024b; Neklyudov et al., 2023) by explicitly modeling unbalanced mass. The Fisher information regularization form (Theorem 4.1) transforms the governing SDE into a probability-flow ODE, building on the Hopf-Cole-type transformations in Li et al. (2020) and Gentil et al. (2017), while the neural solver draws on conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and physics-informed neural network constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a concrete gap in single-cell trajectory inference: existing tools either ignore stochasticity (unbalanced OT) or ignore growth/death (balanced SB), and the paper demonstrates that neglecting growth produces biologically misleading false transitions. The ability to simultaneously recover drift, growth rate, and the Waddington energy landscape from sparse snapshots without prior knowledge is practically valuable for developmental biology. Limitations include computational cost at very high dimensions (beyond ~100D), dependence on time-resolved data, and the need for careful two-stage training with hyperparameter scheduling. Open questions include joint learning of latent embeddings with dynamics and extending to multi-omics settings.
