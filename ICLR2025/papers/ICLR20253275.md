## Problem

Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models for text-to-video generation struggle with long-range temporal dependencies, producing static or repetitive content in long videos, while the multi-step denoising process is computationally expensive. Existing autoregressive long-video approaches condition only on the last frames of prior segments, limiting historical context and causing content repetition. The paper addresses how to leverage the long-range semantic coherence of autoregressive models to guide DiT-based generation, enabling dynamic, temporally consistent long videos at reduced inference cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt (or a sequence of progressive prompts for scene transitions) and produces a long video at 512×512 resolution, with each DiT segment covering 51–136 frames and the full video extending to 600 frames via overlapping segments. Internally, the AR model first emits a sequence of discrete visual token indices (drawn from a 2048-entry codebook) that encode coarse spatial layout and long-range temporal structure; these tokens are decoded back into continuous latent features that condition the DiT. The DiT backbone (OpenSora-V1.2, 1.2B params) remains frozen throughout training.

## Method

ARLON comprises three components: (1) a latent VQ-VAE nested inside the 3D video VAE that quantizes DiT input latents into compact discrete tokens (4×8×8 compression for AR training, 4×16×16 for DiT training); (2) a 12-layer causal Transformer decoder that autoregressively predicts these token indices from T5 text embeddings; and (3) a gated adaptive-norm semantic injection module that projects the decoded AR features into scale, shift, and gate parameters applied at the first 14 of 28 DiT layers. Two noise-robustness strategies bridge the teacher-forcing-to-autoregressive gap: the DiT is trained on coarser (higher-compression) latent tokens so it learns to tolerate AR prediction errors, and an uncertainty sampling module draws perturbation noise from the empirical mean/variance of the feature distribution rather than standard Gaussian, applied stochastically after each adapter block. Only the 92M-parameter injection modules are trained (with Adam at 2×10⁻⁵), while the 1.2B DiT and 384M 3D VAE stay frozen, yielding a 48–64% inference speedup by reducing required denoising steps from 30 to 5–10.

## Results

On the VBench T2V benchmark, ARLON achieves a Dynamic Degree of 52.8 (vs. 47.2 for OpenSora-V1.2, +5.6) and Aesthetic Quality of 61.0 (vs. 56.2, +4.8), outperforming the baseline on 8 out of 11 metrics. For long video generation (600 frames), ARLON scores 50.42 on Dynamic Degree and 56.85 on Aesthetic Quality, surpassing OpenSora-V1.2 (44.79, 56.68) while maintaining competitive consistency scores. Inference efficiency improves by 47–64%: ARLON requires only 5–10 denoising steps (vs. 30 for OpenSora-V1.2), reducing 68-frame generation from ~47 s to ~25 s on a single A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct baseline OpenSora-V1.2, ARLON wins on 8 of 11 VBench metrics, with the largest gains in Scene (+11.9), Object (+6.4), Dynamic Degree (+5.6), and Aesthetic Quality (+4.8), while conceding small losses on Subject Consistency (−1.1), Background Consistency (−0.8), and Temporal Flickering (−0.1). In the long-video (600-frame) setting, ARLON dominates FreeNoise on Dynamic Degree (50.4 vs. 17.4) and Aesthetic Quality (56.9 vs. 47.4), though FreeNoise retains an edge in Imaging Quality (63.9 vs. 53.9); against StreamingT2V, ARLON wins on all consistency and quality metrics but trails on Dynamic Degree (50.4 vs. 85.6). Among commercial models, ARLON surpasses Kling on Dynamic Degree (52.8 vs. 46.9) and Scene (54.4 vs. 50.9) but falls short on Imaging Quality (61.0 vs. 65.6) and Subject Consistency (93.4 vs. 98.3); it dramatically outperforms Gen-2 on Dynamic Degree (52.8 vs. 18.9) yet lags on Aesthetic Quality (61.0 vs. 67.0) and Imaging Quality (61.0 vs. 67.4). Against open-source T2V models, ARLON leads LaVie on Dynamic Degree (52.8 vs. 49.7) and Aesthetic Quality (61.0 vs. 54.9), beats Show-1 on Dynamic Degree (52.8 vs. 44.4) and Aesthetic Quality (61.0 vs. 57.4), and substantially outperforms CogVideo across nearly all metrics (e.g., Aesthetic 61.0 vs. 38.2, Temporal Style 25.3 vs. 7.8). Compared to OpenSoraPlan-V1.1, ARLON gains on Dynamic Degree (+5.1), Aesthetic Quality (+4.1), Scene (+27.3), and Object (+13.5), while OpenSoraPlan retains a slight edge on Imaging Quality (62.3 vs. 61.0) and Subject Consistency (95.7 vs. 93.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

ARLON sits at the intersection of two well-established video generation paradigms: autoregressive Transformer models (VideoGPT, CogVideo, VideoPoet) that excel at long-range temporal coherence but produce lower visual quality, and diffusion Transformer (DiT) models (OpenSora, CogVideoX, SORA) that produce high-quality videos but struggle with dynamics and long-range consistency. The paper builds directly on OpenSora-V1.2 as its frozen DiT backbone and adapts the VQ-VAE architecture from VideoGPT, but departs from prior long-video approaches (sliding-window attention, diffusion-over-diffusion, conditional mask inputs) by using AR-generated discrete tokens as semantic guidance injected via a gated adaptive normalization module. The uncertainty sampling mechanism is borrowed from image retrieval work (Chen et al., 2022) and repurposed here to simulate AR inference noise during DiT training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AR+DiT hybrid addresses a core practical tension in video generation: diffusion models are slow and produce static content, while AR models are fast but lower quality. The 47–64% inference speedup (30→10 denoising steps) combined with improved dynamic degree and aesthetic quality makes long video generation substantially more tractable for open-source practitioners. Key limitations remain: the framework is capped by the OpenSora-V1.2 backbone quality, AR code sequence length becomes impractical at 2K resolution, and fine-grained actions (hand movements) still fail. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger DiT backbones (CogVideoX-5B, MovieGen) and whether parallel AR prediction can mitigate the resolution bottleneck.
