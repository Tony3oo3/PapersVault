## Problem

While prior interpretability work identified critical attention heads for specific tasks using carefully constructed sentences, it remained unclear whether small subsets of heads (knowledge circuits) govern correct predictions on complex, open-ended tasks like commonsense reasoning. Moreover, no method existed to leverage such identified circuits for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, leaving a gap between mechanistic understanding and practical adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251200/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard text sequence fed into a decoder-only LLM (LLaMA2-7B or LLaMA3-8B) along with a pre-computed knowledge circuit: a set of n=8 attention heads per layer identified via the layer-conditioned locating algorithm on 64 lowest-loss samples per dataset. The trainable output is a pair of small linear projections (down-project to r=32, up-project to d) per selected head, introducing only ~0.07–0.08% of total parameters versus ~0.8–0.95% for LoRA. At inference, the model produces standard next-token predictions with no additional latency.

## Method

The paper contributes two coupled components. First, a layer-conditioned locating algorithm performs greedy head selection layer by layer, evaluating each head's importance only after all previously selected heads in earlier layers are already masked, thereby capturing inter-layer dependencies that a flat greedy search misses. Second, HeadMap is a PEFT method that takes the raw outputs of the selected attention heads, passes them through a bottleneck (down-projection to r dimensions initialized to zero, then up-projection to d dimensions), and injects the result either directly into the residual stream (HeadMap_HS) or into the MHA block output before W_O (HeadMap_MHA). This selectively amplifies the knowledge flow from the circuit without modifying the base model weights. The approach is complementary to LoRA/DoRA: combining HeadMap with LoRA applied only to W_V, W_up, and W_down yields the best results with fewer total parameters than either method alone.

## Results

On eight commonsense reasoning datasets, HeadMap (0.07–0.08% trainable parameters) achieves 76.78% average accuracy on LLaMA2-7B and 85.74% on LLaMA3-8B, matching or exceeding LoRA (76.38%/84.82%) and DoRA (77.02%/85.50%) which use ~10× more parameters (0.80–0.96%). Combining HeadMap with DoRA on W_V, W_up, W_down yields the best results: 78.31% on LLaMA2-7B (+1.29% over DoRA alone) and 86.51% on LLaMA3-8B (+1.01% over DoRA alone), while still using fewer parameters than full LoRA/DoRA. On five NLU datasets (Table 2), HeadMap_HS + LoRA(V,U,D) achieves 77.55% average on LLaMA2-7B, outperforming LoRA alone (72.36%) by +5.19%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HeadMap is compared against LoRA and DoRA, both applied to all six weight matrices (W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O, W_up, W_down) at every layer. On LLaMA3-8B, HeadMap_MHA alone (0.07% params) surpasses both LoRA (84.82%) and DoRA (85.50%) in average accuracy (85.74%), demonstrating that targeting only 8 critical attention heads per layer is more effective than updating all weights. On LLaMA2-7B, HeadMap_MHA (76.78%) is slightly below DoRA (77.02%) but above LoRA (76.38%), while using only 0.08% vs. 0.95–0.96% parameters. The key finding is complementarity: combining HeadMap with DoRA on a subset of matrices (W_V, W_up, W_down) yields the best results—78.31% on LLaMA2-7B (+1.29% over DoRA alone) and 86.51% on LLaMA3-8B (+1.01% over DoRA alone)—while still using fewer total parameters (0.67%/0.61%) than full DoRA (0.96%/0.81%). On NLU tasks (Table 2), HeadMap_HS + LoRA(V,U,D) achieves 77.55% average on LLaMA2-7B, a +5.19% improvement over LoRA alone (72.36%), confirming the complementary effect across task types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability and parameter-efficient fine-tuning. On the interpretability side, it builds on causal mediation analysis and path patching methods (Vig et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2024; Yao et al. 2024) that identify critical attention heads, but departs from them by replacing the need for carefully constructed input sentences with a layer-conditioned greedy locating algorithm that operates on naturally occurring task samples. On the PEFT side, it contrasts with LoRA/DoRA (which update low-rank matrices across all weight projections) by instead routing the outputs of a small, task-specific subset of attention heads through two linear projections into the residual stream, making the fine-tuning signal explicitly grounded in the identified knowledge circuit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a PEFT method that uses roughly one-tenth the parameters of LoRA while achieving comparable accuracy, and that composes additively with LoRA/DoRA for further gains — a useful recipe for practitioners with tight parameter budgets. However, the evaluation is limited to two model families (LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA3-8B) and to classification-style tasks (commonsense QA, GLUE), leaving open whether the layer-conditioned circuit identification and HeadMap transfer to generation tasks, larger models, or encoder-decoder architectures. The finding that knowledge circuits are largely task-specific (low cross-task overlap) also raises the question of whether a single set of enhanced heads can serve multiple downstream tasks simultaneously, which would be needed for multi-task deployment.
