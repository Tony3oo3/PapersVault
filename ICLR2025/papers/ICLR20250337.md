## Problem

Off-policy training methods for GFlowNets (replay buffers, local search, ε-exploration) promote exploration by perturbing the current policy, but they can only discover modes near those already visited. This leaves a fundamental gap: modes sufficiently separated from the explored region remain undiscovered, causing mode collapse in multimodal target distributions. The paper asks whether an adaptive training distribution can explicitly target the Student's high-loss regions across the entire sample space, rather than relying on proximity to previously sampled points.

## Input / Output

The method takes a black-box unnormalized reward/energy function R(x) defined over a discrete combinatorial space (molecular graphs, DNA/RNA sequences) or a continuous space (diffusion trajectories), with no access to target samples or energy gradients during training. It produces a trained Student GFlowNet policy that generates samples via multi-step sequential decisions (token-by-token sequence construction or Euler-Maruyama diffusion steps). The Teacher's reward is computed on-the-fly from the Student's trajectory-balance loss, making the training distribution non-stationary and co-evolving with the Student's learning progress.

## Method

The paper introduces a Teacher-Student framework where a secondary GFlowNet (Teacher) is trained to sample terminal states where the primary GFlowNet (Student) incurs high trajectory-balance loss. The Teacher's reward is defined as the Student's TB discrepancy squared, with an additional weighting factor (C=19) that further emphasizes undersampled regions where backward flow exceeds forward flow, plus a mixing term α·log R(x) to anchor the Teacher toward high-reward areas. Both models are jointly trained using a behavior policy that periodically selects among the Student, Teacher, and a prioritized replay buffer, with local search (backtracking-and-reconstruction) applied to the Teacher's samples to mitigate the non-stationarity of its evolving reward. The key insight is that this amortizes an ideal prioritized experience replay: unlike a finite replay buffer that requires poorly-captured modes to have been previously visited, the Teacher can generalize to high-loss regions anywhere in the sample space, enabling discovery of modes that are completely unexplored by the Student.

## Results

On the 25GMM diffusion sampling task, Teacher achieves an EUBO of 0.115±0.009 and W₂² of 0.86±0.07, substantially outperforming PER (EUBO 1.833, W₂² 1.87) and on-policy TB (EUBO 18.321, W₂² 4.64). On the 32-dimensional Manywell task, Teacher reaches EUBO 165.800±0.045 and W₂² 5.46±0.01, compared to PER (EUBO 210.440, W₂² 5.91) and on-policy TB (EUBO 427.850, W₂² 6.15). On the deceptive grid world (d=4, H=32), Teacher discovers 246.6±14.7 modes versus 16.6±4.8 (on-policy TB), 120.4±19.1 (PRT), and 46.6±14.6 (PER). Across four biochemical discovery tasks (QM9, sEH, TFbind8, L14-RNA1), Teacher+PER consistently outperforms all baselines in mode count, ELBO, and EUBO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Teacher outperforms all eight baselines across every task and metric reported. On 25GMM, Teacher's EUBO (0.115) is approximately 16× lower than on-policy TB (18.321) and 16× lower than PER (1.833), while its W₂² (0.86) matches the MCMC baseline SMC (0.86) and beats GGNS (1.19). On Manywell, Teacher's EUBO (165.800) is 2.6× better than PER (210.440) and 2.6× better than on-policy TB (427.850), and its W₂² (5.46) is the best among all methods including MCMC baselines. On the deceptive grid world (d=4, H=32), Teacher discovers 246.6 modes versus 16.6 (on-policy TB), 120.4 (PRT), 46.6 (PER), and 24.6 (GAFN), a 15× improvement over on-policy TB. On the four biochemical tasks, Teacher+PER surpasses on-policy TB, ε-exploration, PRT, and PER in mode count, ELBO, and EUBO, with the largest gains on L14-RNA1 where Teacher+PER leads in all three metrics. Notably, Teacher also outperforms gradient-based local search (PER+LS) on Manywell without requiring access to ∇E, achieving EUBO 165.800 vs. 168.953 for PER+LS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework for amortized sampling (Bengio et al., 2021; Malkin et al., 2022) by introducing a learned auxiliary policy (the Teacher) that adaptively generates training trajectories for the primary sampler (the Student). Conceptually, it amortizes Prioritized Experience Replay (Schaul, 2016): rather than re-sampling high-loss items from a finite buffer, the Teacher is a parametric model that can generalize to high-loss regions never previously visited. It departs from existing off-policy exploration methods (ε-exploration, GAFN, PRT, PER) that perturb the current policy or replay stored samples, by instead learning a distribution over the entire sample space that tracks the Student's loss landscape. The method is shown to be objective-agnostic (TB and DB) and space-agnostic (discrete and continuous GFlowNets).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mode coverage remains a fundamental bottleneck in amortized sampling from multimodal distributions, and the Teacher provides a general-purpose, plug-in solution that consistently improves coverage across discrete (molecular/sequence design) and continuous (diffusion) settings. The key limitation is the added training cost of a second network, and in very large search spaces the Teacher itself may collapse to a subset of modes, motivating future multi-agent Teacher designs. The paper also opens directions toward applying adaptive training distributions to LLM inference, red-teaming, and broader RL agent training beyond amortized samplers.
