## Problem

The literature contains many predictive uncertainty measures (entropy, mutual information, EPKL, predictive variance, MaxProb, etc.), but their relationships to each other remain unclear: it is unknown whether they are special cases of a general class, whether they complement or contradict one another, and how to systematically generate new ones. This paper addresses this gap by deriving a unified statistical framework based on pointwise risk decomposition that recovers existing measures and produces new ones from a single principled construction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) and requires a deep ensemble of ResNet-18 models (4 members × 5 independently trained groups = 20 total) to obtain multiple predictive probability vectors per input. It produces three types of scalar uncertainty scores per image—aleatoric (Bayes risk), epistemic (Excess risk), and total—each parameterized by a choice of strictly proper scoring rule (Log, Brier, or Spherical) and an approximation strategy, yielding a family of 12 distinct measures per scoring rule. The scores are then used as inputs to downstream AUROC evaluation for OOD detection and misclassification detection.

## Method

The paper decomposes pointwise risk R(η, η̂|x) into Bayes risk (aleatoric) and Excess risk (epistemic), then specializes to strictly proper scoring rules where the Excess risk takes the closed form of a Bregman divergence D_G(η‖η̂). Since the true conditional η is unknown, the paper applies Bayesian estimation over a posterior p(θ|D_tr) and introduces three approximation strategies for each argument of the risk function: (1) Bayesian averaging over the posterior, (2) the central label (posterior predictive mean), and (3) the central prediction (argmin of expected Bregman divergence). This yields 9 Excess risk and 3 Bayes risk approximations, from which well-known measures emerge as special cases—e.g., Mutual Information is Bregman Information under Log score, EPKL is Expected Pairwise Bregman Divergence, and predictive variance is Bregman Information under Brier score. The framework also reveals a connection to energy-based OOD detection, showing that a specific Excess risk approximation equals the difference between two Bayesian energy estimates. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet validate that Log Score-based measures generally perform best, that Excess risk excels for soft-OOD while Bayes risk is better for hard-OOD, and that Total/Bayes risk outperform Excess risk for misclassification detection.

## Results

On TinyImageNet in-distribution OOD detection (Table 3), the best Total risk measure R̃_Tot^(1,1) achieves 84.26 AUROC on ImageNet-A and 83.32 on ImageNet-R (hard-OOD), while the best Excess risk measure R̃_Exc^(1,2) achieves 74.79 AUROC on ImageNet-O (soft-OOD), outperforming both energy-based baselines (72.72 and 71.15). For CIFAR-10 in-distribution (Table 2), Log Score-based Bayes risk reaches 96.01 AUROC on SVHN and 91.36 on CIFAR-100, while Excess risk measures reach ~94 AUROC on soft-OOD (CIFAR-10-C). Across all scoring rules, Log Score consistently yields the highest AUROC, and the framework's R̃_Exc^(3,1) (MRBI) outperforms both individual energy estimates E(x; E_θ f_θ) and E_θ E(x; f_θ) for soft-OOD and misclassification detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its framework-derived measures against the two energy-based OOD scores from Liu et al. (2020): E(x; E_θ f_θ) and E_θ E(x; f_θ). For hard-OOD detection (ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R), the framework's Total risk R̃_Tot^(1,1) (84.26 / 83.32 AUROC) slightly outperforms E(x; E_θ f_θ) (83.96 / 83.28), while R̃_Exc^(3,1) (77.56 / 77.02) underperforms both energy estimates. For soft-OOD detection (ImageNet-O), the framework's Excess risk measures clearly win: R̃_Exc^(1,2) achieves 74.79 AUROC versus 72.72 for E(x; E_θ f_θ) and 71.15 for E_θ E(x; f_θ), a gap of 2–3.6 AUROC points. The paper shows that R̃_Exc^(3,1) equals (1/T)(E(x; E_θ f_θ) − E_θ E(x; f_θ)), meaning it captures the *difference* between the two energy estimates and is most effective when the OOD shift is mild (soft-OOD) but degrades for hard-OOD where the individual energy estimates are more reliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the pointwise risk decomposition introduced by Kotelevskii et al. (2022a), Lahlou et al. (2023), and Liu et al. (2019) with the theory of strictly proper scoring rules (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007), showing that all resulting epistemic uncertainty measures are instances of Bregman divergence. It positions well-known measures—Mutual Information, Expected Pairwise KL, predictive variance, and MaxProb—as special cases within a single generative framework parameterized by the convex function G. A notable departure from prior work is the identification of the 'central prediction' as a third approximation option (beyond Bayesian averaging and posterior predictive), and the demonstration that energy-based OOD scores (Liu et al., 2020b) emerge as a particular Excess risk approximation, thereby subsuming them under the same risk-decomposition umbrella as Gruber & Buettner (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides practitioners with a principled taxonomy for selecting uncertainty measures: Log Score-based measures are consistently strong, Excess risk excels for soft-OOD detection while Bayes risk is preferable for hard-OOD, and Total risk is a safe default. A key open question the paper surfaces is that no single best approximation of Excess risk exists—the optimal choice depends on whether Total risk is over- or underestimated at a given input, which is unknowable in practice. The assumption that the conditional η(x) remains well-defined for all inputs (including far OOD) is a fundamental limitation that constrains the framework's applicability to truly novel data, and the modest empirical margins (1–2 AUROC points between measures) suggest the theoretical unification, while elegant, does not yet yield dramatically superior practical tools.
