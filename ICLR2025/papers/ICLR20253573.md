## Problem

Existing backdoor defenses are evaluated against a single attack at a time, which is unrealistic for large internet-scraped datasets that may be poisoned by multiple independent attackers simultaneously. This paper demonstrates that multiple backdoors can be installed in a single model without substantial clean-accuracy degradation, and that all eight evaluated defenses fail to effectively defend in this multi-attack setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253573/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard supervised training dataset (RGB images with class labels, ranging from 32×32 CIFAR-10 to variable-size GTSRB/Imagenette up to 256×256 ImageNet) that may contain multiple simultaneous backdoor triggers, along with a small set of 250 bona fide clean examples guaranteed to be unpoisoned. It produces per-example anomaly scores (log-mean L2 distance to nearest clean trajectories) and a clean retrained model after filtering out the top 40% most anomalous examples and retraining from scratch.

## Method

BaDLoss (Backdoor Detection via Loss Dynamics) tracks the per-example loss trajectory over a short pretraining phase (30% of total epochs) and computes each example's anomaly score as the log of the mean L2 distance between its trajectory vector and the k=50 nearest bona fide clean example trajectories. The key insight is that backdoored examples exhibit anomalous training dynamics regardless of whether they are learned faster or slower than clean examples, so BaDLoss is agnostic to the direction of the anomaly—unlike Anti-Backdoor Learning, which only detects low-loss examples at a fixed epoch. The top r_reject=40% of examples by anomaly score are removed, and the model is retrained from scratch on the filtered dataset. This trajectory-based formulation generalizes ABL as a special case (single-point, threshold-based detection) and is inspired by the MAP-D technique for identifying data subsets via training dynamics.

## Results

In the multi-attack setting, BaDLoss achieves an average attack success rate (ASR) of 7.98% on CIFAR-10, 10.29% on GTSRB, and 19.17% on Imagenette, compared to the average of other defenses at 63.44%, 74.83%, and 41.74% respectively. On ImageNet-1k, BaDLoss reduces the average ASR from 88.57% (no defense) to 15.61% while maintaining 54.49% top-1 clean accuracy. Clean accuracy degradation is modest: 88.43% on CIFAR-10 (vs. 91.76% no-defense), 94.75% on GTSRB (vs. 97.09%), and 85.58% on Imagenette (vs. 90.85%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BaDLoss outperforms all eight baselines on average ASR across all three main datasets while maintaining competitive clean accuracy. The closest competitor is Causal Backdoor Defense, which achieves 15.06% ASR on CIFAR-10 and 9.66% on GTSRB, but at the cost of severe clean accuracy degradation (70.84% and 71.98% respectively, vs. BaDLoss's 88.43% and 94.75%). Among defenses that preserve clean accuracy, Spectral Signatures is the strongest on CIFAR-10 (46.36% ASR) and Cognitive Distillation leads on Imagenette (24.68% ASR), yet both are substantially outperformed by BaDLoss (7.98% and 19.17%). Neural Cleanse, Activation Clustering, Frequency Analysis, Anti-Backdoor Learning, and Poisoned Sample Sensitivity all fail to reduce ASR below 50% on at least two datasets, confirming that existing defenses do not generalize to the multi-attack setting. On ImageNet-1k, BaDLoss is the only defense evaluated, reducing ASR from 88.57% to 15.61% with only 3.5% clean accuracy loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

BaDLoss generalizes Anti-Backdoor Learning (Li et al., 2021), which flags poisoned examples by their low loss at a fixed epoch, by instead comparing each example's full loss trajectory against a small set of bona fide clean trajectories and flagging both unusually high- and low-loss dynamics. The trajectory-based scoring is inspired by MAP-D (Siddiqui et al., 2022), which uses per-example loss curves to identify data subsets. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant single-attack evaluation paradigm and from defenses that rely on attack-specific structural assumptions (mask magnitude in Neural Cleanse, activation separability in Spectral Signatures, frequency patterns in Frequency Analysis), arguing these assumptions systematically break when multiple heterogeneous attacks coexist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the multi-attack setting is both feasible (multiple backdoors install simultaneously with <1% clean accuracy loss) and that essentially all prior defenses fail in it, which is a significant negative result for the field. BaDLoss provides a practical, architecture-agnostic defense that scales to ImageNet-1k, but its 40% removal fraction causes ~7% clean accuracy degradation, and it struggles on small datasets and may disproportionately harm minority classes. Open questions include whether the detection module can be paired with lighter-weight cleansing (e.g., gradient ascent unlearning) to reduce the accuracy cost, whether adaptive attackers can mimic clean loss trajectories, and whether the approach transfers to non-vision modalities such as language models.
