## Problem

Combustion safety research lacks a high-quality, standardized, open-source benchmark for combustion fluid dynamics. Existing datasets (WildfireDB, FireSpread, Prometheus) either rely on image data without physical significance, suffer from low spatial-temporal resolution, or fail to capture multi-physics field coupling, limiting model generalization across fire scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252287/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a 4D array [T, C, H, W] representing historical time steps, physical field channels (temperature, velocity, pressure), and 2D spatial sensor grids at 0.5 m spacing on a 5 m plane, with resolutions ranging from 20×20 to 600×600 depending on the scenario. Data is stored as NPY files in (T, sensor_type, row, col) format after preprocessing from raw CSV sensor logs collected at 1 Hz. The model predicts the next time-step state Y_{t+1} for all physical fields simultaneously, enabling multi-physics coupled forecasting.

## Method

The paper introduces Open-CK, a benchmark generated by running 300 industrial park fire scenarios through FDS v6.9.1 on supercomputers (360+ hours total), varying heat release rates, ignition sources (single/triple), wind speeds (1–5 m/s), and wind directions. The core empirical contribution is a systematic evaluation of 19 models across three architecture families—spatio-temporal predictors (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, PhyDNet, SimVP, Earthfarseer), vision backbones (ResNet, U-Net, ViT, Swin Transformer, MLP-Mixer), and neural operators (FNO, LSM, CNO, UNO, NMO)—trained with MSE loss, ADAM optimizer (lr=1e-3), batch size 50, and early stopping at 500 epochs on 64 A100 GPUs. Evaluation spans error metrics (MSE, MAE), similarity metrics (SSIM, PSNR), perceptual metrics (LPIPS), physical consistency (energy spectrum error), and computational cost (params, FLOPs, FPS). Key findings show PhyDNet achieves the best spatio-temporal accuracy (MSE 0.0224), U-Net leads among vision backbones (MSE 0.0252), and CNO is the strongest neural operator (MSE 0.0251), with each family excelling in different aspects of combustion dynamics prediction.

## Results

On the Open-CK benchmark, PhyDNet achieves the best overall accuracy among spatio-temporal models with MSE 0.0224, MAE 70.02, SSIM 0.9502, and PSNR 23.72. Among vision backbones, U-Net leads with MSE 0.0252, MAE 72.00, SSIM 0.9463, and PSNR 23.20, while CNO is the top neural operator with MSE 0.0251, MAE 72.01, SSIM 0.9458, and PSNR 23.21. In terms of inference speed, SimVP (200 FPS) and U-Net (280 FPS) are the fastest, whereas PredRNNv2 is the slowest (17 FPS). All experiments are conducted on 64 NVIDIA A100 GPUs with batch size 50 and ADAM optimizer at learning rate 1e-3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the nine spatio-temporal models, PhyDNet (Guen & Thome, CVPR 2020) clearly outperforms all others on accuracy, achieving MSE 0.0224 vs. 0.0243 for PredRNN and 0.0246 for SimVPv2, while SimVP (Gao et al., CVPR 2022) offers the best speed-accuracy tradeoff at 200 FPS with MSE 0.0271. In the vision backbone category, U-Net (Ronneberger et al., MICCAI 2015) dominates with MSE 0.0252 and SSIM 0.9463, substantially outperforming Swin Transformer (MSE 0.0362) and MLP-Mixer (MSE 0.0359), though ResNet and ViT match U-Net's inference speed (~280 FPS) at the cost of higher error. For neural operators, CNO (Raonic et al., NeurIPS 2024) edges out FNO (Li et al., ICLR 2021) and UNO (Rahman et al., 2022) with MSE 0.0251 vs. 0.0298 and 0.0274 respectively, while NMO (Wu et al., 2024) and LSM (Wu et al., 2023) perform poorly (MSE > 0.032). Notably, the best spatio-temporal model (PhyDNet, MSE 0.0224) still outperforms the best vision backbone (U-Net, MSE 0.0252) and best neural operator (CNO, MSE 0.0251), suggesting that explicit temporal modeling remains critical for combustion dynamics prediction. The case study in Section 4.4 further confirms U-Net's superiority in a three-source fire scenario, achieving SSIM 0.9332 and LPIPS 9.86 versus Earthfarseer (SSIM 0.9217, LPIPS 10.02) and FNO (SSIM 0.8977, LPIPS 11.29).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by constructing the first open-source multi-physics combustion kinetics benchmark, drawing on the general paradigm of scientific ML datasets (WeatherBench, PDEBENCH, EAGLE) but departing from prior combustion-specific resources (WildfireDB, FireSpread, Prometheus) that rely on image data or offer low spatial-temporal resolution. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes a broad suite of existing spatio-temporal, vision, and neural-operator models as baselines to establish a standardized evaluation protocol for fire dynamics. The multi-physics coupling (simultaneous temperature, velocity, and pressure fields) extends the single-field focus of PDEBENCH and the single-grid environments of EAGLE and Prometheus into a more physically rich setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Open-CK provides the combustion ML community with a high-resolution, multi-physics, standardized benchmark that was previously absent, enabling fair comparison of architectures for fire prediction and management. Key limitations include reliance on simulated (FDS) data that may not capture real-world stochasticity, and the evaluation is confined to a single industrial-park geometry, raising open questions about generalization to diverse building layouts and fuel types. Future work on real-time predictive models, multi-physics coupling benefits, and integration with sensor-based sparse observations are identified as important next steps.
