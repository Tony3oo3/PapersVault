## Problem

Modern language models exhibit position bias, where performance depends on the order in which content appears in the input (e.g., favoring the first response in LM-as-a-judge, or documents at the beginning/end in retrieval-augmented QA). Prior solutions either mitigate rather than eliminate this bias, require additional training, or are limited to classification tasks. This paper identifies the two mechanistic causes—causal attention and position embedding—and proposes a training-free, zero-shot method to provably eliminate inter-document position bias.

## Input / Output

The method operates on any text input that can be partitioned into position-invariant 'documents' (retrieved passages, candidate responses, molecular properties, or math conditions). It is applied at inference time without modifying model weights, and the output format is identical to vanilla inference (a text answer, a preference label, or a generated molecule). The key constraint is that the method must be applied at every layer, every attention head, and every token to maintain the invariance guarantee; the additional computational cost is O(nk log k) where n is sequence length and k is the number of documents.

## Method

PINE eliminates position bias through two jointly necessary modifications to the attention computation. First, it replaces the causal attention mask between documents with a bidirectional mask (while preserving causal attention within each document), so that all documents attend to each other equally regardless of their input order. Second, it re-assigns key positions based on importance scores computed from raw attention values (Softmax of q·k^T/√d without position encoding), aggregated across document pairs with length normalization; documents with higher importance are placed at positions closer to the query. The proof of position invariance (Lemma 1 and Theorem 1) requires both modifications: the bidirectional mask ensures the attention pattern is order-independent, while the importance-based re-assignment ensures the position encoding applied to keys is also order-independent. The approach is training-free, works with any position embedding type (not limited to RoPE), and is applied identically across all layers and heads.

## Results

On RewardBench (LM-as-a-judge), PINE consistently improves accuracy across all model sizes, with the largest gains on the Reasoning subset: +8.1 pp (Llama-3-8B), +8.7 pp (Llama-3-70B), +11.7 pp (Qwen-1.5-1.8B), and +8.2 pp (Qwen-1.5-110B). Notably, Llama-3-70B-Instruct with PINE reaches 87.6% on the Reasoning set, surpassing GPT-4-0125-preview (86.9%) and GPT-4o-2024-08-06 (86.6%). On retrieval-augmented QA (Llama-3-70B), PINE matches vanilla inference on average (+1.2 / −2.0 for 10/20 documents) while being fully position-invariant. On R-GSM math reasoning, PINE improves accuracy by 12.6% (Qwen-1.5-7B) and 5.3% (Qwen-1.5-110B); on QM9 molecule generation, PINE improves 5 out of 6 property criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PINE is compared against two mechanical baselines that also aim for position invariance: PCW (Ratner et al., 2023) and SP (Hao et al., 2022). On Llama-3-8B-Instruct, PINE achieves 66.7% (full set) and 73.4% (reasoning), outperforming PCW (61.7% / 56.5%) by +5.0 / +16.9 pp and SP (60.8% / 55.4%) by +5.9 / +18.0 pp. On Qwen-1.5-7B-Chat, PINE reaches 61.5% (full set) and 63.0% (reasoning), beating PCW (55.2% / 53.4%) by +6.3 / +9.6 pp and SP (55.4% / 52.4%) by +6.1 / +10.6 pp. The gap is especially large on the Reasoning subset, where PINE's bidirectional inter-document attention preserves contextual information that PCW and SP discard by masking inter-document attention. PINE also outperforms permutation-based debiasing (69.0% / 65.9% for Llama-3-8B) and calibration methods (which produce incoherent outputs in open-ended generation), confirming that the mechanistic approach is superior to post-hoc correction strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the mechanistic understanding of Transformer attention, identifying causal attention and position embedding (specifically RoPE) as the two sole sources of position bias, and departs from prior mitigation strategies (calibration, data augmentation, permutation) by proposing a training-free approach with a formal invariance proof. Its closest predecessor is PCW (Ratner et al., 2023), which masks inter-document attention and assigns all documents identical positions; PINE instead makes inter-document attention bidirectional and re-assigns positions by computed importance scores, preserving cross-document context that PCW discards. The paper also contrasts with permutation-based elimination (Zheng et al., 2024a), which has O(k!) cost, and with calibration (Zhao et al., 2021), which relies on a uniform-distribution assumption that fails in open-ended generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PINE offers a provably position-invariant, training-free inference procedure that is immediately applicable to any existing Transformer LM, making it practically attractive for LM-as-a-judge reliability and RAG pipelines where document order is arbitrary. The 8–10 point gains on reasoning-pair evaluation (lifting Llama-3-70B above GPT-4-0125-preview on RewardBench reasoning) highlight a concrete reliability win for model-based evaluation. Key limitations include the O(nk log k) overhead (roughly 2×–8× wall time) and the assumption that input segments are genuinely position-invariant; the paper does not address intra-document position bias or long-sequence scaling beyond 20 documents. Open questions include whether the importance-score computation degrades gracefully with more documents, whether the approach extends to multimodal or autoregressive generation settings, and whether the computational cost can be reduced through vectorized or approximate sorting.
