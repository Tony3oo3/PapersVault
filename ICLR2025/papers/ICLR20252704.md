## Problem

RL agents suffer from sample inefficiency because blind exploration neglects causal relationships among state dimensions, actions, and rewards, leading to spurious correlations and wasted interactions. Existing causal RL methods address only one of the two reward-guided causal relationships (state-to-reward or action-to-reward) rather than both simultaneously, and lack a grounded mechanism to translate causal understanding into goal-oriented exploration behavior.

## Input / Output

The method operates on factored MDPs where state and action vectors are decomposed into individual dimensions. For state-vector environments (MuJoCo, DMControl, Meta-World, Adroit Hand), raw state and action vectors are used directly; for pixel-based tasks, IFactor's latent state encoding provides the factored representation, with reward-irrelevant latent features identified as uncontrollable dimensions. The output is a continuous action vector from a Gaussian policy network, with the policy trained via a modified SAC Bellman operator that incorporates a causality-aware empowerment term in place of standard entropy.

## Method

CIP constructs two reward-guided structural causal models using DirectLiNGAM: one mapping state dimensions to reward (M^{s→r}) and another mapping action dimensions to reward (M^{a→r}). The state-reward matrix identifies uncontrollable state dimensions, which are then swapped across paired trajectories to generate counterfactual synthetic transitions without additional environment interactions, accentuating causally relevant state information. The action-reward matrix reweights action dimensions by their causal influence on reward. A causality-aware empowerment objective is defined as the mutual information between causally-weighted actions and next states, decomposed into a causal-entropy diversity term (encouraging exploration of causally informed actions) minus a causal-entropy predictability term (encouraging actions leading to predictable outcomes via an inverse dynamics model). This empowerment term replaces standard entropy in the SAC Bellman operator, guiding the agent to prioritize actions with high causal impact on reward.

## Results

Across 8 locomotion tasks in MuJoCo and DMControl, CIP achieves the best average return in 6 out of 8 tasks, with notable gains on HalfCheetah (12594 vs. 10888 for SAC) and Cheetah Run (893 vs. 767 for SAC). In 17 Meta-World robot-arm manipulation tasks, CIP reaches a near-perfect normalized score of 100, and on the high-dimensional Adroit Hand door task (28 DoF), CIP achieves a near 100% success rate after 700k steps while all other baselines fail to make significant progress. On 3 pixel-based DMControl tasks with video distractors, CIP (applied on top of IFactor) surpasses IFactor in average return.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIP outperforms SAC on 6 of 8 locomotion tasks with statistically significant margins, most notably on HalfCheetah (+1706) and Cheetah Run (+126), while matching SAC on Hopper Stand. Against the causality-aware baseline ACE, CIP wins on all 8 locomotion tasks, with the largest gap on HalfCheetah (+3204) and Walker2d (+702). Compared to BAC, CIP wins on 6 of 8 tasks but trails slightly on Ant (6418 vs. 6511) and Quadruped Walk (948 vs. 962), though BAC's Walker2d result (3316) is substantially worse than CIP's (5624). On the Adroit Hand door task (28-dimensional action space), CIP reaches near 100% success while SAC, BAC, and ACE all fail to learn a viable policy. On pixel-based DMControl tasks, CIP (integrated with IFactor's latent encoder) surpasses IFactor alone in average return, demonstrating that the causal prioritization and empowerment components add value even when combined with a strong representation learner.

## Relation to Prior Work

CIP sits at the intersection of three research threads: causal RL (ACE, IFactor, CDL, AdaRL), counterfactual data augmentation (Pitis et al., Urpí et al.), and empowerment-based intrinsic motivation (Mohamed & Rezende, Klyubin). Prior causal RL methods address only one of the two reward-guided causal relationships—ACE models action-reward causality, IFactor models state-reward causality—while AdaRL considers both but exclusively for generalization in locomotion. CIP departs by jointly leveraging state-reward causality for counterfactual data augmentation and action-reward causality for a novel causal action empowerment objective within a single actor-critic framework, using DirectLiNGAM on factored MDPs to discover both causal matrices from collected trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding exploration in reward-relevant causal structure, CIP offers a principled mechanism to reduce sample inefficiency without additional environment interactions (counterfactual augmentation) and without incurring significant computational overhead (<10% over SAC). The consistent outperformance across 39 tasks spanning manipulation, locomotion, sparse rewards, and pixel-based settings suggests the causal prioritization principle is broadly applicable. However, the reliance on DirectLiNGAM (linear, non-Gaussian, stationary) limits applicability to real-world non-stationary or high-dimensional 3D robotic settings, and the method has not been extended to model-based RL or object-centric representations—key directions the authors identify for future work.
