## Problem

Molecular generation and molecular property prediction are both critical for drug discovery but have been developed independently, despite their shared reliance on molecular representations. Naive multi-task learning combining both tasks fails because molecular properties are only well-defined once the structure is formed, creating an inherent inconsistency with the diffusion process that operates from fully disordered to structured states. The paper asks whether a unified diffusion model can simultaneously improve both tasks and identifies the precise conditions under which the two objectives align.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model starts from Gaussian noise in the zero-center-of-mass subspace (dimension 3(M−1)) and iteratively denoises to produce 3D atomic coordinates; atom types are then predicted via a separate L1-regression head on the final clean coordinates rather than being jointly diffused. For property prediction, the model takes clean 3D coordinates at t=0 and outputs scalar property values (e.g., LUMO, HOMO, polarizability) through an L2-regression head. The nucleation time t_n=10 (out of T=1000 steps) gates when prediction losses activate, meaning the model only predicts atom types and properties during the first 1% of the diffusion trajectory where the molecular scaffold is already discernible.

## Method

UniGEM introduces a two-phase diffusion process inspired by crystal nucleation: a nucleation phase (t ∈ [t_n, T]) where only coordinate denoising occurs, and a growth phase (t ∈ [0, t_n]) where atom-type and property prediction losses are added alongside coordinate denoising. The backbone is an EGNN modified into a multi-branch architecture—shared shallow layers split into two deeper branches, one handling the nucleation phase and the other the growth phase—preventing the oversampled growth-phase training from degrading generation quality. Crucially, UniGEM decouples discrete atom types from the diffusion process entirely, treating them as a prediction task on the generated scaffold rather than jointly diffusing them as continuous one-hot vectors, which reduces the diffusion dimensionality and avoids oscillation artifacts in discrete type assignment. Theoretical analysis via the InfoMax principle shows that mutual information between intermediate representations and the original data decreases monotonically with noise level, justifying why prediction losses must be restricted to low-noise (growth) timesteps, while a generative error bound demonstrates that the reduced coordinate-only diffusion dimensionality yields tighter total variation error than joint coordinate+type diffusion.

## Results

On QM9, UniGEM achieves 99.0% atom stability, 89.8% molecule stability, 95.0% validity, and 93.2% V*U, outperforming the strongest baseline GeoLDM (98.9/89.4/93.8/92.7) and the direct EDM baseline (98.7/82.0/91.9/90.7). On GEOM-Drugs, UniGEM reaches 85.1% atom stability and 98.4% validity. For property prediction on QM9, UniGEM achieves 16.7 MAE on LUMO (vs. 25.0 for EGNN from scratch and 18.2 for 3D-EMGP), 34.5 on Δε, and 20.9 on HOMO, surpassing all pre-training baselines without requiring additional pre-training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniGEM outperforms all generation baselines on QM9 across every metric, with the largest gains over EDM (+7.8% molecule stability, +3.1% validity) and GeoLDM (+0.4% molecule stability, +1.2% validity). On GEOM-Drugs, UniGEM leads in atom stability (85.1% vs. 84.4% for GeoLDM) but trails GeoLDM on validity (98.4% vs. 99.3%). For property prediction, UniGEM (16.7 MAE on LUMO) beats EGNN from scratch (25.0), GraphMVP (26.3), 3DInfomax (25.7), GEM (27.7), and 3D-EMGP (18.2), despite using no additional pre-training data. Among the more recent baselines, UniGEM also surpasses GFMDiff (96.3% validity) and GeoBFN (95.3% validity) on QM9 generation, and EquiFM (94.7% validity). The paper further shows that simple multi-task joint training degrades both tasks (76.0% mol stability, 51.8 LUMO MAE) and that freezing the generation backbone for prediction ('Gen Pre-train') preserves generation but yields poor prediction (51.2 LUMO MAE), confirming that UniGEM's two-phase design is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniGEM builds directly on the EDM equivariant diffusion framework (Hoogeboom et al., 2022) for 3D molecular generation, adopting its EGNN backbone, noise schedule, and evaluation protocol, but fundamentally departs from the joint diffusion paradigm by decoupling discrete atom type generation from continuous coordinate generation. Conceptually, it draws on the InfoMax principle and data processing inequality to explain why naive multi-task learning fails, and borrows the nucleation/growth metaphor from crystal physics to motivate a two-phase generative process. It also relates to the denoising pre-training literature (Zaidi et al., 2022; Feng et al., 2023a; Ni et al., 2024), which showed that diffusion-based denoising learns useful molecular representations, but UniGEM integrates this insight directly into a single unified training loop rather than requiring a separate pre-training stage or additional unlabeled data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-phase generation paradigm (nucleation then growth) offers a principled way to resolve the inherent tension between generative and predictive objectives, and the authors argue its principles could transfer to other domains like NLP and computer vision where generation and prediction are similarly coupled. The theoretical error-bound analysis showing that reducing diffusion dimensionality (by treating atom types as prediction rather than generation) provably lowers total generation error provides a clear design principle for future molecular generators. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small molecules (QM9 ≤9 heavy atoms, GEOM-Drugs ≤181 atoms), a hand-tuned nucleation time hyperparameter, and the multi-branch architecture adding implementation complexity; whether the approach scales to very large biomolecular systems or benefits from the same degree of improvement remains open.
