## Problem

Standard softmax-based attention in Transformers requires positional embeddings (e.g., RoPE) to encode token order, yet these methods struggle to generalize to sequence lengths beyond the training context. The paper investigates whether replacing softmax with a stick-breaking process—which naturally encodes recency bias without any positional encoding—can alleviate length generalization failures while maintaining competitive downstream performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250146/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a causal text token sequence of length L and produces next-token predictions, with attention weights computed per head via a stick-breaking process over the preceding tokens. Unlike softmax attention, the resulting attention weights sum to at most 1 (not exactly 1), allowing the mechanism to effectively 'attend to nothing' when all intermediate logits are low. The Triton kernel processes the sequence in blocks of size 64, accumulating from right to left in the forward pass and left to right in the backward pass, maintaining O(L) memory complexity.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the softmax normalization with a stick-breaking process: for token j attending to position i, the weight is σ(z_{i,j}) × ∏_{i<k<j}(1−σ(z_{k,j})), where σ is the sigmoid applied to the attention logit. This formulation naturally biases toward the most recent high-scoring token, since any intervening high logit 'breaks' the stick and suppresses earlier positions, eliminating the need for positional embeddings. The paper derives a numerically stable log-space formulation using softplus, implements a Triton kernel adapted from Flash Attention 2 (with reversed accumulation direction and atomic adds for both ∇K and ∇V in the backward pass), and adds block skipping when cumulative weights reach 1. Experiments on 350M, 1B, and 3B parameter models show stick-breaking attention outperforms RoPE, ALiBi, FIRE, and NoPE baselines on length extrapolation, matches or exceeds softmax+RoPE on NLP benchmarks, and achieves better in-context learning on MMLU and GSM8K few-shot settings.

## Results

On the 1B model, stick-breaking attention achieves 59.5% average accuracy across 8 NLP benchmarks (vs. 58.8% for Softmax+RoPE) and 13.4 Wikitext perplexity (vs. 13.8). At 3B scale, stick-breaking reaches 64.1% average accuracy and 10.8 Wikitext perplexity, outperforming its Softmax+RoPE counterpart (63.3%, 11.3) and Qwen1.5-4B (60.7%, 12.5), though trailing Gemma2-2B (65.8%). On MMLU, the 3B stick-breaking model scores 50.8% (0-shot) and 52.9% (5-shot), surpassing Softmax+RoPE (46.1%/49.1%) and Gemma2-2B (49.3%/53.1 at 0-shot). On the RULER long-context benchmark, stick-breaking dominates Softmax+RoPE across all 13 tasks, with particularly strong needle-in-a-haystack retrieval up to 16K context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against position-encoding baselines on the 350M length-extrapolation experiment (Figure 5a), stick-breaking is the only method whose negative log-likelihood consistently decreases as context length grows from 2K to 64K; RoPE, NoPE, ALiBi, and FIRE all exhibit increasing loss at longer contexts. Specifically, NoPE (Kazemnejad et al., 2024) and RoPE (Su et al., 2021) without scaling show the worst degradation, while ALiBi (Press et al., 2021) acts as a soft window and performs better than FIRE (Li et al., 2023) but still degrades. On the MQRAR synthetic task, stick-breaking correctly retrieves variable assignments up to 192 key-value pairs, whereas Softmax+RoPE fails beyond 128, with attention visualisations confirming that RoPE heads are 'distracted' by earlier assignments. On MMLU, the 1B stick-breaking model (28.4% 0-shot, 29.3% 5-shot) outperforms TinyLlama (25.3%, 26.0%) and its own Softmax+RoPE counterpart (25.7%, 25.2%), and notably improves with few-shot examples while the Softmax model degrades. On GSM8K (3B), stick-breaking with 8-shot CoT achieves 49.7% versus 44.2% for Softmax+RoPE, a 5.5-point gain, though the 5-shot setting slightly underperforms (42.3% vs. 44.1%). On RULER, stick-breaking dominates Softmax+RoPE with scaling across all 13 tasks, with the largest gaps on needle-in-a-haystack retrieval at 8K–16K contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper scales the stick-breaking (GEM) attention mechanism from Csordas et al. (2021, 'Geometric attention' in a bidirectional encoder) and Shen et al. (2023, decoder-only ModuleFormer) to 1B–3B parameter language models, providing the first large-scale empirical study of its properties. It adapts the IO-aware block computation strategy from Flash Attention (Dao et al., 2022) into a Triton kernel, and draws a formal equivalence between stick-breaking attention heads and single-gate selective state-space models (Mamba). Conceptually, it positions stick-breaking as a learnable, score-dependent generalization of ALiBi's fixed linear decay, and as a mechanism that naturally subsumes the 'most recent' bias observed in grammar-parsing analyses of softmax attention (Allen-Zhu & Li, 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Length generalization remains a central open problem for Transformer-based LLMs, and this paper shows that a simple, position-embedding-free attention variant can consistently outperform RoPE, ALiBi, NoPE, and FIRE on extrapolation at 350M scale, while matching or exceeding softmax+RoPE on downstream NLP benchmarks at 1B–3B scale. The 29% throughput penalty relative to FA2 and the absence of validation at frontier scale (≥7B) are significant practical limitations that temper near-term adoption. Open questions include whether the efficiency gap can be closed with CUDA-level optimisation, whether stick-breaking-specific architectural modifications (remainder bias, group norm) are necessary at scale, and how the mechanism interacts with other efficiency techniques like grouped-query attention and MoE routing.
