## Problem

Existing Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) methods assume all unlabelled images share the same domain as the labelled data, ignoring the low-level covariate shift (e.g., different cameras, rendering styles, weather) commonly present in real-world unlabelled pools. This paper formalizes and tackles the dual challenge of GCD under domain shifts: the model must be robust to domain variation while remaining sensitive to semantic novelty in unlabelled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252909/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method receives a labelled set from a single source domain (with class labels) and an unlabelled pool drawn from one or more additional domains, where categories may overlap with or extend beyond the labelled set. Domain labels for unlabelled samples are unavailable; only the total number of categories is known. The model outputs a cluster assignment (category label) for every unlabelled image, evaluated via optimal-permutation clustering accuracy on both the source-domain and target-domain subsets separately.

## Method

HiLo builds on SimGCD and introduces three key mechanisms. First, it extracts domain features from the first ViT layer and semantic features from the last layer, then minimizes their mutual information via a Jensen-Shannon divergence estimator to enforce domain-semantic disentanglement. Second, it adapts PatchMix (embedding-space patch mixing) into a contrastive learning objective, using attention-weighted semantic proportions (α) to correctly weight mixed labelled/unlabelled and in-domain/out-of-domain samples for both representation and classification losses. Third, a curriculum sampling scheme gradually increases the sampling probability of unlabelled samples predicted to come from unseen domains, prioritizing semantic discovery early and cross-domain robustness later. The overall loss combines the mutual information term, semantic contrastive+classification losses, domain contrastive+classification losses, and entropy maximization regularizers.

## Results

On the SSB-C benchmark (fine-grained datasets with 45 synthetic corruptions), HiLo achieves 56.8% clustering accuracy on CUB-C (Original) and 52.0% on CUB-C (Corrupted), compared to 31.9% and 28.8% for the strongest baseline SimGCD — a relative improvement of ~44% and ~81% respectively. On the large-scale DomainNet dataset (Real+Painting), HiLo reaches 61.4% All accuracy on the source domain and 34.1% on the target domain, with a notable 41.3% on New classes in the unseen domain versus SimGCD's 33.5%. Across all three SSB-C datasets, HiLo provides 20–80% relative gains over SimGCD on corrupted (out-of-domain) samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiLo substantially outperforms all seven baselines across both DomainNet and SSB-C. Against the strongest GCD baseline SimGCD (Wen et al., ICCV 2023), HiLo gains +24.9 points on CUB-C Original All (56.8 vs 31.9) and +23.2 on CUB-C Corrupted All (52.0 vs 28.8); on DomainNet Real+Painting, the advantage is most pronounced on New classes in the unseen domain (41.3 vs 33.5, a ~23% relative gain). The non-parametric GCD method (Vaze et al., CVPR 2022) and ORCA (Cao et al., ICLR 2021) are further behind, with GCD reaching only 26.6% on CUB-C Original All. NCD-adapted baselines RankStats+ (Han et al., TPAMI 2021) and UNO+ (Fini et al., ICCV 2021) perform worst on corrupted domains, confirming that methods ignoring domain shift degrade severely. The UDA method UniOT (Chang et al., NeurIPS 2022) and SFA (Li et al., ICCV 2021) also fall well short of HiLo, demonstrating that standard domain adaptation techniques are insufficient for the dual challenge of category discovery under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) framework of Vaze et al. (2022) and SimGCD (Wen et al., 2023) by introducing domain shifts into the unlabelled data, a setting previously assumed away. Methodologically, it adapts PatchMix from the unsupervised domain adaptation literature (Zhu et al., 2023) into a contrastive learning objective tailored for open-world category discovery, and introduces a mutual information minimization loss (via Jensen-Shannon estimation) to disentangle low-level domain features from high-level semantic features in a ViT backbone. The curriculum sampling strategy draws from curriculum learning but is specifically designed to progressively incorporate cross-domain samples, bridging the gap between single-domain GCD and multi-domain robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that all existing GCD methods degrade substantially when unlabelled data contains domain shifts, a condition that is ubiquitous in web-scale and real-world deployments. The SSB-C benchmark (45× larger than SSB) provides a valuable fine-grained evaluation resource, and the finding that simply bolting UDA techniques onto GCD methods yields only marginal gains highlights a fundamental tension between domain invariance and semantic novelty detection that remains open. Limitations include the assumption that the number of categories is known, reliance on DINO pretraining, and the assumption that all labelled samples share a single domain; extending to truly open-world settings with unknown category counts and multiple source domains would be a natural next step.
