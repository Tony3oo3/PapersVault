## Problem

Existing RAG methods struggle with knowledge-intensive reasoning tasks because the useful information needed to answer such questions is badly scattered across many documents, making it difficult to retrieve all relevant chunks without substantial noise and to perform integrated global reasoning. While cognitive science shows that humans convert raw information into task-appropriate structured knowledge (tables, graphs, etc.) to shorten reasoning paths, no prior RAG system dynamically selects and constructs the optimal structure type at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a question and a set of up to 20+ documents (totaling up to 200K tokens) as input. The router consumes only the question plus core content (titles or first sentences) of each document to select one of five structure types (table, graph, algorithm, catalogue, chunk). The structurizer then processes each document individually to produce structured knowledge in the chosen format—markdown tables, head-relationship-tail triplets, pseudo-code, hierarchical numbered text, or plain chunks—along with a textual description. The final output is an answer generated by decomposing the question into sub-questions, extracting precise knowledge for each, and integrating them.

## Method

StructRAG is a three-module inference-time framework: (1) a Hybrid Structure Router, a Qwen2-7B model trained with DPO on 900 synthetic preference pairs (constructed via a synthesizing-simulating-judging pipeline) to select the optimal structure type from five candidates; (2) a Scattered Knowledge Structurizer (Qwen2-72B) that converts each raw document into structured knowledge of the selected type using type-specific prompts; and (3) a Structured Knowledge Utilizer that decomposes complex questions into sub-questions, extracts precise knowledge for each from the assembled structured knowledge, and infers the final answer. The key architectural insight is that the router's structure-type selection is the most critical component—ablation shows removing it drops overall LLM score from 60.38 to 45.33—and that no single fixed structure type suffices across diverse task types, validating the cognitive fit principle that matching representation format to task demands improves reasoning accuracy.

## Results

On the Loong benchmark (knowledge-intensive multi-document QA), StructRAG achieves an overall LLM-judged score of 60.38 and EM rate of 0.23, outperforming all baselines across all four document-length settings (10K–250K tokens). The advantage grows with document length: StructRAG leads Long-context by ~9 points on Set 1 (69.43 vs. 60.11) but by ~23 points on Set 4 (51.42 vs. 28.92). On Podcast Transcripts (query-focused summarization), StructRAG achieves a 95.75% head-to-head win rate against Long-context and 53–62% win rates against RAG, RQ-RAG, and GraphRAG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StructRAG outperforms all four baselines on every document-length setting of the Loong benchmark. Against Long-context (Qwen2-72B with 128K window), StructRAG gains 9.3, 15.2, 22.0, and 22.5 LLM-score points on Sets 1–4 respectively, showing the gap widens as information becomes more scattered. Against standard RAG (Lewis et al., 2020), StructRAG leads by 23.3, 15.5, 15.3, and 22.1 points; against RQ-RAG (Chan et al., 2024), by 15.9, 13.9, 17.0, and 19.5 points. Against GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024), which extracts entity-relation triples into multi-layered graphs, StructRAG leads by 28.6, 27.9, 24.6, and 27.9 points, and is also ~22× faster in total latency (9.7 min vs. 217.1 min). On Podcast Transcripts, StructRAG achieves a 95.75% average win rate over Long-context but only 53–62% over the RAG-family baselines, suggesting the advantage is most pronounced in long-document, multi-hop reasoning rather than summarization.

## Relation to Prior Work

StructRAG extends the RAG paradigm (Lewis et al., 2020) by replacing chunk-based retrieval with task-adaptive knowledge structurization, drawing on cognitive fit theory (Vessey, 1991) to argue that different structure types (table, graph, algorithm, catalogue, chunk) suit different reasoning tasks. It departs from GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024) by not restricting augmentation to entity-relation triplets, and from RQ-RAG (Chan et al., 2024) by performing question decomposition *after* structurization rather than before retrieval. The DPO-based router training (Rafailov et al., 2023) is repurposed from general preference alignment to the specific decision problem of structure-type selection, with a novel synthesizing-simulating-judging pipeline to generate the required preference data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of current RAG systems—scattered information across long documents—and offers a principled, cognitively-motivated solution that is both more accurate and ~22× faster than GraphRAG. However, the approach's reliance on LLMs for structurization introduces a non-trivial error source (visible in the EM-rate gap), and the five structure types may not cover all reasoning patterns. Open questions include whether the router generalizes to unseen structure types, whether structurization quality degrades with domain shift, and whether the DPO training data pipeline can be automated for new domains without manual seed tasks.
