## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) used as supervisory signals in masked image modeling produce spatially inconsistent token representations, where neighboring tokens receive conflicting class predictions. This spatial noise in token-wise targets hinders the online encoder from learning discriminative representations, as the model must fit a one-to-one mapping to noisy, incoherent targets. The paper identifies this inconsistency as a previously unaddressed bottleneck in MIM pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253355/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images patchified into 196 tokens (16×16 patches) for classification pre-training, or 512×512 images for ADE20K segmentation. The online encoder receives block-wise masked patches (40% masking ratio) while the target encoder (CLIP-B/16) receives the unmasked image; both produce N×d token representations. The output is a set of learned visual representations that, after fine-tuning, yield class labels (ImageNet-1K) or per-pixel segmentation masks (ADE20K). The morphing matrix M ∈ {0,1}^{n̄×N} is derived solely from target tokens and applied symmetrically to both online and target representations.

## Method

Dynamic Token Morphing (DTM) addresses spatial inconsistency by dynamically aggregating contextually related tokens into morphed tokens while preserving the full contextual information. A dynamic scheduler samples the final token count n̄ ~ U(N̄, N) and iteration count k ~ U(1, K), then progressively reduces tokens via k rounds of bipartite matching on the target representations, producing a morphing matrix M that maps each original token to exactly one morphed group. The morphed online and target tokens are computed as normalized weighted averages (Eq. 3), and the DTM loss (Eq. 4) aligns corresponding morphed pairs using cosine distance weighted by group size. Multiple DTM losses with independently sampled (n̄, k) pairs are summed (Eq. 5), covering a spectrum from token-wise (n̄=N) to image-level (n̄=1) supervision. The dynamic randomness in both the number of remaining tokens and the number of morphing iterations is critical: ablations show that fixing these values degrades performance by 0.3–0.5%p, while the full dynamic scheduler yields +1.1%p over the baseline.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K fine-tuning (300-epoch pre-training with CLIP B/16), DTM achieves 83.2%, 85.4%, and 86.7% top-1 accuracy with ViT-S/16, ViT-B/16, and ViT-L/16 respectively, surpassing the strongest CLIP-based MIM baselines (BEiT v2: 85.0%, MVP: 84.4%, DeepMIM: 84.8%) by 0.4–1.0 percentage points at ViT-B scale. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, DTM reaches 53.1% mIoU, outperforming FD-CLIP (52.8%) and BEiT v2 (52.7%). Transfer learning on iNaturalist 2018 shows a +3.5 pp gain over the baseline (78.5% vs 75.0%), and fine-grained classification (FGVC) average improves by +0.9 pp (91.4% vs 90.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTM outperforms all CLIP-based MIM methods at ViT-B/16 scale: +0.4 pp over BEiT v2 (85.4 vs 85.0), +1.0 pp over MVP (85.4 vs 84.4), +0.6 pp over DeepMIM (85.4 vs 84.8), and +0.5 pp over FD-CLIP (85.4 vs 84.9). At ViT-L/16, DTM edges out BEiT v2 and data2vec by +0.1 pp (86.7 vs 86.6) and surpasses MVP by +0.4 pp (86.7 vs 86.3). On ADE20K segmentation, DTM leads FD-CLIP by +0.3 pp (53.1 vs 52.8) and BEiT v2 by +0.4 pp (53.1 vs 52.7). Transfer learning gains are substantial: DTM improves iNaturalist 2018 by +3.5 pp (78.5 vs 75.0), iNaturalist 2019 by +0.8 pp (81.9 vs 81.1), and FGVC average by +0.9 pp (91.4 vs 90.5) over its own baseline without DTM. DTM also generalizes beyond CLIP, improving SLIP-based pre-training by +0.3 to +0.5 pp and extending to MAE+CLIP (+0.6 pp), BEiT v2 (+0.2 pp), and BYOL (+0.4 pp) frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the BEiT v2 CLIP-distillation MIM framework, where a frozen vision-language model provides token-wise supervisory targets for a masked ViT. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'spatial inconsistency' in these token-wise targets—neighboring tokens receiving contradictory class signals—and proposing Dynamic Token Morphing (DTM) to address it via context-preserving token aggregation using bipartite matching. This distinguishes DTM from prior token aggregation work (e.g., Token Merging, LIT) that targets computational efficiency through token reduction; here, aggregation is a representational tool that preserves token count while smoothing noisy targets. The paper also positions itself against the broader MIM literature (MAE, iBOT, data2vec, MVP, DeepMIM) by arguing that the quality of the supervisory signal, not just its source, is the critical factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spatial inconsistency diagnostic is a useful and previously underappreciated lens for understanding why token-wise distillation from pre-trained models underperforms its potential, and DTM offers a lightweight, broadly applicable fix (works with CLIP, SLIP, MAE, BEiT v2, BYOL) that achieves SOTA ImageNet-1K and ADE20K results at only 300 pre-training epochs—surpassing methods requiring 800–1600 epochs. The finding that non-dynamic (fixed) token morphing actually degrades performance relative to the baseline highlights the importance of the dynamic scheduling component. Limitations include verification only up to ViT-L/16 (no ViT-H or ViT-G), and the moderate absolute gains (0.4–1.0%p) over already-strong CLIP-based baselines suggest the ceiling of this particular signal-quality improvement may be approaching.
