## Problem

4D content generation (dynamic 3D objects) is bottlenecked by the scarcity of synchronized multi-view video data, making it infeasible to train a single diffusion model that captures both geometric consistency and temporal smoothness. Existing 4D methods rely on slow score distillation sampling (SDS) with recurrent diffusion evaluations, while the efficient 3D-aware image diffusion paradigm cannot be directly extended to 4D. The paper asks whether pretrained video and multi-view diffusion models can be combined in a training-free manner to directly sample consistent multi-view videos without any 4D dataset.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a reference image, a single-view driving video, or a text prompt as input and produces a continuous 4D representation in the form of deformable 3D Gaussians with a learned deformation field. Internally, it synthesizes a dense image matrix of 21 orbital views × 25 frames at 576×576 resolution in a shared latent space (SVD/SV3D), which then supervises the Gaussian optimization over 5,000 iterations. The full pipeline completes in roughly 20 minutes on 8 A6000 GPUs, with the parallel denoising stage taking about 8.5 minutes.

## Method

The core contribution is a score composition theorem: under a conditional independence assumption on the V×F image matrix, the score of the joint distribution decomposes into the sum of a multi-view score (along views) and a video score (along frames) minus a single-image score, enabling direct sampling with off-the-shelf SVD and SV3D denoisers in a single reverse diffusion pass. In practice, the intractable single-image score is replaced by a convex combination (weight s=0.5) of the two model scores, and an augmented inpainting formulation handles conditional generation from images, videos, or text. To resolve distributional conflicts between the two heterogeneous models, the paper introduces variance-reducing sampling (VRS), which inserts interpolated forward-noise steps during the first five denoising iterations to merge divergent modes at higher noise levels at only ~10% extra cost. The generated image matrix then drives a geometry-texture decoupled reconstruction: static Gaussians are first optimized on the reference frame's multi-view images for 3,000 warm-up iterations, after which the deformation field is trained on the full matrix while fine-tuning Gaussians at reduced scale for non-reference views.

## Results

On the Consistent4D test split (video-to-4D), Diffusion² achieves the best CLIP score (0.922), lowest LPIPS (12.35), and lowest FVD (831.32) among all compared methods, while matching the fastest generation time of 12 minutes (tied with DG4D and Efficient4D). In the user study on image-to-4D, Diffusion² receives the highest preference on all four criteria: reference consistency (88.3%), multi-view consistency (58.9%), temporal smoothness (43.1%), and overall model quality (59.6%). The method completes full 4D generation in ~20 minutes on 8 A6000 GPUs, with the first stage taking 8 min 31 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diffusion² outperforms all three baselines on every quantitative metric in the video-to-4D task. Compared to Consistent4D (SDS-based, 120 min), it improves CLIP by 0.017, reduces LPIPS by 0.46, lowers FVD by 62.35, and is 10× faster. Against DreamGaussian4D (DG4D, 11 min), it achieves substantially better perceptual quality (LPIPS 12.35 vs. 16.17) and temporal coherence (FVD 831 vs. 1144) at comparable speed, while also improving CLIP by 0.037. Versus Efficient4D (12 min), it wins on all three quality metrics (CLIP +0.005, LPIPS −2.31, FVD −41.93) at the same generation time. In the user study, Diffusion² dominates on reference consistency (88.3% vs. ≤5.5%) and overall quality (59.6% vs. ≤18.2%), with STAG4D being the closest competitor on temporal smoothness (36.2% vs. 43.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SDS-based paradigm for 4D generation (MAV3D, 4D-fy, Consistent4D, DreamGaussian4D) by eliminating iterative optimization entirely. Instead of distilling 2D diffusion priors into a 4D representation over thousands of gradient steps, it directly samples a dense multi-view multi-frame image matrix in a single reverse diffusion pass by composing scores from a pretrained video diffusion model (SVD) and a multi-view diffusion model (SV3D). The theoretical justification rests on a conditional independence property of the image matrix (Theorem 3.1), which allows the joint score to decompose into row-wise and column-wise scores. For reconstruction, it builds on deformable 3D Gaussians (Yang et al., 2024) with a geometry-texture decoupled training strategy, distinguishing it from prior 4D Gaussian approaches that optimize with single-view driving videos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework eliminates the need for expensive, hard-to-scale synchronized multi-view video data, positioning 4D generation to benefit directly from the ongoing scaling of foundation video and multi-view diffusion models. The ~20-minute generation time (vs. 90–120 minutes for SDS-based methods) and the training-free, architecture-agnostic design make it a practical and extensible pipeline. Key limitations include the conditional independence assumption breaking down for extreme rotations, texture degradation in views far from the reference due to aggressive variance reduction, and a memory-efficiency tradeoff inherent to the parallel denoising design. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-orbital camera paths, longer temporal sequences, and whether the shared-latent-space requirement between video and multi-view models will be naturally satisfied by future foundation models.
