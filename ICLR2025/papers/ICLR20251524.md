## Problem

Existing geometric GNNs for RNA structure evaluation suffer from two key limitations: they either lose directional information (invariant GNNs like PaxNet) or incur high computational cost (high-degree equivariant models like ARES), and neither is explicitly designed to handle the size generalization challenge where training RNAs (50–100 nt) are substantially smaller than test RNAs (100–200 nt). This size gap is a practical necessity because experimentally determined large RNA structures are extremely scarce—only 3.60% of high-resolution entries exceed 100 nucleotides.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a full-atom 3D RNA candidate structure (heavy atoms only, excluding hydrogens) represented as a graph with 3D coordinates and atom-type embeddings, where each nucleotide is a joint graph of its ribose-phosphate backbone and nucleobase. It outputs a single scalar predicted RMSD value per candidate, which is used to rank all FARFAR2-generated candidates and select the one closest to the native structure. The size-generalization protocol trains exclusively on 50–100 nt RNAs (200 RNAs, 80k candidates) and evaluates on 100–200 nt RNAs (15 RNAs, 6k candidates), with TM-score < 0.45 enforced between train and test to prevent leakage.

## Method

EquiRNA is a three-level E(3)-equivariant GNN built on the EGNN backbone that performs hierarchical message passing: (1) atom-level equivariant updates within the ribose-phosphate (ERN) and nucleobase (EBN) subgraphs, (2) subunit-level attention-based cross-interaction between ribose-phosphate and nucleobase atoms (EB2R/ER2B), and (3) nucleotide-level message passing using learnable atom and nucleotide templates to produce fixed-size messages despite variable atom counts per nucleobase. The key insight for size generalization is that nucleotide representations are computed identically regardless of total RNA length, so the model reuses the same building-block features across different sizes. A size-insensitive K-nearest neighbor sampling strategy (sample K from M neighbors during training) further regularizes the model against size shifts. The entire pipeline preserves E(3)-equivariance, and the final RMSD prediction is obtained by summing nucleotide features and passing through an MLP with Smooth L1 loss.

## Results

On the newly introduced rRNAsolo dataset (train: 50–100 nt, test: 100–200 nt), EquiRNA achieves a test-set Mean RMSD of 18.22 Å and Median RMSD of 17.79 Å, outperforming the best baseline GET (20.03 / 19.19) by 1.81 and 1.40 Å respectively. The Relative Error of Mean RMSD drops to 0.48 (vs. 0.63 for GET and 0.84 for ARES), representing up to a 17% improvement over prior SOTA. On the existing ARES benchmark, EquiRNA likewise leads on all four metrics (Mean RMSD 11.74, Median RMSD 9.57, R-ER 0.93, R-DR 0.62), surpassing the original ARES model by 0.59 Å in Mean RMSD and 1.65 Å in Median RMSD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiRNA outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both rRNAsolo and ARES. On rRNAsolo (test), the closest competitor is GET (Kong et al., 2024), a generalist equivariant transformer, which trails EquiRNA by 1.81 Å in Mean RMSD and 1.40 Å in Median RMSD; RDesign (Tan et al., 2024) is second-closest at 20.22 / 19.34. The original ARES model (Townshend et al., 2021), which uses high-degree irreducible representations, degrades noticeably on rRNAsolo (20.98 / 20.89) compared to its performance on its own ARES dataset (12.33 / 11.22), suggesting limited robustness to size shifts. PaxNet (Zhang et al., 2022b), an invariant GNN, is the weakest equivariant-agnostic baseline on rRNAsolo (21.64 / 21.53), confirming the importance of preserving directional information. On ARES, EquiRNA beats the ARES model by 0.59 Å (Mean RMSD) and 1.65 Å (Median RMSD), and outperforms dyMEAN (Kong et al., 2023b, a protein-domain method) by 1.82 Å in Mean RMSD. The Relative Ranking metric (Table 4) further confirms EquiRNA's superiority: 0.280 on rRNAsolo and 0.238 on ARES, versus 0.423–0.628 for all other methods on rRNAsolo.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on EGNN (Satorras et al., 2021) as a scalarization-based equivariant backbone, departing from prior RNA evaluation methods that either discard directional information (PaxNet, which operates on invariant scalars like bond angles) or incur high computational cost through high-degree irreducible representations (ARES, SE(3)-Transformer). The key conceptual departure is a biologically-motivated three-level hierarchical decomposition (atom → subunit → nucleotide) that treats nucleotides as reusable building blocks, combined with a size-insensitive KNN sampling strategy to explicitly address the train-small/test-large gap. The paper also introduces rRNAsolo, a new benchmark that extends the existing ARES dataset to a much wider size range (50–200 nt) with more recent structures, specifically designed to stress-test size generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The size generalization problem is practically critical because experimentally determined large RNA structures remain scarce (only ~17% of RNAsolo entries exceed 100 nt), yet therapeutically relevant RNAs are often large. The rRNAsolo dataset and the Relative Ranking metric provide the community with a more rigorous and size-aware evaluation protocol. Open questions include whether the hierarchical nucleotide-reuse strategy scales beyond 200 nt, whether the FARFAR2 candidate quality bottleneck limits the ceiling of any evaluator, and whether the approach transfers to other hierarchical biomolecular systems (e.g., proteins with secondary-structure elements). The finding that E(3)-invariance (not SE(3)) is the appropriate symmetry for RMSD scoring, and that adding chirality-aware SBDD terms slightly hurts performance, is a useful clarification for the field.
