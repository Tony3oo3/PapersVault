## Problem

Generative masked transformers excel at modeling large-scale motion distributions but overfit to sequence-level global patterns when applied to a single reference motion, causing model collapse. The challenge of learning diverse yet faithful internal motion patterns from just one MoCap sequence—without large datasets—remains unexplored, as standard global self-attention and standard VQ training both fail under this data constraint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252808/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference motion sequence of arbitrary length and skeleton topology (human, animal, or creature) and produces novel motion sequences of arbitrary generated length L_g, decoupled from the reference duration. Internally, the motion is tokenized via 1D convolutional encoding with a down-sampling factor of 8 into a sequence of discrete tokens drawn from a codebook of 48 entries, and generation proceeds autoregressively in sliding windows. Downstream variants accept additional constraints: fixed sub-parts for temporal editing, or beat features extracted via librosa for rhythm-aligned dance synthesis.

## Method

MotionDreamer combines two key innovations to adapt generative masked modeling to single-instance synthesis. First, a codebook distribution regularization loss (KL divergence between the posterior token distribution and a uniform prior) is added to the VQ training objective to prevent codebook collapse and under-utilization of entries that would otherwise blur learned patterns. Second, the standard transformer backbone is replaced with SlidAttn layers that unfold the token sequence into overlapping local windows (size 2W+1, stride S), compute attention within each window using window-wise relative positional encoding and learnable queries, and aggregate outputs via overlap attention fusion (AttnFuse) that blends overlapping regions by average voting while passing non-overlapping regions through directly. A differentiable dequantization module using sparsemax enables gradient flow from the decoded motion back into the transformer, adding a reconstruction loss alongside the masked prediction loss. At inference, a fully-masked template is filled progressively in sliding windows with iterative re-masking of low-confidence tokens, enabling generation of sequences longer than the reference.

## Results

On the SinMotion dataset (60 reference motions from Mixamo and Truebone-ZOO), MotionDreamer achieves a Harmonic Mean of 0.43, improving over the best baseline SinMDM (0.36) by 19%. It attains the highest Global Diversity (1.33), Inter Diversity (0.25), and lowest Intra Diversity (0.28) among all methods, while maintaining competitive Coverage (93.47%). In user studies with 20 participants, MotionDreamer scores highest in both coverage and diversity, and is comparable to GenMM in naturalness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionDreamer outperforms all three baselines on the Harmonic Mean (0.43 vs. 0.36 for SinMDM, 0.32 for GenMM, 0.26 for Ganimator). Against SinMDM (diffusion-based), MotionDreamer wins on Coverage (93.47% vs. 91.82%), Global Diversity (1.33 vs. 1.31), Inter Diversity (0.25 vs. 0.22), and Intra Diversity (0.28 vs. 0.32), while SinMDM edges ahead on Local Diversity (1.20 vs. 1.17). Against GenMM (non-parametric motion matching), MotionDreamer achieves substantially higher diversity across all four diversity metrics (e.g., Global Div. 1.33 vs. 0.50) while GenMM retains a slight Coverage advantage (95.29% vs. 93.47%). Against Ganimator (GAN-based), MotionDreamer dominates on every metric, with the largest gap in Global Diversity (1.33 vs. 0.97) and Harmonic Mean (0.43 vs. 0.26). In the user study, MotionDreamer is rated highest in coverage and diversity, and is closely comparable to GenMM in naturalness, while clearly surpassing SinMDM across all perceptual dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionDreamer sits at the intersection of two research threads: single-instance motion synthesis (Ganimator, SinMDM, GenMM) and generative masked transformers for motion (MoMask, MMM, T2M-GPT). It departs from prior single-instance methods by replacing implicit continuous latent modeling (GAN/diffusion) or non-parametric patch matching with explicit categorical distribution modeling over a discrete codebook, and departs from prior masked motion transformers by shrinking the global self-attention to a sliding-window local attention (SlidAttn) to prevent overfitting on a single sequence. The codebook distribution regularization loss (KL-based) is a new training objective that addresses under-utilization of code entries, a problem not previously tackled in the single-sequence VQ setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the overfitting failure mode of generative masked transformers on single sequences can be resolved architecturally (local attention) rather than through data augmentation or regularization hacks, offering a clean design principle for few-data generative modeling. The codebook distribution regularization shows transferable benefits across VQ architectures (validated on MMM and MoMask in Appendix A.7), suggesting broader utility beyond this specific task. Key limitations include dependence on reference motion quality, limited generalization beyond the single-instance setting, and the inability to capture long-range global dependencies—open questions for extending to few-shot or multi-style synthesis.
