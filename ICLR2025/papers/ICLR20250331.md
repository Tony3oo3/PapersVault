## Problem

Transformer inference scales quadratically with sequence length, and existing linear-complexity KV caching methods (e.g., Streaming LLM) naively evict tokens at a fixed window boundary, irreversibly losing important context. Moreover, these methods process prompts one token at a time or with full quadratic attention, making prefill latency worse than Flash Attention 2 at realistic sequence lengths. The paper asks: can we extend the effective context span of a fixed-size cache exponentially while maintaining linear complexity, without any retraining, and with a fast linear prefill?

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained transformer (Llama 3.1 8B/70B, Qwen2 7B/72B) and a token sequence, consuming the prompt in fixed-size strided chunks (e.g., 4K tokens per stride) during prefill rather than one token at a time. The KV cache is fixed in total size (e.g., 65K tokens plus 64 sink tokens), but the effective token span it covers grows exponentially with the number of cascades (up to 8), reaching spans of 1M+ tokens. Output is standard autoregressive token generation, with per-token attention score EMAs maintained internally to drive eviction decisions.

## Method

The core mechanism splits a fixed-size KV cache into N cascading sub-caches, where each successive sub-cache accepts only a fraction (1/2) of the tokens evicted from the previous one, creating an exponential token span (S̃ = |C|/N · Σ 2^(i-1)) while keeping total cache memory constant. At sub-cache boundaries where a token is not accepted, the method compares the EMA attention score of the incoming token against the newest token already in the target sub-cache and retains the higher-scoring one, enabling dynamic, importance-based eviction rather than fixed-position discarding. A strided prefill algorithm processes the prompt in parallel chunks of size K (between 1 and S), computing dense attention within each chunk against the current cache, which reduces 1M-token prefill latency by 6.8× over Flash Attention 2. Circular buffers with tracked insertion pointers replace costly tensor concatenation for cache updates, and positional encodings are reassigned by cache index to maintain relative position semantics. The entire approach is training-free and requires no architectural modification to the pretrained model.

## Results

On PG19 streaming perplexity, Cascading KV Cache achieves 7.72 (Llama 3.1 8B, 16K cache) and 7.60 (65K cache), consistently outperforming Streaming LLM (7.78, 7.61) and Flash Attention 2 (8.08, 7.73). On LongBench, the method delivers a 12.13% average improvement over the next-best linear baseline (31.8 vs. 26.37 mean for Llama 3.1 8B Instruct). On BookSum, it achieves 4.48% average ROUGE improvement over the best linear baseline, and on passkey retrieval at 1M tokens with a 65K cache, it maintains 24 percentage points higher accuracy than Streaming LLM after four doublings of context length. The strided prefill strategy reduces 1M-token latency by 6.8× compared to Flash Attention 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Streaming LLM (Xiao et al., 2023), Cascading KV Cache wins on every benchmark: lower PG19 perplexity (7.72 vs. 7.78 at 16K), higher LongBench mean (31.8 vs. 26.37 for Llama 3.1 8B), better BookSum ROUGE (34.47 vs. 33.04 Rouge-1), and dramatically superior passkey retrieval (maintaining >10% accuracy after 4 doublings vs. near-random after the first). Against Flash Attention 2 (Dao, 2023), the method achieves lower PG19 perplexity and is 6.8× faster on 1M tokens, while also outperforming truncated Flash Attention on LongBench (31.8 vs. 21.39). Against BigBird (Beltagy et al., 2020), Cascading KV Cache wins on PG19 (7.72 vs. 12.76 for Llama 3.1 8B at 16K) and LongBench (31.8 vs. 25.18). Against quadratic score-based eviction methods SnapKV (Li et al., 2024) and H2O (Zhang et al., 2024), Cascading KV Cache trails slightly on BookSum ROUGE-1 (34.47 vs. 36.80 and 35.62) but operates at O(N) complexity versus their O(N²) prefill. Against MInference (Jiang et al., 2024) and PyramidKV (Cai et al., 2024), the method achieves higher LongBench scores (31.8 vs. 25.72 and 25.63) and avoids OOM at 1M tokens where MInference fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly generalizes Streaming LLM (Xiao et al., 2023), which introduced attention sinks and a fixed sliding-window KV cache for streaming inference, by decomposing the single window into cascading sub-caches that accept tokens at decreasing rates. It departs from score-based eviction methods like H2O and SnapKV, which still require a quadratic prefill stage, by introducing a strided prefill that processes fixed-size chunks in parallel, achieving linear complexity throughout both prompt and decode phases. The token selection mechanism (EMA of attention scores) is conceptually related to H2O's heavy-hitter identification but is applied at cache boundaries rather than as a global eviction policy, and the circular-buffer implementation addresses a practical inefficiency in Streaming LLM's concatenation-based cache management.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is training-free and immediately applicable to any pretrained transformer, offering a 6.8× latency reduction over Flash Attention 2 at 1M tokens and extending effective context by four doublings beyond the cache size without additional memory. A key open question is the fundamental trade-off between sparsity and accuracy: performance degrades beyond 8 cascades (>96% window sparsity), and the authors suggest O(N log N) top-k search as a path to eliminate eviction entirely. The practical impact is significant for streaming and resource-constrained deployments where quadratic attention is prohibitive, though the method still cannot match full-context quadratic models on tasks requiring dense recall across the entire sequence.
