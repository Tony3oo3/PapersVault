## Problem

No algorithm with theoretical guarantees exists to find a near-optimal policy in a tabular robust constrained MDP (RCMDP), where one must minimize worst-case cost while robustly satisfying constraints across an uncertainty set of environments. The conventional Lagrangian max-min formulation, the primary existing approach, can be trapped in suboptimal local minima when the policy gradients of the objective and constraint functions conflict and cancel out, and neither dynamic programming nor linear programming is applicable due to the nonconvexity of the robust formulation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a tabular RCMDP specification (finite state and action spaces, discount factor, uncertainty set U, cost functions c_0 through c_N, constraint thresholds b_1 through b_N, and initial state distribution μ) together with two oracle interfaces: a robust policy evaluator that approximates J_{c_n,U}(π) within ε_est and a subgradient evaluator that approximates ∂J_{c_n,U}(π) within ε_grd. It outputs an ε-optimal policy π (satisfying both the objective gap and constraint violation bounds) along with the optimal return value J*, requiring Õ(ε^{-4}) robust policy evaluations in total. The uncertainty set U need not be rectangular; it can be finite, (s,a)-rectangular KL, ball, or R-contamination, as long as the evaluators are implementable.

## Method

The key insight is transforming the RCMDP into an epigraph form: min_{b_0} b_0 subject to min_π max_n {J_{c_n,U}(π) − b_n} ≤ 0, where the max operator over cost indices means the policy gradient at each step selects a single subgradient (from whichever cost is most violated) rather than summing conflicting gradients from the objective and constraints. This eliminates the gradient-conflict pathology that causes the Lagrangian approach to stall at local minima. The resulting algorithm, EpiRC-PGS, uses a double-loop structure: an outer bisection search over the threshold b_0 ∈ [0,H] (exploiting the monotonicity of the auxiliary optimum Δ*_{b_0} in b_0), and an inner projected policy gradient subroutine that solves the auxiliary problem min_π Δ_{b_0}(π) by iteratively selecting the most-violated cost index and applying its subgradient. Under a coverage assumption on the initial distribution, Theorem 4 proves that every stationary point of Δ_{b_0}(π) is globally optimal, and Corollary 1 establishes that EpiRC-PGS returns an ε-optimal policy in Õ(ε^{-4}) robust policy evaluations.

## Results

The paper evaluates EpiRC-PGS in three settings (finite uncertainty set, (s,a)-rectangular KL uncertainty set, and CMDP) with five constraints (N=5), averaged over 10 random seeds. Results are presented as convergence plots (Figure 3) rather than a single numerical table. EpiRC-PGS rapidly converges to a feasible policy with a low objective cost return across all three settings, while Lagrangian-based baselines fail to identify feasible policies in the robust settings (a) and (b). In the CMDP setting (c), EpiRC-PGS and the occupancy-measure-averaged Lagrangian baseline both converge to a near-optimal policy, but the policy-averaged Lagrangian baseline does not.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EpiRC-PGS is compared against three Lagrangian-based baselines: LF (standard Lagrangian policy gradient), LF-π^(k)-avg (with policy averaging), and LF-d_P^π^(k)-avg (with occupancy measure averaging, CMDP-only). In the finite uncertainty set setting (a), both LF and LF-π^(k)-avg fail to identify any feasible policy, while EpiRC-PGS rapidly converges to a feasible, low-cost policy. In the KL uncertainty set setting (b), LF fails to find a feasible policy, again while EpiRC-PGS succeeds. In the CMDP setting (c), EpiRC-PGS and LF-d_P^π^(k)-avg both converge to a near-optimal policy (matching the LP-computed optimum), but LF-π^(k)-avg does not. The key differentiator is that EpiRC-PGS avoids the gradient conflict problem inherent in the Lagrangian formulation (Theorem 1), where conflicting gradients from the objective and constraint can trap policy gradient at a local minimum, whereas the epigraph form selects a single gradient via the max operator, ensuring no local minima under the coverage assumption (Theorem 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Lagrangian formulation approach for RCMDPs (Russel et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2022) by demonstrating that it is fundamentally flawed due to gradient conflicts between objective and constraint terms under multiple worst-case environments. Instead, it repurposes the epigraph form from classical constrained optimization (Boyd & Vandenberghe, 2004) to the RCMDP setting, combining it with the direct-parameterization policy gradient framework of Agarwal et al. (2021) and robust DP machinery from Iyengar (2005) and Yang et al. (2023). The paper also corrects a crucial error in the stationary-point optimality proof of Wang et al. (2023), extending their result to infinite uncertainty sets via Sion's minimax theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm with a rigorous guarantee of finding an ε-optimal policy in a tabular RCMDP, filling a notable gap where neither DP, LP, nor Lagrangian approaches could provide such guarantees. The negative result (Theorem 1) showing that the standard Lagrangian + policy gradient method can be trapped in local minima even with only two environments is a useful diagnostic for practitioners who have been applying these methods without guarantees. Key limitations include the double-loop structure (inefficient in practice), the O(ε^{-4}) iteration complexity, and the coverage assumption on the initial distribution; developing single-loop variants and removing the coverage requirement are identified as important open problems.
