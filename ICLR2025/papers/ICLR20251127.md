## Problem

Despite the rapid growth of Training Data Detection (TDD) methods for assessing data leakage, copyright infringement, and unlearning verification, no existing benchmark comprehensively evaluates them across multiple data modalities, recent algorithm families, and practical deployment constraints. Prior benchmarks are limited to image data, omit algorithms from the past two years (especially those targeting LLMs), and ignore efficiency metrics like memory and runtime that matter for real-world use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251127/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each TDD algorithm receives a focal data point (32×32 image, tabular row, or text sequence) and black-box access to the target model's predictions, with optional auxiliary data for building reference or shadow models. The output is a binary membership decision scored primarily by AUROC, supplemented by nine metrics (Precision, Recall, F1, Accuracy, FNR, FPR, Membership Advantage, TPR@1%FPR, TPR@10%FPR), alongside peak memory usage and total runtime per algorithm category.

## Method

TDDBench organizes 21 state-of-the-art TDD algorithms into four paradigms—metric-based (loss, confidence, entropy), learning-based (auxiliary classifier on shadow model outputs), model-based (16 reference models for de-biased comparison), and query-based (augmented, adversarial, or neighbor queries)—and evaluates them on 13 datasets spanning image, tabular, and text modalities against 41 target models including Pythia-12B. The evaluation protocol splits each dataset 50:50 into target and auxiliary sets, then further halves the target set into training (positive) and non-training (negative) examples, with five independent random repetitions for robustness. Beyond detection accuracy, the benchmark profiles each algorithm category on maximum runtime and memory consumption, and probes sensitivity to target model architecture mismatch, model size, train-test accuracy gap, and defense strategies (dropout, label smoothing, output noise, label-only exposure). The key finding is that model-based methods achieve the highest average AUROC (~0.71) but at 17× the runtime and 16× the memory of metric-based methods, while overall performance remains unsatisfactory (most AUROCs between 0.5–0.6) especially for non-image modalities.

## Results

Across 12 datasets and 41 target models, most TDD algorithms achieve AUROC scores between 0.5 and 0.6, indicating generally unsatisfactory detection performance. Model-based methods (e.g., Model-lira, Model-loss) consistently outperform others, achieving average AUROC of 0.706 across all datasets, while metric-based methods average only 0.626. The best single result is Query-ref (a hybrid query+model method) at 0.941 AUROC on CIFAR-100 and 0.735 on CIFAR-10. In terms of efficiency, metric-based methods run in 232 s with 0 MB extra memory, whereas model-based methods require 4,089 s and 13,680 MB, and query-based methods take up to 40,963 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Model-based methods (Model-lira, Model-loss, Model-robust) consistently outperform metric-based and learning-based baselines across all modalities, with Model-lira achieving the best average AUROC of 0.679 across 12 datasets and 0.728 across model architectures. Query-ref, a hybrid method combining query-based and model-based approaches, achieves the highest single-dataset AUROC (0.941 on CIFAR-100, 0.735 on CIFAR-10) but is only applicable to image data. Metric-based methods (e.g., Metric-loss from Yeom et al. 2018, Metric-conf from Song et al. 2019) are the fastest (232 s, 0 MB) but trail model-based methods by 8–10 AUROC points on average. Learning-based methods (Learn-original from Shokri et al. 2017, Learn-merge from Amit et al. 2024) occupy the middle ground, with Learn-merge (0.612 avg) slightly outperforming Learn-original (0.580 avg) by leveraging merged detection criteria. Query-based methods (Query-neighbor, Query-qrm, Query-transfer) generally perform worst (avg 0.52–0.58), though Query-ref is the notable exception. When target model architecture knowledge is limited (Table 6), all methods degrade, with Learn-original dropping 5.3% and model-based methods losing 3–15 points depending on the architecture mismatch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper consolidates and extends the TDD/MIA benchmarking landscape by unifying three prior, modality-specific benchmarks (He et al. 2022 for images, Niu et al. 2023 for image+tabular, Duan et al. 2024 for LLMs) into a single framework covering all three modalities simultaneously. Rather than proposing a new detection algorithm, it repurposes 21 existing TDD methods—spanning metric-based, learning-based, model-based, and query-based paradigms—and evaluates them under a standardized protocol across 41 target models including large language models (Pythia, Llama). The key departure from prior benchmarks is the inclusion of practical deployment metrics (runtime, memory) and the explicit examination of how target model architecture knowledge affects detection, dimensions that earlier evaluations largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that TDD performance is broadly unsatisfactory (AUROC 0.5–0.6 in most settings) and that the best-performing model-based methods carry prohibitive computational costs (13.7 GB memory, ~4000s runtime), creating a fundamental performance-practicality tradeoff that practitioners must navigate. The finding that detection degrades sharply when the target model architecture is unknown, and that non-image modalities remain particularly weak, points to clear open problems: architecture-agnostic TDD, efficient detection for LLMs, and modality-specific algorithm design. As TDD becomes critical for copyright verification, unlearning audits, and LLM data provenance, this benchmark provides the infrastructure for tracking progress on these open questions.
