## Problem

Existing PTM prediction methods rely predominantly on sequence motifs while neglecting the tertiary structural context that influences modification sites, and no large-scale residue-level sequence-structure PTM dataset has been available to support fair benchmarking. Additionally, the severe long-tail distribution of PTM types—where a few modifications dominate while many are rare—causes under-representation of rare PTMs in both training and prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252699/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a protein's amino acid sequence and 3D backbone atom coordinates (Cα, N, C, O) to construct a residue-level protein graph, where each residue's micro-environment is defined by three edge types: sequential (within cutoff d_s), radius (within 3D distance d_r), and K-nearest spatial neighbors. The output is a per-residue multi-class PTM type prediction across 26 classes (24 distinct PTM types, one aggregated 'rare sites' class, and one 'no modification' class), trained with cross-entropy loss.

## Method

MeToken tokenizes each residue's micro-environment graph into a unified discrete token via a learned codebook, where the codebook is partitioned into uniform sub-codebooks of equal size per PTM type to counteract the long-tail distribution. A uniform loss (Eq. 2) simultaneously maximizes intra-class cosine similarity and minimizes inter-class similarity within the codebook. Quantization is made differentiable through temperature-scaled vector quantization (TS-VQ), which replaces hard nearest-neighbor assignment with a softmax-weighted combination of codebook vectors, where the temperature τ_v is annealed from 1 toward 0 during training to transition from exploratory to deterministic assignments. The final PTM prediction is performed by a 3-layer PiGNN that processes the retrieved codebook vectors, trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss. The authors also compile a large-scale dataset of 1.26M annotated sites across 187K proteins by integrating dbPTM sequences with PDB and AlphaFold structures, clustered via MMseqs2 at 40% sequence identity to prevent leakage.

## Results

On the large-scale sequence-structure PTM dataset (1.26M sites, 187K proteins, 26 classes), MeToken achieves an F1-score of 50.40%, MCC of 76.03%, AUROC of 92.20%, and AUPRC of 51.26%, outperforming the strongest sequence-only baseline ESM-2 (F1=43.94%) by 6.46% in F1. On the PTMint dataset, MeToken reaches F1=42.07% and AUPRC=73.22%, and on qPTM it achieves F1=61.95% and AUPRC=64.05%, consistently leading all baselines across every metric. The ablation confirms that the uniform sub-codebook contributes +8.0% F1 over the micro-environment-only baseline, while temperature-scaled VQ alone (without the structured codebook) actually underperforms (F1=37.46%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeToken outperforms all 14 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Among sequence-only methods, ESM-2 is the strongest (F1=43.94% on the large-scale dataset), but MeToken surpasses it by 6.46% F1 and 3.95% AUPRC, demonstrating that structural micro-environment information provides substantial gains beyond sequence alone. ESM-3 (F1=40.65%) and SaProt (F1=45.18%) from supplementary experiments also fall short of MeToken. Among structure-only baselines, GVP is the best (F1=17.51%), yet MeToken exceeds it by over 32% F1, confirming that neither modality alone suffices. Among sequence-structure co-modeling baselines, MIND is the closest competitor (F1=26.70%), but MeToken still leads by 23.7% F1 and 29% AUPRC; CDConv (F1=18.11%) and GearNet (F1=16.63%) trail further behind. The supplementary baselines STEPS (F1=15.96%) and MPRL (F1=19.04%) perform poorly on residue-level PTM prediction, underscoring that protein-level pretraining objectives do not transfer well to this fine-grained task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequence-structure co-modeling paradigm (GearNet, CDConv, MIND) from protein-level property prediction to residue-level PTM prediction, introducing a micro-environment tokenization framework that encodes local sequence-structure context into discrete tokens. It builds directly on vanilla vector quantization (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) but departs from hard assignment via temperature-scaled VQ, and from prior tokenization work in molecular modeling (FoldToken, MAPE-PPI) by introducing a uniform sub-codebook strategy specifically designed to counteract the long-tail distribution of PTM types. The large-scale dataset (1.2M sites across 180K proteins) fills a critical gap, as prior sequence-structure PTM resources like PRISMOID contained only ~17K sites.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.2M-site sequence-structure PTM dataset is the largest of its kind and provides a much-needed benchmark for fair comparison in structure-aware PTM prediction. The uniform sub-codebook + TS-VQ combination offers a principled way to handle class imbalance in multi-class PTM prediction, though the ablation revealing that TS-VQ without the structured codebook is actually harmful (F1 drops from 40.04 to 37.46) underscores that the architecture, not just the quantization mechanism, drives performance. Key limitations include reliance on AlphaFold-predicted structures (which may introduce systematic biases), exclusion of protein-protein interaction context, and potential redundancy in the uniform sub-codebook for common PTM types. Open questions include whether the tokenization approach extends to PTM crosstalk prediction and whether incorporating co-factors or modifying enzymes would further improve rare PTM detection.
