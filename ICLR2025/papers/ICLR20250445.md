## Problem

Diffusion-based PDE solvers struggle with two fundamental challenges: (1) accurately modeling states with abrupt changes such as shocks and discontinuities, and (2) generalizing to finer spatial-temporal resolutions than those seen during training. Existing methods either operate in the raw space-time domain where discontinuities are hard to capture, or are locked to a fixed resolution, limiting their utility as neural PDE operators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250445/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For simulation, the method takes wavelet-transformed equation parameters (initial condition, force term, boundary condition) as conditioning and generates the full state trajectory u[0,T] in the wavelet domain. For control, it additionally incorporates the gradient of a task-specific objective J during denoising to steer the generated control sequence f[0,T] toward optimality. For super-resolution, a separate Super-Resolution Model takes low-resolution wavelet coefficients and high-resolution equation parameters to iteratively produce outputs at 2×, 4×, or 8× the training resolution (e.g., from 80×120 to 640×960 in 1D). All data must reside on static, uniform grids.

## Method

WDNO performs diffusion-based generative modeling entirely in the wavelet domain, using a U-Net denoiser trained with DDPM loss on wavelet coefficients (bior2.4 for 1D, bior1.3 for 2D) rather than raw space-time values. The wavelet transform's joint space-frequency localization makes it naturally suited to representing abrupt changes, and its linearity and locality enable seamless integration with multi-resolution training. For control, an energy-based guidance term λ∇J is added to the denoising step, where J is evaluated on a noise-free estimate of the control sequence. The multi-resolution framework exploits approximate scale invariance of PDEs: training data is downsampled to create resolution pairs, and a conditional Super-Resolution Model learns to map low-resolution wavelet coefficients to high-resolution ones, enabling zero-shot super-resolution at inference by iteratively applying the SRM. Sampling uses DDIM for acceleration, and classifier-free guidance conditions generation on the wavelet-transformed equation parameters.

## Results

On simulation (MSE over full trajectories), WDNO achieves the best or near-best results across all five PDE systems: 2.898e-05 on 1D Advection (vs. 4.209e-05 for DDPM), 0.2195 on 1D Compressible Navier-Stokes (vs. 5.5228 for DDPM, a >25× improvement), 0.00231 on 2D Incompressible Fluid (vs. 0.01578 for DDPM), and 12.83 on ERA5 (vs. 14.39 for FNO). On the 1D Burgers' equation, WDNO (0.00014) is essentially tied with DDPM (0.00013). For control, WDNO achieves J=0.0205 on 1D Burgers' (vs. 0.0235 for SL, the next best) and J=0.0679 on 2D incompressible fluid (vs. 0.3066 for BPPO, a 78% reduction in smoke leakage).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WDNO outperforms all 16 baselines on at least one task. In simulation, WDNO beats FNO (the strongest non-diffusion baseline) on all five systems, with the largest margin on 1D Compressible Navier-Stokes (0.2195 vs. 0.2575) and 2D Fluid (0.00231 vs. 0.00684). Against DDPM, WDNO wins decisively on 1D Navier-Stokes (0.2195 vs. 5.5228, >25× lower MSE), 2D Fluid (0.00231 vs. 0.01578, ~7× lower), and ERA5 (12.83 vs. 15.21), but is marginally behind on 1D Burgers' (0.00014 vs. 0.00013). Among additional baselines in Table 5, WDNO's 0.2195 MSE on 1D Navier-Stokes is 2× better than CNO (0.3987) and 23× better than Transolver (4.9984). In control, WDNO (J=0.0205 on 1D Burgers') beats the closest competitor SL (0.0235) by ~13%, and on 2D fluid (J=0.0679) it outperforms the next-best BPPO (0.3066) by 78%, while DDPM (0.3124) and all RL/IL methods (SAC, BC) are 4–10× worse.

## Relation to Prior Work

WDNO sits at the intersection of three research threads: diffusion-based generative modeling for PDEs (building on DDPM and its conditional variants), wavelet-domain neural operators (WNO, MWT), and multi-resolution/super-resolution PDE solvers. It departs from prior diffusion-based PDE work (e.g., Wei et al. 2024, Li et al. 2024) by performing the entire denoising process in the wavelet domain rather than the spatial or Fourier domain, motivated by the wavelet's joint space-frequency localization for capturing shocks and discontinuities. The multi-resolution training framework, grounded in approximate scale invariance of PDEs, is a new contribution that has no direct analogue in prior diffusion or neural-operator literature, and it integrates naturally with the wavelet representation due to the transform's linearity and locality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in neural PDE solvers—modeling abrupt changes (shocks, fluid-solid coupling) and generalizing across resolutions—that have limited the deployment of learned solvers in real engineering settings. The 78% reduction in smoke leakage on the 2D indirect control task and the zero-shot super-resolution capability (no retraining at finer grids) are practically significant for applications like weather forecasting and fluid control. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to static uniform grids (no irregular or moving meshes), does not incorporate physics-informed losses or explicit PDE structure, and has not been validated on real-world experimental data. Open questions include extending to geometric wavelets for irregular domains and integrating equation-aware regularization.
