## Problem

Variational samplers that leverage pre-trained diffusion models for inverse problems suffer from two compounding failures in high-dimensional settings: mode collapse (all particles converge to the same solution) and latent space inversion artifacts from the adversarial autoencoder in models like Stable Diffusion. No existing method simultaneously addresses both issues while remaining computationally tractable at high resolutions (512×512).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252168/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy measurement y, a forward operator f(·) (linear or nonlinear), and a frozen pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v2.1), and produces a batch of N diverse reconstructed images at 512×512 resolution. The scalar repulsion weight γ directly controls the diversity–quality trade-off: higher γ yields more diverse but slightly lower-fidelity samples, while γ=0 recovers a single-mode augmented solver. Unlike posterior samplers (DPS, PSLD), RLSD does not require score Jacobian computation, and unlike pixel-domain solvers it operates entirely in the latent space with a pixel-space augmentation variable.

## Method

RLSD formulates posterior sampling as KL minimization over an augmented variational distribution q(z₀, x₀|y) that decouples the latent variable z₀ (governed by the diffusion prior) from the pixel variable x₀ (governed by the measurement), connected by a Gaussian coupling with variance ρ². To prevent mode collapse, the variational distribution is instantiated as an ensemble of N interactive particles coupled by a repulsive force derived from pairwise RBF kernels evaluated on DINO features, inspired by the Wasserstein gradient flow of score distillation. The resulting gradient (Proposition 2) decomposes into three terms: a measurement-matching loss, a latent–pixel consistency loss, and a regularization term combining score-matching along the diffusion trajectory with the repulsive kernel gradient. The optimization is solved via half-quadratic splitting, alternating z-updates (one backprop through the decoder, no backprop through the score network) and x-updates (measurement and consistency losses), with a decreasing timestep schedule from T to 0. This design avoids the score Jacobian required by DPS/PSLD, handles both linear and nonlinear forward operators, and scales to 512×512 images using the latent diffusion prior.

## Results

On box inpainting (half-face, FFHQ 512×512), RLSD (γ=50) achieves 24.69 dB PSNR, 0.111 LPIPS, and 31.41 FID, outperforming PSLD (22.72 dB, 57.7 FID) and Latent RED-Diff (23.5 dB, 92.59 FID) while maintaining higher diversity (0.015 vs. 0.009). On the nonlinear phase retrieval task, NonAug-RLSD (γ=30) reaches 24.21 dB PSNR and 130.09 FID, substantially surpassing Latent RED-Diff (19.65 dB, 173.18 FID) and Latent DPS (14.98 dB, 291.68 FID). For HDR reconstruction, RLSD (γ=50) achieves 28.13 dB PSNR and 37.32 FID, beating Latent RED-Diff (25.68 dB, 93.71 FID) by a wide margin. The Hybrid-RLSD variant (3 interacting + 1 independent particle) achieves the best quality–diversity trade-off at 24.72 dB PSNR with 0.018 diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PSLD (Rout et al., 2024), the closest latent-diffusion baseline, RLSD improves PSNR by ~2 dB (24.69 vs. 22.72) and FID by nearly half (31.41 vs. 57.7) on inpainting, while PSLD retains a diversity advantage (0.03 vs. 0.015); the Hybrid-RLSD variant narrows this gap to 0.018. Against Latent RED-Diff (Mardani et al., 2024), RLSD wins on all three quality metrics for inpainting (PSNR +1.2 dB, FID −61) and for phase retrieval (PSNR +4.6 dB, FID −43), confirming that the repulsive particle ensemble explores multiple posterior modes that the unimodal variational approximation misses. Against pixel-domain DPS (Chung et al., 2022a), RLSD achieves higher PSNR (24.69 vs. 23.4) and substantially lower FID (31.41 vs. 78.88) on inpainting, and on phase retrieval NonAug-RLSD outperforms Latent DPS by 9.2 dB in PSNR. Against ReSample (Song et al., 2024), RLSD dominates on every metric (PSNR 24.69 vs. 19.44, FID 31.41 vs. 146.68) and is also faster in practice. Against ΠGDM (Song et al., 2022) and FPS-SMC (Dou and Song, 2024) on the 256×256 inpainting comparison, NonRepuls-RLSD achieves the best PSNR (24.98 dB) and competitive FID (29.18 vs. 33.8 and 59.59). The one setting where RLSD with repulsion underperforms its non-repulsive variant is phase retrieval with augmentation (RLSD γ=30: 20.43 dB vs. NonRepuls-RLSD: 18.33 dB, but NonAug-RLSD: 24.21 dB), which the authors attribute to the intractability of the augmented conditional for nonlinear operators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational inference framework for diffusion-based inverse problem solving (RED-Diff, Mardani et al. 2024) by introducing two key modifications: a repulsive particle coupling inspired by kernel-density-estimation-based Bayesian ensembles (D'Angelo and Fortuin, 2021b) to combat mode collapse, and an augmented variational distribution (analogous to half-quadratic splitting) to decouple latent and pixel spaces. It builds on the Wasserstein gradient flow interpretation of Score Distillation Sampling (Poole et al., 2022; Song et al., 2021a) but departs from prior diversity-promoting approaches (VSD, CSD/SVGD) by using a pairwise RBF kernel over DINO features rather than full SVGD, which is known to suffer from the curse of dimensionality. The method is specifically designed for latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion), addressing the adversarial autoencoder inversion problem that plagues direct latent-space optimization (PSLD, ReSample).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RLSD provides a practical, plug-and-play solver that simultaneously addresses mode collapse and latent-space inversion at 512×512 resolution, a combination no prior method achieves. The repulsion mechanism offers a simple, tunable knob (γ) for controlling the diversity-quality trade-off, and the augmented formulation avoids expensive score Jacobian computations. Key limitations include the added computational cost of the repulsive kernel, the sensitivity of the DINO-based RBF to high noise levels, and the proliferation of hyperparameters (λ, γ, ρ̃, learning rates) that require per-task tuning. Open questions include whether adaptive kernel learning or data-driven repulsion weights could reduce the manual tuning burden, and whether the augmentation strategy generalizes to other nonlinear forward operators beyond phase retrieval and HDR.
