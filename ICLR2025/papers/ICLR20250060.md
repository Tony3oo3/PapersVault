## Problem

Existing attentive GNNs underperform in practice because their scoring functions—whether linear transformation-based or MLP-based—exhibit finite expressive power under realistic parameter constraints, limiting their ability to rank neighbor importance. Moreover, prior theoretical analyses (static vs. dynamic attention) are too coarse to quantitatively differentiate the expressive power of different scoring function designs.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard graph inputs (node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} and adjacency matrix A) and produces node or graph embeddings for downstream classification/regression. The critical internal computation is the scoring function, which takes a pair of node representations (h_i, h_j) through a non-learnable alignment function (concatenation, subtraction, dot product, etc.) to produce a d'-dimensional vector, then maps it to a single scalar score via the learnable score mapping. This scalar is softmax-normalized across all neighbors to yield attention weights for aggregation.

## Method

KAA unifies all attentive GNN scoring functions into the form s(h_i, h_j) = Ψ ∘ AF(h_i, h_j), where AF is a fixed alignment operation and Ψ is the learnable score mapping, then replaces Ψ with a single-layer KAN parameterized by B-spline functions. The key theoretical contribution is Maximum Ranking Distance (MRD), which upper-bounds the worst-case ranking error a scoring function family can achieve; the paper proves that linear and two-layer MLP score mappings have finite MRD lower bounds, while a single-layer KAN with zero-order B-splines achieves MRD ≤ δ for any δ > 0 (nearly infinite expressive power) with fewer parameters than the MLP baseline. KAA is drop-in compatible with GAT, GLCN, CFGAT, Graph Transformer, and SAN backbones, and extends naturally to multi-head attention by applying K independent KANs per head.

## Results

On node-level tasks (Table 2), KAA-enhanced models achieve an average improvement of 1.63% over their original counterparts across 9 datasets, with KAA-GAT reaching 83.80% accuracy on Cora and 94.87% ROC-AUC on PubMed link prediction. On graph-level tasks (Table 3), improvements are substantially larger: KAA achieves an average enhancement of 7.71%, with standout results including KAA-GLCN on PPI (74.86% → 94.96% F1, +20.1%) and KAA-GAT on QM9 (0.4749 → 0.2124 MAE, ~55% reduction). The paper also introduces Maximum Ranking Distance (MRD) as a theoretical metric, proving that KAA with a single-layer KAN achieves MRD ≤ δ for any δ > 0, while linear and MLP-based scoring functions have finite lower bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KAA-enhanced models outperform their original counterparts on all 12 datasets across both node-level and graph-level tasks, with no single exception. Among GAT-based backbones, KAA-GAT (83.80% on Cora) surpasses non-attention baselines GCN (81.99%), GraphSAGE (81.48%), and GIN (79.85%), whereas the original GAT (82.76%) only marginally beats them; KAA-GLCN achieves a dramatic +20.1% F1 gain on PPI (74.86% → 94.96%). For Transformer-based backbones, KAA-GT and KAA-SAN improve over their originals (e.g., KAA-GT: 70.16% → 71.86% on Cora; KAA-SAN: 71.72% → 72.98%) but still trail GAT-based models on node-level tasks. Against GATv2 (Brody et al., 2021), KAA-GAT wins on all four node classification datasets (e.g., 83.80% vs. 72.03% on CiteSeer). Compared to other KAN-GNN variants (KAGIN, KAGCN, GKAN), KAA-GAT achieves the highest accuracy on both Cora (83.80%) and CiteSeer (73.10%), and is the only KAN-GNN variant that consistently outperforms classical GNN baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: attentive GNNs (GAT, GATv2, Graph Transformers) and Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN). It builds directly on Brody et al.'s (2021) identification that GAT's linear scoring function is too restrictive, and on Liu et al.'s (2024) introduction of KAN as an MLP alternative. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the scoring function in attentive GNNs has the same multi-dimensional-input-to-scalar-output structure where KAN excels (symbolic regression, PDE solving), and unifying all existing scoring functions into a single 'alignment function + score mapping' form so that KAN can be swapped in as a universal drop-in replacement. The Maximum Ranking Distance (MRD) metric extends the qualitative static/dynamic attention dichotomy from Brody et al. into a quantitative, continuous measure of expressive power.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KAA provides a practical, architecture-agnostic upgrade path for any attentive GNN, and the MRD framework gives the field a principled tool for comparing scoring functions beyond the binary static/dynamic distinction. The theoretical result that a single-layer KAN with zero-order B-splines achieves near-zero MRD with fewer parameters than a two-layer MLP is a clean and useful characterization. Open questions include: whether KAN's training instability (noted in the literature) becomes a practical bottleneck at scale, whether the large graph-level gains (some >20%) persist on larger molecular datasets, and whether the theoretical MRD advantage translates to consistent gains in all regimes or is most pronounced in low-data settings.
