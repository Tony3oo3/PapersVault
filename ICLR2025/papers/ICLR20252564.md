## Problem

Existing MLLMs for referential tasks (referring and grounding) rely on additional syntax—either proxy tokens decoded by external vision modules (RoIAlign, SAM, GroundingDINO) or coordinate-like geometry tokens—imposing heavy training burdens, limiting generalization, and preventing joint optimization of referring and grounding. The paper asks whether fine-level vision-language alignment can be achieved natively within an autoregressive framework without any extra referential syntax.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252564/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image and a text prompt as input. The image is encoded by EVA-CLIP into 256 patch features that are processed in two parallel passes: continuous embeddings (MLP-projected) and discrete codes (VQ-quantized into a 16384-entry codebook), both inserted into the language sequence via <boi>…<eoi> markers. For grounding, the model outputs a sequence of discrete visual tokens (token collectives) rather than coordinates; these are decoded into bounding boxes at inference time by a GMM-based region sampler that optionally leverages a lightweight detector (YOLOX or Co-DETR) as a region proposer. The model can also emit interleaved image-text sequences to ground multiple objects in a single autoregressive pass.

## Method

ClawMachine introduces 'token collectives'—groups of quantized visual tokens whose centers fall within a target region, sorted in raster order—as the sole referential notation, eliminating proxy tokens and coordinate syntax. A hybrid perception mechanism feeds both continuous (MLP-projected) and discrete (VQ-quantized) patch features into a LLaMA-2-7B backbone initialized from LaVIT-2-7B, giving the model a joint vision-language vocabulary of 48,384 tokens. Training proceeds in two stages: alignment pre-training on scene- and region-level captions (optionally scaled to 15M GRIT-20M pairs), followed by instruction-tuning on a dual dataset that jointly supervises referring and grounding as next-token prediction. At inference, a region sampler retrieves the spatial positions of generated tokens from the image lattice, applies a fuzzy search against detector proposals, and fits a 2D Gaussian Mixture Model to nominate the final bounding box. This unified formulation enables multi-object grounding, grounding-upon-referring, and region-text interleaved VQA within a single autoregressive sequence without task-specific decoders.

## Results

On visual grounding (REC, IoU@0.5), ClawMachineX-7B achieves 89.7/92.5/86.9 on RefCOCO val/test-A/test-B and 86.7/87.1 on RefCOCOg, matching or slightly exceeding Groma-7B (89.5/92.1/86.2) while using only 16M grounded data versus Groma's 20M+. On visual referring, ClawMachineX-7B sets SOTA with 17.4 METEOR and 118.4 CIDEr on RefCOCOg, surpassing Groma (16.8/107.3) and Osprey (16.6/108.3). On the multi-object LVIS-Ground benchmark, ClawMachineX-7B achieves AR=36.7 (AR@s=20.5), substantially outperforming Groma (28.8/8.7) especially on small objects. On the proposed Ref-Hal hallucination test, ClawMachine-7B scores 75.8 accuracy, far exceeding the next-best Qwen-VL (61.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On visual grounding, ClawMachineX-7B (16M data) marginally outperforms Groma-7B (20M+ data) on RefCOCO val (89.7 vs 89.5) and test-B (86.9 vs 86.2), while ClawMachine-7B achieves competitive results (88.6 val) using only 700K grounded data—roughly 30× less than Groma or Qwen-VL. On visual referring, ClawMachineX-7B clearly leads all MLLM baselines on RefCOCOg (17.4 METEOR, 118.4 CIDEr vs Groma's 16.8/107.3 and Osprey's 16.6/108.3). On LVIS-Ground multi-object grounding, ClawMachineX-7B dominates with AR=36.7 vs Groma's 28.8, with a particularly large gap on small objects (AR@s: 20.5 vs 8.7). On the Ref-Hal referential hallucination test, ClawMachine-7B (75.8 accuracy) substantially outperforms all proxy-encoding (GPT4RoI 44.0, Groma 53.9, Ferret 56.8) and geometry-encoding (Shikra 47.3, Qwen-VL 61.5) baselines. On LLaVA-Bench, ClawMachine-7B achieves the highest average (87.3) among compared MLLMs, edging out Ferret (86.7) and Groma (85.2). On POPE adversarial hallucination, ClawMachine-7B (85.36) is competitive with Groundhog (86.33) while using fewer image tokens (256×2 vs 576+256).

## Relation to Prior Work

ClawMachine builds directly on LaVIT's joint vision-language vocabulary with vector-quantized visual tokens and the LLaVA-style ViT-MLP-LLM architecture, but departs fundamentally from both dominant paradigms for referential MLLMs: proxy encoding (which relies on auxiliary modules like RoIAlign, SAM, or GroundingDINO) and geometry encoding (which introduces coordinate tokens or text-based location syntax). Instead, it proposes 'token collectives'—groups of quantized visual tokens fetched from a bounding box region and inserted directly into the language sequence—thereby unifying referring and grounding as a single next-token prediction task without any additional spatial syntax. The hybrid perception mechanism (concatenating continuous CLIP features with discrete VQ tokens) is a novel design choice that leverages LaVIT's VQ layer while preserving CLIP's perceptual fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a pure autoregressive MLLM with only 700K grounded instruction-tuning pairs can match or exceed models trained on 20M+ grounded data and equipped with large auxiliary detectors, suggesting that the joint vocabulary + token collective formulation is a more data-efficient path to referential understanding. The significant reduction in referential hallucination (75.8% vs. 44–61.5% on Ref-Hal) and the ability to ground multiple objects in a single inference open the door to more complex, flexible visual dialogues. Key open questions remain: quantization loss limits fine-grained spatial precision, visual token generation can be incomplete or unstable, and the region sampler still requires an external detector at inference time—addressing these would require unified LLM-encoder training or more advanced encoding mechanisms.
