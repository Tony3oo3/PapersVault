## Problem

Token-level RLHF suffers from the credit assignment problem over long sequences: delayed rewards make it difficult to attribute preference signals to specific tokens, slowing convergence and destabilizing policy gradient estimates. This is further exacerbated by subword tokenization (e.g., BPE), which inflates sequence length by ~33% and fragments natural language constructs like multi-word phrases into isolated decisions. Standard RLHF also overlooks local co-occurrence patterns and higher-level linguistic structures that are better evaluated holistically.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts paired with human preference data (chosen/rejected response pairs) and produces aligned text outputs including summaries, dialogue responses, QA answers, and executable code snippets. The policy model still outputs token-level probabilities over the original vocabulary (no re-architecting), but the RL optimization operates on grouped token sequences (macro actions) whose boundaries are determined by a termination rule. The only additional computation during training is the termination step itself; inference incurs zero extra cost since macro actions are a training-time abstraction.

## Method

MA-RLHF integrates macro actions (options from the SMDP framework) into the standard RLHF pipeline by grouping consecutive tokens into temporally extended actions using one of four termination conditions: fixed n-gram, randomized n-gram, parsing-based (constituent tree DFS with a leaf-token threshold), or perplexity-based (terminating when adding a token increases the macro action's perplexity). The PPO objective is adapted so that the importance-sampling ratio and advantage are computed at the macro-action level using the joint probability π_θ(ω_τ|s_τ) = ∏ π_θ(a_t|a_<t), while the macro-action value function is estimated by aggregating token-level critic outputs with configurable weights (equal, unit, or position-decayed). This shortens the decision horizon from T to T/n, reducing the temporal distance between actions and rewards and thereby lowering gradient variance. The framework generalizes existing methods: n=1 recovers standard token-level PPO, while n→∞ approaches REINFORCE/GRPO-style sequence-level optimization, with intermediate values providing a tunable trade-off between credit assignment and fine-grained control.

## Results

MA-PPO achieves substantial improvements over vanilla PPO across all evaluated tasks: +68% RM score on TL;DR (2B), +30% on TL;DR (7B), +18% on HH-RLHF (both sizes), and +8% on WebGPT (7B). In code generation (APPS), MA-PPO gains +11% pass@1 (2B) and +35% pass@1 (7B) over vanilla PPO. GPT-4 pairwise evaluations confirm these gains, with MA-PPO achieving 78% and 86% win rates over vanilla PPO on TL;DR for 2B and 7B models respectively. MA-PPO reaches parity with vanilla PPO 1.7–2× faster in training steps while continuing to outperform with further training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla PPO (token-level RLHF), MA-PPO wins on every task and model size: +68%/+30% RM score on TL;DR (2B/7B), +18% on HH-RLHF, +3%/+8% on WebGPT, and +11%/+35% pass@1 on APPS code generation. Against DPO, MA-PPO substantially outperforms on both TL;DR (1.40 vs. 0.03 RM score) and HH-RLHF (1.55 vs. 0.64), with GPT-4 win rates confirming the gap. Against RLOO (a REINFORCE-style baseline equivalent to n→∞ macro actions), MA-PPO with n=5 achieves 1.40 RM score on TL;DR versus RLOO's 0.81, demonstrating that moderate-length macro actions (SMDP) outperform both token-level (MDP) and sequence-level (contextual bandit) extremes. The paper also validates on Llama-3.2-3B, where MA-PPO (3.96) again beats vanilla PPO (3.33) on TL;DR, confirming cross-architecture generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the well-established concept of macro actions (options) from the semi-Markov Decision Process (SMDP) literature (Sutton et al., 1999b; Precup et al., 1997) to the RLHF setting for LLM alignment, where prior work (Ouyang et al., 2022; Stiennon et al., 2020) operates exclusively at the token level. The key conceptual move is treating sequences of tokens as atomic decision units, which the paper shows generalizes the spectrum from standard PPO (n=1, MDP) to REINFORCE/RLOO/GRPO (n→∞, contextual bandit). The practical contribution lies in designing termination conditions (fixed/randomized n-gram, parsing-based, perplexity-based) that define macro action boundaries without modifying the model architecture or vocabulary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MA-RLHF offers a drop-in improvement to standard RLHF pipelines with no additional training or inference cost, achieving 1.7–2× faster convergence and up to 30% reward gains across summarization, dialogue, QA, and code generation. The robustness to sampling temperature (stable at T=1.2 where vanilla PPO degrades) and consistent scaling from 2B to 27B parameters make it practically attractive for production alignment pipelines. Open questions include whether learnable (rather than rule-based) termination strategies could further improve performance, whether the approach extends to complex multi-step reasoning tasks, and how it scales to frontier-scale models (405B+) where the credit assignment problem is most acute.
