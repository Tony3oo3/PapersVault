## Problem

Existing locate-then-edit methods (ROME, MEMIT, PMET) rely on causal tracing to identify which FFN layers store factual knowledge, but this localization is overly sensitive to the subject entity and largely ignores the relation. As a result, edited models exhibit poor locality: when the relation is changed in a query, the model still outputs the edited target object, indicating that the edit has been applied too broadly. The paper argues this stems from insufficient precision in knowledge localization and seeks a finer-grained alternative.

## Input / Output

The method takes a knowledge triple (subject, relation, object) along with a pre-trained decoder-only LLM (GPT-J-6B, LLaMA-2-7B, or LLaMA-3-8B) and produces an edited model in which only the second linear layer (W_out) rows corresponding to a small set of selected FFN neurons are modified. Unlike ROME/MEMIT which rewrite entire FFN weight matrices (~10^7–10^8 parameters), FiNE alters approximately 10^4 parameters per edit, with the number of modified neurons controlled by a top-k selection (default k=5 per token of the target object). The last l_f=3 layers are frozen and never modified to preserve language fluency.

## Method

FiNE operates in two stages. First, for each token t in the target object, it computes a per-neuron contribution score c_(i,l,t) = q_i^l · (W_u W_out^l)_{t,i}, where q_i^l is the activation of neuron i at layer l and (W_u W_out^l)_{t,i} is the projection coefficient linking that neuron to vocabulary token t; the top-k neurons across all layers are selected as knowledge carriers. Second, for each selected neuron, a learned vector z is added to the corresponding row of W_out^l, and the collection Z_j is optimized via a combined objective of negative log-likelihood on the target object, KL divergence on the prompt distribution, and a repetition penalty that discourages the model from generating the target after it has already appeared. A layer-freezing constraint excludes the final l_f layers from modification, and the entire procedure modifies only ~10^4 parameters per edit, yielding 4–6× speedup over ROME/MEMIT while substantially improving locality (RSA/FA) without sacrificing edit success or portability.

## Results

On WikiData_counterfact, FiNE achieves near-perfect Edit Success (99.8–100.0%) across GPT-J, LLaMA-2, and LLaMA-3, while dramatically improving Locality (RSA) over causal-tracing baselines: 84.2% vs. 37.4% (ROME) and 53.2% (MEMIT) on GPT-J, 92.6% vs. 49.1% and 45.0% on LLaMA-2, and 90.5% vs. 41.9% and 39.5% on LLaMA-3. FiNE also leads on Subject Aliasing Accuracy (SAA: 89.6–90.6% vs. 72–75% for ROME) and Forgetfulness Accuracy (FA: 54.2–65.0% vs. 31.5–40.7% for ROME). The method modifies only ~10⁴ parameters (vs. 10⁷–10⁸ for ROME/MEMIT) and runs 4–6× faster at Float32 precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiNE decisively outperforms all three causal-tracing locate-then-edit baselines (ROME, MEMIT, PMET) on Locality metrics: RSA improves by 35–53 points and FA by 13–32 points across the three models, confirming that neuron-level localization captures relation-specific knowledge that causal tracing misses. Against ROME specifically, FiNE gains ~15 points on SAA (e.g., 90.6 vs. 74.1 on GPT-J) while matching or exceeding its Edit Success. PMET, the strongest causal-tracing variant, still trails FiNE by 20–28 points on RSA. The memory-based IKE baseline achieves 100% Edit Success but with substantially lower Locality (RSA 60.6–73.6%) and no Fluency score, while LoRA also reaches 100% Edit Success but with weaker Locality (RSA 17.5–73.5%). Knowledge Neurons (KN), the other neuron-level method, achieves very low Edit Success (17–21%) despite high Locality, indicating its gradient-based neuron selection is poorly aligned with the editing objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

FiNE departs from the causal-tracing paradigm that underpins ROME, MEMIT, and PMET by replacing layer-level localization with neuron-level localization within FFNs. The contribution-score mechanism is directly adapted from Pan et al. (2024) for multimodal neuron identification, while the editing objective (editing loss + KL divergence + repetition penalty) is inherited from ROME. The paper's core conceptual departure is the argument that causal tracing is fundamentally imprecise because it is overly subject-driven and neglects relations, a limitation the authors demonstrate empirically and address by targeting individual neurons rather than entire FFN layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that causal tracing — the foundational localization technique for the entire locate-then-edit family — is systematically biased toward subject entities challenges a core assumption in the knowledge editing literature and motivates rethinking how knowledge locations are identified. The practical efficiency gain (modifying ~10^4 parameters versus 10^7–10^8 for ROME/MEMIT, with 4–6× speedup) makes precise editing substantially more accessible. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-FFN architectures (e.g., mixture-of-experts), whether the safety of edits can be formally guaranteed, and how the method behaves under adversarial or multi-hop knowledge updates.
