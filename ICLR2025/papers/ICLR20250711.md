## Problem

While GNN feature propagation has been linked to diffusion processes, there has been no principled dynamical-systems tool to analyze and refine the underlying feature dynamics, especially as GNNs incorporate increasingly complex physics (reaction-diffusion, oscillation, etc.). This paper bridges that gap by connecting GNN dynamics to Koopman operator theory and its numerical approximation via Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD), providing both a theoretical guarantee that DMD recovers the principal slow-decay spectral subspace and a practical mechanism to enhance GNN performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph adjacency matrix and node feature matrix (or a sequence of time-series snapshots for spatial-temporal tasks). An initial GNN layer (e.g., one GCN or SGC step) generates two consecutive snapshots H(ℓ) and H(ℓ+1), which are fed into a one-time DMD computation that produces a low-rank spectral basis Ψ ∈ ℝ^{N×r} with r ≪ min(N,d) controlled by a truncation rate ξ. The DMD-GNN then performs spectral filtering in this reduced basis, outputting node class labels, predicted future node values, or link-existence probabilities depending on the downstream task.

## Method

The core mechanism treats GNN layer-to-layer feature propagation H(ℓ+1) = F(H(ℓ)) as a discrete dynamical system and applies DMD to estimate a finite-dimensional linear operator K ≈ F from the two snapshots, via SVD-based projection K = M_r* H(ℓ+1) V_r Σ_r^{-1}. The eigendecomposition of K yields DMD modes Ψ = H(ℓ)VΣ^{-1}U(K) that serve as a data-driven, low-rank eigenbasis replacing the fixed graph Laplacian/adjacency eigenvectors in spectral filtering: H(ℓ+1) = Ψ diag(θ) Ψ^T H(ℓ) W(ℓ). A key theoretical result (Lemma 1) proves that under mild conditions DMD output is locally topologically conjugate to the linearized dynamics on the slow-attracting spectral submanifold, guaranteeing it captures the principal components driving the system. The choice of initial dynamic (GCN, SGC, ACMP, or a mixed DMD++ formulation) controls the spectral range of Ψ, enabling adaptation to homophilic (wider spectrum, ξ=0.85) versus heterophilic (narrower spectrum, ξ=0.7) graphs. The approach is further extended to physics-informed DMD with symmetry constraints for directed graphs and to spatial-temporal forecasting where DMD provides a dynamic eigenspace that adapts to time-varying graph structure.

## Results

On node classification, DMD-ACMP achieves the best overall performance, reaching 81.2% on Pubmed (vs. 79.8% for GPRGNN) and 75.5% on OGB-arXiv (vs. 74.1% for GRAND++), while DMD++ leads on heterophilic graphs with 92.6% on Texas, 91.9% on Wisconsin, and 91.4% on Cornell. DMD-SGC achieves the highest Cora accuracy at 84.1%. On long-range graph benchmarks (COCO-SP, PascalVOC-SP), DMD++ attains the best Macro-F1 of 0.091 and 0.241 respectively, surpassing GCN (0.079/0.238) and SGC (0.056/0.216). For spatial-temporal prediction, DMD++ achieves the lowest MSE on all three datasets (Chickenpox 0.910, Covid 0.784, WikiMath 0.805), outperforming GCN, GAT, and ChebNet baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMD-ACMP outperforms all baselines on Pubmed (81.2% vs. 79.8% GPRGNN) and OGB-arXiv (75.5% vs. 74.1% GRAND++), and matches or exceeds ACMP (its source dynamic) on all heterophilic graphs. DMD++ dominates on heterophilic graphs, beating APPNP (90.1%) on Wisconsin (91.9%) and H2GCN (85.9%) on Texas (92.6%), while DMD-SGC achieves the best Cora score (84.1% vs. 83.8% GPRGNN). On homophilic graphs, DMD-GNNs are competitive but do not uniformly surpass the strongest baselines: GPRGNN and APPNP still lead on Citeseer (75.9% vs. 73.2% DMD++). On long-range benchmarks, DMD++ (0.091 Macro-F1 on COCO-SP) beats GCN (0.079) and SGC (0.056) but remains below methods specifically designed for over-squashing mitigation. For spatial-temporal prediction, DMD++ achieves the lowest MSE across all three datasets (e.g., 0.784 on Covid vs. 0.956 GCN, 0.861 GAT-KNN), and DMD-GCN outperforms all baselines on WikiMath (0.848 vs. 0.826 GAT-KNN is actually lower — correction: GAT-KNN 0.826 is best, DMD++ 0.805 is best). In link prediction, DMD-GCN leads on Squirrel (78.8% vs. 75.3% SAGE) while SAGE leads on Chameleon (82.1% vs. 78.3% DMD-SGC), showing mixed results where simpler dynamics (GCN) benefit link prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: (1) the growing body of work interpreting GNN feature propagation as graph diffusion or reaction-diffusion processes (Chamberlain et al., 2021; Choi et al., 2023; Han et al., 2024), and (2) Koopman operator theory and its numerical approximation via Dynamic Mode Decomposition (Tu, 2013; Schmid, 2010), a tool well-established in fluid dynamics but largely unexplored in graph learning. The key conceptual move is treating successive GNN layer outputs as snapshots of a discrete dynamical system, then applying DMD to extract a low-rank spectral basis (the DMD modes) that replaces the fixed graph Laplacian eigenvectors used in classical spectral GNNs. The theoretical grounding draws on Haller & Kaszás (2024), who showed DMD estimates converge to the slow spectral submanifold, and on Baddoo et al. (2023) for physics-informed DMD variants with structural constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, data-driven mechanism for selecting the spectral domain in GNNs, replacing the one-size-fits-all graph Laplacian eigendecomposition with a task- and graph-adaptive basis. The flexibility to choose initial dynamics (GCN, SGC, ACMP) to match homophily/heterophily provides practical guidance, and the physics-informed DMD extension opens a path toward GNNs that respect known symmetries or PDE structure. However, empirical gains over strong baselines (ACMP, GRAND++) are modest on most benchmarks, the method requires an extra forward pass to generate DMD snapshots, and the LRGB results remain limited. Open questions include how the approach scales to very large or non-stationary graphs, whether the DMD spectral basis can be updated online, and how to systematically choose the initial dynamics for unseen graph types.
