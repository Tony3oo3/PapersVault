## Problem

Existing research on catastrophic forgetting in LLMs during continual instruction tuning primarily examines a single training sequence and lacks tools to characterize the internal mechanisms driving forgetting. Traditional measures like feature similarity and parameter L2 distance fail to capture the model-dependent nature of forgetting, and it remains unclear whether forgetting stems from overwriting task-specific functions or from a shift in how inputs activate those functions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251689/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (Llama2-7b/13b-chat, Llama3-8b-chat, Mistral-7b-instruct), a sequence of instruction tuning tasks (SuperNI or TRACE benchmarks with 1,000–3,000 samples per task), and function vectors extracted via activation patching on 10-shot ICL prompts to identify top-10 causal attention heads. It produces a fine-tuned LLM (via LoRA, rank 8, targeting query/value matrices) that retains general zero-shot and 5-shot in-context learning abilities while still acquiring new task capabilities. The function vector head set S is computed once from held-out datasets and reused across all training tasks.

## Method

The paper establishes that catastrophic forgetting in LLMs is driven by a bias in the mapping P_M(θ|x) (input-to-latent-concept) rather than erasure of the task function P_M(y|x,θ), demonstrated through intervention experiments showing that adding the source FV or subtracting the biased target FV restores performance. Building on this, the proposed FV-guided training introduces two regularization terms on top of the standard language modeling loss: (1) an FV consistency loss (ℓ_FV) that penalizes L2 drift in the activations of the causal attention heads S between the pre-task and current model, and (2) an FV-guided KL divergence loss (ℓ_KL) that aligns the current model's zero-shot logits with those of the previous model after injecting the task's source function vector at layer 9. The total objective is ℓ = ℓ_LM + α₁ℓ_FV + α₂ℓ_KL with α₁=1 and α₂=0.08, and the approach is composable with existing continual learning baselines (IncLora, EWC, OLora, InsCL), yielding consistent gains of ~5–8 points in general performance and ~15–27 points in in-context learning across four benchmarks.

## Results

On Llama2-7b-chat, Function Vector Guided (FVG) training achieves average improvements of +5.44 in GP (general zero-shot performance) and +17.52 in IP (in-context learning performance) across all four continual learning baselines and three SuperNI/TRACE benchmarks. The largest single gains appear in IP: EWC+FVG recovers +27.18 on IP for NI-Seq-G1, and OLora+FVG gains +33.93 on IP for NI-Seq-C1. On the TRACE benchmark, IncLora+FVG improves FP (fine-tuned task performance) by +12.13 on Llama2-7b-chat, showing that FVG also protects plasticity on new tasks. Across Llama3-8b-chat and Mistral-7b-instruct, FVG consistently yields +3 to +8 GP and +5 to +16 IP gains over the corresponding baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FVG is evaluated as an add-on to four continual learning baselines: IncLora (incremental LoRA adapters), EWC (elastic weight consolidation), OLora (orthogonal LoRA), and InsCL (instruction-based memory replay). FVG consistently wins on GP and IP across all four baselines and all three models, with the largest IP gains reaching +33.93 (OLora+FVG on NI-Seq-C1) and +27.18 (EWC+FVG on NI-Seq-G1). On FP (plasticity on newly learned tasks), FVG is largely neutral to positive: IncLora+FVG gains +12.13 on TRACE, while EWC+FVG and OLora+FVG show small FP changes (±1–8). The one notable exception is EWC+FVG on TRACE, where FP drops by −8.77, and InsCL+FVG on NI-Seq-M1 where FP drops by −2.22, attributed to gradient conflicts between the memory replay buffer and the FV regularization. Overall, FVG dominates on the stability metrics (GP, IP) while maintaining competitive plasticity, making it a drop-in improvement over all four baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the function vector framework from mechanistic interpretability (Todd et al., 2023) as a diagnostic and intervention tool for catastrophic forgetting in continual instruction tuning, bridging the gap between interpretability research and continual learning. It departs from the dominant parameter-level view of forgetting (EWC, gradient orthogonalization, parameter L2 distance) by reframing the problem through the latent variable lens of in-context learning (Xie et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2024a), arguing that the critical quantity is the input-to-function-vector mapping P_M(θ|x) rather than the task function P_M(y|x,θ) itself. The proposed FV-guided training is designed as a plug-in regularization layer on top of existing continual learning baselines (IncLora, EWC, OLora, InsCL) rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic causal explanation for why LLMs forget during continual instruction tuning, shifting the focus from 'functions are overwritten' to 'function activation is biased,' which has direct implications for how future anti-forgetting methods should be designed. The FV-guided training offers a practical, model-agnostic regularization that yields substantial in-context learning recovery (15–25+ IP points) without sacrificing plasticity on new tasks. Key limitations include the expensive causal mediation analysis required to extract the attention head set S (model-specific and dataset-dependent), evaluation limited to 7B–13B models, and the assumption that the FV head set is transferable across tasks. Open questions include whether the activation-bias mechanism holds for larger models, non-ICL evaluation settings, and whether the FV framework can be made cheaper to compute at scale.
