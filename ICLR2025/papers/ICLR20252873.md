## Problem

Unsupervised ground metric learning via Wasserstein Singular Vectors (WSVs) is effective but computationally prohibitive, requiring O(n²m²(n log n + m log m)) per power iteration due to repeated full pairwise Wasserstein distance computations. This limits the method to small datasets and prevents its application to large-scale problems such as single-cell RNA sequencing with thousands of cells and genes. The paper asks whether the tree-Wasserstein distance (TWD) can serve as a principled low-rank surrogate that preserves solution quality while reducing per-iteration cost by at least a quadratic factor.

## Input / Output

The method takes a non-negative data matrix X ∈ ℝ⁺ⁿˣᵐ (e.g., gene expression counts per cell) and constructs two trees via ClusterTree: one embedding samples as leaves (T_A) and one embedding features as leaves (T_B). The tree topology is fixed once; only edge weight vectors w_A ∈ ℝ⁺ⁿ⁻¹ and w_B ∈ ℝ⁺ᵐ⁻¹ are learned iteratively. The output is these weight vectors, from which full pairwise distance matrices on samples and features can be recovered, enabling downstream clustering (e.g., cell-type assignment via silhouette-scored k-means on the learned metric).

## Method

Tree-WSV replaces the full pairwise ground metric matrices in the WSV fixed-point equations with tree metrics parameterized by edge weights, converting each power iteration into a non-negative least squares (NNLS) problem of size (N−1)×(N−1) rather than an n²×m² Wasserstein computation. The key theoretical result (Lemma 2.3, Theorem 2.4) shows that for any tree with root degree ≥ 3, the pairwise leaf-to-leaf path matrix Y′ has rank exactly N−1, guaranteeing a unique strictly positive solution and enabling a fast recursive algorithm to extract a full-rank basis set U without materializing the full n²-column tensor. Each iteration computes only N−1 or M−1 tree-Wasserstein distances (each O(n) or O(m) via the closed-form TWD expression) and solves one NNLS, yielding overall per-iteration complexity O(n³ + m³ + mn). A meta-iteration loop re-constructs the ClusterTree topology using the previously learned ground metric as the clustering distance, increasing tree depth and improving clustering accuracy while remaining fully unsupervised.

## Results

On the Neurons V1 dataset (1,468 cells), Tree-WSV achieves an average silhouette width (ASW) of 0.602 at its best meta-iteration, substantially outperforming Sinkhorn Singular Vectors (SSV) at 0.256, while also running faster (32 min vs. 70 min). On the large Human Lung Cell Atlas dataset (7,000 cells), SSV fails to converge after 12 hours, whereas Tree-WSV completes in 71 minutes with ASW 0.457. On the PBMC 3k dataset, SSV achieves a slightly higher ASW (0.348 vs. 0.313) but requires 110 minutes versus 72 minutes for Tree-WSV. On the synthetic toy dataset, Tree-WSV is 2–3 orders of magnitude faster than standard WSV and achieves lower Hilbert distance error than SSV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Sinkhorn Singular Vectors (SSV) from Huizing et al. (2022), the entropic-regularised variant of the standard Wasserstein Singular Vectors approach. Tree-WSV decisively outperforms SSV on the Neurons V1 dataset, achieving ASW 0.602 versus 0.256 (more than double) while also being faster (32 min vs. 70 min). On the large 7,000-cell Lung dataset, SSV does not converge within 12 hours, whereas Tree-WSV completes in 71 minutes with ASW 0.457, demonstrating a critical scalability advantage. On the PBMC 3k dataset, SSV retains a modest ASW edge (0.348 vs. 0.313) but at a 1.5× runtime cost (110 min vs. 72 min). On the synthetic toy dataset, Tree-WSV achieves lower Hilbert distance error than SSV while running 2–3 orders of magnitude faster, confirming the theoretical complexity reduction from O(n²m²(n log n + m log m)) to O(n³ + m³ + mn) per iteration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established threads: the unsupervised Wasserstein Singular Vector (WSV) framework of Huizing et al. (2022), which learns ground metrics via power iterations on pairwise Wasserstein distances, and the Tree-Wasserstein Distance (TWD) approximation of Yamada et al. (2022), which embeds measures on trees for linear-complexity transport computation. The key conceptual move is to replace the full pairwise cost matrix in the WSV fixed-point equations with tree edge weights, yielding a low-rank geometric approximation that reduces per-iteration complexity from O(n²m²(n log n + m log m)) to O(n³ + m³ + mn). The paper further departs from prior TWD work by proving that the tree structure can be chosen flexibly (any root degree ≥ 3 suffices) and by introducing a recursive algorithm for computing the basis set of pairwise leaf-path matrices, which is essential for scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tree-WSV makes unsupervised ground metric learning practical for datasets of 10,000+ samples/features, where the standard WSV and its Sinkhorn-regularised variant (SSV) are intractable or fail to converge (SSV exceeded 12 hours on the 7k-cell lung atlas). The geometric low-rank interpretation via tree embeddings provides a principled understanding of what the learned metric approximates, and the recursive basis-set algorithm has potential applicability beyond this specific problem. Open questions include the lack of convergence guarantees for the outer meta-iteration loop, the sensitivity to initial tree construction (Euclidean-based ClusterTree), and whether the stochasticity in basis-set selection can be better controlled. The method's utility for other high-dimensional unlabeled data (e.g., neuronal activity) remains to be demonstrated.
