## Problem

Implicit Transfer Operator (ITO) learning can accelerate molecular dynamics by learning surrogate transition densities at large time lags, but it requires extensive unbiased MD data that is often unavailable in practice. While Markov state models can integrate off-equilibrium and enhanced-sampling data, no deep generative surrogate for the transition density had a principled way to incorporate prior knowledge of the equilibrium distribution. This paper addresses both the data-efficiency bottleneck and the lack of guaranteed asymptotically unbiased equilibrium statistics in deep generative MD surrogates.

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular configuration x_t, a time-lag integer N (sampled from a discrete exponential distribution during training), and a pre-trained Boltzmann Generator providing the fixed equilibrium score s_eq. It outputs a sampled configuration at t+Nτ via a conditional denoising diffusion process, enabling one-step sampling of long-time-scale dynamics. For the interpolation protocol, an additional unbiased dynamic observable (e.g., a time-correlation function from experiment or long MD) is used to select the interpolation parameter N_int, producing a conformational ensemble that bridges off-equilibrium simulation data and the equilibrium distribution.

## Method

BoPITO decomposes the transition density score into a fixed equilibrium component s_eq (from a pre-trained Boltzmann Generator) and a learned dynamic component s_dyn scaled by λ̂^N, where 0 < λ̂ < 1 is a hyperparameter. This factorization is principled: it mirrors the spectral decomposition of the transfer operator where the first eigenvalue λ_1=1 yields the stationary Boltzmann distribution, and the λ̂^N decay guarantees that as N→∞ the model samples from the equilibrium distribution. The dynamic score s_dyn is trained via a conditional DDPM objective on off-equilibrium MD data while s_eq remains frozen, reducing the effective parameter count and improving sample efficiency by an order of magnitude. For interpolation beyond the training lag N_max, the model fixes s_dyn at N_max (avoiding out-of-distribution evaluation) and varies N_int in the decay factor, selecting N_int by minimizing the discrepancy between the model's predicted dynamic observable and an unbiased reference value. The architecture uses SE(3)-equivariant or MB-ITO networks with 500–1000 diffusion steps and sigmoidal or polynomial noise schedules.

## Results

The headline result is that BoPITO requires approximately one order of magnitude less training data than ITO to achieve the same accuracy in predicting time-correlation functions, as measured by |Δ correlation| (absolute difference from MD ground truth). This advantage is consistent across all three test systems (Prinz potential, Alanine Dipeptide, and Chignolin) and is most pronounced at long time-scales where ITO models fail due to insufficient effective samples. Additionally, BoPITO interpolators trained on biased (off-equilibrium) Alanine Dipeptide data, when calibrated to an unbiased dynamic observable, recover correlation functions that match the unbiased MD ground truth for lags beyond the training horizon (N > 100), overcoming systematic errors present in both the biased ITO and biased BoPITO models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the original ITO (Schreiner et al., 2023), and BoPITO wins across all three systems and all time-scale regimes. On the Prinz potential, ITO suffers from poor long-term dynamics modeling when data is scarce, while BoPITO accurately captures long-term dynamics without degrading short- and medium-time-scale performance. For Alanine Dipeptide and Chignolin, the paper reports that an order of magnitude more trajectories are needed to train an ITO to the same accuracy as a BoPITO model trained on the same data. In the biased-data interpolation experiment (Figure 4), both ITO and BoPITO are trained on the same biased trajectories; the BoPITO interpolator, when fitted to the unbiased dynamic observable via eq. 16, recovers the correct long-lag correlation function, whereas the ITO model trained on the same biased data retains a systematic error. The paper does not compare against other deep generative transition-density surrogates (e.g., Timewarp, Score Dynamics), noting that no such methods leverage equilibrium distribution information.

## Relation to Prior Work

BoPITO sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Implicit Transfer Operator (ITO) learning, which uses conditional diffusion models to learn multi-timescale transition density surrogates for MD, and Boltzmann Generators (BGs), which learn to sample equilibrium distributions via invertible neural networks. The key conceptual departure is using the spectral decomposition of the transfer operator to motivate a principled separation of the score into a fixed equilibrium component (from a pre-trained BG) and a learnable dynamic component, something that prior deep generative MD surrogates (Timewarp, Score Dynamics) did not exploit. The BoPITO interpolator further extends the framework by introducing a tunable sampling protocol that bridges off-equilibrium simulation data and the equilibrium model, drawing on ideas from augmented Markov models in molecular biophysics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-of-magnitude reduction in required simulation data and the guaranteed asymptotically unbiased equilibrium statistics address two practical bottlenecks that have limited the adoption of deep generative MD surrogates. The interpolation protocol opens a path to integrating heterogeneous data sources (off-equilibrium MD, biased/enhanced sampling via BGs, and experimental observables) into a single generative model, which is a significant step toward practical multi-source inference for molecular systems. Key open questions include the lack of Chapman-Kolmogorov self-consistency guarantees when relying on interpolation, the challenge of transferring the hyperparameter λ̂ across systems with different relaxation timescales, and whether the approach scales to larger, more complex molecular systems beyond the 10-residue Chignolin benchmark.
