## Problem

Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) offer theoretically superior function approximation to MLPs, but naive integration into transformers fails at scale: B-spline base functions are GPU-hostile due to recursive computation, per-edge unique functions cause O(GK) computational blowup, and default initialization violates variance-preserving principles. Prior attempts (e.g., ViT+KAN) collapse to ~63% on ImageNet or diverge entirely at Base scale, leaving a clear gap between KAN's theoretical promise and practical deployability in large vision models.

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, KAT consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet classification, 800×1333 for COCO detection, 512×512 crops for ADE20K segmentation) and produces class labels, bounding boxes with instance masks, or pixel-wise semantic masks respectively. The architecture is a direct drop-in replacement for ViT's MLP blocks, preserving identical patch size (16×16), layer count, hidden dimensions, and head counts, so pre-trained ViT weights can be loaded one-to-one into the GR-KAN linear layers. For tabular and graph tasks, the same GR-KAN substitution is applied within TabTransformer and Graph Transformer backbones, accepting feature vectors or node/edge embeddings and outputting AUC scores or regression/classification predictions.

## Method

KAT replaces each two-layer MLP in a Vision Transformer with a Group-Rational KAN (GR-KAN) layer, which combines three key innovations. First, B-spline base functions are replaced with Safe Padé Activation Units (rational functions of degree m=5/n=4) implemented in custom CUDA kernels using Horner's method, reducing per-edge FLOPs from 204 to 21. Second, instead of learning a unique activation per input-output pair, the rational function coefficients are shared across g=8 groups of input channels while each edge retains a unique scalar weight, bringing parameter count to MLP-level (d_in×d_out + d_out + (m+n×g)) and enabling a matrix-form computation: GR-KAN(x) = Linear(GroupRational(x)). Third, a variance-preserving initialization scheme first fits the rational coefficients to approximate identity (first layer) and Swish (second layer) via Levenberg-Marquardt, then sets the scalar weights from N(0, α/d_in) where α = E[F(x)²]/Var[x] is computed numerically. This allows KAT to load pre-trained ViT weights directly and continue training, achieving 82.3% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K (KAT-B) versus 79.1% for ViT-B at nearly identical FLOPs.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, KAT-B achieves 82.3% Top-1 accuracy (82.8% with pre-trained ViT initialization), surpassing ViT-B (79.1%) by 3.2% and DeiT-B (81.8%) by 0.5%, while maintaining comparable FLOPs (17.06G vs 16.87G). On MS-COCO detection, KATDet-S gains 3.0 AP^box over ViTDet-S (47.5 vs 44.5) with only 1 GFLOPs overhead. On ADE20K segmentation, KAT-S achieves 46.1 mIoU, outperforming DeiT-S by 2.6%. The vanilla ViT+KAN baseline fails catastrophically at scale (64.9% at Tiny, 62.9% at Small, and NaN at Base), highlighting the critical role of KAT's design choices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KAT consistently outperforms both ViT and DeiT across all three model sizes on ImageNet-1K: KAT-T (74.6%) beats ViT-Ti (72.7%) and DeiT-T (72.2%) by 1.9–2.4%; KAT-S (81.2%) beats DeiT-S (79.8%) by 1.4%; KAT-B (82.3%) beats DeiT-B (81.8%) by 0.5%. The direct KAN replacement (ViT+KAN, Chen et al.) fails dramatically—achieving only 64.9% at Tiny, 62.9% at Small, and diverging to NaN at Base—while using 2–3× more parameters and FLOPs, confirming that KAT's rational base function, group sharing, and variance-preserving initialization are essential for scalability. On COCO detection, KATDet-S gains 3.0 AP^box over ViTDet-S (47.5 vs 44.5) and 1.5 over Swin-T (46.0), while KATDet-B gains 1.4 AP^box over ViTDet-B (47.7 vs 46.3). On ADE20K segmentation, KAT-S (46.1 mIoU) outperforms DeiT-S (43.5) and Swin-T (45.8) but trails ConvNeXt-T (46.7), which benefits from a hierarchical architecture. On tabular data, KAT improves average AUC by +0.9 over TabTransformer across 15 datasets, and on graph tasks it improves ZINC (0.211 vs 0.226), CLUSTER (74.1 vs 73.2), and PATTERN (85.3 vs 84.8) over Graph Transformer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) of Liu et al. (2024), which proposed learnable univariate functions per edge as a replacement for fixed activations in MLPs, and on the Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture of Dosovitskiy et al. (2021). It departs from KAN in three specific ways: replacing B-spline base functions with rational functions (building on the Safe Padé Activation Unit of Molina et al., 2020), introducing group-wise parameter sharing to reduce the per-edge function count, and designing a variance-preserving initialization scheme. The resulting GR-KAN layer is positioned as a hybrid that retains KAN's learnable-activation spirit while matching MLP's computational structure (activation-then-linear), making it a practical drop-in replacement for the MLP block in standard transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that KAN-based channel mixers can scale to ImageNet-level training and consistently outperform their MLP counterparts across recognition, detection, and segmentation, closing a gap that prior attempts (ViT+KAN) left wide open. The group-sharing insight is broadly applicable: it shows that the per-edge uniqueness that defines KAN is not strictly necessary for expressiveness, which reframes the design space for learnable-activation architectures. Key limitations remain: throughput is ~13% lower than standard activations, rational functions introduce higher-order gradient instability, and the authors acknowledge GR-KAN is a hybrid rather than a pure KAN. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to NLP/LLM settings, whether alternative base functions (Fourier, wavelet, RBF) can close the remaining speed gap, and whether the near-identity behavior of most trained rational functions indicates that the added expressiveness is underutilized.
