## Problem

Despite extended context windows, LLMs still struggle to accurately identify and reason over key passages in long documents, largely because dense coreference structures (pronouns, modifiers, similar noun phrases) create ambiguity that confuses the model. Existing long-context strategies such as retrieval-augmented generation, compression, and chain-of-thought prompting do not explicitly resolve these referential ambiguities, leaving the model to disambiguate references on its own.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long context document (up to 128k tokens) and a question as input. The document is first partitioned into sub-documents of at most 512 tokens via a sentence-aligned sliding window, each processed by the Maverick coreference model. The output is a coreference-resolved version of the original text in which all mentions within each coreference cluster are replaced by a single representative non-pronoun mention, which is then paired with the question and fed to an LLM for answer generation.

## Method

LQCA is a four-step, zero-shot pipeline that bridges coreference gaps across sub-document boundaries. First, the Maverick (DeBERTa-v3) model performs mention extraction and coreference resolution independently on each sliding-window sub-document. Second, a sparse co-reference score matrix is built for mentions co-occurring in the same sub-document, and cross-sub-document mention distances are computed via a multiplication principle (max over all intermediate mentions of the product of pairwise distances), solved as an all-pairs longest-path problem on a restricted graph; mentions whose distance exceeds a threshold k (default 0.9) are grouped into strongly connected components representing global coreference clusters. Third, spaCy part-of-speech tagging identifies pronoun mentions, and the representative for each cluster is chosen as the most frequent non-pronoun mention (earliest occurrence as tiebreaker). Finally, all mentions in the original text are replaced by their cluster's representative (largest-overlap priority for overlapping spans), and the cleaned context is passed to the LLM for question answering. The key insight is that eliminating referential ambiguity at the text level—rather than relying on the LLM to resolve it during inference—substantially improves long-context comprehension, yielding up to +3.61% average improvement on GPT-4o.

## Results

LQCA achieves the best average score across all five tested LLMs and nine long-context datasets. On GPT-4o, LQCA reaches 63.76 average (Rouge-L, Accuracy, F1), improving +3.61% over Vanilla LM (60.15) and +2.55% over Zero-shot CoT (61.21). On OpenAI o1-mini, LQCA attains 67.78 average, a +3.18% gain over Vanilla LM (64.60). The largest single-dataset gains appear on long-dependency tasks: +7.59% F1 on HotpotQA (GPT-4o) and +5.05% Rouge-L on LooGLE arXiv summarization (o1-mini).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LQCA outperforms all four baselines (Vanilla LM, Zero-shot CoT, RAG with self-Corpus, and RECOMP with self-Corpus) on the average score for every model tested. The margin is largest against retrieval-based baselines: on GPT-4o, LQCA (63.76) beats RAG (57.72) by +6.04% and RECOMP (56.28) by +7.48%, indicating that coreference-resolved full-context input is more effective than retrieving compressed slices. Against Zero-shot CoT, LQCA gains +2.55% on GPT-4o and +3.48% on o1-mini, showing that explicit mention replacement complements rather than replaces chain-of-thought prompting. On smaller open-source models (Llama-3-8b, Qwen2-7b), LQCA still leads on average (39.37 vs. 38.46 for CoT; 38.44 vs. 37.06 for CoT), though the gap narrows and RAG occasionally matches or exceeds LQCA on individual sub-tasks (e.g., Llama-3-8b on L-Eval-Coursera: RAG 48.77 vs. LQCA 49.15). The paper notes that for smaller models with limited context understanding, retrieval augmentation can be competitive, but LQCA's advantage is most pronounced on long-dependency tasks (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA) where coreference ambiguity is highest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Maverick (Martinelli et al., 2024), a DeBERTa-v3-based coreference resolution model, repurposing it as a preprocessing component for long-context LLM question answering. It departs from the dominant long-context strategies—retrieval-augmented generation (Lewis et al., 2020; Ram et al., 2023) and context compression (RECOMP, Xu et al., 2024)—by instead improving text quality through systematic mention replacement before feeding context to the LLM. The cross-document mention merging via a distance-based graph and strongly connected components is a novel pipeline design, but the individual NLU components (Maverick, spaCy POS tagging) are off-the-shelf. The work sits at the intersection of information extraction (coreference resolution) and long-context LLM evaluation, positioning itself as a text-quality intervention rather than a new retrieval or compression paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that resolving coreferential ambiguity in long texts is a meaningful lever for improving LLM performance, particularly on long-dependency tasks like multi-hop QA where entity tracking is critical. The middle-position accuracy improvement (+6.1% in the [0.4, 0.6] interval) offers a concrete, low-cost mitigation for the well-documented 'lost in the middle' problem without requiring architectural changes. However, the approach inherits limitations from its dependency on Maverick's 512-token window, is sensitive to the distance threshold hyperparameter, and may offer diminishing returns for shorter contexts or knowledge-intensive questions where domain terminology matters more than referential clarity. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to non-English languages, whether adaptive thresholding could reduce the grid-search burden, and whether the method composes well with emerging native long-context models that eliminate the need for partitioning.
