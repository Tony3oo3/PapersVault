## Problem

Zero-shot Vision-Language Models (VLMs) underperform significantly on Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) compared to task-specific fine-tuned models, yet standard white-box fine-tuning requires full access to model weights, gradients, and architecture—impossible for closed-source or proprietary VLMs accessed only via APIs. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a black-box adaptation method that improves VLM performance on REC without any access to the VLM's internal parameters.

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image and a natural-language referring expression as input. A frozen VLM (e.g., Grounding-DINO or Florence-2) first generates 2–45 candidate bounding boxes with labels and confidence scores. These candidates are serialized into a structured text prompt listing each box's xyxy coordinates, label, and score, followed by the query and a request for the best-matching box index. The LLM outputs a single integer index selecting one candidate box, which is the final prediction. No pixel-level output is produced; the method operates entirely on the VLM's discrete detection outputs.

## Method

LLM-wrapper converts the frozen VLM's open-vocabulary detection outputs into a natural-language prompt and delegates the disambiguation reasoning to a separately fine-tuned LLM. The LLM is trained with LoRA (low-rank adaptation) using a cross-entropy loss on the correct box index, with training samples filtered to retain only those where at least one VLM proposal achieves IoU > 0.5 with ground truth, and box order randomly permuted to prevent positional shortcuts. Training is efficient—using flash attention and 4-bit quantization, a single 40 GB A100 GPU suffices for under 7 hours. The approach is VLM-agnostic (works with any open-vocabulary detector), supports ensembling multiple VLMs by concatenating their candidate lists in the prompt, and transfers across VLMs and datasets without retraining, since the LLM learns spatial-semantic reasoning over text rather than model-specific activations.

## Results

On RefCOCOg (the most challenging standard REC benchmark), LLM-wrapper with Llama 3 8B improves zero-shot Grounding-DINO from 59.32 to 77.36 P@1 (+18.0) and zero-shot Florence-2 from 66.16 to 78.03 P@1 (+11.9). On RefCOCO, gains are even larger, reaching +23.0 P@1 for GD and +14.7 for Flo2. On the driving-domain Talk2Car dataset, improvements range from +8.7 to +18.3 P@1 across VLM/LLM combinations. On the complex HC-RefLoCo benchmark (84-word queries), LLM-wrapper achieves +19.1 P@1 over zero-shot Flo2, nearly doubling the +8.2 gain from white-box fine-tuning transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot Grounding-DINO (Liu et al., ECCV 2024), LLM-wrapper achieves large gains of +17.5 to +24.1 P@1 across RefCOCO/+/g, and notably narrows the gap to white-box fine-tuned GD(B) to within ~1 P@1 on RefCOCOg (77.36 vs. 77.99 test). Against zero-shot Florence-2 (Xiao et al., CVPR 2024), improvements are +10.9 to +18.3 P@1, though a significant gap remains versus white-box fine-tuned Flo2 (78.03 vs. 91.02 test on RefCOCOg). Compared to Contrastive Region Guidance (Wan et al., ECCV 2024), LLM-wrapper substantially outperforms on RefCOCOg test (82.61 vs. 69.60 P@1 when applied on top of white-box FT GD(B)), while remaining fully black-box and requiring only a single VLM inference per input. Against Kosmos-2 (Peng et al., 2023), a model designed specifically for REC, LLM-wrapper provides a modest +1.49 P@1 boost without degrading performance. On the related Referring Expression Segmentation task (Appendix B), LLM-wrapper+SAM (58.59 cIoU test) trails the white-box SOTA methods LISA-7B (70.6) and GLaMM (74.9), reflecting that pixel-level segmentation requires more than box-level reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard paradigm of white-box fine-tuning VLMs for REC by introducing a black-box adaptation strategy that wraps a frozen VLM's detection outputs with a fine-tuned LLM performing semantic and spatial reasoning over natural-language prompts. It builds conceptually on the LLM-as-reasoner line of work (e.g., LISA, GLaMM) but repurposes it for a fundamentally different interface: rather than integrating visual tokens into the LLM, it serializes bounding boxes and labels into text, making the method agnostic to the VLM's architecture. Among prior black-box adaptation methods (Ouali et al., Yu et al., Oh et al., Wan et al.), LLM-wrapper is the first to require only a single forward VLM call per input, no access to internal representations or output probability distributions, and to enable ensembling and cross-VLM transfer through its text-based interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLM-wrapper addresses a practically critical gap: adapting proprietary or API-only VLMs (e.g., Grounding-DINO 1.5) to downstream tasks without weight access, a scenario increasingly common as foundation models become closed-source. The demonstrated transferability across VLMs and datasets, plus the ability to ensemble heterogeneous detectors, makes it a versatile tool for practitioners who cannot or should not fine-tune the underlying VLM. Key limitations remain: performance is bounded by the VLM's detection recall, bounding-box-plus-label text may be insufficient for visually subtle distinctions (e.g., object orientation, fine spatial relations), and the additional LLM inference adds latency. Open questions include whether richer visual grounding (e.g., cropped images, multi-modal prompts) could close the remaining gap to white-box fine-tuning, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D grounding tasks.
