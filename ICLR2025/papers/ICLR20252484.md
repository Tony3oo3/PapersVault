## Problem

Most test-time adaptation (TTA) methods assign definite pseudo-labels to unlabeled test samples, inevitably introducing false labels and discarding per-sample uncertainty. This rigidity prevents pseudo-labels from being flexibly refined as the model progressively adapts to the target domain, limiting their potential for performance improvement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252484/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB test images streamed in mini-batches of 128 from the target domain, along with a pre-trained CNN (ResNet-18 or ResNet-50) initialized from ImageNet-1K weights. It produces a single class label prediction per image. A key operational detail is the buffer mechanism: samples that cannot yet be reliably labeled are stored (up to K=32) and revisited in later adaptation steps, so the effective training set grows over the streaming process.

## Method

PASLE partitions each incoming batch into a confident subset (where the top-two prediction margin exceeds a dynamic threshold τ(r), receiving one-hot pseudo-labels trained with cross-entropy) and an uncertain subset (where at least one class can be ruled out but the true label is ambiguous, receiving candidate pseudo-label sets trained with a classifier-consistent loss). Proposition 1 provides a theoretical guarantee that the top prediction is correct when the margin exceeds τ(r), and that any class whose score falls below the top score by more than τ(r) cannot be the true label. The threshold τ(r) is monotonically decreased over adaptation steps, progressively converting uncertain samples into confident ones as the model aligns with the target distribution. The framework is supported by a generalization bound showing tighter target error with more effectively supervised samples, and a pseudo-label error term E[‖q−p‖₂] that quantifies how closely the assigned label distribution approximates the Bayes posterior.

## Results

PASLE achieves the best average accuracy across all six benchmark datasets with both ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 backbones. On domain generalization benchmarks (ResNet-18), PASLE reaches 66.74% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline TSD (65.66%) by 1.08 points, with the largest gains on VLCS (+2.44%) and DomainNet (+1.03%). On image corruption benchmarks, PASLE attains 76.67% on CIFAR-10-C and 45.32% on CIFAR-100-C, surpassing the second-best SHOT-IM (75.85% / 44.36%) by 0.82 and 0.96 points respectively. With ResNet-50, PASLE further extends its lead to 71.09% average on domain generalization, a 1.76-point improvement over TSD (69.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASLE outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and both backbone architectures. The closest competitor is TSD (Wang et al., 2023), which uses test-time self-distillation with a dynamic memory bank; PASLE exceeds it by 0.28 points on PACS, 2.44 on VLCS, 0.57 on OfficeHome, and 1.03 on DomainNet (ResNet-18). Among entropy-minimization methods, DEYO (Lee et al., 2024) and TENT (Wang et al., 2021) trail PASLE by 1.78 and 4.14 points in average accuracy respectively. Pseudo-labeling baselines PL (Lee, 2013) and T3A (Iwasawa & Matsuo, 2021) are surpassed by 2.17 and 2.30 points on average, while the prototype-graph approach PROGRAM (Sun et al., 2024) lags by 3.11 points. On image corruption benchmarks, PASLE leads SHOT-IM (Liang et al., 2020) by 0.82% on CIFAR-10-C and 0.96% on CIFAR-100-C, with the gap widening on harder corruptions (e.g., +5.24% on contrast for CIFAR-10-C). Notably, T3A and ERM collapse on corruption datasets (23.52% and 20.66% on CIFAR-10-C), while PASLE maintains 76.67%, highlighting the robustness of selective label enhancement under severe distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

PASLE draws directly from the partial label learning literature, specifically the label enhancement framework of Xu et al. (2019, 2020), which partitions samples into confident and uncertain subsets and assigns candidate label sets to the latter. The classifier-consistent loss for uncertain samples is adopted from Feng et al. (2020). In the TTA context, the paper departs from the dominant paradigm of definite pseudo-labels (prototype-based, nearest-neighbor-based, or entropy-minimized) by introducing label-level uncertainty through candidate pseudo-label sets, combined with a progressive threshold schedule that refines labels as the model adapts. The theoretical analysis extends the disparity discrepancy framework of Zhang et al. (2019) to derive a generalization bound specific to the mixed source/target sampling in OTTA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation of pseudo-labeling in TTA: definite labels discard per-sample uncertainty and cannot be refined as the model improves during adaptation. By preserving uncertainty through candidate sets and progressively tightening them, PASLE achieves consistent gains across six benchmarks and two architectures without additional inference cost, making it practical for streaming OTTA deployment. However, the approach still fundamentally relies on pseudo-labels and a manually scheduled threshold, and the buffer mechanism introduces memory overhead. Open questions include whether the threshold schedule can be learned end-to-end, whether the method extends to non-classification tasks (segmentation, detection), and whether the gains persist under more extreme distribution shifts or very large-scale settings beyond DomainNet.
