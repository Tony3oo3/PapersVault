## Problem

Existing active learning methods exhibit a sharp regime split: representation-based selectors (e.g., MaxHerding, Typiclust) outperform uncertainty-based ones (e.g., Margin, Entropy) at low label budgets, while the reverse holds at high budgets. No single method reliably works across both regimes, and prior switching heuristics (SelectAL, TCM) are fragile, computationally expensive, or require large validation holdouts. The paper asks whether one objective can smoothly interpolate between the two behaviors without discrete regime detection.

## Input / Output

At each acquisition round the method consumes (i) the current labeled set L_t, (ii) the remaining unlabeled pool U_t, (iii) a classifier f retrained from scratch on L_t (cold-start), and (iv) a fixed self-supervised feature extractor g (e.g., SimCLR or DINO embeddings) used only for kernel similarity. It outputs a batch of B_t points selected greedily from U_t. The classifier f is used solely to compute per-point uncertainty (margin, entropy, or confidence) after temperature scaling; g is never updated. The Gaussian kernel lengthscale σ is set to the minimum pairwise g-distance within L_t, so it shrinks automatically as the labeled set grows.

## Method

The paper defines uncertainty coverage (UCoverage), which multiplies the generalized coverage kernel term by a per-point uncertainty U(x;f), yielding a monotone submodular set function. Greedy maximization of its empirical estimate is called UHerding and inherits a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee plus a uniform-convergence bound (Theorem 2) showing the noisy estimate is close to the true coverage for all size-B subsets. Two adaptive parameter mechanisms drive the interpolation: (1) temperature scaling of f's logits, chosen to minimize ECE on a small validation split of L_t, makes U(x;f) near-constant when the model is poorly calibrated (low budget → UCoverage ≈ GCoverage ≈ MaxHerding); (2) the kernel lengthscale σ is set to the minimum pairwise g-distance in L_t, so as |L_t| grows, σ→0 and UCoverage degenerates to pure uncertainty-based selection (Proposition 4). The paper further shows that existing hybrid methods (BADGE, ALFA-Mix, weighted k-means) are special cases of UHerding with particular uncertainty choices, and that applying the same parameter adaptation to them improves their performance.

## Results

UHerding achieves the highest mean improvement over Random selection across all budget regimes and all five datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, DomainNet, ImageNet). In the low-budget regime, UHerding gains +5.5% on CIFAR-10, +5.5% on CIFAR-100, +7.4% on DomainNet, and +11.2% on ImageNet; in the high-budget regime it still leads with +3.0% (CIFAR-10), +2.1% (CIFAR-100), +2.3% (DomainNet), and +2.0% (ImageNet). No competing method outperforms UHerding in any setting, and several baselines (Coreset, Typiclust at high budget, Entropy at low budget) fall below Random selection in at least one regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UHerding outperforms every baseline in every budget regime and dataset setting. Against the strongest representation-based competitor MaxHerding, UHerding leads by 0.5–1.2 points in the low-budget regime (e.g., +5.5 vs +5.0 on CIFAR-10, +11.2 vs +10.6 on ImageNet) and by 2–4 points in the high-budget regime where MaxHerding degrades below Random (e.g., +2.1 vs −2.2 on CIFAR-100, +2.0 vs −1.2 on ImageNet). Against the strongest uncertainty-based method Margin, UHerding wins by 5–9 points in the low-budget regime (e.g., +5.5 vs −0.4 on CIFAR-10) while matching or slightly exceeding Margin in the high-budget regime (+3.0 vs +2.5 on CIFAR-10). Among hybrid methods, ALFA-Mix is the closest competitor in the low-budget transfer-learning setting (+5.1 on ImageNet vs UHerding's +11.2), but UHerding still leads by 6+ points; in the high-budget regime UHerding exceeds ALFA-Mix by 0.8–1.1 points. Coreset and Typiclust are substantially worse than Random in the high-budget regime (−9.6 to −12 for Coreset; −3.2 to −9.9 for Typiclust), confirming that pure representation-based strategies fail as budgets grow. ActiveFT, designed for single-iteration selection, underperforms all iterative methods in the high-budget transfer-learning setting (0.0 on DomainNet, −0.1 on ImageNet vs UHerding's +2.3 and +2.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MaxHerding (Bae et al., ECCV 2024), which introduced generalized coverage (GCoverage) as a continuous, parameter-light alternative to discrete covering objectives for low-budget active learning. The key conceptual step is weighting GCoverage by a model's uncertainty to produce UCoverage, then showing that existing hybrid methods (BADGE, ALFA-Mix, weighted k-means) are recoverable as special cases of the resulting greedy optimizer, UHerding. The paper departs from the binary 'switch between low- and high-budget methods' framing of SelectAL and TCM by instead using continuous parameter adaptation (temperature scaling for calibration and adaptive kernel radius) to smoothly interpolate between representation-based and uncertainty-based behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that UHerding is, to the authors' knowledge, the first single active learning method that reliably outperforms both representation-based and uncertainty-based baselines across the full range of label budgets without requiring regime detection or re-training. This removes a significant practical burden: practitioners no longer need to guess whether their budget is 'low' or 'high' for a given dataset-model pair. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-image modalities (text, audio), whether the self-supervised feature extractor requirement is a bottleneck in settings where such models are unavailable, and whether the submodularity guarantee on the coverage objective translates uniformly to downstream task performance in all architectures.
