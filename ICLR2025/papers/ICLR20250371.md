## Problem

All existing convergence analyses for multi-objective optimization (MOO) algorithms such as MGDA, CAGrad, PCGrad, MoCo, and SDMGrad rely on standard L-smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions that are known to fail for neural networks like LSTMs and Transformers. This paper asks whether the fundamental MGDA algorithm can provably converge under the much weaker generalized ℓ-smoothness condition, where the Lipschitz constant is a non-decreasing function of the gradient norm and may be unbounded, while still achieving a small conflict-avoidant (CA) distance.

## Input / Output

The method takes K scalar-valued objective functions (e.g., semantic segmentation, depth estimation, surface normal losses) parameterized by a shared model x ∈ ℝᵐ, along with access to their gradients (full or stochastic). It outputs a sequence of model parameters converging to an ε-accurate Pareto stationary point, together with a conflict-avoidant update direction d* = ∇F(x)w* at each iteration. In the stochastic setting, the algorithm consumes three independent mini-batch gradient estimates per iteration via a double-sampling strategy, and the iteration-wise CA distance guarantee requires a mini-batch size of Ω(ε⁻⁶).

## Method

The paper proves convergence of MGDA under generalized ℓ-smoothness by showing that a bounded function value implies a bounded gradient norm (via the monotonicity of φ(a) = a²/(2ℓ(2a))), then using induction to maintain this bound throughout training with appropriately small step sizes. An ℓ₂ regularization term ρ‖w‖² is added to the weight optimization to ensure the optimal weight w* is Lipschitz continuous, enabling CA distance analysis. A warm-start process (Algorithm 2) pre-iterates the weight update at the initial point to guarantee an ε-level iteration-wise CA distance from the first main-loop iteration, at the cost of smaller step sizes (α = O(ε⁹), β = O(ε⁴)) and higher sample complexity O(ε⁻¹¹) deterministic / O(ε⁻¹⁷) stochastic. A fast-approximation variant (MGDA-FA) replaces the O(K) gradient storage for weight updates with a Taylor-based forward-pass approximation, reducing time and space to O(1) while preserving the same convergence guarantee.

## Results

On Cityscapes (2 tasks: segmentation + depth), MGDA-warm start achieves the best overall balance with Δm% = 3.93±1.19, substantially outperforming Nash-MTL (6.82%), FAMO (8.13%), and MoCo (9.90%). On NYU-v2 (3 tasks: segmentation + depth + surface normal), MGDA-warm start achieves Δm% = −4.42%, the best among all methods, compared to FAMO (−4.10%), Nash-MTL (−4.04%), and MoCo (0.16%). The vanilla MGDA without warm start performs poorly on Cityscapes (Δm% = 44.14%), highlighting the critical role of the warm-start process in controlling per-iteration CA distance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGDA-warm start achieves the best Δm% on both datasets, indicating the most balanced multi-task performance. On Cityscapes, it reduces the per-task performance drop to 3.93%, beating the next-best Nash-MTL (6.82%) by nearly 3 percentage points and MoCo (9.90%) by over 6 points. On NYU-v2, it reaches −4.42% (i.e., slightly better than single-task on average), edging out FAMO (−4.10%) and Nash-MTL (−4.04%), while MoCo (0.16%) and MoDo (0.49%) show a clear performance degradation. The vanilla MGDA without warm start is the worst MOO method on Cityscapes (Δm% = 44.14%), confirming that controlling the per-iteration CA distance via warm start is essential. In terms of individual task metrics, MGDA-warm start ties Nash-MTL for the best segmentation mIoU (75.41) on Cityscapes and achieves the best depth relative error (31.07%), while MoCo leads on segmentation pixel accuracy (93.55%). The fast-approximation variant MGDA-FA is ~34% faster in wall-clock time (1.93 vs. 2.93 min/epoch) but sacrifices some depth accuracy (Rel Err 41.78 vs. 31.07), yielding Δm% = 19.44%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fundamental MGDA algorithm (Désidéri, 2012) by providing the first convergence analysis under generalized ℓ-smoothness, a condition that subsumes both standard L-smoothness and (L₀,L₁)-smoothness. It adopts the double-sampling strategy from Xiao et al. (2024) and Chen et al. (2024) for unbiased stochastic multi-gradient estimation, and draws on the fast approximation idea from FAMO (Liu et al., 2024) to derive an O(1)-space variant. The key departure from all prior MOO convergence analyses (CAGrad, PCGrad, MoCo, MoDo, SDMGrad) is the removal of both the L-smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions, which are known to fail for neural networks such as LSTMs and Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable theoretical gap: MGDA was empirically observed to converge on neural network training despite violating L-smoothness, but no proof existed. The generalized ℓ-smoothness framework and the warm-start strategy for iteration-wise CA distance control are likely to be adopted when analyzing other MOO methods (CAGrad, PCGrad, FairGrad, FAMO) under realistic smoothness conditions. Limitations include the high sample complexity for iteration-wise CA distance (O(ε⁻¹¹) deterministic, O(ε⁻¹⁷) stochastic), the lack of a convergence proof for stochastic MGDA-FA, and the fact that the fast approximation can introduce substantial errors in practice. An open question is whether the bounded-function-value-to-bounded-gradient-norm induction trick generalizes to more complex MOO formulations.
