## Problem

Existing RPM solvers, whether discriminative or generative, are limited to selecting an answer from a fixed choice list and cannot generalize to create entirely new puzzles from a set of abstract rules. Generative approaches in particular struggle with noisy and distractive puzzle attributes, lack high-level abstraction beyond image-level generation, and fail to scale to puzzles with large feasible solution spaces (e.g., PGM, VAD). This paper tackles the substantially harder task of modeling the entire RPM generation process, from abstract rules to complete visual puzzles.

## Input / Output

For puzzle solving, GenVP takes a 3×3 context matrix (64×64 RGB images with the bottom-right panel masked) and a choice list of 8 candidate images, and outputs the selected correct answer along with a predicted rule matrix R (K_R × N_R one-hot encoding over attributes and rule types). For puzzle generation, the input is solely the abstract rule matrix R, and the model produces a complete 3×3 RPM via ancestral sampling, or multiple valid solutions for a single missing panel by sampling different latent realizations. The latent space is explicitly decoupled into rule-relevant (Z_o, 54-dim) and rule-irrelevant/distractor (Z_o_bar, 10-dim) components at the image level, with a puzzle-level row representation Z_r (192-dim) bridging to the rule space.

## Method

GenVP is a hierarchical VAE structured as a graphical model with a simultaneous inference and generative pipeline. The inference path encodes images into latents Z, splits them into rule-relevant Z_o and distractor Z_o_bar, infers row-level representations Z_r, and predicts rules R through a Mixture of Experts (MoE) ensemble of four predictors that each see different subsets of the puzzle (all 9 images, 8 context images, 6 partial-row images, or 3 row representations), improving robustness to noise and shortcut learning. The generative path performs ancestral sampling from R → Z_r → Z_o → Z → X, enabling creation of complete puzzles from rules alone. Training combines a standard HVAE ELBO with a novel dual-level contrastive objective: a global masked contrastive loss that compares rule predictions across different puzzles (pushing apart rules that differ, pulling together shared rules via attribute-level masks), and a local masked contrastive loss that contrasts the valid puzzle against seven invalid completions formed by substituting negative candidates, making the model explicitly aware of rule violations. A warm-up phase maximizes ELBO alone before introducing the contrastive terms, and rule-invariant data augmentations (flips, rolling, grid shuffling) are applied during training.

## Results

On the RAVEN/I-RAVEN datasets, GenVP achieves 94.7%/96.1% average RPM solving accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA RAISE (90.0%/92.1%) by ~5 points. In out-of-distribution evaluation across 22 of 24 scenarios (unseen attribute values, rule exclusion, domain transfer, compositional generalization), GenVP consistently outperforms RAISE, with the largest gaps on size extrapolation (45.0% vs 28.5% on RAVEN) and PGM in-distribution (85.5% vs 22.4%). On the RAVEN-FAIR zero-shot benchmark, GenVP reaches 97.3% average accuracy, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenVP outperforms all four generative baselines across every configuration. Against GCA (Pekar et al., 2020), GenVP leads by ~62 points on RAVEN average accuracy (94.7% vs 32.7%), reflecting GCA's fundamental limitation in rule learning. Against ALANS (Zhang et al., 2022), GenVP leads by ~40 points (94.7% vs 54.3%), with ALANS struggling particularly on complex layouts. Against PRAE (Zhang et al., 2021), GenVP leads by ~15 points on average (94.7% vs 80.0%), and PRAE cannot even run on the 3×3 configuration due to out-of-memory issues. Against the closest competitor RAISE (Shi et al., 2024), GenVP leads by ~5 points in-distribution (94.7% vs 90.0%) but the gap widens dramatically in OOD settings: on PGM in-distribution, GenVP scores 85.5% vs RAISE's 22.4%, and on PGM compositional generalization (ARP), GenVP achieves 78.6% vs RAISE's 19.4%. GenVP also achieves perfect 100% on the CS-HELD-OUT rule-exclusion OOD test where RAISE reaches 97.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenVP extends the Hierarchical VAE framework (Doersch, 2016) to model the full RPM generation process, departing from prior generative solvers (GCA, PRAE, ALANS, RAISE) that only reconstruct the missing panel. Where RAISE uses dedicated rule-executor modules per rule type, GenVP employs a general generative decoder combined with a Mixture of Experts mechanism that aggregates rule predictions from multiple puzzle views (full, partial-row, context-minus-one, and row-level). The contrastive learning scheme builds on prior RPM contrastive work (CoPINet, DCNet, MLCL) but shifts the contrast from choice-list images to rule representations, introducing both cross-puzzle (global) and cross-candidate (local) masked losses that were absent in earlier formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GenVP is the first AVR model to generate complete RPM puzzles from an abstract rule matrix, providing a form of visual interpretability where model understanding can be verified by inspecting generated outputs. The critical role of contrastive learning (50.5% → 94.7% on RAVEN) highlights that ELBO alone is insufficient for learning discriminative rule representations, a finding that may generalize to other structured generative tasks. However, the model still struggles with complex grid configurations (O-IG, 2×2, 3×3) and arithmetic/progression rules on number and position attributes, and the generation coherence (~78%) leaves room for improvement. Open questions include whether the MoE+contrastive paradigm scales to more complex reasoning tasks beyond RPMs and how to improve numerical relationship comprehension.
