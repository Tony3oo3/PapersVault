## Problem

Aligning LLMs with human preferences requires large-scale human-annotated preference data, which is prohibitively expensive to acquire. Existing approaches to reduce this cost—LLM-as-judge via in-context learning and external reward models—suffer from distribution mismatch during iterative training and have not addressed the risk of labeling noise in self-generated preference data. The paper asks whether a small seed of human preference labels can be progressively 'spread' through iterative self-generated data to achieve alignment comparable to or exceeding full-dataset training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250735/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small seed preference dataset (e.g., 2K triplets of prompt/preferred/dispreferred responses, ~3.3% of UltraFeedback), an SFT LLM as the initial model, and disjoint prompt sets for each iteration (8K, 20K, 30K prompts in the main experiments). It produces a progressively aligned LLM after T iterations. Notably, the framework also functions without any seed preference data when the initial model is a sufficiently fine-tuned instruct model, using the base model as the reference. Self-generated responses are capped at 300 tokens to mitigate length bias.

## Method

SPA iterates two steps: (1) data expansion, where two responses are sampled per prompt from the current model and a preference label is assigned by directly computing the DPO reward (log-ratio of current model to the fixed SFT reference model) on the two responses, and (2) preference fine-tuning with a noise-aware loss. The key insight for preference judgment is using the training model's own logits with the original SFT model as reference, which avoids the distribution shift that degrades external reward models over iterations. For noise robustness, SPA introduces self-refinement: the K% least confident samples (by the training model's own preference probability) receive label-smoothed losses that down-weight the assigned label. To improve noise detection, a de-coupled mechanism approximates a more strongly aligned model's prediction via linear logit extrapolation h̃ = (1+λ)h_θ − λh_ref (motivated by the geometric mixture structure of RLHF-aligned models), using this extrapolated confidence to flag noisy labels without additional forward passes beyond standard DPO computation.

## Results

Using only 3.3% of UltraFeedback's ground-truth preference labels (2K out of 60K samples), SPA achieves a 21.13% win rate against GPT-4 on AlpacaEval 2.0 and a 15.39% length-controlled win rate, compared to 7.68% and 9.03% respectively for standard DPO on the same data. The MT-Bench score improves from 6.34 (SFT) to 6.94. Notably, SPA outperforms Zephyr-7b-β, which uses 100% of the UltraFeedback preference data, on both AlpacaEval 2.0 win rates (21.13% vs. 10.03% and 15.39% vs. 11.75%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPA outperforms standard DPO (same 3.3% seed data) by a wide margin, improving the AlpacaEval 2.0 win rate from 7.68% to 21.13% and the length-controlled win rate from 9.03% to 15.39%. Against Zephyr-7b-β, which trains on 100% of UltraFeedback's preference labels, SPA still wins on both AlpacaEval 2.0 metrics (21.13% vs. 10.03% win rate; 15.39% vs. 11.75% LC win rate), demonstrating that iterative self-generated preference data with direct logit-based judgment is more effective than simply scaling human annotations. Compared to Iterative DPO with the external PairRM reward model, SPA achieves a substantially higher win rate (21.13% vs. 9.46%) and LC win rate (15.39% vs. 11.87%), though it trails by 0.04 on MT-Bench (6.94 vs. 6.98). Against Iterative DPO with LLM-as-judge (in-context learning), SPA dominates across all three metrics (21.13% vs. 9.18% win rate, 15.39% vs. 9.28% LC win rate, 6.94 vs. 6.67 MT-Bench). The authors attribute the advantage over external reward models to reduced distribution shift, since SPA's intrinsic reward model is updated each iteration while PairRM remains fixed.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPA builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as its preference optimization backbone and on the iterative self-training paradigm (Snorkel 2024; Wu et al., 2024; Xiong et al., 2024) where a model generates its own training data across rounds. Its key departure is replacing external reward models (PairRM) or LLM-as-judge in-context learning with a *direct* preference judgment derived from the training model's own logits via the DPO reward formula (Eq. 4), combined with a de-coupled noise-detection mechanism borrowed from the noisy-label learning literature (Han et al., 2018; Li et al., 2020) and a logit-extrapolation trick from Liu et al. (2024). Conceptually, SPA reframes the preference-labeling step as an intrinsic property of the policy being trained rather than an external oracle, and treats the resulting labels as potentially noisy rather than ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for settings where human preference annotation is expensive or scarce: SPA achieves alignment competitive with or superior to models trained on 30× more labeled data (3.3% vs. 100% of UltraFeedback), and the method requires only a few lines of code beyond a standard DPO implementation. A key open question is whether the observed length-inflation tendency (acknowledged in Appendix D) can be fully mitigated without sacrificing the alignment gains, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to much larger models or to domains where the SFT model's intrinsic preference signal is weaker. The finding that a self-updating logit-based judge outperforms a fixed external reward model trained on ~250× more data also raises the broader question of how much external supervision is truly needed for preference judgment in iterative alignment loops.
