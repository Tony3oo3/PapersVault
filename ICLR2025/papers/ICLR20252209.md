## Problem

Existing latent-space fairness methods (e.g., Fair-Mixup, FAAP) either assume a manifold between demographic groups or project features toward a sensitive boundary without identifying where misclassification disparities actually concentrate. This paper introduces the concept of the 'unfair region'—a subspace of the latent space where misclassification rates for certain demographic groups are disproportionately high—and asks how to automatically detect and correct these regions without manual thresholding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained encoder's latent features (512-d for tabular, 768-d for ResNet-50/ViT, 384-d for BERT) along with binary sensitive attribute labels and class labels. The encoder is frozen throughout; only the linear last layer is re-initialized and fine-tuned. The output is a corrected binary classifier whose decision boundary has been adjusted to reduce the Equalized Odds gap (ΔEOd) and Demographic Parity gap (ΔDP) while preserving accuracy. For Logistic Regression (no encoder), perturbation is applied directly in input space as a special case.

## Method

ALFA operates in three stages: (1) balanced upsampling selects equal numbers of samples from all four A×Y subgroups; (2) an adversarial fairness attack maximizes the covariance between the sensitive attribute and the signed logit distance to the decision boundary (Zafar et al.'s fairness constraint) while minimizing the Sinkhorn distance between original and perturbed features to preserve semantic distribution, with perturbation magnitude bounded by the mean distance to the decision boundary; (3) the last layer is fine-tuned on the union of original inputs and perturbed latent features using standard cross-entropy loss. The counter-intuitive key insight is that deliberately pushing features into the unfair region (making predictions more biased) and then retraining on those samples effectively upweights the unfair region during gradient descent, shifting the decision boundary to equalize FPR and TPR gaps. A theoretical result (Theorem B.2) proves that the fairness attack objective L_fair is a lower bound of ΔEOd under a piecewise-linear logit approximation, guaranteeing that fine-tuning on the attacked samples reduces the fairness gap.

## Results

On the COMPAS dataset with Logistic Regression, ALFA reduces ΔEOd from 0.5319 (baseline) to 0.0876 while improving accuracy from 0.6578 to 0.6694. On the German Credit dataset, ALFA achieves the largest relative improvement, reducing ΔEOd from 0.3382 to 0.0469 (86% reduction) while also boosting accuracy from 0.7220 to 0.7940. Across all four tabular datasets with MLP backbones, ALFA achieves the best ΔEOd in every case (e.g., 0.0410 on COMPAS, 0.0640 on Drug), and on CelebA and Wikipedia it similarly attains the lowest ΔEOd across ResNet-50, ViT, Swin Transformer, BERT, and DistillBERT backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALFA achieves the best ΔEOd on three of four tabular datasets with Logistic Regression (Adult: 0.0616 vs. Convex-Concave-Loss 0.0687; German: 0.0469 vs. Convex-Concave-Loss 0.0979; Drug: 0.1170 vs. Convex-Concave-Loss 0.1429) and on all four with MLP backbones. On COMPAS (LR), Covariance-Loss (Zafar et al., 2017) slightly edges out ALFA on ΔEOd (0.0671 vs. 0.0876), but ALFA recovers higher accuracy (0.6694 vs. 0.6565). FAAP and Fair-CDA consistently underperform, with ΔEOd values near or above the unregularized baseline (e.g., FAAP: 0.5184 vs. baseline 0.5319 on COMPAS LR), indicating that projecting features toward a sensitive hyperplane or disentangling sensitive/non-sensitive components does not effectively correct the decision boundary. Fair-Mixup and FDR are competitive but generally trail ALFA by 5–20% in ΔEOd across datasets. TabFairGAN and Influence-Reweight show moderate improvements but are outperformed by ALFA on both fairness and accuracy in most settings. The paper reports that ALFA ranks first or second in ΔEOd across all 19 experimental configurations (4 tabular × 3 backbones + CelebA × 3 backbones × 3 attributes + Wiki × 3 backbones), a consistency no other baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALFA repurposes the covariance-based fairness constraint from Zafar et al. (2017) in an inverted role: rather than minimizing it as a regularizer during training, it maximizes it as an adversarial attack objective to deliberately generate maximally biased latent features. The Sinkhorn distance from Genevay et al. (2018) serves as the distributional constraint preserving semantic integrity of perturbations. Conceptually, ALFA departs from prior latent-space fairness methods (Fair-Mixup's manifold interpolation, FAAP's projection toward the sensitive hyperplane, FDR's balanced fine-tuning) by explicitly identifying and targeting 'unfair regions'—subspaces where misclassification disparities are concentrated—rather than applying uniform transformations across the latent distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counter-intuitive strategy of maximizing bias to then correct it provides a theoretically grounded (Theorem 3.2) and modality-agnostic framework for fairness that consistently outperforms or matches strong baselines across tabular, image, and text data without significant accuracy loss. The 'unfair region' concept offers a useful diagnostic lens for understanding *where* in latent space bias manifests, which prior methods largely overlooked. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to multi-class, multi-label, and multi-sensitive-attribute settings (only briefly addressed in appendices), whether the piecewise-linear logit approximation in the theoretical bounds holds in practice for very confident predictions, and whether the method generalizes to non-linear final layers or generative models.
