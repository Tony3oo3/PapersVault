## Problem

Online sequence learning for decision-making in non-stationary, partially observable environments remains poorly addressed: gradient-based temporal memories (LSTM, RWKV) are unstable and require batch data, while probabilistic models (HMM, CSCG) cannot adapt online without retraining from scratch. There is little work on temporal memory algorithms that operate fully online with only one observation at a time, use local learning rules, and form successor features for planning in POMDPs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250374/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives either a 64×64 RGB first-person image (AnimalAI) or a categorical cell-color observation (Gridworld), plus the previously taken action. Images are encoded into categorical variables via a pre-trained K-Means quantizer (50 states) or a Categorical VAE (5 variables × 50 states); Gridworld observations are already categorical. The output is a discrete action (3-way in AnimalAI, 4-way in Gridworld) selected via softmax over Q-values computed from successor features, which are discounted cumulative predictions of future feature-state distributions formed by the memory model.

## Method

DHTM divides the hidden state space into per-feature subspaces (analogous to Factorial HMM) and represents each transition factor as a collection of dendritic segments on artificial neurons, where each segment detects a specific combination of presynaptic cell states. Belief propagation is performed via a modified sum-product algorithm that accounts for interdependence of presynaptic cells through learned synapse-specificity weights, and the emission factor uses a columnar structure (inspired by neocortical minicolumns) to enforce sparsity. Learning is entirely local and Hebbian: segment excitation values and synapse weights are updated by one-step Monte Carlo coincidence rules (exponential moving averages), requiring no backpropagation or complete sequences. The agent forms successor features by rolling out the memory model under a uniform policy up to a dynamically determined horizon, then combines SFs with per-feature-state rewards to compute Q-values for action selection. This episodic, distributed, locally-trained architecture enables fast online adaptation to environmental changes without reinitialization, outperforming LSTM, RWKV, and CSCG in both Gridworld and AnimalAI navigation tasks.

## Results

In the AnimalAI 3D navigation task, DHTM converges to a stable goal-reaching policy in approximately 150 episodes (~3.5×10³ action steps), making it roughly 10× more sample-efficient than Dreamer V3 (18M parameters), which requires ~35×10³ steps (1000 episodes). In the non-stationary Gridworld task (optimal path blocked at episode 300), DHTM adapts online without reinitialisation, while CSCG, LSTM, and RWKV all fail to recover in the online regime. DHTM with distributed Categorical VAE encoding (factor size 4) converges to a slightly shorter path than the single-variable K-Means encoding, demonstrating the benefit of handling statistical dependencies between feature variables.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DHTM clearly outperforms CSCG (George et al., 2021) in both environments: in Gridworld, CSCG cannot adapt to the blocked path without full retraining (oracle mode), while DHTM adapts online; in AnimalAI, CSCG improves slowly but never converges stably within 1000 episodes. LSTM and RWKV (Peng et al., 2023) perform poorly in the non-stationary Gridworld phase 2 even with oracle reinitialisation, and show no measurable learning in AnimalAI on the 1000-episode timescale, suggesting their predictive horizon is too short for this task. The Episodic Control (EC) dictionary baseline (Pritzel et al., 2017) performs on par with DHTM in Gridworld, confirming that DHTM's core mechanism resembles a probabilistic trajectory buffer; however, DHTM additionally supports distributed representations and handles statistical interdependencies between feature variables, which EC cannot. Against Dreamer V3 (Hafner et al., 2023), DHTM achieves comparable task performance with roughly 10× fewer action steps (3.5×10³ vs. 35×10³), highlighting its sample efficiency advantage in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

DHTM synthesizes three distinct lineages: the Factorial HMM's decomposition of hidden state into independent subspaces, the HTM neuron model's dendritic-segment architecture for sequence memory, and the factor graph / sum-product framework for probabilistic inference. It departs from gradient-based temporal memories (LSTM, RWKV) by replacing backpropagation with local Monte-Carlo Hebbian updates, and from batch-trained HMM variants like CSCG by operating fully online with one-step updates. The paper positions DHTM as a probabilistic reinterpretation of HTM that enables belief propagation, and as a distributed-representation upgrade over table-based Episodic Control, which it shows performs comparably in simple settings but cannot handle multi-variable encodings or statistical dependencies between features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical claim is that a purely local, Hebbian episodic memory can match or exceed gradient-based sequence models in non-stationary POMDPs while being an order of magnitude more sample-efficient than Dreamer V3, and that it is implementable on neuromorphic hardware. However, validation is confined to small Gridworld mazes and a single 10×10 m AnimalAI room, and the finding that a simple EC dictionary matches DHTM in the Gridworld task tempers the architectural novelty. Open questions include automatic context-factor-size discovery, segment pruning for scalability, and whether the approach transfers to richer, higher-dimensional environments or combines productively with slower generalizing world models.
