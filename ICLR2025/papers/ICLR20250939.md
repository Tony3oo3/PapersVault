## Problem

RLHF-based alignment of LLMs depends on explicit human feedback that is expensive to collect, suffers from annotator inconsistency and bias, and is difficult to scale. While AI-generated feedback (RLAIF) improves scalability, it remains unclear what additional signals could better capture human preferences. No prior work has integrated implicit cognitive signals—specifically eye-tracking data—into the Reward Model to augment preference prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250939/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The RM receives a prompt-response pair formatted in a chat protocol (e.g., Llama 3's header/termination tokens) as text, augmented with per-token synthetic eye-tracking features (1–5 features depending on the ET prediction model) generated by a frozen ET model on the same text. Because the ET prediction models use different tokenizers than the RM base models, features must be remapped from the ET tokenizer's token space to the RM tokenizer's token space before integration. The output is a scalar reward score per response, with the Bradley-Terry loss trained on preferred-vs-rejected pairs.

## Method

GazeReward augments a standard transformer-based RM with synthetic eye-tracking features generated by two frozen ET prediction models: a T5+BiLSTM+MLP model producing per-token total reading time (TRT), and a RoBERTa-based model producing five features (FFD, fixProp, GPT, TRT, nFix). Two integration strategies are explored: GazeConcat, which appends the ET embedding (flanked by special ⟨eye⟩/⟨/eye⟩ tokens) to the text embedding sequence, and GazeAdd, which performs element-wise addition of the projected ET embedding to the text embedding. An MLP projector maps the low-dimensional ET features to the model's embedding dimension, and a word-level tokenizer alignment procedure handles the mismatch between ET and RM tokenizers. The RM (Llama 3 8B, Llama 3 8B-Instruct, or Mistral 7B) is fine-tuned with QLoRA on OASST1 and HelpSteer2 preference datasets, with the ET modules kept frozen throughout training.

## Results

On the HelpSteer2 dataset, GazeReward achieves up to 17.0% absolute accuracy improvement over the baseline RM (62.4% vs. 53.3% for Llama-3-8B with GazeAdd and fcomb1), and up to 13.9% for Llama-3-8B-Instruct (62.3% vs. 54.7%). On OASST1, improvements range from 4.4% to 9.2%, with the best result being 71.5% accuracy (vs. 65.5% baseline) for Llama-3-8B with GazeConcat and fcomb2.5. On RewardBench, the Mistral-7B model shows the largest relative gain, improving from 41.2% to 60.5% accuracy (46.9% relative improvement) with GazeConcat and fcomb2.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the standard RM (text-only input) and GazeReward (text + synthetic ET features), using identical base models and training data. GazeReward consistently outperforms the baseline across all three base models (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B) and both datasets, with no configuration showing a statistically significant degradation. The largest absolute gains appear on HelpSteer2 (up to 17.0% for Llama-3-8B with GazeAdd/fcomb1), while OASST1 shows more modest but still significant improvements (up to 9.2%). Among integration methods, GazeConcat provides more consistent gains across models, whereas GazeAdd yields the single largest improvement on HelpSteer2. Among ET feature combinations, fcomb1 (single TRT feature from the T5+BiLSTM model) and fcomb2.2 (TRT+FFD from the RoBERTa model) generally outperform the full 5-feature set fcomb2.5, suggesting that reading-time features are the most informative signal for preference prediction. No external reward modeling methods (e.g., DPO, RLAIF-based RMs) are directly compared; the evaluation is strictly an ablation of ET feature inclusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard RLHF reward modeling pipeline (Ouyang et al., 2024) by augmenting the RM input with synthetic eye-tracking features generated by two frozen ET prediction models (Huang & Hollenstein, 2023; Li & Rudzicz, 2021), drawing an analogy to RLAIF where feedback is artificially generated rather than collected from humans. The closest prior work, Kiegeland et al. (2024), used ET signals for controlled sentiment generation to create DPO training data, but was task-specific and did not directly modify the RM architecture. GazeReward departs from this by treating ET features as a general-purpose implicit feedback signal injected into the RM via two integration strategies (GazeConcat with special separator tokens, and GazeAdd via element-wise addition), making it applicable to any preference dataset without task-specific engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a frozen 17.5M-parameter ET predictor can consistently improve RM accuracy by 10%+ across multiple base models and datasets, offering a scalable, hardware-free path to incorporating cognitive signals into LLM alignment. However, several limitations temper the impact: the ET prediction models were trained on small English-only corpora (ZuCo, GECO, PROVO, Dundee), the experiments are restricted to 7–8B models with QLoRA, and the method has not yet been validated within a full RLHF or DPO training loop. Open questions include whether the gains persist at larger scale, whether real (rather than predicted) ET data would yield further improvements, and how ET features might interact with attention mechanisms or process-based reward models.
