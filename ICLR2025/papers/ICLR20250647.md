## Problem

Existing energy-guided generative models (e.g., Contrastive Energy Prediction) require auxiliary neural networks to estimate an intermediate energy function E_t(x) and its gradient, introducing additional training complexity and approximation error. Moreover, no method existed to inject exact energy guidance into general flow matching models, where the guided velocity field is highly non-trivial to derive. This paper asks: can we directly learn an energy-guided flow or diffusion model without any auxiliary model, and can we do so exactly?

## Input / Output

The method consumes data samples x_0 paired with their scalar energy values E(x_0) (or, in the RL setting, state-action pairs (x,a) with Q-function values Q(x,a)) plus Gaussian noise for the forward noising process. The energy weight is computed as a batch-level softmax of -βE(x_0), making it a relative reweighting that requires no normalization constant. The output is a single neural network (score function s_t^θ or velocity field v_t^θ) that, when integrated along the reverse ODE/SDE, generates samples from the energy-guided distribution q(x) ∝ p(x)exp(-βE(x)); in offline RL this yields a policy π(a|x) ∝ μ(a|x)exp(βQ(x,a)) that strictly preserves the KL-regularized form.

## Method

The core contribution is showing that weighting the standard conditional flow matching (or score matching) loss by exp(-βE(x_0))/E[exp(-βE(x_0))] is provably equivalent to matching the true energy-guided velocity field ū_t(x) (Theorem 4.3), eliminating the need to estimate the intermediate energy E_t(x) or its gradient. The energy-guided flow has a closed-form expression (Theorem 4.1) as an energy-weighted average of conditional velocity fields, valid for any conditional flow including optimal transport, rectified flow, or Gaussian diffusion paths. For offline RL, the Q-function serves as the negative energy, and the proposed QIPO algorithm iteratively refines the policy by periodically resampling a support action set from the current score model, which implicitly increases the effective guidance scale while strictly maintaining the form π_l(a|x) ∝ μ(a|x)exp(l·βQ(a|x)) (Eq. 5.4). Unlike QGPO, QIPO requires no back-propagation through the Q-function and no auxiliary network, yielding a 25–64% reduction in action-generation time while achieving competitive or superior D4RL scores.

## Results

On the D4RL locomotion benchmarks (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d across medium-expert, medium, and medium-replay), QIPO-Diff achieves an average normalized score of 86.2 and QIPO-OT achieves 86.3, competitive with the strongest baselines SRPO (87.1) and QGPO (86.6). On AntMaze tasks, QIPO-Diff reaches 77.3 average, close to IDQL (79.1) and QGPO (78.3), while QIPO-OT trails at 71.96. A key practical advantage is inference speed: QIPO-OT generates actions in 27.26 ms (63.7% faster than QGPO's 75.05 ms) and QIPO-Diff in 55.86 ms (25.6% faster), since neither requires back-propagation through an auxiliary energy network.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QGPO (Lu et al., 2023), QIPO-Diff wins on Hopper-Medium-Expert (112.12 vs 108.0), Hopper-Medium-Replay (101.23 vs 96.9), Walker2d-Medium-Replay (90.08 vs 84.4), and AntMaze-large-Play (73.25 vs 66.6), while QGPO retains an edge on Hopper-Medium (98.0 vs 89.53) and AntMaze-large-Diverse (64.8 vs 40.5); the locomotion averages are nearly tied (86.2 vs 86.6). Against Guided Flows (Zheng et al., 2023), both QIPO variants dominate across all locomotion tasks (e.g., 94.14 vs 97 on HalfCheetah-Medium-Expert is the only loss; 101.23 vs 89 on Hopper-Medium-Replay is a 12-point gain), confirming the paper's claim that exact energy guidance outperforms classifier-free guidance for flow matching. Against IDQL and SRPO, QIPO-Diff is competitive on locomotion (86.2 vs 82.1/87.1) and wins on AntMaze-large-Play (73.25 vs 63.5/53.6) but trails on AntMaze-umaze-Diverse (73.88 vs 80.2/82.1). The classifier-free guidance and classifier-guidance baselines (Ho & Salimans 2021; Dhariwal & Nichol 2021) are compared qualitatively in Figure 1, where energy-weighted diffusion matches the ground-truth distribution at β>1 while CFG deviates, and quantitatively in Table 1 where only energy-weighted diffusion achieves both exact guidance and no auxiliary model. The most distinctive practical advantage over QGPO is inference speed: QIPO-OT is 63.7% faster (27.26 ms vs 75.05 ms) and QIPO-Diff is 25.6% faster (55.86 ms), because neither requires back-propagation through an intermediate energy network at sampling time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Conditional Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and score-based diffusion models (Song et al., 2020) by showing that energy guidance can be injected purely through a reweighting of the training loss, eliminating the auxiliary intermediate-energy networks required by Contrastive Energy Prediction (Lu et al., 2023) and force-field guidance (Wang et al., 2024). It also generalizes the classifier-free guidance setting (Ho & Salimans, 2021) by proving that CFG is inexact for guidance scales β≠1, whereas the proposed energy-weighted loss recovers the exact guided distribution for any β. In the offline RL context, QIPO builds on the KL-regularized policy optimization formulation shared with QGPO and Guided Flows (Zheng et al., 2023), but replaces their auxiliary-model-based or classifier-free guidance with the direct energy-weighted objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of auxiliary models simplifies the training pipeline and yields a 63% speedup in action generation (QIPO-OT vs. QGPO), making energy-guided generative policies more practical for deployment. The energy-weighted loss is general—it applies to any conditional flow (optimal transport, rectified flow, Gaussian path)—so the technique could transfer to molecular generation, image synthesis, or other energy-guided tasks beyond RL. However, the method remains restricted to offline settings, and the empirical gains over strong baselines are modest and inconsistent (e.g., QIPO-Diff underperforms IDQL on AntMaze-large), leaving open whether the iterative policy refinement and exact-guidance advantages translate to larger-scale or online RL problems.
