## Problem

Neural operators are universal approximators for continuous operators, but general operator approximation suffers from the curse of parametric complexity, where model size grows exponentially with desired accuracy. While quantitative approximation theorems exist for specific PDEs (Darcy, Navier-Stokes, Hamilton-Jacobi) with tailored architectures, no general quantitative result was available for nonlinear parabolic PDEs within a flexible neural operator framework. This paper fills that gap by proving a quantitative approximation theorem showing that depth and width grow only polylogarithmically in the target accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial data function u₀ ∈ L^∞(D) on a bounded domain D ⊂ ℝ^d and produces the solution u(t,x) of the nonlinear parabolic PDE ∂_t u + Lu = F(u) over a short time interval [0,T], measured in the mixed Lebesgue norm L^r(0,T;L^s(D)). The parameters r,s must satisfy ν/s + 1/r < 1/(p−1) where p is the growth exponent of the nonlinearity F, and T depends on the L^∞-bound R of the initial data. Long-time solutions are obtained by iteratively re-applying the operator, with error accumulation controlled by a separate term.

## Method

The key insight is to identify each forward pass of a neural operator layer with one step of Picard's iteration for the integral (Duhamel) form of the PDE. The Green function G of the linear equation ∂_t u + Lu = 0 is expanded in a product basis {φ_n}×{ψ_m} and truncated to rank N, yielding finite-rank non-local operators K_N^(ℓ) that implement the integral kernel in each layer. The nonlinearity F: ℝ→ℝ is approximated by a 1D ReLU network F_net, so each layer computes (W + K_N) ∘ F_net, mimicking the contraction mapping Φ. The depth L equals the number of Picard iterations needed (≈ log(ε⁻¹)), while the width H comes from the 1D approximation of F (≈ ε⁻¹ log(ε⁻¹)), giving the final bounds L ≤ C(log ε⁻¹)² and H ≤ Cε⁻¹(log ε⁻¹)². The architecture is weight-tied across layers (related to deep equilibrium models), and the choice of basis (Fourier, Haar wavelet, etc.) determines the rank N and connects to FNO or WNO architectures.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) establishes that for any target accuracy ε ∈ (0,1), there exists a neural operator Γ with depth L(Γ) ≤ C_L (log(ε⁻¹))² and number of neurons H(Γ) ≤ C_H ε⁻¹ (log(ε⁻¹))² that approximates the solution operator of nonlinear parabolic PDEs to within ε in L^r(0,T;L^s(D)) norm. Crucially, neither the depth nor the number of neurons grows exponentially in ε⁻¹, thereby avoiding the curse of parametric complexity that plagues general operator approximation. The rank N of the Green function expansion must satisfy E_G(N) ≤ C_G ε and E_G′(N) ≤ C_G ε, with concrete rates depending on the chosen basis (e.g., O(N^{−σ+d/2}) for Fourier bases on H^σ(T^d)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, so there are no direct numerical comparisons against other neural operator architectures (FNO, WNO, DeepONet, PCA-Net, etc.). The theoretical contribution is positioned against prior quantitative approximation results: unlike general operator learning (Lanthaler & Stuart, 2023) which suffers exponential parameter growth, and unlike prior PDE-specific results (Kovachki et al., 2021; Lanthaler, 2023; Chen et al., 2023) that are tied to specific architectures, this work provides architecture-agnostic bounds within a general neural operator framework. The key distinction is the use of Picard's iteration structure to achieve logarithmic depth scaling, whereas prior DeepONet-based results (Lanthaler et al., 2022; Deng et al., 2022) and FNO-based results (Kovachki et al., 2021) require architecture-specific analyses. The paper also connects to deep equilibrium models (Bai et al., 2019) and weight-tied neural operators (Marwah et al., 2023) by showing the constructed operator naturally has a weight-tied structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of quantitative approximation theorems for PDE solution operators (established for Darcy, Navier-Stokes, and Hamilton-Jacobi equations by Kovachki et al., Lanthaler, and Lanthaler & Stuart) to a broad class of nonlinear parabolic PDEs, using a fundamentally different proof strategy. The key departure is aligning each neural operator layer with a step of Picard's iteration via Duhamel's principle, rather than relying on universal approximation or problem-specific spectral analysis. The finite-rank integral operator structure K_N^(l) is inherited from the nonlocal neural operator framework of Lanthaler et al. (2023), while the 1D ReLU approximation of the nonlinearity draws on Gühring et al. (2020). The resulting weight-tied architecture also connects conceptually to deep equilibrium models (Bai et al., 2019) and recent weight-tied neural operators for steady-state PDEs (Marwah et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first general quantitative approximation theorem for nonlinear parabolic PDEs that avoids the curse of parametric complexity, with depth growing only as (log ε^{-1})² and width as ε^{-1}(log ε^{-1})². The structural insight—that Picard's iteration naturally maps to forward propagation—offers a principled justification for why neural operators are well-suited to PDE solving and suggests a generalizable framework for any PDE solvable by fixed-point iteration (Navier-Stokes, nonlinear dispersive, hyperbolic equations). Key open questions include: extending to Sobolev space frameworks, handling unbounded domains, establishing concrete rank-N rates for specific Green functions, and verifying that the constructed approximators preserve qualitative PDE properties (maximum principle, comparison principle). The paper is purely theoretical with no experiments, so practical validation of the weight-tied architecture remains future work.
