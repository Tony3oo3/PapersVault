## Problem

A straightforward combination of FedAvg and LoRA for federated fine-tuning of LLMs fails to capture global knowledge under data heterogeneity, suffering from both an intrinsic limitation (updates confined to a rank-r subspace) and an extrinsic one (client drift exacerbated by LoRA's locally quadratic parameterization). Existing heterogeneous FL methods like FedProx and SCAFFOLD do not transfer well to LoRA-based FL due to fundamentally different update patterns between full fine-tuning and low-rank adaptation, leaving data heterogeneity in LoRA-based FL largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (RoBERTa-base for NLU, LLaMA-2-7B for NLG) and private text datasets distributed across K clients, with only the low-rank matrices B (d₁×r) and A (r×d₂) communicated per round rather than full model weights. A one-time SVD of the pre-trained weight W⁰ is computed at initialization to derive the principal singular vectors and values used for LoRA initialization. The output is the full fine-tuned weight matrix W̃ = Ŵ^T + B_G⁰A_G⁰, where Ŵ^T accumulates residual updates over T communication rounds.

## Method

FRLoRA introduces two coupled mechanisms to overcome the rank constraint and client drift. First, during the global update step, it computes a residual ΔW^t = B_G^t A_G^t − B_G⁰A_G⁰ and adds it directly to the global model weights (Ŵ^t = Ŵ^{t−1} + ΔW^t), then reinitializes all local low-rank matrices back to B_G⁰ and A_G⁰ for the next round; this residual accumulation allows the effective update rank to grow as O(r(T+1)) across rounds, enabling representation in a higher-rank parameter space analogous to full fine-tuning. Second, B and A are initialized (and reinitialized each round) using the principal singular values and vectors of the pre-trained weight via SVD (following PiSSA), ensuring all clients train in the same principal singular space, which calibrates inconsistent convergence rates and mitigates client drift. The synchronization of Ŵ across all clients keeps local and global models consistent, and the final fine-tuned weight is recovered by adding back the initial low-rank product.

## Results

On NLU benchmarks (non-IID, β=0.5, 5 clients), FRLoRA achieves 75.81% on RTE, 64.80 on COLA, 69.41% on 20NG, and 91.10% on QNLI, outperforming FedAvg (70.75/63.96/66.83/90.37) by up to 5.06 points. On NLG tasks, FRLoRA improves the MetaMathQA average from 19.71% (FedAvg) to 24.74%, and the Alpaca-GPT4 average from 4.486 to 4.733. On realistic heterogeneous benchmarks, FRLoRA achieves the best average scores: 5.56 on Fed-Aya, 5.80 on Fed-ChatbotIT, and 6.63 on Fed-WildChat (vs. 4.90, 5.51, and 6.22 for FedAvg respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRLoRA consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark. Against the standard FedAvg+LoRA baseline, FRLoRA gains +5.06 points on RTE, +2.58 on 20NG, and +5.03 on MetaMathQA average, confirming that the residual accumulation mechanism effectively expands the parameter space beyond rank r. Heterogeneous FL methods (FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedAvgM, FedAdagrad, FedYogi, FedAdam) all underperform FedAvg when combined with LoRA, validating the paper's claim that FFT-oriented corrections are incompatible with LoRA's update pattern. Among LoRA-specific baselines, FFA-LoRA (which freezes matrix A) performs worse than FedAvg on five of nine benchmarks, while FlexLoRA is competitive on QNLI (90.03 vs. 91.10) but trails on all other tasks. The largest margins appear on RTE (+5.06 over FedAvg, +7.12 over SCAFFOLD) and MetaMathQA GSM8K (+9.32 over FedAvg), suggesting FRLoRA's advantage is most pronounced when data heterogeneity and task complexity are high.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRLoRA sits at the intersection of three research threads: LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2021), FedAvg-style federated aggregation (McMahan et al., 2017), and SVD-based initialization (PiSSA, Meng et al., 2024). Its key departure from prior LoRA-based FL work is the residual accumulation mechanism: rather than averaging low-rank matrices across rounds (which confines updates to a rank-r subspace), FRLoRA adds each round's low-rank product residual directly into the full model weights, effectively expanding the update rank over time. This design also distinguishes it from FFT-based heterogeneous FL methods (FedProx, SCAFFOLD) that the paper shows fail when naively combined with LoRA, and from FFA-LoRA, which freezes one LoRA matrix and thereby sacrifices learning capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap: prior empirical studies showed that standard heterogeneous FL algorithms degrade LoRA-based FL, but no principled solution existed. FRLoRA's residual accumulation plus per-round PiSSA reinitialization offers a lightweight, drop-in fix that consistently improves across nine benchmarks and multiple LLM architectures, while keeping communication overhead near that of vanilla LoRA. Open questions include how the method behaves under model heterogeneity (different architectures across clients), whether the SVD initialization cost becomes prohibitive for trillion-parameter models, and whether the rank-growth argument holds under strong non-convexity or very few communication rounds.
