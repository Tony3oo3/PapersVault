## Problem

Standard Transformers leave the norms of embedding vectors, attention/MLP matrix rows, and hidden states unconstrained, causing inaccurate cosine-similarity estimation, ill-conditioned attention matrices that degenerate toward low rank, and slow convergence. While individual normalization layers (RMSNorm, LayerNorm) and weight decay have been applied piecemeal, no prior architecture enforces a unified hyperspherical constraint on every vector component simultaneously, leaving the representation geometry uncontrolled across layers.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a causal text token sequence (LLaMA-2 tokenizer, 32k vocabulary) and produces next-token logits via a dot product between the final hidden state and the output embedding matrix. Because all embeddings are unit-norm, raw logits are bounded in [-1,1], so a per-vocabulary trainable scaling vector s_z is applied element-wise before softmax to recover adjustable temperature. The hidden state is retracted to unit norm after every attention and MLP sub-layer, so it always resides on the d_model-dimensional hypersphere rather than growing in norm as in standard GPT.

## Method

nGPT normalizes every vector forming the embedding dimensions of all network matrices (E_input, E_output, W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o, W_u, W_ν, W_oMLP) to unit norm after each training step, turning matrix-vector products into bounded cosine similarities and eliminating the need for weight decay and RMSNorm/LayerNorm. Hidden-state updates replace the standard residual addition with a LERP (approximating SLERP) toward the normalized block output, weighted by per-dimension learnable eigen learning rates α_A and α_M that act as the diagonal of a variable-metric matrix, decoupling each block's predictive direction from its magnitude of influence. Query and key vectors are additionally normalized with trainable per-head scaling s_qk to keep their dot products bounded despite RoPE distortion, and MLP intermediate states are rescaled by √d_model to preserve SiLU non-linearity. The final Norm() after each sub-layer serves as a Riemannian retraction, projecting the updated hidden state back onto the hypersphere. This unified spherical constraint yields 4×–20× fewer training steps to reach the same validation loss depending on context length, and improves attention-matrix condition numbers relative to GPT.

## Results

nGPT reduces the number of training tokens required to reach a target validation loss by a factor of 4×, 10×, and 20× at context lengths of 1k, 4k, and 8k tokens, respectively, compared to the baseline GPT. For the 1B-parameter model at 4k context, nGPT reaches the same validation loss as GPT after 20k iterations (~40B tokens) versus 200k iterations (~400B tokens) for GPT. On downstream tasks (average accuracy across five benchmarks), nGPT consistently outperforms GPT at matched compute budgets, with the gap widening at longer context lengths. nGPT also maintains stable perplexity on PG19 when extrapolated beyond its training length, whereas GPT's perplexity degrades sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard GPT (Transformer with RMSNorm, weight decay, and unconstrained parameter norms), trained under identical data, batch size, and compute budgets. nGPT wins decisively on training efficiency: at 4k context length with 1B parameters, it requires only 1/10th the tokens to match GPT's validation loss, and at 8k context the speedup reaches 20×. On downstream task accuracy (LAMBADA, PIQA, WinoGrande, ARC-E, HellaSwag), nGPT achieves higher average accuracy than GPT at every compute budget tested, with the advantage growing with context length. nGPT also exhibits substantially lower condition numbers in its attention matrices (avoiding the near-rank-deficiency observed in GPT) and superior length extrapolation on PG19, where GPT's perplexity spikes beyond its training length while nGPT remains stable. The trade-off is a ~60–80% higher per-step wall-clock cost for nGPT due to additional normalization operations, though the authors note this gap is expected to shrink at larger model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

nGPT unifies several previously separate threads—hyperspherical representation learning (Wang & Isola, 2020), QK normalization (Henry et al., 2020), weight-norm control as an alternative to weight decay (Loshchilov, 2023; Franke et al., 2023), and the interpretation of Transformers as implicit meta-optimizers (Von Oswald et al., 2023; Dai et al., 2022)—under a single architectural principle: every vector in the network (embeddings, projection matrices, hidden states, queries, keys) is constrained to the unit hypersphere. The hidden-state update is derived from SLERP (Shoemake, 1985) and approximated by LERP, with per-dimension 'eigen learning rates' playing the role of a diagonal variable-metric matrix in a Riemannian optimization step. This departs from prior normalization-layer approaches (RMSNorm, LayerNorm, NormFormer, ReZero) by normalizing the parameters themselves rather than the activations, and by removing weight decay entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4–20× reduction in training steps (even after accounting for ~80% per-step overhead) is a substantial efficiency gain if it holds at scale, and the spherical representation makes the network's internal geometry directly interpretable (condition numbers, pairwise dot products, eigen learning rate magnitudes). However, all experiments are limited to 0.5B–1B parameters on OpenWebText, and the per-step cost penalty from additional normalizations has not yet been amortized at larger scale. Open questions include whether the speedup persists for 7B+ models on high-quality corpora, whether the eigen learning rates and scaling factors transfer across tasks, and whether the Riemannian-optimizer interpretation can be leveraged to design new update rules or convergence guarantees.
