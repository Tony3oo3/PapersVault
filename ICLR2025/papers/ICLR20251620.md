## Problem

Existing causal inference-based root cause analysis methods for multivariate time series either assume causal structure as prior knowledge or rely on causal discovery algorithms that do not explicitly model exogenous variables. This makes them unable to pinpoint not only which time series is the root cause but also at which specific time steps the anomalous intervention occurred, limiting their diagnostic precision in complex systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251620/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is trained exclusively on normal multivariate time series (d-dimensional vectors per time step) and deployed in a streaming fashion, processing one new time step at a time. Beyond identifying the root cause time series, AERCA outputs the specific time steps receiving exogenous interventions, a summarized Granger causal adjacency matrix, per-step exogenous variable estimates, and z-score-based root cause scores thresholded via streaming peaks-over-threshold (SPOT). The window length K controls how many past lags are considered for both causal discovery and reconstruction.

## Method

AERCA is an encoder-decoder autoencoder that jointly performs Granger causal discovery and root cause localization. The encoder simulates abductive reasoning by computing exogenous variables as u_t = x_t − Σ_k ω_{θ_k}(x_{t−k})x_{t−k}, where K neural networks parameterize the Granger causal coefficients at each lag; the decoder simulates deductive reasoning by reconstructing x_t from exogenous variables and observed series from a prior window, as justified by Proposition 1 which proves only K steps of each are needed rather than all history. A KL divergence constraint forces the derived exogenous variables to follow an isotropic standard Gaussian, enforcing mutual independence across series. The training objective combines reconstruction loss, the KL independence term, L1/L2 sparsity penalties on coefficient matrices, and temporal smoothness penalties. At deployment, z-scores of the encoder-derived exogenous variables relative to their normal-data statistics serve as root cause scores, with SPOT providing a dynamic threshold for flagging anomalous interventions at specific (series, time step) pairs.

## Results

On the four synthetic datasets, AERCA achieves near-perfect or perfect root cause identification: 1.000 AC@1 through AC@10 on Linear, Nonlinear, and Lotka-Volterra, and 0.996–0.997 AC@1–AC@10 (0.990 Avg@10) on Lorenz96, while the best baseline (CIRCA) reaches only 0.360 AC@1 on Lorenz96 and 0.120 on Lotka-Volterra. On the real-world SWaT dataset, AERCA attains 0.220 AC@1 and 0.342 Avg@10, substantially outperforming ε-Diagnosis (0.075 AC@1, 0.180 Avg@10) and RCD/CIRCA (0.000 AC@1, 0.100 Avg@10). On MSDS, AERCA achieves 0.896 Avg@10, surpassing RCD (0.821) and CIRCA (0.809). For causal discovery, AERCA achieves perfect F1/AUC-PR/AUC-ROC (1.000) and zero Hamming distance on the Linear dataset, and consistently leads on all nonlinear datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AERCA dominates all three root-cause baselines (ε-Diagnosis, RCD, CIRCA) on every dataset. The gap is most dramatic on high-dimensional synthetic data: on Lotka-Volterra (40 variables), AERCA scores 1.000 AC@1 while all baselines cluster around 0.10–0.12; on Lorenz96 (20 variables), AERCA reaches 0.996 AC@1 versus 0.100–0.360 for baselines. On the real-world SWaT dataset (51 variables), where all methods degrade due to hidden confounders and instantaneous effects, AERCA still leads with 0.220 AC@1 and 0.342 Avg@10, compared to 0.075/0.180 for ε-Diagnosis and 0.000/0.100 for RCD and CIRCA. On MSDS, AERCA achieves the best Avg@10 (0.896) over RCD (0.821) and CIRCA (0.809), though it trails CIRCA slightly on AC@1 (0.381 vs. 0.454). For causal discovery, AERCA outperforms all nine baselines (VAR, cMLP, cLSTM, TCDF, eSRU, PCMCI, PCMCI+, GVAR, CUTS) on the nonlinear datasets, achieving perfect scores on Linear and consistently the lowest Hamming distance on Nonlinear, Lotka-Volterra, and Lorenz96.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two-step paradigm in causal inference-based root cause analysis (e.g., RCD, CIRCA) that first learns a causal graph and then localizes anomalies, instead proposing a unified autoencoder framework where Granger causal discovery and root cause identification are inherently coupled. It also diverges from existing Granger causal discovery methods (cMLP, cLSTM, GVAR, CUTS) that model only endogenous variable relationships, by explicitly modeling exogenous variable distributions via an encoder that performs abductive reasoning and a KL-divergence-based independence constraint. The conceptual framing of anomalies as interventions on exogenous variables in a structural causal model, combined with the Proposition 1 showing a finite window suffices for reconstruction, provides a principled bridge between the two tasks that prior work had not established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AERCA's ability to localize root causes at specific time steps (not just which variable) provides significantly more actionable diagnostic information for operational systems, and the unified framework eliminates the error propagation inherent in two-step approaches. However, the substantial performance drop on the SWaT real-world dataset (AC@1 of 0.220 vs. near-perfect on synthetic data) reveals sensitivity to violations of Granger causality assumptions (hidden confounders, instantaneous effects), which limits immediate deployment in complex industrial settings. Open questions include whether the independence constraint on exogenous variables can be relaxed for correlated noise sources, how the method scales to very high-dimensional systems (hundreds of variables), and whether the streaming SPOT thresholding generalizes across non-stationary regimes.
