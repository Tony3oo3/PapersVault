## Problem

Existing automated curriculum learning methods for RL optimize for reward maximization and ignore cost constraints, so when naively paired with constrained RL algorithms they propose tasks that yield high rewards but also high costs, causing large constraint violation regret during training. This misalignment between the curriculum generator's objective (maximize reward) and the constrained RL objective (satisfy a cost constraint while maximizing reward) means no off-the-shelf curriculum approach can simultaneously ensure safe training and sample-efficient convergence to an optimal policy.

## Input / Output

SCG takes a contextual constrained MDP (CCMDP) parameterized by a continuous context space (e.g., 2D goal position plus tolerance), a target context distribution φ over that space, an initial context distribution ϱ₀, and a safety threshold D. It outputs a sequence of context distributions {ϱ_k} that guide which tasks the agent trains on at each curriculum iteration, along with a final policy π_K. The curriculum update is a non-parametric constrained optimization over distributions (solved via Wasserstein distance), and the source distribution ϱ₊ is represented as a Gaussian mixture model during the initial phases and as a particle-based distribution in the final phase, allowing the method to handle multi-modal target distributions without assuming a parametric form.

## Method

SCG extends CURROT's optimal-transport-based curriculum generation by adding a cost constraint V_c^{π_k}(x) ≤ D̃ to the distribution update (Eq. 4), ensuring the next context distribution's support lies entirely over contexts where the current policy is safe. The core novelty is a three-phase prioritization mechanism: Phase 1 labels contexts as successful based on low discounted cumulative cost and builds a GMM source distribution weighted toward low-cost contexts; Phase 2 switches to rewarding high-reward contexts while annealing the GMM weight ratio α_k from 1 (pure safety) toward 0 (pure performance); Phase 3 jointly requires both reward ≥ ζ and cost ≤ D̃, then interpolates toward the target distribution using a particle-based source. The annealing of α_k provides a smooth transition between safety and performance prioritization, and the GMM standard deviations are modulated by how far each context's cost or reward is from the respective threshold, concentrating the distribution over the most relevant safe/performant regions.

## Results

SCG achieves zero expected discounted cumulative cost and the highest success rate (100%) in all three primary environments (safety-maze, safety-goal, safety-push) while simultaneously attaining the lowest constraint violation regret (CV regret) among all methods that reach optimal policies. CURROT also reaches zero cost and 100% success in safety-maze but incurs substantially higher CV regret due to misaligned objectives; in safety-goal and safety-push, CURROT fails to maintain the lowest CV regret. NAIVESAFECURROT and CURROT4COST either fail to achieve the highest median success consistently or exhibit high variance across training runs, and neither is sample-efficient relative to the DEFAULT (no-curriculum) baseline in the more complex environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCG outperforms CURROT (Klink et al., 2022), the method it directly extends, on constraint violation regret in safety-goal and safety-push while matching its zero-cost, 100%-success final policy in all environments; CURROT's Wasserstein-distance-driven curriculum places goals over hazards early in training, inflating CV regret. Against the naively safety-modified baselines, SCG dominates: NAIVESAFECURROT (penalized reward) achieves comparable CV regret in safety-maze but is less stable in success rates across runs, while CURROT4COST (cost constraint replacing performance constraint) fails to be sample-efficient in any environment and does not consistently reach the highest success. The remaining state-of-the-art curriculum methods—SPDL (Klink et al., 2021), PLR (Jiang et al., 2021), GOALGAN (Florensa et al., 2018), and ALP-GMM (Portelas et al., 2020)—all fail to learn optimal policies (non-zero cost or sub-maximal success) in at least one environment, with ALP-GMM and PLR showing the fastest-growing CV regret during training. The DEFAULT (no-curriculum) baseline either attains the lowest CV regret in safety-maze (by being overly conservative) or one of the highest in other domains, while failing to consistently learn optimal policies, confirming that automated curricula are beneficial but must be safety-aware to avoid the misalignment problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CURROT (Klink et al., 2022), which generates curricula via Wasserstein-distance-based optimal transport over contextual MDPs, by adding a cost constraint to the curriculum optimization and introducing a three-phase prioritization mechanism (safety → performance → target distribution) with annealed GMM source distributions. It departs from prior curriculum learning methods (SPDL, PLR, GOALGAN, ALP-GMM) that ignore cost constraints, and from safety-oriented RL approaches (reset policies, curriculum induction) that intervene in agent-environment interactions rather than shaping the task distribution. The key conceptual contribution is identifying and formalizing the 'misalignment' between curriculum learning's reward-maximization objective and constrained RL's safety-first objective, then resolving it through a principled phase-based curriculum design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode: state-of-the-art curriculum generators actively produce unsafe training trajectories in constrained settings, which is critical for safety-critical deployment (autonomous driving, robotics). The three-phase SCG approach provides a practical, plug-in solution that works across low- and high-dimensional environments with different dynamics. However, SCG offers no theoretical guarantees on constraint violation regret, and it inherits a dependency on the initial context distribution being safe—both noted as limitations. Open questions include combining SCG with safety-guaranteed RL algorithms (e.g., shield-based or Lyapunov-based methods) to obtain end-to-end safety guarantees while preserving the sample-efficiency benefits of curricula.
