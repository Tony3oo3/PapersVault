## Problem

Small language models (SLMs) suffer an 'alignment tax' where standard RLHF/DPO training yields minimal or negative gains in preference alignment due to their limited capacity to capture nuanced human feedback. Existing knowledge distillation methods focus on pre-training and instruction-tuning stages but largely overlook the preference alignment phase, preventing student models from inheriting the teacher's alignment knowledge. This paper asks how a well-aligned large teacher can effectively transfer its preference knowledge to a smaller student.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250683/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a preference dataset of (prompt, preferred response, dispreferred response) triples, a DPO-trained teacher model, and a pre-DPO reference teacher model (both SFT-initialized on instruction data). During training, the student model generates its own on-policy responses for each prompt, which serve as the state context for computing the advantage function. The output is a preference-aligned student model that generates text responses; the training also retains an SFT loss on ground-truth preferred responses to preserve base capabilities and prevent over-optimization.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary distillation objectives. DCKD performs knowledge distillation from a DPO-aligned teacher to the student using two KL-divergence constraints—one on preferred responses and one on dispreferred responses—plus an SFT term, enabling the student to capture the teacher's behavior on both positive and negative examples. ADPA introduces an advantage function A_dpo(s_t, a_t) = β log(π_dpo(a_t|s_t)/π_ref(a_t|s_t)) computed from the DPO teacher and the pre-DPO reference teacher, which provides distribution-level reward signals at O(1) sample complexity per action. The student is trained to maximize the expected advantage over its own generated responses (on-policy), combined with an SFT loss, yielding a gradient that closely mirrors policy gradient updates weighted by the advantage. ADPA+ further initializes the student with DCKD before applying ADPA, combining distributional matching with contrastive advantage signals for stronger alignment.

## Results

On LLaMA-3.2-1B (student) with LLaMA-3.1-8B (teacher), ADPA+ achieves the highest MT-Bench score of 4.02 (DPO-MIX-7K) and 3.99 (HelpSteer2), surpassing the best baseline WPO (3.68/3.51) by 0.34/0.48 points. ADPA+ also attains the best AlpacaEval win rates of 53.8% and 60.9%, while ADPA alone achieves the best Open LLM Leaderboard average of 43.38 (DPO-MIX-7K). All baseline methods score below 50% AlpacaEval win rate when ADPA is used as the reference, confirming ADPA's superior alignment quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADPA+ outperforms all nine baselines on MT-Bench and AlpacaEval across both datasets. On DPO-MIX-7K, ADPA+ (MT-Bench 4.02, WR 53.8%) beats the strongest baseline WPO (3.68, 39.4%) by 0.34 MT-Bench points and 14.4% win rate; on HelpSteer2, the margin widens to 0.48 MT-Bench points and 12.2% win rate over WPO. Among distillation baselines, DCKD (the paper's own simpler method) is the closest competitor, achieving 3.50/3.44 MT-Bench, but ADPA+ still leads by 0.52/0.55 points. On the Open LLM Leaderboard, ADPA alone achieves the best average (43.38 on DPO-MIX-7K), while ADPA+ leads on HelpSteer2 (43.07), both surpassing WPO (42.67/42.85) and DPO (42.70/42.47). Notably, DPKD and DDPO—the two most recent preference-distillation baselines—underperform even the unaligned student on MT-Bench (3.29/3.21 vs. 3.29), suggesting that naive preference-pair distillation without explicit advantage signals can be counterproductive for small models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends knowledge distillation (Hinton, 2015) to the preference alignment phase, a stage largely overlooked by prior KD work that focused on pre-training and instruction-tuning. The core mechanism builds directly on the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2024b) and the Q-function/advantage derivation from 'From R to Q*' (Rafailov et al., 2024a), repurposing the log-ratio between a DPO-trained teacher and its reference as a distribution-level reward signal for the student. Unlike prior preference distillation methods (DPKD, PLaD) that treat teacher outputs as preferred and student outputs as dispreferred in a Bradley-Terry setup, ADPA provides fine-grained, per-token advantage signals that guide the student's policy gradient without requiring online RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the practical 'alignment tax' problem where small language models lose general capability after RLHF, offering a teacher-guided distillation pipeline that narrows the gap with larger models without requiring the student to perform its own RLHF. The O(1) sample complexity of the distribution-level advantage (vs. O(|A|) for token-level and O(|A|^(T-t)) for sequence-level rewards) provides a clean theoretical justification for the observed training stability. Key open questions include generalization beyond the tested datasets and domains, the dependence on well-aligned teacher models, and whether the two-teacher computational overhead can be further reduced for production deployment.
