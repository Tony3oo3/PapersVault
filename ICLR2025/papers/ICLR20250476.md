## Problem

Existing minimax-optimal tabular RL algorithms all rely on empirical-variance-based bonus terms to guarantee optimism, which makes them overly conservative and computationally heavy in practice, often underperforming even sub-optimal methods like UCRL2. The paper asks whether the tradeoff between provable minimax optimality and practical efficiency is inherent, or whether a simpler exploration strategy can achieve both simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a single sequence of constants {c_k} as its only tunable input (reducible to one constant when the number of episodes K is known), along with the current state from the tabular MDP. It outputs a deterministic policy (one action per state per time step) for each episode, plus a high-probability regret bound. Unlike prior methods requiring multiple interacting parameters for variance estimation and bonus scaling, EQ0 consolidates all exploration control into the single scalar c_k, making practical tuning straightforward.

## Method

EQ0 replaces the empirical-variance-based bonus of prior UCBVI-style algorithms with a simple c_k/N(s,a) term proportional to the inverse visit count. The key theoretical insight is quasi-optimism: rather than requiring the estimated value V_h^k(s) to fully dominate V_h^*(s), the analysis only requires V_h^k(s) + (3/2)λ_k H ≥ V_h^*(s), i.e., a bounded underestimation is tolerated. This relaxation is made possible by a novel application of Freedman's inequality that decouples the variance term from the 1/N term in the concentration bound, so the bonus only needs to compensate for the 1/N component while the variance term is absorbed into the induction via a difference-type variance bound. The result is the sharpest known minimax regret bound (H√SAK + HS²A with improved log factors) under the weakest boundedness assumption (bounded value rather than bounded reward or return), along with tight PAC and best-policy identification guarantees.

## Results

On the RiverSwim MDP (S=30 and S=40, varying H), EQ0 achieves consistently lower cumulative regret than all five baselines (UCRL2, UCBVI-BF, EULER, ORLC, MVP) across all configurations, as shown in Figure 1. Theoretically, EQ0 attains a regret bound of Õ(H√(SAK) + HS²A) with sharper logarithmic factors than the prior state-of-the-art (Zhang et al., 2021a), and does so without requiring empirical variance estimation. EQ0 also demonstrates superior computational efficiency, taking less execution time than the compared algorithms (Table 4, Appendix G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EQ0 outperforms all five baselines on the RiverSwim MDP in both cumulative regret and computational efficiency. Against UCRL2 (Jaksch et al., 2010), which has sub-optimal theoretical guarantees, EQ0 achieves lower regret while also providing minimax-optimal bounds. Against the minimax-optimal baselines UCBVI-BF (Azar et al., 2017), EULER (Zanette & Brunskill, 2019), ORLC (Dann et al., 2019), and MVP (Zhang et al., 2021a), EQ0 achieves lower empirical regret in all tested configurations (S=30, S=40 with varying H). Theoretically, EQ0 matches the leading-term regret bound H√(SAK) of Zhang et al. (2021a) but with improved logarithmic factors, and is the first to achieve minimax optimality under the weakest boundedness assumption (bounded value rather than bounded reward or return). EQ0 also requires no empirical variance computation, reducing both algorithmic complexity and execution time compared to all variance-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

EQ0 builds directly on the UCBVI-BF framework (Azar et al., 2017) and the subsequent line of minimax-optimal tabular RL algorithms (Zanette & Brunskill, 2019; Dann et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2021a), all of which construct bonus terms from empirical variances to guarantee full optimism. The key departure is the introduction of quasi-optimism: instead of requiring estimated values to exceed optimal values, EQ0 allows a bounded underestimation controlled by a parameter λ_k, and replaces the variance-dependent bonus with a simple c/N(s,a) term. The analysis leverages Freedman's inequality in a decoupled form that separates variance and 1/N factors, a technique not previously exploited in this context. The bounded-value assumption (weaker than bounded-reward or bounded-return) also broadens the problem class relative to all prior minimax-optimal results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a perceived tradeoff between provable minimax optimality and practical efficiency in tabular RL, showing that the single-parameter 1/N bonus is both theoretically sufficient and empirically superior to variance-based alternatives. The quasi-optimism principle is a genuinely new analytical tool that the authors anticipate could transfer to model-free estimation and general function approximation settings, potentially simplifying exploration in those domains as well. Limitations include the restriction to tabular, finite-horizon, time-homogeneous MDPs, and empirical validation on a single environment (RiverSwim) with limited S and H configurations. Open questions include whether quasi-optimism extends to infinite-horizon or time-inhomogeneous settings, and whether the 1/N bonus structure generalizes to function-approximation-based algorithms.
