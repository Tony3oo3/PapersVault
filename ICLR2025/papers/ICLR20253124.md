## Problem

KV cache compression in LLMs has been extensively studied along the layer, head, and sequence-length axes, but the feature-dimension axis remains underexplored due to the difficulty of modifying a well-structured feature space. While PCA-based orthogonal projections can reduce KV cache dimensionality in a training-free manner, they suffer sharp performance degradation at high compression rates because principal components are optimal for per-layer key/value recovery but suboptimal for preserving global model outputs under the non-linearity and compounding effects of multi-layer attention.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA2-7B-base, Mistral-7B-v0.3-base) and a small calibration set (5–10 samples) as input, and produces per-head, per-layer orthogonal projection matrices of size d×d (where d is the head dimension, typically 64 or 128) that reduce the cached feature dimension from d to r. At inference, a greedy search algorithm outputs two tensors R^K and R^V of shape (L×H) specifying heterogeneous compression rates for keys and values at each attention head in each layer, given a target cache budget γ. The compressed KV cache is then used for standard autoregressive decoding, yielding generated text with reduced memory footprint.

## Method

MatryoshkaKV initializes orthogonal projection matrices via PCA on a small corpus subset, then jointly fine-tunes all projections under a knowledge-distillation objective (KL divergence between the original and compressed model's output distributions) combined with a language-modeling loss. Orthogonality is enforced throughout training via Cayley parameterization (U = (I+Q)(I−Q)⁻¹ with Q skew-symmetric), ensuring that at full rank the compressed model exactly reproduces the original. The key training innovation is a Matryoshka strategy: at each iteration, a rank r is independently and randomly sampled from a predefined schedule (e.g., {d/8, d/4, d/2, d}) for each head, layer, and K/V separately, and the model is trained using only the first r columns of each projection, which preserves a hierarchical structure across columns. At inference, a greedy algorithm searches for heterogeneous compression rates per layer and head by iteratively decrementing the rank of the head whose compression causes the smallest KL-divergence increase, until the target cache budget is met.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B-base, MatryoshkaKV retains 93.10% of the baseline's average accuracy (56.94 vs. 61.16) at a 37.5% KV cache budget, while PCA projection collapses to 33.14 (54.2% of baseline). On Mistral-v0.3-7B-base, MKV retains 92.63% of baseline accuracy (61.29 vs. 66.17) at the same 37.5% budget, versus PCA's 33.24 (50.2%). In the SFT setting on GSM8K, MKV achieves 92.47% of full-cache accuracy at 50% budget. All results are obtained after training on only ~200M tokens (30 GPU×hours) from RedPajama.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PCA (Eigen-Attention, Saxena et al. 2024), MatryoshkaKV achieves a decisive advantage at all compression levels below 62.5%: at 37.5% budget on LLaMA2-7B-base, MKV scores 56.94 average accuracy versus PCA's 33.14 (a 23.8-point gap), and on Mistral-v0.3-7B-base, 61.29 versus 33.24 (28.1 points). PCA essentially collapses below 62.5% budget (dropping to ~33 average), while MKV degrades gracefully, retaining over 90% of baseline accuracy even at 37.5%. Against ASVD (Yuan et al. 2024), MKV outperforms at all three tested budgets: 61.25 vs. 60.92 at 95%, 62.03 vs. 60.06 at 90%, and 61.13 vs. 59.29 at 85% on LLaMA2-7B-base. Notably, MKV's advantage over ASVD grows with compression (1.84 points at 85% vs. 0.33 at 95%), and MKV supports much lower budgets (down to 25%) whereas ASVD only provides checkpoints at 85–95%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the orthogonal-projection approach to KV cache compression initiated by Eigen Attention (Saxena et al., 2024), which uses PCA-derived projections on the feature dimension axis. The key departure is making these projections trainable via a knowledge-distillation objective with Cayley parameterization to preserve orthogonality, rather than relying on fixed PCA eigenvectors. The Matryoshka training strategy, borrowed from Kusupati et al. (2022), is repurposed here to enforce a column hierarchy in the projection matrices, enabling smooth trade-offs between compression rate and quality. The paper also positions itself as complementary to compression along other axes (sequence length via H2O, quantization via KIVI, head sharing via GQA), rather than competing with them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory is a primary bottleneck for LLM inference at scale, and the feature-dimension axis had been underexplored relative to sequence-length and head-count compression. MatryoshkaKV demonstrates that a lightweight training procedure (~200M tokens, 30 GPU-hours) can unlock substantially higher compression rates (60–75%) while retaining >90% accuracy, a regime where PCA-based methods collapse. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models (70B+), whether the greedy per-head search generalizes across diverse task distributions without re-calibration, and whether the distillation objective can be replaced by self-supervised signals to reduce dependence on a teacher model. The method's compatibility with GQA, H2O, and KIVI suggests it can serve as a modular component in a broader compression stack.
