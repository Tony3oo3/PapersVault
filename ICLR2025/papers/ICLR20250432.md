## Problem

Static weight interpolation methods like WiSE-FT apply a single global coefficient λ to all test samples, but the paper's pilot study shows that optimal coefficients vary substantially across samples and domains, leaving significant performance on the table. Existing dynamic interpolation approaches that adapt λ per sample require additional trainable router modules and careful hyperparameter design, introducing non-trivial training complexity. The paper asks whether per-sample interpolation coefficients can be estimated in a training-free manner from unlabeled test inputs alone.

## Input / Output

DaWin consumes unlabeled test images (no ground-truth labels required) along with the weight vectors of a pre-trained model (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/32) and one or more fine-tuned counterparts of identical architecture. For each test sample, it computes the output entropy of each candidate model, derives a per-sample interpolation coefficient λ(x) ∈ [0,1], and then produces a class label via the dynamically merged model. A Beta mixture model clusters the N per-sample coefficients into K components (K=3,5,2 for ViT-B/32, B/16, L/14 respectively), so that only K distinct merged weight vectors are actually instantiated at inference time rather than N.

## Method

DaWin estimates per-sample model expertise using the exponentiated negative output entropy of each model, forming a softmax-like ratio λ(x) = exp(−H(f(x;θ₁))) / (exp(−H(f(x;θ₀))) + exp(−H(f(x;θ₁)))) that requires no labels or hyperparameters. The paper validates via scatter plots and Pearson correlations that this entropy ratio strongly tracks the oracle X-entropy ratio across all four correctness splits (TrueTrue, TrueFalse, FalseTrue, FalseFalse) and all six ImageNet distribution-shift benchmarks. To reduce inference cost from O(N) to O(K), a Beta mixture model fitted via EM over the estimated coefficients assigns each sample to one of K clusters, and the cluster mean is used as the interpolation weight for all members. A domain-wise offset term (Eq. 2) incorporating per-domain entropy statistics stabilizes coefficient estimation, and temperature scaling on the ID validation set mitigates overconfident outputs. The paper further proves (Lemma 6.1) that under an entropy-dominancy assumption, DaWin's coefficients are always biased toward models that correctly classify the given sample, providing a theoretical guarantee for the observed expert-biased behavior.

## Results

On the robust fine-tuning benchmark with CLIP ViT-B/32, DaWin achieves 78.70% ImageNet (ID) accuracy and 54.36% average OOD accuracy, surpassing WiSE-FT (79.14% ID, 51.02% OOD) by 3.34 points on OOD while requiring no hyperparameter tuning. Scaling to ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14, DaWin reaches 66.9% and 76.0% OOD accuracy respectively, outperforming the strongest baselines (WiSE-FT at 64.2% and 73.2%) by 2.7 and 2.8 points. In the multi-task learning setting across 8 datasets, DaWin achieves 86.3% average accuracy, exceeding Pareto Merging (84.8%) and AdaMerging++ (80.9%) while approaching the ground-truth expert selection upper bound (90.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DaWin decisively outperforms all static interpolation and soup-based methods on OOD accuracy: it beats WiSE-FT by 3.34 points (54.36 vs 51.02), Uniform Soup by 2.28 points, and Model Stock by 3.37 points on ViT-B/32, while requiring only 1 training run versus 48 for soup methods. Against state-of-the-art robust fine-tuning methods on ViT-B/16, DaWin (66.9% OOD) surpasses CaRot (64.0%), Lipsum-FT (62.7%), and LP-FT (61.3%) by 2.9–5.6 points. On ViT-L/14, DaWin (76.0% OOD) leads CaRot (74.1%) and WiSE-FT (73.2%) by 1.9 and 2.8 points respectively. In multi-task learning, DaWin (86.3% avg) outperforms Pareto Merging (84.8%), AdaMerging++ (80.9%), Ties-Merging (72.6%), and Task Arithmetic (68.8%), approaching the oracle expert selection (90.5%) that requires ground-truth domain labels. The only trade-off is a marginal ID accuracy drop (78.70 vs 79.14 for WiSE-FT on B/32), which the authors argue is negligible given the substantial OOD gains and the elimination of hyperparameter search.

## Relation to Prior Work

DaWin extends the weight interpolation lineage initiated by WiSE-FT (Wortsman et al., 2022b) and Model Soups (Wortsman et al., 2022a), which use a single static coefficient λ for all samples, into a per-sample dynamic formulation λ(x) that requires no additional training. It departs from recent dynamic merging methods (Twin-Merging, DAM, MoE-based merging) that introduce learnable router modules and require extra optimization, by instead using the entropy of each model's output distribution as a training-free proxy for X-entropy to determine sample-wise expertise. The efficiency component draws on Beta mixture modeling (Ma & Leijon, 2011) to cluster the continuous coefficient space into K discrete groups, reducing inference from O(N) to O(K) interpolation operations. Conceptually, the approach also connects to dynamic classifier selection (Giacinto & Roli, 2001) and the linear mode connectivity hypothesis (Frankle et al., 2020), which the paper extends into a 'sample-wise entropy valley' conjecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DaWin offers a practical, training-free plug-in that consistently improves OOD accuracy by ~4.5% over the strongest static interpolation baseline across three CLIP scales, without any hyperparameter tuning — a notable advantage over methods like WiSE-FT or AdaMerging that require grid search or additional optimization. The mixture modeling trick makes the per-sample approach computationally tractable, addressing a key practical barrier that has limited prior dynamic merging methods. Limitations include the assumption that all test samples are available simultaneously (needed for offset adjustment and mixture fitting), the inherent ceiling in the FalseFalse regime where both individual models fail, and evaluation restricted to CLIP-based classification. Open questions include whether the entropy–X-entropy correlation generalizes to non-CLIP architectures, generative models, or other modalities, and whether the approach extends to tasks where the output space is not a fixed-class simplex.
