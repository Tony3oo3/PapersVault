## Problem

Existing GNN + conformal prediction pipelines fail on dynamic graphs because the standard block-diagonal stacking of adjacency snapshots breaks exchangeability across time, producing de-aligned embeddings that yield invalid or excessively large prediction sets. The paper addresses how to construct provably valid conformal prediction sets on dynamic graphs, particularly in the semi-inductive regime where only historical labels are available and the test node/time pair is fixed in the future.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252796/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T adjacency matrices over a fixed node set (allowing directed, bipartite, or weighted graphs), optional node attribute vectors, and observed labels for training/calibration node/time pairs. These are assembled into a single square symmetric matrix A^UNF of size (p+nT)×(p+nT) via dilated unfolding, which is fed to a standard GNN. The output is a conformal prediction set (a subset of the discrete label space) for a target node/time pair, guaranteed to contain the true label with probability at least 1−α, along with d-dimensional embeddings for every node/time pair.

## Method

The core contribution is representing the dynamic graph as a dilated unfolding of its adjacency tensor—column-concatenating the T adjacency matrices into a bipartite structure between p global nodes and nT temporal node/time pairs—rather than the conventional block-diagonal stacking. This single square matrix is input to any standard GNN (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, JKNet) without architectural modification, producing embeddings that are exchangeable over time. For the transductive regime, validity of the resulting conformal set (via split conformal with Adaptive Prediction Sets) requires no assumptions beyond the random test label; for the semi-inductive regime, validity additionally requires joint exchangeability of the data (A1) and permutation equivariance of the GNN (A2). The key insight is that the unfolding links each node to itself across all time points, preventing the temporal shift artifact that causes block-diagonal GNNs to encode spurious distributional changes between snapshots.

## Results

In the semi-inductive regime, UGCN achieves 98.5% accuracy on SBM (3 classes) and 91.5% on School (10 classes), compared to 33.4% and 11.6% for Block GCN (near random guessing). In the transductive regime, UGNN margins are smaller (e.g., 98.0% vs 96.4% on SBM). For conformal coverage (target ≥0.90), UGNN achieves valid coverage in all semi-inductive examples without drift (e.g., 0.918 on SBM, 0.924 on School), while Block GNN fails in every semi-inductive case (0.659 on SBM, 0.812 on School). UGNN also produces smaller prediction sets, e.g., 1.097 vs 1.977 on semi-inductive SBM and 3.037 vs 8.079 on semi-inductive School.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the block diagonal adjacency representation (Fey and Lenssen, 2019), where time-point snapshots are stacked as independent blocks. The unfolded representation (UGNN) wins decisively in the semi-inductive regime: on SBM, UGCN reaches 98.5% accuracy vs. 33.4% for Block GCN (3-class random baseline is 33.3%), and on School, 91.5% vs. 11.6% (10-class random is 10%). For conformal coverage in the semi-inductive regime, UGNN achieves valid ≥0.90 coverage on all non-drift datasets (SBM: 0.918, School: 0.924, Flight: 0.910), while Block GNN under-covers in every case (SBM: 0.659, School: 0.812, Flight: 0.853). In the transductive regime, both methods achieve valid coverage, but UGNN produces meaningfully smaller prediction sets (e.g., 1.097 vs. 1.977 on semi-inductive SBM; 3.037 vs. 8.079 on semi-inductive School). The one exception is the Trade dataset, which exhibits temporal drift; here UGNN also fails to achieve valid semi-inductive coverage (0.847), though it still matches or slightly exceeds Block GNN accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: tensor unfolding representations for dynamic graphs (from the spectral embedding literature, Davis et al. 2023) and conformal prediction for GNNs (Huang et al. 2024b; Clarkson 2023; Zargarbashi and Bojchevski 2023). Prior conformal GNN work addressed static graphs or strictly growing graphs, while unfoldings had been used exclusively for unsupervised spectral embedding. The key conceptual departure is showing that the standard block-diagonal representation of dynamic graphs introduces a 'temporal shift' that breaks exchangeability, and that a simple dilated unfolding restores the symmetry needed for valid conformal inference without modifying the GNN architecture or conformal procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a modular, drop-in interface for valid uncertainty quantification on dynamic graphs, with clean theoretical guarantees across three inference regimes (transductive, temporal transductive, semi-inductive). The honest characterization of the drift failure mode on the trade dataset, and the identification of downstream remedies (Barber et al. 2023; Clarkson 2023), points to a clear research agenda. Open questions include scaling unfoldings to megascale dynamic graphs, achieving fully inductive inference (emulating the projection operator from spectral embedding), and handling non-stationary dynamics where the stationarity-like assumptions of the semi-inductive regime break down.
