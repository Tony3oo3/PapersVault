## Problem

Post-training quantization of LLMs suffers from large errors when activation or weight outliers stretch the quantization range, leaving too few effective bits for the majority of values. While random rotations can statistically reduce outliers, the paper reveals that different random rotations produce up to 13 points of variance in downstream accuracy, and no prior method had learned rotations specifically to minimize quantization loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253314/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen pre-trained LLM weights and 800 calibration samples from WikiText-2 (sequence length 2048 for GPTQ) as input. It produces two types of output: (1) learned orthonormal rotation matrices R1 (shape D_token × D_token) and R2 (shape D_head × D_head, per-layer) that are merged directly into the weight matrices, requiring no architecture change at inference; and (2) optional online Hadamard rotations R3 and R4 for extreme 4-bit activation/KV-cache quantization, which add ~8% latency overhead. The rotation matrices account for only ~0.26% of total weight size and are constrained to the Stiefel manifold.

## Method

SpinQuant exploits rotational invariance in Transformer architectures to insert paired rotation matrices (R·R^T = I) at strategic points—residual stream (R1), attention value/out-projection (R2), and MLP/KV-cache (R3, R4 as Hadamard)—so that full-precision outputs remain numerically identical while intermediate distributions become outlier-free. The key innovation is optimizing R1 and R2 on the Stiefel manifold via Cayley SGD, which parameterizes each update as a Cayley transform of a skew-symmetric matrix derived from the projected gradient of the quantized network's task loss, guaranteeing orthonormality is preserved at every step. Two variants are offered: SpinQuant_nohad (R1, R2 only, fully absorbed into weights for zero inference overhead) and SpinQuant_had (all four rotations, for W4A4KV4 settings). The optimization runs for 100 iterations with a linearly decaying learning rate (1.5→0) on 800 calibration samples, taking ~13–30 minutes for 1B–8B models and ~3.5 hours for 70B, after which GPTQ is applied to the rotated weights for final 4-bit quantization.

## Results

Under extreme W4A4KV4 quantization, SpinQuant_had narrows the gap to full precision to just 2.9 points on LLaMA-2 7B (64.0 vs 66.9 zero-shot 8-task average), 1.4 points on LLaMA-2 13B (66.9 vs 68.3), and 1.7 points on LLaMA-2 70B (71.2 vs 72.9). For the more practical W4A8KV8 setting, SpinQuant_nohad achieves only a 1.6-point gap on Mistral-7B (69.4 vs 71.0) and a 1.0-point gap on LLaMA-3 8B (68.6 vs 69.6). WikiText-2 perplexity under W4A4KV4 drops from 5.5 (FP) to 5.9 (SpinQuant_had) on LLaMA-2 7B, compared to 14.9 for LLM-QAT and 9,253 for GPTQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpinQuant_had decisively outperforms all prior quantization baselines under W4A4KV4: it surpasses LLM-QAT by 19.1 points on LLaMA-2 7B (64.0 vs 44.9), SmoothQuant by 25.0 points (64.0 vs 39.0), and GPTQ by 27.2 points (64.0 vs 36.8). Against the concurrent random-rotation method QuaRot, SpinQuant_had achieves improvements of 2.0 to 28.6 points depending on model and bit-width (e.g., 70.1 vs 41.5 on LLaMA-3 70B W4A4KV4 with RTN), while using fewer online Hadamard matrices (two per block vs four). For W4A8 settings, SpinQuant_nohad matches or exceeds state-of-the-art weight-only methods (QuIP#, OmniQuant, AWQ) on WikiText-2 perplexity without requiring vector quantization kernels. On Mistral-7B, SpinQuant_had with learned rotations improves over random Hadamard rotations by up to 16.2 points (68.6 vs 52.4 under W4A4KV4), confirming that optimization on the Stiefel manifold is critical. SpinQuant_nohad, which requires no architectural modification at inference time, already closes the W4A8KV8 gap to 1.6 points on Mistral-7B, making it a drop-in replacement for standard quantized inference pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpinQuant extends the rotation-based outlier mitigation paradigm established by QuaRot (Ashkboos et al., 2023b) and QuIP# (Tseng et al., 2024), which apply random or Hadamard rotations to redistribute activation magnitudes and ease quantization. The key departure is replacing these fixed random rotations with *learned* rotations optimized on the Stiefel manifold via Cayley SGD (Li et al., 2020), directly minimizing the quantized network's task loss while keeping all pretrained weights frozen. This builds on the rotation invariance property of Transformer architectures noted by Elhage et al. (2023) and SliceGPT (Ashkboos et al., 2023a), but is the first to treat the rotation matrices as optimizable parameters rather than fixed random choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: W4A4KV4 quantization with only a 2.9-point gap to full precision on LLaMA-2 7B (and ~1 point on LLaMA-3 8B) makes extreme low-bit deployment viable without architectural changes or specialized vector-quantization kernels. The discovery that random rotations produce up to 13 points of variance in downstream accuracy is a conceptual finding that reframes rotation-based quantization from a 'set-and-forget' trick to a tunable optimization problem. Open questions include whether a closed-form optimal rotation exists for a known activation distribution, and whether the approach extends to non-Transformer architectures or to scenarios where the rotation cannot be absorbed into weights (e.g., dynamic or streaming settings).
