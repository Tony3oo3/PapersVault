## Problem

The emerging double-layer HDR image format (SDR + Gain Map) enables adaptive HDR display across devices, but no learning-based method exists to estimate the Gain Map from an SDR image. The core difficulty is that the GM encodes regional intensity variation with a fluctuating peak value, making direct pixel-wise regression unstable and imbalanced between local contrast and global luminance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250976/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 8-bit RGB SDR image (3840×2160 synthetic or 4096×3072 real-world) and produces a single-channel 8-bit grayscale Gain Map, which is logarithm-encoded, normalized to [0,1], and typically downsampled to 1/4 or 1/16 of the SDR resolution. The GM is then combined with the SDR via element-wise multiplication in the linear domain to reconstruct the final HDR image, with metadata (Q_max, Q_min, offsets) stored alongside.

## Method

GMNet decomposes the GM into a normalized GM (I_NGM) and a scalar peak value (Q_max), assigning each to a dedicated branch. The Local Contrast Restoration (LCR) branch processes full-resolution SDR patches through cascaded ResBlock groups to predict I_NGM, while the Global Luminance Estimation (GLE) branch operates on a downsampled SDR to predict Q_max and generate a spatial kernel (W_ker) and channel weights (W_chn) via squeeze modules (adaptive pooling + 1×1 convolutions). These global signals modulate the LCR branch through spatial-aware masking and channel-wise attention, aligning local restoration with global luminance context. Training uses a composite L1 loss on I_NGM directly and on the product I_GM = I_NGM × Q_max indirectly, avoiding the tensor-vs-scalar supervision imbalance that arises from directly regressing Q_max.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset, GMNet achieves 43.55 dB PSNR (linear), 47.63 dB PSNR (PQ), 1.63 ΔE_ITP, and 9.95 HDR-VDP3, outperforming all SI-HDR and SDR-to-HDRTV baselines across every metric. On the real-world dataset, it reaches 33.95 dB PSNR (linear), 40.21 dB PSNR (PQ), and 4.03 ΔE_ITP, again leading all competitors. The method uses only 1.83M parameters and runs at 75.26 ms per 4096×3072 image on an A100, making it the second-fastest method after the LUT-based ITM-LUT (19.81 ms) while delivering substantially better quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMNet outperforms all seven baselines on every metric in both the synthetic and real-world evaluations. Among SI-HDR methods, DCDR-UNet is the strongest competitor (41.81 dB PSNR-L, 3.05 ΔE_ITP), yet GMNet still leads by ~1.7 dB in linear PSNR and reduces ΔE_ITP by 1.42. Among SDR-to-HDRTV methods, HDCFM is closest in the PQ domain (44.90 dB PSNR-PQ), but GMNet surpasses it by 2.73 dB in PQ PSNR and 0.85 in ΔE_ITP. The lightweight HDCFM (0.10M params) is competitive in the linear domain but falls short in PQ and HDR-specific metrics. Notably, the much larger EPCE-HDR (33.79M params) performs worst among SI-HDR methods, confirming that parameter count alone does not guarantee quality. On the real-world dataset, the same ranking holds: GMNet leads DCDR-UNet by 0.41 dB PSNR-L and 1.88 dB PSNR-PQ, and beats FMNet (best SDR-to-HDRTV) by 1.32 dB PSNR-L and 0.45 dB PSNR-PQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the Gain Map (GM) from the emerging double-layer HDR display standard (Apple/Adobe/Google/ISO) as a learning target for inverse tone mapping, rather than predicting HDR or PQ-encoded HDR directly as in prior SI-HDR and SDR-to-HDRTV work. Conceptually, it sits between SI-HDR (which reconstructs scene irradiance) and SDR-to-HDRTV (which predicts PQ-encoded output), but shifts the optimization target to a compact 8-bit intermediary that is applied at display time. The dual-branch GMNet architecture draws on standard CNN building blocks (ResBlocks, adaptive pooling, channel attention) but introduces a specific decomposition of GM into normalized spatial content (I_NGM) and a global peak scalar (Q_max), with spatial-aware and channel-wise modulation to couple the two branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the double-layer SDR+GM format becomes an industry standard (ISO 2024, Apple, Google, Adobe), the ability to synthesize a GM from existing SDR content is practically critical for up-converting the vast library of SDR media. The paper provides the first dedicated datasets (synthetic 4K and real-world 4096×3072) and a baseline method for this task, establishing a foundation for future research. Limitations include the restriction to single-channel (grayscale) GMs, the absence of video/temporal modeling, and the reliance on a specific degradation pipeline for synthetic data. Open questions include whether the decomposition strategy generalizes to three-channel GMs, whether the approach extends to video with temporal consistency, and how performance scales with the down-sampling factor of the GM in practice.
