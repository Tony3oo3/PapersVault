## Problem

Convergence theory for score-based generative models typically assumes a global Lipschitz bound on the score function, but it is unclear whether such a bound holds for all time or what the sharp temporal and spatial behavior of the score is. This paper shows that the finite-time uniform Hessian bound is optimal (it genuinely blows up at the predicted time), and that for data supported on low-dimensional manifolds the score singularity is O(1/t) in the gradient and O(1/t) in the Hessian almost everywhere, rather than the previously assumed O(1/t²).

## Input / Output

The analysis takes an initial data distribution p_0 with various regularity assumptions (log-concave, general smooth with bounded Hessian, or compactly supported on a smooth manifold), a neural-network score approximation s_θ(t,x) satisfying an L₂ estimation error bound ε_0², and a uniform discretization of the backward SDE with N steps. The outputs are explicit KL-divergence convergence bounds of the form (M₂+n)e^{-T} + Tε_0² + O(n·poly(log n)·L^k/N), sharp pointwise Lipschitz and Hessian estimates of the score potential with tracked dimension dependence, and a well-posedness guarantee for the continuous backward SDE up to generation time t=0 without requiring separated temporal regimes.

## Method

The core technique is a two-step time reparameterization that transforms the Fokker-Planck equation for the forward OU process into a viscous Hamilton-Jacobi equation, which in turn reduces to the standard heat equation for the density. The paper proves the finite-time Hessian bound is sharp by constructing an explicit counterexample where the Hessian of log p blows up exactly at the predicted critical time. For the global-in-time result, it derives local-in-space Lipschitz estimates of the score and its Hessian via the heat-kernel representation, tracking dependence on the initial data's Hessian norm L, the Gaussian tail parameter α₂, and dimension n. For compactly supported manifold data, it uses a geometric decomposition of the nearest-point map to show the Hessian is O(1/t) almost everywhere, with the exceptional set having measure zero. These estimates are then plugged into the KL chain inequality from Chen et al. (2023) to obtain convergence bounds for the exponential integrator discretization with polynomial complexity in dimension.

## Results

The paper establishes that the finite-time uniform Hessian bound for the score potential is sharp: Theorem 3.4 constructs an explicit initial distribution for which ‖D²q(t,x)‖₂ blows up exactly at T = −log(1−1/M₀), confirming that no global-in-time uniform bound exists in general. For the smooth case, Theorem 4.4 yields KL(P₀‖Q̂_T) ≲ (M₂+n)e^{−T} + Tε₀² + nT²C_T²/N with N = O(n log²n) under log-concavity, while Theorem 4.6 extends to general smooth p₀ with N = O(n³ log²n) using only local Lipschitz estimates valid globally in time. For compactly supported manifold data, Theorem 3.8 improves the Hessian bound from O(1/t²) to O(1/t) almost everywhere, which is shown to be optimal by Example 3.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or datasets. The comparisons are to prior theoretical bounds. Compared to Chen et al. (2023), which achieves O((n log n)²) complexity but requires a non-uniform (data-dependent) discretization schedule, this paper's Theorem 4.6 achieves O((n log n)³) with a uniform discretization that requires no prior knowledge of p₀, making it more practical. Compared to Bortoli (2022), who established an O(1/t²) Hessian bound for manifold-supported data, this paper's Theorem 3.8 shows the sharper O(1/t) bound holds almost everywhere, with Example 3.9 demonstrating the O(1/t²) rate is only attained on a measure-zero set. The paper's point-wise Lipschitz estimates (Theorem 3.5) are stronger than the expected-value estimates used by Benton et al. (2024) and Conforti et al. (2025), making them directly applicable to convergence analysis of the discretized backward process rather than just the continuous one. The sharp counterexample (Theorem 3.4) is novel and resolves an open question about whether the finite-time Hessian bound could be extended globally.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the convergence framework of Chen et al. (2023), which established KL-divergence bounds for discretized backward SDEs under global Lipschitz assumptions on the score, and on Bortoli (2022), who analyzed convergence under the manifold hypothesis with singular scores. The key departure is methodological: rather than assuming a global Lipschitz bound on the score (which the paper proves is fundamentally unattainable in finite time for general data), the authors derive sharp local-in-space, global-in-time Lipschitz estimates via a Hamilton-Jacobi PDE approach using convex envelopes and semiconcavity arguments (building on Strömberg 2010 and Alvarez et al. 1997). This replaces the need for separated time regimes in discretization schedules, and the point-wise analysis (as opposed to expected-Lipschitz bounds in Benton et al. 2024 or Conforti et al. 2025) makes the results directly applicable to the discretized process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sharp counterexample (Theorem 3.4) showing that the Hessian of the score blows up in finite time even for smooth, bounded-curvature initial data is a fundamental limitation result: it proves that the global Lipschitz assumption on the score, widely used in prior convergence theory, is not merely conservative but actually false. The O(1/t) Hessian bound for manifold-supported data (versus the previously known O(1/t²)) justifies a more optimistic convergence rate in the Wasserstein metric and aligns with empirical observations of score blow-up. Open questions remain: the authors cannot provide implementable guidance on optimal discretization schedules or early-stopping times, and the measure-zero exceptional set where O(1/t²) behavior persists in the non-convex manifold case complicates practical convergence guarantees.
