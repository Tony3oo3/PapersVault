## Problem

Federated Domain Generalization (FDG) requires a model trained on distributed, domain-specific client data to generalize to unseen target domains, but standard domain generalization methods that jointly optimize across all domains are infeasible when data is siloed across clients. The open question is how to leverage cross-domain knowledge for domain-invariant learning in a federated setting without incurring additional communication overhead or requiring direct data sharing among clients.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard classification images (grayscale 28×28 for MNIST/EMNIST, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100, variable-resolution RGB for PACS, VLCS, and OfficeHome) and outputs class labels. Unusually, the server-side optimization does not receive raw data or model parameters beyond the standard FL upload; instead, it operates solely on local gradient vectors (the difference between each client's post-training model and the initial global model), making the server step data-free. For FDG benchmarks, a pretrained ResNet-18 backbone is used; for FL benchmarks, a lightweight two-convolutional-layer CNN is employed.

## Method

FedOMG formulates the server-side aggregation as a meta-learning problem where the invariant gradient direction is expressed as a convex combination of local gradients with learnable coefficients Γ, avoiding the second-order derivatives required by direct Gradient Inner Product optimization. The optimization is constrained to a ball of radius κ‖g_FL‖² centered at the reference FL gradient (e.g., FedAvg), which limits the search space and reduces computational cost. By invoking Pareto optimality, the min-max objective over all client pairs is reduced to a single closed-form solution (Theorem 1): g_IGD = g_FL + (κ‖g_FL‖/‖Γ*·g‖)·Γ*·g, where Γ* minimizes a simple linear-plus-norm objective. This makes FedOMG a drop-in replacement for any FL aggregation rule, orthogonal to client-side FDG methods like FedSAM or FedSR, and introduces no additional communication rounds beyond the standard model upload.

## Results

On the three FDG benchmarks, FedOMG achieves 82.0% average accuracy on VLCS, 88.4% on PACS, and 70.0% on OfficeHome, outperforming the strongest baselines (FedIIR, StableFDG) by 4–6 percentage points. In standard FL settings (α=0.1, 100 clients, 20% participation), FedOMG reaches 98.75% on MNIST, 90.40% on CIFAR-10, 48.76% on CIFAR-100, and 98.91% on EMNIST, consistently ranking first or second. When combined with FedRod, the method achieves the best overall results: 99.63% (MNIST), 95.81% (CIFAR-10), 55.39% (CIFAR-100), and 99.62% (EMNIST) under α=0.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedOMG decisively outperforms all six FDG baselines (FedAvg, FedGA, FedSAM, FedIIR, FedSR, StableFDG) on every domain of VLCS, PACS, and OfficeHome, with the largest margins on VLCS (82.0 vs. 76.6 for FedIIR, +5.4%) and OfficeHome (70.0 vs. 65.0 for StableFDG, +5.0%). In FL settings, FedOMG beats FedAvg by 5–10 points across all four datasets and surpasses FedAS (the strongest FL baseline) on MNIST (98.75 vs. 97.91) and EMNIST (98.91 vs. 97.71) at α=0.1, though FedAS edges it on CIFAR-100 (50.37 vs. 48.76). The combination FedRod+OMG achieves the best overall FL accuracy (e.g., 95.81% on CIFAR-10, 55.39% on CIFAR-100), confirming orthogonality between layer disentanglement and gradient matching. Among FDG combinations, only FedSAM+OMG marginally exceeds standalone FedOMG (82.5 vs. 82.0 on VLCS; 70.9 vs. 70.0 on OfficeHome), while FedIIR+OMG and FedSR+OMG underperform standalone FedOMG, suggesting their client-side regularization overlaps with FedOMG's server-side gradient alignment. FedOMG also surpasses centralized DG methods (ERM, Fish, Fishr) reported in the appendix, e.g., 88.4% vs. 86.9% (Fish) on PACS and 82.0% vs. 78.2% (Fishr) on VLCS, demonstrating that the federated setting does not preclude competitive domain-invariant learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedOMG extends the gradient matching framework of Fish (Shi et al., 2022) from centralized domain generalization to the federated setting, where direct access to all domain data is prohibited. While Fish requires either second-order derivatives or continuous model transmission to implement GIP optimization, FedOMG reformulates the problem as a convex optimization over a small set of mixing coefficients (U_S-dimensional vs. M-dimensional), using only the local gradients already transmitted in standard FL. The method is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to existing FDG approaches (FedSR, FedIIR, FedSAM, StableFDG), which modify local training, by operating exclusively at the server aggregation stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that FedOMG requires zero additional communication overhead beyond standard FL gradient uploads, making it a drop-in enhancement for any existing FL pipeline. The demonstrated orthogonality (e.g., FedRod+OMG outperforming both individually by 5-7%) suggests it can be layered onto future FL/FDG methods without conflict. Open questions include whether the searching radius κ can be made adaptive rather than a fixed hyperparameter, whether the approach extends to non-convex loss landscapes beyond the μ-strongly-convex assumption, and how it behaves under extreme client heterogeneity (very few participating clients per round).
