## Problem

Existing 4D generation methods rely on score distillation sampling (SDS) from 2D diffusion models to infer unseen views and motion, which inherently produces over-saturated textures and the Janus (multi-face) problem. The paper asks whether 4D objects can be generated by explicitly synthesizing temporally and multi-view consistent videos first, then reconstructing from them, thereby eliminating SDS and its associated artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251103/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 576×576 RGB image as input and produces a 4D Gaussian Splatting representation (canonical Gaussians + HexPlane-based deformation field + per-timestamp color affine parameters) that supports free-viewpoint dynamic rendering at arbitrary azimuth angles. Internally, SVD generates a 25-frame video, and SV3D produces 21 azimuth views (360° evenly divided, 0° elevation) per frame, yielding a 25×21 multi-view video matrix used as pseudo ground-truth for optimization. The entire pipeline runs in ~1h15min on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

EG4D is a three-stage, training-free framework that avoids SDS entirely. Stage I uses SVD for temporal dynamics and SV3D for multi-view synthesis, with a key training-free attention injection: in each spatial self-attention layer of SV3D, the key-value latents are blended via exponential moving average (EMA) with the previous timestamp's latents (z_t ← α·z_t* + (1−α)·z_{t−1}), enforcing temporal consistency without retraining. Stage II optimizes a 4D-GS representation with two robustness mechanisms: a per-timestamp color affine transformation (W_t^c, b_t^c predicted by an MLP from HexPlane features) that disentangles unwanted texture color drift from true geometric deformation, and a multiscale rendering augmentation that randomly downsamples supervision to prevent overfitting to video-diffusion noise. Stage III performs one-pass diffusion refinement using SDXL-Turbo (strength 0.167) to restore semantic details, fine-tuning only the deformation field while freezing canonical Gaussians, with a sine-scheduled view-dependent loss weight to handle per-view quality variation.

## Results

On the Animate124 benchmark, EG4D achieves the best scores across all six metrics: CLIP-I 0.9535, PSNR 23.28, SSIM 0.904, LPIPS 0.173, FVD 142.34, and CD-FVD 459.10. The FVD improvement is particularly dramatic—142.34 vs. 736–1904 for baselines—indicating substantially better per-frame quality and temporal consistency. In a user study with 47 participants, EG4D is preferred by ~80–84% of raters on overall quality, reference view alignment, 3D appearance, and motion realism. Against the training-based SV4D, EG4D still leads on image fidelity (PSNR 23.28 vs. 22.57, SSIM 0.904 vs. 0.852) but trails on temporal consistency (CD-FVD 459.10 vs. 311.06).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EG4D outperforms all four SDS-based and video-to-4D baselines (Animate124, Consistent4D, Efficient4D, DreamGaussian4D) on every metric in Table 2, with the largest margins on FVD (142 vs. 736–1904) and PSNR (23.28 vs. 9.0–20.4), confirming that explicit multi-view video generation plus pixel-wise 4D-GS optimization avoids the over-saturation and Janus artifacts inherent to score distillation. In the user study, EG4D is preferred by 83.75% of participants on overall quality versus 11.27% for the strongest baseline DreamGaussian4D. Against the training-based SV4D (Table 5), EG4D wins on image fidelity (PSNR 23.28 vs. 22.57, SSIM 0.904 vs. 0.852, LPIPS 0.173 vs. 0.196) but loses on temporal consistency (FVD 142.34 vs. 138.81, CD-FVD 459.10 vs. 311.06), which the authors attribute to SV4D's joint spatiotemporal training on large 4D datasets versus EG4D's training-free pipeline. The paper also notes that EG4D's motion range (45.98% user preference) is on par with DreamGaussian4D (39.96%) but limited by the base SVD model's inability to produce large motions.

## Relation to Prior Work

EG4D departs from the dominant SDS-based paradigm for 4D generation (DreamFusion, Animate124, DreamGaussian4D, Consistent4D) by explicitly generating temporal-consistent multi-view videos using two orthogonal video diffusion models—SVD for dynamics and SV3D for novel views—then reconstructing a 4D Gaussian Splatting representation from those explicit observations. The training-free attention injection (spatial KV latent blending with EMA) is a novel modification to SV3D's inference that addresses the temporal inconsistency inherent in frame-by-frame multi-view generation, while the color affine transformation and one-pass diffusion refinement are practical additions to the 4D-GS/HexPlane reconstruction pipeline. The framework is modular and dataset-independent, positioning it as a drop-in alternative to SDS that can absorb future advances in video diffusion without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating score distillation, EG4D sidesteps the over-saturation and Janus artifacts that have plagued SDS-based 4D generation, and the large quantitative margins (PSNR 23.3 vs. ≤20.4; FVD 142 vs. ≥736) and ~80% user preference suggest this is a meaningful quality leap for the field. However, the framework inherits limitations from its base models: SVD's restricted motion range and SV3D's imprecise camera pose conditioning constrain both dynamics and geometric accuracy. Open questions include whether more advanced I2V models (CogVideoX, AnimateAnyone) can unlock high-dynamic 4D generation within this pipeline, whether adaptive camera pose estimation can improve reconstruction fidelity, and whether the training-free attention injection generalizes to other multi-view diffusion architectures beyond SV3D.
