## Problem

There is no systematic, quantitative method to determine whether Large Vision Models (LVMs) extract medically and semantically relevant features comparable to those identified by human pathologists. Existing evaluations rely on small-scale, qualitative expert assessments of attention maps or activation layers, which are subject to inter-observer variability, limited sample sizes, and evolving diagnostic guidelines. This paper introduces the Boltzmann Semantic Score (BSS), a model-agnostic metric that uses LLM-encoded pathology reports as a reference to quantitatively measure the semantic alignment of LVM embedding spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253656/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired data: pathology report text (from the TCGA-Reports dataset, ~9,500 patients across 32 cancer types) encoded by an LLM into a dense vector of dimension d₁ (ranging from 1,024 for Jamba to 8,192 for Command-R), and Whole Slide Images tiled into 224×224 patches at 20× magnification, encoded by an LVM into a dense vector of dimension d₂ (ranging from 384 for Lunit-Dino to 1,024 for UNI/SwinT). WSI-level representations are obtained via mean pooling (unsupervised) or AbMIL attention-based pooling (supervised). The output is a per-query scalar BSS in [0,1], averaged over all queries to yield a single dataset-level score; a score of 1 indicates perfect structural alignment between the LVM's k-NN neighborhood graph and the LLM's reference graph.

## Method

The BSS is inspired by state space modeling and the Boltzmann distribution from statistical mechanics. For each query, k-nearest-neighbor star graphs are constructed in both the text (reference) and vision (observer) embedding spaces. Matching states (present in both graphs) contribute a first-order Boltzmann Factor exp(−‖Lᵢ−L_q‖₂/√d₁), while non-matching states (present in the vision graph but absent from the text graph) contribute a second-order Boltzmann Factor that additionally penalizes the stochastic displacement between the true and estimated states. The per-query score is B_q = 1 − (sum of non-matching state costs)/(sum of all state costs), and the final BSS is the mean over all queries. This formulation makes the score differentiable and model-agnostic, enabling comparison of any LVM against any LLM reference without task-specific training, and the paper demonstrates that BSS correlates significantly with downstream performance in information retrieval and survival prediction.

## Results

On organ-specific information retrieval with original text reports across 32 TCGA cancer datasets (~9,500 patients), Command-R achieves the best Top-1 accuracy of 0.871 and Top-1 F1 of 0.923, with performance remaining robust after removing medical keywords (Top-1 accuracy drops only to 0.848). For survival prediction, LLM encodings yield C-indices ranging from 0.522 (GBM) to 0.721 (KIRP), with Command-R achieving the best average C-index of 0.628. When evaluating seven LVMs via BSS (k=5, averaged over five LLMs), all LVMs score below 0.4 on most cancer datasets, indicating poor semantic alignment with expert pathology reports. BSS shows statistically significant Pearson correlations with downstream task performance, reaching r=0.879 (p=1.4e-23) for Gemma-7b on LUAD retrieval and r=0.373 (p=2.9e-13) for Bio-Llama3-8b on UCEC survival prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five LLMs, Command-R consistently outperforms all others on retrieval (Top-1 accuracy 0.871 vs. 0.860 for Bio-Llama3-8b and 0.852 for Llama3-8b) and survival prediction (average C-index 0.628 vs. 0.621 for Llama3-8b). Notably, the smaller Bio-Llama3-8b (8B parameters, bio-specific) nearly matches the much larger Command-R and Jamba, ranking second on most retrieval metrics. Among the seven LVMs evaluated via BSS, UNI (trained on 100K WSIs, no TCGA data) outperforms the majority of TCGA-pretrained models (CTransPath, Phikon, Lunit-Dino), suggesting training data scale matters more than domain-specific pretraining. ViT (ImageNet-pretrained) performs worst on BSS, confirming that non-medical pretraining yields poor semantic alignment. On survival prediction, LLM encodings outperform LVM encodings on average (e.g., Command-R 0.628 vs. LVM average 0.598 on BRCA), though pathology-pretrained LVMs like UNI and Phikon surpass LLMs on specific cancers like GBM and LGG. LLMs reach substantial Cohen's Kappa agreement (0.61–0.80) in ranking LVMs by BSS, with the strongest consensus on KIRC, BRCA, and LUAD, and weaker agreement on GBM and KIRP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the Boltzmann distribution from statistical mechanics as a structural similarity metric between two embedding spaces (LLM text encodings vs. LVM image encodings), rather than building on a specific prior cross-modal evaluation method. It departs from the standard qualitative evaluation paradigm in digital pathology (attention maps, GradCAM, small-scale expert review) by introducing a large-scale, quantitative, and model-agnostic metric grounded in the TCGA-Reports resource (Kefeli & Tatonetti, 2024). The k-nearest-neighbor graph comparison and the second-order degeneracy term for non-matching states are novel constructions that have no direct precedent in the LVM evaluation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BSS provides the first systematic, quantitative, and collectively-derived measure of whether LVMs capture medically semantically relevant features, replacing biased small-sample expert assessments. The finding that all seven LVMs score below 0.4 BSS (even with supervised pooling) highlights a substantial gap between self-supervised visual encodings and expert textual knowledge, while the demonstrated correlation between BSS and downstream retrieval/survival performance suggests the metric has predictive utility. Key open questions include whether BSS generalizes beyond the two tested tasks and TCGA, whether it can serve as a differentiable training loss to improve LVM semantic alignment, and whether the low scores reflect a fundamental limitation of current LVM architectures or simply insufficient training data.
