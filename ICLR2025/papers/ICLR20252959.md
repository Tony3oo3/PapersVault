## Problem

CLIP's embedding space exhibits a pronounced modality gap, with image and text embeddings densely occupying separate subregions of the hypersphere, rendering the space sparse and disconnected. Previous attempts to close this gap via naive embedding translation distort the relative distances of unpaired samples, harming downstream performance. The paper asks how to reduce the modality gap while preserving the meaningful geometric structure of the embedding space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252959/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB images (16×16 patches) and text sequences up to 77 tokens (vocabulary 49,408), producing 768-dimensional L2-normalized embeddings for each modality. A pre-trained SBERT all-mpnet-base-v2 sentence encoder is additionally invoked during training to compute pairwise semantic distances between paired captions, which modulate the intra-modality separation loss. At inference, the shared encoder outputs cross-modal embeddings used for zero-shot classification (via prompt matching), linear probing, or retrieval ranking.

## Method

AlignCLIP combines two refinements to CLIP's dual-stream architecture. First, SharedCLIP shares the entire transformer encoder and projection layer between the vision and language towers, increasing the inductive bias toward a common embedding region so that the two modalities cannot diverge into disjoint subspaces. Second, an Intra-Modality Separation (IMSep) loss is added: an InfoNCE-style objective whose denominator contains image-image cosine similarities re-scaled by pairwise semantic distances D_ij computed from a frozen SBERT sentence encoder, so that semantically dissimilar images are pushed apart while similar ones remain close. The final training objective is L = L_CRsep + α·L_IMsep (α=0.5), where L_CRsep is the standard symmetric cross-modal contrastive loss. Only the image modality requires explicit IMSep, since the cross-modal loss already regularizes the text side. The model is trained from scratch on CC12M with ViT-B-16 (12 layers, 12 heads) using AdamW, cosine scheduling, and batch size 512 for 30 epochs.

## Results

AlignCLIP achieves the best cross-modal alignment score across all evaluated datasets, ranging from 0.62 to 0.67 (vs. 0.38–0.47 for CLIP), reducing the average angle between paired image-text embeddings from ~70° to ~47°. On zero-shot image classification, AlignCLIP attains 32.8% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K (+1.4% over CLIP), 36.5% on CIFAR-100 (+8.4%), and 69.4% on CIFAR-10 (+7.8%). For robustness to natural distribution shifts, AlignCLIP improves Top-1 accuracy by ~2% over CLIP on ImageNetV2 (29.1% vs. 27.1%) and ImageNet-A (7.0% vs. 6.5%). In fine-tuned retrieval on MSCOCO, AlignCLIP reaches R@1 of 41.7% (I→T) and 27.9% (T→I), outperforming CLIP by 2.1 and 1.2 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), AlignCLIP wins on every zero-shot classification benchmark: +1.4% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K, +8.4% on CIFAR-100, +7.8% on CIFAR-10, and +2.0% on Stanford Cars Top-5. On linear probing, AlignCLIP improves over CLIP by up to 5% (e.g., 67.4% vs. 62.6% on CIFAR-100). Against Naive Shifting (Liang et al., 2022), AlignCLIP achieves a substantially higher alignment score (0.63 vs. 0.16) and better classification accuracy (e.g., 17.6% vs. 16.6% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K with CC3M training), while the paper demonstrates that naive shifting distorts relative distances of unpaired samples. Against CyCLIP (Goel et al., 2022), AlignCLIP outperforms on alignment (0.63 vs. 0.32) and on all classification datasets (e.g., 17.6% vs. 15.0% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K, 57.9% vs. 44.7% on CIFAR-10). Notably, SharedCLIP alone (parameter sharing without IMSep) sometimes underperforms CLIP on fine-grained datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100 drops to 26.4%), indicating that the IMSep loss is critical for consistent gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP's dual-stream contrastive architecture by sharing the transformer encoder and projection layer between the vision and language towers, building on the parameter-sharing idea explored by You et al. (2022) but extending it to include the projection head. It departs from the naive embedding-shift approach of Liang et al. (2022) and Schrodi et al. (2024) by demonstrating that such translations distort relative distances of unpaired samples, and instead introduces a semantically-regularized intra-modality separation loss (IMSep) that uses a frozen SBERT sentence encoder to modulate pairwise image-image repulsion. The method thus combines an architectural inductive bias (shared parameters) with a new contrastive objective, positioning itself between pure architecture modifications and pure loss-function changes in the CLIP improvement literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AlignCLIP provides a principled, training-time solution to the modality gap problem that has been widely observed but previously addressed only via post-hoc embedding shifts. The consistent improvements across zero-shot classification, distribution-shift robustness, and retrieval suggest the gap reduction is not merely cosmetic but functionally beneficial. However, the approach is limited to English, depends on the choice of pre-trained sentence encoder for semantic regularization, and the paper acknowledges that exact modality alignment may be suboptimal (per Jiang et al., 2023), leaving open the question of the optimal gap size. Multilingual extension and exploration of whether image-based semantic supervision could replace the text-based SBERT signal are natural next steps.
