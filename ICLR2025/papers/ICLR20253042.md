## Problem

Federated Class-Incremental Learning (FCIL) suffers from two distinct forgetting mechanisms—local forgetting due to within-client class imbalance and global forgetting due to cross-client class imbalance—that existing exemplar-based or data-free approaches address only partially. Exemplar-based methods incur high memory costs and privacy risks, while data-free generative methods underperform, and no prior work provides a unified mathematical framework that simultaneously formalizes and tackles both forgetting types under federated constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for ImageNet-Subset and TinyImageNet) arriving in sequential tasks, where each client holds only a subset of classes at any time. Classification is performed entirely in the encoder's latent space by computing the Euclidean distance between the encoded input and stored class centroid embeddings, outputting the nearest centroid's class label. No raw images are stored at inference; only low-dimensional latent exemplars and centroid vectors are retained, reducing memory by roughly an order of magnitude compared to raw exemplar storage.

## Method

HR builds on a customized autoencoder (ResNet-18 encoder + 4-layer CNN decoder) trained with a modified VAE objective that adds a centroid-clustering loss to enforce class-specific structure in the latent space. The server aligns new-task class centroids across all clients using Lennard-Jones Potential energy minimization, ensuring globally separated embeddings that prevent inter-client task confusion. At each client, local forgetting is mitigated by decoding stored latent exemplars back to input space for replay, while global forgetting is addressed by perturbing class centroids with Gaussian noise and decoding them to synthesize data representing other clients' tasks. Knowledge distillation losses on both encoder and decoder outputs further constrain weight drift across tasks, and a fixed-memory exemplar update policy keeps storage bounded as tasks accumulate.

## Results

HR achieves the highest accuracy across all five FCIL configurations: 65.84% on CIFAR-100 (10/10/50/5), 60.48% on CIFAR-100 (20/5/50/5), 56.48% on ImageNet-Subset (10/20/100/10), 52.35% on ImageNet-Subset (20/10/100/10), and 57.86% on TinyImageNet (10/5/300/30). These results surpass the best exemplar-based baseline (GLFC) by 3.5–4.1 points and the best hybrid baseline (REMIND+) by 2.0–2.5 points. Even with 10× less memory (HR-mini), HR still outperforms all data-free baselines by 5–10 points, demonstrating strong memory efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HR outperforms all 14 baselines across every FCIL configuration. Against the strongest exemplar-based method GLFC (61.83% on CIFAR-100 10/10/50/5), HR leads by 4.0 points; against the best data-free method MFCL (50.03%), the margin is 15.8 points. Among hybrid baselines, REMIND+ is the closest competitor at 63.47% on CIFAR-100 (10/10/50/5), with HR ahead by 2.4 points; i-CTRL (63.31%) and REMIND (62.29%) trail by 2.5 and 3.6 points respectively. On the longer-sequence CIFAR-100 (20/5/50/5) setting, HR's advantage over REMIND+ narrows to 0.9 points (60.48 vs. 59.61), while on TinyImageNet (10/5/300/30) the gap widens to 1.9 points (57.86 vs. 55.92). Notably, even HR-mini with 10× less memory (60.42% on CIFAR-100 10/10/50/5) still exceeds all exemplar-based baselines, confirming that the latent-space storage strategy provides a favorable accuracy–memory trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work in FCIL—exemplar-based replay (iCaRL, GLFC, REMIND, i-CTRL) and data-free synthetic generation (MFCL, TARGET, FedFTG)—into a single autoencoder-based framework. It builds on the VAE architecture (Kingma & Welling, 2013) but repurposes it for dual classification-in-latent-space and synthetic data generation, and introduces the Lennard-Jones potential (Jones, 1924) as a physics-inspired mechanism for globally aligning class centroid embeddings across clients. The fixed-memory exemplar management follows Rebuffi et al. (2017), while the federated aggregation uses standard FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017). Conceptually, HR departs from prior hybrid methods (REMIND, REMIND+, i-CTRL) by performing classification directly in the encoder's latent space via Euclidean distance to aligned centroids rather than after decoding, and by using a single autoencoder for both replay and generation rather than separate components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HR provides a principled, mathematically grounded solution to FCIL that simultaneously addresses local and global forgetting with low memory overhead, making it practical for resource-constrained federated settings. The ablation showing that compressed latent exemplars perform nearly identically to perfect exemplars reinforces that replay quality is less critical than coverage, which has broader implications for memory-efficient continual learning. Key open questions include the decoder design trade-off between replay fidelity and privacy (an overly powerful decoder risks memorizing training data), scalability to very long task sequences, and whether the Lennard-Jones alignment generalizes to non-uniform class distributions or asynchronous client participation. The approach's reliance on a four-layer CNN decoder for generation also limits its applicability to high-resolution or complex visual domains.
