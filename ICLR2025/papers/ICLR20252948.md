## Problem

Existing offline black-box optimization benchmarks (notably Design-Bench) provide fixed, artificially constructed narrow distributions and lack any indicator to evaluate whether an algorithm stably surpasses the offline dataset throughout the optimization process. Because surrogate models are increasingly misled in regions uncovered by the narrow offline data, algorithms can find a good solution at the final step yet degrade severely mid-optimization—a failure mode that no prior benchmark captures. This paper is the first to formalize stability as a core evaluation objective alongside optimality for offline BBO.

## Input / Output

Each task provides a ground-truth oracle objective function f(x) over a d-dimensional solution space, plus a uniformly sampled initial dataset from which users construct narrow-distribution offline datasets by removing the top-n% (near optimal) and bottom-m% (near worst) solutions, yielding a middle 50% baseline or any custom split. The benchmark also supports constrained variants (with a 2:3 feasible-to-infeasible ratio by default) and noisy datasets. Evaluation outputs the Stability-Optimality indicator SO (harmonic mean of OI and SI), the weighted variant SO_ω with a linearly decreasing weight ω(t)=max(0,1−t/(T+1)), the Final Score at a chosen step t, and per-step OI/SI curves over T=150 optimization steps.

## Method

SOO-Bench contributes three interlocking components. First, a customizable narrow-distribution construction: an initial dataset is uniformly sampled and evaluated, sorted by objective value, then trimmed by removing n% near the optimum and m% near the worst value to produce datasets of controlled difficulty. Second, the Stability-Optimality indicator, defined as SO = 2·OI·SI/(OI+SI), where OI = S/S₁ measures cumulative performance relative to the offline optimum and SI = S/S₂ measures performance relative to the algorithm's own best found solution; a weighted variant SO_ω allows users to trade off optimality versus stability, with a default linearly decreasing ω(t) that prioritizes OI early and SI late in optimization. Third, an extensive task suite spanning four benchmarks—GTOPX (7 satellite trajectory tasks), CEC (5 industrial/design constrained problems), and PROTEIN (2 DNA sequence tasks)—covering discrete and continuous spaces, constrained and unconstrained settings, and dimensions from 2 to 26. Baseline and SOTA algorithms (BO-qEI, CMA-ES, CbAS, ARCOO, Tri-mentoring, TTDDEA, CCDDEA, CARCOO, CCOMs, DDEA-PF/SPF) are evaluated by training surrogate models on the offline data and running T=150 gradient-ascent steps, with performance recorded at each step to compute the stability metrics.

## Results

On the unconstrained GTOPX tasks (128 solutions, 100th percentile, t=50 out of T=150 steps), TTDDEA achieves the highest SO score of 19.11 on GTOPX 2 (FS=-245.69), while ARCOO and Tri-mentoring show the most consistent performance across all three GTOPX tasks. On constrained GTOPX tasks, CARCOO achieves the best SO of 0.99±0.02 on GTOPX 5, substantially outperforming DDEA-PF (SO=0.69±0.35) and DDEA-SPF (SO=0.50±0.58). Across all tasks, most algorithms experience performance degradation from 100 to 150 optimization steps, with ARCOO maintaining the most stable improvement trajectory due to its energy-based risk suppression mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unconstrained baselines, TTDDEA achieves the highest SO (19.11) on GTOPX 2 and competitive results on GTOPX 4 (18.78), but degrades on GTOPX 3 (13.44), matching CMA-ES. Tri-mentoring shows strong SO on GTOPX 2 (18.33) and GTOPX 3 (14.84) but collapses on GTOPX 4 (1.17), indicating sensitivity to task complexity. BO-qEI and CMA-ES, despite being classical online methods adapted for offline use, remain competitive with dedicated offline BBO methods, consistent with Design-Bench findings. Autofocusing CbAS is the weakest performer, essentially returning the offline optimum (SO=10.00 across all tasks) without improving beyond the dataset. For constrained tasks, CARCOO clearly dominates with SO=0.99±0.02 on GTOPX 5, while DDEA-PF (0.69) and DDEA-SPF (0.50) suffer from poor feasibility maintenance, and CCOMs stagnates due to overly strict constraint handling. The paper concludes that no single algorithm dominates all tasks, and simply adapting unconstrained offline methods to constrained settings is insufficient for complex satellite missions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a direct successor to Design-Bench (Trabucco et al., 2022), the only prior comprehensive benchmark for offline black-box optimization, which it identifies as no longer maintained and limited by its fixed, artificially constructed narrow distributions and absence of a stability evaluation metric. Conceptually, the paper departs from the prior offline BBO literature (COMs, CbAS, TTDDEA, ARCOO, Tri-mentoring) by elevating 'stability'—the ability to consistently surpass the offline dataset throughout optimization—co-equal with optimality as a core evaluation objective, a framing the authors claim is novel. The benchmark repurposes existing real-world task suites (GTOPX, CEC, PROTEIN) but adds a new axis of control (customizable n%/m% distribution trimming) and a new composite metric (the SO indicator) that no prior benchmark provided.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOO-Bench fills a genuine gap by giving the offline BBO community a standardized, customizable way to stress-test algorithm stability under varying degrees of data sparsity, which is arguably the central practical challenge in deploying these methods. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: the task variety is still narrow (no robotic control or other domains), the SO indicator is one of potentially many reasonable trade-off formulations, and the simulated narrow distributions may not fully capture the complexity of real-world data collection biases. Open questions include whether the linearly-decreasing weight function ω(t) is the right default, how the benchmark scales to higher-dimensional or mixed-integer problems, and whether the stability metric generalizes to multi-objective or sequential decision-making settings.
