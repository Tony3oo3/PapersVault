## Problem

Label smoothing (LS) is a widely used regularisation technique that improves classification accuracy, yet prior work has empirically observed it degrades selective classification (SC) — the ability to reject likely misclassifications using model uncertainty — without explaining the underlying mechanism. Additionally, while post-hoc logit normalisation has been shown to partially recover SC performance for LS-trained models, the reason for its effectiveness remained unclear. This paper addresses both gaps by providing a gradient-level explanation for LS's negative effect on SC and elucidating why logit normalisation compensates for it.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard inputs across modalities: 224×224 RGB images for ImageNet classification, 768×768 random crops for Cityscapes segmentation, 32×32 images for CIFAR, GloVe-embedded token sequences for IMDB, and raw feature vectors for tabular data. The output is a standard class label (or per-pixel segmentation mask) paired with a binary accept/reject decision, determined by comparing a scalar uncertainty score (negative max softmax probability) against a user-set threshold τ. The critical diagnostic quantity is the relative uncertainty ranking across samples, which governs which predictions are accepted versus rejected at a given coverage level on the risk-coverage curve.

## Method

The core contribution is a logit-level gradient analysis showing that the LS loss introduces an imbalanced suppression on the max logit: the suppression gradient ∂L_sup/∂v_max = α[1−P_error] − α/K is stronger when a prediction is likely correct (low P_error) and weaker when likely wrong (high P_error), degrading the uncertainty ranking of correct versus incorrect predictions. This is validated empirically by showing LS-trained models exhibit higher v_max at the same MSP for errors compared to correct predictions, and that the effect is exacerbated on distribution-shifted data (ImageNet-Sketch) where the model is less well-fit. To recover SC performance, the paper applies post-hoc p-norm logit normalisation at inference time and proves (Result 1) that for positive logits, the normalised max logit v'_max strictly decreases as the original v_max increases for the same softmax output — effectively reversing the imbalanced suppression by increasing uncertainty precisely where LS made the model overconfident on errors.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-50, label smoothing catastrophically degrades selective classification: coverage at 1% risk drops from 15.66% (CE) to 0.05–0.06% (LS α=0.1–0.2), rendering LS models effectively useless under strict error tolerances. Post-hoc logit normalisation recovers this loss, boosting coverage@1% risk to 22.31% (exceeding CE). On ImageNet+Sketch at 10% coverage, LS α=0.3 increases the error rate on distribution-shifted Sketch data from 6.4% (CE) to 27.4%, confirming that LS causes increasing overconfidence on less-well-fit data. The degradation is consistent across ResNet-50, ViT-S-16, DeepLabV3+, DenseNet, MLPs, and LSTMs on image, segmentation, tabular, and NLP tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between CE-trained and LS-trained models: LS consistently degrades SC across all architectures (ResNet-50, ViT-S-16, DeepLabV3+, DenseNet, MLP, LSTM) and tasks (ImageNet, Cityscapes, CIFAR-100, tabular, IMDB), with stronger α causing greater degradation. Logit normalisation (Cattelan & Silva, 2024) is shown to effectively recover SC performance for LS-trained models (e.g., 22.31% vs 0.06% coverage@1% risk on ImageNet) but provides little to no benefit for CE-trained models, confirming that the fix targets the specific imbalanced logit suppression introduced by LS. Among alternative uncertainty scores, DOCTOR (Granese et al., 2021) and entropy degrade similarly to MSP under LS, while the Energy score (Liu et al., 2020) significantly underperforms MSP at SC regardless of LS, consistent with prior findings that OOD-detection scores are unsuitable for selective classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the empirical observations of Zhu et al. (2022; 2024) that label smoothing degrades selective classification for CNNs, validating the effect across a broader range of architectures (ResNet-50, ViT-S-16, DeepLabV3+, DenseNet) and tasks (image classification, semantic segmentation, tabular, NLP). It builds on the logit normalisation approach proposed by Cattelan & Silva (2024) as a post-hoc fix, and on the large-scale benchmarking of Galil et al. (2023) and Cattelan & Silva (2024) that revealed strong classifiers surprisingly underperforming at SC. The key departure is providing the first mechanistic explanation via logit-level gradient analysis, showing that LS introduces an imbalanced suppression of the max logit that depends on the true probability of error, thereby degrading the uncertainty ranking of correct vs. incorrect predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since label smoothing is a near-universal component of modern ImageNet training recipes (EfficientNet, ViT, ConvNeXt, etc.), this work provides a concrete explanation for why many widely-used pre-trained classifiers exhibit poor selective classification performance — a previously unexplained phenomenon. The practical implication is that practitioners deploying models for safety-critical rejection tasks must either avoid LS or apply the simple post-hoc logit normalisation fix. Open questions include the interaction with other regularisation techniques like Mixup (which Zhu et al. also observe harms SC), the effect of LS on OOD detection scores, and whether the gradient-based explanation extends to other loss modifications or training paradigms.
