## Problem

Simply enlarging kernel sizes in historically successful CNNs (AlexNet, VGG, ResNet) degrades performance — the so-called large kernel problem — yet modern architectures like RepLKNet succeed with kernels up to 50×50. Additionally, CNNs use rectangular, dense, uniform connectivity while biological V1 neurons exhibit circular, sparse, Gaussian-distributed connectivity, and it remains unexplored whether bridging this gap could simultaneously resolve the large kernel problem and improve alignment with brain representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for TinyImageNet) or 9×9 Sudoku grids and produces class labels, solved grids, or neural activity predictions. A distinctive aspect is that each output channel receives its own independently learned Lp-mask (a 2D soft mask of the same spatial size as the kernel), so the effective receptive field shape varies per channel. For transfer learning, pretrained convolutional weights are embedded in a zero-padded enlarged kernel with a high initial p to preserve the original model's behavior before the mask adapts.

## Method

Lp-convolution overlays a channel-wise trainable soft mask, derived from the multivariate p-generalized normal distribution (MPND), onto each convolutional filter: M_{o,h,w} = exp(−‖C_o ΔS_{h,w}‖_p^p), where C_o is a per-channel 2×2 covariance matrix and p ≥ 1 is a per-channel scalar, both learned end-to-end. The parameter p controls the mask's conformation — p=2 yields a Gaussian (biologically faithful) shape, while large p approaches a binary square mask (reducing to standard convolution) — and C controls scale, rotation, and distortion via its singular value decomposition. The key empirical finding is that p_init=2 (Gaussian structured sparsity) consistently outperforms other p values across architectures and tasks, suggesting biological connectivity patterns serve as a beneficial inductive bias. The method is a drop-in replacement for any Conv2d layer (via an LpConvert function), requires no architectural redesign, and adds negligible computational cost (e.g., RepLKNet gains +0.9% top-1 with identical FLOPs). In the Sudoku task, ablation of orientation-selective masks (high distortion, ~90°) in later layers sharply reduces column accuracy, confirming that the masks adapt their conformation task-dependently to balance competing spatial constraints.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, Lp-Convolution with p_init=2 (Gaussian-like, biologically inspired) achieves the best or second-best top-1 accuracy in 9 out of 10 model-dataset combinations, e.g., 74.95% on ResNet-34 (vs. 72.47% base) and 72.88% on VGG-16 (vs. 70.26% base). Simply enlarging kernel sizes (the 'Large' baseline) consistently degrades performance, dropping to 54.53% on AlexNet and 54.13% on ConvNeXt-T. On TinyImageNet, Lp-Conv(p=2) similarly improves all architectures, reaching 70.72% on ConvNeXt-T and 69.96% on VGG-16. In transfer learning (16-shot, 5 datasets), Lp-Conv(p=16) on pretrained ConvNeXt-V2 achieves 73.94% average accuracy vs. 73.68% for the base model, while simple kernel enlargement collapses to 19.24%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lp-Conv(p=2) consistently outperforms the base CNNs: VGG-16 improves from 70.26% to 72.88% (+2.62), ResNet-18 from 71.22% to 73.86% (+2.64), ResNet-34 from 72.47% to 74.95% (+2.48), and ConvNeXt-T from 58.36% to 62.61% (+4.25) on CIFAR-100. The 'Large' kernel baseline (simply doubling kernel size) is the clearest loser, dropping 11.5 points on AlexNet and 4.2 points on ConvNeXt-T, confirming the large kernel problem. On modern large-kernel CNNs, Lp2-RepLKNet improves over RepLKNet from 66.2% to 67.1% on TinyImageNet with negligible FLOP increase (2.54G both). In transfer learning, Lp-Conv(p=16) on pretrained ConvNeXt-V2 wins 4 out of 5 datasets (73.94% avg vs. 73.68% base), while Large Conv catastrophically fails (19.24% avg). For V1 neural activity prediction (MMV1), Lp(p=2) achieves the best cross-correlation (0.595 vs. 0.585 base) and lowest MSE (0.0722 vs. 0.0746). In the broader architecture comparison (Table 9), Lp-Conv(p=2) improves ResNeXt-50 from 73.55% to 77.38% and DenseNet-121 from 74.12% to 77.14% on CIFAR-100, with all improvements statistically significant (p<0.001).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multivariate p-generalized normal distribution (MPND, Goodman & Kotz 1973) as a mathematical bridge between the circular, sparse, Gaussian-distributed connectivity observed in biological V1 neurons and the rectangular, dense, uniform connectivity of standard CNNs. It departs from prior large-kernel CNN work (RepLKNet, SLaK) that relies on unstructured sparsity by introducing structured, trainable Lp-masks whose conformation (scale, rotation, distortion) adapts to the task. The RSA methodology follows established neuroAI protocols (Khaligh-Razavi & Kriegeskorte 2014; Bakhtiari et al. 2021), while the Sudoku diagnostic experiment draws on prior Sudoku-CNN literature to probe mechanistic properties of the learned masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, drop-in mechanism (LpConvert) to make large kernels work in historically successful CNNs without the performance collapse that naive kernel enlargement causes, with modest but consistent gains (1–3%) across AlexNet, VGG, ResNet, and ConvNeXt. The RSA finding that p≈2 (Gaussian) yields better visual-cortex alignment is a concrete, testable link between a hyperparameter and biological fidelity, though it rests on standard RSA with known limitations. Open questions include whether the gains persist at ImageNet scale, whether the conformational adaptability generalizes beyond 2D spatial masks to other structured sparsity patterns, and whether the biological-alignment claim translates into downstream task benefits beyond classification accuracy.
