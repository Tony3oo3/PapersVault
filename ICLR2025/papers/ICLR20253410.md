## Problem

Perplexity (PPL), the de facto metric for evaluating LLMs, shows almost no correlation with actual long-context benchmark performance (e.g., LongBench, RULER). The paper identifies the root cause: PPL averages uniformly over all tokens, but the vast majority of tokens in natural text are long-context-agnostic, while only a small fraction of 'key tokens' genuinely depend on long-range information. This dilution obscures the model's true long-context capability.

## Input / Output

For LongPPL evaluation, the method takes a long text sequence (up to 32k tokens, e.g., from GovReport) and a target language model to evaluate, plus a separate evaluator model (e.g., Qwen2-72B-Instruct or Llama-3.1-8B) that computes per-token Long-Short Difference (LSD) and Long-Context Likelihood (LCL) scores using a truncated short context of length K=4096. The output is a single perplexity score computed only over tokens exceeding thresholds α=2 (LSD) and β=-2 (LCL). For LongCE training, the input is a 32k-token sequence during fine-tuning, and the output is a reweighted cross-entropy loss where each token's weight is min(exp(LSD), γ) with γ=5, computed via a sliding-window forward pass for efficiency.

## Method

The core mechanism is a long-short context contrastive intervention: for each token x_i, the Long-Short Difference (LSD) measures the log-probability gap between prediction under the full long context l_i and a truncated short context s_i of length K, effectively estimating the individual treatment effect of long context via causal intervention. A second criterion, Long-Context Likelihood (LCL), excludes tokens that remain fundamentally unpredictable even with long context. LongPPL then computes perplexity exclusively over tokens satisfying both LSD > α and LCL > β, using an external evaluator model to ensure accurate key-token identification (achieving 98.2% accuracy on LongEval answer tokens). For training, LongCE replaces uniform cross-entropy with a soft reweighting I_soft = min(P_θ(x_i|l_i)/P_θ(x_i|s_i), γ), which upweights long-context-dependent tokens while keeping easy and hard tokens near weight 1. This creates an EM-style bootstrap where the model estimates its own key tokens and then optimizes on them, requiring no external evaluator and adding only ~80% computational overhead over standard CE training.

## Results

LongPPL achieves Pearson correlations of −0.96 (LongBench), −0.86 (LongEval), and −0.84 (RULER) with long-context benchmark scores, compared to standard PPL's near-zero correlations (−0.11, +0.31, +0.33). For training, LongCE consistently outperforms standard CE loss across all settings, with a maximum accuracy gain of +22.0 points on LongEval (Llama-2-7B, PG-19, EABF, 100/200 steps) and +11.5 points on RULER (Llama-2-13B, Pile-arxiv, EABF, 100 steps). The LongCE training overhead is controlled to ~80% of CE via a sliding-window technique, reducible to 36% with adjusted hyperparameters. LongCE does not degrade non-long-context performance (e.g., MMLU, ARC-C, RACE scores remain within 1 point of CE baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is standard Cross-Entropy (CE) loss for long-context fine-tuning and standard Perplexity (PPL) for evaluation. LongCE outperforms CE across all three experimental settings (PG-19+EABF, PG-19+PI, Pile-arxiv+EABF) and all three benchmarks, with the largest gains on LongEval (+16 to +22 points) and RULER (+0.7 to +11.5 points), while LongBench gains are more modest (+0.1 to +2.7 points). The gains are consistent across base models: Mistral-7B-v0.1 shows +10 to +16 points on LongEval, and Llama-2-13B shows +4 to +6 points on LongEval and +9.7 to +11.5 points on RULER. For the evaluation metric, LongPPL (hard indicator) decisively outperforms both standard PPL and the soft-reweighting variant (LongPPL-soft) in correlation with benchmark scores (e.g., −0.96 vs. −0.11 vs. −0.43 on LongBench). The paper also shows that using the model itself as the evaluator for LongPPL fails (all models get ~1.65–1.92), confirming the need for an external evaluator, and that re-occurred N-gram selection is a much weaker proxy for key tokens (−0.41 vs. −0.96 correlation on LongBench).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the observation by Hu et al. (2024a) and Hsieh et al. (2024) that perplexity poorly correlates with long-context benchmark scores, but provides the first mechanistic explanation: PPL fails because it averages over tokens that are largely long-context-agnostic. The key-token identification via Long-Short Difference (LSD) draws on causal inference (individual treatment effect estimation), while the LongCE loss is conceptually related to token re-weighting strategies in NMT (Gu et al., 2020) and the more recent LongRecipe (Hu et al., 2024b), though it targets long-context dependence rather than distribution shift. The paper also positions itself against N-gram retrieval explanations (Sun et al., 2021; Arora et al., 2024) by showing that re-occurred N-gram PPL does not capture long-context ability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since PPL is the de facto standard for reporting LLM quality, demonstrating that it is essentially uninformative for long-context capability (correlation near zero) while a simple re-weighting of the same log-likelihoods recovers a -0.96 correlation is a significant methodological correction for the field. LongCE offers a plug-and-play training improvement (up to 22% on LongEval) with modest overhead (~80% of CE cost), making it immediately adoptable. Open questions include whether the key-token framework generalizes to multimodal or non-text long-context settings, whether the external evaluator requirement for LongPPL can be eliminated, and whether the approach transfers to pre-training rather than fine-tuning.
