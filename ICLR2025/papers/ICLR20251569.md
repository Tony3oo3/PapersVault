## Problem

While the optimal batch-regret tradeoff for multi-armed bandits was fully characterized by Gao et al. (2019), the analogous question for linear contextual bandits remained open. Prior work (Ruan et al., 2021) showed that O(log log T) batches suffice to recover the unconstrained minimax-optimal regret, but the exact regret achievable with an arbitrary number M of batches across all parameter regimes (T, d, K) was unknown. This paper closes that gap by establishing matching upper and lower bounds (up to logarithmic factors) for the full parameter range.

## Input / Output

At each time step the learner receives a context set of K feature vectors in R^d drawn i.i.d. from an unknown distribution D, and must select one arm to play, receiving a linear reward plus sub-Gaussian noise. The algorithm is constrained by a batch structure: at the start of each of M batches, it must commit to both a batch length and a policy using only data from prior batches, with no intra-batch feedback. The output is a sequence of M exploration policies (each a mapping from context sets to distributions over arms) and a regret guarantee that takes the minimum of two terms depending on whether T is small or large relative to d·min{K,d}^{2−2^{−M+2}}.

## Method

The algorithm is elimination-based: it maintains elliptical confidence intervals via ridge regression, eliminates arms whose intervals are strictly dominated, and plays from the survivors according to a learned exploration policy. The key technical contribution is the Exp-Policy procedure (Algorithm 2), which simulates a reward-free LinUCB on a smaller dataset from the previous batch to construct the next batch's policy. Two innovations make this work: (1) a scaled-and-clipped update rule that clips each feature vector by the current information matrix, improving the dependence on the previous batch size m from √(d/m) to d/m (critical since m ≪ n in the batch setting), and (2) a new matrix concentration inequality with dynamic upper bounds (Lemma 20) that handles the fact that the clipping threshold itself is a random variable. The batch schedule is designed in two cases—when T is small (giving a T^{1/(2−2^{−M+2})} regret) and when T is large (matching the multi-armed bandit exponent T^{1/(2−2^{−M+1})} with an additional min{K,d}^{2^{−M+1}} factor)—and the resulting upper bound is shown tight by a lower bound construction using M independent sub-problems with a context-blind hard instance for the second batch.

## Results

The paper establishes a matching upper and lower bound (up to logarithmic factors) for the batch-regret tradeoff in linear contextual bandits. Theorem 2 shows that Algorithm 1 achieves regret Õ(min{T^{1/(2−2^{−M+2})}·d^{(1−2^{−M+2})/(2−2^{−M+2})}, T^{1/(2−2^{−M+1})}·d^{(1−2^{−M+1})/(2−2^{−M+1})}·min{K,d}^{2^{−M+1}/(2−2^{−M+1})}}), and Theorem 4 provides a matching lower bound for all M ≥ 1, T ≥ d. As a corollary, M = ⌈log log T⌉ + 1 batches suffice to recover the unconstrained minimax-optimal regret Õ(√(Td)), extending the applicability to all T ≥ d (whereas prior work required T ≥ Õ(d^{32})).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Ruan et al. (2021, STOC), this paper's algorithm works for all T ≥ d rather than requiring T ≥ Õ(d^{32}), and improves the single-phase exploration bound from √(d/m) to d/m dependence on the previous-batch sample size m, which is critical for achieving the optimal regret curve. Against Zanette et al. (2021, NeurIPS), the Exp-Policy algorithm strictly improves the minimax expected maximum width bound, replacing their √(d/m) term with d/m, which the authors note is essential for recovering the optimal M = Θ(log log T) batch result. The lower bound (Theorem 4) is strictly stronger than both Gao et al. (2019, NeurIPS) for multi-armed bandits and Han et al. (2020) for Gaussian-context linear bandits, particularly in the regime d ≤ T ≤ d·min{K,d}^{2−2^{−M+2}}, demonstrating additional intrinsic difficulty of the general stochastic-context setting. The paper also introduces a new matrix concentration inequality with dynamic upper bounds (Lemma 20), which the authors note is the first of its kind in the literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the elimination-based batch linear contextual bandit framework of Ruan et al. (2021) and the reward-free LinUCB design-of-experiments approach of Zanette et al. (2021) to establish the full-parameter-range optimal batch-regret tradeoff. The key technical departure is a scaled-and-clipped update rule in the exploration policy that improves the single-phase learning bound from √(d/m) to d/m, which is critical when the previous batch is much smaller than the current one. The paper also generalizes beyond the Gaussian-context restriction of Han et al. (2020) to arbitrary stochastic context distributions, and introduces a new matrix concentration inequality with dynamic upper bounds as an independent analytical tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By proving matching upper and lower bounds (up to logarithmic factors) for all M, T, d, and K, this paper closes the open problem of characterizing the exact batch-regret tradeoff for linear contextual bandits, completing the picture that Gao et al. (2019) established for the simpler multi-armed bandit case. The two-phase structure of the optimal regret—where a simpler analysis dominates for small T and the batch-mimicking bound dominates for large T—reveals an intrinsic difficulty of general context distributions that was not captured by prior lower bounds. The dynamic matrix concentration inequality is noted as potentially of independent interest for other online learning analyses. Open directions include extending these techniques to adaptive contexts, linear MDPs, and other linear-reward decision-making problems.
