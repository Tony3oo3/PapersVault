## Problem

Existing Vision-Language Generalists (VLGs) apply a single unified transformer architecture with shared parameters to both text and images, ignoring the fundamental inductive bias mismatch: text is inherently sequential and one-dimensional, while images are two-dimensional with strong local spatial priors. This leads to poor interleaved generation quality, including text repetition and image distortion. Additionally, current VLGs lack instruction-following capability for interleaved generation because they are only instruction-tuned on single-modality outputs, and no large-scale interleaved instruction dataset exists.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text instruction and an interleaved context of text segments and images, then autoregressively generates a response that alternates between text tokens and fixed-length (H×W) image patch sequences, delimited by special <IMG> and </IMG> tokens. The model autonomously decides when to switch between text and image generation modes during inference, and the process terminates upon producing an end-of-generation token. Images can be represented either as discrete VQGAN tokens (Chameleon) or continuous CLIP embeddings (Emu2), and the framework handles both without architectural changes.

## Method

MOSS introduces two modality-specialized LoRA variants injected into the frozen VLG backbone: a standard Linear LoRA for text hidden states and a novel Convolutional LoRA for image hidden states. The ConvLoRA performs a k×k convolution directly in the original feature space (avoiding the information loss of prior designs that reduce dimension before convolving), with a causal constraint where the kernel only covers top and left neighbors to respect autoregressive ordering; zero-padding on the top and left preserves the H×W spatial shape. During training, the interleaved hidden state sequence is split into text and image subsequences, each routed exclusively to its corresponding LoRA, so that only modality-specific parameters are updated while the pretrained cross-modal integration in the backbone remains intact. The method is trained on LEAFINSTRUCT, a newly curated 184,982-instance interleaved instruction dataset spanning 10+ domains, and demonstrates strong generalizability across both continuous-token (Emu2) and discrete-token (Chameleon) VLG backbones.

## Results

On InterleavedBench (context-based split, 465 instances), Emu2+MOSS achieves 2.61 Text Quality, 3.62 Perceptual Quality, 3.41 Image Coherence, 3.54 Text-Image Coherence (TIC), and 2.71 Helpfulness (scale 0–5), improving over the original Emu2 by up to 190.2% on TIC and 97.8% on average across all five aspects. Chameleon+MOSS shows even larger relative gains on image-related metrics (e.g., +275% Image Coherence, +261.7% TIC) but a slight −10.5% drop in Text Quality due to reduced verbosity. On MagicBrush image editing, Emu2+MOSS achieves 62.26 average (CLIP-I, DINO, CLIP-T), surpassing SEED-X-Edit (60.53). On MSCOCO-30K text-to-image generation, Emu2+MOSS achieves FID 18.2, outperforming Emu2+MoE-LoRA (22.7) and Emu2+LoRA (23.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Emu2+MOSS outperforms all open-source VLG baselines: it beats GILL (the previous open-source SOTA) by 34.7% on average across five aspects, with particularly large margins on Image Coherence (+37.8%) and TIC (+31.6%), though GILL retains a slight edge on Perceptual Quality (4.02 vs. 3.62). Against MiniGPT-5, MOSS wins on every metric, most dramatically on Image Coherence (3.41 vs. 2.06) and Helpfulness (2.71 vs. 1.76). Compared to its own backbone Emu2, MOSS nearly doubles performance on TIC (3.54 vs. 1.22) and Image Coherence (3.41 vs. 1.71). Chameleon+MOSS dramatically rescues the original Chameleon's near-zero image generation (Image Coherence 0.28 → 1.05, TIC 0.47 → 1.70) at the cost of a small Text Quality drop (3.33 → 2.98), which the authors attribute to LLM-judge verbosity bias rather than genuine quality loss (human evaluation shows 28 wins vs. 18 losses). Against proprietary pipelines, a notable gap remains: Gemini 1.5+SDXL leads on Text Quality (3.37 vs. 2.61) and Helpfulness (3.28 vs. 2.71), while GPT-4o+DALL-E 3 leads on Perceptual Quality (4.44 vs. 3.62) and TIC (4.39 vs. 3.54). Among PEFT ablations, MOSS consistently beats both traditional LoRA and MoE-LoRA across all five aspects at every tested rank (32–256), with the gap widening as rank increases; on MagicBrush, Emu2+MOSS (62.26 avg) also surpasses SEED-X-Edit (60.53 avg).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm by introducing modality-specialized variants: a standard Linear LoRA for text and a novel Convolutional LoRA (improved from Zhong et al., 2024) for image patches, both injected into frozen autoregressive VLGs like Emu2 and Chameleon. It departs from prior modality-aware approaches (e.g., MoE-LoRA, Akbari et al.) that separate parameters by modality but still apply the same linear architecture to both, arguing that images require a convolutional inductive bias to model local patch priors that transformers handle poorly. The paper also introduces LEAFINSTRUCT, the first open-sourced instruction-tuning dataset where both inputs and outputs contain interleaved text and multiple images, filling a gap left by existing datasets (LLaVA, InstructPix2Pix, Mantis-Instruct) that only produce single-modality outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modality-specialized architecture provides a practical, low-cost recipe (rank-256 LoRA on frozen 33B backbones) that substantially improves interleaved generation quality, and the LEAFINSTRUCT dataset (185K instances) enables the community to train and evaluate interleaved instruction-following at scale. A key open question is whether the convolutional inductive bias generalizes beyond 2D image patches to other spatially-structured modalities (e.g., video, audio spectrograms), and whether the approach can close the remaining gap to proprietary pipelines (Gemini 1.5 + SDXL, GPT-4o + DALL-E 3), which still lead by a notable margin on most metrics. The paper also highlights a subtle evaluation issue: LLM-based judges exhibit verbosity bias, causing Chameleon+MOSS to appear worse on text quality than the original despite human evaluators preferring the more concise outputs.
