## Problem

Reconstructing high-quality 3D open scenes from images remains challenging because existing methods require precise camera poses and dense viewpoint supervision, while standard SfM pipelines like COLMAP scale poorly (exceeding 24 hours and failing on 2k+ images) and 3DGS overfits Gaussian points to sparse viewpoints in large outdoor scenes. This paper tackles the gap of performing efficient, accurate large-scale 3D reconstruction from uncalibrated image collections without ground-truth poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251760/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of RGB images (e.g., ~600 per scene block on Waymo, ~100 on KITTI, or large Mill-19 scenes) with no ground-truth camera poses; coarse relative poses are estimated via a pre-trained model in ~0.01 s per pair. It outputs a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation, novel-view renderings at real-time FPS (~52–79), and a weighted undirected camera graph encoding spatial topology. An octree-based initialization prunes sparse SfM points from ~300k to ~100k to reduce GPU memory and training time.

## Method

GraphGS combines two key innovations. First, a spatial prior-based structure estimation module replaces COLMAP's exhaustive matching with concentric nearest neighbor pairing (selecting local neighbors plus concentric rings of cameras) and a quadrant filter (a 6-bit binary encoding of relative position and orientation that discards >38% of noise pairs), reducing matching time by 20× and bundle adjustment by 5× while producing a connected camera graph. Second, the camera graph guides 3DGS optimization through (a) a multi-view consistency photometric loss that warps pixels between adjacent views weighted by an edge function of inter-camera distance and orientation, acting as implicit data augmentation against overfitting, and (b) an adaptive sampling strategy that assigns each camera node a participation probability proportional to its betweenness centrality, cutting total optimization iterations by ~50% while improving reconstruction quality.

## Results

On Waymo, GraphGS achieves 29.43 PSNR, 0.899 SSIM, and 0.217 LPIPS at 52 FPS, outperforming the strongest 3DGS-based baseline PVG (28.11 PSNR, 0.849 SSIM, 0.279 LPIPS) and all NeRF-based methods. On KITTI, it reaches 26.98 PSNR, 0.887 SSIM, and 0.157 LPIPS at 79 FPS, surpassing PVG (26.63 PSNR, 0.885 SSIM) and 3DGS (19.54 PSNR). On the large-scale Mill-19 dataset, GraphGS achieves 26.60 PSNR on Building and 27.03 PSNR on Rubble, exceeding VastGaussian by over 3 dB in PSNR. The method also reduces structure estimation time from >24 hours (COLMAP) to under 4 hours for 2k images while maintaining or improving reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphGS outperforms all 16 baselines on Waymo across every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FPS), with the largest margin over NeRF-based methods like BARF (9.07 PSNR) and S-NeRF (19.22 PSNR) which fail to converge on these large outdoor scenes. Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor PVG, GraphGS gains +1.32 dB PSNR and +0.050 SSIM on Waymo while also achieving higher FPS (52 vs. 50). On KITTI, GraphGS leads PVG by +0.35 dB PSNR and +0.002 SSIM, though PVG retains a slight LPIPS advantage (0.127 vs. 0.157), suggesting PVG produces slightly more perceptually similar textures in that setting. On the large-scale Mill-19 dataset, GraphGS surpasses VastGaussian by +3.10 dB PSNR on Building and +0.11 dB on Rubble, while VastGaussian maintains a lower LPIPS (0.130 vs. 0.163 on Building; 0.132 vs. 0.185 on Rubble). Pose-free methods like BARF and SPARF are unsuitable for these large outdoor scenes, with BARF failing to converge and SPARF designed only for sparse image sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphGS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its rendering and optimization backbone, but departs from the standard COLMAP-based SfM pipeline that prior large-scene methods (VastGaussian, PVG, Mega-NeRF) rely on for pose estimation and initial point clouds. It introduces a spatial prior-based structure estimation stage (using DUST3R for coarse poses, concentric nearest neighbor pairing, and a quadrant filter) that replaces exhaustive or vocab-tree matching, and then leverages the resulting camera graph to guide 3DGS optimization via multi-view consistency constraints and betweenness-centrality-based adaptive sampling. This graph-guided optimization is the key conceptual departure: prior 3DGS methods treat all training views symmetrically, whereas GraphGS encodes scene topology into the training loop to prevent overfitting to sparse viewpoints in open scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine practical bottleneck: COLMAP-based SfM fails or takes >24 hours on large outdoor image sets (2k+ images), making high-quality 3D reconstruction of street-level or urban scenes infeasible without ground-truth poses. By reducing structure estimation to minutes and cutting 3DGS training iterations by ~44% while improving quality, GraphGS makes large-scene reconstruction accessible from consumer or vehicle-mounted cameras without precise calibration. Key limitations remain: the method is anisotropic (no rear-view capture), relies on feature-point matching that can fail with dynamic objects, and still produces blurring at distant views. Open questions include whether the graph-guided strategy generalizes to indoor or object-centric scenes where viewpoint coverage is dense and node importance is more uniform, and whether the coarse-pose dependency on DUST3R becomes a bottleneck for very large or texture-poor environments.
