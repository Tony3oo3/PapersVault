## Problem

Standard LLM alignment methods like DPO rely on the binary Bradley-Terry model, which can only represent 'prefer' and 'disprefer' rankings. However, real preference data contains substantial ties—17% in Ultrafeedback and 29.4% in Chatbot Arena—where responses have equivalent quality. When these ties are forced into binary labels, DPO either learns incorrect preference signals or discards the data entirely, leaving a gap in how to extract useful information from tied pairs and model preferences with appropriate nuance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise preference data consisting of a text prompt, two candidate text responses, and a ternary indicator (clear preference or tie), then produces an aligned LLM policy that generates text responses. The key structural difference from DPO is the tie indicator I^i that routes each pair to either the preference loss or the tie loss, and the hyperparameter α (set to 0.5) that controls the width of the tie region in reward space. Training uses full-parameter fine-tuning on 20k-sample subsets of Ultrafeedback or Chatarena with varying tie proportions (0–30%).

## Method

TODO extends the Bradley-Terry model into the Tie-rank Oriented BT (TOBT) model by introducing a positive margin α into the integral bounds, yielding three outcome probabilities: r_{12} (prefer), r_{(12)} (tie), and r_{21} (disprefer), where the tie probability is nonzero only when the log-strength difference falls within ±α. The resulting loss has two components: for clearly preferred pairs, L^p = -log σ(μ - α), which shifts DPO's decision boundary by α to increase gradient weight on misestimated margins; for tied pairs, L^t = -log[(exp(2α)-1)/((1+exp(μ+α))(1+exp(-μ+α)))], whose gradient G(μ) is an odd, monotonically decreasing function with G(0)=0, ensuring no update when rewards are equal and corrective updates when they are not. This mechanism lets TODO learn from both the content and format of tied responses simultaneously, rather than arbitrarily favoring one, and also improves robustness on purely binary data by accounting for inherent label noise through the α margin.

## Results

On Mistral-7B, TODO with 0% tie data achieves 71.02% average accuracy across six benchmarks (Piqa, ARC-c, ARC-e, MMLU, Hellaswag, Winogrande), outperforming DPO's 66.24% by 4.78 points. On MT Bench, TODO with 20% tie data on Ultrafeedback scores 5.96 versus DPO's 5.55, and on Chatarena with 17% tie data, TODO reaches 78.47% preference test accuracy versus DPO's 76.33%. TODO also outperforms DPO on Reward Bench (out-of-distribution), achieving 73.64% average accuracy with 0% tie data versus DPO's 71.03%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TODO consistently outperforms DPO when tie data is present: on Ultrafeedback with 20% ties, TODO achieves 76.40% test accuracy and 5.96 MT Bench score versus DPO's 75.00% and 5.55; on Chatarena with 17% ties, TODO reaches 78.47% and 5.83 versus DPO's 76.33% and 5.71. Against KTO, TODO wins on every setting by a wide margin (e.g., 78.47% vs 74.80% on Chatarena with ties). Compared to SimPO, TODO is competitive on Ultrafeedback without ties (77.20% vs 77.80%) but clearly superior when tie data is included (76.40% vs 76.35% on Ultrafeedback, 78.47% vs 76.33% on Chatarena). Against ODPO (evaluated only on Ultrafeedback without ties), TODO edges out on test accuracy (77.20% vs 76.78%) but trails slightly on MT Bench (5.85 vs 5.95). Overall, TODO's advantage is most pronounced in the presence of tie data, where it leverages the ternary preference signal that binary baselines cannot exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bradley-Terry (BT) pairwise comparison model by incorporating a tie rank, drawing on the statistical literature of Rao & Kupper (1967) and Davidson (1970) that modified BT for tied outcomes in sports and ranking contexts. It then applies this tie-aware BT model (TOBT) to the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023), replacing the binary sigmoid loss with a ternary objective that separately handles preferred, dispreferred, and tied pairs. Unlike concurrent DPO variants (IPO, RPO, KTO, SimPO, ODPO) that address overfitting or reference-free optimization while remaining binary, TODO explicitly models the tie relation as a first-class preference category, enabling the policy to learn from tied pairs rather than discarding or mislabeling them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prevalence of ties in real preference data (17% in Ultrafeedback, 29.4% in Chatbot Arena) means that binary-only methods like DPO are systematically misaligned with the structure of available training signals. TODO's gradient analysis reveals that DPO's update direction is invariant for tied pairs, potentially injecting noise into the policy, while TODO's tie-specific objective preserves preference consistency. Open questions include whether TOBT integrates cleanly with online RLHF reward models, whether other tie-aware ranking models (Glenn & David 1960, Davidson 1970) offer further gains, and whether the benefits persist at larger model scales beyond 7B/8B. The approach is also composable with existing margin-based methods like ODPO, suggesting a broader design space for tie-aware alignment.
