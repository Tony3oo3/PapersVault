## Problem

The Koopman operator provides a linear state-space representation of nonlinear systems, but its fixed (time-invariant) matrices cannot adequately capture time-varying dynamics. Existing online-update strategies for time-varying Koopman models require solving optimization problems at each step, which is computationally expensive and may yield infeasible solutions. This paper asks whether the selective matrix-generation mechanism from the Mamba architecture can produce time-varying Koopman operators in real time while preserving the linear structure needed for efficient control.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a window of H historical shifted states z_{k-H:k-1} (obtained by passing raw states through a learned observable function ψ) concatenated with the corresponding historical inputs u_{k-H:k-1}, forming a single data sequence. It outputs a full sequence of discrete Koopman matrices {Ā_k, B̄_k, C_k} over a 30-step prediction horizon, which are then used recursively for multi-step-ahead state prediction or fed into a convex MPC formulation that yields an optimal control input trajectory. Crucially, the matrices are generated from past data rather than the current input, so the bilinear term B_k·u_k does not appear in the prediction model, keeping the MPC problem convex.

## Method

MamKO replaces Mamba's negative-exponential activation on the diagonal A matrix with a negative CELU, allowing positive eigenvalues to represent unstable systems (e.g., oscillators). A 1D convolution over the concatenated [z, u] historical sequence extracts temporal features, which are then decoded by fully connected networks into B_k, C_k sequences and per-step sampling periods T_k. The continuous diagonal A is discretized via zero-order hold (ZOH) together with the generated B and T to produce the discrete matrix sequences used in the linear time-varying SSM. Training minimizes a multi-step-ahead prediction loss over a 30-step horizon using Adam, jointly optimizing the observable function ψ, the matrix-generation network φ, and the diagonal A. For control, the pre-generated matrix sequence is plugged into a standard MPC formulation whose linear dynamics guarantee a convex optimization problem, enabling real-time solution (≈10–30 ms per step) even for 9-state chemical processes.

## Results

On the 30-step-ahead prediction task, MamKO achieves the lowest test MSE across all five benchmark systems, with the largest gains on time-varying systems (e.g., 4.31×10⁻⁴ on Time-varying CartPole vs. 8.30×10⁻⁴ for the Multiplication ablation). For set-point tracking control, MamKO-based MPC achieves the smallest steady-state error on every system: 6.40×10⁻² m (CartPole cart position), 5.35×10⁻² (GRN), 1.28×10⁻² (RSCP), and 6.78×10⁻² (Time-varying RSCP). Computation time per MPC step is 10–30 ms, reducing online solve time by 83–99% compared to MLP-based MPC (which requires 0.1–3.5 s for nonlinear optimization). On the large-scale BSM1 water treatment plant (130 states), MamKO-based MPC reduces tracking error by 11.56% over a PID controller and 10.07% over SAC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DKO (Lusch et al., 2018), MamKO wins on both modeling and control for all five systems; the control cost reduction is most dramatic on time-varying systems (92.10% on GRN, 84.74% on rapidly-changing CartPole), while gains on time-invariant systems are more modest (3.70–6.56%). Against MLP-based MPC (Chua et al., 2018), MamKO achieves superior control on every system despite MLP sometimes matching or beating MamKO on raw prediction MSE (e.g., GRN and RSCP modeling); the paper attributes this to MLP's non-convex optimization yielding suboptimal control solutions, whereas MamKO's linear SSM enables convex MPC. Against SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), MamKO-based MPC outperforms the model-free RL baseline on all control tasks, with steady-state error reductions of 52–93% depending on the system; SAC's advantage in inference speed (0.3 ms vs. 10–30 ms) is offset by its inability to handle time-varying dynamics or enforce state constraints. Against subspace identification (Van Overschee & De Moor, 2012), MamKO produces accurate 30-step predictions for all CartPole states, whereas the linear subspace model diverges on the pole angle and velocity states.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Koopman operator theory (Koopman, 1931; Korda & Mezić, 2018) and the selective state-space model architecture from Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023). Prior Koopman-based methods (DKO, EDMD) use a fixed linear operator, while time-varying Koopman approaches (Zhang et al., 2019; Hao et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023) update operators via online optimization that is computationally expensive and potentially infeasible. The key departure here is borrowing Mamba's matrix-generation mechanism but applying it to historical data rather than the current input sequence, which avoids the bilinear terms that would make the MPC problem non-convex, and replacing Mamba's negative-exponential eigenvalue constraint with CELU to accommodate unstable systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a linear time-varying SSM generated from a lightweight neural network can match or exceed the modeling accuracy of fully nonlinear MLPs while preserving the convex structure needed for efficient MPC, achieving 83–99% computation time reductions over MLP-based MPC. The water treatment plant experiment (145 states) suggests practical applicability to industrial-scale processes. Open questions include formal convergence and stability guarantees for the MamKO-based controller, scalability to even larger systems, and whether the generative approach can be extended to handle structural (not just parametric) changes in system dynamics.
