## Problem

Existing MoE+LoRA fine-tuning methods for LLMs suffer from three key limitations: they apply uniform routing granularity across all layers despite shallow and deep layers capturing features at different levels of abstraction; task-level routing relies on task labels and fails to generalize to unseen tasks; and standard load-balancing losses introduce routing uncertainty that undermines expert specialization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253286/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plugin inserted into the dense layers (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o, W_gate, W_up, W_down) of causal decoder LLMs such as Qwen2 1.5B and LLaMA 3.2 1B. It takes a text instruction (input token sequence) and a trainable task embedding, producing a generated text response while only updating LoRA expert matrices, router weights, a single-layer task encoder, and the task embedding (~3.9% of total parameters). The task encoder processes the input tokens concatenated with the task embedding to produce a task-level representation used for routing, computed once per input rather than per token.

## Method

HMoRA introduces a hierarchical hybrid routing mechanism where a layer-dependent coefficient α^(l) = σ(−ε + 2ε·l/L + μ) blends token-level and task-level gate values, causing shallow layers to emphasize fine-grained token distinctions while deeper layers shift toward broader task-level understanding. A novel Constrained GJS (CGJS) auxiliary loss simultaneously maximizes the entropy of the average gate distribution (promoting expert balance) and minimizes the mean individual gate entropy (promoting routing certainty), with hyperparameters γ_b and γ_c bounding each term to preserve model flexibility. The task router, trained with this auxiliary loss, learns to cluster similar tasks together and separate dissimilar ones in an unsupervised manner, enabling generalization to unseen tasks without explicit task labels. Additional lightweight designs—applying single LoRA to W_o and W_down, sharing routers across W_q/W_k/W_v and W_up/W_gate, and using Hydra LoRA+ with a shared W_A and differential learning rate on W_B—further reduce trainable parameters and computational cost while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Results

On Qwen2 1.5B fine-tuned on Flan v2, HMoRA with lightweight designs (3.90% trainable parameters) achieves an average accuracy of 63.88 across seven NLP benchmarks, outperforming full fine-tuning (63.15) on 5 out of 7 benchmarks. Without lightweight designs (6.31% parameters), HMoRA reaches 64.16 average accuracy, surpassing full fine-tuning on all seven benchmarks. The best individual scores include 54.63 on MMLU, 26.59 on MMLU-Pro, 71.47 on ARC-Challenge, and 72.08 on CommonsenseQA. On LLaMA 3.2 1B, HMoRA with lightweight designs achieves 42.97 average accuracy, again outperforming full fine-tuning (33.61) and all PEFT baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HMoRA w/o LW (6.31% params) outperforms full fine-tuning on all seven benchmarks, with the largest margins on ARC-Challenge (+2.34), MMLU-Pro (+1.50), and OpenBookQA (+0.88). HMoRA w LW (3.90% params) beats full fine-tuning on 5 of 7 benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, ARC-C, ARC-E, SWAG, CommonsenseQA) while trailing slightly on MMLU (−0.10) and OpenBookQA (−0.15). Against the closest MoE+LoRA competitor, MoLoRA (3.82% params), HMoRA w LW gains +0.86 average accuracy, with notable improvements on ARC-Challenge (+1.63) and SWAG (+1.97). MixLoRA (3.97%) and HydraLoRA (3.20%) trail HMoRA w LW by 1.50 and 1.18 average accuracy points respectively, with HydraLoRA particularly weak on SWAG (53.66 vs 56.40). Standard LoRA (r=8, 0.60%) underperforms HMoRA w LW by 3.12 average points, confirming that the mixture-of-experts structure provides substantial gains over single-adapter fine-tuning even at comparable parameter budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

HMoRA sits at the intersection of two active research threads: Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) for LLMs and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning. It builds directly on prior MoE+LoRA combinations (MoLoRA, MixLoRA, HydraLoRA) but departs from them in two key ways. First, it introduces hierarchical hybrid routing that assigns token-level routing to shallow layers and task-level routing to deeper layers, grounded in the observation (Geva et al., 2021) that LLM layers capture features at different granularities—something prior multi-granular routing methods (Ren et al., 2023; Kudugunta et al., 2021) applied uniformly across all layers. Second, it replaces the standard load-balancing auxiliary loss with a Constrained Generalized Jensen-Shannon (CGJS) divergence that simultaneously promotes routing certainty and expert balance, rather than trading one for the other.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is clear: outperforming full fine-tuning on 5/7 benchmarks while training only 3.9% of parameters makes HMoRA a compelling option for multi-task PEFT in resource-constrained settings. The auxiliary loss's emergent clustering-like behavior—enabling the task router to differentiate unseen tasks without explicit task labels—is a conceptually interesting side effect that could inform future work on task-agnostic routing. However, the paper is limited to small models (Qwen2 1.5B, LLaMA 3.2 1B), and the authors themselves note that efficiency in industrial-scale applications remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the hierarchical routing principle holds at 70B+ scale, whether the CGJS loss transfers to standard (non-LoRA) MoE architectures, and whether the modest gains over HydraLoRA persist with more training data or larger base models.
