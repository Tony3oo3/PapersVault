## Problem

Although SAM has been adapted to various downstream tasks, prior work treats each task independently via single-task fine-tuning, overlooking SAM's potential as a multi-task foundation model. Two specific barriers prevent this: SAM's prompt-guided architecture cannot natively produce outputs with different channel counts per task, and no method exists for simultaneously fine-tuning SAM across multiple tasks while exploiting inter-task shared structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253436/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (3×H×W) and simultaneously produces task-specific outputs at 1/4 spatial resolution: segmentation masks (1×H/4×W/4), depth maps (1×H/4×W/4), surface normal maps (3×H/4×W/4), saliency maps, and human-parts segmentation maps. The key architectural constraint is that each task's output channel count N_t is determined by its task embedding, so the decoder must handle heterogeneous output dimensions within a unified encoder-decoder pipeline.

## Method

MTSAM removes SAM's prompt encoder and replaces it with per-task learnable embeddings (E_t ∈ R^{N_t×D}) that are broadcast-added to the frozen image encoder's features before feeding a task-specific two-way Transformer decoder, enabling heterogeneous output channel counts. For parameter-efficient multi-task fine-tuning, the proposed ToRA aggregates all T task-specific LoRA update matrices into a single 3D tensor ΔW ∈ R^{d×k×T} and applies Tucker decomposition (core tensor G ∈ R^{p×q×v} plus factor matrices U_1, U_2, U_3), where U_1 and U_2 capture task-shared subspace structure while U_3 encodes task-specific variation. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves ToRA can reconstruct any set of T LoRA updates with strictly fewer parameters (sublinear in T vs. linear for independent LoRAs). An orthogonal regularization term on U_1, U_2, and G (inspired by HOSVD) further reduces redundancy, and the pre-trained encoder weights remain frozen throughout training.

## Results

On NYUv2 (3 tasks), MTSAM achieves 65.98 mIoU for segmentation, 0.2898 Abs Err for depth, and 16.34° mean angular error for surface normals, yielding the best overall Δ_b of +23.93% over the HPS baseline while using only 59.59M trainable parameters. On CityScapes (2 tasks), MTSAM reaches 87.45 mIoU and 33.01 Rel Err for depth with just 37.44M parameters (Δ_b = +20.99%), substantially outperforming SwinMTL (73.33 mIoU, 333.31M params). On PASCAL-Context (4 tasks), MTSAM attains 74.13 mIoU for segmentation, 71.04 for human parts, 76.28 for saliency, and 17.10° for normals (Δ_b = +8.95%), surpassing all baselines including MultiLoRA and Hyperformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTSAM with ToRA achieves the best Δ_b across all three datasets, outperforming the strongest Transformer-based baseline SwinMTL by +4.38% on NYUv2 (65.98 vs 64.23 mIoU) and by a dramatic +14.12% on CityScapes (87.45 vs 73.33 mIoU), while using roughly 5–9× fewer trainable parameters (59.59M vs 333.91M on NYUv2; 37.44M vs 333.31M on CityScapes). Among LoRA-based multi-task methods, MTSAM consistently beats MultiLoRA on all datasets (e.g., 65.98 vs 64.85 mIoU on NYUv2, 87.45 vs 87.23 on CityScapes, 74.13 vs 72.39 on PASCAL-Context) and also surpasses LoRA-STL(r=16) and LoRA-HPS(r=32), confirming that ToRA's joint task-shared/task-specific decomposition is more effective than independent or shared low-rank updates. On PASCAL-Context, MTSAM also outperforms the multi-task PEFT methods Polyhistor (70.87 mIoU) and Hyperformer (71.43 mIoU) by 3.26 and 2.70 mIoU respectively, while using fewer parameters (74.71M vs 287.32M for Hyperformer). The CNN-based baselines (Cross-Stitch, MTAN, NDDR-CNN) and DenseMTL trail significantly behind, with Δ_b values below +6%, highlighting the advantage of leveraging SAM's pre-trained encoder for multi-task dense prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SAM from its original single-task, prompt-guided segmentation role into a multi-task foundation model by removing the prompt encoder and replacing it with task-specific embeddings and decoders. For fine-tuning, it builds directly on LoRA's low-rank update assumption but generalizes it from per-task matrices to a shared update parameter tensor decomposed via Tucker decomposition (ToRA), thereby capturing both task-shared and task-specific structure. This departs from prior multi-task PEFT methods (Polyhistor, HiPro) that use hypernetworks or hierarchical prompts, and from multi-task LoRA variants (MultiLoRA, LoRA-STL/HPS) that either share or fully separate low-rank factors without exploiting inter-task correlation through tensor structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sublinear parameter growth of ToRA relative to the number of tasks (vs. linear for LoRA) makes multi-task fine-tuning of large vision encoders more practical, and the theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that ToRA strictly dominates multiple LoRAs in expressive power provides a principled justification. However, the evaluation is limited to 3–4 dense prediction tasks on three indoor/outdoor datasets, leaving open whether the low-rank tensor assumption and the architectural modifications generalize to heterogeneous task types (e.g., classification + detection + dense prediction) or to substantially larger task sets. The moderate gains over LoRA-STL (e.g., +3.9 mIoU on NYUv2 segmentation) suggest the benefit of shared structure is real but bounded, and the method's sensitivity to rank hyperparameters (p, q, v) across datasets remains a practical concern.
