## Problem

Generating images at higher resolutions than a pre-trained diffusion model's training size is computationally expensive because existing methods perform the entire sampling process at full resolution, processing all frequency components simultaneously. This contradicts the inherent coarse-to-fine nature of latent diffusion models, which synthesize low-frequency content first and high-frequency details later, leading to wasted computation on premature high-frequency details and long inference latency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250233/cropped_page3_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

FreCaS takes a text prompt and a pre-trained latent diffusion model (SD2.1, SDXL, or SD3) as input, requiring no additional training or fine-tuning. It produces RGB images at 4× or 16× the model's native training resolution (e.g., 2048×2048 and 4096×4096 from SDXL's 1024×1024), with the sampling process split across multiple resolution stages. The method supports arbitrary aspect ratios and operates with standard samplers (DDIM for SD2.1/SDXL, Euler flow-matching for SD3).

## Method

FreCaS divides the T-step sampling process into N+1 stages of increasing resolutions, where each stage progressively expands the latent's frequency band from [0, s_{i-1}/2] to [0, s_i/2], avoiding unnecessary high-frequency computation in early stages. Between stages, the latent transitions via a denoise → decode → bilinear interpolate → encode → diffuse pipeline with SNR equivalence to preserve signal content. A key innovation is the Frequency-Aware Classifier-Free Guidance (FA-CFG) strategy, which decomposes both unconditional and conditional denoising scores into low-frequency and high-frequency components (via downsample-resize and residual subtraction) and applies different guidance strengths (w_l for low, w_h for high) to prioritize detail generation in the newly expanded frequency band. Additionally, cross-attention maps from the previous stage are linearly interpolated with current-stage maps to maintain spatial layout consistency and prevent duplicated objects across resolution transitions.

## Results

On SDXL ×4 generation (2048×2048), FreCaS achieves the best FID of 16.48, FIDb of 12.63, and FIDp of 17.91, while running 2.46× faster than direct inference (13.84s vs 34.10s on A100). On SDXL ×16 generation (4096×4096), FreCaS achieves FID 42.75, FIDb 40.63, and FIDp 39.82 with a 3.64× speedup (85.87s vs 312.36s). On SD2.1, FreCaS similarly leads: FID 16.38 (×4) and 19.95 (×16) with 2.16× and 3.69× speedups respectively. The paper reports FreCaS is 2.86× and 6.07× faster than ScaleCrafter and DemoFusion for 2048×2048 SDXL generation, with FIDb improvements of 11.6 and 3.7 over those methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreCaS outperforms all four primary baselines on both image quality and speed. Against MultiDiffusion, FreCaS achieves substantially better FID (16.48 vs 21.05 on SD2.1 ×4) and is ~47× faster (2.56s vs 120.21s), though MultiDiffusion does not address the duplicated-object problem. Against ScaleCrafter, FreCaS improves FIDb by 11.6 points on SDXL ×4 (12.63 vs 24.23) while being 2.86× faster (13.84s vs 39.64s). Against FouriScale, FreCaS achieves lower FID (16.48 vs 26.44) and FIDb (12.63 vs 26.88) on SDXL ×4 and is ~4.8× faster. Against DemoFusion, the closest competitor in quality, FreCaS improves FIDb by 3.7 points (12.63 vs 16.33) on SDXL ×4 and is 6.07× faster (13.84s vs 83.95s); on ×16, FreCaS leads by 3.9 FIDb points (40.63 vs 44.54) and is 7.6× faster. HiDiffusion is the second-fastest method but sacrifices quality (FIDb 20.69 vs 12.63 on SDXL ×4), confirming that FreCaS achieves the best quality-speed tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreCaS builds on the cascaded diffusion paradigm (Ho et al., 2022) and the training-free higher-resolution generation line (MultiDiffusion, ScaleCrafter, FouriScale, HiDiffusion, DemoFusion, AccDiffusion), but departs from all of them by introducing a frequency-aware decomposition of the sampling process. While prior methods process all frequency components simultaneously at full resolution, FreCaS exploits the empirically validated coarse-to-fine behavior of latent diffusion (via PSD analysis) to progressively expand the frequency band across stages. The FA-CFG strategy, which applies different classifier-free guidance strengths to low- and high-frequency components, is a novel repurposing of the standard CFG mechanism, and the CA-maps reutilization draws on cross-attention structure for layout consistency without any architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.86×–6.07× speedup over strong baselines (ScaleCrafter, DemoFusion) while simultaneously improving FID_b makes high-resolution generation (up to 4096×4096) practically accessible on a single A100 GPU, which is significant for real-world deployment. The frequency-aware perspective provides a principled explanation for why cascaded sampling works and offers a general recipe applicable across architectures (SD2.1, SDXL, SD3). Open questions include whether the heuristic low-frequency extraction (downsample-then-resize) can be replaced with a more principled spectral filter, whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, and how it interacts with emerging rectified-flow or consistency-model samplers beyond the tested settings.
