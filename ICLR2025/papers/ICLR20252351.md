## Problem

Surrogate models for complex energy systems (buildings, wind farms) are typically queried through rigid tabular interfaces (one-hot encodings, key-value pairs), limiting accessibility for non-expert stakeholders who make consequential policy and investment decisions. It remains unclear whether natural language descriptions of system configurations can serve as effective regression inputs that match or exceed traditional encodings in accuracy while unlocking new capabilities such as semantic generalization across paraphrased or synonym-substituted descriptions.

## Input / Output

The surrogate takes two inputs: a text-based system caption (SysCap) describing building or wind-farm attributes in either key-value format (e.g., 'A:1.0|B:blue') or LLM-generated natural language (2–9 sentences), and a timeseries input (8,760-hour multivariate weather for buildings, or 3 scalar atmospheric conditions for steady-state wind farms). The text is tokenized and encoded by a fine-tuned BERT/DistilBERT into a single 768-dimensional embedding, which is broadcast across all timesteps and concatenated with the (cyclically-encoded) timeseries features. The output is a per-timestep scalar prediction (hourly energy consumption or instantaneous power generation), with the top model sharing parameters across timesteps.

## Method

The core architecture is a lightweight multimodal surrogate f = h_θ ∘ g_ψ, where the encoder g_ψ composes a fine-tuned BERT/DistilBERT text encoder (producing a single [CLS] embedding from the SysCap) with a bidirectional sequence encoder (S4 state-space model or LSTM) that processes the concatenated text-timeseries sequence, and the top model h_θ is a shared single-hidden-layer MLP predicting each timestep's output. To address the absence of human-written system descriptions, the authors use llama-2-7b-chat with a structured prompt template to synthesize natural language SysCaps from simulator metadata, and introduce prompt augmentation (varying the description style tag) to increase caption diversity and regularize training in small-data settings. The key design choice is broadcasting the single text embedding to every timestep before concatenation, which lets the sequence encoder learn timestep-specific interactions between system attributes and exogenous conditions. Experiments on EnergyPlus building stock (330K buildings, 13 RFE-selected attributes) and FLORIS wind farm wake (500 configurations) show that SysCaps-kv with BERT matches or beats one-hot baselines in stock-annual NRMSE, while SysCaps-nl gains robustness to attribute synonyms (≤13% NRMSE increase vs. 54–90% for removal/random baselines) and provides a regularizing effect in the small-data wind farm setting.

## Results

On the Buildings-900K dataset (6K held-out buildings, 8,760 hourly timesteps), the best SysCaps surrogate (LSTM + BERT + SysCaps-kv) achieves a Buildings-Hourly NRMSE of 0.439±0.037 and Stock-Annual NRMSE of 0.022±0.011, matching or beating the one-hot SSM baseline (0.450/0.041) while using text-encoded attributes. On the Wind Farm Wake dataset (500 unique configurations), SysCaps-nl with prompt augmentation achieves an NRMSE of 0.035±0.001, dramatically outperforming the one-hot ResNet baseline (0.212±0.009) and LightGBM (0.196±0.000). The SSM architecture consistently outperforms LSTM and ResNet sequence encoders, and switching from DistilBERT to BERT roughly halves the Stock-Annual NRMSE for SysCaps-nl models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the tuned LightGBM baseline (Buildings-Hourly NRMSE 0.679, Stock-Annual 0.094), all SysCaps surrogates with sequential encoders (SSM or LSTM) achieve substantially better accuracy: the best SysCaps-kv model (LSTM+BERT) reaches 0.439/0.022, a ~35% reduction in Buildings-Hourly NRMSE and ~77% reduction in Stock-Annual NRMSE. Compared to the one-hot SSM baseline (0.450/0.041), SysCaps-kv with BERT matches on Buildings-Hourly (0.450) and improves Stock-Annual NRMSE by half (0.020 vs. 0.041), while SysCaps-nl with BERT trades a small Buildings-Hourly increase (0.543) for a large Stock-Annual gain (0.035). On the wind farm task, SysCaps-nl with prompt augmentation (NRMSE 0.035) outperforms LightGBM (0.196) by ~5.6× and the one-hot ResNet (0.212) by ~6×, demonstrating a strong regularizing effect in the small-data regime (only 300 training systems). The ablation without any attribute input (SSM, NRMSE 1.712) confirms that the multimodal architecture successfully fuses text and timeseries rather than ignoring one modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of surrogate modeling for complex energy systems, language interfaces for scientific ML, and multimodal text-timeseries learning. It departs from prior LLM-for-regression work (LIFT, TabLLM) by not using an LLM as the regressor but instead using LLMs solely to synthesize training captions, then training a lightweight BERT/DistilBERT + SSM/LSTM architecture. It also differs from multimodal timeseries forecasting work like Time-LLM, where text is an auxiliary covariate, by making text-encoded system attributes a critical input that the model fundamentally depends on. The 'system caption' (SysCap) concept repurposes the LLM data-augmentation paradigm (as in WavCaps, LP-MusicCaps) for a novel application: generating natural-language descriptions of simulator metadata to serve as first-class inputs to surrogate models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that language is a viable and practical interface for interacting with simulation surrogates, with concrete benefits: semantic generalization (robustness to attribute synonyms), a regularizing effect in small-data regimes (wind farm), and enabling non-experts to perform design space exploration via natural language prompts. Key limitations include BERT tokenizers' poor handling of numerical values (interpolation failures on unseen magnitudes) and 9–12% attribute omission/hallucination rates in LLM-generated captions. Open questions include scaling to simulators with 100+ attributes, cross-simulator generalization (surrogate foundation models), and whether more powerful LLMs will sufficiently reduce caption errors to close the remaining gap between key-value and natural-language SysCaps.
