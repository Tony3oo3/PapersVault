## Problem

Computing the positive non-clashing teaching dimension of a concept class (represented as balls in a graph) was known to be NP-hard only for large, input-dependent teaching dimension k, and the open question of whether the problem remains NP-hard for constant k was unresolved. Additionally, prior running time bounds were far from tight, and fixed-parameter tractability was established only for the highly restrictive vertex cover parameter, leaving the complexity landscape under other natural structural parameters (pathwidth, feedback vertex number) completely open.

## Input / Output

The input consists of a graph G, a set B of balls (or all balls in the strict variant), and an integer k; the output is a YES/NO decision on whether a positive non-clashing teaching map of dimension at most k exists, where each ball B_r(u) is assigned a teaching set T(B_r(u)) ⊆ B_r(u) such that for every pair of distinct balls, at least one vertex in their combined teaching sets lies outside their intersection. The algorithm is constructive: when a solution exists, it can be explicitly output as a witness. The strict variant (STRICT NON-CLASH) fixes B to be the set of all balls in G, while the general variant (NON-CLASH) allows an arbitrary subset of balls.

## Method

The paper establishes four main results through distinct technical mechanisms. For NP-hardness at k=2, a reduction from 3-SAT constructs split graphs using variable and clause force-gadgets that enforce well-defined truth assignments while keeping the teaching dimension at exactly 2. For running time bounds, a more careful analysis of the reduction yields a near-tight ETH lower bound of 2^{o(n·d·k)} matched by an upper bound of 2^{O(n·d·k·log n)}. The FPT result for vertex integrity relies on a kernelization that partitions connected components of G−X into twin-blocks (isomorphism classes preserving edges to X and ball membership), defines 'blueprints' capturing how a teaching set interacts with each component, and proves the existence of 'compact' teaching maps that only use examples from X and a bounded number of core components, enabling instance reduction to size 2^{2^{O(p³)}}. Finally, W[1]-hardness for fvs + pw + k is shown via a reduction from NAE-INTEGER-3-SAT that encodes variable assignments as positions on paths and clause satisfaction as ball containment relationships, with long connecting paths preventing unwanted shortcuts.

## Results

The paper establishes that STRICT NON-CLASH is NP-hard even for k=2 on split graphs (Theorem 1), improving upon prior results that only showed NP-hardness for input-dependent k. For running time bounds, the authors achieve a near-tight gap: a lower bound of 2^{o(|V(G)|·d·k)} under ETH (Theorem 4) and an upper bound of 2^{O(|V(G)|·d·k·log|V(G)|)} (Proposition 5), separated by only a logarithmic factor in the exponent. On the parameterized side, NON-CLASH is shown to be FPT parameterized by vertex integrity (Theorem 17), while simultaneously W[1]-hard parameterized by fvs(G) + pw(G) + k (Theorem 18), ruling out FPT for those more common structural parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no experimental baselines; the 'comparisons' are improvements over prior complexity bounds from Chalopin et al. (COLT 2024). The prior work established NP-hardness only for large (input-dependent) k and obtained running time bounds of 2^{o(n·d)} (lower) and 2^{O(n²·d)} (upper); this paper tightens both to 2^{o(n·d·k)} and 2^{O(n·d·k·log n)} respectively, closing the gap to a single log factor. For parameterized tractability, the prior FPT result was parameterized by vertex cover number; this paper extends it to the strictly less restrictive vertex integrity parameter, broadening the class of tractable instances. The new W[1]-hardness result for fvs + pw + k (Theorem 18) answers an open question from Chalopin et al. by showing that the more commonly used structural parameters (treewidth, pathwidth, feedback vertex number) do not yield FPT algorithms even when combined with k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the non-clashing teaching model introduced by Kirkpatrick et al. (ALT 2019) and Fallat et al. (JMLR 2023), and extends the initial complexity investigation of Chalopin et al. (COLT 2024), which established NP-hardness only for large (input-dependent) teaching dimension and FPT for vertex cover. The key departure is a sharp NP-hardness result for the constant dimension k=2 (answering an explicit open question from Chalopin et al.), near-tight exponential-time bounds closing the gap from 2^{O(n²·d)} to 2^{O(n·d·k·log n)}, and a generalization of the FPT result from vertex cover to the strictly more general vertex integrity parameter. The paper also introduces the first complexity analysis for the non-strict setting (NON-CLASH), where not all balls in the graph need to be present, complementing the previously studied strict variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results provide a nearly complete complexity landscape for computing the positive non-clashing teaching dimension, resolving two open questions from the literature and delineating precisely which structural graph parameters admit efficient algorithms. The sharp boundary between k=1 (trivial) and k=2 (NP-hard) is practically significant since small teaching dimensions are the primary candidates for efficient teaching in applications like recommendation systems and robotics. Remaining open questions include the small logarithmic gap between the upper and lower exponential-time bounds, the relationship between non-clashing teaching dimension and VC-dimension, and extending the analysis to the non-positive setting and approximation algorithms.
