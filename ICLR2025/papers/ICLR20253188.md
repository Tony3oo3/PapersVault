## Problem

Reward hacking—where optimizing a proxy reward function leads to poor performance under the true, unspecified objective—lacks both a formal definition that captures realistic cases and a theoretically grounded mitigation method. Existing definitions either fail to characterize what makes a proxy 'reasonable' or set arbitrary thresholds, and the standard KL penalty on action distributions in RLHF lacks theoretical guarantees and can be insufficient in multi-step environments.

## Input / Output

The method operates in an infinite-horizon MDP where the agent observes environment states and takes actions, with a proxy reward function that is r-correlated with the unknown true reward under the reference policy's state-action occupancy measure. The reference policy (e.g., a human-driving policy in traffic control or an SFT LLM in RLHF) provides both the correlation baseline and the true-reward threshold. The output is a policy trained via PPO with an adjusted reward that incorporates a discriminator-estimated χ² occupancy measure penalty, producing actions that improve proxy reward while provably avoiding reward hacking.

## Method

The paper defines a proxy reward as one whose values correlate with the true reward over state-action pairs sampled from a reference policy, and defines reward hacking as the optimized policy achieving lower true reward than the reference policy. Theorem 5.1 then shows that for any r-correlated proxy, the true reward improvement over the reference policy is lower-bounded by the normalized proxy reward gain minus a term involving the square root of the χ² divergence between the policies' occupancy measures, yielding the regularized objective J(π, R̃) − λ√(χ²(μ_π‖μ_πref)) with λ = σ_R̃√(1−r²). In practice, ORPO implements this by alternately training a discriminator network to approximate the χ² OM divergence (following the GAIL-style adversarial formulation) and updating the policy via PPO with an adjusted per-step reward R′(s,a) = R̃(s,a) − (λ/√χ̂²)·e^{d̂_φ(s,a)}. The key design choices—occupancy measure over action distribution and χ² over KL divergence—are motivated by the fact that OM divergence captures long-horizon state visitation differences that AD divergence misses, and χ²'s exponential penalty on out-of-distribution state-action pairs more strongly constrains the policy than KL's linear penalty.

## Results

Across five environments, χ² occupancy measure (OM) regularization consistently outperforms action distribution (AD) regularization and the no-regularization baseline. In glucose monitoring (×10³), state-action OM χ² achieves −47.6±0.6 true reward versus −72.6 for the reference policy and −599.0 for no regularization, approaching the true-reward upper bound of −43.4. In RLHF, AD χ² regularization reaches 16.94±0.07 true reward, improving over the reference policy (16.37) by 0.57 and outperforming the standard KL penalty (16.81±0.27). In pandemic mitigation, state OM χ² achieves −10.68±0.15, a 1.6-point improvement over the reference policy (−12.26), while AD regularization fails to improve on the reference at all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the standard KL penalty between action distributions used in RLHF (Stiennon et al., 2020), corresponding to the 'Action dist. KL' row. In RLHF, χ² AD regularization (16.94±0.07) outperforms KL AD regularization (16.81±0.27) by 0.13 true reward and is more stable across seeds (0.07 vs 0.27 std). In the four non-RLHF environments, the paper's OM regularization substantially outperforms AD regularization: in glucose monitoring, state-action OM χ² (−47.6) improves over AD KL (−73.4) by 25.8 points, while AD regularization actually performs at or below the reference policy (−72.6). In pandemic mitigation, state OM χ² (−10.68) beats AD KL (−12.20) by 1.5 points, and AD regularization fails to improve on the reference at all. In the AI safety gridworld, state-action OM χ² (9.17) outperforms AD KL (6.33) by 2.84 points, approaching the true-reward upper bound of 8.54. Overall, χ² divergence tends to match or exceed KL divergence across all environments, and OM regularization is strictly better than AD regularization in multi-step settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of the Goodhart's Law / reward hacking literature (Skalse et al. 2022, Pan et al. 2022, Krakovna 2018) and the RLHF regularization literature (Stiennon et al. 2020, Bai et al. 2022). It departs from prior reward hacking definitions by introducing a correlation-based formalism tied to a reference policy, and from prior RLHF practice by replacing the standard KL penalty on action distributions with a χ² penalty on occupancy measures. The practical implementation borrows the discriminator-based occupancy measure estimation from GAIL (Ho & Ermon, 2016), and the χ² divergence choice is related to but distinct from Huang et al. (2024), who propose χ² in a DPO/offline setting rather than online RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable lower bound on true reward improvement when optimizing a proxy with regularization, and shows that the standard KL-on-action-distributions penalty used in industry RLHF lacks such a guarantee (Theorem A.5). This has direct practical implications for LLM alignment: as RLHF moves from single-turn to multi-turn or tool-use settings where occupancy measures diverge from action distributions, the proposed χ² OM regularization offers a principled upgrade. Open questions include whether the lower bound is optimizable in all realistic MDPs (the paper shows counterexamples exist), how the discriminator-based approximation scales to very large state spaces, and whether the approach transfers to offline RL settings.
