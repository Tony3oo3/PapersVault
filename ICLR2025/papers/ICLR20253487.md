## Problem

Existing agentic benchmarks for LLMs and VLMs involve only a few dozen interaction rounds, falling far short of the orders-of-magnitude more interactions required by realistic tasks. There is no comprehensive, reproducible benchmark that systematically evaluates long-horizon sequential decision-making, spatial reasoning, and long-term planning across environments spanning from seconds to years of human mastery time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253487/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep, the agent receives a chat-formatted prompt containing game rules, an action list with descriptions, and a 16-step observation-action history. Observations are provided in two modalities: language-only (natural language descriptions plus ASCII maps for MiniHack/NetHack) or vision-language (a single RGB image of the current state alongside the text description). The model outputs one natural language action string per step; invalid actions trigger a fallback (e.g., 'do-nothing') and are logged. Scoring is 0–100 per episode, binary for BabyAI/MiniHack/Baba Is AI, proportional for TextWorld/Crafter, and based on a novel data-informed progression curve for NetHack derived from human-played game statistics.

## Method

BALROG is a unified benchmark framework that aggregates six procedurally generated reinforcement learning game environments (BabyAI, Crafter, TextWorld, Baba Is AI, MiniHack, and NetHack Learning Environment) into a single testbed, spanning difficulty from ~10 turns to ~10⁴–10⁵ turns. The framework explicitly decouples inference-time prompting strategies from the underlying model via a modular agent class (prompt builder + API client), enabling rapid prototyping of new strategies without re-implementing environment interactions. A key technical contribution is a data-informed NetHack progression metric: using a dataset of human games, they construct probability curves mapping dungeon level and experience level to win probability, replacing the inadequate in-game score. Evaluation is zero-shot with a general, model-agnostic prompt, multiple seeds per environment, and highly parallelized asynchronous execution. The benchmark also supports submissions of new models (any architecture producing natural-language actions) and novel agentic strategies (e.g., chain-of-thought, few-shot, multi-agent) via a leaderboard.

## Results

Across six game environments, Claude 3.5 Sonnet achieves the highest average progression at 32.64% in language-only mode, narrowly ahead of GPT-4o (32.34%) and Llama 3.1 70B (27.88%). On the hardest environment, NetHack, even the best model (o1-preview) reaches only 1.57% average game progression, and no model successfully solves MiniHack's Boxoban or Quest tasks. In vision-language mode, Claude 3.5 Sonnet leads at 35.48%, while GPT-4o drops sharply from 32.34% to 22.56% when images are added, revealing severe deficiencies in vision-based decision-making.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-4o are nearly tied in language-only mode (32.64% vs. 32.34%), but diverge sharply in vision-language mode: Claude 3.5 Sonnet improves to 35.48% while GPT-4o drops to 22.56%, a 9.8-point decline. Llama 3.2 90B shows a similar vision penalty, falling from 27.29% to 20.99%, and on Crafter specifically its VLM score (14.54%) is worse than the much smaller Llama 3.2 11B (15.91%). Gemini 1.5 Pro is the only model that improves in vision-language mode (21.00% → 25.76%), though it scores 0% on TextWorld due to API safety flags. On NetHack, o1-preview (1.57%) outperforms Claude 3.5 Sonnet (0.58%) by roughly 3×, but all models remain near zero. On Baba Is AI, open-source Llama models (3.2 90B at 43.90%, 3.1 70B at 40.00%) outperform both GPT-4o (33.66%) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (37.50%) in language-only mode, an unusual reversal of the typical closed-source advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of agentic LLM evaluation (AgentBench, SmartPlay, SWE-bench, OfficeBench) by deliberately pushing from short-horizon settings (dozens of interaction steps) to long-horizon sequential decision-making spanning thousands to hundreds of thousands of steps. It repurposes established RL game environments (BabyAI, Crafter, TextWorld, MiniHack, NLE) that were originally designed for deep RL research, aggregating them into a unified benchmark with a wide difficulty range (seconds to years for human mastery) and procedural generation to prevent memorization. Unlike prior game-based LLM benchmarks that are already saturating, BALROG includes NetHack, where even specialized neural agents trained on billions of in-domain datapoints make very limited progress, ensuring the benchmark remains challenging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BALROG reveals that current frontier LLMs and VLMs are far from solving long-horizon agentic tasks, with the best model achieving only 1.57% progression on NetHack. The finding that VLMs perform worse when visual input is added (rather than better) challenges the assumption that multimodal information is always beneficial for decision-making, and the 'knowing-doing gap'—where models correctly identify dangerous actions in isolation but still execute them during gameplay—highlights a fundamental disconnect between declarative knowledge and procedural decision-making. Open questions include whether inference-time strategies (chain-of-thought, in-context RL, multi-agent collaboration) can bridge these gaps at the required context lengths, and whether VLM architectures need fundamental redesign for action-oriented visual reasoning.
