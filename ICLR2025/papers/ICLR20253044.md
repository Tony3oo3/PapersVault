## Problem

Ego-centric 3D visual grounding suffers from two compounding issues: (1) sparse fusion of depth-reconstructed point clouds with multi-view RGB images discards ~98% of semantic features, losing fine-grained object details especially for small objects, and (2) natural language descriptions in existing datasets are inherently ambiguous, lacking spatial anchors and context needed to disambiguate among similar objects in complex scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253044/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes V ego-centric RGB-D views (each H×W×3 RGB + H×W depth) along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus a free-form language description. Depth maps are back-projected into a holistic 3D point cloud P∈R^{N×3} via global alignment matrices, while RGB features are lifted to 3D using per-view camera parameters. The output is a single 9DoF bounding box (center xyz, dimensions lwh, orientation θφψ) localizing the described object. LSE-augmented text is used only during training; at inference the model processes raw descriptions directly.

## Method

DenseGrounding introduces two complementary modules on top of an EmbodiedScan-style pipeline (ResNet-50 + MinkNet34 encoders, FPN fusion, DETR decoder). The Hierarchical Scene Semantic Enhancer (HSSE) operates in three stages: view-level aggregation (FPN + cross-attention + self-attention to produce compact per-view semantic queries), scene-level interaction (concatenating all view queries with CLIP-encoded language features and applying L_scene=3 self-attention layers for cross-modal fusion), and semantic broadcast (cross-attending the enriched scene-level queries back into multi-scale feature maps to restore fine-grained semantics lost during sparse point sampling). The Language Semantic Enhancer (LSE) builds a Scene Information Database from ground-truth annotations (object relationships and 3D locations), then prompts GPT-4o-mini with k=50 relevant context descriptions to augment each training description with additional spatial anchors, reducing ambiguity without altering the original content. The enhanced baseline further replaces RoBERTa with CLIP for text encoding and incorporates CBGS during 3D detection pretraining.

## Results

On the EmbodiedScan benchmark, DenseGrounding achieves 44.41% OverallACC25 when trained on the full dataset, a 5.81% absolute improvement over the previous SOTA (EmbodiedScan at 38.60%). On the mini training subset (~20% of full data), DenseGrounding reaches 41.34% OverallACC25, a 7.56% gain over EmbodiedScan (33.78%) and 5.57% over the enhanced baseline EmbodiedScan† (35.77%). The method also secures 1st place in the CVPR 2024 Autonomous Grand Challenge Multi-view 3D Visual Grounding Track. Gains are consistent across Easy, Hard, view-independent, and view-dependent evaluation splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DenseGrounding decisively outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest prior method, EmbodiedScan (Wang et al., 2024), it gains +5.81% OverallACC25 on the full set (44.41 vs. 38.60) and +7.56% on the mini set (41.34 vs. 33.78), with the largest relative gains on Hard scenes (+3.35% full, +4.13% mini) and view-dependent descriptions (+5.12% full, +7.99% mini). Against the earlier two-stage methods BUTD-DETR (22.14%) and L3Det (23.07%), the improvement exceeds 20 percentage points, reflecting the architectural shift from proposal-based to single-stage ego-centric multi-view grounding. Even against the enhanced baseline EmbodiedScan† (which incorporates CLIP text encoding and CBGS pretraining), DenseGrounding still leads by 5.57% on the mini set, confirming that the gains come from the HSSE and LSE modules rather than from the baseline improvements alone. In the zero-shot cross-dataset setting (trained on 3RScan+Matterport, tested on ScanNet), DenseGrounding more than doubles the baseline's overall accuracy (18.14% vs. 9.81%), demonstrating superior generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EmbodiedScan (Wang et al., 2024), the current SOTA for ego-centric multi-view 3D visual grounding, and extends the broader 3D visual grounding lineage (ScanRefer, ReferIt3D, ViewRefer, MiKASA) by targeting two specific failure modes: semantic loss from sparse point-cloud fusion and ambiguity in natural language annotations. The Hierarchical Scene Semantic Enhancer (HSSE) introduces a view-level aggregation → scene-level cross-modal interaction → broadcast pipeline that is architecturally distinct from prior multi-view fusion strategies (e.g., ViewRefer's inter-view attention or MiKASA's scene-aware encoder). The Language Semantic Enhancer (LSE) departs from ViewRefer's template-based LLM rephrasing by grounding the LLM in a structured Scene Information Database with object relationships and 3D locations, enabling more targeted disambiguation during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.81–7.56% overall accuracy gains and the CVPR 2024 Autonomous Grand Challenge 1st-place result validate that addressing both visual sparsity and textual ambiguity simultaneously yields compounding benefits in ego-centric 3D grounding. The data-efficiency finding (40% training data outperforming 80% baseline) and the near-2× zero-shot cross-dataset improvement suggest the enriched representations generalize well, which is practically important for deploying grounding in new environments. However, the method still relies on training-time LLM augmentation and cannot resolve genuinely underspecified instructions at inference, leaving open the question of interactive clarification between agents and users in real-world deployment.
