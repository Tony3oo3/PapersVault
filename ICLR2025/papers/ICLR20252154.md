## Problem

Complementary label learning (CLL) trains a multi-class classifier from labels specifying only the classes an instance does NOT belong to. Existing CLL methods focus on designing robust loss functions or exploiting feature-space information, but largely neglect the rich signal available in the model's output space. This paper reformulates CLL as an inverse problem of recovering full label information from output-space confidences, addressing the gap of how to systematically leverage both positive and negative label recovery from model predictions.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images paired with one or more complementary (negative) labels indicating classes the image does not belong to, and produces a standard multi-class classifier. Training uses a two-view PreAct-ResNet18 with shared weights, where each image is processed through both a weakly augmented and a strongly augmented view. The complementary label sets can contain anywhere from 1 to K−1 labels per instance, and the method is evaluated on datasets ranging from 10 (CIFAR-10, STL-10, SVHN, FashionMNIST) to 200 (Tiny-ImageNet) classes.

## Method

PLNL splits the CLL inverse problem into two subtasks: Positive Label Guessing (PLG) for highly-confident instances and Negative Label Enhancement (NLE) for moderately- and under-confident ones. A confidence-based selection module uses a two-view network with shared backbone and memory banks tracking historical prediction confidences over t epochs, applying three criteria (non-complementary argmax, cross-epoch stability, and an instance-aware self-adaptive threshold) to partition instances into three subsets. For highly-confident instances, PLG assigns the argmax class as a pseudo positive label and treats all other classes as negative labels. For the remaining instances, NLE computes k-NN neighbors in the model's output space and enhances each instance's negative label set by adding the most frequently appearing labels among its neighbors' complementary sets, with a more conservative enhancement level for under-confident instances. The paper proves upper bounds on both PLG and NLE error rates and derives a generalization bound showing the learned classifier converges to the clean-label classifier as errors vanish.

## Results

PLNL achieves state-of-the-art classification accuracy across all five benchmark datasets in both single and multiple complementary label settings. The most dramatic improvement is on STL-10 (SCLL), where PLNL reaches 55.25% accuracy versus 34.96% for the previous best (POCR), a gain of 20.29%. On the harder CIFAR-100 (MCLL) setting, PLNL achieves 64.33%, outperforming the previous SOTA ComCo (57.88%) by 6.45%. On SVHN (MCLL), PLNL reaches 98.13%, and on CIFAR-10 (MCLL) it achieves 96.80%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLNL outperforms all seven CLL baselines (UB-EXP, UB-LOG, SCL-EXP, SCL-LOG, POCR, SELF-CL, ComCo) on every dataset in both SCLL and MCLL settings. The largest margin is on STL-10 (SCLL), where PLNL (55.25%) surpasses POCR (34.96%) by 20.29%, and on CIFAR-100 (MCLL), where PLNL (64.33%) beats ComCo (57.88%) by 6.45%. Among the URE-based methods, UB-LOG is generally the strongest but still trails PLNL by 14+ points on STL-10 (SCLL). The feature-learning baselines POCR and ComCo are the closest competitors, yet PLNL still leads by 2.60% on STL-10 (MCLL) and 6.45% on CIFAR-100 (MCLL). In comparisons with SSL pseudo-labeling methods (Fixmatch, Freematch, SemiReward), PLNL achieves both a higher selected ratio (93.40% vs. lower values) and a significantly larger average negative label set size, recovering nearly all 9 negative labels per instance on CIFAR-10 by the end of training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant CLL paradigms—unbiased risk estimator (URE) methods and feature learning (FL) methods—by reframing CLL as an inverse problem solvable from output-space information rather than through loss design or feature-space regularization. It repurposes pseudo-labeling and two-view confidence-based selection from semi-supervised learning (FixMatch, FreeMatch) but extends them with a novel negative label enhancement (NLE) mechanism that recovers supervision from untrustworthy instances, which prior SSL methods discard or handle only via consistency regularization. The unified framework connecting PLG and NLE to pseudo-labeling from a negative-label-recovery perspective is a new conceptual lens not present in prior CLL or SSL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20%+ accuracy gains on STL-10 and consistent improvements across eight datasets (including real-world CLCIFAR benchmarks) demonstrate that output-space information is a substantially underexploited signal in CLL, and the NLE technique is a practical, generalizable component that could transfer to other weakly-supervised settings. The finding that frozen BLIP-2 features yield 93.34% NLE precision versus 80.84% for the model's own output space opens a promising direction for leveraging VLMs in weakly-supervised learning. Open questions include scalability of k-NN-based NLE to very large label spaces, the persistent failure of all methods on biased CIFAR-100, and whether the approach extends beyond image classification to other modalities.
