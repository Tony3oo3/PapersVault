## Problem

Existing convergence analyses for diffusion models with deterministic samplers are fragmented, each targeting a specific forward process and sampler combination, and cannot leverage Girsanov's theorem which is the standard tool for stochastic samplers. The paper asks whether a single unified framework can accommodate general forward processes and diverse deterministic numerical schemes while providing non-asymptotic TV-distance guarantees.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a forward SDE (e.g., VP with f(t,x)=-x, g(t)=√2, or VE with f=0, g=1), a neural-network-estimated score function s_θ trained via denoising score matching, and a deterministic numerical scheme (exponential integrator or DDIM-type). It produces a TV-distance bound between the final sample distribution and the target q_δ, decomposed into five explicit error terms: score estimation error, divergence estimation error, score discretization error, divergence discretization error, and a bias term from the interpolation operator. The analysis uses early stopping at t_N = T−δ to handle unbounded scores near t=0.

## Method

The core technical tool is Lemma 3.2, which bounds the time derivative of TV distance between two ODE flows purely in terms of the drift difference and the divergence of the drifts, avoiding the need for the sampling process's own score (which can be unbounded even under small estimation error). For discrete-time samplers, an interpolation method converts each step's map T_k into a continuous-time ODE with drift b̂(t,x) = ∂F/∂t(t, F_t^{-1}(x)), enabling direct application of Lemma 3.2. The unified framework then decomposes the total error into five terms via two operators: an estimation-error operator Φ_k capturing score mismatch and a divergence-error operator Ψ_k capturing Hessian mismatch. Applied to VP+EI, this yields Õ(d²/ε) iteration complexity; applied to VE+DDIM, it gives the first convergence result for DDIM-type samplers with estimated scores, achieving polynomial complexity Õ(LR⁴d⁴/ε⁵).

## Results

For the VP forward process with the Exponential Integrator (EI) scheme, the paper achieves a TV-distance convergence bound of $\widetilde{O}(d^2/\epsilon)$ iteration complexity (Corollary 5.7), requiring $\widetilde{O}(\epsilon/\sqrt{d})$ score estimation error and $O(\epsilon)$ divergence error. For the VE forward process with the DDIM sampler, the paper establishes a polynomial bound of $\widetilde{O}(LR^4 d^4/\epsilon^5)$ (Corollary 5.11), which is the first convergence guarantee for DDIM-type samplers with estimated scores. In the continuous-time setting for the OU process, the TV distance is bounded by $\sqrt{dT + d\log(1/\delta)}\,\epsilon_{\text{score}} + \epsilon_{\text{div}}$ (Theorem 3.4), which is notably only log-dependent on the early-stopping parameter $\delta$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Li et al. (2024b), which achieves $\widetilde{O}(d^2/\epsilon + d^3/\sqrt{\epsilon})$ for a specific deterministic sampler, this paper's VP+EI result of $\widetilde{O}(d^2/\epsilon)$ removes the $d^3/\sqrt{\epsilon}$ term and applies to a different (EI-based) sampler within a unified framework. Against Li et al. (2024c), which achieves the sharper $\widetilde{O}(d/\epsilon)$ bound, this paper's $\widetilde{O}(d^2/\epsilon)$ is worse in dimension dependence, but the authors note this gap stems from their weaker divergence-only assumption (vs. full Jacobian) and their unified framework's generality. Versus Chen et al. (2023b), which only handles the exact-score case in KL divergence, this paper is the first to provide convergence guarantees for DDIM-type samplers with estimated scores, albeit in TV distance and with a polynomial (not linear) dimension dependence. Compared to Huang et al. (2024a), which requires boundedness of up to second-order derivatives of the score and achieves $O(d^{(p+1)/p}/\epsilon^{1/p})$, this paper's continuous-time bound is strictly better in its $\delta$-dependence (log vs. $1/\delta$) and avoids higher-order derivative assumptions, though the discrete-time iteration complexities are comparable in order.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the probability flow ODE framework (Song et al., 2020c) and DDIM (Song et al., 2020a), but departs fundamentally from prior convergence analyses that relied on Girsanov's theorem or stochastic smoothing. While Chen et al. (2023b), Li et al. (2024b), and Huang et al. (2024a) each provided convergence guarantees for specific forward-process/sampler combinations, this work introduces a unified framework via a key Fokker-Planck-based lemma (Lemma 3.2) that decomposes error into score estimation, divergence estimation, and three discretization terms. The critical conceptual departure is showing that score estimation error alone is provably insufficient for ODE convergence (Theorem 2.1), necessitating an additional divergence error assumption that replaces the full Jacobian closeness required by Li et al. (2024b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence guarantee for DDIM-type samplers with estimated scores, filling a notable gap where prior work (Chen et al., 2023b) required exact score access. The unified framework makes it straightforward to analyze new sampler/forward-process combinations, and the ε^{-1} iteration complexity for deterministic samplers (vs. ε^{-2} for stochastic ones) provides theoretical justification for the empirical efficiency advantage of DDIM over DDPM. Key limitations include the quadratic dimension dependence for VP+EI (vs. linear in Li et al., 2024c), the bounded support assumption, and only polynomial bounds for VE+DDIM due to slow 1/√T decay. An open and practically relevant direction is designing training objectives that simultaneously minimize score and divergence estimation errors.
