## Problem

Standard conformal prediction guarantees only marginal coverage, which can mask severe under- or over-coverage in specific regions of the input space. Existing methods that target conditional coverage—such as conformalized quantile regression or probabilistic conformal prediction (PCP)—are either restricted to one-dimensional outputs, produce suboptimal interval sets for multimodal predictive distributions, or use a fixed-radius ball that fails to adapt to local variability. This paper addresses the gap by combining the distribution-free validity of conformal prediction with an estimate of the conditional distribution P_{Y|X} to achieve approximate conditional coverage in a general, including multi-dimensional, setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a feature vector x, a pre-trained conditional distribution estimator (either an explicit density such as a Mixture Density Network or an implicit generative model from which samples can be drawn), a calibration set of size n, and a significance level α. A key operational detail is that the method requires drawing two independent sets of samples from the conditional distribution at each calibration and test point: one set of M samples to form the prediction set geometry, and a second set of M̃ samples to estimate the adaptive radius τ. The output is a prediction set C_α(x) that takes the form of an interval in 1D or a union of d-dimensional balls of a pointwise-adaptive radius in multi-D settings.

## Method

The CP² framework introduces a pointwise adaptation parameter τ_{x,z} computed from the estimated conditional distribution Π_{Y|X=x}, which determines the radius of the confidence set R_z(x;t) needed to capture 1−α probability mass under the model. The core mechanism balances two quantities via a monotone transformation f_τ(v): the observed conformity score V_z(x,y) (the minimal set size to cover y) and the model-based radius τ_{x,z}. Calibration proceeds by computing transformed scores f_{τ_k}^{-1}(V_k) on the calibration set and taking their (1−α)-quantile, preserving the standard split-conformal marginal validity guarantee. The final prediction set at a test point uses radius f_{τ_{x,z}}(Q_{1−α}(μ_n)), so the set size adapts to local conditional variability. Two instantiations are provided: CP²-HPD, which uses an explicit density to construct highest-predictive-density regions, and CP²-PCP, which uses an implicit generative model and forms prediction sets as unions of balls around sampled centers. Theoretical analysis establishes marginal validity, a non-asymptotic conditional coverage bound depending on the total variation distance between the true and estimated conditional distributions, and asymptotic conditional coverage under a weak consistency assumption on the estimator.

## Results

On 9 real-world regression datasets (bike, bio, blog, fb1, fb2, meps19–21, temp), CP²-PCP achieves valid worst-slab conditional coverage (target 1−α = 0.9, 1−δ = 0.1) across all datasets, while CQR fails on blog and temp, and PCP consistently undercovers. In terms of prediction set size (scaled by response std), CP²-PCP significantly outperforms CQR and CQR2 on bike, bio, and temp, and produces smaller sets than CPCG on meps datasets. On 5 multi-target regression datasets (rf1, rf2, scm1d, scm20d, indoor, sgemm_small), CP² maintains marginal coverage near 0.9 and conditional coverage close to nominal, whereas naive 1D methods (CQR, CQR2, CHR) produce significant overcover and PCP significantly undercovers on rf1, rf2, and scm1d.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CP²-PCP outperforms PCP (Wang et al., 2023) on all datasets in conditional coverage: PCP consistently struggles with worst-slab coverage on 1D tasks and significantly undercovers on multi-target datasets (rf1, rf2, scm1d), while CP²-PCP maintains valid coverage by adapting the ball radius pointwise via τ_{x,z}. Against CQR (Romano et al., 2019), CP²-PCP wins on conditional coverage (CQR fails on blog and temp) and on set size (significantly smaller on bike, bio, temp), though CPCG and CP²-PCP have comparable set sizes on blog/fb1/fb2. Against CHR (Sesia & Romano, 2021), CP²-PCP is more stable in achieving conditional coverage, while CHR sometimes produces narrower sets but fails more often. Against CPCG (Gibbs et al., 2023), CP²-PCP matches or slightly exceeds conditional coverage on blog/fb1/fb2 with comparable set sizes, but produces smaller sets on meps datasets and runs ~10× faster at prediction time. Against LCR (Guan, 2023), CP²-PCP achieves substantially higher conditional coverage on larger datasets. Against CDsplit+ (Izbicki et al., 2022), CP²-PCP provides more stable conditional coverage with less run-to-run variance, while CDsplit+ tends to overcover marginally and shows high variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the split conformal prediction framework by incorporating a conditional distribution estimator to achieve approximate conditional coverage, going beyond the marginal-only guarantees of standard conformal methods. It builds most directly on Probabilistic Conformal Prediction (PCP; Wang et al., 2023), which uses conditional generative models to form prediction sets as unions of balls, but departs from PCP's fixed-radius design by introducing a pointwise adaptation parameter τ that rescales the radius based on the local conditional distribution. The theoretical analysis draws on total variation distance bounds connecting the estimated conditional distribution to the true one, and the calibration mechanism via the transformation function f_τ(v) connects to prior work on rescaled conformity scores (Han et al., 2022; Deutschmann et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical pathway to conditional coverage in conformal prediction by showing that a good conditional distribution estimator (e.g., a Mixture Density Network) combined with pointwise radius adaptation yields sets that are both marginally valid and approximately conditionally valid, with non-asymptotic bounds depending on the TV distance between the true and estimated conditional distributions. The natural extension to multi-dimensional targets (Y ∈ R^q) is practically important, as most prior conditional conformal methods are inherently one-dimensional and require Bonferroni corrections that produce overly conservative sets. Open questions include how the method performs when the conditional distribution estimator is misspecified, the computational cost of sampling-based implementations at scale, and whether the TV distance bound can be tightened or replaced with more interpretable quantities.
