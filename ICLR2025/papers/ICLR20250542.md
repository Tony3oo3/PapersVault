## Problem

Existing time-varying counterfactual prediction (TCP) methods face a dual challenge: Transformer-based backbones like the Causal Transformer scale quadratically with sequence length and accumulate estimation error over multi-step horizons, while adversarial covariate balancing strategies cause over-balancing that corrupts covariate representations and degrades prediction accuracy. No prior work has combined a linear-complexity sequence backbone with a bias-correction mechanism that preserves covariate information in the time-varying setting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a per-patient sequence of time-varying covariates X_t ∈ R^{d_x}, binary/categorical treatment assignments A_t, observed outcomes Y_t, and static features V (e.g., gender, age), concatenated at each time step into a single input vector. Given a counterfactual treatment sequence a_{t:t+τ-1} of length τ, the model outputs the expected counterfactual outcome E[y_{t+τ}(a_{t:t+τ-1}) | H_t] as a scalar or d_y-dimensional vector. Sequences range from 20 to 200 time steps across benchmarks, with prediction horizons τ from 1 to 10.

## Method

Mamba-CDSP replaces the Transformer backbone with a single Mamba (selective state-space model) block, where the 1D convolutional layer is swapped for a dropout layer to prevent overfitting on temporal data. The key debiasing mechanism, CDSP, regularizes Mamba's selective parameters (B̄, C̄) by minimizing the cross-covariance between the current treatment a_t and the hidden state h_{t-1}, which decomposes into a step-by-step orthogonal constraint K_i Σ_{X̃_i^h, a_t} Σ_{X̃_i^h, a_t}^T = 0 at each time step. This is equivalent to enforcing first-moment matching across treatment groups without aligning variances, yielding a tighter finite-sample risk bound than adversarial balancing (which aligns both means and covariances). The total training objective is L_MSE + α·L_CDSP, where L_MSE updates all parameters and L_CDSP updates only the selective parameters, keeping the debiasing computation at O(B((d_X^h)^3 + d_A^h)T) versus O(B((d_X^h)^3 + (d_X^h)^2 d_Y^h + |A|)T) for adversarial discriminators.

## Results

On the semi-synthetic MIMIC-III benchmark, Mamba-CDSP achieves the lowest RMSE across all prediction horizons (τ=1 to 10), reaching 0.19 at τ=1 and 0.47 at τ=10, compared to 0.21 and 0.59 for the best baseline (Causal Transformer). On real-world MIMIC-III data, Mamba-CDSP reduces RMSE by nearly 50% at τ=5 (5.25 vs. 10.12 for CT), and on the M5 dataset it achieves 4.08 RMSE at τ=1 versus 4.59 for CT. In terms of efficiency, Mamba-CDSP requires only 12±2.3 min total runtime and 0.16 sec inference time per sample, compared to 156 min and 4.64 sec for CT on real-world data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mamba-CDSP outperforms all six baselines (MSMs, RMSNs, CRN, G-Net, EDCT, and Causal Transformer) across every dataset and prediction horizon tested, with statistically significant improvements (p ≤ 0.01). Against the strongest baseline, Causal Transformer (CT), Mamba-CDSP reduces RMSE from 0.21 to 0.19 at τ=1 and from 0.59 to 0.47 at τ=10 on semi-synthetic MIMIC-III, and from 10.12 to 5.25 at τ=5 on real-world MIMIC-III (a ~48% reduction). On the M5 real-world dataset, Mamba-CDSP achieves 4.08 RMSE at τ=1 versus 4.59 for CT, and maintains a 1.96 RMSE advantage at τ=6 (8.39 vs. 10.35). The gap widens substantially as the prediction horizon τ increases and as confounding strength γ grows (e.g., at γ=20, τ=5: Mamba-CDSP 6.52 vs. CT 18.32 on the TG simulator). In terms of efficiency, Mamba-CDSP trains in 12 min versus 156 min for CT and uses only 27.3K parameters versus 69K for CT on real-world data, while achieving 29× faster per-sample inference (0.16 sec vs. 4.64 sec).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) time-varying counterfactual prediction (TCP) methods that correct sequential confounding bias via adversarial balancing (CRN, Causal Transformer) or inverse-probability weighting (RMSNs, MSMs), and (2) selective state-space models (Mamba) that achieve linear-time long-sequence modeling. It departs from the dominant adversarial-balancing paradigm by proposing Covariate-based Decorrelation towards Selective Parameters (CDSP), which regularizes Mamba's selective parameters to remove cross-covariance between current treatment and historical representations rather than enforcing full distributional invariance. The theoretical analysis builds on the risk-bound framework of Shalit et al. (2017) and the over-balancing observations of Huang et al. (2024), formalizing why mean-only alignment (CDSP) yields tighter bounds than full distributional matching (ADB).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first SSM-based counterfactual predictor, Mamba-CDSP opens a new architectural direction for TCP, offering ~13× speedup over Causal Transformer with substantially lower RMSE (up to ~50% on real-world MIMIC-III). However, the CDSP mechanism is explicitly designed for linear SSMs, limiting its immediate generalizability to nonlinear sequence backbones. Key open questions include whether the decorrelation principle extends to non-linear state-space models, how confounding bias risk evolves under multi-step prediction, and whether multi-task learning theory can provide principled guidance for balancing bias correction against covariate preservation in sequential settings.
