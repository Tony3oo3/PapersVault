## Problem

Existing LLM evaluators apply a fixed set of coarse-grained dimensions (e.g., helpfulness, harmlessness) uniformly across all queries, ignoring that different scenarios—creative writing, math reasoning, legal drafting—demand fundamentally different evaluation criteria. Moreover, most evaluators output only a single overall score or ranking, providing no interpretable breakdown of which specific quality factors drive the judgment and how much each contributes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251442/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SaMer takes a query (instruction) and either a single model response (for rating) or a pair of responses (for pairwise comparison) as input. It produces a four-part structured output: (1) a binary mask over 42 pre-defined dimensions indicating which are relevant to the query's scenario, (2) a continuous score in (0,1) for each active dimension, (3) a set of non-negative normalized weights summing to 1 across dimensions, and (4) a scalar overall score computed as the weighted sum of dimension scores. The model operates on a frozen 4096-dim embedding from Llama-3-8B, with all prediction done through lightweight MLP heads, yielding inference times of ~0.04s (rating) and ~0.10s (pairwise) per instance.

## Method

SaMer couples a frozen Llama-3-8B text encoder with three specialized MLP heads trained jointly: a dimension predictor (multi-label classification over 42 dimensions using ZLPR loss to handle label imbalance), a dimension scorer (pairwise margin-ranking loss on concatenated query-response embeddings, with regression loss for tied dimensions), and a dimension weighter (softmax-normalized non-negative weights over dimensions). The overall score is the dot product of predicted weights and dimension scores, trained with an additional pairwise ranking loss on the aggregate. Training uses a 135K-sample fine-grained preference dataset spanning 36 scenarios (categorized by Maslow's hierarchy), where GPT-4o annotated dimension-level pairwise preferences and a STaR-inspired filtering step retained only 78% of samples whose GPT-4o overall judgment matched human-verified labels. The dimension predictor undergoes a warm-up pretraining phase before joint optimization, and the entire model is initialized from an ArmoRM-8B reward model checkpoint.

## Results

On single-rating benchmarks, SaMer-8B achieves Pearson correlations of 0.476 (Vicuna Bench), 0.515 (FLASK Eval), and 0.790 (Feedback Bench), surpassing GPT-4o (0.434/0.501/0.735) and GPT-4o-mini (0.456/0.493/0.752) across all three. On pairwise comparison, SaMer achieves 87.33% on HHH Alignment (total), 77.72% on AutoJ Eval (w/o TIE), and 81.38% on AlpacaEval (w/o TIE), ranking first among open-source models in 9 of 15 sub-tasks. On the fine-grained MD-Eval benchmark, SaMer reaches 75.67% dimension-level accuracy and 82.33% overall accuracy, outperforming GPT-4o-mini (72.99%/78.00%) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (61.63%/74.15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary LLMs, SaMer outperforms GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini on all three single-rating benchmarks (e.g., 0.790 vs. 0.735 Pearson on Feedback Bench) and on fine-grained MD-Eval (82.33% vs. 78.00% overall accuracy), but GPT-4o still leads on HHH Alignment total (90.95% vs. 87.33%) and LLMBar Natural (99% vs. 84%). Among open-source evaluator baselines, SaMer clearly surpasses AutoJ-13B (77.72% vs. 76.74% on AutoJ w/o TIE; 81.38% vs. 77.72% on AlpacaEval w/o TIE) and all Prometheus variants (e.g., 87.33% vs. 74.66% HHH total vs. Prometheus2-7B). The closest competitor is ArmoRM-8B (the backbone initialization), which edges out SaMer on HHH Alignment total (88.24% vs. 87.33%) and LLMBar Natural (93% vs. 84%), but SaMer wins decisively on AlpacaEval (81.38% vs. 76.74% w/o TIE) and AutoJ Eval (77.72% vs. 76.74% w/o TIE). General-purpose LLMs (Llama-2/3, Mistral) are far behind, with Llama-3.1-8B-Inst reaching only 83.18% on HHH total and 74.24% on AlpacaEval w/o TIE. On the dimension-selection task (Table 6), SaMer achieves 74.84% precision and 72.33% recall, substantially exceeding GPT-4o (63.42%/38.10%) and all open-source baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SaMer departs from the dominant LLM-as-judge paradigm (GPT-4, Claude) and fine-tuned generative evaluators (PandaLM, AutoJ, Prometheus/Prometheus2) by replacing free-form text generation with a discriminative architecture: a frozen Llama-3-8B text encoder topped with three lightweight MLP heads for dimension prediction, scoring, and weighting. It builds on the ArmoRM reward-model backbone but extends it with scenario-conditioned multi-dimensional structure, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior multi-attribute evaluators (HelpSteer, UltraFeedback) that use fixed, coarse dimensions regardless of query type. The ZLPR multi-label loss for dimension selection and the margin-ranking + regression hybrid for scoring are borrowed from multi-label classification and learning-to-rank literature, respectively, but their joint application to scenario-aware LLM evaluation is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SaMer provides a practical path toward interpretable LLM evaluation: by outputting per-dimension scores and scenario-adaptive weights, it makes the 'why' behind an overall quality judgment transparent, which is valuable for both model debugging and alignment research. The paper's limitation that 42 pre-defined dimensions across 36 scenarios may not cover all real-world use cases, and that overlapping dimensions require further disambiguation, leaves open the question of how to scale this framework to truly open-ended dimension spaces. The authors also note SaMer could serve as a multi-objective reward model for RLHF, potentially guiding LLMs to optimize specific quality dimensions rather than a single scalar score — a direction that could influence future alignment pipelines.
