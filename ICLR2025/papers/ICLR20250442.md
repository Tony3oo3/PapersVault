## Problem

Deforming neural implicit surfaces to recover physically plausible intermediate shapes between two point clouds remains challenging because implicit representations lack explicit surface point locations and traceable neighborhood information, preventing direct manipulation and the application of physical constraints like as-rigid-as-possible regularization. Existing implicit-field deformation methods either require intermediate shape supervision, pre-trained SDF networks, user-defined handle points, or produce degenerated signed distance fields, and none simultaneously recover both the underlying surfaces and physically plausible deformations in a fully unsupervised, end-to-end manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes two 3D point clouds P0 and P1 (each ~20,000 sampled surface points) along with sparse ground-truth correspondences between them (as few as 1–5% of points suffice for strong results). It outputs a single time-varying neural implicit surface f(x,t) parameterized over t∈[0,1] that encodes the SDF at every intermediate time step, together with an explicit velocity field V(x) that drives the deformation. Notably, the two input point clouds can differ substantially in sparsity, completeness, and topology, and the method handles both rigid (extrinsic) and non-rigid (intrinsic) deformations within the same framework.

## Method

The approach jointly trains two 8-layer MLPs — a Velocity-Net (256 nodes/layer) and an Implicit-Net (512 nodes/layer) with Softplus activations and invertible Lipschitz positional encoding — using a Lagrangian fluid-mechanics perspective. The velocity field is regularized by a smoothness operator L = −αΔ + γI (guaranteeing diffeomorphic trajectories) and an optional divergence-free constraint for volume preservation. The key technical contribution is a Modified Level-Set Equation (MLSE) that compactly embeds the Eikonal constraint ‖∇f‖=1 directly into the transport PDE ∂t f + V·∇f = −λ_l f R(x,t), eliminating the need for a separate reinitialization step or pre-trained SDF networks. Training is fully end-to-end and self-supervised via JAX (for higher-order derivatives), with a warm-up phase that first converges the velocity field before gradually introducing the implicit-field loss, and the Implicit-Net is initialized as a unit sphere to satisfy the SDF initial condition without pre-training.

## Results

On the DeformingThings4D dataset (fox and bear sequences, 55 intermediate meshes each), the method achieves CD of 0.108 and HD of 0.114 on fox, and CD of 0.260 and HD of 0.265 on bear (all scaled by 10³ and 10² respectively). On the 4D-Dress dataset, the method reports CD of 0.270 (×10⁴), HD of 0.047 (×10²), and surface-area standard deviation of 0.023 (×10), demonstrating volume preservation. Training takes approximately 20 minutes per pair on a single GPU, roughly 1/5 the time of NISE excluding pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms both LipMLP and NISE on all metrics across both fox and bear sequences. On fox, CD improves from 1.745 (LipMLP) and 0.178 (NISE) to 0.108, while HD improves from 2.456/0.262 to 0.114; on bear, CD improves from 2.649/0.366 to 0.260 and HD from 1.401/0.560 to 0.265. Against NFGP (Yang et al., 2021), the comparison is primarily qualitative in the main paper since NFGP requires user-defined handle points and separate training per time step (~15 hours per step); on the 4D-Dress quantitative evaluation (Table 1), NFGP achieves a similar CD (0.272 vs. 0.270) but a lower HD (0.025 vs. 0.047) and higher surface-area variance (0.075 vs. 0.023), and cannot report per-point RMSE. Against the mesh-based SmS (Cao et al., 2024a), the method produces comparable intermediate shapes but with smoother meshes (arbitrary resolution rendering) and the added ability to handle inconsistent topology and incomplete inputs that mesh-based methods cannot address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of neural implicit surface representation (Sitzmann et al. 2020, Gropp et al. 2020), level-set-based implicit field deformation (Mehta et al. 2022, Novello et al. 2023), and diffeomorphic velocity field theory from fluid mechanics (Dupuis et al. 1998, Beg et al. 2005). It departs from prior implicit deformation methods (NFGP, NISE, LipMLP) by eliminating the need for pre-trained SDF networks, user-defined handle points, or intermediate shape supervision, instead jointly learning the time-varying implicit surface and an explicit velocity field in a single end-to-end optimization. The key technical departure is the Modified Level-Set Equation (MLSE), which compactly embeds the Eikonal constraint into the transport equation following Bothe et al. (2024) and Fricke et al. (2022), replacing the separate reinitialization PDE that prior approaches required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to recover physically plausible intermediate shapes from two point clouds using neural implicit representations without any intermediate shape supervision, opening the door to unsupervised 4D shape generation from sparse inputs. The ability to handle topology changes, incomplete shapes, and highly asymmetric sparsity (20k → 2k points) addresses practical limitations of mesh-based deformation that cannot change connectivity. However, the method still requires sparse correspondences (even 1% helps significantly), struggles with large deformations producing surface artifacts, and the physical plausibility guarantee is limited to volume preservation via divergence-free constraints rather than full mechanical simulation. Open questions include extending to fully dynamic (video) implicit surface generation and removing the correspondence requirement entirely.
