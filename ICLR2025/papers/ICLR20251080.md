## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting and NeRF-based novel view synthesis methods assume high-quality input images and degrade significantly under real-world conditions such as low resolution, noise, blur, and JPEG compression. Existing degradation-specific solutions (e.g., NeRFLiX for blur, SRGS for low resolution) fail to generalize across degradation types and even underperform the original baselines on out-of-domain degradations. The paper identifies that under degraded conditions, 3DGS allocates too few Gaussian primitives to detail-rich regions, causing structural omissions in rendered views.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N multi-view low-quality RGB images (N×H×W×3) along with COLMAP-calibrated camera poses and a sparse point cloud initialization. From the input images, it derives high-frequency edge-aware maps via a Sobel gradient operator and normalizes them to [0,1] before element-wise multiplication with the original image. Both the images and edge maps are downsampled 2× for the ESFG module to match the number of Gaussian primitives M via MLP-based scale transformation. The output is a set of novel-view rendered images at the original resolution, produced by the modified 3DGS rendering pipeline in approximately 0.2 seconds per frame.

## Method

HQGS extends 3DGS with two complementary mechanisms targeting different frequency bands. The Edge-Semantic Fusion Guidance (ESFG) module extracts edge-aware features from Sobel-derived high-frequency maps and semantic-aware features from the low-quality images, then fuses them via cross-attention (edge features as query, semantic features as key/value) and concatenation; the resulting fused feature, passed through a sigmoid gate, modulates the 3D position parameters of each Gaussian primitive to concentrate primitives in detail-rich regions. The Structural Cosine Similarity (SCS) loss computes cosine similarity between low-frequency structure-aware maps of the rendered and target images (obtained by masking with 1−∇I′), enforcing global structural consistency beyond pixel-level L1. The total loss is L = λ₁·L₁ + λ₂·L_SCS with λ₁=1 and λ₂=5, and the ESFG module is trained with a learning rate of 1e-6 while 3DGS parameters follow the original schedule.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset, HQGS achieves the best PSNR across all five degradation types: 26.48 dB (low resolution), 26.19 dB (JPEG compression), 26.55 dB (motion blur), 27.20 dB (Gaussian noise), and 27.27 dB (mixed degradation), with corresponding LPIPS values of 0.065, 0.077, 0.056, 0.071, and 0.057. On the DeblurNeRF dataset, HQGS reaches 27.74 dB PSNR, 0.904 SSIM, and 0.045 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline NeRFLiX by 0.79 dB in PSNR. HQGS also matches the ~0.2 s per-frame rendering speed of 3DGS and SRGS, while NeRF-based methods require 4.3–10.7 s per frame.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HQGS outperforms all six baselines across every degradation type on both LLFF and DeblurNeRF. Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor SRGS, HQGS gains 0.52 dB PSNR / 0.022 LPIPS on low resolution, 0.65 dB / 0.014 on JPEG compression, 0.63 dB / 0.020 on blur, 0.73 dB / 0.015 on noise, and 0.97 dB / 0.017 on mixed degradation (LLFF). Against the strongest NeRF-based method NeRFLiX, HQGS leads by 0.90 dB / 0.028 (low res) and 1.23 dB / 0.007 (mixed) on LLFF, and by 0.79 dB / 0.016 LPIPS on DeblurNeRF. The gap widens substantially under severe degradation: at 8× downsampling, HQGS achieves 31.37 dB vs. 30.11 dB (SRGS) and 29.66 dB (NeRFLiX); at noise variance 50, HQGS reaches 26.31 dB vs. 23.21 dB (SRGS) and 23.32 dB (NeRFLiX). HQGS also matches the ~0.2 s rendering speed of 3DGS and SRGS, while NeRF-based methods (NeRF, NeRFLiX, NVSR, NaN) require 4.3–10.7 s per frame.

## Relation to Prior Work

HQGS extends the 3DGS framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) by adding two targeted mechanisms: an Edge-Semantic Fusion Guidance (ESFG) module that uses Sobel-derived edge maps and cross-attention to modulate Gaussian primitive placement, and a Structural Cosine Similarity (SCS) loss for low-frequency consistency. Prior degradation-aware methods (NeRFLiX, SRGS, NVSR, NaN) each target a specific degradation type (blur, low resolution, noise) and show poor cross-degradation generalization, as the paper demonstrates empirically. HQGS departs from this degradation-specific paradigm by treating edge structure as a universal high-frequency prior that remains informative across all degradation types, positioning itself as a general robustness solution rather than a single-degradation fix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a single, real-time (0.2s/frame) framework that handles five degradation types simultaneously, with gains that grow as degradation severity increases (up to 3.1 dB over SRGS at high noise). This is relevant for real-world AR/VR pipelines where camera captures are rarely pristine. Limitations include reliance on COLMAP for camera poses, use of a fixed Sobel operator rather than learned edge detection, and evaluation limited to two relatively small datasets (LLFF, DeblurNeRF). Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether learned edge detectors would further improve the ESFG module, and whether the cross-attention mechanism is truly necessary versus simpler fusion strategies.
