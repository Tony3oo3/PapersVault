## Problem

Existing benchmarks for multimodal foundation models (MMFMs) either assess general helpfulness or focus on a single trustworthiness dimension such as fairness or privacy, leaving no unified framework to holistically evaluate safety, hallucination, fairness, privacy, adversarial robustness, and OOD generalization simultaneously. This gap obscures the full trustworthiness profile of deployed MMFMs and hinders targeted improvements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250148/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For T2I models, the benchmark consumes text prompts in vanilla, transformed, jailbroken, Shakespearean, or rare-linguistic forms (plus adversarial suffixes) and produces generated images scored via HGR/BR (safety), task accuracy (hallucination), G/I/O fairness scores, CLIP-embedding similarity (memorization), and robust/OOD accuracy. For I2T models, inputs include clean, adversarially perturbed, corrupted (Zoom Blur, Gaussian Noise, Pixelate), or style-transformed (Van Gogh, oil painting, watercolor) images paired with questions, yielding VQA accuracy, location/PII inference rates, and performance-drop metrics. Challenging instances are selected via surrogate-model filtering so that surrogates succeed on in-distribution samples but fail on the generated OOD/adversarial variants.

## Method

MMDT is a modular evaluation platform with four components—benchmark orchestration, job scheduling, inference runtimes (supporting vLLM and cloud APIs), and results analysis—that unifies six trustworthiness perspectives under one pipeline. For each perspective, the authors design dedicated red-teaming algorithms: a 36-category risk taxonomy with jailbreak/typography/illustration scenarios for safety; six hallucination scenarios (natural selection, distraction, counterfactual reasoning, co-occurrence, misleading, OCR) crossed with five tasks; three fairness metrics (group, individual, overkill) over 1,776 T2I and 12,232 I2T prompts; CLIP-embedding memorization checks on LAION-1k plus PII and street-view location inference; GCG-style adversarial suffix optimization for T2I and MMP-style image perturbation for I2T; and LLM-driven text-style or image-corruption transformations for OOD testing. Data quality is ensured through manual verification and surrogate-model-based difficulty selection, and the platform supports dynamic data regeneration to remain effective as models evolve.

## Results

Across six trustworthiness perspectives, no single MMFM consistently outperforms others. On safety (Table 1), GPT-4V achieves the lowest harmful content generation rate (HGR) among I2T models (0.014 avg), while Nova Canvas leads T2I models (0.117 avg HGR); open-source T2I models like Flux (0.306) and SDXL (0.258) are significantly less safe. On hallucination (Table 2), all models score below 50% average accuracy, with Flux (39.7%) and DALL-E 3 (38.7%) leading T2I, and Nova Pro (46.9%) leading I2T. On adversarial robustness (Table 6), GPT-4o achieves the best I2T robust accuracy (90.04%) while Nova Canvas leads T2I (62.42%), with spatial reasoning being the hardest task for all models (best: 54.51% for Gemini Pro-1.5). On privacy, GPT-4o achieves 98.16% country-level location inference and 27.13% ZIP-code accuracy, while T2I diffusion models exhibit concept-level memorization of LAION training data (CLIP similarity up to 0.7765 for Nova Canvas).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 8 T2I models (DALL-E 2/3, SDXL, Dreamlike, Openjourney, DF-IF, Flux, Nova Canvas) and 10 I2T models (GPT-4V/4o, LLaVa, Gemini Pro-1.5, Llama-3.2, CogVLM, InternVL2, Mini-InternVL, Nova Lite/Pro) across six trustworthiness perspectives. On safety, GPT-4V dominates I2T models with near-zero HGR (0.014) across all scenarios, outperforming GPT-4o (0.089) by 6× and Llama-3.2 (0.161) by 11×; among T2I models, Nova Canvas (0.117) and DALL-E 3 (0.138) are substantially safer than Flux (0.306) and SDXL (0.258). On hallucination, Nova Pro (46.9%) edges out GPT-4o (44.6%) and Llama-3.2 (44.6%) for I2T, while Flux (39.7%) and DALL-E 3 (38.7%) lead T2I, with open-source models like SDXL (24.7%) and LLaVa (31.4%) trailing by 10–15 points. On adversarial robustness, GPT-4o (90.04%) surpasses GPT-4V (85.27%) and Llama-3.2 (84.39%) for I2T, while Nova Canvas (62.42%) and Flux (61.60%) lead T2I over SDXL (54.00%) and Openjourney (46.22%). On privacy, GPT-4o achieves the highest location inference accuracy (98.16% country, 60.23% city, 27.13% ZIP), outperforming GPT-4V (91.03%, 40.00%, 12.18%) and Llama-3.2 (88.97%, 41.61%, 11.26%), while Nova Canvas shows the strongest training-data memorization (CLIP sim 0.7765) versus DALL-E 3 (0.6335). On fairness, Llama-3.2 achieves the lowest group unfairness for I2T (Gs=0.033) but suffers extreme overkill fairness (O=0.995) due to over-refusal, while GPT-4o balances group fairness (Gs=0.142) with the lowest individual unfairness (I=0.681). No model wins across all perspectives, confirming the paper's central finding that trustworthiness is multi-faceted and no current MMFM is uniformly safe.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMDT unifies and extends the fragmented landscape of MMFM trustworthiness evaluation, where prior benchmarks either assess helpfulness (e.g., HRS-Bench, MMMU) or focus on a single perspective such as fairness (DALL-Eval) or safety (HEx-PHI, AIR-Bench 2024). For adversarial robustness, it repurposes GCG-style prompt optimization (Zou et al., 2023) and MMP/Cheating Suffix-style targeted attacks (Yang et al., 2024a) to generate transferable adversarial inputs for both T2I and I2T models. The risk taxonomy is synthesized from government regulations, company policies (Stability AI, Hive), and existing safety benchmarks, while the hallucination and OOD scenarios draw on COCO and HRS-Bench as base data but introduce novel red-teaming transformations (counterfactual reasoning, co-occurrence, Shakespearean style, image corruptions) not present in prior single-perspective evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMDT is the first unified platform covering six trustworthiness perspectives (safety, hallucination, fairness, privacy, adversarial robustness, OOD) for both T2I and I2T models, and its central finding—that no single MMFM consistently outperforms across all perspectives—challenges the assumption that scaling or a single alignment strategy can yield broadly trustworthy models. The practical implication that input-level bypass rate does not correlate with output-level harmfulness urges the field to move beyond rejection-rate metrics toward evaluating actual generated content. Open questions include how to jointly optimize across competing trustworthiness objectives (e.g., the observed trade-off between group fairness and overkill fairness in I2T models), how to address concept-level memorization in diffusion models without degrading quality, and whether the platform's dynamic data generation can keep pace with rapidly evolving MMFMs.
