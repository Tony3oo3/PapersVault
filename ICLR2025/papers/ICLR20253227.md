## Problem

Conventional MoE-based LLMs use static Top-K routing that activates a fixed number of experts for every token, ignoring the varying semantic importance and difficulty of different tokens. This one-size-fits-all allocation wastes compute on simple tokens while under-serving complex ones, creating a fundamental tension between efficiency and performance that has not been addressed by prior dynamic routing approaches, which rely on fixed thresholds rather than learnable per-token decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253227/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each MoE layer, the allocator receives the token's hidden state (a C-dimensional vector) and produces a probability distribution over all N possible expert counts via a single linear projection followed by softmax. A non-differentiable sample from this distribution yields the per-token expert count k*, which is then passed to the frozen router to select the top-k* experts. The final MoE layer output is the weighted sum over the k* activated experts, identical in form to standard Top-K but with a variable k per token rather than a global constant.

## Method

Ada-K introduces a lightweight linear allocator (one projection matrix per MoE layer, ~2M total parameters) that learns a per-token policy over expert counts, with the base LLM kept entirely frozen. Because the sampling operation is non-differentiable, the allocators are trained end-to-end via PPO: the token representation is the state, the sampled expert count is the action, and the reward is the log-likelihood of the next token at the final layer, with a reinforce-with-baseline advantage using the default Top-K as baseline. A separate activation regularization loss (the expectation of each allocator's output distribution) is added with a tunable weight λ to control the trade-off between accuracy and expert-count reduction. A P-Warm warm-start strategy pre-trains allocators using nucleus-sampling (Top-P) pseudo-labels derived from the frozen router's probability distribution, mitigating the instability of random initialization over the large discrete action space. Training requires only 10K samples, one epoch, and under 8 hours even for the 140B-parameter Mixtral-8x22B model.

## Results

Across four MoE-based LLMs (Mixtral-8x22B, Mixtral-8x7B, DeepSeek-MoE-16B, Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B), Ada-K routing consistently improves average benchmark accuracy by 0.61–0.77 points over the default Top-K baseline while reducing activated expert counts by 30–40%. This translates to over 25% FLOPs reduction (e.g., 14.08T vs. 20.04T for Mixtral-8x22B) and 20–39% inference speedup (1.22×–1.31×). Training requires only ~1–3.5M trainable parameters and under 8 hours even for the 140B-parameter Mixtral-8x22B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Ada-K against two prior dynamic-routing methods (MoED and D2DMoE) and against static Top-K with reduced k, all evaluated on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B. Ada-K (55.13 avg acc, 35.5% reduction) substantially outperforms MoED(p=0.3) (53.45 acc, 32.4% reduction) and MoED(p=0.4) (53.60 acc, 28.6% reduction) by 1.5–1.7 accuracy points at a comparable or higher compression rate. Similarly, Ada-K beats D2D(τ=0.1) (53.73 acc, 27.8% reduction) and D2D(τ=0.2) (53.64 acc, 31.5% reduction) by 1.4–1.5 points while achieving a higher activation reduction. Against static Top-K with reduced k (e.g., k=2 at 50% reduction yielding 52.54 acc, or k=3 at 25% reduction yielding 53.34 acc), Ada-K at 35.5% reduction achieves 55.13 acc, demonstrating that the learned per-token dynamic allocation is strictly superior to any fixed-k configuration at the same or lower compression level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard Top-K MoE routing paradigm (Shazeer et al., 2017) by replacing the fixed activation count with a per-token dynamic decision made by a lightweight learnable allocator. The training framework repurposes PPO (Stiennon et al., 2020) from its typical RLHF application to optimize a non-differentiable discrete routing action, with a warm-start strategy based on nucleus sampling to stabilize early training. It departs from prior quasi-dynamic approaches (MoE-D, D2DMoE) that use fixed probability thresholds to approximate variable expert counts, instead learning a full distribution over expert counts end-to-end. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play module that leaves the original router and all expert weights frozen, distinguishing it from approaches that retrain the full MoE stack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 25%+ FLOPs reduction and 20%+ inference speedup with simultaneous accuracy gains across four MoE architectures (14B–140B) makes this a directly deployable efficiency technique for production MoE LLMs. The remarkably low training overhead (~2M parameters, under 8 hours even for a 140B model) lowers the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the PPO-based training remains stable and effective as MoE models scale to hundreds of experts per layer, whether the learned allocator policies transfer across model families without retraining, and whether the per-layer allocator can be further compressed or distilled into a closed-form heuristic. The finding that middle layers and content words drive most of the expert activation budget suggests future work could target layer-specific or token-type-specific sparsity schedules.
