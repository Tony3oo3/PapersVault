## Problem

Existing neural spatiotemporal models assume dense observations on fixed spatiotemporal grids or at fixed sensor locations, making them inapplicable to real-world sensor networks that collect data at random times and locations (e.g., crowdsourced earthquake detection, floating buoys, vehicle-mounted pollution sensors). No prior method simultaneously models both the underlying PDE-driven system dynamics and the stochastic observation process (when and where measurements occur), leaving a gap for learning from unconstrained, point-process-style observations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a variable-length sequence of (time, location, value) triplets where the number of observations N differs per trajectory and observations are non-overlapping in both time and space. A fixed initial time window defines the context (e.g., first 0.5 s of a 2 s trajectory), whose length in observations varies across trajectories. The model outputs both a predicted observation value at any queried (t, x) and a learned intensity function over space-time that predicts where and when the next observation will occur, unifying state forecasting with event prediction.

## Method

The model uses a low-dimensional latent state z(t) evolved by a neural ODE, decoded to a continuous spatiotemporal field u(t,x) via an MLP that takes z(t) + proj(x) as input (implicit neural representation). This field parameterizes both a non-homogeneous Poisson process intensity λ(u(t,x)) for observation times/locations and a Gaussian observation distribution for values. A transformer encoder with a learnable aggregation token amortizes the variational posterior over the latent initial state, and the full model is trained by maximizing an ELBO comprising observation log-likelihood, STPP log-likelihood, and a KL term, with Monte Carlo integration for expectations and the intensity integral. A key efficiency contribution is replacing the dense observation-time grid with a sparse auxiliary grid (n ≪ N) for ODE solving, then interpolating latent states to the full grid, which yields up to 9× faster simulation and 4× faster training without sacrificing accuracy.

## Results

On the four spatiotemporal datasets, the proposed method achieves the best or near-best performance on both observation MAE and point-process log-likelihood. On synthetic data, it outperforms the strongest dynamic baseline CNN-ODE by 43% on Burgers' (MAE 0.043 vs 0.076) and 63% on Shallow Water (MAE 0.012 vs 0.032), and beats the strongest point-process baseline AutoSTPP on log-likelihood for Burgers' (3.77 vs N/A), Shallow Water (3.62 vs 3.37), and Navier-Stokes (5.96 vs 5.91). On the real-world Scalar Flow dataset, performance is competitive: MAE 0.092 (vs CNN-ODE 0.091) and log-likelihood 8.41 (vs AutoSTPP 8.49). The method also achieves up to 4× faster training than the sequential ODE evaluation approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among dynamic spatiotemporal models, CNN-ODE is the strongest baseline but is outperformed by the proposed method on all three synthetic datasets (e.g., MAE 0.043 vs 0.076 on Burgers', 0.012 vs 0.032 on Shallow Water); on Scalar Flow the two are essentially tied (0.092 vs 0.091). FEN and NSPDE perform poorly, often failing to beat the trivial median predictor, with FEN unable to run on Burgers' at all. Among point-process baselines, AutoSTPP is the strongest competitor, achieving log-likelihood 5.91 on Navier-Stokes and 8.49 on Scalar Flow, but the proposed method still edges it out on synthetic data (5.96 on Navier-Stokes, 3.62 vs 3.37 on Shallow Water) while being marginally behind on Scalar Flow (8.41 vs 8.49). DSTPP and NSTPP are substantially weaker, frequently underperforming even the constant-intensity baseline. Overall, the proposed method is the only one that jointly models both observation values and observation times/locations, and it achieves the best combined performance across all four datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies four previously separate research threads—neural ODEs for latent dynamics, neural point processes for observation timing/location, implicit neural representations for continuous field decoding, and amortized variational inference with a transformer encoder—into a single generative model for spatiotemporal systems observed under sparse, random sampling. Prior spatiotemporal dynamical models (FEN, NSPDE, PDE-Net) assume dense observations on fixed grids, while neural spatiotemporal point process methods (NSTPP, AutoSTPP, DSTPP) model only the observation process without capturing underlying PDE dynamics. The paper's key departure is treating the observation process (times and locations) as a non-homogeneous Poisson process whose intensity is a function of the latent system state, thereby coupling the two modeling tasks in a joint likelihood. A practical contribution is the sparse-grid interpolation technique that avoids forcing ODE solvers onto extremely dense time grids, yielding up to 4× faster training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a genuine practical gap: real sensor networks (crowdsourced earthquake detection, ocean buoys, vehicle-mounted pollution sensors) produce sparse, asynchronously-timed observations that existing methods cannot handle without heavy preprocessing like time binning. The unified model's ability to predict both system state and where/when the next observation will occur makes it directly applicable to adaptive sensing and monitoring. Key limitations include the Poisson process assumption (no simultaneous events, no observation-dynamics feedback), the need for sufficiently dense sensor placement in high-intensity regions, and the fact that the Scalar Flow real-world dataset uses a non-Poisson measurement process, suggesting the generative assumption may not always hold. Open questions include whether richer point process models (Hawkes, Cox) or interaction terms between observations and dynamics would improve performance in settings where sensing affects the system.
