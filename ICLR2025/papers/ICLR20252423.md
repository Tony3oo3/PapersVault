## Problem

Existing speech separation and enhancement datasets are either static-source synthetic data (LibriMix, WHAMR!, DNS Challenge) with limited acoustic realism, or small-scale real-world recordings (RealMAN) that lack scalability and flexibility. RIR simulation methods like the image source method struggle with occlusion, non-cuboid geometry, and diverse material properties, while moving sound source scenarios—critical for applications like robotic navigation—remain severely underrepresented in the literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252423/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SonicSim ingests 90 Matterport3D 3D scenes (with semantic material annotations), 360 hours of LibriSpeech audio, FSD50K environmental noise, and FMA musical noise (vocals removed via BSRNN), all at 16 kHz. It produces 60-second mixed audio clips with physically simulated reverberation, where speech is rendered as a moving source (3–5 segments per speaker, -17 LUFS) and noise as static sources (environmental at -21 LUFS, musical at -24 LUFS), alongside RIRs, source/microphone position maps, and clean reference audio. The dataset supports both two-speaker separation and single-speaker-plus-noise enhancement configurations, with 57,596 trajectories across 1,001 speakers (~960 hours total).

## Method

SonicSim is built on Habitat-sim's bidirectional path tracing audio renderer, which simulates sound reflections, diffraction, and occlusion across arbitrary 3D geometries with material-specific absorption, scattering, and transmission coefficients mapped from semantic scene labels. For moving sources, RIRs are computed at discrete positions along a navigable trajectory, and adjacent convolution results are blended using a distance-weighted interpolation α(t) = dist(r_{j+1}, r_t) / dist(r_j, r_{j+1}) to produce smooth spatial transitions. The toolkit supports multi-level customization—scene geometry and materials, microphone array topology (mono, binaural, ambisonics, custom linear/circular arrays), and source/microphone trajectories—enabling controlled ablation of environmental factors. For benchmarking, models are trained with a dynamic augmentation strategy (random 2-of-3 speaker selection, random 3s extraction from 60s clips) and evaluated on both the synthetic SonicSet test set and real-world recordings (RealSEP, RealMAN, HumanSEP, HumanENH) to quantify the synthetic-to-real acoustic gap.

## Results

On the RealMAN real-world speech enhancement benchmark, models trained on SonicSet achieve substantially lower CER than those trained on VoiceBank-DEMAND or DNS Challenge: Inter-SubNet reaches 18.73% CER (vs. 47.73% and 22.96%), and FullSubNet reaches 19.15% (vs. 49.23% and 32.39%). On the SonicSet speech separation test set, TF-GridNet achieves the best SI-SNR of 15.38 dB (environmental noise) and 14.37 dB (musical noise), with WER of 12.04% and 14.43% respectively. On the RealSEP real-world separation dataset, SonicSet-trained models consistently outperform LRS2-2Mix- and Libri2Mix-trained counterparts across SI-SNR, SDR, and WER metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For speech separation on SonicSet, TF-GridNet (SI-SNR 15.38/14.37 dB, WER 12.04/14.43%) outperforms all other baselines, including Mossformer2 (14.84/11.12 dB) and Mossformer (14.72/11.80 dB), with a clear gap over RNN-based models like DPRNN (4.87/4.37 dB) and BSRNN (9.10/6.96 dB). CNN-based models (SuDoRM-RF, A-FRCNN, TDANet) show more balanced performance across environmental and musical noise than RNN-based counterparts. For speech enhancement on the RealMAN real-world test, SonicSet-trained models dominate: Inter-SubNet achieves 18.73% CER vs. 47.73% (VoiceBank-DEMAND) and 22.96% (DNS Challenge), a 29-point improvement over VoiceBank-DEMAND. On the RealSEP real-world separation dataset, SonicSet-trained models consistently beat LRS2-2Mix- and Libri2Mix-trained models across SI-SNR, SDR, and WER, confirming that the physics-based simulation better captures real acoustic conditions. Among enhancement models on SonicSet itself, Inter-SubNet leads (SI-SNR 10.34/12.07 dB, WER 18.83/20.07%), while sub-band methods (FullSubNet, Fast-FullSubNet, FullSubNet+) generally outperform full-band and Taylor-series approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SonicSim builds directly on the Habitat-sim embodied AI platform and its Soundspaces 2.0 audio renderer, repurposing a physics-based 3D simulation engine originally designed for visual navigation into a speech data generation toolkit. It departs from the dominant RIR simulation paradigm (image source method, ray tracing, finite element analysis) used in datasets like WHAMR! and DNS Challenge by leveraging bidirectional path tracing in realistic scanned 3D scenes, thereby handling occlusion, complex geometry, and material-dependent acoustics that cuboid-room approximations cannot capture. Conceptually, it extends the static-source speech separation/enhancement task (LibriMix, LRS2-2Mix) to dynamic moving-source scenarios, filling a gap the authors identify as critical for applications like robotic navigation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SonicSet (~960 hours, 90 scenes, 1001 speakers) is the first large-scale synthetic dataset supporting moving sound sources with realistic reverberation, noise, and material properties, and the SonicSim toolkit is publicly available for community use. The key practical finding is that models trained on SonicSet generalize better to real-world moving-source recordings (RealMAN, RealSEP) than those trained on comparable-scale synthetic datasets (DNS Challenge, Libri2Mix), suggesting physics-based simulation can partially close the synthetic-to-real acoustic gap. However, the real-world validation relies on small self-recorded datasets (5 hours for RealSEP) and a single external test set (RealMAN), and the authors acknowledge that simulation fidelity is bounded by the quality of input 3D scene geometry. Open questions include whether the approach scales to outdoor or highly dynamic scenes, and whether the gap to real-world performance can be further reduced with more detailed material models.
