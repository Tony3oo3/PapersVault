## Problem

Large language models struggle with formal theorem proving because they cannot effectively leverage the rich interactive feedback (tactic states, verification errors) provided by proof assistants like Lean. Two specific gaps remain: (1) how to use Lean's verification outcomes as a training signal to align the model with formal specifications, and (2) how to address the extreme reward sparsity in proof search, where non-zero reward is only obtained upon full proof completion, making standard tree search degenerate.

## Input / Output

The model takes a Lean 4 theorem statement as input, optionally augmented with an incomplete proof prefix and a comment block containing the current tactic state extracted from the Lean prover (e.g., local hypotheses and pending goals). During tree search, the input is a truncated proof code ending at the last successfully applied tactic, with the intermediate tactic state appended as a 'tactic state' comment. The output is a complete Lean 4 proof that passes formal verification by the Lean 4 prover (with Mathlib4 and Aesop imported), verified within a 300-second time limit. The model operates in two prompting modes: CoT (with natural-language reasoning comments before each tactic) and non-CoT (direct tactic generation).

## Method

The approach combines three components: (1) supervised fine-tuning that trains a 7B model to predict intermediate tactic states as an auxiliary task and generate chain-of-thought reasoning before each tactic, enabling the model to interpret Lean's feedback; (2) online reinforcement learning via GRPO, where Lean verification provides a binary reward (1/0) and the model is optimized on ~4.5k theorems of moderate difficulty to mitigate compounding errors in long proofs; (3) RMaxTS, a novel MCTS variant that introduces a truncate-and-resume mechanism to decompose whole-proof generation into tactic-level tree nodes, assigns intrinsic rewards (RMax) for discovering novel tactic states to combat reward sparsity, and uses Discounted UCB (DUCB, γ=0.99) to handle the non-stationary nature of intrinsic rewards as exploration progresses. A virtual-node technique allows the tree policy to expand non-leaf nodes, and the expansion step performs full whole-proof generation from the selected node rather than single-tactic prediction, bridging the two dominant proof-generation paradigms.

## Results

DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL+RMaxTS achieves a new state-of-the-art pass rate of 63.5% on miniF2F-test (32×6400 samples, CoT+non-CoT mixture) and 25.3% on ProofNet-test (4×6400 samples). In single-pass whole-proof generation, DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL reaches 60.2% on miniF2F-test and 23.7% on ProofNet-test, outperforming all prior methods. The RL stage alone contributes a 3.7% improvement over the SFT model on miniF2F-test, and the RMaxTS tree search adds a further 2.5% on top of RL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On miniF2F-test, DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL+RMaxTS (63.5%) outperforms the strongest prior tree-search method, InternLM2-StepProver (48.8%), by 14.7 points, and the prior whole-proof method DeepSeek-Prover-V1 (50.0%) by 13.5 points. On ProofNet-test, the same configuration achieves 25.3%, surpassing InternLM2-StepProver (18.1%) by 7.2 points and ReProver (13.8%) by 11.5 points. The SFT-only variant with RMaxTS (60.2%) already exceeds all prior baselines, confirming that the supervised training with tactic-state augmentation is a major contributor. The RL stage provides an additional 3.3-point gain over SFT+RMaxTS at the 32×6400 budget, and the CoT/non-CoT mixture strategy adds a further 0.8 points. Notably, at matched sample budgets (e.g., 1×3200), the RL+RMaxTS configuration (55.0%) outperforms the SFT+RMaxTS configuration (53.5%), demonstrating that the improvements from RL and tree search are orthogonal and complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DeepSeek-Prover V1 by unifying two previously separate paradigms in LLM-based theorem proving: whole-proof generation (treating proof construction as code completion) and proof-step generation (iterative tactic-level search). The key conceptual bridge is the truncate-and-resume mechanism, which decomposes a single whole-proof generation pass into a sequence of tactic-level tree nodes, enabling MCTS over the tactic state space. For exploration, the paper adapts RMax (Brafman & Tennenholtz, 2002) to tree search with a novel discounted UCB (DUCB) to handle the non-stationary intrinsic reward signal, and for training it applies GRPO with Lean's binary verification as the reward, following the RL-from-verification paradigm established by prior work on Lean and other proof assistants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, end-to-end pipeline (SFT with tactic-state auxiliary tasks → GRPO from Lean verification → RMaxTS inference) that achieves new SOTA on both miniF2F (63.5%) and ProofNet (25.3%), demonstrating that a 7B model with careful training and search can substantially outperform larger or more specialized baselines. The ablation showing that UCT without intrinsic rewards degenerates to non-search performance highlights that exploration strategy is the critical bottleneck in proof search, not model capacity. Open questions include whether a learned critic for partial proofs could enable temporal credit assignment (the authors identify this as the key next step), whether the approach transfers to other proof assistants (Isabelle, Coq), and whether the expert-iteration loop can be made more sample-efficient for harder theorem collections.
