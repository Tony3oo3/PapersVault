## Problem

Task arithmetic enables model editing via simple weight operations, but suffers from poor reproducibility and expensive coefficient tuning due to interference between task vectors. While prior work showed that NTK linearization reduces weight disentanglement error, the underlying mechanism—why and how linearization suppresses interference—remained theoretically unexplained, leaving no principled way to further reduce interference or eliminate the need for coefficient search.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained weights θ₀, fine-tuned weights θ*, and unlabeled input data from other tasks (no labels required for the regularization term). It produces edited model weights via task vector addition or subtraction, yielding either a multi-task model or a model with a specific capability suppressed. A key practical detail is that the regularization only needs the input distribution of other tasks, not their labels, making it applicable in settings analogous to unsupervised domain adaptation where only source-domain data is accessible.

## Method

The paper identifies the τ-Jacobian product (τJp)—the mean squared norm of the product between a task vector and the pre-trained model's Jacobian evaluated on another task's inputs—as the causal driver of weight disentanglement in the NTK regime, proving that τJp = 0 is necessary and sufficient for zero interference. Building on this, it introduces a regularized fine-tuning loss that adds an L2 penalty on (θ − θ₀)ᵀ∇_θ f_lin(x_t, θ₀) for each other task t, steering the task vector into a subspace orthogonal to other tasks' Jacobians. An efficient cyclical variant computes the penalty for only one other task per iteration (rotating through T_orth), reducing per-iteration cost to near that of standard linearized fine-tuning while retaining most of the interference reduction. The approach is shown to work in incremental settings (regularizing only against previously learned tasks) and can be applied as a short fine-tuning continuation on existing publicly available task vectors to further reduce τJp without retraining from scratch.

## Results

On the 8-task vision task addition benchmark, the proposed τJp regularization achieves 97.2% normalized accuracy with fixed coefficients (α=1.0) on ViT-B-32, 98.4% on ViT-B-16, and 99.0% on ViT-L-14, outperforming all baselines even without coefficient tuning. With grid-searched coefficients, absolute accuracy reaches 84.5% (ViT-B-32), 87.6% (ViT-B-16), and 90.8% (ViT-L-14). For task negation, the method reduces target-task accuracy to 6.7% (ViT-B-32) while preserving 60.8% control-task accuracy, surpassing Linear FT (11.8%/60.6%) and Ties-Merging (21.8%/61.7%). On GLUE task addition with T5-small, the method achieves 94.0% average normalized accuracy, compared to 84.1% for Linear FT and 80.6% for Ties-Merging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed τJp regularization consistently outperforms all five baselines across vision and NLP tasks. Against Non-lin. FT (Ilharco et al., 2023), the improvement is dramatic: with fixed coefficients, normalized accuracy jumps from 20.5% to 97.2% on ViT-B-32, and even with grid search, the gap remains 19.2 points (97.6% vs. 78.0%). Against Linear FT (Ortiz-Jimenez et al., 2023), the method gains 12.2 points in normalized accuracy with fixed coefficients (97.2% vs. 61.7%) and 12.6 points with grid search (97.6% vs. 85.0%) on ViT-B-32. Against Ties-Merging (Yadav et al., 2023), the advantage is 12.4 points with fixed coefficients (97.2% vs. 84.8%) and 12.8 points with grid search on ViT-B-32; notably, Ties-Merging shows no benefit from coefficient tuning (identical results at 1.0 and grid). Against AdaMerging (Yang et al., 2024), the method leads by 8.7 points with fixed coefficients (97.2% vs. 88.5%) and 1.2 points with grid search (97.6% vs. 96.4%) on ViT-B-32, with the gap narrowing on larger models. Against MTL (Caruana, 1997), the method achieves 97.6% normalized accuracy (ViT-B-32) while requiring only input data from other tasks (no labels), whereas MTL requires full labeled data for all tasks and offers no post-hoc flexibility for task addition/negation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task arithmetic framework (Ilharco et al., 2023) and the linearized fine-tuning approach of Ortiz-Jimenez et al. (2023), which showed that operating in the NTK regime reduces weight disentanglement error. The key departure is identifying *why* linearization helps: the paper proves that the causal mechanism is the τ-Jacobian product (τJp), the product of a task vector with the pre-trained model's Jacobian on other tasks, and that linearization alone is insufficient without also driving τJp toward zero. This positions the work as a theoretical completion of the Ortiz-Jimenez et al. analysis, complementing interference-reduction strategies like Ties-Merging (masking) and AdaMerging (adaptive scaling) with a principled orthogonality-based regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the τJp regularization eliminates the need for expensive coefficient grid-search in task addition (α=1.0 becomes near-optimal) and substantially improves task negation while preserving control-task performance. However, the method is constrained to the NTK regime, which doubles or triples forward-pass cost, and all experiments use relatively small models (ViT-B-32, T5-small, GPT-2 small). A critical open question is whether the approach transfers to large language models using approximate linearization (e.g., LoRA-based tangent-space methods), which the authors explicitly leave as future work. The finding that cosine similarity of task vectors is a poor proxy for τJp also challenges a common heuristic in the model-merging literature.
