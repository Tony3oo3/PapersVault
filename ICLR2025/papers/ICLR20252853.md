## Problem

Multimodal document understanding models are predominantly trained on closed-source, restrictively-licensed datasets, with 80% of 133 surveyed datasets lacking permissive licenses or clear licensing information. Existing open datasets like DocStruct4M and Cambrian-7M focus on basic OCR and simple QA, failing to address the complexity of processing intricate visual documents or generating long-structured outputs (HTML, LaTeX, JSON) that are essential for real-world applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252853/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes RGB document images (website screenshots, table images, chart images, flowchart images, GUI screenshots) paired with text instructions as input, and produces task-specific outputs ranging from OCR text and bounding boxes to long-format structured code (HTML, LaTeX, SVG, JSON, GraphViz) and natural-language summaries or answers. BigDocs-Bench tasks specifically emphasize generating lengthy, valid code outputs from images, with text token counts averaging up to 32,700±53,105 for Screenshot2HTML, while models are constrained to a maximum context length of 8192 tokens during training.

## Method

BigDocs-7.5M is curated from 16 permissively-licensed academic datasets plus newly crawled data (CDIP-1M, ArxivOCR, ArxivTableCap), unified into 7.5M image-text pairs across four task families (OCR, structured parsing, captioning, QA) with a novel unified metadata framework tracking per-sample licensing, provenance, and transformations. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: continual pretraining (CPT) on BigDocs-7.5M to align the vision encoder and connector with the LLM, followed by downstream finetuning (FT) on BigDocs-Bench (329k samples across 10 structured-output tasks) to teach instruction-following and code generation. The BigDocs Toolkit provides modular tools for data preprocessing, NSFW/PII filtering, CLIP-based contamination assessment, and standardized formatting. Experiments use FSDP on 8×8 H100 GPUs with batch size 256 and learning rate 2e-5 (AdamW), evaluating four base models (DocOwl1.5-8B, Qwen2-VL-2B, Phi-3.5-Vision-4B, LLaVA-NeXT-7B) against DocStruct4M-trained counterparts and closed-source models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Gemini-Pro-1.5).

## Results

On the proposed BigDocs-Bench (10 novel structured-output tasks), the best BigDocs model (Phi3.5-Vision-4B + BigDocs) achieves an average score of 56.34, outperforming GPT-4o (36.84) by ~19.5 points and Qwen2-VL-72B (31.36) by ~25 points. Gains are most pronounced on flowchart generation (Image2Flow JSON: 71.86 vs. GPT-4o's 27.28) and GUI reasoning (GUI2UserIntent: 86.91 vs. 17.57). On general document benchmarks (Table 2), BigDocs-trained models match or exceed DocStruct4M-trained counterparts, with Phi3.5-Vision-4B + BigDocs reaching 60.80 average score. Human evaluation confirms a 63% win rate over GPT-4o on Table2LaTeX and 88% over Phi3.5 Instruct.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four BigDocs-trained models (46.92–56.34 avg) substantially outperform every closed-source baseline, with GPT-4o (36.84) being the strongest closed model. The gap is largest on structured code generation: Image2Flow-JSON scores 48.54–71.86 for BigDocs models versus 27.28 for GPT-4o and 0.23–0.65 for open models (which required 1-shot prompting). On GUI reasoning tasks (GUI2UserIntent, GUI2Summary, GUI-VQA), BigDocs models achieve 71–89 ROUGE-L F1 versus 10–34 for open baselines and 13–37 for closed models. Among open models, Qwen2-VL-72B (31.36) and Llama-3.2-90B (25.66) are the strongest but still trail the smallest BigDocs model (Qwen2-VL-2B + BigDocs, 46.92) by 15+ points. On Screenshot2HTML and Image2SVG, the gap narrows (e.g., GPT-4o scores 10.33 DOM/Dim vs. 12.05 for Phi3.5+BigDocs; Gemini-Pro-1.5 leads on Image2SVG at 15.21 DINO vs. 60.63 for LLaVA-NeXT+BigDocs), suggesting these tasks have received more prior attention. On general document benchmarks (Table 2), BigDocs training yields higher average scores than DocStruct4M training across all four base models, with the exception of Qwen2-VL-2B where DocStruct4M slightly edges out on DocVQA (59.53 vs. 57.23) but BigDocs wins on KLC (68.60 vs. 54.44) and WTQ (35.67 vs. 34.89).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of open-source multimodal datasets and document understanding, departing from prior efforts like DocStruct4M and Cambrian-7M that either lack permissive licenses or focus on basic OCR/QA tasks. Rather than proposing a new model architecture, it repurposes existing VLMs (DocOwl1.5, Qwen2-VL, Phi-3.5-Vision, LLaVA-NeXT) and contributes a large-scale, license-permissive training corpus (BigDocs-7.5M) assembled from 16 curated sources plus newly crawled data, alongside a benchmark suite (BigDocs-Bench) that extends existing tasks—such as FlowchartQA and WebSight—toward longer, more structured code outputs (HTML, LaTeX, SVG, JSON, GraphViz). The curation methodology draws on responsible-data principles from StarCoder, The Stack, and FineWeb, but applies them specifically to visually-rich document data with a unified metadata framework for traceability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BigDocs-7.5M addresses a critical gap: 80% of the 133 document-related datasets surveyed have non-permissive or unclear licenses, blocking open-source and commercial use. The practical impact is significant—models trained on BigDocs outperform GPT-4o by up to 25.8% on structured-output tasks and are preferred by human evaluators 63% of the time—suggesting that careful, permissively-licensed curation can rival closed-source training data for document tasks. Key limitations include the 8192-token context window (restricting very long HTML/SVG generation), suboptimal performance on some benchmarks (e.g., MMMU for LLaVA), and uncertainty about base-model pretraining data. Open questions include whether the data mixture can be further refined, whether longer contexts unlock better code generation, and whether the contamination analysis methodology generalizes to other multimodal settings.
