## Problem

Existing canonical-representation avatar methods either model deformations globally over the entire coordinate space (twisting local structures like arm and face contours) or use part-wise encodings without canonical rendering (introducing black artifacts under novel poses). No prior method simultaneously enforces locality-sensitive non-rigid deformation and canonical-space rendering, leaving a gap between generalization to unseen poses and faithful reproduction of fine-grained, pose-dependent details like cloth wrinkles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251096/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video sequence with per-frame SMPL skeleton poses (6D rotation parameters per joint) and camera intrinsics/extrinsics, using SAM for foreground segmentation and off-the-shelf estimators for pose and camera recovery. It outputs SDF values and per-point colors in canonical space for volume rendering, producing novel-view images, novel-pose animations (driven by out-of-distribution poses from AIST++), and explicit 3D geometry via SDF extraction and normal maps. Training uses 24×24 pixel patches sampled from input images rather than random rays, with a delayed optimization strategy that disables non-rigid offsets for the first 5000 iterations.

## Method

The core architecture decomposes per-frame motion into a skeletal rigid component (inverse linear-blend skinning mapping observation-space query points to canonical coordinates) and a locality-sensitive non-rigid offset. A graph neural network with per-node MLP weights processes the skeleton graph to produce part features; query points are transformed into each part's local frame, positionally encoded, concatenated with the corresponding GNN feature, and passed through per-bone fully connected layers. A learnable window function (exponential decay of the L2-norm of scaled local positions, with α=2, β=6) determines each part's contribution, and the weighted sum of per-part features is regressed by an MLP into a 3D offset Δx. The final deformed position (x_c + Δx) is positionally encoded and fed to a second MLP that outputs SDF and color, rendered via VolSDF-style volume rendering. Training combines L1 reconstruction loss, LPIPS perceptual loss, an Eikonal constraint on the SDF, and an L2 regularization on Δx to prevent overfitting of high-frequency patterns.

## Results

On ZJU-MoCap (the primary benchmark), Locality Sensitive Avatar achieves the best average LPIPS of 28.36 (novel view) and 27.84 (novel pose), FID of 22.13 and 35.22, and KID of 8.26 and 7.16, respectively, outperforming the best baselines (GoMAvatar: LPIPS 32.40/32.03, FID 26.90/43.09, KID 12.80/13.81) by over 10% in LPIPS, 20% in FID, and 50% in KID. On ActorsHQ, the method achieves the best LPIPS of 103.78 (novel view) and 100.95 (novel pose) with SSIM of 0.930/0.933. For geometry reconstruction on SynWild, the method achieves the best average Chamfer Distance of 0.485 and Normal Consistency of 0.690.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 9 baselines spanning NeRF-based (HumanNeRF, MonoHuman, NPC, Vid2Avatar, PM-Avatar) and 3DGS-based (3DGS-Avatar, GoMAvatar, GauHuman) methods. Locality Sensitive Avatar consistently outperforms all baselines on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, FID, KID) across ZJU-MoCap, ActorsHQ, MVHumanNet, and in-the-wild YouTube videos, with the largest margins over NPC (which lacks canonical space rendering) and PM-Avatar (which lacks locality-sensitive deformation). Against the strongest baselines GoMAvatar and 3DGS-Avatar, the method wins on LPIPS by ~12% and FID by ~18%, though GoMAvatar retains a slight PSNR edge (30.29 vs. 30.25) due to pixel-level misalignment sensitivity. Against Vid2Avatar (the closest canonical-space competitor), the method achieves substantially better LPIPS (28.36 vs. 35.61) and KID (8.26 vs. 27.65) while matching PSNR. On geometry reconstruction (SynWild), the method achieves the best Chamfer Distance (0.485 vs. Vid2Avatar's 0.499) and Normal Consistency (0.690 vs. 0.687), though the authors note that imperfect pseudo-ground-truth limits the quantitative gap. On MVHumanNet, the method improves LPIPS by over 17.5% relative to the best baseline (47.18 vs. 55.45 for HumanNeRF).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms in neural avatar modeling: (1) canonical-space rendering methods (HumanNeRF, MonoHuman, Vid2Avatar) that learn pose-invariant representations for generalization, and (2) part-based locality encoding methods (DANBO, NPC, PM-Avatar) that use GNNs to capture local body-part relationships for fine detail. The key departure is applying GNN-derived locality features not to modulate frequency encodings in observation space (as in PM-Avatar) but to compute point-dependent non-rigid offsets in canonical space, bridging the gap between holistic rigid deformation (inverse LBS from Vid2Avatar) and adaptive local detail synthesis. The SDF-based volume rendering follows VolSDF, and the window function for part aggregation is inherited from DANBO and PM-Avatar.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that localizing non-rigid deformations in canonical space is sufficient to simultaneously achieve state-of-the-art novel view synthesis, novel pose animation, and 3D shape reconstruction from monocular video — a combination no prior method achieved. This addresses a fundamental tension in the field: observation-space methods generalize poorly to unseen poses, while canonical-space methods lose pose-dependent details like cloth wrinkles. Key limitations include the per-actor NeRF training cost (no real-time rendering), dependence on accurate pose estimation, and the lack of a reliable pseudo-ground-truth for shape evaluation. The authors identify extending the locality-sensitive formulation to 3D Gaussian Splatting as the natural next step for efficiency.
