## Problem

Existing methods for mitigating spurious correlations in deep networks either require substantial unbiased data and spurious feature labels, or incur heavy computational costs from multi-stage retraining of entire networks. The paper asks whether spurious features can be identified and erased using only activation-level signals from a pretrained model, with minimal or no additional unbiased data, while preserving computational efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253427/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained network (ResNet-18/50 or ViT-B) whose penultimate-layer activations serve as the feature space. It takes standard RGB images and produces class labels, but the key intermediate input is the per-channel, per-class activation distribution over a training (and optionally validation) set. The output is a re-weighted final linear layer with spurious-feature weights zeroed out; no new architecture or modified forward pass is introduced.

## Method

EvA is a post-hoc, two-stage pipeline: (1) detection, where each feature channel is scored for spuriousness per class using either Consistency (Wasserstein distance between training and unbiased activation distributions, EvA-C) or Evidence Energy (the linear Taylor term of the network's energy function with respect to each activation, EvA-E), and (2) erasure-and-reweighting, where the top-ε fraction of most spurious channels per class are zeroed in the final linear layer and the remaining weights are re-learned via a constrained optimization on a frozen feature extractor. The key insight is that spurious features, being simpler and more easily learned, produce disproportionately high prediction confidence (low evidence energy) on biased training data, allowing them to be flagged without any unbiased data or spurious-feature labels. This reduces the debiasing cost from ~10^9 FLOPs (full-network retraining) to ~10^3 FLOPs (linear-layer-only updates), achieving state-of-the-art results with 6 orders of magnitude less compute.

## Results

On the BAR dataset, EvA-C achieves 89.43% top-1 accuracy, outperforming DFR (83.23%) by 6.2% and SiFER (72.08%) by 17.4%. On Waterbirds, EvA-C reaches 86.70% worst-group accuracy versus DFR's 83.32% (4.1% gain), while EvA-E achieves 81.31% worst-group accuracy without any extra data, surpassing SiFER (77.22%) by 4.1%. On the biased Waterbirds tuning setting, EvA-E attains 85.8% worst accuracy, beating DivDis (81.0%) by 4.8% and JTT (62.5%) by 23.3%. All EvA variants require only ~10³ FLOPs for debiasing, six orders of magnitude less than the ≥10⁹ FLOPs needed by competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest no-extra-data baseline SiFER, EvA-E wins on every reported metric: BAR top-1 (73.70% vs 72.08%), CelebA unbiased (90.51% vs 89.00%), CelebA conflicting (88.74% vs 88.04%), Waterbirds accuracy (96.95% vs 96.11%), and Waterbirds worst-group accuracy (81.31% vs 77.22%, a 4.1-point gain). EvA-E also dominates the other no-extra-data methods (LfF, LWBC, DebiAN) by 4–13 points on BAR and 1.5–6.3 points on CelebA. In the extra-data setting, EvA-C outperforms DFR on all three datasets, with the largest margin on BAR (89.43% vs 83.23%, +6.2%) and Waterbirds worst-group accuracy (86.70% vs 83.32%, +3.4%). On the biased-tuning Waterbirds setting (Table 4), EvA-E achieves 85.8% worst accuracy versus DivDis's 81.0% (+4.8%) and JTT's 62.5% (+23.3%), demonstrating substantially better robustness when the tuning data itself is biased. All EvA variants require ~10³ FLOPs for the debiasing stage, compared to ≥10⁹ FLOPs for LfF, LWBC, DebiAN, SiFER, JTT, and DivDis, a six-orders-of-magnitude reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvA extends the DFR (Kirichenko et al., 2022) insight that retraining only the last linear layer suffices for spurious-correlation robustness, but adds a channel-level detection-and-erasure step: it first identifies which specific penultimate features are spurious (via Wasserstein-based consistency or a novel evidence-energy score) and zeros their weights before reweighting. It departs from prior debiasing methods (LfF, LWBC, SiFER, JTT, DivDis) that require multi-stage training of full networks or auxiliary classifiers, and from DFR itself, which degrades when the reweighting data is small or biased. The evidence-energy variant (EvA-E) is novel in that it requires no unbiased data or spurious-feature labels at all, using only the biased training set's prediction confidence as a spuriousness proxy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6-orders-of-magnitude compute reduction (≈10 minutes vs. ≈6 days on a single GPU) makes spurious-correlation debiasing practically deployable in real-world pipelines where repeated full-network retraining is infeasible. The ability to operate without unbiased data or spurious-feature annotations (EvA-E) addresses a key practical gap, since such information is rarely available outside controlled benchmarks. Limitations include reliance on the assumption that the frozen feature extractor already encodes both core and spurious signals, restriction to the final linear layer, and sensitivity to the erase-ratio hyperparameter. Open questions include whether the approach extends to intermediate layers, more complex multi-attribute spurious correlations, or can be combined with data-level reweighting methods (AFR, BAM, SELF) for further gains.
