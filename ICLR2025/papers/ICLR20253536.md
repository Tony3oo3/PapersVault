## Problem

Recent work shows that deeper layers in LLMs (e.g., LLaMA, GPT) can be pruned with minimal performance loss, yet the community has largely treated this as a compression opportunity rather than a training failure. The authors identify the root cause as the widespread use of Pre-Layer Normalization, which causes gradient norms to diminish in deeper layers, rendering those parameters underutilized despite the enormous computational cost of training them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253536/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Mix-LN is a drop-in replacement for the normalization layer within standard transformer blocks, so the input/output interface is unchanged: text sequences (max length 256) for LLM pretraining on C4, and image patches for ViT experiments on ImageNet-1K. The only structural change is that the normalization placement (Pre vs. Post) varies by layer index, controlled by a single hyperparameter α that determines the fraction of early layers using Post-LN. No additional parameters, inputs, or output modifications are introduced.

## Method

Mix-LN applies Post-LN to the first ⌊αL⌋ layers and Pre-LN to the remaining ⌈(1−α)L⌉ layers of an L-layer transformer, with α=0.25 found optimal via sweep. The theoretical motivation comes from analyzing the Jacobian of LayerNorm: in Post-LN, the 1/σ scaling factor accumulates multiplicatively across layers (∏ 1/σ^l), causing gradient vanishing in early layers; in Pre-LN, the residual path is decoupled from LN's derivative, preventing vanishing but causing the block derivative to approach identity in deep layers, making them contribute little to learning. By placing Post-LN early (where it preserves gradient magnitude flowing to deeper layers) and Pre-LN late (where it stabilizes training and avoids the accumulated attenuation that causes Post-LN divergence), Mix-LN achieves more uniform gradient norms across all layers. The approach is validated across 71M–7B LLaMA models, showing consistent perplexity gains over Pre-LN, Post-LN, and DeepNorm, as well as superior SFT and RLHF performance, and generalizes to ViT image classification.

## Results

On C4 pre-training, Mix-LN achieves the lowest perplexity across all LLaMA sizes: 33.12 (71M), 26.07 (130M), 21.39 (250M), and 18.18 (1B), outperforming Pre-LN by 1.65, 0.71, 0.53, and 0.47 perplexity respectively. Post-LN and DeepNorm both diverge at 250M and 1B scales (perplexity >1400), confirming severe training instability. In supervised fine-tuning on Commonsense170K, Mix-LN achieves 41.34% average accuracy on LLaMA-250M (vs. 36.93% for Pre-LN) and 44.66% on LLaMA-1B (vs. 43.01% for Pre-LN), with a notable 17.31% gain on BoolQ at 250M. For RLHF on UltraFeedback, Mix-LN reaches a final reward of 1.32 versus 0.75 for Pre-LN on LLaMA-1B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mix-LN consistently outperforms all six baselines. Against Pre-LN (Dai, 2019), the most widely used normalization in LLMs, Mix-LN reduces perplexity by 0.47–1.65 points across model sizes and improves SFT average accuracy by 4.26–1.65 points. Post-LN (Nguyen & Salazar, 2019) and DeepNorm (Wang et al., 2024) both suffer catastrophic training divergence at 250M and 1B scales (perplexity exceeding 1400), making them non-competitive at larger sizes; at 71M and 130M, Mix-LN still leads by 1.65–2.06 perplexity over Post-LN. Against the more recent normalization variants evaluated on LLaMA-250M, Mix-LN (22.33) beats Sandwich-LN (23.26), Group-LN (23.10), and Admin (24.82) by 0.77–2.49 perplexity, confirming that simply repositioning or grouping LN within a single layer is insufficient compared to the depth-wise hybrid strategy. The gains also transfer to downstream tasks: Mix-LN's superior deep-layer quality yields a 0.76-point SFT average improvement at 1B and a 0.57-point RLHF reward gain, demonstrating that the pre-training advantage is not merely a perplexity artifact but reflects genuinely more informative representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the gradient analysis of Xiong et al. (2020), which characterized how Pre-LN and Post-LN affect gradient magnitudes across depth, but extends it to explain *why* deep layers in modern LLMs are empirically ineffective. It departs from the dominant framing in recent work (Gromov et al., 2024; Men et al., 2024; Yin et al., 2023) that treats deep-layer redundancy as a compression opportunity, instead identifying Pre-LN as a training shortfall. The proposed Mix-LN is a simple architectural combination of two well-established normalization placements (Post-LN from Ba, 2016; Pre-LN from Dai, 2019) that had not previously been mixed within a single model, and it is positioned against a range of LN variants including DeepNorm, Sandwich-LN, Group-LN, and Admin.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the Pre-LN → diminished deep-layer gradient hypothesis holds at scale, Mix-LN offers a drop-in replacement that improves parameter utilization without increasing model size, with potential implications for how future LLMs are architected. Key limitations temper the impact: the 7B experiment covers only 13K steps (no final perplexity), the optimal α shifts with scale (25% at ≤1B vs. 6.25% at 7B), and the method has not been validated on 70B+ models or with modern training recipes (e.g., multi-node, longer contexts). Open questions include whether the α sensitivity at scale can be resolved analytically, whether Mix-LN interacts favorably with other stabilization techniques (the paper shows Scaled Embed degrades performance), and whether the SFT/RLHF gains persist at larger model sizes where Pre-LN deep-layer ineffectiveness may be less pronounced.
