## Problem

DPO and its length-bias-mitigating variants (SimPO, SamPO) assign uniform coefficients to all tokens in chosen and rejected sequences, ignoring the temporal dynamics of autoregressive generation. The paper shows via KL-divergence analysis that earlier tokens are disproportionately affected by preference optimization, yet existing methods do not exploit this asymmetry, leading to suboptimal alignment and persistent verbosity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252979/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts paired with chosen (y_w) and rejected (y_l) responses, along with a frozen SFT reference model (Llama3-8B, Gemma2-9B, or Mistral-NeMo-12B instruct checkpoints). In on-policy mode, responses are self-sampled (5 per prompt at temperature 0.8) and scored by ArmoRM-Llama3-8B to form preference pairs. The output is a fine-tuned policy model; a reference-free variant drops the SFT model entirely, using only per-token log-probabilities weighted by the decay schedule.

## Method

D2PO modifies the DPO loss by multiplying each token's log-ratio term β·log(π_θ/π_ref) by an exponential decay factor γ^t, where t is the token position and γ∈(0,1) is a single hyperparameter (best at 0.98). This is derived from a token-level MDP formulation where γ acts as the discount factor in the Bellman equation, and the paper proves via a suboptimality decomposition that an optimal γ*∈(0,1) exists balancing two opposing terms in the upper bound. The decay is applied from the start of the prompt (not the first generated token), and the method extends to a reference-free loss by omitting the π_ref terms under on-policy settings where the reference margin approaches zero. Experiments across three model families show consistent 5.9–8.8 point gains over DPO on AlpacaEval 2 and 3.3–9.7 points on Arena-Hard, with no degradation on reasoning benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, MATH).

## Results

Under on-policy settings, D²PO achieves 57.2% WR / 59.7% LC on AlpacaEval 2 and 66.4% WR on Arena-Hard with Gemma2-9B, outperforming DPO by 10.2 and 9.7 points respectively. Across all three model architectures (Llama3-8B, Gemma2-9B, Mistral-NeMo-12B), D²PO consistently gains 5.9–8.8 points in LC win rate on AlpacaEval 2 and 3.3–9.7 points on Arena-Hard over vanilla DPO. The reference-free variant (D²PO†) reaches 62.4% LC on AlpacaEval 2 and 63.6% on Arena-Hard with Mistral-NeMo-12B, competitive with reference-based models. On general capability benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, MATH), D²PO maintains or improves over SFT baselines, confirming no degradation in reasoning ability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²PO outperforms all six baselines across every model and benchmark in the on-policy setting. Against DPO, the gains are most pronounced on Arena-Hard (e.g., 66.4% vs 56.7% for Gemma2-9B, a 9.7-point margin) and AlpacaEval 2 LC (59.7% vs 53.4%, a 6.3-point margin). Against SimPO, the closest reference-free competitor, D²PO leads by 2.4–7.4 points in LC win rate on AlpacaEval 2 and 0.7–4.2 points on Arena-Hard; the reference-free D²PO† variant also beats SimPO by similar margins. SamPO and IPO show moderate improvements over DPO but fall 5–12 points behind D²PO on Arena-Hard. KTO and ORPO underperform DPO on most metrics, with ORPO showing severe degradation (e.g., 10.6% AE2 WR vs 47.4% for D²PO on Llama3-8B in off-policy). The consistent pattern across Llama3, Gemma2, and Mistral-NeMo confirms the temporal decay mechanism generalizes across architectures and scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO by introducing an exponential temporal decay factor (γ^t) into the per-token reward summation, motivated by the observation that KL divergence between instruct and DPO-tuned models is concentrated at earlier positions. It departs from SimPO and SamPO, which address DPO's length bias through uniform length normalization or truncation, by instead modulating each token's contribution based on its position. The approach draws a parallel to Yang et al. (2024), who applied temporal decay to diffusion denoising steps in text-to-image generation, and provides a token-level MDP theoretical analysis with a suboptimality upper bound that characterizes the trade-off in choosing γ. The method also extends naturally to a reference-free variant by dropping the KL margin term under on-policy settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simplicity of the modification (multiplying each token's log-ratio by γ^t) makes D2PO easy to adopt, and the consistent 5–9 point gains across three model families (Llama3-8B, Gemma2-9B, Mistral-NeMo-12B) on both off-policy and on-policy data lend credibility. The reference-free variant matching reference-based performance is practically significant for memory-constrained deployments. Open questions include whether the empirically optimal γ ≈ 0.98 generalizes to longer sequences or larger models, whether the decay should be applied from the prompt or the first generated token (the paper shows prompt-based is better but the theoretical justification is incomplete), and whether the mechanism transfers to non-autoregressive or multimodal generation settings.
