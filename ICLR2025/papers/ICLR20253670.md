## Problem

Imitation learning-based manipulation policies achieve precision on seen tasks but overfit and fail on novel ones, while foundation-model-based approaches generalize linguistically but lack the fine-grained 3D spatial understanding needed for accurate execution. The paper asks whether the precision of learned policies and the generalization of foundation models can be unified through a shared intermediate representation that grounds language into 3D space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253670/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a front-view 256×256 RGB-D image, a natural language instruction, and proprioceptive history (short action sequence), and outputs a keypose (3D position, 6D rotation, binary gripper state) that is interpolated into a trajectory via RRT*-Connect. The critical intermediate artifact is the GravMap: a pair of voxel maps (spatial cost and gripper openness) downsampled by farthest-point sampling into 1024 points and encoded by a lightweight DP3 MLP into a token vector. During training, sub-goals are extracted from expert demonstrations; during inference, they are produced by a GPT-4o + Semantic-SAM pipeline, so the same GravMap generator serves both phases with different upstream sources.

## Method

GravMAD introduces Sub-goal Keypose Discovery, a data-distillation heuristic that segments demonstrations into sub-task stages using gripper-state changes and touch-force thresholds, yielding per-stage sub-goal positions and gripper states. These sub-goals are converted into GravMaps (a spatial cost map with lower values near the target plus a gripper openness map) that provide region-level 3D guidance rather than a single fixed point. The action diffusion architecture extends 3D Diffuser Actor by injecting GravMap tokens via cross-attention into the position, openness, and two auxiliary prediction heads (which directly regress the sub-goal position and gripper state), while the rotation head deliberately omits GravMap conditioning to avoid distributional mismatch. Training combines L1 noise-prediction losses, BCE gripper loss, auxiliary sub-goal regression losses, and a contrastive loss over GravMap point-cloud features to encourage discriminative spatial representations. At inference, a three-stage foundation-model pipeline (Semantic-SAM-based Detector → GPT-4o Planner → GPT-4o Composer) replaces the demonstration-based sub-goal extraction, generating the same GravMap format from raw visual observations and language instructions.

## Results

On 8 novel RLBench tasks (generalization test), GravMAD (VLM) achieves 62.92% average success rate with rank 1.0, outperforming the best baseline (Voxposer at 34.29%) by 28.63%. On 12 base tasks (seen during training), GravMAD (Manual) reaches 69.17% average success rate, a 13.36% improvement over the best baseline (3D Diffuser Actor at 55.81%). In the VLM setting on base tasks, GravMAD still achieves 56.72%, marginally exceeding 3D Diffuser Actor by 0.91%. Real-world validation on 10 robotic tasks further confirms generalization to unseen tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GravMAD is compared against three baselines: Voxposer (foundation-model-based, using GPT-4 for value map generation and heuristic planning), 3D Diffuser Actor (imitation-learning-based diffusion policy with 3D scene representations), and Act3D (imitation-learning-based 3D feature field transformer). On the 8 novel tasks, GravMAD (VLM) outperforms Voxposer by 28.63%, 3D Diffuser Actor by 33.54%, and Act3D by 45.09% in average success rate. On the 12 base tasks, GravMAD (Manual) beats 3D Diffuser Actor by 13.36%, Act3D by 35.06%, and Voxposer by 54.06%; in the VLM setting, GravMAD still edges out 3D Diffuser Actor by 0.91% while substantially outperforming Act3D (+22.61%) and Voxposer (+41.61%). Voxposer's weakness is its heuristic motion planner failing on fine manipulation tasks (e.g., 0% on Stack Cups Blocks), while 3D Diffuser Actor and Act3D suffer from overfitting to training-specific task configurations, limiting their transfer to novel scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

GravMAD sits at the intersection of two lines of work: diffusion-based imitation learning policies for 3D manipulation (3D Diffuser Actor, Ke et al. 2024) and foundation-model-driven planning (VoxPoser, Huang et al. 2023). It departs from VoxPoser by encoding value maps into sparse point-cloud tokens (via DP3) that condition a learned diffusion policy, rather than using them to drive a heuristic motion planner. It extends 3D Diffuser Actor by introducing GravMap tokens as spatial guidance and auxiliary sub-goal prediction heads, while the sub-goal keypose discovery method builds on the keypose identification heuristic from Q-Attention (James & Davison, 2022). The conceptual novelty is the sub-goal-driven bridge: foundation models identify *what* sub-goals to pursue at inference, while the diffusion policy learns *how* to execute them, with GravMaps providing the shared spatial representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized tension in robot learning: imitation policies overfit to training tasks while foundation-model planners lack 3D precision. GravMAD's sub-goal decomposition offers a principled way to combine both, and the 28.63% gap on novel RLBench tasks is meaningful. However, the practical impact is tempered by the VLM setting's much smaller gains (+0.91% on base tasks vs. +13.36% with manual positions), the acknowledged sensitivity to prompt engineering and camera perspective, and the inability to handle tool-use tasks. Open questions include closing the distributional shift between training-time (expert-derived) and inference-time (VLM-derived) sub-goals, extending rotation guidance to foundation models, and scaling to longer-horizon real-world tasks.
