## Problem

Sign language ML research is severely data-limited, and existing large-scale corpora like YouTube-ASL cover only a single language. No open-domain, multilingual sign language parallel corpus existed that could support translation research across the >300 sign languages used worldwide, leaving most Deaf/Hard of Hearing communities without comparable resources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252954/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 512 frames of MediaPipe Holistic landmarks sampled at half frame rate from 34-second random video clips, concatenated with 256 tokens of text (control tokens specifying task/language plus surrounding caption context to mitigate misalignment), and produces 256 tokens of text output. For translation, the output is the target spoken-language sentence with timestamps; for sign language identification (activated with probability 0.1), the model first predicts the source sign language code before proceeding to translate, and the target spoken language is deliberately withheld to avoid trivializing the task.

## Method

The paper's primary contribution is the YouTube-SL-25 corpus (3207 hours, >25 sign languages), constructed by automatically retrieving 81,623 candidate YouTube videos via Knowledge Graph tags, then triaging them over four days by a single non-native signer using channel-level grouping sorted by total duration, with targeted audits of outlier-length videos. For baselines, the authors extend the FLEURS-ASL multitask mixture to a T5-v1.1 Small encoder-decoder that jointly handles translation across multiple source/target language pairs and sign language identification, trained first on caption-level clips then on a mixture of caption-level and random clip-level data. The key empirical finding is that continued pretraining on the full multilingual YouTube-SL-25 dataset (vs. its ASL-only subset or no sign language pretraining) yields consistent BLEURT gains on both higher-resource (ASL) and lower-resource (Swiss German, Swiss French, Swiss Italian) sign languages, demonstrating cross-lingual transfer in sign language understanding.

## Results

On sentence-level translation (BLEURT), full YouTube-SL-25 pretraining achieves 40.1 zero-shot on FLEURS-ASL (vs. 33.7 for ASL-only pretraining) and 37.7 finetuned on WMT23 Swiss German Sign Language, far exceeding the prior top WMT23 score of 23.6 BLEURT. For lower-resource Swiss French and Swiss Italian Sign Language, full pretraining yields 18.8 and 25.2 BLEURT finetuned respectively, versus 11.1 and 20.8 with ASL-only pretraining. On sign language identification (top-1 accuracy), full pretraining reaches 99.6–100.0% finetuned across all four languages, with zero-shot accuracy of 72.0% on Swiss German (vs. 19.6% with no pretraining).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three pretraining conditions—no sign language pretraining, ASL-only (1400 h subset), and full multilingual YouTube-SL-25 (3207 h)—rather than external method baselines. For translation, sign language pretraining consistently outperforms no pretraining: on How2Sign, ASL pretraining lifts finetuned BLEURT from 31.2 to 46.6, and full pretraining reaches 47.9. Multilingual transfer benefits lower-resource languages most: on WMT23 Swiss German, full pretraining achieves 37.7 BLEURT finetuned versus 27.9 with ASL-only (a 9.8-point gain), and on Swiss French it reaches 18.8 vs. 11.1. The finetuned Swiss German result of 37.7 BLEURT (7.5 BLEU) substantially exceeds the top WMT23 SignSuisse shared task score of 23.6 BLEURT (0.3 BLEU). For sign language identification, full pretraining dominates zero-shot (72.0% on Swiss German vs. 19.6% with no pretraining), though finetuning on some benchmarks (e.g., FLEURS-ASL with ASL pretraining) can degrade due to signer overlap and domain shortcuts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the YouTube-ASL corpus (Uthus et al., 2023) from a single sign language to 25+ sign languages by adapting its automatic retrieval pipeline and replacing the months-long per-video expert annotation with a four-day channel-level triage by a single non-native signer. The baseline modeling approach is inherited from FLEURS-ASL (Tanzer, 2024), which itself evolved from YouTube-ASL's baselines, and is extended here to support multiple source/target languages and a sign language identification task within the translation model. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior multilingual sign language work (JWSign, AfriSign) by targeting open-domain content rather than closed domains like Bible translations, and from YouTube-ASL by accepting a lower per-video annotation standard in exchange for massive multilingual coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

YouTube-SL-25 is the largest supervised sign language dataset to date and the first or largest parallel corpus for many of its component languages, providing a critical resource for a field where data scarcity is the primary bottleneck. The demonstration that multilingual transfer benefits both higher- and lower-resource sign languages validates the value of shared multilingual pretraining for sign languages, though the paper acknowledges that the triage-based curation trades off quality and long-tail signer diversity, and that demographic underrepresentation (particularly dark skin tones) remains a concern. Open questions include whether robust automated filtering tools can push dataset size up another order of magnitude, how to handle multi-signer videos, and whether the current data volume is sufficient for broadly usable translation quality.
