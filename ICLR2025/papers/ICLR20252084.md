## Problem

Physics-informed (gray-box) approaches for learning Lagrangian dynamics have so far been limited to low-dimensional systems (2–5 DoF), while high-dimensional systems such as deformable bodies and multi-link robots remain an open problem due to the combinatorial growth in network complexity and data requirements. Existing model-order-reduction methods are either intrusive (requiring known high-dimensional dynamics) or restricted to linearly reducible subspaces, leaving a gap for non-intrusive, structure-preserving reduction of systems with unknown dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional trajectory data — generalized coordinates q ∈ ℝⁿ, velocities q̇, accelerations q̈, and generalized forces τ — from physical systems with n up to 600 DoF (not images of low-dimensional systems). It outputs a reduced-order Lagrangian model consisting of a learned reduced mass-inertia matrix M̌(q̌) ∈ S₊₊ᵈ and reduced potential energy V̌(q̌) on a d-dimensional latent manifold (d ≪ n), from which high-dimensional trajectories are recovered via the learned embedding φ. The reduced model conserves total energy along unforced trajectories, providing physically consistent long-horizon predictions.

## Method

The proposed RO-LNN jointly learns (1) a structure-preserving nonlinear embedding/reduction pair via a constrained autoencoder whose biorthogonal weight pairs (Φₗ, Ψₗ) are optimized directly on the biorthogonal manifold B_{n,d} using Riemannian Adam, and (2) the reduced Lagrangian dynamics via a geometric LNN that parameterizes the mass-inertia matrix on the SPD manifold S₊₊ᵈ using an exponential-map layer followed by optional gyrocalculus-based SPD layers (GyroAI or GyroSpd++). The key architectural insight is that both the AE weights and the SPD-network parameters are treated as elements of their respective Riemannian manifolds, so all optimization steps (gradient, exponential map, parallel transport) respect the intrinsic geometry rather than relying on Euclidean overparametrization or Cholesky decompositions. Training minimizes either a single-step acceleration-matching loss or a multi-step Euler-integration loss that propagates latent dynamics over H timesteps before decoding, with the latter consistently yielding superior long-horizon predictions.

## Results

On the 16-DoF coupled pendulum, the RO-LNN trained with multi-step integration (ODE) achieves a position prediction error of (9.39±3.16)×10⁻³, which is over three orders of magnitude lower than the full-order LNN (1.33×10⁰) and two orders of magnitude lower than L-OpInf (1.31×10¹). The RO-LNN(ODE) also achieves the lowest velocity prediction error at (3.53±2.17)×10⁻¹. On the 192-DoF rope and 600-DoF cloth, the RO-LNN produces accurate long-term predictions (up to 2500 timesteps for the cloth) where full-order LNN and L-OpInf fail to produce stable solutions entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The RO-LNN(ODE) decisively outperforms all four baselines on the 16-DoF coupled pendulum. Against the full-order geometric LNN, the RO-LNN reduces position error from (1.33±0.79)×10⁰ to (9.39±3.16)×10⁻³ — a factor of ~140 improvement — while the full-order LNN's velocity errors are so large (1.97×10²) that stable trajectory integration is impossible. Against L-OpInf (Sharma & Kramer, 2024), the RO-LNN(ODE) achieves a position error roughly 1400× lower (9.39×10⁻³ vs. 1.31×10¹). Against the enhanced L-OpInf with a latent geometric LNN (Sharma et al., 2024), the RO-LNN(ODE) still wins by a factor of ~26 on position error (9.39×10⁻³ vs. 2.45×10⁻¹), demonstrating the advantage of nonlinear structure-preserving embeddings over linear subspaces. On the 192-DoF rope and 600-DoF cloth, both L-OpInf and full-order LNN fail to produce any stable solution, while the RO-LNN accurately predicts dynamics over 2500-step horizons. The joint Riemannian training on the biorthogonal manifold also outperforms the overparametrized AE variant (which produced NaN values in 4 of 6 runs) and the sequentially-trained AE+LNN, confirming that geometry-aware optimization is critical for stable joint learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: (1) Lagrangian neural networks (DeLaN, Lutter et al. 2019; Lutter & Peters 2023) that learn low-dimensional dynamics with physics inductive bias, (2) structure-preserving model order reduction via constrained autoencoders (Otto et al. 2023; Buchfink et al. 2024) that learn nonlinear reduced representations while preserving Lagrangian/Hamiltonian structure, and (3) Riemannian optimization on SPD and biorthogonal manifolds (López et al. 2021; Nguyen et al. 2024). The key departure is making the entire pipeline non-intrusive and jointly optimized: rather than assuming known high-dimensional dynamics (as in intrusive MOR) or restricting to linear projections (as in L-OpInf, Sharma & Kramer 2024), the paper learns both the nonlinear embedding and the reduced Lagrangian parameters simultaneously via Riemannian Adam on a product of Euclidean, SPD, and biorthogonal manifolds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that physically-consistent reduced-order models can be learned end-to-end for systems up to 600 DoF, a regime where full-order LNNs completely fail and linear MOR methods (L-OpInf) produce unstable or inaccurate predictions. The practical finding that Riemannian optimization on the biorthogonal manifold is essential for stable joint training (4/6 overparametrized runs diverged to NaN) provides a concrete recipe for practitioners combining constrained AEs with latent dynamics. Key limitations remain: prediction quality is bounded by AE reconstruction fidelity, latent dimension selection is non-trivial without knowing the FOM's coupling structure, and all experiments are on simulated systems. The authors identify control design for deformable objects and soft robots as the natural next step, suggesting the learned ROMs could serve as real-time surrogates for manipulation planning.
