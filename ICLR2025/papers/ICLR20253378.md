## Problem

Current video question answering models rely on cross-frame reasoning without explicit 3D/4D scene knowledge, making them unable to reason about dynamic physical properties like velocity, acceleration, and collisions in 3D space. Existing synthetic benchmarks either lack diverse physical dynamics, lack natural language integration, or use unrealistic rendering that prevents zero-shot evaluation of large multimodal models. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a more realistic 4D dynamics VideoQA dataset and a model that explicitly reconstructs 4D world states before reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip of 120 frames at 60 fps (2000 ms total) for factual and counterfactual questions, or only the first 30 frames for predictive questions, paired with a natural language question. Factual questions are open-ended (answered with a few words), while predictive and counterfactual questions are binary true/false. Beyond the text answer, the pipeline produces an explicit per-frame 4D scene representation (3D position, pose, velocity, acceleration per object) and, for predictive/counterfactual queries, a physics-engine re-simulation of future or modified world states.

## Method

NS-4DPhysics is a neural-symbolic model with three stages. First, a 3D neural mesh scene parser (3D-NMM) performs per-frame 6D pose estimation via analysis-by-synthesis: it soft-rasterizes learned neural mesh textures into 2D feature space and maximizes a Gaussian rendering likelihood against CNN-extracted image features. Second, a physics prior module converts the discriminative PyBullet engine into a probabilistic correction term (inspired by Bayes-Kalman filtering), adding a Gaussian prior q(R_t, T_t | R̂_{t-1}, T̂_{t-1}) to the rendering likelihood so that pose estimation enforces temporal physical consistency even through collisions. Third, an LSTM sequence-to-sequence question parser converts the text question into an executable program (e.g., query_velocity, compare_velocity, query_accelerating), which is then run over the estimated 4D scene representation; for predictive and counterfactual questions, the physics engine re-simulates from the estimated or modified initial conditions before program execution. Velocity and acceleration are derived from finite differences of consecutive translations with a 5-frame moving-average filter, and collisions are read directly from the physics engine output.

## Results

On DynSuperCLEVR, NS-4DPhysics achieves 82.64% overall accuracy across factual (87.70%), predictive (85.71%), and counterfactual (74.51%) question types, substantially outperforming all baselines. The best prior method, PO3D-VQA (a 3D neural-symbolic model), reaches only 62.93% average, a 19.71-point gap. Among large multimodal models, PLLaVA performs best at 59.24% average, while GPT-4o with chain-of-thought reasoning reaches 56.06%. Ablation shows the physics prior contributes a 6.67-point improvement (82.64% vs. 75.97% without it).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NS-4DPhysics outperforms all nine baselines by a wide margin on every question type. Against the strongest neural-symbolic baseline PO3D-VQA (62.93%), it gains 19.71 points overall, with the largest gap on predictive questions (85.71% vs. 65.33%) and collision sub-tasks (88.46% vs. 51.20%), confirming that moving from 3D to 4D representation with physics priors is critical. Among Video-LLMs, PLLaVA is the strongest at 59.24% average but still trails NS-4DPhysics by 23.4 points; GPT-4o with explicit reasoning (56.06%) and fine-tuned InternVideo (52.62%) perform even worse, indicating that large-scale pretraining does not compensate for the lack of explicit 4D scene structure. The simplest baselines (CNN+LSTM at 48.03%, FiLM at 50.18%) and the 2D neural-symbolic NS-DR (51.44%, factual only) are far behind, underscoring that both 3D/4D scene representation and physical priors are necessary for accurate dynamic reasoning. Notably, even when GPT-4o is given the predicted 4D scene structure as text input (61.39%), it still underperforms the symbolic program executor (82.64%), highlighting the advantage of structured program-based reasoning over free-form LLM reasoning for precise 4D dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D neural mesh model (3D-NMM) line of work from Ma et al. (2022) and Jesslen et al. (2023), which learned generative scene representations for single-frame 6D pose estimation and 3D-aware classification, into the video domain by adding temporal consistency through a physics prior inspired by Bayes-Kalman filtering. For the reasoning component, it follows the neural-symbolic program-execution paradigm established by CLEVRER (Yi et al., 2019) and ComPhy (Chen et al., 2022), but elevates the scene representation from 2D/3D to full 4D dynamics (position, velocity, acceleration, collisions in 3D space). The dataset itself extends SuperCLEVR and CLEVRER by introducing acceleration as a dynamic property, 3D (not 2D-plane) collision events, and significantly more realistic rendering via HDRI environment maps, addressing the domain gap that limited prior synthetic VideoQA benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic benchmark showing that even frontier models like GPT-4o (51–56%) and Video-LLMs (38–59%) fundamentally struggle with 4D dynamic reasoning, while an explicit 4D scene representation with physics prior achieves 82.64%—a gap that highlights a specific and important capability gap in current video understanding. The ablation showing that GPT-4o still underperforms (61.39%) even when given the explicit 4D scene structure as text input suggests that programmatic symbolic reasoning over structured world states remains superior to LLM-based reasoning for physical dynamics. Key limitations include the synthetic setting, restriction to rigid objects with linear velocity/acceleration, and single-dataset evaluation; extending the approach to real-world videos with articulated and non-rigid objects is identified as future work.
