## Problem

Existing causal discovery methods that handle latent variables impose restrictive assumptions (purity, measurement, no-triangle) that are frequently violated in real-world systems. The only prior practical algorithm for the fully general setting with both latent variables and complex relations (Jin et al., 2024) has exponential time complexity, severely limiting its applicability. This paper addresses both gaps by providing the first polynomial-time algorithm for this setting and proving that it raises an explicit error signal when its core assumption is violated, rather than silently returning an incorrect causal graph.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251227/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. observational samples from a linear non-Gaussian acyclic model (LiNGAM) with latent variables, where all exogenous noises are independent and non-Gaussian. It outputs a complete directed acyclic graph explicitly representing both observed and latent variables along with all causal edges, or an error signal if the pure children assumption (each latent variable has at least two pure children and at least three neighbors) is violated. The algorithm requires no prior knowledge of the number of latent variables and operates under bottleneck-faithfulness of the distribution to the graph.

## Method

The algorithm follows a bottom-up-then-top-down pattern with cubic time complexity. Stage 1 sequentially identifies latent variables from leaves to roots by locating identifiable pairs (S1, S2, S3) within an active set via pseudo-residual independence tests, then classifying them to determine pure children and their parents, iteratively updating the active set until no further updates are possible. Stage 2 sequentially infers causal relations from roots to leaves by identifying root variables through pseudo-residual independence conditions, estimating their causal effects via covariance ratios, and removing their influence before proceeding to the next root. The key efficiency gain over prior work comes from this clean separation: rather than alternating between latent identification and relation inference (as in PO-LiNGAM), the two stages are decoupled, reducing the search from power-set traversal to linear traversal. Trustworthiness is established by proving that when the pure children assumption fails, Stage 1 leaves either an unidentified latent variable or a misidentified pure-child set, which necessarily triggers a failure condition in Stage 2's root-identification step, producing an explicit error signal.

## Results

On four synthetic causal graphs (Cases 1–4) with 10k samples, the proposed method achieves 0.0 error in latent variables and 1.00 correct-ordering rate on all cases, with F1-scores of 0.99–1.00. The key efficiency gain is dramatic: running time is 2.3–5.9 s versus 37.6–89.1 s for PO-LiNGAM (the only other method handling all four cases), a 10–20× speedup. GIN and LaHME completely fail on Cases 3 and 4 (0.00 ordering rate), where the purity or measurement assumption is violated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: GIN (Xie et al., 2020), LaHME (Xie et al., 2022), and PO-LiNGAM (Jin et al., 2024). GIN fails on Case 2 (1.0 latent variable error, 0.43 ordering rate) and completely collapses on Cases 3–4 (0.00 ordering rate) because it requires the purity and measurement assumptions. LaHME similarly fails on Cases 3–4 (0.00 ordering rate) and shows 1.0–2.0 latent variable errors on Case 4. PO-LiNGAM is the only baseline that handles all four cases correctly, but it is 10–20× slower (37.6–89.1 s vs. 2.3–5.9 s at 10k samples) because it alternates between inferring causal relations and identifying latent variables in a bottom-up fashion, requiring traversal of power sets. The proposed method matches or slightly exceeds PO-LiNGAM's accuracy (e.g., 1.00 vs. 0.91 ordering rate on Case 3) while being an order of magnitude faster, thanks to its two-stage bottom-up-then-top-down design that avoids combinatorial search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on LiNGAM with latent variables initiated by Adams et al. (2021), who first characterized identifiability for the setting where latent and observed variables interact through complex causal relations (violating purity, measurement, and no-triangle assumptions simultaneously), but whose algorithm required the number of latent variables as prior knowledge and was computationally intractable. Jin et al. (2024) made this setting practically addressable with PO-LiNGAM, but at exponential time complexity. The present work departs from both by introducing a bottom-up-then-top-down strategy that achieves cubic time complexity, and by leveraging the pseudo-residual (from Cai et al., 2019) and a novel quintuple constraint as the core statistical primitives for identifying latent variables and inferring causal relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first polynomial-time algorithm for causal discovery in the fully general setting with latent variables and complex relations, making the problem tractable for larger systems. The trustworthiness guarantee—raising an explicit error signal when the pure children assumption is violated rather than silently returning an incorrect DAG—is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such result in causal discovery with latent variables, addressing a critical reliability concern for downstream decision-making. However, the pure children assumption (each latent variable needs at least two pure children and three neighbors) remains somewhat restrictive, and the method does not extend to non-stationary or cyclic causal structures, leaving important open questions for real-world applications where these conditions may not hold.
