## Problem

Existing data selection methods rely solely on single-modality image features to score sample importance, which leads to inaccurate assessments when noisy or corrupted samples are present and fails to capture the 'group effect'—the interaction between selected samples that significantly influences overall model performance. This paper addresses both limitations by introducing multimodal (image + text) information via CLIP and formulating selection as a multi-objective optimization problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252363/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled image classification dataset (e.g., CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet-1k at 224×224) along with category names formatted as the text prompt 'A photo of [CLS]'. It produces a binary selection vector d ∈ {0,1}^N at a user-specified selection ratio s_r (e.g., 20%–90%), which directly indexes the retained subset. The actual selection ratio is constrained within ±0.05% of the target via a threshold θ = 5×10⁻⁴ on the selection loss.

## Method

The framework comprises three modules built on frozen CLIP encoders. First, dimension-preserving MLP adapters (A_I, A_T) are fine-tuned with InfoNCE loss to bridge domain gaps between CLIP's pretraining data and the target dataset. Second, two scores are computed per sample: the Semantic Alignment Score (SAS), a cosine similarity between adapted image and text embeddings, and the Sample Diversity Score (SDS), the mean L2 distance to k nearest same-class neighbors in adapted image space. Third, a sample-wise continuous parameter d is optimized via SGD with a composite loss L = L_sa + α·L_sd + β·L_s, where L_sa and L_sd encourage high-SAS and high-SDS samples respectively, and L_s enforces the target selection ratio using a straight-through estimator through a sigmoid-binarized indicator. After optimization, d is strictly binarized at 0.5 to yield the final subset, addressing the group effect that purely score-thresholding methods cannot capture.

## Results

On Tiny-ImageNet with VGG-16 at 70% selection ratio, the proposed method achieves 53.40% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (GraNd, 50.79%) by 2.61%. Under 20% label noise on CIFAR-100, it reaches 46.05% accuracy versus 42.29% for the best baseline (Herding), a 3.76% gain. On ImageNet-1k with ViT-Large at 90% selection, it achieves 84.74% top-1 accuracy, exceeding the full-data baseline (84.59%) while saving 10.31% training cost. The method also selects only 0.24% noisy samples under 20% label noise, compared to 5–45% for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and selection ratio tested. On Tiny-ImageNet (VGG-16, 70%), it leads GraNd (50.79%) and MoSo (50.47%) by 2.61% and 2.93% respectively, while Moderate-DS (50.78%) and Herding (48.59%) trail by similar margins. Under 20% label noise on CIFAR-100, the gap widens dramatically: the method achieves 46.05% versus Herding (42.29%), Moderate-DS (40.25%), and Forgetting (36.53%), with CG-Score collapsing to 6.82%. On Tiny-ImageNet with 20% noise, it reaches 26.09% versus MoSo (21.55%) and Glister (21.61%), a 4.4–4.5% advantage. On ImageNet-1k, the selected 90% subset with ViT-Large (84.74%) and Swin-T (78.63%) both exceed their full-data counterparts (84.59%, 78.31%), whereas baselines like Glister and CG-Score are excluded from ImageNet-1k due to prohibitive computational costs. The method also demonstrates superior cross-architecture generalization, beating all baselines on VGG-16, DenseNet-121, and ViT-small, and achieving the best trade-off between selection efficiency (0.40 h on CIFAR-10) and accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the static data selection literature (importance criteria like EL2N/GraNd, distribution-based methods like Herding/Moderate-DS, and optimization-based methods like Glister/CG-Score) by introducing multimodal information via CLIP into the selection pipeline. Prior work relies exclusively on single-modality image features to score samples, which the authors argue leads to ambiguous assessments under noise or corruption. The key conceptual departure is treating the category text as a complementary signal that anchors image features to their semantic meaning, combined with a multi-objective SGD optimization (using the straight-through estimator) to address the group effect rather than relying on combinatorial submodular objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, efficient (O(N) complexity, ~0.4–0.8 h selection cost) data selection method that simultaneously reduces training costs and improves data quality by filtering noisy/corrupted samples, with demonstrated cross-architecture generalization across CNNs and Transformers. A notable practical implication is that selecting 90% of data can yield higher accuracy than training on 100%, effectively turning data selection into a data-cleaning tool. Open questions include whether CLIP's own biases propagate into the selected subsets, how the method behaves under high modality imbalance, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-classification tasks or datasets without clean category text.
