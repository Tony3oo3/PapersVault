## Problem

Existing unified speech-text models are limited to autoregressive architectures, while non-autoregressive (NAR) approaches handle ASR and TTS in separate systems, missing opportunities for shared representations and joint training. Additionally, NAR CTC-based ASR suffers from the conditional independence assumption, and NAR TTS typically depends on external alignment tools, and discrete speech tokens remain underexplored for ASR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251681/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For ASR, 16KHz speech is converted to 50Hz discrete content tokens (1024-cluster K-Means on HuBERT-large features) and the model outputs a byte-level UTF-8 text transcription. For TTS, byte-level text tokens plus a 3–8 second speaker prompt are consumed; the model first predicts discrete content tokens, which are then converted to 12-level RVQ acoustic tokens via a SoundStorm model and decoded to waveform by a DAC codec. Both tasks share the same 50Hz token rate, and text uses a compact byte representation requiring minimal preprocessing.

## Method

T2V2 uses a single 6-layer Conformer backbone with rotary positional embeddings shared across all tasks. ASR is trained with CTC loss on content tokens, while TTS uses a conditional masked language modeling objective where text-speech alignment is obtained via Monotonic Alignment Search applied to intermediate CTC logits, eliminating external aligners. Two auxiliary tasks address key limitations: a CTC error correction head iteratively refines low-confidence tokens by combining masked CTC outputs with speech embeddings (relaxing CTC's independence assumption), and an unconditional speech MLM enables classifier-free guidance at TTS inference. Inference for TTS follows a cosine-scheduled iterative unmasking over 4 steps with CFG (λ=1.0), while ASR inference applies up to 16 iterative correction passes at a 0.7 confidence threshold. All five losses (CTC, TTS, MLM, CTC-correction, length prediction) are combined with equal weights in a single training objective.

## Results

On zero-shot TTS (LibriSpeech test-clean + DAPS speakers), T2V2 achieves the best CER of 0.55, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: WhisperSpeech at 0.66, XTTS at 0.78), while matching the top UTMOS (4.43 vs. HierSpeech++'s 4.46) and SECS (0.94) with only 500 hours of paired data. The text-to-content-token inference time is 0.06 s, roughly 5 orders of magnitude faster than the autoregressive WhisperSpeech (2.84 s). On ASR (LibriSpeech test-clean), T2V2 achieves CER 2.71 / WER 8.27 on the small subset (comparable to Conformer-CTC at 2.69/8.28) and outperforms it on the large subset (CER 1.31 vs. 1.53, WER 4.09 vs. 4.36), though it remains behind the non-discrete Zipformer-Transducer (CER 0.66 / WER 1.99 on large).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the large-scale TTS baselines, T2V2 (trained on only 500 h paired data) achieves the lowest CER (0.55) by a wide margin over WhisperSpeech (0.66), XTTS (0.78), and HierSpeech++ (0.88), while matching HierSpeech++ on UTMOS (4.43 vs. 4.46) and SECS (0.94). Its text-to-content-token latency of 0.06 s is ~47× faster than WhisperSpeech's 2.84 s. Among small-scale baselines, T2V2 dominates YourTTS (CER 0.55 vs. 2.02; UTMOS 4.43 vs. 3.69) and StyleTTS2 (CER 0.55 vs. 1.59; SECS 0.94 vs. 0.91), though StyleTTS2 has a slightly lower end-to-end latency (0.27 s vs. 0.57 s). On ASR, T2V2 is essentially tied with the discrete Conformer-CTC baseline on the small subset (CER 2.71 vs. 2.69) but clearly outperforms it on the large subset (CER 1.31 vs. 1.53, WER 4.09 vs. 4.36). However, both discrete methods remain behind the non-discrete Zipformer-Transducer (CER 0.66 / WER 1.99 on large), confirming that discrete-token ASR still lags continuous-feature models. Subjective evaluation (CMOS/SCMOS) shows T2V2 is statistically indistinguishable from HierSpeech++ and StyleTTS2 on speech quality, while significantly outperforming XTTS and WhisperSpeech on speaker similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

T2V2 unifies two previously separate non-autoregressive pipelines—CTC-based ASR and masked-language-modeling TTS—into a single Conformer+RoPE backbone, departing from prior unified speech-text models (AudioPalm, VIOLE, VoxTLM) that rely on autoregressive encoder-decoder or decoder-only architectures. For alignment, it repurposes Monotonic Alignment Search (Glow-TTS) on intermediate CTC outputs to eliminate external aligners, and adapts classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans) to a discrete-token masked-prediction setting for TTS refinement. The content-token representation draws on HuBERT self-supervised features, extending a line of work that had primarily explored discrete tokens for TTS rather than ASR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-contained alignment via MAS-on-CTC-outputs removes a practical dependency on external tools like MFA, and the shared discrete-token representation opens a path toward modality-agnostic speech-text models that could support additional downstream tasks. However, discrete ASR still lags behind continuous-feature transducer models (2.71 vs. 2.01 CER on LibriSpeech test-clean), and TTS quality is bottlenecked by the separate SoundStorm content-to-acoustic-token stage, leaving open whether a fully end-to-end discrete pipeline can close the gap. Scaling experiments on 50K hours show the approach generalizes, but the field still lacks evidence that unified NAR multitask training can match dedicated single-task systems at scale across diverse languages and domains.
