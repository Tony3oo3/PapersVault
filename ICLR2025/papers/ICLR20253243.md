## Problem

Deep RL agents progressively lose plasticity as neurons become dormant during training, severely limiting their ability to adapt to new experiences in non-stationary environments. Existing mitigations—periodic parameter resets, normalization, weight clipping—either cause performance drops from forgetting, lack generalizability, or do not directly target the underlying dormancy mechanism. The field lacks a unified approach that maintains high plasticity throughout training while preserving stability and learned knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either raw state vectors (MuJoCo continuous control) or RGB image frames (DMC tasks) and outputs continuous actions, operating as a plug-in atop standard actor-critic algorithms (TD3 or DrQ/SAC). The key structural detail is that the network topology is not fixed: it begins at 20–25% of full capacity and grows asynchronously during training, with the first and last layers kept dense for encoding/decoding stability. For image-input tasks, observations pass through a data-augmented representation module before the expanding MLP backbone.

## Method

Neuroplastic Expansion (NE) maintains plasticity by dynamically growing the actor and critic networks from a small initial size to full capacity through three coordinated mechanisms. First, elastic topology generation selects new connections via gradient-magnitude ranking (ArgTopk over |∇L|) rather than random sampling, using Erdős–Rényi initialization and cosine-annealed growth schedules to ensure warm, stable expansion. Second, dormant neuron pruning identifies fully inactive neurons (activation ratio f(θ_i)=0), removes them from the active topology, and returns them to the candidate pool for future re-selection, with a Truncate process capping pruning at ω×|I_grow| per layer to prevent over-pruning. Third, neuron consolidation via Experience Review monitors the slope ε of the activated-neuron-ratio curve; when ε falls below a threshold (indicating diminishing plasticity gains), the agent adaptively samples from the earliest quarter of the replay buffer to reinforce previously learned skills and prevent catastrophic forgetting in later training stages.

## Results

On MuJoCo long-term training (3M→6M steps), NE-TD3 achieves significant and consistent improvements over all baselines across Hopper, Walker2D, HalfCheetah, Ant, and Humanoid, with larger margins in more complex environments (e.g., Humanoid, Ant). NE maintains a higher activated neuron ratio throughout training compared to the most competitive plasticity-preserving baseline (LN), while also achieving superior final performance. On DMC image-input tasks (Reacher Hard, Reacher Easy, Walker Walk, Cartpole Swingup Sparse), NE-DrQ outperforms all baselines in both performance and neuron activation retention. In continual adaptation (4-task cycle × 3 repetitions), NE significantly outperforms vanilla TD3 and matches or exceeds Reset in most tasks, with a notable advantage on the challenging Humanoid task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NE outperforms all five baselines across the majority of evaluated tasks. Against Reset (Nikishin et al., 2022), NE avoids the periodic performance drops caused by parameter reinitialization and achieves higher final returns on MuJoCo tasks; in continual adaptation, NE matches Reset on most tasks but significantly outperforms it on Humanoid. Against ReDo (Sokar et al., 2023) and LN (Lyle et al., 2024), NE achieves both higher performance and a higher activated neuron ratio, indicating better plasticity retention. Against Plasticity Injection (PI, Nikishin et al., 2024), which maintains the highest neuron activation but only performs well on the simpler Hopper environment, NE achieves a superior performance–plasticity trade-off, outperforming PI on Walker2D, HalfCheetah, Ant, and Humanoid. Against vanilla TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018), NE provides substantial gains in all settings, with the largest improvements in complex and continual learning scenarios where standard RL suffers from catastrophic forgetting and primacy bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on TD3's actor-critic architecture and the sparse network training framework of Tan et al. (2022) for gradient-based topology selection, but departs fundamentally from the dominant reset-based paradigm (Nikishin et al., 2022; Sokar et al., 2023) and the one-time expansion approach of Plasticity Injection (Nikishin et al., 2024). Rather than periodically reinitializing layers or performing a single large-scale growth event, NE introduces a continuous, gradient-guided growth-prune-consolidation cycle that draws on biological cortical expansion as its conceptual anchor. The method also repurposes Erdos-Renyi sparse initialization and weight clipping from the sparse-training literature, but applies them in a dynamic topology-evolution context rather than for static sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Plasticity loss is a fundamental bottleneck preventing deep RL agents from adapting in non-stationary and continual learning settings, and NE offers a principled, architecture-level solution that avoids the performance drops and knowledge loss inherent in reset-based methods. The finding that the critic network is the primary locus of plasticity loss (with the actor benefiting less from expansion) provides useful guidance for where to focus future plasticity-preserving interventions. Open questions remain around computational overhead from the growing network, sensitivity to hyperparameters (growth rate, pruning ratio, starting sparsity), and whether the approach generalizes beyond actor-critic backbones to model-based or offline RL settings.
