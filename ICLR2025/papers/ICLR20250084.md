## Problem

LLM-based theorem proving is constrained by scarce formal proof training data and difficulty performing precise symbolic manipulations, while purely symbolic methods (e.g., cylindrical algebraic decomposition) suffer from double-exponential complexity and cannot produce human-readable proofs. The paper asks which aspects of inequality reasoning are best delegated to LLMs versus symbolic tools, and how to integrate them effectively for competition-level problems that neither approach can solve alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250084/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input is a formal inequality problem in Lean 4 consisting of positive real variables, algebraic hypotheses (e.g., a²+b²+c²=1), and a single conclusion inequality involving constants, algebraic operations, and optionally transcendental functions. Output is a complete formal proof in Lean 4 expressed as a sequence of named tactics (scaling lemmas and LLM-generated rewrites) that is both machine-verifiable by the Lean kernel and human-readable as a step-by-step reasoning path. Each proving task is bounded by a 90-minute wall-clock limit, matching standard IMO time constraints.

## Method

LIPS categorizes inequality proof tactics into two types with distinct generation strategies: scaling (applying a finite library of 96 inequality lemmas such as AM-GM, Cauchy-Schwarz, and Muirhead via e-matching, then pruning invalid instantiations by searching for counterexamples with a portfolio of CAD solvers and numerical optimizers) and rewriting (prompting GPT-4o with 16 tailored templates for operations like simplification, rearrangement, and denominator cancellation to implicitly prune the infinite space of equivalent transformations). Goal selection uses a two-stage pipeline: symbolic filtering computes homogeneity and decoupling scores to discard unpromising subgoals, followed by chain-of-thought neural ranking where the LLM compares and orders the top-k remaining candidates. The framework iterates tactic generation, application, and goal selection until the residual goal is trivially provable, requiring no fine-tuning and producing proofs that are simultaneously formally verified and human-interpretable.

## Results

LIPS achieves a 76.3% overall proof success rate across 161 Olympiad-level inequalities, outperforming the best symbolic baseline (CAD at 59.0%) by 17.3 percentage points and the best neural baseline (MCTS at 8.6%) by 67.7 points. On the ChenNEQ dataset (41 problems), LIPS reaches 95.1% success, a 24.4-point margin over CAD (70.7%). On MO-INT (20 competition problems), LIPS proves 80.0% of problems within 90 minutes, compared to 50.0% for AIPS and 60.0% for CAD/MMA. Crucially, LIPS generates human-readable, Lean-verified proofs, whereas CAD and MMA only output verification results without interpretable reasoning paths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIPS decisively outperforms all five baselines across every dataset. Against the strongest symbolic baselines, CAD (59.0%) and MMA (57.1%), LIPS gains 17.3 and 19.2 percentage points respectively on the combined 161-problem set, with the largest margin on ChenNEQ (95.1% vs. 70.7%). Against the best neural baseline AIPS (50.0% on MO-INT), LIPS achieves 80.0%, a 30-point improvement, while also producing formally verified Lean proofs that AIPS cannot. The other neural methods, DSP (0.0%) and MCTS (8.6%), fail almost entirely, with DSP unable to generate correct Lean 4 proofs and MCTS unable to identify correct reasoning paths among numerous subgoals. A key qualitative advantage is that LIPS produces step-by-step, human-readable proofs verified by Lean, whereas CAD and MMA are black-box verifiers that cannot output interpretable reasoning paths.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIPS carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: symbolic inequality provers (CAD, SMT solvers like Z3/CVC5, Mathematica) and neural theorem provers (INT, AIPS, DSP). The closest conceptual predecessor is AlphaGeometry, which also pairs an LLM with a symbolic solver, but LIPS departs in two key ways: it operates in a general-purpose formal language (Lean 4) rather than a domain-specific geometry language, and it makes a principled division of labor—symbolic methods handle the finite tactic space of scaling (lemma instantiation via e-matching), while LLMs handle the infinite tactic space of rewriting (equivalent transformations). Unlike AIPS and INT, LIPS requires no fine-tuning on large-scale formal proof datasets, instead leveraging off-the-shelf LLMs (GPT-4o) with chain-of-thought prompting for both tactic generation and goal ranking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a carefully designed neuro-symbolic decomposition can substantially outperform both pure symbolic (CAD: 59.0%) and pure neural (MCTS: 8.6%) approaches on Olympiad-level inequalities, while producing human-readable, Lean-verified proofs that symbolic solvers cannot. Key limitations include reliance on 96 manually crafted scaling tactics and 16 rewriting operations, restriction to elementary algebraic inequalities with a finite number of variables, and dependence on a proprietary LLM (GPT-4o). Open questions the authors identify include automating tactic discovery to reduce manual effort, extending to more complex inequality structures (concentration inequalities, expectations, infinite variables), and broadening to other mathematical domains. The framework's modular design—where symbolic tools can be swapped or extended independently—positions it as a template for neuro-symbolic integration in other constrained mathematical reasoning tasks.
