## Problem

Offline RL requires an explicit reward function that is often infeasible to design, while preference-based RL learns rewards from human comparisons but typically demands online environment interaction. This paper formalizes and tackles the previously unexplored problem of preference elicitation in a fully offline setting: which trajectory pairs should be queried to minimize the number of human feedback queries needed to recover a near-optimal policy, given only an observational dataset and no environment access?

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline trajectory dataset (no reward labels, no environment access) and iteratively queries a human for binary preference labels over pairs of trajectories (τ₁, τ₂), where trajectories are either sampled from the offline buffer or simulated via rollouts in a learned transition model. It outputs a pessimistic policy optimized over both transition and reward uncertainties, along with the learned reward function and transition model. The preference budget N_p is a user-specified constraint, and the algorithm operates in batches to reduce the number of reward model updates.

## Method

Sim-OPRL bridges offline RL and preference-based RL by using a learned environment model to generate simulated rollouts for preference elicitation, rather than sampling only from the offline dataset. The key mechanism is a dual inductive bias: pessimism with respect to transition uncertainty (to avoid out-of-distribution regions) combined with optimism with respect to reward uncertainty (to target informative preferences about the optimal policy). Concretely, the algorithm constructs a near-optimal policy set under the pessimistic transition model, selects the two policies within this set that maximize preference uncertainty, rolls them out in the learned model to produce trajectory pairs for human feedback, and iteratively refines the reward model. Theoretically, this policy-based sampling eliminates the reward concentrability term C_R present in the OPRL bound, yielding a sample complexity of O(κ²d/ε²) for the reward term that matches active preference learning in known environments. The practical implementation uses neural network ensembles trained on bootstrapped data for uncertainty estimation and a Lagrangian penalty formulation for pessimistic policy optimization.

## Results

The primary metric is sample complexity N_p (number of preference queries needed to reach a suboptimality gap of ε=20 over normalized returns). Sim-OPRL consistently requires the fewest preference queries among all offline methods across all five environments, achieving 4±2 queries on Star MDP (matching the online PbOP baseline), 49±7 on Gridworld, 41±5 on MiniGrid-FourRooms, 50±10 on HalfCheetah-Random, and 225±46 on Sepsis Simulation. On Sepsis Simulation, OPRL Uniform fails entirely to reach the target suboptimality, while Sim-OPRL reduces the query count from 642±72 (OPRL Uncertainty) to 225±46, a ~65% reduction. The online PbOP baseline still outperforms Sim-OPRL on most environments (e.g., 32±4 vs 49±7 on Gridworld), reflecting its advantage of direct environment interaction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sim-OPRL outperforms both OPRL baselines (Uniform and Uncertainty sampling) on every environment tested, with the largest gains on Sepsis Simulation where OPRL Uniform fails entirely and Sim-OPRL reduces queries from 642±72 to 225±46 (~65% fewer). On Star MDP, Sim-OPRL achieves 4±2 queries, matching the online PbOP baseline exactly, while OPRL variants require 30–32 queries. On the remaining environments (Gridworld, MiniGrid-FourRooms, HalfCheetah-Random), Sim-OPRL is the best offline method but still trails PbOP by a factor of roughly 1.5–1.6×, which the authors attribute to PbOP's ability to refine its transition model through direct environment interaction. The results confirm the paper's theoretical prediction that policy-based sampling (reducing the candidate optimal policy set) is more sample-efficient than uncertainty-based sampling (maximizing information gain on the reward function), echoing conclusions from prior online preference elicitation work (Chen et al., 2022; Lindner et al., 2021).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate frameworks: offline RL (which requires explicit reward functions and uses pessimism to handle distribution shift) and preference-based RL (which learns rewards from human comparisons but typically requires online environment interaction). It builds directly on Zhan et al. (2023a) for the pessimistic offline RL framework and concentrability analysis, on Chen et al. (2022) and Saha et al. (2023) for the policy-based preference elicitation strategy (reducing candidate optimal policies rather than maximizing reward information gain), and on Shin et al. (2022) for the offline preference-based RL setting. The key departure is combining pessimism w.r.t. transition uncertainty with optimism w.r.t. reward uncertainty in a fully offline loop, using simulated rollouts from a learned model rather than sampling from the offline buffer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize and solve active preference elicitation in a fully offline RL setting, which is critical for safety-sensitive domains (healthcare, robotics) where environment interaction is infeasible but domain experts can be queried. The theoretical result that Sim-OPRL eliminates the reward concentrability term C_R (present in OPRL's bound) provides a clean explanation for its superior sample efficiency, and the empirical validation across five environments including a sepsis simulation confirms the theory. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional continuous control, how to handle non-stationary expert preferences, and whether alternative expert feedback modalities (e.g., k-wise comparisons, verbal explanations) could further reduce query budgets without environment access.
