## Problem

Neural retrieval systems require embedding entire datastores to build parametric indexes, incurring high GPU costs (30+ hours), large storage (31 GB), and index staleness during co-training with LLMs. This paper addresses the need for retrieval frameworks that support efficient, low-cost, and parameter-agnostic indexing while maintaining competitive effectiveness, particularly for emerging RAG scenarios involving real-time data, local deployment, and joint training pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250620/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and retrieves ranked passages from a datastore, supporting two interchangeable index types: a parametric index of neural embeddings (768-dim sparse vectors) and a non-parametric binary bag-of-tokens index (30k-dim 0/1 vectors from tokenization). The non-parametric index requires only a tokenizer to construct (1 CPU hour, 2 GB storage) versus 30 GPU hours and 31 GB for the parametric index. A late-parametric variant additionally re-ranks top-m retrieved passages on-the-fly, embedding only N_q × m passages rather than the full corpus.

## Method

S1DR builds on the VDR disentangled retriever architecture, replacing the softmax head with elu1p activation, applying max-pooling over token representations, and top-k sparsification to produce parametric token-weight vectors in vocabulary space. The key training innovation is a semi-parametric contrastive loss that jointly aligns parametric representations (learned token weights) with non-parametric binary bag-of-tokens representations, ensuring the learned query embeddings remain compatible with a simple tokenization-based index. This enables three search pipelines: full parametric search over an embedding index, semi-parametric beta search over a BoT index, and late-parametric search that retrieves from the BoT index then re-ranks top-m passages with neural embeddings. For negative mining, S1DR performs in-training retrieval using the fixed non-parametric index, eliminating the need for periodic re-indexing as model parameters update during training.

## Results

On the Wiki21m benchmark, S1DR_full (parametric index) achieves 49.1% top-1 accuracy on NQ, outperforming DPR (46.0%) and VDR (43.8%) by 2.6% and 3.8% respectively. With a non-parametric bag-of-tokens index, S1DR_β reaches 39.8% top-1 on NQ, surpassing BM25 (22.7%) by 10.6% while maintaining comparable indexing cost (0.5h CPU vs. 0.6h CPU). The late parametric variant S1DR_β(m=100) achieves 50.3% top-1 on NQ, exceeding all primary baselines while embedding only 1% of the corpus (364k vs. 21M passages). On BEIR, S1DR_β(m=100) achieves 43.4 average NDCG@10, approaching advanced baselines like E5-base (47.1) and LexMAE (48.7) at a fraction of the indexing cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S1DR_full outperforms DPR by 2.6–3.1% top-1 and VDR by 3.1–3.8% top-1 across NQ/TQA/WQ with a parametric index, confirming that the semi-parametric training objective does not degrade (and slightly improves) embedding-based retrieval. With a non-parametric index, S1DR_β dramatically outperforms VDR_β by 28.5–33.2% top-1 (e.g., 39.8 vs. 12.3 on NQ), showing that the semi-parametric alignment is critical for making the BoT index functional. S1DR_β also beats BM25 by 10.6–12.6% top-1 in-domain (NQ: 39.8 vs. 22.7; TQA: 50.4 vs. 48.2; WQ: 32.1 vs. 19.5), though it falls behind BM25 on most out-of-domain BEIR datasets due to distribution shift sensitivity and the absence of in-training retrieval on MS MARCO. The late parametric S1DR_β(m=100) surpasses BM25+DPR(m=100) by 5.9% top-1 on NQ (50.3 vs. 44.4) and matches or exceeds S1DR_full across all three datasets, while embedding only 1% of the corpus. On BEIR, S1DR_β(m=100) achieves 43.4 average NDCG@10, trailing advanced baselines like E5-base (47.1) and LexMAE (48.7) but exceeding all primary baselines (DPR: 35.8, VDR: 44.5, BM25: 41.1) at a fraction of the indexing cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

S1DR extends the VDR disentangled retrieval architecture (Zhou et al., 2024) by introducing a semi-parametric contrastive loss that aligns learned parametric representations with a non-parametric binary bag-of-tokens (BoT) representation, grounded in the MLM pre-training objective. This departs from prior semi-parametric retrieval work (TILDE, SPARTA) that applied non-parametric tokenization on the query side for fast online processing; S1DR instead applies it on the index side, targeting the more costly and latency-sensitive indexing problem. The in-training retrieval component builds on ANCE and REPLUG but uniquely eliminates the need for periodic index rebuilds by leveraging a fixed non-parametric index, addressing the index staleness problem in co-training retrievers with LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for RAG applications: indexing a 21M-passage Wikipedia corpus drops from 30 GPU hours to 1 CPU hour and 31GB to 2GB storage, enabling online indexing and rapid index iteration for exploratory workloads. The late parametric mechanism (re-rank top-m on-the-fly) achieves near-full-parametric effectiveness while embedding only ~1% of the corpus, making it highly cost-effective for evaluation and deployment scenarios. Key limitations include degraded out-of-domain performance on BEIR (the non-parametric index lacks the distributional robustness of learned embeddings) and the requirement for answer strings to enable in-training negative mining, restricting applicability to benchmarks with such annotations. Open questions include whether the semi-parametric alignment generalizes to larger vocabulary spaces or multilingual settings, and whether the approach integrates cleanly with retrieval-oriented pre-training or knowledge distillation pipelines.
