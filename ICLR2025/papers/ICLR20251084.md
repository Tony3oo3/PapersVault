## Problem

Existing Lagrangian geometry representations (Gaussian point clouds, surface triangles) produce meshes with irregular or degenerated triangles, non-manifoldness, and floating artifacts because their primitives are too fine-grained to enforce structural coherence. Eulerian representations avoid some of these issues but face a fundamental trade-off between computational cost and geometric detail, particularly for thin or slender structures. The paper addresses the gap of a Lagrangian representation that produces high-quality, manifold meshes without the resolution limitations of fixed grids.

## Input / Output

For multi-view reconstruction, the method takes rendered RGBA and depth images of a ground-truth shape as input; for single-view reconstruction, it uses Wonder3D to synthesize six multi-view images from one RGB photo; for text-to-3D, it consumes a text prompt via SDS loss. The output is a union of deformed tetrahedral spheres whose surface triangles form the final mesh, optionally with a 2048×2048 texture map optimized via differentiable rasterization. Initialization uses a 300×300 voxel grid and a linear-programming-based silhouette coverage algorithm to place M≈20 TetSpheres, completing in roughly one minute.

## Method

TetSphere Splatting represents a 3D shape as a collection of tetrahedral spheres (TetSpheres), each a volumetric mesh of N vertices and T tetrahedra initialized as a uniform sphere. Reconstruction is formulated as a deformation optimization: vertex positions are adjusted to minimize a differentiable rendering loss (MSE on alpha/depth, L1 on color, cosine on normals) plus a bi-harmonic energy on the deformation gradient field (computed via a Laplacian matrix over tetrahedron-face connectivity) plus a soft non-inversion penalty (det(F) > 0 per tetrahedron). The bi-harmonic term regularizes relative vertex displacements rather than absolute positions, preserving sharp local detail while preventing irregular triangles and bumpy surfaces. Energy weights are scheduled with a cosine ramp, and the entire pipeline is solved by standard gradient descent with a CUDA-accelerated PyTorch extension. The volumetric tetrahedral structure is the key differentiator: it imposes local connectivity constraints among vertices that individual points or triangles cannot, yielding 100% manifoldness and zero connected-component discrepancy in experiments.

## Results

On multi-view reconstruction (Table 1), TetSphere splatting achieves the best Volume IoU (0.6844 vs. 0.6423 for VolSDF), Area-Length Ratio (0.6602 vs. 0.1193 for DMesh), Manifoldness Rate (100% vs. 81.8% for VolSDF), and Connected Component Discrepancy (0.0 vs. 3.75 for DMesh), while remaining competitive on Chamfer Distance (0.0184 vs. 0.0136 for DMesh). On single-view reconstruction (Table 2), it achieves the best Volume IoU (0.6317 vs. 0.5945 for Open-LRM) and ALR (0.3665 vs. 0.0812 for DreamGaussian) with 100% manifoldness and zero floating artifacts. In the computational cost test (Table 3), TetSphere splatting supports a maximal batch size of 120 and runs at 6.59 iter/s on a 40GB A100, outperforming DreamGaussian (80, 4.43) and all Eulerian baselines (≤48, ≤1.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Eulerian baselines (NIE, FlexiCubes, NeuS, VolSDF), TetSphere splatting dominates on all mesh quality metrics: ALR is 5–30× higher (0.6602 vs. 0.0273–0.0722), MR reaches 100% (vs. 45.5–81.8%), and CC Diff. is 0.0 (vs. 7.0–201.3), while matching or exceeding their Volume IoU. Against the Lagrangian DMesh, TetSphere splatting trades a small Chamfer Distance gap (0.0184 vs. 0.0136) for dramatically better mesh quality: ALR is 5.5× higher (0.6602 vs. 0.1193), MR is 100% vs. 9.09%, and CC Diff. is 0.0 vs. 3.75. On single-view reconstruction, TetSphere splatting outperforms all Eulerian feed-forward methods (Magic123, One-2-3-45, SyncDreamer, Wonder3D, Open-LRM) on Volume IoU (0.6317 vs. ≤0.5945) and ALR (0.3665 vs. ≤0.0574), while matching their 100% manifoldness and zero floating artifacts; it trails SyncDreamer on Chamfer Distance (0.0351 vs. 0.0261). Against the only Lagrangian single-view baseline DreamGaussian, TetSphere splatting wins on Volume IoU (0.6317 vs. 0.3476), ALR (0.3665 vs. 0.0812), and CC Diff. (0.0 vs. 237.4). In the SDS-based computational cost test, TetSphere splatting achieves the highest throughput (6.59 iter/s) and largest batch size (120) among all methods, exceeding DreamGaussian by ~49% in speed and 50% in batch capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of Lagrangian geometry representations and classical geometry processing. While prior Lagrangian methods (Gaussian Splatting, DMesh, 2DGS) use fine-grained primitives—individual points or surface triangles—that lack structural coherence, TetSphere Splatting repurposes FEM concepts (deformation gradients, bi-harmonic energy, local injectivity constraints) to introduce volumetric tetrahedral spheres as a more structured primitive. It departs from the Eulerian tradition (NeRF, DMTet, FlexiCubes) by avoiding fixed grids and iso-surface extraction, and from single-primitive inverse rendering (Nicolet et al., Palfinger) by using multiple independently deformed spheres with stronger volumetric regularization, eliminating the need for intermediate remeshing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D reconstruction: producing meshes that are actually usable for downstream rendering and simulation. The 100% manifoldness rate and zero connected-component discrepancy, combined with competitive reconstruction accuracy, suggest that volumetric constraints within each primitive are a viable path to high-quality meshes without sacrificing geometric fidelity. Key limitations remain: the method produces piecewise overlaid meshes rather than a globally consistent tetrahedral mesh, and the union of multiple TetSpheres offers no strong topological guarantees. Open questions include whether direct volumetric supervision (beyond image rendering) could further improve quality, and whether topology preservation can be enforced across the full collection of spheres.
