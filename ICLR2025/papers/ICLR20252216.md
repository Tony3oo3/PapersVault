## Problem

Existing driving scene generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: NeRF-based approaches (e.g., UniSim) fail to render unseen scenes when the simulated trajectory deviates from source data, while diffusion-based methods (e.g., Panacea) are restricted to offline, fixed-length video generation with high memory consumption and no adaptability to dynamic trajectory changes. No prior method supports online, frame-by-frame video generation of arbitrary length while maintaining temporal consistency and responding to changing ego trajectories in real time.

## Input / Output

Glad accepts either pure Gaussian noise (generator mode) or a VAE-encoded reference frame (simulator mode) as the initial latent, conditioned on a BEV semantic layout (10 object categories + 3 map element types, converted to camera-view bounding boxes, depth maps, road maps, and pose embeddings via ControlNet) and a text prompt (via CLIP). It outputs a sequence of 6-view driving images at 256×512 pixels per frame, generated one frame at a time, with no upper bound on sequence length. During training, images are processed at 512×256 resolution with random resize (0.3–0.36 scale) and crop to match downstream StreamPETR input.

## Method

Glad extends Stable Diffusion 2.1 with two key modules. First, Latent Variable Propagation (LVP) replaces the standard Gaussian noise in the forward diffusion process with the layer-normalized denoised latent features of the previous frame, so each new frame is denoised from the prior frame's output rather than from random noise, enforcing temporal consistency across the sequence. Second, the Streaming Data Sampler (SDS) processes a video clip frame-by-frame across consecutive training iterations, caching each frame's denoised latent for use as the next frame's noise prior, which keeps training and inference consistent while capping GPU memory at a constant 38 GB regardless of clip length (vs. linear growth in sliding-window samplers). The denoising UNet is conditioned via ControlNet (BEV layout) and CLIP (text), and training follows a two-stage protocol: image-level pre-training from Gaussian noise, then video-level fine-tuning on nuScenes with the SDS. At inference, DDIM sampling with 25 steps and CFG scale 5.0 produces each frame sequentially, enabling theoretically unbounded video length.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, Glad (without reference frame) achieves FID 12.57 and FVD 207, while with a reference frame it improves to FID 11.18 and FVD 188. For downstream 3D detection on generated data, Glad (nuImage pre-trained, 8-frame video) reaches mAP 28.3 and NDS 41.3, corresponding to 74.9% and 83.6% of the Oracle (mAP 37.8, NDS 49.4). When used for training augmentation with nuImage pre-training, Glad boosts StreamPETR to mAP 39.8 and NDS 51.3 (+1.9 NDS over the stronger baseline), and improves HD map construction mAP by +4.9 over baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Glad achieves the best FID among all compared methods, outperforming the strongest offline baselines Drive-WM (15.80) and DrivingDiffusion (15.83) by over 3 points, and single-frame methods BEVGen (25.54) and BEVControl (24.85) by more than 12 points. However, Glad's FVD of 207 is higher than Panacea (139) and Drive-WM (123), indicating that while individual frame quality is superior, temporal smoothness as measured by FVD is slightly weaker than the best offline methods. On downstream 3D detection (Table 2), Glad substantially outperforms Panacea (mAP 28.3 vs. 19.9, NDS 41.3 vs. 32.3) and Drive-WM (mAP 28.3 vs. 20.7), confirming that the generated videos are more useful for perception tasks. For training augmentation (Table 3), Glad matches Panacea exactly on the ImageNet-pretrained setting (both +2.3 NDS) but surpasses it by 0.1 NDS and 2.8 AMOTA when combined with nuImage pre-training. On HD map construction (Table 4), Glad's generated data pre-training yields +4.9 mAP over baseline, demonstrating practical utility beyond detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLAD builds directly on Stable Diffusion's VAE + denoising UNet architecture and extends it to video generation by replacing the standard Gaussian noise prior with the denoised latent features of the previous frame (Latent Variable Propagation). This departs from offline driving video generators like Panacea, DriveDreamer-2, and DrivingDiffusion, which produce fixed-length clips in a single forward pass using sliding windows, and from autoregressive approaches (VideoGPT, CogVideo) that predict discrete tokens. The Streaming Data Sampler is a training-side innovation that keeps per-iteration memory constant regardless of clip length, contrasting with the sliding-window sampler used by Panacea. For conditioning, GLAD follows the established recipe of ControlNet + CLIP for BEV layout and text control, consistent with MagicDrive and Panacea.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling frame-by-frame generation of arbitrary length with constant memory, GLAD addresses a practical bottleneck in driving simulation: the inability of offline generators to adapt to dynamically changing ego trajectories or to produce unbounded video sequences. The paper demonstrates that generated data can meaningfully improve downstream 3D detection, tracking, and HD map construction, positioning streaming diffusion as a viable data-augmentation pipeline. However, the authors acknowledge that high-dynamic scenes remain a weakness, and the quality gap to Oracle (e.g., 74.9% mAP) suggests that temporal fidelity for fast-moving objects is an open challenge. Whether the simple LVP mechanism generalizes to longer horizons (beyond ~16 frames) or to more complex multi-agent interactions remains to be seen.
