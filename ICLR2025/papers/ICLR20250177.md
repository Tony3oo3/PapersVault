## Problem

Offline model-based RL suffers from model exploitation, where learned policies exploit regions of the state-action space where the dynamics model is inaccurate, leading to policies that perform worse than the data-logging policy. Existing approaches rely on probabilistic ensembles with heuristic uncertainty estimators (e.g., maximum ensemble variance) that are not distance-aware and struggle particularly in low-quality data regimes with poor state-action coverage or suboptimal demonstrations. The paper asks whether a single learned dynamics model can provide principled, distance-aware uncertainty estimates that enable effective conservatism without the computational overhead of ensembles.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of state-action trajectories from MuJoCo environments (D4RL and NeoRL benchmarks), where states are decomposed into position and velocity components and time steps Δt between consecutive states are known. It produces two outputs: a learned neural SDE dynamics model (drift + diffusion terms) and a control policy trained via SAC. The policy is evaluated by human-normalized scores, and the dynamics model is assessed by prediction error over multi-step rollouts. The distance-aware uncertainty estimator η_φ is trained jointly with the SDE and used at inference time to penalize rewards and truncate rollouts.

## Method

NUNO learns a single dynamics model as a neural SDE ds = f_θ(s,a)dt + (σ_φ(s,a) + h_φ(η_φ(s,a)))⋆dW, where the drift term f_θ encodes rigid body dynamics as an inductive bias (position derivative equals velocity; velocity dynamics decomposed into G(s_vel)·a + H(s)·s_vel + residual), and the diffusion term separates aleatoric uncertainty (σ_φ) from distance-aware epistemic uncertainty (h_φ(η_φ), a bounded monotonic sigmoid transform). The key novelty is the parametric distance-aware uncertainty estimator η_φ, trained with a composite loss enforcing local strong convexity near training data, zero-gradient/zero-value constraints at data points, and a regularization term that pushes η_φ to saturate near dataset boundaries—effectively clustering the dataset without explicit KNN search. A theoretical result (Lemma 1) shows that the optimal η_φ is convex and its negative gradient points inside the convex hull of the training data, guaranteeing that reward penalization by η_φ incentivizes the policy to stay close to the data. Policy training uses a pessimistic reward R̃(s,a) = R(s,a) − λ_pen·η_φ(s,a) and truncates synthetic rollouts when accumulated uncertainty exceeds a CVaR-based threshold computed from the full dataset.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo v2 (12 tasks), NUNO achieves the highest average human-normalized score of 83.8, outperforming MOBILE (80.0), EDAC (76.0), TATU+MOPO (71.3), RAMBO (67.7), COMBO (66.8), MOREL (64.3), and MOPO (49.4). In the low-quality random datasets, NUNO leads by large margins (e.g., 73.2 vs. 38.1 for MOREL on hopper-random, a ~93% improvement over the best baseline). On NeoRL (9 tasks), NUNO achieves the highest average score of 70.6, surpassing MOBILE (60.7), CQL (56.1), TD3+BC (54.5), EDAC (50.7), BC (45.4), and MOPO (28.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NUNO decisively outperforms all baselines in the low-quality random datasets: it beats MOBILE by 13.4, 41.3, and 9.8 points on halfcheetah, hopper, and walker2d random respectively, and surpasses the next-best method (MOREL on hopper-random at 38.1) by ~93%. Against MOPO and TATU+MOPO, NUNO's advantage is most pronounced in random tasks (e.g., 73.2 vs. 16.7 and 31.9 on hopper-random) and medium-replay tasks (97.0 vs. 73.7 and 75.3 on walker2d-medium-replay), while in medium and medium-expert tasks the gap narrows or reverses. MOBILE is the strongest competitor in high-quality settings, outperforming NUNO on halfcheetah-medium (74.6 vs. 68.8), halfcheetah-medium-expert (108.2 vs. 97.0), and walker2d-medium-expert (115.2 vs. 113.2), but NUNO wins on hopper-medium-replay (107.8 vs. 103.9) and walker2d-medium-replay (97.0 vs. 89.9). COMBO, MOREL, RAMBO, and EDAC are consistently outperformed by NUNO across all dataset qualities, with the largest gaps in random and medium-replay tasks. On NeoRL, NUNO dominates all baselines in the low-quality datasets (e.g., 26.9 vs. 17.4 for MOBILE on hopper-low) and achieves the best or near-best scores in medium and high datasets, with an average lead of ~10 points over MOBILE.

## Relation to Prior Work

NUNO sits at the intersection of two research threads: physics-informed neural SDEs for dynamics modeling (Djeumou et al., 2023) and uncertainty-penalized offline model-based RL (MOPO, TATU+MOPO). It departs from the standard deep probabilistic ensemble architecture used in most offline model-based RL by replacing it with a single neural SDE whose diffusion term simultaneously captures aleatoric uncertainty (via a learned σ) and epistemic uncertainty (via a distance-aware η_φ transformed through a bounded monotonic function). The distance-aware estimator itself builds on the KNN-based theoretical framework of DARL (Zhang et al., 2023a) but replaces the intractable nearest-neighbor search with a parametric neural network trained via local strong-convexity and zero-gradient constraints, enabling joint training with the SDE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single neural SDE can match or exceed probabilistic ensembles in both prediction accuracy over long horizons and uncertainty calibration, which is practically significant because ensembles are computationally expensive and their uncertainty heuristics (max std, pairwise differences) lack theoretical grounding. The 93% improvement on low-coverage datasets highlights that model exploitation is the dominant failure mode in offline RL with suboptimal data, and that distance-aware uncertainty is the right signal for penalization. Key limitations include evaluation only on MuJoCo with full state observation (no image-based or partially observed settings), and the distance-aware estimator's theoretical properties (convexity, gradient direction) are proven only for the idealized centroid-distance solution, not the learned parametric version. Extending to non-Euclidean state-action spaces and formally characterizing the learned η_φ remain open.
