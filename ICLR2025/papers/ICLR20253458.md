## Problem

Existing source-code LLMs do not generalize to assembly code because assembly has low information density (a single source statement maps to many instructions, so semantics span long token sequences) and exhibits extreme syntax diversity across compiler optimization levels. Prior binary-code LLMs are either encoder-only architectures that cannot perform generation tasks, or generative models lacking designs specific to assembly's structural challenges, resulting in poor decompilation accuracy and suboptimal similarity detection.

## Input / Output

For decompilation, the model receives a prompt containing the optimization level and the normalized X86-64 assembly (addresses replaced with sequential [INST-N] labels, hex converted to decimal, whitespace standardized) and generates a C source function. For similarity detection, a query O0-assembly is encoded and ranked against a pool of K O3-assembly candidates; the embedding is computed as the mean of all [INST] token hidden states from the final transformer layer. The [INST] tokens are the key structural element: they are appended to each instruction and serve as per-instruction semantic summaries that the hierarchical attention mechanism explicitly models.

## Method

Nova is built on DeepSeek-Coder and introduces a three-level hierarchical self-attention mask applied to half of the attention heads: intra-instruction attention (standard causal within each instruction, with the [INST] label attending to all its tokens to form a summary), preceding-instruction attention (each token attends to the [INST] label of the immediately preceding instruction for local register/context dependencies), and inter-instruction attention ([INST] labels attend to all prior [INST] labels to capture long-range functional dependencies). During pre-training, Nova is trained with two additional contrastive objectives beyond language modeling: functionality CL pulls embeddings of the same function across source and O0–O3 assembly forms closer together, while optimization CL enforces a monotonic distance ordering so that higher optimization levels are progressively farther from the source embedding. The combined loss is L = L_lm + 0.1·(L_fcl + L_ocl), trained for one epoch after an initial LM-only epoch on ~4.3M assembly functions from AnghaBench and The-Stack. The model is then fine-tuned separately for decompilation (next-token prediction on assembly-to-source pairs) and similarity detection (InfoNCE-style ranking loss over O0-vs-O3 pairs).

## Results

On HumanEval-Decompile (164 C functions per optimization level), Nova-1.3B achieves an average Pass@1 of 25.17% and Pass@10 of 38.30%, while Nova-6.7B reaches 34.36% and 46.91% respectively. These represent absolute improvements of 14.84% (Pass@1) and 21.58% (Pass@10) over the closest same-size baseline LLM4Decompile-1.3B, and 8.38%/13.80% over GPT-4o. On binary code similarity detection (BCSD), Nova-6.7B achieves the highest average Recall@1 across all pool sizes (K=50–500), reaching 0.86 at K=50 and 0.67 at K=500, outperforming CodeArt by up to 6.17% Recall@1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nova-1.3B outperforms LLM4Decompile-1.3B (the closest same-size baseline) by 14.84% absolute Pass@1 and 21.58% Pass@10 on average, and also surpasses GPT-4o (an order of magnitude larger) by 8.38% Pass@1 and 13.80% Pass@10. Nova-6.7B extends this lead over LLM4Decompile-6.7B by 12.41% Pass@1 and 15.53% Pass@10. All general-purpose code LLMs (CodeLlama-7B, StarCoder2-7B, DeepSeekCoder-7B, Qwen-2.5-Coder-7B) and Meta LLMCompiler-7B trail Nova by large margins (Pass@1 Avg ≤ 7.82% vs. Nova-1.3B's 25.17%). On BCSD, Nova-6.7B achieves the highest average Recall@1 at every pool size (K=50: 0.86, K=100: 0.75, K=200: 0.79, K=500: 0.67), beating CodeArt (the strongest encoder-based baseline) by up to 6.17% at K=50; Nova-1.3B ties CodeArt at K=500 (both 0.64) but wins at K=50, 100, and 200. jTrans and DiEmph are consistently outperformed across all benchmarks and pool sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Nova builds on DeepSeek-Coder (a decoder-only source code LLM) and adapts it for assembly by introducing a three-level hierarchical self-attention (intra-instruction, preceding-instruction, inter-instruction) that replaces standard causal attention on half the heads, plus two contrastive learning objectives (functionality and optimization) inspired by SimCSE. It departs from encoder-style binary models (CodeArt, jTrans, DiEmph) that cannot perform generation, and from prior generative decompilers (LLM4Decompile, SLaDe) that lack architecture-specific designs for assembly's low information density and compiler-optimization diversity. Unlike AST-based hierarchical approaches (CAST, PA-former, HierarchyNet) that require source code structure, Nova's instruction-level hierarchy operates directly on flat assembly sequences and is pluggable into any pre-trained generative LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Assembly code analysis underpins critical security tasks (vulnerability detection, malware analysis, binary recovery), yet LLMs have struggled to generalize from source code to assembly due to its low information density and optimization-induced syntax diversity. Nova demonstrates that targeted architectural and training modifications can close this gap substantially, with a 1.3B model outperforming GPT-4o on decompilation—a result suggesting task-specific inductive biases can compensate for scale. Key limitations include X86-64/C-only scope, model sizes capped at 6.7B due to compute constraints, and the fine-tuned-vs-few-shot asymmetry in the GPT-4o comparison. Open questions include generalization to ARM/MIPS, scaling to larger models, and whether the hierarchical attention and contrastive objectives transfer to other low-density sequential domains.
