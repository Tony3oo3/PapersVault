## Problem

Current Natural Language Explanations (NLEs) for visual classifiers are unfaithful because a separate textual decoder is explicitly trained on annotated (prediction, explanation) pairs, causing explanations to reflect annotator reasoning rather than the model's actual decision process. No prior method can generate faithful, sentence-level NLEs in a zero-shot setting for arbitrary visual classifiers without any training data or contrastive pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253066/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image processed by a frozen visual classifier of any architecture (CNN, Transformer, or hybrid) and a text prompt (class name, concept descriptor, or free sentence) encoded by a frozen text encoder. The key internal signals are the classifier's visual feature vector f and its fixed class weight matrix W. Outputs include natural language explanation sentences (generated by a frozen GPT-2 with optimized prefixes, ~35 s per explanation on an RTX 3090), zero-shot class predictions, top-B concept scores, and image captions. Notably, the only 'training data' required is the set of class names paired with the classifier's own weight vectors (e.g., 1,000 pairs for ImageNet-1K), and the MLP trains in roughly 10 seconds.

## Method

The core insight is that a classifier's weight matrix W already encodes class semantics in the same space as visual features f, so a small MLP trained to regress text embeddings onto W effectively learns the classifier's visual-semantic space. Concretely, a frozen text encoder produces sentence embeddings for prompts like 'an image of a {class}', which a two-layer MLP (with LayerNorm, GELU, and dropout) maps to d-dimensional class embeddings; the MLP is trained with a cosine-distance loss against the ground-truth classifier weights, augmented by Input Dropout (randomly zeroing 50% of input features) and a Learned Soft Dropout (GLU gating) to handle the extreme data scarcity of ~1,000 training pairs. For NLE generation, a frozen GPT-2 is steered via inference-time prefix-tuning: at each decoding step, top-K candidate tokens are scored by passing their continuations through the text encoder and MLP and computing cosine similarity with the image's visual features, and the prefixes are updated by cross-entropy against the softmax-normalized scores. After I=20 iterations each producing one full sentence, the explanation with the highest CLIP-Score is selected as the final NLE, ensuring faithfulness to the classifier's own feature space rather than to any annotated corpus.

## Results

On zero-shot NLE generation (the primary task), the method achieves CLIP-Score of 30.56–30.92 across CNN and Transformer classifiers, consistently outperforming all four supervised baselines (NLX-GPT, NLX-GPT^ft, Uni-NLX, Uni-NLX^ft) which score 28.38–28.53. On zero-shot image classification (validating the learned class embeddings), the method achieves 33.28% Test@1 on ResNet-50 in the generalized zero-shot setting, more than doubling ICIS's 13.04%. On zero-shot image captioning (COCO), BEiT-L/16 achieves state-of-the-art CIDEr of 17.6 and SPICE of 7.6, surpassing ZeroCap (14.6/5.5) and ConZIC (12.8/5.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ICIS (the state-of-the-art zero-shot classification baseline), the method wins decisively on the generalized zero-shot Test@1 metric across nearly all 38 models (e.g., ResNet-50: 33.28% vs. 13.04%; DINOv2-B: 21.52% vs. 13.96%; ViT-B/16swag: 9.18% vs. 4.98%), though it trails slightly on Train@1 accuracy. Against the four supervised NLE baselines (NLX-GPT, NLX-GPT^ft, Uni-NLX, Uni-NLX^ft), the zero-shot method wins on CLIP-Score by ~2 points across all models and on LPIPS (both AlexNet and SqueezeNet variants) by 0.005–0.015, while remaining comparable on the Cosine metric (which the authors note is suboptimal for semantic alignment). On zero-shot image captioning (COCO), the method with BEiT-L/16 achieves the best CIDEr (17.6) and SPICE (7.6), beating ZeroCap (14.6/5.5) and ConZIC (12.8/5.2), though ZeroCap retains a slight edge on BLEU-4 (2.6 vs. 1.8). For concept discovery, adding detected concepts to the class-name prompt improves Top-1 accuracy by ~10 points across all models (e.g., ResNet-50: 73.66% vs. 63.42% baseline), confirming the faithfulness of the discovered concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ICIS framework (Christensen et al., 2023) of regressing classifier weights from text via a small MLP, but repurposes it for explanation generation rather than classification. For the explanation generation step, it adapts the inference-time prefix optimization loop from ZeroCap (Tewel et al., 2021) and the prefix-tuning mechanism from Li & Liang (2021), steering a frozen GPT-2 to produce text that maximizes similarity with the classifier's visual features. The key conceptual departure from prior NLE work (NLX-GPT, Uni-NLX, DeVIL) is eliminating the need for annotated (prediction, explanation) pairs entirely: instead of training a separate explainer to mimic annotator reasoning, the method queries the classifier's own embedding space, making explanations structurally faithful to the model's decision process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes the primary barrier to faithful model explanations — the need for large annotated explanation datasets — and makes the technique applicable to any visual classifier (CNN or Transformer) with roughly 10 seconds of training on a single GPU. The demonstrated generality across 38 architectures and the additional applications (zero-shot classification, concept discovery, image captioning) suggest the learned text-to-classifier-embedding mapping is a broadly useful primitive. Limitations include the ~35-second inference time per explanation, occasional drift toward background or general features, and the reliance on CLIP-Score as an external faithfulness judge, which itself may not perfectly capture model-specific reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-label or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the faithfulness guarantee degrades for classifiers with highly non-semantic weight spaces.
