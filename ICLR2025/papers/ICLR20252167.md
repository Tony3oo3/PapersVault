## Problem

Existing differentially private in-context learning methods, such as DP few-shot generation (Tang et al., 2023), add a fixed amount of Gaussian noise during private aggregation of next-token probability vectors, ignoring the fact that these vectors are naturally highly clustered. This data-independent noise calibration leads to a suboptimal privacy-utility tradeoff: tokens with high inter-example agreement receive unnecessarily large noise, degrading ICL accuracy. The paper asks whether a data-adaptive mechanism can reduce noise where the data structure permits while preserving formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a private dataset D_priv of (x_i, y_i) pairs, a pre-trained LLM (e.g., Llama-2-7b-hf), and a randomly selected label ỹ from the task's label set. It produces n_shot synthetic demonstrations (x̃_i, ỹ_i) where each x̃_i is generated token-by-token over a reduced vocabulary of top-K=100 tokens, with each token determined by privately aggregating M next-token probability vectors (each computed from a disjoint subset of N private examples). These synthetic demonstrations are then concatenated with a user query to form an ICL prompt sent to the LLM, which returns the final prediction. The entire synthesis process is one-time: once generated, the demonstrations can serve unlimited queries at no additional privacy cost.

## Method

AdaDPSyn's core innovation is the Precision-Focused Iterative Radius Reduction technique, which exploits the empirical observation that next-token probability vectors from different private examples cluster tightly (radius 0.05–0.15) compared to the full probability simplex (radius √2/2). The algorithm first uses the GoodRadius subroutine (Nissim et al., 2016) to differentially privately establish a target radius r covering at least 80% of the vectors. It then starts with R = √2/2 and iteratively: (1) estimates the projected mean of the M vectors using a Gaussian mechanism with noise calibrated to sensitivity 2R, (2) checks via a noisy count whether a sufficient fraction (μ=0.55) of vectors fall within the current ball, and (3) projects all vectors onto the ℓ₂-ball of radius R centered at the estimated mean before re-estimating. As R shrinks toward r, the additive noise decreases proportionally, yielding more accurate aggregation. Privacy is tracked via Rényi DP composition across all iterations, with subsampling amplification from drawing MN samples per token, and converted to (ε,δ)-DP via the Balle et al. (2020) lemma.

## Results

AdaDPSyn achieves 4-shot ICL accuracy of 65.42% on AGNews, 66.76% on DBPedia, 69.32% on TREC, 37.59% on MIT-G, and 73.35% on MIT-D at a strict privacy budget of ε=1, consistently outperforming the DP few-shot generation baseline (Tang et al., 2023) across all five datasets and all tested privacy levels (ε=1, 2, 4, 8). At ε=1, AdaDPSyn nearly matches the non-private baseline on AGNews (65.42% vs. 65.92%) and DBPedia (66.76% vs. 69.06%), with the largest absolute gap to the non-private baseline appearing on MIT-G (37.59% vs. 43.23%). The method also shows stable performance across hyperparameter choices (λ, T̂) and different LLM backends (Llama-2-7B, GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4o mini, Gemma-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is DP few-shot generation (Tang et al., 2023), which uses a data-independent Gaussian noise mechanism with a fixed sensitivity of √2 during private aggregation. AdaDPSyn outperforms this baseline on all five datasets at every tested privacy level (ε=1, 2, 4, 8). The largest gains appear on TREC at ε=1 (69.32% vs. 62.48%, a +6.84 pp improvement) and MIT-G at ε=1 (37.59% vs. 31.15%, a +6.44 pp improvement), where the data-adaptive radius reduction most effectively reduces unnecessary noise. On AGNews at ε=1, AdaDPSyn achieves 65.42% versus 60.74% for DP few-shot generation (+4.68 pp), and at ε=8 it essentially matches the non-private baseline (65.92% vs. 65.92%). On MIT-D the gap is smaller (73.35% vs. 72.87% at ε=1) because the task already has high token agreement, leaving less room for adaptive noise reduction. Empirically, AdaDPSyn also reduces membership inference attack AUC to near random-guessing levels (~50–53) compared to 77.37 for the non-private baseline, confirming the practical privacy benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP few-shot generation framework of Tang et al. (2023), which uses a PATE-like sample-and-aggregate pipeline with data-independent Gaussian noise calibrated to the global sensitivity of the probability simplex (√2). The key departure is replacing this fixed-noise aggregation with a data-adaptive mechanism: the authors observe that next-token probability vectors from an LLM are tightly clustered (radius r ≈ 0.05–0.15, versus the simplex radius √2/2 ≈ 0.707), and exploit this via a Precision-Focused Iterative Radius Reduction technique that projects vectors into a shrinking ball before adding noise, reducing effective sensitivity by 5–14×. The target radius is obtained differentially privately using the GoodRadius subroutine from Nissim et al. (2016), connecting this work to the DP 1-clustering literature. The privacy analysis composes Rényi DP guarantees across the iterative radius-refinement loop, the projected mean estimation, and the coverage check, with subsampling amplification per token generation step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for privacy-sensitive ICL deployments (healthcare, finance): at ε=1, AdaDPSyn nearly matches non-private accuracy (65.42% vs 65.92% on AGNews) while prior DP methods lag by ~5 points, and the synthetic demonstrations can be reused for unlimited queries without additional privacy cost. The clustering-based sensitivity reduction is a clean, generalizable principle that the authors note may apply beyond prompt synthesis to other DP aggregation problems. A stated limitation is the absence of a theoretical utility guarantee (no closed-form expression for ICL accuracy as a function of ε), and the clustering property has only been verified on five benchmarks with Llama-2-7B, leaving open whether it holds for smaller models, different architectures, or out-of-distribution tasks.
