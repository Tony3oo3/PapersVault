## Problem

Foundation models like DINOv2 are widely considered inadequate for Visual Place Recognition without extensive fine-tuning, and existing adaptation strategies rely on external pooling modules (NetVLAD, GeM, optimal transport) or trainable adapters that produce very large feature vectors (8K–16K dimensions), increasing memory footprint and reducing generalization. This paper challenges the necessity of these external aggregation components, arguing that the ViT's internal self-attention mechanism already performs implicit pooling that can be directly exploited for both global representation and local re-ranking.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query RGB image (processed at 504×504 resolution at inference, 224×224 at training) and a gallery of geo-tagged images, producing a ranked list of matching gallery images via a two-stage pipeline. The global stage outputs a compact feature vector (as small as 128 dimensions via a linear projection on the [CLS] token) used for nearest-neighbor retrieval, while the re-ranking stage outputs a mutual-nearest-neighbor match count computed from Value-facet local descriptors filtered by an attention-map threshold. The entire re-ranking computation is integrated into the forward pass of the ViT, requiring no separate feature extraction or spatial verification, with an average matching latency of ~1 ms per candidate pair.

## Method

EffoVPR initializes a ViT-L/14 backbone with DINOv2 pre-trained weights and fine-tunes only the last five layers using a CosFace classification loss on the [CLS] token, following EigenPlaces' 15×15 m cell-based partitioning to enforce viewpoint invariance. The key architectural insight is that the [CLS] token's self-attention over all patch tokens already performs implicit global pooling, eliminating the need for external modules like GeM or NetVLAD; a simple linear layer on the [CLS] output then yields compact global features down to 128D. For re-ranking, the method extracts Value (V) facets from the n−1 self-attention layer as local descriptors, selects discriminative keypoints by thresholding the attention map S = Softmax(Q_l · k_cls) at T₁, and scores candidates by counting mutual nearest-neighbor pairs with cosine similarity above T₂. This re-ranking requires no additional training, spatial verification, or learned parameters, and works effectively even in a zero-shot setting with vanilla DINOv2, where it outperforms AnyLoc and approaches fine-tuned VPR methods.

## Results

EffoVPR-R (two-stage) achieves state-of-the-art R@1 on Tokyo24/7 (98.7%), MSLS-val (92.8%), and MSLS-challenge (79.0%), while ranking second only on Pitts30k (93.9% vs. CricaVPR's 94.9%). The single-stage EffoVPR-G achieves 97.5% R@1 on Tokyo24/7 and 93.5% on Nordland with 1024D features, and retains 94.6% on Tokyo24/7 even at 128D — a 66× reduction versus SALAD's 8448D for equivalent performance. On the most challenging benchmarks, EffoVPR-R leads by large margins: +4.3% on Nordland (95.0%), +7.9% on SF-XL-Occlusion (59.2%), and +15% on SF-XL-Night (61.6%) over the next best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EffoVPR-R outperforms all 12 baselines on Tokyo24/7 (98.7% R@1 vs. 94.6% SALAD, 94.0% SelaVPR, 93.0% CricaVPR/EigenPlaces), MSLS-val (92.8% vs. 92.2% SALAD, 90.8% SelaVPR), and MSLS-challenge (79.0% vs. 75.0% SALAD, 73.5% SelaVPR). On Pitts30k, CricaVPR edges ahead at 94.9% R@1 vs. EffoVPR-R's 93.9%, though the authors note CricaVPR used Pitts30k as a validation set. Against the zero-shot baselines, EffoVPR-ZS dominates: 90.8% on Tokyo24/7 vs. 60.6% AnyLoc and 62.2% vanilla DINOv2, and 57.9% on Nordland vs. 16.1% AnyLoc. On the challenging datasets (Table 4), EffoVPR-R leads by wide margins — 95.0% on Nordland vs. 90.7% CricaVPR, 61.6% on SF-XL-Night vs. 46.6% SALAD, and 59.2% on SF-XL-Occlusion vs. 51.3% SALAD. The single-stage EffoVPR-G also surpasses all baselines on Tokyo24/7 (97.5%) and Nordland (93.5%) at 1024D, and matches SALAD's Tokyo24/7 score (94.6%) at just 128D versus SALAD's 8448D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DINOv2 as a pre-trained ViT backbone and adopts the cell-based classification training paradigm from EigenPlaces (CosFace loss over 15×15m grid cells). Its key departure from the VPR literature is a conceptual challenge to the near-universal reliance on external pooling modules (GeM, NetVLAD, VLAD, optimal transport in SALAD, bag-of-queries in BoQ): the authors argue that the [CLS] token already performs implicit aggregation through self-attention, and that internal Value facets from intermediate layers serve as effective keypoint descriptors for re-ranking without any learned adapters or spatial verification. This positions EffoVPR as a simplification of the SelaVPR/CricaVPR/SALAD/BoQ line of work, which all introduce additional trainable components on top of DINOv2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale deployment: achieving SOTA with 128D global features (vs. 8,448D for SALAD or 16,384D for BoQ) reduces gallery memory by over 60×, and the 1ms re-ranking latency makes two-stage search nearly free. The zero-shot capability (competitive with trained methods on several benchmarks) lowers the barrier to entry for new cities or domains. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond street-level VPR to indoor or aerial settings, whether the fixed thresholds T₁/T₂ transfer across very different gallery distributions, and whether the reliance on DINOv2's specific self-attention structure limits applicability to other foundation model families.
