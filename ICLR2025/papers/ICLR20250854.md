## Problem

The VAE + latent diffusion paradigm that drives 2D generation has not translated to 3D because no existing 3D representation simultaneously offers high-fidelity rendering, efficient embedding into a low-dimensional latent space, and the ability to generate a large number of 3D primitives without scaling network parameters. Prior 3D Gaussian decoding methods either lack an information bottleneck, rely on complex multi-component pipelines, or require additional upsampling networks that cap the number of Gaussians.

## Input / Output

The VAE encoder consumes a sparse point cloud (2048 points) and 4 RGB views at 224×224, producing a latent set of 512 vectors. The latent diffusion model takes either a CLIP text embedding or a CLIP image embedding as conditioning and outputs a latent code that the decoder maps to M=2048 Atlas Gaussian patches, from which an arbitrary number of 3D Gaussians (e.g., 32K–100K+) are decoded via UV-space query sampling. Rendering produces 512×512 RGB, alpha, and depth images in real time, and the number of Gaussians is decoupled from network size—increasing it requires no additional parameters.

## Method

Atlas Gaussians represent a shape as a union of M local patches, each parameterized by a 3D center, four geometry feature vectors, and four appearance feature vectors placed at the corners of a unit UV square. A learned nonlinear weight function (based on sinusoidal positional encoding with MLP projection, rather than bilinear interpolation) interpolates these corner features at arbitrary UV query points to decode Gaussian centers and attributes, enabling a theoretically infinite number of Gaussians without extra parameters. The VAE encoder aggregates point-cloud and ViT image features into a latent set via cross-attention transformers; the decoder upsamples the latent via pixel shuffle, then uses separate transformer branches with local self-attention (reducing complexity from O(β²M²d) to O(β²Md)) to decode patch centers and disentangled geometry/appearance features. A two-stage training first optimizes geometry losses (Chamfer/EMD distance) without rendering, then adds differentiable 3D Gaussian splatting rendering losses (MSE + LPIPS) at 512×512. The learned latent space is then used with an EDM-based latent diffusion model (12–24 transformer blocks, 40 denoising steps, classifier-free guidance) for unconditional or text/image-conditioned 3D generation.

## Results

On ShapeNet unconditional generation, Atlas Gaussians achieves FID@50K of 9.90 (Chair), 12.15 (Car), and 8.09 (Plane), outperforming the strongest baseline LN3Diff (16.90, 17.60, 8.84) by 41%, 31%, and 8% respectively. On Objaverse text-conditioned generation, the method attains the best CLIP score (30.66), FID@6K (109.5), and KID@6K (4.04%) across all baselines, while also achieving the fastest inference time at 4 seconds on a TITAN V GPU—roughly 8× faster than Shap-E (33 s) and 1.5–2× faster than LGM (6 s) and LN3Diff (7.5 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Atlas Gaussians outperforms all nine baselines on both unconditional (ShapeNet) and conditional (Objaverse) generation. Against the closest competitor LN3Diff (which also uses the VAE+LDM paradigm but relies on volume rendering), the method reduces FID by 41% on Chair and 31% on Car, and improves KID on all three categories. On text-conditioned Objaverse, it surpasses Shap-E (the strongest VAE+LDM baseline) by 0.44 CLIP score points and 5.0 FID points, while running 8× faster (4 s vs. 33 s). Compared to the multi-view-based LGM, Atlas Gaussians achieves a 1.04-point higher CLIP score and 2.96 lower FID, and avoids multi-view inconsistency artifacts. Against the volumetric GVGEN, the method improves CLIP by 3.33 points and FID by 22.9 points. The older GAN/implicit baselines (EG3D, GET3D, DiffRF, RenderDiffusion, SSDNeRF) are all substantially outperformed, with FID gaps exceeding 10 points in most categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VAE + LDM paradigm established for 2D image generation (Rombach et al., 2022) and adapts it to 3D by introducing Atlas Gaussians as the bridge between latent space and 3D content. Conceptually, it extends the patch-based surface representation from AtlasNet (Groueix et al., 2018) by replacing per-patch MLPs with a learned UV-space interpolation over 3D Gaussian attributes, and borrows the attention-based weight function design from 3DShape2VecSet (Zhang et al., 2023a). The most direct departure is from LN3Diff (Lan et al., 2024), which uses volume rendering (NeRF) in its VAE; Atlas Gaussians replaces this with 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), gaining real-time rendering and the ability to scale the number of Gaussians without additional network parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to successfully integrate 3D Gaussian Splatting into the VAE + LDM feed-forward generation pipeline, demonstrating that the representation's real-time rendering and parameter-free scaling properties translate into both quality and efficiency gains over volume-rendering-based alternatives. The key open question is whether vanilla 3D Gaussians are sufficient for high-precision geometry extraction, as the authors themselves note; incorporating surface-aligned or opacity-field variants could close this gap. Additionally, the approach is validated on single-object categories (ShapeNet) and a ~18K subset of Objaverse, leaving open whether the representation scales to multi-object scenes or larger, more diverse datasets without the canonical-alignment assumption.
