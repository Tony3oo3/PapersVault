## Problem

Sparse Mixture of Experts (SMoE) models require manual tuning of two critical hyperparameters—the total number of experts K and the per-token activation count top-k—whose optimal values vary across tasks and can cause 1–3% performance swings. Identifying the right configuration demands extensive ablation studies that become prohibitively expensive as model scale grows. The paper asks whether a training strategy can automatically determine both the number of experts and the number of experts each token activates, eliminating this tuning burden entirely.

## Input / Output

DYNMOE is a drop-in replacement for the MLP layer inside existing Transformer backbones (ViT-S, BERT-large, StableLM-1.6B, Qwen-1.8B, Phi-2-2.7B), so inputs and outputs are unchanged from the host architecture: RGB images (336×336 for vision-language, standard resolution for vision), text sequences, or image-plus-question pairs go in, and class labels, GLUE scores, or free-form text answers come out. The only structural change is that each MoE layer now holds a variable number of experts (bounded by a user-set maximum of 4–16) whose count and per-token activation are decided dynamically during both training and inference rather than fixed at initialization.

## Method

DYNMOE introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, a top-any gating method reformulates expert selection as multi-label classification: each expert has a trainable representation vector and a trainable threshold G_e; a token's cosine similarity to each expert is passed through a sigmoid and compared against G_e, so each token independently activates any subset of experts (including zero). A custom backprop rule bypasses the non-differentiable sign function. Second, an adaptive training process periodically (every 100–300 iterations) inspects routing records: if tokens exist that activated no expert, a new expert is added and initialized from the normalized sum of those tokens' embeddings; if an expert was never selected, it is removed. A novel auxiliary loss—combining a diversity term ‖W_gᵀW_g − I_K‖₂ that pushes expert representations toward orthogonality (preventing all-expert activation) and a simplicity term that bounds the norm of each expert vector—replaces the standard load-balancing loss to enforce sparsity without a fixed top-k cap.

## Results

On vision-language tasks (Table 2), DYNMOE-LLaVA with StableLM-1.6B activates only 1.75B parameters (avg top-k=1.25) versus 2.06B for MoE-LLaVA (K=4, k=2), a ~15% reduction, while improving VQA-v2 from 76.7 to 77.4, GQA from 60.3 to 61.4, and MMBench from 60.2 to 63.2. With Phi-2-2.7B, DYNMOE-LLaVA activates 3.35B parameters (avg top-k=1.68) versus 3.62B for MoE-LLaVA, achieving higher scores on VQA-v2 (77.9 vs 77.6), GQA (61.6 vs 61.4), VisWiz (45.1 vs 43.9), and MMBench (66.6 vs 65.2). On GLUE (Table 11), DYNMOE achieves an average of 81.64, competitive with the best fixed MoE setting (K=8, k=4 at 81.77) while eliminating the need to search over K and top-k configurations that cause up to 3% performance fluctuation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MoE-LLaVA (Lin et al., 2024), DYNMOE-LLaVA clearly wins with StableLM-1.6B (activating ~15% fewer parameters while improving VQA-v2 by 0.7, GQA by 1.1, VisWiz by 4.4, and MMBench by 3.0) and with Phi-2-2.7B (activating ~7.5% fewer parameters while improving VQA-v2, GQA, VisWiz, and MMBench). With Qwen-1.8B, DYNMOE-LLaVA is roughly on par with MoE-LLaVA, with its average top-k (1.86) naturally converging near the fixed k=2 setting. Against GMoE (Li et al., 2023a) on vision tasks, DYNMOE with the diverse-and-simple gating loss achieves 72.4 average on DomainBed versus GMoE's 72.8, within random fluctuation, while outperforming the carefully tuned GMoE (Qiu et al., 2023) on VLCS (80.3 vs 79.6). Against the top-p gating baseline (Huang et al., 2024, p=0.4), DYNMOE achieves better VQA-v2 (77.4 vs 77.1) and VisWiz (40.6 vs 36.0) without introducing the additional hyperparameter p. Against dense LLaVA-1.5 (Liu et al., 2024), DYNMOE-LLaVA with StableLM-1.6B achieves competitive performance (e.g., 77.4 vs 78.5 on VQA-v2) while activating only 1.75B parameters versus 7B or 13B.

## Relation to Prior Work

DYNMOE extends the Sparse Mixture of Experts framework (Shazeer et al., 2017; Lepikhin et al., 2020; Fedus et al., 2022) by replacing the fixed top-k gating with a 'top-any' mechanism inspired by multi-label classification, where each token independently decides how many experts to activate via per-expert trainable thresholds. It further departs from prior MoE training by introducing an adaptive process that dynamically adds or removes experts during training, eliminating the need to pre-specify the total number of experts K. The paper also replaces standard load-balancing auxiliary losses (GShard, Switch) with a novel diversity-and-simplicity gating loss that encourages expert orthogonality and sparse activation simultaneously. As a training technique, DYNMOE is designed to be plug-and-play with existing MoE architectures such as GMoE, MoEfication, and MoE-LLaVA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value of DYNMOE lies in removing two of the most sensitive hyperparameters in MoE training (K and top-k), which the paper shows can cause up to 3% performance swings and require extensive ablation studies. The method achieves competitive or superior performance while activating fewer parameters (up to 15% reduction), making it attractive for practitioners who want MoE efficiency without the tuning burden. However, limitations remain: the routing overhead of top-any gating is non-trivial (Table 22), unused experts still occupy GPU memory in the current implementation, and validation is limited to models up to ~2.7B parameters. Open questions include whether the adaptive expert mechanism scales to trillion-parameter MoE models, whether CUDA-optimized implementations can close the routing overhead gap, and how the approach interacts with other dynamic computation techniques like layer skipping.
