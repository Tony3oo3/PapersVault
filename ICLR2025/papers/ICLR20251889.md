## Problem

MAXCUT-based graph pooling, which selects uniformly distributed sparse node subsets, has only been implemented in non-differentiable frameworks that ignore node features and downstream task objectives. Existing differentiable pooling methods either produce dense, uninterpretable pooled graphs (soft-clustering) or select non-uniform, locally clustered node subsets (scoring-based), limiting their effectiveness especially on heterophilic graphs where adjacent nodes carry dissimilar information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes node features X ∈ R^{N×F}, an adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N}, and optionally edge features, and produces a pooled graph with K supernodes (reduced feature matrix and coalesced adjacency), a binary MAXCUT partition, and downstream predictions (graph or node labels). A key design detail is that the RED operation multiplies selected node features by their scores via a Hadamard product, enabling gradients to flow through the otherwise non-differentiable top-K selection, while the CON operation coalesces edges by summing weights between supernode groups to produce a connected yet sparse pooled adjacency.

## Method

MaxCutPool uses a ScoreNet built from Heterophilic Message Passing (HetMP) layers with δ>1, which act as high-pass graph filters that amplify differences between adjacent nodes—directly opposing the smoothing effect of standard MP and aligning with the MAXCUT objective of separating connected nodes. The ScoreNet outputs a score vector s ∈ [-1,1]^N; the top-K nodes become supernodes, and remaining nodes are assigned to their nearest supernode via a GPU-parallel BFS procedure that guarantees a connected pooled graph. An auxiliary loss L_cut = s^T A s / |E| is minimized jointly with the task loss, encouraging connected nodes to receive opposite scores (i.e., be cut). The overall architecture follows the Select-Reduce-Connect framework, combining the uniform spatial distribution of one-every-K methods with the feature-awareness and end-to-end trainability of scoring-based poolers, while the Hadamard-product RED and S^T A S CON operations bridge the expressiveness gap with soft-clustering approaches.

## Results

On graph classification (Table 3), MaxCutPool-E achieves the best overall score (7/7 datasets in the top-performing group), with standout results of 100% on EXPWL1 (vs. 99% for k-MIS, 90% for EdgePool/Graclus), 90±2% on the novel Multipartite heterophilic dataset (vs. 58±2% for k-MIS, the next best), and 77±2% on COLLAB (vs. 72±3% for EdgePool/Graclus). On node classification over heterophilic graphs (Table 4), MaxCutPool-E scores 5/5 datasets in the top tier, most dramatically on Roman-Empire (60±4% accuracy vs. 22±5% for NDP and 26±7% for Top-k). For pure MAXCUT computation (Table 2), MaxCutPool finds the best cut on 15 out of 16 benchmark graphs, outperforming both the Goemans-Williamson SDP relaxation and a GCN-based GNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Goemans-Williamson SDP solver and the GCN-based GNN of Schuetz et al. (2022), MaxCutPool finds a strictly better cut on 15/16 benchmark graphs (e.g., 0.9815 vs. 0.9787 on Grid 60×40, 0.9040 vs. 0.8733 on RandRegular), with the sole exception being Grid(10×10) where all methods reach 1.0. In graph classification, MaxCutPool-E dominates on the two most heterophilic datasets: it achieves 100% on EXPWL1 (beating k-MIS's 99% and EdgePool/Graclus's 90%) and 87–90% on Multipartite (beating k-MIS's 58% and EdgePool's 55% by ~30 points). On COLLAB, all MaxCutPool variants reach 77%, a statistically significant improvement over EdgePool/Graclus (72%) and k-MIS (71%). Against soft-clustering baselines (DiffPool, DMoN, MinCutPool), MaxCutPool-E wins or ties on 6/7 datasets while using far less GPU memory (13–62 MB vs. 1,000–13,000 MB). In node classification on heterophilic graphs, MaxCutPool-E outperforms NDP (the closest one-every-K competitor) on Roman-Empire (60% vs. 22%) and Tolokers (91% vs. 88%), while NDP retains a narrow edge on Minesweeper (98% vs. 97%); Top-k and k-MIS are consistently outperformed across all five datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied areas: MAXCUT optimization (traditionally addressed via SDP relaxations like Goemans-Williamson or eigenvector-based methods like LEVS/NDP) and hierarchical graph pooling in GNNs (organized under the Select-Reduce-Connect framework into soft-clustering, scoring-based, and one-every-K families). The key departure is that prior MAXCUT-based pooling (NDP) was non-differentiable and feature-agnostic, while trainable poolers (DiffPool, MinCutPool, DMoN) relied on minCUT-like clustering objectives. By leveraging heterophilic message passing (δ > 1 in Bianchi's 2022 formulation) as a high-pass graph filter, the authors resolve the fundamental tension between the smoothing nature of standard MP layers and the anti-smoothing MAXCUT objective, enabling a differentiable, feature-aware MAXCUT pooler for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first trainable, feature-aware MAXCUT pooling operator, which is particularly valuable for heterophilic graphs where existing poolers (designed around homophilic assumptions) degrade significantly. The introduction of the Multipartite dataset fills a notable gap as the first heterophilic graph classification benchmark. The nearest-neighbor assignment scheme also offers a practical bridge between sparse scoring-based and dense soft-clustering poolers, potentially enhancing the expressiveness of the entire scoring-based family. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multiway cuts or higher-order pooling ratios, how it scales to very large graphs beyond the tested sizes, and whether pretraining with the auxiliary MAXCUT loss (as the authors suggest) can yield general-purpose graph representations.
