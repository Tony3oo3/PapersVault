## Problem

Existing time-series Transformers operate on contexts of only hundreds of patch tokens, far shorter than the thousands-to-millions used in NLP and vision, limiting their ability to capture global non-stationary trends and inter-variable correlations. Prior attempts to unify multivariate forecasting either use encoder-only architectures that degrade in long contexts or channel-independent designs that ignore cross-series dependencies. The paper asks whether a causal, decoder-only Transformer can be extended to thousands of patch tokens while simultaneously modeling fine-grained intra- and inter-series dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253027/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2D time series (N variables × TP time points) tokenized into non-overlapping patches of size P, which are flattened into a 1D sequence of NT tokens in temporal-first order. For covariate-informed forecasting, exogenous variables are appended with a custom variable-dependency matrix C that restricts which variables can attend to which. The output is the next patch token (P consecutive values) per variable per time step, supervised independently via MSE, enabling arbitrary lookback and prediction lengths at inference without retraining.

## Method

Timer-XL generalizes next-token prediction to multivariate next-token prediction, where each token's prediction conditions on all tokens at earlier time steps across all N variables, increasing context from T to NT tokens. The key architectural component is TimeAttention, which applies a Kronecker-product mask (C ⊗ T) to the attention scores, where C is the variable-dependency adjacency matrix and T is the standard temporal causal mask, thereby disentangling inter-series and intra-series dependencies while preserving causality. Positional encoding combines RoPE on the temporal axis with two learnable per-head scalars (u for endogenous, v for exogenous) on the variable axis, ensuring permutation-equivalence across variables. The decoder-only architecture with token-wise projection (rather than a flatten head) reduces parameter count and, empirically, mitigates the performance degradation that encoder-only Transformers exhibit in excessively long contexts. FlashAttention is integrated to reduce memory footprint from O(LHN²T²) to O(LDNT) without altering the attention computation.

## Results

On multivariate forecasting (96-pred-96), Timer-XL achieves the best MSE across all five standard benchmarks: 0.138 on ECL, 0.381 on ETTh1, 0.387 on Traffic, 0.165 on Weather, and 0.200 on Solar-Energy. In the one-for-all rolling forecast setting (672-pred-{96,192,336,720}), Timer-XL leads on ECL (0.155 MSE), ETTh1 (0.409), Traffic (0.374), and Solar-Energy (0.198), though UniTST slightly edges it on Weather (0.231 vs. 0.240). For zero-shot forecasting, Timer-XL Base (84M params) achieves the highest 1st-place count (15 wins) among all compared large models, outperforming Time-MoE Ultra, Moirai Large, TimesFM, MOMENT, and Chronos Large on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Timer-XL outperforms all eight supervised baselines (PatchTST, Timer, UniTST, iTransformer, DLinear, TimesNet, Stationary, Autoformer) on every dataset in Table 3, with the largest margins over PatchTST (e.g., 0.138 vs. 0.197 MSE on ECL) and Autoformer (0.387 vs. 0.612 on Traffic). Against the closest competitor UniTST (encoder-only with inter-series modeling), Timer-XL wins on ECL (0.138 vs. 0.139), ETTh1 (0.381 vs. 0.385), Traffic (0.387 vs. 0.389), and Solar-Energy (0.200 vs. 0.203), while tying on Weather (0.165). Compared to its direct predecessor Timer (channel-independent decoder-only), Timer-XL improves by 13.2% on ECL and 6.3% on Traffic, confirming the benefit of explicit inter-series modeling via TimeAttention. In the one-for-all rolling forecast (Table 4), Timer-XL leads on 4 of 5 datasets, with UniTST narrowly winning on Weather (0.231 vs. 0.240 MSE). For covariate-informed forecasting (Table 6), Timer-XL beats TimeXer (0.302 vs. 0.306 avg MSE) and Crossformer (0.302 vs. 0.362), and its causal variant consistently outperforms the noncausal variant. In zero-shot evaluation (Table 7), Timer-XL Base surpasses all large pre-trained models including Time-MoE Ultra, Moirai Large, TimesFM, MOMENT, and Chronos Large, achieving the most 1st-place finishes (15) with a smaller parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

Timer-XL directly extends Timer (Liu et al., 2024c), a decoder-only Transformer trained via univariate next token prediction, by generalizing the objective to multivariate next token prediction and introducing TimeAttention with Kronecker-product masking to capture fine-grained intra- and inter-series dependencies on flattened 2D patch tokens. It departs from encoder-only approaches like UniTST (which the authors show degrade in long contexts) and from channel-independent models like PatchTST and Timer by explicitly modeling inter-variable correlations while preserving temporal causality. The positional design combines RoPE on the temporal axis with learnable per-head scalars for permutation-equivalence on the variable axis, drawing on RoFormer (Su et al., 2024) and the variable-embedding idea from Moirai (Woo et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical unified framework that handles univariate, multivariate, and covariate-informed forecasting under a single decoder-only architecture, and introduces long-context benchmarks (ERA5-S/MS/Large spanning 40 years) that push beyond the short-context saturation of prior TSLib-style evaluations. Open questions remain: performance at very long (yearly) contexts still degrades due to noise and training difficulty, the O(N²T²) attention cost limits scalability to very high-dimensional series despite FlashAttention mitigating memory, and the long-context forecasting paradigm has yet to be validated on non-meteorological domains. The finding that causal masking within exogenous covariates improves accuracy challenges the common assumption that covariates should be treated as fully observable context.
