## Problem

Standard latent similarity metrics such as CKA, SVCCA, and Distance Correlation fail to detect meaningful correlations between multimodal representations (e.g., paired image and text embeddings) even when semantic alignment is known to exist. The correlations between these high-dimensional latent manifolds are highly nonlinear, rendering them invisible to both linear methods and existing nonlinear alternatives. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a correlation metric grounded in intrinsic dimension estimation that can capture such nonlinear dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251150/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two standardized paired datasets X ∈ R^{n,d1} and Y ∈ R^{n,d2} (latent representations from two models, possibly of different embedding dimensions) and produces a scalar correlation coefficient IdCor along with a p-value from a permutation test. The two datasets are concatenated row-wise to form a joint dataset for intrinsic dimension estimation, requiring only 3 calls to the TwoNN estimator (one per dataset, one for the concatenation). The p-value is computed by shuffling the pairings S times (typically 100) and comparing the observed joint Id against the null distribution.

## Method

The core insight is that intrinsic dimension (Id) satisfies the key properties of entropy (as shown by Bailey et al., 2022), enabling a Normalized Mutual Information formulation where Id replaces entropy: IdCor(X,Y) = (Id(X) + Id(Y) − Id(X⊕Y)) / max(Id(X), Id(Y)). If two representations are correlated, the joint Id of their concatenation is reduced because information in one representation describes aspects of the other, yielding a positive correlation coefficient. The method uses TwoNN for Id estimation (fast, robust to non-uniform point density) and a permutation test for statistical significance. Unlike CKA or dCor, IdCor is largely insensitive to the specific form of nonlinear transformations between representations, making it effective at detecting statistical dependencies in high-dimensional latent spaces where standard methods return near-zero values.

## Results

On the spiral synthetic dataset (strongly nonlinear correlation), IdCor achieves a correlation coefficient of 0.98 (p-value 0.01), while linear R² yields 0.01 and Distance Correlation yields 0.02. On ImageNet representations across 9 pre-trained models, IdCor achieves a mean off-diagonal correlation of 0.87, substantially exceeding dCor (0.50), SVCCA (0.46), and CKA (0.43–0.44). On the multimodal N24News dataset, IdCor detects a mean cross-modal correlation of 0.66, whereas all baselines (dCor, CKA, SVCCA) only capture same-modality correlations with off-diagonal values of 0.25–0.29, revealing a clear block structure that misses the text–image relationship entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IdCor consistently outperforms all four baselines in detecting nonlinear correlation. Against Distance Correlation (Székely et al., 2007), IdCor achieves 0.98 vs. 0.02 on the spiral synthetic dataset, 0.87 vs. 0.50 on ImageNet, and 0.66 vs. 0.25–0.29 on N24News multimodal data. Against SVCCA (Raghu et al., 2017), IdCor leads by a wide margin: 0.62 vs. 0.31 on average under coarse ImageNet alignment, and 0.66 vs. ~0.25 on N24News. Against CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019), both linear and RBF kernels are outperformed (0.62 vs. 0.42/0.42 on coarse alignment; 0.87 vs. 0.43/0.44 on aligned ImageNet). Against CCA (Hotelling, 1936), IdCor achieves 0.67 vs. 0.02 in the fully nonlinear synthetic scenario. The sole exception is the coarse-alignment setting for CLIP and BLIP (contrastive vision-language models), where dCor and CKA marginally exceed IdCor (0.62/0.59 vs. 0.53 for CLIP), which the authors attribute to the influence of the contrastive pre-training objective on representation geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: information-theoretic correlation (specifically Horibe's Normalized Mutual Information formulation) and intrinsic dimension estimation (via TwoNN). The key conceptual move is substituting intrinsic dimension for entropy in the NMI formula, justified by recent results (Bailey et al., 2022; Ghosh & Motani, 2023) showing that Id satisfies the axiomatic properties of entropy. In the representation-similarity literature, the paper positions itself as a complement to existing metrics (CKA, SVCCA, dCor, CCA) that it demonstrates are insufficient for detecting strongly nonlinear, cross-modal dependencies in high-dimensional latent spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard nonlinear correlation methods (including dCor) essentially fail to detect cross-modal similarity in multimodal representations—while IdCor recovers strong signal—has direct implications for anyone studying latent space compatibility, model transfer, or multimodal alignment without relying on downstream task performance. Limitations include dependence on Id estimator accuracy (degrading at high intrinsic dimensions due to the curse of dimensionality) and the current requirement for uniform Id across the dataset. Open questions include extending the method to locally varying Id, using it for active disentanglement via Total Correlation, and understanding why contrastive vision-language models (CLIP, SigLIP, BLIP) show lower correlation under coarse alignment compared to supervised models.
