## Problem

Point-supervised facial expression spotting (P-FES) requires localizing expression instances in untrimmed videos using only a single timestamp per instance. Existing point-supervised temporal action localization methods rely on hard pseudo-labeling (binary 0/1), which cannot distinguish neutral frames from low-intensity expression frames near instance boundaries, leading to ambiguous class-agnostic scores and degraded spotting performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252079/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed facial video (downsampled to 30 fps) and a single timestamp annotation per expression instance during training. Optical flow features are extracted via SpotFormer into snippet-level embeddings. At inference, the model outputs per-frame continuous intensity scores in [0,1], from which expression proposals (onset frame, offset frame, expression type MaE/ME) are generated using multi-threshold segmentation and OIC scoring, followed by class-wise NMS.

## Method

The core contribution is a Gaussian-based instance-adaptive Intensity Modeling (GIM) module that replaces binary pseudo-labeling with soft pseudo-labels modeling each expression instance's intensity as a smooth Gaussian distribution. For each point label, GIM detects the pseudo-apex frame (highest intensity score in a class-specific window), estimates the instance duration by thresholding intensity scores, and constructs a per-instance Gaussian whose mean is the pseudo-apex feature and whose variance is computed from Euclidean distances to other pseudo-expression frame features. The intensity branch is trained as a regression task with MSE loss (rather than BCE), and an Intensity-Aware Contrastive (IAC) loss modulates contrastive learning weights by the absolute intensity difference between frame pairs—pulling same-class frames together less when their intensities differ greatly, and pushing different-class frames apart less when their intensities are similar. Training follows an easy-to-hard three-stage curriculum: hard labels on adjacent frames, small-range soft labels centered at the point label, then full GIM with updated pseudo-apex detection each epoch.

## Results

On SAMM-LV, the proposed GIM framework achieves an overall F1 score of 0.3587 (MaE: 0.4189, ME: 0.2033), outperforming the best point-supervised baseline LAC (0.3223) by 11.3%. On CAS(ME)², it reaches an overall F1 of 0.4000 (MaE: 0.4395, ME: 0.0588), surpassing the best P-FES baseline LAC (0.3598) by 11.2%. Compared to fully-supervised methods, GIM achieves competitive MaE spotting (0.4189 vs. SpotFormer's 0.4447 on SAMM-LV) but lower ME spotting, attributed to the aggressive neutral-noise suppression that can overshadow extremely subtle micro-expressions without frame-level annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the three point-supervised baselines (LAC, HR-Pro, TSP-Net), GIM wins on overall F1 on both datasets: 0.3587 vs. 0.3223 (LAC) on SAMM-LV and 0.4000 vs. 0.3598 (LAC) on CAS(ME)², with margins of 11.3% and 11.2% respectively. On MaE spotting, GIM outperforms all P-FES baselines on both datasets (e.g., 0.4189 vs. 0.3714 for LAC on SAMM-LV; 0.4395 vs. 0.3889 for LAC on CAS(ME)²). On ME spotting, GIM leads on SAMM-LV (0.2033 vs. 0.1983 for LAC) but trails HR-Pro on CAS(ME)² (0.0588 vs. 0.1345), which the authors attribute to the neutral-noise suppression overshadowing extremely subtle micro-expressions. Against fully-supervised methods, GIM's MaE F1 on SAMM-LV (0.4189) is close to SpotFormer (0.4447) and SpoT-GCN (0.4631), while its ME F1 (0.2033) remains well below SpotFormer (0.4281) and SpoT-GCN (0.4035), reflecting the inherent difficulty of spotting subtle MEs with only point-level supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the two-branch framework from point-supervised temporal action localization (P-TAL) to the facial expression spotting domain, but makes a key conceptual departure: it replaces the binary-classification class-agnostic branch with a regression-based expression intensity branch supervised by Gaussian soft pseudo-labels rather than hard 0/1 labels. The feature backbone is SpotFormer (the authors' prior work), and the inference pipeline borrows the OIC proposal score from AutoLoc and the L1 normalization loss from cross-modal consensus networks. The Intensity-Aware Contrastive loss extends supervised contrastive learning by weighting pair interactions according to intensity differences, a modification not present in the original formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a single point label per expression instance suffices to achieve competitive spotting performance (within ~8% of fully-supervised methods on SAMM-LV), this work makes facial expression spotting far more practical for real-world deployment where frame-level annotation is prohibitively expensive. The Gaussian intensity modeling assumption is intuitive but remains unvalidated beyond facial expressions; whether it transfers to other point-supervised spotting tasks (e.g., gesture spotting, event detection) is an open question. A notable limitation is that ME spotting on CAS(ME)² lags behind fully-supervised methods, suggesting the neutral-noise suppression strategy may inadvertently suppress extremely subtle micro-expressions without precise boundary annotations.
