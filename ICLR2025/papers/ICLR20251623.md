## Problem

Global Covariance Pooling (GCP) applies matrix power or logarithm normalization to SPD covariance matrices before feeding them into Euclidean classifiers, yet no satisfactory theoretical explanation exists for why Euclidean classifiers work after matrix power, whose co-domain remains the non-Euclidean SPD manifold. This paper addresses the gap between the theoretical principles of Riemannian geometry and the practical success of matrix functions in GCP.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251623/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB images, extracts final-layer activations from a CNN backbone (ResNet-18/50, AlexNet, or SoT-7), computes a 256×256 covariance matrix from channel-reduced features, applies a matrix function (power θ=0.5 or logarithm), vectorizes, and passes through an FC layer plus softmax to produce a class label. The critical intermediate representation is an SPD matrix, and the paper's analysis focuses on the geometric structure of this intermediate space rather than the raw image or final logits.

## Method

The paper provides a unified Riemannian-geometric interpretation of matrix functions in GCP by showing they implicitly construct SPD Multinomial Logistic Regression (SPD MLR) classifiers rather than tangent classifiers. Theoretically, they systematically derive Riemannian logarithmic maps under seven metric families (yielding three types: matrix-log-based, matrix-power-based, and LCM-based), then prove (Thm. 2) that Pow-EMLR is equivalent to SPD MLR under the Power Euclidean Metric when SPD parameters are optimized via RSGD and Euclidean parameters via SGD. They further show matrix logarithm corresponds to SPD MLR under LEM, attributing the empirical superiority of matrix power over logarithm to the balanced properties of PEM (interpolating between EM and LEM) versus LEM's tendency to over-stretch eigenvalues. Empirically, they validate the theory by demonstrating that Pow-EMLR consistently outperforms the tangent classifier Pow-TMLR across ImageNet-1k and three FGVC datasets, while ScalePow-EMLR (the explicit Riemannian form) matches Pow-EMLR, confirming the Riemannian classifier as the true underlying mechanism.

## Results

The paper's main empirical contribution validates its theoretical claim that matrix power in GCP implicitly constructs a Riemannian classifier (SPD MLR under PEM) rather than a tangent classifier. On Stanford Cars (ResNet-50), Pow-EMLR achieves 80.43% top-1 / 94.15% top-5 accuracy, outperforming the tangent classifier Pow-TMLR (51.14% / 74.29%) by a large margin, while ScalePow-EMLR (the theoretically equivalent Riemannian classifier) matches Pow-EMLR at 80.31% / 94.07%. On ImageNet-1k (ResNet-18), Pow-EMLR reaches 73.00% top-1 / 90.91% top-5, again surpassing Pow-TMLR (71.62% / 89.73%) and matching ScalePow-EMLR (72.43% / 90.44%). The consistent pattern across all four datasets (ImageNet-1k, Aircrafts, Birds, Cars) confirms that the Riemannian classifier, not the tangent classifier, underpins the efficacy of matrix power normalization in GCP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four classifier variants to test whether tangent or Riemannian classifiers explain matrix power in GCP. Pow-EMLR (the standard iSQRT-COV implementation) consistently outperforms Pow-TMLR (the tangent classifier) across all datasets, with the largest gaps on Cars (80.43% vs. 51.14% top-1) and Birds (63.29% vs. 52.97% top-1), refuting the tangent classifier hypothesis. Pow-EMLR matches ScalePow-EMLR (the theoretically 'correct' Riemannian classifier with explicit 1/θ scaling) within 0.1–0.6% across all datasets, validating the Riemannian classifier interpretation. Cho-TMLR (a tangent classifier based on the Log-Cholesky Metric) shows unstable performance: it wins on Aircrafts (78.97% top-1) but is worst on Birds (48.07%) and Cars (51.06%), likely due to over-stretching from the diagonal logarithm. The ablation with different powers (θ = 0.25, 0.5, 0.7) and the AlexNet backbone confirm the same ordering: Pow-EMLR ≈ ScalePow-EMLR > Pow-TMLR, reinforcing that the Riemannian geometry, not the tangent space approximation, is the operative mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical understanding of Global Covariance Pooling (GCP) by building on the SPD Multinomial Logistic Regression framework from Chen et al. (2024a, CVPR) and Chen et al. (2024d, NeurIPS), which generalized Euclidean MLR to Riemannian manifolds. It departs from prior explanations of matrix power normalization in GCP—Li et al. (2017) claimed the induced distance approximates LEM geodesic distance, and Wang et al. (2020b) offered an optimization-based account—by showing both are insufficient and that the true mechanism is an implicit Riemannian classifier under the Power Euclidean Metric. The paper also systematically catalogs Riemannian logarithmic maps under seven deformed metric families, unifying matrix logarithm, matrix power, and Cholesky-based maps as three distinct types of tangent-space projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a long-standing theoretical gap: why Euclidean classifiers (FC + softmax) can be applied directly after matrix power normalization, whose co-domain remains the non-Euclidean SPD manifold. The finding that tangent classifiers are consistently and substantially inferior to Riemannian classifiers (e.g., ~7–20% top-5 gap on FGVC datasets) despite being mathematically 'closer' to the tangent-space formulation provides a principled justification for the empirical success of iSQRT-COV and opens a path toward designing GCP heads that explicitly exploit other SPD metrics (AIM, BWM, LCM). A key limitation is that the Riemannian MLRs under non-PEM metrics involve computationally expensive Riemannian operations, making them impractical for large-scale training; the authors identify simplifying these computations as a primary future direction.
