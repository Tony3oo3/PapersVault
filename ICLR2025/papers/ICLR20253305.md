## Problem

Existing long-context data synthesis methods for LLM continued pretraining face a fundamental trade-off: random document concatenation (Standard) ensures diversity but lacks semantic coherence, while similarity-based methods (KNN, ICLM) enforce semantic correlation but produce redundant, low-diversity contexts. This paper identifies that both semantic correlation and context diversity are necessary for effective long-range dependency learning, and proposes a method to balance them simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253305/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a document corpus (30B tokens extracted from the 300B-token Pile dataset) and a target context length L (32k or 128k tokens). It produces long-context training sequences by concatenating documents that share a representative keyword, sampled without replacement until the sequence reaches length L. The resulting sequences are used for continued pretraining of Pythia (1.4B, 6.9B, 12B) and LLaMA-3-8B models, with the only variation between methods being how documents are rearranged into contexts.

## Method

Quest is a five-step query-centric pipeline: (1) a lightweight doc2query model predicts multiple potential queries per document; (2) RAKE extracts keywords from each query, filtered by score ≥ 3.0 and a stop-word list, with one keyword randomly selected per document to maximize diversity; (3) a keyword-based inverted index groups documents sharing the same representative keyword, simulating an inverse search process; (4) keywords are split into short-index (less frequent) and long-index sets, with the short-index oversampled to balance token distribution across keyword groups; (5) during training, documents from a sampled keyword group are concatenated without replacement up to length L. The key insight is that query-based grouping naturally aggregates semantically relevant yet low-redundancy documents—mimicking how search engines return diverse results for a query—thereby achieving the optimal balance between semantic correlation and context diversity that neither random nor pure-similarity methods can reach.

## Results

On Longbench (32k context), Quest achieves the highest average score across all Pythia model sizes: 22.06 (1.4B), 23.23 (6.9B), and 25.24 (12B), outperforming Standard, KNN, and ICLM. On Longbook QA (128k context), Quest scores 11.30 (1.4B), 17.95 (6.9B), and 18.92 (12B), again leading all baselines. Applied to LLaMA-3-8B, Quest achieves 97% accuracy on Needle-in-a-Haystack (128k, sentence retrieval) and 100% at 1M context length, and scores 32.39 on Longbook QA—surpassing LLaMA-3.1-8B-base (30.11) and approaching GPT-4-Turbo-128k (37.40). Short-text performance is preserved, with Quest-trained Pythia scoring 0.4831 average vs. 0.4830 for the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Quest outperforms all three data-synthesis baselines (Standard, KNN, ICLM) on average across every model size on both Longbench (32k) and Longbook QA (128k). The gap is most pronounced at 128k: Quest's 6.9B model scores 17.95 on Longbook QA versus 14.47 (Standard), 13.38 (KNN), and 14.92 (ICLM), a 3–4.5 point margin. On the SOTA comparison (Table 4), Quest-LLaMA-3-8B-128k (32.39 Longbook QA) surpasses LLaMA-3.1-8B-base (30.11), Yi-9B-200K (30.35), LLaMA-2-7B-80K (27.40), YaRN Mistral (26.30), LongLoRA-13B (24.60), DeepSeek-V2-Lite (21.56), and Qwen2.5-7B (16.57), trailing only GPT-4-Turbo-128k (37.40). Quest also matches or slightly exceeds the base Pythia model on short-text benchmarks (0.4831 vs. 0.4831 average), confirming no degradation in general capability. The paper further shows that Quest-synthesized data outperforms using only natively long documents from the Pile corpus (22.06 vs. 21.11 average on Longbench), and that no KNN variant (TopK, Mid-Ranking, Random, Reverse) can match Quest's performance despite similar document similarity levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

Quest builds on the long-context data synthesis line of work that includes random document concatenation (Standard, Rozière et al. 2023), retrieval-augmented pre-training via KNN (Guu et al. 2020; Levine et al. 2021), and the traveling-salesman-based ICLM method (Shi et al. 2023). Its key conceptual departure is borrowing the inverse-search paradigm from information retrieval: rather than retrieving documents by similarity to a seed document, Quest predicts potential queries per document (via doc2query), extracts keywords from those queries (via RAKE), and groups documents sharing keywords—effectively simulating what a search engine would return for a query. This positions Quest between the two extremes of prior methods: it preserves more semantic coherence than random shuffling while maintaining more diversity than KNN/ICLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in long-context training: filtered long documents from existing corpora are scarce and domain-skewed, while naive synthesis methods sacrifice either coherence or diversity. By demonstrating that a query-centric inverted-index approach scales predictably (sub-1% relative error in the fitted scaling law) and achieves 100% Needle-in-a-Haystack accuracy at 1M tokens as a base model, Quest provides a reliable, reproducible recipe for practitioners extending context windows. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to multimodal or non-English corpora, whether higher-quality query prediction (e.g., LLM-generated queries, which the authors note is computationally prohibitive) would yield further gains, and whether the keyword-based grouping remains effective as context lengths push beyond 1M tokens.
