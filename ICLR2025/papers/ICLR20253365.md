## Problem

In deep heteroscedastic regression, the covariance is sample-dependent and lacks direct supervision, forcing existing methods to either modify the negative log-likelihood or use expensive parameterizations that trade accuracy for computational cost. This paper asks whether self-supervision—specifically, pseudo-labels for the covariance combined with a stable distance metric—can break this trade-off and enable accurate, computationally cheap covariance estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253365/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an observation X (tabular features or an RGB image processed through ViTPose with soft-argmax to yield 2N keypoints) and a target Y, and produces a predicted mean μ_Y(X) and a full covariance matrix Σ_Y(X) via separate neural networks. Crucially, training uses triplets (x, y, Σ̃_Y(x)) rather than pairs, where the pseudo-label Σ̃_Y(x) is computed offline from the full dataset using a k-nearest-neighbor heuristic with Mahalanobis distances (k set to 10× the target dimensionality). For pose estimation, the covariance must be transformed under affine augmentations as RΣRᵀ.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part self-supervised framework. First, the paper derives an upper bound on the 2-Wasserstein distance between multivariate normals with non-commutative covariances: W₂ ≤ ‖μ₁−μ₂‖² + ‖Σ₁^{1/2}−Σ₂^{1/2}‖_F², which eliminates the eigendecomposition required by the exact formulation and yields stable gradients. Second, a neighborhood-based heuristic generates pseudo-labels: for each sample, the k nearest neighbors are found via Mahalanobis distance (using Cov(X) as the metric), a softmax over distances provides probabilistic weights, and the weighted covariance of neighbor targets serves as the pseudo-label. The mean and covariance networks are then trained jointly by minimizing this 2-Wasserstein bound against the pseudo-label, decoupling covariance learning from residual sensitivity. For human pose, a hybrid strategy pre-trains with the 2-Wasserstein bound for 20 epochs before switching to NLL:TIC, combining the stability of the bound with the expressiveness of the TIC parameterization.

## Results

On 12 UCI regression datasets, the proposed 2-Wasserstein bound with pseudo-labels (2-W Bound) matches Faithful heteroscedastic regression on MSE (e.g., 0.16 on Abalone, 0.20 on Appliances, 0.72 on Concrete) while achieving the best Task Agnostic Correlations (TAC) score across all 12 datasets (e.g., 0.24 on Abalone vs. 0.29 for NLL:TIC, 0.12 on Naval vs. 0.25 for NLL:TIC). On NLL, 2-W Bound wins on 8 of 12 datasets against NLL:TIC (e.g., 21.48 vs. 42.52 on Parkinson, 22.72 vs. 30.41 on Appliances). Computationally, 2-W Bound runs in ~4.5–7 ms per step and uses ~3–25 MB, compared to ~45–68 ms and ~11–821 MB for NLL:TIC, a roughly 10× speedup and 30× memory reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla NLL (Nix & Weigend, 1994) and KL-Divergence, the 2-W Bound method dominates on all metrics: MSE is 10–200× lower (e.g., 0.16 vs. 3.74 on Abalone) and NLL is 5–10× lower, confirming that likelihood-based objectives suffer from residual sensitivity. Against Beta-NLL (Seitzer et al., 2022), 2-W Bound achieves lower MSE on 10 of 12 datasets (e.g., 0.16 vs. 0.35 on Abalone, 0.20 vs. 3.69 on Appliances) and lower NLL on all 12. Against Faithful (Stirn et al., 2023), 2-W Bound essentially ties on MSE (differences ≤0.01 across all datasets) but achieves substantially lower NLL on 8 of 12 datasets (e.g., 21.48 vs. 57.04 on Parkinson, 22.72 vs. 65.15 on Appliances), indicating a better-calibrated covariance. Against NLL:TIC (Shukla et al., 2024), 2-W Bound wins on TAC (covariance correlation quality) across all 12 datasets (e.g., 0.24 vs. 0.29 on Abalone, 0.12 vs. 0.25 on Naval) and on NLL for 8 of 12 datasets, while NLL:TIC retains a slight edge on MSE for a few datasets (e.g., 0.21 vs. 0.16 on Abalone is actually 2-W winning; NLL:TIC wins on Air at 0.82 vs. 0.34 — correction: 2-W wins here too). The key trade-off is that NLL:TIC achieves marginally better NLL on 4 datasets (Abalone, Electrical, Gas, Power) but at 10× the compute time and up to 30× the memory, making 2-W Bound the preferred choice when computational budget is constrained.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep heteroscedastic regression line of work that has sought to fix the optimization pathologies of the negative log-likelihood (NLL) when the covariance is unsupervised. Prior approaches either modify the NLL (β-NLL, Faithful, Empirical-Bayes) or improve the covariance parameterization (TIC-TAC), all of which introduce a trade-off between computational cost and accuracy. The key departure here is introducing self-supervision for the covariance: the authors derive a stable upper bound on the 2-Wasserstein distance for non-commutative covariance matrices (avoiding eigendecomposition) and pair it with a simple Mahalanobis-weighted k-NN pseudo-label heuristic, shifting the problem from 'how to fix the NLL' to 'how to directly supervise the covariance without ground-truth labels.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a computationally cheaper and more stable training recipe for deep heteroscedastic regression that matches or beats state-of-the-art methods (notably TIC-TAC) on both mean and covariance metrics across synthetic and UCI benchmarks. The 2-Wasserstein bound is a clean theoretical contribution that removes a known source of gradient instability (eigendecomposition) in optimal-transport-based losses. Open questions include whether the neighborhood pseudo-label heuristic remains effective in very high-dimensional or highly structured input spaces (the human pose experiments showed it underperformed TIC-TAC there), and whether the hybrid 2-Wasserstein-then-NLL training strategy generalizes beyond the specific ViTPose setup tested.
