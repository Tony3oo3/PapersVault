## Problem

Latent diffusion models for TTS have traditionally relied on domain-specific factors like phoneme sequences and phoneme-level durations to achieve text-speech alignment, complicating data preparation and hindering large-scale training. While recent works (Simple-TTS, E3 TTS) attempted to remove these factors, their performance remained suboptimal, leaving open whether LDM-based TTS can truly match state-of-the-art quality at scale without such domain-specific components.

## Input / Output

The model takes a raw text transcript and a 3-second speech prompt as input, producing a synthesized speech waveform at 22,050 Hz. A separate speech length predictor (encoder-decoder transformer) first determines the total number of latent frames to generate, which sets the diffusion sequence length; the diffusion backbone then generates Mel-VAE latents at 10.76 Hz (7–8× more compressed than EnCodec), which are decoded via the Mel-VAE decoder and a BigVGAN vocoder. The system supports both continuation (extending a 3-second prefix) and cross-sentence (reading new text in the prompt speaker's voice) tasks across nine languages.

## Method

DiTTo-TTS replaces the conventional U-Net backbone with a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) augmented by a long skip connection between the input and output of the final transformer block and a single global AdaLN layer shared across all layers (reducing parameters by ~30% while improving performance). Text conditioning is handled entirely through cross-attention with frozen text encoder embeddings (SpeechT5 or ByT5), eliminating phonemization and duration prediction; a random span-masking strategy (following Voicebox) enables zero-shot voice prompting by training the model to denoise masked segments given their unmasked context. A dedicated speech length predictor—an encoder-decoder transformer with causal masking—predicts the total number of latent frames to generate, replacing fixed-length padding and enabling variable-length synthesis and speech-rate control. The key empirical finding is that semantic alignment between text and speech representations is critical: fine-tuning the Mel-VAE codec with an auxiliary language-modeling loss (Mel-VAE++) or using a jointly-trained text encoder (SpeechT5) substantially improves WER and CER, with the codec-only fine-tuning being the most efficient path. The model uses v-prediction with a cosine noise schedule (scale-shift 0.3), classifier-free guidance at scale 5.0, and 25 diffusion steps at inference, achieving 4.6× faster inference than CLaM-TTS with 3.84× fewer parameters.

## Results

On the English-only continuation task (LibriSpeech test-clean), DiTTo-en-XL (740M params) achieves WER 1.78, CER 0.48, SIM-o 0.5773, and SIM-r 0.6075 with 25 diffusion steps in 1.616 s, outperforming all baselines on WER and CER. The base-sized DiTTo-en-B (152M params) already surpasses the state-of-the-art autoregressive CLaM-TTS (584M) on every metric (WER 1.87 vs 2.36, CER 0.52 vs 0.79, SIM-r 0.5855 vs 0.5128) while running 4.6× faster (0.903 s vs 4.15 s). With extended training and DDIM sampling (DiTTo-en-XL†), speaker similarity reaches SIM-o 0.6051 and SIM-r 0.6283, exceeding Voicebox's reported 0.593/0.616. Subjective evaluation (Table 3) confirms DiTTo-en-XL scores SMOS 3.91±0.16 and CMOS 0.00, approaching ground truth (SMOS 4.08, CMOS +0.13) and clearly surpassing Simple-TTS (SMOS 2.15, CMOS −1.64) and CLaM-en (SMOS 3.42, CMOS −0.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTTo-en-XL achieves the best WER (1.78) and CER (0.48) among all compared models, beating VALL-E (WER 3.8), CLaM-TTS (WER 2.36), Simple-TTS (WER 3.86), and YourTTS (WER 7.57) by wide margins. Against Voicebox, DiTTo-en-XL wins on WER (1.78 vs 2.0) and inference speed (1.616 s vs ~6.4 s at 64 NFE), while Voicebox retains a slight edge on SIM-o (0.593 vs 0.5773) and SIM-r (0.616 vs 0.6075) without extended training; with DDIM (†), DiTTo reverses this gap (SIM-o 0.6051, SIM-r 0.6283). The base-sized DiTTo-en-B (152M) outperforms CLaM-TTS (584M) on every objective metric and runs 4.6× faster, and surpasses VALL-E (302M) on WER, SIM-o, SIM-r, and speed. DiTTo also dominates Simple-TTS on all metrics (e.g., CER 0.48 vs 2.24, SIM-r 0.6075 vs 0.4668) and is ~11× faster at inference. On the cross-sentence task (Table 2), DiTTo-en-XL achieves WER 2.56 and SIM-r 0.6554, outperforming CLaM-TTS (WER 5.11, SIM-r 0.5382) and Simple-TTS (WER 4.09, SIM-r 0.5305), while remaining competitive with Voicebox (WER 1.9, SIM-r 0.681). For baselines available only as demo samples (NaturalSpeech 2/3, Mega-TTS, E3 TTS, StyleTTS 2, XTTS-v2), subjective evaluation shows DiTTo-en-XL surpasses all except NaturalSpeech 3 in SMOS and all except Voicebox in CMOS, confirming competitive zero-shot quality without phoneme or duration conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'no domain-specific factors' TTS direction pioneered by Simple-TTS and E3 TTS, which showed that phonemes and durations could be eliminated but at the cost of suboptimal performance. It builds on DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) as the backbone, adopts span masking from Voicebox, uses Mel-VAE from CLaM-TTS as the latent codec, and incorporates shared AdaLN from Pixart-alpha and pooled-text conditioning from GenTron. The key departure is a systematic empirical study identifying three specific ingredients—DiT over U-Net, variable-length modeling via a speech length predictor, and semantically aligned text-speech embeddings—that collectively close the performance gap to state-of-the-art.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 152M-parameter diffusion model can match or exceed a 584M-parameter autoregressive model in naturalness, intelligibility, and speaker similarity while being 4.6× faster, this work provides a practical, scalable recipe for TTS that removes the data-preparation bottleneck of phonemization and duration prediction. This opens the door to applying the full breadth of LDM research (from image and video generation) directly to speech synthesis. Open questions include whether cross-domain knowledge from other pre-trained DiT models can be transferred, whether the approach extends to instruction-following TTS, and whether the separate speech length predictor can be unified into the diffusion backbone.
