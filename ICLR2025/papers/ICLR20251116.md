## Problem

Heat-diffusion-based continuous-depth GNNs (e.g., GRAND, GRAND++) inevitably drive node representations toward uniformity, causing over-smoothing at equilibrium. Existing curvature-based graph learning methods treat edge curvature as a static, topology-only property requiring quadratic-time precomputation and a single fixed curvature definition. No prior work leverages time-varying, attribute-dependent edge curvature to evolve node features in a way that simultaneously avoids over-smoothing and over-squashing with data-independent convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a node-attributed graph G=(V,E) with initial feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×n}; the initial representation H(0)=F(X) is projected onto the unit sphere (|h_i(t)|≡1) and edge weights are defined as w_ij(t)=cos(h_i(t),h_j(t))+1+ε ∈ [ε, 2+ε]. The ODE is integrated via the Adams-Moulton fixed-step solver (torchdiffeq) over a finite horizon [0,T], and the final H(T) is fed to a linear classifier for node labels. Computational complexity is O(l|V|n² + l|E|n), linear in graph size and matching l-layer GCN.

## Method

GNRF generalizes discrete Ricci flow to attributed graphs (Attri-DRF), where edge weights are functions of evolving node representations, making curvature time-varying and attribute-dependent. By expanding the chain rule and solving a constrained quadratic program (minimize ‖∂_t h_i‖ subject to the Attri-DRF constraint and unit-norm constraint), the paper derives a closed-form ODE: ∂_t h_i = Σ_{j~i} −κ'_ij(t)[h_j − cos(h_j,h_i)h_i], where the negative curvature acts as a repulsive/attractive weight and the cosine term serves as a damping factor that suppresses updates when nodes are already similar or dissimilar. An auxiliary EdgeNet (a permutation-invariant message-passing module over edge neighborhoods) approximates any curvature definition—Forman-Ricci, Ollivier-Ricci, or approximate resistance curvature—in linear time, enabling adaptive curvature without committing to a single definition. Theoretically, the paper proves that Attri-DRF guarantees bilateral Dirichlet energy bounds (preventing both over-smoothing and over-squashing) and a data-independent curvature decay rate of O(ln δ^{-1}), meaning all edges reach near-zero curvature within a small, uniform number of time steps regardless of initial conditions.

## Results

GNRF achieves the best or near-best performance across 12 node classification benchmarks spanning both homophilic and heterophilic graphs. On heterophilic datasets, GNRF shows the largest gains: 86.25% on Roman-Empire (vs. 71.23% GCN, 60.12% GRAND), 95.03% ROC-AUC on Minesweeper (vs. 74.79% GCN), and 73.86% on Questions (vs. 50.21% GCN). On homophilic citation graphs, GNRF also leads: 90.37% on Pubmed, 85.73% on DBLP, and 72.12% on Cora_Full. On OGBN-Arxiv (scalability test), GNRF achieves 62.11% accuracy with 5.5k parameters at hidden size 16, outperforming GCN (60.95%) and ACMP (61.03%) while training 10x faster than ACMP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classic discrete-depth GNNs (GCN, GAT), GNRF achieves substantial improvements on heterophilic graphs: +15.0% on Roman-Empire, +23.7% on Questions, and +20.2% on Minesweeper, while also leading on homophilic citation graphs (e.g., +3.6% on Pubmed over GCN). Among continuous-depth baselines, GNRF outperforms GRAND and GRAND++ on 11 of 12 datasets, with the largest gaps on heterophilic data (e.g., +26.1% over GRAND on Roman-Empire). ACMP (Allen-Cahn) is the strongest continuous-depth competitor but suffers OOM on Roman-Empire, Tolokers, and Cora_Full due to its adaptive-step solver; where it does run, GNRF still leads (e.g., +1.9% on Texas, +9.9% on Minesweeper). HiD-Net is consistently outperformed by GNRF across all 12 datasets. Among SOTA discrete methods, FSGNN is the closest competitor (beating GNRF by 0.26% on Cora_ML and 0.15% on Texas), but GNRF wins on 10 of 12 datasets overall. The EdgeNet variants (GNRF_FRC, GNRF_ARC) confirm that adaptive curvature via EdgeNet provides a consistent 3–10% boost over fixed curvature definitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant heat-diffusion paradigm in continuous-depth GNNs (GRAND, GRAND++, HiD-Net) by replacing the heat equation with the discrete Ricci flow, arguing that heat diffusion inevitably drives node features to uniformity while Ricci flow drives curvature to zero without destroying representational diversity. It also breaks from prior curvature-based graph learning (graph rewiring, edge sampling, neighbor reweighting) that treated edge curvature as a static, topology-only property precomputed in quadratic time; here, curvature becomes a time-varying, attribute-dependent quantity computed on-the-fly in linear time via an auxiliary EdgeNet. The theoretical foundation draws on Ollivier's discrete Ricci flow for unweighted graphs, which the authors generalize to attributed graphs (Attri-DRF), and the DE-GNN continuous-depth framework established by Chamberlain et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-independent curvature decay rate (O(ln δ⁻¹)) and the bilateral Dirichlet energy bounds provide a principled, curvature-driven alternative to ad-hoc over-smoothing mitigations, and the adaptive EdgeNet sidesteps the open question of which curvature definition (Forman-Ricci, Ollivier-Ricci, resistance) is universally best. However, the method still suffers OOM at hidden dimension 256 on OGBN-Arxiv, and the authors acknowledge that excessively long evolution time can blur class boundaries, requiring careful hyperparameter tuning. Open questions include whether the bi-Lipschitz continuity assumption on curvature-as-a-function-of-weights holds for all practical curvature definitions, and whether the framework extends naturally to dynamic or multi-relational graphs.
