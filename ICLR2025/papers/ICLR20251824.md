## Problem

Prompt-based continual learning methods on frozen pretrained backbones still suffer from poor initial performance, limited generalizability, and severe catastrophic forgetting in General Continual Learning (GCL) settings with blurry task boundaries and one-pass online data streams. The tunable prompt parameters and output layers remain vulnerable: prompts trained on the fly converge poorly, and the output layers overwrite old class knowledge due to naturally imbalanced and overlapping class distributions across sessions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251824/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images in a one-pass online stream under the Si-Blurry protocol (50% disjoint classes, 10% blurry sample ratio, 5 sessions), where classes overlap between sessions and the model sees each sample exactly once. It outputs a class prediction vector over all N classes (100 or 200 depending on dataset), with a non-parametric logit mask zeroing out entries for classes absent from the current mini-batch. No replay buffer is required, and no task or session identifiers are needed at test time (anytime inference).

## Method

MISA introduces two plug-in components for prompt-based GCL. First, Forgetting-Aware Initial Session Adaption (ISA) warms up prompt parameters on the backbone's pretraining data (ImageNet-1k) before any GCL session begins, using a modified Sharpness-Aware Minimization (FAM) that replaces SAM's in-distribution gradient perturbation with gradients computed on a held-out out-of-distribution subset, thereby simulating future distribution shifts and finding flat minima robust to forgetting. A shallow MLP-based prompt augmentation is applied during ISA to expand the learnable parameter space so that flatness-aware optimization can operate effectively. Second, a non-parametric logit mask (batch-level or session-level) zeros out logits for classes not present in the current data, preventing the cross-entropy loss from overwriting old class knowledge in the output layer. Both components are replay-independent, hyperparameter-free, and add no extra parameters or compute at inference time.

## Results

On the replay-independent (buffer=0) setting, MISA achieves A_AUC of 80.55% on CIFAR-100, substantially outperforming the previous state-of-the-art MVP (68.10%) by 12.45 points. In A_Last, MISA leads MVP by 18.39, 22.06, and 11.96 percentage points on CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet-R, respectively. The method also reduces average forgetting (F_Last) from 35.12% to 10.35% on CIFAR-100 and from 43.27% to 19.91% on ImageNet-R compared to the DualPrompt baseline, while maintaining the same parameter count (637k) and per-batch inference time (1.32s vs. 1.31s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MISA decisively outperforms the previous state-of-the-art GCL method MVP (Moon et al., ICCV 2023) by 12.45–12.45 points in A_AUC and 11.96–22.06 points in A_Last across CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet-R under the replay-independent setting, with the largest gap on Tiny-ImageNet. Against conventional prompt-based CL methods (DualPrompt, L2P), MISA achieves substantially higher A_AUC (80.55 vs. 67.07 for DualPrompt on CIFAR-100) and dramatically lower forgetting (F_Last 10.35% vs. 35.12%), confirming that the non-parametric logit mask and forgetting-aware ISA address weaknesses that conventional prompt methods cannot handle in blurry-boundary GCL. Replay-based methods (ER, DER++, ER-ACE, RM, CLIB) and regularization-based methods (LwF, EWC) all trail MISA by wide margins, particularly without a buffer, where their reliance on stored samples or parameter regularization becomes ineffective. The plug-in nature is validated by showing that adding MISA's components to L2P improves A_AUC from 29.42 to 34.11 and A_Last from 20.46 to 27.86 on ImageNet-R, and to MVP improves A_AUC from 40.60 to 43.01 and A_Last from 31.96 to 33.52. MISA also maintains its advantage when the pretrained checkpoint has no class overlap with the downstream data (Table 6), outperforming all baselines by 6–12 points in A_AUC, and extends to a fully out-of-distribution medical imaging dataset (NCH) with 71.2% A_AUC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends prompt-based continual learning methods (DualPrompt, L2P) to the more challenging General Continual Learning (GCL) setting with blurry task boundaries, building on the Si-Blurry formulation from Moon et al. (2023). It departs from prior GCL work (MVP) in two key ways: (1) introducing a forgetting-aware initial session adaption that repurposes pretraining data to warm up prompt parameters using a modified SAM (FAM) that uses out-of-distribution gradients as perturbations rather than in-distribution ones, and (2) replacing learnable logit masks with a simple non-parametric, class-appearance-based mask. The approach is explicitly designed as a plug-in for existing prompt-based CL methods rather than a standalone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The replay-independent, hyperparameter-free, and plug-in nature of MISA makes it practically attractive for real-world deployment where storing old data is infeasible or undesirable (privacy, memory constraints). The finding that a trivially simple non-parametric logit mask outperforms learnable masks challenges the assumption that more complex learned components are necessary for output-layer stability in GCL. Open questions include whether the approach extends to extreme online settings (batch size 1, where the batch-level mask fails), whether it generalizes beyond ViT backbones, and whether the ISA requirement of accessing pretraining data limits applicability to proprietary models. The paper also notes that a domain adaptation module (shift-and-scale) could further bridge gaps when ISA data differs from downstream data.
