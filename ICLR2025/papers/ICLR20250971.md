## Problem

LLMs exhibit strong zero-shot generalization but struggle to learn effective policies in adversarial, multi-agent environments with partial information, where they must reason about opponents, plan strategically, and navigate vast policy spaces. Traditional RL approaches require extensive training data and parameter optimization, while simply querying an LLM per-move is computationally prohibitive and fails to capture long-horizon strategic structure. The paper asks how LLMs can autonomously learn and iteratively improve complex game policies without any human-generated training data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes partial-observation game states (information sets), game rules, and self-play trajectory feedback (visited states, actions, MCTS win-rate estimates, final outcomes, and intermediate heuristic values) as natural-language prompts to an LLM. It produces a high-level strategy as executable Python code implementing a value heuristic v: S → R^|N| that estimates expected end-game scores for all players simultaneously, a low-level policy as an action distribution derived via MCTS, and (for Avalon) a chain-of-thought dialogue guide structured as a Q&A worksheet that conditions the LLM's speech generation on an intended action.

## Method

STRATEGIST is a bi-level, non-parametric framework where an LLM iteratively improves high-level strategy abstractions (value heuristics in Python) while a low-level MCTS executor refines them into executable policies. At the high level, a strategy tree and a priority-queue of 'improvement ideas' are maintained; each cycle alternates between idea generation (the LLM proposes concrete modifications based on key states where the value heuristic most disagrees with MCTS estimates) and strategy implementation (the LLM applies a UCB-sampled idea to a UCB-sampled strategy, producing a new variant). Feedback comes from population-based self-play: the top ten strategies are pitted in round-robin tournaments, and win rates plus trajectory discrepancies are backpropagated to update both strategy scores and idea scores. At the low level, MCTS with a PUCT formula adapted for information sets uses the value heuristic as a prior, and the resulting search estimates provide a shaped reward signal more informative than raw win/lose outcomes. For dialogue-heavy games like Avalon, the strategy takes the form of a chain-of-thought guide (a Q&A worksheet) that the agent completes before generating speech, decoupling strategic reasoning from surface-level language production.

## Results

On GOPS, STRATEGIST achieves a point difference of 1.5±0.99 over opponents, substantially outperforming the best LLM self-improvement baseline (BFS at 0.092±0.67) and both RL baselines (AlphaGo at −0.39±0.22, DeepRole at −0.56±0.18). On Avalon, STRATEGIST reaches a 0.59±0.11 winrate for value heuristics and 0.88±0.063 for Merlin dialogue guides, exceeding all baselines. Against LLM agents, STRATEGIST wins 61.1% of games versus ReCon (38.9%) and 52.5% versus ReAct (47.5%). In human evaluations, STRATEGIST achieves a 0.333 winrate comparable to human players (0.367), while outperforming humans in concealment and strategic randomization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STRATEGIST outperforms all seven baselines across both game environments. Against LLM self-improvement methods, it beats Self-Refine (line search) by ~2.0 points on GOPS (1.5 vs −0.47), Eureka (greedy search) by ~2.0 points, Tree of Thoughts (BFS) by ~1.4 points, and BFS-with-thought (ReAct-style) by ~2.0 points on GOPS, while also leading on all Avalon metrics. Against RL baselines, STRATEGIST surpasses AlphaGo by 0.72 points on GOPS (0.33 vs −0.39) and by 8 percentage points on Avalon winrate (0.38 vs 0.30), and outperforms DeepRole by 1.70 points on GOPS (1.14 vs −0.56) and by 11 percentage points on Avalon (0.44 vs 0.33), all while using only 100 transition steps versus ~3,840 for RL. Against the ReCon LLM agent, STRATEGIST achieves a 61.1% winrate versus ReCon's 38.9%, and against ReAct it wins 52.5% to 47.5%. In human evaluation, STRATEGIST's 0.333 winrate is statistically comparable to humans (0.367), with survey feedback indicating superior concealment but weaker reasoning and cooperation.

## Relation to Prior Work

STRATEGIST sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) MCTS-based planning (MuZero's PUCT formula is directly adapted for partial-observation information sets), (2) LLM self-improvement via iterative refinement (Self-Refine, Reflexion, Eureka, Tree of Thoughts), and (3) RL-based game playing (AlphaGo, DeepRole). The key conceptual departure is the bi-level architecture: an LLM generates and evolves high-level strategies as interpretable text (Python value heuristics or chain-of-thought dialogue guides), while a modular MCTS executor refines these into executable policies. The population-based self-play with an idea queue (sampled via UCB) distinguishes the improvement process from prior LLM self-refinement methods, which typically operate on a single trajectory or greedy/best-first search without a persistent, reusable pool of improvement ideas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a parameter-free LLM+MCTS pipeline can match human performance in Avalon and outperform adapted AlphaGo/DeepRole with orders of magnitude fewer training transitions (100 vs. ~3,840 steps), suggesting that LLM prior knowledge can substitute for large-scale RL training in structured game settings. The finding that STRATEGIST employs more strategic randomization than humans—making its identity harder to deduce—hints at a qualitative behavioral difference in how LLM-driven agents play social deduction games. However, the evaluation is limited to two games, uses only GPT-3.5, and individual runs show high variance; open questions include whether the bi-level framework scales to larger action spaces (e.g., Diplomacy), whether it generalizes beyond zero-sum or team-based games, and whether the idea-queue mechanism provides consistent gains over simpler BFS-with-thought baselines at larger budgets.
