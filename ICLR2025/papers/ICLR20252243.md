## Problem

While self-supervised contrastive learning has achieved strong empirical success on sequential data, the theoretical understanding of how these algorithms perform non-linear system identification remains limited. Standard contrastive learning with symmetric encoders (no explicit dynamics model) provably cannot identify non-trivial latent dynamics, yet it is unclear what architectural modifications are needed and what identifiability guarantees can be provided for general non-linear dynamical systems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a time-series of observations y_t ∈ ℝ^D (optionally with control signals u_t) and produces a shared encoder h: ℝ^D → ℝ^d that maps all samples (reference, positive, negative) into a common latent space, an estimated dynamics model f̂ operating in that latent space, and for switching systems a per-timestep mode assignment k_t. The recovered latents and dynamics are identifiable only up to an unknown affine transform Lx + b, where L = QΣ_ε^{-1/2} for an orthogonal Q, so evaluation metrics (R², LDS error, dyn R²) must account for this indeterminacy via a linear regression alignment step.

## Method

DCL composes a shared encoder h, an explicit dynamics model f̂, a similarity function φ (negative squared Euclidean norm for Gaussian noise), and a KDE correction term α into a single scoring function ψ(y, y') = φ(f̂(h(y)), h(y')) − α(y'), trained with the InfoNCE loss where positives are adjacent timesteps and negatives are uniformly sampled. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that at the global optimum, h∘g is a bijective affine transform and f̂ conjugates the true dynamics f through that transform, establishing structural identifiability of both latents and dynamics. For non-linear dynamics, the paper introduces ∇-SLDS: a parameter-free, differentiable switching model that maintains a bank of K linear matrices W and selects the active mode at each step via a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation of an argmin over prediction residuals, approximating continuous non-linear dynamics through piecewise first-order Taylor expansions around reference points.

## Results

DCL with a suitable dynamics model achieves near-oracle identifiability across all three system classes. For linear dynamics (LDS), DCL reaches 99.03% R² and an LDS error of 0.77×10⁻², closely matching the ground-truth oracle (99.46% R², 0.44×10⁻²). For switching linear dynamics (SLDS), ∇-SLDS attains 99.52% latent R² and 99.93% dynamics R², versus 76.80% and 85.47% for the identity-dynamics baseline. For the non-linear Lorenz attractor, ∇-SLDS achieves 94.08% R² and 93.91% dyn R², while the baseline fails entirely at 40.99% and 27.02%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is CEBRA-time (Schneider et al., 2023), implemented as standard contrastive learning with symmetric encoders and identity dynamics (no explicit dynamics model). DCL with an LDS dynamics model outperforms this baseline by a wide margin on linear systems: 99.03% R² vs. 73.56%, and an LDS error of 0.77×10⁻² vs. 21.24×10⁻² (a ~27× reduction). On switching linear dynamics, ∇-SLDS improves latent R² from 76.80% to 99.52% and dynamics R² from 85.47% to 99.93%. The gap is most dramatic on the non-linear Lorenz attractor, where the baseline achieves only 40.99% R² and 27.02% dyn R², while ∇-SLDS reaches 94.08% and 93.91%. A single linear dynamics model (LDS) is insufficient for the Lorenz system (81.20% R²), confirming that the piecewise-linear ∇-SLDS approximation is necessary for non-linear dynamics. DCL with the correct dynamics model consistently matches or approaches the ground-truth oracle in all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability theory of contrastive learning (Wang & Isola, 2020; Zimmermann et al., 2021) and nonlinear ICA (Hyvarinen & Morioka, 2016, 2017; Khemakhem et al., 2020) from static representation recovery to the identification of latent dynamical systems. It generalizes the CPC/wav2vec paradigm (Oord et al., 2018; Schneider et al., 2019) by making the dynamics model an explicit, interpretable component (LDS or ∇-SLDS) rather than an implicit RNN, and provides the first structural identifiability guarantee for the dynamics function itself. The ∇-SLDS parameterization draws on switching linear dynamical systems literature (Linderman et al., 2017; Smith et al., 2021) but introduces a parameter-free, differentiable switching mechanism via Gumbel-Softmax that integrates directly into the contrastive loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical characterization of when contrastive learning can and cannot identify dynamics, with the key negative result (Corollary 1) showing that symmetric-encoder CL without an explicit dynamics model fundamentally cannot recover non-trivial dynamics—explaining both the success and failure modes of methods like CEBRA-time and CPC. The ∇-SLDS parameterization offers a practical, differentiable tool for fitting switching and piecewise-linear nonlinear dynamics end-to-end. Key limitations include the reliance on synthetic data, the injectivity assumption on the mixing function g, and the Gaussian noise requirement; extending to partially observable systems, non-Gaussian noise, and real-world benchmarks (e.g., neural recordings, DynaDojo) remains open.
