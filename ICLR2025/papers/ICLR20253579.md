## Problem

Existing Federated Class Incremental Learning (FCIL) methods assume clients hold abundant training data, making them impractical in resource-constrained settings where clients possess only a handful of samples per new class. Under this data scarcity, prototypes computed from few local samples suffer from prototype bias (representing observed samples rather than the true class population), and full-backbone training leads to over-fitting and high communication costs. The paper formalizes this gap as a new problem—Federated Few-Shot Class-Incremental Learning (FFSCIL)—where clients must handle catastrophic forgetting, over-fitting, and prototype bias simultaneously under data privacy constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100/MiniImageNet, variable for CUB-200) as input, processed through a frozen ViT backbone augmented with task-specific prefix-tuning prompt key-value pairs. At inference, the system also consumes the unified prototype set (a union of static prototypes from previously learned classes and rectified dynamic prototypes from the current task) to compute similarity-based predictions. The output is a class label, with the classifier choice (MLP vs. prototype-based) determined by a task-ID prediction that matches the input feature against each task's prompt key via a matching loss.

## Method

UOPP builds on a frozen ViT backbone and introduces three coupled mechanisms: (1) task-wise prefix tuning where each task has its own prompt key-value pair that is frozen after its task completes, minimizing inter-task interference and over-fitting; (2) a unified static-dynamic prototype system where static prototypes from prior tasks are shared via the server to handle non-i.i.d. data, while dynamic prototypes for the current few-shot task are iteratively rectified using a trainable Neural ODE (GradNet) that operates entirely in embedding space—drawing support samples from the Gaussian distribution of static prototypes and query samples from the dynamic prototype set, eliminating the need for stored exemplars; (3) an adaptive dual-head classifier that routes predictions to an MLP head (base task) or a prototype-based head (few-shot tasks) based on predicted task ID. Federated aggregation is weighted by client participation, and clients share only prompts, prototypes, and MLP parameters (≈0.3–0.4 M) rather than the full backbone, keeping communication cost low.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (5-shot), UOPP achieves 92.92% average accuracy with only 0.73% performance drop, outperforming the best competitor Fed-DualP (85.91%) by 7.01%. On MiniImageNet (1-shot), UOPP reaches 92.21% average accuracy, and on CUB-200 (1-shot) it achieves 76.73%. In terms of harmonic mean accuracy (stability-plasticity balance) on CIFAR-100 5-shot, UOPP scores 90.5% versus 69.2% for Fed-DualP, while TARGET, LANDER, and PILoRA score 0% due to complete failure on novel classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UOPP outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and shot setting. The closest competitor is Fed-DualP (prompt-based, ECCV 2022), which achieves 85.91% on CIFAR-100 5-shot versus UOPP's 92.92% (7.01% gap), and 86.88% on MiniImageNet 1-shot versus UOPP's 92.21% (5.33% gap). Among FCIL SOTAs, LGA (TPAMI 2023) reaches 58.05% on CIFAR-100 5-shot, TARGET (ICCV 2023) reaches 53.09%, and LANDER (CVPR 2024) reaches 49.11%—all trailing UOPP by 32–41%. The FSCIL SOTA Fed-S3C (ECCV 2022) performs worst at 43.21% on CIFAR-100 5-shot, confirming that standalone FSCIL methods cannot transfer to the federated few-shot setting. Notably, TARGET, LANDER, and PILoRA (ECCV 2024) achieve 0% harmonic mean accuracy on CIFAR-100 5-shot, indicating complete inability to recognize novel classes, while UOPP maintains 90.5% harmonic mean. Prompt-based methods (Fed-L2P, Fed-DualP, Fed-CODAP, Fed-CPrompt) are substantially more competitive than FCIL/FSCIL baselines, with gaps to UOPP of 10–24%, suggesting prompt learning is the right paradigm for FFSCIL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: federated class-incremental learning (FCIL, e.g., LGA, TARGET), few-shot class-incremental learning (FSCIL, e.g., S3C, TEEN), and prompt-based continual learning (L2P, DualPrompt, CODAPrompt). It departs from FCIL by rejecting the assumption of abundant local data, and from FSCIL by rejecting the assumption that all classes are available at a single client. The prototype rectification mechanism is directly inherited from MetaNode's GradNet (Zhang et al., 2022) and the Neural ODE framework (Chen et al., 2018), but is repurposed to operate entirely in embedding space using Gaussian-drawn pseudo-samples rather than real images, making it compatible with strict data-privacy constraints. The prompt structure follows prefix-tuning (Li & Liang, 2021) applied to a frozen ViT backbone, with the novelty of task-wise key-value pairs and a matching loss to enforce inter-task separability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically important gap: real-world federated systems often face both data scarcity (few samples per class) and privacy constraints (no exemplar storage, no synthetic data sharing), a combination that neither FCIL nor FSCIL literature addresses. The unified static-dynamic prototype mechanism is a clean solution to the non-i.i.d. problem in federated settings, allowing clients to learn representations for classes they never observe locally. However, performance degrades substantially at extreme non-i.i.d. levels (η=20% drops to 68.8%), the GradNet introduces 34M trainable parameters in few-shot tasks (though communication remains low), and the method is validated only on ViT backbones with three mid-scale datasets. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger-scale benchmarks (ImageNet-scale), whether the ODE-based rectification can be replaced by lighter alternatives, and how the method behaves under client dropouts or heterogeneous model capacities.
