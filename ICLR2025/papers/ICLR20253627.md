## Problem

Existing antibody co-design methods require known experimental structures of framework regions or templates from datasets, which are unavailable when targeting a novel antigen. This dependency on pre-existing structural information prevents true de novo design in practical scenarios where only the target antigen and a chosen framework sequence are known. IgGM addresses this gap by jointly generating both CDR sequences and the full antibody backbone structure without requiring any pre-existing antibody structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253627/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes as input the antigen's sequence and 3D structure, the antibody framework region sequences (not structures), and optionally epitope residue information to constrain binding location. It outputs the discrete CDR amino acid sequences (any subset: CDR H3, all heavy-chain CDRs, or all six CDRs) together with the continuous 3D backbone coordinates (Cα positions and SO(3) orientations of N, C, O atoms) for the entire antibody. The framework region sequences are held fixed while the model designs CDR sequences and predicts the full antibody conformation, including framework structural changes induced by binding.

## Method

IgGM is a consistency model distilled from a diffusion model that jointly denoises discrete sequences (categorical diffusion over 20 amino acids) and continuous structures (Gaussian diffusion on R³ translations and SO(3) rotations). The architecture comprises a frozen ESM-PPI language model for evolutionary sequence features, an inter-chain feature embedding module and structure encoder for positional/epitope context, 16 Sgformer blocks for multi-level feature fusion, and 8 invariant-point-attention prediction layers that iteratively refine coordinates while outputting sequence logits. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: first the model learns structure-only reconstruction (Frame MSE + inter-chain Frame MSE + geometric + violation losses), then it is extended to sequence design with a mixed sampling ratio (4:2:2:2 for CDR H3, CDR H, all CDRs, and no-design) to support flexible task configurations. The final consistency model is obtained via distillation, enabling single-step or few-step (e.g., 10-step) generation that balances speed and quality, with DockQ used as the selection criterion among multiple samples.

## Results

On the SAb-23H2-Ab test set (60 antibodies), IgGM achieves a CDR H3 amino acid recovery rate (AAR) of 0.360, a DockQ of 0.246, and a docking success rate (SR) of 0.433, outperforming all baselines across nearly all metrics. IgGM is the only method achieving iRMS below 8 (6.579) and LRMS below 20 (19.678). When initialized with AlphaFold 3 structures (IgGM(AF3)), DockQ improves to 0.326 and SR rises to 0.627. On the nanobody test set (SAb-23H2-Nano), IgGM achieves a DockQ of 0.2907 and SR of 0.4400 for structure prediction, and outperforms DiffAb(AF3) in nanobody design with DockQ 0.267 vs 0.211 and SR 0.415 vs 0.346.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IgGM outperforms dyMEAN (the strongest prior end-to-end antibody design method) on every metric: AAR H3 improves from 0.294 to 0.360 (+22.4%), DockQ from 0.079 to 0.246 (3.1×), and SR from 0.049 to 0.433 (8.8×). Against DiffAb, IgGM achieves substantially higher AAR H3 (0.360 vs 0.214–0.226) and DockQ (0.246 vs 0.022–0.208), with the gap most pronounced when DiffAb uses IgFold-initialized structures (DockQ 0.022, SR 0.000). MEAN, limited to CDR H3 design, trails IgGM on AAR H3 (0.248 vs 0.360) and DockQ (0.022–0.207 vs 0.246). In structure prediction (Table 1), IgGM is competitive with specialized predictors: its TM-Score (0.9591) is slightly below AlphaFold 3 (0.9729) and tFold-Ag (0.9634), but it surpasses both on DockQ (0.2986 vs 0.2951 and 0.2522) and SR (0.4667 vs 0.3684 and 0.4068), and dramatically outperforms IgFold+HDock (DockQ 0.0218, SR 0.000). For nanobody design (Table 3), IgGM beats DiffAb(AF3) on DockQ (0.267 vs 0.211), iRMS (6.927 vs 13.265), and SR (0.415 vs 0.346), though CDR sequence recovery rates are lower than for full antibodies due to the longer CDR3 in nanobodies.

## Relation to Prior Work

IgGM builds on the co-design paradigm established by MEAN, dyMEAN, and DiffAb, which jointly generate CDR sequences and structures for antibody-antigen binding. However, it departs from these methods by eliminating the requirement for known framework region structures or templates, instead accepting only framework sequences and the antigen structure as input—a more realistic scenario for novel antigens. The architecture draws on ESM-PPI for multi-chain sequence features, Sgformer-style attention for feature fusion, and invariant point attention for structure refinement, while the consistency model distillation (following Song et al., 2023) enables single-step or few-step generation, a technique previously applied to image generation but not to antibody co-design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IgGM addresses a practical gap in antibody design: the inability of prior co-design methods to operate when framework structures are unavailable, which is the norm for novel antigens. The extension to nanobodies and the ability to correct AlphaFold 3 docking errors broaden its utility in therapeutic antibody development. However, the paper acknowledges a critical limitation—no wet-lab validation is performed, and in silico metrics (DockQ, AAR) remain insufficient to confirm true binding affinity. A key open question is the development of effective discriminators or screening tools to bridge the gap between computational design and experimental validation, a challenge the authors identify as central to the field's progress.
