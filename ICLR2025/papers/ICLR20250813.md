## Problem

Existing jailbreak defenses operate at the prompt level—perturbing user queries or appending safety system prompts—but are inadequate against adaptive jailbreak prompts that can take arbitrary forms. This paper identifies that harmful queries have far less manipulation flexibility than their enclosing jailbreak prompts, and that this asymmetry produces a distinctive, exploitable attention pattern in the LLM's KV caches that no prior defense leverages.

## Input / Output

RobustKV takes a single text prompt (potentially containing a concealed harmful query within a jailbreak prompt), the target LLM, and an eviction rate p (default 20%). It first performs a forward pass to collect key-query attention maps using the last 16 tokens of the prompt as the observation window, then evicts the KVs of the lowest-ranked p fraction of tokens from all layers and all heads simultaneously (unlike standard KV compression which operates per-head). The output is the LLM's generated text response, now conditioned on a reduced KV cache that has diminished the harmful query's presence.

## Method

RobustKV's key insight is that for a jailbreak prompt to bypass the LLM's safety guardrails, its tokens must accumulate high attention scores, which necessarily suppresses the importance of the concealed harmful query's tokens. The method computes overall token importance by summing attention maps across all heads within each layer, selecting the top-k token indices per layer, then taking the top-k most frequent tokens across all layers as the retained set (with a Chernoff-bound-based proposition guaranteeing high recall of truly important tokens). The remaining tokens' KVs are evicted from all caches before decoding proceeds. This creates an evasiveness dilemma: an adversary who increases harmful-query token importance to evade eviction simultaneously weakens the jailbreak prompt's ability to bypass the LLM's built-in safeguards, and vice versa.

## Results

RobustKV dramatically reduces attack success rate (ASR) across all evaluated jailbreak attacks and LLMs. For example, against AutoDAN on Llama2, ASR drops from 61.5% (undefended) to 6.3% with RobustKV, compared to 40.0% (SmoothLLM) and 30.8% (GoalPriority). Against GCG on Vicuna, ASR falls from 89.4% to 16.4%, outperforming SmoothLLM (23.7%) and GoalPriority (34.7%). On utility, RobustKV retains 63% WinRate on AlpacaEval and 0.415 Rouge-L, closely matching the undefended Llama2 (68%, 0.453) while substantially outperforming SmoothLLM (62%, 0.306) and GoalPriority (59%, 0.281).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustKV outperforms both prompt-level defenses (SmoothLLM and GoalPriority) on the majority of attack–LLM combinations, with particularly large margins on AutoDAN (e.g., 6.3% vs. 40.0% and 30.8% on Llama2) and GCG on Vicuna (16.4% vs. 23.7% and 34.7%). The one notable exception is AdvPrompter on Mistral, where GoalPriority achieves a lower ASR (21.8%) than RobustKV (30.3%), likely because Mistral's weaker safety guardrails reduce the importance gap between jailbreak prompt and harmful query tokens. Applying SnapKV directly as a defense provides negligible protection (e.g., 58.3% ASR for AutoDAN on Llama2 vs. 61.5% undefended), confirming that standard KV eviction for efficiency does not translate to security. On utility, RobustKV preserves LLM performance far better than both baselines: 63% WinRate and 0.415 Rouge-L on AlpacaEval versus 62%/0.306 (SmoothLLM) and 59%/0.281 (GoalPriority), with only a ~5-point WinRate drop from the undefended model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the KV cache optimization literature (SnapKV, H2O, ScissorHands, FastGen) — originally developed for inference efficiency — as a security mechanism, which is a conceptual departure from both the KV compression line and the prompt-level jailbreak defense line (SmoothLLM, GoalPriority, SelfReminder). The key insight connecting these threads is that effective jailbreak prompts must concentrate attention importance on their own tokens, necessarily suppressing the importance of the concealed harmful query, making the lowest-ranked tokens a reliable proxy for harmful content. Unlike prior defenses that attempt to neutralize or refuse the jailbreak prompt, RobustKV directly diminishes the harmful query's presence in the model's internal state, representing the first use of KV eviction as a jailbreak defense.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By operating at the KV cache level rather than the prompt level, RobustKV avoids the computational overheads (repeated generation, external evaluators, prompt length inflation) that plague existing defenses, making it more deployable in practice. The evasiveness dilemma it creates — adversaries must trade off between bypassing the LLM's safeguards and evading the defense — provides a structural robustness property against adaptive attacks that prompt-level defenses lack. Key open questions include extending the approach to in-context-learning-based attacks (e.g., many-shot jailbreaking) where the harmful query is distributed across examples, and developing more flexible eviction policies (e.g., learned rather than deterministic) that could handle edge cases where harmful query tokens are not consistently low-ranked.
