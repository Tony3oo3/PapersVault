## Problem

Existing continuous-time sequence models (ODE-RNN, GRU-ODE, Neural CDE) model hidden state evolution continuously over time but discretely over depth, requiring exhaustive model selection over network depth and limiting adaptability to data of varying complexity. The prior continuous-depth approach (CDR-NDE) uses a non-homogeneous heat equation, whose diffusive nature causes initial information to be smoothed out and lost. This paper asks whether a wave equation, which preserves initial information through propagation rather than diffusion, provides a more effective PDE for modeling sequence data.

## Input / Output

The model takes an entire irregularly sampled sequence of D-dimensional feature vectors [x₁, x₂, …, x_K] with corresponding observation timestamps [t₁, t₂, …, t_K] as a single batch input (not processed sequentially). The timestamps serve as spatial coordinates in the wave equation, and the input features are projected through a shallow MLP to form the initial displacement condition h(t,0). Output is a per-timestep prediction vector (class logits for classification or real values for regression) obtained by passing the final-depth hidden states through a post-processing MLP.

## Method

The neural wave equation models hidden state evolution as a non-homogeneous 1D wave equation ∂²h/∂d² − c²∂²h/∂t² = F_θs(·), where the sequence time dimension maps to the spatial variable and architectural depth maps to the wave's temporal variable, with a learnable wave speed c. The source function F_θs is parameterized by neural networks (GRU cells and/or MLPs) that take neighboring hidden states as input, adding non-linearity and cross-dimension mixing. The PDE is solved via the method of lines using an adaptive step-size ODE solver (Dopri5/Tsit5), after converting the 2nd-order equation to a system of 1st-order ODEs, and gradients are computed via the adjoint sensitivity method. D'Alembert's analytical solution reveals that each hidden state at depth d depends on source terms from all previous depths within a window determined by c, yielding denser inter-layer connections than both RNNs (which only connect to the immediately preceding time step and depth) and the heat equation (whose exponential decay term diminishes contributions from lower depths).

## Results

On Person Activity Recognition, Neural Wave-Double Gating achieves 93.62% ± 0.38 test accuracy, outperforming the best baseline CDR-NDE-heat(Dopri5) (88.60%) by over 5 points. On Walker2d-v2 kinematic simulation, Neural Wave-Single MLP achieves 0.11 ± 0.01 test MSE, a 4.5× improvement over CDR-NDE-heat(Dopri5) (0.49) and an 8× improvement over ODE-LSTM (0.91). On PhysioNet 2019 sepsis prediction, Neural Wave-Double Gating reaches 0.890 ± 0.006 AUC, surpassing Neural CDE (0.880) and CDR-NDE-heat (0.880). On stance classification (RumourEval 2019), Neural Wave models are competitive but do not clearly dominate, with GRU-D and CT-GRU remaining strong baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neural Wave models decisively outperform all continuous-time discrete-depth baselines (ODE-RNN, GRU-ODE, ODE-LSTM, CT-GRU, CT-LSTM) across Person Activity and Walker2d, with the largest margin on Walker2d where Single MLP achieves 0.11 MSE versus 0.91 for ODE-LSTM. Against the closest competitor CDR-NDE-heat (the only other continuous-depth PDE-based model), Neural Wave-Double Gating wins on Person Activity by 5.0 points (93.62% vs. 88.60%) and Neural Wave-Single MLP wins on Walker2d by 4.5× in MSE (0.11 vs. 0.49). On PhysioNet sepsis prediction, Neural Wave-Double Gating (0.890 AUC) edges out Neural CDE (0.880) and CDR-NDE-heat (0.880) by a narrow but consistent margin. On stance classification, the advantage is less clear: GRU-D (0.65/0.65 AUC) and CT-GRU (0.63/0.65) remain competitive, and Neural Wave-MLP+GRU (0.60/0.63) is in the same range, suggesting the wave equation's denser connectivity benefits are most pronounced on lower-dimensional sensor/kinematic data rather than high-dimensional text embeddings. The paper also notes that Neural Wave is an order of magnitude faster per epoch than Neural CDE (18–30 s vs. 300 s on Person Activity), though it consumes more GPU memory (1972 MB vs. 244 MB for Neural CDE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) for continuous-depth modeling, ODE-RNN (Rubanova et al., 2019) for continuous-time hidden state evolution on irregularly sampled sequences, and CDR-NDE (Anumasa et al., 2023) which unified both via a non-homogeneous heat equation PDE. The key departure is replacing the heat equation with a non-homogeneous wave equation, motivated by the observation that the heat equation's diffusive nature causes exponential decay of lower-depth information (visible in its separation-of-variables solution), whereas the wave equation's propagative nature and d'Alembert's analytical solution preserve and densify connections across hidden states. The source function is parameterized with GRU-cells and MLPs to introduce nonlinearity and cross-dimensional mixing, and the PDE is solved via adaptive-step ODE solvers (Dopri5/Tsit5) with adjoint sensitivity for gradient computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled PDE-based framework for sequence modeling that simultaneously handles irregular sampling and varying data complexity without manual depth selection, which is a practical pain point for ODE-RNN and its variants. The d'Alembert solution analysis offers a clear theoretical explanation for why wave equations outperform heat equations in this setting, giving the community a design principle for future PDE-based architectures. Key limitations include substantially higher memory consumption (1807–2137 MB vs. 244 MB for Neural CDE) and the fact that the wave speed parameter c's sensitivity is only partially characterized. Open questions include whether the PDE-based continuous-depth approach generalizes beyond 1D wave equations, whether the memory overhead can be reduced without sacrificing the speed advantage, and whether the theoretical advantages of the wave equation's analytical solution translate to other PDE families (e.g., Schrödinger-type equations).
