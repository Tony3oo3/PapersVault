## Problem

Generative zero-shot learning methods assume abundant seen-class samples, yet performance degrades sharply when per-class training data is scarce. The paper identifies and quantifies that this degradation stems from spurious visual-semantic correlations that are amplified as training samples decrease, causing discriminators to memorize limited data and fail to generalize to held-out seen-class features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252178/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely in feature space: RGB images are projected to 2048-D visual features via a frozen ResNet-101 (CE-pretrained) and to contrastive representations via a frozen PaCo-pretrained ResNet-101. The diffusion-based feature generator G takes as input a concatenation of class attribute vectors (85-D for AWA2, 1024-D for CUB/SUN), a latent variable, diffusion time step, a noised feature vector, and the SC-based representation, producing synthetic 2048-D visual features for unseen classes. At test time, a separate representation generator R synthesizes SC representations from class semantics alone, since no real unseen-class representations are available.

## Method

ZeroDiff extends f-VAEGAN with three mechanisms to solidify visual-semantic correlation under data scarcity. First, a diffusion forward chain (T=4 steps, VP-SDE schedule) augments each limited training sample into infinite noised variants, and the generator G learns to denoise/generate clean features conditioned on the noised feature, diffusion time, semantics, latent code, and SC representation. Second, supervised contrastive (SC) representations provide instance-level semantics that capture intra-class variation (e.g., distinguishing white fox from red fox), addressing the mismatch between static class-level attributes and individual samples. Third, three Wasserstein-distance discriminators—D_adv (predefined semantics), D_diff (diffusion process), and D_rep (contrastive representations)—evaluate generated features from complementary perspectives, and a mutual learning loss L_mu with noise-to-data-ratio reweighting (κ_t^γ) distills knowledge bidirectionally between them, preventing D_adv from overfitting while strengthening D_diff's denoising discrimination.

## Results

On the standard ZSL protocol, ZeroDiff achieves top-1 accuracy of 87.3% on AWA2, 87.5% on CUB, and 77.3% on SUN, setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks. For Generalized ZSL (GZSL), ZeroDiff attains harmonic mean H of 81.4% (AWA2) and 81.6% (CUB), outperforming all prior generative and embedding methods, though it trails DFCAFlow (61.2%) on SUN GZSL H (59.8%). Under limited training data (10% of seen-class samples), ZeroDiff retains 83.3% T1 on AWA2 and 82.9% on CUB, while f-VAEGAN drops to 73.1% and f-CLSWGAN collapses to 54.0% on AWA2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroDiff outperforms all 16 compared baselines on ZSL top-1 accuracy across AWA2, CUB, and SUN, with the largest margin over VADS (the strongest prior generative method): +4.8% on AWA2 (87.3 vs 82.5), +0.7% on CUB (87.5 vs 86.8), and +1.0% on SUN (77.3 vs 76.3). For GZSL harmonic mean, ZeroDiff leads on AWA2 (81.4% vs VADS 79.3%) and CUB (81.6% vs TFVAEGAN 81.1%), but trails DFCAFlow on SUN (59.8% vs 61.2%), making it the one dataset where a prior method retains the edge. Against large-scale vision-language models (CLIP, CoOp, SHIP), ZeroDiff substantially outperforms on GZSL CUB (81.6% H vs 55.0–57.1%), though these models do not follow strict class-splitting protocols. Under the 10% limited-data protocol, ZeroDiff's advantage widens dramatically: on AWA2 it scores 83.3% T1 versus f-VAEGAN's 73.1% and f-CLSWGAN's 54.0%, confirming that diffusion augmentation and SC-based representations specifically mitigate the spurious-correlation failure mode that degrades standard GAN/VAEGAN baselines under data scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroDiff builds directly on the f-VAEGAN baseline (Xian et al., 2019), integrating three novel components: a diffusion forward chain for data augmentation (drawing on Ho et al., 2020 and DDGAN, Xiao et al., 2022), supervised contrastive representations repurposed as instance-level semantics (Khosla et al., 2020), and a Wasserstein-distance-based mutual learning loss across three discriminators. It departs from prior generative ZSL work by explicitly diagnosing and quantifying spurious visual-semantic correlations via discriminator critic scores, and by positioning diffusion not as a standalone generative paradigm (as in Stable Diffusion-based classifiers) but as a data-augmentation mechanism within a GAN framework. The paper also distinguishes itself from dynamic-semantic approaches like DSP and VADS by arguing that SC-based representations naturally capture intra-class variation without sacrificing class discriminability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and previously underexplored concern: generative ZSL methods degrade sharply when seen-class training samples are scarce, a scenario common in real-world deployment. The quantification of spurious correlations via Δ_adv provides a useful diagnostic tool for the community, and the finding that VAEGAN can underperform vanilla GAN under limited data challenges the assumption that VAE regularization always helps. Limitations include evaluation on only three standard ZSL benchmarks with a ResNet-101 backbone, and the SUN results remain relatively weak (59.8% H), suggesting the approach may not generalize equally well to scene-level tasks. Open questions include whether the diffusion augmentation strategy scales to higher-dimensional feature spaces or larger class counts, and whether the mutual learning mechanism extends to more than three discriminators.
