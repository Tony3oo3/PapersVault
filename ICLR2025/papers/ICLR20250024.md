## Problem

Existing zero-shot image editing methods face a fundamental trade-off: inversion-based approaches (e.g., NTI, PnP) require time-consuming iterative optimization to preserve background features, while inversion-free methods (e.g., DDCM, TurboEdit) lack theoretical grounding for background consistency. The root cause is error accumulation from the unconditional score term in classifier-free guidance, which no prior method addresses without either expensive network re-inference or additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a target text prompt as input, operating entirely in the latent space of LCM-SD1.5. The initial image is encoded to a latent z₀, from which a masked measurement y is derived (each latent element randomly masked with probability 0.5). The output is a single edited image decoded from the optimized latent, produced in approximately 1.5 seconds with 18 GB GPU memory on an A100. No additional conditioning signals (depth maps, canny edges, etc.) are required.

## Method

PostEdit extends posterior sampling theory to text-guided editing by formulating the editing process as progressive sampling from the posterior p(z_t | y), where y is a masked observation of the initial latent. Instead of iterative DDIM inversion, it adds random noise in a single step, then uses the LCM solver (1 step) to estimate ẑ₀, and optimizes ẑ₀ via Langevin dynamics with two L₂ terms: one pulling toward the diffusion estimate and one enforcing consistency with the masked measurement y. A weighted injection of the original latent (Proposition 1, w ≤ 0.1) corrects layout inconsistencies inherent to large text-to-image models. The entire loop (LCM estimation → Langevin optimization → re-noising to the next timestep) repeats over 5 posterior sampling steps with a predefined schedule {501, 401, 301, 201, 101, 1}, requiring no training, no text-embedding optimization, and no iterative network inversion.

## Results

On PIE-Bench (700 images, 10 editing types, 512×512), PostEdit achieves state-of-the-art CLIP similarity scores of 26.76 (Whole) and 24.14 (Edited), outperforming all 12 compared baselines. It also attains the best MSE (3.24×10⁻³) among all sub-2-second methods, while completing inference in approximately 1.5 seconds on a single A100 GPU with 18 GB memory. For reconstruction, PostEdit achieves PSNR 24.39 and LPIPS 9.00×10⁻² in ~1.5s, substantially outperforming the comparable-speed inversion-free baselines iCD (PSNR 19.64) and DDCM (PSNR 18.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PostEdit achieves the highest CLIP Whole (26.76) and CLIP Edited (24.14) scores across all 12 baselines, with the closest competitors being TurboEdit (26.29 / 23.05) and DDCM (26.07 / 22.09) on editing alignment. On background preservation, PostEdit's MSE of 3.24×10⁻³ is the best among all methods running under 2 seconds, beating iCD (7.96), DDCM (7.06), and TurboEdit (9.51); however, slower methods SPD (2.33) and DI (3.25) achieve marginally lower MSE at 30–60× the runtime. In terms of PSNR and SSIM, PostEdit (27.04 / 82.20) trails SPD (28.86 / 86.86) and DDCM (28.08 / 85.26), but these methods require 15–30 seconds per image. Compared to the training-free inversion-free baselines iCD and DDCM, PostEdit improves CLIP Edited by 1.81 and 2.05 points respectively while matching or exceeding their speed. The user study (34 participants) confirms these quantitative findings, with PostEdit receiving 81/240 preference votes for editing—nearly 3× the next-best method (OmniGen, 55 votes).

## Relation to Prior Work

PostEdit extends the posterior sampling framework from Chung et al. (DPS, ICLR 2023), originally designed for inverse problems like inpainting and super-resolution, to the text-guided image editing task. It builds on the DDIM solver (Song et al., 2021) and Latent Consistency Models (Luo et al., 2023) for efficient few-step denoising, but departs from both inversion-based methods (NTI, NPI, PnP) that require iterative network inference to preserve background, and inversion-free methods (DDCM, TurboEdit) that lack theoretical grounding for background consistency. The key conceptual departure is replacing the time-consuming inversion or training pipeline with a lightweight Langevin dynamics optimization guided by a masked measurement term, explicitly correcting the bias introduced by the unconditional score in classifier-free guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PostEdit offers a practically compelling solution to the efficiency–fidelity tradeoff in zero-shot image editing: ~1.5 seconds and 18 GB GPU memory while achieving SOTA CLIP similarity on PIE-Bench, making interactive editing feasible on consumer hardware. The theoretical grounding via posterior sampling provides a principled explanation for why background preservation works, unlike heuristic approaches in prior inversion-free methods. However, the authors acknowledge limitations in handling highly specific scene descriptions and note that performance is tightly coupled to the underlying base model (LCM-SD1.5), leaving open whether the framework transfers cleanly to newer architectures like SDXL-Turbo or Flux. The single-benchmark evaluation (PIE-Bench, 700 images) and the confounded comparison with TurboEdit (different base model) limit the generality of the claims.
