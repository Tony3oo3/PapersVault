## Problem

Large multimodal models (LMMs) like LLaVA-v1.5 encode a single image into 576 vision tokens that must pass through the entire LLM backbone, creating substantial computational overhead that limits real-time deployment. Previous efficiency efforts focused on shrinking the LLM or quantizing weights, while overlooking the token count itself; direct token-merging approaches at the vision encoder inevitably discard visual information and degrade performance. The paper asks whether vision tokens can be compressed to a single token fed into the LLM without sacrificing understanding quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251917/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at 336×336 (standard) or 672×672 (high-resolution, split into four 336×336 sub-images), video frames extracted at 1 fps, and a text instruction. Critically, only 1 compressed vision token per image (or 64 for high-res) is fed into the LLM backbone, while the full 576 (or 2304) vision tokens are consumed by the pre-fusion module before compression. For an M-frame video, the LLM receives M vision tokens plus l_q pooled fusion tokens, enabling processing of 10,000+ frames within 24 GB VRAM. The output is a free-form text response generated by the LLM.

## Method

Layer-wise attention analysis reveals that vision tokens receive most of their attention in the early LLM layers, where text tokens fuse visual information, and that removing vision tokens in later layers has minimal impact. LLaVA-Mini exploits this by inserting two modules before the LLM: (1) a modality pre-fusion module of 4 Transformer blocks (matching the LLM's architecture) that concatenates all vision tokens with text tokens so that text representations absorb visual information in advance; (2) a query-based compression module that uses C×C learnable queries with 2D sinusoidal positional encoding to cross-attend over all vision tokens, producing C² compressed tokens (C=1 standard, C=8 high-res). The LLM then receives only C² + l_q tokens instead of N² + l_q. Training follows LLaVA's two-stage paradigm: Stage 1 aligns the projection layer with frozen encoder and LLM; Stage 2 trains the compression, pre-fusion, projection, and LLM end-to-end on instruction data, keeping only the CLIP vision encoder frozen.

## Results

LLaVA-Mini achieves 57.9% average accuracy across 11 image benchmarks using only 1 vision token (vs. 576 for LLaVA-v1.5), matching or exceeding LLaVA-v1.5's 56.3% average while reducing FLOPs by 77% (8.55T → 1.96T) and cutting inference latency from ~100 ms to 40 ms. On video benchmarks, LLaVA-Mini (1 fps, 1 token/frame) achieves 53.5% on ActivityNet-QA and 2.94 average generative score, outperforming Video-LLaVA (45.3%, 2.84) and LLaMA-VID (47.4%, 2.88). On long-video MLVU, LLaVA-Mini reaches 42.8% average accuracy (trained on <60-frame videos), surpassing MA-LMM (36.4%) and LLaMA-VID (33.2%), and achieves 51.2% on EgoSchema vs. 42.2% for VideoChat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline LLaVA-v1.5 (576 tokens), LLaVA-Mini (1 token) achieves a higher average (57.9% vs. 56.3%), winning on VisWiz (+6.2), MMBench (+1.3), LLaVA-W (+5.5), and MM-Vet (+6.1), while slightly trailing on VQA² (−0.9), GQA (−1.1), and MME (−44.7). Compared to other extreme-compression methods, LLaVA-Mini substantially outperforms MQT-LLaVA (2 tokens: 61.0/50.8/54.4 on VQA²/GQA/MMB), VoCo-LLaMA (1 token: 72.3/57.0/58.8), and LLaMA-VID (2 tokens: 55.5 GQA), confirming that modality pre-fusion preserves visual information lost by direct token merging. On video QA, LLaVA-Mini beats Video-LLaVA on ActivityNet-QA (53.5% vs. 45.3%) and generative average (2.94 vs. 2.84), and surpasses LLaMA-VID on all three QA benchmarks (e.g., ActivityNet-QA 53.5% vs. 47.4%). On long-video MLVU, LLaVA-Mini (42.8%) outperforms MA-LMM (36.4%), LLaMA-VID (33.2%), and MovieChat (25.8%) despite being trained only on <60-frame videos. On EgoSchema, LLaVA-Mini (51.2%) leads VideoChat (42.2%), LLaMA-VID (38.5%), and Video-LLaVA (38.4%), demonstrating strong length extrapolation from 1 fps sampling with one token per frame.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaVA-Mini builds directly on the LLaVA architecture (CLIP ViT-L + Vicuna-7B) and adapts the Q-Former cross-attention mechanism from BLIP-2 for its query-based compression module. It departs from prior efficient-LMM work (PruMerge, MQT-LLaVA, TokenPacker) that reduces vision tokens at the vision-encoder level by instead performing compression *after* the encoder and introducing a modality pre-fusion module that shifts the vision-to-text information transfer from the LLM's early layers to a dedicated pre-LLM stage. The key conceptual departure is the empirical finding—validated across four LLaVA variants—that vision tokens receive most of their attention in the first few LLM layers, justifying extreme compression once that fusion is done externally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 576× vision-token compression with comparable (or slightly better) benchmark performance, 77% FLOPs reduction, and sub-40 ms latency make real-time multimodal interaction feasible on consumer hardware (24 GB GPU), a practical milestone for deploying LMMs in interactive applications. The layer-wise attention analysis provides a broadly applicable mechanistic insight that could inform token-reduction strategies beyond the LLaVA family. Open questions include whether the four pre-fusion layers are optimal, whether the approach transfers cleanly to non-LLaVA architectures (e.g., those with interleaved vision-text tokens), and whether the slight residual gap on fine-grained OCR and counting tasks can be closed without increasing token count.
