## Problem

Reference-free LLM judges, which are the only option for best-of-N decoding at test time, significantly underperform compared to reference-based judges, yet human references are unavailable during inference. This creates a chicken-and-egg problem: the most reliable evaluation paradigm cannot be used for test-time output selection. Additionally, even when reference-based evaluation is made feasible, the quadratic compute cost of MBR decoding at inference time is prohibitive for deployment.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single-turn text prompt and generates N_cand=30 candidate outputs via temperature sampling (t=0.3) from a generator LLM (Llama-2 or Llama-3 variants up to 70B). Each candidate is then scored pairwise against all other candidates using a reference-based LLM judge (Prometheus-2-7B), which outputs a 1–5 utility score per pair; the candidate with the highest average utility is selected. For the distillation pipeline, the same MBR scoring produces preference pairs (highest- vs. lowest-scoring candidate per prompt) from 3000 UltraChat prompts per iteration, which are consumed by DPO to yield a fine-tuned model that matches MBR-level quality under greedy decoding.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary mechanisms. First, MBR inference replaces the inaccessible human reference in reference-based evaluation with the other N_cand−1 sampled candidates as pseudo-references, computing expected utility as a Monte Carlo average over all pairwise judge scores and selecting the highest-consensus output. This consistently outperforms best-of-N decoding with the same judge because the averaging over pseudo-references smooths out individual judge errors, and reference-based evaluation is inherently more reliable than reference-free scoring. Second, MBR distillation iteratively applies DPO on preference pairs formed from MBR-scored candidates (highest vs. lowest per prompt), repeating three rounds with fresh prompts each time. The key finding is that DPO on MBR-selected pairs recovers and exceeds MBR decoding performance under greedy decoding, while SFT on the same MBR-selected outputs yields substantially smaller gains, indicating that the contrastive signal between best and worst candidates is what drives improvement.

## Results

MBR decoding with Prometheus-2-7B as the utility metric yields an average improvement of +3.6% on AlpacaEval 2.0 (length-controlled win rate) and +0.28 on MT-Bench across five LLMs (Llama-2-7B/13B/70B, Llama-3-8B/70B) relative to greedy decoding. Notably, Llama-2-7B with Prometheus MBR decoding (6.10) outperforms Llama-2-13B with greedy decoding (5.90) on MT-Bench, and Llama-2-13B with Prometheus MBR (23.4%) outperforms Llama-2-70B with greedy (22.8%) on AlpacaEval 2.0. For MBR distillation, three rounds of DPO self-training on MBR-decoded outputs improve Llama-2-13B by +7.1% on AlpacaEval 2.0 and +0.90 on MT-Bench over its SFT baseline using only greedy decoding, matching or exceeding the base model's MBR-decoding performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prometheus MBR decoding consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all five models on both AlpacaEval 2.0 and MT-Bench. Compared to the closest competitor, Prometheus BoN decoding (same judge, reference-free), MBR yields an additional +1.88% on AlpacaEval and +0.15 on MT-Bench on average; this gap widens with stronger judges (e.g., Llama-3-70B-Instruct: MBR +0.41 vs. BoN +0.06 on MT-Bench). Lexical and embedding-based MBR metrics (ROUGE +1.33%, BERTScore +1.50%) provide modest gains, while SFR-Embedder MBR actually degrades AlpacaEval performance (−1.42%). Beam search and Longest decoding both underperform greedy decoding. In the distillation setting, DPO on MBR-decoded preference pairs (dpo-3-MBR: 15.3% AlpacaEval, 6.75 MT-Bench for 13B) substantially outperforms DPO on BoN-decoded pairs (dpo-3-BoN: 12.8%, 6.56) and SFT self-training (sft-3-MBR: 6.48%, 5.51), confirming that preference learning on MBR-selected pairs is the most effective distillation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Minimum Bayes Risk decoding from its established domain in machine translation and summarization (where utility metrics like COMET, BLEURT, ROUGE, and BERTScore were used) to open-ended instruction-following, introducing LLM judges (e.g., Prometheus-2-7B) as the utility metric for the first time in this setting. It builds directly on Yang et al. (2024), who first demonstrated DPO self-training on MBR-decoded outputs for machine translation, by transferring that distillation recipe to instruction-following and comparing it against BoN-based self-training. The work also sits at the intersection of the LLM-as-a-Judge literature (Zheng et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024) and inference-time compute scaling (Welleck et al., 2024; Snell et al., 2024), repurposing reference-based evaluation capabilities previously used only for scoring into an active supervision signal for generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical two-stage recipe—MBR inference for immediate test-time gains, followed by iterative DPO distillation to internalize those gains into greedy decoding—that practitioners can adopt without external labels. The finding that a 7B judge can consistently supervise models up to 70B parameters lowers the barrier to deploying MBR decoding in production settings where large judges are unavailable. Open questions include whether the modest gains on reasoning-heavy categories (coding, math) can be closed with better utility metrics or question-specific rubrics, and whether the approach transfers to multi-turn or agentic settings beyond the single-turn AlpacaEval/MT-Bench evaluation. The quadratic O(N_cand²) cost of pairwise utility computation remains a practical bottleneck that speculative decoding only partially alleviates.
