## Problem

Existing spike-driven Transformers (e.g., SD-Transformer v2) achieve strong accuracy on ImageNet but require 55M+ parameters and 52mJ energy, making them impractical for resource-constrained neuromorphic hardware. Directly quantizing these models to low bit-width (2–4 bits) causes severe performance degradation because the bimodal distribution of quantized spike-driven self-attention (Q-SDSA) leads to spike information distortion (SID), a problem unexplored in prior SNN quantization work.

## Input / Output

The model accepts 224×224 RGB images for ImageNet classification, 1333×800 images for COCO detection, and DVS event streams (converted into T three-channel frames encoding positive, negative, and all events) for neuromorphic classification. A key operational detail is the train/inference asymmetry: during training the IE-LIF neuron emits multi-bit values {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1} in a single timestep, while at inference these are converted to binary spikes spread over 4 virtual timesteps, preserving the spike-driven nature of deployment.

## Method

The QSD-Transformer quantizes SD-Transformer v2 weights from 32-bit to 4-bit (or 2/3-bit) via LSQ with straight-through estimation, then applies a bi-level optimization strategy grounded in mutual information entropy maximization. At the lower level, an information-enhanced LIF (IE-LIF) neuron emits multi-bit spikes during training to maximize the entropy of attention scores, paired with a membrane potential rectify function (MPRF) that normalizes Q-SDSA membrane potentials to counteract the right-tail bias of the discrete distribution. At the upper level, a fine-grained distillation (FGD) loss minimizes the L2 distance between ℓ2-normalized attention similarity matrices of the QSD-Transformer and its ANN counterpart across all layers and heads, thereby minimizing conditional entropy H(p^S|p^A). The MPRF is fused into convolution weights at inference (Eq. 26) so it incurs zero additional computational cost, and the overall training objective combines cross-entropy with the FGD loss (λ=2).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the QSD-Transformer (SD-Transformer v2-L, 4-bit weights, 1-bit spikes) achieves 80.3% top-1 accuracy with only 6.8M parameters and 8.7 mJ energy consumption, outperforming the prior SNN state-of-the-art SD-Transformer v2-L (79.7%, 55.4M params, 52.4 mJ) by 0.6% while reducing parameters by 87.6% and power by 83.4%. On COCO object detection, the QSD-Transformer (M) reaches 57.0% mAP@0.5, surpassing SD-Transformer v2-M (51.2%) by 5.8%. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, it achieves 40.5% mIoU versus 35.3% for SD-Transformer v2-M, a 5.2% gain with 83.9% fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The QSD-Transformer (L) achieves 80.3% top-1 on ImageNet, beating SD-Transformer v2-L (79.7%) by 0.6% while using 87.6% fewer parameters (6.8M vs 55.4M) and 83.4% less power (8.7 vs 52.4 mJ). It also surpasses SpikingResformer-L (78.7%) by 1.6% and the ANN CAformer baseline (79.9%) by 0.4%, all with substantially lower energy. On COCO detection, QSD-Transformer (M) reaches 57.0% mAP@0.5, exceeding SD-Transformer v2-M (51.2%) by 5.8% with fewer than half the parameters (34.9M vs 75.0M). On ADE20K segmentation, QSD-Transformer (M) achieves 40.5% mIoU versus 35.3% for SD-Transformer v2-M, and even approaches the ANN DeepLab-V3 (42.7%) with 86% fewer parameters. In transfer learning, QSD-Transformer (6.8M) attains 98.4% on CIFAR-10, 87.6% on CIFAR-100, and 89.8% on CIFAR10-DVS, outperforming SpikingResformer by 1.0%, 1.7%, and 5.0% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly quantizes the SD-Transformer v2 architecture (Yao et al., 2023a) using LSQ-based quantization-aware training, extending a quantization technique previously applied only to CNN-based SNNs (Deng et al., 2023) into the spike-driven Transformer domain. The core methodological departure is the identification of a 'spike information distortion' (SID) problem unique to quantized attention modules in SNNs—where the bimodal distribution of Q-SDSA scores contrasts with the normal distribution in ANN Transformers—and the proposal of a bi-level optimization strategy (IE-LIF neuron at the neuron level, fine-grained distillation at the network level) inspired by mutual information entropy theory. The IE-LIF neuron itself modifies the standard LIF model (Wu et al., 2018) by allowing multi-bit activations during training while preserving binary spike-driven inference, a compromise not previously explored in this form for Transformer-based SNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The QSD-Transformer demonstrates that spike-driven Transformers can achieve competitive accuracy (80.3% on ImageNet) with 87.6% fewer parameters and 83.4% less power than the prior SNN SOTA, making neuromorphic deployment of Transformer-class models on edge devices more realistic. The SID diagnosis provides a useful conceptual framework for understanding why naive quantization fails specifically in SNN attention modules, which should inform future compression work in this area. However, scalability remains untested (Spikformer-V2 with 173M parameters was not quantized), actual hardware deployment is absent, and the energy analysis excludes memory access costs—leaving open whether the efficiency claims hold on real neuromorphic platforms. The approach's transferability to other SNN architectures beyond SD-Transformer v2 and Spikformer is also not yet established.
