## Problem

LoRA's low-rank weight update imposes an inherent representational ceiling: increasing the rank (and thus trainable parameters) yields diminishing returns and fails to close the accuracy gap with full fine-tuning, especially on complex vision-language tasks. The paper asks whether this gap stems from the reduced parameter count or from the rank deficiency itself, and shows it is the latter.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the weight matrices of pre-trained transformer models (CLIP ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14, ViT-H/14; DinoV2 ViT-B/14; Llama3-8B, Phi3-3B, Qwen2-0.5B), replacing the standard LoRA update ΔW = BA with a full-rank update constructed from random bases. For vision and vision-language tasks, RGB images are classified into class labels (21–22 datasets including ImageNet); for commonsense reasoning, text prompts produce text answers across 8 tasks. The trainable parameters are only the diagonal scaling matrices, with the random bases generated once and kept frozen, keeping memory comparable to or lower than LoRA.

## Method

RandLoRA expresses the full-rank weight update ΔW as a sum of n = d/r low-rank random bases: ΔW = Σⱼ Bⱼ Λⱼ A Γⱼ, where Bⱼ ∈ ℝ^{D×r} and A ∈ ℝ^{r×d} are fixed random matrices, and Λⱼ ∈ ℝ^{r×r}, Γⱼ ∈ ℝ^{d×d} are the only learnable diagonal scaling matrices. A single shared A across all n bases reduces memory without loss of expressivity. Unlike LoRA, the number of trainable parameters (d²/r + d) is inversely proportional to the base rank r, so increasing r makes the method more parameter-efficient while preserving full rank. A convergence theorem (Theorem 4.1) bounds the Frobenius-norm approximation error by n·ε, and an Eckart-Young-Mirsky lower bound shows LoRA's rank-r approximation is fundamentally limited by the tail singular values, whereas RandLoRA has no such structural floor.

## Results

On 8 commonsense reasoning tasks with Llama3-8B trained on 170k samples, RandLoRA (Performant) achieves 85.6% average accuracy, outperforming LoRA rank-32 (85.2%) and LoRA rank-16 (84.4%) with equal or fewer parameters. On CLIP ViT-B/32 vision-language classification (22 datasets, 100% data), RandLoRA bridges the performance gap with full fine-tuning that LoRA rank-32 cannot close, even when LoRA's rank is increased. On DinoV2, LoRA already performs near full fine-tuning due to the backbone's training objective, and RandLoRA matches or slightly exceeds it. Across all settings, RandLoRA maintains full-rank updates while training only d²/r + d parameters per layer (inversely proportional to base rank), and its base-sharing strategy reduces VRAM usage compared to LoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (the primary baseline), RandLoRA consistently outperforms on LLM fine-tuning with 170k samples: +0.6 on Qwen2-0.5B (57.9 vs 57.3), +0.2 on Phi3-3B (85.2 vs 85.0), and +0.4 on Llama3-8B (85.6 vs 85.2) at matched parameter budgets. On CLIP vision-language classification, RandLoRA closes the gap to full fine-tuning that LoRA rank-32 leaves open, and this gap persists even when LoRA's rank is increased, confirming the limitation is rank deficiency rather than parameter count. Against VeRA and NoLA, RandLoRA is competitive or superior in larger-data regimes (50–100% training data) where both random-basis methods plateau, while VeRA and NoLA can match or beat RandLoRA in strict few-shot (1–4 shot) settings due to their lower parameter counts. Against DoRA (compared only on Llama3), RandLoRA outperforms both DoRA and LoRA at larger parameter budgets, while DoRA and LoRA remain competitive at the 'Efficient' (rank-16) budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

RandLoRA sits at the intersection of two PEFT lineages: low-rank adaptation (LoRA, DoRA, AdaLoRA) and random-basis compression (PRANC, NoLA, VeRA). It departs from LoRA by rejecting the assumption that a single low-rank factorization suffices, instead decomposing the full SVD of the weight update into multiple rank-r blocks. It departs from VeRA and NoLA, which use random bases to approximate a single low-rank LoRA update, by summing n = d/r independent random-basis terms to recover full rank while learning only diagonal scaling coefficients. The shared random matrix A across terms is a memory-saving design choice not present in prior random-basis methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides mechanistic evidence (CKA activation alignment, loss-landscape connectivity, rank ablation) that the LoRA-to-full-fine-tuning gap is driven by rank deficiency rather than parameter count, a distinction the field had not cleanly isolated. This reframes the PEFT design space: methods should target full-rank expressivity under a fixed parameter budget rather than simply increasing rank. Open questions include whether non-random (e.g., SVD-derived) bases can tighten the approximation bound ε, whether hybrid LoRA+RandLoRA decompositions can capture dominant and residual spectral components separately, and whether matmul-free sparse-base implementations can eliminate the current training-time overhead.
