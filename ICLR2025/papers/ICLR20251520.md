## Problem

Scaling TD-based offline RL to large models across diverse visual tasks remains unsolved: prior multi-task offline RL methods either rely on supervised behavior cloning with expert trajectories or use per-task Q-heads that reset during fine-tuning, and larger TD networks are known to become unstable. The paper asks whether an image-observation-based world model can simultaneously unlock scaling trends in offline RL and enable sample-efficient transfer to unseen games.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251520/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 84×84 grayscale Atari frames, each tokenized by a frozen VQ-VAE into 36 discrete visual tokens (vocabulary size 2048), interleaved with discrete actions from a 18-action space and learnable task-ID embeddings, forming sequences of L=8 timesteps (~296 tokens total). At inference, the method outputs a single optimal action selected via beam search over the learned world model (horizon H=2, beam width K=2–4 depending on game), while during training it produces C51-style return distributions (51 atoms over [−10, 30]) for all actions alongside next-frame token, reward, and termination predictions.

## Method

JOWA uses a single shared transformer backbone (GPT-2-based, up to 150M parameters) with two sets of prediction heads: a world-part (next-token, reward, termination predictors trained with cross-entropy) and an action-part (distributional CQL critic using C51 Bellman targets). Both losses back-propagate through the shared transformer, so the world-modeling loss acts as a regularizer that stabilizes TD-learning at scale—a key insight enabling reliable scaling where prior TD methods degrade. At inference, a provably efficient parallelizable beam-search planning algorithm (Theorem 4.1 provides error bounds) rolls out H steps in the learned world model restricted to top-K Q-valued actions, compensating for Q-value estimation error and yielding consistent optimal-policy identification. Training is two-stage: first the VQ-VAE and world-part are trained for 250k steps, then the VQ-VAE is frozen and the full world-action model is jointly optimized for 1.5M steps on ~6B tokens from 15 Atari games (10% subsampled DQN-Replay data).

## Results

On 15 Atari games with only 10% subsampled offline data (10M transitions per game), JOWA-150M achieves an IQM human-normalized score of 78.9% and median HNS of 45.6%, surpassing the best baseline (EDT-200M at 50.2% IQM HNS) by a large margin. JOWA also demonstrates clear scaling: JOWA-40M → 70M → 150M yields IQM HNS of 37.1% → 47.6% → 78.9%, the steepest scaling curve among all compared methods. For out-of-distribution transfer, fine-tuning JOWA-150M on just 5k transitions (~4 trajectories) per unseen game achieves 64.7% IQM DQN-normalized score, outperforming the best baseline (FICC-85M at 57.5%) and a from-scratch JOWA-150M (18.1%) by wide margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JOWA-150M (78.9% IQM HNS) decisively outperforms all five baselines on the 15-game pretraining benchmark: EDT-200M (50.2%), MGDT-200M (49.8%), FICC-85M (43.3%), Scaled-QL-80M (42.0%), and MTBC-120M (32.9%), corresponding to an average relative improvement of 71.4% over the four large-scale baselines. On the 5-game OOD fine-tuning task (5k transitions per game), JOWA-150M achieves 64.7% IQM DNS, beating FICC-85M (57.5%), EDT-200M (38.0%), MGDT-200M (37.7%), Scaled-QL-80M (35.5%), and MTBC-120M (20.5%), while a from-scratch JOWA-150M trained on the same 5k data scores only 18.1%, confirming the value of multi-game pretraining. Notably, JOWA-150M achieves these results with fewer parameters than MGDT-200M and EDT-200M, and without the data augmentation those methods rely on. The scaling trend is unique to JOWA: MTBC and MGDT show flat or diminishing returns with increased capacity, whereas JOWA's IQM HNS rises monotonically from 37.1% (40M) to 78.9% (150M). JOWA also achieves the most superhuman games (6 out of 15) compared to 3–4 for all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IRIS-style transformer world model (VQ-VAE tokenization + autoregressive prediction) by coupling it with a distributional CQL critic through a shared backbone, so that the world-modeling loss regularizes TD-learning at scale. It departs from prior multi-task offline RL (MGDT, EDT) that relies on behavior cloning, and from Scaled-QL which uses per-game Q-heads, by learning a single jointly-optimized world-action model with task embeddings. The inference-time planning algorithm is positioned as a provably efficient alternative to MCTS, drawing on the constrained policy space idea from the distributional Bellman optimality operator (C51).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical insight is that a world-modeling loss can stabilize TD-learning in large transformer models, directly contradicting prior observations that scaling TD-based RL causes instability. This opens a path to scaling offline model-based RL that was previously thought intractable. Limitations include evaluation on only 20 Atari games (not full 2600), the negative result on synthetic data (which is standard in single-task model-based RL), and non-scaling behavior in individual games. Open questions include how to effectively leverage synthetic rollouts in multi-task pretraining and whether the joint-optimization recipe transfers to continuous control or higher-dimensional observation spaces.
