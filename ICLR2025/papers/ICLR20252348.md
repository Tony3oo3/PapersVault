## Problem

Despite a rich library of learning-based TSP solvers spanning supervised, reinforcement, generative, and meta-learning paradigms, no unified framework exists to transparently compare their components or identify which design choices are genuinely beneficial. The interleaving of learning and search elements across different implementations makes their individual contributions and interplay opaque, hindering principled method design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252348/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes TSP instances as N 2D coordinates in the unit square (scales 50–1000) with a corresponding distance matrix, and optionally reference solutions from LKH-3 for supervised training. The solving stage follows a construction-refinement pipeline: a complete feasible tour is first decoded from neural predictions (edge heatmaps or per-step node heatmaps) via greedy, sampling, beam search, or random initialization, then refined by improvement-based search (Two-Opt, guided local search, or MCTS variants). The modular design allows any combination of learning functionality, training objective, learning-based search, construction method, and improvement search to be specified independently.

## Method

The paper proposes ML4TSPBench, a unified modular framework that decomposes all mainstream ML4TSP solvers into a common design space with aligned components: four learning functionalities (edge prediction, edge regret prediction, solution generation, sequential node prediction), six training objectives (supervised/unsupervised/RL/meta-learning variants), learning-based search (active search, gradient search), construction methods, and improvement-based search. Through systematic ablation within this framework, the paper identifies four desirable design principles—joint probability estimation (via node-wise probability normalization), symmetry solution representation (undirected graph supervision, multi-start decoding, symmetry baselines), online optimization (instance-specific fine-tuning at test time), and search friendliness (informative predictions, solution diversity, meta-learning for optimal initialization)—and introduces novel enhancements compensating for missing attributes in existing methods. The strategic recomposition of these techniques yields a factory of solvers whose optimal configurations achieve 0.000% drop on TSP-50, 0.001% on TSP-100, and 0.142% on TSP-500, surpassing prior SOTA while also demonstrating that non-autoregressive and supervised paradigms hold a clear performance advantage over their autoregressive and unsupervised counterparts.

## Results

The best ML4TSP solver (Edge Prediction + SL with Node Normalization + Random(n=50) + MCTS) achieves drops of 0.004% on TSP-50, 0.002% on TSP-100, and 0.142% on TSP-500, outperforming LKH-3 (trials=500) which yields 0.322% on TSP-500. The best generative solver (Diffusion + Symmetry + Gradient Search + 8×Greedy + Two-Opt) reaches 0.002%, 0.003%, and 0.187% on TSP-50/100/500 without relying on heavy heuristic search. On generalization to TSP-1000, the best ML4TSP method (Edge Pred + SL+Node Norm + Random(n=10) + MCTS) achieves 0.603% drop, while the generative approach with 8×Greedy reaches 1.153%. The exact solver Concorde remains the reference at 0.000% across all scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Concorde (the exact solver), all ML4TSP methods show a small but nonzero gap, with the best achieving 0.000–0.142% drop depending on scale. Against LKH-3 (trials=500), the best ML4TSP solver clearly wins on TSP-500 (0.142% vs. 0.322% drop) while being comparable on TSP-50/100. However, LKH-3 with more trials (5k: 0.072%, 50k: 0.014%) still outperforms the best ML4TSP configuration on TSP-500, though at lower wall-clock time (4.2s and 26.3s vs. 55.3s). Against GA-EAX, ML4TSP methods substantially outperform on TSP-100 and TSP-500 (GA-EAX shows 9.7% and 12.5% drop respectively in Table 9). The generative (diffusion-based) approach is competitive with MCTS-based methods on TSP-50/100 (0.002–0.003%) and offers a lighter search dependency, making it attractive for scenarios where heavy heuristic search is impractical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented landscape of learning-based TSP solvers—spanning GCN-based edge prediction (Joshi et al., 2019), attention-based autoregressive models (Kool et al., 2018; Kwon et al., 2020), diffusion-based generative solvers (Sun & Yang, 2023; Li et al., 2023b), meta-learning (Qiu et al., 2022), and MCTS (Fu et al., 2021)—into a single modular design space with separable components for learning functionality, training objective, construction, and improvement-based search. Unlike prior unified efforts such as RL4CO (Berto et al., 2023), which focused solely on RL-based models, or Joshi et al. (2020), which analyzed generalization of naive pipelines, this framework covers all major learning paradigms and explicitly decouples learning from search to enable transparent ablation. The paper departs from the incremental 'add one component' methodology by treating the entire ML4TSP system as a recomposable factory, where design principles (joint probability estimation, symmetry, online optimization, search friendliness) are identified and validated across method families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the field with a principled reference framework for understanding which components of ML4TSP solvers genuinely contribute to performance, directly addressing the opacity that has hampered methodological progress. The identification of actionable design principles—particularly that non-autoregressive supervised methods outperform autoregressive unsupervised ones, and that node-level normalization yields large gains at zero computational cost—offers concrete guidance for future method design. The framework's extension to CVRP and MIS (albeit preliminary) suggests the principles may generalize across combinatorial optimization, though the gap between ML solvers and traditional solvers like LKH-3/Concorde at larger scales (TSP-500+) remains substantial. Open questions include whether generative/diffusion approaches can close this gap with further architectural innovation, and whether the design principles hold under distribution shift beyond uniform random instances.
