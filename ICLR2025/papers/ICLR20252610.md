## Problem

Existing structured pruning methods for LLMs determine which weight channels to remove using a fixed calibration dataset, but LLMs exhibit batch-dependent outlier patterns that shift across different input batches and cannot be accurately predicted from a static calibration set. This mismatch causes pruning decisions to miss critical channels for the current batch, leading to substantial performance degradation during real-time inference. The paper addresses this by proposing an online, dynamic pruning framework that adapts channel-selection decisions to each batch's specific hidden-state characteristics without requiring fine-tuning or additional model modules.

## Input / Output

During inference, the method consumes a text batch of shape N×S×D (batch size × sequence length × feature dimension) along with the full LLM weights and a pre-computed set of historical states derived from a C4 calibration dataset. It produces per-batch, per-block pruning decisions specifying which input channels to retain in each attention output projection and MLP FC2 layer, followed by the model's inference output computed on the remaining weights. The probing stage that enables these decisions uses only 5% of samples and 50% of tokens, consuming approximately 1.5% of the FLOPs of dense model inference.

## Method

PP operates in three stages per block during inference: (1) probing, where residual importance (L2 norm of hidden states across non-target dimensions) selects the top x% of samples and y% of tokens to form a small probe that executes the intermediate transformations ahead to capture intermediate hidden states; (2) history-informed pruning, where importance-scaled fusion (a weighted combination proportional to each source's magnitude) merges the probing states with historical states maintained via exponential moving average (λ=0.99), and the PPsp metric—defined as the L2 norm across output channels of the squared Wanda importance |W|²·‖X‖²—is computed to rank channels for pruning; (3) full inference on the remaining unpruned weights. The key insight is that a minimal probe captures sufficient intermediate hidden state information to identify batch-specific important channels, while importance-scaled fusion balances calibration-derived priors with current-batch observations to minimize the bias introduced by relying solely on a fixed calibration set. No fine-tuning, LoRA adapters, or additional neural network modules are required.

## Results

At a 40% pruning ratio on LLaMA-2-7B (WikiText2), PP achieves a perplexity of 16.8, substantially lower than FLAP (38.9), Wanda-sp (43.8), LLM-Pruner (51.1), and LoRA-Prune (129.5). On LLaMA-2-13B at 40% pruning, PP attains 11.3 perplexity and 61.0% average commonsense reasoning accuracy, outperforming all baselines. The Performance Runtime Ratio (PRR) of PP is 37.37, which is 2.56× lower than FLAP (95.64) and 2.85× lower than Wanda-sp (106.48), indicating far less performance degradation per unit of runtime reduction. Probing overhead is only ~1.5% of dense model FLOPs (66 TFLOPs vs. 4420 TFLOPs on WikiText2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PP consistently outperforms all four baselines across every model and pruning ratio tested. Against FLAP (An et al., 2024), PP reduces WikiText2 perplexity from 38.9 to 16.8 on LLaMA-2-7B at 40% pruning (a 2.3× improvement) and from 51.0 to 26.7 on OPT-13B. Against Wanda-sp (also from An et al., 2024), PP achieves 16.8 vs. 43.8 perplexity on LLaMA-2-7B at 40% pruning. Against the fine-tuning-based methods, PP without any fine-tuning surpasses both LoRA-Prune (Zhang et al., 2023) and LLM-Pruner (Ma et al., 2023) even when those methods are given their LoRA fine-tuning: at 40% pruning on LLaMA-2-13B, PP reaches 61.0% average reasoning accuracy versus 55.5% (LoRA-Prune w/ LoRA) and 54.7% (LLM-Pruner w/ LoRA). On LLaMA-3-8B at 40% pruning, PP achieves 58.9% average accuracy versus 53.3% (Wanda-sp) and 53.6% (FLAP), with a perplexity of 14.9 versus 18.4 and 18.5 respectively. PP also approaches its theoretical upper bound (Full-Batch Probing) while using only 1.5% of dense FLOPs for probing, whereas Full-Batch Probing requires more computation than the dense model itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structured pruning line for LLMs (FLAP, Wanda-sp, LLM-Pruner, LoRA-Prune) by shifting from static, calibration-dataset-driven channel selection to online, batch-dependent dynamic pruning. Its core departure is the probing mechanism: rather than relying on a fixed calibration set to estimate weight importance, PP runs a tiny subset of hidden states (5% samples, 50% tokens) through a few layers ahead to capture intermediate states that reflect the current batch's outlier structure. The PPsp metric adapts Wanda's unstructured importance to structured pruning by squaring the per-weight score and taking an L2 norm across output channels, and the importance-scaled fusion with historical states mitigates the calibration-dataset bias that the authors demonstrate empirically (e.g., FLAP's perplexity jumping from 18.5 to 38.9 depending on calibration set).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PP offers a practically attractive recipe for LLM deployment: only 1.5% FLOPs overhead, no fine-tuning, no extra network modules, and a 2.56× better performance-runtime ratio than FLAP at 40% pruning. The finding that dynamic pruning without fine-tuning outperforms static pruning with LoRA fine-tuning (e.g., 61.0 vs. 55.5 average accuracy on LLaMA-2-13B at 40%) challenges the assumption that recovery fine-tuning is necessary. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 40% pruning (attention heads saturate earlier than MLPs, as shown in Table 13), whether parallel probing can hide the 1.5% latency in practice, and how robustness holds under adversarial or poisoned inputs. The method still requires loading the full model for weight extraction, which limits memory savings despite the compute savings.
