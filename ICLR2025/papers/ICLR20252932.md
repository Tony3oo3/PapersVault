## Problem

Existing chain-of-thought distillation methods transfer reasoning from LLMs to SLMs but suffer from two critical limitations: (1) hallucinated explanations from teacher LLMs may be inconsistent with the gold answer, degrading student performance, and (2) they treat all CoTs as flat chains, ignoring the diverse reasoning structures (tree, graph) that mirror human backtracking and feedforward logic. No prior work unifies these structural variants into a single distillation pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a question (or text description) q and its gold answer a* as input. The teacher LLM iteratively generates a set of explanation nodes V = {v_0, ..., v_Nv} organized according to a chosen structure (linear chain, three-layer binary tree, or random connected directed graph), along with a relation matrix R that encodes both connectivity and proximity-to-answer via Dijkstra shortest-path distances. The student SLM consumes the question plus the structured explanation set and outputs a single answer label; at inference, all answer tokens within explanations are replaced with |MASK| to prevent leakage.

## Method

UniCoTT constructs a unified structured explanation (UniCoT) by iteratively prompting an LLM to generate each node conditioned on the question, the gold answer, and the prefix explanations of parent nodes, with the adjacency matrix A initialized per structure type. The student is trained with three jointly optimized losses: standard cross-entropy on the answer, a node-level supervised contrastive loss that pulls hidden states of same-answer nodes together and pushes different-answer nodes apart, and a structural consistency loss derived from an upper bound on the structural error (Theorem 1) that is minimized by maximizing the rank of the covariance matrix of the structured hidden states S = RH. The structural consistency loss decomposes into a decoupling term (maximizing per-dimension variance via ReLU) and an entanglement term (minimizing off-diagonal covariance), directly adapting VICReg-style regularization to enforce that the SLM's latent geometry respects the directed-graph topology of UniCoT.

## Results

On the factual reasoning benchmark (Table 1), UniCoTT with RoBERTa-base achieves 96.8% accuracy on CREAK (graph structure), 88.8% on CSQA2 (tree structure), and 94.6% on StrategyQA (tree structure), outperforming all baselines by 4.6–24.6 points. On the multiple-choice QA benchmark (Table 2), UniCoTT (graph) reaches 92.5% accuracy on CSQA and 88.8% on OBQA with RoBERTa-base, surpassing SCOTT by 2.7 and 4.0 points respectively. On GLUE (Table 3), UniCoTT (graph) achieves 94.7 average with RoBERTa-base vs. 87.5 for SCOTT. The LAS metric (Table 5) confirms UniCoTT generates more rational explanations (e.g., 10.4 on OBQA vs. 6.4 for SCOTT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCoTT consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and both student architectures (BERT-base and RoBERTa-base). Against CoT (Wei et al., 2023), UniCoTT (chain) already surpasses it by 6.9 points on CREAK and 9.5 on CSQA2 (RoBERTa-base), with tree/graph structures widening the gap to 8.3 and 16.1 points respectively. Against SCOTT (Wang et al., 2023), UniCoTT (chain) leads by 3.2 points on CREAK and 2.1 on StrategyQA, while tree and graph structures extend the advantage to 4.6 and 5.4 points on CREAK; notably, SCOTT actually underperforms the base model on QASC with RoBERTa (30.8 vs. 32.3), whereas UniCoTT (tree) reaches 80.1. Against DSbS (Hsieh et al., 2023), UniCoTT achieves the largest margins: +21.2 points on CREAK, +34.6 on CSQA2, and +14.6 on StrategyQA (RoBERTa-base, graph structure), indicating that DSbS's step-by-step decomposition is far less effective than structured CoT distillation. The LAS rationality metric further confirms UniCoTT's advantage: graph UniCoTT scores 10.4 on OBQA vs. 6.4 for SCOTT and 6.0 for CoT, validating that answer-conditioned iterative generation produces more faithful explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CoT distillation line initiated by SCOTT (Wang et al., 2023) and DSbS (Hsieh et al., 2023), which transfer chain-structured reasoning from LLMs to SLMs via cross-entropy fine-tuning. The key departure is the unification of diverse structural CoTs (chain, tree, graph — inspired by ToT and GoT) into a single 'UniCoT' representation, coupled with a structural consistency loss derived from VICReg-style variance-invariance-covariance regularization (Bardes et al., 2022; Shwartz-Ziv et al., 2023). Unlike SCOTT, which only produces linear chain explanations, UniCoTT iteratively constructs structured thought graphs guided by an adjacency matrix and a Dijkstra-based relation matrix, then transfers both semantic and structural knowledge to the student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that structural reasoning topology (tree vs. graph vs. chain) carries transferable knowledge beyond mere text content, and that a VICReg-inspired structural consistency loss can encode this topology into SLM latent representations. This opens a path toward distilling not just *what* an LLM reasons but *how* it reasons structurally, which could benefit resource-constrained deployments. However, the reliance on LLM APIs for UniCoT construction limits practicality, and the evaluation is confined to relatively small encoder models (BERT/RoBERTa/XLNet-base) plus one 3B decoder model, leaving open whether the structural gains persist at larger scales or in fully offline settings.
