## Problem

Existing structured pruning methods for transformers are designed to reach a single target compression ratio, requiring the entire pruning process to be restarted whenever a different sparsity level is desired. This restart cost is prohibitive for over-parameterized models, and no prior method can theoretically guarantee convergence while simultaneously producing a continuum of sparse models at varying sparsity levels within one optimization run.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250066/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully pretrained transformer (DeiT, Swin, CLIP, Llama2-7B, OPT-6.7B) with all weights frozen, plus optional calibration data for LLM post-training. It outputs a Transformer Weight Family: a sequence of pruned checkpoints at progressively higher sparsity levels, all extracted from a single search-stage run. The sparsity is structural and column-wise, with pair-wise shared masks applied to QK, V-projection, and MLP input-output pairs, so that dimensionality is preserved within each pair while the hidden dimension shrinks.

## Method

SPP formulates mask-based structural pruning as a differential inclusion coupling two variables: a dense mask M updated by gradient descent on the task loss, and a sparse mask Γ updated by a mirror descent flow with an ℓ₁ penalty and box constraint [0,1]. The discretized iteration alternates a gradient step on M, a gradient step on a sub-gradient V, and a proximal (soft-thresholding) update on Γ, which yields a solution path with the inverse scale space property—important weights become non-zero in Γ early, so early checkpoints in the path are the most informative sparse structures. Structural pruning is enforced through pair-wise shared masks (QK pair, V-projection pair, MLP input-output pair) that keep output dimensions consistent while allowing asymmetric compression of the hidden dimension. Global convergence to a critical point of the empirical loss is proven via a Lyapunov function satisfying the Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz inequality, extending the Bregman inverse scale space framework from Fu et al. (2020) from training-from-scratch CNNs to backward pruning of pretrained transformers.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with DeiT-Base, SPP achieves 81.9% Top-1 accuracy while reducing FLOPS to 56.0% and parameters to 55.5% of the uncompressed model (81.8% Top-1), and further reaches 78.1% Top-1 at 25.1% FLOPS. For DeiT-Small, SPP maintains 80.2% Top-1 at 73.9% FLOPS and 77.7% Top-1 at 41.3% FLOPS. On CLIP-Large for image-text retrieval, SPP retains 70.3% R@1 (Image→Text) at 61.9% FLOPS. For LLMs at 50% sparsity, SPP on Llama2-7B achieves 38.57% ARC-c and 71.80% ARC-e, comparable to RIA and Wanda.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DeiT-Base, SPP (81.9% Top-1 at 56.0% FLOPS) outperforms SCOP (79.7%), IA-RED (80.3%), PoWER (80.1%), and X-Pruner (81.02%) while using fewer FLOPS than all of them. On DeiT-Small, SPP matches UPop (80.2%) and ViT-Slim (80.0%) at similar compression ratios, and clearly surpasses WDPruning (78.6%) and X-Pruner (78.9%) by 1.3–1.6 points. On DeiT-Tiny, SPP (72.3%) leads all baselines by approximately 2 percentage points over GOHSP (70.2%), S²ViTE (70.1%), and WDPruning (70.3%). For CLIP-Large image-text retrieval, SPP retains 70.3% R@1 at 61.9% FLOPS, showing only a 1.2-point drop from the uncompressed 71.5%. On LLMs at 50% sparsity, SPP is competitive with RIA and Wanda on Llama2-7B (e.g., 71.80% ARC-e vs. RIA's 71.59%) but slightly trails on OPT-6.7B BoolQ (63.15% vs. RIA's 66.82%). Compared to the training-from-scratch DessiLBI method, SPP consistently achieves higher accuracy at the same compression ratio (e.g., 80.2% vs. 78.9% on DeiT-Small), confirming the advantage of pruning pretrained weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the differential inclusion / Bregman Inverse Scale Space framework (Osher et al., 2016; Fu et al., 2020 DessiLBI) from training sparse networks from scratch to pruning *pretrained* transformers, where the pretrained weights are frozen and only mask parameters are updated. It departs from standard Lasso-based structured pruning, which yields a single sparsity level per optimization run, by formulating the mask dynamics as a differential inclusion whose solution path naturally traces out a continuum of sparsity levels. The pair-wise shared mask design (QK pair, V-proj pair, MLP input-output pair) is a new architectural choice that enables asymmetric dimensionality reduction within attention heads, going beyond the uniform-dimension pruning assumed by methods like WDPruning and UPop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'one search stage, all sparsity levels' property eliminates the need to restart the entire pruning pipeline for each target compression ratio, which is a meaningful practical saving for over-parameterized models. The KL-based global convergence guarantee for this non-convex, mirror-descent-based iteration is stronger than what competing methods (OFA, UPop) offer, providing a more principled foundation. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines like UPop and ViT-Slim are modest (often <1% Top-1), and the LLM extension is limited to 50% sparsity with only five evaluation datasets, leaving open whether the approach scales to higher compression ratios or longer fine-tuning regimes for LLMs.
