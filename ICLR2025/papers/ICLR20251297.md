## Problem

Existing molecular graph constructions (radial cutoff, k-nearest neighbors, chemical graphs) cannot simultaneously guarantee sparsity, connectivity, and rigidity — properties essential for GNN performance and scalability. Radial cutoff graphs either disconnect at small thresholds or become prohibitively dense at large ones, and none of these methods provide theoretical rigidity guarantees, leaving GNNs unable to uniquely determine atomic spatial arrangements from graph features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251297/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud of atom coordinates (with optional node features like atom types) and produces a hyperparameter-free graph with edge attributes consisting of pairwise Euclidean distances and dihedral angles computed on the projected convex hull, plus node attributes encoding each atom's distance from the centroid. No user-specified cutoff distance or neighbor count is required; the graph structure is fully determined by the geometry of the input points. The resulting graph is guaranteed to have an edge-to-node ratio strictly below 3 and to be connected for any point cloud with more than 2 points.

## Method

SCHull constructs graphs in two steps: (1) project all atom coordinates onto a unit sphere centered at the point cloud's centroid, and (2) compute the convex hull of the projected points (via QuickHull, O(m log m)) and transfer the hull's edge structure back to the original points. This yields a geometric polyhedral graph whose sparsity follows from Steinitz's theorem (edges ≤ 3|V|−6) and whose rigidity follows from Stoker's theorem — edge lengths plus dihedral angles uniquely determine the spatial arrangement up to isometry. The paper proves that a maximally expressive GNN with depth T=1 can distinguish any two non-isomorphic generic point clouds using only these attributes. In practice, SCHull edges and attributes are unioned with those of an existing graph (e.g., radial cutoff) to combine global geometric completeness with local detail, and this integrated graph is fed into off-the-shelf GNNs (DimeNet, SphereNet, LEFTNet, ProNet, GVP-GNN, MACE, SEGNN) without architectural modification.

## Results

On the protein Fold classification task, ProNet-Backbone+SCHull achieves 56.1% (Fold), 74.6% (Superfamily), and 99.4% (Family) accuracy, improving over the baseline ProNet-Backbone (52.7%, 70.3%, 99.3%) and outperforming all other baselines including SEGNN+SCHull (32.0%, 36.8%, 86.9%) and MACE+SCHull (27.0%, 23.1%, 65.0%). On MD17 atomic force prediction, SCHull-integrated models consistently reduce MAE (e.g., LEFTNet+SCHull: 0.098 vs. 0.147 for Benzene; DimeNet+SCHull: 0.427 vs. 0.499 for Aspirin). On the NestedSquares synthetic benchmark, MPNN+SCHull achieves 0.068 MSE, outperforming MPNN with radial cutoff (0.657) and kNN (0.213) by nearly an order of magnitude. On LBA prediction, ProNet-Backbone+SCHull achieves RMSE 1.321 and Pearson 0.581, surpassing the baseline (1.458, 0.546) and all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCHull-integrated models consistently outperform their respective baselines across all tasks. On Fold classification, ProNet-Backbone+SCHull (56.1% Fold, 74.6% Super) beats ProNet-Backbone (52.7%, 70.3%) by 3.4 and 4.3 points, and substantially outperforms SEGNN+SCHull (32.0%, 36.8%), MACE+SCHull (27.0%, 23.1%), and GVP-GNN+SCHull (24.5%, 27.1%). On React classification, ProNet-Backbone+SCHull (88.1%) surpasses IEConv (87.2%), ProNet-Amino-Acid+SCHull (87.9%), and GVP-GNN+SCHull (77.1%). On MD17, all three SCHull-integrated models (DimeNet+SCHull, SphereNet+SCHull, LEFTNet+SCHull) reduce MAE by 10–30% over their baselines (e.g., LEFTNet+SCHull: 0.098 vs. 0.147 for Benzene). On LBA, ProNet-Backbone+SCHull achieves the best RMSE (1.321) and Pearson (0.581), outperforming GVP-GNN+SCHull (1.401, 0.475) and HoloProt (1.464, 0.509). On the NestedSquares benchmark, MPNN+SCHull (0.068 MSE) dramatically outperforms MPNN with radial cutoff (0.657), kNN (0.213), and Voronoi (0.281), and also beats DimeNet++ (0.281), SphereNet (0.661), and ComENet (0.885) with their respective graph constructions. The runtime overhead of adding SCHull is minimal (e.g., 71.4s → 75.8s per epoch for ProNet-Backbone on Fold).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining spherical projection with convex hull construction to produce a graph that simultaneously guarantees sparsity, connectivity, and rigidity — properties that no existing molecular graph construction (radial cutoff, kNN, chemical graphs) achieves together. Theoretically, it draws on classical discrete geometry results (Steinitz's Theorem for planar graph edge bounds, Stoker's Theorem for polyhedral rigidity via edge lengths and dihedral angles) and connects them to modern GNN expressivity analysis (Joshi et al., 2023; Sverdlov & Dym, 2024). The spherical projection step has conceptual roots in symmetry detection (Welzl et al., 1988) and equivariant frame construction (Baker et al., 2024), but the paper repurposes it for graph construction on the original point cloud rather than on projected points. Empirically, SCHull is positioned as a drop-in augmentation to existing GNN architectures (DimeNet, SphereNet, LEFTNet, ProNet, GVP-GNN, MACE, SEGNN) rather than a replacement for the learning framework itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first hyperparameter-free graph construction with provable edge-to-node ratio < 3, guaranteed connectivity, and rigidity (sufficient for 1-layer GNNs to distinguish non-isomorphic generic point clouds) addresses a fundamental gap in molecular GNN practice, particularly for large proteins where radial cutoff graphs become either disconnected or prohibitively dense. The consistent but modest empirical gains (1–5% accuracy on protein classification, small MAE reductions on MD17) with minimal runtime overhead suggest SCHull is a practical, low-risk upgrade for existing pipelines. Open questions include extending theoretical guarantees to non-generic (symmetric) point clouds, handling coplanar configurations, and exploring whether the construction can serve as a foundation for higher-order structures like simplicial complexes. The paper acknowledges that for non-generic cases, empirical evidence is promising but no formal guarantee exists yet.
