## Problem

Action anticipation datasets capture only a subset of valid action orderings, introducing inherent ordering bias that limits model generalization, while noisy inputs from upstream action recognition further degrade prediction quality. No formal sequence augmentation strategy existed for action anticipation that could generate logically and temporally coherent alternative sequences to address both challenges simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253236/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on discrete action label sequences (verb-noun pairs or action class labels) extracted from egocentric videos, not raw frames. Given M consecutive observed actions, it produces top-k predicted next action candidates. The augmentation pipeline expands the training set by generating new (observed sequence, next action) pairs: CTEP proposes alternative next actions beyond the ground truth, and random modifications create perturbed observed sequences, effectively tripling the training data in the paper's configuration.

## Method

ActSeq is a three-step augmentation pipeline that separately augments observed actions and next actions. First, Action Grammar Induction (AGI) decomposes interactions into object-specific atomic actions, computes a skew-symmetric action dependency matrix via thresholded co-occurrence probabilities, and constructs a context-free grammar using AND rules (for dependent actions) and OR rules (for independent actions). Second, a Cross-Tree Earley Parser (CTEP) extends the standard Earley parser to predict next-action candidates across multiple grammar trees simultaneously, handling interleaved multi-goal sequences with selective tree initialization to control search space. Third, Random Modifications (Action Replacement and Action Deletion) are applied to observed sequences to simulate recognition noise. The key design choice is using grammar-constrained generation for next actions (ensuring validity) while using stochastic perturbation for observed actions (ensuring robustness), rather than applying a single uniform augmentation to both.

## Results

On EGTEA Gaze+, ActSeq improves RAFTformer's Top-5 Recall from 63.5% to 66.3% (+2.8), AVT from 62.1% to 64.8% (+2.7), and RULSTM from 58.6% to 60.4% (+1.8). On Epic-Kitchens-100, the best model (MotionFormer) improves from 21.5% to 22.4% Action Top-5 Recall, while RAFTformer gains +1.7 (19.1→20.8). On 50Salads, RAFTformer's Top-1 Accuracy rises from 53.2% to 53.9%. The Mean Action Order Bias (MAOB) drops substantially across all datasets (e.g., 0.32→0.12 on 50Salads, 0.46→0.19 on Epic-Kitchens-100), confirming the method's effectiveness at reducing ordering bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActSeq consistently outperforms all baselines across every dataset and model. Against RAFTformer (Girase et al., CVPR 2023), the strongest baseline, ActSeq yields the largest gains: +2.8 on EGTEA Gaze+ (63.5→66.3), +1.7 on Epic-Kitchens-100 (19.1→20.8), and +0.7 on 50Salads (53.2→53.9). Against AVT (Girdhar & Grauman, ICCV 2021), improvements are +2.7 on EGTEA Gaze+ (62.1→64.8) and +1.7 on 50Salads (48.0→49.7). Against RULSTM (Furnari & Farinella, TPAMI 2020), gains are +1.8 on EGTEA Gaze+ (58.6→60.4) and +1.9 on Epic-Kitchens-100 (14.0→15.9). Against MotionFormer (Patrick et al., NeurIPS 2021), the best Epic-Kitchens-100 model, ActSeq adds +0.9 Action recall (21.5→22.4) and +2.5 Noun recall (46.9→49.4). Against the CNN baseline (Abu Farha et al., CVPR 2018) on 50Salads, the gain is +1.3 (29.8→31.1). In the augmentation-method comparison (Table 6), ActSeq (66.3%) substantially outperforms the EDA-style random modification baseline from Wei & Zou (62.2%) and the simpler 'Next D' heuristic (64.8%), confirming that grammar-guided candidate generation is more effective than unstructured perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: grammar-based activity modeling (Pei et al. 2011, Vo & Bobick 2014, Qi et al. 2020, Gong et al. 2024) and sequence augmentation from NLP (Wei & Zou 2019) and video understanding (Prananta et al. 2022, Falcon et al. 2020). It departs from prior grammar work by introducing a Cross-Tree Earley Parser that handles interleaving goals across multiple dependency matrices, whereas earlier approaches were limited to single-goal or deterministic inputs. It also departs from prior augmentation work by being the first to formally apply sequence augmentation specifically to action anticipation, separating augmentation of observed sequences (for robustness) from augmentation of next actions (for bias reduction), rather than applying uniform random modifications to the entire sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces MAOB as a diagnostic metric for action ordering bias and demonstrates that grammar-guided augmentation can reduce it substantially (e.g., 0.32→0.12 on 50Salads), offering a principled alternative to ad-hoc data augmentation. The low-data result—3 videos per task with ActSeq (64.7%) outperforming full-data without ActSeq (63.5%)—suggests the method could be valuable where annotation is expensive. However, the framework assumes independence between goals when multiple are pursued simultaneously, which breaks down when a single action contributes to overlapping goals; addressing this intertwined case remains open. Additionally, the finding that a simple 'Next D' heuristic captures most of the grammar-based next-action augmentation benefit raises questions about whether the full AGI+CTEP pipeline is necessary in practice.
