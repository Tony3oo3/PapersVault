## Problem

Existing heuristic multimodal RAG methods use fixed, single-pass retrieval processes that suffer from non-adaptive queries (inability to re-retrieve based on intermediate findings) and overloaded queries (concatenating multiple retrieval intents into one ambiguous search). Current knowledge-seeking VQA benchmarks fail to expose these flaws because most questions are solvable with a standard two-step retrieval, masking the rigidity of heuristic mRAGs on real-world dynamic questions.

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image and a bilingual (Chinese/English) text question as input, and produces a free-form text answer. Unlike single-shot retrieval, OmniSearch iteratively consumes intermediate retrieval results (web snippets, similar images with captions) as feedback to plan subsequent actions, with the number of retrieval steps and the choice of tool (web search, image search by image, image search by text) varying per question. The Dyn-VQA benchmark contains 1,452 questions across 9 domains where answers may change rapidly, require external visual knowledge, or demand more than two reasoning hops.

## Method

OmniSearch is a self-adaptive planning agent with three modules: a Planning Agent that decomposes the question into a chain of sub-questions and selects retrieval actions (web search, image search by image, image search by text) based on real-world feedback; a Retriever that executes the chosen API calls; and a Sub-question Solver that summarizes retrieved content and feeds back to the planner. At each step, the planner can pose clarifying sub-questions, refine queries, switch retrieval tools, verify answers, or conclude—mimicking human incremental problem-solving. Two instantiations are built: OmniSearch(G) via prompt engineering on GPT-4V, and OmniSearch(Q) via LoRA instruction fine-tuning of Qwen-VL-Chat on ~13K synthesized planning trajectories (from GPT-4V and InfoSeek data) mixed with general instruction data. The sub-question solver is pluggable, allowing smaller or larger MLLMs to be swapped in for cost control, and the entire pipeline operates as a plug-and-play RAG module compatible with arbitrary backbone MLLMs.

## Results

On Dyn-VQA (1,452 dynamic questions), OmniSearch with GPT-4V achieves 50.03 F1-Recall overall, substantially outperforming the best heuristic two-step mRAG (GPT-4V + image caption retrieval at 37.15) and the strongest commercial generative search engine (Gemini at 37.69). OmniSearch with Qwen-VL-Chat reaches 41.20, still surpassing GPT-4V with two-step heuristic mRAG (37.15). Human performance on Dyn-VQA is 55.12, indicating a remaining gap of ~5 points for the best system. On the most challenging subcategories (fast-changing answers, >2-hop reasoning, external visual knowledge), OmniSearch (GPT-4V) scores 44.04, 49.06, and 49.73 respectively, compared to 17.63, 28.74, and 29.26 for the original GPT-4V.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniSearch (GPT-4V) at 50.03 F1-Recall outperforms all backbone MLLM baselines by large margins: GPT-4V original (30.25), Qwen-VL-Chat (15.28), DeepSeek-VL-7B-Chat (21.31), VisualGLM-6B (16.19), LLaVA-V1.6-Mistral-7B (21.97), mPLUG-Owl2.1 (13.44), and InstructBLIP-Vicuna-7B (7.44). Even the best heuristic two-step mRAG applied to GPT-4V (37.15) trails OmniSearch (GPT-4V) by 12.88 points, confirming that rigid retrieval processes are the bottleneck rather than model capacity. OmniSearch (Qwen-VL-Chat) at 41.20 surpasses GPT-4V with two-step heuristic mRAG (37.15) by 4.05 points, demonstrating that adaptive retrieval planning can compensate for a smaller backbone. Among commercial generative search engines, Gemini (37.69) is the strongest but still falls 12.34 points short of OmniSearch (GPT-4V), primarily due to its inability to ground visual references in the input image. The estimated upper bound (GPT-4V + golden query retrieval) reaches 54.52, showing OmniSearch (GPT-4V) closes 88% of the gap between heuristic mRAG and the oracle retrieval setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mRAG line of work (KAT, Revive, RA-VQA, RA-VQA-v2) by identifying that their fixed, single-pass retrieval pipelines are fundamentally inadequate for real-world questions requiring adaptive, multi-step, multi-modal retrieval. It draws conceptual inspiration from text-domain agentic RAG (ReAct, ReWOO) and Chain-of-Thought reasoning, but departs by introducing a self-adaptive planning agent (OmniSearch) that dynamically decomposes multimodal questions into sub-question chains with tool calls, rather than following a predefined retrieval template. The Dyn-VQA benchmark is positioned as a necessary extension of existing knowledge-seeking VQA datasets (A-OKVQA, InfoSeek, S3VQA), which the authors argue are too static and 2-hop-limited to expose the rigidity flaws of heuristic mRAGs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dyn-VQA provides the first benchmark specifically designed to stress-test mRAG systems on dynamic, multi-modal, and multi-hop questions, filling a gap where prior datasets could be solved by a standard two-step retrieval. The substantial remaining gap between OmniSearch (GPT-4V) and human performance on the three hardest subcategories (fast-changing answers, >2-hop reasoning, external visual knowledge) indicates that adaptive multimodal retrieval is far from solved, pointing to human-like search logic as a key open direction. The finding that commercial generative search engines lack basic multimodal grounding (failing to link pronouns to image objects) suggests that real-world deployment of mRAG requires more than wrapping an LLM around a search API. Practical limitations include the small dataset size (~1.5K), the need for ongoing manual answer updates every three months, and the over-retrieval tendency of GPT-4V-based OmniSearch on easier subdomains.
