## Problem

Existing LLM consistency assessments focus on simple paraphrase invariance and ignore complex logical queries requiring correct handling of negation, conjunction, and disjunction. No benchmark exists for systematically evaluating and improving LLM logical consistency on propositional logic fact-checking over knowledge graphs, leaving a gap in understanding whether LLMs can be trusted with logically structured queries in high-stakes domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250714/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a propositional logic fact (ranging from a single atomic triplet r(u,v) to complex DNF expressions like p∧(q∨r)) paired with a KG context of ~1000 tokens of relevant triplets retrieved via BFS or vector-embedding search, and produces a binary yes/no fact-checking response. Consistency is then scored by checking whether the LLM's binary responses across logically related queries (negated facts, conjunctive/disjunctive decompositions, commutative reorderings) satisfy the semantics of the connecting logical operators, yielding a per-query consistency indicator aggregated over a batch.

## Method

The paper introduces three logical fact-checking datasets (FreebaseLFC, NELLLFC, WikiLFC) that convert KG triplets into (Fact, Context) LLMQuery pairs, and formalizes consistency criteria for DNF facts: an LLM is consistent if its response to a compound fact equals the logical combination of its responses to the constituent atomic facts. For improvement, the authors construct an instruction dataset of primitive logic facts (p, ¬p, p∨q, p∧q) with ground-truth labels determined by whether the fact appears in the retrieved KG context, then apply supervised fine-tuning via QLoRA (4-bit quantized low-rank adaptation, r=16) for 20 epochs. The key insight is that fine-tuning on single-operator facts generalizes to unseen multi-operator facts and logic rules (commutative, distributive, associative, syllogism), attributed to emergent compositional abilities, yielding a 14% average consistency improvement while PEFT is 3× faster than full fine-tuning.

## Results

Across three LLMs (Llama2-13B, Llama2-7B, Gemma-2B) and three KG-based fact-checking datasets, supervised fine-tuning with QLoRA improves average accuracy from 0.69 to 0.88 (+19%) and average logical consistency from 0.74 to 0.88 (+14%). The largest gains appear on conjunction facts (p∧q), where accuracy jumps from as low as 0.26 (Llama2-7B on NELLLFC) to 0.81, and consistency from 0.47 to 0.86. Fine-tuning on FreebaseLFC alone generalizes to unseen datasets (NELLLFC, WikiLFC) and to out-of-distribution complex facts (p∨(q∧r), p∧(q∨r)) and commutative rules, confirming that learning primitive operators transfers to multi-operator logic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external competing methods but instead benchmarks three internal baselines: (1) the base model without fine-tuning, (2) zero-shot instruction prompting, and (3) full fine-tuning (for efficiency). Supervised fine-tuning (QLoRA) consistently outperforms zero-shot instruction prompting on both accuracy and consistency across all three models and datasets — e.g., on Llama2-7B/FreebaseLFC, consistency rises from 0.76 (base) to 0.94 (FT) while instruction prompting drops it to 0.64. Full fine-tuning achieves comparable accuracy but takes 12.52 hours per epoch versus 4.43 hours for QLoRA-based PEFT (≈3× speedup). On out-of-distribution complex facts (p∨(q∧r), p∧(q∨r)) and commutative rules, fine-tuned Llama2-13B improves consistency from 0.63–0.85 to 0.79–0.98, while accuracy on p∧(q∨r) jumps from 0.53 to 0.82 on FreebaseLFC. The one notable exception is disjunctive facts (p∨q), where fine-tuning sometimes slightly reduces consistency (e.g., Llama2-7B on FreebaseLFC: 0.83→0.77), suggesting that the model over-corrects on OR queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM consistency assessment literature (Elazar et al. 2021; Kuhn et al. 2023), which has focused on paraphrase-based invariance, to a new dimension: logical consistency under primitive operators (negation, conjunction, disjunction) in the specific setting of KG-grounded fact-checking. It repurposes the RAG paradigm (Lewis et al. 2020) not merely for accuracy but as a controlled context for isolating logical reasoning, and applies QLoRA-based supervised fine-tuning (Dettmers et al. 2024) to a task—logical consistency in fact-checking—where prior fine-tuning work (Wei et al. 2021; Li & Liang 2021) has not been applied. The authors explicitly position their work as the first to systematically measure and improve propositional-logic consistency of LLMs with KG contexts, carving out a gap between paraphrase-consistency research and KG fact-checking research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first logical fact-checking benchmarks (FreebaseLFC, NELLLFC, WikiLFC) and a formal consistency metric for propositional logic queries, giving the community a concrete tool to evaluate whether LLMs respect basic logical laws—a prerequisite for trustworthy deployment in high-stakes domains. A key open question is whether the binary-response setting generalizes: the authors acknowledge that multi-class, non-binary, and natural-language logical queries remain unaddressed, as do incomplete KGs requiring link prediction. The finding that zero-shot prompting degrades consistency (rather than improving it) suggests that logical consistency may require weight-level updates rather than prompt-level interventions, a hypothesis with broader implications for how we think about LLM reliability.
