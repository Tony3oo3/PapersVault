## Problem

Aligning pre-trained diffusion models with specific rewards (e.g., aesthetic quality, human preference) is a central challenge in text-to-image generation. Fine-tuning methods (RL, direct backpropagation) achieve high target rewards but suffer from reward over-optimization, mode collapse, and loss of diversity, while training-free guidance methods (DPS, FreeDoM, MPGD) under-optimize rewards due to inexact Tweedie-based score approximations. No existing approach simultaneously achieves strong reward optimization, preserves sample diversity, and generalizes to unseen rewards without additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a differentiable reward function (e.g., LAION aesthetic score, PickScore, CLIPScore), and a frozen pre-trained diffusion model (SD v1.5 or SDXL) as input, and produces a set of reward-aligned latent images at test time. It operates entirely in the latent space of the diffusion model using N particles (as few as 4 in experiments), with no gradient updates to the diffusion model weights. The reward must be differentiable with respect to the latent representation for the guidance step, though non-differentiable rewards can be handled by learning a differentiable surrogate online.

## Method

DAS (Diffusion Alignment as Sampling) formulates reward alignment as sampling from the KL-regularized target distribution p_tar ∝ p_pre · exp(r/α) using a Sequential Monte Carlo sampler tailored to the reverse diffusion process. At each diffusion step, it maintains N particles with weights, proposing new candidates from a Gaussian proposal that incorporates the reward gradient (a locally optimal proposal minimizing weight variance), and resamples adaptively when the effective sample size drops below a threshold. The key design choice is a tempered sequence of intermediate targets π_t ∝ p_t · exp(λ_t · r̂(x_t)/α) where the inverse temperature λ_t ramps from 0 at the noisiest step to 1 at the cleanest step (using λ_t = (1+γ)^t − 1), which the paper proves theoretically reduces asymptotic variance and empirically prevents over-optimization and off-manifold drift. Backward kernels are defined via Bayes' rule from the pre-trained stochastic diffusion sampler, and the algorithm uses Srinivasan Sampling Process for low-variance resampling. The paper provides formal proofs of asymptotic exactness and a CLT-based variance bound showing tempering strictly improves sample efficiency over untempered SMC.

## Results

In the online black-box optimization setting (Table 1), DAS-UCB achieves an aesthetic score of 6.77 with ImageReward of 1.25 and LPIPS diversity of 0.66, compared to SEIKO-UCB's 6.88 aesthetic but -0.31 ImageReward and 0.51 LPIPS. DAS-Bootstrap similarly scores 6.73 aesthetic with 1.27 ImageReward and 0.65 LPIPS versus SEIKO-Bootstrap's 6.51 aesthetic, -1.01 ImageReward, and 0.49 LPIPS. In the single-reward experiments (Figure 2), DAS consistently occupies the upper-right quadrant, achieving comparable or superior target rewards to fine-tuning methods (DDPO, AlignProp) while maintaining cross-reward generalization (HPSv2, ImageReward) and diversity (TCE, MPD) on par with guidance methods (DPS, FreeDoM, MPGD). The paper reports up to 20% improvement over the pre-trained model in both target and unseen rewards in the online setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAS outperforms all fine-tuning baselines (DDPO, AlignProp, TDPO, DiffusionDPO) on cross-reward generalization and diversity while matching or exceeding their target rewards, directly addressing the reward over-optimization problem that causes these methods to collapse in diversity and degrade unseen reward scores. Among guidance methods (DPS, FreeDoM, MPGD), DAS achieves substantially higher target rewards (aesthetic score and PickScore) while preserving similar levels of diversity and generalization, overcoming the reward under-optimization inherent to Tweedie-based approximate guidance. In the online setting, DAS matches SEIKO on target aesthetic scores (6.77 vs. 6.88 for UCB; 6.73 vs. 6.51 for Bootstrap) but dramatically outperforms it on unseen rewards (ImageReward: 1.25 vs. -0.31 for UCB; 1.27 vs. -1.01 for Bootstrap) and diversity (LPIPS: 0.66 vs. 0.51; 0.65 vs. 0.49), demonstrating that SMC-based sampling preserves the pre-trained model's general capabilities where direct backpropagation fine-tuning causes mode collapse. In multi-objective optimization, DAS achieves a new Pareto front between CLIPScore and aesthetic score without any fine-tuning, outperforming interpolated LoRA weights from separately trained DDPO/AlignProp models.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAS repurposes Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) sampling — a classical Bayesian inference tool — for the diffusion alignment problem, where prior work has treated alignment as either a fine-tuning objective (DDPO, AlignProp, TDPO) or a single-particle guidance correction (DPS, FreeDoM, MPGD). The target distribution formulation (p_tar ∝ p_pre · exp(r/α)) is inherited directly from the DPO/KL-regularized reward framework, but DAS departs from both fine-tuning (which suffers mode-seeking via reverse KL) and approximate guidance (which is inexact due to Tweedie's formula errors) by framing alignment as a multi-particle sampling problem with tempering. The key conceptual departure is that the diffusion model is never modified; instead, the SMC sampler navigates the reward-aligned distribution at test time, with tempering parameters λ_t controlling how aggressively the reward signal is applied at each denoising step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAS offers a practical, training-free path to reward alignment that sidesteps the fundamental over-optimization/under-optimization trade-off plaguing existing methods, which is significant for practitioners who need to adapt a single pre-trained model to many different rewards without retraining. The theoretical guarantees (asymptotic exactness and provable variance reduction from tempering) provide a principled foundation absent in heuristic guidance methods. Limitations include the requirement for differentiable rewards (mitigated but not eliminated by surrogate models), the computational overhead of multiple particles per generation, and evaluation primarily on SD v1.5/SDXL with 2D image rewards. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video diffusion, 3D generation, or very large latent spaces where particle counts become prohibitive, and whether the tempering schedule can be made fully adaptive without the current heuristic γ parameter.
