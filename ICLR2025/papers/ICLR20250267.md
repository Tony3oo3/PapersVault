## Problem

Reconstructing the 3D distribution of dark matter from weak gravitational lensing is inherently ill-posed: galaxies are observed from a single viewpoint, and the unknown intrinsic shapes of source galaxies introduce shape noise that exceeds the lensing signal by one to two orders of magnitude. Existing 3D mass-mapping methods (Wiener filtering, sparsity priors) impose strong Gaussian or halo-based assumptions that smooth out or miss non-Gaussian structures, which are among the most scientifically important features of the cosmic web.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a galaxy catalog containing sky positions, redshifts, and observed (and optionally kinematically estimated) intrinsic ellipticities, typically on the order of hundreds of thousands to millions of galaxies over a 5°×5° field. The output is a continuous 3D matter overdensity field δ(x) parameterized by a neural network, sampled at arbitrary spherical coordinates (θ₁, θ₂, w) rather than a fixed voxel grid, with the final reconstruction obtained as the median over 100 independently initialized networks to mitigate the ill-posedness of single-view tomography.

## Method

The matter overdensity field is represented as a coordinate-based MLP with positional encoding (Fourier features at L=2 for angular and L=5 for radial coordinates), which implicitly imposes a tunable smoothness prior via spectral bias. Training follows an analysis-by-synthesis paradigm: a fully differentiable physics forward model computes convergence (a weighted line-of-sight integral) and then shear (a complex convolution) from the neural field's output, and the network weights are optimized via Adam to minimize a χ² data loss matching predicted shears to observed ellipticities, plus a power-spectrum regularization loss that prevents overfitting to shape noise. A deep ensemble of 100 four-layer MLPs (256 units, ReLU) is trained independently, and the median across ensemble members yields the final reconstruction while also providing calibrated uncertainty estimates. This approach avoids the hard Gaussian prior of Wiener filtering, enabling accurate recovery of non-Gaussian structures such as sharp density peaks that the baseline method smears or mislocalizes.

## Results

The method is evaluated using normalized cross-correlation (CC) against ground truth, both unblurred and with optimal Gaussian blurring, across three experimental settings. On kinematic weak lensing, the neural field achieves CC=0.60 (unblurred) and CC=0.80 (blurred), compared to 0.39 and 0.70 for the Wiener filter. On traditional weak lensing (higher shape noise), it reaches CC=0.52 and 0.77 versus 0.34 and 0.61. On the non-Gaussian MNIST toy experiment, it scores CC=0.61 and 0.86 versus 0.52 and 0.68, with the largest gap in the blurred metric (0.86 vs. 0.68), indicating superior recovery of non-Gaussian structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Wiener filter from Simon et al. (2009), which employs a Gaussian prior based on the dark matter power spectrum and has been the most widely used 3D mass mapping method on real survey data. The neural field method outperforms the Wiener filter on every metric across all three experimental settings. The advantage is most pronounced in the non-Gaussian MNIST experiment, where the blurred CC gap reaches 0.18 (0.86 vs. 0.68), reflecting the Wiener filter's inability to reconstruct sharp, non-Gaussian structures due to its Gaussian prior. In the kinematic weak lensing setting, the unblurred CC gap is 0.21 (0.60 vs. 0.39), and the paper further shows that the optimal radial blur width for the neural field reconstruction is significantly smaller, indicating better localization of structures along the line of sight. In the traditional weak lensing setting (roughly 2.6× more aggregate shape noise), the method still maintains a clear advantage (CC 0.52 vs. 0.34 unblurred), suggesting applicability to existing survey data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural-field approach to 3D dark matter mass mapping from Zhao et al. (2024), which optimized a neural field against simulated lensing images but struggled under shape noise, by instead fitting directly to estimated galaxy shear measurements in the noise-dominated weak lensing regime. It departs from the dominant Wiener filter baseline (Simon et al. 2009), which imposes a Gaussian prior on the matter field, by using a coordinate-based MLP with positional encoding as an implicit smoothness prior that does not assume Gaussianity. The analysis-by-synthesis optimization through a fully differentiable lensing forward model, combined with a deep ensemble of 100 independently initialized networks for robustness, draws on the broader coordinate-based neural fields literature for ill-posed inverse problems (Tancik et al. 2020, Levis et al. 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Accurate 3D dark matter maps are essential for localizing structures relevant to open questions in fundamental physics (e.g., dark matter gamma-ray emission, primordial non-Gaussianity), yet prior methods are limited by their Gaussian priors and heavy line-of-sight smoothing. The demonstrated ability to recover non-Gaussian features (MNIST digits) and better localize structures radially suggests the method could unlock analyses of real survey data that Gaussian-prior methods cannot perform. Key limitations include evaluation solely on simulations (no real data), a single N-body seed in the main results, and the toy MNIST experiment as the only non-Gaussian test; scaling to the full sky and millions of galaxies in upcoming surveys like Roman remains an open challenge.
