## Problem

Reinforcement learning agents struggle with long-horizon exploration and world model learning, particularly in environments with rich object interactions where pixel-level methods require orders of magnitude more samples. This paper asks whether providing an agent with an object-centric abstraction (a set of items and their attributes) enables more efficient exploration and world model learning, and shows that the answer is yes when combined with a discriminative (rather than generative) forward model and hierarchical temporal abstraction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250429/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The forward model f_θ takes an abstract state X (an N×600 matrix of item identity and attribute embeddings, each 300-dim) and a behaviour b (a 600-dim vector specifying which item to change and to what attribute), and outputs a single scalar success probability in [0,1]. At inference time, Dijkstra's algorithm over the learned model graph produces a sequence of abstract behaviours (item-attribute changes) that maximizes the probability of reaching a goal state. The raw observations (glyph grids, inventory, messages in MiniHack; word-embedding grids in 2D Crafting) are mapped to the abstract state via a hand-crafted or supervised encoder, and low-level policies execute each behaviour as a sequence of primitive actions.

## Method

MEAD defines an Abstracted Item-Attribute MDP (Ab-MDP) that provides both state abstraction (objects with identities and attributes) and temporal abstraction (transitions occur only when the abstract state changes or k low-level steps elapse). The key architectural choice is a discriminative forward model: rather than generatively predicting the full next-state distribution, it predicts only whether a specific item-attribute change proposed by a behaviour will succeed, reducing the problem to binary classification. The model uses a key-query attention mechanism where item embeddings (processed through a 2-layer MLP) form keys and values, and the behaviour embedding serves as the query, producing a single logit for success probability. Training uses binary cross-entropy on empirically counted success rates, with a count-based intrinsic reward and MCTS for exploration planning during training, and Dijkstra's algorithm (with edge weights of −log f_θ) for goal-directed planning at inference time. This discriminative inductive bias yields significantly better sample efficiency than generative baselines (Dreamer-v3, MuZero) while producing an interpretable world model of which item-attribute transitions are possible.

## Results

MEAD achieves approximately one order of magnitude sample efficiency improvement over Dreamer-v3 (the strongest model-based baseline) on both 2D Crafting and MiniHack environments, while PPO requires roughly two orders of magnitude more data (hundreds of thousands vs. ~10 million low-level steps). On MiniHack, all low-level SOTA exploration methods (IMPALA, RND, ICM) fail to solve the tasks even after 50 million steps, whereas the least efficient Ab-MDP method still outperforms them. In the compositional planning environment (requiring precise multi-step abstract behaviour sequences), MEAD succeeds the majority of the time while all other methods remain stuck at 0 reward. For transfer, MEAD exhibits strong zero-shot performance to new object types, while Dreamer-v3 and PPO show negative transfer without privileged reward information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEAD outperforms Dreamer-v3 (Hafner et al., 2023) by approximately one order of magnitude in sample efficiency on both 2D Crafting and MiniHack, and decisively wins on transfer: Dreamer-v3 shows negative transfer from sandbox pre-training without privileged reward information, while MEAD achieves good zero-shot performance. Against MuZero (Schrittwieser et al., 2020), MEAD is more sample-efficient, as MuZero's online MCTS planning is less efficient in this setting. PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) reaches comparable peak performance but requires ~100x more data, and also exhibits negative transfer. All low-level SOTA exploration methods from Henaff et al. (2022) (IMPALA, RND, ICM, E3B) are outperformed by even the weakest Ab-MDP method, highlighting the value of the object-centric abstraction. Against the non-parametric attribute planner of Zhang et al. (2018), MEAD's parametric model demonstrates better sample efficiency and generalization to new objects. The NCS method of Chang et al. (2023) (k-means clustering with greedy planning) fails completely in 2D Crafting and underperforms MEAD in MiniHack, while a hybrid k-means + Dijkstra variant works in MiniHack but still trails MEAD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper most directly extends Zhang et al. (2018) 'Composable planning with attributes,' replacing their fixed binary attribute set with a flexible object-centric abstraction (the Ab-MDP) and their non-parametric transition table with a parametric discriminative neural model. The Ab-MDP is positioned within the Options Framework (Sutton et al., 1999) but departs from bottom-up option discovery by using top-down prior knowledge in the form of object perception to define behaviours as single item-attribute perturbations. The discriminative world model (predicting success probability of a specific change rather than the full next-state distribution) is a key departure from generative model-based RL methods like Dreamer-v3 and MuZero, which the paper contrasts empirically. The work also relates to Factored MDPs, OO-MDPs, and Neural Constraint Satisfaction (Chang et al., 2023), but differs by requiring no pre-specified interaction rules or class relations, learning transition dynamics purely data-driven through an attention-based network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple discriminative objective (binary cross-entropy on item-attribute change success) combined with object-centric abstraction yields substantially better sample efficiency than generative world models, and enables zero-shot transfer to unseen object types by clustering on shared attributes. The compositional planning result—where MEAD solves a long-horizon task that all baselines (with and without pre-training) fail entirely—suggests the Ab-MDP structure is particularly well-suited to tasks requiring precise multi-step sequences. The main open question is unsupervised discovery of the object-centric mapping, which currently requires labelled data; the authors note that segmentation models and visual-language models are moving toward making this feasible. The framework is limited to discrete attributes, though the authors argue this does not restrict the low-level policies.
