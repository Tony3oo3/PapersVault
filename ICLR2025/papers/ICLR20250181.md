## Problem

When LLMs learn new information via gradient updates, individual samples can cause the model to inappropriately apply that knowledge in unrelated contexts—a 'priming' effect that drives hallucination and undermines safe continual learning. How much a given new text will disrupt existing knowledge, and whether this disruption is predictable before training, remained uncharacterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250181/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each experiment inserts a single Outlandish text sample (a context prefix followed by one of 12 keywords across 4 themes: colors, places, jobs, foods) into one slot of a size-8 minibatch for 20–40 consecutive steps during Alpaca instruction fine-tuning or Wikipedia continued pre-training. After learning, the model is queried on a battery of thematic prefixes sharing the keyword's theme but unrelated to the original context; the priming score is the expected ratio of post- to pre-learning keyword token probability across these prefixes, while the memorization score is the analogous ratio on the original context. The key predictive signal is the keyword's token probability measured before any learning occurs.

## Method

The central finding is that pre-learning keyword token probability robustly predicts post-learning priming across PALM-2, Gemma, and Llama at multiple sizes and training stages, with a threshold near 10⁻³ below which 'surprising' keywords cause substantial priming. To mitigate priming, the paper introduces two interventions: (1) a 'stepping-stone' text augmentation that rewrites the sample to introduce intermediate related terms before the keyword, raising its pre-learning probability and reducing priming by 50–75% while preserving memorization; and (2) an 'ignore-topk' gradient pruning strategy that, contrary to standard sparsification, zeros out the top-K% (K=8%) of parameter updates rather than keeping them, decimating priming by ~96% in PALM-2 while leaving memorization and generic language performance intact. The Outlandish dataset (1320 samples, 11 categories spanning real facts to random word permutations) was specifically designed to provide controlled, diverse contexts for measuring this probability-priming relationship.

## Results

The paper's central finding is that the degree of priming (S_prime) after gradient-based learning is robustly predicted by the keyword token probability measured *before* learning, with a threshold near 10⁻³ separating 'surprising' contexts (high priming) from 'unsurprising' ones (minimal priming). This relationship holds across PALM-2, Gemma-2b, and Llama-7b, across model sizes (XS, S), and across training stages (pretraining, FLAN fine-tuning). For mitigation, the 'stepping-stone' text augmentation reduces median priming by 75% (PALM-2) and 50% (Gemma-2b, Llama-7b), while the 'ignore-topk' (8%) gradient pruning reduces median priming by 96% (PALM-2), ~70% (Gemma-2b), and ~50% (Llama-7b), in all cases preserving memorization (S_mem) and not degrading generic Wikipedia next-word prediction performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external SOTA methods but instead compares its two proposed mitigation strategies against each other and against simpler alternatives. Among text augmentation strategies, stepping-stone outperforms both rewrite/rearrangement (à la Allen-Zhu & Li, 2023) and consequence augmentation (à la Golovneva et al., 2024) in reducing priming, achieving a 75% median reduction in PALM-2 versus smaller reductions for the other two (Fig. 29). For gradient pruning, the counterintuitive 'ignore-topk' strategy (discarding the largest 8% of parameter updates) dramatically outperforms the standard 'keep-topk' approach (retaining the top 15%), which preserves both memorization and priming unchanged — confirming that the largest updates are precisely those driving spurious priming. Cross-model comparisons show the keyword-probability-to-priming relationship is robust across PALM-2, Gemma-2b, and Llama-7b despite different architectures and training mixtures, though the coupling between memorization and priming dynamics differs (coupled in PALM-2, decoupled in Llama and Gemma, Fig. 4). In-context learning of Outlandish samples produces a much weaker probability-priming relationship than in-weight learning (Fig. 22), suggesting the effect is specific to gradient-based parameter updates. The Outlandish dataset itself is shown to occupy a broader range of natural-language richness and priming severity than the CounterFact dataset (Fig. 19), justifying its use for this study.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as complementary to the interpretability and knowledge-editing literature (Meng et al., Hase et al., Nanda et al., Geva et al.), which has focused on architectural and algorithmic factors governing how LLMs store and retrieve knowledge. Here, the authors shift the lens to the training data itself, asking how the statistical properties of individual samples (specifically keyword token probability) determine the collateral 'priming' effects on unrelated knowledge. The ignore-topk pruning strategy is a deliberate inversion of the TIES-MERGE trimming procedure (Yadav et al., 2023), which keeps the top-k updates; instead, this paper removes them, revealing that the largest gradient updates are precisely those driving unwanted generalization. The priming formalization draws on experimental psychology, and the surprisal-based learning dynamics parallel findings in computational neuroscience (McClelland et al., Wagatsuma et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, pre-training diagnostic (keyword probability) that predicts how much a new sample will contaminate unrelated knowledge, enabling practitioners to screen training data before insertion. The two mitigation strategies—stepping-stone augmentation and ignore-topk pruning—offer simple, architecture-agnostic tools to reduce priming by 50–95% without sacrificing memorization, with potential implications for continual learning, data poisoning defense, and safe model updates. However, the mechanism behind the probability-priming relationship remains unexplained, the Outlandish dataset (1320 samples, 12 keywords) is small relative to natural language diversity, and the study is limited to vanilla gradient-based learning rather than state-of-the-art knowledge injection methods. The connection between ignore-topk and differential privacy clipping is noted but left unexplored, representing a promising open direction.
