## Problem

While self-attention is widely accepted as crucial for encoding causal structure in sequences, the precise mechanism by which causal Transformers learn to predict the next token under a context-dependent generating function f remains unclear. Prior work demonstrated that Transformers can implement single-step gradient descent for in-context learning, but how they solve general autoregressive prediction—where f varies per sequence and must be inferred from the context alone—was an open question.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253133/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an autoregressive sequence (x_1, ..., x_t) of unit-norm vectors in R^d, generated by a hidden context-dependent function f in a vector-valued RKHS, and produces an estimate of x_{t+1}. The raw tokens are first converted into augmented tokens e_t^0 ∈ R^{4d+2} that encode successive pairs (x_{t-1}, x_t) via a one-layer Transformer with dot-product positional encoding, and the final prediction is extracted by projecting the last d coordinates of the iterated token representation.

## Method

The core contribution is an explicit construction showing that a stack of identical two-head causal Transformer layers with residual connections implements a 'causal kernel descent' algorithm in its forward pass. This method modifies standard least-squares gradient descent to be causal—each prediction u_t depends only on past observations x_{1:t}—while preserving the parallelization benefits of the attention mechanism. The authors provide explicit weight matrices for three kernel/attention configurations (linear attention with dot-product kernel, exponential attention, and softmax attention with exponential kernel) and prove that the prediction error converges to zero as t → ∞ for linear mappings (via spectral radius arguments) and periodic sequences (via Kaczmarz algorithm effectiveness in Hilbert spaces). The infinite-depth limit admits a neural ODE interpretation, and experiments show that fine-tuning a finite-depth (n=6) model outperforms the infinite-depth construction for moderate sequence lengths.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: Theorem 1 proves that an explicitly constructed two-head causal Transformer predicts x_{t+1} with error → 0 as t → ∞ for three specific instances (linear f with dot-product kernel, linear f with exponential kernel, and periodic sequences with exponential kernel). Convergence is exponential in t for instances (1) and (3), and for almost all x_1 and W in instance (1). Experimentally, the fine-tuned 6-layer model (d=15, T=100, 2^12 samples) outperforms both the untrained constructed model and the infinite-depth limit model M when t ≳ 20, demonstrating that finite-depth Transformers can surpass the theoretical infinite-depth construction in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper without standard baseline comparisons against other architectures or methods. The internal empirical comparisons focus on three variants of the constructed model: (i) the untrained explicit construction M_{θ_0}^n, (ii) the fine-tuned model M_{θ_*}^n (n=6 layers, Adam, 5000 epochs on MSE loss), and (iii) the infinite-depth limit M (n→∞). The key finding is that the fine-tuned 6-layer model significantly outperforms the untrained construction and, notably, surpasses the infinite-depth model M when sequence length t ≳ 20 (Figure 5). The causal kernel descent method is also validated on a non-linear instance (4) involving complex rotations with q=2, d=4, where convergence to zero is observed empirically, suggesting generalizability beyond the theoretically proven cases. No comparison against RNNs, state-space models, or other sequential architectures is provided, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work showing that Transformers implement optimization algorithms in their forward pass (Von Oswald et al., 2023a; Cheng et al., 2024) by moving from single-step gradient descent on input-output pairs to multi-step causal kernel descent on autoregressive sequences. It generalizes Sander et al. (2024), which was restricted to linear Transformers, to cover linear, exponential, and softmax attention, and introduces a key departure: the descent must be causal (each prediction uses only past observations), which is enforced through a lower-triangular attention structure. The connection to the Kaczmarz algorithm in Hilbert spaces for proving convergence is a new analytical tool not present in prior in-context learning theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable construction showing that a fixed, explicitly parameterized Transformer can predict the next token of an autoregressive sequence with vanishing error as context length grows, grounding the empirical success of LLMs in a concrete optimization interpretation. However, the guarantees hold only for very restricted settings (linear maps with orthogonal matrices, or periodic sequences), and the sequences are deterministic—noisy dynamics, general nonlinear f, and the comparison to RNNs or state-space models remain open. The finding that a fine-tuned 6-layer model outperforms the infinite-depth theoretical construction suggests practical relevance, but the gap between the theory's restricted instances and real language modeling is substantial.
