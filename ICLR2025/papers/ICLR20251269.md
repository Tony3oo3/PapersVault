## Problem

Training goal-conditioned GFlowNets (GC-GFlowNets) is hampered by extremely sparse binary rewards and limited trajectory coverage, causing agents to get trapped in constrained distributions and fail to scale to high-dimensional problems. Existing approaches like Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) and OC-GAFN only relabel or augment observed trajectories, which is insufficient when the state space is large and the number of distinct goal-reaching paths grows exponentially. This paper addresses how to efficiently generate diverse, high-quality training signals for GC-GFlowNets without requiring dense rewards or extensive environment interaction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a goal state y sampled from the goal space and a task specification (DAG structure, state/action spaces, and a mapping function φ) as input, with the goal conditioning both the forward policy P_F(·|s,y,θ) and backward policy P_B(·|s',y,θ). It produces a complete trajectory in the DAG from the initial state s₀ to a terminal state x that matches the goal (i.e., ‖φ(x)−y‖≤ε), yielding a compositional object such as a sequence, path, or set. The reward is strictly binary (1 if the goal is reached, 0 otherwise), and the method operates in both online (interactive) and offline (fixed dataset) settings, with the offline variant synthesizing new trajectories purely from the learned backward policy without further environment access.

## Method

Retrospective Backward Synthesis (RBS) augments forward trajectories collected by P_F with synthesized backward trajectories generated by sampling from the backward policy P_B starting at the goal state y and walking backward to s₀; the reversed trajectory always reaches the goal, converting failed experiences into successful ones and introducing entirely novel paths. Three supporting techniques stabilize and enhance training: (1) age-based sampling assigns highest priority to newly added experiences and decays it to zero after learning, balancing exploration of fresh data with exploitation of learned knowledge; (2) backward policy regularization penalizes KL divergence between P_B and a uniform distribution to prevent P_B from collapsing into a deterministic policy and maintain diversity of synthesized trajectories; (3) intensified reward feedback scales the terminal reward by a large coefficient C in the loss, which amplifies the gradient of P_B by log(CR(x,y)) and strengthens reward signal propagation through long trajectories. For extremely large state spaces (e.g., 20⁵⁰ AMP sequences), a hierarchical variant decomposes the goal into shorter sub-sequence sub-goals, trains separate sub-level policies, and concatenates their outputs.

## Results

On GridWorld (H=32/64/128), RBS-GFN achieves ~100% success rate across all scales, while OC-GAFN degrades to ~60% on the large grid and GFN w/ HER drops to ~20%. On bit sequence generation (small/medium/large), RBS-GFN reaches ~100% success rate with fast convergence, whereas OC-GAFN struggles and all other baselines completely fail. On the AMP generation task (20^50 state space), Hier-RBS-GFN achieves ~100% success rate while all baselines fail entirely. In the offline GridWorld setting, RBS-GFN attains nearly 100% success rates across all grid sizes, while baselines decline significantly as problem size increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RBS-GFN decisively outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark. Against OC-GAFN (Pan et al., 2023a), the strongest baseline, RBS-GFN gains approximately 15 percentage points on unseen-goal generalization (~100% vs. ~85%) and achieves ~100% on unseen obstacles where OC-GAFN drops to 9%. On bit sequence generation, RBS-GFN reaches ~100% success rate at all scales while OC-GAFN struggles even on the small task and all other baselines (GFN w/ HER, DQN w/ HER) completely fail. Against GFN w/ HER (Andrychowicz et al., 2017), RBS-GFN's advantage grows with problem complexity: on the large GridWorld (H=128), RBS-GFN maintains ~100% while GFN w/ HER degrades to ~20%. DQN w/ HER (Mnih et al., 2013) is consistently the weakest baseline, failing entirely on unseen obstacle maps and showing poor scalability. In the offline setting, vanilla GFN (Bengio et al., 2021) without data augmentation performs worst, confirming that RBS's backward trajectory synthesis is critical for leveraging limited offline data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the goal-conditioned GFlowNet (GC-GFlowNet) formulation introduced by Pan et al. (2023a) and Roy et al. (2023), which themselves generalize the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021) to conditional generation. The key conceptual departure is from Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) in goal-conditioned RL: rather than relabeling achieved states as goals, RBS exploits the backward policy P_B (whose freedom is established in Bengio et al., 2023) to synthesize entirely new successful trajectories from the commanded goal, transforming failed forward rollouts into positive-reward training data. The method also introduces task-specific stabilization techniques—backward policy regularization via KL divergence and intensified reward feedback—that have no direct analogue in the GCRL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sparse-reward problem in goal-conditioned generation is a fundamental bottleneck that limits the applicability of GFlowNets to practical tasks like molecular and peptide design; RBS addresses this with a simple, model-internal mechanism that requires no additional learned components. The ~100% success rate on AMP generation (20^50 state space) where all baselines fail demonstrates that the approach can handle extreme combinatorial complexity, though this relies on a hierarchical decomposition that may not generalize to tasks without natural sub-goal structure. Open questions include extension to continuous state spaces, environments with unknown dynamics (requiring a learned backward model), and whether the intensified reward coefficient C (scaling up to 10^40) reveals a fundamental credit-assignment pathology in long-horizon GFlowNet training that warrants deeper theoretical understanding.
