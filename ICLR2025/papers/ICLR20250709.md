## Problem

Preference optimization methods like DPO and PPO for reasoning tasks (math, coding) depend on high-quality human-verified labels to construct preference pairs, but such datasets are limited and increasingly expensive to scale. Existing pseudo-labeling approaches based on self-consistency are restricted to single-answer tasks and cannot generalize to multi-test-case problems like code generation, leaving a gap for scalable, annotation-free preference learning across diverse reasoning domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250709/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes reasoning problem prompts (math word problems or coding challenges) and produces preference pairs of solutions for DPO training. For single-test-case tasks (math), the pseudo feedback is a single answer extracted from a frontier LLM solution or majority-voted from the policy model's own samples. For multi-test-case tasks (coding), the pseudo feedback consists of K input-output test case pairs, where inputs are generated by a general LLM and outputs are determined by majority voting over executed solutions. The preference pair construction uses relaxed criteria (r_w ≥ ε and r_w − r_l > σ) rather than requiring the winning solution to pass all test cases, accommodating noise in pseudo labels.

## Method

PFPO (Pseudo-Feedback Preference Optimization) unifies solution verification across task types by framing labeling as evaluation against test cases, then generates pseudo feedback through two complementary mechanisms: (1) frontier LLM feedback, where for coding the pipeline decomposes into generating test inputs via a general LLM, generating solutions via a frontier LLM (e.g., GPT-4o), and determining pseudo outputs via majority voting over executed solutions; and (2) self-consistency feedback, where the policy model itself samples multiple solutions and uses test-case-level majority voting to establish pseudo outputs, extending self-consistency beyond single-answer settings to multi-test-case problems. Preference pairs are constructed at both the outcome level (full solutions) and the step level (solution prefixes scored by expected returns of sampled completions, following pDPO). The training proceeds iteratively—frontier LLM feedback first, then self-consistency feedback—with the reference model updated from the previous iteration's policy, enabling continuous self-improvement without additional human annotation.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning, Mathstral-7B with PFPO-Self Iter.2 achieves 68.6 on MATH (up from 58.3 base), 90.3 on GSM8K (up from 85.6), and 42.3 on College Math (up from 34.3), surpassing NuminaMath-72B-CoT (67.1/91.7/39.8) and GPT-4-Turbo-1106-preview (64.3) on MATH. On code generation, Deepseek-coder-7B-v1.5 with PFPO-Self Iter.2 reaches 24.3 on LiveCodeBench (up from 21.1) and 19.1 on APPs Overall (up from 15.4 after SFT), surpassing Claude-3-Haiku (24.0) on LiveCodeBench. Iterative self-consistency training yields consistent gains across 3–5 iterations before saturating, with the largest single-iteration jump occurring at Iter.0 (frontier LLM feedback) followed by diminishing returns from self-consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mathstral-7B w/ PFPO-Self Iter.2 (68.6 MATH) outperforms NuminaMath-72B-CoT (67.1), Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (68.1), and GPT-4-Turbo-1106-preview (64.3) on MATH, and beats NuminaMath-72B (39.8) and Llama-3.1-70B (41.8) on College Math, despite having only 7B parameters. However, it trails NuminaMath-72B-CoT on GSM8K (90.3 vs. 91.7) and Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (95.5) by a wider margin. On code generation, the best model (PFPO-Self Iter.2) achieves 24.3 on LiveCodeBench, narrowly surpassing Claude-3-Haiku (24.0) and GPT-3.5-Turbo-0125 (24.0), but remains well below Claude-3.5-Sonnet (51.3) and DeepSeekCoder-V2-236B (41.9). On APPs Overall, the method reaches 19.1, below Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (24.9) and GPT-4o (34.0), though it improves substantially over the base Deepseek-coder-7B (14.3). On HumanEval and MBPP, the method does not show consistent gains over the SFT baseline after introducing xCodeEval, likely due to distribution shift from standard I/O-only problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as its optimization backbone and extends self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) from single-answer majority voting to a multi-test-case setting for code generation. It also incorporates pDPO (Jiao et al., 2024) for step-level process preference data. The key conceptual departure from prior work is reframing solution labeling as test-case evaluation, which unifies single-answer tasks (math) and multi-test-case tasks (coding) under one verification framework and eliminates the need for human-verified labels that prior preference optimization methods (DPO, PPO) require. This positions the work beyond scalable oversight (Bowman et al., 2022) and debating-based labeling (Khan et al., 2024a), which still rely on some human or frontier-LLM supervision, by showing that fully synthetic pseudo feedback suffices for iterative self-improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: removing the human annotation bottleneck for preference optimization in verifiable reasoning domains (math, coding) makes large-scale iterative training feasible without domain experts. The finding that a 7B model can surpass 72B-parameter models and GPT-4-Turbo through iterative pseudo-feedback DPO suggests that data quality and training methodology can compensate for model scale in reasoning tasks. Key limitations include performance saturation after a few iterations (due to test-case quality plateau and diminishing moderately-difficult problems), and the approach is restricted to tasks with verifiable outputs. Open questions include whether combining pseudo reward models with scaling inference (best-of-N, lookahead search) yields further gains, and whether the framework extends to non-verifiable reasoning tasks.
