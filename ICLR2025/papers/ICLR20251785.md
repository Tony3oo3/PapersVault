## Problem

Existing deep learning approaches for fluid prediction either rely purely on data-driven mappings between past and future states without encoding fluid dynamics, or model only the velocity field while neglecting the full coupling of multiple physical quantities (velocity, pressure, concentration) inherent in the Navier-Stokes equations. This leads to poor temporal extrapolation, weak spatial generalization, and a lack of physical interpretability. The paper addresses this gap by integrating discretized PDEs directly into both the model architecture and the loss function while simultaneously estimating all coupled latent quantities.

## Input / Output

The model takes K past frames of a passive scalar concentration field (e.g., dye in water, smoke, or radar VIL) plus spatial embeddings ψ = (grid coordinates, Signed Distance Function to obstacles, boundary/region masks) as input. It outputs the next-frame concentration prediction along with two latent fields never present in the training data: the velocity field u(x,t') and the pressure field p(x,t'). Prediction is performed recurrently in a sliding-window fashion (one frame at a time, feeding the prediction back into the input window), enabling long-horizon rollouts far beyond the training horizon. The Peclét and Reynolds numbers are treated as learnable scalar parameters rather than fixed constants.

## Method

PINP discretizes the incompressible Navier-Stokes advection-diffusion equation via the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem, introducing an intermediate time t' ∈ [t_k, t_{k+1}] so that the next-frame concentration is computed as c(x,t_k) + (−u·∇c + Pe⁻¹∇²c)|_{t'} · Δt. A 3D U-Net maps the past K concentration frames and spatial embeddings to the physical quantities at t' (concentration, velocity, pressure), and a lightweight U-Net correction network refines the discrete-PDE prediction to absorb numerical discretization errors. The training loss combines three terms: a data L2 loss on the concentration (both pre- and post-correction), a physical loss enforcing the NS momentum and incompressibility residuals (which introduces the pressure field and Reynolds number as additional constrained variables), and a temporal loss that bounds the intermediate concentration c(x,t') between the known endpoints c(x,t_k) and c(x,t_{k+1}). This architecture-level embedding of the PDEs—rather than treating them solely as a soft penalty—gives the model explicit interpretability through the estimated velocity and pressure fields and enables robust extrapolation to unseen time horizons and spatial configurations.

## Results

On the three synthetic fluid dynamics benchmarks, PINP achieves the best MAE and MSE across all settings: Flow 2D (MAE 0.0107, MSE 0.0003), Smoke 2D (MAE 0.0209, MSE 0.0057), and Smoke 3D (MAE 0.1073, MSE 0.0249), outperforming the second-best methods by 41% (Flow 2D), 18% (Smoke 2D), and 5% (Smoke 3D) in MAE. On the real-world SEVIR nowcasting benchmark, PINP achieves the best mean CSI (0.3800) and the best CSI at all high-precipitation thresholds (CSI-219: 0.0989, CSI-181: 0.1952, CSI-160: 0.2448, CSI-133: 0.3573), though it trails SimVP (3.8508) and NowcastNet (3.8883) on MSE (4.1684). The model also demonstrates temporal extrapolation (predicting 40–100 frames from 4-frame inputs) and spatial generalization (unseen obstacle configurations) that data-driven baselines fail to match.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the five neural operator baselines (FNO, U-NO, U-FNO, F-FNO, LSM), PINP wins on all three synthetic datasets by substantial margins — e.g., on Flow 2D, PINP's MAE of 0.0107 beats the best operator (U-NO, 0.0183) by 41%, and on Smoke 3D it beats F-FNO (0.1124) by 5%. Against HelmFluid, the closest physics-aware competitor, PINP dominates on Flow 2D (MAE 0.0107 vs. 0.1222) and Smoke 3D (0.1073 vs. 0.1217), and also wins on Smoke 2D (0.0209 vs. 0.0254); HelmFluid's velocity-only modeling without explicit NS discretization limits its long-horizon accuracy. Against the two vision baselines (U-Net, ResNet), PINP improves MAE by 70–75% on Flow 2D and 39–49% on Smoke 2D. On the SEVIR nowcasting task, PINP outperforms NowcastNet (CSI-M 0.3800 vs. 0.3743) and all video-prediction baselines (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, SimVP, EarthFormer) on mean CSI and every high-precipitation threshold (≥133), but trails SimVP (3.8508) and NowcastNet (3.8883) on MSE (4.1684) and the lowest CSI threshold (CSI-16: 0.7279 vs. SimVP's 0.7424), indicating that the physics-informed formulation excels at capturing extreme-precipitation structure at the cost of some overall pixel-level fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of neural operator methods (FNO, U-NO, F-FNO, LSM) and physics-informed neural networks (HFM, HelmFluid, DVP). Prior neural operators learn black-box mappings from past to future states without modeling underlying physics, while existing latent-quantity methods (e.g., HelmFluid) typically estimate only the velocity field and approximate or simplify the governing equations. PINP departs from both by discretizing the full incompressible Navier-Stokes system (including pressure) via the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem and embedding the discrete PDEs directly into the prediction architecture, simultaneously estimating velocity and pressure as coupled latent variables rather than treating them independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to extrapolate far beyond training horizons (4→40 frames on Flow 2D, 4→100 on Smoke 2D) while generalizing to unseen obstacle configurations addresses a critical practical limitation of current fluid prediction models. The simultaneous estimation of pressure alongside velocity provides interpretable physical evidence that purely data-driven methods cannot offer, and the approach transfers to real-world radar nowcasting (SEVIR) where NS equations are only approximately satisfied. Key limitations include the restriction to incompressible passive-scalar transport, the unknown physical scale requiring post-hoc calibration of estimated quantities, and the fact that on SEVIR the method does not dominate all metrics (MSE is not best). Extending the framework to compressible flows, multi-phase systems, or other PDE families remains open.
