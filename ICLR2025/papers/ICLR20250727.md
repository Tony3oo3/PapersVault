## Problem

In cooperative MARL under the CTDE paradigm, agents rely solely on local observations during decentralized execution, creating a critical information gap that limits collaborative decision-making. Existing remedies—explicit inter-agent communication or shared abstract representations like common knowledge—either impose bandwidth/noise constraints or are lossy in complex environments. The paper asks whether an agent can infer the full global state of all teammates purely from its own partial observations, without any communication channel.

## Input / Output

During centralized training, the MAE receives the full trajectory window τ_{t-T+1:t}^{1:n} (observation-action pairs for all n agents over T timesteps) with k randomly selected agents' trajectories masked out, and outputs reconstructed trajectories for the masked agents. During decentralized execution, only the current agent's local observation-action history (τ_{t-T+1:t-1}^{i}, o_t^i) is available; the MAE reconstructs the other agents' current-time trajectories, which are then combined with the agent's own observed (o_t^i, u_t^i) and passed through self-attention to produce the individual Q-value or policy for action selection. The reconstruction target is the full (observation, action) vector per agent per timestep, not a compressed latent.

## Method

MA²E is a Transformer-based autoencoder (3 encoder layers, 2 decoder layers, 4 attention heads) that applies agent-level masking—randomly selecting k agents and zeroing all their trajectory tokens—then learns to reconstruct the masked (observation, action) pairs via MSE loss. Positional encoding is applied along both the agent-identity and time-step dimensions, which ablations show is critical for performance. Training is two-stage: a pre-training phase on random-policy rollouts until the reconstruction loss drops below a threshold, followed by periodic fine-tuning interleaved with the backbone MARL policy updates. At execution time, the reconstructed global trajectories are fed into a self-attention module where the current agent's state attends to inferred teammate states, and the resulting attention output is concatenated with the backbone network's latent features before the final value/policy head. Because MA²E is trained independently of the policy, it can be plugged into arbitrary value-based (QMIX, VDN, QPLEX) or policy-based (RIIT, IPPO) MARL algorithms and even transferred across backbones without re-pretraining.

## Results

On SMAC HARD and SuperHARD scenarios, QMIX+MA²E achieves win rates of 0.99±0.01 (3s_vs_5z), 0.97±0.03 (2c_vs_64zg), 0.91±0.10 (corridor), and 0.53±0.08 (6h_vs_8z) at 2M steps, substantially outperforming all value-based and communication-based baselines. In terms of sample efficiency, MA²E reduces steps to convergence by 57% (2.03M vs 3.52M in 3s_vs_5z) and 63% (0.639M vs 1.021M in 2c_vs_64zg) compared to fine-tuned QMIX. Performance with MA²E is comparable to using full global observations, confirming effective inference of unobserved agent information from local observations alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against value-based baselines (VDN, QMIX, OW-QMIX, CW-QMIX, QPLEX, MA2CL, COLA), QMIX+MA²E consistently achieves higher win rates and faster convergence across all SMAC HARD/SuperHARD and SMACv2 scenarios, with the gap most pronounced in difficult scenarios like corridor and 6h_vs_8z where baselines fail to achieve victories even after 2M steps. Against communication-based methods, MA²E wins decisively on corridor (0.91 vs 0.0 for MAIC/CommFormer) and 6h_vs_8z (0.53 vs 0.0), but loses to MAIC on 5m_vs_6m (0.48 vs 0.69) and MMM2 (0.79 vs 0.97), scenarios where direct information exchange is more critical. Compared to full global observations, MA²E achieves comparable win rates, validating its ability to reconstruct unobserved agent information. The method also extends to policy-based algorithms (RIIT, IPPO) and other value-based backbones (VDN, QPLEX), consistently improving performance over their unmodified versions. A trained MA²E can be transferred to a different backbone (QMIX→VDN) without re-pretraining, maintaining competitive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) architecture from vision (He et al., 2022) to the multi-agent RL setting, repurposing the reconstruction objective as a global-information inference module within the CTDE paradigm. It departs from prior masked-modeling work in RL (MaskDP, MTM, Masked World Model) which targets single-agent tasks, and from MA2CL which jointly trains the policy and reconstruction module, by separately pre-training MA2E and then fine-tuning it alongside independently trained MARL policies. Conceptually, it positions itself as an alternative to communication-based approaches (MAIC, QMIX-att, CommFormer) and shared-structure methods (MACKRL, COLA), arguing that an agent can infer global state from local observations without any inter-agent messaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MA2E offers a practical, plug-and-play solution to partial observability in cooperative MARL that does not require communication infrastructure, making it applicable in bandwidth-constrained or communication-restricted settings. Its demonstrated transferability across backbone policies (QMIX→VDN) and algorithm families (value-based and policy-based) suggests it could become a standard component in MARL pipelines. Key limitations remain: performance degrades when agent observations have no spatial overlap, and the fixed agent-count architecture makes it difficult to scale to variable team sizes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly large-scale settings (dozens of agents) and whether the pre-training requirement can be eliminated.
