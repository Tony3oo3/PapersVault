## Problem

Training robust vision-and-language navigation (VLN) agents is bottlenecked by the lack of high-quality instruction-trajectory pairs. While prior work has scaled synthetic data and environments, the paper shows that large-scale synthetic data (352× more pairs, 13× more environments) actually degrades performance compared to a small human-annotated set, revealing that instruction quality and trajectory alignment are critical yet under-explored. Evaluating this alignment is particularly difficult because instructions encode both semantic landmarks and directional cues across multiple 3D scenes, making standard metrics like CLIP score inadequate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250848/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The navigator (DUET dual-scale graph transformer) consumes a natural language instruction and a sequence of panoramic RGB observations (multi-view per viewpoint) to predict discrete navigation actions or continuous waypoints. The instruction generator (Mantis-8B MLLM with LoRA) takes an interleaved image-action sequence encoding a trajectory—where each viewpoint contributes key-view images paired with raw-text action descriptions (e.g., 'left 135.71° and up 0.00°')—and produces a single natural language instruction. The generator's output is then fed back into the navigator for quality filtering, creating a closed loop between the two models.

## Method

The Self-Refining Data Flywheel (SRDF) iteratively improves both a navigator and an instruction generator through mutual data feedback without human annotation. The key insight is using the trained navigator's path-fidelity scores (nDTW ≥ 0.9 for navigator training data, SPL = 1 for generator training data) as a data quality filter, which transforms the hard instruction-trajectory alignment problem into a simpler trajectory-trajectory similarity computation. Each round, the navigator filters high-quality samples to retrain the generator, and the improved generator regenerates instructions for previously low-quality samples to retrain the navigator. The generator is initialized as Mantis (SigLIP + LLaMA-3-8B) fine-tuned with LoRA on an interleaved image-action template, while the navigator is DUET pre-trained with Masked Language Modeling and Single Action Prediction objectives. After three rounds on 860 HM3D/MP3D environments, the process yields the SRDF-20M dataset (20M instructions, 10K+ vocabulary) and elevates R2R test SPL from 70% to 78%, surpassing human performance (76%) for the first time.

## Results

On the R2R test-unseen split, SRDF achieves 78% SPL and 85% SR, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art ScaleVLN (70% SPL) by 8 points and, notably, exceeding human performance (76% SPL) for the first time. On R2R-CE test-unseen, SRDF reaches 56% SPL, outperforming ETPNav (48%) and ScaleVLN (50%) by 8 and 6 points respectively. For instruction generation on R2R val-unseen, the final SRDF generator achieves 26.2 SPICE, surpassing the previous best KEFA (23.4) by 2.8 points. Across downstream tasks, SRDF sets new state-of-the-art on all six benchmarks, including +17.4% SR on R4R and +7.8% SR on RxR-English over prior best methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRDF decisively outperforms ScaleVLN (Wang et al., 2023), the closest prior data-scaling method, by +8% SPL on R2R test-unseen (78 vs. 70) and +6% SPL on R2R-CE test-unseen (56 vs. 50), demonstrating that data quality via the flywheel matters more than raw data volume. On R2R, SRDF also surpasses MAGIC-L (70% SPL), VER (65%), and GOAT (68%) on val-unseen by 9, 14, and 11 SPL points respectively. For downstream tasks, SRDF beats PRET (71.0% SR) and MAGIC-L (72.9%) on RxR-English by +7.8 and +5.9 SR points, and outperforms VER (47.0% SR) on R4R by a massive +17.4 SR points. On REVERIE, SRDF exceeds ScaleVLN by +3.4% SR and +3.5% SPL, and on SOON it surpasses the pre-exploration RREx-Bot by +1.1% SR. For instruction generation, the SRDF generator (26.2 SPICE) beats KEFA (23.4), LANA+ (22.8), and EnvDrop (21.8) by 2.8, 3.4, and 4.4 SPICE points respectively, establishing a clear new state-of-the-art across all 28 compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DUET dual-scale graph transformer as its navigator backbone and the Mantis interleaved multi-image MLLM as its instruction generator base, but its core contribution is a fully automated data-refinement loop (SRDF) that departs from prior VLN data-scaling work (ScaleVLN, Prevalent, AutoVLN) which focused on quantity over quality. Conceptually, it draws from the SAM data flywheel (Kirillov et al., 2023) and self-improving LLM literature, but removes the human-in-the-loop by using the navigator's own path-fidelity scores (nDTW, SPL) as a quality filter — a key departure from CLIP-based or MLLM-based scoring that the paper shows fails to capture multi-scene directional alignment. The paper also extends the instruction generation line (EnvDrop, KEFA, LANA) by leveraging interleaved image-text trajectory encoding in a pre-trained MLLM rather than training a speaker from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 352× more synthetic instruction-trajectory pairs across 13× more environments actually degrades performance relative to 14K human-annotated samples (Table 1) challenges the prevailing 'scale data and environments' paradigm in VLN and shifts focus to data quality as the binding constraint. The SRDF-20M dataset (20M instructions, 10K+ vocabulary, 860 environments) is a substantial resource likely to become a standard pre-training set for future VLN work, and the navigator-as-filter methodology is generalizable to other embodied tasks where instruction-trajectory alignment is hard to evaluate with off-the-shelf metrics. Open questions include how many flywheel rounds are needed before convergence, whether the approach transfers to tasks with richer action spaces (e.g., manipulation), and the substantial compute cost of running the navigator as a filter at scale.
