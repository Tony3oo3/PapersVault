## Problem

Existing long-sequence recommendation models (e.g., TWIN) share a single embedding table between the attention module (measuring target-behavior correlation for retrieval) and the representation module (generating discriminative features for prediction). The paper demonstrates via gradient analysis that representation gradients dominate attention gradients by ~5× and conflict in direction in ~2/3 of cases, causing attention to misidentify relevant behaviors and representation to lose separability. Common decoupling techniques like linear projections (effective in NLP) fail in recommendation due to the low embedding dimensions imposed by interaction collapse theory.

## Input / Output

The model takes a user's lifelong behavior sequence (item IDs and category IDs, up to ~10,000 behaviors in production) and a single target item, producing a binary CTR prediction. In practice, the two-stage paradigm first retrieves top-k (typically 20) relevant behaviors via attention scores over the full sequence, then aggregates their representations into a fixed-length vector for an MLP classifier. A key deployment constraint is strict online inference latency, which motivates the ability to reduce the attention embedding dimension independently of the representation dimension.

## Method

DARE replaces the single shared embedding table with two fully independent tables: E^Att for attention and E^Repr for representation. The attention module computes scaled dot-product scores between E^Att embeddings of history behaviors and the target to retrieve top-k behaviors, while the representation module forms a target-aware representation via element-wise product (e_i^Repr ⊙ v_t^Repr) weighted by attention scores and summed into a user history vector. Because gradients now back-propagate to separate tables, the domination and conflict between the two modules is completely eliminated. This decoupling also enables independent dimension tuning: reducing the attention embedding dimension to half accelerates the O(K_A·N) search stage by 50% with negligible AUC loss, while the representation dimension can remain large for discriminability.

## Results

On the Taobao dataset with embedding dimension 16, DARE achieves 0.92568 AUC, outperforming the state-of-the-art TWIN (0.91688) by 9%. On Tmall with the same setting, DARE reaches 0.96800 AUC versus TWIN's 0.95812 (a 6% gain). Across all embedding dimensions (16, 64, 128) and both datasets, DARE consistently achieves the highest AUC, with improvements of 1–9% over TWIN. Additionally, DARE accelerates the search stage by 50% (by halving the attention embedding dimension) with minimal performance loss, and achieves a 1.47% GMV lift in online A/B testing on Tencent's advertising platform.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARE outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and embedding dimension setting. Against the primary state-of-the-art TWIN (Chang et al., KDD 2023), DARE gains 9% AUC on Taobao (d=16: 0.92568 vs. 0.91688) and 6% on Tmall (d=16: 0.96800 vs. 0.95812), with smaller but consistent margins at higher dimensions (e.g., +0.13% on Taobao d=128). Against DIN (Zhou et al., SIGIR 2018), DARE leads by 2.1% on Taobao (d=16) and 0.9% on Tmall (d=16). ETA (Chen et al., SIGIR 2021) and SDIM (Cao et al., CIKM 2022), which sacrifice accuracy for search efficiency, trail DARE by 1.2–2.1% and 2.1–3.3% respectively. TWIN-V2 (Si et al., CIKM 2024), a domain-specific variant optimized for video recommendation, underperforms DARE by 3.1% on Taobao (d=16), confirming its limited transferability to general item-based recommendation. The paper also shows that DARE's advantage is most pronounced with fewer retrieved behaviors (e.g., +5.7% AUC over TWIN when retrieving only 1 behavior on Taobao), indicating superior attention accuracy in the search stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage long-sequence recommendation paradigm (SIM → TWIN → TWIN-V2) by identifying a previously overlooked multi-task learning conflict: the shared embedding table used for both attention scoring (search) and representation aggregation (prediction) suffers gradient domination and directional conflict, with representation gradients being ~5× larger and opposing attention gradients in ~2/3 of cases. It draws on MTL gradient analysis (Yu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021) to formalize this interference and on the interaction collapse theory (Guo et al., 2024) to explain why the standard NLP decoupling via Q/K/V projection matrices fails in recommendation due to the low embedding dimension constraint. The target-aware representation (element-wise product) is adopted from TIN (Zhou et al., 2024), and the overall architecture is a direct modification of TWIN (Chang et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a straightforward NLP technique (linear projections for decoupling) actively degrades recommendation performance is a useful negative result that clarifies the boundary of cross-domain transfer. The 9% AUC gain (where 1–2% is considered significant in CTR prediction) combined with a 50% search-stage speedup and a 1.47% GMV lift on Tencent's advertising platform makes this both a conceptual and practical contribution. Open questions remain: the paper cannot fully explain why small embedding dimensions cause an 'over-confidence' failure mode in projections, the Tmall dim=16 anomaly where target-aware representation hurts, and whether one-stage methods (e.g., IFA) will eventually render the two-stage paradigm obsolete.
