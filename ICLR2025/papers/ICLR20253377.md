## Problem

Knowledge distillation during pre-training of language models faces three unresolved issues: online KD methods incur prohibitive training-time overhead from per-token teacher inference, most approaches require matching tokenizers between teacher and student limiting cross-family transfer, and offline KD methods that train on teacher-generated data lose the difficulty and diversity essential for effective pre-training. MINIPLM addresses all three simultaneously by distilling teacher knowledge into the pre-training data distribution rather than into the model's output distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large pre-training corpus (105B tokens from The Pile in the main experiments), the teacher LM's sequence-level log-probability (a single scalar per 1,024-token instance, requiring only ~200MB storage for 50B tokens), and a tiny 104M reference LM trained on a 5B-token subset. It produces a difference-sampled corpus of 50B tokens (controlled by sampling ratio α=0.5), on which student LMs of 200M, 500M, or 1.2B parameters are pre-trained from scratch with standard next-token cross-entropy loss. Because only sequence-level scalars are needed, the refined corpus is reusable across multiple student architectures and tokenizers without recomputation.

## Method

MINIPLM reformulates KD as a reward maximization problem (minimizing reverse KLD between teacher and student), then introduces Difference Sampling to optimize this reward offline. Instead of sampling candidates from the student model and computing rewards with the student (as in Best-of-N), it samples from the original pre-training corpus and replaces the student in the reward function with a tiny reference LM, yielding the reward r(x) = log p(x)/p_ref(x). A theoretical proposition (Prop 2.1) shows that top-K selections from the corpus and from the student's own samples converge to the same set as corpus size grows. The resulting top-K selection down-samples easy common patterns (where teacher and reference agree), up-samples hard diverse instances (where the teacher substantially outperforms the reference), and discards noisy instances (where the teacher assigns lower probability than the reference). The student LM is then pre-trained from scratch on this refined corpus with standard cross-entropy loss, incurring zero additional training-time computation compared to vanilla pre-training.

## Results

MINIPLM achieves the best average zero-shot accuracy across all three student model sizes: 41.3% (200M), 44.8% (500M), and 46.6% (1.2B), outperforming the strongest baseline Vanilla KD by 1.4, 1.2, and 1.2 points respectively. On the DCLM language modeling benchmark, MINIPLM-trained models achieve the lowest test loss at matched FLOPs, with scaling-law extrapolation showing the advantage persists at 1T and 10T token scales (e.g., 2.88 vs. 2.91 loss for 1.2B at 10T). MINIPLM also reduces pre-training computation by 2.2× compared to Vanilla KD to reach equivalent performance, and reduces data requirements by 2.4× in the data-limited setting (α=0.25, 4 epochs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MINIPLM consistently outperforms all three KD baselines and the no-KD baseline across all student sizes. Against Vanilla KD (online token-level forward KLD), MINIPLM wins by 1.4/1.2/1.2 points at 200M/500M/1.2B while requiring no additional training-time computation (Vanilla KD needs online teacher inference, reducing effective training steps). Against MiniLLM (online reverse KLD with PPO), MINIPLM's advantage is largest—2.3/3.3/3.0 points—because MiniLLM's PPO exploration further reduces usable training steps and its online sampling from q_θ limits data diversity. Against SeqKD (offline teacher-generated data), MINIPLM wins by 1.6/2.5/2.3 points, attributed to Difference Sampling preserving data difficulty and diversity that SeqKD's teacher-generated text loses (semantic diversity drops from 32.25 to 30.16 for SeqKD vs. 36.70 for MINIPLM). Notably, at 200M, Vanilla KD ties with Pre-Train w/o KD (both 39.9), indicating that online KD provides no benefit at small student sizes under matched FLOPs, whereas MINIPLM still gains 1.4 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends knowledge distillation from the fine-tuning regime (where methods like MiniLLM and Vanilla KD operate) to the pre-training stage, identifying that direct transfer of fine-tuning KD methods fails due to online inference overhead, tokenizer-matching constraints, and loss of data difficulty/diversity. It reformulates KD as a reward maximization problem (drawing on RL literature) and introduces Difference Sampling, which uses a small reference LM to decouple the student from reward computation, enabling offline, cross-family distillation. The theoretical Proposition 2.1 justifies sampling from the original corpus rather than the student's distribution, connecting to order-statistics arguments. The approach is conceptually distinct from SeqKD (teacher-generated data) by operating on the original corpus and preserving its diversity through the teacher-reference probability gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MINIPLM offers a practically attractive recipe for pre-training small LMs: no online teacher inference, no tokenizer alignment, and a 2.4× reduction in data requirements—relevant as web-crawled corpora face exhaustion. The finding that online KD methods (Vanilla KD, MiniLLM) provide no benefit over no-KD when training FLOPs are held constant challenges the default assumption that KD always helps during pre-training. Key limitations include the requirement for teacher LM probability access (ruling out black-box APIs without expensive logit extraction) and the observation that an excessively large teacher (4B) can actually degrade Difference Sampling due to log-probability scale mismatch. Future directions include weak-to-strong generalization (using difference-sampled data to pre-train models larger than the teacher) and optimizing the teacher-student size gap.
