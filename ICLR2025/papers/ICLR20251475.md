## Problem

Standard generative model evaluation metrics like FID and KID rely on InceptionV3 features trained on ImageNet, introducing a domain bias that distorts rankings when evaluating generators on non-ImageNet datasets (e.g., agricultural or remote-sensing images). FD-DINOv2 partially mitigates this by using a larger backbone, but at ~14× the computational cost and with residual bias from its own (closed-source) training data. The paper addresses the absence of a truly domain-agnostic, computationally cheap, and interpretable metric for comparing generative image synthesis approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes two sets of RGB images (real and generated) at resolutions of 64×64, 128×128, or 256×256, with pixel values rescaled to [0,1]. It applies a level-2, level-3, or level-4 Wavelet Packet Transform respectively, producing P=4^l packets each of spatial size (H/4^l)×(W/4^l)×C. The output is a single scalar FWD score (the mean of per-packet Fréchet distances) along with the individual per-packet Fréchet distances, which enable frequency-band-level interpretability of the ranking.

## Method

FWD replaces the InceptionV3 feature space in the Fréchet distance computation with wavelet packet coefficients. It constructs 2D Haar wavelet filter quadruples (approximation, horizontal, vertical, diagonal) via outer products of 1D low-pass and high-pass filters, then recursively applies stride-2 convolutions to build a full wavelet packet tree to a fixed depth (level 4 for 256×256 images). At each level, all four filters are applied to every coefficient from the previous level, yielding 4^l packets that partition the frequency spectrum into hierarchically organized subbands. For each packet, the method computes the mean vector and covariance matrix across the image set, then evaluates the Fréchet distance between the real and generated multivariate Gaussian distributions for that packet. The final FWD score is the arithmetic mean of these per-packet Fréchet distances, making the metric deterministic, training-free, and ~36× faster than FD-DINOv2 while remaining robust to domain shifts, perturbations, and FID-optimization attacks.

## Results

FWD correctly ranks DDGAN above Proj. FastGAN on CelebA-HQ (0.408 vs. 1.388), Sentinel (0.115 vs. 0.755), and DNDD-Dataset (1.357 vs. 1.442), whereas FID consistently prefers Proj. FastGAN across all four datasets due to ImageNet domain bias. FWD is over 36× faster than FD-DINOv2 (0.006 vs. 15.566 GFLOPs; 1923 vs. 53 imgs/sec) and remains unchanged under FID-optimized sample selection (+17.75% penalty), while FID improves by ~52.5% and FD-DINOv2 by ~1.5%. On the CelebA-HQ/FFHQ state-of-the-art comparison (Table 4), FWD and FID largely agree, with FWD providing a consistent ranking of DDIM > DDPM > StyleSwin > StyleGAN2 > DDGAN across both CelebA-HQ and LSUN-Churches, unlike FID which swaps StyleSwin's position between datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FID, FWD wins decisively on domain-agnostic ranking: FID prefers Proj. FastGAN over DDGAN on all four datasets (e.g., 6.358 vs. 7.641 on CelebA-HQ), while FWD correctly ranks DDGAN higher (0.408 vs. 1.388), matching human perception (HER: 32.5% vs. 20.0%). Against FD-DINOv2, FWD agrees on CelebA-HQ, FFHQ, and Sentinel but diverges on DNDD-Dataset (agricultural images), where FWD correctly prefers DDGAN (1.357) while FD-DINOv2 still favors Proj. FastGAN (171.625 vs. 232.884); FWD is also 36× faster (0.006 vs. 15.566 GFLOPs). Against KID, FID∞, IS, IS∞, and Clean-FID (all sharing the InceptionV3 backbone), FWD is the only metric that avoids domain bias, as confirmed in Table 8 where all InceptionV3-based metrics prefer Proj. FastGAN. Against SWD, FWD is deterministic (0.00 std across 5 runs) while SWD shows high variance (±3.54 to ±6.81), making FWD more reliable in practice. On the state-of-the-art generator comparison (Table 4), FWD and FID agree on most rankings, but FWD provides a consistent ordering of DDIM/DDPM/StyleSwin/StyleGAN2 across CelebA-HQ and LSUN-Churches, whereas FID inconsistently ranks StyleSwin differently on the two datasets due to class-distribution bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Fréchet distance framework introduced by Heusel et al. (2017) for FID, replacing the InceptionV3 penultimate-layer feature space with coefficients from a fixed Haar Wavelet Packet Transform. It is positioned as a response to the domain-bias problem identified by Kynkäänniemi et al. (2023) and partially addressed by Stein et al. (2023) via FD-DINOv2, and contrasts with frequency-based alternatives like Sliced Wasserstein Distance (Karras et al., 2018) that suffer from reproducibility issues due to random projections. Conceptually, it repurposes classical signal-processing tools (wavelet packet trees, Mallat 1989; Daubechies 1992) that have appeared in generative architectures (Gal et al., 2021; Phung et al., 2023) but had not been used as a standalone evaluation metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FWD offers a practical, 36× faster alternative to FD-DINOv2 that is genuinely domain-agnostic, interpretable via per-packet analysis, and robust to the ImageNet-class manipulation that inflates FID scores by ~50%. The finding that a fixed, non-learned transform can outperform a large pretrained network (DINOv2) for distribution comparison on out-of-domain data challenges the assumption that learned features are necessary for meaningful generative evaluation. Open questions include whether other wavelet bases (e.g., Daubechies-4, Symlets) would improve sensitivity, whether the per-packet Gaussian assumption is appropriate for all frequency bands, and whether the metric extends naturally to video or 3D generation.
