## Problem

Existing co-speech gesture synthesis methods are designed for single-speaker self-talking scenarios and overlook the practical task of generating concurrent, temporally coherent gestures for two speakers engaged in interactive conversation. This gap is compounded by the absence of large-scale datasets containing synchronized 3D whole-body postures of two speakers with separated speech audio, making it difficult to train or evaluate models for this setting.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a mixed conversational speech signal C_mix and two separated per-speaker audio tracks (C_a, C_b), each converted to 128×186 mel-spectrograms (FFT window 1024, hop 512). It produces concurrent upper-body gesture sequences for both speakers simultaneously: 90 frames at 15 FPS, each frame encoding 46 joints (16 body + 30 hand) in 6D rotation representation, yielding a per-speaker tensor of shape R^{90×276}. Lower-body joints are fixed in a seated T-pose and facial expressions/shape parameters are excluded from generation.

## Method

Co³Gesture employs two cooperative transformer-based diffusion denoising branches (8 blocks, 8 heads, latent dim 768), one per speaker, to handle the asymmetric body dynamics of concurrent speakers and prevent mode collapse. The key architectural component is the Temporal Interaction Module (TIM), which performs cross-attention between each speaker's audio embedding (query) and their own motion embedding (key/value), then computes a temporal correlation matrix between the audio-conditioned and mixed-audio-conditioned motion embeddings; a sigmoid-gated Hadamard product fuses these two representations to balance individual speech coherence against interactive dynamics. A mutual attention layer is then applied across the two branches, exploiting the observation that exchanging speaker identities preserves the marginal distribution of interaction data, so the denoisers share weight-update strategies. Training uses a standard diffusion simple loss (λ=15) plus velocity and foot-contact losses over 1000 cosine-scheduled steps; inference uses DDIM with 50 denoising steps. The framework is trained on the newly constructed GES-Inter dataset (7M+ frames, 70 hours, 27,390 clips) built from in-the-wild talk-show videos via PyMAF-X pose estimation, pyannote-audio speaker separation, and WhisperX transcription.

## Results

On the GES-Inter dataset, Co³Gesture achieves the best performance across all three metrics: FGD of 0.769 (lower is better), Beat Consistency of 0.692 (higher is better), and Diversity of 72.824±2.026 (higher is better). This represents a ~24% FGD improvement over the closest competitor InterGen (1.012) and a ~38% improvement over the best single-person co-speech method DiffSHEG (1.209). The method also leads in BC by 0.022 over InterGen (0.670) and in Diversity by 3.37 over InterGen (69.455).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven baselines split into two groups: single-person co-speech gesture methods (TalkSHOW, ProbTalk, DiffSHEG, EMAGE) and text-to-motion/interaction methods (MDM, InterX, InterGen). Co³Gesture wins on all three metrics against every baseline. The closest competitor is InterGen (IJCV'24), which also employs bilateral branches for two-speaker generation but lacks explicit temporal interaction modeling; Co³Gesture improves FGD by 24% (1.012→0.769), BC by 0.022, and Diversity by 3.37 over InterGen. Among single-person co-speech methods, DiffSHEG is the strongest (FGD 1.209), yet Co³Gesture still reduces FGD by 36% over it. The text-to-motion baselines MDM and InterX show competitive diversity scores (65.5 and 65.2) but lag significantly in FGD and BC, confirming that audio-conditional bilateral diffusion with temporal interaction modeling is critical for concurrent gesture quality. In the user study, Co³Gesture also leads in naturalness (4.4 vs. 4.0 for InterGen), smoothness (4.5 vs. 4.2), and interaction coherency (4.2 vs. 4.0), with all differences statistically significant (p < 0.05, t-test).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends single-speaker co-speech gesture synthesis (TalkSHOW, ProbTalk, DiffSHEG, EMAGE) to the concurrent two-speaker setting, while also drawing on multi-person motion generation (InterGen, InterX) for interaction modeling. It departs from prior conversational gesture work (e.g., the GENEA challenge) that only generates a single speaker's gestures with interlocutor reactions, and from text-to-motion multi-person methods that lack speech conditioning. The bilateral cooperative diffusion architecture is motivated by the asymmetric dynamics of two speakers, and the Temporal Interaction Module adapts cross-attention mechanisms from single-speaker audio-to-gesture pipelines to model inter-speaker temporal dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GES-Inter dataset (7M+ frames, 70 hours, SMPL-X + FLAME + phoneme + text annotations) is the first large-scale resource for concurrent two-speaker 3D co-speech gestures, unblocking research that was previously limited by data scarcity. The paper's limitation to upper-body joints without facial expressions, and its reliance on pseudo-label pose estimation rather than mocap, leave open questions about full-body expressiveness and data fidelity. Scaling beyond two speakers, incorporating real-time inference, and developing dedicated interaction-coherence metrics are natural next steps that the authors acknowledge.
