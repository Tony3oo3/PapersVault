## Problem

Existing ViT attribution methods generate explanations from internal model representations (attention, gradients, class activation maps), yet their quality is assessed using perturbation-based metrics like insertion, deletion, and MAS. The paper asks why these evaluation metrics are not themselves used to drive attribution generation, identifying a fundamental disconnect between how attributions are created and how they are scored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251130/cropped_page9_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image, a pre-trained ViT model (base 16×16, tiny 16×16, or base 32×32), and a seed attribution (Bi-Attn) that prunes the search space from O(M⁴) to O(M³). It outputs an M×M per-patch attribution grid (e.g., 14×14 for 16×16 patches) upscaled to 224×224, where a user-controlled parameter γ∈[0,1] blends between a sparse attribution (only minimally important patches) and a dense attribution (all relevant patches) post-hoc without additional model calls.

## Method

MDA separates attribution generation into two optimization stages that mirror the structure of the evaluation metrics. First, patch order optimization greedily selects patches that maximize the insertion model response from a blurred baseline (stopping at τ=0.9 of full response), then determines remaining patches via a reversed deletion process that minimizes model response from a black baseline, jointly optimizing both insertion and deletion scores. Second, patch magnitude optimization formulates a quadratic programming problem that finds an attribution definition (AD) curve minimizing the MAS alignment penalty while enforcing two properties: monotonic increase and strictly monotonically decreasing derivatives, which together guarantee the patch order is preserved. A small patch-order correction term handles the degenerate case where the QP yields equal derivatives. The seed attribution (Bi-Attn) restricts each search step to the top 2M candidate patches, reducing runtime to O(M³) and enabling practical use on standard hardware.

## Results

On ImageNet with ViT-base 16×16, MDA achieves 0.856 insertion and 0.624 insertion-deletion (RISE), and 0.775 insertion and 0.497 insertion-deletion (MAS), outperforming the best baseline (TIS) by 12.5% and 10.4% respectively on the joint ins-del metric. On ViT-base 32×32, MDA reaches 0.908 insertion and 0.708 ins-del (RISE), and 0.851 insertion and 0.616 ins-del (MAS). On the pointing game (ViT-base 32×32), MDA achieves 0.796 MAP and 0.702 IoU, and on positive/negative perturbation it scores 0.122/0.789, all best-in-class. Across 12 attribution metrics and 3 ViT models, MDA outperforms 7 SOTA methods by an average of 12%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDA outperforms all 7 baselines (GC, IG, ViT-CX, T-Attn, T-Attr, Bi-Attn, TIS) on insertion, insertion-deletion, MAS insertion, and MAS insertion-deletion across all three ViT models, with the largest gap being 51% on MAS ins-del (0.497 vs. Bi-Attn's 0.329). MDA slightly underperforms TIS on raw deletion (0.232 vs. 0.196 for ViT-base 16×16), a trade-off of its joint insertion-deletion optimization, but still wins the joint ins-del score. On the pointing game (ViT-base 32×32), MDA leads in MAP (0.796 vs. T-Attr's 0.758) and IoU (0.702 vs. Bi-Attn's 0.653) but trails Bi-Attn by 0.004 on F1 unless the F1-focused variant is used. On positive/negative perturbation, MDA achieves the best scores (0.122 positive, 0.789 negative) over TIS (0.126, 0.741). On monotonicity, MDA scores 0.820 versus GC's 0.798. In the appendix comparison, MDA also substantially outperforms SHAP on the segmentation test, confirming its advantage over other perturbation-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDA departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm in ViT attribution, which derives explanations from internal model representations (attention weights, gradients, class activation maps) as in GradCAM, T-Attr, T-Attn, and Bi-Attn. Instead, it directly optimizes the perturbation-based quality metrics (RISE insertion/deletion and MAS) that are conventionally used only to *evaluate* attributions, turning the evaluation criterion into the generation objective. The method also connects to the broader perturbation-based explanation lineage (occlusion, SHAP) but achieves O(M²) runtime through greedy patch selection rather than exhaustive combinatorial search, and uses a seed attribution (Bi-Attn) to prune the search space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that attribution quality metrics can serve as generation objectives rather than merely evaluation tools—opens a path toward self-consistent explanation methods where the explanation is guaranteed to score well by construction. The cross-metric generalization (optimizing for insertion/deletion yet also outperforming on pointing game, monotonicity, and positive/negative perturbation) suggests the learned patch ordering captures genuine model importance beyond the optimized objective. Key limitations include the 13-second runtime for ViT-base 16×16 (vs. milliseconds for gradient-based methods), a slight degradation on the deletion metric relative to TIS, and dependence on a seed attribution for practical speed. The open question of what constitutes the 'ideal' attribution metric remains, and MDA's design is modular enough to adapt to future metric definitions.
