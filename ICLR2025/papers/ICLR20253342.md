## Problem

Existing graph sparsification methods apply a single global sparsity level and a uniform pruning criterion to all edges, ignoring the fact that different nodes have varying connectivity patterns and distinct rationales for edge removal (e.g., non-bridge vs. non-homophilic edges). This rigidity prevents customized sparsification tailored to each node's local context, limiting both the achievable sparsity and the quality of the resulting sparse graph.

## Input / Output

MoG takes an undirected graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F}) and a plug-and-play GNN backbone as input. It decomposes the graph into 1-hop ego-graphs, routes each node to k=2 sparsifier experts out of K=12 total experts, and outputs per-node sparse ego-graphs that are reassembled into a global sparse subgraph. The global sparsity is controlled by adjusting the three per-expert sparsity levels {s₁, s₂, s₃}, and the resulting sparse graph can be fed into any MPNN or graph transformer for end-to-end training.

## Method

MoG applies a Mixture-of-Experts paradigm to graph sparsification with four key stages. First, a noisy top-k gating network routes each node to k sparsifier experts based on its feature vector. Second, each expert applies a distinct pruning criterion (edge degree, Jaccard similarity, effective resistance, or gradient magnitude) at a specific sparsity level to produce a sparse ego-graph. Third, the k sparse ego-graphs are ensembled on the Grassmann manifold: the method formulates a trace-minimization objective that balances preserving node connectivity (via spectral embedding of the graph Laplacian) and proximity to each expert's subspace (weighted by the routing score), yielding a closed-form ensemble Laplacian. Fourth, a post-sparsification step re-prunes the ensembled graph to the weighted-average target sparsity. Training combines the downstream task loss with an expert importance loss (coefficient of variation of expert scores) to prevent collapse to a single expert group.

## Results

On OGBN-ARXIV (node classification, GraphSAGE), MoG achieves 71.93% accuracy at 10% sparsity (beating the dense graph's 71.49%) and maintains 69.06% at 50% sparsity, outperforming the best baseline AdaGLT (69.13%) by a narrow margin while surpassing all others. On OGBN-PROTEINS (ROC-AUC), MoG reaches 77.78 at 10% sparsity (+0.38 over dense) and 76.12 at 70% sparsity, exceeding the next-best AdaGLT (75.44) by 0.68 points. For graph classification on OGBG-PPA (DeeperGCN), MoG boosts accuracy from 77.09 (dense) to 78.43 at 10% sparsity (+1.34), and on MNIST (PNA) it achieves 97.79% at 50% sparsity, surpassing the dense baseline by 0.44%. MoG also delivers 1.47–2.62× inference speedup with negligible performance drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoG outperforms all 16 baseline sparsifiers (9 topology-guided + 7 semantic-guided) across every sparsity level on both OGBN-ARXIV and OGBN-PROTEINS. The closest competitors are AdaGLT and ER (effective resistance): on OGBN-ARXIV at 50% sparsity, MoG (69.06) edges out AdaGLT (69.13) by a narrow margin but significantly beats ER (69.00) and all others by 3+ points; at 70% sparsity, MoG (67.31) surpasses AdaGLT (67.02) and ER (67.15). On OGBN-PROTEINS, MoG's advantage is more pronounced—at 70% sparsity it achieves 76.12 ROC-AUC versus AdaGLT's 75.44 (+0.68) and G-Spar's 75.89 (+0.23), while ER and WD-GLT fail entirely (OOM/OOT). For graph classification on OGBG-PPA, MoG at 10% sparsity reaches 78.43% accuracy, beating ER (77.94) and G-Spar (77.68), and at 30% sparsity it achieves 77.90% versus ER's 75.15% (+2.75). On MNIST, MoG uniquely maintains or exceeds dense-graph accuracy (97.35%) even at 50% sparsity (97.79%), while the best baseline ICPG drops to 96.80%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm (originally from NLP and vision) to the graph sparsification domain, where prior methods (UGS, DSpar, AdaGLT, GEBT) all apply a single global sparsity level and uniform pruning criterion across all nodes. The key conceptual departure is treating sparsification as a per-node routing problem: multiple sparsifier experts with distinct criteria (degree, Jaccard similarity, effective resistance, gradient magnitude) and sparsity levels are dynamically selected per node via top-k gating. The Grassmann manifold ensembling mechanism for combining sparse ego-graphs is borrowed from multi-view subspace clustering literature (Dong et al., 2013) and adapted here for graph Laplacian fusion, which is a novel application in the graph sparsification context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoG provides a practical plug-and-play module that simultaneously accelerates GNN inference (1.47–2.62×) and can boost even top-ranking backbones (RevGNN, DeeperGCN) by 1–1.74%, addressing both efficiency and accuracy concerns in a single framework. The per-node customization principle is well-motivated by the observation that k-hop neighborhood expansion rates vary across nodes, yet the current 1-hop ego-graph decomposition limits the expressiveness of local context. Open questions include whether extending to k-hop contexts improves performance, how the method scales to graphs with millions of edges where per-node Grassmann operations may become costly, and whether the learned expert routing generalizes across different GNN architectures without retraining.
