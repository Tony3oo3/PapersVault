## Problem

Sampling from diffusion models requires solving a probability flow ODE, and existing solver search frameworks (RL-based, evolutionary) are inefficient because the iterative nature of ODE solving makes it hard to predict how intermediate strategy choices affect final sample quality. These frameworks typically require tens of GPU hours and are limited to narrow design spaces such as time steps or solver combinations, leaving prediction coefficients and time scaling factors unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251193/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The distillation phase takes a frozen pretrained diffusion model (pixel-space EDM or latent-space Stable-Diffusion/Latent-Diffusion) and a teacher solver (DPM-Solver++) with M interpolation steps per interval, producing a lightweight designer network (~9k parameters) that outputs per-step prediction coefficients, adaptive time steps, and time scaling factors. At inference, the trained designer network guides a linear multistep solver to generate images from Gaussian noise (plus optional text prompt or class label) in as few as 4–10 NFEs, with resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 512×512 (MS-COCO).

## Method

The paper introduces a unified prediction formula (equations 8–9) that subsumes DDIM, PLMS, DPM-Solver++, UniPC, and DEIS within a single linear multistep framework parameterized by prediction coefficients {a_k} and time scaling factors {s_n}. A solver distillation algorithm then trains a tiny designer network g_φ (two-layer MLP, ~9k params) to output these coefficients, adaptive time steps, and scaling factors at each step, conditioned on bottleneck features extracted from the frozen denoising model. The training objective is the mean Euclidean distance between the student trajectory and a teacher trajectory (generated by DPM-Solver++ with M extra interpolation steps per interval), with strategic stop-gradient operations to prevent memory blowup and time-schedule collapse. Practical techniques include high-order initialization from PLMS, an Analytical First Step that skips the denoiser at t=T, EMA parameter averaging, and replacing the final-step pixel loss with Inception Distance for pixel-space models. The full search for Stable-Diffusion completes in under 12 GPU-hours on 8 V100s, a >10× speedup over RL-based baselines.

## Results

With only 5 NFE, DLMS achieves FID scores of 3.23 on CIFAR-10, 7.16 on ImageNet-64, 5.44 on LSUN-Bedroom, and 12.52 on MS-COCO (Stable-Diffusion), substantially outperforming all handcrafted solvers (DEIS, DPM-Solver++, UniPC) which require 10+ NFE for comparable quality. This yields a 2× sampling acceleration ratio over the best handcrafted baselines. The solver distillation search itself completes in under 12 GPU hours (0.7 h on CIFAR-10, 10 h on MS-COCO with 8× V100), representing a >10× efficiency gain over RL-based search frameworks like USF (12.15–106.64 GPU hours) and DPM-Solver-v3 (28–88 GPU hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against handcrafted solvers, DLMS dominates at low NFE: at 5 NFE on CIFAR-10, DLMS achieves FID 3.23 versus 14.39 (DEIS), 24.97 (DPM-Solver++), and 23.98 (UniPC); on MS-COCO (Stable-Diffusion), DLMS reaches 12.52 while the best handcrafted method (DEIS) only reaches 15.16. Against search-based solvers on CIFAR-10 at 5 NFE, DLMS (3.23) outperforms GITS (6.77), AMED-Plugin (6.61), USF (6.86), and DPM-Solver-v3 (12.21), with the gap widening at 4 NFE where DLMS scores 4.52 versus 10.11–11.50 for the others. The advantage is most pronounced in the 4–6 NFE regime, where handcrafted polynomial-interpolation solvers degrade sharply while DLMS maintains near-10-NFE quality. In terms of search cost, DLMS requires 0.7 GPU hours on CIFAR-10 and 10 GPU hours on MS-COCO, compared to 12.15–106.64 hours for USF and 28–88 hours for DPM-Solver-v3, confirming the claimed >10× efficiency improvement. DLMS also surpasses the closely related AMED-Plugin (Zhou et al., 2024) by jointly optimizing the full adaptive time schedule and prediction coefficients rather than only adjusting intermediate time steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the exponential integrator framework from DPM-Solver++ and the polynomial interpolation solvers (DEIS, UniPC) under a single linear multistep prediction formula, then introduces a lightweight designer network (modified from AMED-Plugin's bottleneck-feature architecture) to learn per-trajectory, per-step prediction coefficients, adaptive time steps, and time scaling factors. It departs from RL-based solver search (USF, DPM-Solver-v3) by replacing the expensive FID-proxy optimization with direct trajectory distillation against a higher-NFE teacher solver, and from Bespoke solver by using intermediate trajectory distances rather than only the final output as the training signal. The approach extends AMED-Plugin's partial time-step adjustment to full adaptive schedule learning while simultaneously optimizing the solver coefficients themselves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× reduction in search cost (under 12 GPU hours for Stable-Diffusion vs. 88–107 hours for prior RL-based methods) makes solver optimization practical for large-scale latent diffusion models where retraining-based distillation is infeasible. The finding that different datasets and model architectures learn qualitatively different optimal time schedules (time-square for CIFAR10/FFHQ, time-uniform for ImageNet-64/LSUN, logSNR for Stable-Diffusion) suggests that handcrafted schedule choices are suboptimal and that per-model adaptation is necessary. Key limitations include restriction to ODE solvers (stochastic samplers often outperform deterministic ones at very low NFE) and the observation that increasing teacher interpolation steps M beyond a small value can hurt performance in latent space, indicating a fundamental capacity mismatch between student and teacher that remains unresolved.
