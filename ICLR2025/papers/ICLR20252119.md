## Problem

Standard restless multi-armed bandits (RMAB) algorithms rely on scalar reward signals, but specifying exact reward functions is often infeasible in practice, while pairwise preference feedback is easier to collect yet contains strictly less information. No prior work has studied RMAB under preference-only feedback or provided regret guarantees for this harder setting, leaving open whether sublinear regret is achievable when the decision maker only observes pairwise comparisons rather than absolute rewards.

## Input / Output

At each decision epoch, the decision maker observes the discrete states of all N restless arms and receives Bernoulli preference feedback (drawn from a Bradley-Terry model) for B−1 pairwise duels among the B activated arms, rather than O(B²) comparisons. The output is a stationary index policy that assigns each arm a scalar direct index computed from the solution of a linear program over occupancy measures parameterized by estimated preference values, then activates the B highest-indexed arms. The reference arm and reference state are fixed once selected, and the entire preference matrix is reduced to a single column that must be estimated.

## Method

DOPL is an episodic RL algorithm with three coupled components: (1) Hoeffding-based confidence sets for unknown transition kernels following the UCRL framework; (2) an online preference learning pipeline that combines empirical estimation with a novel transitivity-based inference (Lemma 2) to recover unobserved BT probabilities from two observed ones, reducing required comparisons from O(B²) to B−1 per epoch, followed by an optimism bonus for exploration; and (3) a direct index policy that solves an extended LP whose objective is expressed purely in terms of the preference-reference term Q_n(s) = ln(F_n^★/(1−F_n^★)) rather than scalar rewards, leveraging the fact that the constant offset r_★(s_★) cancels out due to the probability-measure constraint on occupancy. The key theoretical result is a Õ(√(T ln T)) regret bound decomposed into a preference-learning term and an index-policy execution term, matching the rate of standard RMAB with scalar rewards despite observing strictly less information.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative contribution is a theoretical regret bound of Õ((2c₁ + 4B/(1−D))√(2NT|S| ln(4|S||A|NT/ε))), which is Õ(√(T ln T)) — matching the best known rate for standard RMAB with scalar rewards. Empirically, across three environments (synthetic App Marketing, real-world CPAP therapy, and real-world ARMMAN maternal healthcare), DOPL achieves sublinear cumulative regret while all MLE-based baselines (MLE_WIBQL, MLE_QWIC, MLE_LP) fail to do so. In episodic reward, DOPL converges close to the Oracle upper bound, significantly outperforming all other baselines. The paper presents results as reward/regret curves (Figures 1–3) rather than a numerical summary table.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOPL is compared against five baselines: Oracle (full-knowledge upper bound), MLE_WIBQL (Whittle index Q-learning with MLE reward estimation), MLE_QWIC (Q-learning from Fu et al. 2019 with MLE), MLE_LP (LP-based index policy with MLE), and RANDOM. DOPL wins decisively on all three environments in both episodic reward and cumulative regret. The critical distinction is in regret: DOPL achieves sublinear regret consistent with its theoretical guarantee, while none of the MLE-based baselines (MLE_WIBQL, MLE_QWIC, MLE_LP) achieve sublinear regret under preference feedback — their regret curves grow superlinearly. DOPL also approaches the Oracle performance in reward, whereas the MLE baselines plateau at substantially lower reward levels. Additionally, DOPL is computationally more efficient because it directly leverages preference feedback via the BT model and preference inference, avoiding the expensive MLE reward estimation step required by all MLE-based baselines. The RANDOM baseline, as expected, performs worst across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the restless multi-armed bandits (RMAB) framework by replacing scalar reward feedback with pairwise preference feedback, building on the UCRL-based exploration machinery of Jaksch et al. (2010) and the Whittle index / LP-relaxation tradition for RMAB. It departs from the RLHF/DPO line of work (Rafailov et al., 2024; Christiano et al., 2017) by operating in an online, episodic setting rather than offline with pre-collected preference datasets, and by avoiding explicit reward modeling entirely. The key technical departure from standard RMAB RL is the 'preference inference' mechanism (Lemma 2), which exploits the Bradley-Terry model's algebraic structure to reduce required comparisons from O(B²) to B−1 per epoch, and a direct index policy whose LP objective is expressed purely in terms of preference feedback rather than scalar rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal regret characterization for RMAB under preference feedback, showing that the information loss from scalar rewards to pairwise preferences does not degrade the asymptotic regret rate (Õ(√(T ln T))), which is conceptually reassuring for practitioners in healthcare (CPAP, maternal health) and advertising where preference queries are more natural than absolute scoring. The preference inference reducing comparison overhead is a practical contribution for deployment. However, the experimental validation is limited to small-scale synthetic and semi-synthetic environments, and the paper acknowledges that the online preference learning requires a platform for continuous user feedback collection, which is a non-trivial practical barrier. Open questions include scalability to large N, robustness to non-BT preference models, and whether the reference-arm selection strategy generalizes beyond the fixed-reference assumption.
