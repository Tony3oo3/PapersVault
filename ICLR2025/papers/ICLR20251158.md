## Problem

Training, evaluating, and sampling from equilibrium Restricted Boltzmann Machines on highly structured (multimodal/clustered) datasets is severely hindered by critical slowing down at phase transitions, making MCMC mixing prohibitively slow. Standard log-likelihood estimation via Annealed Importance Sampling is computationally impractical for online use and unreliable for multimodal distributions, while conventional Parallel Tempering fails on clustered data because temperature changes induce first-order phase transitions that suppress configuration exchanges. The paper addresses all three challenges—efficient training, reliable online evaluation, and fast equilibrium sampling—simultaneously for datasets where prior methods break down.

## Input / Output

The method takes binary datasets (e.g., MNIST-01 at 784 dims, CelebA at 1024 dims, Human Genome Dataset at 805 dims, 2D Ising configurations at 64 dims) and produces equilibrium samples from the trained RBM along with log-likelihood estimates. A key operational detail is that PTT sampling requires a set of RBM checkpoints saved at different training stages (selected to maintain ~0.25 acceptance rate between consecutive models), and the pre-training phase requires the first d principal components of the dataset (d ≤ 4 in practice due to discretization memory limits). The online Tr-AIS log-likelihood is computed at every gradient update using only one additional MCMC step per permanent chain, making it negligible relative to the 100-step PCD cost.

## Method

The paper introduces three complementary techniques built on the insight that RBM training follows a sequence of continuous (second-order) phase transitions, unlike temperature annealing which induces first-order transitions on clustered data. First, Trajectory Annealing Importance Sampling (Tr-AIS) replaces the temperature ladder with the actual training trajectory for partition function estimation, enabling online log-likelihood computation at one MCMC step per update. Second, Parallel Trajectory Tempering (PTT) exchanges configurations between RBMs at different training checkpoints (rather than at different temperatures) using a Metropolis acceptance rule based on energy differences between adjacent models, achieving 10–100× more mode jumps than standard AGS, PT, or Stacked Tempering. Third, a low-rank RBM pre-training phase encodes the first d PCA directions via a convex optimization over a Restricted Coulomb Machine surrogate (with an added bias direction for image data), bypassing the critical slowing-down transitions that degrade early PCD gradients and enabling efficient static Monte Carlo initialization of the sampling chains.

## Results

PTT consistently outperforms all compared MCMC methods (AGS, PT, PTref, Stacked Tempering) in the number of mode-to-mode jumps across MNIST-01, HGD, and Ising 2D, achieving proper equilibrium in ~10⁴ MCMC steps where AGS requires 10⁶–10⁷ steps. Online Tr-AIS yields lower log-likelihood estimation error than standard temperature AIS (across all tested N_β values) and AIS with a non-flat reference distribution, while being computationally cheap enough for online evaluation during training. Pretraining with a low-rank RBM (via Restricted Coulomb Machine convex optimization) improves test log-likelihood and balances multimodal sample distributions on all clustered datasets (MNIST-01, Mickey, HGD, Ising 2D), with minimal benefit on less-structured CelebA and full MNIST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard Parallel Tempering (PT, Hukushima & Nemoto 1996) and its reference-configuration variant (PTref, Krause et al. 2020), PTT produces substantially more mode jumps on all three tested datasets (MNIST-01, HGD, Ising 2D), because the training trajectory induces continuous (second-order) transitions rather than the first-order transitions that cause sharp acceptance-rate drops in temperature-based PT. Against Stacked Tempering (Roussel et al. 2023), PTT achieves comparable or superior jump counts without requiring sequential training of multiple stacked models, making it directly applicable to any trained RBM. Against standard Annealed Importance Sampling (AIS, Neal 2001), the proposed Trajectory AIS (Tr-AIS) achieves lower LL estimation error at every training step in controlled experiments (≤20 hidden nodes, exact partition function available), and uniquely enables online LL computation at negligible per-step cost (1 MCMC step vs. 100 for standard PCD). The low-rank RBM pretraining (extending Decelle & Furtlehner 2021a) is not directly compared as a sampling method but improves downstream PCD training quality: pre-trained+PCD RBMs achieve higher test log-likelihood and more balanced cluster weights than PCD-from-scratch on all clustered datasets, while showing no significant advantage on CelebA or full MNIST where the data lack strong multimodal structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the RBM training trajectory as a natural annealing schedule, building on the physics of phase transitions in RBM learning (Decelle et al. 2017, 2018; Bachtis et al. 2024) and repurposing two classical MCMC tools—Parallel Tempering (Hukushima & Nemoto, 1996) and Annealed Importance Sampling (Neal, 2001)—by replacing temperature ladders with sequences of saved model checkpoints. The pretraining component extends the low-rank RBM / Restricted Coulomb Machine framework of Decelle & Furtlehner (2021a) from synthetic low-dimensional data to real datasets by adding a trainable bias direction and refining the static Monte Carlo sampling procedure. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the observation that temperature-based annealing induces first-order (discontinuous) transitions in multimodal RBMs, while the training trajectory traverses only continuous (second-order) transitions, making trajectory-based exchanges fundamentally more effective for ergodicity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For scientific ML applications (genomics, neuroscience, statistical physics) where RBMs are valued for interpretability and where data are often small and highly structured, this paper provides a practical toolkit—PTT for sampling, Tr-AIS for online log-likelihood monitoring, and low-rank pretraining for initialization—that addresses the core bottleneck of MCMC mixing on multimodal distributions. The demonstration that standard Parallel Tempering can silently forget entire data clusters while maintaining high swap acceptance rates is a cautionary finding for practitioners relying on PT. Limitations include the modest dataset scale (all under 50k samples, ≤1024 dimensions), the fact that pretraining offers little benefit for less-clustered data (CelebA, full MNIST), and the acknowledgment that for long training runs, chains still drift from equilibrium, suggesting future work should combine these strategies with more aggressive MCMC acceleration.
