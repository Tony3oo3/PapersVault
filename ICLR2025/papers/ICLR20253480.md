## Problem

Applying generative AI to analog IC design is hindered by two critical gaps: the absence of a comprehensive, multi-type analog circuit topology dataset, and the lack of a scalable, unambiguous representation that captures low-level device-pin connections. Existing methods (CktGNN, LaMAGIC, AnalogCoder) are restricted to single circuit types, small circuits (≤22 devices), or error-prone code generation, leaving the most creative and foundational stage of analog design—topology discovery—largely unautomated.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single starting token 'VSS' (the universal ground node) as its sole input and autoregressively predicts a sequence of device-pin tokens (e.g., NM1_D, PM1_G, PM1_S) that form an Eulerian circuit encoding the full topology. The output sequence is constrained to maximum length 1024 and must start and end at VSS, visiting every directed edge exactly once. After fine-tuning, a circuit-type specification conditions generation toward a target class (e.g., Op-Amp, Bandgap reference), enabling the model to produce topologies with up to 64 devices spanning 11+ circuit types.

## Method

AnalogGenie represents each circuit topology as a pin-level undirected graph (each node is a device pin, not a device), then sequentializes it into an Eulerian circuit by converting to a directed graph and traversing every arc exactly once—a representation proven to always exist for any connected undirected graph. A decoder-only transformer (6 layers, 6 heads, 11.8M params, vocab 1029) is pre-trained with a standard next-token prediction objective on 3,350 real-world topologies augmented 70× via multiple Eulerian circuit traversals per topology, with BFS-based node ordering and fixed VSS start to enforce permutation invariance. The pin-level granularity eliminates the ambiguity of device-level graphs (where a single edge could map to multiple pins), and the Eulerian circuit's O(e) space complexity makes it far more efficient than O(n²) adjacency matrices for sparse analog circuits. Fine-tuning on performance-labeled subsets then steers generation toward high-FoM topologies for specific circuit classes.

## Results

AnalogGenie (augmented + pre-trained + fine-tuned) achieves 93.2% valid circuit generation, 99% novel topologies, and generates circuits with up to 56 devices across >11 circuit types. On Op-Amp design, it reaches a figure-of-merit (FoM) of 36.5, nearly 3.4× higher than CktGNN (10.9). It is the only method capable of generating bandgap reference circuits, achieving a FoM of 21.9. Data augmentation alone reduces validation loss by ~8.5× and increases valid circuit count by 73.5× over the unaugmented pre-trained model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnalogGenie outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated metric. Against CktGNN (graph VAE, 22-device limit), AnalogGenie improves valid circuit rate from 67.5% to 93.2%, extends maximum circuit size from 22 to 56–63 devices, and boosts Op-Amp FoM from 10.9 to 36.5, while matching its novelty rate (99% vs. 93.1%). Against LaMAGIC (masked LM, 4-device limit), AnalogGenie surpasses it on valid circuits (93.2% vs. 68.2%), novelty (99% vs. 12.7%), and power converter FoM (3.3 vs. 2.2), and generalizes to >11 circuit types versus LaMAGIC's single type. Against AnalogCoder (LLM code generation, 10-device limit), AnalogGenie more than doubles the valid circuit rate (93.2% vs. 57.3%), generates circuits 5–6× larger, and achieves 21× higher Op-Amp FoM (36.5 vs. 1.7). AnalogGenie is also the only method that successfully generates bandgap reference circuits, a task none of the baselines address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on analog circuit topology generation (CktGNN, LaMAGIC, AnalogCoder) by making two key conceptual departures: (1) shifting from device-level or code-level representations to a pin-level graph representation where each transistor pin is an individual node, resolving the ambiguity that a single generated graph could map to multiple valid topologies; and (2) replacing one-shot adjacency-matrix or code generation with sequential autoregressive generation over an Eulerian circuit, enabling scalable generation of arbitrarily large circuits. The autoregressive framework draws on the GPT language-modeling objective (Radford et al., 2018), while the BFS-based node ordering to handle permutation invariance is borrowed from GraphRNN (You et al., 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two fundamental bottlenecks in analog IC design automation: the absence of a comprehensive, multi-type topology dataset (3,350 circuits across 11 types, augmented 70×) and the lack of a scalable, unambiguous circuit representation. The pin-level Eulerian circuit formulation is clean and generalizable, potentially extending to digital and mixed-signal design as the authors note. Key open questions include whether the genetic-algorithm-based sizing step can be replaced by a learned optimizer, whether the approach scales to even larger circuits beyond 64 devices, and whether the zero-shot generalization to unseen circuit types holds as the design space grows. The practical impact is significant for EDA practitioners, as topology selection is the most creative and time-consuming stage of analog design.
