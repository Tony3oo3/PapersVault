## Problem

In-sample offline RL methods (IQL, SQL) avoid querying out-of-distribution actions but suffer from over-regularization on suboptimal or compositional datasets. The V-function overfits initial Q-value errors and propagates them through bootstrapping, causing catastrophic overestimation that prevents using larger expectile τ or smaller regularization α—precisely the settings needed to filter suboptimal actions effectively.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples collected by one or more behavior policies, with no further environment interaction. It outputs a policy π(a|s) learned via weighted behavior cloning with adaptive temperature, a Q-function Q(s,a), and an ensemble of m value functions (typically m=5–7) whose minimum is used for bootstrapping and whose c-th quantile shapes the advantage estimate for policy extraction. The ensemble aggregate for Q-updates can be either min or mean depending on the dataset.

## Method

ACTIVE combines two key modifications to generalized IQL. First, an in-sample V-ensemble of m independently trained value functions replaces the single V-network: the ensemble minimum serves as a lower-confidence-bound penalty during Q-bootstrapping (suppressing error accumulation), while the c-th quantile of the ensemble reshapes the advantage distribution for policy extraction, trading bias against sparsity. Second, an Adaptive Cloning Temperature (ACT) mechanism inspired by SAC's automated entropy adjustment performs dual gradient descent on a temperature β that controls the strength of imitation, combined with advantage-weighted behavior cloning as a safety net against over-regularization. An NTK-based theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.5) shows the V-ensemble penalizes the initial Q-error term Ω by a non-negative quantity B, formally guaranteeing reduced catastrophic overestimation.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (18 datasets across MuJoCo, AntMaze, and Kitchen), ACTIVE-S (SQL-based) achieves the best normalized scores on most MuJoCo tasks: 52.3 on halfcheetah-medium, 86.1 on hopper-medium, and 87.2 on walker2d-medium, outperforming SQL by 4.0, 10.6, and 3.0 points respectively. On suboptimal (medium-replay) datasets, ACTIVE-I achieves 85.4 on walker2d-medium-replay vs. SQL's 77.2 and IQL's 73.8. On compositional AntMaze tasks, ACTIVE-I reaches 91.6 on antmaze-umaze and 78.4 on antmaze-umaze-diverse, while on Kitchen tasks ACTIVE-S achieves 73.2 on kitchen-mixed vs. SQL's 67.4. Runtime overhead is modest: ACTIVE-S runs at 627 iter/s vs. SQL's 727 iter/s (~14% slower).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACTIVE-S (SQL-based) consistently outperforms SQL on MuJoCo medium and medium-replay datasets, with the largest gap on hopper-medium (86.1 vs. 75.5, +10.6) and halfcheetah-medium-replay (51.7 vs. 44.8, +6.9). ACTIVE-I (IQL-based) outperforms IQL on nearly all datasets, with particularly large gains on suboptimal data: walker2d-medium-replay (85.4 vs. 73.8, +11.6) and antmaze-umaze-diverse (78.4 vs. 62.2, +16.2). Against CQL and TD3+BC, both ACTIVE variants dominate on AntMaze and Kitchen tasks where these methods struggle (e.g., TD3+BC scores 0.0–10.6 on AntMaze), while on MuJoCo expert datasets CQL remains competitive (105.4 on hopper-medium-expert vs. ACTIVE-S's 109.9). Against ensemble-based baselines RORL and MSG on AntMaze (Table 1), ACTIVE-I achieves a 69.9 average vs. RORL's 65.1 and MSG's 74.4, with ACTIVE-I outperforming both on antmaze-large-play (51.2 vs. 16.3 and 46.8). On Kitchen tasks, SAC-N with ensemble sizes 20 and 40 completely diverges (score 0.0), while ACTIVE-S achieves 66.5–73.2. The main weakness is ACTIVE-S on antmaze-umaze-diverse (43.8±22.1), where high variance and the SQL-style loss with small α=0.5 still struggle, though ACTIVE-I handles this case better (78.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalized IQL framework (Kostrikov et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2023; Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023) by identifying V-function overfitting as the specific bottleneck that prevents milder regularization in in-sample offline RL. It departs from prior ensemble-based offline RL methods (SAC-N, RORL, MSG) that target out-of-distribution uncertainty by instead capturing in-sample epistemic uncertainty through a V-ensemble, and adapts SAC's automated entropy adjustment (Haarnoja et al., 2018) into a dual-gradient-descent mechanism for adaptive cloning temperature. The theoretical analysis draws on the NTK framework used by Ghasemipour & Gu (2022) to characterize how the ensemble minimum suppresses initial Q-error propagation during bootstrapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, modular fix (V-ensemble + adaptive temperature) that can be dropped into any IQL variant to alleviate over-regularization on suboptimal or compositional datasets, which is a common failure mode in real-world offline RL. However, the NTK analysis relies on a strong fixed-implicit-policy assumption that does not hold in actual IQL training, and the authors acknowledge that in-sample critics may only generalize locally, leaving open how to combine in-sample and out-of-sample learning for broader generalization. The finding that SAC-N completely diverges on Kitchen tasks (scoring 0.0) while ACTIVE succeeds highlights a practical fragility of OOD-based ensemble methods in compositional settings.
