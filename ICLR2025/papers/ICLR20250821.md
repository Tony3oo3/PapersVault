## Problem

Existing ECHO video generation models rely on complex conditional prompts (segmentation masks, clinical text, optical flow) that require expert annotations, suffer from high computational costs and slow inference due to diffusion-based architectures, and fail to incorporate time-series cardiac information. No prior work has used readily accessible ECG signals as a conditioning input for controllable ECHO video generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250821/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ECHOPulse takes a multi-lead ECG signal (variable length, processed through the ST-MEM foundation model into 768-dim patch embeddings) and an optional conditioning image (first ECHO frame at 128×128) as input, producing a 128×128 ECHO video. The video tokenizer operates on 11-frame sequences with temporal patch size 2 and spatial patch size 8, yielding 1536 tokens per sequence. Unlimited-length generation is achieved by autoregressively re-encoding the last K frames and feeding new ECG segments to the transformer, enabling continuous multi-beat video synthesis.

## Method

ECHOPulse comprises three components trained in two stages: (1) a ViViT-inspired VQ-VAE video tokenizer with Lookup-Free Quantization (LFQ) and LoRA-based domain adaptation, trained first on WebVid-10M then fine-tuned on CAMUS; (2) a bidirectional transformer using MaskGIT-style masked visual token modeling (MVTM) that aligns ECG patch embeddings with video tokens via cross-modal attention, trained on a 94K-sample private ECHO-ECG dataset; and (3) a progressive generation pipeline that iteratively decodes masked tokens with classifier-free guidance and extends video length autoregressively. The tokenizer uses an adaptive GAN loss balanced against perceptual loss via gradient-norm weighting, while the transformer is regularized with a token-critic BCE loss. This design achieves 6.4s generation time for 64 frames (vs. 147s for EchoDiffusion) while producing ECHO videos whose cardiac phases (end-diastole at R-wave, end-systole at T-wave) are tightly synchronized with the input ECG.

## Results

On the private ECHO-ECG dataset with ECG conditioning, ECHOPulse (domain transfer) achieves FID 15.50 (A2C) / 20.82 (A4C), FVD 82.44 / 107.40, and SSIM 0.67 / 0.66, outperforming EchoNet-Synthetic (FID 18.39/26.13, FVD 91.29/120.91) and EchoDiffusion (FID 20.71/23.20, FVD 379.43/390.17). For ejection fraction accuracy, ECHOPulse achieves R²=0.85, MAE=2.51, RMSE=2.86, with ED/ES frame errors of only 0.18/0.21 (vs. 1.06/0.97 for EchoNet-Synthetic). Generation of 64 frames takes 6.4 seconds, roughly 3× faster than EchoNet-Synthetic (20.2s) and 23× faster than EchoDiffusion (147s), with 279M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EchoNet-Synthetic (Reynaud et al., 2024), ECHOPulse wins on all image quality metrics under ECG conditioning on the private dataset (FID 15.50 vs. 18.39 A2C; FVD 82.44 vs. 91.29; SSIM 0.67 vs. 0.56), achieves lower EF error (MAE 2.51 vs. 2.71; RMSE 2.86 vs. 3.13), and generates 64 frames in 6.4s vs. 20.2s. Against EchoDiffusion (Reynaud et al., 2023), ECHOPulse dominates across every metric: FID 15.50 vs. 20.71, FVD 82.44 vs. 379.43, and generation time 6.4s vs. 147s. On the CAMUS dataset with text conditioning, ECHOPulse achieves the best FID (5.65 A2C) and SSIM (0.79) among all compared methods, surpassing HeartBeat (FID 25.23, SSIM 0.66) and MoonShot (FID 48.44), though HeartBeat retains a lower FVD (97.28 vs. 211.85) due to its six multimodal conditions. For cardiac phase alignment, ECHOPulse's ED/ES frame errors (0.18/0.21) are roughly 5–8× smaller than EchoNet-Synthetic (1.06/0.97) and EchoDiffusion (1.55/1.25), confirming tighter ECG-to-video temporal synchronization. Overall, ECHOPulse is the only method that uses a time-series ECG signal as the sole conditioning input, eliminating the need for expert-annotated masks, sketches, or clinical text while matching or exceeding the quality of methods that rely on multiple expert-provided conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECHOPulse sits at the intersection of discrete video tokenization (VQ-VAE, MAGVIT2's LFQ) and masked generative modeling (MaskGIT/MVTM), repurposing these well-established components for a medical imaging domain. It departs from prior ECHO video generation work (HeartBeat, EchoNet-Synthetic, EchoDiffusion) in two key ways: replacing diffusion-based iterative denoising with a parallel masked-token decoding scheme for speed, and substituting expert-curated conditions (segmentation masks, clinical text, EF values) with readily available ECG time-series signals encoded via a pretrained ECG foundation model (ST-MEM). The spatial-temporal transformer architecture for video tokenization draws directly from ViViT and C-ViViT, while LoRA-based domain transfer from natural video (WebVid-10M) to medical video addresses the data scarcity problem specific to ECHO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By conditioning on ECG—a signal universally available in clinical and wearable settings without expert annotation—ECHOPulse removes the primary bottleneck in prior ECHO generation pipelines and enables patient-specific, time-synchronized video synthesis at 3–23× faster inference than diffusion-based alternatives. The substantially lower ED/ES frame errors (0.18/0.21 vs. 1.06/0.97) suggest ECG conditioning captures cardiac phase dynamics more faithfully than scalar EF or text prompts. However, the main ECG-conditioned results rely on a non-public private dataset, the model cannot yet improve resolution or frame rate, and failure cases persist under poor-quality inputs, leaving open questions about robustness at scale and whether integrating diffusion-based refinement can close the remaining quality gap with the best diffusion baselines.
