## Problem

Bradley-Terry and Regression style reward models are the two dominant paradigms for training reward models in RLHF, but no public dataset exists that provides adequately matched data for both approaches, making a fair head-to-head comparison impossible. The two paradigms require incompatible annotation formats (pairwise preferences vs. individual Likert ratings), and post-hoc conversion between them loses fine-grained preference signal. This paper releases preference annotations collected alongside the existing HelpSteer2 ratings to enable the first controlled comparison and to explore whether combining both paradigms yields a stronger reward model.

## Input / Output

For reward modeling, the input is a prompt paired with a response, and the output is either a single scalar reward (Bradley-Terry variants) or five attribute scores on a 0–4 Likert scale (SteerLM Regression). The HelpSteer2-Preference dataset provides 7,118 preference pairs where each pair carries a preference direction, a strength magnitude (1 = slightly better, 2 = better, 3 = much better), and a human-written free-text justification (~4 sentences, ~500 characters). For downstream alignment, the input is a text prompt and the output is a generated text response, evaluated on MT-Bench, AlpacaEval 2.0 LC, and Arena Hard.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage reward model: (1) train a Helpfulness-only SteerLM Regression model on HelpSteer2 ratings, (2) initialize a Scaled Bradley-Terry model on top of it and fine-tune on HelpSteer2-Preference using a novel Scaled BT loss that multiplies the standard BT loss by the preference magnitude m (placing the margin outside the log-sigmoid rather than inside, unlike Margin BT), and (3) apply ExPO weak-to-strong extrapolation (factor 1.52) between the regression model (weak) and the Scaled BT model (strong). This combined model achieves 94.1 on RewardBench. For alignment, the reward model drives REINFORCE with KL-regularized reward and a leave-one-out baseline (4 samples per prompt), which outperforms PPO and all DPO variants, reaching 85.0 on Arena Hard. The paper also trains Pairwise Justifier models that generate human-style preference explanations, finding them less accurate but more interpretable than score-based reward models.

## Results

The best reward model (Scaled BT + ExPO, built on Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct) achieves 94.1 overall on RewardBench, ranking #1 among 140+ models as of 1 Oct 2024. When used with REINFORCE for RLHF alignment, the resulting Instruct model reaches 85.0 on Arena Hard (No.1 as of 1 Oct 2024), 57.6 on AlpacaEval 2.0 LC, and 8.98 on GPT-4-Turbo MT-Bench. The SteerLM Regression (Helpfulness Only) and Scaled BT variants perform comparably in isolation (93.0 vs 92.7), but combining them via initialization plus ExPO extrapolation yields a synergistic gain to 94.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the top external baseline Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B (93.8), Scaled BT + ExPO wins on overall RewardBench (94.1) and on Chat, Safety, and Reasoning, but trails substantially on Chat-Hard (85.7 vs 91.4). The authors attribute this gap to GPT-4 annotation bias in Chat-Hard subsets (LLMBar-Adversarial-GPTInst/GPTOut and MT-Bench hard), where Skywork's training data includes GPT-4-judged samples; on human-annotated Chat-Hard subsets the gap shrinks to ≤2.2%. Against Nemotron-4-340B-Reward (92.0) and Llama3-70B-SteerLM-RM (88.8), the proposed model wins by 2.1 and 5.3 points respectively. For aligned models, REINFORCE (85.0 Arena Hard, 57.6 AlpacaEval 2.0 LC) outperforms Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (55.7 / 38.1) by a large margin and surpasses GPT-4o (79.3 / 57.5) on Arena Hard while matching it on AlpacaEval, though it trails Claude-3.5-Sonnet (79.2) on Arena Hard by 5.8 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established reward-modeling paradigms: Bradley-Terry preference optimization (Ouyang et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2022) and SteerLM regression-style scoring (Wang et al., 2024d). It introduces the Scaled Bradley-Terry loss as a simple modification of the standard BT objective that weights samples by preference magnitude, and builds on the Margin BT formulation from Llama 2 (Touvron et al., 2023). The combined pipeline—initializing a Scaled BT model with a Helpfulness-only SteerLM Regression checkpoint and then applying ExPO weak-to-strong extrapolation (Zheng et al., 2024)—repurposes an alignment technique from policy optimization into reward-model weight space, a use not previously explored in the reward-modeling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first adequately-matched head-to-head comparison of Bradley-Terry and Regression reward models, finding them roughly equivalent (92.7 vs 93.0) when data quality is held constant—a result that challenges the field's implicit assumption that one paradigm is inherently superior. The released HelpSteer2-Preference dataset (7,118 pairs with human-written justifications) is the first open-source general-domain preference dataset with justifications, enabling future work on interpretable reward modeling. Key limitations include the small dataset size (6.6k training pairs), general-domain-only scope, a noted 5.1% position bias, and the fact that the best model's Chat-Hard weakness is partly attributable to GPT-4-judgment bias in the benchmark rather than true capability gaps. Open questions include whether the BT-Regression synergy generalizes to larger datasets, other domains, and whether the Scaled BT loss advantage persists at scale.
