## Problem

Existing two-character interaction generation methods either require the counterpart's complete motion sequence or jointly generate both characters' motions from shared conditions, neither of which models the real-time, independent reaction process humans use. This paper addresses the gap by proposing an online reaction policy where each character independently predicts its next pose from only past observed motions, enabling streaming two-character interaction without a length limit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252955/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes W=60 frames (2 seconds at 30 FPS) of past motion for both the agent and opponent, where each frame encodes root offset/direction (2D), joint positions, 6D rotations, and velocities in a root-relative coordinate system; the opponent's motion is expressed relative to the agent's root. For two-character generation, only 4 initial pose frames are required to bootstrap the streaming loop. The output is 4 frames of agent motion per step (d=4, matching the VQ-VAE downsampling factor), produced continuously in a streaming fashion with a bounded history buffer, and optionally conditioned on sparse head/hand position and rotation signals for VR control.

## Method

The architecture combines a VQ-VAE motion encoder (downsampling factor d=4, codebook size 512) with a transformer-based condition encoder that fuses the agent's quantized motion latents and the opponent's MLP-encoded features, followed by a diffusion-based next-latent predictor (a single-layer MLP denoiser) that generates the next motion latent conditioned on the transformer output. A key design choice is replacing GPT-style token prediction with a diffusion head, which preserves the continuous nature of motion and reduces cumulative error over long sequences. An online motion decoder (transformer) then converts the predicted latent into explicit poses using only the last d frames and two consecutive latent codes, plus root information (position/direction relative to opponent and distance to ring center), enabling real-time decoding without the full sequence. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-training the VQ-VAE for 40k iterations, then jointly training the predictor and decoder with causal masks and ground-truth latents for 40k iterations, using DDIM (50 steps) at inference.

## Results

On the DuoBox dataset, Ready-to-React achieves a per-clip FID of 9.599 in reactive motion generation and 25.283 in two-character interaction generation, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin (next-best: 13.606 and 47.194 respectively). In the long-term 1800-frame generation setting, the method achieves a per-clip FID of 36.755, more than 2× better than the next-best baseline (InterFormer at 87.697). The method also matches ground-truth foot sliding (0.97 vs. 0.97) and root orientation (24.1% vs. 24.7%) in the two-character setting, while reducing jitter to 16.844 (vs. 6.117 for InterFormer, which is deterministic and lacks diversity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ready-to-React outperforms all four baselines on per-clip FID in both reactive and two-character settings, with the largest margins over T2MGPT-online (30.159→9.599 reactive; 122.463→25.283 two-character) and CVAE-AR (26.010→9.599; 92.978→25.283). InterFormer, a deterministic model, achieves the lowest jitter (6.712 reactive, 6.117 two-character) but has zero diversity (APD=0) and substantially worse FID; it also produces incorrect root orientations over time. CAMDM shows the worst foot sliding (1.96–2.01) and high jitter (53.6–54.0), indicating characters tend to get stuck, while T2MGPT-online suffers severe error accumulation with the highest jitter (78.3) and worst two-character FID (122.463). Duolando-offline is competitive on reactive FID (13.606) but cannot perform two-character generation due to its reliance on future information and offline decoding. On the Inter-X dataset (Appendix H), the method also outperforms CAMDM and T2MGPT-online across chat, kick, and dance categories, confirming generalizability beyond boxing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of reactive motion generation (InterFormer, ReGenNet, REMOS) and two-character motion generation (Starke et al., text-driven diffusion methods), but departs from both by framing the problem as an independent, streaming reaction policy applied separately to each character. It builds directly on the VQ-VAE latent motion representation from T2M-GPT (Zhang et al., 2023) and the conditional diffusion framework from CAMDM (Chen et al., 2024), but replaces the GPT-style token prediction with a diffusion-based latent predictor and introduces a dedicated online motion decoder that avoids the need for full-sequence decoding. The key conceptual departure is from prior two-character methods that either require the opponent's complete future motion (offline reactive) or jointly generate both characters from a shared condition, neither of which models the independent, real-time decision-making of two interacting agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap for VR, gaming, and robotics applications where characters must react in real time without access to the other's future actions, and demonstrates that error accumulation can be substantially mitigated (1800 frames vs. ~200 for GPT-based baselines). The sparse-signal controllability makes the approach directly applicable to VR headsets and hand trackers with limited input channels. However, the method is currently restricted to two characters and a single domain (boxing), with no modeling of environment or object interactions, leaving open questions about generalization to multi-person scenes, diverse contact-rich activities, and physically simulated characters.
