## Problem

Similarity measures such as CKA, angular Procrustes, NBS, and linear regression are widely used to quantify alignment between artificial models and neural data, yet it remains unclear what drives high scores and what constitutes a 'good' value in a given context. The paper shows that high similarity scores do not guarantee encoding of task-relevant information, that no universal threshold exists across measures or datasets, and that different measures differentially prioritize principal components of the data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252286/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference neural dataset (reshaped from time×sample×feature to time*sample×feature with mean-centered columns) and a synthetic dataset initialized as standard Gaussian noise of identical shape, along with a chosen similarity measure. During optimization it simultaneously outputs the evolving similarity score, logistic-regression decode accuracy for task variables (with stratified 5-fold CV, averaged across time steps for temporal data), per-PC capture R² via linear regression, and—when jointly optimizing two measures—the boundary of their jointly achievable score region. Optimization uses Adam and halts when the score reaches a fixed threshold near 1.

## Method

The core technique is to differentiate through the similarity score function and use gradient-based optimization (Adam) to directly maximize score(X, Y) with respect to a synthetic dataset Y initialized as Gaussian noise, thereby revealing what the measure actually rewards. In parallel, the authors track task-variable decode accuracy and per-principal-component R² to determine whether high scores correspond to meaningful information capture. A theoretical analysis derives that CKA depends quadratically on the variance of a perturbed principal component while NBS depends linearly, explaining CKA's bias toward high-variance dimensions; angular Procrustes inherits NBS's linear dependence at high variance but gains extra sensitivity at low variance through the arccosine transform. The authors further jointly optimize pairs of similarity measures to map their allowable score ranges, revealing asymmetric dependencies (e.g., high angular Procrustes implies high CKA but not vice versa). Task-optimized RNNs (LSTM, CTRNN, LowPassCTRNN with ReLU/ReTanh/Tanh) are additionally compared to neural data to show that different measures disagree on model rankings.

## Results

Across five nonhuman primate neural datasets, the similarity score required for a synthetic dataset to encode 90% of task-relevant information ranges from below 0.5 to nearly 1.0, depending on both the measure and the dataset (e.g., angular Procrustes >0.5 suffices for Mante 2013 PFC but >0.8 is needed for Siegel 2015 FEF). Theoretically, CKA depends quadratically on the variance of a perturbed principal component while NBS depends linearly, explaining CKA's strong bias toward high-variance dimensions. Joint optimization reveals that a high angular Procrustes score (0.9) guarantees a high CKA score, but the converse does not hold—a high CKA score of 0.9 permits a wide range of angular Procrustes values. Even cross-validated ridge regression (λ=100) can achieve near-perfect similarity scores without the synthetic data encoding task variables to the same degree as the neural data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper systematically compares five similarity measures—CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019), angular Procrustes (Williams et al., 2021), NBS (Tang et al., 2020), linear/ridge regression (Schrimpf et al., 2018), and angular CKA (Lange et al., 2022)—on the same neural datasets. CKA and unregularized linear regression achieve high scores while failing to capture lower-variance principal components that carry task-relevant information, whereas angular Procrustes captures those components at much lower similarity scores. NBS, positioned between CKA and angular Procrustes via the arccosine duality (Harvey et al., 2023), shows linear rather than quadratic sensitivity to PC variance, making it more balanced across dimensions. Angular CKA partially mitigates CKA's high-variance bias but does not fully match angular Procrustes in capturing lower-variance components. The measures are not mutually independent: high angular Procrustes subsumes high CKA, but high CKA does not subsume high angular Procrustes, and regularized cross-validated ridge regression can take values largely independent of angular Procrustes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the theoretical duality between Normalized Bures Similarity and Procrustes distance established by Harvey et al. (2023), using NBS as a mathematical bridge to explain why CKA (Frobenius norm) and NBS (nuclear norm) weight principal components differently. It departs from Ding et al. (2021), who evaluated similarity metrics by perturbing pre-trained models, by instead starting from unstructured Gaussian noise and differentiating through the similarity measures to directly optimize them, providing a dynamic view of what each measure prioritizes during the learning process. Prior reviews (Sucholutsky et al., 2024; Klabunde et al., 2023) catalogued invariance properties of these metrics but offered limited guidance on interpreting intermediate scores, which is the gap this paper addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that high CKA or linear regression scores do not guarantee task-relevant information is encoded in a manner consistent with neural data challenges a core assumption in model-brain comparison studies and has broad implications for the many papers that rely on these metrics. The theoretical result that CKA depends quadratically on PC variance while NBS depends linearly provides a clear mechanistic explanation for CKA's bias toward high-variance components, and the joint optimization revealing asymmetric dependencies between measures (high Procrustes implies high CKA but not vice versa) gives practitioners concrete constraints to consider. Limitations include the focus on differentiable geometry-based measures and the conflation of optimizer dynamics with measure properties; the authors note that extending the framework to non-differentiable measures and disentangling these effects are important next steps. The accompanying Python package standardizing ~100 similarity measures from 14 packages addresses a practical reproducibility gap in the field.
