## Problem

The common justification for Rotary Positional Encodings (RoPE) is that they decay attention coefficients as relative token distance increases, but this claim relies on unrealistic assumptions about constant query/key vectors. The paper asks: what is RoPE actually doing inside a trained LLM, and how are its different rotation frequencies being exploited by attention heads?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253330/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on d-dimensional query and key vectors (d=256 in Gemma 7B), decomposing them into d/2 two-dimensional chunks. Each chunk is rotated by a position-dependent angle at a distinct frequency g_k = θ^{-2(k-1)/d}. For the proposed p-RoPE variant, only the top p·(d/2) highest-frequency chunks receive rotation, while the remaining lowest-frequency chunks are left unrotated (equivalent to NoPE on those dimensions), producing a hybrid encoding that is partially positional and partially position-agnostic.

## Method

The paper combines theoretical proofs with mechanistic analysis of Gemma 7B and Llama 3.1 8B. It proves that (i) RoPE does not inherently decay activations for Gaussian-distributed queries/keys, (ii) high-frequency rotations enable sharp positional attention patterns (diagonal, previous-token) that NoPE provably cannot construct, and (iii) low-frequency 'semantic bands' used by Gemma are not robust over sufficiently long contexts due to the density of irrational rotations. Building on these findings, the authors propose p-RoPE, which truncates the lowest (1−p) fraction of RoPE frequencies to create distance-agnostic semantic channels while retaining positional information from higher frequencies. They validate p-RoPE by training Gemma 2B models from scratch on Wiki and FlanV2, showing that 0.75-RoPE matches or slightly exceeds standard RoPE (θ=10k) and outperforms NoPE, RoPE with θ=500k, and partial-RoPE baselines.

## Results

On Gemma 2B models trained from scratch with an 8k context, the proposed p-RoPE (0.75-RoPE, keeping 75% of RoPE frequencies) achieves the best validation perplexity of 4.4414 on Wiki and 6.4422 on FlanV2. This outperforms standard RoPE (θ=10k) at 4.4627/6.4429, RoPE with increased wavelength (θ=500k) at 4.4485/6.4593, and NoPE at 4.8594/6.6429. Truncating more aggressively (0.25-RoPE) degrades performance to 4.5302/6.5111, though still well above NoPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

p-RoPE (0.75-RoPE) outperforms all four baselines on both Wiki and FlanV2. Against NoPE (Haviv et al., 2022), p-RoPE improves Wiki perplexity by 0.42 (4.4414 vs 4.8594) and FlanV2 by 0.20 (6.4422 vs 6.6429), confirming that positional information is essential. Against standard RoPE θ=10k (Su et al., 2024), p-RoPE achieves a small but consistent improvement (4.4414 vs 4.4627 on Wiki; 6.4422 vs 6.4429 on FlanV2), supporting the claim that the lowest frequencies act as non-robust semantic channels. Against RoPE θ=500k (Xiong et al., 2023), p-RoPE wins on both datasets (4.4414 vs 4.4485; 6.4422 vs 6.4593), showing that removing the lowest frequencies is more effective than simply stretching the wavelength. Finally, p-RoPE outperforms RoPE-partial (Black et al., 2022), which removes the highest frequencies instead, by 0.0123 on Wiki and 0.0140 on FlanV2, validating the paper's theoretical argument that it is the lowest frequencies—not the highest—that should be removed to create robust semantic channels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the mechanistic interpretability tradition (Elhage et al., 2021; Olsson et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022) but applies it to a 'lower level' question: how RoPE's individual frequency components are actually exploited by trained attention heads, rather than cataloguing high-level head functionalities. It departs from the original RoPE justification (Su et al., 2024) and the long-context scaling literature (Xiong et al., 2023; Dubey et al., 2024) by arguing that the commonly cited distance-decay property is neither necessary nor the primary mechanism, and reframes the role of increasing base wavelength as providing more 'slow enough' frequencies for semantic channels. The NoPE line of work (Haviv et al., 2022; Kazemnejad et al., 2024) is engaged with directly, with the paper showing that NoPE and RoPE have complementary strengths—NoPE cannot construct positional heads, while RoPE lacks robust semantic channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why Llama 3's choice of θ=500k helps long-context performance (more low-frequency 'semantic channel' capacity) and proposes p-RoPE as a simpler alternative that removes the least-robust frequencies. However, the practical validation is limited to 2B-parameter Gemma models at 8k context, and the authors explicitly note they lack resources to test at the 32k+ contexts where wavelength effects are typically observed. Open questions include whether the frequency-usage patterns generalize to non-RoPE architectures, whether p-RoPE's gains persist at scale, and whether the 'positional head' constructions (diagonal, previous-token) serve a genuine functional purpose or represent training artifacts.
