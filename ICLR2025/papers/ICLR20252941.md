## Problem

Symbolic regression methods based on genetic programming or neural networks struggle to consistently recover accurate formulas on challenging benchmarks, with most algorithms achieving below 30% success rates on SRBench within 8 hours. Continuous optimization approaches like EQL overcome some limitations but rely on nested linear layers that inflate parameter counts, use local optimizers prone to local minima, and cannot handle key operators like division or exponentials within unary positions. ParFam addresses these gaps by reformulating SR as a continuous optimization over rational-function parametric families, enabling global optimization while maintaining interpretability.

## Input / Output

ParFam takes tabular input-output pairs (x_i, y_i) with x_i ∈ R^n (up to 9 variables) and produces a symbolic formula composed of rational functions and a user-specified set of base functions (sin, exp, √, cos). The DL-ParFam variant additionally outputs predicted model parameters—polynomial degrees for numerator/denominator and which base functions to use—via a pre-trained Set Transformer, which then constrains the search space for the subsequent basin-hopping optimization. Input data is normalized to [-1, 1] for the neural-guided variant, and the final formula is extracted by zeroing out coefficients below a threshold and refining with L-BFGS.

## Method

ParFam constructs a single-hidden-layer architecture where inter-layer connections are rational functions Q_i(x) = p_i^1(x)/p_i^2(x) rather than linear maps, and activations are physically relevant unary functions (sin, exp, √). The learnable parameters are the polynomial coefficients, optimized via basin-hopping (a global optimizer combining BFGS local search with Metropolis-acceptance random perturbations) on an MSE + L1 sparsity loss, followed by a thresholding-and-refinement step. The theoretical expressivity analysis uses generating functions to show that ParFam covers ~90% of all expression trees of complexity 5 and ~82% of complexity 10, despite forbidding nested unary compositions. DL-ParFam adds a pre-trained Set Transformer encoder (trained on ~4M synthetic functions) that predicts the optimal model parameters (degrees and base functions) from the data, reducing the search space and accelerating optimization by up to two orders of magnitude while maintaining an end-to-end differentiable training pipeline.

## Results

On the SRBench ground-truth problems (Feynman + Strogatz, 8 CPU-hour limit), ParFam ranks second to PySR on symbolic solution rate, with both outperforming all other competitors by over 20 percentage points. ParFam achieves the best accuracy solution rate (percentage of problems with R² > 0.999) among all tested methods, both with and without noise. DL-ParFam, the neural-guided variant, achieves competitive accuracy while being up to 100× faster than its competitors. On the 77 black-box problems (≤10 variables, 48 CPU-hour limit), ParFam remains among the top-performing algorithms in median R² and formula complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParFam is second only to PySR on symbolic solution rate but surpasses PySR on accuracy solution rate (R² > 0.999), making it the most accurate method overall. Against AI Feynman and uDSR, ParFam achieves higher accuracy solution rates while being competitive on symbolic recovery. DL-ParFam outperforms EndToEnd (Kamienny et al., 2022) on all three metrics (symbolic rate, accuracy rate, and training time) and also beats NeSymRes (Biggio et al., 2021) on the reduced 3-variable subset where NeSymRes is applicable. Compared to EQL (Martius & Lampert, 2017), the closest architectural relative, ParFam achieves substantially better results on a reduced benchmark due to its rational-function layers and global optimization. On the broader SRBench leaderboard, ParFam outperforms MRGP, Operon, and all genetic-programming-based methods (including NGGP and SPL) on accuracy solution rate, while PySR remains the strongest on symbolic recovery thanks to its noise filter. DL-ParFam's main weakness is reduced symbolic recovery under low-noise conditions, where the pre-trained Set Transformer's top-1 model-parameter prediction accuracy drops from 31.4% (synthetic) to 30.4% (Feynman), limiting its ability to select the exact parametric family needed.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParFam is positioned as a direct architectural successor to EQL (Martius & Lampert, 2017), replacing its linear inter-layer connections with rational functions and its local ADAM optimizer with basin-hopping global optimization. This departs from the dominant genetic programming paradigm (PySR, AI Feynman, MRGP) by eliminating the discrete search over expression trees entirely, and from pre-training-based methods (NeSymRes, EndToEnd, uDSR) by predicting compact model parameters (polynomial degrees, base functions) rather than full symbolic expressions. The expressivity analysis extends the expression-tree counting framework of Lample & Charton (2019) to quantify exactly what fraction of formulas the restricted single-hidden-layer architecture can represent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first continuous-optimization-based SR method to match the performance of top genetic programming algorithms on SRBench, validating that the discrete-to-continuous translation is viable for practical scientific discovery. The theoretical expressivity bounds (covering ~80–90% of formulas at typical physical-law complexities) provide rare formal guarantees in a field dominated by heuristic search. Key limitations remain: the parameter count grows as O(n^d) making high-dimensional problems (>10 variables) expensive, and DL-ParFam's pre-training cost (~93 GPU-hours) limits flexibility. Open questions include whether alternative regularization or custom-tailored optimizers can further close the gap with PySR on noisy data, and whether the Set Transformer guidance generalizes beyond the 9-variable limit.
