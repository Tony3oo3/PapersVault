## Problem

Backdoor attacks on Vision-Language Models for image-to-text generation tasks remain largely unexplored, and existing methods either destroy the semantic coherence of generated text under poisoned inputs or assume unrealistic access to the original training data. This paper addresses the practical and more challenging scenario where an attacker must inject a backdoor using only Out-of-Distribution (OOD) public data while preserving both clean-model behavior and conceptual consistency of poisoned outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253158/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The backdoored VLM receives an RGB image (optionally with a small pixel-pattern trigger, e.g., 20×20 Gaussian noise patch at a random location) paired with a text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of' for captioning) or a question (for VQA), and generates a free-form text description or answer. Under poisoned inputs, the output embeds a predefined target phrase (e.g., 'bad model with backdoor injection') within otherwise semantically coherent text; the attacker constructs training data from only 3000 OOD image-text pairs, with poisoned counterparts created by attaching the trigger to the image and inserting the target text into the ground-truth caption.

## Method

VLOOD fine-tunes only the Q-Former adapter of a pre-trained VLM (BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4, or InstructBLIP) on 3000 clean + 3000 poisoned OOD samples, combining three loss components. Clean Knowledge Preservation (CKP) applies KL-divergence-based knowledge distillation between the frozen benign model and the trainable backdoored model on clean inputs, anchoring the backdoored model's output distribution to the original. Conceptual Consistency Preservation (CCP) constrains predicted token embeddings to ground-truth token embeddings via L1 (Manhattan) distance on poisoned samples, normalized through a sigmoid to yield smooth gradients and sparse alignment. A dynamically adjusted weight λ is updated each epoch based on the cross-entropy impact scores of clean versus poisoned data, shifting emphasis between the two branches of the overall loss to balance clean-input fidelity, poisoned-input ASR, and semantic coherence simultaneously.

## Results

On the image captioning task (Flickr8K), VLOOD achieves a CIDEr of 115.0 and BLEU@4 of 36.9 on clean inputs (matching the clean model's 113.5/36.9) while maintaining a CIDEr of 110.7 and BLEU@4 of 36.1 on poisoned inputs with a 99.9% attack success rate (ASR). On the VQA task (VQAv2), VLOOD achieves a VQA score of 60.9 on clean inputs (vs. 66.1 for the clean model) and 56.6 on poisoned inputs with 98.3% ASR. The method generalizes across BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4, and InstructBLIP architectures, consistently achieving >99% ASR while preserving conceptual consistency on both clean and poisoned inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLOOD's key advantage is maintaining high text quality on both clean and poisoned inputs simultaneously, whereas all six baselines sacrifice one or the other. Against BadNet and AnyDoor, VLOOD matches poisoned-input quality (CIDEr 110.7 vs. 109.6 and 107.7) but dramatically outperforms on clean inputs (CIDEr 115.0 vs. 50.0 and 57.6), showing these baselines severely degrade the model's normal behavior. Against Blended and Shadowcast, VLOOD is overwhelmingly superior on poisoned inputs (CIDEr 110.7 vs. 6.9 for both), as these methods completely destroy semantic coherence while injecting the backdoor. Against Poisoning (CLIP-based), VLOOD achieves comparable poisoned-input quality (CIDEr 110.7 vs. 111.6) but far better clean-input performance (115.0 vs. 46.7). BadEncoder fails entirely in the VLM setting, achieving 0% ASR and near-zero quality metrics on both input types, confirming that encoder-level attacks do not transfer to image-to-text generation. On the VQA task, VLOOD achieves the best clean-input VQA score (60.9 on VQAv2) among all backdoored methods while maintaining 98.3% ASR, outperforming AnyDoor (54.8 VQA score, 98.9% ASR) and Poisoning (54.5, 99.1%) on conceptual consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM backdoor attack line (Shadowcast, AnyDoor, TrojVLM) by removing the unrealistic assumption of access to original training data, instead operating solely on Out-of-Distribution public data. Conceptually, it repurposes knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) as a clean-knowledge preservation mechanism and introduces an L1-embedding constraint for conceptual consistency in the generation setting—neither of which has a direct predecessor in the backdoor literature. The dynamic weight adjustment mechanism is novel, balancing the tension between CKP's tendency to wash out the backdoor and CCP's tendency to leak ASR onto clean inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a practical, low-resource attacker (3000 OOD pairs, ~8 min/epoch) can implant a persistent backdoor in production VLMs while preserving output quality, and that no existing defense (Spectral Signatures, Beatrix) is effective against image-to-text generation backdoors. This exposes a significant and currently unaddressed security gap: defenses designed for classification tasks fundamentally cannot transfer to open-ended generation. Open questions include whether trigger-free or semantic-level backdoors are possible, whether fine-tuning-based defenses can be adapted, and how to detect backdoors in autoregressive text generation where there is no fixed target class.
