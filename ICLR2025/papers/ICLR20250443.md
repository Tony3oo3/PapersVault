## Problem

Standard privacy analysis for Noisy-SGD via composition theorems yields a divergent privacy bound as the number of iterations grows, while output perturbation gives a convergent but loose bound. Recent works by Altschuler & Talwar (2022) and Ye & Shokri (2022) proved convergent, tighter bounds for hidden-state Noisy-SGD, but only under smooth and (strongly) convex loss assumptions. The open question is whether these restrictive assumptions can be relaxed while still achieving a privacy bound that outperforms the Pareto frontier of standard composition and output perturbation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Noisy-SGD training configuration (step size η, batch size b, noise standard deviation σ, gradient clipping norm K, domain diameter D, total iterations T) and a loss function whose gradient is (L,λ)-Hölder continuous for some λ ∈ (0,1]. It produces a worst-case Rényi DP bound ε(α) on the last iterate W_T, expressed as a constrained optimization over a burn-in time τ, per-step noise allocation parameters β_t ∈ [0,1], and shift parameters a_t ≥ 0. The bound is convergent in T, meaning it plateaus after a finite burn-in period rather than growing linearly with iterations.

## Method

The analysis builds on the shifted Rényi divergence framework of Altschuler & Talwar (2022), decomposing privacy loss into a standard composition term (Part A) and a shifted divergence term (Part B) via a coupling that splits the Gaussian noise into shared and independent components. Three key improvements enable the generalization: (1) a forward Wasserstein distance tracking lemma (Lemma 3.5) that recursively bounds W_∞(W_τ, W_τ') using the actual contraction/expansion of the gradient update map rather than the crude domain diameter D; (2) an optimal shift allocation derived via Cauchy-Schwarz that distributes the accumulated discrepancy across time steps with weights c^{-(t-τ+1)}, yielding a strictly tighter bound even for smooth strongly convex losses; and (3) a Hölder reduction lemma (Lemma 3.8) that replaces the Lipschitz reduction lemma, showing that a (L,λ)-Hölder continuous map φ transforms shifted divergence via the inverse map h of x + Lx^λ, thereby removing the smoothness requirement entirely. The resulting privacy bound is a closed-form optimization that can be solved numerically and is strictly better than both prior hidden-state analyses under their shared assumptions.

## Results

The paper establishes that Noisy-SGD over a bounded domain achieves convergent Rényi DP privacy loss (bounded as T→∞) even for non-convex, non-smooth losses, requiring only Hölder continuous gradients of order λ∈(0,1]. For smooth strongly convex losses, the derived bound is strictly tighter than both Altschuler & Talwar (2022) and Ye & Shokri (2022), as shown in Figure 1(b). The key quantitative improvement comes from forward Wasserstein distance tracking (Lemma 3.5) combined with optimal shift allocation, which avoids using the full domain diameter D as a crude upper bound. In the non-smooth case (Theorem 3.7), the privacy loss converges after a burn-in period, with the convergence rate governed by the Hölder constant L and order λ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Altschuler & Talwar (2022), the paper's bound is strictly tighter for smooth strongly convex losses (Figure 1b), while it degenerates exactly to their bound for smooth convex losses. Against Ye & Shokri (2022), the same strict improvement holds for smooth strongly convex losses, and the paper additionally removes the convexity requirement entirely. Against the concurrent work of Kong & Ribero (2024), the comparison is stark: their bound (Theorem 2.2 in their paper) takes the form α/(2σ²)·(C₁D + C₂)² with C₁>1, which is strictly worse than the output perturbation bound αD²/(2σ²), whereas this paper's bound is strictly better than output perturbation. The paper also notes that Kong & Ribero (2024) do not consider Hölder continuous gradients and use different conditions to control convexity. Overall, the paper's method wins on all three comparisons: it requires fewer assumptions (no convexity, no smoothness needed) while simultaneously providing tighter privacy bounds in the overlapping regime of smooth strongly convex losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the shifted Rényi divergence analysis framework introduced by Feldman et al. (2018) and refined by Altschuler & Talwar (2022) and Ye & Shokri (2022), which established convergent privacy bounds for Noisy-SGD under smooth (strongly) convex losses. The key departure is replacing the joint smoothness-and-convexity requirement with the strictly weaker condition of Hölder continuous gradients of order λ ∈ (0,1], which encompasses non-smooth and non-convex losses. Additionally, the paper introduces a forward Wasserstein distance tracking technique (Lemma 3.5) that tightens the shifted divergence bound even under the original smooth strongly convex assumptions, yielding a strict improvement over both Altschuler & Talwar (2022) and Ye & Shokri (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work answers an open question explicitly raised in two prior papers, showing that the restrictive smoothness and convexity assumptions are not fundamental to convergent hidden-state privacy loss. The practical significance is that many real-world losses (e.g., neural network training objectives) are neither smooth nor convex, yet may satisfy Hölder continuous gradient conditions, making tighter privacy accounting applicable. Key open questions remain: whether gradient clipping's induced discontinuity can be handled without additional assumptions, whether per-layer or non-uniform Hölder constants yield practically useful bounds for deep networks, and how tight the derived bounds are (no matching lower bound is established for the non-smooth case).
