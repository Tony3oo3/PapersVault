## Problem

Existing lifelong learning frameworks for PLMs suffer from three interrelated limitations: growth operators that do not guarantee strict function preservation (causing a function gap that destabilizes training), growth schedules that only expand a subset of Transformer dimensions (typically layers and FFN width), and an assumption that emerging data shares the same distribution as pre-training data, which leads to catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initial PLM checkpoint (e.g., 27.59M-parameter GPT or BERT model) and a stream of incremental text corpora from distinct domains (Wikipedia/Books, RealNews, Amazon Reviews, Biomedical, CS papers), each sampled at 10 GB and split 9:1 into pre-training and recovery-memory subsets. At each of five growth stages, one architectural dimension is expanded (hidden size → FFN width → attention heads → layer count), and the model is trained on the next domain's data. The output is a progressively larger language model (up to 104.78M or 1.11B parameters) that produces standard next-token probability distributions while retaining knowledge from all previously seen domains.

## Method

LOIRE introduces four strict function-preserving growth operators covering all Transformer dimensions (hidden, FFN, MHA heads, and layers). The key novelty is a plug-in layer growth operator that replicates a selected layer and inserts it between the original and subsequent layer, with a learnable gate λ_l initialized to 1 that routes around the new layer via a residual connection, guaranteeing identical output to the pre-growth model; λ_l then decays to 0 over 5,000 steps, fully activating the new layer. A fixed five-stage growth schedule expands dimensions in the order hidden → FFN → MHA → layer, motivated by findings that wider-then-deeper growth yields better performance. To combat catastrophic forgetting under shifting data distributions, LOIRE employs an iterative distillation warmup where each intermediate growth model alternates between student and teacher roles, minimizing KL divergence against all previously trained models with per-teacher weighting coefficients β_j, so that the final model inherits knowledge from the entire data stream rather than only the most recent domain.

## Results

On the 5-domain lifelong pre-training benchmark, LOIRE-GPT1 achieves the lowest Average Increased Perplexity (AP+) of −3.84 at the final growth stage M5, compared to ELLE (3.13), ER (19.22), and GPT_R (8.58), indicating superior knowledge retention. On downstream tasks spanning 5 domains (10 tasks total), LOIRE-GPT1 attains 84.84% average accuracy, outperforming ELLE (83.35%), GPT_R (83.18%), and GPT_L (82.74%). For the BERT-style architecture, LOIRE-Bert reaches 86.60% GLUE average and 79.08 F1 on SQuAD v2.0, surpassing LIGO (81.83% GLUE, 71.24 F1). In terms of efficiency, LOIRE reduces FLOPs by an average of 29.22% versus GPT_R and saves ~40% wall time compared to LIGO on the BERT structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOIRE-GPT1 outperforms all GPT-style baselines on both lifelong learning metrics and downstream tasks. Against ELLE (the closest competitor), LOIRE achieves a 6.97-point lower AP+ (−3.84 vs. 3.13) and 1.49% higher average downstream accuracy (84.84% vs. 83.35%), with notable gains on ACL-ARC (78.13 vs. 73.43) and Hyperpartisan (81.68 vs. 78.75). Against Token KD, LOIRE improves AP+ by 82.19 points (−3.84 vs. 78.35) and average accuracy by 3.54%, showing that iterative distillation with model growth far exceeds single-stage knowledge distillation. Against ER, LOIRE reduces AP+ by 23.06 points and gains 2.23% average accuracy. For the BERT-style comparison, LOIRE-Bert surpasses LIGO by 4.77% on GLUE average (86.60 vs. 81.83) and 7.84 F1 on SQuAD v2.0 (79.08 vs. 71.24), while also saving ~40% training wall time (28h48min vs. 48h25min).

## Relation to Prior Work

LOIRE extends the function-preserving model growth lineage from Net2Net (Chen et al., 2015) and bert2BERT (Chen et al., 2021) to the lifelong pre-training setting, building directly on ELLE (Qin et al., 2022) and LiGO (Wang et al., 2023). It departs from prior work in three ways: (1) a novel plug-in layer growth operator with gated residual connections that skips new layers during initial training, replacing the standard layer-stacking approach; (2) a systematic multi-dimensional growth schedule (hidden → FFN → MHA → layer) that covers all four Transformer dimensions with strict function preservation, whereas ELLE only grows in limited dimensions; and (3) an iterative distillation warmup where intermediate growth-stage models alternate as student and teacher, addressing the size-mismatch problem identified by Mirzadeh et al. (2020). The MHA, FFN, and hidden-dimension operators are adapted from Gesmundo & Maile (2023), while the layer operator and schedule are the paper's original contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LOIRE offers a practical recipe for reducing pre-training FLOPs by ~29% while avoiding catastrophic forgetting across shifting data distributions, which is directly relevant to organizations that must continuously adapt PLMs to new domains without full retraining. However, the largest models tested are only 1.1B parameters, leaving open whether the approach scales to the 70B+ regime where growth-stage training costs dominate. The paper also acknowledges empirical deviations from the theoretical function-preservation guarantee and notes that some downstream tasks (e.g., IMDB, SCIERC) see slight regressions, suggesting the distillation warmup may not fully compensate for distribution shift in all domains. Extending the framework to multimodal settings and deriving a theoretically optimal growth schedule (rather than the current empirical 5-stage heuristic) are natural next steps.
