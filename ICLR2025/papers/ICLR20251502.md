## Problem

Sparse LLMs suffer severe performance degradation at high sparsity ratios, and applying standard LoRA to fine-tune them is inadequate because (1) LoRA weights cannot be merged into the sparse weight matrix, adding inference latency that negates sparsity gains, and (2) uniform sparsity rates and uniform LoRA ranks fail to account for the heterogeneous importance and reconstruction errors across layers. The paper asks how to jointly optimize sparsity and low-rank adaptation so that the fine-tuned model remains fully sparse and mergeable while recovering accuracy efficiently.

## Input / Output

The method consumes dense LLM weights, 128 calibration sequences from C4 (for sparsification via SparseGPT or Wanda), and a 10K-sample subset of Alpaca-GPT4 for fine-tuning. It outputs a fully sparse LLM whose weights already incorporate the low-rank adaptation (i.e., the LoRA matrices are merged into the sparse weight matrix), along with per-layer sparsity rates and per-layer rank allocations. A key structural constraint is that the LoRA matrices B and A share the same binary sparsity mask M as the LLM weights, so the product (M ⊙ (W + BA)) remains sparse and the model's inference latency is unchanged from the un-fine-tuned sparse model.

## Method

LoSA formulates sparse fine-tuning as a single constrained layer-wise reconstruction problem that jointly optimizes the sparsity mask, layer-wise sparsity rates, and layer-wise LoRA ranks. Layer-wise sparsity rates are determined by Representation Mutual Information (RMI) between layer feature maps, approximated via normalized linear HSIC: layers highly correlated with other layers are deemed less important and assigned higher sparsity. LoRA ranks are allocated proportionally to each layer's reconstruction error relative to the dense model, so layers with larger output discrepancies receive a larger fine-tuning budget. The entire process is executed dynamically over T progressive steps using a cubic sparsity schedule, where at each step the sparsity rate increases, the rank budget grows linearly, and the LoRA matrices are re-sparsified with the updated mask—ensuring the final LoRA is always mergeable into the sparse weights without any additional inference overhead.

## Results

On 70% sparse LLaMA-2-7B (Wanda), LoSA reduces WikiText-2 perplexity from 79.67 to 10.94 (a 68.73-point reduction) and increases mean zero-shot accuracy across seven downstream tasks from 35.33% to 51.65% (+16.32%). The method achieves a 2.60× inference speedup on CPU and 2.23× on GPU while requiring only 45 minutes of fine-tuning on a single NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU. LoSA consistently outperforms LoRA across all tested models (LLaMA-1/2/3/3.1, OPT, Vicuna; 7B–70B) and sparsity levels (40%–90%), with the largest gains at high sparsity ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoSA outperforms all 11 baselines across every tested configuration. Against the two primary sparsification methods, LoSA reduces perplexity by 16.60 (SparseGPT) and 68.73 (Wanda) on 70% sparse LLaMA-2-7B, and improves mean zero-shot accuracy by 9.44 and 16.32 points respectively, consistently beating the corresponding LoRA fine-tuning baselines by 1.75–3.47 points. Against other dynamic-rank PEFT methods, LoSA surpasses AdaLoRA (51.65% vs. 48.61%) and SoRA/Sparse LoRA (51.65% vs. 48.83% and 47.76%) on 70% sparse LLaMA-2-7B, attributed to its reconstruction-error-based rank allocation and mergeable sparse weights. Against structured pruning methods (SliceGPT, LLM-Pruner, LoRAPrune, LoRAShear) on LLaMA-1-7B at 20% pruning, LoSA achieves 62.19% mean accuracy vs. 46.99–60.63%, with the largest gap over SliceGPT (+15.20 points). Against other training-free sparsity methods (Pruner-Zero, ALPS) at 70% sparsity, LoSA improves mean accuracy by 7.23 and 8.11 points over the respective LoRA baselines. The key structural advantage over LoRA is that LoSA weights can be merged into sparse LLM weights, preserving the 2.23–2.60× inference speedup that LoRA forfeits due to unmergeable dense low-rank matrices.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoSA unifies two previously separate threads—training-free LLM sparsity (SparseGPT, Wanda) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA)—into a single joint optimization where the low-rank adaptation matrices share the same sparse mask as the pruned weights, making them mergeable post-training. It departs from prior LoRA-based sparse fine-tuning (LoRAPrune, LoSparse, LoRAShear) by addressing unstructured sparsity specifically, where dense low-rank matrices cannot be absorbed into a sparsely-masked weight matrix. The layer-wise sparsity rate determination via Representation Mutual Information (approximated by normalized HSIC) extends the Information Bottleneck principle from single-layer to multi-layer redundancy analysis, and the reconstruction-error-driven rank allocation generalizes the uniform-rank assumption in LoRA and AdaLoRA to a sparsity-aware setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that LoSA removes the inference-latency penalty that previously forced practitioners to choose between sparse acceleration and LoRA-based recovery: the fine-tuned weights merge directly into the sparse matrix, preserving the 2.2–2.6× speedup on GPU/CPU. The RMI-based layer importance computation (≈48 s for a 7B model) offers a lightweight alternative to activation-outlier heuristics (OWL) for non-uniform sparsity. The authors acknowledge a remaining gap to lossless high-ratio sparsity, and the approach is currently validated only on unstructured and N:M sparsity; whether the same joint framework transfers to quantization-aware or structured-pruning settings with comparable efficiency remains an open question.
