## Problem

Existing polyhedra representation methods either rely on hand-crafted face descriptors that lose geometric information, or encode vertex coordinate sequences that require a fixed ordering, ignore inter-face relationships, lack rotation and translation invariance, and discard face-level semantic attributes (e.g., color, material). No prior framework captures the full surface geometry—vertices, edges, faces, and their hierarchical interrelationships—while simultaneously preserving face semantics and achieving geometric invariance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253092/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D polyhedron defined by oriented polygonal faces (each a closed sequence of 3D vertices) plus per-face semantic attributes (color, material), and converts it into a Surface-Attributed Graph (SAG) where nodes are vertices, directed edges are face-boundary segments, and face-hyperedges are ordered edge sequences carrying attributes. The SAG transformation is proven invertible (Lemma 4.2), so no geometric or semantic information is lost. The output is a fixed-dimensional vector q_v ∈ R^d that is invariant to rotation and translation, suitable for downstream classification or retrieval; the final representation is formed by concatenating summed node embeddings across all GNN layers.

## Method

PolyhedronNet operates in three stages. First, the polyhedron is transformed into a Surface-Attributed Graph (SAG) that unifies vertices, directed edges, and face-hyperedges (ordered edge sequences with attributes) into a single invertible graph structure. Second, the SAG is decomposed into local rigid representations: for every two-hop path π(i,j,k), a five-tuple (d_ij, d_jk, θ_ijk, φ_ijk, ψ_ijk) encodes Euclidean distances, the angle at the middle node, the dihedral angle between the two incident faces, and face-hyperedge indices—yielding rotation/translation invariance and, by Theorem 4.5, sufficient information to reconstruct the original graph. Third, PolyhedronGNN hierarchically aggregates these local representations via heterogeneous message passing: separate MLPs handle intra-face (R_inner) and inter-face (R_cross) two-hop paths, with messages guided by concatenated geometric features and face attributes; node embeddings are updated by summing all incoming messages, and the final graph embedding concatenates summed node vectors across all layers. Theorem 4.6 guarantees that with sufficient output dimension, the method can approximate any continuous set function of the two-hop path collection to arbitrary precision.

## Results

PolyhedronNet achieves the best performance across all four datasets on both classification and retrieval tasks. On classification, it reaches 85.8% accuracy and 0.985 AUC on MNIST-C, 98.0% accuracy and a perfect 1.000 AUC on Building, 62.7% accuracy and 0.936 AUC on ShapeNet-P, and 43.5% accuracy and 0.824 AUC on ModelNet-P. On retrieval, it attains 0.714 F1 and 0.945 NDCG on MNIST-C, 0.843 F1 and 0.966 NDCG on Building, 0.327 F1 and 0.674 NDCG on ShapeNet-P, and 0.246 F1 and 0.576 NDCG on ModelNet-P, outperforming all baselines on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolyhedronNet outperforms all five baselines (NUFT-DDSL, ResNet1D, NUFT-IFFT, VeerCNN, PolygonGNN) on every metric across all four datasets. The most dramatic gains appear on MNIST-C, where PolyhedronNet achieves 85.8% accuracy versus 43.5% for the strongest baseline PolygonGNN, a 72% precision improvement over the average of all other methods. On the Building dataset, PolyhedronNet edges out PolygonGNN (98.0% vs. 97.3% accuracy) and achieves a perfect 1.000 AUC. On the more challenging ShapeNet-P and ModelNet-P datasets, which involve complex 3D shapes with fine-grained differences, PolyhedronNet still leads: 62.7% vs. 57.3% accuracy on ShapeNet-P and 43.5% vs. 43.0% on ModelNet-P. The 1D sequence-based methods (ResNet1D, VeerCNN, NUFT-DDSL, NUFT-IFFT) perform poorly on 3D polyhedra, confirming that vertex-sequence encodings struggle to capture inter-face relationships and rotation/translation invariance inherent to 3D polyhedral geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PolygonGNN (Yu et al., KDD 2024), the first GNN-based method for 2D polygon representation learning, to the 3D polyhedral domain by introducing the Surface-Attributed Graph (SAG) with face-hyperedges that unify vertices, edges, and faces in a single invertible structure. It departs from prior polyhedra representation approaches—both feature-engineering descriptors and raw coordinate-sequence encoders—by explicitly modeling face-level semantics (color, material) and inter-face geometric relationships (dihedral angles) that those methods neglect. The local rigid representation via two-hop paths and the heterogeneous intra-face/inter-face message passing in PolyhedronGNN are novel architectural choices not present in prior GNN-based polygon or mesh methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SAG formalization provides a clean, invertible data structure that could serve as a general-purpose representation for any task involving 3D polyhedral objects (classification, retrieval, generation, CAD manipulation), and the built-in rotation/translation invariance removes a common preprocessing burden. However, the evaluation is limited to four relatively small datasets (1.3K–13.7K samples), several of which are simple 3D extrusions of 2D shapes, and the strongest baselines are 2D polygon methods rather than purpose-built 3D polyhedra encoders. Open questions include scalability to complex non-convex polyhedra with thousands of faces, generalization to truly 3D-native shapes (not extruded 2D), and whether the framework extends to polyhedron generation or optimization tasks the paper mentions but does not evaluate.
