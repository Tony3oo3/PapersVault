## Problem

LLMs process text as sub-word tokens via BPE, yet they exhibit robust word-level understanding. No systematic investigation existed of whether and how models internally reconstruct whole-word representations from sub-word sequences, or whether they maintain a latent vocabulary beyond the tokenizer's scope. This paper provides the first comprehensive mechanistic analysis of this 'detokenization' process across transformer layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252523/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The interpretability experiments feed multi-token words (naturally BPE-split, artificially split single-token words, or typo-corrupted variants) with 100 tokens of surrounding context into frozen LLMs, then extract the last token's hidden state at every layer to track when full-word identity emerges. The vocabulary expansion pipeline consumes the model's existing embedding matrix E and unembedding matrix U plus detokenized hidden-state vectors r (extracted via Patchscopes at the earliest successful decoding layer), and produces new input/output embedding vectors for previously multi-token words. All core model parameters remain frozen; only two small d×d refinement matrices are trained on 20M tokens.

## Method

The paper identifies a two-stage detokenization mechanism: early layers (1–2) use attention to aggregate information from prefix sub-word tokens into the final token's representation, then middle layers (3–7) use FFN layers as key-value memories to retrieve the full word's concept from an implicit inner lexicon and write it into the residual stream. This is validated through logit lens adapted to the input embedding space, Patchscopes (feeding hidden states back as input to test whether the model regenerates the original word), k-NN probing distinguishing words from nonwords (89% accuracy at layer 13), and targeted FFN ablation (removing word-carrying updates drops retrieval from 85% to 18%). For the practical application, the 3-step vocabulary expansion extracts the earliest layer where a multi-token word is decoded via Patchscopes, fits orthogonal Procrustes maps from that layer's hidden states to the embedding/unembedding spaces using only existing vocabulary tokens, and trains small d×d refinement matrices W_E and W_U via short continued pretraining while freezing all other parameters. The resulting embeddings enable the frozen model to consume and generate expanded vocabulary entries, reducing token count by 10.5–14.5% with no accuracy loss.

## Results

On the vocabulary expansion task (Table 1), the proposed detokenized-representation method achieves 0.171, 0.180, and 0.402 top-1 accuracy on new tokens for WIKITEXT-103, PUBMED, and WIKI40B-Arabic respectively, while maintaining overall token-level accuracy of 0.519, 0.511, and 0.532 (vs. 0.522, 0.517, 0.535 for the original model). The method reduces input sequence length by 10.5%–14.5% across the three datasets. On the interpretability side, a k-NN probe distinguishes words from nonwords at 89% accuracy (layer 13), and Patchscopes retrieves multi-token words at 64% (layers 5–7) despite the model never seeing those fused vectors as input during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its detokenized-representation method against a mean-embedding baseline (Gee et al., 2022) that initializes new vocabulary entries with the average of the constituent sub-word embeddings. The proposed method wins decisively on every metric across all three datasets: on WIKI40B-Arabic, it achieves 0.402 new-token accuracy vs. 0.117 for mean embeddings (a 3.4× improvement), and 0.532 overall accuracy vs. 0.211 (a 2.5× improvement). On WIKITEXT-103, new-token accuracy is 0.171 vs. 0.071, and overall accuracy is 0.519 vs. 0.473. The mean-embedding baseline consistently degrades overall model performance (e.g., 0.473 vs. 0.522 on WIKITEXT-103), whereas the proposed method nearly matches the original model's accuracy (0.519 vs. 0.522), confirming that detokenized representations are far more compatible with the frozen model's internal geometry than simple averaging of sub-word vectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interpretability toolkit (logit lens, Patchscopes) to systematically study word-level detokenization in LLMs, going beyond the case-study observations of Elhage et al. (2022) and Lad et al. (2024) to provide comprehensive, multi-model evidence. It builds on the FFN-as-key-value-memory framework (Geva et al., 2021, 2022) by showing that FFN layers specifically store and retrieve whole-word representations, and on concurrent work by Feucht et al. (2024) on implicit vocabulary items. The practical vocabulary expansion application departs from prior post-hoc expansion methods (Gee et al., 2022; Kim et al., 2024) by requiring no parameter updates to the core model, instead leveraging the model's own internal detokenized representations as initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LLMs maintain a latent vocabulary beyond the tokenizer's scope has both conceptual and practical implications: it suggests a more complete internal lexicon than the BPE vocabulary implies, and enables a finetuning-free method to reduce sequence length by 10–15% with maintained accuracy. However, the 23% of multi-token words never decoded from any layer, and the low success rate (16%) on Arabic, reveal that the inner lexicon is incomplete, particularly for morphologically rich languages. Open questions include whether this approach scales to larger models, how to improve coverage for low-resource languages, and whether the inner lexicon structure can be further characterized or manipulated for other interpretability or editing tasks.
