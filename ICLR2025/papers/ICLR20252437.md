## Problem

ReFlow (Rectified Flow) can accelerate generative sampling by straightening ODE trajectories, but in practice it causes a significant drop in sample quality. Prior attempts to mitigate this relied on heuristic losses (LPIPS, Pseudo-Huber) and premetrics that violate the flow-matching theoretical guarantees, leaving it unclear whether the learned marginals are preserved. This paper systematically identifies the design choices in ReFlow that cause quality degradation and proposes theoretically sound improvements within the FM framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard Gaussian noise (and optionally a class label for conditional ImageNet-64 generation) and produces synthetic RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) or 64×64 (AFHQv2, FFHQ, ImageNet-64). Training pairs are pre-generated by solving the EDM probability flow ODE both backward (noise→data) and forward (data→noise), with the forward pairs providing exact data marginals. Inference requires only 9 neural function evaluations using DPM-Solver with a sigmoid time discretization, and optional auto-guidance or classifier-free guidance can further improve perceptual quality without additional NFEs.

## Method

The paper generalizes the flow-matching loss into a GFM loss (Eq. 13) with three free components—weight w(x_t,t), time distribution T, and loss function ℓ_φ—and proves (Proposition 1) that any positive weight, supported time distribution, and invertible linear map φ preserve the same minimizer as standard FM. Seven concrete improvements are then proposed: (1) per-sample loss normalization w = 1/sg[L_GFM] to handle the large loss variance across x_t at fixed t (unlike DMs where relative loss is bounded); (2) an exponential time distribution dT ∝ 10^t emphasizing t≈1; (3) a high-pass-filter loss ℓ_{I+10·HPF} that amplifies gradients along high-frequency features while preserving invertibility; (4) reduced dropout (e.g., 0.09 vs. 0.15) to increase UNet capacity for the harder straight-ODE task; (5) mixing forward ODE pairs (exact data marginals) with backward pairs; (6) projecting the coupling to the true marginals via Wasserstein-constrained optimization; and (7) a sigmoid discretization schedule with DPM-Solver (r=0.4) that concentrates steps near t∈{0,1} where curvature is highest. All techniques are verified via ablations on three datasets and scale to ImageNet-64, achieving state-of-the-art FID at 9 NFEs without perceptual losses or latent encoders.

## Results

Simple ReFlow achieves state-of-the-art FID scores for fast neural ODE generation with only 9 NFEs: 2.23 on CIFAR-10, 2.30 on AFHQv2, 2.84 on FFHQ, and 3.49 on ImageNet-64. With guidance (AutoGuidance/CFG), these improve to 1.98, 1.91, 2.67, and 1.74 respectively, also at 9 NFEs. These results substantially outperform prior fast ODE methods (e.g., AMED-Solver at 2.63/4.24/5.60 FID) and even surpass methods using 100+ NFEs such as their own FM baseline (2.96/2.73/3.30 at 100 NFEs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Simple ReFlow at 9 NFEs decisively outperforms all prior fast ODE solvers: it beats AMED-Solver (2.63/4.24/5.60 FID) by 0.40/1.94/2.11 on CIFAR-10/FFHQ/ImageNet, DPM-Solver (4.98/9.26/6.64) by 2.75/6.42/3.15, and MinCurv (8.76/13.63/10.44) by 6.53/11.33/7.60. It also surpasses its own 100-NFE FM baseline (2.96/2.73/3.30) by 0.73/0.43/0.46 FID while using 11× fewer NFEs. Against the original ReFlow (110 NFEs, FID 3.36 on CIFAR-10), Simple ReFlow improves by 1.13 FID with 12× fewer evaluations. The method also outperforms minibatch OT flow matching (MOT-50: 11.82 FID on ImageNet at 132 NFEs) by 8.33 FID at 9 NFEs. With guidance, Simple ReFlow achieves FIDs (1.98/1.91/2.67/1.74) that approach or match the 35–79 NFE EDM reference (1.97/1.96/2.39/2.30) while using 4–9× fewer function evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper systematically studies the ReFlow/Rectified Flow procedure (Liu et al., 2022) within the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023), using EDM (Karras et al., 2022) as the initialization and ODE structure. It departs from prior ReFlow work (Lee et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2024) by showing that widely-used heuristic losses (LPIPS, Pseudo-Huber) violate the flow matching theoretical guarantees and produce incorrect marginals, then proposing a generalized FM loss with invertible linear maps (specifically a high-pass filter) that preserves correctness while improving performance. The paper also identifies that ReFlow training dynamics differ fundamentally from diffusion model training, requiring distinct choices for loss normalization, time distribution, and dropout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By maintaining theoretical correctness (preserved marginals via Proposition 1), the method retains access to exact inversion and tractable likelihood evaluation—capabilities lost when heuristic losses are used—while achieving state-of-the-art FID at only 9 NFEs. The finding that ReFlow requires smaller dropout and different time distributions than the underlying DMs is a practically important insight for anyone fine-tuning flow models. Open questions include whether the coupling projection and forward-pair strategies scale to higher resolutions or video, whether the high-pass filter generalizes to other modalities, and whether multiple ReFlow iterations (beyond the single step studied here) further benefit from these techniques.
