## Problem

Shapley Interactions (SIs) extend Shapley Values to capture joint contributions of feature groups, but their exponential complexity (2^n model calls) makes exact computation infeasible for GNNs with more than a few dozen nodes. Existing GNN explainability methods either compute only first-order SVs without interactions, or rely on model-agnostic Monte Carlo approximations that do not exploit the graph or GNN structure. This paper asks whether the specific architecture of message-passing GNNs can be leveraged to compute exact SIs of any order at a fraction of the standard cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph instance (node features, edge topology), a trained message-passing GNN with linear global pooling (e.g., mean/sum) and a linear output layer, and a target explanation order k. It outputs exact Möbius Interactions and Shapley Interactions for all node subsets up to size k, which additively decompose the GNN's prediction (logit for classification, value for regression). Masked nodes are imputed with a per-feature baseline vector (BShap), preserving graph topology. The key practical detail is that the number of required GNN evaluations equals the size of the union of power sets over all ℓ-hop receptive fields, which for sparse graphs with few layers is orders of magnitude smaller than 2^n.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 3.7) proves that under linear global pooling and output layer, all Möbius Interactions of the GNN-induced graph game that are not fully contained in some node's ℓ-hop receptive field are exactly zero, reducing the complexity from 2^n to at most n·2^(d_max^ℓ). GraphSHAP-IQ exploits this sparsity by (1) identifying the set I of non-trivial MIs from the graph's receptive fields, (2) evaluating the GNN on all 2^|S| maskings for S ∈ I, (3) computing MIs via the Möbius transform, and (4) converting MIs to SIs of any order k using closed-form distribution weights. For settings where even |I| is infeasible, a hyperparameter λ caps the maximum interaction order, yielding an exact-when-budget-sufficient approximation. The paper further shows that non-linear readouts break the receptive-field sparsity, producing interactions outside the GNN's message-passing range.

## Results

GraphSHAP-IQ reduces the complexity of computing exact Shapley Interactions from 2^n model calls to |I| calls bounded by n·2^(d_max^(ℓ+1)-1)/(d_max-1), where the exponential term depends only on the receptive field rather than the full graph. For a 30-atom molecule on MTG, exact SIs require 2^30 ≈ 10^9 model calls naively, but GraphSHAP-IQ needs only 7,693. In approximation experiments on MTG, PRT, and BZR (20–40 node graphs), GraphSHAP-IQ achieves the lowest MSE among all methods at budgets where it reaches exact SIs, and remains unaffected by increasing explanation order while baselines degrade. Interaction-informed variants of all baselines substantially improve both approximation quality and runtime over their standard versions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the approximation experiments (Section 4.2, Figure 4), GraphSHAP-IQ outperforms all ten model-agnostic baselines in MSE for both SV (order 1) and k-SII (orders 2 and 3) on MTG, PRT, and BZR, particularly in settings where lower-order MIs dominate. Among SV baselines, KernelSHAP, Unbiased KernelSHAP, k-additive SHAP, Permutation Sampling, SVARM, and L-Shapley all achieve higher MSE than GraphSHAP-IQ at the same model-call budget; L-Shapley in particular performs poorly on GNN-induced graph games as it only converges to exact SVs with λ=n (i.e., 2^n evaluations). Among k-SII baselines, KernelSHAP-IQ, Inconsistent KernelSHAP-IQ, Permutation Sampling (Tsai et al.), SHAP-IQ, and SVARM-IQ all show higher MSE and greater sensitivity to increasing explanation order compared to GraphSHAP-IQ, which remains unaffected by order. Interaction-informed variants of every baseline substantially reduce MSE and runtime relative to their standard versions, narrowing but not closing the gap to GraphSHAP-IQ. GraphSHAP-IQ is also among the fastest methods overall, as its runtime is determined by the number of non-trivial MIs (bounded by receptive fields) rather than the total number of nodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Shapley Interaction Index (SII) framework from cooperative game theory (Grabisch & Roubens, 1999) and the Möbius Interaction backbone (Harsanyi, 1963) to GNNs, using BShap (Sundararajan & Najmi, 2020) as the node masking strategy. It is positioned as the GNN analog of TreeSHAP (Lundberg et al., 2020): a model-specific method that exploits architectural structure to achieve exact computation, in contrast to prior GNN explanation methods (SubgraphX, SAME, GraphSVX) that either use SVs as heuristics for subgraph scoring or rely on model-agnostic Monte Carlo approximation. The key conceptual departure is proving that the sparse MI structure of node embeddings transfers to graph-level predictions under linear readouts, which no prior GNN XAI work had established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that exact SI complexity depends only on receptive fields (not total graph size) is a substantial practical win, reducing model calls from 2^n to roughly n·2^(receptive field size), enabling exact explanations for graphs with 100+ nodes. The SI-Graph visualization offers a new interpretability tool that reveals multi-node interactions invisible to SVs alone (e.g., benzene rings as 6-way MIs, NO₂ group synergies). However, the linear readout assumption (Assumption 3.4) is a real limitation: Section 5 shows non-linear readouts produce interactions outside receptive fields, invalidating the sparsity guarantee and calling into question the implicit assumption in many existing SV-based GNN XAI methods that distant interactions are negligible. Extending the theory to non-linear readouts or other spatially-restricted architectures (e.g., CNNs) remains open.
