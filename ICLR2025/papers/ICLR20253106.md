## Problem

State-of-the-art vectorized HD map construction methods rely exclusively on either point-level queries (accurate geometry but incomplete instance description) or instance-level queries (good category capture but imprecise geometry for elongated/irregular elements), overlooking the strong intrinsic relationship between points and instances. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a unified framework that simultaneously models both granularities to balance detailed local positions with comprehensive global classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253106/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes surround-view RGB images (6 synchronized cameras on nuScenes, 7 ring + 2 stereo cameras on Argoverse2) and produces vectorized map elements as permutation-equivalent sequences of 20 points per instance, each with 2D or 3D coordinates, plus a category label (pedestrian crossing, divider, or boundary). The BEV feature encoder generates two-scale features (full and half-resolution) via a ResNet-50 backbone with LSS lifting, and the decoder outputs 100 instance predictions per frame, with point-level queries driving coordinate regression and instance-level queries driving classification.

## Method

MGMapNet introduces a multi-granularity attention (MGA) decoder layer with two core components. The multi-granularity aggregator improves multi-head deformable attention by assigning multiple reference points per query and N_rep=8 sampling points per reference point, then computes softmax-normalized weights at two levels: a global softmax over all N_p×N_rep points to produce instance-level queries, and a local softmax over N_rep points per position to produce point-level queries, both sampled from concatenated multi-scale BEV features. The point-instance interaction module then performs P2P cross-attention (current-layer point queries attending to previous-layer point queries with positional embeddings from sampling locations and weights) followed by P2I cross-attention (point queries attending to instance queries), and finally aggregates point queries back into updated instance queries via an MLP. Training uses an auxiliary loss combining instance segmentation (dot-product of instance queries with BEV features vs. ground-truth masks) and reference point supervision (L1 distance after Hungarian matching), on top of the standard MapTRv2 losses.

## Results

On nuScenes (24 epochs), MGMapNet achieves 66.8 mAP, surpassing MapTRv2 by 5.3 points and MapQR by 0.4 points. After extended training (110 epochs), MGMapNet reaches 73.6 mAP, outperforming MapQR (72.6) and MapTRv2 (68.7). On Argoverse2 (6 epochs), MGMapNet achieves 71.2 mAP (2D) and 69.1 mAP (3D), leading all compared methods including HIMap (69.6/68.4) and MapQR (68.2/65.9). The model runs at 11.7 FPS with 70.1 MB parameters, comparable to MapQR (11.9 FPS, 125.3 MB) and MGMap (12 FPS, 55.9 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGMapNet outperforms all nine baselines on the primary nuScenes mAP metric. Against the closest competitor MapQR (66.4 mAP at 24 epochs), MGMapNet leads by 0.4 points while using roughly half the parameters (70.1 vs. 125.3 MB); at 110 epochs the gap widens to 1.0 mAP (73.6 vs. 72.6). The primary baseline MapTRv2 is surpassed by 5.3 mAP at 24 epochs and 4.9 mAP at 110 epochs. On Argoverse2, MGMapNet beats HIMap (the strongest Argoverse2 baseline) by 1.6 mAP in 2D (71.2 vs. 69.6) and 0.7 mAP in 3D (69.1 vs. 68.4), and leads MapQR by 3.0 mAP (2D) and 3.2 mAP (3D). On the IoU-based metric, MGMapNet achieves 46.9 mAP_raster, exceeding MGMap (44.7) and MapVR (38.5). Older rasterized or early vectorized methods (HDMapNet, VectorMapNet, BeMapNet, PivotNet) are all trailed by 7–44 mAP points, confirming the generational gap between end-to-end vectorized approaches and earlier paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

MGMapNet sits at the intersection of two dominant paradigms in end-to-end vectorized HD map construction: point-level query methods (MapTR, MapTRv2) that excel at geometric precision but miss global lane relationships, and instance-level query methods (StreamMapNet) that capture category holistically but struggle with elongated or irregular geometry. The paper builds directly on MapTRv2's training framework and loss design, and adapts the multi-head deformable attention from Deformable DETR into a multi-granularity aggregator that simultaneously produces both query types from multi-scale BEV features. The key departure is the point-instance interaction module (P2P and P2I attention), which has no direct analogue in prior DETR-like map construction work and explicitly models the information exchange between the two granularities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-granularity formulation offers a principled way to resolve the long-standing tension between local geometric fidelity and global categorical coherence in vectorized map construction, and the +5.3 mAP gain over MapTRv2 on nuScenes (and +4.4 on Argoverse2) demonstrates that this tension was a real bottleneck. The generality ablation (Table 5) showing the modules transfer into the MapTRv2 framework suggests the multi-granularity aggregator and point-instance interaction are reusable components rather than a monolithic architecture. Open questions remain around temporal integration (the method is single-frame), computational efficiency at higher query counts, and whether the two-scale BEV feature is sufficient for very long-range or dense urban scenes.
