## Problem

Existing SE(3)-equivariant neural networks for Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian prediction rely on elementwise losses (MAE/MSE) that become physically meaningless for large molecules: a seemingly small relative MAE of 0.01% in the Hamiltonian matrix can produce energy errors exceeding 1,000,000 kcal/mol. This failure mode, which the authors term Scaling-Induced MAE-Applicability Divergence (SAD), was previously hidden because the standard QH9 benchmark is limited to molecules with ≤31 atoms. The paper addresses this by introducing a physics-derived loss function, a substantially larger training set, and a more scalable architecture.

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular structure defined by atomic numbers and 3D Cartesian coordinates and outputs a B×B Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian matrix in a non-orthogonal atomic-orbital basis (Def2TZV for PubChemQH, def2SVP for QH9), where B scales with system size. Because the basis is non-orthogonal, extracting orbital energies requires solving a generalized eigenvalue problem H C = S C ε with the overlap matrix S. From the predicted Hamiltonian, one can derive HOMO/LUMO energies, total energy, eigenvectors (basis coefficients), dipole moments, and electronic spatial extent without any SCF iteration.

## Method

The core contribution is the Wavefunction Alignment Loss (WALoss), which replaces elementwise matrix comparison with a basis-transformed eigenstructure comparison: the predicted Hamiltonian is projected into the ground-truth eigenvector basis via (C*)ᵀ Ĥ C* and compared against the diagonal orbital-energy matrix ε*, with heavier weighting on occupied orbitals and the LUMO. This avoids backpropagation through iterative eigensolvers while directly penalizing deviations in the physically relevant eigenspace. The architecture (WANet) combines eSCN-based SO(2) convolutions for node features (reducing tensor-product cost from O(L⁶) to O(L³)), a sparse Mixture-of-Experts layer for pairwise (off-diagonal) Hamiltonian blocks that routes interactions by interatomic distance, and a MACE-style many-body interaction layer for diagonal blocks. Training is supervised on the newly generated PubChemQH dataset (50,000+ molecules, 40–100 atoms, B3LYP/Def2TZV), and the total loss combines WALoss with a standard MAE-MSE term on the Hamiltonian matrix.

## Results

On PubChemQH (40–100 atoms), WANet with WALoss achieves a System Energy MAE of 47.193 kcal/mol and requires only 82% of the SCF iterations of the initial guess, compared to 65,721 kcal/mol and 371% for QHNet. The HOMO and LUMO energy MAEs are 0.712 and 0.730 kcal/mol respectively, with a HOMO-LUMO gap MAE of 1.327 kcal/mol. On QH9 (≤9 heavy atoms), WANet with WALoss achieves 99.81% eigenvector cosine similarity and 0.2500 kcal/mol occupied-energy MAE in the dynamic setting. The paper reports a 1347× reduction in total energy prediction error and an 18% SCF speed-up over the initial-guess baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WANet with WALoss dominates all Hamiltonian-prediction baselines on PubChemQH: it reduces System Energy MAE from 65,721 kcal/mol (QHNet) to 47.193 kcal/mol and cuts relative SCF iterations from 371% to 82%. Against the closest competitors that also use WALoss (QHNet w/ WALoss at 75.625 kcal/mol and PhisNet w/ WALoss at 60.325 kcal/mol), WANet w/ WALoss achieves the lowest System Energy MAE and the fewest SCF iterations (82% vs. 90%). Compared to the minao initial-guess baseline, WANet w/ WALoss improves System Energy MAE by ~8× (47.193 vs. 374.313) while requiring fewer SCF iterations (82% vs. 100%), despite having a higher raw Hamiltonian MAE (0.4744 vs. 0.5512). Against property-regression models (Equiformer V2, UniMol+), WANet w/ WALoss achieves an 88.88% lower ε_LUMO MAE (0.730 vs. 6.562) and 58.81% lower ε_Δ MAE (1.327 vs. 3.222) than Equiformer V2, while also enabling SCF acceleration and multi-property extraction from a single model. On QH9, WANet w/ WALoss slightly increases Hamiltonian MAE (0.0914 vs. 0.0502 for WANet alone) but improves ε_occ MAE (0.4587 vs. 0.5231) and eigenvector cosine similarity (96.95% vs. 96.86%), confirming that WALoss trades elementwise accuracy for physically meaningful eigenstructure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of SE(3)-equivariant Hamiltonian prediction (QHNet, PhisNet) by identifying a fundamental failure mode—Scaling-Induced MAE-Applicability Divergence (SAD)—where elementwise losses produce physically meaningless Hamiltonians for molecules beyond ~31 atoms. Architecturally, WANet repurposes eSCN's SO(2) convolution for efficient node representations, borrows the MACE density trick for many-body diagonal blocks, and introduces a distance-gated sparse mixture-of-experts for pairwise off-diagonal blocks. The key conceptual departure is WALoss, which replaces elementwise MAE/MSE with an eigenspace-alignment objective derived from first-order perturbation theory, avoiding backpropagation through eigensolvers while preserving the physical meaning of orbital energies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SAD phenomenon reveals that the field's standard evaluation metric (Hamiltonian MAE) is decoupled from physical applicability at scale, a finding that should reshape how Hamiltonian prediction is benchmarked and trained. WALoss's 1347× reduction in total energy error and 18% SCF speedup make neural-network-accelerated DFT practically viable for 40–100-atom systems, opening the door to high-throughput screening of drug-like molecules. Open questions include whether WALoss generalizes to periodic/solid-state Hamiltonians, whether the approach extends to excited states or response properties, and whether the high cost of generating PubChemQH (~1 month on 128 V100s) limits broader adoption of the dataset.
