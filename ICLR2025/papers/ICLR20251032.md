## Problem

Deep generative models for tabular data frequently produce samples that violate domain background knowledge, with violation rates exceeding 50% in 13 of 25 tested scenarios and reaching 100% in five cases. Existing remedies—rejecting non-aligned samples or restricting outputs via linear-inequality layers—are either infeasible at high violation rates or limited to convex constraint spaces. No prior method could enforce constraints as expressive as quantifier-free linear real arithmetic (QFLRA) formulas, which can define non-convex and even disconnected admissible regions.

## Input / Output

The DRL layer takes a raw feature vector x̃ produced by any DGM and a pre-compiled set of QFLRA constraints (expressed as disjunctions of linear inequalities over D real-valued features) and returns a refined sample DRL(x̃) that satisfies all constraints. Compilation is a one-time offline step that produces per-variable constraint sets Π₁,…,Π_D; at inference the layer processes variables sequentially in a user-chosen order, snapping each feature to the nearest satisfying boundary. The output is guaranteed to be the closest constraint-satisfying point to the input under the specified variable ordering, and the entire refinement completes in a single forward pass with no cycles.

## Method

The Disjunctive Refinement Layer (DRL) generalises Fourier-Motzkin variable elimination to disjunctions of linear inequalities via a novel CP resolution rule that eliminates one variable at a time while preserving satisfiability. During compilation, the layer recursively applies CP resolution to derive, for each variable x_i, a set of constraints Π_i over x_i and its predecessors, ensuring that any satisfying assignment to earlier variables can be extended to x_i. At inference, for each variable in the chosen order the layer computes the closest satisfying left and right boundaries (accounting for all disjunctive intervals) and snaps the feature value to the nearer one, leaving it unchanged if already satisfying. This mechanism guarantees constraint satisfaction, supports gradient backpropagation, requires no rejection sampling, and can be inserted after the generator/decoder of any DGM topology (GAN, VAE, or graph-based). The paper proves soundness, refutational completeness, and optimality of the CP resolution procedure, and empirically shows that adding DRL to five DGMs eliminates all constraint violations while improving downstream F1, wF1, and AUC by up to 21.4%, 20.5%, and 20.9% respectively.

## Results

DRL achieves 0% constraint violation rate (CVR) across all five datasets and all five DGMs, while unconstrained DGMs exhibit CVR up to 100% (e.g., WGAN on House). In terms of machine learning efficacy, DRL improves F1-score by up to 21.4%, weighted F1 by up to 20.5%, and AUC by up to 20.9% (all on GOGGLE/CCS) over the unconstrained counterparts. DRL improves performance in 19/20, 18/20, and 17/20 cases for F1, wF1, and AUC respectively, whereas rejection sampling (RS) degrades performance in 17/20 cases for each metric. Sample generation time with DRL (0.05–0.22 s for 1000 samples) is substantially faster than RS (0.37–1.54 s) and only marginally slower than unconstrained DGMs (0.05–0.15 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates DRL on top of five DGMs: WGAN, TableGAN, CTGAN, TVAE, and GOGGLE. Compared to the unconstrained DGMs, DRL improves F1 in 19/20 cases, wF1 in 18/20, and AUC in 17/20, with the largest gains on GOGGLE/CCS (F1: 0.039→0.253, +21.4%; AUC: 0.549→0.758, +20.9%). Rejection sampling (RS), the standard alternative for enforcing constraints, consistently degrades efficacy (e.g., TVAE/CCS F1 drops from 0.325 to 0.024), confirming that simply discarding violating samples is counterproductive. Against the linear-layer baseline (DGMs+LL from Stoian et al., 2024), DRL wins on F1 in 13/20 cases, wF1 in 10/20, and AUC in 11/20, with the most notable advantage on CCS where DGMs+LL still violate QFLRA constraints (CVR up to 55.7%) while DGMs+DRL achieve 0% CVR. On the regression dataset (House), DRL improves MAE/RMSE over unconstrained models in all 10 model-metric combinations, and is comparable to DGMs+LL (6/10 improvements). Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm statistical significance (p<0.01 for F1/wF1, p<0.05 for AUC) for the DRL vs. unconstrained comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Stoian et al. (ICLR 2024), which introduced a linear layer constraining DGM outputs to convex spaces defined by linear inequalities, by generalising the underlying variable elimination procedure to handle disjunctions of linear inequalities (QFLRA formulas). The core technical contribution is a novel CP resolution rule that generalises Fourier-Motzkin elimination (Dechter, 1999) to the disjunctive setting, drawing on the cutting planes proof system of Krajicek (1998). Unlike prior neuro-symbolic approaches that encode constraints as differentiable loss penalties (giving no satisfaction guarantee) or that compile only propositional-logic-level constraints into neural layers, DRL provides hard guarantees for the more expressive QFLRA fragment while remaining a single forward-pass layer compatible with any DGM topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical and previously underappreciated problem: standard DGMs violate even simple domain constraints in the majority of scenarios (13/25 cases exceed 50% violation, five reach 100%), making rejection sampling infeasible and the prior linear-layer approach insufficient for non-convex knowledge. DRL closes this gap with a theoretically grounded (sound, refutationally complete, optimal) layer that also improves downstream efficacy rather than merely patching violations. Open questions include the non-polynomial blowup of the compiled constraint set for high-dimensional or highly disjunctive formulas, the sensitivity to variable ordering, and whether the approach extends beyond QFLRA to full first-order or nonlinear constraints.
