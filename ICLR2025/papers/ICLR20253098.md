surprise_factor: The main conceptual clarification — that spurious correlations by themselves do not necessarily cause poor generalization (the model can still adjust its decision boundary to the core feature), and that it is specifically the availability of example-specific features enabling memorization that locks the model into the spurious solution — is a useful and cleanly proven distinction, but the qualitative picture was largely anticipated by prior work on benign overfitting and shortcut learning. ## Problem

Neural networks trained with ERM can achieve zero training loss by exploiting spurious correlations for the majority of data while memorizing minority exceptions via example-specific features, leaving no incentive to learn robust, generalizable patterns. This paper formalizes the interplay between memorization and spurious correlations, showing that it is their combination—not spurious correlations alone—that causes catastrophic generalization failure on minority subpopulations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253098/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard image inputs (resized to 224×224, no augmentation) or text sequences, processed by a pre-trained ResNet-50 or BERT backbone with a zero-initialized linear head. Unusually, it also requires a pre-trained XRM auxiliary model that provides per-example held-out logits f^ho(x) for every training sample; these logits are the sole additional input beyond the standard (x, y) pairs. The output is a standard K-class probability vector via softmax, with the only new hyperparameter being the softmax temperature τ controlling the sharpness of the held-out probability distribution.

## Method

MAT redefines the training loss by adding a per-example logit shift derived from calibrated held-out probabilities: L^MAT = (1/n) Σ l(softmax(f(x_i;w) + log p̄^ho(·|x_i)), y_i). The shift is computed in three steps: (1) a pre-trained XRM model produces held-out logits, converted to probabilities via softmax with temperature τ; (2) a calibration matrix p(y|y^ho) is estimated as the empirical confusion between true labels and held-out predictions; (3) calibrated probabilities p̄^ho(y|x) = Σ_{y^ho} p(y|y^ho)·p(y^ho|x) are formed. Adding log p̄^ho to the logits increases the effective loss for examples the model gets wrong on held-out data (i.e., memorized examples), redirecting gradient signal toward learning invariant core features. Theoretically, the paper proves that under ERM with spurious correlations and example-specific features (σ_ε > 0, d ≫ log n, γ ≫ σ_ε√(d/m)), the classifier converges to p(ŷ(x)=a)→1 on held-out data, while MAT's logit shift breaks this fixed point and drives the model toward the core feature x_y.

## Results

On the four subpopulation-shift benchmarks, MAT achieves worst-group accuracy (WGA) of 88.1% on Waterbirds, 90.5% on CelebA, 74.6% on MultiNLI, and 74.0% on CivilComments (with validation annotations), substantially outperforming ERM's WGA of 72.6%, 47.2%, 67.9%, and 69.5% respectively. In the fully annotation-free setting (no training or validation group labels), MAT still reaches 88.1% WGA on Waterbirds and 89.9% on CelebA, compared to ERM's 66.4% and 54.3%. MAT introduces only a single hyperparameter (temperature τ) beyond the standard ERM hyperparameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAT most dramatically outperforms ERM on worst-group accuracy: 88.1% vs 72.6% on Waterbirds (+15.5 pts) and 90.5% vs 47.2% on CelebA (+43.3 pts), confirming that ERM's high average accuracy masks severe minority-group failure. Against the strongest annotation-free baseline XRM, MAT matches on Waterbirds WGA (88.1 vs 88.1) and slightly edges it on CelebA (89.9 vs 89.1) and MultiNLI (73.0 vs 72.1), while XRM retains a small advantage on CivilComments (72.2 vs 68.1). In the validation-annotation setting, MAT surpasses JTT on CelebA WGA (90.5 vs 81.1) and LfF on CelebA (90.5 vs 77.2), but AFR achieves a higher Waterbirds WGA (90.4 vs 88.1), suggesting AFR's last-layer retraining is particularly effective on that dataset. Compared to GroupDRO (which requires full training group annotations), MAT achieves comparable or better WGA on CelebA (90.5 vs 88.3) and MultiNLI (74.6 vs 73.4) without any training labels, while GroupDRO leads on Waterbirds (86.5 vs 88.1 is actually MAT winning here). Overall, MAT is competitive with or superior to all baselines on WGA across the majority of dataset–setting combinations, with its clearest advantage on CelebA where the spurious correlation (gender) is strongest.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAT builds directly on Cross-Risk Minimization (XRM, Pezeshki et al. 2023), repurposing its held-out logits as a per-example memorization signal rather than as environment annotations. Conceptually, the paper departs from the reweighting-based approaches (GroupDRO, LfF, JTT, AFR) by showing that logit-shifting with calibrated held-out probabilities changes the optimization fixed point, whereas reweighting only alters the trajectory while converging to the same max-margin solution (Appendix B). The theoretical analysis extends the interpretable setups of Sagawa et al. (2020) and Puli et al. (2023) by isolating memorization as the critical factor that transforms spurious correlations from a manageable bias into a generalization failure, connecting to the benign/malign overfitting taxonomy of Belkin et al. (2019) and Wald et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of 'spurious correlations alone' from 'spurious correlations + memorization' as the true failure mode provides a useful theoretical lens for diagnosing why models with near-perfect training accuracy still fail on minority groups. MAT's group-agnostic design (no training group labels needed) makes it applicable in realistic settings where subpopulation structure is unknown, though it requires a pre-trained XRM model, adding a non-trivial computational overhead. The 'good/bad/ugly' memorization taxonomy raises an important open question: can we automatically distinguish benign memorization (which aids generalization on the long tail) from malign memorization (which blocks learning of invariant features)? The authors explicitly flag extending this analysis to LLMs as future work, where the interplay between memorization and generalization is even less understood.
