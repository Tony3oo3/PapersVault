## Problem

Estimating treatment effects from observational data is compromised when post-treatment covariates (bad controls) or unobserved confounders are present, yet the causal graph is typically unknown. Existing invariance-based methods like Shi et al. (2021) require full observability of the outcome's parents, failing when those parents are unobserved. No prior method achieves point identification of the ATE in the presence of both bad controls and unobserved variables without requiring knowledge of the complete causal graph.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple heterogeneous observational datasets (environments), each containing i.i.d. tuples of observed covariates X ∈ R^d, binary treatment T, and continuous outcome Y, with the requirement that at least one of T or Y has its parents fully observed and satisfies a conditional-mean invariance across environments. It outputs a per-environment ATE estimate via a doubly robust formula and the corresponding valid adjustment set S ⊆ [d]. The combinatorial variant requires oracle access to nuisance functions (pooled conditional means), while the differentiable variant learns them jointly with a neural network.

## Method

RAMEN identifies a valid adjustment set by minimizing a doubly robust invariance loss: it searches over subsets S of observed covariates for one where either the conditional mean of T or Y given S is invariant across all environments, formalized as a supremum over measurable test functions in an RKHS. The loss is made tractable via a universal Gaussian kernel and estimated with cross U-statistics, yielding a closed-form kernel-based objective. Two algorithms solve the subset selection: an exhaustive combinatorial search (Algorithm 1) and a scalable Gumbel-softmax relaxation (Algorithm 2) that replaces discrete subset indicators with differentiable sigmoid gates annealed via a temperature schedule. The key theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows that under an environment-heterogeneity condition (Assumption 4.1), any minimizer of the invariance loss is a valid adjustment set, so the resulting doubly robust ATE estimator is unbiased regardless of whether T or Y is the invariant node—providing robustness to unobserved variables rather than model misspecification.

## Results

On the real-world Cattaneo (2010) dataset (n=4,642, 4 environments, 20 covariates), RAMEN (θ_insta-⊕) estimates an ATE of −214.60 ± 25.20 g, aligning with the 200–250 g birth-weight reduction reported in epidemiological literature. In synthetic experiments with known DAGs (n=2,500, d=5, 5 environments), both RAMEN variants (θ_⊕ and θ_insta-⊕) achieve the lowest MAE across all three invariance scenarios (T, Y, or both), while θ_irm degrades markedly under T-invariance and θ_all is biased when post-treatment colliders/descendants are present. On random Erdős–Rényi DAGs (p=20 nodes, n=2,000 per environment), θ_insta-⊕ significantly outperforms all baselines across all numbers of environments, with θ_irm failing to surpass even the trivial θ_null and θ_all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RAMEN against three baselines: θ_irm (invariant risk minimization from Shi et al., 2021), θ_all (adjust for all covariates), and θ_null (no adjustment). On the real-world Cattaneo dataset, RAMEN (−214.60 g) is the only method whose estimate falls within the epidemiologically established range of 200–250 g; θ_null overestimates the effect (−275.25 g) due to unaddressed confounding, while both θ_all (−157.55 g) and θ_irm (−182.65 g) underestimate it, with θ_irm also exhibiting the highest variance (±48.32 vs. ±25.20 for RAMEN). In synthetic experiments, θ_irm specifically fails under T-invariance (where Y's parents are unobserved), performing worse than θ_all in some settings, because it lacks the double robustness that RAMEN provides. θ_all is biased whenever post-treatment colliders or descendants are present, while θ_null is consistently biased due to confounding. RAMEN's differentiable Gumbel variant (θ_insta-⊕) matches the combinatorial search variant (θ_⊕) in statistical performance while being computationally feasible for high-dimensional settings (d=20).

## Relation to Prior Work

RAMEN extends the invariance-based treatment effect estimation framework of Shi et al. (2021), which applies invariant risk minimization (IRM) to identify ATEs from multiple environments but requires the outcome Y to always be the invariant node with all its parents observed. The paper's key departure is a novel 'doubly robust identification' guarantee: the ATE is identifiable if either the parents of T or the parents of Y are fully observed and invariant, relaxing the full-observability requirement to partial observability. The invariance conditions themselves draw on the invariant causal prediction literature (Peters et al., 2016) and the broader invariance-based domain generalization line (Arjovsky et al., 2019; Gu et al., 2024), but the loss function is a kernelized MMD-style invariance criterion rather than a prediction-based one, and the subset selection uses a Gumbel-softmax relaxation rather than combinatorial search alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provide point identification of the ATE in the presence of both bad controls (post-treatment covariates) and unobserved variables without requiring knowledge of the causal graph, a setting that is common in observational medicine and social sciences. The doubly robust identification guarantee—robustness to unobserved variables rather than model misspecification—opens a new axis of robustness distinct from classical double robustness. Key limitations include the strict environment-heterogeneity requirement (Assumption 4.1), the curse of dimensionality inherent to kernel methods, and the heuristic nature of the Gumbel relaxation, all of which constrain practical applicability to moderate-dimensional settings with sufficiently diverse data sources.
