## Problem

In-context learning in LLMs is highly sensitive to the ordering of demonstrations, and this vulnerability can be exploited as a natural, hard-to-detect attack that degrades performance by over 80% on LLaMA-3. Existing mitigations either modify the transformer architecture (lacking scalability) or rely on inference-stage post-processing (adding per-call overhead), leaving no method that fundamentally enhances the model's inherent robustness to input permutations during training.

## Input / Output

The LLM receives an instruction prompt containing n few-shot demonstrations (input-output pairs) followed by a query input, and produces a text prediction scored by ROUGE-L (for instruction tuning) or normalized MSE (for linear function tasks). The P-Net, a BERT-base or FLAN-large encoder, takes the same ICL prompt and outputs a permutation matrix that reorders the demonstration embeddings before they are fed to the LLM; after training, only the LLM is retained and the P-Net is discarded, so inference incurs no additional cost.

## Method

PEARL formulates permutation robustness as a distributionally robust optimization problem where the ambiguity set is the convex hull of all n! permutations of the training prompts, and solves the inner maximization via a Permutation-proposal Network (P-Net). The P-Net uses a cross-demonstration layer to compute a pairwise relationship matrix R, then applies the Sinkhorn operator (iterative row/column normalization of exp(R)) to produce a doubly stochastic matrix, from which a permutation is sampled differentiably via the Gumbel-Sinkhorn trick. The LLM and P-Net are co-trained through alternating minimax optimization: the P-Net maximizes the LLM's loss by proposing the most challenging permutation, while the LLM minimizes its loss under that adversarial permutation, with an entropy regularization term (coefficient β) preventing the P-Net from collapsing to trivial uniform permutations. At convergence, the P-Net ideally represents a uniform distribution over all permutations, indicating the LLM handles every ordering equally well.

## Results

On Llama3-8B instruction-tuned on Super-Natural Instructions, PEARL achieves 62.9 average ROUGE-L at 2 shots (vs. 57.3 for ERM, +9.8%) and 56.4 worst-case ROUGE-L (+14.2% over ERM). The worst-case advantage grows with more shots: +22.5% at 3 shots and +29.4% at 4 shots, while average gains remain at 5.7–9.8%. In the synthetic linear-function setting (GPT-2), PEARL reduces normalized MSE from 1.45 to 0.86 (avg) and 2.67 to 0.92 (worst-case) at 3 shots, a 65.5% worst-case improvement. When scaled to 64-shot / 8k-token settings after training on only 5-shot / 512-token data, PEARL achieves 24–40% worst-case gains over ERM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEARL dominates all four baselines on both average and worst-case ROUGE-L across every shot setting. Compared to standard ERM (Min et al., 2022), PEARL improves average performance by 5.7–9.8% and worst-case by 14.2–29.4%, with the worst-case gap widening as shots increase. ERM+IM (Zhang et al., 2018), which uses instance mixup as a min-mean strategy, actually degrades average performance (e.g., 53.5 vs. 57.3 at 2 shots) and provides only marginal worst-case gains (+3.9 to +5.9%), confirming that min-mean optimization is insufficient for permutation robustness. InfoAC (Xiang et al., 2024), which uses contrastive learning to break autoregressive constraints, improves worst-case by only 3.1–7.8% and still lags PEARL by 14–22 points in worst-case ROUGE-L. In the synthetic linear-function experiment (Table 1), PEARL outperforms ERM+CL (Bengio et al., 2009) by 32–41% in average normalized MSE and 65–74% in worst-case, with the worst-case advantage growing from 65.5% at 3 shots to 73.6% at 5 shots.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes distributionally robust optimization (DRO), previously applied to label shift and group shift, to the permutation space of in-context learning demonstrations, and couples it with optimal transport (Gumbel-Sinkhorn) to parameterize the worst-case adversary. It departs from prior ICL robustness work—InfoAC's contrastive bidirectional attention, DeepSet-based permutation-equivariant architectures, and inference-stage calibration/order-optimization—by operating purely at the training stage without modifying the transformer architecture or adding inference overhead. The minimax formulation with a lightweight P-Net as adversary is a novel instantiation of adversarial training for combinatorial input perturbations, distinct from virtual adversarial training or gradient-based adversarial examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a frontier model (LLaMA-3) suffers >80% attack success from pure demonstration reordering—no text modification—raises a concrete safety concern for deployment, since such attacks are semantically invisible to model providers. The 24–40% worst-case gains when generalizing from 5-shot/512-token training to 64-shot/8k-token inference, plus the 2–4× shot-efficiency improvement, suggest the learned robustness features transfer well, making this a practical training recipe rather than a theoretical curiosity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full fine-tuning of larger models, whether the P-Net's Sinkhorn-based adversary remains effective as the LLM's permutation sensitivity diminishes with scale, and whether the framework extends to multi-modal or multi-document set-structured inputs as the authors speculate.
