## Problem

Existing cross-table learning frameworks for tabular data lack a generative model backbone and an effective mechanism to decode heterogeneous feature values across diverse schemas. Current tabular synthesizers are data-specific and cannot leverage diverse pre-training data to generate information beyond what is present in limited training sets, while PLM-based approaches treat tables as unstructured text, distorting structural and metadata information critical for effective tabular modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251717/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous table rows (mixed numerical and categorical columns) along with a table schema (column names) and a free-text metadata description (generated via GPT-4o from the first 10 rows). Column names and categorical values are embedded via a frozen GTE-large text encoder, while numerical values are mapped to a uniform distribution via a quantile transformer; all embeddings are interleaved as (column_name, value) pairs into a fixed-dimension sequence with no positional encoding to preserve permutation invariance. The output is synthetic mixed-type table rows decoded from the latent space, with numerical cells recovered via a sigmoid-linear head and categorical cells via cosine-similarity matching against the PLM embeddings of all known categories.

## Method

CTSyn comprises two components: (1) a cross-tabular autoencoder built on the Perceiver Resampler, where learnable latent queries attend to the flattened input sequence to produce a fixed-dimensional latent vector (ℓ=16, M_agg=64), and a cross-attention decoder that uses column-name embeddings as queries and the concatenation of metadata embedding with the latent code as keys/values to reconstruct cell values in an order determined by the target schema; (2) a conditional latent diffusion model (12-layer pre-LayerNorm transformer, hidden dim 768, GeGLU) trained with v-parameterization and classifier-free guidance (condition dropped at p=0.1), conditioned on the schema embedding [e_m, E_c]. The VAE is trained with a β-VAE objective (MSE for numerical, cross-entropy via cosine similarity for categorical, plus KL regularization with annealed β from 10⁻² to 10⁻⁵), and the full system is pre-trained on 5M rows across 86 OpenTab tables before fine-tuning the decoder and diffusion model on downstream datasets while freezing the encoder to preserve latent-space alignment.

## Results

On statistical fidelity across 11 benchmark datasets, CTSyn achieves the best correlation score (0.95) and a substantially higher recall (0.075) than all baselines (next best: TabDDPM at 0.027), while matching or exceeding SOTA on shape similarity (0.94 vs. TabSyn's 0.97). In the low-data regime (N ≤ 100 shots), CTSyn outperforms all baselines and even real data on classification F1 (e.g., 0.83 at 100 shots vs. 0.82 real, 0.81 TabSyn) and regression RMSE (0.17 at 100 shots vs. 0.21 real, 0.22 TabSyn). On privacy, CTSyn achieves the highest authenticity score (0.97) among non-DP methods, indicating minimal memorization of training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTSyn achieves the best column-correlation score (0.95) and a 2.8× higher recall (0.075 vs. 0.027 for TabDDPM) than the strongest diffusion-based baseline, indicating superior capture of inter-column relationships and sample-level coverage. On shape similarity, TabSyn (0.97) and SMOTE (0.96) slightly edge out CTSyn (0.94), as these methods focus on replicating marginal distributions rather than structural relationships. In ML utility, CTSyn dominates in the low-data regime: at 30–200 shots it beats TabSyn by 0.01–0.03 F1 on classification and by 0.05–0.13 RMSE on regression, and even surpasses real data at the same scale. At 500+ shots the advantage narrows, with TabSyn matching CTSyn on classification (0.85 vs. 0.84) and slightly leading on full-data classification (0.86 vs. 0.86). GReaT, the PLM-based generator, fails entirely below 100 shots (cannot produce valid tabular format) and trails CTSyn by 0.07 F1 at 100 shots. On privacy, CTSyn's authenticity score (0.97) exceeds TabDDPM (0.87) and TabSyn (0.93), while DP methods (AIM, PATE-CTGAN) achieve perfect PCT=1.00 but at the cost of near-zero fidelity (Shape 0.63 and 0.15 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

CTSyn builds on the latent diffusion paradigm (Ho et al., 2020; Lovelace et al., 2024) and the Perceiver Resampler architecture (Yuan et al., 2021a) to create a cross-table generative model, combining a β-VAE (Higgins et al., 2017) for heterogeneous table encoding with a conditional diffusion transformer for latent-space sampling. It departs from prior tabular generators (CTGAN, TVAE, TabDDPM, TabSyn) that are data-specific and lack transferable encoding/decoding, and from PLM-based generators (GReaT, Tabula) that treat tables as unstructured text, losing structural information. The key conceptual departure is using a unified latent space with schema-conditioned decoding (via GTE-large text embeddings and cross-attention) to enable a pre-training/fine-tuning paradigm across heterogeneous tables, rather than training a separate generator per dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CTSyn demonstrates that the pre-training/fine-tuning paradigm can be extended to tabular data generation, with pre-training acting as implicit regularization that improves diversity, privacy, and downstream utility in low-data regimes without requiring differential privacy mechanisms. However, the advantage diminishes as real data increases, and the current transfer setup (freeze encoder, fine-tune decoder + diffusion) is acknowledged as suboptimal, leaving open questions about more sophisticated adaptation strategies (in-context generation, parameter-efficient fine-tuning). The requirement for semantically meaningful column names and the relatively modest pre-training scale (86 tables, 5M rows) limit immediate practical deployment, though the framework is extensible to text and date-time columns.
