## Problem

Efficiently modeling sequences with infinite context length remains challenging: Transformer-based models suffer from quadratic computational complexity, while pure State Space Models (SSMs) like Mamba struggle with precise memory recall and length extrapolation. Prior hybrid SSM-attention architectures have not demonstrated substantially better language modeling performance than state-of-the-art Transformers at scale, nor have they achieved both linear complexity and improved perplexity beyond training sequence length.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251926/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SAMBA consumes text token sequences and produces generated text tokens autoregressively. It is pre-trained on 4K-length sequences but extrapolates zero-shot to 1M tokens with improved perplexity on Proof-Pile, and after 500 steps of instruction tuning on 4K sequences, it achieves perfect memory recall at 256K context length. The architecture maintains a constant-size recurrent state (d_s=16 per Mamba layer) and a fixed 2048-token sliding window for attention, ensuring linear memory and compute regardless of sequence length.

## Method

SAMBA layer-wise interleaves three block types in a repeating pattern: Mamba (selective SSM with input-dependent gating over recurrent states), Sliding Window Attention (window size 2048 with RoPE, implemented via FlashAttention 2), and SwiGLU MLP layers. Mamba layers compress the sequence into recurrent hidden states to capture time-dependent semantics, while SWA layers provide precise retrieval of recent context that recurrent states cannot faithfully preserve. The key architectural insight, supported by attention/selection entropy analysis, is that this division of labor allows Mamba to focus on modeling recurrent structure (higher selection entropy in middle layers) while SWA handles precise retrieval (lower attention entropy in middle layers), yielding specialization that outperforms both pure-attention and pure-SSM baselines. Scaled to 3.8B parameters with 3.2T tokens, SAMBA outperforms Phi-3-mini on short-context benchmarks (MMLU 71.9, GSM8K 87.6, HumanEval 62.8) while achieving 3.73× higher prompt-processing throughput and 3.64× faster decoding than Llama-3 with grouped-query attention.

## Results

SAMBA-3.8B-IT achieves 71.9 MMLU, 87.6 GSM8K (8-shot CoT), 62.8 HumanEval (0-shot pass@1), and 18.9 ROUGE-L on GovReport, substantially outperforming Phi-3-mini-4K-instruct (68.8/82.5/58.5/14.4) under identical training recipes. At the 1.7B scale, SAMBA achieves the best average score (54.33) across 15 downstream benchmarks, outperforming Llama-3 1.6B (51.17), Mistral 1.6B (51.12), and Mamba 1.8B (52.31). Pretrained at 4K context length, SAMBA extrapolates to 1M tokens with improved perplexity on Proof-Pile (256× extrapolation ratio) and achieves 3.73× higher prompt-processing throughput and 3.64× faster decoding than Llama-3 at 128K/64K lengths respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Phi-3-mini-4K-instruct (same 3.2T tokens, same data), SAMBA-3.8B-IT wins on MMLU (+3.1), GSM8K (+5.1), HumanEval (+4.3), and GovReport (+4.5 ROUGE-L), while slightly trailing on SQuALITY (−0.4). At the 1.7B scale, SAMBA outperforms Llama-3 1.6B, Mistral 1.6B, and Mamba 1.8B on the 15-benchmark average (54.33 vs. 51.17/51.12/52.31), with particularly large gains on retrieval-intensive SQuAD (77.64 vs. 74.88/72.82/67.66) and MMLU (48.01 vs. 43.84/43.54/45.28). On length extrapolation, SAMBA achieves improved perplexity up to 1M tokens zero-shot, whereas Self-Extend (SE-Llama-3) still suffers perplexity explosion and introduces significant inference latency overhead; SAMBA also delivers 3.73× higher prompt-processing throughput at 128K. On Passkey Retrieval, SAMBA 1.7B reaches near-perfect recall at 256K after only 500 fine-tuning steps on 4K sequences, while Mistral 1.6B (SWA-only) plateaus at ~30% accuracy. On the Phonebook task, SAMBA-3.8B-FT (100 steps, 4K) closes most of the gap with Llama-2 7B (full attention, 2× parameters) within the 4K training length and shows significantly better extrapolation beyond it. Compared to Jamba-1.5-Mini (12B active/52B total), SAMBA-3.8B delivers comparable post-trained benchmark performance while using roughly 3× fewer active parameters and 13× fewer total parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMBA sits at the intersection of two active research threads: selective State Space Models (Mamba, S6) and sparse/efficient attention (Sliding Window Attention from Longformer). Prior hybrid architectures—H3, Jamba, Mambaformer—interleave Mamba with full quadratic self-attention, while Griffin interleaves a different linear recurrent unit (RG-LRU) with SWA. SAMBA's key departure is the specific combination of Mamba's input-dependent selective SSM with SWA (rather than full attention or RG-LRU), plus separate MLP blocks, and the demonstration that this simple layer-wise interleaving substantially outperforms both pure Transformers and pure SSMs at scale up to 3.8B parameters while retaining linear-time complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAMBA is the first hybrid architecture to show that a linear-complexity model can beat state-of-the-art Transformers (Phi-3-mini) on standard short-context benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval) at 3.8B scale, while simultaneously achieving 256× perplexity-based extrapolation and 3.7× throughput gains on 128K prompts. This challenges the prevailing assumption that SSMs are inherently weaker than attention for general language modeling. Open questions remain: zero-shot retrieval still lags behind full-attention models, the transferability of long-context capabilities across tasks is unexplored, and whether the architecture's advantages persist at much larger scales (tens of billions of parameters) is untested. The paper also acknowledges a trade-off between perplexity improvement and retrieval performance during length extrapolation.
