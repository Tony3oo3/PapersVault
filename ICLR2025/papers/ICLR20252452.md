## Problem

Amortized simulation-based inference methods that train neural networks on simulated data struggle to scale to time series simulators because they require many expensive full-sequence simulations, and the resulting high-dimensional observations (e.g., ~1M dimensions for Kolmogorov flow) are intractable for direct posterior estimation. Most scientific time series simulators are Markovian, yet this temporal structure has been largely unexploited for efficient inference.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes single-step transition triplets (θ, x^t, x^{t+1}) where x^t is drawn from a user-specified proposal distribution independent of θ, and x^{t+1} is generated by one call to the simulator's transition function. At inference time, an arbitrary-length observation x^{0:T} is decomposed into T single transitions, each scored by the trained network; the composed global score drives a reverse diffusion sampler (Euler-Maruyama) to produce posterior samples p(θ|x^{0:T}). The method amortizes over sequence length: a network trained on single transitions generalizes to time series of any T without retraining.

## Method

The key insight is that for a Markovian simulator, the global posterior score ∇_θ log p(θ|x^{0:T}) factorizes as a sum of local scores ∇_θ log p̃(θ|x^t, x^{t+1}) over single transitions minus a prior correction term, enabling training on cheap single-step simulations rather than full sequences. A score network s_φ(θ_a | x^t, x^{t+1}) is trained via denoising score matching on single-transition data. At inference, local scores are composed into a global score using a Gaussian approximation (GAUSS) or Tweedie moment projection (JAC) to correct for the fact that the noisy posterior p(θ_a|x^{0:T}) no longer satisfies the Markov property at diffusion time a>0. The composed score is then used in a reverse SDE sampler to draw posterior samples. The same factorization extends directly to neural likelihood and ratio estimation (FNLE, FNRE) by learning transition densities instead of scores.

## Results

On the four synthetic benchmarks (Gaussian RW, Mixture RW, Periodic SDE, Linear SDE, Double Well), FNSE achieves C2ST scores of 0.50–0.53 at T=1, degrading gracefully to 0.69–0.90 at T=101, consistently outperforming the NPE baseline and FNRE. On the Lotka-Volterra and SIR ecological/epidemiological models, FNSE reaches C2ST of 0.57 and 0.67 at T=1 respectively, with the SIR task showing the largest margin over NPE. On the high-dimensional Kolmogorov flow problem (~1M data dimensions), FNSE recovers a well-calibrated posterior using only 200k transition evaluations, a regime where direct NPE is infeasible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FNSE outperforms the NPE baseline (RNN-embedded normalizing flow) on nearly all tasks, with the largest gains on the SIR model where NPE fails to generalize beyond T=10 while FNSE maintains stable performance up to T=100. FNLE also beats NPE on most benchmarks but is significantly slower at inference due to requiring T forward passes through a normalizing flow per MCMC iteration. FNRE is the weakest factorized method, showing performance degradation over long time series due to sensitivity to local errors, and underperforms NPE on the Lotka-Volterra task. On the Double Well task, NPE performs relatively competitively because the SDE rapidly converges to a stationary distribution, allowing the RNN to learn a generalizable summary statistic. The prior-predictive proposal construction (using 5k of 100k budget) yields comparable or slightly better C2ST than the hand-designed default proposal, confirming that the method is robust to proposal choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional/factorized SBI framework developed for i.i.d. observations (Geffner et al., 2023; Linhart et al., 2024; Boelts et al., 2022) to Markovian time series simulators, where the local inference unit becomes a single state transition rather than a single observation. The score-based diffusion machinery (Song et al., 2021) and the GAUSS/JAC score composition rules from Linhart et al. (2024) are adapted to handle the fact that the noisy-parameter likelihood no longer satisfies the Markov property for a > 0. The approach also subsumes Kalman filtering as a special case of FNLE and contrasts with sequential (non-amortized) SBI methods and score-based data assimilation (Rozet & Louppe, 2023), which solves a forward rather than inverse problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is a T-fold reduction in simulator calls (from T×N full-sequence simulations to N single-step transitions), making SBI feasible for expensive Markovian simulators and enabling inference on very high-dimensional data (~1M dimensions in Kolmogorov flow) that is intractable for direct NPE. The amortized nature means a single trained model generalizes to arbitrary observation lengths. Open questions include extending to non-Markovian and hidden-state models, improving the Gaussian-approximation-based score composition for strongly multimodal posteriors, and incorporating cost-aware simulation allocation to further reduce the budget.
