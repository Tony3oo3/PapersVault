## Problem

Existing equivariant message-passing networks for predicting DFT quantum operators (Hamiltonian, density, overlap matrices) suffer from two critical limitations: their effective receptive field grows linearly with each message-passing layer, making parallelization and large-system inference intractable, and the O(l⁶) cost of high-order tensor products prevents handling the f and g orbitals (l up to 8) required for heavy-element systems. No prior method simultaneously achieves strict locality, high-order equivariant representability, and computational efficiency for unified multi-operator prediction.

## Input / Output

The model takes 3D atomic positions and species (atomic numbers) as input, with atom-specific cutoff radii matched to the radial extent of the numerical atomic orbitals used in DFT. Outputs are the upper-triangular blocks of the Hamiltonian and density matrices, parameterized via Wigner-Eckart decomposition into equivariant spherical tensor features o_{l₃,m₃}^{i,j} (supporting irreps up to l=8 for g-orbital bases), and the overlap matrix expressed as invariant Slater-Koster radial parameters s_{l₁,l₂,|m|}^{Zi,Zj}(r_ij) rather than full matrix elements. The overlap prediction adds only ~1.4% to the total parameter count.

## Method

SLEM maintains three feature types—hidden (scalar + tensor), node, and edge—that are updated iteratively under a strict locality constraint: hidden states V^{ij} depend only on the center atom i's node features, the edge shift vector r_ij, and atom j's type/coordinates, explicitly excluding j's neighbors to keep the receptive field fixed at r_cut. The key computational innovation is an SO(2) convolution that exploits the sparsity of spherical harmonics when aligned with the bond axis (nonzero only at m₂=0), reducing tensor product complexity from O(l⁶) to O(l³) and enabling efficient handling of l=8,9,10 irreps. The overlap matrix is parameterized as two-center radial integrals (Slater-Koster parameters) learned by a small MLP from atomic species and distance, avoiding the need for a separate equivariant network. Updates use gated non-linearities, E3Linear channel mixing, and attention-like weights derived from scalar hidden features to enforce radial decay, with the entire architecture supporting parallel graph partitioning for device-scale systems.

## Results

SLEM achieves state-of-the-art Hamiltonian prediction MAE of 0.34, 0.26, and 0.10 meV on MoS₂, Graphene, and Si (d-orbital basis) with only 0.7M parameters, outperforming DeepH-E3 (0.46/0.40/0.16 meV) and HamGNN (1.20/0.35/0.19 meV). For high-order f/g orbital systems, SLEM reaches 0.21 meV on GaN and 0.28 meV on HfO₂, where DeepH-E3 achieves 0.87 meV on GaN and fails with out-of-memory errors on HfO₂. On the QH9 molecular benchmark, the semi-local variant LEM reduces Hamiltonian MAE to 56.57×10⁻⁶ Eh (vs. 83.22 for QHNet) and density matrix MAE to 0.0219 (vs. 0.0643). SLEM also demonstrates superior data efficiency, maintaining near-full-data accuracy (0.37 meV) even with only 20% of training data on MoS₂, while DeepH-E3 degrades to 1.46 meV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepH-E3 (Gong et al., Nat. Commun. 2023), SLEM wins on every tested system: on d-orbital materials it reduces MAE by 26–38% at comparable or smaller model size (e.g., 0.34 vs. 0.46 meV on MoS₂ with 0.7M vs. 1.0M params), and on f/g-orbital systems the gap widens dramatically—SLEM achieves 0.21 meV on GaN versus DeepH-E3's 0.87 meV, while DeepH-E3 cannot train on HfO₂ at all due to out-of-memory errors from O(l⁶) tensor products. Against HamGNN (Zhong et al., npj Comput. Mater. 2023), SLEM is consistently superior, with the largest margin on MoS₂ (0.34 vs. 1.20 meV, a 3.5× improvement) and clear wins on Graphene (0.26 vs. 0.35) and Si (0.10 vs. 0.19). On the QH9 molecular benchmark (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2024), the semi-local LEM variant outperforms QHNet by 32% on Hamiltonian MAE (56.57 vs. 83.22 ×10⁻⁶ Eh) and by 66% on density matrix MAE (0.0219 vs. 0.0643), confirming transferability beyond periodic systems. In data-efficiency experiments, SLEM's MAE remains nearly flat (0.34→0.37 meV) when training data is reduced from 100% to 20% on MoS₂, whereas DeepH-E3 degrades by 3.2× (0.46→1.46 meV), highlighting SLEM's strict locality as a key advantage for data-limited regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLEM extends the strictly local equivariant design of Allegro (Musaelian et al., 2023) from scalar/vector MLIPs to high-order spherical tensor prediction of quantum operators, and incorporates the SO(2) convolution of Passaro & Zitnick (2023) to reduce tensor-product complexity from O(l⁶) to O(l³). It departs from standard E-MPNNs (DeepH-E3, HamGNN, E3NN) by fixing the receptive field at a constant cutoff rather than expanding it through iterative message passing, and unifies Hamiltonian, density matrix, and overlap prediction in a single architecture. The overlap matrix is parameterized via Slater-Koster invariant scalars (inspired by DeePTB), avoiding the need for a separate equivariant network or post-hoc DFT extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is enabling quantum operator prediction for heavy-element systems (f, g orbitals) that were previously intractable due to O(l⁶) tensor-product costs, and the strict locality design opens the door to GPU-parallel inference on device-scale (10⁴–10⁷ atom) structures. A key limitation is that the locality prior relies on electronic screening, which is weaker in confined molecular systems; the authors acknowledge this and provide a semi-local variant (LEM) but it underperforms on QH9. Open questions include robust active-learning sampling strategies for quantum operator models and efficient parallel solvers for extracting physical quantities from predicted non-orthogonal-basis operators.
