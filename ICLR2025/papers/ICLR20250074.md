## Problem

Despite rapid growth in Imbalanced Graph Learning (IGL) algorithms, the field lacks a unified benchmark: prior works use inconsistent datasets, data-splitting strategies, and evaluation metrics, making cross-study comparisons unreliable. This paper addresses that gap by establishing IGL-Bench, the first open-sourced comprehensive benchmark that unifies experimental protocols for 24 IGL algorithms across 17 graph datasets, covering both class-imbalance and topology-imbalance at node and graph levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250074/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 17 real-world graph datasets (9 node-level spanning homophily 0.22–0.82, 8 graph-level) with controlled imbalance ratios ρ manipulated per dataset-specific definitions: class-imbalance uses the max/min class-size ratio, local topology-imbalance uses the head-to-tail average degree ratio (top 20% vs. bottom 80% by degree), global topology-imbalance uses −10·log|RC·SC| (reaching × squashing coefficients), and graph-level topology-imbalance uses the head-to-tail average graph-size ratio. Outputs go beyond standard accuracy to include balanced accuracy, macro-F1, AUC-ROC, training time, peak GPU memory, t-SNE embedding visualizations, and Silhouette scores for classifier boundary analysis, all reported over 10 runs with standard deviations.

## Method

IGL-Bench is not a new algorithm but a unified experimental framework that standardizes data processing, a 1:1:8 train/val/test split, and evaluation across 24 IGL algorithms (10 node-level class-imbalance, 12 node-level topology-imbalance, 4 graph-level class-imbalance, 2 graph-level topology-imbalance) on 17 datasets. The benchmark is organized around four research questions: (RQ1) effectiveness under fixed imbalance ratios, (RQ2) robustness as ρ varies from balanced (ρ=1) to extreme (ρ=100), (RQ3) classifier boundary quality measured via Silhouette scores and t-SNE visualizations, and (RQ4) time-space efficiency and scalability. A key design choice is the taxonomy of imbalance: node-level topology-imbalance is further split into local (degree distribution) and global (under-reaching/over-squashing), while graph-level topology-imbalance captures uneven graph-size distributions. The framework is released as an open-source package with all processed datasets, baseline implementations, and reproduction scripts.

## Results

Across 17 datasets and 24 IGL algorithms, TOPOAUC achieves the best or near-best accuracy on 5 of 7 node-level class-imbalanced datasets (e.g., 83.34% on Cora, 69.03% on CiteSeer), while DataDec dominates all 8 graph-level class-imbalanced datasets (e.g., 78.39% on REDDIT-B, 71.48% on COLLAB). For node-level local topology-imbalance, Cold Brew leads on heterophilic graphs (59.16% on Chameleon, 43.04% on Squirrel) while GraphPatcher excels on homophilic ones (92.28% on Photo). Over half of all IGL algorithms fail (OOM or timeout) on the large-scale ogbn-arXiv dataset, and PASTEL's quadratic complexity (O(|V|²)) makes it infeasible at scale despite achieving the best global topology-imbalance results (e.g., 47.12% on Chameleon vs. 33.90% GCN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For node-level class-imbalance, TOPOAUC outperforms the GCN backbone by up to 7.0% (Cora: 83.34% vs. 76.36%) and beats all other IGL methods on 5 of 7 datasets, while GraphSHA achieves the best result on Photo (91.55%) and ogbn-arXiv (60.30%). DataDec is the clear winner for graph-level class-imbalance, surpassing GIN by 7.9–17.3% across all 8 datasets (e.g., 78.39% vs. 61.10% on REDDIT-B), whereas TopoImb and ImGKB show high variance and frequently underperform the GIN backbone. For node-level local topology-imbalance, Cold Brew leads on heterophilic graphs (Chameleon: 59.16% vs. 52.74% GCN; Squirrel: 43.04% vs. 28.70%) while GraphPatcher dominates homophilic ones (Photo: 92.28% vs. 89.43%), and DEMO-Net is the only method to beat GCN on all 9 datasets. PASTEL achieves the best global topology-imbalance results (e.g., 47.12% on Chameleon vs. 33.90% GCN) but its O(|V|²) complexity renders it infeasible on ogbn-arXiv, where sub-quadratic methods like ReNode and TAM degrade significantly. Overall, no single algorithm handles both class- and topology-imbalance effectively at scale, and over half of all 24 algorithms fail on ogbn-arXiv due to memory or time constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a single prior method but rather consolidates the entire Imbalanced Graph Learning (IGL) subfield, which has grown from scattered individual algorithm papers (DRGCN, GraphSMOTE, Tail-GNN, PASTEL, DataDec, etc.) without a unified evaluation framework. It departs from prior IGL work by identifying three critical gaps— inconsistent dataset preparation, variable experimental protocols, and non-standardized metrics—that made cross-paper comparisons impossible. The benchmark unifies 24 existing IGL algorithms (10 node-level class-imbalance, 12 node-level topology-imbalance, 4 graph-level class-imbalance, 2 graph-level topology-imbalance) under a single protocol with 17 datasets, positioning itself as the first open-sourced, reproducible evaluation infrastructure for this area.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that over half of IGL algorithms fail entirely on the large-scale ogbn-arXiv dataset due to memory or time constraints, and that no current algorithm effectively handles both class-imbalance and topology-imbalance simultaneously without relying on homophily assumptions—two findings that sharply delineate the field's practical limitations. The observation that class-imbalance and topology-imbalance are not fully orthogonal (algorithms designed for one partially help the other) opens a concrete research direction toward unified 'one-for-both' solutions. Key open questions include: developing IGL methods that scale to graph foundation models, reducing the quadratic complexity of structure-learning approaches like PASTEL, and improving robustness under extreme imbalance ratios (ρ=100) where all algorithms degrade substantially.
