## Problem

Offline RL methods that handle model mismatch (robust RL) are predominantly model-based, requiring estimation and storage of full transition kernels (O(S²A) memory) and solving NP-hard inner optimization problems, which limits scalability to large environments. Conversely, scalable model-free offline RL methods assume the deployment environment matches the data collection environment, leaving them vulnerable to the sim-to-real gap. No model-free algorithm with provable robustness guarantees existed for offline RL under model mismatch.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a pre-collected offline trajectory dataset (K episodes of length H for finite-horizon, or a single trajectory of length T for infinite-horizon) generated by a behavior policy under a nominal kernel, along with a per-state-action uncertainty radius R defining the l_α-norm perturbation region. It outputs a robust policy as a Q-table of size O(SA) that maximizes worst-case performance over the uncertainty set, requiring only visitation counts and Q-values in memory—no transition model is stored. The model-mismatch penalty κ is computed entirely from observed next-state values V(s′) without any knowledge of the true transition probabilities.

## Method

The core contribution is a double-pessimism principle that augments standard pessimistic Q-learning with a second penalty term for model mismatch. The Q-update at each step subtracts both a dataset pessimism term b_n (scaling as 1/√n where n is the visitation count, handling under-explored state-action pairs) and a model-mismatch penalty κ_{s,a}(V) that lower-bounds the worst-case transition σ_P(V) = min_{p∈P} pV without requiring model estimation. For l_α-norm uncertainty sets, κ takes the closed form R·min_w ‖we − V‖_β (β being the Hölder conjugate of α), computable in O(S) per step from a single observed next-state value. This yields a universal, model-free algorithm with O(SA) memory and polynomial-time computation, and the paper provides the first sample complexity analysis for model-free offline robust RL, achieving near-optimal rates of Õ(√(SC*/T)) for finite-horizon and Õ(√(SC*/(T(1−γ)⁵))) for infinite-horizon settings.

## Results

The proposed double-pessimism Q-learning achieves a sample complexity of $\tilde{O}\left(\frac{SC^*}{\epsilon^2(1-\gamma)^5}\right)$ for infinite-horizon MDPs and $\tilde{O}\left(\frac{H^6 SC^*}{\epsilon^2}\right)$ for finite-horizon MDPs, matching the rate of model-free offline non-robust RL without variance reduction. Memory complexity is reduced to O(SA) compared to O(S²A) for prior model-based robust methods, and computational complexity is polynomial versus NP-hard for Blanchet et al. (2023). In experiments, the double-pessimism algorithm consistently achieves lower optimality gaps than single-pessimism baselines across Garnet, Frozen-Lake, Taxi, and American Option environments, and maintains higher average reward under parameter perturbations in MountainCar and CartPole.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the single-pessimism Q-learning baseline (Yan et al., 2022), the double-pessimism method consistently achieves lower optimality gaps in all tabular environments (Garnet, Frozen-Lake, Taxi, American Option) and higher average reward under parameter perturbations in MountainCar and CartPole, confirming that the additional model-mismatch pessimism term is essential for robustness. Compared to vanilla CQL (Kumar et al., 2020), the double-pessimism CQL variant outperforms it under CartPole perturbations (τ = 0.2, 0.5, 0.8), demonstrating that the framework extends to function-approximation settings. Against the model-based methods of Blanchet et al. (2023) and Shi & Chi (2022), the proposed algorithm reduces memory from O(S²A) to O(SA) and eliminates the NP-hard inner optimization, at the cost of a slightly higher (1−γ) dependence (order 5 vs. 4) and, for Shi & Chi, the removal of the P_min factor. The paper does not report direct head-to-head numerical comparisons against the model-based baselines (which are intractable for the larger Classic Control environments), but the theoretical comparison in Table 1 shows the trade-off: better scalability and memory at a modest sample-complexity cost in the discount factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: the single-pessimism principle for offline RL (Rashidinejad et al., 2021; Yan et al., 2022; Shi et al., 2022), which penalizes under-explored state-action pairs, and robust RL frameworks (Iyengar, 2005; Nilim & El Ghaoui, 2004) that optimize worst-case performance over an uncertainty set. The key departure from prior offline robust RL (Shi & Chi, 2022; Blanchet et al., 2023) is that those methods are model-based, requiring O(S²A) memory to store transition kernels and solving NP-hard inner problems, whereas this paper introduces the first model-free offline robust RL algorithm with O(SA) memory and polynomial-time updates. The conceptual insight is that an unbiased estimator of the worst-case transition is not necessary—any pessimistic lower bound (the model-mismatch penalty κ) suffices to learn a robust policy, enabling a data-driven, model-free construction of κ for l_α-norm and χ²-divergence uncertainty sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a fundamental trade-off in offline RL where enhancing robustness to model mismatch (sim-to-real gap) previously required model-based approaches that scale poorly to large environments. The near-optimal sample complexity (matching non-robust offline Q-learning) demonstrates that robustness need not come at a data-efficiency cost, and the model-free design opens the door to applying robust offline RL to large-scale and continuous problems via function approximation (as demonstrated with CQL). Limitations include the tabular setting for the main theoretical results, a higher-order (1−γ)⁻⁵ dependence compared to model-based methods, and the fact that the penalty κ is biased so convergence is to a relaxed uncertainty set. Open questions include tightening the (1−γ) dependence, extending guarantees to continuous state-action spaces, and understanding the fundamental limits of model-free robust offline RL.
