## Problem

Existing neural network building blocks on non-Euclidean spaces (FC layers, attention, classifiers) are either restricted to a single family of symmetric spaces (hyperbolic or SPD) or depend on special algebraic structures that limit generality. No unified framework exists for constructing point-to-hyperplane distances and network components that works across all symmetric spaces of noncompact type, which include both hyperbolic spaces and SPD manifolds as special cases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253491/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on manifold-valued data: RGB images (32×32, CIFAR-10/100) are processed by a Euclidean ResNet-18 backbone whose output is mapped into the Poincaré ball; EEG signals are converted to sequences of SPD matrices via convolutional feature extraction; sentence pairs are encoded by a hyperbolic GRU; and graph node features are processed by a hyperbolic GCN. All network layers produce outputs that are points on the same symmetric space (manifold-valued output), and the final classification or generation step uses a Poincaré MLR or SPD-based classifier to yield class labels, generated images, entailment labels, or node labels.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified point-to-hyperplane distance defined via Busemann functions on symmetric spaces of noncompact type: d̄(x, H_{ξ,p}) = d(x,p) · B_ξ(⊖p ⊕ x) / ‖⊖p ⊕ x‖_S. Under G-invariant Riemannian metrics, this reduces to the closed-form ⟨a, H(g⁻¹hk)⟩ (Corollary 4.10), where H is the Iwasawa projection and a is a unit vector in the Cartan subalgebra. FC layers are then constructed by evaluating m Busemann functions at the input (one per output dimension) and mapping the resulting vector through the exponential map to produce a manifold point (Proposition 5.2). The attention mechanism uses these FC layers for query/key/value projections, computes attention scores from negative Riemannian distances between queries and keys, and aggregates values via the weighted Fréchet mean. The framework recovers the g-distance (Ganea et al., 2018) and h-distance (Fan et al., 2023) as special cases, and the SPD hypergyroplane distance (Nguyen & Yang, 2023) under Log-Euclidean metrics.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed Euclidean-Poincaré-B model achieves 95.23±0.08% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the closest baseline Euclidean-Poincaré-G (95.14±0.11%) and Hybrid Poincaré (95.04±0.13%). On CIFAR-100, it reaches 77.78±0.15%, matching Euclidean-Poincaré-G while surpassing Poincaré ResNet (76.60±0.32%). For EEG signal classification, AttSymSpd-LE achieves the best accuracy on all three datasets: 78.24±5.4% (BCIC-IV-2a), 70.96±8.6% (MAMEM-SSVEP-II), and 78.02±2.3% (BCI-NER), while AttSymSpd-GI uses roughly 5× fewer parameters (0.007 MB vs. 0.034 MB on BCIC-IV-2a) with comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed b-distance consistently outperforms or matches the g-distance (Ganea et al., 2018b) across all tasks: CIFAR-10 (95.23 vs. 95.14), image generation FID (38.32 vs. 39.68 on CIFAR-10), SNLI (81.01 vs. 80.89), and node classification on Disease (89.05 vs. 88.98) and Airport (85.04 vs. 84.78). The h-distance (Fan et al., 2023) is significantly outperformed in every setting, e.g., 81.72% vs. 95.23% on CIFAR-10 and 44.35% vs. 77.78% on CIFAR-100, attributed to its horospherical-projection construction lacking the geometric nature of closest-distance formulations. On image classification, the method also beats Hybrid Poincaré (Guo et al., 2022) by 0.19% on CIFAR-10 and 0.59% on CIFAR-100, and Poincaré ResNet (van Spengler et al., 2023) by 0.72% and 1.18% respectively. For EEG classification, both AttSymSpd-LE and AttSymSpd-GI surpass all 10 baselines (EEG-TCNet, MBEEGSE, MAtt, Graph-CSPNet, ShallowNet, EEGNet, SCCNet, TCNet-Fusion, FBCNet) on every dataset, with AttSymSpd-LE leading by 3.53% over MAtt (Pan et al., 2022) on BCIC-IV-2a and 5.46% over MAtt on MAMEM-SSVEP-II. AttSymSpd-GI achieves near-parity with AttSymSpd-LE on BCIC-IV-2a (78.08 vs. 78.24) while using only 0.007 MB of parameters versus 0.034 MB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the construction of point-to-hyperplane distances across hyperbolic spaces (Ganea et al. 2018, Fan et al. 2023) and SPD manifolds (Nguyen & Yang 2023, Chen et al. 2024a) under the general framework of symmetric spaces of noncompact type, using Busemann functions as the unifying geometric primitive. It departs from prior SPD manifold work that relies on rich algebraic structures (gyrovector spaces, PEM diffeomorphisms) by instead leveraging the Iwasawa and Cartan decompositions available for any G-invariant metric, yielding a closed-form distance in higher-rank spaces that was previously unavailable. The FC layers and attention mechanism proposed here are the first such building blocks designed for general symmetric spaces of noncompact type, extending the Poincaré MLR and hyperbolic attention (Shimizu et al. 2021) beyond rank-one settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified Busemann-function-based distance provides a single theoretical lens through which existing hyperbolic and SPD neural network components can be understood and compared, potentially guiding future architecture design across the full family of symmetric spaces. The G-invariant metric FC layers achieve comparable accuracy to Log-Euclidean variants with roughly 5× fewer parameters, suggesting a practical path to more efficient SPD networks. Open questions include whether the framework extends to compact-type symmetric spaces, how it scales to very high-rank spaces (e.g., large SPD matrices), and whether the modest empirical gains over strong baselines (e.g., 95.23 vs. 95.14 on CIFAR-10) will grow with more complex architectures or larger datasets.
