## Problem

Despite the empirical success of masked autoencoders (MAEs), their underlying mechanisms remain insufficiently understood, with prior analyses through contrastive learning and feature representation providing only implicit insights rather than a systematic optimization framework. This paper addresses the need for a theoretically grounded understanding of MAE training objectives that can directly guide architectural and loss-function improvements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251019/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images and generates N mutually orthogonal masks (N=4 with 75% masking ratio for ViT-B) whose visible patches collectively partition the entire original image, ensuring no patch is left uncovered (X_0 = 0). The encoder produces a latent feature for each masked view, which is then optimized via mutual information losses before being decoded to reconstruct the masked patches. An auxiliary lightweight approximation network takes the input and latent feature to estimate the posterior distribution q_θ(ẑ|X) required for the MI minimization loss.

## Method

MI-MAE applies the information bottleneck principle to MAEs, identifying the latent feature as the information bottleneck and proving that performance improves by maximizing relevant information between latent features of different orthogonal masks while minimizing irrelevant information between the input and latent space. The MI maximization loss uses InfoNCE to align latent features across N orthogonal masks of the same image, while the MI minimization loss employs a CLUB-style upper bound with a variational Gaussian approximation network to suppress input-specific information in the latent features. The total training objective combines the original MAE reconstruction loss with both MI losses, where the MI losses only backpropagate through the encoder while the reconstruction loss updates the full autoencoder. The orthogonal mask design is critical: it guarantees X_0 = 0, satisfying a theoretical condition in their corollary and enabling complete mutual information learning across all image patches, as confirmed by ablation showing 0.2% accuracy drop with independently generated masks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ViT-B, MI-MAE pre-trained for 400 epochs achieves 84.1% fine-tuning accuracy, 69.3% linear probing accuracy, and 52.3% 1%-data fine-tuning accuracy, surpassing the 1600-epoch MAE by 0.5, 1.3, and 1.2 percentage points respectively. On COCO detection, MI-MAE reaches 52.0 AP^box and 46.1 AP^mask (vs. 51.2/45.5 for MAE), and on ADE20K segmentation it achieves 49.3 mIoU (vs. 48.1 for MAE). For robustness, MI-MAE improves ImageNet-A accuracy from 35.9% to 37.4% and reduces ImageNet-C mean corruption error from 51.7 to 49.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MI-MAE outperforms all nine baselines on ImageNet-1K fine-tuning accuracy. Against the strongest direct competitor, 1600-epoch MAE (83.6%), MI-MAE at 400 epochs gains 0.5% (84.1%), and at 200 epochs still leads by 0.6% over 800-epoch MAE (83.9% vs. 83.3%). On linear probing, MI-MAE (69.3%) surpasses MAE-1600 (68.0%) by 1.3% and MFF (67.0%) by 2.3%. On the 1%-data fine-tuning setting, MI-MAE (52.3%) exceeds MAE-1600 (51.1%) by 1.2% and MFF (48.0%) by 4.3%. On COCO detection, MI-MAE improves over MAE by +0.8 AP^box and +0.6 AP^mask, and on ADE20K segmentation by +1.2 mIoU. The method also generalizes to the SimMIM/Swin-B architecture, where MI-SimMIM (84.1% FT) beats SimMIM (83.8%) and PixMIM (83.5%) at half the training epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoder (MAE) framework by introducing an information-theoretic lens via the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle, departing from prior analyses that understood MAEs through contrastive learning (Kong & Zhang, 2023; Zhang et al., 2022) or feature representation analysis (Xie et al., 2023; Pan et al., 2023). Concretely, it builds on MAE's encoder-decoder architecture and adds two mutual information losses on the latent features: an InfoNCE-based maximization loss across orthogonally masked views and a CLUB-based minimization loss between input and latent space. The orthogonal masking strategy (N mutually disjoint masks per image) is a practical departure from MAE's single random mask, and the method is also applied to the SimMIM framework to demonstrate generality across MIM architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IB-based theoretical analysis provides a principled explanation for why optimizing latent features matters in MAEs, moving beyond the implicit contrastive-learning interpretations that prior work offered. Practically, MI-MAE matches or exceeds 1600-epoch MAE with only 400 epochs (4× fewer mask samples), which is a meaningful efficiency gain for practitioners. However, the approach introduces additional complexity (an approximation network, orthogonal mask generation, and a staged training schedule tied to Assumption 3), and the absolute improvements over strong baselines (0.5–0.6% on ImageNet) are modest. Open questions include whether the IB framing generalizes to other self-supervised paradigms (e.g., video MAE, audio) and whether the orthogonal masking constraint is truly necessary or merely sufficient for the theoretical guarantees.
