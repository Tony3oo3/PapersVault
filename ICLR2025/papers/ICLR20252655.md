## Problem

Selective state space models like Mamba excel at 1D sequential modeling but struggle with 2D visual data. Existing visual SSMs flatten images into 1D sequences via multi-directional scanning patterns, which distorts inherent spatial relationships (e.g., vertical neighbors become image-width apart in a row-major scan) and incurs high computational cost from lengthened scanning paths. The paper asks whether spatial dependencies can be captured directly in the latent state space without relying on multiple scanning directions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252655/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 (classification), 1280×800 (detection), or 512×512 crops (segmentation). Internally, the 2D feature map is flattened into a 1D sequence via a single unidirectional sweeping scan, processed through the SSM state transition, then reshaped back to 2D before the structure-aware fusion step. Outputs are class logits (1000-way for ImageNet), bounding boxes with instance masks (COCO), or pixel-wise class maps (ADE20K), produced by task-specific heads on the hierarchical backbone features.

## Method

Spatial-Mamba replaces multi-directional scanning with a single unidirectional scan plus a Structure-Aware State Fusion (SASF) equation inserted between the state transition and observation equations. After computing the original state x_t via the standard Mamba recurrence, the state is reshaped to 2D and fused with its spatial neighbors using multi-scale dilated depth-wise convolutions (3×3 kernels with dilation factors d=1,3,5), producing a structure-aware state h_t that aggregates local spatial context. The observation equation then reads from h_t rather than x_t. The overall architecture is a Swin-like four-stage hierarchical backbone with three model sizes (T/S/B). Theoretically, the paper shows that Mamba, linear attention, and Spatial-Mamba all reduce to y = Mu under a unified matrix framework, where M is a lower triangular matrix for the first two but a structured adjacency matrix for Spatial-Mamba, explaining why the latter captures spatial structure more effectively.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, Spatial-Mamba-T/S/B achieve top-1 accuracies of 83.5%, 84.6%, and 85.3% with 27M/43M/96M parameters and 4.5G/7.1G/15.8G FLOPs, respectively. On COCO with Mask R-CNN (1× schedule), Spatial-Mamba-T/S/B attain box mAP of 47.6/49.2/50.4 and mask mAP of 42.9/44.0/45.1. On ADE20K with UPerNet, Spatial-Mamba-T/S/B reach single-scale mIoU of 48.6/50.6/51.8 and multi-scale mIoU of 49.4/51.4/52.6. Notably, Spatial-Mamba achieves these results with only a single unidirectional scan, whereas competing SSMs require multiple scanning directions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spatial-Mamba consistently outperforms all SSM-based baselines: it beats VMamba-T/S/B by 0.9/1.0/1.4% top-1 accuracy and LocalVMamba-T/S by 0.8/0.9%, while using fewer FLOPs (4.5G vs. 4.9G for T; 7.1G vs. 8.7G for S) and a single scan instead of four-way or windowed scans. Against the strongest Transformer baselines, Spatial-Mamba-T exceeds Swin-T by 2.2% and NAT-T by 0.3%; Spatial-Mamba-S surpasses NAT-S by 1.6% and Swin-S by 1.6%; Spatial-Mamba-B beats NAT-B by 1.0% and Swin-B by 1.8%. Compared to CNN baselines, Spatial-Mamba-T outperforms ConvNeXt-T by 1.4% with fewer parameters (27M vs. 29M). On COCO detection (1× schedule), Spatial-Mamba-T achieves 47.6 box mAP, surpassing VMamba-T (47.3) and Swin-T (42.7) by 0.3 and 4.9 points respectively, while on ADE20K segmentation, Spatial-Mamba-B reaches 52.6 multi-scale mIoU, exceeding VMamba-B (51.6) by 1.0 mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

Spatial-Mamba builds directly on Mamba's selective state space formulation and the broader SSM lineage (S4, S5, HiPPO), but departs from the dominant strategy in visual SSMs (Vim, VMamba, LocalMamba, PlainMamba) of flattening 2D images into multiple 1D sequences via sweeping, continuous, or windowed scans. Instead of adding more scanning directions to recover spatial structure, it introduces a structure-aware state fusion (SASF) equation using multi-scale dilated convolutions in the latent state space, establishing neighborhood connectivity without lengthening the sequential path. The paper also provides a theoretical unification showing that linear attention, Mamba, and Spatial-Mamba are all instances of y = Mu with different structured matrix M (lower-triangular vs. adjacency), offering a coherent algebraic perspective on their relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single unidirectional scan with SASF matches or exceeds multi-directional scanning approaches (4-way, bidirectional) across classification, detection, and segmentation, reducing computational overhead while improving spatial modeling. This suggests the field may not need to keep adding scanning directions as a primary mechanism for spatial awareness in visual SSMs. Open questions include whether SASF generalizes to video/3D data, whether more expressive fusion operators (dynamic or deformable convolution, attention) can close the remaining gap at acceptable cost, and whether the matrix-unification framework can guide the design of future hybrid SSM-attention architectures.
