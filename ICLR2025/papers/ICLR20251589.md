## Problem

Existing ADMM variants for nonconvex composite optimization require at least one block of the objective to be smooth (i.e., its nonsmooth component h_n = 0), which excludes many practical problems involving nonsmooth, non-Lipschitz regularizers like indicator functions of orthogonality or sparsity constraints. This paper closes that gap by showing that continuity in just one block suffices, and provides the first iteration-complexity guarantee for ADMM on this class of fully nonsmooth nonconvex problems.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a multi-block problem with n blocks where (n−1) blocks may be nonsmooth, nonconvex, and non-Lipschitz (e.g., indicator functions of Stiefel manifolds or ℓ₁ norms), while the nth block must be convex and Lipschitz continuous. The constraint matrix A_n must be either bijective (with condition number κ < 2) or surjective. The output is an ε-approximate critical point, but notably the critical-point certificate is evaluated at the proximal intermediate point x̌_n (the argmin of the Moreau envelope subproblem) rather than the primal iterate x_n itself, which is a subtle but important distinction for the complexity bound.

## Method

IPDS-ADMM is a proximal linearized ADMM that couples an Increasing Penalization and Decreasing Smoothing (IPDS) strategy: the penalty β^t = β⁰(1+ξt^p) grows sublinearly while the Moreau-envelope smoothing parameter μ^t = 1/(λ̄δβ^t) shrinks inversely, applied only to the convex nonsmooth block h_n. Each primal block is updated via a proximal linearized subproblem (a proximal operator call), and the dual variable is updated with either over-relaxation σ∈[1,2) when A_n is bijective (faster convergence) or under-relaxation σ∈(0,1) when A_n is surjective (global convergence). A novel Lyapunov potential function combining the augmented Lagrangian, dual-difference, and primal-difference terms is constructed to prove sufficient decrease, yielding an O(ε^{-3}) iteration complexity for reaching an ε-critical point — improving upon the O(ε^{-4}) bound of the prior Riemannian ADMM (RADMM) that used a fixed large penalty and fixed small smoothing parameter.

## Results

On the sparse PCA problem (ρ=100, β⁰=50ρ, σ≈1.618), IPDS-ADMM consistently achieves the lowest objective function values across all 8 test datasets (randn, MNIST, TDT2, sector) compared to SubGrad, SPGM, and RADMM. The convergence curves (Figure 1) show IPDS-ADMM reaching lower objective values within the same wall-clock time budget, with the advantage being most pronounced on larger datasets (e.g., randn-2500-500, TDT2-3000-500). The theoretical iteration complexity is O(ε⁻³) to reach an ε-approximate critical point, improving upon RADMM's O(ε⁻⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPDS-ADMM outperforms all three baselines on objective value across all 8 datasets. SubGrad (Li et al., 2021) is the weakest performer, converging slowest and reaching the highest objective values, consistent with its sublinear rate for nonsmooth problems. SPGM (Bohm & Wright, 2021), which uses a variable smoothing strategy on the primal side only, is more competitive than SubGrad but still lags behind the primal-dual IPDS-ADMM, confirming that multiplier-based splitting methods are generally more robust and faster than primal-only approaches. RADMM (Li et al., 2022) is the closest competitor, using a fixed large penalty (β=100ρ) and fixed small smoothing parameter, but IPDS-ADMM's increasing penalty and decreasing smoothing strategy allows it to achieve lower objective values in the same time budget. The key practical advantage over RADMM is that IPDS-ADMM avoids the overly conservative fixed step sizes that RADMM requires, while also improving the theoretical complexity from O(ε⁻⁴) to O(ε⁻³).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ADMM lineage from Gabay & Mercier (1976) through nonconvex variants (Li & Pong, 2015; Hong et al., 2016; Yang et al., 2017) by introducing the IPDS strategy—simultaneously increasing the penalty and decreasing the Moreau-envelope smoothing parameter—within an ADMM framework. It directly builds on RADMM (Li et al., 2022), which first applied Moreau envelope smoothing to ADMM for nonsmooth nonconvex problems but used fixed penalty and smoothing parameters, yielding O(ε^{-4}) complexity. The paper also adapts the under-relaxation technique for surjective matrices from Boț et al. (2019), combining it with over-relaxation for bijective matrices in a unified framework. The key conceptual departure is that prior nonconvex ADMM methods require at least one block to be smooth (h_n = 0), whereas IPDS-ADMM only requires continuity in one block, a strictly weaker condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first iteration complexity result (O(ε^{-3})) for ADMM applied to multi-block nonsmooth nonconvex problems where no block is smooth, improving upon RADMM's O(ε^{-4}) and matching the rate of smoothing proximal gradient methods. The minimal continuity assumption broadens the class of solvable problems to include indicator functions of constraint sets (e.g., orthogonality, sparsity) in all but one block. Limitations include the requirement that proximal operators be computable exactly, the restriction to linear equality constraints, and the fact that O(ε^{-3}) remains worse than the O(ε^{-2}) achievable in convex settings. Open questions include whether the rate is optimal, whether the IPDS strategy extends to stochastic or nonlinearly constrained settings, and whether the bijective/surjective dichotomy can be further relaxed.
