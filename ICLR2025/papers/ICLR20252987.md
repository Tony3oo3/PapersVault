## Problem

Distance-field-based implicit representations (SDF/UDF) introduce surface extraction distortions because MC-based interpolation along cube edges assumes the closest surface at both endpoints, and UDF gradients become ill-defined at equidistant or on-surface locations. No prior representation directly encodes the spatial relationship between a query line segment and the surface, which is the geometric primitive actually used during extraction.

## Input / Output

For the neural representation task, the method takes a 3D shape (rescaled to a unit bounding cube) and overfits an 8-layer MLP on 10 million randomly sampled line segments, producing a Line Segment Field that outputs a binary intersection flag and a continuous ratio in [0,1] for any query segment. For the point-cloud reconstruction task, the input is a raw 3D point cloud from which K=20 nearest-neighbor patches are extracted around the midpoint of each query segment; the output in both cases is a triangle mesh extracted at grid resolution 128 via the proposed Edge-based Dual Contouring.

## Method

The core contribution is a Line Segment Field (LSF) that assigns each spatial line segment two attributes: a binary intersection flag o and an intersection ratio s locating the crossing point as a convex combination of the endpoints. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that the unoriented surface normal at the intersection point is parallel to the spatial gradient of s with respect to either endpoint, enabling differentiable normal estimation via backpropagation. Surface extraction uses Edge-based Dual Contouring (E-DC): for each cube, normals are computed from the gradient of s at intersecting edges, a quadratic error function is solved to place a vertex, and vertices across the four cubes sharing an edge are connected to form triangles. The neural variant is a lightweight MLP (512-wide, Softplus activations, Sigmoid head) trained with binary cross-entropy on o and L1 on s; the point-cloud variant replaces the endpoint input with two MLP+MaxPool feature extractors over K-NN patches relative to each endpoint, concatenated and passed through a third MLP to predict (o, s).

## Results

On the ABC dataset, SALS achieves the best Chamfer Distance (4.493×10⁻³), Normal Consistency (0.980), and F1-score at 0.005 (0.638) and 0.01 (0.965) among all 10 compared methods, with only 1.84M parameters. On the non-manifold ABC dataset, SALS maintains its lead over all baselines, while BOF and SDF-based methods degrade significantly since they cannot represent non-manifold geometry. For point cloud surface reconstruction (Table 2), SALS achieves CD=4.560×10⁻³ and F1-0.005=0.630 on ABC, outperforming the closest competitor GeoUDF (CD=4.678, F1-0.005=0.615) and substantially beating POCO (CD=8.855) and GIFS (CD=5.726).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALS wins on all four metrics (CD, NC, F1-0.005, F1-0.01) against every baseline on both ABC and non-manifold ABC. The closest competitor is Neural UDF + DCUDF (CD=4.593 vs. 4.493; F1-0.005=0.625 vs. 0.638), a margin of ~0.1 in CD and ~0.013 in F1. SDF-based methods (Neural SDF + NDC, CD=4.607) and the heavier UODF (5.55M params, CD=4.794) trail by a wider gap. BOF and SDF collapse on non-manifold shapes (BOF CD jumps from 6.385 to 14.371), confirming their inherent inability to represent open or multi-layer geometry. For point cloud reconstruction, SALS beats GeoUDF by 0.118 in CD and 0.015 in F1-0.005, and outperforms POCO by 4.295 in CD on ABC; the gap widens on non-manifold ABC where POCO's CD reaches 16.830 versus SALS's 6.033. NGLOD (10.14M params) underperforms SALS despite 5.5× more parameters, attributing the advantage to SALS's line-segment-centric field design rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SALS departs from the dominant point-centric implicit representation paradigm (SDF, UDF, BOF) by shifting the fundamental query unit from individual 3D points to spatial line segments, motivated by the observation that MC and DC extraction methods inherently interpolate along cube edges. It builds on the dual contouring framework of Ju et al. (2002) but reformulates the QEF around edge-level intersection data rather than vertex-level field values, and introduces a theoretical result (Theorem 1) linking the surface normal to the spatial gradient of the intersection ratio. For point cloud reconstruction, it follows the local-patch + MLP architecture common in the field (POCO, GeoUDF) but applies it to the 6D line segment query space rather than 3D point queries, distinguishing it from GIFS (which still relies on UDF interpolation) and UODF (which is non-differentiable and uses Poisson meshing).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The line segment field offers a principled way to handle non-manifold, open-boundary, and multi-layer geometries that SDF fundamentally cannot represent, while avoiding the gradient ill-definedness of UDF at critical positions. The practical limitation is that the 6D input space demands substantially more sampled line segments (10M) for accurate modeling compared to 3D point queries, and the method's advantage over the strongest baseline (GeoUDF) is moderate on standard watertight shapes, becoming more pronounced only on non-manifold structures. Open questions include whether the sampling requirement can be reduced through adaptive or hierarchical line segment sampling, and whether the LSF paradigm extends naturally to dynamic or 4D shape representation.
