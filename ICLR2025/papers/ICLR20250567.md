## Problem

Existing description-based prompt learning methods for VLMs extract text descriptions from LLMs (e.g., GPT) to enrich prompts, but these descriptions suffer from high variability and low reliability due to open-ended queries and inherent model biases. Additionally, in multi-prompt settings, no prior work accounts for the varying importance of individual prompts during training, treating all prompts equally despite their differing semantic contributions to classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250567/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and outputs class labels via a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone in a few-shot regime (1–16 labeled samples per class). The key intermediate representation consists of M=32 learnable continuous prompt vectors (each with N=16 context tokens) per class, which are optimized jointly with a mapping function φ that projects CLIP embeddings into the 3072-dimensional GPT text-embedding-3-large space. No hand-crafted text templates or extracted descriptions are used; prompts remain as continuous vectors throughout training and inference.

## Method

DeMul eliminates the description-extraction step by directly distilling GPT's pre-trained semantic knowledge into learnable prompts: a mapping function φ projects CLIP prompt embeddings into GPT's embedding space, and a distillation loss (Eq. 3) aligns each mapped prompt with the corresponding GPT class embedding via a softmax cross-entropy objective. A cyclic mapping (φ followed by a frozen pseudo-inverse ψ) is trained with a cosine-similarity loss to preserve directional structure during the space transfer, with ψ frozen during fine-tuning to maintain CLIP-space clustering. For the multi-prompt setting, each prompt t_ij within class c_i is assigned a learnable scalar weight w_ij (normalized per class) that modulates its contribution to the classification loss, with an L1 penalty encouraging sparsity so that fewer but more informative prompts dominate. The total training objective combines the weighted classification loss, the GPT-space distillation loss (scaled by α=0.5), and the cyclic mapping loss, optimized over 100 epochs with SGD and cosine decay on a frozen CLIP backbone.

## Results

DeMul achieves an average top-1 accuracy of 75.5% (1-shot), 78.6% (2-shot), 80.9% (4-shot), 83.6% (8-shot), and 85.3% (16-shot) across 11 downstream datasets, representing a 10.5% improvement over zero-shot CLIP in 1-shot and 20.3% in 16-shot. Compared to the strongest learnable multi-prompt baseline GalLoP, DeMul improves by 1.8% in 1-shot (75.5% vs. 73.7%) and 0.9% in 16-shot (85.3% vs. 84.4%). The method also outperforms description-based zero-shot methods (dCLIP, WaffleCLIP) by a wide margin in all few-shot settings, confirming that direct LLM embedding distillation is more effective than inserting GPT-generated text descriptions into prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeMul outperforms all seven baselines across every shot setting and nearly every individual dataset. Against the strongest few-shot baseline GalLoP, DeMul leads by 1.8% average in 1-shot (75.5% vs. 73.7%) and 0.9% in 16-shot (85.3% vs. 84.4%), with the largest per-dataset gains on Stanford Cars (+3.7% at 1-shot) and DTD (+6.1% at 1-shot). Compared to PromptSRC, the gap is larger: 3.2% in 1-shot and 2.4% in 16-shot. Against MaPLe, DeMul improves by 6.2% (1-shot) and 3.5% (16-shot). The description-based zero-shot methods (dCLIP at 66.5%, WaffleCLIP at 67.1%) are substantially outperformed by DeMul even in the 1-shot setting (75.5%), confirming that direct embedding-space distillation from GPT is more effective than inserting GPT-generated text descriptions. CoOp, the single-prompt baseline, trails DeMul by 17.3% in 1-shot and 5.8% in 16-shot, highlighting the benefit of multi-prompt learning with weighted aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM prompt learning lineage from CoOp (continuous prompt optimization) and GalLoP (multi-prompt global/local learning) by introducing a description-free distillation mechanism that maps learnable prompts into GPT's embedding space via a conformal cyclic mapping, rather than extracting textual descriptions through the Completion Endpoint as in dCLIP and WaffleCLIP. The key conceptual departure is repurposing the LLM's Embedding Endpoint as a semantic anchor for prompt optimization, eliminating the discrete text-generation step entirely. Additionally, the paper introduces L1-regularized prompt weighting in multi-prompt settings, a component absent from prior multi-prompt methods like MaPLe and PromptSRC that treat all prompts symmetrically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that direct embedding-space distillation consistently outperforms description-based prompt construction (10.5% over CLIP in 1-shot, 1.8% over GalLoP) validates a simpler, more reliable pipeline for leveraging LLM knowledge in VLM adaptation, removing a source of stochastic variability. However, the method still relies on shared learnable vectors across all classes for memory efficiency, limiting class-specific prompt specialization, and performance remains sensitive to the few-shot training distribution. Open questions include whether class-specific prompts can be achieved without prohibitive memory costs, and whether the conformal mapping approach generalizes beyond CLIP to other VLM architectures or to non-classification tasks like detection and segmentation.
