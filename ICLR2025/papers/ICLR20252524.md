## Problem

The Lyapunov energy function introduced by Krotov & Hopfield (2020) for associative memory becomes non-discriminative in non-compact flat regions wherever dead neurons (saturated or invariant activation directions) are present. In these flat regions, the energy alone cannot determine stability, identify steady states, or characterize basins of attraction, undermining the central theoretical guarantee of the energy-based framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252524/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a continuous state vector y ∈ ℝ^N, a symmetric weight matrix W, a bias vector b, and an activation function g(y) defined as the gradient of a Lagrange function L(y). Beyond identifying flat directions and performing stability analysis on the original system, the method outputs a modified dynamical system in a new coordinate u (related by y = Wu + b) together with a parametric family of Lyapunov functions E(u; α, β, γ, S) that are free of flat energy directions. The family is parameterized by scalars (α, β, γ) and a symmetric matrix S, with an additional matrix-valued function R(u) chosen to satisfy a matrix inequality, allowing non-symmetric W and non-positive-definite Hessian Λ.

## Method

The paper first proves (Proposition 1) that k dead neurons defined by a matrix V produce a non-compact flat energy region {y + Vc : c ∈ ℝ₊^k}, then performs a direct linearization of the ODE to show that stability is fully determined by the restriction of ΛWΛ − Λ to the range of the Hessian, and that the entire flat region constitutes the basin of attraction when the steady state is stable. As a remedy, the authors introduce a modified dynamical system u̇ = R(u)(g(Wu+b) − u) whose steady states are affinely equivalent to the original ones, and derive (Proposition 5) a parametric family of Lyapunov functions E = αE₁ + βE₂ + γE₃ whose non-increase is guaranteed by a matrix inequality RᵀF + FᵀR ≥ Q. Concrete choices of R and W (via polar decomposition, Lyapunov equations, or numerical abscissa bounds) yield valid energy functions without flat directions, and the framework extends to non-symmetric weight matrices and non-positive-definite Hessians, as demonstrated with a hierarchical Leaky-ReLU memory.

## Results

The paper's main results are theoretical. Proposition 1 formally proves that dead neurons in the Krotov–Hopfield energy function (2) create non-compact flat regions in state space, rendering the Lyapunov function unable to determine stability or locate steady states in those regions. Proposition 4 shows that stability can still be recovered by restricting analysis to the range of the Hessian matrix Λ, and that the entire flat region belongs to the basin of attraction when the steady state is stable. Proposition 5 constructs a parametric family of Lyapunov functions (E₁, E₂, E₃) for a modified dynamical system (5) that eliminates flat directions entirely, with concrete choices (Examples 5–7) that also permit non-symmetric weight matrices and non-positive-definite Hessians.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run empirical benchmarks against baselines; instead it compares the original Krotov–Hopfield dynamical system (1) with energy (2) against the proposed modified system (5) with energy family (6)/(10). The original energy (2) is shown to be completely equivalent to the 1984 Hopfield energy (3) whenever dead neurons are present, losing all information about dead-neuron degrees of freedom and becoming insensitive to bias changes (Proposition 2). The modified system (5) retains the same steady-state structure (related by the affine map y = Wu + b) but admits Lyapunov functions without flat directions, removes the requirement that W be symmetric (Example 6), and allows non-positive-definite Hessian matrices (Example 7 with smooth Leaky ReLU). The trade-off is that the modified system introduces a matrix-valued function R(u) in the dynamics, which must be chosen to satisfy a matrix inequality (7); the paper shows this is always possible with an orthogonal R (Proposition 5, item 2) and provides explicit constructions for several important cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Krotov & Hopfield (2020) framework for general associative memory with Lyapunov functions, using the dense model from Krotov (2021) as its base dynamical system. The key departure is identifying a fundamental flaw: the energy function becomes non-discriminative in non-compact flat regions whenever activation functions saturate (dead neurons), rendering it equivalent to the original Hopfield (1984) energy and useless for stability analysis. The authors then propose a modified dynamical system (Eq. 5) that preserves the same steady-state structure while admitting a family of Lyapunov functions free of flat directions, and further relaxes the symmetry requirement on weight matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the widely-adopted Krotov-Hopfield energy function silently degenerates in the presence of dead neurons challenges a core theoretical guarantee of the framework and has implications for all downstream applications (energy transformers, predictive coding, diffusion-model connections). The modified dynamical system offers a concrete remedy, but the paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the authors themselves note the Lyapunov function is 'underutilized' in practice, limiting immediate practical impact. Open questions include whether the modified system preserves memory capacity at scale, how the non-symmetric weight relaxation interacts with training, and whether the flat-direction analysis generalizes to architectures with pooling or convolutional layers.
