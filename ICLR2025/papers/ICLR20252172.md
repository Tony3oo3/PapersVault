## Problem

Existing equivariant neural networks for volumetric data face a fundamental trade-off: group convolutional networks achieve rotational equivariance only on discrete rotation groups (e.g., 90° rotations), while steerable convolutional networks achieve analytical equivariance on continuous SO(3) but impose rigid constraints on filter structures. Additionally, all previously published activation functions in the irreducible-representation space are 'global'—the output at any rotational point depends on values at all other points—preventing the formation of compact, locally meaningful feature representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 28×28×28 voxel 3D medical images (from MedMNIST3D) and outputs a class label for binary or multi-class classification. Internally, data is lifted into a 6D representation (3D spatial × SO(3) rotational) where the rotational component is encoded as Wigner matrix expansion coefficients up to a maximum degree L, and the spatial component is discretized into voxels. The final classification head applies SO(3) SoftMax pooling to collapse the rotational dimension before a linear readout.

## Method

EquiLoPO performs group convolution in the full SE(3) space by expressing both input and filter in Wigner matrix (irreducible SO(3) representation) basis, reducing the SO(3) integration to algebraic operations involving Clebsch-Gordan coefficients and spherical harmonics—yielding analytical equivariance on continuous SO(3) with fully unconstrained trainable filter weights. The key architectural innovation is a local activation function in the SO(3) space that approximates ReLU via a quadratic polynomial applied to Wigner coefficients, with adaptive coefficients computed from the per-voxel mean and standard deviation of the rotational distribution; this ensures a well-defined pointwise mapping in real rotational space (each input value maps to exactly one output value) and prevents the architecture from collapsing to a steerable network. The full model follows a ResNet-18 layout with these equivariant convolutions, SO(3)-adapted batch normalization, SO(3) SoftMax pooling, and dropout, trained end-to-end with Adam on the six MedMNIST3D classification tasks.

## Results

On the MedMNIST3D benchmark (six 3D medical imaging datasets, 28×28×28 voxels), the best EquiLoPO variant (local adaptive activation, 418k parameters) achieves AUC of 0.968/0.984 on Vessel/Synapse and ACC of 0.958/0.940, outperforming all baselines with far fewer parameters than 3D ResNet-50 (44M). The local trainable activation variant reaches AUC 0.991 on Organ and 0.965 on Synapse. Across all six datasets, EquiLoPO matches or exceeds state-of-the-art on nearly every metric, with the sole exception being accuracy on the already-saturated Organ dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiLoPO (418k params) substantially outperforms the closest equivariant baselines: on Vessel it achieves AUC 0.968 vs. 0.829 for ILPOResNet-50 (38k) and no reported AUC for Regular SE(3) convolution (172k); on Synapse it reaches AUC 0.984 vs. 0.940 for ILPOResNet-50. Against the much larger 3D ResNet-50 (44M params), EquiLoPO wins on Vessel AUC (0.968 vs. 0.907), Synapse AUC (0.984 vs. 0.851), and Fracture AUC (0.803 vs. 0.725) while using ~100× fewer parameters. The invariant ILPOResNet-50 (38k) is the only method with fewer parameters, but EquiLoPO surpasses it on 11 of 12 reported AUC/ACC metrics. Against AutoML baselines (auto-sklearn, AutoKeras) and FPVT, EquiLoPO leads by large margins on most datasets (e.g., Synapse AUC 0.984 vs. 0.631 for auto-sklearn). The sole exception is Organ accuracy, where 3D ResNet-18 (0.907) slightly edges out EquiLoPO's local adaptive variant (0.767), though the local trainable variant reaches 0.866.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of equivariant deep learning for 3D data: group convolutional networks (which achieve equivariance only on discrete rotation groups) and steerable convolutional networks (which achieve analytical SO(3) equivariance but constrain filter structures). By using Wigner matrices as the Fourier basis in a group convolution, the authors obtain analytical equivariance on continuous SO(3) while keeping filters unconstrained. The critical departure from steerable networks is a novel local activation function in the Wigner coefficient space that prevents the architecture from collapsing into a steerable network (demonstrated in Appendix A), and this builds directly on the authors' prior ILPO-NET and 6DCNN work on invariant roto-translational pattern recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that true analytical rotational equivariance on SO(3) with unconstrained filters is achievable and beneficial for 3D medical image classification, consistently matching or exceeding state-of-the-art on MedMNIST3D. However, the approach is limited to regular volumetric data at 28×28×28 resolution, incurs substantial computational overhead (27 GB memory vs. 2.5 GB for ResNet-18), and the Python implementation of the activation is a significant bottleneck. Open questions include extending the framework to irregular or larger-scale data, reducing computational cost through C++ implementation, and whether the local activation principle generalizes to other continuous groups beyond SO(3).
