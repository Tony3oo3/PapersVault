## Problem

Existing layer aggregation methods (DenseNet, RLA, MRLA) treat layer outputs as discrete states, a perspective that becomes inadequate for very deep networks where hundreds of layers form a more naturally continuous process. This paper asks whether the selective state space model (S6/Mamba), designed for long-sequence modeling, can be repurposed to aggregate information across the depth of a network by treating layer outputs as states of a continuous dynamical system.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252841/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1K, variable resolution for COCO) fed into conventional CNN (ResNet-50/101/152) or ViT (DeiT, Swin, PVTv2) backbones. The S6LA module does not alter the raw image input; instead, it intercepts the feature maps (H×W×D tensors for CNNs, L×D token sequences for ViTs) at each layer and maintains a latent state h of configurable dimension N (default 32) that is updated sequentially across layers. Outputs are standard class logits, bounding boxes, or segmentation masks depending on the downstream head.

## Method

S6LA reformulates layer aggregation as a selective state space model update: at each layer t, the hidden state is updated via h^t = e^{ΔA}·h^{t-1} + ΔB·X^t, where Δ and B are input-dependent (selective) parameters derived from the current layer's output, and A is a fixed structured state matrix initialized following Mamba. For CNNs, h^{t-1} is concatenated with the current input X^t along the channel dimension before passing through the convolutional block; for ViTs, the class token computes the selective parameters and the hidden state modulates patch tokens via element-wise multiplication (X̂_p = X_p + W·X_p·h^t). The selective mechanism allows the model to adaptively decide how much information from each layer to retain in the latent state, effectively solving the long-sequence aggregation problem across network depth without the quadratic memory cost of retaining all prior layer features.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, S6LA achieves 78.0% Top-1 accuracy on ResNet-50 (vs. 76.1% vanilla), 79.1% on ResNet-101 (vs. 77.4%), and 79.4% on ResNet-152 (vs. 78.3%), representing a consistent ~1.9% improvement over vanilla ResNets with only 0.3B additional FLOPs. On vision transformer backbones, S6LA yields ~1.5% Top-1 gains (e.g., DeiT-S: 81.3% vs. 79.9%; PVTv2-B2: 82.3% vs. 81.4%). On MS COCO 2017 object detection with Faster R-CNN, S6LA reaches 40.3 AP^bb with ResNet-50 (vs. 36.4 vanilla) and 41.7 with ResNet-101 (vs. 38.7), while on instance segmentation with Mask R-CNN it achieves 42.7 AP^bb and 38.3 AP^m with ResNet-101.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S6LA outperforms all 14 compared baselines across every backbone and task. On ResNet-50 ImageNet classification, S6LA (78.0% Top-1) beats the strongest prior layer-aggregation method MRLA (77.5%) by 0.5% and RLA (77.2%) by 0.8%, while also surpassing channel-attention methods like ECA (77.4%), CBAM (77.3%), and GCNet (77.7%) with fewer parameters (25.8M vs. 28–34M). Against the dense-connection baselines, S6LA exceeds DenseNet-161 (77.7%) and DIANet (77.2%) by 0.3–0.8% Top-1. On vision transformers, S6LA consistently improves over MRLA: DeiT-S 81.3% vs. 80.7%, Swin-B 83.3% vs. 82.5%, PVTv2-B2 82.3% vs. 81.6%, with lower FLOPs in most cases. In object detection (Faster R-CNN, COCO), S6LA achieves 40.3 AP^bb with ResNet-50, edging out MRLA (40.1) and RLA (38.8), and 41.7 with ResNet-101 vs. MRLA's 41.3. For instance segmentation (Mask R-CNN), S6LA reaches 42.7 AP^bb / 38.3 AP^m with ResNet-101, surpassing MRLA (42.5 / 38.1) and RLA (41.8 / 37.3). In the appendix, S6LA also outperforms BA-Net-50 (78.0 vs. 77.8 Top-1) and RelationNet++ (40.3 vs. 39.4 AP^bb on detection).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) layer aggregation methods for deep networks (ResNet skip connections, DenseNet, RLA, MRLA) that treat layer outputs as discrete states to be combined, and (2) selective state space models (S6/Mamba) developed for long-sequence modeling in NLP. The key conceptual departure is reframing layer outputs as states of a *continuous* process—drawing on the ResNet-as-neural-ODE analogy (Sander et al., 2022; Queiruga et al., 2020)—and then applying the S6 selective discretization (Eq. 4) as the aggregation rule. Unlike RLA (recurrent additive aggregation) or MRLA (attention-based cross-layer retrieval), S6LA introduces input-dependent gating (Δ, B) that lets the model selectively retain or forget information from previous layers, borrowing the selective mechanism directly from Mamba.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous-process framing offers a principled justification for why layer aggregation should behave differently in very deep networks versus shallow ones, and the S6LA module provides a lightweight, plug-in mechanism that works across both CNN and ViT backbones. However, the empirical gains are modest (~1–2% Top-1 on ImageNet, ~1–2 AP on COCO) and come with a small parameter/FLOP overhead, so the practical impact is incremental rather than transformative. Open questions include whether the continuous-process perspective yields larger benefits at extreme depths (e.g., 1000+ layers), whether the SSM state can be exploited for more fundamental architectural changes beyond a bolt-on module, and whether the selective mechanism's advantage over simpler recurrent aggregation (RLA) persists at scale. The paper also notes that reducing parameters and FLOPs while maintaining accuracy remains a goal.
