## Problem

Existing LLM jailbreak attacks either rely on single-step prompts that trigger immediate safety rejections or multi-step methods that inefficiently iterate prompts using other LLMs for refinement. No prior work provides a principled, automated mechanism for learning which sequence of context-modifying queries most effectively weakens a model's safety boundaries over multiple conversational turns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251196/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a Direct Malicious Query (DMQ) — an explicitly harmful prompt from one of 18 categories — and a trained policy consisting of Q-values over 7 CSQ categories. It produces an ordered sequence of context-switching queries appended to a growing context window H = {DMQ; (c₁,s₁); …; (c_T,s_T)}, where each CSQ–response pair accumulates to progressively shift the LLM's interpretive frame. A judge function (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) evaluates each LLM response and returns a binary reward (harmful/benign) that drives the bandit updates during both training and testing.

## Method

The paper frames CSQ selection as a K-armed bandit problem where each arm corresponds to one of 7 CSQ categories (context misdirection, emotional engagement, storytelling, authority imposition, conflicting choices, ethical reassurance, statistical justification). During training, an ε-greedy policy selects a CSQ category at each step, the target LLM generates a response within the accumulating context window, and a judge function assigns a binary reward; Q-values are updated via incremental mean tracking with a cost function C_total that penalizes both long sequences and excessive exploration. The learned policy π* is then applied to unseen DMQs to generate SoC attack sequences, achieving 95% ASR with a 15-second cost-per-prompt. Theoretically, the paper derives Hoeffding-based concentration bounds showing the expected number of suboptimal CSQ selections is O(log T / Δ²ᵢ), yielding an upper bound of O(K log T / Δ²_min) on the total attack sequence length until the optimal CSQ category is consistently exploited.

## Results

The proposed SoC attack achieves a 95% Attack Success Rate (ASR) with a Cost Per Prompt (CPP) of only 15 seconds, substantially outperforming all baselines on both metrics. Cross-validation across 18 DMQ categories yields an average ASR of 92.14% (range: 90.5%–93.3%) on unseen categories. The method generalizes to larger and closed-source models, achieving 90.5% ASR on Llama-2-13B-chat, 90.2% on Vicuna-13B-v1.5, and 91% on Cohere-Command-R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The SoC attack dominates all four baselines on both ASR and CPP. Against ReNeLLM (ASR 0.47, CPP 132 s), the SoC attack improves ASR by 48 percentage points while reducing CPP by 8.8×. Against AutoDAN (ASR 0.38, CPP 955 s), the improvement is 57 percentage points in ASR with a 63.7× reduction in cost. Against PAIR (ASR 0.35, CPP 146 s), the SoC attack gains 60 percentage points in ASR and is 9.7× faster. Against GCG (ASR 0.32, CPP 564 s), the gap is 63 percentage points in ASR with a 37.6× speedup. Notably, GCG is a white-box gradient-based method, making the SoC attack's black-box superiority particularly striking; the paper attributes the performance gap to the sequential context-switching strategy that gradually weakens safety boundaries rather than relying on a single optimized prompt.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical multi-armed bandit framework (Sutton & Barto; Bubeck et al.; Auer) for the LLM jailbreak setting, where each 'arm' is a category of Context-Switching Query (CSQ) and the reward is a binary harmfulness signal from a judge LLM. It departs from prior black-box jailbreak methods (PAIR, AutoDAN, ReNeLLM) that either iterate single prompts with an external LLM or optimize adversarial suffixes, by instead learning a sequential policy over multiple context-modifying turns via ε-greedy exploration. The theoretical analysis directly adapts Hoeffding-based finite-time regret bounds to the sequential, context-dependent reward structure, though the i.i.d. assumption underlying standard MAB theory is only loosely satisfied here due to the growing context window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 95% ASR (vs. 32–47% for PAIR, AutoDAN, ReNeLLM, GCG) and 15-second cost-per-prompt demonstrate that sequential, context-gradual attacks are far more effective and efficient than single-shot or iterative-refinement approaches, implying that current single-prompt safety filters are insufficient. A key open question is whether defenses must shift from static prompt classification to tracking cumulative context drift across multi-turn conversations. Limitations include the fixed CSQ template database (no generative variation), reliance on a same-family judge LLM (Llama-3.1-8B judging Llama targets), and theoretical bounds that assume near-i.i.d. rewards despite the inherently sequential context dependency.
