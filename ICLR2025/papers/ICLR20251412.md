## Problem

Pretrained Stable Diffusion T2I models require dozens of iterative denoising steps, and existing one-step distillation methods either depend on real training data or introduce adversarial/regression losses. Score Identity Distillation (SiD) demonstrated data-free one-step distillation for non-T2I EDM models, but adapting it to T2I latent diffusion models requires integrating classifier-free guidance (CFG) into the distillation loss and generalizing from EDM to DDPM noise scheduling—neither of which had been addressed.

## Input / Output

The distilled generator takes a CLIP-encoded text prompt and a random noise vector z ~ N(0,I) as input, producing a 512×512 image in a single forward pass through the SD U-Net in latent space. During training, 10% of text embeddings are replaced with empty-string embeddings to support CFG, and the Aesthetics6+ prompt set is used. Evaluation generates 30k images from COCO-2014 validation prompts, scored against the 40,504 reference images for FID and CLIP, with HPSv2 and Precision/Recall computed on additional category-specific prompts.

## Method

The core contribution is Long-Short Guidance (LSG), which applies CFG asymmetrically: the fake score network f_ψ is trained with an enhanced CFG scale (κ₁ > 1) but evaluated at the same or lower scale, while the pretrained teacher f_φ is evaluated with an enhanced CFG scale (κ₄ > 1), creating a 'long' teacher signal and a 'short' fake-score signal that incentivizes the student to produce more text-aligned images. Training alternates between updating f_ψ (standard denoising loss on synthetic images) and updating the generator G_θ (a model-based explicit score matching loss, a Fisher divergence comparing teacher and fake score predictions), entirely without real data. The method generalizes SiD's score identities from EDM (a_t = 1) to DDPM scheduling (a_t ∈ [0,1]) and employs a timestep-weighted loss ω(t) = σ_t⁴/(a_t²) · C/‖x_g − f_{φ,κ₄}‖₁,sg to balance contributions across the noise schedule. With κ = 1.5 and doubled training, the approach achieves a record data-free FID of 8.15 on COCO-2014 for one-step SD1.5 distillation.

## Results

SiD-LSG achieves a record-low data-free FID of 8.15 on the COCO-2014 validation set (30K images) when distilling SD1.5 with κ=1.5 and doubled training time, while maintaining a competitive CLIP score of 0.304. With the default κ=2 setting, it reaches FID 9.56 and CLIP 0.313 for SD1.5, and FID 10.97 and CLIP 0.318 for SD2.1-base, all in a single generation step (0.09s inference). These results surpass all prior one-step data-free distillation methods and approach the multi-step teacher SD1.5 (FID 8.78, 50 steps) while being ~29× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiD-LSG (κ=1.5, doubled training) achieves FID 8.15, beating the SD1.5 teacher (FID 8.78, 50 steps) and all prior one-step distillation methods: DMD (11.49), InstaFlow (13.10), UFOGen (12.78), ADD/SD-Turbo (16.25), and SwiftBrush (16.67). At κ=2, SiD-LSG reaches FID 9.56 with CLIP 0.313, outperforming DMD (CFG=3) by ~1.9 FID points and InstaFlow by ~3.5 FID points while matching or exceeding their CLIP scores. Against the multi-step baselines (DPM++ 4-step FID 22.44, LCM-LoRA 4-step FID 23.62), SiD-LSG is dramatically better in both FID and CLIP. The only methods with lower FID are Muse (7.88, 24 steps, 3B params) and Parti-3B (8.10, autoregressive), both of which require many more inference steps or substantially more parameters. On HPSv2 and Precision/Recall (Table 2), SiD-LSG outperforms all data-free baselines (BOOT, Guided Distillation, LCM, InstaFlow, SwiftBrush) across all four HPSv2 categories, with κ=4.5 achieving the highest HPSv2 scores (e.g., 27.39 Anime, 27.30 Photo) and κ=1.5 achieving the best Recall (0.52–0.53).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Score Identity Distillation (SiD) from Zhou et al. (2024), which was previously demonstrated only on non-T2I EDM-based diffusion models in pixel space, to the DDPM-scheduled latent diffusion setting of Stable Diffusion. The key methodological departure is the introduction of Long-Short Guidance (LSG), which applies Classifier-Free Guidance not only to the pretrained teacher score network (as in DMD and prior work) but also to the fake score network during both its training and evaluation—a strategy not explored in any prior distillation method. Unlike adversarial-based distillers (ADD, UFOGen) or regression-based approaches (DMD, Guided Distillation), SiD-LSG minimizes a model-based explicit score-matching (Fisher divergence) loss entirely on synthetic images, making it data-free in the same spirit as SwiftBrush but through a fundamentally different optimization mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-free one-step distillation achieving FID 8.15 on COCO-2014—below the 50-step teacher's own FID of 8.78—demonstrates that a single forward pass can match or exceed multi-step generation quality without access to real training data, which is critical for privacy-sensitive or legally restricted settings. The LSG scale provides a practical, interpretable knob for trading off FID (diversity) against CLIP (text alignment), giving practitioners a clear design choice. However, the distilled model still lags the teacher in CLIP score (0.304 vs. 0.322), and the authors note a performance plateau beyond ~10M synthetic images, suggesting the data-free constraint imposes a ceiling. Future work combining SiD-LSG with adversarial training and real data may close this gap and extend the approach to domain adaptation.
