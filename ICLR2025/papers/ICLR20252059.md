## Problem

Existing Hessian-based pruning methods (OBD, OBS) approximate the full Hessian matrix as diagonal or use the Fisher information matrix, discarding critical inter-parameter second-order interactions that capture how removing one parameter affects the loss sensitivity of another. This approximation limits pruning accuracy, especially for modern architectures like Transformers where matrix multiplication introduces parallel connectivity between layers. The paper asks whether the exact Hessian-vector product can be computed efficiently for each parameter without such approximations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252059/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fully trained neural network (CNN or Transformer) along with 200 batches of training data (batch size 64) to compute per-parameter importance scores via second-order Taylor expansion. The output is a set of importance scores per parameter (or per neuron group for structured pruning), which are ranked and used to iteratively prune the lowest-scoring parameters toward a target FLOPs or sparsity level. For unstructured pruning, the weight magnitude is added to the importance score to handle zero-gradient parameters; for structured pruning, scores are aggregated across upper and lower layer parameters within each dependency group.

## Method

OBA decomposes the full network Hessian into inter-layer submatrices and proves that non-zero submatrices arise only under two connectivity conditions: series connectivity (differentiable mapping between layer outputs and inputs) and parallel connectivity (two layers feeding into a matrix multiplication, as in attention). Theorem 4.2 and Theorem 4.4 provide closed-form expressions for the Hessian-vector product element under each condition, requiring only Jacobian-vector products rather than explicit Hessian construction. To compute the surrogate inputs \hat{X}^{(l)} efficiently, the paper introduces Jacobian-Vector Product Forward Propagation (JVPF), which replaces each layer's forward function with a JVP-augmented version so that all series-connectivity groups can be processed in a single forward pass. The resulting per-parameter importance score combines the first-order gradient term with the exact second-order Hessian-vector product, enabling both structured and unstructured pruning without any Hessian approximation.

## Results

On structured pruning of ResNet32 (CIFAR-10), OBA achieves 95.19% top-1 accuracy with 71.13% FLOPs reduction, outperforming all baselines including Taylor (95.06%), EigenDamage (95.17%), and C-OBS (95.04%). On ImageNet with ResNet-50, OBA achieves 75.62% accuracy with only a 0.53% drop from the unpruned baseline while delivering a 2× speedup. For unstructured pruning at 90% sparsity on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-20), OBA retains 86.27% accuracy (94.80% of original), substantially surpassing CHITA++ (79.32%, 86.82% ratio). On ViT-B/16 (ImageNet), OBA achieves 79.64% accuracy with a 1.43% drop, far exceeding Taylor (77.65%, 3.42% drop) and Weight (77.03%, 4.04% drop).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OBA consistently outperforms all Hessian-based structured pruning baselines. On ResNet-32 (CIFAR-100), OBA achieves 76.47% accuracy vs. Taylor (76.13%), EigenDamage (75.51%), C-OBS (75.16%), and Kron-OBS (75.98%), while also achieving the highest FLOPs reduction (70.02%). On VGG-19 (CIFAR-100), OBA reaches 72.36% vs. NN Slimming (71.89%) and C-OBS (72.27%), with the largest weight reduction (53.33%). For unstructured pruning, Taylor and OBD catastrophically fail at all sparsity levels (dropping to ~10% accuracy within the first step), while OBA maintains 86.27% accuracy at 90% sparsity on CIFAR-10, surpassing CHITA++ (79.32%) by nearly 7 points. On ImageNet (ResNet-50) at 90% sparsity, OBA retains 5.65% accuracy (7.43% ratio) versus CHITA-BSO++ at 0.43% (0.56% ratio), a dramatic margin. OBA also achieves the best Spearman rank correlation (0.443–0.500) with ground-truth neuron importance across all normalization schemes, indicating superior importance estimation robustness compared to Weight, Taylor, OBD, OBS, and EigenDamage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hessian-based pruning lineage initiated by OBD (LeCun et al., 1989) and OBS (Hassibi & Stork, 1992), which estimated parameter importance via second-order Taylor expansion but relied on diagonal or Lagrangian approximations of the Hessian. It also draws on Pearlmutter's (1994) efficient Hessian-vector product computation and Fang et al.'s (2023) dependency-graph framework for organizing structured pruning groups. The key departure is a theoretical decomposition of the full Hessian into inter-layer submatrices, identifying precisely when series and parallel connectivity (from matrix multiplication in attention) produce nonzero entries, thereby enabling exact Hessian-vector product computation without Fisher or Kronecker-factored approximations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing an approximation-free second-order importance criterion that is computationally tractable for both CNNs and Transformers, OBA offers practitioners a more principled pruning signal than first-order (Taylor, Weight) or approximate second-order (OBD, OBS, EigenDamage) alternatives, with acceptable overhead (~4.2 hours for full pruning). The clean separation of series and parallel connectivity conditions gives a reusable analytical framework for extending second-order pruning to new architectures. However, the authors note the method does not yet extend to RNNs or State Space Models, and the structured pruning gains over strong baselines like EigenDamage are modest, while the unstructured pruning advantage over CHITA at high sparsity is more pronounced.
