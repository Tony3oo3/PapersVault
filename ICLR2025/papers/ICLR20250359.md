## Problem

While discounted FTRL exhibits last-iterate convergence to Nash distribution (a QRE variant) in normal-form games, it is completely unknown whether it can approximate exact Nash equilibrium in multiplayer partially observable stochastic games (POSGs), which unify NFGs, EFGs, and Markov games. Existing last-iterate convergence results are largely restricted to two-player zero-sum settings, and the perturbation inherent in regularization prevents convergence to the true equilibrium. The paper asks whether a basic learning dynamic under general action value can be extended to POSGs while preserving convergence guarantees and ultimately reaching exact NE.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a POSG specification (state/observation/action spaces, transition and observation kernels, per-player reward functions, initial state distribution, discount factor, and horizon), a full-support base policy, and a regularization parameter ε > 0. It outputs a joint policy defined over all decision points of all players; in single-round mode the output is a QRE (generalized Nash distribution) with NashConv bounded by ε·n·log K·Σγ^t, while in multi-round mode the limit point is an exact NE. The per-iteration cost is O(|H|) via dynamic programming for advantage computation, making it comparable to CFR-family methods in computational budget.

## Method

DRDA operates as a continuous-time ODE at each decision point, where the score update is the advantage value minus the current score (discounted aggregation), and the policy is selected via a KL-divergence-regularized argmax against a base policy, yielding a closed-form softmax-like expression. The key architectural choice is using the history-averaged advantage value v^i(π)(x^i, a^i) as the driving signal, which generalizes the NFG utility w^i to POSGs with imperfect information and infinite horizons. Multi-round DRDA iteratively replaces the base policy with the rest point from the previous round, causing the regularization perturbation to vanish asymptotically and driving the limit to exact NE rather than QRE. Convergence of each single round to its rest point is proven under local λ-hypomonotonicity (a relaxation of global monotonicity) when ε > λ, with a linear rate via a Fenchel-coupling-based Lyapunov argument. The framework unifies NFGs, EFGs with perfect recall, and Markov games under a single POSG formulation, and the discrete-time implementation (SDRDA) achieves near-exponential convergence in NFGs and outperforms CFR+, MMD, and R-NaD in EFGs, MGs, and POSGs.

## Results

The paper evaluates DRDA using NashConv (Nash convergence) as the primary metric across NFGs, EFGs, MGs, and POSGs, with results presented as learning curves rather than tabular numbers. In NFGs, multi-round DRDA achieves near-exponential convergence to Nash equilibrium, with the rest-point policy sequence approaching NE across rounds. In 3-player and 4-player Kuhn poker (EFGs), 20-round DRDA clearly outperforms all baselines including CFR+, PCFR+, ECFR+, R-NaD, and MMD, which either oscillate or fail to converge in the multiplayer setting. In the infinite-horizon soccer game (MG), 5-round DRDA (100 total iterations) converges significantly faster than FP, PSRO, and ED. In Adversarial Tiger (POSG), 10-round DRDA with ε∈{0.05, 0.1, 0.15} and horizons H∈{2,3,4} consistently outperforms HSVI and SFLP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRDA wins against all 12 baselines in their respective game categories. In NFGs, vanilla FTRL's last iterates cycle (no convergence), OFTRL and ERM+ converge in 2-player games but fail in the 3-player setting, while MMD converges in all NFG scenarios but DRDA matches or exceeds its rate. In multiplayer EFGs (Kuhn poker), CFR+ shows oscillating last iterates, PCFR+ works in 3-player but oscillates in 4-player, ECFR+ and MMD fail entirely in multiplayer EFGs, and R-NaD is much slower with oscillation in 4-player — DRDA outperforms all of these. In the infinite-horizon soccer MG, DRDA converges significantly faster than FP, PSRO (both require preserving history policies), and ED (the only other last-iterate method). In typical POSGs (Tiger games), DRDA outperforms both HSVI and SFLP across all tested horizons and regularization parameters. The paper notes that MMD shares an intrinsic relationship with single-round DRDA under a different discretization, making it the closest conceptual competitor, but DRDA's multi-round mechanism achieves exact NE convergence where MMD's moving-magnet variant is slower.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRDA extends the discounted FTRL dynamic (Leslie & Collins, 2005) from normal-form games to the general POSG framework by replacing the NFG utility with a history-averaged advantage value and switching from entropic to KL-divergence regularization. The multi-round learning structure—iteratively replacing the base policy with the previous round's rest point—follows the technique introduced by Perolat et al. (2021) for regularized Nash dynamics in two-player zero-sum EFGs, but is here generalized to multiplayer POSGs with a weaker hypomonotonicity assumption in place of global monotonicity. The convergence analysis draws on Fenchel coupling (Mertikopoulos & Zhou, 2019) and the strong convexity of KL-divergence, while the generalized Nash distribution (GND) extends the Nash distribution concept from NFGs to the POSG setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a single, unified learning system with last-iterate convergence guarantees that covers NFGs, EFGs, MGs, and POSGs—settings that previously required separate algorithmic treatments (CFR for EFGs, FP/PSRO/ED for MGs, FTRL variants for NFGs). The hypomonotonicity condition is a meaningful relaxation of monotonicity, broadening the class of games where linear convergence is guaranteed. Key open questions remain: the paper does not establish a general convergence condition for the multi-round rest-point sequence (Theorem 4 assumes convergence), and the uniqueness-of-NE assumption, while relaxable under individual concavity, limits the generality of the exact-NE guarantee. For practitioners, the near-exponential convergence in NFGs and consistent outperformance of CFR+ variants in multiplayer EFGs make DRDA a practical alternative, though the per-iteration cost of dynamic programming for advantage computation may limit scalability to very large games.
