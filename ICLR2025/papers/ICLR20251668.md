## Problem

RLHF is the dominant framework for aligning LLMs with human preferences, yet its impact on specific trustworthiness dimensions—such as toxicity, stereotypical bias, truthfulness, and privacy—has never been systematically evaluated in controlled settings. This paper asks whether preference alignment on general-purpose datasets actually improves or degrades model trustworthiness, and finds that RLHF often produces reverse effects (e.g., 150% increase in bias, 25% decrease in truthfulness) while only improving machine ethics.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a pretrained language model (Pythia 1.4B/2.8B/6.9B or Llama-7B) and the Anthropic HH pairwise preference dataset (~100k prompt/chosen/rejected triples), then evaluates trustworthiness at three stages—pre-RLHF, post-SFT, and post-RLHF—using zero-shot generations with task-specific decoding configurations (e.g., temperature 0.5 with 5 samples for toxicity, temperature 0.01 with 1 sample for bias/ethics/truthfulness). The data attribution component outputs per-sample influence scores normalized to [-1, 1] and aggregated contribution scores per RLHF step per trustworthiness aspect, enabling identification of individual training samples most responsible for observed behavior changes.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic evaluation of two RLHF variants (reward-based PPO and reward-free DPO) across five trustworthiness benchmarks, isolating the effect of each training stage (SFT, PPO/DPO) on model behavior. The core technical novelty is adapting the DataInf influence-function framework to the RLHF pipeline by substituting the generic model loss in the influence score formula with stage-specific losses: autoregressive language modeling loss for SFT, Bradley-Terry preference loss for the PPO reward model, and the closed-form DPO policy loss for DPO. To make DataInf applicable to fully fine-tuned models, the authors convert weight deltas to LoRA adapters via SVD (rank 4), and exploit the observation that early layers contribute minimally to attribution scores to reduce computation. The resulting per-sample influence scores are aggregated into normalized contribution scores that quantify how much each RLHF step drives changes in each trustworthiness dimension, enabling practical applications like identifying and pruning detrimental training samples.

## Results

RLHF on the general-purpose Anthropic HH dataset produces a 31% average improvement on machine ethics (FNR drops from 56.8% to 38.3% after PPO and 40.3% after DPO), but has negligible net effect on toxicity. In contrast, stereotypical bias increases by 150% (agreement index rises from <0.4 to >0.8), truthfulness accuracy decreases by 25%, and privacy leakage increases by 12%, averaged across Pythia-1.4B/2.8B/6.9B and Llama-7B with both PPO and DPO. SFT is the dominant driver of the bias and truthfulness degradation, while PPO/DPO contribute most to the privacy leakage increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods; instead it performs a controlled stage-wise comparison (pre-RLHF → post-SFT → post-PPO/DPO) across four models (Pythia-1.4B, 2.8B, 6.9B, Llama-7B) and two RLHF variants (PPO with a GPT-j-6B reward model at 69% ranking accuracy, and DPO). PPO and DPO produce broadly similar trustworthiness trends, with SFT accounting for most of the bias and truthfulness degradation, while the RLHF-specific step (PPO or DPO) drives the privacy leakage increase. Machine ethics is the only aspect where both RLHF variants consistently improve over the SFT-only checkpoint, confirming that the Anthropic HH dataset's harmlessness signal aligns with ethical reasoning but not with the other four trustworthiness dimensions. The authors note that trends are consistent across benign and adversarial system prompts, and that the 1.4B Pythia model is the minimum size for reliable format adherence (91.8% on truthfulness vs. 100% for Llama-7B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the influence function framework of Koh & Liang (2017) and the efficient DataInf approximation of Kwon et al. (2023) to the RLHF setting, adapting the loss terms to SFT language modeling loss, Bradley-Terry reward model loss, and DPO policy loss respectively. It builds on the trustworthiness evaluation benchmarks established by Wang et al. (2024) and Sun et al. (2024), but departs from prior alignment-conflict observations (Perez et al., 2022; Anwar et al., 2024) by providing the first controlled, stage-wise, multi-dimensional evaluation of RLHF's causal impact rather than anecdotal or single-dimension reports. The data attribution component repurposes a technique originally designed for LoRA-tuned models to full-parameter RLHF pipelines via SVD-based LoRA conversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard RLHF on a general-purpose preference dataset degrades trustworthiness on 4 of 5 dimensions (with a 150% increase in stereotypical bias) challenges the field's implicit assumption that preference alignment and safety are aligned goals, and motivates more nuanced dataset curation and multi-objective alignment frameworks. The data attribution scores offer a practical tool for identifying and pruning harmful training samples, though the approach is validated only on models up to 7B parameters and a single dataset (Anthropic HH), leaving open whether the trends and attribution scores generalize to frontier-scale models. A key open question is how to construct preference datasets that simultaneously optimize helpfulness and multiple trustworthiness dimensions without the trade-offs observed here.
