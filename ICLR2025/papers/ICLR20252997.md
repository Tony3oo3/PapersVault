## Problem

Current VLMs rely on pre-trained ViT backbones (CLIP, SigLIP) trained with image-level supervision and minimal spatial inductive biases, causing them to fail at encoding local semantics—i.e., what class contents appear at each image region. This leads to persistent spatial reasoning errors in object localization, counting, and relational tasks. The paper asks whether local semantics can be recovered from existing pre-trained models without new annotated data or expensive retraining.

## Input / Output

For the locality alignment stage, the input is an RGB image split into n patches (e.g., 577 for CLIP ViT-L @ 336px, 729 for SigLIP SO400M @ 384px), with random subsets of patches masked (set to dataset mean) at both the input layer (teacher) and the embedding layer (encoder). The output is a sequence of n patch embeddings in R^{n×d} that collectively encode both local and global semantics. For the downstream VLM, these patch embeddings are projected into the LM embedding space via the MaskEmbed decoder (repurposed as the vision-language adapter) and concatenated with text token embeddings to produce free-form text responses.

## Method

MaskEmbed is a self-supervised post-training procedure that fine-tunes a pre-trained ViT to produce patch embeddings encoding localized semantics. An encoder (initialized from the pre-trained ViT) generates patch embeddings, which are masked at the embedding level; a small transformer decoder (trained from scratch) then reconstructs the frozen teacher's masked output—specifically the second-to-last layer's full embedding sequence—under the same mask. The loss is MSE between the decoder's reconstruction and the teacher's masked output, trained over random uniform masks (plus their complement and the null mask) on ImageNet-21k for 5 epochs, costing less than 1% of CLIP/SigLIP pre-training compute. The key design choice over simpler additive or crop-based reconstruction is the expressive decoder, which compresses richer per-patch semantics without leaking information from neighboring patches. For VLM training, the trained MaskEmbed decoder replaces the standard MLP adapter, resolving the embedding-space mismatch between the fine-tuned ViT and the text-aligned LM.

## Results

On the MSCOCO patch-level probing benchmark (macro-averaged recall), locality alignment improves local probing for all image-level-supervised models, with the largest gains for high-resolution backbones used in VLMs: CLIP ViT-L @ 336px improves from 46.05 to 52.71 (+6.66) and SigLIP SO400M @ 384px from 50.25 to 53.00 (+2.75). Global probing also improves in most cases (e.g., CLIP ViT-L @ 336px: 55.13 → 57.75). In VLM experiments using the Prismatic recipe with Llama-2 7B, locality-aligned backbones improve performance across spatial benchmarks (RefCOCO, OCID-Ref, TallyQA, VSR, AI2D) and maintain or improve holistic QA (VQAv2, VizWiz) and text comprehension (TextVQA), with the strongest gains observed for the SigLIP SO400M @ 384px backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIPSelf (Wu et al., 2024), MaskEmbed clearly wins: on CLIP ViT-B, CLIPSelf degrades local probing from the teacher's 44.63 to 36.16 and global from 52.61 to 42.48, while MaskEmbed improves both to 46.32 and 54.55 respectively. The paper attributes CLIPSelf's failure to its use of average-pooling as a reconstruction decoder rather than a learned transformer decoder. Against DINOv2 feature fusion (Karamcheti et al., 2024) as an alternative spatial-understanding strategy, both methods improve RefCOCO and TallyQA, but feature fusion can degrade VizWiz and TextVQA (likely because DINOv2's pre-training lacks text), whereas locality alignment does not introduce such regressions. The paper notes that a careful study of composing locality alignment with feature fusion is left to future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions locality alignment as a lightweight post-training stage for image-level-supervised ViTs (CLIP, SigLIP), drawing on the interpretability literature's insight that masked inputs reveal per-patch contributions to model outputs. It departs from CLIPSelf (Wu et al., 2024), which uses crop-based reconstruction with average pooling, by introducing a transformer decoder that reconstructs the teacher's full embedding sequence from masked patch embeddings, and from RegionCLIP and LocCa by requiring no synthetic annotations or auxiliary models. The VLM experiments build directly on the Prismatic training recipe (Karamcheti et al., 2024) and the LLaVA patches-as-tokens architecture (Liu et al., 2023c), with the key architectural twist of repurposing the MaskEmbed decoder as the vision-language adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even the strongest high-resolution backbones (CLIP ViT-L @ 336px, SigLIP SO400M @ 384px) gain substantial local probing and VLM spatial-reasoning performance from a <1%-compute post-training stage suggests that scale alone does not resolve the local-semantics gap, and offers a cheap, general fix for a widely-adopted VLM recipe. Key limitations include evaluation on a single VLM architecture (frozen-ViT, single-stage Prismatic) and a single LM (Llama-2 7B), leaving open how locality alignment composes with end-to-end fine-tuning, feature fusion, multi-crop strategies, and native-resolution ViTs. The paper also notes that MaskEmbed does not preserve zero-shot classification ability, a trade-off that may matter for some downstream uses.
