## Problem

Prior work on catastrophic forgetting in continual learning attributes the non-monotonic (Maslow's hammer) forgetting profile to neuron specialisation and feature reuse, but this explanation implicitly assumes specialisation always occurs. The paper challenges this assumption by showing that whether a network specialises depends critically on its weight initialisation, not merely on the activation function as previously believed, and that non-specialised networks exhibit fundamentally different (monotonic) forgetting profiles that invalidate standard regularisation strategies like EWC.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework consumes weight initialisation parameters—specifically the imbalance λ = h² − wwᵀ between hidden and output weights, the first-layer standard deviation σ_W, and polar-coordinate parameters (r, θ) for readout weights—and produces closed-form singular value trajectories, hitting/escaping times, and entropy-based specialisation metrics (H_h, H_Q, H_m). Empirically, the method takes Gaussian input vectors in the teacher-student setup, 3DShapes images for β-VAE disentanglement experiments, and MNIST digits for continual learning validation, outputting phase diagrams of specialisation regimes, forgetting-versus-task-similarity curves, DCI scores, and EWC generalisation error across regularisation strengths.

## Method

The paper derives closed-form dynamics for pathway singular values in deep linear networks (Eq. 3), showing that the conserved weight imbalance λ controls learning speed and that a phase transition from shared to specialised representations occurs when the faster pathway's imbalance sufficiently exceeds the slower pathway's. This is formalised via 'escaping time' (when a pathway begins learning) and 'hitting time' (when it converges): specialisation occurs when one pathway hits before the other escapes. The analysis is then extended to high-dimensional mean-field two-layer networks trained with SGD, where polar-coordinate initialisation of readout weights provides precise control over entropy and asymmetry, revealing that initialisation—not activation function—determines specialisation. In the continual learning setting, the authors show that non-specialised networks produce monotonic forgetting (breaking Maslow's hammer) and that EWC degenerates into an inflexible regulariser without specialisation, since all nodes carry equal Fisher importance and cannot be selectively protected.

## Results

The paper derives closed-form dynamics (Eq. 3) for deep linear networks showing that the conserved weight-imbalance parameter λ = h² − wwᵀ is the sole initialisation-dependent factor controlling learning speed, with larger |λ| accelerating the 'neural race' and promoting specialisation. On 3DShapes with a β-VAE, reducing the Xavier initialisation gain from 3 to 0.3 (increasing output-to-input weight imbalance) improves DCI disentanglement scores while keeping reconstruction loss essentially unchanged. In the continual-learning teacher-student setup, initialisation that suppresses specialisation converts the non-monotonic 'Maslow's hammer' forgetting profile into a monotonic one, and renders Elastic Weight Consolidation ineffective (either over-regularising at ξ=1 or under-regularising at ξ=10⁻⁶), whereas specialised initialisations allow EWC to achieve low error on both tasks at intermediate strengths (ξ=10⁻², 10⁻⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper without a standard method-vs-baseline comparison table. Its key empirical contrasts are: (1) Against Goldt et al. (2019), who attributed specialisation to the choice of activation function (sigmoidal specialises, ReLU does not), this paper shows that with appropriate weight-imbalance initialisation, ReLU networks specialise and sigmoidal networks do not, reversing the prior conclusion. (2) Against the monotonic-forgetting assumption of Goodfellow et al. (2013) and the Maslow's-hammer finding of Ramasesh et al. (2020) / Lee et al. (2022), the paper demonstrates that both profiles emerge from the same mechanism—neuron specialisation—and that initialisation alone can switch between them. (3) Against the standard application of EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017), the paper shows EWC's effectiveness is contingent on the network having specialised after the first task; without specialisation, EWC degenerates into a uniform regulariser that either blocks all learning or permits full catastrophic forgetting, with no intermediate sweet spot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the deep linear network dynamics framework (Saxe et al., 2013; 2019; 2022) and the λ-balanced weights concept (Kunin et al., 2024; Dominé et al., 2024) to derive closed-form expressions for how weight imbalance drives specialisation. It departs from Goldt et al. (2019), who attributed specialisation to the choice of activation function (sigmoid vs. ReLU), by demonstrating that initialisation—specifically weight imbalance and readout entropy—is the primary determinant, with activation function playing a secondary role. In the continual learning context, it extends the mean-field teacher-student analysis of Lee et al. (2021; 2022) by showing that the Maslow's hammer forgetting profile is a consequence of specialisation and can be disrupted by initialisation schemes that suppress it, thereby reconciling it with the monotonic forgetting profile of Goodfellow et al. (2013).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unifying theoretical account that reconciles two apparently contradictory empirical findings in continual learning (monotonic vs. inverted-U forgetting profiles) and reveals a hidden dependency of EWC on specialisation, which has practical implications for how regularisation-based continual learning methods are deployed. However, the analysis is confined to simplified settings (Gaussian inputs, two-layer networks, small hidden dimensions), and empirical validation is limited to 3DShapes and a coarse MNIST task, leaving open whether these initialisation effects persist in deeper architectures, convolutional networks, and realistic data distributions. A key open question is whether the identified initialisation strategies (output-heavy imbalance, asymmetric readout initialisation) can be translated into practical guidelines for continual learning systems without requiring task-specific tuning.
