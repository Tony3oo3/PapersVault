## Problem

Despite DPO's simplicity as a reward-model-free alternative to PPO for LLM alignment, it remains underutilized in state-of-the-art models and exhibits unexplained phenomena: both chosen and rejected response likelihoods decrease during training, performance is unstable, and results are highly sensitive to data distribution. This paper identifies the root cause as three structural properties (3D-properties) inherent to DPO's optimization dynamics, which had previously lacked a unified theoretical explanation.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard preference triplets (prompt x, chosen response a⁺, rejected response a⁻) and produces an aligned LLM policy π_θ. A critical input characteristic is the distributional relationship between the preference data and the policy model's own outputs: on-policy pairs (both responses sampled from π_θ) versus off-policy pairs (responses from external sources or dataset solutions) produce markedly different optimization trajectories. The output is evaluated on mathematical reasoning (MATH, SuperCLUE-Math) and structured instruction following (Chinese poem and slogan generation with rule-based scoring on format, rhythm, tone, and content).

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form gradient analysis of DPO's loss, showing that the ratio of rejected-to-chosen gradients equals −π⁺/π⁻, which causes the rejected response likelihood to collapse faster than the chosen one (Property 1), DPO to degenerate into pure suppression once π⁻→0 (Property 2), and probability mass to disperse onto unseen responses (Property 3). To mitigate these effects, they propose Flex-DPO, which decouples the temperature parameters into β⁺ and β⁻ to independently control the gradient magnitudes for chosen and rejected responses, and SFT-DPO, which adds a supervised cross-entropy term on the chosen response so that ∂ℓ/∂π⁺ remains nonzero even as π⁻→0. They validate the theory with a 4-prompt/10-response toy MLP model that amplifies the 3D-properties, then confirm on Baichuan2-13B and 33B across four on/off-policy data scenarios, showing that on-policy DPO (Scenario 1) consistently outperforms mixed or off-policy settings and that Flex-DPO surpasses vanilla DPO, IPO, and SLiC on mathematical reasoning.

## Results

On instruction-following tasks (Poem and Slogan generation with Baichuan2-33B), RLHF-PPO consistently outperforms DPO across all metrics: e.g., Rhythm 0.87 vs. 0.83, Tone Pattern 0.82 vs. 0.75, and Slogan Content 0.78 vs. 0.70. On mathematical reasoning (MATH*), on-policy DPO (Scenario 1) achieves 37.13% 5-point accuracy for Baichuan2-13B (vs. 32.24% base model) and 47.47% for Baichuan2-33B (vs. 44.49% base), while off-policy scenarios degrade performance (e.g., Scenario 4 drops to 26.83% for 13B). The proposed Flex-DPO regularization consistently outperforms vanilla DPO, IPO, and SLiC on both MATH* and SuperCLUE-Math.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RLHF-PPO (Schulman et al.), DPO underperforms on all Poem and Slogan metrics, with the largest gaps in Tone Pattern (0.75 vs. 0.82) and Slogan Content (0.70 vs. 0.78), confirming the paper's central claim that DPO's 3D-properties cause sub-optimality relative to RM-based alignment. Against vanilla DPO (Rafailov et al.), the proposed Flex-DPO (adaptive β⁻) achieves consistent improvements on MATH* and SuperCLUE-Math, with the optimal β⁻ around 0.08 for poem generation. Against IPO (Azar et al.) and SLiC (Zhao et al.), Flex-DPO also outperforms both on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (Table 9), though vanilla DPO surprisingly outperforms SLiC and IPO on instruction-following tasks like poem generation, suggesting that variants diverging from the Bradley-Terry model may lose generalization in preference learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) as a case study, extending its analysis to broader RM-free alignment methods including IPO, SLiC, and SimPO. It departs from concurrent theoretical work (Feng et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024a) by providing a unified gradient-based framework that simultaneously explains multiple previously unconnected empirical observations (likelihood drops, training instability, on-policy superiority) under a single '3D-properties' lens. The proposed Flex-DPO (adaptive β⁺/β⁻) and SFT-DPO regularization techniques are novel modifications to the DPO objective, while the comparison with RM-based PPO alignment is positioned as a diagnostic tool rather than a competing method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3D-properties framework gives practitioners a concrete theoretical diagnostic for why DPO training degrades, and the regularization techniques (adaptive β, SFT loss augmentation) are simple enough to adopt immediately. However, the analysis is validated primarily on Baichuan2-13B/33B models, and the optimal β⁻ value is task-dependent, leaving open how to set it in practice without extensive tuning. A key open question is whether these gradient-pathology findings generalize to much larger models or to iterative/on-policy DPO variants that partially mitigate the issue by construction. The paper also highlights a fundamental trade-off: over-suppressing the rejected-response gradient risks collapsing DPO into SFT-like behavior, a tension that remains unresolved.
