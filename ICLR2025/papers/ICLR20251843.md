## Problem

Knowledge distillation-based incremental object detection suffers from knowledge shift because previous task data is invisible, and existing prototype methods (feature centroids or single Gaussian distributions) cannot accurately model the complex, high-dimensional feature distributions inherent to detection tasks. This leads to inter-class feature overlap and severe catastrophic forgetting that is more pronounced than in incremental classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251843/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed through a standard detection pipeline (ResNet-50 backbone with RoI head) and outputs bounding boxes with class labels in a class-incremental setting where new classes are introduced sequentially without access to prior task data. The prototype module operates on features extracted from the linear layer of RoI, leveraging both recognition and location-based detection features (256-dim after LSPL scaling) rather than classification features alone.

## Method

The paper proposes GMDP, which represents each class prototype as a Gaussian Mixture Distribution with K components (typically 2), each having its own mean, covariance, and mixing weight, enabling flexible modeling of the non-Gaussian, multi-modal detection feature distributions. During incremental training, a transfer prototype knowledge loss (KL divergence between old and new model prototype likelihoods) constrains distributional consistency without requiring previous task data. A Length Scaling Progressive Learning (LSPL) strategy reduces feature dimensionality to 256 and defers GMDP activation for 1000 initial steps to aid convergence. A Dynamic Adaptive Prototype Optimization (DAPO) strategy further refines prototypes via three auxiliary losses: inter-class mean dispersal (maximizing separation between class means), intra-class component cohesion (minimizing distances between components within a class), and prototype variance minimization (compacting distributions). The final classification score is a weighted combination (α=0.5) of GMDP likelihood and the detector's own classification output.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2007 (mAP@0.5), GMDP-ABR achieves 74.6% overall mAP in the 19-1 setting, 73.2% in 15-5, 72.7% in 10-10, and 70.7% in 5-15, consistently outperforming the ABR baseline by 2.4–3.7 points. GMDP-ILOD improves over ILOD by up to 9.1 points in the 10-10 setting (70.8 vs. 61.7) and 2.9 points in 15-5 (71.8 vs. 68.9). On MS-COCO 2017, GMDP-ABR reaches 36.8 mAP@[50:95] in the 40-40 setting and 32.5 in 70-10, surpassing ABR by 2.3 and 1.4 points respectively, and exceeding the Joint Training upper bound of 35.9 in the 40-40 configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMDP-ABR outperforms the ABR baseline [38] across all four two-stage PASCAL VOC settings, with the largest gain of 3.7 mAP in the 15-5 configuration (73.2 vs. 71.0) and 2.4 mAP in 19-1 (74.6 vs. 70.9). GMDP-ILOD surpasses ILOD [57] by a substantial margin, most notably in the 10-10 setting where overall mAP jumps from 61.7 to 70.8 (+9.1) and in 5-15 from 43.2 to 61.7 (+18.5), demonstrating that GMDP is particularly effective when the number of new classes is large. Against other SOTA methods, GMDP-ABR achieves the best overall mAP in 19-1 (74.6 vs. CAT's 73.8 and Wu et al.'s 71.9) and 15-5 (73.2 vs. CAT's 72.2), while CAT [40] retains a slight edge in the 15-5 old-class column (76.5 vs. 75.8). On MS-COCO, GMDP-ABR exceeds ABR by 2.3 mAP@[50:95] in 40-40 and 1.4 in 70-10, and GMDP-CL-DETR outperforms CL-DETR [36] by 3.1 mAP in 70-10 (42.6 vs. 39.5) and 3.3 in 40-40 (40.8 vs. 37.5). The method also improves over ERD [13] by 1.5 mAP in 70-10 and 1.5 in 40-40 on MS-COCO, confirming consistent gains across both Faster R-CNN and Transformer-based detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends prototype-based continual learning from classification to incremental object detection, arguing that the more complex, multi-modal feature distributions in detection (evidenced by t-SNE visualizations and Shapiro-Wilk tests) render single-Gaussian or centroid prototypes inadequate. It builds directly on four IOD baselines—ILOD, ABR, ERD, and CL-DETR—by inserting a Gaussian Mixture Distribution-based Prototype (GMDP) as a plug-and-play knowledge transfer mechanism, complementing rather than replacing their existing distillation losses. The supporting strategies (LSPL for progressive convergence and DAPO for prototype plasticity) are novel but serve the core GMDP idea rather than constituting independent contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GMDP offers a practical, drop-in improvement for KD-based IOD pipelines without requiring storage of previous-task data, which is a meaningful constraint in real-world deployment. However, the method introduces ~7% inference overhead, requires a warm-up phase before GMDP training, and only 2 GMM components are stable—more components degrade performance due to convergence difficulties, limiting the expressiveness of the distributional model. The authors acknowledge they could not validate the approach on transformer-based detectors (DETR variants) due to time constraints, leaving open whether GMDP's benefits transfer to attention-based architectures. Broader open questions include whether the multi-modal detection feature hypothesis generalizes to video or 3D detection, and whether the LSPL dimensionality reduction (to 256) masks information that would matter at larger scale.
