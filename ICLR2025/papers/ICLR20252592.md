## Problem

Despite the empirical success of canonicalization for constructing invariant function classes, its generalization properties and sample complexity remained theoretically unexplored, particularly in comparison to the well-studied group averaging baseline. The paper asks: under what conditions does canonicalization outperform or underperform group averaging in terms of generalization error, and can this transition be precisely characterized?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252592/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical setting takes n i.i.d. samples from the unit sphere S^{d-1} with a finite subgroup G ⊂ O(d) acting on them, where the target function f* is G-invariant and belongs to the Sobolev space H^s(S^{d-1}). The base function class consists of polynomials of degree at most k, and the output is an L² generalization error bound decomposed into a variance term (proportional to the dimension of the invariant polynomial space divided by n) and a bias term (k^{-2β} for canonicalization, k^{-2s} for group averaging), together with a closed-form critical sample size N_critical marking the regime transition. Experiments additionally use point clouds of m points in R^d with permutation invariance.

## Method

The paper derives matching upper and lower (minimax-tight) bounds on the L² generalization error for ERM over canonicalized and group-averaged polynomial function classes, using spherical harmonics as the spectral basis and decomposing error into bias and variance via kernel regression in polynomial spaces. The key structural result (Theorem 1) shows that F_CAN^k ⊇ F_GA^k whenever the base space is a vector space closed under the group action, so canonicalization always has lower approximation error but higher variance (its function class dimension is |G| times larger). The paper introduces 'alignment' (Definition 2): a target function is β-aligned with canonicalization if its best approximation by canonicalized polynomials decays as k^{-β}, with β > s indicating alignment beyond what Sobolev smoothness alone guarantees. This yields a sharp phase transition at N_critical = σ²k^{2s+d-1} / ((d-1)!(1-|G|^{-1})(1-k^{2(s-β)})): below this threshold the variance term dominates and group averaging wins due to its smaller function class; above it the bias term dominates and canonicalization wins whenever β > s, with the gap growing unboundedly as alignment increases.

## Results

The paper establishes a phase transition in sample complexity between canonicalization and group averaging, characterized by a critical threshold N_critical (Corollary 7). In the point cloud experiment (m=d=5, permutation invariance), group averaging achieves lower test loss at n=10 (0.425 vs. 0.471), but canonicalization surpasses it at n=100 (0.290 vs. 0.367) and n=1000 (0.280 vs. 0.365). Theoretically, the generalization bound for canonicalization scales as Σ dim(Y_{d,ℓ})·σ²/n + k^{-2β}, while group averaging scales as Σ dim(Y_{d,ℓ}^G)·σ²/n + k^{-2s}, where β > s (alignment) drives canonicalization's advantage in the large-sample regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares canonicalization (Kaba et al., 2023) against group averaging (Murphy et al., 2019) as the primary baseline. In the point cloud experiment, group averaging wins at the smallest sample size (n=10: 0.425 vs. 0.471 test loss), consistent with its lower variance due to the smaller function class. However, canonicalization clearly outperforms group averaging at n=100 (0.290 vs. 0.367, a 21% relative reduction) and n=1000 (0.280 vs. 0.365, a 23% relative reduction), confirming the predicted phase transition. Theoretically, the paper shows that when the target function is β-aligned with the canonicalization scheme (β > s), the gap in generalization error grows unboundedly as alignment increases, while in the absence of alignment (β = s), group averaging is always at least as good. The alignment experiment (Section 5.1) further demonstrates that as the target function shifts from a smooth linear form (α→0, favoring group averaging) to a max/min form (α→1, favoring canonicalization), the performance gap reverses accordingly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical generalization analysis for canonicalization (Kaba et al., ICML 2023), positioning it against group averaging (Murphy et al., ICML 2019) as the primary baseline. While prior work has established generalization bounds for group averaging in kernel and polynomial settings (Tahmasebi & Jegelka, NeurIPS 2023; Bietti et al., NeurIPS 2021), canonicalization's sample complexity remained uncharacterized. The paper draws on the algorithmic alignment concept from GNN expressiveness theory (Xu et al., ICLR 2019b) to formalize when canonicalized models are well-suited to a target function, and uses spherical harmonics and Sobolev space analysis as its mathematical backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a sharp phase transition (N_critical) at which the preferred invariant-learning method switches from group averaging to canonicalization provides practitioners with a principled criterion for method selection based on sample budget and task alignment. The alignment concept (β > s) offers a theoretical explanation for why learned canonicalization can dramatically outperform group averaging in practice, connecting to empirical observations in GNNs and point cloud learning. Key limitations include the restriction to finite subgroups of O(d) acting on the sphere with polynomial base functions; extending the analysis to continuous groups, deeper architectures, and non-smooth canonicalization maps (which can introduce discontinuities) remains open.
