## Problem

Existing LLM safety refusal benchmarks suffer from three key deficiencies: they use coarse-grained and imbalanced taxonomies (e.g., self-harm is 3x underrepresented compared to fraud), they overlook linguistic and formatting variations in user prompts (language, dialect, encoding, persuasion techniques), and they rely on expensive GPT-4-scale models for automated evaluation without a systematic study of which design choices trade off efficiency against accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252159/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds 440 base unsafe instructions (10 per category, all in imperative style) plus 8.8K linguistically mutated variants into target LLMs, then pairs each (instruction, model response) with a fine-tuned Mistral-7b-instruct-v0.2 judge that outputs a binary fulfillment/refusal label. The final reported metric is a per-category and per-model fulfillment rate (0–1), where lower values indicate stronger safety refusal. The human judgment dataset used to train and validate the judge contains 7,040 annotations split into 2,640 training and 4,400 test records, covering both in-distribution and out-of-distribution (linguistically mutated) cases.

## Method

SORRY-Bench constructs a 44-class safety taxonomy across 4 high-level domains (Hate Speech Generation, Assistance with Crimes or Torts, Potentially Inappropriate Topics, Potentially Unqualified Advice) by aggregating and refining taxonomies from 10 prior datasets through a human-in-the-loop process with GPT-4 as a classifier. On top of this taxonomy, 440 class-balanced unsafe instructions are curated, then paraphrased through 20 linguistic mutations (writing styles, persuasion techniques, encoding/encryption, and multi-language translations) to yield 8.8K additional prompts. For automated evaluation, the paper conducts a meta-evaluation over 7K+ human annotations comparing GPT-4o, GPT-3.5-turbo, Llama-3-8B/70B, Mistral-7B, and Gemma-7B under zero-shot, chain-of-thought, few-shot, and fine-tuned configurations, finding that fine-tuned 7B models achieve 80–84% Cohen Kappa agreement with humans at ~10s per pass on a single A100, comparable to GPT-4o's 79–80% at ~260s. The fine-tuned Mistral-7b-instruct-v0.2 is adopted as the default judge for benchmarking 56 LLMs.

## Results

In the meta-evaluation of safety evaluators (Table 1), fine-tuned 7B LLMs achieve Cohen Kappa agreement with human annotations of 80.8–81.2%, matching or exceeding GPT-4o (78.9%) at a fraction of the cost (~10–14s per pass vs. ~260s). GPT-3.5-turbo + Fine-tuned achieves the highest agreement at 83.8%. All off-the-shelf safety baselines (Llama-Guard-2-8B, WildGuard, HarmBench Classifier, MD-Judge, Perspective API, Keyword Match) perform substantially worse, ranging from 1.1% to 60.6% agreement. On the main benchmark, 56 LLMs are evaluated: Claude-2 and Gemini-1.5 achieve the lowest fulfillment rates (<10%), while Mistral-series models fulfill over 50% of unsafe instructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The fine-tuned 7B LLMs (Llama-3-8b: 80.8%, Mistral-7b: 81.0%, Gemma-7b: 81.2%) substantially outperform all dedicated safety baselines: WildGuard (60.6%), HarmBench Classifier (52.5%), Llama-Guard-2-8B (39.0%), Keyword Match (37.3%), MD-Judge (36.0%), and Perspective API (1.1%). They also match or exceed GPT-4o (78.9%) while being 20–25× faster per evaluation pass. GPT-3.5-turbo + Fine-tuned achieves the best overall agreement (83.8%) but at higher cost (~112s) than the 7B fine-tuned models (~10–14s). Bert-Base-Cased + Fine-tuned (74.6%) is the fastest (~4s) but lags behind the 7B LLMs by ~6–7 points. Among the cross-benchmark comparisons (Table 4), SORRY-Bench scores span 5.23% (Claude-2.1) to 90.23% (Mistral-7b-instruct-v0.1), showing strong discriminative power consistent with trends observed on HarmBench, SALAD-Bench, ALERT, and StrongREJECT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established paradigm of LLM safety refusal evaluation (HarmBench, AdvBench, SALAD-Bench, ALERT, StrongREJECT, and others) but departs from it in three specific ways: it unifies the disparate, often coarse-grained taxonomies of prior benchmarks into a single 44-class fine-grained system via a human-in-the-loop procedure; it introduces 20 linguistic mutations to systematically decouple prompt formatting effects that prior datasets largely ignore; and it conducts the first large-scale meta-evaluation of LLM-as-a-judge design choices (CoT, few-shot, fine-tuning) on 7K+ human annotations. Rather than proposing a new evaluation methodology, it repurposes the existing LLM-as-a-judge framework with a systematic ablation to identify the most cost-effective configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SORRY-Bench addresses a real practical gap: prior safety benchmarks are imbalanced across categories (self-harm is 3x underrepresented vs. fraud) and lack linguistic diversity, making it difficult to diagnose *where* a model's safety fails. The finding that a fine-tuned 7B model (Mistral-7b) achieves 81% Cohen Kappa agreement with humans at ~11s per pass—comparable to GPT-4o at ~260s—makes large-scale safety auditing substantially more accessible. Key limitations include the binary (fulfillment/refusal) framing that cannot capture graded harmfulness, the exclusion of multi-category risk scenarios and active jailbreaking attacks, and the acknowledged risk of dataset contamination in future model training. The paper explicitly recommends pairing SORRY-Bench with over-refusal benchmarks (OR-Bench, XSTest) and capability benchmarks to avoid rewarding models that refuse everything.
