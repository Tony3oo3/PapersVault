## Problem

Existing neural combinatorial optimization methods rely on handcrafted rules—most commonly forcing diverse first construction actions (POMO)—to increase solution-space exploration, but these rules impair quality on problems where the initial move strongly constrains the solution (e.g., CVRPTW). Multi-decoder alternatives like Poppy and MDAM avoid handcrafted rules but incur large computational overhead by maintaining separate decoder networks per strategy. The paper asks whether a single decoder can learn a portfolio of complementary solution strategies without any explicit diversity enforcement.

## Input / Output

The model takes a graph-structured CO problem instance (node features, edge structure, and problem-specific attributes such as time windows) plus a short binary strategy vector v (e.g., 4 bits for K=16 strategies) as input. The bit vector is injected only into the decoder, so a single encoder pass produces embeddings from which K diverse solutions are generated in parallel via autoregressive decoding. At test time, M solutions are sampled using M (possibly repeated) bit vectors and the best is returned; optionally, EAS fine-tunes only the PolyNet residual layers for guided search.

## Method

PolyNet extends the POMO transformer architecture with a single residual block in the decoder that accepts a strategy bit vector v and produces an additive update to the masked multi-head attention output via a concatenation followed by two 256-dim linear layers with a ReLU activation. Training follows a winner-takes-all scheme: K solutions are sampled per instance using K distinct bit vectors, and only the best solution's gradient updates the shared weights, implicitly incentivizing each strategy to specialize on a subset of instances without any explicit diversity penalty. Crucially, no first action is forced, so exploration relies entirely on the learned conditional strategies rather than problem symmetries. At inference, the model generates diverse solutions in parallel (one decoder pass per bit vector, sharing the encoder), and can be combined with EAS that fine-tunes only the PolyNet layers to explore a wider solution neighborhood for a single instance.

## Results

On CVRP (n=100), PolyNet achieves a 0.49% gap to the HGS solver in 5 minutes, outperforming POMO (1.23%), Poppy (0.91%), and SGBS+EAS (0.20% but requiring 6h). On CVRPTW (n=100), PolyNet with EAS reaches a 0.96% gap to PyVRP in 5h, compared to 1.43% for SGBS+EAS (7h) and 2.73% for unguided PolyNet. On TSP (n=100), PolyNet matches the exact Concorde solver (0.000% gap) in 4 minutes versus 8 hours for LKH3. Across all four problems and all instance sizes, PolyNet consistently achieves the lowest gap among learning-based methods while being orders of magnitude faster than traditional OR solvers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolyNet outperforms POMO (Kwon et al., 2020) across all four problems and all instance sizes; on CVRP n=100 the gap drops from 1.23% to 0.49%, and on CVRPTW n=100 from 4.67% to 2.73%. Against Poppy (Grinsztajn et al., 2023), which uses multiple decoders, PolyNet's single-decoder design achieves lower gaps on every setting (e.g., 0.49% vs 0.91% on CVRP n=100) while avoiding the per-decoder computational overhead. Among guided methods, PolyNet+EAS beats SGBS+EAS on CVRP (0.14% vs 0.20% at n=100) and CVRPTW (0.96% vs 1.43% at n=100), and matches or exceeds DPDP and COMPASS on TSP and CVRP. On CVRPTW, PolyNet+EAS approaches the state-of-the-art PyVRP solver (0.96% gap vs 0%) in 5h versus PyVRP's 39h. On FFSP, PolyNet outperforms MatNet (Kwon et al., 2021) at all sizes (e.g., makespan 24.9 vs 25.4 for ×128 augmentation) and substantially beats CPLEX, genetic algorithms, and particle swarm optimization. The ablation (Table 5) further shows that PolyNet's free first-move selection outperforms forced first-move variants on all three routing problems, confirming that the learned diversity mechanism is superior to symmetry-based exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolyNet builds on the transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture for neural CO (Kool et al., 2019) and the POMO multiple-optima framework (Kwon et al., 2020), but departs from both in a key way: rather than enforcing diversity through forced first actions (POMO) or multiple decoders with KL/population-based regularization (MDAM, Poppy), it conditions a single decoder on learned bit vectors and uses a winner-takes-all training objective (borrowed from Poppy) to implicitly encourage K complementary strategies. The inference-time search leverages EAS (Hottung et al., 2022) but restricts fine-tuning to the new PolyNet layers only, making the search more targeted. For the FFSP experiments, the approach is adapted to the MatNet architecture (Kwon et al., 2021), demonstrating generality beyond the routing-problem transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that implicit diversity (via winner-takes-all conditioning) outperforms explicit symmetry-based exploration (forced first actions) is practically significant for problems like CVRPTW where the first move heavily constrains solution quality, and it removes a key assumption that limited prior neural CO methods to simpler routing variants. The single-decoder design also reduces computational overhead compared to multi-decoder approaches, making it more scalable in the number of learnable strategies. Open questions include whether the attention-based architecture can be extended beyond ~1000 nodes, whether the bit-vector conditioning generalizes to continuous strategy spaces, and whether the black-box nature of the learned strategies can be made interpretable for decision-support contexts.
