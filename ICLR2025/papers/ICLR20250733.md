## Problem

In RAG systems, when a model produces an incorrect answer, it is unclear whether the error stems from insufficient retrieved context or from the model's failure to use adequate context. Prior work on 'irrelevant' or 'noisy' context lacks a precise, answer-agnostic definition of what makes context sufficient, making it impossible to cleanly stratify model errors by information availability. This paper introduces a formal notion of sufficient context and uses it to reveal that even SOTA LLMs hallucinate with sufficient context while still answering correctly with insufficient context.

## Input / Output

The sufficient context autorater takes a (query, context) pair and outputs a binary label without requiring a ground truth answer; context is truncated at 6000 tokens for analysis, and the FLAMe-based variant processes context in 1600-token chunks. The selective generation module additionally consumes a model self-rated confidence score (P(True) via multi-sample voting for open-source models, or P(Correct) via a single probability-generating prompt for proprietary models) and outputs a continuous hallucination prediction score that is thresholded to produce an answer-or-abstain decision. The LLM-based QA evaluator (LLMEval) classifies each response as correct, abstain, or hallucinate using semantic matching rather than exact string matching.

## Method

The paper defines sufficient context as the existence of some plausible answer derivable from the context alone, distinguishing it from entailment (which presupposes a specific ground truth answer). An LLM-based autorater (Gemini 1.5 Pro with 1-shot prompting, achieving 93% accuracy on a 115-instance human-labeled set) provides scalable binary labels. For selective generation, the binary sufficient context label from FLAMe (a 24B fine-tuned autorater) is combined with the model's self-rated confidence in a logistic regression trained to predict hallucinations, with 100 iterations of random hyperparameter search. At inference, the regression score is thresholded to control the accuracy-coverage trade-off, and the paper shows this combined signal outperforms confidence-only abstention by 2–10% in selective accuracy for Gemini, GPT, and Gemma across HotPotQA, Musique, and FreshQA.

## Results

The proposed sufficient context autorater (Gemini 1.5 Pro, 1-shot) achieves 93.0% accuracy and 0.935 F1 on a 115-instance human-labeled dataset, outperforming FLAMe (87.8% acc, 0.892 F1), TRUE-NLI (82.6% acc, 0.818 F1), and Contains GT (80.9% acc, 0.810 F1). The selective generation method combining sufficient context labels with self-rated confidence improves the fraction of correct answers among responded queries by 2–10% for Gemini, GPT, and Gemma, with gains exceeding 10% for Gemma 27B on HotPotQA in the highest-accuracy region and over 5% for Gemini 1.5 Pro near 70% coverage. Stratified analysis reveals that SOTA models (Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT 4o, Claude 3.5) answer 35–62% of insufficient-context instances correctly, while hallucinating more than they abstain across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Gemini 1.5 Pro (1-shot) sufficient context autorater against three baselines: FLAMe (a fine-tuned PaLM 24B autorater), TRUE-NLI (a fine-tuned T5 11B entailment model), and a simple Contains GT string-matching method. Gemini 1.5 Pro (1-shot) wins on F1 (0.935 vs. 0.892 for FLAMe, 0.818 for TRUE-NLI, 0.810 for Contains GT) and accuracy (0.930 vs. 0.878, 0.826, 0.809 respectively). FLAMe is the closest competitor, achieving 0.892 F1 with a notably higher recall (0.935) but lower precision (0.853), making it a viable cheaper alternative for the selective generation pipeline. TRUE-NLI has the highest precision (0.938) but the lowest recall (0.726), consistent with the paper's observation that entailment is a stricter condition than sufficient context. For selective generation, the combined sufficient-context + confidence signal outperforms confidence alone by up to 10% selective accuracy on HotPotQA, with the gap most pronounced for smaller models like Gemma 27B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Attributable to Identified Sources (AIS) framework (Rashkin et al., 2023) to define sufficient context as a property of (query, context) pairs that is distinct from entailment: it asks whether *any* plausible answer exists given the context, rather than whether a specific ground truth answer is entailed. It departs from prior RAG robustness studies (Shi et al., 2023a; Yoran et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024) that evaluate retrieval quality using proxies like 'does not contain the answer' or NLI-based entailment scores, by introducing a formal, GT-free definition. The selective generation method combines confidence estimation techniques from Kadavath et al. (2022) and Li et al. (2024) with the sufficient context signal in a logistic regression thresholding framework inspired by Joren et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SOTA LLMs produce correct answers 35–62% of the time even with insufficient context (after filtering out closed-book-correct questions) implies that retrieval-quality improvements alone cannot solve open-book QA, and that intervention strategies must account for parametric knowledge and partial context utility. The observation that RAG paradoxically *reduces* abstention (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro drops from 100% to 18.6% abstention) challenges the assumption that providing context makes models more cautious. Open questions include whether a fine-grained (score-based) sufficient context autorater could enable iterative retrieval decisions, how the framework extends to multi-modal RAG, and whether the selective generation gains (2–10%) hold in production settings with noisier retrieval.
