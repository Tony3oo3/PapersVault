## Problem

Existing neural combinatorial optimization methods for Vehicle Routing Problems face two critical scalability barriers: conventional self-attention incurs O(n²) computational and memory complexity that becomes infeasible beyond a few thousand nodes, and standard training paradigms (supervised learning requiring near-optimal labels, or reinforcement learning with sparse rewards) fail to scale to large instances. This paper addresses both obstacles simultaneously to enable NCO methods to solve VRPs with up to 100K nodes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250534/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a VRP instance as a graph where each node is represented by a feature vector (2D coordinates for TSP; coordinates plus normalized demand for CVRP, with vehicle capacity normalized to 1). The model constructs solutions autoregressively, selecting one unvisited node per decoding step, and outputs either a single Hamiltonian tour (TSP) or a sequence of sub-tours each beginning and ending at the depot with capacity constraints (CVRP). During inference, the Parallel local ReConstruction (PRC) procedure samples non-overlapping partial solutions of random length between 4 and 1000 nodes, reconstructs them in parallel, and merges the better segments back into the complete solution.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is a lightweight cross-attention mechanism that replaces self-attention: at each decoding step, two representative nodes (the first and last visited nodes) first attend to all graph node embeddings to update themselves, then all graph nodes attend back to the representative nodes, reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(n) while preserving effective inter-node information flow. The Transformer network consists of a single linear embedding layer and L=6 stacked cross-attention modules in the decoder, with the remaining vehicle capacity fused into the representative node embeddings for CVRP. The Self-Improved Training (SIT) algorithm eliminates the need for external labels by iteratively performing parallel local reconstruction (PRC) to improve solutions in a maintained dataset, then training the model in a supervised manner on the resulting pseudo-labels (partial solutions of length 4–1000), alternating between reconstruction and training until a time budget is exhausted.

## Results

On synthetic TSP instances up to 100K nodes, the proposed method with PRC1,000 achieves optimality gaps of 0.38% (TSP1K), 1.36% (TSP5K), 1.81% (TSP10K), 2.51% (TSP50K), and 2.45% (TSP100K) relative to LKH3. Remarkably, on CVRP instances, the method with PRC1,000 *beats* the state-of-the-art classical solver HGS on CVRP10K (gap −0.66%), CVRP50K (gap −1.83%), and CVRP100K (gap −2.55%), marking the first time a learning-based NCO method surpasses a specialized solver on large-scale VRPs. Most competing NCO methods (POMO, BQ, LEHD, SIGD, ELG) encounter out-of-memory failures at 50K or 100K nodes, while the proposed method handles all scales within a single 24GB GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classical solvers, the method achieves near-optimal TSP gaps (0.38%–2.55% vs. LKH3) and, notably, *outperforms* HGS on CVRP10K/50K/100K by 0.66%, 1.83%, and 2.55% respectively—the first learning-based method to beat a specialized solver at these scales. Among NCO baselines, the method dominates: POMO, BQ, LEHD, SIGD, and ELG all hit OOM at 10K–100K nodes, while the proposed method scales to 100K nodes on a single 24GB GPU. INViT-3V is the only NCO baseline that completes all TSP scales but trails by 4–5% gap (e.g., 7.20% vs. 2.45% at TSP100K) and is far worse on CVRP (44.67% vs. −2.55% at CVRP100K). Decomposition-based GLOP completes all TSP scales but with 2–3× larger gaps (5.14% vs. 2.45% at TSP100K) and OOMs on CVRP50K/100K. On real-world TSPLIB/CVRPLIB (Table 2), the method with PRC1,000 achieves the smallest average gaps (2.56% TSP, 9.57% CVRP) and is the only method solving all 33 TSP and 14 CVRP instances up to 85,900 nodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the heavy-decoder Transformer paradigm established by LEHD (Luo et al., 2023) and BQ (Drakulic et al., 2023), which use the first and last visited nodes as context for autoregressive solution construction. It departs from these works in two fundamental ways: (1) replacing the O(n²) self-attention with a lightweight cross-attention that routes information through a fixed number of representative nodes to achieve O(n) complexity, and (2) introducing Self-Improved Training (SIT), which eliminates the need for labeled data or RL rewards by iteratively using the model's own local reconstruction to generate pseudo-labels. The paper positions itself against generalization-based NCO methods (which fail beyond ~10K nodes), decomposition-based approaches (which require expert-designed subproblem solvers), and heatmap-based methods (which are TSP-specific), arguing that a single end-to-end construction model with linear complexity and self-supervised training can handle all scales uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most impactful finding is that a learning-based method (with PRC500+) outperforms the specialized HGS solver on CVRP instances of 10K, 50K, and 100K nodes — a first for the NCO field on large-scale VRPs. The linear-complexity cross-attention and SIT algorithm are practical, generalizable techniques: the paper demonstrates SIT can be plugged into POMO and LEHD to yield 36–70% gap improvements, and the cross-attention can be swapped into BQ for 30×+ speedups. Open questions include whether the fixed partial-solution length (l_max=1000) can be made adaptive, whether the approach extends to other COP families (scheduling, bin packing, knapsack), and whether the random-insertion initialization for PRC can be further improved. The method's reliance on a single GPU (RTX 3090) for both training and inference at 100K scale makes it immediately deployable, but the 2.5-hour inference time for PRC1000 on CVRP100K still limits real-time applicability.
