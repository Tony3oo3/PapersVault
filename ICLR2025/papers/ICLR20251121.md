## Problem

Fine-tuning safety-aligned LLMs on task-specific data—even benign data—can significantly compromise the model's pre-existing safety alignment, yet standard data selection methods (e.g., DSIR) use single-level scoring that does not directly optimize for the post-selection safety loss. This paper addresses the gap by formulating data selection as a bilevel optimization problem where the selector is explicitly trained so that the model fine-tuned on selected samples minimizes the safety alignment loss.

## Input / Output

SEAL takes three inputs: a fine-tuning dataset D of N instruction-response pairs (potentially containing harmful samples), a safe alignment dataset D_safe of M safe responses, and a safety-aligned LLM as the starting point. The method produces a softmax-weighted data selector σ(ω) that ranks all N fine-tuning samples, from which the top p% are retained as D_top for final fine-tuning. Notably, the selector can be trained with a smaller proxy model (e.g., Phi-3-Mini-3.8B) and transferred to fine-tune a larger model (e.g., Merlinite-7B), and the selected dataset is reusable across subsequent fine-tuning runs on the same source data.

## Method

SEAL formulates data selection as a bilevel optimization: the upper level minimizes the safety loss on D_safe, while the lower level minimizes a σ(ω)-weighted fine-tuning loss on D, with σ(ω) being a softmax over per-sample logit parameters. To solve this efficiently, the paper applies a penalty-based reformulation (following Shen & Chen, 2023) that converts the bilevel problem into a single-level one, then derives a first-order gradient algorithm (Algorithm 1) where the selector update uses a 'loss gap'—the difference between the loss of a model fitting both safe and weighted data versus an auxiliary model fitting only weighted data—to determine which samples conflict with safety and should be down-ranked. A memory-efficient variant (Algorithm 2) drops the auxiliary model entirely when the parameter dimension far exceeds the dataset size, approximating the min-term gradient as zero and updating the selector with only the current model's loss. The full pipeline is: (1) obtain a safety-aligned LLM, (2) train the data selector via the bilevel algorithm, (3) select the top-p% ranked samples, and (4) fine-tune on the selected subset.

## Results

On LLAMA-3-8B-INSTRUCT, SEAL achieves 60.22% win rate on ANTHROPIC HH, 53.88% on SLIMORCA, and 69.29% on HEX-PHI (all evaluated by GPT-4 against standard SFT reference), representing an 8.5% average win rate increase over random selection. On MERLINITE-7B, SEAL reaches 76.72% on ANTHROPIC HH and 61.5% on HEX-PHI, a 9.7% average gain over random selection. Combining SEAL with SafeInstr (adding 3% safety instruction data) further boosts HEX-PHI win rate to 77.28% on LLAMA-3-8B-INSTRUCT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEAL consistently outperforms DSIR (importance resampling) across all three test datasets on LLAMA-3-8B-INSTRUCT, with an average gain of ~6% (e.g., 60.22 vs. 57.57 on ANTHROPIC HH, 69.29 vs. 53.95 on HEX-PHI). Against SafeInstr, SEAL wins on ANTHROPIC HH (60.22 vs. 57.97) and HEX-PHI (69.29 vs. 64.49) without requiring any extra safety instruction data during fine-tuning, while SafeInstr edges ahead on SLIMORCA (54.22 vs. 53.88). On MERLINITE-7B, SEAL's advantage over DSIR is even larger (~7% average), and SEAL achieves 76.72% on ANTHROPIC HH versus SafeInstr's 64.38%. The paper notes that SEAL's gains are more pronounced on larger models (LLAMA-3-8B, MERLINITE-7B) than on the smaller PYTHIA-2.8B, suggesting the data selector quality benefits from model capacity. When combined with SafeInstr, SEAL achieves the best overall safety performance (77.28% on HEX-PHI), indicating the two approaches are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEAL builds on the penalty-based bilevel optimization framework of Shen & Chen (2023) and Liu et al. (2022), adapting it to the LLM data selection setting where prior bilevel data reweighting work (Grangier et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024) relied on memory-inefficient second-order methods. It departs from single-level data selection approaches like DSIR (Xie et al., 2023) and LESS (Xia et al., 2024), which score samples by similarity to a target dataset without explicitly optimizing the post-selection safety loss. The key conceptual departure is formulating safe fine-tuning as a bilevel problem where the upper level minimizes safety loss and the lower level performs weighted fine-tuning, solved via a first-order penalty method that avoids second-order derivatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Safe fine-tuning is a critical practical bottleneck: standard SFT on even benign data can break safety alignment, yet fine-tuning is essential for downstream deployment. SEAL's data-centric approach is attractive because it requires no modification to the fine-tuning procedure itself and the selector is a one-time cost reusable across models. The transferability finding—that a 3.8B model can train a selector effective for a 7B model—significantly reduces the computational barrier. Open questions include how the method scales to very large fine-tuning corpora (the selector parameter ω has dimension N), whether the penalty-based approximation remains tight at larger scales, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-turn or multi-modal safety alignment.
