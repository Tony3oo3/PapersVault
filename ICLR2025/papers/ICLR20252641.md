## Problem

Current 3D-based LLMs lack situated understanding — the ability to reason about a 3D scene from a dynamic egocentric perspective where the agent's position and orientation change the correct answer. This stems from two gaps: existing 3D datasets are built from a global (bird's-eye) perspective without agent-relative context, and existing architectures simply concatenate modality tokens without explicitly aligning 3D spatial representations to natural language descriptions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a triple <C, S, Q>: a 3D scene represented as per-object point clouds (up to 60 objects, 1024 points each, encoded by PointNet++), a situation S comprising a textual description plus standing-point coordinates (x,y,z) and orientation quaternion (qx,qy,qz,w), and a question. During training the standing point and orientation are used to rotate and translate the scene; at test time only the textual situation and question are provided. Outputs are free-form text: situated captions (≤300 words describing the 360° panorama), QA answers, or navigation actions (turn left/right, move forward, stop). An optional 2D ego-centric image can be added for navigation tasks.

## Method

The paper contributes two components. First, Spartun3D (~133k examples) is generated by an automated GPT-4o pipeline that constructs a situated scene graph encoding rotation angles, four-directional categories, Euclidean distances, and passby objects relative to the agent's standing point and orientation, then prompts the LLM to produce situated captions and three types of QA (attribute/relation, affordance, 2-hop planning). Second, Spartun3D-LLM builds on LEO (PointNet++ + LLM backbone) and adds a Situated Spatial Alignment Module: for each object it generates a situated textual description (name, attributes, nearby objects in front/right/back/left), encodes it with a frozen CLIP text encoder, and aligns it to a spatially-aware object representation produced by a self-attention layer augmented with a pairwise spatial feature matrix (distance, sin/cos of horizontal and vertical angles between object pairs). The alignment uses an MSE loss between the spatially-aware object embedding and the CLIP text embedding, jointly optimized with the standard autoregressive language modeling loss via LoRA fine-tuning.

## Results

On SQA3D (ground-truth objects), Spartun3D-LLM* achieves 55.0% exact match in the fine-tune setting, outperforming LEO (52.5%), 3D-LLM (50.2%), and 3D-Vista (48.5%). In the zero-shot setting, Spartun3D-LLM reaches 37.1% EM versus only 13.9% for LEO, demonstrating strong generalization from the Spartun3D dataset. On the navigation task (MP3D ObjNav), Spartun3D-LLM achieves 20.3% zero-shot action accuracy while LEO scores 0%. On the Spartun3D benchmark itself, Spartun3D-LLM* (Vicuna7B) attains 56.9% EM on Attributes & Relations, 69.7% on Affordance, and 88.7% on Situated Planning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spartun3D-LLM consistently outperforms all baselines across both zero-shot and fine-tune settings on SQA3D. In the fine-tune setting, it beats LEO by 2.5 EM points (55.0% vs. 52.5%), 3D-LLM by 4.8 points (55.0% vs. 50.2%), and 3D-Vista by 6.5 points (55.0% vs. 48.5%). The zero-shot gap is even more dramatic: Spartun3D-LLM achieves 37.1% EM versus LEO's 13.9%, a 23-point improvement attributable to training on the Spartun3D dataset. The explicit spatial alignment module provides an additional ~1–2 EM gain over LEO+Spartun3D in both settings (e.g., 55.0% vs. 54.2% fine-tune; 37.1% vs. 35.9% zero-shot). On navigation, Spartun3D-LLM achieves 20.3% zero-shot accuracy while LEO produces no valid actions (0%), highlighting the transferability of situated spatial understanding to embodied tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LEO (Huang et al., 2023), the state-of-the-art 3D-based LLM that injects point cloud representations into an LLM via token concatenation, and on spatial self-attention mechanisms from Chen et al. (2022) for encoding pairwise object relations. It departs from prior 3D-based LLMs (3D-LLM, 3D-Vista, LEO) in two key ways: (1) it introduces a situated (egocentric) perspective to scene understanding, extending the global/bird's-eye-view scene graphs used by prior work into agent-relative spatial descriptions, and (2) it replaces simple token concatenation with an explicit CLIP-based 3D object-text alignment loss. The dataset construction pipeline, which uses GPT-4o to generate situated QA and captions from structured scene graphs, contrasts with the human-annotated SQA3D dataset and the global-perspective datasets (ScanQA, Scan2Cap) used to train prior 3D LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 133k-example Spartun3D dataset addresses a critical scalability bottleneck in situated 3D understanding, where the only prior dataset (SQA3D) was small and human-annotated, making it impractical for training large 3D-based LLMs. The finding that LEO defaults to 'left' 97% of the time on direction questions reveals that token-concatenation architectures do not genuinely encode spatial orientation, motivating explicit alignment mechanisms. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4o for data generation (introducing potential error propagation), only 2-hop planning depth, and evaluation on a small navigation subset (~1000 examples). Open questions include whether the alignment module generalizes to dynamic or outdoor environments, whether deeper multi-hop planning is feasible, and whether the approach transfers to real-time embodied agents with latency constraints.
