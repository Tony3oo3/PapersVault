## Problem

Learning a probability distribution over the finite symmetric group S_n is intractable due to its factorial size (n! states) and discrete nature, which prevents standard gradient-based learning and makes existing discrete diffusion models inapplicable since they require explicit n!×n! transition matrices or assume small/decomposable state spaces. No prior method could scale to large n while maintaining expressiveness over the full permutation space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of n objects (image patches of varying sizes up to 6×6, 4-digit MNIST images up to length 200, or 2D points for TSP up to 50 nodes) presented in a shuffled order as an n×d matrix, and outputs a permutation in S_n that recovers the correct ordering. Unlike sequence-based discrete diffusion models that require a fixed finite alphabet, here the 'vocabulary' is the entire permutation group S_n, and the objects themselves are continuous (pixel values or coordinates) but the diffusion trajectory is confined to the discrete permutation space, avoiding the O(n^n) sequence space of prior approaches.

## Method

SymmetricDiffusers decomposes learning a distribution over S_n into a sequence of simpler transition-learning subproblems via a discrete diffusion process. The forward process uses the GSR riffle shuffle (identified as optimal due to its fast mixing time with cutoff at ~3/2 log₂ n), and the reverse process is parameterized by a novel Generalized Plackett-Luce (GPL) distribution using n² scores (vs. n for standard PL), which is provably expressive enough to model any distribution over S_n. A Transformer with masked self-attention over 2n tokens (n object embeddings + n dummy position tokens) predicts the GPL score matrix, and training maximizes a variational lower bound via Monte Carlo estimation since analytical posteriors are unavailable. A theoretically grounded denoising schedule merges reverse steps based on exact TV distances between riffle-shuffle marginals (computed via Eulerian numbers in O(n) time), and hierarchical beam search handles decoding at test time.

## Results

On the 4-digit MNIST sorting benchmark, SymmetricDiffusers achieves Kendall-Tau of 0.786 at n=52, 0.641 at n=100, and 0.453 at n=200, substantially outperforming Error-free DiffSort (0.170, 0.140, 0.002) and DiffSort (0.341, 0.166, 0.107) on longer sequences. On the Jigsaw Puzzle task, it reaches 0.8126 Kendall-Tau on 3×3 Noisy MNIST (vs. 0.6908 for Gumbel-Sinkhorn) and 0.4859 on 4×4 (vs. 0.3578). On TSP-20, it achieves a 0.18% optimality gap, comparable to DIFUSCO (1.07%) and close to OR solvers (0.00%), while on TSP-50 it achieves 0.41% gap versus DIFUSCO's 0.10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymmetricDiffusers clearly dominates DiffSort and Error-free DiffSort on long sequences: at n=52, it achieves 0.786 Kendall-Tau versus 0.341 (DiffSort) and 0.170 (Error-free DiffSort), and at n=200 it reaches 0.453 versus 0.107 and 0.002 respectively. On shorter sequences (n≤32), SymmetricDiffusers is roughly on par with Error-free DiffSort (e.g., 0.959 vs. 0.962 Kendall-Tau at n=7). On the Jigsaw Puzzle task, SymmetricDiffusers outperforms Gumbel-Sinkhorn on all puzzle sizes ≥3×3 (e.g., 0.8126 vs. 0.6908 Kendall-Tau on 3×3 Noisy MNIST; 0.4859 vs. 0.3578 on 4×4), while Gumbel-Sinkhorn only wins on the trivial 2×2 case. On TSP-20, SymmetricDiffusers (0.18% gap) beats DIFUSCO (1.07%) and GCN (3.10%), approaching OR solvers (0.00%); however, on TSP-50, DIFUSCO (0.10%) slightly outperforms SymmetricDiffusers (0.41%), though both are far ahead of GCN (3.10%). In the synthetic permutation experiment (Appendix G.1), SymmetricDiffusers achieves 100% accuracy on learning a single permutation at n=100 and n=200, while SEDD degrades to 88.75% at n=200.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of research: the classical theory of random walks on finite groups (particularly the GSR riffle shuffle model of Bayer & Diaconis, 1992) and modern discrete diffusion models. It departs from existing discrete diffusion frameworks (Austin et al., 2023; Vignac et al., 2023) that require explicit transition matrices or decomposable state spaces, by instead using shuffling operations as the forward process over the intractable S_n state space. The reverse process generalizes the Plackett-Luce distribution (Plackett, 1975) to a provably universal GPL distribution, and the denoising schedule is grounded in exact total-variation-distance computations via Eulerian numbers rather than heuristic noise schedules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion model that operates directly on the full symmetric group S_n, opening a principled framework for learning arbitrary distributions over permutations without the factorial-size transition matrices that block prior discrete diffusion approaches. The provable universality of GPL (Theorem 2) and the exact TV-distance formulas for riffle shuffles provide theoretical grounding that is rare in applied diffusion work. Key limitations include degradation at very large n (accuracy drops to 0.1% at n=200 for sorting), and the method is currently restricted to symmetric groups rather than general finite groups or Lie groups. Open questions include whether the framework can incorporate concrete scores or score-entropy objectives, and whether it scales to n > 200 with architectural or algorithmic changes.
