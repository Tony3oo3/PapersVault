## Problem

Existing shape restoration methods are trained and evaluated on specific defect types (e.g., noise-free completion or noisy refinement) in isolation, limiting their applicability to real-world scenarios where multiple artifacts co-occur. Training on narrow subsets of defective shapes also prevents knowledge transfer across restoration subtasks, hindering generalization to in-the-wild inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250034/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a defective 3D shape as a 256³ sparse TSDF grid (upsampled from lower resolutions such as 32³ or 64³) along with an optional text prompt, and produces a restored intact shape as a 256³ TSDF grid. Defective inputs may exhibit arbitrary combinations of incompleteness, sensor noise, and low-resolution artifacts, and the model handles objects under arbitrary poses. Multiple diverse restoration samples (k=10) can be generated per input to capture the multi-modal nature of the restoration distribution.

## Method

The framework comprises three modules: a cascaded hierarchical VAE (H-VAE) that jointly learns multi-level latent representations of intact shapes, a noise-robust defective-shape encoder fine-tuned via multi-level feature alignment to the frozen H-VAE encoder, and a hierarchical latent diffusion model (H-LDM) with sparse U-Net denoisers that generates intact shapes coarse-to-fine conditioned on the aligned defective encodings. The key training insight is a two-stage strategy: the H-VAE is first trained on intact shapes, then the defective-shape encoder is fine-tuned to align its multi-level outputs to the intact encodings (L1 loss on shared sparse voxels), which simultaneously pre-compresses defective shapes and enforces noise robustness. This pre-compression eliminates the need to load high-resolution defective shapes during LDM training, yielding a ~20× speedup. Patch-wise encoding with sparse convolution (TorchSparse) is used throughout to handle both complete and partial/noisy inputs, and the H-LDM uses v-parameterization continuous-time diffusion with DDIM sampling at inference.

## Results

On the 3DQD noise-free completion benchmark, UniRestore3D achieves the best MMD of 2.268 on the Half subset (vs. NeuSDFusion's 2.290) and the best TMD of 5.330, while on the Octant subset it achieves TMD 9.080 (best) but trails NeuSDFusion on MMD (3.840 vs. 3.030). On the PatchComplete noisy completion benchmark, UniRestore3D achieves the best CD/IoU of 3.90/70.6 on ShapeNet novel categories and 6.75/53.3 on ScanNet novel categories, outperforming the strongest baseline SC-Diff (4.08/68.3 and 7.04/51.9 respectively). The model operates at 256³ TSDF resolution, significantly higher than the 64³ (3DQD) or 32³ (PatchComplete) resolutions used by baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the 3DQD benchmark, UniRestore3D achieves the best MMD on the Half subset (2.268), narrowly beating NeuSDFusion (2.290) and substantially outperforming 3DQD (2.933), AutoSDF (3.510), and point-cloud-based methods PoinTr (5.316) and SeedFormer (4.972). However, NeuSDFusion retains an edge on AMD for both Half (5.900 vs. 6.143) and Octant (9.590 vs. 9.930) subsets, and also leads on Octant MMD (3.030 vs. 3.840), reflecting the known quality–diversity tradeoff of conditional generative models. On the PatchComplete noisy completion benchmark, UniRestore3D clearly wins on both CD and IoU across all novel categories: on ShapeNet it achieves 3.90 CD / 70.6 IoU versus SC-Diff (4.08/68.3), DiffComplete (4.10/67.5), PatchComplete (4.27/65.4), AutoSDF (5.86/45.2), and 3D-EPN (5.58/59.4); on ScanNet it achieves 6.75/53.3 versus SC-Diff (7.04/51.9). Notably, UniRestore3D does not exploit the observability information in the input TSDF grid (unlike PatchComplete and AutoSDF), making its task strictly harder while still yielding superior results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the sparse voxel hierarchy framework of XCube (Ren et al., 2024) for hierarchical 3D generation and the cascaded VAE design of VQ-VAE-2/NVAE for multi-level latent representations, combining them with a latent diffusion model (Rombach et al., 2022) to form a conditional hierarchical latent diffusion model (H-LDM). It departs from prior shape restoration work (AutoSDF, 3DQD, PatchComplete, DiffComplete, SC-Diff), each of which targets a single subtask (noise-free completion, noisy completion, or denoising), by unifying all subtasks under a single 'general shape restoration' formulation. The key architectural departure is the noise-robust encoder trained via multi-level feature alignment to intact-shape encodings, which simultaneously pre-compresses defective inputs and enforces denoising, enabling a ~20× training speedup for the downstream diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying completion, super-resolution, denoising, and refinement into one model, this work removes the practical need to classify defect type before selecting a restoration pipeline, and the joint training demonstrably improves performance over single-task models across both known and in-the-wild categories. The pre-compression strategy is a practical contribution that makes high-resolution conditional 3D generation tractable (70 days → 3.5 days for the first H-LDM stage). Open questions remain around large-scale multi-object scenes (the model is limited to single objects), the quality-diversity tradeoff inherent to conditional diffusion, and whether more compact representations (primitive-based or CAD) could replace the still-bulky 256³ TSDF grids.
