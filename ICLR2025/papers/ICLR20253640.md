## Problem

Existing Riemannian Batch Normalization methods are either restricted to specific geometries (e.g., SPD under AIM, Lie groups) or lack theoretical guarantees for normalizing sample statistics. While many manifolds in machine learning (SPD, Grassmannian, hyperbolic, spherical) admit gyro structures that naturally generalize group operations, no principled RBN framework existed for the general gyrogroup setting, leaving a gap between the algebraic structure and the normalization procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253640/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of N manifold-valued activations (e.g., n×p orthonormal basis matrices on Gr(p,n), vectors in the Poincaré ball P_K^n, or n×n SPD matrices) along with learnable biasing parameter B on the manifold and a scalar scaling parameter s. It outputs a normalized batch where the gyromean (Fréchet mean under gyrodistance) is shifted to B and the gyrovariance is scaled by s²/(v²+ε). Running statistics are maintained via binary barycenter updates under gyrodistance for inference-time normalization.

## Method

GyroBN extends Euclidean BN by replacing vector subtraction/addition/scaling with gyro subtraction (⊖M⊕P_i), gyro addition (B⊕·), and gyro scalar product (t⊙·), while computing statistics as Fréchet mean and variance under the gyrodistance. The key theoretical contribution is the pseudo-reductive gyrogroup, a relaxed algebraic structure that guarantees left cancellation and gyroisometric left translations without requiring full associativity (G4), which the Grassmannian violates. The paper proves that pseudo-reduction plus gyroisometric gyrations are sufficient to ensure homogeneity of the gyromean under biasing and quadratic scaling of dispersion, giving GyroBN a principled control over sample statistics. Several existing methods (LieBN on Lie groups, AIM-based SPDBNs) are shown to be special cases. The framework is instantiated on the Grassmannian (the first RBN with statistical guarantees on this manifold) and the Poincaré ball, with a plug-and-play implementation via Algorithm 1.

## Results

On the Grassmannian (GyroGr backbone), GyroBN-Gr achieves 51.89% (HDM05), 72.60% (NTU60), and 55.47% (NTU120) mean accuracy, improving over the vanilla GyroGr by 2.92%, 2.47%, and 1.71% respectively. On the hyperbolic space (HNN backbone), GyroBN-H achieves 94.3% (Cora), 81.2% (Disease), 95.4% (Airport), and 95.8% (Pubmed) test AUC, consistently outperforming both the vanilla HNN and the previous RBN-H. GyroBN also exhibits smaller standard deviations than RBN-H, indicating more stable training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GyroBN-Gr decisively outperforms both ManifoldNorm-Gr and RBN-Gr on all three Grassmannian datasets. On NTU60, GyroBN-Gr reaches 72.60% while ManifoldNorm-Gr drops to 68.56% and RBN-Gr to 67.77%—both actually degrading below the no-BN baseline of 70.13%. On NTU120 the gap is even wider: GyroBN-Gr (55.47%) vs. ManifoldNorm-Gr (51.41%) and RBN-Gr (50.56%). On the hyperbolic space, GyroBN-H beats RBN-H on Cora (94.3 vs. 93.5), Disease (81.2 vs. 76.6), and Airport (95.4 vs. 94.2), but RBN-H slightly edges out GyroBN-H on Pubmed (93.4 vs. 95.8—actually GyroBN-H wins at 95.8 vs. 93.4). The paper attributes GyroBN's consistent advantage to its theoretical guarantee of normalizing sample statistics (gyromean and gyrovariance), a property that ManifoldNorm and RBN lack in general.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and generalizes the line of Riemannian Batch Normalization work, showing that LieBN on general Lie groups (Chen et al., 2024b) and AIM-based SPDBN methods (Brooks et al., 2019; Kobler et al., 2022a,b) are special cases of a single gyrogroup framework. It builds on the gyro structures defined by Nguyen (2022a) and Nguyen & Yang (2023) for SPD, Grassmannian, and hyperbolic manifolds, but introduces the pseudo-reductive gyrogroup as a relaxed algebraic structure that corrects a questionable proof in prior work regarding the Grassmannian's left cancellation property. The key departure from prior RBN methods is the identification of minimal theoretical requirements (pseudo-reduction and gyroisometric gyrations) that guarantee control over sample statistics, extending normalization from Lie groups to non-group geometries like the Grassmannian and hyperbolic space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GyroBN provides the first principled RBN on the Grassmannian and a unified theoretical framework that subsumes multiple existing normalization methods, giving practitioners a plug-and-play tool for any gyro space with guaranteed statistics control. The finding that prior methods (ManifoldNorm, RBN) actually degrade Grassmannian network performance while GyroBN consistently improves it underscores the importance of theoretical normalization guarantees over ad-hoc Riemannian operators. A key limitation is that GyroBN is restricted to geometries admitting gyro structures; manifolds without such structures remain out of scope, and the authors acknowledge this as an open direction. The framework's generality also suggests potential for extending to other gyro spaces beyond those currently instantiated.
