## Problem

Last-iterate convergence of extra-gradient (EG) and optimistic-gradient (OG) algorithms in time-varying multi-player games remained largely unexplored: prior results were restricted to two-player bilinear zero-sum games, strictly monotone games, or required decreasing step sizes and unconstrained action sets. The open question was whether these classic first-order methods achieve a meaningful last-iterate convergence rate with a constant step size in general constrained, time-varying multi-player games converging to monotone games.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a sequence of time-varying cost functions f_t(z) = f_∞(z) + g_t(z) (where g_t → 0 and the perturbation satisfies a bounded-accumulated-perturbation condition), a closed convex action set Z ⊆ ℝⁿ, a constant step size η, and an initial action profile z₀ ∈ Z. The output is the last-iterate action z_T (EG) or w_T (OG) together with a convergence rate bound on the tangent residual r^tan(z_T), which upper-bounds both the gap function and total gap function. The rate takes the form O(max{1/√T, √(Σ_{k≥T/2} max‖G_k‖), max‖G_T‖}), matching the tight O(1/√T) bound for the time-invariant case when perturbations vanish sufficiently fast.

## Method

The analysis uses the tangent residual r^tan(z) = ‖Π_{J_Z(z)}[z − F(z)] − z‖ as a potential function rather than the gap function, because the tangent residual is (approximately) non-increasing in time-invariant monotone games while gap functions are not. The proof proceeds in three steps: (i) a best-iterate bound showing some iterate within T steps has tangent residual O(1/√T) plus a perturbation term, derived via a Pythagorean-descent argument on ‖z_k − z*‖²; (ii) an approximate monotonicity lemma showing r^tan(z_k)² ≥ r^tan(z_{k+1})² − O(max‖G_k‖/η), so the potential drifts upward by at most the perturbation magnitude each step; and (iii) combining (i) and (ii) by splitting the horizon at T/2 to bound the last iterate by the best iterate plus the tail sum of perturbations. For OG, the potential is augmented to Δ(z_k, w_k) = r^tan(z_k)² + ‖F_∞(z_k) − F_∞(w_k)‖² to handle the momentum-like w-sequence. Three perturbation assumptions (BAP variants) are treated independently, yielding slightly different rate terms involving max‖G_T‖, √(Σ max‖G_k‖), or the Lipschitz constant L_{G_k}.

## Results

The paper proves that both EG and OG algorithms achieve last-iterate convergence rates of O(1/√T) in time-varying multi-player games converging to monotone games, matching the known tight bounds for time-invariant games. Under bounded accumulated perturbation (BAP) assumptions, the full rate is max{O(1/√T), O(√(Σ_{k≥T/2} max‖G_k‖)), O(max‖G_T‖)} for general convex bounded settings. Under the stronger Assumption 4 (L_G-smooth perturbing with G_k(z*)=0), the rate improves to max{O(1/√T), O(max_{k≥T/2} L_{G_k}), O(√(Σ_{k≥T/2} max‖G_k‖))}. Numerical experiments on three bilinear game examples confirm that r^tan(z_t) and r^tan(w_t) converge to 0 under all three BAP assumptions, with perturbation rates decelerating convergence as predicted.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no direct empirical baselines; the 'comparison' is against prior theoretical results. The paper significantly generalizes Cai et al. (2022), which proved O(1/√T) last-iterate convergence for EG and OG only in time-invariant monotone games, by extending the result to time-varying games with vanishing perturbations. It also generalizes Feng et al. (2023), which established last-iterate convergence for EG (but not OG) in two-player bilinear time-varying zero-sum games with unconstrained action sets, by handling general multi-player games with constrained (bounded convex) action sets and both EG and OG. The paper addresses an open question from Feng et al. (2023) about whether constant-step-size last-iterate convergence holds in constrained time-varying settings, providing an affirmative answer under BAP assumptions. The convergence rates match the known tight O(1/√T) bound for time-invariant games, showing that vanishing perturbations do not degrade the fundamental rate. Unlike Duvocelle et al. (2023), which required decreasing step sizes and only showed almost-sure convergence (probability 1) without a rate, this paper uses constant step sizes and provides explicit O(1/√T) rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tangent-residual-based last-iterate convergence framework of Cai et al. (2022) from time-invariant monotone games to time-varying convergent perturbed games, and generalizes the two-player bilinear time-varying results of Feng et al. (2023) to arbitrary multi-player games with bounded convex constraints. The analysis technique for the OG algorithm draws on the potential-function approach of Hsieh et al. (2019) and Wei et al. (2021), adapted to handle time-varying operators. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of bounded accumulated perturbation (BAP) assumptions that quantify how fast the time-varying cost functions converge to their monotone limit, enabling explicit rate bounds rather than mere almost-sure convergence (as in Duvocelle et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves an open question about whether EG and OG achieve last-iterate convergence with constant step size in constrained, time-varying multi-player games, showing that the O(1/√T) rate matching the time-invariant tight bound is preserved under vanishing perturbations. The practical implication is that these classic algorithms are robust to slowly decaying environmental changes in multi-agent settings, which is relevant for applications like dynamic resource allocation and adversarial training with drifting objectives. Open questions remain about whether the rate can be improved in bilinear cases (as numerical experiments suggest), and whether analogous results hold for periodic or stochastic time-varying games rather than merely convergent ones.
