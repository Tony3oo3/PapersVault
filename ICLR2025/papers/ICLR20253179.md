## Problem

Existing knowledge editing benchmarks (WikiData recent, ZsRE, WikiBio) do not verify whether LLMs actually produce hallucinated answers to evaluation questions before editing, making it impossible to directly interpret post-edit accuracy as hallucination-correction effectiveness. This paper asks the fundamental question: can knowledge editing really correct real-world hallucinations, and if so, how well do different methods perform across multiple dimensions beyond simple accuracy?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253179/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes as input verified hallucinations—knowledge triplets (s, r, o, o*) where the target LLM (Llama2-7B, Llama3-8B, or Mistral-v0.3-7B) demonstrably produced the wrong object o before editing—ensuring pre-edit Efficacy Scores are exactly 0% by construction. GPT-4o generates evaluation QA pairs per facet, including multi-hop portability questions (N=1–6 hops) and adversarial robustness prompts repeated over M=1–10 turns. The output is five percentage scores (Efficacy, Generalization, Portability, Locality, Robustness) computed per domain, per LLM, and overall, with Locality measured as the unchanging rate on unrelated questions rather than accuracy.

## Method

HalluEditBench is constructed in two phases. First, 143,557 raw knowledge triplets are retrieved from Wikidata across 26 topics in 9 domains, filtered to remove ambiguous (subject, relation) pairs with multiple valid objects, converted into factual questions, and then verified against three LLMs to retain only questions where the model actually hallucinates (yielding 12,619 / 13,210 / 14,366 hallucinations for Llama2-7B / Llama3-8B / Mistral-v0.3-7B). Second, ~2,000 hallucinations per LLM are sampled to cover all topics and domains, and GPT-4o generates evaluation QA pairs for five facets: Efficacy (the original hallucinated question), Generalization (rephrased, yes/no, multi-choice, and reversed questions), Portability (1–6 hop downstream questions), Locality (unrelated questions about the same subject), and Robustness (adversarial 'your answer is wrong' prompts over up to 10 turns). Seven editing methods spanning four families—Locate-then-Edit (ROME, MEMIT), Fine-tuning (FT-L, FT-M, LoRA), In-Context Editing (ICE), and Memory-based (GRACE)—are then applied and scored across all five dimensions, revealing that methods with near-100% accuracy on prior benchmarks (e.g., FT-M, MEMIT) achieve far lower Efficacy on verified hallucinations, and that no single method dominates across all five facets.

## Results

On existing evaluation datasets (WikiData recent, ZsRE, WikiBio), Llama2-7B already achieves 47.40%, 37.49%, and 61.35% accuracy before any editing, and methods like FT-M and LoRA push post-edit accuracy to ~100%. However, on HalluEditBench (where pre-edit Efficacy is guaranteed 0%), FT-M's Efficacy drops to only ~60% on Llama2-7B and Mistral-v0.3-7B, revealing a large gap between existing-dataset performance and true hallucination-correction ability. The overall Efficacy ranking across three LLMs is FT-L < FT-M < MEMIT < ROME < LoRA < ICE < GRACE, with ICE and GRACE (parameter-preserving methods) outperforming all parameter-modifying techniques. On Locality, FT-M (~80% on Mistral) and ICE (~60% on Llama3) are the only methods with satisfactory scores, while all others fall below 40% on Llama3-8B and Mistral-v0.3-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks seven knowledge editing methods (FT-L, FT-M, MEMIT, ROME, LoRA, ICE, GRACE) across five dimensions on HalluEditBench. On Efficacy, ICE and GRACE (both parameter-preserving) outperform all five parameter-modifying methods, while FT-M and MEMIT—despite near-100% scores on existing datasets—achieve only ~60% Efficacy, exposing the unreliability of prior evaluations. On Generalization, all methods except ICE either slightly improve or degrade performance below pre-edit levels; GRACE shows the most severe degradation across all question types. On Portability (multi-hop reasoning), all methods except ICE underperform pre-edit scores, indicating LLMs do not truly reason with edited knowledge in multi-hop settings. On Locality, FT-M and ICE are the only methods with acceptable scores (above 60%), while ROME, MEMIT, LoRA, and GRACE all fall below 40% on two of three LLMs, showing significant collateral damage to unrelated knowledge. On Robustness, parameter-preserving methods ICE and GRACE show the weakest resistance to adversarial prompting, while MEMIT and ROME maintain high scores on Llama3-8B and Mistral-v0.3-7B but collapse on Llama2-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying a fundamental methodological gap in the knowledge editing evaluation literature: existing benchmarks (WikiData recent, ZsRE, WikiBio) do not verify that LLMs actually produce hallucinated answers before editing, making post-edit scores unreliable indicators of true hallucination correction. Rather than proposing a new editing method, the paper repurposes the established knowledge editing paradigm (ROME, MEMIT, LoRA, ICE, GRACE, FT-L, FT-M) under a rigorous verification protocol where pre-edit Efficacy is guaranteed to be 0%. It extends the evaluation framework beyond single-question accuracy to five dimensions (Efficacy, Generalization, Portability, Locality, Robustness), drawing on prior multi-hop reasoning benchmarks (MQuAKE) and sycophancy literature for the Portability and Robustness facets respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that methods scoring ~100% on standard benchmarks (FT-M, MEMIT) drop to ~60% on verified hallucinations fundamentally challenges how the field assesses editing effectiveness, suggesting prior comparisons between methods may be distorted. The discovery that editing can actively degrade Generalization and Portability performance (negative side effects beyond the target fact) raises open questions about whether current editing techniques are safe for deployment. A key unresolved question is whether any editing approach can simultaneously achieve high Efficacy, Generalization, Portability, Locality, and Robustness, or whether these objectives are fundamentally in tension. The benchmark also highlights that performance is highly domain- and model-dependent, complicating general claims about method superiority.
