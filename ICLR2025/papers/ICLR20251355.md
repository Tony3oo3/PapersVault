## Problem

Despite the dominance of Transformer and SSM-based architectures in recent DNA foundation model research, CNNs have not been systematically re-evaluated on modern foundation model benchmarks. This paper asks whether CNNs are truly surpassed by these newer paradigms, motivated by CNNs' inherent advantages: local inductive bias, O(n) computational complexity, robustness to variable sequence lengths, and better data efficiency for small training sets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251355/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

ConvNova takes double-stranded DNA sequences as one-hot encoded nucleotide strings (character-level tokenization, no k-mer or BPE), with input lengths ranging from ~200 bp (Genomic Benchmark) to 16K bp (Gene Finding). Outputs are task-specific: sequence-level MCC/F1 scores for histone markers, regulatory elements, and splice sites; per-nucleotide gene structure labels (9 classes) for gene finding; or 919 binary chromatin profile predictions for DeepSEA-style tasks. Pretraining uses bidirectional MLM where 10% of nucleotides on the primary strand are replaced with 'N' and the model predicts the original base.

## Method

ConvNova is a CNN architecture built from stacked Gated Convolution Blocks (GCBs), each combining three key designs: dilated convolutions (rates [1,1,d,d²,d³,...]) to expand the receptive field without downsampling, gated convolutions for dynamic per-channel feature selection, and a dual-branch structure where one branch (B) produces sigmoid gating signals that modulate the other branch (A) via element-wise multiplication with residual connections. The paper demonstrates that U-Net-style downsampling severely degrades DNA task performance (e.g., 50-point drop on splice sites) compared to dilation, making dilation the critical design choice. The full pipeline is: one-hot encoding → 1D convolution → N GCBs (kernel size 9, dilation rate 4 by default) → MLP → task-specific head, pretrained via bidirectional MLM on HG38 for 400 epochs before fine-tuning on downstream tasks.

## Results

On the NT Benchmark (18 tasks), ConvNova (1.7M params) achieves state-of-the-art performance on 12 out of 18 tasks, outperforming the second-best model (Caduceus-Ph, 1.9M) by an average of 5.8% on histone-related tasks and by 10.5% on H3K4me3 (67.15 vs. 56.72 MCC). On the BEND Gene Finding task (long-sequence, 1.4K–14K bps), ConvNova achieves 0.55 MCC, surpassing HyenaDNA (0.35), GENA-LM (0.52), DNABERT-2 (0.43), and NT-H (0.41). On Chromatin Profile Prediction (919 binary classes, DeepSEA), ConvNova outperforms all baselines including the previous SOTA supervised model DeepATT, with an average AUC of 92.25 (6.8M) and 92.47 (27.4M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Caduceus-Ph (1.9M, SSM-based), ConvNova (1.7M) wins 12 of 18 NT benchmark tasks, with the largest gap on H3K4me3 (+10.4 MCC) and H3K14ac (+9.9 MCC), while losing only on splice site tasks where NTv2 dominates. Against HyenaDNA (1.6M, SSM-based), ConvNova outperforms on nearly all tasks except Splice Acceptor, with particularly large margins on histone marks (e.g., H3K4me2: 57.45 vs. 42.68 MCC). Against the much larger transformer models DNABERT-2 (117M) and NTv2 (500M), ConvNova wins 15/18 and 12/18 tasks respectively, demonstrating that the CNN architecture achieves superior performance with 68×–294× fewer parameters. On the long-sequence Gene Finding task, ConvNova (0.55 MCC) decisively beats HyenaDNA (0.35), GENA-LM (0.52), DNABERT-2 (0.43), and NT-H (0.41). On Chromatin Profile Prediction (DeepSEA), ConvNova surpasses all HyenaDNA sizes (0.4M–6.5M), all NTv2 sizes (50M–500M), DNABERT-2, and the previous SOTA supervised model DeepATT, even when trained from scratch without pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions CNNs within the DNA foundation model landscape, where Transformers (DNABERT, NTv2) and SSMs (HyenaDNA, Caduceus) have become the dominant paradigms since 2021. It adapts the gated convolution mechanism from image inpainting (Yu et al., 2019) into a dual-branch architecture specifically designed for nucleotide sequences, combining it with dilated convolutions to expand receptive fields without downsampling. The work deliberately departs from the U-Net-style downsampling common in computer vision, demonstrating that this approach severely degrades performance on DNA tasks, and instead follows the MLM pretraining recipe established by HyenaDNA on HG38 to enable fair comparison with recent foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 1.7M-parameter CNN outperforms 500M-parameter Transformers on 12 of 18 NT benchmark tasks challenges the prevailing assumption that architectural complexity (attention, state-space recurrence) is necessary for competitive DNA modeling, suggesting practitioners may be over-parameterizing for many classification tasks. The observation that optimal receptive field size correlates with the biological localization of specific histone marks (H3K4me3, H3K14ac enriched near TSS) hints at a connection between inductive bias and genomic structure that could inform future architecture design. Key limitations include evaluation only on the human reference genome (no multi-species pretraining), restriction to classification tasks (no generation or variant effect prediction), and the absence of autoregressive modeling, leaving open whether CNNs can match Transformers on generative or long-context tasks.
