## Problem

Existing Local SGD methods for distributed LLM training fail to handle model sharding (limiting them to small models), introduce non-overlapped communication and memory overhead, and remain vulnerable to loss spikes from noisy large-scale corpora and consistent stragglers in heterogeneous clusters. This paper addresses the gap between the communication-efficiency promise of Local SGD and the practical demands of training multi-billion-parameter LLMs at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252372/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text corpus (FineWeb-Edu at 1.3T tokens or an in-house pre-training dataset), initial Llama model weights (350M–7B parameters with 32 layers), and an M×N device mesh configuration that partitions K workers into model shard groups (columns, for parameter sharding) and model sync groups (rows, for periodic synchronization). It produces standard trained LLM weights, with the key structural constraint that each worker holds only a shard of each layer's parameters, and the outer optimizer state (Nesterov momentum) is also sharded per-worker to minimize memory footprint.

## Method

EDiT organizes workers on a 2D device mesh where parameters are sharded along the model-shard dimension and synchronized along the model-sync dimension. Its core mechanism performs layer-wise parameter synchronization during the forward pass (rather than before the next step), combined with a prefetch strategy that overlaps sync communication with ongoing forward computation, reducing both communication volume and peak memory. A pseudo gradient penalty strategy stabilizes training by (1) eliminating anomalous workers via an EMA z-test on pseudo-gradient norms, (2) applying norm-based exponential weighting to suppress large updates, and (3) clipping the aggregated pseudo gradient before the outer Nesterov-momentum update. The asynchronous variant A-EDiT replaces fixed-step synchronization with a fixed time interval (τ_time), allowing faster workers to complete more local iterations before syncing, thereby eliminating idle waiting caused by consistent stragglers.

## Results

On the FineWeb-Edu dataset (Llama 1B, 420B tokens), EDiT achieves the best average benchmark score of 44.82, surpassing the synchronous Baseline (44.60), DiLoCo (43.96), CO2 (43.00), and Post Local SGD (42.34). On the more challenging in-house dataset with diverse, lower-quality corpora, EDiT again leads with 34.96 average, while DiLoCo degrades to 34.62 due to loss spikes from noisy data. In terms of throughput, EDiT reaches 2.25e5 tokens/sec (158 TFLOPS) on the 1B model, and is the only method (alongside A-EDiT) that can train 3B and 7B models without OOM, achieving 1.11e5 and 5.42e4 tokens/sec respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDiT outperforms all three Local SGD baselines on the FineWeb-Edu dataset: it beats Post Local SGD by 2.48 points in average benchmark score (44.82 vs. 42.34), DiLoCo by 0.86 points (44.82 vs. 43.96), and CO2 by 1.82 points (44.82 vs. 43.00). On the in-house dataset with noisier, more diverse corpora, EDiT's pseudo gradient penalty strategy proves critical: it achieves 34.96 average versus DiLoCo's 34.62, which suffers noticeable loss spikes and performance degradation on low-quality data. In terms of scalability, EDiT is the only method (with A-EDiT) that can train 3B and 7B Llama models on 16 A100 GPUs without OOM, while Post Local SGD, DiLoCo, CO2, and CO2* all fail at 3B scale. A-EDiT, the asynchronous variant, achieves 43.90 average on FineWeb-Edu (slightly below EDiT's 44.82) but maintains high throughput under consistent straggler conditions where EDiT degrades, making it the preferred choice for heterogeneous clusters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Local SGD lineage (Zhang et al. 2016, Post Local SGD, SlowMo, DiLoCo) by integrating it with ZeRO-3/FSDP-style model sharding, which prior Local SGD methods could not handle because they held full parameter copies on each GPU. It departs from CO2 (Sun et al. 2023) by performing layer-wise parameter synchronization during the forward pass with a prefetch strategy, avoiding the non-overlapped communication and memory overhead that CO2* introduces when sharding extra parameters. The pseudo gradient penalty (anomaly elimination via EMA z-test, norm-weighted averaging, and gradient clipping) is a novel stability mechanism not present in prior Local SGD work, and A-EDiT extends the paradigm to fully asynchronous training with time-based rather than step-based synchronization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of 7B-scale LLM pre-training (420B tokens) using a Local SGD variant, and the finding that EDiT's optimal learning rate is invariant to worker count (unlike synchronous Baseline) provides a concrete, practical advantage for elastic training where resources scale dynamically. The method is directly applicable to production clusters with heterogeneous hardware and stragglers, addressing pain points that synchronous paradigms cannot. Open questions remain: the elastic training simulation still requires halting and restarting upon node changes, and A-EDiT's performance is still degraded by consistent stragglers, suggesting that truly seamless elastic frameworks and better straggler mitigation are important next steps.
