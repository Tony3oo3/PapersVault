## Problem

Diffusion models frequently replicate their training data verbatim during inference, posing serious privacy and copyright risks. Existing mitigations—differential privacy, ambient diffusion, and data corruption—either cause severe image quality degradation (FID scores jumping from ~3 to 10–100) or require expensive retraining of the diffusion model from scratch. The paper asks whether training-data replication can be prevented at sampling time, without retraining the denoiser and with minimal quality loss.

## Input / Output

CPSample operates on a pretrained diffusion model (DDIM or Stable Diffusion) and requires only a separately trained classifier as additional input. During generation, the model consumes standard random Gaussian noise (and optionally a text prompt for guided models) and produces synthetic RGB images at the native resolution of the base model (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for CelebA-64). Two hyperparameters—tolerance threshold α ∈ (0, 0.5) and scale s—control the aggressiveness of protection and can be tuned post-training without retraining either the classifier or the denoiser.

## Method

CPSample trains a modified U-Net classifier to memorize random Bernoulli(0.5) binary labels assigned to each training image, so that the classifier's high-confidence regions correspond to neighborhoods of the training data. During DDIM sampling, at each denoising step t, if the classifier predicts either label with probability exceeding 1−α, the noise prediction ε_θ is replaced by ε_θ − s√(1−ᾱ_t)·∇log p_φ(y_opposite|x_t,t), steering the sample toward the opposite label and away from the training-data manifold. A small numerical-stability constant τ is added inside the log to prevent float16 overflow. Theoretically, under Lipschitz continuity of the classifier and a memorization assumption, CPSample generates samples outside δ-balls around every training point with probability ≥ 1−ε, matching the guarantee of rejection sampling but without the need to resample. Empirically, this reduces the fraction of near-exact replicates from 6–12% to 0–0.1% while achieving FID scores of 4.97 (CIFAR-10) and 2.97 (CelebA-64), substantially outperforming DPDM, DP-LDM, and ambient diffusion.

## Results

CPSample achieves FID scores of 4.97 on CIFAR-10 and 2.97 on CelebA-64 while eliminating exact replication of training data, compared to 3.17 and 1.27 for unprotected DDIM. On similarity reduction, CPSample reduces the fraction of generated images exceeding the near-identical threshold from 6.25% to 0.00% on CIFAR-10 and from 12.5% to 0.10% on CelebA (p < 0.0001). For membership inference attacks, CPSample yields p-values of 0.28–0.81 (no statistically significant difference between train and test reconstruction loss), whereas unprotected DDIM gives p ≈ 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPSample outperforms all four privacy-protection baselines on FID while still eliminating training-data replication. Against Ambient Diffusion (Daras et al., 2024), CPSample achieves 4.97 vs. 11.70 FID on CIFAR-10 and 2.97 vs. 25.95 on CelebA, a 2.4× and 8.7× improvement respectively. Against DPDM (Dockhorn et al., 2023), the gap is dramatic: 4.97 vs. 97.7 on CIFAR-10 and 2.97 vs. 78.3 on CelebA. Against DP-Diffusion (Ghalebikesabi et al., 2023), CPSample scores 4.97 vs. 9.8 on CIFAR-10. Against DP-LDM (Lyu et al., 2024), CPSample achieves 4.97 vs. 8.4 on CIFAR-10 and 2.97 vs. 16.2 on CelebA. The only method with a lower FID is unprotected DDIM (3.17 / 1.27), but DDIM replicates up to 12.5% of training images, which CPSample reduces to 0–0.10%.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPSample repurposes the classifier-guidance mechanism from Dhariwal & Nichol (2021) within the DDIM sampling framework (Song et al., 2021), but inverts its purpose: rather than steering generation toward a desired attribute, it steers away from training data by using a classifier trained to memorize random binary labels. This positions the method as an inference-time intervention, in contrast to the dominant training-time privacy approaches (DP-SGD, ambient diffusion masking, PAC-privacy regularization) that require retraining the diffusion model from scratch. Conceptually, CPSample is closest to rejection sampling in its goal of excluding training-data neighborhoods, but achieves this through continuous gradient-based guidance rather than discrete accept/reject decisions, eliminating the need for costly resampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that CPSample can be applied to any existing pretrained diffusion model by training a small auxiliary classifier, avoiding the weeks-to-months retraining cost of DP or ambient diffusion methods while achieving substantially better FID scores (4.97 vs. 11.70 on CIFAR-10). However, the method is not formally differentially private, the theoretical guarantees rely on strong assumptions (Lipschitz continuity, controlled classifier confidence), and the permutation-based membership inference test still fails on CelebA. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large training sets (the classifier training cost grows), whether the Lipschitz constant can be practically bounded to yield meaningful formal guarantees, and whether the random-label memorization strategy remains effective when the training set contains near-duplicates at scale.
