## Problem

Existing audio-driven portrait animation methods, including the Hallo framework, are limited to generating only seconds-long videos at modest resolutions, suffering from appearance drift, error accumulation, and temporal artifacts over extended durations. No prior method achieves 4K-resolution portrait animation lasting tens of minutes or hours, nor provides adjustable textual control over facial expressions during long-term generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253299/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference portrait image, an audio clip of arbitrary duration (minutes to hours), and optional textual expression prompts (formatted as {human}{expression}) that can be injected at arbitrary time intervals to modulate expressions and head poses. The base diffusion model generates 512×512 video frames, which are then upscaled to a maximum of 4096×4096 pixels via a VQGAN-based high-resolution enhancement module. During inference, classifier-free guidance (scale 3.5) is applied uniformly to the reference image, audio, and textual prompt conditions.

## Method

The core mechanism for long-duration generation is a patch-drop data augmentation applied to preceding motion frames: each frame is partitioned into p×p patches, and patches are randomly zeroed with a configurable drop rate (20–30% optimal), corrupting appearance while preserving coarse spatial structure for motion. Gaussian noise is additionally injected into the latent representations of these motion frames to further suppress accumulated artifacts, forcing the denoising U-Net to source identity from the reference image while extracting only lip movements, expressions, and poses from the motion frames. For 4K synthesis, the method extends VQGAN codebook prediction into the temporal dimension by reshaping self-attention hidden states in a code-index prediction transformer to apply scaled dot-product attention across frames, ensuring temporally coherent code sequences before decoding with a high-quality VQGAN decoder. Textual expression control is implemented via adaptive layer normalization: CLIP text embeddings pass through a zero-initialized MLP to produce per-channel scaling and shifting parameters applied between the cross-attention and audio-attention layers of the U-Net, enabling fine-grained modulation of facial expressions without disrupting the audio-driven motion pipeline.

## Results

On the HDTF dataset (4-minute generated videos), Hallo2 achieves the best FID of 16.616, FVD of 239.517, and E-FID of 6.702, with competitive lip-sync scores of Sync-C 7.379 and Sync-D 7.697. On the proposed 'Wild' dataset, it attains FID 24.072, FVD 360.192, Sync-C 6.760, Sync-D 8.156, and E-FID 33.316. The method also demonstrates stable FID degradation over extended inference durations (Figure 5), unlike parallel-generation baselines that suffer significant quality drops. For super-resolution, the proposed high-resolution enhancement (HRE) outperforms ESRGAN and MagVITv2 on FVD (360.71 vs. 418.06 and 425.68) while achieving the best MUSIQ score (0.7187).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hallo2 outperforms all five portrait-animation baselines on FID, FVD, and E-FID on HDTF. Against its direct predecessor Hallo, the improvement is most dramatic in FVD (239.5 vs. 366.1, a 35% reduction) and E-FID (6.70 vs. 7.08), with modest gains in FID (16.62 vs. 16.75) and Sync-C (7.38 vs. 7.27). Compared to the parallel-generation methods EchoMimic and AniPortrait, Hallo2 leads by large margins on every metric (e.g., FVD 239.5 vs. 532.7 and 362.0; Sync-C 7.38 vs. 5.93 and 3.91), confirming that the incremental generation strategy with patch-drop augmentation avoids the blurriness and expression distortion inherent to inter-frame continuity constraints. Against the non-diffusion baselines Audio2Head and SadTalker, Hallo2 wins decisively on FID (16.6 vs. 41.8 and 21.9) and FVD (239.5 vs. 246.0 and 293.1), though Audio2Head retains a slight edge on Sync-C (8.05 vs. 7.38) and Sync-D (7.12 vs. 7.70), suggesting a trade-off between lip-sync tightness and overall visual fidelity. On the more challenging 'Wild' dataset, Hallo2 again achieves the best FID (24.07), E-FID (33.32), and Sync-C (6.76), while Hallo degrades noticeably (FID 28.19, FVD 572.0), highlighting the robustness of the proposed augmentation strategies under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct extension of Hallo (Xu et al., 2024a), which introduced hierarchical audio-driven visual synthesis for portrait animation, and departs from it by addressing the two major limitations of short duration and low resolution. For long-duration generation, it follows the incremental (autoregressive) paradigm shared by Hallo and EMO, but introduces patch-drop and Gaussian noise augmentation on motion frames to prevent appearance contamination—a problem that parallel-generation methods (AniPortrait, EchoMimic) sidestep at the cost of expressiveness. For 4K synthesis, it extends the VQGAN discrete codebook prediction framework (Esser et al., 2021; CodeFormer, Zhou et al., 2022c) into the temporal dimension with a novel temporal alignment mechanism, and adds CLIP-based textual prompt control via adaptive layer normalization, a design not present in prior audio-driven portrait animation systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hallo2 is the first method to simultaneously achieve 4K resolution, hour-long duration, and text-controllable expressions in audio-driven portrait animation, making it practically relevant for film production, virtual assistants, and interactive content. The patch-drop augmentation is a simple, transferable technique that directly addresses error accumulation in incremental video generation, and the ablation showing that augmenting the reference image actively harms identity (FID 38.5 → 98.4) provides a useful design principle. Open questions include the computational cost of 4K inference at scale, generalizability beyond single-person portraits, and whether the temporal alignment in the VQGAN codebook can be extended to other video super-resolution or generation tasks.
