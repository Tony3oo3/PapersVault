## Problem

Procedure planning in instructional videos requires generating coherent action sequences from only start and end visual observations, but existing methods rely on text-level supervision (events, task labels, knowledge graphs, LLM descriptions) that is decoupled from the action reasoning process and fails to capture fine-grained temporal relationships among intermediate actions. This paper addresses the gap by introducing visual-level mid-state supervision that is generated end-to-end within the diffusion model itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251667/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes two 3-second pre-extracted video clips (start V_s and goal V_g, using HowTo100M-trained encoder features) as input, along with a predicted task class label from a transformer classifier. It outputs a discrete action sequence a_{1:T} (T ∈ {3,4,5,6}) generated via iterative DDIM denoising over a structured matrix of shape T×(C+A+O), where C is the task class dimension, A is the action vocabulary, and O is the observation feature dimension. The action dimension is masked so that only task-relevant actions receive noise during initialization, effectively restricting the generation space.

## Method

MTID decomposes procedure planning into three sub-problems solved jointly: (1) a 4-layer transformer classifier predicts the task class from the observation pair; (2) a latent space temporal interpolation module encodes V_s and V_g via 1D convolutions, generates M intermediate latent features through a learnable sigmoid-gated interpolation matrix, and refines them with transformer encoder blocks; (3) a 3-layer U-Net with DDIM denoising generates the action sequence, with cross-attention layers injecting the interpolated features into each residual temporal block. A masked projection mechanism adds Gaussian noise only to task-relevant action positions in the initial matrix, and a task-adaptive masked proximity loss applies gradient weights that are highest near the endpoints (where real observations provide strong supervision) and lowest in the middle, while a mask coefficient ρ penalizes predictions of task-irrelevant actions.

## Results

On CrossTask (the primary benchmark), MTID achieves a Success Rate of 40.45% and mean Accuracy of 67.19% at T=3, outperforming all 11 baselines on both metrics. At longer horizons (T=4, T=5), MTID maintains the top SR (24.76% and 15.26%) and mAcc (60.69% and 10.30%). However, SCHEMA achieves substantially higher mIoU (79.82% vs. 69.17% at T=3), indicating MTID's advantage is concentrated in strict per-step accuracy rather than set-level overlap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTID achieves the highest SR and mAcc across all prediction horizons on CrossTask, beating the closest competitor SCHEMA by 1.52 SR points (40.45 vs. 38.93) and 3.39 mAcc points (67.19 vs. 63.80) at T=3. However, SCHEMA substantially outperforms MTID on mIoU (79.82 vs. 69.17 at T=3; 76.48 vs. 67.67 at T=4), suggesting MTID's per-step predictions are more accurate but its action sets overlap less with ground truth. Against PDPP (the direct predecessor), MTID gains +3.25 SR and +2.52 mAcc at T=3, with similar margins at T=4 and T=5. On COIN (Table 3), MTID leads on mAcc (51.70 vs. 49.84 for SCHEMA at T=3) but trails SCHEMA on both SR (30.44 vs. 32.09) and mIoU (59.74 vs. 83.83). On NIV, MTID wins on SR (28.52 vs. 27.93) and mAcc (44.44 vs. 41.64) over SCHEMA at T=3, but again loses on mIoU (56.46 vs. 76.77). All earlier baselines (DDN, P³IV, EGPP, Ext-GAIL, PlaTe, UPN, UAAA, WLTDO) are clearly outperformed by MTID on every reported metric across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based procedure planning framework of PDPP (Wang et al., 2023b) by introducing a latent space temporal interpolation module that generates intermediate visual features from start and end observations, replacing the text-level supervision (events, task labels, knowledge graphs, LLM descriptions) used by EGPP, KEPP, and SCHEMA. The DDIM sampling strategy (Song et al., 2021) is adopted for efficient inference, and a masked projection mechanism inspired by Gao et al. (2023) constrains the action space to task-relevant actions. The key conceptual departure is providing visual-level mid-state supervision through learnable interpolation rather than relying on external textual or structured knowledge sources, enabling end-to-end training where the interpolation module and diffusion model co-adapt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in procedure planning: prior methods bridge the observation-to-action gap with text-level signals that lack the granularity needed for precise temporal reasoning. However, the paper's own Table 15 reveals that interpolated features substantially underperform real intermediate features on CrossTask (SR 40.45 vs 49.05), suggesting the interpolation module captures only a fraction of the available temporal information. Open questions include whether more sophisticated interpolation (e.g., learned generative models for mid-states) could close this gap, whether the approach scales to longer horizons beyond T=6, and why performance on COIN mIoU (59.74) lags significantly behind SCHEMA (83.83) despite competitive SR. The masked projection and task-adaptive proximity loss are practical techniques likely to be reusable in other constrained generation settings.
