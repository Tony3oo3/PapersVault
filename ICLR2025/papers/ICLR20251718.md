## Problem

Existing memorization detection methods for text-to-image diffusion models require access to the original paired prompts to identify memorized outputs, making them inapplicable when an image owner only has the image itself and no prompt or metadata. This paper formalizes image-level memorization detection: determining whether a given image has been memorized by a diffusion model using only the image, without any prompt information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251718/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input (no prompt, no metadata) and encodes it into the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model. It produces two scalar detection scores: an L1 similarity distance in latent space between the original image and the perturbed reconstruction (higher = more likely memorized), and the mean magnitude of text-conditional noise prediction (MTCNP) across timesteps during perturbed inference (higher = more likely memorized). Both scores are computed without ever accessing the original training prompt.

## Method

IIP (Inversion-based Inference Perturbation) operates in two stages. First, it applies unconditional DDIM Inversion (using a null-text prompt) up to a small timestep t_I=20 to extract a latent code that retains the primary semantic and structural information of the original image while minimizing inversion error. Second, it optimizes a random prompt embedding to minimize MTCNP across timesteps (making the prompt 'far from memorized'), with regularization to prevent trivial solutions by adding noise to the latent code and constraining the embedding's distance from the null-text embedding. The optimized prompt is then used to run DDIM sampling from the inverted latent code. The key insight is that memorized images, after this perturbation, exhibit both lower L1 similarity to the original and larger MTCNP magnitudes compared to non-memorized images, providing two complementary detection signals that achieve near-perfect AUC (>0.99) across training and generated image settings.

## Results

On the primary training-image detection task (SDv1.4), IIP_sim achieves AUC 0.999 and TPR@1%FPR 0.987 for memorized vs. non-memorized training images, and AUC 1.000 with TPR@1%FPR 0.993 for memorized vs. non-training images. The MTCNP metric (IIP_mtcnp) also reaches AUC 0.995–0.997 with TPR@1%FPR 0.842–0.875. On generated images (8K samples), IIP_mtcnp attains AUC 0.978, accuracy 95.7%, and TPR@1%FPR 0.920, while IIP_sim reaches AUC 0.964 and accuracy 93.7%. Under data augmentation attacks, IIP_sim retains AUC 0.996–0.998 on training images, far exceeding the next-best baseline (DDIM Inversion at AUC 0.847–0.865).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IIP decisively outperforms all five baselines across every setting. Against the strongest reconstruction baseline, DDIM Inversion (AUC 0.938–0.943 on training images), IIP_sim improves AUC to 0.999–1.000 and TPR@1%FPR from ~0.39 to ~0.99, a gap of over 60 percentage points in detection sensitivity at low false-positive rates. The membership-inference baselines SecMI (AUC 0.584) and PIA (AUC 0.507) perform near chance level on training images, confirming that memorization cues are not captured by standard membership signals. On generated images, image-to-image reconstruction (AUC 0.933, TPR@1%FPR 0.715) is the closest competitor, yet IIP_mtcnp still leads by 4.5 AUC points and 20.5 TPR@1%FPR points. The paper also directly benchmarks Wen et al.'s (2024) prompt-perturbation and MTCNP-minimization techniques adapted to the image-level task (Table A11), where both yield AUC ≈ 0.67, roughly 30 points below IIP_sim, confirming that the inversion-based pipeline is essential rather than merely the MTCNP metric itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memorization detection line initiated by Wen et al. (2024), who showed that memorized prompts exhibit larger MTCNP magnitudes during generation, by demonstrating that this signal persists even when the original prompt is replaced with a random one — revealing it as an image-level rather than prompt-level property. The method builds on unconditional DDIM Inversion (Song et al., 2020) to recover semantic latent codes without prompt access, then perturbs subsequent inference with optimized non-memorized prompt embeddings. It departs from Ma et al. (2024), who attempted image-level detection but restricted to exact duplication, by formalizing the task across partial replication, generated images, and non-training images with a comprehensive multi-setting evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: image owners can now verify whether their content has been memorized by a diffusion model without needing the original prompt or metadata, addressing a real privacy and copyright concern. The near-perfect AUC (>0.99) on training-image detection and robustness against data augmentation attacks make the method deployable in realistic audit scenarios. Limitations include degraded performance on generated images under augmentation, dependence on Stable Diffusion architecture specifics (latent space, DDIM schedule), and the fact that baselines are reconstruction-based or membership-inference methods rather than purpose-built image-level memorization detectors, leaving the competitive landscape somewhat thin. Open questions include generalization to newer diffusion architectures (e.g., SDXL, DiT-based models) and whether the two complementary metrics can be unified into a single robust score.
