## Problem

Guided diffusion for 3D molecule generation requires either labeled data to train conditional models or neural property predictors, both of which are expensive to acquire and struggle to generalize to novel structures. Quantum chemistry software (e.g., GFN2-xTB) provides accurate physical guidance but is inherently non-differentiable, preventing direct integration into the diffusion sampling loop. The paper addresses how to leverage such a non-differentiable domain oracle to steer an unconditional diffusion model toward stable, property-optimized molecules without any additional labeled training.

## Input / Output

The method starts from random Gaussian noise in a latent space (z_T ∈ R^{N×(3+d_z)}) and optionally a scalar target property value y. During the last 400 of 1000 denoising steps, the latent state is decoded via a VAE decoder into a 3D point cloud (N atoms with 3D coordinates and type features), passed through the xTB oracle, and the resulting force/property signal is used to compute a guidance gradient that shifts the sampling mean. The final output is a 3D molecular geometry with atom types, decoded from z_0, with the constraint that coordinates maintain zero center of gravity (equivariance requirement).

## Method

CHEMGUIDE estimates the guidance gradient from a non-differentiable quantum chemistry oracle (GFN2-xTB) using Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (SPSA): a random zero-mean perturbation matrix U ∈ R^{N×3} ~ N(0,1) is added to and subtracted from the latent coordinates, and the finite difference of the oracle's output (e.g., mean atomic force) divided by 2ζU yields an unbiased gradient estimate. This avoids backpropagation through the proprietary xTB code while preserving the zero-center-of-gravity equivariance constraint required by the EGNN-based latent diffusion model (GeoLDM). The method is further embedded in a bilevel optimization framework where the inner loop optimizes a molecular property via a differentiable neural regressor (noisy or clean guidance) and the outer loop optimizes stability via the non-differentiable oracle, enabling joint property-and-stability generation without retraining the diffusion backbone.

## Results

On QM9 (500 molecules, best scale=0.0001), CHEMGUIDE reduces force RMS to 0.0104 Eh/Bohr (6.76% below GeoLDM's 0.0111) and energy above ground state to 0.0042 Eh (15.78% below GeoLDM's 0.0050), while improving validity to 91.40% (+1.60%) and molecule stability to 90.60% (+2.20%). On GEOM (500 molecules, best scale=0.1), the improvements are larger: force RMS drops 14.16% to 0.0411 Eh/Bohr and energy above ground state drops 13.92% to 0.1935 Eh. DFT/B3LYP validation (Table 3) confirms the gains hold at a higher level of theory, with 96.2% validity and 2.542 kcal/mol energy above ground state.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHEMGUIDE consistently outperforms both EDM and GeoLDM on all stability metrics across QM9 and GEOM. On QM9, it reduces force RMS by 6.76% and energy above ground state by 15.78% relative to GeoLDM, while improving validity by 1.60% and molecule stability by 2.20%; the gains over EDM are even larger (e.g., 8.77% lower force RMS, 41.67% lower energy). On GEOM, where molecules are larger and more complex, the advantage widens: force RMS drops 14.16% and energy drops 13.92% vs. GeoLDM. Against the neural-network guidance baseline AIMNet2 (Anstine et al., 2024), CHEMGUIDE achieves substantially lower energy above ground state on both datasets (0.0042 vs. 0.0055 Eh on QM9; 0.1935 vs. 0.2299 Eh on GEOM), while cosine-similarity analysis shows the two guidance gradients are nearly orthogonal, indicating xTB provides a fundamentally different and more effective optimization direction than the learned potential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the guided diffusion framework for 3D molecule generation (EDM, GeoLDM) by replacing the standard differentiable neural-network property predictor with a non-differentiable quantum chemistry oracle (GFN2-xTB), whose guidance gradients are estimated via Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (SPSA). It departs from prior implicit guidance work (Vignac et al., 2022; Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) that always backpropagates through a trained regressor, and from explicit conditional diffusion (Hoogeboom et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2023) that requires labeled retraining. The bilevel optimization formulation connecting property guidance and stability guidance is a new compositional framework, though bilevel optimization itself is a well-studied paradigm in operations research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHEMGUIDE removes the dependency on large labeled datasets for training property predictors, which is a practical bottleneck in molecular design where quantum chemistry labels are expensive to compute. The DFT-level validation (Table 3) confirms that the cheaper xTB oracle still yields molecules closer to ground states than baselines, and the finding that performance gains are larger on bigger molecules (GEOM) suggests relevance to protein and material design. Key limitations include the CPU-bound xTB computation (6–18 hours per 100 molecules), the sensitivity to guidance scale selection, and the fact that SPSA gradient estimation introduces variance that may not vanish at practical perturbation sizes. Open questions include GPU-accelerated xTB, automated scale scheduling, and whether the approach extends to other non-differentiable physics oracles beyond quantum chemistry.
