## Problem

The ℓ2-CPCC regularizer (Zeng et al., 2022) measures inter-class distances using Euclidean centroids, which misrepresents multi-modal class-conditional distributions (e.g., coarse categories like 'household furniture') and fundamentally cannot achieve perfect hierarchical embedding because certain tree metrics are not exactly embeddable in ℓ2 space. This paper addresses both limitations by replacing centroid distances with distributional distances from optimal transport.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252935/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled RGB images and a pre-specified class hierarchy tree (dendrogram with weighted edges) as input. During training, it operates on batch-level class-conditional feature distributions, computing pairwise OT distances between all class pairs within each batch to form the CPCC regularizer. The output is a feature encoder whose representations embed the hierarchical structure, evaluated for both classification (accuracy at coarse/fine levels) and retrieval (MAP) under source-target hierarchical splits that introduce subpopulation shift.

## Method

The core contribution is the OT-CPCC family, which replaces the ℓ2 distance between class centroids in the CPCC regularizer with Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) between full class-conditional distributions, yielding a more fine-grained gradient that assigns different importance to each sample pair rather than treating all points equally. The key algorithmic contribution is Fast FlowTree (FastFT), which achieves linear-time O((m+n)d) computation by augmenting the given label tree with sample leaves (each leaf extended with its class's samples at unit edge weight), making the tree distance between any two cross-class samples identical and reducing the flow computation to a 1D greedy matching problem (Alg.1) without requiring QuadTree construction. Differentiability is handled via Danskin's theorem: gradients flow through the pairwise distance matrix D while the flow matrix P is treated as a constant, avoiding backpropagation through the non-differentiable transport plan. The paper proves EMD-CPCC generalizes ℓ2-CPCC (reducing to it under Gaussian assumptions with shared covariance) and provides a full comparative analysis of forward/backward computation across all OT approximation variants (Sinkhorn, SWD, TWD, FT, FastFT).

## Results

On fine-level classification, OT-CPCC methods consistently outperform ℓ2-CPCC: on CIFAR-100, FastFT achieves 24.71% target accuracy vs. 23.76% for ℓ2-CPCC, and on BREEDS, SWD reaches 76.24% source MAP vs. 62.86% for ℓ2-CPCC. On coarse-level generalization (CIFAR-100), FastFT improves target accuracy from 43.09% (Flat) to 44.29%. In terms of hierarchical interpretability (TestCPCC), FastFT achieves 93.85 on CIFAR-100 vs. 83.08 for ℓ2-CPCC, and 73.20 on iNaturalist vs. 67.01 for ℓ2-CPCC. FastFT runs in O((m+n)d) time, making it the most efficient OT-CPCC variant while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ℓ2-CPCC (Zeng et al., 2022), OT-CPCC methods win consistently on fine-level target accuracy: FastFT improves CIFAR-100 tAcc from 23.76% to 24.71%, and SWD achieves 26.18% (vs. 23.76%). On BREEDS, SWD dramatically outperforms ℓ2-CPCC on source MAP (76.24% vs. 62.86%), while FastFT and Sinkhorn show more modest gains. Against Sinkhorn (Cuturi, 2013), FastFT is strictly faster (linear vs. quadratic time) and matches or exceeds it on most accuracy metrics (e.g., CIFAR-100 tAcc: 24.71% vs. 23.53%), though Sinkhorn occasionally edges ahead on BREEDS tAcc (46.87% vs. 45.75%). Against SWD (Bonneel et al., 2015), FastFT is more consistent across metrics and datasets, while SWD shows higher variance (e.g., CIFAR-100 sMAP 86.82% but sAcc drops to 80.50%). On TestCPCC (hierarchical interpretability), FastFT achieves the best score in 5 out of 7 datasets, confirming that OT-based distances better preserve the label tree structure than Euclidean centroids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Zeng et al. (2022), which introduced the Cophenetic Correlation Coefficient (CPCC) as a regularizer to embed class hierarchy trees into feature space using l2 distances between class centroids. The key conceptual departure is replacing centroid-based l2 distances with Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) and its approximations, motivated by the observation that class-conditional feature distributions are often multi-modal, making single-point centroid representations misleading. Algorithmically, the paper builds on FlowTree (Backurs et al., 2020) for tree-based OT approximation but exploits the known label tree structure to skip QuadTree construction, yielding the linear-time FastFT algorithm whose core primitive is the greedy 1D flow matching of Hoffman (1963). The theoretical contribution shows EMD-CPCC generalizes l2-CPCC (recovering it exactly under Gaussian class-conditional distributions with shared covariance) and provides a Danskin's-theorem-based differentiability analysis for backpropagation through the OT regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled and computationally efficient upgrade to hierarchical representation learning: FastFT-CPCC achieves the optimal O((m+n)d) time complexity, making OT-based CPCC practical for large-scale fine-grained classification where exact EMD would be prohibitively expensive. Empirically, OT-CPCC consistently improves fine-level classification and retrieval while preserving hierarchical structure, with the advantage being most pronounced on datasets with genuinely multi-modal class distributions (CIFAR-100, iNaturalist). A notable limitation is that on less multi-modal datasets (BREEDS E13, E30), OT-CPCC offers little or no advantage over l2-CPCC, suggesting the method's benefit is conditional on distributional complexity. Open questions include whether the greedy flow matching in FastFT has broader applicability beyond the CPCC setting, and how the method scales to very deep hierarchies or non-tree label structures.
