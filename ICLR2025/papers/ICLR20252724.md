## Problem

Prior zero-shot monocular depth methods fail to simultaneously deliver metric depth with absolute scale (without camera intrinsics), sharp high-frequency boundary delineation (hair, fur, vegetation), and sub-second inference at high resolution. Relative-depth methods (Depth Anything, Marigold) lack absolute scale; metric methods (Metric3D, ZoeDepth) require known intrinsics or produce blurry boundaries; high-resolution methods (PatchFusion, Marigold) are orders of magnitude too slow for interactive use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252724/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at any resolution and internally resizes it to a fixed 1536×1536 canvas (2.25 MP), producing a metric depth map in meters at that native resolution plus a horizontal focal length estimate in pixels. No camera intrinsics, EXIF metadata, or domain labels are required as input. The depth is derived from a predicted canonical inverse depth map scaled by the estimated focal length and image width, and the fixed internal resolution guarantees constant runtime regardless of input size.

## Method

Depth Pro applies a plain ViT-L DINOv2 encoder to 384×384 patches extracted at multiple scales from the 1536×1536 canvas, sharing weights across all scales so the encoder learns a scale-invariant representation; a separate image encoder processes the full downsampled image to anchor predictions in global context. Patch features (35 patches total, plus 50 additional intermediate features at the finest scale) are merged into feature maps and decoded by a DPT-style decoder. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 trains on a mix of real and synthetic datasets with MAE loss plus a scale-and-shift-invariant gradient loss on synthetic data to learn robust features; Stage 2 trains exclusively on synthetic data with additional multi-scale first- and second-order derivative losses (MAGE, MALE, MSGE) to sharpen boundaries. A focal length estimation head is trained separately after depth training, ingesting frozen depth-network features and a dedicated ViT encoder, which decouples the two tasks and allows using different training data for each.

## Results

Depth Pro achieves the best average rank (2.5) across six zero-shot metric depth benchmarks using the δ₁ metric, outperforming the next-best method Metric3D v2 (3.7). On boundary accuracy, Depth Pro leads all baselines on every dataset, e.g., achieving 0.409 F1 on Sintel vs. 0.321 for Metric3D v2 and 0.312 for PatchFusion, and 0.173 recall on AM-2k vs. 0.107 for Depth Anything v2. The model produces a 2.25-megapixel depth map in 341 ms on a V100 GPU, making it ~13× faster than Marigold (~4.4 s) and ~246× faster than PatchFusion (~84 s). For focal length estimation, Depth Pro achieves 64.6% δ₂₅% on PPR10K, more than doubling the second-best method SPEC (34.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Metric3D v2 (the strongest metric-depth competitor), Depth Pro wins on 4 of 6 datasets (Middlebury 60.5 vs 29.9, Sintel 40.0 vs 38.3, Sun-RGBD 89.0 vs 75.6, Booster 46.6 vs 39.4) but loses on ETH3D (41.5 vs 87.7) and NuScenes (49.1 vs 82.6); however, Depth Pro dominates on all boundary metrics (e.g., 0.176 vs 0.096 iBims F1) and is ~4× faster (341 ms vs 1300 ms). Against Depth Anything v2, Depth Pro wins on 5 of 6 datasets and all boundary metrics, with a particularly large gap on Sintel (40.0 vs 5.9 δ₁). Against UniDepth, Depth Pro wins on 4 of 6 datasets and all boundary metrics, though UniDepth leads on NuScenes (83.6 vs 49.1) and Sun-RGBD (95.8 vs 89.0). Against PatchFusion, Depth Pro wins on 5 of 6 datasets and all boundary metrics while being ~246× faster (341 ms vs 84 s). Against the diffusion-based Marigold, Depth Pro wins on every boundary metric (e.g., 0.409 vs 0.068 Sintel F1) and is ~13× faster. For focal length estimation, Depth Pro outperforms all three baselines (UniDepth, SPEC, im2pcl) by a wide margin on every dataset, e.g., 69.8% vs 23.2% δ₂₅% on ZOOM and 84.2% vs 51.8% on RAISE.

## Relation to Prior Work

Depth Pro builds directly on the DPT decoder (Ranftl et al., 2021), the mixed-dataset training protocol and scale-and-shift-invariant normalization from MiDaS (Ranftl et al., 2022), and the canonical inverse depth formulation with focal-length scaling from Metric3D (Yin et al., 2023). It departs from prior high-resolution approaches (PatchFusion, ZeroDepth) that use variable-resolution tile-based processing, and from diffusion-based methods (Marigold) that sacrifice speed for sharpness, by instead applying a plain ViT-L DINOv2 encoder at multiple fixed scales with shared weights and fusing via a DPT decoder. The two-stage training curriculum inverts the common synthetic-to-real fine-tuning practice, and the boundary evaluation metrics repurpose matting/segmentation annotations rather than requiring manual edge annotation as in iBims.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Depth Pro provides a practical foundation model for interactive applications (novel view synthesis, image editing) by combining sub-second inference at 2.25MP with metric accuracy and sharp boundaries—three properties that prior methods could not simultaneously achieve. The boundary metrics based on matting and segmentation datasets are a useful evaluation tool that extends beyond the small, manually-annotated iBims benchmark to diverse real-world imagery. The focal length estimation head, trained separately from the depth network, dramatically outperforms prior methods (e.g., 66.9% vs. 14.6% δ25% on DDDP), removing a key practical barrier for in-the-wild use. Limitations include ill-posed depth for translucent surfaces and volumetric scattering, and the fixed 1536×1536 resolution, though the authors note guided super-resolution could be applied post-hoc.
