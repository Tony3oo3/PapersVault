## Problem

Existing SVD-based LLM compression methods (weight truncation, activation-aware SVD like ASVD and SVD-LLM) suffer from severe performance degradation at low compression ratios, with PPL dropping by over 600% at 0.4 ratio. The paper identifies three unresolved challenges: finding per-layer optimal truncation positions in an exponentially large search space, deriving a theoretically optimal weight update after activation truncation, and overcoming the inherent injective (non-bijective) mapping between compression ratio and truncation position that forces unnecessary information loss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-7B), 256 calibration samples (2048 tokens each from WikiText-2 for LLMs, or 660 tokens from TextQA for VLMs), and a target compression ratio. It outputs a compressed model with per-layer updated rank-k weight matrices and learned truncation positions, requiring only 224 trainable parameters (one k per weight matrix) and ~8 GPU-hours of training. The compressed model is hardware-agnostic and can be further combined with post-hoc quantization (e.g., GPTQ-4bit) to reduce memory from 6.8 GB to 1.8 GB at 0.4 ratio.

## Method

Dobi-SVD introduces a three-stage post-training pipeline. First, it makes SVD rank selection differentiable via a tanh-smoothed truncation function T(σᵢ) = σᵢ[0.5·tanh(β(k−i))+0.5], optimizing per-layer k with a multi-objective loss (task PPL + |R_now − R_tar|) while stabilizing SVD backpropagation through Taylor expansion of 1/Eᵢⱼ to prevent gradient explosion when singular values are near-degenerate. Second, it proves via the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem that directly truncating activations (not weights) yields the optimal k-rank approximation, then derives the theoretically optimal updated weight W̃ = WV_GₖVᵀ and computes it memory-efficiently using Incremental PCA (IPCA) over the projected weight set. Third, it establishes a bijection between compression ratio and truncation position by exploiting the near-normal distribution of SVD singular vector matrices: it quantizes the first n rows of UΣ and all rows of Vᵀ to 8-bit, concatenates them, and stores the result in a single max(m,n)×k matrix, reducing storage from k(m+n) to k·max(m,n) and eliminating the forced half-rank truncation of traditional SVD.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B compressed to a 0.4 ratio, Dobi-SVD achieves a PPL of 9.95 on WikiText-2, a 78.5% improvement over the prior SOTA SVD method (SVD-LLM at 53.74) and a 9.83% improvement over the best pruning method (FLAP). At 0.8 ratio, Dobi-SVD reaches PPL 6.08 on WikiText-2 with only a 3.84% average accuracy drop across seven commonsense reasoning tasks, compared to 17.3% for ASVD and 15.4% for SVD-LLM. On a 12GB NVIDIA Titan Xp GPU, the 0.4-compressed model achieves 25.97 tokens/s, a 12.4× speedup over the uncompressed model. Combined with GPTQ-4bit quantization, Dobi-SVD compresses LLaMA-7B to 2.4GB at 0.6 ratio while maintaining PPL 9.97.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two SVD baselines, Dobi-SVD dominates at every compression ratio: at 0.4, its WikiText-2 PPL of 9.95 is roughly 5,700× lower than ASVD (57,057) and 5.4× lower than SVD-LLM (53.74), and its average commonsense accuracy (40%) exceeds ASVD (29%) and SVD-LLM (31%) by 11 and 9 points respectively. Against pruning methods, Dobi-SVD matches or beats FLAP at 0.8 ratio (0% vs 1.64% average accuracy drop) and substantially outperforms LLM-Pruner (0% vs 9.83% drop at 0.8; on Llama2-7B at 0.4, Dobi-SVD PPL is 9.47 vs LLM-Pruner's 253.1). On Llama3.1-8B, Dobi-SVD at 0.6 achieves PPL 8.53 while LLM-Pruner and Wanda-sp reach 44.4 and 58.5, and at 0.4 Dobi-SVD (15.8) remains usable whereas both pruning methods exceed 120. Dobi-SVD also outperforms SliceGPT and Bonsai on Llama2-7B and Llama3.1-8B at 0.6 and 0.4 ratios (e.g., 13.8% vs 18.5–26.2% average drop at 0.6 on Llama2-7B). The only caveat is that Dobi-SVD still shows a 23.1% accuracy drop at 0.4 ratio, so it does not fully eliminate degradation at extreme compression, but no competing SVD or pruning method comes close at that ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the SVD-based LLM compression lineage (ASVD, SVD-LLM) by shifting the truncation target from weight matrices (or activation-scaled weights) to the activations themselves, arguing via the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem that this is the theoretically optimal choice. It builds on the differentiable SVD framework of Wang et al. (2021b) but extends it to general (non-symmetric) matrices and LLM-scale dimensions using Taylor-expansion-based gradient stabilization. The weight reconstruction step repurposes Incremental PCA (Weng et al., 2003) in a novel way to bridge the activation-to-weight gap, and the remapping strategy draws on the quantization-friendly distributional properties of SVD-decomposed matrices to establish a bijection between truncation rank and compression ratio—something no prior SVD method achieved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dobi-SVD is the first SVD-based method to outperform structured pruning (FLAP, LLM-Pruner) at equivalent compression ratios, repositioning SVD as a genuinely competitive compression paradigm rather than a niche alternative. Its hardware-agnosticism (no special quantization hardware required) and extremely low training cost (224 parameters, ~8 GPU-hours) make it attractive for edge deployment, where the 12.4× speedup on a 12GB Titan Xp is practically significant. Open questions include whether the activation-truncation paradigm generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, whether the SVD computation bottleneck (currently fp32-only in Python) can be resolved with low-precision SVD kernels, and whether the remapping strategy degrades gracefully below 4-bit quantization. The extension to VLMs and VLAs (OpenVLA) suggests broader applicability, though the VLA results show only modest 1.17× speedup.
