## Problem

Current LLM safety alignment is 'shallow': it primarily shifts the model's generative distribution over only the first few output tokens (refusal prefixes like 'I cannot'), leaving deeper harmful generation patterns largely intact. This single shortcut explains a wide range of vulnerabilities—adversarial suffix attacks, prefilling attacks, decoding parameter exploits, and fine-tuning attacks—all of which succeed by bypassing or perturbing just those initial tokens. The paper asks whether deepening the alignment beyond the first few tokens can meaningfully improve robustness, and proposes concrete mechanisms to do so.

## Input / Output

The data augmentation approach consumes triplets (harmful instruction x, harmful response h, refusal response r) and benign instruction-response pairs, producing a fine-tuned model that generates safe refusals even when conditioned on up to 100 prefilled harmful tokens. The constrained fine-tuning objective takes a standard downstream fine-tuning dataset (e.g., Samsum, SQL Create Context, GSM8k) and produces a model that adapts to the task while preserving safety alignment. A notable detail is that the safety recovery examples are deliberately incoherent synthetic text (harmful prefix abruptly followed by a refusal), covering outlier trajectories that human labelers would never naturally produce.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary mitigations grounded in the shallow-alignment diagnosis. First, a data augmentation strategy trains the model on 'safety recovery examples'—triplets where a refusal response r is conditioned on a harmful prefix h_{≤k} with k sampled uniformly from [1,100]—pushing the KL divergence between aligned and base models to later token positions and enabling the model to recover from harmful starting conditions. Second, a token-wise constrained fine-tuning loss (Eq. 3) wraps the per-token log-likelihood ratio in a softplus function scaled by position-dependent β_t: large β_t (0.5–2) for the first 5 tokens strongly regularizes those positions toward the aligned model's distribution, while small β_t (0.1) for later tokens permits normal adaptation. The resulting gradient is adaptively down-weighted by 2σ(β_t Δ_t), so positions where the fine-tuned model has already deviated significantly from the aligned model receive diminishing updates, effectively capping distributional drift at the critical early-token positions that drive safety failure.

## Results

The data augmentation approach (safety recovery examples) reduces prefilling attack ASR from 42.1–57.0% to 2.8–4.5%, GCG attack ASR from 36.5–65.6% to 18.4–19.0%, and decoding parameters exploit ASR from 54.9–84.3% to 11.3–1.0% on Llama-2-7B-Chat. The constrained fine-tuning objective (with β₁=0.5, β₂₋₅=2, βₜ>₅=0.1) reduces harmful fine-tuning attack ASR from 88.9% (standard SFT) to 4.6% on Harmful Examples, from 79.5% to 8.1% on Identity Shifting, and from 90.9% to 10.9% on Backdoor Poisoning (with trigger), while preserving downstream utility (e.g., Samsum ROUGE-1: 50.1 vs. 51.7 for standard SFT). Utility benchmarks (AlpacaEval, MMLU, BBH, MATH, GSM8K, HumanEval) show negligible degradation after data augmentation (e.g., MMLU 46.6 vs. 46.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external defense methods but instead contrasts three configurations: the initial aligned model, standard SFT fine-tuning, and the proposed constrained SFT objective. Against standard SFT, the constrained objective dramatically reduces ASR across all three fine-tuning attacks (e.g., 4.6% vs. 88.9% on Harmful Examples for Llama-2; 1.9% vs. 81.6% for Gemma) while maintaining comparable utility on benign tasks (Samsum ROUGE-1: 50.1 vs. 51.7; SQL Create Context: 98.5 vs. 99.1; GSM8k accuracy: 37.4 vs. 41.7 for Llama-2). For the data augmentation approach, the augmented model outperforms the initial model on all three inference-stage attacks: prefilling ASR drops from 42.1–57.0% to 2.8–4.5%, GCG from 36.5–65.6% to 18.4–19.0%, and decoding exploit from 54.9–84.3% to 11.3–1.0%. The augmented model also shows slightly better durability against benign fine-tuning safety regression, though it remains vulnerable to adversarial fine-tuning with harmful data. Utility is preserved with only marginal drops (e.g., AlpacaEval 49.5 vs. 51.8, MMLU 46.6 vs. 46.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the unifying concept of 'shallow safety alignment' to explain a family of previously disparate vulnerabilities (prefilling attacks, GCG adversarial suffixes, decoding parameter exploits, and fine-tuning attacks) under a single mechanistic diagnosis: alignment primarily modifies only the first few output tokens. It builds conceptually on the Superficial Alignment Hypothesis (Zhou et al., 2023) and token-position asymmetries noted by Zhang & Wu (2024) and Lin et al. (2024a), but provides the first systematic KL-divergence evidence tying these effects specifically to safety alignment and connecting them to concrete attack vectors. The two proposed mitigations—a data augmentation with 'safety recovery examples' and a token-wise sigmoid-constrained fine-tuning objective—draw on ideas from DPO/KTO-style regularization and safe reinforcement learning, but are novel in their token-position-biased application to safety preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unifying 'shallow vs. deep' framing gives the LLM safety community a concrete diagnostic and design principle: future alignment should suppress harmful content beyond just the first few tokens, not merely promote refusal prefixes. The constrained fine-tuning objective is directly applicable to production fine-tuning APIs (e.g., OpenAI's), offering a practical defense layer that preserves utility while blocking adversarial fine-tuning. Key limitations include evaluation only on 7B open-source models (Llama-2, Gemma), the data augmentation being a post-hoc fine-tuning rather than an end-to-end alignment change, and the fact that the augmented model remains vulnerable to adversarial fine-tuning on harmful data. Open questions include whether these techniques scale to frontier models, whether adaptive attacks can circumvent the token-wise constraints, and whether deeper alignment can be achieved without the synthetic (incoherent) training examples used here.
