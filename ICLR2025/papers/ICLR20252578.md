## Problem

Existing long-tailed object detection methods assume that category bias stems primarily from imbalanced instance counts, yet models still exhibit significant bias even on relatively balanced datasets like Pascal VOC. This paper identifies that the complexity of intra-category features—quantified as category information amount—better explains per-class learning difficulty than instance count alone, and that current approaches fail to account for this dimension of difficulty when allocating decision space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252578/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images within conventional object detection pipelines (Faster R-CNN, Cascade Mask R-CNN, DETR) and outputs bounding boxes with class labels. A key implementation detail is that category information amount is computed from the classification head's feature embeddings (not the regression branch), and the dynamic update mechanism uses a bounded queue of 50,000 instance embeddings to periodically recompute per-category covariance matrices at the end of each training epoch, avoiding the need to store or re-extract embeddings for the full dataset.

## Method

The paper defines category information amount as the log-determinant volume of each class's perceptual manifold in embedding space, estimated via a Ledoit-Peché nonlinearly shrunk covariance matrix for robustness in high dimensions. Building on this, the IGAM Loss modifies the standard cosine-based classifier by introducing a per-pair angular margin m_ij that is positive when category i has greater normalized information amount than category j, effectively expanding the decision space for harder categories and compressing it for easier ones. To keep the information amount current during training without prohibitive memory cost, the authors propose a queue-based strategy that accumulates local covariance matrices from batches of embeddings and merges them into a global covariance at epoch boundaries, reducing storage by roughly 56–74% compared to storing all embeddings. The loss is drop-in replaceable for cross-entropy in the classification head and is validated across multiple backbones (ResNet-50/101, Swin-T) and detection frameworks.

## Results

On LVIS v1.0, IGAM achieves 26.8% mAP^b and 19.0% AP_r with Faster R-CNN + ResNet-50-FPN, and 28.0% mAP^b and 20.1% AP_r with ResNet-101-FPN, outperforming the cross-entropy baseline by 7.5% and 7.1% in overall mAP respectively. On COCO-LT, IGAM reaches 25.8% mAP^b (ResNet-50) and 27.0% mAP^b (ResNet-101), with the rarest group (AP_1^b) improving to 6.1% and 6.6% versus 0% for the CE baseline. On the relatively balanced Pascal VOC, IGAM achieves 77.7% and 78.6% mAP^b, surpassing the second-best method (BACL) by 0.8% and 1.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LVIS v1.0, IGAM outperforms all 18 compared baselines in overall mAP^b on both backbones. Against the strongest end-to-end competitor EFL (26.0% mAP, 16.6% AP_r), IGAM gains +0.8% mAP and +2.4% AP_r; against C2AM (25.4%, 15.6%), it gains +1.4% mAP and +3.4% AP_r; and against the decoupled BACL (26.1%, 16.0%), it gains +0.7% mAP and +3.0% AP_r. On COCO-LT, IGAM leads EQLv2 (25.7% mAP, 3.8% AP_1) by +0.1% mAP and +2.3% AP_1, and EFL (25.0%, 3.8%) by +0.8% mAP and +2.3% AP_1, while matching or exceeding their AP_4 (frequent) scores. On Pascal VOC, IGAM surpasses BACL (76.8%) by 0.9% mAP and Seesaw (76.9%) by 0.8%, with the largest per-class gains on the hardest categories: bottle (+3.4% over C2AM) and pottedplant (+2.1% over BACL) with ResNet-50. Notably, IGAM does not sacrifice frequent-category performance: its AP_f (31.4%) is within 0.3% of EQLv2 (31.7%) and 0.2% of Seesaw (31.6%), confirming that information-amount-guided margins improve rare classes without degrading head classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decision-boundary adjustment line initiated by C2AM (Wang et al., CVPR 2022), which identified pathological compression of tail-class decision spaces due to weight-norm imbalance and proposed equalizing them. IGAM departs from C2AM's equalization strategy by instead dynamically allocating decision space proportional to a newly defined category information amount, drawing on the perceptual-manifold volume framework from Ma et al. (ICLR 2023) and the Ledoit-Peché covariance shrinkage of Burda & Jarosz (2022) for robust high-dimensional estimation. Conceptually, the paper shifts the explanatory variable for category difficulty from instance count (the standard long-tail framing) to intra-category feature diversity, positioning itself at the intersection of long-tailed detection and the emerging class-difficulty literature (DSB, CR, IDR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that information amount correlates more strongly with per-class AP than instance count does—even on the relatively balanced Pascal VOC—challenges the field's default assumption that rebalancing sample counts is sufficient to eliminate category bias. The method is a drop-in loss replacement requiring no architectural changes or multi-stage training, making it easy to adopt. Open questions include whether the information-amount signal remains informative when computed from a frozen backbone (vs. the end-to-end queue-based update proposed here), whether the approach transfers to detection-free tasks like segmentation or tracking, and whether the modest 1–2% mAP gains over the strongest baselines (EQLv2, Seesaw, BACL) will hold under different training schedules or larger backbones.
