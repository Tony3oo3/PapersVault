## Problem

Existing speculative sampling methods that leverage target LLM hidden states (e.g., EAGLE) suffer from two disharmonies: (1) context misalignment, where the draft model sees perfect target features during training but must rely on its own imperfect features during decoding (a feature-level exposure bias), and (2) objective misalignment, where training distills over the full vocabulary while decoding only benefits from correctly recalling the small set of high-probability tokens that nucleus/top-k sampling actually uses.

## Input / Output

The draft model takes as input the target LLM's second-to-top-layer hidden states concatenated with token embeddings, and produces a sequence of draft tokens organized in a dynamic tree structure (depth 6, 60 total tokens). The target LLM then verifies all draft tokens in a single parallel forward pass, and a modified rejection-sampling scheme accepts a prefix that exactly preserves the target distribution. The method is lossless: it neither fine-tunes the target LLM nor relaxes acceptance conditions, so output quality is identical to vanilla auto-regressive decoding.

## Method

HASS introduces two complementary training strategies on top of the EAGLE-2 architecture. First, Harmonized Objective Distillation replaces full-vocabulary cross-entropy with a Top-K loss (K=10) that only penalizes the draft model on the K most probable tokens under the target distribution, extending ranking distillation from recommender systems to speculative sampling. Second, Harmonized Context Alignment performs n-step (n=3) sequential training where each step feeds the draft model's own previous-step features as query while mixing target and draft features in the key-value positions, with a customized attention mask that ensures each position sees exactly the features it would encounter during auto-regressive decoding. This eliminates the train-decode context gap without adding any inference overhead, at the cost of roughly 3× training FLOPs compared to EAGLE-2.

## Results

HASS achieves 2.81x–4.05x wall-clock time speedup over vanilla auto-regressive decoding, averaging across MT-bench, HumanEval, and GSM8K on LLaMA2-Chat 7/13B and LLaMA3-Instruct 8/70B (NVIDIA H800 GPU). In terms of acceptance length τ, HASS reaches 5.15–5.58 (T=0) and 4.92–5.42 (T=1) across the four target models, surpassing EAGLE-2 by 8%–16%. The largest absolute speedup is 4.05x on LLaMA3-Instruct 70B (T=0, HumanEval), while the smallest is 2.81x on LLaMA3-Instruct 8B (T=1, mean).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HASS consistently outperforms all six baselines across every model, dataset, and temperature setting. Against the closest competitor EAGLE-2, HASS achieves 8%–20% higher speedup ratios (e.g., 3.24x vs. 2.81x on L2-7B T=0; 4.05x vs. 3.37x on L3-70B T=0) and 8%–16% higher acceptance lengths. EAGLE is notably weaker than EAGLE-2, particularly at T=1 where its speedup drops significantly (e.g., 1.76x vs. 2.60x on L2-7B), suggesting EAGLE-2's dynamic draft tree is more robust to sampling stochasticity. Medusa achieves moderate speedups (2.0x–2.7x) but is consistently outperformed by both EAGLE variants. Training-free methods (PLD, Lookahead) and vanilla speculative sampling with small draft models (SpS) yield the weakest results, with speedups below 2.0x in most settings, confirming that a well-aligned learned draft model is essential for effective acceleration.

## Relation to Prior Work

HASS builds directly on the EAGLE/EAGLE-2 architecture (Li et al., 2024b,c), which uses target LLM hidden states as draft model input with a feature regression loss, and on the speculative sampling framework established by Leviathan et al. (2023) and Chen et al. (2023). Its two key departures are: (1) replacing the full-vocabulary cross-entropy distillation with a Top-K ranking distillation loss borrowed from recommender systems (Tang & Wang, 2018), and (2) introducing a multi-step context-aligned training procedure that simulates the draft model's own imperfect features during training, addressing the exposure-bias-like context misalignment that GLIDE (Du et al., 2024) identified but could not fully resolve. The paper positions itself as explicitly linking the training and decoding stages, which prior speculative sampling works treated as separate concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HASS provides a practical, drop-in improvement to the most widely adopted speculative decoding pipeline (EAGLE-2) with no additional inference overhead, making it immediately deployable for production LLM serving. The finding that HASS with only 1/4 of the training data matches EAGLE-2 trained on the full dataset suggests the harmonized objectives extract signal more efficiently, which is valuable when target-model-generated data is expensive to produce. Open questions include whether the reweighting factor β for balancing early vs. late speculation steps has a principled optimum, whether better loss functions than Top-K exist for the harmonized objective, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the LLaMA family to other architectures. The counterintuitive result that token-level alignment (scheduled sampling) hurts performance, while feature-level alignment helps, raises questions about the appropriate level at which to correct exposure bias in draft models.
