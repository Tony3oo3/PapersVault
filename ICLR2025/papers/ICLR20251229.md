## Problem

Contrastive vision-language models like CLIP are architecturally limited to 77 tokens by their fixed absolute positional encodings, preventing them from processing the long, detailed captions increasingly common in modern datasets. Existing work such as Long-CLIP stretches these absolute encodings via interpolation, which removes the hard token cap but still fails to model pairwise token relationships over longer distances and generalizes poorly to unseen sequence lengths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251229/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TULIP takes an RGB image and a text caption of arbitrary length (up to 248 tokens in main experiments, extendable further) and produces a pair of aligned embeddings in a shared contrastive space. The text encoder processes the full caption without truncation, while the vision encoder remains standard ViT-B-16 or ViT-L-14. The model can also be dropped directly into Stable Diffusion XL as a text encoder for text-to-image generation without any additional diffusion-model training.

## Method

TULIP replaces CLIP's absolute positional encodings with Rotary Positional Encodings (RoPE) in every transformer block of the text encoder, enabling length-agnostic position modeling. Training proceeds in two phases: first, a knowledge distillation step where the original CLIP text encoder (teacher) is distilled into the RoPE-equipped student using cosine similarity on 77-token truncated captions for 20 epochs; second, a relative position expansion step where the full model is fine-tuned for one epoch on 1M image-caption pairs using NTK-aware scaled RoPE (α=8.0) to adapt rotational frequencies to the extended context. A combined contrastive loss jointly optimizes short-caption (77-token) and long-caption (248-token) alignment, preserving the original model's short-text capability while unlocking long-caption understanding. This plug-and-play adaptation avoids expensive multi-modal retraining from scratch and generalizes to any CLIP-like model constrained by the 77-token window.

## Results

On the new Long-DCI benchmark (7,000 human-annotated image-caption pairs, ~200 tokens average), TULIP with ViT-L-14 achieves 55.7% Img2Txt R@1 and 56.4% Txt2Img R@1, outperforming Long-CLIP (54.0/46.1) and original CLIP (35.0/37.0). On ShareGPT4V and Urban-1K, TULIP reaches 99.0/99.0 and 90.1/91.1 respectively with ViT-L-14, setting new state-of-the-art across all three long-caption retrieval datasets. For short-caption retrieval on COCO and Flickr30k, TULIP matches or slightly exceeds Long-CLIP (e.g., 56.7 vs 53.4 Img2Txt R@1 on Flickr30k with ViT-L-14) without requiring the tailored first-20-token attention mechanism. In text-to-image generation, a human evaluation with 15 annotators shows SDXL+TULIP winning 89% of pairwise comparisons against SDXL+CLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against original CLIP, TULIP achieves substantial gains on long-caption retrieval: +20.7 points on Long-DCI Img2Txt (55.7 vs 35.0) and +18.4 on Txt2Img (56.4 vs 37.0) with ViT-L-14, confirming that the 77-token truncation severely limits CLIP on dense captions. Against Long-CLIP (the primary long-caption baseline), TULIP wins on all three long-caption datasets for both model sizes, with the largest margin on Long-DCI Txt2Img (+10.3 for ViT-L-14: 56.4 vs 46.1), while Long-CLIP retains a slight edge on short-caption COCO retrieval (62.8 vs 62.6 Img2Txt R@1 for ViT-L-14), attributed to its tailored first-20-token attention. For text-to-image generation, TULIP is compared qualitatively against T5-based models PIXART-Alpha and ELLA; the paper reports that TULIP matches or exceeds them on capturing nuanced details beyond the 77-token boundary, while offering a plug-and-play advantage since no diffusion model retraining is needed. On the DOCCI benchmark (appendix), TULIP further extends its lead with 77.9 Img2Txt R@1 vs 66.5 for Long-CLIP and 65.7 for CLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

TULIP builds directly on CLIP's architecture by replacing its fixed absolute positional encodings with Rotary Positional Encodings (RoPE), a relative encoding scheme well-established in NLP but previously unexplored in contrastive vision-language models. It departs from Long-CLIP, which stretches absolute encodings via interpolation to extend the token window, by fundamentally changing the encoding paradigm rather than merely extending it. The two-step training procedure—knowledge distillation from the original CLIP text encoder followed by NTK-aware scaled RoPE fine-tuning—avoids the expensive multi-modal retraining that a from-scratch architectural change would normally require, making the approach generalizable to any CLIP-like model constrained by the 77-token limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TULIP provides a practical, plug-and-play upgrade path for the widely deployed CLIP ecosystem: swapping the text encoder in Stable Diffusion XL yields improved long-caption generation without any additional diffusion model training. The introduction of Long-DCI, a 7,000-image human-annotated benchmark with ~200-token captions, addresses a real evaluation gap where prior benchmarks were either scene-specific or in-distribution and saturated. Key limitations include reliance on GPT4V-generated ShareGPT4V captions (bounding quality) and observed diminishing returns beyond ~231 tokens, leaving open how far relative encodings can be pushed in contrastive VLMs and whether human-annotated long-caption training data would unlock further gains.
