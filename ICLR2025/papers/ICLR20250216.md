## Problem

Existing algorithmic recourse methods train for validity, proximity, and plausibility separately and then rely on non-robust gradient-based search during inference to balance these conflicting objectives. This disconnect between training and inference leads to poor recourse outputs that are either invalid, implausible, or excessively costly. No prior method jointly optimizes all three recourse criteria during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250216/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a negative instance profile x (a tabular vector with continuous and one-hot categorical features) and a trained classifier h as input, and produces one or more recourse instances x⁺ via autoregressive forward sampling from the generative model. During inference, no gradient descent is performed—recourse is obtained by a single forward pass through the encoder-decoder, sampling each feature sequentially from a kernel density. The user can generate multiple candidates and select the one with highest classifier confidence. Immutable features are enforced through the cost function (infinite cost for changes to immutable attributes).

## Method

GenRe trains a transformer encoder-decoder that models the conditional recourse likelihood R_θ(x⁺|x), where the encoder processes the negative instance and the decoder autoregressively generates each feature of x⁺ using a mixture of RBF kernels with fixed bin centers/widths and learned softmax weights. The key challenge—lack of direct recourse supervision—is solved by synthesizing training pairs from unpaired classification data: for each negative instance x, positive instances are sampled with probability proportional to exp(-λC(x,x⁺)), which is proven to be a self-normalized importance sampling estimator of the ideal recourse density with error converging at rate O(1/N₊). This joint training over cost, plausibility (via the positive-class density), and validity (via the classifier confidence threshold γ) eliminates the need for expensive and non-robust gradient-based search at inference time, replacing it with simple forward sampling that consistently produces plausible, valid, and low-cost recourse across all three evaluation metrics.

## Results

GenRe achieves the best combined Score (Val + LOF − Cost/d, max = 2) across all three datasets: 1.93 on Adult Income, 1.89 on COMPAS, and 1.90 on HELOC. It attains near-perfect validity (≥0.99) and high plausibility (LOF ≥ 0.97) while maintaining low cost (0.51–2.01). The runner-up CRUDS scores 1.72, 1.83, and 1.37 respectively, with substantially higher costs (e.g., 4.30 vs. 2.01 on HELOC). GenRe also outperforms pre-trained diffusion models (TabSyn+SVDD) on both cost and LOF across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenRe outperforms all eight prior recourse baselines on the combined Score metric across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor CRUDS (a VAE-based plausibility method), GenRe reduces cost by 3.6× on Adult Income (0.69 vs. 3.17) and 2.1× on HELOC (2.01 vs. 4.30) while matching or exceeding validity and LOF. Cost-minimizing methods (Wachter, GS, DICE) achieve lower raw cost but suffer validity near 0.5 because they push instances to the classifier boundary; robust methods (ROAR, PROBE) achieve high validity but produce extreme costs (10–34) and near-zero LOF, indicating implausible outliers. TAP, which adds a verifier model, improves validity over ROAR/PROBE but still trails GenRe by 0.3–1.0 Score points. Against pre-trained diffusion models (TabSyn+SVDD), GenRe achieves 4.5× lower cost on Adult Income (0.69 vs. 3.11) and higher LOF (0.98 vs. 0.83), while TabSyn+SVDD also fails immutability constraints in many cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenRe departs from the dominant paradigm in algorithmic recourse where methods (cost-minimizing, robust, or plausibility-seeking) train components separately and then perform gradient-based or random search over a joint objective at inference time. Instead, it trains a single autoregressive transformer to model the conditional recourse likelihood R(x⁺|x) jointly over validity, proximity, and plausibility, reducing inference to a simple forward sampling step. The training supervision is synthesized via an importance-sampling-based pairing strategy over unpaired classification data, a technique the paper proves consistent at rate O(1/N₊). Conceptually, the work repurposes the autoregressive kernel-density parameterization and the CARLA benchmarking framework, but the joint-training-plus-sampling formulation is novel relative to all three prior method families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the disconnect between training and inference in prior recourse methods is a fundamental source of suboptimal recommendations, and that a generative model trained jointly on all three objectives can achieve near-optimal trade-offs (scores approaching the theoretical maximum of 2.0) while remaining robust to the cost-magnitude hyperparameter λ. Open questions include scalability to very high-dimensional or truly black-box settings where even the classifier h is unavailable, extension to multi-class or ordinal outcomes, and whether the importance-sampling pairing strategy degrades when the positive class is extremely small. The reliance on a trained classifier h during pair construction (for the validity indicator V) limits applicability in fully opaque deployment scenarios.
