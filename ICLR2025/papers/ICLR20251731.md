## Problem

Existing self-supervised pre-training methods for motion prediction are tightly coupled to specific model architectures (e.g., requiring explicit map embeddings for masked autoencoder reconstruction) and are trained on a single dataset, limiting their scalability and generalizability. The driving trajectory domain operates in a 'small-data regime' (320K–480K sequences) far below NLP/CV scales, yet no unified SSL framework has been shown to work across diverse architectures and multiple datasets simultaneously.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method samples a single scenario with horizon T = T_h + T_f and extracts two non-overlapping sub-scenarios at different temporal offsets; each sub-scenario provides historical trajectories of all agents plus HD map context, standardized to a fixed map resolution and zero-padded to a common length across datasets. The pre-training outputs are (a) trajectory embeddings for contrastive learning and (b) reconstructed trajectories of the other sub-scenario via a 2-layer MLP decoder. After pre-training, the encoder is fine-tuned on the standard motion prediction task, producing up to 6 multimodal future trajectories with associated probabilities.

## Method

SmartPretrain combines two trajectory-focused pretext tasks within an online/momentum dual-branch architecture (EMA momentum 0.996→1.0 cosine schedule). Trajectory Contrastive Learning (TCL) pulls together embeddings of the same agent across two temporal windows while repelling embeddings from different agents or different sub-scenarios, using a cosine-similarity InfoNCE loss with temperature τ. Trajectory Reconstruction Learning (TRL) feeds the embedding of sub-scenario t through a trajectory decoder to reconstruct the trajectories of sub-scenario t′, using an L1 loss that generalizes the downstream regression objective. Both tasks operate exclusively on trajectory embeddings (never map or custom embeddings), removing architectural constraints and enabling plug-and-play application to raster, transformer, or graph-based backbones. A dataset-agnostic sampling strategy standardizes map resolution, filters incomplete trajectories, and mixes all available training splits without per-dataset balancing, maximizing data volume and diversity for pre-training.

## Results

SmartPretrain consistently improves all four tested prediction models (HiVT, HPNet, QCNet, Forecast-MAE) across both Argoverse and Argoverse 2. The largest gains appear on Argoverse 2: QCNet reduces minFDE by 4.9%, minADE by 3.3%, and MR by 7.6% (validation), while Forecast-MAE reduces MR by 10.6% (validation) and 8.5% (test). Pre-training on all three datasets (Argoverse, Argoverse 2, WOMD) yields further improvements over single-dataset pre-training, e.g., HiVT on Argoverse drops from -2.0% to -2.9% minFDE and -3.9% to -6.3% MR when scaling from one to all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only direct head-to-head pre-training comparison is against Forecast-MAE's own masked-autoencoder pre-training strategy, applied to the same Forecast-MAE backbone on Argoverse 2. SmartPretrain substantially outperforms Forecast-MAE's pre-training on all three metrics: minFDE improves by 4.5% vs. 1.9%, minADE by 3.1% vs. 1.2%, and MR by 10.6% vs. 6.2% (all relative to the no-pre-training baseline of 1.436 / 0.811 / 0.189). The paper notes that Forecast-MAE was the only existing pre-training method with open-source code available at submission time, limiting broader comparisons. Beyond this direct comparison, SmartPretrain's model-agnostic design is demonstrated by consistently improving four architecturally diverse backbones (HiVT, HPNet, QCNet, Forecast-MAE), whereas prior SSL methods are typically restricted to a single architecture or require explicit map embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SmartPretrain builds on the momentum-contrast / BYOL dual-branch architecture (He et al., 2020; Caron et al., 2021) for self-supervised representation learning, adapting the online/momentum branch, projector, and predictor design to the motion prediction domain. It departs from prior SSL-for-motion-prediction work (e.g., Forecast-MAE, PreTraM, Traj-MAE) by restricting pretext tasks to trajectory embeddings only, thereby avoiding the architectural constraints of masked-reconstruction methods (which require explicit per-token or per-segment features) and the representation constraints of contrastive methods (which have been limited to rasterized maps). The dataset-agnostic sampling strategy further distinguishes it from all prior pre-training pipelines, which operate on a single dataset due to format incompatibilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SmartPretrain provides the first unified, model-agnostic and dataset-agnostic SSL pre-training framework for motion prediction, directly addressing the 'small-data regime' that has constrained the field. The consistent 2–10% improvements across four diverse backbones (HiVT, HPNet, QCNet, Forecast-MAE) and two downstream datasets demonstrate broad applicability, making it a practical tool for practitioners seeking to boost any existing predictor. A notable open question is whether the gains saturate as more datasets or longer pre-training are added (diminishing returns are observed), and whether the framework extends to end-to-end driving or planning tasks. The finding that supervised pre-training on additional datasets actually degrades performance (Appendix A.1) suggests SSL objectives learn more transferable features than task-specific ones, an insight with broader implications for multi-dataset learning.
