## Problem

Existing visual-tactile datasets target low-level tasks like material classification or tactile localization, and prior works leveraging human manipulation data for robotic pretraining rely solely on vision and language, neglecting the tactile modality. No dataset or benchmark exists for studying how human visual-tactile priors transfer to complex multi-fingered dexterous manipulation, where finger coordination and contact-rich interactions are essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253261/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes ego-centric RGB images (224×224, center-cropped from 420×240), binary contact signals from 20 piezoresistive sensors (thresholded at 0.01 N in simulation or 0.2 V in hardware, reducing continuous force to a 20-dimensional 0/1 vector), and proprioceptive joint angles and velocities (24 joints each). The output is a 20-DoF action vector for the Shadow Hand (four tendon-driven joints immobilized) and, during pretraining, a fused visual-tactile CLS token of dimension 384 that serves as the perceptual representation for the downstream policy network.

## Method

VT-JointPretrain adapts the MAE masking-reconstruction framework to jointly process two modalities: the RGB image is split into 196 patches (16×16) and the tactile signal into 20 patches, each projected to a 384-d embedding with positional encodings. Patches are randomly masked at modality-specific ratios (75% visual, 50% tactile), and a transformer encoder fuses the visible patches with a learnable CLS token; a separate transformer decoder reconstructs the masked patches, trained with weighted MSE loss (λ_v=1, λ_c=10 to upweight the sparse tactile signal). The frozen CLS token is then concatenated with proprioception and fed to a PPO policy trained in Isaac Gym across six dexterous tasks. The key finding is that joint masking of both modalities during pretraining outperforms separately pretrained visual and tactile encoders concatenated at inference, and that even binary, sparse tactile signals boost success rates by ~20% over vision-only, with an additional ~20% gain from joint pretraining.

## Results

Across six dexterous manipulation tasks, VT-JointPretrain achieves a mean success rate of 74.3% (seen) and 65.7% (unseen), outperforming the best visual-pretrained baseline V-CLIP+T (65.4%/55.9%) by ~9% and the non-pretrained tactile-only baseline T (50.8%/47.0%) by ~24%. The method also exhibits the lowest variance across tasks and random seeds (±0.6% seen, ±0.7% unseen), indicating substantially improved consistency. On the most challenging tasks (Lever Sliding, Bimanual Hand-over), VT-JointPretrain achieves 89.3%/79.6% and 45.5%/26.6% respectively, where the Base (proprioception-only) method scores only 5.8%/2.2% and 8.0%/3.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VT-JointPretrain outperforms all five visual-pretrained baselines (MVP, Voltron, R3M, CLIP, ResNet18) and their tactile-augmented variants by a wide margin. The strongest competitor is V-CLIP+T (65.4% seen / 55.9% unseen), which still trails VT-JointPretrain by 8.9% and 9.8% respectively; the gap widens to over 30% against V-MVP+T (38.5%/35.3%) and V-R3M+T (38.9%/31.0%). Simply concatenating separately pretrained visual and tactile features (V-Pretrain+T-Pretrain, 62.6%/53.3%) underperforms joint pretraining by 11.7%/12.4%, confirming that the MAE-style masking-reconstruction fusion is critical rather than mere feature concatenation. Among non-pretrained baselines, tactile-only (T) substantially outperforms vision-only (V) and vision+tactile (V+T), suggesting that raw visual features without pretraining are poorly suited for dexterous manipulation, while even untrained tactile signals carry useful contact information. The paper further shows that VT-JointPretrain's advantage is most pronounced on tasks with significant visual occlusion (Lever Sliding: 89.3% vs. 53.1% for T-Pretrain) and on unseen objects, where the gap over V-CLIP+T reaches 25.7% (Lever Sliding unseen: 79.6% vs. 53.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) visual-tactile datasets for perception tasks (Touch and Go, SSVTP, TaRF, PHYSICLEAR), which it extends from material/texture classification to complex multi-fingered manipulation; (2) human-video pretraining for robot manipulation (MVP, R3M, Voltron, CLIP), which it augments with a tactile modality previously absent from these pipelines; and (3) task-specific visual-tactile RL for dexterous hands (Guzey et al., Qi et al.), which it generalizes via a shared pretrained representation. Architecturally, the VT-JointPretrain method adapts the MAE masked-reconstruction paradigm (He et al. 2022; Radosavovic et al. 2023) to a multi-modal setting where binary tactile patches are jointly masked and reconstructed with image patches, departing from prior work that either concatenates modality features post-hoc or trains each modality independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale (565k frames, 182 objects, 10 tasks) visual-tactile dataset purpose-built for complex dexterous manipulation, filling a gap where prior visual-tactile data targeted perception rather than control. The robustness analyses (threshold invariance across 20× force ranges, noise tolerance via hysteresis, viewpoint insensitivity) offer concrete deployment guidance that is often missing from simulation-only papers. Open questions remain: how to exploit continuous pressure and shear signals beyond binary contact, whether tactile information can be distilled into purely visual policies to reduce hardware cost, and how to handle the sim-to-real gap for high-fidelity tactile simulation. The asymmetric transfer finding (tactile benefits vision but not vice versa) suggests the discrete nature of contact signals poses a fundamental challenge for cross-modal knowledge transfer.
