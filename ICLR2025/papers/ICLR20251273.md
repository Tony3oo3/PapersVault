## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting training is confined to a single GPU, capping the number of Gaussians (e.g., ~11.2M on an A100 40GB) well below the quality saturation point for high-resolution, large-scale scenes like 4K Rubble. Existing distributed DNN training frameworks assume regular dense tensor operations and balanced workloads, making them unsuitable for 3DGS's dynamic, spatially-local, and inherently imbalanced computation pipeline.

## Input / Output

Grendel consumes an initial point cloud and a set of posed training images (ranging from ~1K to 4K resolution, up to 5,620 images for MatrixCity) and produces a distributed set of 3D Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics) totaling up to 40M+ Gaussians across multiple GPUs. Unlike standard 3DGS which processes one view per iteration, Grendel batches multiple views simultaneously (up to batch size 64), partitioning both the batch and pixel dimensions across GPUs to maintain utilization.

## Method

Grendel exploits 3DGS's mixed parallelism by using Gaussian-wise distribution (each GPU transforms a disjoint subset of Gaussians) for the transformation step and pixel-wise distribution (each GPU renders a contiguous block of pixels) for rendering and loss computation. Transitions between these stages use sparse all-to-all communication that transfers only the small subset of Gaussians intersecting each GPU's pixel partition, leveraging the spatial locality of 3DGS. An iterative load balancer reassigns 16×16 pixel blocks across GPUs based on measured per-block rendering times from previous epochs to handle dynamic workload shifts. For batched training, the paper derives a sqrt(batch_size) learning rate scaling rule and an exponential momentum scaling rule (β′ = β^batch_size) from the Independent Gradients Hypothesis, enabling stable large-batch optimization without manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

On the 4K Rubble dataset, Grendel achieves a test PSNR of 27.28 using 40.4 million Gaussians distributed across 16 GPUs, compared to 26.28 with 11.2 million Gaussians on a single GPU. Throughput scales from 5.55 images/s (4 GPUs, batch size 1) to 38.03 images/s (32 GPUs, batch size 64). On the smaller Train scene, 16 GPUs with batch size 32 complete 30K training images in 2 minutes 43 seconds. Across 13 scenes from Mip-NeRF360, Tanks & Temples, and DeepBlending, 4 GPUs with batch size 4 yield a 3×–4× speedup with no PSNR degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is CityGaussian (Liu et al., 2024), a divide-and-conquer approach that partitions large scenes into cells and trains each independently. Grendel 200K-images outperforms CityGS Official on PSNR for Rubble (27.39 vs. 25.88) and Building (22.69 vs. 22.14), while slightly trailing on MatrixCity Block_All (27.33 vs. 27.41). Grendel achieves 3×–6.7× faster training times across all three scenes (e.g., 0.85 hrs vs. 2.88 hrs on Rubble; 1.22 hrs vs. 8.25 hrs on MatrixCity). Against the matched-budget CityGS 200K-images baseline, Grendel wins on every metric for all three scenes, with the largest gap on MatrixCity Block_All (PSNR 27.33 vs. 23.68, LPIPS 0.205 vs. 0.422). The paper also notes that Grendel is simpler to use, requiring no multi-stage pipeline or per-cell hyperparameter tuning unlike CityGaussian.

## Relation to Prior Work

Grendel builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its rendering and optimization backbone, but departs from the algorithmic scaling strategies (divide-and-conquer in CityGaussian/VastGaussian, level-of-detail in Hierarchical Gaussian/Octree-GS) by treating multi-GPU training as a systems problem. It draws conceptual inspiration from distributed DNN training frameworks like FSDP and Megatron-LM, yet must fundamentally rework their assumptions because 3DGS is not a neural network: its mixed parallelism (Gaussian-wise for transformation, pixel-wise for rendering) and sparse, view-dependent communication patterns are incompatible with dense all-gather or data-parallel designs. The hyperparameter scaling rules adapt ideas from large-batch SGD/Adam literature (Goyal et al., Malladi et al., Busbridge et al.) to 3DGS's non-layered, non-dense computation structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Grendel removes the single-GPU memory ceiling that has constrained 3DGS to scenes well below the quality saturation point, enabling 4K reconstruction of large-scale scenes (e.g., 40M Gaussians on the Rubble scene) that were previously infeasible. The sqrt(batch_size) learning-rate scaling rule is a simple, tuning-free recipe that practitioners can adopt immediately for multi-GPU 3DGS training, and the open-source implementation lowers the barrier to entry. Open questions include how Grendel's system-level parallelism composes with algorithmic innovations (hierarchical representations, LOD rendering), whether the independent gradients hypothesis holds at batch sizes beyond 64 or for very different scene types, and how the approach extends to heterogeneous or cloud-scale hardware beyond NVLink-connected A100 nodes.
