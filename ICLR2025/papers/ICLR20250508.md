## Problem

Existing equivariant diffusion models for crystal generation either learn atom types, coordinates, and lattice parameters in separate stages (e.g., VAE for lattice/types + score network for coordinates) or assume atom types are given, failing to capture their joint distribution end-to-end. Moreover, these models rely solely on local message passing and are unconditional, offering no mechanism for users to specify desired material properties such as chemical formula, space group, or formation energy.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form textual description of a target material as input, ranging from detailed Robocrystallographer-generated paragraphs (encoding coordination geometry, bond lengths, space group, mineral type) to shorter expert prompts (chemical formula, constituent elements, crystal system, space group number, and generic property signs like positive/negative formation energy). The output is a complete crystal unit cell: a one-hot atom type matrix, a fractional coordinate matrix in the quotient space R^{N×3}/Z^{N×3}, and a 3×3 lattice matrix, all generated jointly from noise in a single diffusion process.

## Method

TGDMat performs joint equivariant diffusion over three heterogeneous components simultaneously: the lattice matrix via DDPM (Gaussian noise in continuous space), atom types via D3PM with an absorbing [MASK] state (discrete categorical diffusion), and fractional coordinates via score matching with Wrapped Normal noise on the periodic torus. The denoising backbone is a periodic-E(3)-equivariant GNN (an extension of CSPNet built on EGNN) that uses Fourier features on relative fractional distances for periodic-translation invariance. A frozen MatSciBERT encoder produces a [CLS] contextual embedding C_p from the text prompt, which is concatenated with atom embeddings and sinusoidal timestep encoding at the input layer and propagated through K layers of equivariant graph convolution to steer all three denoising heads (lattice L2 prediction, type categorical prediction, and coordinate score prediction). Training minimizes a weighted sum of the three component losses, and sampling uses a Predictor-Corrector scheme, requiring only ~500 steps versus 1K–5K for baselines.

## Results

On the Crystal Structure Prediction (CSP) task with just 1 generated sample per test material, TGDMat(Long) achieves Match Rates of 90.46% (Perov-5), 44.63% (Carbon-24), and 55.15% (MP-20), outperforming the strongest baseline DiffCSP (52.02%, 17.54%, 51.49%) by large margins, especially on Perov-5 (+38.44 pp). On the Random Material Generation (Gen) task, TGDMat(Long) achieves the best Property Statistics (EMD) across all three datasets (e.g., 0.0090 for #Element on Perov-5 vs. 0.0119 for DiffCSP+) and the highest compositional validity on MP-20 (92.97% vs. 88.47% for SyMat+). TGDMat also reduces sampling steps from 5K (CDVAE) or 1K (DiffCSP) to just 500, while requiring only 500 training epochs vs. 3K–4K for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGDMat outperforms all four baselines (P-cG-SchNet, CDVAE, DiffCSP, SyMat) on both CSP and Gen tasks. On CSP with 1 sample, TGDMat(Long) leads DiffCSP by +38.44 pp Match Rate on Perov-5 (90.46 vs. 52.02) and +27.09 pp on Carbon-24 (44.63 vs. 17.54), while also achieving lower RMSE across all datasets. On Gen, TGDMat(Long) surpasses DiffCSP+ (the text-fusion variant of the closest baseline) on Property Statistics EMD for #Element (0.0090 vs. 0.0119 on Perov-5; 0.043 vs. 0.0763 on Carbon-24; 0.2890 vs. 0.3122 on MP-20) and on compositional validity for MP-20 (92.97% vs. 85.07%). TGDMat also beats SyMat+ on coverage and property metrics for Perov-5 and Carbon-24. The only metric where TGDMat does not clearly lead is COV-P on Carbon-24 (92.43 vs. 97.63 for SyMat+), and compositional validity on Perov-5 where it is on par with CDVAE (98.63 vs. 98.59). Overall, the joint diffusion + text-guidance paradigm consistently yields the best or near-best results across all 7 Gen metrics and both CSP metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

TGDMat extends the equivariant diffusion framework for crystal generation (CDVAE, SyMat, DiffCSP) by introducing two key departures: (1) a joint diffusion paradigm that simultaneously denoises atom types (via D3PM absorbing-state diffusion), fractional coordinates (via score matching with Wrapped Normal noise), and lattice (via DDPM) within a single periodic-E(3)-equivariant GNN, rather than the sequential VAE-then-diffusion pipeline used by CDVAE and SyMat; and (2) text-guided conditioning through a frozen MatSciBERT encoder whose [CLS] embedding is injected into input atom features at every denoising step. The backbone denoising network is an extension of CSPNet (Jiao et al., 2023), which itself was designed for the narrower CSP task where atom types are given. The text-guided diffusion concept is borrowed from image/molecule generation (DALL-E, TGM-DLM) but is applied here for the first time to periodic crystal structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that textual structural knowledge (formula, space group, bond lengths, formation energy) can serve as a strong inductive bias that both improves generation quality and reduces the number of denoising steps needed (500 vs. 1K–5K for baselines), making text-guided crystal generation practically viable. The finding that a single generated sample from TGDMat outperforms 20 samples from unconditional baselines on CSP is practically significant for high-throughput screening. Key limitations include reliance on a frozen MatSciBERT encoder with a rigid prompt format, the absence of an independent text-only evaluation dataset, and performance degradation on the more complex MP-20 dataset. Open questions include whether fine-tuned LLMs can replace MatSciBERT to handle free-form expert prompts, and whether the joint diffusion paradigm generalizes to larger, more diverse material databases.
