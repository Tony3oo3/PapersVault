## Problem

Existing MARL methods assume a fixed number of entities between training and inference, overlooking real-world scenarios where agents, adversaries, or obstacles dynamically enter or leave mid-trajectory (e.g., search-and-rescue, combat). When the inference-time entity composition exceeds anything seen during training (zero-shot OOD), prior methods that expand Q-networks with new parameters suffer severe reward degradation and high uncertainty, making them unreliable for safety-critical deployment.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a flat state-vector observation containing per-entity features (type one-hot, 2D position, 2D velocity) for all currently active entities, plus its own last action (5-dim one-hot). The critical constraint is that at inference the observation dimensionality can exceed the trained dimensionality because new entities appear intra-trajectory; FLICKERFUSION resolves this by stochastically masking entities so the effective input always matches the in-domain size N^train. Output is a discrete action per agent from a 5-way set {no_action, move_left, move_right, move_down, move_up}, chosen greedily from the Q-network and executed fully decentralized.

## Method

FLICKERFUSION is a parameter-free, plug-in input augmentation that stochastically drops entities from each agent's observation at every timestep, keeping the observation matrix row-count equal to the in-domain size. During training, both the entity composition and the number of entities to drop are randomized per episode and per step, so the network learns to act on partial views. During inference, the method computes the excess entities per type (N^inf − N^train) and each agent independently and uniformly samples that many entities of each type to mask, ensuring no new parameters θ_Q' are needed regardless of how many OOD entities appear. The key insight—borrowed from the physiological flicker-fusion phenomenon—is that because the dropped set changes stochastically at each timestep, aggregating the partial observations over time reconstructs a virtually complete view, while the per-step stochasticity also allows immediate on-the-fly adaptation to intra-trajectory dynamics. This mechanism is backbone-agnostic, attaching identically to both QMIX-MLP and QMIX-Attention, and incurs only ~4–9% additional training time with reduced memory since no extra parameters are introduced.

## Results

On the 12 MPEv2 benchmarks (6 environments × 2 OOD settings), FLICKERFUSION achieves the best mean final inference reward in 10 out of 12 benchmarks and ranks at least second in all others. For example, on Repel OOD2, FLICKERFUSION-MLP reaches 1189.0 ± 48.6 reward vs. 1016.5 for QMIX-MLP and 1008.9 for QMIX-Attention; on Guard OOD2, FLICKERFUSION-MLP achieves −962.9 ± 49.1 vs. −1161.0 (QMIX-MLP) and −1356.2 (QMIX-Attention). FLICKERFUSION also uniquely reduces inference uncertainty relative to its backbone (lowest median across all methods in the box-and-whisker analysis), with a negative correlation between reward and uncertainty observed across methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLICKERFUSION (both MLP and Attention variants) outperforms all 11 baselines in 10 of 12 benchmarks, with the sole exception being Adversary OOD2 where ACORM (12.6) edges out FLICKERFUSION-Attention (6.9), though FLICKERFUSION-MLP (8.8) still beats QMIX-Attention (−4.0). Against the closest MARL-specific competitors, FLICKERFUSION-MLP dominates CAMA on Guard (−967.2 vs. −4328.6 IQM) and ODIS on Repel (1204.6 vs. −3291.3), while ACORM is competitive only on Adversary OOD2. The model-agnostic domain generalization methods (MLDG, SMLDG, DG-MAML, Meta DotProd) perform worst overall, with Meta DotProd and MLDG producing catastrophic rewards (e.g., −12447.5 on Repel OOD1), confirming they are ill-suited for MARL. Relative to its own backbone, FLICKERFUSION improves reward in 22 of 24 benchmark-backbone pairs and is the only method that reduces inference uncertainty (standard deviation) below the backbone level, making it the most robust OOD MARL approach tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in dynamic team composition MARL (Iqbal et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2021; Shao et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023a), which handles entity count changes by expanding Q-networks with inductive biases (e.g., attention tokenizers, policy decoupling). FLICKERFUSION instead takes an orthogonal input-augmentation approach: stochastically dropping entities from observations to keep the input dimensionality constant, drawing on the dropout mechanism (Srivastava et al., 2014) but applied specifically to the entity/observation layer. The method is built on QMIX value decomposition (Rashid et al., 2018) and is positioned as a universal plug-in compatible with both MLP and attention backbones, contrasting with prior methods that require architecture-specific modifications. The paper also introduces MPEv2, a 12-benchmark suite extending Multi Particle Environments (Lowe et al., 2017) to support intra-trajectory entity dynamics, analogous to how SMACv2 (Ellis et al., 2023) extended SMAC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap—entities entering or leaving mid-trajectory under zero-shot OOD conditions—that is common in search-and-rescue and defense scenarios but previously unstudied. The finding that a parameter-free stochastic input dropout outperforms carefully designed network expansion methods challenges the assumption that architectural inductive bias is necessary for OOD MARL, and the unique uncertainty reduction relative to the backbone is relevant for safety-critical deployment. Limitations include evaluation only on 2D MPE-based environments with 5 discrete actions and small entity counts (≤7), and the method is demonstrated only with off-policy value decomposition (QMIX); scalability to on-policy methods, continuous action spaces, and larger-scale environments (e.g., SMAC) remains open.
