## Problem

Physical adversarial attacks on person detectors have low success rates because human bodies are non-rigid: clothing distorts and poses change continuously, breaking attacks designed for rigid objects or static poses. Prior methods either add a single patch to a T-shirt (failing under viewpoint change) or use static 3D models that cannot simulate diverse human actions, leaving a gap in attacks that remain effective across unseen poses and movements.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short monocular video of the target person (to train a UV-Volume model via SPIN pose estimation and DensePose pseudo-labels), 100 background images from indoor/outdoor scenes, and sampled SMPL pose, camera (azimuth ±180°, elevation 0–30°), and light parameters. It produces a 256×256 adversarial texture patch optimized in Stable Diffusion latent space, which is printed on fabric (long sleeves and pants) for physical deployment, and rendered adversarial human images across novel poses and viewpoints for digital evaluation.

## Method

UV-Attack decouples the dynamic NeRF shape model (UV-Volume) from texture representation: instead of modifying NeRF parameters, it renders IUV maps (part assignments + UV coordinates) and separately optimizes a view-independent texture stack in the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model. The adversarial patch is generated by contaminating the initial noisy latent z_T and removing classifier-free guidance, which expands the perturbation space beyond what prior diffusion-based attacks achieve. A novel Expectation over Pose Transformation (EoPT) loss minimizes detection confidence in expectation over GMM-sampled SMPL poses, camera positions, light configurations, and backgrounds, making the attack robust to unseen actions. Optimization combines Particle Swarm Optimization (30 epochs, 50 swarms) for coarse search followed by Adam fine-tuning (300 epochs), and Thin Plate Spline warps the texture to simulate cloth distortion during rendering.

## Results

On the unseen-pose multi-pose dataset (1000 GMM-sampled poses), UV-Attack (GMM) achieves 92.75% ASR on FastRCNN and 90.40% ASR on YOLOv3 at IoU threshold 0.5, dramatically outperforming the best baseline AdvCamou (28.50% and 24.00% respectively). In transferability tests (Table 2), the attack trained on FastRCNN reaches 49.50% ASR on YOLOv8 and 93.50% on MaskRCNN, while the YOLOv3-trained variant achieves 58.55% on YOLOv8. In physical-world free-pose settings, UV-Attack attains 85% ASR versus AdvCamou's 25%, and maintains ASRs of 0.92–0.98 across indoor and outdoor scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UV-Attack outperforms all eight baselines by large margins across every evaluation setting. Against the strongest prior method AdvCamou (Hu et al., 2023), UV-Attack achieves 92.75% vs. 28.50% ASR on FastRCNN (IoU 0.5) and 90.40% vs. 24.00% on YOLOv3 — a gap of over 60 percentage points. The diffusion-based attacks Diff2Conf (Lin et al., 2023) and DiffPGD (Xue et al., 2023) perform poorly in this multi-pose setting (14.25% and 13.40% on FastRCNN), confirming that their limited noise levels are insufficient for physical attacks with pose variation. Patch-based methods (AdvYolo, AdvTshirt, NatPatch) all remain below 17% ASR, as they cannot account for non-rigid body deformations. The most recent SOTA, DAP (Guesmi et al., 2024), achieves only 38% ASR on unseen real-world pose sequences from ZJU-Mocap, compared to UV-Attack's 79% average. In transferability (Table 2), UV-Attack trained on FastRCNN reaches 49.50% ASR on YOLOv8, while all baselines stay below 26% on that detector, and the YOLOv3-trained variant further improves to 58.55% on YOLOv8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on UV-Volume (Chen et al., 2023) for real-time editable human rendering and on latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022) for adversarial patch generation, but departs from both in key ways. Unlike prior person-detection attacks (AdvTshirt, AdvTexture, AdvCamou) that relied on static 3D meshes or single-patch assumptions, UV-Attack is the first to combine dynamic NeRF with UV mapping to handle non-rigid pose deformations. It also diverges from prior diffusion-based adversarial methods (Diff2Conf, DiffPGD) by contaminating the initial high-noise latent z_T rather than intermediate latents, and by removing classifier-free guidance to explore a broader latent space. The EoPT loss, which takes expectation over sampled pose, camera, and lighting parameters, is a novel formulation not present in prior physical-attack literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a fundamental gap in physical adversarial attacks: the inability of prior methods to handle dynamic, non-rigid human motion. The demonstrated 85% physical ASR in free-pose settings (vs. 25% for AdvCamou) and 49.5% black-box ASR on YOLOv8 have direct security implications for surveillance and autonomous systems. Limitations include a 20–35% ASR drop on unseen subjects, reduced effectiveness against architecturally different detectors (SSD, Co-DETR-SWIN), and the requirement for a short video of the target to train the UV-Volume. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other non-rigid objects (animals, deformable structures), how defenses might be designed against pose-averaged adversarial textures, and whether the technique scales to multi-person scenes.
