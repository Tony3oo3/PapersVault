## Problem

Existing self-supervised physics-informed neural motion planners (e.g., NTFields, P-NTFields) solve the Eikonal equation but fail to preserve its dual nature as both an optimal value function and a geodesic distance, leading to spurious local minima in complex, cluttered environments and an inability to generalize to unseen scenes. The paper addresses this by enforcing Bellman optimality at finite time scales and constraining the solution within a metric space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a start configuration q_s, goal configuration q_g, and an environment represented as a point cloud X_obs (processed by a PointNext encoder into a global latent feature). At inference, a sampling-based MPC uses the learned travel-time field T(q_s, q_g) as a cost-to-go function, sampling actions from a zero-mean Gaussian and selecting the lowest-cost rollout over a fixed horizon, eliminating the need for gradient computation at runtime. The output is a collision-free trajectory in configuration space (2–12 DOF) along with the learned travel-time field.

## Method

The core contribution combines temporal difference (TD) learning with metric learning to solve the Eikonal equation more accurately. The TD loss L_TD enforces Bellman's principle of optimality over a finite step Δt, complementing the infinitesimal Eikonal loss L_E to avoid local overfitting of the tangent landscape; an obstacle-aware normal alignment loss L_N and an exponential causality weight L_C further stabilize training from random initialization. For the network architecture, the travel time is defined as D(f_θ(q_s), f_θ(q_g)) where D is a novel L_1×L_∞ distance over an a×b latent space, preserving non-negativity, symmetry, and the triangle inequality while supporting multiple shortest paths in multi-connected regions. An attention mechanism conditions the feature encoder on the environment point cloud (via PointNext global features as keys/values and random positional encodings as queries), enabling generalization to unseen environments. At inference, a sampling-based MPC replaces gradient descent, improving both speed and robustness to poorly learned regions.

## Results

On the Gibson 3D home-like environments (7–16 rooms), the proposed method achieves 95.1% success rate with 0.056 s average planning time, outperforming prior self-supervised methods NTF (68.1% SR) and P-NTF (81.2% SR) by 27 and 14 percentage points respectively, while being 13× faster than FMM (0.74 s) and 38× faster than RRTC (2.14 s). On the C3D cluttered 3D benchmark, it reaches 99.4% SR at 0.025 s, and on the 7-DOF manipulator task it achieves 87.0% SR at 0.074 s. In the 12-DOF dual-arm real-world cabinet task, the method attains 91% SR with ~0.09 s planning time, where NTF and P-NTF fail to converge entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior self-supervised baselines NTF and P-NTF, the proposed method consistently wins on success rate across all three benchmarks: 95.1% vs. 68.1%/81.2% on Gibson, 99.4% vs. 93.8%/93.4% on C3D, and 87.0% vs. 74.3%/73.3% on the 7-DOF manipulator, while maintaining comparable or lower inference times. Against classical planners (FMM, RRTC, L-PRM), the method trades a small SR gap (95.1% vs. 99.7% for RRTC on Gibson) for 13–38× speedup in planning time, and on C3D it actually matches or exceeds their SR (99.4% vs. 100%) at 5–30× lower cost. Against supervised methods MPNet and MPiNet, the method achieves higher SR on C3D (99.4% vs. 97.0%) at 9× lower time, and on the 7-DOF task it is slightly below MPiNet (87.0% vs. 91.0%) but 8× faster (0.074 s vs. 0.57 s) without requiring any expert demonstrations. In the 2D maze ablation, the proposed L∞-L1 distance metric achieves 0.08 mean absolute error to the FMM ground truth, substantially outperforming IQE (0.32), PQE (0.46), MRN (0.19), and Deep Norm (0.29), confirming that existing RL metric formulations fail to capture the Eikonal solution's geodesic structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTFields/P-NTFields line of physics-informed neural motion planners, which solve the Eikonal equation via a factorized Euclidean distortion of travel time. The key departures are threefold: (1) introducing a temporal difference loss that enforces Bellman optimality at finite time scales to complement the infinitesimal Eikonal loss, (2) replacing the factorized parameterization with a metric-learning architecture using a combined L1×L∞ distance to preserve geodesic properties (triangle inequality, symmetry, non-negativity), and (3) adding an attention-based environment encoder (PointNext + positional encoding) to enable generalization to unseen environments. The causality-preserving weight is borrowed from Wang et al. (2024b) for training physics-informed networks, and the sampling-based MPC inference draws from model-predictive path integral control literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that prior self-supervised Eikonal solvers fundamentally fail in complex, cluttered, and high-DOF environments because they violate the geodesic and Bellman-optimal structure of the solution, and that explicitly enforcing both properties (via complementary losses and metric-constrained architecture) yields substantially more accurate value functions. The ability to generalize to unseen environments via point-cloud conditioning is a practical advance over prior methods that required retraining per environment. However, the authors acknowledge that generalization to complex Gibson-scale environments remains imperfect, and extending to partial observability and kinodynamic constraints is left as future work. The L1×L∞ metric outperforming more sophisticated RL distance metrics (IQE, PQE, MRN, Deep Norm) suggests that the specific geometric structure of Eikonal solutions may not be well-captured by general-purpose quasimetric learning.
