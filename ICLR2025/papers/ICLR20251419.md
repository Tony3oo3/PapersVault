## Problem

Graph Neural ODEs (GNODEs) model node representations as continuous trajectories over time but provide no mechanism to quantify uncertainty, limiting their applicability in safety-critical settings. This paper addresses the gap by asking: can we extend the continuous-depth GNODE framework to simultaneously capture both epistemic (model) and aleatoric (data) uncertainty in graph-structured data, with theoretical guarantees that the resulting uncertainty estimates are meaningful and stable?

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,E) with a node feature matrix X_in ∈ R^{n×d} as initial conditions H(0) for a stochastic process, and produces a posterior predictive distribution over node class labels via Monte Carlo sampling (N trajectories) from the learned posterior SDE. Unlike standard GNNs that output a single point prediction, LGNSDE outputs a full distribution whose variance and entropy serve as calibrated uncertainty estimates. The model depth is not fixed a priori but is determined dynamically by the number of SDE solver steps, with memory complexity O(1) via the stochastic adjoint sensitivity method.

## Method

LGNSDE defines a Bayesian prior-posterior pair of SDEs on the latent node representation H(t): the prior is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with constant drift and diffusion (hyperparameters), while the posterior replaces the drift with a GCN parameterized by learned weights φ, keeping the diffusion identical to the prior to bound the KL divergence. Training proceeds via variational inference, maximizing an ELBO where the expectation is approximated by integrating the posterior SDE with a Stochastic Runge-Kutta solver. Brownian motion in the diffusion term captures aleatoric uncertainty, while the Bayesian prior-posterior gap captures epistemic uncertainty. Two theoretical results are proven: (1) the variance of the latent state upper-bounds the output variance via the Lipschitz constant of the readout layer, ensuring meaningful uncertainty estimates; and (2) the expected deviation between solutions under small input perturbations grows at most exponentially with rate (L_f + ½L_g²)t, guaranteeing robustness.

## Results

In the standard node-classification setting (Table 1), LGNSDE achieves the best AUROC on Computers (0.9691) and Photo (0.9909), the best AURC on Computers (0.0687) and Photo (0.0186), and the best accuracy on Computers (82.47%) and Photo (91.69%). On Cora, GNODE slightly outperforms LGNSDE on AUROC (0.9664 vs. 0.9659) and AURC (0.0628 vs. 0.0702), while on Pubmed the plain GCN retains a marginal AUROC edge (0.9006 vs. 0.9004). The Graph GP baseline fails with out-of-memory errors on three of five datasets (Computers, Photo, Pubmed), and BGCN degrades severely on Pubmed (AUROC 0.7030, accuracy 45.81%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the plain GCN, LGNSDE wins on Computers (AUROC 0.9691 vs. 0.9680; accuracy 82.47% vs. 80.98%) and Photo (AUROC 0.9909 vs. 0.9905; accuracy 91.69% vs. 91.16%), while essentially tying on Cora and Pubmed. Compared to GNODE, LGNSDE shows a clear advantage on the larger graphs—Computers AUROC improves from 0.9569 to 0.9691 and AURC drops from 0.1055 to 0.0687—but GNODE retains a slight edge on Cora (AUROC 0.9664 vs. 0.9659; AURC 0.0628 vs. 0.0702). LGNSDE dominates BGCN across every dataset, with the gap most dramatic on Pubmed (AUROC 0.9004 vs. 0.7030; accuracy 75.89% vs. 45.81%). Against the GCN ensemble, LGNSDE wins on Computers (AUROC 0.9691 vs. 0.9669; AURC 0.0687 vs. 0.0877) and Photo (AURC 0.0186 vs. 0.0244; accuracy 91.69% vs. 90.70%) but trails on Citeseer AUROC (0.9111 vs. 0.9181). The Graph GP baseline is infeasible on three of five datasets due to OOM, and where it runs (Cora, Citeseer) it is substantially worse (e.g., Cora AUROC 0.8970 vs. 0.9659). In the extended OOD-detection benchmarks (Appendix Tables 13–14), LGNSDE is competitive with the strongest baseline GNSD (Lin et al., 2024), which achieves slightly higher AUROC on Cora label-leave-out (94.76 vs. 93.14) and feature-perturbation (91.77 vs. 90.61), while LGNSDE matches or exceeds GNSD on AUPR-out and DET-ACC in several settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

LGNSDE directly extends GNODE (Poli et al., 2019) by replacing the deterministic ODE governing continuous-depth node evolution with a stochastic differential equation, and borrows the Bayesian prior-posterior SDE framework from infinitely deep Bayesian neural networks (Xu et al., 2022; Li et al., 2020) to quantify epistemic uncertainty via an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck prior and a GCN-parameterized posterior. It departs from the earlier Graph Neural SDE proposal of Poli et al. (2021), which used finite-dimensional KL divergences and prior distributions rather than prior processes, and from Lin et al. (2024), which models uncertainty through SPDEs with Q-Wiener processes rather than a Bayesian latent-space formulation. The key conceptual move is unifying aleatoric uncertainty (Brownian motion in the diffusion term) and epistemic uncertainty (posterior drift learned by a GNN) within a single continuous-depth graph architecture, accompanied by variance-bound and perturbation-robustness theorems that prior GNN uncertainty methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantees—output variance bounded by latent variance and exponential bounds on perturbation deviation—provide a principled foundation for trusting the uncertainty estimates, which is a gap in most existing GNN uncertainty methods (BGCN, ensembles, Graph GPs). However, the empirical gains over strong baselines are modest (LGNSDE is typically best or second-best with small margins), and the computational cost is notably higher (~6× GCN, ~3× GNODE per epoch), limiting immediate practical adoption. Open questions include whether adaptive SDE solvers can reduce the step count without sacrificing accuracy, how the framework scales to very large graphs (the Graph GP baseline already OOMs on Computers/Photo/Pubmed), and whether the theoretical bounds are tight enough to be useful in practice for calibration-sensitive applications.
