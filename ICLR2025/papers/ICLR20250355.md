## Problem

Existing scientific LLM benchmarks (GPQA, MMLU, PubMedQA) rely on short-sequence, multiple-choice questions that do not reflect how scientists actually work, while long-context benchmarks (ZeroScrolls, Bamboo, RULER) operate on non-scientific or synthetic data. Real scientific problem-solving requires synthesizing information dispersed across full-length research papers while applying deep domain expertise — a capability no prior benchmark tests. CURIE addresses this gap with a set of realistic, expert-annotated scientific tasks demanding long-context comprehension, multi-step reasoning, and cross-domain knowledge.

## Input / Output

Each task takes a full-length scientific paper (averaging ~15k words) as context, except BIOGR (map image + caption) and PDB (3D protein coordinates stripped of all functional annotations). Outputs are heterogeneous and task-specific: structured JSON/YAML for information extraction (DFT parameters, material properties, geospatial datasets, error-correcting code entries), executable Python code for DFT reproduction, latitude/longitude bounding boxes for map georeferencing, amino acid sequences for protein reconstruction, and LaTeX Hamiltonian expressions for Hartree-Fock derivations. Ground truth outputs average ~954 words and mix structured fields, equations, code, and free text in a single response.

## Method

CURIE comprises 10 tasks across 6 scientific disciplines (materials science, condensed matter physics, quantum computing, geospatial analysis, biodiversity, proteins), totaling 580 problem-solution pairs curated from 429 research papers by domain experts with PhDs. The tasks span seven assessment categories: concept extraction, concept tracking, aggregation, algebraic manipulation, summarization, visual comprehension, and cross-domain expertise. For evaluation, the paper proposes two model-based metrics: LLMSim, which uses an LLM with chain-of-thought prompting to match predicted dictionary elements against ground truth and compute precision/recall/F1 on structured extractions, and LMScore, a 3-point weighted score derived from LLM log-probabilities over 'good/okay/bad' judgments. Eight state-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3 Opus, Gemini 1.5/2.0, Mixtral, Command-R+, LongLLaMa) are evaluated zero-shot, with ROUGE-L and BERTScore-F1 as standard metrics alongside the proposed ones.

## Results

On the CURIE benchmark (10 tasks, 580 problems across 6 scientific disciplines), Claude-3 Opus achieves the best overall performance with consistent high scores across all domains, while Gemini 2.0 Flash leads on PDB (0.56 IDr) and BIOGR (0.49 IoU). The best average performance across all 10 tasks is approximately 32%, indicating substantial room for improvement. GPT-4o performs well on GEO (27.30 ROUGE-L) and HFE (48.93 ROUGE-L) but fails dramatically on PDB (0.13 IDr) due to repetitive sequence generation. Among open-weight models, Command-R+ outperforms Mixtral and LongLLaMa on most tasks, particularly HFE (41.23 vs. 9.15 and 10.33 ROUGE-L).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Claude-3 Opus (Anthropic) is identified as the best overall model with consistent high performance across all six scientific disciplines, particularly excelling on DFT-S (40.45 LLMSim-F1) and MPV (41.35 LLMSim-F1) retrieval tasks. Gemini 2.0 Flash (Google) achieves the highest scores on PDB (0.56 IDr, generating correct Python code ~50% of the time) and BIOGR (0.49 IoU), as well as the best HFE score (50.06 ROUGE-L). GPT-4o (OpenAI) performs competitively on GEO (27.30) and HFE (48.93) but collapses on PDB (0.13 IDr) due to repetitive subsequence generation, and underperforms on HFD (9.81) due to formatting failures. Among open-weight models, Command-R+ (Cohere) substantially outperforms Mixtral (Jiang et al., 2024) and LongLLaMa (Tworkowski et al., 2024), particularly on HFE (41.23 vs. 9.15 and 10.33) and DFT (13.79 vs. 12.20 and 8.17 ROUGE-L). The paper notes that given the high variability (25%–75% error bars), differences among the top closed models (Claude-3, Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o) are not statistically significant, and all models show considerable room for improvement, with the best average score reaching only ~32%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate benchmark lineages: science-domain evaluation (GPQA, QASPER, QASA, MMLU) which operates on short inputs and multiple-choice answers, and long-context evaluation (ZeroScrolls, Bamboo, RULER, Ada-Leval) which uses non-scientific or synthetic data. CURIE is the first benchmark to combine deep scientific domain expertise across six disciplines with full-length paper inputs (~15k words average) and a diverse set of task types including algebraic derivation, code generation, visual georeferencing, and 3D-to-sequence reconstruction. The proposed model-based evaluation metrics (LLMSim for structured dictionary matching, LMScore for weighted 3-point scoring) extend the LLM-as-judge paradigm to handle heterogeneous mixed-format outputs that resist standard ROUGE/BERTScore evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With the best model (Claude-3 Opus) achieving only ~32% average performance, CURIE establishes a clear ceiling that current LLMs cannot reach on realistic scientific workflows, providing a concrete target for future model development. The model-based evaluation methodology (LLMSim, LMScore) addresses a practical gap in automatically scoring complex structured outputs (JSON, YAML, code, equations) that are common in scientific NLP but poorly served by existing metrics. Key open questions include whether retrieval-augmented or agentic multi-step approaches can close the performance gap, whether the evaluation metrics generalize beyond the tested domains, and how to establish human performance baselines given the multi-disciplinary expertise required. Limitations include the narrow domain coverage (6 fields), zero-shot-only evaluation, and the absence of a human expert performance reference point.
