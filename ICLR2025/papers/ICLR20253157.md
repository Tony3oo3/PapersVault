## Problem

Universal dexterous grasping across thousands of diverse objects remains difficult because existing RL-based multi-task approaches (UniDexGrasp, UniDexGrasp++) rely on complex curriculum designs or numerous specialist policies, leading to long training times, gradient interference, and a ~7% generalization gap on unseen objects. The paper asks how to directly learn a single multi-task dexterous grasping policy that is both training-efficient and generalizes without degradation to unseen objects and categories.

## Input / Output

The state-based hyper-policy consumes robot proprioception (123-dim vector covering wrist pose, 18 DoF joint angles, fingertip states and forces), the object's 3D position and quaternion, a 64-dim PointNet geometry code, and the previous action; the geometry-agnostic base policies receive only proprioception and the object's 3D position (no geometry, no orientation). The vision-based student replaces the pose and geometry code with an N×3 point cloud from cameras. The output is a 22-dim target joint-angle vector plus a 6D force applied at the wrist, and the hyper-policy additionally outputs a k-dim weight vector for the MoE gating.

## Method

ResDex trains geometry-agnostic base policies (one per K-Means cluster center) using only proprioception and object position with a pose-only reward that avoids leaking object geometry, yielding policies that generalize broadly despite being trained on a single object. A hyper-policy is then trained via multi-task PPO across all 3,200 objects, outputting both a residual action and normalized MoE weights; the final action is the residual plus a weighted sum of the frozen base policies' actions (Eq. 4). A two-stage reward schedule first uses the full grasping-proposal reward to encourage natural poses, then removes it to maximize success rate. The state-based hyper-policy is finally distilled into a vision-based policy via DAgger. This residual-plus-MoE design decouples exploration (handled by the generalizable base policies) from refinement (handled by the residual), avoiding the gradient interference that plagues direct multi-task RL.

## Results

On DexGraspNet (3,200 training objects), ResDex's vision-based policy achieves an 88.8% success rate on the training set, with only a 0.3% drop to 88.5% on unseen objects in seen categories and a 1.6% drop to 87.2% on unseen objects in unseen categories. The state-based hyper-policy (stage-2) reaches 94.6% on training objects and 95.4% on unseen categories. Training completes in approximately 12 hours on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, compared to ≥3 multi-task training rounds for UniDexGrasp and ≥20 for UniDexGrasp++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResDex outperforms UniDexGrasp++ by 3.4%, 8.9%, and 10.5% on the training, unseen-object/seen-category, and unseen-category test sets respectively (vision-based policy). Against UniDexGrasp, the margins are even larger: +15.1%, +19.9%, and +22.1% on the same three splits. The most striking difference is generalization: ResDex shows virtually no gap between training (88.8%) and test performance (87.2–88.5%), whereas UniDexGrasp++ degrades by 5.8–8.7 percentage points and UniDexGrasp by 8.6–15.1 percentage points. In the state-based comparison (Table 1), ResDex stage-2 leads UniDexGrasp++ by 6.7% (train), 10.1% (unseen obj/seen cat), and 12.3% (unseen cat). Both baselines rely on complex curriculum or generalist-specialist training pipelines requiring multiple rounds, while ResDex trains a single multi-task hyper-policy in one pass with 1–6 lightweight single-object base policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResDex builds directly on the residual policy learning paradigm (Silver et al., 2018; Johannink et al., 2019), repurposing it for multi-task dexterous grasping where a geometry-agnostic base policy provides exploration bias and a residual hyper-policy refines actions. It departs from the curriculum-learning approach of UniDexGrasp (Xu et al., 2023) and the generalist-specialist pipeline of UniDexGrasp++ (Wan et al., 2023) by replacing iterative multi-round training with a single multi-task RL stage over frozen base policies. The geometry-agnostic design draws on the blind-grasping insight from Agarwal et al. (2023), while the MoE structure adapts the classical mixture-of-experts framework (Jacobs et al., 1991) to diversify grasping poses across object clusters identified via PointNet-based K-Means.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero generalization gap (consistent ~90%+ success on both seen and unseen objects/categories) is a meaningful step toward truly universal grasping policies, and the 12-hour single-GPU training time makes large-scale dexterous policy learning far more accessible than the multi-round pipelines of prior work. However, the method has not been deployed on hardware, does not address functional grasping (only lifting), and fails on extreme object geometries (very thin or very large items). Open questions include whether the geometry-agnostic principle transfers to more complex manipulation tasks, how the approach scales to bimanual or multi-object settings, and whether the sim-to-real gap can be closed without additional adaptation.
