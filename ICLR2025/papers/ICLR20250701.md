## Problem

Existing guided diffusion-model generation methods either require differentiable objective functions, pre-collected offline datasets for training a guidance model, or expensive online fine-tuning of model parameters. For real-world target generation tasks—such as image generation with human preferences or molecular design for drug discovery—the objective is a black box, no offline dataset exists, and each query evaluation is costly, making query efficiency critical. This paper asks whether a pre-trained diffusion model can be guided at inference time to produce target data using only online black-box feedback while minimizing the number of expensive queries.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (for image generation) or a protein receptor structure (for molecule generation) as conditioning input, along with a black-box objective function (e.g., Gemini 1.5 LLM scoring prompt-image alignment on a 1–5 scale, or AutoDock Vina estimating binding affinity in kcal/mol). It produces a batch of 32 high-resolution 1024×1024 images or 3D molecular structures per iteration, constrained to a total budget of 50 batch queries. No model parameters are updated; only the noise sequence fed to the pre-trained diffusion sampler is optimized at inference time, and the method supports any stochastic diffusion scheduler (EDM, DDIM).

## Method

The core technique is Guided Noise Sequence Optimization (GNSO), which updates the entire noise sequence {ε₀,…,ε_K} along a universal direction x* − x_K (from the current generated output toward a target) rather than along per-step directions x* − x_k, keeping updates on the data manifold and preventing quality degradation even when the target is noisy. Fast Direct extends GNSO to the black-box setting by constructing a pseudo-target x̂* on the data manifold—either via a one-step gradient descent on a Gaussian Process surrogate (using shift-invariant kernels whose gradients provably lie in a low-dimensional subspace spanned by collected data, per Proposition 1) or via the historical best-scoring sample—and then applying GNSO to steer generation toward x̂*. The algorithm iterates: generate a batch, query the black-box objective, update the pseudo-target model, and re-run GNSO, requiring no parameter updates and a single tunable hyperparameter (step size α). This yields 6×–10× query-efficiency gains on image tasks and 11×–44× on molecule tasks compared to RL-based fine-tuning baselines (DDPO, DPOK, D3PO) and direct noise optimization (DNO).

## Results

On 12 high-resolution (1024×1024) image-prompt alignment tasks, Fast Direct achieves a near-perfect Gemini rating (≈5/5) within a 50 batch-query budget, requiring on average only 20 batch queries to surpass DDPO (granted 200 queries), 23 for DPOK, and 30 for D3PO — yielding 6×–10× batch-query-efficiency gains. On 6 3D-molecule drug-discovery tasks (minimizing AutoDock Vina score), Fast Direct requires only 7.5, 4.5, and 18 batch queries on average to outperform DDPO, DPOK, and D3PO respectively, achieving 11×–44× efficiency gains. The method is sampler-invariant (comparable scores with both EDM and DDIM) and insensitive to step-size and batch-size hyperparameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fast Direct consistently outperforms all four baselines across both image and molecule tasks. Against DDPO (RL fine-tuning with PPO), Fast Direct achieves a 10× average efficiency gain on images and 26.67× on molecules, meaning it reaches the same objective quality in roughly one-tenth the queries. Against DPOK (PPO + KL regularization), the gains are 8.70× on images and 44.44× on molecules — the largest gap in the paper. Against D3PO (DPO-based preference fine-tuning), gains are 6.67× on images and 11.11× on molecules. DNO (zeroth-order noise optimization) is not included in Table 1 because it is a per-instance method producing only one image per run; the authors note it requires 16× more total queries (800 vs. 50) and still fails to reach the target on several tasks even with an expanded 500-query budget. Notably, when baselines are granted an extra 200-query budget, DDPO and DPOK nearly match Fast Direct on images, while D3PO and DNO remain unsuccessful, underscoring the structural advantage of Fast Direct's inference-time, parameter-free approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new niche at the intersection of inference-time diffusion guidance and black-box optimization. It departs from differentiable-objective guidance methods (universal guidance, manifold-preserving guidance) by eliminating the gradient requirement, and from RL-based fine-tuning (DDPO, DPOK, D3PO) by avoiding any parameter updates. The core mechanism—updating the noise sequence along a universal direction x* − x_K on the data manifold (GNSO)—is conceptually related to DNO's noise optimization but is repurposed as a backbone for iterative pseudo-target guidance rather than single-instance optimization. The GP-based pseudo-target construction borrows from Bayesian optimization but is specifically designed so that the gradient lies on a low-dimensional subspace (Proposition 1), making it compatible with high-dimensional diffusion latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world target generation (human preference alignment, drug discovery) inherently involves expensive black-box evaluations with no offline data, yet prior methods either require differentiability, pre-collected datasets, or costly parameter updates. The 6×–44× query-efficiency gains over RL-based baselines make iterative human-in-the-loop or lab-in-the-loop workflows substantially more affordable. Open questions include: (1) why the universal direction x* − x_K is so robust to noisy targets (the paper acknowledges this is empirical and calls for theoretical understanding), (2) whether the GP surrogate scales beyond the tested latent dimensions, and (3) how the method behaves when the target lies far outside the diffusion model's learned distribution.
