## Problem

Rectified flow (e.g., InstaFlow) is widely believed to require three coupled components—linear flow-matching form, v-prediction, and reflow—to straighten ODE paths for few-step generation, forcing practitioners to convert pretrained diffusion models (like Stable Diffusion's ε-prediction DDPM form) before retraining. This paper argues that the essential ingredient is simply retraining with pre-collected noise-sample pairs from a pretrained model, and that the true training target is a first-order approximate ODE path (which can be inherently curved for DDPM, Sub-VP, etc.), not straightness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251129/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a 512×512 (SD v1-5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL) RGB image in 1–25 inference steps. Crucially, the pretrained diffusion model's architecture, noise scheduler, prediction type (ε-prediction for SD), and inference code remain entirely unchanged; the only modification is that training data consists of deterministic noise–sample pairs generated by the pretrained model rather than independently sampled Gaussian noise and real images. This means no conversion to v-prediction or flow-matching form is needed, and the model retains full flexibility for multi-step refinement at inference time.

## Method

Rectified Diffusion generalizes rectification to any diffusion form x_t = α_t x_0 + σ_t ε by replacing randomly paired (x_0, ε) in standard diffusion training with pre-collected deterministic pairs (x̂_0, ε) obtained by running the pretrained model. The theoretical target is a first-order approximate ODE path, proven equivalent to the condition that ε-predictions remain constant along each trajectory (Theorem 1), which for flow-matching reduces to straight lines but is inherently curved for DDPM and Sub-VP. Two extensions are proposed: (1) Rectified Diffusion (Phased), which segments the ODE into M sub-intervals and enforces first-order property locally within each segment, reducing training cost while capping minimum steps at M; and (2) replacing InstaFlow's naive distillation with consistency distillation, which converges faster because the rectified ODE already approximates the first-order target, achieving better 1-step FID at ~3% of the GPU days used by InstaFlow's distillation stage.

## Results

On COCO-2017, Rectified Diffusion achieves FID 27.26 at 1 step and FID 22.98 at 2 steps, dramatically outperforming Rectified Flow (FID 47.91 at 1 step, FID 31.35 at 2 steps). With consistency distillation, Rectified Diffusion (CD) reaches FID 22.83 at 1 step and FID 21.43 at 4 steps, surpassing Rectified Flow (Distill) which degrades to FID 73.49 at 2 steps and FID 103.48 at 4 steps. The phased variant achieves FID 20.64 at 4 steps on COCO-2017 (FID 10.21 on COCO-2014 30k), outperforming PeRFlow (FID 22.97 / 18.59) and approaching the 25-step upper bound of FID 20.1. Training cost is ~20 A800 GPU days for rectification and 4.6 A800 GPU days for distillation, roughly 3% of InstaFlow's distillation cost (110 A100 GPU days).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rectified Diffusion decisively outperforms Rectified Flow (InstaFlow) at every step count: at 1 step, FID drops from 47.91 to 27.26 (a 43% reduction), and at 2 steps from 31.35 to 22.98. Against the distilled variant, Rectified Diffusion (CD) achieves FID 22.83 at 1 step vs. 23.72 for Rectified Flow (Distill), and the gap widens dramatically at higher steps where Rectified Flow (Distill) collapses (FID 73.49 at 2 steps, 103.48 at 4 steps) while Rectified Diffusion (CD) remains stable (FID 21.66 and 21.43). In the phased setting, Rectified Diffusion (Phased) beats PeRFlow by 2.33 FID on COCO-2017 (20.64 vs. 22.97) and by 1.25 FID on SDXL (25.81 vs. 27.06), while also achieving higher CLIP scores. On COCO-2014 30k, the phased variant reaches FID 10.21 at 4 steps, approaching the 25-step DPM-Solver upper bound of 9.78, whereas PeRFlow only reaches 18.59. Across all comparisons, Rectified Diffusion also requires substantially less training: ~20 A800 GPU days vs. 75.2 A100 GPU days for rectification, and 4.6 A800 GPU days vs. 110 A100 GPU days for distillation (~3% of the cost).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on rectified flow (Liu et al., 2022) and its text-to-image instantiation InstaFlow (Liu et al., 2023), as well as the phased rectification approach of PeRFlow (Yan et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is the argument that rectification's effectiveness stems from retraining with pre-computed noise-sample pairs rather than from the specific flow-matching parameterization and v-prediction that prior works treat as prerequisites. By reframing the training target from 'straightness' to 'first-order approximate ODE path,' the paper generalizes rectification to arbitrary diffusion forms (DDPM, EDM, Sub-VP) without requiring model conversion, and incorporates consistency distillation (Song et al., 2023) as a more efficient post-rectification refinement step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical simplification is significant: practitioners can apply rectification to any pretrained diffusion model without converting prediction types or noise schedulers, reducing both implementation complexity and training cost (8% of InstaFlow's training images for superior 1-step FID; 3% of its distillation GPU days). The conceptual reframing—that first-order approximate ODE path, not straightness, is the correct target—provides a clearer theoretical lens for understanding why few-step generation works and opens the door to applying rectification to diffusion variants previously considered incompatible. A remaining limitation is that even with rectification plus consistency distillation, 1-step performance still trails the best GAN-based and distribution-matching distillation methods (e.g., DMDv2, SiD-LSG), suggesting that rectification alone may not close the gap to the absolute state of the art in ultra-low-step regimes.
