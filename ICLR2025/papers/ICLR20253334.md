## Problem

All prior algorithms achieving the optimal O(T^((1-p)/(3p-2))) convergence rate for nonconvex stochastic optimization under heavy-tailed noise (finite p-th moment, p ∈ (1,2]) rely on gradient clipping, and all assume the tail index p is known a priori to tune hyperparameters. This paper asks whether clipping is truly necessary and whether convergence can be guaranteed when p is unknown, which is the common practical scenario.

## Input / Output

The method takes a differentiable nonconvex objective F: R^d → R, a batch of unbiased stochastic gradient estimates whose noise has only a finite p-th moment, and an initial point x_1 ∈ R^d. It produces an approximate stationary point with a guaranteed rate on the average gradient norm: O(T^((1-p)/(3p-2))) when p is known and O(T^((1-p)/(2p))) when p is unknown. The analysis extends to generalized (L_0, L_1)-smooth objectives and a relaxed noise condition where the p-th moment can scale with the gradient norm (σ_1 > 0).

## Method

The paper revisits Batched NSGDM, which differs from standard momentum SGD only by dividing the momentum vector by its ℓ_2 norm before the update. The key insight is that normalization naturally bounds the first moment of the accumulated error E[||ε_t||], which stays finite under heavy-tailed noise (p > 1), whereas clipping is needed to control the second moment E[||ε_t||²] that may be infinite. The technical core is a novel expected inequality for vector-valued martingale difference sequences, proved via the regret analysis of AdaGrad (inspired by Rakhlin & Sridharan, 2017), bounding E[||Σv_t||] in terms of E[(Σ||v_t||^p)^(1/p)] for p ∈ [1,2]. For the unknown-p setting, the algorithm simply uses stepsize and momentum tuned as if p = 2, which surprisingly suffices to guarantee convergence without any knowledge of the actual tail index. A matching lower bound with tight dependence on Δ_1, L_0, and σ_0 confirms the optimality of the analysis.

## Results

The paper establishes that Batched NSGDM achieves the optimal O(T^((1-p)/(3p-2))) expected convergence rate for smooth nonconvex objectives under heavy-tailed noises (finite p-th moment, p ∈ (1,2]) without gradient clipping (Theorem 3.2). When the tail index p is unknown, the same algorithm achieves the first provable O(T^((1-p)/(2p))) rate (Theorem 3.6). A refined lower bound (Theorem 3.3) of Ω(Δ₁L₀σ₀^(p/(p-1)) ε^(-(3p-2)/(p-1))) matches the upper bound's dependence on problem-dependent parameters (Δ₁, L₀, σ₀), confirming optimality beyond just the T-dependence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary theoretical comparison is against Clipped SGD and its variants (Zhang et al. 2020c; Cutkosky & Mehta 2021; Nguyen et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2024), all of which require gradient clipping and a known tail index p to achieve the O(T^((1-p)/(3p-2))) rate. Batched NSGDM matches this optimal rate without clipping when p is known, and additionally provides the first convergence guarantee (O(T^((1-p)/(2p)))) when p is unknown — a setting where all prior Clipped SGD results become vacuous since the clipping magnitude and stepsize depend on p. The paper's lower bound also improves upon Zhang et al. (2020c) by correctly capturing the dependence on the noise level σ₀, which the prior lower bound (based on a finite p-th moment on the stochastic gradient rather than the noise) fails to do. In the finite variance case (p=2), the results recover the known O(T^(-1/4)) rate and the matching Ω(Δ₁L₀σ₀²ε⁻⁴) lower bound from Arjevani et al. (2023).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the Batched NSGDM algorithm (Cutkosky & Mehta, 2020) and proves it achieves the optimal O(T^{(1-p)/(3p-2)}) convergence rate under heavy-tailed noises, departing from the dominant paradigm of gradient clipping used in all prior heavy-tailed convergence results (Zhang et al., 2020c; Cutkosky & Mehta, 2021; Nguyen et al., 2023). The core analytical tool—a novel expected inequality for vector-valued martingale difference sequences (Lemma 4.3)—is proven via the AdaGrad regret analysis of Rakhlin & Sridharan (2017), creating an unexpected bridge between online learning and stochastic optimization. The lower bound construction extends the probability zero-chain framework of Arjevani et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2020c) to achieve tight dependence on problem-dependent parameters (noise level σ₀, smoothness L₀, initial gap Δ₁), which prior lower bounds lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that gradient normalization—a simple, well-known technique—can replace the more complex gradient clipping machinery for handling heavy-tailed stochastic gradients, and does so without requiring knowledge of the tail index p. This opens the door to understanding why adaptive methods (Adam, AdaGrad, RMSprop) that implicitly normalize gradients perform well in practice despite heavy-tailed noise, a gap the authors explicitly flag as important. Key open questions include whether the optimal O(T^{(1-p)/(3p-2)}) rate is achievable when p is unknown (the paper only achieves O(T^{(1-p)/(2p)})), whether the generalized heavy-tailed assumption (σ₁ > 0) preserves the p-agnostic property, and extending the analysis to the more practically relevant adaptive gradient family.
