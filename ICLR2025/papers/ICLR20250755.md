## Problem

Existing multimodal representation models are limited to specific modality pairs, and training omni models from scratch is infeasible due to the scarcity of large-scale multi-modal paired data. When merging multiple pre-trained spaces with fixed-weight averaging, interference between heterogeneous knowledge sources causes trade-offs between modalities rather than true versatility, making it difficult to scale beyond a handful of spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250755/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

OmniBind accepts 3D point clouds, audio clips, RGB images, videos, and text as inputs, each encoded by multiple specialized encoders (up to 5 audio-text, 5 image-text, 3 3D-image-text, and 1 audio-image-text source spaces) that are projected into a shared EVA-CLIP-18B-based embedding space. The output is a single fused embedding per modality (a weighted combination of all encoders for that modality) that supports zero-shot classification, cross-modal retrieval across all 10 modality pairs, any-query object localization, audio separation, and serial embedding arithmetic. Training requires only several million unpaired unimodal items and 4090 GPUs, with the entire pipeline completing in a fraction of the cost of training from scratch.

## Method

OmniBind first constructs pseudo item-pairs {p,a,v,t} by using state-of-the-art cross-modal retrieval models to retrieve top-1 matches across all modalities from unpaired data, then trains lightweight two-layer MLP projectors to bind each of 14 pre-trained spaces onto EVA-CLIP-18B via multimodal contrastive loss. The key architectural innovation is replacing fixed combining weights with per-modality learnable routers (two-layer MLPs with sigmoid activation) that dynamically assign softmax-normalized weights to each encoder based on the input. Router training is guided by two objectives: (1) cross-modal overall alignment, which applies InfoNCE contrastive loss across all six modality pairs to encourage balanced, high-quality alignment everywhere, and (2) language representation decoupling, a binary cross-entropy loss that trains the text router to identify which modality a caption describes and up-weight the corresponding specialized text encoder, reducing the domain gap between audio, image, and 3D captions. The final loss is L = λ·L_dec + L_align (λ=3), and the resulting models (OmniBind-Base/Large/Full with 5/13/14 spaces) achieve state-of-the-art results across 14 benchmarks covering all modality pairs.

## Results

OmniBind-Full achieves state-of-the-art cross-modal retrieval across all modality pairs: 49.28 R@1 on AudioCaps (audio-text), 49.36 R@1 on VGG-SS (audio-image), 63.01 R@1 on COCO (image-text), 32.89 R@1 on MSRVTT (text-video), and 46.55 R@1 on Objaverse (3D-image). On zero-shot classification, OmniBind-Full reaches 25.87 mAP on AudioSet, 94.55% Top-1 on ESC-50, 79.11% Top-1 on ImageNet, and 58.13% Top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS. Notably, OmniBind-Full matches or exceeds the 'Individual Best' (best single specialist model) on audio-image (15.64 vs 14.82 FlickrNet R@1) and 3D-image (46.55 vs 43.57 R@1), demonstrating effective cross-space knowledge transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniBind-Full decisively outperforms all prior omni-representation baselines across every modality pair. Against the strongest audio-image-text models (ImageBind++ and InternVL$_{IB}$++), OmniBind-Full gains +20.1 R@1 on AudioCaps (49.28 vs 29.16/29.11), +16.2 R@1 on VGG-SS (49.36 vs 33.19/32.75), and +6.0 R@1 on COCO (63.01 vs 57.01/61.07), while also being the only method supporting 3D input. Against 3D-audio-image-text models (Ex-MCR, PointBind), OmniBind-Full achieves 46.55 R@1 on 3D-image retrieval versus 2.54 and 5.86 respectively, and 49.28 vs 19.07/9.24 on AudioCaps. On zero-shot classification, OmniBind-Full surpasses LanguageBind (the strongest audio-text specialist) by +7.54 mAP on AudioSet (25.87 vs 18.33) and beats Uni3D(Objav.) by +3.39 Top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS (58.13 vs 54.74). OmniBind-Full slightly trails Individual Best on ImageNet (79.11 vs 82.43) and ESC-50 (94.55 vs 94.05, actually wins), but matches or exceeds it on audio-image and 3D-image retrieval, confirming effective cross-space knowledge transfer rather than simple trade-offs.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniBind extends the space-binding paradigm introduced by FreeBind (Wang et al., 2024b), which connected two bi-modality spaces via a shared modality, to the scale of 14 pre-trained spaces across five modalities. It departs from prior knowledge-fusion methods (C-MCR, Ex-MCR, FreeBind) that used fixed or manually tuned combining weights by introducing learnable routers inspired by Mixture-of-Experts in LLMs, enabling input-dependent dynamic weighting. The pseudo item-pair retrieval strategy replaces FreeBind's embedding-level aggregation, making the binding process more efficient and robust when scaling to many source spaces. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of multimodal contrastive representation learning (CLIP, ImageBind, LanguageBind, Uni3D) and model merging/ensemble techniques from the LLM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniBind demonstrates a practical recipe for constructing large-scale omni multimodal representations without requiring infeasible multi-modal paired datasets, using only millions of unpaired items and consumer-grade GPUs. The emergent 3D-audio alignment and composable embedding arithmetic (serial addition/subtraction) reveal capabilities that no prior single-model approach achieved, suggesting that binding diverse specialist knowledge can produce genuinely new cross-modal understanding. Open questions include whether this binding-space scaling approach will saturate, how it interacts with generative pipelines, and whether the router mechanism generalizes to even more modalities or source spaces. The paper's efficiency claim (30B-parameter model trained on 4090 GPUs with millions of data points) makes this approach accessible to a much broader research community than training omni models from scratch.
