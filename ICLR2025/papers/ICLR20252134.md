## Problem

Existing diffusion-prior methods for blind face restoration suffer from two critical limitations: (i) conventional diffusion models exhibit inferior semantic consistency (identity, structure, color) across sampling steps, making it difficult to optimize the restoration model toward faithful outputs; and (ii) their reliance on hundreds of denoising iterations prevents the integration of perceptual and adversarial losses in the image domain, which are essential for high-quality restoration. The latent consistency model (LCM), which maps any point on the ODE trajectory directly to its origin, has not been explored for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252134/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-quality face image at 512×512 resolution with unknown, complex degradation (blur, noise, downsampling, JPEG artifacts) as input. Rather than treating the LQ image as a noisy observation to be denoised, it is encoded into latent space and inserted as the intermediate state after the first noise-addition step in a 4-step LCM sampling process. The output is a high-quality restored face image at the same 512×512 resolution, produced after only 3 remaining LCM denoising steps, with inference time (~0.42 s) comparable to CNN-based methods.

## Method

InterLCM's key insight is to treat the LQ image as the 2nd-step intermediate state of a 4-step LCM (empirically validated via t-SNE distribution matching), so that the LCM's consistency mapping preserves semantic identity, structure, and color while the remaining 3 steps refine quality. A Visual Module (CLIP image encoder followed by a Visual Encoder initialized from CodeFormer's decoder) extracts face-specific semantic embeddings that replace the text prompt, and a Spatial Encoder (a ControlNet-style copy of the UNet encoder) injects structural information from the LQ image. Unlike standard ControlNet, the Spatial Encoder is trained by backpropagating image-domain losses (L1 + VGG19 perceptual + adversarial) from the final reconstructed image through the LCM denoising steps back to the LQ input, rather than using the diffusion noise-prediction loss. This few-step, image-domain training loop is what enables the integration of perceptual and adversarial losses that are infeasible with standard multi-step diffusion models.

## Results

On the synthetic CelebA-Test, InterLCM achieves the best LPIPS (0.223), MUSIQ (76.58), IDS (33.64), PSNR (25.19), and SSIM (0.718), though it trails DifFace (39.23) and VQFR (41.84) on FID (45.38). On real-world datasets, InterLCM achieves the best FID and MUSIQ on WebPhoto-Test (75.48 / 75.88) and WIDER-Test (35.43 / 76.29), and the best MUSIQ on LFW-Test (76.16). Inference time is 0.421 s per image, comparable to CNN-based methods (e.g., DFDNet 0.438 s) and substantially faster than diffusion-based baselines (PGDiff 14.77 s, WaveFace 19.37 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterLCM outperforms all CNN/Transformer-based baselines (PULSE, DFDNet, PSFRGAN, GFPGAN, GPEN, RestoreFormer, VQFR, CodeFormer) on LPIPS, MUSIQ, IDS, PSNR, and SSIM on CelebA-Test, with the closest competitor being CodeFormer (LPIPS 0.227 vs. 0.223; MUSIQ 75.55 vs. 76.58). Among diffusion-based methods, InterLCM achieves the best FID and MUSIQ on both WebPhoto-Test and WIDER-Test, surpassing WaveFace (FID 78.01→75.48 on WebPhoto; 37.23→35.43 on WIDER) and PGDiff (FID 101.68→75.48 on WebPhoto). However, InterLCM trails DifFace (FID 39.23) and VQFR (FID 41.84) on CelebA-Test FID, and PGDiff/DR2/DifFace lead on LFW-Test FID (44.65/45.71/46.31 vs. 51.32). Inference speed is a major advantage: at 0.421 s, InterLCM is ~35× faster than PGDiff (14.77 s) and ~46× faster than WaveFace (19.37 s), while remaining comparable to CNN methods like DFDNet (0.438 s). Overall, InterLCM delivers the best perceptual quality (MUSIQ) and identity/structure preservation (IDS, SSIM) across all four datasets, with the trade-off of slightly higher FID on the synthetic CelebA-Test compared to the strongest diffusion baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Latent Consistency Model (LCM), which maps any point on the ODE trajectory directly to its origin, as the backbone for blind face restoration — a task previously dominated by standard diffusion models (DR2, DifFace, PGDiff) or GAN/VQGAN priors (CodeFormer, GFPGAN). The key conceptual departure is treating the low-quality image as the 2nd-step intermediate state of 4-step LCM rather than starting from random noise, which preserves semantic consistency inherent in LCM's consistency mapping. The Spatial Encoder borrows the ControlNet architecture but differs in training: it backpropagates gradients from the reconstructed real image through the denoising steps to the LQ input, rather than being trained with a separate diffusion loss. The Visual Module (CLIP + distilled Visual Encoder) replaces text prompts with face-specific visual embeddings, avoiding the need for accurate text descriptions of degraded inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InterLCM achieves SOTA on four datasets while running at 0.42s per image — comparable to CNN-based methods and 35–46× faster than other diffusion-based BFR methods — by exploiting LCM's few-step inference and the ability to apply perceptual and adversarial losses in the image domain, which was impractical with multi-step diffusion models. The empirical finding that LQ images align closely with the 2nd-step noisy intermediate state (FID=2.83 vs. 103.70 for the 1st step) provides a principled justification for the starting point. Limitations include poor handling of hands (due to FFHQ's limited coverage) and the approach being specific to face restoration; whether the intermediate-state formulation generalizes to broader image restoration or other modalities remains an open question.
