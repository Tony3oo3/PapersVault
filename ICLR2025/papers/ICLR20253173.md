## Problem

Multi-modal LLMs exhibit three critical deficiencies in visual mathematics: standard CLIP vision encoders fail to capture abstract mathematical elements in diagrams, diagram-language alignment is poor so LLMs cannot accurately describe geometric or functional content, and chain-of-thought reasoning quality degrades when visual diagrams are included. Compounding these issues, no large-scale, high-quality mathematical visual dataset with verified CoT rationales exists, and manual annotation of such data is prohibitively expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253173/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a math diagram image (plane geometry, analytic geometry, or function plot rendered via Matplotlib) paired with a deliberately text-lite question that strips away redundant information directly observable from the diagram, forcing the model to extract essential content from visual tokens. The output is a structured chain-of-thought rationale (averaging ~150 words) with step-by-step formula substitution and numerical calculation, followed by a final answer. Questions come in both multiple-choice (62.4%) and free-form (37.6%) formats, and the text-lite design is a key differentiator from standard VQA benchmarks.

## Method

MAVIS introduces a fully automated, rule-based data engine (Python + Matplotlib) that generates 558K diagram-caption pairs (MAVIS-Caption) and 582K problem-solving instances (MAVIS-Instruct) without GPT API calls, using iterative shape-extension for geometry and parameterized function templates for curves. Training proceeds in four progressive stages: (1) contrastive fine-tuning of CLIP ViT-L on MAVIS-Caption to produce a math-specific encoder (CLIP-Math); (2) diagram-language alignment via a two-layer MLP projection layer with LoRA (rank 128) on Mammoth2-7B; (3) instruction tuning on MAVIS-Instruct with unfrozen projection and LoRA LLM; (4) DPO preference alignment where the model's own incorrect CoT outputs (88K filtered from 582K data-engine problems) serve as negative samples against the annotated rationales as positives. The key architectural insight is that mathematical diagrams require a dedicated vision encoder and domain-specific alignment rather than relying on general-purpose CLIP, and that minimizing textual redundancy in questions forces genuine visual interpretation rather than text shortcuts.

## Results

On MathVerse (test-mini), MAVIS-7B achieves 35.2% CoT-E and 28.4% accuracy overall, the best among all open-source MLLMs. It surpasses the previous best 7B model InternLM-XComposer2 (25.9% CoT-E) by +9.3% and the much larger LLaVA-NeXT-110B (28.3% CoT-E) by +6.9%. On six additional benchmarks (Table 2), MAVIS-7B leads open-source models on GeoQA (68.3%), FunctionQA (50.0%), MMMU-Math (42.4%), and MathVision (19.2%), and even exceeds GPT-4V on all three MathVista sub-categories (GPS 64.1, ALG 59.2, GEO 63.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAVIS-7B (7B) decisively outperforms all open-source MLLMs across MathVerse and the six additional benchmarks. Among 7B open-source models, it beats InternLM-XComposer2 by +9.3% CoT-E and G-LLaVA by +19.5% CoT-E. It also surpasses larger open-source models: ShareGPT4V (13B) by +17.8% CoT-E, Math-LLaVA (13B) by +11.1% CoT-E, SPHINX-MoE (8×7B) by +12.4% CoT-E, and LLaVA-NeXT-110B by +6.9% CoT-E. Against closed-source models, MAVIS-7B still trails GPT-4V (54.4% vs. 35.2% CoT-E) but exceeds Gemini-Pro (35.3%) and Qwen-VL-Max (37.2%) on accuracy. On MathVista's three math sub-categories, MAVIS-7B surpasses even GPT-4V (64.1/59.2/63.2 vs. 50.5/53.0/51.0), and on We-Math it leads all open-source models by roughly +10% over the next-best. The DPO stage (Stage 4) contributes a consistent +1.5% CoT-E and +0.9% Acc over the w/o-DPO variant, with larger gains on multi-step We-Math S3 problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA-style multi-stage training paradigm (vision encoder → projection → LLM) by inserting a domain-specific contrastive pre-training stage (CLIP-Math) before alignment, and appending a DPO-based CoT preference alignment stage after instruction tuning. It departs from prior mathematical visual work (G-LLaVA, Math-LLaVA) that relied on small manually-annotated datasets (GeoQA, Geometry3K) by introducing a fully rule-based automatic data engine that generates 558K diagram-caption pairs and 834K problem-solving instances without GPT API calls for core synthesis. The four-stage progressive pipeline—contrastive encoder training, diagram-language alignment, instruction tuning, and DPO—systematically addresses three identified gaps (visual encoding, cross-modal alignment, and CoT reasoning) that prior MLLMs left unaddressed in mathematical domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAVIS provides the first large-scale, automatically generated mathematical visual dataset and a reproducible four-stage training recipe, lowering the barrier for building domain-specific MLLMs beyond mathematics. The finding that a 7B model with domain-specific training outperforms a 110B generalist by ~7% on MathVerse suggests that targeted data and encoder adaptation can substitute for raw model scale in narrow domains. Key limitations remain: OCR is still a bottleneck for vision-only problems, the data engine cannot generate all diagram types (e.g., inscribed triangles), and solid geometry is only covered through manual collection rather than automatic generation. Open questions include whether the automatic data engine approach generalizes to other visual-reasoning domains and whether the CLIP-Math encoder can be further improved for complex multi-object diagrams.
