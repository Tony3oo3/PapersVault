## Problem

Linear-complexity architectures such as Mamba and Linformer offer efficient alternatives to transformers, but pretrained models for these architectures are largely unavailable, especially in non-text domains like speech. Existing conversion approaches either require expensive re-pretraining from scratch or rely solely on parameter transfer, which leads to severe performance degradation and training instability. This paper addresses how to jointly convert a pretrained transformer into a linear-complexity model and fine-tune it to a target task in a single, computationally accessible phase without redoing pretraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained transformer (RoBERTa, Wav2Vec2, or Pythia) as the source and produces a converted linear-complexity model (Linformer, Mamba2, or Mamba) fine-tuned on a downstream task. For speech, the bidirectional Wav2Vec2 encoder is converted by replacing each attention layer with two unidirectional Mamba2 mixers (forward and backward with time-inverted inputs). Hidden states for layerwise distillation are extracted after the feed-forward layer rather than immediately after the sequence-mixing layer, as the latter proves difficult to match across architectures and causes training instability.

## Method

CALD combines parameter transfer—replacing only attention layers with the target sequence-mixing module while preserving embeddings, feed-forward, and layernorm—with layerwise distillation that enforces MSE alignment on hidden states between teacher and student at each layer. The total loss combines cross-entropy (or CTC for ASR), KL-divergence output distillation, and the layerwise MSE term, weighted by task-specific hyperparameters. The key contribution is four guidance modes controlling how the teacher signal is applied: target guided (distill from the fully fine-tuned teacher), trajectory guided (simultaneously fine-tune teacher and student, distilling from the teacher's current state at each step, inspired by homotopy/continuation methods), waypoint guided (a cheaper approximation using pre-saved teacher checkpoints), and hybrid (distill early in training, then switch to unguided fine-tuning). Experiments show trajectory/waypoint guidance is most effective when hidden states shift gradually during fine-tuning (NLP), while hybrid mode works better when features undergo large early shifts (speech).

## Results

On language processing (RoBERTa→Linformer), the best CALD variant (Trajectory Guided) achieves 92.5% average accuracy across QNLI, QQP, SST2, and IMDB, matching the fully re-pretrained Linformer (92.3%) and within 1.3% of standard RoBERTa (93.8%), while unguided parameter transfer collapses to 80.0%. On speech processing (Wav2Vec2→Mamba2), CALD Target Guided reaches 6.56 WER on TED-LIUM ASR (vs. 6.24 for standard Wav2Vec2 and 11.29 for unguided), 90.43% intent classification accuracy on SLURP, and 96.56% speaker ID accuracy on VoxCeleb1. On language modeling (Pythia-1B→Mamba with only 2% of Pile), CALD Hybrid achieves 0.525 average zero-shot score, approaching the original Pythia-1B (0.544) and substantially outperforming unguided conversion (0.514).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the fully re-pretrained Linformer (Wang et al., 2020), CALD Trajectory Guided slightly outperforms it (92.5% vs. 92.3% average accuracy) without any pretraining, while CALD Target Guided is marginally below (91.6%). Against standard RoBERTa (the source teacher), the best CALD variant loses only 1.3% on average, whereas unguided parameter transfer suffers a catastrophic 13.8% drop. On speech tasks, CALD Target Guided nearly matches standard Wav2Vec2 (Baevski et al., 2020) on ASR (6.56 vs. 6.24 WER) and even exceeds it on speaker ID (96.56% vs. 96.09%), while unguided conversion degrades WER by 5 points. On language modeling, CALD Hybrid with 2% Pile data (0.525 avg) narrows the gap to Pythia-1B (Biderman et al., 2023; 0.544 avg) to just 0.019, compared to a 0.030 gap for unguided conversion, and the gap shrinks further with more data. The key finding across all three domains is that CALD consistently recovers 95–100% of the original transformer's performance, while unguided parameter transfer alone is insufficient and often unstable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model conversion literature (Kasai et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024a; Bick et al., 2024) by shifting the conversion from the pretraining phase to the fine-tuning phase, making it accessible to downstream users without large-scale pretraining data or compute. It builds on layerwise distillation (Sun et al., 2019) and standard KD (Hinton et al., 2015) but introduces trajectory-guided and waypoint-guided modes inspired by homotopy/continuation methods, which prior conversion work did not explore. The bidirectional Mamba2 architecture for speech follows Zhang et al. (2024b), but the joint fine-tuning-and-conversion framework with multiple guidance strategies is novel. Unlike prior work restricted to NLP, this paper extends the scope to speech processing (ASR, SLU, SID), a domain where pretrained linear-complexity models simply do not exist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: converting Wav2Vec2-large to Mamba2 for ASR takes 1.6 days on a single RTX3090 versus 5.2 days on 128 V100s for pretraining, making linear-complexity speech models accessible to academic labs. The finding that trajectory-guided distillation helps for NLP (where hidden states shift gradually) but is harmful for speech (where they shift dramatically, reaching cosine distance 0.372 after just 2,000 steps) provides a useful diagnostic for choosing guidance strategies. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models (e.g., 7B+), whether it generalizes to other linear-complexity architectures beyond Linformer/Mamba, and whether the domain-dependent guidance findings hold for other modalities like video or music.
