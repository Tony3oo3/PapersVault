## Problem

In misspecified linear bandits, sparsity k in the ground-truth parameters reduces sample complexity from exponential in d to polynomial (Dong & Yang, 2023), but whether this extends to the RL setting with horizon H was open. Standard approaches fail: Bellman backup amplifies approximation error exponentially in H, and optimistic elimination (OLIVE) yields suboptimality scaling with Bellman rank, which can be as large as d. This paper closes the gap by proving both failures are fundamental and designing an algorithm that achieves O(Hε) suboptimality with polynomial sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a d-dimensional feature map φ(s,a) with ‖φ(s,a)‖ ≤ 1, a sparsity constant k, and interacts with an episodic MDP of horizon H by collecting full trajectories (one trajectory = H samples). It outputs a deterministic greedy policy π_θ over the action space. The continuous parameter space is discretized into a finite set of size at most (1+4/ε_net)^k · C(d,k) per level, constructed from an ε_net/2-net of the (k−1)-sphere crossed with all k-subsets of [d], so the algorithm operates over a finite hypothesis class.

## Method

Algorithm 1 is a modified optimistic value function elimination that maintains independent parameter candidate sets P_h for each level h, breaking the inter-level coupling present in OLIVE. At each iteration, it selects θ_h^t to maximize the empirical roll-in value at level h (rather than only the initial-state value), collects m trajectories, computes empirical Bellman errors, and eliminates only the single parameter θ_h^t that exceeds the Bellman error threshold at its level. The key insight is that if a parameter shows large Bellman error at level h, it must be incorrect because the next-level parameter was already chosen to maximize value, so the residual cannot be attributed to approximation error alone. This targeted, per-level elimination guarantees at least one parameter is removed per iteration, bounding the total iterations by the parameter space size. The paper proves O(Hε) suboptimality with O(kd^k H^3 · ε_net^{-k} ε_stat^{-2}) samples and a matching Ω(Hε) lower bound via binary-tree MDP constructions and a reduction from a MULTI-INDEX-QUERY problem.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.3) shows that Algorithm 1 achieves suboptimality $O(H\epsilon)$ with sample complexity $O(kd^k H^3 \cdot \ln(dH/\epsilon_{\text{net}}\delta) \cdot \epsilon_{\text{net}}^{-k}\epsilon_{\text{stat}}^{-2})$, which is polynomial in the feature dimension $d$ when sparsity $k$ is constant. This is complemented by a matching $\widetilde{\Omega}(H\epsilon)$ suboptimality lower bound (Theorem 3.1), and a sample complexity lower bound showing that achieving $H/(2C)\cdot\epsilon$ suboptimality requires $\Omega(C\cdot 2^C)$ samples even when $d=k=1$ (Theorem 3.2). In the bandit special case ($H=1$), the algorithm achieves suboptimality $2\epsilon + 2\epsilon_{\text{net}} + 4\epsilon_{\text{stat}}$, improving upon the $4\epsilon + \epsilon_{\text{stat}}$ guarantee of Dong & Yang (2023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's algorithm strictly improves upon the level-by-level Bellman backup approach, which suffers exponential error amplification in $H$ (each level doubles the approximation error, yielding $O(2^H\epsilon)$ suboptimality). Compared to OLIVE (Jiang et al., 2017), which achieves $H\sqrt{M}\epsilon$ suboptimality where $M$ is the Bellman rank, this paper shows via Proposition 1.2 that the Bellman rank can be as large as $d$ in their setting, making OLIVE's guarantee $O(H\sqrt{d}\,\epsilon)$—worse than the paper's $O(H\epsilon)$. In the bandit special case ($H=1$), the paper's Corollary 4.5 achieves suboptimality $2\epsilon + 2\epsilon_{\text{net}} + 4\epsilon_{\text{stat}}$, which is tighter than Dong & Yang's (2023) $4\epsilon + \epsilon_{\text{stat}}$, though Dong & Yang additionally provide a for-all arm guarantee that this paper does not. The paper also proves (Theorem 1.1) that the 2× error amplification inherent in for-all bandit guarantees is information-theoretically unavoidable, explaining why the level-by-level approach cannot be fixed by improving the bandit subroutine. Overall, the $O(H\epsilon)$ suboptimality is shown to be near-optimal: no algorithm can achieve better than $\Omega(H\epsilon)$ without samples, and achieving $H/(2C)\cdot\epsilon$ requires $\exp(\Omega(C))$ samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse linear bandit result of Dong & Yang (2023) from the H=1 setting to full episodic RL, and builds on the optimistic value function elimination framework of Jiang et al. (2017, OLIVE). The key departure is that existing elimination-based RL algorithms (OLIVE and its generalizations) achieve suboptimality scaling with Bellman rank, which the paper shows can be as large as d in this setting, yielding O(H√d·ε) guarantees. The paper's algorithm instead exploits sparsity directly by maintaining per-level parameter sets and eliminating only the selected parameter each iteration, achieving O(Hε) suboptimality with polynomial sample complexity in d. The paper also demonstrates that the natural level-by-level approach (applying the bandit algorithm at each horizon) is information-theoretically impossible to make work due to an unavoidable factor-2 error amplification per level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first complete (matching upper and lower bound) characterization of the sample complexity for misspecified sparse linear RL, showing that O(Hε) suboptimality is achievable with poly(d) samples when sparsity k is constant, and that this is near-optimal. The information-theoretic hardness constructions (binary tree MDPs, multi-index-query reduction) are technically interesting and rule out a family of natural algorithmic approaches. Open questions include reducing the exponential dependence on ε_net in the sample complexity, achieving the H/C·ε vs. exp(C) trade-off in upper bounds, and extending to stochastic transitions. The results are purely theoretical with no empirical validation, limiting immediate practical impact.
