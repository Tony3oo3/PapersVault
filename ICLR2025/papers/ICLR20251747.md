## Problem

Traditional RL methods for sequential decision-making suffer from high sample complexity and poor generalization because they must explore large action spaces from scratch without leveraging existing domain knowledge. While LLMs encode vast amounts of world knowledge, they cannot reliably generate optimal action sequences in interactive tasks. The paper asks how to formally integrate a fixed LLM as a probabilistic action prior into RL frameworks to reduce exploration and optimization complexity without expensive fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251747/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes textual state observations (e.g., room descriptions in ALFWorld, grid positions in Frozen Lake) and a finite set of admissible textual actions. A fixed LLM (Qwen-1.5 7B/14B) is prompted with the state and admissible actions to produce k free-form outputs, which are mapped to executable actions via a rule-based projection. The Q-network uses a frozen BERT encoder on state-action pairs with a trainable 3-layer MLP adapter to output scalar Q-values; the final action is selected by softmax over Q-values of the k LLM-proposed candidates.

## Method

The paper formulates MDP solving as Bayesian inference where the LLM defines the action prior p_LLM(a|s) and the optimality likelihood is estimated by Q-values. Two complementary inference strategies are derived: (1) variational inference yields a KL-regularized policy objective, implemented as GFlan-Prior (PPO with a KL penalty against the LLM prior distribution approximated from k samples); and (2) direct posterior sampling, where k actions are drawn from the LLM prior and reweighted by a softmax over Q-values, implemented as DQN-Prior (online) and CQL-Prior (offline). The key mechanism is restricting both exploration and the Bellman optimal operator to the narrowed prior action space C^k ⊆ A, eliminating the need to evaluate or fine-tune over the full action space. Proposition 1 proves that as k→∞, the posterior sampling policy converges to the variational inference solution, unifying both approaches. This design requires no LLM fine-tuning—the LLM remains fixed and is queried only for action proposals, while all learning occurs in the lightweight Q-network or small policy model.

## Results

On the offline ALFWorld(Pick) task, CQL-Prior achieves a reward of 0.80, substantially outperforming CQL (0.03), DQN (0.27), and Behavioral Cloning (0.31). On Overcooked(Salad) with only 1,000 offline examples, CQL-Prior reaches a reward of 1.01 versus 0.32 for CQL and 0.14 for DQN, demonstrating that the LLM prior reduces required training samples by at least 90% (CQL-Prior succeeds with ~1,000 examples while CQL requires >12,000). In online settings, DQN-Prior outperforms both DQN and GFlan on ALFWorld tasks where the action space reaches up to 50 actions, and also improves sample efficiency on smaller action spaces (Frozen Lake, Overcooked). For policy-based RL, GFlan-Prior achieves performance comparable to DQN-Prior while significantly outperforming the unmodified GFlan baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DQN (Mnih, 2013), DQN-Prior more than doubles the reward on ALFWorld(Pick) (0.49 vs. 0.27) and achieves 0.98 vs. 0.14 on Salad(1000), confirming that restricting exploration to the LLM prior action space dramatically improves sample efficiency. Against CQL (Kumar, 2020), CQL-Prior achieves a 26× improvement on ALFWorld(Pick) (0.80 vs. 0.03) and a 3× improvement on Salad(1000) (1.01 vs. 0.32), with the gap narrowing only at the largest dataset size (1.31 vs. 1.33 at Salad(24000)). Against GFlan (Carta, 2023), GFlan-Prior significantly outperforms the unmodified GFlan in policy-based RL (Figure 4a) by adding a KL regularizer toward the LLM prior, reaching performance comparable to the value-based DQN-Prior. Against TWOSOME (Tan, 2024), DQN-Prior(Cache) achieves comparable task performance while reducing training time from 60.4 to 13.8 minutes and LLM queries from 5,000 to 434, demonstrating that the fixed-prior approach avoids the costly LLM fine-tuning loop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of Control-as-Inference (Levine, 2018) and LLM-as-agent research, but departs from both in a key way: rather than fine-tuning LLMs with RL (as in GFlan, TWOSOME, or Pangu-Agent) or using LLMs as world models/reward generators, it treats a *fixed* LLM as a Bayesian action prior distribution that narrows the exploration and optimization space for standard RL algorithms (DQN, CQL, PPO). The theoretical framework unifies variational inference and direct posterior sampling under a single MDP formulation, showing that reweighting LLM-proposed actions by learned Q-values is equivalent (in the limit) to solving a KL-regularized policy optimization. This contrasts with prior LLM-agent work that either relies on elaborate prompting, search-based methods (Tree-of-Thought, MCTS), or expensive LLM fine-tuning per task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the >90% sample reduction in offline RL and the finding that a Q-function trained with one LLM prior can guide decision-making for entirely different LLMs at inference time, enabling a 'train big, deploy small' paradigm. However, the work is limited to text-based games with finite, discrete action spaces (≤50 actions), and the LLM must be queried (or cached) at each state, which introduces latency. Open questions include whether this approach extends to continuous or free-form action spaces, whether the benefits persist in high-dimensional environments like robotics, and how sensitive performance is to the quality of the LLM prior when the LLM's domain knowledge is weak or misaligned with the task.
