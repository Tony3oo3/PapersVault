## Problem

Existing multi-objective optimization methods aim to approximate the full Pareto front with a dense solution set, but the required number of solutions grows exponentially with the number of objectives, making them intractable for problems with >100 objectives. This paper instead asks whether a small set of solutions (e.g., K=5) can collaboratively and complementarily cover a large number of objectives (e.g., m>100), ensuring each objective is well addressed by at least one solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes m differentiable objective functions, a preference vector λ (uniform 1/m in all experiments), a small number of solutions K (typically K≪m, e.g., 5 solutions for 128–1024 objectives), and an initial solution set X_K. It outputs K solutions evaluated by the worst and average per-objective best values (min over the solution set for each objective). The key constraint is K≪m, distinguishing this from both single-solution multi-objective optimization (K=1) and the degenerate case K≥m where each objective is solved independently.

## Method

The core contribution is the Tchebycheff Set (TCH-Set) scalarization, which replaces the single-solution Tchebycheff formulation with a set-based one: max over m objectives of λ_i(min over K solutions of f_i(x^(k)) − z_i*), so each objective is handled by whichever solution achieves its best value. To make this differentiable, the Smooth Tchebycheff Set (STCH-Set) scalarization applies nested log-sum-exp smoothing: an outer smooth max over objectives and an inner smooth min over solutions, yielding a fully differentiable objective optimizable by standard gradient descent. Theoretical results establish that STCH-Set optimal solutions are weakly Pareto optimal (and Pareto optimal under positive preferences or uniqueness), that STCH-Set uniformly approximates TCH-Set as smooth parameters → 0, and that gradient convergence implies Pareto stationarity for each solution in the set.

## Results

On convex many-objective optimization with m=128 objectives and K=5 solutions, STCH-Set achieves a worst objective value of 1.91e-01 and average of 5.12e-02, substantially outperforming all baselines (e.g., LS: 4.17e+00 worst, TCH: 3.56e+00, MosT: 1.02e+00, SoM: 9.20e-01). The Wilcoxon rank-sum test confirms STCH-Set is significantly better than every baseline on worst objective value (6/0/0 across all K values) and on average objective value (5/0/1). On noisy mixed linear regression with 1,000 data points and K=5 models, STCH-Set achieves a worst value of 2.10e+00 versus 3.84e+01 (LS) and 2.50e+00 (SoM). The advantage of STCH-Set over its non-smooth counterpart TCH-Set is consistent across all settings, confirming the critical role of smoothness for gradient-based set optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STCH-Set dominates all five external baselines on the worst objective metric across every configuration tested (Wilcoxon 6/0/0 for m=128, 12/0/0 for m=1024). Linear scalarization (LS), Tchebycheff (TCH), and smooth Tchebycheff (STCH) with random preferences are the weakest, achieving worst values roughly 5–7× higher than STCH-Set (e.g., 4.17e+00 vs. 1.91e-01 at K=5, m=128), confirming that single-solution scalarization cannot handle the few-for-many setting. MosT (Li et al., 2024) improves substantially over traditional scalarization by actively constructing diverse solutions, but is still outperformed by STCH-Set by a factor of ~5 on worst value (1.02e+00 vs. 1.91e-01 at K=5) and cannot scale to 1024 objectives within reasonable time. SoM (Ding et al., 2024) is the closest competitor, occasionally beating STCH-Set on average objective value (e.g., 2.02e-01 vs. 2.12e-01 at K=3, m=128), but consistently loses on worst value (1.86e+00 vs. 6.08e-01). The non-smooth TCH-Set variant is uniformly worse than STCH-Set (e.g., 4.94e-01 vs. 1.91e-01 at K=5), demonstrating that the smooth log-sum-exp approximation is essential for effective gradient-based optimization of the set scalarization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tchebycheff scalarization lineage (Bowman 1976; Steuer & Choo 1983) and its recent smooth variant (Lin et al. 2024) from single-solution to set-based optimization, introducing a min-over-solutions operator inside the max-over-objectives structure. The smoothing technique draws directly on the log-sum-exp framework of Beck & Teboulle (2012) and Nesterov (2005), applied to both the outer max and inner min. It departs from the dominant MOO paradigm of approximating the full Pareto front with exponentially many solutions, and from MGDA-based methods that require per-iteration QP solves, positioning itself alongside concurrent few-for-many approaches (SoM by Ding et al. 2024; MosT by Li et al. 2024) but with a simpler, directly optimizable scalarization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world problems with >100 objectives where decision-makers can only act on a handful of solutions, a setting the authors note has received little attention from the MOO community. The theoretical contribution is notable in showing that the non-smooth TCH-Set scalarization does NOT guarantee even weak Pareto optimality without uniqueness (a somewhat counterintuitive result for scalarization methods), while the smooth STCH-Set variant recovers full Pareto optimality under mild conditions. Key limitations include the deterministic setting (all objectives always available) and the reliance on gradient-based optimization, which may struggle with highly non-convex landscapes; the authors identify partially observable objectives as a natural future direction.
