## Problem

Uniform replay in online RL is inefficient because the distribution of states an agent visits differs from the distribution it should train on, and prioritized replay risks overfitting to rare but useful transitions. The paper asks how to build a scalable, parametric memory that both densifies past experience beyond the observed data distribution and guides generation toward more learning-relevant transitions without collapsing to stale or low-quality samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251686/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes online transitions (s, a, s', r) collected by the agent, along with a scalar relevance value F(τ) computed by a lightweight curiosity head (or alternative metric). For pixel-based tasks, raw 3×h×w RGB observations are first passed through the policy's CNN visual encoder, and the diffusion model operates entirely in that latent space. The output is a synthetic replay buffer of generated transitions, mixed with real transitions at a configurable ratio r (default 0.5) to train an off-policy policy (SAC, REDQ, or DRQ-V2). Both real and synthetic buffers are maintained at 1M transitions.

## Method

PGR replaces the non-parametric replay buffer with a conditional diffusion model trained end-to-end on the agent's online experience, where the conditioning signal is a relevance function F that measures transition priority. The key mechanism is a 'prompting' strategy: at generation time, conditioning values are sampled from the top-k highest-F transitions in the real buffer, and classifier-free guidance (with scale ω) steers the denoising process toward those relevant regions of transition space. The paper identifies intrinsic curiosity (prediction error of a forward dynamics model in a learned feature space) as the optimal default F, because it promotes generation diversity and reduces Q-function overfitting—unlike return- or TD-error-based alternatives that overfit to low-diversity high-value transitions. The diffusion architecture (residual MLP denoiser) mirrors SYNTHER exactly, adding only a lightweight curiosity head updated for 5% of policy gradient steps, keeping overhead under 5% additional training time. The framework is plug-and-play: any off-policy RL algorithm can serve as the policy backbone, and the generative inner loop periodically retrains the diffusion model on fresh real data to track the evolving policy distribution.

## Results

On DMC-100K (state-based), PGR(Curiosity) achieves the highest average return across all four tasks: 927.98 on Quadruped-Walk, 817.36 on Cheetah-Run, 915.21 on Reacher-Hard, and 885.98 on Finger-Turn-Hard (300K steps). On pixel-based DMC-100K, PGR(Curiosity) reaches 570.99 on Walker-Walk and 529.70 on Cheetah-Run. On OpenAI Gym (100K steps), PGR attains 9234.61 on HalfCheetah-v2 and 5682.33 on Hopper-v2, substantially outperforming SYNTHER (8165.35 and 3395.21 respectively), though SYNTHER leads on Walker2d-v2 (4829.32 vs. 4101.79).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGR(Curiosity) consistently outperforms all model-free baselines (SAC, REDQ, DRQ-V2), model-based baselines (MBPO, DREAMER-V3), and the unconditional generative replay baseline SYNTHER across DMC-100K. The margin over SYNTHER is particularly large on harder tasks: +200.97 on Quadruped-Walk (927.98 vs. 727.01) and +331.97 on Finger-Turn-Hard (885.98 vs. 554.01), where SYNTHER actually underperforms vanilla REDQ. Against model-based methods, PGR(Curiosity) leads DREAMER-V3 by +538.35 on Quadruped-Walk and MBPO by +422.07. On OpenAI Gym, PGR dominates SYNTHER on HalfCheetah (+1069.26) and Hopper (+2287.12) but trails on Walker2d (−727.53). Among PGR variants, curiosity conditioning clearly surpasses reward-based (which underperforms even SYNTHER), return-based, and TD-error-based guidance, confirming that the choice of relevance function is critical. PGR also beats prioritized experience replay (PER) baselines and exploration-bonus-augmented SYNTHER/REDQ, demonstrating that generative densification with guidance is more effective than simply reweighting or rewarding existing experience.

## Relation to Prior Work

PGR extends SYNTHER (Lu et al., 2024), which learns an unconditional diffusion model over the replay buffer, by adding a conditional relevance signal that guides generation toward more learning-informative transitions. The relevance function is drawn from the intrinsic curiosity module (Pathak et al., 2017), and the conditioning mechanism uses classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) with a top-k prompting strategy inspired by Peebles et al. (2022). Conceptually, PGR departs from both prioritized experience replay (which reweights existing data) and model-based RL (which plans through a learned dynamics model) by synthesizing entirely new off-policy transitions whose distribution is shaped by a separately learned relevance criterion, avoiding compounding model errors while still targeting useful regions of state space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, plug-and-play recipe for data-efficient online RL that scales to higher update-to-data ratios (UTD 40) than prior generative replay methods, with minimal overhead (<5% additional training time). A key open limitation is that PGR fails catastrophically at synthetic data ratios above 0.875, meaning real environment interactions remain indispensable; fully replacing online data with synthetic transitions is still unsolved. The mechanistic finding that curiosity-based conditioning reduces overfitting (measured via dormant ratio) rather than improving generation quality opens a broader question about how to design relevance functions that promote diversity in generative replay for more complex, high-dimensional, or multi-task settings.
