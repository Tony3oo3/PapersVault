## Problem

Transformer-based language models struggle to extrapolate beyond their training sequence length because positional encodings are limited to positions seen during pre-training. RoPE, the dominant approach, relies on absolute positions and a single fixed scale (analogous to a single-scale wavelet), while ALiBi uses relative positions with multiple window sizes but restricts the attention receptive field, preventing capture of long-range dependencies. No existing method combines relative positions with flexible multi-scale windowing without constraining the attention field.

## Input / Output

The method takes a token sequence of arbitrary length L and, for each query-key pair (m, n), computes a d-dimensional relative positional vector p_{m,n} by evaluating d distinct Ricker wavelet functions at the relative position t = m − n. Unlike RPE, which clips relative distances to a fixed window (e.g., 16 or 32 tokens), this method applies no clipping, so the position expression range is unbounded and independent of sentence length. The resulting vector is added to the standard attention score as an inner product with the query, following the RPE formulation.

## Method

The paper first proves that RoPE is mathematically equivalent to a wavelet transform using Haar-like wavelets at a fixed scale (a = 2) applied along the head dimension rather than along the position axis, explaining its poor extrapolation. It then shows that ALiBi's per-head slopes correspond to different effective window sizes, analogous to the scale parameter in wavelet transforms. Building on these insights, the proposed method replaces RPE's learnable position embeddings with Ricker wavelet functions ψ(t) = (1−t²)exp(−t²/2), evaluated at the relative position t = m−n. For a head dimension d, the method uses s distinct scale parameters a ∈ {2⁰, 2¹, …, 2^{s−1}} (powers of 2, following discrete wavelet transform conventions) and d/s shift parameters b ∈ {0, 1, …, d/s − 1}, generating d unique wavelets whose outputs form the position vector. This multi-scale, relative-position design captures dynamic, non-periodic structure in language at multiple window sizes while preserving the full attention receptive field, enabling extrapolation without sequence segmentation.

## Results

On WikiText-103 with L_train=512, the proposed Wavelet (Ricker) method achieves the lowest perplexity across all extrapolation lengths without non-overlapping inference: 18.23 at 1012 tokens, 18.00 at 1512, and 17.99 at 2512, outperforming ALiBi (18.41 at 2512) and Trans-XL (19.05 at 2512), while RoPE degrades catastrophically to 93.94. With L_train=1024, Wavelet achieves 17.44 perplexity at 5024 tokens versus ALiBi's 17.88 and RoPE's 122.52. In the long-context Llama-2-7B experiment (L_train=4096, CodeParrot), Wavelet achieves 8.60 perplexity at 32k tokens, outperforming RoPE (θ=500k) at 8.90.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Wavelet (Ricker) method outperforms all five baselines on extrapolation perplexity. Against ALiBi (the closest competitor), Wavelet achieves 17.99 vs. 18.41 perplexity at 2512 tokens (L_train=512) and 17.44 vs. 17.88 at 5024 tokens (L_train=1024), a consistent ~0.4–0.5 perplexity advantage. Against Trans-XL, the gap widens to ~1.0 perplexity at 2512 tokens (17.99 vs. 19.05). Against RoPE (both θ=10k and θ=500k), the advantage is dramatic: RoPE's perplexity explodes to 93.94 and 77.90 respectively at 2512 tokens, while Wavelet remains stable at 17.99. Against NoPE, Wavelet wins by a large margin on extrapolation (17.99 vs. 48.48 at 2512). In the large-scale Llama-2-7B experiment on CodeParrot, Wavelet (8.60 at 32k) beats RoPE with θ=500k (8.90 at 32k), confirming the advantage scales to 7B parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RPE framework (Shaw et al., 2018), replacing its learnable relative-position embeddings with fixed wavelet functions, while drawing a novel theoretical bridge to RoPE by showing it is mathematically equivalent to a restricted Haar-like wavelet transform at a single fixed scale. It also reinterprets ALiBi's effectiveness through the lens of multi-scale windowing, connecting its head-specific slopes to the scale parameter in wavelet analysis. The key departure from prior work is the combination of relative positioning (for extrapolation) with multi-scale wavelet functions (for capturing non-stationary signal dynamics), which neither RoPE (absolute, single-scale) nor ALiBi (relative, but receptive-field-limited) achieves simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, parameter-free positional encoding that extrapolates beyond training length without constraining the attention receptive field, addressing a core limitation of both RoPE (requires fine-tuning or interpolation for longer contexts) and ALiBi (cannot capture long-range dependencies). However, the long-context validation is limited to a 7B model pre-trained on only 1B tokens, and the computational overhead of computing relative positions for every query-key pair remains a practical bottleneck that the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve. Open questions include whether the specific Ricker wavelet choice is optimal, how the method interacts with existing RoPE interpolation strategies at scale, and whether the gains persist in fully pre-trained frontier models.
