## Problem

Existing multi-objective NAS methods that balance accuracy against hardware metrics (latency, energy) require repeating the entire search for each hardware constraint and each target device to profile the Pareto front, making cross-device Pareto profiling prohibitively expensive. No gradient-based NAS method returns a diverse set of Pareto-optimal architectures across multiple devices in a single search run, leaving users without an efficient way to explore trade-offs across heterogeneous hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250977/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At search time, the method consumes a preference vector r sampled from a Dirichlet distribution over the M-dimensional probability simplex (encoding user trade-off preferences) and a fixed-size device embedding d_t (obtained by evaluating 10 reference architectures on the target device to capture its hardware characteristics). At inference time, 24 equidistant preference vectors are passed through the pretrained MetaHypernetwork conditioned on a new device's embedding to yield 24 Pareto-optimal architectures in a zero-shot manner, with no additional search or fine-tuning required for unseen devices.

## Method

MODNAS introduces a MetaHypernetwork that combines a bank of K independent hypernetworks (each mapping a scalarization vector to architectural parameters via embedding layers) with a learned linear attention layer that maps device feature vectors to mixing weights over the bank, enabling a single model to generate device-specific architectural distributions. The upper-level optimization uses Multiple Gradient Descent (MGD) with a Frank-Wolfe solver to compute a shared descent direction across all T devices simultaneously, ensuring all objectives improve monotonically, while the lower level updates supernetwork weights via standard gradient descent. Discrete architectures are sampled through the ReinMax estimator for second-order-accurate gradient estimation without second-order derivatives, and a pretrained MetaPredictor (frozen during search) provides differentiable estimates of hardware metrics like latency and energy. This design reduces the Pareto-front profiling cost from O(T) or O(CT) independent searches to O(1) single search run, while the meta-learning over device embeddings enables zero-shot transfer to new hardware.

## Results

On NAS-Bench-201 (19 devices, CIFAR-10), MODNAS consistently achieves the highest hypervolume (HV) across every device, with most baselines requiring more than 2× the number of architecture evaluations to reach comparable HV. On the HAT Transformer space (WMT'14 En-De), MODNAS evaluates only 24 generated architectures and outperforms all baselines (which require 1,300–6,000 evaluations) in HV. On MobileNetV3/OFA (ImageNet-1k, 12 devices), MODNAS achieves a higher average HV across all devices compared to baselines run for 192 hours. On HW-GPT-Bench (OpenWebText, 8 GPU devices), MODNAS matches or surpasses all MOO baselines with the same time budget, demonstrating effectiveness on non-convex Pareto fronts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MODNAS outperforms all 12 baselines across every search space and device in hypervolume. Against blackbox MOO methods (NSGA-II, CMA-ES, qEHVI, LaMOO, RS-BO, MO-ASHA, MO-RE), MODNAS achieves higher HV with only 24 full architecture evaluations, whereas these methods require 1,300–6,000 evaluations (on HAT) or equivalent search-time budgets (on NAS-Bench-201 and OFA). Random Search, while a strong baseline, is consistently dominated by MODNAS in HV across all 19 devices on NAS-Bench-201. Against the closest constrained-NAS competitor MetaD2A+HELP, MODNAS wins on both HV and efficiency: MetaD2A+HELP requires 24 separate search runs (one per constraint) to profile the Pareto front, while MODNAS profiles it in a single run, and MetaD2A+HELP prioritizes performance over diversity, yielding less varied solutions. LEMONADE and ProxylessNAS, which apply hardware constraints during search, require an independent search per device (O(NT) or O(CT) complexity), whereas MODNAS achieves O(1) for both search and Pareto set construction. On the OFA/ImageNet-1k experiment, even NSGA-II augmented with a zero-cost proxy (Zico) underperforms MODNAS, confirming that direct accuracy optimization via the supernetwork is more effective than proxy-guided search.

## Relation to Prior Work

MODNAS sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: differentiable one-shot NAS (DARTS, OFA), hypernetwork-based Pareto front learning (Lin et al. 2020, Navon et al. 2021), and multi-gradient descent for multi-task/multi-objective optimization (Désidéri 2012, Sener & Koltun 2018). The key departure from prior hardware-aware NAS (ProxylessNAS, MetaD2A+HELP, OFA+HELP) is that those methods embed hardware constraints directly in the objective, requiring a separate search per constraint and per device. MODNAS instead conditions a MetaHypernetwork on both preference vectors and device embeddings, and uses MGD with a Frank-Wolfe solver to jointly optimize across all devices in a single search run, reducing Pareto set construction cost from O(CT) to O(1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for teams deploying models across heterogeneous hardware: a single trained MetaHypernetwork yields Pareto-optimal architectures for any new device zero-shot, eliminating the need to re-run expensive searches per device or per constraint. The method scales to three objectives (accuracy, latency, energy) and diverse architectures (CNNs, encoder-decoder Transformers, GPT-2) without architectural changes. Limitations include inheriting gradient-based NAS fragility (sensitivity to hyperparameters, risk of local optima) and reliance on pretrained MetaPredictors whose predictions may diverge from ground truth. Open questions include how the approach behaves with many more objectives or in settings where the Pareto front is highly non-convex, and whether the one-shot weight-sharing assumption holds for very large model families.
