## Problem

Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs) for molecular discovery rely on random mutations and crossovers that ignore task-specific chemical knowledge, requiring thousands of expensive oracle evaluations to converge. While chemistry-aware LLMs encode rich structural and property knowledge from large corpora, they have not been integrated as genetic operators within EA frameworks for black-box molecular optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251858/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial pool of 120 molecules (SMILES strings sampled from ZINC 250K) and a text prompt describing the target objective (e.g., 'This molecule inhibits JNK3'). For GPT-4, crossover consumes two parent SMILES strings plus their fitness scores; for BioT5, mutation consumes a SELFIES representation (guaranteeing valid output); for MolSTM, mutation operates in embedding space via gradient descent. The output is a population of optimized molecules whose objective scores improve over the initial pool, evaluated under a fixed oracle budget of 10,000 calls (1,000 for docking). A Tanimoto-distance-based selection pressure prunes offspring to those structurally closest to the current fittest molecule.

## Method

MOLLEO replaces the random crossover and mutation operators in Graph-GA with LLM-guided proposals. For GPT-4, the LLM acts as the crossover operator: given two parent SMILES strings and their fitness scores, it is prompted to generate a new molecule with a higher objective value, while the default Graph-GA random mutation is retained. For BioT5, the LLM acts as the mutation operator: the top-Y molecules are converted to SELFIES and prompted to produce a SELFIES molecule satisfying the objective description, with the default Graph-GA crossover retained. For MolSTM, mutation is performed by gradient-descent optimization of a molecule embedding toward the text-prompt embedding (Eq. 6–7), with a regularization term limiting deviation from the input embedding. In all variants, a selection pressure filters offspring by Tanimoto distance to the fittest molecule in the pool, and the evolutionary loop (selection, crossover, mutation, evaluation, Pareto or fitness-based selection) repeats until the oracle budget is exhausted. The key insight is that LLMs trained on chemical corpora make structurally informed edits guided by natural-language objective descriptions, producing higher-fitness offspring per oracle call than random graph-level perturbations.

## Results

On the PMO benchmark (23 single-objective tasks), MOLLEO (GPT-4) achieves the best overall Top-10 AUC total of 17.862, outperforming all baselines and winning 15 out of 23 individual tasks. MOLLEO (BioT5) ranks second with a total of 15.424, closely trailing GPT-4 while being freely available. On the more challenging protein-ligand docking tasks (DRD3, EGFR, Adenosine A2A), MOLLEO (BioT5) achieves the best docking scores and fastest convergence. In multi-objective optimization (Table 2), MOLLEO (GPT-4) consistently outperforms Graph-GA on both summation AUC and hypervolume across all three tasks (e.g., 2.482 vs 2.120 Sum AUC and 0.727 vs 0.603 hypervolume on Task 1 with Pareto selection).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOLLEO (GPT-4) outperforms all five baselines overall, achieving the highest total Top-10 AUC (17.862) and winning 15 of 23 individual tasks. Against the base Graph-GA (total 13.823), all three MOLLEO variants improve performance, with GPT-4 yielding the largest gains (e.g., +0.237 on JNK3, +0.454 on scaffold_hop). Among RL-based baselines, Augmented Memory (15.356) and REINVENT (14.036) are the strongest competitors but are still surpassed by both MOLLEO (GPT-4) and MOLLEO (BioT5). GP BO (13.182) is the weakest baseline across all task categories. On protein-ligand docking (Figure 3), MOLLEO (BioT5) achieves the best docking scores for all three proteins, while MOLLEO (GPT-4) surprisingly underperforms Graph-GA on the Adenosine A2A receptor task. In multi-objective settings (Table 2), open-source LLMs (BioT5, MolSTM) degrade relative to single-objective performance, whereas MOLLEO (GPT-4) maintains its lead over Graph-GA on both summation and hypervolume metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Graph-GA evolutionary algorithm (Jensen, 2019) for molecular optimization and repurposes chemistry-aware LLMs (GPT-4, BioT5, MoleculeSTM) as informed genetic operators (crossover and mutation) rather than standalone generators or direct editors. It departs from prior LLM-in-chemistry work that relies on in-context learning or single-step molecular editing by embedding LLMs within an iterative EA loop with fitness-based selection and structural-similarity pruning. The framework also extends beyond Graph-GA, demonstrating compatibility with Genetic GFN, JANUS, and Augmented Memory, positioning LLM-based operators as a modular component transferable across evolutionary and RL-based molecular design pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the number of expensive oracle calls needed to reach competitive objective values, MOLLEO makes it more feasible to deploy costly but accurate oracles (e.g., protein-ligand docking, wet-lab assays) in practical drug discovery workflows. The finding that the free, open-source BioT5 achieves near-GPT-4 performance on most tasks lowers the barrier to adoption. Key limitations include reliance on proxy benchmarks (pre-trained classifiers, docking scores) rather than true biological activity, sensitivity of open-source models to prompt phrasing, and degradation of open-source models under multi-objective settings. Open questions include validation on real experimental oracles, scaling to larger and more complex objective landscapes, and whether the framework generalizes to 3D structure-based generation.
