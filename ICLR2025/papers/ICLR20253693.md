## Problem

Concept-based RL policies (which route decisions through human-interpretable concept bottlenecks) require concept labels for every state encountered during training, typically millions to tens of millions of annotations. Prior work such as CPM assumes unlimited access to an oracle for concept labels, making interpretable RL impractical for real-world deployment where human annotation is costly and VLM-based labeling incurs prohibitive API costs. LICORICE addresses this by enabling high-performance interpretable RL with only 500–5000 concept labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB environment state images (e.g., 84×84 Atari frames, 4×4 or 3×3 Minigrid grids, rendered CartPole frames) along with a small budget of ground-truth concept labels (500–5000 total, queried adaptively during training). The policy outputs a distribution over discrete actions via PPO, while the concept bottleneck layer produces either continuous values (MSE loss) or categorical predictions (one classification head per discrete concept). The CNN feature extractor is shared between the policy and value networks, and the action head receives only concept predictions as input, enforcing the interpretability constraint.

## Method

LICORICE combines three mechanisms to minimize concept annotation queries. First, iterative training alternately freezes the concept network g and the action network f across M iterations, ensuring concept learning always operates on data from the current (improving) policy rather than stale off-policy states. Second, data decorrelation uses a per-state acceptance probability p and a budget multiplier τ to subsample rollout states, exploiting Markov mixing time to break temporal correlations and build a diverse candidate pool. Third, disagreement-based active learning trains an ensemble of N concept models from scratch each iteration and selects the b most informative states for labeling using a query-by-committee score (variation ratio for classification) or prediction variance (for regression). The full pipeline uses PPO for behavior learning, a CNN backbone for feature extraction, and in complex environments adds a KL-anchored retraining step in the final iteration to avoid local optima.

## Results

LICORICE achieves 99–100% of the maximum reward in all five environments (PixelCartPole, DoorKey, DynamicObstacles, Boxing, Pong) using only 300–5,000 concept labels, matching the budget-unconstrained CPM baseline that uses 1M–15M labels (2,000–13,000× more). Concept error is comparable to CPM in four of five environments, with the largest gap in Pong (0.24 vs. 0.00). Against budget-constrained baselines (Sequential-Q, Disagreement-Q, Random-Q), LICORICE achieves the highest reward in 4 of 5 environments, with the largest margins in PixelCartPole (100% vs. 36% for the second-best) and DoorKey (99% vs. 89%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the budget-unconstrained CPM baseline (Zabounidis et al., CoRL 2023), LICORICE achieves nearly identical reward in all five environments (99–100% of the upper bound) while using 2,000–13,000× fewer concept labels (300–5,000 vs. 1M–15M). The only notable gap is concept error in Pong (0.24 for LICORICE vs. 0.00 for CPM), where the large number of discrete concept values (up to 704 per concept) makes accurate prediction harder with limited labels. Against the three budget-constrained baselines (Sequential-Q, Disagreement-Q, Random-Q), LICORICE wins on reward in 4 of 5 environments, with the largest margins in PixelCartPole (1.00 vs. 0.36 for Disagreement-Q) and DoorKey (0.99 vs. 0.89 for Random-Q). In DynamicObstacles, LICORICE ties with Disagreement-Q (both ~0.99 reward, 0.00 concept error), as the environment is simple enough that even a single-iteration strategy suffices. The VLM-annotated variant (LICORICE+GPT-4o) performs well in DoorKey (83% reward) and DynamicObstacles (88%) but degrades severely in PixelCartPole (6%), Boxing (22%), and Pong (13%), where GPT-4o's labeling error is high (0.24–0.88 per-concept error).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept bottleneck model framework (Koh et al., 2020) and its RL adaptation CPM (Zabounidis et al., 2023) by addressing a practical bottleneck that prior work ignores: the assumption of unlimited concept label access during training. LICORICE departs from CPM's joint training paradigm (L = L^RL + λ_C·L^C) by introducing iterative training that alternates between concept learning and behavior learning, combined with data decorrelation and query-by-committee active learning (Seung et al., 1992) to select the most informative states for annotation. The method is positioned as the first to formalize and solve the label-efficiency problem specifically for concept-based RL, rather than treating concept labels as a free resource.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing concept annotation from 1M–15M labels (CPM) to 300–5000 (LICORICE) makes interpretable RL feasible in domains where continuous human or API-based labeling is prohibitively expensive, such as autonomous driving or healthcare. However, the VLM experiments reveal a significant open question: GPT-4o succeeds on simple grid environments but fails catastrophically on complex Atari games (0.98 concept error in Boxing), suggesting that automated annotation remains unreliable for continuous or high-cardinality concepts. Broader open questions include how to design concept representations that are both VLM-annotatable and sufficient for policy performance, and whether the iterative training scheme generalizes beyond PPO to other RL algorithms or to settings where the concept set itself must be discovered.
