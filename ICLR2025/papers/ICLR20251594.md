## Problem

Existing egocentric video representation learning methods align video features with high-level narrations (e.g., 'C draws on a book') but overlook the fine-grained dynamics of hands and objects that dominate egocentric content. No suitable training data captures detailed hand-object motion in natural language, and prior LLM rephrasing approaches can only operate at the same abstraction level as the original annotations, leaving a critical gap in what the model learns about hand-object interactions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes egocentric video clips through a dual-rate sampling scheme: the ViT backbone receives 4 frames while the motion adapter receives 16 frames (λ=4 upsampling), with spatial resolution of 320px shorter side during pretraining. The HOD pipeline first extracts hand/object bounding boxes via 100DOH across 16 uniformly sampled frames, then uses Yi-34B to generate narrations describing trajectories, contact states, and spatial relationships. The output is a D-dimensional video embedding aligned with a text embedding via InfoNCE, which downstream supports classification, retrieval, action segmentation, and robot manipulation policy learning.

## Method

The paper contributes two coupled components. On the data side, HOD is a pipeline that converts 100DOH bounding-box trajectories into natural-language descriptions of hand-object dynamics (movement direction, contact, relative position) using Yi-34B, producing 7.4M enriched video-text pairs from Ego4D and HowTo100M. On the model side, EgoVideo introduces a lightweight motion adapter injected into each ViT layer, composed of a 2D spatial convolution (aggregating information across hands and objects at different locations) followed by a 1D temporal convolution (modeling dynamics between adjacent frames), with a downsample ratio γ=0.5 to keep parameter overhead low. A co-training strategy trains the backbone at 4fps with all parameters unfrozen while simultaneously training the adapter at 16fps with the backbone frozen, then concatenates both pathway outputs through a linear projection. This design lets the model capture fine-grained spatiotemporal hand-object dynamics at a fraction of the cost of simply increasing frame count across the entire backbone.

## Results

In zero-shot evaluation, EgoVideo-G (1B params, 7.4M pretraining clips) achieves 47.1 mAP on EK-100 MIR, 28.5% top-1 on EK-100 CLS, 58.0% mean accuracy on EGTEA, and 63.0% top-1 on EgoMCQ, setting new state-of-the-art across all four benchmarks. Compared to the previous best EMBED-L (38.3M clips), EgoVideo-G improves EK-100 MIR mAP by 6.3%, EK-100 CLS top-1 by 5.7%, and EGTEA mean accuracy by 16.3%, while using only 7.4M pretraining clips. In fine-tuning, EgoVideo-G reaches 60.3 mAP on EK-100 MIR and 80.0% top-1 on EGTEA, outperforming EMBED-L by 4.3% and 3.9% respectively. On the Franka Kitchen robot manipulation benchmark, EgoVideo achieves 66.0% average success rate, surpassing MVP (56.0%) and Voltron (54.1%) and matching MPI+Det (66.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoVideo outperforms all prior egocentric representation learning methods (EgoVLPv2, LaViLa, AVION, EMBED, Helping Hands) across every zero-shot and fine-tuning benchmark, despite using only 7.4M pretraining clips versus 35–38M for competitors. The largest margins appear on EGTEA (hand-object interaction focused), where EgoVideo-G reaches 58.0% mean accuracy vs. EMBED-L's 40.3% (+17.7%) and LaViLa-L's 34.1% (+23.9%), directly validating the hand-object dynamics modeling. On EK-100 MIR, EgoVideo-G achieves 47.1 mAP vs. EMBED-L's 40.8% (+6.3%) and Helping Hands' 37.5% (+9.6%). On the Franka Kitchen robot manipulation task, EgoVideo (66.0% avg.) surpasses MVP (56.0%) and Voltron (54.1%) by over 10%, and is within 0.9% of MPI+Det (66.9%), which uses additional detection and prediction transformers. On action segmentation (HOI4D, GTEA), EgoVideo features with ASFormer outperform I3D, AVION, MS-TCN, MS-TCN++, and even DiffAct, achieving 74.8 F1@50 on HOI4D vs. AVION's 70.2 and I3D's 35.0. The only comparison where EgoVideo does not clearly win is against MPI+Det on Franka Kitchen, where MPI's additional video prediction supervision gives it a slight 0.9% edge in average success rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-style dual-encoder contrastive framework for egocentric video representation learning by introducing a data pipeline (HOD) that converts hand-object motion trajectories into natural language, and a model (EgoVideo) with a lightweight spatiotemporal motion adapter. It departs from prior egocentric pretraining methods (LaViLa, EMBED, AVION) that rephrase or augment narrations only at the same high-level abstraction as original annotations, and from Helping Hands which uses hand-object coordinates as auxiliary targets but ignores temporal dynamics. The co-training strategy—training the backbone at low framerate and the adapter at high framerate—draws directly from SlowFast's dual-pathway architecture, repurposing it for parameter-efficient fine-grained motion learning rather than speed/accuracy trade-offs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly encode fine-grained hand-object dynamics into egocentric video-language pretraining, and the finding that 7.4M high-quality HOD clips outperform 35–38M clips from prior methods suggests data quality (semantic richness of motion descriptions) can substitute for data quantity in representation learning. The generalization to robot manipulation (Franka Kitchen), where EgoVideo matches or exceeds dedicated robot learning methods like MPI, hints at broader utility for embodied AI. Key limitations include error accumulation from the hand-object detector and LLM rephrasing pipeline, and the reliance on 100DOH bounding boxes that may miss occluded or partially visible hands. Open questions include whether the dynamics can be encoded in non-linguistic formats (e.g., structured motion tokens) and whether the approach transfers to exocentric or multi-person settings.
