## Problem

Current video LLMs for temporal grounding rely exclusively on natural language generation, indiscriminately blending timestamps, scores, and captions into a single text stream. This ignores the inherent structure of videos, where timestamps, salient scores, and textual captions are distinct, sequentially related components, creating a fundamental mismatch between the model's output format and the video's structure that limits zero-shot VTG performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253226/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TRACE takes a T-frame video (uniformly sampled, encoded via CLIP ViT-L and compressed to 8 visual tokens per frame via Slot-Based Compression, augmented with 6 time tokens per frame from a dedicated time encoder) and a text instruction as input. It produces a structured sequence of event triplets, where each event contains fixed-length timestamp tokens (4 integer digits + dot + 1 fractional digit), a salient score in the same numeric format, and a free-form text caption, all separated by special <sync> and <sep> tokens rather than rendered as natural language.

## Method

TRACE introduces a causal event modeling framework that factorizes the joint probability of each event as P(t_k|prev)·P(s_k|t_k,prev)·P(c_k|s_k,t_k,prev), treating timestamps, scores, and captions as sequentially conditioned sub-tasks within each event. Architecturally, it employs separate encoders and decoding heads for each of the four tasks (visual frames, text, timestamps, scores) on top of a Mistral-7B-v0.2 backbone, with task tokens arranged in an interleaved sequence (time→score→text per event, events ordered chronologically). During generation, an adaptive head-switching mechanism uses the <sync> token to cycle between the time, score, and text decoding heads. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 initializes the vision compression layer and task-specific encoders/heads on captioning and VTG data, then Stage 2 fine-tunes the full LLM backbone with instruction tuning across VTG, captioning, and video QA datasets.

## Results

In zero-shot evaluation, TRACE (7B) achieves CIDEr 8.1 and F1 22.4 on Youcook2, R@1(IOU=0.5) 40.3 and R@1(IOU=0.7) 19.4 on Charades-STA, and mAP 26.8 and HIT@1 42.7 on QVHighlights, outperforming the strongest baseline VTG-LLM (7B) by 3.1 CIDEr, 4.9 F1, 6.5 R@1(0.5), 3.7 R@1(0.7), 10.3 mAP, and 9.2 HIT@1 respectively. After fine-tuning on Youcook2, TRACE reaches CIDEr 35.5 and F1 31.8, surpassing task-specific models like CM² (CIDEr 31.7, F1 28.4). On Charades-STA after fine-tuning, TRACE achieves R@1(0.5)=61.7 and R@1(0.7)=41.4, competitive with non-generative models like InternVideo2-6B (70.0/49.0) while retaining zero-shot multi-task capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACE decisively outperforms all traditional video LLMs (Valley, VideoChat, Video-LLaMA) by large margins across every metric, e.g., CIDEr 8.1 vs. 0.0–0.6 on Youcook2 and R@1(0.5) 40.3 vs. 2.7–4.7 on Charades-STA. Among temporal grounding video LLMs, TRACE beats VTG-LLM (its closest competitor) by 3.1 CIDEr and 6.5 R@1(0.5), and surpasses the larger 13B VTimeLLM (R@1(0.5)=34.3) with only 7B parameters. On QVHighlights, TRACE's mAP of 26.8 exceeds Momentor (7.6) and VTG-LLM (16.5) by substantial margins. After fine-tuning, TRACE (CIDEr 35.5, F1 31.8 on Youcook2) surpasses task-specific models PDVC (CIDEr 22.7), Vid2Seq without audio (CIDEr 25.3), and CM² (CIDEr 31.7, F1 28.4). On Charades-STA fine-tuned, TRACE (R@1(0.5)=61.7, R@1(0.7)=41.4) beats VDI (52.3/31.4) and Moment-DETR (55.7/34.2) but remains below InternVideo2-6B (70.0/49.0), a non-generative model lacking zero-shot and multi-task capability. On general video understanding benchmarks, TRACE-uni (MVBench 53.8, VideoMME 49.6) matches or exceeds VideoLLaMA2 (54.6/46.6) using only ~2M training samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACE builds directly on VTG-LLM (Guo et al., 2024), adopting its time token formatting and slot-based visual compression, but departs fundamentally from the broader line of video LLMs for temporal grounding (TimeChat, VTimeLLM, Momentor, HawkEye) that treat all outputs as flat natural language sequences. The key conceptual shift is the causal event modeling framework, which restructures LLM outputs into ordered event triplets (timestamps, salient scores, captions) with separate encoders and decoding heads per modality, rather than interleaving all information into a single text token stream. This positions TRACE as a task-interleaved architecture that preserves the pretrained LLM's language capabilities while imposing the structural inductive bias that videos inherently possess.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRACE demonstrates that a single 7B video LLM can handle dense video captioning, moment retrieval, highlight detection, and video summarization simultaneously in zero-shot, with performance competitive to task-specific non-generative models after fine-tuning — a practical capability that prior video LLMs lacked. The ablation showing complete instruction-following failure when independent encoders/heads are removed validates that the structured decomposition is not merely a performance tweak but a necessary architectural constraint. Open questions include whether the decoder-only causal structure limits modeling of complex inter-event causal relationships (the authors suggest integrating causality discovery graphs), and whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific VTG task family to broader video reasoning.
