## Problem

Audio LLMs can extract content and paralinguistic information from speech but remain unaware of signal quality (distortion, noisiness, discontinuity), because existing speech quality datasets contain only numerical MOS scores with no natural language descriptions, making this task invisible during multi-task pre-training. This limits the ability of multimodal agents to self-assess input quality or automate evaluation of generative speech systems.

## Input / Output

For MOS prediction, the model receives a single raw speech waveform and produces a multi-sentence descriptive analysis covering four sub-dimensions (noisiness, coloration, discontinuity, loudness) followed by a final MOS score. For A/B testing, it takes two speech samples and generates a comparative analysis across sub-dimensions culminating in a preference judgment. For synthetic word detection (SWD), it receives speech containing 1–3 edited words and must identify which specific words are synthetic. The training corpus (20k examples) is generated by prompting LLaMA-3.1-70B-Instruct with NISQA human-annotated metadata using in-context learning demonstrations, rather than being directly annotated by humans.

## Method

ALLD (Alignment with LLM Distillation) formulates speech quality evaluation as a preference optimization problem where the audio LLM's generated response y_a is the 'dispreferred' completion and an expert text LLM's response y_t (generated from meta-information scores) is the 'preferred' completion, optimized via a DPO-style loss. The key departure from standard RLHF is that the reference model π_ref is an expert text LLM (e.g., Qwen-7B) providing token-level distillation, rather than a frozen copy of the policy model. Training proceeds in two stages: a warm-up SFT on a subset of the corpus to give the audio LLM basic zero-shot capability, followed by sampling y_a from the audio LLM and constructing preference pairs for DPO optimization. The audio LLM (Qwen2-Audio) must learn to extract quality-relevant acoustic features from raw waveforms and generate descriptive, reasoning-based responses that align with the expert LLM's text-only analysis, effectively performing cross-modal distillation from text to audio-conditioned generation.

## Results

On the NISQA in-domain test set, ALLD (2×) achieves the best MOS prediction performance with LCC 0.93, SRCC 0.93, MSE 0.17, and BLEU 25.84, surpassing the best regression baseline Wav2vec2 (MSE 0.27) and the best audio LLM baseline Qwen2-Audio Full-ft (MSE 0.21, BLEU 23.84). On the A/B test task, joint training with MOS prediction yields 98.6% accuracy and BLEU 30.17. For synthetic word detection on LibriSpeech, the model achieves 51.72% accuracy for single-word edits with VoiceCraft-330M, compared to 6.76% for random guessing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALLD (2×) outperforms all six baselines on the primary MOS prediction task. Against the best regression model Wav2vec2 (LCC 0.93, SRCC 0.92, MSE 0.27), ALLD matches LCC but improves SRCC to 0.93 and reduces MSE from 0.27 to 0.17, while also providing descriptive analysis (BLEU 25.84) that regression models cannot produce. Against the SOTA NISQA regression model CNN-SA-AP (MSE 0.23), ALLD achieves a 26% relative MSE reduction (0.17 vs. 0.23). Among audio LLM baselines, ALLD surpasses Qwen2-Audio Full-ft (the strongest audio LLM baseline) by 0.02 in LCC/SRCC, 0.04 in MSE, and 2.0 in BLEU. SALMONN and Qwen-Audio show substantially weaker performance (MSE 0.33–0.34 and 0.26 respectively), confirming that their multi-task pre-training did not expose them to quality evaluation. On the A/B test, joint training with MOS prediction boosts accuracy from 95.6% to 98.6% and BLEU from 29.02 to 30.17, demonstrating complementary task benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio LLM line (SALMONN, Qwen-Audio, Qwen2-Audio) by addressing a gap those models leave: they process speech content and paralinguistic features but remain blind to signal quality. It also departs from the MOS-prediction regression tradition (CNN-SA-AP, WavLM, Wav2vec2) by reframing quality evaluation as a natural-language generation task grounded in the NISQA multidimensional annotations. Methodologically, ALLD repurposes the DPO preference-optimization framework (Rafailov et al., 2024) in a non-standard way: the reference model π_ref is an expert text LLM providing token-level distillation rather than a frozen copy of the policy, which is a conceptual departure from mainstream RLHF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first natural-language speech quality evaluation corpus and the ALLD training recipe give audio LLMs a practical path to human-like qualitative assessment, which is essential for automating evaluation of TTS and speech-editing systems and for enabling self-improvement loops in multimodal agents. Key limitations remain: evaluation is utterance-level (word-level defects are only partially addressed via SWD), the causal link between descriptive analysis and numerical MOS prediction is observed but not mechanistically explained, and the approach depends on a warm-up SFT before preference optimization. Open questions include whether the CoT-like benefit of descriptive reasoning generalizes to other perceptual tasks and how to obtain fine-grained (word/segment-level) quality labels at scale.
