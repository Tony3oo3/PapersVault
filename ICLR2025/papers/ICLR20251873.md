## Problem

While piano key presses can be directly derived from sheet music or MIDI, the transitional hand movements and fingerings between key presses remain an open problem for AI-assisted piano instruction. Existing piano-hand datasets are either too small (under 5 hours), lack continuous gesture sequences, or do not jointly provide music and hand motion annotations, making it infeasible to learn the nonlinear mapping from audio to realistic hand motions.

## Input / Output

The model takes an 8-second piano audio clip (normalized to 30 FPS) as input, processed through a frozen pre-trained audio feature extractor (Wav2Vec2.0 or HuBERT) to produce frame-level feature vectors. It outputs a 3D hand position sequence P ∈ R^{N×3} and a hand gesture sequence Θ ∈ R^{N×J×3} (Euler angles per joint) for both left and right hands, parameterized in the MANO hand model space. The underlying dataset consists of 30-second clips from bird's-eye-view videos at minimum 1080×1920 resolution, with hand poses annotated via HaMeR.

## Method

The baseline decomposes hand motion generation into two stages: a discriminative position predictor and a generative position-guided gesture generator. The position predictor uses a Transformer or Mamba (SSM) decoder to map audio features to 3D hand positions, trained with L1 position loss plus a velocity loss for temporal stability. The gesture generator is a 4-layer U-Net diffusion model (DDPM with velocity prediction) that denoises hand gesture sequences conditioned on both audio features and the predicted positions, capturing the complex distribution of finger movements at different keyboard locations. Training proceeds in two stages: the position predictor is trained first, then frozen while the gesture generator is trained with the predicted positions as guidance. This disentanglement addresses the highly nonlinear audio-to-gesture relationship that causes discriminative models to collapse to average poses.

## Results

On the PianoMotion10M validation set, the best configuration (Our-Large with HuBERT + Transformer decoder) achieves an FID of 3.281, FGD of 0.372 (left) / 0.351 (right), WGD of 0.217 / 0.244, Position Distance of 0.037 / 0.030, and Smoothness of 0.248 / 0.205. The base model (Our-Base, HuBERT + SSM) reaches FID 3.412 with comparable gesture fidelity. Across all configurations, the two-stage position-predictor + diffusion-gesture-generator architecture consistently outperforms single-stage baselines on FID by 0.87–1.36 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two re-implemented baselines adapted from speech-driven face/gesture generation: EmoTalk (Peng et al., ICCV 2023) and LivelySpeaker (Zhi et al., ICCV 2023). The best Our-Large (HuBERT+TF) configuration outperforms EmoTalk by 1.36 FID points (3.281 vs. 4.645), reduces left-hand FGD from 0.445 to 0.372, and improves smoothness from 0.353 to 0.248 (left hand). Against LivelySpeaker, the improvement is even more pronounced: FID drops from 4.157 to 3.281, left-hand FGD from 0.538 to 0.372, and right-hand FGD from 0.535 to 0.351. The paper attributes EmoTalk's weaker performance to its tendency to produce a static average gesture, while LivelySpeaker's MLP-based diffusion backbone trades fidelity for speed. The two-stage design (position predictor + position-guided diffusion gesture generator) is the key architectural advantage, as it disentangles the highly nonlinear audio-to-position mapping from the stochastic gesture generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of piano-hand datasets (PianoHand2.5M, TwohandsMusic, Piano-fingering) and music-conditioned motion generation (AIST++, MDM, MotionDiffuse), but departs from both in critical ways. Prior piano datasets provide discrete fingering labels or static poses rather than continuous 3D hand motion sequences, while music-motion datasets target full-body dance rather than fine-grained hand articulation. The baseline model's two-stage decomposition—discriminative position prediction followed by position-guided diffusion-based gesture generation—is a novel architectural choice that avoids the mode-averaging problem of end-to-end discriminative generation, drawing on DDPM and recent SSM/Transformer sequence modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PianoMotion10M fills a clear gap as the first publicly available large-scale dataset (116 hours, 10M frames) pairing piano audio with continuous 3D hand motion, enabling research on AI piano coaching and human-piano interaction that was previously infeasible due to data scarcity. Key limitations include subject imbalance (14 experts, heavily skewed toward two), unaligned piano key positions across videos, and failure on out-of-distribution inputs like extremely fast passages. Open questions include whether the dataset's bird's-eye-view constraint generalizes to other camera angles, whether the 30-second clip length is sufficient for learning long-range fingering strategies, and how to handle multi-instrument or multi-player scenarios.
