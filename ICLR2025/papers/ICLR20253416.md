## Problem

Score-based diffusion models have become state-of-the-art generative models, but it remains unclear how to adapt them to generate long multivariate time series as continuous-time signals. Existing function-space diffusion approaches lack benchmarks on real-world temporal data, and prior signature-based methods are limited by non-scalable inversion algorithms that only work on short piecewise-linear paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253416/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series as piecewise-linear paths in R^d, augmented with time and basis-specific channels (e.g., sin(t), cos(t)-1 for Fourier inversion) to form paths in R^{r+2}. Log-signatures are computed via Chen's relation and truncated at level N+2, yielding a compact Euclidean vector of dimension β(d,N+2). The output is a reconstructed time series of 1000 points, recovered by evaluating closed-form polynomial expressions on the generated log-signature to obtain Fourier or orthogonal polynomial coefficients, then summing the basis expansion.

## Method

SigDiffusion operates on log-signatures, which live in a finite-dimensional Lie algebra isomorphic to R^{β(d,n)}, making them a natural Euclidean space for standard score-based diffusion. A transformer with sinusoidal positional embeddings learns the score function to reverse a linear-variance SDE that perturbs log-signatures while preserving their algebraic structure. The key theoretical contribution is a set of closed-form inversion formulae proving that Fourier coefficients (Theorem 3.1) and orthogonal polynomial coefficients (Theorem 3.2) can be expressed as explicit linear functionals on the signature, equivalently polynomial functions on the log-signature, via the shuffle and half-shuffle products. This eliminates the need for iterative or optimization-based inversion, reducing reconstruction to evaluating linear combinations of signature terms with O(nd^{n+2}) complexity. The pipeline is: compute log-signatures → train/sample diffusion model → invert via closed-form formulae to recover the path.

## Results

On five time series datasets (Sines, Predator-prey, HEPC, Exchange Rates, Weather), SigDiffusion achieves the best or near-best Discriminative Score on 3 of 5 datasets (HEPC: 0.070, Exchange Rates: 0.278, Weather: 0.350) and the best Predictive Score on 3 of 5 (Exchange Rates: 0.057, Weather: 0.168, Predator-prey: 0.056 tied with DDO). The model is also the most efficient, with only 206K–282K parameters, 8–9 min training time, and 11–12 sec sampling time, compared to 4.12M parameters and 2.6–3.6 h training for the strongest competitor DDO. For signature inversion, the proposed closed-form formulae reduce L₂ reconstruction error substantially compared to the Insertion and Optimisation methods (Figure 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDO (Lim et al., 2023), the strongest competitor, SigDiffusion wins on Discriminative Score for HEPC (0.070 vs 0.081), Exchange Rates (0.278 vs 0.326), and Weather (0.350 vs 0.356), and on Predictive Score for Exchange Rates (0.057 vs 0.094) and Weather (0.168 vs 0.307), while DDO edges ahead on Sines (0.040 vs 0.100) and HEPC Predictive Score (0.044 vs 0.050). Against Diffusion-TS (Yuan & Qiao, 2024), SigDiffusion dominates on every dataset for both metrics, with the largest gaps on Weather (Predictive Score 0.168 vs 0.438) and Predator-prey (0.056 vs 0.482). Against CSPD-GP (Biloš et al., 2023), SigDiffusion clearly outperforms the Transformer variant on all datasets and is competitive with the RNN variant on Predator-prey (0.184 vs 0.168 Discriminative) while winning on HEPC, Exchange Rates, and Weather. For signature inversion (Section 5.1), the proposed closed-form formulae via Legendre polynomials and Fourier series achieve substantially lower L₂ reconstruction error than both the Insertion method (Chang & Lyons, 2019) and the Optimisation method (Kidger et al., 2019), as shown in Figure 2. Notably, SigDiffusion achieves these results with 15–20× fewer parameters (206K–282K vs 4.12M for DDO) and 20–25× faster training (8–9 min vs 2.6–3.6 h).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: score-based diffusion models (Song & Ermon, 2019; Song et al., 2020) and rough path signature theory (Lyons, 1998; Chen, 1957). While prior diffusion-based time series generators (Diffusion-TS, CSPD-GP, DDO) operate directly on raw signals or in infinite-dimensional function spaces, SigDiffusion instead performs diffusion in the finite-dimensional Lie algebra of log-signatures, leveraging Chen's relation for efficient computation. The key departure from prior signature inversion work (Chang & Lyons, 2019; Kidger et al., 2019) is the provision of closed-form, scalable inversion formulae expressing Fourier and orthogonal polynomial coefficients as explicit polynomial functions of the log-signature, replacing iterative or optimisation-based approaches that only worked on short piecewise-linear paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form inversion formulae are a significant theoretical contribution that removes a major practical barrier to using signatures in generative modelling, and the resulting pipeline achieves competitive generation quality with roughly 200K parameters versus 4M+ for function-space diffusion baselines, training in minutes rather than hours. A key limitation is the exponential growth of signature dimensionality with truncation level, which constrains fidelity for high-frequency signals and motivates the authors' suggestion of alternative Lie-group embeddings from rough path theory. Open questions include whether these inversion techniques extend to other path developments, how discrete-time signatures might enable diffusion-based text generation, and whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional or very long-horizon time series.
