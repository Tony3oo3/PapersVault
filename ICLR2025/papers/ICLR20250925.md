## Problem

Existing multi-objective molecular optimization (MOMO) methods either treat pretrained property predictors as mere discriminators without decoding the learned chemical knowledge, or rely on linear scalarization that produces biased solutions and leaves regions of the Pareto front unexplored. The core challenge is how to simultaneously handle conflicting or correlated chemical properties in the discrete molecular space while remaining sample-efficient, since evolutionary and Bayesian approaches require thousands of oracle calls.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250925/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial molecule (converted to a differentiable scaffolding tree with K expansion nodes for gradient computation) and a weight vector λ ∈ R^m specifying the relative importance of each target property. It outputs a discrete molecule assembled from the optimized scaffolding tree, along with its multi-property scores. By sweeping over many weight vectors, the method approximates the full Pareto front; a single weight vector targets a specific trade-off point. The differentiable scaffolding tree representation is the key interface that makes the discrete molecular graph amenable to gradient-based Pareto optimization.

## Method

InversionGNN is a dual-path GNN where the direct prediction path trains a 3-layer GCN with multi-head MLP to predict m property scores (serving as a frozen surrogate oracle), while the inversion generation path back-propagates gradients through this oracle to the differentiable scaffolding tree to guide molecular editing. The key mechanism is a gradient-based Pareto inversion: at each iteration, a Quadratic Programming problem (Eq. 5) computes a non-dominating gradient direction d_nd = Gβ* that balances conflicting objectives, where β* is found by minimizing ||G^T G β − a||² subject to KL-divergence constraints tied to the weight vector λ. This adapts continuous Pareto optimization (Mahapatra & Rajan, 2020) to discrete chemical space via the differentiable scaffolding tree relaxation, performing K rounds of gradient descent per iteration before sampling a discrete tree and assembling it into a molecule. A convergence theorem guarantees that after T iterations the solution lies within a bounded admissible set approaching the Pareto front at rate γ = (1−α^T)/((1−α)N), making the method sample-efficient (≈15K oracle calls vs. 50K+ for baselines).

## Results

On the 2-objective GSK3β+JNK3 task, InversionGNN achieves a Top-100 Average Property Score (APS) of 0.841 with 0.768 diversity, using only 10K+5K Oracle calls — substantially fewer than most baselines (50K+20K for HN-GFN, 50K+5K for DST). On the 4-objective GSK3β+JNK3+QED+SA task, InversionGNN reaches APS 0.773 and diversity 0.769 with 20K+5K Oracle calls. In Hypervolume (Table 4), InversionGNN scores 0.763 (2-obj) and 0.519 (4-obj), outperforming the next-best HN-GFN (0.592 / 0.374) by a wide margin. In the weight-conditioned setting (Table 2), InversionGNN achieves APS 0.648 and Non-Uniformity 0.026, versus I-LS at 0.529/0.049 and HN-GFN at 0.418/0.072.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InversionGNN outperforms all 16 baselines on APS in both the 2-objective and 4-objective drug discovery settings, achieving 0.841 vs. the next-best Graph-GA (0.825) and DST (0.827) on GSK3β+JNK3, and 0.773 vs. DST (0.752) and I-LS (0.734) on the 4-property task. It also achieves the highest Hypervolume (0.763 for 2-obj, 0.519 for 4-obj), surpassing HN-GFN (0.592/0.374) and MOGFN-AL (0.567/0.377) by 17–29% relative. In the weight-conditioned task (Table 2), InversionGNN achieves the lowest Non-Uniformity (0.026) and highest APS (0.648), beating I-LS (0.049/0.529), HN-GFN (0.072/0.418), and MOGFN-PC (0.088/0.393). A notable trade-off is that InversionGNN's diversity (0.768) is slightly below HN-GFN (0.784) and LigGPT (0.845) on the 2-obj task, though it still exceeds all other baselines. Critically, InversionGNN requires far fewer Oracle calls (10K+5K) than Bayesian-optimization-based methods like HN-GFN and MOGFN-AL (50K+20K) or RL-based methods like GCPN and MolDQN (0+200K), making it substantially more sample-efficient while delivering superior property scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

InversionGNN builds on two distinct lines of work: the differentiable scaffolding tree (Fu et al., ICLR 2022) provides the locally differentiable molecular representation, and the Efficient Pareto Optimization (EPO) framework (Mahapatra & Rajan, ICML 2020) supplies the non-dominating gradient computation via quadratic programming. The key departure from prior multi-objective molecular optimization (MOMO) methods is the dual-path architecture: rather than treating a pretrained GNN as a black-box discriminator (as in GAN-GA, MARS, or GCPN) or relying on Linear Scalarization to combine objectives (as in most RL and Bayesian optimization approaches), InversionGNN inverts the GNN's gradients with respect to the molecular structure to directly guide substructure editing. This reframes the GNN from a scoring oracle into a knowledge source whose learned property-structure mappings are decoded through gradient flow in the inversion path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized limitation of Linear Scalarization in multi-objective drug discovery—biased solutions and unexplored regions of the Pareto front—by providing both a practical algorithm and a convergence guarantee in discrete chemical space. The sample-efficiency advantage (achieving higher Hypervolume with roughly half the Oracle calls of the strongest baselines) is practically significant for drug discovery where property evaluation is expensive. Open questions include whether the submodularity assumption (diminishing returns in property scores) holds for larger or more complex molecules, how the method scales beyond 4 properties, and whether the differentiable scaffolding tree representation remains the best discrete-to-continuous bridge as more expressive molecular representations become available.
