## Problem

Neural networks suffer from loss of plasticity during continual learning, where their ability to adapt to new data degrades over a sequence of non-stationary tasks. While the phenomenon is well-documented, the underlying cause was unclear: prior work attributed it to nonlinear gradient dynamics, but it remained unknown whether nonlinearity in representations or in optimization dynamics was the true culprit. This paper identifies that it is the combination of nonlinear representations and nonlinear dynamics that causes plasticity loss, and asks how to retain the expressiveness of nonlinear networks while preserving the sustained trainability of linear ones.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 28×28 for MNIST variants) and outputs class labels, operating within a continual supervised learning framework where the input-target distribution shifts every T iterations. The non-stationarity manifests as diminishing label noise, class-incremental learning, or random label permutations across tasks. Because each pre-activation is split into a sin and cos unit, the effective hidden width is halved relative to a ReLU network of the same parameter budget, giving the nonlinear baseline a capacity advantage in comparisons.

## Method

The paper proves that linear function approximation and deep linear networks (a special case with diagonal weight matrices) do not suffer from loss of trainability, establishing that linearity in representations is the key inductive bias for sustained plasticity. It then introduces deep Fourier features: every hidden layer concatenates sin(z) and cos(z) for each pre-activation z, so that at least one of the two units is always well-approximated by a linear function with bounded error (c ≈ 0.05), a property the authors call adaptive linearity. This means a network built entirely from deep Fourier features approximately embeds a full deep linear network using all of its parameters, dynamically balancing trainability (from the embedded linear path) with expressiveness (from the nonlinear sinusoidal path). The approach is validated as a drop-in replacement for ReLU in ResNet-18 and MLP architectures, and when combined with spectral regularization, the high trainability of deep Fourier features enables training at much larger regularization strengths, yielding superior generalization on continual learning benchmarks.

## Results

The paper presents results as accuracy curves over sequential tasks rather than a single summary table. On the diminishing label noise setting (10 tasks), deep Fourier features (DFF) with ResNet-18 consistently achieve the highest or near-highest test accuracy across tiny-ImageNet, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, despite having approximately half the parameters of the ReLU baseline. In class-incremental learning (80 tasks), DFF largely outperforms all baselines, with the largest gains on tiny-ImageNet where the first 40 tasks involve a growing subset of classes. A key finding is that DFF's high trainability enables training with much stronger regularization (e.g., spectral regularization), which is the primary mechanism for its superior generalization over baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Deep Fourier features outperform the standard ReLU ResNet-18 baseline across all three datasets in both continual learning settings, despite having roughly half the parameters. Among the plasticity-mitigation baselines, Shrink and Perturb (Ash & Adams, 2020) is identified as the strongest competitor on diminishing label noise, but DFF still surpasses it, particularly on tiny-ImageNet. CReLU (Shang et al., 2016; Abbas et al., 2023) underperforms even the plain ReLU baseline in the diminishing label noise setting. Spectral regularization (Lewandowski et al., 2024) is the closest competitor on CIFAR-10, where DFF is described as 'on-par,' but DFF clearly surpasses it on CIFAR-100 and tiny-ImageNet. L2 regularization toward initialization (Kumar et al., 2023b), Dormant Neuron Recycling/ReDO (Sokar et al., 2023), and Streaming EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017; Elsayed & Mahmood, 2024) all show only marginal improvements over the ReLU baseline, while DFF achieves substantially larger gains. In class-incremental learning, DFF outperforms all baselines on both tiny-ImageNet and CIFAR-100, with the advantage being most pronounced on tiny-ImageNet due to its higher difficulty (more classes, higher resolution, more data points).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fourier features literature (Tancik et al., 2020; Rahimi and Recht, 2007) by applying sinusoidal activations in every layer rather than only the input layer, and grounds this choice in a theoretical analysis of deep linear network convergence (Arora et al., 2019). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant approach of mitigating loss of plasticity through regularization (S-EWC, spectral regularization, L2-to-initialization) or architectural patches (CReLU, ReDO, Shrink-and-Perturb) by instead identifying linearity of representations as the root cause of plasticity loss and proposing an activation function that dynamically embeds a deep linear network within a nonlinear one. The unit sign entropy metric generalizes previously identified issues of unit saturation (Abbas et al., 2023) and unit linearization (Lyle et al., 2024) into a single framework applicable to arbitrary activation functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that loss of plasticity stems from nonlinear representations rather than nonlinear gradient dynamics reframes how the field should think about continual learning architectures, suggesting that preserving representational linearity (even approximately) is the key inductive bias. Practically, deep Fourier features offer a simple drop-in replacement for ReLU that sustains trainability across 100+ tasks, though the tendency to overfit without strong regularization and the halving of effective width limit immediate adoption. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees extend beyond diagonal linear networks, whether the approach scales to very deep or very wide architectures, and whether it transfers to reinforcement learning or other modalities where loss of plasticity is also documented.
