## Problem

Fine-tuning text-to-image diffusion models with human feedback via DPO requires thousands of GPU hours on large-scale datasets (e.g., 850k pairs for Pick-a-Pic v2), and the noisy, ambiguous nature of human preference labels slows convergence and degrades generalization. Existing data-pruning methods (coresets, loss-based selection) are not designed for diffusion alignment, and manual curation does not scale to large feedback datasets. The paper addresses the lack of an automated, principled filtering framework that can extract a small yet informative subset from large-scale preference data for efficient diffusion model alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full human feedback dataset of (text prompt, winning image, losing image) triples, a pre-trained proxy reward model (PickScore or HPSv2, trained on the same dataset via Bradley-Terry), and an LLM (gpt-3.5-turbo) that scores each text prompt on a 0–10 scale for quality, learnability, and absence of harmful content. It produces a filtered subset of K data pairs (e.g., 5k pairs out of 850k, ~0.5% of the full set) ranked by a composite importance score, with a hard constraint of at most 5 pairs per unique text prompt to prevent over-representation. The filtered subset is then used directly as the training set for Diffusion-DPO fine-tuning of SD1.5 or SDXL.

## Method

FiFA formulates data selection as an optimization problem that jointly maximizes three per-sample components: (1) preference margin, the absolute reward difference |r(c, x^w) − r(c, x^l)| computed by a proxy reward model, which identifies informative pairs where the preference is unambiguous; (2) text quality, an LLM-assigned score penalizing typos, triviality, and NSFW content; and (3) text diversity, estimated via a k-nearest-neighbor entropy estimator on embedded text prompts to ensure the selected subset covers a broad prompt space. Rather than solving the combinatorial subset selection exactly, FiFA approximates the solution by computing a per-pair importance score f̃ = m^reward + α·LLM_Score + γ·log‖c − c^{k-NN}‖₂ and selecting the top-K pairs, making the procedure linear in dataset size and fully automated. The theoretical grounding connects this selection to G-optimal design: under a linear reward model assumption, collecting diverse, high-margin samples increases the singular values of the design matrix, reducing the prediction error bound and thus requiring fewer samples for a given accuracy level (Theorem 1).

## Results

Using only 5k pairs (0.6% of the 850k Pick-a-Pic v2 dataset), FiFA-trained SD1.5 achieves PickScore 21.64 and HPSv2 26.95 on the Pick-a-Pic test set, surpassing the full-data DPO baseline (21.19 / 26.37) while requiring only 13.6 GPU hours vs. 56.2 (24% of training time). For SDXL, FiFA requires just 18.3 GPU hours (1% of the 1760.4 hours for full training) and achieves HPSv2 benchmark scores of 28.18 vs. 28.09 for the full-data model. In human evaluation on the HPSv2 benchmark, annotators prefer FiFA-generated images 54.8% of the time versus 37.0% for the full-data model (a 17% margin), with gains of 17% in aesthetics and 15% in text-image alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against DPO trained on the full dataset (850k pairs for Pick-a-Pic v2, 645k for HPSv2). FiFA consistently outperforms the full-data baseline across nearly all metrics on all three benchmarks (Pick-a-Pic test, PartiPrompt, HPSv2) for both SD1.5 and SDXL, while using less than 0.5% of the data and less than 1% of GPU hours for SDXL (18.3h vs. 1760.4h). In the ablation comparison (Figure 6a), FiFA also beats coreset selection (Mirzasoleiman et al., 2020) and loss-based selection (Paul et al., 2021), which perform poorly because they are not designed for diffusion model alignment and require more filtering time. Random selection and high-reward-only filtering also underperform FiFA, confirming that simply reducing dataset size or selecting by absolute reward is insufficient. The gains are most pronounced on SD1.5 (e.g., +0.75 HPS on HPSv2 benchmark) and in human evaluation (54.8% vs. 37.0% preference), while SDXL gains are smaller but still positive across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

FiFA builds directly on Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2023) as its training objective and on the general DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2024), but its core contribution is an automated data filtering layer that sits upstream of the DPO loss. It departs from prior data pruning methods (coresets, loss-based selection, data distillation) by formulating selection as a multi-criteria optimization over preference margin, LLM-assessed text quality, and k-NN entropy-based text diversity, rather than relying on gradient or loss signals that are expensive to compute for diffusion models. The theoretical grounding in G-optimal design (Pukelsheim, 2006) and the k-NN entropy estimator (Singh et al., 2003) connect the practical filtering procedure to classical experimental design and information-theoretic diversity, while the LLM scoring component draws from recent LLM data-efficiency work (Sachdeva et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: achieving better alignment with 99.5% less data and under 1% of GPU hours makes large-scale diffusion model alignment far more accessible, and the 50%+ reduction in harmful content generation addresses a critical safety concern that full-dataset RLHF inadvertently worsens. The G-optimal design interpretation provides a principled justification for why high-margin, diverse data is informative, potentially guiding future data curation strategies beyond diffusion models. Key limitations include validation only under the DPO objective (not policy-gradient or online DPO variants), reliance on a specific proxy reward model for margin estimation, and the fact that the optimal K and hyperparameters (α, γ) may be dataset-dependent. Open questions include whether FiFA extends to online/iterative alignment settings, other generative modalities, and whether the margin-based filtering can be replaced by model-internal signals to avoid the proxy reward dependency.
