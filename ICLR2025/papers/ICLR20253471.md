## Problem

Sinusoidal neural fields (SIREN) are widely used for representing data as continuous functions, but their training is slow, often requiring hours of GPU computation per datum. The standard SIREN initialization, designed to preserve signal propagation across layers, was never optimized for training speed. This paper asks: how should SNFs be initialized to maximize training speed, and why does the conventional scheme fall short?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253471/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The SNF takes d₀-dimensional coordinates (2D pixel positions, 3D voxel positions, 3D spherical coordinates, or 1D timestamps) normalized to the hypercube [-1, +1]^{d₀} and outputs a single scalar signal value (image intensity, occupancy label, temperature, or audio amplitude). Networks are shallow MLPs (typically 5 layers, width 256–512) trained with full-batch or mini-batch Adam on MSE loss. The key constraint is that inputs must be pre-scaled to unit range, and the first layer uses a distinct frequency multiplier ω₀ while hidden layers use ω_h.

## Method

The proposed weight scaling (WS) multiplies every initialized weight matrix except the last layer by a constant factor α ≥ 1, yielding a simple generalization of the standard SIREN initialization. The last layer is deliberately left unscaled to avoid direct amplification of the initial function, which would harm generalization. Theoretically, WS preserves the arcsin(-1,1) activation distribution across all hidden layers for any α ≥ 1 (Proposition 1), so it can be applied to arbitrarily deep SNFs without destabilizing forward propagation. The acceleration mechanism has two components: (1) scaling the first-layer weights increases the frequency of every sinusoidal basis, and (2) scaling intermediate-layer weights increases the relative power of higher-order harmonics (via Bessel-function coefficient growth proportional to α²), collectively mitigating spectral bias. Empirically, WS also produces a lower eNTK condition number and better kernel-task alignment throughout training, yielding a well-conditioned optimization trajectory that is 10× faster than the standard initialization across image, audio, 3D occupancy, and spherical data domains.

## Results

After a fixed number of training steps (150 iterations for images, 50 for occupancy, 5k for spherical, 1k for audio), weight-scaled SNF achieves 42.83 dB PSNR on Kodak and 42.03 dB on DIV2K, compared to 24.58 dB and 22.86 dB for the standard SIREN initialization — a gap of roughly 18 dB. On the 3D occupancy task (Lucy), it reaches 0.9941 IoU vs. 0.9925 for SIREN and NFSL. On ERA5 spherical data and Bach audio, it attains 45.28 dB and 45.04 dB PSNR respectively, outperforming all baselines by 6–28 dB. The paper reports up to 10× training speedup over the standard SIREN initialization across all domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Weight scaling dominates all nine baselines across every data domain and metric. Against the closest sinusoidal-activation baselines — SIREN init. and NFSL — it gains approximately 18 dB PSNR on Kodak (42.83 vs. 24.58/24.93) and 6.4 dB on ERA5 (45.28 vs. 38.72/38.92), while also achieving the highest IoU on Lucy (0.9941 vs. 0.9925). Against the strongest non-sinusoidal baselines (WIRE at 28.94 dB and MFN at 28.54 dB on Kodak), the gap widens to over 14 dB. On the Bach audio task, the advantage is most dramatic: 45.04 dB vs. 37.37 dB (SIREN) and 16.16 dB (MFN), a ~29 dB margin over the wavelet-based methods. The only near-tie is on Lucy IoU, where Sinc NF (0.9936) and SIREN/NFSL (0.9925) are within 0.001 of the proposed method (0.9941), though weight scaling still leads. Overall, the method consistently achieves the best training accuracy at every fixed step count, confirming the claimed up-to-10× speedup without requiring architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SIREN (Sitzmann et al., 2020b), whose sinusoidal activation and distribution-preserving initialization form the baseline that the proposed weight scaling modifies. It departs from the lazy-training framework of Chizat et al. (2019) by showing that sinusoidal activations break the positive-homogeneity assumption, leading to fundamentally different effects of weight scaling (higher-frequency initial functionals and disproportionate early-layer gradients rather than functional amplification). The eNTK and kernel-task alignment analyses connect to the broader NTK literature (Jacot et al., 2018; Kopitkov & Indelman, 2020), while the Bessel-function spectral analysis draws on the structured dictionary perspective of Yüce et al. (2022). The paper positions itself as filling a gap left by prior NF acceleration work (architectural changes, meta-learning, data transformations) by showing that the initialization itself is the underexploited lever.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× training speedup from a one-line change (multiply weights by α) is immediately actionable for any practitioner using SIREN or similar sinusoidal NFs, and the fact that it outperforms purpose-built architectures (MFN, WIRE, Sinc NF) across five data domains makes it a strong default choice. The conceptual reframe—that distribution preservation, the design principle behind the standard SIREN initialization, is suboptimal for the actual NF objective of fast convergence—opens the door to more principled efficiency-oriented initialization research. Key limitations include the analysis being restricted to sinusoidal activations (though experiments suggest transfer to other periodic activations), the lack of an explicit convergence bound, and the need to select α based on workload structure (resolution, depth) rather than a closed-form rule.
