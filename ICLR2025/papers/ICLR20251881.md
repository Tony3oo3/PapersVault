## Problem

Existing generative world models focus on state transition probabilities without explicitly modeling an agent's observations and beliefs, which are critical in partially observable environments where the true world state is unknown. Physically exploring an unfamiliar environment to update beliefs is costly, unsafe, and sometimes impossible, yet no prior method enables an embodied agent to mentally simulate exploration and revise its belief without physical movement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251881/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single equirectangular panorama RGB image (576×1024) as the initial observation, along with a 360° navigation direction and travel distance as action inputs; for goal-driven exploration, a text instruction is additionally provided to a GPT-based planner that decomposes the task into sequential exploration configurations. The output is a 25-frame panoramic video sequence at 576×1024 resolution representing forward navigation, which can be decomposed into six cubemap faces for consumption by LMM-based policy models that produce embodied decisions.

## Method

GenEx adapts the Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) latent diffusion backbone to operate on equirectangular panorama images rather than conventional rectangular frames, using CLIP-encoded panorama conditioning. The key architectural contribution is Spherical-Consistent Learning (SCL), a training regularization that randomly applies spherical rotation transformations to both the denoised generated video and the ground-truth video, then computes MSE in the latent space of a temporal VAE encoder, explicitly enforcing continuity across the 360° seam that standard equirectangular training ignores. The combined loss weights SCL against the standard noise-prediction objective. For embodied decision-making, the paper formalizes imagination-driven belief revision as an extension of POMDP: between physical time steps, the agent freezes time and generates a sequence of imagined observations via the video diffuser, updating its belief distribution without physical traversal. This framework generalizes to multi-agent settings where one agent imaginatively navigates to other agents' positions to infer their beliefs and aggregate them for more informed joint decisions.

## Results

On the GenEx-EQA embodied QA benchmark, GenEx(GPT-4o) achieves 85.22% single-agent and 94.87% multi-agent Decision Accuracy, substantially outperforming multimodal GPT-4o without imagination (46.10% and 21.88% respectively). In video generation quality (Table 1), GenEx achieves FVD of 69.5, PSNR of 30.2, and SSIM of 0.94 after tuning on GenEx-DB, compared to 759.9 FVD and 17.6 PSNR for the unmodified SVD panorama baseline. For novel view synthesis (Table 3), GenEx achieves LPIPS of 0.15 and PSNR of 28.57, surpassing Stable Zero123 (LPIPS 0.50, PSNR 14.12) and TripoSR (LPIPS 0.76, PSNR 6.69). The model also demonstrates zero-shot generalizability to real-world scenes with IELC ≤ 0.1 on both Google Street View and indoor environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SVD backbone (Blattmann et al., 2023a), GenEx with spherical-consistent learning reduces FVD from 759.9 to 69.5 (10.9× improvement) and increases PSNR from 17.6 to 30.2 dB after tuning on GenEx-DB, while the six-view baseline (FVD 196.7, PSNR 26.1) is also clearly outperformed on all five generation metrics. In the novel view synthesis comparison (Table 3), GenEx dominates all three 3D reconstruction baselines: it achieves LPIPS of 0.15 versus 0.50 for Stable Zero123, 0.75 for SV3D, and 0.76 for TripoSR, while also reporting near-zero background MSE (0.00) that the other methods either do not report or perform worse on. In the embodied QA setting, GenEx(GPT-4o) nearly doubles the multi-agent Decision Accuracy of multimodal GPT-4o (94.87% vs. 21.88%) and exceeds even human performance with image input (94.87% vs. 55.24% for multi-agent), demonstrating that imagination-driven belief revision provides a decisive advantage over both unimodal and standard multimodal LLM agents.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenEx builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its video generation backbone, adapting the input representation from conventional images to equirectangular panoramas and introducing spherical-consistent learning (SCL) to enforce 360-degree continuity. Conceptually, it departs from prior world models (Ha & Schmidhuber, LeCun) and action-conditioned video generation (Du et al., Yang et al.) that model state transition probabilities without explicitly representing agent observations and beliefs. The paper positions itself at the intersection of generative video and POMDP-based embodied decision-making, introducing imagination-driven belief revision as a mechanism to update beliefs without physical traversal, and extends the framework to multi-agent settings where one agent can simulate another's perspective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The imagination-driven belief revision framework offers a principled way to integrate generative video into partially observable decision-making, potentially reducing the cost and risk of physical exploration for embodied agents. The multi-agent generalization—where an agent can mentally navigate to another agent's position to infer their beliefs—is a compelling direction for cooperative AI. However, significant open questions remain: the evaluation relies on a self-created benchmark of only 200+ synthetic scenarios, training is entirely on synthetic data (UE5, Unity, Blender), and the LMM-as-policy-model component is a black box whose reliability in open-world deployment is untested. Scaling to real-time, safety-critical applications and validating the belief revision framework against ground-truth physical exploration remain important next steps.
