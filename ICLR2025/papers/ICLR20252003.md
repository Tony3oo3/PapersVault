## Problem

Computing approximate Nash equilibria in large imperfect information extensive-form games (e.g., HUNL with ~10^165 states) is infeasible without reducing game tree size, yet existing pruning methods are dynamic and tightly coupled to specific CFR variants, while abstraction methods are either too simplistic (ignoring future value) or prohibitively expensive (requiring months of offline simulation). No prior approach achieves permanent, CFR-agnostic pruning combined with fast, subgame-specific information abstraction in a unified online framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252003/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a depth-limited subgame constructed from the current public state (board cards, pot size, chip counts) and each player's private hand, but notably excludes range information of both players from the feature vector. The M=10 expected value networks output scalar EV estimates (relative to pot size) per information set, which feed into pruning decisions and k-means++ clustering features. The final outputs are a permanently pruned game tree, K abstracted root information set buckets per player (with all subgame information sets mapped to buckets), and the resulting approximate Nash equilibrium strategy from DCFR on the reduced tree.

## Method

EVPA integrates three synergistic components: (i) M=10 independent 6-layer MLPs (1536 hidden units, ReLU) trained with Huber loss on Nash equilibrium EV values sampled from randomized depth-limited subgames, producing per-information-set EV estimates without requiring probability distributions; (ii) Minimax-based permanent pruning that eliminates actions whose maximum EV estimate falls below the minimum EV of sibling actions (with a tolerance δ ≥ O(1/√T) to absorb CFR error), executed once before CFR begins and verified recursively from the root; (iii) online information abstraction that constructs feature vectors by weighting average EV estimates at each future node by f(g(s),n)=1/(pot(s)·max{n,0.2}), then clusters root information sets via k-means++ (T=8 iterations) into K buckets, completing in under 1 second. The key distinction from prior work is that pruning is permanent and CFR-variant-agnostic, and abstraction is dynamic and subgame-specific rather than a fixed offline mapping.

## Results

EVPA reduces the solving time to reach an ε-Nash equilibrium to just 1%–2% of DeepStack's replication (e.g., EVPA-30 reaches exploitability ≤ 0.08 with 1×10⁶ information set touches vs. ~1×10⁸ for the baseline). Against DeepStack's replication, EVPA-169 achieves a win-rate of 930±23 mbb/h at 1×10⁷ touches, 202±31 mbb/h at 1×10⁸, and 82±60 mbb/h at 1×10⁹. Against Slumbot, EVPA-full wins 96±43 mbb/h at 0.2 s solving time and 100±68 mbb/h at 2 s, while the DeepStack baseline loses (−109±52 mbb/h) at 0.2 s. Game tree pruning rates range from 42.67% (EVPA-30, flop) to 79.51% (EVPA-full, river).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVPA decisively outperforms DeepStack's replication (Moravčik et al., 2017) across all touch budgets, winning 930±23 mbb/h at 1×10⁷ touches and maintaining a positive margin even at 1×10⁹ touches (82±60 mbb/h). Against Slumbot (Jackson, 2013), EVPA-full achieves 96±43 mbb/h at 0.2 s while the DeepStack baseline loses (−109±52 mbb/h), and EVPA-169 is roughly break-even (10±26 mbb/h) at the extreme 0.02 s limit. In the broader HUNL AI comparison (Table 4, Appendix B), EVPA-full at 0.2 s (96±43 mbb/h vs. Slumbot) surpasses Tartanian7 (33±16), Baby Tartanian8 (36±12), Modicum (11±9), and ReBeL (45±5), and is competitive with Supremus (176±44) and AlphaHoldem (112±16) while requiring only 4 days of training plus 0.2 s solving time. EVPA also outperforms Libratus (63±28 mbb/h) in win-rate while using a fraction of the 8,370-day training budget. The key differentiator is that EVPA's online pruning and abstraction complete in under 1 second per subgame, whereas offline abstraction methods (used by Tartanian7, Baby Tartanian8, and the original DeepStack) require months of precomputation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DeepStack's depth-limited subgame solving framework and DCFR, but departs from prior work in two key ways. First, whereas existing pruning methods (regret-based, best-response, partial pruning) are temporary and require intermediate CFR values computed during iterations, EVPA performs permanent Minimax-style pruning *before* CFR using pre-trained expected value networks. Second, while prior information abstraction (expectation-based or potential-aware) is offline and can take months, EVPA clusters information sets online in under one second using k-means++ on current and future expected value features. The use of M independent networks to produce max/min/average estimates that jointly enable safe pruning and abstraction is a new architectural choice not present in DeepStack or Libratus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1–2% solving time relative to DeepStack's replication is a substantial practical efficiency gain that could make near-equilibrium play feasible in real-time or resource-constrained settings. The shift from offline, months-long abstraction to online subgame-specific abstraction opens the door to dynamic, context-adaptive strategies that prior blueprint-based AIs (Tartanian7, Baby Tartanian8) could not achieve. However, validation is limited to HUNL with a self-replicated DeepStack baseline, and the 4-day training cost for the value networks (though far less than DeepStack's 70 days) remains non-trivial. Open questions include generalization to multi-player games (Omaha, Mahjong, Stratego), whether the pruning error bounds hold under distribution shift, and integration with reinforcement-learning-based solvers like AlphaHoldem.
