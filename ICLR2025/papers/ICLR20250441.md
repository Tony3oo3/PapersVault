## Problem

CLIP-based open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods produce patch-wise noisy class predictions and struggle to accurately localize target objects within images. While prior training-free approaches modify the last attention layer of CLIP (e.g., identity matrix in MaskCLIP, self-self attention in SCLIP/GEM), they still yield imprecise segmentation boundaries. The paper identifies that despite noisy per-patch predictions, class distributions across patches of the same object remain highly correlated—a property not previously exploited for attention refinement.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 224×224, processed with a 448×448 sliding window at stride 224) and text prompts for target class names as input, using CLIP ViT/B-16 (patch size 16×16) as the backbone. It produces a pixel-level segmentation mask evaluated by mIoU, along with the intermediate class distribution-induced attention map. An additional 80 augmented text prompts (attribute classes and supercategories) are used solely for constructing the CDAM, not for final inference. The entropy-based background thresholding dynamically adjusts the foreground/background decision threshold per image without dataset-specific tuning.

## Method

CDAM constructs a localized attention map by computing the Jensen-Shannon divergence between class distributions (softmax of image-text similarity scores) of every pair of patches, then applying softmax to the negated divergence to yield attention weights that emphasize same-class patches. This CDAM replaces the identity matrix in MaskCLIP's last attention layer or augments the attention in other baselines (SCLIP, GEM, ClearCLIP), with the value features reused from the (L−1)-th layer. Three refinements enhance the base CDAM: (1) multi-scale CDAM averages attention maps computed at seven downsampled scales for spatial consistency, (2) augmented text prompts (80 attribute/supercategory names from PACO and COCO) enrich the class distribution used only for CDAM construction, and (3) entropy-based background thresholding sets the foreground threshold proportional to the center entropy of the similarity map, exploiting the observation that foreground patches yield low-entropy (confident) distributions while background yields high-entropy (uncertain) ones. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no dense annotations.

## Results

On datasets with a background class (VOC21, Context60, COCO-Obj), GEM+CDAM achieves the best training-free average mIoU of 41.5%, outperforming CaR (39.2%), ClearCLIP (37.2%), SCLIP (35.9%), and MaskCLIP (27.1%). The largest single improvement is MaskCLIP+CDAM, which gains +13.1 mIoU over MaskCLIP alone (27.1→40.2). On datasets without a background class (COCO-Stuff, Cityscapes, ADE20K), MaskCLIP+CDAM achieves 23.3% average mIoU, surpassing all training-free baselines and most weakly-supervised methods except PACL. CDAM introduces minimal inference overhead (~34 ms per image) compared to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDAM consistently improves every CLIP-based training-free baseline it is integrated with: MaskCLIP gains +13.1 mIoU (27.1→40.2), SCLIP +5.4 (35.9→41.3), ClearCLIP +3.4 (37.2→40.6), and GEM +3.5 (38.0→41.5). Among training-free methods, GEM+CDAM (41.5 avg) is the strongest, surpassing CaR (39.2) and all other training-free baselines. Against prototype-generation methods that rely on three foundation models (CLIP+SD+DINO), GEM+CDAM beats OVDiff on Context60 (30.6 vs 30.1) and COCO-Obj (35.2 vs 34.8), and beats FreeDA on VOC21 (58.7 vs 55.4), though OVDiff (44.0) and FreeDA (43.7) still lead on average. MaskCLIP+CDAM (40.2) outperforms all weakly-supervised methods (GroupViT 32.2, SegCLIP 34.6, TCL 35.3) without any additional training data, falling short only of PACL (72.3/50.1 on VOC21/Context60) which requires large-scale CC3M+CC12M+YFCC15M training. On datasets without a background class, MaskCLIP+CDAM (23.3 avg) surpasses all training-free baselines and weakly-supervised methods except PACL and FreeDA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP and MaskCLIP, which established the paradigm of using pre-trained vision-language models for dense prediction by modifying the final attention layer. It departs from concurrent training-free methods (SCLIP, GEM, ClearCLIP, CLIPSurgery) that enhance localization through self-self attention mechanisms (query-query, key-key, value-value interactions). Instead, CDAM constructs attention maps from the Jensen-Shannon divergence between class distributions of patches, exploiting the observation that same-object patches share correlated distributions even when individual predictions are noisy. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play module that integrates with these existing approaches rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDAM offers a practical, training-free technique that consistently improves multiple CLIP-based segmentation methods by 3–23 mIoU without requiring additional data, annotations, or retraining, making it immediately applicable to existing pipelines. The entropy-based background thresholding provides a principled alternative to dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning. However, the method inherits CLIP's fundamental limitations on rare classes and out-of-distribution scenarios, and the empirically designed thresholding struggles with complex multi-object scenes where local thresholding would be needed. Open questions include whether training-based strategies (domain-specific prompt tuning, multi-modal models with richer class coverage) could push performance further, and whether the distributional correlation property generalizes beyond CLIP to other VLM architectures.
