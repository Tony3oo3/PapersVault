## Problem

Anomaly detection methods are highly vulnerable to adversarial attacks because training data contains only normal samples, leaving detectors unexposed to adversarial perturbations on anomalous patterns. While adversarial training with contrastive loss is a natural remedy, the standard contrastive objective treats all intra-group pairs (normal-normal, anomaly-anomaly) as negatives, creating 'spurious negative pairs' that misdirect inter-group perturbations and reduce the discriminative margin between normal and anomaly distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251155/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images of varying resolution (32×32 for CIFAR/MNIST, up to 1024×1024 for MVTecAD/CityScapes) and produces an anomaly score computed as the negative maximum cosine similarity between the test sample's embedding and all normal training sample embeddings in a 128-dimensional projection space. A binary classification head (ResNet-18 encoder + 2-layer MLP) simultaneously outputs a normal/anomaly label used during training. The anomaly score at inference relies solely on the encoder's learned representations without requiring the classifier head.

## Method

COBRA introduces two coupled components: (1) a distribution-aware hard transformation pipeline that applies randomized sequences of severe augmentations (Jigsaw, CutPaste, Mixup, elastic distortion, etc.) to normal samples and uses a GMM-fitted p-value threshold (default 0.05) to filter out samples that still belong to the normal distribution, producing verified pseudo-anomalies paired as 'opposite pairs' with their source; and (2) a modified contrastive loss L_COBRA that augments the standard NT-Xent numerator with an explicit opposite-pair repulsion term, pulling each sample's embedding away from its pseudo-anomaly counterpart while still attracting positive (light-augmentation) pairs. During adversarial training (PGD-10, ε=4/255 or 2/255), generated adversarial examples are treated as additional positive views, so the model learns to be invariant to perturbations within each group while the opposite-pair term enforces a wide inter-group margin. This design avoids the spurious negative pair problem by targeting only cross-group pairs for repulsion rather than all non-positive pairs.

## Results

On the MVTecAD benchmark (one-class setup, PGD-1000, ε=2/255), COBRA achieves 89.1% clean AUROC and 75.1% adversarial AUROC, outperforming the best prior adversarially-trained method ZARND (71.6%/30.1%) by 45 percentage points in the adversarial setting. Across 10 diverse datasets (Table 2), COBRA attains an average of 84.1% clean and 65.8% adversarial AUROC, a 26.1 percentage-point improvement over the strongest robust baseline (ZARND at 39.7% adversarial). Under stronger attacks (AutoAttack, Adaptive AutoAttack, FGSM, CAA), COBRA maintains 51.7–89.6% AUROC depending on dataset, while most baselines drop below 10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COBRA decisively outperforms all adversarially-trained baselines (PrincipaLS, OCSDF, APAE, ZARND) in the adversarial setting across every dataset, with the largest gap on MVTecAD (75.1% vs. ZARND's 30.1%, a 45-point margin) and VisA (73.8% vs. 24.9%). Against non-adversarial methods that achieve high clean AUROC (Transformaly 88.1%, MSAD 86.2%, PatchCore 75.9%), COBRA sacrifices only modest clean performance (84.1% average) while gaining 60+ points in adversarial robustness where those methods collapse to 3–7%. Among the robust baselines, ZARND is the closest competitor (39.7% average adversarial), but COBRA still leads by 26.1 percentage points. On MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, PrincipaLS and OCSDF remain competitive in the adversarial setting (83.1% and 68.9% on MNIST), yet COBRA still edges them out (96.4% and 89.6%). In the unlabeled multi-class setup (Table 3), COBRA similarly dominates, e.g., achieving 98.5/78.6 on CIFAR10→SVHN versus ZARND's 84.3/42.7.

## Relation to Prior Work

COBRA builds directly on the contrastive learning framework for anomaly detection established by CSI (Tack et al., 2020) and the NT-Xent loss (Chen et al., 2020), but identifies a fundamental flaw in applying standard CL to adversarial training: intra-group negative pairs (normal-normal, anomaly-anomaly) act as 'spurious negatives' that misdirect inter-group adversarial perturbations. The method departs from prior robust AD approaches (PrincipaLS, OCSDF, APAE, ZARND) by framing the problem through the lens of contrastive pair construction rather than architectural modifications or distance-function constraints. For pseudo-anomaly generation, COBRA replaces prior strategies (CPAD's CutPaste, CSI's rotation, diffusion-based Dream-OOD, GAN-based GOE) with a distribution-aware hard-augmentation pipeline gated by a GMM likelihood threshold, avoiding the need for external anomaly datasets or pretrained generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 26.1% average improvement in robust AUROC across diverse benchmarks (MVTecAD, CityScapes, ISIC, ImageNet) without requiring pretrained models or external anomaly data makes COBRA practically relevant for safety-critical industrial and autonomous-driving applications where adversarial robustness is non-negotiable. However, absolute robustness remains modest under the strongest attacks (e.g., CIFAR-100 drops to 51.7% under PGD-1000), and the clean-to-adversarial performance gap (84.1% → 65.8%) reflects the well-known robustness-accuracy trade-off. Open questions include whether the GMM-based thresholding generalizes to high-dimensional or non-image feature spaces, whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViT, etc.), and whether the opposite-pair formulation can be extended to multi-class or open-world AD settings beyond the binary normal/anomaly split.
