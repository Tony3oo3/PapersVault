## Problem

Existing differentially private diffusion model (DPDM) training methods suffer from three coupled limitations: severe utility degradation under tight privacy budgets (e.g., DPDM fails to produce recognizable CIFAR-10 images at ε=1), excessive memory requirements driven by large batch sizes (e.g., 8,192 samples) needed to reduce per-step noise, and expensive iterative inference. No prior work has integrated retrieval-augmented generation into the DP training loop to simultaneously address all three issues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251307/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, RAPID takes random Gaussian noise x_T (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and produces a synthesized RGB image at 32×32 or 64×64 resolution. The key operational detail is that the sampling trajectory is split into three phases: the early steps (T to k) are computed normally, the intermediate steps (k to v) are skipped by retrieving a similar trajectory from a pre-built knowledge base, and only the final steps (v to 0) are executed. This retrieval-based skipping reduces inference cost by up to 50% compared to full iterative sampling.

## Method

RAPID pre-trains a latent diffusion model on public data, then builds a trajectory knowledge base by computing forward diffusion trajectories for a held-out public subset, storing key-value pairs (z_k, x_v) where z_k is a contrastively-learned feature of the latent at timestep k and x_v is the latent at a later timestep v. A SimCLR-based feature extractor is trained to embed noise-augmented latents so that semantically similar trajectories are close in feature space. During DP fine-tuning on private data, for each sample the method computes the early trajectory up to timestep k, retrieves the nearest-neighbor trajectory from the knowledge base, and trains the denoiser only on the later steps (v to 0) using DP-SGD with gradient clipping and Gaussian noise. This design reduces the required batch size from 8,192 to 64, improves FID by large margins (e.g., 63.2 vs. failure on CIFAR-10 at ε=1), and provides a formal (α, 2α/σ²)-RDP privacy guarantee for the sanitized per-batch gradient update.

## Results

On the class-conditional ImageNet32→CIFAR10 task, RAPID achieves an FID of 63.2 at ε=1, where DPDM fails to produce recognizable images entirely and DP-LDM scores 79.1. On the unconditional FFHQ64→CelebA64 task, RAPID attains an FID of 37.3 with 0.93 coverage at ε=10, outperforming DP-LDM (45.2 FID) and DPDM (80.8 FID). RAPID also reduces the required batch size to 64 (vs. 8,192 for DPDM) and saves up to 50% of inference cost by skipping intermediate sampling steps via retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPID substantially outperforms DPDM (Dockhorn et al., 2023) across nearly all settings: on CIFAR10 at ε=1, DPDM produces no recognizable output while RAPID achieves FID 63.2; on MNIST at ε=0.2, RAPID scores 24.0 vs. DPDM's 125.7. Against DP-LDM (Lyu et al., 2023), RAPID wins on most configurations (e.g., 63.2 vs. 79.1 on CIFAR10 ε=1; 28.0 vs. 38.0 on CelebA32 ε=10), though DP-LDM edges out on MNIST at ε=10 (27.2 vs. 14.1 is actually RAPID winning; the one case where DP-LDM wins is MNIST ε=10 where DPDM gets 12.9). Compared to DPSDA (Lin et al., 2024), RAPID achieves significantly lower FID on CIFAR10 (63.2 vs. 113.6 at ε=1; 25.4 vs. 60.9 at ε=10). Against PRIVIMAGE (Li et al., 2024), RAPID wins on CelebA64 (60.5 vs. 71.4 at ε=1; 37.3 vs. 49.3 at ε=10) but loses on CIFAR10 at ε=1 (63.2 vs. 29.8), which the authors attribute to PRIVIMAGE's targeted public-data selection being advantageous for highly structured private data under strict privacy budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAPID sits at the intersection of three research threads: differentially private diffusion model training (DPDM, DP-LDM, PrivImage), retrieval-augmented generation for diffusion models (Blattmann et al., 2022; REDI/Zhang et al., 2023), and contrastive representation learning (SimCLR). The key departure from prior DPDM work is that instead of applying DP-SGD to the entire end-to-end sampling process, RAPID decomposes the trajectory into early 'layout' steps (retrieved from a public-data knowledge base) and late 'detail' steps (privately fine-tuned), drawing on the observation that small perturbations to early steps have limited impact on the final output. The trajectory-reuse mechanism is conceptually related to REDI's inference-time retrieval, but RAPID repurposes it for the training phase and augments it with a contrastively-learned feature space for more effective nearest-neighbor search across diverse initial latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing the required batch size from 8,192 to 64 (a 128× memory saving) makes DP training of large latent diffusion models feasible on a single GPU, and skipping intermediate sampling steps cuts inference cost by up to 50%. However, the approach is explicitly framed as a pilot study with clear limitations: it requires access to a diverse public dataset with similar high-level layouts to the private data, retrieves only top-1 trajectories, and does not account for the privacy cost of the diffusion process itself. Open questions include whether the method extends to highly dissimilar public/private distributions, whether aggregating multiple retrieved trajectories improves quality, and whether the framework generalizes beyond image synthesis to tasks like text-to-video generation.
