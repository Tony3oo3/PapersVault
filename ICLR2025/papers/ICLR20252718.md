## Problem

Existing feature caching methods for diffusion transformers (e.g., FORA, Δ-DiT) apply a uniform caching strategy to all tokens and all layers, ignoring that individual tokens exhibit vastly different temporal redundancy and error propagation sensitivity. The paper demonstrates that caching certain tokens can cause up to 10× more degradation in final generation quality than caching others, yet no prior method exploits this token-level heterogeneity to selectively skip computation.

## Input / Output

ToCa is a training-free, plug-and-play acceleration layer applied on top of existing DiT-based models (PixArt-α, OpenSora, DiT-XL/2, FLUX.1-dev/schnell). It takes the same inputs as the base model—text prompts or class labels plus Gaussian noise—and produces the same outputs (images at 256×256 to 1024×1024, or 480p 51-frame videos at 24fps), but with 1.5×–2.4× speedup. Internally, the method operates on the token sequence (H×W patches) at each transformer layer, partitioning tokens into a computed set and a cached set per layer per timestep based on four attention-derived scores.

## Method

ToCa introduces a token-wise caching score S(x_i) composed of four sub-scores computed from existing attention maps at O(N) cost: (s1) self-attention column sum measuring each token's influence on others, (s2) cross-attention entropy measuring how strongly a token depends on the text condition, (s3) accumulated cache frequency to prevent error accumulation, and (s4) a spatial uniformity penalty ensuring cached tokens are not clustered in one region. Tokens with the lowest scores are selected for caching (skipping computation and reusing cached features), while the rest are computed and their cache entries updated. Crucially, ToCa applies different caching ratios per layer depth (r_l, higher for deeper layers) and per layer type (r_type, with self-attention layers receiving full computation while MLP layers absorb the caching load), and updates the cache at every timestep rather than only at the start of a caching period. The entire scheme requires no training and adds less than 1% overhead to the main computation.

## Results

On PixArt-α (text-to-image, 20 DPM++ steps), ToCa at R=70% achieves 1.93× speedup with FID-30k of 28.33 (vs. 28.09 baseline) and CLIP score of 16.44 (vs. 16.32), while at R=90% it reaches 2.62× speedup with only a 1.64 FID increase. On OpenSora (text-to-video, 30 rflow steps), ToCa at R=85% achieves 2.36× speedup with a VBench score of 78.34 (vs. 79.13 baseline), outperforming the 50%-steps baseline (76.78) by 1.56 points. On DiT-XL/2 (class-conditional, 250 DDPM steps), ToCa at R=93% achieves 2.75× speedup with FID of 2.58 and sFID of 5.74, compared to 2.31 and 4.98 for the unaccelerated model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToCa consistently outperforms FORA across all three tasks: on PixArt-α at similar ~2× speedup, ToCa (R=70%) achieves FID 28.33 vs. FORA (N=3) at 29.88, and a higher CLIP score of 17.75 vs. 17.15 on PartiPrompts; on OpenSora, ToCa (R=85%) reaches VBench 78.34 at 2.36× speed vs. FORA's 76.91 at 1.87×; on DiT-XL/2, ToCa achieves FID 2.58 vs. FORA's 2.80 at comparable ~2.7× speed. Against δ-DiT on OpenSora, ToCa achieves a higher VBench score (78.34 vs. 78.21) at a dramatically higher speedup (2.36× vs. 1.04×). Compared to T-GATE on OpenSora, ToCa wins on both VBench (78.34 vs. 77.61) and speed (2.36× vs. 1.16×). Against PAB on OpenSora, PAB1 achieves a marginally higher VBench (78.51 vs. 78.34) but at only 1.24× speed, while ToCa delivers 2.36× speedup with a 0.79-point VBench drop versus PAB1's 0.62-point drop — a 64.4% reduction in quality loss relative to the non-accelerated baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

ToCa extends the line of training-free feature caching methods for diffusion models (DeepCache, Block Caching for U-Nets; FORA, Δ-DiT for DiTs) by introducing token-level granularity and layer-aware caching ratios. The key conceptual departure is the empirical analysis showing that different tokens exhibit up to 10× differences in error propagation sensitivity through self-attention, motivating selective rather than uniform caching. Unlike Learning-to-Cache, which requires training to learn a caching policy, ToCa derives its four token-selection scores (self-attention influence, cross-attention entropy, cache frequency, spatial uniformity) directly from existing attention maps at zero additional computation cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 2.36× acceleration on OpenSora video generation and 1.93× on PixArt-α with near-lossless quality, demonstrated across four model families (PixArt-α, OpenSora, DiT-XL/2, FLUX) without any training. The error propagation analysis provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why naive uniform caching degrades quality, and the finding that partial computation in self-attention is counterproductive (errors propagate to all tokens regardless of fraction cached) challenges the intuitive assumption that 'less caching is always better.' Open questions include whether the scoring functions generalize to architectures without cross-attention (e.g., class-conditional DiT, where s₂ is unavailable) and whether the per-model hyperparameter tuning (N, R, λ_l, λ_type, λ_t) can be further simplified or made architecture-agnostic.
