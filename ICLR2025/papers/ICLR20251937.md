## Problem

Standard MoE routers compute token-expert assignments via dot-products in the full feature space, implicitly assuming all experts depend equally on all dimensions. However, latent clusters in high-dimensional data typically depend on different, potentially disjoint subsets of features, making them unidentifiable under uniform weighting. This leads to slow convergence, poor robustness to data contamination, and degraded expert specialization.

## Input / Output

The method operates on hidden representations (d-dimensional vectors) from Transformer layers — text token embeddings for language modeling or image patch embeddings for vision tasks. The routing transformation M_k requires the expert assignment from the immediately preceding MoE layer, so ACMoE can only be applied from the second MoE layer onward and requires the embedding dimension to remain constant between adjacent MoE layers. The final output is unchanged from standard MoE: next-token logits for language modeling or class scores for image classification.

## Method

The AC router formulates token-expert assignment as a feature-weighted clustering optimization problem with KL-regularized cluster-dependent feature weights, yielding a closed-form solution where each expert k gets a diagonal transformation M_k = diag(1/s_1k, …, 1/s_dk) that upweights features along which cluster k is tight and downweights features along which it is dispersed. At routing time, the dot-product between a token h and expert e_k is computed as h^T M_{k*} e_k, where k* is the expert assigned to h in the previous layer, effectively projecting tokens and experts onto the axes that best identify each expert's specialization. The method introduces no learnable parameters and adds only O(n) overhead (two parallel mean computations per cluster). Theoretically, the paper proves the transformation increases inter-cluster separation (Lemma 1), causes mis-assignment probability to decay exponentially with cluster distance (Lemma 2), and improves Hessian conditioning of the loss with respect to expert embeddings for faster convergence (Proposition 2).

## Results

On WikiText-103, ACMoE achieves 34.42 PPL with the Switch-medium backbone and 36.26 PPL with the GLaM-medium backbone, outperforming the best baselines (StableMoE at 35.33 and 38.04 respectively) by 1.06 and 1.78 PPL. On ImageNet-1K with the Swin backbone, Swin-ACMoE-Top2 reaches 76.31% top-1 accuracy on clean data and 43.74% under PGD attack (vs. 40.85% for Swin-Top2, a 7% relative gain). Finetuning on Banking-77 shows consistent ~3% accuracy improvements (86.01% vs. 83.82% for Switch), and under Text Attack contamination, GLaM-ACMoE reduces PPL degradation from 50.84 to 47.91 (a 5.8% improvement over GLaM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACMoE outperforms all compared routing baselines across every configuration. Against SMoE (Shazeer et al., 2017), ACMoE-medium reduces PPL from 35.48 to 34.42 on Switch and from 38.27 to 36.26 on GLaM. Against XMoE (Chi et al., 2022), the gains are even larger: 34.42 vs. 35.88 (Switch) and 36.26 vs. 38.10 (GLaM). Against StableMoE (Dai et al., 2022), ACMoE-medium still leads by 0.91 PPL on Switch (34.42 vs. 35.33) and 1.78 PPL on GLaM (36.26 vs. 38.04). On ImageNet, Swin-ACMoE-Top2 beats Swin-Top2 (Liu et al., 2021) by 0.21% on clean top-1 accuracy and by 2.89% under PGD (43.74% vs. 40.85%) and 2.90% under SPSA (63.47% vs. 60.57%). In the SoftMoE setting (Puigcerver et al., 2023), Soft-ACMoE improves PGD top-1 from 45.29% to 48.25% and SPSA top-1 from 66.59% to 70.63%, confirming the method's generality across routing paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the MoE routing problem through the lens of feature-weighted clustering from the statistics literature (Witten & Tibshirani, 2010; Friedman & Meulman, 2004), deriving optimal per-cluster feature weights that adaptively scale the input space before computing token-expert dot-products. It departs from standard MoE routing (Shazeer et al., 2017) and its variants (XMoE, StableMoE, SoftMoE) by arguing that the implicit assumption of uniform feature importance across all experts is suboptimal when latent clusters concentrate on disjoint feature subsets. The resulting AC router is a parameter-free diagonal transformation applied to the routing computation, positioned as a drop-in modification to any existing MoE backbone rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free, negligible-overhead nature of the AC router makes it an attractive plug-in for any MoE system, and the theoretical guarantees (improved Hessian conditioning for faster convergence, exponential decay of mis-assignment probability with cluster separation) provide a principled explanation for the observed robustness gains under adversarial attacks. A key limitation is the dependence on the previous layer's expert assignment to compute the transformation, which restricts applicability to the first MoE layer and requires consistent embedding dimensions across adjacent layers. Open questions include whether cluster memberships can be estimated without relying on prior layers, whether the approach extends to non-Transformer MoE architectures, and whether the benefits persist at much larger scales (trillion-parameter models) where the current experiments (70M–280M parameters) may not be representative.
