## Problem

Payoff-perturbed learning algorithms in monotone games achieve last-iterate convergence, but the best prior rates (APMD/APGA) are Õ(1/√T) with full feedback and Õ(1/T^{1/10}) with noisy feedback. The magnitude of perturbation must be carefully tied to a periodically re-initialized anchoring strategy, and the abrupt reset of this anchor at each interval limits the achievable convergence speed. This paper asks whether a simple modification to the perturbation structure can accelerate both rates without changing the overall anchoring scheme.

## Input / Output

At each iteration t, each player i receives a gradient feedback vector (the true gradient ∇_{π_i}v_i(π^t) in the full-feedback case, or ∇_{π_i}v_i(π^t)+ξ_i^t with zero-mean, bounded-variance additive noise in the noisy case) and outputs an updated strategy π_i^{t+1} in a compact convex set X_i ⊂ R^{d_i}. The algorithm is parameterized by a constant perturbation strength μ, a learning-rate schedule (constant η for full feedback; decreasing η_t = 1/(κ(t−T_σ(k(t)−1))+2θ) for noisy feedback), and an anchoring update interval T_σ. The primary performance metric is the gap function GAP(π^{T+1}) (upper-bounded by D·r^{tan}(π^{T+1})), which vanishes if and only if the last-iterate profile is a Nash equilibrium.

## Method

GABP is a one-line modification of APGA: it adds the term μ(σ_i^{k(t)}−σ_i^1)/(k(t)+1) to the gradient perturbation, so that the effective anchor becomes the convex combination σ̂_i^{k(t)} = (k(t)σ_i^{k(t)}+σ_i^1)/(k(t)+1) rather than the raw anchor σ_i^{k(t)}. This makes the perturbation target evolve gradually toward the current anchor instead of jumping at each re-initialization, stabilizing the learning dynamics. The proof hinges on a novel potential function P^{k(t)} that is approximately non-increasing across anchoring updates even under additive noise—unlike the potential in Cai & Zheng (2023), it contains no terms depending on the noisy gradient. This yields an O(1/k(t)) bound on the distance between the perturbed stationary point and the anchor (improving the prior O(1/√k(t))), which combined with the exponential contraction within each block gives Õ(1/T) (full) and Õ(1/T^{1/7}) (noisy) last-iterate rates, plus an O((ln T)^2) individual regret bound.

## Results

GABP achieves Õ(1/T) last-iterate convergence in the full feedback setting and Õ(1/T^{1/7}) in the noisy feedback setting, improving upon APGA's Õ(1/√T) and Õ(1/T^{1/10}) rates respectively. In the full feedback setting, GABP also guarantees individual regret of O((ln T)²), which is significantly better than OG's O(√T) and slightly worse than AOG's O(ln T). Empirically, on the hard concave-convex game with noisy feedback, GABP demonstrates a substantial gap-function advantage over all baselines, while on random payoff games with full feedback it performs comparably to the other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against APGA (Abe et al., 2024), GABP strictly dominates in both theoretical and empirical settings: it improves the full-feedback rate from Õ(1/√T) to Õ(1/T) and the noisy-feedback rate from Õ(1/T^{1/10}) to Õ(1/T^{1/7}). Empirically, GABP outperforms APGA on the gap function in the noisy feedback setting for both random payoff and hard concave-convex games, with the margin being slight on random payoff games but substantial on the hard game. Against OG (Daskalakis et al., 2018; Wei et al., 2021), GABP achieves lower dynamic regret under both full and noisy feedback on the hard concave-convex game, and a faster theoretical convergence rate; OG's last-iterate convergence is primarily established only for full feedback. Against AOG (Cai & Zheng, 2023), GABP is competitive on the gap function under full feedback for the hard concave-convex game, but clearly outperforms AOG under noisy feedback where AOG's convergence degrades. On regret, GABP's O((ln T)²) bound is slightly worse than AOG's O(ln T) but far superior to OG's O(√T).

## Relation to Prior Work

GABP is a direct one-line modification of APMD/APGA (Abe et al., 2024), adding a 'boosting' term μ(σ^k − σ^1)/(k+1) to the payoff perturbation so that the effective anchoring point evolves gradually rather than jumping at each re-initialization. The periodically re-initializing anchoring strategy scheme itself descends from Perolat et al. (2021) and Abe et al. (2023), while the potential function used in the analysis is adapted from Cai & Zheng (2023) (AOG), with the key modification of removing gradient-dependent terms to handle noisy feedback. The paper also draws on the broader payoff-perturbation lineage (Facchinei & Pang, 2003; Koshal et al., 2010) and positions GABP against optimistic-gradient methods (OG, AOG) that achieve last-iterate convergence primarily in the full-feedback regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rate improvements—Õ(1/T) vs. Õ(1/√T) for full feedback and Õ(1/T^{1/7}) vs. Õ(1/T^{1/10}) for noisy feedback—are meaningful for practical applications like GAN training and preference-based LLM fine-tuning where last-iterate convergence avoids the memory overhead of averaging. The individual regret bound of O((ln T)²) is competitive with AOG's O(ln T) while being more robust to noise. A key open direction identified by the authors is extending the analysis to bandit feedback, where each player only observes scalar payoffs rather than full gradients. The experiments, however, are limited to two small-scale concave-convex game classes, leaving open whether the theoretical advantages persist in high-dimensional or non-smooth settings.
