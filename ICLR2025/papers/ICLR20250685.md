## Problem

Existing Multi-Turn Composed Image Retrieval (MTCIR) methods rely on a 'multiple single-turn' paradigm that only leverages the most recent turn's image, ignoring attributes from historical turns. This causes retrieval failures when modification requests reference or combine attributes from previous turns (e.g., 'keep the color from turn 2'). Additionally, existing MTCIR datasets are constructed by concatenating single-turn data, lacking genuine historical context and suffering from small scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of N turns, each containing a reference image (ViT-L patch embeddings), its generated caption (via Flan-T5-XXL), and a modified text that may explicitly reference historical turns through rollback phrases ('I prefer the item from turn 2') or combination phrases ('Keep the pattern in the 1st turn, and...'). The output is a reference-side embedding (32 learned tokens) that is matched against gallery-side bimodal embeddings (image + caption) via cosine similarity to rank target images. The MIO mechanism ensures only 32 tokens (64 MB) are retained per turn regardless of the number of historical turns, unlike concatenation or RNN-based approaches that scale linearly with N.

## Method

MAI introduces a Two-stage Semantic Aggregation (TSA) paradigm built on BLIP-2's Q-Former: Bimodal Semantic Aggregation (BSA) first fuses the reference image with its caption via self-attention and cross-attention, then Modification Semantic Aggregation (MSA) interacts the resulting tokens with the modified text embedding. Captions serve as a modality-bridging transition, and the paper provides a theoretical proof (Proposition 1) showing that this pre-fusion approach yields a tighter generalization error bound than naive cross-modal losses. A parameter-free Multi-turn Iterative Optimization (MIO) mechanism applies DPC-kNN clustering to the concatenated previous and current learned tokens, estimating density and pruning to retain only the top-k most semantically diverse tokens, reducing storage from O(N) to O(1). Training uses a Cyclic Combination Loss (CCL) that imposes four batch-based contrastive constraints in a cycle (reference tokens → target tokens → modified text → target caption → reference tokens), enforcing semantic consistency across all modalities simultaneously.

## Results

On the proposed FashionMT dataset, MAI achieves a mean recall of 66.64, surpassing the previous SOTA method SPRC (58.01) by 8.63 points. Under the Combination setting, MAI reaches R@1 of 51.51 and R@20 of 86.52, while under the Rollback setting it achieves R@1 of 28.94 and R@20 of 81.50. On existing datasets, MAI also leads: 50.6 mean on MT FashionIQ and 33.8 on MT Shoes, outperforming SPRC (48.0 and 28.2 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAI outperforms all 14 baselines across every metric on FashionMT. The closest competitor is SPRC (ICLR'24), a sentence-level prompt method built on BLIP-2, which achieves a mean of 58.01; MAI improves upon it by 8.63 points (66.64), with particularly large gains in the Rollback setting (28.94 vs. 23.31 R@1). Among interleaved MLLMs, MMICL (54.87) and Flamingo-9B (38.76) trail MAI by 11.77 and 27.88 mean points respectively, indicating that general-purpose multimodal LLMs struggle with the fine-grained multi-turn attribute tracking required by MTCIR. Zero-shot CIR methods adapted with FashionNTM memory (Pic2word 28.94, Context-I2W 45.89, Image2Sentence 49.15) show progressively stronger performance as their single-turn capability improves, yet all remain well below MAI. Dedicated MTCIR methods CFIR (26.19) and FashionNTM (36.26) are the weakest baselines, confirming the paper's argument that the 'multiple single-turn' paradigm fails to exploit historical context. On the existing MT FashionIQ and MT Shoes benchmarks, MAI also leads all compared methods (50.6 and 33.8 mean respectively), demonstrating generalization beyond the proposed dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-turn composed image retrieval (MTCIR) line initiated by CFIR (Yuan & Lam, 2021) and FashionNTM (Pal et al., 2023), which modeled multi-turn retrieval via memory networks or RNNs over concatenated single-turn data. It departs from the prevailing 'multiple single-turn' paradigm by introducing retrospective-based modifications where new turns explicitly reference historical images, and by repurposing BLIP-2's Q-Former into a two-stage semantic aggregation pipeline (BSA → MSA) that uses image captions as a modality bridge before interacting with modified text. The Multi-turn Iterative Optimization mechanism, which applies DPC-kNN density-peak clustering to prune redundant learned tokens across turns, is a novel parameter-free alternative to the LSTM/GRU/NTM memory modules used in prior MTCIR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FashionMT dataset (1.07M images, 248K transactions, 95 subcategories) is the first MTCIR benchmark with genuine historical-context dependencies, addressing a gap where prior datasets degraded multi-turn retrieval into sequential single-turn queries. The 8.63-point mean-recall improvement over the prior SOTA (SPRC) and the finding that a parameter-free clustering-based token pruner (MIO) outperforms learned memory modules (LSTM, GRU, NTM) while using a fixed 64 MB of storage are practically significant for deployment. Open questions include whether the standardized 3-turn setup generalizes to longer interactions, whether the e-commerce-specific modification patterns transfer to other domains, and whether the retrospective formulation can be integrated with end-to-end dialogue systems rather than relying on template-matched rollback detection.
