## Problem

Combining Local Training (LT) and Communication Compression (CC) in federated learning while preserving the acceleration benefits of both techniques has been a major open challenge. The only prior algorithm achieving doubly-accelerated complexity (CompressedScaffnew) required a specific permutation-based compressor with shared randomness across all clients, ruling out standard sparsification and quantization. This paper asks whether one can combine LT with any unbiased compressor from the generic class U(ω) and still obtain a doubly-accelerated communication complexity.

## Input / Output

Each of n clients holds a private smooth, strongly convex function f_i over R^d, and all clients additionally have access to a shared function g (e.g., a regularizer or shared dataset cost). The algorithm takes as input local initial estimates x_i^0, a common initial estimate y^0, initial dual variables satisfying a feasibility constraint, and a choice of unbiased compression operators C_i ∈ U(ω) (e.g., rand-k sparsification, 9-bit quantization, or l1-selection). The output is the unique minimizer x* of (1/n)Σf_i(x)+g(x), to which all local estimates x_i^t and the shared estimate y^t converge almost surely. Notably, all gradient calls (to both ∇f_i and ∇g) are made by the clients, not the server.

## Method

LoCoDL reformulates the distributed problem as a consensus problem over n+1 variables (x_1,…,x_n,y) via a 'double lifting,' introducing a shared anchor variable y that all clients maintain identically. The key mechanism is that clients compress the model-difference vectors x̂_i − ŷ (not gradient differences), which vanish at convergence and thus make the algorithm variance-reduced. Communication occurs probabilistically with probability p (the same coin-flip LT mechanism as Scaffnew); when it fires, clients send compressed differences to the server, which aggregates them and broadcasts the result. Primal-dual updates with dual variables u_i and v enforce a consensus constraint while maintaining dual feasibility, and the choice of p, step sizes, and compression variance yields a doubly-accelerated uplink complexity of O((√d√κ + d√κ/√n + d) log ε⁻¹) reals. Unlike CompressedScaffnew, LoCoDL works with any independent unbiased compressor in U(ω), including rand-k sparsification and quantization, without requiring shared randomness.

## Results

LoCoDL achieves a doubly-accelerated uplink communication complexity of O((√d·√κ + d√κ/√n + d) log ε⁻¹) in number of reals, matching the theoretical state of the art (CompressedScaffnew, 5GCS-CC, ADIANA) while supporting any unbiased compressor in U(ω) (rand-k sparsification, natural quantization, l₁-selection) rather than a single permutation-based scheme. In experiments on logistic regression (κ=10⁴) across six datasets (a5a, diabetes, w1a, australian, covtype.binary, MNIST) with n ranging from 6 to 6,000 clients, LoCoDL consistently converges fastest in total communicated bits per client, outperforming all baselines including ADIANA, which has a strictly better theoretical complexity in the n ≫ d² regime. The advantage is most pronounced when combining rand-k sparsification with 9-bit natural quantization, where LoCoDL reduces communication by a large factor over DIANA and EF21.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoCoDL matches the theoretical complexity of CompressedScaffnew, 5GCS-CC, and ADIANA in the n = O(d) regime (all achieve (d/√n)√κ + d reals), but strictly outperforms DIANA, EF21, and FedCOMGATE, which remain at dκ or (d/n)κ + d. In practice, LoCoDL wins against every baseline across all six datasets and all tested values of n: it converges faster in total communicated bits than DIANA, EF21, 5GCS-CC, ADIANA, CompressedScaffnew, GradSkip, and Scaffold. Notably, ADIANA has a strictly better theoretical complexity than LoCoDL when n = Ω(d²) (achieving (√κ + d) log ε⁻¹), yet LoCoDL still outperforms it empirically in all experiments, including the covtype.binary dataset with n = 6,000 and d = 54. Against CompressedScaffnew specifically, LoCoDL matches the complexity bound but is more practical because it works with any independent unbiased compressor (rand-k, quantization, l₁-selection) rather than requiring shared-randomness permutation-based compression. The paper also shows that combining rand-k sparsification with 9-bit natural quantization (joint sparsification + quantization) gives LoCoDL its best empirical performance, a flexibility not available to CompressedScaffnew.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoCoDL inherits its probabilistic local training mechanism (coin-flip communication with probability p) directly from Scaffnew (Mishchenko et al., 2022) and its class of unbiased compressors from DIANA (Mishchenko et al., 2019). The key departure is the 'double lifting' reformulation of the distributed problem into a consensus problem with an additional anchor variable y, which enables compressing model differences rather than gradient differences. This achieves the same doubly-accelerated communication complexity as CompressedScaffnew (Condat et al., 2022a) but with the general class of independent unbiased compressors U(ω) — including rand-k sparsification and quantization — rather than the restrictive permutation-based compressor requiring shared randomness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Communication efficiency remains the primary bottleneck preventing large-scale federated learning deployment, and this paper demonstrates that local training and compression can be combined with generic compressors without sacrificing either acceleration benefit. The practical outperformance of ADIANA (which has superior theoretical complexity for large n) suggests that LT-based methods offer advantages not captured by current theoretical bounds. However, the analysis is restricted to strongly convex problems, and the experiments are limited to small-scale logistic regression on LibSVM datasets and MNIST digit classification; extensions to nonconvex objectives, stochastic gradients, and partial participation remain open.
