## Problem

State-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit 'face blindness': they can engage in general image-based conversations but fail to recognize and discuss specific individuals provided as reference photos, even when explicitly named in the prompt. Prior personalization approaches (MyVLM, Yo'LLaVA) require adding trainable parameters and fine-tuning for each new individual, making them impractical for real-world settings where the person of interest changes frequently and training data may be unavailable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251552/cropped_page7_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of multi-modal prefixes, each consisting of a personal reference image (either a cropped face or a PhotoMaker-generated augmented photo) paired with a textual introduction (a name drawn from a pool of ~600, optionally with personal attributes like hobbies or family), all enclosed in special wrapper tokens. This is followed by a scene image (original or a composite of concatenated person crops) and a natural-language query targeting a specific individual. The output is a free-form text response that either correctly identifies and describes the named person in the scene or explicitly refuses when the person is absent or was never introduced.

## Method

PVIT reframes personalization as an in-context learning task: each individual is supplied as a <personal image, personal introduction> pair wrapped in <|person_start|> and <|person_end|> tokens, and the MLLM is trained via LoRA fine-tuning (on LLaVA-1.6-7B) to answer queries conditioned on these prefixes, requiring no per-individual training at inference. The critical enabler is an automated three-phase data pipeline that synthesizes 3M training instances (PVIT-3M): (1) Visual Concept Curation uses GroundingDINO and a face detector to localize individuals, then PhotoMaker to generate identity-preserving augmented photos from different angles; (2) Dual-Level Textual Information Extraction has an MLLM (InternVL2-26B) produce both per-person cropped-image descriptions and holistic scene descriptions, which an LLM (LLaMA-3.1-8B) fuses into contextually grounded individual descriptions; (3) PVIT Dataset Generation uses the LLM to create diverse QA pairs (description, free-form, multi-choice) with name swapping, personal pronoun handling, and adversarial samples (unintroduced names, absent individuals) that teach the model to refuse unanswerable queries. Ablations confirm that face augmentation improves recognition robustness and adversarial samples are essential for correct refusal behavior.

## Results

On P-Bench MC questions, P-LLaVA (trained with PVIT) achieves 96.69% average accuracy on answerable questions and 99.72% on unanswerable questions, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline LLaVA-OneVision-7B (80.71% and 12.60% respectively). On personalized description tasks, P-LLaVA scores 82.55% (answerable, LongCLIP similarity) and 100% rejection rate on unanswerable queries, compared to the best baseline Deepseek-VL-7b-chat at 78.13% and 2.50%. The most dramatic gap appears on unanswerable questions, where all six SOTA MLLMs fail catastrophically (2.06%–31.49%) while P-LLaVA correctly refuses to answer in nearly all cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P-LLaVA outperforms all six SOTA MLLM baselines on every metric in P-Bench. On answerable MC questions, the strongest baseline is LLaVA-OneVision-7B (80.71% avg), which P-LLaVA surpasses by 16.0 points; the remaining baselines range from 50.03% (VILA1.5-7B) to 63.62% (mPLUG-OWl2). The gap widens on harder sub-tasks: on Aug-Sc-3 (three-person composite images), P-LLaVA scores 97.62% versus 78.31% for LLaVA-OneVision and 38.29% for VILA1.5-7B. On unanswerable questions, the disparity is extreme—P-LLaVA achieves 99.72% (correctly refusing to answer), while the best baseline InternVL-Chat-V1.5-26B reaches only 31.49%, and most others fall below 20%. On personalized description tasks (Table 2), P-LLaVA leads by 4.4 points on answerable descriptions (82.55% vs. 78.13% for Deepseek-VL) and by 97.5 points on unanswerable rejection (100% vs. 2.50%), confirming that no existing MLLM has been trained to handle unanswerable personalized queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior personalized MLLM approaches (MyVLM, Yo'LLaVA) that require per-individual fine-tuning with additional parameters, instead reframing personalization as an in-context learning problem where individuals are specified via multimodal prefixes (reference image + name). It builds on the in-context learning paradigm established for LLMs (Wei et al., 2023) and extends it to the multimodal setting, leveraging the auto-regressive nature of MLLMs to condition on arbitrary individuals at inference time. The data construction pipeline repurposes existing tools—GroundingDINO for person detection, PhotoMaker for identity-preserving augmentation, and LLMs/MLLMs for text generation—into an automated annotation framework, while the personalized wrapper tokens resolve ambiguity in multi-person contexts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The in-context personalization paradigm eliminates the need for per-individual retraining, making personalized MLLMs practical for real-world applications like family robots and mobile assistants where the set of recognized individuals changes frequently. The P-Bench benchmark and PVIT-3M dataset provide the first systematic resources for evaluating and training personalization in MLLMs, filling a clear gap in the literature. Key limitations include the vision encoder bottleneck (the authors acknowledge this as a cause of the gap to GPT-4o), difficulty with large age gaps and look-alike individuals, and the fact that experiments are conducted on a single base model (LLaVA-7B) with a self-constructed benchmark, leaving open whether the paradigm generalizes across architectures and scales.
