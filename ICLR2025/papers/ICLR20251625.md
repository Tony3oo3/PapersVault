## Problem

Graph OOD detection methods that rely on real OOD data for exposure are impractical because such data is rarely available at training time and may not represent the true test-time distribution. OOD synthesis approaches from computer vision depend on large pre-trained generative models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) that have no graph-data equivalent, leaving a gap for synthesising OOD-like graph instances without auxiliary data or pre-trained generators.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph G=(X, A) with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and adjacency matrix A, processed by a 2-layer GCN (hidden size 64) to produce hidden representations H at layer L−1. These hidden embeddings feed the latent generative model, while the full GNN output Z produces logits for classification. The final output is a binary OOD/ID decision per node based on a transformed energy score e′ computed from the GNN logits and an MLP detector, with energy propagated across the graph structure (k=2 iterations, α=0.5) before scoring.

## Method

GOLD employs an implicit adversarial training paradigm with alternating optimisation between two components: (1) a latent generative model (LDM or VAE) that mimics ID embeddings from an evolving GNN, and (2) a GNN classifier plus MLP OOD detector that maximises energy divergence between ID and generated pseudo-OOD embeddings. The key insight is that the LGM always generates embeddings close to the current ID distribution, while the detector and classifier implicitly push the energy scores apart via gradient flow, transforming the synthetic embeddings into pseudo-OOD instances without ever directly generating them as OOD. The detector uses a novel uncertainty loss (L_Unc) for binary ID/pOOD classification and a divergence regulariser (L_DReg) that amplifies the gap between original GNN energy and transformed MLP energy, combined with the standard energy regulariser (L_EReg) into a composite L_Div objective. This alternating scheme prevents the pseudo-OOD from drifting too far from ID while progressively aligning its energy distribution with real OOD data.

## Results

GOLD (with LDM) achieves state-of-the-art OOD detection without any real OOD data, with the headline result being a FPR95 reduction from 33.57% (GNNSAFE++) to 1.78% on Twitch. Across all five benchmark datasets, GOLD w/LDM attains AUROC of 99.46 (Twitch), 95.84 (Cora), 98.81 (Amazon), 99.00 (Coauthor), and 73.90 (Arxiv), consistently surpassing non-OOD-exposure SOTA (NODESAFE, GNNSAFE) by large margins. On Twitch and Cora, GOLD also outperforms the real-OOD-exposure SOTA (NODESAFE++, GNNSAFE++), while on Amazon, Coauthor, and Arxiv it achieves comparable performance to GNNSAFE++ without requiring auxiliary OOD data. The lightweight VAE variant (GOLD w/VAE) achieves near-identical OOD detection performance with faster training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOLD decisively outperforms all non-OOD-exposure baselines: on Twitch, it improves AUROC from 89.99 (NODESAFE) to 99.46 and FPR95 from 47.00 to 1.78; on Cora, FPR95 drops from 26.04 (NODESAFE) to 17.83. Against the real-OOD-exposure SOTA, GOLD w/LDM surpasses GNNSAFE++ on Twitch (FPR95: 1.78 vs 33.57) and Cora (FPR95: 17.83 vs 38.44), and matches or slightly edges out GNNSAFE++ on Amazon (FPR95: 2.07 vs 2.10) and Coauthor (FPR95: 3.16 vs 3.18). On Arxiv, where OOD is defined by temporal shift and the classifier's ID accuracy is only ~50%, NODESAFE++ retains a slight edge (AUROC 75.49 vs 73.90), and GOLD's pseudo-OOD generation is limited by the weak classifier. The simpler baselines (MSP, ODIN, Mahalanobis, Energy, GKDE, GPN, OE) are consistently outperformed by large margins across all datasets, with FPR95 values often exceeding 80–100% on challenging datasets like Twitch and Arxiv.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOLD builds directly on the energy-based OOD detection framework for graphs established by GNNSAFE (Wu et al., ICLR 2023), adopting its energy propagation schema and energy regulariser as the detection backbone, and on the energy score formulation from Liu et al. (NeurIPS 2020). It departs from two prior lines: (1) OOD-exposure methods (GNNSAFE++, NODESAFE++) that require a separate real OOD dataset during training, and (2) OOD-synthesis methods from computer vision (VOS, Dream-OOD, DFDD) that rely on large pre-trained generative models unavailable for graph data. The key conceptual departure is the implicit adversarial paradigm: rather than directly generating OOD-like samples or requiring auxiliary OOD data, a latent generative model (LDM or VAE) produces ID-like embeddings that are then implicitly transformed into pseudo-OOD through energy divergence with the evolving GNN, effectively simulating OOD exposure without any external data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GOLD addresses a practical bottleneck in graph OOD detection: the most effective methods (OOD exposure) require auxiliary OOD data that is typically unavailable in deployment, while non-exposure methods underperform. The implicit adversarial mechanism offers a principled middle ground that could be adopted whenever real OOD samples are infeasible to obtain. However, the approach is currently limited to node-level tasks, incurs higher training cost (especially with LDM), and shows dataset-dependent gains—on Arxiv, where OOD is defined by temporal shift and ID accuracy is only ~50%, GOLD does not surpass GNNSAFE++. Open questions include extension to graph-level OOD detection, applicability to other detection paradigms beyond energy scores, and whether the adversarial pseudo-OOD mechanism generalizes to settings with more extreme distribution shifts.
