## Problem

Most NeuroAI benchmarking work evaluates deep learning models against human responses to static images, focusing on the ventral visual stream. Real-world vision is highly dynamic and involves complex social interactions processed by the lateral visual stream, yet no comprehensive benchmark has tested whether current image, video, or language models can match human behavioral ratings and neural responses to naturalistic social action videos. This paper identifies a critical gap: even state-of-the-art models substantially underperform in predicting lateral stream responses and human social judgments.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 3-second videos from the Moments in Time dataset (250 total: 200 train, 50 test) depicting multi-agent social interactions, along with human-annotated sentence captions (≥5 per video from 150 Prolific participants). Image models consume 7 evenly-sampled frames averaged per video; language models consume averaged caption embeddings. Outputs are Pearson correlations predicting 8 behavioral ratings (spatial expanse, interagent distance, agents facing, object-directedness, joint action, communicating, valence, arousal) and voxel-wise fMRI responses in 8 ROIs spanning the ventral (FFA, PPA) and lateral (EBA, LOC, pSTS, aSTS) streams plus EVC and MT, evaluated against inter-rater agreement and test-retest reliability noise ceilings.

## Method

The paper performs a large-scale opportunistic benchmark of 350+ models (348 image, 8 video, 20+ language) using DeepJuice for memory-efficient feature extraction from every layer, projected to ~4732 dimensions via sparse random projection. A GPU-optimized leave-one-out ridge regression maps model features to behavioral or neural targets, with the optimal layer selected per rating/voxel via 4-fold cross-validation on the training split. A caption perturbation experiment (masking nouns, verbs, or both while preserving syntactic structure) isolates which linguistic content drives language-model alignment with behavior and brain. Statistical significance between model classes is assessed via 5,000-iteration permutation tests. The key finding is a dissociation: language models best predict social behavioral ratings (relying on both noun and verb content), video models best predict mid-level lateral stream responses (MT, EBA, LOC, pSTS), but no model class matches human performance in lateral regions, and language models fail to predict any neural responses well.

## Results

Across 350+ models benchmarked on 250 three-second social action videos, language models (e.g., paraphrase-MiniLM-L6-v2) achieve the highest average behavioral rating prediction (mean r ≈ 0.60–0.70 across social features like acting jointly, valence, arousal), while video models (e.g., x3d_s) achieve the highest average fMRI encoding performance in lateral stream ROIs (MT: 0.440, EBA: 0.427, LOC: 0.352, pSTS: 0.251). Image models (e.g., BEiT-v2, DeiT-3) dominate ventral stream ROIs (FFA: 0.536–0.552, PPA: 0.588–0.593) and early visual cortex (EVC: 0.369–0.399). No single model class matches both human behavioral ratings and neural responses simultaneously, and all models fall well below the inter-subject noise ceiling for social ratings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For behavioral ratings, language models (MiniLM, GPT-3 embeddings, RoBERTa) significantly outperform image and video models on social features (acting jointly, valence, arousal; p < 0.01–0.001), with paraphrase-MiniLM-L6-v2 ranking #1 overall (mean r ≈ 0.64). For fMRI encoding, video models (x3d_s, x3d_m, i3d_r50, SlowFast) significantly outperform image models in mid-level lateral ROIs (MT, EBA, LOC, pSTS; p < 0.001), with x3d_s achieving r = 0.440 in MT versus ~0.24 for the best image models. Image models (BEiT-v2, DeiT-3, ConvNeXt) retain the advantage in ventral stream regions (FFA, PPA) and early visual cortex, where video models score 0.10–0.20 lower. CLIP image encoders (clip_vitl14, clip_rn50x4) perform competitively with other image models in ventral ROIs but do not close the gap in lateral regions. Language models, including GPT-3 and Cohere embeddings, perform poorly on neural responses across all ROIs and significantly underperform image models in EVC, FFA, and PPA (p < 0.05). The hand-engineered features from McMahon et al. (2023) serve as a strong baseline, approaching video model performance in MT (0.392 vs. 0.440) but falling short in EBA and LOC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeuroAI benchmarking paradigm (Conwell et al., 2024; Schrimpf et al., 2018) from static scene recognition in the ventral visual stream to dynamic social interactions in the lateral visual stream. It adopts the caption perturbation methodology from Conwell et al. (2023) to probe which linguistic features drive language-model–brain alignment, and uses the DeepJuice framework for scalable feature extraction and linear mapping. The key conceptual departure is treating the lateral stream (EBA, LOC, pSTS, aSTS) as a distinct computational target rather than assuming ventral-stream models generalize, and introducing language models as first-class predictors of both behavior and visually-evoked neural responses in a dynamic setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental and quantified gap: no model class simultaneously matches human social behavior ratings and lateral-stream neural responses, and even the best models substantially underperform in lateral regions relative to ventral regions. The finding that language models predict social ratings well yet fail to explain neural responses challenges strong 'language alignment' assumptions in visual cortex and suggests that social perception requires image-computable relational representations over time. Open questions include whether larger, more diverse video datasets and models trained on human-aligned social tasks (e.g., GNN-RNN architectures, generative inverse planning) can close this gap, and whether the absence of hierarchical layer-to-region correspondence in the lateral stream reflects a genuinely different computational organization.
