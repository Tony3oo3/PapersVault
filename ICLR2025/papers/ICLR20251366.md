## Problem

LLM-based embedding models rely on task instructions to generalize across domains, but instructions alone provide limited information, causing performance degradation on unseen tasks. While in-context learning (ICL) is a core capability of LLMs for generation, no prior work has successfully integrated ICL into embedding models to enable few-shot adaptation without additional training or architectural changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251366/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a query text, a task instruction, and a variable number (0 to 5) of few-shot query-passage pairs as input, formatted with special tokens (<Instruct>, <query>, <response>) and concatenated into a single sequence capped at 2048 tokens. The output is the final hidden state of the [EOS] token, which serves as the embedding vector for both queries and passages. The number of examples is the key variable: zero examples yields a zero-shot embedding, while one or more examples produce a few-shot embedding that incorporates task-specific context.

## Method

The ICL Embedder uses a simple training strategy: at each training step, a random number of examples (0 to 5) is sampled from the same batch and prepended to the query, so the model learns to handle both zero-shot and few-shot scenarios without degrading either. Training uses standard InfoNCE contrastive loss (temperature 0.02) with in-batch and hard negatives, applied via LoRA (rank 64) on Mistral-7B for one epoch. A key empirical finding is that the original causal-attention architecture with last-token pooling outperforms bidirectional attention or mean pooling in the ICL setting, suggesting that preserving the LLM's pre-trained inductive biases is more beneficial than architectural modifications. The approach requires no synthetic data generation, no new model components, and no changes to the backbone beyond standard instruction-tuning fine-tuning.

## Results

On the MTEB benchmark (56 tasks), bge-en-icl trained on the Augmented E5-Mistral dataset achieves 71.24 (zero-shot) and 71.67 (few-shot) average score, reaching SOTA as of August 29, 2024. Under the fairer E5-Mistral-only training setup, the model scores 64.67 (zero-shot) and 66.08 (few-shot), with the few-shot gain being most pronounced on clustering (+3.94) and classification (+1.84) tasks not seen during training. On AIR-Bench, the model achieves 55.92 (QA, nDCG@10) and 75.98 (Long-Doc, Recall@10) in the few-shot setting with the E5-Mistral dataset, outperforming all baselines including NV-Embed-v2 (52.28 and 74.78 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against E5-mistral-7b-instruct (66.63) and GritLM-7B (66.76), bge-en-icl (few-shot, 71.67) achieves a substantial 5.0 and 4.9 point MTEB average improvement, respectively, with the largest gains on clustering (+7.63 over E5-mistral) and retrieval (+5.26 over E5-mistral). Compared to LLM2Vec-Mistral-supervised (64.80) under the same E5-Mistral training data, bge-en-icl (few-shot, 66.08) leads by 1.28 points, with particularly strong gains on clustering (+10.01) and classification (+0.68). Against gte-Qwen2-7B-instruct (70.24), the proposed model wins by 1.43 points on MTEB average. On AIR-Bench, bge-en-icl clearly outperforms all baselines: it leads NV-Embed-v2 by 3.64 points on QA (55.92 vs 52.28) and by 1.20 points on Long-Doc (75.98 vs 74.78) in the few-shot setting. The one notable exception is NV-Embed-v2 (72.31), which slightly edges out bge-en-icl (71.67) on MTEB average, though NV-Embed-v2 was released one day after the paper's SOTA cutoff date.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the E5-Mistral instruction-tuning framework for LLM-based embedding models by replacing the static task instruction with dynamically sampled in-context learning (ICL) examples during training. While prior work (GritLM, LLM2Vec, NV-Embed) focused on architectural modifications—bidirectional attention, latent attention layers, or mean pooling—to adapt decoder-only LLMs for embedding, this paper deliberately retains the original causal-attention, last-token-pooling architecture and instead leverages the LLM's native ICL capability. The key conceptual departure is that ICL, a hallmark of generative LLMs, had not previously been exploited in the embedding setting, and the paper shows that the training strategy (randomly sampling 0–5 in-batch examples per step) is sufficient to endow the model with few-shot adaptability while preserving zero-shot performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that practitioners can improve embedding model generalization to unseen tasks simply by providing a few examples at inference time, without retraining or architectural changes. However, the few-shot gains are modest (~1–2 MTEB points) and diminish when the model has already seen the task during training, raising the question of how much headroom ICL provides for truly novel domains. The finding that bidirectional attention and mean pooling degrade ICL performance challenges a prevailing assumption in the LLM-as-encoder literature, but the evidence is limited to a single 7B model (Mistral-7B) and a single training dataset family, leaving open whether this holds at larger scale or with different backbones. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to multilingual settings, whether the number of examples can be further reduced, and whether the ICL benefit compounds with model scale.
