## Problem

While pretrained diffusion models have been extensively leveraged as plug-and-play priors (e.g., SDS and VSD losses) for text-to-3D generation and image editing, it remains unexplored whether the newer and more efficient rectified flow models can fulfill the same role. This paper investigates whether large-scale pretrained rectified flow models can be distilled into effective loss functions for downstream generative and inversion tasks, and how the unique straight-line trajectory structure of rectified flow can be exploited beyond what diffusion priors offer.

## Input / Output

For text-to-3D generation, the method takes a text prompt and optimizes a 3D representation (InstantNGP NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting) over 15,000 steps at 256→500 resolution, using a pretrained rectified flow model (SD3 or InstaFlow) as the loss with CFG scale 50. For image inversion and editing, it takes an RGB image and a target text prompt, first inverting the image to a noise code via iRFDS (1000 optimization steps with a Gaussian regularization loss), then generating an edited image by running the forward flow for 5 steps with the modified caption. The inverted noise representation is the intermediate output that enables prompt-faithful editing of real-world images.

## Method

The core contribution is a family of three distillation losses derived from the flow-matching training objective by treating the pretrained velocity network as a fixed loss function. RFDS (the baseline) computes the gradient of the flow residual (v_φ(x_t,t) − α̇_t·x − σ̇_t·ε) with respect to the generator parameters, dropping the network Jacobian just as SDS drops the U-Net Jacobian; this is proven identical to SDS when reformulated in score-function form. iRFDS exploits the time-symmetry of rectified flow to optimize the noise ε given a fixed image x, enabling image inversion without iterative denoising. RFDS-Rev addresses the mode-averaging blur of RFDS by alternating one step of iRFDS (to recover a static, view-consistent noise) with RFDS (to refine the output), effectively bridging the gap between random-noise score distillation and deterministic Euler sampling. The approach is grounded in the Stochastic Interpolants framework, making it applicable to both rectified flow and flow-matching variants, and extends naturally to score-matching diffusion models.

## Results

On the T³ Bench text-to-3D benchmark (300 prompts), RFDS-Rev with SD3 achieves the highest average quality score of 56.5, surpassing ProlificDreamer/VSD (46.4) and DreamFusion/SDS (20.5) among all 2D lifting methods. The RFDS baseline with SD3 already reaches 43.9 average, exceeding the SDS loss (20.5) by over 20 points. For 2D image editing, iRFDS with InstaFlow achieves the best CLIP score (0.306) and user preference for semantic coherence (43.6%), outperforming Null Inversion (0.298 CLIP, 20.1% semantic) and DDS (0.294 CLIP, 7.3% semantic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamFusion's SDS loss, RFDS-Rev (SD3) achieves a 56.5 average quality score versus 20.5, a nearly 3× improvement across all T³ Bench categories. Against ProlificDreamer's VSD loss, RFDS-Rev (SD3) leads by 10.1 points on average (56.5 vs. 46.4), with particularly large gains on Surroundings (+12.0) and Multiple Objects (+9.5); the paper also notes VSD suffers from 'exploding' artifacts absent in RFDS-Rev. For 2D editing, iRFDS (InstaFlow) outperforms Null Inversion on CLIP score (0.306 vs. 0.298) and semantic user preference (43.6% vs. 20.1%), while matching it on consistency (33.4% vs. 32.5%); it decisively beats DDS on all three metrics. Against LucidDreamer, DreamCraft3D, and GaussianDreamer (compared qualitatively in Appendix O), the paper reports 'highly competitive performance' across NeRF and 3DGS backbones but provides no quantitative scores for these comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the plug-and-play prior paradigm established by DreamFusion's SDS loss and ProlificDreamer's VSD loss from diffusion models to rectified flow models, grounded in the Stochastic Interpolants framework (Albergo et al., 2023) that unifies flows and diffusions. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the time-symmetry of rectified flow trajectories to derive iRFDS (inverse RFDS) for image inversion, and RFDS-Rev, which recovers the 'fixed noise' implicit in Euler sampling via a single inversion step. The authors prove that RFDS reduces exactly to SDS when applied to score-matching diffusion models, positioning their framework as a strict generalization rather than a parallel approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of rectified flow models as plug-and-play priors, and the resulting methods achieve state-of-the-art text-to-3D quality on T³Bench among 2D lifting methods while converging roughly 2× faster than diffusion-based counterparts. The unification (RFDS ≡ SDS for diffusion models) provides a clean theoretical bridge that may simplify future work on hybrid flow-diffusion pipelines. Open limitations include the multi-face artifact inherited from pose-unaware 2D base models and the unaddressed CFG mismatch in iRFDS; combining iRFDS with null-inversion and training pose-aware rectified flow models are natural next steps.
