## Problem

While perplexity-based data pruning has been shown to improve LLM pretraining, prior work only evaluated on a single web-scrape dataset (CommonCrawl), relied on upstream test-set perplexity as the quality metric, and did not establish whether a much smaller reference model can effectively prune data for a significantly larger target model. This paper addresses whether small reference models can guide perplexity-based pruning across diverse dataset compositions, how the optimal pruning strategy shifts with domain mix, and whether upstream perplexity faithfully reflects downstream task performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tokenized text dataset (The Pile or Dolma, tokenized with the GPT-4 tokenizer), a selection criteria (low, medium, or high perplexity), and a selection rate r_s ∈ (0,1). The dataset is randomly split into a reference training portion and a final-model training portion; the reference model's per-sample perplexity is computed over the final-model portion, and samples are retained based on their percentile rank within the perplexity distribution. The output is a pruned training set (at rate r_s of the original) and a final MPT transformer (1B or 3B parameters) trained to Chinchilla-optimal token count on the pruned data, evaluated on 33 downstream QA benchmarks via the MosaicML evaluation gauntlet.

## Method

A 125M-parameter MPT reference model is trained for 26B tokens on a random subset of the pretraining corpus, then used to compute the per-sample perplexity (2^NLL) of every sample in the remaining training split. Samples are pruned by selecting those whose perplexity falls within a percentile window determined by the selection criteria: low (bottom r_s percentiles), medium (centered on the 50th percentile with width r_s), or high (top r_s percentiles). The final 1B or 3B MPT model is then trained on the pruned subset using the decoupled Lion optimizer with a cosine schedule. The key empirical finding is that the optimal selection criteria is dataset-composition-dependent: high-perplexity selection is best for the multi-domain Pile (15.61% web-scrape), while medium-perplexity selection is best for the web-scrape-dominated Dolma (81.31% CommonCrawl), and the best strategy for one dataset can degrade performance on the other. The reference model is 30× smaller than the final model, and the approach yields up to 2.04 points average downstream accuracy gain and up to 1.45× fewer pretraining steps to match baseline performance.

## Results

Perplexity-based data pruning with a 125M reference model improves average normalized downstream accuracy of 3B models by up to 2.04 points (Pile, high-perplexity selection) and 0.59 points (Dolma, medium-perplexity selection), and 1B models by 1.89 and 1.51 points respectively. Pruned models reach commensurate baseline performance in 1.31×–1.45× fewer pretraining steps (Pile) and 1.14×–1.29× fewer steps (Dolma). Gains persist in over-trained (5× Chinchilla) and data-constrained (up to 2× repetition) regimes, though the Dolma over-training gain shrinks from 1.51 to 0.84. Notably, pruned models exhibit *worse* test-set perplexity (e.g., 8.51 vs. 7.83 on Pile) while achieving significantly better downstream accuracy, demonstrating that upstream perplexity is a misleading evaluation metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is training on the full unpruned dataset (no pruning). Across all four dataset–model-size combinations, perplexity-pruned data outperforms the no-pruning baseline on average downstream accuracy: +1.89 (1B Pile), +2.04 (3B Pile), +1.51 (1B Dolma), and +0.59 (3B Dolma). The optimal selection criterion is dataset-dependent: high-perplexity selection is best for the Pile (diverse curated domains, 15.6% web-scrape) while medium-perplexity selection is best for Dolma (web-scrape-heavy, 81.3% CommonCrawl). Applying the wrong criterion can *degrade* performance below baseline—e.g., medium-perplexity selection on the Pile drops average accuracy to 13.50 (−0.23 vs. baseline), and low-perplexity selection on the Pile drops it to 9.56 (−4.17). In the over-trained regime (5× Chinchilla tokens), the Pile gain is nearly unchanged (1.89 → 1.74) while the Dolma gain shrinks (1.51 → 0.84). In the data-constrained regime, pruning still helps up to 2× data repetition but gains vanish beyond 4× post-pruning repetitions, consistent with prior findings on data repetition saturation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Marion et al. (2023), which established sample perplexity under a reference model as the best heuristic for pruning LLM pretraining data, by demonstrating that the reference model can be 30× smaller than the final model and by evaluating impact through downstream benchmarks rather than upstream test-set perplexity. It also relates to the broader data-selection literature (DoReMi's learnability-based domain reweighting, importance resampling, embedding-based deduplication) but departs from n-gram perplexity filtering by training the reference model on the same distribution, making perplexity a difficulty signal rather than an in-distribution score. The key conceptual departure from prior perplexity-pruning work is the systematic study of how dataset domain composition (curated multi-domain Pile vs. web-scrape-dominated Dolma) changes which perplexity range is optimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the optimal pruning strategy is dataset-composition-dependent (high-perplexity selection for Pile, medium-perplexity for Dolma, with cross-dataset transfer actually degrading performance) is a practical warning for practitioners who might assume a single heuristic generalizes. The demonstration that upstream test-set perplexity can worsen while downstream accuracy improves corrects a common evaluation shortcut in the data-curation literature. A key limitation is that code and scientific domains are heavily pruned, yet the authors lack the compute to verify coding benchmarks like HumanEval, leaving open whether the technique is safe for code-centric applications. Scaling to frontier-scale models (70B+) and understanding the interaction with domain-specific downstream tasks remain important open questions.
