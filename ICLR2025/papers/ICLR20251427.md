## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) suffer from object hallucination, generating descriptions that include nonexistent objects or misrepresent existing ones, which undermines reliability in safety-critical applications. Existing training-free decoding strategies (VCD, DoLA, OPERA, AGLA) improve performance but fail to fully exploit intrinsic visual information such as sub-image-level attention, and often rely on static masking or external modules that cannot adapt dynamically during token generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251427/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 448×448 if exceeding 672px on any side) and a text prompt (a VQA question or a captioning instruction). The image is dynamically split into N=4 overlapping sub-images of 336×336 pixels each, matching the CLIP-ViT-L-336px encoder resolution used in LLaVA-1.5. Output is a generated text sequence — a short Yes/No answer for POPE-style existence queries or a detailed multi-sentence caption for CHAIR/LLaVA-Bench — produced token-by-token with per-step attention-guided logit ensembling.

## Method

Ensemble Decoding (ED) is a training-free decoding strategy that, at every token generation step t, computes a refined attention map from the LVLM's self-attention (averaging top-K layers and top-H heads), aggregates attention scores over each sub-image's spatial region, and converts them into softmax-normalized attention-guided weights. The final logit distribution is a weighted combination of the original image's logits and the attention-weighted sum of sub-image logits, controlled by a mixing coefficient α. An ED adaptive plausibility constraint then truncates the vocabulary to tokens whose summed sub-image probabilities exceed a β-scaled threshold, preserving fine-grained tokens that single-image constraints would discard. FastED reduces computation from N+1 to 2 forward passes by retaining only the sub-image with the highest attention-guided weight, achieving 2.36× speedup with minimal performance loss.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark, ED achieves an average F1 score of 85.86% and accuracy of 86.31% across Random, Popular, and Adversarial settings, representing an average improvement of 6.57%p in F1 and 6.47%p in accuracy over Regular decoding. On the CHAIR benchmark, ED achieves the highest recall (82.5%) while matching the best CHAIR_s score (43.0) and achieving the lowest CHAIR_l (14.0), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art AGLA by 3.6%p in recall. On MME hallucination subtasks, ED achieves near-perfect accuracy on existence questions and the highest performance on position and color questions, though it struggles with object counting due to image fragmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ED outperforms all four baselines (DoLA, OPERA, VCD, AGLA) on every POPE setting in both F1 score and accuracy. Against the strongest baseline AGLA, ED gains +1.97%p F1 and +1.54%p accuracy on Random, +1.32%p F1 and +0.95%p accuracy on Popular, and +0.54%p F1 and +1.62%p accuracy on Adversarial. On CHAIR, ED ties AGLA on CHAIR_s (43.0) but improves CHAIR_l by 0.1%p (14.0 vs 14.1) and recall by 3.6%p (82.5 vs 78.9), indicating ED generates more complete and accurate captions. DoLA and VCD consistently underperform ED by 3–5%p in F1 across all POPE settings, while OPERA, though competitive on precision, lags ED by 2–3%p in F1. The FastED variant reduces inference time by 2.36× compared to full ED (6.96s vs 16.42s per image) while retaining competitive CHAIR scores, making it suitable for speed-critical deployments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free decoding strategy line for LVLM hallucination mitigation (VCD, DoLA, OPERA, AGLA) by replacing contrastive or penalty-based logit manipulation with attention-guided ensembling over spatially split sub-images. It borrows the top-K-layer / top-H-head attention aggregation from Lee et al. (2023, Volcano) and generalizes the adaptive plausibility constraint from Li et al. (2022) from single-image to multi-image ensembling. The key conceptual departure from the closest competitor AGLA is dynamic per-token attention re-weighting of sub-images versus AGLA's static, one-time masking, and the explicit use of image splitting to increase per-object resolution while reducing irrelevant context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ED offers a module-free, training-free decoding strategy that consistently outperforms prior SOTA across POPE, CHAIR, MME, and LLaVA-Bench, making it a practical drop-in for LVLM deployments where hallucination is critical (autonomous driving, manufacturing). However, it is restricted to patch-wise projector architectures that preserve spatial locality, and the 3× inference latency of full ED (mitigated by FastED) limits real-time applicability. The lack of a theoretical explanation for why attention-guided sub-image ensembling reduces hallucination, and the observed degradation on counting tasks due to object fragmentation, remain open questions for future work.
