## Problem

Existing temporal causal discovery methods (DYNOTEARS, NTS-NOTEARS, TECDI) assume that the contemporaneous (intra-slice) DAG topology remains invariant over time, which fails to capture systems where causal relationships between variables evolve within the same time slice. Prior work on dynamic causal structures has focused exclusively on inter-slice (time-lagged) dynamics, leaving the evolution of intra-slice dependencies unaddressed. DyCAST targets this gap by learning how the intra-slice causal graph changes continuously over time while preserving the directed acyclic property at every time step.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series of shape N×T×d (N independent samples, T time steps, d variables) and produces a continuous trajectory of d×d intra-slice weight matrices W_t (one per time step, each guaranteed to be a DAG) plus a set of static pd×d inter-slice matrices A_k. The intra-slice structure is parameterized through a lower-dimensional latent state of size dr (where r < d is a chosen embedding dimension), making the ODE integration tractable for high-dimensional systems. The decoder optionally concatenates the time index t to the latent state to capture periodic dynamics, and can employ SIREN activations when the causal structure exhibits known periodicity.

## Method

DyCAST formulates the evolution of the intra-slice matrix W_t as a Neural ODE whose solution trajectory must remain on the DAG manifold defined by h(W) = tr(e^(W∘W)) − d = 0. To enforce this constraint without augmented Lagrangian penalties, the vector field is augmented with a stabilization term −γG⁺(W_s)h(W_s), where G⁺ is the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the Jacobian ∇h(W), projecting the dynamics back onto the manifold (inspired by White et al., 2023). For high-dimensional settings, an encoder-processor-decoder architecture is used: the encoder compresses the initial structure W_0 into a latent vector z_0 via S_0 = CONCAT[W_0, W_0^T] followed by a linear-ReLU projection; the processor integrates the constrained latent ODE; and the decoder reconstructs W_t from the time-conditioned latent state. Training minimizes a least-squares reconstruction loss plus L1 sparsity penalties on both W_t and A, solved with Adam, and the entire pipeline requires no hard DAG enforcement beyond the manifold projection.

## Results

On the CausalTime benchmark (3 real-world scenarios: AQI, Traffic, Medical), DyCAST-CUTS+ achieves the best AUROC (0.91/0.65/0.84) and AUPRC (0.93/0.73/0.77) across all subsets, outperforming the strongest baselines LCCM (0.93/0.59/0.76 AUPRC) and CUTS+ (0.80/0.64/0.55 AUPRC). On the NetSim fMRI dataset (17 simulations), DyCAST achieves an average AUPRC of 0.85, surpassing TECDI (0.73), CUTS+ (0.79), and LCCM (0.82). On synthetic dynamic causal structure data (d=5–20), DyCAST consistently attains F1 scores near 1.0 for intra-slice recovery, while NOTEARS-family baselines plateau at 0.67–0.69.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyCAST decisively outperforms all NOTEARS-family baselines (DYNOTEARS, NTS-NOTEARS, TECDI) across every dataset and metric; on CausalTime Traffic AUPRC, DyCAST (0.70) exceeds TECDI (0.63) and NTS-NOTEARS (0.58) by 7–12 points. Against nonlinear inter-slice methods, DyCAST-CUTS+ surpasses CUTS+ (0.80/0.64/0.55 AUPRC) on all three CausalTime subsets, reaching 0.93/0.73/0.77, and also beats LCCM (0.93/0.59/0.76) on Traffic and Medical while matching it on AQI. On NetSim, DyCAST's average AUPRC of 0.85 exceeds LCCM (0.82) and CUTS+ (0.79), and it outperforms TECDI on 82.4% of the 17 simulations. On the DREAM-3 gene regulatory benchmark (d=100), DyCAST-CUTS+ achieves 0.65 AUPRC, edging out CUTS+ (0.64) and substantially surpassing DYNOTEARS (0.50) and NTS-NOTEARS (0.52). The only scenario where a baseline matches or slightly exceeds DyCAST is CausalTime Medical AUPRC, where LCCM (0.76) narrowly beats vanilla DyCAST (0.74), though DyCAST-CUTS+ (0.77) still wins.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyCAST sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) NOTEARS-family continuous optimization for causal structure learning (Zheng et al., 2018; Pamfil et al., 2020), which provides the DAG acyclicity constraint h(W)=tr(e^{W∘W})−d; (2) Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) for modeling continuous temporal dynamics; and (3) constrained differential equations on manifolds (White et al., 2023), which supplies the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse stabilization technique. The key departure from prior work is that while DYNOTEARS and its variants (NTS-NOTEARS, TECDI) assume static intra-slice topology, and dynamic causal methods (TVDBN, KWgL) only model inter-slice evolution, DyCAST is the first to explicitly learn time-varying intra-slice DAGs by integrating a Neural ODE whose trajectory is constrained to the DAG manifold via a stabilization term. The encoder-processor-decoder architecture for the latent ODE follows the relational inductive biases framework (Battaglia et al., 2018) to handle high-dimensional structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: real-world systems like traffic networks, brain connectivity, and physiological signals exhibit evolving instantaneous causal relationships that static DAG models cannot capture. The constrained Neural ODE formulation is a clean technical contribution that avoids the need for augmented Lagrangian or projection-based enforcement of acyclicity, and the latent state extension makes the approach tractable for d up to 300 variables where baselines fail entirely. However, several limitations remain: the inter-slice structure is still assumed static, the SEM is linear (though a CUTS+ extension is provided), and the DAG constraint itself may be inappropriate for some domains (NetSim brain data benefits from removing it). Open questions include unifying intra- and inter-slice dynamics in a single latent ODE, extending to nonlinear intra-slice models, and understanding when the DAG assumption is valid versus when a more general directed graph is needed.
