## Problem

Existing interpretable language models based on concept bottlenecks are restricted to small-scale text classification (≤250 samples) and rely on expensive per-sample LLM queries for concept labeling, making them impractical for large benchmarks or the increasingly important text generation setting. No prior CBM framework scales to datasets with over 500,000 samples or extends to autoregressive generation, leaving a critical gap in building inherently interpretable LLMs that match black-box performance.

## Input / Output

For classification, the model takes a text sample and produces a class label along with top-k concept explanations derived from CBL neuron activations weighted by the final linear layer. For generation, it takes a text prompt and produces autoregressive token sequences, while simultaneously exposing per-token concept neuron activations that enable real-time concept detection and user-driven steering by overriding specific neuron values. A key design choice is that concept labels are computed offline via sentence-embedding similarity (ACS) rather than per-sample LLM queries, and the concept set itself requires only n ChatGPT calls (one per class) to generate.

## Method

CB-LLMs inserts a Concept Bottleneck Layer (CBL) between a pretrained LLM backbone and a prediction head, with k interpretable neurons each tied to a human-readable concept. For classification, a five-step pipeline automates concept generation (ChatGPT), concept scoring (cosine similarity via all-mpnet-base-v2 sentence embeddings), concept correction (zeroing scores for cross-class concepts), CBL training (maximizing cos-cubed similarity to corrected scores), and sparse linear prediction. For generation, the architecture adds a parallel unsupervised layer alongside the CBL to preserve generative capacity, and the key innovation is an adversarial disentanglement module: the unsupervised layer is trained with a negative-entropy loss to erase concept information while a linear classifier is trained to detect concepts from it, forcing the CBL to retain all concept-related signal. This adversarial training is essential for steerability—without it, the unsupervised layer dominates predictions and CBL interventions have negligible effect on generated text.

## Results

For text classification, CB-LLMs with ACC achieves 94.07% on SST2, 98.06% on YelpP, 94.53% on AG News, and 99.28% on DBpedia, matching or exceeding the fine-tuned RoBERTa-base black-box baseline on YelpP and DBpedia while remaining within 0.5–0.6% on SST2 and AG News. For text generation, CB-LLMs (Llama3-8B backbone) achieves concept detection accuracy within 1% of the black-box Llama3-8B (e.g., 96.38% vs. 96.92% on SST2) while providing steerability scores of 0.76–0.95 across datasets—a capability entirely absent in the black-box model. In the toxicity case study, the interpretable chatbot reaches 99.96% toxicity detection accuracy and 0.9137 steerability. Human evaluation shows CB-LLMs w/ ACC earns a 1.5× higher average faithfulness rating (4.12 vs. 2.75) than prior interpretable baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TBM (Ludan et al.) and C³M (Tan et al.), CB-LLMs w/ ACC wins on all four classification datasets by 1.4–4.8% (e.g., 94.07% vs. 92.70% on SST2; 98.06% vs. 95.34% on YelpP), and also achieves a 1.5× higher human-rated faithfulness score (4.12 vs. 2.75). Compared to the RoBERTa-base fine-tuned black-box baseline (Liu et al.), CB-LLMs w/ ACC is within 0.55% on SST2 (94.07% vs. 94.62%) and AG News (94.53% vs. 95.08%) but actually surpasses it on YelpP (98.06% vs. 97.78%) and DBpedia (99.28% vs. 99.17%). Against the GPT-2 fine-tuned black-box (Radford et al., Table 6), CB-LLMs w/ ACC trails by 0.8–1.9% (e.g., 90.72% vs. 91.54% on SST2), a larger gap than with RoBERTa. For generation, CB-LLMs on Llama3-8B (AI@Meta) matches the fine-tuned Llama3-8B black-box within 0.5% on concept accuracy (e.g., 96.38% vs. 96.92% on SST2) and achieves comparable perplexity, while uniquely providing steerability (0.76–0.95) that the black-box model cannot offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) paradigm from Koh et al. (2020) and the label-free CBM line of work (Oikarinen et al., 2023) into the NLP domain at scale, replacing expensive human or LLM-based concept labeling with a sentence-embedding-based Automatic Concept Scoring (ACS) strategy. For the generation setting, it builds on the hybrid CBM architecture from Ismail et al. (2024) but introduces an adversarial training module that forces unsupervised neurons to discard concept-related information, a design absent from prior image-domain hybrid CBMs. The paper departs from the two prior NLP CBM efforts (TBM and C3M) by eliminating per-sample LLM API calls entirely, enabling scaling to 500k+ sample datasets and to autoregressive generation tasks that those works did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CB-LLMs provide the first CBM framework that simultaneously scales to large classification benchmarks and to text generation, offering intrinsic interpretability (concept detection, steering, unlearning) at near-black-box accuracy. The practical applications demonstrated—concept unlearning for fairness and token-level toxicity detection with user-controllable steering—address real safety and trustworthiness gaps in deployed LLMs. Open questions include how the approach generalizes to truly open-ended generation (beyond classification-like topics), whether the adversarial disentanglement remains effective with larger concept sets or more entangled concepts, and whether the slight perplexity degradation in generation (e.g., 116 vs 85 on SST2) becomes problematic for longer or more creative outputs.
