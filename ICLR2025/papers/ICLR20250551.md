## Problem

Existing preference optimization objectives for LLM alignment (DPO, IPO, KTO, SimPO, etc.) require one or more hyperparameters that must be extensively tuned per model, and most also require a frozen reference model, increasing both computational cost and training complexity. The paper asks whether a hyperparameter-free, reference-model-free preference optimization method can match or exceed the performance of these tuned baselines.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard preference dataset of (prompt, chosen response, rejected response) triples and produces a fine-tuned language model policy. Unlike DPO or KTO, it does not require a separate reference model forward pass during training, and unlike SimPO it introduces no tunable parameters (no β, γ, λ, or δ) beyond the standard learning rate. The length normalization is applied as a geometric mean over token probabilities, making the objective invariant to response length without an explicit length penalty term.

## Method

SimPER defines its loss as the negative inverse perplexity of the chosen response plus the inverse perplexity of the rejected response, where inverse perplexity is the geometric mean of per-token probabilities (exp of the length-averaged log-likelihood). This single expression—L = -exp((1/|y_w|) log π_θ(y_w|x)) + exp((1/|y_l|) log π_θ(y_l|x))—requires no reference model and no hyperparameters. Gradient analysis shows the ratio of rejected-to-chosen gradient contributions is constant (unlike DPO/SimPO where it diverges as rejected likelihood → 0), preventing the chosen-response likelihood from collapsing during training. A theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) proves that minimizing SimPER approximates minimizing Total Variation Distance between the chosen-response distribution and the model distribution, which promotes mode-seeking behavior analogous to RLHF, in contrast to the mass-covering behavior of forward KL used by SFT.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2 (length-controlled win rate), SimPER achieves 48.5% on Llama3-8B-Instruct, outperforming SimPO (44.7%) by 3.8 points and DPO (40.3%) by 8.2 points. On the Open LLM Leaderboard (10 benchmarks), SimPER attains the best average rank across all four model settings: 1.3 on Llama3-8B-Base, 1.5 on Mistral-7B-Instruct, 1.7 on Llama3-8B-Instruct, and 2.0 on Mistral-7B-Base, compared to SimPO's 3.4, 3.3, 3.5, and 3.8 respectively. On Llama3-8B-Base, SimPER leads SimPO by 19.5 points on GSM8K (51.02 vs 31.54) and 4.2 points on IFEval (41.78 vs 37.55). SimPER requires zero hyperparameters and no reference model, unlike all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimPER outperforms all six baselines (DPO, SLiC, IPO, KTO, CPO, SimPO) on the majority of benchmarks across all four model settings, achieving the best average rank in every setting. Against SimPO (the closest competitor, also reference-free), SimPER gains 3.8–5.7 points on AlpacaEval 2 LC and leads by 1.4–4.2 points on IFEval; the gap is largest on Llama3-8B-Base GSM8K (51.02 vs 31.54, +19.5). Against DPO, SimPER improves GSM8K by up to 19.5 points (Llama3-Base) and IFEval by up to 8.2 points (Mistral-Base), while DPO requires a reference model and one hyperparameter (β). KTO is the strongest baseline on Mistral-7B-Base (avg rank 2.5) but still trails SimPER (2.0) on IFEval, MMLU-PRO, and GSM8K. SLiC and IPO are consistently outperformed by SimPER on most tasks, with IPO showing the weakest GSM8K scores (22.67 on Llama3-Base vs SimPER's 51.02). CPO is competitive on GSM8K for Mistral-Base (33.06 vs SimPER's 33.02) but lags on IFEval and MMLU-PRO across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of offline preference optimization (DPO, IPO, KTO, CPO, SLiC) and the recent reference-free variant SimPO, but departs from all of them by repurposing perplexity—a standard language-modeling evaluation metric—as the direct optimization objective for alignment. Rather than building on a single prior loss, SimPER carves new ground by showing that the geometric mean of token probabilities (inverse perplexity) is sufficient as a preference signal, eliminating both the reference model and all tunable hyperparameters simultaneously. The theoretical analysis further distinguishes SimPER from DPO/SimPO by connecting its objective to Total Variation Distance (mode-seeking) rather than KL divergence (mass-covering), aligning it conceptually with RLHF's behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: removing hyperparameter search and the reference model reduces both computational cost and the risk of suboptimal tuning, which is especially valuable given that post-training alignment runs are expensive. The consistent outperformance across five base models and twelve benchmarks (including reasoning-heavy tasks like GSM8K and MATH) suggests the gradient-balancing property of TVD optimization is a genuine advantage over KLD-based methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-turn or on-policy settings, whether the TVD connection holds under length-averaging in practice, and whether the simplicity comes at a cost in more nuanced alignment objectives (e.g., multi-dimensional safety constraints).
