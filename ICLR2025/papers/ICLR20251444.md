## Problem

Existing GNNs face a trade-off between local message-passing (prone to underreaching and oversquashing) and global attention (lacking graph inductive bias). While random walk encoding, graph rewiring, and attention mechanisms have each been explored in isolation, their synergistic combination in a single architecture with a graph-tailored attention design remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251444/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GRASS takes a graph with node features, edge features, and an adjacency matrix as input. At each training iteration, the input graph is randomly rewired by superimposing edges from a random regular graph (generated via the Permutation Model), making the model's output inherently non-deterministic. For graph-level tasks, the output is produced by sum-pooling node representations and edge representations separately for original and randomly added edges, concatenated into a single vector; for node-level tasks, per-node representations are used directly.

## Method

GRASS integrates three components: (1) RRWP encoding (or its decomposed variant D-RRWP, which approximates random walk probabilities via truncated eigendecomposition of the symmetric transition matrix in O(km(|V|+|E|)) time) to inject structural information into node and edge features; (2) random rewiring that superimposes a random regular graph (degree r ≤ 6) on the input graph at every epoch to reduce diameter and alleviate underreaching/oversquashing; and (3) a novel additive attention mechanism where edge representations—updated by an MLP aggregating head and tail node features—directly compute attention weights via a linear layer and component-wise softmax, rather than using node features as keys/queries as in standard Transformers. The attention layer alternates edge directions between odd and even layers (edge flipping) to preserve directionality while enabling bidirectional information flow, and applies dropout to attention scores (random edge removal) to reduce dependence on specific edges. The overall architecture achieves O(|V|+|E|) time and space complexity per layer, with sparsity arising naturally from the local attention structure on the rewired graph.

## Results

GRASS achieves state-of-the-art performance on 7 of 9 main benchmark datasets. On ZINC (graph regression), it attains 0.047±0.001 MAE, a 20.3% reduction over the second-best model GRIT (0.059). On CIFAR-10 (graph classification), GRASS reaches 83.75% accuracy, surpassing NeuralWalker (80.03%) and GRIT (76.47%). On the long-range PascalVOC-SP and COCO-SP node classification tasks, GRASS achieves 0.567 and 0.475 Macro F1 respectively, outperforming NeuralWalker (0.491 and 0.440) by a substantial margin. GRASS ranks second on CLUSTER (79.54% vs. GRIT's 80.03%) and fifth on Peptides-func (0.674 AP vs. NeuralWalker's 0.710).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRASS outperforms all classical MPNN baselines (GCN, GIN, GAT, GatedGCN) by large margins across every dataset, e.g., 0.047 vs. 0.367 MAE on ZINC and 83.75% vs. 55.71% on CIFAR-10. Among Graph Transformer baselines, GRASS surpasses SAN (0.139 MAE on ZINC), Graphormer (0.122), and GPS (0.070) on ZINC, and beats Exphormer (98.55% MNIST, 74.69% CIFAR-10) on both classification benchmarks. The closest competitor is GRIT (Ma et al., 2023), which GRASS beats on ZINC (0.047 vs. 0.059 MAE, a 20.3% improvement), MNIST (98.93% vs. 98.11%), CIFAR-10 (83.75% vs. 76.47%), and PATTERN (89.18% vs. 87.20%), but loses narrowly on CLUSTER (79.54% vs. 80.03%). NeuralWalker (Chen et al., 2024) is the strongest baseline on long-range tasks, yet GRASS still leads on PascalVOC-SP (0.567 vs. 0.491 Macro F1) and COCO-SP (0.475 vs. 0.440), while NeuralWalker retains the edge on Peptides-func (0.710 vs. 0.674 AP). Overall, GRASS achieves the best result on 7 of 9 evaluated datasets, with the two exceptions being CLUSTER (by 0.5% over GRIT) and Peptides-func (by 3.6% over NeuralWalker).

## Relation to Prior Work

GRASS synthesizes three previously separate threads in GNN research: RRWP structural encoding (Ma et al., 2023, GRIT), random graph rewiring for long-range communication (Shirzad et al., 2023, Exphormer), and graph attention mechanisms (Veličković et al., 2017; Rampašek et al., 2022, GPS). The paper's conceptual contribution is demonstrating that these components are synergistic rather than merely additive, and it introduces two genuinely new pieces: a decomposed RRWP variant (D-RRWP) that reduces encoding complexity from O(k|V|²) to O(km(|V|+|E|)), and an additive attention mechanism where edge representations (updated via MLP) directly produce attention weights rather than using scaled dot-product over node features. This positions GRASS as a graph-native sparse Transformer that avoids the O(|V|²) attention cost of prior GTs while retaining their global-reach benefits through rewiring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRASS provides a practical, scalable (O(|V|+|E|)) architecture that achieves or matches SOTA across nine diverse benchmarks spanning graph regression, graph classification, and node classification, with a 20.3% MAE reduction on ZINC over the prior best (GRIT). The ablation studies convincingly isolate the contribution of each component, particularly showing that the novel additive attention is critical (≥26% degradation when replaced) and that both regularity and per-epoch resampling of the rewired graph matter. Open questions include scalability to extremely large graphs (beyond the ~500-node LRGB datasets tested), whether the nondeterministic output is acceptable in safety-critical applications, and whether the specific combination and hyperparameters (e.g., r=6, 32 random walk steps) generalize across graph domains without re-tuning.
