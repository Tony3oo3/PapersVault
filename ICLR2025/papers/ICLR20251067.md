## Problem

Existing MLLMs like LLaVA-1.5 and VILA are over-parameterized for many inputs, yet prior MoE approaches require redesigning the architecture and training from scratch. This paper asks whether a well-trained, dense MLLM can be converted into a dynamic mixture of experts without structural retraining, and identifies three obstacles: feature-space disruption from layer skipping, the model's default preference against skipping, and the mismatch between multi-turn conversation training and per-example routing.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (336×336 for LLaVA/VILA, 1024×1024 for LLaVA-HR) and a text question, and produces a free-form text answer. Unusually, the input sequence is augmented with learnable routing tokens inserted before each question-answer pair; these tokens are consumed by a lightweight binary router at every Transformer layer to decide whether to execute the full layer or a low-rank adapter, so the output implicitly includes a per-layer skip/keep decision that varies across examples.

## Method

RoE treats each Transformer layer as an expert and introduces three mechanisms to make dynamic routing work on a frozen, well-trained MLLM. First, a low-rank adapter (x′ = ReLU(xW_d)W_u, c≪d) replaces skipped layers to bridge the feature gap that direct skipping would cause. Second, a structure sparsity regularization L_s = max(t − (1/n)ΣR_i(x_i)_1, 0) is weighted by e^{−|L_t|} so that easier examples are pushed toward sparser paths while harder ones retain full depth. Third, per-question routing tokens are inserted into the multi-turn training sequence so that each question-answer pair learns its own routing path, aligning the parallel training objective with per-example inference. Training proceeds in three stages—adapter warmup (MLLM frozen), router warmup (MLLM frozen, sparsity loss active), and full instruction tuning—requiring only ~333k SFT examples and roughly half the GPU-hours of standard SFT.

## Results

On 5 traditional VL benchmarks, RoE-LLaVA achieves an average accuracy of 63.8% at 6.77 samples/s, improving over the base LLaVA (63.1%, 6.76) on ScienceQA (+1.6) and VizWiz (+2.5) while matching or exceeding it on others. RoE-VILA attains the best combined accuracy-speed trade-off among all methods (64.7% avg accuracy, 7.34 samples/s). Compared to the sparse MoE-LLaVA-2.7B×4 baseline, RoE-LLaVA gains +2.7 on VQAv2 (80.3 vs 77.6) and +1.6 on average accuracy (63.8 vs 60.6) while being 1.61× faster (6.77 vs 4.95 samples/s). On MLLM-specific benchmarks (Table 4), RoE-VILA scores 36.7 on MM-Vet vs 34.3 for MoE-LLaVA-2.7B×4, and RoE-LLaVA-HR reaches 1558.2 on MME vs 1554.9 for LLaVA-HR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dense LLaVA baseline, RoE-LLaVA improves ScienceQA accuracy from 66.8 to 68.4 (+1.6) and VizWiz from 50.0 to 52.5 (+2.5) while maintaining comparable speed (6.77 vs 6.76 samples/s). Against the dense VILA baseline, RoE-VILA slightly drops average accuracy (64.7 vs 65.5) but gains speed (7.34 vs 7.15 samples/s), with the largest drop on VizWiz (53.7 vs 57.8). Against the sparse MoE-LLaVA-2.7B×4, RoE-LLaVA wins on every benchmark: +2.7 on VQAv2, +1.6 on GQA, +8.6 on VizWiz, +1.6 on TextVQA, and +3.2 on average accuracy, while also being 1.61× faster (6.77 vs 4.95 samples/s). Against MoE-LLaVA-1.6B×4, RoE-LLaVA gains +3.6 on VQAv2, +1.1 on GQA, +16.3 on VizWiz, +5.8 on ScienceQA, and +6.7 on TextVQA. Against the dense LLaVA-HR, RoE-LLaVA-HR improves ScienceQA (67.4 vs 65.1) and VQAv2 (80.9 vs 81.9, slightly lower) but drops on TextVQA (64.6 vs 67.1), reflecting LLaVA-HR's sensitivity to layer skipping at higher skip rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoE departs from the standard MoE paradigm (which builds parallel FFN experts from scratch) by treating each layer of an already-trained MLLM as an independent expert and routing between them via adapter-based skip connections. It builds on the adapter architecture from VL-Adapter (Sung et al., 2022) for the lightweight replacement modules, and on the dynamic inference literature (SkipBERT, SmartBERT, MoD) for the concept of layer skipping, but uniquely applies this to well-trained multimodal models without structural redesign. The routing token mechanism and structure sparsity regularization are novel contributions that address the specific challenge of multi-turn conversation training in MLLMs, where prior dynamic models (trained from scratch) do not face the same train-inference misalignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoE offers a practical, low-cost path to accelerating existing MLLMs (only ~94 GPU hours vs. thousands for base training), making dynamic inference accessible without re-architecting or retraining from scratch. The finding that 20–30% of layers can be skipped with minimal or even positive performance impact on several benchmarks suggests substantial inherent redundancy in current MLLMs, which has broader implications for model design. Limitations include sensitivity to skip rate (LLaVA-HR degrades noticeably above ~12% actual skip), evaluation only on 7B-scale models, and the three-stage training pipeline adding complexity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether more aggressive skipping is possible with better adapters, and how RoE interacts with other efficiency techniques like quantization or KV-cache compression.
