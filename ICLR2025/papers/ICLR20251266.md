## Problem

Existing protein generative models handle sequence and structure with separate models (e.g., a structure generator plus ProteinMPNN for inverse folding), preventing them from capturing the intricate joint distribution between the two modalities. This limits performance on tasks requiring simultaneous understanding and generation of both, such as motif-scaffolding and antibody design. The paper asks whether a single multimodal protein foundation model can simultaneously model, understand, and generate both sequences and structures.

## Input / Output

The model consumes amino acid sequences (20 categorical types) and 3D backbone coordinates (N, Cα, C, O per residue) that are first discretized into structure tokens via an LFQ-based tokenizer (codebook size 8192). The two token sequences are concatenated position-by-position with identical position encodings, so z_i and s_i refer to the same residue. Outputs include co-generated structure-sequence pairs, conditional structure (folding), conditional sequence (inverse folding), scaffold structures with sequences (motif-scaffolding), and structure-aware representations for downstream prediction. Training uses proteins up to 512 residues, but the model extrapolates to 1000-residue generation.

## Method

DPLM-2 extends the discrete diffusion protein language model (DPLM) to jointly denoise structure tokens and amino acid tokens under a unified absorbing diffusion framework, with distinct noise schedulers (t_z, t_s) per modality enabling flexible conditional and unconditional sampling. The core enabler is a lookup-free quantization (LFQ) structure tokenizer that converts 3D backbone coordinates into discrete tokens via a frozen GVP-Transformer encoder, a sign-based binary quantizer (avoiding VQ-VAE codebook collapse), and an IPA-based decoder for reconstruction. An efficient warm-up strategy initializes from pre-trained sequence-only DPLM with LoRA adapters to transfer evolutionary inductive bias while limiting catastrophic forgetting on the much smaller structure dataset (~200K vs. 45M sequences). A self-mixup training strategy mitigates exposure bias in discrete diffusion by conditioning predictions on the model's own generated tokens rather than ground-truth, improving both generation quality and diversity. The multimodal training objective jointly optimizes log p_θ(z_i, s_i | z^(t), s^(t)) assuming conditional independence between the two modalities at each position.

## Results

On unconditional structure-sequence co-generation, DPLM-2 (650M) achieves scTM 0.925±0.085 and pLDDT 82.7, approaching native PDB quality (scTM 0.904) and outperforming MultiFlow without distillation (scTM 0.750) and ESM3-Open (scTM 0.624). On forward folding (CAMEO 2022), DPLM-2 (3B) with supervised fine-tuning reaches TMscore 0.85/0.90, approaching ESMFold (0.85/0.93) while far exceeding MultiFlow (0.50/0.46). On inverse folding, DPLM-2 (3B) achieves AAR 52.4/53.7, surpassing ESM3 (47.1/46.2) and MultiFlow (32.3/33.6). On predictive tasks, DPLM-2 shows modest gains over sequence-only DPLM on some tasks (e.g., thermostability Spearman's ρ 0.714 vs 0.695) but remains behind SaProt on most benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MultiFlow, DPLM-2 achieves comparable co-generation scTM (0.925 vs 0.930 with distillation) but higher pLDDT (82.7 vs 79.4); critically, DPLM-2 substantially outperforms MultiFlow without distillation (scTM 0.925 vs 0.750) and MultiFlow retrained on the same data (0.925 vs 0.871), demonstrating superior direct learning from the joint structure-sequence distribution. Against ESM3-Open, DPLM-2 dominates on co-generation (scTM 0.925 vs 0.624) and inverse folding AAR (52.4 vs 47.1 on CAMEO 2022), while ESM3 retains a slight edge on folding TMscore (0.92 vs 0.89 for DPLM-2 650M). On backbone generation, DPLM-2 outperforms RFDiffusion in scTM (0.945 vs 0.914) but has higher scRMSD (4.45 vs 1.97), reflecting DPLM-2's more natural secondary structure distribution. On folding, DPLM-2 (3B + SFT) approaches ESMFold (TMscore 0.90 vs 0.93) while MultiFlow fails catastrophically (TMscore 0.46–0.50). On predictive tasks, DPLM-2 improves over DPLM on thermostability (0.714 vs 0.695) and DeepLoc Binary (93.64 vs 93.09) but trails SaProt on most tasks, which the authors attribute to smaller training data causing partial catastrophic forgetting of sequence pre-training knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPLM-2 extends the discrete diffusion protein language model (DPLM) from sequence-only to a multimodal framework that jointly models amino acid sequences and 3D backbone structures. It draws on Lookup-Free Quantization (LFQ) from visual tokenization to discretize continuous 3D coordinates into structure tokens, and leverages GVP-Transformer and IPA-based modules from AlphaFold2 for encoding/decoding. Conceptually, it departs from MultiFlow (which uses structure-first flow matching and requires external inverse-folding distillation for sequence quality) and ESM3 (which trains from scratch on massive synthetic data with a sequence-first cascaded generation order) by treating both modalities symmetrically within a single discrete diffusion process and bootstrapping from pre-trained evolutionary sequence knowledge via LoRA fine-tuning on a modest 200K-structure dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multimodal protein generation (co-generation, folding, inverse folding, motif-scaffolding) can be achieved with moderate structure data (~200K) by transferring evolutionary inductive biases from large-scale sequence pre-training, offering a more data-efficient and open-source alternative to ESM3's massive from-scratch training. Key limitations include lossy structure tokenization that discards fine-grained atomic details, partial catastrophic forgetting in representation tasks due to smaller training data, and no support for multimers or very long chains. Open questions center on recovering quantization residuals (e.g., via hybrid discrete-continuous generation) and scaling to longer, multi-chain proteins while preserving the data-efficiency advantage.
