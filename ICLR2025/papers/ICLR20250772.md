## Problem

Zero-shot quantization (ZSQ) aims to quantize pre-trained models without access to training data, a critical need when data are unavailable for privacy or security reasons. Existing ZSQ methods that fine-tune quantized models with synthetic datasets suffer from three persistent limitations: high-frequency noise in generated samples that diverges from real image spectra, quantized models that predict based on incorrect image regions rather than the salient areas the pre-trained model uses, and misguidance from erroneous hard labels assigned to difficult samples where the pre-trained model is unreliable.

## Input / Output

SYNQ takes a pre-trained image classification model (ResNet, MobileNetV2, DeiT, or Swin variants) and a target quantization bit-width (e.g., W3A3, W4A4) as input, requiring no real training images. It first generates 5,120 synthetic images via noise optimization guided by batch normalization statistics and inception loss, then applies a Gaussian low-pass filter in the frequency domain to each sample before fine-tuning. The output is the quantized model parameters θ^q, obtained by initializing with Rounding-To-Nearest and fine-tuning for 100 epochs; at inference time the model produces standard class label predictions using dequantized parameters.

## Method

SYNQ introduces three complementary mechanisms into the fine-tuning stage of ZSQ. First, a Gaussian low-pass filter (Eq. 4) is applied in the Fourier domain to each synthetic sample, suppressing high-frequency noise and aligning the amplitude distribution with that of real images. Second, a class activation map (CAM) alignment loss (Eq. 5) minimizes the Frobenius-norm distance between Grad-CAM saliency maps of the pre-trained and quantized models, directly distilling object-localization knowledge so the quantized model attends to the same image regions. Third, a difficulty threshold τ (Eq. 3) gates the cross-entropy loss: for samples where the pre-trained model's confidence is low (difficulty > τ), only the KL divergence soft-label loss is applied, avoiding misguidance from potentially wrong hard labels. The combined objective (Eq. 6) sums KL divergence, conditionally-gated cross-entropy, and CAM alignment losses, and is compatible with any synthetic-dataset-based ZSQ pipeline regardless of model architecture or quantization scheme.

## Results

SYNQ achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot quantization accuracy across all evaluated settings. On ImageNet with ResNet-18 (W3A3), SYNQ reaches 52.02% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous best TexQ (50.28%) by 1.74 percentage points. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20 (W3A3), SYNQ achieves 88.11%, and on CIFAR-100 (W3A3) it reaches 57.28%. The method also extends to Vision Transformers, improving PSAQ-ViT by up to 0.58%p average on ImageNet, and to post-training quantization, improving Genie by 0.66%p average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SYNQ outperforms all 12 compared ZSQ baselines across every reported setting. Against the strongest prior methods TexQ (NeurIPS 2023) and PLF (CVPR 2024), SYNQ gains up to 1.74%p on ResNet-18/ImageNet W3A3 (52.02% vs. 50.28%) and 0.07%p on CIFAR-10 W3A3 (88.11% vs. 88.04% for PLF). Against HAST (CVPR 2023), the improvement is larger at lower bits: +9.44%p on R-18/ImageNet W3A3 (52.02% vs. 42.58%). Generator-based methods (GDFQ, Qimera, AdaSG, AdaDFQ) suffer severe accuracy collapse at 3-bit (e.g., Qimera drops to 1.17% on R-18/ImageNet W3A3), while SYNQ maintains 52.02%. For ViT quantization, SYNQ applied to PSAQ-ViT improves average accuracy by 0.58%p (W4A8) and 0.39%p (W8A8) across DeiT and Swin variants. In the PTQ setting, SYNQ integrated with Genie yields a 0.66%p average gain over Genie alone across W2A2–W4A4.

## Relation to Prior Work

SYNQ extends the established two-step zero-shot quantization pipeline (synthetic dataset generation followed by quantized model fine-tuning) by targeting three specific failure modes of the fine-tuning step that prior methods overlook. It builds directly on TexQ's calibration center synthesis and HAST's difficult sample generation for the data generation step, and on Grad-CAM for the proposed alignment loss. The key departure is treating the fine-tuning phase as a synthesis-aware problem: rather than simply applying standard cross-entropy and KL divergence losses to noisy synthetic samples, SYNQ introduces a frequency-domain low-pass filter, a class activation map alignment objective, and a difficulty-gated soft-label strategy. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-in module compatible with any existing ZSQ baseline, positioning it as an orthogonal improvement rather than a competing pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of high-frequency noise as a universal artifact of noise-optimization-based synthetic data generation is a useful diagnostic that explains why fine-tuning on synthetic samples underperforms relative to real data, and the low-pass filter offers a cheap, model-agnostic remedy. The CAM alignment loss provides a principled mechanism to transfer spatial localization knowledge from the teacher to the quantized student, addressing a failure mode (off-target predictions) that prior distillation losses do not capture. Open questions include whether these techniques transfer to non-classification tasks (object detection, segmentation) and to generative models like diffusion models, which the authors note as future work. The modest but consistent gains (up to 1.74%p over TexQ) suggest the synthetic-data fine-tuning paradigm still has headroom, but the fundamental bottleneck of generating truly realistic samples without real data remains unresolved.
