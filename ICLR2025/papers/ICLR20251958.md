## Problem

The Sliced Wasserstein Barycenter (SWB) is the standard scalable method for averaging probability measures, but no prior work has investigated finding a barycenter that is approximately equidistant from all marginals (a 'marginal fairness' barycenter). The uniform-weighted SWB does not guarantee this fairness due to heterogeneous marginal structures, and the formal constrained formulation is computationally intractable with O(K²) complexity, a sensitive hyperparameter, and biased gradient estimates.

## Input / Output

The method takes K ≥ 2 probability measures (marginals) in R^d — in practice empirical distributions from 3D point clouds (2048 points from ShapeNet), image color palettes (RGB pixel distributions), or class-conditional data distributions (MNIST, CIFAR-10, STL-10) — and produces a barycenter measure that simultaneously minimizes aggregate distance and enforces approximate equidistance to all marginals. The barycenter is parameterized either as a free-supports empirical distribution (n movable atoms) or as the output of a neural encoder in the autoencoder setting, and is optimized via stochastic gradient descent with L random 1D projections per step.

## Method

The paper defines the formal MFSWB as a constrained SWB with a pairwise absolute-difference constraint on SW distances, then proposes three hyperparameter-free surrogates that are O(K) rather than O(K²). The first surrogate (s-MFSWB) minimizes the maximum SW distance to any marginal, motivated by Fair PCA; the second (us-MFSWB) minimizes the expectation over random projections of the maximum 1D Wasserstein distance, yielding an unbiased gradient via Danskin's envelope theorem; the third (es-MFSWB) reweights projections using an energy-based slicing distribution proportional to exp(max_k W_p^p), concentrating mass on marginally unfair directions and estimated via importance sampling. The paper proves the chain SF ≤ USF ≤ ESF and shows each surrogate minimizes a lower bound of the Sliced Multi-marginal Wasserstein distance with the maximal ground metric, establishing it as a generalized metric. All three surrogates are solved with the same O(Ln(log n + d)) per-iteration cost as standard SWB.

## Results

On the MNIST SWAE experiment (Table 2), es-MFSWB achieves the best latent-space fairness (F_latent = 1.760) and centrality (W_latent = 7.268), while us-MFSWB achieves the best image-space fairness (F_images = 6.898). In the 3D point-cloud averaging task (Table 1), es-MFSWB attains the lowest F-metric (0.84) and W-metric (39.18) at 10,000 iterations, outperforming USWB (F=1.55, W=39.81) and the formal MFSWB with λ=1 (F=1.03, W=39.45). Across all four experimental settings (Gaussian barycenter, point-cloud averaging, color harmonization, and SWAE), the three proposed surrogates consistently reduce both the F-metric (marginal fairness) and W-metric (centrality) faster than USWB, with es-MFSWB generally performing best.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to USWB (uniform SWB), all three proposed surrogates (s-MFSWB, us-MFSWB, es-MFSWB) substantially reduce the F-metric (marginal fairness) while maintaining competitive W-metric (centrality). On MNIST SWAE, es-MFSWB reduces F_latent from 5.190 (USWB) to 1.760 and W_latent from 12.448 to 7.268; on point-cloud averaging, es-MFSWB reduces F from 1.55 (USWB) to 0.84 at convergence. Compared to the standard SWAE baseline (no barycenter loss), the proposed methods achieve much better fairness (F_latent drops from 17.661 to ~1.8–2.1) but sacrifice reconstruction quality (RL increases from 3.002 to ~2.8–3.2) and generative quality (W²_image increases from 26.572 to ~27.9–28.3). The formal MFSWB (constrained formulation) is highly sensitive to the Lagrange multiplier λ: with λ=0.1 or λ=10 it performs poorly (F_latent ≈ 17–18.5), while λ=1 gives moderate results, confirming the paper's motivation for hyperparameter-free surrogates. On CIFAR-10 and STL-10 (Appendix D), us-MFSWB and es-MFSWB again outperform USWB on both latent and image-space fairness metrics, with es-MFSWB achieving the best F_latent on STL-10 (0.557 vs. 0.585 for USWB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Sliced Wasserstein Barycenter (SWB) framework of Bonneel et al. (2015) by introducing a marginal fairness constraint that enforces approximately equal distances from the barycenter to all marginals. The first surrogate is directly motivated by the min-max fairness objective in Fair PCA (Samadi et al., 2018), while the third surrogate leverages the energy-based slicing distribution selection approach from Nguyen & Ho (2023). The authors explicitly position their work as the first to study marginal fairness barycenters, distinguishing it from prior fair-learning-with-Wasserstein-barycenter work (Chzhen et al., 2020; Jiang et al., 2020; Hu et al., 2023) that addresses Demographic Parity in one-dimensional settings with response variables rather than multi-dimensional measure averaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal and practical treatment of a barycenter problem where equal treatment of marginals is a design requirement, with applications to shape templates, balanced style mixing, and class-fair generative representations. The key practical contribution is that the three surrogates are hyperparameter-free and scale linearly in the number of marginals (O(K) vs. O(K²) for the formal constrained problem), making them drop-in replacements for standard SWB in existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the marginal fairness barycenter always exists for a given configuration, how the approach behaves under non-global-optimal optimization (the Karcher mean problem), and whether replacing SW with generalized or augmented sliced Wasserstein metrics (as the authors suggest for future work) preserves the fairness guarantees.
