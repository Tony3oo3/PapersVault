## Problem

Standard document retrieval treats documents as unstructured text, ignoring the internal field structure (titles, authors, abstracts, metadata) that real semi-structured documents possess. No existing framework flexibly combines arbitrary numbers of fields with arbitrary scoring methods (lexical, dense, or both) while adaptively weighting fields based on the query, leaving a gap for retrieval over knowledge-graph-derived or metadata-rich documents.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language query and a corpus of semi-structured documents, each decomposed into named fields (5–22 fields depending on the dataset, e.g., title, abstract, authors, brand, entity type). At inference, a top-100 shortlist is retrieved per field and scorer to form a candidate set, over which full weighted scores are computed to produce a final ranked document list. The Contriever encoder's 512-token window constrains per-field sequence length, with field-specific maximum sequence lengths set to cover >99% of documents.

## Method

MFAR decomposes each document into fields and scores the query against every field using both a lexical scorer (BM25) and a dense scorer (finetuned Contriever dot-product), yielding |F|×|M| per-field scores. A lightweight adaptation function G(q,f,m) = softmax({a_f^{mT} q}) produces query-conditioned weights that adaptively emphasize the most relevant field–scorer pairs, where a_f^m are learnable vectors of dimension equal to the query embedding. Training uses a bidirectional contrastive loss (P(d|q) + P(q|d)) with in-batch negatives and a temperature of 0.05, finetuning a shared 110M-parameter Contriever encoder. Per-field batch normalization whitens scores across differently-scaled scorers before the weighted sum, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with a single shared encoder. The key finding is that hybrid per-field scoring (lexical + dense) consistently outperforms either scorer alone, and multi-field decomposition improves dense retrieval over single-field encoding.

## Results

On the STaRK benchmark (Amazon, MAG, Prime), MFAR_All+2 achieves state-of-the-art average performance of 0.496 H@1, 0.710 R@20, and 0.602 MRR, substantially outperforming the strongest prior methods. MFAR_All (multi-field hybrid without single-field representation) reaches 0.437 H@1, 0.662 R@20, and 0.545 MRR on average. The gains are most dramatic on Prime, where MFAR_All achieves 0.409 H@1 versus 0.167 for BM25 and 0.184 for AvaTaR, a +20% relative improvement over the prior SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFAR_All+2 outperforms all four primary baselines across every metric on the STaRK average. Against BM25 (0.374 H@1, 0.561 R@20, 0.462 MRR), MFAR_All+2 gains +12.2 H@1, +14.9 R@20, and +14.0 MRR points. Against Contriever-FT (0.360 H@1, 0.569 R@20, 0.467 MRR), the gains are +13.6 H@1, +14.1 R@20, and +13.5 MRR. Against the agentic AvaTaR method (0.376 H@1, 0.502 R@20, 0.455 MRR), MFAR_All+2 leads by +12.0 H@1, +20.8 R@20, and +14.7 MRR, with the recall gap being particularly large. Against the GPT4 LLM reranker (0.347 H@1, 0.460 R@20, 0.465 MRR), MFAR_All+2 wins by +14.9 H@1, +25.0 R@20, and +13.7 MRR, demonstrating that a small finetuned 110M-parameter encoder with multi-field structure can surpass generalist LLM reranking. Notably, even MFAR_Dense alone (0.411 H@1) already exceeds BM25 and Contriever-FT, providing the first positive evidence that multi-field decomposition benefits dense retrieval specifically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multi-field retrieval (BM25F, Zamani et al. 2018), hybrid lexical-dense retrieval (Gao et al. 2021, Kuzi et al. 2020), and semi-structured document retrieval (Li et al. 2023, Su et al. 2024). Prior multi-field work was predominantly lexical or used early neural models, while prior semi-structured approaches required pretraining to embed structure into the encoder. MFAR departs from both by operating as a post-training framework that decomposes documents into fields, scores each with both lexical and dense scorers, and learns query-conditioned weights via a lightweight adaptation function—requiring no pretraining and using off-the-shelf encoders like Contriever.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MFAR provides the first positive evidence that multi-field decomposition benefits dense retrieval (not just lexical), and demonstrates that hybrid scoring is superior to either modality alone in the multi-field setting. The query-conditioned weighting mechanism is interpretable and controllable at test time, which is valuable for RAG and search applications over structured data. Limitations include reliance on a single encoder family (Contriever, 512-token context), evaluation on one benchmark suite (STaRK), and a weak BM25F comparison (uniform weights only). Open questions include whether the framework scales to more scorers or modalities, how it interacts with larger encoders, and whether the adaptive weighting generalizes beyond the STaRK knowledge-graph-derived documents.
