## Problem

State-of-the-art robust federated learning methods are theoretically optimal, yet their empirical success has unknowingly depended on pre-aggregation gradient clipping with a fixed (static) threshold. Static clipping is fragile: the optimal threshold depends on data heterogeneity and the type of Byzantine attack, and it provably breaks the (f,κ)-robustness property of the underlying aggregation. The paper asks whether a principled adaptive clipping scheme can preserve theoretical guarantees while consistently improving empirical robustness.

## Input / Output

ARC takes as input the n worker gradients (vectors in R^d) and the robustness parameter f (the number of adversarial workers to tolerate), and produces a set of clipped gradients ready for robust aggregation. The clipping threshold is not a fixed hyperparameter but is computed on-the-fly as the Euclidean norm of the (k+1)-th largest gradient, where k = ⌊2(f/n)(n−f)⌋. This means the method requires no a priori knowledge of honest gradient magnitudes or distributions, and its computational overhead is O(nd + n log n), negligible compared to the O(dn²) cost of NNM or Multi-Krum.

## Method

ARC sorts all n worker gradients by norm and clips the k = ⌊2(f/n)(n−f)⌋ largest ones to the norm of the (k+1)-th largest gradient, ensuring fewer than n−f vectors are clipped so that at least one honest gradient always survives unclipped. The key robustness guarantee is that composing any (f,κ)-robust aggregation F with ARC yields an (f, κ + 2f/(n−2f))-robust method, i.e., ARC preserves the original robustness up to a constant factor. Beyond preservation, ARC introduces a 'bounded output' property: the norm of the aggregated result is upper-bounded by the maximum norm among honest gradients, a property that classical (f,κ)-robust aggregations do not satisfy. This boundedness enables a provable improvement over the known lower bound on stationarity error when the model is well-initialized (i.e., honest gradients at θ₁ are small), effectively raising the breakdown point from 1/(2+B²) toward 1/2.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with f=1 adversarial worker among n=17, ARC boosts worst-case maximal accuracy from 41.2% to 67.0% for GM (α=0.2) and from 16.0% to 60.0% (α=0.075), while CWTM improves from 51.6% to 67.8% and from 40.7% to 60.5% respectively. On MNIST under extreme heterogeneity, ARC enables CWTM and GM to reach ~90% worst-case accuracy versus ~10% without clipping, and increases the breakdown point from f=1 to f=3. Under well-initialized conditions (μ=1), ARC boosts worst-case accuracy by approximately 70% over plain Robust-DSGD on MNIST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SOTA Robust-DGD without clipping (Allouah et al., AISTATS 2023), ARC consistently wins across all tested configurations. On CIFAR-10 at α=0.075, GM improves from 16.0% to 60.0% worst-case accuracy (a 44-point gain), and CWTM from 40.7% to 60.5%. On MNIST under extreme heterogeneity, the gap is even more dramatic: without clipping, both CWTM and GM stagnate near 10% accuracy, while ARC lifts them to ~90%. Against standard DSGD (McMahan et al., AISTATS 2017) as the no-attack benchmark, ARC-enhanced Robust-DSGD approaches but does not fully match DSGD: on CIFAR-10 (α=0.2), ARC achieves 67.8% (CWTM) versus DSGD's 76.5%, and on MNIST DSGD reaches 98.5% while ARC-enhanced methods achieve 85–90%+ under extreme heterogeneity. Notably, ARC's advantage over no-clipping Robust-DGD grows with the fraction of adversarial workers and data heterogeneity, while the gap to DSGD narrows under well-initialized models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the (f,κ)-robustness framework and nearest-neighbor mixing (NNM) pre-aggregation established by Allouah et al. (2023a), and on the tight lower bounds and convergence guarantees for Robust-DGD from Allouah et al. (2023b). It departs from prior empirical work (Mhamdi et al. 2021; Farhadkhani et al. 2022) that used static gradient clipping without formal justification, by proving that static clipping actually breaks the (f,κ)-robustness property. Unlike other adaptive clipping schemes (Diakonikolas et al. 2020; Abdalla & Zhivotovskiy 2024) that require a priori knowledge of honest gradient distributions, ARC relies solely on the standard robustness parameter f, making it a minimal and assumption-light extension of the existing Robust-DGD pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARC provides a drop-in, tuning-free replacement for the ad-hoc static clipping that prior SOTA robust federated learning methods unknowingly depended on, with provable preservation of convergence guarantees and a clean theoretical explanation for when it beats the known lower bound (under good model initialization). The paper highlights a significant theory-practice gap in Byzantine ML: worst-case guarantees are far from capturing real-world behavior where models are well-initialized, opening a direction for practically-driven robust learning research. Open questions include formalizing ARC's interaction with differential privacy (where bounded gradients are standard) and whether the initialization-dependent improvement extends to more complex architectures or larger-scale deployments.
