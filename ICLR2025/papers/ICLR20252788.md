## Problem

Policy gradient methods are the standard tool for training RL policies with Gaussian or mixture parameterizations, yet they were conjectured to be inefficient for diffusion-based policies due to the multi-step denoising process inflating the effective horizon and action variance. This paper challenges that conjecture by showing that, with a careful formulation and set of design choices, policy gradient fine-tuning of pre-trained Diffusion Policies achieves the strongest overall performance and training stability across continuous control and robot manipulation benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252788/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy receives either a low-dimensional robot state vector or an RGB image (encoded via a Vision Transformer) as conditioning, and outputs an action chunk of T_a consecutive actions (typically 4–8 steps) sampled through the iterative denoising process. During pre-training, the diffusion policy is trained via behavior cloning on expert demonstration trajectories; during fine-tuning, it interacts with the environment online, receiving sparse or dense rewards upon task completion. The denoising process itself is treated as a sequence of K (or K′) stochastic steps, each producing a Gaussian-distributed intermediate action that is refined toward the final action.

## Method

DPPO formulates the diffusion denoising process as an inner MDP embedded within the environment MDP, creating a two-layer 'Diffusion Policy MDP' where each denoising step has an analytically evaluable Gaussian likelihood, making policy gradient directly applicable without approximating the intractable marginal action likelihood. The method instantiates PPO on this two-layer MDP with a denoising-discounted advantage estimator (γ_DENOISE^k scaling) and a value function that depends only on the environment state rather than the denoised action, which the authors show is critical for training stability in high-stochasticity settings. Key practical design choices include fine-tuning only the last K′ denoising steps (freezing earlier steps), clipping the noise schedule to a higher minimum (σ_min^exp) to encourage structured on-manifold exploration, and optionally using DDIM sampling for efficiency. The authors demonstrate that this formulation yields structured exploration that stays near the expert data manifold while covering it broadly, greater robustness to action noise and dynamics perturbations, and strong sim-to-real transfer performance.

## Results

DPPO achieves >90% success on the challenging bimanual Transport task (state input) and >50% (pixel input), the first RL method to solve Transport from either modality. On FURNITURE-BENCH, DPPO improves zero-shot sim-to-real performance to 80% (16/20 trials) on the One-leg task, while the Gaussian-MLP baseline achieves 0% on hardware despite 88% in simulation. Across all six FURNITURE-BENCH settings (3 tasks × 2 randomness levels), DPPO maintains strong performance while Gaussian-MLP collapses to 0% success in all three Med-randomness tasks and Round-table Low. On GYM locomotion tasks, DPPO is competitive with the best diffusion-RL baselines (IDQL, DIPO) while exhibiting superior training stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against diffusion-based RL methods (DQL, IDQL, DIPO, QSM, DRWR, DAWR), DPPO is the strongest performer on all four ROBOMIMIC tasks, with the largest margin on Transport where off-policy Q-learning baselines suffer training instability from biased Q-gradient propagation in sparse-reward, high-dimensional action-chunk settings; on GYM locomotion, IDQL and DIPO are competitive but DPPO trains more stably. Against demo-augmented off-policy methods (RLPD, Cal-QL, IBRL), DPPO achieves much better final performance on sparse-reward manipulation (Can, Square) where RLPD and Cal-QL fail to learn at all and IBRL saturates at lower success levels, though these off-policy methods are more sample-efficient on HalfCheetah-v2. Against alternative policy parameterizations (Gaussian, GMM with MLP or Transformer backbones), DPPO outperforms on the challenging Square and Transport tasks, reaching >90% on Transport (state) where Gaussian does not improve from its 0% pre-trained rate, and is the first method to solve Transport from pixel input (>50% vs. Gaussian's 0%). On FURNITURE-BENCH, DPPO improves all six settings while Gaussian-MLP collapses to 0% in all Med-randomness tasks; in zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, DPPO achieves 80% hardware success versus 0% for Gaussian (and only 50% for Gaussian with an auxiliary BC regularization loss). Against policy gradient using the exact diffusion likelihood (solving the probability-flow ODE), DPPO outperforms on Can (where exact-likelihood PG drops to 0%) and offers the practical advantage of fine-tuning fewer denoising steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'denoising process as MDP' formalism introduced by Black et al. (2023) and Psenka et al. (2023) by embedding the diffusion denoising MDP into the environmental MDP, creating a two-layer 'Diffusion Policy MDP' amenable to PPO updates. It directly contravenes the conjecture in Psenka et al. (2023) and Yang et al. (2023) that policy gradient would be inefficient for diffusion policies due to the increased effective horizon from denoising steps, showing instead that with specific design choices (denoising-discounted advantages, environment-state-only value functions, and noise schedule clipping), PG fine-tuning of pre-trained diffusion policies outperforms Q-learning-based alternatives. The framework builds on Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2024b) as the base parameterization and PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) as the optimizer, positioned within the broader pre-training-plus-fine-tuning pipeline now standard in robot learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that policy gradient is not only viable but superior to Q-learning methods for fine-tuning diffusion policies in challenging manipulation tasks, this work removes a perceived barrier to adopting the most stable and widely-used RL optimizer for the most expressive policy parameterization in robotics. The zero-shot sim-to-real transfer result (80% hardware success vs. 0% for Gaussian) suggests the structured exploration and robustness properties of diffusion policies translate to real-world deployment. Key open questions include the lower sample efficiency compared to off-policy methods (limiting practical hardware RL), whether the approach scales to even longer-horizon or multi-task settings, and how DPPO integrates with model-based planning or video-prediction-based decision-making tools.
