## Problem

LLM-generated synthetic data often deviates from real-world distributions, and standard cross-entropy training on such data can perform worse than training on much smaller real-world datasets. Existing remedies either discard potentially useful samples through filtering or rely on computationally expensive meta-learning to adjust per-sample weights. The paper asks whether one can efficiently reweight synthetic data to align with the real-world distribution using only a tiny reference set of real data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an LLM-generated synthetic dataset of text-label pairs (D_Q) and a small real-world reference set of roughly 200–400 labeled examples (D_P′). It outputs a fine-tuned BERT-based text classifier whose training loss is modulated by per-sample weights computed as a ratio of a quality-checker score (a BERT model trained on D_P′) to a diversity-checker score (either a BERT model trained on D_Q for IMP-Loss, or the model's own current predictions for DIMP-Loss). The quality checker's scores are precomputed once and remain fixed throughout training, while DIMP-Loss's diversity checker updates dynamically at every optimization step.

## Method

The paper introduces two weighted cross-entropy objectives that transform training from the synthetic distribution Q toward the real-world distribution P. IMP-Loss assigns each sample the importance-sampling weight P′(y|x)/Q(y|x), where P′ and Q are estimated by fitting separate BERT models on the small real-world set and the synthetic set, respectively, yielding asymptotic convergence to the expected cross-entropy under P. DIMP-Loss replaces the static Q(y|x) denominator with the current model's prediction P̂(y|x;θ_t), making the weight dynamic at each step and eliminating the need for a second trained model. Both losses decompose into a quality checker (up-weighting data more representative of the real-world distribution) and a diversity checker (up-weighting data that is less redundant or harder for the current model). DIMP-Loss is further shown to be a lower bound on a regularized empirical distilled cross-entropy loss with a maximum-entropy term, and it requires only ~11% more wall-clock time than standard CE-Loss, compared to ~120% for IMP-Loss and ~700% for the meta-learning baseline SunGen.

## Results

On LLM-generated (GPT-3.5) data, DIMP-Loss achieves the best accuracy across all three benchmarks: 82.67% on Financial, 78.44% on Tweet Irony, and 75.83% on MRPC. IMP-Loss is competitive, reaching 82.09% on Financial and 81.89% on Tweet Irony (the highest F1 of 81.71% on Tweet Irony). Both methods surpass the GPT-3.5 few-shot predictor itself (79.46% on Financial, 63.39% on Tweet Irony, 69.28% on MRPC) using a much smaller BERT-base model. On large real-world data, DIMP-Loss also leads with 85.4% Acc on Financial and 82.84% Acc on MRPC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Both IMP-Loss and DIMP-Loss outperform all four baselines (CE-Loss, Focal-Loss, Hu et al., SunGen) on every benchmark when training on GPT-3.5-generated data. The largest gains are over CE-Loss: +5.28% Acc on Financial (82.67 vs. 77.39) and +3.92% Acc on MRPC (75.83 vs. 72.00). Against the strongest baseline SunGen, DIMP-Loss improves by +2.22% Acc on Financial (82.67 vs. 80.45) and +4.18% Acc on MRPC (75.83 vs. 71.65), while IMP-Loss gains +1.64% on Financial and +4.18% on MRPC. Hu et al.'s meta-learning method performs worst overall (e.g., 71.70% on Financial, 67.13% on MRPC), confirming that bilevel optimization is both less effective and less scalable. Notably, both proposed methods also surpass the GPT-3.5 few-shot predictor's own accuracy on all three benchmarks, demonstrating that a small fine-tuned BERT model guided by quality/diversity weighting can exceed the capability of the LLM that generated the training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical importance sampling weight ratio P(y|x)/Q(y|x) (Hesterberg, 1995) as a per-sample loss weight to align LLM-generated data distribution Q with real-world distribution P, and borrows the Bayesian data selection approximation from Deng et al. (2023) to derive a dynamic variant (DIMP-Loss) that uses the training model itself as the diversity checker. It departs from meta-learning-based data weighting (Hu et al., 2019; SunGen/Gao et al., 2023) by replacing bilevel optimization with a closed-form weight computed from two auxiliary models, and from filtering strategies by retaining all synthetic data while down-weighting low-quality or redundant points. The knowledge-distillation interpretation of DIMP-Loss (Eq. 10) further connects the method to the distillation literature, framing the small real-world model as a 'teacher' whose predictions regularize the student trained on synthetic data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a ~10% overhead over CE-Loss (DIMP-Loss) can match or beat meta-learning baselines that require ~8× the compute, making distribution-aware data weighting feasible at scale. The empirical finding that standard CE-Loss on >3k LLM-generated samples can underperform CE-Loss on ~200–400 real samples (Financial, MRPC) underscores that naive synthetic-data scaling is unreliable and motivates the weighting approach. Limitations include evaluation only on BERT-base/large for three text-classification benchmarks, reliance on the assumption Q(x)≈P(x) (i.e., the LLM's input distribution matches the real one), and the quality checker being trained on the same dev set used for model selection. Open questions the authors flag include extension to QA, text generation, and LLM pretraining, and whether the approach generalizes to larger model families or multimodal settings.
