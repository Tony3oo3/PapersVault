## Problem

The de-facto standard for LLM pretraining is cosine decay to 10% of peak LR, yet no prior work has comprehensively compared LR schedules when each is tuned to its own optimal peak learning rate. This paper asks which schedule truly minimizes loss for a fixed training budget and provides a theoretical explanation for why the answer is linear decay-to-zero (D2Z).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250799/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Experiments train GPT-like transformers (111M–1.7B parameters, ALiBi, SwiGLU) on text corpora (primarily SlimPajama, up to 137B tokens) using AdamW, sweeping peak LRs by 2× factors around a μP proxy-tuned base value for each schedule independently. The output is validation loss on 1.1B held-out tokens plus downstream benchmarks (MMLU, commonsense reasoning, reading comprehension); the headline result is that a 610M model at 80 TPP with D2Z matches a 200 TPP 10× model, a 60% compute saving.

## Method

The paper's core theoretical contribution is a novel interpretation of AdamW as a convex combination of prior weight updates, where the LR schedule and weight decay jointly determine the combination coefficients (the 'dual' of the schedule). Linear D2Z produces a favorable flattening of these coefficients: the high early LR ensures rapid movement from initialization (bias reduction), while the gradual linear decrease toward zero progressively expands the effective averaging window over updates, reducing gradient noise (variance reduction). This dual perspective explains why abrupt drops (step decay) or faster-than-linear decay (cosine D2Z) underperform: they prematurely reduce the weight of later, valuable updates. Empirically, across hundreds of runs spanning model sizes, batch sizes, datasets, and vocabularies, Linear-D2Z consistently achieves the lowest loss at compute-optimal 20 TPP and increasingly dominates at higher TPP, with optimal peak LRs far more stable than for 10× or Constant schedules.

## Results

For 610M-parameter μP models trained to compute-optimal 20 TPP, the optimal Linear-D2Z schedule achieves 0.77% lower validation loss than the optimal Linear-10× schedule (2.573 vs. 2.591 at the proxy-tuned peak LR). The gap widens substantially with training duration: at 200 TPP, D2Z exceeds the best 10× setting by 1.6%, and a 610M model trained for only 80 TPP with D2Z matches the validation loss of the same model trained for 200 TPP with 10× decay, yielding a 60% compute saving. At 1.7B scale and 80 TPP, D2Z achieves roughly 3.05% lower loss than 10× at the proxy-tuned LR, and the scaling-law slope for D2Z is ~2.5% steeper than for 10×, projecting a ~2% loss improvement at 70B scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Chinchilla-standard Cosine 10× decay (Hoffmann et al., 2022), Linear-D2Z wins by 0.77% at 20 TPP and by 1.6–2.8% at 200 TPP; the advantage grows monotonically with TPP across all model sizes tested (111M, 610M, 1.7B). Against WSD (Hägele et al., 2024), D2Z is ~0.84% better at 80 TPP, and WSD's optimal peak LR shifts lower with training duration, confirming it is not truly schedule-free. Against the Cyclic schedule (Smith, 2017), D2Z also wins at 80 TPP (Figure 8). Against InvSqrt (Vaswani et al., 2017), D2Z is consistently 0.09–0.19 loss points better across all peak LRs in Table 1. The paper's findings are consistent with Defazio et al. (2023), who showed linear decay equals or outperforms cosine across problems; this paper extends that result by showing the specific combination of linear decay *to zero* (rather than to 10%) is what drives the advantage, and that the benefit scales with dataset size and model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EMA interpretation of AdamW from Wang & Aitchison (2024) to time-varying learning rate schedules, showing that the LR schedule holistically determines the convex combination of weight updates rather than just the instantaneous smoothing parameter. It builds on the bias-variance trade-off framework of Andriushchenko et al. (2023) to argue that D2Z optimally balances early-training bias reduction against late-training variance reduction. The work departs from the de-facto 10× cosine standard (Chinchilla, Llama, GPT-3) and from Defazio et al. (2023), who found linear decay superior but did not sweep peak LRs or decay ratios, and from 'schedule-free' approaches like WSD, which the paper shows are not truly schedule-free from the dual EMA perspective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that linear D2Z saves ~60% compute for over-trained models (80 TPP D2Z beats 200 TPP 10×) is practically significant for the growing trend of training smaller models on more tokens for efficient inference. The paper also reveals that D2Z makes optimal peak LR far more stable across batch size, weight decay, and dataset size changes, improving hyperparameter transfer under μP and exposing LR decay ratio as a confounder in prior scaling studies. Open questions include how to jointly tune weight decay, batch size, and LR at very high TPP, whether the proposed 'Rational' schedule works for continuous pre-training, and whether findings hold for models with unstable training dynamics that cannot tolerate the higher peak LRs D2Z requires.
