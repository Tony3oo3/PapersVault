## Problem

Training LLMs with large mini-batches improves convergence but is memory-prohibitive, and the natural remedy—selecting smaller mini-batch coresets that match the gradient of larger batches—fails for LLMs due to three compounding issues: highly imbalanced data sources cause small sources to be underrepresented in coresets, the Adam optimizer requires matching normalized rather than vanilla gradients, and the extreme gradient dimensionality renders pairwise distances vacuous for medoid selection.

## Input / Output

At each training step, CoLM consumes a large random mini-batch (e.g., 128 examples) drawn from a data mixture with known source labels (or clusters discovered by embedding hidden states), along with the current LoRA-adapted LLM parameters and running Adam statistics. It outputs a smaller coreset (e.g., 64 examples) that is then used for the actual gradient update. The method requires only one additional forward pass per step to compute zeroth-order gradient estimates of the last V-projection, and the sparsified gradient retains as few as 0.7% of the LoRA last-layer dimensions (h=2560 for Phi-2).

## Method

CoLM addresses the three LLM-specific challenges with three coupled mechanisms. First, for imbalanced sources, it provably shows that gradient-matching coresets w.h.p. omit representative examples of small sources, so it retains all small-source examples in the coreset and applies submodular facility-location medoid selection only to big sources, with uniform weights across all selected examples. Second, for Adam compatibility, it normalizes per-example gradients by their historical exponential averages (m̂, v̂) computed exclusively from big-source gradients, so medoids are found in the Adam-scaled gradient space rather than vanilla gradient space. Third, to handle the prohibitive gradient dimensionality, it uses SPSA (simultaneous perturbation stochastic approximation) to estimate the gradient of the last LoRA V-projection matrix in a single forward pass, then sparsifies the normalized gradient by a source-specific mask keeping only the top-h dimensions with the largest magnitude, and computes pairwise dissimilarity via ℓ1 distance on the sparse vectors. The full procedure is provably variance-reducing relative to random mini-batches of the same size and stacks directly with LoRA and gradient accumulation.

## Results

On MathInstruct (fine-tuning Phi-2 with LoRA, 1K iterations), CoLM with batch size 64 (selected from 128) achieves 56.6% average accuracy across in-domain (GSM8K, MATH, NumGLUE) and out-of-domain (SVAMP, Mathematics, SimulEq) benchmarks, outperforming fine-tuning with 4× larger batch size (bs=256, 55.3%) while using 45% less memory and being 2.7× faster. On the SuperGLUE benchmark (SST-2, CB, MultiRC), CoLM with clustering of the fine-tuned model reaches 81.0% average accuracy, surpassing standard fine-tuning with bs=128 (78.9%) by 2.1%. CoLM also matches the performance of fine-tuning for 2K iterations with bs=128 (56.5%) using only half the training steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLM (bs=64) outperforms all five adapted one-shot selection baselines on MathInstruct: Middle Perplexity (MP, Marion et al.) is the strongest at 53.0% average, followed by Facility Location on hidden states (FL, Bhatt et al.) at 52.5%, Least Confidence (LC) at 46.7%, Grad Norm (GN, Katharopoulos & Fleuret) at 45.3%, Big Loss (BL, Jiang et al.) at 42.7%, and Completion Length (CL, Zhao et al.) at 34.6%. CoLM's 56.6% average exceeds the best baseline (MP) by 3.6% and the weakest (CL) by 22.0%. Notably, CoLM also surpasses standard fine-tuning with 4× larger batch size (FT bs=256, 55.3%) by 1.3% while consuming 45% less GPU memory and training 2.7× faster. On SuperGLUE, CoLM with fine-tuned-model clustering (81.0%) beats FT(bs=128) (78.9%) by 2.1% and FT(bs=64) (74.2%) by 6.8%, confirming gains beyond the MathInstruct setting. The ablation in Table 2 shows that retaining all small-source examples contributes the largest single improvement (~3%), with Adam-normalized gradient matching and per-source selection adding another ~2% combined.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mini-batch coreset framework from Yang et al. (2023) and Mirzasoleiman et al. (2020), which uses submodular facility location to find gradient-matching medoids, from balanced image classification with SGD to the LLM fine-tuning setting. It identifies three fundamental obstacles that make the prior approach infeasible for LLMs—highly imbalanced data sources, the Adam optimizer's per-dimension scaling, and the extreme dimensionality of LLM gradients—and addresses each with targeted modifications: retaining all small-source examples, normalizing gradients by Adam's historical terms, and using SPSA zeroth-order estimates with source-wise sparsification of the last V-projection. The zeroth-order gradient estimation builds on MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023), while the overall method is orthogonal to and stackable with LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) and other memory-efficient training techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CoLM reduces fine-tuning memory by 2x while outperforming training with 4x larger random mini-batches, and it composes with LoRA to further reduce memory—addressing a core bottleneck for practitioners training LLMs on limited hardware. The theoretical guarantee that small sources must be retained entirely (rather than subsampled) provides a clear, actionable principle for handling imbalanced instruction-tuning data. Limitations include that the main results are on LoRA fine-tuning (not full fine-tuning), the pre-training validation is on a 60M-parameter model, and the method adds a forward-pass overhead for zeroth-order gradient estimation. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to full-parameter training, how it interacts with other optimizer choices beyond Adam, and whether the sparsification strategy generalizes to architectures without a clear V-projection structure.
