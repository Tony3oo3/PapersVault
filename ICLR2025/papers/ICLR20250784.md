## Problem

Prior work measured the multimodal degree of VL encoders with MM-SHAP and the self-consistency of LLM decoders with CC-SHAP, but no analysis existed for autoregressive VLM decoders on either dimension. The paper asks whether VLMs rely differently on image versus text when generating answers compared to explanations, and whether their natural-language self-explanations are self-consistent with the input tokens that actually drove the answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250784/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes RGB images (tiled into patches, with patch count adjusted to match text sequence length for fair modality comparison) and text inputs (questions, captions, or foils) as input to 7B-parameter VLM decoders. For each output token in the generated sequence, Shapley values are computed for all input tokens, then normalized into contribution ratios in [-1,1] and averaged across output tokens to yield per-token aggregate contributions. These are aggregated modality-wise into T-SHAP/V-SHAP proportions, and the answer-versus-explanation contribution distributions are compared via cosine distance to produce CC-SHAP scores in [-1,1].

## Method

The core contribution is extending MM-SHAP from the encoder setting (single prediction) to autoregressive decoders (multi-token generation). For a decoder producing T output tokens, each input token j receives T Shapley values φ_j^1…φ_j^T; these are normalized into contribution ratios r_j^t = φ_j^t / Σ_i|φ_i^t| to handle varying magnitudes across output positions, then averaged over t to obtain a single aggregate contribution per input token. Absolute contributions are summed per modality to compute T-SHAP and V-SHAP. For self-consistency, CC-SHAP computes the cosine distance between the contribution distributions C(A) (answer generation) and C(E) (explanation generation), yielding a continuous score where 1 indicates perfect alignment and negative values indicate misalignment. The paper also extends six edit-based faithfulness tests (Counterfactual Edits, Biasing Features, and four Corrupting CoT variants) to the multimodal decoder setting, and benchmarks four 7B VLMs (BakLLaVA, LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral, LLaVA-NeXT-Vicuna, mPLUG-Owl3) on VALSE, VQA, GQA, and MSCOCO in both post-hoc and CoT explanation settings.

## Results

On the VALSE benchmark, mPLUG-Owl3 achieves the best average pairwise accuracy (acc_r) of 88%±6, followed by LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral (85%±9), BakLLaVA (83%±8), and LLaVA-NeXT-Vicuna (79%±10). On the harder image-sentence alignment accuracy (acc), all models except mPLUG-Owl3 (76%±10) perform near the 50% random baseline. For multimodal degree (MM-SHAP), all four VL decoders are heavily text-centric during answer generation, with T-SHAP values of 85–97% on VQA and GQA. For self-consistency (CC-SHAP), BakLLaVA, LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral, and LLaVA-NeXT-Vicuna show negative scores on VALSE multiple-choice (−0.02 to −0.07), while mPLUG-Owl3 achieves positive scores (+0.11 to +0.12), indicating it is the only model with convergent answer-explanation behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against VL encoder results from the original VALSE benchmark (Parcalabescu et al., 2022). On the easier pairwise accuracy metric (acc_r), 2024 VL decoders outperform 2019–2021 VL encoders, with mPLUG-Owl3 reaching 88% versus the best encoder results. However, on the more challenging alignment accuracy (acc), decoders do not generally outperform encoders — most models score near the 50% random baseline, suggesting they exploit linguistic priors rather than true image-text alignment. For self-consistency, the paper compares VLM decoders against LLMs evaluated by Parcalabescu & Frank (2024): most tested VLMs (BakLLaVA, LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral, LLaVA-NeXT-Vicuna) show negative CC-SHAP on VALSE, indicating they are less self-consistent than LLMs, while mPLUG-Owl3 is the exception with positive CC-SHAP. The key finding across all comparisons is that image contributions increase by 1–30 percentage points during explanation generation versus answer generation, with the gap being larger in CoT than post-hoc settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two measurement frameworks from the same research group to a new class of models: MM-SHAP (Parcalabescu & Frank, 2023), originally designed for VL encoders, is adapted to autoregressive VLM decoders by normalizing and aggregating per-token Shapley values across output sequences; CC-SHAP (Parcalabescu & Frank, 2024), originally for LLM self-consistency, is applied in a multimodal context. The paper also extends edit-based faithfulness tests (Counterfactual Edits, Biasing Features, Corrupting CoT) from unimodal to multimodal settings, and benchmarks VLM decoders on VALSE, a benchmark previously restricted to VL encoders. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of interpretability (Shapley-based attribution) and explanation faithfulness, filling a gap where decoder VLMs had received no systematic multimodal-degree or self-consistency analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all tested VLM decoders are heavily text-centric (85–97% T-SHAP) while VL encoders are more balanced raises important questions about whether decoder architectures, training data, or task-level linguistic biases drive this asymmetry — a question the authors flag as open. The observation that image contributions increase significantly during explanation generation (by 1–30 percentage points, more in CoT) suggests a conceptual inconsistency: models answer using text priors but 'look at the image' when explaining, undermining the trustworthiness of VLM self-explanations. Limitations include evaluation of only four 7B-parameter models, high compute costs for CC-SHAP (~25 min/sample), and the absence of ground-truth faithfulness annotations for explanations. Future work on bias-free datasets and internal mechanism analysis could clarify whether the text-centricity is architectural or data-driven.
