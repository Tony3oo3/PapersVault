## Problem

Despite the promise of surface-based representations for protein analysis, no direct and fair benchmark comparison exists between the best available surface encoders and alternative representations (graphs, grids) under a standardized protocol. Moreover, prior efforts to combine surface and graph information rely on global early or late fusion, leaving unexplored whether node-level, layer-wise feature sharing between the two representations can yield complementary gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251423/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes protein 3D structures (atomic coordinates) and protein sequences, from which it derives a coarsened molecular surface via MSMS and a residue-level graph enriched with ESM-650M sequence embeddings. A bipartite graph is constructed by connecting each surface vertex to its 16 nearest graph nodes, with edge features encoding direction, distance, and angle relative to the surface normal. Outputs are task-specific: per-residue interaction probabilities (PIP), binary mutation-stability labels (MSP), per-structure quality scores (PSR), ligand-binding class predictions, and antibody/antigen binding-site residue labels.

## Method

AtomSurf integrates a DiffusionNet-based surface encoder and a graph encoder (GCN for the benchmark variant, ProNet for the full model) through a bipartite graph that links surface vertices to their 16 nearest graph nodes. At every layer, message passing over this bipartite graph—using separate parameter sets for surface-to-graph and graph-to-surface directions—enables node-wise feature exchange across all learned layers, rather than the global pooling used in prior hybrid approaches. The DiffusionNet component is adapted for protein scales by adding batch normalization, batch support, and initializing learned diffusion times from N(10, 5) to match the ~10 Å spatial scale of binding sites, resolving instabilities from the original shape-independent time parameters. The bipartite message-passing layer uses Graph Attention (GAT) with geometric edge features, and the full AtomSurf model (600k parameters) combines the residue graph with ProNet's equivariant conformational features and ESM embeddings, achieving state-of-the-art results across all Atom3D tasks and binding-site benchmarks.

## Results

On the Atom3D benchmark, AtomSurf (600k params) achieves 90.9 AuROC on PIP, 71.6 AuROC on MSP, and 61.7/85.7 (R_l/R_g) on PSR, setting new state-of-the-art on PIP and MSP while closing the gap on PSR. The lighter AtomSurf-bench variant (200k params, benchmark protocol) reaches 87.6 PIP AuROC and 70.7 MSP AuROC, outperforming all single-representation baselines. On the MaSIF-ligand task, AtomSurf achieves 0.88 balanced accuracy vs. 0.81 for the previous SOTA (HMR). On the large-scale PINDER dataset, AtomSurf reaches 92.8 AuROC on Pinder-Pair (Holo) vs. 80.1 for ProNet, with consistent 10+ point gains across all splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AtomSurf outperforms ProNet (2M params) by 3.8 AuROC points on PIP (90.9 vs. 87.1) and by 1.0 on PSR R_g (85.7 vs. 84.9), while using less than one-third the parameters. Against GVP (11M params), AtomSurf leads by 4.3 on PIP, 3.6 on MSP, and 10.6 on PSR R_l. Even against GVP with pretraining, AtomSurf wins on PIP (90.9 vs. 87.4) and PSR R_l (61.7 vs. 51.5) without any pretraining itself. On the MaSIF-ligand task, AtomSurf (0.88) surpasses the previous SOTA HMR (0.81) and ProNet (0.75). On PINDER, AtomSurf dominates ProNet by 12.7 AuROC on Pinder-Pair Holo (92.8 vs. 80.1) and by 14.0 on Pinder-Site Holo (88.3 vs. 74.3), with similar margins on Apo and AF2 splits. The fair-benchmark comparison (Table 2) shows that pure surface learning (Surface Diff) underperforms graph and 3D-CNN baselines, confirming that the hybrid integration is essential for the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts DiffusionNet (Sharp et al., 2022), a state-of-the-art spectral surface encoder from the geometry processing literature, to protein structure analysis, addressing instabilities and scale-dependence issues that arise when moving from near-isometric shapes to proteins of diverse sizes. It positions itself as the first fair benchmark comparison of surface-based learning against graphs and grids within the Atom3D protocol (Townshend et al., 2020), which had previously excluded surface methods. The hybrid architecture departs from prior multi-representation efforts (Somnath et al., 2021; Pegoraro et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024) that performed only global early or late fusion between surface and graph encoders, instead enabling node-wise feature sharing across all layers via a bipartite graph constructed from spatial proximity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a state-of-the-art surface encoder underperforms even on interaction tasks challenges the long-held assumption that surface representations are inherently well-suited for protein interaction prediction, providing important calibration for the field. The hybrid approach's SOTA results across seven diverse tasks (with fewer parameters than competitors) demonstrate that complementary representations with localized cross-communication are a powerful strategy for structural bioinformatics. Key limitations include significant memory requirements from surface operations and the reliance on ESM embeddings for smaller datasets; open directions include reducing the memory footprint of surface networks, exploring information sharing across tasks, and extending to generative modeling.
