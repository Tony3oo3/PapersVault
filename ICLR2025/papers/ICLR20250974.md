## Problem

Knowledge distillation and weak-to-strong generalization are widely used in practice, yet the statistical properties of training a target model on surrogate-generated labels in the high-dimensional overparameterized regime remain poorly understood. Prior analyses either provide loose bounds, restrict to isotropic covariance, or do not characterize the optimal surrogate model. This paper asks: what is the precise excess risk of a ridgeless target model trained on surrogate labels, what form should the optimal surrogate take, and can it provably outperform ground-truth labels under the same data budget?

## Input / Output

The framework takes feature vectors x ~ N(0, Σ_t) from a target distribution and synthetic labels y^s = x^T β^s + z generated by a surrogate model, operating in the proportional regime where p, n, and m all grow together with fixed ratios κ_t = p/n > 1 and κ_s = p/m > 1. The output is a closed-form asymptotic risk expression decomposed into bias and variance terms, the optimal surrogate parameter β^{s*} (which per-feature amplifies or shrinks the ground-truth coefficients depending on the covariance spectrum), and an optimal 0-1 feature mask M* for the weak-to-strong setting. A key structural detail is that the optimal surrogate is fully determined by the covariance statistics ζ_i = τ_t/(λ_i + τ_t) and the scalar Ω, making it a data-dependent, feature-wise transformation of β_★.

## Method

The core technique applies the non-asymptotic distributional characterization of the minimum ℓ₂-norm interpolator (via the convex Gaussian min-max theorem) to the two-stage ERM problem, yielding a fixed-point equation for the target model's test risk as a function of the surrogate parameter β^s. By jointly minimizing the bias term (which favors β^s = θ_1^{-1}β_★) and the variance term (which favors β^s = 0), they derive a closed-form optimal surrogate β^{s*} whose per-feature gain β_i^{s*}/β_{i,★} transitions from amplification to shrinkage at the threshold 1−ζ_i = Ω. For weak-to-strong generalization, they show that restricting β^s to a 0-1 mask on β_★ yields an optimal feature selection rule: keep feature i iff 1−ζ_i² > Ω, which is a strictly larger set than the amplification region. Under power-law covariance (λ_i = i^{−α}), they prove the optimal surrogate shifts the cut-off indices linearly with n (making the surrogate sparser with less data) and that while the risk is strictly improved, the scaling law exponent Θ(n^{−(β−1)}) or Θ(n^{−2α}) remains unchanged relative to the standard target model.

## Results

The paper provides a sharp non-asymptotic risk characterization for ridgeless high-dimensional regression under knowledge distillation, showing that the optimal surrogate model β^{s*} exhibits a phase transition from amplification (gain > 1) to shrinkage (gain < 1) as one moves from principal to tail eigendirections, with the transition point at ζ_i = 1 − Ω. Under a power-law covariance (λ_i = i^{-α}) and signal (λ_i β_i² = i^{-β}), the optimal surrogate-to-target risk scales as Θ(n^{-(β-1)}) for β < 2α+1 and Θ(n^{-2α}) for β > 2α+1, matching the standard target model's scaling exponent exactly despite a strict constant-factor improvement. On CIFAR-10, fine-tuning a ResNet-50 with predictions from weak 3-layer CNN surrogates consistently outperforms the surrogate models themselves across all capacities (big, medium, small), but does not surpass the standard target model trained on ground-truth labels, consistent with the theoretical prediction that the surrogate cannot follow the optimal feature-selection mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the surrogate-to-target model (trained on synthetic labels from a surrogate) and the standard target model (trained on ground-truth labels). In the overparameterized regime (p > n) with non-isotropic covariance, the optimal surrogate strictly improves the test risk over the standard target model, as shown by Corollary 1 and Proposition 1; however, this improvement vanishes when the covariance is a scalar multiple of the identity or in the underparameterized regime (n > p, Proposition 2). The surrogate-to-target model also outperforms the surrogate model itself on CIFAR-10 across all model sizes, confirming that distillation adds value beyond the weak teacher's own accuracy. Critically, the optimal surrogate does not alter the scaling law exponent compared to the standard target model (Proposition 6), meaning the gain is a constant-factor improvement rather than a change in the power-law rate. The paper also establishes equivalence with the covariance shift model of Mallinar et al. (2024) via Observation 1, showing that model shift and covariate shift are dual formulations, and extends their upper/lower bounds to a precise non-asymptotic risk characterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the non-asymptotic distributional characterization of ridgeless interpolators by Han & Xu (2023), which itself relies on the convex Gaussian min-max theorem (Thrampoulidis et al., 2015), to analyze a two-stage ERM process where the first stage produces a surrogate model whose predictions serve as labels for the second stage. It extends the covariance shift analysis of Mallinar et al. (2024) by showing that model shift in distillation is equivalent to covariate shift, but goes further by optimizing over the surrogate parameter to obtain a closed-form optimal teacher. In the distillation and W2S literature, prior works (Mobahi et al., 2020; Menon et al., 2020; Nagarajan et al., 2023) either considered low-dimensional settings or provided loose bounds, while the closest scaling-law work (Jain et al., 2024) was restricted to isotropic covariance; this paper handles general power-law covariance in the overparameterized proportional regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clear fundamental limit: while an optimally crafted weak teacher can strictly reduce test risk over ground-truth labels in the overparameterized regime, it cannot improve the exponent of the data scaling law. This sharpens the understanding of weak-to-strong generalization by identifying a concrete feature-selection mechanism (the 0-1 mask at the ζ² threshold) that explains when and why a weaker model's labels help a stronger student. Key limitations include the restriction to linear models with Gaussian features in the proportional regime, and the CIFAR-10 experiments confirm that practical surrogate models (shallow CNNs) do not follow the optimal feature-selection prescription, so the theoretical gain does not materialize in the neural network setting. Open questions include extending to multi-stage distillation, data pruning, and non-linear architectures where non-asymptotic ERM characterizations remain unavailable.
