## Problem

Classic GNN architectures are provably incapable of distinguishing between symmetric variables in ILPs, because the permutation equivariance and invariance properties inherent to GNNs interact with the formulation symmetry of the problem to force identical outputs for all variables in the same orbit. No prior work had provided a theoretical characterization of this failure mode or developed a symmetry-aware feature augmentation scheme specifically tailored to ILP structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes an ILP instance encoded as a bipartite graph (variable nodes with objective coefficients, constraint nodes with RHS values, edges with constraint coefficients) plus pre-computed symmetry group orbits (detected via Bliss), and produces a continuous prediction vector over the n decision variables that is rounded to an integer solution. Augmented features are appended as an extra row to the bipartite matrix, with values sampled per-orbit from a discrete uniform distribution, so the GNN sees a (m+2)×(n+1) matrix rather than the standard (m+1)×(n+1).

## Method

The paper first proves (Proposition 1) that under permutation equivariance/invariance, any GNN must assign identical predictions to all variables in the same symmetry orbit, making correct solution prediction impossible for symmetric ILPs. To break this, the authors propose three guiding principles for augmented features—distinguishability (distinct values within each orbit), augmentation parsimony (minimize the cardinality of the feature space), and isomorphic consistency (isomorphic inputs must map to isomorphic labels)—and then design an orbit-based scheme that samples distinct integers from {1,…,|O_k|} without replacement for each nontrivial orbit and assigns zeros to trivial orbits. An enhanced variant (Orbit+) further exploits structural couplings between orbits of equal cardinality to share sampled features across coupled orbits, reducing the feature-space cardinality from the product of all orbit-factorials to a strictly smaller product. Training uses a standard 4-layer GNN with half-layer graph convolutions plus a 2-layer MLP head, supervised on 8 random augmented samples per instance, with the SymILO framework applied to resolve isomorphic-consistency violations in the labels.

## Results

The paper evaluates feature augmentation schemes using the Top-m% error metric (lower is better) on three symmetric ILP benchmarks. On the largest dataset SMSP (23K variables), Orbit+ achieves a 90% Top-m% error of 50.3, compared to 113.9 (No-Aug), 80.0 (Uniform), and 79.8 (Position). On BIP (1,083 variables), Orbit+ reaches 79.2 at 90% versus 91.1, 80.2, and 81.6 for the baselines. Additionally, Orbit+ converges in approximately 20 epochs versus 30–40 epochs for Uniform and Position, demonstrating improved training efficiency alongside better prediction accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two existing augmentation baselines: Uniform (random noise from a continuous uniform distribution, from Chen et al. 2022) and Position (unique integer positional IDs, from Han et al. 2023). Orbit and Orbit+ consistently outperform both baselines across all three datasets. On SMSP at 90%, Orbit reduces the error from 80.0 (Uniform) and 79.8 (Position) to 51.1, while Orbit+ further improves to 50.3 — a roughly 37% reduction over the best baseline. On BIP at 50%, Orbit+ achieves 5.5 versus 15.9 (Uniform) and 13.4 (Position), a more than 2× improvement. On BPP, all augmentation methods perform well at lower m% thresholds, but Orbit/Orbit+ maintain their advantage at 90% (4.3 vs. 6.4 and 5.6). The gap between Position and Orbit is larger than between Uniform and Position, indicating that symmetry-aware construction (exploiting orbit structure) provides a more substantial gain than simply reducing the feature space cardinality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts orbit-based symmetry-breaking ideas from equivariant neural networks in chemistry and physics (Xie & Smidt, 2024; Morris et al., 2024) to the specific setting of ILP solving with GNNs, departing from prior ILP-focused approaches that used generic random features (Chen et al., 2022) or positional embeddings (Han et al., 2023) without exploiting the underlying symmetry group structure. It builds on the SymILO framework (Chen et al., 2024) for handling label consistency under symmetry, and provides the first formal proof connecting formulation symmetry with GNN permutation equivariance/invariance to explain the indistinguishability failure. The three guiding principles (distinguishability, augmentation parsimony, isomorphic consistency) synthesize and formalize ideas scattered across the equivariant-networks literature into a principled design framework specific to ILPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since roughly 32% of MIPLIB instances exhibit symmetry, a principled way to handle this in GNN-based ILP solvers has practical relevance for operations research and combinatorial optimization. The three guiding principles are general enough to inform feature-augmentation design beyond ILPs, and the orbit-based scheme offers a drop-in improvement for existing GNN-ILP pipelines. However, the approach is limited to formulation symmetry (variable permutations preserving the constraint matrix structure), and the isomorphic consistency principle only applies to supervised settings with multiple valid labels, leaving open whether the framework extends to other symmetry types or unsupervised/RL-based ILP solving.
