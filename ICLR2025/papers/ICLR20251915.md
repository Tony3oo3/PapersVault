## Problem

Li et al. (2016) provided the first provable guarantees for alternating minimization on weighted low rank approximation, but their algorithm requires solving O(n) exact linear regressions per iteration, yielding a runtime of O(||W||₀k² + nk³)·log(1/ε) and a non-robust analysis that cannot tolerate any approximation error. This paper asks whether the alternating updates can be computed approximately in nearly linear time while preserving the same convergence rate, bridging the gap between theory and practical implementations that inevitably use inexact solvers.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a noisy n×n matrix M = M* + N, a non-negative weight matrix W ∈ ℝ≥0^{n×n}, a target rank k, and an accuracy parameter ε. It outputs a rank-k matrix M̃ = X̂_T Y_{T-1}^⊤ satisfying ||M̃ − M*|| ≤ O(α⁻¹kτ)·||W∘N|| + ε in spectral norm, where τ is the condition number of M*. The algorithm only accesses the weighted entries W∘M and exploits the sparsity of W (||W||₀ nonzero entries) to achieve near-linear runtime in the number of observed entries. Random Rademacher initialization is used rather than SVD initialization, which would dominate the runtime at O(n³).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a high-accuracy weighted multiple response regression solver (Algorithm 2) that uses an SRHT sketch as a preconditioner: it computes the QR decomposition of SA to obtain a preconditioner R, initializes via a constant-factor approximate solution, then runs O(log(1/ε)) iterations of preconditioned gradient descent, each in Õ(nd) time. This achieves a log(1/ε₀) dependence on the regression accuracy, which is critical because the backward error conversion (Lemma 4.2) requires ε₀ to be polynomially small in σ_min(A) and the optimal cost. The robustness analysis (Lemma 4.5) shows that if the approximate regression solution is within Δ_u of the exact solution in Frobenius norm, then the subspace distance dist(U, X) and generalized incoherence ρ(X) remain bounded, enabling an inductive convergence argument. Exploiting the sparsity of W reduces each regression to size O(||W_i||₀k) rather than O(nk), yielding the overall runtime Õ((||W||₀·k + nk³)·log(1/ε)), improving the k² dependence to k.

## Results

The main theorem (Theorem 4.6) establishes that the proposed algorithm runs in $\widetilde{O}((\|W\|_0 \cdot k + nk^3)\log(1/\epsilon))$ time and outputs a rank-$k$ matrix $\widetilde{M}$ satisfying $\|\widetilde{M} - M^*\| \leq O(\alpha^{-1} k\tau)\|W \circ N\| + \epsilon$ in spectral norm. This improves the runtime of Li et al. (2016) from $O((\|W\|_0 k^2 + nk^3)\log(1/\epsilon))$ by removing a factor of $k$ in the dominant term. For the concrete regime $k = O(\sqrt{n})$ with dense $W$ ($\|W\|_0 = O(n^2)$), the runtime drops from $O(n^3\log(1/\epsilon))$ to $\widetilde{O}(n^{2.5}\log(1/\epsilon))$. In the noisy matrix completion setting where $\|W\|_0 = \widetilde{O}(nk^{2+o(1)})$, the overall runtime is $\widetilde{O}(nk^{3+o(1)}\log(1/\epsilon))$, nearly matching the state-of-the-art of Kelner et al. (2023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Li et al. (2016), the primary baseline for weighted low rank approximation, this paper achieves a strict runtime improvement by a factor of $k$ in the $\|W\|_0$-dependent term (from $\|W\|_0 k^2$ to $\|W\|_0 k$), while also providing a robust analysis that tolerates approximate regression solves at each step—something Li et al.'s framework cannot handle. Against Gu et al. (2024b), which addresses the strictly simpler noiseless matrix completion problem and assumes direct access to entries of the ground truth $M^*$, this paper is a strict generalization: it handles noisy observations ($M = M^* + N$), provides a spectral norm guarantee (stronger than Gu et al.'s Frobenius norm), and works for general weighted low rank approximation, not just Boolean weights. The error term $O(k\tau)\|W\circ N\|$ vanishes when $N=0$, recovering the noiseless case. Against Yalcin et al. (2022) and Zhang et al. (2024), which focus on characterizing the nonconvex optimization landscape and proposing complexity metrics rather than designing algorithms, this paper provides concrete algorithmic guarantees; the authors note it would be interesting to study whether alternating minimization can be verified against those landscape metrics. No empirical baselines are run, as the contribution is entirely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the alternating minimization framework of Li et al. (2016) for weighted low rank approximation by replacing their exact regression solves with fast, sketching-based approximate ones. The high-accuracy regression solver (Algorithm 2) is directly adapted from the preconditioned gradient descent approach of Rokhlin & Tygert (2008) and the dense SRHT-based solver of Gu et al. (2024b), which itself targets the simpler noiseless matrix completion problem. The key departure is a robust analysis showing that polynomially large per-step errors in the regression solves do not degrade the overall convergence rate, combined with exploiting the sparsity of the weight matrix W to reduce the per-iteration cost from O(||W||₀k²) to Õ(||W||₀k).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first theoretical justification for why alternating minimization with approximate updates (as used universally in practice due to floating-point arithmetic and efficiency constraints) still converges at the same rate, closing a gap between theory and practice. The framework is general enough to apply to any alternating minimization scheme requiring multiple response regressions, with potential extensions to robust PCA and multi-view learning noted by the authors. Open questions include whether SVD initialization can be accelerated to maintain the nearly-linear runtime, and whether the incoherence and spectral-gap assumptions can be weakened. The runtime improvement is most impactful in the matrix completion regime where ||W||₀ = Õ(nk^{2+o(1)}), yielding a near-verification-time algorithm.
