## Problem

Existing conservative contextual bandit algorithms (Wu et al., 2016; Kazerouni et al., 2017; Garcelon et al., 2020) rely on UCB-style exploration and are limited to multi-armed or linear cost functions, while practical costs are often non-linear. The natural Neural UCB extension is infeasible because its regret bound depends on the effective dimension and is Ω(T) in the worst case. This paper fills the gap by providing the first conservative contextual bandit algorithms with sub-linear regret guarantees for general non-linear cost functions.

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm receives K adversarially chosen context vectors (bounded norm ≤ 1) and a known baseline action with its expected cost, along with a safety parameter α > 0 controlling conservatism. The output is a binary decision per round: either play the IGW-sampled arm (when a safety inequality involving the regression oracle's predictions and a confidence term is satisfied) or fall back to the baseline arm. The regret guarantee is expressed in terms of the online regression oracle's regret rather than problem-specific quantities, and the performance constraint (cumulative cost ≤ (1+α) × baseline cumulative cost at all times) holds with high probability.

## Method

The core mechanism reduces conservative contextual bandits to online regression via Inverse Gap Weighting (IGW): the regression oracle predicts costs for all K arms, an IGW distribution is constructed from prediction gaps relative to the best-predicted arm, and a candidate action is sampled. A safety condition checks whether the predicted cumulative cost (including a confidence term scaling as √(m·Reg) where m is the number of IGW rounds played) stays within (1+α) times the baseline cumulative cost; if violated, the algorithm conservatively plays the baseline. The key analytical challenge is bounding the number of baseline plays n_T, which requires a time-dependent exploration parameter γ_t = √(K|S_t|/Reg) that simultaneously controls IGW regret and limits baseline fallbacks. C-SquareCB uses a squared-loss oracle yielding Õ(√(KT) + K/α) regret with neural OGD, while C-FastCB uses a KL-loss oracle with an episodic γ_t schedule (doubling a threshold on cumulative optimal loss) to achieve first-order Õ(√(KL*·log L*) + K log T) regret. The neural instantiation uses a feed-forward network with Rademacher perturbation and an ensemble of S = O(log T) networks updated by projected OGD.

## Results

On openml multiclass classification datasets (averaged over 10 runs), both C-SquareCB and C-FastCB exhibit consistently sub-linear cumulative regret, outperforming the C-LinUCB baseline across all tested environments. C-FastCB achieves lower regret than C-SquareCB on several datasets, attributable to its first-order regret guarantee that scales with the optimal policy's cumulative loss L* rather than the horizon T. In terms of safety, both conservative algorithms maintain the (1+α) performance constraint with near-zero violation rates, while their vanilla (non-conservative) counterparts violate the constraint in a significant percentage of runs (Figure 2, averaged over 100 runs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is C-LinUCB (Kazerouni et al., 2017), a conservative linear UCB algorithm that uses least-squares regression and high-probability confidence bounds for its safety condition. C-SquareCB and C-FastCB both consistently outperform C-LinUCB in cumulative regret across all openml datasets, with C-FastCB showing the largest gains on datasets where the optimal policy's cumulative loss L* is substantially smaller than T. The key advantage is that C-LinUCB is restricted to linear cost functions, while the proposed methods handle general non-linear functions via neural network function approximation. On the safety dimension (Figure 2), both proposed algorithms maintain the performance constraint with negligible violation rates, whereas their non-conservative versions (without the safety check) violate the constraint in a meaningful fraction of runs, confirming that the safety mechanism is both necessary and effective. No comparison with other non-linear conservative bandit baselines is provided, as no such methods exist in the literature at the time of writing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conservative contextual bandit formulation (Wu et al. 2016; Kazerouni et al. 2017; Garcelon et al. 2020) from multi-armed and linear settings to general non-linear cost functions by replacing the UCB-based exploration mechanism with Inverse Gap Weighting (IGW) and an online regression oracle, following the reduction framework of Foster & Rakhlin (2020) and Foster & Krishnamurthy (2021). The neural network instantiation closely follows Deb et al. (2024a), which showed that prior Neural UCB bounds are Ω(T) in the worst case, motivating the oracle-based approach. The key technical departure from both the conservative bandit literature (which relies on confidence sets) and the standard IGW literature (which has no safety constraint) is the careful coupling of a time-dependent exploration parameter with a safety condition that bounds the number of baseline actions played.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide sub-linear and first-order regret guarantees for conservative contextual bandits with general non-linear cost functions, addressing a critical gap for deploying bandit algorithms in safety-sensitive real-world settings where performance must not degrade beyond a (1+α) factor of a deployed baseline. The first-order bound (scaling with L* rather than T) is particularly relevant when the optimal policy incurs low cumulative cost. Open questions include extending to stage-wise safety constraints, the more general reinforcement learning framework, and relaxing the NTK positive-definiteness and known-baseline-cost assumptions. The empirical validation is limited to small openml classification datasets with a single baseline (C-LinUCB), leaving open whether the theoretical advantages translate to large-scale practical deployments.
