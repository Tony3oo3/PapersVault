## Problem

Deep learning models achieve state-of-the-art time series classification accuracy but are opaque black boxes, while interpretable models (e.g., shapelet-based or neuro-symbolic classifiers) provide human-understandable rules but sacrifice performance due to limited expressivity. The paper addresses the gap of achieving both: interpretable, rule-based predictions on samples where shape-level features suffice, and high accuracy on harder samples that require additional representational capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251789/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series of shape M×T (M variables, T timestamps) and produces a class label along with a rule-based explanation identifying which learned shapelets (per channel and length) are present or absent and their importance weights for the predicted class. A per-sample gating value η(x_i) determines whether the final prediction is fully interpretable (routed through the shapelet bottleneck) or relies on the DNN expert. Shapelets are learned end-to-end at multiple lengths per channel, and the linear classifier weights directly encode which shapelets matter for each class, enabling both local (per-sample) and global (per-class) explanations.

## Method

InterpGN is a Mixture-of-Experts framework combining a Shapelet Bottleneck Model (SBM) as the interpretable expert with a Fully Convolutional Network as the DNN expert. The SBM introduces a novel variant of the Shapelet Transform that builds logical predicates via a Gaussian RBF on shapelet distances (rather than threshold-based predicates), feeding them into a linear classifier whose weights yield rule-like explanations. The key architectural insight is a confidence-based gating function η(x_i) — a modified Gini index over the SBM's softmax output — that routes high-confidence samples to the interpretable expert and low-confidence (boundary) samples to the DNN, with the interpretable expert itself serving as the gating network to reduce parameters. Training is end-to-end with a hybrid loss L_hybrid = β·L_int + L_ce, where L_int includes cross-entropy, shapelet diversity regularization, and L1 sparsity on classifier weights to encourage compact, interpretable rules. During inference, samples with η(x_i) above a threshold are classified solely by the SBM, guaranteeing interpretability for the majority of predictions.

## Results

On the 30-dataset UEA multivariate time series classification archive, InterpGN achieves the best average accuracy of 76.0% and the best average rank of 3.50, winning top-1 on 8 datasets and top-3 on 16 datasets. The interpretable-only SBM component achieves 72.6% average accuracy, competitive with deep baselines like FCN (74.6%) and ShapeConv (74.3%). On the MIMIC-III in-hospital mortality prediction task, InterpGN reaches 70.3% accuracy and 70.3% ROC-AUC, outperforming FCN (69.3%/69.8%) and SBM alone (65.8%/65.8%). Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm statistical significance (p < 0.01) over all baselines except SVP-T (p=0.066) and RLPAM (p=0.112).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterpGN (76.0% avg. accuracy) outperforms all 11 baselines on the UEA archive, with the largest margins over classical methods STRF (64.5%) and DTW (65.0%), and moderate gains over the strongest deep baselines FCN (74.6%) and ShapeConv (74.3%). The closest competitors are RLPAM (74.0%) and SVP-T (73.0%), though InterpGN's advantage over these is not statistically significant (p=0.112 and p=0.066 respectively). The interpretable-only SBM (72.6%) trails InterpGN by 3.4 points, confirming the DNN expert's value on difficult boundary samples. On MIMIC-III, InterpGN (70.3% accuracy, 78.4% precision) surpasses FCN (69.3%, 73.4%) and SBM alone (65.8%, 65.9%), demonstrating that the gating mechanism recovers performance lost by the interpretable expert on challenging clinical samples. Notably, InterpGN achieves top-1 accuracy on 8 of 30 datasets (vs. 7 for RLPAM and 5 for SVP-T) while simultaneously providing rule-based shapelet explanations for the majority of predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three established lines of work: shapelet-based time series modeling (Lines et al. 2012; Grabocka et al. 2014), Concept Bottleneck Models (Koh et al. 2020), and Mixture-of-Experts architectures (Shazeer et al. 2017). The key conceptual departure is replacing threshold-based logical predicates (as in Mueen et al. 2011 and neuro-symbolic approaches like Yan et al. 2022) with RBF-kernel predicates that directly measure shapelet existence, and using the interpretable expert's own prediction confidence as the gating signal rather than a separate gating network. This positions InterpGN as a practical middle ground between purely interpretable models (which sacrifice performance) and black-box deep models (which sacrifice trustworthiness).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent tension in applied ML: interpretable models for time series (ECG, EEG, clinical signals) are often too weak to match deep learning performance, while deep models lack the transparency needed in safety-critical domains like healthcare. By demonstrating that a confidence-gated mixture can match SOTA accuracy (0.760 avg on UEA) while providing rule-based explanations on MIMIC-III, the work offers a practical template for deploying interpretable TS classifiers in clinical settings. Open questions include whether the two-expert design scales to more complex multi-expert hierarchies, whether the RBF predicate advantage holds for very long sequences (where memory overhead is significant), and whether the observed counterfactual explanations in binary classification tasks can be resolved with a unified single-rule design.
