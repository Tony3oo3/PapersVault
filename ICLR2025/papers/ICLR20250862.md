## Problem

Arithmetic circuits, which encode symbolic knowledge in neurosymbolic AI as differentiable computation graphs of sums and products, exhibit high irregular sparsity that makes them poorly suited to dense tensor operations on GPUs. Existing frameworks either evaluate circuits naively on the CPU or require custom hardware-specific kernels, preventing neurosymbolic models from scaling to larger real-world applications on standard accelerators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250862/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

KLAY takes as input a set of Bernoulli probabilities (predicted by a neural network) for the Boolean variables at the circuit leaves, along with a pre-compiled d-DNNF arithmetic circuit that has been layerized and tensorized into per-layer index vectors S_l and scatter-reduce vectors R_l. The circuit is compiled once (a #P-hard knowledge compilation step) and then re-evaluated many times with different leaf probabilities, amortizing the compilation cost. Output is the satisfaction probability of the circuit, computed in the logarithmic domain (using logsumexp and sum reductions) for numerical stability, with the full computation graph remaining end-to-end differentiable for gradient-based training.

## Method

KLAY restructures a d-DNNF circuit into a layered representation where nodes are grouped by height, and unary 'pass-through' nodes are inserted so that every node's children reside in the immediately preceding layer. A Merkle-hash-based deduplication step (proven linear in the number of edges) merges equivalent sub-circuits across multi-rooted circuits and within single circuits, often reducing total node count below the original SDD. The key tensorization insight is that each layer transition can be expressed as a single index operation (selecting edge values via S_l) followed by a scatter-reduce operation (aggregating by R_l with sum for ∨-layers or product for ∧-layers), both of which are highly optimized primitives in PyTorch and Jax. This reduces the entire circuit evaluation to a sequence of embarrassingly parallel index-and-scatter operations, eliminating the need for custom CUDA kernels or specialized hardware while dramatically outperforming both naive node-by-node traversal and existing GPU implementations like JUICE.

## Results

On the Warcraft(12×12) neurosymbolic benchmark, KLAY on GPU achieves a forward+backward pass time of 1.59 ms, compared to 18,931 ms for the naive PyTorch baseline — a speedup of approximately 11,900× (four orders of magnitude). On the MNIST-addition task with 3 digits, DeepProbLog times out after 2 hours while KLAY on GPU completes one epoch in 8.85 s. On synthetic SDD circuits spanning 5 orders of magnitude in size, KLAY on GPU outperforms all baselines by over one order of magnitude on the largest instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the naive node-by-node PyTorch traversal (the standard in DeepProbLog and Pylon), KLAY on GPU wins by 1–4 orders of magnitude across all four neurosymbolic tasks, with the largest gap on Warcraft (18,931 ms → 1.59 ms). Against JUICE (Dang et al., 2021), which uses custom CUDA kernels for circuit layerization, KLAY on GPU is faster on all synthetic SDD benchmarks, and the paper notes that JUICE's GPU implementation is actually slower than its own CPU implementation, indicating missed parallelization. On the MNIST-addition integration experiment, KLAY replaces the SDD circuit inside DeepProbLog and achieves a 2-order-of-magnitude speedup (1.26 s vs. 241.63 s per epoch for 1 digit), while also supporting batched inference that DeepProbLog cannot. Compared to Scallop (Li et al., 2023), which approximates via top-k provenance, KLAY remains exact yet still faster: for 3-digit addition, KLAY on GPU runs in 8.85 s per epoch while Scallop on CPU takes 5,346 s, and DeepProbLog times out entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

KLAY builds directly on the arithmetic circuit framework (Darwiche, 2003) that underpins neurosymbolic systems like DeepProbLog and Pylon, which encode symbolic knowledge as d-DNNF circuits evaluated node-by-node. The key conceptual departure is from prior GPU acceleration attempts: JUICE (Dang et al., 2021) used custom CUDA kernels, Shah et al. (2020, 2021) designed dedicated hardware, and Vasimuddin et al. (2018) targeted multi-core CPUs. KLAY instead reduces circuit evaluation to a sequence of index and scatter-reduce operations—primitives already highly optimized in PyTorch and Jax—making it hardware-agnostic and leveraging existing compiler stacks rather than requiring bespoke implementations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By refuting the widely-held belief that arithmetic circuits are too irregularly sparse for GPU acceleration, KLAY removes a major bottleneck that has kept neurosymbolic models from scaling to larger real-world problems. The four-orders-of-magnitude speedup on the Warcraft benchmark and the ability to run batched inference via multi-rooted circuits open the door to neurosymbolic tasks previously infeasible within reasonable training times. Open questions include how the approach behaves for highly unbalanced circuits (where unary-node overhead grows, as seen with D4-compiled circuits), whether the scatter-reduce formulation remains efficient at extreme circuit sizes, and how it integrates with emerging probabilistic circuit architectures that have denser sum nodes.
