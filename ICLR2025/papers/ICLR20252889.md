## Problem

Equivariant networks are fundamentally unable to break symmetries: by Curie's Principle, a self-symmetric input must yield a self-symmetric output, making tasks like predicting dichlorobenzene from benzene or decoding from symmetric latent spaces impossible. This paper addresses the gap by showing that equivariant conditional distributions (rather than equivariant functions) can represent any symmetry-breaking mapping, and provides a practical, theoretically grounded framework for implementing this in existing equivariant architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard equivariant network inputs (graph adjacency matrices with node features, point clouds, or image-encoded Hamiltonian parameters) and augments them with a sampled group element applied to a learned vector, which is concatenated to each component (node, pixel, etc.) before processing. The output is a sample from an equivariant conditional distribution—e.g., a reconstructed graph, a generated molecular graph, or a spin configuration—rather than a deterministic equivariant function output. For the Ising model experiments, the input is a 128×128 two-channel image encoding of coupling constants J and external field h, and the output is a 64×64 binary spin configuration.

## Method

The core mechanism is SymPE (Symmetry-breaking Positional Encoding): a group element g̃ is sampled from the inversion kernel (implemented via randomized canonicalization, e.g., sorting for permutation groups or energy minimization for rotation groups), applied to a learned vector v, and the result g̃v is concatenated to the input before passing through any equivariant network f₀. The theoretical foundation (Theorem 3.4) establishes that any equivariant conditional distribution P(Y|X) can be represented as Y = f(X, g̃, ε) where f is jointly equivariant in its first two arguments, with g̃ drawn from the inversion kernel. This is interpreted as a positional encoding: the sampled group element specifies the 'position' of the input relative to a canonical representative, disambiguating symmetric elements (e.g., isomorphic graph nodes) while preserving the equivariance inductive bias. The paper further proves (Proposition 5.1) that more general equivariant noise injection suffices for representational completeness, and (Theorem 6.1) that equivariant noise injection yields provable generalization benefits via orthogonal decomposition of the hypothesis space.

## Results

On graph autoencoding (Erdős-Rényi graphs, edge probability 0.25), SymPE achieves a BCE loss of 3.7 and 0.77% reconstruction error, substantially outperforming no symmetry breaking (BCE 10.0, 3.9% error), random noise (BCE 10.1), uniform sampling (BCE 5.7), and Laplacian features (BCE 5.6). On molecular generation with DiGress on QM9 (explicit hydrogens), SymPE reduces the negative log-likelihood from 129.7 (DiGress with Laplacian) to 30.3, a 4.3× improvement, while also improving molecular stability from 79.8% to 82.5%. On Ising model ground-state prediction, SymPE achieves significantly lower average energy than all baselines on the out-of-distribution test set and is only slightly worse than relaxed group convolutions in-distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiGress with Laplacian spectral features (Vignac et al., 2023), SymPE achieves a 4.3× reduction in NLL (30.3 vs. 129.7) on QM9, indicating far better distributional learning, while also improving molecular stability by ~2.7 percentage points (82.5% vs. 79.8%). The ablation showing DiGress+SymPE on nodes only (NLL 128.8) confirms that applying the symmetry-breaking encoding directly to the adjacency matrix is critical. Against relaxed group convolutions (Wang et al., 2024) on Ising model ground-state prediction, SymPE achieves significantly lower energy on the out-of-distribution test set (where Hamiltonians are rotated 90°) but is only slightly worse in-distribution, suggesting SymPE better preserves the equivariant inductive bias under distribution shift. On graph autoencoding, SymPE (BCE 3.7) outperforms all baselines including Laplacian positional encodings (BCE 5.6) and uniform random permutation sampling (BCE 5.7), with the gap over no symmetry breaking being 2.7× in BCE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bloem-Reddy & Teh's (2020) representation of equivariant distributions—which required the group to act freely (no self-symmetries)—by introducing the inversion kernel to handle self-symmetric inputs, building on Chiu & Bloem-Reddy's (2023) first ML application of the inversion kernel and Kaba et al.'s (2023) learned canonicalization framework. It subsumes and unifies several prior symmetry-breaking approaches: relaxed equivariance (Kaba & Ravanbakhsh, 2023), equivariant symmetry breaking sets (Xie & Smidt, 2024), and generic noise injection, showing they are all special cases of equivariant conditional distributions. The practical method (SymPE) is positioned as a principled alternative to ad-hoc positional encodings (Laplacian, random walk) that either break equivariance or cannot break automorphism symmetries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both a clean theoretical characterization (necessary and sufficient conditions for representing equivariant conditional distributions) and a drop-in practical method (concatenate a learned vector transformed by a sampled group element) that works across group types and architectures. Key open questions remain: sampling from inversion kernels for general or infinite groups, handling partial symmetry breaking (output breaks some but not all input symmetries), and the tradeoff between structured low-entropy symmetry breaking and generic noise injection. The method's reliance on a canonicalization procedure limits its applicability to groups where such procedures are tractable, and the NLL gains, while large, are measured on a single dataset configuration with other metrics already near saturation.
