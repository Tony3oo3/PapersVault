## Problem

Existing pre-training data detection methods for LLMs (e.g., Min-K%, Loss, Reference) are built on simple heuristics without a principled theoretical foundation for why training data leaves detectable footprints in the model. This paper asks what structural characteristics training samples actually exhibit in the modeled distribution and provides a score-matching-based answer that reframes detection as local-maxima identification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252334/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single forward pass of the target LLM to obtain logits, from which it computes per-token log probabilities, the vocabulary-wide mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) of log probabilities at each position, and the final sentence-level score via averaging the k% lowest token scores. No reference model, multiple forward passes, or external statistics are needed, making it as computationally cheap as the simplest baselines (Loss, Zlib, Min-K%). The output is a continuous score per input sentence that is thresholded for binary training/non-training classification.

## Method

The key theoretical insight, derived via implicit score matching, is that maximum likelihood training drives training samples to be local maxima (or near local maxima) of the modeled likelihood along each input dimension. Translated to discrete LLMs, this means each training token should form a mode or have relatively high probability under the conditional categorical distribution p(·|x_<t). The scoring function computes a z-score normalization: (log p(x_t|x_<t) − μ_{·|x_<t}) / σ_{·|x_<t}, where μ and σ are the mean and standard deviation of log-probabilities across the full vocabulary given the prefix, effectively measuring how much the target token's log-likelihood deviates above the vocabulary average in units of local spread. The sentence-level score is the mean of the k% lowest token scores, following the Min-K% aggregation strategy. This calibration makes the score robust to inputs where training tokens have low absolute probability but still dominate the local distribution, a failure mode of raw-probability methods like Min-K%.

## Results

On the WikiMIA benchmark, Min-K%++ achieves an average AUROC of 78.3% (original) and 75.9% (paraphrased) at length 32, outperforming the runner-up Min-K% by 10.5% and 8.0% respectively. At length 128, the average AUROC reaches 77.2% (original) and 70.6% (paraphrased), still leading Min-K% by 6.2% and 2.4%. On the more challenging MIMIR benchmark, Min-K%++ achieves an average AUROC of 58.7% across 7 subsets with Pythia-12B, matching the reference-based Ref method (58.7%) while requiring no extra model, and outperforming all other reference-free methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Min-K%++ substantially outperforms the prior state-of-the-art Min-K% (Shi et al., 2024) by 6.2–10.5% AUROC on WikiMIA (original setting) and by 0.5–2.8% on MIMIR, with the gap widening for larger models. Against the Loss baseline (Yeom et al., 2018), Min-K%++ leads by 10–17% AUROC on WikiMIA and by 4–5% on MIMIR. Compared to the reference-based methods from Carlini et al. (2021) (Ref, Zlib, Lowercase), Min-K%++ surpasses Zlib and Lowercase by 8–16% on WikiMIA, and on MIMIR it matches the Ref method's average AUROC (58.7% vs. 58.7% at 12B) without requiring an additional LLM for calibration. Against the Neighbor method (Mattern et al., 2023), Min-K%++ leads by 8–12% on WikiMIA and by 2–4% on MIMIR, while also avoiding the 25× computational overhead of Neighbor's perturbation-based approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Min-K% (Shi et al., 2024), the first method purpose-built for pre-training data detection in LLMs, by replacing its heuristic token-probability scoring with a theoretically grounded formulation derived from implicit score matching. The key departure is the insight that maximum likelihood training drives training samples toward local maxima along each input dimension, which in the discrete LLM setting translates to identifying whether the input token forms a mode of the conditional categorical distribution. The resulting score is a z-score normalization of the token log-probability using the conditional distribution's mean and standard deviation, connecting the method to calibration literature (temperature scaling) while remaining reference-free, unlike Carlini et al.'s reference-based MIA approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pre-training data detection is critical for addressing copyright infringement, privacy leakage, and benchmark contamination in the LLM era, yet prior methods lacked principled foundations. Min-K%++ provides the first theoretically motivated scoring function for this task and establishes a strong reference-free baseline that matches reference-based methods requiring an extra LLM. However, gains on the harder MIMIR benchmark (where training and non-training data come from the same distribution) are modest (0.5–2.8% relative), suggesting the local-maxima signal weakens when distributional and temporal cues are absent. Open questions include whether the insight generalizes to other modalities (the authors hint at diffusion models) and whether the k%-aggregation step can be replaced by a more principled sequence-level scoring.
