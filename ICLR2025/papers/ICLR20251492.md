## Problem

Existing video captioning benchmarks either provide a single holistic caption or rely on annotators to subjectively segment events, failing to explicitly model the multi-shot structure that naturally organizes events in short videos. Moreover, no prior benchmark captures both shot-level visual descriptions and human narration captions, nor provides comprehensive video-level summaries that track event progression, scene transitions, and subject identity across shots.

## Input / Output

For captioning and summarization, the model receives 4 uniformly sampled frames per shot (resized to 224×224) arranged sequentially to preserve shot boundaries, plus optional ASR text; for QA, it additionally receives a question string. Outputs are structured as: per-shot visual captions, per-shot narration captions (linking speech to visual referents), a coherent multi-shot summary averaging 218.3 words with explicit transition language, and short QA answers. The shot-structured token arrangement is a key design choice distinguishing SUM-shot from holistic frame sampling.

## Method

The benchmark introduces 42,958 multi-shot videos (2–8 shots, 10–40s) sourced from HD-VILA-100M with multi-stage filtering (CLIP-based visual-ASR correlation, inter-shot diversity, static-content removal). For modeling, the paper adapts MiniGPT-4 and VideoChat2 by freezing the vision encoder (ViT-G/14 or UMT-L) and LLM (Vicuna-7B), fine-tuning only the Q-Former and MLP bridge (or LoRA adapters for VideoChat2) on shot-structured frame tokens concatenated with ASR text. A key architectural finding is that explicitly arranging tokens per shot (SUM-shot) substantially outperforms treating the video as a flat frame sequence (SUM-holistic), confirming that shot boundaries carry critical structural information. For the QA task, the approach bypasses instruction tuning entirely: the SUM-shot model generates a text summary, which is then fed to a frozen Vicuna model alongside the question to produce an answer, validated by GPT-3.5-turbo binary scoring.

## Results

On the proposed Shot2Story-QA benchmark, VideoChat2-SUM-shot achieves an overall accuracy of 40.5% (36.1% temporal, 41.5% holistic, 43.8% audio), substantially outperforming the best existing video-VLM (VideoChat2 at 14.5%). In zero-shot video QA on external benchmarks, VideoChat2-SUM-shot reaches 58.5% on MSRVTT-QA and 47.1% on ActivityNet-QA, matching or exceeding SOTA models like Video-LLaVA (59.2%, 45.3%) without any instruction-tuning data. For multi-shot video summarization, VideoChat2-SUM-shot achieves the best CIDEr score of 9.0, nearly 9× higher than Video-ChatGPT (1.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary Shot2Story-QA benchmark, VideoChat2-SUM-shot (40.5% overall) dramatically outperforms all four existing video-VLMs: VideoChat2 (14.5%), Video-LLaVA (14.3%), Video-ChatGPT (14.2%), and LLaMA-VID (9.7%), with the gap driven by the absence of ASR input and multi-shot training data in those baselines. On zero-shot MSRVTT-QA, VideoChat2-SUM-shot (58.5%) is competitive with the SOTA Video-LLaVA (59.2%) and LLaMA-VID (57.7%), while on ActivityNet-QA it surpasses all baselines including VideoChat2 (49.1%) and Video-LLaVA (45.3%). For single-shot captioning, VideoChat2-C with ASR (CIDEr 43.9) outperforms VAST with all modalities (CIDEr 34.0), and adding ASR text to MiniGPT4-C boosts CIDEr from 25.1 to 35.9. In summarization, the shot-structured architecture (SUM-shot) consistently beats the holistic approach (SUM-holistic) by 4.8 CIDEr points, and incorporating ASR adds another 2.8 CIDEr points. The key finding is that converting video QA into text-based QA via generated summaries enables competitive zero-shot performance without any instruction-tuning, a setting none of the compared baselines support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MiniGPT-4 and VideoChat2 architectures (ViT-G/14 + Q-Former + Vicuna/UMT-L) as base models, but its core contribution is a new benchmark that departs from prior video captioning datasets (MSRVTT, ActivityNet, YouCook2) which annotate multi-shot videos in a coarse-grained, holistic manner. The closest prior work, VAST (Chen et al., 2024), also targets multi-shot video understanding but processes the video as a whole without explicit shot structure and relies on unverified generated captions. Shot2Story introduces explicit shot-level segmentation via TransNetV2, dual visual+narration captions per shot, and GPT-4-generated (human-verified) video summaries that capture transitions and subject identity across shots.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a clear gap: no prior dataset provides shot-granular annotations combining visual and narration captions with long coherent summaries for multi-shot videos, making it a natural testbed for evaluating temporal reasoning and cross-shot coherence in video LLMs. A key open question is whether the summary-as-intermediate-representation paradigm (video → text summary → text QA) generalizes to more complex or longer videos, and whether the GPT-4-generated summaries introduce systematic biases that limit downstream task performance. The dataset's human-centric bias (inherited from HD-VILA-100M filtering) and short video duration (10–40s) also constrain its applicability to longer-form or non-human-centric content.
