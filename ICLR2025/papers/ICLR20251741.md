## Problem

State-of-the-art video-to-audio generation methods (Diff-Foley, See and Hear, FoleyCrafter) rely on heavy U-Net architectures with 860M–1252M parameters, consuming large memory and slow inference, while also using redundant video features (CAVP frame features share >90% cosine similarity) and spatial-aware masking strategies designed for images rather than audio. The paper asks whether a lightweight, pure-Transformer masked diffusion model can surpass these systems in alignment accuracy while being orders of magnitude more efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251741/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a silent video clip truncated to 8.2 seconds (32 frames at 224×224×3), encodes it with a frozen pre-trained CAVP extractor into 32×512 features, and reduces them to a single 1×768 vector via a learnable Reducer. The output is a mel-spectrogram (128×512) decoded from a 4×16×64 latent (256 tokens of dim 768) by a frozen Stable Diffusion VAE, then converted to a waveform via Griffin-Lim or a HiFi-GAN vocoder. The framework also supports an AudioLDM VAE (64×816 mel-spectrogram, 816 latent tokens) as an alternative, trading slightly lower alignment accuracy for better FAD.

## Method

MDSGen replaces the U-Net denoiser with a DiT backbone (12 layers, 3 model sizes from 5M to 131M params) and introduces two key innovations. First, a Reducer module (MLP + 1×1 conv) condenses the 32-frame CAVP features into a single 768-dim vector injected via AdaLN modulation, eliminating the >90% inter-frame redundancy that causes overfitting in Diff-Foley's cross-attention design. Second, a Temporal-Aware Mask (TAM) strategy masks entire temporal columns of the 256-token latent (30% ratio by default) rather than spatial patches, with a side-interpolator block reconstructing masked tokens before the final 4 decoder layers; this exploits audio's inherent temporal structure and yields a 4-point IS gain over spatial masking. Training combines standard ε-prediction diffusion loss with a masking loss (λ=1.0), uses DPM-Solver (25 steps) with dynamic CFG (scale 5, power 0.01) at inference, and requires no pre-trained diffusion backbone.

## Results

On VGGSound, MDSGen-T (5M params) achieves 97.91% alignment accuracy, 14.18 FID, and 37.51 IS, surpassing the 860M-parameter Diff-Foley (93.92% alignment, 10.55 FID, 56.67 IS) while using 172× fewer parameters and 36× faster inference (0.01s vs 0.36s). The larger MDSGen-B (131M) reaches 98.55% alignment accuracy, 11.19 FID, 52.77 IS, and 1.34 FAD, achieving state-of-the-art alignment and FAD across all baselines. On Flickr-SoundNet, MDSGen-B attains the best CIoU (82.01%) among all methods, closest to the ground-truth value of 83.94%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diff-Foley (860M, the strongest prior method), MDSGen-B wins on alignment accuracy (98.55% vs 93.92%), FAD (1.34 vs 4.71), and inference speed (0.05s vs 0.36s), but trails on IS (52.77 vs 56.67) and FID (11.19 vs 10.55); the tiny MDSGen-T (5M) already beats Diff-Foley on alignment (97.91% vs 93.92%) and FAD (2.21 vs 4.71) while being 172× smaller. Against See and Hear (1099M), MDSGen dominates on every metric: FAD (1.34 vs 5.55), FID (11.19 vs 21.35), IS (52.77 vs 19.23), alignment (98.55% vs 58.14%), and speed (0.05s vs 18.25s). Against FoleyCrafter (1252M), MDSGen-B wins on FAD (1.34 vs 2.45), alignment (98.55% vs 83.54%), IS (52.77 vs 42.06), and speed (0.05s vs 2.96s), though FoleyCrafter has a slightly lower KL (5.67 vs 6.27). Against SpecVQGAN and Im2Wav (autoregressive baselines), MDSGen wins decisively on alignment accuracy (97.9% vs 49.2% and 67.4%) and inference speed (0.01s vs 5.47s and 6.41s), though both older methods report lower FID (9.70 and 11.44 vs 14.18 for MDSGen-T). Overall, MDSGen sets new state-of-the-art on alignment accuracy and FAD across all model sizes, with the primary trade-off being a modest IS gap versus Diff-Foley's pre-trained Stable Diffusion backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDSGen builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) for denoising diffusion and the Masked Diffusion Transformer (Gao et al., 2023) for masked generative modeling, but departs from both by replacing spatial-aware masking with a temporal-aware masking strategy tailored to audio's sequential structure. It also builds on Diff-Foley (Luo et al., 2023) for the CAVP video feature extractor and the overall video-to-audio formulation, but fundamentally restructures the pipeline: replacing the 860M-parameter U-Net backbone with a lightweight transformer, and introducing a Reducer module that collapses 32 redundant video frame features into a single 768-dim vector via a 1×1 convolution. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that audio's temporal dimension, not spatial structure, is the natural axis for masked denoising, and that the high inter-frame similarity (>90% cosine) in CAVP features is actively harmful rather than informative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video-to-audio generation does not require hundreds of millions of parameters: a 5M-parameter model trained from scratch outperforms the 860M-parameter Diff-Foley on alignment accuracy while being 36× faster and using 371% less memory, a gap large enough to shift the field's assumptions about the parameter-efficiency frontier for this task. The temporal-aware masking insight and the finding that aggressive feature reduction (32→1 channels) improves rather than degrades generation quality are transferable principles for other sequential-signal generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos (currently fixed at 8.2s), whether single-step diffusion can eliminate the iterative sampling cost, and whether the Image VAE's channel-selection quirk (G channel outperforming R) reflects a deeper mismatch between image-trained VAEs and audio data that a purpose-built 1D VAE could resolve.
