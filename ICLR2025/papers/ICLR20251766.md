## Problem

Traditional video moment retrieval (VMR) systems assume each query maps to exactly one target segment, but real-world queries often correspond to multiple non-consecutive moments or to no relevant content at all. This paper introduces the Generalized Video Moment Retrieval (GVMR) task to handle both one-to-many and no-target queries, and provides a dedicated dataset and model to address this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251766/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed video (128 uniformly sampled clips processed by SlowFast and CLIP ViT-B/32 encoders) and a natural language description (token-level features from CLIP text encoder) as input. It outputs a variable number of temporal (start, end) timestamp pairs along with per-prediction evidence scores; if all evidence scores fall below a threshold δ=0.7, the sample is classified as no-target. Unlike fixed-output VMR models, the number of predicted segments is not predetermined and can be zero, one, or multiple.

## Method

BCANet is a transformer encoder-decoder that fuses SlowFast and CLIP video features with CLIP text features in a shared 256-dim space. Its core innovation is the Boundary-aware Cross Attention (BCA) module, which combines two sub-components: (1) Boundary-aware Contrastive Learning (BCL) aggregates ground-truth region features via average pooling and contrasts them with sentence features using a symmetric InfoNCE-style loss to produce boundary-aware video representations, and (2) Query-region Cross Attention (QCA) applies cross-attention between L=10 learnable queries and the enhanced video features, supervised by a proxy loss that encourages each query to attend to the correct temporal span. The final predictions are obtained through a three-layer MLP over decoder outputs, trained with a set-prediction loss based on Hungarian-algorithm bipartite matching (L1 + IoU cost) plus the BCL and proxy losses. During inference, BCL and proxy components are discarded, and no-target detection relies solely on the evidence-score threshold.

## Results

On the NExT-VMR dataset, BCANet achieves an average score of 56.75 (mAP@0.25/0.5/0.7), outperforming the strongest baseline EaTR (55.67) and Moment-DETR (47.45). In terms of recall, BCANet reaches R1@0.5 = 48.61 and R1@0.7 = 37.10, surpassing all competing methods. For no-target detection, BCANet attains N-accuracy of 77.92% and T-accuracy of 95.79%, marginally exceeding EaTR (76.44% / 95.68%) and Moment-DETR (75.12% / 94.28%). On traditional VMR benchmarks, BCANet also leads on Charades (R1@0.5 = 59.47, mIoU = 51.97) and TACoS (R1@0.3 = 42.81, mIoU = 30.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BCANet consistently outperforms all transformer-based VMR baselines on the NExT-VMR benchmark. Against Moment-DETR, BCANet gains +9.3 points in average mAP (56.75 vs. 47.45) and +6.75 in R1@0.5, reflecting the advantage of explicit multi-region detection over single-moment prediction. Compared to QD-DETR, BCANet improves average mAP by +3.1 points and R1@0.7 by +2.0 points, while also achieving higher T-accuracy (95.79% vs. 92.67%). Against the strongest baseline EaTR, BCANet leads by +1.1 in average mAP and +3.8 in mAP@0.7 (41.22 vs. 36.91), with a marginal edge in N-accuracy (77.92% vs. 76.44%). The video-LLM baseline Chat-UniVi (prompt-based) trails significantly, scoring only 25.26 average mAP and 68.48% T-accuracy, indicating that current LLM-based approaches struggle with multi-segment temporal localization. On traditional single-target benchmarks (Charades, TACoS), BCANet also achieves the best R1@0.5 (59.47) and mIoU (51.97) on Charades, and the best R1@0.3 (42.81) and mIoU (30.01) on TACoS, confirming generalizability beyond the GVMR setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer encoder-decoder paradigm for video moment retrieval established by QVHighlights/Moment-DETR (Lei et al., 2021), reusing its bipartite matching loss and cross-attention decoder structure while introducing a Boundary-aware Cross Attention (BCA) module that combines contrastive learning with query-region attention for boundary prediction. Conceptually, it departs from the single-target assumption in prior VMR work (DiDeMo, Charades-STA, TACoS, QVHighlights) by formulating a generalized task (GVMR) that accommodates no-target and one-to-multi queries, drawing inspiration from generalized referring expression segmentation (Liu et al., 2023a). The dataset construction pipeline repurposes YFCC100M (the same source as DiDeMo and TEMPO) but augments it with LLM-based query refinement (LLaVA for scene captioning, GPT-3.5-turbo for polishing), a practice not seen in earlier VMR benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GVMR formulation and NExT-VMR dataset address a genuine gap: real-world video search queries frequently have zero or multiple relevant segments, yet all prior VMR benchmarks assume exactly one ground-truth moment per query. The 77.92% no-target accuracy and the modest ~1% average improvement over EaTR on the new benchmark suggest the task is still far from solved, particularly for no-target detection where a simple evidence-score threshold (δ=0.7) is the sole mechanism. Open questions include whether the (s,p,o)-tuple-based query construction limits semantic diversity compared to free-form natural language, how the approach scales to truly long-form video (the dataset uses 128 uniformly sampled clips), and whether the contrastive boundary learning component generalizes beyond the YFCC100M domain.
