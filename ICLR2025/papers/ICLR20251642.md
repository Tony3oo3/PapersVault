## Problem

Uncertainty quantification for Bird's Eye View semantic segmentation (BEVSS) remains largely unexplored, with no systematic benchmark comparing methods across datasets and architectures. The standard UCE loss used in evidential networks provably drives the predicted Dirichlet distribution toward a degenerate peak, producing overconfident epistemic uncertainty estimates that fail to flag out-of-distribution pixels. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a comprehensive benchmark and a novel loss function that corrects the overconfidence pathology.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n surround-camera RGB images (each 3×H×W) together with their extrinsic (3×4) and intrinsic (3×3) calibration matrices, which are used by the backbone (LSS, CVT, or Simple-BEV) to project features into a shared BEV grid of size M×N. The network outputs, per BEV pixel, a Dirichlet concentration vector α ∈ ℝ⁺ᶜ from which the expected class probabilities, aleatoric uncertainty (u^alea = −max_c p̄_c), and epistemic uncertainty (u^epis = C/α₀) are derived in closed form, requiring only a single forward pass at inference.

## Method

The core contribution is the UFCE (Uncertainty Focal Cross-Entropy) loss, which replaces the expected cross-entropy in the standard UCE objective with the expected focal loss over the predicted Dirichlet distribution, yielding a closed-form expression involving Beta and digamma functions. The paper proves that UFCE's gradient norm relative to UCE's is larger for low-confidence predictions and smaller for high-confidence ones, acting as an implicit weight regularizer that shifts optimization focus toward harder, less-confident samples. Two additional components complete the framework: Epistemic Uncertainty Scaling (EUS), which multiplies the per-pixel loss by (1 + C·ξ/α₀) to upweight pixels with low total evidence, and Evidence-Regularized Learning (ER), which adds a KL-divergence-to-flat-Dirichlet term on auxiliary OOD training data to explicitly shape epistemic uncertainty. The full objective L^UFCE-EUS-ER is trained end-to-end on three BEVSS backbones across CARLA, nuScenes, and Lyft, with the final softmax replaced by ReLU to produce Dirichlet parameters in a single pass.

## Results

On nuScenes vehicle segmentation with pseudo-OOD supervision, the proposed UFCE-EUS-ER framework achieves the best OOD detection AUPR of 0.335 (LSS) and 0.269 (CVT), with an average 4.758% AUPR improvement over the runner-up (ENN-UCE). For calibration, it attains the lowest ECE of 0.00193 (LSS) and 0.00019 (CVT), while maintaining competitive segmentation IoU (0.356 LSS, 0.319 CVT). Across all 12 OOD detection metrics (AUROC, AUPR, FPR95 × 2 backbones × 2 datasets), the proposed method outperforms every baseline, with average gains of 2.7% AUROC, 6.5% AUPR, and 4.3% FPR95 over the runner-up.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed UFCE-EUS-ER framework is compared against five uncertainty quantification methods: softmax Entropy, Energy, Deep Ensemble, MC Dropout, and ENN with UCE loss. Against the strongest baseline (ENN-UCE), the proposed method wins on OOD detection AUPR (0.335 vs. 0.315 on LSS, 0.269 vs. 0.212 on CVT), calibration ECE (0.00193 vs. 0.00342 on LSS), and segmentation IoU (0.356 vs. 0.343 on LSS). Against Energy-Focal, the gap is dramatic on OOD AUPR (0.335 vs. 0.0444 on LSS), though Energy-Focal achieves slightly higher misclassification AUROC (0.951 vs. 0.911 on LSS). Ensemble and Dropout baselines cannot leverage pseudo-OOD supervision and perform poorly on OOD detection (AUPR < 0.001 without pseudo-OOD), making them unsuitable for the primary epistemic uncertainty task. The ablation confirms each component contributes: ER adds ~26% OOD AUPR, EUS further improves misclassification detection, and UFCE reduces ECE by 0.002555 and improves misclassification AUROC by 4.15% over UCE alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the evidential deep learning framework (Sensoy et al., 2018) from image classification to pixel-level Bird's Eye View semantic segmentation, replacing the standard UCE loss with a novel UFCE loss motivated by the calibration properties of focal loss (Lin et al., 2017). It departs from prior uncertainty quantification work in segmentation (Mukhoti & Gal, 2018; Mukhoti et al., 2021) by addressing the unique challenges of multi-view BEV representation, where perspective distortion and sensor fusion complicate uncertainty propagation. The paper also positions itself against post-hoc methods (entropy, energy) and multi-pass methods (ensembles, dropout) by demonstrating that a single-pass evidential approach with appropriate loss design can match or exceed their OOD detection performance at a fraction of the inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a first benchmark for uncertainty quantification in BEVSS, this paper provides the autonomous driving community with a structured evaluation protocol across three datasets, three architectures, and five uncertainty methods—filling a gap that limits safe deployment of BEV-based perception. The finding that OOD detection AUPR values are near zero without pseudo-OOD exposure across all methods reveals a fundamental challenge: the multi-view-to-BEV transformation appears to degrade epistemic uncertainty signals in ways not present in standard 2D segmentation. Open questions include whether incorporating camera-view-level uncertainties before BEV projection could recover lost signal, and whether the UFCE loss generalizes to other dense prediction tasks beyond BEV segmentation.
