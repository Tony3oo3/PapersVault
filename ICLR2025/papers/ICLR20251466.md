## Problem

Existing multi-modal 3D object detection fusion methods either apply uniform weights across entire feature maps (MoE-based) or combine outputs from independently trained detectors (late fusion), failing to capture object-level uncertainty variation and enabling feature-level cross-modal enrichment simultaneously. The paper identifies that optimal camera-to-LiDAR fusion ratios vary significantly per object based on size, distance, and lighting conditions, yet no prior method quantifies this uncertainty at both the object and intermediate feature levels.

## Input / Output

Cocoon operates on top of a query-based transformer detector (FUTR3D or TransFusion), consuming per-query features extracted by modality-specific encoders (ResNet-101 for 6 RGB cameras, VoxelNet for 32-beam LiDAR) at each of the 6 transformer decoder layers. Rather than operating on final detection outputs, it works on intermediate per-object features projected into a 128-dimensional common space, producing per-modality weights (α, β) that modulate the element-wise sum of camera and LiDAR features before they pass through the remaining decoder layers to yield 3D bounding boxes, class labels, and confidence scores.

## Method

Cocoon introduces Feature Impression (FI) nodes—learnable per-class surrogate ground truths that serve as geometric medians of aligned multi-modal features—combined with a multi-layer perceptron feature aligner that projects heterogeneous camera and LiDAR features into a shared representation space. Uncertainty is quantified via conformal prediction: nonconformity scores (distance from aligned features to their nearest FI node) are computed on a calibration set, and the p-value of the online test feature's score against this pool, combined with a stability score measuring top-1 FI consistency across decoder layers, yields per-object, per-modality weights. The training objective (Eq. 3) jointly optimizes the aligner and FI nodes using three terms: L_center pulls features toward their FI, L_geomed enforces the Weiszfeld geometric-median condition, and L_separate maintains inter-class FI separation. This eliminates the need for a modality-specific decoder (g) required by prior Feature CP, enabling direct feature-space uncertainty quantification for heterogeneous representations.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set with FUTR3D as the base model, Cocoon achieves 66.80 mAP under normal conditions, outperforming the static 1:1 fusion baseline (66.16) and the closest uncertainty-aware competitor Cal-DETR(Q) (66.38). The most dramatic gains appear under sensor corruption: in camera blackout, Cocoon reaches 51.87 mAP versus 45.13 for static fusion (+6.74 mAP, ~15% relative improvement) and 45.25 for Cal-DETR(Q). Across all eight evaluated scenarios (natural weather variations and artificial corruptions), Cocoon consistently achieves the highest mAP, with improvements of 0.4–6.7 mAP over the best competing method depending on severity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cocoon outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated scenario. The closest competitor is Cal-DETR(Q) (query-level uncertainty via decoder variance), which Cocoon beats by 0.42 mAP in normal conditions and by 6.62 mAP under camera blackout (51.87 vs. 45.25). MoE-based AdapNet* and self-attention (Zhu et al.) underperform even the static 1:1 fusion baseline in most scenarios, confirming that uniform or attention-based weighting without explicit uncertainty modeling is insufficient. Late fusion (Zhang et al.) improves over MoE methods but still lags Cocoon by 2.87 mAP in normal conditions and 12.48 mAP under blackout, illustrating the limitation of combining independently trained detectors. The NC function validity is further confirmed on 10 regression datasets (Table 5), where Cocoon's geometric-median-based surrogate ground truth achieves the lowest absolute deviation from the target 90% coverage (mean Abs Diff 0.08) compared to Basic CP (0.19) and Feature CP (0.19). On TransFusion (Appendix F), Cocoon similarly improves over AdapNet* under all corruption types, with the largest gain of +3.4 mAP on trucks during camera blackout.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cocoon builds on transformer-based multi-modal 3D detectors (FUTR3D, TransFusion) and extends conformal prediction from the output space to the feature space for heterogeneous representations. It departs from prior adaptive fusion methods—MoE-based gating (AdapNet), self-attention weighting, and late/output-level fusion (Zhang et al., Cal-DETR)—by introducing a feature aligner that projects camera and LiDAR features into a shared space, combined with a learnable surrogate ground truth (Feature Impression) defined as the geometric median of aligned features. This enables conformal prediction at intermediate decoder layers without requiring modality-specific decoders, a requirement that Feature CP (Teng et al., 2023) imposes but which is infeasible in multi-modal detection architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cocoon addresses a concrete gap: no prior method simultaneously handles object-level uncertainty variation and feature-level cross-modal enrichment, and the 15% mAP gain under camera blackout demonstrates practical value for safety-critical autonomous driving. However, the 0.5-second runtime overhead on FUTR3D (mitigated to ~50ms via fine-tuning) and the reliance on a fixed number of FI nodes (one per class) limit immediate deployment. Open questions include whether the geometric-median FI formulation generalizes to more than two modalities (e.g., radar, language) and whether the conformal calibration assumption of exchangeability holds under distribution shift in real-world deployment.
