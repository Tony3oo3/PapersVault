## Problem

Existing high-fidelity 3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) produce entangled representations that are neither semantically interpretable nor precisely editable at the part level. Prior compositional approaches each suffer from at least one of three limitations: low fidelity (primitive-based), semantic incoherence (geometric fitting without semantic guidance), or only holistic editability (segmentation-based). GaussianBlock aims to simultaneously achieve semantically coherent part disentanglement, precise per-part physical editing, and competitive reconstruction fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252528/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes calibrated multi-view RGB images (resized to 400×300) and their corresponding binary silhouettes (from a pre-trained dichotomous segmentation model) as input. It produces a hybrid 3D scene representation where semantically coherent superquadric primitives serve as coarse, independently manipulable building blocks, and 3D Gaussians are bound to individual triangle faces of those primitives via identity parameters (id_k, id_t) that persist through densification. This hierarchical binding structure means that translating, rotating, or deleting a primitive automatically propagates the transformation to all Gaussians attached to its triangles, enabling drag-mode physical editing.

## Method

GaussianBlock uses a two-stage pipeline. In Stage 1, parameterized superquadrics are optimized against silhouettes using a novel Attention-guided Centering (AC) loss: soft dual-rasterization derives point and box prompts from each superquadric, which query SAM's cross-attention maps; HDBSCAN clustering of these maps identifies semantic groups, and outlier vertices are penalized for deviating from the major cluster's centroid, encouraging each primitive to cover a single semantic part. A dynamic splitting/fusion strategy (triggered every 1k iterations) further enforces compactness by splitting primitives that span multiple semantics and fusing those that share one. In Stage 2, 3D Gaussians are initialized at superquadric triangle centers and rendered via a localized-to-global transformation (position offset by triangle center, rotation composed with triangle orientation), inspired by GaussianAvatars. A positional regularization loss (L_pos) keeps Gaussians near their bound primitives, and the binding inheritance strategy ensures identity parameters propagate through Gaussian Splatting's adaptive density control (splitting/cloning), preserving the primitive-Gaussian association throughout optimization.

## Results

On DTU, GaussianBlock achieves the best Chamfer Distance (3.69) among all methods for primitives reconstruction, outperforming DBW (4.78) and EMS (5.67). For reconstruction fidelity, GaussianBlock achieves SSIM 94.3 / PSNR 30.7 / LPIPS 10.3 on DTU, 90.8 / 28.8 / 11.2 on BlendedMVS, 81.3 / 22.0 / 20.7 on Truck, and 84.8 / 23.1 / 16.9 on Garden. While it trails pure Gaussian-based 3DGS by ~4–5 PSNR points, it substantially outperforms the only other editable primitive-based method DBW by over 11 PSNR on DTU and 12 PSNR on BlendedMVS, while being the only method that supports both part-aware segmentation and precise part-level editing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianBlock is compared against four baselines: 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023), OmniSeg3D (Ying et al., 2024), DBW (Monnier et al., 2023), and EMS (Liu et al., 2022). On primitives reconstruction quality (Chamfer Distance on DTU), GaussianBlock achieves 3.69, beating DBW (4.78) by 23% and EMS (5.67) by 35%, despite EMS using ground-truth 3D point clouds during optimization. On reconstruction fidelity, GaussianBlock trails 3DGS by 4–5 PSNR points across all four datasets (e.g., 30.7 vs 35.7 on DTU) and OmniSeg3D by 2–4 PSNR, which the authors acknowledge as expected since their method is not fidelity-oriented. However, GaussianBlock dramatically outperforms DBW in fidelity (30.7 vs 19.6 PSNR on DTU; 28.8 vs 16.4 on BlendedMVS), making it the only method that simultaneously supports part-aware semantic segmentation, precise part-level editing, and competitive reconstruction quality. The paper positions itself as filling the gap: 3DGS and OmniSeg3D lack editability, DBW lacks fidelity and semantic coherence, and GaussianBlock is the sole method checking all three boxes (segmentation + editing + competitive fidelity).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: primitive-based compositional reconstruction (DBW, DPA-Net) and segmentation-based Gaussian/NeRF decomposition (OmniSeg3D, GaussianGrouping, SegAnyGaussians). It departs from both by proposing a hybrid representation where superquadrics provide semantically coherent, editable building blocks while bound 3D Gaussians (inspired by GaussianAvatars' localized parameterization) supply reconstruction fidelity. The novel Attention-guided Centering loss repurposes SAM's cross-attention maps as a clustering signal to enforce semantic disentanglement of primitives, and the binding inheritance strategy extends Gaussian Splatting's density control to maintain primitive-Gaussian associations during optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in 3D reconstruction: current high-fidelity methods (3DGS, NeRF) produce entangled representations that resist precise part-level editing, while existing compositional methods sacrifice fidelity or semantic coherence. The binding inheritance mechanism offers a reusable pattern for making any Gaussian-based scene editable through primitive manipulation. However, the method is limited to object-centric entities (silhouette-bounded), cannot handle complex backgrounds, and the fidelity gap versus pure 3DGS (~5 dB PSNR on DTU) remains significant. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to unbounded scenes, whether more diverse primitives (cuboids, curved surfaces) can close the fidelity gap, and whether the AC loss can be extended beyond SAM to other semantic models.
