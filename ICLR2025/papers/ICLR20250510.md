## Problem

Existing node anomaly detection (NAD) methods embed graphs in a single fixed-curvature space, overlooking that different structural patterns in NAD datasets (hierarchical, circular, plain) are best captured by different geometries. Additionally, under the extremely limited supervision typical of real NAD tasks, data augmentation techniques such as pseudo-label generation introduce substantial noise that degrades rather than improves detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250510/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed graph G = (V, E, X) with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{|V|×d} and a very small set of labeled nodes (experiments use only 50 training and 50 validation labels per dataset). It produces a binary probability vector Z ∈ R^{|V|×2} for each node, indicating the likelihood of being anomalous. The framework is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss on the limited labeled subset, with all curvature parameters and ensemble weights learned jointly.

## Method

SpaceGNN ensembles three base GNNs operating in Euclidean (κ=0), hyperbolic (κ<0), and spherical (κ>0) spaces via the κ-stereographic model, where each layer l has its own learnable curvature κ^l. The Learnable Space Projection (LSP) module projects node representations into the optimal space per layer using exp/log maps parameterized by the learned κ, motivated by an empirical 'Expansion Rate' metric showing different node triplets benefit from different curvatures. The Distance Aware Propagation (DAP) module computes edge weights ω_ij^κ via an MLP over node features and an approximated κ-dependent distance (derived from a Taylor expansion in Theorem 2), implementing a weighted homogeneity principle that amplifies intra-class information while suppressing noisy inter-class signals. The Multiple Space Ensemble (MulSE) module combines the three GNN outputs with learnable coefficients α and β, and Propositions 1–2 prove that this ensemble reduces cross-entropy loss relative to any single-space model, providing a theoretically grounded alternative to data augmentation under label scarcity.

## Results

On 9 real-world NAD datasets (50/50 train/val split), SpaceGNN achieves an average AUC of 0.7833 and F1 of 0.6573, outperforming the best rival (CONSISGAD) by 8.55% in AUC and 4.31% in F1 on average. Notable individual results include 0.9389 AUC / 0.8541 F1 on Weibo, 0.9392 AUC / 0.7571 F1 on T-Social, and 0.9331 AUC / 0.8935 F1 on Amazon. SpaceGNN also handles large-scale graphs (DGraph-Fin with 3.7M nodes, T-Social with 5.8M nodes) where several baselines (HYLA, GAGA) fail with OOM or TLE errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpaceGNN consistently outperforms all 16 baselines on 8 out of 9 datasets (tied on Reddit F1 where all methods converge to the majority-class baseline of 0.4915). Against the most direct competitor CONSISGAD (pseudo-label-based, ICLR 2024), SpaceGNN leads by 8.55% AUC and 4.31% F1 on average, with the largest gap on Weibo (0.9389 vs 0.7195 AUC) and T-Social (0.9392 vs 0.9129 AUC). Against generalized GNNs, SpaceGNN surpasses HGCN by 17.13% AUC and HYLA by 20.63% AUC on average, demonstrating that single-space non-Euclidean encoding is insufficient. Among specialized NAD models, SpaceGNN beats XGBGraph by 11.54% AUC, BWGNN by 12.75% AUC, and GHRN by 12.77% AUC on average. On large-scale graphs (DGraph-Fin, T-Social), GAGA times out (>72h) and HYLA runs out of memory, while SpaceGNN completes successfully with competitive scores (0.6548 AUC on DGraph-Fin, 0.9392 AUC on T-Social). The only notable exception is Amazon F1, where BWGNN (0.9006) and XGBGraph (0.9231) slightly exceed SpaceGNN (0.8935), though SpaceGNN still leads on AUC (0.9331).

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaceGNN builds directly on the κ-stereographic model from Bachmann et al. (2020), which unified Euclidean, hyperbolic, and spherical geometries under a single curvature parameter, and extends it by making the curvature learnable per layer rather than fixed. It departs from prior NAD-specific GNNs (GDN, SparseGAD, GAGA, XGBGraph, AMNet, BWGNN, GHRN) that operate exclusively in Euclidean space, and from non-Euclidean GNNs (HGCN, HYLA) that commit to a single fixed curvature, by ensembling GNNs across multiple simultaneously learned curvatures. Most notably, it challenges the recent pseudo-labeling approach of CONSISGAD for handling limited supervision, arguing that model augmentation via multi-space ensemble is a safer and more effective alternative to data augmentation in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded answer to the practical problem of NAD with extremely limited labels, showing that ensemble of multi-space GNNs outperforms both single-space models and pseudo-label-based data augmentation. The finding that CONSISGAD's generated anomalous pseudo-labels have high false rates (Figure 4) and that this actively degrades performance is a useful caution for the broader semi-supervised graph learning community. Open questions include whether the learned curvatures (which are small, ~0.07–0.13, suggesting the benefit is a mild geometric adjustment rather than a dramatic space change) generalize to other graph tasks beyond NAD, and whether the ensemble approach scales efficiently to graphs with tens of millions of nodes.
