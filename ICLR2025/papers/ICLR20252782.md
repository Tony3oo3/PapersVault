## Problem

Rate-encoded spiking neural networks (SNNs) offer inherent adversarial robustness from Bernoulli encoding noise but suffer poor clean accuracy due to the randomness introduced by the encoding. Standard rate encoding restricts inputs to [0,1]^d, preventing mean-centering that would reduce this randomness. Additionally, no efficient adversarial attack exists in the discrete encoding space of SNNs, limiting the ability to perform meaningful adversarial training in that space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252782/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32×3 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 224×224×3 for ImageNet-100), mean-centers them to [-1,1]^d, and encodes each pixel into T independent signed Bernoulli spikes in {-1,0,1}^{d×T}. The SNN (VGGSNN or SEW-ResNet-34) processes these spikes over T time steps using LIF dynamics, and the output is a class probability distribution computed as the temporal average of the final layer's output across the latency period.

## Method

The paper introduces Signed Rate Encoding (SRATE), which extends rate encoding to negative inputs via a signed Bernoulli (sBer) distribution: positive pixels generate {0,1} spikes and negative pixels generate {0,-1} spikes, enabling mean-centering that provably reduces encoding randomness (quantified via expected l0-distance between two independent encodings). The second contribution is the Sparse Encoding Attack (SEA), an l0-norm restricted adversarial attack in the discrete encoding space that, given a sparsity budget k per frame, selects the top-k coordinates by gradient magnitude to flip or set spikes, with proven optimality under first-order loss approximation. SEA is adapted to both standard {0,1} and signed {-1,0,1} encoding spaces, and its sparsity budget is calibrated to be comparable with l∞-norm input-space attacks (e.g., k=10 ≈ ε=8/255). Adversarial training with SEA under SRATE yields superior robust accuracy across FGSM, PGD, and SEA attacks on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN, while SRATE also improves clean accuracy by 2–4% over standard rate encoding.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (T=4), signed rate encoding (SRATE) improves clean accuracy from 78.79% (RATE) to 82.05%, a gain of 3.26%. Adversarial training with the proposed Sparse Encoding Attack (SEA) under SRATE achieves 80.18% robust accuracy under SEA (k=10) and 75.77% under SEA (k=20), substantially outperforming FGSM (47.37%/34.67%) and PGD (46.94%/34.52%) adversarial training under the same attacks. On SVHN, the SEA+SRATE model reaches 92.27% under SEA (k=10) versus 54.06% for the RATE counterpart. The average robust accuracy across all attacks for SEA+SRATE on CIFAR-10 is 70.25%, compared to 54.74% for SEA+RATE and 56.34% for PGD+RATE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed SEA adversarial training against FGSM and PGD adversarial training (both l∞-norm input-space attacks) as baselines. On CIFAR-10, SEA+SRATE achieves 80.18% robust accuracy under SEA (k=10), outperforming FGSM-trained (47.37%) and PGD-trained (46.94%) models by over 30 percentage points under the same attack. Under FGSM and PGD attacks, SEA+SRATE also leads: 56.58% vs. 55.10% (FGSM-trained) and 51.87% vs. 50.21% (PGD-trained). The equivalent sparsity budget analysis (Section 4.3) shows that ε=8/255 in input space corresponds to k≈10 for RATE but only k≈4 for SRATE, explaining why SEA+RATE (trained at k=5) is less robust than SEA+SRATE (also trained at k=5) under k=10 attacks. SRATE consistently improves clean accuracy over RATE by 2–4% across all datasets (CIFAR-10: +3.26%, CIFAR-100: +4.18%, SVHN: +3.76%), while the SEA+SRATE combination yields the highest average robust accuracy (70.25% on CIFAR-10, 79.79% on SVHN, 45.28% on CIFAR-100) among all tested configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on rate-encoded spiking neural networks (Sharmin et al., 2020; Mukhoty et al., 2024), which established that Bernoulli-based rate encoding confers inherent adversarial robustness at the cost of clean accuracy. It departs from prior encoding attacks (Liang et al., 2021; Marchisio et al., 2021) by formulating a principled l0-norm sparse attack directly in the discrete spike space with a provable optimality guarantee under first-order loss approximation, rather than relying on gradient-based perturbations in the continuous input space. The signed Bernoulli distribution introduced here generalizes the standard Bernoulli to accommodate negative inputs, enabling mean-centering—a standard normalization trick unavailable to conventional rate encoding due to its [0,1] domain restriction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of signed rate encoding and SEA-based adversarial training offers a practical recipe for SNNs that simultaneously improves clean accuracy (~3–4% on CIFAR-10/100, SVHN) and achieves substantially higher robust accuracy than FGSM/PGD-trained counterparts, which is valuable for neuromorphic deployments where the encoder is external to the model. However, the paper acknowledges that clean accuracy still lags behind state-of-the-art rate-encoding models, and the robustness gains are most dramatic under the SEA attack itself, raising questions about transferability to other attack families at matched budgets. Open questions include whether the signed Bernoulli framework extends to higher-dimensional or multi-channel encodings, whether the optimality guarantee holds beyond first-order approximation for deeper networks, and whether the approach scales to full ImageNet without the SEW-ResNet shortcut.
