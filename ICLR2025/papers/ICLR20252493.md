## Problem

Existing speech enhancement approaches, whether deterministic or generative, largely ignore the rich semantic information (phonetics, syntax, contextual meaning) that humans naturally exploit to reconstruct speech in noisy environments. While LM-based SE methods like SELM have begun using discrete semantic tokens, they lack acoustic tokens for high-fidelity reconstruction, and conventional multi-quantizer codecs produce long token sequences that are computationally expensive and difficult for LMs to predict autoregressively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252493/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a 16 kHz noisy speech signal (with optional reverberation) as input and produces an enhanced 16 kHz waveform. Internally, the noisy signal is tokenized into two streams: semantic tokens at 50 Hz (1024-cluster k-means indices from XLSR) and acoustic tokens at 100 Hz (single-quantizer indices from SimCodec with an 8192-entry codebook). The LM generates clean semantic tokens followed by clean acoustic tokens, which are decoded by SimCodec's waveform decoder into the final enhanced signal.

## Method

GenSE introduces a hierarchical two-stage LM framework that decouples denoising from generation: a N2S module autoregressively transforms noisy semantic tokens into clean ones, and an S2S module then generates clean acoustic tokens conditioned on a token chain prompt (noisy semantic tokens + clean semantic tokens + noisy acoustic tokens) to preserve speaker timbre. To make single-quantizer acoustic tokenization viable, the paper proposes SimCodec, which uses a two-stage training procedure with a codebook reorganization step: two group quantizers are first trained, then their top-used embeddings are pairwise concatenated into a single 8192-entry codebook, achieving 1.3 kbps reconstruction quality with only 100 tokens/s. The entire system is trained with decoder-only LMs using next-token prediction, with the N2S and S2S modules trained separately to reduce prediction difficulty and improve stability.

## Results

On the DNS Challenge test set (without reverb), GenSE achieves DNSMOS OVL of 3.43, SECS of 0.67, and VQScore of 0.717, outperforming the strongest baseline DOSE (OVL 3.31, SECS 0.61, VQ 0.692) by +17.1%, +9.8%, and +3.6% respectively. With reverb, GenSE reaches OVL 3.19 and SECS 0.65, again leading all baselines. On the out-of-domain CHiME-4 dataset, GenSE achieves OVL 2.89, SECS 0.62, and WER 28.4%, demonstrating superior generalization compared to deterministic baselines that degrade sharply under domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenSE outperforms all seven speech enhancement baselines across every metric on both the DNS Challenge and CHiME-4 test sets. Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline DOSE, GenSE gains +17.1% in DNSMOS OVL (no reverb) and +9.8% in SECS, while also achieving the highest VQScore (0.717 vs. 0.692). The prior LM-based approach SELM, which uses only semantic tokens without acoustic tokens or hierarchical modeling, trails GenSE by +9.3% OVL and +8.1% SECS, confirming the benefit of the proposed hierarchical N2S+S2S architecture and token chain prompting. On the out-of-domain CHiME-4 set, deterministic baselines (FullSubNet, Inter-Subnet) degrade severely (OVL drops to ~2.0–2.1), while GenSE maintains OVL 2.89 and achieves the lowest WER (28.4% vs. 31.2% for DOSE), highlighting its robustness to unseen noise. In the codec comparison (Table 3), SimCodec at 1.3 kbps with a single quantizer (100 tokens/s) achieves PESQ 3.05 and UTMOS 3.37, surpassing DAC at 1.0 kbps (PESQ 1.32) by a large margin and matching or exceeding multi-quantizer codecs operating at 3–6 kbps.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenSE extends the AudioLM/VALL-E paradigm of hierarchical semantic-to-acoustic token generation into the speech enhancement domain, building directly on SELM (the first LM-based SE system using semantic tokens) while adding acoustic tokens and a two-stage denoising/generation decomposition. It departs from the dominant generative SE literature (diffusion, GAN, VAE) by reframing SE as conditional discrete-token language modeling rather than continuous signal regression, and from multi-quantizer neural codecs (EnCodec, DAC) by proposing a single-quantizer SimCodec with a codebook reorganization process that reduces the temporal token count the LM must predict. The hierarchical N2S→S2S structure and token chain prompting mechanism are novel design choices that address the specific challenge of noise corrupting both semantic and acoustic representations simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LM-based generative SE can outperform strong diffusion-based baselines (StoRM, DOSE) in both quality and, critically, generalization to unseen noise domains (CHiME-4), suggesting that discrete semantic modeling provides a more robust inductive bias than continuous signal regression. The single-quantizer SimCodec with codebook reorganization is a practical contribution that reduces LM prediction complexity by an order of magnitude in token count while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality. Key open questions include real-time inference (autoregressive generation is a bottleneck, with the authors proposing alternating token prediction as a fix), scaling to larger and more diverse training data, and whether the hierarchical decomposition generalizes to other speech restoration tasks beyond enhancement.
