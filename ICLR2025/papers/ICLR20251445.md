## Problem

For the single-index regression problem min_x ||f(Ax) − b||_p with a Lipschitz function f, prior work only achieved constant-factor approximations, whereas the canonical case f(x)=x admits (1+ε)-approximation guarantees. This paper provides the first (1+ε)-approximation algorithm for the general Lipschitz single-index model and proves matching lower bounds showing the query complexity is near-optimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251445/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives the full matrix A ∈ R^{n×d} and the Lipschitz function f, but b ∈ R^n is only accessible through individual entry queries (the core resource to minimize). The output x̂ satisfies ||f(Ax̂) − b||_p^p ≤ (1+ε)·OPT + ε·||Ax*||_p^p with probability ≥ 0.9, where x* is the minimum-norm minimizer. The query complexity is Õ(d^{p/2∨1}/ε^{p∨2}), independent of n, achieved via a two-stage sampling scheme that first reduces the effective dimension from n to poly(d/ε) rows.

## Method

The algorithm uses Lewis-weights-based row sampling to construct a subspace embedding matrix S, then solves a regularized problem min_x ||S(f(Ax)−b)||_p^p + ε·||Ax||_p^p where the regularization term ε·||Ax||_p^p serves a dual purpose: it controls the additive error in the final guarantee and bounds the norm ||Ax̂||_p^p to keep the analysis domain T small. The key technical contribution is an iterative bootstrapping argument: a concentration bound (via Dudley's integral over T) yields an error estimate, which is plugged back into the norm bound to shrink T, which in turn tightens the concentration bound, repeating for O(log log 1/ε) iterations to achieve the optimal ε-dependence. A two-stage sampling design (S° followed by S) removes the log n factor by first reducing the problem to poly(d/ε) rows. Lower bounds are established via Yao's minimax theorem using hard-instance distributions that encode a distinguishing problem requiring Ω(d/ε^{p∨2}) queries.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 1) shows that a (1+ε)-approximation to the single-index regression problem min_x ‖f(Ax)−b‖_p can be achieved by querying Õ(d^(1∨p/2) / ε^(2∨p)) entries of b, where f is L-Lipschitz. This is the first (1+ε)-approximation for the single-index model; all prior work only achieved constant-factor approximations. Matching lower bounds (Theorems 2–3) show Ω(d/ε²) queries are necessary for 1≤p≤2 and Ω(d/ε^p) for p>2, establishing tightness up to logarithmic factors for p∈[1,2] and tight ε-dependence for p>2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the canonical active regression problem (f(x)=x), accommodating a general Lipschitz f increases the ε-dependence from 1/ε to 1/ε² for p∈[1,2] and from 1/ε^{p−1} to 1/ε^p for p>2, while the d-dependence remains the same. Against prior single-index results, this paper improves the approximation guarantee from constant-factor to (1+ε) while matching or improving the query complexity: for p=2, it matches the Õ(d/ε²) of Gajjar et al. (2024) but with a stronger (1+ε) guarantee; for general p, it improves the ε-dependence from 1/ε⁴ (Huang et al., 2024) to 1/ε^{p∨2}. The lower bounds confirm that the 1/ε² dependence for p∈[1,2] is optimal up to log factors, and the 1/ε^p dependence for p>2 is optimal in ε, though the full d^{p/2} dependence for p>2 remains an open problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the active regression literature from the canonical linear case (settled by Musco et al. 2022 and Yasuda 2024) to the single-index model with a general Lipschitz nonlinearity f. Prior single-index results (Gajjar et al. 2023a; Huang et al. 2024; Gajjar et al. 2024) achieved only constant-factor approximations, and Gajjar et al. 2023a proved that the 'natural' (1+ε) guarantee is impossible with poly(d) queries. This paper circumvents that impossibility by adopting a weaker additive error term ε‖Ax*‖_p^p and a regularized regression formulation (borrowed from Gajjar et al. 2024), combined with an iterative bootstrapping argument over a two-parameter domain to achieve the first (1+ε)-approximation with Õ(d^{p/2∨1}/ε^{p∨2}) queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a fundamental gap in the theory of active learning for single-index models, which arise naturally in the training of neural network prediction layers. The matching lower bounds (tight up to log factors for p∈[1,2], and tight in ε-dependence for p>2) establish that the query complexity is essentially optimal, giving a complete picture of the problem's difficulty. The main open question is the full tightness of the d^{p/2} dependence for p>2, where the canonical case has a stronger lower bound (Yasuda 2024) that has not yet been extended to the single-index setting. As a purely theoretical paper with no experiments, its practical impact is indirect but foundational for understanding the limits of query-efficient learning in non-convex regression.
