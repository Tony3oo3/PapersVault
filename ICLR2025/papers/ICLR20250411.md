## Problem

Transformer-based end-to-end RF sensing (e.g., RadarFormer) incurs quadratic complexity on long-sequence radar echoes, while State Space Models offer linear-complexity alternatives but have never been adapted to RF signals. The adaptation is non-trivial because RF echoes are complex-valued (amplitude + phase), exhibit scenario-dependent frequency responses (broadband in free space vs. low-frequency-concentrated under wall occlusion), and suffer from specular reflection that captures only partial body limbs in a single frame.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250411/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 12 consecutive complex-valued SFCW radar echoes of shape C^(144×201) (144 virtual MIMO antennas × 201 frequency steps at 0.8–2.8 GHz), which are merged into a 2D patch layout (32×39 patches) rather than flattened to 1D, preserving the antenna–frequency spatial structure. It jointly predicts 10 3D body keypoints (MPJPE), one of 19 action categories, and one of 10 person identities, all from the same shared encoder via a lightweight multi-task head.

## Method

RFMamba introduces an RF-SSM block with two parallel branches. The frequency modeling branch applies a six-way omni-dimensional scanning strategy (forward and backward along the antenna, fast-time, and slow-time dimensions) separately to the amplitude and phase spectrograms, then recombines them via a complex linear projection; a Frequency Adaptive Feed-Forward Network (FA-FFN) with a learnable weight matrix W_fft adaptively gates informative frequency components. The spatiotemporal branch converts echoes to spatial heatmaps via 1D IFFT (range) and 1D FFT (azimuth/elevation), stacks 12 frames, and applies a spatiotemporal Mamba scan to extract distance, angle, and velocity features. The two branches are concatenated and fused with a residual connection. A 3D patch embedding with separate learnable spatial and temporal position encodings feeds a two-stage encoder (complex conv down-sampling + RF-SSM blocks), and the whole network is trained end-to-end with a joint loss of MPJPE plus cross-entropy for action and ReID.

## Results

On the THP-W through-wall dataset, RFMamba achieves a mean per-joint position error (MPJPE) of 50.64 mm for 3D pose estimation, substantially outperforming all baselines (RFPose3D: 85.35 mm, ResNet3D-50: 105.34 mm, mm-Pose: 165.98 mm, RadarFormer: 258.90 mm). For action recognition, RFMamba reaches 99.97% accuracy and 0.9994 F1 score, and for person ReID it achieves 0.9991 mAP and 0.9967 CMC-1. Notably, RFMamba uses only 1.94M trainable parameters, compared to 10.91M–352.30M for the baselines. On the free-space THP-F subset, the model generalizes to 60.69 mm MPJPE with perfect 1.0 mAP and F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFMamba outperforms all four baselines across every task it is evaluated on. Against RFPose3D (Zhao et al., 2018), the strongest prior pose method, RFMamba reduces MPJPE from 85.35 mm to 50.64 mm (−40.7%), while using roughly 5.6× fewer parameters. Compared to ResNet3D-50 (Hara et al., 2018), RFMamba improves pose error by 51.9 mm, action accuracy by 1.26 percentage points, and ReID mAP by 14.16 percentage points, all while being 181× smaller in parameter count. Against mm-Pose (Sengupta et al., 2020), RFMamba cuts pose error by more than half (165.98 → 50.64 mm). The most dramatic gap is versus RadarFormer (Zheng et al., 2023), the first end-to-end Transformer for through-wall radar: RFMamba reduces MPJPE by 208.26 mm (258.90 → 50.64), lifts action F1 from 0.7020 to 0.9994, and raises ReID mAP from 0.4817 to 0.9991, demonstrating that the SSM-based architecture with omni-dimensional scanning captures long-range temporal and frequency dependencies that the two-frame Transformer cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba selective state space model (Gu & Dao, 2024) and its vision adaptation (Zhu et al., 2024) to the fundamentally different domain of RF-based human-centric perception, where signals carry coupled amplitude and phase information across three physical dimensions (fast-time/range, antenna/angle, slow-time/velocity). It departs from the Transformer-based RadarFormer (Zheng et al., 2023b), the first end-to-end through-wall radar network, by replacing quadratic-complexity self-attention with linear-complexity SSM scanning, and from multi-stage RF pipelines (RFPose3D, mm-Pose) by operating directly on raw complex-valued echoes. The key conceptual departure is the omni-dimensional scanning strategy, which generalizes the bidirectional 1D/2D scanning of prior Mamba variants to six directions across three RF-specific axes, and the dual-branch RF-SSM block that jointly models frequency-domain and spatial-domain representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the THP dataset (351K RF-frame pairs with 3D keypoints, identity, and action labels across wall-occlusion and free-space settings) fills a critical gap, as prior RF sensing datasets predominantly cover free-space scenarios and lack through-wall ground truth. The 1.94M-parameter model achieving 50.64 mm MPJPE (vs. 258.90 mm for RadarFormer) with 180× fewer parameters than ResNet3D-50 suggests SSMs could become the default backbone for long-sequence RF sensing, enabling deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. Open questions include whether the omni-dimensional scanning strategy generalizes to other 3D signal modalities (e.g., 4D imaging radar, UWB), how performance degrades with more than three concurrent subjects, and whether the approach transfers to different radar hardware (FMCW vs. SFCW) without retraining.
