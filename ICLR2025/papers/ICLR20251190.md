## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods designed for CNNs and Transformers (e.g., QuaRot with Hadamard rotation) suffer severe accuracy drops when applied to Mamba models—up to 21% on Vim-T even at W8A8. The paper identifies three root causes: significant outliers in gate/output projections and matrix multiplications, amplification of these outliers by the Parallel Scan operator, and the inability of Hadamard transforms to equalize channel variances due to their fixed orthogonal structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained Mamba model weights (Vim, Mamba-ND, Mamba-LLM) and a small calibration set (128 ImageNet images for vision, HellaSwag samples for language) to compute the KLT eigenvector matrix offline. It outputs a quantized model at W8A8 or W4A8 with only ~0.01% additional parameters (smoothing scalars fused into existing weights) and ~0.91% extra FLOPs from online Hadamard transforms. For vision tasks, static quantization is used; for language tasks, dynamic quantization adapts to varying input structures at inference time.

## Method

MambaQuant is a PTQ framework built around two rotation strategies that fix Hadamard's failure to equalize channel variances. In the offline mode, KLT-Enhanced Rotation multiplies the Hadamard matrix H by the KLT eigenvector matrix K (from eigenvalue decomposition of the calibration covariance), yielding H_K = KH; this guarantees uniform per-channel variance because H^T Λ H produces identical diagonal entries (1/((n-1)m)·Σλ_j). In the online mode, Smooth-Fused Rotation introduces a per-channel smoothing vector before Hadamard rotation: for the output projection, a Smooth SiLU (S-SiLU) fuses the smoothing factor into gate and output projection weights; for the matrix multiplication, the smoothing factor is absorbed into C-projection weights, B-projection weights, and Δ (via an addcmul operation on the first token to account for the exponential in PScan). The Hadamard matrix is then applied online to activations while its transpose is absorbed into corresponding weights, preserving computational invariance.

## Results

On vision tasks (ImageNet/UCF-101), MambaQuant quantizes Vim and Mamba-ND models to W8A8 with less than 1% accuracy loss (e.g., Vim-T†: 77.8% vs. 78.3% FP16; Vim-S: 80.3% vs. 80.5% FP16; Mamba-3d: 89.0% vs. 89.6% FP16). Under the stricter W4A8 setting, MambaQuant retains 73.7% on Vim-T† (vs. 78.3% FP16), substantially outperforming QuaRot (48.5%) and RTN (25.0%). On language tasks (average of ARC-E, ARC-C, PIQA, Winogrande, HellaSwag), MambaQuant achieves 62.1% on Mamba-2.8b at W8A8 (vs. 62.2% FP16) and 58.5% at W4A8, matching or exceeding QuaRot across all model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaQuant decisively outperforms all four baselines across every model and bit-width setting. Against RTN, GPTQ+RTN, and SmoothQuant, the gains are dramatic: on Vim-T† at W8A8, MambaQuant scores 77.8% versus 32.3–32.5% for these three methods (a gap of ~45 percentage points), and at W4A8 the gap widens to ~48 points (73.7% vs. 25.0–27.9%). QuaRot is the strongest baseline, leveraging Hadamard rotation, yet MambaQuant still surpasses it by 20.4 points on Vim-T† (W8A8: 77.8% vs. 57.4%) and by 25.2 points at W4A8 (73.7% vs. 48.5%). On language models (Table 2), MambaQuant leads QuaRot by 1.2 points on Mamba-370m (W8A8: 50.0% vs. 48.8%) and by 0.5 points on Mamba-2.8b (W4A8: 58.5% vs. 58.5%, a tie), while maintaining near-FP16 accuracy at W8A8 across all sizes. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that the KLT-enhanced rotation contributes ~15 points over plain Hadamard on Vim-T† (W8A8), and the smooth-fused rotation adds another ~30 points, together closing the gap to FP16.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hadamard-rotation-based quantization paradigm established by QuaRot and QuIP# for Transformer LLMs to the Mamba family of state-space models. It departs from prior work by identifying a fundamental limitation: the fixed Hadamard matrix cannot align channel variances when the data's eigenvalue spectrum is input-dependent (as in Mamba's PScan-amplified activations), a problem absent in the more uniform attention-based distributions of Transformers. The KLT-enhanced rotation (offline) and smooth-fused rotation with S-SiLU (online) are novel adaptations that incorporate data-dependent eigendecomposition and architecture-aware parameter fusion, respectively, drawing on SmoothQuant's smoothing philosophy and I-LLM's S-SiLU activation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Mamba-based models gain traction as efficient alternatives to Transformers for vision, language, and multimodal tasks, a reliable quantization pathway is essential for edge deployment. This paper provides the first comprehensive PTQ framework for the Mamba family, achieving <1% accuracy loss at W8A8 across multiple architectures. Key limitations remain: 4-bit weight quantization still incurs ~1% drops (more for vision), and the KLT-enhanced rotation is currently restricted to offline use due to eigenvalue decomposition cost. An open question is whether the KLT approach can be made online-compatible, and whether the variance-alignment principle generalizes to next-generation SSM variants (e.g., Mamba-2 with structured attention).
