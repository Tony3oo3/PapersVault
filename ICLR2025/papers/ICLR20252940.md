## Problem

Standard GPFA requires pre-specifying the number of latent factors and assumes all factors contribute uniformly across all time points, making it unable to capture temporally varying, compositional neural encoding. Existing alternatives like ARD-based Bayesian GPFA select a single set of factors via marginalisation over all observations, overlooking observation-specific variability in which factors are active. The paper addresses this by enabling simultaneous posterior inference over both the number of latent factors and the identity of active factors at each time point.

## Input / Output

The model takes population spike counts binned into fixed time windows (30 ms for the neural data) as input, with a fixed number of neurons (e.g., 204 CA1 place cells) observed over a continuous recording (e.g., 2187 s). It produces latent GP factor trajectories with temporal smoothing, a binary activation matrix Z indicating which factors are expressed at each timestep, posterior responsibilities per factor per timestep, and posterior predictive firing rates. The activation matrix is the key novel output: it is a dense N×D matrix of Bernoulli variational parameters that modulate the contribution of each latent factor at each time point.

## Method

Infinite GPFA extends standard GPFA by placing an Indian Buffet Process (IBP) prior, via its stick-breaking construction, over a binary factor-activation matrix Z that gates the loading of each latent GP factor into the neural space at each timestep. The model is fitted with sparse variational inference using inducing points for scalability, and a Gamma prior on the IBP concentration parameter α enables fully Bayesian inference over the effective latent dimension. Because the binary mask Z makes q(h) non-Gaussian, the expected Poisson log-likelihood is approximated via a second-order Taylor expansion of exp(h), and the intractable expected log-product term in the IBP cross-entropy is lower-bounded using Jensen's inequality with an auxiliary categorical distribution. Learning proceeds via variational EM with gradient-based updates on the free energy objective, alternating between updating variational parameters (inducing point posteriors, stick-breaking weights, activation probabilities) and generative parameters (loading matrix, offset, kernel hyperparameters).

## Results

On synthetic data with stochastic factor loading, infinite svGPFA achieves a mean squared error of 0.0043 ± 0.025 for predicted log-firing rates, compared to 0.40 ± 0.87 for standard svGPFA — a roughly 100× reduction. The R² score between posterior latent means and ground-truth latents is significantly higher for infinite svGPFA than for standard svGPFA (one-sided t-test, p = 0.0028) and bGPFA (p = 2.17 × 10⁻¹⁶). On the Pfeiffer–Foster hippocampal place cell dataset, infinite svGPFA yields higher variational free energy and stronger canonical correlations with behavioural variables (position, speed, head direction) than both baselines, and its posterior predictive firing-rate estimates are significantly more accurate than neuron-specific spatial ratemaps alone (p = 3.84 × 10⁻⁶). The model infers an effective latent dimension of approximately 6, consistent with prior reports on CA1 place cell manifolds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares infinite svGPFA against two baselines: standard sparse-variational GPFA (svGPFA) and Bayesian GPFA with automatic relevance determination (bGPFA; Jensen et al., 2021). On synthetic data with stochastic factor loading, infinite svGPFA dramatically outperforms standard svGPFA in log-rate prediction (MSE 0.0043 vs. 0.40) and latent recovery (R², p = 0.0028), because standard svGPFA lacks any mechanism to account for periods when a latent factor is inactive, leading to erroneous gradient updates. Against bGPFA, infinite svGPFA achieves significantly higher R² (p = 2.17 × 10⁻¹⁶) and more stable training; bGPFA's circulant covariance approximation causes its fitted latents to degrade quickly as dimension increases and incurs substantially higher per-iteration computational cost. On the real hippocampal place cell dataset, infinite svGPFA attains the highest variational free energy and the strongest canonical correlations with allocentric position, speed, and head direction, while also discovering behaviourally meaningful switching dynamics (e.g., a latent factor selectively active at the home well during both homing and late foraging phases) that neither baseline captures. The key advantage over bGPFA is that infinite svGPFA infers observation-specific factor activations via the IBP prior, whereas bGPFA's ARD selects a single global set of relevant factors by marginalising over all observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GPFA (Yu et al., 2008) by incorporating an Indian Buffet Process prior (Ghahramani and Griffiths, 2005) over the factor loading matrix, enabling simultaneous inference over the number of latent factors and their observation-specific activations. It departs from bGPFA (Jensen et al., 2021), which uses ARD to select a single set of factors marginalised over all observations, by allowing the active factor set to vary per timestep. The sparse variational GP framework (Titsias, 2009) and the mean-field variational inference for IBP (Doshi et al., 2009) provide the tractable inference machinery, while the compositional loading mechanism distinguishes the model from switching linear dynamical systems (Linderman et al., 2017) that assume categorical context switching rather than graded factor activation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model addresses a fundamental limitation in neural manifold analysis: the assumption that a fixed set of latent factors contributes uniformly across all timesteps. By discovering that hippocampal place cell populations exhibit temporally compositional encoding—where specific latent factors activate selectively during foraging versus homing phases—the paper provides a data-driven mechanism for interpreting the 'dynamic grouping effect' and goal-oriented replay without pre-specifying behavioural variables. Key limitations include the finite upper bound on latent factors required by variational inference (preventing truly infinite factor discovery), the reliance on second-order Taylor approximation for non-Gaussian likelihoods, and evaluation on a single recording session. Open questions include whether the approach generalises to larger-scale recordings, multi-trial analyses, and whether the compositional representation captures the full complexity of internal-state-dependent neural dynamics.
