## Problem

Machine unlearning in Random Forests has received insufficient attention compared to neural networks and other model families, and existing RF-specific methods (DaRE, HedgeCut) are limited to small removal fractions, lack batch unlearning support, or suffer from high per-instance latency. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a framework that achieves exact unlearning with orders-of-magnitude speedups across sequential, batch, and online mixed data stream settings.

## Input / Output

The system consumes tabular feature vectors (d-dimensional with categorical attributes one-hot encoded) and operates as an online interface handling three interleaved request types: sample addition, sample removal (unlearning), and prediction queries, with no prior knowledge of future requests. For queries it returns a class label or regression value aggregated across the forest; for modifications it updates internal tree structures and split statistics in-place. The unlearning guarantee is exact in the distributional sense: the unlearned model is statistically indistinguishable from one retrained from scratch on the reduced dataset.

## Method

DYNFRS combines three mechanisms operating at different granularities. OCC(q) subsampling assigns each training sample to only ⌈qT⌉ of T trees (chosen uniformly at random), so unlearning a sample touches a fraction q of the forest, yielding an expected 1/q² speedup over naive retraining while preserving or improving ensemble accuracy. The LZY lazy tag strategy exploits the fact that a modification request affects only a root-to-leaf path plus one subtree, while a query observes only a single path: when a best-split change is detected at a node, a tag is placed and the subtree is deleted, but actual reconstruction is deferred until a query traverses that node, at which point only the query-relevant path is regrown and the tag is pushed to children. This automatically batches multiple modifications between queries. The base learner is Extremely Randomized Trees, which restrict each node to s random candidate thresholds per attribute (typically 5–30), making splits far more robust to individual sample changes and enabling O(ps) split-change detection via cached statistics instead of O(p|S_u|) full re-evaluation.

## Results

On predictive performance, DYNFRS(q=0.2) achieves the best accuracy/AUC-ROC in 6 out of 9 binary classification datasets (e.g., 0.8650 on Adult, 0.7475 on Higgs), while DYNFRS(q=0.1) matches DaRE and scikit-learn RF. In sequential unlearning, DYNFRS achieves a 22×–523× speedup over DaRE and a 4,000×–1,500,000× speedup over naive retraining. In the online mixed data stream setting on the 8.8M-sample Higgs dataset, DYNFRS sustains an average latency of 0.12 ms for modification requests and 1.3 ms for queries (single-threaded), with query latency dropping to 0.07 ms when modification requests are sparse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DaRE (Brophy & Lowd, ICML 2021), DYNFRS achieves a 22×–523× speedup in sequential unlearning and completes batch unlearning of 0.1%–1% of the training set in a fraction of the time (e.g., 39.2 s vs. infeasible on Higgs for 0.1% removal); DYNFRS(q=0.2) also matches or exceeds DaRE's predictive accuracy on 7 of 9 datasets. Against HedgeCut (Schelter et al., SIGMOD 2021), DYNFRS dominates on every metric: HedgeCut cannot unlearn more than 0.1% of the dataset, cannot be trained on Synthetic or Higgs due to O(pn²) memory blowup, and is 10–100× slower on single-sample unlearning (e.g., 1245 ms vs. 0.40 ms on Purchase). Against OnlineBoosting (Lin et al., KDD 2023), DYNFRS is the only method that excels in both sequential and batch unlearning: OnlineBoosting achieves a boost below 10 in sequential settings and requires 73,832 ms to unlearn one sample on Higgs versus DYNFRS's 1.60 ms, though OnlineBoosting edges out DYNFRS on predictive accuracy for Vaccine (0.8012 vs. 0.7934) and Bank (0.9436 vs. 0.9436, tied). In the online mixed data stream setting, DYNFRS is the only tree-based method that simultaneously handles additions, removals, and queries, sustaining sub-millisecond modification latency where DaRE requires ~180 ms per unlearn.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RF unlearning work initiated by HedgeCut (Schelter et al., 2021) and DaRE (Brophy and Lowd, 2021), which introduced robustness-based split preservation and random-split caching respectively, but were limited to sequential unlearning of small fractions of data. DYNFRS departs from these by introducing OCC(q) subsampling (contrary to DaRE's claim that subsampling is irrelevant) and the LZY lazy-tag strategy that amortizes subtree retraining into query-time path construction, a mechanism absent in all prior tree-based unlearning methods. It also draws on the ERT variant (Geurts et al., 2006) as a more unlearning-robust base learner, and positions against GBDT-specific methods like OnlineBoosting (Lin et al., 2023) that cannot be directly transferred to RFs due to fundamentally different training mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DYNFRS is the first RF unlearning framework that simultaneously supports sequential unlearning, batch unlearning, sample addition (continual learning), and online mixed data streams with microsecond-level latency, making it the only tree-based model applicable to real-time GDPR/CCPA compliance scenarios. The 4000–1,500,000× speedup over naive retraining and 22–523× over DaRE are backed by consistent results across 9 datasets up to 8.8M samples, lending strong empirical credibility. Open questions include whether the lazy-tag amortization strategy generalizes to other ensemble architectures (e.g., gradient-boosted forests with different tree interactions), how performance degrades under adversarial or correlated unlearning request patterns, and whether the OCC(q) accuracy benefit observed in binary classification persists in high-dimensional or imbalanced multi-class settings.
