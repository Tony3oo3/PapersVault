## Problem

The standard two-stage paradigm for math LLMs—CPT with general math corpora followed by SFT with problem-solving data—yields disproportionately small gains from CPT relative to SFT. This paper investigates whether substituting problem-solving data for general math corpora during CPT can close that gap, and systematically characterizes how the two training stages differ in their capacity to acquire mathematical reasoning from the same data.

## Input / Output

The model receives math word problems via zero-shot or few-shot Chain-of-Thought prompts and generates step-by-step reasoning solutions terminating in a final numerical answer, with an answer-comparison model handling irregular base-model outputs. Training data comprises three tiers: 48.3B tokens of general corpus (RedPajama, AlgebraicStack, StarCoder, C4, Wikipedia), 13.7B tokens of mathematical corpus (OpenWebMath), and 7.2B tokens of problem-solving data (NuminaMath, Lila, proprietary), with synthetic augmentation adding up to 30.54B further tokens across four synthesis methods.

## Method

The paper runs controlled CPT and SFT experiments on Llama2-7B to isolate the effects of data type, synthesis method, data distribution, and difficulty level on mathematical reasoning. Four synthesis techniques are compared—Response Diversification, Query Expansion, Retrospective Enhancement, and Tutorship Amplification—with Tutorship Amplification (a teacher model checks a student model's answer, flags erroneous steps, and continues the correct solution from that point) emerging as distinctly superior. A key finding is that CPT with problem-solving data yields roughly 60% more reasoning improvement than SFT with the same data, driven primarily by CPT's superior capacity to learn from harder, multi-step problems. The optimal strategy (3:7 corpus-to-problem ratio, 5+ reasoning-step filtering, Tutorship Amplification expansion) is applied to Llama3-8B to produce MathGPT-8B, which matches Qwen2-Math-72B-level performance using only 140B tokens—about one-tenth of Qwen2.5-Math-7B's reported budget.

## Results

MathGPT-8B achieves an average accuracy of 70.62% across GSM8K (81.20%), MATH (60.38%), GAOKAO (60.43%), and ZHONGKAO (80.49%), trained on only 140B total tokens (100B math-related) from the Llama3-8B base. This surpasses Qwen2-Math-7B (63.22%) and DeepSeekMath-Base-7B (46.41%), and is comparable to Qwen2-Math-72B (70.92%) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B (69.13%). On MMLU, MathGPT-8B scores 0.6222 versus Llama3-8B's 0.6211, confirming retention of general knowledge. The Tutorship Amplification synthesis method yields the largest single improvement, boosting average accuracy from 39.50% (Base2) to 50.60% in the Llama2-based experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MathGPT-8B (70.62 avg) dramatically outperforms Llama3-8B (32.91) by 37.7 points and even surpasses the much larger Llama3.1-70B (54.31) by 16.3 points, demonstrating that targeted math CPT with problem-solving data is far more effective than scaling general pre-training. Against math-specific baselines, MathGPT-8B beats DeepSeekMath-Base-7B (46.41) by 24.2 points and Qwen2-Math-7B (63.22) by 7.4 points, while achieving near-parity with Qwen2-Math-72B (70.92, −0.30) using only 1/10 the training tokens of Qwen2.5-Math-7B. Compared to Qwen2.5-Math-7B (69.13), MathGPT-8B edges ahead by 1.49 points despite starting from a weaker base model and using 140B tokens versus 1T. The model also outperforms Qwen2-72B (65.56) by 5.06 points, though it falls slightly short of Qwen2.5-Math-72B (72.76) by 2.14 points. LLEMMA-7B (30.10) is the weakest baseline, trailing MathGPT-8B by 40.5 points, highlighting the gap between early math CPT approaches and the problem-solving-data strategy proposed here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in math-specific LLMs (LLEMMA, DeepSeekMath, Qwen2-Math, InternLM-Math) that reserves problem-solving data for the SFT stage while using general mathematical corpora for CPT. It builds on the 'Physics of LLM' line of work (Allen-Zhu & Li, 2023; Ye et al., 2024), which showed that pre-training should teach models to *apply* memorized knowledge rather than merely accumulate it, and on data synthesis techniques from MetaMath and WizardMath. The Tutorship Amplification method specifically extends Ye et al.'s observation that models can detect but not correct errors by constructing a teacher-student error-correction loop, and the CPT-vs-SFT comparison framework draws on MiniCPM's findings about the importance of pre-training-stage knowledge utilization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CPT with problem-solving data substantially outperforms SFT with the same data (and that this gap widens with problem difficulty) provides a concrete, actionable reordering of the training pipeline that could reduce the compute needed for competitive math models—MathGPT-8B matches Qwen2.5-Math-7B using roughly 1/10 the tokens. The Tutorship Amplification synthesis method offers a simple, implementable technique for generating high-quality training data without expensive human annotation. Open questions remain: whether these findings generalize beyond 8B-scale models and the Llama family, how the strategy interacts with post-training alignment (RLHF/DPO), and whether the 'relatively naive' synthesis methods can be further refined to close the remaining gap to the largest math models.
