## Problem

Adam is the de facto optimizer for transformers in practice, yet rigorous theoretical analysis of how it optimizes transformer architectures remains largely intractable due to Adam's complex formulation. Sign Gradient Descent (SignGD) is a well-known effective surrogate for Adam, but its training dynamics on transformers with trainable query-key parameterization had not been characterized. This paper provides the first provable, stage-by-stage description of how SignGD optimizes a two-layer transformer, revealing a fast-convergence-but-poor-generalization phenomenon that also applies to Adam.

## Input / Output

Each data point consists of two d-dimensional patches (one containing a 1-sparse signal vector yμ correlated with the binary label y, and one containing an s-sparse Gaussian noise vector ξ) plus the label y ∈ {±1}. The two-layer transformer (single-head softmax attention with trainable W_Q, W_K, W_V followed by a fixed linear head) maps these two patches to a scalar logit for binary classification. The primary outputs are not just predictions but a complete four-stage training dynamics characterization, a linear convergence rate for training loss, and a Θ(1) lower bound on test loss demonstrating noise memorization in the attention layer.

## Method

The paper analyzes SignGD on a two-layer transformer with trainable query-key parameterization using the feature learning framework, tracking parameter-data inner products (query/key/value signals and noise) rather than raw parameters. The key technical insight is that SignGD combined with the sparse data model yields constant-magnitude updates for all tracked inner products at every iteration, reducing the dynamics to pure sign-tracking. This enables a clean four-stage decomposition: (I) mean value noise shifts linearly and stabilizes while query/key noise remain near initialization; (II) query and key noise align their signs per neuron; (III) the sum of key noise acts as a majority vote to determine the sign of query/key signals; (IV) noise-signal softmax outputs decay exponentially, triggering delayed sign alignment of negative noise to signals. The analysis proves linear convergence of training loss but a constant test loss, with the attention matrix concentrating all weight on the noise patch (sparse attention), and empirically validates that Adam exhibits the same four-stage dynamics and poor generalization.

## Results

The paper proves that SignGD achieves a linear convergence rate on training loss, reaching L_S(W^(T)) ≤ ε in T = O(log(ε⁻¹)η⁻¹σₚ⁻¹s⁻¹) steps, while the test loss remains at a constant order L_D(W^(T)) = Θ(1), demonstrating fast convergence but poor generalization. The attention layer memorizes training noise, producing a sparse post-softmax attention matrix where all weights concentrate on the noise patch (s_{i,11} = o(1), s_{i,21} = o(1)). Empirically on noisy MNIST (classes 3 vs. 7), SignGD and Adam both exhibit high test loss under strong noise, while GD consistently generalizes better across all noise levels despite slower convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SignGD against both Adam and GD. Against Adam, SignGD serves as a strong proxy: on synthetic data, Adam follows the same four-stage dynamics (Stages III and IV confirmed in Fig. 2a–b), and on noisy MNIST, Adam also exhibits high test loss under strong noise, mirroring SignGD's poor generalization. However, when Adam's β₁ and β₂ are close to 1 (common in practice), Adam does not fully replicate SignGD's Stage IV sign alignment of query noise, likely due to momentum effects. Against GD, the comparison reveals a fundamental trade-off: SignGD converges exponentially fast (linear rate in training loss) while GD converges sublinearly, but GD consistently achieves lower test loss across all noise levels on noisy MNIST (Fig. 2d). This demonstrates that SignGD's poor generalization is not solely due to data noise but is an inherent algorithmic property, implying that both SignGD and Adam require higher data quality than GD for real-world tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature learning framework (Allen-Zhu & Li, 2023; Cao et al., 2022) to analyze transformer training dynamics, departing from prior work that typically uses Gradient Descent or SGD and simplifies the attention mechanism into a single joint matrix. It is the first provable characterization of SignGD dynamics on a softmax attention layer with trainable query-key parameterization, building on the observation (Kunstner et al., 2023) that SignGD serves as an effective proxy for Adam. The multi-stage decomposition of dynamics and the combination of SignGD's sign-only updates with a sparse data model are technical novelties that simplify the analysis relative to prior transformer dynamics studies (Jiang et al., 2024; Li et al., 2023c; Tian et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that SignGD (and by extension Adam) achieves fast linear convergence yet poor generalization on noisy data provides a theoretical counterexample to the 'train faster, generalize better' stability argument, and suggests that practitioners using Adam should invest in data quality. However, the analysis is restricted to a highly simplified setting—sparse orthogonal signal/noise patches, context length 2, single-head attention, and linearly separable data—leaving open whether these dynamics persist in the multi-head, deep, and non-sparse regimes of real-world transformers. Extending the theory to language data, deeper architectures, and non-sparse features are natural next steps.
