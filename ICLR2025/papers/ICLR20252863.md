## Problem

Existing activation intervention methods for LLMs (e.g., ITI, CAA) rely on fixed steering vectors derived from contrastive pairs, which do not adapt to the semantic direction of individual inputs. This misalignment between the static intervention direction and the input's representation can degrade predictive performance, particularly when the discrepancy is large. The paper addresses this by asking whether steering vectors can be made dynamic and semantics-adaptive at inference time without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method requires a small set of contrastive pairs (~150 items) consisting of a question paired with a positive (correct) and negative (incorrect) output, from which an identification mask over model elements (attention heads, hidden states, or FFN neurons) is computed once. At inference, the user input text is forwarded through the frozen LLM, and only the last-token activations at each layer are extracted, masked, and rescaled before completing the forward pass. The output is the steered text response, with the identification mask serving as a reusable, task-specific artifact that requires no gradient updates.

## Method

SADI operates in three steps: (1) Difference Extraction computes per-layer activation differences D_i^(l) = A_i^{pos,(l)} − A_i^{neg,(l)} between positive and negative contrastive examples, focusing on last-token activations; (2) Binary Masking averages these differences across all instances to form a global vector D, then binarizes it by retaining only the top-K elements with the largest magnitude as a 0/1 identification mask M; (3) Adaptive Steering, at inference, updates the input's own activations via A'_q = A_q + δ(A_q ⊙ M), where δ controls intervention strength. The critical distinction from prior work is that the steering signal is the input's own masked activations rather than a fixed contrastive difference vector, so the intervention direction automatically aligns with the input's semantic representation. This is applied independently to three model components—hidden states (SADI-HIDDEN), attention head outputs (SADI-HEAD), and FFN neuron activations (SADI-NEURON)—with SADI-HEAD consistently yielding the largest gains across tasks.

## Results

On seven multiple-choice tasks (zero-shot, LLAMA2-7B-CHAT), SADI-HEAD achieves an average accuracy of 70.08%, outperforming the BASELINE (64.85%) by +5.23 and the best fixed-vector baselines ITI (67.72%) and CAA (68.42%) by +2.36 and +1.66 respectively. The largest single-task gain is +14.69 on StoryCloze (79.75 vs. 65.06). On open-ended generation, SADI-HEAD improves TruthfulQA True×Info from 66.50 to 76.58 (+10.08) and reduces ToxiGen toxicity from 49.71 to 34.50. SADI also stacks on top of SFT, raising the SFT average from 90.59 to 90.97 (SADI-HEAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SADI is compared against two fixed-steering-vector baselines: ITI (Li et al., NeurIPS 2023), which identifies top attention heads via contrastive pairs and applies a fixed amplification, and CAA (Rimsky et al., 2023), which adds a fixed mean-difference steering vector to hidden states. SADI-HEAD outperforms both on average accuracy (70.08 vs. 67.72 for ITI and 68.42 for CAA), with the largest margin on StoryCloze (+11.25 over ITI, +5.10 over CAA). SADI-HIDDEN, however, underperforms the BASELINE on StoryCloze (55.99 vs. 65.06) and NLI (59.28 vs. 63.11), showing that the choice of intervened component matters. SADI-NEURON is competitive with CAA on BoolQ (70.40 vs. 74.98) but trails on most other tasks. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that replacing SADI's dynamic, input-adaptive steering with a fixed vector (Eq. 6) degrades performance by 1–7 points, validating the semantics-adaptive design as the key differentiator over both ITI and CAA.

## Relation to Prior Work

SADI extends the activation engineering paradigm established by ITI (Li et al., 2023) and CAA (Rimsky et al., 2023), both of which extract activation differences from contrastive pairs to identify critical model elements. The key departure is replacing the fixed steering vector (a constant offset added to activations) with a dynamic, input-adaptive mechanism that scales the input's own activations along identified critical dimensions. This shift is grounded in the linear representation hypothesis (Park et al., 2024; Turner et al., 2023), which motivates preserving the semantic direction of each input rather than imposing a uniform directional shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SADI offers a practical, training-free alignment technique requiring only ~150 contrastive pairs, making it accessible for practitioners who cannot afford fine-tuning. Its generalizability across four 7B architectures, eleven tasks, multiple model sizes, and eight languages strengthens its case as a broadly applicable intervention tool. However, hyperparameters K and δ are task-specific and require per-task validation sweeps, and the method has only been validated on 7B-scale models, leaving open whether the approach scales to much larger LLMs. The occasional underperformance of SADI-HIDDEN (e.g., a 9-point drop on StoryCloze) also suggests that the choice of intervention target remains non-trivial and task-dependent.
