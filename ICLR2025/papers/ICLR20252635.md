## Problem

Existing memory replay methods for continual learning on growing graphs suffer from two underexplored issues: buffer selection typically ignores inter-class diversity (storing all classes in one undifferentiated buffer) and intra-class representativeness, and the limited buffer size constrains replay effectiveness because models overfit to a few stored nodes. This paper addresses both by asking how to select more diverse, representative buffer nodes and how to broaden the effective knowledge captured by those limited nodes during replay.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of graph snapshots G¹⊂G²⊂…⊂Gᵐ where each snapshot adds new nodes with novel classes (class-incremental setting), along with per-class memory buffers of size b storing representative nodes from prior tasks. At each step the model extends its classifier head to cover new classes and trains jointly on new-task nodes and buffer nodes. The output is a node-level probability vector over all accumulated classes; internally, the variational layer also produces synthesized buffer embeddings of the same dimensionality as the original GCN hidden representations (h=256) used for replay.

## Method

DMSG combines two complementary mechanisms. First, a heuristic diversified memory selection uses a greedy submodular maximization algorithm (with (1−1/e) approximation guarantee) to pick buffer nodes that simultaneously maximize intra-class spread (L2 distance on model probability outputs within the same class buffer) and inter-class separation (distance to nodes in other class buffers). Second, a diversified memory generation replay module passes buffer node embeddings through a variational layer (identity mean + ReLU variance) to sample synthesized embeddings, then constrains them via an adversarial discriminator with a gradient reversal layer (maintaining embedding integrity) and a Bernoulli graph-reconstruction decoder with KL regularization (consolidating label-space generalization). The overall loss combines the new-task cross-entropy, replay loss on synthesized embeddings, the adversarial min-max objective, and the reconstruction ELBO, optimized in a single synchronized pass per training step.

## Results

DMSG achieves Average Accuracy (AA) of 77.8% on CoraFull, 50.7% on OGB-Arxiv, 98.1% on Reddit, and 66.0% on OGB-Products, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline SEM (77.7%, 49.9%, 96.3%, 65.1%) across all four datasets. Notably, DMSG achieves a positive Average Forgetting (AF) of +0.9% on Reddit, meaning the model actually improves on previously learned tasks, while all other baselines show negative AF. DMSG approaches the Joint upper bound (which retrains on all data) within 3.4% on CoraFull and 0.6% on OGB-Arxiv, while surpassing Joint on Reddit (98.1% vs. 97.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMSG outperforms the strongest memory-replay baseline SEM by 0.1% AA on CoraFull, 0.8% on OGB-Arxiv, 1.8% on Reddit, and 0.9% on OGB-Products, with dramatically better AF (e.g., -0.5 vs. -10.0 on CoraFull, +0.9 vs. -0.6 on Reddit). Against the next-best memory method SSM, DMSG gains 2.4% AA on CoraFull and 2.7% on OGB-Products. Regularization-based methods (EWC, MAS, GEM, LwF, TWP) perform poorly, with GEM and MAS collapsing to near-chance accuracy (<13% AA) on CoraFull and OGB-Arxiv, confirming that parameter-constraint approaches are insufficient for 20–35-step class-incremental learning on graphs. ER-GNN, the earliest graph-specific experience replay method, lags DMSG by 43.3% AA on CoraFull and 9.3% on OGB-Products. DMSG also surpasses the Joint upper bound on Reddit (98.1% vs. 97.1% AA), attributed to its diversified buffer selection eliminating noise nodes that Joint's full-data training retains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memory replay paradigm for graph continual learning (ER-GNN, SSM, SEM) by addressing two underexplored dimensions: buffer selection diversity and replay effectiveness under limited buffer sizes. It repurposes variational autoencoder and adversarial training machinery (GRL-based min-max optimization) to generate diversified synthetic node embeddings from a small set of stored buffer nodes, a creative fusion not previously applied to graph incremental learning. The theoretical grounding via Wasserstein distance between the true graph distribution and the buffer distribution, combined with a submodular greedy selection with (1−1/e) approximation guarantee, provides a principled justification for the diversity objectives that prior graph replay methods (which typically use random or sparsified subgraph sampling) lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical and theoretically grounded recipe for continual graph learning that approaches the joint-training upper bound with only a small buffer, which is critical for real-world growing graphs where storing all historical data is infeasible. The positive Average Forgetting on Reddit (the model actually improves on old tasks after learning new ones) suggests that diversified selection can actively denoise rather than merely preserve knowledge. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the GCN backbone is never updated, which may become a bottleneck as graph complexity grows; future work on parameter-incremental or architecture-adaptive components is a natural next step. The buffer-size sweet spot finding (performance degrading at very large buffers due to noise) also opens questions about optimal buffer sizing strategies.
