## Problem

Most LLM developers report only carbon emissions from final training runs, omitting the substantial environmental costs of model development (hyperparameter tuning, ablations), hardware manufacturing (embodied carbon and water), and total water consumption. This paper is the first to provide a holistic, lifecycle-level accounting of environmental impact for a series of LLMs, revealing that development alone accounts for ~50% of training's impact and that water usage is a significant but under-reported externality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sub-second GPU power time series from a single node (extrapolated to full cluster), data center-specific PUE/WUE/carbon-intensity coefficients, hardware manufacturing specs (embodied carbon per GPU, water per wafer), and model configurations (parameter count, token count, node count). Outputs are structured as per-phase breakdowns (development, training, inference) of carbon emissions in tCO2eq, water in kL, and energy in MWh, plus a 'break-even' inference count indicating how many inferences equal the training footprint. Power measurements are treated as lower bounds since only GPU (not CPU/RAM) consumption is captured.

## Method

The paper extends the Schwartz et al. cost equation C(R) ∝ E·D·H by explicitly measuring the H term (development experiments) alongside E·D (final training), applying the formula CO2e = P·PUE·CI and Water = P·PUE·(WUE_onsite + WUE_offsite) at each lifecycle stage. Embodied impacts are computed by amortizing manufacturing emissions (3700 kg CO2eq per 8-GPU node, 100.4 L water per H100) over a 4-year GPU lifespan, then multiplying by total GPU-hours. Inference costs are simulated via SGLang serving on a single H100 with ShareGPT prompts at three request rates (∞, 8, 1 req/s), measuring cumulative energy with CodeCarbon. A key empirical finding is that GPU power fluctuates between ~15% and ~85% of maximum draw during training due to checkpointing, contradicting the common assumption of constant 100% utilization used in prior estimates.

## Results

Across the full OLMo model series (20M–13B parameters, up to 5.6T tokens), total environmental impact is 493 tCO₂eq and 2.769 million liters of water, equivalent to powering 98 US homes for a year and 24.5 years of one US person's water use. Development (hyperparameter tuning, ablations, scaling-law experiments) accounts for ~50% of training's impact (159 tCO₂eq, 843 kL), a cost almost never reported by other developers. Power draw during training fluctuates between ~15% and ~85% of H100 maximum (700 W), driven by checkpointing pauses, with implications for grid-scale planning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks OLMo models against Llama 2 (7B, 13B), Llama 3.1 (8B), Llama 3.2 (1B), and Gemma (2B & 9B) on training-phase carbon emissions and water consumption. OLMo 2 7B (52 tCO₂eq) emits more than Llama 2 7B (31 tCO₂eq) but far less than Llama 3.1 8B (420 tCO₂eq), reflecting the latter's much larger training token budget. OLMo 2 13B (101 tCO₂eq) exceeds Llama 2 13B (62 tCO₂eq) by ~63%, again attributable to longer training. Gemma 2B & 9B combined (131 tCO₂eq) sits between OLMo 2 7B and OLMo 2 13B. Crucially, the OLMo report is the first to include development-phase costs (~50% of training) and water consumption alongside carbon, dimensions that Llama and Gemma reports omit entirely, making direct like-for-like comparison on total lifecycle impact impossible with publicly available data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the environmental impact assessment methodology established by Schwartz et al. (2020) and Luccioni et al. (2023) for BLOOM, and the water footprint framework of Li et al. (2023), by applying them across the full model lifecycle rather than just the final training run. The key departure is the explicit measurement of model development costs (hyperparameter tuning, ablations, scaling law experiments), which prior reports from Llama, Gemma, and OLMo omitted entirely. The authors also introduce sub-second power time-series analysis to characterize intra-training power fluctuations, a dimension absent from all prior LLM environmental reports.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that development costs account for roughly 50% of final training costs fundamentally changes how the field should account for LLM environmental impact, since virtually no prior report included this phase. The power fluctuation observation (15%–85% of max draw during checkpointing) raises practical grid-stability concerns as training scales to tens of thousands of GPUs. Open questions remain around embodied emissions (NVIDIA discloses nothing), full supply-chain water accounting, and whether Jevons' Paradox will cause total inference impact to outpace training impact as deployment-optimized small models proliferate. The paper's methodology provides a template, but the authors acknowledge that models orders of magnitude larger than 13B parameters will have proportionally larger impacts that remain unreported by their developers.
