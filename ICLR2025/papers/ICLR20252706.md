## Problem

Spectral risks (e.g., CVaR, ESRM, Extremile) assign non-uniform weights to sample losses based on their ranking, enabling risk-averse optimization between average and worst-case performance. However, no stochastic algorithm with convergence guarantees for the original (non-smooth) spectral risk minimization existed: prior methods either relied on smoothing via a strongly concave term (lacking guarantees for the original problem) or were deterministic, requiring O(n) loss evaluations per iteration. The core difficulty is that each sample's weight depends on the full loss ordering, making unbiased subgradient estimation from mini-batches impossible.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a training set of n sample points, a fixed spectral risk weight vector σ (defining the specific risk measure such as CVaR with parameter α, ESRM with ρ, or Extremile with r), a convex L-smooth loss function, and a μ-strongly convex regularizer g. It outputs a single parameter vector w that is ε-optimal for the spectral risk minimization problem. In practice, the algorithm is single-loop: it updates the dual variable λ once per full pass over the data, and the step size is independent of the target accuracy ε, unlike smoothing-based approaches that require step sizes of O(ε).

## Method

SOREL reformulates the spectral risk minimization as a minimax problem over the permutahedron Π_σ (the convex hull of all permutations of σ), then solves it via a primal-dual scheme. The key innovation is adding a proximal term (1/2η_k)‖λ−λ_k‖² to the dual update, which stabilizes the primal trajectory by preventing the discontinuous jumps in λ that occur when losses are tied—without this, the algorithm oscillates and fails to converge. For the primal update, since λ is fixed during the inner loop, the finite-sum objective becomes smooth, enabling SVRG-style variance reduction with periodic full-gradient recomputation. A momentum term v_k = (1+θ_k)ℓ(w_k) − θ_kℓ(w_{k−1}) accelerates the dual update, and the proximal parameter τ_k = O(1/k) tightens primal updates over time. This yields a near-optimal sample complexity of Õ(n^{3/2}G/(μ√ε)) for an ε-optimal solution, matching the deterministic lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

On the fair machine learning benchmarks (acs and law), SOREL achieves the lowest training suboptimality in nearly all spectral risk settings, often reaching values below L-BFGS (e.g., −2.38×10⁻⁸ for ESRM on acs, −2.12×10⁻⁷ for Extremile on law). On the acs classification task, SOREL attains the best Equal Opportunity metric across all spectral risks (e.g., 0.00130 for Extremile vs. 0.00237 for Prospect and 0.00377 for LSVRG). On the least-squares regression benchmarks, SOREL outperforms all baselines in both sample complexity and runtime in most cases, while SGD fails to converge and LSVRG/Prospect frequently fail to reach the true optimum. Theoretically, SOREL achieves a near-optimal rate of Õ(1/√ε) sample complexity, matching the deterministic lower bound up to a logarithmic factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SOREL against three baselines: SGD and LSVRG from Mehta et al. (AISTATS 2022), and Prospect from Mehta et al. (ICLR 2024). SOREL decisively outperforms all three on training suboptimality: it reaches the true optimal point (suboptimality ≤ 0, i.e., better than L-BFGS) in most settings, while SGD fails to converge entirely, and LSVRG/Prospect frequently stall at suboptimal solutions (e.g., LSVRG suboptimality of 7.90×10⁻³ vs. SOREL's 1.14×10⁻⁴ for Extremile on acs). On fairness metrics, SOREL achieves the best Equal Opportunity on acs for all three spectral risks (e.g., 0.00130 vs. Prospect's 0.00237 for Extremile) and the best SMD on law for CVaR (0.03890 vs. 0.03893). The one setting where SOREL is outperformed is sample complexity on the power regression dataset, where Prospect requires fewer passes; however, SOREL's runtime is still significantly shorter because Prospect incurs O(n) permutahedron projection cost per step. Overall, SOREL is the only method that consistently converges to the true spectral risk optimum across all tested settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOREL builds on the minimax reformulation of spectral risk via the permutahedron (Blondel et al., 2020) and variance-reduced proximal gradient methods (SVRG, Xiao & Zhang 2014) to solve the resulting saddle-point problem. It departs from prior stochastic spectral risk methods (Mehta et al. 2022, 2024b; Levy et al. 2020; Kawaguchi & Lu 2020) that either optimized a smoothed surrogate with a strongly concave term—lacking guarantees for the original risk—or abandoned convergence guarantees entirely. The key methodological departure is the proximal stabilization of the dual variable λ, which prevents the oscillation caused by the non-smoothness of the spectral risk at points where losses are tied, enabling a clean telescoping argument in the convergence proof.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first stochastic algorithm with a convergence guarantee for the original (unsmoothed) spectral risk minimization, achieving a near-optimal O~(1/√ε) rate that matches the deterministic lower bound. The practical relevance is broad: spectral risks underpin fairness-aware learning (CVaR, Extremile), distributionally robust optimization, and risk-averse decision-making, all of which previously lacked a principled stochastic optimizer. Key limitations include the requirement of a strongly convex regularizer, convex individual losses, and experiments restricted to linear models; extending SOREL to nonconvex settings (e.g., deep networks) and to the recently proposed DRAGO algorithm (Mehta et al. 2024a) are natural next steps.
