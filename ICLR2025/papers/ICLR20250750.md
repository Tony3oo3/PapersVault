## Problem

Causal discovery for continuous-time stochastic dynamical systems remains largely unsolved: existing constraint-based methods assume discrete-time sampling on regular grids, cannot handle path-dependence or diffusion dependence, and lack practical conditional independence tests for path-valued random variables. Moreover, even when CI constraints are available, standard constraint-based algorithms in the static setting only recover Markov equivalence classes rather than the full causal graph, and no method simultaneously handles irregularly sampled, partially observed data with path- and diffusion-dependence in continuous time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sample paths of a d-dimensional stochastic process X = (X¹,…,Xᵈ) over a maximum observation time T, where different coordinate processes may be observed at different, irregularly spaced time points (no common sampling grid required). It outputs the full directed acyclic dependence graph G = (V, E) with self-loops allowed (i.e., the complete causal structure), or in the partially observed setting, the induced ancestral graph over observed variables with edge types {←, →, ↔}. The CI tests operate on path-valued random variables over sub-intervals [a,b] ⊂ [0,T], testing independence of increments rather than point values, which is essential for capturing path-dependence.

## Method

The paper constructs a 'lifted dependence graph' by splitting each variable into a past copy (V₀) and a future copy (V₁), adding edges from past to future to encode the arrow of time, and proves a global Markov property for h-local conditional independence constraints (⊥ₛ,ₕ⁺) with respect to d-separation in this lifted graph. This enables sound and complete constraint-based algorithms that uniquely recover the full DAG (not just a CPDAG) by exploiting time directionality, with a separate FCI-inspired procedure for partially observed data that reduces the typical partial ancestral graph output to a single maximally informative ancestral graph. To make the algorithms practical, the paper introduces a signature kernel-based CI test on path space: it computes Gram matrices using the signature kernel (evaluated efficiently via a Goursat PDE 'kernel trick') and runs a permutation-based kernel CI test (KCIPT or SDCIT), proving consistency for path-valued random variables without requiring densities. The approach requires no hyperparameter tuning beyond a kernel length scale (selected via median heuristic) and handles both drift and diffusion dependence, path-dependence, and irregularly sampled observations.

## Results

In multi-dimensional causal discovery (Table 2), SigKer with post-processing (⊥_sym + p.p.) achieves SHD×10² of 13±4 at d=3, 38±7 at d=5, and 157±15 at d=10, substantially outperforming PCMCI (29±16, 243±37, 793±84) and the best SCOTCH configuration (110±21, 270±48, 250±61). In bivariate settings (Table 1), SigKer achieves SHD×10² of 7±3 (linear, n=400), 6±2 (path-dependence, n=400), and 5±2 (non-linear, n=400), dominating all baselines except in diffusion dependence where SCOTCH with favorable hyperparameters reaches 9±8. In the real-world pairs trading study (Table 3), SigKer achieves a Sharpe ratio of 1.500 and maxDDD of 21, versus 0.242 and 153 for the best baseline (ADF & Granger).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SigKer decisively outperforms CCM, Granger causality, and PCMCI across all bivariate SDE settings (linear, path-dependence, non-linear) and in multi-dimensional causal discovery up to d=10, where it achieves 5–10× lower SHD. Against SCOTCH (the state-of-the-art neural SDE method), SigKer wins in linear, path-dependence, and non-linear bivariate settings and in causal discovery at d=3, 5, and 10, but SCOTCH with optimally chosen hyperparameters (λ=100, n_e=2k) achieves lower SHD in diffusion dependence (9±8 vs 63±5) and at d=20 (370±174 vs 725±439); however, the authors note SCOTCH's hyperparameter sensitivity makes it unreliable in practice since the sparsity parameter λ cannot be selected without ground truth. Against Laumann et al. (2023) (Lau), SigKer achieves near-1 test power at n≥40 for the unconditional independence test, while Lau is limited to unconditional tests and cannot handle partial observations. In the real-world VBR Small-Cap ETF pairs trading study, SigKer substantially outperforms ADF, Granger, and their combination in total return (0.076 vs 0.008), Sharpe ratio (1.500 vs 0.242), and maxDDD (21 vs 153), demonstrating practical downstream impact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: constraint-based causal discovery (PC/FCI algorithms), signature kernels for path-valued data, and kernel-based conditional independence tests (KCIPT, SDCIT). It departs from existing time-series causal discovery methods (PCMCI, CCM, Granger) by dropping the discrete-time and regular-sampling assumptions, and from SDE-specific methods (SCOTCH) by using a non-parametric, constraint-based approach that handles path-dependence, diffusion-dependence, and partial observability simultaneously. The lifted dependence graph construction, which exploits the arrow of time to recover the full DAG rather than just the Markov equivalence class, is a novel theoretical contribution not present in static constraint-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to simultaneously handle irregularly sampled observations, partial observability with unobserved confounders, path-dependence, and diffusion-dependence in continuous-time causal discovery, with soundness and completeness guarantees. The signature kernel CI test for path-valued random variables is of independent interest beyond causal discovery, applicable to any functional data or stochastic process setting. Key limitations include the acyclicity assumption (except self-loops), stationarity, and faithfulness; the impossibility result for cyclic SDE models (Example A.1) highlights a fundamental barrier that would require infinitesimal-scale tests (e.g., strong instantaneous non-causality) to overcome, an open direction for future work.
