## Problem

Differential equation (DE)-constrained optimization problems require simultaneously solving ODEs/SDEs and optimizing decision variables subject to the resulting dynamics, but traditional numerical methods (shooting, collocation, discretization) are computationally intractable for high-dimensional nonlinear systems in real-time settings. Existing proxy optimizer approaches handle only the steady-state snapshot, systematically ignoring dynamic constraints and producing solutions that violate system stability requirements. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a fully differentiable learning-based surrogate that jointly captures system dynamics and optimization objectives in near real-time.

## Input / Output

The method takes problem parameters ζ (e.g., customer load demands for power systems, initial asset price vectors for portfolio optimization) as a single vector input and produces two coupled outputs: the optimal decision variables ū (generator dispatch set-points, portfolio allocations) and the full state variable trajectories ŷ(t) (rotor angles/speeds over time, asset price trends). The dual-network design means the decision output from the proxy optimizer F_ω is fed directly into the neural-DE model N_θ, so the state trajectories are conditioned on the predicted decisions rather than ground-truth ones. Inference completes in 1–9 ms depending on problem size, enabling near real-time application across a distribution of problem instances.

## Method

DE-OP employs a dual-network architecture: a feedforward proxy optimizer F_ω maps problem parameters to decision variables, while a neural-ODE or neural-SDE model N_θ (one per generator or per asset) learns the parametric family of governing differential equations. The key architectural insight is expressing the ODE/SDE dynamics and initial conditions in implicit residual form h′(u, y(t)) = [dy(t) − F(u,y(t),t)dt; y(0) − I(u); h(u,y(t))], so that dynamic constraints are treated identically to static equality constraints within a unified Lagrangian. Training follows a primal-dual scheme: the loss L^DE-OP combines the decision MSE, weighted dynamic constraint violations |h′|, and weighted inequality violations max(0,g), with Lagrange multipliers updated via dual ascent at each epoch. The neural-DE model is hot-started on near-optimal decisions sampled from feasible bounds to ensure its training distribution matches the proxy optimizer's output distribution, and the entire pipeline is end-to-end differentiable, enabling joint gradient-based optimization of both networks.

## Results

On the IEEE 57-bus stability-constrained AC-OPF problem, DE-OP achieves zero stability constraint violations while all baselines (MSE, LD, DC3) exhibit 23–29% instability, at a marginal cost of 0.22% steady-state optimality gap versus 0.16–0.17% for baselines. On the Dynamic Portfolio Optimization task (n=50 assets), DE-OP with a neural-SDE predictor achieves a 9.11% optimality gap, compared to >100% for static-only proxy optimizers (DC3, LD, PDL) and 21.17% for an LSTM-based dynamic predictor — a up-to-25× improvement in decision quality. Inference times are 1 ms (WSCC 9-bus) and 9 ms (IEEE 57-bus), comparable to the fastest baselines and ~15× faster than DC3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DE-OP decisively outperforms all three steady-state proxy optimizer baselines (MSE [Zamzam & Baker 2020], LD [Fioretto et al. 2020], DC3 [Donti et al. 2020]) on the critical stability constraint: it achieves 0.00% instability while MSE, LD, and DC3 produce 23.30%, 23.10%, and 28.60% unstable dynamics respectively on the IEEE 57-bus system. The trade-off is a slightly higher steady-state optimality gap (0.22% vs. 0.16–0.17%), which the authors attribute to the restricted feasible space imposed by stability constraints. On the portfolio optimization task, DE-OP with a neural-SDE predictor (9.11% gap) substantially outperforms the self-supervised PDL baseline [Park & Van Hentenryck 2023] and all static methods (>100% gap), while the LSTM-based dynamic predictor (21.17%) and Feed Forward predictor (102.45%) confirm that explicit dynamic equation modeling is essential. For dynamics estimation, the neural-ODE achieves 5.17% ℓ² error on generator state variables versus 69.45% for PINNs [Raissi et al. 2019], demonstrating that data-driven neural-DE surrogates generalize far better than physics-informed architectures across parametric families of ODEs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates two previously separate research threads: proxy optimizers (Fioretto et al., 2020; Park & Van Hentenryck, 2023; Donti et al., 2020), which learn mappings from problem parameters to optimal decisions, and neural differential equations (Chen et al., 2018; Kidger, 2022), which model system dynamics through parameterized hidden-state derivatives. The key architectural contribution is a dual-network design where a feedforward proxy optimizer handles decision variables and a neural-ODE/SDE model handles state trajectories, coupled end-to-end via a Lagrangian primal-dual training loop. This departs from prior proxy optimizers that treat the problem as a steady-state snapshot (ignoring dynamics entirely) and from PINNs (Raissi et al., 2019) or LSTMs, which model dynamics but lack the optimization-aware constraint enforcement mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that ignoring system dynamics in optimization surrogates leads to systematic and severe constraint violations (23–28% stability violations in power systems), while incorporating neural-DE models achieves zero violations at negligible inference cost (1–9 ms) and only a ~0.05% increase in steady-state optimality gap. This opens a practical path for near real-time DE-constrained optimization in domains like power grid dispatch and portfolio management where traditional solvers are too slow. Open questions include scalability to very large networks (hundreds of generators), generalization to unseen problem structures beyond the training distribution, and whether the primal-dual training framework extends cleanly to more complex coupled SDE systems with multiple interacting stochastic processes.
