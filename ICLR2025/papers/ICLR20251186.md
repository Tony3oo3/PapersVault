## Problem

Existing cache-based acceleration methods for video diffusion models directly reuse adjacent-step attention features, which degrades video quality by ignoring subtle inter-step variations essential for fine detail synthesis. Additionally, classifier-free guidance (CFG) nearly doubles inference time, yet its internal redundancy—particularly the high similarity between conditional and unconditional outputs at the same timestep—remained largely unexplored as an acceleration opportunity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251186/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is applied as a training-free inference-time wrapper around existing video diffusion transformers (Open-Sora 1.2, Open-Sora-Plan, Latte, CogVideoX, Vchitect-2.0), taking a text prompt and producing a generated video at various resolutions (480P, 512×512) and frame counts (16–221 frames). It also extends to image-to-video (DynamiCrafter, 1024×576, 16 frames) and text-to-image (PixArt-σ) models. No architectural modification or retraining is required; the method operates entirely on intermediate features during the iterative denoising loop.

## Method

FasterCache combines two complementary mechanisms. First, a Dynamic Feature Reuse Strategy computes attention outputs at every alternate timestep and approximates the skipped intermediate feature by adding a bias term derived from the difference between adjacent cached features, weighted by a linearly increasing function w(t) that grows from 0 to 1 over the sampling schedule—this preserves the subtle inter-step variations that vanilla caching discards. Second, CFG-Cache exploits the high similarity between conditional and unconditional outputs at the same timestep by caching their high-frequency and low-frequency biases in the FFT domain; during reuse steps, only the conditional branch is computed and the unconditional output is reconstructed by adding the cached frequency-domain biases to the current conditional output, with timestep-adaptive weights (w₁, w₂) that shift emphasis from low-frequency to high-frequency components as sampling progresses. Full inference is performed every 5 timesteps starting from one-third of total steps, while attention features are fully computed every 2 timesteps.

## Results

FasterCache achieves 1.54×–1.68× speedup across five video diffusion models (Open-Sora 1.2, Open-Sora-Plan, Latte, CogVideoX, Vchitect-2.0) while maintaining or slightly exceeding baseline VBench scores (e.g., 80.84% vs. 80.80% on Vchitect-2.0). On Vchitect-2.0, it reduces latency from 260.32 s to 156.13 s with LPIPS of 0.0282, SSIM of 0.9224, and PSNR of 31.45 relative to the original model. On Open-Sora 1.2 (192 frames, 480P), it cuts latency from 192.07 s to 118.44 s (1.62×) with VBench 78.46% and LPIPS 0.0835.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FasterCache consistently outperforms both Δ-DiT and PAB across all five base models on every metric. Against Δ-DiT, the advantage is substantial: on Open-Sora 1.2, FasterCache achieves 1.62× speedup vs. 1.14–1.34× for Δ-DiT, while VBench is 78.46% vs. 76.60–77.43%, and LPIPS drops from 0.28–0.33 to 0.0835. Against PAB, FasterCache provides a clear speed advantage (1.54–1.68× vs. 1.23–1.35×) and matches or exceeds PAB on VBench (e.g., 80.84% vs. 79.56% on Vchitect-2.0; 80.19% vs. 80.06% on Open-Sora-Plan). On fidelity metrics, FasterCache achieves lower LPIPS and higher SSIM/PSNR than PAB in most settings (e.g., LPIPS 0.0282 vs. 0.0489, PSNR 31.45 vs. 27.38 on Vchitect-2.0), indicating better preservation of fine details. In the user preference study, raters preferred FasterCache over PAB 69–74% of the time and over Δ-DiT 77–81% of the time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cache-based acceleration line of work (DeepCache, Faster Diffusion, Δ-DiT, PAB) by identifying a fundamental flaw in their core assumption: directly reusing adjacent-step attention features, despite high MSE similarity, degrades video quality due to subtle inter-step variations. It departs from prior methods in two key ways: (1) replacing blind feature reuse with a dynamic strategy based on first-order Taylor approximation to preserve feature distinction, and (2) introducing CFG-Cache, which opens an entirely new acceleration dimension by exploiting redundancy between conditional and unconditional outputs within the same timestep — a target that prior cache-based methods had not explored. The frequency-domain bias analysis draws conceptual inspiration from FreeU but applies it to the CFG context rather than U-Net feature maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training-free acceleration of video diffusion models is practically critical given that current systems take 2–5 minutes to synthesize a 6-second 480P video, and this paper achieves 1.6–1.7× speedup across five diverse models while matching or slightly exceeding baseline quality. The CFG-Cache insight — that conditional and unconditional outputs share high redundancy at the same timestep but low similarity across adjacent timesteps — opens a new axis of optimization that is orthogonal to attention-level caching and could be combined with future architectural changes. Limitations include degraded performance on complex high-motion scenes and dependence on base model quality; the authors note that adaptive caching strategies remain an open direction for future work.
