## Problem

Nearest neighbor graphs built from noisy samples of a low-dimensional manifold contain shortcut edges that bridge distant neighborhoods through the ambient space, distorting geodesic distances and degrading downstream tasks like manifold learning, persistent homology, and dimension estimation. Existing pruning heuristics (density-based, MST-based, distance-based) lack theoretical justification and fail on manifolds with multiple connected components or varying curvature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250802/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a k-NN or ε-radius nearest neighbor graph over noisy point cloud samples from a tubular neighborhood of a compact submanifold, plus two tolerance parameters δ, λ ∈ [0,1]. It outputs a pruned graph with shortcut edges removed. A key implementation detail is that ORC is computed on unweighted edges (all snapped to weight 1) to ensure scale invariance and bound curvature values to [-2, +1], while the graph-distance validation step uses Euclidean edge weights ‖x−y‖₂.

## Method

ORC-MANL operates in two stages. First, it computes Ollivier-Ricci curvature for every edge using the 1-Wasserstein distance between uniform measures over each endpoint's neighborhood (excluding the edge partner), flagging edges with κ ≤ −1+4(1−δ) as shortcut candidates. Second, it constructs a thresholded graph G′ by removing all candidates, then for each candidate edge (x,y) computes the weighted shortest-path distance d_{G′}(x,y); if this exceeds the theoretically derived bound π(π+1)(1−λ)/(2√(24λ)) · ε, the edge is permanently removed. The theoretical justification rests on three results: shortcut edges have ORC → −1 as noise vanishes, every vertex acquires arbitrarily many non-shortcut edges with ORC → +1 as sample size grows, and the graph distance in G′ between shortcut edge endpoints is necessarily large relative to ε.

## Results

On 2D synthetic manifolds, ORC-MANL removes 0.0% of good edges while removing 89.4–100.0% of shortcut edges across all five test manifolds (Concentric Tori, Concentric Hyperboloids, Hyperboloid+Paraboloid, Paraboloids, Swiss Roll), using fixed parameters δ=0.8 and λ=0.01. In contrast, ORC ONLY achieves comparable shortcut removal (~100%) but removes 13–18% of good edges, and all other baselines (BISECTION, MST, DENSITY, DISTANCE) remove far fewer shortcut edges (1–92%) while also removing 0.4–63.6% of good edges. Downstream, ORC-MANL pruning improves Isomap, LLE, UMAP, and t-SNE embeddings on the Swiss Roll, reduces Wasserstein distance to ground-truth persistence diagrams, and improves pointwise intrinsic dimension and scalar curvature estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORC-MANL dominates all four baselines on the joint objective of removing shortcut edges while preserving good edges. ORC ONLY (which skips the graph-distance validation step) matches ORC-MANL on shortcut removal (~100%) but removes 13–18% of good edges, confirming that the second-stage filtering is essential. BISECTION (Xia et al., 2008) performs poorly on most manifolds (8.8–61.3% shortcut removal), excelling only on manifolds where shortcut edges traverse long distances through low-density regions. MST (Zemel & Carreira-Perpiñán, 2004) achieves high shortcut removal on single-component manifolds (100% on Swiss Roll, 89.6% on Concentric Tori) but removes 20–29% of good edges and struggles on multi-component manifolds (58.5% on Hyperboloid+Paraboloid). DENSITY (Chao et al., 2006) and DISTANCE are the weakest baselines, with DENSITY removing only 0.6–16.8% of shortcut edges while destroying up to 63.6% of good edges on Paraboloids. Notably, ORC-MANL uses fixed hyperparameters (δ=0.8, λ=0.01) across all manifolds, whereas MST, DENSITY, and DISTANCE required per-manifold manual threshold tuning (Table 3), yet still underperform.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Ollivier-Ricci curvature (ORC), previously applied to community detection in network science (Sia et al., 2019; Ni et al., 2019), for the distinct task of identifying shortcut edges in nearest-neighbor graphs built from noisy manifold samples. It departs from prior graph-pruning heuristics (density-based bisection, MST, distance thresholds) by providing a theoretically grounded criterion rooted in Riemannian geometry: shortcut edges necessarily exhibit ORC ≤ −1 in the vanishing-noise limit, while non-shortcut edges concentrate near +1. The key methodological departure is the use of unweighted edges for ORC computation (bounding values to [−2, +1]) and a two-stage validation via graph-distance in a thresholded subgraph, which prior ORC-based pruning (Sia et al.) did not employ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ORC-MANL offers a theoretically justified, parameter-light preprocessing step that improves a wide range of downstream geometric data analysis tasks (Isomap, LLE, persistent homology, intrinsic dimension estimation, scalar curvature) without requiring per-dataset tuning, which is practically valuable for the manifold learning pipeline. However, the theoretical guarantees hold only in the limit of vanishing noise and infinite samples, and the empirical validation relies on relatively small synthetic manifolds and qualitative real-data (scRNAseq) results, leaving open how the method behaves under heavy noise, high intrinsic dimension, or very large-scale datasets. An open question is whether the unweighted-edge ORC modification and the specific threshold −1 + 4(1−δ) generalize beyond the ε-radius connectivity assumed in the theory to the k-NN graphs used in practice.
