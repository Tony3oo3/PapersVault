## Problem

Existing post-hoc detoxification methods for LLMs (decoding-time control, embedding/logits modification, model editing) are model-specific: they require training a separate toxic model or module for each target architecture and tokenizer, and demand per-model hyperparameter tuning due to the trade-off between detoxification efficacy and language modeling performance. No standardized, universally applicable approach exists that can consistently remove toxic content across diverse LLMs without model-specific optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a base LLM (e.g., GPT-2 XL) and a toxic text corpus (DGHS, 25,150 examples across gender, sexual orientation, race, and religion domains) as input. It produces a compact set of 640 detoxifying text sequences (each up to 256 tokens) distilled via contrastive decoding, which can then be used to fine-tune any LLM—regardless of architecture, size, or tokenizer—into a detoxified model. Notably, the detoxifying text is distilled solely from GPT-2 XL and transferred directly to OPT-6.7B, Falcon-7B, and LLaMA2-7B without re-distillation or per-model hyperparameter tuning.

## Method

UNIDETOX creates a toxic model by fine-tuning the base model on toxic text, then distills detoxifying text via contrastive decoding between the base and toxic models, where the contrastive score is the difference in log-probabilities assigned by the two models. An adaptive plausibility constraint (parameter α) filters out tokens with low base-model probability to maintain text coherence. The key theoretical insight is that under a first-order Taylor approximation, the contrastive score equals the dot product between the negative toxic vector (−τ_toxic) and the gradient of the log-likelihood, meaning contrastive decoding preferentially samples texts whose fine-tuning gradients push the model in the anti-toxicity direction—equivalent in effect to Task Arithmetic but realized through discrete, transferable text. This avoids the computationally expensive second-order derivatives required by gradient-matching dataset distillation, and because the output is plain text rather than continuous parameters, it can be applied to any LLM with a different architecture or tokenizer.

## Results

UNIDETOX (lr=5e-5) achieves the lowest Toxicity Probability across all evaluated models when using detoxifying text distilled solely from GPT-2 XL: TP drops from 0.78→0.28 (ID) and 0.82→0.17 (OOD) on OPT-6.7B, from 0.60→0.33 (ID) and 0.53→0.27 (OOD) on Falcon-7B, and from 0.58→0.29 (ID) and 0.49→0.26 (OOD) on LLaMA2-7B. Perplexity remains competitive (10.62, 7.85, 7.70 respectively), and MMLU accuracy degrades by at most ~5 points. A single hyperparameter configuration (α=0.1, lr=5e-5, 3000 steps) tuned on GPT-2 XL transfers directly to all larger models without re-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIDETOX (lr=5e-5) outperforms all three baselines on OPT-6.7B, reducing TP from 0.58 (Task Arithmetic, best baseline) to 0.28, and from 0.62 (DEXPERTS) to 0.28, while also achieving the lowest perplexity (10.62 vs. 25.89–28.19). On LLaMA2-7B, UNIDETOX again leads with TP 0.29 vs. 0.45 (DEXPERTS) and 0.47 (LM-Steer), with Task Arithmetic failing to improve over the base model (TP 0.58). On Falcon-7B, DEXPERTS achieves a slightly lower TP (0.30 vs. 0.33) but at substantially higher perplexity (28.71 vs. 7.85), and LM-Steer is competitive on TP (0.37) but degrades PPL to 29.05. Critically, UNIDETOX applies a single hyperparameter set across all models, whereas LM-Steer, DEXPERTS, and Task Arithmetic each require model-specific toxic modules and separate hyperparameter tuning (e.g., DEXPERTS β ranges from 0.1 to 1.8, Task Arithmetic λ from 0.04 to 0.14), and their performance is highly sensitive to these choices—Task Arithmetic with λ=0.14 inflates perplexity to 275.51 on Falcon-7B but only 25.81 on OPT-6.7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNIDETOX repurposes contrastive decoding (Liu et al., 2021; Li et al., 2023) from its traditional role as a decoding-time control mechanism (as in DEXPERTS) into a dataset distillation procedure for LLMs, drawing on the gradient-matching framework of dataset distillation (Wang et al., 2018; Zhao et al., 2020) while avoiding the computationally prohibitive second-order derivatives those methods require. The theoretical connection to Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) is made explicit: fine-tuning on contrastively decoded text approximates subtracting the toxic vector from model parameters, but in a form that produces discrete, transferable text rather than model-specific weight edits. This positions UNIDETOX at the intersection of dataset distillation (previously applied to image and text classification) and LLM safety, extending the former to the generative text domain and the latter to a model-agnostic fine-tuning paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating per-model hyperparameter tuning and toxic-module training, which are major deployment bottlenecks for existing detoxification methods; a single distilled text corpus and learning-rate configuration transfers across GPT-2, OPT-6.7B, Falcon-7B, and LLaMA-2-7B. The political-bias analysis of the distilled text (reduced left-leaning skew) offers a concrete, interpretable signal about what content properties drive detoxification efficacy, potentially guiding future data curation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to toxicity types beyond hate speech (e.g., misinformation, self-harm), whether the small distilled corpus (640 sequences) suffices for models substantially larger than 7B, and whether the diversity degradation at higher learning rates can be mitigated without sacrificing detoxification strength.
