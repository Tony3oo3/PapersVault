## Problem

Existing skill transfer methods in reinforcement learning learn only flat sets of sub-policies that choose directly among primitive actions, lacking the ability to compose skills hierarchically across multiple levels of abstraction. While a few multi-level hierarchy methods exist, none use skill transfer as an explicit mechanism for accelerating adaptation to unseen tasks, and state-based sub-goal approaches fail to transfer across different state spaces. This paper addresses the gap by asking how to discover, select, and compose multi-level skills that are explicitly optimised for reuse in future, unseen tasks.

## Input / Output

In tabular settings the agent receives a 7×7 ego-centric grid observation (walls, goal direction) plus a scalar reward-direction indicator; in deep Procgen settings it receives 64×64×3 RGB frames. Beyond the live observation, the method consumes a bank of trajectories from previously completed tasks, from which fixed-length action sequences (fractures of chain length b) are extracted and clustered. The agent's output at each step is either a primitive action or a temporally-extended FraCO option; once created, FraCOs are frozen and the agent only learns a meta-policy over the combined set of primitives and options.

## Method

FraCOs builds a multi-level skill hierarchy bottom-up in three stages. First, it extracts all length-b action sequences (fractures) from successful trajectories, embeds them, and clusters them with HDBSCAN (or, in deep settings, groups by action sequence alone and uses a GAN-augmented classifier for initiation prediction). Second, it ranks clusters by an expected-usefulness score that averages three terms: a Bayesian appearance probability (Beta-Bernoulli posterior over whether the cluster appears in a successful trajectory), a relative frequency among all successful fractures, and a normalised Shannon entropy of usage across tasks. Third, the top-ranked clusters are converted into options with a threshold-based initiation set, a deterministic policy that executes the highest-probability fracture's action sequence (recursing into nested options), and a termination condition triggered when the sequence completes or a nested option cannot be matched. The process is repeated iteratively so that higher-level fractures may contain lower-level options as actions, yielding a true multi-level hierarchy. Unlike top-down approaches such as DDO, FraCOs selects options explicitly for generality and avoids state-based sub-goals, making it transferable across state spaces; unlike Option-Critic variants, the fixed option set and threshold-based initiation naturally prevent option collapse.

## Results

In tabular grid-world experiments (Four Rooms, Nine Rooms, Ramesh Maze, MetaGrid), FraCOs demonstrates progressively faster learning on unseen tasks as the hierarchy depth increases from 1 to 4 levels, with episodic returns converging more rapidly at deeper levels. In the deep Procgen experiments across eight environments, FraCOs and FraCOs-SSR achieve the highest mean min-max normalised IQM returns on both IID and OOD tasks, outperforming PPO, OC-PPO, and PPG. The OOD advantage is particularly pronounced, with FraCOs maintaining performance on unseen Procgen levels (beyond level 100) where baselines degrade more sharply. Results are reported as interquartile means (IQM) over eight seeds with standard error bands.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FraCOs is compared against three baselines: PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), OC-PPO (Klissarov et al., 2017), and Phasic Policy Gradient/PPG (Cobbe et al., 2021). Across all eight Procgen environments, FraCOs (without SSR) consistently outperforms all three baselines on both IID and OOD min-max normalised IQM returns, with the gap widening on OOD tasks drawn from unseen Procgen levels. FraCOs-SSR, which retains shared convolutional features after the warm-up phase, further extends this advantage and provides a fairer comparison to OC-PPO (which inherently relies on a shared state representation). In the tabular experiments, FraCOs at depth 3–4 substantially accelerates convergence on unseen reward and state generalisation tasks compared to a flat Q-learning agent, with no direct baseline comparison at equivalent hierarchy depth. The paper notes that OC-PPO suffers from option collapse in standard implementations, which the PPO-based variant mitigates but does not fully eliminate, whereas FraCOs' fixed option structure naturally avoids this failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

FraCOs extends the options framework (Sutton et al., 1999) by introducing a bottom-up, trajectory-driven method for discovering multi-level hierarchical skills, in contrast to top-down approaches like DDO (Fox et al., 2017) that rely on expert demonstrations. It departs from simultaneous option-learning methods such as HOC (Riemer et al., 2018) and Option-Critic by using a principled usefulness metric—combining Bayesian appearance probability, relative frequency, and entropy—to select which fracture clusters become options, thereby avoiding option collapse. The clustering of action-sequence 'fractures' via HDBSCAN and the iterative construction of hierarchy levels from mixed primitive/option trajectories represent the core methodological novelty, positioning FraCOs between skill-discovery and hierarchical policy-gradient methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FraCOs offers a principled, interpretable mechanism for building transferable skill hierarchies that accelerate OOD generalisation, addressing a persistent gap where existing skill-transfer methods are limited to single-level options over primitive actions. The consistent improvement with hierarchy depth in tabular settings and the outperformance of PPO, OC-PPO, and PPG on Procgen (both IID and OOD) provide encouraging evidence that usefulness-guided option selection is a viable design principle. However, the method is currently restricted to discrete action spaces, and the deep-learning implementation required substantial simplifications (action-sequence-only clustering, neural-network initiation prediction) that weaken the connection to the core fracture-clustering idea, leaving open whether the approach scales to continuous control or high-dimensional perceptual inputs without further architectural changes.
