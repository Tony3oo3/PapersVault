## Problem

While alignment and bias of LLM-as-a-Judge evaluators have received substantial attention, the stability of model-based LLM evaluation—specifically whether evaluators produce consistent, reliable judgments across inputs and domains—remains largely unexplored. This paper investigates the prevalence, sources, and practical consequences of evaluation uncertainty in LLM evaluators, and asks whether uncertainty can be both mitigated and leveraged to improve evaluation quality, particularly on out-of-distribution data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251893/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the ConfiLM evaluator, each input instance consists of a user instruction, two candidate responses, and the verbalized response confidence of each response (mapped from token-probability averages into natural-language statements like 'Highly confident' or 'Clearly confident' to avoid numerical reasoning errors). The output is a preference label (1 or 2) accompanied by a brief evaluation explanation. Evaluation confidence itself is extracted as the token probability of the preference token during generation, while response confidence is computed as the mean probability across all generated tokens of the candidate model.

## Method

The paper first conducts a systematic empirical study across 9 LLM evaluators (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, GPT-3.5-Turbo, Llama3-70B, Llama2-70B, Qwen2-72B, Prometheus2-7B, Prometheus2-bgb-8x7B, PandaLM-7B) under single-answer grading and pairwise comparison on MT-Bench and PandaLM, quantifying uncertainty via token probabilities. Key findings show that CoT prompting and specially trained evaluators (e.g., Prometheus2) substantially reduce evaluation uncertainty, while self-preference bias inflates confidence within the same model family. To exploit uncertainty for OOD evaluation, the authors fine-tune Llama3-8B-Instruct into ConfiLM on 694 human-annotated pairwise instances from Alpaca 52K, incorporating verbalized response confidence as auxiliary input. ConfiLM is evaluated on a newly constructed Olympic 2024 test set (220 instances across writing, math, extraction, reasoning, and roleplay, annotated by three PhD-level evaluators with 97.27% agreement), where it outperforms its non-confidence baseline by 3.9% F1, demonstrating that uncertainty-aware fine-tuning improves OOD evaluation robustness.

## Results

On the Olympic 2024 OOD test set (220 instances), ConfiLM achieves an overall F1 of 0.621 with 0.982 evaluation confidence, outperforming its ablation Llama3-8B-Instruct-Finetune (0.582 F1) by 3.9% and the base Llama3-8B-Instruct (0.536 F1) by 8.5%. All 12 evaluators struggle on this OOD benchmark, with the best overall F1 reaching only 0.678 (GPT-4o). On the Writing subtask, ConfiLM achieves 0.723 F1, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (0.391) and GPT-4o-mini (0.423), demonstrating that incorporating response confidence as auxiliary information is particularly effective for detecting hallucinations in creative tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConfiLM (0.621 F1) outperforms its direct ablation Llama3-8B-Instruct-Finetune (0.582) by 3.9% and the un-fine-tuned Llama3-8B-Instruct (0.536) by 8.5%, confirming that incorporating response confidence during fine-tuning improves OOD evaluation. Against proprietary models, ConfiLM trails GPT-4o (0.678) and GPT-4o-mini (0.677) by 5.7% and 5.6% respectively on overall F1, but dramatically surpasses them on the Writing subtask (0.723 vs. 0.391/0.423), where hallucination detection is critical. Among open-source general LLMs, ConfiLM beats Qwen2-72B-Instruct (0.631) by a narrow margin and outperforms Llama3-70B-Instruct (0.542) and Llama2-70B-Instruct (0.534) by 7.9% and 8.7%. Against specialized evaluators, ConfiLM surpasses PandaLM-7B (0.560), Prometheus2-bgb-8x7B (0.556), and Prometheus2-7B (0.515) by 6.1%, 6.5%, and 10.6% respectively. The key finding is that while no single evaluator dominates all subtasks, ConfiLM's uncertainty-aware design provides the most consistent gains in OOD scenarios, particularly where factual accuracy (Writing, Extraction) matters most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation paradigm (Zheng et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024b) and confidence estimation for LLMs (Varshney et al., 2023; Kumar et al., 2024), repurposing token-probability-based confidence from the hallucination-detection literature to study evaluation stability. While prior work has extensively examined alignment and bias in LLM evaluators, the authors explicitly position their contribution as filling a gap in understanding evaluation *stability*—a dimension they argue has been underexplored. The ConfiLM model extends the idea of using model confidence for OOD detection (Yang et al., 2024b; Farquhar et al., 2024) into the evaluation setting, where response confidence of candidate models becomes an auxiliary input to the evaluator rather than a signal for the model's own reliability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM-as-a-Judge becomes the dominant scalable evaluation paradigm, understanding when and why evaluators are unreliable is practically critical for anyone building or trusting automated evaluation pipelines. The finding that the most capable general model (GPT-4o) exhibits the lowest evaluation confidence challenges the implicit assumption that capability transfers to evaluation stability, and the Olympic 2024 dataset (220 instances, 97.27% annotator agreement) provides a small but useful OOD benchmark. Key limitations include the restriction to single-round evaluations, dependence on token probabilities (excluding models like Claude), and the modest 3.9% F1 gain from ConfiLM over its fine-tuned baseline, leaving open whether uncertainty-aware evaluation scales to more complex multi-turn or multimodal settings.
