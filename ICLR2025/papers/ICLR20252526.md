## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks for VLMs treat spatial relations as unambiguous text-image matching, overlooking the fundamental ambiguity introduced by frames of reference (FoR) — the coordinate system a speaker or listener adopts to interpret expressions like 'to the left of.' This paper asks whether VLMs represent space and how, specifically whether they adopt language-specific FoR conventions when resolving ambiguous spatial expressions, and whether their spatial representations are robust and consistent across continuous spatial configurations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252526/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each test case pairs a synthetic 3D-rendered image (Blender) showing a referent object at one of 36 angular positions (10° intervals) around a relatum with a text query of the form 'Is [A] [relation] [B]?' optionally prefixed with a perspective specification (camera, addressee, or relatum viewpoint). The model outputs a Yes/No judgment, but evaluation extracts response probabilities to compute region parsing errors against analytically motivated reference curves (hemisphere indicator and cosine mapping), plus robustness metrics (standard deviation across scene variants, prediction noise via Butterworth low-pass filtering) and consistency metrics (spatial symmetric and opposite), going well beyond binary accuracy.

## Method

COMFORT introduces two complementary datasets — COMFORT-BALL (non-fronted relata, testing coordinate transformation conventions: translated, rotated, reflected) and COMFORT-CAR (fronted relata with a human addressee, testing FoR preferences: egocentric, intrinsic, addressee-centered) — rendered in Blender with continuous 360° rotational trajectories to capture spatial continuity rather than discrete axis-aligned positions. The core methodological contribution is a multi-dimensional metric suite: region parsing error (ε) comparing normalized response probabilities against hemisphere and cosine reference functions, robustness metrics (standard deviation across object/scene variants and prediction noise as RMSE against a Butterworth low-pass filter), and consistency metrics (spatial symmetric consistency for θ/−θ pairs and spatial opposite consistency for antonym relations). The protocol extends to 109 languages across 170 regions to test whether multilingual VLMs follow language-specific FoR conventions or default to English. Nine VLMs are evaluated, revealing that most prefer the egocentric relative FoR with reflected projection (the English convention) but fail to flexibly adopt alternative FoRs when explicitly prompted, and show poor robustness and consistency in continuous spatial space.

## Results

On COMFORT-BALL (egocentric relative FoR with reflected transformation), the best-performing model InternLM-XComposer2 achieves 92.4% accuracy and a cosine region parsing error (ε_cos) of 20.0×10⁻², while GPT-4o reaches 89.2% accuracy and ε_cos of 27.4×10⁻². On the harder COMFORT-CAR task (which requires disambiguating among three FoRs), XComposer2 drops to 85.6% accuracy (ε_cos = 18.8) and GPT-4o to 78.3% (ε_cos = 28.1). Critically, even the strongest models show poor spatial consistency: GPT-4o's spatial opposite consistency (c_opp) is 14.2/16.7 (BALL/CAR), barely better than the 30-trial random baseline of 44.2, and the BLIP series models perform at chance level (47.2% accuracy) due to severe object hallucination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper evaluating 9 VLMs, so the 'baselines' are the models under test. InternLM-XComposer2 achieves the highest accuracy (92.4% BALL, 85.6% CAR) and lowest ε_cos (20.0/18.8), outperforming GPT-4o (89.2%/78.3%, ε_cos 27.4/28.1) and MiniCPM-V (81.0%/72.4%, ε_cos 22.4/24.6). LLaVA-1.5-7B achieves the best ε_cos on CAR (18.4) but only moderate accuracy (55.2%), while GLaMM—despite near-perfect object grounding (F1 ≈ 100%)—performs at chance accuracy (47.2%), showing that pixel-level grounding does not translate to spatial reasoning. The InstructBLIP series (7B/13B) and mBLIP-BLOOMZ all hover at or near chance (47.2–51.9% accuracy) with the worst robustness (σ up to 48.6) and consistency (c_opp up to 42.0), largely due to severe object hallucination. A key finding is that GPT-4o, despite strong accuracy, has the worst prediction noise (η = 43.1/38.8) and spatial opposite consistency (c_opp = 14.2/16.7) among top-accuracy models, indicating its spatial representations are less geometrically coherent than those of LLaVA-1.5-7B (c_opp = 8.3/10.7) or XComposer2 (c_opp = 10.5/12.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of VLM spatial reasoning benchmarks (Kamath et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023a) by introducing frame-of-reference (FoR) ambiguity as a first-class evaluation dimension, drawing on the spatial cognition literature (Levinson, 2003; Frank, 1998) that distinguishes egocentric relative, addressee-centered relative, and object-centered intrinsic FoRs. The task formulation follows object hallucination evaluation setups (Li et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024c), but departs from prior spatial benchmarks by incorporating spatial continuity (rotational trajectories rather than fixed canonical axes), novel robustness/consistency metrics, and a multilingual evaluation across 109 languages. Unlike SpatialVLM or SpatialRGPT, which aim to improve spatial reasoning through additional training data, COMFORT is purely an evaluation protocol designed to diagnose how VLMs resolve spatial ambiguities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that VLMs default to English conventions (egocentric relative FoR with reflected projection) across all 109 tested languages raises serious concerns about cultural bias in multilingual AI systems, echoing the Linguistic Transmission Hypothesis from cognitive science. The near-chance performance when models are explicitly prompted to adopt intrinsic or addressee-centered FoRs—even for GPT-4o—reveals a fundamental gap in perspective-taking that is a prerequisite for human-like situated communication. Open questions include whether 3D training with camera poses and multiview data can address the perspective-taking deficit, and whether naturally generated multilingual image-text data (rather than machine-translated captions) can prevent English dominance. Limitations include synthetic-only scenes, no occlusion, and simplified pragmatic contexts.
