## Problem

Existing entropic-regularized OT solvers face a fundamental tradeoff: methods with strong theoretical convergence guarantees (e.g., APDAGD, Jambulapati et al.) underperform aggressively tuned Sinkhorn iterations in wall-clock time, while fast GPU-parallel methods like Sinkhorn or Mirror Sinkhorn hit a precision wall at high accuracy. No prior algorithm simultaneously achieves superlinear local convergence, GPU parallelization, numerical stability at weak regularization, and competitive wall-clock performance in high dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an n×n cost matrix C with entries in [0,1] (constructed from pairwise L1 or L2² distances between pixel locations or RGB values), source and target marginals r, c ∈ Δ_n, and initial/final temperature parameters γ_i and γ_f. It outputs a transport plan P ∈ U(r,c) (rounded onto the feasible polytope) and the corresponding dual variables (u, v) ∈ R^{2n}. Experiments use images upsampled to 64×64 (n=4096) for benchmarking and 1024×1024 (n≈10⁶) for scalability, with a memory-efficient O(n) variant via PyKeOps that computes C and P on-the-fly.

## Method

The core contribution is a truncated Newton solver for the EOT dual problem that replaces the PSD Hessian with a ρ-discounted PD version (dampening off-diagonal entries by √ρ), which yields Bellman-equation structure and enables matrix inversion for theoretical guarantees. A diagonally-preconditioned linear CG solver (Alg. 2) approximately solves the resulting n-variable system while annealing ρ from 0 toward 1, with a helper ChiSinkhorn routine (Alg. 3) bounding the χ² divergence to control the CG condition number. The Newton direction is combined with an Armijo backtracking line search (Alg. 4) to produce Bregman projections, achieving local quadratic convergence in the L1 gradient norm without assuming a Lipschitz Hessian. The entire solver is embedded in the MDOT temperature annealing framework, where an adaptive schedule (adjusting the decay rate q based on the ratio δ_k of actual to predicted gradient reduction) eliminates the need to tune q and keeps the algorithm in the superlinear convergence zone. Asymmetric marginal smoothing (allocating more of the smoothing budget to the row marginal) ensures numerical stability at high precision.

## Results

On n=4096 dimensional OT problems (upsampled MNIST and color transfer with L₁ and L₂² costs), MDOT-TruncatedNewton solves to 6-decimal precision in 1–5 seconds on an RTX 2080 Ti GPU, and demonstrates numerical stability up to 9-decimal precision. At the highest precision range, it is more than 10× faster than the best alternative (MDOT-PNCG). The empirical error-vs-time dependence ranges from O(n²ε^(−1/6)) to O(n²ε^(−1/2)), substantially better than the best known theoretical rate of Õ(n²ε^(−1)). Scalability is demonstrated on a 1024×1024 color transfer problem (n≈10⁶) solved in ~10 hours with ~600 MB memory using an O(n) memory-footprint variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDOT-TruncatedNewton outperforms all six compared baselines by orders of magnitude at high precision (≥6 decimal digits) on both upsampled MNIST and color transfer problem sets (n=4096, 24 total problem configurations). The closest competitor, MDOT-PNCG (Kemertas et al., 2025), is surpassed by more than 10× in wall-clock time at the highest precision range. Feydy's (2020) temperature-annealing approach (Alg. 3.5) hits a 'precision wall' because a single Sinkhorn update per temperature decay is insufficient to control the dual objective. Mirror Sinkhorn (Ballu & Berthet, 2023), APDAGD (Dvurechensky et al., 2018), Mirror Prox Sherman Optimized (Jambulapati et al., 2019), and AAM (Guminov et al., 2021) all exhibit significantly slower convergence in the GPU-parallel, high-precision regime. The authors additionally omit APDAMD, APDRCD, and PDASMD from direct comparison, noting they exhibit 'significantly longer convergence times' in the high-dimensional GPU setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MDOT temperature annealing framework of Kemertas et al. (2025), replacing its non-linear conjugate gradient inner solver (PNCG) with a truncated Newton method that uses a ρ-discounted Hessian. The discounting strategy—dampening off-diagonal entries rather than amplifying diagonal ones as in Tikhonov regularization—yields a connection to Bellman equations in reinforcement learning, reducing the 2n-variable PSD Newton system to an n-variable PD system. The paper also integrates with classical truncated Newton theory (Nocedal & Wright, Nash) but departs by proving local quadratic convergence without a Lipschitz Hessian assumption and by bounding the L1 gradient norm (the natural stopping criterion in OT) rather than L2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The algorithm achieves 6-decimal precision on n=4096 problems in 1–5 seconds on a 2018-era GPU, outperforming the best alternatives (including MDOT-PNCG) by more than 10× at high precision, and scales to n≈10⁶ with only ~600 MB memory via on-the-fly cost computation. The adaptive temperature schedule eliminates a key hyperparameter (q) that previously required manual tuning, and the asymmetric marginal smoothing improves numerical stability at extreme precision. Open questions include deriving a global convergence rate for the inner solver, developing a stochastic variant with O(nm) memory for very high dimensions, and whether the empirical Õ(n²ε^{-1/6}) rate can be made rigorous.
