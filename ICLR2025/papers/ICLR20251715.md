## Problem

Over 60% of public models on Hugging Face lack accurate documentation of their training lineage, making it impossible to resolve intellectual property disputes, attribute fine-tuned models to their parents, or detect wrongful use of proprietary training data. Existing approaches either rely on incomplete metadata, require expensive gradient computations with access to training data, or can only identify a single parent rather than recovering full hierarchical structures. This paper introduces the task of Unsupervised Model Tree Heritage Recovery (MoTHer Recovery), which aims to reconstruct the complete directed tree of fine-tuning relationships from model weights alone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the raw weight matrices of a collection of neural networks (no input data, no forward passes, no gradients) along with a binary label per model indicating whether it was trained in a generalization or specialization stage. It outputs a directed Model Graph — a forest of Model Trees — where each directed edge (parent → child) encodes that the child was fine-tuned from the parent. The output is a set of directed edges over the input models, fully specifying the hierarchical lineage without requiring any prior knowledge of which models are related.

## Method

The approach exploits two empirical properties of model weights: (1) the L2 weight distance (or, for LoRA models, the rank of the weight difference) between two models correlates strongly (ρ=0.99) with their edge distance on the Model Tree, and (2) the kurtosis of layer weights changes monotonically during training — increasing during generalization (pre-training) and decreasing during specialization (fine-tuning) — providing a directional signal. These are combined into a pairwise distance matrix D and a binary directional matrix K (adjusted by the training-stage label via XOR), merged into a single cost matrix M = D + λ(K ⊕ T). The Model Tree is then recovered by running the Chu-Liu-Edmonds minimum directed spanning tree algorithm on M. For collections containing multiple unrelated trees, hierarchical clustering over pairwise L2 distances first partitions models into separate Model Trees before the MDST is applied independently to each cluster.

## Results

On the MoTHer dataset (500+ models across 20+ Model Trees), MoTHer achieves perfect accuracy (1.0) on the LoRA-V sub-graph and 0.96 on LoRA-F, 0.94 on full fine-tuning (FT), and 0.83 on the Mixed sub-graph at the Model Graph level. On the in-the-wild Stable Diffusion hierarchy (5 models, 4 levels), MoTHer correctly reconstructs all but a single edge. Scaling to a deeper 5-level tree with 121 models yields 0.79 accuracy, and the method generalizes to ResNet-50 with perfect reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper introduces a new task (Unsupervised MoTHer Recovery) and does not compare against a direct baseline method for the full pipeline. The closest prior work, Yu & Wang (2024), uses model Jacobians to identify a parent from a suspected set but cannot recover edge direction or complex hierarchies, and requires running samples through the model. As implicit ablation baselines, replacing the LoRA-specific rank-based distance (ℓ_LoRA) with the generic full fine-tuning distance (ℓ_FT) drops LoRA-V accuracy by 22% (from 1.0 to 0.78), confirming the importance of the low-rank prior. Among directional scores, kurtosis is the only metric that remains consistently monotonic across all Model Trees, outperforming variance, skewness, and entropy. The method also shows robustness to 90% pruning (only 4% accuracy drop) and 50% quantization (<1% drop), with 68% accuracy even at 99% pruning versus a ~5% random baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of weight-space learning and graph-theoretic structure recovery. The most directly related prior work is Yu & Wang (2024, Neural Lineage), which uses model Jacobians to identify a parent from a suspected candidate set, but cannot recover edge direction or full hierarchical structure, and requires running data through the model. This paper departs by being fully data-free and unsupervised, using only raw weights, and formulating the problem as a minimum directed spanning tree over a combined distance-and-direction matrix. It also draws on the broader weight-space literature (Eilertsen et al. 2020, Schürholt et al. 2024) for the premise that weights encode rich training information, and on the Chu-Liu-Edmonds algorithm from combinatorial optimization for the tree-recovery step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With over 60% of Hugging Face models lacking usable heritage documentation, automated model attribution is a pressing practical need for IP protection, legal dispute resolution, and detecting proprietary data misuse. The method's simplicity (weight distance + kurtosis + MST) and data-free nature make it broadly applicable, and the 500+ model MoTHer dataset provides a reusable benchmark. Key open questions include: automatically inferring the training stage (generalization vs. specialization) without supervision, scaling to web-scale model collections (millions of models), and handling merged models with multiple parents. The current assumption that training stage is known limits real-world deployment.
