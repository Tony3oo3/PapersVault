## Problem

Humor generation requires multi-hop creative reasoning over a sparse knowledge graph, where each reasoning hop depends on non-obvious rationales connecting disparate concepts. Existing approaches like CLoT only train on question-answer pairs (the endpoints of the reasoning path), capturing surface patterns without enabling genuine 'thinking outside the box,' and even strong reasoning models like GPT-4o and QwQ underperform on humor tasks. The paper addresses this by explicitly enriching the knowledge graph with external information and structured reasoning rationales to support multi-hop humor reasoning.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a single text question (e.g., a community post or game prompt) and produces a short, conversational humorous response—typically one to two sentences, deliberately briefer than GPT-4o or CLoT to leave interpretive space for the reader. For judgment, it takes a question paired with two to four candidate answers and selects the funniest, optionally producing a causal rationale explaining the humor mechanism (puns, irony, incongruity). During training, seed conversation datasets are consumed by a three-agent instruction-evolution pipeline that outputs multi-turn dialogue pairs with progressively enriched instructions, and the DPO stage consumes (question, preferred answer, dispreferred answer, rationale) tuples.

## Method

LoL is a two-stage training framework built on Qwen1.5-32B with LoRA. Stage 1 (SFT) combines human-designed judgment-oriented templates (selection, ranking, answer rewriting, and a teacher-student prompt loop that simulates trial-and-error, reflection, and backtracking) with an Automatic Instruction Evolution (AIE) system using three cooperative agents—Generator, Imaginator, and Selector/Critic—that iteratively enrich seed conversations over up to three rounds, with the Imaginator specifically probing the boundaries of the underlying knowledge graph by guessing alternative storylines from the punchline. Stage 2 (GESIT) performs online DPO where a frozen copy of the Stage-1 model acts as a pairwise humor judge over newly sampled responses, while GPT-4o extracts causal rationales for each answer, re-introducing external knowledge into the preference data to reinforce logical reasoning and human-preference alignment. The core insight is framing humor as a knowledge-graph traversal problem where correlation entities (E_Z) bridge question and answer entities, and that enriching both entities and relations through instruction evolution and rationale-augmented preference learning enables the multi-hop creative leaps that pattern-matching alone cannot achieve.

## Results

On English humor-judgment benchmarks, LoL (32B) achieves 96.58% on SemEval 2021 2T1, 48.06% on 3T1, 35.90% on 4T1, 64.57% on SemEval 2020 2T1, and 97.20% on Oogiri-GO 2T1, outperforming GPT-4o by an average of 5.91% across all English test sets. On Chinese benchmarks, LoL reaches 90.95% (2T1) and 69.97% (2T1-hard), a 16.22% average improvement over GPT-4o. On the Ruozhiba dataset, LoL scores 95.35% top-1 accuracy versus 90.80% for QwQ-32B and 76.40% for GPT-4o. In human evaluation (3000 votes across 200 questions), LoL receives the highest win-rate among four compared LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoL outperforms all four primary baselines across nearly every English and Chinese humor-judgment task. Against GPT-4o, LoL gains the largest margins on harder multi-option tasks (3T1: 48.06 vs. 43.71; 4T1: 35.90 vs. 34.63) and on Oogiri-GO (97.20 vs. 85.09), though GPT-4o retains a slight edge on SemEval 2021 2T1-Hard (60.77 vs. 57.45). Compared to the reasoning-focused QwQ-32B, LoL leads by 16.53 points on Oogiri-GO and 14.57 points on SemEval 2021 2T1, confirming that general reasoning ability alone is insufficient for humor judgment. Against the prior humor-specific method CLoT-7B, LoL achieves substantially higher scores on all tasks (e.g., 97.20 vs. 52.49 on Oogiri-GO), demonstrating that the instruction-evolution and DPO stages add significant value beyond the CLoT paradigm. On the Chinese Ruozhiba dataset, LoL (95.35%) surpasses QwQ (90.80%) and GPT-4o (76.40%), while CLoT drops to 50.20%, highlighting the cross-lingual robustness of the proposed framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLoT (Creative Leap of Thought) paradigm for humor generation by addressing its core limitation: CLoT only trains on question-answer pairs (the endpoints of the multi-hop reasoning path), capturing surface creative patterns without the intermediate rationales. LoL departs from CLoT by formulating humor as a knowledge-graph multi-hop reasoning problem and introducing two key mechanisms: (1) an automatic instruction-evolution system (AIE) with three agents (Generator, Imaginator, Selector) that enriches the sparse knowledge graph with external world knowledge, and (2) a Guided Explorative Self-Improvement Tuning (GESIT) stage using DPO where a frozen judgment model and GPT-4o jointly provide online rationales. The instruction-evolution component adapts Evol-Instruct with a humor-specific 'imaginator' agent, while the preference-optimization stage builds directly on DPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured multi-hop reasoning with explicit rationales can meaningfully improve humor judgment and generation in LLMs, outperforming even GPT-4o on several benchmarks. However, the approach's reliance on GPT-4o for rationale extraction during training limits its autonomy and scalability, and the subjectivity of humor makes evaluation inherently noisy. Open questions include whether the knowledge-graph framing generalizes to other creative reasoning tasks (irony, satire, wordplay beyond puns), whether the AIE process can operate without a powerful external LLM, and whether the gains persist as base models improve natively in creative reasoning.
