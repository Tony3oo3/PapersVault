## Problem

Current style transfer diffusion models struggle to disentangle content from style in reference images, causing non-stylistic elements (e.g., a person's face) to leak into generated outputs. Existing training-free fixes like InstantStyle subtract content text features from image features, but this cross-modal subtraction introduces image-text misalignment that degrades style fidelity. The paper identifies the root cause as over-conditioning: using too many image feature elements as guidance, and shows that appropriately fewer conditions can more effectively suppress content leakage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a style-reference image, a target text prompt, and a content text description of the reference (or a generic default like 'person, animal, plant, or object in the foreground' when content is unknown). The content text is used only to compute which image feature elements to mask; it is never fed as a conditioning signal during the denoising loop. The output is a single generated image that follows the target text prompt while adopting the reference's visual style, with the reference's content suppressed.

## Method

The core mechanism is a training-free masking operation on IP-Adapter's d-dimensional image feature: the element-wise product of the style-reference image feature and the content text feature is computed, clustered via K-means (K=2 by default), and elements in the highest-means cluster are zeroed out to remove content-correlated dimensions. This masked image feature is then passed through IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention alongside the target text feature, so the denoising process is guided by fewer, style-pure conditions. The paper provides theoretical grounding: Proposition 1 proves that clustering on the element-wise product maximizes the energy score for the content feature (i.e., best identifies content elements), Theorem 1 shows that conditioning on fewer masked elements yields smaller distribution divergence than InstantStyle's full-feature subtraction, and Theorem 2 extends the 'less is more' principle to tuning-based models, demonstrating that an Image-Adapter extracting content directly from image features outperforms a Text-Adapter learning content from text embeddings. Unlike InstantStyle, the method performs all feature manipulation within the image feature latent space, avoiding cross-modal subtraction and its associated misalignment.

## Results

On the constructed CIFAR-10-based evaluation dataset (10 content objects × 21 styles), the proposed masking-based method achieves the best fidelity score (0.972), the lowest leakage score (0.478), and the highest human preference (36.7%) among all compared methods. On the StyleBench benchmark (73 styles), the method attains 0.265 text alignment and 0.657 image alignment, with a dominant 42.1% human preference score. The method is training-free (no parameter tuning required) and fixes the image-condition coefficient λ_i at 1.0, unlike InstantStyle which requires labor-intensive coefficient tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all baselines on the two most critical metrics for content-leakage mitigation: fidelity score (0.972 vs. best baseline StyleShot at 0.956) and leakage score (0.478 vs. best baseline DEADiff at 0.523). On human preference, the method achieves 36.7%, substantially exceeding StyleShot (24.3%), StyleDrop (19.6%), and InstantStyle (8.3%). InstantStyle achieves the highest style score (0.290 vs. Ours 0.273), reflecting its stronger style preservation, but at the cost of significantly worse fidelity (0.820) and leakage (0.596). On StyleBench (Table 2), the method dominates with 42.1% human preference versus StyleShot's 21.1% and InstantStyle's 7.9%, while InstantStyle edges out slightly on text alignment (0.275 vs. 0.265) but trails on image alignment (0.575 vs. 0.657). Training-based baselines (StyleCrafter, StyleDrop, DEADiff, StyleAligned) all show markedly lower human preference (1.7%–19.6%) and higher leakage scores, confirming the advantage of the training-free masking approach. The tuning-based Image-Adapter variant (Ours(FT)) further validates Theorem 2 by outperforming the Text-Adapter in both style and fidelity scores during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention architecture and positions itself as a training-free alternative to InstantStyle's cross-modal feature subtraction for decoupling content from style. The key conceptual departure is operating entirely within the image feature space (masking elements identified via clustering on element-wise products) rather than subtracting text features from image features, thereby avoiding the image-text misalignment that plagues InstantStyle. The theoretical contribution frames the problem through an energy-guidance lens and divergence bounds, showing that fewer, more coherent conditions provably reduce the gap between generated and real image distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, parameter-free nature of the masking approach makes it a practical drop-in improvement for existing IP-Adapter-based pipelines, and the theoretical results (Theorems 1–2) provide a principled justification for the 'less is more' principle that could inform future condition-design choices in diffusion models. However, the method still requires knowing (or guessing) the content of the style reference to compute the element-wise product, and the independence assumption on feature elements may not hold in practice for complex multi-object scenes. Open questions include generalization to video style transfer, multi-reference style blending, and whether the clustering-based element selection can be replaced by learned or adaptive criteria.
