## Problem

Hindsight relabeling in goal-conditioned RL (GCRL) fails in object-centric domains because it assigns high rewards to trajectories where the agent never interacts with the target object (e.g., the block is already at the goal), creating a distribution mismatch between hindsight and desired goals. Additionally, interactions between objects lack a consensus statistical definition that is both principled and tractable for downstream RL, making it difficult to filter out these uninformative trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method operates on factored state transitions (s, a, s') where the state is decomposed into n object factors, along with binary null vectors indicating which objects are present in each trajectory. NCII produces an n×(n+1) interaction matrix B where B_ji=1 indicates factor i interacts with factor j (the extra column encodes action influence). HInt then consumes these per-timestep interaction matrices to produce a binary accept/reject decision for each trajectory and a sampled hindsight goal timestep t_r from the set of interaction timesteps, ultimately yielding a goal-conditioned policy via DDPG.

## Method

NCII introduces a null counterfactual interaction assumption: a cause object interacts with a target if, in a world where the cause is absent (nulled), the target's transition probability changes. This is operationalized by training a masked forward dynamics model f(s, a, B; θ) on trajectories with varying subsets of objects present, where the mask B controls which factors are included. An interaction is detected when nulling a variable reduces the predicted log-likelihood of the observed outcome by more than ε_null. An interaction model h(s, a; φ) is jointly trained with f via iterative optimization (train f with fixed h, then train h to predict f's null-test outputs), enabling O(1) inference at deployment. HInt uses the inferred interaction graphs to construct a temporal unrolled graph and filters hindsight trajectories by checking for a path from the action node to the target object, ensuring only trajectories where the agent exerted causal control on the target are retained in the hindsight buffer.

## Results

NCII achieves the lowest misprediction rate (lower is better) in most evaluated domains. On the 1-in Random DAG domain, NCII with PointNet reaches 0.8±0.2% misprediction, compared to JACI (1.6±0.1%), Gradient (4.5±2.5%), Attention (4.8±1.6%), and NCD (30.3±2.9%). In physical domains, NCII with PointNet achieves 5.1±0.3% on Robosuite, 11.3±1.3% on Air Hockey, and 7.2±1.4% on Franka Kitchen, substantially outperforming gradient- and attention-based baselines. For GCRL, HInt with NCII-learned interactions improves sample efficiency by up to 4× over vanilla hindsight and other baselines across Spriteworld, Robosuite, Air Hockey, and Franka Kitchen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCII with PointNet achieves the best or near-best misprediction rate in 7 out of 9 evaluated domains, with particularly large margins over gradient-based (ELDEN/Wang et al.) and attention-based (Pitis et al.) methods in physical domains (e.g., 5.1% vs. 40.9% on Robosuite, 11.3% vs. 47.5% on Air Hockey). Against the closest causal baseline JACI (Chuck et al. 2024b), NCII wins on all domains except 6-in Random DAG where they are comparable (25.1% vs. 27.3%), and Robosuite where JACI is marginally better (6.2% vs. 5.1%). NCD (Hwang et al.) is competitive only on 6-in Random DAG and Sprite-6 but degrades severely on 1-in (30.3%) and Robosuite (49.5%). For GCRL sample efficiency (Figure 4), HInt with NCII-learned interactions matches or exceeds HInt with ground-truth interactions, and outperforms vanilla HER, Prioritized Replay, f-policy gradients, ELDEN, and CAI by up to 4× in sample efficiency across all four physical domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) by introducing interaction-based trajectory filtering, and builds on the actual causation framework of Pearl and Halpern by replacing the intractable globally minimal invariant set with a simpler null counterfactual assumption (what if object i did not exist?). The interaction inference component (NCII) draws on prior work by Chuck et al. (2024b) on functional actual causes and adapts their passive-error reweighting from Granger-causal skill discovery, while the GCRL component positions itself alongside distributional GCRL methods (f-policy gradients, SMORE) as an alternative way to align the hindsight goal distribution with the desired goal distribution. The key conceptual departure is that the null counterfactual provides a tractable, physically interpretable inductive bias that outperforms gradient-based, attention-based, and contextual-decomposition interaction detectors across multiple domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underappreciated failure mode of HER in object-centric domains: hindsight relabeling rewards trajectories where the agent never touches the target object, flooding the replay buffer with trivially-achieved goals that mismatch the desired goal distribution. The up-to-4× sample efficiency gain and the clean visualization of the distribution mismatch (Figure 5) make this practically relevant for robotics and manipulation research. Limitations include the requirement for a factored state with a well-defined null state, reduced utility in locomotion or navigation tasks where interactions are less critical, and the fact that the method has not yet been validated at scale with high-dimensional visual inputs. Open questions include integrating HInt with curriculum-based or entropy-regularized hindsight sampling, and whether the null assumption generalizes to domains where objects cannot be cleanly 'removed.'
