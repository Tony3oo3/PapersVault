## Problem

The Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), the de facto standard for evaluating video generation quality, relies on three fragile assumptions that the paper systematically dismantles: the I3D feature space is non-Gaussian (violating the Fréchet Distance's parametric assumption), I3D features are insensitive to temporal distortions (prioritizing per-frame image quality over motion consistency), and FVD requires thousands of samples to converge, making it impractical for many real-world datasets. These flaws mean FVD can even report *improved* quality when videos are degraded with mild blur, undermining its reliability as a standalone evaluation metric.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two sets of 32-frame video clips (e.g., a real reference set and a generated set) and produces a single scalar distribution distance score. Features are extracted per clip using the V-JEPA_SSv2 encoder with an attentive pooler, yielding 1280-dimensional vectors. A key practical advantage is sample efficiency: JEDi converges to a stable estimate within ~700 samples on UCF-101 and ~300 on T2VQA-DB, compared to ~4,350 and ~900 for FVD, making it viable for smaller datasets where FVD's estimates remain unreliable.

## Method

JEDi (JEPA Embedding Distance) replaces both the feature extractor and the distance metric in the FVD pipeline. For features, it uses V-JEPA_SSv2 — a Video Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture fine-tuned on Something-Something-v2 with an attentive classification probe — whose pre-logit representations are more sensitive to temporal distortions and better aligned with human perception than I3D or VideoMAE features. For the distance, it uses Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) with a polynomial kernel (degree 2, γ=1, coef=0), which is distribution-free (no Gaussian assumption) and exhibits the fastest convergence rate among all tested metrics (FD, Energy, MMD variants, Mixture Wasserstein) across all feature spaces. The paper validates JEDi through multivariate normality tests (Mardia's skewness/kurtosis, Henze-Zirkler), convergence analysis across 11 datasets, progressive blur/elastic/salt-and-pepper distortion experiments, tracking of fine-tuned Stable Video Diffusion checkpoints (where only JEDi shows a statistically significant negative Spearman correlation with training step), a 20-rater human study using AHP aggregation, and evaluation on AIGC databases (T2VQA-DB, TIP-I2V) where JEDi outperforms FVD in both sample efficiency and alignment with human MOS rankings.

## Results

JEDi (V-JEPA_SSv2 + MMD_POLY) requires only ~700 samples to converge on UCF-101 versus ~4,350 for FVD (I3D + FD), a 16% sample efficiency improvement. On the T2VQA-DB AIGC benchmark, JEDi converges at 100/200/300 samples for worst/median/best models, while FVD requires 600/800/900. In the human evaluation study, V-JEPA-based metrics achieve 83–87% cosine similarity with human preference rankings, outperforming I3D-based metrics (50–70%) by an average of 34%. JEDi is the only metric that tracks incremental training improvements with a Spearman correlation of −1.0, while FVD achieves only −0.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is FVD (I3D + Fréchet Distance), the de facto standard for video generation evaluation. JEDi decisively outperforms FVD on sample efficiency: it converges with roughly 16% of the samples FVD requires across all tested datasets (e.g., 700 vs. 4,350 on UCF-101; 100 vs. 600 on the worst T2VQA-DB model). On human alignment, JEDi's V-JEPA_SSv2 + MMD_POLY combination ranks 8th overall (78.96% on UCF-101, 78.39% on Sky Scene), while the best V-JEPA_PT configurations reach 83–87%, compared to I3D-based metrics at 50–70% and VideoMAE-based metrics at 50–79%. Critically, FVD fails to detect low-level temporal blur (reporting a 10–20% *improvement* in quality), whereas JEDi correctly identifies degradation at all noise levels. On tracking generative model training progress, JEDi achieves a Spearman correlation of −1.0 with training steps, while FVD reaches only −0.6 and is not statistically significant. Both metrics rank T2VQA-DB models consistently (Tune-A-Video worst, LaVie best), confirming JEDi's validity as a drop-in replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the FVD metric (Unterthiner et al., 2019), which itself extends the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) paradigm from images to video using I3D features. It draws on Ge et al. (2024), who identified FVD's content bias and suggested self-supervised features as a remedy, and on the broader line of critiques showing FVD's sensitivity to implementation details and insensitivity to temporal distortions (Brooks et al., 2022; Skorokhodov et al., 2021). The proposed JEDi metric repurposes V-JEPA's self-supervised video representations (Bardes et al., 2024) with a distribution-free MMD distance (Gretton et al., 2012), departing from FVD's reliance on the Gaussianity assumption and I3D's supervised classification features. The paper also positions itself against sample-level evaluation frameworks like VBench (Huang et al., 2023), arguing for the continued need for distributional metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FVD is the de facto standard for video generation evaluation, and this paper provides comprehensive evidence that it is unreliable: it rewards temporal degradation (lower FVD with added blur), requires thousands of samples for convergence, and misaligns with human perception. The practical impact is significant—JEDi's 16× sample efficiency reduction makes evaluation feasible on small datasets and with limited compute, which is critical for the rapid iteration cycle in video generation research. Open questions include whether JEDi's advantages persist for very long videos (>128 frames), whether the V-JEPA_SSv2 fine-tuning on Something-Something-v2 introduces domain bias for non-human-action content, and whether the polynomial kernel choice is optimal across all video domains. The paper does not address conditional evaluation (e.g., text-video alignment) or diversity metrics, leaving those aspects of video generation assessment unaddressed.
