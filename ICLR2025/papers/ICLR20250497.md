## Problem

Existing diffusion-based drag editing methods perform all drag operations at a single diffusion time step, causing accumulated perturbations that lead to severe image distortions and failure to adhere to control points. Additionally, handle points gradually drift from their original features during iterative editing, resulting in inaccurate point tracking and artifacts. The field also lacks a comprehensive benchmark dataset and reliable quality assessment metrics for drag editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250497/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB source image, a set of 2D handle points (starting locations) and corresponding target points, and an optional binary mask defining the editable region. The image is encoded into VAE latent space via DDIM inversion (with inversion strength κ=0.75, yielding T=38 out of T_max=50 steps), where all editing occurs. The output is a drag-edited image in pixel space where semantic content at each handle point has been transferred to its target while the non-editable region (outside the mask) remains unchanged. Total runtime is approximately 71 seconds on an A100 GPU with <13 GB memory.

## Method

GoodDrag introduces two key practices within a Stable Diffusion 1.5 + LoRA (rank=16) framework. First, the Alternating Drag and Denoising (AlDD) framework distributes K=70 drag operations across multiple diffusion time steps by performing B=10 drag steps followed by one DDIM denoising step, preventing the accumulation of large perturbations that occur when all edits are applied at a single time step. Second, Information-Preserving Motion Supervision modifies the feature alignment loss to compare the edited image's features at the next handle position against the original (un-edited) handle point features rather than the current handle point, preventing progressive feature drift; this larger optimization gap is addressed by using J=3 gradient descent steps per drag operation with a small step size. Point tracking after each drag step localizes the updated handle by searching a neighborhood for the location most similar to the original point's features, and the Latent-MasaCtrl mechanism is incorporated from the 10th U-Net layer for consistency.

## Results

On the Drag100 benchmark, GoodDrag achieves a DAI of 0.070 (γ=1) and 0.062 (γ=20), substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: w/o AlDD at 0.090/0.070, DragDiffusion at 0.148/0.115). In terms of perceptual quality, GoodDrag scores 8.04±0.05 on GScore (0–10 scale), compared to 6.75±0.10 for DragDiffusion, 5.81±0.19 for SDE-Drag, and 3.05±0.17 for DragonDiffusion. On the DragBench dataset, GoodDrag similarly leads with DAI of 0.1339 (γ=1) and GScore of 7.91±0.04. A user study with 27 participants confirms GoodDrag is preferred for both drag accuracy and perceptual quality over DragDiffusion and SDE-Drag.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoodDrag outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor, DragDiffusion (Shi et al., 2023), GoodDrag reduces DAI by more than 50% (0.070 vs. 0.148 at γ=1) and improves GScore by 1.29 points (8.04 vs. 6.75); even when DragDiffusion is given 210 drag operations to match GoodDrag's total motion supervision steps (DragDiffusion*), it still trails (0.119 vs. 0.070 DAI). SDE-Drag (Nie et al., 2023) and DragonDiffusion (Mou et al., 2024a) are further behind, with DragonDiffusion showing the largest gap (DAI 0.213, GScore 3.05), indicating severe artifact issues. Against the GAN-based DragGAN (Pan et al., 2023), GoodDrag achieves dramatically better results: MD of 15.83 vs. 73.00, DAI(γ=1) of 0.078 vs. 0.196, and GScore of 8.10 vs. 2.12. The three most recent baselines—DragNoise (Liu et al., 2024), EasyDrag (Hou et al., 2024), and InstantDrag (Shin et al., 2024)—all score DAI above 0.17 at γ=1, roughly 2.5× worse than GoodDrag's 0.070. GoodDrag does not lose on any metric; its only relative weakness is a slightly lower IF score (0.86 vs. 0.89 for SDE-Drag), which the authors argue is a flawed metric that penalizes necessary edits.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoodDrag builds directly on the drag editing paradigm established by DragGAN (Pan et al., 2023) and DragDiffusion (Shi et al., 2023), which define the core operations of motion supervision and point tracking within a diffusion latent space. The key conceptual departure is the AlDD framework, which reorders the computation by interleaving drag operations with denoising steps across multiple diffusion time steps, rather than applying all drag operations at a single time step as prior methods do. The information-preserving motion supervision modifies the feature alignment loss to anchor against the original (un-edited) handle point rather than the current one, addressing a subtle but cumulative feature-drift failure mode. The paper also introduces the first comprehensive drag editing benchmark (Drag100) and LMM-based quality metrics (GScore), filling a gap in evaluation infrastructure for this nascent task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For a field where prior methods suffer from accumulated perturbations and feature drift, GoodDrag provides two simple but effective 'good practices' that substantially improve both accuracy and fidelity, establishing a stronger baseline for future work. The Drag100 dataset and the DAI/GScore metrics address a critical lack of standardized evaluation, with GScore's use of LMMs as evaluators offering a more human-aligned alternative to flawed metrics like IF (which paradoxically rewards no-change outputs). A key open limitation is the reliance on DDIM inversion, which degrades on complex images with fine details; more robust inversion or inversion-free approaches remain an important direction. The AlDD principle of distributing perturbations incrementally could potentially generalize to other iterative diffusion editing tasks beyond point-based drag.
