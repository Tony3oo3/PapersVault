## Problem

Current PTQ methods freeze the weight's quant-step and optimize only the AdaRound rounding parameter and activation quant-step during feature reconstruction, leaving the de-quantization step locked to the same value. The paper identifies that integer weights are computed before inference (unlike activations), meaning the quant-step used to produce integers and the step used to de-quantize them back to FP32 can be decoupled, opening a wider optimization space that existing methods ignore—especially critical at extremely low bit-widths (W2A2) where reconstruction error dominates accuracy loss.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained FP32 model and a small unlabeled calibration set (e.g., 1024 ImageNet images, 100 DIV2K low-resolution images, or LAMBADA validation tokens) and produces a quantized model with per-channel scaling ξ and translating η parameters. These extra parameters are algebraically merged into the existing quantization scale and bias at deployment (Formula 9: s̃ = ξ·s_w·s_x, b̃ = b + η), so the inference graph is structurally identical to the baseline PTQ model with zero additional latency or memory overhead.

## Method

AdaQTransform decouples the weight's single quant-step s_w into a frozen quant-step s_w (which determines the integer weight w_int via AdaRound) and a learnable dequant-step s_w' (which scales the integer weight back to FP32). Because weight quantization is per-channel while activation quantization is per-tensor, s_w' becomes a per-channel vector that acts as an adaptive linear scaling on the convolution output, directly transforming the quantized feature toward its FP32 ground truth. A learnable per-channel bias b' (equivalently, translating factor η) is added to provide per-channel translation, yielding the unified form ŷ = ξ·(ŵ*x̂) + b + η. The reconstruction objective jointly optimizes α, s_x, ξ, and η via self-distillation against the FP32 output, and the entire formulation subsumes existing PTQ (ξ=1, η=0), QAT (LSQ-style), and BN-not-folded settings as special cases, providing a general quantization paradigm.

## Results

On ImageNet classification, AdaQTransform_NWQ achieves 32.19% top-1 accuracy on MobileNet-v2 at W2A2, a 5.77-point improvement over NWQ (26.42%) and 17.75 points over BRECQ (0.24%). At W3A3, it reaches 63.44% on MobileNet-v2 (+2.2% over NWQ) and 69.52% on MnasNet2.0 (+0.67%). On MS COCO object detection at W2A2, AdaQTransform_NWQ gains ~1% mAP over NWQ across all four backbone-architecture combinations, with the largest gain of 2.78% on ResNet-50 Faster RCNN (27.79 vs. 25.01 mAP). For LLMs, AdaQTransform applied to SmoothQuant improves W6A6 OPT-1.3B from 66.2% to 67.5% last-token prediction accuracy on LAMBADA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaQTransform consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across every task and bit-width tested. Against the strongest PTQ baseline NWQ (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2022), it gains 0.87% at W4A4 MobileNet-v2 (70.01 vs. 69.14), 2.2% at W3A3 (63.44 vs. 61.24), and 5.77% at W2A2 (32.19 vs. 26.42), with similar margins on ResNet-18, RegNet-600, and MnasNet2.0. Earlier PTQ methods (BRECQ, QDROP, PD-Quant, MRECG, AdaRound, AdaQuant) are all surpassed by larger margins, especially at extreme low-bit (W2A2) where BRECQ collapses to 0.24% on MobileNet-v2 while AdaQTransform_NWQ reaches 32.19%. For ViTs, AdaQTransform_NWQ beats NWQ by 0.33–0.5% at W4A4 and AdaQTransform_RepQ-ViT improves RepQ-ViT by ~4% at W4A4 (70.40 vs. 65.05 on ViT-S), while PTQ4ViT and APQ-ViT are outperformed by 10–32%. On LLMs, AdaQTransform applied to SmoothQuant gains 1.3% over SmoothQuant and 1.1% over OS+ at W6A6 on OPT-1.3B (67.5% vs. 66.2%/66.4%). For super-resolution, AdaQTransform applied to AdaBM improves PSNR by 0.1–2.0 dB across Set5/Set14/BSD100/Urban100 at W4A4/W3A3/W2A2, with the largest gain of ~2.0 dB at W4A4 on Set5 (31.41 vs. 29.42). LSQ (Esser et al., ICLR 2020) applied as a QAT-style quant-step update on PTQ (Case 1.2) underperforms the proposed decoupling Case 2.2 by 2.95% on MobileNet-v2 W3A2 (39.65 vs. 42.60), confirming that directly borrowing QAT's update rule is suboptimal for PTQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PTQ reconstruction lineage (AdaRound → BRECQ → QDROP → NWQ) by identifying a previously overlooked degree of freedom: the weight quant-step can be decoupled into a frozen quant-step (for computing integer weights) and a learnable dequant-step (for converting back to FP32). Unlike redistribution methods (SmoothQuant, RepQ-ViT, LSQ+) that shift quantization difficulty between weights and activations without changing the output feature distribution, AdaQTransform directly applies a per-channel linear transformation to the quantized output, narrowing the reconstruction gap. The paper also positions itself against QAT's LSQ setting, showing empirically that directly borrowing QAT's quant-step update into PTQ (Case 1.2) is suboptimal due to PTQ's frozen FP32 weights and tiny calibration set, and builds a unified paradigm subsuming PTQ, QAT, and the new decoupling case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-extra-inference-cost property (parameters merge into existing scale and bias) makes AdaQTransform a drop-in enhancement for any existing PTQ pipeline, and the unified quantization paradigm provides a clean theoretical framework for understanding how different PTQ/QAT settings relate. The consistent gains across CNNs, ViTs, LLMs, and super-resolution networks (especially at W2A2 where PTQ is most fragile) demonstrate broad applicability. Limitations include: improvements are modest at W4A4 (0–1%), the method still requires a calibration set and self-distillation finetuning, and the unified paradigm, while elegant, does not close the fundamental PTQ-vs-QAT gap. An open question is whether the decoupling principle extends to non-uniform or asymmetric quantization schemes.
