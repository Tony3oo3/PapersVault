## Problem

Generative foundation models have transformed simulation in the physical world (video, robotics) but remain largely unexplored for virtual worlds like financial markets. Existing market simulators—statistical or agent-based—lack the resolution, interactivity, and realism to capture order-level microstructure dynamics, making it impossible to safely test strategies, assess market impact, or train agents in a realistic environment.

## Input / Output

The system consumes four conditional inputs: a starting sequence of recent real orders (each tokenized as a 4-tuple of type/price/volume/interval mapped to an index in [0, 49152)), a text scenario description (DES_TEXT) or replay curve for controllability, user-injected interactive orders for interactivity, and market matching rules (MTCH_R) defining the feasible order space. At the order-batch level, minute-level order flows are converted into 32×32×3 RGB 'order images' (pixel value = count of identical orders) before tokenization. The output is a continuous stream of generated orders that, when matched in a simulated clearing house, produce fine-grained LOB states, price trajectories, and order-batch distributions at minute-level granularity.

## Method

LMM is an ensemble of three components: (1) an Order Model—a causal LLaMA2 transformer that encodes each order plus its 10-level LOB volumes and mid-price as a single composite token (Eq. 1) and autoregressively predicts the next order; (2) an Order-Batch Model—a two-stage pipeline where a fine-tuned VQGAN (f=4, Z=8192) discretizes 32×32 order images into 64 tokens, and a LLaMA2 autoregressive transformer generates sequences of these tokens to model macro-level market behavior over minute intervals; (3) a cross-attention Ensemble Model that conditions order-level generation on the selected order-batch channel, balancing fine-grained responsiveness with scenario-level control. A Fine-grained Signal Generation Interface uses GPT-4o mini to retrieve matching historical minute-level trajectories from a text prompt, converting vague descriptions into concrete control signals. The MarS engine wraps LMM with a simulated clearing house that matches generated and user-injected orders in real time, following two principles: 'Shaping the Future Based on Realized Realities' (order-batch generation conditioned on realized matching results) and 'Electing the Best from Every Possible Future' (selecting the best-matching order-batch from N candidates via control-signal filtering). Scaling law experiments confirm performance improves with both data size (up to 32B tokens) and model size (up to 1.02B parameters for the Order Model, 3B for the Order-Batch Model).

## Results

On the forecasting task, LMM-based simulations significantly outperform the DeepLOB baseline on trend prediction accuracy (Fig. 7a), with the 1.02B-parameter model further outperforming the 0.22B-parameter model, confirming that scaling translates to downstream gains. For detection, MarS maintains distribution similarity >0.87 between simulated and real spread distributions in normal periods, with a measurable drop during manipulation periods. In the RL agent training experiment, the trading agent improves from −6 BP to +2–6 BP price advantage over the best-configured TWAP baseline. Controllability experiments show correlation between simulated and replay trajectories rising from 0.23 to 0.47 with control signals enabled.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary quantitative baseline is DeepLOB (Zhang et al., 2019), a well-known CNN-based LOB forecasting model. MarS/LMM significantly outperforms DeepLOB on the 3-class trend prediction task (up/down/flat over t to t+k minutes), as shown in Fig. 7a. Additionally, the 1.02B-parameter LMM outperforms the 0.22B-parameter variant on the same task, demonstrating that the scaling-law gains observed in validation loss (Fig. 3) translate directly to downstream forecasting accuracy. Beyond forecasting, MarS validates the Square-Root-Law of market impact (Δ ∝ σ√(Q/V)) on synthetic data, confirming that its interactive simulations reproduce a well-established empirical regularity. The paper does not report head-to-head comparisons against other LOB generators (e.g., GAN-based or RNN-based approaches) on a unified benchmark, so the DeepLOB comparison remains the sole direct quantitative baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Large Vision Model pipeline (VQGAN tokenizer + autoregressive transformer, following Bai et al., 2024) to the financial domain by converting order batches into RGB 'order images' and generating them autoregressively, while a separate causal transformer (LLaMA2-based) handles individual order sequences. It departs from prior financial simulation work (ABIDES, GAN-based LOB generators, Nagy et al. 2023) by operating at the finest granularity—individual orders rather than LOB snapshots—and by integrating a simulated clearing house that provides real-time feedback between generation and matching. The key conceptual departure is framing market simulation as a unified conditional generation problem (analogous to language modeling), enabling a single model to serve forecasting, detection, analysis, and RL training through different conditioning configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first order-level generative foundation model for financial markets with demonstrated scaling laws, establishing a template for domain-specific foundation models beyond NLP and vision. The unified conditional-generation interface that subsumes four distinct financial tasks (forecasting, anomaly detection, market-impact analysis, RL training) is a practical contribution that could reshape how financial institutions approach simulation-based workflows. Key limitations include evaluation on a single market (Chinese A-shares), resource-constrained data scale (32B tokens for the order model), and the detection application remaining preliminary (single metric, 10 stocks). Open questions include whether scaling continues to unlock new capabilities, multi-market generalization, and real-time deployment feasibility.
