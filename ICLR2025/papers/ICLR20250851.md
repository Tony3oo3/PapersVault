## Problem

Chain-of-Thought prompting has dramatically improved LLM inference by injecting intermediate reasoning steps into prompts, but extending this to purely visual in-context learning is blocked by two obstacles: visual data lacks the syntactic and semantic structure needed to decompose into sequential reasoning steps, and visual prompts cannot be easily hand-crafted or restructured to match a specific test input the way text prompts can. This paper asks whether a saliency-driven sequential annotation strategy and an automated prompt-selection criterion can together unlock analogous gains for Large Autoregressive Vision Models (LAVMs).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a test RGB image and a candidate pool of 50,000 prompt pairs (image + target annotation) from MS-COCO, all tokenized into 2048-dimensional VQ-GAN codebooks. A U2-Net saliency model produces a pixel-wise probability map over each prompt query, which drives the construction of intermediate annotation steps. The autoregressive LAVM (LLaMA-300M/1B/7B) outputs a sequence of VQ-GAN tokens that are decoded back to pixel space, yielding a segmentation mask, keypoint skeleton, bounding-box image, or inpainted patch depending on the downstream task.

## Method

CoF prompting has two coupled components. First, for each selected prompt pair, a pre-trained saliency detector (U2-Net) scores every annotated region by summing its pixel-wise saliency probability; regions are then incrementally revealed from most to least salient, producing m intermediate targets x_pt^1 ⊂ x_pt^2 ⊂ … ⊂ x_pt^m = x_pt. These are interleaved with the prompt query in the sequence [x_pq, x_pt^1, x_pq, x_pt^2, …, x_pq, x_pt^m, x_tq] to match the paired-query-target format the LAVM was pre-trained on. Second, prompt selection is automated in two stages: (a) query relevance is measured by the Jaccard similarity between the sets of unique VQ-GAN codebook indices of the test image and each candidate prompt image, shortlisting the N most semantically aligned prompts; (b) annotation diversity is scored by counting the number of unique indices in each shortlisted prompt's target codebook, and the top-k targets with the richest annotations are retained. The entire pipeline is training-free and plugs into any pre-trained LAVM without fine-tuning.

## Results

On MS-COCO image segmentation with LLaMA-7B, CoF prompting achieves 52.53% IoU and 67.05% P-ACC, outperforming the best baseline (SupPR) by 3.12% IoU and 2.01% P-ACC. On pose estimation with LLaMA-7B, CoF reaches 13.34% P-ACC (vs. 11.29% for SupPR), a 17.3% relative improvement, while reducing failure rates by up to 14.7% across model sizes. On image inpainting (LLaMA-7B), CoF lowers MSE to 0.61 and LPIPS to 0.47, beating SupPR by 30% in MSE and 14.5% in LPIPS. Ablations confirm that saliency-based intermediate reasoning steps (cognitive reasoning) are the dominant contributor, with prompt selection (query relevance + annotation diversity) providing additional gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoF prompting is compared against two baselines: SupPR (Zhang et al., 2023), a prompt retrieval framework that selects semantically similar images, and SegGPT (Wang et al., 2023), which uses same-color masking for segmentation. Against SupPR, CoF wins on image segmentation across all three model scales (e.g., +3.12% IoU on LLaMA-7B), on pose estimation P-ACC for all models (e.g., +2.05% on LLaMA-7B), on object detection L-IoU (19.74% vs. 19.65%), and on image inpainting (MSE 0.61 vs. 0.87; LPIPS 0.47 vs. 0.55). The one exception is pose estimation IoU on LLaMA-7B, where SupPR edges out CoF by 0.07% (2.87 vs. 2.80). Against SegGPT, CoF achieves consistent gains on segmentation (e.g., +7.15% IoU on LLaMA-7B), though SegGPT is only applicable to the segmentation task. Overall, CoF dominates or matches both baselines on every reported metric, with the largest margins on P-ACC and inpainting quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Chain-of-Thought prompting paradigm from large language models to the purely visual domain, building directly on the Large Autoregressive Vision Model (LAVM) framework of Bai et al. (2024) and Hao et al. (2024). Where prior visual in-context learning work (SupPR, SegGPT) focused on prompt retrieval or task-specific masking heuristics, CoF prompting introduces two orthogonal mechanisms: saliency-based intermediate reasoning steps that decompose the target annotation into a progressive sequence, and a two-criteria prompt selection strategy (Jaccard similarity on VQ-GAN codebooks + annotation diversity). The conceptual lineage traces from CoT in LLMs through visual prompting (Bar et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023a) to this work's attempt to replicate sequential reasoning without any language intermediary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that injecting structured intermediate steps into visual prompts can meaningfully improve LAVM inference, with the ablation confirming that cognitive reasoning (saliency-based decomposition) is the dominant contributor while prompt selection alone offers little benefit over random sampling. However, the improvements are moderate (3–5% IoU on segmentation, 1–2% on pose estimation), evaluated only on MS-COCO with four tasks, and the method requires ground-truth masks to compute region-level saliency scores, limiting its applicability to tasks without such auxiliary annotations. Open questions include whether the saliency-based decomposition generalizes to tasks without explicit region structure, whether the approach scales to more complex multi-object or video settings, and whether the alternating query-target prompt construction is truly optimal or merely an artifact of the pre-training format.
