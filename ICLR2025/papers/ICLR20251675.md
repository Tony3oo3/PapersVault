## Problem

Previous theoretical analyses of synthetic data for model training (e.g., Feng et al., 2024) assume that only labels are noisy while features are drawn from the exact real distribution, and they study only the infinite sample size limit where performance transitions are sharp. In practice, generative models trained on finite real data produce synthetic features whose covariance is a biased empirical estimate, inducing a distribution shift that prior work overlooks. This paper asks: under what conditions does adding pruned synthetic data actually improve classifier performance when both feature-space distribution shift and label noise are present in a high-dimensional regime?

## Input / Output

The method takes n real p-dimensional samples from a two-component isotropic Gaussian mixture (means ±μ, unit covariance) and m synthetic samples generated from empirical mean and covariance estimates computed on a subset of n̂ real samples, with labels corrupted at rate ε and pruned by a verifier characterized by (ρ, φ). The output is a closed-form asymptotic test accuracy for a ridge classifier, expressed as Φ of a signal-to-noise ratio determined by a fixed-point system of three scalar quantities. The key structural detail is that the synthetic covariance Ĉ is itself a random matrix whose eigenvalue distribution follows the Marchenko-Pastur law, making the distribution shift a function of the ratio p/n̂ rather than a fixed perturbation.

## Method

The paper models synthetic data generation as maximum-likelihood estimation of real data statistics on a finite subset, so that the synthetic covariance Ĉ is an empirical Wishart-type matrix whose eigenvalues spread according to the Marchenko-Pastur law when p/n̂ → η̂ > 0. Using random matrix theory (resolvent concentration, deterministic equivalents, and trace identities), they derive that the ridge classifier's decision function on a test sample is asymptotically Gaussian with mean and variance expressible in terms of three fixed-point scalars (δ_r*, δ_s*, δ_g*) capturing real-data, synthetic-data, and generative-model contributions respectively. The main theorem gives the asymptotic accuracy as Φ((ν−μ²)^(−1/2)·μ), where μ and ν are explicit functions of these scalars, the regularization γ, the pruning parameters, and the label noise rate. A corollary for the fully synthetic case (n=0, no feature noise) yields a smooth phase transition in label noise, showing that the sharp transition of Feng et al. (2024) is recovered only in the limit η_s→0. Experiments on Amazon Reviews, MNIST, and LLM safety alignment (Falcon 2-11B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-2B) validate the theory, confirming that synthetic data helps only under strong verification and high generative model quality.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: Theorem 4.2 provides a closed-form expression for the asymptotic test accuracy of a ridge classifier trained on a mixture of real and pruned synthetic data in the high-dimensional regime (p, n, m → ∞ with fixed ratios). The key quantitative finding is that the critical label noise threshold is ε* = (1 + ρ/φ)⁻¹, below which synthetic data degrades performance to random-guessing level. Empirically, with oracle supervision (ρ,φ)=(0,1), adding synthetic data yields a monotonic performance boost across Amazon Reviews, MNIST, and LLM safety tasks, while weak supervision (ρ,φ)=(0.5,0.5) causes significant performance drops. In the LLM Q&A safety generation experiment, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as the generative model produces a larger performance boost than Gemma-2-2B-it, confirming the theoretical prediction that lower distribution shift (smaller η̂) yields better synthetic data quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not present a traditional method-vs-baseline comparison table; its main contribution is a theoretical framework validated empirically. The closest comparisons are: (1) Oracle supervision (ρ,φ)=(0,1) vs. weak supervision (ρ,φ)=(0.5,0.5) or (1,0.5), where oracle supervision consistently yields monotonic gains from synthetic data while weak supervision causes performance degradation, especially at high synthetic proportions. (2) Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct vs. Gemma-2-2B-it as generative models for Q&A safety prompts, where Llama-3.1-8B produces a larger accuracy improvement as synthetic data volume increases, attributed to lower feature distribution shift. (3) The paper generalizes Feng et al. (2024), whose sharp phase transition in the infinite-sample limit (η→0) is shown to be a special case of the smooth phase transition predicted here in the high-dimensional regime, with the critical noise value ε*=(1+ρ/φ)⁻¹ matching in both settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gaussian mixture model analysis of Feng et al. (2024) on synthetic data with verification by incorporating feature-space distribution shift (via finite-sample covariance estimation governed by the Marchenko-Pastur law) alongside label noise, and by moving from the infinite-sample-size regime to a high-dimensional RMT regime where p/n → η > 0. It also situates itself within the broader model collapse literature (Shumailov et al. 2023; Seddik et al. 2024) but focuses on the non-recursive, single-generation setting. The key methodological departure is the use of random matrix theory to derive closed-form performance expressions for a ridge classifier trained on mixed real/synthetic data, rather than relying on asymptotic arguments in the n → ∞ limit only.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization showing that in high dimensions, feature distribution shift from imperfect generative model estimation can negate the benefits of synthetic data even under perfect label verification, identifying the generative model quality (controlled by p/ñ) as a critical factor alongside label noise and verification accuracy. The smooth phase transition result generalizes the sharp transition of Feng et al. (2024) and better reflects finite-sample practical scenarios. Limitations include the restriction to binary classification, Gaussian mixtures, and ridge regression; the authors note that feature verification (as opposed to label-only verification) and non-Gaussian distributions remain open extensions. The practical relevance is tempered by the simplified model assumptions, though the LLM experiments (Falcon 2-11B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-2B) provide encouraging validation.
