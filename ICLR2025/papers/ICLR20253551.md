## Problem

Existing protein conformation generation methods either rely on computationally expensive MD simulations or model structures directly in 3D Euclidean space using diffusion/flow matching, which demands equivariant architectures, intensive computation, and tends to capture only local perturbations around metastable states rather than diverse remote conformational modes. There is no efficient framework that can model the full sequence-conditioned conformational distribution while remaining scalable to modern language model architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single amino acid sequence (20 standard residues, length L from 10 to 1000) as the sole condition and produces an ensemble of N 3D backbone conformations, each represented as L×4×3 coordinates for all backbone heavy atoms. The generation is roto-translation invariant: the discrete latent tokens encode local structural patterns independent of global orientation, and the decoder reconstructs 3D coordinates from these tokens. No MSA, initial structure, or ligand information is required for the sequence-based SLM variants.

## Method

SLM decouples conformation generation into two stages: (1) a frozen discrete VAE (from ESM3/FoldSeek) encodes 3D structures into per-residue structure tokens from a vocabulary of ~4000, removing high-frequency geometric detail; (2) a conditional language model p_θ(z|c) learns the sequence-to-structure-token distribution, which is then decoded back to 3D coordinates. The key instantiation, ESMDiff, fine-tunes the 1.4B ESM3 BERT backbone with masked discrete diffusion (log-linear schedule, absorbing [MASK] state), using position-coupled input embeddings that sum amino acid, structure token, and sinusoidal timestep embeddings at each position. This design exploits the strict position-to-position correspondence between sequence and structure tokens, allowing the bidirectional transformer to naturally handle the seq2str translation without autoregressive ordering. The two-stage training (first dVAE, then LM prior) reduces the language model objective to a simple KL minimization, making it compatible with any LM architecture including T5, GPT-2, and BERT variants.

## Results

On the BPTI conformation generation benchmark, ESMDiff(DDPM) achieves the best Jensen-Shannon divergence across all three distributional metrics: JS-PwD 0.372, JS-TIC 0.420, and JS-Rg 0.439, substantially outperforming the next-best methods (AlphaFlow: 0.503/0.462/0.777; ESMFlow: 0.524/0.439/0.804). ESMDiff(DDPM) also attains 0.940 validity (clash-free ratio) and a competitive ensemble TM-score of 0.843. The paper reports a 20–100× speedup over diffusion-based baselines such as AlphaFlow when generating N=100 conformations, with runtime scaling favorably with protein length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESMDiff(DDPM) decisively outperforms all sequence-based diffusion baselines on the BPTI task: it reduces JS-PwD from 0.503 (AlphaFlow) and 0.524 (ESMFlow) to 0.372, and JS-Rg from 0.777/0.804 to 0.439, while maintaining 0.940 validity (vs. 1.000 for AlphaFlow and 0.620 for EigenFold). Against Str2Str, ESMDiff(DDPM) achieves far better TM-ens (0.843 vs. 0.705/0.664) and RMSD-ens (1.437 vs. 2.155/2.480), though Str2Str retains an edge on JS-Rg (0.302/0.325 vs. 0.439). On conformational changing pairs (apo/holo and fold-switching), MSA-based methods (AlphaFlow, MSA-Subs.) generally outperform all SLM variants on residue flexibility correlation, with AlphaFlow achieving ResFlex r(gl.) of 0.455/0.385 versus ESMDiff(DDPM)'s 0.420/0.402, indicating that MSA information remains important for these tasks. On intrinsically disordered proteins (PED), ESM3 zero-shot and ESMDiff(ID) achieve the best pairwise distance MAE (6.606/6.886 vs. 7.129 for AlphaFlow) and competitive contact map scores, while MSA-based methods remain strong on radius of gyration. The key differentiator is efficiency: SLMs are 20–100× faster than AlphaFlow and other diffusion baselines, making them practical for large-scale conformational sampling where MSA retrieval or iterative denoising steps are prohibitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of modeling protein conformations directly in 3D Euclidean space via diffusion or flow matching (AlphaFlow, EigenFold, Str2Str, ConfDiff), which requires equivariant architectures and iterative denoising. Instead, it repurposes the discrete VAE structure tokenizer from ESM3 (Hayes et al., 2024) to project conformations into a compact latent token space, where standard language model architectures (T5, GPT-2, BERT) can perform conditional generation without geometric constraints. The ESMDiff instantiation specifically fine-tunes ESM3 with masked discrete diffusion (Austin et al., 2021), bridging the protein foundation model literature with generative conformation sampling. Conceptually, this positions conformation generation as a sequence-to-structure translation problem in a discrete vocabulary rather than a continuous geometric denoising problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20–100× speedup over 3D diffusion baselines makes large-scale conformation ensemble sampling practically feasible, and the framework's compatibility with any LM architecture opens a broad design space for future work. However, MSA-based methods (AlphaFlow, MSA subsampling) still outperform SLMs on conformation-changing pairs, suggesting that evolutionary information remains critical for capturing large-scale structural transitions. Key open questions include whether continuous latent spaces or MSA-conditioned SLMs can close this gap, and whether the approach extends to side chains, ligands, and multi-chain systems. The discrete VAE quality acts as a hard ceiling on reconstruction fidelity, making tokenizer design a critical bottleneck.
