## Problem

Vision-language foundation models (e.g., CLIP, EVA-CLIP) achieve strong zero-shot classification on high-resolution benchmarks, but their robustness to low-resolution/pixelated images—a common real-world condition in surveillance, satellite imagery, and privacy-protected data—remains unexplored. No prior work has systematically benchmarked how 10+ foundation models across 66 backbones degrade as resolution drops to 16×16 or 32×32, nor identified which architectural and pre-training factors govern this degradation.

## Input / Output

The benchmark downsamples HR test images via bicubic interpolation to target resolutions (16×16, 32×32, 64×64, 128×128), then applies each model's standard preprocessing (e.g., resize to 224×224, center crop) to simulate real pixelation artifacts. On the text side, class labels are expanded through multiple dataset-specific templates (e.g., 'a photo of a [label]') and averaged into a single text embedding. The model outputs a Top-1 class prediction via dot-product matching between the image embedding and text embeddings, evaluated across 15 datasets ranging from ImageNet (1000 classes) to fine-grained sets like FGVC Aircraft and Oxford Flowers102.

## Method

LR-TK0 introduces trainable low-resolution tokens that are added (not concatenated) to the frozen spatial patch tokens before every transformer block, with the number of tokens equal to #spatial_tokens × (N+1) blocks, compensating for detail loss at early layers where LR features deviate most from HR. Training uses a self-supervised distillation scheme: the frozen pre-trained model acts as a teacher producing HR feature embeddings, while the student (same frozen weights + LR tokens) produces both HR and LR embeddings, which are matched to the teacher via a contrastive loss—anchoring HR-LR features around the frozen teacher to prevent collapse. The training data consists of synthetic images generated by PIXART-α from 7,000 randomly sampled Conceptual Captions (30 images per caption), downsampled across three multi-scale buckets [16,32], [32,64], [64,128], ensuring task-agnostic, label-free, and dataset-agnostic training. The approach adds only ~3% parameters, preserves all pre-trained weights, and is further supported by a new evaluation metric, Weighted Aggregated Robustness (WAR), which corrects for misleading robustness scores near random-guess accuracy and rebalances dataset contributions in model rankings.

## Results

On EVA-B/16 at 16×16, LR-TK0 improves SAR from 34.1 to 38.9, WAR from 26.8 to 29.5, and top-1 accuracy from 25.0% to 28.4%, outperforming all five super-resolution baselines (BSRGAN, ESRGAN, SwinIR, AddSR, Inf-DiT). Across three foundation models (EVA-B/16, MetaCLIP-B/16, OpenCLIP-B/16), LR-TK0 consistently boosts 16×16 SAR by 4–10 points (e.g., MetaCLIP: 32.1→41.9) while incurring only a 1–2% accuracy drop at 224×224. The method adds only ~3% parameters (e.g., 149.7M→155.2M for EVA-B/16) and generalizes to other zero-shot techniques, matching Visual Prompt Tuning (WAR-16: 35.4 vs. 35.5) and outperforming RobustSAM (35.4 vs. 32.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against all five super-resolution baselines on EVA-B/16 at 16×16, LR-TK0 (SAR 38.9) substantially outperforms the best SR method Inf-DiT (SAR 29.0) by ~10 points, and the traditional methods BSRGAN (12.4), ESRGAN (14.2), SwinIR (17.9), and AddSR (20.5) by 18–27 points; SR methods actually degrade below the no-SR baseline, confirming they fail in zero-shot very-low-resolution settings. Against Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) at 16×16, LR-TK0 achieves WAR 35.4 vs. VPT's 35.5 — essentially a tie — while preserving HR accuracy better (WAR-224: 98.7 vs. 97.8). Against RobustSAM (a segmentation model adapted for classification), LR-TK0 wins at every resolution, with the largest gap at 16×16 (WAR 35.4 vs. 32.2) and 224×224 (98.7 vs. 93.0). The key differentiator is that LR-TK0 improves low-resolution robustness without degrading high-resolution performance, whereas SR methods destroy zero-shot accuracy and VPT/RobustSAM sacrifice HR scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot classification evaluation protocol established by CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) to a previously unexplored stress condition—very low resolution (16×16 to 128×128)—across 10 foundation models and 66 backbones, far exceeding the 4–11 backbones in prior zero-shot benchmarks. Methodologically, LR-TK0 draws on the multi-scale training paradigm from person re-identification (Chen et al., 2019) and the contrastive alignment objective from CLIP, but departs from visual prompt tuning (Jia et al., 2022) by adding/merging tokens at every transformer block rather than concatenating them before the first block. The paper also positions itself against super-resolution pipelines, demonstrating that existing SR methods (BSRGAN, ESRGAN, SwinIR, AddSR, Inf-DiT) fail catastrophically at ≤64×64 in zero-shot settings, motivating a feature-space rather than pixel-space solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark of FM robustness to very low resolution, providing practitioners with actionable guidance: pre-training data quality outweighs quantity, model size helps, and fine-tuning or higher input resolution paradoxically hurts LR robustness. The LR-TK0 method offers a lightweight (+3% parameters) drop-in improvement that preserves pre-trained weights, making it practical for deployment in surveillance, satellite, and privacy-sensitive settings. Open questions include whether the findings generalize beyond zero-shot classification (e.g., detection, segmentation), whether the 7K-caption synthetic training set is sufficient for broader domain coverage, and the dataset-specific robustness patterns identified via t-SNE clustering, which the authors explicitly leave for future work.
