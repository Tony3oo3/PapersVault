## Problem

Existing neural operator architectures (FNO, DeepONet, NoMaD) are designed from mathematical intuition about PDE solution spaces and rely on simple building blocks like Fourier layers or MLPs, limiting their ability to capture complex multi-scale spatial dependencies. Meanwhile, advances in computer vision—particularly vision transformers and conditioned neural fields—offer powerful tools for continuous function representation that have not been systematically integrated into operator learning. The paper asks whether bridging these two fields can yield a more flexible, parameter-efficient neural operator that supports resolution-independent evaluation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a gridded spatio-temporal tensor u ∈ R^{T×H×W×D} (T time steps, H×W spatial grid, D channels) which is patchified into 3D tokens, along with arbitrary 2D query coordinates y ∈ [0,1]² at which the output is evaluated. The output is a continuous PDE solution field that can be queried at any spatial resolution, not just the training grid. The grid-based coordinate embedding uses a uniform grid of trainable latent features at the dataset's spatial resolution, with a Nadaraya-Watson interpolation parameter β=10⁵ controlling locality, enabling smooth evaluation between grid points.

## Method

CViT combines a vision transformer encoder with a novel grid-based coordinate embedding and a cross-attention decoder. The encoder patchifies inputs (8×8 patches), applies temporal aggregation via a Perceiver-style cross-attention module to compress the time axis into a fixed number of latent tokens, then processes them through L self-attention transformer blocks. For continuous output, a uniform grid of trainable latent features is interpolated at query coordinates using a Nadaraya-Watson kernel (parameterized by β), and the resulting interpolated feature serves as the query in a cross-attention decoder where the encoder output provides keys and values. The key theoretical insight is that the grid-based embedding provides a directly tunable Lipschitz constant (scaling as √β), offering more precise control over spectral bias than random Fourier features, which require increasing network width or frequency scale. Training uses a one-step MSE loss at randomly sampled query coordinates without rollout fine-tuning, yet CViT outperforms larger pretrained models (e.g., DPOT-H at 1.03B params) with as few as 13M parameters.

## Results

On the Navier-Stokes, Compressible Navier-Stokes, and Diffusion-Reaction benchmarks (Table 3), CViT-L (92M params) achieves relative L2 errors of 2.35%, 1.29%, and 0.68% respectively, outperforming the previous best DPOT-L (Fine-tuned, 500M params) at 2.78%, 1.31%, and 0.73% — all without pretraining or rollout fine-tuning. Notably, CViT-S (13M params) matches or exceeds the 1.03B-parameter DPOT-H (Pre-trained) on NS (3.75% vs. 3.79%) and DR (1.13% vs. 1.91%). On the shallow-water benchmark, CViT-L achieves 1.56% relative L2 error, beating U-F2Net (344M params, 1.89%) and FNO (268M params, 3.97%). On the discontinuous advection benchmark, CViT attains the lowest mean relative L2 error (11.70%) and worst-case L2 error (35.44%) among all compared operators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CViT outperforms all 12 baselines across the three PDE benchmarks in Table 3. Against the strongest pretrained competitor DPOT-L (Fine-tuned, 500M params, trained with rollout loss), CViT-L (92M params, one-step loss, no pretraining) wins on all three tasks: NS 2.35% vs. 2.78%, CNS 1.29% vs. 1.31%, DR 0.68% vs. 0.73%. The smaller CViT-S (13M) already beats the 1.03B-parameter DPOT-H (Pre-trained) on NS (3.75% vs. 3.79%) and DR (1.13% vs. 1.91%), demonstrating strong parameter efficiency. On the shallow-water benchmark (Table 2), CViT-L (92M, 1.56%) surpasses U-F2Net (344M, 1.89%), FNO (268M, 3.97%), and UNO (440M, 3.79%), while CViT-S (13M, 4.47%) is competitive with FNO. On the discontinuous advection benchmark (Table 5), CViT (3.03M) achieves the best mean L2 (11.70%) and worst-case L2 (35.44%) errors, beating FNO (4.98M, 13.94%/56.52%), DeepONet (2.20M, 13.77%/39.64%), and NoMaD (2.21M, 13.64%/69.00%), though NoMaD edges out CViT slightly on mean TV error (0.0326 vs. 0.0334). Transformer-based baselines without continuous query support (DPOT, MPP) cannot be evaluated at arbitrary resolutions, a structural limitation CViT overcomes via its grid-based coordinate embedding and cross-attention decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

CViT repurposes the vision transformer encoder (ViT patch embeddings, Perceiver-based temporal aggregation) and the conditioned neural field decoder paradigm from computer vision (NeRF, Instant NGP) into the neural operator setting, where prior architectures (FNO, DeepONet, NoMaD) relied on Fourier layers or MLPs. It departs from existing transformer-based operators (OFormer, GNOT, DPOT) by replacing random Fourier features or plain MLPs for query coordinates with a trainable grid-based Nadaraya-Watson interpolation, and by framing all encoder-decoder and integral-kernel operators as instances of conditioned neural fields (Appendices B–C). The theoretical contribution connects to the spectral bias literature (Rahaman et al., 2019; Tancik et al., 2020) by showing how the interpolation parameter β directly controls the Lipschitz constant of the coordinate embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CViT demonstrates that a 92M-parameter model trained with a simple one-step MSE loss can match or exceed 500M–1B-parameter pretrained operators (DPOT-H, MPP-L) on Navier-Stokes, compressible Navier-Stokes, and diffusion-reaction benchmarks, suggesting that architectural design may matter more than scale and pretraining for PDE surrogates. The grid-based coordinate embedding offers a single hyperparameter (β) for tuning spectral resolution, which is more interpretable than Fourier feature bandwidth. Key limitations remain: quadratic attention scaling restricts high-resolution 3D problems, the method assumes regular grids (not unstructured meshes), and the comparison to DPOT is confounded by DPOT's use of a rollout training objective. Open questions include extending CViT to 3D and irregular domains, integrating physics-informed losses for data efficiency, and exploring whether pretraining strategies can further improve generalization.
