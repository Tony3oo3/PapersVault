## Problem

Meta-Black-Box Optimization (MetaBBO) algorithms still depend on human-crafted Exploratory Landscape Analysis (ELA) features to profile low-level optimization status for the meta-level policy. These hand-crafted features suffer from high inter-feature correlation, sensitivity to sampling strategies, an inherently 'offline' character incompatible with dynamic MetaBBO, and additional function-evaluation overhead. The paper asks whether a single learnable landscape analyser can replace all hand-crafted ELA pipelines end-to-end and generalize across diverse MetaBBO algorithms and problem sets.

## Input / Output

At each optimization step t, the analyser receives a population of m d-dimensional candidate solutions {X_i^t} and their objective values {y_i^t}, along with the problem's search-range bounds [lb^j, ub^j]. A Population Information Embedding module min-max normalizes candidates against the search range and objective values against the current-step extremum, then embeds per-dimension tuples (X_i^j, y_i) into h-dimensional vectors of shape d×m×h. The output is dual-granularity: a per-candidate feature vector F_indiv (m×h) and a population-level feature vector F_pop (h), making the analyser compatible with MetaBBO algorithms that require either individual or aggregate landscape features.

## Method

NeurELA instantiates the landscape analyser Λ_θ as a two-stage attention encoder (Ts-Attn) preceded by a Population Information Embedding (PIE) module. Ts-Attn first applies a cross-solution attention block over the d×m×h tensor to share information among candidates at the same dimension, then transposes to m×d×h, adds cosine/sine positional encodings, and applies a cross-dimension attention block to share information across dimensions within each candidate; mean-pooling over dimensions and candidates yields the final features. Because the meta-objective (expected relative performance across K MetaBBO tasks) is non-differentiable, Λ_θ is trained via Fast CMA-ES neuroevolution over a multi-task operating space comprising three representative MetaBBO algorithms (LDE, RLEPSO, RL-DAS) on the BBOB test suite, with 10 analysers per generation over 50 generations parallelized via Ray. The pre-trained analyser supports zero-shot integration into unseen MetaBBO algorithms and efficient fine-tuning (under 20% of meta-training epochs) for task-specific adaptation.

## Results

NeurELA (3,296 parameters, h=16, l=1) achieves consistently superior zero-shot relative performance (Z-score normalized) against Original, ELA, and DeepELA across all three generalization settings: unseen MetaBBO algorithms (DED-DQN, RLPSO, GLEET, MelBA, GLHF), unseen problem sets (BBOB-Noisy, Protein-Docking), and both simultaneously. On BBOB-Noisy and Protein-Docking, NeurELA shows the largest margins over both ELA and DeepELA. Computationally, NeurELA matches or beats the Original baseline wall-time (e.g., 5.42 ms vs 4.99 ms at d=10, m=100) while ELA scales poorly (148 s at d=100 vs 6.24 ms for NeurELA). Fine-tuning NeurELA on a specific task requires less than 20% of the meta-training epochs to recover zero-shot-level performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the **Original** hand-crafted landscape analysers, NeurELA achieves superior relative performance across all five unseen MetaBBO algorithms (DED-DQN, RLPSO, GLEET, MelBA, GLHF) and both unseen problem sets (BBOB-Noisy, Protein-Docking), with the largest gains observed when both algorithm and problem set are simultaneously unseen. Against **traditional ELA** (Mersmann et al. 2011, implemented via pflacco), NeurELA consistently outperforms, particularly on BBOB-Noisy and Protein-Docking where ELA's results are described as 'quite noisy' due to inter-feature correlations; ELA also suffers severe curse-of-dimensionality (148 s at d=100 vs 6.24 ms for NeurELA). Against **DeepELA** (Seiler et al. 2024), NeurELA shows similar or better performance on standard BBOB but clearly outperforms on BBOB-Noisy and Protein-Docking, attributed to NeurELA's two-stage attention (cross-solution + cross-dimension) and its dynamic MetaBBO-oriented training objective versus DeepELA's static contrastive-learning objective. In the fine-tuning setting, NeurELA recovers or exceeds zero-shot performance within <20% of meta-training epochs, whereas the Original baseline cannot be 'fine-tuned' in the same manner.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Meta-Black-Box Optimization (MetaBBO) and Exploratory Landscape Analysis (ELA). While MetaBBO algorithms (LDE, RLEPSO, RL-DAS, GLEET) have established the bi-level paradigm of meta-learning control policies, they all still rely on hand-crafted ELA features for profiling low-level optimization status. Prior learning-based landscape analysis work (Prager et al. 2021, Seiler et al. 2022, DeepELA 2024) targets static algorithm selection/configuration in Automated Algorithm Selection, not the dynamic, step-by-step profiling required by MetaBBO. NeurELA repurposes Transformer-style attention (two-stage: cross-solution and cross-dimension) and Fast CMA-ES neuroevolution to learn a universal, end-to-end landscape analyser pre-trained across multiple MetaBBO tasks, departing from prior work in both its dynamic operating context and its non-differentiable, multi-task training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeurELA removes the last human-crafted component in MetaBBO pipelines, making these systems genuinely end-to-end and reducing the expert knowledge required to deploy them on new problems. The zero-shot transfer to five unseen MetaBBO algorithms and realistic benchmarks (Protein-Docking) is practically significant for the optimization community. However, a key limitation is training efficiency: the non-differentiable meta-objective forces reliance on evolution strategies, which struggle as model complexity grows (performance drops sharply beyond ~3K parameters), capping the expressiveness of the learned analyser. An important open question is whether a differentiable surrogate objective could enable gradient-based training and unlock larger, more expressive landscape analysers.
