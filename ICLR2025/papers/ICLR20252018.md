## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting and other novel view synthesis methods produce severe artifacts when test camera angles deviate significantly from the training view distribution, a scenario critical for AR/VR free-viewpoint navigation yet largely unexplored. The paper formalizes this as Out-of-Distribution Novel View Synthesis (OOD-NVS) and demonstrates through a new benchmark that existing regularization, data-driven prior, and feed-forward methods all fail to generalize to such extreme viewpoint shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SplatFormer takes as input an initial 3DGS set (up to 100k Gaussian splats, each with 3D position, scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, and spherical harmonic coefficients) optimized from 32 calibrated low-elevation input images, and outputs a refined 3DGS set by adding predicted residuals to each splat's attributes. The model is trained on 256×256 rendered images from both in-distribution (low-elevation) and OOD (high-elevation, ≥70°) views, with 70% of supervision coming from OOD views. At inference, a single forward pass over the initial splats yields the refined representation in ~108 ms on an RTX 4090 with ~900 MB GPU memory for typical object-centric scenes.

## Method

SplatFormer adapts the Point Transformer V3 (PTv3) architecture to process 3DGS splats as an unordered point cloud, where Gaussian attributes serve as per-point features. A hierarchical encoder with 5 down-pooling and 4 up-pooling stages (using serialization-based attention and grid pooling at resolution 384) extracts spatially contextualized features, which a 5-branch MLP decoder then maps to residuals for position, scale, opacity, quaternion, and spherical harmonics. The key design choice is residual prediction with zero-initialized final layers, ensuring the network starts as an identity mapping and learns only corrective adjustments. Training uses a photometric L1 + LPIPS loss on rendered images from both input and OOD views, supervised on a large-scale dataset of 33k ShapeNet and 48k Objaverse scenes where initial 3DGS are deliberately trained on low-elevation views only, creating pairs of flawed splats and ground-truth OOD renderings. This data-driven prior enables the model to learn generalizable corrections for input-view bias without hand-crafted regularization, outperforming both per-scene optimization methods and 2D denoising approaches.

## Results

On the proposed OOD-NVS benchmark (test views with elevation ≥70°), SplatFormer achieves 27.98 PSNR / 0.920 SSIM / 0.136 LPIPS on ShapeNet-OOD and 23.06 PSNR / 0.821 SSIM / 0.170 LPIPS on Objaverse-OOD, outperforming all 13 baselines. The closest competitor, 2DGS, reaches 23.52 PSNR on ShapeNet-OOD and 20.56 PSNR on Objaverse-OOD, yielding gaps of ~4.5 dB and ~2.5 dB respectively. Cross-dataset generalization to Google Scanned Objects (25.01 PSNR) and real-world iPhone captures (24.33 PSNR) confirms the learned prior transfers beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplatFormer outperforms all 13 baselines on both ShapeNet-OOD and Objaverse-OOD across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. Among regularized 3DGS variants, 2DGS is the strongest baseline (23.52 / 20.56 PSNR), but SplatFormer still leads by 4.46 dB on ShapeNet and 2.50 dB on Objaverse; SplatFields trails 2DGS on Objaverse (18.85 PSNR), suggesting its Deep Image Prior regularization is less effective under large viewpoint shifts. Among external-prior methods, Nerfbusters performs worst on ShapeNet (11.42 PSNR) due to over-aggressive floater removal, while SSDNeRF and InstantSplat struggle with cross-category generalization (15.36 and 18.49 PSNR respectively). Among feed-forward models, LaRa (20.94 / 19.04 PSNR) is the closest competitor but is limited to 4 input views due to memory constraints, whereas SplatFormer leverages all 32 input views through the initial 3DGS. Standard per-scene methods (3DGS, MipNeRF360, InstantNGP) all cluster around 19–20 PSNR, confirming that direct optimization without OOD-aware refinement cannot handle the 70°+ elevation gap. The 2D denoising ablation (DiffBIR + retrained 3DGS, 25.16 PSNR) further validates that operating in 3D space is essential for geometrically consistent artifact removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplatFormer sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and point transformer architectures, being the first work to apply a Point Transformer V3 encoder-decoder directly to a set of Gaussian splats as a point cloud with Gaussian attributes as features. It departs from the dominant line of work on robust 3DGS—hand-crafted regularization (2DGS, SplatFields, GeoGaussian) and external 2D/depth priors (FSGS, SSDNeRF, Nerfbusters)—by instead learning a data-driven 3D refinement prior from large-scale synthetic shape datasets. Unlike feed-forward 2D-to-3D methods (LaRa, MVSplat, PixelNeRF) that are constrained to ≤4 input views, SplatFormer operates on the full 3DGS optimized from all 32 input views, leveraging dense multi-view geometry while correcting its OOD bias in a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OOD-NVS protocol exposes a practically critical but previously unmeasured failure mode of 3DGS and related methods: rendering quality collapses when test views deviate significantly from training camera distributions, a common scenario in AR/VR and everyday capture. SplatFormer's large margin over all baselines (4–7 dB PSNR) and its cross-dataset generalization from synthetic to real-world captures demonstrate that learned 3D priors can substantially close this gap. Key open questions remain: the method struggles with high-frequency textures, is sensitive to the specific input-view distribution it was trained on (e.g., it fails when the OOD direction is reversed), and its extension to unbounded scenes (MVImgNet experiments) shows only partial artifact removal, leaving the generalization to in-the-wild environments as a significant challenge.
