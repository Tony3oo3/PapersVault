## Problem

Existing DPO-based detection evasion methods require directly fine-tuning the source LLM, which is computationally prohibitive for large models (e.g., 70B parameters) and impractical for adversaries who cannot access or re-deploy the target model. This paper asks whether a lightweight proxy attack—using a small, separately fine-tuned model—can achieve comparable evasion of leading text detectors without modifying the source model's weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252460/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (e.g., an 8-token prefix from human-written text, a tokenized question, or a scientific title) and produces a full generated passage from the source LLM. The critical constraint is that the humanized SLM and the source LLM must share the same vocabulary. The attack operates entirely at the decoding phase, modifying the LLM's next-token logit distribution at each step via a scalar attack ratio α ∈ {0.1, …, 1.0}, so no architectural changes or weight updates to the source model are required.

## Method

HUMPA is a two-stage proxy attack. First, a small language model (e.g., Llama3-8B) is fine-tuned with DPO using LoRA, where preference pairs are constructed by scoring candidate responses with a detector (Fast-DetectGPT) and labeling the higher-scoring response as preferred. Second, at inference time, the source LLM's logits are shifted by adding α times the logit difference between the humanized SLM and its pre-trained reference SLM (equation 5), effectively transplanting the SLM's learned distribution shift onto the large model. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) proves this decoding-time logit manipulation is mathematically equivalent to directly DPO-fine-tuning the large model with β = β₀/α, so the attack ratio α controls the evasion-versus-quality trade-off at inference rather than at training. This makes the attack plug-and-play: the SLM is trained once and can be applied to any compatible LLM without retraining.

## Results

HUMPA achieves an average AUROC drop of 70.4% across multiple datasets, with a maximum single-dataset drop of 95.0% (Binoculars on Mixtral-8×7B in black-box setting). In cross-discipline scenarios, detection accuracy drops by up to 90.9%, and in cross-language (English→German) settings, the drop reaches 91.3%. Critically, generation quality is preserved within a modest utility budget (ΔS_Bert ≤ 0.02, ΔROUGE-1 ≤ 0.03), with BERTScore remaining above 0.79 for all attacked models. The attack is also significantly more efficient than direct DPO fine-tuning of the source model, reducing fine-tuning time from 9.54 hrs (Llama3-70B) to 3.20 hrs (HUMPA with Llama3-8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares HUMPA against two text-level evasion baselines: DIPPER paraphrasing (Krishna et al., NeurIPS 2024) and query-based word substitutions (Shi et al., TACL 2024), evaluated on Llama2-13B with a humanized Llama2-7B proxy. HUMPA (α=1.2) dramatically outperforms both baselines across nearly all detectors: for example, Likelihood AUROC drops to 0.1647 vs. 0.8125 (DIPPER) and 0.9843 (substitutions), and Fast-DetectGPT drops to 0.0794 vs. 0.9754 and 0.9972. At α=1.5, HUMPA pushes most detector AUROCs below 0.06, effectively rendering them useless, while both baselines retain AUROCs above 0.19. Importantly, HUMPA maintains equal or better generation quality: S_Bert is 0.8053 (α=1.2) vs. 0.8006 (DIPPER) and 0.7989 (substitutions), and ROUGE-1 is 0.2281 vs. 0.2076 and 0.2015. The key advantage is that HUMPA operates at the model level (decoding-time logit manipulation) rather than modifying text post-hoc, making it a single-pass attack that does not require per-prompt processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate research threads: (1) DPO-based fine-tuning of small language models to evade text detectors (Nicks et al., 2024), which was limited to small models due to compute cost, and (2) proxy/decoding-time alignment methods (Mitchell et al., 2023b; Liu et al., 2024a,b) that transfer a small model's fine-tuning effect to a large model by manipulating logits at inference. HUMPA combines these by DPO-fine-tuning a small model against a detector's scoring function and then injecting the resulting distribution shift into a large model's decoding via a logit offset, eliminating the need to fine-tune the large model at all. The key theoretical departure is Theorem 3.2, which proves this proxy attack is mathematically equivalent to directly DPO-fine-tuning the large model with a rescaled β, providing a clean justification for the approach that prior proxy-tuning work lacked in the adversarial setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: any LLM deployment where an adversary can intercept the decoding process (e.g., open-source models, API access with logit-level control) is vulnerable to a lightweight, plug-and-play attack that requires no retraining of the source model. The cross-domain (90.9%) and cross-language (91.3%) AUROC drops suggest the 'human-like' distribution shift learned by the SLM is broadly transferable, making targeted defenses per-domain or per-language insufficient. A key open question the paper raises is how to build detectors robust to this class of decoding-time distribution manipulation, since the attacked model's output is statistically indistinguishable from human text under the detector's implicit reward. Limitations include the requirement that the SLM and LLM share vocabulary, and that the attacker has access to the source model's logits during inference.
