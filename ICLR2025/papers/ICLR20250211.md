## Problem

Low-rank decomposition is a standard tool for reducing parameter counts in neural networks, but it inherently constrains the effective rank of weight matrices to k, leading to a well-known accuracy gap relative to full-rank counterparts. This paper addresses the trade-off between parameter efficiency and representational capacity by asking whether the rank of a low-rank factorization can be increased without adding any trainable parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250211/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a drop-in modification to feed-forward layers: it takes the same inputs as the base architecture (text token sequences for LLM fine-tuning, RGB images for ViT/ConvNeXt classification, sparse 2D image sets for NeRF view synthesis, or 3D coordinate queries for occupancy networks) and produces the same outputs (classification scores, class labels, radiance/density values, or binary occupancy). The only architectural change is that each dense weight matrix W is replaced by sin(ω·UVᵀ)/g where U and V are the standard low-rank factors, ω is a fixed non-learnable frequency hyperparameter, and g=√n is a fixed gain factor. No additional parameters are introduced beyond the original U and V.

## Method

The core mechanism applies a sinusoidal non-linearity element-wise to the low-rank product UVᵀ, yielding the modified layer y = sin(ω·UVᵀ)x/g + b. The theoretical contribution (Proposition 1 and Theorem 1) proves that for any non-zero matrix A, the rank of sin(ω·A) grows linearly with ω (bounded below by ω·(A_min⁰/‖√|A|‖_op)²) provided ω ≤ π/(3A_min⁰), and that for standard initializations (Kaiming, Xavier) there always exists a threshold ω₀ above which rank(sin(ω·UVᵀ)) > rank(UVᵀ) = k. The sine function is specifically chosen over alternatives like ReLU or sigmoid because its periodicity, tunable via ω, is what generates the additional linear independence among matrix rows. In practice, ω is tuned as a hyperparameter (typically 50–1000 depending on rank and architecture), and the gain g=√n stabilizes gradients during backpropagation. The method is validated as a plug-in across LoRA/DoRA for LLMs, ViT/ConvNeXt pretraining, NeRF, and 3D occupancy networks, consistently outperforming vanilla low-rank baselines at the same parameter budget.

## Results

On LLaMA 3-8B commonsense reasoning, Sine LoRA at k=4 achieves 83.61 average accuracy, surpassing standard LoRA at k=4 (80.04) by 3.57 points and even exceeding LoRA at k=16 (82.90) while using half the parameters. On the NeRF LLFF benchmark, Sine Low-Rank at k=1 (1.3% of full-rank parameters) achieves 19.77 average PSNR, a 5.77 dB improvement over naive Low-Rank (14.00), with a BD-Rate of −64.72%. On 3D occupancy (Thai Statue), Sine Low-Rank at k=1 reaches 90.8 IoU versus 84.3 for Low-Rank, using only 2.1% of full-rank parameters. On RoBERTa V3 GLUE, Sine LoRA consistently improves average score by 0.32–0.73 points across ranks k=1–8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), Sine LoRA wins at every rank level on both RoBERTa V3 (GLUE avg +0.32 to +0.73) and LLaMA 3-8B (avg +2.45 to +3.57), with the most striking result being Sine LoRA k=4 (83.61) outperforming LoRA k=16 (82.90) at half the parameter count. Against DoRA (Liu et al., 2024), Sine DoRA at k=8 achieves 85.2 average on commonsense reasoning, matching DoRA k=32 (85.2) while using only 25% of its parameters (14.9M vs. 57.4M). On NeRF (Mildenhall et al., 2020), Sine Low-Rank at k=60 (48.6% of full-rank params) reaches 26.36 average PSNR, within just 0.49 dB of the full-rank baseline (26.85), while naive Low-Rank at the same rank only achieves 25.59. Against the full-rank ConvNeXt-Tiny baseline (Liu et al., 2022), Sine LR-ConvNeXt at k=20 achieves 62.8% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-100, slightly exceeding the full-rank model's 62.3% while using ~50% fewer parameters. For 3D occupancy (Mescheder et al., 2019), Sine Low-Rank at k=1 (2.1% params) achieves 90.8 IoU on the Thai Statue versus 84.3 for naive Low-Rank, recovering fine structural details that the low-rank model completely loses. On ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021), Sine Low-Rank at k=250 (50% of full-rank params) matches the full-rank ViT-Base accuracy on ImageNet-1k, while naive Low-Rank at the same rank shows a clear accuracy gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the low-rank adaptation lineage (LoRA, DoRA) by introducing a sinusoidal non-linearity applied element-wise to the UV^T product, theoretically showing via a rank-increase theorem that sin(ω·UV^T) can exceed the rank of UV^T without adding parameters. Conceptually, it repurposes the sinusoidal activations already well-known in coordinate-based MLPs (NeRF, occupancy networks) as a rank-amplification mechanism for general feed-forward layers, bridging the parameter-efficient fine-tuning literature with implicit neural representation techniques. The method is explicitly positioned as a drop-in plug-in rather than a new architecture, building directly on the LoRA and DoRA formulations it modifies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that a non-learnable sine with sufficient frequency provably increases rank provides a principled justification for a simple, zero-parameter-cost enhancement applicable across LLMs, ViTs, NeRF, and 3D shape models. However, the authors acknowledge that even with rank recovery, accuracy still falls short of full-rank counterparts, leaving open the question of how much overparameterization is truly necessary for strong generalization. The frequency ω remains a manually tuned hyperparameter with no principled selection rule, and the modest per-task gains (0.3–3.5 points on LLM benchmarks, 0.77–5.77 dB on NeRF) suggest the technique is a useful but not transformative improvement over existing low-rank methods.
