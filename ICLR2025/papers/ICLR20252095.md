## Problem

Multi-camera synchronized video generation from arbitrary viewpoints remains largely unexplored: prior multi-view video methods are restricted to 4D object synthesis from fixed orbital positions, and concurrent camera-control works only handle narrow viewpoint ranges. Two core challenges persist — maintaining 4D (spatiotemporal) consistency across widely separated viewpoints, and the severe scarcity of high-quality multi-camera video data with diverse poses for open-domain scenes.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and n camera extrinsic parameters (each a 3×4 [R|t] matrix, normalized so one camera defines the global frame) and produces n synchronized videos at 384×672 resolution with f frames each. For the novel-view-synthesis extension, a reference video is additionally provided: its latent replaces the first view's noisy latent at 90% probability during training, and at inference the reference video's encoded latent is injected at every denoising timestep while weighted classifier-free guidance (s_V=1.8, s_T=7.5) balances video and text conditioning.

## Method

SynCamMaster freezes a pre-trained DiT-based text-to-video model (using Rectified Flow / Conditional Flow Matching) and inserts a plug-and-play Multi-View Synchronization (MVS) module into every Transformer block. Each MVS module first embeds the 12-dim camera extrinsic parameters via a per-block fully-connected camera encoder and adds them element-wise to the spatial tokens, then performs a cross-view self-attention over all n views of the same frame to aggregate inter-view features, and finally projects back with a linear layer and residual connection. Training uses a hybrid data scheme — 500 UE-rendered multi-view video scenes (36 cameras each), multi-view images sampled from DL3DV-10K, and 12K filtered static-camera general videos — with a progressive curriculum that gradually widens the relative azimuth/elevation angle between sampled viewpoints (0–60° → 30–90° → 60–120° over 50K steps). Only the MVS modules and camera encoders are trained (zero-initialized projector, view-attention initialized from temporal-attention weights), keeping the base model frozen.

## Results

On the main evaluation set of 100 text prompts with 4 viewpoints each, SynCamMaster achieves a Mat. Pix. of 527.1K (vs. 150.4K for SVD-XT and 172.9K for CameraCtrl), FVD-V of 1470 (vs. 1742 and 1661), and CLIP-V of 93.71 (vs. 89.14 and 89.33), demonstrating substantially better inter-view synchronization. In terms of visual quality, SynCamMaster reports FID 116.7, FVD 1401, CLIP-T 33.40, and CLIP-F 99.36, which are comparable to the same-base I2V-Ours baseline (FID 113.1, FVD 1376). For camera control accuracy, SynCamMaster achieves the lowest rotation error (0.12) and translation error (0.58) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes three baselines by extracting the first frame from SynCamMaster-generated multi-view images and feeding them into existing image-to-video pipelines: SVD-XT, CameraCtrl, and a custom I2V model trained on the same base T2V model. SynCamMaster decisively outperforms all baselines on view synchronization metrics: Mat. Pix. is 3.5× higher than SVD-XT (527.1K vs. 150.4K) and 3.0× higher than CameraCtrl (527.1K vs. 172.9K), while FVD-V is 15–24% lower. On camera control accuracy (Table 4), SynCamMaster achieves RotErr 0.12 and TransErr 0.58, beating CameraCtrl (0.16 / 0.67) and SVD-XT (0.25 / 0.72). On visual quality, SynCamMaster is essentially on par with the same-base I2V-Ours (FID 116.7 vs. 113.1; FVD 1401 vs. 1376), confirming that the multi-view synchronization module does not degrade generation fidelity. The I2V-Ours baseline, while matching visual quality, still lags significantly on synchronization (Mat. Pix. 116.8K, FVD-V 1930), highlighting that simply generating each view independently cannot achieve cross-view consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and Rectified Flow training framework (Esser et al., 2024) to introduce a plug-and-play multi-view synchronization (MVS) module that enables a frozen text-to-video model to generate synchronized videos from arbitrary camera viewpoints. It departs from prior multi-view video generation work (SV4D, Vivid-ZOO, 4Diffusion) which is restricted to single-object 4D reconstruction with fixed orbital viewpoints, and from camera-control methods (CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl) that handle single-camera trajectories. The closest concurrent work, CVD (Kuang et al., 2024), extends camera control to multiple sequences but only for narrow viewpoint differences, whereas SynCamMaster targets open-domain scenes with large, arbitrary viewpoint discrepancies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SynCamMaster addresses a practically important gap for virtual filming and combinational shot production by being the first to generate open-domain multi-camera synchronized videos from arbitrary viewpoints, and it releases a multi-view synchronized video dataset (SynCamVideo-Dataset) to support future research. The hybrid training strategy—combining UE-rendered multi-view videos, multi-view images extracted from single-camera videos, and general static-camera videos—offers a practical recipe for overcoming multi-camera data scarcity that could transfer to other multi-view generation tasks. Key limitations include detail inconsistencies in complex scenes, inherited base-model weaknesses (e.g., hand generation), and the assumption of fixed camera poses per video, leaving open questions about dynamic multi-camera trajectories and scaling to more than a handful of viewpoints.
