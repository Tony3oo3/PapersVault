## Problem

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is highly sensitive to noise in preference training data, which manifests as pointwise noise (low-quality individual responses) and pairwise noise (erroneous chosen/rejected label associations). While DPO's regularization parameter β provides some inherent pointwise robustness, the method remains vulnerable to pairwise label flips, and existing remedies like rDPO rely on explicit noise estimation that is computationally costly and may miss complex noise patterns. The paper asks: can DPO be made robust to both noise types through a principled, lightweight mechanism that avoids explicit noise modeling?

## Input / Output

The method consumes the standard DPO preference dataset—triplets of (context x, chosen response y_w, rejected response y_l)—and produces a preference-aligned language model policy π_θ, identical in form to standard DPO output. The key operational detail is that Dr. DPO operates on the same per-sample DPO log-likelihood h_DPO(x, y_w, y_l) but aggregates them via a log-sum-exp (LSE) reduction instead of a simple mean, controlled by a new hyperparameter β′. Experiments use Pythia-2.8B and LLaMA2-13B as base models, with noise injected by randomly swapping y_w and y_l at rates of 10–40%, and evaluation is performed via preference accuracy, GPT-4-judged win rate on MT-Bench, and KL divergence from the reference policy.

## Method

Dr. DPO replaces the standard DPO mean loss with a distributionally robust objective: L_Dr.DPO = −β′ log E_O[exp(h_DPO(x,y_w,y_l)/β′)], where h_DPO is the per-sample DPO log-likelihood and β′ ∈ (0,∞) is a new robustness hyperparameter. The theoretical foundation shows that DPO is implicitly a pointwise DRO under KL divergence (Theorem 3.1), with β inversely related to the DRO robustness radius η (Lemma 3.2), and Dr. DPO extends this to pairwise robustness by optimizing against the worst-case reweighting of preference pairs within a KL ball around the empirical distribution (Theorem 4.1). Gradient analysis reveals that Dr. DPO introduces a data-dependent weight w(x,y_w,y_l) = exp(h_DPO/β′) / E[exp(h_DPO/β′)] that automatically downweights mislabeled pairs while upweighting correctly ranked ones, without any explicit noise estimation. A generalization bound (Theorem 4.2) via McDiarmid's inequality shows the empirical Dr. DPO loss upper-bounds the true worst-case loss with a gap that shrinks as N grows. Implementation requires only one additional line of PyTorch code (replacing losses.mean() with −β′·log(mean(exp(−losses/β′)))), and β′=1.0 serves as a stable default across noise levels.

## Results

On the Anthropic HH dataset, Dr. DPO achieves 66.22% preference accuracy at 0% label noise and 58.83% at 40% noise, outperforming DPO (63.63% / 55.23%), IPO (65.95% / 56.64%), and rDPO (64.37% / 57.11%). On the GPT-4-based Win Rate metric, Dr. DPO reaches 56.67% at 0% noise and 61.65% at 40% noise, compared to DPO's 54.36% and 49.00% respectively. On LLaMA2-13B, Dr. DPO achieves a 63.67% win rate versus 59.05% for DPO and 60.54% for rDPO. Dr. DPO also outperforms DPO on MT-Bench in both noise-free and 40%-noise settings, and on the Reddit TL;DR summarization task across all sampling temperatures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dr. DPO consistently outperforms all four baselines across every noise level on both preference accuracy and win rate. Against DPO, Dr. DPO gains +4.07% preference accuracy at 0% noise and +6.52% at 40% noise, with a particularly large +12.65% win-rate improvement at 40% noise (61.65% vs 49.00%). Against cDPO, Dr. DPO wins on all metrics; notably cDPO actually underperforms DPO on win rate (51.46% vs 54.36% at 0% noise), while Dr. DPO improves on both. Against IPO, Dr. DPO leads by +0.27% to +7.02% in preference accuracy and by +5.86% to +25.81% in win rate; IPO does beat DPO at 40% noise on win rate (54.82% vs 49.00%) but is still surpassed by Dr. DPO (61.65%). Against rDPO, Dr. DPO achieves +1.85% to +7.02% higher preference accuracy and +4.52% to +8.11% higher win rate, confirming that the DRO-based reweighting is more effective than rDPO's explicit noise-debiasing approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as an implicit pointwise Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) procedure, drawing on the DRO framework of Namkoong & Duchi (2017) and Duchi & Namkoong (2018) to reinterpret the KL-regularized reward maximization as worst-case distributional optimization. It then extends this DRO lens to the pairwise level, introducing Dr. DPO as a log-sum-exp reweighting of the DPO loss that targets label-flip noise without requiring explicit noise estimation—departing from rDPO (Chowdhury et al., 2024) which relies on a known flip probability, and from cDPO which patches the BT model with a BCE term. The Bradley-Terry model and the standard RLHF→DPO derivation remain the backbone; the novelty lies in the DRO reinterpretation and the resulting pairwise robustness mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: Dr. DPO adds a single line of code (a log-sum-exp over per-sample DPO losses) and a default β′=1.0 that consistently outperforms DPO, cDPO, IPO, and rDPO across noise levels on Pythia-2.8B and LLaMA2-13B, without inflating KL divergence. The theoretical reframing of β as a 'noise reflector' (inversely related to the DRO robustness radius η) gives practitioners a principled knob for handling SFT-stage data quality. Open questions include whether β′=1.0 remains optimal at larger scales (70B+), whether the framework extends to multi-turn or multi-preference settings, and whether adaptive β′ scheduling during training can eliminate the residual tuning burden the authors acknowledge.
