## Problem

Generating images in arbitrary canonical spaces (spheres for 360° panoramas, mesh surfaces for textures) using pretrained 2D diffusion models has two dominant approaches with complementary weaknesses: Diffusion Synchronization (DS) produces realistic outputs but fails to converge without strong conditioning (e.g., depth maps), while Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) handles unconditional generation but lacks fine detail. The paper reveals for the first time the formal interconnection between DS and SDS—showing that each SDS step is a one-step DDIM refinement at maximum stochasticity—and uses this insight to design a unified method that achieves both coherence and realism in the unconditional regime.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as the sole required input (plus an optional depth map via ControlNet for 3D mesh texturing) and produces either a 2048×4096 equirectangular 360° panorama or a texture map on an arbitrary 3D mesh surface. Internally, the canonical space is decomposed into N non-overlapping perspective views (512×512 each, with 72° FoV for panoramas), and the pretrained Stable Diffusion 2.1 Base model operates on these 2D instance-space patches. The method is fully zero-shot: no finetuning on panoramic or mesh-specific data is required, and the denoising loop can stop early at T_stop=270 (out of T=900) since it iterates SDEdit rather than a full reverse process.

## Method

StochSync modifies the Diffusion Synchronization framework with three key changes derived from analyzing SDS: (1) maximum stochasticity (σt=√(1−αt−1)) in the DDIM denoising step, which eliminates the noise-prediction term εt and resets the denoising trajectory at each step to improve cross-view coherence; (2) replacing the one-step Tweedie's formula prediction of x0|t with a multi-step deterministic DDIM denoising process G(xt), which keeps samples on the clean data manifold and restores realism lost to stochasticity; and (3) sampling non-overlapping views at each timestep (alternating between two shifted sets) to avoid the blurriness introduced by averaging overlapping projections. From the SDS perspective, the method equivalently replaces random timestep sampling with a linearly decreasing schedule, swaps single gradient-descent updates for full l2-minimization over the canonical space, and uses multi-step denoising—making it interpretable as iterating SDEdit across views rather than performing a traditional reverse diffusion process.

## Results

On 360° panorama generation (PanFusion prompts), StochSync achieves the best FID of 57.88, IS of 10.02, GIQA of 20.30, and CLIP of 31.01, outperforming the closest zero-shot baseline L-MAGIC (FID 59.83) and all finetuning-based methods (MVDiffusion FID 70.49, PanFusion FID 93.85). On 3D mesh texturing, StochSync achieves FID 22.29, KID 1.31, and CLIP 28.57, matching the base DS method SyncTweedies (FID 21.76) while outperforming all other baselines by large margins (e.g., TEXTure FID 34.98, Paint3D FID 31.66).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StochSync outperforms all SDS-based methods (SDS, SDI, ISM) by large margins on FID (57.88 vs. 96–144) and IS (10.02 vs. 8.1–8.2), though ISM edges it slightly on CLIP (31.31 vs. 31.01). Against finetuning-based panorama methods, StochSync beats MVDiffusion on FID (57.88 vs. 70.49) and GIQA (20.30 vs. 18.81) but trails on IS (10.02 vs. 10.87), and dominates PanFusion across all four metrics. Against the strongest zero-shot baseline L-MAGIC, StochSync wins on every metric (FID 57.88 vs. 59.83, IS 10.02 vs. 9.12, GIQA 20.30 vs. 19.13, CLIP 31.01 vs. 29.73), and a user study confirms 56–65% preference for StochSync, particularly on horizon-specific prompts where L-MAGIC exhibits wavy distortions. For 3D mesh texturing, StochSync is comparable to the base DS method SyncTweedies (FID 22.29 vs. 21.76; KID 1.31 vs. 1.46) while substantially outperforming SDS-based Paint-it (FID 28.23), finetuning-based Paint3D (FID 31.66), and outpainting-based TEXTure (FID 34.98) and Text2Tex (FID 26.10) on all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—Diffusion Synchronization (DS, building on SyncTweedies) and Score Distillation Sampling (SDS, from DreamFusion)—by revealing for the first time that each SDS step can be interpreted as a one-step DDIM refinement with maximum stochasticity. StochSync then combines the strengths of both: it adopts DS's reverse-process structure and l2-minimization for realism, while incorporating SDS's maximum stochasticity (σt=√(1−αt−1)) for cross-view coherence, augmented with multi-step x0|t denoising and non-overlapping view sampling. The method is also interpretable as an iterative SDEdit loop, connecting it to the image editing literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StochSync provides a principled zero-shot framework for generating images in arbitrary canonical spaces (spheres, cylinders, mesh surfaces) without finetuning, addressing the key practical limitation of prior DS methods that require strong conditioning (e.g., depth maps) to avoid visible seams. The theoretical result that maximum stochasticity causes the forward process to diverge rather than converge to an SDE as timesteps increase is a genuine insight that explains why simply adding more steps does not help under this regime. A noted limitation is that the l2-minimization synchronization struggles with 3D NeRF/Gaussian generation due to overfitting to inconsistent intermediate views, leaving this as an open direction.
