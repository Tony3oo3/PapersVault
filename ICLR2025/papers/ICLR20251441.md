## Problem

Conventional multi-layer GNNs (e.g., GCN, GAT) assume a synchronization barrier between message-passing layers, but real-world decentralized systems (robotic swarms, sensor networks) operate asynchronously with delayed or lost messages. Under asynchrony, the effective computation graph diverges from the trained architecture, causing catastrophically incorrect and nondeterministic predictions. The paper identifies which GNN architectures are provably robust to this failure mode and proposes a new architecture within that robust class.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with adjacency matrix, node features, and edge features, and produces node embeddings by iteratively minimizing a convex per-node energy function until convergence (no fixed number of layers). A readout MLP then maps each embedding to a node-level prediction (classification or regression). Unlike standard GNNs where depth is a hyperparameter, the effective 'depth' here is determined by the convergence tolerance of the optimization, and each node only needs fixed-size messages from its 1-hop neighbors at any time step.

## Method

The paper introduces the Energy GNN, an implicitly-defined, optimization-based GNN whose node embeddings minimize a parameterized convex graph energy E_θ constructed from partially input-convex neural networks (PICNNs). The energy is separable per node: each node's term combines a PICNN message function (convex and nondecreasing in neighbor embeddings) aggregated over neighbors, a PICNN update function (convex in embeddings and messages), and a squared-norm penalty ensuring strong convexity. Training uses implicit differentiation through the optimality conditions (∇E_θ = 0) to backpropagate without unrolling iterations, plus warm-starting from the previous epoch's solution. The key theoretical result proves convergence under partial asynchrony by adapting Bertsekas–Tsitsiklis distributed optimization guarantees, with a critical design choice: each neighbor j sends its own gradient contribution computed from j's view of its neighbors (not the receiver's view of 2-hop neighbors), preserving fully local, fixed-size communication.

## Results

On the synthetic multi-agent tasks, the Energy GNN (edge-wise) achieves the best or near-best performance across all five benchmarks: 1.2% error on Chains, 4.0% relative RMSE on Count, 4.9% on Sum, 33.5% on Coordinates, and 13.0% classification error on MNIST Terrain. The Energy GNN+Attention variant further improves Chains to 0.25% and Coordinates to 30.9%. Under asynchronous inference, all implicitly-defined GNNs (including Energy GNN variants) maintain performance with <0.1% degradation, while explicitly-defined GCN and GAT suffer catastrophic drops (e.g., GCN Count error jumps from 40.7% to 584.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Energy GNN (edge-wise) decisively outperforms both implicitly-defined baselines: IGNN (26.9%→1.2% on Chains, 40.2%→4.0% on Count, 52.0%→33.5% on Coordinates) and GSDGNN (35.9%→1.2% on Chains, 40.3%→4.0% on Count, 44.0%→33.5% on Coordinates), with the largest gains on long-range communication tasks. Against explicitly-defined GCN and GAT, the Energy GNN wins on all five synthetic tasks; GCN and GAT are essentially at chance on Chains (47.0%) due to their fixed 5-layer depth limiting information propagation. On benchmark graph datasets, the Energy GNN (edge-wise) achieves the best MUTAG accuracy (87.6% vs. IGNN 73.0%, GSDGNN 78.4%, GCN 78.0%) and competitive PPI micro-F1 (76.2, tied with GCN), though GCN edges it slightly on PROTEINS (73.7% vs. 72.5%). The critical differentiator is robustness: under simulated asynchronous inference, GCN and GAT degrade catastrophically (e.g., GAT Coordinates error jumps from 39.3% to 97.0%), while all Energy GNN variants show <0.1% performance loss, confirming the theoretical convergence guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a sharp conceptual boundary between explicitly-defined GNNs (GCN, GAT) and implicitly-defined GNNs (fixed-point and optimization-based), showing that only the latter are provably robust to partially asynchronous inference by adapting convergence results from Bertsekas (1983) and Bertsekas & Tsitsiklis (1989). The proposed Energy GNN extends the optimization-based GNN family (GSDGNN, APPNP, EIGNN) by replacing their restricted quadratic objectives with partially input-convex neural networks (PICNNs, Amos et al. 2017), yielding a much richer class of convex energy functions while preserving the separability and local-communication properties needed for decentralized execution. The work is distinct from concurrent asynchrony-invariant GNN approaches (Dudzik et al. 2023; Monteagudo-Lago et al. 2024) that require nodes to retain all-layer information, and from Faber & Wattenhofer (2024) which assumes deterministic, controlled update ordering rather than random delays.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first provable guarantee that a class of GNNs can execute multi-layer inference in fully decentralized, asynchronous settings — a prerequisite for deploying learned state representations in robotic swarms, sensor networks, and other systems where synchronization barriers are impractical. Key limitations include the unknown a priori convergence time of the forward pass, sensitivity to the condition number of the energy Hessian, and the fact that the asynchrony simulation uses a simplified bounded-staleness model (B=5) that may not capture all real-world communication failures. Open questions include whether these architectures scale to dynamic graphs with real-time inference, whether they can serve as state representations for control (not just prediction) tasks, and how to handle the practical challenge of distributing inference across many processors for very large graphs.
