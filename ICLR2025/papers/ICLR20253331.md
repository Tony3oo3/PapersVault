## Problem

LLM-based embodied planners struggle with long-horizon tasks because existing refinement methods (self-reflection, tree-of-thought, demonstration imitation) rely on trajectory-level outcome feedback that is costly, non-proactive, and prone to compounding errors. Meanwhile, LLMs lack domain-specific grounding knowledge while few-shot demonstrations alone suffer from poor generalization out-of-distribution. The paper addresses how to provide dense, step-level guidance to LLM planners using only a limited set of expert demonstrations.

## Input / Output

The discriminator takes a triple of (task description l, summary of the last 10 actions h_t, candidate action a_t) and outputs an integer alignment score in [0, 10], where 10 indicates full agreement with the expert policy. The LLM planner receives a prompt containing the task goal, current environment state, and the full trajectory of previously executed action-score pairs, then generates the next action following valid templates (e.g., 'use X on Y'). Scores are deliberately rounded to integers rather than decimals because LLMs process integers more effectively for optimization reasoning. When a score falls below a task-specific threshold (5 for ScienceWorld, 6 for VirtualHome), a score-based search replanning loop is triggered before the action is executed.

## Method

DGAP trains a 125M-parameter RoBERTa-based discriminator with a linear regression head (L2 loss) on ~500k samples constructed from three sources: expert oracle trajectories (score 10), semantically unrelated random pairs (score 0), and 400k augmented samples generated by a fine-tuned FLAN-T5-Large via beam search, scored by cosine similarity to ground-truth actions. At inference time, the LLM is prompted with historical action-score pairs in a process-supervised format, implicitly treating itself as an optimizer that maximizes the discriminator's score without any parameter updates. The theoretical analysis shows this process is equivalent to critic-regularized RL optimization, yielding an optimal policy π*(a|s) ∝ π^llm(a|s)·exp(R_φ(s,a)/β) that provably achieves higher expected return than the base LLM policy. A closed-loop refinement mechanism replans when scores drop below threshold, and high-scoring actions are highlighted in subsequent prompts to reinforce successful patterns.

## Results

On ScienceWorld (30 task types), DGAP-GPT4 achieves 84.52% success rate on long-horizon tasks and 81.84% on medium tasks, outperforming SwiftSage-GPT4 (83.00% and 77.79%) and all other baselines. On VirtualHome, DGAP-GPT4 reaches 88.00% success rate (SR) in In-Distribution, 62.67% in NovelScenes, and 72.17% in NovelTasks, consistently surpassing ProgPrompt, Inner Monologue, and Tree Planner on SR across all three settings. DGAP also demonstrates lower standard deviation (e.g., ±0.86 vs ±2.02 for ProgPrompt in In-Distribution EXEC), indicating greater stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGAP outperforms SwiftSage (the primary SOTA baseline) on medium and long ScienceWorld tasks: D-GPT4 achieves 81.84% vs 77.79% (medium) and 84.52% vs 83.00% (long), while D-Llama3 reaches 84.01% vs 83.83% (medium) and 78.47% vs 62.31% (long). On short tasks, SwiftSage-GPT4 (92.22%) slightly edges out DGAP-GPT4 (90.84%), which the authors attribute to limited action-score pairs in short sequences. Against Reflexion and TDT, DGAP wins by large margins (e.g., 84.52% vs 30.17% for Reflexion and 6.11% for TDT on long tasks). On VirtualHome, DGAP-GPT4 surpasses Tree Planner on SR in all settings (62.67% vs 41.67% in NovelScenes; 72.17% vs 52.33% in NovelTasks) despite Tree Planner achieving higher EXEC (89.33% and 90.33%), and dominates ProgPrompt and Inner Monologue by 20–30 points on SR in out-of-distribution settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGAP carves new ground by repurposing the discriminator concept from GANs and the critic-regularized optimization framework from RL into a step-level scoring mechanism that guides an LLM planner via in-context prompting, without any parameter updates. It departs from SwiftSage (which uses demonstrations for direct imitation) and Reflexion/ToT (which rely on trajectory-level outcome feedback) by converting limited expert demonstrations into a dense, per-step numeric signal through a RoBERTa-based regression model. The theoretical framing as a KL-constrained policy improvement problem connects it to RLHF, but the key distinction is that the 'reward model' is trained on a simple L2 regression over augmented demonstration data rather than human preferences, and the 'policy optimization' is performed implicitly by the LLM as a prompted optimizer rather than through gradient-based RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DGAP offers a practical, API-compatible framework for improving LLM embodied planning with provable policy improvement over the base LLM, requiring only a small number of demonstrations (10–30 trajectories per task) and no model fine-tuning. However, the approach has notable limitations: it underperforms on short-sequence tasks, requires substantial data engineering (500k augmented samples for the discriminator), and the ablation reveals that the score signal can be counterproductive when augmented training data diverges from real interaction distributions. Open questions include whether the discriminator-based scoring generalizes beyond the two text-based benchmarks tested, whether the approach scales to truly open-world embodied settings with continuous action spaces, and how to reduce the data preparation overhead that currently limits practical adoption.
