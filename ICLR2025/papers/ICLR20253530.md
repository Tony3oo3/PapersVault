## Problem

Sparse regularization via L1 penalties is incompatible with standard SGD due to non-differentiability, limiting its use in neural network training. While shallow weight factorization (D=2) was recently shown to enable differentiable L1 regularization, it remains unclear whether deeper factorizations (D≥3) are theoretically justified, how they affect training dynamics, and what practical requirements (initialization, learning rate) are needed to make them work.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard neural network architecture (LeNet, VGG, ResNet) with its usual weight parameters and factorizes each scalar weight into D multiplicative factors, then trains on standard image classification datasets (32×32 or 28×28 grayscale/RGB images). The output is a sparse collapsed network obtained by multiplying the D factors back together, with near-zero weights thresholded to exactly zero—no post-hoc pruning or fine-tuning is required. The sparsity level is controlled by the L2 regularization strength λ applied to the factors during training.

## Method

Deep Weight Factorization (DWF) decomposes every network weight w into D≥2 factors (w = ω₁ ⊙ … ⊙ ω_D) and applies standard L2 weight decay to the factors during SGD training. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that the smooth factorized objective is equivalent to a non-convex L_{2/D} quasi-norm regularized objective on the collapsed weights, with D=2 recovering L1 and larger D approaching L0-like behavior. Two critical practical contributions enable successful training: a tailored initialization combining variance matching (Var(ω_d) = Var(w)^{1/D}) with absolute-value truncation to prevent kurtosis explosion and dead weights, and a requirement for sufficiently large initial learning rates that trigger a 'stochastic collapse' into balanced factorizations. Training exhibits three distinct phases—initial fitting, a reorganization phase with temporary accuracy drops and norm increases, and a final mixed sparsification/generalization phase—where the reorganization phase contracts with increasing λ, producing delayed generalization analogous to grokking.

## Results

The primary metric is compression ratio (CR) at a fixed accuracy tolerance (5% or 10% below dense accuracy). DWF with D=3 achieves the highest compression in 9 of 10 evaluated scenarios, reaching CR=1456 on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 and CR=1014 on VGG-19/CIFAR-100 at 10% tolerance. The best DWF model achieves 2–5× the compression ratio of the best pruning method (SynFlow), and surpasses shallow factorization (D=2) in all but one setting with improvements ranging from 8% to 298%. On LeNet-300-100/MNIST, DWF with D=3,4 remains within 5% of dense performance even above CR=500, where all pruning baselines have collapsed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DWF (D=3) dominates all three pruning baselines across every architecture/dataset combination. Against SynFlow (the strongest pruning baseline), DWF D=3 achieves 381% higher CR on VGG-19/CIFAR-100 (1014 vs. 218) and 102% higher CR on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 (1456 vs. 721) at 10% tolerance. Against GMP, the gap is even wider: DWF D=3 reaches CR=1456 vs. GMP's 484 on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 (3×), and CR=1014 vs. 68 on VGG-19/CIFAR-100 (15×). SNIP is the weakest baseline, collapsing to CR=42 on VGG-19/CIFAR-100 at 10% tolerance versus DWF's 1014. DWF also outperforms its own shallow D=2 variant in 9 of 10 settings, with the largest gains (up to 298%) on LeNet-300-100/F-MNIST, confirming that deeper factorization (D≥3) is essential for extreme sparsity regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weight factorization (Hadamard product parameterization) lineage from Hoff (2017) for linear models and Ziyin & Wang's (2023) shallow (D=2) 'spred' method for neural networks to arbitrary depth D≥3. The key conceptual departure is proving that D-fold factorization with L2 weight decay is exactly equivalent to non-convex L_{2/D} regularization on the collapsed weights (Theorem 1), generalizing the known D=2 equivalence to L1. The paper also draws on the artificial symmetry and stochastic collapse literature (Ziyin 2023; Chen et al. 2024) to explain why large learning rates are necessary and how SGD dynamics collapse onto balanced factorization subspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DWF offers a drop-in, differentiable sparsification mechanism for any neural network that requires no post-hoc pruning or fine-tuning, and empirically achieves 2–5× higher compression ratios than state-of-the-art pruning methods (SynFlow, GMP, SNIP) at matched accuracy. The three-phase training dynamics with delayed generalization (analogous to grokking) and the requirement for large initial learning rates are practically important findings that distinguish DWF training from standard deep learning. Key limitations include restriction to unstructured sparsity, diminishing returns beyond D=4, and the need for careful initialization (variance matching + truncation) that adds implementation complexity. Extending the framework to structured sparsity and understanding the stochastic collapse dynamics more deeply remain open directions.
