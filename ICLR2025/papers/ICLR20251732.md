## Problem

State-of-the-art GCD methods rely on self-distillation with soft labels that inherently bias the model toward known classes, since unlabelled samples lack ground-truth supervision. Additionally, existing approaches treat all unlabelled samples uniformly regardless of their certainty level and fail to explicitly identify semantic distribution shifts between known and unknown categories, leading to suboptimal clustering of novel classes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a partially-labelled RGB image dataset where labelled images carry class labels and unlabelled images may belong to either known or unknown categories, with the total number of categories K assumed known. The backbone is a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 producing 768-dimensional classification-token features, processed in batches of 128 over 200 epochs. At inference, only the GCD classifier is retained, so the method incurs no additional computational overhead beyond the baseline SimGCD pipeline.

## Method

DebGCD introduces three jointly trained components: (1) an auxiliary debiased classifier operating in the same prototype-based feature space as the GCD classifier but trained exclusively with one-hot hard labels (converting soft self-distillation labels to hard labels and filtering by a confidence threshold τ), thereby eliminating the known/unknown label bias; (2) a semantic distribution detector consisting of M one-vs-all binary classifiers in a separate MLP-projected, L2-normalized feature space that learns to distinguish in-distribution (known) from out-of-distribution (unknown) samples via supervised multi-binary cross-entropy on labelled data and entropy minimization on unlabelled data; and (3) a curriculum learning mechanism that computes a normalized distribution certainty score d_i = |2·s_i − 1| and a task-consistency filter to progressively weight unlabelled samples in the debiased loss, prioritizing samples with clear semantic distribution membership. All three tasks are optimized end-to-end in a single stage with the combined loss L_all = L_gcd + λ_sdl·L_sdl + λ_adl·L_adl, requiring no warm-up phases or additional inference-time computation.

## Results

On the fine-grained SSB benchmark, DebGCD achieves an average 'All' ACC of 64.4%, surpassing the second-best SPTNet (61.4%) by 3.0%, with standout results on Stanford Cars (65.3% vs. 59.0%) and FGVC-Aircraft (61.7% vs. 59.3%). On generic datasets, DebGCD sets new state-of-the-art on CIFAR-100 (83.0%), ImageNet-100 (85.9%), and ImageNet-1K (65.0%), where it outperforms the previous best (SimGCD, 57.1%) by 7.9%. On CIFAR-10, performance is near-saturated at 97.2%, comparable to the strongest baselines (PromptCAL 97.9%, CiPR 97.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DebGCD outperforms all 15 baselines on CIFAR-100 (83.0% vs. best prior 81.5% CiPR) and ImageNet-100 (85.9% vs. 85.4% SPTNet), and establishes a new state-of-the-art on ImageNet-1K by a large margin of 7.9% over SimGCD (65.0% vs. 57.1%). On the fine-grained SSB benchmark, DebGCD wins on Stanford Cars (65.3% vs. 59.0% SPTNet) and FGVC-Aircraft (61.7% vs. 59.3% SPTNet) but trails InfoSieve on CUB (66.3% vs. 69.4%), which is attributed to InfoSieve's hierarchical encoding specifically designed for fine-grained recognition. On CIFAR-10, DebGCD (97.2%) is marginally behind PromptCAL (97.9%) and CiPR (97.7%), though all methods are near-saturated above 97%. The comparison with the direct baseline SimGCD (Wen et al. 2023) shows consistent gains: +2.9% on CIFAR-100, +2.9% on ImageNet-100, and +7.9% on ImageNet-1K, validating the contribution of debiased learning and distribution guidance over the self-distillation-only approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

DebGCD builds directly on the SimGCD parametric baseline (Wen et al., 2023), adopting its self-distillation classifier with K normalized prototypes and DINO-pretrained ViT backbone. It departs from this lineage by introducing two auxiliary mechanisms absent in prior GCD work: (1) a parallel debiased classifier trained with one-hot hard labels to counteract the inherent label bias between known and unknown classes, and (2) a one-vs-all semantic distribution detector in a decoupled ℓ₂-normalized feature space, drawing from the open-set semi-supervised learning literature (Saito & Saenko, 2021; Li et al., 2023). The curriculum learning strategy that gates debiased training via a distribution certainty score is a novel synthesis of OOD detection and self-training principles, positioned as the first explicit treatment of label bias in the GCD setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying and addressing label bias as a first-class problem in GCD, this paper reframes how the community thinks about the interaction between known and unknown classes during training, suggesting that soft-label self-distillation alone is insufficient. The single-stage, inference-free design makes the approach practically attractive for deployment. However, the method still assumes the total number of categories K is known, relies on a DINO-pretrained backbone, and the debiasing threshold τ requires calibration on a validation split of labelled data—constraints that limit generality. Open questions include whether the auxiliary debiased classifier generalizes to non-prototype-based GCD formulations and whether the curriculum component remains beneficial at larger scale or with stronger pre-training.
