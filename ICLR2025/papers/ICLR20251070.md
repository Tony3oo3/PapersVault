## Problem

Most existing individual kinase-targeted phosphorylation site prediction methods rely solely on sequence inputs, neglecting 3D structural information that captures surface accessibility, grooves, and pockets relevant to phosphorylation. Additionally, no publicly available dataset integrates structural elements with kinase-substrate pairs, limiting the field's ability to benchmark structure-aware predictors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251070/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes four input streams per prediction: a local 11-mer substrate sequence encoded by frozen ESM2-650M, a local 11-residue 3D structure (Cα coordinates from AlphaFold2) encoded by a 6-layer R-GCN, the full-length kinase sequence (also ESM2-encoded, then mean-pooled and broadcast to the 11-mer), and four binary physicochemical property vectors (aliphatic, aromatic, acidic charged, basic charged). The output is a single binary label indicating whether the central S/T/Y residue is phosphorylated by the given kinase, trained with a standard cross-entropy loss over 18,360 positive samples balanced 1:1 with negatives.

## Method

SAGEPhos introduces a two-stage Multimodal Gated-Residual Integration (MERGE) module built around a Dynamic fusiOn meChanism (DOC) that combines a gated network (which preserves the primary modality while filtering auxiliary noise via sigmoid gating) and a residual network (which selectively injects auxiliary features to enrich the primary representation), weighted by learnable scalars α and β. In the first stage (Bio-Augmented Fusion), the local substrate sequence serves as the primary modality and the R-GCN-encoded local structure as the auxiliary, injecting spatial accessibility information while discarding prediction noise. In the second stage (Bio-Coupled Fusion), the fused substrate representation is the primary modality, while the pooled kinase sequence and physicochemical properties act as auxiliaries, creating a shared semantic space that captures kinase-substrate interaction patterns. The key design principle is a modality-prior hierarchy: the larger, task-concentrated substrate space is always primary, and the smaller kinase or noisier structural space modifies it rather than dominating, enabling the model to distill relevant interaction signals while suppressing irrelevant noise.

## Results

On the warm-start benchmark (18,360 positive samples from Phospho.ELM, PhosphoNetworks, and PhosphoSitePlus with AlphaFold2 structures), SAGEPhos achieves 80.6% accuracy, 88.3% AUC-ROC, 86.2% AUC-PRC, and 21.7% FPR. This represents approximately 10% and 12% improvements in accuracy and AUC-ROC over the strongest baseline (Phosformer-ST: 64.7% Acc, 78.9% AUC-ROC). On the zero-shot CDK17 test set (a kinase absent from training), SAGEPhos reaches 91.6% accuracy and 97.4% AUC-ROC, far exceeding the next-best method (Phosformer-ST at 52.2% Acc, 90.4% AUC-ROC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGEPhos outperforms all six baselines on accuracy, AUC-ROC, and AUC-PRC in the warm-start setting. The closest competitor, Phosformer-ST (Zhou et al., 2024), achieves 64.7% accuracy and 78.9% AUC-ROC, trailing SAGEPhos by 15.9 and 9.4 percentage points respectively; however, Phosformer-ST's FPR of 95.6% indicates it heavily over-predicts positives. MusiteDeep (Wang et al., 2017) is second-best on accuracy (73.5%) but has a critically low AUC-PRC of 7.3%, reflecting poor precision in imbalanced settings. Phosformer (Zhou et al., 2023) achieves the lowest FPR (1.4%) but at the cost of a very low true-positive rate (~11.3%), effectively predicting almost all negatives. On cold-start splits, SAGEPhos maintains its lead: 68.6% Acc / 76.9% AUC-ROC on kinase-cold-start and 79.1% Acc / 87.2% AUC-ROC on substrate-cold-start, versus 61.0% / 72.7% and 63.5% / 79.3% for Phosformer-ST. In the zero-shot CDK17 experiment, SAGEPhos (91.6% Acc, 97.4% AUC-ROC) dramatically surpasses Phosformer-ST (52.2% Acc, 90.4% AUC-ROC) and MusiteDeep (69.3% Acc, 94.2% AUC-ROC), demonstrating strong generalization to entirely unseen kinase families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the kinase-specific phosphorylation site prediction task introduced by Phosformer (Zhou et al., 2023) by incorporating 3D structural information via AlphaFold2 predictions encoded with R-GCN, a modality that prior individual-kinase methods entirely neglected. The sequence backbone leverages ESM2 (a substantially larger pre-trained protein language model than Phosformer's), and the multimodal fusion strategy draws conceptually from joint representation learning in protein science (SAPROT, ESM-GearNet) and cross-modal gated/residual fusion from vision-language tasks (TIRG, Vilbert). The 'Bio-Coupled' and 'Bio-Augmented' fusion framings are novel in explicitly assigning primary/auxiliary roles to modalities based on data volume and reliability, rather than treating all inputs symmetrically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The new dataset combining curated phosphorylation sites from three databases with AlphaFoldDB structural information fills a gap in the field and provides a benchmark for structure-aware phosphorylation prediction. The zero-shot generalization to completely unseen kinases (e.g., CDK17 at 91.6% accuracy) has direct practical implications for kinase inhibitor design, where novel targets are the norm. Key limitations include reliance on predicted (not experimental) structures, a higher FPR than Phosformer (21.7% vs. 1.4%, though Phosformer's TPR is only 11.3%), and evaluation limited to human proteins. Open questions include whether experimental structures would further improve performance and whether the approach scales to non-human proteomes or other PTMs.
