## Problem

Existing GPU top-k implementations (Dr. Top-k, AIR Top-k, RadiK) are optimized for a small number of very large vectors (≈2^20 elements, batch ≤100), while row-wise top-k in GNN training requires simultaneously processing millions of short vectors (≤1024 elements). PyTorch's row-wise top-k relies on RadixSelect, which is overly complex per vector and produces unnecessarily sorted results, making it suboptimal when minimalistic per-vector selection is the bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The method takes an N×M matrix (N up to 2^20 rows, M typically 256–768 columns) and an integer k (16–128), and outputs the top-k element values and their column indices for each row. No sorting of the selected or remaining elements is required. The critical constraint is that M is small while N is extremely large, so each vector must be processed with very limited GPU resources (one warp per vector, shared memory bounded by 8192 elements).

## Method

RTop-K assigns one warp per vector and performs a binary search over a threshold to identify the top-k elements in three stages: (1) load the vector into shared memory, (2) iteratively compute min/max and a midpoint threshold, count elements ≥ threshold via tree-reduction with shuffle primitives, and update the search interval until the count matches k or a max_iter cap is reached, and (3) select elements using ballot and popcnt prefix-sum to write exactly k values and indices to global memory. An early-stopping variant (Algorithm 2) forces the loop to exit after max_iter iterations and uses the final min as a single-pass selection threshold, trading a small, controllable approximation error (≤5% relative error for max_iter ≥ 5) for substantial speed gains. Theoretical analysis (Appendix A) derives the expected iteration count as log₂(2M√(ln M/π)) − Φ⁻¹(1−k/M)²/(2 ln 2), confirming that most vectors converge in 8–10 iterations. This design achieves 7.29×–11.49× average kernel speedup over PyTorch and 11.97%–33.29% end-to-end training speedup on MaxK-GNN models.

## Results

RTop-K achieves an average speedup of 11.49× over PyTorch's torch.topk with early stopping (max_iter=2) and 7.29× without early stopping, across vector sizes M=256–768 and k=16–128. When integrated into MaxK-GNN training, RTop-K delivers overall training speed-ups of 11.97%–12.21% on Reddit, 22.00%–22.74% on Ogbn-products, 31.21%–32.42% on Yelp, and 32.48%–33.29% on Flickr, while maintaining testing accuracy within ~1% of the exact top-k baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is PyTorch's torch.topk (RadixSelect-based), which is the state-of-the-art row-wise top-k GPU implementation supporting large batch sizes. RTop-K wins across every tested configuration (N=2¹⁴–2²⁰, M=256–768, k=16–128), with the speedup most sensitive to vector size M: 8.88×–13.07× at M=256, 7.27×–11.66× at M=512, and 5.72×–9.73× at M=768. The paper notes that PyTorch's RadixSelect is overly complex for limited-size vectors and performs unnecessary sorting of the k selected elements, both of which RTop-K avoids. In the end-to-end MaxK-GNN training evaluation, RTop-K with early stopping (max_iter=5–8) matches or slightly exceeds the testing accuracy of the exact top-k baseline on all model–dataset combinations except GIN on Flickr, where accuracy drops by less than 0.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of GPU top-k work (Dr. Top-k, AIR Top-k, GridSelect, RadiK), which targets large vectors (~2^20 elements) with small batch sizes (≤100), by addressing the opposite regime: millions of short vectors (M ≤ 1024) processed simultaneously. It also builds on MaxK-GNN (Peng et al., 2024), which introduced row-wise top-k as a nonlinearity and acceleration mechanism in GNN training, identifying the top-k operation as a critical bottleneck. Rather than adapting existing algorithms like RadixSelect or QuickSelect, the authors propose a simpler binary-search-based selection that exploits warp-level primitives (shuffle, ballot, popcnt) and the tolerance of neural networks to approximate selection via early stopping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Row-wise top-k accounts for 11–27% of MaxK-GNN training time, so the 7–11× kernel speedup translates into meaningful end-to-end training acceleration (12–33%) across multiple GNN architectures and graph datasets. The early stopping analysis is practically important: it shows that 4–5 iterations of binary search suffice to keep relative error below 5% and hit rate above 80%, with negligible impact on model accuracy, enabling a tunable speed-accuracy tradeoff. Limitations include testing on a single GPU (A6000), a shared-memory constraint that caps M at 8192, and evaluation only within the MaxK-GNN framework; whether the approach generalizes to other GPU architectures, larger hidden dimensions, or non-GNN workloads remains open.
