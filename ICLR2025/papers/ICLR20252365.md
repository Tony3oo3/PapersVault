## Problem

Fusing heterogeneous open-source LLMs with varying architectures and sizes into a single target model is hindered by the need for vocabulary alignment and distribution matrix merging in existing explicit fusion methods, which are complex and introduce noise. While implicit model fusion via preference optimization avoids these alignment steps, directly applying DPO to preference pairs drawn from heterogeneous source and target LLMs suffers from significant distributional deviations that lead to sub-optimal performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252365/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes ~64K text prompts from UltraFeedback, from which N=5 responses are sampled per source LLM (10 models, 9B–236B parameters) and per target LLM using top-p sampling (p=0.95, T=0.8). An external reward model (ArmoRM-Llama-3-8B) scores all responses to construct preference quadruples (x, y_ws, y_wt, y_l). The output is a fine-tuned 8B-parameter target LLM (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) that generates text responses, evaluated on instruction-following benchmarks (AlpacaEval-2, Arena-Hard, MT-Bench) and general knowledge tasks.

## Method

WRPO extends DPO by reformulating the Bradley-Terry model over a preference quadruple (x, y_ws, y_wt, y_l), where y_ws is the highest-reward response from source LLMs and y_wt/y_l are the best/worst responses from the target LLM. A fusion coefficient α dynamically weights the internal reward: r(x, y_w) = α·r(x, y_ws) + (1−α)·r(x, y_wt), producing a loss that interpolates between on-policy DPO (α=0) and hybrid-policy DPO (α=1). Training proceeds in two stages: first, SFT on y_ws using one-third of the data; second, preference optimization on the remaining two-thirds with α linearly increasing from 0 to 0.1, progressively shifting reliance from the target's own distribution toward the source LLMs' distribution. Gradient analysis shows α acts as a constraint on how aggressively the policy absorbs gradient information from off-policy source responses, and the weighted mechanism balances conservative on-policy margins (~0.2) with aggressive hybrid-policy margins (>1.0) to achieve stable intermediate margins (~0.5).

## Results

With Llama-3-8B-Instruct as the target model, Target-SFT-WRPO achieves a length-controlled win rate of 55.9% on AlpacaEval-2 (surpassing all 10 source LLMs, including Mistral-Large-123B at 54.3% and Gemma-2-27B at 55.5%), a win rate of 46.2% against GPT-4-0314 on Arena-Hard, and an overall score of 7.63 on MT-Bench. On the Huggingface Open LLM Leaderboard, Target-SFT-WRPO attains an average of 69.12 across six tasks, exceeding the original Target model (68.01) while preserving general knowledge and mathematical reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WRPO decisively outperforms explicit model fusion methods: Target-SFT-WRPO beats FuseLLM by 19.9 points (55.9 vs 36.0) and FuseChat by 17.8 points (55.9 vs 38.1) on AlpacaEval-2 LC, and by 14.1 and 13.5 points on Arena-Hard, respectively. Against preference optimization baselines, WRPO improves over Target-DPO by 7.7 points on AlpacaEval-2 LC and 11.0 points on Arena-Hard, over Target-SimPO by 2.2 and 9.7 points, and over Target-IPO by 9.1 and 9.6 points. Among collective LLMs, WRPO outperforms PackLLM-Top1-PPL (49.1 LC) and LLM-Blender-Top1 (46.2 LC) on AlpacaEval-2, but falls short of MoA (61.3 LC, 83.1 Arena-Hard), which requires 106× more parameters at inference. On MT-Bench, WRPO achieves the best 8B score (7.63), narrowly exceeding Target-IPO (7.54) and Target-SFT-DPO (7.61), though all 8B methods trail the larger source models (8.13–8.57). The medium variant (5 source LLMs) still beats FuseLLM and FuseChat by 17.5 and 15.4 points on AlpacaEval-2 LC, confirming that even a subset of sources yields substantial gains over explicit fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes LLM model fusion as a preference optimization problem, departing from the explicit model fusion (EMF) paradigm of FuseLLM, FuseChat, and ProFuser, which require vocabulary alignment and distribution matrix merging across heterogeneous architectures. It builds directly on DPO's Bradley-Terry reformulation and extends it with a weighted-reward mechanism that blends on-policy (target model) and hybrid-policy (source model) preference signals, drawing conceptual inspiration from WPO's likelihood-weighted off-policy pairs. The progressive adaptation strategy—linearly increasing the fusion coefficient α from 0 to a target value—serves as the key mechanism to mitigate the distribution shift that makes naive DPO on cross-model preference data sub-optimal, a problem documented in prior work on DPO's sensitivity to off-policy data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WRPO offers a substantially simpler pipeline for fusing heterogeneous LLMs compared to EMF methods, eliminating vocabulary alignment and distribution merging while achieving competitive or superior results with only ~16% additional training time over DPO. The finding that an 8B target model can surpass all source models (up to 236B) on AlpacaEval-2 suggests that preference-optimization-based fusion may be a more scalable path to capability transfer than parameter-level merging. However, the method currently relies on a single reward model (ArmoRM), uses only the top-scoring source response per prompt, and shows measurable degradation on knowledge-intensive tasks (MMLU, GSM8K), leaving open questions about broader domain generalization, theoretical grounding of the fusion dynamics, and whether incorporating multiple source responses could further improve results.
