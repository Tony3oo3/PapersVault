## Problem

Existing model-level GNN explainers for molecular graphs (XGNN, GNNInterpreter) produce chemically invalid explanations because they generate structures atom-by-atom or align with average graph embeddings, failing to capture critical substructures like rings. This undermines the interpretability and practical utility of the explanations for molecular tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251716/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained GNN (a 3-layer GCN feature extractor and 2-layer MLP classifier) and a molecular dataset as input. It outputs, for each class, a set of class-specific motifs and a trained VAE-based generator that samples chemically valid molecular explanation graphs. The generator operates on motif junction trees rather than raw atoms, and the graph decoder selects the assembled molecule that maximizes the target GNN's predicted probability for the target class.

## Method

MAGE is a three-stage pipeline: (1) motif extraction via bridge-bond decomposition (or other methods like RECAP, BRICS, or WordPiece tokenization); (2) attention-based class-wise motif identification, where a single-layer attention operator learns molecule-motif relation scores by minimizing cross-entropy between the target classifier's predictions on the original molecule encoding and a motif-aggregated encoding, then class-motif scores are computed by multiplying the molecule-motif score matrix with the molecule-class probability matrix; (3) a VAE-based motif graph generator that decomposes each molecule into a junction tree of motifs, encodes the tree with a GNN, decodes it top-down with a tree decoder, and assembles the final molecule via a graph decoder that ranks motif combinations by the target GNN's class probability. Training combines a reconstruction loss (tree structure) with a property loss (embedding alignment and classification accuracy), and the motif-based assembly inherently guarantees chemical validity since each motif is a valid substructure.

## Results

MAGE achieves 100% chemical validity across all six molecular datasets (Mutagenicity, PTC_MR, PTC_MM, PTC_FM, AIDS, NCI-H23) for both classes, compared to XGNN (0.21–0.92) and GNNInterpreter (0.05–0.62). On the Average Probability metric, MAGE outperforms both baselines in 10 out of 12 class-level experiments, with the largest gap on AIDS (0.9893 vs. 0.9618 for XGNN and 0.7286 for GNNInterpreter). MAGE also substantially outperforms instance-level explainers GNNExplainer (0.8339/0.8250) and GEM (0.8739/0.8887) on Mutagenicity, reaching 0.9977/0.9941.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against XGNN (atom-by-atom RL generation), MAGE achieves perfect 1.00 validity on all datasets versus XGNN's 0.21–0.92, and wins on Average Probability in 10 of 12 class-level runs; XGNN also fails entirely (OOM) on NCI-H23. Against GNNInterpreter (embedding-alignment generation), MAGE's validity advantage is even more pronounced (1.00 vs. 0.05–0.62), and it wins on Average Probability in 10 of 12 runs, with the largest margin on AIDS (0.9893 vs. 0.7286). A variant without the attention-based motif identification step (using only per-motif scoring) is consistently outperformed by MAGE, confirming the contribution of the attention module. In a cross-paradigm comparison on Mutagenicity, MAGE (0.9977/0.9941) substantially surpasses the instance-level explainers GNNExplainer (0.8339/0.8250) and GEM (0.8739/0.8887), demonstrating the advantage of model-level over instance-level explanation for capturing global class patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGE extends the generation-based model-level explanation paradigm established by XGNN (atom-by-atom RL generation) and GNNInterpreter (probabilistic generative model aligned to average embeddings) by replacing atomic-level generation with motif-level assembly. The graph generation component directly adapts the junction tree VAE framework (Jin et al., 2018) and hierarchical motif-based generation (Jin et al., 2020), repurposing them from molecular design to explanation generation. The key conceptual departure is the attention-based class-wise motif identification step, which bridges the gap between a trained GNN's decision behavior and the structural motifs that drive it, rather than relying on hand-crafted validity constraints or embedding similarity heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The guaranteed 100% chemical validity across all six datasets addresses a fundamental practical limitation of prior model-level explainers for molecular GNNs, making the generated explanations immediately usable for downstream chemical analysis. The dramatic sampling speed advantage (milliseconds vs. tens of seconds per explanation) also makes large-scale explanation generation feasible. However, the evaluation is limited to binary classification with a single GCN architecture on relatively small datasets (336–40K molecules), leaving open questions about scalability to multi-class settings, deeper GNN variants (GAT, GIN), and larger or 3D molecular representations. The 100% novelty score, while reassuring, is somewhat expected for a VAE-based generator and does not strongly differentiate from other generative approaches.
