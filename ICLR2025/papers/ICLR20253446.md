## Problem

Existing multimodal retrieval-augmented generation benchmarks are text-centric, evaluating whether LVLMs can retrieve and utilize external textual knowledge for visual question answering. However, in many real-world scenarios—such as identifying a car from an unusual angle or recognizing a fruit after oxidation—retrieving visual information is more beneficial or more accessible than textual data, yet no systematic benchmark exists to evaluate LVLMs' ability to leverage retrieved visual knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253446/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides a query tuple (one query image plus a short textual question, max 20 words) and a set of 5 retrieved or ground-truth images as augmented knowledge. The LVLM must output a single multiple-choice letter (A–D) from 4 options. The full benchmark contains 16,130 unique images (average 1076×851 px) and 1,353 human-annotated MCQA questions across 9 scenarios, with 9,673 human-selected ground-truth images forming the knowledge corpus from which retrievers draw.

## Method

MRAG-BENCH is structured around two cognitive aspects—perspective (angle, partial, scope, occlusion) and transformative (temporal, deformation, incomplete, biological)—plus a geographic 'others' category, each targeting scenarios where visual knowledge outperforms textual knowledge. Perspective data is sourced from ImageNet, Flowers102, and StanfordCars (with 50% random cropping for partial views), while transformative data is manually scraped via Bing Image Search using predefined object-condition keywords. The evaluation protocol feeds a query image, question, and top-5 retrieved images (from CLIP or other retrievers) to the LVLM, measuring accuracy under three conditions: no RAG, noisy retrieved RAG, and ground-truth RAG. The paper evaluates 14 LVLMs (10 open-source, 4 proprietary) and additionally compares image vs. text knowledge augmentation to confirm the benchmark's vision-centric nature, finding that all models benefit more from image knowledge and that proprietary models uniquely handle noisy retrieved examples without performance degradation.

## Results

On MRAG-BENCH (1,353 MCQA questions, 9 scenarios), GPT-4o achieves the highest overall accuracy at 68.68% without RAG and 74.50% with ground-truth image RAG (a 5.82% improvement). The best open-source model, LLaVA-OneVision, reaches 53.29% and 58.98% under the same settings, trailing GPT-4o by 15.39% and 15.52% respectively. Humans achieve 38.47% without RAG but jump to 71.63% with GT RAG (33.16% improvement), far exceeding any model's gain. All 14 evaluated LVLMs show greater improvement from image RAG than from text RAG, confirming the benchmark's vision-centric design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads all proprietary models, outperforming Gemini Pro (61.71%) by 6.97% and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (59.87%) by 8.81% without RAG; with GT RAG, Claude 3.5 Sonnet narrows the gap to 71.10% vs. GPT-4o's 74.50%. Among open-source models, LLaVA-OneVision (53.29%) surpasses mPLUG-Owl3-7B (49.74%), Pixtral-12B (47.97%), and LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave-7B (43.46%), but all open-source models trail GPT-4o by 15+ points. A critical finding is that nearly all open-source models *degrade* with noisy retrieved RAG (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave drops from 43.46% to 40.35%, mPLUG-Owl3-7B from 49.74% to 41.83%), while proprietary models maintain or improve (GPT-4o: 68.68%→68.96%, Gemini Pro: 61.71%→65.93%), indicating proprietary models can better distinguish relevant from irrelevant retrieved images. In the text-vs-image RAG comparison (Table 4), both LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave and GPT-4-Turbo benefit more from image knowledge than text knowledge (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave gains 9.53% with GT image RAG vs. −2.37% with GT text RAG). GPT-4o's 5.82% improvement with GT RAG is dramatically smaller than the 33.16% human improvement, highlighting a significant gap in models' ability to leverage visually augmented knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of multimodal RAG benchmarks (OK-VQA, A-OKVQA, WebQA, InfoSeek, Encyclopedia VQA) that all treat external textual knowledge as the retrieval target. It repurposes the RAG-VQA paradigm by shifting the knowledge modality to images, systematically identifying 9 scenarios (perspective and transformative) where visual retrieval is more informative than textual retrieval. The evaluation leverages the multi-image input capability of recent LVLMs (LLaVA-Next-Interleave, Mantis, LLaVA-OneVision) that were trained on interleaved image-text corpora, but no prior benchmark specifically probes whether these models can effectively ground their reasoning in retrieved visual examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a striking and underappreciated limitation: even GPT-4o achieves only a 5.82% improvement with ground-truth visual knowledge versus 33.16% for humans, suggesting current LVLMs fundamentally struggle to integrate retrieved visual evidence into their reasoning. The finding that open-source models degrade with noisy retrieved images while proprietary models improve highlights a critical capability gap that existing text-centric RAG benchmarks cannot surface. Open questions include whether position bias in retrieved image ordering (analogous to text RAG) can be mitigated, how many visual examples are adaptively needed per question, and whether training on interleaved image-text data is sufficient to develop robust visual RAG skills.
