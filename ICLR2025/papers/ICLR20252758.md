## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of two-layer neural networks with feature learning (one gradient descent step) rely on isotropic Gaussian or spiked covariance inputs, which fail to capture the mixture structure and low intrinsic dimensionality of real-world data. This paper addresses that gap by characterizing training and generalization performance under Gaussian mixture data with finite-rank plus identity covariance in the asymptotically proportional limit.

## Input / Output

The model consumes input vectors x drawn from a C-component Gaussian mixture with finite-rank plus identity covariance (Σ_c = I_n + Σ θ_{c,i} γ_{c,i}γ_{c,i}^T), labels y generated by a single-index target function σ_*(ξ^T x, c), and a nonlinear activation σ. It produces closed-form characterizations of training and generalization errors, along with an equivalent finite-degree Hermite polynomial model whose degree l is determined by the strength parameter β = log(η‖Σ‖)/log(n). The analysis operates in the proportional asymptotic regime where n, m, and k all diverge with finite ratios, and the joint scaling η‖Σ‖ ≍ n^β with β ∈ [0,1] governs the transition between linear and nonlinear equivalent models.

## Method

The core technique proceeds in three stages: (1) a spike+bulk decomposition of the gradient G = uv^T + Δ, where the rank-one spike captures the dominant feature-learning signal and the bulk vanishes as k^{-t}; (2) a structure+bulk decomposition of F̂x that separates the component along the data's low-dimensional subspace (spiked directions and input-label alignment) from the isotropic bulk noise; and (3) a conditional Gaussian equivalence (Theorem 3) showing that σ(F̂x) can be replaced by a Gaussian feature map conditioned on the mixture component c and the alignment vector κ_c, followed by a polynomial approximation (Theorem 4) proving that a finite-degree Hermite polynomial activation σ̂_l(x) = Σ_{j=0}^{l-1} (h_j/j!)H_j(x/b) + h_l^* z is performance-equivalent to the original nonlinear activation. The key insight is that the required polynomial degree l is determined by β: for (l-2)/(l-1) < β < (l-1)/l, the structure term a_{i|κ_c} vanishes fast enough that higher-order Hermite coefficients become negligible, linking the data spread and learning rate directly to the effective complexity of the learned representation.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 4) establishes that a two-layer NN after one gradient step under Gaussian mixture data with spiked covariance is equivalent in training and generalization error to a finite-degree Hermite polynomial model, where the degree l is determined by the strength parameter β via (l−2)/(l−1) < β < (l−1)/l. Simulations with n=m=1000, C=2 mixture components, and d₁=d₂=1 confirm that the NN and Hermite model generalization errors match closely across all k/m ratios (Figure 1a). Key empirical findings: (i) higher data spread (lower α) outperforms higher learning rate for generalization (Figure 1b); (ii) larger β reduces generalization error (Figure 1c); (iii) on Fashion-MNIST binary classification via a conditional GAN (m=500, λ=1e-4, l=5), the degree-5 Hermite model achieves nearly identical generalization error to the full NN (Figure 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional baseline comparisons; the central 'comparison' is between the original nonlinear NN and its equivalent Hermite polynomial model, which the paper proves are asymptotically equivalent (Theorems 3 and 4) and validates numerically. Across all tested configurations (varying k/m, α, β, mixture ratios, alignment, and covariance rank), the NN and Hermite model generalization errors match within Monte Carlo noise (20 runs). Among activation functions, ReLU and Sigmoid achieve near-zero error on imbalanced classification tasks, while tanh is insensitive to mixture ratio due to its symmetry with zero-mean inputs. The paper also implicitly compares against prior isotropic-Gaussian analyses (Ba et al. 2022; Moniri et al. 2024; Dandi et al. 2023a) by showing that the Gaussian mixture + spiked covariance setting introduces qualitatively new dependencies on data spread and mixture structure that are absent in the isotropic case. On Fashion-MNIST (cGAN-generated), the equivalence holds with l=5, suggesting the theoretical framework extends to realistic high-dimensional data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-gradient-step feature learning analysis of Ba et al. (2022), Moniri et al. (2024), and Dandi et al. (2023a) from isotropic or spiked Gaussian inputs to Gaussian mixture data with finite-rank-plus-identity covariances. It builds on the Gaussian universality framework of Hu & Lu (2023) and Dandi et al. (2023b) for mixtures, but introduces a novel conditional equivalence (Theorem 3) that conditions on both the mixture component index and the alignment with the spiked subspace — a conditioning structure absent from prior results. The Hermite polynomial equivalence (Theorem 4) generalizes Moniri et al. (2024) from isotropic to mixture+spiked data, with the polynomial degree governed by a joint strength parameter β linking learning rate and data spread.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a tractable finite-degree polynomial surrogate (the 'Hermite model') for analyzing two-layer NNs under realistic structured mixture data, enabling precise asymptotic predictions of generalization error without simulating the full nonlinear network. The finding that data spread (structure in the covariance) benefits generalization more than a high learning rate (the α effect) offers a concrete, counterintuitive design principle. Key limitations include the restriction to a single gradient step, single-index target functions, zero-mean mixture components, and β ∈ [0,1]; extending to β > 1, multi-index targets, and deeper architectures are natural open directions. The cGAN-on-Fashion-MNIST experiment is a promising but preliminary bridge to real data.
