## Problem

Existing multi-person 3D pose tracking systems rely on a two-stage detect-and-associate paradigm that fails during occlusions in online settings, since missed detections cannot be recovered without future context. No prior method combines state-of-the-art 3D pose accuracy with robust, temporally coherent multi-person tracking in a streaming fashion, and no dataset provides ground-truth 3D poses for groups of people in cluttered real-world scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252981/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a stream of monocular RGB frames (padded and resized to 512×512, no per-person cropping) along with a camera intrinsics matrix K, and produces per-person 3D SMPL parameters (translation γ∈ℝ³, joint angles θ∈ℝ⁷², shape β∈ℝ¹⁰) in the camera coordinate frame at every timestep. Predictions are organized as persistent identity tracks with complete trajectories; the model operates strictly online, using only past and present frames, and does not perform re-identification after long occlusions.

## Method

CoMotion follows a tracking-by-attention paradigm with two parallel modules operating on shared ConvNeXtV2 image features: a single-shot detection head (feature pyramid with 4 candidate SMPL poses per spatial location, pruned by OKS-based NMS) proposes new tracks, while a pose update module encodes all existing tracks into tokens, cross-attends to full-image features, and decodes refined poses plus a GRU-maintained hidden state per track. Track management uses OKS-based heuristics (instantiation when max OKS < 0.2, deletion when exponential moving average OKS < 0.15, and duplicate-track removal when OKS > 0.6 persists for 20+ frames). Training follows a three-stage curriculum—single-image pretraining on pseudo-labeled datasets (InstaVariety, COCO, MPII relabeled with NLF), 8-frame video fine-tuning on synthetic (BEDLAM, WHAC-A-MOLE) and pseudo-labeled real video (PoseTrack, DanceTrack), then 96-frame sequence fine-tuning—frontloading visual feature learning before the slower video stages.

## Results

On PoseTrack21, CoMotion achieves MOTA 67.6 and IDF1 77.9, improving MOTA by 14% and IDF1 by 12% over the prior state of the art. After fixing a bug in the official evaluation code (incorrect handling of 'ignore' regions), CoMotion reaches MOTA 71.4 and IDF1 79.5. CoMotion is approximately 12× faster than 4D Humans (176 ms vs. 2163 ms per frame) and 1.4× faster than PARE (176 ms vs. 258 ms), while also producing more temporally coherent 3D poses with fewer ID switches and jitter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMotion outperforms all tracking baselines on PoseTrack21 by a wide margin: MOTA 67.6 vs. Tracktor++ (59.5), FairMOT (56.3), CorrTrack+ReID (52.0), and TRMOT (47.2). The most direct comparison is against 4D Humans (Goel et al., 2023), the only other system combining 3D pose estimation with multi-person tracking; CoMotion improves MOTA from 56.7 to 71.4 and IDF1 from 70.9 to 79.5 under the corrected evaluation, while also reducing ID switches from 349 to 232 (Table 2). On runtime, CoMotion is 12× faster than 4D Humans (176 ms vs. 2163 ms per frame) and 1.4× faster than PARE (176 ms vs. 258 ms), making it the only method in the comparison that is both more accurate and substantially faster. Qualitatively, CoMotion produces more temporally stable poses with fewer abrupt frame-to-frame jumps compared to 4D Humans, which makes independent per-frame predictions and can exhibit high-frequency pose changes on challenging or truncated poses.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMotion departs from the dominant detect-and-associate paradigm (used by 4D Humans, PHALP, and earlier systems) by adopting the tracking-by-attention framework from TrackFormer, but instantiating it for the substantially harder problem of 3D SMPL pose tracking rather than 2D bounding boxes. It also extends single-person video pose methods (VIBE, PMCE, RoHM) that rely on external trackers and process one subject at a time into a system that performs its own multi-person tracking jointly. The single-shot detection architecture draws on SSD-style multi-scale decoding, while the recurrent GRU-based hidden state update follows the video pose estimation lineage, but is applied here to an unordered set of tracks that must attend to the full image simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoMotion is the first system to deliver online, streaming multi-person 3D pose tracking from monocular video at 12× the speed of the prior state of the art (4D Humans) while simultaneously improving tracking accuracy, making it practically viable for real-time AR/VR and human-centric applications. Key open questions remain: tracks can collapse into a single identity, the model lacks a notion of physicality to ground motion, it does not model camera motion or absolute scale (affecting children and zoomed scenes), and there is a train-test discrepancy between short fixed-length training clips and unbounded online inference. The discovery and fix of a bug in the PoseTrack21 evaluation code also has broader implications for the field's benchmarking reliability.
