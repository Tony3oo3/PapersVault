## Problem

Traditional SAE evaluation relies on MSE and L0 sparsity as proxies for monosemantic feature quality, but improving the MSE-L0 Pareto frontier does not guarantee that SAEs actually decompose polysemantic activations into interpretable, meaning-specific features. No existing metric directly assesses whether SAE features can distinguish different meanings of the same word across contexts, leaving a gap between the stated goal of SAEs (monosemanticity) and how they are actually evaluated.

## Input / Output

The evaluation takes two context sentences sharing a target word (formatted as '{context}. {target word} meas') from the WiC dataset, extracts the LLM activation for the target word token at a specified layer, and passes it through a trained SAE. The output is a binary classification per sample pair—whether the maximum-activated SAE feature is the same or different across the two contexts—aggregated into a confusion matrix yielding accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and specificity. The dataset is constrained to target words that tokenize as a single token in GPT-2 small, with 1112 balanced samples (556 poly-contexts, 556 mono-contexts).

## Method

PS-Eval constructs a confusion matrix by comparing the maximum-activated SAE feature between two contexts of the same target word: same feature in mono-context = TP, different feature in poly-context = TN, and the cross-cases are FP/FN. The paper then systematically varies SAE hyperparameters (expand ratio 8–128, activation functions ReLU/TopK/JumpReLU, layer depth 1–12, and transformer component: residual/MLP/attention) to measure how each affects the five metrics. A logit lens analysis validates that the maximum-activated SAE feature genuinely encodes the word's context-specific meaning by projecting it through the unembedding matrix and inspecting top logits. Key findings include: ReLU outperforms JumpReLU and TopK on F1 (contradicting MSE-L0 Pareto improvements), specificity increases with layer depth (inverse to MSE/L0 trends), and the attention component achieves the highest specificity despite lower F1, indicating it plays a distinct role in disambiguating polysemy.

## Results

On PS-Eval (1,112 polysemous/monosemous word pairs from WiC), the default ReLU SAE (expand ratio 32, layer 4, GPT-2 small) achieves an F1 score of 0.7623, Precision of 0.6593, and Recall of 0.94. Increasing the expand ratio from 8 to 64 improves accuracy from 0.6005 to 0.6669 (layer 4), with saturation around ratio 64. Across open SAE models, GPT-2 small achieves the highest F1 (0.73) and Specificity (0.38), while Pythia 70M and Gemma2-2B score lower (F1 ≈ 0.59–0.60, Specificity ≈ 0.30–0.32), indicating that SAE hyperparameters require per-model tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the default ReLU SAE against two MSE-L0 Pareto-optimized alternatives: TopK (Gao et al., 2024) and JumpReLU (Rajamanoharan et al., 2024). On PS-Eval F1 score (layer 4), ReLU achieves 0.7623, substantially outperforming JumpReLU-STE (0.6667 at jump=0.001) and TopK (0.6550 at k=384, 0.6490 at k=96, 0.6166 at k=192). The performance ranking is consistently ReLU > JumpReLU > TopK across both layer 4 and layer 6, and across all three metrics (F1, Precision, Recall). This is a key negative result: activation functions explicitly designed to improve the MSE-L0 trade-off do not improve — and in fact degrade — the SAE's ability to extract monosemantic features that distinguish polysemous word meanings. The paper also shows that deeper layers (≥6) achieve higher Specificity despite worse MSE and L0, further demonstrating that traditional Pareto metrics are misaligned with semantic interpretability goals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAE evaluation literature (Sharkey et al. 2022; Bricken et al. 2023; Templeton et al. 2024; Gao et al. 2024), which has predominantly focused on the MSE–L₀ Pareto frontier or task-specific probes (IOI circuits, RAVEL), by introducing a semantics-grounded evaluation (PS-Eval) built on the WiC dataset (Pilehvar & Camacho-Collados, 2019). It departs from prior work by directly testing whether SAE features are monosemantic—i.e., whether they activate differently for different meanings of the same token—rather than proxying interpretability through reconstruction fidelity. The logit lens technique (nostalgebraist, 2020) is repurposed as a qualitative validation tool to confirm that the max-activated SAE feature genuinely encodes a specific word sense.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ReLU outperforms TopK and JumpReLU on monosemanticity metrics challenges the field's working assumption that improving the MSE–L₀ Pareto frontier automatically yields more interpretable features, suggesting that SAE design objectives may need to be rethought beyond reconstruction quality. Limitations include reliance on a single max-activation criterion (acknowledged by the authors), a small 1,112-sample evaluation set, and experiments primarily on GPT-2 small, leaving open whether the conclusions generalize to larger or differently-architected models. The observation that specificity increases with depth while MSE/L₀ also increase highlights an inverse relationship that current training objectives do not capture, pointing toward future work on semantics-aware SAE training losses.
