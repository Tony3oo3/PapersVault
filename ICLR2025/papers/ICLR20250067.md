## Problem

Rolling Horizon Optimization (RHO) decomposes long-horizon combinatorial optimization problems into overlapping subproblems, but the temporal overlap introduces redundant re-optimization of variables that rarely change between consecutive iterations. No prior work has exploited machine learning to identify and fix such redundant variables, leaving a gap in accelerating RHO for time-structured COPs like the Flexible Job-Shop Scheduling Problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250067/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each RHO iteration, the neural network receives a structured feature vector per operation (duration statistics, release time, previous assignment, previous end time, delay) and per machine (earliest start time, aggregate statistics of previously assigned overlapping operations), along with RHO parameters (window H, step S, time limit T). It outputs a binary probability per overlapping operation indicating whether to fix its machine assignment, which constrains the CP-SAT subproblem. The final output is a complete FJSP solution (machine assignment and start time for every operation) assembled incrementally across |O|/S RHO iterations.

## Method

L-RHO introduces a Look-Ahead Oracle that solves each unrestricted subproblem Q times to label overlapping operations whose machine assignments remain unchanged between consecutive iterations, then trains a lightweight MLP-based network to imitate this oracle. The architecture embeds operations and machines via separate MLPs, concatenates each overlapping operation's hidden state with its previously assigned machine's embedding (local) and a mean-pooled global feature, and outputs a per-operation fix probability via a final MLP layer. During inference, operations predicted above 0.5 are fixed in the restricted subproblem, which is then solved by CP-SAT under a time limit, reducing solve time by up to 54% while improving solution quality. Training uses weighted binary cross-entropy with a tunable positive-label weight to balance false positives (fixing operations that should change, harming objective) against false negatives (failing to fix stable operations, wasting solve time). A theoretical analysis under a linear-decay assumption provides closed-form FP/FN rates for heuristic baselines (Random, First) and identifies conditions under which the learned method is provably advantageous.

## Results

On the canonical offline FJSP with makespan objective (up to 2000 operations), L-RHO achieves the best makespan across all problem sizes (e.g., 4982±132 at 2000 ops) while also being the fastest competitive method (473±52s), outperforming Default RHO (5207±114 makespan, 862±42s) by ~54% in solve time and ~4% in makespan. Under the start delay objective, L-RHO delivers 35–53% time improvement and 13–21% objective improvement over Default RHO, while all heuristic RHO variants (First, Random, Warm Start) suffer objective degradation of up to −275%. L-RHO also generalizes zero-shot from 60 to 100 operations per job, maintaining the best makespan (4982 vs. 5062 for DRL-20K Sample 500).

## Baselines & Comparisons

L-RHO outperforms all six categories of baselines on both solve time and objective. Against traditional solvers without decomposition (CP-SAT, GA), L-RHO is 14× faster than CP-SAT at 2000 ops (473s vs. 1800s) while achieving a 4.7× better makespan (4982 vs. 39585). Against the best DRL baseline (DRL-20K with 500 samples), L-RHO is 3.7× faster (473s vs. 1738s) and achieves a slightly better makespan (4982 vs. 5062) at 2000 ops, while requiring far fewer training instances (450 vs. 20,000). Against decomposition-based methods (ARD-LNS Time/Machine, Oracle-LNS), L-RHO is 2–4× faster and achieves substantially better makespans, confirming that temporal RHO decomposition is superior to spatial LNS for long-horizon FJSP. Among RHO variants, L-RHO dominates Default RHO (54% faster, better makespan) and Warm Start RHO (45% faster, better makespan), while heuristic fixing strategies (First, Random) improve solve time but degrade the objective by up to 275% under the start delay objective, whereas L-RHO simultaneously improves both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Rolling Horizon Optimization (RHO) from Model Predictive Control and learning-guided decomposition for combinatorial optimization. RHO has been adapted for long-horizon COPs (scheduling, lot-sizing, supply chain) but always solves unrestricted subproblems at each iteration, incurring redundant computation on overlapping variables. In control and robotics, hand-crafted warm-starts and learned constraint prediction reduce such redundancy, but no prior work applies learning to identify which overlapping decision variables in a COP subproblem can be safely fixed. The paper also departs from spatial/structural decomposition methods (ARD-LNS, learned subproblem selection for routing) by introducing a purely temporal, assignment-based restriction that is orthogonal to those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-horizon FJSP instances (600–2000 operations) are far beyond the scale handled by existing DRL solvers, and the 54% speedup with simultaneous quality improvement makes RHO practically viable for industrial scheduling. The theoretical analysis linking FP/FN rates to when learning outperforms heuristics gives practitioners a deployment criterion, and the robustness under observation noise and machine breakdowns points toward realistic online deployment. Key limitations include testing on a single problem class (FJSP), reliance on a CP-SAT subsolver, and the observation that L-RHO alone may not reach SOTA on other long-horizon COPs without integration with additional decomposition strategies.
