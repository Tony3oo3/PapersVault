## Problem

Large-scale pretrained text-to-video models offer strong text-conditioned generalization but lack environment-specific dynamics, while in-domain video models capture environment specifics but cannot generalize to novel tasks via natural language. The paper investigates how to best integrate limited in-domain information into large-scale pretrained video models to achieve novel text-conditioned generalization for robotic tasks, comparing adaptation techniques with varying data and compute requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251100/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The adapted video model takes a current RGB observation and a natural language task prompt, producing either a sequence of 8 future frames (video plan) for visual planning or text-conditioned rewards computed by noise-corrupting achieved frames and evaluating one-step denoising predictions for policy supervision. Adaptation data ranges from 20 static images (subject customization) to 25 short video demonstrations (other methods), with the ~1.5B-parameter AnimateDiff backbone kept frozen in probabilistic adaptation while only a ~109M-parameter in-domain model is trained.

## Method

The paper proposes Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation, which inverts the score composition direction from prior work: the large pretrained model (AnimateDiff) anchors the generation process via its unconditional score, while a small in-domain model provides targeted domain-specific guidance through its text-conditioned score, formalized as ε̃_inv = ε_pretrained(τ_t,t) + α(ε_pretrained(τ_t,t|text) + γε_θ(τ_t,t|text) − ε_pretrained(τ_t,t)). This is evaluated alongside Direct Finetuning (updating only the motion module on in-domain video) and Subject Customization (DreamBooth on static images with a rare token identifier), across both visual planning (via inverse dynamics) and policy supervision (via Video-TADPoLe rewards with TDMPC). The key insight is that inverting the adaptation direction places more weight on the pretrained model's textual priors, making the approach more robust to novel text prompts and suboptimal demonstration data. Empirically, inverse probabilistic adaptation achieves the highest overall success rate on MetaWorld (68.3% via policy supervision) and remains robust when only suboptimal (30% expert) demonstrations are available.

## Results

On MetaWorld policy supervision (the primary benchmark), Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation achieves 68.3% overall success rate across 9 manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming Vanilla AnimateDiff (39.8%), Direct Finetuning (31.0%), Subject Customization (44.0%), and standard Probabilistic Adaptation (40.2%). On visual planning over the same tasks, Probabilistic Adaptation leads at 30.4% overall, with Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation at 25.9%, both surpassing Vanilla AnimateDiff (22.2%). On continuous control (DeepMind Control Suite), Subject Customization achieves the highest overall episode return (146.3) despite using only static images, while Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation (90.7) outperforms the in-domain-only model (39.3) and Direct Finetuning (78.1). Notably, Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation remains robust under suboptimal training data (27.4% overall), where standard Probabilistic Adaptation degrades to 25.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Vanilla AnimateDiff (the unadapted pretrained model), Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation achieves a 28.5-point improvement on MetaWorld policy supervision (68.3% vs. 39.8%) and a 3.7-point gain on visual planning (25.9% vs. 22.2%), but underperforms on continuous control episode return (90.7 vs. 103). Compared to standard Probabilistic Adaptation (Yang et al., 2023), the proposed inverse formulation wins decisively on MetaWorld policy supervision (68.3% vs. 40.2%) and remains robust to suboptimal data (27.4% vs. 25.2%), while standard Probabilistic Adaptation edges ahead on visual planning (30.4% vs. 25.9%). Subject Customization (DreamBooth) surprisingly achieves the best continuous control return (146.3) using only 20 static images, but trails Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation by 24.3 points on MetaWorld policy supervision (44.0% vs. 68.3%). Direct Finetuning of AnimateDiff's motion module performs worst among adapted methods on MetaWorld (31.0%), confirming that small-scale finetuning risks catastrophic forgetting. The results also generalize across backbones: with AnimateLCM, Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation still achieves the best overall MetaWorld success rate (65.2%), confirming the adaptation strategy is model-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of large-scale video model adaptation and robotic decision-making, building directly on the score composition framework of Probabilistic Adaptation (Yang et al., 2023a) by inverting the direction of adaptation so the pretrained model leads generation while consulting a small in-domain model. It reuses the Video-TADPoLe reward computation (Luo et al., 2024) for policy supervision and the UniPi visual planning pipeline (Du et al., 2024a) as standardized evaluation harnesses, while employing AnimateDiff as the frozen backbone and AVDC as the lightweight in-domain model. The key conceptual departure is treating downstream robotic task success—rather than FVD alone—as the primary metric for adaptation quality, and systematically comparing adaptation techniques along data and compute axes (static images → video demonstrations → full finetuning).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a practical decision framework for adapting video models to robotics under varying data and resource constraints, and demonstrates that expert-quality demonstrations may not be strictly necessary—Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation remains robust even when only 30% of adaptation data comes from an expert. This opens the door to leveraging cheaply collected random or suboptimal trajectories for adaptation, which is significant for real-world deployment where expert data is expensive. Open questions include whether these findings transfer to real-robot settings, why performance rankings differ between policy supervision and visual planning setups, and whether the approach scales to more complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks beyond the relatively simple MetaWorld and iTHOR benchmarks.
