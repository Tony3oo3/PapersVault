## Problem

Offline-trained imitation learning policies are limited by the coverage of their demonstration data, and direct RL fine-tuning is impractical because modern large policy models (Behavior Transformer with k-means clustering, Diffusion Policy requiring ground-truth denoising labels) contain non-differentiable components or supervision signals unavailable in RL. The paper asks whether a large frozen policy can be meaningfully improved through online environment interactions without any modification to the base model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251008/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen base policy of arbitrary architecture and a small residual network (MLP or CNN) as its two components. The base policy consumes either low-dimensional proprioceptive state (39–131 dims) or visual observations (64×64 or 128×128 RGBD images) and outputs robot actions; the residual policy receives only the observation (not the base action) and produces a bounded corrective action scaled by hyperparameter α. The executed action is the element-wise sum of both policies' outputs, so the final robot action inherits the base policy's smooth, multi-modal motion characteristics while receiving small online corrections.

## Method

Policy Decorator trains a small residual policy (a Gaussian-parameterized MLP or CNN) via SAC with sparse task-success rewards, whose output is added to the frozen base policy's action at each step. The core technical contribution is a pair of controlled exploration strategies that make online RL stable and sample-efficient: (1) a bounded residual action (tanh output scaled by α) constrains the correction magnitude so the agent stays near the base trajectory, and (2) a progressive exploration schedule linearly ramps the probability of using the residual policy from 0 to 1 over H environment steps, ensuring early training relies on the reliable base policy before the residual has learned useful corrections. The SAC critic takes the sum of base and residual actions as its action input. The framework is fully model-agnostic: it treats the base policy as a black box requiring no architectural changes, in contrast to fine-tuning baselines that must add tanh layers, LoRA adapters, or handle missing denoising supervision.

## Results

Policy Decorator improves offline-trained Behavior Transformer and Diffusion Policy base policies to near-perfect success rates (95%+) on all 8 tasks across ManiSkill and Adroit benchmarks, starting from base policy success rates ranging from 13% (PegInsertionSide) to 78% (Adroit Door) for Behavior Transformer. On the DPPO comparison tasks, Policy Decorator achieves 80%+ success on Transport in ~4M environment steps versus DPPO's ~16M steps, demonstrating a 4× sample efficiency improvement. The method also generalizes to visual observations (64×64 RGBD images) and preserves the smooth motion characteristics of the base imitation learning policies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Policy Decorator outperforms all 9 baselines across the 8 tasks. Among non-fine-tuning baselines, JSRL (Jump-Start RL) is the strongest, exceeding the base policy on roughly half of scenarios, but it learns an entirely new policy rather than refining the base one, losing the smooth motion property. Vanilla Residual RL improves the base policy on only 3 of 6 Diffusion Policy tasks and performs poorly with Behavior Transformer due to uncontrolled exploration destroying the base trajectory. FISH fails on most tasks due to the weak VINN non-parametric base policy. Among fine-tuning baselines, Cal-QL and RLPD improve Behavior Transformer on a few Adroit tasks but completely fail on all ManiSkill tasks; ROT shows limited gains. DIPO (the basic RL fine-tuning method for Diffusion Policy) fails to obtain any success signals across all tasks, rendering all DIPO-based fine-tuning baselines inapplicable. Against the concurrent DPPO method, Policy Decorator wins on both Square and Transport tasks with 4× better sample efficiency on Transport, while also being model-agnostic (DPPO is restricted to a specific Diffusion Policy configuration). GAIL+MLP achieves 0% on StackCube and ~20% on TurnFaucet, confirming that simple architectures cannot capture multi-modal demonstration distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the residual RL paradigm (Johannink et al., 2019; Alakuijala et al., 2021) by adding controlled exploration strategies—bounded residual actions and a progressive exploration schedule—that make residual learning viable for large, complex imitation learning models with non-differentiable components (Behavior Transformer, Diffusion Policy). It departs from the offline-to-online fine-tuning line (Cal-QL, RLPD, ROT) by treating the base policy as a frozen black box rather than modifying its parameters, sidestepping the architectural incompatibilities that make RL fine-tuning non-trivial for modern IL models. The SAC algorithm (Haarnoja et al., 2018) serves as the backbone for training the small residual policy, and the 'decorator' framing emphasizes model-agnosticism as a core design principle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As large policy models (RT-1, RT-2, Open X-Embodiment) become the standard for robot manipulation, the inability to cheaply refine them online is a practical bottleneck; Policy Decorator offers a simple, model-agnostic recipe (small MLP + two hyperparameters) that consistently achieves near-optimal success while preserving the smooth motion properties of IL policies. The paper's thorough ablations and failure analysis of fine-tuning baselines (random critic initialization, long-horizon sparse rewards) provide useful diagnostic insights for the broader offline-to-online RL community. Open questions include whether the approach scales to real-world deployment with limited interaction budgets, whether only a few critical states truly need correction (as the authors hypothesize), and how to handle the computational cost of slow-inference base policies like diffusion models during online training.
