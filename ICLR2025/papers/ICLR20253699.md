## Problem

Specialized foundation models in genomics, satellite imaging, and time series claim breakthrough performance from large-scale pretraining, yet many are only benchmarked against other FMs rather than well-tuned supervised baselines. This paper asks whether these FMs actually outperform a standard supervised workflow (model development, hyperparameter tuning, training) using only target-task data, and finds they consistently do not.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253699/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation spans three modalities with distinct formats: nucleotide base sequences of 200–600 bases for genomics classification, multispectral satellite images with 3–18 input channels at resolutions from 32×32 to 332×332 for land-cover classification, and multivariate time series with 7–862 channels and lookback windows up to 512 timesteps for long-horizon forecasting (horizons 24–720). Critically, the supervised workflows consume only target-task data, while the FMs they are compared against use two to five orders of magnitude more pretraining data (e.g., 20.5T base-pairs for NT, 70M images for Clay, 13M series for MOMENT).

## Method

The paper introduces two automated supervised workflows. DASHA simulates the model-development step of a supervised pipeline by applying DASH (a weight-sharing NAS method) to discover task-specific kernel sizes and dilation rates within CNN backbones (Wide ResNet, UNet), followed by ASHA-based hyperparameter tuning and full training on target data. Auto-AR rescues the classical autoregressive forecaster by using the KPSS test for differencing, BIC to select lookback length, and GPU-accelerated multi-channel likelihood maximization to tune lookback windows up to 512—far exceeding the ~5 default in standard Auto-ARIMA packages. The key finding is that these simple, data-efficient approaches (10.5M parameters for DASHA, <1K for Auto-AR) match or outperform FMs with hundreds of millions to billions of parameters, demonstrating that the 'BERT moment' has not yet arrived in these specialized domains.

## Results

On the Nucleotide Transformer genomics benchmark (18 tasks), DASHA (10.5M params, 0 pretraining data) achieves an average score of 0.761 and mean 46.33% improvement over the Raw Probe baseline, outperforming all 10 foundation models including 2.5B-parameter NT-Multispecies (0.697) trained on 174B base pairs. On satellite imaging (9 tasks), DASHA (32.4M params) reaches 77.85 average accuracy, within 1% of the best FMs (DOFA-Large: 78.80) while using 2–10× fewer parameters and no pretraining. On time series forecasting (7 datasets, 28 tasks), Auto-AR (513 params) achieves 0.551 average RMSE, matching or beating 7 of 9 open-source FMs while using over 2000× fewer parameters than any FM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In genomics, DASHA decisively outperforms all 10 FMs: it beats the strongest FM (Caduceus-PH, 0.725) by 0.036 in average score and 4.7 points in mean % improvement, while using 180× fewer parameters and zero pretraining data. In satellite imaging, DASHA (77.85) wins against 8 of 11 FMs including SatMAE-Large (77.75), CROMA-Base (77.39), and SwinT-Base (76.69), but is narrowly outperformed by CROMA-Large (78.03), DOFA-Base (78.44), and DOFA-Large (78.80). In time series, Auto-AR (0.551 RMSE) beats GPT4TS/OFA (0.555), CALF (0.568), TEMPO-ZS (0.598), TimesFM-ZS (0.574), and TEST-FewShot (0.603), ties MOMENT (0.550), but is slightly outperformed by TTM-A (0.538), TTM-B (0.543), and S²IP-LLM (0.545). Notably, even the untuned AR baseline (0.556) outperforms all zero-shot and few-shot FMs, and the century-old Auto-ARIMA (0.896) is dramatically worse than all deep learning methods. The supervised CNN baselines (Wide ResNet, UNet) also outperform several FMs in genomics (e.g., Wide ResNet beats Enformer, HyenaDNA-32K, and DNABERT-2), underscoring that the gap is not merely about tuning but about the fundamental competitiveness of in-domain supervised learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DASH (Shen et al., 2022), a NAS method for discovering kernel sizes and dilation rates in CNN backbones, and augments it with ASHA-based hyperparameter tuning to create the DASHA workflow for simulating supervised model development. For time series, it repurposes the classical AR model from the Auto-ARIMA lineage (Hyndman & Khandakar, 2008) by dropping the MA component and enabling GPU-based tuning of much longer lookback windows. Conceptually, it extends a small but growing line of critical evaluations of specialized FMs (Yang et al. 2024 on protein sequences, Kedzierska et al. 2023 on single-cell biology, Tan et al. 2024 on time-series LLMs) from individual case studies to a systematic, multi-domain comparison, while departing from the 'lift-and-shift' framing that dominates specialized FM development by insisting on in-domain supervised baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that specialized FMs across genomics, satellite imaging, and time series have not yet achieved their 'BERT moment'—despite 2–5 orders of magnitude more pretraining data—challenges the default assumption that pretrain-then-fine-tune is the right paradigm in these domains and redirects practitioner attention toward well-tuned supervised models. The open-source DASHA and Auto-AR workflows provide immediately usable, automated baselines that lower the barrier to fair FM evaluation, addressing a gap the authors identify as an 'echo chamber' where FMs are only compared to other FMs. Key open questions include whether the gap will close with further scaling, whether the findings extend to tasks beyond classification and forecasting (e.g., exploratory science in genomics), and whether the 'lift-and-shift' architectural choices are fundamentally limiting or simply under-tuned.
