## Problem

Existing scene flow methods, whether supervised or unsupervised, struggle to estimate motion for small, fast-moving objects like birds, tennis balls, and pedestrians, largely because they formulate the task as estimating flow between only two adjacent frames. This two-frame constraint limits the available supervision signal and prevents the method from leveraging the full temporal context of a scene. The paper asks whether reframing scene flow as a continuous ODE estimation problem over an entire observation sequence can unlock higher-quality, more general motion estimates.

## Input / Output

EulerFlow consumes a full sequence of LiDAR point clouds (P_0, …, P_N) along with ego pose and a map for ego-motion compensation and ground removal; RGB is not used during optimization, though the method can be fed monocular depth estimates (e.g., from DepthCrafter) to handle RGB-only video. The primary output is a continuous Eulerian velocity field θ(x,y,z,t) represented by a single ReLU MLP coordinate network, from which per-point 3D flow vectors between any two timesteps (not just adjacent frames) can be extracted via Euler integration. A notable emergent output is 3D point tracks over long horizons, obtained by integrating the ODE from an initial position without any explicit tracking supervision.

## Method

EulerFlow represents the scene's motion as an Eulerian ODE F(x,y,z,t) — the instantaneous velocity at each position and time — and fits this ODE with a single neural prior θ (a ReLU MLP coordinate network taking 5D space-time-direction input: x, y, z, normalized time t∈[-1,1], and a forward/backward direction flag). The key mechanism is multi-frame optimization: using Euler integration with Δt equal to the sensor interval, the method rolls out point trajectories from any frame t to t+k for k∈{-W,…,W}\{0} (W=3) and imposes Truncated Chamfer Distance reconstruction losses against the corresponding observed point clouds, plus a cyclic consistency loss (α=0.01) that composes forward and backward one-step flows. This contrasts with NSFP, which fits a single frame pair with two separate priors; EulerFlow's single-prior, multi-observation formulation allows constructive interference across the full sequence and enables flow estimation to arbitrary future or past timesteps. The neural prior depth is tuned to 18 for the Argoverse 2 challenge (vs. NSFP's default of 8), reflecting the greater capacity needed to fit an ODE over long time intervals without overfitting to noise.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 2024 Scene Flow Challenge test set, EulerFlow (unsupervised) achieves state-of-the-art Mean Dynamic Normalized EPE, surpassing the next-best unsupervised method (ICP Flow) by more than 2.5× in error reduction and exceeding the next-best supervised method (Flow4D) by over 10%. On the Waymo Open validation set, EulerFlow more than halves the error of the next-best method. Per-class analysis shows the largest gains on small, fast-moving objects (PEDESTRIAN, WHEELED VRU, CYCLIST), where EulerFlow at least halves the error of ICP Flow across every class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EulerFlow outperforms all 11 baselines on both Argoverse 2 and Waymo Open. Against the strongest unsupervised competitor ICP Flow, EulerFlow achieves more than 2.5× lower mean Dynamic Normalized EPE on Argoverse 2, with the largest per-class gains on PEDESTRIAN and WHEELED VRU (at least halving error on every class). Against the strongest supervised method Flow4D, EulerFlow (despite being unsupervised) beats it by over 10% on Argoverse 2 and more than halves its error on Waymo Open. Compared to the multi-frame method Liu et al. (2024), EulerFlow produces cleaner flow with fewer artifacts on static objects (e.g., no artifacts on stopped cars in Figure 2). Against NTP (Wang et al., 2022a), which models 25-frame trajectories, EulerFlow significantly outperforms even at the same 20-frame sequence length, indicating the advantage comes from the ODE formulation and multi-step Euler integration objectives rather than sequence length alone. Ablations confirm that removing multi-step rollouts (k>1) degrades performance to near NTP levels, while removing cycle consistency has a smaller but still measurable impact.

## Relation to Prior Work

EulerFlow extends Neural Scene Flow Prior (NSFP) by replacing its two-frame, single-snapshot optimization with a continuous Eulerian ODE estimated over the entire observation sequence. While NSFP fits a coordinate network to one frame pair using Truncated Chamfer Distance and cycle consistency, EulerFlow queries the same architectural prior at arbitrary (position, time) pairs and optimizes multi-step Euler-integrated rollouts across the full sequence. This departs from Lagrangian/deformation-based approaches (Nerfies, DynamicFusion) that require a canonical frame, and from Neural Trajectory Prior (NTP) which uses three separate priors and a sinusoidal time embedding; EulerFlow uses a single ReLU MLP with normalized time and direction encoding, and its ablations show the smooth ReLU prior outperforms higher-frequency alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EulerFlow demonstrates that a simple ODE formulation with standard Chamfer Distance losses can outperform all supervised and unsupervised scene flow methods on major autonomous driving benchmarks, and does so out-of-the-box across domains (driving, tabletop) without hyperparameter tuning. The emergent 3D point tracking via Euler integration is a compelling byproduct that could serve as a lightweight tracking primitive. Key limitations remain: 24-hour optimization per sequence on a single V100, point-cloud-only input (no RGB fusion), and sensitivity to ray-casting artifacts (moving shadows). Open questions include whether the multi-step rollout objective can be distilled into feedforward architectures and whether multi-modal fusion can extend tracking beyond lidar dropout.
