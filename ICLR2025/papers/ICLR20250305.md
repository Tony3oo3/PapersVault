## Problem

Existing offline multi-task MARL skill-learning methods (e.g., ODIS) only discover task-irrelevant common skills, leaving two gaps: (1) the extracted common skills lack cooperative temporal knowledge about how agents should coordinate over time, and (2) no mechanism exists to adaptively incorporate task-specific knowledge for fine-grained action execution. This limits policy transfer to unseen tasks with varying numbers of agents and targets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes local observations from all agents (decomposed into own-information and entity-information portions to handle variable agent counts across tasks) drawn from an offline multi-task dataset, and produces joint actions for all agents. Training is fully offline and end-to-end; at execution time the policy operates in a decentralized manner using only local observations—no global state or centralized information is required, unlike prior hierarchical MARL skill methods. The observation decomposition via a transformer with attention over variable-length entity lists is what enables a single policy to handle tasks with different numbers of agents.

## Method

HiSSD employs a two-level hierarchical architecture. The high-level planner learns common skills by minimizing a KL-divergence objective (derived from probabilistic inference à la Levine 2018) that jointly maximizes an IQL-trained value estimate (exploration) and minimizes a forward-predictor's error on the next global state (prediction), thereby embedding cooperative temporal knowledge into the skill representations. The low-level controller learns task-specific skills via a β-VAE ELBO where the KL-regularization term is replaced by an InfoNCE-style contrastive loss that pulls same-task skill embeddings together and pushes cross-task embeddings apart, enabling adaptive task discrimination. The action decoder then fuses local observations, common skills, and task-specific skills through a transformer-plus-MLP pipeline to emit per-agent actions. A key theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) proves that the contrastive loss provides a lower bound on the KL-divergence between the learned skill distribution and the task prior, formally bridging contrastive learning and the variational objective.

## Results

On SMAC Marine-Hard (Expert data), HiSSD achieves 99.2% win rate on unseen tasks 4m and 5m, 98.4% on 10m, and 86.3% on 10m_vs_11m, outperforming ODIS (95.3%, 89.1%, 93.8%, 42.4%) and BC-best (92.1%, 87.1%, 90.5%, 45.3%). On MAMuJoCo HalfCheetah-v2, HiSSD achieves 3472.12 average return on unseen task 'back shin' and 4175.29 on 'front foot', surpassing ODIS (3217.59, 3930.82) and IQL (1974.62, 3948.17). In medium-quality data settings, the gap widens substantially: HiSSD reaches 98.0% on unseen 10m versus ODIS's 61.3% and BC-best's 56.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ODIS (the closest skill-based baseline), HiSSD consistently outperforms on unseen tasks across all data qualities, with the largest gaps in medium-quality data (e.g., 98.0% vs. 61.3% on SMAC 10m; 3472 vs. 3218 return on MAMuJoCo back shin). Against UPDeT-m (transformer-based offline MARL), HiSSD achieves dramatic improvements, as UPDeT-m frequently collapses to 0% win rate on asymmetric SMAC tasks (7m_vs_8m, 8m_vs_9m, 10m_vs_11m) while HiSSD maintains 35–86%. Against BC-best (behavior cloning with return-to-go), HiSSD wins on the majority of unseen tasks, particularly the harder asymmetric scenarios where BC-best scores below 50%. On MAMuJoCo, HiSSD surpasses both IQL and TD3-BC on both unseen tasks (back shin: 3472 vs. 1975/1690; front foot: 4175 vs. 3948/3683), confirming that the hierarchical skill structure aids generalization beyond what value-based offline methods achieve. HiSSD does not uniformly dominate: on MAMuJoCo source tasks 'front thigh' and 'front shin', IQL and TD3-BC still lead, and on SMAC Stalker-Zealot (heterogeneous units), BC-based methods occasionally match or exceed skill-based methods, suggesting the approach is most effective for homogeneous multi-agent settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiSSD extends the offline multi-task MARL skill discovery line initiated by ODIS (Zhang et al., 2023) and HyGen (Zhang et al., 2024), which learn only task-irrelevant common skills from offline data. The key departure is a hierarchical decomposition that jointly learns (i) common skills enriched with cooperative temporal knowledge via a forward-prediction objective (building on Levine's probabilistic inference framework and Xu et al.'s TD-residual-weighted imitation) and (ii) task-specific skills regularized by a contrastive objective theoretically linked to KL-divergence (Theorem 3.1). The value network is trained with IQL's L2^ε loss, and the controller's ELBO is derived from the β-VAE framework, making HiSSD a synthesis of offline RL, variational inference, and contrastive learning within a single hierarchical MARL architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing offline multi-task MARL methods (ODIS, HyGen) capture only shared cooperation patterns and cannot adaptively encode task-unique action priors, limiting fine-grained transfer to unseen tasks with different agent counts. HiSSD's fully offline pipeline (no online exploration or fine-tuning) with demonstrated gains across four data qualities on both discrete (SMAC) and continuous (MAMuJoCo) benchmarks makes it a practical option for deployment-constrained multi-agent settings. The acknowledged limitation of training stability, and the observation that skill-based methods underperform BC on heterogeneous-unit tasks (Stalker-Zealot), suggest that the approach's benefits are most pronounced for homogeneous-agent scenarios and that robustness to architectural complexity remains an open challenge.
