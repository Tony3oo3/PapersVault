## Problem

Whether WGANs succeed because they minimize the Wasserstein distance has been debated, but prior work could not rigorously answer this because W1 between continuous distributions is intractable to compute exactly. This paper shows that WGANs do minimize W1, but the specific form of the distance depends critically on the discriminator architecture: convolutional discriminators minimize patch-level W1 rather than image-level W1, which explains why successful GANs produce sharp images despite not matching the global image distribution.

## Input / Output

The generator consumes a noise vector sampled uniformly from a fixed set of M discrete vectors (rather than a continuous prior) and produces at most M distinct images, making the generated distribution finite and W1 exactly computable. Experiments use 128×128 FFHQ faces, 64×64 MNIST digits, and synthetic toy datasets, with M ranging from 64 to 70,000. The key output beyond generated images is the exact W1 value between the discrete generator distribution and the empirical training distribution, enabling direct comparison against OT-Means and random baselines.

## Method

The authors introduce a discrete GAN framework where the noise prior is a fixed set of M vectors, enabling exact W1 computation and analytical characterization of optima (Theorem 2.4: M≥N yields copying; M<N yields linear combinations of ≥N/M training images). They prove that a CNN-GAP discriminator (abstracting PatchGAN) makes the WGAN objective equivalent to minimizing W1 over all patches of size S across images, while a CNN-FC discriminator (abstracting DCGAN) minimizes an upper bound on W1 between patches at each specific spatial location. An OT-Means algorithm is introduced to directly optimize discrete W1 as a baseline, and Sliced Wasserstein Distance is used as a tractable proxy for patch-level W1 optimization. Experiments on FFHQ confirm that discrete DCGAN and FastGAN variants match local patch distributions (verified via SWD ≈ 0.04 and near-perfect histogram alignment of random projections) rather than image-level distributions, with the patch size governed by the discriminator's effective receptive field.

## Results

The key quantitative result (Table 1) shows that discrete WGANs with convolutional discriminators (DCGAN, FastGAN) achieve a local patch Sliced Wasserstein Distance (SWD) of 0.033–0.058 across different M values (10K, 70K, ∞), consistently below 0.06. In contrast, OT-Means (which directly minimizes image W1) achieves a local patch SWD of ~0.099, and unrelated datasets (AFHQ, ImageNet) yield 0.145 and 0.181 respectively. With fully connected discriminators, discrete WGANs match OT-Means in minimizing image W1 nearly exactly, confirming that the discriminator architecture determines which form of Wasserstein distance is optimized.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares discrete WGANs against three baselines: (1) OT-Means, which directly optimizes image W1; (2) random batches of training images; and (3) unrelated datasets (AFHQ, ImageNet). With fully connected discriminators, discrete WGANs match OT-Means almost exactly in minimizing image W1, achieving nearly identical W1 values and producing similarly blurred images (as predicted by Theorem 2.4). With convolutional discriminators, however, WGANs achieve a local patch SWD of ~0.04, far below OT-Means (~0.1) and random batches, while matching the quality of direct patch SWD optimization. This demonstrates that CNN discriminators cause WGANs to minimize patch W1 rather than image W1. The discrete GANs also outperform unrelated datasets by a factor of 3–4× in local patch SWD (0.04 vs. 0.145–0.181), confirming they capture genuine local statistics of the training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly responds to a line of criticism (Mallasto et al., 2019; Stanczuk et al., 2021; Fedus et al., 2018) that questioned whether WGANs (Arjovsky et al., 2017) actually minimize the Wasserstein distance, with some authors arguing GANs succeed precisely because they fail to approximate W1. The authors introduce discrete GANs as an analytical tool—sampling noise from a fixed finite set—enabling exact computation of W1 and characterization of its optima, which is impossible with continuous distributions. The theoretical results on convolutional discriminators formalize and extend the patch-level observation first made informally in PatchGAN (Isola et al., 2017), now showing it corresponds to minimizing a specific patch-level Wasserstein distance. The OT-Means algorithm for solving the discrete W1 optimization problem is a new contribution that serves as a rigorous baseline for comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves a long-standing conceptual debate by showing WGANs do minimize Wasserstein distance, but the specific form is dictated by the discriminator architecture—convolutional discriminators minimize patch-level W1 rather than image-level W1. This reframing has practical implications: it suggests that GAN training could be made more stable and interpretable by explicitly optimizing the patch-level distance that convolutional discriminators are already implicitly targeting. A key limitation is the reliance on discrete GANs for exact W1 measurement, though the authors argue continuous GANs with large M behave similarly. Open questions include whether the patch-level interpretation fully explains GAN success on all datasets, how it interacts with more complex architectures (e.g., StyleGAN's multi-scale discriminators), and whether explicitly optimizing patch W1 leads to better or different generation quality than the current adversarial formulation.
