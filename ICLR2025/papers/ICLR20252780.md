## Problem

Prior diffusion-based portrait animation methods suffer from two intertwined failures: identity leakage (the driving subject's facial structure bleeds into the generated output) and poor capture of subtle or extreme expressions. The paper identifies the root causes as (1) explicit or synthetic motion descriptors that are too coarse and inadvertently encode the driving identity's geometry, and (2) spatially-aligned 2D control mechanisms (ControlNet, PoseGuider) that let the diffusion backbone shortcut semantic correspondences by mimicking the driving image's 2D layout.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static reference portrait (I_R) and a driving video (I_D) from a different individual, both processed at 512×512 during training and evaluated at 256×256. The driving frame is augmented with color jittering, random scaling (±30%), and piecewise affine transforms before being encoded into a 512-dimensional 1D latent motion vector, which is the sole motion signal injected into the generator. Output is a frame-by-frame animated portrait sequence preserving the reference identity and background while replicating the driving video's head pose and facial expressions; inference runs 25 DDIM steps with CFG scale 3.5, taking ~20 s per second of video at 24 GB VRAM.

## Method

X-NeMo replaces spatially-aligned additive motion control with a structure-agnostic cross-attention scheme: a motion encoder E_mot (based on a feature-alignment backbone plus self-attention and MLPs) distills a compact 1D identity-agnostic latent code f_mot from the augmented driving image, and this code is injected into the UNet via newly inserted cross-attention layers after each spatial transformer block, eliminating any 2D structural pathway from the driving condition. A dual GAN decoder head (StyleGAN generator conditioned on f_mot and a ResNet-50 appearance embedding f_app) is co-trained with image-level losses (L1, adversarial, feature-matching, VGG perceptual) to bootstrap fine-grained motion learning that the diffusion loss alone converges too slowly to capture. Three additional training strategies enforce disentanglement: color/spatial augmentations on the driving image, 30% reference feature masking to prevent the appearance branch from leaking motion shortcuts, and a three-stage curriculum (appearance pretraining → joint motion-encoder training → temporal module) followed by end-to-end fine-tuning. At inference, classifier-free guidance uses fully masked appearance features and the reference image's own motion latent as the negative prompt, sharpening the separation between identity and motion signals.

## Results

On self-reenactment, X-NeMo achieves the best L1 (0.055), SSIM (0.826), and LPIPS (0.168) among all baselines. On cross-reenactment, it attains the highest identity similarity (ID-SIM 0.787), lowest motion error (AED/APD 0.039/3.42), highest emotion similarity (EMO-SIM 0.65), and lowest FVD (152.8). The FVD advantage is particularly pronounced, with X-NeMo scoring 152.8 versus 237.3 for the next-best method (X-Portrait), a 36% reduction. EMO-SIM also shows a clear gap: 0.65 for X-NeMo versus 0.52 for X-Portrait and 0.49 for PD-FGC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-NeMo outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric in both self- and cross-reenactment. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor AniPortrait, X-NeMo improves ID-SIM from 0.713 to 0.787 (+10.4%), EMO-SIM from 0.46 to 0.65 (+41%), and FVD from 290.5 to 152.8 (−47%). X-Portrait, the strongest baseline on EMO-SIM (0.52) and FVD (237.3), is still surpassed by X-NeMo on all cross-reenactment metrics, with the largest gap in EMO-SIM (0.65 vs. 0.52). The GAN-based PD-FGC and LivePortrait lag substantially on identity preservation (ID-SIM 0.604 and 0.702 respectively) and video quality (FVD 441.8 and 279.2), reflecting limitations of GAN generators on out-of-domain portraits. FYE achieves the second-best ID-SIM (0.725) but has the worst EMO-SIM (0.41) and second-worst FVD (360.9), indicating poor fine-grained expression transfer despite reasonable identity retention.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-NeMo builds on the latent motion representation paradigm introduced by Burkov et al. (2020) and Wang et al. (2022, 2023), which encode facial motion into compact 1D descriptors, and on the diffusion-based portrait animation framework popularized by X-Portrait, AniPortrait, and EchoMimic (reference network + temporal modules + ControlNet/PoseGuider). Its key departure is replacing spatially-aligned 2D control signals (ControlNet, PoseGuider) with cross-attention conditioning on a jointly-trained 1D latent motion descriptor, arguing that spatial alignment is the root cause of identity leakage in cross-identity reenactment. The dual GAN decoder head for fine-grained motion supervision and the reference feature masking strategy are borrowed from prior GAN-based reenactment and masked image modeling literature, respectively, but repurposed within a diffusion training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode in diffusion-based portrait animation—spatially-aligned control causing the UNet to shortcut semantic correspondence learning, leading to identity drift—that had been treated as an appearance-only problem in prior work. The resulting 1D identity-agnostic motion embedding is a unified representation enabling downstream tasks (motion interpolation, video outpainting, emotion-conditioned generation) beyond single-frame reenactment. Limitations include reliance on real human video training data (struggles with 3D cartoon or non-human styles) and absence of extreme expressions not present in training. The authors note future integration into video diffusion backbones as a natural next step for smoother long-sequence generation.
