## Problem

The Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) theoretically guarantees size-independent optimal hyperparameters, but in practice suffers from strong interdependencies between HPs (requiring expensive random search), poor transfer of the embedding LR across widths, limited interpretability of its multipliers, and no support for low-precision training. The paper identifies these practical gaps and asks whether a simpler, more stable scheme can retain μP's transfer guarantees while enabling efficient 1D HP sweeps and out-of-the-box FP8 training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Llama-style transformer architecture (PreNorm, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE) and a text corpus, with a minimal set of user-specified hyperparameters: a global learning rate η and a small number of α multipliers (α_ffn-act, α_attn-softmax, α_res, α_res-attn-ratio, α_loss-softmax) that default to 1. It produces a trained language model whose optimal HPs transfer across width, depth, batch size, and training steps, along with a mixed-precision training recipe in which ~70% of matmul operations run in FP8 (E4M3 for inputs/weights, E5M2 for gradients) and only a few critical tensors remain in BF16.

## Method

u-μP combines μP's abc-parametrization with Unit Scaling by exploiting abc-symmetry to shift scales so that all activations, weights, and gradients begin training at unit variance, eliminating the need for σ_W initialization HPs and base-shape parameters. The HP set is redesigned by associating α multipliers with operations (e.g., attention softmax, FFN activation, residual connections) rather than individual weight tensors, which reduces cardinality and minimizes interdependencies—enabling an 'independent search' strategy where a single LR sweep followed by parallel 1D sweeps of remaining HPs replaces the hundreds-of-runs random search required by μP. A new embedding LR scaling rule (c_emb = 1/√fan-out instead of 1) corrects poor width transfer observed in standard μP. For low-precision training, the unit-scale initialization keeps most tensors' RMS near 1 throughout training, so a simple static cast to E4M3/E5M2 suffices for non-critical tensors, with only the attention output projection, FFN down-projection, and decoder weight requiring BF16 fallback.

## Results

At 7B scale on 0-shot benchmarks, u-μP (BF16) is competitive with the SP baseline, achieving 29.0 MMLU, 53.4 HellaSwag, 31.6 OpenBook QA, 77.1 PIQA, 23.4 TriviaQA, and 63.7 WinoGrande. The FP8 variant of u-μP shows minimal degradation, scoring 31.2 MMLU, 53.4 HellaSwag, 29.6 OpenBook QA, 77.6 PIQA, 21.3 TriviaQA, and 65.7 WinoGrande. In proxy-model experiments, u-μP reaches loss equal to or lower than μP, and a simple 1D LR sweep (9 runs) alone achieves near-optimal loss, whereas μP requires hundreds of random-search runs over coupled HPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against μP (Yang et al., 2022), u-μP achieves equal or lower validation loss across all tested widths (256–4096) and training configurations, while enabling a fundamentally simpler HP search: a single 1D LR sweep suffices for near-optimal performance, whereas μP requires a multi-dimensional random search over hundreds of runs due to strong HP interdependencies (quantified via transfer error in Figure 3). u-μP also corrects μP's poor embedding-LR transfer across width by changing the scaling rule from c_emb=1 to c_emb=1/√fan-out, and shows continued benefit from increasing width where μP exhibits diminishing returns. Against the SP/Pythia baseline (Biderman et al., 2023), u-μP at 7B scale is broadly competitive: it wins on HellaSwag (+1.0), OpenBook QA (+3.8), PIQA (+0.6), TriviaQA (+1.2), and WinoGrande (+0.4) in BF16, while trailing slightly on MMLU (−0.6). The FP8 u-μP model remains on par with its BF16 counterpart on most benchmarks, confirming that the unit-scaled scheme enables out-of-the-box low-precision training without the dynamic per-tensor rescaling required by standard approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two existing frameworks—Maximal Update Parametrization (μP, Tensor Programs V) and Unit Scaling—into a unified scheme (u-μP) by exploiting abc-symmetry to transform μP's scaling rules into a unit-scaled form. It departs from standard μP in two key ways: a modified embedding LR scaling rule (1/√fan-out instead of 1) and a re-parameterization of hyperparameters from weights onto operations, yielding a set with minimal interdependencies. The paper also positions itself against the broader abc-parametrization family (NTK, MFP), noting that u-μP is equivalent to MFP in width exponents except for the embedding LR, and connects to signal-propagation work that independently derives the same 1/√depth residual scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for LLM training: u-μP enables a simple 1D independent HP search (a single LR sweep reaches near-optimal loss) replacing the hundreds-of-runs random search required by μP, and provides out-of-the-box FP8 mixed-precision training validated at 7B scale. However, the modified embedding LR rule lacks theoretical justification, and neither μP nor Unit Scaling guarantees well-behaved tensor scales throughout training—scale growth in critical layers (attention out-projection, FFN down-projection, decoder weight) remains an open problem. The finding that standard μP fails for realistic LLM training without non-parametric norms and independent weight decay also raises questions about how broadly the original μP theory applies.
