## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models struggle to produce legible, multilingual artistic typography that aligns with user-specific aesthetic preferences and cultural contexts. The subjective nature of typographic design and the absence of comprehensive annotated WordArt datasets make it difficult for generative models to capture the full stylistic diversity demanded in real-world applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252308/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language text prompt (specifying style, theme, language, and application context) plus optional iterative user feedback as input, and produces a single RGB WordArt image where the text itself is artistically rendered (e.g., letters formed from thematic objects) rather than simply overlaid on a background. It supports English, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean scripts, with user analytics showing most outputs use up to five characters and a 16:9 aspect ratio. The output is a full-resolution image with optional alpha channel, generated through a multi-stage pipeline rather than a single forward pass.

## Method

MetaDesigner is a four-agent LLM-driven pipeline: a Pipeline Designer uses GPT-4 Chain-of-Thought to decompose user prompts into glyph and texture sub-prompts; a Glyph Designer renders characters via FreeType (normal/traditional) or optimizes SVG vector parameters with DiffVG and SDS loss against a Stable Diffusion depth-to-image model (semantic); a Texture Designer applies ControlNet-based controllable synthesis with a Tree-of-Thought strategy to select and fuse from a library of 68 LoRA models organized in a hierarchical category tree; and a Q&A Evaluation Agent uses LLaVA to score text-to-image consistency and image quality, merging automated metrics with user Q&A feedback to iteratively update hyperparameters. The closed-loop optimization (Algorithm 1) adjusts pipeline, glyph, and texture parameters across iterations until a composite loss threshold is met, enabling progressive convergence toward user-defined stylistic goals without retraining any underlying model.

## Results

In the user study (11 participants, 20 multilingual prompts), MetaDesigner with feedback achieves 96.8% Text Accuracy, 75.4 Aesthetics, and 77.2 Creativity, substantially outperforming all baselines. Without the feedback loop (Ours*), text accuracy remains strong at 88.2% but aesthetics and creativity drop to 5.0 and 10.5 respectively, highlighting the critical role of iterative refinement. On quantitative metrics, MetaDesigner achieves the best SSIM (0.2520 on Promeai ground-truth, 0.2829 on design-website ground-truth) and lowest LPIPS (0.6878 and 0.7915) across all language subsets (English, CJK, All).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaDesigner decisively outperforms all six baselines on every evaluated dimension. On Text Accuracy, it leads Anytext (82.3) by 14.5 points and DALL-E 3 (33.2) by 63.6 points, while SDXL and SDXL-Aug are near-failures at 3.6. On Aesthetics and Creativity, the gap is even more dramatic: MetaDesigner scores 75.4 and 77.2 versus DALL-E 3's 10.9 and 7.3 (the next-best), a roughly 7× margin. Anytext is the strongest text-accuracy competitor but collapses on visual quality (0.9 on both Aesthetics and Creativity), while the TextDiffuser family (44.1/42.7 accuracy) produces visually homogeneous outputs with near-zero creativity scores. On SSIM/LPIPS, MetaDesigner achieves the best structural similarity and perceptual distance across both Promeai and design-website ground-truth sets for all language subsets, confirming its advantage is not limited to subjective user ratings. The ablation (Ours* without feedback) shows that the Q&A Evaluation Agent's iterative loop is essential for the aesthetic and creative gains, while the base pipeline already provides strong multilingual text rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaDesigner sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) diffusion-based text-to-image synthesis (Stable Diffusion, ControlNet), (2) visual text rendering for non-Latin scripts (GlyphDraw, GlyphControl, TextDiffuser, DiffUTE), and (3) LLM-driven prompt engineering and multi-agent orchestration (Chain-of-Thought, Tree-of-Thought). Rather than proposing a new diffusion architecture or a new glyph-rendering primitive, the paper's core contribution is a system-level integration: GPT-4 acts as an orchestrator that decomposes user prompts into glyph and texture sub-tasks, routes them through FreeType/DiffVG rendering and a 68-model LoRA library selected via Tree-of-Thought, and closes the loop with LLaVA-based evaluation and user feedback. This departs from prior WordArt methods (Word-As-Image, DS-Fusion) that are single-pass, single-language pipelines without interactive refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is notable—over 1 million user-generated images and 1M+ ModelScope visits demonstrate real-world adoption, and the 5,000+ multilingual WordArt dataset fills a gap for benchmarking artistic text generation beyond English. However, several limitations temper the contribution: the system is tightly coupled to GPT-4 (cost, latency, availability), the user study involves only 11 participants with subjective scoring, and the 68-LoRA library is a fixed, manually curated resource rather than an open-ended generative capability. Open questions include whether the iterative feedback loop converges reliably across diverse user intents, how the approach generalizes to scripts beyond CJK and Latin, and whether the multi-agent overhead is justified compared to simpler single-pass pipelines with strong prompting.
