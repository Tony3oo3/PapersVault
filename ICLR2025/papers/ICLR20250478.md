## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion models like Stable Diffusion for downstream tasks remains challenging: additive methods (adapters) add inference latency, reparameterized methods (LoRA) risk overfitting and require model-specific layer/rank design, and selective methods suffer from high memory costs and hyperparameter sensitivity. The paper asks whether a universal, model-agnostic fine-tuning strategy can be built by exploiting the observation that the smallest-magnitude parameters in pre-trained diffusion models are temporarily ineffective due to training stochasticity rather than structural redundancy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250478/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (v1.4–3.0) and a small downstream dataset (e.g., 5 style datasets from CIVITAI with ~100 images each) as fine-tuning inputs, and produces an adapted model that generates images from text prompts. During inference, the fine-tuned model accepts a text prompt and outputs a generated image in the standard diffusion latent space (512×512 for SD 1.5/2.0, 1024×1024 for SD 3.0). The fine-tuning process requires only a single-line code modification to the training loop, with no additional modules or architectural changes to the base model.

## Method

SaRA identifies 'temporarily ineffective' parameters by computing a sparse mask M where |p| < θ_t, then fine-tunes only P⊙M while freezing P⊙(1−M) to preserve prior knowledge. To prevent overfitting from the potentially high rank of the sparse trainable matrix, it adds a nuclear-norm low-rank loss L_rank = ‖P_M‖_* computed via SVD. A Progressive Parameter Adjustment (PPA) strategy reselects the ~15–20% of initially masked parameters that remain below threshold after the first half of training and focuses optimization on them in the second half, reducing residual ineffective parameters from 15% to 2%. Finally, an Unstructural Backpropagation mechanism extracts trainable parameters as independent leaf nodes in the computational graph, retaining gradients only for the selected subset and automatically discarding gradients for frozen parameters, saving over 40% GPU memory compared to LoRA and standard selective PEFT methods.

## Results

On the downstream dataset fine-tuning benchmark (5 CIVITAI style datasets), SaRA achieves the best mean VLHI across all Stable Diffusion versions and parameter budgets. For SD 1.5 with 50M trainable parameters, SaRA reaches a mean FID of 134.96 and VLHI of 1.55, outperforming LoRA (FID 150.74, VLHI 1.45), AdaptFormer (FID 146.62, VLHI 1.18), and LT-SFT (FID 150.18, VLHI 0.49). On backbone fine-tuning (ImageNet, FFHQ, CelebA-HQ), SaRA achieves the best FID scores among all PEFT methods, demonstrating it can improve the pre-trained model's performance on its original task. The method also saves over 40% GPU memory compared to LoRA and selective PEFT baselines via unstructural backpropagation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaRA consistently outperforms LT-SFT by a large margin on VLHI (e.g., 1.55 vs. 0.49 for SD 1.5/50M; 1.68 vs. 0.59 for SD 2.0/20M), indicating LT-SFT's parameter selection strategy fails to preserve prior knowledge. Against AdaptFormer, SaRA achieves substantially better VLHI (1.55 vs. 1.18 for SD 1.5/50M) and lower FID, while AdaptFormer occasionally edges out on CLIP score. Compared to LoRA, SaRA achieves lower FID (better style adaptation) at the cost of slightly lower CLIP in some settings, but delivers higher VLHI overall (e.g., 1.69 vs. 1.52 for SD 1.5/20M; 1.77 vs. 1.52 for SD 3.0/50M). Notably, SaRA with only 50M trainable parameters matches or exceeds the VLHI of full-parameter fine-tuning (860M–2085M parameters) across all three SD versions, demonstrating that selectively training the smallest-magnitude parameters is more effective than updating all parameters. On backbone fine-tuning (ImageNet, FFHQ, CelebA-HQ), SaRA achieves the best FID among all PEFT methods, confirming it can improve the base model's original-task performance rather than merely adapting to new domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

SaRA synthesizes three distinct research threads: model pruning theory (which identifies small-magnitude parameters as negligible), selective fine-tuning methods like LT-SFT and PaFi (which train a subset of existing parameters), and low-rank adaptation via LoRA (which constrains the rank of learned updates). Rather than introducing new modules (as adapters do) or applying low-rank updates globally (as LoRA does), SaRA repurposes the pre-trained model's own small parameters as the trainable subset, adding a nuclear-norm low-rank constraint and a progressive reselection strategy. The unstructural backpropagation mechanism is a novel engineering contribution that addresses a shared practical limitation of all prior selective PEFT methods: they must retain gradients for the full parameter matrix, negating their memory advantage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unstructural backpropagation technique is a general-purpose solution that can reduce memory costs for any selective PEFT method, making it potentially broadly adoptable beyond SaRA itself. The empirical finding that small parameters are 'temporarily ineffective' due to training stochasticity rather than structural redundancy provides a useful conceptual reframing, though it is consistent with existing pruning literature. Limitations include the sensitivity to the threshold hyperparameter, the added complexity of the two-stage progressive strategy, and results that are mixed across Stable Diffusion versions (SD 2.0 and 3.0 show less consistent gains). Open questions include whether the temporal-ineffectiveness observation generalizes to non-diffusion architectures and whether the method scales to the next generation of much larger generative models.
