## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) distills 2D diffusion models for 3D generation by seeking a maximum-likelihood point in the noisy image distribution, which causes over-smoothed textures and limited diversity. While Variational Score Distillation (VSD) addresses this by treating the 3D representation as a random variable, it requires k particles and is k times slower. The paper identifies that the consistency of 2D image flows across different camera views on the 3D object surface is the key missing ingredient for high-quality, diverse 3D generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a frozen pretrained text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v2.1 or MVDream) as input, and produces a differentiable 3D representation (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting, or textured mesh) parameterized by learnable parameters θ. For complex prompts, a two-stage pipeline is used: MVDream is first distilled to establish coarse shape (avoiding multi-face artifacts), then Stable Diffusion refines details and colors. The 3D representation is optimized over ~3 hours on an A100 GPU, with the teacher model operating on 64×64 latent representations (SD) or 32×32 (MVDream), while the consistent noise map uses a 2048×2048 reference space.

## Method

CFD introduces a change-of-variable that transforms the noisy PF-ODE state x_t into a 'clean' variable x̂_t^c = (x_t − σ_t ε̃)/α_t, which is provably a non-noisy image at every timestep and can therefore be directly substituted with rendered 3D views g_θ(c). The key insight is that for consistent flow guidance, the fixed noise ε̃ must be the same on the same region of the 3D object surface across all camera views; the paper implements this via a multi-view consistent Gaussian noise function built on the Noise Transport Equation, which projects query pixels onto the object surface, warps them to a 2D reference space, and aggregates noise values with a 1/√|Ω| normalization to preserve per-pixel i.i.d. Gaussian statistics. The 3D representation is updated by backpropagating the clean-flow gradient (ε_φ(α_t g_θ(c) + σ_t ε̃(θ,c), t, y) − ε̃(θ,c)) through the renderer's Jacobian, with an annealing timestep schedule from t_max to t_min. An SDE extension injects small stochastic noise (rate γ ≈ 0.0001) into ε̃ during optimization, equivalent to a discretized diffusion SDE sampler, which further improves quality over the deterministic ODE case.

## Results

On the main text-to-3D generation benchmark (10 prompts × 10 seeds, Stable Diffusion v2.1 as teacher), CFD achieves a 3D-FID of 78.13 and 3D-CLIP of 35.16±0.23, outperforming all baselines. The 3D-FID improvement is substantial: 9.9 points over VSD (83.02), 17.9 over ISM (86.00), and 9.9 over SDS (88.06). In automated win-rate comparisons under reward models, CFD achieves win rates of 0.54–0.60 on Aesthetics and 0.64–0.78 on PickScore against all baselines, with the largest margin against FSD (0.78 PickScore).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFD outperforms all five baselines on both 3D-FID (quality/diversity) and 3D-CLIP (prompt alignment). Against SDS (DreamFusion), CFD reduces 3D-FID by 9.9 points (78.13 vs. 88.06) while slightly improving 3D-CLIP (35.16 vs. 35.07), with the gap attributable to SDS's random per-step noise causing over-smoothed textures and mode collapse. Against VSD (ProlificDreamer), CFD achieves a 4.9-point 3D-FID advantage (78.13 vs. 83.02) and a marginal 3D-CLIP gain (35.16 vs. 35.10); VSD's single-particle variant is theoretically equivalent to SDS, and its multi-particle variant is k× slower. Against ISM (LucidDreamer), CFD improves 3D-FID by 7.9 points (78.13 vs. 86.00) and 3D-CLIP by 0.17, while avoiding ISM's expensive DDIM inversion step. Against FSD (concurrent work), CFD wins on PickScore (0.78) and Aesthetics (0.54) in win-rate comparisons; FSD aligns noise on a sphere independent of the object surface, which can lead to suboptimal geometry. Against HiFA, CFD is compared qualitatively in Figure 4, showing richer details and more photorealistic results.

## Relation to Prior Work

CFD builds directly on the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) paradigm from DreamFusion, which optimizes a 3D representation by matching rendered views to a 2D diffusion model's maximum-likelihood objective. It departs from SDS's optimization framing by reframing 3D generation as following the diffusion PF-ODE or SDE sampling trajectory, enabled by a change-of-variable that yields a 'clean' (noise-free) variable at every timestep. The multi-view consistent Gaussian noise draws on the Noise Transport Equation from Integral Noise (Chang et al., 2024), generalized from video frame warping to 3D surface correspondence. Concurrent work FSD (Yan et al., 2024) shares the deterministic ODE formulation but aligns noise on a sphere independent of the object surface, making it a special case of CFD with γ=0.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFD provides a theoretically grounded sampling-based alternative to the optimization-based score distillation paradigm, with the clean flow formulation unifying ODE and SDE guidance under a single framework. The multi-view consistent noise is a practical, low-overhead technique (8.1% compute) that addresses a fundamental OOD issue when feeding rendered 3D views into 2D diffusion models. However, generation still takes hours, the multi-face Janus problem persists for complex prompts, and the paper acknowledges that 3D rendered-view flows may not exactly replicate 2D image flows due to geometric interference across views. Open questions include whether the consistent noise principle extends to other distillation settings (e.g., video-to-3D) and whether the two-stage MVDream→SD pipeline can be replaced by a single-stage approach.
