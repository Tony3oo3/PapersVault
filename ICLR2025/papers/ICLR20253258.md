## Problem

Understanding how multiple brain regions jointly support distributed computation requires models that capture both complex nonlinear local dynamics and the temporal structure of inter-area signal propagation (delays, frequency filtering). Existing multi-region approaches either use linear dynamics that miss key nonlinear features like attractor structure, or model communication as instantaneous linear coupling without explicit temporal filtering, leaving a gap between expressive local dynamics and interpretable characterizations of how information flows between regions over time.

## Input / Output

The model takes simultaneous spike-count recordings from K anatomically distinct regions (e.g., V1 and V2) along with optional stimulus/task inputs (cues, go signals) that condition the latent dynamics. It produces per-region nonlinear latent state trajectories (visualizable as flow fields), time-domain impulse responses and frequency-domain transfer functions for each directed inter-region channel, and predicted neural responses. A key structural constraint is that each region's observation depends only on its own latent variables, so the likelihood factorizes across regions; the extended state space also includes deterministic channel-filter states that carry the history of inter-region communication.

## Method

MRDS-IR combines region-specific nonlinear dynamics (parameterized by deep networks using minimally gated units) with directed linear communication channels between regions, where each channel is a finite-order linear filter parameterized by its impulse response via a diagonal (A, B, C) realization with complex-conjugate pole pairs (Gilbert's method). The channel states evolve deterministically (zero process noise), making the full state-space model a hybrid of stochastic and deterministic transitions. For inference, the authors develop a state-noise inversion-free variational filtering algorithm that parameterizes the variational conditional as a product of a Gaussian potential and the prior transition, avoiding the need to invert the (singular) state-noise covariance and enabling the deterministic channel transitions to be treated as degenerate Gaussian (delta) distributions. Training proceeds end-to-end via ELBO maximization with the reparameterization trick; for non-conjugate observation models (e.g., Poisson GLM), conjugate potential updates are amortized through neural networks in the style of structured VAEs, while for linear-Gaussian observations closed-form updates are available.

## Results

On V1/V2 macaque recordings, MRDS-IR consistently achieves the smallest held-out neuron prediction MSE across all 8 stimulus conditions compared to linear (LN), nonlinear (NL), and MRM-GP baselines (Fig. 4E). On the synthetic integration task, MRDS-IR produces substantially tighter R² matches to ground-truth RNN inter-region currents than CURBD, and maintains higher forecast R² over horizons up to 225 time bins. On the rhythmic timing task, MRDS-IR outperforms CCA in R² match to ground-truth channel signals and reveals a low-frequency passband aligned with the target oscillation range plus a high-frequency passband for go-signal transients. On the synthetic recovery experiment, MRDS-IR achieves the best negative ELBO and 150-step forecast R² among all model variants, correctly recovering flow fields, impulse responses, and pruning inactive channels to near-zero amplitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CURBD (Perich et al., 2020) on the integration task, MRDS-IR achieves substantially higher R² for matching ground-truth inter-region RNN currents and higher forecast R² over long horizons (up to 225 bins); critically, CURBD infers comparable-magnitude currents for all region pairs including architecturally absent connections, while MRDS-IR correctly identifies the weak 1→3 link and near-zero closed channels. Against MRM-GP (Li et al., 2024) on V1/V2 data, MRDS-IR achieves lower MSE for held-out neuron predictions across all 8 stimuli conditions (Fig. 4E), with the gap visible particularly at intermediate stimuli. Against DLAG (Gokcen et al., 2022), MRDS-IR is competitive on R² of held-out neuron firing rate (Fig. 4F), and additionally reveals a clear feedforward (early transient V1→V2) versus feedback (ramping V2→V1) temporal structure in channel amplitudes that is absent in DLAG's across-area latents (Suppl. Fig. S1). Against CCA on the rhythmic timing task, MRDS-IR achieves higher R² match to ground-truth channel signals and uniquely exposes the spectral passband structure of the communication filter. Overall, MRDS-IR outperforms or matches all baselines on every reported metric, with the largest margins on communication structure recovery and long-horizon forecasting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRDS-IR builds on the state-space modeling framework for neural population dynamics (Paninski et al., 2010) and the multi-region extensions that followed (gLARA, mp-srLDS, MR-SDS, CURBD), but departs from them by replacing instantaneous or GP-based inter-region coupling with linear filters parameterized by their impulse response, drawing directly from classical linear systems theory. The variational inference machinery is inspired by structured VAEs (Johnson et al., 2016) and the Bayesian learning rule (Khan & Rue, 2023), adapted here into a state-noise inversion-free filtering algorithm that handles the hybrid stochastic/deterministic transitions arising from noiseless channel dynamics. This combination—nonlinear (minimally gated unit) within-region dynamics plus interpretable linear communication channels—occupies a unique position in the multi-region modeling landscape, as no prior method simultaneously offers both expressive local nonlinearities and principled time/frequency-domain characterizations of inter-area signal flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impulse-response parameterization of communication channels provides a principled, interpretable handle on inter-area signal propagation that is absent from most existing multi-region models: one can directly read off communication delays, frequency-selective filtering, and channel openness from the learned parameters, bridging the gap between data-driven latent dynamics and circuit-level neuroscience. The state-noise inversion-free filtering algorithm is a practical technical contribution that simplifies approximate inference in state-space models with deterministic sub-dynamics, a structure common in hybrid neural/linear systems. Open questions include whether the all-to-all connectivity assumption and lack of sparsity regularization limit scalability to more than two regions, whether the linear channel assumption is sufficient for capturing genuinely nonlinear inter-area interactions, and how the method performs on larger-scale multi-region recordings (e.g., Neuropixels 2.0 datasets spanning many areas).
