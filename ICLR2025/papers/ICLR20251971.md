## Problem

Existing multi-modal knowledge graph completion (MMKGC) methods perform entity-level multi-modal fusion without accounting for relational context, producing immutable entity embeddings that underutilize the diverse perspectives available within each modality. Different relations emphasize different aspects of entity information, yet conventional fusion treats all relational contexts uniformly, limiting prediction quality especially under modality noise and incomplete information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251971/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted image features (via VGG, 383–4096 dim) and text features (via BERT, 384–768 dim) per entity, alongside the triple structure (head, relation, tail) which is learned from scratch as an additional 'structure modality.' Output is a joint triple plausibility score computed as the sum of per-modality TuckER tensor-factorization scores (structure, image, text, and a learned joint modality), used to rank all candidate entities for head or tail link prediction under the standard filter setting.

## Method

MoMOK introduces three components: (1) ReMoKE builds K=3 expert networks per modality and fuses their outputs via a relation-guided gated fusion network with tunable Gaussian noise and a learnable relation-aware temperature, producing relation-conditioned modality embeddings; (2) MuJoD fuses these per-modality embeddings through a shared attention vector to form a 'joint' modality, then trains a separate TuckER scorer per modality (including joint) in a multi-task cross-entropy objective; (3) ExID applies CLUB contrastive mutual-information minimization to disentangle the K experts within each modality, with a variational network trained via KL divergence in an alternating fashion. The final inference score sums all modality scores, and the overall loss combines the multi-task KGC objective with a weighted CLUB regularization term.

## Results

MoMOK achieves state-of-the-art results on four MMKG benchmarks, with the most dramatic gains on DB15K (+21.1% MRR, +33.8% Hit@1 over the best baseline MMRNS) and KVC16K (+10.6% MRR, +23.0% Hit@1 over AdaMF). On MKG-W, MoMOK reaches MRR 35.89 and Hit@1 30.38, surpassing the previous best (MMRNS: 35.03/28.59). On MKG-Y, MoMOK attains Hit@1 35.09 (vs. AdaMF's 33.49) though AdaMF edges it slightly on MRR (38.06 vs. 37.91). The paper also reports results on FB15K-237 (MRR 36.08, Hit@1 27.54), outperforming AdaMF (32.56/23.33) and all MoSE variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoMOK outperforms all 20 baselines on Hit@1 across all four main datasets, with the largest margins on DB15K (32.38 vs. 23.01 for MMRNS, +33.8%) and KVC16K (10.53 vs. 8.56 for AdaMF, +23.0%). On MKG-W, MoMOK beats the closest competitor MMRNS by 0.86 MRR and 1.79 Hit@1. On MKG-Y, AdaMF narrowly edges MoMOK on MRR (38.06 vs. 37.91) but MoMOK wins decisively on Hit@1 (35.09 vs. 33.49). The ensemble-based baselines MoSE and IMF, which share a similar multi-model philosophy, are consistently outperformed by MoMOK on all datasets, with MoMOK gaining 11.19 MRR over MoSE on DB15K. On the additional FB15K-237 benchmark, MoMOK (MRR 36.08) surpasses AdaMF (32.56) and all MoSE variants, though MoSE-BI (Hit@1 28.1) slightly exceeds MoMOK's Hit@1 (27.54) using a much larger embedding dimension (2000 vs. 250).

## Relation to Prior Work

MoMOK sits at the intersection of three research threads: multi-modal knowledge graph completion (MMKGC), mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures, and mutual information-based representation disentanglement. It builds directly on TuckER's tensor factorization as the per-modality scoring function, on the sparsely-gated MoE routing mechanism from Shazeer et al. (2017) for relation-guided expert selection, and on CLUB (Cheng et al., 2020) for contrastive mutual information minimization. The key conceptual departure from prior MMKGC methods (which perform entity-level modality fusion agnostic to the query relation) is the introduction of relation-conditioned, intra-modality expert networks that produce different entity representations depending on the relational context, combined with a joint-decision ensemble across modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation—immutable entity embeddings across relational contexts—that plagues existing MMKGC methods, and provides a clean architectural solution with theoretical grounding via the CLUB upper bound. The case study showing that different modalities (image, text, structure) dominate different relation types offers practical interpretability for multi-modal KG systems. However, the framework is scoped to MMKGC on medium-scale datasets, does not integrate with LLM-based KG reasoning, and the MoE design (K=3 experts per modality) has not been tested at significantly larger scale, leaving open whether the relational-context specialization generalizes to very large or very sparse KGs.
