## Problem

Few-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting enhances LLM reasoning, but isolated tokens within demonstrations can distract the model by causing it to 'take things out of context,' incorporating irrelevant values or being implicitly biased toward specific numbers. While prior work identified surface-level factors affecting CoT performance, the internal attention-level mechanism by which specific tokens disrupt reasoning remained unexplored, and no method existed to suppress this distracting effect without simultaneously harming CoT's positive instructional role.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at inference time on standard few-shot CoT prompts, consuming the LLM's already-computed attention matrices at each layer to identify and modify attention weights in-place with no additional forward or backward passes. It produces a modified attention matrix where attention from flagged distracting tokens to the current output position is zeroed out, after which the LLM continues generating reasoning steps and the final answer as usual. The intervention is extremely lightweight—targeting only ~15% of demonstration tokens for Llama-3-8B—and the first token is always excluded from blocking since it serves as an attention sink.

## Method

FAI identifies distracting tokens by computing an aggregation coefficient α for each demonstration token at each layer, defined as the average self-attention score across all heads (a high α indicates the token retains its own semantics without integrating information from other tokens). A token is flagged if α exceeds a threshold τ = λ/index_{t_i}, where the index-based threshold approximates the expected uniform attention distribution and λ is a hyperparameter set to 1. For flagged tokens, FAI sets their attention scores toward the current output position to zero across all heads at that layer, effectively blocking the information flow from semantically isolated tokens to the prediction. This approach uses raw attention scores as a lightweight proxy for gradient-based saliency scores (validated in Appendix A.4), avoiding the expensive backpropagation that would be required to compute true saliency at every generation step.

## Results

FAI consistently improves or maintains accuracy across all six benchmarks (AQuA, GSM8K, CSQA, Date Understanding, Sport Understanding, Last Letter Concatenation) and all four model families tested. The headline result is a 5.91% absolute improvement on AQuA with Llama-3-8B-Instruct (40.94% → 46.85%), while GPT-NEO sees the largest relative gain on AQuA (22.83% → 36.22%, +13.39%). On GSM8K, Llama-3-8B improves from 70.32% to 71.24%, and Llama-3-70B maintains 91.28% while gaining on CSQA (77.31% → 78.62%) and Last Letter (84.00% → 85.33%). FAI intervenes on only ~15% of demonstration tokens (Llama-3-8B), confirming its targeted, lightweight nature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is each model's standard few-shot CoT performance (without FAI) versus the same model with FAI applied. FAI wins or ties on every benchmark across all four model families, with the largest gains on AQuA (up to +13.39% for GPT-NEO, +5.91% for Llama-3-8B) and CSQA (+3.11% for Llama-3-8B). A contrastive ablation that blocks ALL attention from demonstrations to the prediction position (rather than only the identified distracting tokens) also suppresses the distracting effect but causes a substantial accuracy drop on GSM_good and significantly reduces the Rate of Answer Following Rationale (RAFR), confirming that FAI's targeted intervention preserves CoT's positive instructional effect while removing the negative distracting effect. On GSM_bad (samples where errors are likely caused by distraction), FAI yields notable accuracy gains, while on GSM_good (consistently correct samples) accuracy remains essentially unchanged, validating the specificity of the intervention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability line of work on in-context learning (Wang et al., 2023b on label-word anchors; Todd et al., 2023 on function vectors) from single-token-output tasks to the multi-token, multi-step setting of few-shot Chain-of-Thought reasoning. It departs from prior CoT analysis (Wang et al., 2023a; Jin et al., 2024; Madaan & Yazdanbakhsh, 2022), which manipulated demonstration text at the surface level, by instead tracing attention patterns dynamically at each generation step to identify a specific failure mode: isolated tokens that channel their unaggregated local semantics directly to the prediction position. The FAI intervention repurposes the attention-sink concept (Xiao et al., 2023a) and standard attention-weight manipulation into a targeted, per-token blocking strategy that preserves the positive instructional effect of CoT while suppressing the distracting effect.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic account of why specific tokens in CoT demonstrations cause errors, estimating that roughly 60% of errors in a constructed 'bad' test set stem from this distracting effect. The FAI method is lightweight (intervening on ~15% of demonstration tokens) and requires no retraining, but its practical adoption is constrained by the need for white-box access to per-layer attention weights, which is unavailable in most API-based LLM deployments. The modest gains on larger models (Llama-3-70B: 66.14→66.53 on AQuA, 91.28→91.28 on GSM8K) raise open questions about whether the distracting effect diminishes with scale or whether the intervention strategy needs adaptation. Future work should investigate whether similar attention-level interventions can be applied to zero-shot CoT, self-consistency decoding, or other reasoning-augmentation strategies.
