## Problem

Language models fail on unseen or rarely-seen tokens because they over-rely on memorized in-weights strategies, yet prior work on ICL transience (Singh et al., 2023) did not distinguish conditional ICL from structural ICL—strategies invariant to the content of token embeddings. This paper shows that structural ICL is also transient, emerging early in pretraining and vanishing as information is encoded in weights, and that standard L2 regularization does not preserve it.

## Input / Output

The method operates on text sequences in both masked (BERT-style MLM on templatic noun/adj/copula sequences) and autoregressive (GPT-2 on token-label pairs and Wikitext) settings. To test structural ICL, token embeddings at specified positions are replaced with randomly-initialized vectors, forcing the model to rely on contextual structure rather than memorized content. Outputs include predicted tokens (MLM), POS classifications extracted via linear probes on intermediate layers, and syllogism completions from a decoder-only model.

## Method

The core technique is temporary forgetting: during the first N steps of pretraining, the embedding matrix is re-initialized every k steps (active forgetting), after which normal training resumes, allowing in-weights memorization to develop while the structural ICL strategy acquired during the forgetting phase persists. The parameter N coarsely controls the IWL/ICL tradeoff—larger N preserves more structural ICL but less memorization. For fine-tuning pretrained models, probabilistic temporary forgetting replaces ~10% of batch tokens with random embeddings each step and restores the embedding matrix after each gradient update, leaving pretrained weights otherwise intact. Across Zipfian distributions of varying skew, this induces dual process learning where head tokens are handled via IWL and tail or unseen tokens via structural ICL, outperforming both vanilla training and L2 regularization on rare-token generalization.

## Results

In the GPT-2 large fine-tuning experiment, probabilistic temporary forgetting (replacing 10% of batch tokens with random embeddings each step, then restoring the embedding matrix) improves unseen-token syllogism accuracy from 0.02 (pretrained) to 0.927, while baseline (seen-token) syllogism accuracy is maintained at 0.923 (vs. 0.933 pretrained). In the synthetic MLM setting, temporary forgetting (k=1000, N grid-searched) achieves near-perfect random-token accuracy across all Zipf skews (α=1.0–1.5) while retaining ~90% of IWL performance on head tokens, whereas vanilla training drops random-token accuracy to chance and L2 regularization (Singh et al., 2023) fails to recover structural ICL. In the autoregressive transformer replication (Chan et al. task, k=1000, N=8000), temporary forgetting preserves structural ICL and achieves approximately 90% of the IWL accuracy that vanilla training attains on common tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against active forgetting (Chen et al., 2024), the paper's temporary forgetting method strictly dominates: both achieve near-perfect structural ICL (random-token accuracy), but active forgetting completely eliminates in-weights learning (head-token accuracy drops to low levels), whereas temporary forgetting retains approximately 90% of vanilla IWL performance on head tokens while still preserving structural ICL. Against L2 regularization (Singh et al., 2023), which was proposed to mitigate conditional ICL transience, temporary forgetting is clearly superior on the structural ICL axis: L2 regularization fails to recover random-token or tail-token performance (Figure 5, Figure 16), while temporary forgetting boosts tail and random token accuracy from near-zero to near-perfect levels. The paper does not report a head-to-head numerical comparison on a single unified metric across all three methods in one table; instead, the comparison is made across multiple figures (Figures 3, 4, 5, 6, 12, 13, 16) showing that temporary forgetting is the only method that simultaneously maintains both IWL (for common tokens) and structural ICL (for rare/unseen tokens) across a range of distributional skews.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL-vs-IWL framework established by Chan et al. (2022b) and Singh et al. (2023) by introducing a critical distinction: structural ICL (invariant to token embedding content) versus conditional ICL (sensitive to token semantics). Methodologically, it builds directly on Chen et al. (2024)'s active forgetting technique, modifying it into 'temporary forgetting' (forgetting for only N steps) and 'probabilistic temporary forgetting' (stochastic per-step embedding replacement during fine-tuning). The paper departs from Singh et al. (2023) by showing that L2 regularization, which preserves conditional ICL, does not preserve structural ICL, thereby identifying a gap in prior analyses of ICL transience.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that structural ICL is transient across both BERT-style and autoregressive models provides a mechanistic explanation for why LMs fail on undertrained or unseen tokens (the 'glitch token' phenomenon), and the temporary forgetting method offers a simple, tunable knob (parameter N) to balance memorization vs. generalization. However, the evidence is largely from small synthetic models and a proof-of-concept GPT-2 large fine-tuning; whether temporary forgetting integrates into large-scale pretraining curricula or interacts with parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods remains open. The dual-process framing (IWL for head tokens, structural ICL for tail/unseen tokens) is practically relevant for domains with highly skewed token distributions, but broader validation across architectures and scales is needed.
