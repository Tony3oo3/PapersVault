## Problem

Orthogonal Low-Rank Embedding (OLE) has never been successfully used as a standalone training metric in self-supervised learning, despite its promise for multi-view tasks. Two fundamental obstacles prevent direct application: (1) SSL treats each instance as its own class, creating a virtually infinite number of classes that makes inter-class orthogonality impossible and drives representational collapse; and (2) the nuclear norm is insensitive to the signs of cosine similarities, so it cannot distinguish aligned from anti-aligned positive pairs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes B RGB images per batch, each augmented into N views (4 large 224×224 crops and 4 small 96×96 crops with standard SwAV-style augmentations), producing a B×N×d feature tensor via a ResNet backbone and 3-layer MLP projector (d=4096 for ResNet-18, d=8192 for ResNet-50). The loss operates at the matrix level: intra-class terms use N×d sub-matrices (views of one image) while inter-class terms use B×d sub-matrices (one view across all images), making the method inherently multi-view without requiring explicit negative pair enumeration or memory banks.

## Method

SSOLE decouples the low-rank (positive-pair alignment) and high-rank (negative-pair separation) components of OLE into independent loss terms. For low-rank enforcement, it applies the nuclear norm to a deviation matrix (each view's feature minus the stop-gradient mean of that image's views), which preserves the signs of cosine similarities and prevents the nuclear norm from treating anti-aligned vectors identically to aligned ones. For high-rank enforcement, it applies a derived function h₂ of the nuclear norm of the B×d matrix for each view, optimizing both lower and upper bounds on average absolute cosine similarity. The final loss L_SSOLE = (1/B)Σ h₁(‖Z̃_{b,:}‖*/(N-1)²) + λ·(1/N)Σ h₂(‖Z_{:,n}‖*) achieves alignment and uniformity without large batch sizes, EMA teachers, or memory banks, and is notably insensitive to the balance hyperparameter λ (stable across 0.5–1.9).

## Results

On full ImageNet (ResNet-50, 200 epochs, 8 crops), SSOLE achieves 73.9% Top-1 and 91.7% Top-5 linear probing accuracy, outperforming all single-encoder baselines. In semi-supervised settings, SSOLE reaches 55.4% Top-1 (1% labels) and 70.3% Top-1 (10% labels), surpassing LORAC by over 5 points at 1% and ~2 points at 10%. On ImageNet-100 (ResNet-18, 100 epochs), SSOLE attains 82.5% Top-1, the highest among all compared methods, while using only batch size 128 and a single-branch encoder without memory banks or EMA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSOLE outperforms all single-encoder baselines on full ImageNet linear probing: it beats LORAC (73.2%), Barlow Twins (73.2%), VICReg (73.2%), BYOL (73.2%), SwAV (72.7%), SimSiam (71.3%), MoCo v2 (71.1%), and SimCLR (69.3%) by 0.7–4.6 points. In semi-supervised learning, SSOLE's advantage is most pronounced against LORAC, gaining +5.4 points at 1% labels (55.4% vs 50.0%) and +2.3 points at 10% labels (70.3% vs 68.0%), demonstrating that using OLE as an intrinsic metric rather than a regularization prior yields substantially better label efficiency. SSOLE slightly trails INTL with EMA (74.3% vs 73.9% linear probing) but outperforms INTL without EMA (73.1%) and beats INTL on both semi-supervised settings (55.4 vs 55.0 at 1%; 70.3 vs 69.4 at 10%). On ImageNet-100, SSOLE achieves 82.5% Top-1, surpassing INTL (81.7%), BYOL (80.2%), VICReg (79.2%), EMP-SSL (78.9%), LORAC (78.7%), and MoCo-M (77.0%) while using fewer forward passes (8 vs 10 for LORAC/MoCo-M) and no memory bank or EMA teacher. On transfer tasks (COCO detection, CIFAR-10/100, Aircraft, DTD, Flowers), SSOLE consistently achieves the best or near-best results, e.g., 41.3 AP on COCO detection and 92.2% on CIFAR-10 linear transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSOLE directly extends Orthogonal Low-Rank Embedding (OLE) from supervised classification (Lezama et al., 2018; Qiu & Sapiro, 2015) into the self-supervised setting, where prior work had only partially exploited OLE's components: LORAC used low-rank constraints as a regularization term within MoCo, while VICReg, TCR, and W-MSE enforced high-rank/orthogonality constraints but neglected low-rank alignment of positive pairs. The key conceptual departure is identifying two fundamental obstacles—virtually infinite 'classes' causing representational collapse, and the nuclear norm's insensitivity to cosine-sign—that prevented OLE from serving as a standalone SSL metric, and then resolving both through decoupled enforcement and deviation-matrix low-rank constraints. The method also draws on the alignment/uniformity decomposition of Wang & Isola (2020) by showing that nuclear norms provide explicit bounds on average cosine similarity, thereby connecting matrix-level rank optimization to contrastive objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSOLE demonstrates that matrix-level nuclear-norm objectives can serve as a primary (not auxiliary) SSL loss, opening a path for richer geometric constraints in representation learning beyond pairwise contrastive or variance-based regularizers. Practically, it achieves competitive accuracy on ImageNet-100 (82.5%) and full ImageNet without large batch sizes, memory banks, or dual-encoder architectures, which reduces computational overhead for practitioners. However, the method has only been validated on ResNet-18/50 backbones; its compatibility with Vision Transformers and Swin Transformers remains unexplored, and the multi-crop gradient-stopping strategy adds implementation complexity that may limit adoption. A broader open question is whether the deviation-matrix trick generalizes to other matrix-norm-based objectives (e.g., log-det, coding rate) in SSL.
