## Problem

Biological brains organize neurons so that spatially nearby cells share similar functions, but artificial neural networks lack this topographic structure. Prior efforts to induce topography into ANNs either reorganized pretrained weights post-hoc (leaving representations unchanged) or added topographic losses during training that caused large drops in task performance. No unified inductive bias existed that could deliver high-performing topographic models across both convolutional and transformer architectures and across vision and language domains.

## Input / Output

For vision, the models consume standard RGB images (ImageNet at 224×224) and output 1000-way class labels; for language, they consume token sequences (Wikipedia or 10B tokens from FineWeb-Edu) and output next-token predictions. The TopoLoss itself operates on the internal weight tensors rather than on input/output data: each linear or convolutional layer's weight matrix is reshaped into a 2D cortical sheet (area = number of output units, depth = number of input units), and the loss is computed on these reshaped weight maps. For transformers, topography is applied specifically to the c_fc feed-forward layers, which prior work identifies as key-value memory modules storing persistent world knowledge.

## Method

TopoLoss promotes topographic organization by maximizing the cosine similarity between each layer's cortical sheet and a blurred version of itself, where blurring is implemented as a downsample-by-3 followed by an upsample-back operation along the sheet's height and width dimensions. This suppresses high-frequency spatial noise in the weight map, analogous to how synaptic pruning in the brain eliminates noisy connections to retain only meaningful ones. The total training objective is L_total = L_training + τ·L_topo, where τ controls the strength of the topographic constraint. The loss is applied to every convolutional layer in ResNet residual blocks, to the c_fc layers in GPT-Neo-125M and NanoGPT transformer blocks, and to the MLP modules in ViT-b32, making it architecture- and domain-agnostic. The resulting TopoNets achieve comparable topography to prior methods (TDANN, LLCNN) while substantially outperforming them on ImageNet accuracy and BrainScore neural prediction, and they exhibit reduced effective dimensionality, greater resilience to L1 pruning and weight downsampling, and brain-like topographic signatures including category-selective maps and temporal integration window clusters.

## Results

On ImageNet, TopoNet-ResNet-18 (τ=10) achieves BrainScore values of 0.7116 (V1), 0.3038 (V2), 0.2923 (V4), and 0.5723 (IT), outperforming TDANN (0.6932, 0.1775, 0.2792, 0.4259) across all four visual regions. On the Natural Scenes Dataset, TopoNet-ResNet-50 reaches a unit-to-voxel correlation of R=0.63 (normalized to noise ceiling), compared to R=0.54 for TDANN. For language models, TopoNet-GPT-Neo-125M maintains BLiMP scores comparable to the non-topographic baseline, while downsampling topographic layers by 80% reduces parameters from 125.6M to 102.3M (19% reduction) with minimal performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TDANN (Margalit et al., 2024), TopoNets win on both engineering and neural metrics: TopoNet-ResNet-18 achieves higher ImageNet accuracy (only 3% drop from baseline when topography is applied in the same 8 locations as TDANN, versus TDANN's 6% drop) and higher BrainScore across all four visual regions (e.g., IT: 0.5723 vs. 0.4259). Against LLCNN (Qian et al., 2024), even the worst-performing TopoNet-ResNet-18 (τ=50) shows a 25% accuracy drop from baseline, substantially better than LLCNN-G's 41% drop, while achieving comparable smoothness (topography) levels. Against Topoformer (Binhuraib et al., 2024), which applied topography to BERT self-attention maps, TopoNets achieve higher levels of measured topography in the output space and are the first to demonstrate topographic organization in the feed-forward (c_fc) layers of autoregressive language models. TopoNets also outperform TDANN on the Natural Scenes Dataset (R=0.60–0.63 vs. R=0.54) and replicate brain-like topographic signatures (category selectivity, temporal integration windows) that prior topographic models did not capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior topography-inducing methods (TDANN, LLCNN, Topoformer) that either matched the brain's spatial correlation structure, imposed distance-dependent constraints, or encouraged information redundancy, all of which suffered significant performance trade-offs. TopoLoss instead draws on the neuroscience of synaptic pruning, using a simple blur-and-cosine-similarity objective on a 2D cortical sheet to suppress high-frequency weight noise. While the cortical sheet formalism is borrowed from Qian et al. (2024), the loss design is novel, and the paper is the first to apply a unified topographic inductive bias across both convolutional and transformer architectures in both vision and language domains, where prior work was largely confined to ResNet-18 vision models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves an open theoretical question by showing that topography itself (not reduced task performance) drives effective dimensionality reduction in learned representations, across both CNNs and transformers. Practically, TopoLoss is a 2–3 line addition that yields models more resilient to L1 pruning and weight downsampling, suggesting a path toward compressing large language models without sacrificing accuracy. Key limitations include reliance on relatively small backbones (ResNet-18, GPT-Neo-125M) chosen for comparability with prior work, an incomplete understanding of how the scaling factor τ interacts with model scale and dataset complexity, and the absence of scaling to frontier architectures like LLaMA. Open questions include whether 'scaling laws for topographic networks' exist and how topographic constraints interact with modern fine-tuning paradigms at scale.
