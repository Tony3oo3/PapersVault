## Problem

Existing video captioning benchmarks (MSR-VTT, MSVD) and their ground-truth annotations describe isolated events or actions without encoding causal-temporal narrative — the cause-effect relationships and temporal sequences that drive video content. Consequently, SOTA captioning models trained on these captions cannot articulate the causal and temporal dynamics inherent in videos, limiting their utility for narrative understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251211/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The CEN model takes raw video frames (sampled at 1 fps, max 20 frames) without requiring explicit cause-effect temporal segmentation, and produces a single coherent caption structured as a Cause statement (~10 words) followed by an Effect statement (~9 words), averaging 19 words on MSRVTT-CTN and 17 on MSVD-CTN. The CTN benchmark itself is generated by feeding original multi-caption annotations into a Mixtral of Experts LLM via few-shot prompting, with quality enforced by an EMScore threshold (≥0.2) and iterative regeneration.

## Method

NarrativeBridge comprises a CTN captions benchmark and a two-stage Cause-Effect Network (CEN). In Stage 1, two separate CLIP-ViT (ViT-B/32) encoders — E_cause and E_effect — are each trained with contrastive loss against the cause or effect portion of the CTN caption, respectively, while both process the full unsegmented video; this yields specialized causal and effect video representations. In Stage 2, the frozen Stage 1 features are passed through two independent two-layer transformer encoders (initialized from Uni-VL), concatenated, and fed to a two-layer decoder that autoregressively generates the final CTN caption via cross-entropy loss. The key architectural insight is that separating cause and effect encoding into dedicated pathways — without requiring explicit temporal boundaries in the video — enables the model to learn distinct causal and consequential dynamics that a single unified encoder cannot capture, as confirmed by ablations showing consistent performance drops when encoders are merged or fine-tuning is removed.

## Results

CEN achieves 63.51 CIDEr and 31.46 ROUGE-L on MSVD-CTN, and 49.87 CIDEr and 27.90 ROUGE-L on MSRVTT-CTN, outperforming all SOTA video captioning methods and fine-tuned VLMs across all three metrics (ROUGE-L, CIDEr, SPICE) on both datasets. The largest margin is in CIDEr, where CEN surpasses the next-best method (GIT) by 17.88 points on MSVD-CTN and 17.44 points on MSRVTT-CTN. Even after fine-tuning, VideoLLaVA and ShareGPT4Video trail CEN by 27–32 CIDEr points, underscoring the benefit of the dedicated cause-effect encoder architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CEN decisively outperforms all five compared baselines on every metric and both datasets. Against the strongest SOTA captioning method, GIT, CEN gains 17.88 CIDEr (63.51 vs. 45.63) on MSVD-CTN and 17.44 CIDEr (49.87 vs. 32.43) on MSRVTT-CTN, with similar margins in ROUGE-L (+3.95 / +3.39) and SPICE (+3.67 / +2.06). The LSTM-based SEM-POS and GNN-based AKGNN trail CEN by 26–28 CIDEr points on MSVD-CTN and 24 CIDEr on MSRVTT-CTN. Among VLMs, even the best fine-tuned variants (VideoLLaVA Simple FT at 36.12 CIDEr and ShareGPT4Video Simple FT at 31.67 CIDEr on MSVD-CTN) fall 27–32 CIDEr points short of CEN, and the gap widens further on MSRVTT-CTN where CEN reaches 49.87 versus 19.98 and 20.76 respectively. Zero-shot VLM performance is notably weaker still (16–31 CIDEr), confirming that general-purpose vision-language models struggle to articulate causal-temporal narratives without task-specific architectural support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard video captioning (MSR-VTT, MSVD) by introducing causal-temporal narrative as an explicit annotation and modeling dimension, building on CLIP/CLIP4Clip contrastive pre-training for Stage 1 and Uni-VL seq2seq transformers for Stage 2. It departs from prior SOTA captioning architectures (SEM-POS, AKGNN, GIT) and recent VLMs (VideoLLaVA, ShareGPT4Video) by using dedicated cause and effect encoders rather than a single unified video representation, and by generating training captions via LLM few-shot prompting (Mixtral of Experts) rather than relying solely on human annotations. The approach also draws on causal inference ideas from image captioning (Liu et al., 2022) and VQA (Xue et al., 2023), but applies them to the full video captioning setting with a new benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CTN benchmark is the first to explicitly encode cause-effect temporal relationships in video captions, providing a concrete testbed for evaluating whether models truly understand narrative causality rather than just describing isolated actions. The large CIDEr gap over fine-tuned VLMs suggests that general-purpose vision-language models do not naturally capture causal-temporal structure, pointing to a genuine capability gap. However, the benchmark is self-generated by an LLM (validated by human evaluation but still synthetic), complex multi-agent causal chains remain unaddressed, and the two-stage CEN architecture is relatively straightforward, limiting broader architectural impact. Open questions include whether the CTN formulation generalizes to longer videos, whether the cause/effect split is the right granularity, and whether the approach transfers to other narrative-rich domains like film or sports.
