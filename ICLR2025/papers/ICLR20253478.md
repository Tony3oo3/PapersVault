## Problem

Message-passing GNNs capture local relationships but fail on long-range dependencies due to over-smoothing and over-squashing, while graph transformers enable global information exchange but compress graph topology into fixed-length vectors, losing structural detail. Existing walk-based methods like CRaWL partially address this but rely on small-kernel CNNs that cannot approximate arbitrary functions on walks and lack message passing to capture local substructure efficiently.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with node and edge feature vectors and samples m non-backtracking random walks of fixed length ℓ (with m controlled by a sampling rate γ = m/n), augmenting each walk with identity and adjacency positional encodings that capture the full induced subgraph along the walk. It produces both node-level and graph-level representations, with the sampling rate and walk length serving as explicit knobs to trade off expressivity against computational cost (complexity O(kdn(γℓ+β))). At inference, the sampling rate is fixed at 1.0 to maximize graph coverage, and the model scales to graphs with up to 1.6M nodes on a single H100 GPU.

## Method

NeuralWalker stacks encoder blocks, each comprising: (1) a walk embedder that concatenates node/edge embeddings with positional encodings along each sampled walk, (2) a sequence layer (bidirectional Mamba, S4, 1D CNN, or Transformer) that processes the walk as a sequence to capture long-range dependencies within it, (3) a walk aggregator that pools walk features back into node features via average pooling over all walks passing through each node, and (4) a local message-passing layer (GIN) plus optional global message passing (virtual node or transformer). The key insight is treating random walks as sequences so that state-space models—particularly bidirectional Mamba—can be applied directly, avoiding the information loss of encoding walks into fixed-length vectors. Theoretically, the combination of random walks and message passing is provably strictly more expressive than 1-WL and the (⌊ℓ/2⌋+1)-subgraph isomorphism test, and the model is Lipschitz continuous with respect to a metric on the space of walk feature vector distributions.

## Results

On the Long-Range Graph Benchmark (LRGB), NeuralWalker achieves 0.4912 F1 on PascalVOC-SP and 0.4398 F1 on COCO-SP, outperforming the best baselines (GPS-Tuned at 0.4440 and GMN at 0.3974) by 10% and 13% respectively. On the Benchmarking GNNs suite, NeuralWalker attains SOTA on 3 of 5 datasets (ZINC: 0.053 MAE, MNIST: 98.69% ACC, CIFAR-10: 76.90% ACC) and matches the best on the remaining two. On large-scale node classification (up to 1.6M nodes on Pokec), NeuralWalker matches or slightly exceeds Polynormer, the current SOTA, while using very long walks (up to 1,000 steps) with a low sampling rate (≤0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuralWalker decisively outperforms all baselines on the two most challenging LRGB datasets: it beats GPS-Tuned (0.4440) by 10% on PascalVOC-SP and GMN (0.3974) by 13% on COCO-SP, confirming the benefit of combining random walks with message passing for long-range dependencies. On the standard Benchmarking GNNs suite, NeuralWalker surpasses GRIT (the previous best on PATTERN/CLUSTER) on ZINC, MNIST, and CIFAR-10, while GRIT retains a small edge on the two small synthetic datasets (PATTERN: 87.20 vs 86.98; CLUSTER: 80.03 vs 78.19). Against the closest walk-based competitor CRaWL, NeuralWalker achieves substantially better results (e.g., 0.053 vs 0.085 MAE on ZINC; 76.90% vs 69.01% ACC on CIFAR-10), attributed to the addition of message passing and the Mamba sequence layer. On large-graph node classification, NeuralWalker matches or marginally exceeds Polynormer (e.g., 92.92% vs 92.55% on Roman-Empire; 86.46% vs 86.10% on Pokec) while replacing Polynormer's transformer layer with scalable random-walk-based sequence modeling. On OGB, NeuralWalker achieves SOTA on OGBG-MolPCBA (0.3086 AP) and OGBG-CODE2 (0.1957 F1) but trails GPS on OGBG-PPA (0.7888 vs 0.8015 ACC), where overfitting with multiple blocks was observed.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuralWalker extends the random-walk-based graph representation paradigm initiated by CRaWL (Tönshoff et al., 2023b), which first proposed treating a graph as a set of non-backtracking walks with positional encodings. The key departures are twofold: (1) replacing CRaWL's convolutional walk processor with general-purpose sequence models (notably bidirectional Mamba), which removes the receptive-field limitation of small-kernel CNNs, and (2) integrating local GIN message passing and optional global message passing (virtual node or transformer layers) to complement the depth-first exploration of walks with breadth-first neighborhood aggregation. The paper also positions itself against graph transformers, arguing that compressing graph topology into fixed-length positional/structural vectors inherently loses structural information that sequential walk representations preserve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of random walks with message passing yields a provably more expressive model than either 1-WL or the subgraph isomorphism test, and the linear-complexity Mamba backbone enables scaling to graphs with 1.6M nodes on a single GPU — a practical milestone for long-range graph learning. Open questions remain around optimal walk sampling strategies for large graphs (current uniform sampling may not achieve ideal coverage), the scarcity of benchmark datasets with well-defined long-range dependencies (only 2 of 5 LRGB datasets appear to truly require them), and whether self-supervised pretraining on walk sequences (demonstrated via masked positional encoding) can be extended to unlabeled graph settings. The finding that transformers underperform SSMs on walk sequences, if confirmed more broadly, could shift the default sequence-model choice for graph-structured sequential data.
