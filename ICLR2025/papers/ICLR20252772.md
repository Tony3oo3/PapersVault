## Problem

Standard empirical risk minimization trains neural networks only on discrete training points, leaving the loss landscape between and beyond those points unconstrained. This is particularly problematic under distribution shift and group imbalance, where the model's behavior in underrepresented regions is unpredictable. The paper asks whether one can impose a principled, PDE-based regularity condition on the loss landscape that yields provable bounds on interior loss and controlled behavior under shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252772/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the joint feature-label space (X, y) ∈ D_X × D_y, where X can be RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for WaterBirds/CelebA/Camelyon17) or tabular vectors, and y is a class label or continuous regression target. During training, the loss is not evaluated only at the original data points but at intermediate points sampled along Brownian bridge paths connecting pairs of data points in this joint space, with the bridge endpoints sampled inversely proportional to pairwise distance. The final output remains a standard class prediction or regression value; the elliptic constraint only modifies the training objective.

## Method

The paper formulates the loss landscape u(X,y) as the solution to an elliptic PDE (σ∇²u = 0) with boundary conditions u = ℓ(f_θ(X), y) on the training set, then uses the Feynman-Kac formula to express the solution as an expectation over Brownian motion hitting times. For tractable computation, this is approximated by sampling Brownian bridges between pairs of data points (endpoints drawn inversely proportional to distance) and minimizing the loss integrated along the bridge path, which by Dynkin's formula exactly recovers the PDE solution at interior points. An importance weighting term derived from Girsanov's theorem (drift b = ξ∇ℓ) is added to reweight sample paths toward underrepresented groups, connecting the method to focal-loss-style reweighting. The maximum principle of elliptic operators then guarantees that the loss at any interior point is bounded between the minimum and maximum training losses, providing a certifiable bound on generalization error within the domain.

## Results

On robust classification (Table 4), Elliptic+IW achieves 92.0% average and 84.1% worst-group accuracy on WaterBirds, 91.3% average on CelebA, and 77.9% average on Camelyon17, outperforming all one-stage baselines (Focal Loss, DRO, DORO) by 5–10 points on worst-group metrics. On balanced classification, Elliptic achieves 18.95% error on CIFAR-100 (PreActRN50) and 70.28% top-1 on Tiny-ImageNet (PreActRN50), surpassing mixupE. On class-imbalanced Med-MNIST with 50% label noise, Elliptic improves worst-class accuracy by 20–35 points over ERM (e.g., 67.6% vs 32.4% on BreastMNIST).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla mixup (Zhang et al., 2017), Elliptic substantially outperforms on Tiny-ImageNet (70.28% vs 54.86% top-1 with PreActRN50) and CIFAR-100 (17.71% vs 19.38% error with Wide-RN28), though mixup is marginally better on CIFAR-10 (3.03% vs 3.09% error). Against mixupE (Zou et al., 2023), Elliptic wins on CIFAR-100 (18.95% vs 20.23% error) and all Tiny-ImageNet architectures, but mixupE is slightly better on CIFAR-10 (3.35% vs 3.56% error with PreActRN101). In regression, Elliptic marginally beats c-mixup (Yao et al., 2022a) on Airfoil (2.85 vs 2.88 RMSE) and is comparable on NO2 and domain-shift datasets. For robust classification, Elliptic+IW dominates all one-stage baselines: it improves worst-group accuracy on WaterBirds by 7–11 points over Focal Loss, DRO, and DORO variants, and achieves the best Camelyon17 accuracy (77.9%) among all methods including two-stage JTT (69.1%) and UMIX (75.1%). Compared to two-stage UMIX, Elliptic+IW is competitive on WaterBirds (84.1% vs 90.0% worst-group) but superior on Camelyon17, while requiring only a single training pass. Against group-informed methods (Table 11), Elliptic+IW matches or exceeds GroupDRO and LISA on WaterBirds average accuracy and achieves the best Camelyon17 result (77.9% vs 77.1% LISA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by importing elliptic PDE theory (Feynman-Kac formula, Girsanov's theorem, Dynkin's formula) into the data-augmentation-as-regularization framework established by mixup and its variants (mixupE, c-mixup). Rather than interpolating between data points with a fixed distribution (as mixup does) or imposing directional derivative constraints (as mixupE does), the authors require the loss landscape to satisfy a Laplace-type elliptic operator, with the Brownian bridge serving as the concrete augmentation that enforces this PDE constraint. The importance weighting via the Radon-Nikodym derivative connects to focal loss and DRO-style reweighting, but is derived from the stochastic representation of the PDE rather than chosen heuristically. The method is positioned as group-oblivious, competing with both single-stage (focal loss, DRO) and two-stage (JTT, UMIX) robust training algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The maximum principle guarantee (Proposition 1) provides a principled bound on interior loss that mixup-family methods lack, giving practitioners a theoretical handle on expected generalization error for unseen points. The PDE perspective also yields qualitative predictions about behavior under affine distribution shifts and class imbalance (Propositions 2–3), though these are currently proven only for two-layer ReLU networks. Key limitations include the computational overhead of sampling Brownian bridges, the numerical error from discretizing the continuous process, and the gap between the simple theoretical setting and the deep architectures used empirically. Open questions flagged by the authors include studying operator coefficients from a stochastic control perspective, extending to parabolic PDEs for other learning regimes, and generalizing to data on manifolds.
