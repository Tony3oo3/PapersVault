## Problem

Existing CLIP debiasing methods suffer from two critical limitations: they strip attribute information even when explicitly stated in the input (e.g., 'a female doctor' loses gender), and they require protected attribute annotations for training, restricting them to scarce face-centric datasets like FairFace. SANER addresses both gaps simultaneously by debiasing only attribute-neutral descriptions while remaining annotation-free.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253504/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, SANER consumes image-caption pairs from any dataset (e.g., 170K COCO captions containing person-related words) without requiring attribute labels. At inference, a text description is encoded by the frozen CLIP text encoder (ViT-B/16) and then passed through a small two-layer MLP debiasing layer to produce a modified text feature vector in the same 768-d space. The debiased features are then used downstream for text-to-image retrieval (MaxSkew evaluation) or swapped into Stable Diffusion's text encoder for generation.

## Method

SANER operates in four stages: (1) attribute neutralization replaces attribute-specific terms in text with neutral ones (e.g., 'woman'→'person'); (2) a learnable MLP debiasing layer r amends the CLIP text feature of the neutralized text via h(ξ_n(t)) = z(ξ_n(t)) + r(z(ξ_n(t))); (3) an annotation-free debiasing loss minimizes the standard deviation of cosine similarities between the debiased neutral feature and CLIP features of all attribute-specific variants (e.g., 'a woman is eating salad' vs. 'a man is eating salad'), forcing equidistance across attribute groups; (4) reconstruction and contrastive regularization losses preserve original CLIP semantics and image-text alignment. The key design choice is that only neutral descriptions are debiased—attribute-specific descriptions retain their original features—so explicit gender/age/race information is preserved. Training uses a single A100 GPU for 5 hours on COCO captions, with all CLIP weights frozen.

## Results

On text-to-image retrieval, SANER achieves the lowest MaxSkew@1000 across all concept types on both FairFace (8.9/14.5/7.7 for adjective/occupation/activity) and PATA (5.4/9.5/3.3), substantially outperforming the original CLIP (22.9/33.7/19.5 on FairFace). For text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion, SANER reduces gender bias (statistical parity) from 0.51 (original) to 0.39 while retaining 100% gender accuracy for attribute-specific prompts, whereas projection-based debiasing drops accuracy to 58%/79%. Zero-shot ImageNet-1K accuracy is preserved at 65.2% (vs. 65.4% original), confirming no degradation in general classification performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SANER outperforms all compared baselines on the primary gender bias metric (MaxSkew@1000) across both FairFace and PATA. Against prompt tuning (Berg et al., 2022), SANER reduces FairFace occupation bias from 29.9 to 14.5 and activity bias from 20.0 to 7.7, while also maintaining ImageNet accuracy (65.2% vs. 64.1%). Against projection-based debiasing (Chuang et al., 2023), SANER achieves lower MaxSkew on all six FairFace/PATA gender sub-metrics and, critically, preserves 100% gender accuracy for attribute-specific prompts in text-to-image generation (vs. 58%/79% for projection), demonstrating that SANER removes bias without erasing explicitly stated attribute information. For text-to-image generation, SANER reduces statistical parity from 0.51 (original Stable Diffusion) to 0.39, outperforming projection-based debiasing (0.47). On BLIP (Li et al., 2023), SANER also achieves the lowest MaxSkew (e.g., 10.9 vs. 12.8 original and 17.4 projection on FairFace activity), confirming generalizability beyond CLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

SANER builds directly on CLIP's text encoder and is conceptually inspired by ARL (Seth et al., 2023) in using a residual MLP to amend features, but departs fundamentally in its training signal: instead of adversarial losses requiring attribute annotations, it uses an annotation-free equidistance loss that only modifies features for attribute-neutral descriptions. It positions itself against two established debiasing families—adversarial (Berg et al., 2022; Seth et al., 2023; Dehdashtian et al., 2024) and projection-based (Chuang et al., 2023)—identifying that both either require scarce annotated datasets or destroy explicitly stated attribute information. The key conceptual departure is the attribute neutralization step, which restricts debiasing to neutral descriptions while leaving attribute-specific ones untouched, a design choice absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SANER's annotation-free design makes debiasing accessible with any standard image-text dataset (e.g., COCO), removing the dependency on ethically sensitive, face-centric annotated datasets like FairFace. The preservation of explicit attribute information is practically critical for generative applications: projection-based debiasing causes Stable Diffusion to generate male images for 'a female doctor,' while SANER maintains 100% gender accuracy. Open questions remain around debiasing the image encoder, handling intersectional biases (gender × age × race), extending to non-binary attributes, and whether the predefined word-list approach generalizes to more nuanced or context-dependent bias expressions.
