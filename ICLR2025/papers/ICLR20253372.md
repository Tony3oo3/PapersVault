## Problem

Object-centric representation learning methods built on pre-trained encoders like DINO have been shown to work on real-world images, but their zero-shot transferability to unseen object categories and datasets has not been systematically studied. Additionally, prior work (DINOSAUR) reported that unfreezing the pre-trained encoder during object-centric training causes collapse, leaving the encoder features sub-optimal for the object discovery task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253372/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images at 224×224 (training) or 518×518 (high-resolution adaptation) as input, encoded by a ViT-S/14 or ViT-B/14 DINOv2 backbone into patch-level feature vectors. It produces 7 object-centric slot vectors (256-dim each) via slot attention, which are decoded into per-patch segmentation masks and reconstructed DINOv2 features. The fixed number of slots means the model always outputs exactly 7 masks regardless of the true object count, a key limitation compared to variable-mask methods like SAM.

## Method

FT-DINOSAUR finetunes the DINOv2 encoder end-to-end for object discovery by introducing a frozen target encoder (a copy of the original) as the reconstruction objective, sidestepping the collapse problem identified in DINOSAUR. Stability is achieved through blockwise exponentially decaying learning rates (decay 0.85) on the encoder, a cosine LR schedule, lower gradient clipping (0.1), and encoder-specific weight decay. A novel top-k decoding scheme reduces decoder cost from O(N·C) to O(N) by only reconstructing the k most active slots per patch (using slot attention masks as a proxy for occupation probabilities). A short high-resolution adaptation stage (224→518, 10k steps) further improves mask quality. The paper also introduces a 7-dataset zero-shot benchmark (CLEVRTEX, SCANNET, YCB, MOVi-C, MOVi-E, ENTITYSEG, plus PASCAL VOC for analysis) to systematically evaluate transfer, finding that training on complex natural data (COCO) yields the strongest zero-shot generalization.

## Results

On COCO, FT-DINOSAUR (ViT-B/14) achieves 48.8 FG-ARI, 36.3 mBO, 49.4 P-ARI, and 23.9 PQ, setting a new state-of-the-art for unsupervised object-centric learning. In the zero-shot benchmark across 6 unseen datasets (MOVi-C, MOVi-E, SCANNET, YCB, CLEVRTEX, ENTITYSEG), FT-DINOSAUR achieves the highest average FG-ARI (71.3) and mBO (41.1) among all unsupervised methods. The method also outperforms the comparable SAM baseline (ViT-Base, fixed masks) on all metrics, and even exceeds SAM (ViT-Huge, variable masks) on FG-ARI (48.8 vs. 44.9) and PQ (23.9 vs. 10.9) on COCO, though SAM retains a large mBO advantage (56.9 vs. 36.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FT-DINOSAUR outperforms all prior unsupervised object-centric methods on COCO: it beats DINOSAUR by +8.3 FG-ARI and +8.6 mBO, SlotDiffusion by +11.5 FG-ARI and +4.9 mBO, and SPOT by +11.8 FG-ARI and +1.5 mBO, though SPOT retains a slight edge on P-ARI (52.4 vs. 49.4). Against the supervised SAM baselines, FT-DINOSAUR dominates the comparable SAM (ViT-Base, fixed masks) on every metric (e.g., 48.8 vs. 12.1 FG-ARI), and even surpasses the much larger SAM (ViT-Huge, variable masks) on FG-ARI (48.8 vs. 44.9) and PQ (23.9 vs. 10.9), while SAM retains a substantial mBO lead (56.9 vs. 36.3) attributed to its variable number of masks and higher mask quality. In the zero-shot setting, FT-DINOSAUR achieves the best average FG-ARI (71.3) among unsupervised methods, surpassing DINOSAUR (68.4), SlotDiffusion (65.8), and SPOT (55.4), while SAM (best.) still leads with 79.5 FG-ARI and 73.2 mBO. Notably, zero-shot transfer from COCO matches or even exceeds in-distribution training on several datasets (e.g., MOVi-C, YCB), indicating that task-specific finetuning adapts the model to the task rather than overfitting to the data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DINOSAUR (Seitzer et al., 2023), which established the paradigm of applying slot attention to frozen pre-trained ViT encoders (DINO/DINOv2) for unsupervised object discovery on real-world images. The key conceptual departure is that DINOSAUR's authors reported that unfreezing the encoder causes collapse, so all subsequent real-world methods (SlotDiffusion, SPOT) also kept encoders frozen. This paper challenges that assumption by introducing a target encoder (a frozen copy of the original) to stabilize finetuning, combined with blockwise learning rate decay and improved hyperparameters, enabling end-to-end adaptation of the encoder for object discovery. The paper also introduces a 7-dataset zero-shot transfer benchmark, extending prior evaluation protocols that typically tested only single-factor distribution shifts on synthetic data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot benchmark provides the community with a standardized way to evaluate whether object-centric models can serve as foundation models for scene decomposition, a question that was previously unaddressed. The finding that training on complex natural data (COCO) yields zero-shot performance comparable to or better than in-distribution training on several datasets is practically important, suggesting a single model could generalize broadly. However, significant gaps remain: the model still uses a fixed number of slots (limiting it on scenes with variable object counts), the mBO gap to SAM is large (30+ points), and the paper reveals that current object-centric models are sample-efficient but fail to benefit from larger datasets, raising open questions about how to achieve meaningful data scaling. Extending the benchmark to zero-shot downstream tasks (robotics, VQA, dynamics prediction) is identified as a critical next step.
