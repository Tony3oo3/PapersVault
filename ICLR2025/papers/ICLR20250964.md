## Problem

Standard Bayesian Optimization is intractable over large combinatorial unstructured spaces (e.g., protein sequences of length 300 with 20 amino acid choices per site, yielding 20^300 candidates) because maximizing the acquisition function requires exhaustive search. Existing combinatorial BO methods either demand parametric surrogates with domain-specific interaction specifications or, like probabilistic reparameterization (PR), still require combinatorially many samples to estimate the expected acquisition function. This paper addresses the gap of tractable acquisition function optimization in such spaces without structural assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a GP prior (RBF/ARD kernel with ESM-1v protein language model embeddings for categorical amino acid inputs), an initial dataset of evaluated protein sequences, and a combinatorial search space defined as the Cartesian product of n discrete variable sets (each of size 20). At each iteration it outputs a batch of B protein sequences selected as the top-B game equilibria of the UCB function, along with the updated GP posterior. A key structural detail is that the search space is unstructured (lacking a lattice), so the method cannot rely on gradient-based or grid-search maximization and instead decomposes the joint optimization into per-variable best-response or multiplicative-weight updates.

## Method

GAMEOPT defines a cooperative game at each BO iteration where n players (one per discrete variable/protein site) jointly maximize the GP's UCB acquisition function as their shared reward. Equilibria of this game—computed via Iterative Best Response (sequential round-robin best responses) or HEDGE (simultaneous multiplicative weights updates yielding coarse correlated equilibria)—serve as formal local optima of the acquisition function in the unstructured domain, reducing the intractable O(d^n) maximization to O(nd) per-variable decisions. The algorithm computes M > B equilibria per iteration and filters to the top B by UCB value before evaluating them, providing robustness against suboptimal equilibria. Under standard GP regularity assumptions, the paper proves that after T = Ω(γ_T β_T / ε²) iterations, the returned strategy is an ε-approximate Nash equilibrium of the true objective, matching the typical BO convergence rate. The approach is validated on four real-world protein design datasets (GB1, Halogenase, GFP) where GAMEOPT consistently discovers higher-fitness variants faster than GP-UCB, PR, directed evolution (IBR-FITNESS), and discrete local search baselines.

## Results

GAMEOPT (with both IBR and HEDGE subroutines) consistently discovers higher-fitness protein sequences faster than all baselines across four real-world protein design datasets. On the most complex domain (GB1(55), n=55 players), GAMEOPT-IBR achieves a cumulative maximum log fitness of 4.72 versus 2.99 for IBR-FITNESS and 1.18 for PR, while RANDOM yields −0.66. On GB1(4), GAMEOPT-IBR finds the global optimum sequence (AHCA) in 33.33% of 18 replications, compared to 16.67% for GAMEOPT-HEDGE and IBR-FITNESS, and 0% for PR, GP-UCB, and RANDOM. In the Halogenase setting, GAMEOPT-IBR samples candidates above the 0.8×max fitness threshold in 100% of iterations, versus 72.22% for GP-UCB and IBR-FITNESS, and 38.89% for PR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAMEOPT outperforms GP-UCB in convergence speed on both feasible domains (Halogenase, GB1(4)), and GP-UCB is entirely intractable for GB1(55) and GFP due to exhaustive search over the combinatorial space; GP-UCB also saturates when initialized with only 100 training points, whereas GAMEOPT remains robust. Against IBR-FITNESS (a directed-evolution mimic that performs best-response on true log fitness), GAMEOPT initially trails but surpasses it within a few iterations because its UCB-based game dynamics balance exploration and exploitation more effectively. GAMEOPT consistently and substantially outperforms PR (probabilistic reparameterization, Daulton et al. 2022) across all six domain configurations, with the gap widening as search space size increases (e.g., cumulative max 4.72 vs. 1.18 on GB1(55), n=55), since PR requires combinatorially many samples to estimate the expected UCB. Compared to DLS and DLS_BEST (discrete local search over k-Hamming neighborhoods), GAMEOPT achieves comparable or better convergence while reducing acquisition-function optimization complexity from O(C(n,k)·d^k) to O(n·d), with total compute of ~11 h per replication vs. ~103 h for DLS on GB1(55), n=55. RANDOM is the weakest baseline, yielding negative cumulative maximum on the two largest domains, confirming that structured exploration is essential in high-dimensional combinatorial protein spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAMEOPT builds on the GP-UCB framework of Srinivas et al. (2009) for acquisition function design and sample complexity analysis, and on the HEDGE multiplicative weights algorithm of Freund & Schapire (1997) as an equilibrium-finding subroutine. It departs from probabilistic reparameterization (Daulton et al., 2022), which requires combinatorially many samples to estimate expected UCB, by instead decomposing the joint optimization into a cooperative game where each variable is a player and equilibria serve as tractable local optima. The approach also contrasts with directed evolution methods (Arnold, 1998; Yang et al., 2019) that discard most past data, since GAMEOPT leverages the full GP posterior to guide a cooperative 'evolution' at every iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GAMEOPT addresses the fundamental scalability bottleneck of combinatorial BO—reducing acquisition function optimization from O(d^n) exhaustive search to O(nd) per equilibrium—making BO feasible for protein sequences of length 55+ where standard methods are intractable. The theoretical guarantee (T = Ω(γ_T ε^{-2}) iterations to reach ε-approximate equilibria) provides the first sample-complexity bound for equilibrium-based combinatorial BO. Open questions include deriving Price of Anarchy bounds for unstructured domains, adaptive player grouping to capture epistasis, and whether the framework extends to mixed discrete-continuous spaces. A key limitation is that PoA guarantees do not directly apply, so robustness relies on computing and filtering multiple equilibria per iteration.
