## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is the standard mechanism for text-conditioned generation in diffusion models, but it requires high guidance scales (ω ∈ [5, 30]) that cause the denoised estimates to extrapolate beyond the data manifold, producing artifacts, mode collapse, and broken DDIM invertibility. The paper identifies this off-manifold phenomenon as the root cause of CFG's well-known pathologies rather than an inherent limitation of diffusion models, and asks whether a geometrically principled fix can restore manifold consistency while preserving text alignment.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and Gaussian noise in latent space (e.g., 4×64×64 for SD v1.5, 4×128×128 for SDXL) and produces a generated image after 50 NFE DDIM steps or as few as 6 NFE with distilled models. For inversion and editing, it additionally takes a source image to invert into a latent trajectory, then re-samples with a modified prompt. For text-conditioned inverse problems, it takes a degraded measurement (e.g., 8× downsampled, motion-blurred, or masked image) plus a text prompt and outputs a reconstructed image. The guidance scale λ ∈ [0, 1] replaces CFG's ω, with λ = 1.0 approximately matching ω = 12.5 at 50 NFE.

## Method

CFG++ reformulates text guidance as an inverse problem: minimizing a text-conditioned score matching (SDS) loss over the clean data manifold, then solving it via decomposed diffusion sampling (DDS) to avoid Jacobian computation. The resulting update rule is nearly identical to standard CFG except for one critical change in the renoising step: the guided noise ε̂_c^ω(x_t) is replaced by the unconditional noise ε̂_∅(x_t), so the renoising trajectory stays on the unconditional manifold while the denoising step interpolates between unconditional and conditional Tweedie estimates. Because the guidance scale λ is constrained to [0, 1], the denoised estimate is always a convex combination of the unconditional and conditional estimates, preventing the extrapolation that causes off-manifold excursions in CFG. This single-line modification requires no retraining, adds no computational overhead, and extends naturally to higher-order solvers (DPM-solver++ 2M/2S), ancestral samplers, distilled models (SDXL-Turbo, SDXL-Lightning), and flow-matching frameworks.

## Results

On COCO 10k with SD v1.5 (50 NFE DDIM), CFG++ consistently improves FID over CFG across all matched guidance scales, e.g. FID drops from 20.01 to 19.34 at ω=9.0/λ=0.8 and from 21.23 to 20.88 at ω=12.5/λ=1.0, while CLIP scores remain comparable (~0.313). ImageReward shows the largest relative gain at intermediate scales (0.170→0.208 at ω=9.0/λ=0.8). For distilled models (SDXL-Turbo, 6 NFE), the improvement is more dramatic: ImageReward jumps from 0.777 to 0.968 and FID from 59.67 to 59.21. On text-conditioned inverse problems (FFHQ 512×512), PSLD+CFG++ achieves FID of 36.58 for 8× super-resolution and 65.67 for motion deblurring, substantially outperforming both vanilla PSLD (46.24, 97.51) and PSLD+CFG (41.24, 91.90).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2021), CFG++ wins on FID at every matched guidance scale on COCO 10k (SD v1.5, 50 NFE), with the largest FID gap at the lowest scale (13.84→12.75 at ω=2.0/λ=0.2) and a notable ImageReward jump at ω=9.0/λ=0.8 (0.170→0.208). CLIP similarity is essentially tied across all scales, confirming that the quality gain comes from reduced artifacts rather than stronger text alignment. The advantage is even more pronounced for distilled models: on SDXL-Turbo (6 NFE), ImageReward improves from 0.777 to 0.968, and on SDXL-Lightning from 0.691 to 0.829. Against PSLD (Rout et al., 2024) on FFHQ inverse problems, PSLD+CFG++ dominates both vanilla PSLD and PSLD+CFG on FID and LPIPS for super-resolution (36.58 vs. 46.24/41.24) and motion deblurring (65.67 vs. 97.51/91.90), while PSNR differences are marginal. The paper also demonstrates that CFG++ restores DDIM inversion fidelity that CFG breaks down, enabling faithful image editing without the saturation and drift artifacts of standard CFG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates classifier-free guidance (CFG) as a manifold-constrained inverse problem, drawing directly on the decomposed diffusion sampling (DDS) framework from diffusion inverse problem solvers (DPS, Chung et al. 2023a; DDS, Chung et al. 2024). Rather than adopting the sharpened posterior distribution p^ω(x|c) ∝ p(x)p(c|x)^ω from Ho & Salimans (2021), it identifies the text-conditioned score matching (SDS) loss as the appropriate objective and applies Tweedie-based manifold projection to derive a one-line fix: using the unconditional noise estimate in the renoising step. This positions CFG++ as a geometric correction to CFG, distinct from empirical guidance-scheduling heuristics (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2024; Howard & Prashanth, 2022) and from the predictor-corrector reinterpretation of Bradley & Nakkiran (2024), while also subsuming the null-text optimization and EDICT work on inversion as special cases of the off-manifold problem it addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFG is the single most widely deployed guidance mechanism across all modern text-to-image and video diffusion models, so a one-line fix that simultaneously improves generation quality, restores DDIM invertibility, enables lower guidance scales (λ∈[0,1] vs. ω∈[5,30]), and makes text-conditioned inverse problems tractable has broad practical impact. The conceptual reframing—that the well-known pathologies of high-ω CFG (mode collapse, inversion failure, early-stage saturation) stem from off-manifold extrapolation rather than from the diffusion model itself—changes how practitioners should think about guidance. Open questions include whether the modest FID gains in low-NFE non-distilled regimes (e.g., 20-NFE DPM++2M) indicate a fundamental limit of the approach, whether the equivalent time-varying ω_t schedule (Appendix D) can be learned or simplified, and how CFG++ interacts with emerging flow-matching and rectified-flow architectures beyond the Euler-step extension shown in Appendix B.
