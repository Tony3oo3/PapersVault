## Problem

While momentum-based SGD (SNAG) empirically accelerates convergence, the theoretical conditions under which this acceleration occurs in convex settings remained poorly understood. The Strong Growth Condition (SGC) is known to be sufficient for SNAG acceleration, but characterizing its constant ρ_K for general finite-sum problems was an open problem, and existing bounds (e.g., ρ_K ≤ L_(K)/μ) can be arbitrarily large, making acceleration guarantees vacuous.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite-sum objective f(x) = (1/N)Σf_i(x) with a batch gradient estimator of size K, and produces (i) a new characterization of the SGC constant ρ_K via the RACOGA correlation constant c, (ii) convergence rate bounds for SNAG in both expectation and almost-sure senses, and (iii) a three-regime analysis of how batch size K affects the total number of ∇f_i evaluations needed. The RACOGA constant c ∈ [-1/2, (N-1)/2] is computed from pairwise inner products of individual gradients ∇f_i(x), and directly yields ρ_1 = N/(1+2c) for batch size 1.

## Method

The core insight is a decomposition (Proposition 1) that splits ||∇f(x)||² into a batch-estimator variance term and an average gradient-correlation term, revealing that the SGC constant is governed by how correlated the individual gradients ∇f_i are. The paper introduces RACOGA (Relaxed Averaged COrrelated Gradient Assumption), a condition bounding the ratio of pairwise gradient inner products to total gradient energy, which generalizes the simpler PosCorr condition and allows quantifying anti-correlation. Proposition 2 establishes the direct link ρ_1 = N/(1+2c), so higher gradient correlation yields a smaller SGC constant and tighter SNAG convergence bounds. Lemma 1 and Theorem 5 extend this to arbitrary batch sizes, revealing three regimes: orthogonal gradients (ρ_1 = N, batch size irrelevant), positively correlated gradients (ρ_1 < N, larger batches hurt), and negatively correlated gradients (ρ_1 > N, larger batches help). The paper also provides new almost-sure convergence rates for SNAG (Theorem 4) and validates the theory on linear regression and neural network experiments showing that RACOGA values along the optimization path predict whether SNAG outperforms SGD.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is Proposition 2, which links the RACOGA constant c to the SGC constant via ρ₁ = N/(1+2c), showing that higher gradient correlation yields tighter SGC bounds and thus faster SNAG convergence. Empirically, on CIFAR-10 (CNN), SNAG achieves 70.88% test accuracy versus 65.40% for SGD and 17.07% for NAG; on the SPHERE dataset (MLP), SNAG reaches 88.73% versus 80.6% for SGD and 50.02% for NAG. In the linear regression experiments, SNAG outperforms SGD specifically when data correlation is high (Gaussian mixture), while SGD is faster on uncorrelated (isotropic) data, confirming the RACOGA-based theoretical prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNAG consistently outperforms SGD on both neural network experiments: by +8.13 pp on SPHERE-MLP (88.73% vs 80.60%) and by +5.48 pp on CIFAR10-CNN (70.88% vs 65.40%), with the gap explained by high RACOGA values along the optimization path. Against the deterministic baselines, SNAG dramatically beats both GD and NAG on CIFAR10 (70.88% vs 17.54% and 17.07% respectively), while on SPHERE it surpasses NAG by +38.71 pp (88.73% vs 50.02%). In the supplementary comparison with adaptive methods, SNAG edges out ADAM (70.88% vs 69.12%) and RMSprop (70.88% vs 67.31%) on CIFAR10, but loses to both on SPHERE (88.73% vs 93.73% for ADAM and 94.02% for RMSprop), where the paper attributes the advantage to adaptive step-size normalization helping escape a flat region. In the linear regression setting, SNAG's advantage over SGD is data-dependent: it wins on correlated (Gaussian mixture) data but loses on isotropic data, directly validating the RACOGA-based theoretical prediction that acceleration requires sufficient gradient correlation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SGC-based convergence framework for SNAG established by Vaswani et al. (2019) and Gupta et al. (2023) by introducing RACOGA, a new condition that characterizes the SGC constant ρ_K through average gradient correlation. It builds on the interpolation-based SGD analysis of Gower et al. (2019, 2021) and Sebbouh et al. (2021) for the comparison baseline, and on Nesterov's (1983) deterministic acceleration theory for the algorithmic structure. The key departure is showing that ρ_K can be bounded by N/(1+2c) via RACOGA rather than the looser L_(K)/μ bound, and that this correlation-based characterization directly determines batch-size scaling behavior (Theorem 5), a connection not previously made explicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first practical diagnostic (RACOGA) for predicting when stochastic momentum will accelerate over SGD, addressing a long-standing gap where the SGC was known to be necessary but its constant was hard to compute or verify. The three-regime batch-size analysis (orthogonal, positively correlated, negatively correlated gradients) has direct implications for parallelization efficiency and explains the performance saturation phenomenon observed empirically. Key limitations include the convexity requirement for theoretical guarantees (neural network experiments fall outside this scope), the fact that RACOGA must be evaluated along the optimization path (not known a priori), and the unclear role of RACOGA in under-parameterized settings. Open questions include extending the correlation-based characterization to non-convex problems and understanding why overparameterized networks naturally exhibit high RACOGA values.
