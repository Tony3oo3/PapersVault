## Problem

While empirical evidence shows that linearly combining individually fine-tuned weights yields effective multi-task models, the conditions under which this compositionality holds for standard non-linear deep networks remain theoretically unexplored. Prior theoretical guarantees (Liu & Soatto 2023; Ortiz-Jimenez et al. 2024) are restricted to linearized or tangent-space networks, leaving open how to train composable modules in incremental settings where each learner sees only its own task distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of T disjoint RGB image classification tasks (e.g., 10 tasks × 20 classes on ImageNet-R) and produces a pool of composable task vectors τ_t, each representing a displacement from shared pre-training weights θ₀. At inference, a single forward pass through the composed model θ_P = θ₀ + (1/T)Στ_t suffices, avoiding the multiple forward passes required by ensemble-of-experts approaches. The task vectors additionally support zero-shot editing: adding a subset of vectors specializes the model on selected tasks, while subtracting one unlearns that task without retraining.

## Method

The paper derives a second-order Taylor approximation of the loss around pre-training weights θ₀ and applies Jensen's inequality to show that the composed model's empirical risk is upper-bounded by the weighted average of individual risks, provided learners remain within the pre-training basin. From this, two dual algorithms are derived: ITA (Incremental Task Arithmetic) trains each module individually with an EWC-like Fisher-regularized penalty anchored at θ₀ to preserve out-of-distribution performance, while IEL (Incremental Ensemble Learning) optimizes the composed model's loss directly, augmented by an Ω term that penalizes pairwise Hessian/Fisher-weighted distances between task vectors. Both algorithms maintain O(1) memory and compute with respect to the number of tasks by treating the composed model as a running average, and are compatible with any parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy (LoRA, (IA)³, full fine-tuning) using a ViT-B/16 backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-21K.

## Results

On the class-incremental benchmarks, the best ITA/IEL variants (across FFT, LoRA, and (IA)³) achieve Final Accuracy of 80.09% on ImageNet-R, 90.66% on CIFAR-100, 85.67% on CUB-200, 92.70% on Caltech-256, 86.60% on MIT-67, 83.73% on RESISC45, and 95.88% on CropDiseases. These results consistently outperform all 9 baselines on 5 out of 7 datasets, with the closest competitors being InfLoRA (76.97–89.05%) and SEED (55.87–92.77%). The Joint training upper bound remains 4–10 points higher, indicating room for improvement, while the plain Finetune baseline collapses to 12–44% due to catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ITA and IEL outperform all 9 baselines on 5 of 7 datasets (ImageNet-R, CIFAR-100, CUB-200, Caltech-256, RESISC45), with margins of 3–20 points over the strongest competitors. On ImageNet-R, IEL-FFT (80.09%) surpasses InfLoRA (76.97%) by 3.1 points and SEED (55.87%) by 24.2 points. On CIFAR-100, ITA-(IA)³ (90.66%) leads L2P (87.32%) by 3.3 points and CODA (86.48%) by 4.2 points. On CUB-200, ITA-(IA)³ (85.67%) edges out SEED (85.35%) by 0.3 points, while on Caltech-256, IEL-(IA)³ (92.70%) beats L2P (91.22%) by 1.5 points. The two exceptions are MIT-67 (where SEED's 86.34% is competitive with ITA-LoRA's 86.60%) and CropDiseases (where DER++ with a 1000-example buffer achieves 98.77% vs. IEL-LoRA's 95.88%). Against the compositionality-focused baselines TMC (60.01–82.30%) and APT (49.99–87.71%), ITA/IEL show substantial gains of 10–25 points, demonstrating the advantage of the non-linear second-order formulation over linearized tangent-space approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task arithmetic paradigm (Ilharco et al., 2023) and its linearized theoretical analysis (Liu & Soatto, 2023; Ortiz-Jimenez et al., 2024) to standard non-linear deep networks via a second-order Taylor approximation of the loss around pre-training weights. It repurposes the Fisher-based regularization mechanism from EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) but with a fixed anchor at pre-training weights rather than a shifting one, reframing compositionality as a continual learning problem where each module must preserve out-of-distribution performance. The dual algorithms (ITA and IEL) bridge the gap between decentralized individual training and joint ensemble optimization, connecting model soups, task arithmetic, and incremental learning under a single second-order framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for why task arithmetic works in non-linear networks (staying within the pre-training basin) and demonstrates that compositional skills—zero-shot specialization and unlearning via simple vector arithmetic—are achievable in incremental settings. Key limitations include the reliance on second-order approximation validity as parameters drift, the diagonal Fisher approximation, and restriction to closed-set classification; extending to open-vocabulary models like CLIP and exploring the pre-training basin more aggressively are identified as important future directions. The finding that ensemble-trained models (IEL) are fragile under member removal, despite strong aggregate performance, raises practical concerns for the growing interest in ensemble-based continual learning.
