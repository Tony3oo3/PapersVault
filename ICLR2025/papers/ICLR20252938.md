## Problem

Mamba-based vision models such as Vim suffer from limited scalability under supervised training, with performance plateauing or even collapsing at large and huge model sizes. While autoregressive pretraining has proven effective for scaling Vision Transformers (iGPT, AIM), it has not been explored for Mamba architectures despite their inherent unidirectional recurrent structure being naturally aligned with next-token prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252938/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, RGB images at 192×192 are patchified into 16×16 patches and further grouped into 64×64 clusters (yielding 9 prediction units), which are scanned row-by-row in forward order to form a 1D visual sentence; the model predicts the next cluster's normalized pixel values via MSE loss. During finetuning, the decoder is removed and the backbone accepts 224×224 (or up to 448×448) images, producing class labels for ImageNet, detection boxes and instance masks via Mask R-CNN on COCO, and pixel-level segmentation maps via UperNet on ADE20K.

## Method

ARM introduces a MambaMLP block that replaces ViT's self-attention with a Mamba selective-scan layer while retaining a SwiGLU MLP, and critically uses only a single causal scan during pretraining to preserve the autoregressive structure (multiple scans would cause information leakage and representation collapse). The key design choice is grouping spatially adjacent 16×16 patches into 64×64 clusters as prediction units, which ablations show outperforms both pixel-level (iGPT) and individual-patch prediction by 2.7% and 0.8% respectively. A 4-layer, 512-width Transformer decoder (MAE-style) reconstructs normalized pixels, and the row-first forward scan order is adopted after ablation shows predefined orders perform comparably while random permutation degrades accuracy by 1.0%. At finetuning time, the block switches to 4 bidirectional scans (initialized from the single-scan pretrained weights) to recover global context, and the entire pipeline trains ~2× faster than MAE and ~10× faster than contrastive learning on the same base-size model.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, ARM-B achieves 83.2% Top-1 accuracy at 224×224 input, improving to 85.2% at 448×448. ARM-L reaches 84.5% (224×224) and 85.1% (384×384), while ARM-H — the largest vision Mamba to date — attains 85.0% (224×224) and 85.5% (384×384). On downstream tasks, ARM achieves 49.2 AP^box on COCO object detection, 43.9 AP^mask on instance segmentation, and 47.7 mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation. ARM also shows strong zero-shot robustness, e.g., 31.9% on ImageNet-A vs. 27.5% for Vim-B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARM-B (83.2%) outperforms the supervised MambaMLP-B (81.2%) and Vim-B (81.2%) by 2.0% on ImageNet-1K, and edges out the convolutional RegNetY-16G (82.9%) by 0.3%. Against VMamba-B (83.9%), ARM-B trails by 0.7% at 224×224 but runs ~4× faster (1301 vs. 315 imgs/s); at 384×384, ARM-B (84.2%) surpasses VMamba-B by 0.3% while maintaining higher throughput. At the large scale, ARM-L (84.5%) beats Vim-L (81.0%) by 3.5%, and at the huge scale, ARM-H (85.0%) is the only Mamba variant that trains stably — Vim-H collapses entirely. In the appendix, ARM-H (85.0%) also outperforms the Transformer-based AIM-0.6B (84.2%) by 0.8% on ImageNet-1K. On downstream tasks, ARM (49.2 AP^box) surpasses DeiT-B (46.8) and Vim-S (43.2) on COCO detection, and achieves 47.7 mIoU on ADE20K vs. 45.5 for DeiT-B and 44.9 for Vim-S.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: autoregressive visual pretraining (iGPT, SAIM, D-iGPT, AIM) which had only been applied to Transformer architectures, and Mamba-based vision models (Vim, Vmamba, MambaMLP) which had only been trained under supervised objectives. The MambaMLP block directly follows ViT's token-mixer-plus-MLP design with Mamba replacing self-attention, while the decoder is borrowed from MAE. The key conceptual departure is the cluster-based prediction unit (grouping spatially adjacent patches into 64×64 clusters) and the observation that Mamba's inherently unidirectional selective scan aligns naturally with autoregressive next-token prediction, making multi-scan bidirectional variants incompatible with the pretraining objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most significant finding is that autoregressive pretraining unlocks the scaling potential of Mamba in vision that supervised training could not: Vim collapses at huge size, while ARM-H (662M params) trains stably and reaches 85.0% ImageNet accuracy. This suggests that the right pretraining paradigm, not just architectural changes, is the bottleneck for scaling SSM-based vision models. Open questions include whether the single-scan constraint during pretraining (required for causal structure) limits the final model's expressiveness, whether the approach extends to video or other sequential modalities, and whether the cluster-based prediction unit generalizes beyond ImageNet-scale classification to dense prediction tasks at scale.
