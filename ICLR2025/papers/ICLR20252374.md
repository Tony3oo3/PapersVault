## Problem

Existing blur data augmentation methods estimate motion from a purely 2D perspective (e.g., regressing 2D non-uniform kernels), ignoring the fact that camera and object motion inherently occurs in 3D space, which produces unrealistic blur patterns. Meanwhile, realistic blur datasets suffer from limited scene and blur-pattern diversity due to the cost of dual-camera collection, and prior augmentation approaches either require extra data (video frames for diffusion-based methods) or yield physically implausible results (kernel regression without 3D modeling).

## Input / Output

The blur synthesizer takes a single blur image and a corresponding sharp image (256×256 crops during training) and outputs a 3D-aware vector field decomposed into a parametric 2D rigid component (axis-angle rotation + 2D translation per camera pose) and a non-parametric 3D residual displacement field, from which M=16 transformed scene images are rendered via grid sampling and averaged to produce a synthetic blur image. During deblurring training, the synthesizer is applied with randomly sampled amplitude (1.0×–1.5×) and phase (−90° to 90°) control parameters and shuffled scene contents to generate millions of diverse augmented blur images; the synthesizer itself is not used at inference time, so it adds no evaluation cost.

## Method

The core insight is that a 3D rigid transformation projected onto the 2D image plane can be decomposed into a 2D rigid transformation (parametric, reducing ill-posedness) plus a projected 3D residual vector (non-parametric, captured by a neural network), enabling 3D motion modeling without explicit depth. A motion estimator (NAFNet-8 encoder-decoder with an MLP head) predicts per-pose rotation/translation parameters and per-pixel residual displacements from the blur image; these are combined via amplitude-phase composition in polar coordinates (δ = |ΔT|·|ε| ∠(φ(ΔT)+φ(ε))) to form the 3D-aware vector field, which is then used with grid sampling to render and aggregate M scene images into a synthetic blur. Two regularizations constrain the non-parametric field: a Laplacian smoothness loss and an invertible geometric consistency loss that back-propagates the transformed scene through the inverse displacement to recover the original sharp image. A compensation network (NAFNet-16) handles photometric drift between blur and sharp pairs during training, and the entire pipeline is jointly optimized with an L1 blur-reconstruction loss plus the two regularizers.

## Results

GeoSyn (the proposed 3D-aware blur synthesizer) consistently improves deblurring PSNR by 0.26–0.49 dB across datasets when applied to NAFNet-64: GoPro 33.69→34.09 dB, RealBlur-J 32.50→32.99 dB, and RSBlur 33.97→34.23 dB. The gains transfer across architectures, with FFTformer+GeoSyn reaching 34.21 dB on GoPro and 40.11 dB on RealBlur-R. On RSBlur, NAFNet-64+GeoSyn (34.23 dB) surpasses all prior methods except SegDeblur-L (34.21 dB), and SegDeblur-L+GeoSyn achieves the best overall result of 34.31 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoSyn outperforms all compared augmentation baselines. On GoPro, it beats MotionETR (Zhang 2021) by 0.17 dB (34.09 vs 33.92) and J-MKPD (Carbajal 2023) by 0.50 dB (34.09 vs 33.59), with the authors attributing the gap to the lack of explicit 3D geometry in those kernel-based methods. On RealBlur-J, GeoSyn surpasses Blur Pipeline (Rim 2022) by 0.42 dB (32.99 vs 32.57) and ID-Blau (Wu 2024) by 0.29 dB (32.99 vs 32.70); the advantage over ID-Blau is larger on RealBlur-J than GoPro because GeoSyn is data-agnostic and can be trained on RealBlur-specific motion patterns, whereas ID-Blau requires video frame images unavailable in RealBlur. On RSBlur (Table 3), NAFNet-64+GeoSyn (34.23 dB) exceeds SRN-Deblur (32.53), MIMO-UNet+ (33.37), MPRNet (33.61), Restormer (33.69), Uformer-B (33.98), ConvIR-L (34.06), and NAFNet-64 alone (33.97), and is competitive with SegDeblur-L (34.21); combining GeoSyn with SegDeblur-L yields the best result (34.31 dB). GeoSyn is also compatible with ID-Blau, and the combination (ID-Blau+GeoSyn) further improves to 33.09 dB on RealBlur-J.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of blur data augmentation work (Blur Pipeline, ID-Blau) by replacing 2D kernel regression or diffusion-based reblurring with an explicit 3D camera/object motion model. It departs from prior 3D deblurring approaches that require depth measurements by decomposing the 3D rigid transformation into a 2D parametric component (rotation + translation) and a 3D non-parametric residual estimated directly by a neural network, eliminating the need for depth. The amplitude-phase composition function for combining parametric and non-parametric vector fields, along with the invertible geometric regularization, are novel contributions that enable both controllability and geometric coherence without additional supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is practically significant because it is data-agnostic (requires only blur-sharp pairs, no video frames, kernels, or depth), controllable (amplitude, phase, scene), and compatible with any existing deblurring architecture, making it a drop-in augmentation strategy for the field. The finding that monocular depth estimates actually degrade performance compared to the depth-free 3D residual approach challenges the intuitive assumption that more geometric information always helps. Open questions include whether the 3D-aware vector fields transfer to optical flow estimation, depth prediction, or video generation as the authors suggest, and whether the approach scales to very long exposure times or extreme motion regimes where the M discrete camera positions may be insufficient.
