## Problem

In multi-draft speculative sampling, the optimal token-level selection rule that maximizes the probability of accepting one of K candidate draft tokens was previously formulated as a linear program (SpecTr), but the structure of the optimal solution remained opaque. This paper asks whether the optimal solution admits a closed-form characterization and whether the acceptance probability can be expressed analytically, particularly for small numbers of drafts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250063/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K candidate tokens sampled i.i.d. from a draft model distribution p(·) over a discrete vocabulary Ω, along with the target model distribution q(·) over the same vocabulary, and produces a single output token whose marginal distribution exactly matches q(·). The key performance metric is the acceptance probability Pr(Z ∈ S), i.e., the probability that the output token is one of the K input tokens. The distributions are conditioned on a fixed context sequence u^t shared by both models, and the effective alphabet size is typically small due to top-p or top-k sampling.

## Method

The central result is a canonical decomposition (Theorem 1) showing the optimal token-level selection rule can be achieved by a two-step scheme: first, an importance weighted sampling step selects an intermediate token from the input set using optimized weights β_y(x_{1:K}); second, standard single-draft speculative sampling is applied to that token against the target distribution. For K=2 identical drafts, the paper derives a closed-form expression for the optimal acceptance probability as a minimization over subsets of the vocabulary (Theorem 3) and establishes a necessary and sufficient condition for the acceptance probability to equal one (Theorem 2), which is strictly weaker than requiring p=q. For practical deployment, the authors propose a truncated LP that reduces the number of optimization variables from O(n²) to O(s²) by fixing weights for low-probability token pairs, and a truncated alphabet scheme that restricts importance sampling to a high-probability subset with a rejection-based correction step.

## Results

On the Dolly task with top-k sampling (k=10, K=2 drafts), the proposed IS scheme achieves a block efficiency of 2.48±0.01, compared to 2.43±0.01 for SpecTr and 2.38±0.02 for SpecInfer. In terms of token-level acceptance probability (Table 2), the IS scheme reaches 0.6348 on Dolly (K=2) and 0.5333 on XSUM (K=8), consistently outperforming SpecTr (0.6246 and 0.5263) and SpecInfer (0.6202 and 0.5227) while remaining close to the theoretical optimum (0.6384 and 0.5419). Under top-p sampling with varying draft temperatures, the IS scheme achieves up to 6.63% token rate improvement over the single-draft baseline, whereas SpecInfer shows negative token rate gains at higher temperatures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed IS scheme consistently outperforms both SpecTr and SpecInfer across all evaluated settings. On acceptance probability (Table 2), IS achieves 0.6348 vs. 0.6246 (SpecTr) and 0.6202 (SpecInfer) on Dolly with K=2 drafts, and the gap widens with more drafts (e.g., 0.6919 vs. 0.6800 vs. 0.6722 at K=8). On block efficiency (Table 3, Dolly, top-k=10, K=2), IS reaches 2.48 tokens per draft call, exceeding SpecTr (2.43, 98% of IS) and SpecInfer (2.38, 96% of IS). Under top-p sampling with heterogeneous draft temperatures (Figure 3), the IS scheme shows consistent positive token rate improvements over the single-draft baseline across XSum, Dolly, and WMT18, while SpecInfer's token rate actually degrades (negative gains) as the second draft temperature increases, because the additional compute does not translate into sufficient block efficiency improvement. The IS scheme also stays within ~1.5% of the theoretical optimal acceptance probability in all settings, whereas SpecTr and SpecInfer lag by 2–4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the multi-draft token-level selection problem formulated by Sun et al. (2024b, SpecTr) as a discrete optimal transport / linear program, and departs from that framing by showing the optimal solution admits a canonical two-step decomposition: importance weighted sampling followed by single-draft speculative sampling (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023). For K=2 identical drafts, the authors go beyond the LP formulation to derive closed-form expressions via Fourier-Motzkin elimination, connecting the problem to polyhedral cone duality. The practical truncated-LP and truncated-alphabet schemes are positioned as faster approximations to the full LP, complementing the heuristic sequential rejection sampling in SpecTr and the tree-based verification in SpecInfer (Miao et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form characterization for K=2 drafts (Theorems 2 and 3) provides the first exact analytical understanding of multi-draft acceptance limits, revealing that perfect acceptance is achievable without p=q — a property absent in the single-draft case. This opens the door to principled draft-model design (e.g., via distillation) targeting the sufficient condition rather than exact distribution matching. Open questions include extending the analytical characterization to K>2 (only numerically verified up to K=6, |Ω|≤14), whether the Remark 2 conjecture generalizes, and whether the practical gains observed on OPT-13B persist at larger scales or with more diverse draft-target pairs. The empirical improvements, while consistent, are modest (a few percentage points in block efficiency), suggesting the theoretical contribution is the primary value.
