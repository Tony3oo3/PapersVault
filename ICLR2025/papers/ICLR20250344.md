## Problem

The Elo/Bradley-Terry framework underlying Chatbot Arena cannot model ties (which constitute ~20% of human-judged comparisons) or capture covariance structure between competitors, and the existing Rao-Kupper and Davidson tie extensions use a single global tie parameter that yields >10% fitting errors on the Chatbot Arena dataset. Additionally, structural symmetries in the likelihood cause parameter non-uniqueness and optimization instability that have not been fully addressed in prior work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse comparison graph G(V,E) with m=129 competitors and |E|=3455 edges, along with win/loss/tie frequency matrices W and T (totaling ~1.37M comparisons), where unconnected pairs have zero entries. Outputs include a score vector x for ranking, pairwise win/loss/tie probability matrices, a factorized covariance structure Σ = D + ΛΛᵀ, and a unique dissimilarity matrix S = diag(Σ)1ᵀ + 1diag(Σ)ᵀ − 2Σ that enables kernel PCA, MDS, and hierarchical clustering into performance tiers. The covariance Σ itself is non-unique (equivalence class under rank-one perturbations), but S and the centered covariance PΣP are identifiable.

## Method

The framework extends Bradley-Terry, Rao-Kupper, and Davidson models along three axes. First, a factored tie model replaces the single tie threshold η with pair-specific η_ij constructed via an additive factor model H = GΦᵀ + ΦGᵀ, where Φ uses DCT-IV basis vectors, reducing parameters from |E| to m·k_tie while capturing latent structure in tie patterns (reducing tie fitting errors by two orders of magnitude). Second, Thurstonian representations replace raw score differences x_i − x_j with standardized z_ij = (μ_i − μ_j)/√s_ij, where s_ij = σ_ii + σ_jj − 2σ_ij, and the covariance is factorized as Σ = D + ΛΛᵀ with rank k_cov. Third, three constraints resolve likelihood symmetries: mean-zero scores (Σx_i = 0), a trace constraint on the centered covariance (tr(PΣP) = 1) to fix scale, and zero column means for Λ (‖Λᵀ1‖² = 0) to fix translation. Parameters are estimated by maximum likelihood via BFGS with analytically derived gradients.

## Results

On the Chatbot Arena dataset (129 competitors, 1,374,996 comparisons), the best proposed model (Model 18: generalized Rao-Kupper with k_cov=3, k_tie=20) achieves a negative log-likelihood of 1.0044 and tie cross-entropy of 0.3193, improving over the original Rao-Kupper (Model 7: NLL 1.0095, tie CE 0.3227) and original Davidson (Model 19: NLL 1.0100, tie CE 0.3231). The paper reports that the factored tie model reduces errors in fitting tie data by two orders of magnitude compared to the single-parameter Rao-Kupper and Davidson models. Within each model family, increasing k_tie from 0 to 20 consistently improves all cross-entropy metrics, while adding covariance (k_cov=3) provides marginal additional gains in NLL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Chatbot Arena baseline (Chiang et al., 2024; Model 1: BT with ties as half-win/half-loss), the proposed tie-inclusive models cannot be directly compared on NLL since BT predicts only two outcomes while RK/Davidson predict three; however, the BT model's win CE (0.3177) is lower than RK's (0.3403) simply because it fits a one-dimensional output space. Against the original Bradley-Terry (Model 4), the proposed models again differ in output dimensionality, but the BT model's inability to model ties means it cannot capture the 20.4% of comparisons that are ties. Against the original Rao-Kupper (Model 7), the generalized RK with factored ties (Model 18) reduces tie CE from 0.3227 to 0.3193 and NLL from 1.0095 to 1.0044, with the paper claiming a two-order-of-magnitude reduction in tie-fitting RMSE. Against the original Davidson (Model 19), the generalized Davidson (Model 30) similarly improves tie CE from 0.3231 to 0.3194 and NLL from 1.0100 to 1.0047. The key finding is that the factored tie model (k_tie=20) drives most of the improvement, while adding Thurstonian covariance (k_cov=3) provides only marginal additional NLL gains but enables richer interpretability through performance-tier clustering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bradley-Terry/Elo framework underlying Chatbot Arena by integrating three previously separate statistical ideas: the Rao-Kupper and Davidson tie models, Thurstonian covariance structures (as used in psychometric choice modeling by Bockenholt and Maydeu-Olivares), and low-rank factor analysis. The key methodological departure is replacing the single global tie parameter in Rao-Kupper/Davidson with a pair-specific, low-rank factored structure (using DCT-IV basis vectors), and extending Thurstonian representations beyond the standard normal assumption to logistic-based models. The paper also identifies and resolves two previously unaddressed symmetries (score-covariance scaling and factor translation) that cause optimization instability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a statistically principled upgrade to the de facto standard for LLM evaluation (Chatbot Arena), with the open-source leaderbot package making it immediately adoptable. The finding that covariance structure drives ranking consistency more strongly than tie modeling is a useful conceptual insight for practitioners choosing model complexity. Open questions include whether the generalized models admit efficient MM-algorithm solutions (currently requiring BFGS with analytic gradients), whether semantic/embedding-based clustering could complement the purely statistical tiers, and whether the factored tie structure generalizes beyond the Chatbot Arena dataset to other paired-comparison domains (sports, marketing, RLHF).
