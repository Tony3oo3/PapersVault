## Problem

Dropout is widely applied in Graph Convolutional Networks, yet its mechanism in graph-structured data remains poorly understood and is often assumed to operate the same way as in standard neural networks (preventing co-adaptation). This paper identifies that dropout in GCNs serves a fundamentally different primary role—mitigating oversmoothing by maintaining feature diversity across nodes—and provides the first comprehensive theoretical framework characterizing how dropout interacts with graph topology, including degree-dependent regularization effects and synergistic interactions with batch normalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d₀} and an undirected graph adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} as input, producing node embeddings for node-level classification or graph-level predictions. A key analytical output is the feature energy (Dirichlet energy) E(H), which measures representation smoothness across edges and serves as the primary metric for quantifying oversmoothing. The dropout mask M^{(l)} ∈ R^{n×d_l} is applied element-wise at each layer, creating dimension-specific stochastic sub-graphs whose edge sets depend on which feature dimensions survive at both endpoints of each edge.

## Method

The paper develops a theoretical framework showing that dropout in GCNs creates dimension-specific stochastic sub-graphs (Theorem 1), where each feature dimension j induces a sub-graph E_t^{(l,j)} containing only edges whose endpoints both retain feature j. The analysis reveals that dropout's regularization is inherently degree-dependent: the expected effective degree scales as (1-p)²·deg_i (Theorem 3), meaning high-degree nodes experience proportionally less perturbation, yielding adaptive regularization tied to topological importance. A generalization bound (Theorem 6) incorporates the fractional chromatic number χ_f(G) of the message-passing dependency graph, showing GCNs gain natural stability over MLPs (where χ_f=1). The paper further derives a lower bound on feature energy when dropout is combined with batch normalization (Theorem 7), revealing a synergistic mechanism where BN's shift parameters establish a non-zero energy floor while dropout's p/(1-p) scaling amplifies preserved features. Empirically, the method is simply standard GCN/SAGE/GAT/GatedGCN with dropout and BN, which outperforms state-of-the-art methods on multiple benchmarks and consistently increases Dirichlet energy compared to no-dropout variants, confirming the oversmoothing-mitigation mechanism.

## Results

On node classification, GCN with dropout and batch normalization achieves 85.22% on Cora, 73.24% on CiteSeer, and 81.08% on PubMed, outperforming all SOTA baselines including GCNII, Polynormer, and SGFormer on these three citation networks. On larger graphs, it reaches 73.13% on ogbn-arxiv and 81.87% on ogbn-products, competitive with Polynormer (73.46% and 83.82%). On graph-level tasks, GatedGCN with dropout+BN achieves 98.68% on MNIST and 76.93% on CIFAR10, while GCN achieves the best MAE of 0.2437 on Peptides-struct. Dirichlet energy consistently increases with dropout (e.g., 74.67 vs 2.95 on Cora), confirming dropout's role in mitigating oversmoothing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCN with dropout+BN achieves the best accuracy on Cora (85.22%), CiteSeer (73.24%), and PubMed (81.08%), surpassing GCNII (85.19/73.20/80.32), Polynormer (83.25/72.31/79.24), and SGFormer (84.50/72.60/80.30). On Computer, Polynormer (93.68%) edges out GCN (93.15%), while on Photo, CS, and Physics, specialized architectures (Polynormer 96.46%, SAGE 95.90%, NAGphormer 97.34%) maintain a 1–2% advantage. On ogbn-products, Polynormer (83.82%) and GOAT (82.00%) outperform GCN (81.87%), though GCN still beats GCNII (79.42%) and GPRGNN (79.76%) by over 2%. On graph-level tasks, GatedGCN with dropout+BN achieves SOTA on MNIST (98.68%) and CIFAR10 (76.93%), beating GRED (98.38%/76.85%) and Exphormer (98.55%/74.70%), while GCN achieves the best Peptides-struct MAE (0.2437) but trails DRew (0.7150 vs 0.7015) on Peptides-func AP. Ablations confirm both dropout and BN contribute: removing dropout drops Cora accuracy from 85.22% to 83.18%, and removing BN drops it to 84.97%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of dropout (Srivastava et al., 2014) from standard neural networks to GCNs (Kipf & Welling, 2017), departing from the co-adaptation narrative that dominates prior dropout theory. While prior GNN regularization work (DropEdge, DropNode, DropMessage) and GNN generalization theory (VC dimension, Rademacher complexity, algorithm stability) addressed overfitting and oversmoothing separately, this paper unifies them by showing dropout's degree-dependent, dimension-specific sub-graph mechanism simultaneously addresses both. The analysis also connects to the oversmoothing literature (Li et al., 2018; Oono & Suzuki, 2019) by providing a mechanistic explanation for how dropout maintains feature diversity, and to the batch normalization literature by formalizing the synergistic interaction between the two regularizers in graph-structured settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual reframe—that dropout in GCNs primarily mitigates oversmoothing rather than preventing co-adaptation—provides a principled basis for designing adaptive, structure-aware dropout strategies that current heuristic approaches lack. The degree-dependent regularization insight suggests that uniform dropout rates may be suboptimal, opening a direction for topology-aware regularization. However, the analysis is primarily developed for GCN-style message passing, and whether the theoretical bounds (particularly the fractional chromatic number term) are tight or transfer cleanly to attention-based GNNs and deeper architectures remains open. The practical takeaway (GCN + dropout + BN is competitive with complex transformers) is valuable but aligns with the broader 'classic GNNs are strong baselines' trend, limiting the immediate methodological impact.
