## Problem

Existing generalization guarantees for Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization (WDRO) are either approximate (introducing vanishing bias terms), restricted to smooth losses and specific transport costs (e.g., squared Euclidean), or rely on assumptions difficult to verify in practice (Gaussian reference distributions, convexity of the sample space). No prior result provides exact generalization guarantees—where the empirical robust risk directly lower-bounds the true population risk with no approximation term—for arbitrary continuous transport costs and nonsmooth loss functions such as those arising from ReLU activations, max-pooling, or optimization layers in deep learning.

## Input / Output

The framework takes i.i.d. samples from a distribution P on a compact metric space (Ξ, d), a compact family of continuous loss functions F (e.g., parametric models with bounded parameter space), a jointly continuous non-negative transport cost c, and a Wasserstein radius ρ. The output is an exact high-probability guarantee that the empirical robust risk R̂_ρ(f) ≥ E_P[f] for all f ∈ F, valid when ρ > α/√n (dimension-free rate) and n exceeds a threshold depending on the critical radius ρ_crit. Additionally, excess risk bounds quantify how much the robust objective may exceed the true risk, and the analysis extends to doubly regularized (Sinkhorn) variants with entropic penalties in both the constraint and objective.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a novel optimization-based proof that directly tackles the nonsmoothness of the WDRO dual objective using tools from variational analysis (Clarke subdifferentials, the envelope formula for differentiating supremum functions, and outer semicontinuity of argmax maps). The proof proceeds in three steps: (1) establishing a dual lower bound λ_low > 0 via a maximal radius function ρ_max(λ) defined from the lower envelope of right-sided derivatives of the dual generator, and proving ρ_max is continuous at zero (hence ρ_max(0) = ρ_crit) using compactness of F and nonsmooth analysis; (2) bounding the uniform concentration gap between empirical and population expectations of the rescaled dual generator μφ(μ⁻¹, f, ξ) via Dudley's entropy integral, yielding a 1/√n rate; (3) combining these to show R̂_ρ(f) ≥ R_{ρ−α/√n}(f) ≥ E_P[f]. This avoids the O(n^{-1/d}) radius requirement of Fournier–Guillin concentration and works for arbitrary continuous costs and nonsmooth losses. The approach extends to entropically regularized (Sinkhorn) WDRO by replacing the nonsmooth dual generator with a smooth one, at the cost of an inexact guarantee involving a KL divergence term.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The headline result (Theorem 3.1) establishes an exact generalization guarantee: for all f in F, the empirical robust risk R̂_ρ(f) ≥ E_P[f] with probability ≥ 1−δ, provided ρ > α/√n and n > 16(α+β)²/ρ_crit². The excess risk bound (Proposition 3.1) further shows R̂_ρ(f) ≤ R_{ρ+α/√n}(f), and for p-Wasserstein costs with Lip_F-Lipschitz losses, R̂_ρ(f) ≤ E_P[f] + Lip_F(ρ + α/√n)^{1/p}. For linear and logistic regression (Proposition 3.2), the constants recover those of Shafieezadeh-Abadeh et al. (2019), with ρ_crit ≥ D² and λ_low ≥ ω/2 (linear) or ω/(8(1+e^{DΩ})) (logistic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run empirical comparisons but positions its theoretical contributions against prior work. Compared to the closest prior result (Azizian et al., 2023a, NeurIPS), this paper removes four restrictive assumptions: (i) the cost c need not be the squared Euclidean norm, (ii) the reference distribution need not be Gaussian, (iii) losses need not be differentiable (covering ReLU, max-pooling, hinge loss, k-means), and (iv) no additional growth/structural conditions on F are needed. The proof technique shifts from requiring smoothness to directly handling nonsmoothness via variational analysis (Clarke subdifferentials, envelope formula). For linear models, the paper recovers the exact constants of Shafieezadeh-Abadeh et al. (2019), confirming tightness. For the regularized setting, the paper extends Azizian et al. (2023b) by covering double regularization with arbitrary reference coupling π₀, whereas the prior work required a specific Gaussian form. The key trade-off is that compactness of the sample space and finite Dudley entropy are required, which excludes non-parametric loss families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of exact generalization guarantees for Wasserstein Distributionally Robust Optimization (WDRO) initiated by Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn (2018) and Azizian et al. (2023a), which required a radius scaling as O(n^{-1/d}) or restrictive assumptions (Gaussian reference, squared Euclidean cost, differentiable losses) to avoid the curse of dimensionality. The key departure is a novel proof strategy using nonsmooth analysis (Clarke subdifferentials, envelope formula) to directly handle the nonsmoothness of the dual generator, rather than circumventing it through smoothness assumptions. This allows the authors to recover the O(n^{-1/2}) rate with arbitrary transport costs and nonsmooth objectives (ReLU, max-pooling, optimization layers), while also extending to doubly regularized (entropic) WDRO, completing the analysis of Azizian et al. (2023b). For linear and logistic regression, the constants recover those of Shafieezadeh-Abadeh et al. (2019), confirming tightness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result closes a significant theoretical gap: prior exact guarantees for WDRO either suffered from the curse of dimensionality or required assumptions (smoothness, specific cost structure, Gaussian reference) that exclude the most common deep learning objectives. By covering nonsmooth losses and arbitrary costs, the paper provides the first universal exact guarantee that practitioners can invoke when training robust models with ReLU networks or mixed-feature data. Limitations include the compactness requirement on the sample space and the finite Dudley entropy condition, which restrict applicability to parametric models with bounded parameter spaces. Open questions highlighted by the authors include designing efficient procedures for selecting the radius ρ and developing scalable algorithms for solving the resulting distributionally robust optimization problems.
