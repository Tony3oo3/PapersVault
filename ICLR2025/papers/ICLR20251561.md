## Problem

Zeroth-order optimizers like MeZO eliminate backpropagation to enable LLM fine-tuning on consumer hardware, but they fail to account for heterogeneous curvatures across parameter dimensions, causing convergence toward saddle points or extremely slow progress. While second-order (Hessian-based) methods can correct this, they inherently require first-order gradients that are unavailable in a zeroth-order setting. The paper addresses the open question of how to estimate and exploit curvature information using only forward passes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM parameters, a minibatch of training data, and a scalar loss function (which may be non-differentiable, e.g., F1 score). It requires exactly three forward passes per step (one baseline plus two perturbed evaluations) and no backward pass. The output is the updated parameter vector, with the diagonal Hessian pre-conditioner Σ maintained as an O(d)-sized state that is updated via exponential moving average each step. For the memory-efficient variant HiZOO-L, the Hessian is stored as two rank-1 vectors (R and C) rather than a full diagonal, reducing Hessian memory to ~10% of MeZO's total footprint.

## Method

HiZOO augments the SPSA zeroth-order gradient estimator with a diagonal Hessian pre-conditioner Σ that is estimated entirely from forward-pass differences. Specifically, it computes ΔL = L(θ+μΣ^{1/2}u) + L(θ−μΣ^{1/2}u) − 2L(θ) and applies the MiNES identity to extract an unbiased diagonal Hessian estimate from this single scalar, then smooths it via EMA. The descent direction becomes (ℓ₊−ℓ₋)·Σ^{1/2}/(2μ)·u, so parameters in high-curvature dimensions receive proportionally larger updates, directly addressing the heterogeneous-curvature pathology that stalls MeZO. The perturbation itself is also scaled by Σ^{1/2}, making the exploration adaptive to local geometry. HiZOO-L further compresses the Hessian into an Adafactor-style outer-product factorization (R·C)/(1ᵀR) with rank k=1, cutting Hessian storage to O(p+q) instead of O(pq). A convergence theorem establishes an O(1/√T) rate under standard L-smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions, matching the best known ZO-SGD rate.

## Results

On RoBERTa-large (350M), HiZOO achieves 70.9% average accuracy across SST-2, SST-5, SNLI, MNLI, and TREC, outperforming MeZO (67.4%) by 3.5% absolute. Scaling to larger models, HiZOO improves over MeZO by 1.9% on Phi-2 (74.3 vs 72.4), 0.8% on Llama3-8B (75.5 vs 74.7), and 1.9% on OPT-13B (72.1 vs 70.2). On OPT-66B, HiZOO(prefix) achieves 71.7% average vs MeZO(prefix) 69.0%, with up to 5.1% absolute gain on RTE. HiZOO also achieves an 8× speedup in training steps on SST-2 while using only 1.5× the per-step time of MeZO due to one extra forward pass.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeZO (the primary zeroth-order baseline), HiZOO consistently wins across all model scales: +3.5% average on RoBERTa-large, +1.9% on Phi-2, +0.8% on Llama3-8B, +1.9% on OPT-13B, and +2.7% on OPT-66B (up to +5.1% on RTE). HiZOO-L, the low-rank variant, matches HiZOO within 0.1–0.2% while reducing Hessian memory to ~10% of MeZO's total memory. Against ZO-Adam (which uses second-order moment estimation), HiZOO-L achieves a 4.3% absolute accuracy improvement (90.4 vs 86.1 average) while using only 50% of the GPU memory (1.12× vs 2.47× MeZO memory). Against first-order Adam/FT, HiZOO approaches full fine-tuning accuracy (70.9 vs 74.9 on RoBERTa-large) without any backpropagation, and on non-differentiable F1 objectives, HiZOO outperforms MeZO by 6.5% absolute on average. HiZOO also outperforms all other ZO variants from Zhang et al. (2024) including ZO-SGD, ZO-SGD-MMT, ZO-SGD-Cons, and ZO-SGD-Sign in Table 5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: zeroth-order optimization for LLM fine-tuning (initiated by MeZO, which adapted SPSA to require only forward passes) and second-order preconditioning via diagonal Hessian (used in Sophia, AdaHessian, and earlier work by Magoulas et al.). The key departure from MeZO is the addition of one extra forward pass to estimate the diagonal Hessian, which serves as a curvature-aware preconditioner for the ZO gradient direction. The Hessian estimation formula is derived from a lemma in MiNES (Ye, 2023), and the memory-efficient HiZOO-L variant directly adapts the row/column factorization from Adafactor. Conceptually, the paper shows that second-order information can be extracted in a purely zeroth-order setting, something that was previously thought infeasible without first-order derivatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for memory-constrained LLM fine-tuning: HiZOO achieves an 8× speedup over MeZO on SST2 while using only one extra forward pass per step, and HiZOO-L keeps Hessian memory to ~10% of MeZO's total. The results are validated across a wide range of scales (350M to 66B) and PEFT methods (FT, LoRA, prefix), making the approach broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the diagonal Hessian approximation is sufficient or whether off-diagonal curvature information would yield further gains, whether the method extends to pre-training (not just fine-tuning), and how the approach interacts with other ZO variants like sparse or subspace methods. The per-step overhead of 1.5× wall-clock time remains a consideration for very large batch settings.
