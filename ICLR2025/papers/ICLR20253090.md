## Problem

Non-overlapping cropping strategies used to process high-resolution images in MLLMs inevitably sever objects and text at tile boundaries, causing a 'semantic sawtooth effect' that degrades recognition of small or irregularly shaped elements—especially in lightweight models. Overlapping cropping, the most obvious fix, introduces redundant information that triggers hallucinations and can actually worsen performance. The paper seeks a parameter-free, plug-and-play mechanism that supplies complementary multi-scale semantics without modifying the model architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253090/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an arbitrary high-resolution RGB image (e.g., 1288×1257) and a text prompt. The image is dynamically resized to match the closest pre-defined aspect ratio from a set of grid configurations (bounded by N_min=1 and N_max=24 tiles), then partitioned into 448×448 pixel tiles across three scale groups. The LLM produces a free-form text answer; no structured output format is enforced.

## Method

The core contribution is the Complementary Image Pyramid (CIP), which dynamically generates three images at different scales from a single input: a detailed group (high tile count, closest aspect ratio to the original), an adaptive group (medium tile count, with crop lines deliberately offset from the detailed group to simulate cross-tile interaction), and a global group (1×1 tile, low-res full view). All tiles are encoded by a shared vision encoder (InternViT) and projected via MLP, then concatenated as visual tokens fed to the LLM. To manage the extra token overhead, the Scale Compression Mechanism (SCM) reuses the first two LLM layers as a parameter-free attention module: tokens from the adaptive, global, and text groups attend to the detailed-group tokens, and only the top-K highest-attention detailed tokens are retained, compressing up to 90% of redundant visual tokens with minimal accuracy loss. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play, though fine-tuning with CIP further improves results.

## Results

Mini-Monkey (2B) achieves 806 on OCRBench, surpassing the 8B InternVL2-8B (794) by 12 points and the 9B GLM4-V (786) by 20 points. On general multimodal benchmarks, Mini-Monkey outperforms the InternVL2-2B baseline on all 8 evaluated benchmarks, with notable gains on POPE (88.0 vs 85.2, +2.8%) and HallusionBench (38.8 vs 37.9, +0.9%). On document understanding accuracy metrics, Mini-Monkey leads InternVL2-2B by 2.6% on TextVQA, 3.2% on InfoVQA, and 4.4% on POIE. The method is training-free plug-and-play and can be further improved via fine-tuning, requiring only 8× RTX 3090 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mini-Monkey (2B) consistently outperforms its direct baseline InternVL2-2B across all 17 benchmarks, with the largest gains on OCRBench (+22) and POPE (+2.8%). The headline result is that the 2B Mini-Monkey surpasses the 8B InternVL2-8B by 12 points on OCRBench (806 vs 794) and the 9B GLM4-V by 20 points (806 vs 786), demonstrating that CIP compensates for model capacity in fine-grained text recognition. CIP is validated as plug-and-play across architectures: it improves MiniCPM-V-2-2.8B (+22 OCRBench), LLaVA-OV-0.5B (+23 OCRBench), and InternVL2-8B (+15 OCRBench) without architectural modification. Overlapping cropping, a natural alternative to address semantic discontinuity, actually degrades performance (OCRBench 758 vs 784 baseline), confirming that CIP's complementary multi-scale design is superior. For token compression, SCM outperforms FastV by 21.5% on OCRBench at 50% compression and 4.4% at 90% compression, while maintaining or slightly improving MME scores. Direct fine-tuning of the baseline without CIP degrades MME by 18.5 points, whereas fine-tuning with CIP improves it by 7.4 points, showing CIP stabilizes the optimization process.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the InternVL2-2B cropping-based MLLM architecture and addresses a specific failure mode—semantic discontinuity at crop boundaries—that prior high-resolution MLLMs (TextMonkey, HiRes-LLaVA, InternVL2) have not adequately resolved. Unlike those prior methods, which modify the model architecture or introduce new trainable modules to handle fragmentation, CIP operates purely at the image level, constructing a multi-scale pyramid with non-overlapping crop lines across scales, making it a parameter-free, plug-and-drop addition. The Scale Compression Mechanism extends the FastV token-pruning idea by leveraging cross-scale attention (using high-information-density tokens from adaptive/global groups to select from low-density detailed-group tokens) rather than pruning within a single scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of CIP means it can be retrofitted onto any existing cropping-based MLLM without retraining, which is practically valuable for the many lightweight MLLMs deployed in resource-constrained settings. The finding that direct fine-tuning of the baseline degrades performance while CIP-augmented fine-tuning improves it suggests that the semantic discontinuity introduced by cropping actively harms the optimization landscape—a non-obvious insight for practitioners. Open questions include whether a trainable Feature Pyramid Network (acknowledged as future work) could outperform the image-centric pyramid, and whether the approach generalizes to video or other sequential modalities where the paper reports only marginal gains.
