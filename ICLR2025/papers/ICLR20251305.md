## Problem

In constrained multi-objective RL, linear scalarization with deep function approximators tends to converge to local optima due to gradient conflicts between objectives, while Lagrangian-based constraint handling introduces additional multipliers that destabilize training. No existing method simultaneously prevents gradient conflicts and handles safety constraints without extra optimization variables, leaving a gap for stable, globally convergent CMORL algorithms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251305/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observation vectors (dimensions range from 8 to 376 across tasks) and a preference vector ω on the probability simplex, producing a single universal policy π(·|s, ω) that outputs an action distribution conditioned on both state and preference. Training is off-policy via a replay buffer of (trajectory, preference) pairs, and after training the user queries the universal policy with arbitrary preferences to retrieve distinct policies from the constrained-Pareto front without retraining.

## Method

CoMOGA reformulates the multi-objective maximization as a constrained optimization problem where each objective is converted into a constraint requiring a minimum improvement proportional to its preference weight, and existing safety constraints are merged into the same constraint set. The policy gradient is then obtained by solving a quadratic program within a trust region (parameterized by the Fisher information matrix, as in TRPO), which structurally guarantees g_i^T g ≥ 0 for every objective, eliminating gradient conflicts without projection or surgery. A recovery step solves a separate QP containing only safety constraints when the current policy is infeasible, avoiding Lagrange multipliers entirely. The universal policy is trained by minimizing KL divergence to intermediate policies generated for sampled preferences. Under Slater's condition and with the Fisher information matrix, the method provably converges to a constrained-Pareto optimal policy in tabular settings, with an O(1/√T) convergence rate derived via a generalized natural policy gradient framework.

## Results

CoMOGA is evaluated on three task suites (Safety Gymnasium, MO-Gymnasium, Legged Robot Locomotion) using hypervolume (HV, higher is better) and normalized sparsity (SP, lower is better) as the primary metrics. CoMOGA achieves the highest HV in all four Safety Gymnasium tasks and 3 out of 6 MO-Gymnasium tasks, while attaining the lowest SP in 5 out of 6 MO-Gymnasium tasks. In legged robot locomotion, CoMOGA achieves the highest HV and lowest SP while satisfying all three safety constraints (body balance, CoM height, foot contact timing), with the largest margin over baselines in the dynamically unstable bipedal task. All baselines that violate constraints (LP3, PD-MORL) are outperformed, and even constraint-satisfying baselines (CAPQL, CAGrad) show lower HV or higher SP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LP3 (Huang et al., 2022), CoMOGA matches performance in the quadrupedal locomotion task but clearly surpasses it in the bipedal task, where LP3 underperforms due to gradient conflicts in unstable dynamics; LP3 also violates safety constraints in at least one Safety Gymnasium task and shows the lowest HV in the MO-Gymnasium walker2d task. Against PD-MORL (Basaklar et al., 2023), CoMOGA achieves higher HV across most tasks and, critically, satisfies all constraints whereas PD-MORL violates them in at least one Safety Gymnasium task. Against CAPQL (Lu et al., 2023), CoMOGA outperforms in HV across all tasks; CAPQL does satisfy all constraints but shows notably weak performance in the single-agent Safety Gymnasium tasks, suggesting difficulty balancing objectives and constraints simultaneously. Against CAGrad (Liu et al., 2021), CoMOGA achieves comparable HV in the Multi-Car-Goal task but substantially lower SP, indicating a more uniformly distributed CP front; in other tasks CoMOGA dominates on both metrics. The ablation study further confirms that removing the gradient-conflict-avoidance mechanism (CoMOGA+LS) reduces HV, while replacing the QP-based constraint handling with Lagrangian multipliers (CoMOGA+Lag) causes training instability.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMOGA unifies three previously separate threads: TRPO's trust-region QP formulation for policy updates, conflict-averse gradient aggregation from multi-task learning (CAGrad, PCGrad, Nash-MTL), and primal methods for constrained RL (CRPO, CPO). The key conceptual departure is reformulating multi-objective maximization as a constrained optimization problem where each objective becomes a lower-bound constraint scaled by its preference weight, thereby guaranteeing no objective decreases during any update. This avoids both the local-optima trap of linear scalarization in deep RL and the training instability of Lagrangian (primal-dual) methods that introduce extra multiplier variables. The convergence analysis builds directly on the natural policy gradient framework from CRPO (Xu et al., 2021), extending it to the multi-objective constrained setting via a generalized policy update rule (Theorem 4.2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first CMORL algorithm with a proven convergence guarantee to a constrained-Pareto optimal policy in tabular settings, while simultaneously avoiding gradient conflicts and eliminating the need for Lagrange multipliers. The generalized policy update rule in Theorem 4.2 serves as a template from which other CMORL algorithms with convergence guarantees can be derived, potentially accelerating future work in this area. A noted limitation is that by strictly preventing any objective from decreasing, the trained policy set may cover only a narrow portion of the CP front; the authors suggest relaxing the gradient-conflict condition as a direction for expanding coverage. Open questions include whether the convergence guarantee extends to function-approximation settings and how the method scales to many objectives or constraints where the QP dimension grows.
