## Problem

Constructing robot simulations remains labor-intensive, and existing generative simulation methods struggle to produce low-level control (e.g., trajectories) from high-level textual descriptions, often requiring a new policy to be trained for each generated reward function. ReGen addresses this by inverting the design process: rather than generating behaviors from text, it generates plausible environments conditioned on a known robot behavior, leveraging the observation that behaviors are far more limited than the diverse contexts in which they occur.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a structured robot behavior—either a motion trajectory τ built from motion primitives (e.g., stationary, driving_forward) or a reward function R—along with its natural-language description and a graph-structured simulator asset database D_asset that enumerates simulatable entities, their properties, and supported states. The output is a directed causal graph over event, entity, and property nodes, which is then translated into an executable symbolic program: a Finite State Machine specifying initial preconditions, a transition function over abstract states, and terminal post-conditions, all parameterized by a CP-SAT solver to satisfy spatial and temporal constraints within the simulation engine (CARLA or PyBullet).

## Method

ReGen performs inverse design through iterative LLM-guided graph expansion (GPT-4o, temp=0, top-p=0), starting from the behavior as a leaf node and expanding backward via two atomic operations: node proposal (the LLM generates candidate event, entity, or property nodes given a source node and optional prior context) and edge construction (the LLM simultaneously evaluates the plausibility of all candidate connections to the source node, performing rejection sampling against D_asset to ensure simulatability). The expansion proceeds in stages—first building a causal event graph, then attaching relevant entities, then assigning properties—enforcing a directed acyclic structure and allowing user-specified stopping criteria. For grounding, each subgraph forming a unique event sequence is converted by the LLM into a Finite State Machine whose abstract states are implemented as executable code checking simulation conditions (e.g., proximity, velocity, sensor readings); the CP-SAT solver then searches for concrete parameter values (coordinates, speeds) that satisfy all FSM constraints, and the simulation is accepted only if it terminates in an accepting state. This architecture decouples scenario reasoning (graph) from physical feasibility verification (FSM + solver), enabling controllable counterfactual generation, mental-state simulation, and multi-sensor reasoning without retraining any policy.

## Results

On driving scenario diversity (Table 1), ReGen achieves 0.2268 Embedding Diversity and 0.7377 Self-BLEU Diversity, outperforming all baselines on embedding diversity (next best: zero-shot GPT-4o at 0.1767) and matching or exceeding the highest-temperature zero-shot on Self-BLEU while using conservative settings (top-p=0, temp=0). On manipulation diversity (Table 2), ReGen reaches 0.6560 embedding diversity with only 10 unique reward functions, surpassing RLBench (0.5819) and RoboGen (0.5787). For corner-case generation in SafeBench (Table 3), ReGen achieves collision rates of 0.90/0.83/0.96/0.77 across four scenario types, exceeding ChatScene (0.89/0.70/0.95/0.79) on three of four. The generated environments also produce a more challenging VLM probing dataset, with GPT-4o accuracy dropping to 0.63 (vs. 0.87 on DriveCoT and 0.90 on DriveLM), confirming greater scenario complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReGen clearly outperforms all baselines on driving embedding diversity (0.2268 vs. 0.1767 for the best zero-shot and 0.1214 for ChatScene), demonstrating that the graph-based inverse design captures broader causal contexts than flat LLM prompting or safety-critical-only generation. On Self-BLEU, ReGen (0.7377) is slightly below the high-temperature zero-shot (0.7814) but substantially above ChatScene (0.2945) and DriveLM (0.4731), and the paper argues the gap to zero-shot is within variance given ReGen uses deterministic decoding. In manipulation, ReGen (0.6560) beats RLBench (0.5819), RoboGen (0.5787), GenSim (0.4350), and Behavior-100 (0.5513) on embedding diversity, despite reusing only 10 reward functions. For corner-case collision rates in SafeBench, ReGen matches or exceeds ChatScene on three of four scenario types (SO: 0.90 vs. 0.89; TO: 0.83 vs. 0.70; LaneC: 0.96 vs. 0.95) and trails slightly on VP (0.77 vs. 0.79), while both substantially outperform adversarial-policy baselines like AdvSim (0.51/0.33/0.86/0.87) and Learn-to-Collide (0.30/0.09/0.87/0.83). On VLM probing, ReGen's generated scenarios are significantly harder for GPT-4o (0.63 accuracy) than DriveCoT (0.87) or DriveLM (0.90), confirming the environments encode more complex reasoning than existing driving datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the inverse design paradigm from computational design (e.g., nanophotonics) into the generative simulation domain, inverting the standard forward pipeline (text → environment → behavior) used by prior LLM-based simulation generators like RoboGen, GenSim, and ChatScene. Rather than generating new behaviors or reward functions from textual descriptions, ReGen conditions on a given robot behavior and works backward to synthesize plausible causal contexts, represented as a directed graph of events, entities, and properties. The grounding step—converting the graph into a finite-state machine solved by a CP-SAT constraint solver—builds on the authors' prior Text2Drive work but applies it to a fundamentally different conditioning direction. The method is positioned as orthogonal to existing generative simulation approaches, complementing them by varying environmental context while reusing fixed behaviors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The inverse design framing enables behavior-level unit testing and targeted data augmentation for robot policies, which is practically valuable for safety-critical domains like autonomous driving where corner-case coverage is essential. The 80% (driving) and 78% (manipulation) success rates, while strong, reveal that overly strict FSM constraints and simulation engine limitations (articulated objects, temperature, dynamic actors in manipulation) remain significant bottlenecks. Open questions include whether the graph expansion approach scales to multi-agent interactions with richer dynamics, how to handle the gap between LLM-reasoned scenarios and physically simulatable ones more gracefully, and whether the diversity gains translate into meaningful improvements in downstream policy robustness beyond the VLM probing experiments.
