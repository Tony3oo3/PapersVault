## Problem

Traditional neuron segmentation in electron microscopy relies on watershed-based clustering to convert predicted affinities into fragments, which introduces unnatural boundary artifacts, makes incorrect neuron mergers irrecoverable, and is inherently non-parallelizable on GPU. Query-based instance segmentation methods from natural images offer a direct-prediction alternative that avoids watershed, but cannot be naively transferred to 3D EM data due to prohibitive memory costs and the homogeneous, morphologically complex appearance of neurons that makes learnable queries difficult to train from scratch.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D volume EM image (D×H×W) and processes it in cropped blocks (e.g., 17×257×257 voxels for AC3/AC4, 76×150×150 for ZEBRAFINCH) to fit GPU memory. It directly outputs N segmentation probability maps of shape (N, D, H, W), from which the final neuron ID per voxel is obtained by argmax over the query dimension (id 0 = background). For large volumes, block predictions are assembled by reusing an agglomeration function to merge neurons split across block boundaries, without requiring the overlap prediction that affinity-based methods need to mitigate border effects.

## Method

The proposed AGQ framework combines a 3D U-Net backbone with a lightweight affinity branch and a K-Net-style 3D neuron decoder. The affinity branch predicts 3-channel affinities, which are thresholded at their mean and processed via connected component labeling to yield coarse binary masks; each mask is multiplied with and averaged over the image features to produce an affinity-guided query that encodes coarse neuron structure. These are concatenated with a fixed set of learnable queries to form the initial decoder input. Each decoder stage updates queries via dynamic convolution (conditioned on previous-stage probability maps and features), self-attention (allowing queries to interact and disambiguate neurons), and a linear projection to produce the next-stage probability maps. Training jointly optimizes a bipartite-matched segmentation loss (dice + cross-entropy via Hungarian matching), a contrastive feature loss that encourages distinct per-neuron feature representations, and a binary cross-entropy affinity loss, enabling the decoder to progressively correct merging errors from the coarse affinity priors across stages.

## Results

On the AC3/AC4 benchmark, AGQ achieves the lowest overall VOI (0.967) and the lowest VOI_split (0.677) among all compared methods, while maintaining a competitive Arand of 0.095. Inference time is 33.7 s for the full test set, representing a ~340% speedup over ResUNet (147.8 s) and ~260% over PEA (122.7 s), with no watershed step required. On ZEBRAFINCH, AGQ achieves the best VOI on all three test volumes (0.177, 1.278, 0.338) and completes inference in 6.4 s versus 17.4 s for PEA and 21.1 s for ResUNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGQ outperforms all watershed-based baselines on overall VOI and VOI_split on AC3/AC4, achieving 0.967 VOI versus 1.054 (FragViT), 1.084 (PEA), and 1.295 (ResUNet). On Arand, AGQ (0.095) is slightly behind APViT (0.078) and FragViT (0.093) but ahead of all other methods including PEA (0.094) and SwinUNETR (0.110). The most striking advantage is inference speed: AGQ's 33.7 s total is roughly 4× faster than ResUNet (147.8 s), 3.6× faster than PEA (122.7 s), and eliminates the watershed step entirely (N/A vs. ~30–43 s for all baselines). On ZEBRAFINCH, AGQ achieves the best VOI on all three test volumes (e.g., 0.338 vs. 0.534 for PEA on Volume-3) while running 2.7× faster than PEA (6.4 s vs. 17.4 s). The method also avoids the overlap-prediction strategy required by affinity-based methods, further reducing model inference time (27.6 s vs. 60.2 s for PEA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant bottom-up paradigm in EM neuron segmentation (affinity prediction → watershed → agglomeration) by adopting a query-based framework inspired by K-Net (Zhang et al., 2021) and DETR-style bipartite matching. The key conceptual departure is replacing watershed with affinity-guided queries: instead of converting predicted affinities into fragments via a non-parallelizable CPU algorithm, the affinities are used to initialize queries that carry coarse structural priors into a lightweight 3D decoder. The method also draws on dynamic convolution and self-attention from natural-image instance segmentation, but introduces the critical adaptation of embedding bottom-up affinity cues into query generation to handle the homogeneous, morphologically complex nature of neurons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating watershed removes a fundamental bottleneck in connectomics pipelines: it is CPU-bound, non-parallelizable, produces straight-line artifacts, and generates tens of thousands of fragments that slow downstream agglomeration. The 2–3× speedup while improving accuracy is practically significant for mm³-scale volumes where processing already requires hundreds of GPU hours. Open questions include whether the reused agglomeration-based block assembly can be replaced by a more efficient dedicated mechanism, how the method scales to petavoxel datasets, and whether the failure modes (redundant predictions, block-boundary splits) can be addressed without additional training data or auxiliary tasks.
