## Problem

ML-based MILP solvers like Neural Diving fix predicted variable values directly, risking infeasible or low-quality reduced problems when predictions are inaccurate, while Predict-and-Search avoids fixing but sacrifices dimensionality reduction. The key unresolved question is how to selectively identify which predicted values are reliable enough to fix, combining the problem-reduction benefit of fixing with the feasibility benefit of neighborhood search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253409/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a MILP instance encoded as a weighted bipartite graph (constraint nodes connected to variable nodes by nonzero coefficients) and a trained GNN predictor that outputs marginal probabilities for binary variables. At each of four iterations (with time budgets of 100, 100, 200, 600 seconds), the GNN predicts on the current reduced instance, and a traditional solver (Gurobi or SCIP) performs a trust-region search to produce a reference solution. The output is the best feasible objective value found within 1,000 seconds, along with a sequence of progressively reduced MILP subproblems. During training, data augmentation generates reduced instances by fixing random variable subsets to near-optimal values, aligning the predictor with the distributional shift encountered at test time.

## Method

Apollo-MILP alternates between a prediction step (GNN predicts marginal probabilities for unfixed binary variables) and a correction step (trust-region search around the predicted partial solution yields a reference solution from the solver). The core novelty is the UEBO metric—an upper bound on the KL divergence between predicted and optimal distributions, decomposed into prediction uncertainty (entropy) and prediction-correction discrepancy (cross-entropy)—which identifies which predicted values are reliable. A key theoretical result (Theorem 2) shows UEBO is monotonically decreasing in prediction-correction consistency, enabling a simple fixing rule: fix variables where the predicted and reference values agree (Equation 7). Under mild consistency conditions (Assumption 1), Theorem 3 guarantees that fixing consistent variables yields higher precision than fixing based on prediction or reference alone, and Corollary 4 ensures feasibility of the reduced problem whenever the trust-region search is feasible. The GNN predictor is trained with cross-entropy loss on an augmented dataset combining original and reduced instances to handle the distributional shift between full and partially-fixed problems.

## Results

Under a 1,000s time limit, Apollo-MILP+Gurobi achieves the lowest absolute primal gap across all four benchmarks: 129.41 on CA (52.2% improvement over Gurobi), 0.10 on SC (77.8% improvement), and 0.00 on both IP and WA (100% improvement, matching the best-known solution). On the more challenging real-world benchmarks IP and WA, Apollo-MILP+Gurobi finds better solutions within 1,000s than Gurobi achieves in 3,600s. With SCIP as the backbone solver, improvements are more modest (27.5% on CA, 70.2% on SC, 29.2% on IP, 6.9% on WA) but Apollo-MILP still consistently outperforms all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Apollo-MILP outperforms all five baselines (Gurobi, SCIP, ND, PS, ConPS) on every benchmark with both Gurobi and SCIP as the backbone solver. Against the strongest ML baseline ConPS+Gurobi, Apollo-MILP reduces the gap from 152.49→129.41 on CA, 0.25→0.10 on SC, 0.19→0.00 on IP, and 0.49→0.00 on WA. Neural Diving (ND) is notably the weakest ML method on CA (gap 1613.59 vs. Gurobi's 319.07), confirming that direct variable fixing without correction is harmful when predictions are inaccurate. PS+Gurobi improves over Gurobi on CA (258.36 vs. 319.07) but is still substantially outperformed by Apollo-MILP (129.41). The advantage of Apollo-MILP is most dramatic on the real-world IP and WA benchmarks, where it achieves zero gap (matching BKS) while all other methods retain non-zero gaps, and it surpasses Gurobi's 3,600s solution quality within just 1,000s.

## Relation to Prior Work

Apollo-MILP sits at the intersection of two established ML-for-MILP paradigms: Neural Diving (ND), which predicts and directly fixes variable values to reduce problem dimensionality, and Predict-and-Search (PS), which performs trust-region search around a predicted partial solution without fixing. The key conceptual departure is treating PS's trust-region search not merely as a final optimization step but as a *correction* signal that validates or invalidates ND's fixing decisions. The novel UEBO metric formalizes this by upper-bounding the intractable KL divergence between predicted and optimal distributions using the solver-provided reference solution, enabling a principled consistency-based fixing rule that neither ND nor PS alone provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the solver's search feedback, previously treated as a black-box optimization step, carries actionable information about prediction reliability — a perspective that had been overlooked in the ML4CO literature. The practical impact is substantial: Apollo-MILP closes the solution gap to zero on the hardest benchmarks (IP, WA) within 1,000s, outperforming Gurobi running 3.6× longer, and the theoretical guarantees (precision improvement, feasibility preservation) provide confidence beyond empirical validation. Open questions include whether the consistency assumption (Assumption 1) holds broadly across problem families, how the method scales to very large instances where multiple solver calls become prohibitive, and whether the GNN predictor's accuracy ceiling limits the framework's ultimate performance.
