## Problem

Existing quantization methods for transformer inference focus almost exclusively on linear layers, leaving the O(N²) attention computation unaccelerated in FP16. No prior work systematically investigates quantization of attention, and direct 8-bit quantization of Q, K, P, V causes severe accuracy collapse (e.g., blurry images, random-guessing LLM accuracy) due to channel-wise outliers in K and inconsistent PV quantization error across layers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard FP16 Q, K, V matrices (N×d, where d is typically 64 or 128) and produces the attention output O in FP16, operating as a direct drop-in replacement for FlashAttention-2's tiled kernel. It is implemented in Triton targeting RTX4090 and RTX3090 GPUs, with block sizes of 128 for Q and 64 for K/V, and supports both causal and non-causal masking. No retraining or model modification is required; the user simply swaps the attention kernel at inference time.

## Method

SageAttention addresses two key challenges: (1) channel-wise outliers in K are handled by subtracting the token-mean before quantization (γ(K) = K − mean(K)), which is mathematically invariant under softmax and costs <0.2% overhead; (2) the PV matmul is kept in FP16 with an FP16 accumulator rather than quantized to INT8, which is both more accurate (99.99% cosine similarity vs. 56–79% for INT8) and 2× faster than an FP32 accumulator on target hardware. Q and K are quantized to INT8 (per-token or per-block) because INT8 matmul is 2–4× faster than FP16 on RTX4090/3090 and more accurate than FP8 for attention. An adaptive strategy selects the faster INT8-PV kernel (SAGEAttn-vB) for layers where its cosine similarity exceeds 99.8%, falling back to the FP16-PV kernel (SAGEAttn-B) otherwise, yielding an additional ~12% speedup. Quantization is fused with the preceding ROPE operation to eliminate extra memory I/O, and the 1/√d scaling is absorbed into the Q quantization step.

## Results

SageAttention achieves a peak throughput of 340 TOPS on RTX4090 (headdim=64/128), reaching 52% of theoretical INT8 throughput, compared to 165 TOPS for FlashAttention2. The average real speedup across five models (CogvideoX, Llama2, UltraPixel, Unidiffuser, TIMM) is 2.83× over the original attention implementation. End-to-end metrics show negligible degradation: Llama2 WikiText perplexity goes from 5.823 to 5.824, MMLU accuracy stays at 0.46, CogvideoX FScore actually improves from 3.768 to 3.834, and UltraPixel FID changes from 179.78 to 179.79. On TIMM ImageNet, SageAttention even slightly surpasses full precision (84.74% vs 84.79% is within noise, while Sketch and ImageNet-R improve).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashAttention2, SageAttention delivers a 2.1× speedup (340 vs. 165 TOPS peak on RTX4090) while maintaining virtually identical end-to-end metrics across all six evaluated models. Against xformers, the speedup is 2.7×, with the largest real-world gain (5.89×) on TIMM where the original uses a naive Torch attention. Against FlashAttention3's FP8 quantized version (Hopper-only), SageAttention achieves substantially better accuracy: Unidiffuser FID of 166.49 vs. 394.13, CogvideoX FScore of 3.834 vs. 3.394, and UltraPixel FID of 179.79 vs. 383.61, while also running on more widely available RTX4090/3090 hardware. Against ViDiT-Q (W8A8) on CogvideoX, SageAttention achieves a higher FScore (3.834 vs. 3.734) and a larger end-to-end speedup (34.3% vs. 22% theoretical maximum), since ViDiT-Q only quantizes linear layers and its official code does not include acceleration kernels.

## Relation to Prior Work

SageAttention builds directly on FlashAttention2's tiling and online-softmax framework, extending it with 8-bit quantization of the QK^T and PV matrix multiplications. It departs from the dominant quantization literature (SmoothQuant, AWQ, Q-diffusion), which targets linear layers and leaves attention in FP16, by systematically addressing the unique challenges of quantizing attention—namely channel-wise outliers in K and the accuracy fragility of quantizing P and V. The K-smoothing transform (subtracting the token-mean) is conceptually related to SmoothQuant's smoothing but is specifically designed for attention's softmax invariance property, and the choice to retain P,V in FP16 with an FP16 accumulator rather than quantizing them to INT8 distinguishes SageAttention from both FlashAttention3's FP8 approach and naive INT8 quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Attention's O(N²) cost dominates inference for long-sequence workloads (video generation, LLM prefilling), and SageAttention delivers a 2×+ speedup on widely available consumer GPUs (RTX4090, 3090) with negligible end-to-end metric loss, making it immediately deployable without retraining. A key limitation is hardware specificity: the INT8 advantage (2–4× over FP16) depends on Tensor Core configurations that vary across GPU generations, and the method has not been validated on Hopper or newer architectures where FP8 may be competitive. Open questions include whether the K-smoothing insight generalizes to training-time quantization, whether INT4 or lower precisions become viable with further outlier control, and how the adaptive per-layer kernel selection scales to models with heterogeneous attention patterns.
