## Problem

In-context learning (ICL) enables LLMs to adapt to new tasks without fine-tuning, but it requires demonstrations in the prompt, consuming ~20× more tokens and interrupting the context. Fine-tuning, while effective, is computationally intensive and lacks the flexibility for dynamic task adaptation. The paper asks whether ICL-elicited capabilities can be stored externally and reactivated on demand without any additional inference tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250918/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, ELICIT takes a plain text query (with a short task template prepended, ~108 tokens total) and a pre-built capability library of task vectors, producing a text response identical in format to zero-shot output. The capability library is constructed offline: for each of 20 tasks, 10 ICL prompts (each with 16 demonstrations) are processed through the model to extract full-layer hidden states (θ ∈ R^{L×d}), paired with the ICL prompt text and a per-task optimal intervention layer l*. No demonstrations appear in the inference prompt, so token usage matches zero-shot exactly.

## Method

ELICIT consists of two modules. (1) Build Capability Library: for each task, ICL prompts are run through the model and the last-token hidden states at all L layers are stored as task vectors; a validation-set sweep identifies the optimal intervention layer l* per task-model pair. (2) Dynamic Capability Elicitation: a SimCSE RoBERTa classifier with a 2-layer MLP head (trained on 10K query–prompt pairs, AUC 0.96) scores the query against all library prompts; if the top score exceeds a recall-0.8 threshold, Dynamic Top-K Thresholding selects the top-10 vectors and a majority vote determines the final intervention layer. The task vector is then additively injected into the hidden state at that layer (h̃_l = h_l + 2·θ_l), a strength chosen to balance task accuracy against language-modeling degradation. The LLM itself is never fine-tuned; only the lightweight retriever is trained, making the system plug-and-play across model architectures (transformer and Mamba SSM).

## Results

Across 20 tasks and 4 models (Llama3-8B, Mistral-7B, Pythia-2.8B, Mamba-2.8B), ELICIT achieves an average improvement of 11.4% over zero-shot while maintaining identical token usage (~108–123 tokens vs. ~1,883–2,655 for 16-shot baselines). For Llama3-8B, ELICIT lifts average accuracy from 31.2% (zero-shot) to 43.5%, surpassing 16-shot BM25 (55.2%) in Reasoning (+15.1% over zero-shot) and Safety (+14.4%). On Mistral-7B, the gain is even larger at +15.5% (28.2% → 43.7%), and on the smaller Pythia-2.8B and Mamba-2.8B models, ELICIT improves averages by +8.1% and +12.8% respectively. ELICIT also generalizes to unseen tasks (e.g., +15.8% on MMLU-Psychology for Llama3) without any task-specific information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to 16-shot ICL (Brown et al., 2020), ELICIT uses roughly 1/20th the tokens (~108 vs. ~2,350 for Llama3) yet achieves competitive or superior performance on several domains: on Pythia, ELICIT's NLU score of 64.0% far exceeds 16-shot's 33.3%, and on Mamba, ELICIT's Safety score of 48.2% surpasses 16-shot's 35.4%. Against 16-shot BM25 retrieval (Robertson et al., 2009), ELICIT matches or exceeds the baseline on smaller models (Pythia avg 32.7% vs. 24.7%; Mamba avg 34.5% vs. 31.3%) while using ~18× fewer tokens. On larger models (Llama3, Mistral), ELICIT trails 16-shot BM25 in absolute accuracy (43.5% vs. 55.2% for Llama3) but the gap narrows substantially in Reasoning and Safety, and ELICIT's advantage is that it is task-agnostic—requiring no knowledge of the query's task type—whereas both ICL and BM25 baselines need task-specific demonstrations. When combined with BM25, ELICIT provides additional gains for smaller models (Pythia: 22.1% → 28.3%) but shows mixed results for larger models, suggesting a scale-dependent interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELICIT builds directly on the task vector framework introduced by Hendel et al. (2023) and the linear-combination intervention strategy from Todd et al. (2023), both of which showed that ICL behavior can be captured by manipulating hidden-state activations. However, prior work treated task vectors as ephemeral, computed on-the-fly representations for a single prompt. ELICIT departs by externally storing task vectors in a persistent capability library and introducing a learned retrieval module (SimCSE + MLP classifier) to dynamically select and apply relevant vectors at inference time, drawing conceptual inspiration from the modular LLM literature (Pfeiffer et al., 2023; Fedus et al., 2022). The paper positions itself as the first to comprehensively explore modular, externally-stored task representations for LLM augmentation, distinguishing itself from in-context vector steering methods (Liu et al., 2023b) that manipulate internal representations without a retrieval or library mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ELICIT offers a practical, plug-and-play mechanism to boost LLM performance by ~11% over zero-shot without increasing inference token cost, which is attractive for deployment scenarios where context length is constrained. The selective activation capability (e.g., a math-only library boosting math from 2.6% to 21.3% while leaving other domains stable) suggests a path toward composable, on-demand capability modules. Key limitations include the scale-dependent degradation when combined with BM25 on larger models, the unresolved challenge of adapting to instruction-tuned models, and the trade-off between task-specific gains and general language modeling quality at higher intervention strengths. Open questions include whether multi-layer intervention (which showed modest gains in preliminary experiments) can be principled, and whether the circuit-level tension between ICL and language modeling circuits can be better understood to optimize intervention strength.
