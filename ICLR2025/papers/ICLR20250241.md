## Problem

Existing watermarking schemes for generative image models either visibly degrade image quality or introduce observable correlations across generations, making them unacceptable for high-value models. No prior scheme achieves *undetectability* — the property that no efficient adversary can distinguish watermarked from un-watermarked images even after many adaptive queries — while also remaining robust to removal attacks and capable of encoding messages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250241/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The watermarking pipeline takes a text prompt, a pre-generated PRC watermarking key (parameterized by latent dimension n, target false-positive rate F, and parity-check sparsity t), and optionally a binary message to encode, and produces a 512×512 RGB image from a 4×64×64 latent space. Detection takes an image and the key, returning True/False with a provably bounded FPR ≤ F; decoding additionally recovers the embedded message (up to 512 bits under attack, 2500 bits without attack) or returns None. The scheme requires no model fine-tuning and integrates directly into existing diffusion model APIs.

## Method

The PRC watermark embeds a pseudorandom error-correcting code (LDPC-based, with t-sparse parity checks) into the *signs* of the initial Gaussian latent of a latent diffusion model: a PRC codeword c ∈ {−1,1}^n is sampled, and the initial latent is set as z_i^(T) = c_i · |y_i| with y ~ N(0, I_n), so magnitudes remain standard Gaussian while signs follow the pseudorandom PRC pattern. Undetectability follows directly from the PRC's pseudorandomness guarantee: any polynomial number of watermarked latents are computationally indistinguishable from i.i.d. Gaussian samples. Detection recovers the initial latent via exact DPM-solver inversion (Hong et al., 2023), converts coordinates to soft sign estimates using an erf-based posterior, and runs a likelihood-ratio test over the parity checks with a threshold calibrated to the user-specified FPR. Message encoding uses belief propagation with ordered statistics decoding over the same LDPC structure, and the entire scheme requires no additional training or fine-tuning of the diffusion model.

## Results

On both MS-COCO and Stable Diffusion Prompt datasets, the PRC watermark is the only scheme that preserves image quality across all three metrics (FID, CLIP Score, Inception Score) within 3 standard errors of the un-watermarked baseline. On the SDP dataset, PRC achieves FID 63.74 vs. 63.46 (original), CLIP 0.6146 vs. 0.6119, and IS 7.50 vs. 7.50, while all other in-processing methods (Tree-Ring, Gaussian Shading, Stable Signature) show significant degradation. Without attacks, the PRC watermark achieves TPR=1.0 at FPR=0.01, and can robustly encode 512 bits (up to 2500 bits for non-attacked images). The LPIPS variability score for PRC (0.7589) is nearly identical to the un-watermarked baseline (0.7570), whereas Gaussian Shading drops to 0.6503.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PRC against 8 baselines: 5 post-processing methods (DwtDct, DwtDctSvd, RivaGAN, StegaStamp, SSL) and 3 in-processing methods (Stable Signature, Tree-Ring, Gaussian Shading). PRC is the only scheme that preserves all three quality metrics (FID, CLIP, IS) within 3 standard errors of the un-watermarked baseline on both datasets; StegaStamp causes the largest degradation (FID 79.89 vs. 76.40 on COCO), while Tree-Ring and Gaussian Shading both inflate FID by 5+ points on SDP. On perceptual variability (LPIPS), PRC (0.7589) matches the un-watermarked baseline (0.7570), whereas Gaussian Shading drops to 0.6503 and Tree-Ring to 0.7413. On detectability, a ResNet18 classifier achieves only 50% accuracy (random) on PRC watermarked images, while it quickly learns to detect all other schemes. Among the three lowest-impact in-processing schemes (PRC, Tree-Ring, Gaussian Shading), PRC is the most robust to all 10 tested removal attacks, with no attack reducing TPR below 0.99 while keeping FID under 70. Tree-Ring is particularly vulnerable to the adversarial surrogate attack, which removes its watermark with minimal quality impact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the undetectable watermarking framework from Christ et al. (2024), originally defined for language models, to generative image models by leveraging the pseudorandom error-correcting code (PRC) construction of Christ & Gunn (2024) as the core cryptographic primitive. It builds on the in-processing latent-modification paradigm introduced by Tree-Ring (Wen et al., 2023) but departs fundamentally from all prior image watermarking schemes: rather than imposing a heuristic latent pattern (Tree-Ring) or restricting to a quadrant of latent space (Gaussian Shading), the PRC watermark makes the sign pattern of initial latents cryptographically indistinguishable from random, yielding a provable undetectability guarantee rather than a single-sample distributional equivalence. The scheme also incorporates belief propagation decoding from the PRC literature to enable multi-bit message embedding, going beyond the zero-bit detection of prior in-processing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first watermark for image generation that provably preserves quality under any efficiently computable metric while remaining robust to removal attacks, resolving a tension the field had implicitly accepted: that robustness requires observable distributional shifts. The practical impact is significant given ongoing regulatory pressure (EU AI Act, California AB-3211, US Executive Order) and the limited adoption of existing watermarks due to quality degradation. Key limitations include the acknowledgment that finite parameter choices do not strictly satisfy the theoretical pseudorandomness bounds, making the scheme unsuitable for undetectability-critical applications; the t=2 setting that improves robustness admits fast undetectability-breaking attacks; and the decoder is less robust than the detector. Open questions include whether alternative PRC constructions (Golowich & Moitra, Ghentiyala & Guruswami) yield stronger watermarks, and whether the approach extends to emerging architectures like flow matching models.
