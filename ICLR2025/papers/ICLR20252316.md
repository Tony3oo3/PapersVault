## Problem

Existing Split Conformal Prediction methods for language modeling either depend on internal model logits (unavailable for API-only MLLMs), are restricted to multiple-choice settings with fixed options, or modify model outputs to ensure factuality at the cost of informativeness. This paper addresses the gap of providing statistically rigorous risk control for open-ended VideoQA tasks where the output space is unbounded, no acceptable response is guaranteed among sampled generations, and internal model information is inaccessible.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a video clip (with extracted audio) and a text question as input, and requires only the ability to sample multiple responses from any MLLM (open-source or API-only). It produces a prediction set of semantically deduplicated responses rather than a single answer, along with the Average Prediction Set Size (APSS) as a calibrated uncertainty/risk score. The prediction set size adapts per query: larger sets indicate higher model uncertainty, and the deduplicated APSS provides a stable evaluation metric across different risk levels.

## Method

TRON is a two-step conformal framework. Step 1 defines a novel conformal score r(X_i, Y_i) as the minimum number of samples M_i needed before the correct answer first appears among sampled responses; the (1−α) quantile of these scores over the calibration set yields the sample size M_test for each test point, bounding the probability of missing all acceptable responses by α. Step 2 performs semantic clustering (via bidirectional NLI entailment with DeBERTa) on the candidate set and defines a nonconformity score s(X_i, Y_i) = 1 − F(ŷ_ref), where F is the normalized frequency of the semantically correct response; the (1−β) quantile of these scores gives a threshold that filters the candidate set into the final prediction set. The combined miscoverage is bounded by ε = α + β − αβ. The framework is model-agnostic (no logits required), handles open-ended output spaces, and the paper further shows that deduplicating semantically redundant responses yields more stable APSS-based risk assessment across MLLMs and risk levels.

## Results

TRON achieves statistically rigorous risk control across all 8 MLLMs and 4 VideoQA datasets, with empirical error rates (EER) consistently bounded by the user-specified risk levels α and β (ε = α + β − αβ). On the closed-ended Video-MME dataset, GPT-4o mini attains the highest accuracy at 73.26% with the lowest APSS of 1.14, while the weakest open-source model (VideoLLaMA-7B) scores 38.10% ACC with APSS 2.81. On open-ended MUSIC-AVQA, GPT-4o mini reaches 86.27% ACC with APSS 1.43, and PandaGPT-13B achieves 82.73% ACC but with a higher APSS of 2.03, demonstrating that higher accuracy does not necessarily imply lower uncertainty. The deduplicated APSS provides stable, monotonic uncertainty estimation across risk levels, unlike the raw APSS which becomes ambiguous as ε varies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark TRON against competing conformal prediction methods in a head-to-head comparison; instead, it evaluates the TRON framework across eight MLLMs to demonstrate generalizability. Among the evaluated models, closed-source MLLMs (GPT-4o mini, Gemini-1.5-Pro, Gemini-1.5-Flash) consistently outperform open-source models on both ACC and APSS across all four datasets, with GPT-4o mini achieving the best ACC on VMME (73.26%), NEXT (82.50%), and MUSC (86.27%) while maintaining the lowest APSS. A notable finding is that PandaGPT-13B achieves higher ACC than GPT-4o mini on MSVD (86.04% vs. 85.71%) yet exhibits substantially higher APSS (2.21 vs. 1.21), revealing that accuracy alone is insufficient for assessing model reliability. In the sensitivity analysis (Table 2), replacing the frequency-based nonconformity score with semantic diversity yields smaller APSS (e.g., PandaGPT-7B drops from 2.70 to 2.42) while maintaining EER below the risk bound, indicating more efficient predictions without sacrificing guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Split Conformal Prediction (SCP) from its established applications in classification and multiple-choice QA to open-ended VideoQA with API-only MLLMs, where internal logits are inaccessible. It builds on the conformal risk control framework (Angelopoulos et al., 2024) and self-consistency theory (Wang et al., 2022), but departs from prior NLP conformal methods that either modify model outputs for factuality guarantees (Mohri & Hashimoto, 2024), rely on token-level logits (Quach et al., 2024), or are restricted to closed-ended settings (Kumar et al., 2023; Ye et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is a two-step 'sample then identify' pipeline: first calibrating a minimum sample size via a novel conformal score to approximate closed-ended conditions, then identifying high-quality responses via a frequency-based nonconformity score derived from semantic clustering with bidirectional entailment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRON provides a practical, model-agnostic risk control framework for deploying MLLMs in open-ended VideoQA without requiring logit access, which is critical for proprietary API-based models. The finding that deduplicated APSS offers more stable uncertainty estimation than raw set size across risk levels introduces a potentially useful evaluation metric for MLLMs in open-ended settings. However, guarantees remain marginal (average over the test set) rather than conditional, and the exchangeability assumption limits applicability under distribution shift. Open questions include whether the framework extends to streaming or non-i.i.d. video inputs, and whether richer reliability measures beyond frequency or semantic diversity can tighten conditional coverage.
