## Problem

Existing computational models of the barrel cortex face a fundamental trade-off: highly detailed biologically realistic models are computationally intractable to train and lack behavioral functionality, while mainstream trainable networks (CNNs, RNNs, SNNs) ignore the heterogeneous neuronal subtypes, anatomical connectivity patterns, and columnar organization that define real cortical circuits. This paper addresses the gap by constructing a model that is simultaneously constrained by experimental anatomy and trainable for sensory discrimination tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251468/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network receives spiking whisker sweep signals of shape (550, 31, 3, 2) — 550 time steps, 31 whiskers, 3 base sensors each, and 2 channels (force and torque) — where analog force/torque magnitudes are converted to spike trains via a sigmoid-mapped Bernoulli sampling process with 5-step time windows. External inputs enter through 200 thalamic neurons, and the output is a 3-class object label read out from three excitatory neuronal populations through two linear layers.

## Method

The model is a spiking recurrent network of 4,218 adaptive Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (aLIF) neurons across 13 anatomically defined subtypes (3 excitatory, 10 inhibitory), with connection counts sampled from experimentally measured projection probabilities and all weights clamped positive while excitatory/inhibitory sign is encoded by a binary constant. A key training innovation is a CV-based initialization pipeline: the coefficient of variation of neuronal firing is computed across candidate initial states (varying θ_init and W_init), and the configuration yielding the most disordered (chaotic) activity is selected as the trainable starting point. Training uses BPTT with a Gaussian surrogate gradient at spike timing, and τ_m and τ_adp are made trainable per-neuron to capture dynamic differentiation. The authors also convert the Zhuang et al. (2017) analog whisker sweep dataset into a spiking format using rate coding, providing a biologically meaningful training platform that enables direct comparison with both ANN and SNN baselines.

## Results

On the real-valued whisker sweep dataset, the barrel cortex model achieves 89.1% classification accuracy, surpassing all ANN baselines (ST CNN 67.2%, DB LSTM 70.9%, RNN 78.2%, UGRNN 76.4%) and the best SNN (STSC SNN 87.3%). On the spiking-based dataset, it reaches 81.8%, matching SRNN 256 and TA SNN while exceeding STSC SNN (80.0%). In the whisker deprivation experiment (0–8 whiskers occluded), the barrel model consistently outperforms all SNN baselines, retaining 58.0% accuracy at 8 deprived whiskers versus 53.2% (SRNN), 57.2% (STSC SNN), and 48.4% (TA SNN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The barrel cortex model outperforms all four ANN baselines by a wide margin on the real-valued dataset: +21.9% over ST CNN (67.2%), +18.2% over DB LSTM (70.9%), +10.9% over RNN (78.2%), and +12.7% over UGRNN (76.4%). Among SNNs, it ties SRNN 256 and TA SNN at 81.8% on the spiking-based dataset but surpasses both on the real-valued dataset (89.1% vs. 85.5% and 83.6% respectively), and beats STSC SNN on both (81.8% vs. 80.0% spiking; 89.1% vs. 87.3% real-valued). In the whisker deprivation experiment, the barrel model achieves the highest accuracy at every deprivation level from 1 to 8 whiskers, with the gap widening as more whiskers are removed (e.g., at 8 deprived: 58.0% vs. 48.4% for TA SNN, a 9.6-point advantage). This robustness under sensory degradation is attributed to the biologically constrained connectivity structure that mirrors the redundancy and distributed processing of the real barrel cortex.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the aLIF spiking neuron model (Yin et al., 2020) by making the membrane time constant and adaptive threshold time constant trainable, and embeds it within a network whose topology, neuron subtypes, and connection probabilities are fixed by anatomical measurements from the mouse barrel cortex (Huang et al., 2022). It departs from prior biologically constrained cortical models (Izhikevich-based three-barrel models, dendritic models, Fmr1-KO layer-4 networks) that faithfully replicate circuit structure but lack behavioral perceptual functions, by introducing a BPTT training pipeline with a CV-based initialization criterion that makes such constrained networks trainable. The paper also repurposes the real-valued whisker sweep dataset of Zhuang et al. (2017) into a spiking format via sigmoid-mapped Bernoulli sampling, creating a new sensory input modality for SNN training that mimics thalamic rate coding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small (4,218-neuron), anatomically faithful barrel cortex model can match or exceed mainstream SNNs on whisker-based object discrimination while spontaneously developing biologically plausible features—input-specific firing selectivity, subtype-differentiated dynamics (Pearson r = 0.73 with Izhikevich reference models), and long-tail weighted degree distributions—without explicit supervision on those properties. This provides a concrete template for how biological constraints can simultaneously improve trainability and interpretability in sensory-motor network models. Key limitations include the very small dataset (91 samples per class), a single behavioral task, and the somewhat ad hoc CV-based initialization; open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to larger cortical areas, other sensory modalities, or tasks requiring motor output, and whether the observed biological features are causal for performance or merely correlated.
