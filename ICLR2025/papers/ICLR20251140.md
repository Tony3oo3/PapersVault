## Problem

Sampling-based motif-scaffolding methods suffer from an inherent trade-off between overall protein generation (designability) and accurate motif reconstruction, requiring 500–1000 diffusion steps with costly and unstable gradient guidance to gradually reconcile the two. Previous approaches treated the protein as an abstract latent variable, failing to exploit the explicit 3D spatial context (orientation, center of mass, residue geometry) that could align the generative direction with motif constraints from the start.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251140/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EVA takes a pretrained flow-based generative model (e.g., FrameFlow or OT-Flow), a desired motif specified as explicit 3D Cα coordinates and SO(3) residue orientations, and motif region indexes (which residue positions in the full protein correspond to the motif). It outputs complete, designable protein backbones (all N residues) with the motif accurately embedded, evaluated via self-consistency protocols (ProteinMPNN inverse folding + ESMFold refolding). The method is entirely training-free: no fine-tuning or additional training is required, and the pretrained model is used as-is for unconditional posterior estimation at each ODE step.

## Method

EVA formulates motif-scaffolding as a Geometric Inverse Design problem on the protein manifold T × SO(3)^N and introduces a coupled flow framework with two key geometric mechanisms. First, a Spatial Motif-Aligned Prior solves an equivariant optimal transport (EOT) problem via the Kabsch algorithm to rotate and translate the random prior so its global orientation and center of mass align with the motif, shortening and straightening the sampling path. Second, at each ODE step, the conditional posterior E_p[g₁|g_t, g^M] is approximated by direct geometric interpolation between the pretrained model's unconditional posterior and the target motif (linear for translations, geodesic for rotations), rather than estimating a guidance gradient. This yields a coupled vector field that is a weighted blend of the unconditional generative direction and a motif-reconstruction OT direction, with coupling strength β_t = 1 − γt ensuring motif alignment dominates early steps while overall designability takes over later. The result is a straighter probability path requiring only 100 Euler ODE steps (vs. 500–1000 for gradient-based methods), achieving 70× speedup over RFDiffusion with competitive or superior success rates.

## Results

On the RFDiffusion benchmark of 24 targets, EVA achieves a 36% success rate, 88% designable rate, 42% mRMSD<1, and 53% scRMSD<2, matching or exceeding all baselines while running 70× faster than RFDiffusion (0.87s vs. 61s per 100-residue backbone). EVA also achieves the highest diversity (173 clusters) and solves 20/24 targets. On the vaccine design benchmark, EVA attains the best success rate (17%) and designable rate (94%), and on multi-motif scaffolding it leads with 28% success rate and 28% mRMSD<1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RFDiffusion (the SOTA training-based method), EVA matches the success rate (36%) and scRMSD<2 (53%), is slightly lower on designable rate (88% vs. 89%) and mRMSD<1 (42% vs. 43%), but achieves substantially higher diversity (173 vs. 141) and is 70× faster (0.87s vs. 61s). Against TDS (the SOTA SMC-based sampling method), EVA ties on success rate (36%), slightly improves designable rate (88% vs. 87%) and mRMSD<1 (42% vs. 40%), and is ~72× faster. Against FF-G (gradient-guidance flow matching), EVA dramatically outperforms on success rate (36% vs. 13%) and mRMSD<1 (42% vs. 21%) while being ~17× faster. On the vaccine design benchmark, EVA leads all methods in success rate (17% vs. 16% RFDiff, 14% TDS) and designable rate (94% vs. 91% RFDiff). On multi-motif scaffolding, EVA achieves the highest success rate (28% vs. 27% RFDiff) and mRMSD<1 (28% vs. 27% RFDiff), confirming its advantage on more challenging real-world tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVA builds on the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and its Riemannian extension to protein geometric manifolds (Chen & Lipman, 2023; Yim et al., 2023), using a pretrained protein flow model (FrameFlow) as its generative backbone. It departs from prior sampling-based motif-scaffolding methods (TDS, FrameFlow-Guidance) that approximate conditional posteriors via gradient-based guidance derived from Tweedie's identity, instead directly approximating the conditional posterior through geometric interpolation between the unconditional posterior and the motif structure. The paper also draws on the image inverse problem literature (DPS, SDEdit, Pokle et al.) but reframes motif-scaffolding as a 'Geometric Inverse Design' problem, emphasizing the exploitation of 3D spatial context (orientation, center of mass) that prior protein-specific methods largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70× speedup over RFDiffusion (from ~61s to ~0.87s per 100-residue backbone) makes training-free motif-scaffolding practically viable for iterative design workflows like vaccine development, where diverse scaffold candidates are needed. The geometric interpolation approach is notably simple—requiring no backpropagation, no SMC particles, and no gradient computation—yet achieves competitive performance, suggesting that explicit spatial context can substitute for expensive implicit guidance. Limitations include insufficient exploration of general conditional sampling beyond motif constraints, limited handling of complex multi-motif layouts, and the fact that performance on longer proteins (≥100 residues) remains slightly below RFDiffusion. The framework's modularity (swap any pretrained flow model, adjust coupling strength γ) positions it as a flexible plugin for broader conditional protein generation tasks.
