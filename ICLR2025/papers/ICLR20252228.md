## Problem

Existing action-repetition methods in continuous-control RL treat all action dimensions as a monolithic unit during act-or-repeat selection, forcing every dimension to either repeat or act simultaneously. This constraint is misaligned with real continuous-control systems where different actuators operate at different frequencies and require different repetition schedules, reducing both exploration efficiency and policy agility.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives the current state vector s and the previous action vector a⁻ (both stored in the replay buffer alongside transitions). The two-stage policy first produces a binary repetition schema b ∈ {0,1}^|A| (one bit per action dimension), then generates a continuous action only for dimensions where b_i = 1. The final output action a is assembled via an element-wise Mix operation that copies a⁻ into repeat dimensions and inserts newly sampled actions into act dimensions, with the action space normalized to [-1,1]^|A|.

## Method

SDAR decomposes the decision into a selection stage and an action stage. The selection policy β(s, a⁻) outputs an independent Bernoulli probability for each action dimension, sampled to yield a per-dimension act-or-repeat schema b. The action policy π(s, a^mix, b) then generates a Gaussian-distributed action only for the 'act' dimensions, where a^mix is constructed by zeroing out a⁻ at act dimensions via a Mix operation to avoid redundant information. The final action is assembled as a = (1−b)⊙a⁻ + b⊙â, guaranteeing exact repetition in selected dimensions. Training follows an SAC-style entropy-regularized objective with automatic temperature tuning for both β and π; the selection policy is optimized either by exhaustive enumeration over all 2^|A| schemas (small action spaces) or by importance sampling from a stale β_old (large action spaces like Humanoid with |A|=17). This spatial decoupling is the core architectural difference from prior closed-loop methods like TAAC, which apply a single global act-or-repeat decision across all dimensions.

## Results

SDAR achieves a normalized AUC score of 1.0 across all three environment categories (Classic Control, Locomotion, Manipulation), outperforming the next-best method TAAC (0.90 average) and SAC (0.76 average). On episode returns, SDAR attains 15,131 on HalfCheetah (vs. SAC 13,122, TAAC 11,148), 7,483 on Humanoid (vs. SAC 6,184, TAAC 7,308), and 6,028 on Walker2d (vs. SAC 5,305, TAAC 5,660). SDAR also achieves a higher average Action Persistence Rate (3.75) with a lower Action Fluctuation Rate (0.208) compared to SAC (1.00 / 0.245), demonstrating a better balance between action persistence and smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDAR outperforms all five baselines on both sample efficiency (AUC) and final episode return across the 11 continuous control tasks. Against the closest competitor TAAC (closed-loop whole-action repetition), SDAR achieves higher AUC (1.0 vs. 0.90 average) and superior episode returns on locomotion tasks (HalfCheetah: 15,131 vs. 11,148; Humanoid: 7,483 vs. 7,308; Walker2d: 6,028 vs. 5,660), with lower action fluctuation (AFR 0.208 vs. 0.244). Against open-loop methods TempoRL and UTE, SDAR wins decisively: both open-loop baselines score only 0.59 average AUC and suffer severe performance drops on complex tasks (e.g., TempoRL achieves only 1,022 on Humanoid vs. SDAR's 7,483). Against naive fixed repetition N-Rep, SDAR achieves comparable or higher episode returns while maintaining much lower AFR (e.g., HalfCheetah AFR 0.62 vs. 0.47 for N-Rep, but with 15,131 vs. 8,378 return). Against vanilla SAC, SDAR improves episode returns on all locomotion tasks and achieves 3.75× higher action persistence with 15% lower fluctuation, confirming that spatially decoupled repetition adds value beyond standard off-policy RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDAR extends the closed-loop action repetition framework introduced by TAAC (Yu et al., 2021), which uses a binary switch policy to decide whether to repeat the previous action at each step. The key departure is spatial decoupling: rather than applying a single act-or-repeat decision to all action dimensions simultaneously, SDAR learns a per-dimension selection policy β, allowing different actuators to repeat or act independently. The overall training infrastructure (entropy-regularized actor-critic, reparameterization trick, automatic temperature tuning, clipped double-Q) is inherited directly from SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), positioning SDAR as a targeted architectural modification within the well-established off-policy continuous control pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spatial decoupling design addresses a practical limitation in multi-actuator systems where different joints or controllers naturally operate at different frequencies, a constraint that whole-action repetition methods cannot accommodate. The paper acknowledges that the selection policy currently treats dimensions independently without modeling inter-dimensional correlations (e.g., groups of actuators that should co-vary), which is identified as a key direction for future work. The consistent sample-efficiency gains (roughly 2× fewer steps to match SAC performance) make the approach practically relevant for compute-constrained RL training, though the method remains limited to model-free continuous control with relatively small action spaces.
