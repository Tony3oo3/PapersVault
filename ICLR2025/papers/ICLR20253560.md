## Problem

Existing long-context LLM benchmarks rely heavily on synthetic tasks like needle-in-a-haystack or arbitrary task subsets, suffer from insufficient context lengths (most natural-language datasets cap well below 128K), use noisy n-gram metrics like ROUGE, and are incompatible with base models. This makes it unclear whether reported scores reflect real downstream capabilities and complicates fair comparison across frontier models.

## Input / Output

Each task accepts a controllable input length L (8K–128K+ Llama-2 tokens) constructed by adjusting the number of retrieved passages, in-context demonstrations, or document truncation. Outputs are scored with task-appropriate metrics: substring exact match (SubEM) for RAG and synthetic recall, NDCG@10 for re-ranking, accuracy for ICL, and a GPT-4o-based reference evaluation (fluency × correctness product for QA; claim-level F1 × fluency for summarization) for long-document QA and summarization. All models are evaluated with greedy decoding and 2-shot demonstrations to ensure consistent output formats across base and instruction-tuned variants.

## Method

HELMET assembles 21 datasets across seven application-centric categories—RAG, generation with citations, passage re-ranking, many-shot ICL, long-document QA, summarization, and synthetic recall—each supporting controllable input lengths up to 128K tokens. Three design choices distinguish it from prior benchmarks: (1) reference-based model evaluation using GPT-4o as a judge (scoring fluency, correctness, and claim-level precision/recall) replaces ROUGE for QA and summarization, achieving Cohen's κ = 0.91 agreement with human judgments; (2) two-shot in-context demonstrations with placeholder documents reduce format noise and enable robust evaluation of base models; and (3) length-instruction-enhanced prompting from L-Eval is applied to long-generation tasks for more consistent scoring. The paper then evaluates 59 LCLMs across five input lengths, demonstrating that synthetic tasks poorly predict downstream performance, that HELMET's categories exhibit low mutual correlations, and that open-source models lag closed-source ones most sharply on complex tasks (citations, re-ranking) at 128K.

## Results

Across 59 LCLMs evaluated at 128K input length, GPT-4o-08 and Gemini-1.5-Pro lead on most categories, with GPT-4o-08 scoring 99.9% on synthetic recall, 70.2% on RAG (HotpotQA), 44.3% on generation with citations, and 50.0% on passage re-ranking. Open-source models (e.g., Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen2.5-72B) achieve competitive recall (90.7%, 38.4%) and RAG (56.2%, 43.0%) but collapse on complex tasks: Llama-3.1-70B scores only 7.5% on citations and 24.5% on re-ranking, a 30–40 point gap versus closed-source leaders. No synthetic task (NIAH, RULER variants) achieves Spearman correlation >0.8 with real-world categories, while RAG tasks (HotpotQA) show near-linear correlation with long-document QA. The model-based evaluation metric for summarization achieves Cohen's κ=0.91 (precision) and κ=0.76 (recall) human agreement, substantially outperforming ROUGE-L which shows <2-point variation across models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HELMET is compared against five prior long-context benchmarks: ZeroSCROLLS, LongBench, L-Eval, RULER, and ∞BENCH. On context length support, HELMET is the only benchmark covering all seven task categories at ≥128K with controllable lengths, whereas ZeroSCROLLS, LongBench, and L-Eval are limited to <128K (Table 2). Against RULER, HELMET resolves an inconsistency where RULER ranks Gemini-1.5-Flash above Gemini-1.5-Pro, while HELMET's multi-category evaluation shows Pro leading on re-ranking (59.7 vs. 50.8) and LongQA (59.6 vs. 57.3). Against ∞BENCH, HELMET corrects the anomaly where the 70B Llama model underperforms the 8B variant; on HELMET's LongQA, Llama-3.1-70B (56.3) clearly outperforms Llama-3.1-8B (43.2). Compared to L-Eval's reference-free pairwise win-rate approach, HELMET's reference-based model evaluation (using GPT-4o as judge) yields more consistent trends—e.g., it captures GPT-4o's performance gain with increasing input length on summarization, while ROUGE-L (used by ∞BENCH) shows <2-point variation. The paper further demonstrates that NIAH (the most widely used synthetic task) is saturated at near-100% for virtually all frontier models, making it useless for differentiation, whereas HELMET's RAG and citation tasks maintain meaningful spread across models.

## Relation to Prior Work

HELMET synthesizes and extends several prior long-context evaluation efforts: it adopts synthetic recall tasks from RULER (Hsieh et al., 2024), long-document QA and summarization datasets from ∞BENCH (Zhang et al., 2024b), citation generation from ALCE (Gao et al., 2023), and length-instruction-enhanced prompting from L-Eval (An et al., 2024). However, it departs from these individually-focused or synthetic-heavy benchmarks by unifying seven application-centric categories under a single framework with controllable lengths up to 128K tokens, reference-based model evaluation replacing ROUGE, and few-shot demonstrations enabling base-model evaluation. The paper explicitly positions itself against the field's over-reliance on NIAH and perplexity, arguing that no single prior benchmark provides a holistic, reliable signal for model development.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By evaluating 59 LCLMs across seven diverse categories at five input lengths, HELMET provides the most comprehensive controlled comparison of long-context models to date and offers practical guidance: RAG tasks are recommended as fast, reliable proxies for downstream performance during model development. The finding that open-source models completely collapse on complex tasks like generation with citations at 128K while closed-source models remain stable highlights a significant capability gap that prior benchmarks masked. Open questions include whether HELMET's model-based evaluation (using GPT-4o as judge) introduces its own biases, how findings generalize beyond 128K to the 1M+ token contexts now being claimed, and whether the low inter-category correlations will persist as models improve uniformly across capabilities.
