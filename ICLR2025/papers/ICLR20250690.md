## Problem

Existing training-free LLM-generated text detectors rely primarily on global statistics (e.g., likelihood, rank) of the full text, neglecting local discriminative patterns in the token probability sequence. This limits their generalization across domains, source models, and under paraphrasing attacks, while training-based alternatives suffer from overfitting and high data requirements.

## Input / Output

The method takes a candidate text and an open-source proxy language model (default: GPT-J-6B) as input, requiring no access to the source model that generated the text. It outputs a single scalar detection score (Lastde or Lastde++) compared against a threshold for binary human-vs-LLM classification. Lastde++ additionally requires 100 contrast samples drawn from the proxy model (1/100 of Fast-DetectGPT's 10,000), and when the sampling and scoring models coincide, the entire detection completes in a single model call.

## Method

The core insight is to treat the token probability sequence (TPS) as a time series and apply multiscale diversity entropy (MDE) to quantify its local temporal dynamics. Concretely, the TPS is first transformed at multiple scales (arithmetic means of τ adjacent log-probabilities), then segmented via a sliding window of size s to form s-probability segments. Cosine similarities between adjacent segments are binned into ε discrete states, and Shannon entropy over the resulting histogram yields the diversity entropy at each scale. The final Lastde score divides the global Log-Likelihood by the aggregated MDE (default: standard deviation across scales), exploiting the observation that human texts exhibit more abrupt TPS fluctuations (higher MDE) than LLM-generated texts. Lastde++ further normalizes this score by the mean and standard deviation of Lastde scores computed over 100 fast-sampled contrast texts, sharpening the separation between the two distributions.

## Results

In the black-box setting (the primary practical scenario), Lastde++ achieves an average AUROC of 91.47% across 12 source models, outperforming Fast-DetectGPT (85.68%) by 5.80 percentage points while using only 100 contrast samples versus Fast-DetectGPT's 10,000. In the white-box setting, Lastde reaches 95.89% average AUROC, surpassing DetectLRR (90.80%) by 5.09 points, and Lastde++ reaches 98.43%, exceeding Fast-DetectGPT (97.12%). The method also demonstrates superior robustness to paraphrasing attacks, with smaller AUROC drops (e.g., −2.10 on Reddit vs. −3.48 for Fast-DetectGPT in white-box) and consistent advantages in cross-lingual detection (German, Chinese).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lastde (sample-based) outperforms all four sample-based baselines: Likelihood (71.32%), LogRank (74.84%), Entropy (48.13%), and DetectLRR (80.45%) in the black-box setting, with the largest gap over DetectLRR at +5.93% average AUROC. Lastde++ (distribution-based) dominates all four distribution-based baselines: DetectGPT (70.94%), DetectNPR (72.75%), DNA-GPT (67.97%), and Fast-DetectGPT (85.68%), achieving +5.80% over Fast-DetectGPT with 100× fewer samples (100 vs. 10,000). The Entropy baseline performs near or below chance (48.13% avg), confirming that raw token entropy is insufficient for cross-model detection. On closed-source models (GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-4o, Claude-3-haiku), Lastde++(ExpStd) reaches 99.97% average AUROC, matching or exceeding Fast-DetectGPT (99.96%). Under paraphrasing attacks, Lastde++ consistently shows smaller AUROC degradation than Fast-DetectGPT across both white-box and black-box settings (e.g., −2.10 vs. −3.48 on Reddit white-box). On third-party datasets (Table 9), Lastde++ achieves 94.2% average AUROC versus 93.7% for Fast-DetectGPT and 88.6% for GPTZero, confirming generalizability beyond the authors' own datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Multiscale Diversity Entropy (MDE), originally developed for fault diagnosis in rotating machinery (Wang et al., 2020), as a local statistical feature for LLM-generated text detection — a domain where it had not previously been applied. It builds on the fast-sampling discrepancy framework of Fast-DetectGPT (Bao et al., 2024) to create Lastde++, but departs from the broader training-free detection literature (DetectGPT, DNA-GPT, DetectLRR) by introducing time series analysis to capture local temporal dynamics in token probability sequences, rather than relying solely on global statistics like likelihood or rank. The key conceptual departure is treating the token probability sequence as a time series whose fluctuation patterns differ systematically between human and machine text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× reduction in required contrast samples (100 vs. 10,000 for Fast-DetectGPT) while maintaining or improving AUROC makes training-free detection substantially more practical for real-time and large-scale deployment. The consistent gains across 18 source models, three languages, and paraphrasing attacks suggest the local temporal dynamics signal is robust rather than an artifact of specific model families. However, open questions remain: performance still degrades under paraphrasing (8–9% AUROC drop), detection of very short texts (<120 words) remains challenging, and the choice of proxy model significantly affects results — limiting the method's universality in truly unknown-source scenarios.
