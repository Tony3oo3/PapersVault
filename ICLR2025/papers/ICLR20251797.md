## Problem

Existing embodied planning methods either model single-agent world dynamics or assume full observability, leaving a gap for decentralized multi-agent cooperation where agents must simulate how an arbitrary number of joint actions affect the world from only partial egocentric views. This paper tackles both the partial observability challenge (estimating a global world state from fragmented egocentric observations) and the compositional dynamics challenge (accurately simulating multiple agents acting simultaneously).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251797/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives 336×336 egocentric RGBD frames plus camera matrices; these are reconstructed into a top-down orthographic world-state image (incomplete and noisy due to occlusion) which is then inpainted by a diffusion model. The compositional world model consumes this world-state image as the initial frame and factorized per-agent text actions as conditions, producing 128×128 video frames (upscaled to 336×336) that simulate the next world state. The final output is a cooperative action plan selected via beam search over P=3 action proposals, B=3 beams, and D=3 rollout depths, with replanning at every step to track changing cooperator behavior.

## Method

The core contribution is a compositional world model that factorizes the joint action of n agents into n per-agent text prompts and composes their video diffusion score functions (following composable diffusion theory) to generate future frames conditioned on all agents acting simultaneously. Training proceeds in two stages: first learning single-agent video dynamics P(X|x₀, aᵢ) with standard denoising loss, then fine-tuning with a composed score loss over joint actions. An Agent-Dependent Loss Scaling technique weights the MSE loss spatially based on each agent's reachable image region, significantly improving per-agent action fidelity. The full COMBO framework integrates this world model with three fine-tuned LLaVA sub-modules (Action Proposer, Intent Tracker, Outcome Evaluator) in a limited tree search that replans every step, using the Intent Tracker's observation history to adapt predictions to unseen cooperator policies.

## Results

On TDW-Game, COMBO achieves a 100% success rate with an average of 17.5 and 17.4 steps for the two cooperators, matching the oracle Shared Belief Cooperator (15.3/15.9 steps) while using only egocentric observations. On TDW-Cook, COMBO reaches 90% and 100% success rates with 22.8 and 22.9 average steps, again approaching the oracle baseline (24.0/21.4 steps). On the adapted 2D-FetchQ task, COMBO achieves 81.3% success rate, substantially outperforming the best baseline Co-GAIL (53.3%). COMBO also generalizes to 2- and 3-agent settings without retraining, maintaining 100% success rate on TDW-Game.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMBO decisively outperforms all learned baselines. Recurrent World Models and MAPPO both fail completely (0% success) on both TDW-Game and TDW-Cook, confirming the difficulty of multi-agent long-horizon planning from egocentric RGBD observations. The LLM-based CoELA (with oracle vision) achieves 90%/60% on TDW-Game but collapses to 15%/5% on TDW-Cook, while the end-to-end VLM LLaVA reaches only 55–60% on TDW-Game and 35–50% on TDW-Cook. COMBO surpasses CoELA by 10–95 percentage points in success rate and reduces average steps by 2–15 across all settings. On 2D-FetchQ, COMBO (81.3%) outperforms Co-GAIL (53.3%) by 28 percentage points. The only method that approaches COMBO is the Shared Belief Cooperator with oracle state access, which achieves slightly fewer steps (15.3 vs. 17.5 on TDW-Game), indicating COMBO operates near the information-theoretic upper bound despite using only partial egocentric observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends compositional diffusion models (Liu et al. 2022; Du et al. 2023a) from image generation to multi-agent video world modeling by factorizing joint actions into per-agent text components and composing their score functions. It builds on the video-language planning paradigm of VLP (Du et al. 2023b) and the compositional video synthesis of RoboDreamer (Zhou et al. 2024b), but departs from both by handling an arbitrary number of agents with partial egocentric observations rather than a single agent with full or static views. The Agent-Dependent Loss Scaling is a novel training technique specific to multi-agent video generation, and the integration of VLM sub-modules (Action Proposer, Intent Tracker, Outcome Evaluator) with a compositional world model in a tree search loop represents a new systems-level combination not present in prior embodied planning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COMBO is among the first frameworks to enable visual multi-agent cooperation with an arbitrary number of agents under partial egocentric observations, and the demonstrated generalization from 4-agent training to 2-3 agent settings suggests the compositional factorization is a principled inductive bias for scaling agent count. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively simple tabletop tasks (puzzle assembly, basic cooking) with scripted cooperators, the compositional world model still fails 25% of the time on 4-agent video generation, and the reliance on multiple sequential VLM inferences makes real-time deployment impractical. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex physical interactions (object mass, collisions), more diverse cooperator behaviors, and whether the tree search can be replaced by learned policies to reduce inference latency.
