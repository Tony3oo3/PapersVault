## Problem

Existing structured pruning methods for LLMs (e.g., SliceGPT) depend heavily on external calibration datasets to determine which parameters to remove, making them inflexible across compression ratios, vulnerable to data quality issues, and slow to adapt to varying resource constraints. Moreover, these methods often suffer severe performance degradation at higher compression rates and can even increase total parameter count at low compression ratios due to intrinsic parameter-learning components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251669/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the FFN1 (up-projection) weight matrices of a pre-trained LLM along with a target compression ratio r ∈ [0,1], and produces a compressed model with selectively pruned (non-contiguous) rows from FFN1, corresponding columns from FFN2, and adjusted biases. The learned policy learner is a small MLP (~45M parameters for LLaMA-2-7B, ~0.67% of the model) that is fully decoupled from the LLM architecture, enabling it to be reused at arbitrary compression ratios without retraining. Compression is applied only to FFN layers (64% of LLaMA-2-7B parameters), leaving self-attention and embedding layers intact.

## Method

PruneNet reformulates structured pruning as a stochastic policy learning problem optimized via REINFORCE. A lightweight MLP policy learner computes per-row importance scores for each FFN1 matrix by projecting through an intermediate matrix that captures inter-row interactions, then applies a sigmoid to produce scores in (0,1). Rows are sampled via a multinomial distribution using the Gumbel-softmax reparametrization trick for differentiability. The penalty is the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between the singular value distributions of the original and compressed matrices, motivated by the Poincaré Separation Theorem showing that row slicing necessarily shrinks the spectral range. A discounted future penalty (γ=0.99) is applied across layers to account for the observation that later layers have higher singular values and carry more semantic information. This calibration-free design eliminates the need for external data and allows the same learned policy to be transferred across compression ratios with <1% performance loss.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B at 30% compression ratio, PruneNet retains 80% of the dense model's average zero-shot accuracy (55.45 vs. 69.00), outperforming SliceGPT (51.50, 75% retention) by 4 points. At 20% compression, PruneNet achieves 61.67 average accuracy (89% retention) versus SliceGPT's 58.17 (84%). PruneNet also achieves higher effective sparsity (19% vs. 21.4% for SliceGPT at 30% nominal) and 73% better inference throughput (20.74 vs. 12.82 tokens/sec) on LLaMA-2-7B, while compressing the model in ~15 minutes versus 29 minutes for SliceGPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PruneNet consistently outperforms all six baselines across compression ratios and model architectures. Against SliceGPT (the strongest baseline), PruneNet achieves 3.5–9.1% higher average zero-shot accuracy at 20–30% compression on LLaMA-2-7B and Phi-2, with the gap widening at higher compression (e.g., 55.45 vs. 51.50 at 30% on LLaMA-2-7B). Against LLM-Pruner, LaCo, and ShortGPT at 30% compression on LLaMA-2-7B (Table 4), PruneNet leads by 0.86%, 1.96%, and 4.98% in average accuracy, respectively. The SVD-based methods (ASVD and SVD-LLM) underperform PruneNet by a large margin on LLaMA-1-7B (44.6% and 47.0% vs. 53.8% average at 30% compression), despite also targeting spectral preservation, because they require calibration data and preserve output-space rather than model-space structure. PruneNet also achieves superior inference throughput (20.74 vs. 12.82 tokens/sec on LLaMA-2-7B; 29.50 vs. 18.48 on Phi-2) and higher effective sparsity, since it decouples the compression policy from the model rather than embedding learned parameters within it.

## Relation to Prior Work

PruneNet builds on the structured pruning lineage initiated by SliceGPT (orthogonal transformations to slice weight matrices) and spectral-preservation methods like SVD-LLM and ASVD, but departs fundamentally by reformulating the pruning decision as a stochastic policy learning problem using REINFORCE. Where prior methods compute parameter importance intrinsically within the model (requiring calibration data to determine which rows/columns to drop), PruneNet decouples this into an external, reusable policy learner that assesses importance solely from intrinsic model properties. The theoretical grounding draws on the Poincaré Separation Theorem to formalize why slicing shrinks the spectral range of weight matrices, motivating the use of Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between pre- and post-compression singular value distributions as the policy's reward signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The calibration-free property addresses a practical bottleneck: existing methods like SliceGPT require repeated calibration for different compression ratios and are sensitive to calibration data quality, limiting deployment in resource-constrained or data-sensitive settings. The policy's reusability across compression ratios (with <1% performance drop when transferring) and the 15-minute compression time for LLaMA-2-7B offer tangible efficiency gains. However, the method currently only compresses FFN layers (64% of parameters in LLaMA-2-7B), leaving self-attention untouched, and the performance gains over SliceGPT, while consistent, are moderate (3–5% on average). Open questions include whether the policy-learning formulation extends to activation sparsity, how it interacts with quantization pipelines, and whether the approach scales gracefully to 13B+ models where performance drops become more pronounced.
