## Problem

Self-rewarding language models (SRLMs) use the same LLM as both policy and reward model, but this forces the model to make confident preference judgments even between responses of similar quality, generating noisy preference data. This overconfident labeling accumulates bias across iterative training rounds, causing performance degradation—particularly in 7B-parameter models that lack the capacity for reliable LLM-as-a-Judge prompting. The paper asks how to regularize the self-rewarding process so the model learns selectively from reliable preference pairs without requiring external reward models or human annotations.

## Input / Output

CREAM takes a small seed SFT dataset (~3.4K examples from Open Assistant), an unlabeled prompt pool (~21K prompts mixed from ARC, OpenBookQA, SIQA, and GSM8K train splits), and initial 7B LLM parameters (Llama-2 or Llama-3). It produces an aligned policy model after T iterative preference-training rounds. A key implementation detail is that CREAM uses the intrinsic DPO reward (log π_θ(y|x) − log π_ref(y|x)) for scoring and ranking responses rather than LLM-as-a-Judge prompting, making it suitable for smaller models. The consistency rate C is computed dynamically per iteration via Kendall's Tau between the current and previous model's rankings, eliminating the need for manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Method

CREAM formulates a generalized two-step iterative preference fine-tuning framework (preference-labeling step followed by a learning step, analogous to EM) and introduces a consistency regularization term L_Reg that penalizes the model's overconfidence in distinguishing similar-quality responses. Lemma 3.2 shows L_Reg equals 2·KL(uniform ∥ P_θ(z)), pushing the preference probability toward 0.5 for ambiguous pairs. Theorem 3.3 proves that DPO + λ·L_Reg is equivalent to a soft-labeled DPO loss C_λ·L_DPO + (1−C_λ)·L_DPO(reversed), where C_λ = (1+λ)/(1+2λ) acts as a label-smoothing coefficient. In practice, CREAM estimates the consistency rate C by computing Kendall's Tau between the ranking produced by the current model θ_t and the previous iteration's model θ_{t-1} over N=5 sampled responses per prompt, then trains with C·DPO_loss + (1−C)·ReverseDPO_loss. This means when two consecutive iterations disagree on a preference pair (low C), the model down-weights that pair and partially learns the reversed order, stabilizing training against accumulated rewarding bias.

## Results

On Llama-3 (M3), CREAM achieves an average accuracy of 83.37% across five downstream tasks (ARC-Easy 89.52%, ARC-Challenge 83.36%, OpenBookQA 90.20%, SIQA 72.06%, GSM8K 81.73%), outperforming the Oracle baseline (82.12% avg) on all tasks except SIQA. On Llama-2 (M3), CREAM reaches 50.51% average, substantially improving over SRLM (40.08%) but still trailing Oracle (59.35%). CREAM shows consistent monotonic improvement across iterations (M1→M2→M3), whereas SRLM degrades after M2, and the six-iteration extension (Table 5) confirms CREAM stabilizes at M4 while SRLM drops to 68.59% by M6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SRLM (the primary self-rewarding baseline), CREAM wins decisively on every task for both model families: on Llama-3 M3, CREAM improves the average from 80.71% to 83.37% (+2.66 pts), and on Llama-2 M3 from 40.08% to 50.51% (+10.43 pts), where SRLM actually degrades below the SFT baseline. Against the Oracle baseline (external InternLM2 reward model), CREAM on Llama-3 surpasses Oracle in average accuracy (83.37% vs. 82.12%) and on four of five tasks, losing only on SIQA (72.06% vs. 73.75%); however, on Llama-2 Oracle still leads CREAM by 8.84 points in average (59.35% vs. 50.51%), indicating the gap narrows but does not close for weaker base models. The ablation CREAM w/o RC (manually tuned consistency rate) underperforms the full CREAM on both models, confirming that the dynamically computed Kendall's-Tau-based consistency rate is essential. SRLM+KL, which adds a fixed KL penalty to DPO, improves over vanilla SRLM but remains below CREAM on average for both Llama-3 (81.81% vs. 83.37%) and Llama-2 (48.08% vs. 50.51%), showing that the consistency-regularized soft-label formulation is more effective than a simple KL constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

CREAM extends Self-Rewarding Language Models (SRLM, Yuan et al. 2024) by introducing a consistency-based regularization that leverages the iterative nature of self-rewarding training to detect and down-weight unreliable preference labels. It builds on DPO (Rafailov et al. 2023) as the underlying preference optimization objective and connects theoretically to the Bradley-Terry model and label smoothing. Unlike reward-hacking mitigation approaches that rely on ensemble reward models or conservative value estimation (Coste et al. 2023; Fisch et al. 2024), CREAM requires no external models and instead uses the previous iteration's model as a built-in consistency signal via Kendall's Tau ranking correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck for 7B-scale LLMs where LLM-as-a-Judge prompting is unreliable and self-rewarding degrades after a few iterations due to accumulated rewarding bias. The consistency regularization is computationally cheap (only requires one extra forward pass with the previous model) and eliminates hyperparameter search for the regularization strength. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 7B models (Llama-2/3), full fine-tuning requirements that limit scalability, and the consistency signal from the previous iteration being inherently weaker than an external oracle. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger models where LLM-as-a-Judge is more reliable, compatibility with PEFT methods, and how to extend consistency estimation to multi-model or multi-iteration settings.
