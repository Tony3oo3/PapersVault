## Problem

In parameter-shared MARL, agents tend to learn homogeneous behaviors, limiting exploration in complex cooperative tasks. Existing mutual information-based diversity methods (EOI, CDS, FoX) suffer from a fundamental bias: agents receive higher intrinsic rewards for revisiting known trajectories that carry strong identity signals than for exploring novel ones, causing them to get stuck in local optima. The paper asks how to encourage multi-agent trajectory diversity without this mutual-information-induced preference for familiar trajectories.

## Input / Output

Each agent consumes its local observation o^a at every timestep; the trajectory encoder (a 2-layer MLP with batch norm followed by a GRU autoregressive model) maps the observation sequence into a low-dimensional trajectory representation c_t^a. The method outputs two things: (1) decentralized actions from the standard policy network (DRQN with GRU), and (2) a per-agent intrinsic entropy reward r_entropy^a computed as the log of the average Euclidean distance to k nearest neighbors in the contrastive representation space. Learnable identity representations (vectors of dimension H, one per agent) are maintained as parameters and used only during contrastive training, not at inference time.

## Method

TEE replaces mutual information maximization with trajectory entropy maximization in a learned contrastive representation space. First, a contrastive encoder (MLP + GRU) maps each agent's trajectory into a representation c_t^a, trained with an InfoNCE loss where learnable identity vectors d^a serve as medium variables to pull same-agent representations together and push different-agent representations apart—avoiding the mutual dependence of fixed one-hot identities. Second, a nonparametric particle-based entropy estimator measures sparsity by computing, for each agent's representation, the log of the average distance to its k nearest neighbors (rather than just the k-th neighbor, which the authors found causes training instability). Third, this entropy value is deployed as a per-agent intrinsic reward; for QMIX, a separate intrinsic utility network Q_a^entropy (2-layer MLP taking agent utility and trajectory representation as input) is trained with a TD loss on the intrinsic reward, and its gradient is added to the standard QMIX loss via a weighted term β·L_TD^entropy. The encoder and policies are trained alternately: the encoder minimizes the contrastive loss on replayed trajectories, then the entropy estimator provides intrinsic rewards for policy updates.

## Results

On the Pac-Men grid-world, TEE+QMIX achieves an average return of 0.83±0.03, nearly 4× the 0.21±0.04 of vanilla QMIX. On the super-hard SMAC corridor scenario, TEE+QMIX reaches 0.85±0.03 win rate versus 0.23±0.03 for QMIX and 0.45±0.05 for the strongest baseline FoX. On stochastic SMACv2 terran_5_vs_5, TEE+QMIX attains 0.89±0.05 compared to 0.43±0.07 for QMIX. On Google Research Football academy_3_vs_1_with_keeper, TEE+QMIX scores 0.87±0.03 versus 0.41±0.04 for QMIX and 0.49±0.02 for FoX.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEE+QMIX outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluated scenario. Against the strongest mutual-information-based baselines, TEE+QMIX leads by a wide margin: on SMAC corridor it beats FoX (0.85 vs 0.45) and PMIC (0.85 vs 0.51) by 0.40 and 0.34 respectively, and on SMACv2 terran_5_vs_5 it surpasses EOI (0.89 vs 0.64) and FoX (0.89 vs 0.61) by 0.25 and 0.28. On the easy SMAC 3s5z scenario, TEE+QMIX (0.95) still edges out EOI (0.87) and PMIC (0.82), confirming that entropy maximization does not hinder homogeneous 'focus fire' behaviors. QTRAN and MAVEN are the weakest baselines, frequently scoring near zero on super-hard SMAC scenarios (e.g., 0.00 on 6h_vs_8z), while TEE+QMIX maintains 0.83. The policy-gradient variant TEE+MAPPO achieves comparable or slightly higher results (e.g., 0.97 on 3s5z, 0.94 on SMACv2 terran_5_vs_5), demonstrating that the method is not tied to a specific MARL backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of mutual information-based multi-agent diversity methods (EOI, CDS/SCDS, MAVEN, FoX, LIPO), which maximize MI between trajectories and agent identities, by instead maximizing trajectory entropy in a learned contrastive representation space. It builds on the particle-based nearest-neighbor entropy estimator from statistics (Singh et al., 2003; Beirlant et al., 1997) and adapts it to multi-agent settings by averaging over k nearest neighbors rather than using only the k-th. The contrastive trajectory encoder draws on InfoNCE (Oord et al., 2018) and contrastive RL (CURL, Stooke et al., 2021), but introduces learnable identity representations as medium variables to handle the near-duplicate trajectory problem caused by parameter sharing. The method is integrated into QMIX via an auxiliary intrinsic utility network and into MAPPO via shaped rewards, making it a plug-in module rather than a new MARL algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical argument (r_known = log|A| vs. r_new → −∞) explaining why MI-based exploration systematically traps agents in known trajectories, and offers a principled entropy-based alternative that avoids this failure mode. The consistent large margins across Pac-Men, SMAC, SMACv2, and GRF (e.g., 0.85 vs. 0.23 in corridor) suggest the MI-based paradigm has a structural limitation that entropy maximization can overcome. However, the method relies on Euclidean distance in a learned representation space as a proxy for policy difference, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation; developing more meaningful policy-difference metrics for the entropy estimator is an open direction. Scalability to very large agent counts and non-cooperative or mixed-motive settings remain unexplored.
