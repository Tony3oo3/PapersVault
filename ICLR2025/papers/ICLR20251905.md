## Problem

Adversarially trained DNNs exhibit significant disparities in robust accuracy across classes (robust fairness), and existing reweighting-based fine-tuning methods are limited by a divergence between training-set and test-set class-wise robust performance. The paper identifies the absence of a principled theoretical framework bounding the worst-class robust error and proposes a complementary regularization approach that avoids explicit class reweighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251905/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces class labels, with the key evaluation metric being worst-class robust accuracy under AutoAttack, PGD-20, or CW-20 at fixed perturbation budgets (ε=8/255 for ℓ∞, ε=128/255 for ℓ2). The regularizer is computed over adversarial training data (perturbed inputs within the ε-ball), and the confusion matrix is constructed from per-class misclassification indicators on this adversarial set.

## Method

The paper derives a PAC-Bayesian robust generalization bound showing that the worst-class robust error (ℓ₁ norm of the expected adversarial confusion matrix) is upper-bounded by the spectral norm of the empirical robust confusion matrix plus a model-weight-dependent term. To make this bound actionable, the authors introduce a spectral regularization term Ψ that penalizes the spectral norm of the empirical confusion matrix, made differentiable by replacing the discrete {0,1} error indicators with per-sample KL divergences between the model's adversarial output and the one-hot label. This regularizer is added to the standard adversarial training objective (e.g., TRADES) with a balancing hyperparameter α, and its gradient is computed via a chain rule that decomposes the expensive discrete gradient into a cheap spectral-norm gradient (cached per epoch via SVD) and a cheap KL-divergence gradient (computed per minibatch). The approach is applied both as a fine-tuning step on pre-trained adversarial models (2 epochs) and as a training-from-scratch regularizer, consistently improving worst-class robust accuracy while maintaining or improving average accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ℓ∞, Preact-ResNet18, trained from scratch), the proposed method with γ=0.1 achieves 49.73% average AutoAttack accuracy and 34.90% worst-class accuracy, outperforming TRADES (49.05% AA, 20.70% worst) and FAAL-AT (49.10% AA, 33.70% worst). On CIFAR-100, it reaches 25.17% AA and 3.00% worst-class (γ=0.0), surpassing TRADES (23.57% AA, 1.00% worst). In the fine-tuning setting on WRN-34-10 with TRADES-AWP, the method improves worst-class AA from 25.80% to 37.60% (γ=0.1) while maintaining 54.49% average AA (vs. 56.18% baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all robust-fairness baselines on worst-class accuracy while maintaining competitive average robust accuracy. On CIFAR-10 (ℓ∞), Ours γ=0.1 achieves 34.90% worst-class AA, beating FAAL-AT (33.70%), FAAL-TRADES (33.60%), WAT-TRADES (30.70%), and CFA-TRADES (26.50%) by 1.2–8.4 points, while also exceeding their average AA (49.73% vs. 49.10%, 48.48%, 46.16%, 50.10% respectively). On CIFAR-100, the method achieves the highest average AA (25.17%, γ=0.0) and ties for best worst-class (3.00–4.00%) among all methods. In the fine-tuning setting (Table 1, WRN-34-10 + TRADES-AWP), Ours γ=0.1 improves worst-class AA from 25.80% to 37.60% (+11.8 points) while retaining 54.49% average AA, outperforming FRL-RWRM (28.60% worst) and FAAL-AWP (37.00% worst). The method also generalizes to ℓ2 attacks (Table 5: 48.40% worst-class AA vs. FAAL-TRADES 46.40%) and DDPM-augmented pre-trained models (Table 2: +2.5–4.0 points worst-class improvement over Pang et al. and Wang et al. baselines). Notably, the method does not sacrifice average accuracy to gain worst-class performance, unlike FRL which drops average AA by 2–6 points in the fine-tuning setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayesian generalization analysis lineage (McAllester 1999; Morvant et al. 2012; Neyshabur et al. 2017b; Farnia et al. 2019) by deriving the first robust generalization bound specifically for worst-class adversarial error, bridging the clean-setting confusion matrix bounds of Morvant et al. with the adversarial perturbation framework of Xiao et al. (2023). It departs from the dominant robust fairness approach of explicit class reweighting (FRL, FAAL, CFA, WAT) by identifying a fundamental limitation—training-test divergence in class-wise robust performance—and instead proposes regularizing the spectral norm of the empirical robust confusion matrix as a principled alternative. The method also draws on the spectral normalization tradition (Yoshida & Miyato 2017; Farnia et al. 2019) but applies it to the confusion matrix rather than weight matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of worst-class robust error within a PAC-Bayesian framework, giving practitioners a principled handle (confusion matrix spectral norm) that is complementary to existing weight-norm-based regularization. The empirical finding that explicit reweighting methods exacerbate training-test divergence is a useful caution for the robust fairness community, suggesting that data-driven reweighting may be counterproductive. Open questions include whether the bound's constants (ν, the Φ' term) are tight enough to be practically informative at scale, whether the sign-approximation in the gradient (Eq. 11) degrades in high-dimensional or imbalanced settings, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-task or structured prediction where confusion matrices are less natural.
