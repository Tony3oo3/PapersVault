## Problem

Video-LLMs are trained exclusively on question-answer pairs derived from video content, leaving them unable to recognize and refuse questions that exceed the video's informational scope. The paper demonstrates that even state-of-the-art Video-LLMs consistently fail on unanswerable questions—not due to poor video understanding, but because they were never trained to identify and decline such queries, resulting in hallucinated responses.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (sequence of frames) and a text question as input, producing a text response that is either a factual answer or a structured refusal containing an explicit unanswerable indicator (e.g., 'The question is unanswerable') plus reasoning explaining why the question falls outside the video's content. The refusal reasoning must be consistent with the ground truth to receive full credit, distinguishing between correct and incorrect justifications for unanswerability. Evaluation uses a balanced 1200-sample set combining 600 answerable questions from ActivityNet-QA and 600 unanswerable questions from the UVQA dataset.

## Method

The core contribution is a formal 'alignment for answerability' framework with a scoring function s(v,x,y) that rewards the model for answering correctly on answerable questions and for refusing with matching reasoning on unanswerable questions, enabling alignment via SFT or DPO. The UVQA dataset (30k training, 600 evaluation samples) is created by altering video descriptions—replacing objects, relations, or attributes within scene-graph triplets or adjective categories—and prompting GPT-4 to generate questions grounded in the altered descriptions, yielding three unanswerability categories (object, relation, attribute). A dedicated evaluation framework measures alignment quality through three behavioral metrics: Excessive Refusal (over-caution on answerable questions), Permissiveness (recovery of previously refused answerable questions), and Discretion (correct refusal of unanswerable questions), alongside overall accuracy and GPT-4-rated response quality. An ablation comparing against a POPE-Style existence-question splitting baseline shows that while the heuristic improves over unaligned models, it underperforms the trained alignment and incurs 4× data and 6× evaluation computational cost.

## Results

Across all three base models (Video-LLaVA, VideoChat2, VLM-RLAIF), DPO alignment achieves the highest overall alignment score (S_align) of 0.68–0.69, compared to 0.62–0.66 for SFT and 0.33 for unaligned baselines. DPO also yields the best answerability F1 (0.64–0.68) and LLMscore (2.93–3.08), while maintaining the lowest excessive refusal score (S_ex-ref = 0.08–0.14) among aligned methods. Overall accuracy (S_acc) improves from 0.22–0.25 (unaligned) to 0.49–0.54 (DPO-aligned), demonstrating that alignment for answerability does not sacrifice performance on answerable questions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SFT- and DPO-aligned models against their unaligned counterparts across three Video-LLM backbones (Video-LLaVA, VideoChat2, VLM-RLAIF). DPO alignment consistently outperforms SFT on the alignment score (S_align: 0.68–0.69 vs. 0.62–0.66) and excessive refusal (S_ex-ref: 0.08–0.14 vs. 0.10–0.50), indicating a softer alignment that preserves the model's willingness to answer valid questions. Both aligned methods dramatically outperform unaligned baselines on answerability F1 (0.61–0.68 vs. 0.00) and overall accuracy (0.47–0.54 vs. 0.22–0.25). The POPE-Style existence-question-splitting baseline (Section 6) improves over unaligned models without training but significantly underperforms the DPO-aligned models and incurs a 6× computational overhead. Prompting alone (Appendix A.9) yields negligible gains (F1 ≤ 0.12), confirming that explicit alignment training is necessary rather than relying on in-context instructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the answerability/abstention problem from LLMs (Yang et al., 2023, 'alignment for honesty') and image-based VQA (Whitehead et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2023) to the video domain, where it had been entirely unaddressed. The alignment mechanism draws directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and SFT, but introduces a novel scoring function s(v,x,y) that encodes video-specific answerability through a scene-graph-inspired taxonomy of unanswerability (object, relation, attribute). The dataset construction repurposes existing video-description datasets (MOMA-LRG, DiDeMo) via description alteration rather than requiring new video collection, and the evaluation framework builds on the multi-metric alignment evaluation paradigm from the RLHF literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that every evaluated Video-LLM scores 0% F1 on unanswerable questions—and that scaling from 7B to 72B parameters provides no improvement—reveals a fundamental blind spot in current video understanding systems that will be critical as these models are deployed in real-world QA, surveillance, and content moderation applications. The paper also demonstrates that intuitive workarounds (prompting, existence-based question decomposition à la POPE) fail or incur prohibitive computational costs, establishing that explicit alignment training is necessary. Open questions include whether the attribute-related unanswerability gap (consistently the hardest category) reflects a deeper limitation in video attribute grounding, whether the excessive-refusal trade-off can be further mitigated, and whether the approach generalizes to audio-visual or longer-form video settings.
