## Problem

Existing Video Scene Graph Generation (VidSGG) methods rely on fully supervised annotations—bounding boxes plus entity and relation labels for every frame—making them far more expensive than their image counterparts. While weakly supervised image SGG can leverage captions, naively transplanting that pipeline to video fails for two reasons: video captions contain temporal markers (e.g., 'before', 'while', 'then') that must be respected for correct frame assignment, and human actions unfold over variable durations that a uniform frame-to-sentence split cannot capture. This paper is the first to train a VidSGG model using only readily available video captions, with no human-annotated scene graphs at all.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a video (a sequence of T frames) and its paired natural-language video caption as the sole training signal; no ground-truth bounding boxes, entity labels, or relation annotations are required. During training, the caption is converted into pseudo-localized scene graphs by parsing triplets and grounding them via a pretrained object detector (VinVL with ResNeXt-152 C4, confidence threshold 0.2). At inference, the trained VidSGG model (STTran or DSG-DETR backbone) outputs a per-frame scene graph of subject-object-action triplets with predicted bounding boxes, evaluated under the SGDet protocol where a prediction is valid only if its box overlaps the ground-truth box with IoU > 0.5.

## Method

NL-VSGG consists of two core modules plus an optional pseudo-labeling strategy. The Temporality-aware Caption Segmentation (TCS) module uses GPT-3.5-turbo with a purpose-built prompt to split the video caption into temporally ordered sentences while resolving coreference (pronouns are replaced with explicit object names), yielding M segmented sentences. The Action Duration Variability-aware Caption-Frame Alignment (ADV) module encodes each frame with a visual encoder and each sentence with a text encoder (DAC vision-language model), clusters the T frame embeddings via K-Means (K = |V|/β, β=4), then aligns each sentence to frames by sorting cluster-centroid similarity scores in descending order and selecting all clusters preceding the steepest decline point—this adaptively assigns more clusters (and thus more frames) to longer-duration actions. Aligned sentences are parsed into triplets via an LLM-based parser, grounded to detected bounding boxes, and used to train the VidSGG model. An optional Pseudo-Labeling with Motion cues (PLM) module assigns negative action classes ('not looking at', 'not contacting') to subject-object pairs in unaligned frames by detecting decreasing GIoU over time, targeting the top-α% most confident pairs (α=15%).

## Results

On the Action Genome dataset with the STTran backbone, NL-VSGG achieves R@20/R@50 of 15.61/19.60 (With Constraint) and 15.92/22.56 (No Constraint) using only video captions as supervision. With the DSG-DETR backbone, it reaches 15.75/20.40 (WC) and 16.11/23.21 (NC). These results substantially outperform the naive WS-ImgSGG pipeline (10.01/12.83 WC) and PLA_cap (10.40/13.26 WC), while remaining below the full-supervision upper bound (33.98/36.93 WC) and the GT-unlocalized-SG weak supervision of PLA (20.94/25.79 WC). On the VidHOI dataset, NL-VSGG also outperforms WS-ImgSGG (10.41 vs. 7.56 R@20 WC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NL-VSGG substantially outperforms the zero-shot ImgSGG baselines RLIP (7.93) and RLIPv2 (8.37) by roughly 7–8 points in R@20 (With Constraint), confirming that static image models cannot capture dynamic video relationships. Against the naive WS-ImgSGG pipeline (10.01) and PLA_cap (10.40), which both ignore temporality and action-duration variability, NL-VSGG gains 5.2–5.6 points in R@20 (WC), validating the TCS and ADV modules. NL-VSGG remains below PLA (20.94) and PLA_simp. (20.42), which rely on a manually annotated GT unlocalized scene graph of the middle frame; however, the paper argues this assumption is unrealistic and still costly. The gap to full supervision (33.98 for STTran, 34.80 for DSG-DETR) is expected, as NL-VSGG uses only captions without any human-annotated scene graphs. On the VidHOI dataset, NL-VSGG (10.41 R@20 WC) again beats WS-ImgSGG (7.56), confirming generalizability beyond Action Genome.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly supervised image scene graph generation (WS-ImgSGG) pipeline—which trains on image captions—to the video domain, identifying two video-specific challenges (temporality in captions and variability in action duration) that prevent naive transfer. It departs from PLA (Chen et al., 2023), the first WS-VidSGG method, by eliminating the need for any ground-truth scene graph (even unlocalized) and relying solely on readily available video captions. The framework builds on LLM4SGG (Kim et al., 2024b) for LLM-based triplet extraction and class mapping, and leverages existing VidSGG backbones (STTran, DSG-DETR) and vision-language models (DAC, VinVL) as components within its pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to train a VidSGG model entirely from video captions without any human-annotated scene graphs, substantially reducing annotation cost and enabling prediction of action classes beyond the training vocabulary. However, the performance gap to fully supervised methods remains large (15.61 vs. 33.98 R@20 With Constraint), and the framework is limited to relatively short videos (~120 seconds), with untrimmed long-video generalization left as future work. The PLM module for negative classes is dataset-specific, and the heavy reliance on an LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo) for caption segmentation introduces a practical dependency, though experiments show smaller models (Mistral-7B) still outperform the baseline.
