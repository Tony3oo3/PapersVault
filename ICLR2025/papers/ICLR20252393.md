## Problem

Existing graph condensation methods for node classification suffer from two fundamental flaws: the majority require training a GNN on the full dataset as a prerequisite for condensation (contradicting the goal of reducing computational cost), and their gradient-matching formulation makes them model-specific, necessitating re-condensation for any change in GNN architecture or hyperparameters. BONSAI addresses both by introducing the first linear-time, gradient-free, model-agnostic graph condensation algorithm.

## Input / Output

BONSAI takes a training graph with node feature vectors and a memory budget in bytes, requiring only approximate knowledge of the number of GNN layers L (not the architecture or hyperparameters). It outputs a sparse induced subgraph over a selected set of exemplar nodes — not a fully-connected or edge-weighted graph — which is critical because GNN forward-pass cost scales with edge count O(|E|) rather than node count. The condensed graph is reusable across any message-passing GNN (GCN, GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE) without re-condensation.

## Method

BONSAI exploits the observation that any L-layer message-passing GNN decomposes a graph into n rooted computation trees, and that topologically similar trees produce similar embeddings regardless of architecture. It embeds all computation trees into WL-kernel space (a single unsupervised message-passing pass, no gradient descent), then identifies exemplar trees via a two-stage criterion: representativeness (measured by reverse k-NN cardinality, approximated in O(n) via Chernoff-bounded sampling) and diversity (greedy submodular coverage maximization with a 1−1/e approximation guarantee). The induced subgraph of selected exemplars is then sparsified using Personalized PageRank with teleportation to root nodes, freeing edge budget to iteratively incorporate additional exemplars. The entire pipeline runs in O(|V|+|E|) time, is fully CPU-bound and embarrassingly parallelizable, and requires no GNN training at any stage.

## Results

On GCN, BONSAI achieves the best node classification accuracy in 12 out of 21 dataset-compression scenarios across 7 real-world graphs. At 0.5% compression, BONSAI reaches 83.95% on Cora (vs. 77.85% for the next-best GC-SNTK), 87.27% on Pubmed (vs. 80.72% for GDEM), and 52.33% on MAG240M where all other baselines fail (OOT/OOM). Condensation time is at least 7× faster than the fastest GPU-based baseline (EXGC) despite BONSAI being CPU-bound, and carbon emissions are 17× lower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BONSAI outperforms all seven baselines in the majority of scenarios. GDEM (Liu et al., 2024a) is the strongest competitor and the only other model-agnostic method, but BONSAI exceeds it by 5–15% on most datasets (e.g., 83.95% vs. 57.49% on Cora at 0.5%). GCSR (Liu et al., 2024b) and GCond (Jin et al., 2021) both require full-dataset GNN training as a prerequisite and fail to complete (OOT) on Ogbn-arxiv, Reddit, and MAG240M. EXGC (Fang et al., 2024) and GC-SNTK (Wang et al., 2024) produce substantially lower accuracy (often 20–40% below BONSAI) and are model-specific. GEOM (Zhang et al., 2024) is the slowest baseline (trains the full model 200×) and fails on all datasets beyond Pubmed. Herding (Welling, 2009) serves as a non-neural baseline and is consistently outperformed by BONSAI by 10–30% across all datasets. Notably, on MAG240M (1.4M nodes, 26M edges), every neural baseline either times out or runs out of memory, while BONSAI completes condensation in ~2 minutes on CPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

BONSAI builds on the computation tree framework established by Xu et al. (2019), which showed that message-passing GNNs decompose into per-node computation trees and that isomorphic trees yield identical embeddings (bounded by 1-WL). It repurposes the Weisfeiler-Lehman kernel (Togninalli et al., 2019) as an unsupervised distance metric over these trees, and applies submodular coverage maximization (Feige, 1998) to select representative exemplars. The key departure from prior graph condensation work (GCond, GDEM, GCSR, EXGC, GEOM) is a shift from gradient-matching in parameter space to distribution-matching in input space: rather than replicating the gradient trajectory of a trained GNN, BONSAI directly encodes the structural diversity of computation trees, eliminating the need for any GNN training during condensation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BONSAI resolves a fundamental paradox in graph condensation—most methods require full-dataset GNN training to produce a condensed dataset, negating the computational savings they aim to provide—and does so with a linear-time, CPU-bound algorithm that is 22× faster and 17× more energy-efficient than GPU-bound baselines. Its model-agnostic property (one condensation reusable across GCN, GAT, GIN, etc.) removes the need for re-condensation upon architectural changes, a practical bottleneck for teams iterating on model design. Open questions include whether the computation-tree framework extends to non-message-passing architectures (e.g., graph transformers) and whether task-agnostic condensation (beyond node/graph classification) is achievable with the same principles.
