## Problem

Computing Nash equilibria in general-sum games is intractable even for two-player matrix games, and existing relaxations like coarse correlated equilibria suffer from large support, potential mass on dominated strategies, and intractability in Markov games. Meanwhile, neither pure bounded rationality (QRE) nor pure risk aversion alone yields a universally computable equilibrium class across all games. The paper asks whether combining both behavioral-economics features—risk aversion and bounded rationality—produces an equilibrium concept that is simultaneously computationally tractable in all n-player matrix and finite-horizon Markov games and predictive of actual human play.

## Input / Output

For matrix games, the method consumes payoff matrices R_i ∈ R^{A_i × A_{-i}} along with per-player risk-aversion parameters τ_i and bounded-rationality parameters ε_i, and outputs a joint mixed-strategy profile π* that is an RQE. For finite-horizon Markov games, it additionally requires transition kernels P_h and per-step reward functions R_{i,h}, and outputs a time-dependent product policy π = (π_1,...,π_n) over S×[H]. In the model-based (unknown environment) setting, the input is N i.i.d. samples per state-action pair from a generative model, with total sample size N_all = N·S·∏A_i; the output is a δ-RQE whose approximation error δ is controlled by the sample budget. A key structural property is that the tractability condition (ε_1·ε_2 ≥ ξ_1*·ξ_2*) depends only on the chosen risk metrics and regularizers, not on the specific payoffs or game size.

## Method

The paper formulates risk-averse games by replacing expected utilities with convex risk measures (e.g., entropic, CVaR) via the Föllmer–Schied dual representation, which introduces an auxiliary 'adversary' variable p_i per player and a penalty function D_i(p_i, π_{-i}) that penalizes deviation from opponents' realized strategies. Bounded rationality is then imposed by adding a strictly convex regularizer ε_i·ν_i(π_i) to each player's cost, yielding the risk-averse quantal response equilibrium (RQE). The central tractability result (Theorem 3) constructs a 2n-player game pairing each original player with their adversary and proves that any coarse correlated equilibrium of this expanded game, when the regularizer strengths satisfy ε_1·ε_2 ≥ ξ_1*·ξ_2*, collapses to an RQE of the original n-player game—meaning standard no-regret algorithms (gradient play, mirror descent) compute an RQE in a fully decentralized manner. For finite-horizon Markov games, the method extends via backward dynamic programming: at each state-time pair (s,h) it constructs a matrix game whose payoffs incorporate both the risk-adjusted future value and the risk of stochastic transitions, then recursively computes the RQE from h=H down to h=1 (Algorithm 1). The paper further provides the first finite-sample guarantee (Theorem 5) showing that with N_all = Õ(S·∏A_i·H·L·√(S/N)·log(1/δ)) samples from a generative model, the output is a δ-RQE, and validates the approach on a two-agent Cliff Walk grid-world where varying τ and ε produces qualitatively different equilibrium policies.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: Theorem 3 establishes that RQE are computationally tractable via no-regret learning in all 2-player matrix games whenever ε₁ε₂ ≥ ξ₁*ξ₂*, a condition independent of the underlying game structure. Theorem 4 extends this to finite-horizon Markov games via a backward-induction dynamic programming procedure (Algorithm 1). Theorem 5 provides the first finite-sample guarantee for computing Markov RQE with a generative model, requiring N_all ≥ 8S∏A_i·H·L·√(S/N)·log(2SH∏A_i/δ) samples. Empirically, the tractable RQE regime (Fig. 1) captures the average strategies played by human subjects in 13 two-player matrix games (from Goeree et al. 2003 and Selten & Chmura 2008) to within 1% accuracy, and a Cliff Walk grid-world experiment (Fig. 2) qualitatively demonstrates that varying risk-aversion and bounded-rationality parameters produce distinct, interpretable agent behaviors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run head-to-head numerical comparisons against competing algorithms; instead, it positions RQE against existing equilibrium concepts on the axes of computational tractability and behavioral fidelity. Compared to Nash equilibria, RQE are tractable in all n-player matrix and finite-horizon Markov games, whereas NE computation is PPAD-complete even for two-player matrix games (Daskalakis 2013) and Markov NE are intractable in general-sum stochastic games (Daskalakis et al. 2023b). Compared to quantal response equilibria (QRE), the paper notes that no class of QRE is universally computable across all games—the required bounded-rationality level depends on game-specific structure—whereas the RQE tractability condition depends only on the agents' risk-aversion and bounded-rationality parameters. Compared to coarse correlated equilibria (CCE), RQE avoid the pathology of placing probability mass on strictly dominated strategies and are not known to be intractable in Markov games. On the behavioral-economics side, the paper cites Goeree et al. (2003) showing that neither risk-aversion nor bounded rationality alone predicts human play in matching-pennies-type games, while the combined RQE model (Fig. 1) recovers observed strategies in all 13 experimental games to 1% accuracy, outperforming either feature in isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three distinct research threads: quantal response equilibria from behavioral economics (McKelvey and Palfrey, 1995), convex risk measures from mathematical finance (Föllmer and Schied, 2002), and no-regret learning dynamics (Cesa-Bianchi and Lugosi, 2006). It departs from the standard NE/CCE framework by arguing that neither bounded rationality alone (QRE) nor risk-aversion alone (RNE) yields universally tractable equilibria, but their combination does—a finding that mirrors behavioral economics evidence that both features are needed to predict human play. The dual representation theorem for convex risk measures is the key technical bridge that transforms the risk-averse game into a form where CCE-to-RQE equilibrium collapse (analogous to zero-sum min-max) can be established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that a class of RQE is computable via no-regret learning in all n-player matrix and finite-horizon Markov games, independent of game structure—provides a principled, tractable alternative to NE and CCE for decentralized MARL algorithms, addressing a long-standing computational bottleneck. However, the empirical validation is limited to a simple Cliff Walk grid world and 13 behavioral economics games, leaving open whether the framework scales to complex, high-dimensional environments or whether the specific risk-aversion/bounded-rationality parameter regime that guarantees tractability is practically achievable. Open questions include extending results to infinite-horizon or continuous-action settings, characterizing the equilibrium selection problem within the RQE class, and developing efficient decentralized algorithms that converge to RQE without requiring the 2n-player adversarial construction.
