## Problem

General-purpose LLMs underperform on scientific literature understanding due to two compounding deficiencies: insufficient domain-specific scientific knowledge and poor instruction-following on specialized scientific tasks. Existing adaptation strategies address only one of these gaps—continual pre-training injects knowledge but leaves models unable to follow task instructions, while supervised fine-tuning improves instruction-following without building a deep scientific knowledge base. The paper also identifies practical obstacles in constructing the required training data: PDF parsing introduces formatting errors that degrade corpus quality, and high-quality diverse instruction sets for less-represented scientific domains are scarce and expensive to annotate manually.

## Input / Output

The model takes a segment of scientific literature (often a long passage extracted from a PDF, potentially containing LaTeX-formatted tables, chemical structures, or molecular SMILES strings) paired with a natural-language task instruction, and produces task-specific structured outputs including extracted entities, tabular data in CSV format, free-text answers, binary fact-verification labels, molecule translations between SMILES formats, and multi-class classification labels. Instructions are structured as three-component tuples (scientific context, question, answer), and the model is trained with a 4,096-token sequence length to accommodate long scientific passages. Evaluation spans both short-context benchmarks (SciRIFF) and end-to-end PDF understanding tasks (SciAssess) across biology, chemistry, materials, and medicine.

## Method

SciLitLLM is built on Qwen2.5-Base through a two-stage pipeline. In the CPT stage, 73k textbooks and 625k research papers are parsed via PyPDF2, then corrected for formatting and syntax errors using Llama3-8B-Instruct (processing ~2.52M tokens per A100 GPU-hour); a 109M BERT classifier fine-tuned from the fineweb-edu-classifier checkpoint (labeled by Llama3-70B-Instruct on 50k samples) scores and filters out the lowest-quality 25% of the corpus, yielding 12.7B scientific tokens mixed with 11B general Redpajama tokens for one epoch of training. In the SFT stage, a novel instruction synthesis pipeline generates diverse scientific instructions by sampling 20 keywords from domain-specific word-frequency probability tables and a task description from a curated task list, then prompting GPT-4o to produce a context-question-answer triple; the resulting 100k+ instructions are deduplicated via Levenshtein-distance-based fuzzy matching and filtered by Llama-3-70B-Instruct scoring across five quality dimensions (clarity, complexity, correctness, usefulness, adaptability), yielding the 93,894-instruction SciLitIns dataset. SFT training combines SciLitIns, SciRIFF, and Infinity-Instruct for five epochs on scientific data after one epoch on general instructions, producing SciLitLLM-7B and SciLitLLM-14B.

## Results

On SciAssess, SciLitLLM-7B achieves a mean score of 51.7, outperforming the next-best sub-10B model (Llama3.1-8B at 47.7) by 4.0 points, while SciLitLLM-14B reaches 58.2, surpassing Llama3.1-70B (57.1) and Qwen2.5-72B (57.0). On SciRIFF, SciLitLLM-7B scores 60.6 (a 10.1-point margin over Llama3.1-8B at 50.5), and SciLitLLM-14B achieves 64.4, exceeding even GPT-4o (60.9) and all open-source models. On base-model benchmarks (MMLU-Pro and MaScQA), SciLitLLM-7B-Base improves average accuracy by 2.6 points over Qwen2.5-7B-Base (58.75 vs. 56.15), confirming the knowledge-injection benefit of CPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against sub-10B open-source baselines, SciLitLLM-7B dominates: it beats Llama3.1-8B by 4.0 points on SciAssess (51.7 vs. 47.7) and by 10.1 points on SciRIFF (60.6 vs. 50.5), and outperforms Qwen2.5-7B (its own base) by 5.2 and 10.3 points respectively. SciTulu-7B, a prior SciRIFF-fine-tuned model, trails SciLitLLM-7B by 22.8 points on SciAssess and 13.6 on SciRIFF, underscoring the added value of CPT plus diverse synthetic instructions. At the 14B scale, SciLitLLM-14B (58.2 / 64.4) surpasses both Llama3.1-70B (57.1 / 58.5) and Qwen2.5-72B (57.0 / 58.2) on both benchmarks despite having roughly one-fifth the parameters. Among API models, SciLitLLM-14B exceeds GPT-4o on SciRIFF (64.4 vs. 60.9) but falls short on SciAssess (58.2 vs. 61.2); GPT-3.5 is outperformed by both SciLitLLM variants on every benchmark. The base-model comparison (Table 2) further shows that CPT alone lifts Qwen2.5-7B-Base from 56.15 to 58.75 average accuracy on MMLU-Pro and MaScQA, confirming that domain knowledge injection is a distinct and necessary contribution beyond instruction tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: continual pre-training (CPT) for domain knowledge injection (as in Galactica, SciBERT, and the quality-filtering approaches of Phi and Qwen2.5) and supervised fine-tuning with synthetic instructions (as in SciGLM, ChemLLM, and SciRIFF). It departs from prior work by combining both stages into a single pipeline specifically for scientific literature understanding, and by introducing a keyword-probability-table-driven instruction synthesis method that targets less-represented scientific domains (materials, medicine, drug discovery) where curated instruction data is scarce. The CPT quality classifier is directly built on the fineweb-edu-classifier checkpoint via transfer learning, adapting its educational-value scoring to scientific text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, end-to-end recipe (PDF parsing → LLM-based correction → quality filtering → CPT → synthetic instruction generation → deduplication → LLM-based filtering → SFT) that other domain-specialization efforts can replicate, and the released SciLitIns dataset fills a concrete gap for underrepresented scientific subfields. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include the relatively small CPT corpus (12.7B tokens), the absence of reasoning-enhancement techniques (CoT/ToT), and the lack of RLHF for preference alignment. Open questions include whether the pipeline scales effectively with larger corpora, whether the instruction synthesis method generalizes to domains without clear keyword distributions, and whether the modest gains from quality filtering (+0.5–0.8%) suggest diminishing returns from further data curation.
