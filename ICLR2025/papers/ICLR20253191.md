## Problem

Automated neural architecture search has largely failed to produce unified frameworks that yield significant improvements in both quality and efficiency over standard Transformer-based recipes, due to search spaces that are either poorly conditioned (candidates fail to train stably) or too narrow to capture meaningfully different architectural patterns. Manual design, while productive, is limited to relatively basic patterns like striped hybridization and requires substantial expert investment. The paper asks whether a principled, comprehensive search space combined with evolutionary optimization can systematically discover architectures that push the quality-efficiency Pareto frontier beyond what either automated or manual methods have achieved.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of optimization objectives (perplexity, parameter count, and/or inference cache size at a fixed sequence length of 4096 tokens), an initial population of 16 genome encodings (seeded with known hybrid backbones and random LIV compositions), and 4096-token sequences from RedPajama for training. It produces a synthesized backbone genome—a hierarchical integer encoding specifying the class, ordering, and interconnection (featurizer sharing, feature group sharing) of 24 linear input-varying operators at 768 dimensions—which is then realized as a trainable model. The genome is structured at three resolution levels: backbone (5-number segments per LIV), operator (5 numbers per LIV), and featurizer (7 numbers per feature group), enabling optimization at different granularities.

## Method

STAR grounds its search space in the theory of linear input-varying systems (LIVs), a framework that unifies attention, linear recurrences, gated convolutions, and memoryless units under a common operator structure T_ij(x), decoupling nonlinear featurization from linear token- and channel-mixing. Each architecture is encoded as a hierarchical STAR genome: the backbone genome specifies LIV class, featurizer weight-sharing groups, sharing strategies, and feature group sharing for each operator in sequence; the operator genome specifies token-mixing structure (diagonal, low-rank, Toeplitz, semi-separable), sparsity masks, nonlinearities, and channel-mixing structure; and the featurizer genome specifies per-feature-group mixing structures, expansion factors, and repeat factors. Evolutionary optimization via NSGA-2 (with tournament selection, 2-point crossover, and constrained 10% mutation) refines populations of 16 genomes over 18 generations, with mutations restricted to valid LIV classes and compatible sharing configurations to ensure training stability. The key design insight is that the LIV framework simultaneously provides comprehensiveness (covering diverse operator families and interconnection patterns) and well-conditioning (most sampled candidates train without numerical instability), enabling gradient-free search to reliably converge on high-performing architectures.

## Results

When optimizing solely for quality, STAR-evolved backbones achieve 51.0 average downstream accuracy (HellaSwag, ARC-e, Winogrande, PiQA, SciQ) and 7.0–7.1 RedPajama perplexity, outperforming Transformer++ (48.8 avg, 7.3 PPL) and StripedMamba (49.5 avg, 7.2 PPL). When jointly optimizing for quality and inference cache size, STAR backbones reduce cache to 16–23 MB at 4096-token sequences — a 90% reduction over Transformer++ (150 MB) and 36% over StripedMamba (25 MB) — while matching or exceeding their downstream accuracy. At 1B parameters, a STAR backbone optimized for quality and cache achieves 59.0 average accuracy (matching StripedMamba) and 5.7 PPL, with 86 MB cache versus 805 MB for Transformer++ and 136 MB for StripedMamba.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transformer++ (85M params, 150 MB cache), STAR-evolved backbones win across all three optimization objectives: on quality alone, STAR achieves 51.0 average downstream accuracy versus 48.8 for Transformer++ (a 2.2-point gap, roughly double the hybrid-over-Transformer gap); on quality+size, STAR reduces parameters by 13% (70–74M vs 85M) while matching or exceeding accuracy; on quality+cache, STAR cuts inference cache by 90% (16–23 MB vs 150 MB) at equal or better quality. Against StripedMamba (80M params, 25 MB cache), STAR clearly wins on quality optimization (51.0 vs 49.5 avg accuracy, 7.0 vs 7.2 PPL), matches on quality+size (49.7–50.4 vs 49.5 avg with 8% fewer parameters), and matches on quality+cache (49.5–50.2 vs 49.5 avg) while achieving a further 36% cache reduction (16–23 MB vs 25 MB). At 1B scale, STAR-1B (1.1B params, 86 MB cache) ties StripedMamba at 59.0 average accuracy and 5.7 PPL while using 37% less cache, and outperforms Transformer++ (57.4 avg, 5.9 PPL, 805 MB cache) by 1.6 points in accuracy with 89% less cache.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NSGA-2 multi-objective evolutionary algorithm (Deb et al., 2002) but departs fundamentally from prior NAS work (Pham et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2018; Howard et al., 2019) by grounding the search space in the theory of linear input-varying systems (LIVs), which provides a unified algebraic framework encompassing attention, linear recurrences, convolutions, and memoryless units. Unlike previous automated approaches that targeted narrow domains (e.g., CNN refinement) or limited primitive substitutions, STAR introduces a hierarchical genome encoding that enables simultaneous optimization of featurization, operator structure, and backbone composition. The paper positions itself against the dominant paradigm of manual, intuition-driven architecture design (Mamba, Hyena, striped hybrids) and argues that the homogeneity of Transformer-era architectures reflects the inadequacy of existing automated search spaces rather than the optimality of current designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAR provides the first systematic, gradient-free framework for synthesizing language model architectures that simultaneously optimize quality, parameter count, and inference cache size, achieving 90% cache reduction and 13% parameter reduction while matching or exceeding strong baselines. The LIV unification is a conceptually valuable contribution that makes the architecture design space both comprehensive and well-conditioned (most candidates train stably), addressing a key practical barrier to automated search. However, the evaluation is limited to 125M–1B parameter scale on a single dataset (RedPajama), fixed depth and width, and autoregressive LM only; open questions include whether the discovered motifs generalize to larger scales, variable-depth architectures, and non-language tasks, and whether multi-level hierarchical optimization (refining featurizer, operator, and backbone levels separately) could yield further gains.
