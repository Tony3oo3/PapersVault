## Problem

While sample ordering techniques (e.g., GraB, herding) have been well studied for single-objective finite-sum optimization, the multi-objective setting remains largely unexplored. Naive extensions—using a weighted gradient for a single shared order or running single-objective ordering independently per objective—are unstable under dynamic reweighting and often fail to beat random reshuffling. The paper asks how to jointly determine sample orders across multiple objectives to provably accelerate convergence.

## Input / Output

At each iteration k of epoch t, the method consumes the current model parameters w_t^(k) and the per-sample gradients ∇ℓ_m(w_t^(k); ξ) for all M objectives, producing a centered gradient g_{m,k,t} = ∇ℓ_m − v_t (where v_t is a stale running mean). The output is a permutation π_{t+1}^m for each objective m (determined online by placing each sample at the front or back of the next epoch's sequence) plus the updated model parameters via a weighted gradient step. The balancing vector s is shared across all objectives, coupling their orderings into a single joint decision.

## Method

JoGBa generalizes the online vector balancing problem (Spencer, 1977) from a single objective to M objectives by jointly balancing centered gradients across all objectives using a shared running vector s. At each iteration, a greedy routine compares ‖s + g_{m,k,t}‖_∞ vs ‖s − g_{m,k,t}‖_∞ to decide whether the current sample goes to the front or back of the next epoch's ordering for objective m; the sign ε_{m,k,t} updates s accordingly. Because s is shared, the balancing constraint couples all objectives, yielding a per-epoch parameter-drift bound of AF (vs MAF for independent per-objective balancing). The theoretical analysis shows this reduces the dominant convergence term from O((KT)^{-1/2}) (random ordering) to O((KT)^{-2/3}), and the method is agnostic to the downstream multi-objective weighting algorithm (MGDA, PCGrad, CAGrad, Nash-MTL).

## Results

On NYUv2 (3 tasks: segmentation, depth, surface normal), JoGBa consistently achieves the best Δm% (gap to single-task upper bound) across all four MTL algorithms: e.g., CAGrad+JoGBa reaches Δm% = 0.03 vs. 0.20 for CAGrad+Random and 0.18 for CAGrad+GraB; Nash-MTL+JoGBa reaches Δm% = −4.27 vs. −4.04 for Random. On QM9 (11 molecular properties), JoGBa similarly leads: Nash-MTL+JoGBa achieves Δm% = 59.2 vs. 62.0 for Random and 61.7 for GraB. The per-iteration overhead of JoGBa's sample ordering step is negligible (0.02–0.06 s) compared to model update (0.91–2.97 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JoGBa outperforms the random reshuffling baseline (Ying et al., 2017) across all four MTL algorithms on both NYUv2 and QM9. On NYUv2, the improvement over Random is most pronounced with CAGrad (Δm% drops from 0.20 to 0.03) and Nash-MTL (−4.04 to −4.27), while with MGDA and PCGrad the gains are smaller but consistent (e.g., PCGrad Δm%: 3.97 → 3.56). Against GraB (Lu et al., 2022), which applies single-objective online vector balancing to the weighted gradient, JoGBa achieves uniformly better Δm% on every MTL algorithm and dataset: on QM9, Nash-MTL+JoGBa reaches 59.2 vs. 61.7 for Nash-MTL+GraB, and MGDA+JoGBa reaches 116.7 vs. 118.4 for MGDA+GraB. The ablation (Table 4) further confirms that the joint balancing across objectives is the key factor: applying GraB separately per objective (the 'separate ordering' variant) yields worse results than the joint JoGBa, consistent with the theoretical bound being M times larger for separate ordering (Proposition 3.10 vs. Proposition 3.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-objective data ordering framework of GraB (Lu et al., 2022), which uses online vector balancing (Spencer, 1977) to find better sample permutations than random reshuffling, to the multi-objective setting. The key conceptual departure is that instead of balancing gradients for a single loss or applying single-objective ordering independently per objective, JoGBa jointly balances gradients across all objectives using a shared running vector, thereby accounting for inter-objective conflicts in the ordering decision. The method is orthogonal to and composable with existing dynamic weighting algorithms (MGDA, PCGrad, CAGrad, Nash-MTL), positioning data ordering as a previously overlooked dimension in multi-objective stochastic optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a practical gap: most multi-objective optimization implementations default to random shuffling without considering how sample order interacts with dynamic gradient weighting. The theoretical guarantee of O((KT)^(-2/3)) convergence versus O((KT)^(-1/2)) for random ordering, combined with negligible computational overhead, makes this a low-cost improvement for practitioners. However, empirical gains are modest (particularly on QM9 with 11 objectives), and the method's benefit may diminish as the number of objectives grows, since the shared balancing vector must accommodate more conflicting gradient directions. Open questions include whether the greedy balancing heuristic can be replaced with provably tighter algorithms, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-finite-sum or online multi-objective settings.
