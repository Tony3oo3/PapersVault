## Problem

Aligning diffusion models to human preferences (via RLHF, DPO, or KTO) faces two fundamental obstacles: the reference density is inaccessible (only a score network is available), and the target distributions are highly multimodal, causing standard particle-based optimizers like mean-field Langevin dynamics to require logarithmic Sobolev inequalities whose constants scale exponentially with dimension. The paper asks whether one can develop a distribution-optimization-based alignment method with rigorous convergence guarantees that is free of such isoperimetric conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained score network s(x,t) (defining the reference diffusion process), preference data in the form of sample pairs (DPO), desirable/undesirable labels (KTO), or a reward function (RLHF), and empirical samples drawn from the reference distribution p_ref. It produces a learned correction term u(x,t) that is added to the reference score at each diffusion timestep, yielding an aligned diffusion process whose terminal distribution approximates the KL-regularized optimum. Notably, the aligned model is not a separately trained score network but a modification of the existing one, and the correction term is computed via conditional expectations over the reference diffusion trajectory rather than by direct density evaluation.

## Method

The framework operates in two phases. Phase 1 applies the Dual Averaging (DA) algorithm in the distribution-optimization setting: at each iteration k, a weighted sum of functional derivatives δF/δq is pulled back from the dual space to define an ideal update q̂^(k+1) ∝ exp(−ḡ^(k)) p_ref, and a neural network f_{k+1} is trained to approximate the potential ḡ^(k) using only samples from p_ref. Phase 2 constructs the aligned sampler via Doob's h-transform: the correction term u(x,t) = ∇_x log E[exp(−f_K(X_T^←)) | X_t^← = x] is estimated (e.g., by Monte Carlo over reference diffusion trajectories) and added to the reference score, so the aligned process is dY_t = (Y_t + 2(s(Y_t,t) + u(Y_t,t)))dt + √2 dB_t. This design bypasses isoperimetric conditions because sampling is delegated to the reference diffusion model (which handles multimodality natively) while optimization is handled by DA (which requires only boundedness and Lipschitz continuity of δF/δq in TV distance). The paper proves O(1/K) objective convergence for convex F and gradient-norm convergence for non-convex F, plus an end-to-end TV bound on the sampling error from discretization and score approximation.

## Results

On the 2D Gaussian Mixture Model alignment task using the DPO objective, the proposed method (β'=0.04) achieves a True DPO loss of 0.328 and a Metric loss (mean Euclidean distance to target mode) of 2.022, substantially outperforming Diffusion-DPO which records 0.340/3.011 (50 iterations, no regularization) and 0.343/3.303 (200 iterations, with regularization). The Metric loss improvement is particularly notable: 2.022 vs. 3.011 (a 33% reduction) compared to the best Diffusion-DPO variant. Phase I (Dual Averaging) of the proposed method requires 1320 seconds and 8.75% GPU memory, compared to 585 s / 6.54% for Diffusion-DPO (50 iter.) and 2280 s / 6.54% for Diffusion-DPO (200 iter.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single baseline, Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024), which minimizes an approximate upper bound of the DPO objective rather than the true loss. The proposed method wins on both headline metrics: it achieves a lower True DPO loss (0.328 vs. 0.340 for 50-iter. Diffusion-DPO and 0.343 for 200-iter. Diffusion-DPO) and a substantially lower Metric loss (2.022 vs. 3.011 and 3.303), indicating that the aligned samples are much closer to the target mode. The paper attributes Diffusion-DPO's weaker performance to its optimization of a surrogate upper bound, which fails to fully control the true DPO loss. In terms of compute, the proposed method's Phase I (1320 s, 8.75% GPU) is more expensive than Diffusion-DPO's 50-iteration run (585 s, 6.54%) but cheaper than its 200-iteration run (2280 s), while the Phase II (Doob's h-transform sampling) adds additional cost not captured in the table. On the image generation tasks (Butterfly color alignment and Medical MNIST tilt correction), the paper provides only qualitative visual comparisons (Figure 3) showing progressive improvement over DA iterations, without a quantitative baseline comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate threads: the Dual Averaging (DA) method for distribution optimization (Nesterov, 2009; Nitanda et al., 2021) and Doob's h-transform for constructing guided diffusion processes (Rogers & Williams, 2000; Uehara et al., 2024b). It departs from mean-field Langevin dynamics and particle DA methods, which all require isoperimetric conditions (e.g., LSI) that fail for multimodal data, by using the reference diffusion model's sampling capability to bypass these conditions entirely. Compared to prior diffusion alignment methods like Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024), which optimize an upper bound of the DPO objective, this work directly minimizes the true DPO and KTO objectives while providing end-to-end convergence and sampling error guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide provable convergence for diffusion model alignment without isoperimetric conditions, which is critical because real-world data distributions (images, text) are inherently multimodal and violate LSI. The practical limitation is the computational cost of the h-transform phase (O(L²) time, O(N) memory for Monte Carlo estimation), and empirical validation is restricted to small-scale settings (2D GMM, 32×32 pixel images). Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-resolution image generation or text diffusion models, and whether neural ODE approximations of the correction term (as suggested by Uehara et al., 2024b) can reduce the h-transform cost to make the method competitive with gradient-based fine-tuning in practice.
