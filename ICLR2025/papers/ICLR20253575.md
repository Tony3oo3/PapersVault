## Problem

Objective Perturbation (OP) is a classical and practical method for differentially private convex optimization, but its performance is known to be suboptimal for nonsmooth loss functions because minimizers of nonsmooth objectives are highly unstable. Prior attempts to fix this via smoothing (e.g., Moreau Envelope or naive convolution) either introduced approximation errors that destroyed rate-optimality or required strong regularity conditions. This paper asks whether OP can be made rate-optimal for a broad class of nonsmooth problems whose nonsmoothness is rooted in an implicit ℓ₁-norm structure (e.g., max{0,x}, pinball loss, ℓ₁-regularized GLMs).

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a private dataset of n i.i.d. points z_i drawn from a Euclidean ball B(D), privacy parameters (ε, δ), a nonsmooth loss f(θ;z) = ‖A(z)θ‖₁ + h(zᵀθ) where A(·) is a known m×d matrix-valued function (m ≤ d) and h is a smooth convex scalar function, plus a choice of kernel (Gaussian, Exponential, or Laplacian) and bandwidth μ. It outputs a single parameter vector θ̂ ∈ ℝᵈ that is (ε,δ)-differentially private, obtained by solving a closed-form regularized ERM on the smoothed objective. The theoretical guarantee is an excess generalization risk bound of O(1/√n + √(d ln(1/δ))/(nε)), matching the known optimal rate for smooth DP-SCO.

## Method

The proposed C-OP algorithm wraps standard OP with a convolution smoothing step applied only to the ℓ₁-norm component: f_μ(θ;z) = E_k[‖A(z)θ + μk‖₁] + h(zᵀθ), then adds Gaussian noise b ~ N(0, σ²I) to the empirical objective and solves the resulting strongly convex ERM. The key technical insight is that the central symmetry of the ℓ₁-norm restricts the pointwise approximation error to a much smaller integration domain V_μ^{ℓ₁}(x) = {v : |v| > |x|/μ} (a hollow set with a central rectangle removed), making the error exponentially small in |x|/μ rather than merely linear in μ as the uniform bound suggests. This exponential decay, combined with the fact that convolution approximates from above (g_μ ≥ g), enables a risk decomposition in which the approximation error appears only at the population optimum θ*, and under a mild 'widespread A(z)θ*' assumption it is dominated by the standard statistical terms. The paper also contrasts convolution with Moreau Envelope, showing that Moreau approximates from below (invalidating the upper-bound decomposition) and approximates poorly at most points, making it unsuitable for the tighter pointwise analysis.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.5) establishes that C-OP achieves the rate-optimal excess generalization risk O(1/√n + √(d ln(1/δ))/(nε)) for nonsmooth DP-SCO with an implicit ℓ₁ structure, matching the optimal rate of Noisy-SGD. Experiments on high-dimensional median estimation (d=5, 50 runs) show C-OP outperforming both Moreau Envelope and Noisy-SGD in high-privacy regimes (small ε), while remaining comparable to Noisy-SGD in moderate-privacy settings. The relative risk metric (F(θ̂)−F(θ*))/F(θ*)×100% is used, with C-OP's advantage attributed to its exploitation of the ℓ₁ structure via convolution smoothing rather than indiscriminate gradient noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares C-OP against two baselines: Moreau Envelope + OP (Bassily et al., 2019) and Noisy-SGD (Bassily et al., 2020), evaluated on high-dimensional median estimation and piecewise linear loss problems. C-OP clearly outperforms Moreau Envelope in high-privacy regimes (small ε), where Moreau's approximation-from-below property leads to looser risk bounds and worse empirical performance. Against Noisy-SGD, C-OP achieves comparable relative risk in moderate-privacy settings but pulls ahead in high-privacy regimes, where Noisy-SGD's indiscriminate gradient noise becomes less efficient. The paper attributes C-OP's advantage to its targeted exploitation of the ℓ₁ symmetry (yielding exponentially small approximation errors) versus Noisy-SGD's uniform noise injection. In lower-privacy regimes, the gap narrows and the methods perform similarly, consistent with the theoretical prediction that the approximation error becomes negligible as μ→0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Objective Perturbation (OP) framework of Kifer et al. (2012) to nonsmooth DP-SCO by wrapping it with a convolution smoothing step, following the general idea of Feldman et al. (2018) and Kulkarni et al. (2021) of smoothing before OP. The key departure is the choice of convolution over the more common Moreau Envelope: convolution approximates from above (enabling a tighter risk decomposition) and, crucially, the symmetry of the L1-norm restricts the integration domain for pointwise approximation error, yielding exponentially small errors rather than the linearly small uniform bounds used in prior work. The privacy proof follows Agarwal et al. (2023) but handles the additional matrix A(z) structure. This directly addresses the open problem identified by Bassily et al. (2014), who concluded that 'straightforward smoothing does not yield optimal algorithms.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a decade-old open question by showing that OP can achieve rate-optimal convergence for nonsmooth DP-SCO under a mild structural assumption (implicit L1-norm), overturning the prior belief that smoothing necessarily introduces irreparable approximation error. The conceptual insight—that L1 symmetry makes convolution errors exponentially small, and that approximating from above (vs. Moreau's from below) enables a fundamentally tighter risk decomposition—may have broader applicability beyond DP. However, the optimal rate requires Assumption 4.4 (which needs knowledge of θ*), the model is restricted to low-dimensional A(z)θ, and practical impact is limited since Noisy-SGD remains the dominant method in practice. Open questions include extending to more general nonsmooth functions and removing the distributional assumptions.
