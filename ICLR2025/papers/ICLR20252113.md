## Problem

Despite the success of foundation models across many downstream tasks, their potential to improve hypernetworks—which generate the weights of implicit neural representations (INRs)—has remained unexplored, likely due to the modality gap between neural network weights and typical visual task outputs. This paper asks whether foundation models can enhance hypernetwork performance on generalizable INR tasks, in what ways they help, and how to best design a foundation-model-based hypernetwork.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252113/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D rendered view of a 3D object (ShapeNet NVS) or a randomly cropped audio clip (LibriSpeech-clean) as input, which is discretized into patches and embedded into a d-dimensional token space. The output is a set of per-layer weights for a ReLU MLP with positional encoding (the INR), generated by passing learnable weight tokens through linear heads and modulating base parameters element-wise; the INR then predicts a novel view image or 1-second audio reconstruction. Notably, the hypernetwork produces the entire INR in a single forward pass, and the weight tokens serve a dual role as both prompt tokens for the foundation model and as the source of INR parameters.

## Method

The framework replaces the randomly initialized Transformer encoder in Trans-INR with a pre-trained foundation model (ViT, DINO, DINOv2, CLIP, MAE, or DeiT for images; Whisper for audio), while retaining the architecture of learnable weight tokens concatenated with data tokens, per-layer linear heads, and base parameter modulation. Three training strategies are studied: random initialization, full fine-tuning of the foundation model backbone, and frozen/prompt-tuning where only the weight tokens, linear heads, and base parameters are trained (100K vs. 87M parameters). The key finding is that even with a completely frozen foundation model encoder, a simple linear head on the weight tokens can produce INR weights that match or exceed a fully trained random-init model, suggesting pre-trained visual features implicitly encode useful structure for weight generation. The approach is algorithm-agnostic, extending to neural-process-based (PONP) and weight-sharing (IPC) hypernetwork variants, and the benefits are robust across modalities (3D objects and audio) and scale with both data volume and foundation model size.

## Results

On the novel view synthesis task (ShapeNet, single-view input), fine-tuning a DINOv2 backbone in the Trans-INR hypernetwork achieves PSNR 22.095, SSIM 0.8609, LPIPS 0.1063, and FID 0.1705, outperforming the randomly initialized Trans-INR (PSNR 20.850) by +1.24 dB in PSNR. A frozen DINO encoder with only 100K learnable parameters (vs. 87M for random init) still achieves PSNR 21.035, matching or exceeding the random baseline. On audio reconstruction (LibriSpeech-clean), prompt-tuned Whisper-base matches the randomly initialized IPC baseline (PSNR 24.432 vs. 24.431) while using only 0.1% of the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three state-of-the-art hypernetwork algorithms—Trans-INR, PONP, and IPC—each with and without a DINOv2 foundation model backbone. Fine-tuned Trans-INR achieves the largest gain, improving PSNR by +1.24 dB (20.850→22.095) and reducing LPIPS by 33% (0.1586→0.1063) over the original. Fine-tuned PONP also shows strong improvement (+1.12 dB PSNR, 20.878→21.993), though it remains slightly below fine-tuned Trans-INR, reversing the original ranking where PONP marginally outperformed Trans-INR. Fine-tuned IPC shows the smallest gain (+0.57 dB PSNR, 20.102→20.672) and actually degrades SSIM slightly (0.8344→0.8324), consistent with the authors' observation that weight-sharing methods underfit when trained across all categories simultaneously. Across all three algorithms, the foundation model consistently improves LPIPS and FID, confirming the benefit is algorithm-agnostic. On the audio modality, prompt-tuned Whisper-base (100K params) essentially ties the full randomly initialized IPC model (72.7M params) at PSNR 24.432 vs. 24.431, demonstrating parameter efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Trans-INR architecture (Chen & Wang, 2022) by replacing its randomly initialized Transformer encoder with pre-trained foundation models (DINOv2, CLIP, DINO, etc.), and similarly adapts PONP (Gu et al., 2023) and Instance Pattern Composers (Kim et al., 2023) by swapping their backbones. The key conceptual departure is bridging the modality gap between image-level features learned during pre-training and the weight-generation objective of hypernetworks, a direction the authors argue was previously unexplored. The parameter-efficient variant draws directly on prompt tuning (Jia et al., 2022), repurposing the existing learnable weight tokens as prompt tokens over a frozen backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a frozen foundation model backbone with only 100K learnable parameters matches or exceeds an 87M-parameter randomly initialized hypernetwork offers a practical, low-cost recipe for building generalizable INRs without large-scale training. The paper also provides actionable design guidance: global image representations (from contrastive or self-distillation pre-training) matter more than the specific pre-training objective, and masked autoencoding (MAE) is insufficient. Open questions include whether these benefits extend to other hypernetwork applications beyond INRs (NAS, meta-learning, continual learning), whether the modality gap can be further exploited with cross-modal foundation models, and why weight-sharing methods like IPC degrade when trained across categories rather than per-category.
