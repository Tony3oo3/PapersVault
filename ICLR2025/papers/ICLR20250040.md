## Problem

LLMs struggle with long-term memory because their KV caches cannot effectively aggregate extensive historical data, and existing external memory methods (MemoryStream, MemGPT) store each experience as an isolated flat lookup-table entry, lacking the hierarchical, schema-like structure that human cognition uses to organize and integrate knowledge. Offline structured methods like RAPTOR and GraphRAG capture hierarchy but require full reconstruction to incorporate new information, making them impractical for real-time, evolving conversations.

## Input / Output

MemTree consumes new information sequentially—either individual conversation rounds (no chunking) or document chunks of 512–1024 tokens—and incrementally inserts each into a growing tree, producing a hierarchical memory where every node stores aggregated text content, a semantic embedding, and a depth index that determines its abstraction level. For retrieval, a query embedding is compared against all node embeddings in a flattened (collapsed) tree, returning the top-k most similar nodes (k=3 for short dialogues, k=10 for long ones) which are then fed to an LLM to generate the final answer. The root node is purely structural (no content or embedding), and the tree's branching factor and depth grow organically with the data.

## Method

MemTree maintains a dynamic tree T=(V,E) where each node v holds aggregated content c_v, an embedding e_v, a parent pointer, a set of children, and a depth d_v. Upon receiving new information, the algorithm traverses top-down from the root: at each node it computes cosine similarity between the new content's embedding and each child's embedding, and if the best similarity exceeds a depth-adaptive threshold θ(d)=θ₀e^(λd) (with θ₀=0.4, λ=0.5), it descends into that child; otherwise it attaches a new leaf. All ancestor nodes along the traversal path are then updated via an LLM-based aggregation prompt that progressively abstracts content as the number of children n grows, and their embeddings are recomputed. Retrieval uses a collapsed-tree strategy (inspired by RAPTOR) that flattens all nodes into a single set for simultaneous similarity comparison, avoiding the coverage limitations of level-by-level traversal. The insertion procedure is shown to be an instance of online top-down hierarchical clustering (OTD), yielding a β/3-approximation guarantee to the optimal Moseley-Wang revenue under a β-well-separated data assumption, with O(log N) insertion complexity.

## Results

On MultiHop RAG (609 articles, 2,556 multi-hop questions), MemTree achieves 80.5% overall accuracy, within 0.5 pp of the offline RAPTOR baseline (81.0%) while outperforming it on temporal queries (68.4% vs. 66.0%). On the 200-round MSC-E dialogue benchmark, MemTree reaches 82.5% accuracy versus 80.7% for MemoryStream and 78.0% for the naive full-history baseline. On QuALITY single-document QA, MemTree scores 59.8% overall (56.5% on hard questions), surpassing MemoryStream (43.8%) and matching RAPTOR (59.0%) while retaining online update capability. MemTree requires only 3.27 LLM calls per insertion on average, compared to ~3,750–3,850 calls needed to rebuild RAPTOR or GraphRAG for a single new observation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemTree consistently outperforms the online baseline MemoryStream across all four benchmarks: 80.5% vs. 74.7% on MultiHop RAG (a 5.8 pp gap driven by comparison and temporal queries), 82.5% vs. 80.7% on MSC-E, and 59.8% vs. 43.8% on QuALITY. Against the offline RAPTOR method, MemTree narrows the overall gap to just 0.5 pp on MultiHop RAG (80.5% vs. 81.0%) and actually surpasses RAPTOR on temporal reasoning (68.4% vs. 66.0%) and QuALITY hard questions (56.5% vs. 53.0%), while retaining the critical advantage of online, incremental updates. Compared to GraphRAG, MemTree wins on MultiHop RAG overall (80.5% vs. 78.3%) and on temporal queries (68.4% vs. 66.3%), but trails on QuALITY overall (59.8% vs. 62.8%). On the short 15-round MSC benchmark, MemTree (84.8%) edges out MemoryStream (84.4%) and substantially outperforms MemGPT (70.4%), though the naive full-history baseline (95.6%) still dominates due to the short context length.

## Relation to Prior Work

MemTree sits at the intersection of two research threads: online hierarchical clustering (specifically the OTD algorithm of Menon et al., 2019, whose inter/intra-subtree similarity comparisons are relaxed to cosine similarity in embedding space) and structured retrieval-augmented generation (adopting RAPTOR's collapsed-tree retrieval). It departs from flat lookup-table memory systems (MemoryStream, MemGPT, MemoryBank) by imposing a hierarchical tree structure that aggregates semantics at parent nodes, and from offline structured RAG methods (RAPTOR, GraphRAG) by operating as an online algorithm that incrementally updates the tree without full reconstruction. The theoretical framing as an instance of online hierarchical clustering with a β/3 Moseley-Wang revenue approximation is the paper's key conceptual contribution over prior memory-augmented LLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling real-time, incremental memory updates for LLM agents without the costly full-rebuild required by offline methods like RAPTOR and GraphRAG, making structured memory viable for streaming conversational and document-ingestion settings. The β-well-separated data assumption underlying the approximation guarantee may not hold for highly non-stationary or adversarial data streams, and the reliance on LLM calls for node aggregation (3.27 per insertion) introduces latency and cost that could limit deployment at very high throughput. Open questions include how the tree structure handles truly massive memory (tens of thousands of nodes), whether the depth-adaptive threshold generalizes across domains without retuning, and whether the less-flexible tree topology (vs. general graphs) becomes a bottleneck for highly cross-cutting knowledge.
