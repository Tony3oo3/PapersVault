## Problem

Generative face restoration methods, particularly diffusion-based ones, inevitably produce false hallucinations—incorrect facial attributes (eye color, skin texture) or even altered identities—because the low-quality input contains insufficient information to constrain the generative process. This paper addresses the gap by showing that widely available multi-modal priors (attribute text, reference photos, identity embeddings) can be jointly leveraged to suppress these illusions and enable targeted, controllable restoration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251814/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 512×512 low-quality face image, structured attribute text prompts (a positive description of present attributes, a negative quality string, and a negated attribute sentence), an optional 512×512 high-quality reference image of the same identity, and an ArcFace identity embedding extracted from that reference. The reference image does not require strict pose or expression alignment with the LQ input. The output is a 512×512 high-quality restored face image generated in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v2.1 via 500 denoising steps with a three-prompt classifier-free guidance formula.

## Method

MGFR builds on Stable Diffusion v2.1 with a Dual-Control Adapter (DCA) comprising two parallel branches: an LQ Control Adapter (LCA) that encodes the degraded input and text embeddings, and a Reference Control Adapter (RCA) that extracts identity-relevant details from the reference image and ArcFace embedding. A key architectural departure from standard ControlNet is the addition of a top-down information link from the frozen UNet encoder into LCA (Link-UL) and a bidirectional feature-exchange link between LCA and RCA at every scale (Link-LR), allowing RCA to condition its extraction on what LCA has already processed. Training is split into two stages: first, LCA is trained for text-guided restoration on FFHQ; second, RCA is introduced on the Reface-HQ dataset (21,500 images, 4,800+ identities) with the LCA frozen. At inference, a modified three-prompt CFG combines positive attribute, negative quality, and negative attribute predictions, and 16K synthetic negative-quality images are included in training so the model learns to interpret negative prompts correctly.

## Results

On CelebA-Test (without reference images), MGFR achieves the best non-reference quality scores across all degradation levels: at ×8, it reaches ManIQA 0.6776, ClipIQA 0.8083, and MUSIQ 75.94, outperforming the next-best DiffBIR (0.6058, 0.7439, 73.87). On Reface-Test (with reference images and identity guidance), MGFR further improves to ManIQA 0.6535, ClipIQA 0.8147, MUSIQ 75.51, and achieves the lowest ArcFace identity distance (ID 0.5166 at ×8), demonstrating superior identity preservation. The model also shows consistent gains at ×16 degradation, maintaining MUSIQ above 75 while competitors drop below 72.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGFR outperforms all non-reference baselines (PSFRGAN, GPEN, VQFR, CodeFormer, DR2, BFRffusion, DiffBIR) on every no-reference quality metric (ManIQA, ClipIQA, MUSIQ) across all three degradation levels on CelebA-Test, with the largest margins over VQFR and BFRffusion (e.g., MUSIQ 75.94 vs. 61.92 and 42.84 at ×8). DiffBIR is the closest competitor on LPIPS at ×4 (0.2611 vs. 0.2925) but trails MGFR by 0.06–0.11 on ClipIQA and 1.4–2.1 on MUSIQ at ×8. On Reface-Test with reference images, MGFR surpasses reference-based methods ASFFNet and DMDNet on all no-reference metrics and achieves substantially lower ArcFace identity distance (0.5166 vs. 0.7053 and 0.6964 at ×8), confirming better identity preservation. Against the large-scale SUPIR model, MGFR delivers superior face restoration quality at roughly one-third the inference time (12.5 s vs. 47.6 s) and lower memory usage (15.4 GB vs. 54.3 GB). The paper notes that full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM) favor some baselines, which the authors attribute to the known limitation of these metrics for generative restoration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based face restoration (DiffBIR, DR2, BFRffusion) by integrating three complementary guidance modalities—attribute text prompts, high-quality reference images, and ArcFace identity embeddings—into a single Stable Diffusion framework via a novel Dual-Control Adapter. It departs from the standard ControlNet architecture by adding bidirectional information flow (UNet encoder → LCA, and LCA ↔ RCA) to resolve the information asymmetry that limits single-adapter designs. The two-stage training strategy (text-guided restoration first, then multi-modal guidance) and the counterintuitive use of negative-quality training samples to sharpen Classifier-Free Guidance represent practical departures from prior single-stage, single-modal restoration pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in generative face restoration—false hallucinations of identity and attributes—by showing that multi-modal priors can substantially reduce these artifacts while preserving fidelity to the LQ input. The Reface-HQ dataset (21,500 images, 4,800 identities at 512×512) fills a notable gap in high-resolution reference-face resources. However, the text-prompt interface remains constrained to fixed template sentences with 28 predefined attributes, limiting real-world flexibility, and the authors acknowledge that full-reference metrics (PSNR/SSIM) may no longer be meaningful discriminators for advanced generative restorers. Open questions include extending the framework to video with temporal consistency, leveraging multiple reference images, and compressing the model for practical deployment.
