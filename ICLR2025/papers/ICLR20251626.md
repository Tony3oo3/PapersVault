## Problem

Existing trusted multi-view classification methods rely on late fusion, limiting cross-view information interaction, and their pseudo-view strategies (e.g., simple concatenation in ETMC) are vulnerable to noisy or underperforming views. Additionally, the pseudo view inherently contains a disproportionate amount of information relative to individual views, causing imbalanced multi-view learning that degrades overall performance—a problem unaddressed by prior pseudo-view-guided TMVC methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251626/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-extracted multi-view feature vectors per sample (5–7 views of varying dimensions, e.g., color histograms, SIFT, deep CNN features, text, or audio features) and produces a class label, a per-class probability distribution, and an overall uncertainty score (1−u) derived from the fused Dirichlet distribution. Each view independently generates a subjective opinion (belief masses + uncertainty) via a Softplus-activated evidential head, and these opinions are combined through Dempster's rule to yield the final decision with calibrated uncertainty.

## Method

TEF operates in two stages. Stage 1 formulates pseudo-view generation as a population-based evolutionary neural architecture search: each chromosome encodes a tuple of selected views and fusion operators (from a set of five basic operators), decoded into a sequential fusion network with Power Law + L2 normalization; fitness is classification accuracy on a validation split, and the search uses crossover, mutation, and selection with fitness caching and core-structure-guided acceleration. Stage 2 performs balanced trusted fusion by enhancing each selected view through concatenation with the fusion architecture's decision output (h^out), then passing the enhanced views through evidential classifiers that produce Dirichlet-parameterized subjective opinions. These opinions (one per view plus one for the pseudo view) are aggregated via Dempster's combination rule, and the overall loss combines variational KL-regularized classification losses for each view, the pseudo view, and the fused decision. The key insight is that concatenating the fusion output back into individual views equalizes information content across views, resolving the imbalance without gradient manipulation.

## Results

TEF achieves the best accuracy across all six main datasets: 93.26% on AWA, 75.12% on NUS-WIDE-128, 82.26% on Reuters5, 86.49% on Reuters3, 92.41% on VoxCeleb, and 86.02% on YouTubeFace. The largest improvements over the second-best method are approximately 2.6% on AWA (vs. DC-NAS at 90.66%) and 3.71% on VoxCeleb (vs. DC-NAS at 92.19%). In terms of trustworthiness (1−u), TEF achieves 0.763 on AWA and 0.949 on NUS, substantially exceeding TMC (0.461, 0.684) and ETMC (0.494, 0.793). On the seven additional benchmark datasets (PIE, HandWritten, Scene15, Caltech101, CUB, Animal, NUS-WIDE-OBJECT), TEF also achieves the best accuracy, with a notable 11.75% improvement over the second-best on NUS-WIDE-OBJECT under conflicting conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEF outperforms all four trusted multi-view classification baselines (TMC, TMOA, ETMC, RCML) on every dataset, with the largest gaps on AWA (93.26% vs. 89.17% for TMOA, a 4.09% improvement) and VoxCeleb (92.41% vs. 88.70% for ETMC, a 3.71% improvement). Among non-trusted SOTA methods, DC-NAS (AAAI'24) is the closest competitor, but TEF still leads by 2.6% on AWA, 0.77% on NUS, and 0.74% on YouTubeFace; the gap narrows to 0.22% on VoxCeleb where DC-NAS reaches 92.19%. TEF also surpasses all five basic fusion operators (Add, Mul, Cat, Max, Avg) and all five advanced operators (MLB, MFB, TFN, LMF, PTP) when used as pseudo views, with the largest margin of 7.66% on YouTubeFace over the best single operator. On the seven additional benchmark datasets used by other TMVC methods (PIE, HandWritten, Scene15, Caltech101, CUB, Animal, NUS-WIDE-OBJECT), TEF achieves the best accuracy in all cases, including 97.57% on PIE and 99.64% on HandWritten. Under conflicting/noisy conditions, TEF maintains its lead, achieving 45.84% on NUS-WIDE-OBJECT versus 34.09% for TUNED (AAAI'25), a 11.75% advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEF extends the trusted multi-view classification (TMVC) lineage initiated by TMC (Han et al., ICLR 2021) and its pseudo-view extension ETMC (Han et al., TPAMI 2023), which used simple feature concatenation to create a pseudo view. The key departure is replacing this fixed concatenation with an evolutionary neural architecture search that adaptively selects views and fusion operators, drawing on the evolutionary multi-view fusion framework of Liang et al. (IEEE TEC 2021). The evidential classification backbone (Dirichlet distribution, Dempster-Shafer combination) follows Sensoy et al. (NeurIPS 2018), while the view enhancement strategy—concatenating the fusion architecture's decision output back into each individual view—is a novel contribution addressing the imbalanced multi-view learning problem identified by Peng et al. (CVPR 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete limitations of existing TMVC methods: pseudo-view degradation under noisy views and the imbalanced information problem between pseudo views and individual views. The view enhancement strategy is notably simple (a single concatenation) yet yields substantial trustworthiness gains (up to 26.9% over TMC on AWA), suggesting that prior methods were leaving significant reliability on the table. Open questions remain: the fusion order of opinions is not optimized, there is no mechanism to exclude entirely noisy views from the Dempster-Shafer aggregation, and the evolutionary NAS stage, while accelerated, still requires hours of GPU time. The method's advantage is most pronounced on many-view datasets (>3 views), leaving open whether it scales further or whether the search space becomes intractable.
