## Problem

Existing cytometry analysis methods either operate at the individual cell level (losing population-level insights) or rely on supervised learning with fixed-size inputs, making them unable to handle the variable-sized sets of hundreds of thousands of cells inherent to single-cell data. No self-supervised architecture existed that could simultaneously handle the scale, variable cardinality, and permutation invariance of cytometry sets to produce compact, clinically informative sample-level representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253488/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a variable-sized matrix of cells (rows) × 30 protein marker measurements (columns), with set sizes ranging from ~12K to ~1.4M cells per sample. The student encoder processes a randomly subsampled 10,000-cell subset with Non-Random Block Masking applied at rates of 20–80%, while the teacher encoder processes the full set without subsampling. Output is a fixed 1,024-dimensional latent embedding per sample (via PMA pooling) plus a reconstructed cell set of the same size as the input subset, with reconstruction evaluated via Sinkhorn Optimal Transport rather than element-wise loss to preserve permutation invariance.

## Method

MAESTRO combines three key mechanisms: (1) Non-Random Block Masking (NRBM), which selects a random reference cell, sorts all cells by cosine similarity to it, and masks contiguous blocks in this similarity-ordered sequence before shuffling, forcing the model to infer semantically coherent regions rather than exploiting random redundancy; (2) a Set Transformer encoder (linear projection → 3 ISAB blocks with 2,500 inducing points → PMA pooling) that is permutation-equivariant at intermediate layers and permutation-invariant at the pooled output, reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(nm); and (3) a DINO/iBOT-style self-distillation loop where an EMA-updated teacher (momentum 0.999) encodes the full set as a stable target, and the student's masked-subset embedding is aligned to it via KL divergence on softmax distributions through non-linear projection heads (temperatures 0.1/0.07). The decoder mirrors the encoder with PMA unpooling + 3 SAB blocks + linear projection, and the total loss is the sum of Sinkhorn OT reconstruction loss and the self-distillation KL loss.

## Results

On the 1,514-sample Whole Blood CyTOF dataset, MAESTRO achieves 92.3% accuracy, 0.992 AUROC, and 0.897 F1 for 14-class diagnosis classification, outperforming all baselines. For sex prediction, it reaches 72.3% accuracy and 0.800 AUROC, and for age regression it achieves the lowest MAE (6.70 years) and highest R² (0.711). In cell-type distribution retrieval, MAESTRO also outperforms all baselines across 16 cell types, confirming that its latent representations encode both global (sample-level) and local (cell-level) immunological information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAESTRO outperforms all three set-representation baselines across every evaluated task. On diagnosis classification, MAESTRO (92.3% acc) beats Set Transformer (88.4%), OTKE (87.8%), and Deep Sets (83.6%), with the largest gap over Deep Sets at ~9 points. On sex prediction, the advantage is even more pronounced: MAESTRO (72.3% acc, 0.800 AUROC) substantially outperforms OTKE (51.3% acc, 0.521 AUROC), which is near chance level, while Set Transformer (69.8%) and Deep Sets (68.2%) are closer but still behind. For age regression, MAESTRO achieves 6.70 MAE and 0.711 R², improving over Set Transformer (7.46 MAE, 0.687 R²), Deep Sets (7.83 MAE, 0.662 R²), and OTKE (8.46 MAE, 0.647 R²). Notably, OTKE and Deep Sets/Set Transformer were restricted to 10,000-cell subsets due to computational constraints, whereas MAESTRO processes the full variable-sized set (up to 1.4M cells), making the comparison partially favorable to MAESTRO in terms of input information.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAESTRO composes three established self-supervised learning paradigms—masked autoencoding (MAE), student-teacher self-distillation with EMA (DINO), and online tokenization (iBOT)—within a Set Transformer backbone (ISAB, PMA, SAB blocks) to create a self-supervised architecture for set-structured data. The key departure from prior work is the application to cytometry at the scale of hundreds of thousands of variable-sized cells, where existing set representation methods (Deep Sets, Set Transformer, OTKE) either cannot handle the input size, require fixed-size sets, or depend on supervision. The paper also introduces Non-Random Block Masking (NRBM) and Sinkhorn Optimal Transport as a permutation-invariant reconstruction loss, both tailored to the structure of single-cell protein measurement data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAESTRO provides the first practical self-supervised method for generating compact, permutation-invariant representations of entire immune profiles from CyTOF data, outperforming manual gating (the clinical gold standard) on diagnosis, age, and sex prediction while also recovering cell-type distributions without labels. This opens the door to scalable, label-free immune profiling for precision medicine applications such as treatment response prediction, early disease detection, and vaccine response assessment. Key limitations include high computational demands (4×A100 GPUs, 156 hours), single-sample batch processing due to variable set sizes, interpretability gaps in the latent space, and dependence on fixed protein panels—constraining real-time or multi-modal deployment.
