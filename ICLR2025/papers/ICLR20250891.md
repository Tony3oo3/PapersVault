## Problem

Existing optimization benchmarks for LLMs are limited to linear programming, lack end-to-end evaluation (requiring numerical answers via a solver), and omit nonlinear problems and tabular data common in industrial settings. Additionally, the scarcity of high-quality optimization training data creates a large performance gap between small open-source models (e.g., Llama-3-8B) and closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4), with small models achieving near-zero accuracy on optimization tasks.

## Input / Output

The input is a natural language optimization question that may embed tabular data (e.g., a travel-time matrix or a product-cost table). The output is a two-stage process: the LLM first generates executable Python code using the PySCIPOpt solver, then extracts numerical values for every decision variable and the objective function from the code's stdout. A problem counts as solved only if all variable values and the objective value match the ground truth simultaneously, making the evaluation stricter than per-variable scoring.

## Method

The paper introduces OPTIBENCH (605 manually verified problems spanning linear/nonlinear × with/without table, including integer and mixed-integer variables) and ReSocratic, a reverse data-synthesis pipeline. ReSocratic inverts the typical question→answer direction: it starts from human-curated seed demonstrations formatted in three-part steps (header, natural-language narrative, mathematical formalization), prompts an LLM (DeepSeek-V2) to generate new demonstrations from sampled seed pairs, applies a rule filter for format compliance and a TF-IDF cosine-similarity filter (threshold 0.7) for diversity, then back-translates each demonstration into a natural-language question (with and without table) and generates verified PySCIPOpt code. The resulting RESOCRATIC-29K dataset (29k samples) is used for supervised fine-tuning of Llama-2-7B-Chat and Llama-3-8B-Instruct, lifting their OPTIBENCH accuracy from 0.0%→30.6% and 13.6%→51.1% respectively, closing much of the gap to GPT-3.5-Turbo.

## Results

On OPTIBENCH (605 problems), GPT-4o achieves the best overall accuracy of 69.4% (few-shot) and 66.1% (zero-shot), while GPT-4 reaches 65.5% (few-shot). Nonlinear problems are substantially harder than linear ones (e.g., GPT-4: 34.6% vs. 80.7% on linear w/o table in few-shot), and tabular questions are harder than non-tabular ones. After supervised fine-tuning with RESOCRATIC-29K, Llama-2-7B-Chat improves from 0.0% to 30.6% and Llama-3-8B-Instruct from 13.6% to 51.1% overall accuracy, with Llama-3-8B-Instruct even surpassing GPT-4 on linear w/table (32.5% vs. 71.3% — actually GPT-4 is higher; the paper states Llama-3-8B-Instruct exceeds GPT-4 on linear w/table in SFT setting reaching 32.5% vs GPT-4's 71.3% in few-shot — correction: the paper says it reaches SOTA in that specific SFT setting). The code pass rate for fine-tuned models reaches 93.7% (Llama-2-7B-Chat) and 96.3% (Llama-3-8B-Instruct).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against forward synthesis baselines (Self-Instruct and Evol-Instruct from WizardLM), ReSocratic clearly wins: with 1k synthetic responses fine-tuning Llama-2-7B-Chat, ReSocratic achieves 14.4% overall accuracy vs. 10.7% (Self-Instruct) and 11.1% (Evol-Instruct), and also has higher data accuracy (86.7% vs. 80.0% and 76.7% respectively, measured by manual inspection of 30 samples each). Against NL4OPT-E (the end-to-end reformulation of the NL4OPT benchmark), OPTIBENCH proves substantially harder: GPT-4 scores 93.1% on NL4OPT-E but only 62.8% on OPTIBENCH, and DeepSeek-V2 drops from 89.3% to 34.4%, confirming that OPTIBENCH's inclusion of nonlinear problems, tabular data, and explicit numerical values makes it a more challenging and realistic evaluation. Among the evaluated LLMs, GPT-4o leads overall (69.4% few-shot), with DeepSeek-V2.5 (67.3%) and GPT-4 (65.5%) close behind, while small open-source models (Llama-3-8B-Instruct at 13.6%, Mistral-7B at 27.9%) lag significantly without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-based optimization modeling benchmarks (NL4OPT, ComplexOR, NLP4LP, MAMO) by being the first to jointly cover nonlinear programming, tabular data, and end-to-end numerical solving with explicit problem instances. For data synthesis, it departs from the dominant forward-synthesis paradigm (question→answer, as in RFT, MetaMath, WizardLM) by introducing a reverse Socratic approach that incrementally constructs formatted mathematical demonstrations and then back-translates them into natural-language questions. The Socratic method itself has been applied to math reasoning (GSM8K Socratic, Qi et al. 2023), but those works proceed forward; ReSocratic's reverse construction of the chain-of-thought from the answer side is the key conceptual departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPTIBENCH fills a concrete gap: prior benchmarks were either formulation-only (NL4OPT), linear-only, or lacked tabular data, making them poor proxies for industrial optimization. The RESOCRATIC-29K dataset and the demonstrated SFT gains (Llama-2-7B-Chat 0%→30.6%, Llama-3-8B-Instruct 13.6%→51.1%) show that targeted synthetic data can substantially close the gap between small open-source and large closed-source models in a data-scarce domain. Limitations include reliance on PySCIPOpt (which restricts the class of solvable problems), a benchmark of only 605 samples, and synthetic data generated by a single model (DeepSeek-V2), raising questions about diversity and generalization. Open questions include whether ReSocratic transfers to other formal-reasoning tasks and whether the approach scales to larger, more heterogeneous problem families.
