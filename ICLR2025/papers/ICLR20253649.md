## Problem

Neural agents in emergent communication struggle to achieve compositional generalization—describing previously unseen objects by combining known concepts in novel ways. Prior work shows that discretization bias alone is insufficient, and that agents must first learn to decompose complex objects into basic concepts before they can compose them. This paper asks whether a structured two-phase training approach, grounded in multi-target coordination games and a discrete codebook, can enable agents to reach perfect compositionality and even zero-shot generalization.

## Input / Output

The sender observes a set of target images (multiple in the DECOMPOSE step, a single image in the COMPOSE step) while the receiver observes one target plus distractors from a candidate set. Image formats vary by dataset: THING uses 270-dim concatenated one-hot vectors, SHAPE uses colored shapes on black backgrounds, MNIST uses 28×28 grayscale digits (paired into two-digit composites), COCO uses real multi-object photos, and QRC uses QR-code images. The sender outputs a fixed-length bag-of-words message drawn from a discrete codebook whose vocabulary size matches the number of basic concepts, and the receiver outputs a target identification from the candidate set.

## Method

CtD (Composition through Decomposition) is a two-phase curriculum: in the DECOMPOSE step, agents play a multi-target coordination game on a single-concept dataset, learning a VQ-VAE codebook where each code-word encodes one basic concept; in the COMPOSE step, the sender reuses this initialized codebook to describe novel composite images by selecting and concatenating the top-l most similar code-words. The multi-target setup (sender averages representations of multiple targets sharing one concept) is shown to be essential—reducing to a single target collapses compositionality to near-random. Training minimizes a combined loss of a task-specific term (cross-entropy, BCE, or MSE) plus a VQ-VAE codebook commitment loss using a straight-through estimator, with both hyperparameters set to 1.0. A key finding is that zero-shot evaluation on the COMPOSITE-PHRASE dataset after only the DECOMPOSE step matches or exceeds fully trained CtD on several datasets, indicating the codebook captures transferable concept structure.

## Results

On the COMPOSITE-PHRASE dataset, CB-CtD achieves perfect accuracy (1.000) and perfect compositionality (AMI=1.000, CBM=1.000, CI=1.000, BOS=0.975) on THING, near-perfect accuracy (0.988) with perfect CBM and CI on SHAPE, and strong CI (0.929) and CBM (0.950) on MNIST. Zero-shot transfer (CtD-ZS) after only the DECOMPOSE step matches or exceeds fully trained CtD on THING (ACC=1.000, CBM=1.000) and MNIST (ACC=0.89 vs. 0.81), demonstrating that the codebook learned during decomposition is sufficient for compositional generalization without further training. On the non-compositional QRC dataset, CB-CtD achieves high accuracy (0.956) but near-random compositionality (CBM=0.172), confirming that the method's gains are specific to compositional structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CB-CtD decisively outperforms both GS-CtD and QT-CtD on all three compositional datasets. On THING, CB-CtD achieves perfect accuracy (1.000) and CBM (1.000), while GS-CtD reaches only 0.741/0.250 and QT-CtD 0.653/0.260. On SHAPE, the gap is even starker: CB-CtD attains 0.988 accuracy and 1.000 CBM versus 0.135/0.609 (GS) and 0.100/0.714 (QT). On MNIST, CB-CtD leads in accuracy (0.811 vs. 0.799 GS, 0.391 QT) and CBM (0.950 vs. 0.862 GS, 0.768 QT). On COCO, CB-CtD wins on accuracy (0.654) and CI (0.342) but QT-CtD edges ahead on CBM (0.750 vs. 0.638), suggesting QT's quantization is slightly more effective for real-world images. On the non-compositional QRC dataset, CB-CtD achieves the highest accuracy (0.956) but all protocols show near-zero compositionality, confirming that the CtD advantage is specific to compositional structure rather than general task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VQ-VAE codebook mechanism (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and the multi-target coordination game setup (Mu & Goodman, 2021), combining them into a novel two-step 'Composition through Decomposition' (CtD) training regimen. It departs from the dominant single-target referential game paradigm in emergent communication by arguing that multi-target games are necessary for concept decomposition, and from prior discretization methods (Gumbel-softmax, quantized communication) by showing that a learned discrete codebook is superior for compositional generalization. The approach also draws on curriculum learning principles from developmental psychology, framing the decompose-then-compose sequence as a scaffolding strategy, and extends the online clustered codebook of Zheng & Vedaldi (2023) into the multi-agent communication setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates for the first time that neural agents can achieve both perfect accuracy and perfect compositionality on multi-word image datasets, and that zero-shot compositional generalization is possible after a dedicated decomposition phase — a result that challenges the assumption that agents must be explicitly trained on composite phrases. Key limitations include the fixed message length constraint, the requirement that the concept set be known a priori, and the failure to generalize to non-compositional encodings (QRC dataset). An important open question the authors raise is whether the sender can self-represent concepts without supervision, which would remove the need for a predefined concept vocabulary and bring the system closer to truly open-ended language emergence.
