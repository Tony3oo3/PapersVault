## Problem

Existing Lasso-based sparse linear bandit algorithms that achieve O(polylog d T) regret require both a compatibility condition on the expected Gram matrix and additional diversity assumptions on context features (e.g., anti-concentration, relaxed symmetry, balanced covariance), whose relative strengths had never been formally compared. This paper asks whether the compatibility condition on the optimal arm alone—without any extra diversity conditions—is sufficient to guarantee polylogarithmic regret, and shows affirmatively that it is, making it strictly weaker than all prior assumptions in the single-parameter setting.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the agent observes K context feature vectors {x_{t,k} ∈ R^d} drawn i.i.d. from an unknown joint distribution (with ℓ∞-norm bounded by x_max), selects one arm, and receives a scalar reward r_{t,a_t} = x_{t,a_t}^T β* + η_t where β* is s_0-sparse. The algorithm's tunable inputs are the forced-sampling length M_0, the weight w between exploration and exploitation losses, and the regularization schedule {λ_t}. The output is a sequence of arm selections and, at each greedy round, a sparse Lasso estimate β̂_{t-1} computed via a weighted least-squares objective that down-weights the M_0 exploration samples by factor w relative to the exploitation samples.

## Method

FS-WLasso operates in two stages: a forced-sampling phase of M_0 rounds with uniform random arm selection, followed by a greedy phase where the agent solves a weighted Lasso problem—minimizing w·L_0(β) + L_{t-1}(β) + λ_{t-1}‖β‖_1, where L_0 is the squared loss over exploration samples and L_{t-1} over exploitation samples—and selects the arm maximizing x_{t,k}^T β̂_{t-1}. The key theoretical contribution is showing that the compatibility condition on the optimal arm's expected Gram matrix Σ* = E[x_{t,a_t*}x_{t,a_t*}^T] (Assumption 3), combined with the α-margin condition, suffices for O(polylog d T) regret, eliminating the need for anti-concentration, relaxed symmetry, or balanced covariance. The proof introduces a novel high-probability induction argument that exploits the cyclic structure between small Lasso estimation error and subsequent optimal-arm selection, carefully controlling the probability of induction failure across rounds. The algorithm also yields a T-agnostic variant (FS-Lasso) and, when stronger diversity assumptions are available, achieves the same regret without any forced-sampling phase (M_0 = 0).

## Results

The paper establishes that FS-WLasso achieves O(poly log d T) regret under only the compatibility condition on the optimal arm (Assumption 3) plus the margin condition, which is strictly weaker than all prior Lasso bandit assumptions in the single-parameter setting. Specifically, for α=1 the regret is O(s₀² log T (log d + log log T)), and for α>1 it is O(s₀^(2+2/α) log d). In numerical experiments across 100 runs, FS-WLasso and FS-Lasso consistently achieve lower cumulative regret than all five baselines in both a correlated Gaussian setting and a setting where sub-optimal arm features are fixed (invalidating anti-concentration assumptions used by prior methods).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FS-WLasso and FS-Lasso against five existing sparse linear bandit algorithms: DR Lasso Bandit (Kim & Paik, 2019), SA Lasso BANDIT (Oh et al., 2021), TH Lasso Bandit (Ariu et al., 2022), L1-CB-Lasso (Li et al., 2021), and ESTC (Hao et al., 2020b). In Experiment 1 (correlated Gaussian features), FS-WLasso and FS-Lasso achieve the lowest cumulative regret across all 100 runs, outperforming all five baselines. In Experiment 2, where sub-optimal arm features are fixed (making anti-concentration and relaxed symmetry assumptions invalid), the proposed algorithms maintain strong performance while baselines relying on those assumptions degrade significantly. Theoretically, the paper's assumption (compatibility on the optimal arm only) is strictly weaker than the combined assumption sets of Oh et al. (2021), Ariu et al. (2022), Li et al. (2021), and Chakraborty et al. (2023), as formalized in Theorem 1 and Figure 1. The regret bound matches or improves upon Li et al. (2021) and Chakraborty et al. (2023) while requiring no additional diversity conditions on context features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the forced-sampling technique introduced by Goldenshluger & Zeevi (2013) and adapted by Bastani & Bayati (2020) for high-dimensional linear bandits, combining it with a weighted Lasso estimator. It departs from the existing sparse linear bandit literature (Kim & Paik 2019, Oh et al. 2021, Ariu et al. 2022, Li et al. 2021, Chakraborty et al. 2023) by showing that the compatibility condition on the optimal arm's expected Gram matrix—without additional diversity assumptions like anti-concentration, relaxed symmetry, or balanced covariance—is sufficient for poly-logarithmic regret under the margin condition. The authors rigorously prove this assumption is strictly weaker than those in prior work, establishing a clear hierarchy of distributional conditions (Figure 1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the weakest known set of assumptions for achieving O(polylog d T) regret in sparse linear contextual bandits, which is practically significant because the additional diversity assumptions in prior work are unverifiable in high-dimensional settings and, when violated, leave existing algorithms with no guarantees. The novel high-probability induction technique that captures the cyclic structure between estimation error and optimal arm selection is noted as potentially applicable to other bandit algorithms. Open questions include extending the result to sparsity-agnostic settings, the multiple-parameter setting, and whether the forced-sampling stage can be further reduced or eliminated in practice.
