## Problem

Customizing pre-trained LLMs to new human preferences (e.g., domain-specific styles or additional alignment traits) typically causes catastrophic forgetting of the model's original capabilities. Existing anti-forgetting mechanisms like KL-divergence constraints in DPO/PPO are empirically insufficient, and the reward functions used to train open-source LLMs are unavailable, making direct reward maximization infeasible. The paper asks: how can we adapt an LLM to a new preference while provably preserving its original knowledge, without access to either the original or the new reward function?

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, aligned via RLHF) and a preference dataset of (prompt, preferred response, unpreferred response) triples. At inference, it requires two forward passes per token — one through the base model and one through the LoRA adapter — whose outputs are combined via a closed-form policy formula (Equation 6) to produce the final token distribution. The output is a text response that simultaneously satisfies the original alignment objective (r₁) and the new preference (r₂), with the relative weighting controlled by the hyper-parameter α₀.

## Method

Q-Adapter reframes LLM customization as maximizing the sum of two unknown reward functions (λr₁ + r₂) using the residual Q-learning framework. The key insight is that for a fixed pre-trained policy π₁*, the new reward r₂ and the residual Q-function Q̂ are in one-to-one correspondence, so Q̂ can be learned directly from preference data via a Bradley-Terry-style loss on the implicitly derived reward r₂(s,a;θ) = Q_θ(s,a) − γ·E[α̃·log Σ exp((Q_θ(s',a') + α₀·log π₁*(a'|s'))/α̃)], augmented with L2 regularization. The adapter is a LoRA module that approximates Q̂, and at inference the customized policy is computed as π̃*(a|s) ∝ exp[(α₀·log π₁*(a|s) + Q_θ(s,a))/α̃], keeping the base model intact and involving it at every inference step. This design ensures the original knowledge is structurally preserved rather than merely regularized during training.

## Results

On the DSP dataset, Q-Adapter consistently outperforms all baselines across five general-capability benchmarks (MMLU, MMLU Pro, GSM8k, BBH, IFEval) in all four domains, e.g., achieving 66.81 MMLU and 78.32 GSM8k in the Business domain versus 64.27 and 76.19 for Replay, 59.84 and 72.63 for DPO, and a catastrophic 55.19 and 23.06 for PPO. On the new-preference metric (length-controlled win rate against SFT via GPT-4o), Q-Adapter achieves the highest win rates in all four domains (55.59%, 53.84%, 63.77%, 58.20%), confirming it learns the target preference while retaining original knowledge. On HH-RLHF (helpful→harmless), Q-Adapter also achieves the best win rates on both the helpful (r₁) and harmless (r₂) splits, outperforming Replay and DPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-Adapter outperforms all three baseline categories on both the anti-forgetting metric (r₁, benchmark scores) and the new-preference metric (r₂, GPT-4o win rate against SFT). Against Replay (Lin et al., 2024), Q-Adapter leads by 1.5–2.5 points on MMLU and 1.5–2.2 points on GSM8k across domains, and by 1–7 points on win rate (e.g., 63.77% vs. 46.66% in Entertainment). Against PR (DPO) (Rafailov et al., 2023), Q-Adapter gains 2–7 points on MMLU and 3–6 points on GSM8k, with the gap widening in the Business domain (66.81 vs. 59.84 MMLU); on win rate, Q-Adapter leads by 1.3–7.5 points. Against PR (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017), Q-Adapter is most dramatically superior: in the Business domain, PPO suffers catastrophic forgetting (MMLU Pro drops to 13.69, GSM8k to 23.06, IFEval to 10.49), while Q-Adapter retains near-base-model performance. On HH-RLHF, Q-Adapter also beats both Replay and DPO on the combined helpful+harmless win rates, whereas DPO shows the weakest performance in this cross-domain setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Residual Q-learning (RQL) framework (Li et al., 2023), originally developed for control tasks, to the problem of LLM customization, combined with the Bradley-Terry preference model and maximum entropy RL (Haarnoja et al., 2018). The key conceptual departure from prior RLHF-based customization methods (DPO, PPO) is replacing the KL-divergence anti-forgetting constraint with an explicit maximization of the original reward r1 via the residual Q-function, which keeps the pre-trained model architecturally intact at inference time. A further technical contribution is Corollary 4.1, which shows that for a fixed pre-trained LLM, the new reward r2 and the residual Q-function are in one-to-one correspondence, enabling direct learning of Q̂ from preference data without first training a separate reward model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Q-Adapter offers a practical recipe for customizing open-source LLMs (e.g., Llama-3.1) to domain-specific or new preferences while preserving general capabilities, without requiring access to the original reward function or training data. The architectural choice of retaining the base model at inference (rather than fine-tuning it) provides a stronger anti-forgetting guarantee than KL-divergence constraints, which the paper empirically demonstrates degrade over training. Limitations include the assumption that the base model was aligned via RLHF, testing on only one model family (Llama-3.1-8B), and a single round of customization; extending to SFT-only base models, multi-round adaptation, and larger/more diverse model families remain open.
