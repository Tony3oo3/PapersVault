## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods rely on pixel-level optimization that becomes prohibitively expensive and ineffective at high resolutions (≥128×128), while recent diffusion-based approaches like Minimax focus on distribution alignment rather than directly optimizing for training effectiveness. The key unaddressed question is how to generate synthetic data that is specifically tailored to maximize downstream training performance, without requiring costly retraining of the diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253068/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained latent diffusion model (DiT with a Stable Diffusion VAE), class labels for conditional generation, and a set of representative checkpoints with pre-computed averaged gradients obtained by training a lightweight surrogate (ConvNet-6) on the original dataset. It produces decoded synthetic RGB images at the target resolution (e.g., 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) at a specified IPC per class, forming a compact surrogate dataset. Guidance is applied only during early-to-mid DDIM sampling steps (e.g., t ∈ [30,45] out of 50), with vanilla denoising handling the remaining steps to preserve image quality.

## Method

IGD reframes dataset distillation as a training-free guided diffusion sampling problem by establishing a formal link between the DD objective and the trajectory influence function, which approximates how much a training sample reduces validation loss. The influence guidance G_I computes cosine similarity between pre-stored averaged real-data gradients (from a gradient-similarity-filtered set of checkpoints) and the gradient of the decoded synthetic data at each checkpoint, avoiding the need to retrain on synthetic data at every sampling step. A complementary deviation guidance G_D penalizes cosine similarity to the most similar previously generated sample in a running memory, enforcing diversity. The guided sampling step subtracts scaled gradients of both G_I (evaluated on the DDIM-predicted clean data ẑ_{0|t}) and G_D from the vanilla denoising update, with an adaptive scale factor ρ_t that normalizes the influence gradient relative to the denoised signal magnitude. A gradient-similarity-based checkpoint selection algorithm retains only representative training checkpoints (typically 3–6 out of 50 epochs), and guidance is restricted to early-to-mid sampling steps to prevent overfitting to the surrogate while still steering semantic content effectively.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224, ResNet-18), Minimax-IGD achieves 60.3% top-1 accuracy at IPC=50, surpassing the previous SOTA RDED (56.5%) by 3.8% and the pretrained DiT baseline (52.9%) by 7.4%. At IPC=10, Minimax-IGD reaches 46.2% vs. RDED's 42.0%. On the ImageNette/ImageWoof subsets, DiT-IGD and Minimax-IGD consistently outperform all pixel-level baselines (DM, IDC-1) and the vanilla DiT/Minimax backbones by 4–7% across ConvNet-6, ResNetAP-10, and ResNet-18 at IPC 10/50/100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGD substantially outperforms all pixel-level distillation baselines: DM and IDC-1 show only marginal gains over random sampling at IPC=10 on ImageNette/ImageWoof, and their advantage vanishes or reverses at IPC≥50, while DiT-IGD/Minimax-IGD maintain 5–10% leads. Against the pretrained DiT backbone, IGD adds +5.9% (IPC=10) and +6.9% (IPC=50) on ImageNet-1K, and +5.8–6.6% on average across Nette/Woof at IPC≥50. Against Minimax (the closest diffusion-based competitor), IGD provides a further +1.9% (IPC=10) and +1.7% (IPC=50) on ImageNet-1K, and +4.7–5.1% on Nette/Woof. On ImageNet-1K, Minimax-IGD surpasses the previous SOTA image-based method RDED by 4.0% at IPC=50 and 4.2% at IPC=10, and also beats G-VBSM (+14.8% at IPC=50) and SRe²L (+13.5% at IPC=50). In cross-architecture evaluation (Table 3), DiT-IGD and Minimax-IGD outperform RDED by an average of 4.6% and 5.0% at IPC=50 across ResNet-101, MobileNet-V2, EfficientNet-B0, and Swin Transformer. On CIFAR-10/100, DiT-IGD approaches the lossless DATM baseline (84.6% vs. 85.5% at IPC=1000 on CIFAR-10) while consistently exceeding SRe²L and RDED. Among other diffusion-based methods on ImageNet-1K, Minimax-IGD (60.3%) beats TDSDM (59.4%) and D⁴M (55.2%) at IPC=50.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two research threads: trajectory influence functions (Pruthi et al., 2020), which quantify how individual training samples affect validation loss, and guided-diffusion sampling (Bansal et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2023), which steers generation via gradient-based metric functions. The key departure from prior diffusion-based dataset distillation (Minimax, Gu et al. 2024) is that IGD is training-free at the diffusion-model level: rather than fine-tuning the generative model with a minimax objective, it injects influence and diversity guidance directly into the DDIM/DPM sampling loop. This reframes dataset distillation from a model-optimization problem into a conditional-sampling problem, where the 'condition' is an abstract training-effectiveness criterion rather than an explicit content specification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of IGD means it can be applied to any pretrained latent diffusion model (DiT, Minimax-tuned DiT) without retraining, making it broadly applicable as new diffusion backbones emerge. The finding that distribution-alignment metrics (FID, Wasserstein distance) do not reliably predict training effectiveness—Minimax-IGD achieves better accuracy than DiT-IGD despite worse FID—challenges the implicit assumption in prior DD work that closer distribution matching is always better. The main open limitation is the 5–6× sampling-time overhead from guidance computation, which the authors acknowledge as a barrier for very large-scale distillation under resource constraints; improving guidance efficiency (e.g., via the DPM-solver variant that halves runtime) is a natural next step.
