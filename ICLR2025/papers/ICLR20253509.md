## Problem

Current nonconvex FL algorithms require problem-specific hyperparameters—smoothness constants, gradient variances, and data heterogeneity bounds—to determine stepsizes, yet these quantities are infeasible to estimate under FL's privacy constraints where raw data is never shared. While recent adaptive methods (FedOpt, FedSPS) reduce some tuning burden, they still depend on at least one problem parameter or assume bounded heterogeneity. This paper asks whether a nonconvex FL algorithm can operate fully autonomously, with all stepsizes determined solely by system-defined constants, while handling arbitrary heterogeneous data and partial client participation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes local stochastic samples from each participating client's private dataset (EMNIST digits, CIFAR-10 images) along with an initial model θ⁰ and three system-defined constants: participating clients S, local update steps K, and total communication rounds T. All stepsizes (local η, global γ, momentum β) are computed exclusively from S, K, and T—no smoothness constant, variance bound, or heterogeneity parameter enters the update rules. The output is a global model reaching an ε-stationary point (‖∇f(θ)‖ ≤ ε) with O(ε⁻⁴) sample and O(ε⁻³) communication complexity, improvable to O(ε⁻³) and O(ε⁻²) with variance reduction.

## Method

PAdaMFed extends SCAFFOLD by integrating three synergistic components: (1) gradient normalization, where each local step uses the adaptive stepsize η/‖g_i^{t,k}‖ so every client advances by exactly η in norm regardless of local gradient magnitude; (2) client-side momentum g^t that recursively accumulates descent directions across clients and rounds, smoothing heterogeneity-induced variability; and (3) SCAFFOLD-style control variates (local c_i^t, global c^t) that correct client drift without requiring any heterogeneity bound. The key insight is that normalizing the descent direction decouples the stepsize from the local gradient scale, making it a pure function of system parameters, while the momentum–control-variate interaction handles arbitrary data heterogeneity. The variance-reduced variant (PAdaMFed-VR) follows the STORM principle, reusing each sample ξ_i^{t,k} to compute gradients at both the current local model and the previous global model, reducing within-client noise and achieving the best-known O(ε⁻³) sample complexity with linear speedup in S and K.

## Results

On EMNIST, PAdaMFed and PAdaMFed-VR significantly outperform FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, and SCAFFOLD-M in both convergence speed and final test accuracy under both i.i.d. and non-i.i.d. data splits, with the non-i.i.d. setting showing even larger performance margins. In terms of stepsize robustness, PAdaMFed maintains test accuracy above 0.8 across the learning rate range [3×10⁻³, 10⁻¹] for i.i.d. data and above 0.7 for non-i.i.d. data, whereas baselines exhibit substantially narrower stable regions. Theoretically, PAdaMFed achieves O(ε⁻⁴) sample complexity and O(ε⁻³) communication complexity with constant stepsizes, while PAdaMFed-VR improves to the best-known O(ε⁻³) sample and O(ε⁻²) communication complexity—all without requiring any problem-specific parameters (L, σ², Δ, or heterogeneity bounds).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAdaMFed outperforms FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, and SCAFFOLD-M in both convergence speed and final test accuracy on EMNIST under i.i.d. and non-i.i.d. splits, with the advantage being most pronounced under non-i.i.d. conditions. Against SCAFFOLD-M, which uses a similar client-side momentum mechanism, PAdaMFed converges faster and reaches higher accuracy, validating the contribution of the adaptive (normalized) stepsize design. In the theoretical comparison (Table 1), PAdaMFed matches the communication complexity of MIME and SCAFFOLD-M (O(1/(SKε⁴))) but is the only method requiring zero problem-specific parameters for stepsize selection, whereas MIME needs L, Δ, σ², σ_h², SCAFFOLD-M needs L, Δ, σ², G₀, and FedSPS needs L and per-client loss lower bounds ℓ_i*. PAdaMFed-VR achieves the best-known O(1/(SKε³)) communication complexity matching FAFED, but unlike FAFED it does not require bounded gradients or bounded data heterogeneity, and unlike SCAFFOLD-M-VR it does not depend on L, Δ, or σ² for stepsize tuning. In the stepsize robustness experiment (Figure 2), PAdaMFed maintains stable accuracy (>0.8 i.i.d., >0.7 non-i.i.d.) across a 30× wider learning rate range than any baseline, confirming its practical advantage in hyperparameter-free deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAdaMFed extends the SCAFFOLD framework (control variates for client drift) by integrating client-side momentum and a gradient-normalization-based adaptive stepsize, while its variance-reduced variant (PAdaMFed-VR) follows the STORM principle for sample-efficient gradient estimation. The key conceptual departure from prior work (MIME, SCAFFOLD-M, FedSPS, FAFED) is the complete elimination of problem-specific parameters—smoothness constants, heterogeneity bounds, and gradient variances—from all stepsize determinations, replacing them with system-defined constants (S, K, T). This positions the paper as the first FL algorithm to simultaneously achieve parameter-agnostic operation, no heterogeneity-bound requirement, and state-of-the-art convergence rates (O(ε⁻⁴) / O(ε⁻³) with VR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating hyperparameter tuning is a major practical barrier to FL deployment, and this paper provides the first theoretical guarantee that such tuning is unnecessary while preserving optimal convergence rates. The stepsize robustness demonstrated empirically (stable accuracy across a 30× range of learning rates) directly addresses a key pain point in real-world FL systems. However, experimental validation is limited to EMNIST and CIFAR-10 with relatively simple architectures, leaving open whether the theoretical advantages persist at scale (deeper networks, ImageNet-scale data, thousands of clients). A notable open question is whether the constant factors hidden in the O(·) bounds are tight enough for practical efficiency, and whether the gradient-normalization trick interacts well with modern deep architectures (batch norm, residual connections).
