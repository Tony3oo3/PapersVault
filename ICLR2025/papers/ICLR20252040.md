## Problem

Existing LiDAR novel view synthesis methods rely on NeRF-based representations that are slow to train and render, and are designed for symmetrical scenes rather than the elongated, dynamic geometry of driving environments. Additionally, LiDAR data is inherently sparse and suffers from ray dropout, making 3D scene reconstruction from LiDAR alone particularly challenging.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse LiDAR point clouds from multiple timesteps (51 consecutive frames per scene, with 4 held out at 10-frame intervals for evaluation) along with LiDAR sensor poses. It initializes 1×10⁶ Gaussian primitives from randomly sampled LiDAR points and produces novel-view LiDAR point clouds as panoramic range maps with 360° horizontal FOV and sensor-specific vertical FOV (26.4° for 64-beam KITTI-360, 40° for 32-beam nuScenes), accompanied by per-pixel intensity and ray-drop probability maps.

## Method

GS-LiDAR represents the scene with 2D Gaussian primitives that carry periodic vibration properties: each Gaussian's center oscillates sinusoidally along a learned direction and its opacity decays over a learned lifetime, enabling a unified representation of both static surfaces and moving objects. The key rendering innovation is a panoramic Gaussian splatting pipeline that computes explicit ray-splat intersections by solving a 2×2 linear system in the LiDAR coordinate system, yielding geometrically accurate depth and alpha values for each pixel. Each Gaussian additionally stores view-dependent intensity and ray-drop probability as spherical harmonic coefficients, which are aggregated via alpha-blending into panoramic maps supervised by ground-truth range, intensity, and dropout masks. A U-Net with residual connections post-refines the ray-drop mask using depth and intensity as auxiliary inputs. Training combines depth (mean + median), intensity L1, ray-drop BCE, depth distortion, normal consistency, and chamfer distance losses over 30,000 iterations, achieving 11 FPS rendering speed.

## Results

On KITTI-360 dynamic scenes, GS-LiDAR achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.1085 and depth RMSE of 3.1212, outperforming the prior SOTA LiDAR4D (CD 0.1089, RMSE 3.5256) by 11.4% in depth RMSE. On nuScenes, GS-LiDAR reduces depth RMSE to 5.8925 versus LiDAR4D's 6.7831 (13.1% improvement) and achieves the best F-score of 0.9055. On static KITTI-360, the method attains depth RMSE of 2.8895 (10.7% lower than LiDAR4D) and intensity PSNR of 18.35 dB. Training takes ~1.5 hours on a single RTX A6000 with rendering at 11 FPS, yielding a 1.67× training speedup and 31× rendering speedup over LiDAR4D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-LiDAR outperforms the closest competitor LiDAR4D on depth RMSE across all three benchmarks (10.7% on static KITTI-360, 11.4% on dynamic KITTI-360, 13.1% on nuScenes) and on point-cloud Chamfer Distance for dynamic scenes (0.1085 vs 0.1089 on KITTI-360; 0.2382 vs 0.2443 on nuScenes). Against NeRF-based baselines (LiDAR-NeRF, D-NeRF, K-Planes, TiNeuVox-B), GS-LiDAR achieves substantially lower depth RMSE (3.12 vs 4.02–4.18 on KITTI-360 dynamic) and higher depth PSNR (28.44 vs 25.99–26.23). On intensity metrics, GS-LiDAR leads in RMSE and PSNR on KITTI-360 but is slightly behind LiDAR4D on nuScenes intensity LPIPS (0.0627 vs 0.0459) and PSNR (27.74 vs 27.80). The method also surpasses traditional simulators (LiDARsim, PCGen) and the per-scene reconstruction method NKSR by large margins on all metrics. Notably, GS-LiDAR achieves these gains while training 1.67× faster and rendering 31× faster than LiDAR4D, addressing the key efficiency bottleneck of NeRF-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

GS-LiDAR departs from the dominant NeRF-based paradigm for LiDAR simulation (LiDAR-NeRF, LiDAR4D, NeRF-LiDAR) by adopting an explicit Gaussian splatting representation, specifically 2D Gaussian primitives with periodic vibration properties borrowed from dynamic scene reconstruction work. The panoramic rendering pipeline with explicit ray-splat intersection is a novel technical contribution that addresses the incompatibility between standard 3DGS projection (designed for pinhole cameras) and the cylindrical geometry of LiDAR range maps. The U-Net-based ray-drop refinement is adapted from NeRF-LiDAR, and the overall framework composes 2DGS geometric losses with LiDAR-specific supervision (range, intensity, ray-drop) in a unified training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 31× rendering speedup over LiDAR4D is practically significant for autonomous driving pipelines where rapid LiDAR simulation is needed for training and validation at scale. However, the method remains per-scene optimized (30k iterations, ~1.5 hours per scene), limiting its applicability to large-scale or generalizable simulation. Open questions include whether the approach can be extended to multi-scene or generalizable settings, whether incorporating camera data alongside LiDAR would improve reconstruction quality, and how the method scales to higher-resolution LiDAR sensors (e.g., 128+ beams). The reliance on a post-hoc U-Net for ray-drop refinement also introduces an additional optimization stage that could be a bottleneck in production settings.
