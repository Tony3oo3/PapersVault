## Problem

Urban time series models (UTSMs) systematically underperform on low-frequency events such as holidays and extreme weather due to their bias toward majority patterns and the scarcity of training samples for rare occurrences. While prior work has attempted to address this at the network level (memory retrieval, mixture-of-experts routing), the neuron-level mechanisms through which UTSMs encode and retrieve low-frequency patterns remain entirely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a look-back window of L=12 past time steps plus auxiliary temporal features (day of week, holiday indicator) and outputs H=12 future predictions, evaluated separately on holiday, non-holiday, and overall splits. Unusually, the training procedure operates at the individual-neuron granularity within a transformer-based UTSM (STAEformer), targeting specific neurons across both linear layers and self-attention Q/K/V projections rather than adjusting the full parameter set. Only ~7–10% of neurons are identified and fine-tuned, with the remaining parameters frozen.

## Method

PN-Train introduces a three-stage pipeline: (1) a Pattern Neuron Detector (PND) that computes per-neuron attribution scores as the L1 norm of activation values on low-frequency samples, then selects pattern neurons as the intersection of top-ε ranked neurons across all B detection samples; (2) a Pattern Neuron Verifier (PNV) that confirms their importance by showing that deactivating detected neurons causes far larger prediction degradation than deactivating an equal number of random neurons; and (3) a Pattern Neuron Optimizer (PNO) that fine-tunes only the detected pattern neurons on a small set (R=10) of low-frequency samples using MAE loss while freezing all other parameters. The key insight is that perturbation-based detection at the individual linear-layer level (finer-grained than attention-score-only methods) identifies a small, consistent set of neurons responsible for low-frequency patterns, and targeted fine-tuning of these neurons improves holiday forecasting without degrading high-frequency performance.

## Results

On Metro-Traffic, PN-Train achieves an overall MAE of 214.29 and WMAPE of 6.40%, outperforming its base UTSM STAEformer (221.37 MAE, 6.62% WMAPE) and the strongest competing method TESTAM (342.19 MAE, 10.22% WMAPE). On Pedestrian, PN-Train reaches an overall MAE of 79.85 and WMAPE of 21.95%, again surpassing STAEformer (84.02 MAE, 23.10% WMAPE) and TESTAM (94.57 MAE, 26.00% WMAPE). For the critical low-frequency holiday subset, PN-Train reduces MAE by 2.9% over STAEformer on Metro-Traffic (430.40 vs 443.23) and by 7.9% on Pedestrian (106.11 vs 115.24), while simultaneously improving non-holiday accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PN-Train (built on STAEformer) outperforms all eight deep learning baselines on overall MAE for both Metro-Traffic (214.29 vs. next-best STID at 233.54) and Pedestrian (79.85 vs. next-best STID at 87.00). Against the strongest holiday-focused competitor TESTAM, PN-Train wins decisively on Metro-Traffic (430.40 vs. 486.89 holiday MAE; 214.29 vs. 342.19 overall MAE) but narrowly loses on Pedestrian holiday MAE (106.11 vs. 103.79), though it still dominates on Pedestrian overall (79.85 vs. 94.57) and non-holiday (78.35 vs. 94.04). Graph-based models (STGCN, GWNet, AGCRN) and memory-based PM-MemNet are consistently outperformed by 20–50% in overall MAE on both datasets. The base STAEformer without PN-Train fine-tuning is also beaten, confirming that the neuron-level optimization contributes a consistent 3–5% MAE improvement over standard training. Notably, PN-Train improves both holiday and non-holiday accuracy simultaneously, whereas TESTAM's mixture-of-experts routing sacrifices overall performance to gain a slight holiday edge on Pedestrian.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two research threads: (1) urban time series forecasting models that handle low-frequency events via architectural mechanisms (memory banks in PM-MemNet, mixture-of-experts routing in TESTAM, dynamic positional embeddings in STID/STAEformer), and (2) neuron-level interpretability in large language models, particularly the 'knowledge neuron' literature (Dai et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2024) that identifies individual neurons responsible for specific knowledge. The key departure is transplanting the perturbation-based knowledge-neuron detection paradigm from LLMs into spatiotemporal transformers for the first time, shifting the intervention point from architecture-level pattern retrieval to neuron-level fine-tuning. Unlike prior UTSM approaches that add external modules (memory, experts) to capture rare patterns, PN-Train operates on the existing model's internal representations without structural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evidence that a small, identifiable subset of neurons (~8–10%) in a transformer-based UTSM is disproportionately responsible for low-frequency pattern forecasting, and that targeted fine-tuning of these neurons with as few as 10 samples yields measurable gains without degrading high-frequency performance. This opens a neuron-level interpretability and intervention pathway for time series models that was previously unexplored, potentially enabling more efficient and targeted model adaptation. Limitations include evaluation on a single UTSM architecture (STAEformer), a small number of datasets, and low-frequency events limited to holidays, parades, and extreme weather; the theoretical grounding for why pattern neurons concentrate in query/key components remains unaddressed, and whether the approach transfers to fundamentally different architectures (e.g., state-space models, graph neural networks) is an open question.
