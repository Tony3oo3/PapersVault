## Problem

Dataset distillation for self-supervised learning (SSL) pre-training has remained an open problem: existing supervised DD methods rely on labels or suffer from representation collapse without them, and the one prior SSL distillation method (KRR-ST) fails to outperform random real subsets. The paper identifies the root cause as the high variance of SSL gradients over mini-batches, which makes trajectory matching infeasible, and provides both a theoretical proof (Theorem 4.1) and empirical evidence for this failure mode.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled RGB image dataset (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, TinyImageNet at 64×64) and a pre-trained teacher encoder (ResNet-18 with BarlowTwins or SimCLR) whose 512-dimensional pre-projection-head representations of all real examples serve as fixed reference targets. It produces a synthetic dataset of 2% or 5% the original size (e.g., 100 images for CIFAR-10) along with a learned synthetic learning rate; the synthetic images are initialized from high-loss real examples and are used to pre-train small ConvNets (3-layer for CIFAR, 4-layer for TinyImageNet) via MSE to the corresponding teacher representations, after which a linear probe is trained on downstream labeled data.

## Method

MKDT (Matching Knowledge Distillation Trajectories) converts the high-variance SSL trajectory-matching problem into a lower-variance supervised one via knowledge distillation. First, a large teacher encoder is trained with SSL (e.g., BarlowTwins) on the full real dataset. Second, K=100 small student ConvNets are trained to minimize MSE to the frozen teacher representations, producing expert trajectories whose gradients have far lower variance than SSL gradients (proven in Theorem 4.1). Third, synthetic images are optimized by matching the student's trajectory on the synthetic data to the expert trajectory on real data using the standard MTT loss (Eq. 4), where the student is updated via the low-variance MSE-to-teacher-representations objective rather than the SSL loss. The synthetic set is initialized from examples with highest average MSE loss across expert trajectories, and a learnable synthetic learning rate is co-optimized to further stabilize matching.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (2% distilled set, 1% downstream labels), MKDT achieves 44.36% linear-probe accuracy, outperforming KRR-ST (37.19%), SAS (36.02%), and random subsets (36.35%), while full-data pre-training reaches 58.21%. On CIFAR-100 (2%, 1% labels), MKDT reaches 12.36% vs. KRR-ST's 8.31% and random's 9.20%. With a 5% distilled set (Table 4), MKDT achieves up to 13% higher accuracy than KRR-ST (e.g., 50.23% vs. 36.69% on CIFAR-10 with 1% labels). MKDT also generalizes across SSL algorithms (BarlowTwins, SimCLR) and to larger architectures (ResNet-10/18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MKDT consistently outperforms KRR-ST (Lee et al., 2023) across all datasets, distillation sizes, and labeled-data fractions. On CIFAR-10 (2% distilled, 1% labels), MKDT achieves 44.36% vs. KRR-ST's 37.19% (+7.2 pp); with a 5% distilled set the gap widens to 50.23% vs. 36.69% (+13.5 pp). On CIFAR-100 (2%, 1% labels), MKDT reaches 12.36% vs. KRR-ST's 8.31% (+4.1 pp), and on TinyImageNet (2%, 1% labels) 3.87% vs. 3.32% (+0.6 pp). Notably, KRR-ST consistently fails to outperform SSL pre-training on random real subsets across all settings, whereas MKDT always exceeds random subsets. Against SAS (Joshi & Mirzasoleiman, 2023), MKDT also wins: on CIFAR-10 (2%, 1% labels) MKDT scores 44.36% vs. SAS's 36.02% (+8.3 pp), and on CIFAR-100 (2%, 1% labels) 12.36% vs. 9.39% (+3.0 pp). The advantage of MKDT over both baselines grows with more downstream labeled data (10%, 50%) and larger distilled set sizes (5%), confirming that the distilled synthetic data captures richer representation structure than either kernel-based meta-model matching or loss-based subset selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Matching Training Trajectories (MTT) framework of Cazenavette et al. (2022) to the self-supervised learning setting, where prior DD methods (gradient matching, distribution matching) fail due to their reliance on labels. The key conceptual departure is the use of a knowledge distillation objective (MSE to a teacher's representations, following Yu et al., 2019) to convert high-variance SSL training trajectories into lower-variance supervised-like trajectories, making trajectory matching feasible. The paper also positions itself against KRR-ST (Lee et al., 2023), the only prior SSL DD attempt, which used kernel-based meta-model matching and which the authors empirically show does not outperform random real subsets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first effective dataset distillation method for SSL pre-training, enabling highly data-efficient pre-training with up to 13% downstream accuracy gains over the best baseline in low-label regimes. The theoretical proof that SSL gradients inherently have higher variance than SL gradients provides a principled explanation for why naive trajectory matching fails and why KD is the right fix. Limitations include evaluation only up to TinyImageNet scale (64×64, 200K images), reliance on a pre-trained teacher on the full dataset, and distillation with small ConvNets (though transfer to ResNet-18 is demonstrated). Open questions include scaling to ImageNet-1K, applicability to non-contrastive SSL paradigms (e.g., masked autoencoders), and whether the teacher-student size gap can be reduced.
