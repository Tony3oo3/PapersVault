## Problem

Despite a variety of deep learning architectures for subsurface imaging, fundamental questions remain unanswered: how latent space size affects translation quality, whether complex architectures are needed for latent translations, the role of manifold learning, and whether jointly solving forward and inverse problems is beneficial. The paper addresses this by proposing a unified Generalized Forward-Inverse (GFI) framework that systematically characterizes prior work and enables principled exploration of these design choices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250604/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the inverse problem, the method takes seismic waveform images of shape (5×1000×70) — representing 5 sources, 1000 time samples, and 70 receivers — and produces 2D velocity maps of shape (1×70×70) in depth-horizontal space. For the forward problem, the roles are reversed: velocity maps are input and waveform images are output. Both domains are projected into latent spaces of equal dimension (constrained to 70×70 by default), which is a key architectural requirement enabling the translation models to operate between them.

## Method

The GFI framework reformulates forward and inverse problems as latent space translations between encoder-decoder manifolds, with two key assumptions: (1) velocity and waveforms can be projected to latent manifolds via auto-encoders, and (2) bidirectional translations exist between these manifolds. Two new architectures are proposed: Latent U-Net, which uses two separate U-Nets (one per direction) for latent translations trained end-to-end, and Invertible X-Net, which uses a single Invertible U-Net (IU-Net) to simultaneously learn both forward and inverse translations, guaranteeing bijectivity between latent representations. The Invertible X-Net is trained with a combined loss on both velocity and waveform predictions, and optionally with cycle-consistency loss for unpaired training. A key finding is that joint training via Invertible X-Net improves forward problem accuracy over disjoint training, as gradients from the inverse problem regularize and enhance forward optimization, achieving state-of-the-art results with fewer parameters (26M vs. 70M for two separate Latent U-Nets).

## Results

On the inverse problem (waveform→velocity) across 10 OpenFWI datasets, Latent U-Net (Large) achieves the best average rank of 1.3 on all three metrics (MAE, MSE, SSIM), with SSIM reaching 0.9967 on FVA and 0.9298 on STA. On the forward problem (velocity→waveform), Invertible X-Net achieves the best average rank of 1.1 (SSIM) and 1.4 (MAE), outperforming Latent U-Net (Large) despite having fewer parameters (26M vs. 35M). On zero-shot Marmousi evaluation, Invertible X-Net achieves SSIM 0.504 versus 0.46 for InversionNet and 0.438 for Auto-Linear, demonstrating superior out-of-distribution generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Latent U-Net (Large) consistently outperforms InversionNet and Auto-Linear on the inverse problem across nearly all 10 OpenFWI datasets, achieving SSIM 0.9967 vs. 0.9894 (InversionNet) and 0.9887 (Auto-Linear) on FVA, and 0.9298 vs. 0.8858 and 0.8422 on the complex STA dataset. On the forward problem, Invertible X-Net dominates all baselines with average SSIM rank 1.1, beating FNO (rank 3.1) on complex datasets like CVB (0.9221 vs. 0.8354) and CFB (0.9156 vs. 0.8160), though FNO retains an edge on the simplest datasets (FVA: 0.9967 vs. 0.9887; FFA: 0.9667 vs. 0.9744). Auto-Linear is the weakest baseline on both problems, with average SSIM ranks of 3.8 (inverse) and 3.5 (forward), particularly struggling on heterogeneous velocity maps where its linear latent-space translation cannot capture non-linear relationships. VelocityGAN, sharing the same architecture as InversionNet, shows comparable (slightly worse) performance and is beaten by both proposed methods. Notably, Invertible X-Net with only 26M parameters outperforms Latent U-Net (Large) with 35M parameters on the forward problem, demonstrating that joint forward-inverse training provides a strong regularization effect that compensates for lower model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the Generalized Forward-Inverse (GFI) framework, which unifies prior DL4SI methods (InversionNet, VelocityGAN, Auto-Linear, FNO) as special cases under two assumptions: manifold learning in latent spaces and latent space translations. It builds directly on U-Net architectures for the translation component (Latent U-Net) and on Invertible U-Nets (IU-Net, Etmann et al. 2020) for the Invertible X-Net, which leverages the bijective property of Invertible Neural Networks (Ardizzone et al. 2018) to simultaneously learn forward and inverse mappings. The key departure from prior work is the systematic treatment of latent space size, translation complexity, and joint vs. disjoint training as design axes within a single framework, rather than as ad-hoc architectural choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GFI framework provides a principled vocabulary for comparing and extending DL4SI methods, and the ablations answer practical questions (latent dimension sizing, skip connections, manifold learning strategy) that the community has lacked guidance on. The finding that joint training via Invertible X-Net improves forward problem accuracy despite using fewer parameters than separate Latent U-Net models, and that 'directly translate' consistently outperforms 'reconstruct then translate,' offers actionable design principles. Limitations include that zero-shot performance on real-world-like Marmousi/Overthrust data remains modest (SSIM ~0.5), and the framework is validated only on 2D acoustic wavefields, leaving 3D and elastic extensions open.
