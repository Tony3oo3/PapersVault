## Problem

Existing certified graph unlearning methods (CGU, CEU) require full re-computation of graph propagation for every removal request, making them impractical on billion-edge graphs where propagation alone takes hours. Scalable approximate propagation techniques exist for GNNs but introduce approximation errors into node embeddings, and it remains unclear whether these errors can be absorbed while preserving the bounded gradient residual norm that certified unlearning requires. The paper asks how to integrate scalable propagation into certified graph unlearning without breaking the privacy guarantee.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained linear GNN model (SGC or GPR-based) with its parameters, the graph adjacency matrix, node feature matrix, and a removal request specifying a node feature, edge, or node to delete. It outputs updated model parameters via a single Newton step and refreshed approximate node embeddings computed by a lazy local propagation framework. A key structural detail is that the propagation uses symmetric normalization (D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}) with Generalized PageRank weights w_ℓ, differing from CGU's asymmetric P = D^{-1}A, and the approximation threshold r_max controls the L2 error bound √n·L·r_max on each embedding column.

## Method

ScaleGUN introduces a lazy local propagation framework that extends InstantGNN's dynamic Personalized PageRank to the Generalized PageRank scheme, enabling efficient embedding updates for layered propagation (e.g., 2-hop SGC) on evolving graphs. Upon a removal request, only the residue vectors of affected nodes are locally adjusted to restore an invariant property, then Forward Push is invoked to re-establish the error bound—yielding O(L²d) amortized cost per edge removal. The core theoretical contribution is proving that the approximation error ε₁ = √n·L·r_max only additively inflates the gradient residual norm bound, so the total model error remains bounded and maskable by the noise perturbation b ~ N(0, cε'/ε)^F, preserving (ε,δ)-certified guarantees for all three unlearning scenarios. The proof leverages the propagation framework's residue structure to bound ‖Ze_j − Z'e_j‖ directly, avoiding the intractable direct analysis of embedding differences under symmetric normalization. For sequential unlearning, only the unlearning component of the data-dependent bound accumulates across requests (the approximation component resets each step), so retraining is triggered only when the accumulated unlearning error exhausts the privacy budget.

## Results

On ogbn-papers100M (111M nodes, 1.6B edges), ScaleGUN achieves 59.82% test accuracy (vs. 59.99% retrain) with a total unlearning cost of 54.79 s per 2,000-edge batch, compared to 6,764 s for retraining — a 123× speedup. The propagation cost drops from 6,703 s (retrain) to 9.02 s (ScaleGUN), a 743× reduction. On ogbn-products, ScaleGUN matches retrain accuracy (56.04–56.24%) while reducing total cost from 101.90 s to 11.69 s (8.7×) and propagation cost from 98.48 s to 10.81 s (9.1×). The headline result: (ε,δ)=(1,10⁻⁴) certified unlearning of 5,000 random edges on ogbn-papers100M completes in 20 s total (5 s for embedding updates) versus 1.91 h for retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScaleGUN is compared against CGU (Chien et al., 2022a) and CEU (Wu et al., 2023b), the two existing certified graph unlearning methods, as well as full retraining. On accuracy, all methods are essentially tied: ScaleGUN matches or is within 0.1–0.2% of retrain, CGU, and CEU across all datasets (e.g., 57.84% on ogbn-arxiv, 56.04–56.24% on ogbn-products). The decisive advantage is in propagation cost, which dominates the total unlearning time: on ogbn-arxiv, ScaleGUN's propagation cost is 0.85 s vs. 2.78 s (CGU) and 2.91 s (CEU); on ogbn-products, 10.81 s vs. 86.81 s (CGU) and 87.95 s (CEU). On ogbn-papers100M, CGU and CEU are infeasible (not reported), while ScaleGUN completes in 54.79 s total versus 6,764 s for retraining. The gap widens with graph size: the propagation speedup over CGU grows from ~3× on ogbn-arxiv to ~8× on ogbn-products, and is unbounded on papers100M where CGU/CEU cannot run at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScaleGUN sits at the intersection of two research threads: certified graph unlearning (CGU, CEU) and scalable approximate graph propagation (Forward Push, InstantGNN). Prior certified unlearning methods (Chien et al., 2022a; Wu et al., 2023b) require exact re-propagation of node embeddings for every removal request, making them infeasible on billion-edge graphs. The key conceptual departure is integrating approximate propagation into the certified unlearning framework, which was previously thought incompatible because approximation error could violate the bounded model-error requirement. The paper also extends InstantGNN's dynamic PPR framework to Generalized PageRank, enabling the layered propagation schemes (SGC, GDC) used in certified unlearning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to make certified graph unlearning practical on billion-edge graphs, achieving a ~1000× reduction in propagation cost on ogbn-papers100M. The theoretical result that Forward Push approximation error adds only a small, controllable term to the gradient residual bound resolves the central obstacle to combining scalable propagation with certified guarantees. However, the certified guarantee is limited to linear models with strongly convex loss; extending to deep GNNs remains open, as the Newton update framework underpinning the certification does not generalize to non-convex objectives. The practical impact is significant for privacy-sensitive graph applications (social networks, financial networks) where retraining is infeasible.
