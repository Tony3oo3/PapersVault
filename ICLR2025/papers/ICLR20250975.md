## Problem

Motif-based molecular generators rely on a fixed top-k vocabulary of frequently occurring substructures, which fundamentally limits their structural expressivity: they cannot represent or reconstruct substructures absent from that vocabulary. The paper demonstrates that state-of-the-art models fail to decode key structures of FDA-approved drugs, raising questions about their utility for discovering genuinely novel compounds.

## Input / Output

During generation, MAGNet takes a 100-dimensional latent code z sampled from a standard normal prior (optionally conditioned on one or more scaffolds or a full typed fragment) and produces a complete molecular graph with atom and bond types. The output is structured hierarchically: first a scaffold multiset S (drawn from a fixed ~350-scaffold vocabulary) and a typed connectivity matrix A, then typed motifs M (atom and bond assignments freely predicted per scaffold), join node positions J, and leaf nodes L. Unlike motif-based competitors that select from a large fixed token set, MAGNet's scaffold vocabulary is small and untyped, while the atom/bond features are predicted continuously, enabling generation of motifs never seen in training.

## Method

MAGNet introduces a novel factorisation P(G) = P(L|M,J)·P(J|M,A)·P(M|G_S)·P(G_S) that decouples molecular structure (scaffolds + connectivity) from features (atom/bond types), enabling a small, structurally expressive scaffold vocabulary with freely learned feature assignments. The architecture is a VAE: a graph transformer encoder produces the latent code, and a hierarchical decoder first autoregressively generates the scaffold multiset via a transformer decoder, predicts the typed connectivity A with an MLP, then assigns atom types per scaffold via another transformer decoder, bond types via an MLP, join node positions via an MLP, and leaf nodes via a final transformer decoder. A key design choice is the fragmentation scheme that removes leaf nodes, collapses acyclic structures into junctions, and strips all atom/bond types to yield concise untyped scaffolds, reducing combinatorial complexity compared to motif vocabularies. To address latent-space over-pruning, a post-hoc normalising flow trained with Conditional Flow Matching (minibatch optimal transport) aligns the VAE posterior with the prior, achieving 100% active latent units.

## Results

On the GuacaMol distribution learning benchmark, MAGNet achieves an FCD of 0.76 and KL of 0.95, outperforming all graph-based baselines except MoLeR (FCD 0.80, KL 0.98). On MOSES, MAGNet attains the best synthetic accessibility score (SA 0.12) among graph-based methods and a QED of 0.01. Critically, MAGNet substantially outperforms all baselines on scaffold reconstruction (Fig. 3b) and matches the reference scaffold distribution most accurately (Fig. 3c), demonstrating superior structural expressivity. On the GuacaMol goal-directed benchmark, MAGNet achieves the best scores on Isomers (0.34), MPO (0.74), and Similarity (0.43), outperforming MoLeR and MiCaM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGNet trails MoLeR on the two primary distribution metrics (FCD 0.76 vs 0.80; KL 0.95 vs 0.98) but outperforms every other graph-based baseline on both. Against PS-VAE, DiGress, JT-VAE, MiCaM, and HierVAE, MAGNet leads on FCD by 0.11–0.48 points and on KL by 0.01–0.12 points. On MOSES, MAGNet achieves the best SA (0.12) among graph-based methods, beating MoLeR (0.06 is actually better for MoLeR—lower is better, so MoLeR wins SA), but MAGNet matches MoLeR on QED (0.01) and beats PS-VAE (SA 1.18) and DiGress (SA 0.09) substantially. On the GuacaMol goal-directed benchmark, MAGNet wins on Isomers (0.34 vs 0.32 MoLeR, 0.31 MiCaM), MPO (0.74 vs 0.70, 0.71), and Similarity (0.43 vs 0.38, 0.38), while losing on Rediscovery (0.17 vs 0.24 for both MoLeR and MiCaM). The most distinctive advantage is structural: MAGNet reconstructs uncommon scaffolds (large rings, complex junctions) far more reliably than any baseline, and its sampled scaffold distribution matches the training distribution most closely, whereas PS-VAE heavily oversamples ring-like scaffolds and MoLeR/MiCaM oversample chain-like ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGNet departs from the dominant motif-based paradigm (JT-VAE, MoLeR, PS-VAE, MiCaM, HierVAE) that decomposes molecules into a fixed vocabulary of typed substructures, arguing that such vocabularies fundamentally limit structural expressivity. Instead, it introduces a scaffold abstraction—stripping atom and bond types from motifs to retain only topology—and then freely learns feature assignments, positioning itself between single-atom and fragment-based generators. The architecture builds on the VAE framework (Kingma & Welling) with a graph transformer encoder, borrows the leaf-node removal fragmentation from HierVAE/MoLeR, and applies Conditional Flow Matching (Tong et al., 2024) post-hoc to remedy latent-space over-pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a concrete failure mode of state-of-the-art molecular generators: they cannot reliably reconstruct or sample uncommon scaffolds (large rings, complex junctions) absent from their top-k motif vocabulary, even for FDA-approved drugs. It also demonstrates that the widely-used FCD metric can be high (0.89) on a subset containing only the 10 most common scaffolds, undermining its utility as a proxy for structural diversity. Open questions include incorporating synthesizability constraints (e.g., via multi-component reaction datasets) and developing more flexible fragmentation that generalizes better across datasets, as ~11% of QM9 scaffolds are unseen from ZINC training.
