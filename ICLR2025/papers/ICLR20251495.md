## Problem

Lensless cameras offer a compact alternative to bulky lens systems for accessing tight areas, but their measurements are encoded patterns lacking visual semantics, making concealed object detection (COD) fundamentally harder than on standard images. No prior work has performed COD directly on lensless imaging measurements, leaving a gap between the miniaturization potential of lensless optics and high-level visual tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251495/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw lensless imaging measurements (encoded sensor patterns from a PHlatCam phase-mask camera, not recognizable images) as input and produces pixel-level binary prediction maps indicating concealed object regions. The input is a 3-channel measurement Y generated by the convolution model Y = A * X + ξ, where A is the camera's point-spread function. The authors also introduce two benchmark datasets: SLCOD (simulated measurements from four COD datasets) and DLCOD (real PHlatCam captures with annotated label maps), split into Test-Easy (220 pairs) and Test-Hard (320 pairs).

## Method

RGANet combines an optical-aware feature extraction (OFE) module using learnable Wiener filtering to extract task-relevant semantics from raw measurements, two region gaze modules (RGMs) that perform spatial-frequency collaborative learning, a region amplifier (RA), and a hierarchical feature decoding (HFD) module. Each RGM pairs a PVTv2-based spatial encoder with a DCT-based frequency encoder that uses an adaptive learnable threshold to separate low- and high-frequency components, then fuses them via correlation-matrix alignment. The RA, placed between the two RGMs, uses the first RGM's prediction as an attention map to perform trilinear sampling that locally magnifies object regions while compressing background, simulating human zoom-in behavior. The network is trained end-to-end with a combined weighted BCE and weighted IoU loss applied to all three prediction stages (both RGMs and the final HFD output), with the PVTv2 backbone initialized from ImageNet pre-training.

## Results

On the Test-Easy dataset, RGANet achieves Fβ^w = 0.815, M = 0.079, Eξ = 0.896, and Sα = 0.834, outperforming the strongest baseline LOINet (Fβ^w = 0.762, M = 0.103) by 7.0% in Fβ^w and 23.3% in M. On the more challenging Test-Hard dataset, RGANet reaches Fβ^w = 0.705, M = 0.098, Eξ = 0.845, and Sα = 0.770, improving over LOINet by 13.0% in Fβ^w and 19.7% in M. The method uses 48.62 GFLOPs and 39.45M parameters, placing it at an intermediate computational cost among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGANet outperforms all eight baselines across every metric on both Test-Easy and Test-Hard. The closest competitor is LOINet (Yin et al., 2022), a lensless-specific method using only 6.42 GFLOPs; RGANet surpasses it by 7.0% in Fβ^w on Test-Easy (0.815 vs. 0.762) and by 13.0% on Test-Hard (0.705 vs. 0.624). Among traditional COD methods retrained on lensless measurements, ZoomNet (Pang et al., 2022) is the strongest, achieving Fβ^w = 0.714/0.619, still trailing RGANet by 10.1/13.0 points. Lensless inference baselines EyeCoD and LLI_T show the weakest performance, with Fβ^w dropping to 0.563 and 0.527 respectively on Test-Hard. Two additional recent COD methods (FEDER and FSPNet, both CVPR 2023) also fall well behind, with Fβ^w of 0.741/0.539 and 0.757/0.584 on Easy/Hard, confirming that spatial-frequency collaborative learning and local amplification provide a clear advantage over feature-decomposition or transformer-pyramid approaches in the lensless setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: lensless imaging (building on PHlatCam's phase-mask convolution model) and concealed object detection (COD) on natural images. It departs from prior lensless work (FlatNet, LOINet, LLI_T) that focused on reconstruction or simple recognition by instead performing high-level semantic segmentation directly from encoded measurements, using a Wiener-filtering-inspired OFE module repurposed for task-specific feature learning rather than visual reconstruction. The Region Amplifier adapts trilinear attention sampling (Zheng et al., 2019) from fine-grained recognition to local magnification of object regions, while the spatial-frequency collaborative learning in the RGM draws on frequency-domain COD methods (Lin et al., 2023; Rao et al., 2022) but introduces an adaptive DCT-based frequency separation with learnable thresholds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that COD—a task requiring fine-grained semantic reasoning—can be performed directly from lensless imaging measurements without full image reconstruction, opening a path toward compact, flexible imaging for medical (in vivo lesion detection) and security applications where bulky lens systems cannot reach. The contributed SLCOD and DLCOD datasets establish the first benchmark for this problem. However, the method still struggles with low-contrast scenes, extremely cluttered backgrounds, and very small objects, and the detection-after-reconstruction comparison shows up to 10% performance gains at 3–4× the computational cost, suggesting the direct approach has not yet fully closed the gap. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other lensless prototypes (DiffuserCam, FZA) and whether the OFE module's strong dependence on the specific PHlatCam PSF limits transferability.
