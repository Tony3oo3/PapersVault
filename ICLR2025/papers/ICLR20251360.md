## Problem

Existing LLM reasoning approaches—whether instruction tuning or prompt engineering—apply static, predefined reasoning strategies uniformly to all questions, ignoring both the specific characteristics of each question and the inherent capability of the task-solving LLM. Different question types benefit from different reasoning strategies (e.g., code vs. natural language), and the same strategy's effectiveness varies across LLMs, yet no prior method dynamically selects the optimal reasoning trajectory per question and per solver.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reasoning question as a text query and produces a structured output consisting of: (1) a trajectory explanation justifying the action selection, (2) a reasoning action trajectory specifying which atomic action to use at each of three layers (analysis: rewriting/decomposition/empty; solution: CoT/PoT; verification: self-verification/empty), (3) the reasoning process, and (4) the final answer. In the external planner setup, the planner LLM outputs only the explanation and trajectory while a separate frozen solver LLM generates the reasoning and answer; in the internalized setup, a single LLM produces all four components in one forward pass.

## Method

DOTS defines atomic reasoning action modules across three layers (analysis, solution, verification) and composes them into candidate trajectories. It then searches for the optimal trajectory per training question by iteratively executing all candidate trajectories against the specific solver LLM, evaluating success rate over multiple runs with non-zero temperature, sorting by accumulated success rate and trajectory length (to favor shorter, more efficient paths), and pruning to the top candidates each iteration. The collected optimal trajectories—paired with GPT-4o-generated explanations of why each is optimal—are used to fine-tune a planner via supervised cross-entropy loss. Two paradigms are supported: external planner tuning (a smaller LLM is fine-tuned to predict trajectories for a frozen, potentially closed-source solver) and internalized planner tuning (the solver LLM itself is fine-tuned to jointly plan and solve, internalizing the planning capability). The key differentiator is that the optimal trajectory is tailored to both the question's characteristics and the solver's specific strengths, rather than being a fixed strategy applied uniformly.

## Results

On in-distribution and few-shot tasks (MATH, BBH, Game of 24, TheoremQA), DOTS with an external planner (Llama-3-8B) and GPT-4o-mini as solver achieves 66.5% average accuracy, outperforming the best static baseline Self-Refine (63.0%) by 3.5 points. With Llama-3-70B-Instruct as solver, DOTS reaches 58.5% average vs. Self-Refine's 56.6%. On out-of-distribution tasks (DeepMind Math, MMLU-pro, StrategyQA, DROP), DOTS with GPT-4o-mini achieves 75.4% average accuracy, surpassing the best baseline PoT (73.7%). The internalized planner variant (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) achieves 35.5% average on in-distribution/few-shot tasks, outperforming Vanilla SFT (33.6%) and all prompt-engineering baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOTS consistently outperforms all five baselines (CoT, PoT, LTM, Self-Refine, PromptAgent) on average across both solver LLMs. Against CoT, DOTS gains +12.3 points (GPT-4o-mini) and +14.0 points (Llama-3-70B) in average accuracy, with the largest gap on Game of 24 (65.2% vs. 27.7%). Against PoT, DOTS wins on average (+6.9 and +4.6 points) but PoT remains competitive on Game of 24 specifically (61.4% vs. 65.2% for GPT-4o-mini). Against Self-Refine, the closest competitor, DOTS achieves +3.5 and +1.9 points average improvement, with the advantage concentrated on BBH (+9.4 and +5.9) and TheoremQA (+6.8 and +0.4) where diverse reasoning actions are needed. Against PromptAgent, DOTS gains +11.4 and +13.2 points, showing that dynamic per-question trajectory selection substantially outperforms static prompt optimization. On OOD tasks, DOTS maintains the most stable performance across all four datasets, while static methods like CoT and LTM show large swings (e.g., CoT drops from 80.2% on DeepMind Math to 61.7% on MMLU-pro with GPT-4o-mini).

## Relation to Prior Work

DOTS builds on the established repertoire of reasoning prompt strategies (CoT, PoT, Least-to-Most decomposition, Self-Refine verification) by treating them as composable atomic modules rather than monolithic prompting templates. It departs from prior prompt-engineering work (PromptAgent, Self-Discover) that still produces a single static prompt for all instances, and from search-based reasoning methods (ToT, GoT, MCTS) that search over individual reasoning steps, by instead searching over *trajectories of reasoning actions* (which modules to apply and in what order) tailored to both the question and the specific solver LLM. The supervised fine-tuning component connects to instruction-tuning literature, but the key distinction is that training data is constructed via an explicit search-and-evaluation loop rather than by generating a single reasoning path per question.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a learned planner can consistently outperform the best static prompting strategy across diverse reasoning tasks and solver LLMs, and that this advantage persists in out-of-distribution settings where static methods fluctuate. The observation that the planner naturally learns to allocate longer reasoning trajectories to harder problems—without explicit difficulty conditioning—is a useful signal for adaptive-computation research. Limitations include reliance on ground-truth answers during the search phase, a fixed and relatively small action space (three layers, two options each), and evaluation limited to text-based reasoning tasks; whether the approach scales to richer action spaces or multimodal reasoning remains open.
