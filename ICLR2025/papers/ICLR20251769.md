## Problem

Existing generative models for driving scenarios synthesize either LiDAR point clouds or multi-view camera images independently, and naively combining them produces severe cross-modality spatial mismatches that create contradictory supervision signals for downstream tasks. The core difficulty is that point clouds (3D XYZ coordinates) and multi-view images (2D RGB in perspective views) occupy fundamentally different geometrical spaces with no explicit one-to-one local correspondence, especially during the denoising process when neither range values nor pixel depths are known.

## Input / Output

X-DRIVE takes text prompts (controlling weather, lighting, scene attributes) and 3D bounding boxes (controlling object positions, sizes, and semantic categories) as primary conditions, and optionally accepts a ground-truth LiDAR range image or multi-view images for zero-shot cross-modality conditional generation. It outputs a LiDAR point cloud in range-image format (32×1024×2, encoding range and intensity in a panoramic cylindrical projection) and 6 synchronized multi-view camera images (224×400×3 each) describing the same driving scene. The range image uses horizontally circular convolutions to respect the panoramic wrap-around geometry of the ego-vehicle's 360° LiDAR view.

## Method

X-DRIVE employs a dual-branch latent diffusion architecture where a range-image LDM (trained from scratch with circular convolutions) and a multi-view image LDM (initialized from Stable Diffusion V1.5) jointly denoise their respective modalities under a shared noise schedule. The key contribution is a cross-modality epipolar condition module: for camera-to-LiDAR conditioning, R points are sampled along each LiDAR ray and projected into camera views to form epipolar lines, from which image latent features are bilinearly sampled and fed via cross-attention to the corresponding range-image position; the inverse LiDAR-to-camera path samples D points at varying depths from each camera pixel, projects them to the range view, and conditions image synthesis on the aligned range features. Cross-attention (rather than simple averaging) with zero-initialization gates allows the model to adaptively learn local correspondence under spatial ambiguity without requiring explicit depth or range values. Training follows a three-stage pipeline—range-image VAE, range-image LDM, then full multi-modality end-to-end—with the image branch's Stable Diffusion weights frozen in the final stage, and an inter-view split cross-attention module (reducing memory from 11 GB to 3 GB per scene) enforces multi-view consistency.

## Results

On nuScenes, X-DRIVE achieves FID 16.01 and VSC 0.721 for LiDAR-to-camera conditional generation, outperforming MagicDrive (FID 16.20, VSC 0.633). For joint multi-modality generation (X-DRIVE_{L+C}), it reaches FID 17.37, VSC 0.675, MMD 1.2×10⁻⁴, JSD 0.052, and DAS 1.69, surpassing the MagicDrive+RangeLDM combination (DAS 2.32) on cross-modality consistency. For camera-to-LiDAR generation, X-DRIVE_{C→L} achieves MMD 1.0×10⁻⁴ and JSD 0.052, beating RangeLDM (MMD 1.9×10⁻⁴, JSD 0.054). In object-level control, X-DRIVE attains mAP 65.4 and NDS 69.6, slightly exceeding MagicDrive (mAP 65.2, NDS 69.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-DRIVE_{L→C} (LiDAR-to-camera) outperforms MagicDrive on both FID (16.01 vs. 16.20) and VSC (0.721 vs. 0.633), showing that conditioning on real LiDAR improves multi-view image quality and consistency. For point cloud generation, X-DRIVE_{C→L} beats RangeLDM on MMD (1.0×10⁻⁴ vs. 1.9×10⁻⁴) and JSD (0.052 vs. 0.054), while the joint X-DRIVE_{L+C} also surpasses RangeLDM (MMD 1.2×10⁻⁴ vs. 1.9×10⁻⁴). The most distinctive advantage is cross-modality consistency: X-DRIVE_{L+C} achieves DAS 1.69 versus 2.32 for the MagicDrive+RangeLDM combination, a 27% reduction in depth misalignment. On object-level control (Table 2), X-DRIVE marginally beats MagicDrive (mAP 65.4 vs. 65.2, NDS 69.6 vs. 69.2) when evaluated with a pretrained SparseFusion detector. The joint model (X-DRIVE_{L+C}) trades a small FID increase (17.37 vs. MagicDrive's 16.20) for substantially better cross-modality alignment and multi-view consistency (VSC 0.675 vs. 0.633), reflecting the cost of enforcing mutual modality constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-DRIVE extends the latent diffusion model paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022) to a dual-branch architecture that jointly models the distribution of LiDAR range images and multi-view camera images, departing from prior work that treats these as independent single-modality generation problems (LiDARGen, RangeLDM for point clouds; MagicDrive, BEVGen for images). The cross-modality epipolar condition module is the key architectural novelty, borrowing the zero-initialization gate mechanism from ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023) and the circular convolution design from LiDARGen/RangeLDM, but introducing a geometrically-grounded spatial transform based on epipolar geometry to bridge the fundamentally different coordinate systems of range images and perspective views. The inter-view consistency module adapts MagicDrive's cross-attention with a split-halves strategy to reduce memory cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to generate spatially aligned LiDAR point clouds and multi-view camera images simultaneously with guaranteed cross-modality consistency, addressing a critical bottleneck in autonomous driving where collecting calibrated multi-sensor data is expensive and long-tail scenarios (extreme weather, rare objects) are underrepresented. The proposed DAS metric provides a principled way to evaluate cross-modality alignment that was previously undefined. Key limitations include single-frame generation (no temporal consistency), restriction to nuScenes-scale data, and inability to synthesize scenarios absent from the training distribution (e.g., snow). Open questions include whether the epipolar-line mechanism generalizes to other sensor pairs (radar, thermal) and whether temporal extensions can maintain cross-modality consistency across frames.
