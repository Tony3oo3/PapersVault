## Problem

Existing text-conditioned motion generation methods produce short, isolated motions from a single sentence and cannot generate long, continuous sequences driven by streams of text in real time. Moreover, integrating precise spatial constraints (goal locations, 3D scene geometry) with text-driven semantics remains an open challenge, as prior approaches either lack text conditioning or are restricted to offline, isolated short motions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250849/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2-frame seed motion, a sequence of N text prompts (encoded via CLIP), and optional spatial goals (keyframe poses, target locations, or 3D scene SDFs) as input, and produces continuous 3D human motion sequences in SMPL-X parameterization (276D per frame) of arbitrary length. Motion is structured as overlapping primitives of 2 history + 8 future frames, canonicalized in a pelvis-centered local coordinate frame, enabling real-time generation at >300 fps on a single GPU. For spatially constrained tasks, the output additionally satisfies goal-reach or scene-contact/collision constraints.

## Method

DART learns a compact 256D latent motion primitive space via a transformer-based VAE (7 layers, MLD-style) that compresses overlapping short motion clips, then trains a latent diffusion denoiser (10 steps, cosine schedule) to predict clean latents conditioned on CLIP text embeddings, history frames, and diffusion timestep. Autoregressive rollout generates unbounded sequences by feeding the last 2 generated frames as the next primitive's history, with scheduled training (progressively replacing ground-truth history with model-predicted history over 300K iterations) to mitigate distribution drift. For spatial control, the deterministic DDIM sampler maps latent noise vectors Z_T to motions, enabling either gradient-based optimization of Z_T against goal/scene constraints or PPO-trained actor-critic policies that output latent noise as actions, both operating in the learned latent space to preserve motion realism while satisfying spatial objectives.

## Results

On the BABEL validation set for text-conditioned temporal motion composition, DART achieves the best segment FID (3.79) and transition FID (1.86), indicating the highest motion realism among all methods. DART generates at 334 frames/s with 0.02s latency on a single RTX 4090, approximately 10× faster than the offline state-of-the-art FlowMDM (31 frames/s, 161s latency) while using less than a quarter of its memory (2,394 vs. 11,892 MiB). In motion in-betweening (HML3D), DART achieves a goal error of 0.59 cm and jerk of 0.61, substantially outperforming DNO (4.24 cm, 0.65) and OmniControl (7.79 cm, 1.41). For text-conditioned goal-reaching, DART policies achieve 100% success rate with 17.08 s average time (walk), compared to GAMMA's 95% success and 31.44 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlowMDM (the state-of-the-art offline composition method), DART achieves a significantly lower segment FID (3.79 vs. 5.81) and transition FID (1.86 vs. 2.39), indicating more realistic motions, and is ~10× faster (334 vs. 31 fr/s) with 5× lower latency (0.02 s vs. 161 s). However, FlowMDM edges out DART on motion-text semantic alignment (R-prec 0.67 vs. 0.62; MM-Dist 5.08 vs. 5.27) and transition smoothness (PJ 0.04 vs. 0.06; AUJ 0.11 vs. 0.21), which the authors attribute to DART's inherent online transition delay. Against TEACH, DoubleTake, and T2M-GPT*, DART dominates on FID, transition quality, and latency, though TEACH is faster in raw throughput (3880 fr/s) due to its simpler architecture. In motion in-betweening, DART outperforms DNO by 7× on goal error (0.59 vs. 4.24 cm) and OmniControl by 13× (0.59 vs. 7.79 cm), while also achieving lower skate and jerk. For goal-reaching, DART's RL policy surpasses GAMMA on every metric: 100% vs. 95% success rate, 17.08 s vs. 31.44 s reach time, and 2.67 vs. 5.14 cm/s skate. In human preference studies, DART is preferred over FlowMDM for both realism (53.3% vs. 46.7%) and semantic alignment (51.3% vs. 48.7%), confirming that the slight metric gap in R-prec does not translate to perceptual degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DART combines three established threads: the latent diffusion architecture from Rombach et al. (2022) and the transformer-based motion VAE from MLD (Chen et al., 2023b), the autoregressive overlapping motion primitive representation from GAMMA (Zhang & Tang, 2022), and text conditioning via CLIP embeddings with classifier-free guidance. The key departure from prior work is the unification of these into a single real-time autoregressive model that simultaneously supports text-driven temporal composition and spatial control (via latent noise optimization or RL policies), whereas prior methods handle these as separate offline problems. Compared to the state-of-the-art offline composer FlowMDM, DART trades a small amount of semantic alignment accuracy (R-prec) for a 10× speedup and online operation; compared to spatial control methods like DNO, DART's primitive-based latent space better harmonizes spatial goals with text semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling real-time (300+ fps) text-driven character animation with spatial control, which opens the door to interactive applications (games, virtual avatars, animation tools) where users can direct characters via natural language while specifying spatial goals. Key limitations include the dependence on frame-level text annotations (BABEL) for precise alignment, the kinematic (non-physical) nature of generated motions, and the inability to handle open-vocabulary prompts due to limited motion-text data scale. Open questions include hierarchical latent spaces for multi-scale semantics, scaling to open-vocabulary generation via video-based motion extraction, and tighter integration with physics-based simulation for physically accurate real-time control.
