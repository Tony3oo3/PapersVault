## Problem

In model-based RL, dynamics models predict the next state from the current state-action pair, necessitating bootstrapping during roll-out where each prediction depends on the previous one. This causes compounding errors that grow exponentially with roll-out length, severely limiting the utility of model-generated samples for policy optimization. Existing approaches rely on ensembles to quantify uncertainty but cannot fundamentally reduce the bootstrapping dependency that drives error accumulation.

## Input / Output

The ADM takes a single state vector and a variable-length action sequence of k steps (where k is uniformly sampled from [1, m] during roll-out) as input. The state is duplicated k times to match the action sequence length before being sequentially fed through a GRU-based RNN, yielding hidden representations that are passed to an MLP head. The model outputs Gaussian parameters (mean and standard deviation) for both the predicted future state and reward, and the variance across predictions at different backtracking lengths serves as an ensemble-free uncertainty estimate for pessimistic value iteration.

## Method

The core contribution is the Any-step Dynamics Model (ADM), an RNN with GRU cells trained to predict the next state from any backtracking length k ∈ [1, m], converting bootstrapping prediction into direct prediction over variable-length plans. During roll-out, the backtracking length k is sampled uniformly at random at each step, so the next-state prediction is attributed to a plan starting k steps earlier rather than always the immediately preceding state, reducing the expected number of bootstrapping iterations and thus the compounding error. For online RL (ADMPO-ON), ADM replaces the ensemble in MBPO with SAC as the policy optimizer; for offline RL (ADMPO-OFF), ADM replaces the ensemble in MOPO, using the variance of predictions across different k values as a pessimistic penalty term in the Bellman operator. The paper proves that this ADM uncertainty is a valid ξ-uncertainty quantifier that bounds the Bellman error under an admissibility assumption, providing theoretical grounding for the pessimistic value iteration framework.

## Results

On the D4RL MuJoCo benchmark (12 tasks), ADMPO-OFF achieves the highest average normalized score of 81.0, outperforming the next-best methods MOBILE (80.0) and CBOP (79.3). On the more challenging NeoRL benchmark (9 tasks with narrower data coverage), ADMPO-OFF achieves 64.0 average, surpassing MOBILE (60.7) and CQL (56.1), while MOPO collapses to 28.5. In the online setting, ADMPO-ON matches the state-of-the-art MACURA on Humanoid (reaching ~6000, 100% of SAC convergence, at 150k steps) while DDPPO requires ~200k steps and MBPO/BMPO/STEVE fail to reach the target even at 300k steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADMPO-OFF wins on 10 out of 12 D4RL MuJoCo tasks, with the largest margins over MOPO (+10.7 avg), RAMBO (+13.3), COMBO (+14.2), and CQL (+19.4); it narrowly beats MOBILE (+1.0) and CBOP (+1.7) on average, losing only on halfcheetah-medium (72.2 vs 74.6) and halfcheetah-medium-replay (67.6 vs 71.7). On NeoRL, where data coverage is tighter, ADMPO-OFF's advantage over MOBILE widens to +3.3 and its lead over MOPO is dramatic (+35.5), confirming that ADM's ensemble-free uncertainty quantification is more robust under limited data. In the online setting, ADMPO-ON matches MACURA (the prior SOTA) on Humanoid at 150k steps and outperforms DDPPO by ~50k steps; it substantially dominates MBPO, BMPO, STEVE, and SAC in sample efficiency, reaching near-SAC asymptotic performance on all four MuJoCo-v3 tasks. The uncertainty quantification analysis (Figure 4) shows ADM achieves a 0.98 correlation between quantified uncertainty and actual model error, surpassing the ensemble dynamics model, and better separates samples from random vs. learned policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-step dynamics model line of work (Asadi et al., 2018/2019; Che et al., 2018) by generalizing from a fixed prediction horizon to an any-step model that accepts variable-length action sequences, implemented via a GRU-based RNN. It drops into the well-established MBPO (Janner et al., 2019) and MOPO (Yu et al., 2020) frameworks, replacing their ensemble dynamics models with a single ADM. The theoretical grounding for the offline uncertainty penalty draws on the PEVI framework (Jin et al., 2021), with the key departure being that model uncertainty is estimated from prediction variance across backtracking lengths rather than across ensemble members.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: (1) compounding error in model rollouts is dramatically reduced (near-zero vs. exponential growth), enabling longer and more reliable imaginary trajectories for both online and offline policy optimization; (2) a single model replaces ensembles for uncertainty quantification, reducing model parameter count by roughly half. Open questions include whether the RNN-based any-step design scales to high-dimensional visual observations, whether the approach handles highly stochastic environments (acknowledged limitation), and whether the GPU memory overhead from large m values becomes prohibitive in practice. The authors note future work on non-Markovian visual RL scenarios.
