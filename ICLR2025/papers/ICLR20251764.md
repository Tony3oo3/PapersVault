## Problem

Local learning algorithms such as Predictive Coding (PC) and Target Propagation (TP) introduce additional hyperparameters (inference step sizes, feedback network weights) beyond standard backpropagation, making it difficult to identify parameter settings that yield stable training. No systematic infinite-width analysis exists for these methods, leaving open how to scale them while preserving stable learning dynamics and enabling hyperparameter transfer across network widths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251764/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes an L-layer fully connected network of width M and produces per-layer abc-parameterization scaling rules (exponents a_l, b_l, c_l) governing weight initialization and learning rate scaling. Empirical validation uses FashionMNIST (28×28 grayscale images) with 3-layer MLPs and CNNs (including VGG5), producing 10-class predictions. The practical output is a set of scaling exponents that guarantee μ Transfer: optimal hyperparameters tuned at one width remain valid at other widths, and the 'wider is better' property holds.

## Method

The paper derives μP for PC and TP by extending the perturbation approach to treat inference step sizes as additional parameters alongside learning rates. For PC, the μP depends on the scaling exponent γ̄_L of the last layer's inference step size γ_L = γ'/M^γ̄_L; linear-network fixed-point analysis reveals that PC's gradient interpolates between first-order GD (γ̄_L = 0, where the preconditioner C_γ vanishes at infinite width) and Gauss-Newton-like gradients (γ̄_L = -1, where C_γ remains O(1)). For TP with linear feedback networks, the μP requires b_L = 1/2 rather than the standard b_L = 1, because the feedback network breaks the statistical dependence between W_L and Δh_{L-1} that would otherwise permit the kernel regime (α = 1/2 instead of α = 1), making TP inherently favor feature learning. The derivation uses a one-step update from random initialization with F-ini and sequential inference, and the resulting parameterizations are empirically validated for learning-rate and inference-step-size transfer across widths.

## Results

The paper derives the maximal update parameterization (μP) for Predictive Coding (PC) and Target Propagation (TP), showing that PC's μP depends on the inference step-size exponent γ̄_L: for γ̄_L = 0 it reduces to SGD's μP, while for γ̄_L = −1 it matches the Gauss-Newton Target (GNT) scaling. For TP, μP requires b_L = 1/2 (vs. b_L = 1 for SGD/GNT), which eliminates the kernel regime entirely. Empirically, on FashionMNIST with 3-layer MLPs and CNNs, the derived μP enables successful learning-rate transfer across widths (the optimal LR is independent of width order) and the 'wider is better' trend holds, confirming stable feature learning in the infinite-width limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to SGD's μP (Yang et al., 2021), PC with γ̄_L = 0 yields an identical parameterization, and the paper confirms that SGD's learning rates transfer directly to PC when F-ini, FPA, and SI are applied (Figure 1 left). However, without FPA, PC requires the new μP with the γ̄_L-dependent scaling, and the paper verifies that this new parameterization still enables μ Transfer (Figure 1 right). Compared to GNT's μP (Ishikawa & Karakida, 2024), PC with γ̄_L = −1 matches GNT's c_l scaling, and the paper shows via cosine similarity (Figure 2) that PC's gradient approaches GNT's direction in this regime; however, for γ̄_L = 0, PC's gradient instead converges to first-order GD. Compared to standard BP (Rumelhart et al., 1986), the key distinction is that TP's μP requires b_L = 1/2 rather than b_L = 1, which causes the kernel regime to disappear entirely (ω_L → 1/2 instead of 1, Figure 6 right), making TP the first known method where b_L = 1/2 induces feature learning rather than kernel behavior in the infinite-width limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the maximal update parameterization (μP) framework of Yang & Hu (2021) and its second-order generalization by Ishikawa & Karakida (2024) to local learning algorithms—specifically Predictive Coding (PC) and Target Propagation (TP)—whose gradients do not generally reduce to backpropagation. While prior work on PC (Alonso et al., 2022; Meulemans et al., 2020) attempted to bridge these algorithms to classical optimization under restrictive conditions (fixed prediction assumption, invertible networks), this paper derives μP directly from the algorithm's native dynamics, including the inference phase and feedback network, without such assumptions. The derivation introduces a new perturbation technique that treats the inference step size γ_L as an additional hyperparameter to be scaled with width, going beyond the standard learning-rate-only perturbation used for SGD and GNT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic theoretical foundation for scaling local learning algorithms to large widths, enabling hyperparameter transfer (μ Transfer) that was previously unavailable for PC and TP. The finding that TP's μP requires b_L = 1/2 and that the kernel regime disappears entirely is a genuinely new conceptual result with no precedent in the infinite-width literature. However, the derivation relies on one-step updates and (for the fixed-point analysis) linear networks, and empirical validation is limited to FashionMNIST with small architectures; extending to full tensor programs for nonlinear networks and deeper architectures remains an open challenge that would be needed for broader practical impact.
