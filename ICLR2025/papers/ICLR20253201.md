## Problem

Existing scaling laws for LLMs primarily characterize upstream (pretraining) cross-entropy as a function of data size, model size, or compute, but leave open whether and how downstream task performance (e.g., BLEU, COMET) scales with pretraining data size in transfer learning settings. This gap is critical because practitioners need to decide how much pretraining data to invest in and whether a given pretraining corpus is relevant to a target task, yet no principled scaling law connects pretraining data size to task-specific quality metrics.

## Input / Output

The scaling laws take the pretraining dataset size D_p (number of tokens seen) as the independent variable, with the pretraining data's language composition (e.g., 50% en-MC4 + 50% fr-MC4) and the finetuning dataset size D_f as conditioning factors that determine the fitted coefficients. The output is a predicted translation quality score (BLEU, ROUGE, or COMET) or downstream cross-entropy loss, each parameterized by three coefficients (A, α, β for the log-law; E, A, α for the power-law) fit from as few as three (pretraining tokens, score) pairs. A translation alignment score A(D, T) is also computed from the percentage of source and target languages present in the pretraining mixture, serving as a diagnostic for when the log-law is expected to hold.

## Method

The paper proposes a log-law f(D_p) = (log(A · D_p^α))^β for translation quality metrics and a power-law L(D_p) = E + A/D_p^α for downstream cross-entropy, both as functions of pretraining data size. Coefficients are fit via Huber loss with L-BFGS optimization (δ=0.1 for scores, δ=1e-3 for cross-entropy), using the first four data points and evaluating prediction error on held-out checkpoints. Systematic experiments pretrain T5-3B and T5-770M encoder-decoder models on controlled subsets of Multilingual C4 (en, de, fr, ro in various mixtures) for up to 1M steps, then finetune on WMT-17 en-de, WMT-15 en-fr, and WMT-16 en-ro at multiple dataset sizes. The key empirical finding is that when the pretraining data is sufficiently aligned with the translation task (alignment score A=1), translation scores improve monotonically and the log-law fits with prediction error ≤0.061; when alignment is insufficient (e.g., 100% en-MC4 for en-fr, A=0.7), BLEU and COMET scores can fluctuate or decrease with more pretraining while cross-entropy still follows the power-law monotonically, demonstrating that cross-entropy is not a reliable proxy for task quality in misaligned settings.

## Results

The proposed log-law f(D_p) = (log(A·D_p^α))^β fits BLEU scores with a maximum prediction error of 0.061 (δ=0.1) across all aligned pretraining configurations, while the power-law L(D_p) = E + A/D_p^α fits downstream cross-entropy with errors as low as 5.95×10⁻¹² (δ=1e-3). For the en-fr task with 100% en-MC4 pretraining (alignment score A=0.7), the BLEU scaling law breaks at larger pretraining sizes while cross-entropy continues to decrease monotonically, demonstrating a critical divergence between the two metrics. When the finetuning dataset is large (e.g., 21B tokens for en-fr), pretraining yields little to no improvement over non-pretrained models, and the scaling law correctly predicts this plateau.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing scaling-law methods but instead contrasts two families of metrics (translation scores vs. cross-entropy) and two alignment regimes. On the en-de task, BLEU and cross-entropy show a consistent correlation across all pretraining datasets, supporting the exponential relationship proposed by Gordon et al. (2021). However, on the en-fr task with 100% en-MC4 pretraining (A=0.7), BLEU becomes non-monotonic and the scaling law breaks at larger pretraining sizes, while cross-entropy continues to decrease smoothly—meaning a practitioner relying solely on cross-entropy would incorrectly conclude that more pretraining is beneficial. The 50% en + 50% target-language mixtures (A=1) consistently outperform 100% en-MC4 (A=0.7) in both BLEU and cross-entropy, and the log-law fits the former well but fails on the latter for en-fr. Notably, 100% target-language pretraining (e.g., 100% fr-MC4 for en-fr, A=0.8) performs nearly as well as the balanced 50/50 mixture, while 100% source-language (en-MC4) performs significantly worse, suggesting the target language in pretraining is more critical for translation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scaling laws literature from upstream cross-entropy (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) and transfer-learning cross-entropy as a function of finetuning data size (Hernandez et al., 2021) to a new axis: downstream task-specific metrics (BLEU, COMET, ROUGE) as a function of *pretraining* data size. It adopts the Huber-loss + L-BFGS fitting methodology from Hoffmann et al. (2022) but applies it to a log-law form for translation scores rather than the standard power-law. The paper also departs from the common assumption (supported by Gordon et al., 2021) that cross-entropy and translation quality are exponentially related, demonstrating empirically that this relationship breaks down under distribution misalignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical guide for pretraining data valuation (Section 3.5) gives MT practitioners a concrete, low-cost procedure to decide whether more pretraining is worthwhile before committing compute. The finding that cross-entropy can monotonically improve while BLEU/COMET degrade under misalignment is a caution against using CE as a proxy for task quality in data-selection decisions. Key limitations include the alignment score being specific to translation (language overlap) and the non-monotonic behavior being observed primarily in one language pair (en-fr) with 100% English pretraining; a more general, linguistically grounded alignment measure remains an open question. The log-law's generalization to SuperGLUE tasks is encouraging but limited to English pretraining.
