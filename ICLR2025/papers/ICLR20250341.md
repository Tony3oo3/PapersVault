## Problem

Existing GFlowNets rely on proxy models for online reward evaluation, but proxy accuracy degrades sharply with limited data, making generated candidates unreliable. The distinctive flow-matching training objective of GFlowNets (where policy is determined by inflow/outflow ratios rather than a free-form policy network) renders standard offline RL techniques—actor-critic, Q-learning, and policy constraint methods—inapplicable. This paper asks: how can GFlowNets be trained effectively in a fully offline setting without online evaluation, while still producing high-quality and diverse candidates?

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline trajectory dataset formatted as a set of state transitions (s, s', r) where each transition corresponds to a supported edge in a DAG, plus an initial state s₀ from which generation begins. The model outputs diverse candidate objects (molecule graphs via junction-tree building blocks, amino-acid sequences, or item lists) sampled with probability proportional to reward. A key structural constraint is that the action space at each state is restricted to supported flows (edges present in the training data), and the model must maintain flow-consistency (inflow = outflow + reward) at every interior node.

## Method

COFlowNet introduces the concept of unsupported flows—edges in the full action space that do not appear in the offline dataset—and regularizes them to near zero via two penalty terms (R_in and R_out) added to the standard flow-matching loss, yielding the Constrained Flow Matching (CFM) objective. Theorem 1 proves this regularization strictly decreases unsupported flow without harming supported flow, while Theorem 2 shows that reducing unsupported inflow at a state necessarily increases supported inflow, concentrating probability mass on data-supported paths. For diversity, the authors extend the framework to quantile flows using a pinball loss, producing the Conservative Quantile Matching (CQM) objective that matches the full distribution of flows rather than just their means. The final training procedure is sampling-free: the model is trained purely on batched states from the offline dataset, requiring no interaction with a proxy or oracle model during training.

## Results

On the Molecule Design task, COFlowNet achieves an average top-10 reward of 8.4638 under data scarcity (14k molecules) and 8.5029 under fully trained (300k molecules), outperforming vanilla GFlowNet (8.4381 / 8.5283) on most metrics. Under data scarcity, COFlowNet's top-100 reward of 8.3034 surpasses GFlowNet's 8.2909, while QM-GFlowNet (8.3272) remains slightly ahead. On the Anti-Microbial Peptide Design task, COFlowNet achieves 0.878 performance, 28.88 novelty, and 12.45 diversity, comparable to online QM-GFlowNet (0.895 / 29.12 / 12.14). On item recommendation (ML1M), COFlowNet reaches 1.998 Avg.R and 109.35 Coverage, matching QM-GFlowNet's reward while substantially exceeding its coverage (65.61).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla GFlowNet (Bengio et al., 2021), COFlowNet wins on most metrics, particularly under data scarcity where it gains +0.026 on top-10 reward (8.4638 vs 8.4381) and +0.013 on top-100 (8.3034 vs 8.2909); under fully trained settings the gap narrows and GFlowNet edges ahead on top-10 (8.5283 vs 8.5029). Against QM-GFlowNet (Zhang et al., 2023d), the online quantile-based method remains slightly superior under fully trained conditions (8.5552 vs 8.5029 top-10), but COFlowNet degrades more gracefully under data scarcity, where QM-GFlowNet's advantage shrinks and COFlowNet's conservative constraint proves more robust. MARS (Xie et al., 2021) performs significantly worse than all flow-based methods, with a top-10 reward of only 8.0778 under fully trained settings, and generates nearly 20× fewer diverse modes. PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) achieves comparable reward scores to flow-based models (8.4249 top-10) but exhibits markedly inferior diversity, overfitting the proxy model. The ablation COFlowNet w/o (removing conservative regularization) consistently underperforms COFlowNet by 0.02–0.12 across all metrics, confirming that the unsupported-flow constraint is the key driver of offline performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GFlowNets (Bengio et al., 2021) to the offline setting by introducing conservative regularization on 'unsupported flows'—edges absent from the training data—arguing that standard offline RL techniques (actor-critic, Q-learning, policy constraint) are fundamentally incompatible with the flow-matching objective. It also builds on the quantile flow matching framework of Zhang et al. (2023d), adapting the pinball-loss-based quantile matching into a conservative quantile matching (CQM) objective that simultaneously enforces flow consistency and restricts exploration to supported regions. The key conceptual departure is from prior offline RL, where the paper explicitly shows why Bellman-based value iteration and policy-matching constraints conflict with the inflow-outflow balance that defines GFlowNet policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COFlowNet addresses a practical bottleneck in GFlowNet applications (drug design, sequence design) where expensive oracle evaluation makes online training infeasible, offering a sampling-free offline alternative that is also ~2× faster per epoch than online baselines. However, the paper acknowledges that offline models fundamentally cannot outperform well-trained online models given a strong proxy, and performance degrades sharply on purely random data, limiting applicability to settings with at least partially informative historical trajectories. Open questions include how to handle genuinely out-of-distribution generation (the conservative constraint explicitly prevents this), whether the unsupported-flow regularization scales to continuous or very large discrete state spaces, and how to combine offline pre-training with limited online fine-tuning.
