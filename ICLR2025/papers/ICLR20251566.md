## Problem

Existing jailbreak research focuses on adversarially crafted or optimized prompts, leaving open whether safety fine-tuning generalizes to natural, in-distribution prompts that are merely semantically related to toxic seed questions. This paper asks how robust aligned LLMs are to such natural prompts and finds that popular models like GPT-4 are brittle even to simple paraphrases and semantically related questions that carry no explicit jailbreaking intent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251566/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single toxic seed question (from JailbreakBench's 100 misuse behaviors across 10 categories) and produces approximately 1000 diverse, natural question augmentations per seed. The generation pipeline requires only API access to an unaligned LLM (Palm-2-Otter) for the Q→A step and a safety-aligned LLM (GPT-4o) for the A→Q step, with no black-box or white-box access to the target model needed for iterative optimization. Generated questions are constrained to lie within a cosine-similarity perturbation radius ε of the seed in Gecko embedding space, ensuring they remain semantically related and in-distribution.

## Method

ReG-QA (Response Guided Question Augmentation) exploits an asymmetry in safety alignment: while aligned LLMs refuse toxic questions (Q→A), they can be prompted to generate toxic questions from toxic answers (A→Q), a reversal-curse-like failure. The pipeline first uses an unaligned LLM to generate 100 long answers from the seed question, filters them for toxicity and length, then prompts GPT-4o with each toxic answer to produce 10 questions that would elicit that answer. Crucially, no adversarial objective, optimization loop, or jailbreaking instruction is incorporated at any step—the prompts are minimal and direct (e.g., 'Generate 10 diverse questions that give the following specific answer'). This yields natural, in-distribution jailbreaks that achieve 82% ASR on GPT-4 and 93% on GPT-3.5, and are significantly more stable against defenses like Smooth-LLM and Synonym Substitution than existing attacks, since they cannot be distinguished from benign prompts by perplexity or perturbation-based filters.

## Results

On JailbreakBench, ReG-QA achieves an overall attack success rate (ASR) of 82% on GPT-4 and 93% on GPT-3.5, substantially outperforming the paraphrasing baseline Para-QA (41% and 66% respectively). Against leading adversarial attacks on the JailbreakBench leaderboard, ReG-QA achieves 95% ASR (no defense) on GPT-3.5-Turbo-1106, compared to 93% for Prompt and Random Search, 71% for PAIR, and 47% for GCG. Critically, ReG-QA remains highly robust under defenses: it retains 82–88% ASR under Smooth-LLM, Synonym Substitution, and non-dictionary word removal, while all baselines drop to 0–21%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReG-QA outperforms all four baselines on every metric reported. Against the paraphrasing baseline Para-QA, ReG-QA gains 20–51 percentage points in overall ASR across GPT-4 (82% vs. 41%), GPT-3.5 (93% vs. 66%), and Gemma-2-27B (82% vs. 36%), and achieves 100% ASR in 5 out of 10 categories on GPT-3.5 where Para-QA does not. Against Prompt and Random Search (Andriushchenko et al., 2024), ReG-QA edges ahead on no-defense ASR (95% vs. 93%) but the decisive gap appears under defenses: ReG-QA retains 82–88% ASR while Prompt and Random Search collapses to 4–11%. Against PAIR (Chao et al., 2023), ReG-QA leads by 24 points without defenses (95% vs. 71%) and by 77–80 points under defenses. Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), the gap is largest: 48 points without defenses (95% vs. 47%) and 82 points under Smooth-LLM (82% vs. 0%). The paper also notes that ReG-QA's generated jailbreaks are judged more natural than all baselines 96% of the time by a GPT-4o judge, and have log-likelihood scores (−54.62) closest to the seed questions (−48.17), versus −105 to −368 for the other attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of jailbreak generation that relies on iterative optimization (GCG, PAIR, Prompt and Random Search) or explicit adversarial prompting objectives (persona modulation, persuasive framing). Instead, ReG-QA exploits a conceptual asymmetry in safety alignment—drawing on the 'reversal curse' literature (Berglund et al., 2023) and distribution-shift analysis (Wei et al., 2024)—by using a safety-aligned LLM in the reverse direction (A→Q) to generate questions from toxic answers, without any jailbreaking objective in the generation prompts. The method is positioned as an in-distribution evaluation tool rather than an adversarial attack, distinguishing it from prior work that produces prompts outside the natural text distribution (R2 in their taxonomy).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that safety fine-tuning in the forward direction (Q→A) does not generalize to the reverse direction (A→Q) has direct implications for how safety training data should be constructed and how models should be evaluated. The robustness of ReG-QA-generated jailbreaks against defenses like Smooth-LLM and Synonym Substitution (which drop other attacks to 0–21% ASR while ReG-QA retains 82–88%) suggests that defenses exploiting non-naturalness are fundamentally insufficient. Open questions include whether bidirectional safety training (explicitly training on A→Q pairs) would close this gap, and whether the vulnerability persists as models scale or as safety training becomes more comprehensive. A limitation is that the method requires access to an unaligned LLM for the Q→A step, which may not always be available.
