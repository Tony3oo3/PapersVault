## Problem

Existing SVD-based LLM compression methods (FWSVD, ASVD) suffer from two fundamental issues: (1) they fail to establish a direct relationship between singular values and compression loss, so truncating the smallest singular values does not guarantee minimal loss, and (2) they do not update the remaining compressed weights after truncation, causing severe accuracy degradation at high compression ratios (≥40%).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250697/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM's weight matrices, 256 randomly sampled calibration sentences from WikiText-2 (to compute per-layer activation statistics), and a target compression ratio. It outputs, for each compressed linear layer, two low-rank matrices W'_u ∈ R^{d×r} and W'_v ∈ R^{r×n} (where r < min(d,n)) that replace the original W ∈ R^{d×n}, reducing weight memory to (1−R_w) of the original. The parameter update stage additionally uses 50K Alpaca instruction samples for sequential LoRA fine-tuning. As a side benefit, the intermediate state M = W'_v × X can be stored in the KV cache instead of the full W'_u × W'_v × X, reducing runtime KV cache memory by a factor of r/d.

## Method

SVD-LLM introduces two key mechanisms. First, truncation-aware data whitening: it computes a whitening matrix S via Cholesky decomposition of XX^T (accumulated incrementally from calibration activations) so that S^{-1}X is orthonormal, then applies SVD to WS rather than W alone. A theoretical proof (Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.3) shows this makes the compression loss of truncating singular value σ_i equal to σ_i itself, so truncating the smallest singular values provably minimizes loss—unlike ASVD's diagonal scaling where loss is a complex function of activations and singular vectors. Second, parameter update with sequential low-rank approximation: rather than applying LoRA to the full product W' = W'_u × W'_v, it applies LoRA updates B_u A_u and B_v A_v to W'_u and W'_v separately in a sequential manner (freeze W'_v, fine-tune W'_u; then freeze updated W'_u, fine-tune W'_v) to preserve the low-rank structure and avoid interdependent gradient interference between the two factors.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B, SVD-LLM achieves WikiText-2 perplexity of 7.94 at 20% compression (vs. ASVD's 11.14 and FWSVD's 1727), 13.73 at 40% (vs. ASVD's 1407), and 31.79 at 80% (vs. ASVD's 80425 and FWSVD's 96872). On six common-sense reasoning datasets, SVD-LLM reaches 0.49 average accuracy at 20% and 0.16 at 80%, while all SVD-based baselines drop to near-zero (≤0.06) at 60%+ compression. When combined with 2-bit QuIP# quantization, SVD-LLM achieves 9.83 perplexity at 1.3 GB memory, outperforming the training-required OneBit method (10.20) without any retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVD-based baselines, SVD-LLM dominates: at 40% compression it reduces WikiText-2 perplexity by 99% over ASVD (13.73 vs. 1407) and 99% over FWSVD (13.73 vs. 18156); at 80%, SVD-LLM (with sequential LoRA update) achieves 31.79 perplexity while ASVD and FWSVD exceed 80,000. Against structured pruning methods (LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT, BlockPruner), SVD-LLM achieves up to 56% lower perplexity under a 7 GB memory budget (9.63 vs. 21.68 for LLM-Pruner). Against 1-bit post-training quantization (PB-LLM at 104.83, BiLLM at 47.67 perplexity), SVD-LLM at the same 1.5 GB memory achieves 47.21, marginally beating BiLLM. Against the training-required OneBit method (10.20 perplexity at 1.3 GB), SVD-LLM alone is competitive but SVD-LLM combined with 2-bit QuIP# quantization achieves 9.83, surpassing OneBit without any retraining. Against FLAP (a structured pruning method), SVD-LLM achieves 99% lower perplexity at 80% compression (31.79 vs. 15023).

## Relation to Prior Work

SVD-LLM builds directly on the SVD-based LLM compression line initiated by DRONE (data-aware low-rank compression for BERT), FWSVD (Fisher-weighted SVD), and ASVD (activation-scaled SVD), but departs from them in two key ways: it replaces their ad-hoc importance weighting with a Cholesky-based data whitening that provably aligns singular values with compression loss, and it introduces a sequential LoRA fine-tuning step to update the decomposed low-rank matrices after truncation—something no prior SVD-based LLM method performs. The paper also establishes a formal equivalence between its theoretical compression loss and DRONE's optimal solution, while showing SVD-LLM achieves this with far lower memory (incremental XX^T updates vs. caching full activations) and faster compression time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVD-based compression is uniquely attractive for LLM deployment because it is hardware-agnostic (unlike quantization or unstructured pruning) and simultaneously enables KV cache reduction, making it a practical path to efficient inference on both GPU and CPU. The paper's demonstration that SVD-LLM + 2-bit quantization (QuIP#) outperforms 1-bit training-required OneBit without any retraining opens a promising hybrid compression direction. However, the authors acknowledge that accuracy still degrades significantly at high compression ratios (80%), KV cache compression introduces additional latency, and generation quality (repetition, coherence) remains an open problem—limiting immediate real-world adoption at aggressive compression settings.
