## Problem

Continual learning methods built on pre-trained models achieve strong empirical performance but lack theoretical guarantees, while principled CL approaches (e.g., the Ideal Continual Learner) suffer from numerical instability when solving minimum-norm least-squares on highly ill-conditioned lifted features. Existing remedies like RanPAC's ridge regression are sensitive to the regularization hyperparameter, especially in long task sequences with small per-task data. This paper bridges the theory-practice gap by designing a CL method that is both provably stable and empirically competitive.

## Input / Output

Each incoming task provides m_t RGB images, which are passed through a frozen ViT (vit_base_patch16_224, d=768) to extract pre-trained features X_t, then lifted into an E=10^5 dimensional space via random ReLU features H_t = relu(P X_t) with a fixed random Gaussian matrix P. Labels are one-hot vectors in R^{c_t} where c_t grows as new classes arrive. At test time, a single image is forward-passed through the same frozen ViT and random ReLU layer, and the predicted class is the argmax of the linear score W_t h. The over-parametrized regime E >> M_t (total samples seen) is essential, yielding infinitely many feasible classifiers from which the minimum-norm solution is selected.

## Method

LoRanPAC identifies that the ill-conditioning of lifted features H_{1:t} worsens as tasks accumulate: the smallest singular values plummet to ~10^{-5} while the largest remain near 10^{11}, causing the incremental SVD solution to the minimum-norm problem to become numerically unstable. The remedy is to continually truncate the SVD of H_{1:t}, preserving only the top k_t = ceil((1-ζ) min{E, M_t}) singular values before solving the least-squares problem. The continual implementation avoids storing all past data by maintaining a compressed representation B_t = [U_tilde_{1:t-1} Σ_tilde_{1:t-1}, H_t] and computing its top-k_t SVD factors via an incremental QR-based update (Algorithm 2), then constructing the classifier as W_tilde_t = J_t U_tilde_{1:t} Σ_tilde_{1:t}^{-2} U_tilde_{1:t}^T where J_t = Y_{1:t} H_{1:t}^T is updated online. Theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1 and 2) bound training and test MSE in terms of the eigenvalue ratio γ_t (minimum preserved eigenvalue over maximum truncated eigenvalue) and the accumulative truncation error a_t, showing both remain small when only the smallest eigenvalues are truncated, even over hundreds of tasks.

## Results

On the 8 CIL datasets with B-0, Inc-{5,10,20}, LoRanPAC achieves an average final accuracy of 78.55% (without first-session adaptation) and 81.16% (with EASE-style FSA), outperforming RanPAC (76.76% / 78.65%) and the joint linear classifier LC(H₁:T) (77.18%). In the challenging Inc-1 setting (one class per task, hundreds of tasks), LoRanPAC achieves 88.19% on CIFAR-100, 73.66% on ImageNet-R, and 62.76% on ImageNet-A, while RanPAC drops to 86.99%, 70.12%, and 36.6% respectively. LoRanPAC also demonstrates superior stability, with standard deviations under 0.2% across all Inc-1 datasets versus up to 37% for RanPAC on CUB-200.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRanPAC uniformly outperforms RanPAC (its closest competitor) across all 8 datasets and all increment sizes, with the largest gaps on ImageNet-A (63.20% vs. 61.75%) and StanfordCars (74.39% vs. 71.40%). Against prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CodaPrompt), LoRanPAC leads by 10–25 points on average, with the most dramatic advantage on StanfordCars (74.39% vs. 13.70–43.68%) where prompt methods collapse. SimpleCIL and ADAM are competitive on some datasets (e.g., CUB-200) but fail catastrophically on StanfordCars (35.46% and 35.61% vs. LoRanPAC's 74.39%). In the Inc-1 setting (Table 2), the gap widens dramatically: LoRanPAC achieves 62.76% on ImageNet-A and 74.32% on StanfordCars, while RanPAC drops to 36.6% and 35.56% respectively, with RanPAC's standard deviation reaching 25–37% on those datasets. The paper attributes RanPAC's instability to its cross-validation-based λ selection failing on small validation sets, whereas LoRanPAC's truncation percentage ζ (set to 25%) is robust across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoRanPAC directly extends RanPAC (McDonnell et al., 2023), which lifts pre-trained ViT features into a high-dimensional random ReLU space and trains a linear classifier via ridge regression. The key departure is replacing the ridge regularization parameter λ (whose optimal value is dataset- and scale-dependent and must be tuned via cross-validation) with continual SVD truncation of the smallest singular values, a standard numerical linear algebra remedy that is far more stable across task sequences. The method also inherits the SVD-based solution structure from the Ideal Continual Learner (Peng et al., 2023), but addresses its critical numerical instability by truncating the ill-conditioned spectrum at every step, yielding a recurrence relation that enables provable error bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper closes a long-standing gap in continual learning between theoretically grounded methods (which are impractical) and high-performing heuristic methods (which lack guarantees), by showing that a simple spectral truncation yields both provable training/test error bounds and competitive empirical performance across 8 datasets. The Inc-1 results (hundreds of sequential classes) are particularly important, as they demonstrate stability where RanPAC's cross-validation-based λ selection catastrophically fails. Limitations include the reliance on a noisy linear regression model assumption, the use of MSE rather than cross-entropy loss, and theoretical bounds that depend on unknown ground-truth quantities (W*, noise norm). Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-linear trainable heads, whether the linear model assumption holds for more complex downstream tasks, and whether the truncation strategy can be made adaptive per-task rather than using a fixed percentage.
