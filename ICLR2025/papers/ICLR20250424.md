## Problem

Most publicly available dense video captioning datasets are in English, leaving a critical gap for Chinese video-language research. This scarcity constrains both the user experience for Chinese-speaking audiences and the development of multi-modal models tailored to Chinese video content, particularly for fine-grained, segment-level descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250424/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 31,466 short videos (avg. 85.68s, 99% between 1–2 min) segmented into 311,921 clips (avg. 8.1s, range 2–32s), each annotated with Chinese captions of 10–26 tokens (avg. 17.9 characters). For the PRVR benchmark, a text query maps to a set of 4.96 partially relevant videos on average; for grounding, a text query plus full video yields a timestamp segment; for generation, a video segment yields a single-sentence Chinese caption. Videos are standardized to 360×640 or 640×360 at 15 FPS (320p, no audio) for model evaluation.

## Method

The dataset is manually annotated from Youku-mPLUG videos sampled across 11 categories and 84 subcategories, following structured guidelines requiring subject, action, and scene descriptions with explicit start/end timestamps. The PRVR benchmark is constructed by aggregating semantically similar annotations via xiaobu-embedding (similarity > 0.9) and then applying an NMS-like deduplication step that removes redundant query-video-set pairs with IoU > 0.7, yielding 28,988 queries over 3,185 target videos. The grounding and generation benchmarks are further filtered by removing videos with self-BLEU > 0.15 (excessive textual repetition) or adjacent-segment color histogram correlation > 0.5 (insufficient scene change), leaving 1,872 videos with 20,099 annotations. Evaluation spans zero-shot retrieval (ViCLIP, InternVideo2, CLIP, Chinese-CLIP, DFN-CLIP, mPLUG-Owl3), grounding via Moment-DETR with CLIP/Chinese-CLIP features, and generation with four MLLMs (InternVideo2-Chat, MiniCPM-V, InternVL2, Qwen2-VL), plus LoRA fine-tuning ablations on Qwen2-VL-2B and ViCLIP demonstrating the dataset's data-augmentation value.

## Results

On the retrieval benchmark, Chinese-CLIP achieves the best Chinese text-to-video Top-1 accuracy of 31.78% and Top-5 of 55.49%, substantially outperforming English ViCLIP (26.78% Top-1) and InternVideo2 (26.56% Top-1) on their native English. When evaluated on Chinese, English video models collapse to near-chance performance (ViCLIP: 0.91% Top-1; InternVideo2: 1.31% Top-1), highlighting a severe language gap. On the generation benchmark, Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct leads with CIDEr 19.32 and BertScore 66.68, while InternVL2-8B and MiniCPM-V-2.6 trail at CIDEr 9.19 and 3.66 respectively. For temporal grounding, Chinese-CLIP (512-dim) achieves R1@0.5 of 13.89 and mAP@avg of 7.76, roughly doubling the performance of English CLIP (6.38 R1@0.5, 4.35 mAP@avg) at the same dimensionality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates nine existing models on the new Chinese benchmarks rather than proposing a new method. Chinese-CLIP (Yang et al., 2022) dominates retrieval on Chinese data, achieving 31.78% text2video Top-1 vs. 26.78% for ViCLIP (Wang et al., 2024b) and 26.56% for InternVideo2 (Wang et al., 2024c) on English, and outperforming DFN5B-CLIP (Fang et al., 2024) (6.61%) and standard CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) (1.05%) by a wide margin. English video models (ViCLIP, InternVideo2) drop to near-zero on Chinese (0.91–1.31% Top-1), confirming that language-specific training is essential. For generation, Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct (Wang et al., 2024a) leads all MLLMs with CIDEr 19.32 and BertScore 66.68, outperforming InternVL2-8B (Chen et al., 2023) (CIDEr 9.19), MiniCPM-V-2.6 (Yao et al., 2024) (CIDEr 3.66), and InternVideo2-Chat-8B (Wang et al., 2024c) (CIDEr 4.97). mPLUG-Owl3 (Ye et al., 2024), a generative model without retrieval training, performs worst on retrieval (2.04% Top-1), confirming that generation-oriented architectures lack effective retrieval representations. The grounding results (Table 3) further confirm that Chinese-CLIP at 512-dim (R1@0.5 = 13.89) more than doubles English CLIP at the same dimension (6.38), while ViCLIP at 768-dim (6.23) offers no advantage over the smaller English CLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dense video captioning (DVC) dataset lineage—ActivityNet Captions, YouCook2, ViTT—by providing the first large-scale, fully human-annotated Chinese DVC resource, sourced from the Youku-mPLUG corpus. For the retrieval benchmark, it adapts the Partially Relevant Video Retrieval (PRVR) formulation from Dong et al. (2022) to Chinese, introducing an NMS-style deduplication over embedding-similar queries to reduce evaluation bias. The grounding and generation benchmarks follow the evaluation protocols of MAD (Soldan et al., 2022) and Pan et al. (2023), while the empirical baselines draw on the standard video-language model zoo (ViCLIP, InternVideo2, Chinese-CLIP, Qwen2-VL, InternVL2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a concrete gap: before this work, no publicly available Chinese dense video captioning dataset exceeded a few thousand clips, and the cultural specificity of Chinese content (tea ceremonies, Mid-Autumn moon gazing, cross-talk) is absent from English corpora, making direct translation inadequate. The ablation showing that mixing YDC with VATEX training data yields the best cross-domain generation results suggests that diverse, culturally grounded Chinese video-text pairs are a non-trivial training signal for multilingual MLLMs. Open questions include whether the 31k-video scale is sufficient for pre-training (vs. Youku-mPLUG's 10M), whether the PRVR deduplication threshold (IoU > 0.7) is optimal, and how the benchmarks generalize beyond the Youku domain distribution.
