## Problem

Existing NeRF and 3DGS-based methods struggle to reconstruct reflective objects in real time while capturing high-frequency specular components and inter-reflection effects. Prior Gaussian-based approaches either use simplified per-Gaussian shading that introduces noise, rely on Monte Carlo sampling that is too slow for real-time use, or lack proper material/lighting decomposition for downstream tasks like relighting. No prior method simultaneously achieves real-time rendering, physically accurate BRDF-based shading, and explicit inter-reflection modeling within a Gaussian splatting framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view RGB images of a scene and learns a per-scene environment map during optimization. It produces a set of 2D oriented planar Gaussian primitives, each carrying position, tangential vectors, scaling, opacity, albedo, metallic, roughness, and view-dependent indirect-light spherical harmonics. From these, the system renders novel views in real time (~122 FPS) and outputs decomposed material maps (albedo, metallic, roughness), normal maps, visibility maps, the learned environment map, and a periodically extracted surface mesh via TSDF fusion, enabling downstream relighting and material editing.

## Method

Ref-Gaussian combines two core components: (1) physically based deferred rendering, where pixel-level material feature maps (albedo, metallic, roughness, normal) are obtained via alpha-blending of 2D Gaussian primitives and then shaded using the Disney BRDF with a split-sum approximation that precomputes the BRDF integral into a 2D lookup texture and the incident radiance integral into a series of pre-integrated cubemaps, avoiding Monte Carlo sampling; and (2) Gaussian-grounded inter-reflection, where visibility along the reflected direction is computed by ray tracing against a mesh periodically extracted via TSDF fusion (accelerated by a BVH), and occluded indirect light is modeled as a per-Gaussian view-dependent spherical harmonic evaluated in the reflected direction. Geometry is further improved by using 2D Gaussian primitives instead of 3D ellipsoids, an initial per-Gaussian shading stage (18k steps) that provides stable geometric gradients before switching to deferred rendering (40k steps), and material-aware normal propagation that periodically enlarges the scale of Gaussians with high metallic and low roughness to propagate accurate normals to neighbors. The training loss combines RGB reconstruction (L1 + D-SSIM), normal consistency against depth-derived normals, and an edge-aware normal smoothness term.

## Results

On the Shiny Blender dataset, Ref-Gaussian achieves the highest PSNR on 5 out of 6 scenes (e.g., ball: 37.01 dB vs. 33.43 for 3DGS-DR; toaster: 28.05 vs. 26.76). On the more challenging Glossy Synthetic dataset, Ref-Gaussian leads on all 8 scenes, with notable margins on bell (32.86 vs. 30.60) and luyu (30.04 vs. 28.96) over the strongest baseline 3DGS-DR. On the real-world Ref-Real dataset, Ref-Gaussian also achieves the best PSNR (garden: 22.97, sedan: 26.60, toycar: 24.27). In terms of efficiency, Ref-Gaussian trains in 0.58 h and renders at 122 FPS, compared to 0.35 h / 251 FPS for 3DGS-DR and 5.84 h / 1 FPS for ENVIDR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ref-Gaussian outperforms the closest competitor 3DGS-DR (Ye et al., 2024) on nearly all scenes across all three datasets, with the largest PSNR gains on Glossy Synthetic (e.g., +2.26 dB on bell, +1.08 dB on luyu) and Shiny Blender (e.g., +3.58 dB on ball, +1.29 dB on toaster). Against GShader (Jiang et al., 2024), Ref-Gaussian leads by 3–6 dB PSNR on most scenes, while GShader suffers from substantial noise in geometry and material estimation. RelightableGaussian (R3DG, Gao et al., 2023) is consistently the weakest 3DGS-based baseline, trailing Ref-Gaussian by 4–9 dB PSNR due to Monte Carlo sampling noise and inaccurate visibility. On the real-world Ref-Real dataset, Ref-Gaussian surpasses 3DGS-DR by ~1 dB PSNR and also achieves the best SSIM (0.617 vs. 0.581 on garden). Among NeRF-based methods, Ref-Gaussian significantly outperforms NeRO (30.33 vs. 29.73 PSNR) and NeuS (30.33 vs. 27.86 PSNR) on Glossy Synthetic while rendering 1000×+ faster (122 FPS vs. 0.11 and 0.02 FPS). The only near-tie is with NDE (Wu et al., 2024) on Shiny Blender (35.04 vs. 35.48 PSNR), where Ref-Gaussian still achieves 2× the rendering speed (122 vs. 66 FPS). ENVIDR (Liang et al., 2023) is an outlier that scores 41.02 PSNR on the ball scene but degrades sharply on other scenes and has the slowest training time (5.84 h) and rendering speed (1 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

Ref-Gaussian sits at the intersection of three research threads: 3D/2D Gaussian splatting for scene representation (Kerbl et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024), physically-based deferred rendering for inverse rendering (Ye et al., 2024), and inter-reflection modeling via ray tracing (Gao et al., 2023). It departs from GShader's per-Gaussian simplified shading by moving to pixel-level BRDF evaluation with the Disney BRDF model, and from RelightableGaussian's Monte Carlo sampling by adopting the split-sum approximation for real-time performance. The key conceptual departure is realizing inter-reflection within the Gaussian splatting paradigm for the first time, using ray tracing on a periodically extracted TSDF mesh rather than tracing rays directly across Gaussian primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ref-Gaussian provides a practical, unified framework that handles both reflective and non-reflective scenes at 122 FPS with full PBR including inter-reflection, supporting downstream tasks like relighting and material editing. A notable limitation is that visibility is approximated only along the reflection direction rather than across the full specular lobe, which may introduce noise in material estimation for highly complex inter-reflection scenarios. Open questions include whether the periodic mesh extraction (every 3000 steps) can be made more adaptive, and whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes or larger-scale environments beyond the single-object scale tested here.
