## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction methods for LLM long-context inference suffer from two fundamental information-loss issues: they apply a uniform eviction strategy across all layers despite the well-documented difference in attention density between shallow (dense, global) and deep (sparse, localized) layers, and they irreversibly discard KV pairs, causing hallucinations and contextual degradation in generated text. No prior training-free method simultaneously addresses both the layer-level allocation imbalance and the token-level information loss from eviction.

## Input / Output

D2O operates as a plug-and-play module on any Transformer-based LLM during both prompt encoding and autoregressive token generation. It takes the full KV cache (key and value tensors per layer and head) and a user-specified compression ratio ρ, then produces a fixed-size compressed KV cache per layer where the per-layer budget S_l = α_l · S is dynamically determined by that layer's attention variance. The method requires no fine-tuning or additional parameters; the only hyperparameters are the EMA smoothing constant β (default 0.7), the ratio of important tokens N to recent tokens M (default 3:1), and the number of attention-sink tokens T.

## Method

D2O introduces two-level dynamic discriminative operations. At the layer level, it computes the variance F_v^l of cumulative attention scores for each layer and allocates KV cache budget via inverse variance softmax (α_l ∝ exp(−F_v^l)), giving dense-attention shallow layers more cache and sparse-attention deep layers less. At the token level, it first evicts KV pairs using cumulative attention scores while retaining T attention-sink tokens and a recent window of M tokens, then applies a compensation mechanism: evicted tokens are matched to their nearest neighbors in the retained cache via cosine similarity on keys, and an exponential moving average (EMA) threshold τ_t dynamically determines whether each evicted token is permanently discarded or recalled and merged into its most similar retained token using GAT-inspired similarity-weighted fusion. This merging preserves information from evicted tokens at minimal computational cost, as the similarity matrix is computed only on keys and shared with values.

## Results

Under a 20% KV cache budget (ρ=0.2), D2O on Llama-3-8B achieves 19.93 on MultiFieldQA, 12.79 on 2WikiMQA, 24.36 on GovReport, 69.72 on TREC, and 60.00 on RB-P (LongBench), consistently outperforming all eviction-based baselines and approaching full-cache performance (22.45, 12.17, 30.80, 71.00, 61.26 respectively). On Needle-in-a-Haystack with an 8192-token cache budget, D2O reaches 94.48% accuracy at 50k context length versus 82.32% for H2O and 86.63% for SnapKV. In throughput experiments on Llama-3-8B, D2O achieves up to 3.04× speedup over the full model at the 2048+8192 setting, comparable to H2O's 3.10×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2O consistently outperforms all six baselines (StreamingLLM, H2O, RoCo, CaM, PyramidKV, SnapKV) across every model and task category in the LongBench evaluation. Against H2O (the strongest eviction-based baseline), D2O gains 2.21 points on MF-en and 3.07 points on TREC for Llama-3-8B, and 1.68 points on 2WikiMQA for Llama-2-7B. On Needle-in-a-Haystack (8192 cache, 50k context), D2O achieves 94.48% versus H2O's 82.32%, SnapKV's 86.63%, and CaM's 87.59%—a 12+ point margin over the next best method. StreamingLLM and RoCo show the largest degradation, with RoCo dropping to 8.95 on 2WikiMQA (Llama-3-8B) versus D2O's 12.79. CaM, which also uses token merging but with a uniform layer strategy, is the closest competitor yet still trails D2O by 2–4 points on most LongBench tasks. In throughput, D2O (3.04×) is marginally below H2O (3.10×) at the longest setting, reflecting the small computational cost of the EMA-based merging, but D2O's accuracy advantage justifies this trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

D2O builds on two complementary lines of KV cache compression: (1) attention-score-based eviction methods (H2O, StreamingLLM, RoCo) that discard low-importance tokens, and (2) token merging approaches (CaM, ToMe) that consolidate similar tokens. It departs from prior work in two key ways: at the layer level, it replaces uniform or fixed layer-wise budgets (as in PyramidKV) with an inverse-variance-softmax allocation that gives denser-attention shallow layers more cache; at the token level, it introduces an EMA-based similarity threshold that dynamically decides whether evicted tokens should be recalled and weighted-merged into the retained cache, rather than being irreversibly discarded. The theoretical grounding in information entropy and marginal information gain (Appendix A.9) provides a principled justification absent from most prior eviction heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

D2O offers a practical, training-free recipe that achieves ~3× throughput gains while preserving long-context quality across Llama, Falcon, and Mistral families, making it immediately deployable for production inference systems. The observation that compressed cache can occasionally outperform full cache (TruthfulQA) hints that eviction acts as a denoising mechanism, an underexplored angle for future work. Open questions include interaction with quantization and distillation, scalability to 100B+ models, and whether the EMA threshold and variance metric remain stable under distribution shift or multi-turn streaming settings. The added computational overhead of similarity computation and merging, though small, may become non-negligible at extreme sequence lengths or with very large batch sizes.
