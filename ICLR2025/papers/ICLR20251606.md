## Problem

While representation engineering has been shown to modulate behavioral traits like emotional valence and honesty in LLMs by steering the residual stream, it remains unclear whether reasoning ability is similarly encoded as a direction in the representational space. Prior research on LLM reasoning has focused on model outputs rather than internal states, leaving open whether the residual stream contains actionable information about a model's reasoning process that can be exploited to improve performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251606/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reasoning task prompt (from IOI, bAbI, or GSM8K) along with a pre-derived layer-specific control vector, which is added to the residual stream at the middle layer during inference with a tunable scale factor α. The output is a modified logit distribution over a constrained set of candidate answer tokens, from which the final reasoning answer is extracted. Control vectors are derived from hidden-state activations at the final token position across all layers, using contrastive pairs of successful and unsuccessful reasoning examples, with the intervention applied only at the middle layer to minimize disruption.

## Method

The paper derives control vectors by extracting residual stream activations at the final token position for each layer when the model processes contrastive pairs of successful and unsuccessful reasoning examples, then computes either a reading vector (mean of positive-minus-negative activations) or a PCA-based vector (first principal component of the contrastive differences). The control vector is applied as a simple additive perturbation c_ℓ·α to the residual stream at the middle layer during inference, requiring no additional training or fine-tuning. The key insight is that reasoning performance can be modulated in the same manner as other behavioral traits by steering the model toward its typical 'correct reasoning' state, and that control vectors derived from one reasoning task (e.g., bAbI deductive reasoning) can transfer to improve performance on a different task (e.g., GSM8K mathematical reasoning), suggesting a shared representational direction for reasoning-like information processing.

## Results

The paper reports logit-based accuracy improvements across all three models and tasks when PCA-derived control vectors are applied to the middle-layer residual stream. For Pythia-2.8B on IOI, accuracy improves slightly across conditions (ABBA, BABA, ABBA-Long, BABA-Long) with KL divergence increasing quadratically in both α directions. For Mistral-7B-Instruct, the intervention improves bAbI deductive reasoning accuracy and GSM8K mathematical reasoning accuracy (optimal at negative α, e.g., α = −2.6), with KL divergence and entropy showing positive correlation. A key cross-task finding is that a control vector derived from bAbI improves GSM8K accuracy almost identically to a GSM8K-derived vector, and vice versa, suggesting the vectors capture a shared 'reasoning' direction in the residual stream.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods; the primary comparison is the model's performance with versus without the control vector intervention (α = 0 vs. α ≠ 0) across a sweep of α ∈ [−1, 1] (or [−3, 3] for Mistral-7B-Instruct). The intervention consistently improves logit-based accuracy over the unmodified model on all three tasks for all three models, though the optimal α direction and magnitude vary by model and task (positive α for Pythia on IOI, negative α for Mistral on GSM8K). The paper also compares three control vector derivation schemes (simple reading vector, contrastive-pair reading vector, and PCA-based vector), finding that contrastive and PCA-based vectors outperform the simple reading vector. Cross-task generalization is assessed by applying a bAbI-derived vector to GSM8K and a GSM8K-derived vector to bAbI, both of which yield accuracy gains comparable to task-matched vectors, indicating the control vectors encode a task-agnostic reasoning direction rather than task-specific surface features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the representation engineering framework of Zou et al. (2023)—contrastive-pair and PCA-derived control vectors added to the residual stream—to the domain of reasoning tasks, extending prior applications that targeted emotional valence, honesty, or game-playing behavior. The conceptual grounding in the residual stream as the primary site of intervention follows Elhage et al. (2021), and the decision to intervene only at the middle layer is adopted from Templeton et al. (2024). The paper's positioning is explicitly empirical: it does not propose a new steering mechanism but tests whether the 'reasoning direction' is as actionable as previously identified behavioral directions, and adds KL-divergence and entropy analyses to characterize how the intervention reshapes the logit distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most notable finding is cross-task transfer: a control vector derived from bAbI (deductive) improves GSM8K (mathematical) accuracy and vice versa, suggesting a partially shared 'reasoning' direction in the residual stream rather than task-specific features. However, the improvements are consistently described as slight or minute, the models tested are small (≤7B), and the contrastive-pair construction for 'bad reasoning' is acknowledged as ad hoc (random character strings). Open questions include whether the effect scales to frontier models, whether more principled negative examples (e.g., chain-of-thought traces with specific error types) yield stronger vectors, and whether the observed modulation constitutes genuine reasoning improvement or merely shifts probability mass toward the correct token without altering the underlying computation.
