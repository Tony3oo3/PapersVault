## Problem

Existing unlearnable examples (UEs) are designed and evaluated for single-task image classification, yet when data is leaked or made public, its downstream use is unknown and may span diverse tasks such as semantic segmentation and depth estimation. The paper reveals that current UEs fail to exhibit cross-task unlearnability on multi-task benchmarks like Taskonomy, raising the fundamental question of how far we are from truly unlearnable data. This gap is compounded by the fact that existing evaluation metrics rely solely on test accuracy, which cannot explain the mechanism of unlearnability or generalize across task formulations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of trained models (clean and poisoned) with identical architecture, their respective training datasets, and a weight perturbation scaling factor ε (default 0.05 with ℓ₂ norm and 10 iterative steps for the max-search). For each layer, all other layers are frozen while computing the SAL, making the metric layer-local. The primary output is the Unlearnable Distance (UD), a single scalar ratio of average learnable-parameter counts between poisoned and clean training, where values near 0 indicate strong unlearnability and values ≥1 indicate the model is learning normally or even more than the clean baseline (as with adversarial-example-based TAP). A secondary output is the per-layer, per-epoch SAL trajectory and the K-Means-derived learnable threshold β(T) that partitions parameters into learnable and unlearnable groups.

## Method

The core insight is that unlearnability manifests in the loss landscape as parameters being trapped in flat regions where small updates produce negligible loss change, so only a small subset of 'key parameters' diverge between clean and poisoned training while most secondary parameters remain unlearned. To quantify this, the paper defines Sharpness-Aware Learnability (SAL) as the maximum absolute loss change within an ε-ball around each layer's parameters (with other layers frozen), directly borrowing the sharpness formulation from SAM. A learnable threshold β(T) is then derived via K-Means clustering of SAL values across all layers and epochs of the clean model, partitioning parameters into learnable and unlearnable groups. The Unlearnable Distance (UD) is the ratio of the time-averaged count of learnable parameters in the poisoned model to that in the clean model, providing a task-agnostic, training-process-based metric that does not depend on a specific downstream loss or test accuracy. The paper benchmarks six representative unlearnable methods (EM, REM, DC, TAP, LSP, OPS) across CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100, and multiple architectures (ResNet-18/50, SENet-18, ViT), showing that UD captures unlearnability more faithfully than test accuracy and reveals that stronger models and multi-task settings erode the effectiveness of existing UEs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, EM achieves the lowest Unlearnable Distance (UD = 0.187) and OPS the second lowest (UD = 0.157), while TAP has UD > 1 (1.639), indicating it is not truly unlearnable. On CIFAR-100, OPS achieves the best UD (0.009) and EM the second (0.111), whereas REM, DC, and LSP all exceed UD = 1. On ImageNet-100, EM achieves the lowest UD (0.011), but all other methods except OPS (1.430) have UD > 1, revealing that unlearnability is highly dataset- and model-dependent. Across architectures (Table 3), ViT shows notably higher UD than ResNet/SENet for the same UEs, confirming that stronger models are harder to render unlearnable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks six unlearnable methods (EM, REM, DC, TAP, LSP, OPS) using the proposed UD metric across three datasets and four architectures. EM (Huang et al., 2021) achieves the best UD on CIFAR-10 (0.187) and ImageNet-100 (0.011), while OPS (Wu et al., 2022) achieves the best UD on CIFAR-100 (0.009), confirming that shortcut-based and error-minimizing perturbations most effectively suppress parameter learnability. TAP (Fowl et al., 2021) consistently shows UD > 1 across all datasets (1.639, 1.213, 2.269), revealing that adversarial-example-based poisons are not truly unlearnable—they merely misdirect optimization rather than halting learning. REM (Fu et al., 2022) and DC (Feng et al., 2019) both exceed UD = 1 on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100, indicating their unlearnability degrades substantially on larger datasets. LSP (Yu et al., 2022) shows moderate UD (0.343 on CIFAR-10) but degrades to 1.796 on ImageNet-100. Among defenses, JPEG compression (Liu et al., 2023) most effectively increases UD (e.g., EM: 0.187→0.373), while UEraser (Qin et al., 2023) and MixUp (Zhang, 2017) also raise UD, and CutOut (DeVries, 2017) shows the weakest defense effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the loss landscape sharpness concept from Sharpness-Aware Minimization (Foret et al., 2020) and related generalization studies (Keskar et al., 2016; Jiang et al., 2019) to explain and quantify unlearnability, shifting the analytical lens from test-time accuracy to the training-time parameter update process. It builds directly on the unlearnable examples literature (EM, OPS, TAP, LSP, DC, REM) but departs from prior evaluations that rely solely on single-task classification accuracy, instead proposing parameter-level (SAL) and data-level (UD) metrics grounded in optimization geometry. The multi-task evaluation setting draws on Taskonomy and ModSquad, extending the unlearnability question beyond the classification-only framing that dominates prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that existing UEs fail to exhibit unlearnability in multi-task scenarios (e.g., semantic segmentation, depth estimation) exposes a critical practical gap: data owners cannot assume single-task protection generalizes to arbitrary downstream uses of leaked data. The UD metric reveals that unlearnability is model-dependent (ViT shows substantially higher UD than ResNet), complicating the design of universally effective protections. Open questions include whether truly multi-task unlearnable perturbations can be constructed given inherent task conflicts, whether SAL/UD scale efficiently to very large models, and whether the optimization-based perspective can guide the design of next-generation unlearnable methods rather than merely evaluating them.
