## Problem

Current diffusion-model watermarking methods (Tree-Ring, RingID) embed identifiable Fourier patterns in the initial noise, which systematically distorts the distribution of generated images and enables steganalysis-based forgery and removal attacks. Even distortion-free single-noise approaches become vulnerable when many images sharing the same noise are examined as a set. The paper addresses the need for a watermarking scheme that is simultaneously distortion-free (indistinguishable from non-watermarked images to any observer other than the model owner) and robust to both removal and forgery attacks at scale.

## Input / Output

During generation, the method takes a text prompt, a secret cryptographic salt, and private diffusion model weights (Stable Diffusion 2.1, 50 DDIM steps) to produce a watermarked image; the watermark is the specific initial noise vector (drawn from a pool of N=100,000) plus a Fourier group identifier. During detection, the method takes a candidate image and inverts it through the private model to recover an approximate initial noise, then performs a two-stage search (Fourier group matching followed by cosine-similarity comparison within the group) to output a watermarked/not-watermarked verdict along with the identified noise index. The method also supports watermarking non-synthetic images via diffusion inpainting, where the input is a natural image and the output is an inpainted watermarked version.

## Method

WIND (Watermarking with Indistinguishable and Robust Noise for Diffusion Models) exploits the observation that the initial noise in a DDIM diffusion process is itself a distortion-free watermark: the noise reconstructed by inverting a generated image with private weights has cosine similarity ~0.89 with the true initial noise, versus ~0.00 for random noise, yielding a detection threshold with p < 10⁻¹⁹ false-positive rate. To make detection efficient over a large pool of N initial noises, WIND partitions them into M groups and embeds a group identifier as a Fourier-space pattern (using the RingID technique) into the initial noise before diffusion. Detection proceeds in two stages: (1) recover the group index from the Fourier pattern in the reconstructed noise, reducing the search space from N to N/M, and (2) compare the reconstructed noise against all N/M candidates in that group via cosine similarity. Cryptographic security is ensured by generating each initial noise from a pseudorandom seed computed as hash(i*, s) with a secret salt s, so that compromising one noise reveals nothing about others (Theorem 4.1). Two variants are provided: WIND_fast searches only within the identified group, while WIND_full falls back to an exhaustive search over all N noises if no match is found, trading speed for additional robustness against attacks that corrupt the Fourier pattern.

## Results

WIND_full with 2048 groups (100,000 total initial noises) achieves 96.9% average watermark detection accuracy across seven image transformation attacks (rotation, JPEG, crop & scale, blur, noise, brightness), substantially outperforming RingID (81.9% at 2048 keys) and Tree-Ring (6.6% at 2048 keys). The method is especially strong on crop & scale (C&S) attacks, where WIND_full_2048 maintains 93.0% detection while RingID drops to 8.0%. WIND also achieves the best image quality with an FID of 24.33 on MS-COCO-2017, lower than Tree-Ring (25.93), RingID (26.13), and traditional methods (24.51–25.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WIND_full_2048 (96.9% avg) outperforms RingID_2048 (81.9% avg) by 15 percentage points, with the largest gap on crop & scale attacks (93.0% vs. 8.0%), where RingID's Fourier-pattern-based multi-key identification collapses as the number of keys grows. Against Tree-Ring, WIND's advantage is overwhelming: even Tree-Ring's best setting (32 keys, 40.4% avg) is 56.5 points below WIND_full_2048, and Tree-Ring degrades to near-zero detection (6.6%) at 2048 keys. Notably, WIND operates with 100,000 distinct initial noises versus only 32–2048 keys for the baselines, making the comparison more demanding in terms of key space. On image quality, WIND achieves the lowest FID (24.33) among all compared methods, indicating that the two-stage watermarking introduces less distributional distortion than Tree-Ring (25.93) or RingID (26.13). WIND also demonstrates superior robustness to regeneration attacks, maintaining 100% detection rate even after 50 iterative regeneration steps, whereas prior methods are shown to be vulnerable to such attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

WIND repurposes two existing components from the diffusion watermarking literature in a novel two-stage architecture. The core watermark is the full initial noise vector, following the distortion-free insight of Gaussian Shading (Yang et al., 2024b), while the Fourier-space pattern from Tree-Ring (Wen et al., 2023) / RingID (Ci et al., 2024) is demoted from being the watermark itself to serving merely as a group identifier that narrows the detection search space from O(N) to O(N/M). This departure is significant: prior methods treat the Fourier pattern as the security-critical element, making it vulnerable to steganalysis attacks that exploit distribution shifts, whereas WIND's security rests on the high-dimensional noise vector whose correlation structure is far harder to exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical and timely vulnerability: steganalysis attacks (Yang et al., 2024a) that can forge or remove Tree-Ring and RingID watermarks by estimating distribution shifts across image sets. WIND's cryptographic security guarantee (Theorem 4.1) and its demonstrated resilience to 50 iterations of regeneration attacks (100% detection) make it a strong candidate for production deployment by model owners. However, open questions remain: detection still requires private model weights (ruling out third-party verification), the 22-second detection time may be prohibitive at scale, and the security argument ultimately rests on the assumption that inverting the diffusion model is as hard as stealing it—an assumption that could erode as inversion techniques improve. The extension to non-synthetic images via inpainting is a practical differentiator but introduces quality trade-offs (SSIM 0.768 vs. 0.98+ for traditional methods).
