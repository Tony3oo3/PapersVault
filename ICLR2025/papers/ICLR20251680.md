## Problem

Standard online bilateral trade assumes the platform has no information about the item being sold, which is unrealistic in practice where product features are typically available. This paper introduces the feature-based online bilateral trade problem, where valuations are linear functions of an adversarially chosen context vector, and characterizes the achievable regret under different combinations of feedback quality (one-bit vs. two-bit) and budget-balance strictness (strong per-round vs. global).

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner observes a d-dimensional feature vector x_t (chosen adversarially) and posts a single price p_t to both the seller and buyer (strong budget balance) or a pair (p_t, q_t) (global budget balance). The learner receives either two-bit feedback (separate acceptance/rejection signals from seller and buyer) or one-bit feedback (only whether the trade occurred). Valuations follow x_t^T θ + ξ_t with unknown θ and bounded i.i.d. noise. The output is a sequence of posted prices with a regret guarantee relative to the best context-dependent pricing policy in hindsight.

## Method

The paper develops three algorithmic contributions. For noiseless valuations, it adapts the ELLIPSOIDPRICING algorithm by maintaining separate Lowner-John ellipsoidal uncertainty sets for seller and buyer parameters, achieving O(log T) regret. For noisy valuations, it first decomposes the expected gain-from-trade into four estimable components (F^s, D^b, I, J) via a contextual extension of a key lemma, then designs a Scouting Bandit with Information Pooling (SBIP) algorithm that runs successive elimination while pooling feedback across different context values sharing the same price increment, achieving a minimax-optimal Õ(T^{2/3}) regret. Finally, it provides a black-box reduction from two-bit strong-budget-balance to one-bit global-budget-balance by simulating individual feedback bits through asymmetric price postings (p, −P) or (P, p), funded by an initial budget-collection phase whose cost depends on a market liquidity parameter α.

## Results

The paper establishes three main regret guarantees for feature-based online bilateral trade with adversarial contexts. In the noiseless setting with two-bit feedback and strong budget balance, the EllipsoidPricing algorithm (EP-BT) achieves O(log T) regret (Proposition 1). In the noisy setting with two-bit feedback, the Scouting Bandit with Information Pooling (SBIP) algorithm achieves Õ(T^{2/3}) regret (Theorem 2), which is minimax-optimal up to poly-logarithmic factors and dimension dependence, matching the Ω(T^{2/3}) lower bound for the simpler non-contextual problem. A black-box reduction (Theorem 3) extends the two-bit EOC algorithm to one-bit feedback with global budget balance, yielding Õ(T^{3/4}) regret when the market parameter α = Ω(1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central theoretical comparison is against the non-contextual stochastic bilateral trade problem of Cesa-Bianchi et al. (2024), which achieves Õ(T^{2/3}) regret without features. The SBIP algorithm matches this rate in the contextual setting (Theorem 2), demonstrating that the feature-based problem is no harder in terms of regret scaling, despite comparing against a stronger context-dependent benchmark. In the noiseless setting, the O(log T) regret of EP-BT parallels the Õ(d² log T) guarantee of Cohen et al. (2020) for feature-based dynamic pricing, adapted here to maintain separate ellipsoidal uncertainty sets for seller and buyer. The one-bit reduction (Theorem 3) introduces a new trade-off not present in prior work: relaxing from strong (per-round) to global budget balance enables operation under one-bit feedback, with regret depending on the market parameter α that measures trade opportunity frequency. When α = Ω(1), the one-bit regret matches the two-bit EOC rate of Õ(T^{3/4}); in the worst case (α → 0), the rate degrades to Õ(T^{7/8}) in the noisy setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the feature-based online bilateral trade problem by combining two previously separate research threads: the online bilateral trade framework of Cesa-Bianchi et al. (2021, 2024), which handles non-contextual i.i.d. valuations with limited feedback, and the feature-based dynamic pricing literature of Cohen et al. (2020), which uses Lowner-John ellipsoids to handle adversarial contexts. The key technical departure is that bilateral trade requires maintaining separate uncertainty sets for seller and buyer valuations and decomposing the expected gain-from-trade into estimable components (via a contextual extension of the Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2024 decomposition lemma), rather than estimating a single demand function. The one-bit feedback reduction further builds on the global budget balance notion of Bernasconi et al. (2024) to trade off feedback granularity against per-round budget constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the contextual stochastic bilateral trade problem is no harder in regret terms than its non-contextual counterpart (matching the Ω(T^{2/3}) lower bound), despite comparing against a stronger context-dependent benchmark — a result that was not obvious given the need to estimate reward functions across a range of price increments. The one-bit reduction reveals a fundamental structural trade-off: relaxing from strong (per-round) to global budget balance enables operation under strictly less informative feedback, which has practical implications for marketplaces where only trade/no-trade outcomes are observable. Open questions include whether the T^{2/3} rate can be improved with richer feedback models, whether the information-pooling technique extends to multi-item or multi-agent settings, and how the results behave under adversarial (rather than oblivious) context generation.
