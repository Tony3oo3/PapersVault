## Problem

Pareto stationarity (PS), the standard first-order necessary condition for Pareto optimality in multi-objective optimization, can yield infinitely many sub-optimal solutions even in convex settings when the function-variable dependency structure is sparse. Existing gradient-based MOO methods like MGDA treat all variables as a monolithic block, ignoring this sparsity, and can get trapped at spurious PS points that are not Pareto optimal. No prior work systematically exploits the function-variable dependency structure to refine the stationary condition.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an m×d binary adjacency matrix A encoding which of the m objective functions depend on which of the d variables, along with the objective functions themselves, initial weights w ∈ R^d, and a learning rate η. The REFINED_PARTITION subroutine (run once) consumes A and outputs a partition P = {P_1, …, P_k} of the variable indices, discovered by iteratively detecting and contracting cycles in the underlying bipartite graph. The final output is the optimized weight vector w* that satisfies refined Pareto stationarity (RPS) w.r.t. P, which is guaranteed to be Pareto stationary and, under partial strict convexity, Pareto optimal.

## Method

The core mechanism is a two-stage procedure: (1) REFINED_PARTITION detects cycles in the bipartite graph of function-variable dependencies, merges all variables in each cycle into a single block, contracts the graph, and repeats until the graph is acyclic, yielding a partition P in O(d(m+d)) time; (2) RP-MGDA then applies the MGDA minimum-norm direction computation independently within each block P_j (solving a smaller dual subproblem per block) and updates all blocks in parallel. This block-wise refinement ensures that the stationary condition is enforced per-block rather than globally, eliminating spurious PS solutions that arise from variables being coupled only through shared objectives. Theoretically, RPS is sandwiched between Pareto optimality and Pareto stationarity (Theorem 1), reduces exactly to Pareto optimality under partial strict convexity (Theorem 2), and RP-MGDA achieves the standard O(1/√T) convergence rate (Theorem 3) while being computationally cheaper than vanilla MGDA since the costly dual subproblem is solved on smaller blocks.

## Results

Across three ML benchmark scenarios (personalized federated learning on MNIST/CIFAR-10, hierarchical classification on CIFAR-10, and multi-objective regression on California Housing), RP-MGDA consistently achieves lower or equal objective values compared to vanilla MGDA. In the chain-structure experiment, RP-MGDA improves upon MGDA's stationary solutions in 18 out of 20 random trials, with lower average MSE across all 4 objectives and substantially smaller standard deviations. In the personalized federated learning setting, RP-MGDA converges faster and reaches better final cross-entropy losses, particularly for objectives f2, f3, and f4. Theoretically, RP-MGDA maintains the O(1/√T) convergence rate of MGDA while reducing the per-step dual subproblem size through block-wise parallel updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is vanilla MGDA (Fliege et al., 2019; Désidéri, 2012), which treats all variables as a single block. RP-MGDA wins or ties on all three ML benchmarks: in personalized federated learning (MNIST and CIFAR-10), RP-MGDA achieves lower final cross-entropy losses for 3 out of 4 objectives and converges faster; in hierarchical classification, objectives 1 and 2 are nearly identical while objective 3 converges faster and to a lower loss with RP-MGDA; in the chain-structure regression task, RP-MGDA yields lower mean MSE with tighter variance across 20 seeds. The second baseline is PCGrad+ (a looped variant of PCGrad from Yu et al., 2020), compared in the PFL setting where RP-PCGrad+ outperforms PCGrad+ in final cross-entropy losses on both MNIST and CIFAR-10. Notably, MGDA cannot improve upon RP-MGDA's stationary solutions (guaranteed by Theorem 1), while RP-MGDA can post-process and improve MGDA's outputs, establishing a strict dominance relationship in the solution space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MGDA lineage (Mukai 1980; Fliege & Svaiter 2000; Désidéri 2012) and its convergence analysis (Fliege et al. 2019) by introducing a graph-theoretic refinement: instead of treating all variables as a single block, it partitions them based on cycles in the function-variable bipartite graph. The MTL formulation of Sener & Koltun (2018) is identified as a special case of the more general partitioning framework. The paper positions itself as the first to systematically refine Pareto stationarity by exploiting sparse function-variable structure, complementing rather than replacing existing gradient aggregation schemes like PCGrad, CAGrad, or Nash-MTL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification that Pareto stationarity is insufficient even in convex settings when strict convexity fails due to sparse dependencies is a clean theoretical gap that practitioners of MGDA-based methods (MTL, federated learning, fair ML) can address with minimal overhead. The RPS concept provides a provably tighter solution guarantee sandwiched between Pareto optimality and Pareto stationarity, and the REFINED_PARTITION procedure is a one-time O(d(m+d)) cost. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to stochastic settings (where per-step descent is not guaranteed), whether it composes well with other gradient aggregation schemes beyond PCGrad+, and whether the partitioning benefit persists at very large scale where the bipartite graph becomes dense.
