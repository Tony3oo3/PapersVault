## Problem

Equivariant diffusion models for molecular generation have relied on intrinsically equivariant neural networks, where every layer is individually constrained to respect group symmetries. This imposes architectural restrictions that degrade training dynamics, increase computational cost, and prevent the use of highly scalable off-the-shelf architectures like Transformers. The paper identifies that stochastic symmetrisation—a technique for obtaining equivariance without per-layer constraints—had not been applied to generative modelling, where the more complex condition of stochastic (rather than deterministic) equivariance is required.

## Input / Output

At sampling time, the model starts from a Gaussian noise vector z_T drawn from the center-of-mass-free subspace U ⊂ R^{N×(3+d)} and iteratively denoises it over T steps to produce a molecule. Each atom i is represented by a 3D coordinate x^(i) and a continuous feature vector h^(i) encoding atom type and charge, with the constraint that the center of mass is at the origin. The model is applied to molecules with up to 9 heavy atoms (QM9) or up to 181 atoms (GEOM-Drugs), with hydrogen atoms modeled explicitly.

## Method

SYMDIFF constructs an E(3)-equivariant reverse diffusion process by stochastically symmetrising an S_N-equivariant kernel k_θ (a Diffusion Transformer) with respect to O(3) rotations. The symmetrisation procedure samples a rotation R from a learned kernel γ_θ, applies R^T to the input, passes it through k_θ, then applies R to the output—requiring only a single forward pass with no Monte Carlo averaging. The rotation kernel γ_θ is obtained recursively by composing the Haar measure on O(3) with an S_N-invariant neural network f_θ (also a DiT), so no intrinsically O(3)-equivariant components are needed. Training uses a Jensen's inequality lower bound on the ELBO that, under the ε-parameterisation, reduces to the standard diffusion loss ||ε − R·ε_θ(R^T·z_t)||² with R sampled from γ_θ, making the objective tractable and compatible with reparametrisable gradients. The paper proves that data augmentation with Haar-distributed rotations is a special case of SYMDIFF (when γ_θ is fixed to the Haar measure), showing SYMDIFF is a strict generalisation that also deploys the learned augmentation at sampling time to guarantee exact stochastic equivariance.

## Results

On QM9, SymDiff (31.2M params) achieves 95.75% validity, 87.49% molecular stability, and NLL of -129.35, substantially outperforming the EDM baseline (91.90% validity, 82.00% molecular stability, NLL -110.70). The larger SymDiff* (117.8M params) reaches 96.36% validity and 89.65% molecular stability, matching or exceeding MUDiff and GeoLDM on most metrics. On GEOM-Drugs, SymDiff achieves 99.27% validity and 86.16% atom stability with NLL -301.21, far surpassing EDM (81.3% atom stability, NLL -137.1). SymDiff is also significantly more computationally efficient: 27.2 s/epoch vs. 88.8 s/epoch for EDM, and 0.09 s sampling time vs. 0.27 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymDiff outperforms EDM (Hoogeboom et al., 2022) on all QM9 metrics except uniqueness: validity improves from 91.90% to 95.75%, molecular stability from 82.00% to 87.49%, and NLL from -110.70 to -129.35, while also being ~3× faster per epoch (27.2 s vs. 88.8 s). Against the more sophisticated baselines, SymDiff* beats GeoLDM on validity (96.36% vs. 93.80%) and MUDiff on validity (96.36% vs. 95.30%), though MUDiff retains a slight edge on molecular stability (89.90% vs. 89.65%) and uniqueness (99.10% vs. 97.66%). On GEOM-Drugs, SymDiff achieves 99.27% validity (matching GeoLDM's 99.3%) and 86.16% atom stability (beating EDM's 81.3% but slightly below END's 87.8%), with a dramatically better NLL of -301.21 vs. EDM's -137.1. The paper also shows that SymDiff outperforms both its Haar-measure-only variant (SymDiff-H) and the data-augmentation baseline (DiT-Aug) on all metrics except uniqueness, demonstrating that the learned symmetrisation component γ_θ provides benefits beyond simple augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SYMDIFF builds on Cornish (2024)'s stochastic symmetrisation framework in Markov categories, extending it from deterministic functions to Markov kernels and, for the first time, to generative modelling. It departs from the dominant paradigm of equivariant diffusion (EDM, GeoDiff, SE(3)-Diff) that requires every layer of the denoising network to be intrinsically equivariant, instead showing that a generic S_N-equivariant backbone (e.g., a Diffusion Transformer) combined with a learned stochastic symmetrisation step suffices for full E(3)-equivariance. The paper also unifies data augmentation as a special case (Proposition 2), positioning SYMDIFF as a strict generalisation of the common practice of applying random rotations during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that equivariant molecular generation can now leverage highly scalable Transformer architectures (DiT) without the complex equivariant parameterisations and message-passing overhead of E(3)-GNNs, yielding both better accuracy and lower compute cost. The approach is general in principle (any compact group, any generative paradigm), and the paper sketches extensions to score and flow matching, though these remain unvalidated empirically. Open questions include whether the learned γ_θ component is necessary at scale (SymDiff-H with Haar measure alone is close), how the method behaves for non-compact groups, and whether the efficiency advantage persists at much larger molecule sizes or for protein generation.
