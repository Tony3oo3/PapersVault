## Problem

Existing anomaly detection methods struggle to generalize to unseen anomalies: supervised approaches overfit to known patterns, GAN/VAE-based synthetic anomaly generation suffers from model collapse and vanishing gradients without a strategic sampling mechanism, and reconstruction-based detectors can be fooled when neural networks memorize anomalies. No prior framework provides a principled way to generate synthetic anomalies that are simultaneously diverse and adversarially challenging to a detection model.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes tabular observation vectors x ∈ R^P from 57 ADBench datasets (spanning domains from image features to NLP), operating in a semi-supervised setting where only 10–40% of data is available for training and anomalies constitute just 1–15% of the dataset. The output is a probabilistic anomaly score in [0,1] produced by the Mixture of Mamba Experts, with only the top-k (typically 2–3) experts activated per sample, keeping inference time comparable to lightweight baselines despite the large effective model capacity.

## Method

Swift Hydra comprises two modules. The Self-Reinforcing Generative Module uses an RL agent (trained via PPO) that operates on the latent space of a Conditional VAE, with a reward function balancing dataset entropy (diversity) against detector evasion (challengingness), controlled by a decay factor γ that shifts the policy from exploration to exploitation across episodes. A key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) proves that gradient descent in the latent space yields feasible actions during early training, eliminating the need for random input-space perturbation. The Inference Module deploys a Mixture of Mamba Experts (MoME) where multiple lightweight Mamba state-space models each specialize in a data cluster, routed by a top-k gating network; a novel probabilistic cluster-assignment scheme (Eq. 6) with cluster-size-aware penalties prevents the winner-take-all collapse, and Theorem 3 guarantees MoME achieves zero training error and test error no worse than a single over-parameterized detector.

## Results

On the ADBench benchmark (57 datasets), Swift Hydra (MoME) achieves the best average AUC-ROC across all train/test ratios: 0.93 (40/60%), 0.91 (30/70%), 0.90 (20/80%), and 0.87 (10/90%), outperforming DTE (0.82–0.78), Rejex (0.78–0.74), and ADGym (0.86–0.77). In terms of total inference time (TIF) across all 57 datasets, Swift Hydra (MoME) achieves 4.01–5.84 seconds, comparable to DTE (4.02–4.42 s) and far faster than Swift Hydra (Single) (13.11–18.52 s) and ADGym (6.12–9.14 s). Notably, even with only 10% of the training data, Swift Hydra (MoME) still outperforms the strongest baseline (ADGym) by 10 percentage points in AUC-ROC while maintaining a shorter inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Swift Hydra (MoME) consistently outperforms all three baselines on AUC-ROC across every train/test ratio. Against DTE (diffusion-based, ICLR 2024), Swift Hydra (MoME) leads by 11 percentage points at 40/60% (0.93 vs. 0.82) and by 9 points at 10/90% (0.87 vs. 0.78), while matching DTE's inference time (4.01 s vs. 4.02 s at 40/60%). Against Rejex (rejection-based, 2023), the gap is even wider: 15 points at 40/60% (0.93 vs. 0.78), and Rejex is the only method with a slightly shorter TIF (3.89 s vs. 4.01 s). Against ADGym (2023), Swift Hydra (MoME) leads by 7 points at 40/60% (0.93 vs. 0.86) and by 10 points at 10/90% (0.87 vs. 0.77), while also being roughly 1.5× faster in inference (4.01 s vs. 6.12 s at 40/60%). The single-detector variant of Swift Hydra achieves competitive AUC-ROC (0.91 at 40/60%) but at a 3× inference cost, underscoring the importance of the MoME architecture for practical deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) generative AI for anomaly detection (VAE- and GAN-based synthetic anomaly generation, e.g., Oversampling GAN, VAE-Geometry), (2) RL-guided generative modeling (drawing from ReST and RLHF in LLMs), and (3) Mixture-of-Experts architectures (Shazeer et al., 2017; Chen et al., 2022). It departs from prior generative anomaly detection by replacing adversarial or reconstruction-based training of the generator with an RL agent that operates in the C-VAE latent space, guided by a reward balancing entropy and detector evasion. The inference module further departs from single-detector designs by introducing a Mixture of Mamba Experts (MoME) with a probabilistic cluster-assignment scheme to avoid the winner-take-all problem, and by establishing a theoretical lower bound (Theorem 2) showing that no single detector can overcome the inherent overlap between normal and anomalous clusters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RL-guided C-VAE + MoME combination offers a practical path to anomaly detection with limited labeled anomalies and fast inference, which is critical for real-time monitoring in cybersecurity and industrial systems. The theoretical results (feasible-action guarantee, single-detector lower bound, MoME error bound) provide a principled justification for the architecture choices rather than purely empirical tuning. Open questions include whether the RL training loop scales to high-dimensional modalities (images, video, time series beyond tabular), how sensitive the framework is to the number of episodes and the entropy-evasion trade-off hyperparameter γ, and whether the Mamba-based experts can be replaced by even lighter architectures without accuracy loss. The evaluation is confined to ADBench (57 tabular datasets), so generalization to other modalities remains untested.
