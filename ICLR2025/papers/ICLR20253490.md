## Problem

Existing model compression techniques (pruning, quantization) require fine-tuning with training data to restore accuracy after removing parameters, and recent data-free merging methods like IFM fail to maintain performance at high sparsity levels due to variance collapse or explosion in activation statistics. The paper addresses the gap of compressing networks by merging structurally similar neurons while preserving internal data statistics, entirely without access to training data or any fine-tuning step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253490/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained neural network (ResNet, VGG, or LLaMA architectures) and applies layer-wise compression by merging similar channels, producing a smaller network with reduced channel count per layer. For CNNs with BatchNorm layers, the method additionally updates running mean/variance statistics; for LLMs without BatchNorm, only the clustering and merging steps are applied. Sparsity is applied uniformly across all layers for CNNs, while for LLaMA-7B it uses differentiated sparsity ratios (20%/50% for decoder blocks 22–29, 10%/40% for blocks 11–21) in attention and feed-forward layers.

## Method

Model folding compresses a network in three phases: (1) k-means clustering of weight rows within each layer to identify structurally similar neurons, which the paper proves minimizes the Frobenius-norm approximation error of the low-rank factorization W ≈ UM; (2) merging by replacing all rows in a cluster with their centroid, with the clustering matrix C = U(UᵀU)⁻¹Uᵀ applied to both the current layer's weights and the next layer's transposed weights to account for inter-layer dependencies; and (3) data statistics repair to prevent variance collapse or explosion. Two data-free repair variants are introduced: Fold-AR estimates mean within-cluster correlation from weight vectors assuming independent inputs and applies a scaling factor to each centroid, while Fold-DIR uses Deep Inversion to synthesize a single batch of class-conditional images and performs one forward pass to update BatchNorm running statistics. The theoretical framework shows that clustering on a combined matrix [W_l | W_{l+1}ᵀ] (or including BatchNorm diagonal matrices) jointly minimizes the total approximation error across adjacent layers.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B at 20% structured sparsity, model folding (data-free, no fine-tuning) achieves 50.17 average zero-shot accuracy and 13.33 WikiText-2 perplexity, compared to 65.55/5.68 for the uncompressed model. On ResNet18/CIFAR-10 at 70% sparsity, folded models maintain over 80% top-1 accuracy while structured magnitude pruning drops to near random chance. Model folding outperforms IFM by a significant margin at high sparsity levels across ResNet18 and VGG11 on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, and surpasses INN (which caps at 54% sparsity) on the original IFM/INN evaluation protocol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IFM (Chen et al., 2023), model folding significantly outperforms at higher sparsity levels and on larger networks (ResNet18, VGG11) across CIFAR-10 and ImageNet; in the original IFM protocol (only last two blocks pruned), IFM has a slight edge at low sparsity but folding dominates at higher sparsity. Against INN (Solodskikh et al., 2023), folding is significantly better, as INN struggles beyond 54% sparsity. Against structured magnitude pruning (Cai et al., 2020; Yin et al., 2022), folding wins by a wide margin that grows with sparsity—at 70% on CIFAR-10, folded ResNet18 retains >80% accuracy while pruned models fall to near random. On LLaMA-7B at 20% sparsity, folding (50.17 avg) trails data-driven methods like Wanda_sp (63.00), FLAP (61.79), and SliceGPT (56.11) by 5–13 points, but substantially beats magnitude pruning (35.36) and is competitive with LLM-Pruner (54.27) and ShortGPT (55.09) without requiring any data or gradients. In the model-merging setting (Appendix C), folding outperforms both Weight Matching (WM) and ZipIt! on both separate-task and same-task merging, e.g., 0.75 vs. 0.48 (WM) and 0.74 (ZipIt!) on ResNet18 for separate tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weight-matching / model-merging line of work (Ainsworth et al., 2023; Jordan et al., 2022) from pairwise neuron alignment between two models to k-means clustering of multiple similar neurons within a single network, providing a theoretical justification that k-means minimizes the Frobenius-norm approximation error for the folding problem. It departs from IFM (Chen et al., 2023), which uses iterative greedy pairwise merging, by showing that global clustering is both theoretically optimal and empirically superior, especially at high sparsity. The data-free statistics repair (Fold-AR, Fold-DIR) adapts the REPAIR framework of Jordan et al. (2022) to eliminate the need for training data, using either an independence assumption on input correlations or a single batch of Deep Inversion–synthesized images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Data-free, fine-tuning-free compression is practically important for edge deployment and privacy-sensitive settings where the original training data is unavailable or inaccessible. The clean theoretical link between k-means and optimal low-rank weight approximation gives practitioners a principled, scalable clustering step, and the variance-ratio diagnostic provides a useful tool for monitoring compression quality. However, the method does not optimize per-layer sparsity, its effectiveness is limited in low-redundancy networks, and the LLM results (e.g., WikiText2 perplexity rising from 5.68 to 13.33 at 20% sparsity) remain noticeably behind data-driven baselines, leaving open the question of whether the approach can close that gap for transformer architectures without BatchNorm.
