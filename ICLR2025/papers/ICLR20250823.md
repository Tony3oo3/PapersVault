## Problem

Existing interpretability methods for multimodal image-text models like CLIP suffer from randomness (noise sampling in IBP-based methods), hyperparameter sensitivity (the β trade-off in M2IB), inability to capture negative feature contributions, and reliance on additional samples or specific model structures. This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a theoretically grounded framework that produces deterministic, hyperparameter-free attributions for both image and text modalities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single image-text pair (image x_I ∈ R^n and text x_T ∈ R^m) encoded by a pretrained CLIP ViT-B/32 model and produces per-dimension attribution maps A(x) ∈ R^|x| for each modality, computed at a chosen intermediate layer (default: layer 9) over num_steps=10 integration steps. Unlike KL-divergence-based methods, the attribution values can be negative, explicitly identifying feature dimensions that reduce the image-text alignment score. No additional samples, downstream task labels, or auxiliary models are required.

## Method

The Narrowing Information Bottleneck Theory replaces M2IB's per-dimension λ vector with a single scalar λ ∈ [0,1] applied uniformly to all feature dimensions, with zero-mean Gaussian noise (no (1−λ) weighting). Three theorems underpin the approach: (1) mutual information I(z̃(λ), x) is monotonically increasing in λ, so λ=0 fully closes the bottleneck and λ=1 fully opens it; (2) as σ²→0, the noise-induced randomness vanishes, making the computation deterministic; (3) by the fundamental theorem of calculus, the total attribution decomposes as A(z_i) = Σ_c ∫₀¹ ∂I(z̃(λ),Y)/∂z̃_ic(λ) · ∂z̃_ic(λ)/∂λ dλ, where I(z̃(λ),Y) is the cosine similarity between the bottlenecked image representation and the text representation. This integral formulation eliminates the β hyperparameter entirely, permits negative attributions for features that harm alignment, and satisfies both the Sensitivity and Implementation Invariance axioms.

## Results

NIB achieves the lowest Image Confidence Drop across all three datasets (0.9439 on Conceptual Captions, 0.9012 on ImageNet, 1.4495 on Flickr8k) and the lowest Text Confidence Drop (0.2705, 0.4193, 0.4562 respectively), substantially outperforming the closest competitor M2IB. NIB also attains the highest or near-highest Confidence Increase scores (e.g., 56.1 Text Conf Incr on ImageNet vs. 25.4 for M2IB) and the best or near-best FPS (1.58–2.45), corresponding to the paper's claimed average improvements of 9% in image interpretability, 58.83% in text interpretability, and 63.95% in processing speed over state-of-the-art baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NIB dominates the closest multimodal-specific baseline M2IB on nearly every metric: it reduces Text Confidence Drop by 61–84% (e.g., 0.2705 vs. 1.706 on Conceptual Captions) and boosts Text Confidence Increase by up to 121% (56.1 vs. 25.4 on ImageNet), while also running ~2.4× faster (FPS 1.58 vs. 0.66). Against unimodal methods, NIB is dramatically superior: Grad-CAM, Saliency Maps, and MFABA all score 10–12× worse on Image Confidence Drop and near-zero on Image Confidence Increase, confirming that unimodal attribution methods fail to transfer to multimodal settings. RISE is competitive on Text Confidence Increase (43.95 on Conceptual Captions, 46.8 on ImageNet) but is 15–25× slower (FPS ≈ 0.1) and still trails NIB on all Confidence Drop metrics. Chefer et al. (2021) slightly edges NIB on FPS for ImageNet (2.79 vs. 2.45) and Flickr8k (2.46 vs. 2.20) and on Text Confidence Increase for Conceptual Captions (45.3 vs. 43.95), but NIB maintains a clear advantage on all Confidence Drop metrics and Image Confidence Increase. FastIG and MFABA, despite being axiomatic attribution methods, perform poorly in the multimodal context (e.g., MFABA scores 0.1 on Image Confidence Increase for Flickr8k), underscoring the need for methods specifically designed for image-text representation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends M2IB (Wang et al., 2023), which applied the Information Bottleneck Principle to multimodal CLIP interpretability, by re-deriving the bottleneck framework with a single scalar λ controlling all feature dimensions simultaneously rather than per-dimension optimization. It departs from M2IB by eliminating the stochastic noise sampling (via the σ²→0 limit) and removing the β hyperparameter that balanced two competing mutual information terms, replacing the optimization problem with a deterministic integral over λ∈[0,1]. The theoretical grounding draws on the original IBP (Tishby et al., 2000) and the axiomatic attribution framework (Sundararajan et al., 2017), while the practical design of the attribution integral parallels Integrated Gradients-style path integration used in FastIG (Hesse et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Deterministic, hyperparameter-free attribution for CLIP is practically important for high-stakes deployments (medical imaging, content moderation) where reproducibility and trust in explanations are non-negotiable. The ability to produce negative attributions—identifying features that actively harm the image-text alignment—opens a new diagnostic angle that prior bottleneck methods could not capture. However, the evaluation is limited to a single CLIP architecture (ViT-B/32) and three standard datasets, leaving open whether the theory and performance gains transfer to larger ViT variants, other multimodal architectures (e.g., BLIP, LLaVA), or more complex downstream tasks. The theoretical proofs, while clean, are somewhat thin (Theorem 3 reduces to the fundamental theorem of calculus), and the 'negative property' concept lacks deeper empirical validation beyond a single qualitative figure.
