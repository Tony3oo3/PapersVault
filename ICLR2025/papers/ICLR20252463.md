## Problem

Autoregressive visual generation models lag behind diffusion models in two critical ways: their discrete tokenizers produce poor reconstructions that cap generation quality, and no existing AR model can directly synthesize 1024×1024 images. The paper asks whether an AR model can match diffusion-model generation quality while retaining its inherent computational efficiency advantage.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, HART takes a natural-language prompt (encoded by Qwen2-1.5B) and directly produces a 1024×1024 RGB image in a single forward pass without super-resolution; it also supports 256px and 512px outputs. For class-conditional generation, a single class label replaces the text prompt. The final image is decoded from the sum of discrete VAR tokens and continuous residual tokens via a shared hybrid decoder, meaning the output is not a pure discrete or continuous reconstruction but a hybrid one.

## Method

HART introduces a hybrid tokenizer that decomposes continuous autoencoder latents into discrete tokens (capturing global structure) and continuous residual tokens (capturing fine details), trained with an alternating strategy that randomly routes the decoder through either the discrete or continuous path at 50% probability. The discrete tokens are modeled by a scalable-resolution VAR transformer extended to text-to-image generation, using interpolation-compatible relative position embeddings (sinusoidal step embeddings plus 1D/2D rotary token embeddings) to finetune from low-resolution checkpoints up to 1024px. The residual tokens are predicted by a lightweight 37M-parameter diffusion MLP conditioned on the AR transformer's hidden states, requiring only 8 sampling steps versus 30–50 for full-token diffusion in MAR. Efficiency is further improved through KV caching, discarding 80% of final-step tokens during training, and fused GPU kernels for position embeddings and RMSNorm.

## Results

On MJHQ-30K at 1024×1024, HART (732M params) achieves FID 5.38 and CLIP score 29.09, outperforming all compared diffusion models including SDXL (FID 8.76), PixArt-Σ (FID 6.34), and Playground v2.5 (FID 6.84). On class-conditioned ImageNet 256×256, HART-d30 (2.0B) reaches FID 1.77 and IS 330.3, improving over VAR-d30 (FID 1.92) by 7.8% while adding only 4% runtime overhead. In terms of efficiency, HART achieves 4.5–7.7× higher throughput and 3.1–5.9× lower latency than state-of-the-art diffusion models at 1024×1024, with 6.9–13.4× fewer MACs (12.5T vs. 86.2T–239T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HART (732M) decisively outperforms all prior autoregressive models: it beats LlamaGen (775M) by 20.2 FID points and Show-o (1.3B) by 9.6 FID points on MJHQ-30K, while also surpassing both in CLIP score and GenEval. Against diffusion baselines at 1024×1024, HART wins on FID against SDXL (5.38 vs. 8.76), PixArt-Σ (5.38 vs. 6.34), and Playground v2.5 (5.38 vs. 6.84), and achieves a higher CLIP score than SDXL (29.09 vs. 28.60) despite being 3.6× smaller. HART trails SD3-medium on GenEval (0.56 vs. 0.62) and DPG-Bench (80.89 vs. 85.80), though it leads by a wide margin on FID (5.38 vs. 11.92). For class-conditioned ImageNet generation, HART-d30 improves FID over VAR-d30 from 1.92 to 1.77 (7.8% reduction) with only 4% additional runtime, and outperforms MAR-L (FID 1.78, IS 296.0) with 10.7× fewer MACs and 12.9× lower latency. Compared to DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27, 57.2T MACs, 113s), HART-d30 achieves better FID (1.77) and IS (330.3 vs. 278.2) while being 3.3× faster even with DiT's 20-step sampler.

## Relation to Prior Work

HART extends VAR's next-scale autoregressive framework by introducing a hybrid tokenizer that decomposes continuous autoencoder latents into discrete tokens (modeled by a scalable-resolution AR transformer) and continuous residual tokens (modeled by a 37M-parameter residual diffusion MLP). This design departs from MAR, which conditions a diffusion module on AR outputs but predicts full continuous tokens, and from purely discrete AR models (VQGAN, LlamaGen) that are limited by VQ codebook reconstruction quality. The paper also builds on the broader diffusion-model lineage (DiT, SDXL, PixArt-Σ) for the continuous autoencoder backbone and on rotary/relative position embeddings from recent LLM and vision work to enable resolution-scalable training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HART is the first autoregressive model to directly generate 1024×1024 images at diffusion-comparable quality while being 3–6× faster, suggesting that the AR paradigm can close the efficiency gap with diffusion without sacrificing fidelity. The key open question is whether the hybrid discrete+residual decomposition generalizes to video generation or unified understanding-generation models, where the token sequence length and computational budget are far larger. A practical limitation is that the residual diffusion module, though lightweight, still adds 4–11% runtime overhead, and the alternating training strategy for the tokenizer, while effective, is somewhat ad hoc and may not transfer cleanly to other tokenizer architectures.
