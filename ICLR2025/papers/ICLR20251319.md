## Problem

Learning high-dimensional PDE solution operators (i.e., mapping initial conditions to solutions at later times without retraining) remains unstable in practice: existing methods like NODE and PINO either collapse during training or violate fundamental structural properties of the solution operator. No prior framework simultaneously guarantees graph topology preservation, semigroup consistency, and Lyapunov stability of the evolving operator, which are all necessary for reliable inference over long time horizons and unseen initial conditions.

## Input / Output

At test time, the method receives an initial condition function u₀(x) defined on a d-dimensional domain (d = 5–20) and produces the PDE solution u(·, t) at any requested time t via a single forward pass of the trained operator network. The intermediate representation is a set of sub-network parameters θ_t that evolve continuously; the solution is recovered by evaluating the fixed-architecture sub-network U_{θ_t} at spatial points. No spatial discretization or PDE solution data is required at inference—only the initial condition and the trained GNN operator.

## Method

SINGER parameterizes the PDE solution as a sub-network U_{θ_t} whose parameters evolve according to a stochastic differential equation dθ_t = V(θ_t, t, G; w)(dt + N dB_t), where V is a Message Passing Neural Network operating on the sub-network's parameter graph (nodes = parameters, edges = intra-neuron dependencies). The GNN architecture inherently enforces permutation invariance and modification robustness (graph topology), the SDE integration structure guarantees the semigroup property T(t₁+t₂) = T(t₂)T(t₁), and the stochastic noise term (implemented as Bernoulli/dropout) provides Lyapunov stability with the condition N² > 2L. Training minimizes the accumulated PDE residual r_T over randomly sampled initial parameters using the adjoint method for memory-efficient gradient computation, requiring no spatial discretization or reference solutions. The theoretical contribution includes proving that the solution operator is a stochastic semigroup (strong and weak) and that the full (θ_t, r_t) system is almost surely exponentially stable under Lipschitz conditions.

## Results

On the Heat and HJB equations (which have explicit solutions), SINGER achieves L2 relative errors of 0.0041–0.0175 (Heat, d=5–20) and 0.0027–0.0099 (HJB, d=5–20), outperforming all baselines in 31 out of 32 cases. On the six additional PDEs without closed-form solutions (Pricing, Sine-Gordon, Burgers, Reaction-Diffusion, HJB-LQ, Allen-Cahn), SINGER consistently beats the strongest baseline NODE(+N), e.g. 0.0020 vs. 0.0032 on Burgers d=5 and 0.00052 vs. 0.00071 on Allen-Cahn d=5. Zero-shot generalization to unseen dimensions (10→15) and sub-network widths (80→105) shows SINGER error growing from 0.0041 to 0.1194 (dim) and staying near 0.0041–0.0061 (width), while NODE(+N+I) degrades to 0.1646 and 0.1857 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SINGER outperforms NODE (Gaby & Ye, 2024) in nearly all settings, with the gap widening at higher dimensions: on Heat d=20, SINGER achieves 0.0175 while NODE collapses (0.0957*), and on HJB d=20, SINGER reaches 0.0052 versus NODE's 0.0602. Against the modified PINO (Li et al., 2024), SINGER is dramatically superior—PINO's error exceeds 0.5 on HJB d=10 and reaches 3.51 on HJB d=20, indicating complete failure to learn the operator. The noise ablation SINGER(−N) confirms that stochastic noise is critical: without it, the model collapses on Heat d=5–15 (errors 0.0137–0.0371 with *), while the full SINGER maintains errors below 0.005. NODE(+N), which adds noise to the non-graph NODE baseline, is the closest competitor but still trails SINGER by 2–5× on most entries, e.g. 0.0220 vs. 0.0041 on Heat d=10 and 0.0398 vs. 0.0052 on HJB d=20, highlighting the benefit of the GNN's graph-structure-aware message passing over a flat-parameter ODE.

## Relation to Prior Work

SINGER extends the NODE line of work (Gaby et al., 2024; Gaby & Ye, 2024) that learns high-dimensional PDE solution operators by evolving sub-network parameters over time. The key departure is replacing the free neural ODE (which flattens parameters and violates graph structure) with a GNN-driven stochastic differential equation that preserves the sub-network's graph topology, inherits the semigroup property from the ODE/SDE integration, and gains stability from Bernoulli noise, drawing on the Neural SDE stabilization framework of Liu et al. (2019). The adjoint-based training scheme is also adapted from Liu et al. (2019), and the problem formulation follows Gaby et al. (2024) directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantees (strong/weak semigroup, Lyapunov stability) for a learned high-dimensional PDE solution operator, addressing a critical gap where prior methods (NODE, PINO) could collapse during training or inference without explanation. The zero-shot generalization to unseen PDE dimensions and sub-network widths is practically valuable for pre-trained PDE solver foundation models. Limitations include the need to choose a sub-network architecture per PDE type, restriction to evolution (initial-value) PDEs, and acknowledged difficulty generalizing across PDE parameters. Open questions include whether the framework extends to boundary-value problems, PDEs with time-dependent coefficients, and whether the noise level can be adapted automatically.
