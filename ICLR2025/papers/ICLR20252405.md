## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for text-to-image diffusion models preserve image quality but fail to maintain text-image alignment, particularly at lower-bit (<8-bit) settings where baselines produce unusable outputs. The paper identifies two distributional root causes: activation outliers that are critical for image quality but are destroyed by uniform quantizers, and distinctive bimodal patterns in cross-attention scores (driven by the <start> token and prompt-dependent ranges) that degrade text-image alignment when quantized with a static uniform quantizer.

## Input / Output

DGQ is a post-training quantization method applied to pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion v1.4, SDXL Turbo). It requires only 64 calibration prompts from MS-COCO to determine per-layer, per-timestep quantization parameters (scales and zero-points), completing in ~20 minutes on a single GPU. At inference time, the quantized model takes a natural language text prompt and produces a generated image at the model's native resolution, with no changes to the input/output interface compared to the full-precision model.

## Method

DGQ consists of two components targeting the two identified distributional challenges. For activations, outlier-preserving group quantization first determines whether outliers concentrate along the channel or pixel dimension using a range-variability metric D_d, then applies K-means clustering along that dimension to form K groups, each with its own quantization scale and zero-point computed from the group's min-max range. For cross-attention scores, the method applies a logarithmic quantizer (preserving small values on a log scale) while keeping the <start> token's attention score in full precision, and uses dynamic quantization where the scale is set to the maximum attention score excluding the <start> token at each inference step. Weight quantization uses BRECQ/Adaround with block reconstruction, and the entire pipeline requires no fine-tuning, making it a pure PTQ approach.

## Results

On MS-COCO with Stable Diffusion v1.4, DGQ at W8A8 (16 groups) achieves an FID of 13.15, which is lower than the full-precision model's 14.44, while maintaining a CLIP score of 0.297 (vs. 0.298 full precision). In the challenging W4A6 setting, DGQ achieves FID 43.66 and CLIP 0.263, whereas baselines Q-Diffusion and TFMQ-DM produce near-unusable images (FID > 200, CLIP < 0.16). On PartiPrompts, DGQ maintains a CLIP score of 0.292 at W8A8, closely matching full precision (0.293), demonstrating strong prompt generalization. The method reduces BOPs from 823T (full precision) to 19.3T (W4A6), a 97.7% reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGQ consistently outperforms both Q-Diffusion and TFMQ-DM across all bit-width settings on both MS-COCO and PartiPrompts. At W8A8, DGQ achieves FID 13.15 vs. 26.12 (Q-Diff) and 18.85 (TFMQ), with CLIP 0.297 vs. 0.273 and 0.286 respectively. The gap widens dramatically at lower bit-widths: at W4A6, DGQ achieves FID 43.66 and CLIP 0.263, while Q-Diff and TFMQ produce essentially broken outputs (FID 242.75/229.64, CLIP 0.072/0.155). On PartiPrompts, DGQ maintains CLIP 0.292 at W8A8 (near full-precision 0.293), while baselines drop to 0.275 and 0.286. DGQ also outperforms both baselines on SDXL Turbo (4-step), achieving FID 22.46 at W8A8 versus TFMQ's 111.69, and FID 45.00 at W4A6 versus TFMQ's 270.45.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) line for diffusion models initiated by Q-Diffusion (timestep-aware calibration) and TFMQ-DM (temporal feature maintenance), but departs from them by shifting focus from timestep sensitivity to the internal distributional structure of activations and cross-attention scores. It also contrasts with mixed-precision approaches (MixDQ, PCR) that allocate variable bit-widths across layers or timesteps, instead pursuing uniform low-bit quantization with distribution-aware grouping. The weight quantization component reuses standard PTQ tools (BRECQ, Adaround), while the activation and attention quantization introduce K-means-based outlier grouping and prompt-specific logarithmic quantization—ideas borrowed from LLM outlier handling and ViT log-quantization but repurposed for the text-to-image diffusion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DGQ is the first PTQ method to achieve sub-8-bit quantization of text-to-image diffusion models without any fine-tuning, while simultaneously preserving both image quality and text-image alignment—two objectives that prior methods could not jointly satisfy. The 93.7% reduction in bit operations makes deployment on edge devices and real-time generation more feasible. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models like FLUX), whether the max-value dynamic scale for cross-attention can be improved (e.g., via lookup tables or reparameterization), and how DGQ interacts with advanced weight quantization methods like QuEST or EfficientDM.
