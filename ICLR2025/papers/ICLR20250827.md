## Problem

Existing sparse adversarial attacks minimize the number of modified pixels but do not constrain their spatial location or perturbation magnitude, producing scattered, high-intensity changes that are easily visible and unexplainable. While recent work has introduced group-wise sparsity regularizers (e.g., nuclear group norm), crafting such attacks poses a non-trivial optimization challenge involving group-level norm computations within a non-convex objective. This paper addresses both the optimization difficulty and the need for explainable, semantically targeted perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (32×32×3 for CIFAR-10, 299×299×3 for ImageNet), a target label (or true label for untargeted attacks), and a frozen DNN classifier (ResNet, ViT, VGG, or WideResNet). It outputs a sparse adversarial perturbation vector w that is group-wise sparse—concentrated in a small number of spatially coherent pixel clusters within semantically salient regions—and of low ℓ₂ magnitude. The final adversarial example is obtained by clipping x + w to the valid image range [I_min, I_max].

## Method

GSE is a two-phase first-order optimization algorithm. In Phase 1 (k̂ iterations), it applies a forward-backward splitting step using the closed-form 1/2-quasinorm proximal operator (Cao et al., 2013) with Nesterov momentum, while dynamically adjusting per-pixel tradeoff parameters λ via a Gaussian-blur-based proximity heuristic (AdjustLambda) that lowers λ for pixels near already-perturbed ones, thereby expanding coherent perturbation regions. In Phase 2 (remaining iterations), it restricts optimization to the selected pixel subspace V and applies projected Nesterov's accelerated gradient descent with ℓ₂-norm regularization to minimize perturbation magnitude. The projection onto V simply zeros out entries outside the selected coordinates, and the paper proves this projected NAG is equivalent to unconstrained NAG on the subspace, guaranteeing convergence. The approach requires no predefined pixel partitions or superpixel segmentation, operating purely through the learned spatial proximity dynamics.

## Results

GSE achieves 100% attack success rate (ASR) across all tested settings (CIFAR-10 ResNet20, ImageNet ResNet50, ImageNet ViT_B_16) for both untargeted and targeted attacks. In the untargeted setting, GSE requires only 41.7 changed pixels (ACP) on CIFAR-10 and 1,629 on ImageNet ResNet50, with group-wise sparsity (d_{2,0}) of 177 and 3,428 respectively — a 70.2% and 70.7% improvement over the next best method. In the targeted setting (average case), GSE achieves a 50.9% reduction in d_{2,0} on CIFAR-10 and a 38.4% reduction on ImageNet ViT_B_16, while also being the fastest method (e.g., 0.13s/image on CIFAR-10 vs. 0.97s for StrAttack and 0.25s for FWnucl).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StrAttack (Xu et al., 2019), GSE achieves 100% ASR in all settings while reducing ACP by 64.7% on CIFAR-10 (41.7 vs. 118) and 77.6% on ImageNet ResNet50 (1,629 vs. 7,265), with d_{2,0} reduced by 58.6% and 70.7% respectively; GSE is also ~7× faster per image. Against FWnucl (Kazemi et al., 2023), GSE dominates on ASR (100% vs. 94.6% on CIFAR-10, 100% vs. 47.4% on ImageNet ResNet50) and sparsity, though FWnucl achieves a slightly lower ℓ₂ norm in some settings; FWnucl's worst-case targeted ASR drops to 0% on ImageNet. Against Homotopy-Attack (Zhu et al., 2021), GSE reduces ACP by 23% (42.5 vs. 55.3), ANC by 32% (1.88 vs. 2.78), and d_{2,0} by 29.7% (180 vs. 256), while being ~170× faster (2.93s vs. 499s per image). Against SAPF (Fan et al., 2020), GSE halves ACP (42.5 vs. 87.3) and reduces d_{2,0} by 44.4% (180 vs. 324), though SAPF achieves a lower ℓ₂ norm (0.41 vs. 0.76). On explainability (IS metric), GSE consistently outperforms both group-wise sparse attacks (StrAttack, FWnucl) and traditional sparse attacks (PGD_0, Sparse-RS), particularly at higher ASM percentiles, confirming that its perturbations target the most semantically salient regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSE extends the group-wise sparse adversarial attack line initiated by StrAttack (ADMM with sliding masks), SAPF (ℓp-Box ADMM), FWnucl (nuclear group norm with Frank-Wolfe), and Homotopy-Attack (non-monotone accelerated proximal gradient). Its key departure is a two-phase strategy: a 1/2-quasinorm proximal operator (from Cao et al., 2013) with a novel proximity-based λ-adjustment heuristic (Gaussian-blurred perturbation mask) to select pixel groups, followed by projected Nesterov accelerated gradient descent with ℓ2 regularization. This replaces the heavier ADMM or Frank-Wolfe machinery of prior methods with a lighter first-order scheme, while the proximity heuristic introduces a data-driven, structure-free mechanism for group selection that is independent of predefined pixel partitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50.9% (CIFAR-10) and 38.4% (ImageNet) gains in group-wise sparsity at 100% ASR, combined with 10–100× speedups over prior methods, make GSE a practical tool for both attack evaluation and adversarial training augmentation. The paper's suggestion to incorporate GSE-generated examples into adversarial training opens a concrete defense pathway, though it remains untested at scale. Open questions include whether the proximity-based selection generalizes to non-convolutional architectures beyond ViT, whether the heuristic's Gaussian-blur parameters transfer across domains, and whether the explainability gains (higher IS scores) hold under distribution shift or multi-modal settings.
