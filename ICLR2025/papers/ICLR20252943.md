## Problem

Existing video-conditioned policy learning methods either handle only single-task demonstrations, require explicit video segmentation annotations to distinguish tasks within multi-task videos, or depend on data from another embodiment. This paper asks whether a video-conditioned policy can be trained to perform multiple skills from a single video and generalize compositionally to unseen task combinations, without any segmentation labels or cross-embodiment data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252943/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video demonstration (a sequence of frames aligned timestep-by-timestep with the robot trajectory) and the current robot state, and outputs a discrete robot action (32 bins). The video is treated as a sequence of N tasks, but the policy is designed to attend only to the segment relevant to the current task at each timestep. At test time, a single unseen video (a novel combination of previously seen tasks) is provided as the sole conditioning signal, with no auxiliary language or segmentation information.

## Method

WL-DM introduces an information bottleneck objective on top of standard behavior cloning to achieve implicit video segmentation. A transformer encoder processes video and state tokens, and the total loss combines three terms: (1) a behavior-cloning loss, (2) a mutual-information-minimization term that pulls the video representation toward a prior encoder's output (which sees only a short future video window as a proxy for the current task segment), and (3) a mutual-information-maximization term that pushes the video representation to predict clustered short-term action sequences (skill approximation). Theorem 1 formally shows that zero mutual information between the video representation and other-task segments implies the full-video-conditioned policy equals the current-task-only policy, providing the theoretical justification for compositional generalization. Only the main encoder and action decoder are retained at inference; the prior encoder and skill decoder are training-only auxiliaries.

## Results

On Franka Kitchen (FK), WL-DM achieves an average of 2.33±0.78 tasks completed (out of 7), outperforming the best baseline V-BET (1.94±1.06) by approximately 20.1%. On Meta World (MW), WL-DM achieves 2.57±0.90 tasks completed (out of 4), a 100.1% improvement over the best baseline V-DT (1.24±0.86), effectively doubling the performance. WL-DM achieves the best per-task score on 12 out of 14 test tasks across both environments, demonstrating strong compositional generalization to unseen task combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WL-DM consistently outperforms all four baselines in both environments. On Franka Kitchen, WL-DM (2.33) beats V-BET (1.94), V-DT (1.66), and VIMA (0.94), with the largest gap over VIMA (~2.5x). On Meta World, the advantage is more pronounced: WL-DM (2.57) more than doubles V-DT (1.24) and V-BET (1.18), and is ~3x better than VIMA (0.87). V-BET and V-DT perform at a similar level in both environments, as the paper notes that action information (used by V-DT but not V-BET) can often be inferred from state changes in these robotic settings. VIMA, originally designed for multimodal prompts, performs worst in both environments, likely because its architecture is not well-suited to pure video-conditioned multi-task settings. An additional baseline ViP (Chane-Sane et al., 2023), adapted to the video-only setting, achieves 1.91±0.88 on MW, still trailing WL-DM by 0.66 tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the C-BET behavior-cloning framework (Cui et al., 2022) as its base architecture and on the information bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000) as its theoretical foundation, while borrowing the short-term-behavior skill representation from Yuan et al. (2024). It departs from prior video-conditioned policy learning (ViP, VIMA) which typically handles single-task demonstrations or requires explicit segmentation annotations from other modalities, and from compositional generalization work (Wang et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023; Shin et al., 2023/2024) that relies on cross-embodiment data or language-based task labels. The key conceptual departure is using a dual mutual-information objective to achieve implicit video segmentation and skill discovery without any external annotation, positioning the method as a self-contained alternative to the annotation-heavy pipelines in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, annotation-free route to compositional generalization for video-conditioned policies, which is practically important given the cost of collecting task-level segmentation labels for robot videos. However, the evaluation is limited to two relatively simple simulated environments (Franka Kitchen, Meta World) with low absolute performance (best average ~2.57 out of 4 tasks), and the approximations of current task and skill remain naive (immediate-future windows). Open questions include whether the information bottleneck objective scales to longer-horizon, higher-dimensional real-world tasks, whether timestep-aligned video data is truly necessary, and whether the approach transfers to multimodal instruction settings the authors mention as future work.
