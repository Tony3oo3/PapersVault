## Problem

Ligand-based drug design requires generating novel molecules that replicate the 3D shape, electrostatic, and pharmacophore interaction profiles of known bioactive compounds, but traditional virtual screening is confined to existing libraries, undirected, and slow. Existing 3D generative models for structure-based drug design struggle to produce ligands that form correct biochemical interactions, and no prior framework jointly models a molecule's 3D structure with its full interaction profile (shape, ESP, and directional pharmacophores) in a context-free setting.

## Input / Output

For conditional generation, ShEPhERD takes target interaction profiles—an ESP surface (n₃ surface points with scalar Coulombic potentials), a set of pharmacophores (positions, one-hot type encodings, and unit/zero direction vectors on S²), and optionally a shape surface point cloud—and forward-noises them before using them as inpainting constraints during denoising. The model outputs a full 3D molecular graph (atom types, formal charges, bond orders, Cartesian coordinates) jointly with its own shape, ESP, and pharmacophore representations. The user must specify the number of atoms n₁ and pharmacophores n₄ per sample, and the model operates on explicit hydrogens with conformations optimized by GFN2-xTB.

## Method

ShEPhERD is a variance-preserving SE(3)-equivariant DDPM that jointly diffuses and denoises four coupled representations: the 3D molecular graph, a shape surface point cloud, an attributed ESP surface, and pharmacophores with spherical direction vectors. The denoising network uses EquiformerV2 (E3NN) modules in three stages—per-component equivariant embedding, a heterogeneous joint interaction module (local message passing over a unified 3D graph plus a global sum-pooled code), and per-component noise prediction—ensuring T(3)-invariance and SO(3)-equivariance so all representations stay aligned during denoising. A key design choice is treating pharmacophore direction vectors as Euclidean points (norm 1 or 0) so they can be noised with standard isotropic Gaussian diffusion alongside coordinates. Conditional sampling is achieved via inpainting: target profiles are forward-noised to each timestep and substituted for the model's denoised counterparts, steering generation toward the desired interaction profile without any protein context. Training uses unweighted L2 regression on predicted versus true noise for all four components simultaneously, with the option to model marginal distributions (e.g., P(x₁,x₃,x₄)) by simply omitting variables.

## Results

On conditional generation (GDB17), ShEPhERD produces molecules with ≥94% having 2D graph similarity < 0.2 to the target while achieving significantly enriched 3D shape, ESP, and pharmacophore similarity scores compared to random dataset sampling. For natural product ligand hopping, ShEPhERD (2500 samples, ~500 valid with SA < 4.5) outperforms both REINVENT (10,000 oracle budget, ~9000 valid) and virtual screening (2500 samples) on combined ESP + pharmacophore 3D similarity for all three natural product targets. In bioactive hit diversification across 7 PDB targets, ShEPhERD (≤500 valid samples per target) enriches the top-1 Vina docking score relative to the redocked PDB ligand for all 7 targets, and beats a 10K-molecule docking screen on 5/7 targets when conditioning on the PDB bound pose (e.g., 3ny8 mean top-10 Vina shifts from −9.96 to −11.70 kcal/mol).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary quantitative baseline is REINVENT 2.0 (Blaschke et al., 2020), a reinforcement-learning-based SMILES generator optimized with the paper's own ESP + pharmacophore 3D similarity as reward. ShEPhERD outperforms REINVENT on combined ESP + pharmacophore similarity for all three natural product targets, despite using only ~500 valid samples versus REINVENT's ~9000 (a 10× oracle budget advantage for REINVENT). ShEPhERD also substantially outperforms virtual screening (random sampling from ShEPhERD-MOSES-aq) on the same metric. For bioactive hit diversification, the baseline is a 10K-molecule AutoDock Vina docking screen from ShEPhERD-MOSES-aq; ShEPhERD (≤500 valid samples) beats the screen's top-1 Vina score on 5/7 targets when conditioning on the PDB bound pose and 3/7 when conditioning on the lowest-energy conformer (the more realistic protein-blind setting). Notably, ShEPhERD is not trained to optimize any target-specific similarity or docking score—it uses the same pretrained weights for all targets and relies solely on inpainting for conditioning—yet still achieves these enrichments out-of-the-box.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShEPhERD unifies and extends three previously separate lines of interaction-aware 3D molecular generation: shape-conditioned generation (Adams & Coley 2022; Chen et al. 2023), pharmacophore-conditioned generation (Ziv et al. 2024; Imrie et al. 2021), and electrostatics-aware generation (Bolcato et al. 2022). It builds on the SE(3)-equivariant DDPM paradigm (Hoogeboom et al. 2022) and EquiformerV2 message passing (Liao et al. 2023), but departs from structure-based drug design models (Schneuing et al. 2022; Igashov et al. 2024) by operating in a context-free setting that requires no protein pocket. The key architectural departure is jointly diffusing/denoising all three interaction profiles simultaneously—including directional pharmacophore vectors on the unit sphere—rather than conditioning on a single interaction type or treating them as post-hoc constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ShEPhERD provides the first unified generative framework that simultaneously models shape, electrostatics, and directional pharmacophores, enabling a single model to address multiple bioisosteric design tasks (ligand hopping, hit diversification, fragment merging) out-of-the-box. The protein-blind docking enrichment result—where generated molecules retain binding modes without any knowledge of the target protein—suggests that 3D interaction profiles carry sufficient information to implicitly encode binding geometry, which could accelerate early-stage drug discovery. Key limitations include purely in silico validation (no wet-lab confirmation), reliance on Vina as a weak affinity proxy, the need to specify atom and pharmacophore counts a priori, and weaker self-consistency for pharmacophores compared to shape/ESP. Open questions include whether the framework extends to additional interaction types (e.g., metal coordination, desolvation penalties), whether inference-time optimization (e.g., genetic algorithms over partial denoising) can further improve scores, and whether the approach generalizes to larger or more diverse chemical spaces.
