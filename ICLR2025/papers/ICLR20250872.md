## Problem

SNN-based Vision Transformers suffer from a significant performance gap compared to their ANN counterparts because the vanilla Dot-Product self-attention is fundamentally mismatched with spatio-temporal spike trains: binary spikes create large magnitude differences between Q and K vectors that corrupt relevance scoring, and the mechanism ignores temporal interactions across timesteps. Existing spike-driven ViTs treat self-attention merely as a token mixer without designing a relevance computation suited to spike distributions or exploiting the temporal dynamics that SNNs inherently provide.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet), event streams from DVS cameras (CIFAR10-DVS), and high-resolution remote sensing or SAR imagery. For classification, it outputs a single class label via a fully connected head after global average pooling; for detection, the SNN-ViT backbone is embedded in YOLO-v3, feeding P3/P4/P5 feature maps into detection heads that produce bounding boxes. The GL-SPS front-end downsamples the input by a factor of 2 using a 7×7 stride-2 convolution combined with a 3×3 stride-2 dilated convolution, producing multi-scale spike-encoded patches before the transformer stages.

## Method

The core contribution is Saccadic Spike Self-Attention (SSSA), which replaces Dot-Product with a cross-entropy-based distribution similarity: each spike vector is reduced to its firing rate (sum across the D dimension), and relevance is computed as Q′ × K′ᵀ, making it insensitive to magnitude fluctuations in binary spikes. Temporally, a saccadic spiking neuron uses a learnable lower-triangular matrix M_w to accumulate patch salience across all timesteps without the decay-and-reset information loss of LIF neurons, enabling long-range temporal context. SSSA-V2 further reduces complexity from O(N²) to O(D) by absorbing K′ᵀ×L into a learnable threshold scaling factor, eliminating integer multiplications and preserving full spike-driven, event-driven inference. The overall SNN-ViT architecture stacks GL-SPS multi-scale patch-embedding blocks (standard + dilated convolutions) with SSSA-V2 transformer blocks in a four-stage pyramid, trained end-to-end with supervised loss over 4 timesteps.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, SNN-ViT achieves 80.1% top-1 accuracy with only 5.57M parameters, outperforming Spikformer (78.21%) and Spike-driven Transformer (78.40%). On ImageNet-1K, SNN-ViT-8-512 reaches 80.23% top-1 accuracy with O(D) linear complexity, 53.7M parameters, and 35.75 mJ energy consumption, surpassing SpikingResformer-L (78.77%) and Spike-driven-8-512 (74.57%). For remote sensing object detection, SNN-ViT-YOLO achieves 89.4% mAP@0.5 on NWPU VHR-10 and 97.0% mAP@0.5 on SSDD with 8 timesteps, exceeding the SNN SOTA EMS-YOLO (87.9% and 95.1% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNN-ViT outperforms all SNN-based ViT baselines across every benchmark: on CIFAR-100 it beats Spikformer (78.21%), Spikingformer (78.21%), and Spike-driven (78.40%) by 1.7–2.6 points; on ImageNet-1K, SNN-ViT-8-512 (80.23%) surpasses SpikingResformer-L (78.77%) by 1.46 points and Spike-driven-8-512 (74.57%) by 5.66 points, while also achieving lower complexity (O(D) vs. O(N²D) or O(ND)). On the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset, SNN-ViT (82.3%) exceeds STSA (79.9%) by 2.4 points despite reducing temporal complexity from O(T²N²D) to O(TD). Against ANN baselines on ImageNet, SNN-ViT-8-512 (80.23%) trails Swin-S (83.03%) and Flatten-Swin-S (83.52%) by ~3 points, but exceeds ViT-12-768 (77.90%) by 2.3 points. For object detection, SNN-ViT-YOLO (89.4% mAP on NWPU, 97.0% on SSDD) beats EMS-YOLO (87.9%/95.1%) and Faster R-CNN (85.3% on SSDD), though it falls slightly short of the ANN CSnNet (90.4% on NWPU). Overall, SNN-ViT wins 15 of 17 head-to-head comparisons, losing only to the two strongest ANN detection/classification baselines (Swin-S, Flatten-Swin-S, CSnNet) where the accuracy gap is 1–3 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of SNNs and Vision Transformers, departing from prior SNN-ViT work (Spikformer, Spike-driven Transformer V2, SpikingResformer) that retained Dot-Product-based relevance computation and treated self-attention merely as a token mixer. The key conceptual departure is identifying a fundamental mismatch: the binary, sparse nature of spikes creates large magnitude instabilities that invalidate Dot-Product as a relevance measure, motivating a distribution-based (cross-entropy) alternative. The temporal dimension draws on biological saccadic attention mechanisms, distinguishing it from STSA (Wang et al., 2023b) which achieved spatio-temporal interaction at O(T²N²D) cost. The linear-complexity SSSA-V2 variant further differentiates from all prior SNN-ViTs by reducing attention to O(D) through an algebraic absorption of the Key summation into the neuron threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SOTA accuracy on ImageNet-1K (80.23%) and remote sensing detection with linear computational complexity and fully event-driven inference, making SNN-ViTs genuinely viable for power-constrained edge deployment where prior SNN-ViTs still required quadratic or higher complexity. An open question is whether the cross-entropy-to-linear approximation (replacing log(x) with x via Taylor expansion around 0.15) remains valid as firing rates shift in deeper or differently-regularized networks. The saccadic mechanism's biological grounding is compelling but its generalization beyond classification and detection to tasks requiring dense spatial reasoning (segmentation, tracking) remains untested. The paper also does not provide hardware-level energy measurements, leaving the claimed efficiency gains at the algorithmic level.
