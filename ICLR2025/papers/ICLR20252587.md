## Problem

General-purpose MLLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL, Gemini-1.5-Pro) consistently fail to detect and interpret traffic accidents because they lack specialized understanding of context-dependent high-level semantics like 'anomaly' and 'accident,' and their visual representations are not aligned with the distinctive spatial-temporal patterns of accident scenes. No existing dataset provides the multi-granularity annotations (recognition, description, temporal localization, spatial grounding) needed to train MLLMs for this domain.

## Input / Output

The model accepts 720p traffic video clips (~10.3 s average duration) or individual traffic images paired with natural-language queries. Temporal localization outputs are normalized to the clip duration as {t_start, t_end}, and spatial grounding outputs are normalized to image dimensions as [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max], both enclosed in special tokens. The TABot-Chat variant additionally produces multi-turn conversational responses covering accident causes, prevention suggestions, and free-form reasoning beyond the four core functional tasks.

## Method

TABot is built on GroundingGPT-7B and trained in two stages. The first stage (functional tuning) jointly fine-tunes the LLM and visual adapters on TAU-106K across four tasks—accident recognition, description generation, temporal localization, and spatial grounding—augmented by two strategies: Negative Segment Referring (NSR), which uses contrastive learning on accident-free segments to sharpen boundary sensitivity, and Video Spatial Alignment (VSA), which injects spatial grounding annotations at specific timestamps into the temporal localization training to complement temporal perception with fine-grained spatial cues. The second stage (instruction tuning) uses LLaMA-70B to generate multi-turn accident-oriented dialogues from structured captions, mixing them with single-round functional data and preserving task-specific flag tokens to prevent catastrophic forgetting of grounding and localization capabilities. The underlying TAU-106K dataset (51.5K videos + 54.8K images) is constructed via a video-to-image annotation pipeline that derives keyframes from labeled accident intervals, ensuring temporal-spatial consistency across modalities and enabling the VSA strategy.

## Results

On video accident recognition, TABot achieves 81.00% accuracy, substantially outperforming Qwen2-VL (72.65%), Gemini-1.5-Pro (69.61%), and the GroundingGPT baseline (50.00%). For the most challenging video temporal localization task, TABot reaches AP@50 of 20.12, surpassing Qwen2-VL (15.76) and TimeChat (7.90). On image accident recognition, TABot attains 90.75% accuracy versus Gemini-1.5-Pro's 80.99% and Qwen2-VL's 58.35%. For image accident region grounding, TABot achieves AP@50 of 70.03, nearly doubling Qwen2-VL's 47.52.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TABot decisively outperforms all general-purpose MLLMs across every task. On video accident recognition, TABot (81.00%) surpasses Qwen2-VL (72.65%) by 8.35 points and Gemini-1.5-Pro (69.61%) by 11.39 points, while VTimeLLM and GroundingGPT collapse to 50% (random-guess level) by classifying all videos as abnormal. On video temporal localization, TABot's AP@50 of 20.12 exceeds Qwen2-VL (15.76) and TimeChat (7.90), with a particularly large margin over GroundingGPT (2.40). On image accident recognition, TABot (90.75%) beats Gemini-1.5-Pro (80.99%) by nearly 10 points and Qwen2-VL (58.35%) by over 32 points; notably, Gemini-1.5-Pro achieves 0% recall on accident samples, indicating it fails to detect any accidents. For image spatial grounding, TABot's AP@50 of 70.03 (region) and 65.86 (object) substantially exceed Qwen2-VL (47.52 and 57.48) and MiniGPT-4 (34.85 and 56.65). The supplementary experiments further confirm that fine-tuning Qwen2-VL on TAU-106K boosts its video recognition to 82.65% and image recognition to 92.00%, validating the dataset's quality while TABot still maintains competitive or superior performance across most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GroundingGPT as its base MLLM, fine-tuning it on a newly constructed traffic accident dataset. Conceptually, it departs from both traditional TAD methods (single-stage/two-stage detectors relying on handcrafted features) and general-purpose MLLMs (Video-LLaVA, TimeChat, VTimeLLM) by arguing that domain-specific fine-grained annotations—particularly free-form accident descriptions and spatial grounding labels—are necessary to bridge the gap between general visual semantics and high-level anomaly comprehension. The video-to-image annotation pipeline unifies temporal and spatial supervision in a way that prior traffic accident benchmarks (TAD, DoTA, CCD) did not provide, enabling the proposed NSR and VSA training strategies that have no direct precedent in the MLLM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAU-106K is the largest and most comprehensively annotated traffic accident dataset to date, filling a critical gap for researchers working on domain-specific MLLM applications in public safety. The demonstration that frontier general MLLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL, Gemini-1.5-Pro) systematically fail at accident recognition—often defaulting to 'normal'—provides concrete evidence for the domain-adaptation argument in multimodal learning. However, temporal localization remains weak (AP@50 ≈ 20%), and the paper acknowledges open challenges including hallucination mitigation, finer-grained grounding, and extending to accident forecasting and causality analysis, which remain unaddressed.
