## Problem

Diffusion models rely on the covariance of the denoising distribution for sampling, yet most methods use fixed isotropic variances or learn diagonal covariances from data via VLB or noise-second-moment regression. The analytically optimal state-dependent covariance derived from the score function's Hessian requires O(D²) storage and O(D) network evaluations per step, making it infeasible for high-dimensional image generation. The paper asks: can we efficiently learn the diagonal of this optimal covariance with an unbiased objective that avoids both the computational cost of full Hessian estimation and the bias of finite Rademacher averaging?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained score (noise-prediction) network with frozen parameters and a noisy data point x_t at time t. A small auxiliary head h_φ (a single Conv or Res+Conv layer for UNet-based models, or AdaLN+Linear for DiT) is appended to the shared base network to predict the diagonal Hessian, adding only 0.2–11% extra parameters and negligible inference time. At generation time, the predicted diagonal forms the covariance Σ_{t'}(x_t;φ) via the closed-form identity, which is then used to sample x_{t'} in skip-step DDPM or DDIM, yielding generated images and tractable NLL estimates.

## Method

The core contribution is the Optimal Covariance Matching (OCM) objective, which trains h_φ to match diag(∇²_{x̃} log q(x̃)) by minimizing E_{q(x̃)p(v)}‖h_φ(x̃) − v⊙H(x̃)v‖²₂ with Rademacher vectors v. The key insight is that placing the expectation over v *outside* the L2 norm (rather than averaging M samples inside, as in the standard Rademacher estimator) yields an unbiased objective that upper-bounds the grounded loss via Jensen's inequality and attains its optimum exactly at h_φ = diag(H), even with M=1 sample. The learned diagonal then plugs into the generalized analytical covariance identity Σ(x̃) = (σ⁴h_φ(x̃) + σ²I)/α² to form the state-dependent covariance for skip-step DDPM and DDIM sampling. The paper further proves (Theorem 3) that in the optimal limit, OCM-DDPM and SN-DDPM recover the same true covariance, but OCM avoids the quadratic error amplification that degrades SN-DDPM at low noise levels.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (cosine schedule) with 10 sampling steps, OCM-DDPM achieves FID 14.32 and NLL 4.99 bits/dim, substantially outperforming DDPM with fixed β̃ (FID 34.76, NLL 75.96) and DDPM with fixed β (FID 205.31, NLL 6.51). On ImageNet 256×256 with the DiT architecture and 10 steps, OCM-DDIM achieves the best FID of 5.33 at CFG=2.0. In terms of sampling efficiency, OCM-DPM requires only 16 steps to reach FID≈6 on CIFAR-10, compared to 90 steps for standard DDPM and 45 for Improved DDPM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) with fixed β or β̃ covariance, OCM-DDPM achieves dramatic improvements at low step counts: at 10 steps on CIFAR-10 (CS), FID drops from 34.76 (β̃) or 205.31 (β) to 14.32, and NLL drops from 75.96 to 4.99. Compared to DDIM (Song et al., 2020), OCM-DDIM consistently achieves lower FID at all step counts (e.g., 16.70 vs 34.34 at 10 steps on CIFAR-10 CS). Against Analytic-DPM (Bao et al., 2022b), OCM-DDPM matches or slightly outperforms on NLL (4.99 vs 5.08 at 10 steps) and achieves better FID (14.32 vs 22.94). Versus SN-DDPM (Bao et al., 2022a), OCM-DDPM provides a better trade-off: SN-DDPM occasionally achieves marginally lower FID (e.g., 16.33 vs 14.32 at 10 steps on CIFAR-10 CS) but suffers severely on NLL (90.85 vs 4.99), while NPR-DDPM sometimes edges out on NLL but produces noticeably worse FID. Against Improved DDPM (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021), OCM-DDPM achieves substantially better NLL at fewer steps (4.45 vs 18.91 at 25 steps on ImageNet 64×64) while matching performance at full 4000 steps (3.59 vs 3.57).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analytical covariance identity from Zhang et al. (2024b), which derived the optimal full covariance of the denoising distribution via a second-order Tweedie's formula, to the computationally tractable diagonal case. Rather than learning the diagonal covariance directly from data (as in SN-DDPM or VLB-based I-DDPM), the authors amortize the Rademacher Hessian-diagonal estimator into a small network trained with a novel unbiased matching objective (OCM). The paper also establishes a formal equivalence (Theorem 3) between OCM-DDPM and SN-DDPM in the optimal-training limit, while arguing that OCM avoids the quadratic error amplification that plagues SN-DDPM at low noise levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a plug-and-play improvement to any pretrained diffusion model (UNet or DiT) with negligible overhead (<1% extra parameters for most architectures), reducing the number of sampling steps needed to reach a target FID by roughly 2–3× on several benchmarks. A key open question is whether extending beyond diagonal covariance (e.g., low-rank or block-diagonal structures) could close the gap to the full-covariance oracle demonstrated in the toy experiments. The paper also highlights connections to distillation and moment-matching that remain unexplored, and notes that the method's advantage over SN-DDPM is most pronounced in likelihood estimation rather than FID, suggesting the two objectives trade off in practice.
