## Problem

The dominant RLHF paradigm is synchronous on-policy RL, where generation, reward labelling, and training occur sequentially on the same GPUs, leaving hardware underutilized. While offline methods are more compute-efficient, they underperform online methods. The paper tackles the underexplored question of how much off-policyness (training on data from previous model iterations) can be tolerated to enable asynchronous generation and training without degrading final performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250145/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts and generates completions from the current LLM policy using a dedicated inference GPU (vLLM), while a separate set of training GPUs updates the policy on completions generated one timestep earlier, scored by a fixed reward model. Generation and training run concurrently as independent processes, with the generation GPU using vLLM (12× faster than HuggingFace transformers for inference) and training GPUs using standard PyTorch backends. The output is an aligned policy model whose final win-rate and KL divergence match the synchronous on-policy baseline, achieved in 25–70% less wall-clock time depending on model scale and task.

## Method

The paper adapts the Cleanba asynchronous RL framework to RLHF: at each timestep, the generation process produces completions with the current policy θ_t while the training process simultaneously updates the policy on completions generated by θ_{t-1}, making learning one-step off-policy. Among PPO, RLOO, and Online DPO, Online DPO is found to be most robust to off-policy data, with robustness increasing as policy model scale grows (410m → 2.8B Pythia). For generation-bound scenarios, multiple PPO epochs on the same batch improve sample efficiency at the cost of higher KL; for training-bound scenarios, sampling K=4 completions and applying DPO only to the best/worst pair provides a stronger gradient, halving required training steps. The approach is validated at scale on LLaMA 3.1 8B (No Robots instruction-following, 38% faster) and Rho-1B (GSM8k math, 68% faster), matching synchronous performance in both cases.

## Results

On GSM8k with Rho-1B, Async Online DPO achieves 52.6% pass@1 (vs. 52.2% for Sync Online DPO and 50.3% for the strong Sync PPO baseline) while training 68% faster (129 min vs. 218 min). On the No Robots instruction-following task with LLaMA 3.1 8B, Async Online DPO matches Sync Online DPO exactly at 57.2% GPT-4o win rate while running 38% faster (142 min vs. 230 min). On TLDR summarization with 2.8B Pythia, asynchronous RLHF matches synchronous win-rate while training 25% faster, with speed improvements scaling with model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strong Sync PPO baseline (Kazemnejad et al., 2024) on GSM8k, Async Online DPO outperforms by 2.3 points in pass@1 (52.6% vs. 50.3%) while being 6.7× faster (129 min vs. 864 min). Against Sync RLOO on GSM8k, Async Online DPO wins by 2.6 points in pass@1 (52.6% vs. 50.0%) and is 3× faster (129 min vs. 385 min). On TLDR, Online DPO is the most robust RLHF loss to off-policy data: at N=64 mini-batches (highly off-policy), PPO and RLOO collapse in performance while Online DPO retains meaningful learning; Best-of-2 SFT also degrades, confirming the contrastive DPO objective is key. On the No Robots 8B chatbot, Async PPO nearly matches Sync PPO (52.6% vs. 53.0% GPT-4o win rate) while being 12% faster, confirming the asynchronous paradigm generalizes beyond Online DPO. Overall, the proposed method ties or beats all baselines on final performance while consistently achieving 25–68% compute speedups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical asynchronous deep RL paradigm (A3C, IMPALA) to the LLM RLHF setting, where prior work has been exclusively synchronous and on-policy. It builds on Online DPO (Guo et al., 2024; Calandriello et al., 2024) as the core loss function, the Cleanba asynchronous training framework (Huang et al., 2023) for the system design, and vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023) for efficient generation. The key departure from prior RLHF work is the explicit study of off-policy robustness across loss functions and model scales, and the derivation of a Proximal RLOO variant with importance sampling and clipping to address the gap in RLOO's off-policy formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple one-step off-policy approach (training on the previous iteration's generations) matches fully on-policy performance while yielding 25–70% compute savings, a practically significant efficiency gain for a widely-used training paradigm already adopted in Tulu 3. The finding that policy scale—not reward model scale—governs off-policy robustness is a useful design principle for practitioners. Open questions include how asynchronous RLHF extends to multi-turn and tool-use RLHF settings, how to reduce the remaining overhead from Python GIL and parameter synchronization, and whether the KL cost observed in optimized (generation-bound or training-bound) scenarios can be further mitigated.
