## Problem

RNNs (e.g., Mamba, RWKV) offer constant-memory inference compared to Transformers' quadratic attention, but their empirical inferiority on retrieval-heavy tasks has lacked a unified theoretical explanation. This paper asks whether Chain-of-Thought prompting can close the representation gap between RNNs and Transformers, and identifies in-context retrieval as the fundamental bottleneck that CoT alone cannot overcome.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251752/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are token sequences of length Θ(n) over a vocabulary of size n + O(1), including graphs tokenized as edge lists (<s> u₁ ~ v₁ u₂ ~ v₂ …), key-value pair strings for associative recall, and regular-expression search queries delimited by <StartSearch>/<EndSearch> tokens. Outputs are autoregressive next-token predictions: a single retrieved token for retrieval tasks, a YES/NO label for decision problems like IsTree, or a sequence of intermediate CoT tokens followed by the final answer. The retrieval-augmented variant appends the regex match result directly to the context after each <EndSearch> token, making the retrieved information available to subsequent RNN steps.

## Method

The paper establishes a hierarchy of representation power using streaming and communication complexity lower bounds: any RNN with o(n)-bit memory (covering Mamba, RWKV, Linear Transformers) cannot solve in-context retrieval tasks (Index, Associative Recall, c-gram retrieval, Counting) or the IsTree problem even with arbitrary-length CoT, while constant-size Transformers solve all of them. On the positive side, the paper proves two constructive upper bounds: (1) In-Context RAG via regular-expression function calls lets constant-size Linear RNNs simulate any polynomial-time Turing machine in O(n^A) CoT steps, and (2) appending a single Transformer layer to an RNN (a hybrid architecture) achieves the same simulation without explicit retrieval calls. Empirically, Mamba and LLaMA-2 models trained on synthetic IsTree and Hotpot-QA with controlled retrieval difficulty confirm that CoT alone leaves RNNs behind while retrieval augmentation or a single attention layer restores near-Transformer performance.

## Results

On the synthetic IsTree task (graphs of 16, 32, 64 nodes), RNNs (Mamba) with CoT show sharp performance degradation as graph size increases, while Transformers (LLaMA 2) maintain consistently higher accuracy across all sizes. When enhanced with In-Context RAG (regular-expression retrieval), all models—RNN, Transformer, and hybrid—reach near-perfect accuracy regardless of graph size. On Hotpot-QA with controlled retrieval difficulty (n paragraphs in random order, n ∈ {2,4,6,8}), the RNN model exhibits the steepest accuracy drop as n increases, while the hybrid model (Mamba + 4 attention layers) performs significantly better, approaching Transformer (Phi-1.5) performance. When the correct paragraphs are placed at the end (reducing retrieval difficulty), all models show stable performance, confirming that the gap is driven by in-context retrieval rather than context length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023) as the RNN baseline, the paper demonstrates that CoT alone is insufficient: Mamba's accuracy on IsTree degrades sharply from 16 to 64 nodes, while the Transformer baseline (LLaMA 2, Touvron et al., 2023) maintains consistently higher accuracy at all graph sizes. On Hotpot-QA with random paragraph ordering, Mamba shows the steepest performance drop as the number of paragraphs n increases from 2 to 8, confirming the theoretical prediction that RNNs are bottlenecked by in-context retrieval. The Transformer baseline (Phi-1.5, Li et al., 2023) degrades more gradually under the same retrieval difficulty. The key positive result is that appending a single Transformer layer to Mamba (hybrid architecture) closes the gap: the hybrid model matches Transformer performance on IsTree with CoT and significantly outperforms pure Mamba on Hotpot-QA, validating the theoretical claim that one attention layer suffices to provide the missing retrieval capability. With In-Context RAG (regular-expression search), even the pure RNN reaches near-perfect accuracy on all tasks, confirming that retrieval augmentation is the sufficient fix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CoT expressiveness theory developed for Transformers (Feng et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024) to RNNs, proving that CoT strictly improves RNNs but cannot match Transformers. It unifies a scattered set of prior lower-bound results on RNN limitations (associative recall in Arora et al., 2023; copying in Jelassi et al., 2024; sparse averaging in Sanford et al., 2023) under a single conceptual lens—in-context retrieval—and applies streaming/communication complexity techniques (Henzinger et al., 1998; Munro & Paterson, 1980) to prove the gap is fundamental. The key departure from prior work is identifying in-context retrieval as the *root cause* of all these failures and showing that two minimal interventions (In-Context RAG via regex, or appending a single Transformer layer) provably close the gap to full polynomial-time Turing machine simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first clean theoretical explanation for why the entire family of modern RNNs (Mamba, RWKV, RetNet, Linear Transformers) systematically underperforms Transformers on retrieval-heavy tasks, and it gives architecture designers a concrete, minimal fix: a single Transformer layer suffices. The practical implication for hybrid architecture design (Jamba, Samba, etc.) is significant—rather than needing many attention layers, theory suggests one is enough for representational parity. Open questions include whether the In-Context RAG mechanism can be made practical (no training data exists for regex-based retrieval), how many Transformer layers are needed in practice versus the theoretical minimum of one, and whether the gap persists at scale with larger state dimensions.
