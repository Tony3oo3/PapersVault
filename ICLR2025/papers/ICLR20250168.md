## Problem

VLMs used to auto-generate language expressions for visual objects in LOD training data frequently hallucinate, producing inaccurate descriptions of object names, colors, shapes, and spatial relations. As training data scales up with these misaligned expressions, VL alignment quality degrades and LOD performance plateaus or worsens. The paper asks how to automatically re-align language to visual objects to preserve data quality while scaling data quantity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250168/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Real-LOD workflow takes as input an image with detected object bounding boxes, an image caption, the object category, and a raw VLM-generated language expression, along with a SigLIP matching score that pre-filters ~75% of pairs (only pairs scoring below 0.5 enter the workflow). It outputs a re-aligned language expression that accurately describes the target object. These re-aligned pairs constitute Real-Data (0.18M images, 1.18M object-expression pairs), which trains the Real-Model (Swin-B backbone) to output object bounding boxes given free-form language queries at 1333×800 resolution.

## Method

Real-LOD is a cyclic agentic workflow with three steps controlled by a LoRA-fine-tuned ChatGLM-6B agent operating in pure text form. In the planning step, the agent reasons over a neural-symbolic design of 5 predefined states (Right, Wrong, Uncertain in category/attribute, relation/accessory, or location/behavior) and selects a corresponding action. In the tool-use step, it adaptively modulates both the image (cropping the object region, extending the crop for context, or highlighting with a red rectangle) and the text prompt, then dispatches to LLaVA-v1.6-34B for visual re-perception or Vicuna-v1.5-13B for expression rewriting. In the reflection step, a separate Vicuna-v1.5-13B instance verifies the refined expression against the caption and feeds structured feedback back to the agent for the next cycle (max 4 iterations). The 5-state design is grounded in a manual analysis of 300 filtered expressions across 6 misalignment aspects, and the agent is fine-tuned on 15k hand-verified examples to achieve 90–99.6% state-selection accuracy.

## Results

On the OmniLabel benchmark (ALL subset), Real-Model achieves 36.5% AP-des and 52.1% AP-des-pos using only 0.18M training images, surpassing the best baseline FIBER (22.3% AP-des, trained on 4M images) by 63.7%. On the DOD benchmark, Real-Model reaches 34.1% Full accuracy, outperforming APE-B (30.0%) by 13.7%. On RefCOCO/g/+ with RefCOCO training data, Real-Model achieves 91.3% val accuracy, exceeding APE-B (84.6%) by 6.7 points. On OVDEval, Real-Model attains 43.6% average AP, beating OmDet (40.4%) by 3.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Real-Model decisively outperforms all baselines on OmniLabel across every subset (COCO, O365, OI, ALL), with the largest margin on OI where it reaches 40.5% AP-des versus mm-GDINO's 23.2% (a 74.6% relative gain). On DOD, Real-Model (34.1% Full) surpasses APE-B (30.0%), GDINO (20.1%), mm-GDINO (24.2%), and OFA-DOD (21.6%), while OWL-V2 (9.6%) and UNINEXT (20.0%) trail significantly. On RefCOCO/g/+ without RefCOCO training data, APE-B (84.6% val) still outperforms Real-Model (74.0%), but when both are trained with RefCOCO data, Real-Model (91.3% val) exceeds APE-B (84.6%) by 6.7 points. On OVDEval, Real-Model (43.6% avg) beats OmDet (40.4%) overall, though OmDet wins the Relationship sub-dataset (51.8 vs. 41.9) due to HOI training data. Notably, Real-Model uses only 0.18M images versus 17.5M for GLIP and 12M for mm-GDINO, demonstrating that data quality from re-alignment compensates for a 100× reduction in data quantity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the mm-GDINO architecture (Zhao et al., 2024) as its base LOD detector and extends the recent trend of using VLMs (e.g., LLaVA) to auto-generate language expressions for LOD training data (Dang et al., 2024; Pi et al., 2024). Its key departure is introducing a structured agentic workflow—planning, tool use, and reflection—borrowed from the LLM-agent literature (HuggingGPT, Self-Refine, CRITIC) but repurposed specifically to diagnose and correct VLM hallucinations in vision-language training pairs. Unlike prior agentic systems that solve end-user tasks, Real-LOD operates as an offline data-quality pipeline, using a fine-tuned ChatGLM-6B agent to reason over expression-object alignment states and adaptively invoke VLM/LLM tools (cropping, highlighting, rewriting) to re-align language to visual objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical insight is that preserving VL alignment quality during data scaling can compensate for a ~100× reduction in training image count (0.18M vs. 12M–17.5M), suggesting that future LOD progress may come from smarter data curation rather than ever-larger corpora. The agentic re-alignment pipeline is fully offline and adds no inference cost, making it a drop-in data preprocessing step for any LOD training pipeline. However, the approach remains bounded by VLM perception quality: small/blurry objects and occluded background objects still produce uncorrectable errors (75% task-solved rate), and the method's generalizability beyond the specific VLM/LLM models used (LLaVA-34B, Vicuna-13B) is not tested. Open questions include whether the neural-symbolic state/action design transfers to other VLM hallucination patterns and whether the workflow can be made more efficient as VLMs improve.
