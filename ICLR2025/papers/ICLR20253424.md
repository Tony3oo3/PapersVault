## Problem

During diffusion sampling, the predicted clean-image distributions at successive timesteps are mismatched (incoherent), allowing random noise to inject inconsistent features and artifacts that persist through the remaining denoising steps. The standard remedy—classifier guidance—relies on one-hot class targets and discriminative features, which sacrifice diversity, risk adversarial generation, and require expensive noise-aware classifier training. The paper asks whether a richer, more coherent guidance target can fix sampling incoherence without these drawbacks.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step the method consumes the current noisy latent x_t, a class label c, and a pre-computed set of K representative vectors g(x₀|c) for that class (extracted once from the dataset via a frozen self-supervised ResNet-50 backbone and stored as model parameters). It outputs a refined clean-image prediction x̃₀ᵗ that is fed back into the standard DDPM reverse step to produce x_{t−1}; the final output is a generated RGB image at 64×64 or 256×256 resolution. Because the representative vectors are pre-computed and the self-supervised backbone is frozen, no additional training is needed at inference time.

## Method

RepG reformulates the DDPM sampling equation to expose incoherence as a distributional mismatch between predicted x̃₀ᵗ at different timesteps, then injects a gradient-based correction at every step: x̃₀ᵗ is updated by subtracting γ·∇_{x̃₀ᵗ} L(f_φ(x̃₀ᵗ), V), where f_φ is a frozen self-supervised encoder (MoCo-v2) and V is the full set of class-wise representative vectors. For each class, K representative vectors are selected as those closest to the class mean in the representation space, and the matching loss is either a contrastive loss (pushing the prediction toward its own class vectors while away from other classes) or a negative cosine-similarity loss. This treats sampling as a downstream task of the self-supervised model, steering features toward coherent, class-typical representations rather than toward a single discriminative one-hot target. The guidance is complementary to classifier-free guidance because it refines details and removes faulty features at the feature level rather than trading off quality against diversity at the class level, and it adds only ~6% computational overhead over vanilla sampling.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64, ADM-CLSFree+RepG achieves the best FID of 1.67 and sFID of 3.44, outperforming all other guidance methods including ADM-G+EDS+ProG (FID 1.77) and ADM-CLSFree (FID 1.89). On ImageNet 256×256, DiT-CLSFree+RepG achieves the best FID of 2.17 and sFID of 4.59, surpassing DiT-CLSFree (FID 2.27) and ADM-CLSFree (FID 3.76). RepG also improves vanilla ADM substantially (FID 2.07→1.69 on 64×64; 10.94→7.83 on 256×256) while maintaining higher recall (diversity) than classifier-guidance variants, which typically sacrifice recall for precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet 64×64, ADM-CLSFree+RepG (FID 1.67, sFID 3.44) outperforms all classifier-guidance variants: ADM-G+EDS+ProG (FID 1.77, sFID 4.25), ADM-G+PxP (FID 1.84), ADM-G+ProG (FID 1.87), and standalone ADM-CLSFree (FID 1.89, sFID 4.45). On ImageNet 256×256, DiT-CLSFree+RepG (FID 2.17, sFID 4.59) beats DiT-CLSFree (FID 2.27, sFID 4.80) and DiT-CLSFree-G+ProG (FID 2.25, sFID 4.56), while ADM-CLSFree+RepG (FID 3.34) surpasses ADM-CLSFree (FID 3.76) and ADM-G+ProG+EDS (FID 3.84). RepG consistently maintains higher recall than classifier-guidance methods (e.g., 0.62 vs. 0.52–0.57 on 64×64; 0.60 vs. 0.49–0.53 on 256×256), indicating better diversity preservation. Against non-diffusion baselines, RepG methods substantially outperform BigGAN (FID 4.06/7.03) and VQ-VAE-2 (FID 31.11) on both resolutions. The key differentiator is that RepG improves quality without the diversity trade-off inherent in classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance, as it operates at the feature level rather than the class level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the classifier guidance lineage (Dhariwal & Nichol 2021, ProG, PxP, EDS) by arguing that one-hot class vectors and discriminative classifier features are fundamentally insufficient targets for generative guidance. It repurposes pre-trained self-supervised models (MoCo-v2, SimSiam, MoCo-v3) as frozen guidance backbones, treating diffusion sampling as a downstream task of representation learning rather than requiring noise-aware classifier training. Conceptually, it also distinguishes itself from classifier-free guidance by framing its role as detail correction and incoherence repair rather than a quality-diversity tradeoff, making the two methods complementary rather than competing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, lightweight guidance mechanism that improves diffusion sampling quality without the diversity suppression characteristic of classifier guidance, and without the additional forward passes required by classifier-free guidance. The incoherence formulation (distributional mismatch of x̃₀ᵗ across timesteps) provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding sampling artifacts. However, on ImageNet 256×256, RepG alone does not surpass the strongest guidance baselines, and its gains are most pronounced at 64×64 where foreground object features dominate; open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image, video, or latent diffusion settings where the class-conditional structure is less explicit.
