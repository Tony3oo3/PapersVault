## Problem

Deep Gaussian processes arising as infinite-width limits of deep BNNs with bounded-variance weights have deterministic covariance kernels, which precludes any posterior learning of features (representation learning). While Aitchison et al. (2021) addressed this by injecting artificial noise into the kernel, such noise does not naturally emerge from a BNN architecture. This paper shows that using elliptically distributed weights with infinite variance at each layer naturally yields a deep kernel process with stochastic covariance kernels and α-stable marginals, enabling genuine feature learning without ad hoc noise injection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes real-valued observations y ∈ ℝⁿ paired with I-dimensional input matrix X ∈ ℝⁿˣᴵ, along with m new input points X* ∈ ℝᵐˣᴵ, and produces posterior predictive samples y*, the full stochastic covariance kernel matrix Σ⁽ᴸ⁾ at the design points, 90% credible intervals, and posterior samples of hidden-layer features. A key structural detail is that the covariance kernel is a random positive-definite matrix (not a fixed function of the inputs), with its randomness driven by positive α/2-stable scale variables at each layer, so the output kernel itself is a random object whose posterior is learned from data.

## Method

The paper proves that an L-layer feedforward BNN with elliptically distributed infinite-variance weights (w = (s₊)^(1/2)·w̃, where s₊ ~ S⁺_{α/2} and w̃ ~ N(0,1)) converges, as each layer width M_ℓ → ∞, to a process with elliptical α-stable marginals that admits a conditionally Gaussian representation: z_j^(ℓ) | s₊^(ℓ), Σ^(ℓ) ~ N(0, s₊^(ℓ)Σ^(ℓ)). Exploiting the Gaussian scale-mixture identity for α-stable characteristic functions, the authors derive a recursive formula (generalizing Cho & Saul, 2009) that links the conditional stochastic covariance kernels Σ^(ℓ) across layers, with the recursion depending on the random scale s₊^(ℓ−1) from the previous layer. Posterior inference proceeds via a Metropolis-Hastings MCMC sampler that proposes new positive α/2-stable scales at each layer from the prior and accepts based on the Gaussian likelihood ratio, yielding valid samples of the posterior predictive distribution and the random kernel. This kernel-space formulation avoids the O(n^{I+2}) combinatorial complexity of the feature-space approach of Loría & Bhadra (2024) and extends naturally to multi-layer architectures.

## Results

On the UCI benchmarks, Dα-KP (α=1, δ=1, L=2) achieves the best RMSE on Energy (0.46) and Yacht (0.31), and is a near-tie on Boston (2.59 vs. GP Bayes 2.58). In the 10-dimensional discontinuous simulation, Dα-KP achieves RMSE 8.08 and MAE 6.48, outperforming GP Bayes (10.39/8.32), DIWP (8.92/7.21), and NNGP (8.92/7.21). Credible interval coverage for Dα-KP stays near the nominal 90% across all settings (89.70%–95.32%), while DIWP and NNGP drop to 60–68% in 10 dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DIWP and NNGP (Aitchison et al., 2021), Dα-KP achieves lower RMSE on all three UCI datasets (e.g., 0.46 vs. 0.48 on Energy; 0.31 vs. 0.60 on Yacht) and substantially better credible-interval coverage in high dimensions (89.70% vs. 60.47% in 10D simulation). Against GP Bayes (Gramacy & Taddy, 2010), Dα-KP is essentially tied on Boston (2.59 vs. 2.58 RMSE) but clearly wins on Energy (0.46 vs. 0.68) and Yacht (0.31 vs. 0.48), and in the 10D simulation (8.08 vs. 10.39 RMSE). Against GP MLE (Dancik & Dorman, 2008), Dα-KP wins on all three UCI datasets and in the 1D and 2D simulations, with a small margin in 10D (8.08 vs. 8.32). Against the Stable method (Loría & Bhadra, 2024), Dα-KP is a close second in 1D (0.57 vs. 0.52 RMSE) and 2D (0.86 vs. 0.57), but the Stable method is infeasible beyond 2 dimensions due to its O(n^{I+2}) complexity, whereas Dα-KP scales to 10D and the 13-dimensional Boston dataset in roughly 10,000 seconds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the infinite-width limit framework of Neal (1996) and Cho & Saul (2009) from bounded-variance Gaussian priors to elliptical infinite-variance priors, showing the limiting process has α-stable marginals with a conditionally Gaussian representation. It generalizes Loría & Bhadra (2024) from shallow to deep networks while replacing their feature-space computation (exponential in input dimension) with a kernel-space recursion. Conceptually, it departs from Aitchison et al. (2021) by showing that stochastic kernels emerge naturally from the infinite-variance prior structure rather than requiring artificial noise injection into the kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a design tension in the deep kernel process literature: deep GPs have deterministic kernels that preclude feature learning, and prior fixes (DIWP, DWP) rely on subjective noise choices. By showing that infinite-variance priors naturally yield stochastic kernels with a recursive Cho–Saul structure, the method provides a principled, architecture-consistent alternative. Open questions include scalability beyond MCMC (variational approximations, inducing points), extension to more general activations, and whether the heavy-tailed SGD noise observed empirically (Simsekli et al., 2019) admits a similar non-Gaussian NTK analog. The finding that depth from L=2 to L=16 yields negligible gains suggests the stable process may already capture a rich enough function class with one hidden layer, though this is conjectural.
