## Problem

Existing bilevel optimization algorithms require prior knowledge of problem-specific parameters (Lipschitz constants, strong convexity parameters, optimal function values) to set their stepsizes, making them difficult to deploy in practice where such quantities are unknown. While partial adaptive methods exist, no fully tuning-free bilevel optimizer with matching convergence guarantees had been developed. This paper closes that gap by introducing the first bilevel optimization algorithms that completely eliminate stepsize tuning while preserving theoretical convergence rates.

## Input / Output

The algorithms require only the outer function f(x,y), inner function g(x,y), arbitrary positive initial stepsize constants α₀, β₀, γ₀ (independent of problem parameters), and a total iteration budget T. The output is an ε-accurate stationary point x̄ satisfying ‖∇Φ(x̄)‖² ≤ ε, certified via the average squared hypergradient norm over T iterations. Notably, D-TFBO additionally requires T to set sub-loop stopping criteria (ε_y = ε_v = 1/T), while S-TFBO can be modified to a repeat-until-convergence loop that removes even this dependence.

## Method

Both D-TFBO and S-TFBO adapt stepsizes via the 'inverse of cumulative gradient norms' strategy, where each stepsize is 1/β with β² accumulating squared gradient norms over iterations. D-TFBO uses a double-loop structure with cold-start stepsizes (reset at each outer iteration) in sub-loops for the inner problem and a linear system approximating the Hessian-inverse-vector product, achieving O(1/ε) outer iterations but O(1/ε²) total gradient complexity. S-TFBO adopts a fully single-loop design where all three variables update simultaneously, with a theory-motivated coupled stepsize design: the stepsize for v uses 1/max{β, γ} to prevent approximation error from y from propagating, and the stepsize for x uses 1/(α·max{β, γ}) to mitigate errors from both y and v. This coupling is the key technical insight that enables S-TFBO to achieve O(1/ε · log⁴(1/ε)) gradient complexity, nearly matching well-tuned single-loop methods up to polylogarithmic factors.

## Results

On the Split CIFAR100 coreset selection task, D-TFBO achieves the best forgetting measure (FGT_T = 0.041±0.005) under the balanced setting and second-best under the imbalanced setting (FGT_T = 0.029±0.002), while BCSR attains the highest average accuracy (A_T = 61.60% balanced, 47.30% imbalanced). On regularization selection (Covtype), D-TFBO achieves the fastest convergence rate among all compared bilevel methods, while S-TFBO converges slightly more slowly but remains comparable. In the sensitivity analysis, D-TFBO and S-TFBO show only 0.3% and 0.4% relative average accuracy change across initial stepsizes {2,4,6,8}, compared to 5.8% for BCSR, demonstrating substantially greater robustness to hyperparameter initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Covtype regularization selection and MNIST data hyper-cleaning tasks, D-TFBO achieves the fastest convergence rate among all bilevel baselines (AmIGO, BSA, FSLA, MRBO, SOBA, StocBiO, SUSTAIN, TTSA, VRBO), while S-TFBO converges slightly more slowly but remains comparable to these well-tuned methods. On Split CIFAR100 coreset selection, D-TFBO achieves the best FGT_T (0.041) under the balanced setting, outperforming BCSR (0.051), iCaRL (0.044), and all non-bilevel baselines (k-means, Grad Matching, GCR, Greedy Coreset, PBCS, Uniform Sampling), though BCSR still leads on A_T (61.60% vs. 59.54%). Under the imbalanced setting, BCSR achieves the best FGT_T (0.022) and A_T (47.30%), with D-TFBO in second place on both metrics (0.029 and 46.68%). The key differentiator is robustness: in the sensitivity analysis, D-TFBO and S-TFBO show only 0.3% and 0.4% relative accuracy variation across initial stepsizes, versus 5.8% for BCSR, confirming the tuning-free design's practical advantage. All non-bilevel continual learning baselines (k-means features/embedding, Uniform Sampling, iCaRL, Grad Matching, GCR, Greedy Coreset, PBCS) are outperformed by both D-TFBO and S-TFBO on FGT_T in the balanced setting, with D-TFBO also surpassing them on A_T except for BCSR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the 'inverse of cumulative gradient norms' stepsize strategy from single-level adaptive optimization (Ward et al., 2020; Xie et al., 2020) to the bilevel setting, where the key challenge is that stepsizes for the outer variable x, inner variable y, and linear-system variable v are interdependent. It departs from prior adaptive bilevel methods (Fan et al., 2024; Antonakopoulos et al., 2024) that still require some problem-specific parameters (Lipschitz constants, strong convexity parameter, or optimal function values) by eliminating all such dependencies. The two-stage analysis framework from Xie et al. (2020) is extended to handle intertwined multi-stage dynamics across variables, a technical challenge absent in single-level settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first fully tuning-free bilevel optimization algorithms with matching convergence guarantees, removing a significant practical barrier for applying bilevel methods in meta-learning, hyperparameter optimization, and continual learning where problem parameters are often unknown. A notable limitation is that D-TFBO's gradient complexity degrades to O(1/ε²) versus O(1/ε) for well-tuned double-loop methods, due to the sub-linear convergence of the tuning-free sub-loops. Open questions include extending the analysis to the stochastic setting and to non-strongly-convex inner problems, which would broaden applicability substantially.
