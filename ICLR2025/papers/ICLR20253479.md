## Problem

Spiking Neural Networks offer energy-efficient computation but the community has focused on scaling model size, undermining their advantage on resource-constrained edge devices. Existing SNN compression methods that combine quantization and pruning either rely on unstructured pruning (requiring specialized hardware) or suffer severe accuracy drops, with evaluations limited to simple datasets. The paper addresses how to jointly apply hardware-friendly uniform quantization and structured pruning to SNNs while preserving high accuracy on challenging benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR, 64×64 TinyImageNet, 224×224 ImageNet), event-stream data from dynamic vision sensors (48×48 DVS-CIFAR10), and SAR/remote-sensing images for detection, all processed over 2–10 discrete timesteps by LIF neurons. Outputs are class labels for classification tasks and bounding boxes (mAP@0.5) for object detection. A distinguishing feature is the extreme model-size reduction: weights are stored at 8, 4, or 2-bit precision with structured channel pruning, yielding models as small as 0.39 MB on CIFAR-100 while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Method

QP-SNN integrates two key innovations into a quantize-then-prune pipeline. First, a weight rescaling strategy (ReScaW) divides each layer's weights by a scale coefficient γ (default: 1-norm mean) before uniform quantization, spreading the weight distribution across the full [−1,1] range so that the allocated bit-width is used efficiently rather than wasting most grids on a narrow cluster near zero. Second, a novel SVS pruning criterion performs singular value decomposition on the time-averaged spike matrix of each convolutional kernel and counts the number of singular values exceeding a small threshold ε as the kernel's importance score, providing input-robust evaluation that avoids the SCA criterion's sensitivity to specific input samples. The workflow trains the quantized SNN with spatio-temporal backpropagation and a straight-through estimator for the quantization non-differentiability, then applies SVS-based structured pruning to remove redundant channels, followed by fine-tuning to recover performance.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, QP-SNN (ResNet20, 4-bit, T=2) achieves 95.41% top-1 accuracy at only 3.16 MB, outperforming the best structured-pruning baseline (Li et al., 91.67% at 17.32 MB) by 3.74% with 82% smaller model. On CIFAR-100, QP-SNN reaches 75.77% (4-bit, T=2, 3.35 MB), surpassing Shi et al. (72.34%, 13.18 MB) by 3.43% with 75% less storage. On TinyImageNet, QP-SNN (VGG-16, 4-bit) achieves 53.11% at 3.78 MB, improving over Li et al. (49.14%, 19.76 MB) by 3.97% with 81% smaller size. On DVS-CIFAR10, QP-SNN matches Shi et al.'s 81.90% accuracy (81.80%) while reducing model size by 93.6% (0.90 MB vs. 14.08 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QP-SNN consistently outperforms all structured-pruning baselines (Li et al., ICML24) across every dataset: +3.74% on CIFAR-10 (95.41% vs. 91.67%), +10.24% on CIFAR-100 (75.77% vs. 65.53%), +3.97% on TinyImageNet (53.11% vs. 49.14%), and +8.80% on DVS-CIFAR10 (81.80% vs. 73.00%), while using 75–93% less storage. Against the unstructured-pruning baseline (Shi et al., ICLR24), QP-SNN wins on CIFAR-10 (95.41% vs. 92.63%) and CIFAR-100 (75.77% vs. 72.34%) with dramatically smaller models (3.16 MB vs. 50.28 MB; 3.35 MB vs. 13.18 MB), and matches on DVS-CIFAR10 (81.80% vs. 81.90%) at 93.6% smaller size. On TinyImageNet, QP-SNN (53.11%, 4-bit) trails Shi et al.'s 8-bit result (61.36%) but exceeds their 4-bit result (58.06%) is not directly comparable due to different architectures; against the same-architecture Li et al. baseline, QP-SNN leads by 3.97%. On ImageNet-1k, QP-SNN is the first to report structured-pruning results, achieving comparable accuracy to Shi et al.'s unstructured pruning with a 15.55% model size reduction. For the Spikingformer architecture, QP-SNN reduces model size by 8.28× and power by 2.25× at a modest 2.15% accuracy drop (76.94% vs. 79.09%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SCA-based structured pruning criterion from Li et al. (ICML 2024) as its baseline, and extends the broader SNN compression literature (Chowdhury et al. 2021, Deng et al. 2021, Rathi et al. 2018) that combines quantization and pruning. The key departures are twofold: (1) identifying that vanilla uniform quantization wastes most of the allocated bit-width because SNN weights cluster near zero, and proposing a simple rescaling (ReScaW) to fix this; and (2) replacing the input-sensitive SCA pruning criterion with an SVS criterion based on singular values of spatiotemporal spike activity. Unlike prior combined approaches that rely on unstructured pruning requiring specialized hardware, QP-SNN is explicitly designed for standard hardware accelerators via uniform quantization and structured pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QP-SNN addresses a practical gap: high-performance SNNs are too large for edge deployment, and prior compression methods either sacrifice hardware-friendliness (unstructured pruning) or performance (aggressive quantization on simple datasets only). The ReScaW strategy is a minimal, easily adoptable fix, and the SVS criterion offers a principled, input-robust alternative to SCA. Limitations include manually selected pruning rates without automated search, evaluation primarily on CIFAR-scale datasets (ImageNet results use a different architecture for fair comparison), and the approach has not been validated on the largest SNN architectures. Open questions include whether the quantize-then-prune ordering and the specific SVS threshold generalize to very deep or transformer-based SNNs at scale.
