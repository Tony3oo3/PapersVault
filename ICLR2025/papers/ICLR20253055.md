## Problem

Existing theoretical proofs of neural collapse (NC) rely heavily on the unconstrained features model (UFM), which treats penultimate-layer features as free variables and is therefore data-agnostic, failing to capture end-to-end DNN training. Moving beyond UFM, prior results either apply only to shallow networks (≤3 layers) or require strong structural assumptions (symmetric quasi-interpolation, block-structured NTK, geodesic features). This paper provides the first end-to-end proof that gradient descent with weight decay on deep networks provably produces all three components of neural collapse (NC1–NC3).

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard image classification datasets (MNIST at 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10 at 32×32 RGB) and trains networks consisting of a non-linear backbone (MLP or ResNet20) followed by a linear head of at least two layers. The analytical output is not just class predictions but the geometric structure of the penultimate-layer feature matrix: within-class variability (NC1), conditioning of the class-mean matrix (NC2), and cosine alignment between features and last-layer weight rows (NC3). The linear head depth is a key architectural parameter that the theory predicts controls the strength of NC2 and NC3.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that approximate interpolation, approximate balancedness of consecutive linear-layer weights (‖W_{ℓ+1}ᵀW_{ℓ+1} − W_ℓW_ℓᵀ‖_op small), and bounded representations/weights together imply NC1, while additionally bounding the condition number of the full linear-head product yields NC2 and NC3. Theorem 4.4 then proves these conditions hold under λ-regularized GD on wide networks with pyramidal topology and smooth activations, via a two-phase timescale separation: a short NTK-like phase (logarithmic in 1/λ) achieves exponential loss decrease to near-interpolation, followed by a longer phase (linear in 1/λ) where weight decay drives the linear layers toward balancedness at an exponential rate. For NC2/NC3, the paper shows that either global minimizers of the ℓ₂-regularized loss or solutions at the edge of stability (large learning rates) have bounded conditioning of the linear head, which combined with balancedness forces κ(W_L) → 1 as the linear-head depth L₂ grows. Numerical experiments on MLPs and ResNet20 confirm that NC2 improves progressively with linear-head depth and that non-linear layers become more balanced and less non-linear as network depth increases.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that NC1 (within-class variability collapse) holds when the network approximately interpolates (‖Z_L − Y‖_F ≤ ε₁) and the linear layers are approximately balanced (‖W_{ℓ+1}^T W_{ℓ+1} − W_ℓ W_ℓ^T‖_op ≤ ε₂), with NC1(Z_{L−1}) = O((ε₁ + √ε₂)²). Theorem 4.4 proves that gradient descent with weight decay on a wide-first-layer pyramidal network satisfies these conditions, yielding the first end-to-end guarantee of NC1. Empirically, on ResNet20 + MLP head on CIFAR-10 and MLP on MNIST, the NC2 metric (conditioning number of class-mean matrix) improves progressively with each additional linear layer in the head, while NC1 shows little dependence on linear head depth. Linear layer balancedness decreases exponentially during training, and non-linear layers become both more balanced and less non-linear as the non-linear depth increases from 4 to 12 layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not compare its method against competing baselines in the traditional sense. Instead, it provides the first provable end-to-end guarantee of neural collapse for deep networks trained with gradient descent and weight decay, moving beyond the unconstrained features model (UFM) framework used by prior works (Mixon et al. 2020; Han et al. 2022; Zhou et al. 2022a) which treat penultimate-layer features as free variables. The empirical validation confirms theoretical predictions: NC2 improves with linear head depth (consistent with the κ(W_L) ≤ c₃^{1/L₂} bound), and the final linear layers are balanced at convergence. The paper notes that its analysis requires at least two linear layers in the head, whereas prior UFM-based results are data-agnostic and existing end-to-end results (Kothapalli & Tirer 2024; Hong & Ling 2024) are limited to at most three-layer networks or require strong assumptions like symmetric quasi-interpolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Unconstrained Features Model (UFM) framework for studying neural collapse, which treats penultimate-layer features as free variables and is data-agnostic. It builds on Nguyen & Mondelli (2020) for the Polyak-Lojasiewicz convergence analysis of deep networks with pyramidal topology, and on Du et al. (2018) for the balancedness dynamics of linear layers under weight decay. The key conceptual departure is identifying balancedness of consecutive linear layers as the mechanism that, combined with low training error, provably yields NC1, NC2, and NC3 in end-to-end trained deep networks—something prior work could only establish under much stronger assumptions (symmetric quasi-interpolation, block-structured NTK, or geodesic feature structure) or for at most three-layer networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous end-to-end proof that gradient descent with weight decay produces neural collapse in deep networks, closing a significant gap between the UFM-based theory and practical training. However, the results require at least two linear layers in the head, pyramidal topology, smooth activations, and learning rates that scale as c^{-L} for depth L—conditions that are restrictive relative to practice. The authors acknowledge a gap between the theoretically required learning rate (η ~ c^{-L}) and the 'edge of stability' regime (η ~ L^{-1}) used in practice, leaving open whether a more careful analysis could bridge this. A natural future direction is proving NC without the two-linear-layer requirement, which empirical evidence suggests is not necessary.
