## Problem

Aligning LLMs to individual users or specific tasks typically requires thousands of pairwise preference annotations or large SFT corpora, which is infeasible for personalization. Few-shot prompting is data-efficient but brittle and cannot overcome the strong RLHF priors baked into instruction-following models. The paper asks whether a very small number of user-provided demonstrations (<10) can serve as a sufficient feedback signal to steer an LLM's style and task behavior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a handful of expert demonstrations (prompt-completion pairs, typically 1–7, sourced from user edits of model outputs, past interaction logs, or scratch writing) along with a base instruction-following LLM (Mistral 7B Instruct). It produces a LoRA adapter that generates style-matched text for the target individual; the adapter can be selectively dropped via a prompted router (MoE-style) to preserve general capabilities like code generation. Demonstrations are consumed as positive examples in a contrastive objective rather than as in-context examples or SFT targets.

## Method

DITTO's core insight is that a small set of expert demonstrations can bootstrap an unbounded preference dataset by treating them as preferred over the model's own outputs at every training iteration. Concretely, it first SFTs on the demonstrations to obtain an initial policy π₀, then iteratively samples M negatives from the current policy for each demonstration prompt, constructs a temporal ranking of all policies seen so far (expert ≻ π_t ≻ π_{t-1} ≻ … ≻ π₀), and samples preference batches (70% expert-vs-current, 20% replay, 10% intermodel) to update via DPO while keeping the reference policy fixed. The intermodel and replay comparisons smooth the implicit reward landscape and combat overfitting in the low-data regime. Theoretically, DITTO is derived as an online imitation learning algorithm via the DPO reparameterization of the KL-constrained RL objective, and a lemma guarantees that the learned policy can extrapolate beyond the demonstrator's expected reward when the optimal policy's objective value exceeds the dataset's average reward by a KL-divergence margin.

## Results

On the CMCC benchmark (emails/blog posts), DITTO achieves a 71.67% average GPT-4-eval win rate, outperforming SFT (56.78%), SPIN (51.56%), and few-shot Mistral (46.89%). On CCAT50 (news articles), DITTO reaches 82.50%, surpassing SFT (73.89%) and SPIN (62.13%). In a user study (N=16) with human-annotated pairwise preferences on email-writing tasks, DITTO achieves a 68.8% win rate versus 55.5% for SFT, 51.6% for few-shot GPT-4, and 27.3% for zero-shot GPT-4. DITTO's improvements over all baselines are statistically significant (ANOVA + Tukey, p < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SPIN (Chen et al., 2024), DITTO outperforms by an average of 9.3 percentage points across both benchmarks (71.67% vs. 51.56% on CMCC; 82.50% vs. 62.13% on CCAT50). The authors attribute SPIN's lower performance to design choices (updating the reference policy, excluding inter-policy and replay comparisons) that are suited to SFT-scale datasets rather than the few-demonstration regime. Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as a sample-efficiency baseline, DITTO with only 4 demonstrations matches or exceeds DPO trained on up to 500 pairwise preferences sampled from the base policy; even when the base policy is first fine-tuned on the demonstrations, DPO still requires >500 preference pairs to approach DITTO's performance. DITTO also outperforms SFT by 11.7% points on average, few-shot GPT-4 by 18% points, and zero-shot GPT-4 by over 50% points, confirming that the iterative online comparison mechanism provides substantial gains over both prompting and single-pass fine-tuning approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DITTO derives its core algorithm from online imitation learning (Brown et al., 2019, 2020a), where ranked demonstrations enable a policy to extrapolate beyond the demonstrator, and instantiates the training step with DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023). It departs from SPIN (Chen et al., 2024) by fixing the reference policy and introducing intermodel/replay comparisons to combat overfitting in the few-demonstration regime, and from standard RLHF by replacing thousands of human-annotated pairwise preferences with comparisons auto-generated from the model's own outputs against a handful of expert demonstrations. The theoretical derivation via the maximum-entropy RL fixed point (Ziebart, 2010) shows DITTO is equivalent to an online imitation learning objective, unifying the preference-optimization and imitation-learning literatures for the LLM setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DITTO enables individual-level LLM personalization with under 10 demonstrations, an order of magnitude fewer data points than pairwise preference collection, and the sample-efficiency result (4 demos outperforming 500 preferences) suggests demonstrated feedback is a fundamentally more efficient signal for individual alignment. Key limitations include evaluation on a single 7B model family, reliance on GPT-4 as evaluator (with known self-enhancement bias), a small user study (N=16), and observed forgetting of general capabilities. Open questions include how to optimally select demonstrations, whether the approach scales to larger models or multi-domain personalization, and how demonstrated feedback compares to other feedback modalities (principles, verbal feedback) across different user populations.
