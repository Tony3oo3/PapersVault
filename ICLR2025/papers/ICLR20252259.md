## Problem

Traditional reward model training conflates genuine contextual quality signals with context-free artifacts (length, format, n-gram patterns), causing the RM to exploit spurious correlations rather than learn true preference. Existing mitigations target only specific observable artifacts like length, leaving a general mechanism to eliminate arbitrary unobserved artifacts unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252259/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pairwise ranking model takes a prompt and two responses formatted as a single text sequence with special tokens ([CONTEXT], [RESPONSE A], [RESPONSE B]) and outputs the probability of decoding 'A' as the preference probability P(y₁ ≻ y₂|x). The augmented training data introduces two new triplet types beyond the original (prompt, chosen, rejected): 'non-contextuals' where one response is swapped from a different example (contextual response always wins), and 'neutrals' where both responses come from other examples (labeled Tie with 0.5 probability).

## Method

The paper formulates reward model training within a causal DAG where the preference label C is influenced by a contextual signal S (depending on prompt and both responses) and a context-free artifact A (depending only on responses), proving that traditional training cannot distinguish whether A causally affects C. To eliminate the A→C edge, they augment the training set by randomly permuting responses across examples: for each original triplet, they generate non-contextual triplets (one response from another example, contextual response wins) and neutral triplets (both responses from other examples, labeled Tie), enforcing the d-separation conditions A⊥C|(Y₁,Y₂) and A⊥C|S. A filtering step retains only augmented examples where the pre-trained RM's prediction deviates from ground truth by ≥0.2, yielding 2.4M training examples. The resulting RRM is trained with the same pairwise ranking objective on Gemma-2-9b-it, and downstream DPO policies trained with RRM-labeled data show substantially improved MT-Bench and AlpacaEval-2 scores with shorter generated responses.

## Results

On RewardBench, RRM improves average accuracy from 80.61% (RM) to 84.15%, with the largest gains on Chat Hard (51.54% → 65.57%) and Safety (78.54% → 83.90%), at the cost of a slight drop on Reasoning (94.58% → 90.62%). For DPO-aligned policies, RRM improves MT-Bench overall score from 7.27 to 8.31 and AlpacaEval-2 length-controlled win-rate from 33.46% to 52.49%, while reducing average response length from 2416 to 1723 characters. Best-of-N policies also benefit: AlpacaEval-2 LC win-rate rises from 36.87% to 47.68% (N=8) and from 40.52% to 62.82% (N=64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary external baseline is ODIN (Chen et al., ICML 2024), which decomposes reward into quality and length components. On DPO-aligned policies, RRM outperforms ODIN on AlpacaEval-2 length-controlled win-rate (52.49% vs. 48.29%) and raw win-rate (43.31% vs. 37.13%), while ODIN edges out RRM on MT-Bench overall (8.39 vs. 8.31) and generates shorter responses (1559 vs. 1723 characters). Against the standard RM baseline, RRM delivers substantial gains across all policy types: AlpacaEval-2 LC win-rate improves by 10.8–22.3 points depending on N, and response length drops by roughly 25–40%. The ablation removing 'Neutrals' (tie-labeled augmented triplets) shows only marginal degradation (51.73% vs. 52.49% LC), confirming that the core non-contextual augmentation drives most of the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions reward hacking mitigation within a causal inference framework, building on the DAG/d-separation formalism from Pearl (2009) and applying it to the preference learning setting. It subsumes and generalizes several prior approaches: ODIN (Chen et al.) is shown to be a special case with a single observed artifact (length), length-controlled AlpacaEval-2 (Dubois et al.) is equivalent in the single-artifact regime, and Contrast Instructions (Shen et al.) addresses a related consistency issue but without the causal grounding. The key departure is the permutation-based data augmentation derived from d-separation rules, which simultaneously handles multiple and potentially unobserved artifacts without requiring explicit specification of which ones exist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal framing provides a principled, artifact-agnostic solution to reward hacking that avoids the whack-a-mole problem of individually penalizing each known bias (length, format, n-grams). The practical impact is significant: the ~19-point AlpacaEval-2 LC improvement and shorter generated responses suggest that much of the apparent quality gap in prior reward models was artifact-driven. However, the approach assumes preference labels are purely prompt-dependent (discussed in Appendix A.7), which may not hold for safety or diversity preferences where some context-free signals are legitimate. Open questions include whether the augmentation strategy scales to truly unobserved artifacts, how to handle cases where rejected responses are genuinely unacceptable (not just suboptimal), and whether the 0.5 tie assumption for non-contextual pairs introduces systematic bias.
