## Problem

Existing crystal generative models either produce structures with only trivial P1 symmetry or merely replicate space groups from training data, failing to generate the diverse, non-trivial symmetries that characterize real crystals. This limits their utility for discovering materials with specific symmetry-dependent properties such as piezoelectricity. The paper addresses this by building symmetry directly into the generative process rather than treating it as an afterthought.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250833/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is conditioned on a target space group type (one of 230) and a number of orbit representatives M, then jointly denoises lattice parameters (6-d vector k), fractional coordinates of M asymmetric-unit atoms, their element types, and their site symmetry groups. The output is a full conventional unit cell crystal obtained by replicating the asymmetric unit via the space group operations, with a final projection step that snaps coordinates to the nearest valid Wyckoff position. Because the model operates on the asymmetric unit (average M≈4.7 vs. N≈18.9 atoms in MP-20), it achieves a ~16× reduction in memory and ~2× speedup in training time compared to full-unit-cell baselines.

## Method

SymmCD decomposes each crystal into its asymmetric unit (orbit representatives with fractional coordinates and element types) and the site symmetry group of each representative, then learns their joint distribution via a denoising diffusion model. Site symmetries and space groups are encoded as binary matrices over 15 crystallographic axes (13 possible operations per axis for site symmetries; 26 for space groups plus a 7-d Bravais lattice one-hot), enabling information sharing across the 230 space groups and avoiding data fragmentation. The diffusion process applies continuous (Gaussian/wrapped-normal) noise to coordinates and lattice parameters, and discrete (categorical transition-matrix) noise to atom types and site symmetries, with transition matrices parameterized to converge to data marginals. A fully-connected GNN denoiser (8 layers, 1024-d embeddings) predicts all four noise components simultaneously, using Fourier embeddings of inter-representative displacements for periodic invariance. At sampling time, the denoised site symmetries are projected to the nearest valid point group and coordinates to the nearest compatible Wyckoff position before the asymmetric unit is replicated into the full crystal.

## Results

On MP-20, SymmCD (All SGs) achieves a space-group distribution distance d_sg of 0.164 ± 0.003, matching DiffCSP++ (0.16) and far outperforming CDVAE (0.69), DiffCSP (0.44), and FlowMM (0.50). SymmCD generates the most diverse structural templates (2,794 unique, 1,654 novel) and achieves the lowest expected maximum force after DFT relaxation (E[|∇|_max] = 0.000057 for All SGs, 0.000038 for 10 SGs), indicating structures closest to true local minima. For S.U.N. stability, SymmCD (10 SGs) yields 7.59% relaxed S.U.N. crystals, competitive with DiffCSP (8.92%) and DiffCSP++ (8.62%). The asymmetric-unit representation enables a 16× larger batch size (8192 vs. 512) and 8.6× less memory (3.6 GB vs. 31 GB) compared to a full unit-cell model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymmCD (All SGs) ties DiffCSP++ on the space-group distribution distance (d_sg = 0.164 vs. 0.16) and substantially outperforms CDVAE (0.69), DiffCSP (0.44), and FlowMM (0.50), confirming that only these two methods faithfully reproduce the dataset's diverse space-group distribution. On template diversity, SymmCD generates 2,794 unique templates with 1,654 novel ones, far exceeding DiffCSP++ (1,905 unique but only 110 novel, since it relies on pre-defined templates) and all other baselines. For S.U.N. stability, SymmCD (10 SGs) achieves 7.59% relaxed S.U.N., slightly below DiffCSP (8.92%) and DiffCSP++ (8.62%) but above FlowMM (6.49%) and CDVAE (4.26%). In DFT relaxation, SymmCD produces structures with the lowest expected maximum force (0.000057 All SGs / 0.000038 10 SGs), beating DiffCSP++ (0.000072), FlowMM (0.000083), CDVAE (0.000235), and DiffCSP (0.000494). SymmCD's main weakness is lower structural validity (90.34% vs. 99.44–99.97% for baselines), likely because the asymmetric-unit representation does not directly encode inter-atom distances.

## Relation to Prior Work

SymmCD builds on the diffusion-based crystal generation framework of DiffCSP (Jiao et al., 2023) for its GNN denoiser architecture and continuous diffusion on coordinates, and on DiffCSP++ (Jiao et al., 2024) for its space-group-constrained lattice parameterization. It also adopts discrete diffusion from Austin et al. (2021) with the marginal-converging transition matrix parameterization of Vignac et al. (2023) for categorical variables. The key departure is that prior methods (CDVAE, DiffCSP, FlowMM) generate full unit cells without symmetry guarantees—often collapsing to the trivial P1 space group—while SymmCD generates only the asymmetric unit plus site symmetries and then 'unfolds' via replication, ensuring the output crystal exactly matches the target space group. A novel binary-matrix representation of site and space group symmetries (15 axes × 13/26 operations) replaces the non-generalizable Wyckoff position labels used by concurrent work (CrystalFormer, WyCryst), enabling information sharing across all 230 space groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental gap in crystal generative models: prior methods either produce crystals with trivial symmetry or merely replicate database symmetries without guaranteeing correctness. By working in the asymmetric unit, SymmCD achieves a ~4× reduction in the number of atoms to model (M≈4.7 vs N≈18.9 on MP-20), yielding 16× larger batch sizes and 8.6× less memory, which is practically significant for scaling to larger crystals (validated on MPTS-52). A notable limitation is that structural validity drops to ~90% (vs ~99% for CDVAE), likely because the model cannot directly see inter-atom distances in the compact representation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to composition-conditioned generation (currently requiring a space-group-first sampling strategy), molecular crystals, and polycrystalline materials.
