## Problem

Current one-step policies (e.g., next-token prediction LLMs) lack long-term planning ability, while explicit search methods like MCTS incur high inference costs and accumulate errors from imperfect value networks. The paper asks whether a model can implicitly search over future representations to enhance action prediction without any explicit search algorithm at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253610/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a fixed-length 77-character FEN string representing the current chess board state as input and produces the next move in UCI notation (from 1968 legal moves) as the primary output, jointly with a future trajectory of h alternating states and actions (S-ASA paradigm). During inference, the action is selected via argmax over the joint probability p(a_i, z_i | s_i) rather than marginalizing over all futures, which is intractable due to the exponential game tree (branching factor ~31). The GPT-2 transformer is modified from causal to full bidirectional attention to allow future tokens to inform the current action prediction.

## Method

DIFFUSEARCH models the joint distribution of the next action and a future trajectory (states and actions over horizon h) using discrete absorbing diffusion, where the forward process corrupts tokens to a [MASK] state and the learned backward process iteratively denoises them. The key mechanism is that the bidirectional self-attention across all tokens (current state, next action, and future trajectory) combined with the multi-step denoising process allows future information to implicitly refine the current action prediction, replacing the iterative select-expand-backup loop of MCTS. Training uses a weighted cross-entropy loss derived from the variational bound, with a linear reweighting schedule λ_t = 1 - (t-1)/T, supervised by Stockfish 16 oracle trajectories from Lichess games. At inference, an easy-first decoding strategy (resetting the least confident tokens at each diffusion step) is used, and deeper search is achieved by extending the context length rather than running more simulations.

## Results

On 10k Lichess training games, DIFFUSEARCH achieves 41.31% action accuracy, 39.49% puzzle accuracy, and 1728 Tournament Elo, outperforming the one-step Transformer (S-A) by 19.2% in action accuracy and the MCTS-enhanced policy by 14% in action accuracy. On 100k games, DIFFUSEARCH reaches 48.66% action accuracy, 58.46% puzzle accuracy, and 1995 Elo, maintaining a 540 Elo advantage over the MCTS-enhanced baseline. The oracle Stockfish 16 (0.05s/move) achieves 100% action accuracy and 2689 Elo, establishing the upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three Transformer baselines from Ruoss et al. (2024) (S-A, S-V, SA-V), DIFFUSEARCH wins decisively on all three metrics: on 10k games it achieves 41.31% action accuracy vs. 22.10% (S-A), 21.45% (S-V), and 31.50% (SA-V), and 39.49% puzzle accuracy vs. 3.95%, 12.20%, and 12.74% respectively. Against the MCTS-enhanced policy (following AlphaZero's framework from Silver et al., 2017a), DIFFUSEARCH achieves a 542 Elo advantage (1728 vs. 1186 on 10k games; 1995 vs. 1469 on 100k games) and a 30% improvement in puzzle-solving (39.49% vs. 6.85% on 10k; 58.46% vs. 20.71% on 100k), despite using no explicit search at inference. Against the Stockfish 16 oracle (0.05s/move), DIFFUSEARCH trails substantially (1728 vs. 2689 Elo), which is expected since Stockfish serves as the labeling oracle and uses exhaustive search. Notably, DIFFUSEARCH uses 20× fewer training records than the SA-V baseline yet still outperforms it, and its advantage over MCTS grows with data scale (from 542 to 526 Elo gap), suggesting the implicit search approach scales favorably.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffuSearch sits at the intersection of discrete diffusion modeling (Austin et al., 2021; Hoogeboom et al., 2021) and the AlphaZero lineage of policy+search architectures (Silver et al., 2017a). It departs from prior unified world-model-and-policy approaches like Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) and Decision Diffuser (Ajay et al., 2022) by using discrete (absorbing) diffusion rather than continuous, modeling both future states and actions jointly rather than states alone, and conditioning on a given board state rather than training unconditionally. The supervised training paradigm, FEN/UCI data format, and baseline Transformer models (S-A, S-V, SA-V) are directly inherited from Ruoss et al. (2024), making this a controlled comparison where the only variable is the search mechanism (explicit MCTS vs. implicit diffusion denoising).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single diffusion-denoising forward pass can outperform 100-simulation MCTS by 540 Elo in chess—a domain where search is considered indispensable—challenges the assumption that explicit tree search is the only viable mechanism for multi-step planning. The ablations showing that future *states* (not just actions) are essential, and that bidirectional attention layers act as the 'search depth' analog, provide mechanistic insight transferable to LLM planning. Key limitations include reliance on Stockfish as an oracle (no self-play), a modest 100k-game dataset, and a maximum search depth of 7, leaving open whether the approach scales to the 10M+ game regimes or to natural-language tasks where the 'state space' is far less structured.
