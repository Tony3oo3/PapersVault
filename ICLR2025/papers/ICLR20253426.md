## Problem

Offline goal-conditioned RL (GCRL) is a natural analogue of unsupervised pre-training for RL, yet the field lacks a standardized benchmark that systematically evaluates algorithm capabilities across key dimensions such as goal stitching, long-horizon reasoning, and stochastic control. Prior evaluations relied on limited tasks (e.g., D4RL AntMaze with single-goal evaluation, simple Fetch pick-and-place) that failed to distinguish between algorithms or to probe multiple capabilities simultaneously.

## Input / Output

Each task provides an offline trajectory dataset (collected by scripted or learned policies with controlled noise levels) and five pre-defined state-goal pairs for evaluation. The policy receives either a full low-dimensional state vector or a 64×64×3 RGB image as observation, concatenated with a goal state (a full state, not a partial one), and outputs actions; episodes terminate upon reaching the goal. The benchmark spans three task families—locomotion (PointMaze, AntMaze, HumanoidMaze, AntSoccer), manipulation (Cube, Scene, Puzzle), and drawing (Powderworld)—with 85 total datasets varying in difficulty, suboptimality, and stochasticity.

## Method

OGBench introduces 8 environment types built on MuJoCo (and NumPy for Powderworld), each with multiple variants and dataset categories (navigate, stitch, explore for locomotion; play and noisy for manipulation) that isolate specific algorithmic challenges such as stitching up to 24 subtasks or handling stochastic teleporters. The evaluation protocol uses multi-goal testing (5 state-goal pairs per task) to avoid the single-goal bias of prior benchmarks, and supports both state-based and pixel-based observations with carefully tuned visual settings (colored floors, transparent arms) to keep image resolution at 64×64×3. Six representative offline GCRL algorithms (GCBC, GCIVL, GCIQL, QRL, CRL, HIQL) are provided as well-tuned reference implementations with minimal dependencies, and task difficulties are calibrated so that current SOTA methods achieve roughly 20–30% success on the hardest variants, leaving room for future algorithmic improvement.

## Results

Across 85 datasets spanning locomotion, manipulation, and drawing, no single offline GCRL method dominates all task categories. HIQL achieves the strongest overall performance, reaching 96% on antmaze-medium-navigate, 89% on humanoidmaze-medium-navigate, and 89% on visual-cube-single-play. Among non-hierarchical methods, CRL excels in state-based locomotion (95% on antmaze-medium-navigate, 94% on visual-antmaze-medium-navigate), GCIQL leads in state-based manipulation (68% on cube-single-play, 95% on puzzle-3x3-play), and GCIVL is best for drawing (99% on powderworld-easy-play). Performance drops dramatically on harder variants: on antmaze-giant-navigate, the best method (HIQL) reaches only 65%, and on humanoidmaze-giant-navigate, no method exceeds 12%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks six offline GCRL algorithms: GCBC (behavioral cloning), GCIVL/GCIQL (implicit value/Q-learning), QRL (quasimetric RL), CRL (contrastive RL), and HIQL (hierarchical implicit Q-learning). HIQL wins or ties on most locomotion and visual manipulation tasks (e.g., 96% vs. 95% CRL on antmaze-medium-navigate; 89% vs. 60% GCIVL on visual-cube-single-play) but underperforms GCIQL on state-based manipulation (15% vs. 68% on cube-single-play; 12% vs. 95% on puzzle-3x3-play). CRL is the strongest non-hierarchical method for locomotion and is notably robust to stochasticity (53% on antmaze-teleport-navigate vs. 35–39% for value-based methods) and pixel observations (94% on visual-antmaze-medium-navigate vs. 22% for GCIVL). GCIQL dominates state-based manipulation tasks, achieving 95% on puzzle-3x3-play where all other methods score below 12%. GCIVL is the clear winner on the stochastic drawing task powderworld (99% easy, 50% medium vs. ≤33% for all others). QRL is the only method that significantly outperforms others on PointMaze (82–86% on medium/large navigate) but collapses on stochastic teleport mazes (4% vs. 39–53% for others), revealing its optimistic bias under stochasticity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the growing family of offline goal-conditioned RL algorithms (GCBC, GCIVL/GCIQL, QRL, CRL, HIQL) and prior evaluation setups (D4RL AntMaze/Kitchen, Fetch, Roboverse), but departs from them by arguing that none of these provide a systematic, multi-faceted evaluation of offline GCRL capabilities. Prior works typically evaluated multi-task policies on a single fixed goal (when reusing D4RL datasets) or on atomic manipulation tasks, or constructed bespoke environments to isolate one property (stitching, stochasticity) without a unified comparison. OGBench unifies these fragmented evaluations into a single benchmark with 8 environment types, 85 datasets, and 6 well-tuned reference implementations, explicitly designed to probe stitching, long-horizon reasoning, stochasticity, and high-dimensional inputs simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that algorithms which rank similarly on prior tasks (e.g., D4RL AntMaze) show stark, task-dependent strengths and weaknesses: CRL excels in locomotion but fails in manipulation, GCIQL shows the opposite pattern, and HIQL is broadly strong but struggles in specific settings. No single method dominates across all categories, and several tasks (humanoidmaze-giant, cube-triple, puzzle-4x5, powderworld-hard) remain unsolved by all current methods, providing clear targets for future algorithmic work. Key open questions include whether hierarchical structure is truly necessary for test-time generalization, how to handle stochasticity while exploiting the recursive subgoal structure of GCRL, and whether a unified non-hierarchical method can match or exceed HIQL's performance. The finding that removing action noise from expert data drops success from 99% to 6% on basic pick-and-place challenges the common assumption that more optimal data is always preferable, suggesting coverage should be prioritized over optimality in data collection.
