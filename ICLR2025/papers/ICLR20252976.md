## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods transfer the entire teacher knowledge to the student, but in practical deployments (especially with large foundation models on resource-constrained devices), only a subset of the teacher's categories is needed. This paper formalizes Selective Knowledge Distillation (SelKD) and further extends it to an open-set variant where the student must also produce a 'not selected' rejection for samples outside its assigned subtask.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252976/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB image samples and label features (one-hot vectors or text encoder embeddings) as input, with a user-specified subset of categories defining the subtask. For closed-set SelKD, the output is a class label restricted to the selected subset; for open-set SelKD, the output additionally includes a rejection probability computed as 1 minus the sum of selected-class coupling entries. The cost matrix is constructed as the negative dot product between image encoder features and label encoder features, and the coupling matrix P (computed via entropic OT) encodes the matching probabilities between samples and categories.

## Method

The core reformulation treats KD as a bi-level Inverse Optimal Transport problem: the inner optimization solves an entropic regularized OT to compute the coupling (matching probability) between image and label features, while the outer optimization supervises the student coupling via cross-entropy with ground truth and KL divergence with the teacher coupling. For closed-set SelKD, the coupling constraint P1=1 enforces row-stochastic matching over only the selected categories, with cost matrices restricted to that subset. For open-set SelKD, the constraint is relaxed to Partial OT (P1≤1, 1^T P 1 = γ), allowing row sums below 1 so that unmatched mass naturally corresponds to the 'not selected' rejection. The key insight is that traditional logit-based KD emerges as a special case of this IOT framework, and the flexibility of OT constraints enables a single comprehensive teacher to selectively transfer knowledge to multiple subtask students without retraining separate teachers per task.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (closed-set SelKD), SelKD achieves a mean top-1 accuracy of 83.30% (FT-SelKD, ResNet-8) and 85.66% (FT-SelKD, ResNet-14), outperforming vanilla KD (82.52% and 84.83%) and self-KD (81.24% and 83.61%) while using only 23.7M teacher parameters versus 117.7M for KD. On Tiny ImageNet, FT-SelKD reaches 54.31% mean accuracy, surpassing FT-KD (53.63%) and the no-distillation baseline (52.69%). In the open-set setting on CIFAR-100, FT-SelKD achieves 86.69% (ResNet-8) and 88.35% (ResNet-14) mean accuracy, consistently leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelKD consistently outperforms both vanilla KD and self-KD across all three feature-based distillation methods (FitNet, FT, AT) and both student architectures on CIFAR-100. For example, FT-SelKD with ResNet-14 achieves 85.66% mean accuracy, beating FT-KD (84.83%) by 0.83 points and FT-SKD (83.61%) by 2.05 points, while requiring only 23.7M teacher parameters versus 117.7M for KD (which trains five separate subtask-specific teachers). The parameter efficiency is a key advantage: SelKD uses a single comprehensive teacher (23.7M) shared across all subtasks, whereas KD requires five separate teachers (117.7M total). Self-KD, which avoids an external teacher entirely, performs worst among the three distillation strategies, suggesting that a strong external teacher with broader knowledge is more beneficial than self-generated soft labels. On Tiny ImageNet, the same pattern holds: FT-SelKD (54.31%) beats FT-KD (53.63%) and the no-KD baseline (52.69%). In the open-set setting, SelKD maintains its lead, with FT-SelKD reaching 88.35% (ResNet-14) versus 88.04% for FT-KD and 87.86% for FT-SKD on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes knowledge distillation through the lens of Inverse Optimal Transport (IOT), building directly on the IOT-based contrastive learning framework of Shi et al. (2023) and the bi-level optimization formulation of Stuart & Wolfram (2020). The key conceptual departure from standard logit-based KD (Hinton et al., 2015) and feature-based KD (FitNet, FT, AT) is that IOT treats classification as a transportation/matching problem between image and label features, which naturally permits selecting a subset of categories for transfer—something the fixed-dimensionality softmax constraint in traditional KD makes awkward. The paper shows that vanilla logit-based KD is a special case within this IOT framework, and extends it with a Partial Optimal Transport constraint (relaxing row-sums to ≤ 1) to handle open-set rejection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SelKD formulation addresses a practically important gap in the foundation-model era: deploying a single large teacher to distill different subtask-specific students for edge devices without retraining a teacher per task. However, the empirical validation is limited to small-scale classification (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny ImageNet) with modest 1–2% gains over strong feature-based KD baselines, and the method has not been tested on larger benchmarks, non-classification tasks, or the very large teacher–student gaps that motivate the setting. Open questions include whether the IOT-based selective transfer scales to vision-language models (e.g., CLIP-style distillation), whether the entropic regularization parameter interacts problematically with very large category spaces, and whether the open-set rejection mechanism generalizes beyond the simple 'not selected' binary case.
