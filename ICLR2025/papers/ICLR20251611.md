## Problem

Offline RL is vulnerable to data poisoning attacks, yet existing certified defenses like COPA are restricted to discrete action spaces and deterministic environments, certifying only individual trajectories rather than overall policy performance. This paper addresses the gap by providing the first practical certified defense framework that offers computable robustness guarantees for both per-state action stability and expected cumulative reward in general offline RL settings spanning continuous/discrete spaces and stochastic/deterministic environments.

## Input / Output

The method takes an offline RL training dataset of M trajectories (or equivalently N transitions) collected by an unknown behavioral policy, along with a test-time state s_t, and produces two types of certification: an action-level guarantee that the policy selects the same action at state s_t under up to r poisoned transitions/trajectories, and a policy-level lower bound J_r on the expected cumulative reward. The framework handles both discrete action spaces (Atari Freeway, Breakout) and continuous action spaces (Mujoco Half Cheetah), with the randomized training producing p=50 policy instances whose outputs are aggregated via majority vote for action selection and empirical CDF estimation for reward bounding.

## Method

The framework comprises two stages: a Differential Privacy-based randomized training process and robustness certification. For training, the Sampled Gaussian Mechanism (SGM) provides transition-level DP guarantees while an adapted DP-FEDAVG provides trajectory-level guarantees, each producing p policy instances trained with calibrated noise. The key theoretical contribution is extending the DP outcomes guarantee from probability bounds to expected-value bounds (Lemma 4.1), which enables policy-level certification by bounding the expected cumulative reward J(π̃) ≥ e^{-ε}(J(π) - bδ) for ADP or the corresponding RDP form. For action-level certification, the method defines inferred scores as the probability of each action being selected across policy instances, bounds these scores via the outcomes guarantee, and certifies robustness when the lower bound of the chosen action's score exceeds the upper bound of all competing actions' scores. The maximum tolerable poisoning size r_t is found via binary search over the DP parameter space, with score bounds estimated using the SIMUEM method at confidence 1-δ.

## Results

On Freeway (DQN, RDP, σ=3.0), the proposed method achieves a mean action-level certified radius of 160.0 (transition-level) and 102.4 (trajectory-level), compared to COPA's 10.1 and 9.7 respectively — approximately 5× larger. On Breakout (DQN, RDP, σ=2.0), the mean radii are 17.7 and 16.9 versus COPA's 6.6 and 6.3. In terms of policy-level robustness, the method certifies that performance drops to no more than 50% with up to 7.17% of Freeway trajectories poisoned (vs. 0.008% for COPA) and 2.05% of Breakout trajectories (vs. 0.0075% for COPA). The method also extends to continuous action spaces (Mujoco Half Cheetah with IQL), where COPA is inapplicable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is COPA (Wu et al., 2022), the prior state-of-the-art certified defense for offline RL poisoning. MuCD achieves approximately 5× larger certified radii across all settings: on Freeway, the mean action-level radius reaches 160.0 (transition) vs. COPA's 10.1 (trajectory-only), and on Breakout, 17.7 vs. 6.6. In terms of tolerable poisoning proportion while maintaining ≥50% performance, MuCD certifies 7.17% for Freeway and 2.05% for Breakout, compared to COPA's 0.008% and 0.0075% respectively — a roughly 900× improvement. COPA is fundamentally limited to discrete action spaces and deterministic environments, and can only certify trajectory-level (not transition-level) poisoning; it also restricts trajectory length to 400 (Freeway) and 75 (Breakout) due to exponential tree-search growth, whereas MuCD imposes no such limitations. On clean performance, COPA retains a slight edge in cumulative reward (e.g., 325.7 vs. 268.4 on Breakout at comparable noise), but this gap narrows at lower noise levels and is the expected trade-off for stronger certification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP-based certified defense paradigm from classification (Lecuyer et al., 2019) to offline RL, building directly on COPA (Wu et al., 2022), which adapted Deep Partition Aggregation to provide per-state action certification but was fundamentally restricted to discrete action spaces and deterministic environments. The key theoretical departure is Lemma 4.1, which extends the DP outcomes guarantee from probability bounds to expected-value bounds, enabling policy-level certification of expected cumulative reward rather than only per-trajectory guarantees. For the randomized training process, the paper combines SGM (Mironov et al., 2019) for transition-level DP with an adaptation of DP-FEDAVG (McMahan et al., 2017) for trajectory-level DP, unifying two previously separate threat models under a single certification framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical certified defense framework that covers general offline RL settings (continuous and discrete actions, stochastic and deterministic environments), addressing a gap where prior theoretical bounds relied on simplified MDPs or expressed robustness only in terms of optimality gaps to the unattainable Bellman optimal policy. The 5× larger certified radii and ~900× improvement in tolerable poisoning fraction (7% vs 0.008%) over COPA make certified defenses in offline RL potentially deployable in safety-critical domains. Open questions include whether a unified DP training algorithm can simultaneously support both transition- and trajectory-level guarantees, and whether adaptive (non-uniform) noise injection could further close the performance-robustness trade-off.
