## Problem

Generative neural models that predict next tokens cannot guarantee logical equivalence when synthesizing circuits, as their probabilistic nature produces occasional wrong predictions that violate the 2^N equivalence constraints. Prior neural circuit-generation approaches reported 5–70% constraint violation rates, leading the field to conclude that pure generative models are unsuitable for equivalence-preserving circuit realization. This paper demonstrates that a formal guarantee of logical equivalence is achievable for next-token prediction models through a carefully designed decoding mechanism with cutoff properties.

## Input / Output

The model takes a Boolean function specified as a DAG of AND/NOT gates (8 inputs, 2 outputs in experiments) and produces a strictly equivalent but more compact circuit in the same format. The input DAG is serialized into a sequential token sequence via depth-first traversal with node duplication (unfolding multi-fanout nodes into a tree), and the output is generated as a sequence of tokens from a vocabulary of 2N+2 symbols (primary inputs, their inverses, AND, and NAND), then converted back to a DAG via on-the-fly functional-equivalence node merging. The only failure mode is exceeding a maximum sequence length of 200 tokens; equivalence is never violated.

## Method

The core contribution is a sequential circuit representation with cutoff properties: the circuit is built top-down by recursively replacing wildcard nodes (U) with specific gates or inputs, using three-valued logic (0, 1, U) with a SIMEQ operator so that partially-constructed circuits can be validated against the target function at every generation step. A masking layer inserted before the softmax in the Transformer decoder blocks any candidate token that would invalidate equivalence, and the representation is designed so the feasible set is never empty (backtrack elimination), enabling smooth autoregressive generation without backtracking. The Circuit Transformer uses a standard encoder-decoder architecture with tree positional encoding (stack of one-hot path encodings), trained via supervised cross-entropy on 15 million circuits optimized by Resyn2. For circuit-size minimization, the generation process is formulated as a Markov decision process with an immediate reward penalizing gate additions, solved via Monte-Carlo Tree Search that uses the Transformer as both prior probability estimator and rollout simulator.

## Results

On 10,240 randomly generated 8-input, 2-output circuits, the Circuit Transformer with MCTS (K=100) achieves an average circuit size of 13.73 with only 0.17% unsuccessful cases (0% equivalence violations), outperforming the Resyn2 ground truth (14.56). On 10,240 IWLS 2023 fanout-free windows, the same configuration reaches an average size of 16.73 (vs. 16.82 for Resyn2) with 2.63% unsuccessful cases and zero violations. All Circuit Transformer variants show 0% equivalence constraint violations, whereas baseline models (Boolean Chain, AIGER) exhibit 4–11% violation rates. The masking layer contributes only 6% of total inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Resyn2 (the ABC optimization flow used as supervised training signal), the Circuit Transformer with MCTS (K=100) produces strictly smaller circuits on both benchmarks: 13.73 vs. 14.56 average gate count on random circuits and 16.73 vs. 16.82 on IWLS FFWs, while maintaining zero equivalence violations. The two baseline Transformer models (Boolean Chain and AIGER representations) are substantially worse on feasibility: even with beam search of size 128, they retain 1.7–1.9% equivalence violations on random circuits and 4.0–5.9% on IWLS FFWs, whereas the Circuit Transformer achieves 0% violations in all configurations. The Circuit Transformer's only failure mode is exceeding the 200-token sequence limit (0.17–4.76% depending on setting), never a logical error. With just K=10 MCTS playouts, the model already surpasses Resyn2 on random circuits (14.02 vs. 14.56) at roughly 23× the inference time (0.210s vs. 0.009s per circuit).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Knuth's backtracking framework for constrained sequence generation, repurposing the concept of 'cutoff properties' (incremental feasibility checks) to the domain of circuit synthesis, and on Shiv & Quirk's tree positional encoding to represent circuit structure in a Transformer. It departs fundamentally from prior neural circuit generation work (Schmitt et al. 2021/2023, d'Ascoli et al. 2023), which accepted 5–70% equivalence violation rates as an inherent limitation of next-token prediction, by introducing a top-down sequential representation with three-valued logic that makes the equivalence constraint checkable at every decoding step. The action-masking idea is borrowed from routing problems (Nazari et al. 2018; Kool et al. 2019) but applied here to a far more complex constraint (2^N equality constraints per output) rather than simple adjacency checks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generative neural model that formally guarantees logical equivalence of produced circuits, shifting the paradigm from 'generate and verify' to 'generate and it is always valid.' The MDP formulation enables principled optimization (e.g., via MCTS) within the equivalence class, and the model with K=100 playouts produces circuits smaller than the Resyn2 ground truth on ~47% of random circuits. Key limitations include evaluation only on 8-input circuits with AND-NOT gates, a fixed maximum sequence length of 200, and a single 88M-parameter model; open questions include scaling to larger circuits, richer gate libraries, and integration into industrial EDA flows for full-chip optimization.
