## Problem

Achieving affine equivariance in neural networks with arbitrary input and output representation types has remained an open challenge; the prior InvarLayer framework, while eliminating the need for group sampling, is restricted to the trivial representation for both input and output. Additionally, canonicalization—a promising approach for retrofitting equivariance onto pre-trained models—has been limited to Euclidean groups because it relies on equivariant networks with specific non-trivial representations that were unavailable for general matrix groups like GL+(2).

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D grayscale images (28×28 MNIST, 40×40 Fashion-MNIST) and produces class labels via a pre-trained ResNet50 prediction network. The canonicalization function itself outputs a 2×2 matrix (a GL+(2) group element) that maps the input to its canonical form before classification. For the PDE prediction task, the input is a sequence of 3 vector-field snapshots of shape (3, 2, 64, 64) and the output is the next time step of shape (1, 2, 64, 64).

## Method

The steerable EquivarLayer generalizes InvarLayer by introducing equivariants—maps that transform according to a specified output representation rather than remaining invariant—and constructing them as products of an equivariant matrix and invariants (Theorem 7). The equivariant matrix is derived from a moving frame via Theorem 10, and a novel SupNorm normalization (Theorem 12) converts relative equivariants from the equi-affine subgroup SA(2) into exact affine equivariants, avoiding complex fractional polynomials and division-by-zero issues. The layer architecture (Eq. 20) applies the equivariant matrix to the output of an MLP over basis invariants, enabling arbitrary input/output representation types. For canonicalization, a Det-Pooling module (Theorem 15) selects the matrix with the largest absolute determinant from the EquivarLayer's matrix-valued output, guaranteeing equivariance under GL+(2) and reducing the risk of selecting a singular matrix. The canonicalization network (ResNet32 with EquivarLayers, <0.4M parameters) is trained independently via MSE alignment against random transformations, then frozen and connected to a pre-trained prediction network at inference.

## Results

On MNIST-GL+(2) (arbitrary rotation, scaling, and shear), the EquivarLayer Canonicalizer achieves 1.32±0.08% test error, outperforming Full Augmentation (1.52±0.08%) while maintaining 0.81±0.08% on original MNIST (vs. 1.19±0.04% for Full Augmentation). On MNIST-RS (rotation + scale), it reaches 1.06±0.07% vs. 1.65±0.06% for Full Augmentation. On MNIST-Scale, it achieves 0.64±0.06%, slightly beating Full Augmentation (0.67±0.04%) and all other baselines on both original and transformed datasets. In the dynamical system prediction task, EquivarLayer achieves RMSE of 0.218×10⁻⁵, dramatically outperforming the SO(2) Steerable CNN baseline (6.881×10⁻⁵) with comparable parameter count (~104K vs. 107K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against data augmentation strategies. The EquivarLayer Canonicalizer (combined with mild augmentation) consistently outperforms Full Augmentation on transformed datasets across all three groups (GL⁺(2): 1.32 vs. 1.52%; RS: 1.06 vs. 1.65%; Scale: 0.64 vs. 0.67%) while preserving competitive accuracy on the original dataset, whereas Full Augmentation degrades original-dataset performance (e.g., 1.19% vs. 0.45% for mild augmentation on MNIST). Against the InvarLayer baseline (Li et al., 2024), which is limited to trivial representations, the EquivarLayer Canonicalizer combined with InvarLayer as prediction network achieves 0.92±0.02% on MNIST-GL⁺(2) vs. InvarLayer with Full Augmentation at 0.87±0.05%, showing comparable performance while using only mild augmentation. For the scale group, the EquivarLayer Canonicalizer combined with SESN (Sosnovik et al., 2019) achieves 0.43±0.04% on MNIST-Scale, matching SESN with Full Augmentation (0.44±0.03%) while maintaining 0.33±0.05% on original MNIST (best among all methods). In the PDE prediction task, the affine-equivariant EquivarLayer (RMSE 0.218×10⁻⁵) dramatically outperforms the SO(2) Steerable CNN (Weiler & Cesa, 2019) baseline (RMSE 6.881×10⁻⁵) by over 30×, demonstrating the advantage of full affine equivariance over rotation-only equivariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the InvarLayer framework (Li et al., 2024), which achieved affine equivariance via differential invariants but was restricted to trivial input/output representations, by introducing equivariants and equivariant matrices constructed through moving frames. The conceptual leap from invariants to equivariants mirrors the earlier generalization from G-CNNs to steerable CNNs (Cohen & Welling, 2016a→2016b; Weiler & Cesa, 2019), but is applied to the affine group where prior steerable approaches (MacDonald et al., 2022; Mironenco & Forré, 2024) required costly group sampling. The Det-Pooling module further connects this steerable layer to the canonicalization framework (Kaba et al., 2023; Mondal et al., 2023), which had previously been limited to rotation groups due to the absence of general steerable equivariant networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a genuine gap by providing the first steerable equivariant layer for the affine group and its continuous subgroups, enabling equivariant adaptation of pre-trained models to non-Euclidean transformations like GL+(2) without architectural retraining. The SupNorm normalization technique for equivariant matrices offers a practical path to numerical stability that avoids the division-by-zero issues of direct affine equivariant computation. However, experiments are confined to 2D MNIST/Fashion-MNIST at modest resolutions, and the canonicalization approach requires a separate alignment training stage; extending to higher dimensions, 3D data, and larger-scale benchmarks remains open.
