## Problem

Existing MLLM benchmarks suffer from three interrelated limitations: small data scales (often <10K QA pairs) that produce high evaluation variance, reliance on model-generated annotations that inherit the annotator model's own errors, and insufficient task difficulty driven largely by low image resolutions. As a result, top models already saturate many benchmarks at 80–90% accuracy, making it hard to distinguish meaningful improvements or to assess whether MLLMs can handle the perceptual challenges they would face in real-world deployments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253229/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a single high-resolution RGB image (average ~2000×1500, maximum 5304×7952, individual files up to 139 MB) with a multiple-choice question offering five options (A–E), where option E explicitly signals 'no correct answer in the image.' The model must output a single letter, which is matched via a rule-based filter against the ground truth. The benchmark spans 5 domains (OCR in the Wild, Remote Sensing, Diagram & Table, Monitoring, Autonomous Driving) comprising 43 subtasks and 29,429 QA pairs, plus a 5,917-pair Chinese subset (MME-RealWorld-CN) built from natively Chinese images rather than machine-translated ones.

## Method

MME-RealWorld is constructed through a fully human-annotated pipeline: 300K+ candidate images from public datasets (FAIR1M, nuScenes, VisDrone, DIV2K, HQ-50K, etc.) and the internet are manually filtered down to 13,366 high-quality images, then annotated by 25 professional annotators and cross-checked by 7 MLLM experts. Question design enforces three quality constraints—every answer must be derivable from the image, the queried object must occupy ≤1/10 of the image area, and each annotation is verified by at least two researchers—while the four distractors are drawn from text actually present in the image or near-synonyms of the correct answer to force fine-grained visual grounding. Evaluation reports both weighted (Avg) and unweighted per-subtask (Avg-C) accuracy, and the paper benchmarks 29 open- and closed-source MLLMs, revealing that even the best model (Qwen2-VL) reaches only ~59% on perception and ~44% on reasoning, with closed-source models penalized by API resolution caps and conservative 'E' responses.

## Results

On MME-RealWorld perception tasks (20,767 QA pairs across 5 domains), Qwen2-VL achieves the highest average accuracy of 58.96%, followed by InternVL-2 (55.82%) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (52.90%). On reasoning tasks (2,832 QA pairs), Claude 3.5 Sonnet leads with 44.12% average accuracy, ahead of Qwen2-VL (40.39%) and InternVL-2 (38.74%). No evaluated model surpasses 60% on perception or 45% on reasoning, and the baseline LLaVA-1.5-7B achieves only 24.9% overall, confirming the benchmark's difficulty. On the Chinese split (MME-RealWorld-CN), InternVL-2.5 reaches 61.72% perception accuracy, with Qwen2-VL close behind at 59.80%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2-VL (58.96% Avg) outperforms all closed-source models on overall perception, beating GPT-4o (46.43%) by 12.5 points and Gemini-1.5-Pro (39.63%) by 19.3 points, despite GPT-4o's strong OCR score of 77.69%. On reasoning tasks, Claude 3.5 Sonnet (44.12%) leads by a clear margin over Qwen2-VL (40.39%) and GPT-4o (37.61%), with a 12.6-point gap over Qwen2-VL specifically on diagram/table reasoning. High-resolution-capable models (Mini-Gemini-HD, SliME, Cambrian-1) consistently outperform CLIP-based encoders (ShareGPT4V, LLaVA-1.5) by 15–20 points on remote sensing and monitoring, confirming that resolution handling is the dominant factor. Closed-source models (GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude) show a conservative bias, outputting 'E' (reject) far more frequently than the <5% ground-truth rate, which suppresses their scores on counting and fine-grained perception subtasks. On the Chinese split, InternVL-2.5 (61.72%) and Qwen2-VL (59.80%) surpass their English-version scores, while GPT-4o drops ~10 points, suggesting domain-specific optimization benefits for Chinese-trained open-source models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in MLLM evaluation by addressing three systemic weaknesses in existing benchmarks (MME, MMBench, MMStar, BLINK, SEED-Bench, MMT-Bench): limited data scale, model-generated annotations, and insufficient task difficulty driven by low image resolution. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes high-resolution source datasets (FAIR1M, nuScenes, VisDrone, DIV2K, LIU4K, UHD4K/8K) into a unified multiple-choice VQA framework spanning five real-world domains. It also provides the first rigorous, large-scale evaluation of high-resolution MLLM architectures (LLaVA-Next, Mini-Gemini-HD, SliME, Cambrian-1) that prior work had only demonstrated through anecdotal examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even frontier models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Qwen2-VL) fail to exceed 60% accuracy, exposing a substantial gap between current MLLM perception and the demands of real-world high-resolution scenarios. A key open question is how to efficiently process images at 2000×1500+ resolution without the 5× computational overhead seen in current high-resolution architectures. The paper also highlights that the intuitive strategy of segmenting images into sub-images and aggregating via majority vote actually degrades performance due to lost positional context, and that closed-source models exhibit a conservative 'refusal' bias (excessive 'E' outputs) that masks true capability. Limitations include the absence of natural-scene data and underrepresentation of domains like healthcare and indoor scenes.
