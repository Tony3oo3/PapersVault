## Problem

The Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory provides a powerful framework for understanding neural network training dynamics, but its validity in classification problems has remained an open question. While NTK convergence has been thoroughly established for regression with MSE loss, the behavior of the empirical NTK under the cross-entropy loss function—standard for classification—was unexplored. This paper addresses whether the NTK regime (uniform convergence of the empirical kernel to a fixed NTK) holds during classification training.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes labeled binary training data {(x_i, y_i)} with x_i in a compact subset of R^d and y_i ∈ {0,1}, paired with a fully connected or residual network architecture using ReLU activations. The theoretical output is a lower bound on the supremum gap between the empirical NTK and the limiting NTK (λ_0/(2n²)), proving non-uniform convergence. Empirically, the method is validated on synthetic 2D data (6 points on the unit sphere) and MNIST 784-dimensional images, tracking NTK values over 10,000–100,000 training epochs.

## Method

The proof proceeds in two stages. First, the paper establishes strict positive definiteness of the NTK for both fully connected networks (via a recursive kernel form with bias terms) and residual networks (via arc-cosine kernel expansions on the positive sphere S_+^d). Second, a proof by contradiction shows that if the empirical NTK were to uniformly converge to the NTK, the minimum eigenvalue of the kernel matrix would remain bounded below by a positive constant, which—combined with the cross-entropy loss dynamics—forces the network output |f_t(x_i)| → ∞ at all training points. This divergence of the network function then causes the NNK itself to diverge (since NNK involves inner products of parameter gradients that scale with network activations), contradicting the assumed uniform convergence. The key mechanism is a Lyapunov-type function V(u) = Σ ln(1−u_i) whose monotonic decrease under the gradient flow drives the prediction residuals u_i → 0, equivalently making the network's logit outputs unbounded.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: Theorem 2 proves that under cross-entropy loss, the empirical NTK (NNK) of both fully connected and residual networks cannot uniformly converge to the NTK, with a guaranteed gap of at least λ₀/(2n²) for some pair of inputs. Theorem 1 further shows that if the NNK remains uniformly positive definite (as it would under NTK convergence), the network output must diverge to infinity at all training points. Numerical experiments confirm these predictions: on a synthetic 6-point dataset (m=2000, 10,000 epochs), all six network outputs diverge, and on MNIST (4-layer FC, m=500, 100,000 epochs), the empirical NTK values at selected points clearly disconverge over training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare competing methods or report standard benchmark metrics (accuracy, loss, etc.) against baselines. Instead, it contrasts the NTK theory's prediction of uniform convergence (well-established for regression with MSE loss, per Lai et al. 2023a, Li et al. 2023) with the actual divergent behavior observed under cross-entropy loss in classification. The key 'comparison' is between the NTK regime (parameters stay near initialization, kernel remains fixed) and the actual training dynamics, where the paper proves the NTK regime fails: the NNK diverges rather than converging to a fixed kernel. The paper also notes that prior works (Ji & Telgarsky 2019; Taheri & Thrampoulidis 2024) show NTK convergence only in the limit m→∞ with fixed finite T, whereas this paper examines fixed m with T→∞, revealing the divergence. No head-to-head method comparisons are presented.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK convergence analysis from the regression/MSE setting to the classification/cross-entropy setting, building directly on Jacot et al.'s (2018) NTK framework, Huang et al.'s (2020) arc-cosine kernel expressions for residual networks, and the strictly positive definiteness proofs of Lai et al. (2023a) and Li et al. (2023). The key departure is that all prior NTK convergence results (pointwise or uniform) were established under MSE loss, where the loss convexity ensures parameters remain near initialization; this paper shows that cross-entropy loss fundamentally breaks this mechanism, causing the empirical NTK to diverge rather than converge. The authors position their work as complementary to recent studies (Ji & Telgarsky, 2019; Taheri & Thrampoulidis, 2024) that confirm NTK regime holds for m→∞ with fixed finite T, whereas here m is fixed and T→∞.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result establishes a fundamental boundary for NTK theory: the kernel-regression approximation that underpins much of the generalization analysis for overparameterized networks does not transfer to classification with cross-entropy loss, necessitating new theoretical tools to understand why classification networks generalize well. The practical limitation is that the divergence is proven in the T→∞ regime with fixed width, so finite-training dynamics may still be well-approximated by NTK for practical purposes. Open questions include whether the divergence rate is slow enough to be negligible in practice, whether alternative loss functions (e.g., focal loss, margin-based losses) exhibit similar behavior, and what replaces the NTK framework as the correct theoretical lens for classification dynamics.
