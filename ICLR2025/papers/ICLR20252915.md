## Problem

Process-based reward models (PRMs) have shown strong potential for inference-time scaling in specialized domains like math and coding, but their development in open-domain tasks (e.g., instruction-following, chat) remains largely unexplored due to the absence of exact answers for process supervision and the high cost of human annotation. This paper asks whether open-domain PRMs can be constructed from existing outcome-based reward models (ORMs) via a weak-to-strong framework, without requiring expensive step-level human labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252915/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The reward model takes a text prompt (instruction) paired with a candidate completion—either a full response (outcome-level) or a partial sentence-segment sequence (process-level)—and outputs a scalar reward score. Training data is unified into a [Q, C, P_c, P_r] format where P_c and P_r are chosen and rejected completions that may be full answers or intermediate sentence prefixes. At inference, the model scores N sampled candidates for best-of-N selection or evaluates individual sentences during process-level beam search, with beam size set to √N to match BoN decoding cost.

## Method

OpenPRM constructs sentence-level preference trees by repeatedly sampling 64 candidate responses per prompt from Llama-3 models, segmenting outputs into sentences, and merging similar sentences across candidates via edit-distance thresholds (analogous to state aggregation in MCTS). Outcome-level rewards from an existing ORM are assigned to leaf nodes, and process-level rewards for internal nodes are derived by back-propagation (averaging descendant leaf rewards). The key insight is identifying the most divergent process steps between chosen and rejected outcomes to provide targeted weak supervision, formalized in a theorem showing cumulative error decreases with step discrepancy, supervision strength, and model sensitivity. Training integrates both ORM and PRM losses under a unified Bradley-Terry pairwise ranking objective, mixing process pairs from preference trees with outcome pairs from UltraFeedback, ScienceQA, MATH, and HelpSteer2. This approach achieves 3–5% improvement over base ORMs on RewardBench and demonstrates superior inference-time scaling compared to ORMs in best-of-N and process-level beam search settings.

## Results

OpenPRM(InternRM) achieves 91.1% overall on RewardBench, outperforming the best ORM baseline Intern2-7B-RM (87.6%) by 3.5 points, with particularly large gains on Chat Hard (81.6% vs. 69.5%) and Safety (89.5% vs. 87.2%). On the process-level PRM800k benchmark, OpenPRM(InternRM) scores 68.1%, surpassing all math-domain PRMs (best: 61.3% for Llama-3-8B Step-DPO) and the parent ORM (61.0%). In inference-time scaling experiments (BoN@16), OpenPRM improves Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct from 37.2% to 50.5% on MMLU-Pro and from 24.3% to 30.8% on GPQA, while the best ORM (InternRM) still lags significantly behind the coverage@N upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest ORM baseline Intern2-7B-RM (87.6% RB Overall), OpenPRM(InternRM) gains +3.5 points overall, with the largest improvement on Chat Hard (+12.1 points, 81.6% vs. 69.5%), confirming that process-level supervision is most beneficial for harder tasks. Compared to FsfairX-7B (84.4%), OpenPRM(FsfairX) improves by +5.0 points on RB Overall and +16.0 on Chat Hard. On the process-level PRM800k benchmark, OpenPRM(InternRM) (68.1%) outperforms all math-domain PRMs: Math-Shepherd (56.9%), TS-LLM (57.7%), and Llama-3-8B Step-DPO (61.3%), demonstrating that open-domain PRMs can generalize to specialized process evaluation. In inference-time scaling (Table 3), OpenPRM via BoN@16 consistently outperforms Vote@16 and all ORM-based selection across Alpaca Eval 2, MixEval, IFEval, GPQA, and MMLU-Pro for both Llama-3.1-8B and 70B, though the gap to the coverage@N upper bound remains substantial. The out-of-distribution Mistral-Nemo model also benefits from OpenPRM (e.g., +11.6 points on MMLU-Pro with BoN@16 vs. reproduced baseline), validating cross-model generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the process-level reward model (PRM) literature, which has been dominated by math and coding domains where golden answers enable step-by-step supervision, to open-domain instruction-following tasks where no exact answers exist. It departs from the standard PRM construction paradigm (human annotation, golden-answer backpropagation, or MCTS-based sampling) by repurposing existing outcome-level reward models (ORMs) as weak supervisors: parallel candidates are sampled, segmented into sentences, merged into preference trees via edit-distance-based state aggregation (analogous to MCTS state aggregation), and outcome rewards are backpropagated to inner nodes. The training unifies ORM and PRM under a single Bradley-Terry pairwise ranking objective, building directly on the InstructGPT reward modeling framework while incorporating the weak-to-strong generalization perspective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic, low-cost recipe for constructing open-domain PRMs without expensive human process annotation, making process-level supervision feasible for general instruction-following. However, the paper's own results temper expectations: even OpenPRM falls well short of coverage accuracy under inference-time scaling, and the gap between reward-model-guided selection and the oracle upper bound remains substantial. Key open questions include whether more sophisticated tree construction (e.g., embedding-based similarity), larger sampling budgets, or iterative self-improvement loops can close this gap, and how to resolve the observed trade-off between Chat Easy and Chat Hard performance. The finding that process-level modeling substantially reduces length bias (correlation 0.05 vs. 0.37) is a practical benefit that may influence future reward model design.
