## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods require labeled or task-specific source data, which is prohibitively expensive for many real-world applications. The paper addresses the more realistic multimodal unsupervised domain generalization (MUDG) setting, where only a large, unlabeled, task-agnostic source dataset (e.g., LAION-2B) and target label names are available. A fundamental obstacle is that cross-modal approximate nearest neighbor search in CLIP's joint embedding space suffers from significantly lower recall than in-modal search due to the modality gap, causing retrieved images to be misaligned with the target task.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of target class name strings, a large unlabeled source image dataset indexed in CLIP's joint vision-language space (LAION-2B with ~20M samples used for indexing), and a pretrained CLIP model (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14). It produces a finetuned CLIP classifier trained on a small pseudo-labeled subset of at most ~100K retrieved images, constructed via text-query retrieval, rank-based pseudo-labeling, and cluster-based deduplication. The source dataset need not be task-specific; the only requirement is that target visual concepts exist in the source and are aligned with their language counterparts in CLIP's embedding space.

## Method

The pipeline has three core components. First, paired k-means modifies the standard IVF indexing scheme by updating cluster centroids with the mean of paired text features (query space) rather than image features (gallery space), directly addressing the theoretical finding (Theorems 1–2) that queries far from image centroids have low probability of sharing a Voronoi cell with their true nearest neighbor. Second, an adaptive label augmentation heuristic selects text descriptors that preserve the pairwise cosine-similarity variance within k-means clusters of label features, diversifying the retrieved image set without requiring an LLM. Third, a diversity-preserving training loss (Eq. 7) blends cross-entropy on pseudo-labels with a KL-regularization term anchoring soft predictions to the initial model's outputs, preventing collapse of textual representations during finetuning. The full pipeline retrieves images via the paired k-means index, assigns pseudo-labels by closest-ranked query, applies cluster-based sample selection for balance and diversity, and finetunes the student CLIP model on the resulting dataset.

## Results

On 11 diverse classification datasets, MUDG (ViT-B/16) achieves a mean accuracy of 71.1%, outperforming the best ZS baseline (waffleCLIP, 65.9%) by 5.2 points and the closest MUDG baseline (SuS-X, 69.0%) by 2.1 points. With the larger ViT-L/14 model, MUDG reaches 75.6% mean accuracy, surpassing the best ZS method (Vis. Desc., 73.8%) by 1.8 points. On domain generalization benchmarks (Table 3), MUDG (ViT-L/14) achieves 73.0% on ImageNet variants, 90.3% on Office-Home, and 67.0% on DomainNet, consistently outperforming both ZS and SFDG (PromptStyler) baselines by 1.1–2.1 points across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUDG (ours) outperforms all ZS baselines by a substantial margin: 5.2 points over waffleCLIP (71.1 vs. 65.9 mean) and 5.4 points over CLIP ZS (71.1 vs. 65.2) with ViT-B/16. Against the closest MUDG competitor SuS-X (69.0 mean), MUDG gains 2.1 points, with particularly large improvements on EuroSAT (61.3 vs. 58.1) and Aircraft (32.8 vs. 30.5). Among coreset selection baselines, Herding (70.9) and K-Ctr Greedy (70.5) are the strongest, but MUDG still leads by 0.2 and 0.6 points respectively. On DG benchmarks (Table 3), MUDG (ViT-L/14) beats PromptStyler (the only SFDG baseline) by 1.2 points on Office-Home (90.3 vs. 89.1) and 1.5 points on DomainNet (67.0 vs. 65.5), while also improving ImageNet-variant accuracy by 1.1 points (73.0 vs. 71.9 for the best ZS ensemble). The method does not improve on VLCS (82.6 vs. 82.2 for CLIP ZS), which the authors attribute to the task being too simple (only 5 classes) for retrieval-based finetuning to help.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends unsupervised domain generalization (UDG) to a more realistic 'multimodal' setting where the source dataset is task-agnostic and unlabeled, building directly on the IVF indexing pipeline from SuS-X (Udandarao et al., 2023) and the diversity-preserving training loss from Liao et al. (2023). The key conceptual departure is identifying the modality gap as a fundamental cause of poor cross-modal approximate nearest neighbor recall, and proposing paired k-means—a simple centroid-update modification—as a principled fix. For label augmentation, the paper moves beyond both LLM-generated descriptors (Menon & Vondrick, 2022) and random descriptors (waffleCLIP, Roth et al., 2023) by introducing an unsupervised heuristic that selects augmentations preserving inter-label feature variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MUDG setting is significantly more practical than UDG or SFDG because it eliminates the need for task-specific source data, enabling a single universal image index (e.g., LAION-2B) to serve arbitrary classification tasks. The theoretical characterization of why cross-modal ANN search degrades (Theorems 1–2) provides a reusable diagnostic for any system relying on CLIP-based retrieval, and the paired k-means fix is broadly applicable beyond this specific pipeline. Limitations include failure when target visual concepts are absent from or misaligned with the source dataset's language alignment (e.g., medical scans, SAR images), and no improvement when zero-shot accuracy is already saturated on simple tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or 3D retrieval, and whether the modality-gap correction generalizes to other contrastive multimodal models beyond CLIP.
