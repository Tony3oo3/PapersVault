## Problem

Message-passing neural networks suffer from exponentially decaying information transmission as node distance increases, limiting their ability to solve tasks requiring long-range communication such as graph property prediction and molecular property inference. While differential-equation-inspired DGNs and graph transformers have been proposed, no prior work provides a theoretically grounded framework that simultaneously guarantees non-dissipative long-range propagation and allows controlled non-conservative behavior through a unified physical formalism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,E) with node features split into position q_u ∈ R^{d/2} and momentum p_u ∈ R^{d/2} components (so each node state x_u ∈ R^d has even dimension), along with the graph adjacency structure. The system evolves over a continuous time interval [0,T] discretized into L=T/ε layers via Symplectic Euler, producing final node representations x_u(T) that retain their full history. The neighborhood aggregation function Φ_G is a free parameter—any permutation-invariant function (GCN, simple weighted sum, etc.) can be plugged in, making the framework a general wrapper around existing DGN architectures.

## Method

PH-DGN models the information flow within a graph as the solution of a port-Hamiltonian ODE: dy/dt = J ∇H_G(y), where H_G is a neural-parameterized Hamiltonian built from a neighborhood aggregation function, and J is a skew-symmetric matrix that couples position and momentum components. In the purely conservative case (null driving forces), the paper proves that the Jacobian eigenvalues lie on the imaginary axis, the vector field is divergence-free, and the backward sensitivity matrix is bounded below by 1, guaranteeing non-vanishing gradients at arbitrary depth. The full port-Hamiltonian extension adds a learnable damping matrix D(q) (positive semi-definite for energy dissipation, negative for acceleration) and a state/time-dependent external force F(q,t), both implemented as small neural networks, allowing the system to deviate from pure conservation when beneficial for the downstream task. Discretization uses the Symplectic Euler method, which preserves the symplectic structure and the BSM lower bound in the discrete setting, and the resulting update equations couple two DGN layers per step (one for p, one for q).

## Results

On the Long-Range Graph Benchmark (LRGB), PH-DGN achieves 0.7012 AP on Peptides-func and 0.2465 MAE on Peptides-struct, outperforming all DE-DGN baselines (e.g., SWAN: 0.6751 AP, 0.2485 MAE) and most MPNN/Transformer baselines without requiring positional encoding or graph rewiring. On the synthetic Graph Property Prediction tasks, PH-DGN achieves log₁₀(MSE) of −0.9348 (Eccentricity), −0.5385 (Diameter), and −4.2993 (SSSP), improving over the best baseline (A-DGN) by 0.81 in average log₁₀(MSE). The purely conservative variant PH-DGN_C also surpasses all baselines, confirming that the Hamiltonian inductive bias alone is sufficient for strong long-range propagation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PH-DGN outperforms all DE-DGN baselines on both LRGB tasks: it beats SWAN (0.6751 AP) by 0.026 and A-DGN (0.5975 AP) by 0.104 on Peptides-func, and beats SWAN (0.2485 MAE) by 0.002 on Peptides-struct. Against MPNN baselines with positional/structural encoding, PH-DGN surpasses GCN+PE/SE (0.6860 AP) by 0.015 and GatedGCN+PE/SE (0.6765 AP) by 0.025 on Peptides-func, while matching GCN+PE/SE on Peptides-struct MAE (0.2465 vs. 0.2460). The only method that exceeds PH-DGN on Peptides-func AP is DRew-GCN+LapPE (0.7150), a multi-hop rewiring approach that relies on Laplacian positional encoding and dynamic graph rewiring—mechanisms PH-DGN explicitly avoids. On the synthetic Graph Property Prediction tasks, PH-DGN dominates all baselines by a wide margin, improving over A-DGN (the strongest competitor) by 1.36 in log₁₀(MSE) on Eccentricity and 1.06 on SSSP. The purely conservative PH-DGN_C variant also outperforms all baselines, confirming that the Hamiltonian conservation inductive bias alone provides substantial long-range propagation benefits even without learned driving forces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DE-DGN paradigm (Poli et al., 2019; Chamberlain et al., 2021a) by replacing the ODE/PDE formulations used in GRAND, GraphCON, and A-DGN with a port-Hamiltonian system (Van der Schaft, 2017), which unifies conservative and non-conservative dynamics under a single energy-based framework. It departs from prior Hamiltonian-inspired DGNs (HamGNN, HANG) that only use Hamiltonian orbits for feature encoding, instead embedding port-Hamiltonian dynamics directly into the message-passing update rule. The Symplectic Euler discretization and the coupling of position/momentum components via neighborhood aggregation represent a novel architectural instantiation that had not been explored in the graph learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically grounded port-Hamiltonian message-passing scheme with provable non-vanishing gradients (BSM ≥ 1) and energy conservation, offering a principled alternative to ad-hoc rewiring or attention mechanisms for long-range propagation. The finding that learned driving forces (dissipation + external forcing) consistently outperform the purely conservative variant suggests that effective long-range tasks require a balance between memory retention and selective forgetting — a practical insight for designing deeper DGNs. Open questions include extension to time-varying graph streams, alternative symplectic integrators, and whether the port-Hamiltonian bias generalizes beyond the tested aggregation functions to higher-order or edge-conditioned message passing.
