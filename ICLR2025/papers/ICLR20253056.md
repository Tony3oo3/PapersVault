## Problem

Controlling camera motion in video diffusion models has relied on either supervised finetuning with camera-annotated datasets (limited in scale and diversity) or self-supervised data augmentation (restricted to simple motions like zoom or truck). No existing method provides robust, precise camera control for off-the-shelf video diffusion models without any additional training, especially for complex or hybrid trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253056/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (user-provided or generated from a text prompt via Stable Diffusion) and a camera motion trajectory specified as a sequence of extrinsic matrices (rotation + translation), and produces a camera-controllable video. It is plug-and-play across base models: SVD at 576×1024 with 14 frames, CogVideoX at 720×480 with 49 frames, and VideoFusion at 128×128 with 16 frames. Camera trajectories range from basic cinematography motions (zoom, pan, tilt, pedestal, truck, roll, rotate) to arbitrary complex paths defined by precise extrinsics extracted from reference videos.

## Method

CamTrol operates in two training-free stages. Stage I models explicit camera motion in 3D point cloud space: a single input image is lifted to a point cloud via monocular depth estimation (ZoeDepth), then a sequence of rendered views is produced by projecting the point cloud under varying extrinsic matrices, with inpainting to fill occlusion holes and per-step depth coefficient optimization to maintain 3D consistency. Stage II exploits the layout prior of noisy latents in diffusion models—the observation that pixel rearrangements in initial noise causally propagate to the generated output—by inverting the rendered image sequence into noisy latents at a chosen timestep t₀ (adding Gaussian noise) and then running the video diffusion model's standard denoising process from t₀. The rendered sequence's spatial layout, encoding the camera trajectory, is inherited by the generated video through this noise prior, while the base model's learned dynamics fill in motion and correct rendering imperfections. The timestep t₀ serves as a single knob trading off motion fidelity (smaller t₀) against video diversity and quality (larger t₀).

## Results

On RealEstate10k (500 prompt-trajectory pairs), CamTrol+SVD achieves the best FVD of 778.46 and the best motion accuracy across all three metrics: ATE 4.17, RPE-T 1.07, and RPE-R 0.010, approaching the ground-truth reference (ATE 3.60, RPE-T 0.89, RPE-R 0.008). For video quality, CamTrol attains FID 68.06 and CLIP-SIM 0.3110, competitive with finetuned baselines. Notably, CamTrol requires no additional GPU memory beyond the base SVD model (11,542 MB vs. 31,702 MB for MotionCtrl and 26,208 MB for CameraCtrl), and its inference time (8 s) is faster than both finetuned alternatives (32 s and 42 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CamTrol outperforms both finetuned baselines on the primary motion-accuracy metrics: it achieves the lowest ATE (4.17 vs. 4.19 for MotionCtrl and 4.22 for CameraCtrl), the lowest RPE-T (1.07, tying MotionCtrl and beating CameraCtrl's 1.17), and the lowest RPE-R (0.010 vs. 0.012 and 0.013). On video quality, CamTrol delivers the best FVD (778.46 vs. 810.59 for MotionCtrl and 951.80 for CameraCtrl) and a competitive FID (68.06), though CameraCtrl edges it slightly on FID (67.59) and CLIP-SIM (0.3138 vs. 0.3110). Against vanilla SVD without motion control, CamTrol dramatically improves motion alignment (RPE-T drops from 1.79 to 1.07, RPE-R from 0.021 to 0.010) while also improving FVD from 1107.93 to 778.46. Qualitatively, the paper shows that MotionCtrl and CameraCtrl fail to capture complex hybrid trajectories (zoom + pan + roll) and suffer domain collapse on non-real-estate scenes, whereas CamTrol preserves the base model's generative diversity and generalizes to arbitrary scenes and styles without any finetuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

CamTrol repurposes the noise/layout prior well-established in diffusion-based image editing (SD-Edit, ILVR, pixel-level manipulation) for a new application: injecting camera motion into video generation without any model modification. It departs from the dominant paradigm of camera control (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, Direct-A-Video), which requires finetuning on camera-annotated datasets or self-supervised data augmentation, by instead exploiting the fact that rearranging noisy latents causally rearranges output content. The 3D point cloud rendering stage draws on multi-view synthesis techniques from 3D generation work (SV3D, Im-3D, MVDream), but uses them purely as a layout-guidance mechanism rather than as a reconstruction target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for camera-annotated training data and additional model parameters, CamTrol offers a plug-and-play camera control solution that generalizes across base models (SVD, CogVideoX, VideoFusion) and scene types without domain collapse — a practical advantage over finetuned methods that overfit to narrow datasets like RealEstate10k. Key limitations include dependence on single-image depth estimation quality, motion blur under large perspective changes with short generation lengths, and the inherent fidelity-diversity trade-off governed by the inversion timestep t₀. Open questions include whether the layout prior remains effective for very long videos or highly dynamic scenes, and whether the point cloud representation can be replaced by richer 3D models without sacrificing the training-free property.
