## Problem

Current acoustic codec models rely on multi-layer residual vector quantization (e.g., DAC uses 9 quantizers producing 900 tokens/sec), which forces downstream language models into complex hierarchical generation architectures and degrades LLM generation quality. Additionally, existing codecs lack sufficient semantic information to support weakly supervised text-to-speech and unified multimodal understanding-generation, and prior attempts to inject semantics via distillation disrupt the unified codec paradigm for music and audio.

## Input / Output

The model takes raw 24 kHz audio waveforms (speech, music, or general audio) and produces a single-quantizer discrete token sequence of 40 or 75 tokens per second (codebook size 4096), along with a reconstructed waveform. During training, 3-second clips are randomly cropped from 10-second segments; the extreme temporal compression (320× or 600× downsampling) means one second of audio maps to only 40 or 75 discrete codes, compared to 900 for DAC. The single-quantizer design eliminates the need for hierarchical channel-dimension handling in downstream autoregressive models.

## Method

WavTokenizer replaces the standard multi-quantizer RVQ with a single quantizer operating over an expanded 4096-entry codebook space, initialized via k-means clustering (200 cluster centers) and maintained with a random awakening strategy to prevent codebook collapse. The encoder follows the EnCodec convolutional architecture with a two-layer LSTM, while the decoder is the key innovation: it uses an inverse Fourier transform upsampling structure (from Vocos) combined with an attention module and ConvNeXt blocks to reconstruct waveforms from highly compressed representations, avoiding aliasing artifacts of transposed-convolution decoders. Training employs a multi-scale STFT discriminator alongside MPD and MRD discriminators with hinge loss, plus mel-spectrum reconstruction and feature matching losses. Extending the contextual modeling window from 1 to 3 seconds during training further improves both reconstruction quality and semantic retention, as validated by ablations on the ARCH benchmark.

## Results

WavTokenizer achieves SOTA reconstruction quality with extreme compression: at 0.9 kbps (75 tokens/s, single quantizer), it reaches UTMOS 4.0486 on LibriTTS test-clean, surpassing DAC at 9 kbps (900 tokens/s, 9 quantizers) which scores 3.9097. At 0.5 kbps (40 tokens/s), WavTokenizer still achieves UTMOS 3.6016, far exceeding DAC's single-quantizer configuration (100 tokens/s) at 1.4940. On the MUSHRA subjective evaluation, WavTokenizer at 0.9 kbps scores 96.1 on speech, 92.9 on music, and 94.4 on audio, outperforming DAC at 9 kbps (92.8/92.6/92.7). For downstream TTS, WavTokenizer-based synthesis achieves CMOS-Q of 0.00 vs. DAC's -0.35, and WER of 5.1% vs. DAC's 6.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WavTokenizer (75 tokens/s, 1 quantizer) decisively outperforms DAC (900 tokens/s, 9 quantizers) on UTMOS across all three test sets (4.05 vs. 3.91 clean, 3.43 vs. 3.36 noisy, 4.26 vs. 4.30 OOD), using only 8.3% of the tokens. Against the directly comparable single-quantizer DAC (100 tokens/s), WavTokenizer at 40 tokens/s achieves UTMOS 3.60 vs. 1.49 — a 2.1-point gap — and at 75 tokens/s reaches 4.05 vs. 1.49. On MUSHRA subjective scores, WavTokenizer beats DAC 9 kbps on speech (96.1 vs. 92.8) and audio (94.4 vs. 92.7) while matching on music (92.9 vs. 92.6). On the ARCH semantic benchmark, WavTokenizer (1 quantizer) surpasses DAC (9 quantizers) on 5 of 12 datasets including AM, MTT, IRMAS, FSD50K, and VIVAE. For downstream TTS, WavTokenizer-based synthesis achieves CMOS-Q 0.00 vs. DAC's -0.35 and WER 5.1% vs. 6.9%, confirming that the single-quantizer large-codebook representation is more effective for autoregressive generation than multi-quantizer alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

WavTokenizer inherits the encoder-VQ-decoder paradigm from SoundStream and EnCodec, adopts EnCodec's 1D convolutional encoder, and borrows Vocos's inverse Fourier transform vocoder for the decoder. Its key departure is collapsing the standard multi-quantizer RVQ stack (typically 4–9 quantizers) into a single quantizer with an expanded 4096-entry codebook, enabled by k-means clustering initialization and a random-awakening strategy to maintain utilization. The addition of an attention module in the decoder and a 3-second contextual window are novel design choices that go beyond the fully-convolutional codec architectures of DAC, EnCodec, and Vocos, drawing instead on attention-based generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving SOTA subjective reconstruction (MUSHRA 96.1 on speech, 94.4 on audio) at only 75 tokens/sec—roughly 12× fewer tokens than DAC's 900—WavTokenizer removes the need for hierarchical generation strategies (AR/NAR, slanted autoregression) in downstream audio LLMs, enabling direct autoregressive integration of speech into multimodal models. The paper's codebook-space analysis, showing that speech vocabulary naturally concentrates below 2^12 entries, provides a principled rationale for the expanded codebook and opens the question of whether speech can be treated as a 'language' with its own large vocabulary. A key limitation is the absence of ASR/understanding capability, and the authors acknowledge that distillation-based semantic injection remains a stopgap; scaling to hundreds of thousands of hours and improving the encoder are identified as critical next steps.
