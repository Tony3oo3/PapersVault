## Problem

Machine learning models trained on publicly sourced, human-annotated data are vulnerable to label poisoning attacks, where adversaries subtly flip training labels to degrade model performance. While adversarial training (AT) is a well-established defense against feature perturbation attacks, its direct application to label poisoning remains largely unexplored, and conventional AT does not adequately defend against such attacks. This paper introduces the first adversarial training strategy specifically designed to counter label poisoning.

## Input / Output

The method accepts input features x_i ∈ R^d (raw 2D coordinates for Moon, 784-dim pixel vectors for MNIST, or 768-dim RoBERTa last-layer embeddings for IMDB) paired with binary labels y_i ∈ {±1}, and produces a kernel SVM classifier f_λ(x) = sign(Σ_j λ_j y_j k(x,x_j) + b) that assigns a binary class label. The core optimization variable is the vector of dual Lagrange multipliers λ ∈ R^n, one per training point, which directly encodes each point's influence on the decision boundary. The method is architecture-agnostic: it can be applied as a robust classifier head on top of any feature extractor (e.g., RoBERTa or an MLP).

## Method

FLORAL casts robust classification under label poisoning as a non-zero-sum Stackelberg game solved via bilevel optimization: the outer (defender) problem minimizes the SVM dual objective under adversarial labels, while the inner (attacker) problem selects the top-k most influential training points by their dual variable λ values and flips their labels within a budget B. The algorithm alternates between the attacker's randomized top-k label-flipping step and a projected gradient descent (PGD) step for the defender, with the projection onto the changing feasible set (defined by the adversarial label constraint y̅^T λ = 0 and box constraints 0 ≤ λ ≤ C) computed via a fixed-point iteration for scalability. The key insight is that dual variables λ directly identify support vectors—the points most critical to the decision boundary—eliminating the need for expensive influence function estimation. The paper proves ε-local asymptotic stability of the iterative scheme, showing that iterates converge to the Stackelberg equilibrium for sufficiently small step size, and provides a global convergence result under additional conditions on the label-sensitivity constants.

## Results

On the IMDB sentiment analysis dataset (768-dim RoBERTa embeddings), FLORAL achieves 86.4% robust accuracy at 40% label poisoning, outperforming fine-tuned RoBERTa (77.9%) by 8.5 percentage points. On the Moon synthetic dataset, FLORAL reaches 80.1% robust accuracy at 25% poisoning versus 75.3% for vanilla SVM and 69.3% for NN. FLORAL matches clean-data accuracy of vanilla SVM (96.8% on Moon, 91.1% on IMDB) while providing substantially higher robust accuracy as the attacker budget increases, with the gap widening from ~1 pp at 5% poisoning to ~5 pp at 25% on Moon and from ~0.7 pp at 25% to ~8.5 pp at 40% on IMDB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLORAL outperforms all seven baselines in adversarial settings on both datasets. On IMDB at 40% poisoning, FLORAL (86.4%) beats RoBERTa (77.9%) by 8.5 pp, vanilla SVM (85.7%) by 0.6 pp, LN-SVM (85.2%) by 1.2 pp, Curie (85.9%) by 0.5 pp, LS-SVM (70.6%) by 15.8 pp, and K-LID (85.9%) by 0.4 pp. On Moon at 25% poisoning, FLORAL (80.1%) surpasses SVM (75.3%) by 4.8 pp, NN (69.3%) by 10.8 pp, NN-PGD (74.0%) by 6.1 pp, LN-SVM (75.4%) by 4.7 pp, Curie (77.9%) by 2.2 pp, LS-SVM (76.6%) by 3.5 pp, and K-LID (74.7%) by 5.4 pp. In clean-data settings, FLORAL ties or nearly ties vanilla SVM and RoBERTa (within 0.06 pp), confirming that the adversarial training overhead does not sacrifice standard accuracy. The gap between FLORAL and baselines widens monotonically with the attacker budget, with LS-SVM and RoBERTa showing the most dramatic degradation under heavy poisoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends adversarial training—previously developed for feature perturbation attacks (Goodfellow et al., 2015; Madry et al., 2017)—to the distinct threat of label poisoning, where only training labels are flipped. It departs from prior label-poisoning defenses (kernel correction, filtering, LID-based weighting) by formulating the interaction as a non-zero-sum Stackelberg game solved via bilevel optimization, rather than relying on heuristic sanitization or distributional assumptions. The bilevel/Stackelberg framing draws on recent work showing adversarial training should be cast as a non-zero-sum game (Robey et al., 2024), and the use of dual variables to identify influential support vectors connects to influence-function literature (Koh & Liang, 2017) while avoiding its computational overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Label poisoning is an increasingly relevant threat as models rely on large-scale human-annotated data (e.g., LLM training), yet dedicated adversarial-training defenses for this specific attack vector were previously absent. FLORAL's generalizability to foundation models (demonstrated with RoBERTa) and its theoretical convergence guarantees make it a practical building block for robust fine-tuning pipelines. Key limitations include the white-box attacker assumption, reliance on SVM dual structure for the core algorithm, and the acknowledged non-adaptive nature of the defense against novel attack strategies. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to black-box settings, how it interacts with multi-turn or adaptive attackers, and whether it can be integrated into certified-robustness frameworks.
