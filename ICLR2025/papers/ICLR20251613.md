## Problem

Cascades and speculative decoding are two complementary strategies for efficient LM inference: cascades offer superior cost-quality trade-offs (sometimes outperforming the large model) but execute sequentially, while speculative decoding provides impressive speed-ups via parallel verification but is quality-neutral to the large model. The paper asks whether the deferral rule that gives cascades their quality advantage can be implemented through speculative execution, thereby combining the best trade-offs of both approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251613/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prefix x_{<t}, a small drafter LM q, a large verifier LM p, and a block size γ, the method generates a block of up to γ+1 tokens. The drafter produces γ draft tokens autoregressively, and the verifier scores all γ prefixes in a single parallel pass. The deferral rule δ(q,p) is evaluated at each position using both models' distributions, and the acceptance/rejection decision is made against a target distribution π = (1−δ)·q + δ·p rather than p alone, allowing the output to deviate from the large model's distribution when the small model is more confident.

## Method

The paper introduces a general speculative sampling recipe (Algorithm 4) that accepts draft tokens against an arbitrary user-specified target distribution π = T(q,p), of which standard and lossy speculative decoding are special cases. Speculative cascades (Algorithm 5) plug in the cascade target distribution π = (1−δ)·q + δ·p, enabling deferral rules like Chow's rule and the Diff rule to be executed speculatively rather than sequentially. The key theoretical contribution is characterizing the optimal deferral rule for speculative cascades (Lemma 4), where the cost of deferral is proportional to the total variation distance D_TV(p_t, q_t) rather than a constant, yielding the plug-in rule max_v q_t(v) < max_v p_t(v) − α·D_TV(p_t, q_t). Additionally, token-specific deferral rules (e.g., TokenV3) compare individual draft token probabilities against the verifier's mode, enabling finer-grained acceptance decisions that outperform distribution-level rules under high-temperature sampling.

## Results

On T5 cascades (T=1, γ=5), SpecCascade[Token] achieves the best quality without exceeding the large model's latency: 22.50 BLEU on WMT EN→DE, 15.85 ROUGE-2 on XSum, and 12.63 ROUGE-2 on CNN/DM (Small→Large), substantially outperforming SpecDecode[Lossy] (17.26/13.90/10.43) and BiLD* (16.49/13.81/10.14). When matching the large model's quality, SpecCascade[OPT] achieves up to 2.61× latency reduction on WMT (Small→XL), compared to 2.17× for SpecDecode[Lossy]. On Gemma 2B→27B cascades, SpecCascade[Token] matches the 27B model's quality at a lower rejection rate and often exceeds 27B quality across 7 of 8 benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpecCascade[Token] dominates all baselines on the quality metric: it achieves 22.50 BLEU on WMT vs. 17.26 for SpecDecode[Lossy] and 16.49 for BiLD*, a gap of 5+ points. On latency reduction when matching large-model quality, SpecCascade[OPT] (1.95× on WMT Small→Large) and SpecCascade[Token] (1.85×) both outperform SpecDecode[Lossy] (1.61×) and BiLD* (1.34×), while sequential cascades (SeqCascade, TokenCascade) are competitive only on WMT and often slower than the large model on summarization tasks. Against TokenCascade[Chow], speculative cascades win on latency due to parallel verification but TokenCascade retains an edge in very low-latency regimes where the large model is rarely invoked. On Gemma 2B→27B, SpecCascade[Token] matches 27B quality at a lower rejection rate than standard SpecDecode across 7 of 8 benchmarks, and the OPT rule is less effective than with T5 due to the larger capacity gap between drafter and verifier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two established lines of work on efficient LM inference—model cascades (Chow's rule, confidence thresholding, Jitkrittum et al. 2023) and speculative decoding (Leviathan et al. 2023, Tran-Thien 2023)—by implementing cascade deferral rules through speculative execution rather than sequential invocation. The closest prior work is BiLD (Kim et al. 2023), which also interleaves small and large models speculatively but targets an approximation to the large model's distribution rather than an optimal cascade between the two. The paper's generalization of speculative decoding to arbitrary target distributions (Algorithm 4) subsumes both standard and lossy speculative decoding as special cases, providing a unified framework in which cascade deferral rules become pluggable components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded recipe for combining the cost-quality trade-offs of cascades with the speed of speculative decoding, yielding a technique that practitioners can adopt with existing speculative decoding infrastructure. A key limitation is that speculative cascades always invoke the large model (in scoring mode) after every γ draft tokens, incurring higher total compute than sequential cascades that skip the large model entirely on easy tokens. Open questions include replacing plug-in estimators with learned router models, extending to more than two models, and adapting the framework to newer speculative decoding variants (Medusa, EAGLE, Sequoia) that involve architectural changes or tree-structured drafts.
