## Problem

Existing embodied AI benchmarks either evaluate robots in isolation or specify tasks without natural language, leaving a gap for realistic human-robot collaboration evaluation. No prior benchmark combines large-scale natural language task diversity, multi-agent coordination, and heterogeneous agent capabilities (e.g., actions only a human can perform) in realistic 3D household environments.

## Input / Output

The planner receives a natural language instruction, a three-layer textual world graph encoding rooms, furniture, and movable objects with semantic categories, 3D bounding boxes, and states (clean/dirty, powered on/off, filled/empty), plus the agent's action history. The evaluation function operates over the complete sequence of simulator states (not just the final state) to handle temporal dependencies, and returns three metrics: percent complete (ratio of satisfied propositions), a binary success flag, and a natural-language failure explanation. The evaluation itself is a generated Python program composed of propositions, temporal dependencies, and constraints (DAGs, same/different-argument links, terminal satisfaction).

## Method

The core contribution is a semi-automated generation pipeline that grounds LLM task and evaluation creation in a Habitat 3.0 simulation: an LLM proposes tasks given a parsed house layout, the simulator instantiates and filters infeasible episodes, and 1,000 human-verified seeds are used as in-context examples to scale to 100,000 tasks via Llama3-70B-Instruct. Evaluation functions are generated by CodeLlama-70B as predicate-based Python programs with propositions, temporal dependencies, and constraints, improving accuracy from 50% to 92% through retrieval-augmented generation of the corresponding annotated evaluation. For planning, the paper compares zero-shot ReAct, ReAct with RAG over successful planning traces, and a LoRA-finetuned Llama3.1-8B distilled from 70B ReAct traces, all operating over the world graph with constrained decoding to valid actions. The finetuned 8B model matches 70B ReAct performance (0.70 vs. 0.73 success) while being 8.6× faster at inference, and offloads more sub-tasks from real human partners in human-in-the-loop evaluation.

## Results

On PARTNR's 1,000-episode test set, the best LLM baseline (centralized ReAct with Llama3.1-70B, oracle skills, full observability) achieves 74% success rate and 88% percent complete, still below the privileged heuristic expert (84% success, 94% percent complete). Under realistic non-privileged conditions (decentralized, learned skills, ConceptGraphs perception), success drops to 30%, while humans solve 93% of tasks. The finetuned Llama3.1-8B planner matches the 70B ReAct baseline (70% vs. 73% success) while being 8.6× faster at inference, and in human-in-the-loop settings, LLM-guided robots require 1.5× more steps than two humans collaborating and 1.1× more than a single human.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five planner configurations (Heuristic Expert, ReAct, ReAct+RAG, Finetuned 8B, and single-agent ReAct) across controllability, skill, and perception axes. Centralized ReAct (74% success) outperforms decentralized ReAct (73% success) with 30% fewer simulation steps (2298 vs. 3295), revealing a significant coordination overhead when LLMs must reason about a partner's actions. ReAct+RAG provides no meaningful improvement over vanilla ReAct (71% vs. 73% success, actually slightly worse), suggesting retrieval of similar planning traces does not help. The finetuned Llama3.1-8B (70% success, 12.85 planning cycles) closely matches the 70B ReAct baseline (73% success, 15.24 cycles) while being 8.6× faster at inference, demonstrating effective knowledge distillation. Replacing oracle skills with learned neural skills drops success from 73% to 57%, and further replacing privileged perception with ConceptGraphs drops it to 30%, exposing LLMs' inability to recover from skill failures and perception errors. In human-in-the-loop evaluation, two humans complete tasks in 2370 steps vs. 3047 for a single human, whereas human+ReAct requires 4268 steps and human+Finetuned requires 3443 steps—both slower than a solo human, confirming that current LLMs are net coordination liabilities compared to human partners.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARTNR carves new ground by combining three previously separate threads: natural-language instruction-following benchmarks (ALFRED, BEHAVIOR-1K), multi-agent embodied collaboration benchmarks (Overcooked, WAH, Co-ELA, RoCo), and LLM-based task generation (GenSim, RoboGen). Prior work satisfies at most two of these axes simultaneously—either tasks are in natural language but single-agent, or multi-agent but without language specification or at limited scale. The simulation-in-the-loop generation pipeline departs from both templated task construction and purely human-crafted evaluations, using LLMs grounded in a live simulator to produce 100k diverse tasks with predicate-based Python evaluation functions, a methodological contribution distinct from PDDL/BDDL-based systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PARTNR is the largest benchmark of its kind (100k tasks, 60 houses, 5,819 objects) and provides the first systematic evaluation of LLM planners along multiple axes (centralized vs. decentralized, privileged vs. learned skills, full vs. partial observability). The finding that LLM-guided robot partners are slower than a human working alone—and that decentralized multi-agent LLMs are slower than single-agent LLMs—challenges the assumption that adding agents should improve throughput, pointing to coordination as a fundamental bottleneck. Open questions include how to enable LLMs to track partner actions, recover from skill/perception failures, and whether multi-modal (VLM) grounding can close the 63-point gap between SoTA LLMs (30%) and humans (93%).
