## Problem

Existing Fourier Neural Operators for Navier-Stokes equations are limited to fixed snapshot counts (product spaces) and, even with end-to-end training, cannot break the 2-digit accuracy barrier in the turbulent regime because they minimize distance to a ground truth that itself carries solver error. The paper addresses both limitations by enabling variable-length spatiotemporal inference and by replacing end-to-end supervision with a physics-based spectral fine-tuning step that targets the true solution directly.

## Input / Output

The model takes a spatiotemporal trajectory of vorticity (scalar) or velocity (vector) snapshots over an input time interval and outputs a predicted trajectory over a subsequent interval. Unlike standard FNO3d, both the number of input snapshots ℓ and output snapshots n_t are free at inference time, enabling zero-shot temporal super-resolution; spatial resolution is also arbitrary (trained at 64×64, evaluated at 256×256). The output is a continuous-in-time spectral representation (Fourier basis on the torus) rather than discrete snapshots, so any number of temporal evaluations can be extracted from a single forward pass.

## Method

The architecture (ST-FNO) modifies FNO3d in three ways: (1) the spectral convolution's temporal kernel is reinterpreted via a continuous measure, (2) the hard-coded lifting operator is replaced by a time-depth separable convolution plus layer normalization that maps arbitrary input lengths to a fixed latent temporal dimension, and (3) a final spatiotemporal spectral convolution layer (K_S) in the projection performs FFT zero-padding to produce arbitrary-length outputs. Training follows a two-phase hybrid paradigm: the full ST-FNO is trained for only ~10 epochs until the energy cascade signature converges, then 95% of parameters are frozen and only K_S is fine-tuned. The fine-tuning loss is the H^{-1} (negative Sobolev) norm of the PDE residual, computed exactly via Parseval identity in the frequency domain, with extra field variables (∂_t u_N) obtained from an auto-differentiable single-step IMEX solver. This loss is convex, requires no ground truth data, and directly measures how well the neural prediction satisfies the Navier-Stokes equations in the variational sense.

## Results

On the 2D isotropic turbulence benchmark (McWilliams, 64×64 training → 256×256 evaluation), ST-FNO3d trained for only 10 epochs with H⁻¹ spectral fine-tuning achieves an L² error of 2.88×10⁻⁴ and H⁻¹ residual of 4.02×10⁻⁶ at the final time step, compared to 3.87×10⁻² L² error for FNO3d trained for 100 epochs without fine-tuning — a ~134× improvement in L² error with 10× fewer training epochs. On the Taylor-Green vortex benchmark, the fine-tuned ST-FNO3d reaches an L² error of 1.24×10⁻⁷, surpassing the traditional Pseudo-Spectral + RK2 solver (5.91×10⁻⁶) by nearly 50×. The H⁻¹ residual after fine-tuning drops to 4.02×10⁻⁶ (turbulence) and 3.21×10⁻⁷ (Taylor-Green), indicating the solution closely satisfies the PDE in the variational sense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the original FNO3d snapshot learner (Li et al., 2021) trained end-to-end with L² loss. ST-FNO3d with 10 epochs of training plus H⁻¹ spectral fine-tuning outperforms FNO3d with 100 epochs (no fine-tuning) by ~134× in L² error (2.88×10⁻⁴ vs. 3.87×10⁻²) and ~6000× in H⁻¹ residual (4.02×10⁻⁶ vs. 2.44×10⁻²), while using only 10% of the training epochs. On the Taylor-Green vortex benchmark, the fine-tuned ST-FNO3d (L² = 1.24×10⁻⁷) also surpasses the traditional Pseudo-Spectral + RK2 solver (L² = 5.91×10⁻⁶) by ~48×, demonstrating that the hybrid spectral fine-tuning paradigm can exceed conventional numerical solvers in accuracy. The ablation in Table 3 further shows that using the H⁻¹ (negative Sobolev) norm for fine-tuning is critical: replacing it with L² fine-tuning degrades the final L² error by ~6× (1.79×10⁻³ vs. 2.88×10⁻⁴) and the H⁻¹ residual by ~24× (9.55×10⁻⁵ vs. 4.02×10⁻⁶), confirming that the functional-type a posteriori error estimator is the key ingredient for the accuracy gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly modifies FNO3d (Li et al., 2021) by replacing its hard-coded temporal dimension with a trajectory-to-trajectory formulation between Bochner spaces, enabling arbitrary-length temporal super-resolution for the first time. Conceptually, it departs from the end-to-end supervised paradigm (minimizing distance to numerical-solver ground truth) and from PDE-Refiner's (Lippe et al., 2023) approach of training a separate error-correcting NO under a diffusion framework, instead proposing a hybrid paradigm where only the final spectral convolution layer is fine-tuned using a negative Sobolev norm loss derived from functional-type a posteriori error estimation (Repin, 2008). The Gelfand triple structure and Parseval identity from classical spectral methods are repurposed to make this loss both exact and convex, a property absent from all prior end-to-end or physics-informed operator learning losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10^5 accuracy improvement over standard end-to-end FNO training, achieved with only 10 epochs plus lightweight spectral fine-tuning, suggests that the dominant bottleneck in current neural operator accuracy is not model capacity but the training objective and evaluation protocol. The convexity of the fine-tuning subproblem and the elimination of ground-truth dependence (the loss uses only the PDE residual, not solver-generated data) open a path toward self-correcting surrogate models. Key limitations include restriction to FNO-based architectures (the spectral structure is essential for the Parseval-based loss evaluation), 2D periodic NSE only, and the need for an auto-differentiable IMEX solver during fine-tuning. Generalization to 3D flows, non-periodic domains, and non-FNO operator families (e.g., Transformers, DeepONet) remains open.
