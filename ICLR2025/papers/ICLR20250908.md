## Problem

While global convergence of policy gradient is well-established for discounted reward MDPs, no complete finite-time convergence proof existed for average reward MDPs. Existing discounted reward bounds grow as (1−γ)^{-5} and diverge as γ→1, making them inapplicable to the average reward limit. Prior average reward results relied on an unverified smoothness assumption for the average cost function, leaving the global convergence question open.

## Input / Output

The method operates on ergodic tabular MDPs with finite state space S, finite action space A, a known transition kernel P, and a bounded single-step reward r, under the assumption that every induced Markov chain is irreducible and aperiodic. Given an initial tabular policy π₀ and step size η < 1/L₂^Π, the algorithm produces a sequence of policy iterates π_k via projected gradient ascent. The convergence bound depends explicitly on MDP-structural constants (mixing rate C_m, transition kernel diameter C_p, reward diameter C_r, reward variance κ_r) rather than solely on |S|·|A|, meaning structurally simpler MDPs of the same size converge faster.

## Method

The key technical contribution is proving smoothness of the average reward ρ^π without assuming it, by first establishing smoothness of a uniquely defined projected value function v_φ^π = (I − ΦP^π)^{−1}Φr^π, where Φ projects onto the subspace orthogonal to the all-ones vector. This projection resolves the non-uniqueness of the average reward value function (which is only defined up to an additive constant), and the authors verify invertibility of (I − ΦP^π) via geometric ergodicity. They then transfer smoothness from v_φ^π to ρ^π through the Bellman equation, obtaining a restricted smoothness constant L₂^Π expressed in terms of MDP complexity parameters. Standard projected gradient ascent analysis with this smoothness yields O(1/T) sublinear convergence with O(log T) regret, and for simple MDPs (L₂^Π ≪ 1) the rate improves to exponential. The framework is further extended to discounted MDPs, replacing the |S||A| dependence in prior bounds with the structural constant L₂^Π, yielding tighter guarantees for low-complexity problems.

## Results

The paper establishes the first finite-time global convergence bound for projected policy gradient in ergodic average reward MDPs: the suboptimality satisfies ρ* − ρ^{π_k} ≤ 1/(1/(ρ* − ρ^{π_0}) + νk), yielding an overall regret of O(log T). For 'simple' MDPs (where the restricted smoothness constant L_2^Π ≪ 1), the convergence is exponential. When extended to discounted MDPs, the iteration complexity improves from O(|S||A|/((1−γ)^5 ε)) (Xiao, 2022) to O(|S|L_2^Π/ε), where L_2^Π is an MDP-complexity-dependent constant that can be independent of |A| in structured problems. Simulations on tabular MDPs with (|S|,|A|) ∈ {(3,3),(9,9),(81,81)} and varying reward variances confirm that convergence speed is governed by the MDP complexity constants C_p, C_r, and C_m rather than solely by state/action space cardinality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct algorithmic baselines; comparisons are made against prior convergence bounds. Compared to Bai et al. (2023), which achieves a regret of O(T^{1/4}) under an unverified smoothness assumption, this paper proves O(log T) regret without any smoothness assumption, by establishing smoothness of the average reward via a projection technique. Compared to Xiao (2022) and Agarwal et al. (2020) for discounted MDPs, the iteration complexity improves from O(|S||A|/((1−γ)^5 ε)) to O(|S|L_2^Π/ε), where L_2^Π can be orders of magnitude smaller than |A|/(1−γ)^5 for low-complexity MDPs (e.g., when C_p = 0, the bound reduces to O(|S|/ε) versus O(|S||A|/ε)). The key advantage over all prior average-reward results (Murthy & Srikant 2023, Grosof et al. 2024) is the elimination of the smoothness assumption, which had been assumed but never verified in the tabular setting. The simulations further demonstrate that for fixed state/action space sizes, MDPs with lower reward variance (smaller C_r) or more stochastic transitions (smaller C_p) converge significantly faster, a phenomenon not captured by prior cardinality-only bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the smoothness-based convergence analysis framework of Agarwal et al. (2020) and Xiao (2022) from discounted to average reward MDPs, while building on the foundational natural policy gradient convergence results of Even-Dar et al. (2009) and Murthy & Srikant (2023). The key departure from prior work (Bai et al. 2023, Ganesh et al. 2024) is the elimination of an unverified smoothness assumption on the average reward objective: the authors prove smoothness directly via a projection technique that resolves the non-uniqueness of the average reward value function. The discounted MDP extension also improves upon Xiao's (2022) bounds by introducing MDP-specific complexity constants in place of the state/action space cardinality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first complete finite-time global convergence proof for projected policy gradient in average reward MDPs without relying on unverified smoothness assumptions, filling a significant theoretical gap that has persisted since the discounted MDP results were established. The introduction of MDP complexity constants (C_m, C_p, C_r, κ_r) that govern convergence rates—rather than merely |S| and |A|—offers a new lens for understanding why some MDPs are inherently harder for policy gradient, with empirical validation showing that fixed-size MDPs can exhibit dramatically different convergence speeds. Key limitations include the restriction to tabular policies, the use of exact gradients (no learning error), and the irreducibility/aperiodicity assumption; extending to parametric policies and stochastic gradient settings remains open.
