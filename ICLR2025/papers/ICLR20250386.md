## Problem

Policy evaluation in RL suffers from high variance due to the sequential nature of importance sampling, requiring massive online data for accurate estimates. Prior work optimizes either the data-collecting behavior policy or the data-processing baseline function in isolation, but no method jointly optimizes both. This paper identifies that the two components are interdependent and that their joint optimization yields substantially greater variance reduction than either alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250386/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target policy π and a behavior-policy-agnostic offline dataset of (t, s, a, r, s') tuples that need not form complete trajectories and may come from unknown, diverse policies. It outputs a closed-form optimal behavior policy μ* and optimal baseline b* = q_{π,t}(s,a), which are then used to collect online data and compute an unbiased PDIS estimate of J(π). All hyperparameters for learning (μ*, b*) are tuned offline and remain fixed across environments.

## Method

The paper formulates variance reduction as a bi-level optimization: the inner problem finds the optimal behavior policy μ* for a given baseline b, and the outer problem finds the optimal baseline b* given μ*. The inner solution is μ*(a|s) ∝ π(a|s)√u_{π,t}(s,a), where u combines the squared q-baseline gap, next-state value variance, and expected future estimator variance. The outer solution is simply b*(s,a) = q_{π,t}(s,a), which eliminates the q-function variance term entirely. A key technical contribution is enlarging the admissible policy space from the classic coverage constraint Λ⁻ to a broader set Λ that guarantees unbiasedness while permitting the optimal μ*. A recursive form of u (Lemma 3) makes the method learnable from offline data via Fitted Q-Evaluation without approximating complicated trajectory-level variances.

## Results

On Gridworld (sizes 1,000 and 27,000), DOpt achieves a relative variance of 0.274 and 0.283 respectively, reducing variance by ~75% compared to on-policy Monte Carlo (normalized to 1.000). On MuJoCo tasks, DOpt requires only 225–492 episodes to match the accuracy that on-policy Monte Carlo needs 1,000 episodes to achieve, saving 50.8%–77.5% of online interactions. The method consistently outperforms both the ODI and DR baselines across all tested environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOpt outperforms both baselines across all environments. Compared to ODI (Liu and Zhang, 2024), which optimizes only the behavior policy without a tailored baseline, DOpt reduces relative variance from 0.467–0.481 to 0.274–0.283 on Gridworld and requires 30–40% fewer episodes on MuJoCo tasks (e.g., 225 vs. 356 on I.Pendulum). Compared to DR (Jiang and Li, 2016), which uses a fixed behavior policy (the target policy itself) with a doubly robust baseline, DOpt achieves lower variance in both Gridworld sizes (0.274 vs. 0.450 at size 1,000; 0.283 vs. 0.541 at size 27,000) and fewer episodes on all MuJoCo tasks. Notably, DR slightly outperforms ODI on the smaller Gridworld (0.450 vs. 0.467) but underperforms on the larger one (0.541 vs. 0.481), indicating no clear dominance between those two methods, while DOpt dominates both in all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work in off-policy evaluation variance reduction: behavior policy design (Hanna et al. 2017; Liu and Zhang 2024) and baseline function selection (Jiang and Li 2016; Thomas and Brunskill 2016). Prior methods optimized one component while holding the other fixed, but this paper formulates the joint problem as a bi-level optimization and derives closed-form solutions for both the optimal behavior policy μ* ∝ π(a|s)√(u_{π,t}(s,a)) and the optimal baseline b* = q_{π,t}(s,a). The key conceptual departure from Liu and Zhang (2024) is recognizing that the behavior policy must be tailored to the baseline, and from Jiang and Li (2016) is that the behavior policy itself should be optimized rather than fixed at the target policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Policy evaluation is a critical bottleneck in RL deployment, and the demonstrated 50–77% reduction in required online samples (e.g., 225 vs. 1000 episodes on InvertedPendulum) has direct practical impact for expensive environments like data-center cooling or robotics. The method is model-free, works with incomplete offline data from unknown policies, and requires no hyperparameter tuning across environments, making it broadly applicable. A stated limitation is that the offline data must be sufficiently large for accurate approximation of μ* and b*; with very little offline data, the authors recommend falling back to on-policy evaluation. Future work extends the framework to temporal difference learning, which would broaden applicability beyond Monte Carlo estimators.
