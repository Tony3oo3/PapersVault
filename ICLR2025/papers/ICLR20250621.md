## Problem

Existing pedestrian trajectory prediction methods struggle to align coordinate-based trajectory features (temporal domain) with image-based scene features (spatial domain). Scene-embedding approaches suffer from feature misalignment because the network must learn dynamic dimension-to-dimension relations, while heatmap-based approaches achieve alignment but represent each pedestrian as a high-dimensional tensor, making human-human interaction computation prohibitively expensive. The paper seeks a unified representation that preserves spatial alignment while keeping per-pedestrian features compact enough for efficient interaction modeling.

## Input / Output

The method takes N pedestrians' historical 2D coordinates (τ observed frames) and a single scene map (RGB image or semantic segmentation, C×H×W) that must share the same pixel coordinate system. Output is structured as cell-level predictions: for each of an L×L goal grid or l×l step grid, the network produces a 1-D confidence score and a 2-D offset from the cell center, from which the final coordinate is recovered. Multi-modality is handled by sampling 20 trajectories per pedestrian via a two-step (m, n) grid-then-cell sampling procedure, with a per-pedestrian hyper-classifier selecting the optimal (m, n) combination at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is the Trajectory-Scene-Cell (TSC) feature, which decouples trajectory in the temporal domain and scene in the spatial domain, then re-organizes both into spatial-temporal cells where each cell concatenates a learnable coordinate embedding (via MLP) with a local CNN-extracted scene patch, yielding a unified feature space with static inter-dimension relations. Three TSC variants are constructed: trajectory cells (one per observed time step), goal scene cells (L×L grid centered on the last observation), and step scene cells (l×l grid centered on each predicted step). Prediction is reformulated as cell classification: a CVAE-based goal network assigns confidence and offset to each goal cell, and a step-by-step trajectory completion network does the same for intermediate positions, with a transformer-based history encoder (K self-attention layers over spatial and temporal axes) capturing human-human interactions and cross-attention layers linking scene cells to trajectory history. A hybrid sampling strategy uses a lightweight MLP hyper-classifier to select the (m, n) sampling parameters per pedestrian, balancing goal variance for short/straight versus long/irregular trajectories.

## Results

On the Stanford Drone Dataset (SDD, short-term T=20, τ=8), TSC-Net achieves the best ADE of 6.44 and FDE of 9.97, outperforming the previous SOTA NSP-SFM* (6.52/10.61) by 0.08 ADE and 0.64 FDE. On ETH-UCY, TSC-Net achieves 0.20 ADE / 0.32 FDE (average), ranking third behind NSP-SFM* (0.17/0.24) and E-V2-Net-SC (0.17/0.27), which the authors attribute to the binary scene maps providing minimal semantic information. In the long-term setting (T=35, τ=5), TSC-Net achieves the best FDE on both SDD (63.97) and InD (19.53), though its ADE is slightly higher than Y-Net* on both datasets (51.39 vs 47.94 on SDD; 17.15 vs 14.99 on InD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SDD (short-term), TSC-Net achieves the best ADE (6.44) and FDE (9.97) among all 15 compared methods, beating the closest competitor NSP-SFM* (6.52/10.61) by 0.08 ADE and 0.64 FDE, and E-V2-Net-SC (6.54/10.36) by 0.10 ADE and 0.39 FDE. On ETH-UCY, TSC-Net (0.20/0.32) trails NSP-SFM* (0.17/0.24) and E-V2-Net-SC (0.17/0.27), which the authors explain is due to the dataset's binary traversable/untraversable scene maps offering negligible semantic signal for TSC features to exploit. In the long-term setting (T=35, τ=5), TSC-Net achieves the best FDE on both SDD (63.97 vs Y-Net*'s 66.71) and InD (19.53 vs Y-Net*'s 21.13), but its ADE is slightly worse than Y-Net* on both (51.39 vs 47.94 on SDD; 17.15 vs 14.99 on InD), which the authors attribute to larger cell sizes summarizing less scene detail over longer horizons. Notably, TSC-Net requires only 20 samples at inference (via hybrid sampling), whereas Y-Net* and NSP-SFM* require clustering 10,000 samples to obtain their final 20 predictions. Against scene-embedding-based methods like SoPhie (16.27/29.38) and MG-GAN (13.60/25.80) on SDD, TSC-Net shows substantial improvements of over 9 ADE and 19 FDE, demonstrating the advantage of the TSC cell representation over global scene feature concatenation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the hierarchical two-stage prediction paradigm (goal estimation followed by trajectory completion) established by Mangalam et al. (2020, 2021) in PECNet and Y-Net, but departs from both the scene-embedding and heatmap-based fusion strategies used in prior work. The core conceptual contribution is the Trajectory-Scene-Cell (TSC) feature, which re-frames the feature alignment problem by decoupling trajectory in the temporal domain and scene in the spatial domain, then re-organizing both into a unified cell format. This positions the work between the two dominant paradigms—CNN-embedded scene vectors (which suffer from dynamic dimension misalignment) and heatmap-based trajectory encoding (which incurs prohibitive cost for human-human interaction modeling)—offering a middle ground that preserves spatial alignment while keeping per-pedestrian features compact enough for attention-based interaction modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TSC feature provides a clean, principled solution to the long-standing feature alignment problem in scene-aware trajectory prediction, and the reformulation of prediction as cell classification plus offset regression is a useful alternative to direct coordinate regression. However, the practical impact is tempered by the modest SOTA margins (e.g., 0.64 FDE improvement on SDD short-term) and the acknowledged limitation that predicted offsets bias toward cell centers, reducing coverage of cell-edge regions. The method's advantage is also dataset-dependent: on ETH-UCY, where scene maps are binary (traversable/non-traversable), the TSC features degenerate to standard trajectory embeddings and performance gains vanish. Open questions include whether the cell-classification paradigm scales to higher-resolution grids or 3D scenes, and whether the hybrid sampling strategy can be replaced by a learned, per-pedestrian sampling distribution.
