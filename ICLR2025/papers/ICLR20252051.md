## Problem

Existing training-free stylization methods for diffusion models suffer from three intertwined failures: they cannot reliably extract style from a single reference image without auxiliary text descriptions, they leak content from the reference style image into the generated output, and they depend on external components (DDIM inversion, IP-Adapter, ControlNet) that compromise fine-grained detail, limit diversity, or are not universally applicable across architectures. No prior training-free framework provides a principled mechanism to simultaneously control style fidelity and prompt adherence while preventing content leakage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (encoded via CLIP), a single reference style image (encoded via CSD's style attention head), and optionally a reference content image (encoded via CSD's content attention head) for the composition task. It operates on the StableCascade two-stage latent diffusion model and produces a single stylized or content-style-composed image. Inference completes in ~44 seconds on a single A100 GPU with no training, no DDIM inversion, and no additional parameters in the UNet, matching the speed of other training-free baselines while avoiding their architectural dependencies.

## Method

RB-Modulation formulates the reverse diffusion SDE as a stochastic optimal control problem where a style descriptor Ψ(·) (from CSD) enters the controller's terminal cost, and the resulting optimal drift modulation is computed at each denoising step. Two modules work in concert: (1) a Stochastic Optimal Controller (SOC) that minimizes the style-feature discrepancy between the reference image and the Tweedie-estimated terminal state via a few steps of proximal gradient descent (Algorithm 2), avoiding expensive backpropagation through the score network; and (2) an Attention Feature Aggregation (AFA) module that replaces the standard cross-attention processor by computing separate attention outputs for text, style, and content keys/values, then averaging them to decouple content from style. The theoretical analysis derives closed-form optimal controllers via the HJB equation in the γ→∞ limit, showing the controller depends only on style features at the terminal time rather than image content, which is the key mechanism preventing content leakage.

## Results

On the StyleAligned benchmark (300 prompts, 100 styles), RB-Modulation achieves an ImageReward of 0.91 without style descriptions and 1.18 with, substantially outperforming InstantStyle (0.09/0.72), StyleAligned (−0.68/0.01), and IP-Adapter (−1.99/−1.51). CLIP-T scores are competitive across all methods (0.30–0.34), while DINO style-similarity scores are slightly lower for RB-Modulation (0.68) than IP-Adapter (0.89) or StyleAligned (0.80), which the authors attribute to content leakage inflating those metrics. In a 155-participant user study, RB-Modulation is preferred over InstantStyle 51–55% of the time, over StyleAligned 65–67%, and over IP-Adapter 75–87% across overall quality, style alignment, and prompt alignment. For content-style composition, RB-Modulation achieves ImageReward 0.74 vs. −0.54 (InstantStyle) and −0.78 (IP-Adapter), with matching CLIP-I content scores (0.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest training-free baseline InstantStyle, RB-Modulation wins decisively on ImageReward (0.91 vs. 0.09 without style descriptions; 1.18 vs. 0.72 with) and narrowly on CLIP-T (0.30 vs. 0.29), while matching on DINO (0.68 vs. 0.68); in the user study it is preferred 51–55% of the time across all three quality metrics. Against StyleAligned, RB-Modulation wins on ImageReward (0.91 vs. −0.68) and CLIP-T (0.30 vs. 0.26) but trails on DINO (0.68 vs. 0.80), a gap the authors explain as StyleAligned's content leakage inflating its style-similarity score; user preference strongly favors RB-Modulation at 65–67%. Against IP-Adapter, the ImageReward gap is the largest (0.91 vs. −1.99) and user preference is overwhelming at 75–87%, though IP-Adapter scores higher on DINO (0.89 vs. 0.68) for the same leakage reason. Against the training-based baselines StyleDrop and ZipLoRA, RB-Modulation requires no training (44 s inference vs. 300+30 s and 1200+40 s respectively) and qualitatively avoids the content leakage and fixed-layout constraints that affect those methods, while achieving competitive or superior ImageReward scores in the composition task (0.74 vs. no reported numbers for ZipLoRA in Table 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on StableCascade as its base diffusion architecture and CSD (Contrastive Style Descriptor) for style feature extraction, but its core conceptual departure is framing reverse diffusion dynamics as a stochastic optimal control problem via the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation. It departs from the dominant training-free stylization paradigm (StyleAligned, InstantStyle, SSA) that relies on DDIM inversion and attention key/value manipulation, which the authors show causes content leakage and fine-grained detail loss. The AFA module introduces a new cross-attention aggregation scheme that explicitly disentangles content and style through separate attention heads, while the SOC module replaces the need for external adapters (IP-Adapter) or ControlNets by modulating the drift field through a terminal cost on style features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free personalization framework grounded in stochastic optimal control, offering a principled alternative to the ad-hoc attention manipulation strategies that dominate the field. The elimination of DDIM inversion, external adapters, and ControlNets simplifies the pipeline and enables 5-20× speedup over training-based methods while matching or exceeding SOTA in human preference. However, the theoretical results assume a Gaussian prior (not true for real data distributions), the practical implementation relies on a proximal approximation (Algorithm 2) that only approximately satisfies the dynamics, and the method has only been validated on StableCascade—open questions remain about generalization to other architectures and personalization tasks like identity preservation or multi-concept composition.
