## Problem

Controllable human image animation relies on skeleton pose as a control signal, but sparse poses provide limited motion guidance while dense alternatives (depth maps, DensePose, SMPL renders) impose rigid geometric constraints that fail when the reference character's body shape differs from the driving video. Existing methods struggle to balance generalizability across body shapes with effective region-level motion control, and dense reference conditioning (e.g., dual U-Net architectures) is both expensive and ineffective for shape-changing actions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image and a driving video, from which only skeleton poses are extracted via DWpose—no depth maps, DensePose labels, or 3D body models are required at inference. The output is a 16-frame animated video at 512×512 (MusePose) or 768×1024 (MimicMotion) resolution, where the reference character follows the driving video's motion while preserving its appearance. The plug-and-play design means the base animation model's parameters remain frozen; only the added ControlNet, motion encoder, and point encoder are trained.

## Method

DisPose disentangles the sparse skeleton pose into two complementary control signals: (1) a motion field guidance that combines a sparse motion field (Gaussian-filtered keypoint displacement trajectories) with a dense motion field predicted by Conditional Motion Propagation (CMP) from the reference image, providing region-level motion without target-pose geometric constraints; and (2) a keypoint correspondence that extracts DIFT diffusion features at reference-image keypoints and transfers them to target-pose positions via trajectory mapping, creating multi-scale semantic point maps for appearance consistency. These signals are injected through a plug-and-play hybrid ControlNet: the motion field is added to the noise latent before entering the ControlNet, while point features are added to convolutional layer inputs and element-wise to the ControlNet's U-Net encoder intermediate features. The entire module is trained with the base animation model frozen, requiring only ~653 MB of trainable parameters (vs. 1454 MB for MimicMotion), and integrates seamlessly into both two-stage (AnimateAnyone-style) and single-stage (SVD-based) training paradigms.

## Results

On the TikTok dataset, DisPose integrated into MimicMotion achieves FVD of 466.93 and CD-FVD of 603.27, substantially outperforming the MimicMotion baseline (FVD 598.41, CD-FVD 621.90) and all other compared methods. The plug-and-play module also improves MusePose from FVD 866.36 to 764.00 and FID-FVD from 11.48 to 11.26. Across VBench sub-metrics, DisPose consistently raises Subject Consistency (95.72 vs 94.95 for MimicMotion), Imaging Quality (71.33 vs 68.42), and Dynamic Degree (29.51 vs 28.42), confirming gains in both perceptual quality and motion fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisPose is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on two base architectures (MusePose/SD1.5 and MimicMotion/SVD) and compared against five baselines. Against MagicPose, DisPose-on-MusePose reduces FVD from 1015.04 to 764.00 and FID-FVD from 15.53 to 11.26, a large margin driven by better motion alignment. Against Moore-AnimateAnyone, the improvement is more moderate (FVD 924.40 → 764.00) but still clear. Against the direct base MusePose, DisPose cuts FVD by ~12% (866.36 → 764.00) and raises Dynamic Degree from 27.31 to 31.34, indicating more expressive motion. Against ControlNeXt (SVD-based), DisPose-on-MimicMotion wins on FVD (466.93 vs 496.87) and FID-FVD (10.24 vs 10.49) while also improving Subject Consistency (95.72 vs 94.58). Against MimicMotion itself, the most significant gain is FVD dropping from 598.41 to 466.93 (−22%) and Imaging Quality rising from 68.42 to 71.33, confirming that the disentangled motion-field and keypoint-correspondence guidance is effective even on a strong SVD-based baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of sparse pose control (OpenPose/DWpose skeletons) and dense geometric conditioning (DensePose, SMPL depth/normal maps) in human image animation, arguing that both extremes are suboptimal: sparse poses under-constrain motion while dense conditions over-constrain shape and fail across body-shape differences. It builds on ControlNet's plug-and-play conditioning architecture, CMP's sparse-to-dense motion propagation, and DIFT's emergent diffusion features for semantic correspondence, but the key conceptual departure is disentangling a single skeleton pose into two functionally distinct signals—region-level motion fields and point-level identity features—without requiring any dense input at inference. Unlike AnimateAnyone/MusePose (dual U-Net reference networks) or MimicMotion (SVD fine-tuning), DisPose freezes the base model entirely and trains only a lightweight hybrid ControlNet, making it applicable across both two-stage and single-stage animation paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design lowers the barrier for improving existing animation pipelines, and the consistent gains across two architecturally different base models (MusePose on SD1.5, MimicMotion on SVD) suggest the disentanglement principle is broadly applicable. However, the paper acknowledges a fundamental limitation: the method cannot synthesize unseen body parts (e.g., multi-view generation from a single reference), which remains an open problem requiring 3D or multi-view conditioning. The reliance on DIFT features for keypoint correspondence is an interesting repurposing of diffusion model internals, but its robustness to large pose changes or occlusions is not thoroughly stress-tested. For practitioners, the main takeaway is that the sparse-to-dense motion propagation via CMP plus DIFT point features is a practical recipe for improving pose-guided animation without the cost of dense signal extraction.
