## Problem

Prior procedural video representation learning methods (DistantSup, ProceduralVRL) rely on computationally expensive large-scale pretraining of prediction models on video clip sequences with noisy ASR or wikiHow text supervision, yet the necessity and effectiveness of this pretraining have not been validated. The paper asks how far one can go without pretraining the predictor on clip sequences or requiring additional language supervision, hypothesizing that a well-designed prediction model over strong frozen visual encoder representations is a compelling alternative.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251478/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of N video clips from a procedural activity, each encoded by a frozen visual encoder (default: SigLIP ViT-SO400M/14@384) into frame-level [CLS] tokens from 16 uniformly sampled frames per clip, yielding embeddings in R^{k×D}. Unlike pixel-level DiT models, VEDiT operates entirely in this abstract latent space at the clip level. Outputs are denoised target clip embeddings (for step forecasting or procedure planning) or a pooled task-level embedding (for task classification), which are passed to a lightweight attentive classifier producing class labels; on Ego4D-v2, the output is a sequence of (verb, noun) action pairs scored by edit distance.

## Method

VEDiT is a two-branch diffusion transformer that generates unseen video clip embeddings from random Gaussian noise via iterative denoising with the Flow Matching Euler Discrete Scheduler (24 steps, classifier-free guidance scale 7). The query branch processes observed (seen) clip embeddings while the target branch processes noisy target embeddings; both are fused through joint attention (concatenating Q, K, V from both branches) with RoPE applied for temporal ordering, followed by separate feed-forward layers without weight sharing. A key distinction from prior work is that training uses a single cross-entropy loss on downstream task labels rather than a combination of embedding reconstruction and video-language matching losses, eliminating the need for ASR or wikiHow supervision. The model is trained directly on downstream datasets (COIN, NIV, CrossTask, Ego4D-v2) without any clip-sequence pretraining, and the frozen encoder (SigLIP) provides language-aligned visual representations that the prediction model leverages to learn temporal transitions.

## Results

On COIN step forecasting, SigLIP+VEDiT achieves 51.8% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA ProceduralVRL (46.8%) by +5.0% without any large-scale pretraining or language supervision. On COIN task classification, the same model reaches 94.6% vs. ProceduralVRL's 90.8% (+3.8%). On Ego4D-v2 long-horizon action anticipation, VEDiT with SigLIP achieves Verb ED@20 of 0.697 and Noun ED@20 of 0.711, outperforming the Ego4D baseline (0.745/0.779 with SlowFast). On procedure planning (NIV, COIN, CrossTask), replacing SCHEMA's vanilla transformer blocks with VEDiT yields gains of up to +5.89% SR, +7.07% mAcc, and +2.57% mIoU on COIN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VEDiT with SigLIP decisively outperforms all prior procedural video representation learning baselines on COIN: ProceduralVRL (46.8%→51.8% step forecasting, 90.8%→94.6% task classification), DistantSup (39.4%→51.8%, 88.9%→94.6%), and TimeSformer variants (34.7%→51.8%, 83.5%→94.6%), all without requiring large-scale video clip sequence pretraining or noisy ASR/wikiHow text supervision. On procedure planning, SCHEMA w/ VEDiT consistently beats the original SCHEMA across NIV, COIN, and CrossTask (e.g., COIN T=3: 32.27% SR vs. 26.38% replicated SCHEMA; 50.15% mAcc vs. 43.08%), and also surpasses earlier methods like EGPP, P3IV, DDN, and Ext-GAIL. On Ego4D-v2 long-horizon anticipation, VEDiT+SigLIP (Verb ED@20: 0.697, Noun ED@20: 0.711) outperforms the Ego4D SlowFast baseline (0.745/0.779) and the SigLIP+Transformer variant (0.718/0.742), though it trails PaMsEgoAI (0.670/0.629), which the authors note uses an ensemble of SlowFast and SlowFast-CLIP with additional label smoothing and co-occurrence constraints, making it not directly comparable. Among visual encoders, SigLIP (CLIP-based, language-aligned) outperforms SSL encoders V-JEPA, DINOv2, and VideoMAE on step forecasting and task classification, confirming the importance of language-aligned representations. The paper also shows that adding HowTo100M clip-sequence pretraining yields only a marginal +0.3% gain, validating that the frozen-encoder + VEDiT design is sufficient without expensive pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

VEDiT adapts the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) from pixel/patch-level image-video generation into a latent-space step prediction model for procedural activities, replacing text conditioning with observed video clip embeddings and introducing joint attention with RoPE for temporal modeling. It departs from prior procedural video learning (DistantSup, ProceduralVRL) by eliminating the need for large-scale clip-sequence pretraining with noisy ASR/wikiHow supervision, instead training a single cross-entropy loss directly on downstream tasks over frozen pretrained encoder embeddings. The procedure planning experiments build directly on SCHEMA (Niu et al., 2024) by swapping its vanilla transformer blocks for VEDiT blocks, while the Ego4D experiments replace the SlowFast encoder with SigLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a core assumption in procedural video learning—that extensive pretraining on video clip sequences with language supervision is necessary—by showing that a well-designed prediction model over strong frozen single-clip representations achieves SOTA without any such pretraining. The finding that HowTo100M clip-sequence pretraining yields only 0.3% improvement is notable but tested under a single pretraining recipe, leaving open what objectives or data distributions might actually help. Practical limitations include the multi-step denoising overhead (not real-time capable) and the reliance on a specific encoder (SigLIP) whose language-aligned features prove critical, suggesting the approach may not transfer to purely visual domains without such alignment.
