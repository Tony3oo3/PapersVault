## Problem

Practitioners fine-tuning small LLMs (3B–7B parameters) lack actionable guidance on optimal training configurations, as most open-source models omit detailed hyperparameter reports and widely recommended practices (e.g., TULU's batch size of 128, phased training) remain empirically unvalidated. This paper addresses how to effectively fine-tune small LLMs on diverse knowledge and skills instruction-tuning data, bridging the resource gap between well-funded industrial labs and individual developers or small organizations.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained decoder-only transformer models (Granite 3B/7B, Mistral 7B, LLaMA 3.2 3B) and instruction-tuning datasets organized into three phases—instruction-following (308K samples), foundational knowledge (231K samples), and complex skills (286K samples)—and produces fine-tuned models along with concrete hyperparameter recommendations. Effective batch sizes of 3,840–7,680 samples are achieved via gradient accumulation on single-node or multi-node GPU setups, and the primary training corpus is the taxonomy-driven LAB synthetic dataset, supplemented by TULU 2 and a domain-specific Math/Reasoning/Code dataset for cross-validation.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical study varying batch size (128, 3,840, 7,680), learning rate (1×10⁻⁶ to 1×10⁻⁴), warmup steps (0, 25, 100), and learning rate schedule (constant vs. cosine decay) across four model families, comparing stacked training (all data simultaneously) against sequential phased training. Key findings challenge established practices: larger batch sizes paired with lower learning rates (2×10⁻⁵ for Granite, 1×10⁻⁶ for Mistral) consistently outperform TULU's recommended configuration; omitting warmup and using a constant learning rate does not degrade performance; and stacked training matches or exceeds phased training while being more sample-efficient. The authors further show that early training dynamics—specifically lower gradient norms and higher loss values—serve as reliable predictors of final benchmark performance, enabling early termination of suboptimal runs and significant compute savings. Training infrastructure employs the Adam optimizer (β₂=0.95) and a Multipack-based distributed sampler that balances token load across GPUs to maximize throughput at large effective batch sizes.

## Results

Using the LAB configuration (batch size 3,840, LR 2×10⁻⁵, constant schedule) with stacked training on Granite 7B, the paper achieves MMLU 0.53, MTBench 6.77, GSM8K 0.39, and ARC 0.76, compared to base model scores of 0.48, –, 0.11, and 0.78 respectively. Larger batch sizes (4K→8K) yield consistent gains: MMLU improves from 0.516 to 0.529 and MTBench from 6.406 to 6.831 in stacked training. On the TULU dataset, a 4K batch size outperforms TULU's recommended 128 batch on MMLU (0.50 vs 0.48), BBH (0.44 vs 0.40), and GSM8K (0.28 vs 0.25). Stacked training reaches peak MMLU performance at ~3.7M samples versus ~7.9M for phased training, demonstrating roughly 2× sample efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TULU (Wang et al., 2023b), the LAB configuration (batch 3,840, constant LR 2×10⁻⁵, no warmup) outperforms TULU's settings (batch 128, warmup + linear decay, LR 2×10⁻⁵) on every benchmark: BBH 0.10 vs 0.08, MuSR 0.07 vs 0.03, ARC 0.75 vs 0.74, and GSM8K 0.37 vs 0.36. On the TULU dataset itself, the 4K batch size beats TULU's 128 batch on MMLU (0.50 vs 0.48), BBH (0.44 vs 0.40), GSM8K (0.28 vs 0.25), and ToxiGen (0.55 vs 0.54), with only a marginal loss on TruthfulQA (0.44 vs 0.45). Against LAB's original phased-training protocol (Sudalairaj et al., 2024), the paper shows that stacked training slightly outperforms phased training (MMLU 0.53 vs 0.52, MTBench 6.77 vs 6.76) while requiring roughly half the samples to reach peak performance (~3.7M vs ~7.9M). The MuSR gap (0.07 vs 0.03) is the largest single-benchmark win over TULU, suggesting the larger batch and constant LR particularly benefit compositional reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LAB multi-phase instruction-tuning framework (Sudalairaj et al., 2024) and positions itself as a systematic empirical study that fills a gap left by prior instruction-tuning work, which often omits hyperparameter details or only reports successful configurations. It directly challenges the TULU configuration (Wang et al., 2023b), widely regarded as a gold standard for LLM fine-tuning, and the phased training strategy popularized by Orca (Mitra et al., 2023). The paper also connects to the broader literature on training dynamics and generalization in neural networks (Keskar et al., 2016; Hoffer et al., 2017), noting that findings from CNN/MLP settings do not straightforwardly transfer to fine-tuning pre-trained transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This study provides actionable, empirically-grounded guidelines for practitioners with limited compute who fine-tune small LLMs, showing that simpler training pipelines (stacked over phased, constant LR over cosine decay, no warmup) match or exceed more complex ones. The identification of early training dynamics (gradient norms, loss values) as reliable predictors of final performance offers a practical mechanism for early termination of suboptimal runs, saving significant compute. Key limitations include the restriction to 3B–7B models, a single random seed, two architecture families, and primarily synthetic datasets, leaving open whether these findings generalize to larger models, PEFT methods like LoRA, or diverse real-world instruction data.
