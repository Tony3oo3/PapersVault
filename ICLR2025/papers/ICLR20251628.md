## Problem

Most existing decentralized bilevel optimization (DBO) methods rely on lower-level strong convexity (LLSC) to guarantee unique LL solutions and a well-defined hypergradient for stationarity measurement. Without LLSC, the LL solution becomes set-valued, the LL Hessian loses invertibility, and the standard hypergradient-norm stationarity measure breaks down—especially in decentralized settings where agents must coordinate without a central server and face heterogeneous local data. This paper is the first to tackle DBO without LLSC under decentralized settings with data heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

Each agent in a peer-to-peer network (modeled as an undirected connected graph) receives a local non-i.i.d. dataset and local UL/LL objective functions, communicating only with neighbors via a doubly stochastic consensus matrix. The algorithm outputs UL parameters at network-wide consensus, per-agent LL parameters, and a novel approximate KKT stationarity measure Π that jointly captures UL stationarity, consensus error, LL optimality gap, and dual multiplier error. The convergence rate O(1/T^(1-5p-11τ/4)) (or O(1/T^(1-3p-11τ/4)) under bounded dual variables) is expressed in terms of control parameters p (regularization decay) and τ (LL learning rate decay), with communication complexity O(1/ε^(7/2)) in the general case.

## Method

DUET is a single-loop algorithm that reformulates the bilevel problem as a constrained optimization by replacing the LL subproblem with its stationarity condition (∇_y g = 0), then introduces a diminishing quadratic regularizer μ_t·h(x,y) into the LL objective to restore strong convexity at each iteration while ensuring the augmented problem converges to the original as μ_t→0. Instead of the ill-defined hypergradient norm, DUET uses the KKT residual of the reformulated problem as its stationarity measure, with dual variables v_i updated via projected gradient ascent. For the UL variables, DUET employs gradient tracking (averaging neighbor gradients and correcting with local estimate differences) to handle data heterogeneity without requiring bounded hypergradients or gradient heterogeneity assumptions. The convergence proof hinges on a new descent lemma for the implied UL objective Φ_{μ_t}(x̄_t) that accounts for consensus error, LL/dual approximation errors, and the diminishing regularization schedule, all unified in a Lyapunov function analysis.

## Results

DUET achieves a convergence rate of O(1/T^{2/7}) (i.e., O(1/ε^{7/2}) communication rounds) for approximate KKT-stationary points without any boundedness assumption on dual variables, and improves to O(1/T^{2/5}) (i.e., O(1/ε^{5/2}) rounds) when dual variables are bounded. On the MNIST decentralized meta-learning task with 5 agents, DUET achieves 6% higher test accuracy than the DSGD baseline in the i.i.d. setting. In the non-i.i.d. setting, DUET with gradient tracking (GT=1) significantly outperforms its GT=0 counterpart, confirming the importance of gradient tracking under data heterogeneity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DSGT (decentralized sl-BAMM with gradient tracking), an extension of Liu et al. (2023a) to the decentralized setting that requires an additional UL strong convexity (ULSC) assumption. DUET outperforms DSGT in the non-i.i.d. MNIST meta-learning setting: both DUET and DSGT with gradient tracking (GT=1) significantly outperform their GT=0 counterparts, but DUET's LL objective values (Fig. 1c) indicate it selects LL solutions that better minimize the LL objective, which is more relevant for DBO, whereas DSGT tends to select LL solutions that also yield good UL values due to its ULSC assumption. On the i.i.d. MNIST task, DUET achieves 6% higher test accuracy than the standard DSGD baseline. The key theoretical advantage is that DUET is the first algorithm with provable convergence for DBO without LLSC under non-i.i.d. data, while all prior decentralized methods (INTERACT, DIAMOND, Prometheus, SLDBO, LoPA) require LLSC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KKT-reformulation approach for LLSC-less centralized bilevel optimization (Liu et al., 2023a, sl-BAMM) to the decentralized setting, but replaces the UL+LL objective aggregation with a diminishing quadratic regularizer applied only to the LL objective, which is the key technical departure. It also builds on the single-loop architecture of SOBA (Dagréou et al., 2022) and the network-consensus framework of Nedic & Ozdaglar (2009), adding gradient tracking to handle data heterogeneity. All prior DBO methods (INTERACT, DIAMOND, Prometheus, LoPA) assume LLSC, and all prior LLSC-less bilevel methods are centralized; this paper is the first to bridge both gaps simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that LLSC is not a fundamental requirement for decentralized bilevel optimization, opening a new research direction for multi-agent systems where LL objectives are naturally non-strongly-convex (e.g., LLM pretraining-finetuning, meta-learning). The approximate KKT stationarity measure is general and could serve as a convergence criterion for other DBO algorithms. However, the convergence rates (O(1/ε^{7/2}) general, O(1/ε^{5/2}) with bounded duals) are suboptimal, the LL objective must remain convex, and experimental validation is limited to MNIST with 5 agents, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger networks, non-convex LL problems, or stochastic settings.
