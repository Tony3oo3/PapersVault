## Problem

At low bitrates (≤0.2 bpp), existing image compression methods—whether traditional, distortion-driven neural codecs, or GAN-based perceptual codecs—suffer from severe blurriness, artifacts, and loss of perceptual realism. While diffusion models offer stronger generative priors, prior diffusion-based compression approaches require costly retraining of diffusion components, lack effective integration of semantic information, or fail to achieve precise bitrate allocation, leaving the trade-off between bit rate and perceptual fidelity largely unexplored in the context of pre-trained latent diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input (512×512 crops during training on LSDIR; up to 1024×1024 center-cropped during evaluation) and produces a compressed bitstream consisting of quantized latent codes from the multi-feature compressor plus a losslessly encoded text caption (≤0.0001 bpp overhead). At the decoding end, the bitstream is expanded through a 50-step (configurable down to 5) diffusion sampling process in the Stable Diffusion 2.1-base latent space, yielding a reconstructed RGB image at the same resolution as the input.

## Method

DiffPC is a two-stage compression framework built on Stable Diffusion. In Stage 1, a variational multi-feature compressor (using SCCTX entropy model) encodes multi-scale features (z₀, f₁, f₂) from the SD encoder into a compact latent, trained with an importance-weighted MSE loss that allocates more bits to high-frequency texture regions; a novel IC-ControlNet (ICCN) provides pixel-level structural control to the denoiser, while a Time-Aware Decoupling (TAD) module prevents condition leakage by converting noise prediction into residual noise prediction. In Stage 2, a pre-embedding module (Q-Former from BLIP-diffusion) fuses visual semantics extracted from the degraded reconstruction with BLIP-2 textual captions into a hybrid semantic vector, which is injected via a linear projection and fine-tuned cross-attention layers. A key theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) proves that the KL divergence between the true latent posterior and the compressed-condition posterior is upper-bounded by a simpler variance-weighted MSE, eliminating the need for expensive diffusion sampling during compressor training.

## Results

On the CLIC2020 dataset (FID-based BD-rate), DiffPC (50 steps) achieves a 0% BD-rate as the reference, while the closest competitor DiffEIC requires 10.45% more bits for equivalent FID, TACO requires 62.96% more, MS-ILLM requires 79.83% more, and ELIC requires 4254.39% more. DiffPC consistently outperforms all baselines in perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS, CLIP-IQA) and statistical fidelity (FID, KID) across all tested bit rates on CLIC2020, DIV2K, and Kodak. Even at 5 sampling steps, DiffPC maintains a BD-rate of only 14.59% while reducing decoding time from 7.325 s to 0.886 s per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffPC decisively outperforms all 10 baselines in perceptual and statistical fidelity. Against the rate-distortion optimizer ELIC, DiffPC wins by a massive margin (4254% BD-rate gap in FID), with ELIC failing to even beat the hand-crafted BPG standard on perceptual metrics. Among GAN-based methods, HiFiC (289% BD-rate gap) and MS-ILLM (80% gap) both degrade significantly at ≤0.1 bpp with visible artifacts, while DiffPC maintains clean reconstructions. The closest diffusion-based competitor, DiffEIC, is outperformed by DiffPC by 10.45% BD-rate in FID, with DiffPC additionally achieving lower distortion through its importance-weighted bitrate allocation. CDC (143.54% gap) suffers from color shifts and detail distortions inherent to its DDPM structure, while TACO (62.96% gap), despite also using textual semantics, achieves notably lower perceptual and statistical fidelity at low bit rates. VQGAN-based compression shows an anomalous monotonically increasing rate-distortion curve at boundary bitrates, whereas DiffPC demonstrates robust bitrate generalization. In encoding speed, DiffPC (0.089 s) is competitive with GAN-based methods and faster than DiffEIC (0.430 s) and Perco (≥0.767 s), though its 50-step decoding (7.325 s) is slower than non-diffusion baselines; reducing to 5 steps brings decoding to 0.886 s with only a 14.59% BD-rate penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffPC builds on the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al., 2022) as its generative backbone, repurposing it from text-to-image synthesis into a conditional reconstruction task for image compression. It departs from prior diffusion-based compression methods (CDC, DiffEIC) that either retrained diffusion components from scratch or relied on VQ-based compressors that cannot recover information lost in the LDM encoder; instead, DiffPC operates entirely in the latent space with a multi-feature variational compressor (using ELIC's SCCTX entropy model) and a pre-embedding semantic module (borrowing BLIP-Diffusion's Q-Former). The Time-Aware Decoupling module, adapted from denoising task routing (Park et al., 2023), is specifically designed to address condition leakage—a failure mode unique to compression where aggressive quantization causes the denoiser to copy distorted conditions rather than reconstruct the true image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffPC demonstrates that pre-trained large-scale LDMs can serve as effective compression priors without full retraining, significantly reducing the computational cost compared to methods like Perco that require training on millions of images. The importance-weighted MSE for bitrate allocation and the two-stage training paradigm (low-level control first, then semantic refinement) offer a practical recipe for integrating generative priors into learned compression. However, the approach still struggles with extremely small faces and text, incurs non-trivial decoding latency (~7.3s at 50 steps), and depends on an external captioning model at encoding time—open questions include whether the text dependency can be eliminated and whether the framework extends to video or multi-image compression.
