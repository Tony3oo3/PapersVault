## Problem

Structured fractional minimization problems with composite numerators (differentiable, nonconvex nonsmooth, concave nonsmooth, and convex nonsmooth terms composed with a linear operator) and weakly convex denominators arise in sparse FDA, robust Sharpe ratio, and sparse recovery, yet no ADMM-based algorithm existed for this class. Existing subgradient and smoothing proximal gradient methods suffer from slow convergence and numerical instability, and no iteration complexity guarantees were available for approximate critical points.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a problem instance specifying the component functions f, δ, g, h, the linear operator A, the denominator d, and algorithmic parameters (penalty β⁰, growth rate ξ, exponent p, smoothing ratio χ, and majorization constant θ). Each iteration requires only proximal operator evaluations for δ and h (assumed computable in closed form), making the per-iteration cost independent of problem dimension beyond the matrix-vector product Ax. The output is a sequence of iterates from which an ε-critical point is certified via the Crit value, with the guarantee that some iterate within T = O(1/ε³) steps satisfies the optimality condition.

## Method

FADMM introduces an auxiliary variable y for the constraint Ax = y and applies Nesterov's smoothing to the nonsmooth term h(y), yielding a modified augmented Lagrangian. Two variants handle different denominator structures: FADMM-D uses Dinkelbach's parametric method (requiring d to be weakly convex), while FADMM-Q uses the quadratic transform (requiring √d to be weakly convex). The x-update is solved via majorization-minimization, reducing to a single proximal operator call for δ with a strongly convex quadratic term (θ > 1) that is essential for the convergence proof. The y-update exploits a key lemma showing that Prox of the Nesterov-smoothed function h_μ can be computed from Prox of the original h, avoiding inner loops. A novel Lyapunov function combining the augmented Lagrangian value with time-decaying correction terms establishes global convergence and the O(1/ε³) iteration complexity for reaching an ε-critical point.

## Results

The paper evaluates FADMM-D and FADMM-Q on sparse Fisher discriminant analysis across 8 datasets (Madelon, TDT2, MNIST, Mushroom, Gisette, and two random datasets) with ρ ∈ {10, 100, 1000, 10000}, comparing objective function values after 20 seconds of computation. Both FADMM variants achieve the lowest objective values across all datasets, outperforming SPM, SPGM, and FSA baselines. The theoretical guarantee is an O(1/ε³) iteration complexity to reach an ε-critical point, matching the best known bound for smoothing proximal gradient methods on this problem class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FADMM-D and FADMM-Q against three families of baselines: (1) Subgradient Projection Method (SPM, Li et al. 2021), (2) Smoothing Proximal Gradient Methods (SPGM-D and SPGM-Q, which are obtained by fixing the dual variable z=0 and smoothing parameter μ=0 in FADMM), and (3) Full Splitting Algorithm (FSA-I and FSA-II, Boț et al. 2023b) with two small fixed step sizes. FADMM-D and FADMM-Q consistently achieve the lowest objective values across all 8 datasets and both ρ settings, clearly outperforming SPM, which suffers from slow convergence due to the large subdifferential set at sparse solutions. They also outperform their own SPGM counterparts, confirming the benefit of the primal-dual (ADMM) structure over primal-only updates. Finally, FADMM-D and FADMM-Q still outperform FSA even when FSA uses a sufficiently small step size to guarantee convergence, supporting the paper's claim that the ADMM framework provides superior numerical stability and faster convergence for structured fractional programs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the first ADMM framework for structured fractional minimization, combining three well-established ingredients: Dinkelbach's parametric method (or the quadratic transform) for handling the fractional structure, Nesterov's smoothing (equivalent to the Moreau envelope) for the nonsmooth convex term h(Ax), and majorization-minimization for constructing tractable proximal subproblems. It departs from the dominant algorithmic paradigms for this problem class—subgradient projection methods, smoothing proximal gradient methods, and full splitting algorithms—by recasting the problem as a constrained optimization with a linear coupling constraint, thereby enabling the primal-dual structure of ADMM. The convergence analysis draws on the Lyapunov-function technique developed for nonconvex ADMM (Li & Pong, 2015; Hong et al., 2016; Boț & Nguyen, 2020) but requires new potential-function constructions to handle the fractional objective and the time-varying penalty/smoothing parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1/ε³) iteration complexity is the first such guarantee for ADMM on fractional programs and matches the best known rate for smoothing proximal gradient methods, establishing ADMM as a viable alternative with the practical advantage of primal-dual robustness. The empirical results across sparse FDA, robust Sharpe ratio, and robust sparse recovery confirm that FADMM outperforms SPM, SPGM, and FSA, supporting the broader principle that primal-dual methods are more stable for nonsmooth composite objectives. Open questions include whether the rate can be improved (e.g., via inertial or accelerated variants), whether the framework extends naturally to multi-ratio fractional programs, and whether the KL-inequality-based analysis used by FSA can yield stronger local convergence guarantees for FADMM.
