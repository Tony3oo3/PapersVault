## Problem

While Value Iteration for discounted MDPs enjoys well-understood geometric convergence, the undiscounted average-reward setting has only asymptotic convergence results for multichain MDPs and no non-asymptotic rates on Bellman error. Moreover, no complexity lower bounds existed for value-iteration-type methods in this setting, leaving open whether the known sublinear rates are optimal. This paper fills both gaps by establishing the first non-asymptotic Bellman-error rates for multichain MDPs and the first matching complexity lower bounds.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a finite-state, finite-action MDP (S, A, P, r) with bounded rewards and an initial value function V⁰ ∈ ℝⁿ, and produces theoretical convergence guarantees: O(1/√k) Bellman-error and policy-error rates for Relaxed VI, O(1/k) rates for Anchored VI, and O(1/k) rates for normalized iterates of standard VI. The near-optimal policy π_k is extracted as a greedy policy satisfying T^{π_k}V^k = TV^k at each iterate. The complexity lower bounds are constructed via explicit worst-case MDP instances (with |A|=1) that any method satisfying the span condition must encounter, yielding a lower bound of Ω(1/k) on Bellman error that matches the O(1/k) upper bound up to a constant factor of 8.

## Method

The paper reframes average-reward VI as an inconsistent fixed-point iteration (the Bellman optimality operator is nonexpansive but has no fixed point), then applies Krasnosel'skii-Mann (Rx-VI) and Halpern (Anc-VI) iterations to the Bellman operator. The key technical tool is a telescoping-sum argument using the modified Bellman equations (g*, h*) to sandwich iterates between upper and lower bounds involving products of averaged transition matrices, yielding explicit non-asymptotic rates without requiring aperiodicity. For the lower bounds, the authors construct explicit unichain and multichain MDPs with |A|=1 such that any iterate sequence satisfying the span condition V^{k+1} ∈ V⁰ + span{TV⁰−V⁰, …, TV^k−V^k} incurs a Bellman error of at least (1/(k+1))‖V⁰−h*‖∞ (unichain) or (2/(k+1))‖V⁰−h*‖∞ (multichain), establishing optimality. The analysis specifically exploits the structure of Bellman operators and the ∞-norm geometry of ℝⁿ, since prior inconsistent fixed-point results in Hilbert or uniformly smooth Banach spaces do not apply to the ∞-norm setting.

## Results

The paper establishes the first non-asymptotic convergence rates for value-iteration-type methods on multichain average-reward MDPs. Specifically, Relaxed Value Iteration (Rx-VI) achieves an O(1/√k) rate on the Bellman error (Theorem 1), while Anchored Value Iteration (Anc-VI) improves this to O(1/k) (Theorem 2, Corollary 2: ‖TV^k − V^k − g*‖∞ ≤ 8/(k+1) ‖V⁰ − h*‖∞). A matching complexity lower bound of Ω(1/k) (Theorem 3) confirms optimality up to a constant factor of 8 for weakly communicating and unichain MDPs. For standard VI, the O(2/k) rate on normalized iterates (Fact 1) is shown to be exactly optimal via a matching lower bound (Theorem 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main advance is providing the first non-asymptotic convergence rates for multichain MDPs, a setting where all prior work (Federgruen et al. 1978; Van Der Wal 1981; Schweitzer & Federgruen 1977, 1979; Bertsekas 1998) offered only asymptotic results or was restricted to unichain/weakly communicating MDPs. For weakly communicating and unichain MDPs, the O(1/k) upper bound of Anc-VI (Corollary 2) is matched by the Ω(1/k) lower bound (Theorem 3) up to a constant factor of 8, establishing near-optimal complexity — a result no prior work achieved. For standard VI on normalized iterates, the paper proves exact optimality: the classical O(2/k) rate from Puterman (2014, Theorem 9.4.1) is tight via the matching lower bound in Theorem 4. For RVI variants, the paper extends non-asymptotic rates from unichain (Bravo & Cominetti 2024; Bravo & Contreras 2024; Bertsekas 2012) to the broader weakly communicating class (Theorems 5–6), with Anc-RVI achieving the same O(1/k) Bellman error rate. The anchoring mechanism (Halpern-type iteration) is the key technical ingredient that upgrades the O(1/√k) Krasnosel'skii–Mann rate to O(1/k), mirroring the known acceleration in fixed-point theory (Sabach & Shtern 2017; Park & Ryu 2022) but adapted to the inconsistent (no-fixed-point) Bellman operator setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Krasnosel'skii-Mann and Halpern fixed-point iteration frameworks to the average-reward MDP setting, where the Bellman optimality operator is nonexpansive but lacks a fixed point (the 'inconsistent' setup). Prior convergence analyses for average-reward MDPs were limited to asymptotic rates (Schweitzer & Federgruen 1977, 1979) or restricted to unichain MDPs (Federgruen et al. 1978, Bertsekas 1998), and the inconsistent fixed-point literature (Park & Ryu 2023) does not directly apply because R^n with the ∞-norm is neither Hilbert nor uniformly convex. The paper explicitly adapts proof techniques from Cominetti et al. (2014) and Contreras & Cominetti (2022) to exploit the specific structure of Bellman operators and modified Bellman equations, and introduces the first complexity lower bounds for this setting using the span condition from information-based complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the 'correct' convergence rates for undiscounted average-reward MDPs are sublinear O(1/k), fundamentally contrasting with the geometric O(γ^k) rates familiar from the discounted setting, and provides the first non-asymptotic guarantees for the general multichain case. The matching of the O(1/k) upper bound (Anc-VI) with a complexity lower bound up to a constant factor of 8, and the exact optimality of standard VI for normalized iterates, closes a long-standing gap in the theory. A key open question remains: the multichain Bellman-error upper bound (Theorem 2) has a K-dependent term that does not exactly match the lower bound (Theorem 4), leaving the precise optimal complexity for general multichain MDPs unresolved. The results are purely theoretical for finite-state tabular MDPs, so extension to function-approximation or sample-based settings is left for future work.
