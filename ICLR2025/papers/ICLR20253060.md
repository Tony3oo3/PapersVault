## Problem

Self-supervised denoising methods face a robustness-expressivity trade-off: SURE-based losses achieve near-supervised performance but require exact knowledge of the noise level, while cross-validation methods (Noise2Void, Noise2Self) are robust to unknown noise but impose overly restrictive zero-derivative constraints that discard useful information. This paper characterizes the trade-off through the constraints on estimator derivatives and proposes UNSURE, which requires only that the expected divergence be zero, achieving performance close to SURE without needing the noise level.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only noisy measurements—single noisy images, k-space samples, tomography projections, or Cryo-EM frames—without any clean reference. For spatially correlated Gaussian noise, it requires a bound on the correlation support (e.g., kernel size) but not the exact covariance. For incomplete inverse problems (m < n), it combines the UNSURE loss with an equivariant imaging (EI) loss to learn in the nullspace of the forward operator. The output is a denoised or reconstructed image at the same spatial resolution as the input.

## Method

UNSURE formulates self-supervised denoising as a min-max problem: minimize the data-consistency term ‖f(y)−y‖² subject to the constraint that the expected divergence of f is zero, enforced via a Lagrange multiplier η. The closed-form solution is f(y) = y + η̂∇log p_y(y), where η̂ = (1/n · E‖∇log p_y‖²)⁻¹ acts as a conservative estimate of the noise variance. In practice, the divergence is estimated via a Monte Carlo approximation (two forward passes with a small perturbation), and training alternates gradient descent on network weights θ with gradient ascent on η. Two variants are offered: direct Lagrange optimization of a U-Net, and a score-matching approach (AR-DAE loss) that learns ∇log p_y and applies the closed-form formula at inference. The framework generalizes to correlated Gaussian noise (circulant covariance parameterization), Poisson-Gaussian noise (two Lagrange multipliers η and γ), exponential-family distributions, and general linear inverse problems via a pseudoinverse correction.

## Results

On correlated Gaussian denoising (DIV2K), UNSURE with unknown noise covariance achieves 28.72±1.03 dB PSNR, within ~1 dB of SURE with known covariance (29.77±1.22 dB) and supervised learning (29.91±1.26 dB), while significantly outperforming Neighbor2Neighbor (23.61 dB) and Noise2Void (19.09 dB). On accelerated MRI (FastMRI, σ=0.03), UNSURE+EI reaches 35.73±1.45 dB, closely matching supervised (36.63±1.38 dB) and dramatically outperforming SURE+EI with a slightly wrong noise level (28.05±4.73 dB). On CT with Poisson-Gaussian noise (LIDC), PG-UNSURE achieves 33.31±0.57 dB, close to PG-SURE with known parameters (33.76±0.61 dB) and well above Noise2Inverse (32.54±0.71 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNSURE consistently outperforms all cross-validation-based baselines: on DIV2K correlated noise it beats Neighbor2Neighbor by ~5 dB (28.72 vs 23.61) and Noise2Void by ~10 dB (28.72 vs 19.09); on MRI it beats CV+EI (SSDU) by 2.5 dB (35.73 vs 33.25); on CT it beats Noise2Inverse by 0.8 dB (33.31 vs 32.54). Against SURE-based methods, UNSURE's key advantage is robustness: when the noise level is slightly misspecified (σ=0.05 vs true 0.03 on MRI), SURE+EI collapses to 28.05 dB while UNSURE+EI maintains 35.73 dB; when noise is known, SURE retains a small edge (~0.5–1 dB). On MNIST, UNSURE outperforms Noise2Score and R2R across all noise levels while remaining within ~0.5 dB of supervised. The EI-only baseline (Chen et al., 2021, assuming zero noise) is outperformed by UNSURE+EI by 1.4 dB on MRI, confirming that explicit noise handling matters even with equivariance. On real Cryo-EM data (Appendix E), UNSURE outperforms the Noise2Noise approach from Bepler et al. (2020) in the absence of any noise model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established families of self-supervised denoising: SURE-based methods (Metzler et al. 2020; Le Montagner et al. 2014) that require full knowledge of the noise distribution, and cross-validation methods (Noise2Void, Noise2Self, Neighbor2Neighbor) that need only pixel-wise independence but sacrifice expressivity. The key conceptual departure is reframing the expressivity-robustness trade-off as a spectrum of constraints on the estimator's derivatives: SURE imposes no derivative constraints, cross-validation imposes pointwise zero-derivative constraints, and the proposed UNSURE relaxes this to zero *expected* divergence, yielding a Lagrange-multiplier formulation with a closed-form solution in terms of the score function. It also builds on equivariant imaging (Chen et al. 2021) for nullspace learning in incomplete problems and AR-DAE (Lim et al. 2020) for score-based training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical unification that explains *why* cross-validation methods are suboptimal and *why* SURE is brittle, and identifies the minimal constraint (zero expected divergence) that bridges the gap. Practically, this enables self-supervised reconstruction in settings where the noise level or spatial correlation is genuinely unknown—common in medical and scientific imaging—without the ~7 dB collapse seen when SURE is given a slightly wrong σ. Limitations include restriction to L2 loss (no perceptual objectives), the need for an extra forward pass during training, and failure on high-entropy signal distributions. Open questions include extending the framework to non-separable noise, non-Gaussian exponential families beyond the Hudson subset, and whether the ZED constraint interacts favorably with diffusion-based or generative priors.
