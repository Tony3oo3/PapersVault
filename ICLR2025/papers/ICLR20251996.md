## Problem

Existing path-based explainers for knowledge graph completion identify only single reasoning chains or disconnected facts, yet complex predictions (e.g., drug interactions, competitive relationships) are determined by the synergy of multiple paths working together. Moreover, no quantitative evaluation framework exists to assess explanation quality because ground-truth explanations are unavailable, leaving prior work reliant on model faithfulness or small-scale manual verification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251996/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a pre-trained KGE model (e.g., RotatE), a knowledge graph, and a target triple ⟨h, r, ?⟩, KGExplainer extracts a k-hop enclosing subgraph around the link and searches within it for key entities via perturbation. The output is a connected subgraph of multiple synergistic paths (the explanation) plus a fidelity score computed by a distilled R-GAT evaluator that verifies whether the explanation subgraph alone preserves the original prediction ranking. The sparsity of the explanation is user-controllable via the top-n entities selected per hop.

## Method

KGExplainer is a post-hoc, model-agnostic framework with three components. First, a perturbation-based greedy search identifies key entities hop-by-hop: at each hop, every neighbor is removed from the enclosing subgraph, the KGE is retrained on the perturbed subgraph (using a subgraph-based retraining strategy that fixes relation embeddings and extended-neighbor embeddings to preserve the representation space), and the resulting score change ranks entity importance. Second, the top-n entities per hop are connected to form a set of synergistic paths constituting a connected explanation subgraph. Third, an R-GAT evaluator is distilled from the target KGE using score-alignment loss plus a pairwise margin loss, enabling it to score arbitrary subgraphs and quantify explanation fidelity via Recall@N and F1@N metrics without requiring ground-truth explanations.

## Results

KGExplainer achieves F1@1 scores of 85.21% (OGB-biokg), 30.47% (Family-rr), and 84.29% (FB15k-237), outperforming the best baseline by 16.92%, 7.74%, and 13.54% respectively. On Recall@1, the gains are 12.08%, 4.77%, and 4.19% over the strongest baseline. In a human evaluation on Family-rr, 83.3% (65/78) of respondents selected KGExplainer's explanations as the best among four methods. The distilled evaluator (KGExp-RotatE) also matches or exceeds the target KGE's predictive performance (e.g., 17.33% vs. 17.12% Hits@1 on OGB-biokg), confirming faithfulness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KGExplainer outperforms all five baselines on every dataset for both F1@1 and Recall@1. Against the subgraph-based methods CRIAGE and Kelpie, KGExplainer gains 16–35 percentage points in F1@1, confirming that connected synergistic paths are far more informative than isolated discrete facts. Among path-based baselines, PaGE-Link is the strongest competitor (68.29% F1@1 on OGB-biokg), yet KGExp-RotatE still leads by 16.92 points, demonstrating that multiple synergistic paths capture richer reasoning than a single path. DRUM and ELEP, which rely on reinforcement learning or rule mining for single-path explanations, trail KGExplainer by 12–22 points on OGB-biokg and FB15k-237. The ablation KGExp-Rand (randomly sampled connected subgraphs) scores 54.32% on OGB-biokg, well below the greedy-search variants, validating that the perturbation-based entity selection is critical rather than merely the connected-subgraph structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KGC explainability literature by moving from single-path or disconnected-fact explanations (DRUM, ELEP, PaGE-Link, CRIAGE, Kelpie) to synergistic path-based explanations that form connected subgraphs. It builds on GraIL's enclosing subgraph formulation for local context extraction and on knowledge distillation to create a subgraph-level evaluator (R-GAT) that can quantitatively assess explanation fidelity without ground truth. The perturbation-based greedy search with hop-by-hop entity importance scoring is a novel mechanism that departs from reinforcement learning (ELEP, PGPR) and GNN-based pruning (PaGE-Link) approaches for path discovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in deploying KGE-based KGC models in risk-sensitive domains (drug repurposing, recommendation) where black-box predictions are insufficient. The distilled evaluator provides a quantitative, ground-truth-free evaluation methodology for explanations that is broadly applicable. Limitations include the greedy search being approximate (not guaranteed optimal), the small human evaluation sample (20 links, 78 responses), and the fact that the synergistic path concept, while well-motivated, is validated primarily on relatively small KGs (Family-rr, FB15k-237) with OGB-biokg as the sole large-scale test. Open questions include whether the approach scales to KGs with millions of entities and whether the information-theoretic synergy formalization in Appendix A.4 can be made computationally tractable.
