## Problem

Standard off-policy deep RL methods rely on state-action-value (Q) functions as critics, which become intractable in high-dimensional action spaces due to the curse of dimensionality. Existing V-function-based off-policy corrections like V-trace were designed for mildly off-policy (asynchronous on-policy) settings and degrade significantly when the behavior and target policies diverge substantially, as in a fully off-policy replay-buffer setup. The paper asks whether a purely V-function-based off-policy method can be made stable and competitive, particularly for high-dimensional continuous control.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a continuous state observation vector from the environment and outputs a continuous action sampled from a Gaussian policy whose mean and covariance are projected into a per-state trust region by the TRPL layer. Twin critic networks each produce a scalar state-value estimate V(s) that is used to compute one-step advantage estimates A = r + γV(s') − V(s) for the policy gradient. The replay buffer stores tuples (s, a, r, s', log π_b(a|s)), where the stored behavior-policy log-probability enables importance sampling at training time without re-evaluating old policies.

## Method

Vlearn replaces the Q-function critic with twin V-function networks trained via a weighted importance sampling (WIS) loss that multiplies the importance ratio ρ = π(a|s)/π_b(a|s) in front of the entire squared Bellman error, rather than only inside the Bellman target as in V-trace. This objective is proven to be a Jensen-inequality upper bound on the naive importance-weighted Bellman loss while sharing the same minimizer, and in the bandit setting it yields the self-normalized importance sampling estimator, which has provably lower variance than V-trace's squared self-normalized estimator. For policy updates, Vlearn uses the TRPL differentiable trust-region layer to project the Gaussian policy parameters into a per-state trust region, combined with delayed policy updates, tanh squashing, advantage normalization, and importance-weight clipping at ε_ρ = 1. The combination of the WIS critic loss with TRPL and these stabilizing components is shown empirically to be essential: removing importance sampling, replacing TRPL with PPO clipping, or dropping the twin critics all lead to unstable or failed learning.

## Results

On the 17-dimensional Humanoid-v4 task, Vlearn achieves a 25% improvement in asymptotic return over SAC, the strongest Q-function-based baseline. On the 38-dimensional DMC dog locomotion tasks, Vlearn is the only method (alongside on-policy TRPL) that reliably learns all three movement types, while SAC and MPO fail to produce consistent policies. On the 39-dimensional MyoHand suite, Vlearn achieves the highest aggregated performance across all 10 tasks. On low-dimensional Gymnasium tasks, Vlearn matches MPO and on-policy methods on Hopper-v4 and Walker2D-v4, though SAC retains an edge on HalfCheetah-v4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), Vlearn wins decisively on all high-dimensional tasks: Ant-v4 (8-dim), Humanoid-v4 (17-dim, +25% return), DMC Dog (38-dim, where SAC's convergence is highly unstable), and MyoHand (39-dim, where Vlearn achieves the highest aggregated score). SAC retains an advantage only on HalfCheetah-v4, where Vlearn converges more slowly and reaches a lower local optimum. Against MPO (Abdolmaleki et al., 2018), Vlearn outperforms on all high-dimensional tasks where MPO is unable to achieve competitive performance, while the two methods perform comparably on low-dimensional Hopper-v4 and Walker2D-v4. Against on-policy PPO and TRPL, Vlearn consistently achieves higher returns on all tasks, demonstrating the benefit of off-policy data reuse. Against V-trace (Espeholt et al., 2018) with the same architecture and hyperparameters, Vlearn wins on every task; V-trace shows minimal learning within the evaluation budget on most tasks and completely fails on the DMC dog tasks, falling behind even on-policy baselines. The ablation in Figure 4 confirms that removing importance sampling entirely prevents learning, replacing TRPL with PPO's clipped loss destabilizes training on Ant-v4, and removing twin critics degrades performance in both environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vlearn extends the linear-domain insight from Mahmood et al. (2014) and Dann et al. (2014) that weighting the entire Bellman error (weighted importance sampling) outperforms weighting only the Bellman target, by deriving a Jensen's-inequality upper bound that makes this objective tractable for deep function approximation. It departs from V-trace (Espeholt et al., 2018), the closest prior V-function method, by relocating importance weights from the Bellman target to the loss itself, avoiding V-trace's interpolation toward the target network that degrades learning in fully off-policy settings. For policy updates, it adopts TRPL (Otto et al., 2021) rather than PPO's clipped surrogate, arguing that exact per-state trust regions are necessary when the behavior policy can be arbitrarily far from the current policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the state-action-value function, Vlearn sidesteps the curse of dimensionality that causes standard off-policy actor-critic methods (SAC, MPO) to fail on 38-dimensional DMC dog locomotion and 39-dimensional MyoHand tasks, offering a practical path for overactuated and musculoskeletal control. The paper acknowledges that sample efficiency remains a challenge and that the method still requires substantial interaction data. Open directions include extending the approach to offline RL, incorporating Q-function ideas such as ensembles or distributional critics into the V-function-only setting, and further stabilizing off-policy V-function learning for even higher-dimensional problems.
