## Problem

Existing dimensionality reduction for sparse vectors either suffers an exponential dependence on p in the embedding dimension (worst-case guarantees) or achieves only the O(s²) birthday-paradox bound under average-case guarantees. The paper asks whether the O(s²) bound is optimal for general sparse vectors, and whether additional structure (non-negativity) can enable substantially smaller embeddings in general ℓ_p norms, including ℓ_∞ where all prior bounds become vacuous.

## Input / Output

The upper bound takes a dataset X of n non-negative s-sparse vectors in R^d and produces a non-linear, oblivious embedding F: R^d → R^{mT} with m = O(s/ε) and T = O(log(ns)/ε), preserving all pairwise ℓ_p distances up to 1±ε for any p ≥ 1 with no dependence on p. For ℓ_∞, the embedding achieves exact distance preservation with m = O(s) and T = O(log n). The lower bounds consider s-sparse vectors in R^{s²} and show that any linear or sufficiently smooth map preserving norms for 99% of vectors requires Ω(s²) dimensions. The embedding is non-smooth (uses max-pooling) and the non-negativity of the input is essential—both are shown to be necessary via separation theorems.

## Method

The paper contributes two complementary results. For lower bounds, it constructs a distribution over sparse vectors (random support with Gaussian entries) and shows via rank arguments on A^T A that any linear or twice-differentiable map preserving ℓ_2 norms for 99% of such vectors must map to Ω(s²) dimensions; the key step uses the Carbery-Wright anti-concentration inequality to show a degree-2 polynomial in the vector entries cannot concentrate in a small interval. For the upper bound on non-negative sparse vectors, the core construction is a non-linear max-pooling map: coordinates are randomly hashed into O(s) buckets, and each bucket outputs the maximum (not sum) of the values landing in it. This max operation makes the map deterministically non-expanding for non-negative vectors, a property that fails for arbitrary sparse vectors. The final embedding stacks O(log n) independent copies of this base map, so that with high probability every pair has at least one copy that certifies the distance while no copy ever overestimates it, yielding the 1±ε guarantee.

## Results

For a dataset of n non-negative s-sparse vectors, the paper achieves a 1±ε embedding in O(s·log(ns)/ε²) dimensions for any ℓ_p norm (p≥1), with no dependence on p. For the ℓ_∞ norm specifically, exact distance preservation is achieved in O(s·log n) dimensions. The paper also proves matching lower bounds: any linear or sufficiently smooth map preserving 99% of norms for general (possibly signed) s-sparse vectors requires Ω(s²) dimensions, confirming the birthday paradox map is optimal in that setting. Experimentally (Figure 1), on 10-sparse vectors in R^1000 embedded to R^50, the proposed max-pooling map hugs the y=x line for ℓ_∞ norm approximation while the Zhu et al. (2015) map shows significant distortion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Zhu et al. (2015), the primary prior work on RIP for general p-norms, the new method eliminates the exponential (log n)^O(p) dependence on p entirely, replacing it with a p-independent O(s·log(n)/ε²) bound. This is a dramatic improvement for large p (e.g., ℓ_∞), where prior bounds become vacuous. Against Candes & Tao (2005) and Berinde et al. (2008), which achieve O(s·log(d/s)/ε²) for p∈{1,2}, the new method gives a comparable O(s·log(n)/ε²) bound (with log(n) vs. log(d/s) being comparable when n ≤ d) but extends to all p ≥ 1 with a single map. The paper also proves that the folklore birthday paradox map (O(s²) dimensions) is information-theoretically optimal for general sparse vectors under average-case guarantees, establishing that the non-negativity assumption is essential for the improved O(s·log(n)/ε²) bound. The non-linearity of the map is also shown to be necessary: any linear map requires Ω(d) dimensions even for O(1)-sparse non-negative vectors to preserve ℓ_∞ norms up to constant distortion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Johnson-Lindenstrauss dimensionality reduction framework to the structured setting of sparse vectors, building on the rank-based lower bound techniques of Alon (2003) but adapting them to a fundamentally weaker average-case hypothesis (preserving norms for 99% of vectors rather than all pairs). It departs from prior sparse-vector embedding work (Zhu et al. 2015, Candes & Tao 2005) by introducing non-negativity as a structural assumption and by using a non-linear max-pooling map rather than linear projections, thereby bypassing the exponential-in-p dependence that plagues all known linear RIP-based embeddings. The paper also draws a sharp conceptual boundary with compressed sensing: while RIP matrices with non-smooth decoders achieve O(s log d) dimensions, the authors prove that any sufficiently smooth encoder-decoder scheme is bottlenecked at Ω(s²) dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-negative sparse vector embedding (O(s log n) for ℓ_∞, O(s log(ns)/ε²) for general ℓ_p) is practically relevant to ML settings where data is naturally non-negative and sparse, such as ReLU activations, one-hot encodings, and recommendation features, and it enables downstream algorithmic speedups for diameter, max-cut, and k-clustering. The separation results showing that both non-linearity and non-negativity are necessary (linear maps or arbitrary sparse vectors both require Ω(d) dimensions) provide a clean theoretical characterization of when dimensionality reduction can beat the birthday paradox bound. Open questions include the tight ε-dependence for the non-negative case, whether the average-case lower bound extends to arbitrary (non-smooth) maps, and whether similar non-negativity-driven separations exist in other structured settings.
