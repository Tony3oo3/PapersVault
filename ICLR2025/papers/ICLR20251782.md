## Problem

Most open-source LLMs are pre-trained on general internet data without math-specific optimization and struggle with complex multi-step quantitative reasoning. Existing approaches either rely on external Python tools, use only supervised fine-tuning (susceptible to hallucinated information from teacher models), or lack a unified framework that combines instruction evolution with process-level reinforcement learning to address false positives in mathematical problem-solving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251782/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a math word problem as text input and produces a step-by-step chain-of-thought solution ending with a final numerical answer, all without external Python tools. At inference time, the model generates N candidate reasoning paths (up to 256) and aggregates them using a PRM-weighted majority vote to select the final answer. The evolved training data spans grade-school to high-school difficulty, with instructions generated through 5 rounds of progressive evolution from GSM8k and MATH seed problems.

## Method

WizardMath introduces RLEIF (Reinforcement Learning from Evol-Instruct Feedback), a fully AI-powered pipeline with three stages. First, Math Evol-Instruct uses GPT-4 to generate 418k diverse instructions via two evolution lines: downward evolution (simplifying problems for grade-school level) and upward evolution (adding constraints, concretizing, and increasing reasoning steps for high-school level). Second, two reward models are trained with GPT-4-generated labels: an Instruction Reward Model (IRM) that scores evolved instruction quality on difficulty and definition clarity, and a Process-supervised Reward Model (PRM) that assigns correctness scores to each individual reasoning step. Third, step-by-step PPO optimizes the LLM using a composite reward that is the product of the IRM instruction score and the minimum PRM step score, ensuring both instruction quality and step-level correctness are reinforced. This design specifically targets the false-positive problem where incorrect intermediate reasoning accidentally yields a correct final answer, which outcome-only supervision cannot detect.

## Results

On GSM8k, WizardMath-Llama-70B achieves 92.8% CoT pass@1, surpassing GPT-3.5-Turbo (81.6%), Claude 2 (85.2%), and Gemini Pro (86.5%), while WizardMath-Mistral-7B reaches 90.7%. On the harder MATH benchmark, WizardMath-Llama-70B scores 58.6%, outperforming GPT-4-original (42.5%), Claude 2 (32.5%), and Gemini Pro (32.6%), and WizardMath-Mistral-7B achieves 55.4%, exceeding GPT-4-0314 (52.6%). The latest WizardMath-Qwen-7B (Qwen2.5-Math base) reaches 77.8% on MATH, surpassing GPT-4-2024-0513 (76.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WizardMath-Llama-70B outperforms MetaMath-70B by 10.5 points on GSM8k (92.8 vs. 82.3) and by 32.0 points on MATH (58.6 vs. 26.6), and beats Xwin-Math-70B by 2.2 and 5.8 points respectively. WizardMath-Mistral-7B surpasses MetaMath-Mistral-7B by 12.8 points on GSM8k (90.7 vs. 77.9) and 26.8 points on MATH (55.4 vs. 28.6), and edges out JiuZhang3.0 (88.6/52.8) and Xwin-Math (89.2/43.7) on both benchmarks. Against proprietary models, WizardMath-Llama-70B exceeds GPT-3.5-Turbo by 11.2 points on GSM8k and 15.5 on MATH, and beats Claude 2, Gemini Pro, and PaLM-2 on both benchmarks, though it trails GPT-4-0314 slightly on GSM8k (92.8 vs. 94.7) while leading on MATH (58.6 vs. 52.6). WizardMath-Mistral-7B surpasses GPT-4-0314 on MATH (55.4 vs. 52.6), and the newer WizardMath-Qwen-7B (Qwen2.5-Math base) exceeds GPT-4-2024-0513 on MATH (77.8 vs. 76.6). On out-of-domain benchmarks (Table 7), WizardMath-Mistral-7B achieves 45.7% average across 7 tasks, outperforming GPT-3.5-Turbo (37.9%) and approaching GPT-4 (49.5%), while beating MetaMath-Mistral (30.9%) and MathScale-Mistral (37.9%) by 14.8 and 7.8 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the Evol-Instruct data synthesis framework from WizardLM/WizardCoder (which evolved instructions to increase complexity) and the process-supervised reinforcement learning paradigm from Lightman et al. (2023) that rewards individual reasoning steps rather than final answers. The key conceptual departure is introducing a *downward* evolution line (making problems easier) alongside the standard upward evolution, and coupling both with a dual-reward RL pipeline (IRM for instruction quality + PRM for step correctness) that was not present in either parent line. Unlike prior math-specific approaches (MetaMath, Xwin-Math, MAmmoTH) that focus on data augmentation or hybrid CoT/PoT, RLEIF positions the entire training loop—data generation, reward modeling, and PPO optimization—as a fully AI-powered pipeline where GPT-4 serves as both the evolution engine and the process-labeling oracle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: RLEIF provides a reproducible recipe for turning any open-source base model (GPT-2 through Qwen2.5) into a competitive math reasoner without external Python tools, and the finding that GPT-4-generated step-level labels outperform both human-annotated PRM800k and MCTS-based Math-Shepherd annotations challenges the prevailing assumption that high-quality process supervision requires expensive human labor. Limitations include the heavy dependence on GPT-4 for both data evolution and reward-model training (raising cost and reproducibility concerns), the fact that the 70B model still trails GPT-4o on MATH (58.6 vs 76.6), and the absence of analysis on whether the method transfers to non-mathematical reasoning domains. Open questions include whether the downward evolution component is essential or merely a data-diversity trick, and whether the IRM's contribution (2.5–4% gain) justifies its additional training cost at scale.
