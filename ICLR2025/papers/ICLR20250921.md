## Problem

No exact robustness certificates exist for label flipping (poisoning) of neural networks, and the problem is entirely unsolved for Graph Neural Networks. Existing poisoning certificates rely on randomized smoothing or partition-based aggregation, which are inherently incomplete and cannot characterize how specific architectural choices affect worst-case robustness. The paper asks whether exact certificates against label poisoning are computable at all, and resolves this affirmatively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250921/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph with node features, a set of m labeled training nodes, a perturbation budget ε (fraction of labels an adversary may flip), and the GNN architecture specification needed to compute its NTK matrix Q. It produces either a per-node binary robustness verdict (sample-wise certificate) or the exact maximum number of test nodes simultaneously flippable by a single label perturbation (collective certificate). The MILP is solved to optimality via Gurobi, and the certificate holds exactly for infinite-width networks and with high probability (1 − exp(−Ω(w))) for finite-width w networks.

## Method

The core insight is that a sufficiently wide GNN trained with soft-margin loss is exactly equivalent to an SVM using the GNN's Neural Tangent Kernel as its kernel, reducing the poisoning problem to a bilevel optimization over SVM dual variables and perturbed labels. The inner SVM dual problem is convex and satisfies Slater's condition, so its KKT conditions (stationarity, feasibility, complementary slackness) replace the inner optimization, yielding a single-level nonlinear program. All remaining nonlinearities—products of binary label variables with continuous dual variables in the objective and stationarity constraints, and bilinear complementary slackness terms—are then linearized exactly using big-M formulations with tight, problem-specific M values derived from the NTK bounds. The result is an exact Mixed-Integer Linear Program (Theorem 1 for sample-wise, Theorem 2 for collective certification) whose optimal solution directly gives the worst-case robustness. The collective certificate additionally introduces binary indicator variables per test node to count simultaneously flippable predictions under a single shared perturbation, capturing the more realistic attack model where the adversary modifies the training set only once.

## Results

Using the exact collective certificate, the paper reports certified ratios (fraction of test nodes provably robust to label flipping) across 8 GNN architectures on 3 datasets at perturbation budgets ε ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15}. On Cora-MLb at ε=0.05, GCN achieves 86.4±6.1% certified ratio, with APPNP (+3.2%) and SGC (+2.4%) slightly outperforming it, while MLP drops to 66.0% (−20.4%). On CSBM at ε=0.15, SGC is most robust at 82.3% (+34.3% over GCN's 48.0%), and on Citeseerb at ε=0.05, GCN Skip-PC leads at 81.2% (+15.6% over GCN's 65.6%). A key empirical finding is a 'robustness plateauing' phenomenon: certified ratios remain nearly constant for intermediate budgets (ε ∈ [0.15, 0.3]) across most architectures and datasets, e.g., GCN on Cora-MLb stays at 43.6% from ε=0.20 to ε=0.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper uses GCN as the reference baseline and reports all other architectures' certified ratios as absolute changes relative to GCN. SGC (linear activation replacing ReLU) is consistently more robust than GCN across all datasets, with the largest margin on CSBM (+34.3% at ε=0.15), confirming that linear activations improve certifiable robustness. GCN Skip-PC (personalized PageRank skip-connections) is the most robust architecture on Citeseerb (+15.6% at ε=0.05) and CSBM (+20.1% at ε=0.15), outperforming all other GNNs in those settings. APPNP is most robust on Cora-MLb at ε=0.05 (+3.2%) but its advantage diminishes at higher budgets. GraphSAGE and GIN show mixed results, sometimes outperforming GCN (GraphSAGE +9.6% on Citeseerb at ε=0.05) and sometimes underperforming. MLP is uniformly the least robust architecture across all datasets and budgets, losing 6.8–20.4% certified ratio compared to GCN, demonstrating that graph structure consistently improves worst-case robustness to label poisoning. The robustness hierarchy is strongly data-dependent: no single architecture dominates across all datasets, with SGC winning on CSBM, GCN Skip-PC on Citeseerb, and APPNP on Cora-MLb.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK-based certification framework of Gosch et al. (2024), which first used the Neural Tangent Kernel to derive model-specific poisoning certificates for wide networks, but was limited to feature perturbations and produced only incomplete sample-wise guarantees. The key technical departure is the reformulation of the label-flipping bilevel problem into an exact Mixed-Integer Linear Program via KKT conditions and big-M linearization of bilinear terms, which is possible because label flipping introduces binary (discrete) variables that admit exact linear modeling, unlike the continuous feature perturbations in prior work. The approach also builds on the established equivalence between infinite-width NNs trained with soft-margin loss and SVMs (Chen et al., 2021) and on NTK analyses of GNN architectures (Sabanayagam et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first exact robustness certificate for any poisoning attack on neural networks, providing a rigorous tool to compare worst-case robustness of architectures in a way that incomplete certificates (randomized smoothing, partition aggregation) cannot. The discovery of a robustness plateau at intermediate perturbation budgets — unobserved in evasion-attack certification — opens a new line of inquiry into the structure of the label-flipping attack landscape. Practical limitations are significant: the MILP is NP-hard and scales only to graphs of Cora-ML/Citeseer size (~2000 nodes), and the certificates hold for infinite-width networks (with high-probability guarantees for finite width). The cause of the plateauing remains unexplained, and extending exact certification to larger graphs or to feature+label joint poisoning are natural open directions.
