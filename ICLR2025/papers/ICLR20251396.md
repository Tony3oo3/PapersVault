## Problem

Adapting CLIP to video action recognition requires addressing two coupled challenges that prior work has treated in isolation: effective temporal modeling that avoids spatiotemporal homogenization and limited receptive fields, and shifting the text branch's focus from static object-noun alignment to dynamic action-verb alignment. The paper argues that both branches must be adapted simultaneously to close the gap between CLIP's image-domain pretraining and the demands of video action understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251396/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips of T frames (8 or 16 in fully-supervised settings, 32 in zero/few-shot) at 224×224 resolution, processed through a ViT image encoder with patch sizes of 14, 16, or 32, followed by a Kronecker mask temporal transformer. The text side accepts multiple diverse descriptions per action category (original verbs/phrases, template-filled sentences, and LLM-generated interpretive prompts up to 76 words each), all encoded by the CLIP text encoder. Output is a cosine similarity score between the pooled video representation (mean of class tokens across frames) and each text representation, aggregated across all text descriptions for the final classification score.

## Method

CLAVER employs a factorized video encoder: a frozen or fine-tuned CLIP ViT image encoder extracts per-frame spatial features, which are then processed by a lightweight Kronecker Mask Temporal (KMT) transformer (1/3 the depth of the image encoder) that performs temporal modeling. The key mechanism is the Kronecker mask M = [I_T ⊗ (J_L − I_L)]_{1=−∞}, which allows each token at time t to attend to all spatial tokens at every other time step while blocking same-frame interactions, thereby expanding the temporal receptive field to the full video length while preserving spatiotemporal structural heterogeneity. A causal variant (KMCTA) adds an upper-triangular temporal mask, guaranteeing full-rank attention matrices and avoiding the low-rank bottleneck that degrades performance with longer sequences. On the text side, interpretive prompts generated by ChatGPT and LLaMA-3 provide sentence-level action descriptions through three strategies—action decomposition, synonym conversion, and body-part involvement—shifting the model's alignment target from concrete nouns to abstract verbs. Training uses a contrastive InfoNCE loss over batches of videos and their multiple text descriptions, with the KMT transformer initialized from scratch at 10× the base learning rate.

## Results

On Kinetics-400 (fully-supervised, 16 frames), CLAVER-B/16 (KMCT) achieves 86.0% Top-1 and 97.2% Top-5 accuracy, outperforming ILA-B/16 (85.0%), ASU-B/16 (84.8%), and X-CLIP-B/16 (84.3%). At the larger L/14 (336↑) scale, CLAVER-L/14 (KMT) reaches 88.8% Top-1, surpassing ILA-L/14 (88.1%) and ASU-L/14 (88.3%). In zero-shot evaluation on Kinetics-600, CLAVER (KMT) achieves 73.8% Top-1, exceeding OST (70.5%) and MAXI (70.9%) by over 3 points. On zero-shot HMDB-51 and UCF-101, CLAVER (KMT) scores 54.1% and 78.6% respectively, setting new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAVER consistently outperforms all CLIP-based baselines at the same model scale on Kinetics-400: at B/16 (16f), it beats ILA by 0.9–1.0%, ASU by 1.2–1.2%, and X-CLIP by 1.7–1.7% Top-1; at L/14 (336↑, 16f), it surpasses ASU by 0.5–0.6% and ILA by 0.7–0.8%. In zero-shot settings, CLAVER (KMT) leads OST by 3.3% on Kinetics-600 (73.8% vs 70.5%), by 1.2% on HMDB-51 (54.1% vs 52.9%), and by 3.3% on UCF-101 (78.6% vs 75.3%). In few-shot (K=16) evaluation, CLAVER (KMT) achieves 72.5% on HMDB-51 and 98.0% on UCF-101, exceeding ASU (70.8%/97.2%) and MAXI (66.5%/93.5%) by 1.7% and 6.0% respectively. Against non-CLIP video transformers (TimeSformer, ViViT, Swin-L), CLAVER-B/16 with only 16 frames and CLIP-400M pretraining matches or exceeds methods using JFT-300M pretraining and 32+ frames, while consuming significantly fewer FLOPs (241G vs 2107G for Swin-L). The only method CLAVER trails is MTV-H (89.1%), which uses a much larger video-text pretraining corpus (WTS*, ~70M pairs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-to-video adaptation line (X-CLIP, ILA, EVL, ASU, ViFiCLIP) by arguing that prior methods insufficiently address both the visual and textual branches simultaneously. For temporal modeling, it departs from pipeline temporal attention and class-token-only attention by proposing Kronecker mask attention, which unifies spatial attention, pipeline temporal attention, and joint attention as special cases of a single algebraic framework (Kronecker product masks over joint attention). For the textual branch, it moves beyond prompt templates and trainable continuous prompts by leveraging LLMs (ChatGPT, LLaMA-3) to generate semantically rich interpretive prompts that decompose actions, convert synonyms, and involve body parts. The theoretical analysis connects to the low-rank bottleneck literature (Bhojanapalli et al., 2020), showing that the causal variant (KMCTA) guarantees full-rank attention matrices while joint attention and KMTA do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Kronecker mask provides a clean, plug-and-play inductive bias for spatiotemporal heterogeneity that is both interpretable and theoretically grounded, potentially applicable beyond action recognition to other video understanding tasks. The spatiotemporal homogenization analysis (token shuffling experiments) offers a diagnostic tool for evaluating whether temporal models genuinely capture spatiotemporal structure rather than merely fitting accuracy. Limitations include reliance on CLIP-scale pretraining (400M pairs), modest gains over strong baselines like ASU and ILA on Kinetics-400, and the KMT/KMCT transformer being only 1/3 the depth of the image encoder. Open questions include whether the full-rank advantage of KMCTA persists at much larger scales and whether interpretive prompts generated by LLMs transfer well to domains with very different action vocabularies.
