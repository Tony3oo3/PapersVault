## Problem

Label smoothing and Mixup are two of the most widely used data augmentation techniques in deep learning, yet no unifying theoretical perspective has explained why models trained with either loss exhibit similar behavioral patterns. This paper identifies the shared mechanism: both label augmentation strategies incentivize learning minimum variance features in the data, a property that has not been previously characterized or connected to the two methods simultaneously.

## Input / Output

Theoretically, the analysis considers classification with inputs in R^d and model outputs as probability vectors over k classes, with the key quantity of interest being the variance of model outputs and hidden representations conditional on the true label. Empirically, the paper uses standard RGB images (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, MNIST at 28×28) processed through ResNet-18/50/101 or logistic regression, tracking both final test error and the total variance of penultimate-layer activations and output probabilities averaged across classes. For the spurious correlation experiments, inputs are modified by injecting a low-magnitude identifying dimension (γ·y) or class-specific background colors, while test data remains unmodified.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical proof that label augmentation losses (label smoothing and Mixup) have minimizers with arbitrarily small correlation with high-variance data dimensions, while weight-regularized ERM requires learning high-variance features due to their greater separability. For linear binary classification, this is established via strong convexity of the loss in w^T YX combined with Jensen's inequality lower bounds, showing that any solution with non-negligible weight in high-variance dimensions remains bounded away from the optimal loss as low-variance dimension variance decreases. For general multi-class models, the paper proves that both label smoothing and Mixup losses are lower bounded by a universal constant times the model output variance, meaning any training algorithm must push outputs toward low variance to make progress. Empirically, the paper validates this with ResNet experiments on CIFAR-10/100 showing 10–40× lower activation variance under label augmentation, and demonstrates the negative consequence via colored MNIST and perturbed CIFAR experiments where the low-variance feature is a spurious correlation that label smoothing and Mixup exploit while weight decay correctly ignores.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, Mixup achieves 5.90% test error (vs. 8.40% for ERM+WD alone), while also reducing penultimate-layer activation variance from 198 to 18.4 (a ~10× reduction) and output probability variance from 0.133 to 0.059. On CIFAR-100, Mixup reaches 26.83% test error (vs. 31.59% baseline) with activation variance dropping from 673 to 82.2. In the spurious-correlation experiments (binary CIFAR and colored MNIST), label smoothing and Mixup consistently fail (high test error across all hyperparameter settings) while weight decay succeeds, demonstrating that minimum-variance feature learning can be detrimental when low-variance features are spurious.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three training strategies: standard ERM with weight decay, ERM + weight decay + label smoothing (α=0.1), and ERM + weight decay + Mixup (Beta(1,1)). On standard image classification (CIFAR-10/100), Mixup dominates both baselines on test error (5.90% vs. 7.96% vs. 8.40% on CIFAR-10) and achieves the largest reduction in activation variance (~10× over weight decay alone). Label smoothing provides a moderate improvement over weight decay on both test error and variance. However, in the spurious-correlation settings (binary CIFAR with a low-magnitude identifying dimension, and colored MNIST with class-specific backgrounds), the comparison reverses: weight decay achieves low test error for all λ>0, while label smoothing and Mixup fail across all hyperparameter settings, confirming the theoretical prediction that these methods lock onto the lowest-variance (here, spurious) feature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the feature learning analyses of Chidambaram et al. (2023) and Zou et al. (2023) — which studied Mixup-specific feature learning in multi-view data with point-mass mixing distributions — by identifying the shared 'label augmentation' mechanism as the common driver behind both Mixup and label smoothing. It extends these results in two directions: (1) generalizing from point-mass to arbitrary symmetric mixing distributions, and (2) showing that the minimum-variance feature learning property also applies to label smoothing, which had no prior feature learning theory. The paper also connects to the neural collapse literature (Papyan et al., 2020; Kornblith et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2022), providing a mechanistic explanation for why label smoothing and Mixup produce lower-variance representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical lens explaining why two widely-used but mechanistically distinct techniques (label smoothing and Mixup) produce similar empirical behavior, and identifies a concrete vulnerability: both are more susceptible to low-variance spurious correlations than standard weight decay training. This is practically relevant given that both methods are standard in SOTA training pipelines. However, the positive results (variance reduction correlating with better generalization) are explicitly correlational rather than causal, and the main theoretical results are restricted to linear models. A key open question is whether directly regularizing toward low-variance features (without the spurious correlation risk) could capture the benefits of label augmentation while avoiding its pitfalls.
