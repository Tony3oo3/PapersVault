## Problem

State-of-the-art implicit neural representations for Earth data, particularly spherical harmonic encodings, exhibit systematic performance disparities against localized subgroups such as islands and coastlines, yet no systematic dataset or framework existed to quantify these biases. The global support of Fourier-based basis functions causes aliasing (Gibbs phenomenon) when fitting sharp discontinuities, creating an inherent tradeoff between global and local representation quality that no existing encoding could resolve.

## Input / Output

The model takes 2D geographic coordinates (longitude, latitude) as input and produces multi-modal Earth signals: binary land-sea classification, continuous surface temperature, precipitation, CO2 emissions, and population density. All data is sampled on a uniform 0.1° × 0.1° grid, and FAIR-EARTH attaches per-location metadata (landmass size, coast distance, population density, country labels) enabling stratified subgroup evaluation. The INR is trained on a subset of grid points (5,000–20,000 samples) and evaluated across all subgroups simultaneously.

## Method

The paper proposes SPHERICAL WAVELETS (SW), a location encoding that replaces spherical harmonics in a SirenNet INR pipeline. The spherical Morlet mother wavelet is constructed via inverse stereographic projection of the 2D Morlet wavelet (width and wave number both set to 1), yielding an admissible spherical wavelet with dual localization in both dilation (scale) and rotation (location). A discrete basis is formed by applying rotation operators at points on a Fibonacci lattice and dilation operators at dyadic scales 2^(i/Q), producing an embedding of size O(NM) where N is the number of rotations and M the number of scales. Unlike spherical harmonics whose global basis functions must collectively represent localized features (causing aliasing elsewhere), each wavelet basis function is spatially concentrated, allowing the network to independently model fine-scale features like island boundaries without degrading global land-sea representation. The encoding is trained end-to-end with the SirenNet via standard supervised loss (binary cross-entropy for classification, MSE for regression), with no additional re-weighting or fairness-aware loss terms.

## Results

On the FAIR-EARTH land-sea binary classification task, SPHERICAL WAVELET achieves a best cross-entropy loss of 0.0762 at 20,000 training samples, matching SPHERICAL HARMONIC (0.0767) while maintaining significantly superior average performance across all resolutions (e.g., 0.0890 vs. 0.0933 at 10,000 samples). The key fairness metric—Pearson correlation between land and island loss—shifts from strongly negative for SPHERICAL HARMONIC (−0.73 to −0.88) to positive for SPHERICAL WAVELET (0.04 to 0.51), indicating that SW reconciles global and local performance rather than trading one off against the other. On surface temperature regression, SW achieves 0.0175 MSE at 20,000 samples, competitive with SH (0.0161) and substantially better than Theory (0.0194).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SPHERICAL HARMONIC (Rußwurm et al., 2024), SPHERICAL WAVELET is competitive on best-case loss (within 0.0005 at 20,000 samples) but achieves consistently lower average cross-entropy across all training resolutions (e.g., 0.0890 vs. 0.0933 at 10,000; 0.1017 vs. 0.1057 at 15,000). The critical distinction is in fairness: SH exhibits a strong negative land–island loss correlation (−0.73 to −0.88), meaning improving global land performance degrades island performance, whereas SW shows a positive correlation (0.04 to 0.51), indicating simultaneous improvement. Against THEORY (Mai et al., 2020), SW is substantially better on all FAIR-EARTH tasks—e.g., 0.0890 vs. 0.1137 on land-sea at 10,000 samples and 0.0175 vs. 0.0194 on temperature at 20,000. Against SPHEREM+/SPHEREC+ (Mai et al., 2023), SW is dramatically superior, with losses roughly 7× lower on land-sea (0.0890 vs. ~0.63) and ~125× lower on temperature (0.0175 vs. ~2.2), suggesting these grid-based encodings fail to learn meaningful representations at these resolutions. On the external checkerboard task (coarse, single-scale), SW falls slightly short of SH, and on the ultra-high-resolution non-gridded land-sea task, SW's advantage is marginal due to polar degradation from the stereographic projection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spherical harmonic encoding framework of Rußwurm et al. (2024) by replacing the global harmonic basis with localized spherical wavelet basis functions, drawing on the inverse stereographic projection construction from Demanet and Vandergheynst (2003) and the rotation-dilation operator formalism from McEwen et al. (2007). The key conceptual departure is that harmonic bases have global support, which the authors show causes Gibbs-phenomenon aliasing on localized Earth signals (islands, coastlines), whereas wavelets provide multi-scale spatial localization. The FAIR-EARTH dataset is positioned as the first systematic fairness benchmark for Earth INRs, unifying multiple geospatial modalities (land-sea, CO2, temperature, precipitation, population) with stratification metadata that prior Earth datasets lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that state-of-the-art Earth INRs exhibit a fundamental global-local performance tradeoff (strong negative land-island correlation up to R = -0.88) that is invisible under aggregate metrics, and that increasing encoding size actually worsens the bias against small landmasses. The spherical wavelet encoding mitigates this while remaining competitive globally, but retains a polar degradation from the stereographic projection and underperforms on single-scale tasks like checkerboards. Open questions include non-projection-based spherical wavelet constructions, broader subgroup coverage in FAIR-EARTH, and whether the fairness gains transfer to downstream decision-making tasks (e.g., flood risk, emissions policy) where the paper motivates the work.
