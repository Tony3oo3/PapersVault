## Problem

Softmax attention in Transformers incurs O(T) per-token generation complexity due to the growing KV cache, forcing a trade-off between accuracy and efficiency. Existing alternatives—linear attention/SSMs (semantic compression), sparse attention (token compression), and MQA/GQA (head compression)—each address only one compression axis and inevitably lose information, leaving open whether LLM complexity can be reduced while preserving performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251299/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard text token sequences and produces next-token predictions via autoregressive generation. During inference, Rodimus maintains only a fixed-size matrix hidden state (n×m per head) with O(1) time and space complexity per token, while Rodimus+ additionally stores a constant-size sliding-window KV cache for local attention. Notably, Rodimus uses an expansion factor of 64, yielding a hidden state half the size of Mamba2's (128), yet achieves lower perplexity.

## Method

Rodimus introduces a Data-Dependent Tempered Selection (DDTS) mechanism within a linear-attention-based recurrent framework: a selection gate g_t (derived from the input via ShortConv) determines whether to retain the previous hidden state or incorporate the current key-value pair, while a temperature gate τ_t (also input-dependent) controls the sharpness of this selection, enabling more aggressive filtering of irrelevant information. A low-rank value gate β̂_t provides per-element flexibility in the value dimension. Rodimus+ augments this with Sliding Window Shared-Key Attention (SW-SKA), where SKA achieves lossless head compression by sharing a single key across all heads (absorbing head-specific key weights into the query) while preserving head-specific values—unlike MQA/GQA which are lossy. The Rodimus block (global semantic view) and SW-SKA (local token view) are bound via a two-hop residual connection, and training uses chunkwise parallelization to achieve sub-quadratic complexity.

## Results

On WikiText-103 (~44M params), Rodimus achieves PPL 21.90 and Rodimus+ achieves 21.56, outperforming Mamba2 (22.78), Transformer++ (22.02), Llama3 (22.12), GLA (22.16), and HGRN2 (22.00). At the 1.6B scale, Rodimus+-1.6B (trained on 1T tokens) achieves an average downstream accuracy of 58.61%, surpassing Qwen2-1.5B (58.30%, trained on 7T tokens) and RWKV6-1.6B (56.31%, trained on 1.4T tokens). On NeedleBench, both Rodimus and Rodimus+ exceed the performance of Pythia, a full softmax-attention model, on recall-intensive tasks where recurrent models typically underperform.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba2 (the strongest recurrent baseline), Rodimus achieves up to 7.21% improvement on downstream tasks; at 1.4B scale, Rodimus (56.55% AVG, 500B tokens) edges out Mamba2 (56.43%, 300B tokens), while Rodimus+ (58.61%, 1T tokens) extends the lead to over 2%. Against Qwen2-1.5B (7T tokens, GQA-based Transformer), Rodimus+-1.6B (1T tokens) achieves 0.31% higher average accuracy, demonstrating that the hybrid recurrent+attention architecture compensates for 7× fewer training tokens. Against RWKV6-1.6B (1.4T tokens), Rodimus+ leads by 2.3% in average accuracy. On WikiText-103, Rodimus (PPL 21.90) outperforms all softmax-attention baselines (Transformer++ 22.02, Llama3 22.12) and recurrent baselines (Mamba 22.58, Mamba2 22.78, GLA 22.16, HGRN2 22.00) at the same ~44M parameter budget. On NeedleBench, Rodimus and Rodimus+ surpass Pythia (a full softmax-attention model), a notable result given that recurrent models typically struggle on recall-intensive tasks. At the 470M scale, Rodimus+ (150B tokens, AVG 50.09) outperforms Qwen2-500M (7T tokens, AVG 49.02) and Mamba2-370M (300B tokens, AVG 48.97), though Rodimus-460M alone (AVG 48.80) trails Qwen2-500M, suggesting the hybrid SW-SKA component is critical for competitive performance at smaller scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

Rodimus extends the linear-attention/SSM lineage (Katharopoulos et al. 2020, Mamba/Mamba2, GLA, HGRN2) by introducing a data-dependent tempered selection (DDTS) mechanism that adds a learnable temperature gate τ_t to modulate the sharpness of the selection between previous state and current input — a degree of freedom absent in prior decay or selection designs. Architecturally, it follows Mamba2's scalar state-space structure and GAU's unified token/channel mixing, while Rodimus+ further integrates sliding-window attention with a novel Shared-Key Attention (SKA) that achieves lossless head compression by sharing keys (not values) across heads, departing from the lossy value-sharing of MQA/GQA. The two-hop residual binding attention and FFN is adapted from Megalodon, and the chunkwise parallel training follows GLA's formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving O(1) per-token inference complexity while matching or exceeding softmax-attention models: Rodimus+-1.6B trained on 1T tokens outperforms Qwen2-1.5B trained on 7T tokens, suggesting a ~7× token-efficiency gain that could substantially reduce pretraining costs for efficient architectures. The lossless SKA and the temperature-gated selection mechanism offer reusable components for future efficient-LLM designs. Open questions include whether these gains persist at 7B+ scale, how the hybrid Rodimus+ block interacts with mixture-of-experts or multimodal extensions, and whether the curated-data advantage (FineWeb) confounds the token-efficiency comparison against Qwen2.
