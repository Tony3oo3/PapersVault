## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of transformers' in-context learning for linear regression assume exogenous noise (uncorrelated with predictors), ignoring the endogeneity problem that is pervasive in econometrics and causal inference. This paper asks whether transformers can inherently leverage instrumental variables to handle endogeneity, providing both reliable in-context predictions and consistent coefficient estimates.

## Input / Output

The input is a prompt matrix of shape (p+q+1)×(n+1) containing n labeled training triples (z_i, x_i, y_i) and one unlabeled query (z_{n+1}, x_{n+1}), where z ∈ R^q are instruments, x ∈ R^p are endogenous predictors, and y ∈ R is the response. The output is the scalar prediction ŷ_{n+1} read from the transformer's final layer, plus regression coefficients β̂ ∈ R^p extracted via finite-difference perturbation of the query's x-dimensions. The embedding dimension is D = qp + 3p + q + 3 to accommodate the vectorized parameters Θ, β, and first-stage estimates x̂ alongside the raw data.

## Method

The paper shows that a looped transformer with a two-layer attention-only block (no MLP) can implement one step of a bi-level gradient descent procedure that converges to the 2SLS estimator at exponential rate O(Λ^t) in the number of loop iterations. The first attention layer (2p heads) updates the first-stage fitted values x̂_i for all samples simultaneously, while the second layer (2(p+1) heads) updates the first-stage parameters Θ and the second-stage coefficient β. Because the block structure is independent of the current parameter values, the same block can be looped L̄ = O(log(1/ε)) times to achieve ε-accuracy. Training uses an ICL pretraining scheme where endogeneity (via a latent confounder u) is present in the n training samples but absent in the query sample, and the paper proves the global minimizer of the empirical ICL loss achieves a small excess loss over the optimal predictor.

## Results

The paper evaluates a looped transformer (10 blocks, 12 heads, D=80, 2 layers per block, trained for 300K steps) on synthetic endogeneity tasks with p=5 endogenous variables, q=10 instruments, and n=50 in-context samples, averaged over 500 simulations. On standard endogeneity tasks, the trained transformer achieves in-context prediction error (ICPE) and coefficient MSE comparable to the 2SLS estimator, with both substantially outperforming OLS. Under weak instruments (IV strength < 0.5), the transformer outperforms 2SLS on both ICPE and MSE. On non-standard tasks (quadratic IV effect and underdetermined IV with q=3 < p=5), the transformer consistently outperforms both 2SLS and OLS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the ICL-pretrained looped transformer against two classical econometric baselines: 2SLS (two-stage least squares) and OLS (ordinary least squares), both computed directly from the in-context training samples. On standard endogeneity tasks with varying sample sizes (n=20 to 50), the transformer matches 2SLS closely on both ICPE and coefficient MSE, while both substantially outperform OLS, which is biased under endogeneity. The key differentiator emerges under weak instruments: when IV strength drops below 0.5, the transformer clearly outperforms 2SLS, suggesting the ICL training process equips the model with a more robust mechanism than the fixed 2SLS procedure. On non-standard scenarios—non-linear (quadratic) IV effects and underdetermined IV (q=3 < p=5)—the transformer again outperforms both 2SLS and OLS, whereas 2SLS degrades significantly. Overall, the transformer never underperforms 2SLS and provides strictly better estimates in the challenging regimes where 2SLS is known to be unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning (ICL) framework for linear regression established by Bai et al. (2023), which showed transformers can emulate single-level gradient descent to implement OLS, to the endogenous setting where predictors are correlated with the error term. It leverages the looped transformer architecture from Giannou et al. (2023) to efficiently implement a bi-level gradient descent procedure that converges to the two-stage least squares (2SLS) estimator, a key departure from prior ICL work that only handled single-level optimization. The authors also contribute the first non-asymptotic MSE bound for the 2SLS estimator under random design, filling a gap in the econometric literature that has focused almost exclusively on asymptotic properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges a notable gap between ICL theory and econometric practice by showing that transformers can natively handle endogeneity—a fundamental problem in causal inference—without task-specific fine-tuning. The empirical finding that the ICL-pretrained transformer outperforms 2SLS in weak-instrument, non-linear IV, and underdetermined IV scenarios suggests the training process may learn richer mechanisms than the 2SLS algorithm alone, though this remains unexplained theoretically. Open questions include whether these advantages persist at scale, whether the bounded-variable assumptions can be relaxed, and whether the approach generalizes to more complex causal structures (e.g., multiple endogenous variables, dynamic panels). The practical impact is currently limited to simulation studies with small dimensions (p=5, q=10, n≤50).
