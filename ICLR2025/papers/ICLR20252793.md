## Problem

Existing activation sparsity methods for LLMs face three critical limitations: they require extensive continual pre-training (up to 150B tokens) to work with non-ReLU activations like SiLU and GELU used in modern LLMs, they depend on predicting active output channels before computation, and they achieve at most ~33% model-level sparsity without retraining. This paper asks whether the non-sparse portion of activations is truly necessary or can be approximated with a lightweight strategy that avoids heavy pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252793/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B, or Mistral-7B) and a text prompt, producing generated text tokens during the decoding phase. Sparsification is applied exclusively during decoding (not pre-filling), and the low-rank SVD decomposition of each weight matrix is pre-computed offline so it adds no inference latency. The method operates on all seven linear layers per block (four attention matrices and three MLP matrices), with a per-layer sparsity budget determined by an evolutionary search over 16 C4 calibration samples.

## Method

R-Sparse decomposes each linear layer Y = XW^T into a sparse component Y_s = σ_{t(s)}(X)W^T (retaining only input channels above a magnitude threshold) and a low-rank residual Y_r = (X − σ_{t(s)}(X))(A_r B_r)^T (routing small-magnitude channels through an offline SVD-based low-rank approximation). The key insight is that the non-sparse channels' contributions form a low-rank structure (stable rank ~400 across thousands of tokens), so a small number of SVD components suffice to approximate them. An evolutionary search algorithm (population 32, 5 generations, group-wise optimization) determines the optimal per-layer ratio ρ between sparse and low-rank components, minimizing average perplexity on C4. This eliminates the need for active channel prediction entirely, since sparsity is determined directly from input magnitudes, and extends sparsity to both attention and MLP blocks, achieving 50% model-level sparsity with a 43% end-to-end speedup via a customized Triton kernel.

## Results

R-Sparse achieves 50% model-level sparsity while maintaining near-full-model performance across three LLM families (Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B) and ten tasks. On Llama-2-7B, R-Sparse at 50% sparsity scores 64.06% average accuracy on common-sense reasoning (vs. 65.88% full model), while at 40% sparsity it reaches 65.00%. With a customized Triton kernel, R-Sparse delivers up to 43% end-to-end generation speedup for Llama-2-7B and 40% for Llama-3-8B. The method is also compatible with INT4 weight quantization (GPTQ), retaining 65.76% average accuracy at 50% sparsity + 4-bit quantization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-Sparse decisively outperforms all three training-free baselines at matched sparsity budgets. Against CATS at 40% model-level sparsity, R-Sparse achieves 65.00% vs. 46.26% average accuracy on Llama-2-7B (an 18.74% absolute gain), with the gap widening on harder tasks like ARC-C (42.66% vs. 27.56%). Against GRIFFIN at 50% model-level sparsity, R-Sparse scores 64.06% vs. 45.91% on Llama-2-7B (18.15% gain), and 68.39% vs. 48.07% on Mistral-7B. ReLUfication without retraining collapses to ~32–35% average accuracy across all three models, confirming that simply replacing SiLU/GELU with ReLU is insufficient. The advantage stems from R-Sparse's ability to sparsify both attention and MLP blocks (vs. MLP-only for CATS/GRIFFIN), its rank-aware low-rank residual that captures non-sparse contributions, and its per-layer adaptive sparsity-rank ratio found via evolutionary search.

## Relation to Prior Work

R-Sparse extends the activation sparsity line of work (ReLUfication, CATS, GRIFFIN) by shifting the sparsification target from output activations to input channels, eliminating the need for active-channel prediction that plagues prior methods. It further departs from pure sparsity by incorporating a low-rank SVD-based residual that captures the contribution of non-sparse input channels, a combination not present in prior training-free approaches. The concurrent work of Liu et al. (2024a) on input-channel sparsity is acknowledged as a special case of R-Sparse's more general sparse-plus-low-rank decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 50% model-level sparsity training-free (versus 33% for the best prior training-free method) with a 43% end-to-end speedup, R-Sparse makes high-sparsity LLM inference practical for edge deployment without the prohibitive cost of 150B-token continual pre-training. The compatibility with 4-bit weight quantization opens a path to combined sparsity-and-quantization pipelines. Open questions include whether the evolutionary search for per-layer sparse/rank ratios generalizes to substantially larger models, whether the approach extends to architectures without SVD-friendly linear layers, and whether the 50% sparsity ceiling can be pushed further without the low-rank component degrading.
