## Problem

Backdoor attacks in Federated Graph Learning (FGL) exploit the complex topological structure of graph data to inject triggers with arbitrary shapes, sizes, and injection positions, rendering traditional FL defenses ineffective. Existing methods either require clean validation datasets, assume data homogeneity, or rely on simple distance metrics that conflate benign structural heterogeneity with malicious perturbations, leaving a significant gap in node-classification backdoor defense for FGL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253389/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client uploads its local model parameters along with a vector of per-node meta energies (energy elements) computed by an Energy-Based GCN. The server consumes these energy element vectors to perform clustering, construct a global energy graph via cosine similarity, and compute per-client aggregation weights. The final output is a defended global model that preserves node classification accuracy while maximizing backdoor failure rate, evaluated via the composite metric V = (A + R)/2.

## Method

FedTGE operates at two levels. At the client level (TEDC), an Energy-Based GCN is trained via a score-matching objective that assigns low meta energy to benign nodes and high meta energy to perturbed substitutes (generated by randomly adding/removing edges and perturbing features), effectively decoupling benign and malicious energy distributions; FINCH clustering then identifies and excludes clients whose energy elements deviate significantly from the benign cluster. At the server level (TESP), the remaining clients' energy elements are treated as nodes in a global energy graph where edges connect pairs exceeding a cosine-similarity threshold τ; energy propagates along these edges to increase similarity among benign clients, and aggregation weights are assigned proportionally to both low energy magnitude and high graph degree, further suppressing any residual malicious influence.

## Results

On the five benchmark graph datasets under Non-IID-Louvain partitioning with 30% malicious clients (Rényi trigger), FedTGE achieves the highest final metric V (average of node classification accuracy A and backdoor failure rate R) on 4 out of 5 datasets: PubMed (V=77.01), Coauthor-CS (V=80.13), Coauthor-Phy (V=76.02), and Amz-Photo (V=86.14). Under IID settings with the same attack configuration, FedTGE leads on all five datasets, with particularly strong backdoor failure rates (R=97.92 on Amz-Photo, R=70.47 on Cora). The method maintains its advantage even at 50% malicious proportion, where most baselines degrade to near-Vanilla performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedTGE outperforms all 14 baselines on 9 out of 10 dataset–setting combinations (IID and Non-IID-Louvain, Y=0.3), losing only on Cora Non-IID where FLTrust achieves V=68.97 vs. FedTGE's 66.58. The most decisive margins appear on Amz-Photo (V=86.14 vs. FLTrust 81.62, FLAME 79.22) and PubMed (V=77.01 vs. FLTrust 71.40, FLAME 68.67), where the backdoor failure rate R jumps from ~56–57% to 67–95%. Distance-based methods (RFA, Trimmed Mean/Median, FreqFed) consistently fail to raise R above 15–25% on most datasets, indicating they cannot distinguish topological triggers from natural heterogeneity. FLAME and FLTrust, which use proxy-data or graph-clustering heuristics, are the strongest competitors but still trail FedTGE by 3–12 points in V on four of five datasets. DnC achieves high R on some datasets (e.g., 80% on Cora Non-IID) but at the cost of severely degraded accuracy (A=41.21), yielding a lower overall V.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the energy-based model framework (Song & Kingma, 2021) and score matching (Hyvärinen & Dayan, 2005) to introduce an Energy-Based GCN that assigns calibrated energy values to graph nodes, then uses these energy distributions for client-level clustering (via FINCH) and server-level aggregation weighting (via an energy graph with cosine-similarity edges). It departs from prior FL backdoor defenses (FLTrust, RFA, DnC, FLAME, etc.) that rely on pairwise model distances, statistical outlier detection, or proxy validation data—approaches that the authors argue fail under graph data's topological complexity and non-iid heterogeneity. The paper also extends the FGL defense line initiated by Yang et al. (2024), which targeted graph classification, to the underexplored node classification setting, positioning itself as the first systematic backdoor defense benchmark for FGL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a clear gap: backdoor defense for federated graph node classification had no dedicated method or benchmark prior to this paper, and the demonstrated failure of all 15 traditional FL defenses under FGL settings (with performance collapsing to near-vanilla FedAvg at high poisoning rates) underscores the inadequacy of existing tools. The energy-based formulation offers a principled way to decouple benign from malicious distributions without requiring a clean validation set, which is critical in privacy-sensitive domains like medical or financial graph data. Limitations include inability to remove already-embedded poisoned parameters, additional O(|N|) computational overhead for energy computation, and evaluation restricted to GCN with 2-layer architectures on moderate-sized graphs—open questions include whether the energy-graph propagation generalizes to deeper GNNs, heterogeneous graph types, or larger-scale federated deployments.
