## Problem

MLLMs frequently generate non-existent objects (hallucinations), yet the internal mechanism remains poorly understood. Through empirical probing, the authors discover that MLLMs actually recognize visual objects in preceding transformer layers, but this visual information is progressively suppressed in deeper layers by the language model's inherent knowledge priors. This layer-level insight motivates a training-free decoding correction method that recovers preceding-layer visual knowledge to mitigate hallucinations at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250494/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., "Describe the image") and produces an image caption or VQA answer, operating entirely at inference time on 7B-parameter, 32-layer decoder-only MLLMs (InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4, LLaVA-1.5, Qwen-VL). During autoregressive generation, DeCo intercepts hidden states at every transformer layer, using top-p truncation to track a small set of candidate tokens across layers. The visual encoder produces image tokens that are concatenated with text embeddings before entering the language model, and DeCo modifies the final-layer logits at each decoding step without any retraining or architectural changes.

## Method

DeCo (Dynamic Correction Decoding with preCeding-Layer Knowledge) has two components. First, dynamic preceding-layer selection: using top-p truncation to obtain candidate tokens at each layer, it identifies an "anchor layer" within a configurable range (default 20–28 for 32-layer models) where the highest-probability candidate token best represents the ground truth, formalized as an argmax over softmax outputs across the layer interval. Second, decoding correction: the final-layer logits are updated as logits = φ(h^N) + α × max_prob × φ(h^A), where α (0.1–0.6) controls correction strength and max_prob (the maximum softmax probability in the anchor layer) serves as a dynamic soft modulation coefficient to prevent abrupt logit shifts and preserve the model's generative style. The method is training-free, model-agnostic, and composable with greedy search, nucleus sampling, and beam search, introducing only ~1.2× latency overhead versus vanilla decoding—substantially less than VCD (1.8×) or OPERA (5.1×).

## Results

On the CHAIR hallucination metric (lower is better) across four 7B MLLMs (InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4, LLaVA-1.5, Qwen-VL), DeCo achieves an average hallucination suppression rate of approximately 10.8% over vanilla decoding. Under greedy decoding, DeCo reduces CHAIR_S from 58.8 to 41.2 on InstructBLIP and from 45.0 to 8.8 on LLaVA-1.5. On the POPE VQA benchmark (F1, higher is better), DeCo improves MiniGPT-4 from 58.5 to 77.4 under greedy decoding (+18.9) and from 52.8 to 63.8 under nucleus sampling (+11.0). DeCo introduces only ~1.2× latency overhead compared to 1.8× for VCD and 5.1× for OPERA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCo consistently outperforms all three baselines across the four evaluated MLLMs. Against DoLa (ICLR 2024) under greedy decoding, DeCo reduces CHAIR_S by 7.2 points on InstructBLIP (41.2 vs. 48.4) and by 39.0 points on LLaVA-1.5 (8.8 vs. 47.8); on POPE F1, DeCo gains +4.9 over DoLa on InstructBLIP and +18.9 on MiniGPT-4. Against OPERA (CVPR 2024) under beam search, DeCo achieves lower CHAIR_S on all four models (e.g., 7.5 vs. 44.6 on LLaVA-1.5) and higher POPE F1 (86.7 vs. 85.4 on LLaVA-1.5), while incurring only 1.2× latency versus OPERA's 5.1×. Against VCD (arXiv 2023) under nucleus sampling, DeCo substantially outperforms VCD on CHAIR_S (e.g., 9.5 vs. 54.0 on LLaVA-1.5) and POPE F1 (63.8 vs. 56.0 on MiniGPT-4), whereas VCD actually worsens hallucination relative to vanilla on several models. The one exception is LLaVA-1.5 CHAIR_I under beam search, where DeCo (15.8) is slightly worse than OPERA (12.8), though DeCo still dominates on CHAIR_S (7.5 vs. 44.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCo is conceptually inspired by DoLa (Chuang et al., 2024), which contrasts shallow and deep layer logits to improve factuality in unimodal LLMs, but repurposes this idea for MLLMs by *adding* preceding-layer knowledge to the final layer rather than subtracting it. It shares the core assumption with VCD (Leng et al., 2023) and OPERA (Huang et al., 2024) that language-model priors suppress visual information in deeper layers, but departs from both by being simpler (no contrastive forward pass, no attention-pattern detection) and by dynamically selecting an anchor layer per token rather than using fixed layer pairs. The empirical motivation draws on probing experiments (Ye et al., 2024), Grad-CAM visualizations (Zhang et al., 2024a), and early-exit analyses to establish that visual recognition peaks in intermediate layers before being overwritten by textual priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in DeCo's efficiency: at ~1.2× latency overhead versus 1.8× (VCD) and 5.1× (OPERA), it is far more deployable in real-time applications like medical imaging or autonomous driving where hallucination costs are high. The empirical finding that MLLMs retain ~80% object-recognition accuracy in intermediate layers yet hallucinate in final output reframes the problem from 'the model cannot see' to 'the model sees but is overridden,' opening a broader design space for layer-aware decoding. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 7B models (generalization to 70B+ architectures is untested), a slight negative impact on caption detailedness, and the need to empirically re-tune the anchor-layer interval for different model families. An open question is whether DeCo can be composited with training-time alignment methods or with other decoding-time corrections (e.g., OPERA's retrospection) for compounding gains.
