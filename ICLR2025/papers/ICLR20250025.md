## Problem

Local loss training methods (e.g., Forward-Forward, LoCo) reduce the memory footprint of deep network training by avoiding full backpropagation, but they produce biased gradients that lead to slow or non-convergence and substantially lower accuracy than BP. Existing unifications like the MPC framework still face an inherent efficiency–performance trade-off: smaller horizons save memory but yield worse accuracy. The paper asks whether the true BP gradient can be selectively injected into the local loss gradient to close this gap without incurring the full memory cost of BP.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two independent mini-batches per update period: a primary batch B (size 64) for computing the local loss gradient at each block, and a secondary batch B′ (size 320, split into sub-batches of 8) for computing the offset Δg̃ = g̃_T − a_h·g̃_h. Inputs are 224×224 normalized RGB images with class labels and a pre-trained model (ResNet-50 or ViT-b16) in full-tuning, LoKr, or LoRA mode. The output is a modified per-block gradient ĝ_{h,t} = a_{h,t}·E_B[g_{h,t}] + λ_{h,t}·E_{B′}[Δg̃_{h,t}] used to update weights, with the offset and scaling factors (a_h, λ_h) refreshed only every K steps (one epoch by default) to limit overhead.

## Method

Within the MPC loss-split framework, the paper modifies the local loss gradient g_{h,t} by adding a BP-derived offset computed on a separate mini-batch: ĝ_{h,t} = a_{h,t}·E_B[g_{h,t}] + λ_{h,t}·E_{B′}[g̃_{T,t} − a_{h,t}·g̃_{h,t}]. The key insight, formalized via stochastic modified equations, is that this offset reduces the bias between the applied gradient and the true BP gradient at the cost of introducing additional variance proportional to λ_h²/B′, creating a bias-variance trade-off. Optimal scaling factors a_h* and λ_h* are derived by minimizing a one-step loss bound, yielding closed-form expressions that balance bias and variance terms; an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck toy model further shows both λ_h=0 (pure local loss) and λ_h=1 (pure BP) are suboptimal. In practice, the offset is updated lazily every K steps and B′ is split into small sub-batches with gradient accumulation, keeping the additional memory overhead to O(b′T) (negligible for LoRA/LoKr) and the runtime increase under 6%.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, the BP-modified local loss method achieves an average 20.5% top-1 accuracy improvement over the Forward-Forward algorithm (e.g., 68.30% vs. 40.10% on ResNet-50 full tuning), 18.6% improvement over LoCo (74.61% vs. 45.29%), and 6.5% improvement over MPC with h=5 (77.24% vs. 66.02%). Compared to full backpropagation, the BP-modified methods incur only a 7.7% average test accuracy loss while saving up to 75% memory. On Tiny-ImageNet, similar gains are observed (e.g., +43.56% for FF on ResNet-50 full tuning), though improvements diminish when the original local loss already performs well (e.g., LoKr tuning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The BP-modified local loss method outperforms all four baselines across every task and model configuration. Against the Forward-Forward algorithm (Hinton, 2022), it achieves the largest gains: +28.20% on ResNet-50 full tuning, +10.91% on LoKr, and +22.65% on ViT-b16 LoRA, reflecting that FF (h=1) has the most biased local gradient. Against LoCo (Xiong et al., 2020), improvements are +29.32%, +10.31%, and +16.23% respectively. Against MPC with h=5 (Ren & Li, 2024), gains are smaller (+11.22%, +2.12%, +6.26%) since the longer horizon already captures more global information. Against full backpropagation (Rumelhart et al., 1986), the BP-modified methods close most of the gap: LoCo BP-modified reaches 90.54% on ViT-b16 LoRA vs. BP's 91.49% (only 0.95% gap), while FF BP-modified still lags by 5.19% on that task. The pattern is consistent: the worse the original local loss performs, the larger the absolute improvement from the BP modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MPC framework (Ren & Li, 2024), which unifies backpropagation and local loss methods (Forward-Forward at h=1, LoCo at h=2) through a truncated-loss formulation with a horizon parameter. The key departure is introducing a BP-modified offset: rather than relying solely on the truncated loss gradient, the method computes the difference between the true BP gradient and the local gradient on a separate mini-batch and uses it to correct the local gradient in subsequent steps. The theoretical analysis draws on stochastic modified equations (Li et al., 2017) and the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, while the implementation strategy (lazy periodic updates, batch splitting) is conceptually analogous to control variate methods like SVRG, though with the opposite motivation—reducing bias at the cost of added variance rather than reducing variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Memory-constrained training of deep networks remains a central practical bottleneck, and this paper demonstrates that a relatively simple offset correction can close most of the accuracy gap between local loss methods and full BP (only 7.7% average loss) while saving up to 75% memory, with negligible additional memory overhead (0.01–5.6%). The bias-variance trade-off analysis provides principled, closed-form guidance for the scaling factors λ_h and a_h, which is more actionable than heuristic tuning. However, experiments are limited to CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-Tiny on a single GPU, and the method's behavior on larger models, distributed settings, or in combination with other memory-efficient techniques (e.g., MeZO, forward-gradient methods) remains unexplored. The method can also degrade performance when the original local loss is already strong (e.g., LoKr on ImageNet-Tiny), highlighting that the bias-variance balance is task-dependent.
