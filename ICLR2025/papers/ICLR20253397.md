## Problem

Neural network-based choice models achieve high predictive accuracy but lack interpretability, making them unsuitable for domains like healthcare and consumer behavior where transparency is essential. Empirical evidence shows humans use heuristic consider-then-choose processes governed by OR-of-ANDs logic rules, yet no existing model efficiently learns and explains these structured decision rationales while handling the NP-hard combinatorial complexity of rule extraction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253397/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a choice set of alternatives, each with a feature vector, plus offer-set-level features (e.g., search criteria for hotels, patient vitals for drugs). Continuous features are discretized into binary predicates via percentile thresholds (a hyperparameter), and the model distinguishes option-specific from offer-set-specific predicates—rules composed solely of offer-set features are discarded since they cancel in the softmax. The output is a mixture of preference types, each defined by a sparse set of weighted OR-of-ANDs conjunction rules, yielding per-option choice probabilities and human-readable decision logic.

## Method

Logic-Logit replaces the linear utility in the Multinomial Logit model with a weighted sum of Boolean conjunction features (each conjunction is a subset of predicates), producing a softmax choice probability over a 2^M − 1 dimensional but sparse weight vector. To capture preference heterogeneity, a Mixed Logic-Logit model places a latent mixture distribution ρ over these rule-weight vectors. Training uses a two-tiered optimization: the outer loop applies a functional Frank-Wolfe (conditional gradient) algorithm to iteratively discover new preference types (Dirac measures) and update mixture proportions, while the inner loop solves the Frank-Wolfe step via column generation—alternating a master problem that re-weights existing rules and a subproblem that searches for new conjunctions using BFS/DFS with a risk-seeking quantile-based selection strategy to avoid premature convergence.

## Results

On the Expedia Hotel dataset, Logic-Logit (RS-30) achieves a test NLL loss of 2.9675 and top-1 accuracy of 17.38%, outperforming the best NN baseline RUMNet (loss 3.0736, acc 14.89%) and the best classic model Mix-MNL (loss 2.9576, acc 16.57%). On the MIMIC-IV sepsis treatment dataset, Logic-Logit (RS-50) achieves a test NLL loss of 2.1713 and top-1 accuracy of 26.0%, dramatically surpassing the best NN baseline RUMNet (loss 2.4426, acc 10.00%) by over 16 percentage points in accuracy. On the synthetic dataset, 8 out of 9 ground-truth OR-of-ANDs rules are perfectly recovered (Jaccard index = 1), demonstrating the model's ability to extract the true decision logic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Logic-Logit outperforms all six baselines on both real-world datasets in terms of NLL loss. On Expedia Hotel, Logic-Logit (RS-30) achieves the best test loss (2.9675) and top-1 accuracy (17.38%), beating Mix-MNL (16.57%) by ~0.8 pp and RUMNet (14.89%) by ~2.5 pp in accuracy, while Vanilla NN achieves the highest accuracy (21.20%) but at a substantially worse loss (3.1669), indicating overfitting. On MIMIC-IV, the advantage is most dramatic: Logic-Logit (RS-50) reaches 26.0% top-1 accuracy versus 10.0% for RUMNet and 8.48% for Mix-DeepMNL, a gap of over 16 pp, because the dataset is dominated by offer-set-level patient features that linear and standard NN models cannot exploit effectively. The risk-seeking (RS) variant consistently matches or slightly exceeds the normal (NM) variant across all datasets, confirming the benefit of the exploratory rule-search strategy. On the synthetic dataset, Logic-Logit recovers 8/9 ground-truth rules exactly (Jaccard = 1), a capability no baseline model possesses since none of them produce interpretable logic rules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Frank-Wolfe (conditional gradient) framework for mixed logit estimation introduced by Jagabathula et al. (2020) by replacing the linear utility function with OR-of-ANDs logic rules, drawing on the column generation approach for rule learning from Wei et al. (2019). The key conceptual departure is the two-tiered optimization: the outer Frank-Wolfe loop discovers preference types (as in Jagabathula et al.), while the inner column generation loop searches over an exponentially large space of conjunction rules to build each type's utility. The consider-then-choose motivation from Hauser (2014) and Liu & Arora (2011) provides the behavioral grounding for why disjunctions of conjunctions are the appropriate rule structure, distinguishing this from purely linear or neural-network utility models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Logic-Logit offers a principled path to interpretable choice modeling in high-stakes domains (healthcare, consumer decisions) where black-box neural networks are unacceptable, and the MIMIC-IV results demonstrate that extracted rules align with clinical reasoning (e.g., low blood pressure + coagulation abnormalities → packed red blood cells). However, the method's scalability is constrained by the combinatorial rule search (bounded by max conjunction length and predicate count), and the large MIMIC accuracy gap over baselines is partly attributable to those baselines being structurally mismatched to offer-set-level features rather than a fundamental superiority of logic rules. Open questions include whether the heuristic BFS/DFS rule search can be replaced with more principled combinatorial optimization, how the model behaves when the true decision process involves more than a handful of rules, and whether the predefined predicate discretization (percentile thresholds) can be learned end-to-end.
