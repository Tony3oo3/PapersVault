## Problem

Despite in-context learning (ICL) underpinning most LLM applications, there is no systematic framework linking ICL's generalization boundaries to training data coverage, and its robustness under distribution shift remains unexplored. Prior work has characterized ICL mechanisms (e.g., implicit OLS, gradient descent) but has not delineated *where* generalization succeeds or fails relative to what the model has seen during training.

## Input / Output

For function-fitting experiments, the model receives a prompt sequence of (x, f(x;φ)) pairs at the embedding level (floating-point, not tokenized text) followed by a query x_{n+1}, and outputs a predicted scalar ŷ. For real-world tasks, inputs are natural-language instructions: tool-calling uses a ReAct template with multi-step API invocations (context length extended to 24,588 tokens), and translation uses mixed English-German code-switched sentences where a random subset of words is replaced with the other language while preserving semantics. All ICL evaluations use 16-shot in-context examples for tool-calling and 5-shot for translation.

## Method

The paper defines a task-centric generalization framework along three nested dimensions—inter-problem (new function families), intra-problem (unseen combinations of known base functions), and intra-task (unseen parameterizations of known functions)—and operationalizes it using orthogonal sinusoidal base functions {φ sin(βx), φ cos(βx)} combined via addition, multiplication, or composition. The core experimental contrast is between a Baseline model trained only on base functions and a ComFuncLearner trained on base functions plus a single combination pattern, with matched training set sizes. Experiments span randomly initialized GPT-2 (9.5M params), finetuned LLaMA-3-8B, LLaMA-2-7B on ToolBench, and a from-scratch Qwen2-1.5B on WMT14 translation. The key finding is that transformers consistently fail inter-problem generalization (Baseline cannot learn unseen combination types via ICL) but achieve strong intra-problem generalization (ComFuncLearner generalizes to unseen combinations of the same base functions after seeing just one combination pattern), and that increasing the diversity of combination patterns in training monotonically improves ICL generalization.

## Results

On function fitting (GPT-2, 4 base sinusoidal functions), the ComFuncLearner reduces mean squared error on unseen convex combinations from 7.96e-2 (Baseline) to 2.21e-2 (CFL) over the 1–10 range, and from 4.94e-2 to 1.49e-3 over the 31–40 range, demonstrating strong intra-problem generalization. On ToolBench (LLaMA-2-7B), CFL+ICL_m achieves 32.0 average accuracy on multi-API tasks, surpassing ToolLLaMA's 29.0 and the Baseline+ICL_m's 26.3. On WMT14 mixed-language translation (Qwen2-1.5B), CFL_mix reaches 35.74 BLEU on mix2en, outperforming the best single-direction Baseline (BL_d2e at 33.31) by 2.4 BLEU points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is ToolLLaMA (Qin et al., 2023), the SOTA tool-calling model based on LLaMA-2-7B. The paper's CFL+ICL_m configuration achieves 32.0 average accuracy on multi-API tasks, beating ToolLLaMA's 29.0 by 3.0 points, and CFL+ICL_s reaches 30.9 on single-API tasks versus ToolLLaMA's 29.0. Internally, the key comparison is between Baseline (trained only on single-API / base functions) and ComFuncLearner (trained on a subset of multi-API / combination tasks): CFL consistently outperforms Baseline on combination tasks by 2–5 points in tool-calling and by 1–3 BLEU in translation, while Baseline retains a slight edge on the exact single-task it was trained for (e.g., BL_d2e scores 25.94 BLEU on de2en vs. CFL_mix's 23.10). The function-fitting experiments confirm this pattern quantitatively: Baseline's mean SE on unseen combinations remains ~10–20× higher than CFL's across all ranges, while both models achieve near-identical SE on the base functions they were trained on, isolating the gap to inter-problem generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL generalization line initiated by Garg et al. (2022), who showed transformers can learn simple function classes in-context, and Ahuja & Lopez-Paz (2023), who studied ICL under distribution shift for linear regression. Where prior work examined generalization to individual function classes or specific distribution shifts, this paper introduces a hierarchical task-centric framework with three nested dimensions (inter-problem, intra-problem, intra-task) and systematically studies how training data composition—specifically the inclusion of function combinations—shapes generalization boundaries. It also builds on Yadlowsky et al. (2023), who showed ICL is heavily reliant on training data coverage, by providing a structured taxonomy of what 'coverage' means across problem and task levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even LLaMA-3, despite massive pretraining, fails at inter-problem generalization while excelling at intra-problem and intra-task generalization provides concrete guidance for LLM training data design: include diverse, mixed-task data rather than focusing solely on the target task. The practical implication—that training on complex combinations improves ICL performance even on simple tasks—has direct relevance to how practitioners curate SFT and instruction-tuning datasets. Open questions remain about the mechanistic explanation for why intra-problem generalization emerges from minimal combination exposure, how these findings scale to more complex real-world problem structures, and whether the distribution-shift vulnerabilities identified in the appendix (extreme sensitivity to even 10 noisy in-context examples) can be mitigated architecturally.
