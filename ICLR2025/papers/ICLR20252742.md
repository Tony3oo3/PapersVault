## Problem

Selecting the optimal graph kernel for unsupervised tasks like clustering is difficult because no single kernel dominates across datasets, and existing multiple kernel learning (MKL) methods either require labels or are designed for Euclidean data. Prior unsupervised MKL approaches (e.g., sparse-UMKL) rely on heuristic k-NN constructions that fail to generalize to the complex, non-Euclidean structure of graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252742/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N unlabeled graphs and M precomputed pairwise kernel matrices (30 base kernels with varied hyperparameters in experiments) as input, along with a power hyperparameter o ∈ {2,3,4}. It outputs a weight vector w on the unit simplex that defines a composite kernel, which can then be directly applied to downstream tasks such as graph-level clustering. Unlike baseline methods that require a GCN to project graphs into vector space, UMKL-G operates purely on precomputed kernel values without learning any explicit graph representation.

## Method

UMKL-G preserves the topological structure of graphs by maintaining ordinal relationships among kernel values through a probability simplex. For each graph G_i, its normalized kernel values to all other graphs form a probability distribution q_i on Δ_N; a target distribution p_i^(o) is constructed by raising kernel values to a power o > 1, which amplifies stronger similarities (concentration effect) while preserving the original ordering. The objective minimizes the reverse KL divergence KL(Q‖P), which simultaneously regularizes Q against over-concentration and aligns it with the structure-emphasizing target P. Weights w are optimized via gradient descent with a projection onto the unit simplex (O(M log M) per step) to maintain positive semi-definiteness of the composite kernel. The paper provides theoretical guarantees including Lipschitz continuity of the gradient, robustness to kernel perturbations, and generalization bounds via uniform stability.

## Results

UMKL-G achieves the best clustering performance across all eight benchmark datasets on all three metrics (ACC, NMI, ARI). The most dramatic improvement is on BZR, where UMKL-G reaches 94.3% ACC and 0.081 ARI versus 74.2% ACC and 0.031 ARI for the best baseline (sparse-UMKL k=100). On MUTAG, UMKL-G achieves 84.6% ACC and 0.339 ARI, nearly doubling the ARI of the best baseline (0.195). Even on the hardest dataset (ENZYMES), UMKL-G improves ACC from 26.2% (AverageMKL) to 29.8% and ARI from 0.022 to 0.040.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMKL-G consistently outperforms both unsupervised MKL baselines (UMKL and sparse-UMKL) across all eight datasets and all three metrics. The gap is largest on BZR (ACC 94.3% vs. 74.2% for sparse-UMKL) and MUTAG (ARI 0.339 vs. 0.195 for sparse-UMKL k=10), while on DD the margin is smaller (ACC 58.2% vs. 57.7%). Against individual graph kernels (Random Walk/VH, Shortest Path, Graphlet, WL, WLOA, Wasserstein WL, and MMD-GK), UMKL-G surpasses every single kernel on every metric across all datasets, as shown in the bar-plot comparisons (Figures 2–8). Notably, the baselines UMKL and sparse-UMKL require a GCN to project graphs into Euclidean space before applying their objectives, whereas UMKL-G operates directly on precomputed kernel matrices without any graph embedding. The learned kernel weights (Figure 2b) demonstrate that UMKL-G automatically selects the most informative kernels and hyperparameters per dataset, effectively performing data-driven kernel selection without labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised Multiple Kernel Learning (MKL) line of work initiated by Zhuang et al. (2011) and Mariette & Villa-Vialaneix (2018), both of which target Euclidean data or rely on heuristic k-NN construction that fails for graphs. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the ordinal-preservation principle from non-metric MDS (Agarwal et al., 2007; Vankadara et al., 2023) and embedding it in a probability simplex over kernel values, thereby avoiding the need for explicit graph vectorization or local neighbor graphs. The method also draws on the rich graph kernel literature (R-convolution, optimal assignment, Wasserstein WL, MMD-GK) as its input space, treating all precomputed kernel matrices as interchangeable weak kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UMKL-G fills a practical gap: prior unsupervised MKL methods simply cannot handle graph data without first projecting into Euclidean space (e.g., via GCN), which introduces representation error and limits applicability to non-R-convolution kernels. The theoretical guarantees (Lipschitz continuity, perturbation robustness, generalization bounds) provide rare formal grounding for an unsupervised kernel combination method. However, all experiments use small benchmark datasets (N ≤ ~1,000 graphs), absolute clustering metrics remain low even for the best method (e.g., NMI ≈ 0.03–0.30), and scalability to large graph collections is unaddressed. The authors themselves flag extension to other unstructured data types (UMKL-X) and efficiency on larger datasets as open directions.
