## Problem

Existing sensitivity verification for tree ensembles relies on either SMT solvers or purely optimization-based approaches, leaving the pseudo-Boolean solving paradigm unexplored for this problem. Additionally, prior work lacked hardness results for bounded (especially single) feature sensitivity and did not incorporate the classifier's confidence (sigmoid output) into the sensitivity definition. This paper addresses both gaps by proving NP-hardness for single-feature sensitivity and introducing a confidence-parameterized sensitivity problem with a novel pseudo-Boolean encoding.

## Input / Output

The tool SENSPB accepts a binary XGBoost tree ensemble model, a set of features F to test for sensitivity, a confidence gap parameter p (default 0.15 in experiments), and a precision parameter α (set to 10× number of trees). It returns either a 'pass' verdict (the ensemble is not (p,F)-sensitive) or a concrete pair of witness inputs (x, x') that agree on all features outside F but whose sigmoid-mapped ensemble outputs differ by at least 2p around 0.5. The precision parameter α controls a soundness-precision trade-off: higher α yields counterexamples closer to the true threshold but may increase solver time.

## Method

The core contribution is a pseudo-Boolean encoding of the (p,F)-sensitivity problem that exploits the arithmetic structure of additive tree ensembles. Feature inputs are discretized into segments defined by guard thresholds and encoded as ordered Boolean bits; tree traversal is encoded via node-visit variables with implication constraints linking guards to child selection; and the ensemble output constraint becomes a pseudo-Boolean sum of discretized leaf values bounded by δ = σ⁻¹(0.5+p). This encoding is structurally distinct from the SMT encoding of Ignatiev et al. (2020a) because it preserves the knapsack-like arithmetic at the output, allowing pseudo-Boolean solvers (Z3) to leverage specialized reasoning. Theorem 2 establishes completeness (unsatisfiable encoding implies non-sensitivity) and α-soundness (satisfiable encoding yields counterexamples within (m+1)/α of the true threshold), with a corollary guaranteeing exactness for sufficiently large α.

## Results

SENSPB (the pseudo-Boolean encoding) achieves an order-of-magnitude speedup over the SMT-based approach on most benchmarks: e.g., 5.8s vs. 57.2s on Diabetes unrobust (~10×), 8.3s vs. 106s on Cod-rna unrobust (~13×), and 14.9s vs. timeout on Binary MNIST (784 features, 50 trees). On the three largest benchmarks (Higgs, IJCNN, Binary MNIST), the SMT approach times out entirely while SENSPB completes in 1188s, 330.7s, and 14.9s respectively. Against VERITAS, SENSPB is faster on all benchmarks where VERITAS produces a solution (e.g., 5.9s vs. 198.1s on Diabetes unrobust, 8.3s vs. 346.2s on Cod-rna unrobust), and VERITAS times out or runs out of memory on the three largest benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SENSPB against two baselines: an SMT-based approach (Ignatiev et al., CP 2020) and VERITAS (Devos et al., ICML 2021). Against the SMT baseline, SENSPB wins on every benchmark where both complete: the speedup ranges from ~1.1× on small benchmarks (Breast cancer, Diabetes robust) to ~10× on Diabetes unrobust (5.8s vs. 57.2s) and ~13× on Cod-rna unrobust (8.3s vs. 106s). Critically, the SMT approach times out (1-hour limit) on 7 out of 13 benchmarks, including all synthetic benchmarks with 100+ trees and the three largest real-world benchmarks (Binary MNIST, Higgs, IJCNN), while SENSPB completes all of them. Against VERITAS, SENSPB is consistently faster on runtime (e.g., 5.9s vs. 198.1s on Diabetes unrobust, 8.3s vs. 346.2s on Cod-rna unrobust) and VERITAS times out or runs out of memory on Binary MNIST, Higgs, and IJCNN. In the accuracy comparison (Table 2), VERITAS given 3600s still fails to match SENSPB on a significant fraction of features (e.g., only 37.5% on Cod-rna unrobust, 88.9% on Diabetes unrobust), confirming that the pseudo-Boolean encoding provides both speed and solution-quality advantages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on sensitivity/fairness verification for tree ensembles initiated by Ignatiev et al. (2020a, SMT-based), Devos et al. (2021, optimization-based VERITAS), and Calzavara et al. (2023, approximation algorithms). It departs from prior encodings by introducing a pseudo-Boolean constraint formulation that exploits the arithmetic structure of additive tree ensembles, rather than the Boolean-heavy SMT encoding or the purely numerical MILP approach. The parametric (p,F)-sensitivity notion, which incorporates sigmoid-based confidence thresholds, is a new formulation not present in earlier sensitivity or fairness definitions for tree ensembles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NP-hardness result for single-feature sensitivity (even with depth-3 trees) is a clean theoretical contribution that clarifies the computational landscape and rules out polynomial-time exact algorithms under standard complexity assumptions. Practically, the pseudo-Boolean encoding with tunable precision α provides a sound-and-complete verification tool that outperforms SMT-based approaches by an order of magnitude on realistic XGBoost benchmarks, making sensitivity auditing feasible for models with hundreds of trees. Open questions include whether hardness persists for depth-2 trees, extension to max-pooling random forests and multiclass settings, and whether the pseudo-Boolean encoding generalizes to other verification properties like robustness.
