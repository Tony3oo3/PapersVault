## Problem

Photo-realistic image restoration methods that sample from the posterior distribution achieve a perfect perceptual index but incur an MSE that is exactly twice the unconstrained MMSE, which is suboptimal. The theoretically optimal estimator X̂₀ that minimizes MSE under a perfect perceptual index constraint has been characterized via optimal transport (Freirich et al., 2021), but no practical algorithm existed to approximate it. GAN-based approaches that attempt to traverse the distortion-perception tradeoff are extremely difficult to optimize, especially at the high GAN-loss weights needed to approach X̂₀.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image y (e.g., blurred, down-sampled, noisy, JPEG-compressed) and produces a single restored photo-realistic image at the same spatial resolution. For blind face restoration, inputs are 512×512 images degraded by a compound pipeline of Gaussian blur (kernel 41×41), bilinear down-sampling (factor 0.8–32), additive Gaussian noise, and JPEG compression (quality 30–100). The flow model operates entirely in pixel space, so source and target signals always share the same dimensionality, unlike methods that flow from the raw degraded measurement which may live in a different domain.

## Method

PMRF is a two-stage framework that approximates the optimal estimator X̂₀ by combining a posterior-mean predictor with a rectified flow transport. In Stage 1, a model f_ω (e.g., SwinIR) is trained to minimize MSE against ground-truth images, yielding an approximation of the posterior mean E[X|Y]. In Stage 2, a rectified flow vector field v_θ (an HDiT model) is trained to predict the straight-line direction from Z₀ = f_ω*(Y) + σ_s·ε to X, where σ_s is a small noise level that prevents singularities when source and target lie on manifolds of different effective dimensionality. At inference, the posterior mean is predicted, small Gaussian noise is added, and the ODE dẐ_t = v_θ*(Ẑ_t, t) dt is solved with K Euler steps (K=25 in the main experiments) to produce the final restoration. The key theoretical result (Proposition 1) guarantees that, under mild conditions, PMRF achieves a perfect perceptual index and an MSE no larger than (and typically strictly smaller than) that of posterior sampling.

## Results

On the CelebA-Test blind face restoration benchmark, PMRF achieves the best FID (37.46), KID (0.0257), PSNR (26.37 dB), SSIM (0.7073), and identity preservation (Deg 30.67°) among all compared methods, while remaining competitive on NIQE (4.118, 2nd best) and LPIPS (0.3470, 3rd best). On four real-world datasets (LFW-Test, WIDER-Test, WebPhoto-Test, CelebAdult-Test), PMRF attains the lowest IndRMSE (e.g., 6.14 on LFW-Test vs. 7.21 for BFRffusion and 9.11 for DiffBIR) while maintaining on-par perceptual quality. In controlled experiments across denoising, super-resolution, inpainting, and colorization tasks, PMRF consistently dominates both posterior sampling and flow-from-Y baselines on the distortion-perception plane.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMRF decisively outperforms DOT (the only other method targeting the same optimal estimator X̂₀), achieving FID 37.46 vs. 100.2 and PSNR 26.37 vs. 21.32 on CelebA-Test, largely because PMRF performs transport in pixel space rather than in a VAE latent space with a Gaussian assumption. Against posterior-sampling diffusion methods (DiffBIR, BFRffusion, DifFace), PMRF achieves substantially better FID (37.46 vs. 59.06/41.53/38.43) and KID while also matching or exceeding their PSNR (26.37 vs. 25.39/26.21/24.80), confirming the theoretical prediction that posterior sampling incurs twice the MMSE. Compared to GAN-based methods (GFPGAN, CodeFormer, VQFR, RestoreFormer++), PMRF improves FID by 9–16 points and PSNR by 1–2 dB, while remaining competitive on LPIPS and identity metrics. In controlled experiments with identical architecture and hyperparameters, PMRF dominates both the flow-conditioned-on-Y (posterior sampling) and flow-conditioned-on-X̂* baselines across denoising, super-resolution, inpainting, and colorization, and also outperforms the flow-from-Y approach of Albergo et al. in most tasks (though the two are comparable on denoising, where the paper proves both are optimal in a Gaussian toy model). On real-world datasets without ground truth, PMRF achieves the lowest IndRMSE on all four benchmarks (e.g., 6.14 on LFW-Test vs. 7.21 for BFRffusion and 9.11 for DiffBIR) while maintaining FID within ~1 point of the best method.

## Relation to Prior Work

PMRF operationalizes a theoretical result from Freirich et al. (2021) showing that the MSE-minimizing estimator under a perfect perceptual index constraint (X̂₀) can be constructed by optimally transporting the posterior mean to the ground-truth image distribution. It uses rectified flow (Liu et al., 2023) as the practical mechanism for learning this transport map, departing from posterior sampling (which achieves 2×MMSE), GAN-based distortion-perception balancing (which is hard to optimize), and DOT (which restricts transport to VAE latent space with Gaussian assumptions). The method also contrasts with 'flow from Y' approaches (Albergo et al., 2023) that transport directly from degraded measurements, which lack a clear distortion-theoretic motivation and can operate in mismatched domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PMRF provides the first practical, theoretically-grounded algorithm that directly targets X̂₀, resolving a gap between the distortion-perception theory and state-of-the-art practice. The controlled experiments convincingly show the framework itself (not architecture or hyperparameters) drives the gains, and the IndRMSE metric offers a practical way to rank real-world restoration methods without ground truth. Open questions include whether reflow (left as future work) further tightens the MSE bound, how sensitive performance is to the σ_s noise hyperparameter, and whether the approach extends to domains where source and target live in fundamentally different spaces (e.g., MRI reconstruction).
