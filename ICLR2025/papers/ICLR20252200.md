## Problem

Existing adversarial RL defenses either enforce action consistency across nearby states (degrading performance when attacks succeed) or maximize worst-case value (overly conservative in benign settings). The paper identifies that adversarial perturbations render the true state partially observable, but this structural aspect has not been explicitly modeled in prior work. The goal is to derive a policy that balances robustness against adversarial observation perturbations with high nominal reward accumulation, without requiring test-time adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single perturbed observation s_o (which lives in the same space as the true state, unlike standard POMDPs where observations are a separate space) along with the current policy network weights. It produces an action, a belief distribution b(s) over candidate true states in the ε-bounded neighborhood N(s_o), and a C-ACoE estimate δ(s_o). For continuous state spaces (MuJoCo), the neighborhood is discretized by uniform sampling of n points; for discrete spaces (Atari, Highway), the full neighborhood is enumerated. The belief is computed per time step using KL divergence between action distributions, making it a lightweight per-step computation rather than a full POMDP belief update requiring transition model knowledge.

## Method

The paper defines Adversarial Counterfactual Error (ACoE) as V(s_o) − U(b), the gap between non-adversarial and adversarial value under a shared policy, and proves that minimizing its scalable surrogate C-ACoE (δ) — which satisfies a Bellman optimality structure — bounds the ACoE up to an uncontrollable TV/Wasserstein structural term. Two adversary-aware belief constructions are proposed: A2B assigns belief proportional to exp(D_KL(π(s)‖π(s_o))) over the ε-neighborhood, while A3B refines this by scoring each candidate state s with the ratio D_KL(π(s_o)‖π(s)) / D_KL(π(ν(s))‖π(s)), where ν is a 50-step PGD surrogate attack, thereby down-weighting states for which the observed perturbation is not a plausible adversarial choice. Training integrates C-ACoE into standard PPO (or DQN) by augmenting the GAE advantage with −λ·δ̂_t, where δ̂_t is the C-ACoE-to-go estimated from sampled trajectories, and a separate δ-network is trained to predict C-ACoE values. The key distinction from prior work is that the belief explicitly models the adversary's intent (choosing perturbations that maximize action-distribution disruption) rather than treating partial observability as a passive sensor limitation.

## Results

On MuJoCo benchmarks, A3B achieves the best robust scores across nearly all domains and attack types: on HalfCheetah under PGD (ε=0.1), A3B scores 5110±22 versus PPO's −27±1288 and RAD's 4022±851; on Hopper under PGD, A3B scores 3465±17 versus PPO's 128±105. Against the long-horizon PA-AD attacker, A3B achieves 4478 (HalfCheetah), 4931 (Walker2d), 2580 (Hopper), and 3205 (Ant), outperforming Protected† (no test-time adaptation) in 3 of 4 domains. Unperturbed performance remains competitive with PPO (e.g., 5538 vs. 5794 on HalfCheetah), confirming the method does not sacrifice nominal reward for robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A3B outperforms all baselines on the majority of adversarial settings. Against PPO (vanilla), A3B improves PGD scores by 27× on Hopper (3465 vs. 128) and from negative to 5110 on HalfCheetah. Against RADIAL (certified adversarial loss), A3B wins on Hopper PGD (3465 vs. 3070), Walker2d MAD/PGD (5025/5292 vs. 3895/3480), and HalfCheetah PGD (5110 vs. 3911), while RADIAL retains a slight edge on Ant PGD (3821 vs. 4418 — actually A3B wins here too). Against WocaR (worst-case value maximization), A3B dominates on Hopper (3465 vs. 2647 PGD) and HalfCheetah (5110 vs. 3475 PGD). Against RAD (regret-based defense), A3B wins on Hopper PGD (3465 vs. 3110), Walker2d (5292 vs. 4136 PGD), and HalfCheetah (5110 vs. 4022 PGD). Against Protected† (the ICLR 2024 SOTA without its impractical 800-round test-time adaptation), A3B wins on Hopper (3465 vs. 2215 PGD), Walker2d (5292 vs. 3613 PGD), and HalfCheetah (5110 vs. 3997 PGD), but loses on Ant PGD (4418 vs. 4368 — actually A3B still wins). The only domain where A3B does not clearly dominate is Ant under MAD, where Protected† (4264) slightly edges A3B (4071), though the gap is within one standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigms in adversarial RL—adversarial retraining (Gleave et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2023), action-consistency regularization (RADIAL, WocaR), and maximin optimization (CARRL)—by reframing the problem as a one-sided POMDP where the adversary drives the observation. The closest prior work is Protected (Liu et al., 2024), which also acknowledges partial observability but relies on test-time adaptation over 800 episodes, making it impractical for safety-critical deployment. The paper builds on PPO and DQN as base algorithms but introduces a fundamentally different objective (ACoE/C-ACoE) defined over belief distributions rather than over the observed state alone, and provides theoretical guarantees (Theorem 3.2, Theorem A.1) bounding the gap between ACoE and C-ACoE via TV and Wasserstein distances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that ACoE-based methods (A2B, A3B) achieve state-of-the-art robustness without any test-time adaptation, addressing a critical deployment constraint for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. Key limitations include reliance on single-step belief estimation (multi-step LSTM variants show only marginal gains at high computational cost), dependence on KL divergence as a proxy for attack effectiveness, and the need for neighborhood sampling in continuous state spaces. Open questions include whether more principled belief constructions (beyond KL-ratio scoring) could further improve robustness, how the approach scales to higher-dimensional observation spaces, and whether the Wasserstein-based theoretical bounds can be tightened to provide certifiable guarantees.
