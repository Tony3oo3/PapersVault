## Problem

Existing generative model evaluation metrics fail to simultaneously capture fidelity, diversity, and novelty in a statistically rigorous way. Likelihood-based metrics are saturated and poorly correlated with sample quality, while sample-based metrics like FID and precision/recall measure only subsets of these properties. The closest prior work, FLD, captures all three but relies on Gaussian mixture approximation and mandatory feature extraction, limiting its applicability to high-dimensional or non-image data where pretrained feature extractors do not exist.

## Input / Output

The method takes two sets of i.i.d. samples (e.g., real data and model-generated samples), a distance metric (default L2, but any metric works including Levenshtein for sequences), and a hyperparameter n_R specifying the number of Voronoi regions. Reference points for the tessellation are drawn from a uniform mixture of both sample sets and then removed from the test sets to preserve independence. The output is a Pearson chi-squared statistic χ²_PQM with n_R−1 degrees of freedom, a corresponding p-value quantifying the probability that both sets originate from the same distribution, and an optional overfitting p-value (Eq. 7) that penalizes suspiciously low χ² values to detect memorization. No auxiliary model is trained or fitted; the method operates directly in the original data space (e.g., pixel space for images) without dimensionality reduction.

## Method

PQMass partitions the sample space into non-overlapping Voronoi cells defined by n_R reference points, then counts how many samples from each set fall into each cell, yielding two multinomial distributions. A Pearson chi-squared test is applied to these multinomials, producing a statistic that follows a χ²(n_R−1) distribution under the null hypothesis of equal distributions. The key theoretical contribution is a pair of consistency propositions: even with just two regions, the test has strictly positive probability of distinguishing any two distinct Lebesgue-absolutely continuous distributions (Prop. 2.1), and this probability approaches 1 as n_R→∞ (Prop. 2.2), meaning no information is lost in the asymptotic limit. For novelty and memorization detection, the method exploits the asymmetry of the chi-squared distribution to define a one-sided p-value that flags suspiciously low χ² values (indicating the generated samples are too close to the training data). The approach requires only pairwise distance computations, making it computationally efficient and scalable to moderately high-dimensional data without feature extraction.

## Results

On ImageNet noise detection, PQMass χ²_PQM increases from 95.55 (σ²=0) to 4495.07 (σ²=1.0), while FLD only rises from 9.29 to 42.54, showing PQMass is substantially more sensitive to distribution shift. On CIFAR-10, PQMass values range from 177±22 (PFGM++) to 12407±10 (ACGAN-Mod) and visibly correlate with human error rate in identifying real vs. generated images. In the astrophysics comparison (Table 5), PQMass with 10 regions yields scores near the expected ~9 for in-distribution null tests (e.g., 9.42 for Probes, 9.83 for SKIRT TNG) and correctly flags out-of-distribution JWST samples (131.70), while FLD returns misleading negative values (−21.72, −4.09) and FID is inconsistent across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLD (Jiralerspong et al., 2023), PQMass is more sensitive to additive noise on ImageNet (χ²_PQM reaches 4495 vs FLD's 42.54 at σ²=1.0), detects small modes earlier (PQMass rises from 100.41 at α=0 to 114.65 at α=0.10, while FLD stays near −4 throughout), and is more consistent in astrophysics where FLD returns negative values for known in-distribution splits. Against FID (Heusel et al., 2017), PQMass provides a calibrated p-value with a clear reference (χ²_{n_R−1} distribution) enabling statistical significance testing, whereas FID offers no such reference; in the astrophysics table, FID is inconsistent (e.g., 594.94 for Probes samples vs GT but 84.64 for GT vs GT, while PQMass stays near 9–11). Against MMD (Gretton et al., 2012), PQMass remains stable as dimensionality increases while RBF MMD becomes increasingly noisy and computationally expensive (Fig. 3–4). Against the Schilling (1986) nearest-neighbor two-sample tests, PQMass confidently rejects the null for N(0,I) vs N(0.5,I) with 50 samples (Fig. 22) while both unweighted (p=0.56) and weighted (p=0.71) Schilling tests fail to detect the difference (Table 6). Against the authenticity score (Alaa et al., 2022), PQMass's overfitting p-value (Eq. 7) correlates with authpct in the memorization experiment (Fig. 8), providing a complementary statistical test for novelty.

## Relation to Prior Work

PQMass repurposes two well-established statistical tools — Voronoi tessellation (inspired by the TARP framework for posterior coverage) and the Pearson chi-squared test for multinomial distributions — into a unified generative model evaluation metric. It departs from the dominant paradigm of feature-extraction-based metrics (FID, FLD) and kernel-based two-sample tests (MMD) by operating directly in the original data space, requiring only a distance metric and no auxiliary model training. The paper positions itself as filling a gap left by existing sample-based metrics: FID and precision/recall capture fidelity and diversity but not novelty, while FLD captures all three but relies on Gaussian mixture approximation and feature compression that limits its applicability to high-dimensional or non-image data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PQMass provides a statistically principled (p-value-based) evaluation of generative models that simultaneously captures fidelity, diversity, and novelty without feature extraction, making it applicable to modalities where pretrained feature extractors do not exist (scientific images, time series, protein sequences). Key limitations include the requirement for i.i.d. samples, sensitivity to the choice of distance metric, and the need for sufficient sample sizes for the chi-squared approximation to hold. Open questions include the rate of convergence of the test, extension to correlated (non-i.i.d.) samples, and whether the method's sensitivity to small distributional shifts (e.g., small modes, subtle noise) holds uniformly across all data types and sample regimes.
