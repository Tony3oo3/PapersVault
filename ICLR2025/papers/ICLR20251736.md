## Problem

Existing generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting methods predict single-scale Gaussians from single-scale features, lacking the hierarchical representation needed to simultaneously capture large-scale scene structure and fine texture details, resulting in mislocation, cracks, blurriness, and artefacts. A naive attempt to mix independently generated multi-scale Gaussians fails to improve over prior methods because the scales cannot exchange error-aware information. The paper asks how to construct hierarchical 3D Gaussians in a coarse-to-fine manner with explicit inter-scale interaction for joint optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes two sparse reference RGB images resized to 256×256 along with their SfM-estimated camera intrinsics and extrinsics as input. During training, three target novel-view images provide photometric supervision (MSE + LPIPS) at each of the three hierarchical stages. At inference, only the final fused set of multi-scale 3D Gaussians is rendered to produce photorealistic novel-view images, with no per-scene optimization required.

## Method

HiSplat uses a shared U-Net backbone with a multi-view transformer to extract hierarchical cross-view features at three resolutions, augmented with frozen DINOv2 features for generalization. Stage 1 estimates depth via plane-sweep cost volume matching at low resolution to place large, solid Gaussians as a structural skeleton. The Error Aware Module renders the previous stage's Gaussians, computes pixel-wise error maps against the input images, and feeds these into a lightweight U-Net that predicts bounded depth offsets (scaled by η≈0.1) and Gaussian features for finer stages, enabling targeted compensation. The Modulating Fusion Module applies spatial attention over concatenated multi-scale Gaussian features modulated by error maps to reweight the opacities of coarser Gaussians, effectively repairing regions where large-scale Gaussians are inaccurate. All three stages are jointly supervised with MSE and LPIPS losses during training, while inference renders only the final fused Gaussians.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, HiSplat achieves 27.21 PSNR / 0.881 SSIM / 0.117 LPIPS, surpassing the strongest baseline TranSplat (26.69 PSNR) by +0.52 PSNR and MVSplat (26.39 PSNR) by +0.82 PSNR in the two-view setting. On ACID, HiSplat reaches 28.75 PSNR / 0.853 SSIM / 0.133 LPIPS, outperforming TranSplat by +0.40 PSNR. In zero-shot cross-dataset transfer (trained on RealEstate10K), HiSplat gains +1.05 PSNR on DTU, +0.49 PSNR on ACID, and +3.19 PSNR on Replica over the best prior method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiSplat outperforms all seven baselines on both RealEstate10K and ACID across every metric. Against the strongest Gaussian-splatting baselines, it beats TranSplat by +0.52 PSNR (27.21 vs 26.69) on RealEstate10K and +0.40 PSNR (28.75 vs 28.35) on ACID, and beats MVSplat by +0.82 and +0.50 PSNR respectively. Among NeRF-based methods, the gap is even larger: HiSplat exceeds MuRF (the best NeRF baseline) by +1.11 PSNR on RealEstate10K and +0.66 PSNR on ACID. In zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation, HiSplat's advantage widens substantially: +1.05 PSNR over TranSplat on DTU (16.05 vs 14.93) and +3.19 PSNR over PixelSplat on Replica (27.17 vs 23.98), indicating that the hierarchical representation generalizes far better to out-of-distribution scenes than single-scale alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiSplat extends the generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting line (PixelSplat, MVSplat, TranSplat) by introducing a hierarchical, coarse-to-fine generation strategy for 3D Gaussians, drawing conceptual inspiration from 2D multi-scale feature pyramids (FPN, SPP-net). The depth estimation stage reuses cost-volume plane-sweep matching from MVS (MVSNet, MVSFormer), and Gaussian parameter prediction follows PixelSplat's unprojection pipeline. The key departure is the Error Aware Module and Modulating Fusion Module, which enable inter-scale interaction—allowing finer Gaussians to compensate for and repair errors in coarser ones—rather than generating multi-scale Gaussians independently. DINOv2 frozen features are incorporated for improved cross-dataset generalization, following MVSFormer++.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical Gaussian representation addresses a fundamental representational limitation of single-scale methods: the inability to simultaneously capture large-scale geometry and fine texture details, which manifests as blurriness, cracks, and mislocation. The strong zero-shot cross-dataset generalization (especially +3.19 PSNR on Replica) suggests the error-aware mechanism is scale-invariant and could transfer to practical open-world deployment. Open questions include whether the fixed 3-stage hierarchy is optimal, how the approach scales to more input views or dynamic scenes, and whether the DINOv2 dependency limits deployment in resource-constrained settings. The finding that vanilla hierarchical Gaussians without inter-scale interaction actually underperform single-scale baselines highlights that the interaction mechanism, not mere multi-scale generation, is the critical ingredient.
