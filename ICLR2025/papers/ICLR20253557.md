## Problem

Diffusion probabilistic models produce high-quality samples but require many sequential denoising steps, making inference computationally expensive. While Richardson extrapolation is a proven technique for reducing numerical error in ODE solvers, it has not been applied to DPMs, and its standard formulation assumes uniform time grids that are suboptimal for diffusion sampling where non-uniform schedules (finer steps near clean data) are preferred.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253557/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RX-DPM is a training-free, drop-in sampling algorithm that takes the same inputs as the underlying DPM (Gaussian noise, optionally a class label or text prompt) and outputs a synthetic image at the target resolution (32×32 to 512×512). It requires no additional network function evaluations (NFEs) beyond the baseline sampler's step count, with only negligible overhead from linear combinations of stored predictions. The method is applied every k denoising steps (typically k=2) within the existing ODE solver loop.

## Method

RX-DPM performs Richardson extrapolation on the probability flow ODE by combining two numerical solutions at the same target time step: a single large-step estimate x̂^(1) and a k-step estimate x̂^(k), both computed over the same interval [t_i, t_{i-k}]. The key theoretical contribution is deriving the local truncation error for the Euler method on non-uniform grids, yielding grid-aware extrapolation coefficients λ_j = (t_{i-j+1} - t_{i-j})/h that adapt to arbitrary time step schedules, unlike standard Richardson which uses fixed coefficients. The extrapolated solution x̃^(k) = (x̂^(k) - Σλ_j² x̂^(1)) / (1 - Σλ_j²) eliminates the leading O(h²) error term, improving the global truncation error from O(N⁻¹) to O(N⁻²) at the same NFE budget. The method generalizes to higher-order solvers (Runge-Kutta, Adams-Bashforth) by replacing the error exponent p in the coefficient formula, and requires no extra NFEs because the single-step prediction is recovered from intermediate network evaluations already computed during the k-step solve.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64 (class-conditional), RX-DPM-Solver-3 achieves FID 2.18 at NFEs=30, outperforming DPM-Solver-3 (2.84) and the best DEIS variant tAB-DEIS (2.81). On CIFAR-10, RX-DDIM reduces FID from 7.74 (DDIM) to 6.30 at NFEs=50, and RX-NPR-DDIM achieves FID 3.34 vs. 3.99 (NPR-DDIM) and 3.64 (LA-NPR-DDIM). On Stable Diffusion V2 (COCO), RX-DDIM improves FID from 18.65 to 17.83 at NFEs=50 while maintaining comparable CLIP scores. Across all tested backbones (EDM, DPM-Solver, PNDM, Stable Diffusion), RX-DPM consistently improves FID without increasing NFEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RX-DPM consistently outperforms all compared baselines across the majority of settings. Against DPM-Solver-2/3, RX-DPM-Solver-2/3 achieves lower FID at every NFE (e.g., 3.90 vs. 5.02 at NFEs=12 on ImageNet). Against DEIS variants, RX-DPM-Solver-3 dominates at all NFEs (2.18 vs. 2.81 for tAB-DEIS at NFEs=30), while RX-DPM-Solver-2 is competitive but slightly behind tAB-DEIS at higher NFEs. Against DDIM, RX-DDIM improves FID by 1.4–6.5 points across NFEs on CIFAR-10 and CelebA (Table 5). Against LA-DPM, RX-DPM wins on most datasets but LA-DPM occasionally matches on CIFAR-10 at specific NFEs. Against Heun's method (EDM), RX-Euler excels at lower NFEs while Heun's slightly edges ahead at very high NFEs, motivating the hybrid RX+EDM approach. Against PNDM, RX-PNDM improves FID in most cases, with one exception: F-PNDM on LSUN Church where the baseline degrades with more steps, limiting RX-DPM's benefit. Against IIA, RX-Euler is generally superior across FFHQ, AFHQv2, and ImageNet, with occasional narrow losses on CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Richardson extrapolation—a classical numerical analysis technique dating to 1911—to the ODE-based sampling of diffusion probabilistic models, a context where it had not previously been applied. It departs from standard Richardson extrapolation by deriving grid-aware coefficients for arbitrary (non-uniform) time step schedules, which are essential for DPMs where smaller steps near the clean sample are beneficial. Unlike other extrapolation-based DPM samplers (LA-DPM, DEIS) that combine predictions from different time steps, RX-DPM combines multiple denoised estimates at the same time step obtained via different discretization granularities, and it generalizes from Euler to higher-order solvers (Runge-Kutta, Adams-Bashforth) without increasing NFEs by reusing stored intermediate network evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RX-DPM offers a drop-in, training-free improvement to any existing ODE-based DPM sampler with negligible computational overhead, making it practically attractive for reducing sampling cost while maintaining quality. The consistent FID improvements across EDM, Stable Diffusion V2, DPM-Solver, and PNDM backbones demonstrate strong generalizability. However, the method relies on a linear error accumulation assumption (Eq. 21) that does not hold in general for higher-order solvers, and its integration with SDE-based samplers is only partial, limiting its applicability in stochastic settings. The observation that a hybrid RX+EDM strategy (applying extrapolation selectively in low-noise steps) outperforms either method alone suggests that adaptive scheduling of extrapolation vs. interpolation remains an open direction.
