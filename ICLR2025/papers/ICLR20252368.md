## Problem

Backpropagation through deep sequential models (ResNets, Transformers, RNNs) is inherently sequential, making it the dominant cost in training time and limiting scalability to deeper and longer models. While residual connections solved vanishing gradients, they also create a natural fast path for gradients that existing parallel backpropagation methods (DeepPCR, DEER) fail to exploit, instead incurring cubic complexity in hidden dimension. The paper asks whether the residual architecture itself can be leveraged to approximate backpropagation in a parallelizable, architecture-aware way.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the forward-pass hidden states h_i, the per-layer loss gradients ∂L_j/∂h_j, and the architectural decomposition of each layer into an expensive block g_i (whose Jacobian J_i is only vector-multiplicable) and a cheap residual function r_i (whose Jacobian K_i is identity, diagonal, scalar, or low-rank). It outputs an approximate gradient estimate w_i^k at each layer after k iterations, which is then used to compute parameter gradients ∂L/∂θ_i. The number of iterations k is a user-controlled hyperparameter: k=0 backpropagates only through residual connections, while k≥L-i recovers the exact gradient, giving a continuous speed-accuracy dial.

## Method

Highway-BP decomposes the full gradient as a sum over all 2^L paths through the network, where each path traverses either the expensive Jacobian J_i or the cheap residual Jacobian K_i (Theorem 1). An iterative algorithm (Theorem 2) progressively recovers these paths: at iteration k, the estimate w_i^k includes all gradients flowing through at most k residual blocks. Each iteration has two parallelizable steps: (1) backpropagating through all J_i simultaneously (vector-Jacobian products), and (2) a parallel prefix scan (CumSumProd) that accumulates the results through the chain of cheap K_i matrices in O(log L) parallel time. The key architectural requirement is that K_i must be cheap to multiply, which is naturally satisfied by residual connections in ResNets (K_i=I), pre-LN Transformers (K_i=I), and gated RNNs like GRU/LSTM (K_i is diagonal). Empirically, only 4–10 iterations suffice to match backpropagation quality across ResNets, GPT-2, RoBERTa, and RNNs, yielding 2–3.5× training speedups.

## Results

On ResNet110 (CIFAR-10, L=54 skip connections), Highway-BP matches full backpropagation accuracy with only k=4 iterations, and even k=0 (residual-path-only gradient) achieves 85% top-1 accuracy. For RNNs, Highway-BP reaches the same final accuracy as backpropagation with k=10 iterations on the challenging CIFAR-10 pixel-level task (sequence length 1024), where fixed-point iteration fails. Training speedups range from ×2 to ×3.5 depending on sequence length and k, with the largest gains (×3.5) observed on the longest sequence (L=1024) at k=0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to standard backpropagation, Highway-BP achieves nearly identical final accuracy with a fraction of the backward-pass cost: ResNet110 matches BP at k=4 (vs. L=54 full iterations), and the 3-layer GRU on CIFAR-10 pixel-level matches BP at k=10. Against fixed-point iteration (FPI, Wang & Ragni 2021), Highway-BP consistently outperforms in convergence speed at the same computational budget, because it exploits the residual/gated structure of the architecture rather than treating each layer opaquely. On the hardest RNN task (GRU×1, L=1024, CIFAR-10 pixel-level), FPI fails to converge to BP-level accuracy while Highway-BP reaches it at k=10. The speedup advantage of Highway-BP over FPI is most pronounced at low k values (e.g., ×3.5 vs. ×3.7 for BP at k=0 on L=1024), and both methods converge to the same cost as k→L.

## Relation to Prior Work

Highway-BP extends the parallel backpropagation line initiated by DeepPCR (Danieli et al., 2023) and DEER (Lim et al., 2024), which both use prefix scan algorithms to parallelize the backward pass but require full Jacobian computations, leading to O(Bd³ log L) complexity. The key departure is an architecture-aware decomposition of each layer's Jacobian into an expensive block component J_i and a cheap residual component K_i, which keeps the CumSumProd operation at vector-matrix product cost rather than matrix-matrix multiplication. The method also subsumes fixed-point iteration (Wang & Ragni, 2021) as a special case (k=0 with no residual structure) and differs from ODE/MGRIT solvers (Günther et al., 2020; Moon & Cyr, 2022) by providing an interpretable, path-based gradient decomposition rather than a continuous relaxation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 2–3× training speedup for RNNs with long sequences and the potential to train very large recurrent models (billions of parameters) that are currently impractical due to sequential backpropagation. However, several open questions limit near-term adoption: the method requires residual-like architecture (not universally available), the γ parameter hack needed for transformers (GPT-2, RoBERTa) lacks theoretical justification, and all experiments use models up to 124M parameters with single-GPU evaluation, leaving scalability to distributed settings unverified. The finding that k=0 (pure residual-path backpropagation) already yields reasonable performance is a useful diagnostic for understanding what residual connections actually contribute to training dynamics.
