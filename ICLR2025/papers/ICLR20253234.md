## Problem

Existing activation-steering methods for generative models (ACTADD, CAA, ITI-C, AURA) apply constant bias shifts that do not preserve the internal activation distributions the model was trained on, risking out-of-distribution activations and degraded general performance. Moreover, these methods require unbounded, task-specific hyperparameter tuning (λ, layer selection) and have not been shown to transfer across modalities. The paper asks whether a principled, distribution-preserving intervention can provide interpretable, bounded control over both LLMs and text-to-image diffusion models without per-task tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained model (Gemma2-2B, Llama3-8B, SDXL, or FLUX) and a small set of a few hundred source/target sentence or prompt pairs defining the desired behavioral shift (e.g., toxic vs. non-toxic, generic vs. concept-specific, unstyled vs. styled). At inference time, a standard text prompt is fed through the model, and the intervention is applied to intermediate activations at chosen layers (typically LayerNorm or attention outputs). The output is generated text or an image whose behavior has been steered, with a single bounded parameter λ ∈ [0,1] controlling the strength of the shift from no intervention to full transport.

## Method

Activation Transport (ACT) frames activation steering as an optimal transport problem: for each of the M×L scalar activations, it fits a univariate affine map T(a) = ωa + β in closed form by minimizing squared cost between sorted source and target samples, rather than the simpler mean-shift used by prior methods. Maps are estimated sequentially (causally) layer-by-layer, so each layer's transport is computed on activations already transformed by all preceding layers, respecting the model's computational graph. A key design choice is the interpolation T(a,λ) = (1−λ)a + λT(a), which bounds the control knob to [0,1] and makes λ directly interpretable as the fraction of transport applied, unlike the unbounded bias multipliers in ACTADD/ITI-C. A transport support constraint (only mapping inputs within the observed range [min A, max A]) prevents out-of-distribution activations in mitigation settings. The paper further shows that all prior bias-based steering methods are degenerate special cases of this linear transport framework (ω=1, β = mean difference or classifier output), and that Linear-ACT's affine map can be fused into the model's linear layers at zero inference cost when λ is fixed.

## Results

On toxicity mitigation, Linear-ACT achieves up to 7.5× CLS toxicity reduction on Gemma2-2B (4.17% → 0.56%) and 4.3× on Llama3-8B (5.80% → 1.35%), with minimal impact on PPL (+0.81/+0.49) and MMLU (−1.8/−0.8). On TruthfulQA, Linear-ACT increases MC1 accuracy by +4.95% on Gemma2-2B (21.05% → 26.00%) and +7.76% on Llama3-8B (25.46% → 33.22%), while MMLU drops by only −1.63%/−0.57%. For T2I style control, Linear-ACT raises style presence from ~12% to ~95% while preserving ~80% CLIPScore to the original prompt. Across all tasks and modalities, ACT consistently achieves its strongest conditioning at λ=1 (full transport), unlike baselines whose optimal λ varies by model, layer, and task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Linear-ACT outperforms all three baselines on toxicity mitigation: it achieves 7.5× reduction on Gemma2-2B vs. ITI-C's 5.6×, AURA's 2.0×, and ACTADD's 1.1×; on Llama3-8B it reaches 4.3× vs. ITI-C's 3.6×, AURA's 3.1×, and ACTADD's 1.0×. On TruthfulQA, Linear-ACT gains +4.95% MC1 on Gemma2-2B and +7.76% on Llama3-8B, exceeding ITI-C (+3.48%/+4.65%), ACTADD (+1.96%/+0.73%), and AURA (+0.15%/−0.12%) while incurring comparable or smaller MMLU drops. A critical practical advantage is that Linear-ACT consistently peaks at λ=1 across all models, layers, and tasks, whereas ITI-C requires task-specific λ values (e.g., 8.0 for Gemma2-2B toxicity but 2.0 for truthfulness) and is highly sensitive to layer choice, and ACTADD's optimal λ also varies (0.3–3.0). On T2I style control, Linear-ACT matches or exceeds ITI-C in style presence while better preserving prompt semantics (higher CLIPScore), and it succeeds at concept negation where ITI-C fails entirely. The paper also shows that Linear-ACT best preserves generation diversity (Self-BLEU 0.134 vs. original 0.130, ITI-C 0.144) and that its transport maps produce better distributional overlap with the target than bias-based methods like ITI-C.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the growing family of activation-steering methods (ACTADD, CAA, ITI-C, AURA, RePE) under the lens of optimal transport theory, showing that all prior approaches are special cases of a linear transport map with ω=1 (pure mean-shift). The key departure is learning both the scale (ω) and shift (β) of the affine map, which becomes critical when source and target activation distributions have different variances—a common real-world scenario the paper documents empirically. The interpolation parameter λ is redefined as a convex combination between the original and transported activation (bounded [0,1]), in contrast to prior methods where λ multiplies an unbounded bias. The framework inherits its factorization design from Suau et al. (2024) and its interpolation principle from McCann (1997), but is the first to apply a single inference-time intervention method effectively across both LLMs and text-to-image diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACT provides practitioners with a drop-in, near-zero-overhead controllability tool with an interpretable strength knob (λ∈[0,1]) that consistently peaks at λ=1 across tasks, models, and modalities—eliminating the need for per-model/per-layer grid searches that plague bias-based methods like ITI-C. The distribution-preservation property (validated in Appendix F) addresses a fundamental concern about OOD activations that prior mean-shift methods introduce. Limitations include the assumption of linear transport between i.i.d. unimodal activations (multimodal distributions would require non-linear maps) and dependence on the quality/expressiveness of the few hundred calibration samples. Open questions include extending to joint (non-factorized) activation distributions, non-linear transport maps, and whether the framework generalizes to autoregressive image generation or video models.
