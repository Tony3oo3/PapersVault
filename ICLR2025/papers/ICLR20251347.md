## Problem

Existing Visual Reprogramming (VR) methods applied to vision-language models like CLIP reuse the unimodal vision-model pipeline, inserting ground-truth labels into fixed text templates (e.g., 'This is a photo of [label]') to guide noise-pattern optimization. This label-based approach overlooks CLIP's ability to align fine-grained attribute descriptions with image embeddings, and the similar syntactic structures across template-prompted labels produce near-identical text embeddings, causing misclassification especially in fine-grained or few-shot settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a downstream RGB image (potentially with different spatial dimensions than CLIP's expected input) and produces a class label prediction. The image is zero-padded to match CLIP's input size and combined with a single trainable noise pattern δ via a Hadamard product with a binary mask, so δ only affects the padded border region. Classification is performed by computing a weighted average of CLIP cosine similarities between the transformed image and the k-nearest LLM-generated attributes per class, followed by a softmax over all classes.

## Method

AttrVR replaces fixed template-prompted labels with two types of LLM-generated attributes per class: Descriptive Attributes (DesAttrs) capturing common visual features and Distinctive Attributes (DistAttrs) capturing features unique to that class, both generated by GPT-3.5 with task-specific prompts. At each training epoch, for every sample, the k most similar DesAttrs and DistAttrs are dynamically selected based on the current VR pattern δ, and a weighted aggregation (controlled by λ) of their CLIP similarity scores forms the supervision signal for cross-entropy optimization. This k-nearest iterative updating strategy allows attribute assignments to adapt as δ evolves, fostering sample-specific image-attribute alignment. Theoretically, the paper proves that DesAttrs reduce intra-class variance (Lemma 1) and DistAttrs increase inter-class separation (Lemma 2), jointly improving class separability over label-based VR.

## Results

Using ViT-B16-based CLIP with 16-shot training, AttrVR achieves 77.7% average accuracy across 12 downstream classification benchmarks, outperforming the strongest label-based VR baseline AR (75.7%) by 2.0% and VP (73.5%) by 4.2%. Gains are most pronounced on fine-grained tasks: Flowers (+7.0% over AR), Aircraft (+4.9%), and DTD (+3.6%). AttrVR also consistently outperforms AR across all five CLIP backbone architectures tested (RN50 through ViT-L14), with average improvements of 3.6%–4.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttrVR outperforms both label-based VR baselines across all 12 datasets. Against AR (Tsai et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2023), which pads trainable noise around images, AttrVR gains 2.0% on average, with the largest margins on Flowers (+7.0%), Aircraft (+4.9%), and DTD (+3.6%). Against VP (Bahng et al., 2022), which overlays noise on resized images, the average improvement is 4.2%, and VP notably underperforms even zero-shot CLIP on RN50 (53.2% vs. 53.4%) and ViT-L14 (61.1% vs. 68.7%), suggesting its overlay strategy degrades image quality at certain resolutions. The zero-shot attribute baseline (AttrZS) already surpasses label-based zero-shot CLIP by 6.5% on average, confirming the value of LLM-generated attributes, but AttrVR further improves over AttrZS by 9.5% on average, demonstrating that training the VR pattern remains essential for domain-shifted tasks like EuroSAT and Resisc45.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual reprogramming (VR) paradigm—originally developed for unimodal classifiers like ResNet and ViT—to vision-language models (CLIP) by replacing the standard template-prompted label supervision with LLM-generated descriptive and distinctive attributes. It builds directly on the input VR pipeline (Tsai et al., 2020; Cai et al., 2024b) and the LLM attribute-querying approach (Pratt et al., 2023), but departs from prior VR-for-CLIP work (Bahng et al., 2022; Oh et al., 2023) that simply reused the same fixed text templates as unimodal VR. The k-nearest iterative updating strategy is a novel mechanism that makes attribute selection sample-specific and epoch-dependent, addressing the mismatch between evolving VR patterns and static attribute assignments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AttrVR provides a principled bridge between unimodal VR and VLMs, showing that CLIP's cross-modal alignment capability can be better exploited through fine-grained attribute descriptions rather than coarse label templates. The theoretical analysis (reduced intra-class variance, increased inter-class separation) gives a clear geometric justification, and the consistent gains across 12 datasets and 5 CLIP backbones validate the approach. However, the ~2% average improvement over the best baseline is modest, the method depends on LLM quality for attribute generation, and the theoretical proofs rely on mild assumptions that are not empirically stress-tested. Open questions include whether the iterative k-nearest strategy is truly necessary versus simpler aggregation, and whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP to other VLMs or to non-classification tasks.
