## Problem

Current LLM evaluation frameworks rely on Regular Expression (RegEx) for key answer extraction, which frequently fails when model responses are non-standard or irrelevant, leading to unreliable rankings. Fine-tuned judge models (PandaLM, JudgeLM) offer an alternative but suffer from poor generalization across different LLMs and benchmarks. The paper identifies that the extraction module is the critical bottleneck in the evaluation pipeline and that no dedicated dataset or model exists for reliable key answer extraction and matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252156/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

xFinder takes as input the original question, the full LLM response (which may contain chain-of-thought reasoning), the key answer type (alphabet option, short text, categorical label, or math), and the answer range (set of candidate options). The output is a single extracted key answer: an uppercase option letter, an exact short-text match, a categorical label, a numeric/LaTeX expression, or the special token [No valid answer] when no valid answer can be identified. The model must handle three forms of key answer sentences—Direct, Prompt-wrapped, and Converted-question-wrapped—and correctly identify the answer appearing after any CoT reasoning chain.

## Method

The paper introduces the KAF dataset (26,900 training / 4,961 test / 4,482 generalization samples) constructed by collecting responses from 10+ LLMs across 19 benchmark datasets under 8 prompt configurations (0-shot, 5-shot, with/without CoT, with/without format restrictions), then annotating key answers via GPT-4 self-consistency plus human recheck. xFinder is trained by QLoRA fine-tuning 19 base models (0.5B–8B parameters) using the XTuner framework, with data augmentation that randomly modifies alphabet options (adding/removing choices) and substitutes prompt-wrapped templates to improve generalization. The key insight is that a small 500M-parameter model fine-tuned specifically for extraction outperforms both RegEx (93.42% vs. 74.38% extraction accuracy on the generalization set) and large judge models (97.61% vs. 84.2% judgment accuracy vs. GPT-4), while running ~50× faster and at 0.4% of GPT-4's API cost. xFinder is designed as a drop-in replacement for the RegEx extraction module within existing evaluation frameworks (OpenCompass, LM Eval Harness, UltraEval), preserving the rest of the pipeline while eliminating extraction-induced errors.

## Results

On the KAF generalization set, xFinder-llama38it (8B) achieves 97.61% judgment accuracy and 95.18% extraction accuracy, while the smallest model xFinder-qwen1505 (0.5B) reaches 97.48% judgment accuracy and 93.42% extraction accuracy. In contrast, the best RegEx-based framework (OpenCompass) achieves only 74.38% extraction accuracy and 88.70% judgment accuracy, and GPT-4 (with CoT) reaches 88.42% judgment accuracy. The accuracy gap between judgment and extraction for xFinder-llama38it is just 2.43%, compared to 14.32% for OpenCompass, indicating far fewer spurious correct judgments from extraction errors. In terms of cost, xFinder-qwen1505 evaluates 800 questions for ~$0.02, versus $5.06–$5.15 for GPT-4 API.

## Baselines & Comparisons

xFinder decisively outperforms all three RegEx-based frameworks (OpenCompass, LM Eval Harness, UltraEval) on both extraction and judgment accuracy across all four answer types. Against GPT-4, xFinder-llama38it surpasses GPT-4-as-Judge (84.20%) by 13.41 points and GPT-4-as-Judge-with-CoT (88.42%) by 9.19 points in judgment accuracy, while also achieving higher extraction accuracy (95.18% vs. 69.57%). Among judge models, xFinder beats PandaLM-7B (51.90%) by over 45 points and JudgeLM-33B (78.13%) by nearly 19.5 points in judgment accuracy. Notably, the 0.5B xFinder-qwen1505 model achieves 97.48% judgment accuracy at a cost of ~$0.02 per 800 questions, compared to $5.06–$5.15 for GPT-4 API, representing a >250× cost reduction. In real-world evaluations across 10 LLMs and 14 tasks, xFinder produces stable rankings while RegEx-based frameworks show significant ranking inconsistencies (e.g., InternLM2-1.8B-Chat ranks 1st in UltraEval but 5th in OpenCompass on CommonsenseQA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RegEx-based answer extraction paradigm used in mainstream LLM evaluation frameworks (OpenCompass, LM Eval Harness, UltraEval) by replacing it with a fine-tuned small LLM. It is conceptually adjacent to the judge-model line of work (PandaLM, JudgeLM), but narrows the scope from holistic response scoring to the specific sub-task of locating and extracting the key answer sentence, which the authors formalize with three structural types (Direct, Prompt-wrapped, Converted-question-wrapped) and a CoT-awareness rule. The KAF dataset is, to the authors' knowledge, the first dedicated resource for this extraction task, and the paper positions itself as the first systematic study of 'reliable evaluation' in this narrow sense, building on prior concerns about data contamination and prompt sensitivity without directly addressing those issues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 0.5B-parameter fine-tuned model outperforms GPT-4 by over 20 points on extraction accuracy has direct practical implications: it makes reliable, low-cost answer extraction accessible to any evaluation pipeline, potentially reducing the 'evaluation noise' that currently distorts LLM leaderboards. However, the scope is limited to objective questions with deterministic answers (multiple-choice, math, classification); open-ended generation tasks remain out of reach. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multilingual settings, whether the KAF dataset's coverage of 19 task types is sufficient for long-tail benchmarks, and how xFinder interacts with the other reliability concerns the paper identifies (data leakage, prompt overfitting) that it explicitly leaves for future work.
