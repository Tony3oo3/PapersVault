## Problem

Standard generative model selection identifies the single model with the best evaluation score (FID, KID, etc.), overlooking the possibility that a non-degenerate mixture of models can outperform every individual model on diversity-sensitive metrics. Because kernel-based scores like KID and RKE are convex functions of the distribution, mixtures can strictly improve them, yet no efficient online algorithm existed to find the optimal mixture using the fewest sample queries from the available models.

## Input / Output

The method takes m pre-trained generative models (image generators such as StyleGAN2-ADA, LDM, Stable Diffusion XL, or text generators such as GPT2-XL, LLaMA, PaLM) and a total budget of T sampling rounds. At each round it must decide which model to query, receiving one sample back; the loss involves cross-terms κ(x^(s), x^(t)) between all pairs of generated samples, not just per-sample rewards. The output is the sequence of T generated samples along with the learned mixture weight vector α* over the m models, which can be used to sample from the optimal mixture distribution going forward.

## Method

The key observation is that kernel-based evaluation scores (KID, RKE, MMD) decompose as a quadratic form L(α) = αᵀKα + fᵀα in the mixture weights, where K is the m×m kernel matrix between model distributions and f encodes the linear (quality) term, making the optimal mixture a convex quadratic program. Mixture-UCB-CAB treats the problem as a continuum-armed bandit where each 'arm' is a probability vector α; at each round it solves the quadratic program on a lower confidence bound (using McDiarmid's inequality for concentration) to obtain α^(t), then pulls a model sampled from α^(t). Mixture-UCB-OGD replaces the quadratic program with a single gradient step at the current proportion vector, pulling the arm with the steepest descent direction, yielding O(T²+Tm²) runtime. The paper proves a distribution-free regret bound of O(√(βm log T / T)) for Mixture-UCB-CAB, which is tight up to a logarithmic factor and reduces to the classical UCB bound when the kernel term vanishes.

## Results

On the FFHQ experiment with five generative models, the optimal mixture achieves FID 168.13 and KID 1.273×10⁻², substantially outperforming the best individual model (StyleNAT, FID 185.07, KID 1.442×10⁻²). The proposed Mixture-UCB-OGD algorithm recovers FID 170.11 and KID 1.334×10⁻² online, while Mixture-UCB-CAB achieves FID 170.58 and KID 1.342×10⁻². On LSUN-Bedroom with four models, the optimal mixture reaches FID 149.78 (vs. best individual iDDPM at 154.68), and Mixture-UCB-OGD achieves FID 151.10. For RKE diversity on text-to-image models, mixing FLUX.1-Schnell and Kandinsky 3.0 for sofa images improves RKE from 7.21 (best single arm) to 14.51 (optimal mixture), and mixing three dog-breed generators increases mode count from 1.5 to 3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Mixture-UCB-CAB and Mixture-UCB-OGD against two single-arm baselines: Vanilla-UCB (the standard UCB algorithm from Hu et al. 2024 applied to generative model selection) and Successive Halving (Chen & Ghosh 2024). Both baselines ultimately converge to pulling a single arm, so they cannot exploit the diversity gain from mixing. On FFHQ, Mixture-UCB-OGD achieves FID 170.11, beating the best individual model (StyleNAT, FID 185.07) by ~15 points and approaching the Mixture Oracle (FID 168.13). On LSUN-Bedroom, Mixture-UCB-OGD reaches FID 151.10 vs. the best single arm iDDPM at 154.68. For RKE diversity on text-to-image generators (sofa, dog breeds, red bird), both Mixture-UCB variants consistently outperform Vanilla-UCB and Successive Halving, which collapse to a single model and lose the diversity advantage. The One-Arm Oracle (best single model) is also outperformed, confirming that the mixture benefit is not merely an artifact of the oracle setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UCB-based online model selection framework for generative models (Hu et al., 2024) and successive halving (Karnin et al., 2013) from selecting a single best arm to finding an optimal mixture of arms. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that kernel-based evaluation scores (KID, RKE, MMD) are quadratic in the mixture weights, transforming the selection problem from a linear-reward bandit into a continuum-armed bandit where each 'arm' is a probability vector over models. The Mixture-UCB-CAB algorithm draws on the continuum-armed bandit literature (Agrawal, 1995b), while Mixture-UCB-OGD adapts online gradient descent (Shalev-Shwartz et al., 2012) to the mixture weight space, with the quadratic kernel term in the gradient being the critical new component absent from standard UCB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that practitioners with multiple trained generative models can improve evaluation scores (FID, KID, RKE) by sampling from a learned mixture rather than committing to a single model, with the Mixture-UCB algorithms finding this mixture online with provable regret guarantees. The tight regret bound (O(√(βm log T / T))) matching the minimax lower rate up to log factors provides theoretical assurance. Open questions include deriving a regret bound for the computationally cheaper Mixture-UCB-OGD variant, characterizing precisely when mixtures help versus when a single model is optimal, and extending beyond bounded loss functions. The framework's generality (any convex quadratic function of mixture weights) suggests applicability beyond generative model evaluation to other online selection problems.
