## Problem

Single-view 3D human reconstruction remains challenging because the back and side views are ill-posed without additional priors, and existing approaches either rely on strong 3D supervision (SDF, occupancy) that limits generalization, or use multi-view diffusion models that introduce cross-view inconsistencies and degrade the original input image quality. Additionally, initial SMPL-X parameter estimation from a single image is inaccurate, causing artifacts like bent legs and wrong elevation in reconstructed geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251540/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB human image as input and produces a set of 3D Gaussians (131,072 total across two views, each Gaussian parameterized by position, scale, rotation, opacity, and color features) that can be rendered at 300 FPS for novel view synthesis. A 3D mesh is additionally extracted via dense depth-map rendering followed by TSDF fusion and normal-map-based optimization. During inference, the pipeline also iteratively refines SMPL-X parameters (45 iterations) to improve geometric alignment before final Gaussian prediction.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-branch Human Gaussian Model (HGM) combined with a generate-then-refine pipeline. The UNet branch predicts per-pixel Image Gaussians from concatenated RGB and ray-embedding features, while a parallel SMPL-X branch attaches learnable tokens to SMPL-X vertices, extracts structural features via cross-attention, propagates them through SparseConvNet into a 3D volume, and samples that volume at the Gaussian centers; a fusion transformer (Tr_mix) then combines both feature streams to produce refined Gaussians. To handle the unseen back view, a ControlNet (initialized from ControlNet-tile, fine-tuned on coarse back-view renderings) refines the perspective-projected back-view image using the coarse Gaussian rendering as a conditioning signal, preserving original resolution and content. The refined back view is then fed alongside the original front view into a second HGM instance for final two-view Gaussian reconstruction. Throughout inference, SMPL-X parameters are iteratively refined by minimizing mask and normal matching losses between Gaussian renderings and SMPL-X renderings, with the updated SMPL-X fed back into HGM every 15 iterations to progressively improve geometric accuracy.

## Results

On THuman2.0, HGM achieves 23.54 PSNR, 0.938 SSIM, and 0.0524 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, outperforming the strongest baseline SIFU (22.10 PSNR) by 1.44 dB. On CustomHumans (loose clothes, challenging poses), it reaches 23.84 PSNR and 0.944 SSIM. For 3D mesh reconstruction, HGM attains 2.134 Chamfer Distance and 0.823 Normal Consistency on THuman2.0, and 1.729 Chamfer Distance on CustomHumans, surpassing all fully-supervised baselines despite being trained without any 3D ground truth supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HGM outperforms all 10 baselines across every metric and dataset. In novel view synthesis, it surpasses the closest competitor SIFU by 1.44 dB PSNR on THuman2.0 (23.54 vs. 22.10) and by 3.01 dB on CustomHumans (23.84 vs. 20.83), with LPIPS reduced from 0.0794 to 0.0524. Against multi-view diffusion-based LGM, HGM gains 5.2 dB PSNR on THuman2.0, demonstrating that the generate-then-refine strategy avoids the multi-view inconsistency and resolution degradation inherent to diffusion-based approaches. On HuMMan, HGM (23.86 PSNR) exceeds the best NeRF-based method SHERF (20.83 PSNR) by 3.03 dB while rendering at 300 FPS versus SHERF's 2 FPS. In 3D reconstruction, HGM achieves the best Chamfer Distance (2.134 on THuman2.0, 1.729 on CustomHumans) and Normal Consistency (0.823, 0.834) without any 3D supervision, beating fully-supervised methods like SIFU (2.063 CD) and ECON (2.342 CD). The iterative SMPL-X refinement specifically addresses the bent-leg and wrong-elevation artifacts observed in SiTH, SIFU, and ECON, as visualized in Fig. 5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its core 3D representation and on Image Splatter (Szymanowicz et al., 2024) for the UNet-based pixel-to-Gaussian prediction branch. It departs from the multi-view diffusion paradigm used by LGM and GRM, which generates all views from a diffusion model and suffers from cross-view inconsistency and input-image degradation; instead, HGM uses a ControlNet to refine only the back view rendered from coarse Gaussians, preserving the original front-view fidelity. It also moves away from SDF/occupancy-based methods (PIFu, ECON, SIFU, SiTH) that require 3D supervision, and from NeRF-based human renderers (SHERF, MPS-NeRF) that are too slow for real-time use, positioning itself as a feedforward, generalizable single-view human Gaussian model with SMPL-X structural priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method demonstrates that high-quality single-view human reconstruction (both novel view synthesis and mesh extraction) is achievable without any 3D supervision, matching or exceeding fully 3D-supervised baselines while running at 300 FPS versus ~2 FPS for NeRF methods—a practical speedup that makes real-time AR/VR and gaming applications feasible. The generate-then-refine strategy with ControlNet offers a reusable pattern for incorporating diffusion priors without the multi-view inconsistency problem that plagues current image-to-3D pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic or multi-person scenes, whether the iterative SMPL-X refinement (45 iterations) can be amortized into a single forward pass, and how to handle the acknowledged weaknesses in hand and face reconstruction.
