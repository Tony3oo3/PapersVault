## Problem

Existing long-context benchmarks (e.g., needle-in-a-haystack, ∞Bench) primarily evaluate localized retrieval or summarization over at most ~10 information pieces, leaving LCLMs' ability to perform holistic reasoning—aggregating, comparing, and synthesizing information distributed across hundreds of textual entries—largely unmeasured. No prior benchmark simultaneously controls context length, information density, information positioning, and query complexity, making it impossible to isolate which factors drive performance degradation.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single long-context prompt (4k–64k tokens) composed of natural-language sentences generated from verbalized database table rows, followed by a natural-language question. Relevant and irrelevant rows are interleaved according to a chosen positioning strategy (beginning, middle, end, uniform, or bimodal), with information density held constant at 50% or the relevant amount fixed at 2k tokens. The model produces a free-form natural-language answer, which is scored per-row against the SQL-derived gold answer using GPT-4o-mini as a judge assigning Exact Match (100%), Partial Match (50%), or No Match (0%).

## Method

HoloBench is constructed by adapting text-to-SQL benchmarks (Spider) into natural-language holistic-reasoning tasks: for each SQL query, relevant and non-relevant tables are identified, their rows are verbalized into natural-language sentences via per-table templates, and the resulting context is assembled under controlled positioning and density parameters. Gold answers are generated automatically by executing the paired SQL query on the relevant sub-tables, eliminating manual annotation. The benchmark spans five query types (Aggregation, Max/Min, Join, Comparison, Ranking) and three difficulty levels (Easy, Medium, Hard) across five domains (wine, store, college, flight, soccer), with 90 hand-written questions. This design enables systematic ablation of context length, information amount, information position, and query complexity—dimensions that prior benchmarks explored only in isolation.

## Results

On HoloBench, Llama-3.1-405B achieves the highest average score (56.5%) under constant information amount (2k tokens) settings, while Gemini-2.0 Flash leads under constant information density (50%) with 56.8% average. Performance degrades substantially as context length increases: under 50% information density, Llama-3.1-405B drops from 64.0% at 4k to 30.2% at 64k tokens, and the smallest model (Llama-3.1-8B) collapses to just 1.1% at 64k. GPT-4o shows the most robust long-context behavior, achieving 55.0% wAvg(inc) versus Llama-3.1-405B's 53.5%, indicating superior consistency across longer sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This benchmark paper evaluates 14 LCLMs rather than proposing a new method, so comparisons are between models. Llama-3.1-405B leads on short-context tasks (64.0% at 4k under constant info amount) but GPT-4o is more robust at longer contexts (59.4% at 32k vs. 55.1% for Llama-3.1-405B), reflected in GPT-4o's higher wAvg(inc) score. Model size matters critically: within the Llama-3.1 family, the 8B model achieves only 1.1% at 64k/50% density while the 405B model reaches 30.2%, a 29-point gap. Reasoning-focused models (o1-mini, o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1) show strong short-context performance but degrade sharply with length—DeepSeek-R1 drops from 51.1% at 4k to 11.3% at 64k under 50% density—whereas general-purpose models like Gemini-2.0 Flash maintain more consistent performance (66.2%→48.2%). Gemini-2.0 Flash emerges as the most balanced performer, achieving the highest average under both evaluation settings (59.6% and 56.8%), outperforming all other models by 2–6 points on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Spider text-to-SQL dataset (Yu et al., 2018) and related text-to-SQL benchmarks (Li et al., 2024a) by repurposing their structured database queries and schemas as the backbone for a long-context reasoning evaluation. It departs from the dominant line of LCLM benchmarks (Needle-in-a-Haystack, ∞Bench, BABILONG, FLENQA) that test localized retrieval or summarization over a small number of information pieces, by introducing database-style aggregation, comparison, and join operations over 100+ verbalized rows. The key conceptual departure is framing 'holistic reasoning'—synthesizing and aggregating information distributed across an entire context—as a distinct capability from point-lookup retrieval, and providing the first benchmark that simultaneously controls context length, information density, information position, and query complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HoloBench fills a critical evaluation gap: prior LCLM benchmarks largely test whether a model can find a single fact in a long document, not whether it can aggregate, compare, and reason over hundreds of facts simultaneously. The finding that information density (not raw context length) is the primary performance bottleneck, and that reasoning-focused models (o1-mini, o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1) degrade more sharply with context length than general-purpose models, challenges the assumption that 'thinking' models are uniformly better at long-context tasks. Open questions include developing training strategies that maintain robust aggregation over extended contexts, creating dynamic retrieval mechanisms that adapt to unknown information density, and extending the framework to multimodal or truly open-ended reasoning tasks.
