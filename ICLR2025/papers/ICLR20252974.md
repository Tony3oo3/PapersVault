## Problem

Recent SFDA methods leverage Vision-Language models (e.g., CLIP) as pseudo-supervision, treating their predictions as ground truth. However, ViL predictions are noisy and inaccurate at an unknown rate, and this noise becomes increasingly harmful as the in-training model approaches the ViL space during adaptation. This paper is the first to systematically investigate and correct this proxy noise in the SFDA context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252974/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target images (batch size 64), a frozen pre-trained source model (ResNet-50 on Office-31/Office-Home, ResNet-101 on VisDA/DomainNet-126), and a frozen CLIP model with learnable text prompts initialized as 'a photo of a [CLASS].'. It outputs an adapted target model and, at each iteration, denoised ViL predictions computed at the logit level before softmax. The ViL image encoder backbone (ResNet or ViT-B/32 or ViT-B/16) is fixed and not updated during training.

## Method

ProDe introduces a Proxy Confidence Theory that models how ViL prediction reliability degrades during adaptation: as the in-training model's distance to the ViL (proxy) space shrinks, the fixed proxy error between the ViL space and the true domain-invariant space becomes relatively more impactful. This motivates a proxy denoising mechanism that corrects raw ViL logits by subtracting a weighted difference between source and target model logits (p'_i = softmax(θ_v(x_i,v) − ω[θ_s(x_i) − θ_t(x_i)])), effectively using the adaptation trajectory to estimate and remove proxy bias. A mutual knowledge distilling regularization then exploits the corrected predictions through two complementary losses: L_Apt maximizes unbiased mutual information between denoised ViL and target predictions (with a category-balance constraint to prevent collapse), while L_Ref applies a standard pseudo-labeling objective using the argmax of the denoised ViL output. The correction strength ω and trade-off parameters α, β, γ are fixed across datasets, and the entire procedure is trained for 15 epochs with SGD.

## Results

On the four standard SFDA benchmarks, ProDe-V (ViT-B/32 backbone) achieves 92.6% on Office-31, 91.0% on Office-Home, 91.0% on VisDA, and 85.0% on DomainNet-126 in average accuracy. The weaker ProDe-R (ResNet) variant reaches 89.9%, 88.7%, 88.7%, and 81.5% respectively. In the more challenging open-set setting on Office-Home, ProDe-V attains 82.6% average accuracy, and in the test-time adaptation setting it reaches 76.5%, substantially exceeding all prior TTA methods (best: SAR at 60.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProDe-V outperforms the previous best ViL-based SFDA method DIFO-V by 0.1% on Office-31, 0.7% on Office-Home, 0.7% on VisDA, and a substantial 5.0% on DomainNet-126 (85.0% vs. 80.0%). Against the strongest non-multimodal SFDA baselines (CPD, TPDS, PLUE, GKD), ProDe-V gains 1.8%, 11.0%, 2.7%, and 16.3% respectively in average accuracy across the four datasets. In the open-set setting, ProDe-V achieves 82.6% versus DIFO-V's 75.9% (a 6.7% margin), and in TTA it reaches 76.5% compared to the best prior method SAR at 60.3%. The only near-tie is in generalized SFDA where ProDe-V (84.8% H-accuracy) trails PSAT-ViT (85.0%) by a negligible 0.2%. Notably, the weaker ProDe-R variant (ResNet backbone) is competitive with the stronger DIFO-V (ViT backbone), suggesting the proxy denoising mechanism compensates for backbone capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ViL-based SFDA paradigm established by DIFO (Tang et al., 2024c), which treats CLIP's zero-shot predictions as pseudo supervision for source-free adaptation. The key departure is identifying that ViL predictions are inherently noisy and that this noise becomes progressively more harmful as the in-training model approaches the ViL space during adaptation — a dynamic effect previously overlooked. To correct this, ProDe introduces a proxy denoising mechanism grounded in a proxy confidence theory, and leverages the unbiased mutual information estimator from Ji et al. (2019) for a mutual knowledge distilling regularization that jointly adapts the target model and learnable CLIP prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and previously underappreciated failure mode: blindly trusting ViL model predictions as ground truth in SFDA degrades performance, especially in later adaptation stages. The proxy confidence theory provides a principled (if approximate) framework for understanding when ViL guidance is reliable, and the logit-level denoising correction is a lightweight, generalizable technique applicable to any ViL-assisted adaptation pipeline. Open questions include whether the proxy confidence theory can be made more rigorous beyond the entropy-ratio proportionality, whether the approach extends to other foundation models beyond CLIP, and whether the correction mechanism generalizes to settings where the proxy model is weaker or the domain shift is more extreme.
