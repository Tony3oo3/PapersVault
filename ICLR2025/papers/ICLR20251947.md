## Problem

Existing optimistic least squares algorithms for contextual bandits with function approximation achieve regret bounds scaling as Õ(√(d_eluder(F)·log(|F|)·T)), where the dominant term depends on the reward bound B rather than the actual noise variance, so the algorithm cannot exploit low-variance regimes where each observation carries more information. Prior work on second-order (variance-dependent) bounds for function approximation required either observing the conditional variances or assuming distributional realizability of the noise, both of which are restrictive in practice. This paper is the first to develop algorithms achieving second-order regret bounds under only a mean-reward realizability assumption, with no knowledge of the variance.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a known function class F over context-action pairs, the action set A, and either a known variance upper bound σ² (Algorithm 1) or just a confidence parameter δ (Algorithm 2 for unknown variance). At each round it receives a context x_t and outputs an action a_t selected by maximizing the optimistic value U_t(x_t,a) = max_{f∈G_t} f(x_t,a) over the intersection of multi-scale confidence sets G_t(τ_i) constructed at geometrically spaced uncertainty thresholds τ_i = B/2^i. The resulting regret guarantee depends on the actual conditional variances σ_t² (or their sum) rather than the reward bound B, and the algorithm simultaneously maintains confidence sets at O(log T) different uncertainty scales per round.

## Method

The core technique is an uncertainty-filtered multi-scale least squares procedure: instead of regressing over all historical data, the algorithm computes separate least squares fits using only data points whose uncertainty radius ω(x_ℓ,a_ℓ,G_ℓ) falls below (or within) a threshold τ, maintaining one such fit at each geometric scale τ_i = B/2^i. This filtering is the key mechanism enabling second-order bounds: when the uncertainty radius is bounded by τ, the martingale difference terms in Freedman's inequality are bounded by τB rather than B², so the confidence radius scales with min(τB, B²) + σ² instead of B². For the unknown-variance setting (Algorithm 2), the algorithm estimates cumulative filtered variance W_t^{b_τ} from the least squares residuals at each scale and uses these estimates to calibrate the confidence radii, achieving a regret bound of O(d_eluder·√(log(|F|)·Σσ_t²) + d_eluder·log(|F|)). The regret analysis proceeds by bounding the number of rounds with large uncertainty via the eluder dimension, then integrating over the geometric scale decomposition to convert the sum of uncertainty widths into the final variance-dependent bound.

## Results

The paper establishes the first second-order (variance-dependent) regret bounds for contextual bandits with general function approximation under only a mean-reward realizability assumption. For unknown fixed variance σ, Algorithm 1 achieves Regret(T) ≤ Õ(σ√(d_eluder(F)·log(|F|)·T) + d_eluder(F)·log(|F|)) (Theorem 4.6). For unknown time-varying variances, Algorithm 2 achieves Regret(T) ≤ Õ(d_eluder(F)·√(log(|F|)·Σ_t σ_t²) + d_eluder(F)·log(|F|)) (Theorem 4.10). These bounds improve upon the standard Optimistic Least Squares rate of Õ(√(d_eluder(F)·log(|F|)·T)) by replacing the B-dependent term with a variance-dependent term, yielding sublinear regret even when σ → 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the standard Optimistic Least Squares (Russo & Van Roy, 2013), this paper's bounds replace the B-dependent √T term with a σ-dependent term, so regret vanishes as noise variance σ → 0 rather than remaining at √T scale. Against Zhao et al. (2022), which requires the conditional variances σ_t to be observed by the learner, this work removes that restrictive assumption entirely. Against Wang et al. (2024a,b), which requires a strong distributional realizability assumption (a known parametric noise class), this work needs only mean-reward realizability. The concurrent work of Jia et al. (2024) achieves a matching upper bound of Õ(d_eluder · √(Σσ_t² · log|F|)) for the unknown-varying-variance case, and this paper's Theorem 4.6 matches their lower bound for the fixed-variance case. The key distinction is that this paper's Algorithm 2 achieves the d_eluder-linear (rather than √d_eluder) dependence in the varying-variance regime, which the authors note may be improvable to √d_eluder with a sharper analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variance-aware (second-order) regret bound framework from linear contextual bandits (Zhao et al., 2023; Kirschner & Krause, 2018) to the general function approximation setting, building on the eluder dimension machinery of Russo & Van Roy (2013) and the optimistic least squares algorithm. The key technical departure is an uncertainty-filtered multi-scale least squares procedure that replaces the standard least squares confidence radius (which scales with B²) with a variance-dependent radius, enabling regret bounds that shrink as noise variance decreases. Unlike Wang et al. (2024b;a), which required distributional realizability of the noise, this work only assumes mean reward realizability, and unlike Jia et al. (2024) (concurrent), it achieves matching lower bounds in the fixed-variance case while providing a unified treatment of both known and unknown variance regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide variance-dependent regret bounds for contextual bandits with general function approximation under only a mean realizability assumption, closing a gap between the rich linear bandit literature and the more general function approximation setting. The techniques are explicitly designed to transfer to reinforcement learning, which would unlock variance-aware sample complexity guarantees for a much broader class of RL problems. A notable limitation is that the changing-variance bound scales linearly (rather than as the square root) in the eluder dimension, which the authors acknowledge is likely suboptimal; sharpening this to a square-root dependence is identified as a key open question. The paper also leaves open whether second-order bounds can be achieved for inverse-gap-weighting algorithms like SquareCB, which would complement the optimism-based approach.
