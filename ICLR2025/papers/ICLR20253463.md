## Problem

Chen et al. (2020) proved that message-passing GNNs cannot count arbitrary subgraphs in the worst case, yet empirically GNNs count substructures with surprising accuracy on real-world molecular datasets. This paper asks: under what conditions on the target graph set and pattern can GNNs provably count subgraphs, and can they do so with sample complexity independent of the maximum graph size?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253463/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled undirected graph G (nodes labeled from a finite alphabet Σ, edges unlabeled) and a fixed pattern graph P of radius at most k, and outputs either the number of (induced) subgraph isomorphisms from P into G or a binary indicator of whether P appears in G. In experiments, only atom types serve as node labels, and patterns are small tree or cyclic substructures (e.g., 3- and 4-node motifs). The GNN operates at the graph level via sum-aggregation of node embeddings, requiring no unique node identifiers or higher-order message passing.

## Method

The paper establishes that subgraph counting is node-decomposable into k-local rooted counting functions, and proves that if the graph set is (ℓ,k)-identifiable (i.e., truncated universal covers of depth ℓ uniquely determine ego-nets of radius k), then a GNN with ℓ layers and O(η²·ℓ) parameters can realize any k-local function, with pseudo-dimension bounded by η+1, yielding sample complexity independent of graph size. For tree patterns, the authors develop TREE-COLSI, a dynamic program inspired by color-coding that replaces random node colors with WL colors and detects 'quite-colorful' subgraph isomorphisms (where nodes at distance ≥3 in the pattern map to target nodes of distinct WL colors). They prove a GNN with l+h layers can simulate this DP with O(η²·l + ζ·h) parameters, where ζ bounds the number of distinct DP state transitions. Empirically, they show that on molecular datasets (ZINC, MCF-7, Mutagenicity, etc.), over 97% of nodes are (ℓ,k)-identifiable for ℓ=k+2, and nearly all subgraph isomorphisms are quite-colorful after just 2–3 WL iterations, explaining the strong empirical performance.

## Results

On molecular graph datasets, a standard message-passing GNN achieves near-perfect subgraph counting: on ZINC, AUC reaches 1.000 (pattern 3) and 0.995 (pattern 4) with nMAE as low as 0.009, while on ogbg-molpcba the nMAE is 0.000 across all four patterns. On the more challenging Mutagenicity dataset, AUC ranges from 0.870 to 0.949 with nMAE between 0.043 and 0.167. Empirically, the sufficient conditions for GNN subgraph counting hold in practice: by ℓ=3 WL iterations, the number of WL isomorphism classes matches the number of true isomorphism classes on all seven datasets (ratio ≈ 1.000), and for ℓ=k+2, over 97% of node ego-nets are (ℓ,k)-identifiable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing GNN architectures or subgraph-counting methods; its contribution is theoretical rather than a method comparison. The implicit 'baseline' is the worst-case negative result of Chen et al. (2020), which proves GNNs cannot count arbitrary subgraphs on adversarial graph pairs. The paper's empirical results show that this worst case is essentially never encountered in practice: on all seven molecular datasets, WL distinguishes all non-isomorphic graphs within 3–4 iterations (Table 2), and the (ℓ,k)-identifiability condition required by Theorem 2 holds for >97% of nodes with ℓ=k+2 (Table 3). The TREE-COLSI dynamic program generalizes the star-counting result of Chen et al. (2020, Theorem 3.5) and the spasm-based characterization of Zhang et al. (2024, Theorem 4.5), providing explicit layer-count, parameter-count, and sample-complexity bounds that the prior worst-case analyses do not offer. Figure 4 further validates that for real-world datasets like MCF-7 and ZINC, nearly all subgraph isomorphisms are quite-colorful after just l=3 WL iterations, confirming that the TREE-COLSI algorithm (and its GNN simulation) captures essentially all valid maps in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the worst-case negative result of Chen et al. (2020) by providing sufficient conditions under which standard message-passing GNNs *can* count subgraphs, moving from impossibility to a nuanced positive analysis. It draws on the 1-WL expressivity framework (Morris et al. 2019; Xu et al. 2018) and the universal cover characterization (Krebs & Verbitsky 2015) to define (ℓ,k)-identifiability, a new fine-grained condition on graph sets. The novel TREE-COLSI dynamic program adapts the classical color-coding algorithm (Alon et al. 1995) by replacing random colors with WL colors, and the GNN simulation is situated within the algorithmic alignment framework of Xu et al. (2020). The work also generalizes and contextualizes the characterization of Zhang et al. (2024) on which subgraphs GNNs can count on arbitrary graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically-grounded explanation for why standard GNNs empirically succeed at subgraph counting on real-world molecular datasets despite the well-known worst-case impossibility, a gap that has puzzled practitioners. The (ℓ,k)-identifiability condition and the TREE-COLSI algorithm offer concrete, checkable criteria and an efficient algorithmic pathway, respectively. Open questions remain: patterns for which not all subgraph isomorphism maps are quite-colorful regardless of WL depth are not yet understood, and the paper leaves open why more expressive GNN architectures sometimes outperform simpler ones if expressivity is not the bottleneck. The analysis is primarily validated on molecular graphs, leaving open whether the conditions hold broadly across other graph domains.
