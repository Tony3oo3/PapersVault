## Problem

Debiasing methods such as Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) promise to reduce costly ground-truth annotation by leveraging cheap LLM judge scores, but it remains unclear how much sample efficiency gain is fundamentally achievable. This question is most pressing at the evaluation frontier, where newly released models may outperform the judge model used to score them. The paper establishes a hard theoretical limit: when the judge is no more accurate than the evaluated model, no debiasing method can reduce the required ground-truth labels by more than a factor of two.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small parallel sample of n joint (s, s̃) pairs alongside a larger sample of N proxy-only scores s̃, where both s and s̃ are binary indicators (e.g., correct/incorrect, safe/unsafe, preferred/not-preferred). The joint distribution is fully specified by three parameters: the model's true score b(m), the true positive rate p(m), and the true negative rate q(m), treating both scoring functions as black boxes. The output is an unbiased estimator of E[s(m)] with provably minimal variance, from which correct model rankings follow with high probability as sample sizes grow.

## Method

The core contribution is a Cramér-Rao bound analysis proving that the optimally interpolated PPI estimator (with λ* = Cov(s,s̃)/((1+n/N)·Var(s̃))) achieves the minimum variance among all unbiased estimators for binary evaluations, making it variance-optimal in this setting. The sample efficiency factor τ is then upper-bounded by 1/(1−ρ²), where ρ² is the squared Pearson correlation between s and s̃. The key result (Theorem 6) shows that whenever the judge's agreement rate AG(m) ≤ b(m) — the frontier condition — the squared correlation satisfies ρ² ≤ 0.5, yielding τ ≤ 2 regardless of how many proxy samples N are available. The proof reparameterizes in terms of agreement rate and the gap δ = b − AG, then exhaustively optimizes ρ² over the feasible (q, x, δ) space to identify the global maximum. Empirical validation on MMLU, MT-Bench, and TruthfulQA confirms that sample efficiency gains rarely exceed a factor of two at the frontier, and that high agreement rates alone do not guarantee meaningful gains.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Corollary 7) establishes that when the judge's agreement rate AG(m) is at most the evaluated model's score b(m) (i.e., the judge is no better than the model being evaluated), no unbiased estimator can achieve a sample efficiency factor τ_max exceeding 2, regardless of how many proxy scores are available. Empirically, on MMLU, MT-Bench, and TruthfulQA, τ_max consistently stays below 2 across all judge–evaluated-model pairs at the frontier, often substantially below (e.g., GPT-4 judging LLaMA3-70B on MMLU yields τ_max well under 2). The only exception is when a current SOTA model judges a significantly weaker model (e.g., GPT-4 judging LLaMA2-7B), where τ_max can exceed 2. For strictly better classifiers (Proposition 11), the bound tightens further to τ_max ≤ 1.125.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing debiasing methods in a head-to-head benchmark; instead, it establishes that PPI (with optimal λ interpolation) is already near-optimal among all unbiased estimators (Theorem 5, via Cramér-Rao), so no alternative method can beat the τ_max ≤ 2 bound. Empirically, the paper compares multiple judge models (GPT-4, LLaMA3-70B, LLaMA3.1-405B, Claude) evaluating various target models on MMLU and MT-Bench. Across all frontier comparisons (judge accuracy ≤ evaluated model accuracy), τ_max remains below 2, confirming the theoretical bound. Switching from binary to non-binary proxy scores (using LLaMA3.1-405B's token probabilities on MMLU) consistently improves τ over the binary case, but the gain is modest: τ still stays below 2 when judging Claude 3.5 or GPT-4, and only slightly exceeds 2 for LLaMA3-70B (which is ~5% less accurate than the judge). Stratified PPI (Fisch et al., 2024) was also tested per-stratum on MMLU subtasks; gains above τ = 2 were observed in only 6/57 strata (Claude judging GPT-4) and 1/57 (LLaMA3.1-405B judging GPT-4), confirming that stratification does not meaningfully circumvent the frontier limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) framework of Angelopoulos et al. (2023) and the earlier debiasing approach of Chaganty et al. (2018) for automatic NLP metrics, adopting their interpolation between PPI and the classical ground-truth estimator. However, it departs from prior work by shifting the question from 'can PPI reduce variance?' to 'how much can *any* unbiased estimator reduce variance?'—answering via a Cramér-Rao bound that PPI is near-optimal for binary evaluations. In the broader LLM-as-a-judge literature (Zheng et al. 2024, Gilardi et al. 2023), prior work has focused on identifying specific biases (self-preference, position bias, length bias) or reporting high agreement rates; this paper instead provides a fundamental information-theoretic ceiling on what debiasing can achieve, showing that high agreement is insufficient for both correct rankings and meaningful sample efficiency gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that no debiasing method can beat a factor-two sample efficiency gain when the judge is no better than the evaluated model is a sobering constraint on the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm precisely where it is most needed—at the evaluation frontier. Practitioners should not expect LLM judges to dramatically reduce annotation budgets for state-of-the-art models; the order of magnitude of required ground-truth labels remains unchanged. Open questions include whether stratified sampling or task-specific evaluators (where judging is easier than the task itself) can circumvent the bound, and whether the factor-two ceiling holds in more complex, multi-dimensional evaluation settings beyond binary scores.
