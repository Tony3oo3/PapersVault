## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of message passing neural networks (MPNNs) on graphs could not simultaneously achieve three essential properties: point separation (expressivity), Lipschitz continuity (stability), and compactness of the input space (enabling universal approximation and generalization bounds). Existing metrics either handled only unattributed graphs, lacked separation power, or failed to yield a compact space, leaving a fundamental gap in the unified theory of GNNs on attributed graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253294/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes attributed graphs (graph-signals with node features in a bounded ball B_r^d) or their continuous analogs (graphon-signals) as input and produces a pseudometric value — the DIDM Mover's Distance — computed via hierarchical optimal transport between distributions of computation trees. The distance is polynomial-time computable in O(L·N^5 log N) for graphs of size N and depth L. The MPNN itself outputs a graph-level feature vector, and the theoretical framework yields universal approximation guarantees and distribution-free generalization bounds parameterized only by the covering number of the compact input space.

## Method

The core construction extends iterated degree measures (IDMs) to attributed graphons by replacing the base space from a singleton to the attribute ball B_r^d, so that each node's computation tree encodes both its signal value and its neighborhood structure at every level. The DIDM Mover's Distance is then defined as the unbalanced optimal transport cost between the push-forward distributions of node-level IDMs, with a ground metric that is a product of L2 distances on attributes and recursively defined Wasserstein distances on neighborhood distributions. This metric is proven to satisfy three key properties simultaneously: MPNNs (with normalized sum aggregation) are Lipschitz continuous with respect to it, they separate points (two graphon-signals are close iff all MPNN outputs are close), and the space of attributed graphon-signals is compact. These properties enable a Stone-Weierstrass-based universal approximation theorem and a uniform Monte Carlo generalization bound that requires no assumptions on the data distribution or the number of GNN parameters.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no standard benchmark tables. The main quantitative results are: (1) a universal approximation theorem (Theorem 15) showing MPNNs are uniformly dense in continuous functions over the space of DIDMs and graphon-signals; (2) a distribution-free generalization bound (Theorem 17) of the form |R − R̂| ≤ ξ⁻¹(N)(2C + (1/√2)B(1+√(log(2/p)))) where ξ(ε) = κ(ε)²log(κ(ε))/ε² and κ is the finite covering number of the compact input space; (3) a Lipschitz bound (Theorem 13) with constant C_φ depending only on layer count and update-function Lipschitz constants. The sole empirical evaluation (Figure 2) demonstrates strong correlation between δ_DIDM² and randomly-initialized MPNN output distance on 50 SBM graphs of 30 vertices, with computation time O(L·N⁵·log(N)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing methods on datasets; it is a theoretical contribution that unifies and extends prior frameworks. Relative to Böker et al. (2023), it extends the compact IDM-based metric from unattributed graphons to attributed graphon-signals, recovering separation power for node attributes. Relative to Chen et al. (2022), it achieves compactness of the full input space (rather than restricting to a compact subspace), enabling universal approximation over all attributed graphs. Relative to Levie (2023), it provides a finer-grained metric under which MPNNs separate points (the cut distance is too coarse for separation), while retaining compactness needed for generalization. Relative to Chuang & Jegelka (2022), it extends the tree mover's distance to a compact limit space, enabling both universal approximation and distribution-free generalization bounds that were previously unattainable on the full space of attributed graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several prior threads in GNN theory: the iterated degree measure framework of Grebik & Rocha (2021) and Böker et al. (2023) for unattributed graphons, the WL-distance analysis of Chen et al. (2022; 2023) which achieved separation and Lipschitz continuity but not compactness, and the graphon-signal cut distance of Levie (2023) which achieved compactness and Lipschitz continuity but not separation. The key conceptual move is changing the base space of iterated degree measures from a singleton to the attribute ball B_r^d, which elegantly incorporates node attributes into the hierarchical measure structure while preserving compactness. This resolves open problems explicitly identified in the Morris et al. (2024) position paper, where no single metric simultaneously satisfied all three desiderata (separation, Lipschitz continuity, compactness) for attributed graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified theory providing expressivity, universal approximation, and generalization guarantees for MPNNs on attributed graphs under a single metric, closing a gap that had persisted across multiple prior works each achieving only subsets of the required properties. The generalization bound requires no assumptions on data distribution or number of parameters, which is notable. However, the theory is limited to dense graphs (sparse graphs collapse to the empty graph under the metric), relies on normalized sum aggregation rather than the more common sum/mean/max, and the DIDM Mover's Distance has O(L·N^5 log N) computational cost, limiting practical use. Open questions include extending the framework to sparse graphs, other aggregation schemes, and node-level tasks.
