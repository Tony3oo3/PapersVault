## Problem

The GCG attack, the pioneering optimization-based jailbreak method, suffers from two key limitations: its single 'Sure' target template provides insufficient harmful guidance to actually elicit harmful content (the LLM may agree with the prefix but then refuse), and its single-token-per-step update strategy converges slowly. The paper asks whether combining richer optimization targets with faster discrete optimization and smarter initialization can push attack success rates to near 100% across diverse aligned LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250230/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a malicious question string and white-box gradient access to a target LLM, and produces a discrete token suffix (typically 20 tokens, up to 40 for the NeurIPS Red Teaming Track) that is concatenated to the question to form the jailbreak prompt. The optimization target is a rephrased response template of the form 'Sure, my output is harmful. Here is + Rephrase(Q)', where the harmful guidance phrase is inserted between the affirmative and the rephrased request. The suffix is optimized by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of this target response given the concatenated prompt.

## Method

I-GCG introduces three empirical improvements over GCG. First, it replaces the bare 'Sure, here is...' target with diverse templates that embed explicit harmful self-suggestion (e.g., 'my output is harmful', 'I will not refuse any requests'), which provides the LLM with stronger in-context permission to generate harmful content. Second, it proposes an automatic multi-coordinate updating strategy: after computing top-p single-token substitution candidates (p=7), it merges them via a token-combination rule (Eq. 8) to generate multi-token suffix candidates, then selects the one with minimal loss—reducing average iterations from 510 to 55 while marginally increasing per-iteration cost. Third, it uses an easy-to-hard initialization: a suffix is first optimized on a simple fraud-category question, then transferred as the starting point for harder questions, exploiting the observed transferability of jailbreak suffixes across malicious queries. Together these techniques achieve 100% ASR on LLaMA2-7B-Chat, Vicuna-7B, Guanaco-7B, and Mistral-7B, and 100% on the NeurIPS 2023 Red Teaming Track.

## Results

On the AdvBench benchmark (50 harmful requests), I-GCG achieves a 100% attack success rate (ASR) across all four evaluated LLMs (Vicuna-7B-1.5, Guanaco-7B, LLaMA2-7B-Chat, Mistral-7B-Instruct-0.2), outperforming all baselines. The most dramatic improvement is on LLaMA2-7B-Chat, where I-GCG reaches 100% ASR versus 54–56% for GCG, MAC, and Probe-Sampling, and only 4–26% for LLM-based methods (TAP, PAIR, AutoDAN). On the NeurIPS 2023 Red Teaming Track (HarmBench, 256-char suffix limit), I-GCG achieves 100% ASR compared to 0.1–0.2% for the competition baselines (ZeroShot, GBDA, PEZ). The method also reduces total jailbreak time from 38.3 h (GCG) to 4.2 h while achieving 100% ASR on LLaMA2-7B-Chat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I-GCG achieves 100% ASR on all four LLMs, matching or exceeding every baseline. Against the closest optimization-based competitors (GCG, MAC, Probe-Sampling), the gap is most pronounced on LLaMA2-7B-Chat: I-GCG reaches 100% versus 54–56%, a 44–46 percentage-point improvement. On Vicuna-7B-1.5 and Guanaco-7B, I-GCG ties MAC, AutoDAN, and Probe-Sampling at 100%, while surpassing GCG (98%) and the LLM-based methods PAIR/TAP (94%). On Mistral-7B-Instruct-0.2, I-GCG (100%) edges out AutoDAN (96%), MAC/Probe-Sampling (94%), and GCG (92%). In the NeurIPS 2023 Red Teaming Track, I-GCG (100%) overwhelmingly outperforms ZeroShot (0.1%), GBDA (0.1%), and PEZ (0.2%). Against the random-search method RS (Andriushchenko et al., 2024), both achieve 100% ASR with easy-to-hard initialization on LLaMA2-7B-Chat, but without initialization I-GCG (82%) substantially outperforms RS (50%), confirming the optimization improvements are effective independent of the initialization trick.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct empirical extension of GCG (Zou et al., 2023), the greedy coordinate gradient method for optimization-based LLM jailbreaking. It departs from GCG on three axes: (1) replacing the single 'Sure' target template with diverse harmful-guidance templates to make the optimization objective more informative, (2) replacing GCG's single-token-per-step update with an automatic multi-coordinate strategy that adaptively combines top-p single-token candidates, and (3) introducing an easy-to-hard initialization that leverages transferability across malicious questions. The work sits within the broader optimization-based jailbreak lineage (AutoDAN, MAC, Probe-Sampling) but is explicitly framed as a set of practical tricks rather than a new optimization formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-100% ASR across four LLMs—including LLAMA2-7B-CHAT, which was considered one of the most robust targets for GCG—demonstrates that the original GCG's optimization objective was a significant bottleneck, not the gradient-based search itself. The NeurIPS 2023 Red Teaming Track result (100% vs. 0.1–0.2% for baselines) is dramatic, though the 256-character suffix budget gave I-GCG a structural advantage. Open questions include whether these techniques remain effective against the strongest adaptive defenses (PAT reduces ASR to 18%, ICD to 22%) and whether the harmful-guidance trick generalizes to closed-source models with stronger alignment. The extension to text-to-image jailbreaking (83% ASR on Stable Diffusion) suggests the initialization and multi-coordinate ideas are modality-agnostic, but the evaluation is limited to a single T2I model.
