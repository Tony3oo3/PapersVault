## Problem

Training models on data generated by other LLMs risks model collapse, where performance degrades as synthetic data replaces human-annotated corpora. Prior work largely recommended discarding or correcting synthetic data, but it remained unclear whether synthesized data inherently contains sufficient signal to recover optimal performance, and under what conditions a verifier can successfully extract that signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253230/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of synthesized (input, generated-label) pairs and a verifier model (ranging from an oracle ground-truth checker to a weaker LLM or the generator itself) as input, and produces a pruned training subset along with a scalar proxy p* = 1/(1+ψ/φ) computed before any downstream training. In the eigenvalue task, inputs are 5×5 symmetric matrices tokenized as sign-mantissa-exponent triplets and outputs are 5 eigenvalues; in summarization, inputs are news articles and outputs are summaries scored by ROUGE-1. The proxy p* is derived from the verifier's conditional keep-probabilities on correct versus incorrect labels, making it computable from a small validation sample without full retraining.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical characterization in the high-dimensional, infinite-sample limit using Gaussian mixtures and logistic regression: a verification-pruning strategy parameterized by (φ, ψ) — the probabilities of retaining correct and incorrect synthesized labels — yields a sharp phase transition at the breakdown point p* = 1/(1+ψ/φ), below which the downstream model achieves Bayes-optimal accuracy and above which it is perfectly inverted. This result is validated across three empirical settings of increasing realism: finite-sample Gaussian mixture simulations, transformers trained to predict eigenvalues of 5×5 matrices (where beam search reveals the generator can produce correct answers it cannot self-select via perplexity), and Llama-2-7B fine-tuned on XLSUM news summarization. A key empirical finding is that p* strongly correlates with final performance across all verifiers and dataset sizes, and that a stronger model (Llama-3) is not automatically a better verifier for Llama-2-generated text, because p* depends on the correlation structure between generator, verifier, and ground truth rather than on verifier accuracy alone.

## Results

On the transformer eigenvalue task, training on 10M synthesized samples without verification (p* = 0.5) still underperforms the original generator, confirming model collapse, while oracle verification (p* = 1) nearly doubles accuracy. In the beam search warmup (Table 1), the best solution among 50 beams achieves 60.4% accuracy at τ=1%, versus only 19.2% for the model's own perplexity-based selection — a 3× gap showing the model generates good solutions but cannot identify them. On XLSUM news summarization with Llama-2-7B, oracle selection with just 12.5% of synthesized data surpasses the model trained on 100% of the original labeled data, and self-selection (perplexity-based) outperforms the generator, while Llama-3-based selection performs comparably to random selection despite Llama-3 having a higher Rouge-1 score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four verification strategies rather than external methods: (1) no verification/random selection, (2) self-selection via perplexity, (3) Llama-3 as an external verifier, and (4) oracle selection using ground-truth Rouge-1 scores. On the eigenvalue task, random selection (p* = 0.5) leads to model collapse even at 10× the generator's training data, while oracle verification (p* = 1) nearly doubles accuracy. On XLSUM summarization, oracle selection with 12.5% data beats the model trained on 100% original data, and self-selection outperforms the generator. Counterintuitively, Llama-3 selection performs at the level of random selection despite Llama-3 achieving a higher Rouge-1 score than Llama-2, because p* depends on the correlation between the verifier and the specific generator's error distribution, not just the verifier's absolute quality. The proxy p* consistently ranks all strategies in the correct order of final performance across both experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical framework of Das & Sanghavi (2023) on self-distillation with label noise by introducing a verification-pruning mechanism and deriving a sharp phase-transition characterization (breakdown point p*) for downstream model accuracy. It deliberately departs from the dominant 'avoid or discard synthetic data' paradigm in the model collapse literature (Shumailov et al., 2023; Dohmatob et al., 2024c) by arguing that synthetic data contains abundant recoverable information when paired with even imperfect verifiers. While data selection for original datasets and verifier-based filtering in code/math (Haluptzok et al., 2022; Trinh et al., 2024) are established, this paper is the first to systematically characterize *when* and *how well* selection prevents collapse across a spectrum of verifier qualities, providing a measurable proxy (p*) that unifies these settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The measurable proxy p* offers practitioners a pre-training diagnostic to assess whether a given verifier will be effective, which is practically valuable in the era of increasingly synthetic training corpora. The counterintuitive finding that a stronger model (Llama-3) can be a *worse* verifier than self-selection (Llama-2) for its own generated data challenges the common assumption that 'bigger model = better filter' and has direct implications for data curation pipelines. Key limitations include the restriction to data selection (excluding augmentation, regeneration, or reweighting), the infinite-sample theoretical regime, and the fact that the Llama-3 result is demonstrated on a single task (summarization). Open questions include whether the phase-transition structure persists in more complex, multi-class, or non-linear settings, and whether iterative self-improvement via verification can compound across training rounds.
