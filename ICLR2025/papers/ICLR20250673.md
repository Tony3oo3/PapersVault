## Problem

Most structure-based drug design (SBDD) methods assume protein binding pockets are rigid, ignoring the conformational changes (apo→holo) that are essential for ligand binding and drug efficacy. While molecular dynamics simulation can in principle capture these dynamics, the transition rates between conformational states are governed by intrinsic energy barriers, making exhaustive sampling computationally prohibitive. This paper addresses the gap by learning the apo-to-holo pocket transformation jointly with ligand generation via generative flow models.

## Input / Output

The method takes an apo protein pocket (predicted by AlphaFold2) as input, represented simultaneously at the atom level (positions and types) and the residue level (amino acid identity, frame translation/rotation in SE(3), and five torsion angles on SO(2) tori). It produces two coupled outputs: a holo pocket conformation (updated residue frames and side-chain torsions) and a novel 3D ligand molecule specified by atom positions, atom types, and bond types. The ligand is initialized as Gaussian noise centered at the apo pocket's center of mass with mask tokens for all atom and bond types, and the pocket starts exactly at the apo state, so the flow transports both from a well-defined source to the data distribution.

## Method

DynamicFlow is a full-atom SE(3)-equivariant conditional flow matching model that simultaneously transforms apo pockets to holo conformations and generates ligand molecules from noise, using apo-holo pairs from MD simulations as the natural data coupling. Continuous variables (residue translations in R³, rotations on SO(3), torsion angles on tori) are modeled with Riemannian flow matching using geodesic and wrapped interpolants, while discrete variables (atom types, bond types) use a CTMC-based discrete flow matching with a mask-token-to-data probability path. The architecture is multiscale: an atom-level EGNN with kNN protein-ligand interaction graphs and a fully-connected ligand graph feeds into a residue-level Transformer with Invariant Point Attention blocks that predict frames and torsions. A stochastic SDE variant replaces deterministic interpolants with noisy conditional paths (Gaussian in R³, isotropic Gaussian on SO(3), wrapped normal on tori) to improve robustness and diversity. An additional interaction loss regresses pairwise atom distances in the predicted holo complex to better capture protein-ligand contacts.

## Results

On 50 held-out protein-ligand complexes from MISATO, DYNAMICFLOW-SDE achieves the best Vina Score of -7.65±1.59 (vs. reference -7.84±3.11), the highest High Affinity ratio (52.5%), and the best Complete Rate (73.6%) among all methods. DYNAMICFLOW-ODE achieves a Vina Score of -7.28±1.98 with the best QED (0.53) and SA (0.61) among generated molecules. Additionally, using the holo-like pockets generated by DynamicFlow as rigid inputs to existing SBDD methods (e.g., IPDiff) improves Vina Score from -7.55 to -11.04 and High Affinity from 51.0% to 83.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynamicFlow-SDE outperforms all four baselines on Vina Score: -7.65 vs. IPDiff (-7.55), TargetDiff (-5.09), TargetDiff* (-5.53), and Pocket2Mol (-5.50), with notably lower variance (±1.59 vs. ±5.59 for IPDiff). On High Affinity, DynamicFlow-SDE (52.5%) edges out IPDiff (51.0%) and substantially exceeds TargetDiff (44.0%) and Pocket2Mol (28.6%). DynamicFlow-ODE matches IPDiff on High Affinity (51.0%) but achieves a better Vina Score (-7.28 vs. -7.55) with much lower variance. For pocket conformation sampling (Fig. 5), DynamicFlow-ODE achieves the best minimum RMSD while DynamicFlow-SDE achieves the best Cover Ratio; Str2Str performs worse than a simple noise-injection baseline, likely because it was designed for whole proteins rather than pockets. A key secondary finding is that feeding DynamicFlow-generated holo-like pockets into rigid-pocket SBDD methods (Table 2) dramatically improves results: IPDiff+Our Pocket reaches Vina Score -11.04 and 83.7% High Affinity, far exceeding IPDiff alone (-7.55, 51.0%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022), discrete flow matching (Campbell et al., 2024), and Riemannian flow matching on SO(3) and tori (Chen & Lipman, 2023; Bose et al., 2024) into a single generative framework that simultaneously models protein conformational change (apo→holo) and de novo ligand generation. The multiscale architecture combines an atom-level SE(3)-equivariant GNN (extending EGNN, Satorras et al., 2021) with residue-level Invariant Point Attention blocks (Jumper et al., 2021; Yim et al., 2023b), drawing on the frame-and-torsion parameterization from AlphaFold and the torus handling from Li et al. (2024). The key departure from prior SBDD work (Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff, IPDiff) is the explicit modeling of protein dynamics as a generative process rather than treating the pocket as a fixed input, and the departure from flexible-docking methods (DynamicBind, DiffDock-Pocket) is the joint generation of both the conformational shift and the ligand from a single flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Protein conformational dynamics are biologically essential for drug binding (induced fit, cryptic pockets, allosteric regulation) yet have been systematically ignored in deep-learning SBDD; this paper provides the first unified generative framework that addresses this gap. The finding that generated holo-like conformations dramatically improve existing rigid-pocket SBDD methods (e.g., IPDiff Vina Score from −7.55 to −11.04) suggests a practical near-term pathway: use DynamicFlow as a pre-processing step before established pipelines. Key limitations include purely in silico evaluation (AutoDock Vina, no wet-lab validation), a moderate dataset size (~5,700 complexes), and dependence on MD simulation trajectories for training. Open questions include whether the learned conformational transitions generalize to cryptic pockets not sampled in MD, scalability to larger multi-domain proteins, and whether the stochastic flow's diversity advantage translates to discovering truly novel binding modes.
