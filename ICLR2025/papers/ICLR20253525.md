## Problem

Reconstructing pedestrian motion from dynamic sensors is critical for autonomous driving safety, but data collection faces two major barriers: achieving precise global-coordinate annotations in real-world settings is technically difficult and limits dataset scale, and capturing rare but critical scenarios (collisions, blind-spot emergence) poses safety and cost concerns. Existing datasets lack simultaneous multi-perspective, multi-modal coverage (third-person video, egocentric video, LiDAR) with accurate ground-truth labels needed to model pedestrian intention in diverse outdoor scenes.

## Input / Output

Each of the 12,138 sequences provides 6 third-person RGB videos from moving vehicles at varying speeds, 1 egocentric RGB video from a VR headset, 2 LiDAR point-cloud sequences, and synchronized camera extrinsics, all at an average 6.72 FPS across 12 urban scenarios and 7 interactive objects. Ground-truth annotations include SMPL-X body parameters, 3D/2D skeletons, bounding boxes, and semantic LiDAR labels, all in a unified global coordinate system. The three benchmark tasks produce 3D pedestrian meshes in world coordinates (third-person), head pose and full-body motion (egocentric), and 3D keypoints from fused RGB-LiDAR (multimodal).

## Method

The PMR dataset is collected via a mixed-reality platform where volunteers wearing VR headsets and optical MoCap suits interact with virtual urban scenes rendered in Unreal Engine 4, while the CARLA simulator drives vehicles and generates sensor data (RGB cameras, LiDAR) in real time. MoCap-driven avatars ensure pedestrian motions are physically realistic and naturally responsive to vehicle behavior, and real-world objects (suitcases, handbags) are tracked via MoCap and reflected in the virtual environment to enrich human-object interactions. SMPL-X body parameters are extracted from raw MoCap recordings using MoSh++, providing labor-free, frame-accurate ground truth aligned with the global coordinate system. The benchmark evaluates three parallel tasks—global mesh recovery from moving cameras (comparing camera-space methods like ROMP and PHALP+ against world-space methods like SLAHMR and GLAMR), egocentric head-pose and full-body estimation via EgoEgo, and multimodal RGB-LiDAR keypoint estimation via a modified LPFormer—along with a cross-dataset domain-gap study using BEVStereo on nuScenes.

## Results

On the PMR dataset, SLAHMR achieves the best RGB-only world-space performance with W-MPJPE of 378.4 mm and WA-MPJPE of 183.9 mm, substantially outperforming TRACE (1258.8 mm / 549.7 mm) and GLAMR (702.1 mm / 320.7 mm). The modified LPFormer* with combined LiDAR+RGB input dramatically reduces errors to W-MPJPE 71.3 mm, WA-MPJPE 60.4 mm, and PA-MPJPE 57.1 mm, far surpassing all RGB-only baselines. Camera-space methods (ROMP, HUMAN4D, HybrIK, PHALP+) achieve competitive local pose accuracy (PA-MPJPE 62–67 mm) but fail catastrophically on global trajectory metrics (W-MPJPE 436–2084 mm), confirming the necessity of world-coordinate reconstruction for moving-camera scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four camera-space baselines (ROMP, HUMAN4D, HybrIK, PHALP+), PHALP+ achieves the best PA-MPJPE (62.5 mm) but all four produce W-MPJPE values above 436 mm, confirming they cannot recover global trajectories from moving cameras. Among world-space RGB methods, SLAHMR (378.4 mm W-MPJPE) outperforms GLAMR (702.1 mm) by ~46% and TRACE (1258.8 mm) by ~70%, though at the cost of higher PA-MPJPE (73.5 mm vs. 66.7 mm for TRACE), reflecting the trade-off between global trajectory accuracy and local pose fidelity. The modified LPFormer* with LiDAR+RGB input dominates all RGB-only methods by a wide margin, reducing W-MPJPE from 378.4 mm (SLAHMR) to 71.3 mm and PA-MPJPE from 73.5 mm to 57.1 mm, demonstrating the substantial benefit of multi-modal fusion. For the egocentric task (Table 5), EgoEgo on PMR yields MPJPE of 192.3 mm and Acc. Err. of 21.6 mm, which is worse than on ARES (121.1 mm / 6.2 mm) and Kinpoly-MoCap (125.9 mm / 8.0 mm), attributed to the VR-to-real domain gap. In the domain-gap experiment (Table 6), mixing 40% PMR data with 60% nuScenes improves 3D pedestrian detection AP from 0.088 to 0.094 and reduces ATE from 0.919 to 0.875, validating that synthetic mixed-reality data can benefit real-world downstream tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of 3D human motion datasets (H36M, SLOPER4D, RICH) and pedestrian interaction datasets (PIE, Euro-PVI, nuScenes, Waymo) by bridging a critical gap: prior datasets either provide real-world footage without accurate global pose annotations or synthetic data without genuine human behavioral responses. The mixed-reality pipeline (VR HMD + optical MoCap + CARLA/UE4) draws on simulation-based autonomous driving research (Dosovitskiy et al., 2017) and motion capture retargeting (MoSh++, SMPL-X) to produce labor-free, globally-consistent annotations. For the reconstruction task itself, the paper builds on the moving-camera HMR lineage (GLAMR, SLAHMR, TRACE) and egocentric pose estimation (EgoEgo, Kinpoly-MoCap), but is the first to benchmark these methods jointly across third-person vehicle cameras, egocentric VR video, and LiDAR in a unified pedestrian-vehicle interaction setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PMR provides the first large-scale resource (12,138 sequences, 54 subjects, 12 scenarios) where pedestrian intention can be studied through synchronized multi-perspective and multi-modal observations, including rare and dangerous scenarios (collisions, blind-spot emergence) that are prohibitively expensive or unsafe to capture in the wild. The finding that adding a second moving camera can degrade SLAHMR's performance when the primary camera is also moving highlights an open challenge in multi-view global pose estimation that the dataset is well-positioned to address. The domain gap evaluation showing that 40% PMR data improves nuScenes 3D pedestrian detection is encouraging but limited to a single downstream task; broader sim-to-real validation and the development of end-to-end global HMR models (as opposed to multi-stage optimization) remain key open questions for the community.
