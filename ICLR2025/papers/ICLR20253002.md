## Problem

State-of-the-art diffusion models for text-to-image generation still fail at basic compositional tasks such as correct attribute binding, object counting, and spatial relationships. Existing remedies either assume a fixed prespecified compositional structure (limiting generalization) or rely on contrastive text prompts without corresponding hard-negative images, leaving a gap in how models can be systematically trained to understand compositionality from simple to complex scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253002/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a natural-language text prompt and produces a 768×768 RGB image via the fine-tuned Stable Diffusion v2 pipeline. During training, the model consumes contrastive image pairs (x⁺, x⁻) each associated with a single positive caption t⁺, where the negative image differs from the positive only in the targeted compositional attribute (e.g., color swap, count change, spatial reordering). The contrastive signal is computed in the latent feature space of the noise estimator at each diffusion timestep, not in pixel space.

## Method

EVOGEN introduces a three-stage curriculum contrastive fine-tuning procedure on Stable Diffusion: Stage-I trains on single-object attribute/action/scene compositions, Stage-II on two-object attribute binding and spatial/non-spatial relationships, and Stage-III on complex multi-object scenes with multiple attributes per object. The contrastive objective is a binary InfoNCE variant that pushes the cosine similarity between the positive image's projected latent feature and the CLIP text embedding up while pushing the negative image's similarity down, applied at every diffusion timestep. The supporting dataset CONPAIR (15,400 pairs) is constructed by GPT-4-generated prompts spanning eight compositional categories, with images produced by diffusion models and quality-controlled via an LLaVA-based VQA checker that decomposes each prompt into verifiable questions; a reverse-alignment strategy revises captions when all candidate images fail alignment, and MagicBrush editing is used for color/texture negatives to ensure minimal visual differences.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, EVOGEN achieves 71.04 (Color), 54.57 (Shape), 72.34 (Texture), 21.76 (Spatial), 33.08 (Non-Spatial), and 42.52 (Complex), outperforming all baselines on 5 out of 6 categories. On the Gen-AI benchmark, EVOGEN-SD3-Medium attains the best advanced-prompt average of 0.72, surpassing DALL-E 3 (0.65) and SD3-Medium (0.68). On the Attn-Exct benchmark, EVOGEN achieves the highest CLIP similarity scores across all three prompt types (0.84 Animal-Animal, 0.86 Animal-Obj, 0.85 Obj-Obj), beating CONFORM (0.82/0.85/0.82) and Attn-Exct v2 (0.80/0.83/0.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVOGEN outperforms the strongest competing methods—PixArt-α and MARS—on 5 of 6 T2I-CompBench categories, with the largest margins in Texture (+1.9 over PixArt-α, +1.1 over MARS) and Complex (+1.35 over PixArt-α, +2.03 over MARS). The only category where EVOGEN trails is Shape, where PixArt-α leads by 1.25 points (55.82 vs. 54.57). Against the base Stable Diffusion v2, EVOGEN yields substantial gains across all categories, most notably in Color (+20.39), Texture (+23.12), and Spatial (+8.34). On the Gen-AI benchmark, EVOGEN-SD3-Medium achieves the best advanced-prompt score (0.72 avg), beating DALL-E 3 (0.65) by 0.07 and SD3-Medium (0.68) by 0.04, though it is slightly behind DALL-E 3 on basic prompts (0.89 vs. 0.90). On the Attn-Exct benchmark, EVOGEN surpasses CONFORM by 0.02–0.03 in CLIP similarity across all three prompt types, confirming consistent improvements in attribute binding for object-object compositions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior compositional T2I approaches (Composable Diffusion, StructureDiffusion, Attend-and-Excite) that assume a fixed prespecified compositional structure, instead proposing curriculum-based contrastive fine-tuning that progressively builds compositional understanding from simple to complex scenarios. It also distinguishes itself from existing contrastive image datasets (COLA, BISON, Winoground) by generating synthetic positive-negative pairs with minimal visual differences, ensuring the model focuses on specific compositional discrepancies rather than broad visual dissimilarities. The method combines three established ideas—curriculum learning (Bengio et al., 2009), InfoNCE contrastive loss, and LLM/VQA-assisted data curation—into a unified pipeline (EVOGEN + CONPAIR) that had not previously been applied to compositional T2I generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Compositional understanding (correct attribute binding, counting, spatial relationships) remains a fundamental weakness of state-of-the-art T2I models, and this paper provides a practical, reproducible recipe—curriculum contrastive fine-tuning on a curated 15k-pair dataset—that yields consistent gains across multiple benchmarks without architectural changes. The finding that synthetic minimal-difference contrastive pairs outperform real-image contrastive datasets (COLA, BISON) suggests that controlling the 'hardness' of negatives is more important than photorealism, a principle that could generalize to other fine-tuning settings. Open questions include whether the curriculum paradigm transfers to video generation or 3D synthesis, whether the approach scales beyond 15k pairs, and whether the slight aesthetic quality trade-off observed in user studies can be mitigated.
