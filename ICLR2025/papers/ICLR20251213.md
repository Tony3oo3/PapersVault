## Problem

Diffusion Probabilistic Models suffer from a distribution mismatch (exposure bias) between training and inference: training uses ground-truth noisy data while inference feeds model-generated intermediate states back as input, causing accumulated errors that degrade sample quality especially at low NFEs. Existing remedies either impose strong Gaussian assumptions on the mismatch distribution or require expensive additional computations. The paper shows this gap can be closed by a simple, theoretically grounded adversarial training procedure derived from Distributionally Robust Optimization.

## Input / Output

For DPM experiments, the model takes Gaussian noise (plus an optional class label for ImageNet) and produces 32×32 or 64×64 RGB images via multi-step denoising (5–50 NFEs with IDDPM, DDIM, DPM-Solver, or ES samplers). For the Latent Consistency Model, the input is a text prompt combined with Gaussian noise in latent space, producing 512×512 RGB images in 1–8 steps. During AT training, an additional bounded perturbation δ (‖δ‖ ≤ η) is injected into the noisy input, and the network must predict the adjusted target ε_t + δ/√(1−ᾱ_t) rather than plain noise.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is proving that the DPM ELBO's distribution mismatch is equivalent to a DRO problem: minimizing the worst-case KL divergence over all input distributions within a KL-ball around the ground-truth noise distribution. This DRO objective is then shown (Theorem 1) to reduce to a perturbed noise-prediction loss where a bounded perturbation δ is added to the noisy input and the target is shifted by δ/√(1−ᾱ_t). In practice, the authors implement this via efficient (Free-AT-style) adversarial training: each gradient ascent step on δ is interleaved with a model parameter update, avoiding the extra backpropagations of PGD and yielding a 2.6× speedup. The same framework extends to Consistency Models, where the perturbation is applied to the ODE-solver output Φ̂_t(x_{t+1}, ε_φ) and the consistency distillation loss is maximized over ‖δ‖ ≤ η, with Theorem 3 guaranteeing that the resulting W₁ distance to the target distribution is bounded by √(T·L̂_CD^Adv + o(1)).

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64 with DPM-Solver at 5 NFEs, ADM-AT reduces FID from 27.72 (original ADM) to 17.36, a 37% improvement. On CIFAR-10 32×32, ADM-AT achieves FID 18.40 at 5 NFEs with DPM-Solver vs. 23.95 for the original model. On MS-COCO 512×512 text-to-image generation, LCM-AT improves FID from 25.43 to 23.34 at 1 step and from 12.62 to 10.68 at 8 steps, while maintaining or slightly improving CLIP scores (e.g., 30.47 → 30.53 at 8 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ADM-IP (Ning et al., 2023), which adds Gaussian perturbation during training to simulate exposure bias, ADM-AT wins decisively across all samplers and NFEs on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. The gap is especially large at low NFEs: on ImageNet with DPM-Solver at 5 NFEs, ADM-AT achieves FID 17.36 while ADM-IP degrades to 32.43 (worse than the original 27.72). At higher NFEs (20–50), ADM-IP also underperforms the original model on DPM-Solver (e.g., 9.16 vs. 4.69 at 20 NFEs), whereas ADM-AT consistently improves. Against the original LCM baseline (Luo et al., 2023) on MS-COCO 512×512, LCM-AT improves FID at every sampling step (e.g., 23.34 vs. 25.43 at 1 step; 10.31 vs. 11.61 at 4 steps) while matching or slightly exceeding CLIP scores (30.53 vs. 30.47 at 8 steps), confirming that AT does not harm text-image alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established DRO–AT equivalence from classification (Shapiro 2017; Madry 2018; Shafahi 2019) and transplants it into the diffusion generative modeling setting, where prior work on distribution mismatch (Ning et al. 2023; Li & van der Schaar 2024; Ren et al. 2024) either imposed strong Gaussian assumptions on the mismatch distribution or required expensive per-step error minimization. The key departure is a clean theoretical derivation showing that the DPM's ELBO, when made distributionally robust over a KL-ball around the true intermediate distribution, reduces exactly to a perturbed noise-prediction objective (Theorem 1), and that the same logic extends to the consistency distillation loss of CMs. By adopting the Free-AT implementation (Shafahi et al. 2019), the method keeps training cost comparable to standard diffusion training, in contrast to the multi-step PGD or ODE-solver-based baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, drop-in training recipe that consistently improves FID across four samplers (IDDPM, DDIM, DPM-Solver, ES) and both DPM and CM, with the largest gains at low NFEs where mismatch is most severe. A notable practical finding is that the naive Gaussian-perturbation baseline (ADM-IP) can actually degrade quality substantially (e.g., FID 43.83 vs. 23.95 at 5 NFEs on CIFAR-10 DPM-Solver), underscoring that the perturbation structure matters. Open questions include the optimal choice of the perturbation radius η (currently tuned per dataset), whether the approach composes with other recent sampling accelerators, and whether the DRO→AT equivalence extends to flow-matching or rectified-flow formulations. The fine-tuning protocol, while practical, limits direct comparison with from-scratch SOTA diffusion models.
