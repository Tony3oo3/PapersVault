## Problem

Standard neural network pruning replaces each dense weight matrix with a single sparse matrix, but this representation is suboptimal at a given sparsity budget. The paper asks whether factorizing each weight matrix into a product of two sparse matrices (sharing the same total non-zero budget) can better preserve layer behavior and downstream accuracy, and whether a practical algorithm can solve this NP-hard factorization problem at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252944/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained weight matrix W (n×m), a small set of calibration inputs X (b×n), and a target total non-zero budget z, and produces two sparse factor matrices A (n×n) and B (n×m) whose product AB approximates W with ||A||₀ + ||B||₀ ≤ z. For LLMs, W is first scaled row-wise by input feature norms before factorization, then A is rescaled back. The sparsity budget is split between the two factors (typically ~1/3 to A and ~2/3 to B), and the output replaces one dense layer with two sequential sparse layers, keeping total FLOPs equal to a single sparse layer with the same non-zero count.

## Method

DSF solves the projection problem min ||AB − W||_F subject to sparsity constraints via alternating minimization: fixing one factor, the other is optimized as an L0-constrained linear regression using an ADMM solver with heuristic mask selection (gradual cubic magnitude pruning in early iterations). Two key heuristics improve convergence: warm-starting ADMM with dual variables from the previous outer iteration, and an annealing schedule that starts with a small penalty ρ₀ (cubic ramp from 0 to 1) to escape local optima. For layer-wise pruning, the algorithm first solves the projection problem (with input-norm scaling for LLMs), then fixes all sparsity masks and runs a final ADMM pass minimizing ||XW − XAB||²; the inner A-update requires solving a Sylvester equation via eigendecomposition of XᵀX and BBᵀ. Initialization uses A = I and B = magnitude-pruned W, and the total non-zero count across both factors matches the target pruning budget exactly.

## Results

On Wikitext-2 perplexity for layer-wise pruning, DSF achieves 4.87 on LLaMA2-13B at 50% density, outperforming the dense LLaMA2-7B (5.12) — the first time a uniformly layer-wise pruned model beats a smaller dense model. At 30% density on LLaMA2-7B, DSF reaches 8.01 perplexity versus 17.51 for ADMM, 26.64 for SparseGPT, and 74.41 for Wanda. On ImageNet with ResNet-50, DSF achieves 74.95% top-1 accuracy at 4× FLOP reduction, surpassing OBC (74.05%). On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20-32 at 10% density, DSF without fine-tuning reaches 84.1% versus 51.1% for magnitude pruning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSF consistently outperforms all three LLM pruning baselines (Wanda, SparseGPT, ADMM) across every model size and sparsity level in Table 1, with the gap widening dramatically at higher sparsity (e.g., 8.01 vs. 17.51 perplexity at 30% on LLaMA2-7B). Against Optimal Brain Compression on ImageNet ResNet-50, DSF wins at every sparsity level, with the advantage growing from +0.13% at 2× FLOP reduction to +0.90% at 4× (74.95% vs. 74.05%). On CIFAR-10 and ImageNet fine-tuning experiments, DSF surpasses both magnitude pruning and cyclical pruning (Srinivas et al., 2022), reaching 75.78% on ImageNet at 20% density versus 75.43% for magnitude+FT and 75.3% for cyclical pruning. In the weight projection ablation (Appendix A.2), DSF also outperforms palm4msa (Le Magoarou & Gribonval, 2016) and Monarch decomposition (Dao et al., 2022) across all tested matrix sizes. The only scenario where DSF shows a modest cost is storage: it requires ~0.4 GiB more than single-mask pruning for LLaMA2-7B at 50% density, though the perplexity gain far exceeds this overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the layer-wise pruning paradigm (OBC, SparseGPT, Wanda, ADMM pruning) by replacing the standard single-sparse-matrix approximation with a product of two sparse matrices, a formulation related to but distinct from Monarch matrices and low-rank factorization. The algorithm builds directly on Boza's (2024) ADMM-based sparse regression solver, applying it within an alternating minimization loop with warm-starting and a cubic annealing schedule to escape local optima. While sparse matrix factorization has been studied in signal processing (Le Magoarou & Gribonval, 2016; Le et al., 2021), its application to neural network compression had seen only limited practical success (Giffon et al., 2021), making this the first to achieve state-of-the-art results at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 50%-pruned LLaMA2-13B outperforms the dense LLaMA2-7B on perplexity is practically significant for LLM deployment, suggesting that compression structure matters as much as parameter count. However, the approach requires manually allocating sparsity per layer and per factor, and it remains unclear how to integrate DSF with gradual pruning schedules that fine-tune between pruning steps. The 10–20% inference overhead from two sparse multiplications and the additional mask storage (though modest) are practical trade-offs that may limit adoption in latency-sensitive settings.
