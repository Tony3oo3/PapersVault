## Problem

Standard average-case calibration of LM confidence scores can be misleading in generative QA, where a model may be well-calibrated overall yet severely miscalibrated for specific topic groups or user interests. This paper introduces QA-calibration, a stronger notion that requires calibration to hold conditionally on groups of semantically similar question-answer pairs, and provides the first post-hoc methods with distribution-free guarantees for achieving it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a question, an LM-generated answer, and a confidence score elicited via prompting (e.g., Verb1S-Top1 or Ling1S-Top1), along with a calibration dataset of (question, answer, confidence, correctness-label) tuples. It outputs a discretized confidence score that is calibrated within the specific QA partition (defined by a kd-tree over DistilBERT embeddings) containing the input pair. The discretization is by construction, since each partition's calibrator maps inputs to a finite set of bin-level probability estimates, making the scores directly interpretable and assessable.

## Method

The paper defines QA-calibration via a fixed mapping β: Q×A → S that partitions the QA space into groups, instantiated as an embed-then-bin pipeline using DistilBERT [CLS] embeddings and a kd-tree with bounded spaces. Two post-hoc calibrators are proposed: (1) QA Binning (QAB), which fits a per-partition UMD histogram binning calibrator on the calibration data within each β-group; and (2) Hierarchical Scaling QA Binning (HS-QAB), which first fits a hierarchical logistic regression with partial pooling (random intercepts and slopes per partition) to produce fitted values as proxy ground truth, then applies per-partition UMD binning on those fitted values. The hierarchical model's partial pooling is key: it shares information across partitions to stabilize estimates in groups with few data points, which becomes critical as the kd-tree depth increases. A distribution-free guarantee is established: for any distribution, the calibrator is (ε,α)-conditionally QA-calibrated with ε = √(log(2N/bα)/(2(b−1))) + ν, where b is the minimum points per bin and ν captures label misspecification.

## Results

On MMLU (the primary benchmark), the proposed methods reduce QA-calibration error CE(h;β) from 0.53–0.64 (no recalibration) to 0.15–0.17, a 70–75% reduction, while also outperforming standard post-hoc baselines (UMD, Platt scaling, scaling-binning) which achieve CE(h;β) of 0.25–0.39. For selective QA (AUAC), HS-QAB achieves up to 0.306 on MMLU/Mistral/Verb1S-Top1, a ~20% improvement over the no-recalibration baseline (0.255), while the no-recalibration baseline sometimes matches or slightly exceeds the proposed methods on AUAC (e.g., 0.269 vs. 0.269 for MMLU/Mistral/Ling1S-Top1). Across all five datasets and both LLMs (Mistral, Gemma), the proposed methods consistently achieve the lowest CE(h;β), with HS-QAB generally performing best among the three variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the no-recalibration baseline (None, Tian et al. 2023), the proposed methods achieve a dramatic reduction in QA-calibration error: e.g., HS-QAB reaches CE(h;β)=0.149 vs. 0.639 for None on MMLU/Mistral/Verb1S-Top1 (a 77% reduction). However, on AUAC the picture is more nuanced: None sometimes matches or slightly exceeds the proposed methods (e.g., AUAC 0.269 for None vs. 0.269 for HS-QAB on MMLU/Mistral/Ling1S-Top1), though HS-QAB achieves up to 20% higher AUAC in other settings (0.306 vs. 0.255 on MMLU/Mistral/Verb1S-Top1). Against UMD histogram binning (B, Gupta & Ramdas 2021), the proposed methods consistently win on both metrics: CE(h;β) of 0.15–0.17 vs. 0.35–0.39, and AUAC of 0.20–0.35 vs. 0.13–0.14. Against Platt scaling (S, Platt 1999), the proposed methods again dominate: CE(h;β) of 0.15–0.17 vs. 0.23–0.26, and AUAC of 0.20–0.35 vs. 0.12–0.14. Against scaling-binning (S-B, Kumar et al. 2019), the gap is largest: CE(h;β) of 0.15–0.17 vs. 0.36–0.39, and AUAC of 0.20–0.35 vs. 0.13–0.14. The key takeaway is that standard post-hoc calibrators (UMD, Platt, S-B) optimize average-case calibration and ignore the β-partition structure, resulting in substantially worse QA-calibration error and selective QA performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the posthoc calibration literature (histogram binning via UMD, Platt scaling, scaling-binning) to the generative QA setting by introducing a group-conditional calibration target. Conceptually, it draws on group-wise calibration for classifiers (Kleinberg et al., 2017; Pleiss et al., 2017) but adapts the grouping mechanism to the open-ended QA space via DistilBERT embeddings and kd-tree partitioning, rather than pre-specified covariates like race or gender. The hierarchical logistic regression with partial pooling (Goldstein, 2011) is repurposed to handle variable data density across embedding-induced partitions, and the UMD subroutine (Gupta & Ramdas, 2021) is applied per-partition to yield distribution-free guarantees. The paper positions itself as a posthoc layer on top of existing confidence elicitation prompts (Tian et al., 2023; Xiong et al., 2024), which it treats as uncalibrated inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling case that average-case calibration is insufficient for generative QA deployed across diverse topics, where individual user groups can experience severe miscalibration even when the global error is moderate. The distribution-free guarantee (Theorem 3.1) provides a principled way to set the number of points per bin, and the 10–40% improvement in QA-calibration error over SOTA elicitation prompts is practically significant for decision-making applications. Key limitations include the fixed output space of β (which may not accommodate an ever-growing QA domain) and the interpretability of partitions depending heavily on the chosen embedding and tree depth. Open questions include extending to multi-calibration across intersecting groupings and handling non-stationary QA distributions over time.
