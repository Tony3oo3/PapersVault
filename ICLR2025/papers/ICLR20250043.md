## Problem

Existing trajectory modeling methods are specialized for a single task—prediction, imputation, or spatial-temporal recovery—making them unable to handle the diverse input formulations encountered in real-world scenarios. Additionally, widely used public datasets focus on pedestrian movements with sparse, casual interactions, failing to represent the dense, structured multi-agent dynamics found in sports games.

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary incomplete trajectory X∈R^{N×T×D} (N agents, T=50 time steps, D=2 coordinates) paired with a binary visibility mask M∈{0,1}^{N×T} and one-hot agent category labels (ball, offensive, defensive). All three tasks—prediction, imputation, and spatial-temporal recovery—are unified into this single masked-trajectory formulation, where visible regions serve as constraints and missing regions are the generation targets. The model outputs K=20 complete trajectories, evaluated via minADE₂₀, out-of-boundary rate, step size, path length, and path length disparity.

## Method

UniTraj is a Conditional VAE whose encoder combines a Transformer with a Ghost Spatial Masking (GSM) module and a Bidirectional Temporal Mamba (BTM) encoder with a Bidirectional Temporal Scaled (BTS) module. GSM generates a permutation-invariant 'ghost' embedding from the mask vector via max-pooling over an MLP-projected mask matrix, prepended as a head token to agent embeddings to capture order-invariant spatial missing patterns. The BTS module computes a forward and backward scaling matrix measuring the distance from the last observation to each time step, then applies a negative-exponential decay (via 1/exp(ReLU(MLP(S)))) to modulate Mamba's convolutional features, ensuring that the influence of distant observations decays monotonically. The Mamba encoder is run bidirectionally (forward and backward passes) to capture long-range temporal dependencies in both directions. Training maximizes a sequential ELBO augmented with a reconstruction loss on visible regions and a Winner-Take-All loss over K generated trajectories to promote diversity.

## Results

On the three curated sports datasets, UniTraj achieves the lowest minADE₂₀ across all settings: 4.77 ft on Basketball-U, 3.55 yd on Football-U, and 94.59 px on Soccer-U, outperforming the strongest baseline GC-VRNN by 17.9%, 28.3%, and 10.7% respectively. UniTraj also attains the lowest Out-of-Boundary (OOB) rate among all learned methods (e.g., 6.12×10⁻⁴ on Basketball-U vs. 9.28×10⁻⁴ for GC-VRNN). On the Step and Path-L metrics, UniTraj closely tracks ground-truth values (Step: 0.27 vs. GT 0.17 on Basketball-U; Path-L: 34.25 vs. GT 37.61), confirming physically plausible trajectory generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTraj outperforms all 12 baselines on the primary minADE₂₀ metric across all three sports datasets. Against the strongest learned baseline GC-VRNN (CVPR 2023), UniTraj reduces minADE₂₀ by 17.9% on Basketball-U (4.77 vs. 5.81), 28.3% on Football-U (3.55 vs. 4.95), and 10.7% on Soccer-U (94.59 vs. 105.87). Among the diffusion-based and GAN-based imputation baselines (SSSD, INAM, Naomi), UniTraj achieves substantially lower displacement errors while also producing fewer out-of-boundary points. The simple statistical baselines (Mean, Median) trivially achieve zero OOB but have minADE₂₀ values 3× higher than UniTraj; Linear Fit is competitive on Step and Path-L metrics (which measure trajectory length rather than positional accuracy) but still lags UniTraj on minADE₂₀ by a wide margin. On the generalizability experiments, UniTraj slightly trails MemoNet and EqMotion on ETH-UCY pedestrian prediction (0.23 vs. 0.21 avg minADE₂₀) but outperforms FlowChain, and on Traffic-Guangzhou time-series imputation it beats CSBI (RMSE 3.942 vs. 4.790) while remaining close to BayOTIDE (3.942 vs. 3.820).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate trajectory modeling tasks—prediction, imputation, and spatial-temporal recovery—into a single masked trajectory generation framework, departing from prior work (GC-VRNN, INAM) that handled these tasks through multi-task architectures with task-specific masking strategies. Architecturally, it extends the Mamba state space model from its original unidirectional form into a Bidirectional Temporal Mamba encoder and introduces a Ghost Spatial Masking module that replaces graph-based interaction modeling with a lightweight pooling mechanism inside a standard Transformer. The CVAE generative framework follows the trajectory prediction literature (Trajectron, SocialVAE), but the key conceptual departure is treating arbitrary incomplete inputs uniformly rather than designing separate pipelines per task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three curated sports datasets (Basketball-U, Football-U, Soccer-U) fill a notable gap: existing public trajectory datasets focus on sparse pedestrian interactions, whereas sports games involve dense, structured multi-agent coordination that better reflects real-world multi-agent scenarios. The unified formulation has practical value since real-world sensing produces arbitrary missing patterns that don't conform to a single task definition. Open questions include accommodating variable agent counts (current datasets fix N), developing more expressive decoders beyond MLPs, and whether the Mamba-based temporal encoding advantage generalizes to longer sequences or higher-dimensional state spaces.
