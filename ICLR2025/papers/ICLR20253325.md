## Problem

Post-training of diffusion-based text-to-video models has relied heavily on reward feedback from low-quality WebVid data, while training-free conditional guidance methods like MotionClone impose prohibitive per-step computational costs and require external reference videos. No prior work has systematically integrated high-quality training data, diverse vision-language reward models, and conditional guidance into a single consistency distillation framework, leaving a large design space for energy functions that augment the teacher ODE solver unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a generated video decoded from latent space via a VAE decoder, supporting 4-step (~1 s), 8-step (~1.5 s), or 16-step (~3 s) inference in BFloat16. Training consumes video-caption pairs from VidGen-1M (high-quality, dense captions) and WebVid-10M (mixed quality, short captions) in equal proportions, with the CD loss optimized over the full mixture while reward optimization is restricted to the short-caption WebVid subset to avoid context-length truncation in the reward models.

## Method

T2V-Turbo-v2 distills a consistency model from VideoCrafter2 by augmenting the teacher PF-ODE solver with both classifier-free guidance and a motion-guidance energy function derived from MotionClone's primary temporal-attention (PTA) guidance. The key insight is that each training video naturally serves as its own motion reference when the prompt is its caption, eliminating the need for external reference videos; the expensive DDIM inversion and temporal-attention gradient are pre-computed in a data preprocessing phase, freeing GPU memory to enable full-parameter training (rather than LoRA) and to remove the EMA target network from the CD loss. The student CM is conditioned on both the CFG scale ω and the motion-guidance strength λ, and the total training objective combines the self-consistency distillation loss with a reward maximization term that aggregates feedback from HPSv2.1, CLIPScore, and InternVideo2. Motion guidance is applied only to the first τ=50% of sampling steps, and the CD loss acts as a regularizer that prevents reward over-optimization and color distortion.

## Results

On VBench with 16 inference steps, T2V-Turbo-v2 with motion guidance (w/ MG) achieves a Total Score of 83.52, Quality Score of 85.13, and Semantic Score of 77.12, establishing a new state-of-the-art that surpasses proprietary systems Gen-3 (82.32) and Kling (81.85). The variant without motion guidance (w/o MG) also outperforms all baselines with a Total Score of 82.93 and a notably higher Semantic Score of 78.33. On T2V-CompBench motion metrics, incorporating motion guidance boosts Dynamic Degree from 61.39 to 90.00 and Motion Binding from 22.15 to 24.38 without degrading Motion Smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2V-Turbo-v2 w/ MG outperforms all seven baselines on Total Score, beating the strongest proprietary system Gen-3 by 1.20 points (83.52 vs. 82.32) and Kling by 1.67 points (83.52 vs. 81.85). The w/o MG variant also surpasses every baseline, exceeding T2V-Turbo's 4-step result (81.01) by 1.92 points and VideoCrafter2 (80.44) by 2.49 points. Notably, T2V-Turbo-v2 w/o MG achieves the highest Semantic Score (78.33) among all methods, surpassing Kling (75.68) and Gen-3 (75.17) by over 2 points, while the w/ MG variant achieves the highest Quality Score (85.13), exceeding Gen-3 (84.11) by 1.02 points. Compared to its direct predecessor T2V-Turbo, the v2 model improves Total Score by 2.51 points (16-step: 83.52 vs. 80.51) and Semantic Score by 3.69 points, demonstrating the impact of curated training data and diverse reward models. On motion-specific metrics from T2V-CompBench, the w/ MG variant improves Dynamic Degree by 28.61 points over w/o MG (90.00 vs. 61.39), confirming the effectiveness of the motion guidance energy function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends T2V-Turbo (Li et al., 2024b), which first integrated mixed reward feedback into consistency distillation for T2V, by simultaneously incorporating three supervision signals: high-quality training data, diverse reward model feedback, and conditional guidance via an energy function. The motion guidance mechanism is adapted from MotionClone (Ling et al., 2024), but the key departure is using training videos themselves as motion references rather than requiring external reference videos, and pre-computing the expensive ODE solver solutions to enable full-parameter training instead of LoRA. The distillation framework follows the consistency model paradigm (Song et al., 2023; Luo et al., 2023), with the teacher being VideoCrafter2 (Chen et al., 2024a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a new SOTA on VBench (85.13 Total Score) surpassing proprietary systems like Gen-3 and Kling, demonstrating that careful post-training design can close the gap with closed-source models. A practically important finding is that high-quality datasets with dense captions (e.g., OpenVid-1M) actually hinder reward optimization due to the limited context length of existing RMs (77 tokens for CLIP/HPSv2.1, 40 for InternV2), motivating future work on long-context reward models. The framing of conditional guidance as an energy function design space for augmenting the teacher ODE solver is underexplored and could inspire broader applications beyond motion. The paper's limitation that the teacher model's CLIP text encoder constrains handling of dense captions suggests a ceiling on current approaches.
