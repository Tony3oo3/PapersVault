## Problem

In-context learning (ICL) is attributed to two underlying abilities—task recognition (TR) and task learning (TL)—but how these abilities interact during pre-training and how their interplay affects ICL emergence remains unexplored. Prior work observed that TR dominates in smaller models while TL emerges in larger ones, yet no quantitative measure of their relationship or its impact on ICL performance existed.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes a test input paired with 16 in-context demonstrations (formatted with minimal newline separators) and two pre-trained LLM checkpoints: one selected for best TR performance (evaluated under the random-label setting) and one for best TL performance (evaluated under the abstract-label setting). It outputs a single predicted label by argmax over the weighted sum of the two models' probability distributions. The evaluation uses 1000 sampled examples per dataset across 16 classification datasets, with temperature=0 for deterministic decoding.

## Method

The paper introduces a competition intensity metric (C_i^S) computed from the performance deltas of TR and TL between adjacent pre-training checkpoints, flagging competition when the two abilities change in opposite directions and quantifying its magnitude as the ratio of the declining ability's drop to the rising ability's gain. Empirical analysis across 7 LLM families reveals that competition is widespread, follows a 'stable-rise' pattern during pre-training, and exhibits a negative Pearson correlation (−0.714) with final ICL performance. To exploit this insight at inference time, the method selects the TR-best and TL-best checkpoints from pre-training and fuses them via adaptive ensemble learning: each model's probability distribution is weighted by its normalized performance above random-guessing baseline (w_r and w_l in Eq. 6), and the final prediction is the argmax of the weighted sum. This logit-level fusion of two small models (e.g., Pythia-1B + Pythia-2.8B, totaling 3.8B parameters) outperforms single 7B–12B models with more than twice the parameters and lower inference cost.

## Results

Across 16 classification datasets, the proposed adaptive logit fusion of two small checkpoints (Pythia-1B TR + Pythia-2.8B TL) achieves 56.83% average accuracy with only 3.8B total parameters and 4.84 TFLOPs, outperforming all 7B-parameter baselines (best: CrystalCoder-7B at 55.66%) and the 12B Pythia model (52.77%). The best 2B-parameter fusion (Pythia-1B TR + Pythia-1B TL, adaptive) reaches 56.25% accuracy at just 2.48 TFLOPs, surpassing every single 7B model. Parameter fusion (weight averaging) of the same checkpoints yields only 46.63–52.18%, confirming that logit-level ensemble is substantially more effective than weight-level merging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its adaptive logit fusion against five categories of baselines: (1) large 7B/12B LLMs, (2) individual small models, (3) parameter (weight) fusion, and (4) fixed-weight logit fusion. The best fusion configuration (Pythia-1B TR + Pythia-2.8B TL, adaptive) at 56.83% outperforms CrystalCoder-7B (55.66%) by 1.17 points, Baichuan2-7B and Pythia-12B (both 52.77%) by ~4 points, OLMo-7B (52.10%) by 4.73 points, and Amber-7B (50.08%) by 6.75 points — all while using less than half the parameters and roughly half the inference FLOPs. Against the individual small models, the fusion gains are even larger: +12.28 points over Pythia-1B (44.55%) and +11.45 over Pythia-2.8B (45.38%). Parameter fusion (weight averaging) of the same checkpoints achieves only 46.63–52.18%, well below the logit fusion, confirming that combining probability distributions is far more effective than merging weights. The adaptive weighting consistently outperforms fixed 50/50 weighting by 0.1–1.5 points, with the largest gap appearing in the cross-model setting (Pythia-1B TL + MiniCPM-2B TR: 55.85 vs. 54.31).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Pan et al. (2023) and Wei et al. (2023), which identified task recognition (TR) and task learning (TL) as two underlying abilities of in-context learning and observed that TR dominates in smaller models while TL emerges in larger ones. However, prior work examined these abilities only in final trained models or at inference time; this paper is the first to track their interaction across pre-training checkpoints, introducing a quantitative 'competition intensity' metric to capture their dynamic trade-off. The proposed adaptive ensemble learning method also draws on the broader model fusion literature (weight averaging, logit ensembling) but repurposes it specifically to fuse TR-specialized and TL-specialized checkpoints for ICL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that TR and TL are competitive rather than synergistic during pre-training, and that this competition negatively correlates with final ICL performance (Pearson r = −0.714), provides a new diagnostic lens for understanding why ICL emerges with fluctuations. The practical implication—that two small models fused via adaptive logit ensemble can outperform a 7B+ model with less than half the parameters—offers a low-cost inference-time strategy. Key limitations include the inability to run fully controlled pre-training experiments (only 7 models with varying settings), the use of only 16 sampled checkpoints, and the fact that the competitive metric is defined relative to adjacent checkpoints, making it sensitive to checkpoint granularity. Open questions include whether pre-training objectives or data scheduling can be explicitly designed to minimize TR-TL competition, and whether these dynamics persist in instruction-tuned or RLHF-aligned models.
