## Problem

World models for reinforcement learning have grown increasingly complex, relying on RNNs or transformers for long-term dependencies, discrete latent representations, and pixel-level image reconstructions. This paper asks what the truly essential components of a world model are and how far one can push simplification—removing sequence models, discretization, and reconstructions—while still achieving competitive sample efficiency on Atari 100k.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251303/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4 stacked grayscale Atari frames (84×84) and 4 stacked discrete actions as input, replacing the need for recurrent or attention-based memory. It outputs a deterministic latent transition (Gaussian mean with unit variance), a reward prediction via discrete regression with symlog encoding, a Bernoulli terminal prediction, and ultimately a policy action. Notably, the transition distribution is learned with stop-gradient on the representations to decouple it from the moving target of the encoder, while reward and terminal losses use augmented observations for robustness.

## Method

SGF learns representations through a VICReg-inspired Siamese objective: two augmented views of successive observations are encoded by a shared CNN encoder, projected into 2048-d embeddings, and an action-conditioned predictor maps the current embedding to the next. Temporal consistency is enforced by minimizing MSE between predicted and actual embeddings, while variance-covariance regularization (hinge loss on per-dimension variance, L2 on off-diagonal covariance) maximizes information content and prevents collapse. The dynamics model is a simple feedforward MLP (5 hidden layers, 1024-d, with a residual connection) that predicts the next latent state deterministically, avoiding RNNs, transformers, and stochastic transitions. Frame and action stacking (m=4) capture short-term dependencies, and data augmentation (random shifts, intensity jittering) introduces robustness against model errors in place of stochastic predictions. The policy is trained in imagination via an advantage actor-critic with GAE, entropy regularization, and DreamerV3-style return normalization, using a 10-step imagination horizon.

## Results

On the Atari 100k benchmark (26 games, 400k environment steps), SGF achieves a human-normalized mean score of 0.884 and a normalized median of 0.152. While this is below EfficientZero (1.943 mean), IRIS (1.046), and DreamerV3 (1.12), it substantially outperforms the model-free baseline SPR (0.616). The key advantage is training speed: SGF completes training in ~3 hours on a V100 GPU, which is 4× faster than DreamerV3 (12 h), ~10× faster than EfficientZero (29 h), and ~56× faster than IRIS (168 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGF (normalized mean 0.884) clearly outperforms the model-free baseline SPR (0.616) by a wide margin, confirming that the learned world model adds substantial value. However, SGF trails all three model-based baselines on the normalized mean: EfficientZero (1.943), DreamerV3 (1.12), and IRIS (1.046). The gap is most pronounced on games with long-horizon or complex dynamics (e.g., Crazy Climber: 15,751 vs. 97,190 for DreamerV3; Battle Zone: 3,747 vs. 12,250), which the authors attribute to the absence of sequence models. SGF does win on several individual games—Boxing (83.4 vs. 78 for DreamerV3), Krull (7,814 vs. 7,782), and Chopper Command (1,775 vs. 420 for DreamerV3)—and is competitive on Ms Pacman (1,357 vs. 1,327). The critical trade-off is speed: SGF trains in ~3 h on a V100, versus 12 h for DreamerV3, 29 h for EfficientZero, and 168 h for IRIS, making it the fastest model-based method evaluated while retaining reasonable performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGF builds directly on VICReg's variance-covariance regularization for self-supervised representation learning and BYOL's predictor network concept, adapting them to the sequential setting by augmenting two successive observations and inserting an action-conditioned predictor between them. It deliberately departs from the Dreamer, IRIS, and TWM line of work by removing RNNs/transformers, discrete representations, pixel reconstructions, and stochastic transitions, replacing them with frame/action stacking, data augmentation, and a feedforward dynamics model. The paper positions itself as a 'minimal ingredients' distillation of world models, arguing that the essential properties are information maximization and temporal consistency, which can be achieved without the architectural complexity that has accumulated in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful counterpoint to the trend of increasing architectural complexity in world models, showing that a feedforward, reconstruction-free, discretization-free model can achieve competitive Atari 100k performance (0.884 normalized mean) at 4× the speed of DreamerV3. However, it does not reach SOTA (EfficientZero: 1.943, DreamerV3: 1.12), and the authors acknowledge the limitation to deterministic MDPs with short-term dependencies. Open questions include whether a sequence model could be reintroduced for long-horizon tasks without sacrificing the simplicity gains, and whether the VICReg-based approach extends to non-image modalities or stochastic environments.
