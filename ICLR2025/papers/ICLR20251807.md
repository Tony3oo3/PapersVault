## Problem

Existing post-training KV-Cache compression methods—quantization (reducing bit-width) and token eviction (discarding tokens)—both neglect the hidden dimension of KV tensors, where substantial redundancy resides. This paper addresses the unexplored compression axis of reducing the hidden dimensionality of cached Key and Value states without incurring prohibitive runtime decomposition costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251807/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pre-trained K/V projection weight matrices of an LLM (decomposed offline via truncation-aware SVD) and the input token sequence at inference time. It produces a lower-dimensional latent representation h ∈ R^r (where r < d) that replaces the full d-dimensional key/value in the KV-Cache, and reconstructs the original keys/values on-the-fly during decoding. For RoPE-based models, reconstruction happens inside a fused GPU kernel in shared memory; for non-RoPE models, the reconstruction matrix is fused offline into the query/output projection weights, eliminating reconstruction entirely.

## Method

Palu statically decomposes each K/V projection weight matrix W ≈ AB via SVD, caching only the latent h = xA instead of the full key/value, then reconstructing y = Bh during decoding. To balance accuracy against reconstruction cost across multiple attention heads, it introduces Group-Head Low-Rank Decomposition (G-LRD), which decomposes a group of s heads jointly rather than per-head (M-LRD) or all heads together (J-LRD). An automatic rank allocation algorithm based on Fisher information assigns higher ranks to more sensitive layers. For quantization compatibility, a Walsh-Hadamard Transform is fused into the A and B matrices offline (W ≈ (AR)(R^T B)), eliminating SVD-induced outliers at zero runtime overhead. Offline matrix fusion absorbs B^v into W^o and B^k into W^q to skip explicit reconstruction, and a custom Triton/CUDA kernel integrates key reconstruction, RoPE application, and QK multiplication in a single fused operation for RoPE-based attention.

## Results

At 50% low-rank compression with 2-bit quantization, Palu achieves 6.41 perplexity on WikiText-2 with 93.75% KV-Cache compression (4.0 GB for 128K context on Llama-2-7B), outperforming KVQuant (6.95 PPL, 87.50% compression) by 1.19 in perplexity while saving 30% more memory. For latency, Palu delivers up to 1.89× speedup on RoPE-based attention and 2.91× with 4-bit quantization at 64K sequence length, compared to KIVI-4-bit's 1.89×. On LongBench, Palu-30% (3-bit) maintains 40.77% average accuracy on Mistral-7B with a 7.59× compression ratio, versus KIVI-2's 41.45% at 5.05× compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KVQuant, Palu decisively wins at 2-bit quantization, achieving 6.41 PPL versus KVQuant's 6.95 (a 1.19 perplexity improvement) while also compressing 6.25% more (93.75% vs 87.50%). At 3-bit, KVQuant retains a slight edge in perplexity (5.35 vs 5.77), but Palu still achieves 9.38% higher compression (90.63% vs 81.25%). Against Atom, Palu is overwhelmingly superior at 2-bit (6.41 vs 117.88 PPL), where Atom essentially collapses. On LongBench, Palu-30% (3-bit) scores 40.77% average on Mistral-7B, slightly below KIVI-2's 41.45%, but with a substantially higher 7.59× compression ratio versus KIVI's 5.05×. On latency, Palu with 4-bit quantization achieves 2.91× speedup at 64K RoPE attention, nearly 1.5× faster than KIVI-4-bit's 1.89×, and extends to 6.17× on non-RoPE attention where KIVI is out-of-memory beyond 32K.

## Relation to Prior Work

Palu extends the SVD-based weight compression lineage (FWSVD, ASVD, SVD-LLM) from compressing model parameters to compressing the KV-Cache hidden dimension, using SVD-LLM's truncation-aware SVD as its decomposition backbone. It is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to existing KV-Cache compression axes—quantization (KIVI, KVQuant, Atom) and token eviction (H2O, SnapKV, AttentionSink)—by targeting the hidden dimension rather than bit-width or token count. Conceptually, it resembles DeepSeek's MLA mechanism (low-rank down-projection of K/V with runtime reconstruction) but is designed for post-training integration into existing MHA/GQA models rather than requiring pre-training. The Walsh-Hadamard outlier mitigation draws from the QuaRot/QuIP# quantization literature but is uniquely fused into the decomposition matrices at zero runtime cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV-Cache memory is the dominant bottleneck for long-context LLM inference, and Palu provides a practical, training-free compression axis that composes multiplicatively with quantization, achieving >91% total compression (11.4×) with minimal accuracy loss. The group-head decomposition and Fisher-information-based rank allocation offer a principled way to balance accuracy against reconstruction overhead, and the zero-overhead Hadamard integration makes low-bit quantization of the latent cache viable. Open questions include whether the online reconstruction kernel can be accelerated with INT4 Tensor Cores for RoPE-based attention, whether combining Palu with token-selection methods (Quest, SnapKV) yields further gains, and how the approach generalizes to MoE architectures where per-expert KV-Cache dynamics differ.
