## Problem

Applying homomorphic encryption (HE) to transformer-based LLMs for privacy-preserving personalized fine-tuning and inference remains computationally intractable due to two bottlenecks: full fine-tuning produces large ciphertext-ciphertext matrix multiplications (CCMMs), and the softmax in attention requires approximating division, max, and exponential functions under HE. Prior HE transformer work has largely avoided fine-tuning altogether, focusing only on secure inference, leaving the practical scenario of private personalized adaptation unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The client transmits token-embedded data encrypted under CKKS (sequence length 128, hidden dimension 768) to a server holding plaintext pre-trained 2-layer BERT weights. The server performs encrypted fine-tuning, producing encrypted LoRA weights (rank 2 on Q, K, V layers), then runs inference on new encrypted inputs combined with the plaintext base weights and encrypted LoRA adapters, returning encrypted classification scores. The token embedding layer remains plaintext and frozen throughout; only the LoRA parameters and classification head are updated in the encrypted domain.

## Method

The architecture resolves both HE bottlenecks with two algorithmic substitutions. First, LoRA fine-tuning (rank 2) replaces full weight updates, converting O(n²) CCMMs into O(nr) CCMMs plus O(n²) PCMMs, and reducing AdamW optimizer states from 368 to 15 ciphertexts, which also minimizes expensive inverse-square-root approximations. Second, the softmax attention is replaced with a Gaussian kernel (GK) attention S(Q,K)_{ij} = exp(−‖Q_i − K_j‖²/(2√n)), which eliminates the need for division, the max function, and restricts the exponential approximation to x ≤ 0 where it is numerically stable (approximated via (1+x/2^k)^{2^k}). The full pipeline runs under CKKS (HEaaN, FGb parameters, 9 multiplicative levels) using JKLS homomorphic matrix multiplication with custom LoRA-friendly packing and an optimized sequential CCMM algorithm that is 4.45× faster than standard rectangular MM, yielding 6.94× fine-tuning and 2.3× inference speedups over full fine-tuning with softmax while maintaining GLUE scores within ~1–4 points of the plaintext baseline.

## Results

The proposed LoRA+GK architecture under CKKS homomorphic encryption achieves a 6.94× speedup for fine-tuning (61.03s vs. 423.55s for Full+SM) and a 2.33× speedup for inference (26.5s vs. 61.84s) on a 2-layer BERT model with 128-token inputs. On the GLUE benchmark, the HE-encrypted model (LoRA+GK under HE) achieves an average score of 0.6621, compared to 0.7068 for the plaintext Full+SM baseline and 0.6772 for plaintext LoRA+GK, indicating only a minimal accuracy drop from encryption. Per-task, the largest gap is on CoLA (0.1575 vs. 0.2688 Matthews corr.), while SST-2, MRPC, RTE, and STS-B show gaps of ≤0.08.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Full+SM (full fine-tuning with standard softmax attention), which represents the conventional plaintext transformer. The paper's LoRA+GK under HE achieves 0.6621 average GLUE score versus 0.7068 for Full+SM, a gap of ~0.045 that is largely attributable to the LoRA rank-2 constraint rather than encryption itself (plaintext LoRA+GK scores 0.6772). For the softmax-vs-GK comparison specifically, the paper benchmarks against HETAL's polynomial approximation of softmax (Lee et al., 2023b), showing GK is 6.24× faster (1.44s vs. 8.99s) for 128×128 attention score computation. The CCMM-vs-PCMM comparison shows LoRA reduces the expensive ciphertext-ciphertext matrix multiplication cost by a factor of 4.58× for the 128×768 by 768×768 case. Overall, the method trades a modest ~4–10% accuracy drop (depending on task) for a 6.94× fine-tuning and 2.3× inference speedup, establishing a viable proof-of-concept for privacy-preserving LLM fine-tuning and inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied threads: parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) and softmax-free attention using Gaussian kernels (Lu et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2021; Richter & Wattenhofer, 2020). In the HE-for-transformers literature, prior work (THE-X, HETAL, NEXUS, Powerformer, Zimerman et al.) has focused almost exclusively on secure inference, explicitly avoiding the far more complex encrypted fine-tuning step. This paper is the first to address the full encrypted fine-tuning-then-inference pipeline, repurposing LoRA not for its usual memory-saving benefit but specifically to convert expensive ciphertext-ciphertext matrix multiplications (CCMM) into cheaper plaintext-ciphertext ones (PCMM), and replacing softmax with a Gaussian kernel to eliminate the division and max-function approximations that dominate HE computation time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is a viable proof of concept that a BERT-style transformer can be fine-tuned and run end-to-end on encrypted data with only a modest accuracy drop (average GLUE score 0.6621 vs. 0.7068 for plaintext full fine-tuning), opening a path toward privacy-preserving LLM services in regulated domains. However, the demonstration is limited to a 2-layer BERT with 768 hidden dimensions under a semi-honest threat model, and the paper explicitly does not address model-weight protection. Key open questions include whether the LoRA+GK recipe scales to decoder-only LLMs with hundreds of layers, whether the polynomial-approximation precision loss compounds over deeper stacks, and how to handle the bootstrapping bottleneck that already dominates 27–45% of per-layer runtime.
