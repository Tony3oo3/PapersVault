## Problem

Instruction-based universal spoken language models (e.g., SALMONN, Qwen-Audio, WavLLM) have emerged but lack a comprehensive evaluation benchmark. Existing speech benchmarks (SUPERB, SLUE, HEAR, MARBLE) contain only 7–19 tasks each, are designed for encoder evaluation rather than instruction-following, and cover only a narrow slice of task types. Dynamic-SUPERB Phase-1 addressed this with 55 tasks but was limited to classification, leaving regression and sequence-generation capabilities unmeasured.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252990/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task provides one or more audio clips (speech, music, or environmental) plus a natural-language text instruction; some tasks also include auxiliary text inputs (e.g., reference lyrics for singing translation). Models must produce free-form natural-language outputs, which are then scored by an automated pipeline: GPT-4o acts as a chain-of-thought judge for classification, an LLM post-processor extracts numeric values from prose for regression (with an N/A rate tracking instruction-following failures), and standard metrics (WER, CER, F1) are applied directly to raw outputs for sequence generation. Domain-level scores are computed as relative improvements over a Whisper-LLaMA cascaded baseline, with regression metrics scaled by (1 − N/A rate).

## Method

Dynamic-SUPERB Phase-2 expands the benchmark from 55 to 180 tasks through a structured community call-for-tasks process (140+ proposals received, 120+ accepted between March–July 2024), covering speech (8 domains), music, and environmental audio (9 domains). A hierarchical task taxonomy clusters tasks by the model capability they probe, enabling targeted model development rather than exhaustive evaluation. Core tasks are reformulated from SUPERB, MARBLE, and HEAR into instruction-following format with crafted prompts and reduced data for quick-round experiments. The evaluation pipeline handles the heterogeneous output formats of universal models by routing each task to one of three scoring strategies (LLM-judge, LLM post-processor, or direct metric), and a relative-score-based comparison normalizes results across differently-metriced tasks against a Whisper-v3-large + LLaMA3.1-8B cascaded baseline. The paper evaluates seven publicly available models (SALMONN-7B/13B, LTU-AS, Qwen-Audio-Chat, Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct, WavLLM, MU-LLaMA, GAMA-IT) and finds no single model generalizes well across all domains.

## Results

No single model dominates across all 180 tasks. On the 10 SUPERB core tasks, SALMONN-13B achieves the best ASR (WER 2.8%) and phoneme recognition (PER 24.6%), while Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct leads in emotion recognition (75.8% accuracy) and Qwen-Audio-Chat in keyword spotting (60.5% accuracy). The cascaded Whisper-LLaMA baseline surprisingly wins on intent classification (36.5%) and slot-filling F1 (53.6%), and all models perform near-random on speaker verification (45–51% accuracy) and query-by-example (≤51.5% accuracy). At the domain level, speech models outperform the ASR baseline in paralinguistics and speaker tasks but lose in speech recognition and spoken language understanding, while music-specialized models (GAMA-IT, Mu-LLaMA) are competitive in speaker/paralinguistics but fail on speech tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALMONN-13B is the strongest on ASR (WER 2.8% vs. WavLLM 6.9% and Whisper-LLaMA 34.0%) and phoneme recognition (PER 24.6% vs. all others ≥100%), but it collapses on keyword spotting (2.0% acc) and slot-filling CER (106.3). Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct leads in emotion recognition (75.8% vs. Qwen-Audio-Chat 70.4% and all others ≤25.8%) and is competitive on ASR (36.7% WER), while Qwen-Audio-Chat is the only model above random on keyword spotting (60.5% vs. Whisper-LLaMA 36.5%). The cascaded Whisper-LLaMA baseline wins on intent classification (36.5% vs. next-best 28.0%) and slot-filling F1 (53.6% vs. SALMONN-7B 61.6% on F1 but Whisper-LLaMA has the lowest CER at 51.3), confirming that ASR+LLM remains a strong floor for semantic tasks. Music-specialized models GAMA-IT and Mu-LLaMA are near-useless on speech tasks (ASR WER 116.7% and 103.7% respectively) yet achieve the best speaker diarization DER (187.6 for GAMA-IT) and competitive paralinguistics scores at the domain level. Across all models, speaker verification hovers around 44–51% (near the 50% random baseline) and query-by-example is unsolved (best 51.5%), indicating these remain open challenges for universal speech models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Dynamic-SUPERB Phase-1 (Huang et al., 2024a), the first benchmark for instruction-following universal speech models, by expanding from 55 to 180 tasks and broadening task types beyond classification to include regression and sequence generation. It also integrates core tasks from three established encoder-focused benchmarks—SUPERB (speech), MARBLE (music), and HEAR (audio)—reformulating them for instruction-based evaluation, a departure from their original design for assessing frozen representations. The community-driven, dynamically expanding task-submission model distinguishes it from fixed benchmarks like SLUE, AIR-Bench, or the original SUPERB, which have limited and static task sets. The LLM-as-judge evaluation pipeline (GPT-4o) for scoring natural-language outputs is borrowed from NLP practice (G-Eval, MT-Bench) and adapted here for the first time at scale in speech research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the largest benchmark for speech, music, and audio evaluation (180 tasks), Dynamic-SUPERB Phase-2 provides the field with a comprehensive, fine-grained diagnostic tool that no prior benchmark offers, and its task taxonomy gives researchers a structured path for targeting specific model weaknesses. The central finding—that no current model generalizes well across the full task space, and that even the best models (SALMONN-13B, Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct) excel only in narrow domains—underscores that universal spoken language models remain far from mature. Key open questions include whether unified models can genuinely bridge speech, music, and environmental audio (the paper's cross-domain transfer observation hints at this), how to incorporate speech-generation tasks as such models emerge, and whether LLM-based evaluation will remain reliable as task diversity grows. The benchmark's dynamic, community-driven design positions it to evolve alongside the field rather than become stale.
