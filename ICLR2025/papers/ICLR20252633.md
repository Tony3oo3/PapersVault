## Problem

Expected Information Gain (EIG) in Bayesian Optimal Experimental Design is doubly intractable: both the EIG and its gradient require expectations over posterior distributions that are themselves intractable to sample from. All prior BOED methods also assumed the prior was available in closed form, excluding the use of powerful generative models like diffusion networks. This paper removes both barriers by introducing a tractable gradient estimator and a single-loop optimization scheme that accommodates data-based priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prior (either a closed-form density or a pre-trained diffusion model), a likelihood p(y|θ,ξ) with known gradient, a continuous design parameter ξ ∈ R^d, and in sequential settings the accumulated observation history D_{k-1}. It produces the optimal design ξ*, posterior samples of θ at each step, and a stochastic EIG gradient estimate. In the image reconstruction experiment, θ is a 28×28 MNIST digit and ξ is the center pixel of a 7×7 observation mask; in source localization, θ is a set of 2D source positions and ξ is a 2D measurement location.

## Method

The key insight is the introduction of a pooled posterior distribution q_{ξ,N} ∝ ∏ᵢ p(θ|yᵢ,ξ)^{νᵢ}, a logarithmic pooling of all per-observation posteriors, which provably minimizes the weighted sum of KL divergences to each posterior and thus serves as an optimal importance sampling proposal. Its score decomposes as a weighted sum of individual posterior scores, enabling the same sampling operators (Langevin dynamics, conditional diffusion SDEs) to be reused without additional complexity. A single-loop bi-level optimization framework interleaves one step of the joint-distribution sampler, one step of the pooled-posterior sampler, and one gradient update of ξ, avoiding the expensive nested sampling-optimization loop. For data-based priors, conditional diffusion sampling via the FPS approximation (Dou & Song, 2024) is used to draw from the pooled posterior without retraining, and Tweedie's formula provides a one-shot denoising correction for early-iteration gradient quality. The resulting contrastive objective encourages selecting ξ that maximally separates the pooled posterior from the prior, analogous to noise-contrastive estimation.

## Results

On the MNIST image reconstruction task (28×28 images from 7×7 sub-image observations), CoDiff achieves SSIM of 0.826 after 6 sequential experiments, compared to 0.463 for random design selection. On the source localization task (2 sources in R², 30 sequential experiments), CoDiff improves SPCE by 30% over the non-myopic RL-BOED baseline and achieves an L₂ Wasserstein distance two orders of magnitude lower than all competing methods, while also providing substantially higher SNMC bounds. Each measurement step takes ~2.9 s (source localization, 400 samples) or ~7.3 s (image reconstruction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the source localization experiment (density-based, 30 sequential experiments), CoDiff outperforms all four baselines—RL-BOED (Blau et al., 2022), VPCE (Foster et al., 2020), PASOA (Iollo et al., 2024), and non-tempered SMC—on all three metrics (SPCE, L₂ Wasserstein distance, SNMC). The improvement over the strongest baseline RL-BOED is 30% on SPCE, and the L₂ Wasserstein distance is two orders of magnitude lower, indicating substantially better posterior estimation. In the image reconstruction experiment (data-based, diffusion model prior on MNIST), the only comparison is against random design selection, since the authors note that previous BOED methods do not apply to this generative setting; CoDiff achieves 0.826 SSIM after 6 experiments versus 0.463 for random, a 78% relative improvement. The paper does not provide a head-to-head comparison of CoDiff against RL-BOED, VPCE, or PASOA in the data-based (diffusion) setting, as no prior method handles that regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends two threads of EIG gradient estimation in BOED: Goda et al. (2022) using randomized MLMC with a Laplace posterior approximation, and Ao and Li (2024) using MCMC-based nested Monte Carlo. The authors show these differ only in how the intractable posterior is approximated, then introduce a third expression based on a novel 'pooled posterior' distribution (a logarithmic/geometric mixture of posteriors) that serves as an importance sampling proposal, eliminating the nested estimation bottleneck. The single-loop optimization procedure adapts the 'sampling-as-optimization' and implicit diffusion framework of Marion et al. (2025) to the doubly intractable EIG objective, and the data-based sampling operators draw on the FPS conditional diffusion method of Dou and Song (2024) for linear inverse problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first extension of BOED to diffusion-based generative models, opening the door to high-dimensional inverse problems (image inpainting, protein engineering, MRI) that were previously intractable for experimental design. The single-loop contrastive procedure outperforms two-stage and RL-based baselines while simultaneously producing posterior samples, which is practically important since posterior inference is the ultimate goal of BOED. Key limitations remain: the approach is greedy (myopic), requires an explicit likelihood expression, and the diffusion-based sampler currently handles only linear forward models. Open questions include extending to non-linear forward models, settings without explicit likelihoods (simulation-based inference), and non-myopic design in the data-based regime.
