## Problem

Making LLMs safe during supervised fine-tuning typically requires either large amounts of human-labeled safe responses to toxic prompts (as in Safety-Tuned Llamas) or reliance on another powerful LLM to generate corrective data, both of which are expensive and undermine the motivation for a lightweight approach. This paper asks whether a base LLM can be made safe at the SFT stage using only a very small set of easily obtainable *harmful* responses to toxic prompts, without any safe reference responses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251293/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base (non-SFTed) LLM and a combined dataset of ~20,000 safety-unrelated prompt-response pairs (from Alpaca) plus a small set (as few as 100) of toxic prompt–harmful response pairs, where the harmful responses are generated by the base LLM itself and filtered via the OpenAI Moderation API. The output is a LoRA-fine-tuned LLM that produces safe responses to toxic prompts while preserving response quality on benign prompts. Notably, the safety-related data contains only the undesired (harmful) responses—no safe or refusal responses are provided—making the data requirement roughly 0.5% of the total dataset.

## Method

The core contribution is Toxicity Avoiding SFT (TA-SFT), which augments standard SFT with a semantic penalty that pushes the model's next-token prediction distribution away from the distribution of unsafe next tokens observed in the harmful responses. The penalty is an Earth Mover Distance (EMD) computed over the token vocabulary with a cosine-distance cost between normalized token embeddings, so the model is penalized for generating tokens that are semantically close to toxic tokens, not just the exact tokens. Since the squared-norm cosine distance does not satisfy the triangle inequality, the authors derive a novel lower bound on EMD (Proposition 1) that is tractable to optimize, replacing the intractable linear-program EMD with a closed-form expression involving the difference of embedding-weighted probability vectors. In practice, the data distribution P is approximated by a one-hot vector of the observed next token (following Ren et al., 2023), and the final loss combines standard NLL on safety-unrelated data with the EMD lower-bound loss on safety-related data, weighted by a hyperparameter λ. A simpler likelihood-based variant (NLCL) that minimizes the probability of the exact harmful response without semantic information is also proposed as an ablation, confirming that the semantic EMD penalty is what enables superior data efficiency.

## Results

On AlpacaEval (preference rate over text-davinci-003), EMD achieves 57.37% on Llama 7b, 62.24% on Llama 13b, 71.06% on Mistral 7b, and 73.35% on Llama3.1 8b, slightly outperforming standard SFT (56.14%, 61.99%, 69.81%, 72.05%) and substantially outperforming KTO (35.47%, 50.9%, 64.85%, 61.5%). On BoolQ, all methods are comparable (e.g., EMD 78.75% vs SFT 78.26% on Llama 7b). In the data-efficiency setting with only 100 toxic prompts, EMD yields 0–5 harmful responses across four safety benchmarks for Llama 7b, while NLCL produces 6–42 harmful responses, demonstrating that EMD achieves near-perfect safety with |D_safety-related| ≈ 0.005|D_safety-unrelated|.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard SFT (Wei et al., 2021), EMD matches or slightly exceeds response quality (e.g., 57.37% vs 56.14% AlpacaEval on Llama 7b; 78.75% vs 78.26% BoolQ) while simultaneously achieving 100% safe responses on all four harmfulness benchmarks after 500 training steps. Against KTO (Ethayarajh et al., 2024), EMD substantially outperforms on AlpacaEval (57.37% vs 35.47% on Llama 7b; 73.35% vs 61.5% on Llama3.1 8b) because KTO's reference model is merely a non-SFTed base model, setting an unreasonably low bar; KTO also fails to achieve comparable safety improvements on the DeBERTa harmfulness scores. Against Safety-Tuned Llamas / STL (Bianchi et al., 2023), EMD is safer overall and the gap widens in the low-data regime: with only 100 toxic prompts, EMD produces 0–5 harmful responses on Llama 7b while STL produces 4–70, and on I-CoNa specifically EMD yields 5 vs STL's 70. The paper also shows that over-refusal (measured on XSTest) follows the same safety-vs-over-refusal curve for both EMD/NLCL and STL, empirically refuting the hypothesis that inclusion of refusal responses in the SFT dataset causes over-alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against Safety-Tuned Llamas (STL, Bianchi et al. 2023), which achieves SFT-stage safety by training on high-quality safe responses to toxic prompts generated by a powerful external LLM, and against RLHF methods (KTO, DPO) that require pairwise preference data. The key departure is using only easily available *unsafe* responses with a semantically-informed Earth Mover Distance penalty (with a novel lower bound for tractable optimization) rather than requiring safe exemplars or preference pairs. The NLCL baseline adapts ORPO's odds-ratio loss to the single-response setting, and the EMD formulation draws on optimal transport for language modeling (Ren et al. 2023) but repurposes it as a toxicity-avoidance signal in SFT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving near-zero harmful responses with only ~0.5% safety-related data (vs. 3% for STL) and no external LLM dependency makes SFT-stage safety alignment far more accessible. Two findings open important questions: (1) over-refusal persists even without refusal examples in training data, undermining the intuitive causal explanation and leaving the true mechanism unresolved; (2) augmenting with LLM-generated safe responses to seemingly toxic prompts *degrades* model language ability, echoing the 'curse of recursion' concern. Limitations include evaluation only on 7–13B models and the persistence of over-alignment, which the paper acknowledges but does not solve.
