## Problem

Iterative post-training methods for LLM self-improvement (e.g., STaR, iterative DPO) consistently improve pass@1 accuracy on problem-solving benchmarks, but it remains unclear whether these gains reflect genuine progress in solving harder problems or merely a narrowing of the model's output distribution. No prior work has systematically examined whether self-improvement through post-training inadvertently degrades broader capabilities such as solution diversity and out-of-distribution generalization, leaving the progress-versus-regress question unanswered.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a foundation LLM (LLaMA-2-7B, Mistral-7B, or LLaMA3-8B) and a task-specific dataset of query-answer pairs (CSQA, GSM8K, MATH, or MBPP), then iteratively samples N=50 outputs per query using top-p=0.95 sampling at temperature 0.75 to construct self-generated training sets without any external data. Beyond standard pass@1 evaluation, the framework produces a sequence of fine-tuned models M_1 through M_T scored on improvement-set pass@N (N from 2 to 64), three diversity metrics (distinct n-grams, Sentence-BERT cosine similarity, distinct equations), and OOD generalization measured via whole accuracy and group disparity on a 5-level MATH algebra test set.

## Method

The paper formulates three iterative post-training paradigms—Iterative SFT, Iterative DPO, and Iterative SFT-DPO—within a unified algorithm where each iteration samples the current model's outputs, filters or pairs them by correctness, and applies the corresponding post-training function. A key empirical finding is that the correct answer coverage of M_1 (the proportion of the correct answer space occupied by the model's sampled outputs) predicts which paradigm yields the best improvement: high coverage (>0.5) favors DPO-based methods, while low coverage (≤0.5) favors SFT. The critical evaluation framework introduces an improvement set IS(t) to test whether M_1 already possesses the capability to solve newly-correct problems, revealing that pass@N on IS(t) rapidly saturates and indicating selection optimization rather than new capability acquisition. Diversity and OOD metrics then collectively expose the 'self-improvement reversal' phenomenon where pass@1 gains coincide with declining output diversity and widening performance gaps across difficulty levels.

## Results

Across all three foundation models (LLaMA2-7B, Mistral-7B, LLaMA3-8B) and four benchmarks, iterative post-training consistently improves pass@1 accuracy, with gains of +2 to +12 points depending on model and task. On GSM8K, LLaMA2-7B achieves the largest relative improvement (+12.31 with Iterative SFT, from 41.6 to 53.91), while LLaMA3-8B reaches the highest absolute accuracy (73.31 with Iterative SFT-DPO). A key finding is that correct answer coverage of M₁ (>0.5 vs. ≤0.5) predicts which paradigm is optimal: DPO/SFT-DPO dominate at high coverage, while SFT is preferable at low coverage. Critically, despite pass@1 gains, all three paradigms exhibit self-improvement reversal: solution diversity (syntactic, semantic, and logical) consistently declines, and OOD generalization to MATH algebra is harmed, with Group Disparity widening across difficulty levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines but instead contrasts three iterative post-training paradigms: Iterative SFT, Iterative DPO, and Iterative SFT-DPO. The optimal paradigm depends on the correct answer coverage of M₁: when coverage exceeds 0.5 (e.g., Mistral-7B on GSM8K at 0.627, LLaMA3-8B on CSQA at 0.825), Iterative DPO or SFT-DPO outperforms Iterative SFT by 2–7 points; when coverage is at or below 0.5 (e.g., LLaMA2-7B on GSM8K at 0.398, MATH at 0.167), Iterative SFT is the most effective, beating DPO by up to 5.4 points on GSM8K. On OOD generalization (GSM8K→MATH algebra transfer), Iterative DPO shows the least harm to whole accuracy but still widens Group Disparity between easy and hard levels, while Iterative SFT and SFT-DPO significantly degrade OOD performance. All three paradigms uniformly reduce output diversity (Distinct-1/5, Sentence-BERT cosine similarity, and Distinct Equations), with Iterative DPO retaining slightly higher semantic diversity than Iterative SFT over multiple iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative self-training lineage from STaR (iterative SFT on self-generated reasoning) and iterative preference optimization (Pang et al., 2024) by unifying them under a single framework with three paradigms (Iterative SFT, Iterative DPO, Iterative SFT-DPO) and systematically decoupling the influence of base model, task dataset, iteration count, and post-training method. It departs from prior work primarily in its evaluative contribution: rather than reporting pass@1 gains as evidence of progress, it introduces a multi-faceted framework (improvement-problem pass@N analysis, solution diversity metrics, OOD generalization with group disparity) to interrogate whether those gains represent genuine capability acquisition or merely selection optimization within an existing generation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that iterative self-improvement is largely 'selection optimization'—the model already contained correct answers in its sampling distribution—challenges the foundational assumption that iterative post-training progressively unlocks harder problem-solving. The concurrent collapse in output diversity and widening OOD group disparity suggest that current self-improvement practices may be narrowing rather than broadening model capabilities, with implications for how the field designs and evaluates future self-improvement pipelines. Open questions include whether these reversal effects persist at larger scales (70B+), whether diversity-preserving regularization can decouple accuracy gains from capability narrowing, and whether the correct-answer-coverage heuristic generalizes beyond the tested 7B models and four task domains.
