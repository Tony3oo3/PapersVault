## Problem

Hand-written GPU kernels for AI operations consistently fall short of theoretical performance, and existing frameworks force a tradeoff between expressiveness and usability: CUTLASS offers full C++ power but requires navigating a myriad of nested templates, while compiler-based tools like Triton simplify interfaces but limit access to specialized hardware instructions. The paper asks whether a small, opinionated set of abstractions can simultaneously achieve state-of-the-art performance and remain accessible to AI researchers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250077/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes standard AI tensor inputs—input matrices for GEMM, Q/K/V tensors for attention (causal, non-causal, grouped-query at head dimensions 64 and 128), input sequences with learned filters for SSMs and FFT convolutions, and embedding vectors with precomputed sin/cos tables for rotary encodings—all in BF16, FP16, FP32, or FP8 precision. Outputs are the corresponding tensor results (matrix products, attention outputs, convolved sequences, rotated embeddings) written back to HBM. A key constraint is that all computation is organized around 16×16-element tiles mapped to the GPU's three-level memory hierarchy (registers, shared memory, HBM), with TK automatically selecting swizzled shared-memory layouts (32/64/128-byte boundaries) to minimize bank conflicts while remaining compatible with Hopper's TMA and HGMMA hardware instructions.

## Method

THUNDERKITTENS provides three levels of abstraction mapped to the GPU hierarchy. At the warp level, it uses 16×16 matrix tiles as the basic data structure with PyTorch-like operations (mma, exp, cumsum, pointwise multiply) and automatically assigns swizzled shared-memory layouts that minimize bank conflicts while preserving tensor-core register formats. At the thread-block level, a single Load-Compute-Store-Finish (LCSF) template implements the producer-consumer paradigm: the developer populates four functions (load, compute, store, finish) while the template manages N-stage pipelined shared-memory buffers, synchronization barriers, and asynchronous I/O (wrapping cp.async and TMA) to hide memory latencies. At the grid level, TK supports persistent block launches that eliminate pipeline bubbles by pre-loading the next task's data during the current task's finish stage, and provides block-ordering controls that exploit L2 cache reuse (e.g., a 3D stride over GEMM tiles that avoids the >50% performance degradation of naive row-major ordering at 16384³). The result is a <1 MB library (vs. 22 MB for CUTLASS) whose kernels average under 200 lines of code yet match CuBLAS and FlashAttention-3 on GEMM and attention inference, while outperforming the strongest baselines by 10–40% on attention backwards, 8× on SSMs, and 14× on linear attention.

## Results

THUNDERKITTENS (TK) matches CuBLAS on GEMM (0.98–2.05× speedup range depending on matrix dimensions) and FlashAttention-3 on attention inference (0.87–1.14×), while outperforming the strongest baselines by 10–40% on attention backwards, up to 14× on polynomial-based linear attention, up to 8× on state space models (FFT convolution), and >3× on Mamba-2. TK achieves these results with dramatically fewer lines of code: 84 LoC for GEMM vs. 463 for CUTLASS, and 217 LoC for attention vs. 2,325 for FlashAttention-3. NCU profiling reveals TK eliminates bank conflicts (85% fewer shared-memory stall cycles vs. FA3) and achieves higher issue-slot utilization (34.8% vs. 25.1% on attention backwards).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CuBLAS, TK's single GEMM kernel (84 LoC) matches or exceeds performance across K dimensions 64–1024, winning at K≤512 (e.g., 450 vs. 407 TFLOPS at K=512) and trailing slightly at K=1024 (600 vs. 633 TFLOPS). Against FlashAttention-3, TK matches on the non-causal forward pass but outperforms by over 40% at shorter sequences and 10% at longer sequences on both causal and non-causal backwards passes, with NCU profiling showing TK has zero bank conflicts versus up to 9.6-way conflicts in FA3. Against FlashLinearAttention (FLA, Triton-based), TK achieves 14× speedup on polynomial-based linear attention and 6.5× on learned-map linear attention. Against FlashFFTConv (CUDA), TK achieves 4.7× speedup at sequence length 4096 and 7.9× at 1024, with 4.1× higher tensor core utilization. Against the Mamba-2 Triton kernels (Dao & Gu, 2024), TK achieves >3× speedup primarily through easier fusion of complex operations. Against PyTorch's FFT operations, TK achieves up to 8.7× speedup. On consumer hardware (NVIDIA 4090), TK's attention kernel is competitive with FlashAttention-2 across head dimensions 64 and 128.

## Relation to Prior Work

THUNDERKITTENS positions itself as a deliberately opinionated alternative to two dominant paradigms for GPU kernel development: C++ embedded template libraries (CUTLASS/CuTe) and compiler-based frameworks (Triton, TVM, XLA). It builds on the classical producer-consumer paradigm (Dijkstra, 1968; Bauer et al., 2011) to structure its block-level LCSF template, and draws its tile-based data structure design from the PyTorch/NumPy API philosophy. The key conceptual departure is the hypothesis that a small, fixed set of abstractions (16×16 tiles with managed swizzled layouts, a single asynchronous template, and persistent grid scheduling) can match the performance of far more complex systems, rather than requiring the 'myriad of nested templates' in CUTLASS or the full compiler stack in Triton.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPU kernel development is identified as a critical bottleneck for AI progress, with even well-established operations like attention suffering significant performance degradation across GPU generations. TK's demonstration that ~200 lines of code can match CuBLAS and FlashAttention-3 while outperforming prior art by 10–40% on attention backwards and up to 14× on linear attention suggests that kernel accessibility can be dramatically improved without sacrificing peak performance. Open questions include whether the three-level abstraction (tile, LCSF, persistent grid) generalizes to future GPU architectures with different memory hierarchies, and whether the opinionated layout choices remain optimal as tensor core instruction sets evolve. The paper is limited to H100/4090/M2 evaluation, and the large speedups on emerging architectures (linear attention, SSMs) are against Triton-based baselines that may not represent the absolute ceiling.
