## Problem

Existing 3D steganography methods (e.g., StegaNeRF, GS-Hider) require per-scene optimization to embed watermarks, adding minutes of training overhead per asset—up to 30× the generation time itself. This makes them impractical for large-scale 3D asset production where objects are generated in seconds. The paper asks whether a generalizable, zero-overhead 3D steganography pipeline can be integrated directly into feed-forward 3D generative models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250404/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image as the 3D generation source and a hidden payload (watermark image, text string, QR code, audio waveform, or 16-frame video) to embed. It produces a watermarked 3D Gaussian Splatting whose renderings are visually indistinguishable from the unwatermarked baseline, plus a U-Net decoder that recovers the hidden payload from a rendered image at a designated checking viewpoint among 40 camera poses. The hidden information is encoded as DINOv2 features rather than raw pixels, and the decoder architecture is modality-specific (2D image branch for visual payloads, 1D branch for text/audio).

## Method

InstantSplamp fine-tunes the LGM image-to-3D Gaussian generator via LoRA, injecting hidden information through a cross-attention module where DINOv2 features of the watermark image serve as keys and values while intermediate generation features serve as queries. A U-Net decoder recovers the hidden payload from a rendered checking view, trained with a contrastive loss that simultaneously enforces correct recovery on watermarked renders and suppresses false positives on unwatermarked renders. The key training innovation is Adaptive Gradient Harmonization: a binary mask computed from the global cosine similarity between rendering-loss and steganography-loss gradients either retains or discards all parameter updates, preventing conflicting gradient directions from degrading visual quality. The full loss combines positive/negative decoding losses and an RGB photometric term (λ₁=0.3, λ₂=1, λ₃=0.1), and the model is trained for ~30 epochs on 100 Objaverse objects (~20 min) before generalizing to unseen assets with no per-scene optimization.

## Results

On the Objaverse test set, InstantSplamp achieves the best hidden information recovery with PSNR 32.97, SSIM 0.9808, and LPIPS 0.0082, while maintaining rendering quality nearly identical to the unwatermarked baseline (PSNR 20.45 vs. 20.48, SSIM 0.8519 vs. 0.8522). Subjective evaluation scores of 4.11/5 for rendering and 3.67/5 for recovered watermarks further confirm perceptual fidelity. The method also supports multimodal embedding (text, QR codes, audio, 16-frame video) with modality-specific recovery metrics (ACC for text, SSIM for QR, PSNR for audio).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstantSplamp decisively outperforms both 2D steganography baselines (LSB and DeepStega) on hidden recovery: its hidden PSNR of 32.97 exceeds LSB's 8.36 and DeepStega's 12.11 by more than 20 dB, while rendering quality remains virtually unchanged (PSNR 20.45 vs. 20.45/20.43). Against the closest 3D competitor, StegaNeRF (adapted to a GS pipeline), InstantSplamp wins on both axes: rendering PSNR improves from 18.53 to 20.45 (+1.94 dB) and hidden recovery PSNR improves from 31.87 to 32.97 (+1.10 dB), with SSIM rising from 0.9659 to 0.9808. Subjective scores confirm the gap: InstantSplamp scores 4.11/5 on rendering (vs. StegaNeRF's 2.30) and 3.67/5 on hidden recovery (vs. 3.25). The key practical advantage is that InstantSplamp is a generalizable feed-forward method requiring no per-scene optimization, whereas StegaNeRF and the 2D baselines all require per-object training, making InstantSplamp the only method with near-zero watermarking overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D steganography line from StegaNeRF (per-scene NeRF watermarking) and GS-Hider (concurrent per-scene GS watermarking) to a generalizable, zero-overhead setting by integrating watermark embedding directly into a feed-forward 3D Gaussian generation backbone (LGM). The embedding mechanism repurposes cross-attention—standard in vision transformers—to inject DINOv2-extracted watermark features into intermediate generation features, while a U-Net decoder recovers the hidden signal from rendered views. The Adaptive Gradient Harmonization loss, which masks gradient updates where rendering and steganography objectives conflict, is a novel training technique not seen in prior 2D or 3D watermarking work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is clear: eliminating per-scene watermark optimization (previously ~30× generation time) makes large-scale 3D asset watermarking feasible for production pipelines. However, the evaluation is narrow—100 training objects, 100 test objects, 256×256 resolution, and a single generation backbone (LGM)—leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other feed-forward 3D models, higher resolutions, or more aggressive 3D perturbations (rotation, cropping, re-rendering from arbitrary angles). The reliance on a fixed 'checking viewpoint' for recovery also raises questions about robustness in real-world scenarios where the exact verification pose may not be known.
