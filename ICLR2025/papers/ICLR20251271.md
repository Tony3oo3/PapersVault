## Problem

While prior work showed that decoupling actor and critic networks in on-policy RL improves sample efficiency and generalisation, the underlying mechanism remained unclear—attributed to reduced gradient interference or differential overfitting tolerance. This paper asks whether decoupling enables the actor and critic to specialise in extracting fundamentally different types of information from the environment, and how representation learning objectives interact with this specialisation to affect downstream performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251271/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on trained PPO, PPG, and DCPG agents processing 64×64×3 RGB observations (Procgen) or 64×64×9 framestacked RGB (Brax). Mutual information is estimated via k-NN (k=3, L2 distance) on 4096 uniformly subsampled timesteps drawn from 2^16-step trajectory batches, with odd timesteps, episode boundaries, and incomplete episodes excluded to ensure valid (o_t, o_{t+1}) pairs and computable returns. Outputs include four MI ratios I(Z;·)/I(O;·) for overfitting, value, inverse dynamics, and forward dynamics, plus compression efficiency C(Z;·) scores and normalised train/test returns.

## Method

The paper introduces four mutual information metrics—I(Z;L) for level overfitting, I(Z;V) for value information, I((Z,Z');A) for inverse dynamics, and I(Z;Z') for forward dynamics—and provides theoretical characterisations of the optimal actor representation φ*_A (maximising I((Z,Z');A) while minimising I(Z;L)) and critic representation φ*_C (maximising I(Z;V) and I(Z;Z') at the cost of higher I(Z;L)), proving their mutual incompatibility under a shared architecture. Empirically, the study decouples PPO, PPG, and DCPG in Procgen and Brax, applying representation learning objectives (MICo, dynamics prediction, data augmentation, advantage prediction) to the actor, critic, or both, then measures how each objective shifts the four MI quantities and agent returns. A key mechanistic finding is that the critic, acting as a baseline in the actor's advantage targets, biases policy updates to collect trajectories informative for its own learning objective—a data collection bias confirmed by showing I(O;V) increases when the critic is decoupled or given MICo, while the actor's compression efficiency C(Z_A;V) remains unchanged, ruling out implicit knowledge transfer.

## Results

When actor and critic representations are decoupled in PPO on Procgen, the actor's representation systematically specialises in action-relevant information (I((Z,Z');A) increases by +23%) while the critic's specialises in value and dynamics information (I(Z;Z') increases by +324%, I(Z;V) by +41%). The decoupled architecture is more parameter-efficient: it outperforms a shared model with four times its parameter count (Figure 4). Among auxiliary objectives, data augmentation improves both train and test scores across all three algorithms (PPO, PPG, DCPG), dynamics prediction improves PPG and DCPG but not PPO, and MICo generally decreases performance by inducing overfitting (increasing I(Z;L)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four representation learning objectives (MICo, dynamics prediction, data augmentation, advantage prediction) applied to the actor, critic, or both, across PPO, PPG, and DCPG. Data augmentation (Raileanu et al., 2021) is the most consistently beneficial, improving both train and test returns for all three algorithms by increasing data diversity. Dynamics prediction (Moon et al., 2022) improves PPG and DCPG performance but is ineffective for PPO due to its smaller batch size; it enhances I((Z,Z');A) while reducing overfitting-related quantities. MICo (Castro et al., 2021) generally degrades performance by increasing I(Z;L) and I(Z;V) at the expense of I((Z,Z');A), and when applied to the critic it induces a data collection bias that further harms the actor. Advantage prediction (Raileanu & Fergus, 2021) has a neutral effect on performance but interestingly, when combined with MICo on the critic, it cancels the critic's data collection bias and produces a sharp performance gain. Decoupled PPO without any value distillation still achieves 37% higher I(Z_A;V) than shared PPO, and the decoupled architecture outperforms a shared model with 4× the parameters, confirming that the gains are not merely from increased capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the decoupled actor-critic architecture introduced by Cobbe et al. (2021) and Raileanu & Fergus (2021), which reported performance gains from separating actor and critic networks but attributed them primarily to reduced gradient interference. Here, the authors provide a fundamentally different theoretical interpretation: decoupling enables information specialisation, with the actor's and critic's optimal representations being provably incompatible under a shared architecture. The analysis draws on the CMDP evaluation framework of Kirk et al. (2023), the generalisation error bound of Bertran et al. (2020), and generalises the Markov representation conditions of Allen et al. (2021) to continuous mutual information metrics, while empirically comparing representation learning objectives from Castro et al. (2021), Raileanu et al. (2021), Moon et al. (2022), and Raileanu & Fergus (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that the critic's learning objective biases data collection through advantage estimates reveals a previously underappreciated coupling mechanism in decoupled actor-critic algorithms, with direct implications for exploration strategy design. The finding that level-invariant information extraction matters more than the specific quantity targeted (values vs. dynamics) provides a practical design principle for auxiliary objectives. Open questions include extending the analysis to off-policy algorithms, designing objectives that explicitly target level-invariant information, and leveraging the critic's data collection bias as a controllable exploration mechanism. The study is limited to on-policy methods and two benchmark suites, and the mutual information metrics, while theoretically grounded, rely on k-NN estimators that may not scale to very high-dimensional latent spaces.
