## Problem

Existing long video understanding benchmarks rely heavily on multiple-choice questions, where models can achieve correct answers through option elimination and general knowledge without genuinely engaging with the relevant video content. This undermines evaluation credibility, as high MCQ scores do not confirm that a model actually retrieved and understood the specific visual evidence needed. A comprehensive benchmark that verifies whether models ground their answers in accurate video segments was missing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251637/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes long videos (10–80 min, avg. ~27 min) as uniformly sampled frames (128 for long-video MCQ, 32 for clue-based MCQ), optionally augmented with subtitles, subtitle timestamps, frame timestamps, and audio. For white-box evaluation, models must additionally output clue intervals as nested timestamp lists [[s1,e1],[s2,e2],...]; for open-ended QA, models produce free-text answers that are scored by a GPT-4o-based heuristic judge that conditionally requests visual frames from the annotated clue intervals. The evaluation suite outputs MCQ accuracy, mIoU/recall@IoU for temporal grounding, acc@IoU (joint answer+grounding correctness), Clue Recovery Rate, and open-ended accuracy.

## Method

CG-Bench introduces a clue-grounded evaluation framework built on 12,129 human-annotated Question-Answer-Clue (QAC) triplets spanning 1,219 videos, where each triplet includes 14,362 temporal clue intervals (avg. 19.24 s) marking the exact video segments needed to answer the question. Two novel credibility metrics are proposed: (1) a white-box evaluation requiring models to explicitly predict clue intervals, scored via tIoU and a joint acc@IoU metric that demands both correct MCQ selection and non-zero temporal overlap with ground-truth clues; and (2) a black-box evaluation defining Clue Recovery Rate (CRR) as min(long-acc, clue-acc)/clue-acc, measuring how well a model retains clue-relevant information when the full video context dilutes the signal. For open-ended QA, a two-step heuristic evaluation method uses GPT-4o to first judge text-only consistency and only escalate to visual verification (sampling frames from annotated clue intervals) when the prediction and ground truth differ in non-fundamental ways, achieving 1.0% bias versus human evaluation at 14% visual-trigger rate and ~$2 cost per 1000 samples.

## Results

On CG-Bench long-video MCQ (128 frames), GPT-4o-08-06 achieves the highest long-acc. of 45.2% and clue-acc. of 58.3%, while the best open-source model Qwen2-VL-72B reaches 41.3% long-acc. and 56.2% clue-acc. However, credibility metrics reveal a dramatic gap: GPT-4o's Acc@IoU drops to 4.38 (from 45.2% MCQ) and its open-ended accuracy is only 39.5%, compared to 90.3% for human full-video evaluation. The Clue Recovery Rate (CRR) for GPT-4o is 77.5%, indicating substantial degradation when retrieving short clues from long, diluted video context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o-08-06 outperforms all other models across every metric, achieving 45.2% long-acc. versus 42.2% for InternVL2-78B (best open-source on long-acc.) and 41.3% for Qwen2-VL-72B. Among open-source models, Qwen2-VL-72B and InternVL2-78B form a clear first tier (clue-acc. 56–59%, long-acc. 41–42%), substantially ahead of mid-tier models like VITA (33.3%), Kangaroo (30.2%), and LLaVA-OneVision (31.1%). Smaller open-source models (7B or below) cluster in the 16–30% long-acc. range, with Video-LLaVA (16.2%) and VideoChat2 (19.3%) near the bottom. The critical finding is that even GPT-4o's credibility scores collapse: Acc@IoU drops from 45.2% (MCQ) to 4.38%, and CRR of 77.5% means the model loses ~22% of its clue-level accuracy when forced to retrieve clues from the full long video. Gemini-1.5-Pro trails GPT-4o by 8 points on long-acc. and 3.2 points on CRR, while Claude-3.5-Sonnet (evaluated with only 50 frames) achieves 40.5% long-acc., suggesting it would be competitive with more frames. Human evaluation with 128 sparse frames yields 59.9% long-acc., confirming that the benchmark is genuinely challenging and that all current MLLMs remain well below human performance under equivalent visual constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

CG-Bench extends the line of long-video QA benchmarks (VideoMME, MLVU, LongVideoBench, EgoSchema) by introducing human-annotated clue intervals that enable credibility assessment beyond standard MCQ accuracy. It departs from NExT-GQA, which attempted to address answer credibility via temporal grounding but was restricted to short NextQA clips, by operating on 10–80 minute open-domain videos with a three-tier taxonomy. The evaluation methodology borrows tIoU from the video temporal grounding / moment retrieval literature and repurposes it as a diagnostic for whether a model's MCQ answer is genuinely grounded in the relevant video segment, rather than derived from elimination or world knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that GPT-4o's accuracy collapses from 53.9% (long-video MCQ) to 4.4% (Acc@IoU) and 39.5% (open-ended) when answers must be grounded in specific temporal intervals—reveals that current MLLMs largely succeed on long-video MCQs through option elimination rather than genuine video comprehension. This has broad implications: it suggests that MCQ-only benchmarks systematically overestimate long-video understanding, and that the ability to retrieve short, relevant clues from long, diluted contexts remains a major unsolved challenge. Open questions include whether scaling context length or improving temporal attention mechanisms can close the credibility gap, and whether the open-source-to-commercial performance gap (Qwen2-VL-72B at 51.4 vs. GPT-4o at 53.9 on MCQ, but a wider gap on credibility metrics) will narrow with further training.
