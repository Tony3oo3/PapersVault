## Problem

The classical Gromov-Wasserstein distance is restricted to probability mm-spaces with equal total mass, and while unbalanced GW (UGW) extends to general measures, it only yields a discrepancy without established metric properties. This paper identifies that no rigorous metric exists in the literature for comparing arbitrary (non-probability) metric measure spaces via GW-type distances, and addresses this gap by proposing a formulation that provably satisfies all metric axioms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250723/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two discrete mm-spaces specified by point sets with non-negative weights (which need not sum to 1) and their pairwise distance matrices raised to a power q ≥ 1. It outputs a scalar PGW distance value, an n×m transportation plan matrix γ with row/column sums bounded by the weight vectors (i.e., γ ∈ Γ≤(p,q)), and, via the PGW barycenter formulation, an interpolated distance matrix C that can be embedded into Euclidean space via SMACOF MDS to recover a point cloud. The key constraint distinguishing it from GW is the inequality constraint γ1_m ≤ p, γᵀ1_n ≤ q rather than equality.

## Method

The PGW problem replaces the KL-divergence penalty of UGW with a total variation penalty on the product measures, yielding an objective over partial transportation plans γ ∈ Γ≤(μ,ν) with a quadratic GW cost minus a 2λ|γ|² term plus a constant. The central theoretical result proves that (PGW)^{1/p} is a metric on mm-spaces (modulo isometry equivalence), with the triangle inequality established by converting the PGW problem into a standard GW problem on augmented spaces with auxiliary points. For computation, two Frank-Wolfe solvers are proposed: in each iteration, the direction-finding subproblem is a discrete POT problem, which is solved by the Caffarelli–McCann reduction to a standard OT problem (solved via network simplex or similar). The two solver variants differ only in whether the −2λ shift is absorbed into the cost tensor or handled separately, but are proven mathematically equivalent. A PGW barycenter formulation is also introduced, extending the GW barycenter to the unbalanced setting for shape interpolation.

## Results

On the shape retrieval task, PGW achieves 96.25% SVM accuracy on Dataset I (8 classes, 160 shapes) and 100% on Dataset II (6 classes, 120 shapes), outperforming MPGW (23.75% / 25.00%) and UGW (89.38% / 90.00%), while being competitive with GW (98.13% / 80.83%). PGW is also the fastest method, requiring 35.92s and 79.27s for all pairwise distances on Datasets I and II respectively, compared to 1484.49s and 519.91s for UGW. In shape interpolation with 5% and 10% noise, PGW barycenters produce interpolations with consistent width-height ratios, whereas GW barycenters are visibly distorted by outlier points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGW decisively outperforms MPGW (Chapel et al., 2020) on both retrieval datasets, achieving 96.25% vs. 23.75% on Dataset I and 100% vs. 25.00% on Dataset II, while also being slightly faster (35.92s vs. 49.10s). Against UGW (Séjourné et al., 2021), PGW wins on accuracy on both datasets (96.25% vs. 89.38% and 100% vs. 90.00%) and is dramatically faster — roughly 41× speedup on Dataset I (35.92s vs. 1484.49s) and 6.6× on Dataset II. Compared to classical GW (Mémoli, 2011), PGW achieves a small accuracy drop on Dataset I (96.25% vs. 98.13%) but a large gain on Dataset II (100% vs. 80.83%), and is consistently faster in wall-clock time. In the shape interpolation experiment, PGW barycenters (extending Peyré et al., 2016) are significantly less disturbed by 5% and 10% noise than GW barycenters, maintaining natural width-height ratios throughout the interpolation sequence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) distance from Mémoli (2011) to non-probability metric measure spaces by adopting the unbalanced GW framework of Séjourné et al. (2021) but replacing the KL-divergence penalty with a total variation penalty. The key technical departure is the use of the Caffarelli–McCann (2010) reduction to convert the direction-finding subproblem in the Frank-Wolfe solver into a standard OT problem, yielding two mathematically equivalent but computationally distinct solvers. The PGW barycenter formulation directly generalizes the GW barycenter of Peyré et al. (2016) to the unbalanced setting, and the paper explicitly positions itself as the first rigorous metric (as opposed to a mere discrepancy) for arbitrary mm-spaces based on the GW framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The metric property of PGW is a meaningful theoretical gap-filler: prior unbalanced GW formulations (UGW with KL penalty) could only be interpreted as discrepancies, limiting their use in applications requiring a true distance (e.g., nearest-neighbor retrieval, barycenter computation with well-defined geometry). Practically, the total-variation-based PGW provides robustness to outliers and noise in shape matching and interpolation tasks, with wall-clock times comparable to balanced GW and substantially faster than UGW. Open questions include whether the metric property extends to other divergence choices (e.g., Hellinger-Kantorovich), how the method scales to very large point clouds beyond the O(n²m²) gradient computation, and whether the Frank-Wolfe convergence rate can be improved for the non-convex PGW objective.
