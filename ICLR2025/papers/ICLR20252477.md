## Problem

Variational MI estimators avoid the fundamental O(log N) sample-complexity ceiling of discriminative methods (MINE, InfoNCE, NWJ), but their Gaussian variational surrogates are too inflexible to capture multimodal or nonlinear target distributions. This paper closes that gap by replacing the Gaussian assumption with normalizing flows while preserving the efficient moment-matching optimality that makes variational estimators computationally attractive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252477/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The estimators consume only paired samples {(x_i, y_i)} drawn from p(X,Y) — no explicit density or likelihood is required — and output a scalar MI estimate computed in closed form from moment-matched Gaussian parameters in the flow-transformed space. For the BOED application, the method additionally takes a prior p(X), a likelihood p(Y|X,d), and a sequence of T decisions, producing a greedy sequence of design points that maximizes total expected information gain. The flows used are rational quadratic splines with 128 learned knots bounded to [-8, 8], with dropout (rate 0.2) and L2 regularization (1e-5) to prevent overfitting.

## Method

The paper introduces two flow-based variational estimators. JVF applies diffeomorphisms f(X) and g(Y) to the variables, then moment-matches the resulting joint Gaussian q_{Z,V}(Z,V) to the target moments E_p[(f(X), g(Y))] and Cov_p(f(X), g(Y)); Theorem 3.1 proves this moment-matching is optimal for minimizing the variational bound, and the MI estimate reduces to the closed-form Gaussian MI of the transformed variables (the log-determinant terms cancel by MI invariance). NVF removes the joint-distribution assumption by applying separate flows f_marg and f_post to the marginal and posterior, with the posterior mean and covariance parameterized by neural networks conditioned on Y; here the log-determinant terms do not cancel, so the MI is computed via Monte Carlo. Training minimizes a modified upper bound (Lemma 3.3) that includes log-determinant terms from both flows, which the authors found necessary to prevent the Y-flow from overfitting. The key architectural insight is that the moment-matching optimality of the Gaussian case (Theorem 2.2) extends naturally to the flow-transformed space, giving a simple, gradient-free parameter update for the base distribution at each training step.

## Results

In the high-MI experiment (15-dim Gaussian, ρ=0.95, true MI=17.459), JVG and JVF converge nearly instantaneously to the true value, while all four discriminative methods (MINE, InfoNCE, NWJ, DV) plateau at O(log N) ≈ 5–6 nats. In the 36-distribution MI benchmark, flow-based estimators (JVF, NVF) consistently outperform their Gaussian counterparts (JVG, NVG), with NVF uniquely handling fat-tailed Student-t distributions via inverse hyperbolic sin regularization. In the Location Finding BOED task (Table 2), NVF achieves TEIG of 5.10±0.20 (matched prior) and 4.75±0.18 (mismatched prior), outperforming JVG (3.56), JVF (4.30), and NVG (4.61) in the matched setting, while iDAD achieves the highest matched TEIG (7.54) but requires 8827 s of offline training and suffers a 30% TEIG drop under prior mismatch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four discriminative estimators (DV, MINE, NWJ, InfoNCE), the proposed flow-based methods win decisively on the high-MI benchmark: all discriminative methods are theoretically capped at O(log N) and empirically converge to values far below the true MI of 17.459, while JVG/JVF reach the true value almost immediately. In the 36-distribution MI benchmark, JVF generally outperforms JVG and NVF outperforms NVG, with the gap most pronounced on multimodal and fat-tailed distributions; the only case where discriminative methods win is uniform additive noise. Against CCA and KSG, the flow-based estimators match or exceed performance across most of the 36 test distributions. In the BOED Location Finding task, NVF outperforms JVG by 1.54 nats and NVG by 0.49 nats in TEIG under the matched prior, and shows the smallest degradation under prior mismatch (≈7% drop vs. iDAD's 30%). iDAD (InfoNCE) achieves the highest absolute TEIG (7.54) in the matched setting but requires ~8827 s of offline training and is fragile to model mismatch, whereas NVF requires no offline training and maintains 4.75 TEIG under mismatch with only 1253 s deployment time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational MI estimation framework of Foster et al. (2019) and Dahlke et al. (2023) by replacing Gaussian variational distributions with normalizing flows (specifically rational quadratic spline flows). The key theoretical contribution is proving that the moment-matching optimality condition (Theorem 2.2 for Gaussians) carries over to flow-based distributions (Theorem 3.1), enabling efficient parameter computation without gradient descent for the joint flow estimator. The paper positions itself against discriminative-based estimators (MINE, InfoNCE, NWJ, DV) which McAllester & Stratos (2020) showed require exponential samples for large MI, and acknowledges a closely related concurrent work by Butakov et al. (2024) that proposes a similar joint flow estimator but lacks the neural flow variant (NVF).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The flow-based estimators provide a principled way to estimate large MI values without the fundamental sample complexity ceiling of discriminative methods, while maintaining the computational advantages of variational approaches over nested Monte Carlo. The BOED application demonstrates practical value: NVF maintains informative decisions under model mismatch where the amortized iDAD baseline suffers a 30% performance drop, suggesting these methods are more robust for real-world sequential decision making. Open questions include scalability to high dimensions (increased flow parameters), handling distributions where even flows struggle (e.g., additive noise), and whether the need for both flow and neural components (as shown in location finding) can be reduced to simplify training.
