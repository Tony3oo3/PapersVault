## Problem

Standard training pipelines for molecular property prediction fail to learn discriminative representations for molecules with activity cliffs (AC) — pairs of structurally similar molecules with markedly different properties. While prior work identified AC as a source of prediction errors at inference time, no existing study investigated why models cannot fit these molecules during training or proposed a training algorithm to address this gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes molecular graphs (atoms as nodes, bonds as edges) and a pre-constructed matched molecule pair (MMP) graph where edges connect molecules sharing a common structural core with small variable parts (≤13 heavy atoms). It produces per-molecule property predictions (binary labels for classification tasks on MoleculeNet, continuous bioactivity values for regression tasks on ChEMBL). The MMP graph is dataset-specific and property-specific: the same pair of molecules may form an AC edge for one receptor response but not another, so the edge set changes per prediction task.

## Method

LAC (Learning with Activity Cliff) reformulates molecular property prediction as node classification on the MMP graph and introduces two complementary training tasks. At the node level, a weighted curriculum learning scheme selects hard samples for each mini-batch using a weighted loss ℓ̂_i = p_i · ℓ_i where p_i = 1 for AC molecules and p_i = p < 1 otherwise, so that equally difficult molecules with AC are prioritized. At the edge level, a pairwise loss ℓ_eij = −(y_i − y_j)(ŷ_i − ŷ_j) directly penalizes the model for failing to separate AC pairs, with its own curriculum schedule R(t) that progressively focuses on the hardest-to-separate pairs. The two losses are combined as L(w;B̂) + α·L_e(w;Â) and optimized jointly, making LAC a plug-in training procedure compatible with any base model (randomly initialized GIN/GraphGPS or pre-trained 3D-PGT/UniMol/GraphMVP) without architectural changes.

## Results

On the 8 MoleculeNet classification benchmarks, LAC consistently improves every base model. The strongest absolute performance is achieved by UniMol+LAC, reaching 80.2 ROC-AUC on Tox21, 82.7 on MUV, 86.4 on Bace, and 92.2 on ClinTox. The largest relative gains come from randomly-initialized GraphGPS (e.g., +5.6 on ToxCast: 68.5→73.7) and pre-trained 3D-PGT (e.g., +4.8 on ToxCast: 69.2→74.0). On 5 ChEMBL regression targets, MLP(ECFP)+LAC reduces MAE by 0.015–0.034 over the ECFP baseline (e.g., 0.692→0.656 on 5-HT1A).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAC is a plug-in training algorithm evaluated on five base models: GIN, GraphGPS (both randomly initialized), and GraphMVP, 3D-PGT, UniMol (all pre-trained). It improves every base model on every dataset, with the largest gains on randomly-initialized GraphGPS (+2.5 to +5.6 ROC-AUC) and pre-trained 3D-PGT (+1.4 to +5.7), suggesting the method is most impactful when the base model has not already learned discriminative representations. Among pre-trained models, UniMol+LAC achieves the best overall performance, winning 7 of 8 classification tasks; 3D-PGT+LAC edges it out only on ToxCast (74.0 vs. 72.5). The improvement magnitude correlates with the proportion of activity-cliff pairs in each dataset: Tox21 (~9.8% AC ratio) and Sider (~26.7%) show larger gains than MUV (~0.12% AC ratio), where LAC's benefit is minimal. On regression tasks, MLP(ECFP)+LAC outperforms the ECFP baseline on all five ChEMBL targets, with the largest improvement on MOR (0.845→0.827 MAE). No baseline is ever degraded by adding LAC, confirming the method's robustness across architectures and initialization schemes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the curriculum learning framework (Wang et al., 2022) to molecular property prediction by incorporating activity cliff (AC) information into sample selection, and builds on the matched molecule pair definition from Dablander et al. (2023) to construct a pairwise graph structure. It departs from prior AC studies (van Tilborg et al., 2022; Deng et al., 2023; Graff et al., 2023) that only analyzed model failures at inference time, by identifying and addressing the root cause at the training stage. The node-classification reformulation on a matched-molecule-pair graph and the edge-level pairwise loss are novel contributions that go beyond prior curriculum learning for molecules (e.g., CurrMG, Gu et al., 2022), which treated each molecule's difficulty independently rather than exploiting pairwise structural relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first empirical evidence that standard training pipelines systematically fail to fit AC molecules during training (not just at inference), which reframes how the community should think about molecular representation learning. The plug-and-play nature of LAC (applicable to both randomly-initialized and pre-trained backbones) makes it practically useful, though the gains are modest (1–5 ROC-AUC points). Open questions include whether the predefined matched-molecule-pair graph could be learned end-to-end, whether the approach generalizes to other types of structurally-confusable samples beyond AC, and whether the modest improvements scale to larger, more diverse molecular datasets where AC pairs are rarer (e.g., MUV with only 0.12% AC ratio).
