## Problem

Existing finetuning-based hallucination mitigation methods for MLLMs (e.g., HA-DPO, EOS) apply sequence-level preference losses that penalize all tokens in a hallucinated response, providing a coarse and noisy training signal. This causes the model to substantially diverge from its base state, degrading general vision-language capabilities. The paper identifies that hallucinations are inherently local—confined to specific words or phrases—and argues that a fine-grained, phrase-level objective is needed to mitigate hallucination without sacrificing the model's broader competence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252656/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (336×336 for LLaVA-v1.5, 384×384 for VILA-v1.5) paired with a text instruction (descriptive prompts like 'Describe the image in detail' or discriminative yes/no questions) and produces free-form image descriptions, short VQA answers, or single-word yes/no responses. During training, each sample is consumed as a triple: the vision-language instruction, a correct response, and a hallucinated response constructed by selectively swapping ground-truth phrases (objects, attributes, actions, locations) with co-occurring but absent concepts, while keeping the surrounding sentence structure intact. A separate set of reference instruction-response pairs is also fed to the model to compute the KL-divergence regularizer.

## Method

DPA (Data-augmented Phrase-level Alignment) is a two-term training objective applied via LoRA fine-tuning (freezing the vision encoder and projection layers) to off-the-shelf MLLMs. The first term, a phrase-level alignment loss, computes the log-probability ratio of each hallucinated phrase versus its corresponding correct phrase and penalizes only the tokens within those hallucinated spans, rather than the entire sequence as in DPO. The second term is a token-wise forward KL divergence between the trained model and a frozen reference model initialized from the same checkpoint, which restricts how far the model can drift from its base state. The forward (rather than reverse) KL is used because no rollouts of the trained model are performed during training—only reference-model outputs are available. Generative data augmentation produces 21.5K correct/hallucinated pairs from Visual Genome using three strategies: closed-set co-occurrence swaps, open-set LLM-sampled hallucinations, and yes/no inversions. The final loss is L_dpa = L_a + α·L_d, with α tuned per model size (0.2–0.5), and training takes 1.5–3 hours on 4 A100 GPUs for one epoch.

## Results

On the AMBER benchmark, HALVA_7B reduces the hallucination rate from 36.4% to 32.2% (↓4.2) while increasing ground-truth object coverage from 51.0% to 53.0%, and improves the discriminative F1 score from 74.7 to 83.4 (↑8.7). HALVA_13B achieves an F1 of 86.5 (↑13.4 over base) and HALVA_13B/384 reaches 87.9, slightly surpassing GPT-4V (87.4) on discriminative tasks. On MME-Hall, HALVA_13B gains +31.7 points over LLaVA-v1.5_13B (675.0 vs 643.3). Crucially, HALVA maintains or improves general vision-language performance (e.g., MME +16.3, MM-Vet +1.0, LLaVA-Bench +1.8 for 7B), whereas HA-DPO and EOS show statistically significant drops on these tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the base models (LLaVA-v1.5, VILA-v1.5), HALVA consistently reduces hallucination across all benchmarks while preserving or improving general capabilities. Against HA-DPO, HALVA_7B achieves a substantially higher AMBER discriminative F1 (83.4 vs 78.1) and maintains object coverage (53.0% vs 49.8%), whereas HA-DPO degrades coverage and general tasks (MME drops by 8.1 points). Against EOS, HALVA_7B achieves F1 of 83.4 vs 75.6 and preserves generation length (92.2 vs 79.7 words), while EOS substantially shortens outputs and drops MME by 102.6 points. Against inference-based methods (Woodpecker, VCD), HALVA avoids the coverage degradation (Woodpecker drops coverage by 2.1%) and the inference-time overhead. On the 13B scale, HALVA_13B/384 (F1 87.9) marginally outperforms GPT-4V (87.4) on AMBER discriminative tasks, while HALVA_13B (F1 86.5) surpasses all other 13B finetuning baselines including RLHF-V (87.1) and CODE. On HallusionBench (beyond object hallucination), HALVA_13B/384 reduces Yes/No bias from 0.19 to 0.02 and improves overall accuracy by 1.21%, confirming DPA's benefit extends to visual-illusion hallucinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the DPO-based hallucination mitigation line (HA-DPO, RLHF-V, RLAIF-V) by replacing the sequence-level preference loss with a phrase-level alignment objective that penalizes only the tokens where hallucination occurs, combined with a token-wise forward KL regularizer to anchor the model to its initial state. The generative data augmentation strategy for constructing hallucinated/correct pairs repurposes ideas from NLP data augmentation (Qin et al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2024) to the multimodal setting, using co-occurrence statistics and LLM prompting to create targeted negatives. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of preference optimization and contrastive learning, but its key distinction is the localized, fine-grained feedback signal that avoids the model degeneration observed with sequence-level losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DPA provides a lightweight (LoRA, 1.5–3 hours on 4 A100s) drop-in fine-tuning recipe for off-the-shelf MLLMs that reduces hallucination without the capability degradation that plagues existing finetuning-based methods, making it directly deployable in safety-critical applications like medical imaging. The observation that phrase-level alignment also mitigates non-object hallucinations (visual illusions, Yes/No bias) without explicit training for them suggests the approach may generalize to other forms of multimodal unfaithfulness. Open questions include whether the method extends to video or audio modalities, whether the phrase-level granularity is optimal versus token-level or span-level alternatives, and whether the generative data augmentation pipeline scales to open-domain scenarios beyond object-centric datasets like Visual Genome.
