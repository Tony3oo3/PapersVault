## Problem

Existing CBwK algorithms in the small budget regime B=Ω(√T) either require a strict feasibility (Slater's) assumption with a known safety margin, or rely on two-stage parameter search procedures. This paper asks whether a single-stage, fully adaptive algorithm can achieve optimal regret under small budgets without any prior knowledge of the feasibility margin or the problem instance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250133/cropped_page20_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round the learner receives a context vector x_t (136-dimensional in the MSLR-WEB30k experiments) and selects one of 20 actions, receiving a noisy reward and K-dimensional cost vector. The algorithm requires only the initial budget B, time horizon T, and access to online learning oracles (gradient-boosted tree regression for reward, empirical mean for cost) that produce optimistic/pessimistic estimators with bounded cumulative error O(√T log T). The interaction hard-stops when any resource budget is exhausted, with zero reward and cost for remaining rounds.

## Method

AUPD is a single-stage primal-dual algorithm with two coupled components: (1) a budget-aware decision rule that maximizes V·r̂_t(x_t,a) − Σ_k Q_t^(k)·ĉ_t^(k)(x_t,a) where V=b√T is an adaptive tradeoff parameter (smaller V under tighter budgets prompts more conservative choices), and (2) virtual queue updates Q_{t+1}^(k)=[Q_t^(k)+ĉ_t^(k)(x_t,a_t)−b]^+ that track cumulative over-consumed budget without any upper bound or conservative factor. The key theoretical insight is treating the virtual queues as a Markovian process and applying Lyapunov drift analysis to establish their expected upper bound, which translates into a lower bound on the stopping time. This eliminates the need for two-stage parameter search, strict feasibility assumptions, or known safety margins, yielding Ō((1+ν*/δb)√T) regret under strict feasibility and the first-ever Ō(√T+ν*/√b·T^{3/4}) worst-case regret without it.

## Results

Theoretically, AUPD achieves Õ((1+ν*/δb)√T) regret under strict feasibility and Õ(√T + ν*/√b · T^{3/4}) regret without strict feasibility, both in the small budget regime B=Ω(√T) — the latter being the first such result in the literature. Experimentally, on MSLR-WEB30k (T=5000), AUPD achieves the highest average cumulative reward across all tested budgets: 0.344 at B=1000, 0.238 at B=600, 0.233 at B=100, and 0.105 at B=30. The advantage is most pronounced in the tightest budget regime (B=30), where AUPD outperforms the next-best baseline by nearly 9×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AUPD is compared against two baselines: PGD Adaptive (Chzhen et al., 2024), a primal-dual algorithm with a doubling trick for stepsize search that requires a known safety margin, and SquareCBwK (Han et al., 2023), a two-stage algorithm requiring B=Ω(T^{3/4}). AUPD outperforms both baselines across all four budget levels. The margin is largest in the small-budget regime: at B=30, AUPD achieves 0.105 cumulative reward versus 0.012 for PGD Adaptive (≈9×) and 0.007 for SquareCBwK (≈15×); at B=100, AUPD scores 0.233 versus 0.051 and 0.028 respectively. At larger budgets (B=600 and B=1000), the gap narrows considerably — AUPD leads PGD Adaptive by only 0.008 and 0.008, and SquareCBwK by 0.064 and 0.046 — suggesting the baselines recover when the budget constraint is less binding. The key practical distinction is that AUPD requires no prior knowledge of the safety margin and no parameter-search stage, while PGD Adaptive needs δb known a priori and SquareCBwK is theoretically limited to B=Ω(T^{3/4}).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual framework for contextual bandits with knapsacks (CBwK) established by Agrawal & Devanur (2016), Han et al. (2023), and Chzhen et al. (2024), but fundamentally departs from their two-stage designs (warm-up + decision) and their reliance on strict feasibility assumptions or known safety margins. The key conceptual departure is the use of Lyapunov drift analysis on virtual queues (borrowed from stochastic network optimization, Neely 2010; Hajek 1982) to simultaneously bound budget consumption and regret in a single adaptive pass, replacing the doubling tricks and parameter search stages used in prior work. The budget-aware tradeoff parameter V = b√T, which scales with both the average budget and the time horizon, is a novel design choice that makes the algorithm fully adaptive without any prior knowledge of the problem instance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first regret guarantee for CBwK in the small budget regime (B = Ω(√T)) without the strict feasibility (Slater's) assumption, a gap that existed even in the large budget regime (B = Ω(T)). This is practically significant because in real applications (limited inventory, scarce expert queries, constrained ad budgets), the feasibility margin is typically unknown and may not exist. The open question of whether the worst-case bound Õ(√T + ν*/√b · T^{3/4}) is tight without strict feasibility remains unresolved, as no matching lower bound is known. The single-stage adaptive design also suggests a broader template for constrained online learning problems where prior knowledge of constraint tightness is unavailable.
