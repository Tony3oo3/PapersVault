## Problem

When a RAG provider incorporates a data owner's documents into their retrieval corpus without permission, there is currently no way to conclusively prove this unauthorized usage or enforce an opt-out. This paper formalizes the problem as black-box RAG Dataset Inference (RAG-DI) and shows that existing datasets from adjacent RAG privacy work are fundamentally unsuitable for evaluation because they may overlap with LLM training data and fail to model fact redundancy—a key property of real-world RAG corpora that makes detection significantly harder.

## Input / Output

The data owner proactively watermarks each document in their dataset D_do by rephrasing it with a watermarked LLM before publication, then generates one open-ended content-related question per document and submits these as black-box queries to the RAG system. The method aggregates the watermark detector's z-scores across all n responses into a single joint p-value (following Sander et al., 2024), which is compared against a threshold α (≈3×10⁻⁵, corresponding to a z-score of 4) to produce a binary IN/OUT decision with a controlled Type 1 error rate. Crucially, individual responses need not be flagged as watermarked; the joint test can detect even a 1% increase in green-token ratio when n ≥ 100.

## Method

WARD embeds red-green LLM watermarks (PositionPRF with h=2, δ=3.5) into each document of the owner's dataset via watermarked paraphrasing, then audits the RAG system by querying it with natural, content-related questions derived from each watermarked document. The key insight is that the watermark signal, though degraded through the RAG pipeline (retrieval + LLM generation), persists at a level detectable by a joint statistical test across all responses: the required green-token ratio above baseline decreases as O(1/√|R|), so even weak per-response signals become jointly significant with enough queries. The low context width h=2 is deliberately chosen to maximize watermark propagation through RAG, since the data owner never exposes unconstrained query access to watermarked content (unlike LLM watermarking). This design yields three properties absent in all baselines: monotonic improvement with more queries, rigorous statistical guarantees with exceedingly rare false positives, and robustness to system-prompt defenses and MEMFREE decoding that break semantic-similarity-based methods.

## Results

WARD achieves 100% accuracy (zero false positives and zero false negatives) across all evaluation settings: both FARAD-Easy and FARAD-Hard, with both Naive-P and Def-P system prompts, and across all three LLMs (GPT-3.5, Claude 3 Haiku, Llama 3.1-70B). In the Hard setting (with fact redundancy), all baselines (SIB, AAG, FACTS) fail with both false positives and false negatives, while WARD maintains perfect accuracy. WARD's statistical p-values for IN cases drop to as low as 10⁻³⁰¹ (Claude 3 Haiku, Easy, Naive-P) and remain below 10⁻¹⁰ even in the most challenging Hard/Def-P setting, while OUT-case p-values stay well above the decision threshold of ~3×10⁻⁵ (minimum OUT p-value: 2.04×10⁻³). WARD reaches 100% accuracy with at most 80 queries in the Hard setting, translating to API costs below $1 per audit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WARD decisively outperforms all three baselines in the Hard setting (with fact redundancy), which is the realistic RAG scenario: SIB, AAG, and FACTS all produce both false positives and false negatives, while WARD achieves 100% accuracy with zero errors across all models and prompts. In the Easy setting (no fact redundancy), the simple FACTS baseline actually matches or exceeds SIB and AAG, revealing that existing non-redundant datasets fail to stress-test RAG-DI methods. The Def-P system prompt defense (instructing the model not to regurgitate sources) significantly degrades SIB and AAG even in the Easy setting by reducing token overlap, but has no effect on WARD because watermark signals propagate through paraphrasing. WARD also outperforms SIB on monotonicity: while WARD's accuracy improves consistently with more queries (reaching 100% by ~80 queries), SIB exhibits high variance and often decreases in accuracy with more queries. The paper also shows WARD is robust to the MEMFREE decoding defense (preventing n-gram overlap with retrieved documents), still achieving 100% accuracy with only 60 queries, whereas this defense would be expected to further cripple the similarity-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes red-green LLM watermarks (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023; Kirchenbauer et al., 2024) — originally designed to track model-generated text — as a proactive data-provenance mechanism for auditing RAG corpora. The joint p-value aggregation across multiple responses follows Sander et al. (2024), who showed watermarks propagate through fine-tuning; here the signal instead propagates through the retrieval-and-generation pipeline. Conceptually, the paper departs from RAG membership inference (Li et al., 2024; Anderson et al., 2024), which makes document-level decisions, by formulating a dataset-level inference problem with statistical guarantees, and from training-data dataset inference (Maini et al., 2021; Dziedzic et al., 2022), which targets fine-tuning corpora rather than retrieval corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WARD provides data owners with a legally actionable, statistically rigorous tool to prove unauthorized inclusion of their content in RAG systems — a capability that currently does not exist and is critical as RAG becomes the dominant mechanism for integrating new knowledge into LLMs. The FARAD dataset and the Easy/Hard evaluation protocol fill a clear gap: existing RAG privacy benchmarks (EnronEmails, HealthcareMagic) are unsuitable because they may overlap with LLM training data and lack fact redundancy. Open questions include robustness against watermark-specific defenses (full paraphrasing of retrieved documents before insertion), generalization to non-text modalities, and whether the approach scales to very large RAG corpora where retrieval quality degrades significantly.
