## Problem

The DiffC algorithm for lossy compression with pretrained diffusion models was proposed in 2022 but never fully implemented because reverse-channel coding (RCC) posed a significant practical bottleneck. Theis et al. only evaluated hypothetical performance assuming a perfect RCC solution, leaving the algorithm as a theoretical construct. This paper provides the first complete, working implementation of DiffC and demonstrates that flagship pretrained diffusion models are competitive lossy image compressors at ultra-low bitrates.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (evaluated on Kodak at 512×768 and Div2K at 1024×1024) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image. Compression operates entirely in the latent space of the diffusion model's VAE, so reconstruction fidelity is bounded by the VAE's own PSNR (25.7 dB for SD1.5/2.1, 28.8 dB for SDXL, 32.4 dB for Flux). The method is naturally progressive: the same encoding protocol can target any desired bitrate by choosing where in the denoising schedule to stop, and no retraining is needed for new bitrates.

## Method

The core contribution is making DiffC's reverse-channel coding step practical. The algorithm communicates a noisy latent sample x_t by sequentially sending denoised samples from q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0) using the shared model distribution p_θ(x_{t-1}|x_t), requiring roughly D_KL(q||p) bits per step via the Poisson Functional Representation (PFR) algorithm. Two key workarounds handle PFR's practical limitations: when per-step KL divergence is too small (near t=T), denoising steps are skipped to accumulate sufficient information; when it is too large (near t=0), the high-dimensional Gaussian is split into statistically independent chunks of ~16 bits each. A custom CUDA kernel evaluates PFR candidate weights without materializing full sample arrays, achieving 64× speedup over PyTorch/TensorFlow. A greedy dynamic-programming algorithm (Algorithm 3) optimizes the timestep schedule to minimize total bits for a target noise level, and final reconstruction uses a 50-step DDIM probability-flow denoiser. For Flux-dev, a simple rescaling transformation converts Optimal Transport Flow paths into equivalent DDPM paths so the same protocol applies.

## Results

DiffC applied to Stable Diffusion 1.5 encodes Kodak images in under 10 seconds with a <30% bitrate overhead relative to the idealized algorithm. On the Kodak dataset, SD1.5/2.1 VAEs achieve a fidelity ceiling of 25.7 dB PSNR, SDXL's VAE reaches 28.8 dB, and Flux's VAE reaches 32.4 dB, making Flux competitive with HiFiC at 0.46 bpp. The method is zero-shot (no additional training) and naturally progressive, supporting encoding to any desired bitrate. Encoding times range from 0.6 s (SD1.5, low bitrate) to 321 s (Flux, high bitrate) on an A40 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffC achieves lower distortion (PSNR, LPIPS) than both PerCo and DiffEIC across the ultra-low bitrate regime on Kodak and Div2K, while simultaneously achieving higher perceptual quality (Q-Align, CLIP scores) than HiFiC and MS-ILLM. Against VVC-intra (VTM), DiffC is competitive at very low bitrates but falls behind at higher bitrates where traditional codecs retain an advantage. Text+Sketch (PIC) is shown visually in Figure 1; DiffC produces comparable or better reconstructions without requiring a separate sketch encoder. The paper notes that SD1.5 and SD2.1 perform comparably on Kodak, while SDXL and Flux perform comparably on Div2K, with Flux's primary advantage being its higher VAE fidelity (32.4 dB vs. 25.7 dB) enabling competitive compression at higher bitrates (up to 0.46 bpp vs. HiFiC). Prompt conditioning (BLIP captions or Hard Prompts) does not improve distortion metrics and is not worth the additional bits, though it modestly improves Q-Align scores for Flux and SDXL at low bitrates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first complete implementation of the DiffC algorithm proposed by Theis et al. (2022), which itself builds on the DDPM framework of Ho et al. (2020) and the Poisson Functional Representation (PFR) reverse-channel coding algorithm of Theis & Ahmed (2022). While Theis et al. demonstrated that DiffC could theoretically approach the model's negative log-likelihood as a compression rate, they identified reverse-channel coding as a blocking bottleneck and only evaluated hypothetical performance. This paper resolves that bottleneck with two practical workarounds (multi-step skipping when KL is too small, and dimension splitting when KL is too large) plus a custom CUDA kernel, then applies the fully implemented algorithm zero-shot to Stable Diffusion 1.5, 2.1, XL, and Flux-dev, positioning it alongside purpose-built generative compression methods like HiFiC, MS-ILLM, PerCo, and DiffEIC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that any pretrained diffusion model can now serve as a lossy image compressor with no additional training, offering a naturally progressive encoding scheme that works at any bitrate—a property that purpose-built methods (which must be retrained per bitrate) lack. However, the method remains bottlenecked by diffusion inference speed (Flux requires 5+ minutes per image) and is fidelity-limited by the underlying VAE for SD models, restricting its competitive range to ultra-low bitrates below ~25 dB PSNR. Key open questions include adapting DiffC to rectified flow models (the direction of SD3 and newer architectures), accelerating inference via activation caching, and combining DiffC with conditional diffusion approaches like PerCo or CDC for further gains.
