## Problem

Existing differentiable ILP methods require pre-trained supervised neural networks to map raw inputs to symbolic feature labels before rule induction, causing explicit label leakage where ground-truth class information contaminates the symbolic representation. This prevents learning interpretable rules directly from raw data such as time series or images, especially for objects that lack labels yet are important for data description.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw 1D time series sequences or flattened 2D images (e.g., 28×28 MNIST digits) as input, dividing them into fixed-length subsequences with unit stride. It produces interpretable logic rules of the form h_t ← pattern_i(X_j), region_k(X_j), … where pattern predicates index clusters of similar subsequences and region predicates encode temporal or spatial positions, alongside a binary class prediction. Rules are extracted by thresholding the product of softmax-activated weight matrices across all dense layers, and their quality is quantified via precision and recall on discretized interpretation vectors.

## Method

NeurRL integrates three jointly trained modules in a fully differentiable pipeline: (1) an autoencoder that encodes fixed-length subsequences into embeddings, (2) a differentiable k-means clustering module that groups similar subsequence embeddings into K pattern clusters using a Gumbel-softmax-style weight function, and (3) a deep rule-learning module consisting of k dense layers with softmax-activated weight matrices (each row sums to 1, mimicking the logic program matrix M_P) followed by a fuzzy disjunction layer. The key insight is that unsupervised clustering replaces the need for pre-trained supervised networks to generate symbolic labels, thereby eliminating explicit label leakage. Training minimizes a combined objective of autoencoder reconstruction MSE, clustering assignment loss, and rule-learning cross-entropy loss, with the rule-learning module providing gradient signals back to the clustering module to prevent cluster collapse. Rules are extracted post-hoc by computing the program tensor (element-wise product of all layer weight matrices) and thresholding entries to identify significant body atoms.

## Results

On 13 UCR time-series datasets, NeurRL(N) (the neural rule-learning module) achieves a mean classification accuracy of 0.891, outperforming SSSL (0.859), BoW (0.801), and Xu (0.718). The extracted symbolic rules (NeurRL(R)) achieve a mean accuracy of 0.842, slightly below SSSL but above BoW and Xu. NeurRL(N) attains the best accuracy on 7 of 13 datasets (e.g., 1.000 on Coffee, 0.964 on Face Four, 0.946 on WordsSyn), while NeurRL(R) achieves second-best on 5 datasets. On MNIST, NeurRL learns rules with precision and recall of 1.0 for distinguishing digit 1 from digit 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeurRL(N) outperforms all three baselines on mean accuracy across the 13 UCR datasets: 0.891 vs. SSSL (0.859), BoW (0.801), and Xu (0.718). The extracted rules NeurRL(R) (0.842) trail SSSL (0.859) by 1.7 points on average but still exceed BoW and Xu. SSSL wins on 4 datasets (ItalyPow.Dem., Lighting2, CBF, Trace) where shapelet-based features are particularly effective, while NeurRL(N) dominates on 7 datasets including Coffee (1.000 vs. 0.792), Face Four (0.964 vs. 0.851), and WordsSyn (0.946 vs. 0.875). BoW is competitive on ECG (0.955) and Gun Point (0.925) but is consistently outperformed by NeurRL(N) on the remaining datasets. Xu's graph-based semi-supervised approach is the weakest baseline, losing to all other methods on every dataset. The differentiable pipeline also reduces training time substantially compared to non-differentiable k-means (e.g., 42 s vs. 313 s on Coffee) without sacrificing rule accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable ILP line (Evans & Grefenstette 2018; Manhaeve et al. 2018; Gao et al. 2022a, 2024) from symbolic/fuzzy symbolic inputs to raw numeric sequences by replacing the pre-trained supervised encoder that prior methods (∂ILP, αILP) use to generate ground atoms with a self-supervised differentiable k-means clustering module. It also builds on SSSL (Wang et al. 2019) and Das et al. (1998) for sequence-based rule discovery, but unifies clustering and rule induction into a single end-to-end differentiable pipeline rather than treating them as separate stages. The key conceptual departure is eliminating 'explicit label leakage'—the reliance on supervised feature labels as intermediate supervision—by jointly training the autoencoder, clustering, and rule-learning modules under a single objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized bottleneck in neuro-symbolic ILP: the inability to learn interpretable rules directly from raw data without a supervised pre-training stage that leaks class information into the symbolic layer. The fully differentiable design also yields a practical efficiency gain (e.g., ~10× speedup over non-differentiable k-means on Lighting2) while maintaining or improving rule accuracy. Limitations include the focus on binary classification, the restriction to sequence/1-D inputs (images are flattened), and a small fixed number of clusters; the authors note future directions toward multi-class settings, variables over entire inputs, and incomplete data (e.g., healthcare). Whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or multi-modal raw inputs without the current flattening assumption remains open.
