## Problem

Sparsity and quantization are widely combined to compress large DNNs, yet prior work tacitly assumes they are orthogonal—i.e., their combined error equals the sum of their individual errors. No prior study had formally proven whether this assumption holds, established the optimal ordering of the two operations, or quantified the compounded error introduced by their interaction at both the tensor and dot-product levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253428/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained DNN weights and activation tensors from Transformer LLMs (OPT-125M/6.7B, LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-3-8B) and vision models (ViT-B/16, ResNet-50). Sparsity is applied to weight tensors only (50% unstructured or 2:4 N:M structured), while both weights and activations are quantized using max-scaled block-wise formats (INT8, HBFP8/6, MXFP8/6, HBFP4). The output is a compressed model evaluated via perplexity on WikiText2 or cross-entropy loss / top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k, alongside a computed orthogonality threshold that serves as a performance bound for the combined compression.

## Method

The paper provides the first mathematical proof that magnitude-based sparsity and max-scaled block-wise quantization are non-orthogonal: Theorem 3.5 shows S→Q introduces no additional error (the scale factor is preserved because sparsity never prunes the block maximum), while Theorems 3.6–3.7 prove Q→S can introduce additional error bounded by 2·step·(M−N)/M·n, where step is the quantization bin size. At the dot-product level, Theorem 3.10 decomposes the additional error into a correction term ε_t (carrying tensor-level composition error into the inner product) and an interaction term ε_i (cross-product of quantization and sparsity error vectors). Empirically, the authors fine-tune pre-trained models with sparsity-aware training (recomputing masks each iteration) followed by one-shot quantization for S→Q, or jointly quantize-and-sparsify at each iteration for Q→S, and validate non-orthogonality by comparing actual perplexity/CE loss against the orthogonality threshold (baseline + individual errors). Layer-wise ablation on OPT-125M confirms that Q→S errors accumulate more rapidly across depth, and experiments across model sizes (125M–8B) and formats show up to 13% additional perplexity degradation even in the optimal S→Q order.

## Results

The paper proves mathematically and validates empirically that sparsity and quantization are non-orthogonal: combining them introduces compounded errors beyond the sum of individual errors. Applying sparsity before quantization (S→Q) consistently outperforms the reverse order (Q→S); for example, OPT-125M with HBFP6 + 2:4 sparsity achieves 34.58 PPL (S→Q) versus 55.64 PPL (Q→S), and LLaMA-2-7B achieves 10.68 vs. 18.64 PPL. Even in the optimal S→Q order, the combined compression can cause up to 13% additional perplexity error beyond the orthogonality threshold (sum of individual sparsity and quantization errors), with the gap widening for lower-precision formats (HBFP6) and structured sparsity (2:4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares magnitude-based sparsity (with fine-tuning) against two one-shot pruning baselines: Wanda (Sun et al., 2023) and SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023). Magnitude-based sparsity with fine-tuning consistently achieves the lowest perplexity across all models and formats. For 50% unstructured sparsity on OPT-125M (FP32), magnitude-based S→Q yields 29.94 PPL versus 38.97 for Wanda and 33.24 for SparseGPT; on LLaMA-2-7B the gap is smaller (6.31 vs. 6.46 Wanda vs. 6.51 SparseGPT). The advantage is dramatic for 2:4 structured sparsity: on OPT-125M, magnitude-based S→Q achieves 31.89 PPL while Wanda reaches 79.91 and SparseGPT 45.14. A key secondary finding is that Wanda and SparseGPT are largely insensitive to the S→Q vs. Q→S order (their pruning criteria incorporate activation magnitudes that compensate for quantization-induced reordering), whereas magnitude-based sparsity is highly order-sensitive, with Q→S causing 10–25 PPL degradation on OPT-125M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior work on combined sparsity and quantization that either assumed orthogonality (common in CNN-focused studies where quantization errors are small) or empirically tested both orders without formal justification. While several studies (Wu et al., 2023; Zandonati et al., 2023; Park et al., 2019) experimented with both S→Q and Q→S orderings, none provided a mathematical characterization of the interaction. The paper carves new ground by introducing the first formal definitions of tensor-level and dot-product-level orthogonality for compression transformations, proving non-orthogonality, and deriving error upper bounds that depend on quantization step size and sparsity parameters. It extends the empirical scope to modern LLMs (OPT, LLaMA) and vision models (ViT, ResNet) using contemporary block-wise formats (HBFP, MXFP) rather than the per-tensor INT8 or CNN-centric settings of earlier work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that sparsity and quantization are provably non-orthogonal challenges a widely-held tacit assumption in the model compression community, with direct practical implications: even in the optimal S→Q order, combined compression can introduce up to 13% additional perplexity error, meaning practitioners cannot simply add the individual error budgets. The orthogonality threshold metric offers a practical tool for narrowing the search space over sparse-quantized configurations without exhaustive evaluation. Key limitations include the exclusion of heterogeneous (layer-wise varying) sparsity and quantization schemes, which are increasingly used in practice, and the lack of a characterization of when orthogonality *is* preserved. Open questions include extending the analysis to activation sparsity, mixed-precision schemes, and developing error-mitigation strategies that decouple the two compression effects.
