## Problem

LLMs struggle to generate coherent, sufficiently long creative narratives (1,000–2,000+ tokens) without relying on intricate single-prompt instructions or prompt chaining, often producing stories that are too short, repetitive, or lack consistent characters and plot structure. No existing multi-agent framework specifically decomposes the long-form writing task into specialized collaborative subtasks, and standardized evaluation criteria for assessing creative writing quality across multiple dimensions remain lacking.

## Input / Output

The system takes a detailed writing prompt (averaging 113 tokens in TELL ME A STORY, specifying characters, setting, plot constraints, tone, and target length of ~2,500 words) and produces a long-form narrative structured according to Freytag's dramatic arc. The final output is the concatenation of five writing-agent segments (exposition through resolution), yielding stories of ~3,000 tokens—roughly double the length of single-call baselines. Intermediate planning outputs (conflict, character, setting, plot) are stored in a shared scratchpad but excluded from the final story text.

## Method

AGENTS' ROOM decomposes story writing into nine specialized agents: four planning agents (CONFLICT, CHARACTER, SETTING, PLOT) that write exclusively to a shared scratchpad, and five writing agents (EXPOSITION, RISING ACTION, CLIMAX, FALLING ACTION, RESOLUTION) that iteratively produce segments of the final output. A deterministic orchestrator invokes agents in a fixed narrative-theory-driven order, and each agent is a Gemini 1.5 Flash LLM with a task-specific prompt template that formats the scratchpad into its subtask input. Agents are trained via distilled backtranslation: a Gemini Ultra teacher LLM reverse-engineers planning outputs and story segmentations from gold-standard stories, generating synthetic training data for LoRA fine-tuning (rank 4, 250 steps). The key architectural insight is that specialization and sequential collaboration through a shared memory structure outperform monolithic generation, as each agent handles a narrow subtask with high precision rather than a single model attempting the full writing task in one pass.

## Results

On the TELL ME A STORY test set, AGENTS' ROOM systems with writing agents produce stories roughly twice as long as E2E baselines (~3,000 words vs. ~1,200 words) and show the lowest prompt overlap (0.0019–0.0022 vs. 0.0034–0.0036 for E2E), indicating more creative elaboration. In human pairwise evaluation, AR_FT plan+write and AR_FT write are the top-ranked machine systems across all four dimensions (plot, creativity, development, language use), though human-written stories remain preferred overall. The LLM-based evaluator (Gemini 1.5 Pro) correlates significantly with human ratings (Spearman's ρ=0.62 at system level, ρ=0.41 at item level; p<0.01), with highest agreement on development (ρ=0.83) and creativity (ρ=0.85).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AGENTS' ROOM against six E2E baselines: zero-shot prompting (E2E_ZS), zero-shot with explicit planning instructions (E2E_ZS plan), zero-shot with self-reflection (E2E_ZS reflect), zero-shot with automatic decomposition (E2E_ZS decompose), fine-tuned E2E (E2E_FT), and two-stage plan-then-write (2STAGE decompose). In human pairwise evaluation, all AGENTS' ROOM variants that include writing agents (AR_ZS write, AR_ZS plan+write, AR_FT write, AR_FT plan+write) are preferred over every E2E baseline across all four quality dimensions, with AR_FT plan+write and AR_FT write ranking highest among machine systems. The AR plan-only variants (using a single [FINALIZER] writing agent) underperform, likely because the simplistic finalizer cannot leverage the rich planning content in the scratchpad. Fine-tuned agents consistently outperform their zero-shot counterparts, validating the synthetic data generation via distilled backtranslation. Despite these gains, human-written stories remain preferred over all machine-generated outputs, with the gap smallest in the language use dimension. The LLM evaluator (Gemini 1.5 Pro) reproduces the same ranking pattern and shows 90.2% self-consistency across reversed-order evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior story generation work (Yao et al. 2019; Fan et al. 2019; Yang et al. 2022, 2023) that decomposes generation into planning and writing stages within a single model, by instead instantiating each subtask as a separate specialized agent communicating through a shared scratchpad. It draws on the multi-agent LLM systems literature (Talebirad & Nadiri 2023; Hong et al. 2024; Park et al. 2023) but is, to the authors' knowledge, the first to apply such a framework to long-form narrative writing. The agent specializations are grounded in narrative theory (Freytag's five-act structure; Card 1999; Noble 1999), and the synthetic training data for agents is generated via distilled backtranslation from gold stories, extending the synthetic data generation paradigm of Schick et al. (2022) and Josifoski et al. (2023) to a writing-specific setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TELL ME A STORY dataset and the multi-dimensional evaluation framework (plot, creativity, development, language use) address a real gap: existing benchmarks are either too short, not genuinely narrative, or lack standardized evaluation criteria for long-form fiction. The LLM-based evaluator that correlates significantly with human judgments (Spearman ρ=0.62 at system level) offers a scalable path toward automated story quality assessment. Key limitations include reliance on a single backbone model (Gemini 1.5 Flash), a very small training set (123 examples), a deterministic orchestrator, and only one narrative structure (Freytag's). Open questions include whether learned orchestrators with reward models can adapt to diverse narrative structures, whether the framework generalizes beyond fiction (essays, screenplays), and how human-in-the-loop editing at the planning stage would affect final quality.
