## Problem

While prior work (Geshkovski et al., 2023) proved that tokens in a mean-field transformer model generically collapse to a single cluster as depth goes to infinity, numerical simulations reveal intermediate meta-stable phases with multiple clusters that existing long-time theory fails to characterize. This paper addresses the gap by rigorously analyzing the intermediate-time dynamics between uniform initialization and single-cluster collapse, identifying the structure and duration of these meta-stable phases as a function of model hyperparameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250278/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes N tokens sampled i.i.d. uniformly on S^{d-1} (with d=2 as the primary case, extended to d≥3), along with the inverse temperature β and embedding dimension d as hyperparameters. The output is a complete characterization of the meta-stable solution: the number of clusters k_max (determined by the index maximizing the rescaled Gegenbauer coefficient γ_k = k(k+d-2)Ŵ_k), the O(ln N) duration of the meta-stable phase, and the explicit periodic structure (k_max-periodic in d=2, spherical k_max-1-design in d≥3) that the token distribution acquires before eventual collapse. The results are stated in Wasserstein-1 distance and hold in probability as N→∞.

## Method

The paper decomposes the meta-stable phase into three sub-phases and analyzes each with tailored techniques. In the linear phase, a duality argument refines Dobrushin's exponential bound near the uniform equilibrium, showing the perturbation grows along the dominant Fourier mode cos(k_max θ) at rate γ_max until reaching size O(N^{-1/4}). In the quasi-linear phase, Grenier's iterative scheme constructs a higher-order nonlinear approximation to the mean-field PDE, relaxing the growth-rate condition needed to track the dominant mode until it exits a fixed neighborhood of the uniform measure. In the clustering phase, standard Dobrushin estimates apply since the remaining time to collapse is O(1) independent of N. The key identification is that k_max—the number of meta-stable clusters—is the index maximizing γ_k, which for the transformer kernel W(q)=β^{-1}e^{βq} corresponds to the peak of k(k+d-2)I_k(β) (modified Bessel functions), yielding k_max≈√β as β→∞.

## Results

The paper proves that the meta-stable phase of a mean-field transformer lasts O(ln N) time, during which the token distribution develops a k_max-periodic structure determined by the inverse temperature β. Specifically, k_max is the index maximizing γ_k = k(k+d−2)Ŵ_k (a rescaling of Gegenbauer coefficients); for d=2, β=5 yields k_max=3 and β=7 yields k_max=4. Numerical simulations with 10⁴ particles on S¹ confirm these predicted cluster counts, and the exit time from the quasi-linear regime scales logarithmically with N (verified for N from 1000 to 16000). The perturbation at the end of the linear phase has typical size N^{−1/4} in H^{−1} norm, growing from the O(N^{−1/2}) initialization fluctuation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods in the standard empirical sense; it is a theoretical contribution extending the framework of Geshkovski et al. (2023). The key advance over prior work is that Geshkovski et al. characterized only the t→∞ limit (generic convergence to a single cluster), while this paper rigorously identifies the intermediate meta-stable phase with k_max clusters persisting for O(ln N) time. The theoretical predictions (cluster count as a function of β, logarithmic time scaling, N^{−1/4} perturbation size) are validated against numerical simulations of the particle ODEs and the mean-field PDE, with no discrepancies observed. The paper also demonstrates via a counterexample (Appendix B) that the exponential time-dependence in Dobrushin's bound is sharp and cannot be improved, justifying the need for the refined meta-stability analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-field transformer model of Geshkovski et al. (2023) by providing the first rigorous characterization of the intermediate meta-stable clustering phase, which the original authors identified as an open problem. The analytical toolkit draws on propagation of chaos (Sznitman, 1991; Dobrushin, 1979) extended to O(log N) time intervals, Grenier's iterative scheme from fluid-dynamics instability analysis (Grenier, 2000) for the quasi-linear phase, and spectral decomposition via Gegenbauer polynomials/spherical harmonics to identify the dominant unstable mode. Unlike prior mean-field neural network work (Mei et al., 2018; Chizat & Bach, 2018) that models training dynamics, this paper analyzes inference-time evolution through depth, and unlike the t→∞ convergence results of Markdahl et al. (2017) and Criscitiello et al. (2024), it captures the structured intermediate states that precede single-cluster collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the number of clusters in the meta-stable phase is explicitly determined by the inverse temperature β (via the maximizing index of rescaled Gegenbauer coefficients, with k_max ≈ √β for large β), providing a quantitative link between hyperparameters and representation richness in transformers. The O(log N) duration of the meta-stable phase has direct practical implications: for the 128k–2M token contexts of modern LLMs, these intermediate structured phases occupy a non-negligible depth range. Key limitations include the assumption Q=K=V=Id and absence of MLP layers, and the central Assumption 3 (convergence to k_max delta masses) remains unproven. Open questions include extending the analysis to nontrivial Q,K,V matrices, incorporating MLP effects, and studying the impact of stochastic initialization noise on symmetry breaking.
