## Problem

Consistency Trajectory Models (CTMs) enable one-step traversal along probability flow ODEs, but are restricted to translating from Gaussian noise to data, limiting their use for image manipulation tasks like translation, restoration, and editing. Meanwhile, diffusion-based methods for these tasks require tens to thousands of neural function evaluations (NFEs), making real-time application impractical. This paper asks whether the CTM framework can be generalized to enable one-step translation between arbitrary distributions.

## Input / Output

The GCTM network takes a perturbed sample x_t along with two time parameters (t, s) in the unit interval (0,1) and outputs either a translated sample at time s (via G_θ) or a clean/score estimate at time t (via g_θ with s=t). For image-to-image translation, the input is a 64×64 or 256×256 RGB image (e.g., edge map, night photo) and the output is the corresponding translated image in a single forward pass. For restoration, the input is a corrupted image (bicubic-downsampled, Gaussian-blurred, or center-masked) and the output is the restored image. A small Gaussian perturbation (σ=0.05) is added to the input x_1 to enable one-to-many generation, and the perturbation direction doubles as a controllable latent vector for editing.

## Method

GCTMs generalize CTMs by replacing the diffusion PFODE with a Flow Matching ODE between two arbitrary distributions q(x_0) and q(x_1), parametrized on the unit interval t∈(0,1). The key theoretical result (Proposition 1) shows the FM ODE admits the same G/g decomposition as CTMs, with the posterior q(x_0|x_t) replacing p(x_0|x_τ), and Proposition 2 proves CTM is a special case when one distribution is Gaussian. Training minimizes a combined objective: a trajectory-consistency loss L_GCTM that enforces self-consistency along ODE segments (using a stop-gradient target from an EMA network), and a flow-matching regression loss L_FM that anchors the score/velocity estimate. The design space includes three coupling strategies—independent, minibatch entropic optimal transport (via Sinkhorn), and supervised (x_1 = Hx_0 for a corruption operator H)—which determine whether the model is trained unsupervised or on paired data. Time discretization uses the EDM schedule converted to FM coordinates, with σ_max controlling emphasis near t=1, and a second-order Heun solver integrates ODE segments during training.

## Results

On image-to-image translation (64×64), GCTM with NFE=1 achieves FID 40.3 / IS 3.54 / LPIPS 0.097 on Edges→Shoes, FID 148.8 / IS 2.00 / LPIPS 0.317 on Night→Day, and FID 111.3 / IS 2.99 / LPIPS 0.230 on Facades, outperforming all baselines at comparable inference speed. For zero-shot image restoration on FFHQ (64×64) with NFE=32, GCTM attains PSNR 31.61 (SR2), 28.19 (Deblur), and 24.47 (Inpaint), surpassing both DPS and CM. On CIFAR-10 unconditional generation at NFE=1, GCTM with OT coupling achieves FID 5.32, matching teacher-trained CTM (5.28) and outperforming teacher-free CTM (9.00) and CM (8.70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCTM decisively outperforms Palette (a conditional diffusion model) across all three I2I tasks, with FID improvements of 84–239 points, as Palette degrades severely at low NFEs. Against I²SB (Schrödinger bridge), GCTM wins on FID and LPIPS for Edges→Shoes (40.3 vs 53.9) and Facades (111.3 vs 135.2), though I²SB edges out slightly on Night→Day FID (145.8 vs 148.8); GCTM also uses 5× fewer NFEs (1 vs 5). Pix2Pix, despite being a single-step GAN, is outperformed by GCTM on all metrics for all three tasks. For zero-shot restoration, GCTM surpasses DPS by 0.42 dB PSNR on SR2 and 0.31 dB on Deblur, and matches DPS on Inpainting; it also outperforms CM-based restoration, which suffers from error accumulation at high NFEs. On unconditional CIFAR-10 generation, GCTM (FID 5.32) trails iCM (2.51) but matches teacher-trained CTM (5.28) without requiring a pre-trained teacher model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Consistency Trajectory Models (CTMs), which learn to traverse PFODE trajectories between Gaussian noise and data, by re-expressing the underlying ODE through the Flow Matching framework to enable translation between two arbitrary distributions. The key theoretical move is proving that the FM ODE admits the same G/g parametrization as the diffusion PFODE (Proposition 1), and that CTMs are a strict special case of GCTMs when one endpoint is Gaussian (Proposition 2). The design space draws on EDM's time discretization, minibatch entropic optimal transport couplings from Pooladian et al., and supervised coupling via random corruption operators, unifying previously separate formulations for unconditional generation, I2I translation, and inverse problems under one training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GCTMs provide a single, unified one-step framework for image-to-image translation, restoration, editing, and latent manipulation, eliminating the need for task-specific architectures or multi-step SDE solvers. The flexible coupling mechanism (independent, OT, supervised) means the same model class handles both zero-shot and supervised settings, which is practically valuable for deployment. However, GCTMs do not yet match the best unconditional generation quality (FID 5.32 vs. iCM's 2.51 at NFE=1), and the authors acknowledge that further hyperparameter tuning or GAN losses may be needed. Open questions include whether the framework scales to text-conditioned generation, video, or 3D, and whether the one-step constraint fundamentally limits expressiveness for highly ambiguous inverse problems.
