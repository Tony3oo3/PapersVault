## Problem

DPO training yields an implicit reward model as a byproduct, but this signal has not been exploited to further align the model beyond the initial DPO round. The paper asks whether this implicit reward can bootstrap iterative self-alignment without any external feedback (no new human annotations, no external reward model, no LLM-as-a-judge), and how to address the practical failures of naive bootstrapping: length exploitation and catastrophic forgetting from overreliance on an imperfect proxy for human preferences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252012/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only what was already consumed during the initial DPO training: a DPO-tuned policy model, its SFT reference model, a prompt set (extracted from the offline preference dataset), and the offline preference dataset itself (~10k pairs from UltraFeedback). At each iteration, K=16 on-policy responses are sampled per prompt, scored by the implicit reward, and the best/worst pair is retained. The output is an updated policy model after two DPO rounds, evaluated on AlpacaEval 2 and Arena-Hard, achieving >8% LC win rate improvement over the base DPO-tuned model.

## Method

DICE iteratively constructs preference datasets using the DPO implicit reward r(x,y) = β·log(π_θ(y|x)/π_ref(y|x)) to rank K on-policy sampled responses, then trains a new DPO round on the resulting pairs. Two refinements make this work: (1) length-regularized reward shaping subtracts α|y| from the implicit reward, with α* found by minimizing the average absolute length difference between winning and losing responses (solved via Bayesian optimization), producing a length-unbiased dataset without expensive hyperparameter tuning in the loss; (2) experience replay mixes the generated dataset with the original offline preference data at ratio γ (tuned via cross-validation), balancing on-policy freshness against the risk of catastrophic forgetting from the imperfect implicit reward. The reference model for each DPO round is the previous round's policy, and the entire pipeline requires no external reward model, LLM judge, or new human annotations.

## Results

DICE achieves a length-controlled (LC) win rate improvement of 8.02% on Zephyr-7B-beta (12.69% → 20.71%) and 9.35% on Llama-3-8B-DPO (18.20% → 27.55%) on AlpacaEval 2 after two bootstrapping iterations, without any external feedback. On Arena-Hard, DICE reaches 18.45% LC (Zephyr) and 39.13% LC (Llama3). Notably, DICE-Llama3-8B (27.55% LC) surpasses Gemini Pro (24.38% LC) and the official Llama3-8B Instruct (22.92% LC) using only 8B parameters and no additional human annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DICE outperforms all baselines on both base models. Against Offline DPO, DICE wins decisively: repeated offline DPO actually degrades performance (Zephyr LC drops from 12.69% to 4.96% by Iter 2), while DICE improves monotonically. Against LLM-as-a-Judge (the strongest baseline, inspired by Self-Rewarding LMs), DICE achieves 20.71% vs 14.14% LC on Zephyr and 27.55% vs 21.80% LC on Llama3 after two iterations, with the gap widening at Iter 2. In the compatibility study (Table 3), DICE-generated data also improves IPO (14.83→18.51 LC), KTO (13.92→14.88 LC), and Hinge (13.51→15.92 LC) over their offline counterparts, though DPO benefits the most. Compared to Park et al.'s length-regularized DPO (Table 7), DICE's LR reward shaping achieves higher LC (18.88 vs 16.30) while maintaining comparable WR (19.31 vs 19.16) and shorter average response length (2109 vs 2629 tokens). Finally, DPO with implicit rewards (19.03 LC) substantially outperforms PPO with the same implicit reward model (13.32 LC) in the Zephyr setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative DPO framework (Tran et al., 2023) by identifying DPO's implicit reward model (Rafailov et al., 2024b) as a self-contained preference signal for bootstrapping, eliminating the need for external reward models or LLM-as-a-Judge prompting (Yuan et al., 2024). It departs from prior self-alignment work by operating on a DPO-tuned model rather than an SFT model, and incorporates experience replay from continual learning (Rolnick et al., 2019) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting during iterative fine-tuning. The length-regularized reward shaping is distinct from Park et al. (2024), which penalizes length in the loss function, by instead debiasing the dataset construction stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DPO's implicit reward outperforms a dedicated scalar reward model trained on the same data (0.698 vs 0.624 alignment rate) suggests that the reward structure embedded in a DPO-tuned policy is a more faithful proxy for human preferences on its own outputs than a separately trained RM. This provides a practical, resource-efficient path to further improve DPO-tuned models without external annotations or compute-intensive reward model training. However, the method still suffers from the known limitation of performance degradation beyond ~3 iterations, and the reliance on a well-trained initial DPO model means the approach cannot bootstrap from scratch. Open questions include whether different DPO variants (KTO, IPO) yield equally useful implicit rewards, and whether novel strategies can enable sustained improvement over many iterations.
