## Problem

Existing text-to-3D methods generate objects independently, so a collection of generated objects lacks structural alignment across corresponding semantic parts. Sequential editing approaches (generate one object, then edit it into the next) are constrained by the initial object's structure and degrade in visual quality. No prior method jointly optimizes a set of 3D objects so that they simultaneously share structure and each faithfully matches its own text prompt.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251696/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the method takes N text prompts and produces a single NeRF (or DMTet) network parameterized by a latent code u on an (N-1)-dimensional probability simplex; individual objects are recovered by evaluating the network at the simplex vertices, while transitions live along the edges. For structure-preserving transformation, the input is a source 3D mesh (or SMPL model) plus a target text prompt, and the network is initialized photometrically from the source before SDS training. For hybridization, the user places anchor points in 3D space and assigns them to specific objects; the latent code is then spatially interpolated between anchors to blend parts seamlessly.

## Method

A single NeRF-like network (1-layer MLP on a hash grid, following MVDream or RichDreamer) is extended with a latent code u ∈ R^N constrained to the probability simplex, concatenated with the hash-grid embedding at every query point. Training uses SDS with the diffusion model conditioned on a weighted sum of text embeddings y(u) = Σ u_i y_i: at simplex vertices the loss pulls renders toward the individual prompts, while at edges it enforces plausibility of intermediate objects via the blended embedding. Smoothness of transitions is enforced implicitly by capping the MLP to one hidden layer (limiting the Lipschitz constant) and adding a normal-map smoothness loss. The ablation shows both plausibility regularization (blended-prompt SDS at edges) and smoothness regularization (shallow network + normal loss) are individually necessary for structural alignment; removing either degrades DIFT distance substantially. The same framework adapts to structure-preserving editing by initializing the network from a source mesh and fixing the photometric loss at one simplex endpoint.

## Results

For the generation of multiple aligned 3D objects, A3D achieves a CLIP similarity of 27.7, outperforming MVEdit (27.1), LucidDreamer (26.4), and GaussianEditor (22.8). On GPTEval3D, A3D is preferred in 89–99% of pairwise comparisons against MVEdit and GaussianEditor across all quality and alignment criteria, and in 56–80% against LucidDreamer. The DIFT structural alignment distance is 6.1% of object size for A3D, slightly higher than MVEdit (5.5%) and GaussianEditor (2.4%) but substantially lower than LucidDreamer (11.3%). For structure-preserving transformation, A3D performs on par with specialized editing methods, consistently outperforming MVEdit on asset quality and prompt alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A3D outperforms all three baselines on CLIP text-prompt alignment (27.7 vs. 27.1, 26.4, 22.8) and on every GPTEval3D quality criterion, with the strongest margins against GaussianEditor (99% preferred on text-asset alignment, 97% on overall quality) and MVEdit (94% on text-asset alignment, 92% on geometry details). Against LucidDreamer, A3D wins on most criteria but with smaller margins (56–80%), and LucidDreamer diverged on half of the structure-preserving transformation scenes. On DIFT structural alignment, A3D (6.1%) is slightly worse than MVEdit (5.5%) and GaussianEditor (2.4%), but the paper attributes this to those methods often failing to actually modify the initial object's geometry, yielding trivially low DIFT at the cost of poor text-prompt correspondence. For structure-preserving transformation, A3D matches or exceeds specialized editing methods on GPTEval3D and CLIP, while producing more substantial geometric changes (e.g., adding a throne, shrinking a cat to a skeleton) that MVEdit's surface-level deformations cannot achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) paradigm from DreamFusion and its multi-view variant MVDream by introducing a shared latent space (probability simplex) over a set of text prompts, so that a single NeRF represents multiple objects and the transitions between them simultaneously. The key conceptual departure from prior text-to-3D work, which optimizes each object independently, is the regularization of transition trajectories between objects—drawing directly from 2D GAN literature on perceptual path length (StyleGAN) and adversarial interpolative autoencoding (Berthelot et al.). Unlike sequential editing approaches (MVEdit, LucidDreamer, GaussianEditor) that transform one object into another and inherit quality degradation, A3D jointly optimizes all objects and their transitions, trading some per-object fidelity for structural coherence across the collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method explicitly designed to produce collections of structurally aligned 3D objects, filling a gap between independent generation (no alignment) and sequential editing (quality loss). The practical implications for 3D asset pipelines—enabling hybridization, structure-preserving editing, and animation transfer across aligned objects—are concrete and well-demonstrated. Key limitations include restriction to static objects (no pose-changing), dependence on the mode-seeking nature of SDS which limits diversity, and the need for manual anchor placement in the hybridization step. The authors identify extension to 4D/pose-varying generation as the natural next step, and the generalizability across backbones (MVDream/NeRF and RichDreamer/DMTet) suggests the transition-regularization principle could transfer to other 3D generation frameworks.
