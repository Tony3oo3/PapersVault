## Problem

Instruction finetuning (IFT) is a standard step in the LLM pipeline expected to improve a model's ability to follow user-provided context, yet the paper identifies a previously unobserved failure mode: under knowledge conflicts, context reliance initially increases during IFT but then paradoxically decreases, a phenomenon the authors term context-parametric inversion. The underlying optimization dynamics driving this reversal were unexplored, and no existing work had systematically characterized how IFT dataset composition affects context versus parametric reliance over the training trajectory.

## Input / Output

The models (Llama2-7B, Pythia-6.9B, Mistral-7B) receive a user instruction paired with an input context that may contain counterfactual information contradicting the model's pretrained parametric knowledge, and produce a free-form text response. Context reliance is quantified via two complementary metrics—counterfactual accuracy (does the output contain the context-based answer?) and parametric accuracy (does it contain the pretrained factual answer?)—evaluated on three purpose-built knowledge conflict datasets (CF_Bio, CF_World_Facts, CF_Quotes) alongside standard benchmarks (GSM8k, MMLU, SQuAD, ARC-Challenge) tracked every 50 finetuning steps.

## Method

The paper combines controlled empirical studies with a theoretical analysis in a one-layer transformer to explain context-parametric inversion. Empirically, the authors show the drop is not due to train-test fact overlap, insufficient context-bearing data, or classic overfitting, but is specifically caused by non-context-critical datapoints (where the context is redundant with parametric knowledge or inferable from teacher-forced target tokens) that dominate gradients after context-critical points' loss decreases. Theoretically, they prove that in a one-layer transformer, context-critical points initially drive attention toward the context direction θ_C due to their higher loss, but as training progresses, non-context-critical points (C+S) and pure fact-recall points (S) shift the gradient signal back toward the subject direction θ_S, reducing context reliance at test time (Theorem 1). The authors further show that optimizing value matrices W_V during IFT can convert context-critical points into non-context-critical ones by memorizing subject-answer associations (Proposition 3), and propose partial mitigations via target-perplexity-based data filtering, counterfactual augmentation, and QK-only finetuning, each with documented tradeoffs.

## Results

On Llama2-7B finetuned on TULU, context reliance (counterfactual accuracy) on CF_World_Facts rises from ~40% to nearly 90% in early IFT steps, then gradually declines as training progresses, while average performance on standard benchmarks (GSM8k, MMLU, ARC-Challenge, SQuAD) continues to improve throughout. On CF_Quotes, counterfactual accuracy peaks at ~70% (from 40% zero-shot) before dropping. The phenomenon is consistent across Llama2-7B, Pythia-6.9B, and Mistral-7B, and across TULU, Alpaca, and UltraChat datasets. Peak counterfactual performance occurs well before one finetuning epoch, ruling out classic overfitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an analysis paper without external method baselines; the key comparisons are internal ablations. Filtering to context-critical datapoints (removing the 25% with lowest target perplexity without context) eliminates the drop in context reliance, confirming that non-context-critical examples drive the inversion. Counterfactual data augmentation (10% NQ-Corpus-Swap on Alpaca) improves CF_Bio counterfactual accuracy but yields minimal gains on TULU (1% augmentation) and no improvement on CF_Quotes; it also degrades SQuAD accuracy from 76% to 62%, revealing limited generalization. QK-only finetuning (freezing value matrices) enhances CF_World_Facts counterfactual performance but provides no gains on CF_Bio or CF_Quotes, and sacrifices standard benchmark performance. Explicitly prompting the model to 'answer based on the input context only' does not prevent the drop, indicating the issue is in the model's learned attention dynamics rather than instruction following.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge conflicts literature (Longpre et al. 2022; Shi et al. 2023; Yuan et al. 2024), which has primarily focused on inference-time decoding strategies or counterfactual data augmentation to improve context reliance. It departs from this line by identifying a failure mode of instruction finetuning itself: the standard IFT process actively degrades context reliance after an initial improvement, a phenomenon no prior work had documented. The theoretical analysis builds on the memorization framework of Ghosal et al. (2024) for one-layer transformers, applying it to a novel gradient-dynamics explanation of why non-context-critical datapoints in IFT datasets pull attention back toward parametric knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This finding has broad practical implications for any IFT pipeline and downstream applications like RAG, where models must reliably follow user-provided context over parametric memory. The paper shows that standard mitigation strategies (counterfactual augmentation, QK-only finetuning) provide only partial and task-specific gains, leaving the fundamental tension between context-critical and non-context-critical data composition in IFT datasets unresolved. Open questions include whether the phenomenon extends to other context-intensive tasks (multi-hop QA, long-context reasoning), how to optimally balance data composition during IFT, and whether deeper theoretical understanding of multi-layer attention dynamics can yield more effective interventions.
