## Problem

While GPT-4o demonstrated real-time speech interaction with LLMs, the open-source community lacks a practical recipe for building such models. Cascaded ASR+LLM+TTS pipelines suffer from high sequential latency, and existing end-to-end speech-language models like SpeechGPT require massive training data and compute resources, making them impractical to build on top of the latest LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252078/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a raw speech instruction (audio waveform) as input and simultaneously produces both a text response and a streaming speech response (waveform). The speech output is generated in real-time chunks: as the LLM autoregressively emits text tokens, the speech decoder produces discrete HuBERT-based units (from a 1000-cluster K-means codebook) that are fed to a unit-based HiFi-GAN vocoder with a duration predictor every Ω units, enabling the user to hear the response before the full text is generated. The minimum achievable latency is 236 ms (Ω=10).

## Method

LLaMA-Omni couples a frozen Whisper-large-v3 speech encoder, a trainable speech adaptor (5× downsampling + 2-layer MLP), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and a 425M-parameter non-autoregressive streaming speech decoder (2 Transformer layers) trained with CTC loss. The key architectural insight is that the speech decoder consumes the LLM's output hidden states (upsampled by λ=25) and predicts discrete speech units in parallel with the LLM's autoregressive text generation, so both modalities stream out simultaneously without sequential dependency. Training is two-stage: first the adaptor and LLM are fine-tuned for speech-to-text generation (3 epochs, 4×L40 GPUs), then only the speech decoder is trained with CTC while all other components are frozen. The authors also construct InstructS2S-200K, a 200K-sample dataset where text instructions are rewritten for natural speech style (filler words, spoken numbers, brevity) and responses are regenerated to be concise and TTS-compatible, then synthesized with CosyVoice and VITS.

## Results

On the InstructS2S-Eval benchmark (offline), LLaMA-Omni achieves the highest ChatGPT Score of 3.99 on speech-to-text instruction-following (S2TIF) and 3.47 on speech-to-speech instruction-following (S2SIF), along with the best UTMOS of 3.93. Its ASR-WER is 10.82, higher than the cascaded baselines but far below SpeechGPT's 45.00. In the streaming scenario, LLaMA-Omni achieves a minimum end-to-end latency of 236 ms (Ω=10), lower than GPT-4o's reported 320 ms average audio latency, while maintaining near-offline quality at 563 ms (Ω=100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SpeechGPT, LLaMA-Omni dominates on every metric: ChatGPT Score improves from 2.98→3.99 (S2TIF) and 2.19→3.47 (S2SIF), ASR-WER drops from 45.00 to 10.82, and streaming latency falls from >4500 ms to 236 ms. Against the two cascaded baselines (SALMONN+Orca and Qwen2-Audio+Orca), LLaMA-Omni wins on S2TIF ChatGPT Score (3.99 vs. 3.44/3.47) and UTMOS (3.93 vs. 3.83/3.61), but trails on ASR-WER (10.82 vs. 3.78/6.77), which the authors attribute to the speech decoder being trained on only ~1K hours of data versus the industrial TTS models used in the cascaded systems. In the streaming setting, LLaMA-Omni maintains a stable speech rate (~2.74 WPS) across all latency levels, whereas the cascaded systems suffer sharp WPS drops at low latency due to frequent inter-word pauses. Human evaluation (side-by-side, 100 votes per comparison) confirms LLaMA-Omni wins on both helpfulness and naturalness against both cascaded systems at comparable ~350 ms latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaMA-Omni sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: (1) speech-language models that extend LLMs with speech tokens (SpeechGPT, AudioPaLM, AnyGPT) and (2) speech-to-text LLMs that add a frozen speech encoder (SALMONN, Qwen2-Audio, Pengi). It departs from the first by avoiding vocabulary expansion and joint pretraining, and from the second by adding a streaming speech decoder for speech *generation*. The key architectural departure is the use of a CTC-based non-autoregressive streaming Transformer (inherited from simultaneous translation work by Ma et al., 2023 and Zhang et al., 2024b) to align text hidden states with discrete speech units, replacing the autoregressive speech-token decoding used by SpeechGPT and the word-level streaming TTS used by cascaded baselines. The InstructS2S-200K dataset is a new contribution that rewrites and re-synthesizes existing text instruction data (Alpaca, UltraChat) to match speech-interaction style, filling a gap the authors identify in prior speech-language model training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a competitive open-source speech-interaction model (236 ms latency, matching GPT-4o's ~320 ms) can be trained in under 3 days on 4 GPUs with only 200K samples, dramatically lowering the barrier to building such systems. This efficiency claim is the paper's most practically impactful finding, as prior speech-language models required orders of magnitude more data and compute. Open questions remain: the speech decoder is trained on only ~1K hours of data (yielding ASR-WER of ~10.8 vs. ~3.8 for industrial TTS), the evaluation set is small (199 instructions), and the generated speech lacks expressiveness (single VITS voice for responses). The authors note future work on improving speech expressiveness and true real-time (full-duplex) interaction, which would be needed for production deployment.
