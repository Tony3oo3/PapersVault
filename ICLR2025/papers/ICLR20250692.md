## Problem

Vector Quantized VAEs rely on the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) to backpropagate through the non-differentiable codebook lookup, which simply copies the gradient from the codebook vector to the encoder output. This discards all geometric information about where the encoder output sits within its Voronoi region, contributing to codebook collapse, under-utilization, and degraded reconstruction quality. The paper asks whether a different gradient transport through the quantization layer can preserve useful spatial information without reverting to the AutoEncoder gradient.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 256×256×3 RGB images (ImageNet, FFHQ, CelebA-HQ) or 16-frame video clips (64×64×3 or 128×128×3 from BAIR Robot Pushing and UCF-101), compressing them to discrete latent codes of varying shapes (e.g., 64×64×3 with 8192 codebook vectors, or 32×32×4 with 16384 vectors) and reconstructing the input. The forward pass is identical to standard VQ-VAE/VQGAN; the only modification occurs in the backward pass, where the gradient through the quantization layer is transformed rather than copied. Outputs include the reconstructed image/video, discrete codebook indices, and standard reconstruction metrics (r-FID, r-IS, quantization error, codebook usage).

## Method

The rotation trick replaces the STE's identity gradient (∂q/∂e = I) with a rotation-and-rescaling transformation: for encoder output e and nearest codebook vector q, it computes a rotation matrix R (efficiently via Householder reflections) that aligns e to q, multiplied by the norm ratio ||q||/||e||, and treats both as constants during backpropagation. This preserves the angle between q and ∇_q L as the gradient is transported from q to e, encoding the relative angular distance and magnitude of e within its Voronoi region into the gradient update. The resulting push-pull effect—boundary points are pushed toward under-utilized codebook vectors while loosely clustered points are pulled toward the codebook vector—simultaneously increases codebook utilization and reduces quantization error. The approach is drop-in compatible with existing VQ-VAE, VQGAN, ViT-VQGAN, and TimeSformer-VQGAN architectures, requiring only a change to the backward pass with no additional forward-pass computation or architectural modification.

## Results

Across 11 VQ-VAE/VQGAN configurations, the rotation trick consistently improves reconstruction quality, codebook utilization, and quantization error over the standard straight-through estimator (STE). The most dramatic improvement appears in the VQGAN for latent diffusion (32×32×4 latent, 16384 codebook): r-FID drops from 5.0 to 1.1, r-IS rises from 141.5 to 200.2, codebook usage increases from 2% to 27%, and quantization error decreases by two orders of magnitude (1.2e-2 → 2.4e-4). For the standard VQ-VAE on ImageNet (64×64×3, 8192 codebook), r-FID improves from 19.0 to 16.5, r-IS from 97.3 to 106.3, and quantization error drops from 1.0e-3 to 1.4e-4. On video reconstruction (TimeSformer-VQGAN), the STE baseline suffers complete codebook collapse (0.4% usage) while the rotation trick achieves 43% usage and reduces r-FVD from 1661.1 to 21.4 on BAIR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The rotation trick outperforms the standard STE-based VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) on every metric across all 11 configurations: quantization error is reduced by 1–2 orders of magnitude, codebook usage increases substantially (e.g., 2%→27% for the 16384-code VQGAN), and r-FID/r-IS improve consistently. Against Gumbel-Softmax stochastic quantization (Baevski et al., 2019), the rotation trick achieves far higher codebook usage (99% vs. 39% in the 8192-code setting) and better reconstruction (r-FID 16.5 vs. 28.6). For the VQGAN autoregressive paradigm (Esser et al., 2021), the rotation trick reduces r-FID from 7.3 to 4.6 and r-IS from 118.2 to 146.5 on ImageNet, and from 4.7 to 3.7 on FFHQ/CelebA-HQ. The most dramatic win is against the latent-diffusion VQGAN (Rombach et al., 2022): r-FID drops from 5.0 to 1.1 and codebook usage jumps from 2% to 27%. The ViT-VQGAN (Yu et al., 2021) baseline suffers near-total codebook collapse (0.3% usage, r-FID 29.2) while the rotation trick recovers to 2.2% usage and r-FID 11.2. Notably, the Hessian approximation and exact-gradient alternatives both perform worse than the STE baseline, confirming the paper's theoretical argument that computing true encoder gradients reintroduces AutoEncoder-like overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) of Bengio et al. (2013), which has been the standard gradient approximation for vector quantization layers. It departs from two broad lines of prior work: methods that sidestep the STE entirely (Gumbel-Softmax, stochastic quantization, alternating optimization) and methods that modify the codebook or its lookup (cosine similarity, codebook splitting, fixed geometries). The rotation trick instead keeps the deterministic codebook lookup and STE-style forward pass intact, changing only the backward pass to rotate and rescale the gradient so that the angle between the codebook vector and gradient is preserved rather than the gradient's absolute direction. The paper also contrasts against Hessian-based and exact-gradient alternatives, showing that 'more accurate' encoder gradients actually degrade performance by collapsing the VQ-VAE into an AutoEncoder-like training regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rotation trick is a minimal, drop-in modification to VQ-VAE backpropagation (replacing the identity matrix with a Householder-rotation-derived matrix) that consistently improves reconstruction quality, codebook utilization, and quantization error across 11 diverse configurations spanning CNN, ViT, and TimeSformer architectures on images and video. This addresses codebook collapse—a persistent practical failure mode—without introducing new hyperparameters, temperature schedules, or architectural changes. A key open question, explicitly flagged by the authors, is whether Stage-1 reconstruction improvements transfer to Stage-2 generative modeling (diffusion, autoregressive), where the relationship between tokenizer quality and downstream generation is known to be non-linear. The method also has a documented limitation: it can 'over-rotate' gradients when encoder outputs or codebook vectors are forced near zero norm, potentially performing worse than the STE in such regimes.
