## Problem

Existing methods for manipulating latent variables in generative models—such as spherical interpolation (SLERP) or Norm-Aware Optimization—only work well in special cases, require costly numerical optimisation or tunable hyperparameters, and are difficult to generalise beyond Gaussian latents or beyond simple interpolation to broader operations like subspace construction. The paper identifies that the root cause of poor generation quality is not merely an unlikely norm but a mismatch in the broader statistical characteristics of the latent vector relative to the model's assumed latent distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes K seed latent vectors (each a D-dimensional vector obtained by sampling from or inverting into the latent distribution) and a set of linear combination weights w ∈ ℝ^K that encode the desired operation (interpolation, centroid, or subspace projection). It outputs a single transformed latent vector z that provably follows the target latent distribution, or, for subspace construction, a navigable K-dimensional coordinate system where each grid point maps to a valid latent. The approach is model-agnostic, requiring only knowledge of the latent distribution's parameters (μ, Σ for Gaussians, or per-element CDFs for general independent-component distributions), and applies identically to diffusion and flow matching models across image and 3D point-cloud modalities.

## Method

LOL (Latent Optimal Linear combinations) exploits the fact that a linear combination y = Σw_k x_k of K i.i.d. Gaussian latents follows N(αμ, βΣ) with α = Σw_k and β = Σw_k², and applies the closed-form affine transport map T_{w,μ}(y) = (1 − α/√β)μ + y/√β to restore the target distribution N(μ, Σ). This map is proven to be the Monge optimal transport map minimising the quadratic Wasserstein distance W₂ between the source and target distributions, and is extended to general latent distributions with independent components via element-wise CDF/inverse-CDF transformations. The paper further introduces a practical diagnostic: applying the Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality test to seed latents (particularly those obtained via finite-step inversion) to detect when they lack the statistical characteristics needed for reliable manipulation, showing that low p-values predict poor interpolation quality even when reconstruction error is acceptable.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k interpolation (1250 pairs, Stable Diffusion 2.1), LOL achieves 67.4% class-preservation accuracy and FID 38.9, outperforming SLERP (64.6%, FID 42.6) and NAO (62.1%, FID 46.0) while running in 6 ms per path (vs. 30 s for NAO). For centroid determination (1250 centroids), LOL reaches 46.3% accuracy and FID 87.7, beating NAO (44.0%, FID 93.0) and Standardised Euclidean (44.6%, FID 88.8) with a 150× speedup over NAO. LOL is a closed-form transform requiring no optimisation, no hyperparameters, and no model-specific architecture assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SLERP (Shoemake, 1985), LOL wins on both interpolation accuracy (+2.8 pp: 67.4% vs 64.6%) and FID (38.9 vs 42.6), while being slightly faster (6 ms vs 9 ms per path). The paper explains that SLERP only approximately satisfies the distributional condition (β ≈ 1 for high-dimensional unit Gaussians, proven in Appendix C), whereas LOL enforces it exactly (α = β = 1) for any weights and any covariance. Against NAO (Samuel et al., 2023), LOL dominates on every metric: higher accuracy (67.4% vs 62.1% for interpolation; 46.3% vs 44.0% for centroids), lower FID (38.9 vs 46.0; 87.7 vs 93.0), and 5000×–15000× faster (closed-form vs. numerical optimisation). The authors note that NAO underperformed relative to its original paper, partly due to differences in FID feature settings (2048 vs 64) and DDIM inversion steps (400 vs. unspecified), but even under the 64-feature setting where NAO's FID drops to 1.30, LOL's 1.72 remains competitive while being orders of magnitude faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the optimal transport framework (Peyré & Cuturi, 2020) to derive a closed-form Monge-optimal transport map for linear combinations of latent variables, unifying and generalizing prior ad-hoc fixes for latent interpolation. It departs from the norm-matching perspective of White (2016) and the numerical optimization of NAO (Samuel et al., 2023) by showing that the full distributional match — not just the norm — is required, and that the correction is simply an affine rescaling. The subspace construction extends beyond the h-space editing literature (Kwon et al., 2022; Park et al., 2023), which requires UNet-specific architectural modifications, to a model-agnostic closed-form procedure applicable to any generative model with known latent distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LOL provides a drop-in, closed-form replacement for SLERP and NAO that is provably optimal, model-agnostic, and extends naturally to operations (subspaces, centroids) that prior methods cannot handle, enabling off-the-shelf optimization over low-dimensional latent representations without architecture-specific engineering. The distribution-testing diagnostic (Kolmogorov-Smirnov) offers a practical tool for identifying deficient inversion latents before they propagate errors into downstream manipulations. Open questions include whether standard normality tests align well enough with individual network expectations, how to handle latent distributions with dependent elements, and whether the subspace basis vectors can be informed by semantic edit directions from h-space methods.
