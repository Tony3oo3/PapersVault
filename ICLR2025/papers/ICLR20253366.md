## Problem

Prior LLM-based hardware design work has focused almost exclusively on Verilog generation while neglecting the equally critical task of Verilog understanding (summarizing code into natural language). Moreover, existing fine-tuning efforts establish weak alignment between natural language and Verilog code—using template-based line-level translations or small, low-quality datasets—which degrades generation quality and produces non-synthesizable designs. No benchmark or semantically-aware evaluation metric existed for assessing a model's ability to interpret Verilog.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253366/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the understanding task, the model receives a Verilog module, block, or single line (up to 2048 tokens) and produces a natural language description at the corresponding granularity—ranging from a detailed implementation specification to a concise high-level functional summary. For the generation task, it takes a high-level natural language description plus a predefined module header and outputs synthesizable Verilog source code. The encoder-decoder architecture means the input code is encoded by the shallow encoder while the decoder autoregressively generates the output, with cross-attention layers bridging the two.

## Method

DeepRTL fine-tunes CodeT5+ (a shallow-encoder/deep-decoder architecture with cross-attention) on a curated dataset of 61,968 Verilog modules annotated via a chain-of-thought pipeline with GPT-4, producing aligned natural language descriptions at line, block, and module levels with multiple granularity tiers. The key training innovation is a three-layer curriculum learning strategy structured as a tree: the model first learns line-level then block-level then module-level code, progresses from detailed to high-level descriptions within each level, and transitions from GPT-annotated to human-annotated data within each granularity—following a pre-order traversal. Only the shallow encoder and cross-attention layers are trained (the deep decoder is frozen), and instruction tuning with scenario-specific prompts (task type, granularity, comment level) ensures the model adapts its output format per request. Two model sizes are produced (220M and 16B), and the 220M variant outperforms GPT-4 on understanding and matches o1-preview on generation despite being orders of magnitude smaller.

## Results

On the newly introduced Verilog Understanding Benchmark, DeepRTL-220m achieves a GPT Score of 0.705 and embedding similarity of 0.837, outperforming GPT-4 (0.683 / 0.824) and o1-preview (0.643 / 0.806) across all six metrics. On the RTLLM generation benchmark, DeepRTL-220m matches o1-preview on syntax success rate (78.06%) and Pass@5 syntax (80.65%), while surpassing GPT-4 on syntax success rate (78.06% vs. 63.87%) and Pass@1 functional accuracy (32.26% vs. 29.03%). Human evaluation confirms these findings, with DeepRTL-220m achieving 78% accuracy versus 72% for GPT-4 and 67% for o1-preview on the understanding task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Verilog understanding, DeepRTL-220m decisively outperforms GPT-4 on every metric (e.g., BLEU-4: 18.66 vs. 5.36; GPT Score: 0.705 vs. 0.683) and also surpasses o1-preview (GPT Score: 0.705 vs. 0.643), demonstrating that domain-specific fine-tuning with high-quality multi-level annotations yields superior code comprehension. On Verilog generation, DeepRTL-220m ties o1-preview on syntax success rate (78.06%) and Pass@5 syntax (80.65%) but trails on Pass@5 functional accuracy (41.94% vs. 51.61%), while clearly beating GPT-4 on syntax success rate (78.06% vs. 63.87%) and Pass@1 function (32.26% vs. 29.03%). Compared to the unfine-tuned CodeT5+ base models, fine-tuning with curriculum learning yields a dramatic improvement (e.g., GPT Score jumps from 0.032 to 0.705 for the 220M variant). Against LLaMA2-70B-Chat (the annotator used in MG-Verilog), DeepRTL-220m achieves substantially higher scores (GPT Score 0.705 vs. 0.500), indirectly validating the quality concerns raised about MG-Verilog annotations. Finally, DeepRTL-220m outperforms the larger Verilog-specific models RTLCoder (fine-tuned on DeepSeek-coder-6.7B) and VeriGen (fine-tuned on CodeGen-16B) on both understanding and generation, despite having orders of magnitude fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-based Verilog generation work (Thakur et al. 2024, Chang et al. 2024b, Zhang et al. 2024) by introducing Verilog understanding as a first-class task alongside generation, unifying both under a single encoder-decoder model (CodeT5+). It departs from prior efforts that relied on weak or template-based natural-language-to-Verilog alignment (e.g., AST-to-text mapping in Chang et al. 2024b, or LLaMA2-70B-Chat annotations in MG-Verilog) by using GPT-4 with chain-of-thought to produce multi-level (line, block, module) descriptions verified at ~90% accuracy. The curriculum learning strategy, previously applied to code LLMs by Na et al. 2024, is adapted here to a hierarchical annotation structure that no prior Verilog dataset has exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first Verilog understanding benchmark (100 modules, human-verified) and the shift from BLEU/ROUGE to embedding-similarity and GPT-score metrics address a real evaluation gap in the EDA-LLM literature, giving the community a more semantically meaningful way to assess code summarization. The 220M-parameter model matching o1-preview on generation and surpassing GPT-4 on understanding demonstrates that compact, domain-specific models can be practical alternatives to proprietary APIs for hardware design teams with data-security constraints. Key limitations include the 2048-token cap (excluding larger designs), reliance on GPT-4 as an annotation oracle (capping quality), and the small understanding benchmark; open questions include whether the encoder-decoder architecture generalizes to other HDLs or scales to chip-level designs, and whether the curriculum strategy transfers to other multi-granularity code domains.
