## Problem

Mean Reverting (MR) Diffusion offers a natural way to incorporate image conditions by modifying the SDE structure rather than the score function, but its sampling requires hundreds of NFEs because existing training-free fast samplers (DPM-Solver, UniPC, DDIM) are designed for SDEs with linear drift in x, whereas MRSDE has an affine drift with an intercept μ. No fast sampler was available for MR Diffusion prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained MR Diffusion model (NAFNet-based) and a degraded image that serves as the condition vector μ (same spatial shape as the target), along with Gaussian noise to initialize the sampling at x_T = μ + σ_∞·ε. It produces a restored clean RGB image by running the fast sampler in 5–10 NFEs instead of the original 100+. The algorithm is training-free and plug-and-play: it only replaces the sampling loop while keeping the trained network and all hyperparameters unchanged, and it supports noise, data, and velocity prediction parameterizations via closed-form transformations.

## Method

The paper derives semi-analytical solutions to both the reverse-time SDE and the probability-flow ODE of MRSDE. Each solution decomposes into an exact analytical term (involving α_t, σ_t, and μ) plus an integral of the neural network output, which is estimated via exponential integrators with Taylor expansion in the half log-SNR variable λ. The key insight is that the affine drift of MRSDE only changes the analytical part, while the integral part is handled identically to existing solvers like DPM-Solver. The authors further prove that the previously used posterior sampling is equivalent to first-order Euler-Maruyama discretization, whereas MR Sampler achieves higher-order convergence. Experiments across ten image restoration tasks show 10–20× speedup at 5–10 NFEs with comparable quality, and reveal that data prediction is numerically more stable than noise prediction at low NFEs due to bounded network outputs.

## Results

MR Sampler achieves a 10–20× speedup over the standard 100-NFE posterior sampling while maintaining comparable quality across ten image restoration tasks. On the CelebA-HQ inpainting task, MR Sampler-1 (SDE, data prediction, order 1) reaches LPIPS 0.0401 and FID 18.46 at just 10 NFEs, versus LPIPS 0.8097 and FID 374.1 for posterior sampling at the same budget. At 5 NFEs, MR Sampler-1 still achieves LPIPS 0.0428 and PSNR 31.03, while both baselines degrade to LPIPS > 0.84 and PSNR < 9.6. At 100 NFEs all methods converge to similar quality (LPIPS ≈ 0.039), confirming that MR Sampler matches the quality ceiling with far fewer steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MR Sampler against two baselines: posterior sampling (Luo et al., 2024b) and Euler-Maruyama discretization (Kloeden et al., 1992). Both baselines are first-order methods that require ~100 NFEs for acceptable quality; at 50 NFEs they already degrade severely (LPIPS jumps from ~0.04 to >0.42 on inpainting), and at 10 or 5 NFEs they produce near-useless outputs (LPIPS > 0.8, PSNR < 10). MR Sampler-1, by contrast, maintains LPIPS < 0.05 and PSNR > 28 across all NFE budgets from 5 to 100, effectively matching the 100-NFE quality ceiling at 5–10 NFEs. The paper further shows that posterior sampling is mathematically equivalent to a variant of Euler-Maruyama discretization (Appendix A.2), explaining why both baselines fail similarly at low NFEs, while MR Sampler's semi-analytical solution eliminates the analytical-part approximation error. On the Rain100H dataset, data prediction with MR Sampler-1 achieves LPIPS 0.0637 at 5 NFEs, while noise prediction collapses to LPIPS 1.416, highlighting the importance of parameterization choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exponential integrator framework of DPM-Solver (Lu et al., 2022a) and DPM-Solver++ (Lu et al., 2022b) to the Mean Reverting SDE (MRSDE) of Luo et al. (2023b), whose affine drift (with intercept μ) prevents direct application of existing fast samplers designed for VPSDE/VESDE. The key theoretical contribution is deriving semi-analytical solutions to the reverse-time SDE and PF-ODE of MRSDE, where the analytical part is computed exactly and only the neural-network-dependent integral requires numerical approximation. The paper further establishes that the standard posterior sampling used in MR Diffusion is equivalent to Euler-Maruyama discretization, positioning MR Sampler as a higher-order method that eliminates part of the approximation error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–20× reduction in NFEs (from hundreds to 5–10) makes MR Diffusion practically viable for real-time or resource-constrained image restoration, while remaining training-free and compatible with all three mainstream parameterizations. The finding that noise prediction catastrophically degrades below ~20 NFEs while data prediction remains stable provides actionable guidance for practitioners choosing parameterizations in few-step regimes. Open questions include whether the approach can be extended to latent-space MR Diffusion, whether it can be combined with distillation to push below 5 NFEs, and whether the semi-analytical solution framework generalizes to other non-standard SDE structures beyond the affine drift.
