## Problem

Fine-tuned LLMs for conditional text generation (data-to-text, summarization) frequently produce hallucinations—unfaithful content not grounded in the input context—because their internal statistical patterns interfere with context adherence. Existing faithfulness-improvement methods either rely on external tools (NER, QA models), were designed for smaller encoder-decoder architectures, or modify decoding rather than training, and none provide a self-supervised, training-based solution tailored to recent decoder-only LLMs on domain-specific tasks.

## Input / Output

The method operates on aligned context-target pairs (c, y) where c is structured data (Wikipedia tables, knowledge graphs, key-value pairs) or source text (BBC articles, messenger conversations, medical papers) and y is a reference sentence or summary. During the noisy generation step, the fine-tuned model p_θ0 samples tokens conditioned on c while the pre-trained model p_LM samples unconditionally, and the two distributions are mixed token-by-token via a Bernoulli parameter α to produce fluent but ungrounded negative samples y⁻. The final output is a preference-tuned decoder-only model (LLaMA-2-7B/13B or Mistral-7B) that generates text more faithfully grounded in the input context.

## Method

SCOPE is a two-stage self-supervised framework. In stage 1, a pre-trained LLM is fine-tuned on only half the training data (D1) via standard cross-entropy to obtain p_θ0. In stage 2, unfaithful negative samples y⁻ are generated for the remaining half (D2) by a noisy decoding procedure that, at each token step, samples from p_θ0(·|c) with probability (1−α) and from the unconditional pre-trained p_LM(·) with probability α, thereby simulating realistic hallucinations where the model's internal knowledge leaks into the output. The model is then preference-tuned using the DPO loss on triples (c, y, y⁻), encouraging it to increase the likelihood gap between grounded references and unfaithful samples. The key insight is that mixing a context-conditioned fine-tuned model with a context-free pre-trained model produces fluent, domain-appropriate negatives that are challenging enough to yield effective learning signals without requiring external tools or human annotation.

## Results

On data-to-text generation (ToTTo, E2E, FeTaQA, WebNLG), SCOPE achieves up to +8.2 points PARENT and +5.5 points NLI Score over standard fine-tuning (e.g., 51.88 NLI and 86.11 PARENT on ToTTo with LLAMA2-7B vs. 46.42/80.55 for SFT). On text summarization (XSum, SAMSum, PubMed), SCOPE gains up to +8.8 points AlignScore (e.g., 65.10 on XSum vs. 56.25 for SFT with LLAMA2-7B). GPT-4-as-a-judge evaluations show SCOPE achieving 35–74% win rates against SFT, roughly 2.1× higher than the best baseline, and human evaluation confirms SCOPE generations are preferred 40% of the time versus 15.2% for SFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCOPE consistently outperforms all four baselines on faithfulness metrics across every dataset and model. CAD (Shi et al., 2023) and PMI (van der Poel et al., 2022) show negligible improvements over SFT on data-to-text generation (e.g., CAD scores 46.33 vs. SFT's 46.42 NLI on ToTTo) and only marginal gains on summarization (e.g., +1.0 AlignScore on SAMSum). CRITIC (Lango & Dusek, 2023) similarly fails to improve faithfulness on data-to-text and offers only small summarization gains. CLIFF (Cao & Wang, 2021) is the strongest baseline, achieving modest improvements on data-to-text (e.g., 46.69 NLI on ToTTo, 39.67 on FeTaQA) and some summarization gains, but remains 5+ points below SCOPE on NLI and 5+ points below on PARENT. In GPT-4 preference evaluations, SCOPE's win rate is 2–3× higher than CLIFF's (e.g., 35.03% vs. 14.57% on ToTTo), while the other three baselines show predominantly tie rates (>85%) indicating they barely alter generation quality relative to SFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCOPE builds directly on the DPO preference-optimization framework (Rafailov et al., 2023), repurposing it for faithfulness in conditional text generation rather than chat alignment. It departs from prior faithfulness methods in two key ways: (1) instead of modifying the decoding process (CAD, PMI, CRITIC) or constructing negatives with external NER/QA tools (CLIFF), it generates unfaithful samples self-supervised by mixing a fine-tuned model with its pre-trained counterpart during token-by-token decoding; (2) it operates as a two-stage training procedure (SFT on half the data, then preference-tuning on the other half with noisy negatives) rather than a single fine-tuning pass or a decoding-time intervention. The closest prior work is CLIFF (Cao & Wang, 2021), which also uses contrastive learning with synthetic negatives, but relies on external tools and was designed for encoder-decoder architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised generation of unfaithful negatives removes the need for costly human annotation of faithfulness, making the approach broadly applicable across domains without domain-specific tooling. The consistent failure of prior decoding-time and contrastive methods on modern 7B LLMs for data-to-text generation highlights a gap that training-time preference optimization fills. Limitations include testing only on 7B models, reliance on relatively simple datasets (WebNLG, E2E), and primarily automatic evaluation; open questions include whether the noisy-mixing strategy scales to larger models, whether the optimal α range generalizes, and whether the BLEU/ROUGE trade-off is acceptable in deployment settings where reference overlap still matters.
