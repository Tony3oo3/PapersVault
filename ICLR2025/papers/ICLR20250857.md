## Problem

In federated learning, per-round communication cost scales linearly with model dimension d (O(d)), making it prohibitively expensive for large models with billions of parameters. Existing compression techniques such as sparsification and quantization reduce constants but retain this intrinsic dimension dependence. This paper asks whether communication per round can be made completely independent of d, and whether the total communication cost can also be rendered dimension-free.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250857/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives only a small set of scalar gradient values and integer random seeds from the server (O(KP) scalars per round for K local steps and P perturbations), rather than the full d-dimensional model vector. The client returns K·P gradient scalars per round as its uplink. Unusually, the server need not store the model at all—it functions purely as an aggregator of scalars and seeds, while clients reconstruct their local model by replaying historical scalar-seed pairs from their last participation round.

## Method

DeComFL exploits the decomposition of the zeroth-order (SPSA) gradient into a scalar magnitude and a random direction vector: the direction is regenerated identically across all clients from a shared seed, so only the scalar must be transmitted. The standard FL three-step loop (pull model, local update, aggregate) is restructured into (reconstruct model from historical scalars/seeds, local ZO update with a mandatory revert to the pre-update snapshot, and global averaging of gradient scalars). The revert step is critical—it ensures the client model at the start of local updates always equals the server model, preserving the FedAvg-style aggregation invariant without ever transmitting the model. Under standard assumptions the convergence rate is O(√(d/mPKR)); under a low κ-effective-rank assumption on the Hessian, the rate becomes O(√(κ/mPR)), making both per-round and total communication cost independent of d.

## Results

On LLM fine-tuning (OPT-1.3B, SST-2), DeComFL with P=10 achieves 90.78% test accuracy using only 0.24 MB of total communication, compared to FedAvg's 90.38% at 1.27 TB and FedZO's 90.33% at 5.20 TB — a ~5×10⁶ reduction in communication. Across all six NLP tasks and both model sizes (OPT-125M and OPT-1.3B), DeComFL matches or slightly exceeds MeZO and FedZO accuracy while transmitting only 0.12–1.80 MB total, versus 0.12–5.08 TB for FedAvg and 0.38–7.80 TB for FedZO. On the Fashion-MNIST training task (1.2M-parameter CNN), DeComFL achieves comparable test accuracy to FedAvg and FedCom while communicating orders of magnitude fewer scalars per client.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedAvg, DeComFL matches or slightly exceeds accuracy on all six NLP tasks for both model sizes (e.g., 90.78% vs 90.38% on SST-2/OPT-1.3B) while reducing total communication from terabytes to sub-megabyte levels (~10⁷× less). Against FedZO (same P=10), DeComFL achieves nearly identical accuracy (within 0.1–1.5%) but with communication costs of 0.12–1.80 MB versus 0.38–7.80 TB — a reduction of roughly 10⁶–10⁷×. Against MeZO (single-agent ZO baseline), DeComFL matches or slightly surpasses accuracy on most tasks (e.g., 85.08% vs 83.99% on SST-2/OPT-125M; 90.78% vs 90.23% on SST-2/OPT-1.3B), confirming that the federated multi-client setting does not degrade ZO performance. On Fashion-MNIST, DeComFL converges slower in wall-clock rounds than FedAvg and FedCom (quantization) due to the inherent slower convergence of ZO methods, but when plotted against effective communication volume, DeComFL achieves higher test accuracy with far fewer transmitted scalars. Against FedAvg+Top-k (k=10%), DeComFL again shows substantially lower communication while maintaining competitive accuracy, and reducing k further to 1% causes severe accuracy degradation for Top-k while DeComFL is unaffected.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeComFL recombines two well-established ideas—the SPSA zeroth-order gradient decomposition (a scalar magnitude times a random direction) and the shared-seed reconstruction trick from distributed optimization—into a federated learning protocol that neither FedAvg nor FedZO achieves. It departs from FedZO, which made only the uplink O(P) while leaving the downlink at O(d), by also eliminating dimension-dependent downlink communication through a 'reconstruct model' step that replays historical gradient scalars and seeds. The low-effective-rank convergence analysis adapts Malladi et al.'s (2023) assumption from single-agent ZO fine-tuning to the multi-client FL setting, which is a non-trivial extension given data heterogeneity and partial participation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is enormous: fine-tuning a 1.3B-parameter LLM requires only ~1 MB of total client-server communication versus ~10 GB per round for FedAvg, a reduction of roughly seven orders of magnitude that makes FL fine-tuning feasible over low-bandwidth links. Key limitations remain—ZO convergence is inherently slower than first-order methods, the approach does not yet address data heterogeneity (Scaffold-style corrections are unexplored), and the low-effective-rank assumption, while empirically supported, lacks a universal theoretical guarantee. Open questions include whether the technique composes with advanced optimizers (ZO-Adam, ZO-SVRG) without breaking the dimension-free property, and whether the server's storage of full seed/gradient history scales to thousands of rounds in production.
