## Problem

Integrating self-organizing maps into deep neural networks to replicate cortical topography has consistently caused large drops in object recognition performance (15–37% relative decline). The paper identifies the root cause as a fundamental discord between the bottom-up, activation-driven competitive updates in classical SOMs and the top-down, credit-based gradient updates that govern deep network learning, and asks whether topography can be induced without this conflict.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252800/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (ImageNet at 224×224) and outputs a 1000-way object class label, operating identically to a vanilla ResNet-18 at inference time with no additional computation. The distinguishing output is the spatial arrangement of filters on a 2D simulated cortical sheet per layer, which encodes topographical organization (orientation pinwheels in early layers, category-selective patches in deep layers) but is purely a training-time structural property rather than an inference-time output.

## Method

CB-SOM modifies Kohonen's SOM by replacing the activation-based Best-Matching Unit (BMU) selection with a gradient-based one: at each training step, the filter with the highest L2 norm of the cross-entropy loss gradient (∂L/∂w) is chosen as the winner, rather than the filter with the highest average activation. All weights are first updated via standard SGD to reduce the task loss, then the SOM neighborhood update (Gaussian kernel centered on the BMU, with exponentially decaying learning rate α and variance σ) is applied to pull neighboring filters toward the winner's weights. This credit-based BMU selection aligns the topography-inducing updates with the direction of gradient descent, reducing the conflict that plagued activation-based SOMs. The procedure is applied at every convolutional layer and every training step within a ResNet-18 (and CORnet-S) trained on ImageNet for 90 epochs, yielding V1-like pinwheel organization in early layers and IT-like category-selective clusters in deep layers while narrowing the performance gap to ~11% relative decline versus 26–27% for prior topographical models.

## Results

On ImageNet top-1 accuracy, CB-SOM incurs only an 11% relative performance drop compared to the non-topographical ResNet-18 baseline, substantially smaller than the 27% drop for AB-SOM (activation-based Kohonen SOM) and 26% for TDANN. On BrainScore representational similarity, CB-SOM achieves scores comparable to ResNet-18 across most macaque ventral visual cortex benchmarks, significantly outperforming AB-SOM and TDANN, with the gap most pronounced on the behavioral benchmark. On fMRI prediction (NSD dataset), CB-SOM category-selective clusters predict BOLD responses in FFA, PPA, and EBA patches significantly better than all topographical baselines and even surpass the ResNet-18 linear-projection baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AB-SOM (the activation-based adaptation of Kohonen's SOM), CB-SOM wins decisively on every metric: ImageNet relative drop is 11% vs. 27%, CTA alignment indices are consistently positive (near zero) vs. consistently negative for AB-SOM, and CB-SOM produces V1-like pinwheel structures and Gabor-like filters whereas AB-SOM yields color-blob filters with over-represented BMUs. Against TDANN (Margalit et al., 2024), CB-SOM again shows a smaller ImageNet drop (11% vs. 26%) and higher BrainScore similarity, particularly on the behavioral benchmark where topographical models typically lag most. Against the non-topographical ResNet-18 baseline, CB-SOM retains an 11% ImageNet accuracy gap but matches or exceeds ResNet-18 on BrainScore representational similarity and fMRI prediction in all three category-selective patches (FFA, PPA, EBA), indicating that the topographical reorganization does not degrade—and in some cases improves—brain alignment. Against ITN (Blauch et al., 2022), CB-SOM outperforms on fMRI Pearson correlation for all three cortical patches. Overall, CB-SOM is the only topographical model that simultaneously achieves strong topographical organization (pinwheels, category-selective clusters, smooth selectivity transitions) while keeping task performance close to the non-topographical baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Kohonen's Self-Organizing Map (1982, 1990) to deep convolutional networks by replacing the traditional activation-based Best Matching Unit selection with a gradient-magnitude (credit-based) selection, aligning the competitive learning rule with top-down backpropagation. It departs from recent topographical DNN approaches (TDANN, ITN, Lee et al.) that impose topography via auxiliary loss functions or two-phase training, which the authors identify as the source of the large performance penalties (15–37% accuracy drops). The key conceptual shift is that topography should be induced by the same credit signals that drive task learning, rather than by a separate bottom-up or auxiliary objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By narrowing the topography-performance gap from ~27–36% to ~11%, CB-SOM makes brain-like topographical models substantially more viable for both neuroscience modeling and potential interpretability applications in AI. The paper demonstrates that the performance cost of topography was largely an artifact of misaligned learning signals rather than an inherent trade-off, reframing how the field should approach cortical map formation. Open questions include whether the remaining 11% gap can be closed, whether the approach generalizes beyond vision to other modalities (language, motor), and the lack of formal convergence guarantees for the combined gradient + SOM update rule.
