## Problem

Test-time prompt tuning (TPT) adapts CLIP prompts independently per sample, ignoring the rich distribution information available in related test samples. Naively extending TPT to an online setting (using the previous sample's optimized prompt as initialization) leads to severe prompt collapse due to error accumulation from entropy minimization without ground-truth labels. The paper asks how to exploit relevant online test information while preventing this collapse.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB test image and a set of class-name text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a [class c]') as input, producing a predicted class label. Each test image is augmented into 64 samples via AugMix (random resize-crop plus auto-contrast, posterization, rotation, solarization, shearing, and translation), and the entropy and probability-difference selection metrics are computed over these augmentations. The method operates in CLIP's text embedding space with a prompt buffer of at most M=10 learnable prompts, each initialized from a hand-crafted or pretrained prompt v0.

## Method

DynaPrompt maintains a dynamic prompt buffer and, for each test sample, adaptively selects a subset of relevant prompts via the intersection of two criteria: (1) prediction entropy below the initial-prompt threshold (more confident predictions) and (2) probability difference above the initial-prompt threshold (greater sensitivity to input structural changes, guarding against overconfident collapsed prompts). Only the selected prompts are optimized by one step of entropy minimization over the augmented batch, while all other buffered prompts remain frozen, preventing conflicting gradient directions and reducing error accumulation. When no prompt passes both selection thresholds, a new prompt initialized from v0 is appended to the buffer (dynamic prompt appending), and the least-recently-active prompt is evicted once the buffer reaches its maximum size M. This select-optimize-append-delete cycle allows the buffer to accumulate distribution-specific information from relevant past samples while autonomously discarding collapsed or irrelevant prompts.

## Results

On the domain generalization setting, DynaPrompt achieves an OoD mean accuracy of 61.81%, outperforming TPT (60.81%) by 1.0% and the best prompt-learning method Any-shift Prompt (60.85%) by 0.96%. When combined with pretrained prompts, CoOp+DynaPrompt reaches 64.31% OoD mean and MaPLe+DynaPrompt reaches 64.22%, surpassing their respective TPT counterparts (62.84% and 62.31%) by 1.47% and 1.91%. On the cross-dataset setting, DynaPrompt achieves a mean accuracy of 65.52%, and MaPLe+DynaPrompt reaches 67.29%, the best overall result across all 10 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaPrompt outperforms all 12 baselines on the domain generalization OoD mean. Against the zero-shot CLIP baseline (57.20%), it gains +4.61%; against the strongest prompt-learning method Any-shift Prompt (60.85%), it gains +0.96%. Among test-time prompt tuning methods, DynaPrompt beats TPT (60.81%) by +1.0%, DiffTPT (60.65%) by +1.16%, C-TPT (60.70%) by +1.11%, and AdaPrompt (57.18%) by +4.63%. When combined with pretrained prompts, CoOp+DynaPrompt (64.31%) surpasses CoOp+TPT (62.84%) by +1.47% and MaPLe+DynaPrompt (64.22%) surpasses MaPLe+PromptAlign (63.56%) by +0.66%, confirming that the dynamic selection and appending strategies are orthogonal to and complementary with existing prompt-learning approaches. On the cross-dataset setting, DynaPrompt (65.52%) beats TPT (65.10%) and DiffTPT (65.47%), while MaPLe+DynaPrompt (67.29%) achieves the best overall mean, edging out CoPrompt (67.00%) and MaPLe+PromptAlign (66.92%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends test-time prompt tuning (TPT, Shu et al. 2022) from independent per-sample optimization to an online sequential setting, where prompts are carried across test samples. The key conceptual departure is the identification of 'prompt collapse'—a severe degradation caused by error accumulation when naively chaining optimized prompts—and the introduction of a dynamic prompt buffer with dual-metric selection (entropy and probability difference) plus an appending/deletion mechanism to mitigate it. The method is orthogonal to existing prompt learning frameworks (CoOp, MaPLe) and can be layered on top of their pretrained prompts, positioning it as a complementary test-time refinement rather than a replacement for offline prompt training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnosis of why online test-time prompt tuning fails (entropy minimization without ground truth drives prompts into overconfident wrong directions, and errors compound), which is useful context for anyone attempting sequential adaptation of vision-language models. The dynamic buffer approach is practical and modular, but its utility is bounded by the modest 1–2% gains over TPT, the added per-sample latency (~0.39s), and sensitivity to test-sample ordering. Open questions include whether the buffer concept generalizes to larger model families or non-CLIP architectures, and whether the online setting is the right abstraction for most real-world deployment scenarios where test data arrives in batches rather than strict sequences.
