## Problem

Existing evaluations of machine unlearning for language models (e.g., TOFU) are narrow in scope, typically measuring only question-answering accuracy on small synthetic datasets. They fail to capture the full range of data owner expectations (verbatim memorization, privacy leakage) and deployer concerns (utility preservation, scalability to large forget sets, sustainability over sequential requests), leaving practitioners without a holistic view of whether an unlearning algorithm is actually deployable.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a 7B-parameter LM fine-tuned on a forget set plus retain set, along with the forget set text and optionally the retain set distribution, and produces an unlearned model scored on six criteria. The evaluation corpora consist of verbatim text (0.8M tokens for BBC news, 1.1M tokens for Harry Potter books) and GPT-4-derived QA pairs for knowledge evaluation, with a separate holdout set for membership inference. For scalability and sustainability tests, the NEWS forget set is extended to 3.3M tokens and partitioned into four sequential folds of 0.8M tokens each.

## Method

MUSE defines six evaluation criteria spanning data owner expectations (no verbatim memorization via ROUGE-L on prompted continuations, no knowledge memorization via ROUGE on QA pairs, no privacy leakage via AUC-ROC of Min-K% Prob membership inference relative to a retrained baseline) and deployer expectations (utility preservation via KnowMem on the retain set, scalability by varying forget set size, sustainability by applying unlearning sequentially across disjoint folds). Eight unlearning methods from four algorithmic families—Gradient Ascent, Negative Preference Optimization, Task Vectors, and Who's Harry Potter interpolation—are evaluated, with GA and NPO additionally combined with two regularizers (gradient descent on the retain set and KL divergence minimization) to yield four more variants. The key finding is that while most methods eliminate verbatim and knowledge memorization, they all suffer from severe privacy leakage (under- or over-unlearning detectable by MIA), significant utility degradation (24–100% loss), and poor scalability and sustainability, indicating that no current method is ready for real-world deployment.

## Results

On both NEWS (0.8M tokens) and BOOKS (1.1M tokens) corpora, GA and NPO achieve 0.0 on both VerbMem and KnowMem (complete removal of memorization) but simultaneously collapse utility to 0.0% (a 100% drop from f_retrain's 55.0% on NEWS and 74.5% on BOOKS). Task Vector best preserves utility (55.8% on NEWS, 64.7% on BOOKS) but fails to reduce memorization, with VerbMem actually increasing by 174.7% (NEWS) and 595% (BOOKS) relative to f_retrain. Critically, no method achieves PrivLeak within the acceptable [-5%, +5%] range: all eight methods either over-unlearn (e.g., WHP at +109.6% on NEWS) or under-unlearn (e.g., Task Vector at -99.8% on NEWS), indicating severe privacy leakage across the board.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GA (Jang et al., 2023) and NPO (Zhang et al., 2024b) are the most aggressive unlearners, achieving 0.0 on both VerbMem and KnowMem on both corpora, but they completely destroy model utility (0.0% on D_retain, a 100% drop from f_retrain) and cause significant privacy leakage (over-unlearning on NEWS, under-unlearning on BOOKS). Task Vector (Ilharco et al., 2023) is the only method that preserves utility close to f_retrain (55.8% vs. 55.0% on NEWS; 64.7% vs. 74.5% on BOOKS), but it catastrophically fails to unlearn: VerbMem actually increases by 174.7% (NEWS) and 595% (BOOKS) relative to f_retrain, and PrivLeak remains at -99.8% (NEWS), meaning the model is essentially unchanged. WHP (Eldan & Russinovich, 2023) achieves moderate unlearning on NEWS (VerbMem 19.7 vs. f_retrain's 20.8) but over-unlearns severely (PrivLeak +109.6%) and degrades utility by 48.5%; on BOOKS it fails to reduce knowledge memorization (KnowMem 55.7 vs. f_retrain's 28.9). No method satisfies all six MUSE criteria simultaneously, and the paper concludes that none is ready for real-world deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the machine unlearning evaluation landscape beyond the narrow question-answering-focused assessments of Eldan & Russinovich (2023) and the synthetic Q&A benchmark TOFU (Maini et al., 2024). It repurposes existing unlearning methods from four families—gradient ascent, negative preference optimization (DPO-based), task vectors (weight arithmetic), and model interpolation (WHP)—and subjects them to a unified six-criteria framework that jointly captures data-owner concerns (verbatim memorization, knowledge memorization, privacy leakage) and deployer concerns (utility preservation, scalability, sustainability). The privacy leakage metric builds on the Min-K% Prob membership inference attack (Shi et al., 2024a), and the utility metric reuses the ROUGE-based knowledge memorization score, but the key conceptual contribution is the systematic enumeration of all six desiderata and their joint evaluation on real-world corpora (Harry Potter books, BBC news) at a scale 22× larger than TOFU.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that no existing unlearning algorithm simultaneously satisfies all six criteria, with most methods causing severe privacy leakage and 24–100% utility degradation—directly challenges the practical viability of current approaches for GDPR compliance and copyright takedown. The scalability and sustainability failures (utility collapses with larger forget sets and sequential requests) reveal that even methods that appear effective on small, single-shot unlearning tasks are not deployment-ready. Open questions include whether any algorithmic family can balance all six dimensions, whether the findings generalize beyond 7B models and text-only settings, and whether formal unlearning guarantees (e.g., differential-privacy-based) can be made computationally tractable for LMs. The benchmark itself is likely to become a standard reference for future unlearning method development.
