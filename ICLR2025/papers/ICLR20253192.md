## Problem

Deep RL algorithms are highly sensitive to hyperparameters, and existing AutoRL methods either require additional environment samples to evaluate candidate configurations (hurting sample efficiency) or are adapted from AutoML without accounting for the non-stationarity inherent to RL optimization. No prior method dynamically adapts hyperparameters at each Bellman update while remaining sample-efficient and theoretically grounded in the RL objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253192/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment observations (state vectors for MuJoCo, 4-frame grayscale 84×84 image stacks for Atari) and mini-batches sampled from a shared replay buffer. It outputs an action selected via an ε_b-greedy policy over the ensemble, Q-function values from K parallel networks, and—critically at each target update—a selected target network index ψ that determines which network's parameters become the shared target for all K online networks. The selection is based on accumulated empirical loss over the replay buffer, requiring no additional environment interaction.

## Method

AdaQN maintains K online Q-networks, each trained with a distinct hyperparameter configuration (e.g., different learning rates, optimizers, architectures, or activation functions), all sharing a single target network. At each target update, the network with the smallest empirical approximation error (i.e., the lowest accumulated QN loss against the previous target) is selected as the new shared target, pulling all networks toward the one currently closest to the Bellman fixed point. The selection criterion is theoretically justified: Theorem 4.1 proves that minimizing the empirical loss is equivalent to minimizing the true approximation error under unbiased sampling, and Farahmand's Theorem 3.4 bounds the performance loss by the sum of approximation errors. To avoid passive learning of non-selected networks, the behavioral policy uses an ε_b-greedy strategy that samples actions from a random network with probability ε_b and from the selected target's corresponding network otherwise. The approach is orthogonal to the underlying critic-based algorithm, demonstrated with both DQN (AdaDQN) and SAC (AdaSAC, where K Polyak-averaged target networks are maintained and the two lowest-loss targets are used for the shared target computation).

## Results

On 6 MuJoCo environments, AdaSAC is an order of magnitude more sample-efficient than grid search, outperforms random search throughout training, and achieves the highest AUC while beating 13 out of 16 individual SAC runs in final performance. On 6 Atari 2600 games with an infinite hyperparameter space (18 activation functions, 24 optimizers, 4 losses, wide learning rate range, parameterizable architectures), AdaDQN outperforms SEARL at 40M frames and surpasses both random search and DEHB before 100M frames. On Lunar Lander, AdaDQN outperforms the best individual architecture, demonstrating that dynamic target selection yields superior overall performance than any static hyperparameter choice at no additional sample cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SEARL (Franke et al., ICLR 2021), AdaDQN clearly wins on the infinite hyperparameter space Atari benchmark: at 40M frames AdaDQN outperforms SEARL-max, and in 4 out of 6 games it exceeds SEARL's best return across all tested settings. SEARL's weakness stems from wasting environment steps evaluating poorly performing networks, which also pollutes the shared replay buffer. Against DEHB (Awad et al., IJCAI 2021), AdaDQN matches performance at 40M frames but pulls ahead before 100M frames, suggesting DEHB's multi-fidelity advantage diminishes with longer training. Against random search (Bergstra & Bengio, JMLR 2012), AdaSAC dominates throughout MuJoCo training, while AdaDQN matches random search at 40M frames on Atari and overtakes it before 100M frames. Against Meta-gradient RL (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2018), AdaDQN and Meta-gradient DQN achieve similar performance on 2 Atari games for discount factor selection, though AdaQN is not limited to continuous differentiable hyperparameters. In all finite-space comparisons (grid search, individual runs), AdaQN matches or exceeds the best static hyperparameter configuration while being more robust to stochasticity, as evidenced by its worst-seed performance being on par with random search in MuJoCo.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaQN extends the AutoRL landscape by departing from the dominant paradigm of multi-trial hyperparameter search (grid/random search, Bayesian optimization, evolutionary methods like SEARL) toward a single-trial ensemble approach where multiple Q-networks with different hyperparameters are trained simultaneously. It builds directly on DQN and SAC as base algorithms, and borrows the exploitation/exploration pipeline from SEARL for infinite hyperparameter spaces, but replaces environment-based fitness evaluation with approximation-error-based target selection. Conceptually, it also contrasts with Meta-gradient RL, which tunes the target to ease optimization, whereas AdaQN uses diverse online networks to overcome optimization hurdles, enabling discrete hyperparameters and avoiding non-stationarity issues from hyperparameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaQN addresses a practical bottleneck in RL deployment—hyperparameter sensitivity—without the sample cost of multi-trial AutoRL methods, and provides theoretical guarantees (convergence in tabular settings, unbiasedness of the selection criterion) that prior AutoRL methods lack. The finding that dynamic target selection can exceed the best static hyperparameter configuration (not merely match it) suggests that non-stationarity in RL optimization is exploitable rather than merely a nuisance. Limitations include increased memory/compute for the ensemble, restriction to agent-side hyperparameters (not environment properties like reward shaping), and the need for a reasonable initial set of hyperparameter candidates; future work on mixture-of-Q-functions and parallelized training could further reduce the practical overhead.
