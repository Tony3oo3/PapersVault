## Problem

Continual learning for vision-language models like CLIP remains underexplored: existing methods either focus on unimodal classification, ignore the forgetting of CLIP's original zero-shot generalization, or enforce a trade-off where reducing forgetting necessarily sacrifices new-task performance. No prior work simultaneously evaluates image-text retrieval on downstream domains, zero-shot retrieval on unseen data, and zero-shot classification degradation while adapting CLIP across diverse image-caption domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251663/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes 224×224 RGB images paired with free-form text captions from eight domain-specific datasets (e.g., Flickr30K, COCO, AI-generated Lexica, art WikiArt, sketches), processed sequentially as one task per dataset. At inference, it produces bidirectional image-text retrieval rankings (Recall@1 for I2T and T2I) on all trained datasets plus an unseen HAVG dataset, and zero-shot class labels on six classification benchmarks (ImageNet, CIFAR-100, StanfordCars, Flowers, DTD, Food101) using text prompts as class descriptions. No task identity is required at test time.

## Method

C-CLIP combines two mechanisms applied to CLIP's dual ViT encoders. First, LoRA (rank 16) is attached to both vision and text encoders while freezing all prior parameters; at the end of each continual stage the LoRA weights are merged into the backbone with a scaling factor α=0.5, which the paper proves is equivalent to constraining the feature-space drift within a Lipschitz bound. Second, Contrastive Knowledge Consolidation (CKC) introduces a shared projector after both encoders and constructs a contrastive loss where the old model's projected features for the current sample act as the positive and all other old features act as negatives, effectively doubling the number of positive-negative pairs. This CKC loss is added to the standard CLIP InfoNCE loss, and unlike regularization-based methods (EWC, LWF), CKC's optimization direction aligns with CLIP's own contrastive objective rather than conflicting with it, enabling the model to learn a better feature space for new tasks while still anchoring to old knowledge.

## Results

On the VLCL benchmark of 8 image-caption datasets, C-CLIP achieves an average I2T R@1 of 40.83 and T2I R@1 of 37.97, outperforming the best baseline (ZSCL) by +9.58 and +7.14 points respectively. On zero-shot classification, C-CLIP maintains ImageNet-1K accuracy of 60.31% after 8 continual tasks (performance degradation of only 7.42 points from the original 67.73%), compared to 49.60% for ZSCL (PD 18.13) and 40.76% for EWC (PD 26.97). Notably, C-CLIP even surpasses full fine-tuning on Flickr30K (84.40 vs. 74.20 I2T R@1) and COCO (56.92 vs. 43.26 I2T R@1) while preserving general zero-shot ability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-CLIP decisively outperforms all six fine-tuning-based baselines (EWC, ZSCL, Mod-X, MOE-CL, DKR, and full fine-tuning) on both I2T and T2I retrieval across all 8 datasets, with the largest gaps on domain-specific datasets like Lexica (+17.66 I2T over ZSCL) and Kream (+2.84 I2T over ZSCL). On zero-shot classification, C-CLIP reduces performance degradation to 7.42 points on ImageNet-1K, roughly half that of the best prior method ZSCL (18.13) and a third of EWC (26.97). Against prompt-based methods (L2P, CPE-CLIP), C-CLIP achieves substantially higher downstream retrieval (e.g., 84.40 vs. 47.74 I2T R@1 on Flickr30K for L2P) while also maintaining better zero-shot accuracy (60.31% vs. 56.04% for L2P after 8 tasks). The key distinction is that C-CLIP breaks the traditional stability-plasticity trade-off: it matches or exceeds full fine-tuning on new tasks (e.g., 84.40 vs. 74.20 I2T on Flickr30K) while preserving general representations, whereas all regularization-based baselines sacrifice new-task performance to reduce forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's dual-encoder contrastive architecture and LoRA's parameter-efficient fine-tuning, but departs from prior continual learning work in two key ways. First, it extends the CL problem from unimodal image classification (CIL, MTIL) to multimodal image-text retrieval, arguing that the forgetting and adaptability challenges differ fundamentally when both vision and text encoders must be updated. Second, it challenges the standard regularization-based approach (EWC, LWF, ZSCL, Mod-X) by showing theoretically and empirically that LoRA's parameter reduction is a simpler equivalent for forgetting prevention, then introduces Contrastive Knowledge Consolidation (CKC) to break the stability-plasticity trade-off that plagues all prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important problem—continually adapting CLIP to new domains without destroying its zero-shot generalization—and provides a new three-track benchmark (downstream retrieval, unseen-domain retrieval, zero-shot classification) that prior work lacked. The finding that CKC can simultaneously reduce forgetting and exceed full fine-tuning on downstream tasks is a meaningful step toward resolving the long-standing stability-plasticity dilemma in continual learning. Limitations include the method's design for contrastive VLMs (not generative models like LLaVA), a benchmark limited to 8 datasets, and the need for per-dataset learning rate tuning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to autoregressive multimodal models and whether it scales to dozens of sequential tasks without cumulative drift.
