## Problem

Off-policy TD methods like Q-learning are notoriously unstable when combined with nonlinear function approximation, requiring target networks and large replay buffers to stabilise training. These components introduce memory overhead, implementation complexity, and delayed parameter updates that harm sample efficiency. The paper asks whether regularisation techniques such as LayerNorm and L2 can provably stabilise TD without either of these mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252820/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw environment observations (RGB frames for Atari, ~8000-dimensional float vectors for Craftax, or task-specific vectors for Smax/Overcooked/Hanabi) and outputs Q-values for all state-action pairs, from which an ε-greedy action is selected. A key operational detail is that PQN processes I parallel environments simultaneously at each timestep, computing λ-returns over short trajectories of length T from the current batch of interactions rather than sampling from a historical replay buffer. This means the input distribution at any step reflects only the current policy's stationary distribution, not an average over all past policies.

## Method

PQN is a simplified deep Q-learning algorithm that replaces DQN's target network and large replay buffer with two stabilising mechanisms: (1) LayerNorm in the Q-network, which provably bounds the nonlinear instability term to O(1/√k) and the off-policy term to a residual independent of the distributional shift between sampling and target policies, and (2) L2 regularisation on the final layer weights, which eliminates the remaining off-policy instability. The theoretical contribution is a general Jacobian analysis decomposing TD stability into off-policy and nonlinear components, showing that sufficiently wide LayerNorm networks with L2 regularisation satisfy the negative-definiteness criterion for convergence. Practically, PQN interacts with I vectorised environments in parallel, computes λ-returns over trajectories of length T, and performs synchronous gradient updates on minibatches drawn from the current batch of interactions—enabling pure-GPU training with minimal memory. The special case λ=0, T=1 reduces to vectorised original Q-learning with regularisation, while the general case supports RNNs and multi-agent extensions via VDN without the hidden-state complications of replay-based methods.

## Results

On Atari-10, PQN outperforms PPO in both sample efficiency and training time (1 hour vs. 2.5 hours), and surpasses Double-DQN and Prioritised DDQN in the same number of frames while using 16× fewer gradient updates (780k vs. 12.5M). On the full Atari-57 suite, PQN achieves the median score of Rainbow with only 400M+100M frames of training, completing the full suite in the time DQN takes for a single game (~50× speedup). In Craftax, PQN-RNN reaches 16% score vs. PPO-RNN's 15.3% at 1B steps. In Smax, PQN-VDN outperforms both MAPPO and QMix, learning coordination in ~10 minutes versus QMix's ~1 hour.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PQN outperforms PPO on Atari-10 in sample efficiency, final score, and wall-clock time (1 hr vs. 2.5 hr), and matches or slightly exceeds PPO-RNN on Craftax (16% vs. 15.3% at 1B steps). Against Rainbow on Atari-57, PQN achieves the same median score but is ~50× faster in wall-clock time, though Rainbow remains slightly more sample-efficient per frame. In multi-agent Smax, PQN-VDN outperforms both QMix (learning in ~10 min vs. ~1 hr) and MAPPO on the 9 most popular maps. PQN-VDN also outperforms MAPPO in Overcooked and is significantly more sample-efficient than MAPPO in Hanabi (reaching 24 average points). Compared to replay-buffer-based VDN, PQN achieves higher returns in Overcooked while using far less memory. Against Double-DQN and Prioritised DDQN on Atari-10, PQN surpasses both in the same number of frames despite performing 16× fewer gradient updates (780k vs. 12.5M), and the 4× speedup over R2D2 in Hanabi further confirms the advantage of eliminating large replay buffers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TD stability analysis framework of Bhandari et al. (2018) and Fellows et al. (2023) from linear to nonlinear function approximation, providing the first rigorous proof that LayerNorm combined with ℓ² regularization yields provably convergent off-policy TD without target networks or replay buffers. It builds directly on the original Q-learning algorithm (Watkins, 1989) and the λ-returns formulation of Daley & Amato (2019), while departing from the DQN paradigm (Mnih et al., 2013, 2015) by eliminating both the replay buffer and target network. The empirical motivation connects to CrossQ (Bhatt et al., 2024), which showed BatchNorm can stabilize TD without target networks but provided no theoretical analysis, and to the vectorized-environment frameworks (IsaacGym, Jumanji, Craftax, JaxMARL) that previously lacked an off-policy Q-learning baseline. For multi-agent settings, PQN adopts the VDN decomposition (Sunehag et al., 2017) as its coordination mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PQN reestablishes off-policy Q-learning as a practical alternative to PPO in an era where PPO has become the default, offering provable convergence guarantees that PPO lacks while matching or exceeding its performance across 79+ tasks. The elimination of the replay buffer enables pure-GPU end-to-end training for off-policy methods—a previously infeasible setup due to memory constraints—opening the door to massively parallel simulation environments. Key limitations include the reliance on ε-greedy exploration (insufficient for hard-exploration Atari games), the theoretical guarantee requiring wide networks (k → ∞), and the need for careful ℓ² regularization tuning. Open questions include whether richer exploration strategies (curiosity, bootstrapped DQN) can close the remaining gap on hard-exploration tasks, and whether the stability theory extends to deeper architectures or continuous action spaces.
