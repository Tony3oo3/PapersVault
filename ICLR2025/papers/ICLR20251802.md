## Problem

Current AI methods for crystal structure generation produce materials in a single static step, lacking the iterative refinement and multi-step reasoning that human materials experts employ. These models also struggle to cohesively integrate textual property descriptions with structural information, limiting their ability to generate materials that satisfy specific target properties. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a decomposed, expert-mimicking workflow that separates retrieval, modification planning, and structural generation into distinct stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251802/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language description of desired material properties (e.g., composition, space group number, band gap, formation energy, energy above hull) as input and produces a CIF file of the predicted crystal structure as output. The generation proceeds through an intermediate ALX representation—lattice vector lengths/angles followed by element types and fractional coordinates—which is automatically converted to CIF via pymatgen. For unconditional generation, a reference material is randomly sampled from the database; for conditional generation, the user-specified property constraints guide the retrieval and transition stages.

## Method

MatExpert decomposes material generation into three stages mimicking human expert workflow. In the retrieval stage, two T5-based encoders are trained with a contrastive NT-Xent loss to embed property descriptions and RoboCrystallographer structural descriptions in a shared space, enabling nearest-neighbor retrieval of a reference material. In the transition stage, GPT-4 generates ground-truth chain-of-thought modification pathways during training, and a smaller LLM (LLaMA-2/3) is fine-tuned via LoRA to produce these pathways at inference, describing how to alter the reference material to meet target properties. In the generation stage, the same fine-tuned LLM extends the conversation to output the ALX representation (lattice parameters and atomic coordinates), which is parsed and converted to CIF. The key architectural insight is that grounding generation in a retrieved, physically valid reference material and an explicit modification plan substantially reduces out-of-distribution hallucinations compared to single-step generation.

## Results

On unconditional generation (MP-20), MatExpert with LLaMA-3 70B (τ=0.7) achieves the best overall performance: 0.998 structural validity, 0.961 compositional validity, 0.18 Wasserstein distance on density, 0.04 on number of elements, 51.0% metastable (M3GNet), and 12.0% DFT-stable — surpassing Crystal-LLM 70B (τ=0.7) which scores 0.996/0.954/0.81/0.44/49.8%/10.6% respectively. On conditional generation (NOMAD, 2.9M materials), MatExpert with LLaMA-3 70B (τ=0.7) achieves 0.999 structural validity, 0.986 compositional validity, and Wasserstein distances of 0.14 (density) and 0.09 (N_el), compared to Crystal-LLM 70B (τ=0.7) at 0.997/0.971/0.77/0.42. MatExpert's distribution metrics are roughly 4–5× better than Crystal-LLM, reflecting the benefit of grounding generation in a retrieved reference material.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatExpert consistently outperforms all three baseline families across the majority of metrics. Against CDVAE, MatExpert LLaMA-3 70B (τ=0.7) achieves substantially higher compositional validity (0.961 vs. 0.867), lower Wasserstein distances (0.18 vs. 0.68 on density; 0.04 vs. 1.43 on N_el), and more than doubles the DFT-stable rate (12.0% vs. 5.4%), though CDVAE retains a perfect 1.00 structural validity score. Against LM-AC, MatExpert wins on structural validity (0.998 vs. 0.958), compositional validity (0.961 vs. 0.889), and both distribution metrics, while LM-AC has a slight edge on recall (0.996 vs. 0.986). Against the closest competitor Crystal-LLM (Gruver et al., 2024), MatExpert wins on every metric except recall at τ=0.7 (0.986 vs. 0.858 for Crystal-LLM, where MatExpert actually wins); the most striking gap is in distribution fidelity — MatExpert's wdist(ρ) of 0.18 is 4.5× lower than Crystal-LLM's 0.81, and wdist(N_el) of 0.04 is 11× lower than 0.44 — attributed to the retrieval-and-transition pipeline anchoring generation to a real reference structure. On the conditional generation task (NOMAD), MatExpert LLaMA-3 70B (τ=0.7) achieves 0.999 structural validity and 0.986 compositional validity versus Crystal-LLM's 0.997 and 0.971, with Wasserstein distances roughly 5× lower (0.14 vs. 0.77 on density), confirming the advantage generalizes to property-constrained generation at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatExpert departs from the single-step generation paradigm of prior crystal structure models (CDVAE, Crystal-LLM, CrystaLLM, LM-AC/LM-CH) by decomposing material design into a three-stage pipeline—retrieval, transition, and generation—that explicitly mimics how human experts iteratively refine materials. Conceptually, it repurposes contrastive learning (T5 encoders trained with NT-Xent loss) to bridge textual property descriptions and structural descriptions, and leverages GPT-4 to synthesize ground-truth chain-of-thought transition pathways that are then distilled into a smaller LoRA-fine-tuned LLaMA model. The ALX representation (lattice vectors + atomic coordinates in natural language) as an intermediate output before CIF conversion is a practical choice that keeps the generation task within the LLM's text-generation comfort zone, building on the text-based generation philosophy of Crystal-LLM while adding the retrieval-and-modify structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-stage decomposition offers interpretability and controllability that single-step generative models lack, making it a more practical tool for materials scientists who need to reason about *why* a particular modification was made. The curated NOMAD dataset of ~2.9M VASP-computed structures is a substantial open resource for the community. Key limitations include the dependence on LLM text generation (susceptible to hallucination in coordinate values), the computational cost of the 70B model (~5 hours per 10K samples), and the fact that retrieval inherently anchors generation near existing materials, potentially limiting exploration of truly novel chemical space. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to multi-property simultaneous constraints, whether the contrastive retrieval stage can be replaced by more scalable indexing for databases exceeding 10M entries, and whether the ALX-to-CIF conversion introduces systematic structural biases.
