## Problem

Theoretical analyses of gradient descent for two-layer ReLU networks in the teacher-student setting were previously limited to specific cases (m=k=1, m≥k=1, or m=k=2) or required strong recovery assumptions that preclude understanding the full training trajectory. The general case with m,k=O(1) student and teacher neurons remained unresolved, leaving open how GD recovers teacher neurons and balances features beyond local analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250338/cropped_page9_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. Gaussian input vectors x ~ N(0, I_d) and a target function f* that is a sum of k orthogonal ReLU neurons, and trains a two-layer ReLU network with m student neurons (m ≥ k) via gradient descent on the population squared loss. The output is a trained student network whose weights converge to a minimum 'balanced' ℓ2-norm solution with an explicit O(T^{-3}) excess-risk convergence rate. The analysis operates in expectation (population loss rather than finite-sample), requires weak recovery at initialization, orthogonal teacher neurons of equal norm, and a balance condition on the number of student neurons assigned to each teacher.

## Method

The paper develops a three-phase dynamical analysis of gradient descent: (1) alignment, where each student neuron aligns with its nearest teacher neuron by reducing the angular difference to O(ε₁), handled via sine/cosine approximations to decouple cross-teacher interactions that break monotonicity in the multi-teacher case; (2) tangential growth, where student neurons grow along their aligned teacher directions, analyzed through a novel dynamical system formulation that expresses the tangential residual as a matrix iteration and bounds its evolution via eigenvalue estimation of the transition matrix; and (3) local convergence, where the loss decreases at O(T^{-3}). The key technical contribution is resolving the coupling between multiple teacher and student neurons, which invalidates the recursive relationships available in the single-teacher setting of Xu and Du (2023). The results reveal an implicit bias of GD toward a minimum balanced ℓ2-norm solution where student neurons are evenly distributed among teacher directions, with the balance determined by the nearest-teacher assignment at initialization.

## Results

The paper proves global convergence of gradient descent for learning k ReLU teacher neurons with m student neurons (m,k=O(1)) at a rate of O(T^{-3}) for the excess risk, specifically L(W(T*+T)) ≤ O(k^{12}‖v‖²/(η³T³)). The analysis is structured in three phases: alignment (duration Θ(ε₁²/η)), tangential growth (duration Θ((1/η)ln(1/ε₂))), and local convergence (O(T^{-3}) rate). Additionally, the paper shows that GD implicitly balances student neurons, with each neuron's norm converging to Θ(k‖v‖/m) and the solution achieving a minimum 'balanced' ℓ₂-norm. Numerical experiments (d=100, ‖v‖=5, 5000 batches) confirm the T^{-3} convergence rate and the three-phase 'align then fit' dynamics across multiple (k,m) configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct empirical baseline comparisons; instead, it extends and improves upon prior theoretical results. Compared to Xu and Du (2023), which handles only the single-teacher case (m≥k=1), this paper generalizes to multiple orthogonal teachers (m,k=O(1)) and achieves a faster constant factor in Phase 1 while relaxing the learning rate condition by a factor of m in Phase 2. Compared to Zhou et al. (2021), which provides only local convergence at rate O(1/T) under strong recovery assumptions, this paper achieves global convergence at the superior O(T^{-3}) rate starting from the weaker 'weak recovery' condition. Compared to Yehudai and Ohad (2020) (m=k=1) and Wu et al. (2018) (m=k=2), this is the first polynomial-time GD convergence result beyond local analysis for general m,k=O(1). The authors note that when k=1, their results subsume Xu and Du (2023) with only minor modifications and a better constant factor in Phase 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'align then fit' three-phase analysis framework (Maennel et al., 2018; Boursier and Flammarion, 2024) from the single-teacher setting (Xu and Du, 2023; Yehudai and Ohad, 2020) to the general multi-teacher case with m,k=O(1). The key technical departure is handling cross-terms between multiple orthogonal teacher neurons: in Phase 1, sine/cosine approximations decouple angular dynamics that are no longer monotonic for k>1, and in Phase 2, a new matrix-form dynamical system with eigenvalue analysis replaces the classical recursive relationships that fail under multi-teacher coupling. The local convergence phase builds directly on Zhou et al. (2021) and Safran et al. (2021) for the O(T^{-3}) rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first polynomial-time global convergence guarantee for GD training of two-layer ReLU networks beyond local analysis in the general m,k=O(1) regime, revealing an implicit bias toward a minimum balanced ℓ₂-norm solution. The weak recovery assumption (each student neuron is nearly orthogonal to all but one teacher) remains a significant limitation, and extending to non-orthogonal teacher neurons or removing this assumption is explicitly left open. The balanced-norm result has practical implications for understanding why over-parameterized networks distribute capacity evenly across learned features, but the O(1) parameter regime and strict orthogonality assumptions limit immediate applicability to deeper or more complex architectures.
