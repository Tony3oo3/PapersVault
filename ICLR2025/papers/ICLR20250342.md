## Problem

Existing tree-Wasserstein distance (TWD) methods use trees solely to accelerate Wasserstein computation over Euclidean ground metrics, while hyperbolic representation learning methods assume a known sample-level hierarchy. Neither addresses the problem of recovering a latent hierarchical structure among features from observational data and using it to define a meaningful sample-to-sample distance that reflects feature relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250342/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{n×m} with positive entries (normalized into histograms on the probability simplex) and a maximal scale K_c. It produces a decoded rooted binary tree B with m leaf nodes representing the latent feature hierarchy, and an n×n TWD matrix W. The initial feature distance uses cosine similarity in the ambient space, and the full pipeline runs in O(m^1.2) time via landmark diffusion, enabling applications with thousands of features.

## Method

The method embeds features into a multi-scale Poincaré half-space using diffusion geometry: at each dyadic scale k, the k-th diffusion density of each feature is mapped to a point in H^{m+1} with height 2^{k/2-2}, so that geodesic distances grow exponentially with scale, mirroring tree structure. A novel Hyperbolic Diffusion LCA (HD-LCA) is then defined as the Fréchet mean of the orthogonal projections of pairwise hyperbolic LCAs across all scales, yielding a closed-form scalar a_{j,j'} that encodes hierarchical proximity. A binary tree is decoded bottom-up by iteratively merging the feature pair with the smallest a_{j,j'}, assigning edge weights from geodesic distances. The final TWD is computed on this decoded tree, and Theorem 2 proves it is bilipschitz equivalent to the TWD on the true (unobserved) latent feature tree, establishing the method as an unsupervised Wasserstein metric learning procedure.

## Results

The proposed TWD achieves the highest classification accuracy on 6 out of 7 datasets: 99.8% on synthetic data, 96.1% on BBCSPORT, 73.4% on TWITTER, 96.9% on CLASSIC, 93.1% on AMAZON, 89.1% on Zeisel, and 84.3% on CBMC. The only exception is CLASSIC, where pre-trained WMD (97.2%) slightly edges out the proposed method (96.9%). On the scRNA-seq datasets, the proposed TWD outperforms the next-best method (TSWD-10) by 5.9 points on Zeisel (89.1% vs. 83.2%) and 2.9 points on CBMC (84.3% vs. 81.4% GMD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed TWD outperforms all 12 competing baselines on 6 of 7 datasets, with the sole exception being CLASSIC where pre-trained WMD (97.2%) narrowly beats the proposed method (96.9%). Among existing TWD variants, Quadtree (Indyk & Thaper, 2003) and Flowtree (Backurs et al., 2020) are the strongest random-tree baselines but trail by 0.6–1.6 points on word-document data and by 5–9 points on scRNA-seq. The tree-sliced Wasserstein distance (TSWD, Le et al., 2019) with 10 sampled trees is the best TWD baseline on Zeisel (83.2%) yet still lags the proposed method by 5.9 points. Weighted TWD variants (WCTWD, WQTWD, SWCTWD, SWQTWD from Yamada et al., 2022) and UltraTree (Chen et al., 2024) consistently underperform, with gaps of 3–10 points. Tree-construction baselines using MST, Tree Representation (Sonthalia & Gilbert, 2020), hyperbolic hierarchical clustering (HHC, Chami et al., 2020), gradient-based HC (gHHC, Monath et al., 2019), and UltraFit (Chierchia & Perret, 2019) all fall 2–12 points below the proposed method, confirming that the HD-LCA-based decoding is critical. On scRNA-seq, the proposed TWD surpasses the application-specific Gene Mover's Distance (GMD, Bellazzi et al., 2021) by 4.9 points on Zeisel and 2.9 points on CBMC, demonstrating that geometrically intrinsic tree recovery outperforms pre-trained gene proximity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tree-Wasserstein distance (TWD) framework of Indyk & Thaper (2003) by repurposing the tree from a purely computational device (approximating Euclidean ground metrics) into a learned representation of a latent feature hierarchy. The hyperbolic diffusion embedding step directly builds on Lin et al. (2023, ICML), which showed that multi-scale diffusion densities embedded in Poincaré half-spaces recover hierarchical metrics, but that work considered latent hierarchies of *samples*; here the authors explicitly decode a feature tree via a novel Hyperbolic Diffusion LCA (HD-LCA) defined through Fréchet means in a product manifold of hyperbolic spaces. The approach also departs from hyperbolic representation learning (Nickel & Kiela, Chamberlain et al.), which typically assumes a known sample graph, and from prior tree-decoding methods (Chami et al., 2020) that minimize Dasgupta's cost in a 2D Poincaré disk without recovering the feature-level hierarchy needed for TWD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first TWD variant with a provable bilipschitz guarantee (Theorem 2) that recovers the Wasserstein distance under the true latent feature tree without requiring access to that tree, positioning it as an unsupervised Wasserstein metric learning method. Practically, the O(m^1.2) computation and differentiability make it attractive for large-scale text and genomics pipelines where pre-trained embedding-based distances (WMD, GMD) are expensive or domain-specific. Key limitations include the restriction to probability-simplex samples (non-negative data), the need for eigendecomposition of the feature affinity matrix, and the assumption that local Gaussian affinities in ambient space faithfully encode the hidden hierarchy. Open questions include extension to signed or unnormalized data, whether the dyadic scale grid can be replaced by adaptive scales, and how the method behaves when the latent structure is not strictly tree-like (e.g., DAGs or networks).
