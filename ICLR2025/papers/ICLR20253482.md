## Problem

Existing autoencoders for latent diffusion models achieve good reconstruction at moderate spatial compression ratios (e.g., 8×) but suffer severe accuracy degradation at high ratios (e.g., 64× or 128×), limiting the efficiency gains available for high-resolution diffusion transformers whose cost scales quadratically with token count. The paper identifies two root causes: high spatial-compression autoencoders are harder to optimize, and they suffer a generalization penalty when applied at resolutions higher than their training resolution.

## Input / Output

The autoencoder accepts RGB images at resolutions from 256×256 up to 2048×2048 and produces compressed latent tensors with spatial compression ratios of 32×, 64×, or 128× (e.g., a 512×512 image becomes an 8×8×128 latent at f64). When plugged into a diffusion transformer, the autoencoder handles all spatial compression so the transformer operates with patch size 1, receiving only class labels or text prompts as conditioning. The system outputs reconstructed images (for autoencoder evaluation) or generated images (for diffusion model evaluation) at the target resolution.

## Method

DC-AE introduces two key techniques. First, Residual Autoencoding adds non-parametric space-to-channel shortcuts to every downsample and upsample block, so the learnable modules predict residuals relative to the space-to-channel mapping rather than learning the full transformation from scratch—this alleviates the optimization difficulty that causes high-compression autoencoders to underperform. Second, Decoupled High-Resolution Adaptation replaces single-phase training with a three-phase pipeline: (1) low-resolution full training with L1+LPIPS loss, (2) high-resolution latent adaptation that tunes only the middle layers (encoder head and decoder input) to fix the generalization penalty, and (3) low-resolution local refinement that tunes only the decoder's head layers with PatchGAN loss to sharpen details without destabilizing the latent space. The architecture also substitutes EfficientViT blocks for standard transformer blocks to better handle high-resolution feature maps.

## Results

On ImageNet 512×512 with UViT-H, DC-AE-f64p1 achieves 3.01 FID (w/ CFG) with 111.77 img/s inference throughput and 984 img/s training throughput on H100, compared to SD-VAE-f8p2's 3.55 FID with 5.85 img/s inference and 55 img/s training — a 19.1× inference and 17.9× training speedup while improving FID by 0.54. DC-AE-f32p1 consistently outperforms SD-VAE-f8p2 across all tested diffusion transformers (DiT-XL, UViT-H, UViT-2B), e.g., 2.84 vs 3.04 FID on DiT-XL and 2.53 vs 3.55 on UViT-H. The strongest configuration, DC-AE-f32 + USiT-2B, reaches 1.72 FID on ImageNet 512×512, surpassing SOTA diffusion (EDM2-XXL, 1.81) and autoregressive (MAGVIT-v2, 1.91; MAR-L, 1.73) models. For reconstruction, DC-AE reduces rFID from 16.84 to 0.22 (f64c128) and from 100.74 to 0.23 (f128c512) on ImageNet 512×512.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-AE-f32p1 outperforms SD-VAE-f8p2 (Rombach et al.) on FID across all diffusion transformers: 2.84 vs 3.04 on DiT-XL, 2.53 vs 3.55 on UViT-H, and 2.30 vs 3.57 on UViT-2B, while delivering 4.5–17.9× higher training throughput and 4.8–19.1× higher inference throughput. Against Asym-VAE-f8 (Zhu et al.) and Flux-VAE-f8 (Black Forest Labs), DC-AE-f32p1 achieves lower FID (e.g., 2.53 vs 3.51 and 12.63 on UViT-H) with substantially higher throughput. SD3-VAE-f8 (Esser et al.) and SDXL-VAE-f8 (Podell et al.) show similar FID to SD-VAE-f8 on UViT-S (24.57–26.38 w/ CFG) but DC-AE-f32p1 achieves 18.08, a clear win. At the SOTA level, DC-AE-f32 + USiT-2B reaches 1.72 FID, beating EDM2-XXL (Karras et al., 1.81), MAGVIT-v2 (Yu et al., 1.91), and MAR-L (Li et al., 1.73). SiT-XL (Ma et al.) with DC-AE-f32 achieves 2.41 FID, and the USiT variants further close the gap to 1.89 (USiT-H) and 1.72 (USiT-2B), establishing new SOTA among efficient diffusion-based generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SD-VAE architecture (Rombach et al., 2022) by pushing the spatial compression ratio from the standard 8× to 32×, 64×, and 128×, a direction the authors explicitly identify as underexplored. Prior autoencoder improvements for diffusion models focused on increasing latent channels (Esser et al., 2024; Dai et al., 2023) or heavier decoders (Zhu et al., 2023), while the spatial compression ratio remained fixed at 8×; the token-reduction burden was instead handled by the diffusion model's patch embedding layer. DC-AE departs from this by absorbing the full spatial compression into the autoencoder itself, using residual autoencoding with space-to-channel shortcuts (analogous to ResNet but with non-identity mappings) and a decoupled three-phase training strategy that separates GAN-based local refinement from full-model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 19.1× inference and 17.9× training speedup on H100 with *improved* FID (3.01 vs 3.55) represents a major practical efficiency win that could reshape how latent diffusion models are deployed, particularly for high-resolution and video generation where token count dominates cost. The conceptual finding that shifting spatial compression from the transformer's patch embedding to the autoencoder consistently yields better FID suggests a broader principle about where in the pipeline compression should occur. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video autoencoders, whether the 128× ratio is a practical ceiling, and whether the larger latent channel count (128 vs 4) imposes a capacity requirement that limits applicability to smaller diffusion models (as seen with UViT-S).
