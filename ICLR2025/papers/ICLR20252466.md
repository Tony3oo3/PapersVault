## Problem

Mamba and other SSM-based language models, despite offering linear-time inference and constant memory, significantly underperform Transformers on long-context tasks when input sequences exceed the training length. The paper identifies the root cause: global hidden-state channels in Mamba suffer from exponential cumulative decay that prevents their receptive fields from extending beyond the training sequence, making them unable to capture long-range dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252466/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at inference time on a frozen pre-trained Mamba checkpoint (Mamba-1.4B, Mamba2-1.3B, or Zamba2-1.2B) without any weight modification. It requires a precomputed per-channel lookup table g(S) that maps sequence length to a filtering threshold, calibrated offline from 5 sampled Pile sequences. The output is identical in format to the vanilla model (next-token logits or task answers), but the SSM recurrence is selectively modified only for identified global channels, leaving local channels untouched.

## Method

LongMamba is a training-free, two-step pipeline. Step 1 (channel classification) computes the cumulative decay Π_{k=1}^{L} Ā_k for each hidden-state channel over the training length L and labels channels as 'global' if this product exceeds a threshold θ (e.g., 5×10⁻² for Mamba2-1.3B). Step 2 (receptive field enlargement) applies token filtering only to global channels: at each timestep t, if the per-channel positive factor Δ_t falls below a length-dependent threshold g(S), the decay and update are replaced with (Ā'_t, B̄'_t) = (1, 0), so the hidden state is neither decayed nor updated at that token. This prevents unimportant tokens from accumulating in the hidden state memory, effectively aligning the cumulative decay at the longer test length S with the learned decay at training length L. The threshold g(S) is a per-channel lookup table precomputed offline by calibrating the Δ_t distribution from training data, requiring no gradient updates or architectural changes.

## Results

On LongBench-E, LongMamba improves average task accuracy by 8.76% (Mamba-1.4B: 8.37%→17.33%), 9.13% (Mamba2-1.3B: 8.21%→17.34%), and 6.39% (Zamba2-1.2B: 11.43%→17.82%) over vanilla models, and outperforms the previous SOTA DeciMamba (13.38%) by 3.95% on Mamba-1.4B. On RULER, LongMamba applied to Zamba2-1.2B achieves 31.58% average accuracy at 16k tokens vs. 7.00% for vanilla (4.5× improvement), and 13.83% at 32k vs. 2.72% (5.1×). On PG-19 perplexity, LongMamba keeps perplexity below 20 at 60k tokens, while vanilla Mamba-1.4B exceeds 40 and DeciMamba exceeds 30. On the full LongBench benchmark, LongMamba-enhanced Falcon-Mamba-7B reaches 26.2% average accuracy, surpassing Llama2-7B-chat-4k (26.1%) and narrowing the gap with Vicuna-v1.5-7B-16k (30.1%) from 6.7% to 3.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongMamba consistently outperforms all vanilla SSM baselines (Mamba-1.4B, Mamba2-1.3B, Zamba2-1.2B) across every evaluated benchmark, with the largest gains on retrieval-heavy tasks (e.g., TR: 15.67%→37.00% on Mamba-1.4B; TQA: 10.08%→48.74% on Mamba2-1.3B). Against the previous SOTA DeciMamba, LongMamba achieves 17.33% vs. 13.38% average accuracy on LongBench-E (Mamba-1.4B), a 29.5% relative improvement, and maintains lower PG-19 perplexity at all sequence lengths beyond 10k tokens. On RULER, LongMamba (Zamba2-1.2B) achieves 31.58% average at 16k tokens vs. 7.00% for vanilla, with the most dramatic gain on the S2 passkey-retrieval task (0%→73% at 32k). In the 7B-scale LongBench comparison, LongMamba-enhanced Falcon-Mamba-7B (26.2%) surpasses Llama2-7B-chat-4k (26.1%) and XGen-7B-8k (24.6%), though it still trails Vicuna-v1.5-7B-16k (30.1%) by 3.9%. The method introduces minimal latency overhead (≤4.5% average prefilling increase), preserving Mamba's efficiency advantage over Transformers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mamba/Mamba-2 selective state space model family and the per-channel attention score analysis introduced by Ali et al. (2024), which first quantified how individual hidden state channels attend to input tokens. It departs from the prior SOTA for Mamba long-context extension, DeciMamba (Ben-Kish et al., 2024), which applies uniform token pruning across all channels, by instead identifying a structural split between local and global channels and intervening only on the latter. The method also positions itself against Transformer-based long-context techniques (positional interpolation, attention sinks, external memory) that are architecturally inapplicable to SSMs, filling a gap specific to recurrent state-space architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that Mamba's hidden state channels naturally partition into local and global receptive-field groups, and that exponential decay in global channels is the sole bottleneck for length extrapolation, provides a mechanistic explanation that could guide future SSM architecture design. The training-free nature and minimal latency overhead (~4.5%) make the technique immediately deployable, but the absolute performance gap with Transformers persists (e.g., MK2/MK3 tasks remain unsolved, and LongBench-E accuracy plateaus around 17–18%). Open questions include whether the channel classification is stable across training regimes and model scales, and whether the token-filtering heuristic generalizes to other SSM variants beyond the Mamba family.
