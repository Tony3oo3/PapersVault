## Problem

Computing ψ-class intersection numbers on the moduli space of curves via traditional recursive methods (KdV hierarchy, topological recursion) scales as O(g!·2ⁿ), rendering them infeasible beyond low genera. No prior work has applied Transformer-based models to such high-variance, factorial-growth recursive functions, nor has anyone investigated whether a neural network can implicitly recover the underlying algebraic structure (Virasoro constraints, large-genus asymptotic parameters) from purely data-driven learning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes two modalities: the sparse quantum Airy structure tensor B (a rank-3 tensor with ~1500 non-zero entries, represented as a sequence of COO 4-tuples (i,j,k,B_{i,j}^k)) and the partition d = (d₁,…,dₙ) of d_{g,n} = 3g−3+n as a permutation-invariant set, along with scalar genus g and marked-point count n. The C tensor is deliberately excluded since B contributes linearly while C contributes quadratically in the recursion. The output is the ψ-class intersection number predicted in logarithmic scale (values span 10⁻⁴⁵ to 10⁴⁵), accompanied by a conformal prediction interval with 90% target coverage computed via a dynamic sliding window over equivalent marked-point partitions.

## Method

DynamicFormer is a multi-modal Transformer with two parallel branches: a continuous branch processing the B tensor as an ordered sequence with Dynamic Positional Bias, and a discrete branch processing the partition d as a permutation-invariant set. The key architectural innovation is the Dynamic Range Activator (DRA), a learnable non-linearity x + a·sin²(x/b) + c·cos(bx) + d·tanh(bx) that simultaneously captures periodic structure and rapid factorial growth/drop, outperforming ReLU, GLU, Snake, and KAN on recursive extrapolation. Cross-modal information is aggregated via Principal Neighbourhood Aggregation, and [DYN] register tokens with a Barlow Twins-inspired Talking Modalities loss enforce correspondence between the B and d representations. The model is trained with MAE loss plus the self-supervised Talking Modalities objective, and uncertainty is quantified post-hoc via Inductive Conformal Prediction with a rolling quantile-regression window adapted to the severe heteroscedasticity of the target distribution.

## Results

In the Out-of-Distribution (OOD) regime (genus 14–17, unseen during training), DynamicFormer with the DRA activation achieves R² = 95.8 and a Conformal Width (CW) of 3.42, substantially outperforming all baselines. In the In-Distribution (ID) regime (genus 1–13, unseen marked-point counts), R² ranges from 98.7 to 99.9 with empirical conformal coverage between 74.8% and 96.7% at a 90% target. The model predicts ψ-class intersection numbers spanning a dynamic range of 10⁻⁴⁵ to 10⁴⁵, a regime where traditional recursive algorithms (O(g!·2ⁿ)) become infeasible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRA decisively outperforms all three activation-function baselines in the OOD regime: it improves R² by 12.9 points over Snake (95.8 vs. 82.9), by 21.1 points over GLU (95.8 vs. 74.7), and by 24.3 points over ReLU (95.8 vs. 71.5), while simultaneously halving the Conformal Width relative to Snake (3.42 vs. 6.55). Against the full-architecture baselines in Table 4, DynamicFormer (R² = 95.8, CW = 3.42) also beats the DRA-only MLP (R² = 76.5, CW = 7.73) and the Snake MLP (R² = 69.3, CW = 8.44), confirming that the multi-modal Transformer trunk contributes roughly 19 points of R² beyond what the DRA activation alone provides. In the auxiliary recursive-function experiment (Figure 1), KAN (Liu et al., 2024) and standard activations (ReLU, Tanh, GLU) all fail to capture the recursive pattern within finite training time, while Snake learns partial periodicity but DRA captures both the oscillatory and factorial-growth components. The paper does not report a head-to-head comparison against KAN in the full ψ-class intersection task, so the KAN comparison is limited to the toy recursive benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) to the domain of enumerative algebraic geometry by introducing DynamicFormer, a multi-modal model that processes quantum Airy structure tensors as sequences and partitions as permutation-invariant sets. It draws on Dynamic Positional Bias (Wang et al., 2022) for the B-modality attention, adapts Barlow Twins (Zbontar et al., 2021) into a 'Talking Modalities' cross-correlation loss, and repurposes register tokens (Darcet et al., 2024) as [DYN] tokens for cross-modal information sharing. The key departure from prior ML-for-math work (Belcak et al., 2022; d'Ascoli et al., 2022) is the explicit handling of factorial-growth recursive functions via the new DRA activation, combined with conformal prediction for uncertainty and interpretability tools (probing, causal tracing) for knowledge extraction—none of which appear together in prior literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first application of explainable Transformers to enumerative geometry, and the interpretability findings—that the network implicitly encodes Virasoro constraints and the large-genus asymptotic constant A=2/3 in a non-linear fashion—offer a concrete template for using ML as a hypothesis-generation tool in algebraic geometry. The DRA activation is a lightweight, drop-in modification that could benefit other recursive or factorial-growth regression tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other quantum Airy structures or to the joint (g,n)→∞ regime where no asymptotic formula is known, and whether the non-linear encoding of asymptotic parameters observed here reflects a general property of Transformer representations of recursive mathematics.
