## Problem

Existing multi-task model merging methods optimize solely for clean-data performance and neglect the security risk of backdoored models in the open-source ecosystem. The paper identifies two previously overlooked failure modes: backdoor succession, where backdoor effects persist in the merged model despite SOTA merging, and backdoor transfer, where backdoor effects propagate from compromised models to clean models through shared parameter regions during merging.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained CLIP-ViT checkpoint, a set of task-specific fine-tuned model weights (some potentially backdoored), and unlabeled test data only—no training labels, trigger patterns, or knowledge of which models are compromised are required. It outputs a merged multi-task model whose parameters in the identified unsafe subspace are reset to pre-trained weights, along with a learned safety-aware mask parameterized by logits via concrete relaxation. The entire optimization relies on entropy loss over unlabeled data, fitting the 'share-and-play' deployment scenario where defenders have only model checkpoints.

## Method

DAM formulates merging as a bi-level optimization with two alternately optimized masks: a Task-Shared Mask (M₁) that identifies common beneficial parameter subspaces to reduce inter-task interference, and a Backdoor-Detection Mask (M₂) that isolates parameters sensitive to synthesized adversarial perturbations to neutralize backdoor threats. The inner level optimizes per-task merging coefficients λ on clean data and synthesizes a unified perturbation Δ per task that approximates trigger-induced behavior changes without knowing the actual trigger, leveraging the insight that backdoored models produce adversarial samples with features similar to triggered images (Eq. 6). The outer level jointly optimizes both masks using entropy loss on clean and perturbed unlabeled data, with a balance weight α controlling the position along a proven Pareto front between performance and safety (Theorem 1). After alternating convergence, parameters in the identified unsafe regions are reset to pre-trained weights, yielding a merged model that reduces ASR by 2–10 percentage points with only ~1% accuracy degradation across multiple backdoor types and numbers of compromised models.

## Results

On the primary setting (2 backdoored + 4 clean models, CLIP-ViT-B/32, TrojVit attack), DAM achieves the best average ASR across all six tasks (ASR(6) = 35.77%) and the best ASR on backdoor-related tasks (ASR(2) = 64.47%), while maintaining competitive accuracy (ACC(6) = 85.23%, ACC(2) = 89.77%). Compared to the SOTA merging method Concrete AM (Layer-Wise), DAM reduces ASR(6) by ~1.9 pp and ASR(2) by ~6.4 pp at only a 0.7 pp ACC(6) cost. In the harder 4-backdoor setting, the advantage widens: DAM cuts ASR(4) by ~10.6 pp and ASR(6) by ~8.8 pp relative to Concrete AM (Layer-Wise) with <1 pp accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAM outperforms all standard multi-task merging baselines (Weight Average, Fisher Merging, RegMean, Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, AdaMerging, Concrete variants) on ASR while matching or exceeding their ACC; the gap is most dramatic against Ties-Merging (ASR(2): 64.47 vs 99.38) and Weight Average (ASR(6): 35.77 vs 53.82). Against the strongest merging baseline Concrete AM (Layer-Wise), DAM achieves the best ASR(6) (35.77 vs 37.66) and ASR(2) (64.47 vs 70.83) at a negligible 0.7 pp ACC(6) cost. Compared to post-defense methods (ANP, AWM, SAU) applied on top of Concrete AM, DAM achieves lower ASR(6) (35.77 vs 37.11–37.28) and substantially lower ASR(2) (64.47 vs 72.96–73.28) without any additional training stage, and is ~9–14 minutes faster in total wall-clock time. Against the non-multi-task backdoor defenses WAG and LoRA-as-an-Attack, DAM achieves higher ACC (85.79 vs 83.81) and lower ASR (24.52 vs 29.51) in the 2-backdoor+2-clean+4-clean setting. Finally, DAM successfully resists the BadMerging attack, with ASR increasing by only ~0.6–1.1 pp when BadMerging is applied on top of DAM, confirming its robustness as a defense.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: multi-task model merging (Concrete, Ties-Merging, AdaMerging) which resolves task interference via subspace or gradient-conflict methods, and post-hoc backdoor defense (ANP, AWM, SAU) which purifies already-merged models. It departs from both by formulating backdoor mitigation as an end-to-end component of the merging optimization itself, using a dual-mask (Task-Shared + Backdoor-Detection) meta-learning framework built on Concrete's concrete relaxation and AdaMerging's entropy-based test-time adaptation. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'backdoor transfer'—where backdoored models contaminate clean models during merging—as a distinct phenomenon absent from prior single-model backdoor defense literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a real and previously unexamined security risk in the open-source model ecosystem: merging publicly available checkpoints can propagate backdoors to tasks that were never compromised. However, the evaluation is confined to CLIP-ViT on six image classification datasets with two ViT-specific patch attacks (TrojVit, BadVit), leaving open whether the findings generalize to LLMs, diffusion models, or more sophisticated adaptive attacks. The modest ASR reduction (2–10 pp) at ~1% ACC cost is practical but suggests the defense is not yet a complete solution, and the assumption that the pre-trained base model is clean and protected is a significant limitation for real-world deployment.
