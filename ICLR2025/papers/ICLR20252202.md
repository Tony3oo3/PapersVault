## Problem

In class-incremental continual learning with pretrained models, the linear classifier develops a strong bias toward the most recent task's classes, causing severe performance drops on earlier tasks. While prior methods correct this bias using rehearsal memory, the recent shift to memory-free continual learning renders those approaches infeasible. This paper shows that the representation layer retains knowledge well even after overfitting on the latest task, and that the classifier bias is the dominant source of forgetting, motivating a test-time correction strategy that requires no stored data.

## Input / Output

During inference, the method consumes each arriving test image (RGB) along with the model's raw output logits over all classes seen so far; no task identity or ground-truth label is available. It produces two outputs: (1) an updated classifier weight matrix after a single online gradient step (Adaptive Retention), and (2) a corrected class assignment for samples flagged as misclassified past-task data (Adaptive Correction). The entire pipeline is plug-and-play and adds no parameters to the backbone.

## Method

ARC is built on an Out-of-Task Detection (OTD) module that uses two confidence-based assumptions to partition incoming test samples: Assumption 1 identifies past-task samples correctly classified (high softmax confidence on a past-task class), and Assumption 2 identifies past-task samples misclassified into the current task (low ratio w = c/ĉ, where ĉ is the max probability after masking current-task logits). For Assumption-1 samples, Adaptive Retention performs one gradient update on the frozen-backbone classifier using a combined cross-entropy and entropy-minimization loss with the predicted label as a pseudo-supervisory signal. For Assumption-2 samples, Adaptive Correction computes a Task-based Softmax Score (TSS) per past task with a temperature-scaled denominator to counteract inter-task bias, then reassigns the sample to the task with the highest TSS. The approach requires no modifications to the training procedure and is compatible with both memory-based and memory-free continual learning methods.

## Results

ARC is a test-time plug-in that improves any existing continual learning method without modifying its training procedure. On Split CIFAR-100 (Inc5), integrating ARC yields an average accuracy (A_B) boost of 2.7% and a forgetting (F) reduction of 8.0% across all tested methods. On Split Imagenet-R (Inc20), the average A_B increase is 2.6% with a 5.5% drop in forgetting. The largest single-method gains are iCarL (+6.8% A_B on CIFAR-100 Inc5, +6.6% on Imagenet-R Inc20) and DualPrompt (+6.3% on CIFAR-100 Inc5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARC consistently outperforms all eight baselines it is integrated with, since it is a plug-in rather than a competing method. The largest gains appear for methods with stronger classifier bias: iCarL improves by +6.8% A_B on CIFAR-100 Inc5 and +6.6% on Imagenet-R Inc20, while DualPrompt gains +6.3% on CIFAR-100 Inc5. Among memory-free prompt-based methods, L2P benefits most (+4.7% on CIFAR-100 Inc5), whereas CodaPrompt and SLCA—already strong baselines—see smaller but still positive gains (+1.8–2.2% and +1.1–5.3% respectively). In the TTA comparison (Table 3), ARC surpasses TENT by an average of 1.8% on CIFAR-100 Inc10 and 2.9% on Imagenet-R Inc20, attributed to ARC's selective adaptation to past-task data and its additional Adaptive Correction mechanism. On the more diverse 5-dataset benchmark, ARC still delivers an average +2.6% A_B and −2.2% F, confirming robustness across distribution shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes OOD detection concepts as an 'Out-of-Task Detection' mechanism to identify past-task samples at inference time, then applies test-time adaptation selectively to those samples rather than to all incoming data. It departs from prior classifier-bias correction methods (BiC, IL2M, OBC) that require memory or training-time intervention, and from general TTA methods (TENT, CoTTA) that adapt to all test data indiscriminately. The approach is positioned as a plug-and-play test-time module that can be layered onto any existing continual learning method—classical (iCaRL, DER, Memo) or prompt-based (L2P, DualPrompt, CodaPrompt, SLCA)—without modifying the training procedure, which the authors note is among the first analyses of applying TTA principles to CL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design makes ARC immediately practical for practitioners who cannot retrain or store exemplars, and the consistent 2.6–2.7% average accuracy gain across eight diverse methods and four dataset splits demonstrates broad applicability. The empirical finding that pretrained representation layers retain near-joint-training quality under linear probing (Figure 3) provides useful evidence that classifier bias, not backbone overfitting, is the dominant forgetting source in the pretrained-model regime, potentially redirecting future research effort. Limitations include the imperfect precision of the confidence-based assumptions (88.4% and 71.9% on average), the modest contribution of Adaptive Correction to prompt-based methods where few samples trigger Assumption 2, and the restriction to class-incremental image classification with a single shared classifier.
