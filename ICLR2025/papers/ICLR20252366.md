## Problem

Weakly supervised spatio-temporal video grounding (WSTVG) requires localizing a specific subject across both space and time from a free-form text query, using only video-level labels. While foundation models like Grounding DINO show strong zero-shot detection, adapting them to WSTVG exposes critical failures: inconsistent temporal predictions that switch object focus across frames, inability to maintain consistent attention on the correct part of complex queries, and degraded performance in dense multi-subject scenes. Prior weakly supervised approaches rely on computationally expensive hierarchical algorithms or auxiliary modalities like optical flow, leaving a gap for an efficient, foundation-model-based solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (sampled at every 5th frame for detection, 32 equally-indexed frames for tubelet features) and a free-form text caption describing the subject and activity. It outputs a spatio-temporal tubelet — a sequence of bounding boxes tracking the referred subject across frames — along with predicted start and end timestamps for the described activity. Detection uses G-DINO with a 0.4 confidence threshold, and BoTSORT tracking links per-frame detections into tubelets; the final prediction selects the tubelet with highest spatial attention score and the temporal boundary from the reconstruction module.

## Method

CoSPaL builds on a frozen Grounding DINO backbone and introduces three components. (1) Tubelet Phrase Grounding (TPG) performs spatial grounding via a multimodal InfoNCE contrastive loss that aligns BERT word embeddings with tubelet features (enhanced by a temporal self-attention block to weight informative frames), and temporal grounding via a reconstruction loss where a transformer decoder regenerates masked query words from cross-attended video clip features (I3D). (2) Contextual Referral Grounding (CRG) uses GPT-3.5 to decompose the query into local referral-subject phrases (Q_ol) and global noun-adjective-verb features (Q_og), then substitutes these refined word features into both the spatial contrastive and temporal reconstruction losses to concentrate attention on the correct subject. (3) Self-Paced Scene Understanding (SPS) applies a student-driven curriculum that progressively raises the upper bound on tubelets per video (4 → 7 → all), forcing the network to develop discriminative ability from coarse to fine-grained scenes. The entire pipeline trains only the grounding heads (not the detector) for 10 epochs on a single 16 GB GPU.

## Results

On HCSTVG-v1, CoSPaL achieves 22.1 m_vIoU, 31.8 vIoU@0.3, 19.6 vIoU@0.5, and 41.2 tIoU, surpassing the previous weakly-supervised SOTA (WINNER) by 7.9% in m_vIoU and 2×/3× at vIoU@0.3/0.5. On HCSTVG-v2, it reaches 22.2 m_vIoU and 48.6 tIoU. On VidSTG, CoSPaL achieves 16.0 m_vIoU (declarative) and 13.5 m_vIoU (interrogative), outperforming the prior weakly-supervised best by 4.4% and 3.3% respectively, while remaining within 10–15% of the strongest fully-supervised methods (e.g., VGDINO at 34.7 m_vIoU declarative).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSPaL decisively outperforms all prior weakly-supervised baselines: it beats WINNER (previous SOTA) by 7.9% m_vIoU on HCSTVG-v1 (22.1 vs. 14.2) and by 4.4% on VidSTG-Declarative (16.0 vs. 11.6), with gains of 2× at vIoU@0.3 and 3× at vIoU@0.5 over WINNER. Against its own W-GDINO baseline, CoSPaL improves m_vIoU by 13.1 points on HCSTVG-v1 (22.1 vs. 9.0) and tIoU by 23.2 points (41.2 vs. 18.0). Remarkably, as a weakly-supervised method, CoSPaL surpasses two fully-supervised methods (STGVT at 18.2 and STVGBert at 20.4 m_vIoU) on HCSTVG-v1, and on VidSTG it exceeds Groun-R, STPR, WSSTG, and STGRN. However, it remains behind the strongest fully-supervised approaches—TubeDETR (32.4), STCAT (35.1), CSDVL (36.9), CG-STVG (38.4), and VGDINO (38.3 m_vIoU on HCSTVG-v1)—by roughly 10–16% in m_vIoU, a gap the authors attribute to the absence of frame-level bounding box annotations during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Grounding DINO (G-DINO), an open-set multimodal object detection foundation model, to the video domain for weakly supervised spatio-temporal video grounding (WSTVG), a task previously addressed by purpose-built architectures like AWGU, Vis-CTX, and WINNER. It departs from prior WSTVG work by leveraging a frozen foundation model backbone rather than training end-to-end, and introduces three complementary modules—Tubelet Phrase Grounding (contrastive + reconstruction), Contextual Referral Grounding (GPT-3.5-assisted query decomposition), and Self-Paced Scene Understanding (curriculum learning over tubelet count)—that collectively address temporal inconsistency, imbalanced query attention, and complex scene understanding. The approach sits at the intersection of weakly supervised phrase/referral grounding in images and temporal video grounding, but is the first to frame WSTVG as a foundation-model adaptation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoSPaL demonstrates that a frozen multimodal foundation model, augmented with lightweight grounding modules and curriculum training, can substantially close the gap to fully supervised STVG methods while using a single GPU (vs. 8–32 for prior fully supervised approaches), making WSTVG far more accessible. However, the upper-bound analysis reveals that detection and tracking failures on challenging datasets (HCSTVG) cap performance at roughly 35–42% of the theoretical maximum, indicating that the bottleneck has shifted from grounding to perception. Open questions include whether per-object temporal modeling (rather than post-hoc tracking) could overcome these perception limits, and whether the GPT-3.5 dependency for query decomposition generalizes beyond the curated HCSTVG/VidSTG query styles.
