## Problem

Existing expert merging methods in Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (SMoE) architectures assume a flat, Euclidean parameter space, which limits generalization especially during pre-training when the parameter manifold exhibits complex curvature. While curvature-aware merging via the Fisher Information Matrix has been explored for model fusion, these approaches require storing and inverting large matrices proportional to model size, making them intractable for SMoE layers with many experts. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a lightweight, learnable curvature-aware merging protocol that captures manifold geometry without the memory and compute overhead of explicit Fisher estimation.

## Input / Output

The method operates at the weight level within SMoE feedforward layers of transformer backbones (GPT-2 small, T5-base, Swin Transformer small). It consumes pre-trained expert weight matrices E_i ∈ R^{d×h} and router scores s_i, producing a single merged expert weight Ê_m per layer that replaces all N experts while preserving the same number of activated parameters. For autoregressive language models, causal segmenting constrains routing decisions to use only preceding-segment hidden states, and a stop-gradient is applied to the first segment's router scores to prevent information leakage. At inference time, a reparameterization trick folds the curvature matrices into the domain vectors, eliminating the need to store or recompute them.

## Method

CAMEx inserts learnable curvature matrices M_i into the standard domain-specific expert merging formula, yielding Ê_m = E_m + α Σ M_i · (s_i * τ_i), where τ_i = E_i − E_m are domain vectors and s_i are router scores. The curvature matrices are learned end-to-end via backpropagation: the gradient of the task loss with respect to M_j is the outer product of the task-loss gradient and the j-th domain vector, implicitly approximating the empirical Fisher without explicit estimation. To keep M_i tractable, the paper applies a rank-1 Kronecker product decomposition (M_i ≈ M_i^in ⊗ M_i^out), reducing memory from O(n⁴) to O(n) and compute from O(n⁴) to O(n^{2.5}). A dynamic merging architecture further propagates a global expert across layers via a two-step update (average curvature-aware domain vectors to form the next layer's base, then apply CA-Merg), cutting total parameters by ~9% while maintaining per-layer expert count. Theoretical analysis shows the learned curvature introduces a gradient-matching term that performs soft nearest-neighbor voting over experts, amplifying updates aligned with the task loss and dampening conflicting directions.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (T5-base, 8 experts/layer), Ties-CA achieves the highest scores on SST-2 (94.61), MRPC (92.49), CoLA (60.06), and MNLI (86.45), while Dare-CA leads on RTE (78.70). For pre-training on Wikitext-103 (GPT-2 small), Domain-specific-CA attains the lowest perplexity of 21.50, and the Dynamic architecture achieves 21.55 with 9% fewer parameters (470M vs 522M). On SQuAD, Dare-CA reaches 81.76 EM / 88.60 F1, and on ImageNet-1k, Ties-CA achieves 83.38% top-1 and 96.96% top-5 accuracy, all while maintaining identical FLOPs to the SMoE baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMEx consistently outperforms all Euclidean-based merging baselines. Ties-CA beats TIES by +0.69 on SST-2, +1.05 on MRPC, and +1.52 on CoLA, while Dare-CA surpasses DARE by a striking +5.06 on RTE (78.70 vs 73.64) and +0.69 on SST-2. On Wikitext-103 pre-training, Domain-specific-CA (21.50 ppl) improves over Domain-specific (21.64), SMoE (22.42), and Vanilla (23.03), with the Dynamic architecture matching Domain-specific-CA at 21.55 while using 9% fewer parameters. On SQuAD, Dare-CA (81.76 EM) and Ties-CA (81.52 EM) both exceed the best non-CA baseline (SMoE at 81.25 EM) and the Vanilla model (81.01 EM). On ImageNet-1k, Ties-CA (83.38% top-1) edges out Dare-CA (83.38%) and TIES (83.28%), while all CA variants surpass the Vanilla (83.14%) and SMoE (83.15%) baselines. The paper also shows that CAMEx integrates additively with Twin-Merging (Twin-CA: 92.30 MRPC vs 91.97 Twin-Merging) and benefits from more advanced routing strategies (Stable MoE routing CA: 92.96 MRPC vs 91.92 Stable MoE TIES).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of work on SMoE expert merging (SMEAR, Lory) by replacing the Euclidean weighted-average merging with a natural-gradient-inspired update that incorporates a learned curvature matrix. It departs from prior Fisher-based merging methods (Matena & Raffel, 2022; Jin et al., 2023), which require explicit computation and inversion of the Fisher Information Matrix, by instead learning the curvature matrix as a trainable parameter via backpropagation, thereby avoiding the O(n⁴) memory and compute bottleneck. The Kronecker-factored approximation of the curvature matrix follows Martens & Grosse (2015), and the merging protocol is composable with existing masking strategies (TIES, DARE) and the causal segmenting routing from Lory (Zhong et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAMEx offers a practical, low-overhead way to make SMoE expert merging curvature-aware, which is important as SMoE becomes the dominant scaling paradigm for LLMs and expert merging is a key efficiency technique. The dynamic merging architecture's ~9% parameter reduction while maintaining perplexity is a concrete efficiency win for resource-constrained deployment. However, the performance gains over non-CA baselines are modest (typically 0.3–1.5 points), and the observed incompatibility between higher Kronecker ranks and TIES/DARE masking remains unexplained, limiting the method's flexibility. Open questions include whether the learned curvature matrix truly captures meaningful manifold geometry or merely acts as a learned per-expert scaling, and whether the approach generalizes to post-training merging or multi-task settings beyond the single-domain fine-tuning evaluated here.
