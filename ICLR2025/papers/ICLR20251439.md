## Problem

Cryo-EM data processing has not fully benefited from foundation models: existing data-driven approaches like DeepEMhancer and EMReady are designed for specific tasks (e.g., map sharpening) and lack the versatility to generalize across the diverse inverse problems in cryo-EM and cryo-ET. No prior work has learned a general-purpose generative prior over high-quality density maps that can be plugged into arbitrary downstream tasks without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

CRYOFM-S accepts 64³ voxel volumes (lowpass-filtered to 1.5 Å/voxel) and CRYOFM-L accepts 128³ volumes (3 Å/voxel), both in normalized voxel space. The task-specific forward operator A defines how the clean density is degraded (spectral noise, anisotropic noise, Fourier-space wedge mask, or 2D projection with unknown pose), and the observation y is the corresponding degraded measurement. For ab initio modeling, the input is K clean 2D class averages with unknown orientations; for the other tasks, the input is a single noisy or incomplete 3D volume. The output is always a restored 3D density map sampled from the posterior p(x|y).

## Method

CRYOFM is a flow-matching generative model built on a hierarchical Transformer (HDiT) with neighborhood attention at the input/output ends and global attention in the middle, trained via conditional flow matching to regress a vector field v_Θ(t, x_t) that transports noise to the data distribution of 3,479 curated EMDB density maps. The key inference contribution is a flow posterior sampling algorithm that converts the unconditional vector field into a conditional one by subtracting a likelihood gradient term weighted by t/(1-t), yielding v_Θ(t, x_t|y) ≈ v_Θ(t, x_t) + (t/(1-t))·λ_t·∇_{x_t}‖y − A x̂₀(x_t)‖², where x̂₀ is the flow model's posterior-mean estimate. This plug-and-play formulation requires only defining the task-specific forward operator A (and its gradient) at inference time, with no fine-tuning. Two model variants are trained: CRYOFM-S (335M params, 64³, for high-resolution local detail) and CRYOFM-L (308M params, 128³, for global shape at lower resolution), both trained in voxel space rather than latent space to preserve fine structural details.

## Results

On spectral noise denoising (Table 1), CRYOFM achieves FSC_AUC of 0.95±0.01 at 3.2 Å, 0.68±0.04 at 4.3 Å, and 0.62±0.04 at 6.1 Å, with a zero fail rate across all resolutions. On anisotropic noise denoising (Table 2), CRYOFM reaches FSC_AUC of 0.88±0.03 (θ∈[-45°,45°]) and FSC_0.5 of 3.1±0.1 Å, substantially outperforming both baselines. For missing wedge restoration (Table 3), CRYOFM improves FSC_AUC from 0.80 to 0.92±0.02 and recovers 0.76±0.06 FSC_AUC in the previously missing region (vs. 0.0000 before restoration).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRYOFM outperforms DeepEMhancer on FSC_AUC at all tested resolutions (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.92 at 3.2 Å; 0.68 vs. 0.66 at 4.3 Å) and achieves a zero fail rate compared to DeepEMhancer's 6–44% fail rate, which grows with increasing noise. Against EMReady, CRYOFM shows a clear and consistent advantage in FSC_AUC (0.95 vs. 0.73 at 3.2 Å; 0.68 vs. 0.59 at 4.3 Å; 0.62 vs. 0.55 at 6.1 Å) and lower FSC_0.5, indicating better resolution recovery. On anisotropic noise denoising, CRYOFM (FSC_AUC 0.88, FSC_0.5 3.1 Å) substantially surpasses spIsoNet (FSC_AUC 0.65, FSC_0.5 4.15 Å), which preserves signal but fails to improve correlation with ground truth, and also outperforms DeepEMhancer (FSC_AUC 0.80, FSC_0.5 3.2 Å) while avoiding the artifacts and shape distortions that DeepEMhancer introduces. CRYOFM also completes the anisotropic denoising task in ~30 minutes versus over 5 hours for spIsoNet on a V100 GPU. For missing wedge restoration, CRYOFM is the only method evaluated, recovering 0.76 FSC_AUC in the previously unobserved region, demonstrating the generality of the flow posterior sampling framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRYOFM builds on flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022a) as its generative backbone and adopts the HDiT hierarchical transformer architecture (Crowson et al., 2024) to handle 3D density volumes efficiently. Its key conceptual departure from prior cryo-EM denoising methods (DeepEMhancer, EMReady) is that instead of directly learning the posterior p(x|y) for a specific degradation, it learns the prior p(x) once and then combines it with task-specific likelihoods via a flow posterior sampling algorithm derived from diffusion posterior sampling (Chung et al., 2022). This plug-and-play prior-likelihood decomposition contrasts with both pretrained posterior models and self-supervised per-dataset methods (spIsoNet, M), positioning CRYOFM as a foundation model rather than a task-specific tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single pretrained flow model can handle four distinct cryo-EM/cryo-ET tasks (spectral denoising, anisotropic denoising, missing wedge restoration, ab initio modeling) without fine-tuning, achieving zero fail rates where baselines fail on 6–44% of cases. This versatility validates the foundation-model paradigm for cryo-EM densities, though the training set is modest (~3.5k maps) and all experiments use synthetic noise applied to real data rather than genuinely noisy experimental reconstructions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to raw 2D particle data, whether latent-space training can be made to work with larger datasets, and whether the method generalizes to the full ~40k EMDB maps at diverse resolutions.
