## Problem

Real-world spatio-temporal networks (traffic, air quality, wind energy) grow continuously as new sensors are installed, yet existing STGNNs assume a static graph and are trained once on a fixed dataset. Continual fine-tuning of the full STGNN on each new period suffers from catastrophic forgetting and incurs prohibitive retraining costs, while prior continual learning methods (TrafficStream, STKEC, TFMoE) still require optimizing all backbone parameters without a principled mechanism to prevent knowledge loss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dynamic streaming spatio-temporal graph where the node set expands across periods (e.g., PEMS-Stream grows from 655 to 871 sensors over 7 years). At each period τ, the input is a 3D tensor X_τ ∈ R^{n×t×c} of node features over t past time steps plus a growing adjacency matrix A_τ ∈ R^{n×n}; the output is forecasts for the next 3, 6, or 12 time steps for all current nodes. A key constraint is that only newly added nodes require new parameters—existing node prompts and the frozen backbone are carried forward unchanged in memory.

## Method

EAC freezes the base STGNN backbone and adapts solely through a node-level prompt parameter pool that is element-wise added to input features before feeding into the network. Two empirically and theoretically grounded tuning principles govern the pool: (1) Expand—new nodes receive fresh prompt vectors, and Proposition 1 proves this strictly increases feature-space dispersion (heterogeneity) that message-passing STGNNs cannot capture; (2) Compress—each prompt matrix P ∈ R^{n×d} is factorized as A·B with A ∈ R^{n×k} and B ∈ R^{k×d} where k = O(log(min(n,d))), and Proposition 2 guarantees the approximation error vanishes with high probability as n grows. In practice, only the subspace matrix A^(τ) for newly added nodes is appended each period while B remains fixed, keeping total tunable parameters at roughly 59% of the full model and accelerating training by 1.26–3.02× per period.

## Results

On PEMS-Stream (traffic), EAC achieves an average MAE of 13.53, RMSE of 21.77, and MAPE of 18.98%, outperforming the best baseline (Online-ST-AN) by 3.90% in MAE. On Air-Stream (weather), EAC reaches 20.75 avg MAE and 32.35 avg RMSE, and on Energy-Stream (wind power), it achieves 5.10 avg MAE and 5.33 avg RMSE. Across all three datasets, EAC consistently improves MAE by 1.75%–4.85% over the second-best method, with the largest gains on Energy-Stream (−4.85%) and PEMS-Stream 12-step MAE (−7.73%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAC outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against TrafficStream (Chen et al., 2021b), EAC reduces average MAE from 14.22 to 13.53 on PEMS-Stream (−4.9%) and from 5.57 to 5.10 on Energy-Stream (−8.4%), while also using roughly half the tunable parameters and training 1.26–3.02× faster per period. Against STKEC (Wang et al., 2023a), EAC improves PEMS-Stream MAE from 14.14 to 13.53 and Energy-Stream MAE from 5.36 to 5.10, with notably lower variance (±0.06 vs ±0.04 for STKEC on PEMS-Stream). Against PECPM (Wang et al., 2023b) and TFMoE (Lee & Park, 2024), whose official code is unavailable, EAC's 13.53 avg MAE on PEMS-Stream beats their reported values of 14.85 and 14.18 respectively. Against ST-CRL (Xiao et al., 2022), EAC achieves 12.65 vs 18.41 MAE at 3-step and 14.92 vs 24.45 at 12-step on PEMS-Stream, a gap of over 30% in 12-step MAE. EAC also demonstrates universality, improving all six STGNN architecture combinations (spectral/spatial × recurrent/convolution/attention) by 0.34%–6.48% in MAE, and outperforms a LoRA-based prompt adaptation by 8.7% in 12-step MAE while training 33% faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the prompt-based continual learning paradigm (freeze backbone, tune lightweight prompt parameters) from vision (Wang et al., CVPR 2022) to spatio-temporal graph forecasting, and extends the node-specific trainable parameters introduced by Shao et al. (CIKM 2022) from static to continual settings. It departs from existing continual spatio-temporal forecasting methods (TrafficStream, STKEC, PECPM, TFMoE) that all require tuning the full STGNN model, instead freezing the backbone and introducing a node-level prompt parameter pool governed by two empirically and theoretically grounded principles: expansion for heterogeneity capture and compression via low-rank factorization to control parameter growth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The expand-and-compress principles provide a principled, theoretically justified framework for continual spatio-temporal forecasting that is architecture-agnostic and significantly more efficient than full-model fine-tuning, making it practical for real-world streaming sensor networks. Limitations include handling only node expansion (not removal), a maximum of seven periods, and residual parameter inflation that the low-rank compression mitigates but does not eliminate. The authors identify large-scale spatio-temporal pre-training as a key future direction, suggesting the prompt-pool approach could serve as an efficient adaptation layer atop a foundation spatio-temporal model.
