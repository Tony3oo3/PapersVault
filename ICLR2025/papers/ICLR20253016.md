## Problem

DARE, the dominant random-based delta-parameter pruning method, fails catastrophically at high pruning rates (>30%) or when fine-tuned models exhibit large delta parameters, dropping to near-zero accuracy in many settings. The paper identifies two root causes—an excessively large rescaling factor 1/(1-p) and high mean/variance in the delta parameters—and asks whether these failure modes can be addressed to push randomized DPP to extreme pruning rates (up to 99%).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fine-tuned model θ_F and its pre-trained base θ_P (from which delta parameters Δ_θ = θ_F − θ_P are computed), a target pruning rate p, and optionally a labeled validation set or a single unlabeled data batch for rescaling-factor tuning. It outputs a sparse delta parameter tensor with (1−p) fraction of entries retained and rescaled by a tuned factor 1/q (where q > 1−p), plus the optimal q value. For the AdamR variant, the fine-tuning process itself is modified in-place, replacing AdamW's weight decay with delta-parameter regularization before any post-hoc pruning is applied.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary algorithms grounded in a high-probability bound (Theorem 3.1) on the absolute change in intermediate representations after DARE. DAREx-q replaces DARE's fixed rescaling factor 1/(1−p) with a tuned 1/q (q > 1−p), selected either by maximizing validation-set accuracy (1/q_v) or by minimizing mean last-layer output change on a single unlabeled batch (1/q_e), with both global and per-layer variants; the per-layer version uses an analytical bound to avoid L separate searches. AdamR modifies the AdamW optimizer to apply L2 (or L1) regularization directly on the delta parameters Δ_θ during fine-tuning, with the regularization strength scaled by the gradient norm to avoid late-training instability; combining AdamR-L2 with DARE (DAREx-L2) keeps delta statistics small so that post-hoc random pruning preserves performance. The two strategies are orthogonal and can be combined (DAREx-L2-q), and DAREx-q is shown to be compatible with LoRA and structural pruning.

## Results

At an extreme 99% delta-parameter pruning rate (p=0.99) on BERT, DAREx-L2 achieves 57.24% on CoLA and 88.17% on SST-2, compared to 56.24% and 90.25% for the unpruned model — a 40× model size reduction (417.7 MB → 11.4 MB) with minimal performance loss. Vanilla DARE collapses to 4.25% on CoLA and 51.00% on SST-2 under the same conditions. On decoder models (GSM8K, p=0.99), DARE achieves 0.00% accuracy across all four 7B models, while DAREx-q recovers 20–36% accuracy, demonstrating that effective DPP is possible even at extreme pruning rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DARE (Yu et al., 2023), the primary baseline, DAREx-q and DAREx-L2 deliver dramatic gains at high pruning rates: at p=0.99 on CoLA, DAREx-L2 improves from 4.25% to 57.24% (+52.99 points), and on decoder models (GSM8K, p=0.99) DAREx-q recovers 20–36% accuracy where DARE scores 0.00%. Against WANDA (Sun et al., 2023), the comparison is scenario-dependent: on MetaMath-LLaMA-7B with large delta parameters (mean |ΔW| = 0.017), WANDA maintains 54.5% GSM8K accuracy at p=0.3 while DARE drops to 0.00% at p=0.1, making WANDA the preferred method; however, on decoder models with small DPs, DAREx-q outperforms WANDA by over 30 points (e.g., 34.87% vs 0.00% at p=0.99 on MetaMath-7B). Against magnitude pruning (Han et al., 2015), DAREx-L2 substantially wins at p=0.99 (57.24% vs 12.30% on CoLA), though L1+MP with AdamR-L1 regularization narrows the gap. Against sparse fine-tuning (Diff/Diff-struct, Guo et al., 2020), DAREx-q is competitive at p=0.995 (e.g., 89.9% vs 89.7% on MRPC) and DAREx-L2 surpasses both at p=0.999 (88.5% vs 88.2% on MRPC), while being a simpler post-hoc method requiring no iterative retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the delta-parameter pruning (DPP) framework initiated by DARE (Yu et al., 2023a), which randomly drops and rescales fine-tuning deltas to compress model storage. It departs from DARE's theoretical justification—zero-expectation output change via the 1/(1-p) rescaling factor—by showing that controlling the absolute magnitude of output changes (governed by both the rescaling factor and the mean/variance of delta parameters) is what actually determines performance. The AdamR optimizer builds on AdamW and the gradient-norm-aware decay correction from Xie et al. (2024), while the importance-based DPP experiments adapt magnitude pruning (Han et al., 2015) and WANDA (Sun et al., 2023) to the delta-parameter setting, a connection the authors note was previously unexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPP is directly relevant to model merging, federated learning communication, multi-tenant LLM serving, and storage of the many fine-tuned models on platforms like Hugging Face. By enabling 99% delta-parameter pruning with minimal performance loss (e.g., 57.24 vs. 56.24 on COLA), the paper makes these applications far more practical. The provided decision framework (Fig. 4) for selecting among DAREx-q, AdamR-L2, and importance-based methods based on available data and DP magnitude is immediately actionable. Open questions include whether these techniques generalize to non-language modalities, whether the per-layer rescaling theory tightens further, and whether the AdamR approach interacts well with other PEFT methods beyond LoRA.
