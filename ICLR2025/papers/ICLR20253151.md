## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs struggle to generate outputs that satisfy logical constraints, and the dominant approach—greedy per-token logit masking—is myopic, enforcing constraints token-by-token rather than jointly across the full sequence. Additionally, constraint specification relies on verbose regular expressions compiled into DFAs, which can grow exponentially in size for many constraint classes. The paper asks whether one can perform tractable Bayesian conditioning on the full autoregressive distribution to produce globally optimal constrained samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes an autoregressive model (LLM or CNN-LSTM), a logical constraint specified as a Boolean Python/PyTorch function (e.g., 'no toxic words appear', 'every row/column/3×3 square in the Sudoku is unique'), and a prompt (text, terrain map image, or partially-filled Sudoku grid). It produces a token sequence guaranteed to satisfy the constraint, drawn approximately from the true conditional distribution p(y|α). Constraints are compiled into sentential decision diagrams (SDDs) rather than DFAs, and the canonize function ensures all tokenization variants of a banned expression are caught.

## Method

GEN-C performs Bayesian conditioning in three stages. First, it draws a sample ỹ from the autoregressive model p(ỹ), which induces a local, fully-factorized contextualized pseudolikelihood distribution p_ỹ(y) = ∏ᵢ p(yᵢ | ỹ₋ᵢ) that is tractable for exact probabilistic reasoning. Second, it compiles the constraint α into a constraint circuit (SDD) and computes the conditional p_ỹ(y|α) by multiplying the circuit with the factorized distribution and normalizing, then samples y from this conditional. Third, it corrects the sampling bias via self-normalized importance weights w = p(y,α)·p_y(ỹ) / (p(ỹ)·p_ỹ(y|α)) and resamples with replacement proportional to w, yielding samples from the true p(y|α). The use of SDDs instead of DFAs provides exponential succinctness gains, and the Python-function constraint language eliminates the need for regular expressions.

## Results

On Warcraft shortest-path prediction, GEN-C improves exact-match accuracy from 62.00% (CNN-LSTM baseline) and 69.10% (oversampling) to 78.13%, while achieving 100% constraint consistency. On Sudoku, GEN-C achieves a perfect 100% exact accuracy, compared to 31.00%±4.19 for Gemini 1.5 Flash and 40.90%±7.90 for GPT-4o mini. On LLM detoxification (Llama3-8B, RealToxicityPrompts), GEN-C reduces expected maximum toxicity on toxic prompts from 0.43 to 0.37 and toxicity probability from 38.30% to 28.00%, outperforming greedy word-banning (0.40 / 32.43%) while maintaining identical perplexity (14.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Warcraft shortest-path, GEN-C (78.13% exact) outperforms the raw CNN-LSTM baseline (62.00%) by 16.13 points and the oversampling variant (69.10%) by 9.03 points, while also guaranteeing 100% constraint consistency versus 76.60% and 84.50% respectively. On Sudoku, GEN-C achieves a perfect 100% exact accuracy, surpassing Gemini 1.5 Flash (31.00%) by 69 points and GPT-4o mini (40.90%) by 59.1 points — a dramatic margin that highlights the benefit of hard constraint enforcement over unconstrained LLM generation. On LLM detoxification, GEN-C reduces expected maximum toxicity on toxic prompts by 0.06 (0.43→0.37) and toxicity probability by 10.30 percentage points (38.30%→28.00%), roughly doubling the reduction achieved by greedy word-banning (0.40 / 32.43%), while maintaining the same perplexity (14.50) as both the baseline and the greedy method.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEN-C departs from the dominant 'greedy masking' paradigm for constrained LLM generation (Outlines, Guidance, SGLang, Koo et al.) by replacing per-token logit masking with full-sequence Bayesian conditioning. It builds on Darwiche's sentential decision diagrams (constraint circuits) as a tractable compilation form that is provably exponentially more succinct than the DFAs used by prior approaches, and on the contextualized pseudolikelihood framework (Besag; Ahmed et al. 2023a) to construct a local, factorized approximation of the autoregressive distribution amenable to exact circuit-based conditioning. The importance-sampling-and-resampling step to correct for the proposal-target mismatch is the key algorithmic contribution that ties these components into a globally-optimal constrained sampler.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GEN-C provides the first principled, probabilistically-sound alternative to greedy constrained decoding that guarantees constraint satisfaction while sampling from the true conditional distribution rather than a myopic approximation. The 100% Sudoku accuracy versus ~26–45% for frontier LLMs (Gemini 1.5 Flash, GPT-4o mini) demonstrates that logical-constraint tasks are fundamentally different from open-ended generation and that current LLMs are poorly suited to them without external constraint enforcement. Open questions include how constraint circuits scale to long sequences and complex constraints, whether tight integration with circuit libraries (e.g., pyjuice) can close the efficiency gap, and whether the framework can be extended to improve factual accuracy or handle soft/weighted constraints beyond hard Boolean logic.
