## Problem

In autoregressive image generation, increasing token length improves reconstruction quality but degrades generation due to error propagation, higher training cost, and larger model capacity requirements. Conversely, reducing token count aids generation but loses reconstruction fidelity. The paper asks whether it is possible to retain a sufficient number of tokens for high-quality reconstruction without lengthening the autoregressive sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251886/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes a 256×256 RGB image and produces two spatially aligned sets of K×K discrete tokens (semantic and detail branches) via product quantization. The autoregressive generator consumes these as folded token pairs (two tokens per spatial position per scale), yielding an effective sequence length of 286 rather than 680, and outputs a 256×256 generated image conditioned on an ImageNet class label. During inference, the two tokens at each position are sampled in parallel from a single logit using top-k/top-p sampling.

## Method

ImageFolder employs dual-branch product quantization where one branch receives semantic regularization via a CLIP-style contrastive loss against frozen DINOv2 features, while the other branch captures complementary pixel-level details. Each branch uses multi-scale residual quantization (MSRQ) with a quantizer dropout strategy that randomly drops the last several residual layers during training, forcing each scale to encode images at different bitrates and smoothing next-scale autoregressive prediction. Because the two branches capture distinct aspects of the image with low mutual dependency, the spatially aligned token pairs can be predicted in parallel from a single logit (two separate softmax heads), halving the AR sequence length without reducing total token count. The AR generator follows VAR's next-scale prediction paradigm with a GPT-2 backbone, and the tokenizer is trained with composite losses including reconstruction, VQ, adversarial (PatchGAN), perceptual (LPIPS), and CLIP semantic alignment terms.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, ImageFolder achieves a gFID of 2.60, IS of 295.0, and rFID of 0.80 with only 286 token length and 10 AR steps. Among autoregressive (AR) models, this is the best gFID reported, outperforming VAR (gFID 3.30, 680 tokens) and LlamaGen-L (gFID 3.80, 256 tokens). The linear probing accuracy of 58.0% also significantly exceeds VAR (11.3%) and LlamaGen (2.6%), indicating richer semantic content in the learned tokens. Inference latency is 0.130s per image on a single A100, comparable to VAR (0.134s) but with approximately 1/4 the FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among AR models, ImageFolder clearly outperforms VAR (gFID 2.60 vs 3.30, IS 295.0 vs 274.4) while using only 286 tokens versus VAR's 680, reducing sequence length by ~58% and cutting FLOPs to roughly 1/4. It also beats LlamaGen-L (gFID 2.60 vs 3.80, IS 295.0 vs 248.3) despite a similar token budget (286 vs 256). Against diffusion baselines, ImageFolder surpasses DiT-L/2 (gFID 2.60 vs 5.02) and LDM-4 (2.60 vs 3.60), and dramatically outperforms ADM (2.60 vs 10.94). However, it trails the strongest NAR methods MAGVIT-v2 (gFID 1.78) and TiTok-S-128 (gFID 1.94), as well as the diffusion MAR-B (gFID 2.31), indicating that parallel/masked decoding still holds an edge over sequential AR for pure generation quality. ImageFolder achieves the best recall (0.63) among all AR methods, suggesting better coverage of the data distribution, while its precision (0.75) is slightly below VAR (0.84) and LlamaGen (0.83).

## Relation to Prior Work

ImageFolder extends the VAR (Tian et al., 2024) framework for next-scale autoregressive image generation by replacing its single-branch multi-scale residual quantizer with a dual-branch product quantization that separates semantic and pixel-level detail tokens. The core departure from prior tokenizers (VQGAN, VAR, LlamaGen) is the 'folding' mechanism: because product quantization produces spatially aligned token pairs with reduced inter-branch dependency, two tokens can be predicted in parallel from one logit, halving the effective sequence length without reducing total token count. The paper also introduces quantizer dropout (borrowed from audio codec literature) and a CLIP-style semantic regularization applied to only one branch, departing from concurrent work like VILA-U that injects semantics into all quantized tokens and observes generation degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-ablated resolution to the long-recognized trade-off between token length (hurting AR generation via O(n²) attention cost and error propagation) and reconstruction fidelity, showing that 286 folded tokens can match or exceed 680 unfolded tokens in generation quality. The finding that semantic regularization must be confined to a single branch—applying it to all branches degrades gFID from 3.53 to 5.21—offers a practical design principle for future semantic tokenizers. Limitations include evaluation only on class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, reliance on a frozen DINOv2 encoder, and the fact that the comparison with VAR is not perfectly controlled due to differences in tokenizer training recipes (OpenImage pretraining, DINO discriminator). Open questions include whether the folding strategy generalizes to higher resolutions, text-to-image conditioning, or video generation, and whether the independence assumption in parallel decoding becomes a bottleneck at higher token counts.
