## Problem

Diffusion language models (DLMs) have shown promise for parallel, any-order, and controllable text generation, but existing DLMs are limited to 127M–1B parameters and under-trained with fewer than 400B tokens, preventing fair comparison with large AR LLMs. Training diffusion models from scratch at scale is prohibitively expensive due to the lack of computational optimizations developed for AR models. The paper addresses how to leverage the extensive compute already invested in pretrained AR LLMs to build competitive DLMs despite fundamental differences in their modeling objectives (causal vs. bidirectional attention, clean vs. noisy inputs).

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text sequence (a prompt for conditional generation, or a prefix+suffix pair for fill-in-the-middle infilling) and produces a full generated text sequence through iterative denoising over T timesteps. Unlike AR models that generate strictly left-to-right, the DLM can denoise tokens in any order and natively supports infilling without reordering the input sequence or adding special FIM tokens. Due to the inherited shift operation, the model's output logits at position i correspond to the distribution over token i+1, so a start token is prepended before each forward pass during sampling and the first output position is discarded.

## Method

The paper unifies the AR cross-entropy objective and the discrete diffusion ELBO, showing that AR training is a special case of discrete diffusion with a deterministic right-to-left masking schedule. To adapt a pretrained AR model into a DLM, three key modifications are applied: (1) attention mask annealing, which gradually transitions from causal to fully bidirectional attention over 10K training steps to break the left-to-right bias while preserving AR conditionals early in training; (2) a shift operation that keeps the AR convention of predicting the next token at each position, avoiding the misalignment that would arise if the model were forced to predict in-place masked tokens; and (3) a time-embedding-free architecture that relies on the implicit signal from the number of [MASK] tokens rather than explicit timestep conditioning, adding zero extra parameters. The adaptation is implemented as simple continual pre-training (full-parameter fine-tuning in bf16) on 30B–65B tokens, converting GPT2 (127M/355M) into DiffuGPT and LLaMA2-7B into DiffuLLaMA, with the 7B model using direct bidirectional attention and flash-attention 2 for efficiency.

## Results

DiffuGPT-S (127M) outperforms its AR base GPT2-S on Lambada (45.0 vs. 25.9), HellaSwag (33.4 vs. 29.9), GSM8K fine-tuned (50.2 vs. 44.8), and ROCStories infilling (ROUGE-1 13.7 vs. 7.8). DiffuGPT-M (355M) further improves, reaching 61.8 on GSM8K and 18.7 ROUGE-1 on ROCStories. At 7B scale, DiffuLLaMA surpasses LLaMA2 on GSM8K (63.1 vs. 58.6), Lambada (70.9 vs. 68.8), ROCStories infilling (23.3 vs. 11.6 ROUGE-1), and code infilling (15.5 vs. 1.7 pass@1), though it trails on knowledge-intensive tasks like TriviaQA (18.5 vs. 45.4) and PIQA (63.3 vs. 78.3). All adapted DLMs substantially outperform prior DLMs (SEDD, Plaid 1B) across nearly all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT2 (AR baselines), DiffuGPT-S and DiffuGPT-M win on Lambada, HellaSwag, Winogrande, SIQA, GSM8K, and both infilling tasks, but lose on TriviaQA and PIQA where factual knowledge is critical. Against SEDD (discrete diffusion, Lou et al. 2024), the adapted models dominate across all tasks—e.g., DiffuGPT-M scores 61.8 on GSM8K vs. SEDD-M's 53.5, and 60.5 vs. 23.1 on Lambada—while using fewer training tokens. Against Plaid 1B (continuous diffusion, Gulrajani & Hashimoto 2023), DiffuGPT-M outperforms on every conditional task despite being smaller (355M vs. 1.3B), with Plaid 1B only showing strength in unconditional generation fluency. Against LLaMA2 (7B AR), DiffuLLaMA wins on GSM8K (63.1 vs. 58.6), Lambada (70.9 vs. 68.8), and dramatically on infilling (ROCStories 23.3 vs. 11.6 ROUGE-1; Code 15.5 vs. 1.7 pass@1), but trails significantly on knowledge-heavy tasks (TriviaQA 18.5 vs. 45.4, PIQA 63.3 vs. 78.3, HellaSwag 58.7 vs. 74.9), which the authors attribute to training on only 65B tokens versus LLaMA2's 1T. MD4 (Shi et al. 2024) is compared only in unconditional generation (Figure 3), where DiffuGPT achieves lower perplexity at fewer decoding steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the absorbing discrete diffusion formulation of Austin et al. (2021) and the transformer architectures of GPT2 and LLaMA2, but its core conceptual contribution is demonstrating that autoregressive language modeling is a special case of discrete diffusion (deterministic right-to-left masking), thereby unifying the two objectives. This departs from prior DLM work (SEDD, Plaid 1B, MD4) that trained from scratch, and from Ye et al. (2023) which attempted MLM-to-DLM adaptation but lost base capabilities. The practical recipe—attention mask annealing from causal to bidirectional, retaining the AR shift operation, and omitting time embeddings—leverages the structural similarity between AR and DLM training to enable efficient continual pre-training rather than from-scratch training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to scale diffusion language models to 7B parameters via adaptation, providing a practical pathway to leverage the massive compute already invested in AR LLMs rather than retraining DLMs from scratch. The demonstrated infilling advantage (ROCStories, code) without prompt re-ordering is a genuine architectural differentiator over AR models that require FIM-specific training. Key limitations remain: DiffuLLaMA still trails LLaMA2 on knowledge-intensive tasks (TriviaQA, PIQA), chain-of-thought prompting degrades performance without instruction tuning, and the models are trained on only 65B tokens. Open questions include whether instruction tuning and more training tokens close the gap, and whether inference-time planning methods can exploit DLMs' bidirectional nature for superior reasoning.
