## Problem

RKHS-based reinforcement learning policies suffer from two critical limitations: excessive gradient variance that destabilizes training (particularly with wide-bandwidth kernels, as formally shown in Lemma 3.1), and strong hyperparameter sensitivity that prevents consistent performance across diverse environments. Existing variance reduction techniques for RKHS policies are either restricted to low-dimensional tasks or computationally infeasible in high-dimensional settings like MuJoCo, leaving a gap for a scalable, robust RKHS policy learning method.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw environment state vectors from MuJoCo continuous control tasks (e.g., 17-dim Hopper, 24-dim Humanoid) and outputs continuous actions sampled from a multivariate normal distribution whose mean is the sum of an RKHS function and a residual network output, both evaluated on the neural-network-transformed observation. The covariance matrix Σ is itself a learned parameter updated via backpropagation, and the RKHS function h is updated via Fréchet derivative in the kernel space rather than through standard parameter gradients.

## Method

ResKPN integrates three components: (1) a neural network representation layer ψ_θ that dynamically rescales observation distributions to match the kernel bandwidth, eliminating the need for manual hyperparameter tuning; (2) an advantage function A = Q − V that replaces the raw Q-value in the RKHS gradient to reduce variance without introducing bias; and (3) a fully connected residual layer μ_ι added to the policy mean, which acts as a learned baseline that further suppresses gradient variance (Theorem 3.2 proves the ordering Var(∇h U) ≥ Var(∇h U_A) ≥ Var(∇h U_{A,μ})). The RKHS gradient uses one-pair Monte Carlo estimation with PPO-style clipping (ε=1 for RKHS to encourage exploration) and DPO drift functions for exploration-exploitation balance, while the representation and residual networks are updated via standard actor-critic backpropagation with GAE. The RKHS function h is updated only once per training step (not per minibatch) to avoid gradient explosion, and the entire pipeline is accelerated with JAX/Brax on GPU.

## Results

ResKPN achieves up to 30% higher episodic rewards compared to DPO across MuJoCo environments, with faster convergence in Inverted Pendulum, Hopper, Humanoid, and Humanoid Standup. In Walker2D and Half Cheetah, where both PPO and DPO struggle to learn effectively, ResKPN maintains comparable performance. The ablation variants KPN and AdvKPN fail entirely in the most complex environments (Humanoid, Humanoid Standup), while the original RKHS policy (Origin-Kernel) only matches other methods in the simplest Inverted Pendulum task. ResKPN also exhibits the lowest training variance (standard deviation of episodic rewards) among all methods in the Inverted Pendulum environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO, ResKPN wins decisively in complex environments (Humanoid, Humanoid Standup, Hopper) where PPO converges slowly or to suboptimal policies, and in Walker2D/Half Cheetah where PPO fails to learn a good policy at all. Against DPO (the strongest neural-network baseline), ResKPN achieves up to 30% higher episodic rewards in Inverted Pendulum, Hopper, Humanoid, and Humanoid Standup, while matching DPO in Walker2D and Half Cheetah. Against the original RKHS policy (Origin-Kernel), ResKPN is dramatically superior in every environment beyond Inverted Pendulum, where Origin-Kernel only achieves comparable performance; in Humanoid and Humanoid Standup, Origin-Kernel fails to learn effectively. The intermediate ablations (KPN with representation learning, AdvKPN with advantage functions) both fail in the two most complex environments, confirming that the residual layer is essential for robustness in high-dimensional action spaces. In the supplementary Pusher and Reacher environments, ResKPN again achieves the best episodic reward in Pusher and matches DPO in Reacher while maintaining lower training variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of RKHS-based policy gradient methods (Paternain et al., 2020; Cervino et al., 2021) and deep kernel learning (Wilson et al., 2016), explicitly positioning itself as the first work to integrate neural network representation learning into RKHS policy gradient for continuous control. It repurposes the residual layer concept from ResNets (He et al., 2016) — originally designed to combat vanishing gradients — as a learned baseline function for variance reduction in the RKHS gradient, drawing an analogy to the advantage function in actor-critic methods. PPO and DPO techniques (clipping, drift functions) are adapted to the RKHS setting, but the core novelty lies in the combination of representation learning with a residual baseline specifically designed for kernel-space policy updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical barriers to deploying RKHS policies in complex environments — hyperparameter sensitivity and gradient variance — with a theoretically grounded solution that achieves up to 30% reward improvement over DPO in Humanoid. However, the computational cost is roughly 3× that of PPO/DPO (Table 4), and the inability to use minibatch RKHS gradients (quadratic scaling, Table 3) limits scalability. Open questions include whether kernel embedding techniques can enable batch updates, how the method extends to multi-agent settings, and whether the representation learning component can be made more efficient for very high-dimensional observation spaces.
