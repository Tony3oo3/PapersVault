## Problem

Existing model merging methods (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, AdaMerging) were designed for unimodal vision models and rely on static, input-agnostic fusion coefficients. They fail to address two unique challenges of Large Vision-Language Models: (1) identifying which submodules of the multi-component LVLM architecture (vision encoder, projector, LLM) should be adapted for efficient, reusable knowledge transfer, and (2) dynamically adjusting the merge based on the specific visual-linguistic input, since task discrepancies in LVLMs can arise from either the visual or linguistic modality or both.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard image-text pair (RGB image tokens concatenated with text instruction tokens) and produces a generated text response (caption, VQA answer, or multiple-choice selection). The modality-aware allocator processes the entire token sequence in a single forward pass to produce one global set of fusion weights for all recipe layers simultaneously, rather than routing tokens individually. The fused model then performs standard autoregressive text generation using the dynamically composed LoRA and projector parameters.

## Method

REMEDY consists of two stages. First, Recipe Construction defines reusable modules as the LoRA-adapted first two LLM Transformer layers plus the projector, based on empirical evidence that shallow layers capture visual-language understanding while deep layers merely mimic output style. Second, Recipe Merging introduces a modality-aware allocator comprising a self-attention token understanding component (computing modality-sensitive embeddings over the full input sequence) and a token-conditional weight generator (an MLP that maps these embeddings to a single normalized weight vector γ ∈ R^{S×(M×N+K)} in one shot). During inference, γ is used to linearly combine the LoRA updates and projector deltas from S task-specific recipes, yielding input-adaptive fused parameters. The allocator is trained with only ~1000 samples per seen task using standard autoregressive loss while keeping all recipe parameters fixed. Unlike conventional MoE routers, REMEDY generates one sequence-level weight set for all layers globally rather than per-token, per-layer routing, enabling efficient and context-aware cross-modal knowledge integration.

## Results

REMEDY achieves the best H-score of 69.25 and overall average of 71.76 across all 9 evaluation tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline LW AdaMerging (H-score 67.07, Avg 70.08) by +2.18 and +1.68 respectively. On seen tasks (TextVQA, SQA, COCO, Flickr30k), REMEDY reaches an average of 85.20, surpassing LW AdaMerging (84.74) and Simple Average (84.28). On unseen tasks (MM-Vet, MMB-CN, MMB-EN, VizWiz, POPE), REMEDY achieves 58.31, the only method to exceed the zero-shot baseline (57.79), while all other fusion methods degrade below zero-shot performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REMEDY outperforms all three compared fusion methods (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, AdaMerging) on both seen and unseen tasks. Against Task Arithmetic, REMEDY gains +2.68 on seen average (85.20 vs 82.52) and +3.20 on unseen average (58.31 vs 55.11). Against TIES-Merging, the margin is even larger: +8.40 on seen and +6.07 on unseen, with TIES-Merging suffering the most severe degradation on unseen tasks (52.24 vs zero-shot's 57.79). Against the strongest baseline LW AdaMerging, REMEDY leads by +0.46 on seen tasks and a critical +2.90 on unseen tasks, making it the only method that improves upon the zero-shot baseline on unseen benchmarks. Notably, all static fusion methods (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, both AdaMerging variants) underperform the zero-shot baseline on unseen tasks, whereas REMEDY's dynamic, input-conditioned allocation consistently surpasses it, demonstrating that static merging strategies overfit to seen-task knowledge at the expense of generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model merging paradigm established by Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022) and its successors (TIES-Merging, AdaMerging) from unimodal vision models to Large Vision-Language Models. The key departure is the introduction of 'recipes'—reusable modules comprising the projector and shallow LLM layers—replacing the full-model task vectors used in prior work, motivated by the multi-component architecture and limited task-specific data in LVLMs. The modality-aware allocator, which generates a single set of layer-wise fusion weights in one shot from multi-modal input tokens, distinguishes REMEDY from both static merging methods and token-wise MoE routers (e.g., LLaVA-MoLE, MoCLE) that require per-layer deployment and concurrent access to all task data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fine-tuning only the first two LLM layers plus the projector matches or exceeds full-model tuning provides a practical, low-cost recipe for LVLM adaptation that could reduce fine-tuning costs substantially. However, the method is validated only on LLaVA-1.5 with four seen tasks, and the zero-shot gains over the baseline are modest (e.g., +0.5 on MM-Vet, +0.6 on MMB-EN), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger task sets or other LVLM architectures. The paper itself acknowledges that recipe construction relies on empirical heuristics and that zero-shot generalization remains an area for further improvement, suggesting that dynamic, task-aware recipe identification is a natural next step.
