## Problem

Neural audio codecs have relied on convolutional or recurrent architectures with strong inductive biases, limiting their scalability to large parameter counts. Transformers, which scale effectively to billions of parameters in other domains, have not been deployed as the primary component of a codec model. This paper asks whether a predominantly transformer-based codec, scaled to ~1B parameters, can achieve state-of-the-art speech quality at ultra-low bitrates (400–700 bps) suitable for generative speech pipelines.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 16 kHz speech waveform as input, which is first decomposed via a polyphase (patched) transform into 320-sample patches to perform the bulk of temporal downsampling before entering the transformer encoder. The output is a 6-dimensional FSQ latent at 25 Hz, which can be tokenized as a single token per step (codebook size 6⁶ = 46,656 at 400 bps) or post-hoc decomposed into two residual tokens per step (codebook size 5⁶ = 15,625 each at 700 bps). The decoder reconstructs the waveform, and the model can also operate with the quantizer removed, yielding continuous latents for use with diffusion-based generative models.

## Method

The Transformer Audio AutoEncoder (TAAE) replaces the convolutional encoder/decoder of prior codecs with stacked transformer blocks (~950M parameters total) featuring sliding-window self-attention (window 128), RoPE positional embeddings, QK-norm, LayerScale, and gated SiLU feedforward layers, with a polyphase patching layer handling most of the temporal resampling. The discrete bottleneck uses a modified Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) scheme trained with a hybrid of uniform-noise quantization, straight-through gradients, and unmodified latents, enabling near-perfect codebook utilization and post-hoc adjustment of quantization levels. A key contribution is a post-training residual decomposition that splits the single FSQ token into a hierarchical set of lower-order tokens (constrained to L = 2ⁿ+1 levels), providing RVQ-like multi-stream structure without the codebook utilization problems of learned VQ. Training proceeds in two stages: pretraining with adversarial feature-matching loss plus exponentially decaying L1/STFT reconstruction losses, followed by finetuning with a WavLM-Large perceptual loss that proves essential for intelligibility. The discriminator uses unevenly-spaced STFT resolutions derived from the golden ratio to eliminate periodic spectral artifacts caused by systematic bias in standard power-of-two FFT configurations.

## Results

TAAE achieves state-of-the-art speech coding at ultra-low bitrates of 400 and 700 bps. At 700 bps, the model attains SI-SDR of 4.73, Mel distance of 0.86, STFT distance of 1.26, PESQ of 3.09, STOI of 0.92, and MOSNet of 3.36, outperforming all baselines on every objective metric. The MUSHRA subjective test with 24 participants confirms these results, with TAAE scoring close to the ground-truth reference and significantly outperforming all compared codecs. The model uses approximately 950M parameters with a 25 Hz latent rate, achieving 25 or 50 tokens per second depending on the FSQ configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAAE at 700 bps outperforms every baseline on all six objective metrics, despite operating at a substantially lower bitrate than most comparisons. Against Mimi (1100 bps, the strongest baseline), TAAE improves SI-SDR from 2.20 to 4.73, Mel distance from 0.94 to 0.86, STFT distance from 1.31 to 1.26, PESQ from 3.01 to 3.09, STOI from 0.90 to 0.92, and MOSNet from 3.24 to 3.36. Against Encodec at 3000 bps (nearly 4× the bitrate), TAAE still leads on SI-SDR (4.73 vs. 2.77), Mel (0.86 vs. 0.95), STFT (1.26 vs. 1.33), PESQ (3.09 vs. 2.84), and MOSNet (3.36 vs. 2.98), while matching on STOI (0.92 vs. 0.90). Against DAC at 2000 bps, TAAE dominates across all metrics (e.g., SI-SDR 4.73 vs. -0.37, PESQ 3.09 vs. 2.29). Against SemantiCodec at 675 bps (the closest bitrate), TAAE improves SI-SDR from – to 4.73, Mel from 0.98 to 0.86, STFT from 1.32 to 1.26, PESQ from 2.65 to 3.09, and STOI from 0.86 to 0.92. The HuBERT-based unitHiFi-GAN codec at 500 bps (Appendix A.7) performs far worse, with SI-SDR of -45.95 and STOI of 0.16, confirming that end-to-end waveform codec training substantially outperforms semantic-token-plus-vocoder pipelines for reconstruction fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant convolutional architecture paradigm in neural audio codecs (SoundStream, Encodec, DAC, Mimi) by making the transformer the primary encoder/decoder component, scaling to ~950M parameters. It replaces the standard Residual Vector Quantizer bottleneck with a modified Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) scheme (Mentzer et al., 2023) that avoids codebook utilization problems, and introduces a post-hoc residual decomposition of FSQ tokens. The adversarial training setup builds on Encodec's multi-resolution STFT discriminator (Defossez et al., 2022) and Mimi's simplified feature-matching loss (Defossez et al., 2024), while the polyphase/patched transform for input patching draws from signal processing filter-bank theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that transformer scaling laws transfer to speech coding, opening a clear route to higher-quality codecs through parameter count rather than architectural ingenuity. The near-perfect FSQ codebook utilization (0.97 normalized entropy vs. 0.78–0.92 for RVQ baselines) and the post-hoc residual decomposition offer practical flexibility for downstream generative models that struggle with hierarchical token streams. Key limitations include training on only 100k hours of 16 kHz English audiobook data, and the large parameter count may be prohibitive for resource-constrained deployments. Open questions include whether the architecture advantage persists at higher sampling rates (24+ kHz), with much larger and more diverse datasets, and whether the FSQ bottleneck can be further exploited for continuous (diffusion-based) generation.
