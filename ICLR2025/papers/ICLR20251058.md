## Problem

LoRA and its variants constrain the update matrix ΔW to a maximum rank of r, which limits expressiveness on complex tasks such as commonsense reasoning that require training on one dataset but evaluating across multiple sub-tasks. Raising r in LoRA to compensate increases computational cost and causes training instability (e.g., gradient explosion), conflicting with the parameter-efficiency goal. The paper asks whether a higher-rank update can be achieved under the PEFT budget without additional inference overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained LLM weight matrix W₀ (from Llama-2-7B or Llama-3-8B) and a text query, and produces an adapted weight matrix W' = W₀ + W₀⊙(AB) along with generated text. The trainable parameters A ∈ ℝ^{d×r} and B ∈ ℝ^{r×k} match LoRA's parameter count exactly for the same r, so no extra memory or inference cost is introduced. A is initialized to zero (ensuring the initial update is the zero matrix) while B uses Kaiming initialization; at deployment, the update is merged into W₀ and the original weights can be recovered by element-wise division by (AB+1).

## Method

HiRA formulates the update as ΔW = W₀ ⊙ (AB), where W₀ is the frozen pretrained weight and AB is a low-rank factorization with A ∈ ℝ^{d×r}, B ∈ ℝ^{r×k}. The key insight exploits the rank inequality Rank(P⊙Q) ≤ Rank(P)×Rank(Q): because W₀ is full-rank (rank r₀), the Hadamard product can achieve rank up to r₀·r, far exceeding the rank-r ceiling of LoRA's matrix product L₁L₂. The forward pass becomes h = W₀x + (W₀⊙(AB))x with the same O(drk) complexity as LoRA, and the gradients are modulated element-wise by W₀, allowing pretrained knowledge to guide adaptation. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that HiRA's expressive power is bounded by σ_{r+1}(Ē⊘W₀)·‖W₀‖₂ rather than σ_{r+1}(Ē) as in LoRA, and shows empirically that the effective rank of ΔW reaches ~2837 versus ~32 for LoRA at the same parameter budget.

## Results

On the commonsense reasoning benchmark (8 sub-tasks), HiRA(r=32) achieves 86.72% average accuracy on Llama-3-8B, surpassing the best baseline DoRA (85.20%) by 1.52 points and LoRA (80.79%) by 5.93 points. On Llama-2-7B, HiRA(r=32) reaches 81.42% average accuracy vs. DoRA's 79.69%. Notably, HiRA(r=16) with half the trainable parameters (0.3513%) still achieves 86.08% on Llama-3-8B, exceeding LoRA(r=32) at 80.79%. On GSM8K mathematical reasoning, HiRA(r=32) reaches 70.81% accuracy vs. LoRA's 65.89% and DoRA's 66.12%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiRA consistently outperforms all six baselines across all three task suites. Against LoRA(r=32), HiRA(r=32) gains +5.93 points average accuracy on commonsense reasoning (86.72% vs. 80.79%) and +4.92 points on GSM8K (70.81% vs. 65.89%). Against DoRA(r=32), the closest competitor, HiRA leads by +1.52 points on commonsense reasoning and +4.69 points on GSM8K. MoRA(r=32) underperforms both LoRA and HiRA on commonsense reasoning (78.63% average), largely due to a catastrophic drop on HellaSwag (43.53% vs. 95.36% for HiRA), likely from its complex compression/decompression functions. Prompt Tuning and P-Tuning are far behind, scoring 36.96% and 18.11% respectively on commonsense reasoning, confirming that input-level adaptation is insufficient for complex reasoning. On ConvAI2 dialogue generation, HiRA(r=32) achieves 47.80% average score vs. DoRA's 46.62% and LoRA's 46.59%, with HiRA(r=16) at 47.79% using half the parameters. Remarkably, HiRA(r=32) on Llama-3-8B (86.72%) exceeds the ChatGPT reference (77.01%) by nearly 10 points on commonsense reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiRA directly extends LoRA by replacing the matrix multiplication between two low-rank factors with a Hadamard product against the frozen pretrained weight matrix W0, making LoRA a special case (when R is the all-ones matrix). The theoretical analysis builds on Zeng & Lee's (2024) expressive power framework for LoRA, extending the approximation bound to the Hadamard-product setting. Conceptually, HiRA departs from MoRA's approach to high-rank adaptation, which relies on complex static compression/decompression functions that complicate weight merging, in favor of a simpler element-wise operation that merges seamlessly into pretrained weights. The Hadamard product has appeared in adjacent PEFT work (FLoRA for batched adapters, PACE for multiplicative noise), but HiRA is the first to exploit its rank-multiplication property specifically to elevate the update matrix rank under a fixed parameter budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HiRA offers a drop-in replacement for LoRA with identical computational cost and parameter count but substantially higher update-matrix rank (2837 vs. 32 empirically), which translates to consistent 1–2% accuracy gains over DoRA across commonsense reasoning, dialogue, and math tasks. The ablation showing that a random R collapses performance to near-chance (37.63% vs. 86.72%) underscores that the pretrained weights carry essential structural information for effective adaptation, a finding with broader implications for how PEFT methods should interact with frozen parameters. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at larger model scales (beyond 8B), whether the NP-hard weighted low-rank approximation underlying the theoretical bound can be addressed with better optimization, and how HiRA interacts with other emerging PEFT techniques such as mixture-of-experts adapters or quantized fine-tuning.
