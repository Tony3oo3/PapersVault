## Problem

Existing inductive graph models assume training and test graphs share the same feature and label spaces, limiting them to a fixed domain. This paper introduces the fully-inductive setup, where a model must perform node classification on arbitrary test graphs with entirely new structures, feature dimensions, and label spaces without any additional training or fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250864/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is trained on a single graph (e.g., Wisconsin with 120 labeled nodes) and then applied to any new graph that may have different feature dimensionality d', different number of classes c', and arbitrary permuted feature/label orders. At inference, it takes the adjacency matrix, node features, and a small set of labeled nodes on the new graph, and produces soft class predictions for all unlabeled nodes in a single forward pass with no gradient steps.

## Method

GraphAny combines two components: (1) multiple LinearGNNs that replace cross-entropy training with a mean-squared-error objective, yielding a closed-form analytical solution W* = F_L⁺ Y_L for the linear classifier weights, so no gradient descent is needed at inference; five variants use different graph convolution operators (identity, 1-hop and 2-hop SGC low-pass, 1-hop and 2-hop HGC high-pass). (2) An inductive attention module that fuses the t LinearGNN predictions per node via learned attention scores, where the attention input consists of pairwise squared distances between LinearGNN predictions, normalized by an entropy-matching procedure that adaptively rescales distances to a fixed entropy H. This design guarantees permutation invariance to feature and label dimension reordering and dimensional robustness across graphs with varying numbers of classes, enabling the single trained attention MLP to transfer to any new graph.

## Results

GraphAny, trained on a single graph (e.g., Wisconsin with only 120 labeled nodes), achieves a 67.26% average test accuracy across 31 diverse node classification datasets, surpassing transductive baselines (GCN: 66.55%, GAT: 67.03%) that are individually trained on each dataset. On the 27 held-out (fully-inductive) datasets, GraphAny reaches 67.31% average accuracy, outperforming GCN (65.55%) and GAT (65.31%) by 1.8 and 2.0 percentage points respectively. The best non-parametric baseline, LinearSGC2, achieves 64.41% total average, trailing GraphAny by ~2.9 points. GraphAny also delivers a 2.95× speedup in total wall time (6.37 min vs. 18.80 min for GCN across all 31 graphs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphAny (trained on a single graph) outperforms GCN (66.55%) and GAT (67.03%) on the 31-graph total average, despite those transductive models being individually trained and hyperparameter-tuned on each dataset. The gap is most pronounced on the 27 held-out datasets, where GraphAny (67.31–67.66%) leads GCN (65.55%) by ~1.8–2.1 points and GAT (65.31%) by ~2.0–2.4 points, confirming that the advantage comes from inductive generalization rather than transductive fitting. Against the strongest non-parametric baseline LinearSGC2 (64.41% total), GraphAny gains ~2.9–3.1 points, demonstrating the value of the learned inductive attention over a single fixed convolution kernel. Against ACM-GCN (66.92% held-out on 25 graphs, from Appendix E), GraphAny is within 1–2% while requiring only 4 GPU hours versus 672 GPU hours for ACM-GNN hyperparameter search. Notably, GraphAny's performance is remarkably consistent regardless of which single training graph is used (Cora, Wisconsin, Arxiv, or Products), with total averages ranging only from 67.00% to 67.48%.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphAny extends the inductive node classification line (Hamilton et al. 2017; Hang et al. 2021; Qu et al. 2022) by removing the assumption that training and test graphs share feature and label spaces, introducing the 'fully-inductive' setup. Methodologically, it repurposes SGC-style linear graph convolutions (Wu et al., 2019) by replacing gradient-trained weights with a closed-form pseudo-inverse solution (LinearGNN), and adapts entropy normalization from t-SNE/SNE (Hinton & Roweis, 2002; van der Maaten & Hinton, 2008) to construct permutation-invariant, dimension-robust attention features over multiple LinearGNN predictions. The paper positions itself as the first step toward graph foundation models that transfer across arbitrary domains without fine-tuning, contrasting with prior GFMs that assume a shared input space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fully-inductive formulation opens a practical path for cross-domain graph transfer (e.g., knowledge graphs → e-commerce) without per-graph retraining, and the 2.95× speedup over GCN makes it attractive for large-scale deployment. However, the model still requires labeled nodes on the test graph (semi-supervised), and the performance margin over per-graph transductive baselines is modest (~1%), so the practical advantage is primarily in eliminating retraining overhead rather than raw accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach extends to link prediction or graph-level tasks, whether the attention module can be made more expressive without sacrificing inductive generalization, and how it scales to graphs with millions of edges where pre-processing O(|E|) becomes a bottleneck.
