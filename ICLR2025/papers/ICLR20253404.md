## Problem

Existing RNA language models apply standard cross-entropy pre-training that treats all codon prediction errors equally, ignoring the biological reality that synonymous codons (encoding the same amino acid) are far less consequential than non-synonymous ones. This mismatch between the loss function and mRNA's inherent codon hierarchy leads to suboptimal representations for downstream mRNA tasks, and no publicly available mRNA-specific language model incorporates this structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253404/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes mRNA sequences tokenized at the codon level (64 codon tokens plus special tokens for start, stop, padding, mask, and unknown), with a maximum pre-training context of 444 codon tokens (~1332 nucleotides) and positional embeddings supporting up to 2048 tokens. For property prediction, frozen model embeddings are fed to a 1M-parameter TextCNN probe for regression; for generation, the first third of a sequence conditions autoregressive production of the remaining two-thirds; for region annotation, the model predicts seven boundary positions (start/end of V, SP, DJ, and start of C regions) across a fixed 902-nucleotide window.

## Method

HELM replaces the standard cross-entropy loss in MLM or CLM pre-training with a Hierarchical Cross-Entropy (HXE) loss that factorizes the codon prediction distribution along a biologically grounded tree: root → {coding, non-coding} → {start/stop} or {20 amino acids} → synonymous codons. The loss assigns per-level weights λ(C) = exp(−α·h(C)), where h(C) is the node height, so that mispredicting the amino acid (higher in the tree) incurs a larger penalty than mispredicting among synonymous codons (lower in the tree). This requires no architectural or tokenization changes—only the loss function is modified—allowing it to plug into standard Transformer (GPT-2), Mamba, or Hyena backbones. The best-performing configuration uses α=0.2, codon-level tokenization, and a 50M-parameter Transformer pre-trained for 40 epochs on 15.3M curated OAS antibody mRNA sequences, with a reduced learning rate of 1e-4 to accommodate the smaller-scale HXE loss.

## Results

Using Spearman rank correlation as the primary metric, HELM (Transformer with hierarchical cross-entropy loss) achieves 0.767 on Ab1, 0.609 on Ab2, 0.701 on MLOS, 0.525 on iCodon, 0.626 on Tc-Riboswitches, 0.822 on mRFP, and 0.833 on COV-19 Vaccine (MLM variant). HELM outperforms its non-hierarchical XE counterpart by an average of ~8% across all seven downstream property prediction tasks and the antibody region annotation task (83.39% vs 78.68% accuracy for MLM). The best non-hierarchical baseline (Transformer XE MLM) already surpasses the strongest external foundation model (CodonBERT) by 5–17 percentage points, and HELM extends this lead further.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CodonBERT (the only publicly available mRNA LM, 100M params), HELM-MLM outperforms on all seven tasks, with the largest gap on MLOS (0.701 vs 0.543, +15.8 pts) and iCodon (0.525 vs 0.350, +17.5 pts), while the smallest gap is on mRFP (0.822 vs 0.770, +5.2 pts). Against RNA-FM (100M params, general-purpose RNA model), HELM-MLM wins on all five overlapping tasks by 6–29.4 percentage points (e.g., 0.822 vs 0.527 on mRFP). Against SpliceBERT (20M params, pre-trained on pre-mRNA), HELM-MLM wins on all five overlapping tasks by 1.7–23.0 percentage points (e.g., 0.626 vs 0.418 on Tc-Riboswitches). The paper's own non-hierarchical Transformer XE models already beat all three external baselines by 5–17 points, confirming that the curated OAS pre-training data and codon tokenization are major contributors; HELM then adds a further ~2–5 point improvement over XE, with the largest gains on datasets exhibiting strong synonymous codon usage bias (MLOS, Tc-Riboswitches, mRFP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Hierarchical Cross-Entropy (HXE) loss, originally proposed for image classification (Bertinetto et al., CVPR 2020), to the domain of mRNA language modeling by formalizing the codon-to-amino-acid hierarchy as a tree structure and modulating the MLM/CLM loss accordingly. It departs from standard bio-LM pre-training (vanilla cross-entropy with Transformer, Mamba, or Hyena architectures) and from existing RNA foundation models (RNA-FM, SpliceBERT, CodonBERT) that treat all codon prediction errors equally, ignoring the biological reality that synonymous codons encode the same amino acid. The work also contributes a systematic empirical comparison of tokenization strategies, architectures, and pre-training objectives for mRNA, filling a gap the authors identify in the literature where such comparisons have been absent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HELM provides a minimal-modification pre-training strategy (loss reweighting only, no architecture change) that consistently improves mRNA property prediction by ~8% and enhances generative quality, making it a practical tool for mRNA vaccine and antibody design pipelines. The curated 15.3M-sequence OAS mRNA dataset addresses a real data scarcity problem for mRNA-specific pre-training. Key open questions include whether hyperbolic embedding spaces (which naturally represent tree hierarchies) could further improve over the Euclidean-space HXE formulation, and whether the hierarchical prior remains beneficial at much larger model scales beyond the 100M parameter experiments shown here.
