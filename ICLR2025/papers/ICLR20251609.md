## Problem

Long-horizon robotic manipulation requires decomposing complex tasks into reusable sub-skills, but existing approaches either depend on costly human annotations and predefined skills that may misalign with the robot's physical configuration, or use self-supervised methods that capture low-level action discretization rather than higher-level motor-skill abstractions. This paper addresses the gap by autonomously discovering semantically meaningful manipulation concepts (analogous to grasping, pushing, pulling) directly from unlabeled proprioceptive demonstrations, without any human semantic labels or task-specific skill definitions.

## Input / Output

The concept discovery module consumes only 9-dimensional proprioceptive states (gripper position, rotation, opening) from 950 unlabeled demonstrations per task, producing a codebook of 30 discrete manipulation concept embeddings via VQ-VAE. The downstream policy takes front-view and wrist-view RGB images (112×112 each) plus the proprioceptive state as input, and outputs an 8-step action sequence generated by a DDPM denoising process conditioned on the full probability distribution over manipulation concepts (not just the argmax). The observation window spans 4 timesteps, and the entire pipeline is trained on mixed-task demonstrations with task-type information omitted during concept discovery.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, Automatic Concept Discovery (ACD) uses a VQ-VAE encoder to assign each timestep to one of K=30 learnable concept embeddings, refined by three self-supervised strategies: Goal State Detection (a network predicts the proprioceptive state at the next concept-switch boundary, providing a segmentation signal), Goal State Evaluation (a hypernetwork-based value function scores alignment and completion of a state toward its concept's goal, with a gradient-field constraint enforcing monotonic progress), and Goal Consolidation (a reconstruction network maps concept embeddings back to states, enforcing intra-concept consistency). Second, the Closed-Loop Concept-Guided Policy jointly trains a Concept Selection Transformer (CST, 16-layer transformer) that predicts a full distribution over concepts from visual and proprioceptive inputs, and a Concept-Guided Policy (CGP, U-Net-based Diffusion Policy with FiLM conditioning) that denoises actions conditioned on this distribution. The CST's soft distribution (rather than a hard argmax) is injected into the CGP's diffusion process, enabling end-to-end co-training where action prediction loss back-propagates to refine concept selection.

## Results

On the MimicGen benchmark (Robosuite), AutoCGP achieves the highest success rate across nearly all task–difficulty combinations, with particularly large gains on harder settings: 0.72 on Coffee D2 (vs. 0.40 for DP, 0.58 for InfoCon+DP) and 0.50 on Mug D1 (vs. 0.28 for DP, 0.40 for XSkill+DP). On the easiest setting (Coffee D0), AutoCGP reaches 0.98, compared to 0.80 for the standard Diffusion Policy and 0.90 for InfoCon+DP. The Threading task remains extremely challenging for all methods (≤0.04 success rate), but AutoCGP is the only method to achieve any success at D2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoCGP outperforms all five baselines on the majority of task–difficulty cells. Against the standard Diffusion Policy (DP), the gains are most pronounced on harder settings: +0.32 on Coffee D2 (0.72 vs. 0.40) and +0.22 on Mug D1 (0.50 vs. 0.28), while on easier settings the margin narrows (e.g., +0.18 on Coffee D0). Among the concept-discovery baselines, InfoCon+DP is the closest competitor on Coffee D0 (0.90 vs. 0.98) and Threading D0 (0.72 vs. 0.80), but AutoCGP leads by 0.14–0.22 on the more variable D1/D2 levels. XSkill+DP matches AutoCGP on Stk.3 D1 (0.76 vs. 0.72, where XSkill actually edges ahead) and is competitive on 3Pc. D0 (0.74 vs. 0.82), yet falls behind by 0.10–0.22 on most other cells. AWE+DP and BeT are consistently the weakest baselines, with BeT trailing AutoCGP by 0.10–0.50 across tasks, confirming that action-discretization-based representations (BeT) and heuristic waypoint extraction (AWE) capture less useful structure than the self-supervised manipulation concepts discovered by AutoCGP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VQ-VAE for discrete representation learning and Diffusion Policy for action generation, but repurposes the VQ-VAE codebook not for action discretization (as in BeT/VQ-BeT) but for discovering higher-level manipulation concepts from proprioceptive states. It departs from prior skill-discovery methods (InfoCon, XSkill, AWE) by integrating the discovered concepts into a closed-loop policy where a Concept Selection Transformer dynamically adjusts the active concept at runtime, rather than using concepts as fixed open-loop sub-goal labels. The self-supervised discovery from proprioception alone, without human-annotated skill boundaries or language labels, distinguishes it from hierarchical imitation learning approaches that rely on manual segmentation or LLM-generated annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependency on human-annotated skill labels and predefined primitives, this framework offers a more scalable path to long-horizon manipulation that adapts to environmental variability without re-annotation. Key limitations include the fixed codebook size (predefined K concepts), evaluation restricted to single-arm tabletop tasks in simulation, and no testing on more complex morphologies like humanoids. Open questions raised by the authors include integrating multi-modal observations (beyond proprioception), developing automated metrics for concept quality beyond task success rate, and extending to learning-from-play settings where no task objectives are specified at all.
