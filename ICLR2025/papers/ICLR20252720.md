## Problem

Previous Video Text Spotting methods rely on local (adjacent-frame) associations and IoU-based positional distances, treating text tracking like generic multi-object tracking. However, text in videos is morphologically more stable than typical MOT objects, undergoes rapid translations from camera motion that weaken positional cues, and benefits from long-range temporal consistency — characteristics that local matching and IoU fail to exploit.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames resized with the shorter side fixed and aspect ratio preserved, processing them through a ResNet-50 + FPN backbone. It outputs 4-point polygon coordinates per detected text, persistent trajectory IDs linking texts across frames, and recognized text strings. During inference, a global embedding pool with a sliding window (default 8 frames) stores historical tracking embeddings, so each frame is associated against all prior texts in the window rather than only the immediately preceding frame.

## Method

GLOMA replaces local frame-to-frame matching with a Transformer-based global association module: a one-layer encoder processes all historical embeddings in the global pool, and a cross-attention decoder computes similarity scores between current-frame queries and all historical tracklets, trained with a tracklet-level log-likelihood loss. For positional association, the paper models each rotated bounding box as a 2D Gaussian distribution and computes the closed-form Wasserstein distance between pairs, capturing both center displacement and shape/angle differences in a single metric that outperforms IoU under fast camera motion. Tracking embeddings are extracted via Rotated RoIAlign followed by a shallow conv + LSTM + FC stack, supervised by CTC recognition loss to inject semantic discriminability. The final association score is the element-wise maximum of the Transformer tracklet probability and the Wasserstein positional score, with a Hungarian algorithm enforcing unique ID assignment. The full system is trained end-to-end with a multi-task loss (YOLOX detection + CTC recognition + tracking log-likelihood) using learnable balancing parameters, pretrained on COCOText and fine-tuned on target datasets.

## Results

On ICDAR2015 video, GLOMA achieves 56.0 MOTA, 77.4 MOTP, and 70.5 IDF1, surpassing the previous SOTA (CoText) by 4.6 MOTA and the previous Transformer-based method (TransDETR) by 8.3 MOTA. On ICDAR2013 video, GLOMA reaches 56.3 MOTA and 78.7 MOTP, leading all baselines on every metric. On the Minetto dataset, GLOMA attains 87.1 MOTA and 84.2 IDF1, again achieving the best results across all evaluated metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLOMA outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the previous SOTA CoText (Wu et al. 2022b), GLOMA gains +4.6 MOTA on ICDAR2015 video (56.0 vs 51.4), +0.5 MOTA on ICDAR2013 video (56.3 vs 55.8), and +0.2 MOTA on Minetto (87.1 vs 86.9). Against the previous Transformer-based TransDETR (Wu et al. 2022a), the margins are larger: +8.3 MOTA on ICDAR2015 video (56.0 vs 47.7), +1.6 MOTA on ICDAR2013 video (56.3 vs 54.7), and +3.0 MOTA on Minetto (87.1 vs 84.1). The older local-association methods (SVRep, SAVTD, Free, TransVTSpotter, AJOU) are all surpassed by 6.5–39.6 MOTA on ICDAR2015 video. GLOMA also leads on MOTP (77.4 vs 73.6 for CoText) and IDF1 (70.5 vs 68.6 for CoText) on ICDAR2015 video, indicating improvements in both box fitting and ID consistency. On the Video Text Spotting task (Table 2), GLOMA further achieves 62.5 MOTA, beating TransDETR(aug) by 1.6 MOTA and CoText by 3.5 MOTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLOMA builds directly on the Global Tracking Transformer (Zhou et al., 2022b) architecture, adapting its global embedding pool and Transformer encoder-decoder from general MOT to the video text spotting domain. It departs from the dominant paradigm in prior VTS work (CoText, TransDETR, TransVTSpotter) that relies on bilateral adjacent-frame matching with IoU-based positional distance and complex cascaded post-processing, instead performing simultaneous associations across a sliding window of historical frames. The morphological association via Gaussian Wasserstein distance is repurposed from rotated object detection (Yang et al., 2021) and applied here for the first time to text trajectory linking, capturing shape stability that IoU ignores under fast camera motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.6 MOTA gain over the previous SOTA and 8.3 over the prior Transformer-based method demonstrate that global temporal modeling and shape-aware distance are substantially more effective than local IoU matching for text tracking, particularly in crowded scenes and under fast camera movement. The paper identifies a clear limitation: the YOLOX-based detector remains fragile under severe motion blur and extreme text sizes, causing trajectory fractures that the tracking module cannot fully recover from. An open direction the authors flag is feeding trajectory information back into the detection head to close this gap, which could further improve robustness in real-world video conditions.
