## Problem

Masked Frequency Modeling (MFM) for self-supervised visual pre-training has two fundamental limitations: it uses constant low/high-pass filters with a fixed radius that ignore each image's unique frequency structure, resulting in a less challenging reconstruction task; and it only exposes the model to frequency-masked images during pre-training, leaving the model unfamiliar with natural image distributions and requiring substantially more data to adapt during fine-tuning, particularly in few-shot scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253540/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (224×224 for classification, 512×512 for segmentation) and converts them to grayscale solely for generating a shared frequency filter across all three channels. The student model receives spatial-domain frequency-masked images (restored via inverse FFT after filtering), while the teacher receives spatially augmented original images (random crop + flip). During pre-training, outputs are reconstructed masked frequency spectra and teacher-matched feature representations; during fine-tuning, the model produces class labels (ImageNet-1K) or pixel-level segmentation masks (ADE20K).

## Method

FOLK introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, it replaces MFM's constant filters with adaptive Com and RCom filters inspired by Fourier image compression: the Com filter retains the highest-magnitude frequency components (masking finer details), while RCom does the opposite (masking dominant semantics), with the threshold randomly sampled from [0.005, 0.01, 0.05] and the filter type chosen with 50% probability each. Second, it employs a teacher-student self-distillation framework where the teacher (updated via EMA) processes original spatially-augmented views and the student processes frequency-masked views; the student is trained with a combined loss of MFM reconstruction (predicting masked frequencies) and a distillation loss (matching the teacher's softmax-normalized feature representations from the other original view). The framework is architecture-agnostic, supporting both ViT (patch tokens to MFM head, [CLS] to distillation head) and CNN (feature maps to MFM head, average-pooled features to distillation head) backbones.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K full fine-tuning with ViT-S/16, FOLK achieves 81.6% top-1 accuracy after 300 pre-training epochs, matching MFM and surpassing MAE (80.6%), BEiT (81.3%), AttMask (81.3%), MoCo V3 (81.4%), and DINO (81.5%). With ViT-B/16, FOLK reaches 83.4% at 300 epochs and 84.0% at 800 epochs, tying iBOT (84.0% at 1600 epochs) while using fewer pre-training epochs. In the few-shot setting (10% labeled data, 200 fine-tuning epochs), FOLK achieves a mean accuracy of 67.2% (300-epoch pre-training) and 71.2% (800-epoch pre-training), substantially outperforming MFM (52.7%), iBOT (45.7%), and AttMask (57.4%). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, FOLK attains 45.5 mIoU with 800-epoch pre-training, slightly exceeding iBOT's 45.4 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOLK matches MFM on ViT-S (81.6% both at 300 epochs) but outperforms it on ViT-B (83.4% vs. 83.1%), and clearly surpasses MFM+R/Com (81.4%/83.2%) which uses the same Com/RCom filters without the self-distillation component. Against spatial-domain MIM methods, FOLK beats MAE by 1.0 points (81.6% vs. 80.6%), BEiT by 0.3 points (81.6% vs. 81.3%), and AttMask by 0.3 points (81.6% vs. 81.3%) on ViT-S, all without requiring masked tokens or external data. FOLK also edges out contrastive methods MoCo V3 (81.4%) and DINO (81.5%) on ViT-S. The most dramatic advantage appears in few-shot learning: FOLK's mean accuracy of 67.2% (300-epoch pre-training) exceeds MFM by 14.5 points, AttMask by 9.8 points, and iBOT by 21.5 points, demonstrating that the self-distillation component is critical for data-efficient adaptation. On semantic segmentation (ADE20K), FOLK achieves 45.5 mIoU, marginally surpassing iBOT (45.4) and MFM (44.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

FOLK directly extends Masked Frequency Modeling (MFM, Xie et al., 2023), which introduced frequency-domain masking as an alternative to spatial-domain MIM, by replacing MFM's fixed-radius low/high-pass filters with adaptive Com/RCom filters inspired by Fourier image compression. It further departs from MFM's single-model design by incorporating a self-distillation teacher-student framework borrowed from DINO (Caron et al., 2021) and BYOL (Grill et al., 2020), where the teacher sees unmasked images while the student reconstructs from frequency-masked views. The adaptive masking idea is conceptually analogous to AttMask (Kakogeorgiou et al., 2022), which uses attention maps to select the most informative spatial patches to hide, but FOLK transplants this principle into the frequency domain using magnitude-based selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the large few-shot learning gap: FOLK achieves 71.2% top-1 with 10% ImageNet data versus 52.7% for MFM, a ~19-point improvement attributable to the teacher's exposure to natural images during pre-training. This suggests that the distribution mismatch between frequency-masked pre-training inputs and natural fine-tuning inputs is a previously underappreciated bottleneck in frequency-based SSL. However, full-data classification gains over MFM are modest (81.6% vs. 81.4–81.6%), and the method's advantage over strong spatial-domain baselines like iBOT (84.0% at 1600 epochs) is limited. Open questions include whether the adaptive filtering and distillation gains persist at larger model scales (ViT-L/H) or with more diverse pre-training data, and whether the frequency-domain approach offers advantages over spatial MIM in settings beyond few-shot learning.
