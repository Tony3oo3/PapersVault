## Problem

No white-box jailbreak framework existed for large audio-language models (ALMs), whose audio encoders introduce non-differentiable discretization (K-Means tokenization) that shatters gradients and blocks standard gradient-based optimization. Additionally, ALMs exhibit high behavioral variability across models and queries, complicating adversarial target selection, and enforcing stealthiness on adversarial audio further shrinks the already non-convex feasible solution space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful audio query (e.g., 'how to make a bomb') synthesized from text via OpenAI TTS, along with a fixed text instruction template, and produces an adversarial audio waveform constructed by appending a short environmental-noise suffix (car horn, dog bark, air-conditioner hum) to the original waveform. The original speech syllables remain unaltered; only the appended suffix is optimized, ensuring semantic stealthiness. The adversarial waveform is evaluated against three ALMs (SpeechGPT, Qwen2-Audio, Llama-Omni) on 520 audio queries from AdvBench-Audio.

## Method

AdvWave introduces a dual-phase optimization to circumvent gradient shattering: Phase I directly optimizes the discrete audio token vector (bypassing the non-differentiable argmin tokenization) using cross-entropy adversarial loss against an adaptively searched target; Phase II then optimizes the continuous audio waveform via a triplet alignment loss that pulls each encoded frame toward its target cluster center while pushing it away from others. An adaptive adversarial target search algorithm dynamically selects the optimization target per query and per model by detoxifying the harmful query into a benign counterpart, collecting the ALM's response to the benign audio, and using GPT-4o to extract and re-fit the model's response pattern back onto the original malicious query. For stealthiness, a classifier-guided penalty term (cross-entropy against a target environmental-noise label, implemented with Qwen2-Audio as the classifier) is added to the Phase II objective, steering the adversarial suffix to resemble natural urban sounds without sacrificing attack success rate.

## Results

On the AdvBench-Audio dataset (520 audio queries), AdvWave achieves an average word-level attack success rate (ASR-W) of 0.838 and semantics-level attack success rate (ASR-L) of 0.746 across SpeechGPT, Qwen2-Audio, and Llama-Omni, representing over 50% improvement over the strongest baseline (AutoDAN-Trans: 0.536 ASR-W, 0.322 ASR-L). On the most challenging model, SpeechGPT, AdvWave achieves 0.643 ASR-W versus 0.179 for GCG-Trans, a 200%+ relative gain. Stealthiness scores (S_stealth) remain above 0.700 for AdvWave on all three models, while baselines drop to 0.232–0.485 due to audible artifacts in their transferred audio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three baselines are text-domain jailbreak methods (GCG, BEAST, AutoDAN) transferred to audio via OpenAI TTS, since no native ALM jailbreak existed prior to this work. AdvWave dominates on every model and every metric: on SpeechGPT it achieves 0.643 ASR-W vs. 0.179 (GCG-Trans), 0.075 (BEAST-Trans), and 0.004 (AutoDAN-Trans); on Qwen2-Audio it reaches 0.891 ASR-W vs. 0.648 (AutoDAN-Trans, the strongest baseline there); and on Llama-Omni it attains 0.981 ASR-W vs. 0.957 (AutoDAN-Trans). In terms of stealthiness, AdvWave scores 0.704–0.723 across models, substantially outperforming all baselines (0.232–0.485), because the transferred text jailbreaks produce audible gibberish or heavily augmented audio when spoken. The gap is most dramatic on SpeechGPT, where AdvWave's dual-phase optimization and adaptive target search overcome the model's strong instruction tuning, yielding a >200% relative ASR improvement over the best baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first white-box jailbreak framework for large audio-language models (ALMs), explicitly noting that no prior work addresses this setting. It builds conceptually on text-based jailbreak attacks (GCG, BEAST, AutoDAN) and DNN-based audio adversarial attacks, but departs from both: unlike text jailbreaks, it must handle non-differentiable K-Means tokenization in audio encoders (gradient shattering) and enforce perceptual stealthiness on waveforms; unlike DNN audio attacks targeting classifiers, it targets generative ALM backbones. The dual-phase optimization (discrete token optimization followed by waveform alignment via triplet loss) is a novel decomposition, and the classifier-guided stealthiness penalty borrows from diffusion model guidance but applies it to environmental sound classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that SOTA ALMs can be jailbroken with high success rates (up to 98% ASR-W on Llama-Omni) while producing perceptually natural adversarial audio, this work highlights an urgent safety gap before widespread ALM deployment. Key open questions include whether cross-modality safety alignment offers advantages over single-modality alignment, whether audio-specific safety concerns (erotic/violent tones, speech copyright, trait-based discrimination) require dedicated defenses, and whether the white-box techniques transfer to black-box settings. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4o for adaptive target search, evaluation on only three models, and the white-box assumption that may overstate real-world threat.
