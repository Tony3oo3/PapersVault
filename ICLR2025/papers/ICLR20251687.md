## Problem

Soft mixtures of experts (SoftMoEs) were recently shown to enable parameter scaling in value-based deep RL, but the specific architectural component responsible for the gains remained unclear. The prevailing hypothesis attributed the benefit to structured sparsity from multiple experts, yet performance gains persisted even with a single expert, contradicting this explanation. This paper isolates the true driver through systematic ablation of each SoftMoE component.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251687/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes stacked pixel frames from pixel-based environments (Atari 2600, Procgen, CALE) and produces Q-value estimates for action selection. The critical architectural detail is how the 3D convolutional encoder output of shape [h, w, d] is handled: instead of the standard flattening to a vector of length h×w×d, it is reshaped into a 2D token matrix (e.g., [h×w, d] for PerConv or [d, h×w] for PerFeat) and processed through MoE modules before being aggregated (via sum or mean) into the final linear layer.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic ablation of five SoftMoE components—combined token routing, expert specialization, expert width, network depth, and tokenization—using Rainbow with the Impala ResNet encoder on 20 Atari games. The key finding is that tokenizing the encoder output (preserving its spatial structure as distinct tokens rather than flattening) is the primary driver of performance gains, not the use of multiple experts. A single expert with scaled hidden dimensionality and tokenized input matches or exceeds the performance of SoftMoE-4, and even non-combined expert-choice routing with one expert achieves comparable results. PerConv tokenization (maintaining spatial structure) significantly outperforms shuffled tokenization, confirming that preserving the convolutional encoder's spatial layout is essential. The authors further show that experts are largely redundant (pruning two of four experts causes no performance drop) and explore periodic resets and Shrink-and-Perturb to improve expert utilization, with mixed results across agents.

## Results

The paper's central finding is that a single scaled expert (SoftMoE-1) achieves IQM performance comparable to SoftMoE-4 (four experts) on Rainbow with the Impala ResNet architecture across 20 Atari games (5 seeds, 200M steps), demonstrating that tokenization—not multiple experts—drives SoftMoE's efficacy. Adding tokenization alone to the baseline (Rainbow-lite) yields statistically significant IQM, median, and mean improvements over the standard flattened architecture. PerConv tokenization (preserving spatial structure) outperforms PerFeat, PerPatch, and Shuffled variants, confirming that maintaining the convolutional encoder's spatial layout is critical. Results are consistent across all 60 Atari games, the standard CNN encoder, Procgen, and CALE continuous control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard Rainbow baseline (flattened encoder output), SoftMoE-1 with a 4×-scaled expert matches the performance of SoftMoE-4, and even a tokenized baseline (without any MoE module) shows significant IQM gains, indicating that the flattening operation itself is the primary bottleneck. Against DQN, SoftMoE-1 does not provide significant improvements over the baseline, likely due to DQN's inherent training instability, in contrast to Rainbow and DER where gains are clear. Against DER, SoftMoE-1 achieves performance comparable to SoftMoE-4, confirming the tokenization finding generalizes across value-based agents. Against SAC on the CALE continuous control environment, tokenization (summing over the first dimension after reshaping) provides a measurable improvement over the standard flattened encoder, suggesting the finding extends beyond value-based methods to actor-critic agents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SoftMoE architecture introduced by Puigcerver et al. (2024) and its application to RL scaling by Obando Ceron et al. (2024), which attributed performance gains to structured sparsity from multiple experts. It departs from that hypothesis by systematically ablating each SoftMoE component and identifying tokenization of the convolutional encoder output—rather than expert multiplicity—as the primary driver of gains. The work also connects to the broader DQN/Impala architecture lineage (Mnih et al., 2015; Espeholt et al., 2018), where flattening the encoder output has been the standard practice, and to network plasticity techniques (Shrink-and-Perturb, periodic resets) explored for improving expert utilization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single scaled expert with tokenized inputs matches multi-expert SoftMoE performance has immediate practical implications: it suggests practitioners can simplify MoE-based RL architectures while retaining gains, and that the long-standing practice of flattening CNN outputs in pixel-based RL is suboptimal. However, the results do not transfer to DQN (potentially due to its instability) and actor-critic evidence is still preliminary (only initial SAC/CALE experiments). Open questions include whether tokenization benefits generalize to non-pixel-based environments, how to better exploit expert redundancy for further scaling, and whether the interaction between categorical losses (C51) and tokenization explains the DQN gap.
