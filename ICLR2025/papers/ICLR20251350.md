## Problem

Pretrained bidirectional trajectory models (BTMs) trained with masked autoencoding can serve as both policy and world models, yet prior work assigns only a single mask pattern per downstream task (e.g., RCBC for action prediction), leaving the model's forward dynamics, reward prediction, and inverse dynamics capabilities untapped at inference. This paper asks whether integrating multiple mask patterns in a test-time Model Predictive Control loop can improve decision-making without any additional parameter training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251350/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For forward M³PC, the model receives the current state, a short context window of past trajectory tokens (4 timesteps), and a return-to-go or Q-value signal; it outputs a single selected action from N=625 candidates scored by a TD(λ)-style utility. For backward M³PC, the input is the current state plus a sequence of subgoal states (extracted from a guidance trajectory at fixed intervals), and the output is the first action in an inverse-dynamics-derived sequence. All modalities (states, actions, rewards, RTGs) are tokenized into a shared embedding space with modality-type and timestep embeddings, and different subsets are masked per subtask.

## Method

M³PC decomposes decision-making into sample-based MPC steps handled by the same pretrained BTM under different mask patterns: [RCBC] for uncertainty-aware action proposal sampling (trained with NLL loss plus a trajectory-level entropy lower bound solved via Lagrangian dual), [FD] for parallel forward-dynamics rollout of candidate action sequences, and [RP] for predicting intermediate rewards and long-term returns to compute a TD(λ)-style utility. A softmax over utilities selects the final action (or samples stochastically during online finetuning for exploration). For goal reaching, backward M³PC instead uses a Path Inference mask to inpaint intermediate waypoint states toward the goal, followed by an Inverse Dynamics mask to recover the action sequence. The bidirectional architecture enables all future actions and states to be predicted in parallel, avoiding the linear computational growth with planning horizon seen in beam search or CEM-based planners.

## Results

On D4RL locomotion tasks (6 datasets), M³PC-Q achieves a total normalized return of 429.8, outperforming the best baseline TT (403.0) by 6.7% and the same pretrained BTM model (372.8) by 15.3%. In offline-to-online finetuning with 200K online samples, M³PC achieves a total improvement of +101.0 over its offline score, compared to +45.3 for ODT and +4.5 for IQL. On RoboMimic manipulation tasks, M³PC achieves 0.98 success rate on Can-Pair (vs. 0.94 for DT) and 0.70 on the real-world Can-Real task (vs. 0.50 for DT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M³PC-Q outperforms all six offline baselines (BC, TD3+BC, IQL, DT, TT, BTM) on 5 out of 6 D4RL datasets, with the sole exception being hopper-m-r where IQL (92.1) and TT (91.5) remain ahead of M³PC-Q (78.3±16.2). Against the same pretrained BTM model using only the [RCBC] mask, M³PC-M improves total return by 6.2% (395.9 vs. 372.8) and M³PC-Q by 15.3% (429.8 vs. 372.8), confirming that test-time MPC adds value without retraining. In the O2O setting with 200K online samples, M³PC achieves a final total of 530.8, outperforming ODT (422.7) by 25.5% and IQL (405.5) by 30.9%, with 2.2× the improvement magnitude of ODT (+101.0 vs. +45.3). In the broader O2O comparison (Table 5, 100K budget), M³PC (71.7→76.8 average) is competitive with PEX (57.4→80.2) and OFF2ON (48.5→73.5), trailing PEX on hopper-mr and walker2d-mr but leading on halfcheetah-m and halfcheetah-mr. On RoboMimic manipulation, M³PC beats DT on Can-Pair (0.98 vs. 0.94), Square-MH (0.28 vs. 0.21), and Can-Lim (0.54 vs. 0.46), but underperforms DT on Lift-MG (0.77 vs. 0.93), suggesting the planning benefit is task-dependent for manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Bidirectional Trajectory Model (BTM) line of work (Carroll et al. 2022, Liu et al. 2022, Wu et al. 2023), where a single masked Transformer is pretrained to reconstruct random subsets of trajectory elements. The key departure is in inference: prior BTM work assigns one fixed mask pattern per downstream task (e.g., RCBC for policy), whereas M³PC simultaneously invokes multiple mask patterns—RCBC for action proposals, Forward Dynamics for state rollouts, and Reward/Return Prediction for utility scoring—within a test-time MPC loop. It also draws on Trajectory Transformer's (Janner et al. 2021) planning-via-sequence-modeling idea but replaces beam search with parallel masked prediction, and borrows IQL's value estimator as an optional guidance signal for the Q-value variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a pretrained masked trajectory model's full multi-modal predictive capacity (policy, forward dynamics, reward prediction, inverse dynamics) can be exploited at inference time without any parameter updates, yielding a clean 'self-enhancing' agent that outperforms its own single-mask baseline. The O2O finetuning results (26% over ODT, 31% over IQL) and zero-shot goal reaching to unseen states suggest a practical path toward generalist agents that improve with interaction and generalize to new task specifications. Limitations include restriction to state-based observations (no pixel inputs), a fixed small Transformer (2 encoder + 1 decoder layers), and the need for subgoal sequences in goal reaching due to limited planning horizon; whether scaling model capacity further improves test-time planning remains open.
