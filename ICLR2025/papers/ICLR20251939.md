## Problem

Existing LLM pruning methods such as SparseGPT and Wanda optimize a linear objective (approximating XW), which fails to capture the non-linear sensitivity of the Softmax attention mechanism to large positive logit values. This paper identifies that linear-approximation-based pruning masks produce poor approximations of the actual attention matrix, and asks whether one can directly optimize a pruning mask for the non-linear Softmax attention function while retaining theoretical convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes the fused attention weight matrix W = W_Q W_K^T ∈ R^{d×d}, a set of k calibration hidden-state matrices X_j ∈ R^{n×d} extracted from a pretrained LLM (e.g., Llama 3.2-1B with d=2048), and a causal attention mask M_c ∈ {0,1}^{n×n}. It produces a binary pruning mask M ∈ {0,1}^{d×d} by first optimizing a real-valued mask in [0,1]^{d×d} via gradient descent, then thresholding at the ρ-th percentile to enforce the target sparsity ratio. The pruned matrix (M ∘ W) is used in place of W during inference, reducing both memory and FLOPs for the attention computation.

## Method

The core contribution is a new loss function that measures the Frobenius-norm distance between the original and pruned Softmax attention matrices, L(M) = ½‖D⁻¹A − D̃⁻¹Ã‖_F² + ½λ‖M‖_F², where A = exp(XWX^T) and Ã = exp(X(M∘W)X^T), with D and D̃ being the corresponding row-normalization diagonal matrices. The paper derives a closed-form gradient ∂L/∂M = W ∘ (X^T p X) + λM, where p encodes the row-wise difference between pruned and original attention probabilities weighted by the pruned attention itself. Convergence of gradient descent to a near-optimal mask is proven via a g-proxy Polyak-Lojasiewicz inequality, requiring only that XX^T ≽ βI and that the minimum pruned attention probability is bounded below by δ > 0. The algorithm iterates over a calibration dataset of k sequences for T steps with a fixed step size η < 1/L (where L is the Lipschitz constant of the gradient), then binarizes the resulting mask by thresholding at the ρ-th percentile.

## Results

On Llama 3.2-1B with 50% pruning ratio, the proposed attention pruning method achieves the lowest perplexity across all MLP configurations: 13.885 (Dense MLP), 24.427 (Wanda MLP), and 34.946 (SparseGPT MLP), compared to 14.269/26.074/36.641 for SparseGPT attention and 14.912/30.426/45.435 for Wanda attention. On synthetic data (d=64, n=128, ρ=0.5), the method achieves substantially lower relative attention matrix error than both baselines across varying regularization λ, sequence length n, and pruning ratio ρ. On real C4 data with Llama 3.2-1B (d=2048), the method maintains a consistent relative-error advantage over both Wanda and SparseGPT across sequence lengths and pruning ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two linear-approximation pruning baselines: Wanda (Sun et al., ICLR 2024) and SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, ICML 2023), both of which prune W_Q and W_K separately using linear objectives. The proposed non-linear attention pruning method wins on all evaluated metrics. On end-to-end perplexity (Table 1), it outperforms SparseGPT attention by 0.384 PPL (13.885 vs 14.269) and Wanda attention by 1.027 PPL (13.885 vs 14.912) under Dense MLP; the gap widens dramatically under Wanda MLP (24.427 vs 26.074 and 30.426) and SparseGPT MLP (34.946 vs 36.641 and 45.435). On relative attention matrix error (Figures 2–3), the method consistently achieves lower error than both baselines across all settings of regularization coefficient λ, input sequence length n, and pruning ratio ρ, with the margin growing as n and ρ increase. The paper also verifies its theoretical assumptions (XX^⊰ ≽ βI with β≈0.034, and min attention entry ≥ δ with δ≈0.0025) on the C4 dataset, confirming practical applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant linear pruning formulation (minimizing ‖XW − XW̃‖) used by SparseGPT and Wanda by instead optimizing a pruning mask to directly approximate the non-linear Softmax attention matrix. Theoretically, it builds on the proxy PL inequality framework of Frei & Gu (2021) to establish GD convergence guarantees for this non-convex, non-linear objective. Conceptually, it reframes attention weight pruning as a non-linear approximation problem rather than a linear one, arguing that the exponential sensitivity of Softmax to large positive inputs makes linear-approximation-based masks fundamentally suboptimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical foundation for non-linear attention pruning, showing that GD converges to a near-optimal mask despite the highly non-convex Softmax objective, which could guide future pruning algorithm design. However, empirical validation is limited to a single 1B-parameter model (Llama 3.2-1B) on one dataset (C4), and the end-to-end perplexity improvements, while consistent, remain moderate (e.g., 24.4 vs. 26.1 PPL when combined with Wanda MLP pruning). Open questions include scalability to 7B+ models, multi-layer joint pruning, interaction with quantization, and whether the theoretical assumptions (XX⊤ ≽ βI, bounded attention entries) hold broadly across architectures and sequence lengths.
