## Problem

Text-video retrieval is fundamentally constrained by information asymmetry: videos contain rich, multi-layered visual content while their textual descriptions capture only fragments of this complexity. Most CLIP-based methods assume a symmetrical text-video relationship in a shared latent space, ignoring this disparity in information density. The paper asks whether enriching textual representations at both training and retrieval stages can bridge this gap without architectural changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251554/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text query and a video clip (12 uniformly sampled frames resized to 224×224) as input, producing a ranked list of candidate videos by cosine similarity. During retrieval, the single user query is expanded by an LLM into 10 diverse queries, from which Farthest Query Sampling selects k=2 enriched queries; the final ranking is obtained by majority voting over the 3 independent retrieval results (original + 2 selected). The backbone is CLIP ViT-B/32 or ViT-B/16 with X-Pool text-conditioned cross-attention pooling.

## Method

The unified text enrichment framework operates at two stages. During training, videos are segmented into semantically consistent 'atomic' scenes via Kernel Temporal Segmentation on CLIP frame features, and each scene is captioned by BLIP-2 (middle frame), generating ~45K additional queries for MSR-VTT's 9K training videos to ensure comprehensive scene coverage. During retrieval, GPT-4 is prompted to generate 10 semantically diverse queries from the original query, and a novel Farthest Query Sampling (FQS) algorithm—inspired by point cloud sampling—iteratively selects k queries that maximize minimum pairwise distance in embedding space while anchoring on the user query for relevance. The model uses a symmetric cross-entropy contrastive loss with X-Pool's text-conditioned cross-attention to aggregate frame embeddings. The key empirical finding is the 'Oracle Query' upper bound: selecting the single best query from the enriched set boosts MSR-VTT R@1 from 46.7% to 61.4%, demonstrating that query selection simultaneously reduces computational cost (from 11 to 3 similarity computations) and improves accuracy.

## Results

On MSR-VTT 1K-A (the primary benchmark), the method achieves R@1=52.1, R@5=76.8, R@10=86.3, MdR=1.0, and MnR=9.7 with a ViT-B/32 backbone, and R@1=54.6, R@5=79.5, R@10=86.7 with ViT-B/16, setting new state-of-the-art in text-to-video retrieval. With DSL post-processing, performance further improves to R@1=54.1 (ViT-B/32) and R@1=57.1 (ViT-B/16). On LSMDC, the method achieves the best R@1=28.3 and MnR=37.1, outperforming all prior methods. The Oracle Query analysis reveals that optimal query selection alone can boost R@1 from 46.7% to 61.4% on MSR-VTT, demonstrating the large untapped potential of text enrichment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MSR-VTT (ViT-B/32), the method outperforms all 16 compared baselines, achieving +7.6% R@1 over CLIP4Clip (44.5→52.1), +5.2% over X-Pool (46.9→52.1), +2.8% over Cap4Video (49.3→52.1), and +2.0% over CLIP-VIP (50.1→52.1), which notably uses the large-scale HowTo100M dataset for pre-training. Against the most recent competitors, it surpasses T-MASS (50.2) by 1.9% and DITS (51.9) by 0.2% on R@1, while also achieving substantially lower MnR (9.7 vs. 11.6–11.9). On LSMDC, the method achieves the best R@1 (28.3) and MnR (37.1), beating TEFAL (26.8/44.4) and CLIP-VIP (25.6/–). On MSVD and VATEX, the method is competitive but does not always lead on R@1 (51.1 vs. Cap4Video's 51.8 on MSVD; 63.3 vs. 66.6 on VATEX), though it matches or exceeds baselines on R@5 and R@10. The method is also shown to be compatible with stronger backbones (CLIP-VIP + Ours reaches R@1=53.2) and post-processing (DSL), reaching R@1=58.9 on MSR-VTT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on X-Pool's text-conditioned cross-attention pooling as its base retrieval architecture, but deliberately departs from the model-centric line of work (T-MASS, UAVTR) that addresses text-video information asymmetry through stochastic or distribution-based embedding modifications. Instead, it reframes the problem as a data deficiency: the training set's text captions under-cover video content, and single-query retrieval under-represents the semantic space a video occupies. The training-phase enrichment (event-aware KTS segmentation + BLIP-2 captioning) is conceptually related to Cap4Video and CLIP-VIP's synthetic caption strategies, but differs in using temporal segmentation to ensure scene-level coverage rather than global or frame-level captions. The retrieval-phase LLM query generation draws on the query expansion literature but extends it to the cross-modal setting, and the Farthest Query Sampling algorithm adapts farthest-point sampling from point cloud processing to the query selection problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Oracle Query result (46.7% → 61.4% R@1 on MSR-VTT) provides compelling evidence that the text representation, not the retrieval model, is the primary bottleneck in TVR — a conceptual reframing that redirects research attention toward data-centric solutions. The practical contribution is a training-free, architecture-agnostic retrieval enrichment pipeline (LLM query generation + FQS + majority voting) that consistently improves four different base models by 3.8–5.2% R@1, making it immediately applicable to existing systems. Key limitations include the LLM inference cost at query time (mitigated but not eliminated by FQS reducing k to 2), the noise introduced by LLM hallucination in enriched queries, and the dependence on a capable LLM (though Phi-3.5 experiments suggest smaller models may suffice with FQS). Open questions include whether the training-phase enrichment can be made more robust to captioning noise without architectural changes, and whether the approach extends to longer-form or multi-shot video retrieval.
