## Problem

Neural scaling laws for transformers lack a theoretical foundation: prior solvable models (Hutter 2021, Maloney et al. 2022, Bordelon et al. 2024, Nam et al. 2024) either ignore the transformer architecture entirely or only characterize converged parameters, leaving the explicit training trajectory of self-attention unsolved. This gap prevents understanding whether the in-context learning mechanism—specifically the context sequence length—induces scaling laws distinct from other architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes in-context data points Φ(s,X) ∈ R^{(N_s+1)×(ψ_s+1)}, where each row encodes sparse one-hot features (top) or scalar targets (bottom) for ψ_s context tokens plus one query, along with learnable parameters V ∈ R^{(D-1)×D} and W_KQ ∈ R^{D×D}. The model outputs a scalar prediction f_s = [VΦΦ^T W_KQ Φ]_{s,ψ_s+1} approximating the task strength Λ_s. The ultimate output is closed-form expressions for test loss as a function of time t, model size D, data size N, and optimal compute C = Dt, yielding explicit power-law exponents.

## Method

The paper models gradient flow dynamics of linear self-attention on a multitask sparse feature regression problem as a coupled non-linear ODE system for vectors v_s and w_s. The key technical breakthrough is a three-step reduction: (i) a change of variables η_s = v_s + w_s, ρ_s = v_s − w_s symmetrizes the system; (ii) a perturbation expansion in ε_s = 1/ψ_s (valid for large context length) separates the dynamics into zeroth- and first-order ODEs; (iii) the discovery that g_s^0 · h_s^0 = 2C_s is a conserved quantity collapses the zeroth-order system into a pair of Riccati equations admitting closed-form solutions. The resulting solution f_s^0(t) is a sum of two logistic-like terms whose rates a_s = #_s ψ_s(Λ_s²+1)/N depend on data count, sequence length, and task strength. By summing the per-task loss over a power-law task distribution P_α(S=s) ∝ s^{−α}, the paper derives exact scaling law exponents for D, t, N, and C, revealing that fixed context length yields architecture-independent exponents while varied context length (ψ_s ∝ s^{−β}) uniquely slows the time scaling law.

## Results

The paper derives closed-form neural scaling law exponents for linear self-attention in a multitask sparse feature regression (MSFR) in-context learning setup. When context sequence length and task strength are fixed (β=γ=0), the test loss follows L ∝ D^{-(α-1)} for model size, L ∝ t^{-(α-1)/α} for training time, L ∝ N^{-(α-1)/α} for data size, and L ∝ C^{-(α-1)/(α+1)} for optimal compute, where α is the power-law exponent of the task distribution. When context sequence length varies as ψ_s ∝ s^{-β} and task strength as Λ_s ∝ s^{-γ}, the time scaling law exponent changes to -(α+2γ-1)/(α+β+3γ), revealing that varied context length slows learning. The theoretical predictions are validated against empirical simulations (Fig. 3, Fig. 4) with solid lines matching dashed power-law curves across α=1.8 and α=2.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are with scaling law exponents derived for other architectures rather than empirical benchmarks. When context sequence length is fixed (β=γ=0), linear self-attention shares identical scaling law exponents with ReLU MLPs (Michaud et al., 2023), diagonal linear networks (Nam et al., 2024), and linear models (Bordelon et al., 2024; Hutter, 2021), supporting the empirical observation from Kaplan et al. (2020) that architecture does not significantly affect scaling law exponents. The key differentiating result is that when context sequence length varies across tasks (β>0), the time scaling law exponent becomes -(α+2γ-1)/(α+β+3γ) instead of -(α-1)/α, a phenomenon unique to self-attention since other architectures lack a context sequence length parameter. Notably, β does not appear in the model size or data size scaling laws, meaning self-attention still matches other architectures on those dimensions even with varied context length. The paper also validates its theoretical predictions against direct numerical simulations of gradient flow, showing precise agreement when ψ_s ≫ 1 (Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning analysis of linear self-attention from Zhang et al. (2023) and Lu et al. (2024), which characterized only converged parameters or specific asymptotic limits, by deriving the full training trajectory via a Riccati-equation reformulation of the gradient-flow ODE system. It also departs from prior theoretical scaling-law work (Bordelon et al. 2024, Nam et al. 2024, Hutter 2021, Michaud et al. 2023) that studied MLPs, linear models, or random-feature models but explicitly did not consider the transformer architecture. The multitask sparse feature regression (MSFR) problem is positioned as a limiting case of the in-context regression framework of Lu et al. (2024) under a source/capacity condition, while the power-law task distribution connects to the multitask parity setup of Michaud et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first closed-form solution of self-attention dynamics along the entire training trajectory, providing an analytically tractable proxy for studying transformer properties beyond scaling laws (e.g., grokking, task-specific learning rates). The key practical insight is that when context sequence length is fixed, linear self-attention shares identical scaling exponents with ReLU MLPs and diagonal linear networks, theoretically confirming Kaplan et al.'s (2020) empirical observation that architecture does not significantly affect scaling-law exponents. However, the analysis is limited to linear (softmax-free) attention, a simplified single-head block, and a synthetic regression task; whether the Riccati-based solution and the context-length effect on the time law extend to full softmax attention, multi-head/multi-layer transformers, or realistic language modeling tasks remains open.
