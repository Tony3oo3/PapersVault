## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for the DDPM sampler either require strong data assumptions (Lipschitz score functions, log-Sobolev inequality, bounded support, or log-concavity) or achieve suboptimal rates of O(√(d/T)) in total variation distance. The open question is whether the faster O(d/T) rate—previously achieved only for the ODE-based DDIM sampler under restrictive Jacobian-accuracy conditions on score estimates—can be obtained for the stochastic DDPM sampler under minimal assumptions on both the target distribution and the score estimates.

## Input / Output

The sampler starts from pure Gaussian noise Y_T ~ N(0, I_d) and a sequence of pre-trained score function estimates s_t (obtained via score matching) and produces a sample Y_1 whose distribution approximates the target p_data. The analysis requires only that the target distribution have a finite first-order moment (polynomially bounded in T) and that the score estimates be ℓ₂-accurate in L²(p_{X_t}), without any Lipschitz, Jacobian-accuracy, or higher-order smoothness requirements. The error is measured in TV distance between p_{X_1} and p_{Y_1}, where p_{X_1} is the one-step-forward distribution that is vanishingly close to p_{X_0} = p_data since β_1 = T^{-c_0}.

## Method

The key technical contribution is a novel analytical framework that directly tracks total variation error propagation through the reverse process, bypassing the standard KL-divergence route (via Pinsker's inequality and Girsanov's theorem) that provably yields at best O(√(d/T)). The authors introduce two auxiliary Markov chains: one (Ȳ_t) that uses the true score functions and another (Ŷ_t) that uses the estimated scores, both coupled to the actual DDPM chain Y_t. A fine-grained recursive relation (Lemma 2) characterizes how the TV gap ∫Δ_t(x)dx evolves at each reverse step, with the per-step increment controlled by the squared Frobenius norm of the score Jacobian J_t. The discretization error accumulates to O(d log³T / T) via a telescoping sum, while the estimation error is bounded by decomposing the KL divergence between the two auxiliary chains into per-step Gaussian KL terms that depend linearly on the ℓ₂ score error. This direct TV analysis, combined with a carefully designed learning rate schedule and concentration sets E_{t,1} that handle the tail behavior of the forward process, yields the final bound TV(p_{X_1}, p_{Y_1}) ≤ c·d log³T/T + c·ε_score·√(log T).

## Results

The paper establishes that the DDPM sampler achieves an O(d/T) convergence rate in total variation distance (ignoring logarithmic factors), improving upon the previous state-of-the-art O(√(d/T)) for DDPM. The precise bound is TV(p_{X₁}, p_{Y₁}) ≤ c·d·log³(T)/T + c·ε_score·√(log T), where the first term captures discretization error and the second captures score estimation error. This result holds under only a finite first-order moment assumption on the target distribution and ℓ₂-accurate score estimates, which is the weakest data assumption in the current literature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the three prior DDPM results, this paper strictly improves the convergence rate: from O(L√(d/T)) under Lipschitz scores (Chen et al., 2023c), from O(d/√T) (Chen et al., 2023a), and from O(√(d/T)) (Benton et al., 2023a) to O(d/T), while simultaneously weakening the data assumption from finite second-order moment (or Lipschitz scores) to merely finite first-order moment. Against the DDIM baselines, the paper matches the O(d/T) rate of Li et al. (2024b) but does so for the DDPM sampler, for arbitrary T and d (rather than requiring T ≫ d²), and without the Jacobian accuracy requirement on score estimates. Compared to Li et al. (2023), the rate improves from O(d²/T + d⁶/T²) to O(d/T) and the bounded support assumption is replaced by a finite first moment. The key trade-off is that the O(d/T) rate for DDPM is achieved with only ℓ₂-accurate scores (linear in ε_score), whereas the DDIM results of Li et al. (2023; 2024b) require the Jacobian of the score estimates to also be accurate, a significantly stronger condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis lineage for score-based diffusion models initiated by Chen et al. (2023c,a), Benton et al. (2023a), and Li et al. (2023, 2024b), which progressively relaxed data assumptions and improved rates for DDPM and DDIM samplers. The key conceptual departure is in the analytical technique: rather than routing through KL divergence (which fundamentally caps the rate at O(√(d/T)) via Pinsker's inequality), the authors develop auxiliary Markov chains and a fine-grained recursive relation that directly tracks TV error propagation at each reverse step. This allows them to match the O(d/T) rate previously achieved only for the ODE-based DDIM sampler (Li et al., 2024b) while applying it to the stochastic DDPM sampler under strictly weaker assumptions (finite first moment vs. bounded support; ℓ₂ score accuracy vs. Jacobian accuracy).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result closes the theoretical gap between DDPM and DDIM convergence rates, showing that the stochastic sampler can achieve the same O(d/T) rate as the ODE-based sampler without requiring the stronger Jacobian accuracy condition or the T ≫ d² regime. The minimal data assumption (finite first-order moment) makes the theory applicable to heavy-tailed or unbounded distributions common in real data. Open questions remain: whether O(d/T) is tight for DDPM, whether the analysis extends to Wasserstein distance, and whether rates can be improved for data concentrated on low-dimensional manifolds (where the intrinsic dimension k < d should govern convergence rather than the ambient dimension d).
