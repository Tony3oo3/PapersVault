## Problem

In-context reinforcement learning requires training on tens of thousands of diverse tasks with complete learning histories showing policy improvement, yet existing RL datasets contain at most hundreds of tasks and lack the structured exploration-to-exploitation progression needed for in-context ability to emerge. This data bottleneck has confined ICRL research to simple, low-diversity environments, preventing meaningful method comparison at scale and blocking the path toward generalist agents.

## Input / Output

The dataset stores complete learning histories as sequential HDF5 arrays of 5×5 grid observations (tile-color pairs encoded as a single Cartesian-product index to halve storage), 6 discrete actions, float16 rewards, boolean done flags, and uint8 expert actions per transition. Each of the ~29k tasks contains 32 parallel histories of ~120k transitions, with per-history metadata (environment ID, benchmark ID, ruleset ID) enabling filtering by task complexity. AD consumes ordered multi-episode observation-action-reward sequences to predict next actions, while DPT uses unordered task-specific contexts with expert action targets, requiring the relabeled expert actions stored alongside each transition.

## Method

Data collection proceeds in three stages: (1) multi-task task-conditioned recurrent PPO pre-training on 65k tasks with ruleset encodings exposed to the agent, (2) single-task fine-tuning on 30k tasks using 8192 parallel JAX environments (recording only 32 histories per task to bound dataset size), and (3) post-processing that relabels every transition with expert actions from the final PPO policy and filters out tasks with final return below 0.3. The environment is XLand-MiniGrid, a GPU-accelerated grid-world with procedurally generated binary-tree rulesets (0–9 rules), using a single-room 13×13 layout. Storage uses HDF5 with gzip compression and tuned chunk sizes (2048 along the history dimension), achieving a 4× throughput speedup over naive compression and reducing ~5 TB to 326 GB. Benchmarks use 25M-parameter transformers: AD with FlashAttention-2, ALiBi positional embeddings, and concatenated observation-action-reward tokens; DPT without positional encoding and with decorrelated in-context sampling. AD shows emergent in-context improvement on simple rulesets but degrades sharply as rule depth increases, while DPT fails to learn in-context at all, attributed to its inability to reason in POMDP settings.

## Results

On XLand-Trivial-20B, Algorithm Distillation (AD) demonstrates emergent in-context ability, improving mean return from ~0.28 to ~0.4 across 500 evaluation episodes on 1024 unseen tasks. On XLand-100B, AD achieves similar final performance but with a faster pace of improvement, attributed to wider task coverage. However, AD's in-context ability degrades significantly as task complexity (number of rules) increases, struggling on rulesets with more than 3–4 rules. Decision-Pretrained Transformer (DPT) fails to exhibit any in-context RL capability on either dataset, plateauing at suboptimal returns even on the simplest trivial tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks two in-context RL methods—Algorithm Distillation (AD, Laskin et al. 2022) and Decision-Pretrained Transformer (DPT, Lee et al. 2023)—on the proposed datasets. AD clearly wins over DPT: it shows measurable in-context policy improvement on both XLand-Trivial-20B and XLand-100B, while DPT fails to learn in-context even on the simplest trivial tasks (Figure 18). AD's advantage is attributed to its use of ordered learning histories that capture the policy improvement trajectory, whereas DPT relies on random same-task context and expert actions. However, AD's in-context ability is limited to simple rulesets (≤3 rules); performance degrades sharply on deeper rulesets (5–9 rules), where the model cannot yet solve the training tasks in-context. DPT's failure is further diagnosed via a POMDP ablation (Key-to-Door environment, Figure 15), showing that DPT cannot reason about hidden state (e.g., whether a key was already found) from random context, capping its return at a suboptimal plateau. Overall, the dataset reveals that current in-context RL baselines are insufficient for complex, diverse tasks, motivating further architectural research.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the XLand-MiniGrid environment (Nikulin et al., 2023), which provided GPU-accelerated procedural task generation but no pre-collected learning histories. It departs from prior in-context RL work (Laskin et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2023; Kirsch et al., 2023) that relied on small-scale, self-generated data in simple environments with only hundreds of tasks, by collecting complete learning histories across ~29,000 tasks. It also positions itself against large-scale RL datasets (GATO, JAT, Open X-Embodiment, D4RL) that contain expert or fixed-policy data but lack the improving-policy learning histories essential for in-context RL to emerge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

XLand-100B is the first dataset at this scale (100B transitions, 28,876 tasks, 2.5B episodes) specifically structured for in-context RL, removing the primary barrier that forced every prior in-context RL paper to generate its own small-scale data. The benchmarking reveals that AD's in-context ability degrades sharply on complex rulesets and DPT fails entirely in POMDP settings, highlighting that current transformer-based in-context RL methods are far from solving diverse, multi-rule tasks. Open questions include whether more sample-efficient architectures can handle the full complexity range, whether the single-domain (grid-world) and single-structure (binary-tree ruleset) limitations constrain generalization, and how scaling laws behave when task count and history length are varied independently.
