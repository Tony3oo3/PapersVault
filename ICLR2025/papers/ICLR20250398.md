## Problem

Discrete latent spaces (e.g., DreamerV3's one-hot encoding) have shown benefits in visual control but underperform continuous latent spaces (e.g., TD-MPC2) in state-based continuous control. It remains unclear whether discrete latent spaces can be made effective for continuous control, and if so, what encoding and training formulation (classification vs. regression, stochastic vs. deterministic dynamics) is most beneficial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250398/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors of varying dimensionality (e.g., 223-dim observations and 38-dim actions for Dog, 67-dim/24-dim for Humanoid in DMControl) and outputs a single continuous action per environment step. Internally, observations are encoded into a 512-dimensional continuous vector that is quantized via FSQ into one of 15 discrete codes (2 channels with levels [5,3]), and all downstream dynamics, reward, value, and policy networks operate on this compact discrete code rather than the raw observation.

## Method

DCWM replaces the continuous latent space with a discrete codebook obtained via Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ), where each latent state is a b-dimensional code vector drawn from a fixed codebook of |C|=15 entries. The dynamics model predicts a categorical distribution over the next code given the current code and action, trained with cross-entropy (classification) rather than MSE regression, and multi-step predictions during training use straight-through Gumbel-softmax sampling to backpropagate gradients through the stochastic transitions. The encoder is trained self-supervisedly via a latent-state consistency objective (no observation reconstruction), while the policy and an ensemble of 5 critics (REDQ-style) are learned in the discrete latent space using TD3 with N-step returns. At decision time, a modified MPPI planner rolls out candidate action sequences using the expected next code (a probability-weighted sum over codebook entries) rather than sampling, and bootstraps the planning horizon with the ensemble Q-value.

## Results

DC-MPC is evaluated at 1M environment steps across 30 DeepMind Control Suite tasks, 45 Meta-World tasks, and 5 MyoSuite tasks (3 seeds per task), reporting median, interquartile mean (IQM), mean, and optimality gap. DC-MPC generally matches TD-MPC2 in aggregate performance while significantly outperforming DreamerV3, SAC, and TD-MPC across all three benchmarks. The most pronounced gains appear in high-dimensional locomotion tasks (Dog: obs dim 223, act dim 38; Humanoid: obs dim 67, act dim 24), where DC-MPC substantially exceeds both TD-MPC2 and DreamerV3. In Meta-World manipulation, DC-MPC matches TD-MPC2 while clearly surpassing DreamerV3 and SAC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TD-MPC2 (continuous latent space, MSE regression, SimNorm), DC-MPC generally matches aggregate performance across all 80 tasks but achieves significantly higher returns in the hardest high-dimensional locomotion tasks (Dog Run, Dog Walk, Humanoid Walk, Humanoid Run), where the discrete codebook representation appears to simplify transition dynamics learning. Against DreamerV3 (discrete one-hot latent space with observation reconstruction), DC-MPC outperforms across all benchmarks, with the largest gaps in the difficult DMControl locomotion tasks and Meta-World manipulation; the paper attributes DreamerV3's weakness to its reliance on observation reconstruction, which is detrimental in continuous control. Against the original TD-MPC (continuous latent space, MSE, SAC-based policy), DC-MPC outperforms consistently. Against SAC (model-free baseline), DC-MPC outperforms across all tasks, confirming the benefit of the world model for sample efficiency in continuous control. The paper also shows that transplanting DCWM's discrete stochastic codebook latent space into TD-MPC2 yields modest improvements over vanilla TD-MPC2, supporting the latent-space design as the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two competing paradigms in model-based RL: DreamerV2/V3's discrete one-hot latent spaces (trained with classification) and TD-MPC2's continuous latent spaces (trained with MSE regression). It departs from DreamerV2/V3 by replacing one-hot encodings with finite scalar quantization (FSQ) codebook encodings and by eliminating observation reconstruction, while departing from TD-MPC2 by making the latent space discrete and the transition dynamics stochastic (categorical) rather than deterministic (Gaussian). The overall architecture—encoder, dynamics, reward, critic, policy prior, and MPPI decision-time planning—follows TD-MPC2 closely, with the core novelty being the discrete codebook formulation that enables cross-entropy training of the dynamics model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a systematic empirical argument that discrete codebook encodings with stochastic categorical dynamics are a viable and sometimes superior alternative to continuous deterministic latent spaces for continuous control, challenging the implicit assumption that continuous control requires continuous representations. The finding that observation reconstruction actively harms performance in continuous control (Fig. 20) reinforces the decoder-free trend but adds a discrete-space perspective. Open questions include whether the approach scales to transformer or diffusion backbones, whether it generalizes to multi-embodiment settings, and whether the task-specific hyperparameters (noise schedules, N-step returns) can be eliminated. The practical impact is moderate: the technique is straightforward to implement (FSQ is a single layer) and the code is released, but the performance gains over TD-MPC2 are modest rather than transformative.
