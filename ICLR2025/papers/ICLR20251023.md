## Problem

Existing convergence analyses of FedAvg assume clients are sampled independently and uniformly each round, which fails to capture real-world cross-device FL where clients have non-uniform availability probabilities and must wait a minimum number of R rounds (minimum separation) before re-participating. This correlated, non-uniform participation introduces an unavoidable asymptotic bias in the solution attained by FedAvg, yet no prior work provides a unified theoretical framework characterizing this bias or a debiasing algorithm that works under arbitrary R and unknown client availability.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the current global model x_t and round index t from the server, then performs K local gradient steps on its own data (e.g., a 3-layer FC network on MNIST digits or synthetic regression data). The only additional per-client state is a scalar counter t_i tracking how many times the client has been sampled, from which the debiasing factor ν_t^i = 1/(λ_t^i · N) is computed with λ_t^i = t_i/((t+1)B). The system parameter R (minimum separation) and batch size B are set by the server; the client availability probabilities p_i are unknown and need not be estimated explicitly. The output is the global model x_T drawn uniformly from the trajectory, which converges to the unbiased minimizer of F(x) = (1/N)Σf_i(x).

## Method

The paper models the correlated participation pattern as an R-order Markov chain whose state is the ordered sequence of B-client subsets sampled over the last R rounds, then augments this into a first-order chain with state space of size d(M,R) = ∏σ(B(M−k),B). The stationary distribution π_R of this chain determines the asymptotic bias of vanilla FedAvg, and the paper proves that increasing R drives π_R toward the uniform distribution (Theorem 2). For debiasing, each client maintains a running empirical frequency λ_t^i = t_i/((t+1)B) as an unbiased estimator of π_R^i, and scales its local gradient by ν_t^i = 1/(λ_t^i·N), effectively reweighting the local objective to cancel the sampling bias. The key technical challenge is that client samples are not independent across rounds, so standard concentration inequalities do not apply; the authors instead exploit the irreducibility and aperiodicity of the Markov chain (Lemma 1) to show λ_t^i converges almost surely to π_R^i, yielding a convergence rate of Õ(τ_mix/√T) + O(1/T) for arbitrary R < M−1 without requiring bounded gradients or knowledge of p_i.

## Results

The paper presents convergence plots (rather than a numerical table) on two settings: a synthetic dataset (N=100, M=20, d=20, n_i=100) and MNIST with a 3-layer fully-connected network. On the synthetic data, Vanilla FedAvg exhibits asymptotic bias that decreases as minimum separation R increases, while Debiasing FedAvg (Algorithm 1) converges to the unbiased objective (matching oracle uniform sampling) for all values of R. On MNIST at R=8, Debiasing FedAvg converges to the unbiased objective value, whereas both Vanilla FedAvg and FedVARP remain significantly biased. The paper also observes empirically that larger R can accelerate convergence for both Debiasing and Vanilla FedAvg, conjectured to be due to smaller Markov mixing time τ_mix.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Debiasing FedAvg against two baselines: Vanilla FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) and FedVARP (Jhunjhunwala et al., 2022). On both synthetic and MNIST datasets, Debiasing FedAvg wins decisively in terms of asymptotic bias: it converges to the unbiased objective (the red reference line corresponding to oracle uniform sampling) regardless of the minimum separation R, while Vanilla FedAvg converges to a biased solution whose gap from the unbiased optimum shrinks only as R increases. FedVARP, which is designed for uniform client sampling with partial participation, is also significantly affected by the non-uniform correlated participation and fails to eliminate the bias at R=8 on MNIST. The paper explicitly notes that 'both Vanilla FedAvg and FedVARP are designed only for uniform client sampling and hence are significantly affected by bias from client participation.' An additional empirical finding is that increasing R can speed up convergence for all three methods, a phenomenon not yet captured by the theoretical bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of FedAvg from the standard assumption of independent (Bernoulli) client sampling to the practically critical setting of correlated participation enforced by a minimum separation constraint R. It builds on the reweighting-by-estimated-probabilities idea from Wang & Ji (2023) for unknown non-uniform participation, but departs from all prior FL convergence work by modeling the participation process as a high-order (R-order) Markov chain rather than a sequence of independent draws. The extreme cyclic case (R = M−1) was previously analyzed by Cho et al. (2023), and first-order Markov-sampling SGD by Even (2023) and Beznosikov et al. (2024), but no prior work covers the full spectrum of R values or provides a debiasing guarantee under correlated sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Minimum separation is now a default criterion in production cross-device FL systems (e.g., Google's Gboard) for combining differential privacy with federated training, yet its effect on convergence bias was previously uncharacterized. The paper's practical takeaway—that increasing R reduces asymptotic bias and that a two-scalar-per-client counting mechanism can provably eliminate it—gives system designers a concrete, low-cost tool. Open questions include whether the framework extends to time-varying R, whether variance-reduction techniques can accelerate convergence under Markov-sampling, and whether the empirically observed speedup from larger R (smaller mixing time) can be formally established. The analysis also does not yield the linear speedup in N that uniform-sampling FL enjoys, leaving a gap for very large client populations.
