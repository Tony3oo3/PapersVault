## Problem

Best-of-N sampling achieves strong reward-KL trade-offs for LLM alignment but incurs N× inference cost, while standard RLHF (e.g., REINFORCE with KL regularization) requires committing to a fixed regularization strength that trades off reward against KL divergence. No prior method distills the full Best-of-N distribution into a single-sample policy while avoiding the need to pre-select a regularization level or a fixed N.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252131/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x and produces a single autoregressive text generation y at inference time, matching the cost of a standard LLM forward pass. During training, for each prompt the algorithm draws 1 sample from the current policy π_t and 2 samples from a moving anchor policy π_anchor, scores all three with a pre-trained reward model r(·), and uses the pairwise comparisons to compute a distribution-matching loss. The trained policy is a standard transformer LLM (Gemma 2B/7B or T5) that generates text conditioned on a prompt, with no additional inference-time overhead beyond a single decode.

## Method

BOND derives the exact analytical form of the Best-of-N distribution (Theorem 1), showing it reweights the reference policy by a quantile-based factor p_≤(y)^(N−1) times a bounded collision correction. It then casts alignment as minimizing a divergence between the training policy and this BoN distribution, using the Jeffreys divergence (a weighted combination of forward and backward KL with weight β=0.5) to balance mode-covering (SFT on BoN samples) and mode-seeking (policy gradient with a quantile-based advantage) behaviors. The key practical algorithm, J-BOND, iteratively distills the Best-of-2 of an exponential-moving-average anchor (updated each step with coefficient η), avoiding the need to fix N in advance while keeping sample complexity to just 3 generations per prompt. The backward KL component uses a crude binary reward (−log 16 if the policy sample is worse than both anchor samples, 0 otherwise) calibrated so its expectation matches the true log-quantile reward at the median, plus an optional additional KL regularization term toward the anchor for stability.

## Results

On Gemma 7B conversational alignment, J-BOND consistently outperforms standard REINFORCE RLHF across all downstream benchmarks: GPQA (31.2 vs 26.6), GSM8K (54.2 vs 45.4), MATH (28.0 vs 26.8), and BBH (54.1 vs 53.3). In side-by-side quality comparisons judged by Gemini, J-BOND also edges out REINFORCE against Mistral 7B v1 (0.36 vs 0.34), Mistral 7B v2 (0.17 vs 0.16), and Mixtral 8x7B (0.07 vs 0.04). Crucially, J-BOND achieves a better reward/KL Pareto front than all REINFORCE variants (β_RL ∈ {0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1}) while requiring only 1 policy sample and 2 anchor samples per prompt at inference, versus N samples for Best-of-N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against REINFORCE (Ahmadian et al., 2024) with leave-one-out baseline, J-BOND wins on every reported metric: it achieves higher reward at comparable or lower KL, and the advantage is most pronounced on GSM8K (+8.8 points) and GPQA (+4.6 points). A key practical advantage is that REINFORCE is highly sensitive to the regularization coefficient β_RL (larger β yields lower reward and lower KL), whereas J-BOND continuously improves reward with a stable, linear KL increase without requiring the user to commit to a specific regularization strength. Against vBON (Amini et al., 2024), which uses only backward KL and requires a fixed N in advance, J-BOND achieves a better reward/KL trade-off across all tested N values (4, 8, 64, 128, 512) on Gemma 7B, attributed to its iterative moving-anchor procedure and the use of Jeffreys divergence (combining forward and backward KL) rather than backward KL alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOND builds directly on Best-of-N sampling (Stiennon et al., 2020) and standard KL-regularized RLHF, deriving a clean analytical equivalence between the two: Best-of-N is exactly the optimal policy for a KL-regularized RL objective with a log-quantile reward and regularization strength 1/(N-1). This connects to prior work on supervised fine-tuning on Best-of-N data (RAFT, Dong et al. 2023; Llama 2, Touvron et al. 2023), which implicitly minimizes forward KL, and to the concurrent vBON (Amini et al., 2024), which uses only backward KL with a fixed N. BOND departs from both by using the Jeffreys divergence (a weighted combination of forward and backward KL) and by replacing the fixed-N formulation with an iterative distillation procedure using a small n and an EMA moving anchor, drawing on the weight-averaging ideas from WARP (Ramé et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: J-BOND achieves Best-of-N quality with a single inference-time sample, eliminating the N-fold computational overhead that makes Best-of-N impractical for deployment. The iterative formulation with EMA anchor removes the need to commit to a regularization strength upfront, which is a persistent pain point in RLHF tuning. Open questions include whether the crude 2-sample reward estimate (with the fixed -log(16) penalty) remains sufficient at scale, whether the approach extends to settings without an explicit reward model, and whether the gains hold for much larger models or more complex multi-turn tasks. The experimental evidence is currently limited to Gemma 2B/7B and XSum, so broader validation is needed.
