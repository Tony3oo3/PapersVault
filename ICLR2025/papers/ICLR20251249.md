## Problem

Existing variance-reduction methods for off-policy policy evaluation design behavior policies that minimize estimation variance but ignore safety constraints, meaning the data-collecting policy could cause severe damage during online execution (e.g., overheating a data center cooling system). No prior method simultaneously achieves substantial variance reduction and guaranteed safety of the behavior policy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target policy π and a behavior-policy-agnostic offline dataset of logged tuples (t_i, s_i, a_i, r_i, c_i, s'_i) that need not form complete trajectories and may originate from unknown or diverse behavior policies. It outputs a time-indexed behavior policy μ* = {μ*_0, ..., μ*_{T-1}} that minimizes PDIS estimation variance while satisfying a per-step safety constraint E_{a~μ_t}[q^c_{μ,t}(s,a)] ≤ (1+ε)·v^c_{π,t}(s) for all t and s. The safety threshold is defined relative to the target policy's expected cost, with ε=0 guaranteeing the behavior policy is at least as safe as the target policy itself.

## Method

The key insight is to decompose the sequential variance-minimization problem into independent per-step convex optimization problems by introducing an extended reward function r̃_t(s,a) that captures the total variance contribution at each time step. Lemma 5 provides a recursive, model-free expression for r̃_t that avoids approximating the full trajectory variance, enabling learning from segmented offline data via Fitted Q-Evaluation. At each step t (processed backward from T-1 to 0), the algorithm solves a convex program: minimize E_{a~μ_t}[ρ_t² r̃_t(s,a)] subject to the safety constraint, which is proven convex and feasible (Theorem 1). The resulting policy μ* is shown to be unbiased (Lemma 4) and to achieve strictly lower variance than on-policy evaluation while satisfying the safety constraint (Theorems 2–3). The variance itself decomposes into the succinct form V(G^PDIS|S_t=s) = E_{a~μ}[ρ_t² r̃_t(s,a)] − v²_{π,t}(s), enabling model-free variance estimation from offline data without trajectory-level variance terms.

## Results

On MuJoCo (5 environments), SCOPE saves 25.4%–57.5% of the execution cost needed to reach the same estimation accuracy as on-policy Monte Carlo (e.g., 425 vs. 1000 cost units on InvertedPendulum). On Gridworld (1,000 and 27,000 states), SCOPE saves approximately 52% cost. Critically, SCOPE is the only method whose average trajectory cost stays below the on-policy baseline (≤1.0) across all environments, satisfying the safety constraint with ε=0, while ODI reaches 1.4–1.7× the on-policy cost and ROS reaches 0.99–1.06×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ODI (Liu & Zhang, ICML 2024), SCOPE trades a small variance advantage (ODI achieves slightly lower relative variance, e.g., 0.351 vs. 0.439 on I.Pendulum) for a large safety advantage: ODI's trajectory cost is 1.4–1.7× the on-policy baseline (violating the ε=0 safety constraint), while SCOPE stays at 0.88–0.96×. Against ROS (Zhong et al., NeurIPS 2022), SCOPE dominates on both metrics: ROS has higher relative variance (0.90–1.03 vs. 0.44–0.84) and cost at or above the on-policy level (0.99–1.06) in most environments, meaning it neither reduces variance nor satisfies the safety constraint. Against the on-policy Monte Carlo baseline, SCOPE simultaneously reduces variance (relative variance 0.44–0.84 vs. 1.0) and reduces execution cost (0.88–0.96×), confirming the theoretical guarantee that the constrained optimal behavior policy is strictly better than the target policy in both dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Liu and Zhang (2024, ODI), which established an optimal offline-learnable behavior policy for variance reduction in PDIS-based policy evaluation, by embedding the behavior policy design within a Constrained MDP (CMDP) framework with explicit safety constraints. It departs from the broader variance-reduction line (Hanna et al. 2017, Zhong et al. 2022, Mukherjee et al. 2022) that searches for or designs behavior policies purely to minimize estimation variance while ignoring execution safety, and from the safe RL line (Achiam et al. 2017, Berkenkamp et al. 2017, Wachi and Sui 2020) that enforces constraints during policy optimization rather than during the evaluation phase. The key conceptual move is to treat the step-wise behavior policy design as a sequence of convex one-step bandit problems with a safety constraint, using a backward-recursive extended reward to avoid approximating full trajectory variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provably achieve both unbiasedness, variance reduction below on-policy evaluation, and strict safety constraint satisfaction simultaneously, which matters for real-world deployments (data centers, robotics) where an unsafe data-collecting policy can cause irreversible damage. The finding that the prior best method (ODI) incurs 1.5–2× the execution cost of on-policy evaluation reveals that variance reduction without safety is not merely incomplete but actively harmful in practice. Limitations include reliance on sufficiently large offline data (the authors recommend falling back to on-policy when data is scarce) and restriction to the undiscounted, fixed-horizon PDIS setting; extending the constrained variance-minimization framework to temporal difference learning is identified as future work.
