## Problem

Existing Bayesian regret bounds for GP bandit algorithms (GP-UCB, GP-TS) only cover finite or static arm settings, and no regret bound existed for GP-BayesUCB in any setting. This paper fills that gap by providing the first Bayesian cumulative regret bounds for GP-UCB, GP-TS, and GP-BUCB in the combinatorial volatile Gaussian process semi-bandit problem with potentially infinite arm sets, where the available arms and feasible super arms change each round.

## Input / Output

At each round the agent receives a (possibly random) finite subset of base arms from a continuous d-dimensional space, a context vector per arm, and a set of feasible super arms (subsets of size at most K). The algorithm outputs a selected super arm and observes additive rewards for each chosen base arm. In the navigation application, the base arm space is the Cartesian product of road edges and a 3-dimensional context (length, speed limit, incline), and the super arms are simple paths in a directed line graph; the output is a path whose total energy consumption is minimized.

## Method

The paper derives Bayesian regret bounds of order O(√(λ*_K · T · K · β_T · γ_TK)) for all three algorithms, where γ_TK is the maximal information gain from TK base arms and λ*_K is the maximum eigenvalue of posterior covariance matrices of size K. For the infinite-arm case, a discretization argument is used with finer grid requirements than prior work to handle the fact that the discretized optimal super arm may not be feasible under volatile arms; the key technical lemma bounds the discretization error of the upper confidence bound. GP-BUCB replaces the log-based β_t of GP-UCB with a quantile-based parameter β_t = 2(erf⁻¹(1−2η_t))², offering tunable exploration via parameters ω and ξ while retaining sublinear regret. For the navigation experiments, a graph Matérn kernel (encoding line-graph structure) is combined with a 5/2-Matérn context kernel, scaled via sparse variational GPs for tractability, and rectified Gaussian distributions ensure non-negative edge weights compatible with Dijkstra's shortest-path algorithm.

## Results

On the Luxembourg and Monaco road networks (T=500 rounds), GP-TS achieves the lowest cumulative regret among all six evaluated methods, followed by GP-BUCB and GP-UCB. GP-based methods consistently outperform their Bayesian inference (BI) counterparts across all three algorithm families (UCB, BUCB, TS). The theoretically valid β_t parametrization for GP-BUCB yields smaller confidence values than GP-UCB, and lowering ξ from 1 to 0.5 further reduces regret while maintaining O(√T) theoretical guarantees. For GP-based methods, increasing the kernel lengthscale from 0.1 to 2.0 generally increases cumulative regret, while BI-based methods show no discernible pattern.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GP-UCB, GP-BUCB, and GP-TS against their Bayesian inference (BI) counterparts (BI-UCB, BI-BUCB, BI-TS) from Åkerblom et al. (2023) on four routes across the Luxembourg and Monaco road networks. GP-based methods win across all three algorithm families: GP-TS achieves the lowest cumulative regret overall, followed by GP-BUCB and GP-UCB, each outperforming their respective BI-based counterpart. Among the GP methods, GP-TS dominates both GP-UCB and GP-BUCB, while GP-BUCB beats GP-UCB due to its more flexible β_t parametrization that reduces over-exploration. The BI methods from Åkerblom et al. serve as the primary empirical baseline, and the GP framework's ability to exploit spatial correlations in the road network (via the graph Matérn kernel) provides the key advantage over the independent-edge BI assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate extensions of GP bandits—combinatorial (Nika et al., 2022), volatile (Russo & Roy, 2014), and infinite-arm (Srinivas et al., 2012; Takeno et al., 2023)—into a single Bayesian regret analysis for the combinatorial volatile GP semi-bandit. It departs from Nika et al. (2022) by providing Bayesian rather than frequentist bounds and by covering GP-TS and GP-BUCB in addition to GP-UCB. The GP-BUCB algorithm, introduced by Nuara et al. (2018) for combinatorial advertising, had no prior regret guarantees even in the non-combinatorial setting; this paper establishes the first such bound by adapting the BayesUCB quantile framework of Kaufmann et al. (2012) to the GP posterior. The practical application extends the Bayesian inference navigation framework of Åkerblom et al. (2023) to a contextual GP setting with sparse variational inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first Bayesian regret bound for GP-BUCB fills a notable gap in the GP bandit literature and demonstrates that its quantile-based parametrization offers more tunable exploration-exploitation trade-offs than GP-UCB while retaining theoretical guarantees. The combinatorial volatile infinite framework subsumes contextual bandits as a special case, providing a unified theoretical lens for a broad class of practical problems. Open questions include the gap between the O(√(TKβ_T γ_TK)) upper bound and known lower bounds (Ω(√(T(log T)^{d/2})) for the SE kernel), and whether the √β_T factor can be eliminated as Takeno et al. (2023) achieved for the non-combinatorial case. The EV navigation experiments, while on small-scale networks (Luxembourg, Monaco), validate the framework's practicality but leave scalability to city-scale or national networks untested.
