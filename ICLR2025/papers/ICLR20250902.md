## Problem

Offline multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) suffers from severe performance degradation when training data is imbalanced in both temporal dimensions (mixing high- and low-reward segments) and spatial dimensions (some agents underperform while others cooperate well). Existing trajectory stitching and data augmentation methods are designed for single-agent settings and fail to account for the intricate inter-agent interactions, making them ineffective at recovering high-quality coordination from imbalanced multi-agent datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250902/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of joint multi-agent trajectories (observations, actions, rewards) that has been deliberately perturbed to introduce temporal and spatial imbalance, along with a fixed initial joint observation and a target return condition R_s. The diffusion model operates over joint observation sequences of length H (not actions, which are multi-discrete and harder to model), and the output is an augmented dataset D* = D ∪ D_aug where generated trajectories have been validated for environmental dynamics consistency and corrected for underperforming agents. The augmented dataset is then used by any agnostic offline MARL algorithm (BC, OMIGA, CFCQL) for policy learning.

## Method

MADiTS is a three-stage diffusion-based data augmentation pipeline. First, a DDPM is trained on trajectory segments conditioned on high discounted returns to generate candidate joint observation sequences via head-to-tail stitching. Second, a bidirectional dynamics constraint—using both an inverse dynamics model (inferring actions from consecutive observations) and a forward dynamics model (predicting next observations from state-action pairs)—truncates any generated segment that violates environmental dynamics beyond a reconstruction threshold δ_recon. Third, an integrated-gradient-based credit assignment decomposes the team return-to-go into per-agent contributions via PathIG over a learned reward model; agents whose average contribution ranking falls below a threshold δ_rank are identified as underperforming, and a partial noising step re-generates only their trajectory portions while fixing the observations of well-performing agents, narrowing the search space for cooperative behavior correction. This generate–validate–correct loop iterates until a predefined number of augmented trajectories are produced.

## Results

On MPE (Cooperative Navigation, Predator-Prey, World), MADiTS achieves an average normalized return of 45.83 (exp-m) and 43.50 (exp-s), compared to 27.01/18.35 for the original imbalanced dataset, 34.02/28.41 for MA-MBTS, and 40.14/36.88 for MADiff, approaching the balanced-dataset baseline of 53.17. On SMAC (2m_vs_1z, 3m, 2s3z), MADiTS achieves an average battle win rate of 0.82 (exp-m) and 0.74 (exp-s), versus 0.65/0.60 for Original, 0.69/0.65 for MA-MBTS, and 0.78/0.70 for MADiff, compared to 0.87 for Balanced. In several environments (e.g., PP with OMIGA, 2m_vs_1z with OMIGA/CFCQL), MADiTS even surpasses the balanced-dataset baseline, indicating successful exploitation of high-quality segments within imbalanced data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MADiTS consistently outperforms both baselines across all imbalanced datasets and all three evaluation algorithms (BC, OMIGA, CFCQL). Compared to MA-MBTS (model-based trajectory stitching extended to multi-agent), MADiTS achieves substantially larger gains: on MPE, MADiTS improves the average from 34.02 to 45.83 (exp-m), a +11.81 point gain, while MA-MBTS only improves from 27.01 to 34.02 (+7.01). On SMAC, MADiTS reaches 0.82 vs. MA-MBTS's 0.69 (exp-m), a +0.13 gap. Compared to MADiff (diffusion-based offline MARL applied for stitching), MADiTS still leads by a clear margin: 45.83 vs. 40.14 on MPE (exp-m) and 0.82 vs. 0.78 on SMAC (exp-m). Notably, in environments like PP (OMIGA: 63.02 vs. Balanced 58.34) and 2m_vs_1z (OMIGA: 0.98 vs. Balanced 0.93), MADiTS surpasses even the balanced-dataset baseline, demonstrating that the stitching pipeline can extract and recombine high-quality segments more effectively than training on uniformly good data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent diffusion-based trajectory stitching paradigm (DiffStitch, Li et al. 2024) to the multi-agent setting, adding two mechanisms absent from prior stitching work: a bidirectional dynamics constraint (inverse + forward dynamics models) to filter out physically inconsistent generated segments, and an integrated-gradient-based credit assignment with partial noising to selectively re-generate trajectories of underperforming agents. The diffusion backbone follows DDPMs (Ho et al., 2020) with classifier-free guidance conditioned on target return, and the implementation reuses the U-Net architecture from MADiff (Zhu et al., 2023). Conceptually, the paper bridges two previously separate threads—trajectory stitching for offline RL and offline credit assignment in cooperative MARL—into a unified data augmentation pipeline that addresses both temporal and spatial imbalance simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is algorithm-agnostic and directly applicable to any offline MARL algorithm, making it a practical tool for real-world settings where data collection is costly and imbalanced. However, the approach assumes that team return faithfully reflects individual agent quality, which may break down in sparse-reward environments, and the diffusion model cannot generate truly novel high-quality segments if none exist in the training data (OOD problem). The paper identifies LLM integration and extension to more complex embodied multi-agent systems as promising future directions, but the current evaluation is limited to four benchmark suites with relatively small agent counts (≤12).
