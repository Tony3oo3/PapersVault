## Problem

Reward over-optimization in RLHF arises when the reward model extrapolates poorly on out-of-distribution (OOD) responses generated during RL, causing the proxy reward to diverge from true human preferences. Existing mitigations (KL penalties, constrained PPO, reward ensembles) either constrain the policy to a region that does not align with the reward model's in-distribution (ID) region, or pessimistically penalize both ID and OOD values, potentially yielding suboptimal policies. No prior method explicitly models the ID boundary of the reward model while selectively penalizing only OOD values.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and, during RL, uses a ScoreLM model that simultaneously predicts a scalar reward (via a score head) and the next-token distribution over the vocabulary (via the retained language model head) to determine whether each generated token is behavior-supported. The output is an aligned LLM policy (Alpaca-7B in experiments) that generates text responses constrained to the reward model's ID region. A key design choice is that the behavior-supported V-operator checks support at the *previous* token (β(a_{t-1}|s_{t-1})>0) rather than the current one, leveraging the deterministic state transition of autoregressive generation to achieve equivalent policy evaluation with greater training stability.

## Method

BSPO defines a behavior policy β as the next-token distribution of the reward training dataset, partitioning actions into supported (β(a|s)>0, i.e., ID) and unsupported (β(a|s)=0, i.e., OOD) at each state. It introduces a behavior-supported Bellman operator T_β^π that applies the standard Bellman update for supported actions and clamps the Q-value to Q_min for unsupported actions, yielding a regularized value function that underestimates OOD returns while preserving unbiased ID estimates. Theoretically, this operator is γ-contraction, its fixed point equals the standard Q-value for all ID actions under a behavior-supported policy, and the resulting policy iteration guarantees strict monotonic improvement until convergence to the optimal behavior-supported policy. In implementation, a ScoreLM model (transformer with both a score head and the original LM head, trained with a combined preference + supervised loss) provides both reward and behavior distribution predictions, and the algorithm plugs the behavior-supported V-operator into standard PPO's critic update, requiring negligible additional compute over vanilla PPO.

## Results

Across three proxy model scales (774M GPT-2-large, 1.1B TinyLlama, 2.7B ShearedLlama), BSPO consistently achieves the highest gold reward while maintaining upward trends in both proxy and gold rewards, whereas most baselines exhibit reward over-optimization (proxy reward rises while gold reward plateaus or declines). On the 2.7B proxy setting, BSPO achieves the highest win rate against the initial Alpaca-7B model on the test set. The behavior policy OOD detection shows the proxy model predicts preferences with 75.91% accuracy on supported responses but drops to 58.10% on unsupported responses, validating the ID/OOD distinction. BSPO keeps the average number of behavior-unsupported actions per response consistently low throughout training, while traditional methods show a sharp increase coinciding with over-optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSPO outperforms all five baselines in mitigating reward over-optimization and achieving the highest gold reward across all three proxy model scales. Standard PPO and KL-Penalty both suffer from over-optimization; KL-Penalty additionally restricts the policy to a proximal region near the initial model and fails to maintain gold reward gains at larger KL divergence distances, whereas BSPO avoids over-optimization even at larger distances. CPPO (Constrained PPO) keeps the proxy reward near a threshold but the gold reward continues to drift, and it introduces instability from the Lagrangian approach. Ensemble methods (ENS-UWO and ENS-WCO) mitigate overestimation within the ID region but cannot handle OOD responses, as multiple ensemble members may consistently overestimate in the OOD region, and the frequency of OOD responses increases during policy iteration. BSPO's key advantage is that it penalizes OOD values without affecting ID ones, allowing it to find the same optimal ID policies as standard policy evaluation, which explains its superior win rate against the initial model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the concept of behavior-supported actions from offline RL (Wu et al., 2022) to the RLHF setting, using the next-token distribution of the reward training data as a proxy for the reward model's in-distribution region. It departs from the dominant line of reward regularization work (KL penalties, constrained PPO, reward ensembles) by shifting the regularization from the reward signal to the value function, enabling selective penalization of OOD actions without distorting ID evaluations. The implementation builds on PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and introduces a ScoreLM architecture that jointly predicts rewards and behavior distributions, drawing on hidden-state regularization techniques for reward model generalization (Yang et al., 2024b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BSPO provides the first value-regularization-based method for reward over-optimization with a monotonic improvement guarantee and convergence to the optimal ID policy—properties that KL-penalty and ensemble methods lack. The finding that the reward model's ID region is substantially larger than what KL distance constraints capture (Section 5.4) suggests prior methods were unnecessarily conservative. Key limitations include reliance on a synthetic gold-model setup, the assumption of a well-trained reward model, and unaddressed OOD prompts during RL. Extending BSPO to multi-objective reward settings and validating in real human-evaluation pipelines are natural next steps.
