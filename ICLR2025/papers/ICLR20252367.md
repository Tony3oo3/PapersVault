## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for diffusion models (designed for U-Net backbones or class-conditioned DiTs) suffer severe quality degradation when applied to text-to-image and text-to-video generation with larger DiT models. The root causes are high data variation across multiple dimensions (token, condition, timestep, channel), a unique time-varying channel imbalance in DiTs, and the fact that minimizing a single quantization error metric is insufficient to preserve the multi-faceted generation quality (text alignment, visual quality, temporal consistency) required by visual generation tasks.

## Input / Output

ViDiT-Q operates as a post-training quantization pipeline: it takes an FP16 DiT model (e.g., OpenSORA, PixArt-α, Latte, InfDiT) and a small calibration set of text prompts, then produces a quantized model with W8A8, W6A6, or W4A8 bit-widths for the linear layers. Only linear layers are quantized (attention QK computation is left in FP16), and the quantized model generates images (e.g., 512×512) or videos (e.g., 512×512×16 frames) with negligible quality loss relative to the FP16 baseline. The method also produces efficient CUDA kernels that achieve 2–2.5× memory reduction and 1.4–1.7× end-to-end latency speedup on A100 GPUs.

## Method

ViDiT-Q introduces three complementary techniques tailored to DiT quantization. First, it adopts fine-grained (channel-wise for weights, token-wise for activations) and dynamic (online-computed) quantization parameters, which naturally adapt to variation across timesteps and classifier-free-guidance conditions without costly offline calibration. Second, it proposes a static-dynamic channel balancing scheme that decomposes the time-varying channel imbalance into a static component (addressed by SmoothQuant-style per-channel scaling at the initial denoising stage) and a dynamic component (addressed by QuaRot-style Hadamard rotation), combining the strengths of both to reduce incoherence within each quantization group. Third, for the challenging W4A8 setting, it introduces a metric-decoupled mixed precision allocation that categorizes layers by type (cross-attention, spatial attention/FFN, temporal attention) and assigns higher bit-widths to the most sensitive layers within each group using task-specific metrics (CLIPScore, VQA, FlowScore) rather than a single MSE proxy, thereby preserving text alignment, visual quality, and temporal consistency simultaneously.

## Results

On VBench (OpenSORA text-to-video), ViDiT-Q W8A8 achieves an Overall Consistency of 26.06 vs. 26.21 for FP16, and W4A8 (with mixed precision) reaches 25.94, while all baselines collapse at W4A8 (best baseline: QuaRot at 18.43). On COCO (PixArt-α text-to-image), ViDiT-Q W4A8 attains FID 74.33, CLIPScore 0.257, and ImageReward 0.887, closely matching FP16 (FID 73.34, CLIP 0.258, IR 0.901), whereas Q-DiT W4A8 catastrophically degrades to FID 475.8. Hardware experiments on A100 GPUs show 1.99× memory reduction at W8A8, 2.42× at W4A8, and 1.71× end-to-end latency speedup at W8A8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At W8A8, ViDiT-Q matches or slightly exceeds FP16 on most VBench dimensions (Imaging Quality 63.48 vs. 63.68; Overall Consistency 26.06 vs. 26.21), outperforming all baselines including Q-DiT (26.09), SmoothQuant (25.66), and QuaRot (25.43). At the more challenging W4A8, ViDiT-Q with metric-decoupled mixed precision achieves Overall Consistency of 25.94, while Q-DiT collapses to 4.99, PTQ4DiT to 11.46, SmoothQuant to 18.25, and QuaRot to 18.43 — a gap of 7.5–21 points. On COCO image generation, ViDiT-Q W4A8 (FID 74.33) is within 1.4 FID of FP16 (73.34), whereas Q-DiT W4A8 degrades to FID 475.8 and PTQ4DiT to 171.9. General-purpose quantization methods AdaRound and BRECQ also fail at W4A8 (near-zero VQA scores), confirming that DiT-specific channel-balancing is essential. The paper further shows that combining ViDiT-Q with SageAttention yields a marginal additional latency gain (1.71× → 1.72×) without quality loss, since linear layers dominate >80% of computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two complementary channel-balancing techniques from LLM quantization: SmoothQuant's per-channel scaling mask (to shift quantization difficulty between weights and activations) and QuaRot's Hadamard rotation (to spread outlier values across channels). The key departure is the diagnosis that DiT models exhibit a *time-varying* channel imbalance that neither technique handles alone: scaling is sensitive to a timestep-dependent α, while rotation still leaves residual incoherence. ViDiT-Q decomposes the imbalance into a 'static' component (from pretrained scale-shift tables) addressed by scaling and a 'dynamic' component (from time embeddings) addressed by rotation, and pairs this with dynamic (online) quantization parameters and a metric-decoupled mixed-precision allocation. It extends prior DiT quantization work (Q-DiT, PTQ4DiT), which targeted class-conditioned generation, to the substantially harder text-to-image and text-to-video setting with larger models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 2–2.5× memory reduction and 1.4–1.7× end-to-end latency speedup for DiT models that currently consume >10 GB of GPU memory for a single short video, making edge deployment more feasible. The static/dynamic decomposition of channel imbalance provides a reusable diagnostic for any diffusion transformer, and the metric-decoupled mixed-precision framework is generalizable beyond DiTs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to lower activation bit-widths (W4A4, W2A8) without quality loss, whether the mixed-precision allocation can be automated more robustly, and whether the findings generalize to non-DiT diffusion architectures (e.g., U-Net-based video models) or to quantization-aware training settings.
