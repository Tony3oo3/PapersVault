## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for LLMs rely on heuristic linear transformations (smooth-based or rotation-based) that improve data distributions only in specific regions of the quantization space, without a principled metric to evaluate overall quantizability. This leaves a gap in understanding how to globally optimize weight and activation distributions across the entire quantization space to maximize bit precision utilization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained LLM (LLaMA-1/2/3 family, 7B–70B) and approximately 1,000 calibration samples from WikiText2 at 2,048 token length. It outputs a 4-bit quantized model in W4-only, W4A4, or W4A4KV4 configurations where all learned orthogonal and scaling transformation pairs are fused directly into the existing weight matrices, introducing zero additional runtime parameters or computational overhead. Quantization uses per-channel symmetric for weights and per-token asymmetric for activations.

## Method

OSTQuant introduces the Quantization Space Utilization Rate (QSUR), a metric measuring the ratio of data ellipsoid volume to the enclosing quantization hypercube volume, with mathematical derivations showing that maximum QSUR is achieved when the covariance matrix becomes identity. Guided by this, the method assigns learnable equivalent transformation pairs—each a diagonal scaling matrix times a unit orthogonal matrix—to every fully-connected layer, optimized globally via RiemannAdam on the Stiefel manifold so that the network output remains mathematically invariant (preventing overfitting to the small calibration set). A KL-Top loss restricts KL divergence computation to the top-k highest-probability logits (k=1000) to capture richer semantic signal while filtering long-tail noise from the 100k+ vocabulary. Weight Outlier Minimization Initialization (WOMI) seeds the orthogonal matrices using eigenvalue decomposition of concatenated residual-path weights combined with a normalized Hadamard matrix, providing a strong starting point that reduces outliers before optimization begins.

## Results

In the W4-only (4-16-16) setting, OSTQuant retains 99.5%+ of floating-point accuracy across all tested LLaMA models, with only a 0.29-point zero-shot drop on LLaMA-3-8B (67.80 vs. 68.09 FP). In the most challenging W4A4KV4 (4-4-4) setting, OSTQuant achieves 65.37 zero-shot accuracy on LLaMA-3-8B, outperforming the previous SOTA SpinQuant (64.10) by 1.27 points and QuaRot (61.38) by 3.99 points. The method also delivers over 2× inference speedup and 3.5× memory savings in 4-bit deployment, completing quantization of LLaMA-3-8B in ~20 minutes on a single A800 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSTQuant consistently outperforms all six baselines across every quantization setting and model. Against SpinQuant (the closest competitor), OSTQuant gains 1.27 points on LLaMA-3-8B W4A4KV4 (65.37 vs. 64.10) and up to 6.53 points on LLaMA-2-7B W4A4-16 (63.90 vs. 57.37), where SpinQuant's learned rotations struggle with activation outliers. Against QuaRot, the gap is even larger in W4A4KV4 (3.99 points on LLaMA-3-8B), confirming that random Hadamard rotations are less effective than OSTQuant's jointly optimized orthogonal+scaling pairs. In the W4-only setting, OSTQuant edges out AWQ (67.80 vs. 67.03 on LLaMA-3-8B) and QuaRot (67.80 vs. 67.27), while GPTQ and SmoothQuant lag significantly (61.03 and 62.79 respectively), particularly on LLaMA-3-8B where their heuristic transformations fail to handle the model's more pronounced outlier structure. OmniQuant, which uses block-wise reconstruction, is outperformed by OSTQuant in both W4-only and W4A4KV4 settings, and OSTQuant also achieves 5.3× faster quantization training (0.3h vs. 1.6h on 7B models).

## Relation to Prior Work

OSTQuant sits at the intersection of two PTQ paradigms: smooth-based methods (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant) that use diagonal scaling to balance inter-channel variance, and rotation-based methods (QuaRot, SpinQuant) that use orthogonal matrices to suppress outliers. The key departure is that prior approaches are described as 'heuristic'—each addresses a specific symptom (outliers, channel imbalance) without a unifying objective—whereas OSTQuant introduces QSUR as a principled metric and derives the theoretically optimal linear transformation (T = c·Λ^{-1/2}·Q^T) that simultaneously equalizes eigenvalues and minimizes the quantization hypercube. By combining a learnable orthogonal matrix (optimized on the Stiefel manifold via RiemannAdam) with a learnable diagonal scaling matrix into a single equivalent transformation pair, the method subsumes both smoothing and rotation as special cases while preserving network output equivalence, avoiding the overfitting risk of block-wise reconstruction used by OmniQuant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The QSUR metric provides the field with a quantitative, interpretable diagnostic for quantization difficulty that was previously absent, and its positive correlation with accuracy (Fig. 3) gives practitioners a principled way to evaluate transformation quality beyond end-to-end perplexity. Practically, the 5.3× speedup over OmniQuant (20 minutes for LLaMA-3-8B vs. ~1.6 hours) makes high-quality W4A4KV4 quantization accessible on a single GPU, lowering the barrier for deploying 4-bit LLMs on edge and cloud hardware. Open questions include whether the Gaussian-distribution assumption underlying QSUR holds for non-LLM architectures (e.g., multimodal models, diffusion transformers), whether the approach extends to full activation quantization (sketched in Appendix A.5 but not validated), and whether the KL-Top loss generalizes beyond the WikiText2 calibration set used here.
