## Problem

Pretrained ControlNets cannot be directly transferred to new image/video diffusion backbones due to feature space mismatches, and training new ControlNets from scratch requires 500–600 A100 GPU hours. Moreover, applying image ControlNets frame-by-frame to video generation ignores temporal consistency, and no existing framework unifies image control, video control, sparse-frame control, and multi-condition composition across both U-Net and DiT architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253637/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a control condition image (depth map, canny edge, surface normal, segmentation map, human pose, line art, or soft edge) per frame, a text prompt, and optionally an initial frame for I2V models; for sparse-frame video control, conditions are provided for only a subset of frames (e.g., 4 out of 16). The ControlNet itself receives the control image c_f and timestep t (with the noisy latent z_t optionally skipped), while the adapter also receives timestep embeddings and cross-attends to text/initial-frame condition embeddings. Output is a controlled image (1024×1024 for SDXL) or video (8–16 frames depending on backbone) that follows the spatial structure of the control condition while matching the text description.

## Method

CTRL-Adapter trains lightweight adapter blocks to map the middle/output block features of a frozen pretrained SDv1.5 ControlNet onto the corresponding blocks of a frozen target diffusion backbone, with only the adapter parameters updated. Each adapter block stacks four modules—spatial convolution, temporal convolution, spatial attention, and temporal attention—where the temporal modules inject cross-frame consistency for video and the attention modules condition on text/initial-frame embeddings via cross-attention. Three key design choices enable robust cross-backbone transfer: (1) skipping the noisy latent z_t from ControlNet inputs to handle different noise scales (e.g., SVD's LogNormal σ) and sparse-frame conditions; (2) inverse timestep sampling that maps a uniform u∈[0,1] to paired discrete/continuous timesteps via CDF inversion, aligning ControlNet's discrete schedule with continuous-sampler backbones; and (3) a patch-level Mixture-of-Experts router (MLP or Q-Former variant) that assigns per-patch weights across multiple ControlNet experts for fine-grained multi-condition composition. For DiT-based backbones (Latte, PixArt-α), only the largest ControlNet feature map (block A) is mapped to interleaved DiT blocks, since all DiT blocks operate at the same spatial resolution unlike U-Net's coarse-to-fine hierarchy. Training requires fewer than 10 A100 GPU hours to outperform baselines, versus 500–700 hours for training a ControlNet from scratch.

## Results

On DAVIS 2017 for video control, CTRL-Adapter on the SVD backbone achieves the best FID of 3.82 (depth) and 3.96 (canny edge) with optical flow errors of 2.96 and 2.39, outperforming the SVD Temporal ControlNet (FID 4.91, flow 4.84). On I2VGen-XL, CTRL-Adapter reaches FID 7.43 (depth) and 6.42 (canny), the best overall video results. On COCO val2017 for image control, CTRL-Adapter on SDXL achieves SSIM 0.8534 (depth) and 0.6454 (soft edge), surpassing the SDXL ControlNet (SSIM 0.8363, 0.4458) in spatial fidelity while matching it in FID/MSE, all with <10 GPU hours of training versus 700+ for the SDXL ControlNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTRL-Adapter on SVD and I2VGen-XL backbones outperforms all video baselines (Text2Video-Zero, ControlVideo, Control-A-Video, VideoComposer) on both FID and optical flow error, with the SVD variant achieving FID 3.82 vs. the next-best 4.91 (SVD Temporal ControlNet). On the Hotshot-XL backbone, CTRL-Adapter dramatically improves over the directly-compatible SDXL ControlNet (FID 14.63 vs. 45.35 for depth), confirming the adapter's value even when a native ControlNet exists. For image control on COCO, CTRL-Adapter on SDXL beats X-Adapter on FID, MSE, and SSIM for depth (19.26/87.54/0.8534 vs. 20.71/90.08/0.7885) and on SSIM for canny (0.5806 vs. 0.3002), though SDXL ControlNet retains a slight FID edge (17.91 vs. 19.26) after 700 GPU hours of training. Against SDv1.5 ControlNet on the same backbone, CTRL-Adapter wins on FID for depth (19.26 vs. 21.25) and soft edge (18.08 vs. 26.59) and on SSIM across all conditions, but loses on FID for canny (21.04 vs. 18.90). On the DiT-based PixArt-α backbone, CTRL-Adapter outperforms PixArt-δ ControlNet on FID for soft edge (17.52 vs. 20.41) while achieving a slightly lower SSIM (0.6812 vs. 0.6938). Overall, CTRL-Adapter matches or exceeds every baseline in at least one metric per condition, with the most consistent wins in spatial control (SSIM/flow error) and the most dramatic gains when adapting to larger or architecturally different backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

CTRL-Adapter builds directly on ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023c) for spatial conditioning of diffusion models and on the adapter-layer paradigm (Houlsby et al., 2019) for parameter-efficient feature mapping. It departs from X-Adapter (Ran et al., 2024), which also bridges ControlNets to larger backbones but is restricted to image generation and requires the source SDv1.5 U-Net at both training and inference time. The key conceptual extension is adding temporal convolution/attention modules for video consistency, latent-skipping for robustness to different noise scales and sparse frames, inverse timestep sampling for continuous samplers, and a patch-level MoE router for fine-grained multi-condition composition — none of which appear in prior adapter-based control methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: matching or exceeding ControlNet-level performance in under 10 GPU hours versus 500–700+ hours for training a new ControlNet, across six diverse U-Net and DiT backbones, makes controllable generation accessible to users without large compute budgets. The unified framework (image, video, sparse-frame, multi-condition, zero-shot transfer) reduces the fragmentation of the controllable-generation ecosystem. Limitations include reliance on the SDv1.5 ControlNet ecosystem for pretrained condition extractors, qualitative-only evidence for zero-shot transfer, and the added architectural complexity of four-module adapters. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to future architectures beyond U-Net/DiT, whether the unified multi-task adapter degrades further with many more conditions, and whether patch-level routing can be extended to incorporate text/timestep conditioning for even more adaptive composition.
