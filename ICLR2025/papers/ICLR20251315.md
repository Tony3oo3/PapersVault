## Problem

Hypergraph Neural Networks (HGNNs) capture high-order multi-node relationships effectively but suffer from exponentially growing inference time and memory as hypergraph size and depth increase, making them impractical for real-time deployment. Existing distillation methods (GLNN, KRD, LightHGNN) transfer only soft labels from teacher to student, which cannot convey the complex high-order structural knowledge encoded in hyperedges. The paper asks whether a strategy can simultaneously distill soft labels and structural/high-order knowledge to a lightweight student model.

## Input / Output

The teacher consumes the node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×f} and the hypergraph incidence matrix H ∈ R^{n×m}, while the student (TinyGCN) operates on the projected adjacency matrix A^s ∈ R^{n×n} derived from H, eliminating the need for the hypergraph at inference time. The teacher produces node embeddings Z^t ∈ R^{n×d} and soft label distributions Y^t over |C| classes; the student produces its own embeddings Z^s ∈ R^{n×d} and predicted class labels. The deployable artifact is the student model (single-layer TinyGCN + MLP), which requires only the projected graph and node features at inference.

## Method

DistillHGNN employs a dual knowledge-transfer mechanism: (1) the teacher (3-layer HGNN + MLP) generates soft labels that supervise the student via KL divergence, and (2) a contrastive InfoNCE loss aligns the student's TinyGCN embeddings with the teacher's HGNN embeddings, transferring high-order structural knowledge that soft labels alone cannot capture. The student's TinyGCN is a single-layer GCN with no non-linear activation, reducing inference to one linear aggregation Z^s = Â^s X W^s. Training proceeds in two phases: the teacher is pre-trained with BPR loss plus contrastive loss, then frozen; the student is trained with supervised loss on labelled nodes plus KL divergence to the teacher's soft labels across all nodes. This combination yields a High-Order Preservation Score (H_pres) of 0.8275 on average, outperforming LightHGNN (0.7675) while achieving ~69% lower inference time than HGNN.

## Results

On the DBLP dataset at 80% training size, DistillHGNN achieves 80.61% accuracy, surpassing HGNN (80.42%) and LightHGNN (79.35%), while on CC-Cora it reaches 66.81% vs. HGNN's 66.04% and LightHGNN's 65.28%. DistillHGNN reduces inference time by approximately 69% compared to HGNN (e.g., 2.23 ms vs. 175.56 ms on IMDB at 40,635 nodes) while maintaining comparable accuracy. The High-Order Preservation Score (H_pres) reaches 0.8275 mean across four datasets, outperforming LightHGNN (0.7675), KRD (0.6075), and GLNN (0.575), confirming superior retention of structural knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DistillHGNN outperforms all seven baselines on accuracy across the four main datasets at 80% training size: it beats HGNN by 0.19–1.77 points (e.g., 80.61% vs. 80.42% on DBLP, 66.81% vs. 66.04% on CC-Cora) and LightHGNN by 1.29–1.46 points. Against graph-based distillation methods, the gains are larger: GLNN is outperformed by 8.67–11.57 points and KRD is not directly listed in Table 7 but is surpassed in the main Table 1. Compared to vanilla GNN and GCN, DistillHGNN leads by 10–16 points, and over MLP by 12–16 points. In terms of inference speed, DistillHGNN is ~79× faster than HGNN on IMDB (2.23 ms vs. 175.56 ms) and ~82× faster on DBLP (2.06 ms vs. 168.84 ms), while matching LightHGNN's inference time with higher accuracy. The High-Order Preservation Score confirms DistillHGNN retains more structural knowledge than LightHGNN (0.8275 vs. 0.7675 mean), GLNN (0.575), and KRD (0.6075).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation lineage from GNN-to-MLP methods (GLNN, KRD) and the recent HGNN-to-MLP distillation (LightHGNN) by introducing a dual transfer mechanism: soft labels plus contrastive learning (InfoNCE) to align student and teacher embeddings. The teacher is the standard HGNN (Feng et al., 2019) with its hypergraph Laplacian message-passing, while the student replaces the pure MLP used in LightHGNN with a single-layer, activation-free GCN (TinyGCN) derived from the GCN architecture of Kipf & Welling (2016). The key conceptual departure from LightHGNN is that soft labels alone are argued to be insufficient for conveying high-order hypergraph structure, so the contrastive loss explicitly transfers embedding-space geometry, not just class probabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation—enabling real-time inference on hypergraph-structured data (recommendation, citation networks, biological networks) without sacrificing the accuracy gains of HGNNs—is well-posed and the 69% inference speedup over HGNN is meaningful for deployment. However, the accuracy gains over the closest competitor (LightHGNN) are modest (1–3 points), and the method still requires the full hypergraph and HGNN during the offline teacher-training phase, so it does not eliminate the hypergraph dependency entirely. Open questions include whether the contrastive alignment generalises to dynamic or evolving hypergraphs, whether the single-layer TinyGCN bottleneck limits performance on very deep or heterogeneous hypergraphs, and whether the approach extends to link prediction or other hypergraph tasks beyond node classification.
