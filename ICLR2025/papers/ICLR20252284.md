## Problem

SOTA coreset selection methods rely on training-dynamics difficulty scores (AUM, EL2N, forgetting) that require ground-truth labels over the entire dataset, negating the goal of reducing human annotation cost. Existing label-free alternatives use geometry-based scores in embedding space, which perform poorly—sometimes worse than random sampling at high pruning rates. The paper asks how to harness the superior accuracy of training-dynamics scores in a fully label-free setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252284/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled RGB image dataset, the known number of classes C, and a subset budget k (expressed as a pruning rate α). A pretrained vision encoder (DINO or SwAV) extracts embeddings for all images, which are then used for k-nearest-neighbor graph construction and deep clustering. The output is a one-shot, model-agnostic subset of images selected for human annotation; the resulting labeled coreset can train any architecture (ResNet-18/34/50) from scratch and transfers across architectures and downstream tasks such as continual learning.

## Method

ELFS addresses two challenges. First, it generates pseudo-labels via TEMI deep clustering (an ensemble of student/teacher heads trained with a weighted pointwise-mutual-information objective over k-NN pairs in the encoder's embedding space), then trains a supervised model on those pseudo-labels to compute training-dynamics scores (AUM by default). Second, it identifies that pseudo-label noise induces a distribution shift that biases standard sampling (e.g., CCS) toward easy examples, and proposes a double-end pruning strategy: prune β hard examples first, then continue pruning easy examples until the budget k is met. The hyperparameter β is selected by grid search on a 90/10 pseudo-label train/validation split, avoiding any ground-truth labels. This simple modification significantly reduces the over-selection of easy examples and closes much of the gap to supervised coreset selection.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with the SwAV encoder, ELFS achieves 73.2% top-1 accuracy at 30% pruning, outperforming D2 (72.3%) by up to 10.2% at 70% pruning (66.8% vs. 55.6%). With the DINO encoder, ELFS reaches 73.5% at 30% pruning on ImageNet-1K, exceeding FreeSel (72.2%) by up to 3.9% at 90% pruning (54.9% vs. 51.1%). At 30% and 50% pruning on ImageNet-1K, ELFS (DINO) matches or slightly exceeds the best supervised coreset selection (CCS with ground-truth labels: 72.9% and 71.8%). ELFS is the first label-free method to outperform random sampling at all pruning rates across all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELFS outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and pruning rate. Against D2 (Maharana et al., 2023), the strongest SwAV-based label-free baseline, ELFS (SwAV) gains up to 10.2% on ImageNet-1K at 70% pruning (66.8% vs. 55.6%) and 10.2% at 90% (53.4% vs. 43.2%). Against FreeSel (Xie et al., 2024), the best DINO-based label-free baseline, ELFS (DINO) gains up to 3.9% on ImageNet-1K at 90% pruning (54.9% vs. 51.1%) and 2.4% at 70% (67.2% vs. 65.4%). Prototypicality (Sorscher et al., 2022) and BADGE (Ash et al., 2019) are substantially weaker, with Prototypicality dropping below random sampling at 80–90% pruning on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K. Most notably, at 30% and 50% pruning on ImageNet-1K, ELFS (DINO) achieves 73.5% and 71.8%, matching or exceeding the best supervised CCS (Zheng et al., 2023) which uses ground-truth labels (72.9% and 71.8%), demonstrating that label-free selection can approach supervised performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELFS bridges two previously separate lines of work: training-dynamics-based coreset selection (CCS, D2) that requires ground truth labels, and geometry-based label-free selection (Prototypicality, FreeSel) that avoids labels but produces inferior difficulty scores. The method repurposes TEMI deep clustering to generate pseudo-labels that proxy training dynamics scores (AUM, EL2N, forgetting), and adapts CCS's hard-pruning-plus-stratified-sampling strategy into a double-end pruning scheme to correct the distribution shift introduced by noisy pseudo-labels. Conceptually, the paper's key departure from prior label-free work is the claim that even heavily noisy pseudo-labels (ImageNet accuracy <60%) retain enough signal in training dynamics to outperform purely geometric importance scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ELFS demonstrates that label-free coreset selection can nearly match supervised coreset selection at moderate pruning rates (30–50% on ImageNet), which has direct practical implications for reducing human annotation budgets in vision pipelines. The approach is simple to implement (deep clustering + double-end pruning) and robust to encoder choice, though it still depends on access to a pretrained vision encoder and incurs ~17 hours of grid-search overhead for β on ImageNet-scale data. Open questions include whether the pseudo-label training-dynamics proxy generalizes to non-vision modalities, whether the β search can be made more efficient than grid search, and how the method behaves when no pretrained encoder is available at all (the self-trained encoder ablation shows a noticeable but manageable drop).
