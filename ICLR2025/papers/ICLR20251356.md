## Problem

Speculative decoding accelerates LLM inference by drafting and verifying multiple tokens per forward pass, but its naive application to visual AR models yields a 41–58% reduction in mean accepted length compared to LLMs. The root cause is token selection ambiguity: visual AR models assign uniformly low probabilities across the token vocabulary due to the continuous, high-dimensional nature of image data, causing draft tokens to be rejected far more often than in language settings. This paper is the first to systematically diagnose this failure mode and propose a targeted fix.

## Input / Output

LANTERN is a drop-in acceleration layer that wraps an existing visual AR model (e.g., LlamaGen-XL, Anole) without altering its input or output interface. The target model still receives a tokenized text prompt (encoded via Flan-T5 XL) or an ImageNet class label and emits a raster-scan sequence of VQGAN/VQVAE codebook indices, which are decoded into an RGB image at the model's native resolution. The only change is in the acceptance logic during the draft-and-verify loop, so generated images have identical format and resolution to standard autoregressive decoding.

## Method

LANTERN (Latent Neighbor Token Acceptance Relaxation) exploits latent proximity — the empirically validated property that tokens close in VQGAN/VQVAE latent space decode to visually similar patches and are therefore interchangeable. Rather than accepting a draft token x̃ based on the single-token ratio q(x̃|s)/p(x̃|s), LANTERN sums the target model's probabilities over the k-nearest latent neighbors of x̃, raising the average acceptance probability from ~4% (EAGLE-2) to up to ~37%. To prevent the relaxation from catastrophically distorting the target distribution, LANTERN dynamically constructs a constrained neighborhood A_{k,δ}(x̃) by greedily adding nearest neighbors until the total variation distance between the distorted and original target distribution exceeds a user-specified bound δ. Applied on top of EAGLE-2 as the base drafter, LANTERN achieves 2.26× speed-up in greedy decoding and 1.69× in temperature-1 sampling on LlamaGen-XL Stage I, versus 1.29× and 0.93× for vanilla EAGLE-2, while keeping FID, CLIP score, and HPS v2 within a small margin of the unaccelerated baseline.

## Results

On LlamaGen-XL Stage I with MS-COCO validation captions, LANTERN achieves a 2.26× actual speed-up (MAL 2.89) under greedy decoding and 1.69× (MAL 2.40) under sampling (τ=1), compared to 1.00× for vanilla autoregressive decoding. Image quality degrades modestly: FID rises from 28.63 to 30.78 (greedy) and 15.22 to 18.76 (sampling), while HPS v2 drops by less than 1.5 points in both settings. The method also generalizes to LlamaGen-XL Stage II (1.64× speed-up) and Anole (1.17× speed-up), consistently outperforming EAGLE-2 across all tested visual AR models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LANTERN is compared against two baselines: vanilla autoregressive decoding (LlamaGen) and EAGLE-2, the state-of-the-art speculative decoding method for LLMs. Against vanilla AR, LANTERN delivers a 2.26× speed-up under greedy decoding and 1.69× under sampling, with only a modest FID increase (28.63→30.78 and 15.22→18.76) and HPS v2 drop of <1.5 points. Against EAGLE-2, LANTERN improves the speed-up by 1.75× (2.26× vs. 1.29×) in greedy decoding and 1.82× (1.69× vs. 0.93×) in sampling; notably, EAGLE-2 actually slows down generation below vanilla AR under sampling (0.93×), while LANTERN still achieves a net 1.69× speed-up. On LlamaGen-XL Stage II, LANTERN reaches 1.64× speed-up versus EAGLE-2's 0.96×, and on Anole it achieves 1.17× versus EAGLE-2's 0.73×, confirming consistent superiority across models. The trade-off is a slight reduction in recall (e.g., 0.3517→0.2773 greedy, 0.5633→0.4497 sampling), indicating a small loss in diversity, while precision actually improves or stays stable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the speculative decoding framework of Leviathan et al. (2023) and the EAGLE-2 dynamic draft tree method (Li et al., 2024a) from LLMs to visual autoregressive models. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'token selection ambiguity'—near-uniform next-token probability distributions in visual AR models—as a fundamental obstacle that causes the standard acceptance condition to reject nearly all draft tokens. The proposed LANTERN method departs from prior speculative decoding by aggregating target-model probabilities over latent-space neighbors (leveraging VQ-VAE/VQGAN codebook continuity) rather than comparing single-token probabilities, and constrains the resulting distributional distortion via a total variation distance bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of why speculative decoding fails for visual AR models and provides a practical, tunable fix that recovers meaningful speed-ups (up to 2.26×) with controllable quality loss. The identification of token selection ambiguity as a root cause is valuable for anyone attempting to port LLM inference techniques to vision tokenizers. Limitations include the continued need for a trained drafter model, modest absolute speed-ups compared to LLM settings, and a small but measurable degradation in FID and recall. Open questions include whether a purpose-built drafter for visual AR models could close the gap further, and whether the latent-proximity relaxation generalizes to continuous (non-quantized) token representations.
