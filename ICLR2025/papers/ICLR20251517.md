## Problem

Existing progressive refinement methods for LLMs depend on supervision signals—correctness labels, reward models, or task-specific verifiers—to evaluate and guide response quality, making them ineffective for open-ended tasks where correctness is ambiguous. These approaches are also typically designed for specific domains, so transferring them to new tasks often fails. The paper asks whether LLMs can be taught to self-improve iteratively across diverse tasks without external supervision or task-specific fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a text query concatenated with its own previously generated answer and a refinement instruction, then produces a single refined text response expected to be more accurate and thoughtful than the prior attempt. During training, each sample is a triple (query, multi-step thought sequence from a weak model, refined answer from a strong model), where the thought sequence may contain errors but the final answer is progressively improved. No ground-truth labels or external feedback are provided at any stage; the model must learn to improve purely from the thought-to-answer progression.

## Method

PTR comprises two stages. In dataset construction, a weak-strong model collaborative selection strategy pairs a weak model's potentially flawed thought sequence with a strong model's refined answer (generated via in-context learning conditioned on those thoughts), validated by Wilcoxon significance tests and filtered for logical coherence using Sentence-BERT cosine similarity. In training, a weighted thought-mask fine-tuning procedure applies a three-term loss: (1) cross-entropy on the final refined answer only, with intermediate thoughts masked so the model focuses on producing a better response rather than memorizing the answer; (2) a consistency penalty computed as negative cosine similarity between consecutive thought embeddings; and (3) a progressive confidence term where β_t = β₀·(t/n) upweights later reasoning steps. The thought-mask mechanism is the key differentiator: by hiding the thought process during loss computation, the model learns 'how to improve' from prior thoughts rather than 'what is correct,' distinguishing PTR from standard instruction fine-tuning or knowledge distillation.

## Results

On Qwen2-7B, PTR improves the average score across 10 tasks from 49.6% (Iteration 1) to 53.5% (Iteration 3), with MMLU accuracy rising from 57.1% to 64.1% and GSM8K from 76.7% to 79.9%. On Llama3-8B, the average improves from 55.8% to 58.6% over three iterations, with MMLU jumping from 59.6% to 68.6%. Critically, PTR is the only method that consistently improves across iterations; all baselines (Prompt, IFT, RL) degrade in performance from Iteration 1 to Iteration 2, confirming that PTR activates genuine progressive refinement rather than mere knowledge transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTR outperforms all four baselines on the critical metric of iterative improvement: while Prompt (Huang et al., 2023b), IFT, and RL (DPO; Wu et al., 2024b) all degrade in average performance from Iteration 1 to Iteration 2 (e.g., Qwen2-7B Prompt drops from 51.6% to 42.8%), PTR consistently improves (49.6% → 53.5%). Against the IFT baseline specifically, the paper demonstrates that PTR is not knowledge distillation—IFT fails to improve on CommonsenseQA at Iteration 2 (40.3% vs. 46.1% at Iteration 1), whereas PTR gains +7.0 points on that task. In the math-focused comparison (Table 8, Appendix B.4.1), PTR's GSM8K improvement (76.7% → 79.9%) is comparable to Self-refine with ground truth (79.1% → 81.3%) and Reward-model Verifier (80.0% → 81.7%), but unlike Self-refine without ground truth (which drops to 74.7%), PTR improves without any external supervision signal. Pair Self-Correction (Welleck et al., 2022) achieves only modest gains (77.7% → 80.1% on GSM8K), and all verifier-based methods are limited to math tasks, whereas PTR generalizes across all 10 evaluated domains without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of progressive refinement that relies on external supervision signals—correctness labels, reward models, task-specific verifiers, or stronger-model feedback—to instead activate an intrinsic refinement capability through annotation-free training. Conceptually, it repurposes in-context learning and consistency filtering (borrowed from data-quality literature) into a weak-strong model collaborative data construction pipeline, and introduces a weighted thought-mask loss that is distinct from both standard SFT and DPO-based preference optimization. Unlike Self-Refine, Reflexion, or reward-model-based verifiers that operate at inference time or require task-specific reward functions, PTR trains a general refinement behavior on open-domain data, positioning itself as a one-time fine-tuning that transfers across tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in demonstrating that a single general-domain fine-tuning can unlock iterative self-improvement across ten heterogeneous tasks without task-specific adaptation, which is a meaningful operational simplification over per-domain reward models or verifier pipelines. However, the absolute gains are modest (~4% average, with some tasks showing <1% improvement), and the method still depends on access to a significantly stronger model for data construction, limiting its applicability in settings where no such model exists. Open questions include whether the thought-mask mechanism has a principled information-theoretic justification, whether the approach scales to larger models where the base model already has strong reasoning, and whether the refinement ceiling observed after 2–3 iterations can be pushed further with more training or architectural changes.
