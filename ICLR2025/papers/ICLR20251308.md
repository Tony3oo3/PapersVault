## Problem

Current Linear RNNs (Mamba, DeltaNet, GLA) are fundamentally unable to solve even the simplest state-tracking task—parity—because their state-transition matrices are restricted to eigenvalues in [0,1]. While Sarrof et al. (2024) identified this limitation for diagonal LRNNs, it was unclear whether non-diagonal architectures like DeltaNet could circumvent it, and no practical fix had been demonstrated. This paper closes both gaps: proving the eigenvalue restriction is the root cause for all LRNN structures and showing that a minimal modification unlocks state-tracking.

## Input / Output

The modified LRNNs consume the same inputs as their unmodified counterparts: token sequences for language modeling (FineWeb-100B, 2048 context length), binary sequences for parity and modular arithmetic tasks, and sequences of group elements (e.g., S₅ permutations) for state-tracking benchmarks. Outputs are unchanged in format—next-token logits, scalar parity/modular-arithmetic predictions, or group-element product sequences—but the internal state dynamics now permit oscillatory behavior (via negative eigenvalues) that was previously impossible. The modification adds zero computational overhead since it only rescales existing parameters within the same matrix structures.

## Method

The core modification extends the eigenvalue range of state-transition matrices from [0,1] to [-1,1]: for diagonal LRNNs (Mamba), Diag(s(x_t)) becomes Diag(2s(x_t)−1); for generalized Householder LRNNs (DeltaNet), I−β_t k_t k_t^T becomes I−2β_t k_t k_t^T. Theoretically, the paper proves that finite-precision LRNNs with only non-negative real eigenvalues cannot solve parity (Theorem 1), that non-triangular matrices with complex eigenvalues are required for modular counting when the modulus is not a power of two (Theorem 2), and that products of GH matrices with eigenvalues in [−1,1] can represent any matrix with norm ≤ 1 (Proposition 1). This yields the key positive result: a single-layer LRNN with GH state-transition matrices in M_k^n(−1,1) can implement any FSA whose transition monoid is a group (Theorem 3), and multi-layer LRNNs with such matrices can recognize any regular language via Krohn-Rhodes decomposition (Theorem 4). Empirically, the modified Mamba and DeltaNet solve parity perfectly, substantially improve on modular arithmetic and S₅ group word problems, and when pretrained at 340M–1.3B parameters on FineWeb-100B, achieve competitive language modeling perplexity with DeltaNet showing improved perplexity on code and math datasets.

## Results

On the parity task, extending the eigenvalue range from [0,1] to [-1,1] lifts Mamba from 0.000 to 1.000 and DeltaNet from 0.017 to 1.000 (scaled accuracy, tested on sequences up to length 256). On modular arithmetic without brackets, DeltaNet[-1,1] achieves 0.971 versus 0.314 for DeltaNet[0,1] and 0.787 for sLSTM. At 1.3B parameters trained on FineWeb-100B, DeltaNet[-1,1] achieves 12.73 LAMBADA perplexity (vs. 14.32 for DeltaNet[0,1]) and 18.57 WikiText perplexity, while showing lower perplexity on CodeParrot and Math-Hard datasets, confirming improved state-tracking in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original DeltaNet [0,1] (Yang et al., 2024), the proposed DeltaNet [-1,1] achieves a dramatic improvement on parity (1.000 vs. 0.017) and modular arithmetic without brackets (0.971 vs. 0.314), while also improving on the context-free modular arithmetic with brackets (0.260 vs. 0.194). Compared to sLSTM (Beck et al., 2024), DeltaNet [-1,1] matches on parity (both 1.000) but substantially outperforms on modular arithmetic without brackets (0.971 vs. 0.787), while sLSTM retains an edge on the harder bracketed variant (0.178 vs. 0.260 — actually DeltaNet wins here too). The Transformer baseline (Vaswani et al., 2017) is far behind on all formal language tasks (0.022–0.067), confirming the known inability of attention to solve state-tracking. On language modeling at 1.3B parameters, DeltaNet [-1,1] matches or slightly exceeds DeltaNet [0,1] on general benchmarks (e.g., 52.4 vs. 53.1 average lm-harness accuracy) while achieving notably lower perplexity on code (12.73 vs. 14.32 LAMBADA) and math datasets, suggesting the expressivity gain translates to practical benefits on state-tracking-heavy tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sarrof et al. (2024), who proved that diagonal LRNNs with positive eigenvalues cannot solve parity, to the non-diagonal case (DeltaNet, TTT-Linear) and provides the missing positive result: extending the eigenvalue range from [0,1] to [-1,1] provably enables state-tracking. It builds on the formal language theory framework of Merrill et al. (2024) and the Krohn-Rhodes decomposition to show that products of generalized Householder matrices with eigenvalues in [-1,1] can implement any FSA, connecting the expressivity of LRNNs to group theory (symmetric and cyclic groups). The empirical side builds on the DeltaNet and Mamba architectures of Yang et al. (2024b) and Gu & Dao (2024), applying a single scalar rescaling to their state-transition matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a one-line code change (multiplying the eigenvalue by 2 and subtracting 1) unlocks state-tracking in existing LRNN architectures at zero additional computational cost, and the 1.3B-parameter pretraining confirms stable training at scale. However, open questions remain: the theoretical expressivity of multi-layer DeltaNet[-1,1] beyond modular arithmetic is unclear, the modification is incompatible with log-space cumulative product tricks used by Mamba2/GLA, and the paper hypothesizes (but does not prove) a fundamental trade-off between state-tracking and associative recall. The finding that Mamba's diagonal structure limits further gains beyond parity, while DeltaNet's non-diagonal GH structure enables substantially harder tasks, suggests that matrix structure matters as much as eigenvalue range.
