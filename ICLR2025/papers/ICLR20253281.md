## Problem

Finding minimum ℓ₀-norm adversarial examples is NP-hard due to the non-convex, non-differentiable nature of the ℓ₀ norm, and existing attacks exhibit a suboptimal trade-off: accurate methods (e.g., Brendel-Bethge) are slow and require adversarial initialization, while fast methods (e.g., FMN) produce suboptimal perturbations. This gap hinders reliable robustness evaluation, as overestimating adversarial robustness can spread a false sense of security and impede defense development.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a clean input image x ∈ [0,1]^d (e.g., 3072 modifiable pixel values for CIFAR-10), its true class label y, and full white-box access to the target model's architecture, parameters, and gradients. It outputs the minimum ℓ₀-norm adversarial perturbation δ* such that x+δ* is misclassified, with the constraint that x+δ* remains in [0,1]^d. The perturbation is inherently sparse—only a minimal subset of pixel values is modified—and the algorithm tracks the best (smallest ℓ₀-norm) adversarial perturbation found across all iterations.

## Method

σ-zero formulates the attack as minimizing a combined objective: a Carlini-Wagner-style logit-difference loss (which equals zero only when the sample is misclassified) plus a normalized smooth approximation of the ℓ₀ norm, ℓ̂₀(δ,σ) = Σ δᵢ²/(δᵢ²+σ), where σ=10⁻³ controls sharpness. This differentiable surrogate enables standard gradient descent without any trade-off hyperparameter tuning. An adaptive projection operator Π_τ then zeros out perturbation components below a dynamically adjusted sparsity threshold τ, which increases when the current sample is already adversarial (promoting sparser solutions) and decreases otherwise (promoting loss minimization). The optimization uses ∞-norm gradient normalization for dimensionality-independent step sizes, cosine annealing for the learning rate, and box-constraint clipping, requiring no adversarial initialization and no per-sample line search.

## Results

σ-zero achieves 100% ASR∞ across all 18 evaluated models on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet, while finding the smallest median ℓ₀ perturbations. On CIFAR-10 (model C3), it reduces the median perturbation size from 52 (FMN) to 32 features (49% reduction); on ImageNet (model I4), from 58 (FMN) to 23 (60% reduction). In terms of efficiency, σ-zero runs in 0.31 s/sample on MNIST and 1.18 s/sample on ImageNet (I1), making it ~10× faster than BBadv on CIFAR-10 and ~100× faster on ImageNet (I3: 2.76 s vs. 205.11 s), while using only 2,000 queries per sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

σ-zero outperforms all 9 competing attacks (SF, EAD, PDPGD, VFGA, FMN, BB, BBadv, PGD-ℓ₀, Sparse-RS, sPGDp/u) in both ASR and median ℓ₀ across all 18 model-dataset configurations. Against the fastest competitor FMN, σ-zero achieves substantially higher ASR₂₄ (e.g., 83.79% vs. 35.90% on M1; 82.6% vs. 62.6% on I1) and smaller median perturbations (e.g., 9 vs. 24 on M2; 3 vs. 9 on I4). Against the most accurate competitor BBadv, σ-zero finds smaller perturbations (e.g., 11 vs. 16 on C1; 5 vs. 7 on I1) while being 6–100× faster (0.73 s vs. 4.68 s on C1; 1.18 s vs. 231.67 s on I1). The BB attack (without adversarial initialization) frequently fails entirely on CIFAR-10 (ASR∞ as low as 14.70% on C1, median ℓ₀ = ∞), whereas σ-zero consistently reaches 100% ASR∞. In fixed-budget comparisons (Tables 3–4), σ-zero also outperforms PGD-ℓ₀, Sparse-RS, sPGDp, and sPGDu in 17 of 18 configurations, with the sole exception being sPGDu on C4 where it marginally exceeds σ-zero's ASR₂₄.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Carlini-Wagner (2017) gradient-based adversarial attack framework and the minimum-norm attack lineage (Brendel et al. 2019, Pintor et al. 2021, Césaire et al. 2021), but departs from prior work in two key ways: it replaces the non-differentiable ℓ₀ norm with a smooth approximation x²/(x²+σ) to enable pure gradient descent, and it introduces an adaptive projection operator that dynamically adjusts sparsity based on whether the current iterate is adversarial. Unlike BBadv, it requires no adversarial initialization; unlike Carlini-Wagner, it needs no trade-off hyperparameter tuning; and unlike PDPGD's ℓ_{1/2} relaxation, it directly targets the ℓ₀ count rather than a surrogate norm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ℓ₀-norm attacks expose model vulnerabilities invisible to ℓ₂/ℓ∞ attacks, yet prior methods forced a trade-off between accuracy (BB: slow, requires adversarial init) and speed (FMN: fast but suboptimal perturbations). σ-zero's consistent 100% ASR at sufficient budgets addresses a critical reliability gap—attack failures silently overestimate robustness, undermining defense development. The method's simplicity (no tuning, no initialization, ~2 queries/iteration) makes it a practical default for ℓ₀ robustness benchmarking, as already reflected in the AttackBench evaluation. Open questions include whether the smooth approximation generalizes to other non-convex constraints and whether the adaptive projection strategy transfers to black-box or transferable settings.
