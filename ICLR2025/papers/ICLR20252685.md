## Problem

Existing generalization bounds for deep networks are either orders of magnitude too large or explode exponentially with depth, leaving it unclear how DNNs avoid the curse of dimensionality on compositional tasks where kernel methods and shallow networks fail. This paper asks whether a tight, width-independent bound can explain DNNs' ability to learn compositions of functions (e.g., unknown symmetry quotient maps) at near-optimal rates, and identifies the precise regularity conditions under which this is possible.

## Input / Output

The synthetic experiments compose two Gaussian processes with Matérn kernels: an inner function g maps from R^15 to R^3 (reducing dimensionality) and an outer function h maps from R^3 to R^20, with differentiability of each controlled by the Matérn smoothness parameter ν. Real-world experiments use MNIST (28×28 grayscale digits) and WESAD (multimodal physiological and motion data from Empatica E4 wrist sensors) for binary/multi-class classification. The critical structural assumption is that the true function f* = h∘g admits a low-dimensional bottleneck representation, which is precisely what enables DNNs to escape the curse of dimensionality while shallow nets and kernels cannot.

## Method

The paper introduces Accordion Networks (AccNets), which split each middle weight matrix via SVD into two factors, yielding a rank-constrained FCNN whose generalization bound is independent of layer widths. The core technical contribution is a uniform generalization bound governed by a complexity measure R(θ) that combines the F1-norms (width-independent, upper-bounded by parameter norm) and Lipschitz constants of each shallow subnetwork, growing only linearly in depth rather than exponentially. The key approximation result shows that compositions of Sobolev functions can be represented with bounded R(θ), so the test error rate depends only on the maximum dimensionality-to-regularity ratio r_max = max_ℓ(d_ℓ/ν_ℓ) across layers, not the full input dimension. This predicts two distinct learning regimes (g-hard vs. h-hard) that manifest as phase transitions in empirical scaling laws, validated on synthetic Matérn-GP compositions and real datasets. Training uses standard L2 weight decay to control F1-norms while relying on implicit bias from large learning rates to keep Lipschitz constants small, avoiding the need to optimize the Lipschitz term directly.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative finding is that the proposed F₁-norm-based generalization bound R(θ) captures the correct scaling rate (log-log slope) of test error, despite being roughly 10⁷ too large in absolute value. On synthetic compositional tasks (f* = h∘g with d_in=15, d_mid=3, d_out=20), deep networks (AccNets and DNNs) achieve strictly better scaling laws than kernel methods or shallow networks, matching the theoretical rate N^{-min{2ν_g/(2ν_g+d_in), 2ν_h/(2ν_h+d_mid)}}. Empirical experiments confirm phase transitions: when g is easy to learn (high ν_g) but h is hard (low ν_h), the bottleneck is h, and vice versa. On MNIST and WESAD, the bound's scaling behavior is qualitatively consistent with observed test error decay.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Bartlett et al. (2017), the proposed bound achieves three concrete improvements: replacing operator norms with Lipschitz constants, replacing the (2,1)-norm with the nuclear norm, and removing the 2/3 and 3/2 exponents. In the numerical comparison (Figure 4), the Lipschitz-based bound (Jacot24 Jacobian) is strictly tighter than all previous bounds across all differentiability settings, and even the operator-norm version (Jacot24 OP) outperforms prior bounds. In the hardest regime (ν_g=2, ν_h=2), the Lipschitz-based bound is the only one that decreases with N, while the operator-norm bound and all previous bounds actually increase with N. Compared to Golowich et al. (2020), the proposed O(L) depth dependence (from removing the 2/3 and 3/2 exponents) is significantly better than Golowich's product-of-Frobenius-norms complexity, which would grow exponentially as min{d_0*,...,d_L*}^{(L-L*)} when the minimum dimension is ≥2. The paper also shows that the F₁-norm is essential: the (2,1)-norm from Bartlett et al. imposes sparsity on neurons that is incompatible with approximating general Sobolev functions, which require a high variety of active neurons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the F1-norm/Barron norm framework for shallow networks (Bach, 2017; Weinan et al., 2019) to deep networks via the Accordion Network architecture, proving a width-independent generalization bound based on covering numbers. It departs from prior deep network bounds (Bartlett et al., 2017; Golowich et al., 2020) by replacing operator norms with Lipschitz constants and the (2,1)-norm with the nuclear norm, yielding an O(L) depth dependence instead of O(L^{3/2}) or exponential. Crucially, it shows that compositional learning rates previously established only under strong sparsity assumptions (Schmidt-Hieber, 2020; Poggio & Fraser, 2024) can be achieved with purely norm-based complexity, removing the need for bounded width or bounded non-zero parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled theoretical explanation for why DNNs can learn symmetries and break the curse of dimensionality on compositional tasks where kernel methods and shallow networks fail, with empirically validated phase transitions between regimes. Key limitations include the assumption of global minimizer recovery, infinite width, and a +3 artifact in the dimension ratio that prevents predicting rates faster than N^{-1/2}. Open questions include whether finite-width results can be established, whether the +3 can be removed, and how to practically optimize the Lipschitz constants that appear in the bound (the authors suggest implicit bias from large learning rates may suffice).
