## Problem

Instruction-based image editing models are bottlenecked by the lack of high-quality training data pairing detailed edit instructions with corresponding image pairs. Existing datasets (InstructPix2Pix, HIVE, MagicBrush) rely on outdated generation models (SD1.5, GPT-3) or limited human annotation, resulting in low-resolution images, poor instruction-image alignment, and restricted edit diversity. This paper addresses the gap by synthesizing a large-scale, high-fidelity editing dataset using state-of-the-art foundation models.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides paired RGB images at approximately 900×900 resolution (nearly double prior datasets) alongside detailed text editing instructions, inverse-edit instructions, and full input/output image descriptions. During model fine-tuning, images are resized to 512×512 and the model takes an input image plus a text instruction to produce a single edited image. The generation pipeline itself operates on diptych prompts—side-by-side input/output descriptions—rather than separate image generations, which the authors found yields superior alignment and consistency in edit-irrelevant regions.

## Method

HQ-Edit is constructed through a three-phase pipeline: (1) Expansion uses GPT-4 in a Self-Instruct-style loop to grow 293 manually curated seed triplets into ~100,000 diverse input/output descriptions with edit instructions; (2) Generation feeds each triplet through GPT-4 to form an enriched diptych prompt, which DALL-E 3 renders as a single side-by-side image pair, exploiting the finding that joint diptych generation outperforms separate image generation in alignment and consistency; (3) Post-processing decomposes diptychs via a retrained YOLOv8 detector, aligns paired images using DIFT-based semantic correspondence warping, filters distorted samples (>50% dimensional deformation), rewrites instructions with GPT-4V to fix discrepancies and add omitted visual details, and generates inverse-edit instructions to double the training signal. The paper also introduces two GPT-4V-based evaluation metrics—Alignment (semantic consistency of edits with the prompt) and Coherence (aesthetic quality including lighting, style, and edge smoothness)—validated against human judgments on 1,651 image pairs.

## Results

On the HQ-Edit test set, the HQ-Edit fine-tuned InstructPix2Pix achieves the best Alignment score of 47.01 and Coherence score of 86.16, outperforming all baselines. Compared to the vanilla InstructPix2Pix (Alignment 34.71, Coherence 80.52), HQ-Edit yields a +12.30 improvement in Alignment and +5.56 in Coherence. On the Emu Edit test set (real images), HQ-Edit also leads in CLIP_dir (0.1067), CLIP_img (0.8588), and Reward Score (Sigmoid 0.13), though it ranks second on DINO (0.8139 vs. MagicBrush's 0.8278) and SSIM (0.2231 vs. 0.2516).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HQ-Edit fine-tuned InstructPix2Pix outperforms all six baselines on both Alignment and Coherence metrics on the HQ-Edit test set. The closest competitor is MagicBrush (Alignment 43.77, Coherence 84.19), which HQ-Edit surpasses by 3.24 in Alignment and 1.97 in Coherence. HIVE (w/ weighted) trails by 6.33 in Alignment and 1.22 in Coherence. The vanilla InstructPix2Pix base model is outperformed by a substantial margin of 12.30 in Alignment and 5.56 in Coherence, demonstrating the dataset's value. PromptInverse (22.82) and DiffEdit (21.53) are far behind in Alignment, while Imagic performs poorly overall (Alignment 1.50, Coherence 63.58). On the Emu Edit test set with real images, HQ-Edit leads in CLIP_dir, CLIP_img, and Reward Score but is slightly behind MagicBrush on DINO (0.8139 vs. 0.8278) and SSIM (0.2231 vs. 0.2516), suggesting a minor domain gap for photorealistic inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-based image editing dataset construction line initiated by InstructPix2Pix (which used GPT-3 + Prompt2Prompt) and HIVE (which added human feedback), replacing the outdated generation stack with GPT-4 and DALL-E 3. The key methodological departure is the diptych generation strategy—prompting DALL-E 3 to produce input and output images as a single side-by-side composition—combined with DIFT-based warping and GPT-4V instruction rewriting, which together address the alignment and fidelity gaps that plagued earlier synthetic pipelines. The expansion phase follows the Self-instruct paradigm of using an LLM to scale a small seed set into a large corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HQ-Edit provides a 200K-sample, high-resolution (~900×900) dataset that demonstrably outperforms both synthetic (InstructPix2Pix, HIVE) and human-annotated (MagicBrush) baselines on the authors' proposed Alignment and Coherence metrics, suggesting that foundation-model-driven pipelines can now rival or exceed manual annotation for this task. The diptych trick and the finding that raw DALL-E 3 pairs are actually *worse* in alignment than the older InstructPix2Pix data (16.83 vs. 34.71) highlight that generation quality alone is insufficient without careful post-processing. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond API-only access to GPT-4V/DALL-E 3, how the metrics behave on truly photorealistic editing (the Emu Edit results show a domain gap), and whether the ethical risks of fully synthetic training data (bias, provenance) can be systematically mitigated at scale.
