## Problem

Fixed-point iteration (FPI) methods for learning mean field games suffer from three coupled limitations: the sequential forward-backward procedure increases computational cost, fixing one component while updating the other causes oscillations, and the separation prevents applying function approximation to population learning. No existing method treats the policy and population as a unified parameter for simultaneous fully-asynchronous updates, and no population-aware linear function approximation exists for continuous state-action spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251513/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single online observation tuple (s, a, r, s′, a′) per step from one agent, using the same sample to update both the value function parameter θ and the population measure parameter η simultaneously with the same learning rate. The output is the unified parameter ξ = (η; θ), where η defines the population measure as ⟨ψ, η⟩ over a measure basis ψ and θ defines the value function as ⟨φ, θ⟩ over a feature map φ; for finite state-action spaces this reduces to standard Q-value vectors and probability vectors, while for continuous spaces the basis functions provide the linear parameterization. The population update involves a Gram matrix G_ψ and a simplex projection, maintaining O(d₂²) worst-case per-step complexity comparable to the finite-state case.

## Method

The core insight is treating the value function and population measure as a single unified parameter ξ = (η; θ), enabling a simple SGD-type update (SemiSGD) where both components are updated simultaneously and fully asynchronously using the same online observations, eliminating the forward-backward structure of FPI. The semi-gradient combines a TD/SARSA update for the value function (G_φ(O)θ − φ(O)) with a Monte Carlo update for the population (G_ψ η − ψ(s′)), followed by projection onto a Euclidean ball and probability simplex respectively, constituting the first population-aware linear function approximation (PA-LFA). The key theoretical result (Lemma 1) shows that the concatenated semi-gradient points in a descent direction toward the MFE, while neither the value function nor population semi-gradient alone is guaranteed to do so regardless of how small the Lipschitz constant is—providing a mechanistic explanation for SemiSGD's stability advantage over FPI. The convergence analysis treats the learning process as stochastic approximation on a non-stationary Markov chain parameterized by ξ, yielding O(ε⁻² log ε⁻¹) sample complexity under a contractivity condition and convergence to a neighborhood of the MFE under a more practical condition for general (non-regularized) policy operators.

## Results

SemiSGD with PA-LFA achieves a sample complexity of O(ε⁻² log ε⁻¹) for reaching an ε-MFE, which is state-of-the-art among oracle-free MFG learning methods (compared to O(ε⁻² log² ε⁻¹) for multi-loop FPI methods and O(ε⁻⁴) for two-timescale methods). In the PA-LFA vs. discretization comparison (Table 1), PA-LFA consistently achieves lower MSE: e.g., at d₂=20, PA-LFA reaches 0.07±0.04 MSE versus 0.15±0.13 for discretization, and at d₂=25, 0.06±0.04 vs. 0.18±0.15. Across all three MFG benchmarks (speed control, flocking, network routing), SemiSGD converges faster, with lower final MSE and without oscillations, compared to all FPI variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemiSGD outperforms all four FPI-based baselines across all three MFG benchmarks. On speed control, SemiSGD converges to a lower MSE faster and without oscillations, while FPI with ER or FP stabilizes but its learned population distribution significantly deviates from the reference MFE due to regularization bias. On the flocking game, SemiSGD is the only method that captures the gathering behavior near the destination; all FPI variants fail because large regularization (needed for stability) obscures the small reward signal (R ≤ 0.02), leading to near-constant policies. On network routing, only SemiSGD and FPI with ER achieve low MSE, with SemiSGD attaining the lowest error and the most stable convergence, while FPI with FP and MD exhibit persistent oscillations in exploitability. In the PA-LFA vs. discretization comparison, PA-LFA achieves strictly lower MSE at every dimension tested (d₂ = 2 to 50), with the gap most pronounced at small d₂ (e.g., 0.07 vs. 0.15 at d₂=20), demonstrating that a well-chosen measure basis generalizes better than grid discretization for continuous population distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant fixed-point iteration (FPI) paradigm for learning mean field games, which alternates between policy evaluation and population computation in a forward-backward loop. It builds on the online MFG learning literature (Zaman et al. 2023, Yardim et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2024a) and the linear MDP / LFA framework from RL (Jin et al. 2020), but introduces a fundamentally different algorithmic structure: treating the value function and population measure as a single unified parameter updated simultaneously via a single-loop, uni-timescale SGD scheme. The key conceptual departure is that prior multi-timescale or multi-loop methods decouple policy and population updates to manage their interaction, whereas SemiSGD embraces the coupling and shows the concatenated parameter follows a descent direction even when neither component does individually.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified-parameter perspective opens a pathway to transplant the full toolbox of stochastic approximation and function approximation from RL directly into population learning, which was previously locked behind the forward-backward structure. PA-LFA is the first method to apply linear function approximation to the population measure, enabling model-free learning on continuous state-action spaces (autonomous driving, flocking, crowd dynamics) with operation complexity matching the finite-state case. Open questions include: whether the contractivity assumption (Assumption 4) can be relaxed further or verified automatically, how the method behaves for non-linear MFGs beyond the projected-MFE guarantee, and whether the unified-parameter idea extends to other coupled dynamical systems (evolutionary games, graphon games) as the authors speculate.
