## Problem

Current LLM data synthesis methods rely on human-annotated data or predefined task templates to guide generation, which constrains the scope of synthetic data and overlooks model-specific edge cases and novel failure scenarios. There is no automated, trainable framework that systematically discovers diverse instructions exposing a particular target model's weaknesses without manual annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250384/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial seed set of task-specific instructions (e.g., 7,340 harmful prompts from HH-RLHF, 2,448 MMLU questions, or 10,000 math problems), a target model to be enhanced, and a smaller proposer model to be trained. It produces failure-inducing instructions (where the target model's response is judged incorrect, unsafe, or uncertain by a binary indicator S) paired with reference responses (from gpt-4o-mini for knowledge tasks, or from the target model itself prompted with a safety prefix for red-teaming), which are then used to fine-tune the target model. The proposer generates instructions via sampling decoding (top-p 0.98 for safety/honesty, 0.9 for math), and a MinHash-based deduplication step (signature size 128, threshold 0.9) maintains diversity across iterations.

## Method

REVERSEGEN trains a proposer model through iterative Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) where the preference signal is the target model's own failure: instructions that cause the target to produce an incorrect, unsafe, or uncertain response are labeled as preferred (x+), while those the target handles correctly are dispreferred (x−). The pipeline has four stages: (1) SFT warmup of the proposer on initial instructions using three-shot prompts, (2) generating candidate instructions and obtaining binary target-model feedback via a task-specific indicator (Llama-Guard-2-8B for safety, response entropy for honesty, gpt-4o-mini accuracy for math), (3) DPO optimization of the proposer with the reference model updated to the previous iteration's weights each cycle, and (4) SFT of the target model on the generated failure instructions with appropriate reference responses. The key distinction from prior work is that the failure signal is embedded directly into a trainable preference-learning objective rather than used as a static filter, enabling the proposer to continuously discover increasingly challenging and diverse instructions across iterations.

## Results

On safety red-teaming, REVERSEGEN with Llama-2-7b as proposer achieves 87.77% Attack Success Rate (ASR) against Vicuna-7b-v1.5 after 3 iterations, compared to 0.47% for Few-shot and Curiosity baselines. On honesty calibration, REVERSEGEN improves the AP score of Vicuna-7b-v1.5 to 0.763 with 10,000 synthetic samples (vs. 0.701 for R-Tuning with only 2,448 human samples). On mathematical reasoning, REVERSEGEN achieves 80.93% on GSM8k and 59.31% on GSM-Plus, outperforming WizardMath (80.05/58.75) and LLM2LLM (80.14/57.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Curiosity (Hong et al., 2024), REVERSEGEN achieves a dramatic 187× improvement in ASR when targeting Vicuna-7b-v1.5 (87.77% vs. 0.47% with Llama-2-7b proposer), while maintaining comparable or higher diversity. Against R-Tuning (Zhang et al., 2024) on honesty calibration, REVERSEGEN matches the AP score at 2,448 samples (0.703 vs. 0.701) using only synthetic data, and surpasses it substantially at 10,000 samples (0.763 AP, 48.03% accuracy) where R-Tuning cannot scale beyond its fixed human dataset. On mathematical reasoning, REVERSEGEN outperforms WizardMath (Luo et al., 2023) on both GSM8k (80.93 vs. 80.05) and GSM-Plus (59.31 vs. 58.75), and beats LLM2LLM (Lee et al., 2024) on both benchmarks (80.93 vs. 80.14 on GSM8k; 59.31 vs. 57.67 on GSM-Plus). Against Qwen2.5-Math-7B prompting (Yang et al., 2024), REVERSEGEN achieves higher scores on both GSM8k (80.93 vs. 79.37) and GSM-Plus (59.31 vs. 55.00), demonstrating that failure-inducing preference learning is more effective than simply prompting with a stronger model's challenging examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as its optimization backbone, repurposing preference learning from response-quality alignment to instruction-generation for a specific target model. It departs from prior data synthesis methods (Self-Instruct, WizardMath, LLM2LLM) that generate task-relevant data without model-specific feedback, and from red-teaming approaches like Curiosity (Hong et al., 2024) that use PPO with diversity rewards, by framing 'find instructions that make model X fail' as an iterative DPO objective. The key conceptual departure is making the proposer's reward signal the target model's actual failure, creating a closed-loop, model-specific data generation pipeline rather than a one-shot template-based approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a scalable, model-specific alternative to human-annotated data for LLM alignment, demonstrated across safety, honesty, and math tasks. However, the math results are modest (the failure-inducing variant actually underperforms the SFT-only ablation on GSM8k), and the paper acknowledges that difficulty-aware training is needed to fully exploit challenging samples. Open questions include whether the exploration-exploitation balance degrades over many iterations (diversity drops at t=4), whether the proposer must be smaller than the target, and whether the approach generalizes to larger or more capable models where failure signals become rarer.
