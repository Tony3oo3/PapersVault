## Problem

Transformers excel at long-context modeling but lack an explicit, data-dependent mechanism for forgetting past information, a capability that forget gates provide in recurrent sequence models and that has proven critical for their success on short-context tasks. The paper asks whether a forget gate can be naturally incorporated into the Transformer's softmax attention without sacrificing its long-context advantages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252509/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a causal token sequence (up to 16384 tokens in training, 65536 in validation) and produces next-token probability distributions. Notably, FoX requires no positional embeddings by default (unlike the standard Transformer, which depends on RoPE), and the forget gate is a single scalar per timestep per attention head, making the additional parameters and computation negligible relative to the base Transformer.

## Method

The key insight is that the forget gate in recurrent linear attention translates, in its parallel form, to down-weighting unnormalized attention scores by a data-dependent factor F_ij = prod_{l=j+1}^{i} f_l. FoX applies this exact mechanism to softmax attention by adding a logit bias D_ij = sum_{l=j+1}^{i} log f_l to the attention scores, where each scalar forget gate f_t = sigmoid(w_f^T x_t + b_f) is computed from the input at timestep t. This is implemented hardware-efficiently by pre-computing the cumulative sum c_i = sum_{l=1}^{i} log f_l in HBM and computing D_ij = c_i - c_j on-chip during FlashAttention, avoiding materialization of the L×L bias matrix. The 'Pro' architecture further adds QK-norm, an output gate, output RMSNorm, and a data-dependent KV-shift (a simplified token-shift on keys and values), all of which contribute positively in ablations. FoX outperforms the Transformer on long-context language modeling, length extrapolation, and short-context downstream tasks while matching it on long-context tasks, and retains near-perfect needle-in-the-haystack retrieval that recurrent models like Mamba-2, HGRN2, and DeltaNet fail to achieve.

## Results

On LM-eval-harness (760M params, 48B tokens, LongCrawl64), FoX (Pro) achieves the best average score of 50.88, outperforming Transformer (Pro) (50.39), Mamba-2 (50.21), and DeltaNet (47.99). FoX (Pro) also achieves the lowest WikiText perplexity (23.04) and LAMBADA perplexity (14.91). On long-context language modeling, FoX maintains a monotonically decreasing per-token loss across the full 16k training context and extrapolates beyond it, while all recurrent baselines (Mamba-2, HGRN2, DeltaNet) plateau after ~10k tokens. FoX achieves near-perfect needle-in-the-haystack retrieval within the training context length, whereas all tested recurrent models fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoX (Pro) outperforms Transformer (Pro) by +0.49 average on LM-eval-harness (50.88 vs 50.39) and by a larger margin on WikiText perplexity (23.04 vs 24.12) and LAMBADA perplexity (14.91 vs 16.16), while Transformer (Pro) retains an edge on BoolQ (60.86 vs 46.57). Against recurrent baselines, FoX (Pro) leads Mamba-2 by +0.67 average (50.88 vs 50.21), HGRN2 by +1.43, and DeltaNet by +2.89, with particularly large gaps on LAMBADA perplexity (14.91 vs 21.05/20.14/29.14). On long-context language modeling (per-token loss), FoX maintains a steeply decreasing curve across the full 16k context and extrapolates beyond it, while Mamba-2, HGRN2, and DeltaNet all plateau after ~10k tokens. On needle-in-the-haystack, FoX achieves near-perfect retrieval within the training context length in both easy and standard modes, whereas Mamba-2, HGRN2, and DeltaNet all perform poorly even within the training context. On LongBench long-context tasks, FoX performs on par with Transformer (both architectures) and clearly better than the recurrent models, confirming that the forget gate does not sacrifice the Transformer's long-context retrieval advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct conceptual bridge between gated linear attention (GLA, Mamba-2) and standard softmax attention: the parallel form of a forget-gated linear recurrence is structurally identical to softmax attention with a data-dependent log-bias on the logits. The authors exploit this equivalence to transplant the forget gate mechanism—ubiquitous in recurrent sequence models—into the Transformer's softmax attention, yielding Forgetting Attention. The 'Pro' block design (QK-norm, output gate, output normalization, KV-shift) is borrowed from the recurrent sequence model literature (GLA, Mamba-2, Peng et al. 2024), and the paper shows the forget gate is a data-dependent generalization of ALiBi's fixed positional bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a minimal scalar forget gate (a few extra parameters per head) improves long-context language modeling and short-context downstream tasks while preserving near-perfect needle-in-the-haystack retrieval is practically significant: it offers a drop-in, FlashAttention-compatible modification to the Transformer that also eliminates the need for positional embeddings. The Pro block design, which improves both FoX and the vanilla Transformer, is a useful architectural recommendation for future work. Key limitations include the modest scale (760M parameters, 48B tokens, 16k context) and the observation that FoX's advantage over the Transformer shrinks with model size, leaving open whether the benefit persists at frontier scales. Future directions include non-causal attention, adaptive KV-cache eviction based on forget gate values, and larger-scale validation.
