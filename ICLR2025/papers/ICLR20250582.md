## Problem

In a free data market, a model owner must privately select and appraise training data from a data owner before committing to a purchase, requiring joint evaluation over Multi-Party Computation (MPC). However, evaluating Transformer models over MPC is prohibitively expensive—a single BERT forward pass requires 3252 communication rounds and 245 GB of data exchange—making private data selection from tens of thousands of candidates infeasible with existing MPC-based Transformer inference methods like MPCFORMER, which also suffer from poor selection accuracy due to their reliance on model distillation with limited, skewed bootstrap data.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled dataset D (potentially with imbalanced class distribution), a pretrained Transformer model M_target (BERT, DistilBERT, or ViT), and a purchase budget B < |D|. Before MPC selection, a small bootstrap subset S_boot (a fraction of B) is purchased in the clear to generate proxy models. The MPC selection outputs only the indices of the top-B data points ranked by encrypted prediction entropy, plus optionally a one-bit threshold test on the average entropy; neither party learns the other's model weights, raw data, or individual entropy values.

## Method

SelectFormer fuses adjacent nonlinear operators in Transformers (attention softmax, LayerNorm reciprocal, and top-level softmax+entropy) into small MLPs that simultaneously approximate the nonlinearity and reduce dimensionality (e.g., 512-dim softmax → 2-dim MLP), dramatically cutting MPC communication costs. These MLPs are trained on synthesized Gaussian-distributed inputs whose parameters are estimated from actual model activations during bootstrap fine-tuning, requiring only a few hundred real data points. Selection proceeds in multiple phases: early phases run tiny proxy models (1 layer, dim 2) to filter most candidates, while later phases use larger proxies (3 layers, dim 16) for precise selection, with proxy models generated by pruning the target model and substituting nonlinear modules with the trained MLPs. Finally, IO scheduling overlaps latency-bound MPC operations (low-dim ReLU computations) with bandwidth-bound operations (high-dim matrix multiplications) across batches, hiding communication delays behind computation.

## Results

On a 20% purchase budget, SelectFormer's selected data yields test accuracy within 0.08%–0.58% of the Oracle (gold) baseline across all NLP benchmarks (SST-2, QNLI, QQP, AG News, Yelp) for both DistilBERT and BERT, and even slightly exceeds Oracle for ViT-base (+0.13%) and ViT-small (+0.15%) on CIFAR-10/100. Compared to random selection, the accuracy gain ranges from 1.12% (QNLI/BERT) to 25.12% (CIFAR-100/ViT-small). In terms of efficiency, SelectFormer reduces end-to-end selection delay from ~3,740 hours (Oracle on SST-2/DistilBERT) to ~20 hours — a 204× speedup — while maintaining near-Oracle accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MPCFORMER (Li et al., 2022), SelectFormer achieves dramatically higher test accuracy — 84.59% vs. 50.92% on SST-2, 73.68% vs. 50.54% on QNLI, and 72.73% vs. 36.82% on QQP (BERT target) — while also reducing selection delay by 7× (e.g., 37 hrs vs. 279 hrs on SST-2). The accuracy gap is attributed to MPCFORMER's reliance on model distillation from a small, label-skewed bootstrap set, which biases the proxy toward the majority class. Against Bolt (Pang et al., 2024), SelectFormer outperforms by 15.55% in accuracy (84.59% ± 0.95% vs. 69.04% ± 11.86%) with substantially lower variance, indicating greater robustness to the biased bootstrap data. Compared to random selection, SelectFormer consistently wins by 1–25 percentage points across all 14 model–benchmark combinations, with the largest gains on challenging multi-label tasks (CIFAR-100: +25.12% for ViT-small). Against the Oracle (directly evaluating the full target model over MPC), SelectFormer is within 0.08%–0.58% on NLP benchmarks and even slightly exceeds Oracle on ViT models, while being 204× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proxy-model data selection paradigm (Coleman et al., 2019) and maximum-entropy active learning (Settles, 2012) into the multi-party computation setting, building on Xu et al. (2022) who demonstrated private data appraisal over MPC but only for logistic regression with labeled data. It departs from prior MPC-for-Transformer-inference work (MPCFORMER, PUMA, Bolt) by exploiting a key asymmetry: data selection requires only relative entropy rankings, not exact predictions, enabling aggressive dimensionality reduction via data-driven MLPs that would be unacceptable for general inference. The multi-phase progressive sieve and parallel MPC scheduling are novel systems contributions not present in prior MPC frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes private data selection over MPC practical for Transformer models, reducing selection delay from thousands of hours to tens of hours while maintaining near-Oracle accuracy (≤0.20% degradation), which could enable real-world data market transactions. A notable limitation is the semi-honest 2PC threat model and the requirement for a small bootstrap dataset, which may not always be available. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models (e.g., LLMs), whether it extends to 3-party or more complex MPC settings, and whether the MLP approximation strategy generalizes beyond entropy-based selection criteria.
