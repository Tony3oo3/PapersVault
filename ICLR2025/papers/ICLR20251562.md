## Problem

Existing factuality alignment methods based on DPO operate at the response level, inadvertently encouraging incorrect sentences within preferred samples and penalizing correct sentences within non-preferred samples. This ambiguity in the reward signal fundamentally limits the effectiveness of hallucination mitigation, since real LLM responses almost always mix truthful and hallucinated content. The paper asks whether incorporating sentence-level factuality as a mask can resolve this ambiguity and enable more effective, generalizable factuality alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251562/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (question) and constructs preference pairs by sampling K candidate responses from the policy model, then annotating each sentence with a binary hallucination label via a fine-grained annotator (ANAH-v2). The factuality score for each response is the fraction of non-hallucinated sentences, and the highest- and lowest-scoring responses form the preferred/non-preferred pair. The output is a fine-tuned LLM whose generation is steered toward factual content; the sentence-level mask (a binary vector per response) is the key intermediate signal that determines which tokens contribute to the DPO loss.

## Method

Mask-DPO modifies the standard DPO loss by decomposing the log-probability ratio into per-sentence terms and applying a binary mask: for the preferred sample, only sentences annotated as factually correct contribute to the positive signal, and for the non-preferred sample, only sentences annotated as hallucinated contribute to the negative signal. This is implemented via indicator functions in the masked KL divergence terms (Eq. 5), which are then plugged into the sigmoid-based DPO objective (Eq. 6). The preference data is constructed by sampling 32 candidates per question, scoring each with ANAH-v2's sentence-level annotations, and selecting the top- and bottom-scoring responses. The paper further shows that scaling the number of distinct topics in training data is more effective for generalization than scaling question diversity within the same topics, and hypothesizes that factuality alignment implicitly adjusts a model-specific topic-affinity graph, with proof-of-concept experiments via topic clustering and best-of-N sampling supporting this interpretation.

## Results

On the in-domain ANAH test set, Mask-DPO improves Llama3.1-8B-Instruct's factuality score from 49.19% to 77.53% under the ANAH-v2 evaluator, surpassing Llama3.1-70B-Instruct (53.44%) and FactTune (56.83%). Under the FactScore evaluator on the same set, the score rises from 19.43% to 25.56%, again exceeding Llama3.1-70B (21.92%). On the out-of-domain Biography dataset, Mask-DPO lifts the FactScore from 30.29% to 39.39%, approaching Llama3.1-70B-Instruct (40.47%) without any Biography-specific training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mask-DPO (77.53% ANAH-v2) decisively outperforms all open-source baselines, including the largest model Llama3.1-70B-Instruct (53.44%) by 24.09 points, and Qwen2-72B (43.81%) by 33.72 points. Among factuality alignment methods, Mask-DPO beats FactTune (56.83%) by 20.70 points and FLAME (62.96%) by 14.57 points on the ANAH-v2 metric. Against other preference optimization strategies, Mask-DPO surpasses vanilla DPO (68.44%), TDPO (70.95%), DPOP (67.08%), and SePO (58.45%), with the largest margin over SePO. On the out-of-domain Biography set, Mask-DPO (39.39%) edges out FactTune (37.97%) but remains slightly below Llama3.1-70B-Instruct (40.47%), indicating near-parity with a model 8.75× larger in parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mask-DPO extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) by introducing a sentence-level factuality mask into the preference loss, addressing a fundamental ambiguity in prior factuality alignment methods (FactTune, FLAME, etc.) that treat entire responses as atomic units for reward assignment. While those methods rely on response-level factuality scores to construct preference pairs, Mask-DPO leverages a fine-grained hallucination annotator (ANAH-v2) to identify which specific sentences within each response are correct or incorrect, then selectively masks the gradient signal for incorrect sentences in preferred samples and correct sentences in non-preferred samples. The paper also positions itself within the broader RLAIF literature, using an AI-based annotator rather than human feedback, and introduces a novel conceptual hypothesis that LLMs maintain a model-specific topic-affinity graph that factuality alignment implicitly adjusts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that scaling topic diversity is more effective than scaling question diversity for generalization provides actionable guidance for future factuality alignment data curation, suggesting that coverage of the knowledge space matters more than depth within individual topics. The best-of-N experiment showing that alignment modifies internal knowledge structure rather than merely shifting probability distributions (contrasting with observations in math reasoning) opens a new research direction on what preference learning actually does to parametric knowledge. However, limitations remain: the method is validated primarily on Llama3.1-8B with only two evaluation domains, the same annotator used for data construction and evaluation creates residual reward-hacking risk, and the knowledge graph hypothesis, while supported by proof-of-concept experiments, remains unformalized and untested at scale.
