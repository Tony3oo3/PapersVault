## Problem

While large audio-language models have achieved strong results in speech, music, and general audio, no foundation model has been specifically designed for bioacoustics tasks such as detecting animal vocalizations in large recordings, classifying rare and endangered species, or labeling call types and behavior. Existing bioacoustics models are typically species- or task-specific, rely on small labeled datasets, and lack the zero-shot generalization and multi-task flexibility that foundation models offer in other domains.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw audio recordings (focal recordings for classification; 10-second windows with 5-second overlap for detection) paired with a natural-language instruction prompt that may include candidate species lists, recording metadata (location, time, field notes), or task-specific queries. Outputs are free-form text: species names (scientific, common, or flattened taxonomic), multi-label detection lists, open-ended captions, call-type/lifestage labels, or individual counts. Predictions are mapped to valid labels via Levenshtein distance for classification, and low-overlap predictions are discarded for detection.

## Method

NatureLM-audio follows an audio-to-text pipeline: a BEATs audio encoder (fully fine-tuned) produces frame-level embeddings, a window-level Q-Former compresses consecutive frames into a fixed-length sequence of text-space tokens, and a Llama 3.1-8B LLM (frozen except LoRA adapters on all attention layers, rank 32) generates the text response. Training uses a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 pretrains exclusively on focal species classification across thousands of taxa to build a robust audio-language alignment, then Stage 2 introduces detection, captioning, lifestage, call-type, and—critically—speech and music tasks. The key empirical finding is that speech (multi-speaker mixtures with time-scaling and silence insertion) and music (pitch, timbre, and instrument-counting tasks from NSynth) provide positive transfer to bioacoustics, enabling zero-shot individual counting and improving generalization to unseen taxa. The model is trained from scratch rather than fine-tuned from existing LALM checkpoints, and uses flattened taxonomic names (phylum→species) inspired by BioCLIP to improve hierarchical generalization.

## Results

On the BEANS-Zero benchmark, NatureLM-audio achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on 7 out of 9 datasets, including 82.0% accuracy on ESC-50, 78.8% on Watkins (marine mammals), 77.8% on CBI (264 bird species), and 31.4% F1 on ENA-Birds detection. On novel tasks, it sets new records for lifestage classification (79.4%), call-type classification (87.1%), captioning (SPIDEr 0.532), and individual counting (65.5%). For generalization to unseen taxa, the model reaches 34.3% accuracy on unseen species (vs. 0.5% random chance) using taxonomic names, substantially outperforming the CLAP baseline (1.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NatureLM-audio decisively outperforms general-purpose audio-language models (SALMONN, Qwen2-audio) and the LLM-without-audio baseline, which produce near-random outputs on bioacoustic datasets (e.g., 0.4% on CBI vs. 77.8% for NatureLM-audio). Against the strongest bioacoustics-specific baseline, BioLingual, NatureLM-audio wins on 7 of 9 BEANS-Zero datasets, with the largest margins on Watkins (+53.1 pts) and ENA-Birds (+20.5 pts), while losing on HICEAS (0.336 vs. 0.429) and Gibbons (0.005 vs. 0.018). Compared to bird-specific supervised models BirdNET and Perch, NatureLM-audio achieves a new SotA on CBI (77.8% vs. 74.4% for Perch) and competitive detection F1 on DCASE-bird (8.3% vs. 3.5%), despite being evaluated zero-shot. On unseen-taxa generalization, NatureLM-audio (taxonomic names) reaches 34.3% on unseen species, 14.8% on unseen genus, and 30.8% on unseen family, far exceeding CLAP-LAION (1.4%, 2.6%, 8.2%) and random chance, though still below the fully supervised BioLingual topline (68.7%, 68.8%, 54.5%). On the BirdSet benchmark, NatureLM-audio achieves the highest average Top-1 accuracy (0.66), slightly surpassing Perch (0.65).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the standard LALM architecture (audio encoder + Q-Former + LLM) established by SALMONN, Qwen2-audio, and LTU to the bioacoustics domain, where prior models like BirdNET, Perch, AVES, and BioLingual are either task-specific or rely on contrastive/discriminative paradigms that limit task flexibility. The key departure is a two-stage curriculum (perception pretraining on species classification, then generalization fine-tuning across diverse tasks) and the deliberate inclusion of speech and music data to enable cross-domain transfer to bioacoustic tasks. The BEANS-Zero benchmark extends the earlier BEANS benchmark by adding zero-shot generalization splits (unseen species/genus/family) and novel sub-tasks (individual counting, call-type, lifestage, captioning) that had no prior unified evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first audio-language foundation model for bioacoustics, NatureLM-audio demonstrates that a single generative model can handle classification, detection, captioning, and counting across taxa without task-specific heads, which is practically significant for biodiversity monitoring where annotated data is scarce. The BEANS-Zero benchmark fills a recognized gap in the field by enabling standardized zero-shot and cross-task evaluation. Open questions include the acknowledged bias toward bird datasets, the ethical risk of the model being misused for poaching, and whether the cross-domain transfer from speech/music generalizes beyond the individual-counting task. The paper also notes that hallucination and bias issues inherent to LLMs remain unresolved concerns for ecological deployment.
