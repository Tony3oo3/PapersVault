## Problem

Existing character image animation methods are restricted to human figures and fail on anthropomorphic characters (cartoon, game, and poster characters with non-human body structures such as missing limbs or disproportionate proportions). The paper attributes this failure to insufficient motion representation: simple 2D pose skeletons lack image-level motion details, and the self-driven reconstruction training strategy implicitly assumes body-shape alignment between reference and pose that breaks down at inference time for non-human characters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250481/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (any character type, including anthropomorphic figures) and a driving video from which DWPose keypoints and CLIP features are extracted. The reference image and driving video may come from entirely different sources with no body-shape correspondence (cross-driven setting), unlike prior methods that require strict pose alignment. Output is a 32-frame animated video at 768×512 resolution showing the reference character performing the driving video's motions, decoded from latent space via a VAE decoder.

## Method

Animate-X is a latent diffusion model using a 3D-UNet denoiser with Spatial, Motion, and Temporal (Mamba) attention layers, conditioned on a reference image's CLIP appearance feature and VAE latent. The core contribution is the Pose Indicator, which has two components: (1) the Implicit Pose Indicator (IPI) performs cross-attention with CLIP features of the driving video as keys/values and DWPose-keypoint embeddings plus learnable queries as queries, extracting holistic motion patterns (deformations, temporal relations) that pose skeletons cannot represent; (2) the Explicit Pose Indicator (EPI) simulates inference-time misalignment during training by realigning driving poses to random anchor poses from a pose pool and applying random rescaling operations (modifying limb lengths, face size, adding/removing body parts) with a high probability (λ > 98%). The model is trained exclusively on human dance datasets yet generalizes to anthropomorphic characters, and the paper introduces A²Bench (500 GPT-4o-prompted, Kling-AI-generated anthropomorphic character image-video pairs) as a dedicated evaluation benchmark.

## Results

On the newly proposed A²Bench (rescaled pose setting), Animate-X achieves PSNR* of 13.60, SSIM of 0.452, LPIPS of 0.430, FID of 26.11, and FVD of 703.87, outperforming all baselines by substantial margins (e.g., 1.78 PSNR* over the next-best Unianimate, and 452.49 lower FVD). In the self-driven setting on A²Bench, Animate-X reaches PSNR* 14.10 and FVD 849.19, again leading all methods. On the standard human TikTok dataset, Animate-X (FVD 139.01, SSIM 0.806) matches or slightly exceeds Unianimate (FVD 148.06, SSIM 0.811) while generalizing to non-human characters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Animate-X dominates all 18 baselines across every metric on both A²Bench settings. Against the closest competitor Unianimate (which uses Mamba for temporal modeling), Animate-X gains +1.78 PSNR*, +0.054 SSIM, and −452.49 FVD on the rescaled-pose A²Bench, and +1.19 PSNR*, −268.10 FVD in the self-driven setting. GAN-based methods (FOMM, MRAA, LIA) trail significantly, with FOMM scoring FVD 2535 vs. Animate-X's 849 in self-driven mode. Among diffusion-based baselines, MimicMotion and MusePose suffer the largest FVD gaps (2250 and 1760 vs. 704 in rescaled setting), indicating poor temporal coherence on non-human characters. ControlNeXt, the strongest diffusion baseline after Unianimate, still lags by −2.72 PSNR* and −948 FVD. On the human TikTok dataset, Animate-X is essentially tied with Unianimate (FVD 139 vs. 148; SSIM 0.806 vs. 0.811), confirming no regression on the original domain while extending to anthropomorphic characters.

## Relation to Prior Work

Animate-X builds directly on the Animate Anyone / MimicMotion framework (CLIP appearance encoder + VAE latent + 3D-UNet denoiser with spatial/motion/temporal attention) and the latent diffusion model paradigm of Rombach et al. The key departure is the Pose Indicator: rather than relying solely on 2D pose skeletons extracted by DWPose, it augments motion conditioning with CLIP-based implicit features (IPI) and simulates body-shape misalignment during training via pose realignment and rescaling (EPI). This positions the work as a targeted fix for the human-only bias in existing pose-guided animation methods, extending them to anthropomorphic and non-human characters without requiring strict pose alignment at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens character animation to a much broader set of subjects (game characters, cartoon animals, food, furniture) that are highly relevant to gaming and entertainment pipelines, and introduces A²Bench as the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating anthropomorphic character animation. However, the benchmark is self-generated via GPT-4o + Kling AI, which limits its independence, and the comparison setting (rescaling anthropomorphic poses to human body shapes) is somewhat artificial. Open questions include whether the CLIP-based implicit motion signal generalizes to more complex multi-character interactions, whether hand and face fidelity can be improved, and whether the approach can achieve real-time inference given the multi-step diffusion process.
