## Problem

Prior robotic representation pre-training relies on in-the-wild human videos, which introduce an inherent human-robot embodiment gap and lack the dynamics information (actions, proprioceptive states) essential for manipulation. The paper identifies that a representation's ability to focus on manipulation-relevant regions—termed 'manipulation centricity'—strongly correlates with downstream policy success (Pearson R=0.93), yet no prior method explicitly optimizes for this property using robot-specific dynamics labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252952/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a single 224×224 RGB frame as input and outputs a 2048-dimensional visual feature vector (ResNet-50 backbone). During pre-training, the encoder is jointly trained with robot proprioceptive states (Cartesian position + gripper position, 14-dim) and actions (delta 6D pose + 1-DoF gripper, 7-dim) from the DROID dataset. At downstream deployment, the frozen feature vector is concatenated with the current proprioceptive state and fed into a small MLP policy head that outputs a continuous action distribution via behavior cloning.

## Method

MCR pre-trains a ResNet-50 on 36k trajectories from the DROID robot dataset using three jointly optimized objectives: (1) a dynamics alignment loss (InfoNCE) that contrasts the image feature at timestep t against an MLP-projected state-action dynamic chunk of length 3, with negatives sampled from other timesteps in the same trajectory; (2) an action prediction loss (MSE) where a shallow MLP head predicts the 7-dim robot action from the image feature; and (3) a time contrastive loss that pulls temporally adjacent frames closer than distant or cross-video frames. The key design choice is explicitly leveraging robot dynamics labels—proprioceptive states and actions—that are inherently absent in human video datasets, forcing the encoder to retain manipulation-relevant information rather than generic visual features. Training runs for 500k steps with Adam at lr=1e-4 on a single RTX 3090 (~50 hours), and the resulting frozen encoder outperforms the strongest baseline by 14.8% across 20 simulation tasks and by 76.9% on three real-robot tasks.

## Results

Across 20 simulation tasks spanning 4 domains (Robomimic, RoboCasa, MetaWorld, DexArt), MCR achieves a mean success rate of 78.7%, outperforming the strongest baseline R3M-DROID (68.5%) by 14.8% relative improvement. On three real-world manipulation tasks (Lift, Sweep, Rearrange), MCR achieves 23/30 success (76.7%), surpassing the best baseline VC-1 (13/30) by 76.9% relative improvement. MCR also achieves the highest success rate in 19 out of 20 individual simulation tasks. The method requires only ~50 hours on a single RTX 3090 Ti for pre-training, compared to ~120 hours on a V100 for R3M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCR outperforms all five baselines (LfS, MVP, VC-1, R3M, HRP) and the robot-data retrained variant R3M-DROID across every simulation domain. The strongest baseline is R3M-DROID (68.5% overall), which MCR surpasses by 10.2 absolute points (14.8% relative). The gap is most pronounced in MetaWorld (91.0% vs. 79.7% for R3M-DROID) and RoboCasa (75.8% vs. 61.8%), where prior human-data-based representations struggle most. R3M, the most widely used prior method, performs worst overall (56.8%), particularly in MetaWorld (56.9%) where it is barely above LfS. On real-robot tasks, MCR (23/30) nearly doubles the best baseline VC-1 (13/30), with the largest margin in the Sweep task (7/10 vs. 2/10 for VC-1). Notably, MCR also outperforms R3M-DROID on dexterous-hand tasks in DexArt (60.4% vs. 53.8%), despite DROID being collected exclusively with a gripper, suggesting the dynamics-aware objectives partially mitigate the embodiment gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on R3M (Nair et al., 2022), reusing its codebase and time-contrastive learning objective, but departs from the broader human-video pre-training paradigm (MVP, VC-1, HRP) by shifting to the DROID robot dataset and introducing two new objectives: a dynamics alignment contrastive loss that couples visual features with robot state-action chunks, and an action prediction loss. Conceptually, it extends the trajectory-label idea from RPT (Radosavovic et al., 2023) by explicitly aligning pixel representations with proprioceptive dynamics rather than just predicting future frames. The introduction of 'manipulation centricity' as a formal evaluation metric (via Grad-CAM vs. SAM 2 segmentation) is a new analytical tool not present in prior representation-learning-for-robotics work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The manipulation centricity metric (R=0.93 correlation with downstream success) gives the field a principled, interpretable way to diagnose why a representation works or fails for manipulation, moving beyond black-box benchmark comparisons. The practical finding that a 50-hour ResNet-50 pre-training on robot data outperforms 120-hour human-video baselines by 14.8% in simulation and 76.9% on real tasks is a strong efficiency argument for the robotics community. Key limitations include the embodiment gap (DROID uses only a gripper, hurting dexterous-hand tasks) and the thin real-robot evaluation (3 tasks, 10 trials each). Open questions include whether the dynamics objectives generalize to multi-embodiment datasets like Open X-Embodiment and whether language-conditioned or multi-modal extensions can further close the gap to generalist policies.
