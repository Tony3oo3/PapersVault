## Problem

Commuting OD flow generation lacks both a comprehensive dataset spanning diverse urban environments and a unified evaluation framework, as existing studies rely on single-city or few-city datasets with inconsistent metrics, making cross-model comparison impossible. This paper introduces LargeCommuingOD (3,333 U.S. areas covering 9.37M km²) and benchmarks nine models under a common protocol to identify which modeling paradigms generalize best.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253469/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes, for each area, a set of N regions (census tracts or CBGs) each described by 131 urban attributes (97 sociodemographic features from ACS and 34 POI counts from OpenStreetMap) plus pairwise Euclidean centroid distances, and produces a complete N×N directed commuting OD matrix where entry F_ij is the count of residents in region i working in region j. The dataset spans areas with highly variable region counts, and OD flows are aggregated from census-block-level LODES 2018 records to the tract/CBG level, so the output graph density and flow magnitudes vary dramatically across areas.

## Method

WEDAN (Weighted Edges Diffusion condition on Attributed Nodes) formulates OD flow generation as conditional graph generation: the entire area is an attributed directed weighted graph, and a denoising diffusion model generates all edge weights jointly given node attributes. The forward process adds independent Gaussian noise to the log-transformed OD matrix; the reverse process uses a 4-layer graph transformer backbone where node features and noisy edge features are processed through separate MLPs, self-attention computes node-level interactions, and FiLM fuses attention weights with edge features to capture node-edge dependencies. Distance-based Laplacian position encodings (d-LaPEs) obtained via spectral decomposition of the distance matrix are injected at both node and edge levels to encode spatial geometry. Training minimizes the standard DDPM noise-prediction MSE loss, and generation uses DDIM sampling with 250 diffusion steps, averaging 50 samples per area. The key insight is that modeling the joint distribution of all OD flows as a network—rather than predicting pairwise flows independently—captures network-level structural properties (inflow/outflow distributions, degree distributions) more faithfully, and training across 3,333 heterogeneous areas yields superior generalization to unseen urban environments.

## Results

On the LargeCommuingOD benchmark (3,333 areas across the U.S.), WEDAN achieves the best performance across all metrics: CPC 0.593, RMSE 68.6, NRMSE 0.876, and JSD (inflow/outflow/ODflow) of 0.291/0.269/0.147. Compared to the strongest baseline DiffODGen, WEDAN reduces RMSE/NRMSE by 8.0% and improves CPC by 11.5%, while also achieving the lowest JSD on all three distribution metrics. Network-based generative models (NetGAN, DiffODGen, WEDAN) consistently outperform pair-wise models (physical, tree-based, MLP, transformer, GNN), with WEDAN further improving over DiffODGen by 34.9% on RMSE for monocentric and polycentric areas.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WEDAN outperforms all 9 baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor DiffODGen (cascaded diffusion for large cities), WEDAN gains +11.5% CPC (0.593 vs. 0.532), −8.0% RMSE (68.6 vs. 74.6), and −8.0% NRMSE (0.876 vs. 0.953), with marginal JSD improvements. NetGAN, the GAN-based network generator, trails WEDAN by 21.7% on CPC and 22.3% on RMSE. Among pair-wise models, TransFlower (transformer-based) leads with CPC 0.488 and JSD ODflow 0.269, but still lags WEDAN by 17.7% on CPC. Classical ML baselines (RF, GBRT, SVR) cluster around CPC 0.42–0.46, while the physical Gravity Models (GM-P, GM-E) are the weakest at CPC ≈ 0.32 and RMSE > 160, confirming that simple parametric forms underfit complex mobility. The paper further shows WEDAN improves DiffODGen by 33.3% on RMSE specifically for large areas, and that cross-type transfer (e.g., monocentric→polycentric) retains 89.7% of full-training performance, evidencing strong generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of commuting OD flow modeling from physical models (gravity, radiation) through classical ML (RF, GBRT, SVR) and GNN-based pairwise predictors (GMEL, TransFlower) to a network-wise generative paradigm. It builds directly on DiffODGen (Rong et al., 2023b) and NetGAN (Bojchevski et al., 2018) for graph generation, but departs by formulating OD flow generation as conditional weighted-edge diffusion over attributed nodes (WEDAN) rather than cascaded topology-then-weight generation. The key conceptual shift is treating the entire OD matrix as a single graph sample to be generated jointly, rather than predicting pairwise flows independently, and grounding this in a large-scale multi-area dataset that enables evaluation of generalizability across urban environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LargeCommuingOD dataset (3,333 areas, 9.3M km², 131 features per region) fills a critical gap: prior work evaluated on single cities or <8 metropolitans, making cross-model comparison and generalizability assessment impossible. The finding that network-based generative models consistently outperform pairwise predictors across all area sizes and structures suggests a promising new direction for the field. However, limitations remain: single-year data, US-only coverage, commuting flows only (no other mobility types), and the WEDAN model is explicitly described as a 'preliminary try.' Open questions include temporal dynamics, cross-country transferability (partially explored with UK data), and how to improve interpretability of generative models for urban planning stakeholders.
