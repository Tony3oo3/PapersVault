## Problem

The DFIV algorithm (Xu et al., 2021) uses learnable deep neural network features in a two-stage IV regression framework and shows strong empirical performance, but no minimax convergence guarantees existed. It was also unclear under what conditions data-adaptive neural features provably outperform fixed-feature (kernel or sieve) IV estimators, particularly for structural functions with mixed smooth and discontinuous regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253395/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes two separate datasets: m i.i.d. pairs (X, Z) for Stage 1 and n i.i.d. pairs (Y, Z) for Stage 2, with no access to joint (X, Y, Z) triples. The output is the estimated structural function f̂_str = ψ_θ̂x, a scalar-valued DNN on X. A key practical detail is that the Stage 2 predictor must be constrained to a Besov ball (via domain restriction or explicit Besov-norm regularization) to ensure the Stage 1 regression target Tψ_θx has controlled smoothness.

## Method

The paper provides a complete minimax analysis of DFIV in Besov spaces B^s_{p,q}(X). Stage 1 learns the conditional expectation E[ψ_θx(X)|Z] via a DNN φ_θz(Z) by empirical risk minimization; Stage 2 regresses Y against the Stage 1 estimate with an optional Besov-norm regularizer. The upper bound proof constructs a dynamic data-dependent cover to control the supremal Stage 1 estimation error over the Stage 2 hypothesis class, and requires new approximation results for sigmoid DNNs in Besov norms. The key theoretical results are: (i) DFIV achieves the minimax optimal rate n^{-(2s+2γ₀)/(2s+2γ₀+d_x)} in the projected metric and n^{-(2s)/(2s+2γ₁+d_x)} in the full L² metric when γ₀=γ₁ and T has maximal smoothness, with only m=Ω(n) Stage 1 samples needed (versus m/n→∞ for kernel IV); (ii) a strict separation from all linear IV estimators when p<2, where the degree of separation Δ=d_x(1/p−1/2)_+ makes fixed-feature methods suboptimal while DFIV remains rate-optimal.

## Results

The paper establishes that DFIV achieves the minimax optimal rate n^{-(2s+2γ₀)/(2s+2γ₀+d_x)} (up to log factors) in the projected metric and n^{-2s/(2s+2γ₁+d_x)} in the full L² metric, provided γ₀=γ₁ and T has maximal smoothness, requiring only m=Ω(n) Stage 1 samples. In contrast, fixed-feature (kernel/sieve) IV estimators are shown to be strictly suboptimal when the structural function lies in a Besov space with p<2 (low spatial homogeneity), incurring an additional penalty of Δ=d_x(1/p−1/2) in the exponent. For the full metric, the fixed-feature lower bound is n^{-2(s−Δ)/(2(s−Δ)+2γ₁+d_x)}, which is strictly slower than the DFIV rate when Δ>0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to kernel IV (Singh et al., 2019) and kernel IV with minimax analysis (Meunier et al., 2024), DFIV is strictly more data-efficient in Stage 1: it requires only m=Ω(n) to achieve the optimal rate, whereas both kernel methods require m/n→∞ (i.e., m=ω(n)), which leads to suboptimal scaling in total samples m+n. For smooth target functions (p≥2), DFIV recovers the same minimax rates previously established by Chen & Reiss (2011) and Meunier et al. (2024) for fixed-feature estimators, confirming no loss in the well-behaved regime. The key separation result (Corollary 4.2) shows that when p<2 (the structural function has both smooth and spiky/discontinuous regions), DFIV achieves the optimal rate n^{-2s/(2s+2γ₁+d_x)} while every linear IV estimator (including 2SLS, sieve IV, kernel IV, and moment-based methods) is lower bounded by the strictly slower rate n^{-2(s−Δ)/(2(s−Δ)+2γ₁+d_x)}, with the gap Δ=d_x(1/p−1/2) quantifying the degree of spatial inhomogeneity. This separation is the first rigorous demonstration that data-adaptive deep features provide a provable advantage over fixed dictionaries in the IV setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first minimax convergence analysis for the DFIV algorithm (Xu et al., 2021), which extends two-stage least squares regression to learnable deep neural network features in both stages. It builds on Suzuki's (2019) DNN approximation theory for Besov spaces and the classical link/reverse-link condition framework from fixed-feature IV literature (Chen & Reiss, 2011; Singh et al., 2019; Meunier et al., 2024), but departs from these by introducing a novel smoothness condition on the projection operator T and developing new sigmoid DNN approximation results for B-splines. The separation result for spatially inhomogeneous functions (p < 2) extends the classical Donoho–Johnstone wavelet thresholding separation to the two-stage IV setting, where the additional challenge of controlling Stage 2 network smoothness is addressed via Besov-norm regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant theoretical gap by establishing that DFIV achieves minimax optimal rates, validating the empirical superiority of data-adaptive neural features over fixed-dictionary IV methods. The finding that DFIV requires only m = Ω(n) Stage 1 samples (versus m/n → ∞ for kernel IV) has direct practical implications for sample allocation in two-stage causal estimation. Open questions include whether the smoothness condition (Assumption 4) can be verified or estimated from data in practice, whether the results extend to vector-valued or high-dimensional structural functions, and whether the separation gap between DFIV and linear estimators widens further in higher dimensions or with more complex spatial inhomogeneity patterns.
