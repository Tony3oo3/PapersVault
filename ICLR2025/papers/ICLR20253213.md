## Problem

Group distributionally robust optimization (DRO) aims to minimize worst-case loss across a finite set of data distributions, but existing methods rely on heuristics when the loss is non-convex in model parameters (as with neural networks) and no methods exist for non-parametric model classes like gradient boosting. These heuristic approaches are expensive, unstable, and sometimes underperform simple data balancing. The paper asks: what is the optimal data mixture for group DRO, and can it be found via a tractable optimization?

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled datasets D_p from each group distribution p in a finite set P, along with per-group proxy prediction functions f_p (trained on the same or separate data) and, when covariate shift is present, covariate density estimates p(x) (e.g., via Gaussian KDE). It outputs a set of mixture weights {λ_p} over the group distributions, which are then used either to reweight the training loss for a single new model or to form a weighted ensemble of the per-group proxy models. The final output is a group DRO model minimizing worst-case loss across all groups.

## Method

The key theoretical move is reparameterizing group DRO from parameter space to function space (bounded functions L^∞), where a minimax theorem holds via the weak*-topology and Banach-Alaoglu compactness. For cross-entropy and ℓ₂² losses, the optimal mixture weights are shown to maximize a concave objective (expected conditional entropy or conditional variance) over the probability simplex of mixture weights, reducing the max-min problem to a single convex maximization. The MixMax algorithm solves this via stochastic entropic mirror ascent, computing gradients from per-group proxy models and covariate densities. In practice, E²MixMax trains a separate model on each group's data as a proxy for the Bayes optimal function, then iteratively updates mixture weights using empirical gradient estimates, requiring the same training compute as fitting one model on all data.

## Results

On the XGBoost tabular experiments, E²MixMax with Data Reuse matched or outperformed data balancing in all 12 settings, with the largest gains occurring under significant label shift: 1.6% absolute (2.8% relative) improvement on ACSIncome (sex groups, 1 feature), 2.3% (3.7% relative) on CelebA-Young (10 features), and 3.2% (5.9% relative) on CelebA-Young (5 features). On the sequence modeling task, E²MixMax ensemble models achieved lower worst-group cross-entropy than both DoReMi and the group DRO trained model, with improvements growing as the Markov chain distributions became less similar. The improvement over balancing correlated strongly (Pearson r = 0.79, p = 0.002) with the gap between oracle and balanced worst-group accuracy, indicating MixMax is most beneficial when there is substantial room for improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DoReMi (Xie et al., 2024) on the Markov chain sequence modeling task, E²MixMax consistently found mixture weights yielding lower worst-group cross-entropy of the Bayes optimal function, with the advantage growing as the three Markov chains became less similar (lower Dirichlet magnitude). Against the original group DRO gradient ascent/descent algorithm (Sagawa et al., 2019), E²MixMax's ensemble of proxy transformers outperformed the group DRO trained model on worst-group cross-entropy, while group DRO's own mixture weights performed comparably to simple balanced weights—consistent with prior findings that group DRO training is unstable. Against data balancing (Idrissi et al., 2022) on XGBoost tabular experiments, E²MixMax with Data Reuse matched or exceeded balanced worst-group accuracy in all 12 settings, with the largest absolute gains (up to 3.2%) occurring when the oracle-balanced gap was widest; the improvement correlated at r = 0.79 with the available room for improvement, confirming MixMax is most impactful under substantial group shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimax/saddle-point framework for DRO (Shapiro & Kleywegt, 2002) from finite-dimensional parameter spaces to the space of all bounded functions, using the weak*-topology and Banach-Alaoglu's theorem to overcome the non-compactness that prevents standard Sion's minimax from applying. It addresses the group DRO formulation of Sagawa et al. (2019) and the data-mixture optimization of DoReMi (Xie et al., 2024), but departs fundamentally by showing that for cross-entropy and ℓ₂² losses, the non-convex group DRO problem collapses to a concave maximization over mixture weights followed by standard risk minimization. The paper also connects to the invariant risk minimization and data bias literature (Arjovsky et al., 2019; Slowik & Bottou, 2021), which showed DRO solutions minimize specific mixtures existentially but did not provide a method to find them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical impact is providing the first group DRO method applicable to non-parametric models like XGBoost, where no prior DRO approaches existed beyond data balancing. The theoretical reduction—convex optimization for mixture weights followed by classical training—offers a clean, stable alternative to the unstable gradient ascent-descent methods that prior work (Idrissi et al., 2022) showed can underperform simple balancing. Key limitations include the requirement to model covariate shifts (expensive in high-dimensional spaces), restriction to cross-entropy and ℓ₂² losses, and the gap between the theoretical guarantees (requiring Bayes-optimal fitting) and empirical performance. Open questions include extending the minimax theorem to other loss functions, developing better empirical gradient estimators, and scaling to high-dimensional generative models.
