## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has not been extended to thermal imaging, and existing thermal 3D reconstruction methods rely on NeRF-based approaches that suffer from slow rendering and implicit scene representations. Thermal images exhibit low-texture and ghosting characteristics that prevent direct use in SfM-based 3DGS pipelines, and no open-source dataset with properly calibrated, multi-view-consistent RGB-thermal pairs existed for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250045/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB and thermal image pairs (captured at 240×180 by a FLIR E6 PRO handheld camera) along with camera poses estimated via multimodal SfM on registered or MSX-blended images, since raw thermal images lack sufficient texture for feature matching. It produces a multimodal 3D Gaussian representation that renders both thermal and RGB images at 640×480 resolution from arbitrary novel viewpoints, with the Gaussian set shared or paired across modalities depending on the chosen strategy (MFTG, MSMG, or OMMG).

## Method

ThermalGaussian extends 3DGS with three key mechanisms: (1) a multimodal initialization pipeline that calibrates RGB and thermal cameras using a standard chessboard (heated to create thermal contrast) and estimates thermal camera poses via RGB-only, blended (β-weighted), or MSX SfM strategies; (2) three thermal Gaussian training strategies—MFTG (two-stage: train RGB Gaussian then fine-tune with thermal), MSMG (jointly train separate RGB and thermal Gaussian sets with dual-modal losses), and OMMG (a single Gaussian carrying both RGB and thermal spherical harmonics rendered via separate alpha-blending equations); and (3) a thermal-specific loss incorporating a spatial smoothness term that exploits the physical property that thermal images lack abrupt transitions due to continuous heat transfer, combined with a multimodal regularization coefficient γ that dynamically rebalances per-modality loss weights based on the relative number of Gaussians each modality generates, reducing total storage by ~90% while improving rendering quality in both modalities.

## Results

On the RGBT-Scenes dataset (10 scenes), the best variant Ours_OMMG achieves an average thermal PSNR of 25.64 dB, SSIM of 0.882, and LPIPS of 0.170, outperforming 3DGS+MI (24.31 dB) by 1.3 dB and ThermoNeRF (21.13 dB) by 4.5 dB. For RGB rendering, Ours_OMMG reaches 24.34 dB PSNR, improving over the original 3DGS (23.20 dB) by 1.1 dB while simultaneously producing thermal output. With multimodal regularization (MSMG+MR), model storage drops to 9 MB per modality (vs. 159 MB for RGB-only 3DGS), a 92% reduction, while rendering speed increases to 390/420 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023), direct thermal rendering fails on 7 out of 10 scenes due to low-texture and ghosting characteristics of thermal images; where 3DGS does succeed, Ours_OMMG still leads by 2–4 dB in PSNR. Against ThermoNeRF (Hassan et al., 2024), Ours_OMMG achieves a 4.5 dB PSNR gain on thermal rendering (25.64 vs. 21.13) and a 5.9 dB gain on RGB rendering (24.34 vs. 18.46), indicating that ThermoNeRF degrades RGB quality while this method improves it. Compared to 3DGS+MI (3DGS trained on thermal images after multimodal initialization), Ours_OMMG gains 1.3 dB PSNR and 0.023 SSIM on thermal, confirming that the multimodal Gaussian strategies (dual-modal loss, thermal smoothness term) add value beyond pose correction alone. Notably, the multimodal approach also improves RGB over vanilla 3DGS by 1.1 dB, demonstrating that thermal constraints help disambiguate scenes where color information is insufficient (e.g., low-light or low-contrast foreground/background separation).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from its original single-modality RGB setting to a multimodal RGB+thermal framework, positioning itself as the first thermal 3DGS approach. It departs from prior NeRF-based thermal reconstruction methods (ThermoNeRF, Thermal-NeRF) by replacing the implicit radiance field with explicit Gaussians, gaining real-time rendering and editability. The key methodological departures include a multimodal initialization pipeline that leverages RGB camera poses for thermal SfM (since thermal images lack sufficient texture), a thermal-specific smoothness loss grounded in heat-transfer physics, and a dynamic multimodal regularization coefficient that balances modality weights based on Gaussian counts during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% storage reduction through multimodal regularization and the simultaneous improvement of both thermal and RGB rendering quality (≈1 dB PSNR gain on RGB) make this practically relevant for surveillance, industrial inspection, and search-and-rescue applications where thermal and visual data must coexist in a compact, real-time-renderable 3D model. The RGBT-Scenes dataset addresses a recognized gap in the field by providing calibrated, multi-view, multi-scene thermal+RGB pairs, though its scope (10 scenes, 240×180 thermal resolution, single-GPU experiments) limits immediate scalability. Open questions include whether the multimodal regularization generalizes to more than two modalities (e.g., adding depth or LiDAR), how the approach performs on large-scale outdoor scenes, and whether the thermal smoothness prior holds in transient thermal scenarios (e.g., rapidly heating objects).
