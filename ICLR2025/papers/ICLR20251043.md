## Problem

Existing sequence and time-series representation learning methods either assume a shared universal embedding space (as in NLP) or treat each sequence independently, making it difficult to balance learning common domain dynamics with faithful individual representations—especially when observations are limited or partially observed. This paper addresses the gap by providing an unsupervised representation learning method for nonlinear sequences with provable parameter recovery guarantees, rather than relying on heuristic neural architectures without theoretical backing.

## Input / Output

The method takes N vector-valued sequences of length T with C channels each (or symbolic sequences encoded as one-hot probability vectors over an alphabet), along with a chosen autoregressive order d and nuclear norm radius λ. It produces a low-rank parameter matrix B ∈ R^{(C²d+C)×N} whose columns, expressed in the right singular vector basis, yield r-dimensional embeddings per sequence; the left singular vectors span the common domain subspace. For variable-length sequences (e.g., genomes), the method uses stochastic approximation over randomly sampled sub-windows of fixed length G at each iteration.

## Method

Each sequence is modeled by its own vector autoregressive process E[x_{i,t}|H_{i,t}] = φ(R_i ξ_{i,t}) with an arbitrary monotone link function φ (identity for linear AR, softmax for symbolic data, etc.), and the collection of per-sequence parameter vectors is stacked into a matrix B constrained to a nuclear norm ball to enforce low-rank structure across sequences. For the linear case, recovery reduces to a convex least-squares program solved by proximal gradient with singular value thresholding. For the general nonlinear case, the paper casts parameter recovery as a monotone Variational Inequality with vector field Ψ(B) = E_t[A_t*(φ(A_t(B)) − y_t)], where A_t is a linear measurement operator and A_t* its adjoint; monotonicity of φ guarantees Ψ is monotone, preserving convexity. The VI is solved via an extragradient method with backtracking line search and incremental SVD-based nuclear ball projection, yielding Õ(1/ε) iteration complexity. The key design choice is that the low-rank constraint is imposed jointly across all sequences, so information from every sequence informs the representation of each, enabling sample-efficient recovery even when individual sequences are short or partially observed.

## Results

On 36 UCR time series classification datasets, the method achieves mean ARI 0.602, NMI 0.562, macro-F1 0.817, and accuracy 0.788, with an average runtime of 400 s (including full λ grid search and SVD at each step). This is comparable to the contrastive-learning baseline TS2Vec (ARI 0.606, F1 0.840) while using only 37% of its runtime (400 s vs. 1,085 s). On synthetic autoregressive sequences, nuclear-norm regularization reduces the relative parameter reconstruction error from 0.341 (unconstrained) to 0.158 while improving clustering ARI from 0.967 to 0.997 and halving the effective rank (14.4 → 7.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method clearly outperforms all distance-based baselines: it beats ℓ₂+KNN (ARI 0.602 vs. 0.422), DTW+KNN (0.602 vs. 0.447), and shapeDTW (0.602 vs. 0.470) by 0.13–0.18 ARI points, and also surpasses the masked-modeling baseline Ti-MAE (0.602 vs. 0.461 ARI) while running roughly 3× faster (400 s vs. 1,276 s). Against the contrastive-learning baseline TS2Vec, the method is nearly matched on ARI (0.602 vs. 0.606) and within 2.3 points on F1 (0.817 vs. 0.840), but completes in only 37% of TS2Vec's runtime (400 s vs. 1,085 s). The hand-tuned dictionary ensemble MR-Hydra (MultiROCKET+Hydra) outperforms the proposed method on every metric (ARI 0.682 vs. 0.602, F1 0.877 vs. 0.817), though it does not produce latent embeddings and was specifically engineered for UCR-style classification. The authors note that MR-Hydra's advantage is expected given its task-specific tuning, and that their method's embeddings remain useful for downstream tasks beyond classification (clustering, anomaly detection) where MR-Hydra cannot be applied.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the low-rank matrix recovery framework (nuclear norm relaxation, singular value thresholding) from collaborative filtering and PCA into the sequence representation learning setting, where the 'matrix' is the collection of autoregressive model parameters across sequences. The optimization machinery is drawn from the monotone variational inequality framework of Juditsky & Nemirovski (2020, 2023), which provides convex parameter recovery with provable guarantees for generalized linear models, and from extragradient methods with backtracking (Iusem et al., 2019). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant self-supervised paradigm (contrastive learning à la TS2Vec, masked modeling à la Ti-MAE) by using autoregression as the auxiliary task and explicitly enforcing a low-rank structure across sequence parameters rather than learning an encoder implicitly through a neural architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper brings provable recovery guarantees to sequence representation learning—a property largely absent from the deep-learning-based methods that currently dominate the field—and demonstrates that a convex, model-based approach can achieve performance competitive with contrastive and masked autoencoding methods while using substantially less compute (37% of TS2Vec's runtime). Its applicability to both continuous time series and symbolic sequences (natural language, viral genomes) under a single framework is a practical strength, particularly in limited-data or partially-observed settings where deep methods struggle. Key limitations include the reliance on convexity (restricting expressiveness for highly non-convex dynamics), the assumption of autoregressive structure, and being outperformed by the hand-tuned MR-Hydra ensemble on UCR classification. Open questions include non-convex extensions within the VI framework, alternative objectives, and whether the low-rank assumption generalizes beyond the tested domains.
