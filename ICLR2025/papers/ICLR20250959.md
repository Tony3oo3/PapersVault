## Problem

Sparse autoencoders are a promising unsupervised approach for extracting interpretable features from language models, but training them at scale is hampered by the difficulty of tuning the L1 sparsity penalty, the proliferation of dead latents in large autoencoders, and the absence of clean scaling laws. Prior work has been limited to relatively small autoencoders on small language models, leaving open whether the approach can reliably scale to frontier models like GPT-4 and whether larger autoencoders actually yield better features.

## Input / Output

The autoencoder consumes a residual stream activation vector from a fixed layer of a frozen language model (layer 5/6 for GPT-4, layer 8 for GPT-2 small), mean-subtracted and normalized to unit norm over a 64-token context. It produces a sparse latent code with exactly k active dimensions and a dense reconstruction of the input; the trained decoder rows serve as interpretable feature directions for probing, ablation, and circuit discovery. The largest model trained has 16 million latents on GPT-4 activations over 40 billion tokens.

## Method

The core architectural change replaces the L1-penalized ReLU activation with a TopK function that retains only the k largest pre-activations, directly controlling sparsity without a tunable penalty and eliminating L1-induced activation shrinkage. To prevent dead latents at scale, the paper introduces tied initialization (encoder set to the transpose of the decoder) and an AuxK auxiliary loss that forces the top-k_aux dead latents to model the residual reconstruction error, reducing dead latents to roughly 7% even at 16M latents. The paper derives a joint scaling law L(n,k) for reconstruction MSE as a function of total latents n and active latents k, and introduces four evaluation metrics—downstream KL loss, 1D probe loss on 61 binary classification tasks, N2G explanation quality, and logit-diff ablation sparsity—that all generally improve with autoencoder size. Custom sparse matrix-multiplication kernels and 512-way tensor sharding enable training at these scales with a batch size of 131,072 tokens.

## Results

The paper trains a 16 million latent TopK autoencoder on GPT-4 residual stream activations for 40 billion tokens, achieving only 7% dead latents (vs. up to 90% without mitigations). The joint scaling law L(n,k) = exp(α + β_k·log k + β_n·log n + γ·log k·log n) + exp(ζ + η·log k) fits the data with coefficients α=−0.50, β_k=0.26, β_n=−0.017, γ=−0.042, ζ=−1.32, η=−0.085. When the 16M autoencoder is substituted into GPT-4, the resulting language modeling loss corresponds to only 10% of GPT-4's pretraining compute. Ablation sparsity of TopK latents reaches (L₁/L₂)² of 10–14%, far sparser than the ~60% from ablating raw residual stream channels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopK SAEs dominate ReLU SAEs (Bricken et al., 2023) across all evaluated metrics: better reconstruction-sparsity Pareto frontier (Figure 2a), steeper scaling laws at fixed sparsity (Figure 2b), lower downstream KL and cross-entropy loss (Figure 5), better probe scores (Figure 23), and higher N2G F1 with >1.5× better recall at only slightly worse precision (>0.9×) (Figure 24). Against ProLU (Taggart, 2024), TopK achieves a strictly better reconstruction-sparsity trade-off at fixed n=32768 (Figure 2a) and steeper scaling at fixed L₀=128 (Figure 2b). Against Gated SAEs (Rajamanoharan et al., 2024), TopK is comparable on the reconstruction-sparsity frontier (Figure 2a) but clearly superior on downstream loss at fixed sparsity (Figure 5a) and at fixed MSE (Figure 5b), indicating that TopK captures behaviorally relevant features more effectively. The gap between TopK and ReLU widens with scale, suggesting the advantage is most pronounced at the largest autoencoder sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse autoencoder (SAE) framework for language model interpretability established by Bricken et al. (2023) and Cunningham et al. (2023), which primarily trained small SAEs on small models, by demonstrating reliable training at extreme scale (16M latents on GPT-4). The core architectural choice—replacing the L1 sparsity penalty with a TopK activation function—revives the k-sparse autoencoder of Makhzani & Frey (2013), while the compute-optimal training protocol follows Lindsey et al. (2024). The paper departs from concurrent work on activation shrinkage (ProLU by Taggart 2024, Gated SAE by Rajamanoharan et al. 2024) by showing that TopK, combined with an auxiliary dead-latent loss (AuxK) and tied initialization, achieves the best reconstruction-sparsity frontier and the fewest dead latents at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first reliable, well-characterized recipe for training SAEs at the scale needed for frontier models, with clean power-law scaling relationships in both autoencoder size and sparsity that the interpretability community can use to plan compute budgets. The introduction of multiple complementary evaluation metrics (downstream loss, probe loss, N2G explainability, ablation sparsity) moves the field beyond the single MSE/sparsity trade-off toward measuring whether features are actually useful. Key open questions remain: whether monosemanticity improves further with scale, how to make TopK variable (constraining E[L0] rather than L0), whether combining MoE with SAEs can break the current compute ceiling, and whether the 64-token context length captures the most interesting GPT-4 behaviors.
