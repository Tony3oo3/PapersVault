## Problem

MLLMs exhibit a causal performance drop when answering questions about small visual details (e.g., small text, distant objects), yet it remains unclear whether this stems from a failure to locate the relevant region or from an inability to perceive fine details once looking at the right place. Existing mitigations require expensive retraining at higher resolutions or external localization tools, leaving a gap for scalable, training-free solutions that exploit the model's own internal representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252455/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to the MLLM's native input resolution, e.g., 224×224 for InstructBLIP or 336×336 for LLaVA-1.5) and a text question, and produces a text answer. The ViCrop pipeline additionally generates a square-cropped sub-region of the image, resized to the same input resolution, whose ViT tokens are concatenated with the original image tokens before the LLM decodes the answer. For very high-resolution images (V* benchmark, >1K pixels), a two-stage block-wise importance computation is applied before the final crop is selected.

## Method

The paper first establishes via an intervention study (ground-truth bounding-box cropping) that MLLMs' small-detail failure is a perception limitation, not a localization one, and then shows through attention-ratio analysis that MLLMs consistently attend to the correct image region even when they answer incorrectly. Building on this, ViCrop proposes three training-free importance-map estimators: (1) rel-att, which normalizes the answer-to-image cross-attention by a generic instruction to suppress register-token effects; (2) grad-att, which multiplies attention scores by the ReLU of their gradients with respect to the log-max output probability (a Grad-CAM-style weighting); and (3) pure-grad, which computes the L2-norm of input-image gradients and suppresses flat-color regions via a Gaussian high-pass filter. A sliding-window search over the importance map selects a bounding box, which is cropped, resized to the MLLM's input resolution, and fed alongside the original image so the model can perceive the small detail at full effective resolution without any parameter updates.

## Results

On TextVQA, the best internal ViCrop method (grad-att) improves LLaVA-1.5 from 47.80 to 56.06 VQA-score (+8.26) and InstructBLIP from 33.48 to 45.71 (+12.23), all without any training. On the high-resolution V* benchmark, rel-att boosts LLaVA-1.5 from 42.41 to 62.30 (+19.89) and InstructBLIP from 35.60 to 42.41 (+6.81). Performance on larger-visual-concept benchmarks (GQA, AOKVQA, VQAv2) is maintained or slightly improved, confirming that the accuracy gain on fine details does not come at the cost of general perception. Inference overhead is modest: rel-att adds ~1.16 s on GPU for LLaVA-1.5, equivalent to generating only ~5 answer tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its internal ViCrop methods against three external-tool baselines (SAM, YOLO, CLIP) on TextVQA (Table 4) and against the SEAL multi-agent method (Wu & Xie, 2023) across seven benchmarks (Table 7). All internal methods outperform every external tool: for LLaVA-1.5, grad-att reaches 56.06 vs. SAM 49.42, YOLO 48.84, and CLIP 48.55; for InstructBLIP, grad-att reaches 45.71 vs. SAM 39.23, YOLO 36.49, and CLIP 39.61. Against SEAL, LLaVA-1.5+rel-att wins on 6 of 7 benchmarks (e.g., TextVQA 55.17 vs. 36.30, DocVQA 19.63 vs. 5.31, VQAv2 76.29 vs. 65.35) but loses on V* (62.30 vs. 75.30), which the authors attribute to SEAL being specifically tuned for high-resolution images. Inference time also favors the internal methods: rel-att takes ~1.16 s on GPU for LLaVA-1.5, while SEAL averages 4.44 s per question.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Grad-CAM-style gradient-weighted localization (Selvaraju et al., 2017) from single-task classifiers to the multi-stage architecture of MLLMs, deriving a model-agnostic formulation that composes answer-to-token and token-to-image attention across the LLM backbone and connector. It departs from prior work on MLLMs' small-object difficulty (Zhang et al. 2023b, 2024a; Wu and Xie 2023) by establishing a causal link between visual concept size and perception failure through intervention, and by showing the failure is perceptual rather than locational. Unlike concurrent high-resolution fine-tuning efforts (LLaVA-Next, Monkey, etc.), ViCrop is entirely training-free and leverages the model's own internal states, positioning it as an orthogonal, inference-time complement rather than a replacement for those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLLMs are increasingly deployed in detail-sensitive domains (biomedicine, autonomous driving, security), the demonstrated size bias—up to 24 percentage points accuracy drop for small objects even in GPT-4o—represents a concrete and quantified risk. The finding that models attend to the correct region even when wrong reframes the failure mode from 'can't find it' to 'can't resolve it,' which has implications for how practitioners should diagnose and mitigate MLLM errors. Key open questions include extending ViCrop to multi-region and relational questions, reducing inference overhead, and whether the approach generalizes to non-Transformer MLLM architectures. The method's moderate but consistent gains across seven benchmarks and two model families suggest it is a practical, low-cost intervention for production deployments.
