## Problem

Existing 3D dense correspondence methods either rely solely on untextured geometry (limiting semantic understanding) or naively average multiview 2D features without 3D geometric consistency (producing noisy, viewpoint-dependent results). Additionally, prior 3D matching datasets contain only a few categories of synthetic shapes without texture, preventing generalization to diverse in-the-wild objects. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a textured multi-category dataset and a framework that fuses 2D foundation model semantics with 3D geometric structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of textured 3D meshes as input, which are remeshed to ~2000 vertices and rendered from 5 fixed views (3 lateral, 1 top, 1 bottom). It produces dense vertex-to-vertex correspondences via a soft assignment matrix Π ∈ R^{n_N × n_M} and 512-dimensional unit-normalized per-vertex semantic features. Unlike prior geometry-only methods, texture is required as input; unlike Diff3F which renders 100 views, only 5 views suffice due to the 3D refiner enforcing spatial consistency.

## Method

DenseMatcher operates in three stages: (1) multiview 2D features are extracted via SD-DINO (fusing DINOv2 and Stable Diffusion features), upsampled with FeatUp, projected onto mesh vertices by bilinear interpolation, and averaged across visible views; (2) a lightweight 4-block DiffusionNet (the only trainable component, ~5M parameters) refines these features by concatenating them with Heat Kernel Signature descriptors and sinusoidal positional encodings, producing spatially consistent 512-dim per-vertex features; (3) correspondences are computed via functional map with two novel regularization terms beyond the standard isometry and point-to-point commutativity constraints — an entropy penalty on the clamped mapping matrix to enforce sparsity, and row/column sum constraints to make Π a valid soft assignment matrix. Training uses a semantic distance loss that enforces L2 feature distance to scale linearly with geodesic distance between semantic groups, plus a feature preservation loss that trains a linear back-projection to reconstruct the original multiview features, retaining 2D foundation model information.

## Results

On the DenseCorr3D benchmark, DenseMatcher achieves an AUC of 0.737 and Normalized Geodesic Error of 3.49 on the full test set, substantially outperforming all baselines (best baseline: ConsistFMap at AUC 0.568, Err 6.42). On the Fruits & Vegetables subset, DenseMatcher reaches AUC 0.740 and Err 3.41. In real-world robotic manipulation across 6 long-horizon tasks, DenseMatcher achieves a 76.7% overall success rate from a single human demonstration, compared to 30% for Robo-ABC with its original memory and 50% with a restricted memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DenseMatcher outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation split. Against ConsistFMap (Cao & Bernard, 2022), the strongest shape-matching baseline trained on DenseCorr3D, DenseMatcher improves AUC from 0.568 to 0.737 and reduces geodesic error from 6.42 to 3.49 on the full set. URSSM (Cao et al., 2023), whether trained on FAUST or DenseCorr3D, lags behind by 0.17–0.25 AUC and 3.2–4.7 in error. Diff3F (Dutt et al., 2024), which naively averages multiview 2D features without 3D geometry, performs worst (AUC 0.437, Err 8.20), confirming that the DiffusionNet refiner and geometric constraints are critical. In the robotic manipulation setting, DenseMatcher (76.7% success) more than doubles Robo-ABC with original memory (30%) and substantially exceeds the restricted-memory variant (50%), demonstrating that 3D dense correspondence transfers manipulation knowledge more reliably than 2D affordance retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

DenseMatcher sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: geometry-only 3D correspondence methods (ConsistFMap, URSSM) that use spectral descriptors like HKS/WKS on untextured meshes, and 2D semantic feature projection methods (Diff3F) that leverage foundation models but ignore 3D geometry. The paper's core architectural contribution is bridging these by projecting SD-DINO multiview features onto meshes and refining them with a lightweight DiffusionNet, then solving correspondences via functional map augmented with new entropy and sum-to-1 regularization terms. It also introduces the first 3D matching dataset (DenseCorr3D) with colored meshes and semantic group annotations, departing from prior benchmarks (FAUST, SHREC) that are category-specific and texture-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical demonstration of transferring manipulation from a single human demo to a robot across instances and categories (76.7% vs. 50% for Robo-ABC) validates that dense 3D semantic correspondence is a viable pathway for generalizable manipulation without large demonstration datasets. The zero-shot color transfer between 3D assets is a novel application that had not been demonstrated before in 3D generation. Open questions include scalability to scenes with many objects, handling of partial/occluded meshes in real-time settings, and whether the 2D foundation model dependency limits robustness to domain shifts (e.g., novel materials or lighting). The dataset, while first-of-its-kind, is limited to 600 assets across 24 categories, leaving room for broader coverage.
