## Problem

While polysemantic neurons are well-studied in mechanistic interpretability, their relationship to weight sparsity—a leading post-training compression technique—remains unexplored. Specifically, it is unknown which neurons are most sensitive to pruning, why they are sensitive, and whether their entangled input-output mappings can be effectively disentangled to preserve accuracy under high sparsity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253263/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense pretrained LLM (Pythia-70M through 12B, Llama-2-7B/13B/70B, Llama-3-8B) and a calibration subset of Wikitext-2 as input. It produces a sparsified model where each FFN linear layer's neurons are individually expanded into a mixture of sparse experts (up to 32 per neuron), with routing determined by PCA-reduced K-means clustering of layer inputs. Evaluation is via Wikitext-2 perplexity and zero-shot benchmark scores (SQuAD 2.0, GSM8K, MMLU, BBH, ARC, SciQ, Lambada, TriviaQA).

## Method

The paper introduces two key metrics: the 1-Wasserstein distance between a neuron's normalized output distribution and N(0,1) as a measure of entanglement, and a 'mapping difficulty' (MD) score quantifying how often a neuron maps similar inputs to dissimilar outputs. The core framework, Sparse Expansion, clusters each layer's calibration inputs via PCA dimensionality reduction followed by K-means, then applies SparseGPT independently to each cluster's input subset, creating a mixture of sparse experts per neuron that specialize over different portions of the output distribution. This is a one-shot, no-retraining procedure: all experts start from the same dense weights and are sparsified with input-aware Hessian-based optimization tailored to their assigned input subgroup. The paper demonstrates that Wasserstein neurons (top ~3% by WD) are disproportionately sensitive to sparsification, that Sparse Expansion recovers their performance by reducing both weighted WD and MD, and that the number of effective input features (measured via PCA components for 90% variance) empirically bounds both the minimum error and maximum improvement achievable under sparse computation.

## Results

On Llama-2-7B at 50% unstructured sparsity, Sparse Expansion achieves 5.5839 Wikitext-2 perplexity, outperforming SparseGPT (5.7082), Wanda (6.7757), and Magnitude Pruning (16.029), with the dense baseline at 5.1168. Under combined 2:4 structured sparsity and 3-bit quantization on Llama-2-7B, Sparse Expansion achieves 7.0757 perplexity versus SparseGPT's 13.076, a gap that widens substantially at higher compression. On out-of-distribution benchmarks (Llama-3.2-1B, 50% sparsity), Sparse Expansion scores 57.713 on ARC-e, 26.962 on ARC-c, 35.807 on Lambada, 87.5 on SciQ, and 28.729 on MMLU, leading all baselines on every task. Across the full Pythia family (70M–12B) at 50% sparsity, Sparse Expansion consistently outperforms all other one-shot pruning methods, approaching dense performance as model size increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sparse Expansion is compared against three one-shot pruning baselines: Magnitude Pruning (Han et al., 2015), Wanda (Sun et al., 2023), and SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023). Sparse Expansion wins against all three baselines across every model size (Pythia 70M–12B, Llama-2 7B/13B/70B), sparsity level, and compression setting tested. Against Magnitude Pruning, the advantage is overwhelming: on Llama-2-7B at 50% sparsity, Sparse Expansion achieves 5.5839 perplexity versus MP's 16.029, and on OOD tasks like Lambada, Sparse Expansion scores 35.807 versus MP's 4.677 on Llama-3.2-1B. Against Wanda, Sparse Expansion leads by 1.19 perplexity points on Llama-2-7B at 50% sparsity (5.5839 vs. 6.7757) and by 2.2 points on ARC-e (57.713 vs. 50.800). Against SparseGPT—the closest competitor and the underlying algorithm for each expert—Sparse Expansion gains 0.12 perplexity points at 50% unstructured sparsity on Llama-2-7B, but the gap widens dramatically under combined sparsity-quantization: at 2:4 sparsity with 3-bit quantization, Sparse Expansion achieves 7.0757 versus SparseGPT's 13.076 on Llama-2-7B, a 6-point improvement. The paper notes that the performance gap between Sparse Expansion and SparseGPT grows with increasing sparsity and quantization aggressiveness, suggesting that input-aware expert specialization becomes increasingly critical under higher compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: mechanistic interpretability (polysemanticity, superposition theory) and post-training model compression (one-shot pruning). It builds directly on SparseGPT as the per-expert sparsification engine and draws on superposition theory (Elhage et al., 2022; Hänni et al., 2024; Adler & Shavit, 2024) to motivate why entangled neurons should be harder to compress. The key departure is repurposing the 1-Wasserstein distance from optimal transport as a per-neuron entanglement metric, connecting it to a new 'mapping difficulty' formulation, and using it to explain differential sensitivity to sparsity—something neither the interpretability nor the compression literature had previously addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a small set of 'Wasserstein neurons' whose sparsification disproportionately degrades reasoning and math performance provides a concrete, measurable target for future entanglement-aware pruning algorithms. The negative result that keeping these neurons dense while over-sparsifying all others does not help (Table A4) rules out a naive solution and points toward the need for input-conditional computation (as in Sparse Expansion) rather than selective preservation. Limitations include the memory overhead of Sparse Expansion (up to 16× expert copies per layer), which the authors acknowledge makes it impractical without further optimization, and the fact that the framework is validated only on FFN linear layers of decoder-only transformers. Open questions include what circuits Wasserstein neurons participate in, whether the phenomenon persists in attention layers, and whether entanglement-aware sparsification can match Sparse Expansion's gains without the memory cost.
