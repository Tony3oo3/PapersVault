## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods rely on CLIP text embeddings as conditional signals, but CLIP is pretrained on static image-text pairs, causing its text representations to encode visual rather than dynamic motion semantics. This domain mismatch limits the alignment between textual descriptions and generated motions, degrading generation fidelity, retrieval accuracy, and captioning quality across all three tasks simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253036/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a natural language text prompt and produces a 3D human motion sequence by iteratively predicting VQ-VAE tokens (6 layers × 512 codes) over K=10 masked-prediction steps with classifier-free guidance (scale 4 on HumanML3D, 2 on KIT-ML). Motion features encode 263-dim (HumanML3D) or 251-dim (KIT-ML) vectors covering root velocity, height, foot contact, and per-joint positions/velocities/rotations from the SMPL model. For captioning, the input is a motion sequence and the output is a free-form text description generated by a LoRA-finetuned OPT-2.7B LLM; for retrieval, the model outputs ranked cross-modal similarity scores between text and motion embeddings.

## Method

LaMP is a language-motion pretraining model inspired by BLIP-2, comprising a motion transformer and a text transformer that share self-attention layers, with learnable query tokens bridging the two modalities via cross-attention. It is jointly trained on four objectives—motion-text contrastive learning, binary matching, motion-grounded text generation, and text-grounded motion generation—using different attention masking strategies per task to extract motion-informative text features and language-informative motion features. For text-to-motion generation (LaMP-T2M), LaMP's text transformer replaces CLIP as the condition encoder, and a decoder-only masked transformer with causal attention performs autoregressive prediction of masked VQ-VAE tokens, avoiding the rank collapse inherent in bidirectional attention matrices. For captioning (LaMP-M2T), language-informative motion features from LaMP's motion transformer are linearly projected and fed into OPT-2.7B finetuned with LoRA, and the resulting model also defines the LaMP-BertScore metric for evaluating semantic alignment of generated motions.

## Results

On HumanML3D, LaMP-T2M achieves FID of 0.032 (28.9% lower than MoMask's 0.045), R-Precision Top-3 of 0.843, and LaMP-BertScore of 60.81, setting new state-of-the-art on FID, Top-2/Top-3 R-Precision, and semantic alignment. On KIT-ML, it reaches FID 0.141 (vs. MoMask 0.204), R-Precision Top-3 of 0.826, and LaMP-BertScore of 57.54. Notably, LaMP-T2M slightly trails MoMask on R-Precision Top-1 on HumanML3D (0.457 vs. 0.521) but surpasses it on all other reported metrics including FID, Top-2, Top-3, MultiModal Distance, and LaMP-BertScore.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest transformer-based competitor MoMask, LaMP-T2M achieves substantially lower FID (0.032 vs. 0.045 on HumanML3D; 0.141 vs. 0.204 on KIT-ML) and better Top-2/Top-3 R-Precision, LaMP-BertScore, and MultiModal Distance, though it trails on Top-1 R-Precision on HumanML3D (0.457 vs. 0.521). Against diffusion-based methods (MDM, MLD, MotionDiffuse, ReMoDiffuse), LaMP-T2M dominates on FID by large margins (e.g., 0.032 vs. 0.473 for MLD on HumanML3D) and matches or exceeds their R-Precision. Against T2M-GPT, improvements are consistent across all metrics (FID 0.032 vs. 0.141; LaMP-BertScore 60.81 vs. 56.21 on HumanML3D). Against the recent ParCo method, LaMP-T2M wins on FID (0.032 vs. 0.109), Top-2/Top-3 R-Precision, and MultiModal Distance, but again loses on Top-1 R-Precision (0.457 vs. 0.515). For motion-text retrieval (Table 3), LaMP-Feat outperforms TMR by ~1–3 points on R@1 across both datasets (e.g., 67.18 vs. 67.16 on HumanML3D; 52.5 vs. 49.25 on KIT-ML). For motion captioning (Table 4), LaMP-M2T achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 (0.547) and MultiModal Distance (2.808), marginally outperforming MotionGPT (0.543, 2.821) and TM2T (0.516, 2.935).

## Relation to Prior Work

LaMP directly adapts the BLIP-2 architecture (frozen encoder + learnable query tokens + cross-attention) from the language-vision domain to language-motion alignment, replacing CLIP's text encoder with a purpose-built text transformer pretrained on motion-text pairs. For the generation component, it follows MoMask's masked-prediction framework (cosine mask scheduling, 80/10/10 remasking) but departs by adopting a decoder-only causal-attention architecture instead of MoMask's bidirectional encoder, motivated by a rank-collapse argument for attention matrices. A key conceptual departure from BLIP-2 is the addition of text-grounded motion generation as a pretraining objective, which BLIP-2 does not include since it only needs image-grounded text generation for LLM consumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a well-motivated case that CLIP's text embeddings, pretrained on static image-text pairs, are a suboptimal conditioning signal for dynamic motion generation, and provides a practical alternative that improves FID by ~29% on HumanML3D. The LaMP-BertScore metric offers a more semantically grounded evaluation than FID alone, and the LaMP evaluator (LaMP-R Precision) shows substantially higher scores than the standard CLIP-based evaluator, suggesting prior methods may be under- or over-estimated. However, the R-Precision Top-1 actually drops on HumanML3D (0.457 vs. MoMask's 0.521) while Top-2/3 improve, a trade-off the paper does not adequately discuss. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer sequences, physically plausible generation, or other motion representations beyond VQ-VAE tokens, and whether the decoder-only mask prediction advantage holds at larger model scales.
