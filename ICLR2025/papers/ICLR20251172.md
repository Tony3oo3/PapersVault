## Problem

Existing certified unlearning methods (Newton Step, Infinitesimal Jackknife) rely on explicit Hessian matrix computation or inversion, incurring O(d³) cost, requiring convexity for Hessian invertibility, and only applying to empirical risk minimizers. These constraints preclude their use on high-dimensional over-parameterized models, non-convex objectives (e.g., deep networks), and non-converged training trajectories. The paper asks how to achieve efficient second-order unlearning without Hessian operations under non-convex and non-convergence conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained model w_{E,B} (from standard SGD), a set of pre-computed per-sample statistics vectors {a_{E,B}^{-u_j}} (each of dimension d, computed via recursive Hessian-vector products during a one-time O(n²d) precomputation phase), and a deletion request specifying a subset U of m samples to forget. The output is an unlearned model ẃ_{E,B}^{-U} = w_{E,B} + a_{E,B}^{-U} + n (where n ~ N(0, σI) is Gaussian noise calibrated to (ε,δ)-certified unlearning), along with the updated statistics set with deleted vectors removed. Critically, the unlearning step itself requires only a single vector addition in O(md) time with no dataset access, achieving millisecond-level execution.

## Method

The core insight is to characterize the discrepancy between the retrained model (trained without the forgotten sample) and the learned model as an affine stochastic recursion over the SGD update trajectory, rather than as a single Newton step at the optimum. For each sample u, the impact vector a_{E,B}^{-u} = Σ_e M_{e,b(u)} · ∇ℓ(w_{e,b(u)}; u) is computed by recursively applying (I − ηH) matrix-vector products backward through all subsequent updates, where HVPs replace explicit Hessian computation. A key additivity property (Theorem 1) ensures that sequential deletion requests are equivalent to batch deletion, enabling online streaming unlearning via simple vector summation. The approach requires no Hessian inversion (removing the convexity assumption), no optimality of the learned model, and achieves an unlearning guarantee of O(mρ^n) that improves over the O(m²/n²) of prior methods under identical regularity conditions.

## Results

On MNIST with 20% deletion (single-sample online requests), the proposed Hessian-Free (HF) method achieves millisecond-level unlearning: 0.0073 s per sample for Logistic Regression (LR, d=7850) and 0.0268 s for CNN (d=21840), compared to 509 s (NS) and 23 s (IJ) for LR, and 5810 s (NS) and 191 s (IJ) for CNN. HF also requires far less storage (0.03 GB for LR, 0.08 GB for CNN vs. 0.23 GB and 1.78 GB for baselines) and achieves higher test accuracy: 87.50% (LR, −0.25 gap vs. retrain) and 91.50% (CNN, −2.25 gap) versus 86.25% (−1.50) for NS/IJ on LR and 83.50%/82.75% (−10.25/−11.00) for NS/IJ on CNN. The theoretical unlearning guarantee improves from O(m²/n²) (NS, IJ) to O(mρⁿ) under the same regularity conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two certified unlearning baselines: Newton Step (NS, Sekhari et al. 2021) and Infinitesimal Jackknife (IJ, Suriyakumar et al. 2022). HF dominates both on every practical metric. In unlearning time, HF is ~93,000× faster than NS and ~3,000× faster than IJ on LR, and ~26,000× faster than NS and ~7,000× faster than IJ on CNN, reducing per-sample deletion to milliseconds. In storage, HF uses 0.03 GB (LR) and 0.08 GB (CNN) versus 0.23 GB and 1.78 GB for both NS and IJ, a 7–22× reduction. In test accuracy after unlearning, HF achieves 87.50% on LR (beating NS/IJ by 1.25 pp) and 91.50% on CNN (beating NS by 8 pp and IJ by 8.75 pp), with a much smaller gap to the retrain baseline (−0.25 and −2.25 vs. −1.50 and −10.25/−11.00). Theoretically, HF improves the unlearning guarantee from O(m²/n²) to O(mρⁿ) and the deletion capacity from O(n²/d^{1/2}) to O((1/ρ)ⁿ/d^{1/2}) under the same Assumption 1, while also removing the convexity and empirical-risk-minimizer requirements that constrain NS and IJ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the certified unlearning line initiated by Sekhari et al. (2021) and Suriyakumar et al. (2022), which approximate retraining via Newton-style updates requiring explicit Hessian computation and inversion under convexity and optimality assumptions. The key departure is replacing Hessian inversion with an affine stochastic recursion over the SGD training trajectory, leveraging the Hessian-vector product (Pearlmutter, 1994) to avoid ever materializing the Hessian. The influence function framework of Koh & Liang (2017) provides the analytical starting point, but this paper generalizes it from the optimal model to arbitrary training steps, and the additivity property (Theorem 1) enables sequential online deletion that prior Hessian-based methods cannot support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves millisecond-level unlearning (orders of magnitude faster than Newton Step or Infinitesimal Jackknife) and removes the convexity/optimality requirements that blocked prior certified unlearning from applying to over-parameterized deep networks. However, the O(n²d) precomputation and O(nd) storage remain significant for large-scale deployments, and the theoretical generalization and deletion capacity results still rely on convexity assumptions even though the unlearning guarantee itself does not. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly large models (the experiments use only ~20K-parameter networks), how to defend against MIA-U attacks without sacrificing utility, and whether non-convex theoretical frameworks can be developed to fully justify the method in deep learning settings.
