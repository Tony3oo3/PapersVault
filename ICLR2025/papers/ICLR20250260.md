## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for LLMs, which optimize layer-by-layer or block-by-block, suffer severe performance degradation at ultra-low bit widths (W4A4, W2A16, W4A8). The paper identifies through Hessian analysis that as model size grows and quantization bits decrease, inter-layer and intra-layer parameter dependencies intensify, making local optimization strategies fundamentally insufficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250260/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CBQ consumes pre-trained LLM weights (OPT-30B/66B, LLAMA1-30B/65B, LLAMA2-7B) and 128 randomly sampled 2048-token calibration segments from C4. It produces fully quantized models using per-channel weight quantization and per-token activation quantization at target bit widths (W4A16, W2A16, W4A8, W4A4, W6A6), with the quantized model directly deployable for inference. The entire quantization of a 4-bit LLAMA1-65B model completes in 4.3 GPU hours on a single GPU.

## Method

CBQ introduces three synergistic mechanisms. First, a Cross-Block Dependency (CBD) scheme uses a sliding window of 2 blocks with 1 overlapping block to jointly optimize quantization step sizes across multiple transformer blocks, capturing inter-layer dependencies that block-wise methods miss. Second, LoRA-Rounding decomposes the AdaRound compensation matrix into two low-rank factors (A₁ ∈ ℝ^{d×r}, A₂ ∈ ℝ^{r×k}, r=5) to model intra-layer dependencies while reducing learnable parameters from d×k to (d+k)×r, with a binary regularizer driving elements toward {0,1}. Third, a Coarse-to-Fine Pre-processing (CFP) strategy uses IQR-based quartile thresholds for initial outlier detection, then fine-grained intra/inter-set variance optimization to precisely separate true outliers from normal values, applying truncation to weight outliers and per-channel equivalent scaling to activation outliers. The total loss combines L2 and KL-divergence reconstruction error with the compensation regularizer, optimized via cosine-annealed learning rates over 3 epochs per window.

## Results

On LLAMA1-65B under W4A4 quantization, CBQ achieves a C4 perplexity of 7.52 and WikiText2 perplexity of 5.89, substantially outperforming OmniQuant (11.28/9.17) and QLLM (8.89/6.87). At W2A16, CBQ reaches 7.42 C4 PPL versus OmniQuant's 7.78, while GPTQ completely fails (8.8e3). On zero-shot accuracy (Table 1), CBQ reduces the gap to full-precision to within ~1% at W4A16 and W4A8 across OPT-30B/66B and LLAMA1-30B/65B, and uniquely maintains usable performance at W4A4 where OmniQuant degrades by 5–9 points on PIQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPTQ (the standard W4A16/W2A16 baseline), CBQ wins decisively at W2A16 where GPTQ collapses (e.g., 56.47 PIQA on LLAMA1-65B vs. CBQ's 78.12; C4 PPL of 8.8e3 vs. 7.42), and maintains a small but consistent edge at W4A16 (81.12 vs. 80.79 PIQA on LLAMA1-65B). Against OmniQuant (the primary block-reconstruction competitor), CBQ outperforms across every setting tested; the gap is most dramatic at W4A4 on LLAMA1-65B, where CBQ achieves 7.52 C4 PPL versus OmniQuant's 11.28 (a 33% reduction) and 77.69 vs. 71.81 PIQA accuracy. Against QLLM at W4A4, CBQ leads by 1.37 C4 PPL (7.52 vs. 8.89) and 4.13 PIQA points (77.69 vs. 73.56) on LLAMA1-65B. Against RPTQ at W4A8, CBQ achieves lower perplexity on OPT-30B (10.86 vs. 11.01 C4) and higher accuracy on most zero-shot tasks. Overall, CBQ is the only method that maintains near-full-precision performance at W4A4, a setting where all baselines degrade substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

CBQ extends the block-wise reconstruction framework popularized by OmniQuant and QLLM by introducing a cross-block dependency (CBD) mechanism with overlapping sliding windows, motivated by a Hessian-based analysis showing that inter-layer and intra-layer dependencies intensify at ultra-low bit widths. The LoRA-Rounding component adapts AdaRound's adaptive rounding matrix via low-rank decomposition, making it tractable for billion-parameter LLMs where the full rounding matrix is infeasible. The coarse-to-fine pre-processing (CFP) unifies outlier handling for both weights and activations using quartile-based statistics, departing from prior methods (SmoothQuant, Outlier Suppression+) that address only one modality or rely on distributional assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CBQ provides a principled, unified PTQ pipeline that achieves competitive W4A4 and W2A16 results while remaining computationally efficient (4.3h for a 65B model), making ultra-low-bit LLM deployment more practical. The Hessian-based motivation for cross-block optimization offers a clearer theoretical grounding than the heuristic block-wise approaches that preceded it. Open questions include whether CBD scales effectively beyond 4–8 blocks without prohibitive memory costs, how the method generalizes to newer architectures (Mamba, MoE), and whether the per-channel/per-token quantization granularity can be further relaxed for hardware friendliness.
