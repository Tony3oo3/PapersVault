## Problem

Policy gradient methods in single-agent RL enjoy iterate convergence, efficient rollout-based value estimation, and avoidance of importance sampling, but it was unknown whether these properties could coexist in two-player zero-sum imperfect-information extensive-form games. Existing sound algorithms for EFGs either rely on counterfactual values (incompatible with policy gradients and requiring importance sampling with high variance) or only guarantee average-iterate convergence (undesirable for neural network parameterization). This paper asks whether a theoretically sound policy gradient method can achieve all three desiderata simultaneously.

## Input / Output

At each iteration, the algorithm consumes a single sampled trajectory (root-to-terminal path) drawn from the current strategy profile, rather than requiring external sampling over ~√|game tree| infosets. The output is a sequence of sequence-form strategy profiles; the best-iterate among them converges to a regularized Nash equilibrium, with exploitability in the original game bounded by O(τ) where τ is the regularization coefficient. The strategy is maintained per-infoset as a local probability distribution over actions, with a γ-soft constraint ensuring every action retains minimum probability for exploration.

## Method

QFR (Q-Function based Regret Minimization) replaces counterfactual values with trajectory Q-values, which are directly estimable from a single rollout without importance sampling since the trajectory already includes the acting player's reach probability. To make the regularization term also estimable from trajectories, the paper introduces a bidilated regularizer that weights each per-infoset regularizer by the reach probability of all players (acting player, opponent, and chance), rather than only the acting player as in the standard dilated regularizer. The update rule is a variant of Regularized Optimistic Mirror Descent (Reg-OMD) applied per-infoset, where the current strategy serves as a prediction for the next iterate. A key technical ingredient is a learning rate schedule requiring ancestor infosets to have smaller learning rates than descendants (η_s^anc/η_s ≤ τC_s^η), ensuring that ancestor strategies change slowly enough for children's payoff estimates to remain stable. The combination yields best-iterate convergence to the regularized NE with O(1/√T) rate under stochastic (single-trajectory) feedback.

## Results

QFR is evaluated on four benchmark games (4-Sided Liar's Dice, Leduc Poker, Kuhn Poker, and 2×2 Abrupt Dark Hex) using exploitability as the metric (lower is better). QFR achieves the lowest exploitability among all compared methods in three of four games, outperforming outcome-sampling CFR, CFR+, BOMD, and MMD across all four games. In Liar's Dice, BFTRL slightly edges out QFR (0.167 vs. 0.174 exploitability), though the gap is small and the game is solvable within 50 iterations under full-information feedback. The results are reported as exploitability curves over 100 repeated runs with different seeds (Figure 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QFR outperforms outcome-sampling CFR (Lanctot et al., 2009) and CFR+ (Brown & Sandholm, 2019) in all four games, attributed to both the bidilated regularization enabling best-iterate convergence and the avoidance of importance sampling used by CFR variants. Against BOMD (Bai et al., 2022), QFR wins in all four games, again benefiting from its regularization and trajectory-Q-value feedback. QFR also outperforms MMD (Sokota et al., 2023) in all four games, with the paper attributing the advantage to QFR's optimistic updates that predict gradients at the next iteration. The only loss is against BFTRL (Fiegel et al., 2023) on Liar's Dice, where BFTRL achieves 0.167 exploitability versus QFR's 0.174; in the other three games QFR beats BFTRL. Notably, MMD has no theoretical convergence guarantee when using trajectory Q-values, whereas QFR provides a provable best-iterate convergence guarantee under the same stochastic feedback setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) CFR-based algorithms for EFGs that guarantee average-iterate convergence but rely on counterfactual values (requiring importance sampling for stochastic feedback), and (2) last-iterate convergence algorithms (OMD, Reg-OMD) that achieve iterate convergence but were previously only proven under full-information or counterfactual-value feedback. QFR departs from both by introducing the bidilated regularizer—a symmetric extension of the dilated regularizer that weights each infoset's regularizer by all players' reach probabilities—enabling trajectory Q-value estimation without importance sampling while preserving the strong convexity needed for convergence. The algorithm's update rule is a direct application of Reg-OMD (Liu et al., 2023) with a novel depth-dependent learning rate schedule that ensures ancestor strategies change slower than descendant strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result showing that the three standard DRL desiderata—rollout-based value estimation, iterate convergence, and avoidance of importance sampling—can coexist in two-player zero-sum EFGs, a combination that had been considered incompatible. The practical implication is that EFG solving can now leverage the same training infrastructure as modern DRL (single trajectory per iteration, no snapshot storage, no auxiliary networks for averaging), potentially enabling deep RL approaches to imperfect-information games. Key limitations include the restriction to two-player zero-sum tabular games, best-iterate (not last-iterate) convergence, and the non-standard requirement that learning rates increase with tree depth. The open question of whether these guarantees and empirical advantages persist under neural network function approximation in large-scale games (e.g., Stratego, full Hold'em) remains the most important next step.
