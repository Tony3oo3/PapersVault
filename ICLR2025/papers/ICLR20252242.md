## Problem

Causal structure identification and latent representation identifiability have developed largely independently, despite both fundamentally relying on exchangeable non-i.i.d. data to overcome the impossibility of identification from i.i.d. samples. The paper asks what precisely enables identifiability in both settings and whether a single framework can unify the key methods across Causal Discovery, ICA, and Causal Representation Learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252242/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes sequences of random variable pairs or vectors drawn from multiple environments (i.e., exchangeable but non-i.i.d. data), where each environment corresponds to a draw from a latent de Finetti parameter. Depending on the sub-problem, it outputs: a unique bivariate causal direction (CD), latent source variables up to element-wise nonlinear transformations (ICA/TCL), or causal representations with their DAG (CRL). A key relaxation is that only one of the cause or mechanism distributions needs to be non-i.i.d. (cause or mechanism variability), rather than both as required by the original Causal de Finetti theorem.

## Method

The paper introduces Identifiable Exchangeable Mechanisms (IEM), a unified probabilistic graphical model with three variable layers (exogenous sources S, causal variables Z, observed variables O) and separate de Finetti parameters θ^s, θ^g, ψ controlling source distributions, structural assignments, and the observation mechanism. CD, ICA, and CRL are recovered as sub-models by omitting layers. The central technical contribution is the cause/mechanism variability duality: Thm. 2 proves that for bivariate CD, it suffices for either the cause prior p(θ) or the mechanism prior p(ψ) to be non-delta (not both), generalizing the Causal de Finetti theorem. Lem. 4 then shows this duality transfers to representation learning, proving that TCL's identifiability (originally requiring exchangeable non-i.i.d. sources with a fixed mixing function) holds equally under i.i.d. sources with a stochastic, environment-dependent mixing function. Lem. 6 further shows that dim(Z)+1 environments suffice for simultaneous identification of sources, causal variables, and the DAG, versus 2·dim(Z)+1 when solving ICA and CRL separately.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical unification paper with no standard benchmark tables or quantitative metrics. The key results are: (1) Thm. 2 proves that cause variability or mechanism variability alone (rather than both) is necessary and sufficient for bivariate causal structure identification, relaxing the Causal de Finetti theorem; (2) Lem. 3 shows TCL's sufficient variability condition is equivalent to cause variability (exchangeable non-i.i.d. sources); (3) Lem. 4 establishes a duality showing mechanism variability (stochastic mixing with i.i.d. sources) yields the same identifiability as cause variability; (4) Lem. 6 shows that (dim Z + 1) interventions suffice for simultaneous identification of exogenous sources, causal variables, and the DAG, versus (2·dim Z + 1) if the two tasks are solved separately. The paper mentions synthetic experiments in Appx. F verifying Thm. 2, but no numerical results are reported in the main text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform empirical comparisons against baselines; instead, it provides a theoretical unification showing that existing identifiability results are special cases of the IEM framework. Specifically, the Causal de Finetti theorem (Guo et al., 2024a) requiring both cause and mechanism to be exchangeable non-i.i.d. is relaxed by Thm. 2 to require only one. TCL (Hyvarinen & Morioka, 2016) is shown to be a special case of cause variability (Lem. 3), and its identifiability is extended to mechanism variability via the duality in Lem. 4. CauCA (Wendong et al., 2023) is subsumed via Lem. 5, which shows its interventional discrepancy condition implies non-delta mechanism priors. The Identifiable Latent Neural Causal Models of Liu et al. (2024) are shown to rely on two separate exchangeability conditions (Lem. 1), and Lem. 6 demonstrates that their combined identifiability can be achieved with fewer environments than solving ICA and CRL independently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Causal de Finetti theorem of Guo et al. (2024a), which established that exchangeable non-i.i.d. data enables unique bivariate causal structure identification under the Independent Causal Mechanisms principle. It also draws on auxiliary-variable ICA methods (TCL, GCL) and Causal Component Analysis (CauCA), showing each is a special case of a single unified probabilistic model (IEM) governed by exchangeable latent parameters. The key departure is the introduction of 'cause variability' and 'mechanism variability' as relaxed, sufficient conditions that replace the requirement for both mechanisms to be exchangeable non-i.i.d., and the demonstration of a duality between these two conditions that bridges the previously separate literatures on causal discovery and representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IEM framework provides a principled answer to the question of what enables identifiability across causal discovery, ICA, and CRL: exchangeable non-i.i.d. data, with the specific relaxation that only one of the cause or mechanism needs to vary. This has practical implications for experimental design (fewer environments may suffice) and suggests new algorithmic directions (e.g., mechanism-variability-based ICA, multi-view ICA connections). Limitations include the bivariate scope of Thm. 2, the primarily theoretical nature with only synthetic validation, and the open question of how to practically quantify and induce the required non-i.i.d. structure. Extending to multivariate cases and connecting to finite-sample guarantees remain important open problems.
