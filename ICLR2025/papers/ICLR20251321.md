## Problem

There is a gap between privately finding first-order stationary points (FOSP) and second-order stationary points (SOSP): the best prior SOSP guarantee (Ganesh et al., 2023) was strictly worse than the FOSP bound, and a recent analysis revealed an error in their saddle-point escape subprocedure that further weakened the guarantee. This paper asks whether privately finding an SOSP is inherently more difficult than finding an FOSP, and shows it is not.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a dataset of n i.i.d. samples in a single pass, drawing fresh mini-batches of unused functions at each iteration; batch sizes for the gradient-difference oracle O₂ are set adaptively per step (proportional to ‖x_t − x_{t−1}‖) rather than fixed. The output is the full set of iterates {x₁, …, x_T}, of which at least one is guaranteed to be an α-SOSP of the population loss F_P, with α = Õ((√d/(nε))^{1/2} + 1/n^{1/3}).

## Method

Building on the SpiderBoost two-oracle framework (O₁ for direct gradient, O₂ for gradient difference), the paper introduces two key modifications: (1) replacing the Gaussian mechanism with the tree mechanism to prevent noise accumulation across iterations, and (2) using adaptive batch sizes for O₂ that scale with ‖x_t − x_{t−1}‖, so sensitivity and variance are controlled without forcing a worst-case analysis. For saddle-point escape, a new subprocedure applies a stochastic power iteration on a Hessian estimate (obtained via O₃/O₄ oracles) to identify the most negative eigen-direction, guaranteeing a function-value decrease of Õ(γ^{3/2}/√ρ) in Õ(M/√(ργ)) steps when the minimum eigenvalue is below −√(ργ). The drift-based scheduling from Ganesh et al. is retained to decide when to re-query O₁, and a counter forces fresh O₁/O₃ queries after τ saddle escapes to maintain privacy via the Gaussian mechanism.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.17) shows that the proposed algorithm finds an α-SOSP with α = Õ(1/n^(1/3) + (√d/(nε))^(1/2)), where n is dataset size, d is dimension, and ε is the privacy parameter. This matches the state-of-the-art bound for privately finding a first-order stationary point (FOSP) from Arora et al. (2023), suggesting that privately finding an SOSP incurs no additional asymptotic cost. In the non-private limit (ε→∞), the bound reduces to Õ(1/n^(1/3)), which matches the known non-private lower bound of Arjevani et al. (2023) in high dimensions.

| Method | Target | α bound | Privacy exponent |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against Ganesh et al. (2023), whose claimed SOSP bound of Õ((√d/(nε))^(3/7)) was shown to be flawed due to an error in their saddle point escape subprocedure (designed for full-batch but applied to minibatches). Tao et al. (2025) identified this issue and proposed a fix yielding the weaker Õ((√d/(nε))^(2/5)) bound. This paper's adaptive batch size and tree mechanism approach recovers the FOSP-matching rate of Õ((√d/(nε))^(1/2)) for SOSP, strictly improving upon both the corrected Ganesh bound and the Tao et al. correction. The non-private term Õ(1/n^(1/3)) is unchanged across all methods and matches the Arjevani et al. (2023) lower bound, so the improvement is entirely in the privacy-dependent term. No empirical baselines are compared, as the paper is purely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SpiderBoost variance-reduction framework (Wang et al., 2019b) and the private SOSP algorithm of Ganesh et al. (2023), which introduced drift-based oracle scheduling to maintain gradient accuracy for second-order stationarity. The key departures are twofold: (1) replacing the Gaussian mechanism with the tree mechanism to avoid noise accumulation over repeated O₂ queries, and (2) introducing adaptive batch sizes proportional to ‖x_t − x_{t−1}‖, a technique novel to the differential privacy optimization setting. The paper also corrects a flaw in Ganesh et al.'s saddle-point escape subprocedure (identified by Tao et al., 2025) by extending the adaptive-batch idea to Hessian estimation and using a stochastic power-iteration argument.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that privately finding an SOSP achieves the same Õ((√d/(nε))^{1/2} + 1/n^{1/3}) rate as the state-of-the-art for FOSP—suggests that second-order stationarity comes at no additional privacy cost, resolving an open question raised by the gap in prior work. The adaptive batch size technique is the first of its kind in DP optimization and may transfer to other private optimization settings. Open questions include whether the non-private 1/n^{1/3} term can be improved in low dimensions, whether tight lower bounds exist for private SOSP, and whether better generalization error bounds (beyond √(d/n)) are achievable for non-convex objectives.
