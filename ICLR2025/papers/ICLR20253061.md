## Problem

Existing audio benchmarks evaluate LALMs on foundational tasks like ASR, acoustic scene classification, and music genre identification, which do not demand the expert-level reasoning and domain-specific knowledge required for true AGI. No prior benchmark simultaneously covers all three major audio domains (speech, sound, music) with tasks requiring complex, deliberate reasoning grounded in audio perception, leaving a critical gap in assessing the advanced cognitive capabilities of audio-language models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253061/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark instance pairs an audio clip (average 10.14 seconds, capped at 40 seconds due to encoder limitations) with a natural-language multiple-choice question (average 9.28 words) and four answer options (average 5.23 words). Audio is sourced exclusively from test or evaluation splits of 13 existing corpora (AudioSet, IEMOCAP, MELD, MusicCaps, GuitarSet, etc.) plus 1,394 synthetically generated clips to prevent training-data contamination. The model must output a single selected option, evaluated via a token-overlap string-matching algorithm that checks all correct-answer tokens are present while excluding tokens unique to distractor options.

## Method

MMAU is constructed through a 7-step expert-driven pipeline: (1) source selection from 13 audio corpora restricted to test/eval splits, (2) task curation filtering 90 candidate tasks down to 27 (16 reasoning, 11 information extraction) spanning speech, sound, and music, (3) expert annotation of QA pairs by domain specialists, (4) independent expert filtering for ambiguity and quality, (5) GPT-4-based option augmentation to increase distractor difficulty, (6) expert review with skill and task tagging, and (7) balanced finalization into 10,000 MCQs split into a 1,000-question test-mini and 9,000-question main test set. Evaluation uses micro-averaged accuracy with randomized option ordering (5 permutations) and a robust string-matching parser. The paper further probes model behavior by replacing audio with Gaussian noise (to test whether models genuinely attend to audio) and by feeding text-only LLMs weak or strong audio captions (to assess whether textual descriptions can bridge the perception gap).

## Results

On the MMAU benchmark (10k multiple-choice questions across speech, sound, and music), the best LALM, Gemini 2.0 Flash, achieves only 59.93% average accuracy on the test set, while the best open-source model, Qwen2-Audio-Instruct, reaches 52.50%. Human evaluators score 82.23% on the test-mini split, revealing a ~22-point gap between state-of-the-art models and human performance. The best cascaded text-only approach (GPT-4o + strong Qwen2-Audio captions) achieves 58.74%, nearly matching the top LALM. Random guessing yields ~26%, confirming the benchmark's difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini 2.0 Flash (59.93%) leads all LALMs, outperforming the best open-source model Qwen2-Audio-Instruct (52.50%) by 7.4 points and the fully open-source GAMA (31.81%) by 28 points. The cascaded GPT-4o + strong captions approach (58.74%) nearly matches Gemini 2.0 Flash, suggesting that high-quality audio descriptions can substitute for native audio processing. Among specialized models, M2UGen (music-focused) surpasses general-purpose models like LTU and GAMA by up to 15% on music tasks, while MuLLaMa leads on sound (44.80%) but lags on speech (16.56%). Larger models show clear gains: SALMONN (13B) improves over LTU (7B) by ~14% on average. Notably, MuLLaMa and SALMONN show minimal performance drop when audio is replaced with Gaussian noise, indicating they rely on language priors rather than true audio perception, whereas GAMA, Qwen2-Instruct, and Gemini Pro v1.5 exhibit significant drops, confirming genuine audio dependence.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMAU draws conceptual inspiration from text/vision benchmarks like MMLU and MMMU that test expert-level knowledge and reasoning, but applies this paradigm to the audio modality where no comparable comprehensive benchmark existed. It departs from prior audio benchmarks (CompA, OpenASQA, AudioBench, AIR-Bench) that either cover only one or two domains, focus on foundational tasks (ASR, classification), or test limited reasoning types (compositional, temporal). The paper positions MMAU as the first benchmark to simultaneously span speech, sound, and music with 27 distinct skills requiring both information extraction and complex reasoning, explicitly filling the gap identified by the AGI taxonomy that current audio evaluations fall short of the '90th percentile of skilled adults' standard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest proprietary model (Gemini 2.0 Flash) achieves only ~60% accuracy versus 82% human performance, exposing a substantial gap in current LALM capabilities. The noise-substitution experiment showing that models like MuLLaMa and SALMONN barely degrade when audio is replaced with Gaussian noise raises fundamental questions about whether these systems truly perceive audio or rely on language priors. Key limitations include the multiple-choice-only format (no open-ended generation), 40-second audio cap, and disjoint skill sets for extraction vs. reasoning, all of which the authors flag for future iterations. The finding that skill-specific performance is more predictive than difficulty-level performance suggests the field needs to target specific cognitive competencies rather than uniformly increasing task complexity.
