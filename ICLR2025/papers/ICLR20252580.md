## Problem

Despite the empirical success of VAEs and diffusion models, their generalization performance remains theoretically underexplored, particularly the shared encoder-generator structure. Previous VAE bounds (e.g., Mbacke et al., 2024) only guarantee generalization for the encoder while ignoring the generator, and existing DM generalization results either rely on strong distributional assumptions or fail to capture how the diffusion time T affects generalization. This paper asks: can we derive a unified, computable, algorithm- and data-dependent bound that accounts for both encoder and generator generalization across VAEs and DMs?

## Input / Output

The framework takes a training dataset S of m i.i.d. samples, a diffusion time T (for DMs), and score-matching model parameters θ, and produces an upper bound on the KL-divergence (or Wasserstein-1 distance) between the true data distribution P_X and the generated distribution G#π. Unusually, the bound is fully estimable from training data alone—no test set is required—enabling grid-search selection of the optimal T or direct incorporation of the generalization terms as regularization during score-matching optimization. The bound decomposes into an encoder term (KL divergence of the encoded posterior to the prior π) and a generator term (conditional mutual information I(X̂;X|Z)), each with distinct scaling behavior in T.

## Method

The core technical contribution is modeling both the encoder E and generator G as randomized mappings (Markov kernels) and applying the Donsker-Varadhan representation with a sub-Gaussian decoupling estimate to derive a unified generalization bound: |gen| ≤ (√2 R/m) Σᵢ √(E[KL(E(Xᵢ)||π)] + I(X̂ᵢ;Xᵢ|Zᵢ)). For VAEs, this yields a tighter Wasserstein-1 bound than prior PAC-Bayes results by directly bounding the generation error without an unnecessary Wasserstein-2 term, and by covering the generator's generalization via the conditional mutual information term. For score-based DMs, the encoder is an affine SDE (variance-preserving or variance-exploding) with closed-form KL to the prior that vanishes as T→∞, while the generator's conditional mutual information is bounded via a Lipschitz-score argument to grow linearly in T, yielding an explicit trade-off: longer diffusion time eliminates encoder overfitting but amplifies generator overfitting. The resulting sample complexity is O(1/√m), improving over the O(m^{-2/5}) rate from random-feature-model analyses.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: Theorem 6.2 provides a computable upper bound on the KL-divergence between the true data distribution and the generated distribution for score-based diffusion models, decomposed into encoder generalization (T₁, T₂) and generator generalization (T₃) terms. Empirically, on the Swiss Roll synthetic dataset, the bound tracks the estimated test-data KL divergence closely, with the optimal diffusion time T falling in the range 0.4–0.6, and the sample complexity confirmed as O(1/√m). On few-shot MNIST and CIFAR-10 (m=16), the estimated bound identifies an optimal T ≈ 0.8, while the test-data KL divergence and log-likelihood fail to reflect the trade-off due to estimation difficulty in high dimensions. For full-dataset training (MNIST m=60000, CIFAR-10 m=50000), the bound estimated solely from training data successfully captures the diffusion-time trade-off visible in log-likelihood (BPD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For VAEs, the paper compares against the PAC-Bayes bound of Mbacke et al. (2024). The proposed bound is strictly tighter: it removes the unnecessary Wasserstein-2 distance term (replacing it with a direct generation-error bound), extends guarantees from a fixed generator to any generator G_θ via the conditional mutual information term I(Ẑᵢ; Xᵢ | Zᵢ), and relaxes the bounded-support assumption to the more flexible sub-Gaussian condition. For diffusion models, the paper compares against Li et al. (2024), which uses a random feature model and achieves O(m^{-2/5}) sample complexity under a 2-mode Gaussian mixture assumption. The proposed information-theoretic bound achieves O(1/√m) sample complexity under only a sub-Gaussian assumption, and is the first to explicitly characterize the trade-off between encoder and generator generalization as a function of diffusion time T — a phenomenon Li et al. (2024) cannot capture. The empirical validation on Swiss Roll, MNIST, and CIFAR-10 confirms the bound is non-vacuous and tracks actual generation quality, whereas direct estimation of KL divergence or log-likelihood on test data fails to reveal the trade-off in the few-shot regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the generalization analysis of VAEs and diffusion models under a single information-theoretic framework that treats both encoder and generator as randomized mappings, building on the decoupling estimate of Xu & Raginsky (2017) and the SDE formulation of Song et al. (2021). It departs from Mbacke et al. (2024), whose PAC-Bayes bounds for VAEs only guarantee encoder generalization for a fixed generator, by introducing a conditional mutual information term I(Ẑ;X|Z) that captures generator generalization. For diffusion models, it extends the time-dependent mapping viewpoint of Huang et al. (2021) and the Fisher-divergence bound of Song et al. (2021) into a generalization setting, going beyond Li et al. (2024) (random feature model) and Franzese et al. (2023) (population-level ELBO decomposition) by deriving algorithm- and data-dependent bounds under sub-Gaussianity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit trade-off on diffusion time T—where encoder generalization vanishes as T→∞ but generator generalization grows linearly in T—challenges the common heuristic that longer diffusion schedules always improve generation quality, and provides the first computable, training-data-only criterion for selecting optimal T. The unified framework also reveals that standard VAE training implicitly regularizes encoder generalization but overlooks generator generalization, suggesting a concrete regularization term for improvement. Limitations include the sub-Gaussian assumption, the difficulty of estimating the conditional mutual information term in practice, and empirical validation restricted to small-scale settings (2D Swiss Roll, few-shot MNIST/CIFAR-10), leaving open whether the bounds remain informative at the scale of modern large-scale diffusion models.
