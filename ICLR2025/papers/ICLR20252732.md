## Problem

AlphaZero/MuZero combine MCTS with learned models of value and/or dynamics, but MCTS was designed for search with true dynamics and does not propagate the epistemic uncertainty inherent in learned model predictions. As a result, A/MZ cannot harness their search for deep exploration in sparse-reward environments, where optimism in the face of uncertainty is essential for sample-efficient discovery of rare rewards.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment state observations along with learned value, reward, and transition models, plus local uncertainty estimators (RND, hash-based visitation counts, or a UBE network head) that provide per-state-action epistemic variance. During search, each tree node stores both a value estimate and an upper bound on its epistemic variance, which are propagated backward via a modified backup. The output is an action selected from the root visitation distribution, with the search objective at each node combining the mean return and a scaled uncertainty term (q_M^β = q_M + βσ_q).

## Method

EMCTS extends MCTS by treating the learned model as a random variable and propagating epistemic uncertainty through the tree alongside value. The key mechanism is a modified backup (Eq. 12) that accumulates discounted reward and value variances along the trajectory, and a node-level upper bound (Eq. 13) that sums standard deviations across backups to handle correlated returns from a single shared model. The search policy is replaced with Epistemic P/UCT (EPUCT), which selects actions by maximizing q_M^β — the mean return plus β times the averaged backup standard deviation — effectively tracking a UCB on Q* (Theorem 1, proven via Chebyshev's inequality and linearity of value in reward). The paper shows that search improves root-level uncertainty estimates analogously to how it improves value estimates, making the UCB tighter and exploration more sample-efficient than an otherwise equivalent method that estimates uncertainty without search.

## Results

On Deep Sea 40×40, E-AZ reaches the goal in 10,539±9,006 steps (94% of 35 seeds succeed), while the AZ baseline achieves 0% success within the training budget. E-MZ reaches the goal in 14,339±6,845 steps (100% of 23 seeds) versus 0% for the MZ baseline. On the harder SUBLEQ Identity Function task (state space ≈16 million), E-AZ finds a correct program in substantially fewer samples than AZ, with the IO-hash uncertainty variant outperforming the full-state hash variant. E-AZ also solves the stochastic-reward Deep Sea 50×50 variant, which baseline A/MZ cannot solve at all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AZ baseline (Silver et al., 2018), E-AZ achieves a dramatic improvement: AZ never reaches the goal on Deep Sea 40×40 (0% of 20 seeds), while E-AZ succeeds in 94% of seeds with roughly half the steps of the A/MZ+UBE ablation. On SUBLEQ, E-AZ solves the harder Identity Function task in far fewer samples than AZ, confirming the benefit of UCB-based deep exploration. Against the MZ baseline (Schrittwieser et al., 2020), E-MZ achieves 100% success on Deep Sea 40×40 (14,339±6,845 steps) versus 0% for MZ, and the advantage is retained even with the learned transition model. Against Bootstrapped DQN (Osband et al., 2018), a model-free ensemble-based deep exploration method, E-A/MZ match the sub-exponential scaling behavior but additionally benefit from search-based uncertainty estimation, outperforming the non-search A/MZ+UBE ablation by roughly 2× in steps-to-goal. The key finding is that search for uncertainty estimation (EMCTS) provides a consistent ~2× improvement over the equivalent method that applies UCB only at the root without propagating uncertainty through the tree.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AlphaZero/MuZero (A/MZ) family by modifying the MCTS component to propagate epistemic uncertainty from learned value, reward, and transition models through the search tree, creating Epistemic MCTS (EMCTS). It builds directly on PUCT (Rosin, 2011) by replacing the value term with an uncertainty-augmented q_M^β, and on the Uncertainty Bellman Equation (O'Donoghue et al., 2018) for value-uncertainty estimation, while grounding the exploration objective in a formal UCB theorem (Theorem 1) that prior MCTS-for-exploration work (Dreamer, Henaff, Sekar) lacked. The key conceptual departure from standard MCTS is that the search tree now tracks and propagates variance alongside value, enabling the agent to distinguish well-supported predictions from generalizations during planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EMCTS makes the A/MZ framework applicable to sparse-reward and hard-exploration environments (algorithm design, code generation) where baseline A/MZ fails entirely, with practical implications given AZ's demonstrated real-world impact in discovering faster matrix multiplication and sorting algorithms. The finding that search improves epistemic uncertainty estimates at the root—not just value estimates—provides a principled reason why search-based uncertainty estimation outperforms point-estimate approaches, opening the door to using EMCTS for offline RL and off-policy target generation via Reanalyze. Open questions include the full extension to learned transition models (MZ) where the value is non-linear in dynamics and the UCB guarantee breaks, the independence assumption in variance propagation for DNN-based estimators, and scalability to truly large continuous state spaces.
