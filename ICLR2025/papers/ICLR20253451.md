## Problem

Aligning pre-trained diffusion models with user preferences at inference time has been limited to either retraining the model (RL, DPO) or requiring a differentiable reward function (classifier guidance, backpropagation through the denoising chain). This excludes important non-differentiable reward sources such as third-party VLM APIs and interactive human judgements, and makes it impractical to switch preferences without costly retraining. The paper asks whether one can guide the denoising process toward higher-reward outputs at inference time, without backpropagation and without modifying model weights.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and an arbitrary reward function r (mapping a generated image to a scalar score) as input, and produces a single generated image whose reward is higher than standard sampling. The reward function need not be differentiable; it can be a VLM API call, a human click, or a learned scorer like LAION Aes, PickScore, ImageReward, or HPSv2. The method is plug-and-play on existing latent diffusion models (SD v1.4, SDXL) and operates entirely at inference time, with computational cost scaling linearly in the number of noise candidates K and denoising steps T.

## Method

At each reverse-time SDE denoising step, Demon samples K i.i.d. Gaussian noises, uses each to compute a candidate next state via Heun's 2nd-order SDE solver, and estimates the reward of each candidate by running a PF-ODE (or a 1-step Consistency Model for speed) to obtain a clean image, then scoring it with r. The key theoretical result (Lemma 1) shows that this ODE-based reward estimate r∘c approximates the true SDE reward expectation r_β, with error governed by an Itô integral that vanishes as the noise parameter β→0 or when the reward is approximately harmonic. Two weighting schemes then combine the K noises into a single 'optimal' noise z*: Tanh Demon assigns weights proportional to tanh((r_k − μ̂)/τ), effectively flipping low-reward noises and amplifying high-reward ones, while Boltzmann Demon uses a softmax over rewards. The weighted sum is projected onto the √N-radius sphere (justified by concentration of Gaussian norms in high dimensions) to yield a valid Gaussian noise for the actual denoising step. Lemma 2 proves that Tanh Demon strictly improves the reward at every step with probability 1 under the r_β ≡ r∘c assumption, and Lemma 3 provides an expected-improvement guarantee for Boltzmann Demon.

## Results

On SD v1.4 with 45 animal prompts, Tanh+Aes achieves a LAION aesthetics score of 7.35±0.40, substantially exceeding the SD v1.4 baseline (5.34±0.56), Best-of-N (6.32±0.34), and DOODL+Aes (5.59±0.29). The Tanh+Ensemble variant (combining Aes, IR, Pick, and HPSv2) achieves 6.53 Aes, 1.81 IR, 0.236 Pick, and 0.356 HPSv2, outperforming Best-of-N on all four metrics simultaneously. On SDXL, Tanh Demon reaches an aesthetics score of 8.18±0.35 (β=0.1, K=32, T=64), well above the Best-of-N random sampling upper bound of ~7.0 for SDXL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DOODL (Wallace et al., 2023b), Tanh Demon wins decisively on every metric: Tanh+Aes scores 7.35 vs. DOODL+Aes's 5.59 on Aes, and Tanh+Pick scores 0.245 vs. DOODL+Pick's 0.204 on PickScore, while also being ~3.7× faster (18 min vs. 1.1 hr for the Pick variant). Against DPO (Wallace et al., 2023a), Tanh+Aes improves Aes from 5.36 to 7.35 and HPSv2 from 0.229 to 0.257, with the Ensemble variant further lifting HPSv2 to 0.356. Against Universal Guidance / Uni (Bansal et al., 2024), Tanh Demon is superior across all four metrics (e.g., 7.35 vs. 4.11 Aes, 0.356 vs. 0.173 HPSv2 for Ensemble), and Uni actually degrades below the unguided baseline on Aes and IR. Compared to Best-of-N under the same 18-minute budget, Tanh+Ensemble outperforms on all four metrics (6.53 vs. 6.32 Aes, 1.81 vs. 1.69 IR, 0.236 vs. 0.218 Pick, 0.356 vs. 0.291 HPSv2), demonstrating that guided sampling produces a single image better than the best of many random samples. The paper also notes that Tanh Demon is more resistant to reward hacking than DOODL, as it does not require backpropagation through the reward function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the EDM SDE framework (Karras et al., 2022) for the denoising process and the PF-ODE deterministic mapping (Song et al., 2021) to estimate rewards of noisy intermediate states, with Consistency Models (Luo et al., 2023) providing a fast approximation. It departs from the two dominant paradigms for diffusion alignment—training-based RL/fine-tuning (DPOK, DDPO, DPO) and inference-time gradient guidance (DOODL, Universal Guidance)—by introducing a stochastic optimization approach that selects and weights noise samples based on reward estimates, requiring neither backpropagation through the reward nor model retraining. The key conceptual departure is treating noise selection as a combinatorial search problem (analogous to Maxwell's Demon) rather than a differentiable optimization problem, which unlocks non-differentiable reward sources like VLM APIs and human judgements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first inference-time, backpropagation-free preference alignment method for diffusion models, opening a new class of applications where reward signals are inherently non-differentiable (VLM-based scoring, interactive human selection). The plug-and-play nature means any existing pretrained diffusion model can be aligned without fine-tuning, reducing the barrier to preference adaptation. Key limitations include significant computational overhead (18+ minutes per image vs. 5 seconds baseline), sensitivity to the quality of the r∘c reward estimator, and theoretical guarantees that rely on strong assumptions (harmonic rewards, negligible truncation error). Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation, how to handle multi-objective conflicts more gracefully, and whether the β parameter can be made fully adaptive rather than requiring manual tuning.
