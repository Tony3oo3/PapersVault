## Problem

Prior unified visual language models face a fundamental trade-off: VQGAN-based discrete tokens enable autoregressive generation but lack semantic alignment with text, severely degrading understanding performance, while CLIP-based quantized tokens preserve semantics but require an external diffusion model for generation. No single end-to-end autoregressive framework had achieved competitive performance on both visual understanding and generation without additional components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253375/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Visual inputs (images at 256×256 or 384×384, or multi-frame video clips) are converted into discrete token grids of shape 16×16×4 or 27×27×16 via residual vector quantization, then concatenated with text tokens into a unified sequence delimited by special tokens (<image_start>, <video_start>, etc.). The model produces text responses for understanding tasks and decodes autoregressively generated visual tokens back into images or videos through the vision tower's decoder, using classifier-free guidance (CFG=3) for generation.

## Method

VILA-U introduces a unified foundation vision tower that combines a SigLIP vision encoder with residual vector quantization (codebook size 16384, depth D=4 or 16) and a reconstruction decoder, trained jointly with text-image contrastive loss and VQ reconstruction loss while initialized from CLIP weights (text encoder frozen). This dual-objective training endows the discrete visual tokens with both semantic alignment for understanding and reconstruction fidelity for generation. The LLM (LLaMA-2-7B) is trained with a single next-token prediction objective over the interleaved text-visual token sequence; at each visual position, a depth transformer autoregressively predicts the D residual codes conditioned on the LLM's code embedding. The key insight is that introducing text alignment during vision tower pretraining (rather than relying solely on reconstruction) is critical for competitive understanding, and that autoregressive generation on a sufficiently high-quality 15M-image corpus can approach diffusion-model quality without any external generation module.

## Results

On image understanding (384 resolution), VILA-U achieves 79.4 on VQAv2, 60.8 on GQA, 60.8 on TextVQA, 85.8 on POPE, 1401.8 on MME, 59.0 on SEED, and 33.5 on MM-Vet, approaching continuous-token VLMs like VILA (79.9/62.3/64.4) while using discrete visual tokens. On image generation (MJHQ-30K), VILA-U at 384 resolution achieves FID 7.69, outperforming all autoregressive baselines (LWM: 17.77, Show-o: 15.18) and even surpassing SD-XL (9.55) with only 15M training images. On video understanding (384), VILA-U scores 75.3 on MSVD-QA and 60.0 on MSRVTT-QA, matching or exceeding Video-LaVIT and Video-LLava. On video generation (VBench), VILA-U achieves a total score of 74.01, surpassing CogVideo (67.01) but trailing CogVideoX (81.61) and Open-Sora (75.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On image understanding, VILA-U (384) narrows the gap to continuous-token VLMs dramatically: it trails VILA by only 0.5 on VQAv2 (79.4 vs 79.9) and 1.5 on GQA (60.8 vs 62.3), while surpassing LLaVA-1.5 on TextVQA (60.8 vs 58.2), SEED (59.0 vs 58.6), and MM-Vet (33.5 vs 30.5). Among discrete-token methods, VILA-U dominates all baselines—outperforming LWM by 23.6 points on VQAv2 (79.4 vs 55.8), Show-o by 20.1 (79.4 vs 59.3), and SEED-LLaMA by 13.2 (79.4 vs 66.2). On image generation (MJHQ-30K FID), VILA-U at 384 (7.69) beats all autoregressive methods (LWM 17.77, Show-o 15.18) and even SD-XL (9.55) with 133× less training data, though it trails PixArt (6.14) and Playground v2.5 (4.48). On video understanding, VILA-U (384) leads all discrete-token baselines and matches Video-LaVIT on MSVD-QA (75.3 vs 73.5) and MSRVTT-QA (60.0 vs 59.5), but falls short on TGIF-QA (51.9 vs 70.0 for Video-LLava). On video generation (VBench), VILA-U (74.01) surpasses CogVideo (67.01) but is outperformed by both Open-Sora (75.91) and CogVideoX (81.61). The RQ-VAE vision tower comparison (Table 8) shows the unified tower's contrastive loss costs ~1.2 FID in generation quality (13.2 vs 12.0) but is essential for understanding, where removing contrastive loss drops VQAv2 from 75.3 to 57.7.

## Relation to Prior Work

VILA-U builds directly on RQ-VAE (Lee et al., 2022) for its residual quantization scheme, depth transformer, and decoder architecture, and on SigLIP/CLIP for vision encoder initialization. It departs from two prior lines of unified VLMs: VQGAN-based autoregressive models (LWM, Show-o) that suffer from poor text alignment in their visual tokens, and CLIP-token + external diffusion models (SEED-X, SEED-LLaMA) that require a separate generation stack. The key architectural departure is the unified foundation vision tower trained jointly with contrastive and reconstruction losses, which produces discrete tokens that are simultaneously semantically aligned with text and reconstructable, enabling a single next-token prediction objective for both understanding and generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully autoregressive, single-objective framework can achieve near-competitive performance on both visual understanding and generation without external diffusion models, simplifying deployment and training infrastructure. However, the authors note no significant synergy between understanding and generation tasks, and the contrastive loss slightly degrades reconstruction quality (rFID 1.80 vs. 1.30 for pure RQ-VAE), leaving open the question of whether these two capabilities can be made to mutually reinforce each other. The finding that 15M high-quality images suffice for competitive autoregressive generation (FID 7.69 vs. SD-XL's 9.55 on 2B images) suggests data quality may matter more than scale for this paradigm, but the absolute generation quality still trails the best diffusion models.
