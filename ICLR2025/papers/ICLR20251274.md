## Problem

Prior contrastive pre-training methods for 3D hand pose estimation (SimCLR, PeCLR) generate positive pairs solely from data-augmented views of a single image, and TempCLR relies on temporally adjacent frames, both limiting the diversity of backgrounds, hand appearances, and object interactions seen during training. Meanwhile, large-scale in-the-wild hand videos (e.g., 3,670 hours of Ego4D, 131 days of 100DOH) remain largely untapped for pre-training, with prior works using only 32K–86K images versus the 2.0M collected here.

## Input / Output

The pre-training stage consumes 128×128 RGB hand crops (2.0M total from Ego4D and 100DOH) along with their MediaPipe 2D keypoints (21 joints, PCA-reduced to 14 dimensions) for similarity mining. The fine-tuning stage takes a single RGB hand image and regresses 21 3D hand joint positions via a DetNet-style architecture with 2D heatmap and 3D localization heads, evaluated by MPJPE (mm) and PCK-AUC.

## Method

SiMHand mines cross-video positive pairs by embedding each hand crop's 2D keypoints into a 14-dim PCA space and retrieving the nearest neighbor from a *different* video, ensuring diverse backgrounds and interactions in each positive pair. A parameter-free adaptive weighting scheme then scales the NT-Xent contrastive loss: positive-pair and negative-pair weights are computed as linear functions of the Euclidean distance between the pair's 2D keypoint embeddings within the mini-batch, so more similar pairs receive higher weight. The encoder (ResNet-50) and projection head are trained with geometric augmentations (scale, rotation, translation, color jitter) and PeCLR-style inverse-transformation correction to align features back to the original image geometry. At fine-tuning, the projection head is discarded and the encoder is plugged into a DetNet regressor trained with a multi-task loss combining heatmap, location, delta, and L2 regularization terms.

## Results

SiMHand achieves the best 3D hand pose estimation accuracy across all three evaluation datasets when pre-trained on Ego&Exo-2M (2.0M images): MPJPE of 15.79 mm and PCK-AUC of 90.04% on FreiHand, 16.71 mm and 88.86% on DexYCB, and 18.23 mm and 86.90% on AssemblyHands. Compared to the no-pre-training baseline (19.21 mm on FreiHand), SiMHand reduces MPJPE by 17.7%. At the Ego-1M pre-training scale, SiMHand improves over PeCLR by 15.31% on FreiHand, 10.53% on DexYCB, and 4.90% on AssemblyHands.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiMHand consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and pre-training scale. Against SimCLR (which uses only self-positives from data augmentation), SiMHand achieves substantially lower MPJPE—e.g., 15.79 vs. 20.07 on FreiHand at Ego&Exo-2M—while SimCLR actually underperforms the no-pre-training baseline, suggesting that pre-training without geometric priors is counterproductive for hand pose. Against PeCLR (the prior state-of-the-art using pose-equivariant contrastive learning), SiMHand reduces MPJPE by 13.19% on FreiHand, 7.4% on DexYCB, and 3.4% on AssemblyHands at the full 2M scale, with even larger margins (15.31%, 10.53%, 4.90%) at the 1M scale. Against TempCLR (which uses temporally adjacent frames as positives), SiMHand achieves 35.32 vs. 45.17 MPJPE on FreiHand* at Ego-50K and 31.06 vs. 44.54 at Ego-100K, confirming that cross-video similar-hand mining provides far more diverse and informative positive pairs than temporal adjacency.

## Relation to Prior Work

SiMHand extends the contrastive learning lineage for 3D hand pose pre-training from SimCLR's self-positives (augmented views of a single image) and PeCLR's pose-equivariant self-positives to non-self-positives mined across different videos via 2D keypoint similarity. This connects to the broader non-self-positives literature (Zhang et al. 2022 for action recognition, Jie et al. 2024 for multi-view) but is the first to apply keypoint-based similarity mining at 2M-image scale for hand pose. The adaptive weighting mechanism draws on the pairwise-weight idea in Jie et al. (2024) but is parameter-free and computed from inter-sample distance within the mini-batch rather than from multi-view correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2M-image pre-training set (two orders of magnitude larger than prior work) and the 15% MPJPE reduction on FreiHand establish that scale and cross-video diversity are critical for hand pose pre-training, not just geometric augmentation. The finding that SimCLR pre-training can be worse than random initialization for hand pose, and that matching ego/exo viewpoints does not consistently help, challenges the assumption that more pre-training data or view-matched data always transfers better. Open questions include whether the MediaPipe-based mining bottleneck limits further scaling, whether the approach generalizes to other articulated pose tasks (body, face), and whether the PCA dimensionality (D=14) is optimal or task-dependent.
