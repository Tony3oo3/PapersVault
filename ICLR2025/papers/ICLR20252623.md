## Problem

Existing LoRA merging methods rely on element-wise parameter fusion, which disrupts the internal semantic structure of LoRA and causes parameter interference from both MSU misalignment and knowledge conflict, leading to significant performance degradation. No prior approach exploits the fine-grained rank-level modularity of LoRA to enable flexible, interference-free composition of multiple adapters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252623/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts p task-specific LoRA adapters (each defined by A ∈ R^{r_i × d} and B ∈ R^{d × r_i} matrices per layer, where ranks r_i may differ across adapters) and a single hyperparameter k specifying the target rank of the merged LoRA. It outputs a unified LoRA with rank k (where 1 ≤ k ≤ Σr_i), constructed from k centroid MSUs. Notably, the method handles heterogeneous LoRAs with different ranks natively, and can also be applied to a single LoRA for parameter pruning by setting k < r.

## Method

LoRA-LEGO treats each rank in a LoRA as a Minimal Semantic Unit (MSU), defined as the paired row of A and column of B, and proves two key properties: permutation invariance (reordering MSUs does not change output) and concatenation-summation equivalence (summing LoRA outputs equals the output of a concatenated higher-rank LoRA). The framework proceeds in three steps: (1) disassemble all candidate LoRAs into a pool of MSUs, (2) apply K-means clustering to partition the pool into k clusters, and (3) reconstruct the merged LoRA using each cluster centroid as one rank. A dual reweighting strategy addresses scale distortion: parameter reweighting rescales each centroid to match the average infinity norm of its cluster members (compensating for norm decay from averaging), and output reweighting multiplies the merged LoRA output by √r/√k to maintain variance consistency with the original rank-r LoRA, as guaranteed by a formal theorem on product matrix variance.

## Results

On the multi-task GLUE benchmark (7 tasks + 2 OOD tasks), LoRA-LEGO achieves an average score of 62.21 with Llama2-7b and 70.27 with Llama2-13b, outperforming the strongest baseline (Ties-Merging) by 3.9 and 0.7 points respectively. On highly diverse tasks (Table 6), LoRA-LEGO reaches 59.53 average, surpassing the best baseline (DELLA-Merging) by 5.9 points. In the extreme 20-task merging scenario (Table 7), LoRA-LEGO achieves 55.73 average versus 48.03 for DELLA-Merging, while element-wise methods (Weight Average, Ties-Merging, DARE) collapse to near-zero performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA-LEGO consistently outperforms all five baselines across both model sizes. Against Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022), LoRA-LEGO gains 7.4 points on Llama2-7b (62.21 vs. 54.77) and 1.2 points on Llama2-13b (70.27 vs. 69.03). Against Ties-Merging (Yadav et al., 2024), the advantage is 3.9 points on 7b and 0.7 points on 13b, with the gap narrowing as model scale increases. The largest margins are over DARE (Yu et al., 2024), where LoRA-LEGO leads by 15.0 points on 7b and 20.3 points on 13b, and over DELLA-Merging (Deep et al., 2024), with gains of 10.3 and 3.2 points respectively. In the mixed-task retrieval setting (Table 4), LoRA-LEGO surpasses the LoraRetriever-based Selection baseline (Zhao et al., 2024) by 8.4 points on IID (51.4 vs. 43.0) and 9.4 points on OOD (51.0 vs. 41.6) with Llama2-7b, demonstrating that MSU-level merging extracts more cross-task transfer than simply selecting the top-1 retrieved adapter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA's rank decomposition structure (Hu et al., 2021), reinterpreting each rank as an independent Minimal Semantic Unit (MSU) with permutation invariance and concatenation-summation equivalence. It departs from the dominant element-wise merging paradigm (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, DARE, DELLA-Merging) by operating at the rank level via K-means clustering, positioning itself conceptually between model merging (parameter fusion) and model ensemble (output averaging). The linear mode connectivity literature (Ainsworth et al., 2022; Entezari et al., 2021) provides the theoretical backdrop, but this paper applies connectivity reasoning specifically to MSU alignment rather than full-layer permutation symmetries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MSU framework offers a clean, interpretable lens for understanding why naive LoRA merging fails (parameter misalignment and knowledge conflict), and the clustering-based reconstruction is a simple, training-free procedure that practitioners can adopt immediately. The dual reweighting strategy (norm compensation + variance normalization) is a practical detail that stabilizes performance across rank choices, reducing the method to a single hyperparameter (cluster count k). Open questions include whether the Euclidean-distance K-means assumption holds for very heterogeneous LoRAs, whether the approach extends to other PEFT methods (adapters, prefix tuning), and whether more sophisticated distance metrics (optimal transport, as the authors suggest) would further improve alignment. The moderate but consistent gains over strong baselines like DELLA-Merging, combined with the ability to merge heterogeneous-rank LoRAs and perform LoRA pruning, make this a useful addition to the model-merging toolkit.
