## Problem

Spatial localization (estimating camera pose from an image) and novel view synthesis (predicting what a new viewpoint looks like) have traditionally been treated as separate tasks requiring distinct models, despite being deeply intertwined in human spatial cognition. This paper argues that the interconnection between these two tasks is underexploited and that a unified auto-regressive framework can improve both simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251728/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single observed RGB image (center-cropped to a fixed resolution) as the conditioning input, along with a task category token and either a target camera pose (encoded as a dense Plücker-coordinate camera map at the same spatial resolution as the image) or a target view image. It auto-regressively generates the missing modality: a novel view image given a camera pose, or a relative camera pose given a target view. Both the image and camera map are tokenized into sequences of equal length, so the model processes them as a single interleaved token stream.

## Method

GST introduces the camera as a first-class modality in an auto-regressive model by representing it as a dense Plücker-coordinate camera map and tokenizing it with a lightweight VQVAE (22M params, no discriminator), producing tokens of the same length as the VQGAN image tokens (77M params). A 1.4B-parameter decoder-only transformer (initialized from LlamaGen, with 2D RoPE, QK-Norm, and RMSNorm) is trained via next-token prediction on the concatenated token sequence of the observation, target image, and target camera. The key training insight is modeling the joint distribution p(i,c|o) rather than alternating between the two conditional objectives p(i|c,o) and p(c|i,o); this joint formulation yields more stable loss and gradient trajectories while simultaneously improving both novel view synthesis and relative pose estimation. The joint distribution also naturally supports additional sampling modes, such as drawing valid camera poses from p(c|o) or generating unconditional images from p(i|o), without architectural changes.

## Results

On the Objaverse novel view synthesis benchmark, GST achieves LPIPS 0.085 and SSIM 0.871, outperforming Zero-1-to-3 (LPIPS 0.135, SSIM 0.845) and Zero-1-to-3 XL (LPIPS 0.141, SSIM 0.834), though with a slightly lower PSNR (13.95 vs. 14.77). On CO3D, GST significantly outperforms Zero-1-to-3 NVS across all metrics (LPIPS 0.419 vs. 0.515, PSNR 15.6 vs. 13.4, SSIM 0.456 vs. 0.407). For relative camera pose estimation on CO3D, GST achieves 85.1% rotation accuracy @15° on unseen categories, surpassing Ray Diffusion (83.5%) and all other baselines, though trailing Ray Diffusion on seen categories (86.6% vs. 91.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For novel view synthesis on Objaverse, GST beats Zero-1-to-3 and Zero-1-to-3 XL on perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.085 vs. 0.135/0.141) and structural similarity (SSIM 0.871 vs. 0.845/0.834), but trails on PSNR (13.95 vs. 14.77/14.53), which the authors attribute to Zero-1-to-3 being trained on the full Objaverse dataset including the test set. On CO3D, GST dominates Zero-1-to-3 NVS across all three metrics (LPIPS 0.419 vs. 0.515, PSNR 15.6 vs. 13.4, SSIM 0.456 vs. 0.407), demonstrating the advantage of the Plücker-based camera conditioning. For camera pose estimation, GST surpasses RelPose (56.0/48.6%), PoseDiffusion (75.7/63.2%), and RelPose++ (81.8/69.8%) on both seen and unseen categories. Against Ray Diffusion, the strongest pose baseline, GST achieves better generalization on unseen categories (85.1% vs. 83.5%) but lags on seen categories (86.6% vs. 91.8%), which the authors explain by the use of a subsampled CO3D training set for efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

GST builds on the auto-regressive image generation paradigm established by LlamaGen (VQGAN tokenization + decoder-only transformer) and the Plücker-coordinate camera representation introduced by Ray Diffusion (Zhang et al., 2024). It departs from prior work by treating the camera as a first-class modality alongside images—tokenized via a VQVAE analog—rather than as a conditioning signal (as in Zero-1-to-3) or a separate diffusion target (as in Ray Diffusion). The key conceptual departure is modeling the joint distribution p(i,c|o) instead of the two marginal/conditional distributions p(i|c,o) and p(c|i,o) that prior methods optimize separately, unifying novel view synthesis and relative pose estimation in a single auto-regressive sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that joint-distribution training is both more stable and yields better results than alternating between two separate objectives provides a practical recipe for multi-task auto-regressive training that could generalize beyond this specific pairing. The camera-sampling results (Figure 7) suggest the model acquires implicit spatial reasoning—avoiding obstacles and respecting scene geometry—without explicit 3D supervision, hinting at a path toward spatially-aware generative models. Limitations include single-view synthesis only (no multi-view or 3D consistency), a PSNR gap versus Zero-1-to-3 on Objaverse, and evaluation on relatively small object-centric datasets; scaling to real-world multi-view scenes and integrating with 3D representations remain open.
