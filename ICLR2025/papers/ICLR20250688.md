## Problem

Existing hidden state matching losses for language model distillation (cosine loss in DistilBERT, linear projection in TinyBERT) require the student to share the teacher's hidden state dimensionality, capping compression ratios at roughly 2×. This is a severe limitation for modern LLMs where reducing the hidden dimension D (which drives O(D²) per-layer cost) is the most effective way to shrink inference memory. The paper asks whether a dimension-agnostic similarity measure can replace these losses to enable much higher compression ratios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen teacher transformer (BART-large 24×1024, mBART-large 24×1024, Flan-T5-3B 48×2048, or BERT-base 12×768), an unsupervised pretraining corpus (mC4 or C4), and a supervised task dataset (CNN/XSum, WMT, IWSLT, or GLUE). It outputs a student transformer with strictly smaller hidden state dimensionality (e.g., 6×640, 12×384, 12×768) that is initialized from random weights rather than copied teacher layers, since the dimension mismatch precludes weight transfer. The student is trained in two stages—pretraining distillation followed by supervised distillation or fine-tuning—and is evaluated on the same downstream benchmarks as the teacher.

## Method

The core contribution is a hidden state matching loss based on Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) with a linear kernel, defined as L_H = 1 − ‖Σ_TS‖_F / (‖Σ_TT‖_F · ‖Σ_SS‖_F)^{1/2}, where Σ are centered covariance matrices of teacher and student hidden states. Because CKA is invariant to orthogonal transforms and isotropic scaling, it naturally compares representations of different dimensionality without any projection layer. To make the loss tractable during training, the authors compute a minibatch CKA by summing covariance matrices over B consecutive mini-batches before forming the ratio. The total training objective is L_CLM + L_Dist + L_H (causal language modeling + KL divergence + CKA hidden loss), with CKA applied between every student layer and uniformly spaced teacher layers to accommodate shallower students. This replaces the cosine or linear-projection losses used by DistilBERT and TinyBERT, and unlike those methods, it works from random initialization and scales to compression ratios up to ~20× (Flan-T5-3B → 145M student).

## Results

On BART summarization at 5.5× compression (80M params), CKA-B achieves ROUGE-2 of 16.8 on CNN and 15.0 on XSum, improving +1.7 and +1.5 over the no-hidden-loss baseline, while the linear projection baseline degrades by −0.3 and −0.8. On mBART machine translation at 5× compression (122M params), CKA yields 26.3 BLEU on EN→RO and 39.2 on EN→FR, a gain of +9.9 and +4.7 over the no-hidden-loss baseline. On GLUE with BERT-base as teacher, CKABERT(12×512) at 1.6× compression (66.5M) reaches 88.5 on MNLI-m and 50.2 on COLA, outperforming both DistilBERT(6×768) and MiniLM(6×768) of similar size. For Flan-T5-3B distillation at 19.7× compression (145M), the student achieves 27.2 BLEU on EN→ES, only 0.8 below the 3B teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the linear projection baseline (Jiao et al., 2020), CKA consistently wins at every compression ratio tested, with the gap widening as compression increases: at 5.5× for BART, CKA gains +1.7 ROUGE-2 while linear projection loses −0.3; at 5× for mBART, CKA gains +9.9 BLEU on EN→RO while linear projection drops to 8.8 (below the no-hidden-loss baseline of 18.7). For Flan-T5-3B distillation, the linear projection baseline fails to converge entirely regardless of pretraining, whereas CKA produces usable students down to 145M parameters. Against distilBART/distil-mBART (Shleifer & Rush, 2020), CKA students at comparable compression ratios substantially outperform: CKA mB(24×640) at 2.0× achieves 24.6 BLEU on EN→RO versus 15.5 for dmB(2×1024) at 2.1×, and CKA BART students at 1.8× beat distilBART at 2× on all ROUGE metrics. Against DistilBERT (Sanh et al., 2019) and MiniLM (Wang et al., 2020) on GLUE, CKABERT(12×512) at 1.6× (66.5M) surpasses both on MNLI-m (88.5 vs. 82.6 and 83.0) and COLA (50.2 vs. 49.0 and 49.2), though MiniLM retains a slight edge on MRPC (88.4 vs. 87.8) and STS-B (85.0 vs. 84.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Centered Kernel Alignment (Kornblith et al., 2019), originally a representation-similarity diagnostic, as a trainable distillation loss for matching hidden states of different dimensionality. It departs from the cosine-loss paradigm of DistilBERT and Shleifer & Rush (2020), which restricts students to the teacher's hidden dimension, and from the linear-projection approach of Jiao et al. (2020), which the authors show degrades beyond ~3× compression. The two-stage pretraining-then-supervised distillation framework follows Shleifer & Rush (2020), but replaces expensive teacher beam-search label generation with a single unsupervised pretraining pass, making data augmentation feasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling hidden-dimension reduction (not just layer pruning), the method unlocks compression ratios of 5–20× for encoder-decoder models while remaining competitive with same-size distilBART/distil-mBART baselines, and it works from random initialization without a pretrained student. The practical impact is significant for deploying LLMs on resource-constrained devices, and the avoidance of teacher decoding removes a major cost bottleneck in sequence-level KD. Open questions include whether CKA-based matching extends to decoder-only LLMs at scale, whether the convergence difficulties observed with Flan-T5 (requiring weight initialization from larger students) generalize, and whether the approach can be combined with attention-matching or on-policy distillation losses for further gains.
