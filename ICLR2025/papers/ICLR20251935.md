## Problem

Dynamical generative models such as Flow Matching and denoising diffusion lack theoretically-sound reward fine-tuning methods that provably produce the desired tilted distribution p*(x) ∝ p_base(x) exp(r(x)). Existing approaches either ignore the base model or, when using KL regularization, suffer from an 'initial value function bias' caused by the dependency between the initial noise and the final sample in iterative generative processes, leading to biased fine-tuned models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251935/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained text-conditional Flow Matching model (U-Net on 512×512 image latents) and a differentiable reward model (ImageReward), and outputs a fine-tuned generative model whose samples follow the tilted distribution. Fine-tuning must use the memoryless noise schedule σ(t) = √(2η_t), but post-fine-tuning sampling can use any noise schedule including the standard σ(t) = 0 ODE. A scaling hyperparameter λ on the reward controls the tradeoff between fidelity to the base model and reward maximization.

## Method

The paper casts reward fine-tuning as a stochastic optimal control (SOC) problem and proves that a specific 'memoryless noise schedule' σ(t) = √(2η_t) must be enforced during fine-tuning, which makes the initial noise X_0 independent of the final sample X_1 and thereby eliminates the value function bias, guaranteeing convergence to the tilted distribution. To solve the resulting SOC problem, the paper proposes Adjoint Matching: a least-squares regression objective where the control u is regressed onto a 'lean adjoint' state (a simplified adjoint ODE with zero-expectation terms removed), whose unique critical point is the optimal control. This avoids backpropagating through the full SDE trajectory while retaining gradient-based scalability, and reduces variance compared to the continuous adjoint method. The framework is the first to provide a theoretically-grounded fine-tuning procedure for noiseless Flow Matching models, and recovers DDPM sampling as the memoryless schedule for diffusion models.

## Results

On text-to-image generation with a pre-trained Flow Matching model, Adjoint Matching (λ=12500) achieves the best overall performance: ClipScore 31.65, PickScore 19.76, HPSv2 24.49, and DreamSim diversity 37.24 (all with σ(t)=0 sampling). This outperforms the strongest baseline DRaFT-1 (ClipScore 30.95, PickScore 19.37, HPSv2 24.37, DreamSim 27.39) on all four metrics simultaneously. The method also significantly outperforms DPO (ClipScore 27.77, HPSv2 17.30) and ReFL (ClipScore 30.06, HPSv2 23.06), while retaining substantially more diversity than DRaFT-1 (37.24 vs. 27.39 DreamSim).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adjoint Matching (λ=12500) outperforms the strongest baseline DRaFT-1 on all four metrics: ClipScore (31.65 vs. 30.95), PickScore (19.76 vs. 19.37), HPSv2 (24.49 vs. 24.37), and DreamSim diversity (37.24 vs. 27.39), with the diversity gap being particularly large (~10 points). Against ReFL, Adjoint Matching leads on ClipScore (31.65 vs. 30.06), PickScore (19.76 vs. 19.07), HPSv2 (24.49 vs. 23.06), and diversity (37.24 vs. 32.69). DPO performs worst among reward-based methods on consistency and human preference (ClipScore 27.77, HPSv2 17.30), though it retains the highest diversity (54.11) since it barely modifies the base model. Within the same memoryless SOC formulation, Adjoint Matching clearly outperforms both the continuous adjoint (ClipScore 29.49, HPSv2 21.34) and discrete adjoint (ClipScore 29.28, HPSv2 19.73) methods, confirming that the regression-based objective is more effective than direct gradient-based SOC solvers. The paper also shows that Adjoint Matching achieves the best Pareto front across the consistency–diversity–human-preference tradeoff, with the λ hyperparameter providing a smooth and stable control knob, whereas DRaFT-1 requires sensitive early stopping to manage the same tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic optimal control (SOC) framework for generative modeling (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2023; Han & E, 2016) to the specific problem of reward fine-tuning, and identifies a fundamental flaw in prior approaches: naively applying KL-regularized reward objectives to dynamical generative models introduces an 'initial value function bias' that prevents convergence to the desired tilted distribution. The solution—a memoryless noise schedule σ(t)=√(2η_t)—is derived from the requirement that the initial noise X₀ be independent of the final sample X₁, and for diffusion models it recovers the DDPM schedule (Ho et al., 2020). The Adjoint Matching algorithm departs from the standard adjoint method by casting the SOC problem as a least-squares regression, eliminating the need to compute the Jacobian of the control and reducing variance. Unlike prior reward fine-tuning methods (DRaFT, ReFL, DPO) that use heuristics to stay close to the base model, this work provides the first provably correct fine-tuning procedure for Flow Matching models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the initial value function bias corrects a subtle but important theoretical gap: prior 'theoretically motivated' fine-tuning methods for diffusion and flow models were actually biased and did not converge to the tilted distribution. The memoryless noise schedule provides a simple, generic fix that enables correct fine-tuning of any dynamical generative model in the (5)-(6) family, including noiseless Flow Matching for the first time. Adjoint Matching, as a general-purpose SOC solver, has applicability beyond reward fine-tuning (e.g., conditional sampling, transport problems). Open questions include whether the approach scales to video and audio generation, how sensitive it is to reward model quality and adversarial exploitation, and whether the computational overhead of the adjoint computation limits practical adoption at scale.
