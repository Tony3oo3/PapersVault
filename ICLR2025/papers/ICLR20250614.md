## Problem

Transformers require a suite of architectural and optimization choices—adaptive optimizers, layer normalization, learning rate warmup, skip connections—that are largely absent in classical MLPs and CNNs, yet the precise loss-landscape mechanisms driving these requirements remain unexplained. While empirical studies have noted Hessian block heterogeneity in Transformers, no prior work had derived the exact Hessian structure of a self-attention layer or characterized how its blocks depend on data, weights, and attention moments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250614/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on a single self-attention layer consuming token embeddings X ∈ R^{L×d_V}, learnable weight matrices W_Q, W_K ∈ R^{d_V×d_K} and W_V ∈ R^{d_V×d_V}, and sequence labels Y, under a square loss. The output is the full Hessian matrix decomposed into outer-product and functional components, with each parameter block (Q-Q, K-K, V-V, Q-K, Q-V, K-V) explicitly expressed in terms of Kronecker products, attention moment matrices M_1, M_2, M_3, and the residual δ_XY. The derivation is purely symbolic; empirical validation uses a single GPT-2 block on a digit-addition next-token prediction task with cross-entropy loss.

## Method

The paper derives the exact Hessian of a single self-attention layer via matrix calculus (row-wise vectorization, Kronecker and Khatri-Rao products), decomposing it through the Gauss-Newton split into an outer-product Hessian (second derivatives of the loss) and a functional Hessian (second derivatives of the network). Each block is then characterized by its polynomial degree in the data matrix X (ranging from O(X²) for the value block to O(X⁶) for query-key blocks), its dependence on attention central moments (first for value, second for query-key outer-product, third for query-key functional), and its weight-matrix dependencies. The authors further isolate the effects of two Transformer-specific design choices: removing softmax (which restores a block-hollow functional Hessian and uniform data scaling, analogous to MLPs) and replacing the W_Q W_K^T product with a single W_QK matrix (which eliminates the T-functional Hessian term). Empirical validation on a GPT-2 block confirms the predicted growth rates and demonstrates that Pre-LN mitigates the inter-block data heterogeneity.

## Results

The paper derives the exact Hessian of a single self-attention layer and shows that its blocks exhibit highly heterogeneous data dependence: query/key diagonal blocks scale as O(X⁶) (outer-product) and O(X⁵) (functional), while the value diagonal block scales only as O(X²) and O(1) respectively. Empirically, using a single GPT-2 block on a digit-addition task with cross-entropy loss, the Frobenius norm of the query Hessian block grows as σ⁵ while the value block grows as σ², confirming the theoretical predictions. The query Hessian entries are two orders of magnitude smaller than value Hessian entries at initialization, and stacking D linear self-attention layers yields 3^D input covariance factors in the Hessian (vs. only 2 for a deep linear MLP of the same depth).

**Table 1: Dependence of the Hessian of a linear self-attention layer on the intra-sequence covariance Σ_XX and the input-residual covariance Ω_xy in big-O notation.**

| Component | MLP/CNN | Transformer (linear self-attention) |
|---|---|---|
| Outer-product Hessian H_o | O(Σ_XX) | O(Σ_XX³) |
| Functional Hessian H_f | O(Ω_xy) | O(Ω_xy · Σ_XX) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is structural: the Transformer Hessian is contrasted against the well-characterized Hessian of linear MLPs and CNNs (from Singh et al., 2021, 2023). Unlike MLPs where all diagonal Hessian blocks depend quadratically on data through a single covariance Σ_xx, the Transformer's query/key blocks depend on X to the sixth power (outer-product) or fifth power (functional), while the value block depends only quadratically or not at all. This heterogeneity is absent in MLPs/CNNs, where the functional Hessian has a block-hollow structure (zero diagonal blocks) and the outer-product Hessian is uniformly O(Σ_xx). The paper further shows that removing softmax (linear self-attention) makes all blocks depend uniformly as O(Σ_XX³), eliminating the block heterogeneity, and that the query-key two-matrix parameterization introduces an additional T-functional Hessian term with no MLP counterpart. These structural differences explain why Transformers require adaptive optimizers, layer normalization, and learning rate warmup—practices not needed for MLPs/CNNs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical Hessian analysis framework established for MLPs and CNNs (Singh et al., 2021; 2023) to the self-attention mechanism, using the Gauss-Newton decomposition and matrix calculus conventions from that line of work. It builds directly on the self-attention Jacobians derived by Noci et al. (2022) as the starting point for the second-order analysis. Prior Hessian studies of Transformers (Park & Kim, 2022; Zhang et al., 2024) were purely empirical, and the authors explicitly note that a theoretical treatment of the Transformer Hessian had been lacking, positioning this work as the first to derive exact closed-form expressions for all Hessian blocks of a self-attention layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for why Transformers require specific training infrastructure (adaptive optimizers, layer norm, warmup) by tracing these needs to the Hessian's block-heterogeneous data and weight dependencies. The connection between attention moment matrices and Hessian structure is novel and could aid interpretability of how the loss landscape evolves during training. Key limitations include the single-layer, single-sample, MSE-loss setting (though validated on a GPT-2 block with CE loss), and the depth-scaling result (3^D covariances) is a theoretical extrapolation not empirically verified at depth. Open questions include extending the analysis to multi-layer networks, learned embeddings, and translating the block structure into concrete optimizer designs.
