## Problem

Existing comparing-based evaluation (CBE) methods for LLMs each optimize a single objective—random sampling for accuracy, Arena for convergence, or AlpacaEval for scalability—failing to simultaneously achieve all three. This leaves scarce preference signals underutilized, as no prior framework jointly suppresses sampling bias, balances the descent of win-rate uncertainty, and mitigates updating uncertainty when new models are introduced.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253396/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iteration, the method selects a (model_i, model_j, sample_k) tuple from a benchmark of N instructions and M candidate models, sends the paired responses to a judge (LLM or human), and receives a scalar preference r ∈ [0,1] indicating the degree model_i wins over model_j. The iterative process terminates when a preset preference budget T is exhausted, after which a Bradley-Terry model aggregates all T pairwise results into per-model capability scores and a global ranking. The framework also supports list-wise preferences (K models ranked at once), which are decomposed into K(K−1)/2 pairwise signals.

## Method

UNICBE constructs three decoupled sampling probability matrices at each iteration: P^acc penalizes already-sampled model-pair and model-sample combinations via exponential decay (α^{-count}) to enforce uniform tuple coverage and minimize sampling bias; P^con is proportional to the current win-rate uncertainty ε_{i,j} to uniformly reduce observation variance across model pairs; and P^sca allocates extra budget to newly introduced models to reduce updating uncertainty. The three matrices are integrated by element-wise product and normalization into a global probability matrix P^l, from which the next tuple is selected via greedy sampling (argmax) to avoid stochastic sub-optimality. The key theoretical result (Appendix A) proves that completely uniform sampling minimizes the expected squared estimation error when biases sum to zero over all tuples, justifying the uniformity-driven design. Preference aggregation uses the Bradley-Terry model, which the authors show empirically alleviates sampling bias better than Elo or average win rate.

## Results

On AlpacaEval with GPT-4o as judge, UNICBE saves over 17% of the preference budget compared to RANDOM while achieving a Pearson correlation exceeding 0.995 with ground truth model scores. In dynamic scenarios where new models are introduced every 2000 iterations, UNICBE saves over 50% of evaluation costs relative to RANDOM. The quantitative objective-achievement scores (Table 2) confirm UNICBE leads on all three axes: β_acc = 0.7364, β_con = 0.9228, and β_sca = 0.9997, versus the best single-objective baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNICBE outperforms all three baselines on every objective simultaneously. Against RANDOM (Qin et al., 2024), UNICBE improves β_acc from 0.5803 to 0.7364 (+27%) while maintaining comparable convergence (0.9228 vs. 0.9081), translating to >17% budget savings at equal accuracy. Against ARENA (Chiang et al., 2024), which optimizes convergence via variance-gradient sampling, UNICBE matches convergence (0.9228 vs. 0.9172) but substantially improves accuracy (0.7364 vs. 0.5725) and scalability (0.9997 vs. 0.9945). Against ALPACAEVAL (Dubois et al., 2024), which fixes a reference model for scalability, UNICBE dominates on all three metrics (0.7364 vs. 0.0925 on accuracy, 0.9228 vs. 0.3515 on convergence, 0.9997 vs. 0.4987 on scalability), as the fixed-reference design severely compromises tuple and model uniformity. In the dynamic model-introduction scenario, UNICBE saves >50% budget over RANDOM, while ARENA and RANDOM show no scalability advantage and ALPACAEVAL's reference-model strategy provides only partial scalability benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNICBE unifies three previously separate budget allocation strategies for comparing-based evaluation (CBE): random sampling (Qin et al., 2024) for accuracy, Arena's variance-gradient sampling (Chiang et al., 2024) for convergence, and AlpacaEval's reference-model prioritization (Dubois et al., 2024) for scalability. Rather than building on a single prior method, the paper derives a common theoretical principle—uniformity across tuples, win-rate uncertainty, and model allocation—and shows that each prior method is a partial instantiation of this principle. The preference aggregation side draws on the Bradley-Terry model and Elo rating, which are standard tools, but the paper's contribution is the decoupled three-matrix construction and their product integration (Eq. 13) that simultaneously targets all three objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 17% budget savings at near-perfect correlation (Pearson > 0.995) and 50% savings in dynamic model-introduction scenarios are practically significant for teams running continuous LLM leaderboards with limited human or API-judge budgets. The clean theoretical decomposition (uniformity → least bias, uniform uncertainty descent → convergence, uniform model allocation → scalability) provides a principled design template that could extend to other active-evaluation or sequential-decision settings beyond LLMs. Open questions include how the framework behaves when the number of models grows very large (the O(M²) pairwise structure becomes costly), whether the Bradley-Terry aggregation assumption holds under highly non-transitive preference regimes, and whether the α hyperparameter's insensitivity (tested only in [1.5, 3]) persists under more adversarial or noisy judge settings.
