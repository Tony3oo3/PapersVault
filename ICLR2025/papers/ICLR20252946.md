## Problem

U-Nets are the dominant architecture for image denoising and diffusion modeling, yet the theoretical justification for their defining components—encoder-decoder structure, long skip connections, and pooling/up-sampling layers—has remained largely anecdotal. This paper asks whether there is a principled statistical model in which the U-Net architecture emerges naturally as the optimal computational structure, and whether learning the denoising function in such a model admits efficient sample complexity guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252946/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the denoising task, the U-Net receives a noisy observation z ∈ R^d where z = x + g with x ∈ [S]^d being the clean discrete pixel vector and g ~ N(0, I_d) isotropic Gaussian noise; it outputs the posterior expectation m(z) = E[x|z] ∈ R^d. For the classification warm-up, the ConvNet receives the clean discrete vector x ∈ [S]^d and outputs a class probability distribution over S labels. The underlying data-generating process is a tree-structured graphical model of height L with branching factors m^(ℓ), where leaf-level variables are the observed pixels and the root variable is the label; all intermediate layers are latent.

## Method

The core insight is that the belief propagation (BP) denoising algorithm on a generative hierarchical model (GHM) decomposes into a downward message-passing phase (leaves → root) and an upward phase (root → leaves) that reuses the downward intermediate results. This two-phase structure directly induces the U-Net's encoder (downward: convolutional + average-pooling + normalization layers), decoder (upward: convolutional + up-sampling + normalization layers), and long skip connections (which carry the downward messages h↓ and q↓ into the upward computation of b↑). The paper shows that the log-sum-exponential update functions in the message-passing algorithm can be approximated to arbitrary precision by two-hidden-layer ReLU networks, so the entire BP algorithm is realizable as a U-Net with polynomially many parameters. Training proceeds via empirical risk minimization (squared loss for denoising, square loss for classification) over the U-Net/ConvNet parameter class, and the paper derives the first polynomial sample complexity bound for learning the Bayes denoiser in a hierarchical model using U-Nets, with the rate depending polynomially on the leaf dimension d and inversely on ε^6.

## Results

The paper provides two main theoretical guarantees. Theorem 1 (classification) shows that a ConvNet with width D = Θ(S²K²d·3^L/ε) and n = Õ(LS⁴K⁴d²·3^(2L)‖m‖₁/ε⁶) samples achieves D₂² distance ≤ ε² from the Bayes classifier. Theorem 2 (denoising) shows that a U-Net with width D = Θ(S³K²d·18^L/ε) and n = Õ(LS¹⁰K⁴d²·18^(2L)‖m‖₁/ε⁶) samples achieves D₂² distance ≤ ε² from the Bayes denoiser. In the simplified binary case (S=2, K constant, m^(ℓ)=m≥3, d=m^L), the sample complexity reduces to n = Õ(L²m·d⁴/ε⁶) for classification and n = Õ(L²m·d⁸/ε⁶) for denoising, both polynomial in d and 1/ε, avoiding the curse of dimensionality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or method comparisons. The key theoretical contribution is showing that U-Nets naturally approximate the belief propagation denoising algorithm in generative hierarchical models (GHMs), providing the first polynomial sample complexity bound for learning the Bayes denoiser in such models using U-Nets. The classification result (Theorem 1) parallels and extends the polynomial sample complexity results of Petrini et al. (2023) and Tomasini & Wyart (2024) for hierarchical models, while the denoising result (Theorem 2) is novel. The paper positions itself relative to Mei & Wu (2023), who showed ResNets approximate variational inference in high-dimensional graphical models, by instead showing U-Nets approximate belief propagation in tree-structured GHMs. The authors note that their analysis does not address optimization (unlike Petrini et al. 2023, which showed exponential sample complexity in the absence of correlations), and the polynomial degree (d⁸ for denoising) is acknowledged as potentially improvable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural-network-approximates-algorithm paradigm from Mei & Wu (2023), who showed ResNets approximate variational inference in high-dimensional graphical models, to the setting of tree-structured generative hierarchical models (GHMs) and U-Nets. It builds directly on Sclocchi et al. (2024), who identified the downward/upward structure of belief propagation in hierarchical models, and on Petrini et al. (2023), who introduced GHMs in a classification context and showed polynomial sample complexity for ConvNets. The key departure is providing the first theoretical derivation of why U-Nets have their specific architectural components—encoder-decoder structure, long skip connections, and pooling/upsampling layers—by showing these are naturally induced by the message-passing algorithm for computing the Bayes denoiser in GHMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work offers the first principled explanation for the U-Net architecture design, which has been widely used in image segmentation, denoising, and diffusion modeling but lacked a theoretical justification for its specific structural choices. The polynomial sample complexity bounds (though with large exponents, e.g., d^8 in the simplified case) establish that U-Nets can learn the Bayes denoiser in hierarchical models without the curse of dimensionality, and the connection to diffusion models suggests a path toward end-to-end error bounds for sampling. Key limitations include the restriction to discrete covariate spaces, the large polynomial degree in dimension, and the use of point-wise rather than standard convolution operations. Open questions include extending to continuous spaces, improving the rate dependencies, finding graphical models where BP aligns with standard convolutions, and verifying the layer-functionality hypotheses in pre-trained U-Nets like those in Stable Diffusion.
