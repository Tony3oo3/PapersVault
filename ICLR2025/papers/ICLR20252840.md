## Problem

Optimizing physics detector designs for rare-event searches (e.g., neutrinoless double-beta decay) is intractable with traditional simulation because the background rate is so low that enormous event counts are needed for non-zero signal, and the resulting discrete metric m/N has extreme variance that defeats standard continuous surrogate models. The paper formalizes this as a Rare Event Design (RED) problem and proposes a surrogate that can learn the design-parameter-to-background-rate mapping from a small number of expensive high-fidelity runs supplemented by many cheap low-fidelity runs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two fidelity levels of GEANT4-based Monte Carlo simulations: low-fidelity (LF) runs of ~10⁴ neutrons sampled from a uniform distribution (0.15 CPU-hr each) and high-fidelity (HF) runs of ~10⁷ cosmic muons with physics-based propagation (170 CPU-hr each). Each event carries a 5D design parameter vector θ (moderator radius, thickness, panel count, panel angle, panel length) and a 10D event-specific parameter φ (neutron initial/final position, momentum, energy), plus a binary signal label. The model outputs a predicted background rate ŷ_Raw in nuclei/(kg·yr) with calibrated uncertainty σ̂_Raw, from which optimal θ* is extracted via active learning.

## Method

RESuM combines a Conditional Neural Process (CNP) trained at the event level with a Multi-Fidelity Gaussian Process (MFGP) trained at the simulation-trial level. The CNP approximates the unknown triggering probability t(θ,φ) by fitting a Gaussian posterior over the Bernoulli process, converting each binary outcome X_ki into a continuous score β_ki ∈ [0,1]; mixup augmentation (β(0.1,0.1) blending) addresses the ~1:5×10⁴ signal-to-background imbalance. These CNP scores are averaged per trial to produce y_CNP at both fidelities, which together with the raw HF metric y_Raw form three co-kriging inputs to the MFGP, enabling the model to borrow information from the 304 cheap LF trials to constrain the 10 expensive HF evaluations. Active learning with integrated variance reduction then sequentially selects new HF simulation points, balancing exploration of high-uncertainty regions against exploitation of known optima, until the surrogate converges on the optimal moderator geometry.

## Results

RESuM achieves an MSE of 0.002 on the full dataset (310 LF + 10 HF) with proper statistical coverage of 69%, 95%, and 100% at 1σ, 2σ, and 3σ respectively (expected: 68.27%, 95.45%, 99.73%). The model identifies an optimal neutron moderator design that reduces the ⁷⁷ᵐGe background rate from 0.238 to 0.0798 nuclei/(kg·yr), a (66.5±3.5)% reduction, using only 3.3% of the computational resources (1,746.5 vs. 52,700 CPU hours) required by traditional all-HF simulation. A 100-iteration robustness check (RESuM(100x)) confirms consistent performance with MSE 0.003 and coverage of 62.4%, 92.2%, 99.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESuM is compared against three baselines: a standard Multi-Fidelity Gaussian Process (MFGP), a Multi-Fidelity Bayesian Neural Network (MF-BNN), and an Adaptive Importance Sampling + MFGP (AIS-MFGP) ablation. On the full dataset, RESuM (MSE 0.002) achieves both the lowest MSE among models with proper coverage and the only statistically calibrated uncertainty estimates (69/95/100% vs. expected 68.27/95.45/99.73%). The MFGP baseline severely undercovers (17/33/48%) with a 7.5× higher MSE (0.015), indicating its uncertainty bands are unrealistically narrow. The MF-BNN overcovers at 100% across all levels with the worst MSE (0.021), reflecting excessively wide and uninformative prediction bands. The AIS-MFGP ablation achieves the lowest MSE (0.001) but still undercovers (39/64/86%), confirming that the CNP component—not just smarter sampling—is critical for proper uncertainty quantification in the RED setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established surrogate modeling tools—Conditional Neural Processes (Garnelo et al., 2018) and Multi-Fidelity Gaussian Processes (Kennedy & O'Hagan, 2000; Ravi et al., 2024)—in a novel configuration where the CNP is repurposed from its typical generative role into a predictive score-provider that smooths discrete rare-event outcomes before feeding them into the MFGP. The MFGP co-kriging architecture follows Ravi et al. (2024), while the active learning loop uses integrated variance reduction from the Emukit toolkit (Paleyes et al., 2023). The key departure from prior work is the formalization of the 'Rare Event Design' problem: optimizing design parameters when the objective metric is inherently a small, discrete count (m/N), a regime where standard continuous surrogate models fail due to high variance and non-normality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 66.5% neutron background reduction at 3.3% computational cost demonstrates that the CNP-as-prior-knowledge approach can make rare-event design optimization tractable where brute-force simulation is infeasible. The RED problem formulation is explicitly positioned as broadly applicable to astronomy (binary black hole mergers) and materials science (DFT parameter optimization), suggesting a general framework for simulation-intensive fields. However, validation is limited to 100 out-of-sample HF simulations and a single application domain, and the active learning strategy is acknowledged as simplistic; scaling to higher-dimensional design spaces and more complex multi-physics scenarios remains an open question.
