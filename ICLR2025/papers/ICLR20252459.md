## Problem

While feedforward networks are known to develop abstract representations under multi-task training, it remains unclear whether autoregressive models—more relevant to real-world decision-making with noisy, temporally evolving evidence—learn disentangled world models, and under what precise conditions. This paper provides the first theoretical guarantees for the emergence of disentangled representations in optimal multi-task classifiers operating on noisy sequential observations, characterizing the roles of noise level, number of tasks, and evidence accumulation time.

## Input / Output

The agent receives T timesteps of noisy observations X(t) = x* + σN(0, I_D) of an unknown ground truth vector x* ∈ R^D, transformed by a fixed non-linear injective encoder f (a 3-layer MLP with ReLU activations). The network must simultaneously output N_task binary classification probabilities indicating whether x* lies above or below each of N_task random linear decision boundaries in the latent space. The key emergent output is the latent state Z(t), which the paper shows linearly encodes a maximum-likelihood estimate of x* with approximately orthogonal coordinates when N_task ≥ D, enabling zero-shot OOD generalization via simple linear probes.

## Method

The paper proves that any optimal multi-task classifier must encode the MLE μ(t) of x* in its latent state, and derives a closed-form extraction formula μ(t) = (C^T C)^{-1} C^T (σ/√t Φ^{-1}(g(Z(t))) + b) where C contains the decision boundary normals. The key insight is that each classification probability encodes a projection distance to a boundary, so N_task ≥ D boundaries spanning R^D uniquely determine x*; crucially, noise σ > 0 is essential because it prevents probability saturation and preserves boundary-distance information. Empirically, they train leaky RNNs, LSTMs, and GPT-2 transformers via BPTT with cross-entropy loss, then verify abstractness via 4-fold OOD cross-validation of linear probes and disentanglement via pairwise angles between latent factor decoder normals. The RNNs implement the representation as a 2D continuous attractor (confirmed by near-zero eigenvalues in linearized dynamics), while transformers achieve disentanglement already from N_task ≥ D, explaining their superior OOD generalization over RNNs.

## Results

For D=2 with N_task=24, leaky RNNs achieve median OOD r² = 0.96 and ID r² = 0.97 (across 5 networks), approaching the theoretical optimum of r² = 1 − 0.48/T ≈ 0.976 for T=20 timesteps. GPT-2 transformers achieve near-perfect OOD generalization already from N_task ≥ D (i.e., N_task=2 for D=2), while RNNs require N_task ≫ D for full disentanglement (decoder angles approaching 90°). Increasing pretraining noise σ consistently improves OOD r², and the learned representations form 2D continuous attractors with sparse (~10% active neurons) and mixed selectivity, matching cortical properties.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is context-dependent computation (Mante et al., 2013), where one task is executed at a time with a one-hot task cue. In this setting, the RNN learns separate line attractors in different regions of state space for each task (e.g., one tracking evidence difference, another tracking evidence sum), causing the 2D latent space to collapse and become undecodable by a linear probe. In contrast, multi-task learning produces a shared 2D continuous attractor that is linearly decodable and generalizes OOD. Algorithmically, context-dependent computation scales linearly with N_task and exponentially with D (requiring 3^D line attractors for a simple task family), while multi-task learning scales only linearly with D (requiring N_task ≥ D). Among autoregressive architectures, GPT-2 transformers outperform RNNs and LSTMs on OOD generalization and disentanglement, achieving orthogonal latent factor decoders from N_task ≥ D rather than N_task ≫ D, suggesting transformers are particularly suited for constructing disentangled world models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Johnston & Fusi (2023), who showed feedforward networks develop abstract representations from multitask learning, to autoregressive architectures (RNNs, LSTMs, transformers) operating on noisy, temporally evolving evidence streams. It departs from the broader disentanglement literature (Higgins et al. 2017; Kim & Mnih 2018) by requiring no adversarial objectives, sparsity regularization, or explicit factor-separation constraints—disentanglement emerges purely from competence at multiple classification tasks. The theoretical framework also contrasts with context-dependent computation (Mante et al. 2013), showing that simultaneous multitasking produces state-space-efficient representations that scale linearly with input dimensionality D rather than exponentially, and provides the first closed-form extraction of the latent estimate from the network's internal state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal guarantees (Theorem 3.1) for when and why disentangled world models emerge in any optimal multi-task classifier, with conditions on noise, task count, and trial duration. The finding that noise is not merely tolerated but *necessary* for disentanglement, and that more pretraining noise improves OOD generalization, challenges the standard assumption that noise is purely detrimental. Open questions include characterizing sufficient conditions for non-linear (manifold) decision boundaries, extending to self-supervised or unsupervised pretraining settings, and determining whether the linear-scaling requirement (N_task ≥ D) holds for structured (non-random) task families. The result that transformers disentangle from N_task ≥ D while RNNs require N_task ≫ D offers a concrete mechanistic explanation for transformers' superior world-modeling capabilities.
