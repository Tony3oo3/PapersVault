## Problem

Multi-agent communication typically relies on dedicated channels, which may be unavailable in harsh, adversarial, or resource-constrained environments. This paper asks whether an MDP controller can implicitly communicate with a state-observing receiver by embedding messages into its actions, and what the fundamental rate-reward trade-off is when the environment itself serves as the communication channel.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251343/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a k-bit message partitioned into l blocks of μ bits, the current MDP state, and a feedback block of historical (state, action, next-state) triples, and produces a continuous belief map Z ∈ R^{|S|×k/R} that is hard-quantized into a codeword U of decision rules (one action per state per time step). The receiver, which observes only the state sequence (not actions), decodes the message from the resulting state observations. The channel is non-differentiable and stochastic, with the MDP transition kernel T(s'|s,x) defining the channel law.

## Method

Theoretically, the paper derives the capacity of the action-state channel as I(X;S⁺|S) achievable by a stationary randomized policy, and casts the capacity-reward trade-off as a convex optimization over occupation measures with a closed-form gradient. Practically, Act2Comm uses a transformer-based encoder with block-attention feedback coding to map messages and feedback into a belief map, which is quantized into decision rules via Shannon's EAS channel transformation. Because the EAS channel and quantizer are non-differentiable, a critic network is trained to predict decoder logits from perturbed belief maps, enabling gradient-based encoder updates. Training alternates iteratively: the critic is trained for s_in inner steps, then the encoder is updated (with frozen decoder) using a weighted loss combining cross-entropy communication loss and MSE control loss against a target policy, then the decoder is updated (with frozen encoder) using communication loss alone.

## Results

In the 'Catch the Ball' environment (27 states, 3 actions) with a perfect channel (p=0), Act2Comm achieves a coding rate of 0.2 with zero bit error rate while reducing the MDP reward only from 1.66 to 1.5; relaxing the BER target to 10⁻⁴ allows the same rate at reward 1.6. In the noisy variant (p=0.2), coding rates decrease for the same reliability level due to channel stochasticity. In 'Lucky Wheel' (3 states, 2 actions), the trade-off between BER, rate, and reward is tunable via the balancing parameter λ: large λ forces the policy to mirror the target (BER = 0.5, no communication), while smaller λ enables reliable communication at the cost of reward. Inference is fast: encoding completes in <10 ms and decoding in <3 ms per 12-bit message.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare Act2Comm against any competing coding or control methods; no external baselines are evaluated. Instead, the experiments demonstrate the internal trade-off surface of Act2Comm itself by sweeping the balancing parameter λ and the target BER across three MDP environments. The theoretical capacity-reward trade-off (Theorem 2) serves as an upper bound, and the empirical curves in Figures 3–5 show how closely Act2Comm approaches these limits in the finite block-length regime. The 'Catch the Ball' p=0 case (perfect channel) acts as a sanity check where no coding is needed, confirming that Act2Comm can embed messages with negligible reward loss. The p=0.2 case and the 'Erratic Robot' (5-action space) environment stress-test the method under noise and larger action spaces, showing graceful degradation rather than failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Shannon's (1958) method for channels with side information at the transmitter to convert the MDP's action-state channel into an extended action-state (EAS) channel, enabling block coding without knowledge of future states. It departs from prior FSC capacity results (Blackwell et al., Verdu & Han, Goldsmith & Varaiya, Shemuel et al.) which express capacity in multi-letter forms, by exploiting the special structure of the action-state channel (where state equals feedback) to derive a single-letter capacity expression. Unlike Sokota et al. (2022), where the receiver observes both actions and states (making it a source coding problem), this paper assumes the receiver observes only states, shifting the problem to channel coding. The practical Act2Comm framework extends deep-learning channel coding (Turbo autoencoder, DeepPolar, KO codes) to the non-differentiable FSC setting, where standard backpropagation through the channel is infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes fundamental information-theoretic limits for implicit communication through actions in MDPs, showing the capacity-reward trade-off is a convex optimization — a clean result that provides an upper bound for any practical coding scheme. Act2Comm offers a plug-in framework for MDP/RL applications where dedicated communication channels are unavailable (e.g., nuclear decommissioning robots, deep-space or subterranean operations). Key limitations include evaluation on small MDPs (3–27 states), the additional training overhead from the critic network, and the assumption of a pre-determined target policy. Open questions include whether implicit communication through actions can improve overall coordination in multi-agent settings, and how the framework scales to larger state/action spaces or continuous MDPs.
