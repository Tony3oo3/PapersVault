## Problem

Generating synthetic face recognition datasets with sufficient inter-class variation remains a key challenge: while generative models can easily produce intra-class diversity (pose, illumination), ensuring that synthetic identities are well-separated in the embedding space is difficult. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly formulating inter-class variation as a geometric packing problem on the face embedding hypersphere.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251369/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained ArcFace model (512-dim unit-norm embeddings), a gallery of face images (≥ number of identities) whose embeddings approximate the valid face-embedding manifold, and random noise vectors for initialization and generation. It produces a set of optimized reference embeddings on the hypersphere and, via the Arc2Face diffusion generator, a synthetic dataset of 500K aligned RGB face images across 10K identities (64 images per identity by default). The optimization operates purely in embedding space, with images generated only after the packing solution is found.

## Method

HyperFace formulates synthetic dataset generation as a spherical code (Tammes) problem: maximize the minimum pairwise cosine distance among n_id reference embeddings on a unit hypersphere, solved iteratively with Adam gradient descent. A regularization term (weighted by α) pulls each reference embedding toward its nearest gallery embedding, keeping the solution on the manifold of valid face embeddings rather than the full hypersphere surface. After optimization, the Arc2Face diffusion model generates images conditioned on each reference embedding, with Gaussian noise (scaled by β) added to the embedding before normalization to introduce intra-class variation. This decouples inter-class structure (controlled by the packing optimization) from intra-class diversity (controlled by noise injection), unlike prior methods that rely on latent-space exploration of GANs or diffusion models for both.

## Results

HyperFace (10k identities, 500k images) achieves 98.50% on LFW, 84.23% on CPLFW, 89.40% on CALFW, 88.83% on CFP-FP, and 86.53% on AgeDB-30, setting new state-of-the-art among synthetic-dataset-trained models on LFW, CPLFW, and CFP-FP. The closest synthetic competitor, DCFace, leads on CALFW (91.70%) and AgeDB-30 (89.50%), while the real CASIA-WebFace dataset still outperforms all synthetic methods by 1–6 points across benchmarks. Ablations show that scaling to 50k identities (3.2M images) further improves CPLFW to 85.60% and CFP to 92.24%, and stochastic mini-batch optimization (batch size 1,000) reduces optimization runtime from 84 hours to 0.5 hours for 100k identities with comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperFace achieves the best synthetic-dataset performance on three of five benchmarks: LFW (98.50% vs. DCFace 98.35%), CPLFW (84.23% vs. DCFace 83.12%), and CFP-FP (88.83% vs. DCFace 88.43%). DCFace (Kim et al., CVPR 2023) is the strongest competitor, outperforming HyperFace on CALFW (91.70% vs. 89.40%) and AgeDB-30 (89.50% vs. 86.53%), suggesting HyperFace's hypersphere packing strategy excels at frontal and cross-pose generalization but is less effective for cross-age and cross-identity verification. IDiff-Face (Uniform) (Boutros et al., ICCV 2023) trails HyperFace by 0.32 points on LFW but by a larger 3.36 points on CPLFW, while Langevin-DisCo (Geissbühler et al., 2024) lags by 1.43 points on LFW and 7.27 points on CFP. Older GAN-based methods (SynFace, SFace, ExFaceGAN, IDnet) and the computer-graphics-based DigiFace-1M are substantially behind, with LFW accuracies ranging from 78.72% to 93.65%. Against the real CASIA-WebFace reference, HyperFace closes the gap to within 0.92 points on LFW and 5.79 points on CPLFW, the largest remaining gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in synthetic face dataset generation, where prior methods (SynFace, SFace, ExFaceGAN, Langevin-DisCo, DCFace, IDiff-Face) explore the latent space of a generator to create diverse identities. Instead, HyperFace formulates identity diversity as a spherical code / Tammes packing problem on the embedding hypersphere of a pretrained face recognition model (ArcFace), solving it via gradient descent with a manifold-regularization term. The optimized reference embeddings are then fed to a conditional diffusion generator (Arc2Face) to synthesize images, inverting the typical pipeline where the generator's latent space is the locus of identity exploration. This repurposes a classical geometric optimization (packing points on a sphere) for a modern deep-learning task, and the regularization term that keeps embeddings on the face-recognition manifold is a practical necessity absent from the pure packing formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HyperFace achieves state-of-the-art recognition accuracy among synthetic-data-trained models, narrowing (but not closing) the gap to models trained on real web-crawled data like CASIA-WebFace. A notable limitation is that using real images (BUPT) in the regularization gallery outperforms synthetic-only galleries, revealing that current generators do not fully cover the face-embedding manifold; this dependency on real data partially undermines the privacy motivation. Open questions include whether the quadratic optimization complexity can be further reduced for very large identity counts, whether demographic diversity is adequately captured, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific ArcFace + Arc2Face pairing used here.
