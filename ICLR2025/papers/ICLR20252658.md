## Problem

Existing stochastic trajectory prediction methods either extract interaction features only from history (assuming interaction patterns persist unchanged into the future) or apply the Markov property only to isolated sub-components like self-motion. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly modeling the full joint dynamics of agent motion and crowd interactions across the entire future sequence, yielding a framework that is robust to noisy observations and supports group-level reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252658/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes past position trajectories (x,y coordinates) for all visible agents as input—8 frames (3.2 s) on ETH/UCY, 10 frames (2.0 s) on NBA, and 9 frames on JRDB—with no image, map, or environmental context. It outputs N=20 stochastic joint configuration samples (all agents simultaneously) over the future horizon, structured as a Markov chain of K segments each spanning τ frames. Predictions are parameterized as displacements (ΔS) rather than absolute positions for robustness, and the distance threshold d=3 m governs which agent pairs are linked in the interaction graph.

## Method

The core contribution is a neuralized MRF that factorizes the future joint distribution into a Bayesian update term (first segment conditioned on history) and a transition term further decomposed into an agent-level self-evolution factor and a space-level interaction factor with pairwise potentials over a dynamically constructed undirected graph. Two CVAEs approximate this distribution: a Bayesian Update CVAE (Conv1d+GRU history encoder → self-attention → condition; MLP decoder) and a recurrent MRF-based Evolution CVAE (Configuration Encoder for self-transitions + Potential Update module that links edges by distance and accumulates edge features into nodes → condition; MLP decoder). Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 optimizes the ELBO (min-over-N reconstruction + KL), and Stage 2 freezes the CVAEs and trains purposive History and State Samplers using a discrepancy loss that encourages diverse yet plausible samples. The Markov chain over segments with stride τ (typically 3 frames) captures temporal dependencies while the MRF potentials capture evolving pairwise and group-wise spatial relationships, enabling real-time inference at >100 Hz for 57 pedestrians.

## Results

On ETH/UCY, the proposed NMRF achieves the best average minADE20/minFDE20 of 0.19/0.32 m, outperforming SocialCircle (0.20/0.33) and SingularTrajectory (0.22/0.34). On SDD, it reaches 7.20/11.29 px (0.25/0.39 m), beating TUTR (7.90/12.96 px) and Social-VAE-FPC (8.10/11.72 px). On NBA, it achieves 0.75/0.97 m total error, surpassing LED (0.82/1.15) and MID (0.96/1.27). On JRDB (stochastic), it attains 0.15/0.23 m with ~6.8 ms inference for 80 agents, over 17× faster than LED (~118 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NMRF achieves the best average minADE20/minFDE20 across all four datasets. On ETH/UCY, it beats the previous SOTA SocialCircle by 0.01/0.01 m on average and outperforms diffusion-based methods MID (0.23/0.39) and SingularTrajectory (0.22/0.34) by a clear margin, while matching or exceeding their inference speed. On SDD, it improves over TUTR (7.90/12.96 px) and Social-VAE-FPC (8.10/11.72 px) in both pixel and meter units. On NBA, it surpasses LED (0.82/1.15) and MID (0.96/1.27) in total 4 s error while running ~3× faster than LED (~19.3 ms vs ~65.7 ms for 11 agents). On JRDB, it outperforms LED in both deterministic (0.26/0.48 vs 0.32/0.54) and stochastic (0.15/0.23 vs 0.18/0.28) settings, and beats Social-Transmotion (which uses 2D bounding boxes) by ~29% ADE using trajectory-only input. The method also demonstrates robustness: under 50% dropped history frames, ADE degrades only from 0.26 to 0.26 m, and under Gaussian noise (scale=0.2) it maintains 0.32/0.34 m ADE for 4/9 frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the use of Markov properties in trajectory prediction—from prior work that applies them only to sub-components like self-motion (Choi & Hebert, 2006; Bhaskara et al., 2024) or intended actions (Han et al., 2023)—by modeling the full joint state transition and crowd interaction dynamics simultaneously through a neuralized MRF. The MRF graph construction draws on multi-target tracking literature (Khan et al., 2004, 2005), while the variational approximation via two CVAEs (one for Bayesian update, one for MRF-based evolution) is a novel architectural decomposition. The purposive sampling stage builds directly on the discrepancy loss from NPSN (Bae et al., 2022b), replacing standard Gaussian sampling with learned samplers to concentrate the output distribution around ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of SOTA accuracy with real-time stochastic inference (~6.8 ms for 80 agents) makes this method practically deployable on robotic systems at 30 FPS, addressing a gap where prior SOTA methods (LED, MID, SingularTrajectory) are too slow for such settings. The robustness to dropped and noisy history frames is valuable for real-world tracking pipelines where detections are imperfect. However, the paper acknowledges that learned MRF potentials are limited by the graph complexity present in training data, and the pipeline uses only coordinate inputs without environmental context (obstacles, traversable paths), leaving open questions about generalization to unseen interaction topologies and richer scene understanding.
