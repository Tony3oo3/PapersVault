## Problem

Current MLLMs and their benchmarks are designed for narrow-scope scenarios with a single image and short, relevant text, leaving a significant gap in evaluating models on realistic document comprehension where multiple interleaved images and long text contain abundant irrelevant or misleading information. No existing benchmark tests whether a model can locate the correct image-text pair within a complex multi-modal context, follow instructions to reference a specific image by index, and produce a coherent answer grounded in that identified context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252608/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The IITC task feeds the model a long interleaved sequence of up to 8 images (each tokenized as 256 tokens) and up to 8,000 tokens of scientific text, followed by a question targeting one specific image. The model must produce a text answer that explicitly cites the correct image using the notation '[Picture i] (Figure j)', where i is the input sequence position and j is the original document figure number. The auxiliary ITA task instead presents shuffled images and text segments drawn from different articles and requires the model to output the correct image-to-text pairing for each item.

## Method

VEGA is constructed from SciGraphQA by expanding each figure's first-mention paragraph into a longer context (up to 8k tokens) while interspersing 2–8 additional figures from the same paper, then filtering questions to those with unambiguous image references and rewriting answers to include explicit image-index citations. The key training insight is a multi-task, multi-scale strategy: an auxiliary Image-Text Association (ITA) task is jointly trained with IITC to sharpen image-text alignment, and the ITA data is presented at three text scales (caption, first-mention paragraph, expanded context) and two image-count scales (3 and 5 images) so the model learns associations progressively before facing the full-length test setting. The base model is Qwen-VL-Chat 7B, fine-tuned separately at 4k and 8k maximum token lengths, achieving 85.8% image-association accuracy and 0.508 ROUGE-L on IITC-4k—outperforming GPT4V and Gemini-1.5-pro on association accuracy while matching or exceeding them on LLaVA-Bench text-quality scores.

## Results

On the IITC 4k task, VEGA-Base-4k achieves a Rouge-L of 0.508, BLEU of 0.252, and image-association accuracy of 85.8%, surpassing all proprietary models (GPT4V: 80.5% Acc; Gemini-1.5-pro: 75.3% Acc). On the IITC 8k task, VEGA-Base-8k reaches 77.5% accuracy with a Rouge-L of 0.473. On the ITA task, VEGA-Base-4k achieves near-perfect association rates of 99.8% (3 images) and 99.2% (5 images), far exceeding Gemini-1.5-pro (95.6%/89.1%) and GPT4V (91.2%/53.8%). However, on the LLaVA Bench subjective score, GPT4V (7.24) and Gemini-1.5-pro (6.86) still outperform VEGA-Base-4k (5.65).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VEGA-Base-4k outperforms all seven baselines on Rouge-L (0.508 vs. best baseline 0.431 for Qwen2-VL-Instruct) and BLEU (0.252 vs. 0.138 for Gemini-1.0-pro) on IITC 4k, and achieves the highest image-association accuracy at 85.8%, beating GPT4V (80.5%) by 5.3 points and Gemini-1.5-pro (75.3%) by 10.5 points. On the ITA task, VEGA-Base-4k achieves near-perfect scores (99.8%/99.2%), dramatically surpassing Gemini-1.5-pro (95.6%/89.1%) and GPT4V (91.2%/53.8%), while open-source models like Qwen-VL-Chat (4.3%/0.0%) and InternVL-1.5 (23.8%/1.4%) perform near random guessing. The sole metric where VEGA-Base-4k trails is the LLaVA Bench subjective score (5.65 vs. GPT4V's 7.24 and Gemini-1.5-pro's 6.86), which the authors attribute to proprietary models generating more natural-sounding summaries. On IITC 8k, VEGA-Base-8k maintains its lead in Rouge-L (0.473) and BLEU (0.223) over all baselines, with GPT4V (0.322 Rouge-L) and Gemini-1.5-pro (0.363 Rouge-L) trailing significantly on text quality despite competitive accuracy scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM instruction-tuning paradigm (exemplified by LLaVA, Qwen-VL, and InternVL) by introducing a fundamentally more demanding comprehension task: Interleaved Image-Text Comprehension (IITC), where models must navigate multiple interleaved images within long scientific documents and explicitly reference the correct image in their answers. It builds directly on Qwen-VL-Chat as the base model and on SciGraphQA as the foundational data source, but departs from both by constructing multi-image, multi-paragraph contexts with deliberate distractors and requiring image-index output. The multi-task (IITC + ITA) and multi-scale training strategy is methodologically distinct from prior single-task fine-tuning approaches for MLLMs, drawing on curriculum-like progressive difficulty rather than uniform training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VEGA benchmark exposes a significant and previously unmeasured weakness in current MLLMs: even GPT4V and Gemini-1.5-pro achieve only ~75–80% image-association accuracy, while open-source models (InternVL, Qwen-VL-Chat, Qwen2-VL) perform at near-random levels (~0–2%), revealing that instruction-following for image referencing is far from solved. The finding that multi-scale training is critical for the ITA auxiliary task (without it, accuracy drops to random-guess levels) provides a practical recipe for improving image-text grounding. Limitations include the narrow scientific-paper domain, a relatively small 700-sample test set, and the fact that the fine-tuned 7B model still trails proprietary models on LLaVA Bench text-quality scores, suggesting the gap between association accuracy and answer quality remains open.
