## Problem

Existing diffusion-based graph generative models operate on the full adjacency matrix, incurring quadratic complexity in the number of nodes that makes them infeasible for large graphs. While spectral representations offer a natural low-dimensional encoding of graph structure, prior spectral methods (e.g., SPECTRE) generate adjacency matrices conditioned on eigenvectors rather than generating the spectrum itself, precluding direct conditioning on target spectral properties at inference time.

## Input / Output

The model starts from Gaussian noise in the space of k eigenvectors (treated as node embeddings, i.e., rows of Φ) and k eigenvalues, optionally concatenated with node features. It outputs a binary adjacency matrix produced by a PPGN-based graph predictor that consumes the reconstructed (noisy) Laplacian and the generated eigenpairs. The truncation to k eigenpairs (e.g., 8–32 out of n) is the key design choice that reduces the diffusion component from O(n²) to O(kn), though the PPGN predictor retains quadratic complexity.

## Method

GGSD applies denoising diffusion directly to the eigenpairs of the graph Laplacian rather than to the adjacency matrix. The score network is a transformer with L layers, each containing two multi-head cross-attention blocks: one updating eigenvector embeddings conditioned on eigenvalue features (produced by a 1D convolution) and one updating eigenvalue features conditioned on eigenvectors, with residual connections scaled by time-dependent factors. This cross-attention design simultaneously achieves permutation invariance (eigenvectors as node embeddings) and mutual conditioning between spectral components. A second stage trains a PPGN-based graph predictor with binary cross-entropy reconstruction loss and an adversarial loss to map the noisy truncated Laplacian reconstruction to a binary adjacency matrix. For conditional generation, the model supports inpainting-style conditioning on subsets of eigenvalues or eigenvectors via the RePaint mechanism, enabling users to specify target spectral properties at inference time.

## Results

On the three synthetic datasets (Community-small, Planar, SBM), GGSD achieves the best or second-best MMD score across 11 of 12 metric-dataset combinations, with particularly strong results on Planar (best on Deg. 0.0007, Spect. 0.0125, Orb. 0.0047) and SBM (best on Deg. 0.0005, Clus. 0.0115). On the real-world Proteins dataset (100–500 nodes), GGSD ranks first on all four MMD metrics (Deg. 0.0014, Clus. 0.0856, Spect. 0.0059, Orb. 0.0296). On QM9 molecule generation, GGSD achieves 96.6% validity, 86.4% validity-and-uniqueness, and 84.7% V&U&N, the highest combined score among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SPECTRE (the closest spectral-based competitor), GGSD wins on degree distribution across all three synthetic datasets (e.g., 0.0005 vs 0.0044 on SBM) and on clustering for SBM (0.0115 vs 0.0118), while SPECTRE retains an edge on spectral MMD for SBM (0.0015 vs 0.0045) and orbit frequency for SBM (0.0140 vs 0.0289). Against DiGress, GGSD dominates on degree and orbit metrics for Planar (0.0007 vs 0.0005 for Deg., 0.0047 vs 0.0115 for Orb.) but DiGress achieves a much lower clustering MMD on Planar (0.0178 vs 0.1881), which the authors attribute to the insensitivity of eigenvectors to local topological changes that affect planarity. GSDM performs poorly on SBM (Deg. 0.2295, Clus. 0.2280) because it samples eigenvectors uniformly from the training set, limiting its generative capacity. On the large-scale Proteins dataset, GGSD outperforms all baselines on every metric, with the largest margin over GSDM (Deg. 0.0014 vs 0.3792) and GDSS (Clus. 0.0856 vs 0.4160). On QM9, GGSD achieves 84.7% V&U&N, substantially ahead of GDSS (72.57%) and DiGress (31.8%), while SPECTRE's low uniqueness (31.2% V&U) reflects its reliance on a fixed set of learned orthogonal bases.

## Relation to Prior Work

GGSD extends the diffusion-based graph generation paradigm (DDPM, Ho et al. 2020) by moving the diffusion process from the adjacency matrix space into the spectral space of the graph Laplacian, building directly on SPECTRE's (Martinkus et al. 2022) PPGN-based graph predictor and its spectral conditioning philosophy. It departs from SPECTRE by generating eigenpairs directly rather than conditioning adjacency generation on eigenvectors, and from GSDM (Luo et al. 2023) by performing diffusion on both eigenvalues and eigenvectors rather than sampling eigenvectors from the training set. The RePaint inpainting mechanism (Lugmayr et al. 2022) is repurposed to enable spectral-conditioned generation, a capability neither SPECTRE nor GSDM offers. The cross-attention transformer architecture that jointly denoises eigenvectors and eigenvalues while maintaining permutation invariance is a novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from O(n²) to O(kn) complexity for the diffusion component makes diffusion-based graph generation feasible on larger graphs (e.g., 500-node Proteins) where methods like DiGress run out of memory, addressing a key scalability bottleneck in the field. The ability to condition generation on target spectral properties opens applications in shape analysis, graph classification, and adversarial robustness where spectral constraints are natural. However, the PPGN predictor retains quadratic complexity, and the choice of which spectral frequencies to use remains a manual hyperparameter rather than being learned. The finding that orthonormality of generated eigenvectors is not strictly necessary for valid graph reconstruction, and that lowest frequencies do not always yield the best reconstructions, challenges standard assumptions in spectral graph theory and suggests the learned representation captures connectivity in ways not fully explained by classical spectral analysis.
