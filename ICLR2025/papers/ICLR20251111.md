## Problem

Existing style transfer methods inject global stylistic features without respecting semantic correspondence, risking content leakage, while appearance transfer methods convey local object features but ignore overall perceptual style and structural integrity. Both paradigms also treat image and video tasks in isolation, neglecting video continuity. The paper introduces Semantic Style Transfer as a unified task that transfers style and appearance features from a reference to a context based on precise semantic mapping, and proposes a training-free solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251111/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a context image or video (512×512 for images) and a single reference image as input, producing a stylized image or video that preserves the context's structure while adopting the reference's style/appearance features at semantically corresponding regions. For video, the reference image is extended into a video sequence to match the context video's length. The method operates entirely at inference time on pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.5 (images) or AnimateDiff (videos), requiring no additional training or fine-tuning, with DDPM inversion performed to timestep T=601 before guided sampling.

## Method

Semantix is a training-free energy-guided sampler that modifies the denoising trajectory of a pre-trained diffusion model by adding a custom energy gradient to the classifier-free guidance term at each sampling step. The energy function comprises three components: (1) Style Feature Guidance, which uses DIFT-style ℓ₂ feature correspondence plus 2D position encoding to align output features with semantically matched reference features, masked by k-means clustering regions; (2) Spatial Feature Guidance, which minimizes point-to-point feature distance between output and context to preserve structure; and (3) Semantic Distance, a regularisation term that penalises the L2 difference between cross-attention maps after swapping self-attention K,V with reference features and the original context cross-attention maps, preventing overfitting. Because Semantix operates purely as a sampler modifying the noise prediction rather than the model architecture, it is model-agnostic and preserves the pre-trained model's inherent motion coherence for video without additional temporal modules.

## Results

On 1000 context-style image pairs (COCO + WikiArt), Semantix achieves the best score on all six metrics: LPIPS 0.461, CFSD 0.117, SSIM 0.589, Gram Metrics ×10² 2.524, PickScore 19.95, and HPS 18.78. For video style transfer on 100 stylized videos, Semantix leads on Semantic Consistency (0.944), Object Consistency (0.955), Visual Quality (55.86), and Temporal Consistency (60.05), while trailing CCPL slightly on Motion Alignment (−1.894 vs. −1.792) and UNIST on Motion Quality (53.99 vs. 55.07). In the user study (30 volunteers × 40 images), Semantix is preferred for context preservation (51.8%), style similarity (31.1%), and visual appeal (51.1%), roughly doubling the next-best method on each criterion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Semantix outperforms all 10 image baselines on every metric in Table 1. Against the closest structural-preservation competitor CAST (LPIPS 0.465), Semantix improves LPIPS to 0.461 and CFSD from 0.133 to 0.117, while also leading on style similarity (Gram 2.524 vs. 6.594) and aesthetics (PickScore 19.95 vs. 16.72). Among diffusion-based methods, Cross-Image suffers the worst structural degradation (LPIPS 0.703, CFSD 0.232), and DreamStyler shows the poorest style fidelity (Gram 6.990, CFSD 0.789); Semantix beats both by wide margins on all six metrics. StyleID is the strongest diffusion baseline on aesthetics (PickScore 19.68, HPS 18.70) but still trails Semantix (19.95, 18.78) and lags significantly on structure (LPIPS 0.514 vs. 0.461) and style (Gram 2.878 vs. 2.524). For video transfer, Semantix leads on Semantic Consistency (0.944), Object Consistency (0.955), Visual Quality (55.86), and Temporal Consistency (60.05), but is marginally behind CCPL on Motion Alignment (−1.894 vs. −1.792) and UNIST on Motion Quality (53.99 vs. 55.07), indicating a slight trade-off in motion fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Semantix sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) energy-guided diffusion sampling (Liu et al., 2022; Epstein et al., 2023), which provides the theoretical framework for steering generation via a custom energy function without modifying model weights; (2) emergent semantic correspondence in diffusion models (DIFT, Tang et al., 2023), which the paper repurposes to align reference and context features before style injection; and (3) attention-swap appearance transfer (Cross-Image, Alaluf et al., 2023), whose K/V replacement is absorbed as a regularisation term rather than the primary mechanism. The key departure from prior style and appearance transfer work is the explicit unification of both under a single 'Semantic Style Transfer' task, where the energy function's Style Feature Guidance uses DIFT-style correspondence to decide *where* to inject features, rather than applying global style or point-wise appearance blindly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By operating purely as a sampler, Semantix avoids the training cost and architectural modifications of prior diffusion-based transfer methods, and its design transfers directly from image to video models (e.g., AnimateDiff) without retraining—addressing a gap where video style transfer has typically required dedicated temporal modules. The main limitation is reliance on UNet-based diffusion models and reduced effectiveness when the context image already carries a strong inherent style. Open questions include whether the semantic correspondence mechanism generalises to DiT/UViT backbones (where feature geometry differs substantially) and whether the energy-guided formulation can be extended to multi-reference or video-to-video transfer with temporal style drift.
