## Problem

Existing backprop-through-ODE guidance methods for flow matching (D-Flow, FlowGrad) lack systematic convergence guarantees and explicit regularization mechanisms, and are restricted to Euclidean space. There is no theoretically grounded framework for guided flow matching on non-Euclidean manifolds such as SO(3), which is essential for high-stakes scientific applications like protein and peptide design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250333/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained flow model (Rectified Flow for CelebA-HQ faces, EquiFM for QM9 molecules, PepFlow for peptide backbones), an initial noise sample, and a task-specific reward function (CLIP score for text alignment, MAE for molecular properties, MadraX energy for peptides). It outputs guided samples by iteratively solving for additive control terms θ_t that perturb the prior vector field, with a tunable regularization parameter γ controlling the trade-off between reward optimization and fidelity to the prior distribution. On SO(3), the control terms live in the Lie algebra so(3) and the state dynamics use left-invariant vector fields.

## Method

OC-Flow formulates controlled generation as a standard optimal control problem where additive control terms θ_t perturb the prior ODE trajectory, with a quadratic running cost L(θ_t) = -½‖θ_t‖² that provably upper-bounds the KL divergence between the prior and guided distributions. The algorithm applies the Extended Method of Successive Approximations (E-MSA) to iteratively update control terms via a co-state flow μ_t computed in closed form (Euclidean) or via the adjoint equation on SO(3), yielding the update θ_t^{k+1} = βθ_t^k + η∇_{x_t}Φ(X_1^k) with convergence guarantees. The paper shows D-Flow (single control term with LBFGS) and FlowGrad (γ→∞ limit) are special cases of this framework. For SO(3), the E-MSA is extended with a rigorous convergence proof using an Extended Hamiltonian, and practical computation is enabled by decomposing the adjoint variable onto the canonical so(3) basis with vector-Jacobian products reducing complexity from O(D⁴) to O(D²). An asynchronous update scheme allows the control term to be applied at a coarser frequency than the state trajectory simulation, improving computational efficiency.

## Results

On QM9 conditional molecule generation, OC-Flow achieves the lowest MAE across all six quantum chemical properties (e.g., 1.383 Bohr³ for polarizability, 0.314 D for dipole moment), outperforming D-Flow (1.566, 0.330) and FlowGrad (2.484, 0.542). On CelebA-HQ text-guided image manipulation, OC-Flow achieves the best LPIPS (0.207) and highest CLIP score (0.302) among all methods. On peptide backbone design, OC-Flow (trans+rot) achieves the best MadraX energy (−0.263) and highest IMP (15.0%), surpassing the unconditional PepFlow baseline (−0.195, 14.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OC-Flow consistently outperforms FlowGrad on all six QM9 properties, with MAE reductions of 30–44% (e.g., 1.383 vs. 2.484 for polarizability, 0.314 vs. 0.542 for dipole moment), attributed to the explicit running cost regularization that FlowGrad lacks. Against D-Flow, OC-Flow wins on five of six properties (α, Δε, ε_HOMO, μ, c_v) but is slightly worse on ε_LUMO (342 vs. 346 meV), with the gap being small. On CelebA-HQ image manipulation, OC-Flow achieves the best LPIPS (0.207 vs. 0.302 for FlowGrad and 0.359 for StyleCLIP) and highest CLIP score (0.302), while StyleCLIP fails entirely on the 'sad' expression due to distribution shift. For peptide design, OC-Flow (trans+rot) improves MadraX energy from −0.195 (PepFlow) to −0.263 and IMP from 14.3% to 15.0%, with ablations showing that guiding both Euclidean translations and SO(3) rotations together yields the best results. The unconditional EquiFM baseline shows dramatically higher MAE (e.g., 8.969 vs. 1.383 for α), confirming that guidance is essential for conditional generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

OC-Flow unifies and generalizes the backprop-through-ODE guidance line (D-Flow, FlowGrad) by casting controlled generation as an optimal control problem governed by Pontryagin's Maximum Principle, showing both prior methods are special cases of its update rule (D-Flow as the single-control-term asynchronous limit, FlowGrad as the γ→∞ limit). It builds on the Extended Method of Successive Approximations (E-MSA) from optimal control theory to derive convergence guarantees that were absent from prior heuristic gradient-based approaches. The paper further extends this framework from Euclidean space to the SO(3) manifold via Riemannian flow matching, a setting where no prior guided flow-matching method with theoretical grounding existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit KL-divergence bound from the running cost gives practitioners a principled, tunable knob (γ) to balance reward optimization against prior faithfulness, addressing a key practical concern in controlled generation. The SO(3) extension opens the door to guided generation for high-stakes scientific tasks like protein backbone design and molecular rotation optimization, where Euclidean methods are inapplicable. Limitations include higher computational cost compared to posterior-sampling approaches, restriction to additive control terms, and the fact that the SO(3) convergence proof relies on Lipschitz assumptions that may be tight for large molecular systems. Future work on non-additive controls, parameter-space updates, and scaling to larger molecular systems are natural next steps.
