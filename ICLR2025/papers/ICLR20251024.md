## Problem

LLMs' in-context learning is inherently tied to discrete text tokens, limiting its applicability to non-textual data such as sensor readings, time series, brain fMRI signals, and graph structures that lack natural language representations. This paper asks whether LLMs can perform ICL directly on continuous vectors from diverse domains, and if so, what minimal bridging mechanism is required.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251024/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes single continuous vectors from frozen black-box encoders (e.g., 768-d NV-Embed text embeddings, 200-d fMRI PCA factors, 100-d one-hot digit encodings, Chronos time-series hidden states, Graphormer graph embeddings) and produces autoregressive text outputs from a frozen LLM. Each input data point is compressed into exactly one 'box token' — a single vector in the LLM's embedding space — replacing what would otherwise be a multi-token discrete sequence. In-context demonstrations are provided as additional box tokens paired with their textual labels or outputs, and the LLM generates the target (reconstructed text, class label, summary, caption, or numerical answer) conditioned on these projected vectors.

## Method

The core mechanism is a lightweight embedding projector (a linear matrix or 2-layer MLP following Llama's MLP architecture) that maps encoder outputs into the LLM's embedding space, producing 'box tokens' that the frozen LLM processes as if they were native token embeddings. The projector is pretrained with a simple next-token prediction objective: text is split at random sentence boundaries, the first half is encoded and projected into box tokens, and the LLM is trained to predict the second half, with gradients flowing only through the projector. For cross-modal data (time series, fMRI, graphs), pretraining uses modality-to-text reconstruction or synthetic QA pairs. Task-specific finetuning then trains the projector on structured prompts with conditional generation loss. Both the encoder and LLM remain frozen throughout, making the projector the sole trainable component — typically a few million parameters.

## Results

On text classification, finetuned Vector-ICL achieves 98.16% accuracy on SST2, 97.28% on IMDB, 85.20% on Emotion, and 81.68% on Financial Phrasebank, surpassing both few-shot ICL and soft prompt tuning on 6 of 7 datasets (losing only on Rotten Tomatoes: 88.80% vs. 93.24% for soft prompt). On summarization, finetuned Vector-ICL reaches 20.08 RougeL on XSum and 20.49 on XLSum, outperforming few-shot ICL (19.53/19.41) and soft prompt (12.84/12.70). For cross-modal tasks, Vector-ICL consistently outperforms domain-specific baselines: it beats few-shot ICL on 10-digit function regression, surpasses the finetuned Chronos encoder on time-series classification, outperforms the finetuned Graphormer on ogbg-molhiv graph classification (F1), and exceeds random baselines on fMRI text decoding and classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against few-shot ICL (Brown et al., 2020), finetuned Vector-ICL wins on all seven evaluated tasks: SST2 (98.16 vs. 91.74), IMDB (97.28 vs. 93.50), Emotion (85.20 vs. 55.20), Financial Phrasebank (81.68 vs. 71.78), Rotten Tomatoes (88.80 vs. 87.31), XSum (20.08 vs. 19.53), and XLSum (20.49 vs. 19.41), with the largest margin on Emotion (+30 points). Against soft prompt tuning (Li & Liang, 2021), finetuned Vector-ICL wins on six of seven tasks, with particularly large gains on summarization (XSum: 20.08 vs. 12.84; XLSum: 20.49 vs. 12.70) and Emotion classification (85.20 vs. 74.15), but loses on Rotten Tomatoes (88.80 vs. 93.24). For cross-modal tasks, Vector-ICL outperforms the domain-specific Chronos encoder with a trained classification head on time-series classification (FordA, FordB) and surpasses the finetuned Graphormer baseline on ogbg-molhiv graph classification (F1 score), demonstrating that a frozen LLM with a lightweight projector can match or exceed purpose-built models. Pretrained (unfinetuned) Vector-ICL already matches or outperforms few-shot ICL on 4 of 6 directly comparable tasks, confirming that the next-token-prediction pretraining objective alone is sufficient to enable vector-context ICL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning paradigm (Brown et al., 2020) from discrete text tokens to continuous vector representations from arbitrary modalities, using lightweight projectors trained with next-token prediction to bridge black-box encoder outputs into an LLM's embedding space. Conceptually, it is distinct from soft prompt tuning (Li & Liang, 2021), which learns virtual tokens within the LLM, and from multimodal ICL work (Wu et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2024), which typically requires modality-specific adapters or end-to-end training. The key departure is showing that a simple linear or two-layer MLP projector, trained only with a language modeling objective, is sufficient to enable ICL on projected embeddings from text, time series, graphs, molecules, and brain fMRI—without modifying the LLM or encoder at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that this provides a general-purpose, lightweight interface pattern for connecting any pretrained encoder to any LLM for in-context learning, potentially eliminating the need for modality-specific architectures or fine-tuning the LLM itself. The fMRI decoding result—where an LLM extracts meaningful semantic content from 200-dimensional PCA factors of brain activity using only unsupervised next-token prediction pretraining—is particularly striking and suggests LLMs can interpret structured continuous representations far beyond their text training distribution. Key limitations include the restriction to single-token encoders (variable-length embeddings remain unexplored), testing on only four LLMs, and the absence of instruction-tuning analysis. Open questions include whether this approach scales to longer sequences, more complex encoder outputs, and whether the projector's learned structure generalizes across encoder families.
