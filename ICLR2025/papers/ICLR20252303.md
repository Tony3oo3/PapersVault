## Problem

Current LLM-based QA systems suffer from unfaithful hallucination (answers inconsistent with real-time or domain-specific facts), weak multi-step reasoning over heterogeneous information sources, and inefficient token/interaction usage. Existing retrieval-augmented approaches either retrieve without verifying conflicts with the model's parametric knowledge, or reason without grounding in external data, and none efficiently detect when the LLM already possesses sufficient internal knowledge to skip retrieval.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single complex user question as text input and first produces a structured action chain (a sequence of nodes, each containing an assigned action type, a sub-question, a binary missing flag, and an initial guess answer) via in-context prompting. After executing retrieval, verification, and imputation on each node, the system outputs a final grounded text answer. The framework currently handles two data modalities—text (web pages, domain corpora) and tabular data (market databases via generated SQL)—with the action chain serving as an intermediate structured representation that decouples question decomposition from information retrieval.

## Method

CoA decomposes a complex question into an action chain via a single in-context prompt that assigns each sub-question one of three plug-and-play actions (web-querying, knowledge-encoding, data-analyzing) and flags whether the LLM's parametric knowledge suffices (missing flag). Each action executes a three-step pipeline: (i) embedding-based retrieval of top-k relevant content, (ii) conflict verification using a proposed Multi-Reference Faith Score (MRFS) that combines token-level Precision, Recall, and Average Word Length against multiple retrieved references to decide whether to overwrite the LLM's guess answer, and (iii) imputation of missing answers when the flag is set. A key efficiency mechanism is knowledge-boundary detection: when the missing flag is 'False' and MRFS exceeds a threshold, the system skips retrieval entirely, reducing LLM interactions and token usage. The final answer is generated by prompting the LLM with the fully processed (corrected and imputed) action chain, enabling parallel sub-question resolution rather than sequential retrieval.

## Results

CoA achieves the highest accuracy in 12 out of 14 test scenarios across 7 classical QA datasets, 1 fact-checking dataset (FEVER), and 1 long-form QA dataset (ASQA). With information retrieval, CoA (MRFS in verification) reaches 70.7% on WebQuestions, 98.6% on General Knowledge, 83.1% on Social QA, 69.7% on FEVER, and 60.9% GPT-EM on ASQA, outperforming the best baseline SearchChain by 6.14% on average. Without retrieval, CoA w/o actions still leads with 64.7% on WebQuestions and 80.2% on Social QA, a 3.42% improvement over SearchChain w/o IR. CoA also reduces LLM interactions to 4.0–4.4 per question (vs. 5.0–6.7 for baselines) and cuts token usage by roughly 50–70% compared to ReAct and SearchChain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoA outperforms all 11 baselines in 12 of 14 evaluation scenarios. Against the strongest reasoning-only baseline (SearchChain w/o IR), CoA w/o actions gains +13.9 pp on WebQuestions (64.7 vs. 50.8) and +15.3 pp on Social QA (80.2 vs. 64.9). Among retrieval-augmented baselines, CoA (MRFS) beats SearchChain by +5.4 pp on WebQuestions (70.7 vs. 65.3), +11.0 pp on Social QA (83.1 vs. 69.4), +12.4 pp on FEVER (69.7 vs. 57.3), and +3.0 pp on ASQA GPT-EM (68.9 vs. 65.9). CoA also surpasses DSP (the second-best RAG baseline) by +11.3 pp on WebQuestions and +14.9 pp on Social QA. In efficiency, CoA uses only 4.0–4.4 LLM interactions per question versus 5.0–6.7 for Self-Ask, ReAct, and SearchChain, and consumes roughly half the tokens of ReAct and SearchChain (e.g., 11,873 input tokens on WQA vs. 20,273 for ReAct and 45,388 for SearchChain). CoA is also the most resistant to external misinformation, with a misleading ratio of only 1.9–6.1% compared to 7.2–16.1% for baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoA sits at the intersection of chain-of-thought prompting (CoT, SC, ToT, Auto-CoT) and retrieval-augmented agent frameworks (ReAct, Self-Ask, SearchChain, ToolFormer, DSP). It departs from CoT-style methods by treating external retrieval not as a fixed pipeline step but as configurable 'Plug-and-Play' actions (web-querying, knowledge-encoding, data-analyzing) that the LLM selectively invokes per sub-question. Relative to existing RAG agents, CoA introduces two key differentiators: (1) a knowledge-boundary detection mechanism that lets the LLM answer sub-questions from parametric memory without triggering retrieval, and (2) a lightweight multi-reference faith score (MRFS) for verifying conflicts between generated and retrieved content, replacing the heavier sequential verification in SearchChain. The framework is training-free and relies entirely on in-context prompting with GPT-3.5-turbo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play action abstraction offers a practical template for building domain-adaptable QA systems that handle heterogeneous sources (web text, domain corpora, tabular data) without retraining, and the knowledge-boundary detection yields meaningful efficiency gains (roughly 2–3× fewer tokens and interactions than ReAct/SeChain). However, the evaluation is limited to a single backbone model (GPT-3.5-turbo), two data modalities (text and tabular), and a self-reported Web3 deployment, leaving open whether the framework's advantages persist across model families, scale to vision-language inputs, or generalize beyond the tested domains. The MRFS, while effective, is a relatively simple ROUGE-inspired heuristic whose threshold sensitivity and behavior on longer or more nuanced answers remain unexplored.
