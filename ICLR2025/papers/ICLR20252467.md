## Problem

Diffusion planning has shown strong performance in offline RL, but the design space remains poorly understood: prior work uses inconsistent choices for guided sampling (CG vs. CFG), denoising backbone (U-Net vs. Transformer), action generation (joint vs. separate), and planning strategy (dense vs. jump-step) without systematic empirical evidence. This paper asks: what actually makes a good diffusion planner, and which of the common design choices are suboptimal?

## Input / Output

At inference, the planner receives the current state s_t and generates N=50 candidate state trajectories of length H=32 with a jump-step stride M (e.g., M=4 for Kitchen, M=8 for AntMaze/Maze2D), fixing the first state to s_t during denoising. A learned critic selects the best plan, and an MLP-based diffusion inverse dynamics model maps (s_t, s_{t+M}) to the executable action a_t. At training, the offline dataset D provides (state, action, reward) tuples from which the planner, inverse dynamics, and critic are trained jointly for 1M gradient steps each.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a systematic empirical study (6,000+ trained models) that identifies the optimal configuration for diffusion planning, packaged as the Diffusion Veteran (DV) baseline. DV uses a DiT1D Transformer (2 blocks, hidden dim 256) as the denoising backbone, which outperforms U-Net1D in 8 of 9 sub-tasks by capturing long-range temporal credit assignment visible in attention maps. Action generation is decoupled: the diffusion model plans states only, and a separate MLP diffusion inverse dynamics model computes actions, which significantly outperforms joint state-action generation in high-dimensional action spaces (Kitchen, AntMaze). Jump-step planning with stride >1 (e.g., 4 or 8) consistently beats dense-step planning by extending the effective lookahead horizon. For guided sampling, Monte Carlo sampling with selection (MCSS) using a learned critic outperforms both classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance when the dataset contains sufficient near-optimal trajectories, eliminating the need for task-specific guidance hyperparameters.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, Diffusion Veteran (DV) achieves state-of-the-art normalized performance across all three task suites: 83.8 on Kitchen (vs. 72.5 for HD, the next-best diffusion planner), 83.2 on AntMaze (vs. 80.5 for DQL*), and 163.6 on Maze2D (vs. 159.8 for DQL*). DV outperforms all prior diffusion planners (Diffuser, AdaptDiffuser, DD, HD) and diffusion policies (DQL, IDQL, SfBC) on Kitchen and Maze2D, and matches or exceeds the best diffusion policy (DQL*) on AntMaze. On the Adroit Hand validation set, DV achieves an average score of 69.5, competitive with IDQL (71.0) and DD (71.2) while requiring only one-quarter of the inference candidates and no task-specific hyperparameter tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DV outperforms all diffusion planners on Kitchen: it achieves 83.8 average vs. HD (72.5), DD (65.8), Diffuser (54.1), and AdaptDiffuser (53.7), with the largest gap on Kitchen-Partial (94.0 vs. HD's 73.3). On Maze2D, DV leads with 163.6 average, surpassing DQL* (159.8), AdaptDiffuser (144.3), HD (139.9), and Diffuser (119.5); the advantage is most pronounced on Maze2D-Large (203.6 vs. DQL*'s 186.8). On AntMaze, DV (83.2) edges out DQL* (80.5) and IDQL (75.2), though HD achieves a higher score on the single AntMaze-Medium-Diverse sub-task (88.7 vs. DV's 87.4) but does not report all sub-tasks. Against non-diffusion baselines, DV dominates: it exceeds IQL (48.7/57.1/47.0) by 35–117 points across the three suites, and outperforms CQL (50.4/36.4/7.7) by even larger margins. On the Adroit Hand validation set, DV (69.5) is competitive with IDQL (71.0) and DD (71.2) but notably outperforms Diffuser (58.9) and DQL (44.8), while requiring only 50 MCSS candidates versus DD's grid-searched 35 configurations and no task-specific guidance strength tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic empirical study of diffusion planning for offline RL, building directly on the Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) and Decision Diffuser (Ajay et al., 2022) frameworks and the CleanDiffuser library (Dong et al., 2024b). While prior work has proposed various diffusion planners with inconsistent design choices (U-Net vs. Transformer, CG vs. CFG, joint vs. separate action generation, dense vs. jump-step planning), no prior study had systematically ablated these components under controlled conditions. The paper departs from the prevailing assumption that classifier guidance is essential for reward maximization and that U-Net is the natural denoising backbone, instead showing that simpler alternatives (MCSS, Transformer) can be superior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive design-space analysis of diffusion planners (6,000+ trained models), yielding practical guidance that directly challenges common practice in the field. The proposed DV baseline is simple yet achieves state-of-the-art results, giving the community a strong reference point. Open questions remain around computational efficiency of diffusion planners, interpretability and safety of generated plans, whether scaling laws hold for decision-making transformers, and how to integrate diffusion planning (System 2) with diffusion policy (System 1) in a synergistic architecture. The findings are limited to state-based MDPs on D4RL and Adroit Hand, leaving vision-based and partially observable settings unexplored.
