## Problem

The internal parameter mechanism by which aligned LLMs distinguish malicious from normal queries remains unexplored, and fine-tuning—even with entirely benign data—can severely degrade their security alignment. Existing defenses such as freezing discrete security-critical neurons fail to prevent this degradation, leaving a critical gap in both understanding and protecting LLM security during downstream adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253534/cropped_page4_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an aligned LLM's hidden-layer parameters and paired normal/malicious query sets, extracting last-position vectors at every layer during only the first autoregressive step (no full sequence generation needed). It outputs a precise contiguous layer range (e.g., layers 6–12 for Llama-3-8B-Instruct, layers 11–15 for Phi-3-mini-4k-instruct) designated as safety layers, which is then used to freeze those parameters during fine-tuning. The localization process requires generating only 4–8 tokens per query to determine the model's refusal propensity, keeping the pipeline computationally lightweight.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline for identifying and leveraging safety layers. First, layer-wise cosine similarity analysis compares N-N versus N-M query pairs across all hidden layers; the emergence of a significant angular gap between these curves at middle layers reveals the approximate safety layer range, while pre-trained (unaligned) LLMs show no such gap, confirming the layers arise from security alignment. Second, precise localization scales the weights of candidate layers by a factor α and uses the over-rejection phenomenon (refusal of benign queries containing dangerous verbs) as a sensitive security metric, progressively expanding the scaled range until over-rejection peaks (α>1) or bottoms out (α<1) to pin down exact upper and lower boundaries. The practical application, SPPFT (Safely Partial-Parameter Fine-Tuning), freezes all parameters within the identified safety layer range during fine-tuning, updating only the remaining layers. This preserves the model's refusal behavior against malicious queries while maintaining fine-tuning task performance (Rouge-L, MMLU, and AlpacaEval 2.0 scores remain comparable to full fine-tuning), and ablations confirm that freezing non-safety layers does not provide equivalent protection.

## Results

SPPFT (freezing safety layers during fine-tuning) dramatically preserves LLM security across all four aligned LLMs and three attack scenarios. Under the implicit attack (D_I), SPPFT increases harmful rate by only 2.84% on average (e.g., Llama-3-8B-Instruct: 5.77%→6.15%), while full fine-tuning causes a 58.03% increase (5.77%→42.69%) and freezing discrete security-critical neurons (NFFT) yields 41.92%. Under the backdoor attack (D_B), SPPFT limits harmful rate to 8.27% on Llama-3-8B-Instruct versus 52.50% for FullFT. Performance metrics (Rouge-L, MMLU, AlpacaEval 2.0) remain nearly identical between SPPFT and FullFT, confirming no quality trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NFFT (freezing discrete security-critical neurons from Wei et al., 2024), SPPFT wins decisively across all four LLMs and all three attack scenarios: under the implicit attack, SPPFT keeps harmful rate at 3.27–9.04% while NFFT reaches 38.46–81.35%, a gap of 30–75 percentage points. Against Lisa (Huang et al., 2024b) on the harmful data attack, SPPFT achieves 7.9–78.8% harmful rate versus Lisa's 20.7–87.4% across p=0.05–0.2, with the advantage widening as malicious data proportion increases (e.g., at p=0.2 on LLaMA-2-chat: SPPFT 68.3% vs. Lisa 80.3%). Against standard Reverse DPO (Yi et al., 2024a), freezing safety layers reduces harmful rate from 26.5–41.6% to 10.8–16.4% across four LLMs. In all comparisons, SPPFT also matches or slightly exceeds FullFT on task performance (Rouge-L, MMLU, AlpacaEval 2.0), confirming that the security gains come without a quality penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the discrete-neuron perspective of Wei et al. (2024), who identified scattered 'security-critical neurons' in aligned LLMs, by showing that security is instead concentrated in a small set of contiguous middle layers. It builds on the fine-tuning jailbreak literature (Qi et al. 2023; Yang et al. 2023; Kumar et al. 2024) that established the vulnerability of aligned LLMs to parameter-level attacks, and repurposes the over-rejection phenomenon (Cui et al. 2024; Arditi et al. 2024) as a novel localization metric rather than merely a failure mode. The mechanistic interpretability toolkit (layer-wise cosine similarity, attention heatmaps) is applied here in a new context, and the paper explicitly distinguishes its 'safety layers' from the concurrent but differently-defined concept in Zhao et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of safety layers provides the first layer-wise mechanistic account of where security alignment resides in LLM parameters, offering a concrete and simple defense (freeze those layers during fine-tuning) that is easy to deploy in both open-source and fine-tuning-as-a-service settings. However, the study is limited to four relatively small models (2B–8B parameters), and the three-stage layer division (syntactic → safety detection → semantic) remains preliminary and untested at scale. Open questions include whether safety layers generalize to much larger or multimodal models, how they interact with other alignment objectives (helpfulness, honesty), and whether an attacker could learn to circumvent frozen safety layers through more sophisticated fine-tuning strategies.
