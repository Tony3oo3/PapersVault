## Problem

Standard RL methods assume enumerable action spaces, but many real-world sequential planning problems (e.g., dispatching health workers under travel, scheduling, or capacity constraints) have exponentially large combinatorial action spaces where each action couples multiple arms. Existing restless bandit solutions like the Whittle index policy require arms to be independently decoupled, which breaks down when actions have joint constraints (path feasibility, bipartite matching, compounding interventions). This paper introduces CORMAB, a class of strongly coupled restless bandits with combinatorial actions, and proposes the first algorithm to solve them.

## Input / Output

The Q-network takes a joint state vector (discrete states for all J arms) and a binary action vector of length N as input, outputting a scalar Q-value for that state-action pair—unlike standard DQN which outputs one value per action. At inference, the trained Q-network is embedded into a MILP alongside the combinatorial constraints (budget, path, matching, capacity), and the solver returns the optimal binary action vector. The action space is combinatorial (e.g., C(N,B) subsets or valid paths), making enumeration infeasible; the MILP handles feasibility while the network provides the learned objective.

## Method

SEQUOIA integrates a deep Q-network into a mixed-integer linear program by encoding each ReLU hidden unit as a set of linear and big-M constraints (following Fischetti & Jo, 2018), so that solving the MILP simultaneously enforces combinatorial action constraints and maximizes the predicted Q-value. The Q-network is trained with a modified DQN procedure: instead of enumerating all actions to compute the max-Q target, the algorithm solves the MILP (with the target network's weights) to obtain the greedy action for both on-policy sampling and TD target computation. To address the computational cost of repeated MILP solves (E×H×M per training run), the method warm-starts the Q-network with myopic reward fitting, seeds training with perturbed and infeasible actions for diversity, and memoizes MILP solutions between target-network updates. The approach also incorporates double DQN, prioritized experience replay, and gradient clipping, and generalizes across four problem settings (multiple interventions, bipartite matching, capacity constraints, path constraints) without per-domain architectural changes.

## Results

SEQUOIA achieves an average 24.8% improvement in normalized per-timestep reward over all existing baselines across four CORMAB problem settings (multiple interventions, path constraints, bipartite matching/scheduling, and capacity constraints), evaluated with J ∈ {20, 40, 100} arms and N ∈ {5, 10, 20} workers over 50 episodes of length H=20 (averaged over 30 seeds). In the capacity-constrained setting, SEQUOIA outperforms the strongest iterative baseline (ITERATIVE DQN) by 12.1% on average. The SAMPLING heuristic (k=100 random actions) performs comparably to MYOPIC at J=20 but degrades dramatically at J=100, where the action space grows from ~15,504 to ~5.4×10²⁰ feasible actions. Results are normalized so that the RANDOM baseline equals 1 on the y-axis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SEQUOIA against six baselines: NO ACTION (null action, lower bound), RANDOM (uniform feasible action, normalized to 1), SAMPLING (He et al., 2016: sample k=100 actions, pick best myopic reward), MYOPIC (MILP optimizing immediate expected reward), ITERATIVE MYOPIC (greedy component-wise construction using one-step reward), and ITERATIVE DQN (greedy component-wise construction using the trained Q-network). SEQUOIA consistently outperforms all baselines across all four problem settings and all problem sizes. The gap is most pronounced against the iterative baselines: ITERATIVE DQN, which uses the same Q-network but decomposes action selection greedily, underperforms SEQUOIA by 12.1% on average in the capacity-constrained setting and by larger margins elsewhere. The SAMPLING baseline (He et al., 2016) is competitive with MYOPIC at J=20 but falls dramatically behind at J=100, illustrating the intractability of random sampling in exponentially large combinatorial spaces. MYOPIC, while optimal in a static setting, is consistently outperformed by SEQUOIA, confirming the importance of sequential long-horizon planning. Notably, no iterative baseline exists for the path-constrained setting, as there is no simple greedy decomposition for selecting a valid path.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEQUOIA sits at the intersection of two research threads: deep Q-learning (Mnih, 2013) and the encoding of ReLU neural networks into mixed-integer programs (Fischetti & Jo, 2018; Serra et al., 2018). The closest prior work is Delarue et al. (2020), which also embeds a Q-network into a MIP for combinatorial action selection, but in a single-shot deterministic setting rather than a stochastic sequential one. The paper departs from the dominant restless bandit literature (Whittle index policies, Lagrangian decompositions) by explicitly targeting strongly coupled action structures where per-arm decoupling is impossible, and from iterative RL-for-combinatorial-optimization approaches (Dai et al., 2017; Barrett et al., 2020) by avoiding the decomposition of combinatorial actions into sequential single-component choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a previously intractable class of sequential decision problems—restless bandits with combinatorial action constraints (CORMAB)—and provides the first general-purpose algorithm to solve them, with practical implications for public health resource allocation, scheduling, and routing. The major open question is computational scalability: the MILP must be solved at every timestep during both training and inference (hundreds of thousands of solves even for modest instances), and the authors acknowledge that network size is tightly constrained by solver runtime. Future work on reducing MILP calls (e.g., the IPOPT relaxation heuristic mentioned in the conclusion), incorporating problem structure via GNNs, and extending beyond restless bandits to general MDPs with combinatorial actions are natural next steps.
