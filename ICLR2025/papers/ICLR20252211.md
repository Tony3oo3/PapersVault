## Problem

Standard conformal p-values (Bates et al., 2023) target the joint distribution of (X, Y) and thus cannot detect deviations that are purely conditional — e.g., outliers whose Y|X distribution differs from the training data while the marginal distribution of Y remains unchanged. This paper defines localized conformal p-values that capture such conditional distributional deviations, enabling valid and powerful testing in settings where the signal is encoded in Y|X rather than in Y marginally.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled training set split into training and calibration subsets, a test set (labeled for outlier detection and two-sample testing, unlabeled for label screening), a kernel function H(·,·) with bandwidth h, a problem-specific non-conformity score V(x,y), and a nominal level α. For each test point, an auxiliary covariate X̃ is randomly drawn from H(X_2j,·), introducing external randomness that is essential for finite-sample validity. Outputs are application-specific: a rejection set with finite-sample FDR ≤ α (outlier detection), per-component screening decisions with finite-sample FWER ≤ α (label screening), or a standardized U-statistic with asymptotic N(0,1) under H₀ (two-sample test).

## Method

The core construction inverts the randomly localized conformal prediction interval of Hore & Barber (2024): the localized conformal p-value p_L,j is a kernel-weighted rank of the test score V_2j among calibration scores, where weights are H(X_1i, X̃_2j) and X̃_2j ~ H(X_2j,·). This yields finite-sample validity under exchangeability and an O(h) deviation bound under covariate shift, with the p-value function converging to 1 − F_{V|X=x}(v) rather than the marginal 1 − F_V(v), giving power in regions of small conditional score variance. For conditional outlier detection, the authors combine the LCP with the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure and a conditional-calibration pruning step (Fithian & Lei, 2022) to achieve finite-sample FDR control despite the loss of PRDS in weighted p-values. For conditional label screening, a group-superuniform property of the LCP across response components yields finite-sample FWER control. For the two-sample conditional distribution test, a simplified (non-randomized) LCP is aggregated into a kernel-weighted U-statistic whose asymptotic normality under H₀ and local alternatives is established, with a signal-strength term δ_w that conditions on the same covariate value, better capturing conditional deviations than the marginal counterpart in Hu & Lei (2023).

## Results

On the Diabetes Health Indicators dataset (n=500, m=2000, α=0.05), the LCP-ls screening procedure achieves conditional FWER of 0.0329 (LL) and 0.0372 (RF), well below the nominal level, while the unweighted THR baseline inflates to 0.0740 and 0.0887 respectively; both methods maintain marginal FWER near 0.05. For conditional outlier detection (Scenario A1, m=2000, α=0.15), LCP-od achieves power approaching 1 as n grows to 4000, while CP power remains 'stuck' around a fixed value due to its inability to detect outliers in regions of small conditional variance. On the Airfoil dataset (two-sample test, α=0.05), LCT controls type I error at 0.054 (LL) and 0.053 (NN) under the null, while CT inflates to 0.441 under NN; under the alternative, LCT yields the smallest median p-value (0.000) compared to CT (0.022) and DCT (0.018) with NN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unweighted conformal p-value (CP, Bates et al. 2023) for conditional outlier detection, LCP-od achieves substantially higher power (approaching 1 at n=4000 vs. CP stuck at a fixed level) while both maintain valid FDR control at the nominal level; the advantage is most pronounced in regions where the conditional variance of the score is small. For the two-sample conditional distribution test, LCT outperforms CT (Hu & Lei 2023) on type I error control: CT inflates severely (e.g., 0.441 rejection rate vs. 0.05 nominal) under misspecified NN models, while LCT stays at 0.053. LCT also beats DCT (Chen & Lei 2024) on power in Scenarios A3 and B3 (especially at larger n) and is simpler to implement since it avoids the cross-fitting step required by DCT. For conditional label screening, LCP-ls reduces conditional FWER by more than half compared to the unweighted THR baseline (0.0329 vs. 0.0740 with LL; 0.0372 vs. 0.0887 with RF) while both maintain valid marginal FWER, demonstrating the key advantage of local weighting in balancing error rates across covariate subgroups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the randomly localized conformal prediction intervals of Hore & Barber (2024) and Guan (2023) to construct localized conformal p-values, then deploys them across three conditional testing problems. It departs from Bates et al. (2023), whose conformal p-values target the marginal joint distribution and thus miss conditional outliers, by showing that kernel-weighted p-values capture deviations in Y|X that marginal p-values cannot. For the two-sample conditional distribution test, it replaces the density-ratio-weighted U-statistic of Hu & Lei (2023) and its de-biased variant of Chen & Lei (2024) with a kernel-weighted aggregation of localized p-values, inheriting model-agnosticism while gaining robustness to misspecified training models. The conditional calibration technique of Fithian & Lei (2022) is adopted to achieve finite-sample FDR control despite the loss of PRDS in the weighted setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap: marginal conformal p-values are provably insufficient for conditional testing, yet no prior work provided a distribution-free, finite-sample-valid localized alternative with explicit error-rate guarantees (FDR, FWER, type I error). The conditional label screening formulation is genuinely new and directly applicable to LLM factuality checking, where per-claim screening with conditional reliability is needed. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include the randomization step (which introduces instability and uses a sampled covariate rather than the test point itself) and the reduced effective sample size that hurts power at small n. An open question is whether a non-randomized localized p-value can retain finite-sample validity, which would resolve both the stability and local-information issues simultaneously.
