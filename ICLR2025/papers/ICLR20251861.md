## Problem

Learned image compression models suffer from extremely slow training convergence, requiring over 2 million iterations (15+ GPU-days) per rate-distortion point. The paper identifies the root cause as the difficulty of nonlinear transforms in progressively achieving energy compaction—specifically feature decorrelation and uneven energy modulation—during training, a dynamic process that prior work had not addressed.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (trained on ImageNet-1k, evaluated on Kodak at 768×512, Tecnick at 1200×1200, and CLIC at up to 2K resolution) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image. The AuxT module is plug-and-play: it adds roughly 1M parameters and 1–10 GMACs of inference overhead to existing LIC architectures without modifying the entropy model or the nonlinear transform itself.

## Method

The paper proposes an Auxiliary Transform (AuxT), a lightweight purely-linear bypass branch that runs in parallel with the nonlinear analysis/synthesis transforms to handle coarse energy compaction. Each stage of AuxT is a Wavelet-Based Linear Shortcut (WLS) comprising three sequential operations: Haar DWT for spatial downsampling and frequency-domain decorrelation, subband-aware scaling with four learnable per-channel vectors (initialized to 1, 0.5, 0.5, 0 for LL/LH/HL/HH) to progressively amplify low-frequency energy, and an Orthogonal Linear Projection (OLP) enforcing ‖WᵀW−I‖²_F to guarantee channel decorrelation. Four stacked WLSs in the encoder and four inverse WLSs (iWLS) in the decoder add their outputs to the corresponding downsampling/upsampling layers of the nonlinear transform, so the nonlinear branch is freed to learn fine-detail distributions. Training uses the standard R-D loss plus a small orthogonality regularization (λ_orth = 0.1), and the entire system is optimized end-to-end, yielding ~2× faster convergence with an average 1.3% BD-rate improvement across TCM, ELIC, STF, and mbt2018mean.

## Results

The proposed AuxT achieves approximately 2× training speedup (40–68% reduction in GPU hours) across all tested LIC models while maintaining or improving BD-rate. For example, TCM-large+AuxT trained for only 1M iterations reaches −11.3% BD-rate (vs. VTM-18.0), surpassing the anchor TCM-large trained for 2M iterations (−10.6%) with 47% less training time. Similarly, STF+AuxT at 1M iterations achieves −5.7% BD-rate, a 2.5% improvement over the 2M-iteration anchor (−3.2%). The average BD-rate reduction across all models at the 1M-iteration setting is approximately 1.3% compared to their 2M-iteration counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper applies AuxT as a plug-and-play module to four anchor LIC models: mbt2018mean (Minnen et al., 2018), ELIC (He et al., 2022), STF (Zou et al., 2022), and TCM (Liu et al., 2023). In every case, the +AuxT variant trained for 1M iterations outperforms the corresponding anchor trained for 2M iterations in BD-rate: mbt2018mean+AuxT improves by 2.9%, ELIC+AuxT by 1.2%, STF+AuxT by 2.5%, TCM-small+AuxT by 0.7%, and TCM-large+AuxT by 0.7%, all while using roughly half the training time. At the 0.6M-iteration setting, AuxT already narrows the gap to the 2M anchor to within 0–0.9% BD-rate, with STF+AuxT matching the 2M anchor exactly (−3.2%). The additional inference cost is minimal (1–10 GMACs, ~1–3 ms latency), and parameter overhead is approximately 1M. Results are consistent across Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC datasets, with BD-rate improvements of 0.4–2.2% at the 1M-iteration setting on the latter two.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the nonlinear transform coding framework for learned image compression (Ballé et al., 2018; 2020) and prior observations that converged LIC models exhibit energy compaction in their latent representations (Duan et al., 2022; He et al., 2022; Li et al., 2024c). However, it departs from this line by analyzing the *training dynamics* of energy compaction rather than its converged state, identifying two specific bottlenecks—slow feature decorrelation and uneven energy modulation—that cause slow convergence. The proposed auxiliary transform (AuxT) repurposes classical Haar DWT and orthogonality regularization (Le et al., 2011; Wang et al., 2020) in a novel bypass-branch architecture, keeping the nonlinear transform unmodified while offloading coarse energy compaction to a lightweight linear path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training a state-of-the-art LIC model for a single R-D point takes over two weeks, which is a major barrier to practical deployment, fine-tuning for new datasets, and rapid iteration. The plug-and-play nature of AuxT (adding ~1M parameters and 1–10 GMACs) makes it immediately applicable to any existing LIC architecture, and the consistent ~2× speedup with comparable or better BD-rate across four diverse models (CNN and transformer-based) demonstrates broad utility. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video compression, whether it composes with other training-acceleration techniques (e.g., subspace training), and whether the Haar wavelet choice is optimal for all architectures. The finding that adding nonlinearity to AuxT degrades performance suggests a broader principle that linear energy-preserving transforms may be preferable for structured energy compaction tasks.
