## Problem

LoRA adapters are intrinsically bound to their base model and cannot be transferred to a different (e.g., updated or distilled) base model without retraining on the original dataset, which is often inaccessible due to privacy or licensing constraints. No prior method enables training-free, data-free transfer of parameter-efficient adapters across different foundation model architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250354/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The transfer procedure consumes only the pretrained weight matrices of both the source and target base models (no training data, no synthetic data, no forward passes through either model). It produces a set of LoRA-X weight matrices for the target model's attention modules, which are then applied at inference time alongside a text prompt to generate images (512×512 or 1024×1024 depending on the SD family) or text. The transfer is a closed-form projection: for same-dimension modules it is a single matrix multiplication, and for different-dimension modules it involves a least-squares linear transformation of the singular vectors.

## Method

LoRA-X constrains the adapter to the SVD subspace of the base model by parameterizing the weight update as ΔW = Û ΔΣ Ṽᵀ, where Û and Ṽ are the truncated left/right singular matrices of the pretrained weight W₀ (frozen during training) and only the diagonal matrix ΔΣ of singular-value deltas is learned. For training-free transfer, the source adapter is projected into the target model's subspace via ΔW_{t←s} = U_t U_tᵀ ΔW_s V_t V_tᵀ, which rotates ΔΣ_s into a direction of maximum subspace alignment between source and target. A Frobenius-norm subspace similarity metric Φ(A,B) = ‖U_Aᵀ U_B‖²_F / n identifies which source modules map to which target modules, and an optimal-transport formulation over the pairwise similarity matrix yields a scalar Adapter Transferability Cost (ATC) that quantifies cross-architecture transfer difficulty. Modules with low subspace similarity are filtered out, and for dimension-mismatched modules a minimum-Frobenius-norm linear transformation approximates the singular-vector alignment.

## Results

On the BlueFire dataset, training-free transferred LoRA-X achieves HPSv2 scores within ±5% of the trained-from-scratch baseline across all four target models (e.g., 0.307 vs. 0.296 on SD Eff-v1.0, +3.6%), with DINOv2 similarity consistently above 0.85. On Paintings, transferred LoRA-X shows slightly larger LPIPS diversity drops (up to −13.2% on SSD-1B) but maintains HPSv2 within ±3%. The transferred adapter is only 0.16 MB (rank 320 singular values) versus 34.07 MB for a standard LoRA of rank 32, a 213× size reduction. In text generation (TinyLlama 3T→2.5T), transferred LoRA-X actually outperforms the trained version, achieving BLEU 0.8819 vs. 0.8612 and ROUGE-L 0.9874 vs. 0.9349.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), LoRA-X demonstrates a decisive advantage in transferability: when projected onto the target subspace, LoRA-X (rank 320, 0.16 MB) improves HPSv2 by +3.7% over its trained baseline, while LoRA (rank 32, 34.07 MB) degrades by −27.8%, and even LoRA at rank 1 (1.15 MB) still drops −12.5%. Against DoRA (Liu et al., 2024), LoRA-X transfers with smaller HPSv2 degradation (−2.0% to +3.6% for LoRA-X vs. −9.1% to −10.7% for DoRA) and achieves higher DINOv2 scores (0.851 vs. 0.914–0.944). Against FouRA (Borse et al., 2024), LoRA-X again shows smaller transfer gaps (−2.0% to +3.6% vs. −4.7% HPSv2 for FouRA). Against X-Adapter (Ran et al., 2023), LoRA-X achieves a higher DINOv2 score (0.953 vs. 0.892) and 4.6× faster inference (3.7 s vs. 17.1 s), though X-Adapter has a slightly smaller HPSv2 drop (−7.8% vs. −9.5%); however, X-Adapter requires training on the target model while LoRA-X is fully training-free.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoRA-X extends the LoRA framework (Hu et al., 2022) by constraining the adapter to the SVD subspace of the base model, making it inherently transferable across model versions without retraining. It is conceptually related to SVDiff (Han et al., 2023), which also modifies singular values, but diverges in objective: SVDiff targets parameter reduction during fine-tuning, while LoRA-X targets cross-model transferability. The paper departs from prior adapter-transfer methods—X-Adapter (Ran et al., 2023) and Trans-LoRA (Wang et al., 2024)—which both require training data (synthetic or original) on the target model, by formulating transfer as a closed-form subspace projection problem augmented with an optimal-transport module-matching step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is compelling: when a base model is deprecated, all associated LoRA adapters become unusable without access to the original training data, which is often unavailable due to privacy or licensing constraints. LoRA-X's training-free transfer (0.16 MB adapter vs. 34 MB for standard LoRA) offers a lightweight solution, and the proposed Adapter Transferability Cost (ATC) metric provides a useful diagnostic for predicting transfer difficulty. However, the method's effectiveness is concentrated within model families (SD-v1.5 family, SDXL family); cross-family transfer shows substantially higher ATC and degraded performance, leaving cross-architecture transfer as a significant open problem. Extension to LLMs and acceleration adapters (e.g., LCM-LoRA) is identified as future work but remains unvalidated at scale.
