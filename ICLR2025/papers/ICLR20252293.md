## Problem

Existing molecular relational learning methods either extract substructures independently from each molecule (ignoring inter-molecular influence) or condition substructure extraction on the entire partner graph (introducing redundant noise). No prior framework iteratively refines substructures based on the *core substructures* of the interacting partner, and no information-theoretic objective specifically governs the interactive compression of paired substructures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252293/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of molecular graphs (each represented as an atom-bond graph with 8 atomic and 4 bond features) and an interaction label Y (a scalar for solvation/fluorescence regression or a binary class for DDI prediction). Beyond the predicted interaction value, the model outputs node-level importance scores for both molecules that define the extracted interactive substructures; these are pooled via Set2Set into fixed-dimensional representation vectors z_{G_s1} and z_{G_s2} that encode the essential interactive information.

## Method

IGIB-ISE frames substructure extraction as an EM problem where one molecule's substructure G_s1 serves as model parameters and the other's G_s2 as latent variables, iteratively refining both through E-steps (estimate G_s2 given frozen G_s1) and M-steps (update G_s1 to maximize the ELBO). The Interactive Graph Information Bottleneck (IGIB) objective minimizes −I(Y; G_s1, G_s2) + β₁·I(G₁; G_s1 | G_s2) + β₂·I(G₂; G_s2 | G_s1), conditioning on the partner's *substructure* rather than its full graph to avoid redundant information. Architecturally, GIN encoders produce node embeddings, CIGIN-style cosine-similarity interaction maps expand features cross-graph, and Gumbel-Softmax sampling with temperature τ enables differentiable node selection for substructure extraction. The training loss combines a prediction term (cross-entropy or MSE), KL-divergence compression terms (upper-bounding the conditional mutual information), and InfoNCE contrastive losses (lower-bounding I(G_s1; G_s2)) to jointly optimize the IGIB upper bound.

## Results

On the molecular interaction prediction task (RMSE, lower is better), IGIB-ISE achieves the best performance across all six datasets, with headline results of 16.90 on Chromophore Absorption, 0.572 on MNSol, 0.713 on FreeSolv, and 0.343 on Abraham. On the drug-drug interaction prediction task (ACC, higher is better), IGIB-ISE attains 88.84% on ZhangDDI, 95.56% on ChChMiner, and 96.65% on DeepDDI in the transductive setting, and maintains the best inductive performance (e.g., 70.54% on DeepDDI in Setting 2). The method consistently outperforms the strongest Category II baselines (CGIB, CMRL) by 1–2 RMSE points on solvation tasks and 0.5–1.5 ACC points on DDI tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGIB-ISE outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. Against the strongest Category II baselines, CGIB and CMRL, IGIB-ISE reduces RMSE by 1.15 on Chromophore Absorption (16.90 vs. 18.05/18.24) and by 0.205 on FreeSolv (0.713 vs. 0.918/0.927), while gaining 1.15 ACC points on ZhangDDI transductive (88.84 vs. 87.69/87.78). Category I baselines that extract substructures independently (Explainable GNN, D-MPNN, CIGIN, GoGNN, SSI-DDI, MHCADDI) are consistently outperformed by larger margins, e.g., D-MPNN lags by 7.18 RMSE on Chromophore Absorption and MHCADDI trails by 10.98 ACC on ZhangDDI transductive. DSN-DDI, the strongest DDI-specific baseline, is surpassed by 1.19 ACC on ZhangDDI transductive (88.84 vs. 87.65) and 1.37 ACC on DeepDDI inductive Setting 2 (70.54 vs. 69.17). The ablation between ISE and IGIB-ISE confirms that the IGIB loss terms contribute an additional 0.91 RMSE improvement on Chromophore Absorption and 0.39 ACC on ZhangDDI transductive over the EM framework alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB) lineage from single-graph substructure extraction (Wu et al., 2020) through Conditional GIB (CGIB, Lee et al., 2023) to a new Interactive GIB (IGIB) formulation that conditions on the *substructure* of the paired molecule rather than its full graph, thereby filtering out redundant information. The key architectural departure is framing iterative substructure extraction as an EM problem where one molecule's substructure serves as the latent variable and the other's as the model parameter, enabling mutual refinement that neither independent extraction (Category I: SSI-DDI, STNN-DDI) nor single-pass conditional extraction (Category II: CGIB, CMRL) achieves. The cross-graph interaction encoder borrows from CIGIN (Pathak et al., 2020), and the differentiable node masking follows VGIB (Yu et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IGIB-ISE framework provides a principled, interpretable mechanism for identifying which substructures drive molecular interactions, with the iterative EM process offering a transparent view of how core substructures emerge from full molecules. Consistent improvements across nine datasets in both solvation free energy regression and DDI classification, including strong inductive generalization, suggest the substructure-conditioning principle is broadly applicable. However, the framework is limited to two-molecule interactions, computational cost grows with iteration count, and applicability to macromolecular systems (protein-protein, drug-protein) remains untested. Open questions include whether the EM formulation generalizes to multi-molecule interaction networks and whether more efficient pruning strategies can reduce the iteration overhead for large-scale datasets.
