## Problem

Membership inference attacks (MIAs) against multi-modal DocVQA models have been largely unexplored at the document level. Existing approaches either target individual (document, question, answer) tuples rather than whole documents, rely on auxiliary datasets to train shadow models (infeasible for confidential document data), or depend on pre-trained cross-modal alignment models that do not exist for the DocVQA task. This paper addresses the gap by introducing the first document-level MIAs that require no auxiliary data and handle the auto-regressive output format of DocVQA models.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a target document image, a set of M associated question-answer pairs, and access to a trained DocVQA model (full parameters in white-box; API predictions only in black-box). For each question-answer pair, the method fine-tunes the model (or a single layer, or the image pixels) and records the L2-norm parameter displacement Δ, the number of optimization steps s, and the utility score u. These per-pair features are aggregated across all M questions using multiple functions (AVG, MIN, MAX, MED) to form a single document-level feature vector, which is then clustered via KMeans to assign a binary member/non-member label.

## Method

The key insight is that fine-tuning a DocVQA model on a single (document, question, answer) pair requires less parameter movement for documents already in the training set, because the model has partially converged to those answers during original training. The paper formalizes this as an optimization-based distance feature (Eq. 3) and instantiates it through three efficiency-oriented variants: FL (fine-tune one layer, typically the final FC projection), FLLoRA (fine-tune LoRA adapters on that layer), and IG (optimize the document image pixels while freezing all model weights). In the black-box setting, a proxy model initialized from a public pre-trained checkpoint is distilled on the target model's predicted answers over the test documents, after which the same white-box optimization-based features are extracted from the proxy. All features are aggregated across questions per document, normalized, concatenated, and separated into member/non-member clusters using unsupervised KMeans, with the cluster exhibiting larger average Δ identified as non-members.

## Results

In the white-box setting, DocMIA (FL variant) achieves F1 scores of 72.4% on PFL/VT5, 77.96% on PFL/Donut, 76.60% on DVQA/VT5, 82.18% on DVQA/Donut, and 72.22% on DVQA/Pix2Struct-B, consistently outperforming all baselines. In the black-box setting, the proxy-based attack (FL with VT5 proxy) reaches 82.54% F1 against Donut on DocVQA and 69.51% F1 against VT5 on PFL, with improvements of up to +4.88% F1 over the best SCORE-UA baseline. At the low-FPR regime (TPR@3% FPR), the VT5 proxy achieves 23.00% TPR against Donut and 16.67% against Pix2Struct-B on DocVQA, substantially exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DocMIA outperforms all five baseline families across nearly all target models and datasets. Against Yeom et al.'s loss/score threshold attacks (SCORE-TA, LOSS-TA), DocMIA gains up to +14.7% F1 (FL on PFL/VT5: 72.40 vs. LOSS-TA's 62.81) and +28.4% F1 (FL on DVQA/Donut: 82.18 vs. LOSS-TA's 53.46), confirming that simple loss-based signals are insufficient for DocVQA models with strong generalization. Against Tito et al.'s unsupervised score-based attacks (SCORE-UA, SCORE-UA_all), DocMIA achieves consistent gains of +5 to +12% F1 on PFL (e.g., FL: 72.40 vs. SCORE-UA_all: 60.67 on VT5) and +1 to +2% F1 on DocVQA where baselines already perform well. Against Nasr et al.'s gradient-norm attack (GRADIENT-UA), DocMIA improves F1 by up to +10% (FL on PFL/VT5: 72.40 vs. 60.67), demonstrating that single-step gradients are less discriminative than cumulative optimization distance. Against the grey-box LLM baselines Min-K% Prob (Shi et al.) and Min-K%++ (Zhang et al.), DocMIA wins on all targets, with the largest gap on PFL/VT5 (FL: 72.40 vs. Min-K%++: 72.16 F1) and PFL/Donut (FL: 77.96 vs. Min-K%++: 60.35 F1). The only marginal exceptions are DVQA/Donut where SCORELOSS-UA_all reaches 81.10 F1 (vs. FL's 82.18) and DVQA/VT5 where SCORE-TA matches FL at 75.67 ACC, but DocMIA still leads on F1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the membership inference attack (MIA) literature from standard data-point-level formulations to a document-level granularity specifically for DocVQA models, departing from prior work that targets individual (document, question, answer) tuples or, in the closest prior work (Tito et al., 2024), provider-level groups. Rather than relying on standard MIA features such as loss, logits, or single-step gradients—which are ill-suited to auto-regressive generation—the paper repurposes the optimization trajectory (parameter-space distance and convergence steps during fine-tuning) as a discriminative membership signal. The black-box variant introduces a knowledge-distillation mechanism to transfer membership-relevant behavior from a queried model to a proxy, enabling white-box attack strategies without architectural knowledge of the target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that DocVQA models—increasingly deployed in sensitive business workflows (fintech, legal, insurance)—leak document-level membership even under the realistic constraint of no auxiliary training data, a setting where shadow-model-based attacks are infeasible. This opens the door to practical privacy auditing of deployed document-processing systems. Open questions include whether the assumption of knowing (or approximating) training questions holds in practice, whether the optimization-based features generalize to generative (non-extractive) DocVQA, and what defenses (e.g., differential privacy, regularization) can mitigate the identified leakage without degrading utility.
