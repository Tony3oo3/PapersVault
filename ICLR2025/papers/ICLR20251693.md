## Problem

Training LLMs to acquire multiple agent skills simultaneously faces two compounding challenges: balancing data ratios across heterogeneous tasks without favoring any single skill, and the misalignment between standard next-token-prediction loss and actual task-specific performance metrics (e.g., pass@1, success rate). Conventional fine-tuning on mixed datasets yields only marginal gains over single-task experts, and existing model-merging baselines lack mechanisms to escape the convex region spanned by the seed models.

## Input / Output

The evolutionary process consumes a set of single-task expert models (fine-tuned from a shared base like LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct or SAM-ViT-Huge) and produces a merged composite model plus an archive of hundreds of diverse intermediate agents. For the LLM setting, the final model receives text prompts in interactive agentic environments (bash shells, SQL databases) and outputs executable code, OS commands, or SQL queries over up to 5 interaction turns. For the SAM setting, RGB images are segmented into binary masks across four distinct segmentation domains (camouflage, polyp, skin lesion, leaf).

## Method

CycleQD adapts MAP-Elites with three key modifications. First, it cyclically alternates which task metric serves as the quality objective versus a behavioral characteristic (BC) across K archives, so each generation focuses optimization on one skill while tracking the others as BCs—eliminating data-ratio tuning. Second, crossover is implemented as task-vector merging: child parameters are computed as θ_base + (ω₁/(ω₁+ω₂))·τ_parent1 + (ω₂/(ω₁+ω₂))·τ_parent2, where ω's are i.i.d. samples from N(μ,σ²) and can be negative, giving the evolutionary search freedom to extrapolate. Third, SVD-based mutation decomposes each parameter matrix in the child's task vector into UΣVᵀ and scales the singular values by a random vector w ~ Uniform[0, w_max], injecting perturbations along the principal directions of the learned skill rather than arbitrary noise. After N generations, the elite model from each archive is aggregated via softmax-weighted task-vector combination to produce the final multi-skill model.

## Results

On AgentBench computer science tasks, CycleQD applied to LLaMA3-8B-Instruct achieves an average score of 52.4 across MBPP (pass@1: 76.4), DB (success rate: 38.2), and OS (success rate: 42.6), approaching GPT-3.5-TURBO's average of 53.7. This outperforms the best merging baseline (NSGA-II, avg 51.6) and the best fine-tuning baseline (Fine-tuning All, avg 47.0) by 0.8 and 5.4 points respectively. On out-of-distribution coding tasks (HUMANEVAL+, BigCodeBench), CycleQD scores 1.10x and 1.03x the base model, while retaining language capabilities at 0.95–1.02x across reasoning, math, reading comprehension, and commonsense benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-3.5-TURBO (used as a reference, not a trained baseline), CycleQD achieves a near-parity average of 52.4 vs. 53.7, with CycleQD leading on OS (42.6 vs. 38.5) and trailing on MBPP (76.4 vs. 82.0) and DB (38.2 vs. 41.6). Compared to the LLaMA3-8B-Instruct base model, CycleQD improves the average from 32.6 to 52.4 (+19.8 points), with the most dramatic gains on DB (5.3 → 38.2) and OS (25.2 → 42.6). Against the strongest merging baseline, NSGA-II (avg 51.6), CycleQD wins by 0.8 points on average, primarily driven by a 6.2-point advantage on OS (42.6 vs. 36.4) and a marginal 0.5-point edge on MBPP (76.4 vs. 75.9), while NSGA-II retains a 4.2-point lead on DB (42.4 vs. 38.2). All fine-tuning baselines (avg 32.2–47.0) are substantially outperformed, with the combined-data fine-tuning model (avg 47.0) trailing CycleQD by 5.4 points despite using a larger training set. The ablation studies further confirm that the cyclic QD mechanism, SVD-based mutation, and elite sampling each contribute meaningfully, with the cumulative improvement from vanilla QD to full CycleQD reaching 4.8 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

CycleQD builds on the Quality Diversity paradigm (Pugh et al., 2016) and its MAP-Elites implementation (Mouret & Clune, 2015), but departs from both by cyclically rotating which task metric serves as quality versus behavioral characteristic, rather than fixing the BC space at initialization. For the crossover operator, it repurposes task-arithmetic model merging (Ilharco et al., 2022) and the evolutionary merging framework of Akiba et al. (2024) as a genetic recombination step, replacing the parameter-level random swaps typical of evolutionary algorithms. The SVD-based mutation is a novel contribution that constrains exploration to the principal-component directions of task vectors, addressing the convex-hull limitation of linear model merging. In the LLM agent training literature, prior work (Zeng et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2024) has relied on supervised fine-tuning or RLHF; CycleQD is the first to evolve LLM weights directly via QD for multi-task agent skill acquisition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that evolutionary model merging can match or exceed gradient-based multi-task fine-tuning for agent skills, offering a practical alternative that avoids data-ratio tuning and objective-function design. The GPT-3.5-TURBO-level performance of an 8B model on coding, OS, and DB tasks is notable, though the scope is limited to these specific agent benchmarks. A key open question is scalability: the method's reliance on model compatibility (evidenced by the SAM segmentation results where dissimilar experts merge poorly) suggests it may struggle with highly divergent task sets or larger model families. The QD archive of hundreds of diverse agents also opens a path toward multi-agent orchestration that the authors flag as future work.
