## Problem

Existing synthetic face dataset generation methods are limited in the number of well-separated identities they can produce and rely on external models (e.g., ControlNet, style-transfer networks) to introduce intra-class attribute variation. This paper addresses both limitations by proposing a single holistic model that generates large numbers of distinct identities while controlling per-identity appearance variation through simple vector perturbation, without any auxiliary models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250845/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, Vec2Face takes a 512-dimensional vector in the ArcFace-R100 feature space (either a PCA-sampled identity vector or a Gaussian-perturbed version of one) and produces a 112×112×3 RGB face image. The perturbation standard deviation σ directly governs intra-class variation, while a cosine-similarity threshold τ=0.3 on the identity vectors enforces inter-class separation. Optionally, a gradient-descent loop (AttrOP) adjusts the vector to target specific pose angles or image quality scores before decoding.

## Method

Vec2Face is a GAN-type architecture comprising a pretrained ArcFace-R100 feature extractor, a feature masked autoencoder (fMAE) built on ViT-Base, a four-layer image decoder, and a patch-based discriminator. The fMAE projects the 512-dim FR feature into a 49×768 2D map, randomly masks rows (75% mean), and reconstructs the full map using the projected feature as fill, forcing the encoder to learn robust representations. Training is supervised by pixel-level MSE reconstruction, cosine-similarity identity loss, LPIPS perceptual loss, and a GAN loss (introduced after 1000 epochs). At inference, identity vectors are sampled via PCA in the FR feature space with a pairwise cosine-similarity cap of 0.3, and per-identity images are generated by adding Gaussian noise (σ ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.7} with 40/40/20% sampling) to each identity vector, yielding 50 diverse images per identity while preserving identity consistency (similarity ≥ 0.5).

## Results

HSFace10K (Vec2Face, 0.5M images / 10K identities) achieves 92.00% average accuracy across five standard test sets (LFW 98.87%, CFP-FP 88.97%, CPLFW 85.47%, AgeDB-30 93.12%, CALFW 93.57%), the best among all synthetic datasets. On the harder IJBB and IJBC benchmarks, it reaches 83.82% and 86.96% respectively, surpassing the real-data CASIA-WebFace baseline (78.71% and 83.44%). Scaling to 300K identities / 15M images (HSFace300K) further improves the average to 93.52%, and merging HSFace10K with CASIA-WebFace yields 95.50% average. Vec2Face is also 311× faster than Arc2Face (467 FPS vs. 1.5 FPS) with a smaller model (0.68 GB vs. 3.4 GB) and lower FID (35.75 vs. 43.80 on LFW).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among diffusion-based baselines, HSFace10K outperforms Arc2Face (91.73% avg) by 0.27 points on average and leads on LFW, CPLFW, AgeDB, and CALFW, though Arc2Face retains a 2.9-point edge on CFP-FP (91.87 vs. 88.97). It also surpasses DCFace (89.56%), ID³ (89.80%), and IDiff-Face (88.20%) by 2.4–3.8 points on average. Against GAN-based baselines (SynFace 74.75%, SFace 77.71%, ExFaceGAN 80.17%, SFace2 81.62%, IDnet 79.13%), Vec2Face leads by 10.4–17.3 points on average, demonstrating that identity-aware feature conditioning is critical. The 3D-rendering baseline DigiFace (83.45%) trails by 8.6 points. Most notably, HSFace10K exceeds the real-data CASIA-WebFace baseline on CALFW (93.57 vs. 93.35), IJBB (83.82 vs. 78.71), and IJBC (86.96 vs. 83.44), marking the first instance where a synthetic-only training set outperforms same-scale real data on these benchmarks, while CASIA-WebFace still leads on LFW, CFP-FP, CPLFW, and AgeDB.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vec2Face adapts the masked autoencoder paradigm from MAE (He et al., 2022) to operate on 2D face feature maps rather than raw images, and borrows the PCA-based vector sampling strategy from Arc2Face (Papantoniou et al., 2024) for generating well-separated identity vectors. However, it departs sharply from both diffusion-based methods (DCFace, IDiff-Face, Arc2Face) and prior GAN-based approaches (SynFace, SFace, SFace2) by using a single holistic model where the magnitude of perturbation to an identity vector directly and continuously controls intra-class attribute variation, eliminating the need for external models like ControlNet or style-transfer networks. The key architectural departure is treating the FR feature space as a generative latent space where small perturbations preserve identity while changing attributes, rather than treating identity as a fixed conditioning signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a synthetic face dataset can outperform a same-scale real dataset (CASIA-WebFace) on challenging benchmarks (IJBB, IJBC, CALFW), and the 311× speed advantage over diffusion-based alternatives makes large-scale generation (300K identities, 15M images) practically feasible. The scaling experiments showing consistent improvement up to 300K identities suggest the approach has not yet hit a ceiling, and merging synthetic with real data yields further gains, pointing toward hybrid training as a practical recipe. Open questions remain around the computational definition of identity (the paper notes generated identities form a continuum rather than discrete classes), the method's limited effectiveness on fine-grained tasks like doppelganger discrimination, and whether training Vec2Face on larger real data would push accuracy further.
