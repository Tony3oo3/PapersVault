## Problem

Mamba-based multi-modal LLMs suffer from an imbalance between visual and textual latent quality: the linear selective-scan mechanism lacks structural constraints analogous to positional embeddings, causing fine-grained spatial information to degrade progressively through deeper LLM layers. This weakens cross-modal alignment, leading to suboptimal performance and elevated hallucination rates, particularly at billion-parameter scale where the gap between visual and textural features becomes most pronounced.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 384×384 RGB image and a free-form text prompt. The image is encoded by a frozen DINOv2 + SigLIP ViT-SO pair producing 729 visual tokens, which are projected into the Mamba LLM's embedding space and concatenated with tokenized text. At inference, the model outputs only a text response; the visual decoder and multi-scale fusion module used for training-time alignment are discarded, so no additional latency is introduced.

## Method

EMMA introduces two training-only mechanisms on top of the Cobra Mamba MLLM backbone. First, a pixel-wise alignment loss extends autoregressive next-token prediction to visual image patches: a small Mamba-based decoder projects the LLM's visual feature back to pixel space, and an L2 loss against the original image enforces structural preservation of spatial information. Second, a Multi-scale Feature Fusion (MFF) module hierarchically combines visual features from three intermediate LLM layers plus the final layer using pairwise cross-attention and Mamba blocks, preventing the gradual loss of fine-grained details that occurs in deeper layers. The fused multi-scale feature feeds into the pixel-wise alignment loss, so intermediate layers are also supervised to retain structural cues. Both modules add no inference cost, and the entire model is finetuned end-to-end for two epochs on ~1.2M image-text pairs without a separate pretraining stage.

## Results

EMMA-V1 (MambaV1-2.8B) achieves 76.3 on VQAv2, 60.5 on GQA, 52.1 on VizWiz, 57.2 on TextVQA, and 1572.8 on MME, surpassing its Cobra baseline on every metric except VSR (51.5 vs 51.7). The MME gain over Cobra is particularly large (+278.5), and TextVQA improves by 4.8 points, reflecting better fine-grained visual detail capture. EMMA-V2 (MambaV2-2.7B) achieves the best VizWiz (54.1) and VSR (52.7) among all similar-scaled models and reaches 60.8 on MMB. On hallucination benchmarks, EMMA-V1 scores 88.0 on POPE (best among all compared models) and 51.0 on HallusionBench, a 9.6-point gain over Cobra (41.4). Inference speed reaches 149.96 tokens/sec for EMMA-V2, approximately 3.6× faster than TinyLLaVA (41.46) and MobileVLM V2 (39.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary Mamba baseline Cobra (MambaV1-2.8B), EMMA-V1 wins on GQA (+1.4), TextVQA (+4.8), MME (+278.5), and MMB (+1.7), with only a marginal 0.2-point loss on VSR. Against VL-Mamba, EMMA-V1 outperforms on GQA (+4.3), TextVQA (+8.3), and MME (+203.2), though VL-Mamba retains a 0.3-point edge on VQAv2 and a 3.8-point edge on MMB. Among similar-scaled transformer MLLMs (LLaVA-Phi, MobileVLM v2, TinyLLaVA), EMMA-V1 achieves the best VizWiz (52.1 vs 35.9/–/–) and MME (1572.8 vs 1335.1/1440.5/1464.9), while remaining within ~1–2 points on most other metrics despite using a backbone pretrained on far fewer tokens (627B vs 1.3–1.4T). Against the large EMU model (LLaMA-13B, 3.4B training data), EMMA-V1 outperforms on VQAv2 (76.3 vs 62.0), GQA (60.5 vs 46.0), and VizWiz (52.1 vs 38.3) using roughly one-fourth the parameters. On hallucination, EMMA-V1 achieves the best POPE score (88.0) and a 9.6-point HallusionBench improvement over Cobra (51.0 vs 41.4), approaching LLaVA-1.5 (47.1) and BLIP2-T5 (48.1) which are 7B and 12.4B models respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMMA builds directly on Cobra (Zhao et al., 2024), which first adapted the Mamba SSM backbone to the LLaVA-style MLLM pipeline, and extends it with two training-time modules: a pixel-wise alignment loss (conceptually related to EMU's joint visual-textual autoregressive generation but with a far simpler L2-based decoder) and a multi-scale feature fusion module that hierarchically combines intermediate LLM features via cross-attention and Mamba projections. It departs from prior Mamba MLLMs (VL-Mamba, Cobra) by introducing explicit visual supervision during training, arguing that existing approaches only preserve visual features before they enter the LLM but not during autoregressive text generation. The multi-scale fusion design is the first to apply same-modality, multi-layer feature fusion within a Mamba-based MLLM, distinguishing it from prior Mamba fusion work (MambaDFuse, Fusion-Mamba) that targets separate-branch multimodal architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored bottleneck in Mamba-based MLLMs—progressive loss of fine-grained visual structure in deeper LLM layers—and shows that lightweight training-time supervision (pixel alignment + multi-scale fusion) can substantially close the visual-quality gap without any inference overhead. The ~4× inference speed advantage over comparable transformer MLLMs makes this practically relevant for latency-sensitive deployment (robotics, autonomous driving), though the gains are architecturally expected from Mamba's linear complexity. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond ~3B parameters, whether the cross-attention in MFF is truly necessary versus simpler fusion, and whether the pixel-level L2 objective generalizes to higher-resolution or video inputs. The method is also tightly coupled to the Mamba architecture and may not transfer directly to other SSM variants.
