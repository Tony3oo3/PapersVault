## Problem

Hallucination in large vision-language models (LVLMs) arises from a mechanism distinct from that in pure LLMs: the sequential information flow from a separately pre-trained vision encoder to a text decoder makes the decoder sensitive to unstable visual features. Prior work attributed hallucination to language priors or statistical biases that do not exploit this unique LVLM architecture. This paper identifies vision feature instability as a root cause and asks whether it can be corrected at inference time without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text query (e.g., a question or captioning prompt) and produces a text response, operating as a drop-in test-time intervention on existing LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL). All direction vectors are pre-computed once from a fixed set of 50–80 COCO examples and remain unchanged across all benchmarks, making the approach task- and dataset-agnostic. At inference, the intervention requires only a single forward pass with vector additions to hidden states, incurring no additional training or multi-pass overhead.

## Method

VTI (Visual and Textual Intervention) pre-computes two latent-space direction vectors and adds them to hidden states during inference. The visual direction is obtained by applying m random masks to each of N images, averaging the vision encoder hidden states across perturbations, taking the difference from the unmasked state, and extracting the first principal component via PCA to remove image-specific information. The textual direction is computed analogously from paired hallucinated and non-hallucinated captions (generated via GPT-3.5), using hidden-state differences at the final token position. At test time, the visual direction is added to every layer and token position of the vision encoder (scaled by α), while the textual direction is added to the last-token hidden states of the text decoder (scaled by β), jointly steering the model toward more stable visual representations and stronger image-to-text attention dependency.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark, VTI achieves the best accuracy and F1 across all three LVLMs: 86.5% accuracy / 85.9% F1 on LLaVA-1.5, 81.8% / 83.2% on InstructBLIP, and 85.2% / 84.1% on Qwen-VL, consistently outperforming both VCD and OPERA. On CHAIR (open-ended captioning with LLaVA-1.5), VTI reduces CHAIR_S from 51.0 (Vanilla) to 35.8 and CHAIR_I from 15.2 to 11.1 while maintaining competitive recall (76.8%). On MMHAL-Bench, VTI achieves an average score of 2.90 and hallucination rate of 0.51, compared to 1.99 and 0.62 for the vanilla model, with gains across all eight hallucination categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTI outperforms both VCD (Leng et al., 2023b) and OPERA (Huang et al., 2023) across all three models and all three benchmarks. On POPE with LLaVA-1.5, VTI achieves 86.5% accuracy vs. 84.2% (OPERA) and 82.3% (VCD); on InstructBLIP, VTI reaches 81.8% vs. 79.6% (OPERA) and 80.1% (VCD). On CHAIR with LLaVA-1.5, VTI reduces CHAIR_S to 35.8, substantially below OPERA (47.0) and VCD (51.0), and also outperforms DOLA (57.0). On MMHAL-Bench, VTI achieves a 2.90 average score and 0.51 hallucination rate, beating OPERA (2.64 / 0.59) and VCD (2.69 / 0.58). Notably, VTI is a training-free, task-agnostic method requiring only 80 pre-computed examples, whereas VCD and OPERA modify the decoding strategy per query, and DOLA (also compared on CHAIR) actually worsens hallucination relative to the vanilla model.

## Relation to Prior Work

VTI repurposes the activation-steering / representation-engineering paradigm developed for LLMs (Zou et al., 2023; Liu et al.; Cao et al., 2024) and extends it to the vision encoder side of LVLMs, a component that prior hallucination-mitigation methods (VCD, OPERA, DOLA) largely ignore in favor of decoding-strategy modifications on the text decoder. The key conceptual departure is the mechanistic claim that hallucination in LVLMs is rooted in the instability of vision features arising from separately pre-trained encoders and decoders, rather than purely in language priors or statistical biases. By pre-computing a single PCA direction that captures the effect of feature averaging across perturbations, VTI converts an expensive multi-forward-pass averaging procedure into a single additive shift, making it a training-free, task-agnostic test-time intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, testable mechanism linking vision-feature instability to hallucination in LVLMs, which prior work attributed more diffusely to language priors or training bias. The training-free, task-agnostic nature (50 fixed examples, one PCA direction) makes the approach immediately deployable without retraining, which is practically valuable for practitioners working with frozen LVLMs. However, the evaluation is limited to three mid-scale models (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL) and three benchmarks, and the fixed-direction approach raises open questions about generalization to substantially different architectures, video inputs, or truly out-of-distribution domains. Whether the single-PCA-dimension direction remains sufficient as model scale and modality complexity grow is an important unresolved question.
