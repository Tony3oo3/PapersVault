## Problem

Matrix-based formulae for counting paths and cycles in graphs (up to lengths 6 and 7 respectively) have been known since Voropaev and Perepechko (2012), but whether more computationally efficient alternatives exist remained an open question. The paper reframes this search as a context-free grammar (CFG)-constrained language generation problem, asking whether a deep reinforcement learning algorithm can autonomously discover novel, more efficient matrix formulae within the grammar's language.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250731/cropped_page5_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an adjacency matrix A (and derived matrices I, J) of an undirected graph, a target path/cycle length l, and a CFG (G₃ or its edge-level variant G̃₃) that constrains valid expressions to combinations of A, I, J, matrix multiplication, and Hadamard product. The output is an explicit matrix formula P_l* (a linear combination of grammar sentences) whose (i,j) entry gives the number of l-paths from node i to node j, along with the corresponding cycle formula C_{l+1} = A ⊙ P_l. The grammar enforces structural validity (e.g., zero-diagonal constraints in G̃₃) so that only meaningful matrix expressions are generated.

## Method

GRL is a deep MCTS algorithm whose search tree mirrors the PDA derivation tree of a CFG: nodes are PDA states (concatenation of emitted terminals and stack contents), and edges are production-rule applications. The key architectural contribution is Gramformer, a transformer that models PDA transitions by partitioning the transition function δ into variable tokens, rule tokens (further split into terminal and non-terminal), and terminal tokens, with per-variable masks ensuring the decoder's softmax yields a valid probability distribution over applicable rules. During the acting phase, MCTS selects rules via a UCB-like heuristic combining Q-values, the Gramformer's predicted value, and a policy-weighted exploration term; during the learning phase, the network is trained with KL divergence on MCTS-derived policies and MSE on empirical returns. The reward for a leaf node is computed by solving a linear system (Ex = v) to find the optimal linear combination of generated sentences matching ground-truth path counts across a graph dataset, with per-rule computational-cost penalties and a maximum sentence-length constraint to encourage efficiency.

## Results

GRL discovers new matrix-based formulas P_l* for counting l-paths (l ∈ {3,4,5,6}) that are provably equivalent to the state-of-the-art Voropaev–Perepechko formulas P_l but require fewer matrix multiplications. The theoretical time-complexity ratios of P_l* over P_l are 1/2, 4/9, 1/4, and 1/6.25 for l = 3, 4, 5, 6 respectively, corresponding to speedups of 2×, 2.25×, 4×, and 6.25×. Empirical timing experiments on random graphs (Figure 7) confirm strong alignment between the theoretical and measured speedups. For l = 2, GRL recovers the known optimal formula P_2 = J ⊙ A².

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the set of explicit matrix formulas from Voropaev and Perepechko (2012), which are the most efficient known methods for counting paths up to length 6 and cycles up to length 7. GRL recovers the Voropaev–Perepechko formula exactly for l = 2 (P_2 = J ⊙ A²) and also recovers P_3, but additionally discovers a more compact P_3* that halves the number of matrix multiplications. For l = 4, 5, and 6, GRL discovers strictly more efficient formulas P_4*, P_5*, and P_6* with speedups of 2.25×, 4×, and 6.25× respectively, all proven equivalent to the originals via algebraic identities (Theorems D.2–D.5). The paper also evaluates GRL on directed graphs (Appendix E), where it is the only approach capable of discovering novel matrix formulas for paths of lengths 4–5, outperforming baseline algorithms that cannot produce new formulas in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads: (1) the MCTS-based RL framework for combinatorial formula discovery pioneered by Fawzi et al. (2022) for matrix multiplication, (2) the 3-WL Context-Free Grammar introduced by Piquenot et al. (2024) for graph expressivity, and (3) the classical matrix-based path/cycle counting formulas of Voropaev and Perepechko (2012). The key departure is the Gramformer architecture, which models a Pushdown Automaton within a transformer to learn policy and value functions over CFG-constrained search spaces — a setting not addressed by prior MCTS+RL work, which typically operates over fixed or manually-defined action spaces. The paper thus reframes formula discovery as a grammar-constrained language generation problem, bridging formal language theory and deep reinforcement learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovered formulas achieve 2× to 6.25× computational speedups over the best known human-derived formulas for path/cycle counting (lengths 3–6), with mathematical proofs of correctness, making them immediately deployable in GNN feature computation and network analysis pipelines. However, the 3-WL expressivity limit of G₃ caps path counting at length 6, so extending to longer paths requires characterizing k-WL CFGs for k > 3 — a non-trivial open problem. More broadly, the Gramformer/GRL framework is generic and could in principle be applied to other CFG-constrained formula or program discovery tasks, though the paper only validates it on this single use case. The narrow scope (undirected simple graphs, lengths ≤ 6) and the absence of comparison against learned (non-explicit) counting methods limit the immediate practical impact.
