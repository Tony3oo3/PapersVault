## Problem

The infinite-width limit of neural networks yielding task-independent Gaussian Process kernels is well understood, and it is widely assumed that larger widths improve generalization. This paper challenges that assumption by investigating the narrow width limit of Bayesian Parallel Branching Neural Networks (BPB-NNs), where the hidden layer width is much smaller than the number of training samples, and shows that kernel renormalization induces a symmetry breaking between branches that leads to more robust per-branch learning and superior or comparable generalization in bias-limited scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G = (A, X) with adjacency matrix A and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×N₀}, along with P training node labels, and produces a scalar regression prediction per node decomposed into bias and variance over the Bayesian posterior. A key unusual aspect is that the analysis is conducted in the statistical limit (P, N, N₀ → ∞ with P/N = α finite), and the branch-level readout norms ⟨‖a_l‖²⟩σ_w²/N converge to data-dependent values at narrow width that are independent of architectural hyperparameters like σ_w and L, effectively encoding the intrinsic importance of each convolution order. For the residual-MLP extension on CIFAR-10, the input is a 1024-dimensional red-channel vector and the output is a binary class label derived from the sign of the mean predictor over HMC samples.

## Method

The BPB-GCN architecture consists of L independent GCN branches, each applying a different number of adjacency-matrix convolutions A^l to the input features before a shared-width hidden layer, with no weight sharing; the final readout is the sum of branch-level outputs. Under a Bayesian setup with Gaussian prior and squared-loss likelihood, the paper integrates out all weights in the partition function to obtain an effective Hamiltonian governed by L order parameters u_l that renormalize the overall kernel K = Σ_l (u_l/L) K_l. The saddle-point equations for u_l reveal two regimes: at wide width (α → 0) the entropic term dominates and all u_l = σ_w² (the GP limit with no feature learning), while at narrow width (α → ∞) the energetic term dominates and u_l becomes data-dependent, breaking the symmetry between branches. The paper proves an equipartition theorem showing that in the narrow-width limit of a student-teacher setup, each student branch's readout norm exactly matches the corresponding teacher branch's norm, demonstrating robust per-branch learning. The framework is generalized to arbitrary branching architectures (e.g., residual-MLP with linear and ReLU branches) and validated via Hamiltonian Monte Carlo sampling on synthetic CSBM graphs, Cora, and CIFAR-10.

## Results

In the student-teacher CSBM experiment, an extremely narrow student BPB-GCN (N=4) robustly learns the teacher's branch readout norms (β₀²=0.4, β₁²=2), while a wide student (N=1024) fails to differentiate branches and approaches the GP limit where both branches converge to σ_w⁴. On Cora, the bias term increases monotonically with network width N, confirming the narrow-to-wide transition, and using L=6 branches (higher-order convolutions) improves generalization over fewer branches. On CIFAR-10 with a residual-MLP, the ReLU branch completely dominates at narrow width, driving bias to decrease, and test accuracy (averaged over HMC samples) decreases with larger N, confirming the narrow-width advantage in bias-limited scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baseline methods; instead, its central comparison is between the narrow-width regime and the wide-width (GP/infinite-width) regime of the same BPB-NN architecture. The key finding is that in bias-limited settings, the narrow-width BPB-GCN achieves lower bias than its wide-width counterpart because kernel renormalization breaks the symmetry between branches, allowing each branch to learn a distinct, data-driven representation. The theoretical saddle-point predictions are validated against Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) sampling across all experiments, with theory and HMC in close agreement for both branch norms and generalization error. The regularization strength σ_w controls the transition window between the narrow-width and GP regimes: larger σ_w produces sharper transitions, while smaller σ_w yields a more gradual crossover where variance can dominate. The paper also contrasts its BPB-GCN with the linearly decoupled GCN of Cong et al. (2021), noting that the latter shares a single readout vector across branches and therefore does not exhibit the per-branch kernel renormalization that drives the narrow-width effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the kernel renormalization framework of Li & Sompolinsky (2021) from deep linear MLPs to parallel branching graph convolutional networks, introducing the BPB-GCN architecture as a generalization of residual GCNs (Chen et al., 2020) and standard GCNs (Kipf & Welling, 2016). While the NNGP/GP limit (Lee et al., 2018) has been extensively studied as the infinite-width regime, this work defines a complementary narrow-width limit where the energetic term in the effective Hamiltonian dominates, leading to task-dependent kernel renormalization and branch-level symmetry breaking. The Bayesian inference setup (via HMC) connects to prior work on statistical mechanics of neural networks, but the application to branching architectures with independent per-branch readouts is new, distinguishing it from linearly decoupled GCNs (Cong et al., 2021) where a shared readout prevents per-branch kernel renormalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that narrower networks can outperform wider ones in bias-limited scenarios directly challenges the widely-held assumption that increasing width monotonically improves generalization, offering a principled explanation for why ensembles of narrow ResNets (Vyas et al., 2024) exhibit improved performance. The equipartition theorem provides a clean theoretical characterization of how each branch robustly learns its corresponding teacher branch at narrow width, with readout norms reflecting intrinsic data properties independent of architectural hyperparameters. Key limitations include the restriction to the lazy regime with minimal feature learning, the variance-limited nature of real-world generalization, and the primarily linear activation focus; open questions include whether the narrow-width regularizer persists in the rich regime with online learning and how to practically reduce variance (e.g., via ensembles) to make the bias improvement more pronounced.
