## Problem

Deep transformers suffer from representation collapse (over-smoothing), where token representations become nearly identical across layers, severely limiting the model's ability to capture diverse features. The paper identifies the root cause as the rapid vanishing of eigenvalues in the NLM similarity matrix that is mathematically equivalent to the self-attention operator: after n layers, eigenvalues decay as λ^n, driving the system toward a degenerate constant solution. The key question is whether the attention mechanism can be modified to slow this eigenvalue decay while preserving its noise-removal (smoothing) function.

## Input / Output

Twicing Attention is a drop-in replacement for standard self-attention within existing transformer backbones (DeiT, FAN, standard Transformer, Linear Transformer, Switch Transformer), accepting the same inputs: 224×224 RGB images split into 16×16 patches for vision tasks, or 256-token text blocks for language modeling. It can be applied to all layers or selectively to a subset (e.g., only the last 3 layers, denoted [10-12]), with outputs being class labels (1000-way ImageNet), pixel-level segmentation masks (ADE20K), or next-token distributions (WikiText-103). The mechanism adds no extra parameters and maintains O(N²D) computational complexity identical to standard self-attention.

## Method

The core contribution is Twicing Attention, which replaces the standard attention output AV with (2A − A²)V, where A = softmax(QKᵀ/√D) is the row-stochastic attention matrix. This modification is derived from the kernel twicing procedure in nonparametric regression: if A corresponds to a smoothing kernel K, then A² corresponds to the convolved kernel K*K, and 2A − A² corresponds to the twiced kernel 2K − K*K, which has zero second moment and thus eliminates the leading O(h²) bias term in Nadaraya-Watson estimation, reducing bias to O(h⁴). In practice, the O(N³) matrix squaring is avoided by computing the equivalent form AV + A(V − AV), which reuses the standard attention output and only requires one additional O(N²D) matrix multiplication. Theoretically, the eigencapacity (integral of pⁿ(x) over [0,1]) decays at O(n^{-1/2}) for the twicing polynomial 2x − x² versus O(n^{-1}) for the identity polynomial x, provably slowing representation collapse. The residual V − AV, which contains information discarded by the first smoothing pass, is itself smoothed and reincorporated, acting as a per-layer self-correction that preserves salient mid-frequency information.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, DeiT-Twicing achieves 72.60% top-1 accuracy (vs. 72.00% for DeiT) and improves adversarial robustness substantially: 9.15% top-1 under PGD (vs. 8.16%), 32.28% under FGSM (vs. 29.88%), and 67.12% under SPSA (vs. 66.41%). When combined with the FAN backbone, FAN-Twicing reaches 77.18% clean top-1 and 12.80% PGD top-1, outperforming FAN on all attack settings. On WikiText-103, Transformer-Twicing reduces test perplexity from 37.51 to 36.69 and attacked PPL from 55.17 to 54.46. On ADE20K segmentation, DeiT-Twicing improves mean IoU from 34.73 to 35.12 and mean accuracy from 44.48 to 45.53.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DeiT baseline, Twicing Attention consistently wins: +0.60% clean top-1, +0.99% PGD top-1, +2.40% FGSM top-1, and +0.71% SPSA top-1, while also improving on all OOD benchmarks (ImageNet-R: 32.74 vs. 32.22; ImageNet-A: 7.66 vs. 6.97) and reducing ImageNet-C corruption error (mCE 70.33 vs. 72.21). Against NeuTRENO, DeiT-Twicing achieves higher clean accuracy (72.60 vs. 72.44) and better adversarial robustness across all three attacks (PGD 9.15 vs. 8.85, FGSM 32.28 vs. 31.43, SPSA 67.12 vs. 66.98). When plugged into the stronger FAN backbone, FAN-Twicing improves over FAN on every metric: +0.09% clean top-1, +0.89% PGD top-1, +1.71% FGSM top-1, and +1.74% SPSA top-1. On language modeling, Transformer-Twicing beats the standard Transformer by 0.82 PPL on clean WikiText-103 (36.69 vs. 37.51) and 0.71 PPL under Word Swap attack (54.46 vs. 55.17). Against FeatScale (another over-smoothing mitigation method), DeiT-Twicing achieves higher clean ImageNet accuracy (72.60 vs. 72.35). The method also extends to Linear Transformers, where Linear-Twicing reduces test PPL from 41.26 to 40.61 and attacked PPL from 72.13 to 70.40.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established connection between self-attention and non-local means (NLM) smoothing (Nguyen et al., 2023b) by identifying the rapidly vanishing eigenvalues of the NLM similarity matrix as the root cause of representation collapse in transformers. It then draws on the kernel twicing procedure from nonparametric regression (Stuetzle & Mittal, 1979; Newey et al., 2004), a 45-year-old statistical technique for bias reduction, to construct a modified attention operator (2A − A²) that provably slows eigenvalue decay. This positions the work at the intersection of the over-smoothing literature (Shi et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022; Dong et al., 2021) and the Nadaraya-Watson regression interpretation of attention (Nguyen et al., 2022c; Han et al., 2023), unifying them through a single quadratic kernel modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical diagnosis of over-smoothing (eigencapacity decaying as O(n⁻¹) vs. O(n⁻¹/²) with twicing) and a minimal-compute fix that reuses residual information previously discarded at each layer, improving both accuracy and adversarial robustness across vision and language tasks with negligible overhead. The robustness gains (e.g., +1.0% PGD Top-1 over DeiT, +1.7% over FAN) suggest that reduced estimator bias translates into practical resilience, a connection that could inform future robustness research. Open questions include whether the O(N²D) implementation scales efficiently to very long sequences, whether the theoretical framework extends to multi-head or cross-attention settings, and whether approximation or sparsity techniques can further reduce the constant-factor overhead when applied to all layers.
