## Problem

Existing preference alignment methods for diffusion models (DPO, SPO, RL-based) exclusively optimize the conditional branch to generate preferred outputs, while neglecting the unconditional/negative-conditional branch in classifier-free guidance. This oversight means the model never learns to recognize or avoid undesirable outputs, weakening CFG's ability to push generation away from low-preference regions and limiting overall alignment quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (SD1.5, SDXL, VideoCrafter2, or DreamShaper) along with its preference-optimized weight offset η, and trains an additional negative-preference weight offset δ using the same preference dataset with reversed pair ordering or negated rewards. At inference, two weight configurations are loaded simultaneously: θ_pos = θ + η for conditional predictions and θ_neg = θ + αη + βδ for negative-conditional predictions, where α and β are tunable scaling factors that control how much the negative branch deviates from the positive branch. The plug-and-play nature means no architectural changes are needed, though it does require storing and loading two separate weight offsets (mitigated by LoRA-based training).

## Method

NPO trains a dedicated negative-preference model that serves as the negative-conditional predictor in CFG, requiring no new datasets or training strategies—only minor modifications to existing preference optimization procedures. For reward-based methods (differentiable reward, RL), the reward model output is simply negated (R_NPO = 1 − R); for DPO-based methods, the preferred/less-preferred image pair order is reversed. At inference, the guided epsilon prediction becomes ε_ω = (ω+1)ε_θpos(x_t, c, t) − ωε_θneg(x_t, c′, t), where θ_neg = θ + αη + βδ incorporates a scaled portion of the positive offset η to maintain necessary correlation between the two branches and avoid excessive output variance. The key insight is that the negative weight offset δ is not simply a scalar multiple of −η (as a weight-merge approximation would suggest), but contains an orthogonal component δ⊥ that captures distinct negative-preference directions, making NPO fundamentally different from merely scaling or merging DPO weights.

## Results

On the Pick-a-pic test set, Diffusion-NPO consistently improves all four evaluation metrics (PickScore, HPSv2, ImageReward, LAION-Aesthetic) when applied as a plug-and-play inference enhancement. On SD1.5, Diff.-DPO+NPO (reg=1000) achieves the best ImageReward score of 0.3472 (vs. 0.1115 for Diff.-DPO alone), while Diff.-SPO+NPO reaches 21.65 PickScore and 5.999 Aesthetic. On SDXL, Diff.-SPO+NPO attains 23.08 PickScore, 28.70 HPSv2, 1.047 ImageReward, and 6.438 Aesthetic, surpassing all baselines. For text-to-video, VADER+NPO(HPSv2) achieves 6.855 Aesthetic and 30.76 HPSv2 on human prompts, with 97.92% and 86.98% win rates respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NPO consistently outperforms all baselines across metrics. Against the base SD1.5 (Rombach et al.), NPO alone improves HPSv2 from 26.84 to 27.36 and ImageReward from 0.1064 to 0.2028. Compared to DDPO (Black et al.) and D3PO (Yang et al.), NPO-augmented methods achieve substantially higher scores (e.g., Diff.-DPO+NPO reg=1000: 27.36 HPSv2 vs. DDPO's 24.91 and D3PO's 23.97). Against Diff.-DPO (Wallace et al.), NPO boosts ImageReward from 0.1115 to 0.3472 (reg=1000) and HPSv2 from 25.05 to 27.60 (reg=500). Against Diff.-SPO (Liang et al.), NPO improves PickScore from 21.41 to 21.65 and Aesthetic from 5.946 to 5.999 on SD1.5, and from 22.97 to 23.08 PickScore on SDXL. For video generation, VADER+NPO(HPSv2) outperforms VADER (Prabhudesai et al.) by +0.393 Aesthetic and +1.02 HPSv2 on human prompts, and VADER+NPO(PickScore) achieves 98.44% PickScore win rate over VideoCrafter2 (Chen et al.). Against training-free CFG-strengthening methods, NPO outperforms both SEG (Hong et al.) and Autoguidance (Karras et al.) on SDXL: NPO achieves 28.78 HPSv2 vs. SEG's 26.26 and Autoguidance's 27.21, with 81.2% and 73.6% win rates respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the preference alignment pipeline for diffusion models (Diffusion-DPO, Diffusion-SPO, VADER) by identifying that the negative-conditional branch of classifier-free guidance has been systematically neglected: all existing methods train only the positive/conditional predictor while leaving the negative branch as an unoptimized base model. The key conceptual departure is that training a dedicated negative-preference model requires no new data or training strategies—only reversing preference pairs (for DPO) or negating reward signals (for RL/differentiable reward methods). The method is positioned as a plug-and-play inference-time enhancement that composes with any existing preference-optimized model, rather than a replacement for the positive-branch training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: NPO is a minimal-overhead, LoRA-compatible technique that consistently improves all standard preference metrics across SD1.5, SDXL, DreamShaper, and video models, and it outperforms training-free alternatives like Autoguidance and SEG. The finding that a single-iteration NPO offset outperforms up to 10× longer DPO training suggests the negative branch was a low-hanging fruit in preference alignment. Open questions include optimal α/β scaling for the combined weight, whether curated negative data (e.g., targeted corruptions) can further improve results, and whether the approach generalizes to tasks where the 'negative' concept is less well-defined than aesthetic preference.
