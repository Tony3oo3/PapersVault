## Problem

Task arithmetic combines fine-tuned task vectors in weight space to build a unified multi-task model, but a fundamental trade-off exists: non-linear fine-tuning yields accurate individual models yet poor weight disentanglement (large accuracy gap in the unified model), while NTK linearization ensures disentanglement at the cost of reduced individual accuracy and 2–3× computational overhead. The paper asks whether a sub-module exists where non-linear fine-tuning can simultaneously exhibit kernel behavior (enabling disentanglement) and preserve individual-model accuracy.

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, the method consumes RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for CLIP ViT benchmarks) and produces class labels across 8 datasets (Cars, DTD, SUN397, EuroSAT, GTSRB, MNIST, SVHN, RESISC45); for NLP tasks it takes text inputs and produces text outputs on 7 GLUE tasks. During fine-tuning, only the four attention projection matrices (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o) per layer are updated while all other parameters—including MLP blocks, positional encodings, and the frozen zero-shot classification head—remain fixed. The resulting task vectors (differences between fine-tuned and pre-trained attention weights) are then linearly combined with a shared scalar α to form the unified model.

## Method

The key insight is that attention modules in Transformers exhibit kernel behavior during fine-tuning: their output changes are well-approximated by first-order Taylor expansions around the pre-trained initialization, a property verified via a post-hoc linearization test showing attention-only fine-tuning tracks its linear approximation far more closely than full-model fine-tuning. By restricting updates to only the Q, K, V, and O projection matrices (63.7M trainable parameters out of 222M total, ~29%), the method achieves individual-model accuracy within ~1% of full non-linear fine-tuning while attaining weight disentanglement comparable to NTK linearization, yielding up to 2.38% higher unified-model accuracy than the state-of-the-art at less than half the training time. The paper further reformulates task arithmetic by decomposing the network into a representation module and task-specific modules, proving via a disentanglement-error metric (KL divergence between individual and unified representations) that disentanglement emerges from the representation module while classification heads can degrade it, and demonstrating that the attention-only approach maintains low disentanglement error over a wider range of mixing coefficients α.

## Results

On the 8-task vision benchmark (Cars, DTD, SUN397, EuroSAT, GTSRB, MNIST, SVHN, RESISC45), the proposed attention-only fine-tuning achieves 78.37% absolute and 87.42% normalized accuracy with ViT-B-32, outperforming non-linear fine-tuning (70.00%/77.04%) and NTK linearization (76.26%/85.82%). Gains scale with model size, reaching 87.91%/93.66% on ViT-L-14. On the 7-task GLUE NLP benchmark with Flan-T5-base, the method achieves 85.30% average accuracy versus 73.25% (non-linear) and 77.77% (NTK linearization). The method also requires only 63.7M trainable parameters (vs. 222M for full fine-tuning) and 10:15 training time (vs. 40:59 for NTK linearization).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: non-linear fine-tuning (Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023) and NTK linearization (Ortiz-Jimenez et al., NeurIPS 2024). The proposed attention-only fine-tuning wins on every metric across all three ViT sizes: it improves absolute accuracy by 8.37% over non-linear fine-tuning and by 2.11% over NTK linearization on ViT-B-32, with similar margins on larger models. On the GLUE NLP benchmark, the advantage is even larger—85.30% average vs. 73.25% (non-linear) and 77.77% (NTK), a 7.53-point gain over the strongest baseline. The method also dominates on efficiency: it trains in 10:15 minutes with 63.7M trainable parameters, compared to 17:38/222M for non-linear fine-tuning and 40:59/222M for NTK linearization, achieving higher accuracy at roughly one-quarter the computational cost of NTK linearization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task arithmetic framework introduced by Ilharco et al. (2023) and the NTK linearization approach of Ortiz-Jimenez et al. (2024) by identifying attention modules as a specific sub-component that naturally exhibits kernel behavior during non-linear fine-tuning. Rather than linearizing the entire network (as in NTK linearization) or fine-tuning all parameters (as in the original task arithmetic), the authors restrict updates to attention projections (Q, K, V, O), achieving a middle ground that preserves individual-model accuracy while improving weight disentanglement. The paper also departs from prior formulations by explicitly separating the representation module from task-specific modules (e.g., classification heads), showing that disentanglement properties reside in the former while the latter can constrain overall task arithmetic performance. The ablation connection to LoRA-like parameter subsets suggests a broader link between parameter-efficient fine-tuning and task-vector quality that prior work had not explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: fine-tuning only attention modules reduces trainable parameters from 222M to 63.7M and training time from ~41 minutes (NTK linearization) to ~10 minutes, while outperforming both baselines in unified-model accuracy across vision and NLP benchmarks. The finding that weight disentanglement emerges from the representation module rather than the full network opens the door to applying task arithmetic in unsupervised or representation-only settings, broadening its applicability. However, the method remains constrained to models with identical architectures and initializations, and the optimal α still requires per-task tuning. Open questions include whether the kernel behavior of attention modules persists at larger scales, how it interacts with sparsity, and whether the bias-fine-tuning effects observed in the ablation can be systematically exploited.
