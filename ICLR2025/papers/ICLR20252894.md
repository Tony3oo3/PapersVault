## Problem

Existing graph data augmentation (GDA) methods are designed for static graphs and, when naively applied to dynamic graphs, disrupt temporal consistency by causing long-lived edges to vanish and reappear across snapshots. This paper identifies this temporal-consistency disruption as the root cause of performance degradation (e.g., DropEdge increases the proportion of 1-snapshot edges from 43.57% to 69.25% on Yelp) and asks how to design augmentation that preserves the natural evolution of dynamic graph sequences.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a discrete-time dynamic graph sequence of T snapshots, each providing node features X_t ∈ R^{N×D} and a binary adjacency matrix A_t ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}. It produces per-snapshot soft rationale masks M_t^R ∈ [0,1]^{N×N} (via Gumbel-Softmax sampling), augmented node representations in the latent embedding space (combining rationale and swapped environment embeddings via sum/concat/max pooling), and ultimately link-prediction scores for edges at time T+1. Unlike topology-level GDA, all augmentation operates in the DyGNN's node-embedding space rather than modifying the graph structure directly.

## Method

DyAug introduces temporal-conditioned graph rationalization for dynamic graphs: at each snapshot t, the rationale mask M_t^R is generated conditioned on the previous mask M_{t-1}^R (Markov property), using a per-edge FFN with Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable Bernoulli sampling. A consistency regularization loss (L_cr) enforces that rationale subgraphs within a w-step temporal window are similar (positive pairs) while distant ones are dissimilar, preserving temporal coherence. Three environment-replacement augmentations—spatial (swap with another node's environment at the same time), temporal (swap with the same node's environment from an earlier snapshot), and spatial-temporal (both)—are applied in the latent embedding space to sever spurious correlations between the non-causal environment and predictions. Training optimizes a combined objective L_pred + α₁·L_cr + α₂·L_cl, where L_cl is a contrastive loss pulling augmented representations toward the rationale embedding while pushing them away from the environment embedding. The framework is plug-and-play with any DyGNN backbone (GCRN, DySAT, SEIGN) and adds O(Σ|E^(t)|D + NDT²) overhead.

## Results

On five real-world dynamic graph benchmarks (COLLAB, Yelp, Bitcoin, UCI, ACT) with three DyGNN backbones (GRCN, DySAT, SEIGN), DyAug achieves AUC improvements of 0.89%–3.13% over vanilla models, with the largest gains on simpler backbones (e.g., +3.13% on GRCN+Bitcoin, +2.17% on GRCN+COLLAB). Under adversarial attacks (structure, feature, Nettack), DyAug recovers 6.2%–12.2% AUC compared to vanilla, and under temporal distribution shift (OOD categories), SEIGN+DyAug boosts OOD AUC from 67.19% to 76.50% on Yelp. On the large-scale DGraphFin dataset (4.9M nodes), DyAug achieves 74.98% AUC with <2 GB additional GPU memory, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyAug outperforms all seven static GDA baselines across every dataset–backbone combination in Table 1. Rule-based augmenters (DropEdge, DropNode, DropMessage, GraphMixup) frequently degrade performance—GraphMixup drops DySAT+Yelp AUC by 1.48% and SEIGN+Yelp by 1.30%—due to disruption of temporal edge consistency. Among learning-based baselines, RGDA (the closest static rationalization method) achieves the strongest static-GDA results (e.g., 0.8897 on DySAT+COLLAB) but still trails DyAug by 0.28% on that setting and by up to 2.63% on GRCN+Bitcoin (0.8812 vs. 0.9079). NeuralSparse and SUBLIME provide modest gains over vanilla but are consistently outperformed by DyAug by 0.5–2.5%. For distribution-shift robustness (Table 2), DyAug surpasses IRM, DIDA, and DGIB-Bern on both COLLAB and Yelp OOD settings, with SEIGN+DyAug reaching 83.11% (COLLAB) and 76.50% (Yelp) versus DIDA's 81.87% and 75.92% respectively. Under Nettack poisoning, DyAug recovers 8.2% AUC on DySAT+Yelp, whereas RGDA recovers only 2.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph rationalization paradigm (GREA, Liu et al. 2022; RGDA, Liu et al. 2024) from static to discrete-time dynamic graphs by making rationale discovery temporally Markovian—each snapshot's causal mask is conditioned on the previous snapshot's mask rather than being generated independently. It departs from all prior GDA methods (DropEdge, GraphMixup, NeuralSparse, SUBLIME) by operating in the latent representation space via environment replacement rather than perturbing the graph topology directly, and by explicitly enforcing temporal consistency through a windowed contrastive regularization loss. The structural causal model framing, while borrowed from static graph rationalization, is adapted to identify two backdoor paths through both topology and node attributes across the temporal sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first GDA method purpose-built for discrete-time dynamic graphs, filling a clear gap identified through systematic empirical diagnosis: static augmenters disrupt edge timespan distributions, breaking the temporal continuity that DyGNNs rely on. The temporal-conditioned rationale mechanism and three environment replacement strategies offer a reusable template for any DyGNN backbone, and the adversarial robustness gains (6.2%–12.2%) are practically meaningful. Limitations include scope restricted to DTDG (not CTDG), modest absolute performance gains (0.89%–3.13%), and added computational overhead (O(NDT²) from the contrastive loss). Open questions include whether the SCM-based rationale-environment decomposition generalizes to continuous-time settings and whether the approach transfers to other dynamic tasks beyond link prediction.
