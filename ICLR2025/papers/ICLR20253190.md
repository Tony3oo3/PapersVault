## Problem

Attention-based models excel in practice but lack theoretical understanding of how they handle token-wise sparsity (relevant information concentrated in a few tokens) and internal linear representations (interpretable directions learned by the model). Prior theoretical studies of Transformers addressed neither feature simultaneously, leaving open why attention is a natural architecture for tasks where a single, randomly-located token determines the output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253190/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a sequence of L token embeddings X_1,...,X_L in R^d, where exactly one token at a latent random position J_0 has mean √(d/2)·k* and covariance γ²I_d, while all other tokens are N(0, I_d). The output is a scalar Y = X_{J_0}^T v* + ξ, a linear projection of the informative token in direction v*. A key design choice is γ² = 1/2, which makes all tokens have the same expected squared norm, so the informative token cannot be identified by magnitude alone—only by its alignment with the unknown direction k*.

## Method

The paper proposes a simplified single-head attention predictor T_λ^(k,v)(X) = erf(λXk)^T Xv, where erf replaces softmax for analytical tractability and k, v ∈ S^{d-1} are learned parameters approximating the oracle directions k*, v*. Three main results are established: (1) the oracle predictor T_λ^(k*,v*) is asymptotically Bayes optimal under the joint scaling L = o(d) with λ√d → ∞ and λ√L → 0, while the optimal linear predictor degrades to the null predictor as L → ∞; (2) the risk depends on (k,v) only through five scalar inner products, reducing the optimization to a low-dimensional system; and (3) projected Riemannian gradient descent on the invariant manifold M = {(k,v) : k^T v* = v^T k* = k^T v = 0} provably converges to (±k*, ±v*) for generic initializations, with the proof relying on a 2D dynamical system in (κ,ν) = (k^T k*, v^T v*) and the Center-Stable Manifold theorem to exclude convergence to saddle points or maxima.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Corollary 2) shows that the oracle attention predictor T_λ(k*,v*) achieves asymptotic Bayes optimality: its risk converges to the irreducible noise floor ε² under the joint scaling d→∞, L=o(d), λ√d→∞, λ√L→0. In stark contrast, the optimal linear predictor (Proposition 3) has risk ε² + γ² − γ⁴Σⱼ pⱼ²/(1+pⱼ(γ²−1)), which degrades to ε² + γ² (i.e., the null predictor) as L→∞ with uniform pⱼ. Theorem 5 further proves that Projected Gradient Descent on the invariant manifold M recovers the oracle parameters (k*,v*) for any sufficiently small step size and generic initialization, despite the non-convexity of the risk landscape.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between the proposed attention-based predictor and the optimal linear predictor on the single-location regression task. The oracle attention predictor T_λ(k*,v*) is provably asymptotically Bayes optimal, achieving risk ε² (the irreducible noise variance) in the high-dimensional regime. The optimal linear predictor, by contrast, cannot exploit the latent location J₀ and its risk converges to ε² + γ² as L grows—exactly the performance of always predicting zero. This gap is illustrated in Figure 3 for concrete parameters (ε²=0, γ=1/√2, λ=1/d^0.4, uniform pⱼ), where the oracle risk decreases with d while the linear risk plateaus at the null-predictor level. The paper also notes that standard sparse methods (Lasso, group-Lasso) fail for the same reason: the random support of the informative token prevents any fixed linear combination from capturing the signal. No empirical benchmark against other neural architectures is provided; the comparison is entirely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper follows the established pattern of studying simplified tasks to understand Transformer capabilities, joining prior work on in-context learning (Ahn et al., 2023; von Oswald et al., 2023), topic structure (Li et al., 2023b), spatial structure (Jelassi et al., 2022), and sparse token selection (Wang et al., 2024). However, it carves new ground by introducing a task that simultaneously captures two features absent from prior theoretical studies: token-wise sparsity with a *random* support (the informative token's location varies per sequence) and internal linear representations that must be learned. The predictor itself is a simplified single-head attention layer where softmax is replaced by a component-wise erf nonlinearity, a substitution supported by empirical work (Qin et al., 2022; Wortsman et al., 2023; Ramapuram et al., 2024) but here justified by enabling closed-form Gaussian expectations. The optimization analysis draws on two-timescale dynamics frameworks (Marion & Berthier, 2023; Berthier et al., 2024) and the Center-Stable Manifold theorem from dynamical systems theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable demonstration that an attention-based predictor is asymptotically Bayes optimal on a task where all linear predictors fundamentally fail (degrading to the null predictor as L grows), establishing that attention's non-linearity is not merely helpful but *necessary* for handling sparse information with random support. The explicit connection between the simplified erf-based predictor and a single attention head (via the [CLS] token and low-rank parameter assumption) provides a mechanistic model for how Transformers build internal linear representations during training. Key open questions include: whether the invariant manifold M is locally stable for general initializations, whether the results extend to stochastic/empirical risk minimization, and whether multi-location variants (relating to multi-head attention) preserve the same guarantees. The task's limitations (fixed sequence length, independent tokens, single informative token) are acknowledged, and extending to more realistic NLP structures remains future work.
