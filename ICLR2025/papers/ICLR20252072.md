## Problem

Auto-regressive models for quantum state property estimation (QPE) impose a fixed sequential ordering on qubits (e.g., left-to-right for 1D chains), but quantum entanglement structures have no inherent ordering. This sequential bias leads to oversimplified representations of multi-qubit correlations, degrading prediction accuracy—especially at large system sizes and under limited measurement data. The paper asks whether a non-autoregressive generative model can learn quantum state distributions without such ordering constraints.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a vector of physical condition variables x ∈ ℝ^{L-1} (Hamiltonian coupling strengths) and, during inference, a standard Gaussian noise vector z_T ∈ ℝ^{L×d}. It outputs discrete measurement strings σ ∈ {1,…,6}^L (one per qubit, from Pauli-6 POVM), which are then post-processed via classical shadow to estimate pairwise correlations C_ij and second-order Rényi entanglement entropy E_ij. During training, the model also consumes M_in measurement records R ∈ ℤ^{M_in×L} collected from the ground state of H(x). All experiments use system sizes L ∈ {10, 40, 70, 100} with d=128 hidden dimensions.

## Method

QuaDiM is a conditional diffusion model that maps discrete quantum measurement records into a continuous latent space via learnable token embeddings plus positional embeddings, then applies a standard Gaussian forward noising process and a transformer-based reverse denoising process. The denoising network is a 4-layer, 4-head transformer (128 hidden dim) that attends over all L qubits simultaneously at each diffusion step, with the physical condition x injected as a global feature via a one-hidden-layer FFN added to the token embeddings (classifier-free conditioning). The training objective combines the KL divergence between the forward and reverse Markov chains with a negative log-likelihood term from a rounding step that maps the final denoised embedding z_0 back to discrete measurement outcomes. This design eliminates any sequential dependency among qubits, allowing the model to capture non-local entanglement correlations without the ordering bias inherent in auto-regressive baselines.

## Results

On the 1D anti-ferromagnetic Heisenberg model, QuaDiM achieves the lowest RMSE for correlation prediction across all system sizes (L=10 to 100) and sample counts (M_out=100 to 20,000). At the largest system size L=100 with M_out=20,000, QuaDiM reaches RMSE 0.0098, outperforming LLM4QPE (0.0114), RNN (0.0163), and Classical Shadow (0.0122). For entanglement entropy prediction at L=100, M_out=20,000, QuaDiM achieves RMSE 0.0243 versus LLM4QPE's 0.0286 and RNN's 0.0439. The advantage is most pronounced at low sample counts (M_out=100) and large system sizes, where RNN degrades to RMSE 0.2325 while QuaDiM maintains 0.1686.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuaDiM outperforms all four baseline families across every system size and sample count in both correlation and entanglement entropy prediction. Against the SOTA auto-regressive transformer LLM4QPE (Tang et al., 2024a), QuaDiM achieves a consistent 8–14% RMSE reduction (e.g., 0.0098 vs. 0.0114 at L=100, M=20000 for correlation), with the gap widening at larger system sizes. The RNN baseline (Carrasquilla et al., 2019) degrades most severely at L=100, reaching RMSE 0.2325 at M=100 versus QuaDiM's 0.1686, confirming the paper's hypothesis that sequential ordering biases hurt entanglement capture. Kernel methods (RBFK, NTK from Huang et al., 2022) remain competitive at L=40 but fall well behind generative models at L≥70, where their fixed feature maps cannot capture the complex nonlocal correlations. Classical Shadow (Huang et al., 2020), the learning-free protocol, is competitive at small M_out but shows diminishing returns at larger sample counts, while QuaDiM continues to improve, indicating the learned distribution captures structure beyond what raw measurement statistics provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

QuaDiM departs from the dominant autoregressive paradigm in quantum state property estimation (RNN, CNN, and transformer-based models like LLM4QPE) by replacing sequential qubit modeling with a non-autoregressive diffusion process that treats all qubits symmetrically. The method directly adapts the Diffusion-LM framework (Li et al., 2022) for discrete-to-continuous embedding and the rounding step from DiffuSeq (Gong et al., 2023) to the quantum measurement setting, while using a standard transformer denoiser conditioned on Hamiltonian parameters via classifier-free guidance. For post-processing, it relies on the classical shadow protocol (Huang et al., 2020), which is shared with all baselines to ensure fair comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine structural limitation of autoregressive QPE models—imposed qubit ordering conflicts with the non-sequential nature of entanglement—and demonstrates that this bias becomes increasingly harmful at larger system sizes (L=100), where RNN-based baselines degrade substantially. However, the evaluation is confined to the 1D anti-ferromagnetic Heisenberg model simulated classically via tensor networks, leaving open whether the advantage persists for genuinely hard-to-simulate systems (2D/3D lattices, long-range interactions beyond the supplementary XY model) or on real NISQ hardware. The diffusion sampling cost (T=2000 steps) and the moderate but consistent RMSE improvements over LLM4QPE suggest the approach is promising but not yet a drop-in replacement for autoregressive methods in resource-constrained settings.
