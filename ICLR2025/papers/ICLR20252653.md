## Problem

Federated policy gradient (FedPG) methods have not been analyzed in terms of global time complexity, and synchronous aggregation is bottlenecked by stragglers in heterogeneous computing settings. Integrating asynchronous federated learning with policy-based FedRL introduces a fundamentally new challenge: unlike supervised FL with fixed datasets, RL agents collect new samples based on the current (and potentially stale) policy in each iteration, making convergence under lagged policies non-trivial and largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252653/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of N agents receives the current global policy parameters θ from the server, collects a local trajectory τ from its own MDP environment (continuous state and action spaces, e.g., MuJoCo tasks), and computes a normalized policy gradient direction d = g(τ, θ̃)/||g(τ, θ̃)|| using GAE-based advantage estimation. The server consumes these gradients asynchronously—applying one update per received gradient—and outputs the updated global policy parameters θ ∈ ℝ^d, which are broadcast back to the contributing agent. Policies are parameterized by fully connected MLPs (64×64 up to 512×512×512 depending on task complexity), and the entire pipeline operates with a single trajectory per agent per iteration.

## Method

AFedPG replaces synchronous FedPG with an asynchronous server that applies one normalized gradient per received update, eliminating the need to wait for the slowest agent. The key technical innovation is a delay-adaptive lookahead rule: after the server updates θ_k ← θ_{k-1} + η_k d_{k-δ_k}/||d_{k-δ_k}||, it sends the agent a 'lookahead' parameter θ̃_k = θ_k + (1-α_{k-δ_k})/α_{k-δ_k} · (θ_k - θ_{k-1}), where the coefficient depends on the delay δ_k of the applied gradient. This lookahead is specifically designed for RL (not a standard momentum step) because the agent will sample new trajectories from π_{θ̃_k}; it algebraically cancels the second-order Hessian correction terms (1-α)∇²J(θ_k)(θ_{k-1}-θ_k) + α∇²J(θ_k)(θ̃_k-θ_k) = 0 that arise from the time-varying sampling distribution and would otherwise obstruct convergence. The paper proves global convergence at rate O(K^{-2/5}) and FOSP convergence at O(K^{-2/7}), yielding O(ε^{-2.5}/N) per-agent sample complexity (linear speedup) and O(1/Σ(1/t_i)) global time complexity, which strictly dominates the synchronous O(t_max/N) bound and is especially advantageous when t_max ≫ t_min.

## Results

AFedPG achieves a global convergence sample complexity of O(ε^{-2.5}/N) per agent, representing a linear speedup over single-agent normalized PG (O(ε^{-2.5})). In terms of global time, AFedPG reduces the per-iteration cost from O(t_max/N) (synchronous FedPG) to O(t̄) where t̄ = 1/Σ(1/t_i) is the harmonic mean of agent times, which is always ≤ t_max/N. Empirically, across four MuJoCo tasks (Swimmer-v4, Hopper-v4, Walker2D-v4, Humanoid-v4), AFedPG with N=8 agents converges to higher rewards faster than single-agent PG, and achieves significantly lower wall-clock time than synchronous FedPG (with t_max ≈ 4×t_min in experiments).

| Method | Sample Complexity (FOSP) | Sample Complexity (Global) | Global Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vanilla PG (Yuan et al., 2022) | O(ε^{-4}) | O(ε^{-3}) | — |
| Normalized PG (Fatkhullin et al., 2023) | O(ε^{-3.5}) | O(ε^{-2.5}) | — |
| FedPG (synchronous) | O(ε^{-3.5}/N) | O(ε^{-2.5}/N) | O((t_max/N)·ε^{-2.5}) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-agent Vanilla PG (Yuan et al., 2022), AFedPG improves the FOSP sample complexity from O(ε^{-4}) to O(ε^{-3.5}/N) and the global convergence rate from O(ε^{-3}) to O(ε^{-2.5}/N), achieving a linear speedup in N. Compared to Normalized PG (Fatkhullin et al., 2023), AFedPG matches the O(ε^{-2.5}) global convergence rate per agent but adds the 1/N federated speedup. Against synchronous FedPG (Lan et al., 2023b), AFedPG preserves the same O(ε^{-2.5}/N) sample complexity while strictly reducing global time from O(t_max/N) to O(t̄), with the gap widening as computing heterogeneity increases (t_max ≫ t_min). In experiments, AFedPG outperforms the A3C baseline (Mnih et al., 2016) with higher final rewards and lower variance across all four MuJoCo tasks. The ablation (AFedPG without delay-adaptive lookahead, labeled 'vanilla' in Figure 4) shows substantially worse convergence, confirming the necessity of the proposed lookahead technique for handling lagged policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) federated policy gradient (FedPG), where Lan et al. (2023b) demonstrated linear speedup in the synchronous setting; (2) normalized policy gradient (Fatkhullin et al., 2023), which achieves O(ε^{-2.5}) global convergence for single-agent PG; and (3) asynchronous federated learning (Xie et al., 2020; Mishchenko et al., 2022; Koloskova et al., 2022), which has been shown to outperform synchronous FL for supervised tasks. The key departure is that all prior A-FL work assumes fixed client-side datasets, whereas RL agents collect new samples dependent on the current policy each iteration. The paper's delay-adaptive lookahead technique is specifically designed to cancel second-order Hessian correction terms that arise uniquely from this online sampling mechanism, a challenge absent from supervised FL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide convergence guarantees for asynchronous federated policy gradient RL, filling a gap that limits the practical deployment of FedRL in large-scale heterogeneous systems where stragglers dominate wall-clock time. The time complexity improvement from O(t_max/N) to O(harmonic mean) is always strictly better and becomes dramatic when computing power is highly heterogeneous (t_max >> t_min), which is the norm in real edge/cloud federated deployments. Open questions include extending adversarial robustness (studied only in synchronous FedRL by Ganesh et al., 2024a) to the asynchronous setting, compatibility with communication-efficient techniques like quantization or low-rank updates, and whether the results extend to natural policy gradient or average-reward formulations.
