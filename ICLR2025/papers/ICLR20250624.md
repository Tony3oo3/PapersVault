## Problem

Parameter-modifying model editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, MEND) progressively degrade the general abilities of LLMs—reasoning, summarization, QA, NLI—as the number of sequential edits increases, yet prior work neither provides a theoretical explanation for this degradation nor proposes a remedy. The paper identifies the escalating condition number of the edited MLP projection matrix as the bottleneck and asks how to restrain it while preserving newly edited knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the second-layer MLP projection matrix W_proj^l (denoted W) of a single Transformer layer and the cumulative edit update matrix Σ_{j=1}^N ΔW_j produced by N sequential edits. It performs SVD on both W and the cumulative update separately, then reconstructs a restrained edited matrix W̄_N = W + Σ σ̄_i û_i v̂_i^T, where σ̄_i are the (possibly restrained) singular values of the cumulative update. The output replaces W_N in the model; the original W is never directly modified, and the restraint is applied only to singular values of the update that exceed max{σ_i} of W.

## Method

Using matrix perturbation theory, the paper proves that the relative perturbation of any original key-value association (k_i, v_i) after an edit is upper-bounded by a function of the condition number κ = σ_max/σ_min of the edited matrix. Empirically, κ grows rapidly with edit count because the maximum singular value of the cumulative update ΣΔW_j inflates while the minimum singular value of W stays roughly constant. PRUNE counters this by applying SVD to ΣΔW_j and replacing any singular value σ̂_i > max{σ_i} with F(σ̂_i) = log_α(σ̂_i) − log_α(max{σ_i}) + max{σ_i}, a log-based compression that caps the update's spectral norm relative to W. This lowers the condition number of the final edited matrix, tightening the perturbation bound on original knowledge while leaving smaller singular values (which carry the actual editing signal) untouched. The framework is plug-and-play: it is applied once after all N edits are accumulated and can be coupled with ROME, MEMIT, or MEND without modifying their internal editing logic.

## Results

On the ConceptEdit dataset with LLaMA-2 (7B) and ROME, after 200 sequential edits, unrestrained ROME collapses to 0% on reasoning and NLI, while ROME+PRUNE retains 72.9% reasoning and 46.15% NLI accuracy. On the ZsRE dataset (Figures 3–4), MEMIT+PRUNE at 100 edits keeps downstream task performance close to the unedited model, whereas unrestrained MEMIT drops to near 0. For editing performance, PRUNE preserves efficacy, generalization, and locality substantially better than unrestrained baselines at 100–200 edits, while matching them at low edit counts (≤20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRUNE is evaluated as a plug-and-play wrapper on three parameter-modifying editing methods: ROME, MEMIT, and MEND. Against ROME (Meng et al., 2022), PRUNE dramatically improves general abilities at high edit counts — at 200 edits on ConceptEdit, reasoning goes from 0% to 72.9% and NLI from 0% to 46.15% — while editing efficacy at 20 edits actually improves (49.15% → 75.66%). Against MEMIT (Meng et al., 2023), the gap is most striking: at 100 edits on ZsRE, unrestrained MEMIT's downstream performance collapses to near 0, whereas MEMIT+PRUNE remains close to the unedited baseline; editing locality also stays substantially higher with PRUNE. Against MEND (Mitchell et al., 2022a), PRUNE provides the smallest absolute gain because MEND degrades fastest (significant loss after only 20 edits), and PRUNE can extend preservation to roughly 20 edits but cannot fully recover MEND's rapid collapse. Across all three baselines, PRUNE matches or slightly exceeds unrestrained editing performance at low edit counts (≤20) and provides large improvements at high edit counts (≥100), confirming that the spectral restraint removes redundant overfitting without harming newly edited knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential model editing literature (Gu et al. 2024; Gupta et al. 2024b; Lin et al. 2024), which empirically documented the degradation of general abilities with increasing edits but offered neither a theoretical explanation nor a principled remedy. It repurposes classical matrix perturbation theory (Stewart & Sun, 1990; Wedin, 1972) to identify the condition number of the edited MLP weight matrix as the governing factor of knowledge perturbation, and derives a plug-and-play spectral regularization (PRUNE) that constrains the cumulative edit update matrix's singular values. Unlike prior regularization-based approaches for model editing (e.g., Gu et al. 2024's weight decay), PRUNE operates directly on the SVD of the accumulated edit delta rather than on individual edit steps, making it a post-hoc correction applicable to any parameter-modifying editor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The condition-number bottleneck provides a clean, interpretable diagnostic for practitioners: monitoring κ(W) during sequential editing gives an early warning of impending general-ability collapse, and the log-based singular-value restraint is a minimal-compute fix that preserves editing efficacy while stabilizing downstream performance across 100+ edits. Key limitations include the restriction to single-fact sequential editing (batch-sequential editing remains unaddressed), the fact that MEND's rapid degradation cannot be fully rescued even with PRUNE, and the observation that conceptual knowledge editing degrades faster than factual editing, suggesting the spectral analysis may need refinement for non-factual knowledge. Open questions include whether the condition-number framework generalizes to parameter-preserving methods, to multi-layer simultaneous editing, and to much larger models where the singular-value landscape may differ.
