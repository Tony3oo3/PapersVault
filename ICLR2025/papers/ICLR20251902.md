## Problem

Existing black-box prompt learning methods (e.g., BBT) initialize continuous prompts from randomly sampled discrete tokens, which limits adaptability across diverse downstream tasks and models. While discrete prompt optimization methods exist separately, no prior work jointly optimizes both the discrete token selection and the continuous embedding refinement in a collaborative manner, leaving a gap in how task-specific signals guide the transition from discrete to continuous prompt space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251902/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only forward-pass evaluations of a black-box PTM (no gradients or internal parameters), a downstream dataset of text inputs with labels, and a surrogate embedding function to map discrete tokens into the embedding space. The prompt is decomposed into two components: n discrete tokens sampled from a learned per-position categorical distribution over a vocabulary of size |V|, and a continuous vector z ∈ R^d (d=500) projected back to the D-dimensional embedding space via a random matrix A. The output is the optimized vocabulary distribution parameters α, the continuous vector z, and the resulting task prediction from the PTM.

## Method

ZO-PoG alternates between two optimization steps per iteration: (1) discrete prompt optimization, where per-position token distributions are reparameterized via Gumbel-Softmax and updated using a variance-reduced policy gradient estimator (subtracting the batch-mean loss as a baseline), and (2) continuous prompt optimization, where the low-dimensional vector z is updated via a symmetric-difference zeroth-order gradient estimator with Gaussian perturbations. The key design choice is that the discrete distribution is optimized in parameter space (α) rather than token-probability space, which mitigates bias from the Gumbel-Softmax approximation. The alternating schedule ensures that task-specific signals from the discrete tokens inform the continuous refinement and vice versa. The paper proves sub-linear convergence (O(1/T) rate) under block-wise smoothness and bounded variance assumptions, with total query complexity O(√(nκ)/ε³) forward passes.

## Results

On RoBERTa-large in the 16-shot setting, ZO-PoG achieves the best test accuracy across all five GLUE datasets at both prompt lengths (20 and 50). The most notable gain is on WNLI with prompt length 50, where ZO-PoG reaches 56.34% accuracy, a 5.17% improvement over the best baseline (SSPT at 51.17%). On CoLA (Len 20), ZO-PoG scores 56.79% vs. BBT's 56.18%, and on QNLI (Len 20) it achieves 55.44% vs. BDPL's 55.25%. The method also generalizes to decoder-only models (GPT2-XL, Llama3), consistently outperforming baselines on NLI tasks, though all prompt-learning methods underperform manual prompts on CoLA for decoder-only architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZO-PoG outperforms all three baselines (BBT, BDPL, SSPT) on every dataset and prompt length for RoBERTa-large, GPT2-XL, and Llama3. Against BBT (continuous-only, CMA-ES), ZO-PoG gains the most on WNLI (+3.76 pp at Len 20, +11.74 pp at Len 50) and CoLA (+0.61 pp at Len 20, +6.42 pp at Len 50), confirming that joint discrete-continuous optimization surpasses pure continuous tuning. Against BDPL (discrete-only, policy gradient), ZO-PoG leads on CoLA by a wide margin (+17.96 pp at Len 20) and on WNLI (+14.08 pp at Len 20), showing that adding the continuous ZO component substantially improves over discrete-only optimization. Against SSPT (subspace-selected continuous), ZO-PoG wins on WNLI by 5.17 pp (Len 50) and CoLA by 6.58 pp (Len 20), while the gap on MNLI is narrow (42.53 vs. 42.44 at Len 50). The consistent wins across all three backbone models and both prompt lengths indicate that the collaborative discrete-continuous strategy is robust to model architecture and prompt budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZO-PoG unifies two previously separate lines of black-box prompt learning: continuous prompt optimization in low-dimensional intrinsic subspaces (BBT, BBTv2, SSPT) and discrete prompt optimization via policy gradient (BDPL, RL-Prompt). The key architectural departure is the alternating collaboration between a Gumbel-Softmax-reparameterized policy gradient step for discrete token selection and a zeroth-order gradient step for continuous embedding refinement, rather than treating these as independent pipelines. The variance-reduced policy gradient estimator is adapted from Diao et al. (2022), and the low-dimensional subspace + ZO gradient framework follows Sun et al. (2022b), but the joint optimization objective and convergence analysis for the alternating scheme are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in LMaaS environments where random discrete prompt initialization limits task adaptability, and provides the first convergence guarantee (sub-linear rate) for a method that jointly optimizes both discrete and continuous prompts in a black-box setting. However, the experimental scope is limited to GLUE-scale datasets with a restricted 100-token vocabulary, and the method still requires access to the embedding space, limiting direct applicability to fully opaque APIs like GPT-4 (though an adaptation is sketched in the appendix). Open questions include whether the collaborative approach scales to larger vocabularies, longer prompts, and truly discrete-only black-box LLMs without embedding access.
