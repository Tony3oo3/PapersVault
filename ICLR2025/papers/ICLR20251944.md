## Problem

PPCA and its extensions model data in a linear Euclidean coordinate system around the mean, failing to capture nonlinear manifold structure present in many datasets such as neural population activity. Prior nonlinear PPCA extensions (PPGA, torus PPCA) require data to lie precisely on a manifold without deviation, which does not match real data that is distributed *around* a manifold with noise. This paper addresses the gap by developing a probabilistic model that incorporates a pre-fitted nonlinear manifold while explicitly modeling deviations from it.

## Input / Output

The method takes T high-dimensional observations y_{1:T} ∈ R^n, a pre-fitted l-dimensional nonlinear manifold φ(z) (e.g., a cubic spline fitted via K-means and TSP), an orthonormal distribution coordinate K(z) at each manifold point (either Euclidean-aligned or geometric/tangent-normal), M discretized landmarks on the manifold, and a user-chosen model dimension m. It outputs the loading matrix C ∈ R^{n×m}, isotropic noise variance σ², the discretized manifold state distribution p(z) as weights ω_{1:M}, and the data log-likelihood. A key structural feature is that the manifold and coordinate must be supplied externally (via TDA, splines, etc.), and the method can simultaneously perform hypothesis testing between Euclidean and geometric coordinates by comparing log-likelihoods of two fitted models.

## Method

PGPCA generalizes PPCA with the generative model y_t = φ(z_t) + K(z_t)Cx_t + r_t, where φ(z_t) places the observation on the manifold, K(z_t) is an orthonormal distribution coordinate capturing the local frame, C is the loading matrix, and r_t is isotropic noise. The critical theoretical insight is that all nonlinear manifold and coordinate information collapses into a single matrix Γ(q) = (1/T)Σ_i Σ_j K_{z_j}'(y_i − φ_{z_j})(y_i − φ_{z_j})'K_{z_j} q_i(z_j), making the log-likelihood algebraically identical to PPCA's except for replacing the data scatter matrix with Γ(q). This enables a fully analytical EM algorithm: the E-step computes the posterior q_i(z) over M discretized landmarks, and the M-step solves for C as the top-m eigenvectors of Γ(q) with eigenvalue-shifted singular values, σ² as the mean of the remaining eigenvalues, and p(z) via a simple averaging of posteriors. The geometric coordinate K(z) is constructed from tangent vectors (and their cross product for 2D manifolds) plus orthogonal complements via Gram-Schmidt, contrasting with the Euclidean coordinate that simply aligns with R^n axes at every point.

## Results

PGPCA is evaluated using data log-likelihood (higher/less negative is better) across simulated manifolds (loop in R², loop in R¹⁰, torus in R³) and real neural firing rate data from two mice (Mouse12, Mouse28). On real data, PGPCA (GeCOV) achieves log-likelihoods of −31.758 (Mouse12) and −24.752 (Mouse28), substantially outperforming PPCA (−34.622 and −29.316) and FA (−34.615 and −29.310). In simulations, PGPCA with the correct distribution coordinate consistently achieves higher log-likelihood than the mismatched coordinate (e.g., −27.921 vs. −28.484 for loop in R¹⁰, true GeCOV), and PGPCA outperforms PPCA by 2.8–4.5 log-likelihood units on real data. PGPCA also performs dimensionality reduction (any m ∈ [0,n]) and jointly learns the manifold state distribution p(z) while maintaining its ability to distinguish Euclidean from geometric coordinates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGPCA is compared against PPCA (Tipping & Bishop, 1999b) and maximum-likelihood Factor Analysis (FA) (Bartholomew et al., 2011) on both simulations and real neural data. PGPCA consistently outperforms both baselines across all conditions: on real mouse data, PGPCA (GeCOV) exceeds PPCA by ~2.9 log-likelihood units (Mouse12) and ~4.6 units (Mouse28), while FA performs nearly identically to PPCA at full rank (as expected theoretically). In simulations, the gap is even larger for the loop in R¹⁰ (PGPCA GeCOV: −27.921 vs. PPCA: −31.945, a gap of ~4.0). Critically, PGPCA with the *correct* distribution coordinate (GeCOV or EuCOV) always achieves higher log-likelihood than the *mismatched* coordinate, enabling hypothesis testing between coordinate systems—something neither PPCA nor FA can do since they assume a single Euclidean coordinate around the data mean. The paper also notes that at full rank (m=n), PPCA and FA are mathematically equivalent, and their near-identical log-likelihoods in Table 2 confirm this.

## Relation to Prior Work

PGPCA directly generalizes Probabilistic PCA (Tipping & Bishop, 1999b) by replacing the fixed Euclidean coordinate system around the data mean with a distribution coordinate K(z) attached to a user-specified nonlinear manifold φ(z). Prior nonlinear extensions of PPCA—Probabilistic Principal Geodesic Analysis (Zhang & Fletcher, 2013) and Torus PPCA (Nodehi et al., 2020)—require data to lie precisely on the manifold, whereas PGPCA explicitly models deviations from and noise around the manifold. The EM derivation is elegant in that all manifold and coordinate information is absorbed into a single matrix Γ(q), reducing the M-step to the same eigenvalue problem as classical PPCA, while the E-step handles the nonlinear posterior over manifold states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PGPCA fills a practical gap for neuroscience: neural population activity is known to distribute around nonlinear manifolds (rings in head-direction circuits, tori in hippocampal grid cells), yet prior probabilistic dimensionality reduction methods could not incorporate this geometry while also modeling the residual spread around it. The ability to perform hypothesis testing between Euclidean and geometric distribution coordinates is a novel diagnostic that revealed, on real mouse data, that residual noise follows the manifold geometry rather than being isotropic in embedding space. Key limitations are that PGPCA is static (no auto-correlation modeling), assumes stationarity, and requires the manifold to be pre-fitted by external methods (splines, TDA); extending it to dynamical or adaptive settings is identified as important future work.
