key_contribution: Proposes CenPre, a centrality-guided graph pre-training framework that integrates degree centrality (local node importance) and eigenvector centrality (global structural importance) into node representation learning via a degree predictor, a CLIP-inspired contrastive alignment (CReSP) using truncated-SVD structural embeddings and cross-attention, and an L2 representation alignment loss, achieving state-of-the-art performance on node classification, link prediction, and graph classification across 13 benchmark datasets.
limitations: The cross-attention mechanism incurs O(n²) complexity, limiting scalability to very large graphs, and the centrality-based structural representations are pre-computed statically, which may not capture structural changes in dynamic or evolving graphs. The paper does not explore alternative centrality measures (betweenness, closeness, PageRank) beyond degree and eigenvector centrality, and the optimal balance of the three loss components (λ₁, λ₂, λ₃) requires dataset-specific tuning.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that the graph representation alignment loss (a simple L2 norm between pre-trained and original embeddings) is the most critical component in the ablation, causing a larger performance drop (7.12% on Cora-Node) than either centrality loss individually. This suggests that the primary benefit of CenPre comes not from the centrality signals per se, but from the regularization effect of anchoring representations to a semantic prior — a somewhat counterintuitive result for a paper whose headline contribution is centrality-guided learning. ## Problem

Existing self-supervised graph pre-training methods learn structural information by perturbing or reconstructing graphs but treat all nodes uniformly, ignoring that nodes play different roles in the graph structure. This uniform treatment prevents the model from exploiting the distinct importance of nodes when learning graph representations, potentially losing critical structural signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252335/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph specified by its adjacency matrix and node feature matrix, and produces structure-fused node embeddings via a GNN encoder. A key implementation detail is that the adjacency matrix is pre-processed via truncated SVD to extract top-k left singular vectors as structural representations, which are then fused with GNN-encoded node features through cross-attention. The resulting embeddings are frozen and fed into a downstream MLP for node classification, link prediction, or graph classification.

## Method

CenPre integrates node importance into graph pre-training through three jointly optimized modules. First, a node-level importance module trains a degree predictor to map each node's GNN representation to its degree, encouraging nodes with similar degrees to receive comparable embeddings. Second, a graph-level importance module computes truncated SVD of the adjacency matrix to obtain structural embeddings, uses cross-attention to fuse them with GNN representations, and applies a CLIP-inspired contrastive loss (CReSP) that aligns node representations with their structural patterns, maximizing similarity for nodes sharing important neighbors. Third, a representation alignment module imposes an L2-norm penalty between the pre-trained encoder's output and the original encoder's output, preventing semantic drift while learning structural information. The overall loss is a weighted sum of the three components (λ₁=1, λ₂=1, λ₃=5), and the framework is encoder-agnostic, demonstrated with GCN and GIN backbones.

## Results

CenPre achieves the best average rank (1.0) across all three downstream tasks. On node classification, it reaches 85.15% on Cora, 76.94% on CiteSeer, 83.91% on PubMed, 91.22% on Computer, 93.96% on Photo, and 72.47% on arXiv. On link prediction, it attains 97.44% AUC on Cora, 99.02% AUC on CiteSeer, 99.82% AUC on PubMed, and 66.03% Hit@50 on Collab. On graph classification, it scores 88.13% on NCI1, 80.25% on PROTEINS, 85.18% on DD, 94.74% on MUTAG, 78.05% on IMDB-B, and 91.20% on RDT-B, outperforming all 27 baselines on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CenPre outperforms all 27 baselines on every dataset across node classification, link prediction, and graph classification. In node classification, the closest competitor is MaskGAE_p (A.R. 2.7), which CenPre surpasses by 0.85% on Cora, 3.14% on CiteSeer, and 1.31% on arXiv; GraphMAE (A.R. 3.8) trails by up to 3.54% on CiteSeer. In link prediction, CenPre leads MaskGAE_p by 0.99% AUC on Cora and 0.98% AUC on PubMed, while on Collab Hit@50 it edges out MaskGAE_p by 0.05%. In graph classification, CenPre dominates the nearest baseline TopoGCL (A.R. 2.8) by 6.83% on NCI1, 2.95% on PROTEINS, 6.03% on DD, 4.65% on MUTAG, 3.38% on IMDB-B, and 0.80% on RDT-B. Supervised methods (GCN, GAT, GIN, SAGE) and kernel methods (WL, DGK) are consistently outperformed by large margins, confirming the advantage of the centrality-guided self-supervised pre-training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

CenPre extends the self-supervised graph pre-training literature (GraphCL, DGI, GraphMAE, MaskGAE) by incorporating graph-theoretic centrality as an explicit structural signal during pre-training. It departs from prior centrality-based methods (Jin et al. 2020, Hu et al. 2019b) that relied on degree-only or ranking-based centrality losses, by jointly leveraging degree centrality (local) and eigenvector centrality (global) and fusing them into node representations via a CLIP-inspired contrastive alignment (CReSP) with cross-attention. The representation alignment module, which anchors pre-trained embeddings to the original encoder output, addresses a practical concern absent in prior centrality-guided approaches: preserving semantic information while learning structural importance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly encoding node importance through centrality during pre-training yields consistent gains across node classification, link prediction, and graph classification on 13 datasets, suggesting that uniform neighborhood aggregation in standard GNNs leaves exploitable structural signal on the table. The ablation revealing that the simple L2 alignment loss is the single most critical component (larger drop than either centrality loss) is a practical insight for anyone designing multi-objective pre-training. Open questions include scalability beyond the O(n²) cross-attention to very large graphs, whether the static pre-computed centrality representations remain valid for dynamic or evolving graphs, and whether other centrality measures (betweenness, closeness, PageRank) can be incorporated without additional computational overhead.
