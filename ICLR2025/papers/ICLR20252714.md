## Problem

Existing transformer-based meta-RL methods (TrMRL, AMAGO) process long sequences of transitions spanning multiple episodes to extract task meta-features, incurring quadratic runtime complexity in the total sequence length. RNN-based alternatives suffer from vanishing gradients and limited memory, capturing primarily intra-episode dynamics. The paper addresses how to efficiently distill both intra-episode and cross-episode experience into task representations that enable strong few-shot adaptation and generalization to unseen tasks, without the computational overhead of a single monolithic sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252714/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes E episodes of experience, each represented by a sampled sequence of T transitions (state, action, reward, termination flag), where the current episode's sequence is constrained to end at the agent's current state observation. For image-based tasks (ManiSkill), states are RGB frames processed through a CNN before embedding. The policy outputs parameters of a Gaussian action distribution (mean and variance), conditioned jointly on the learned task representation and a linear projection of the current state, enabling stochastic action sampling without gradient-based adaptation at test time.

## Method

ECET employs a two-tiered hierarchical transformer: an Intra-Episodic Transformer (IET) with positional encoding processes T transitions from each episode independently to produce a K-dimensional episode embedding, while a Cross-Episodic Transformer (CET) without positional encoding (since episodes form an unordered set) aggregates the E episode embeddings into a D-dimensional task representation. This decomposition reduces attention complexity from O(E²T²) for a flat transformer to O(E² + T²), enabling longer contexts at lower cost. The policy is conditioned on the task representation concatenated with a linear transformation of the current state, and the entire pipeline is trained with PPO as the outer-loop meta-learning algorithm following the RL² framework. Ablations confirm that positional encoding in CET is harmful (episodes lack causal ordering) and that the flat single-transformer architecture fails to capture comprehensive task representations.

## Results

On the Meta-World ML10 benchmark (10 train / 5 unseen test tasks), ECET is the only method achieving a meta-training success rate above 0.8, roughly 20% higher than all baselines, and reaches a test success rate of ~0.35 on unseen tasks. On the harder ML45 benchmark (45 train / 5 test), ECET exceeds 0.6 meta-training success rate and ~0.4 test success rate. On ManiSkill PickSingleYCB (few-shot adaptation to unseen objects), ECET achieves the best test success rate of 0.39, while all baselines except TrMRL struggle to generalize. On ML1 Push, both ECET and TrMRL reach test success rates above 0.8, with ECET showing higher generalizability across parametric variations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECET outperforms all six baselines across the majority of benchmarks. Against TrMRL (the closest competitor), ECET achieves higher meta-training success rates on ML10 and ML45 and better OOD test performance (~0.35 vs. lower for TrMRL on ML10), while TrMRL remains competitive on ML1 Push where both exceed 0.8 test success rate. AMAGO, the only other cross-episode transformer method, is compared only on ML10/ML45 and fails to learn policies that successfully identify the different training tasks within the 5×10⁷ timestep budget. VariBAD shows robust performance on MuJoCo parametric tasks but struggles on Meta-World OOD settings. RL2 PPO exhibits high variance and fails to robustly generalize, while MAML PPO and PEARL consistently underperform across all benchmarks. On ManiSkill PickSingleYCB, ECET (0.39 test success rate) is the only method that effectively generalizes to unseen objects, with all baselines except TrMRL failing to adapt.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECET extends the RL² framework of generating task meta-features from transition sequences, replacing the RNN backbone with a hierarchical two-tier transformer architecture. The Intra-Episodic Transformer (IET) generalizes TrMRL's single-transformer processing of recent transitions, while the Cross-Episodic Transformer (CET) provides a more efficient alternative to AMAGO's flat transformer over long cross-episode sequences, reducing attention complexity from O(E²T²) to O(E²+T²). The key architectural departure is the explicit separation of intra- and cross-episodic processing into distinct transformer stacks, with CET operating on a set (no positional encoding) of episode-level representations rather than a single long sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical decomposition offers a practical path to scaling cross-episode meta-RL to longer histories without the quadratic blowup that limits AMAGO and TrMRL, making it more accessible for compute-constrained settings. The ablation revealing that positional encoding in CET actively hurts performance—because episodes lack a natural causal ordering—provides a useful design principle for set-based transformer encoders in RL. However, generalization to tasks completely outside the meta-training distribution remains limited, and the authors acknowledge that task augmentation, modified adaptation phases, or increased model capacity are needed to address this gap. The method's reliance on shared state/action spaces across tasks also constrains its applicability to more heterogeneous task collections.
