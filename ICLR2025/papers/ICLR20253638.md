## Problem

Mamba models, despite their theoretically unbounded receptive field from recurrent selective memory, exhibit a limited effective receptive field (ERF) that is tightly constrained by the sequence length used during training. This implicit bias causes attention-like weights to collapse exponentially for sequences longer than the training context, preventing effective length extrapolation. The paper identifies this mechanism and proposes a training-free context-extension method to overcome it.

## Input / Output

The method accepts long text sequences that can be orders of magnitude longer than the training context (e.g., trained on 2K tokens, evaluated on 128K–512K tokens) and produces standard outputs: next-token perplexity, document retrieval IDs, free-form QA answers, and summarization text. Decimation is applied only during the pre-fill (parallel) phase of inference; during autoregressive decoding, DeciMamba operates identically to vanilla Mamba with no additional overhead. The sequence is progressively compressed across selected layers, with the first decimating layer reducing the context to L_base tokens and subsequent layers applying a geometric decay factor β.

## Method

DeciMamba exploits a hidden filtering mechanism intrinsic to the S6 layer: the per-token selective recurrent gate Δ_t, which controls how much each input token influences the hidden state. The method interprets the channel-averaged Δ_t values as token importance scores and applies a Top-P_s pooling operation at selected layers (chosen via the Mamba Mean Distance metric to target layers with the largest ERFs), discarding low-importance tokens before the SSM computation. The number of retained tokens decreases geometrically with depth (P_s = L_base · β^s), ensuring each decimating layer sees a sequence length comparable to its training length. The approach requires no retraining—decimation is applied directly to pre-trained Mamba weights—and reduces inference complexity from O(L log L) to O(L + P log P), yielding roughly 2× speedup at 128K context while extending effective extrapolation from ~16K to 128K tokens.

## Results

On LongBench (zero-shot, Mamba-2.8B), DeciMamba improves the overall LB score on most tasks, with the largest gain on TriviaQA (3.93 → 12.61, +220%) and MultiFieldQA (11.16 → 18.58). On Passkey Retrieval (trained on 2K tokens), Mamba-130M extrapolates only to 16K while DeciMamba-130M reaches 128K. On PG-19 perplexity, Mamba-130M degrades beyond ~5× training length, whereas DeciMamba-130M maintains reasonable perplexity up to ~20× training length. Inference is approximately 2× faster for DeciMamba vs. Mamba (e.g., 4.22 s vs. 8.17 s at 512K context).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba (the primary baseline), DeciMamba consistently wins on long-context extrapolation: on LongBench it improves the 4-8k and 8k+ groups by 50–220% across most tasks, while the 0-4k group (within training length) shows only modest changes. On Passkey Retrieval, Mamba-130M drops to 0% success beyond 16K tokens, whereas DeciMamba-130M maintains 100% success up to 128K. On PG-19 perplexity, Mamba-130M becomes unusable beyond ~10K context, while DeciMamba-130M stays within a few points of the lower-bound model trained at the same length up to ~40K. Against equivalent Pythia Transformers, Mamba already outperforms them on length generalization (Pythia-160M fails at 4K on passkey retrieval; Pythia-2.8B hits infinite perplexity beyond 4K on PG-19), and DeciMamba extends this advantage further, reaching 80K context with finite perplexity where both Mamba and Pythia fail. DeciMamba also achieves ~2× faster inference than Mamba at long context lengths due to the decimation reducing the effective sequence length processed by deeper layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mamba's S6 selective state-space layers (Gu & Dao, 2024) and the implicit attention formulation of S6 (Ali et al., 2024; Zimerman et al., 2024), which reveals a data-driven attention-like structure within the recurrent computation. It departs from the Transformer context-extension literature (positional interpolation, LongLoRA, Landmark Attention) by designing the first context-extension method specifically for SSMs, leveraging the intrinsic Δ_t selective gate as a token importance signal rather than relying on positional encodings or architectural modifications. The decimation strategy conceptually connects to prompt compression and token pruning work, but is uniquely grounded in the SSM's own recurrent dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a limited effective receptive field in Mamba—despite its theoretically unbounded recurrent memory—is a diagnostically important finding that reframes how the community should think about SSM length generalization. The Mamba Mean Distance metric provides a practical tool for auditing ERF across layers, and the training-free decimation approach offers an immediately deployable solution for practitioners working with pre-trained Mamba models. Key limitations remain: the method is a post-hoc patch rather than an architectural fix, pooling may discard critical tokens in adversarial or dense-information scenarios, and the approach is specific to architectures with per-token selective gates. Open questions include whether ERF limitations can be addressed during pre-training and whether the technique generalizes to other gated recurrent architectures like Griffin or HGRN.
