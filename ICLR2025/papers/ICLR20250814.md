## Problem

Audio self-supervised learning (SSL) models are predominantly trained and benchmarked on monophonic audio (single-source environmental sounds, speech), yet real-world audio is inherently polyphonic with multiple overlapping sources. These models are widely deployed in frozen form within multimodal LLMs and other systems under the assumption that pre-training has equipped them for realistic audio, but their polyphonic generalization remains unexplored. Furthermore, the standard pre-training dataset AudioSet is not truly polyphonic—only ~42% of its multi-label files contain at least two distinct sound events—making it insufficient for learning robust polyphonic representations.

## Input / Output

Input audio waveforms are resampled to 16 kHz and converted to 128-bin log-mel spectrograms (25 ms Hanning window, 10 ms hop). During pre-training, pairs of spectrograms are combined via element-wise max in the log-mel domain, with partial mixing applied to only t/2 of the clip duration across three non-contiguous regions to preserve the original content. The model outputs a CLS-token embedding for downstream multi-label audio event tagging (e.g., AudioSet, SPASS) and, during pre-training, predicts masked patch-level representations through a lightweight 6-layer CNN decoder.

## Method

SSLAM extends masked latent bootstrapping (data2vec-style) by feeding the student encoder a partially mixed log-mel spectrogram (element-wise max of two sources) while the EMA teacher processes the individual sources separately and averages their layer outputs as the target. A novel Source Retention Loss (SRL) is introduced: the teacher processes each source independently (discarding unmixed-region tokens), and the student's mixed-audio patch predictions are regressed toward the average of the two teacher representations, explicitly preserving each source's distinct characteristics within the mixture. Training follows a two-stage curriculum—Stage 1 (10 epochs) uses only unmixed audio with standard global/local losses, then Stage 2 (5 epochs) splits the batch half-mixed/half-unmixed and activates all five objectives (unmixed global/local, mixed global/local, and SRL). Key design choices include using only the final teacher layer for the mixed global loss (to avoid over-compression), all 12 layers for local losses, and partial rather than full mixing to balance novelty with content preservation.

## Results

On the standard audio SSL benchmark, SSLAM achieves a new SOTA mAP of 50.2 on AudioSet-2M (a 3.9% relative improvement over the previous best of 48.6) and 40.9 mAP on AudioSet-20K, while also reaching 98.8% accuracy on Speech Commands KS1. On purely monophonic datasets (ESC-50, KS2), SSLAM is competitive but slightly trails the best prior methods (96.2 vs. 98.1 on ESC-50; 98.1 vs. 98.9 on KS2). On polyphonic datasets, SSLAM sets new SOTA in both linear evaluation and fine-tuning, with improvements of up to 9.1% mAP on SPASS (Market) and up to 9.7% on the Degrees of Polyphony dataset at the {8,9} polyphony level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSLAM achieves the best mAP on AS-2M (50.2) and AS-20K (40.9), surpassing the closest competitors BEATs_iter3+, A-JEPA, and EAT (all at 48.6 on AS-2M) by 1.6 mAP, and EAT (40.2 on AS-20K) by 0.7 mAP. On the speech benchmark KS1, SSLAM leads with 98.8%, edging out ASiT (98.2%) and BEATs_iter3+ (98.1%). However, on the purely monophonic ESC-50 dataset, SSLAM (96.2%) trails BEATs_iter3+ (98.1%) by 1.9 points and A-JEPA (96.3%) by a narrow 0.1 point, and on KS2 it falls short of ASiT (98.9%) by 0.8 points. Against older baselines such as SS-AST, MAE-AST, MaskSpec, MSM-MAE, data2vec, and Audio-MAE, SSLAM wins across all reported metrics by substantial margins (e.g., +19.2 mAP over data2vec on AS-20K, +10.6 mAP over MaskSpec on AS-2M). The paper's key differentiator is that SSLAM is the only method to simultaneously improve on the multi-label AudioSet benchmarks while maintaining competitive monophonic performance, whereas prior SOTA methods like BEATs_iter3+ and ASiT sacrifice polyphonic capability for monophonic accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSLAM builds directly on the masked latent bootstrapping paradigm established by data2vec (Baevski et al., 2022) and refined by EAT (Chen et al., 2024) and A-JEPA (Fei et al., 2024), adopting their EMA-teacher self-distillation with multi-layer target averaging and inverse block multi-masking. The key departure is introducing audio mixtures as a pre-training signal: while prior polyphonic audio work (SPASS, URBAN-SED, IDMT-DESED-FL) focused on supervised training or dataset construction, and prior SSL methods (BEATs, ASiT, MaskSpec) were evaluated almost exclusively on monophonic benchmarks, no prior SSL framework explicitly incorporated polyphonic-aware objectives. The mixing strategy draws on CASA's Ideal Binary Mask (element-wise max in log-mel spectrogram), and the novel Source Retention Loss is positioned as a complement to the standard global/local losses, while the authors also explore and reject Mixture Invariant Training (MixIT) for concept separation due to its independence assumption and combinatorial complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: audio SSL models are widely deployed in frozen state within multi-modal LLMs and real-world systems, yet their polyphonic robustness was never systematically evaluated. The empirical finding that only ~42.5% of AudioSet files contain truly distinct sound events (decreasing to 35.6% at hierarchy level 4) provides concrete evidence that multi-label ≠ polyphonic, motivating the need for explicit polyphonic pre-training. The approach is lightweight (no architectural changes, just a mixing strategy and one additional loss) and could be readily adopted by the community. Open questions include whether the method generalizes beyond two-source mixing, whether it transfers to other modalities, and whether the MixIT-based concept separation can be made to work with relaxed independence constraints.
