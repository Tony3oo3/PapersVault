## Problem

Vision-language-action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation map current image observations to control actions but lack awareness of the robot's past movements, making decisions reactive to the present rather than informed by spatial history. This limitation causes poor performance on complex manipulation tasks requiring understanding of kinematics and temporal dynamics, as simply conditioning on the current frame is insufficient for reliable action prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251950/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two 256×256 RGB images per timestep — the original observation and a trace-overlay version with M=5 active point trajectories rendered on top — separated by a special separator token, plus a text instruction prompt. The trace overlay is generated by Co-Tracker tracking a 40×40 grid over N=6 past frames, filtering for points whose cumulative L1 displacement exceeds a threshold κ, and sampling 5 active trajectories. Output is a sequence of discretized action tokens (256 bins per action dimension via quantile-based binning) appended to the VLM vocabulary.

## Method

TraceVLA introduces visual trace prompting: an off-the-shelf dense point tracker (Co-Tracker) extracts K×K point trajectories from a sliding window of N historical frames, active points with significant cumulative movement are identified, and M=5 sampled trajectories are overlaid as colored traces on the current observation. Both the original image and the trace-overlay image are fed to the VLA backbone (OpenVLA 7B or a 4B Phi-3-Vision variant) with a separator token, and a dropout mechanism (probability α) randomly replaces the trace image with the original during training so the model remains robust when Co-Tracker fails at test time. The key insight is that compact visual traces convey spatial-temporal context far more effectively than concatenating raw historical frames (which cause 6% performance degradation due to visual redundancy) or text-based coordinate descriptions (which add ~150 tokens and underutilize the vision encoder). Fine-tuning on 150K annotated trajectories from BridgeData-v2, RT-1, and WidowX data yields 7.5% average improvement over OpenVLA on SimplerEnv and 3.5× improvement on real-robot tasks, with inference overhead of only ~0.036 seconds per step from sparse Co-Tracker queries.

## Results

On SimplerEnv (137 configurations across 3 Google robot tasks), TraceVLA achieves 47.7% overall success rate, outperforming OpenVLA (40.2%) by 7.5 percentage points, with per-task gains ranging from 2.4% to 12.7%. The 4B TraceVLA-Phi3 variant reaches 44.0% overall versus OpenVLA-Phi3's 39.9%. On real WidowX-250 robot tasks, TraceVLA achieves a 3.5× improvement over OpenVLA (e.g., 8/10 vs 1/10 on pick-place corn), and on LIBERO multitask benchmarks, TraceVLA finetuned attains 74.8% average success rate versus 70.6% for OpenVLA finetuned.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenVLA (7B), TraceVLA wins on all six SimplerEnv sub-metrics, with the largest gain on Pick Coke Can under visual matching (+12.7%) and Open/Close Drawer under variant aggregation (+8.5%), yielding a 7.5-point overall improvement. The 4B TraceVLA-Phi3 similarly outperforms OpenVLA-Phi3 across all sub-metrics, with the strongest gain on Open/Close Drawer under visual matching (+8.5%). Against RT1-X (35M), TraceVLA achieves a higher overall score (47.7% vs 45.8%), though RT1-X remains competitive on specific tasks like Move Near under visual matching (55.0% vs 53.7%) and Open/Close Drawer under variant aggregation (56.0% vs 31.0%). Octo-Base (93M) is substantially outperformed by both TraceVLA models across nearly all tasks, with an overall gap of ~40 percentage points. On real-robot WidowX-250 tasks, TraceVLA consistently surpasses OpenVLA, most dramatically on the unseen pick-place corn task (8/10 vs 1/10) and on language-grounding tasks where OpenVLA fails to follow spatial instructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

TraceVLA extends the OpenVLA lineage (itself built on Prismatic VLM and Open X-Embodiment) by introducing visual trace prompting: overlaying Co-Tracker-derived point trajectories onto robot observations as an additional visual input. This departs from prior VLA work that either predicts traces as auxiliary outputs (LLARVA) or uses VLMs for keypoint-to-action pipelines (RoboPoint, RepKep), instead treating historical spatial trajectories as *inputs* that condition the policy. The approach also connects to the broader visual prompting literature for VLMs (MOKA, PIVOT, Set-of-Mark), but is the first to integrate temporal point-tracking traces directly into VLA fine-tuning for manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic way to inject spatial-temporal context into VLA models, with the 4B TraceVLA-Phi3 model making fine-tuning accessible on consumer GPUs (RTX 4090/A5000). The 3.5× real-robot improvement and consistent SimplerEnv gains suggest the technique could become a standard component in VLA training pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond manipulation (locomotion, navigation), how it interacts with 3D point-cloud inputs, and whether the Co-Tracker dependency introduces failure modes in visually challenging conditions (e.g., reflective surfaces, rapid motion).
