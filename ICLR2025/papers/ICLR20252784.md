## Problem

Graph machine learning has advanced rapidly, yet the foundational step of constructing appropriate graphs from common tabular data remains largely unformalized and understudied. Existing automatic methods (e.g., Row2Node, DeepJoin) only handle explicit PK-FK pairs, while real-world enterprise data contains implicit relationships—categorical columns inducing self-relations, tables that should be edges rather than nodes, and task-dependent schema choices—that require laborious expert data engineering. No dedicated benchmark exists to evaluate graph construction quality across these challenges.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-table tabular data in RDB/YAML format where no explicit key relationships are provided (i.e., 'typeless' schemas), along with a natural-language downstream task description. Column types must be inferred from statistics (uniqueness, mode, sampled values) and DeepJoin similarity scores. The output is a graph schema in YAML with explicit PK-FK relations, augmented tables, and proper node/edge type assignments, which is then trivially converted to a heterogeneous graph via Row2Node/Edge heuristics for downstream GML training.

## Method

AutoG is an LLM-based (Claude Sonnet 3.5) framework that decomposes graph construction into four close-ended augmentation actions—CONNECT_TWO_COLUMNS, GENERATE_NEW_TABLE, REMOVE/ADD_PRIMARY_KEY, and UNFOLD_MULTI_CATEGORY_COLUMNS—invoked via function calling rather than open-ended YAML generation, which avoids grammar errors and missed multi-step augmentations. An input context module infers column types and compiles statistics, similarity scores, and chain-of-thought demonstrations to guide the LLM's action selection over up to 10 iterative steps. A discriminator oracle evaluates candidate schemas using early-stage validation performance (10% of total epochs) averaged across a basket of four heterogeneous GNNs (RGCN, RGAT, HGT, PNA), enabling model-agnostic schema selection. The AutoG-A variant runs multiple generation passes and selects the best candidate by oracle score, while AutoG-S uses the single final state directly.

## Results

AutoG achieves an average ranking of 2.0 across all 8 datasets and 12 tasks, tying with human expert schemas and substantially outperforming all automatic baselines (JTD: 3.3, TabGNN: 4.5, DeepFM: 5.3, XGBoost: 5.8). On the most challenging datasets, AutoG shows the largest gains: Diginetica Purchase MRR improves from 15.47 (JTD) to 36.91 (AutoG, matching Expert), and RetailRocket CVR AUC jumps from 48.99 (JTD) to 74.78 (AutoG) vs. 84.70 (Expert). On Stackexchange Churn, AutoG (86.92 AUC) actually surpasses the expert schema (85.58 AUC). The paper also demonstrates that the same graph is not optimal for all tasks: on MAG Year prediction, the expert schema (35.35 Acc) is far worse than the original schema (54.09 Acc), while AutoG-A correctly identifies the original schema as best.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoG clearly outperforms all automatic baselines. Against JTD (DeepJoin + Row2Node/Edge), AutoG wins on 9 of 12 tasks, with the largest margin on Diginetica Purchase MRR (36.91 vs. 15.47) and RetailRocket CVR AUC (74.78 vs. 48.99); JTD's similarity-based join discovery produces incorrect column pairings (e.g., matching 'paper_cite' to 'paper_writer' on MAG). Against TabGNN, AutoG wins on 10 of 12 tasks, with TabGNN's multiplex graph construction being particularly weak on multi-table datasets (e.g., 5.01 MRR on Diginetica Purchase vs. AutoG's 36.91). Against non-graph baselines XGBoost and DeepFM, AutoG wins on 10 of 12 tasks, though XGBoost/DeepFM remain competitive on IEEE-CIS (90.14/90.28 AUC vs. AutoG's 89.17), where graph structure provides limited benefit. Against human expert schemas, AutoG ties on average ranking (2.0 vs. 2.0) and wins on 4 tasks (AVS, MAG Venue, MAG Year, Stackexchange Churn) while losing on 3 (RetailRocket, Movielens, Outbrain) and tying on 5, confirming that LLM-based automatic construction can rival expert data engineering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of database schema transformation and graph machine learning. Prior GML benchmarks (4DBInfer, Fey et al. 2024) assume well-formatted graphs with explicit PK-FK pairs already exist, while database engineering tools (DeepJoin, normalization) optimize for data integrity rather than downstream task performance. AutoG repurposes LLM function-calling and chain-of-thought prompting—techniques from agentic data science (CAFE, Toolformer)—to perform task-aware schema augmentation, positioning graph construction as a distinct, formalizable problem rather than a preprocessing step. The four challenges (C1–C4) explicitly go beyond what join discovery or normalization alone can address by incorporating downstream task semantics into structural decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating graph construction quality, filling a gap where prior work either assumed graphs were given or conflated construction with model evaluation. The finding that graph construction can actively harm performance (IEEE-CIS) and that a single graph schema is suboptimal across tasks (MAG year prediction) challenges the implicit assumption in GML that richer structure always helps. Open questions remain: how to determine a priori whether a graph is beneficial at all, how to scale beyond the moderate complexity of the eight datasets, and how to handle anonymous columns where semantic information is unavailable—limitations the authors acknowledge but do not resolve.
