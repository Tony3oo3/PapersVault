## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering but requires storing millions of per-splat attributes (position, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics), leading to gigabyte-scale storage that is prohibitive for mobile and AR devices. Existing compression approaches either retain large footprints (ScaffoldGS) or degrade rendering fidelity through aggressive quantization and pruning (LightGS, CompactGS), leaving a gap between storage efficiency and visual quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252837/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a COLMAP SfM point cloud and multi-view RGB images with known camera poses, using scene contraction to map positions into [0,1] before hash-grid queries. The output is a compact tree-structured representation storing only parent splat positions and scales (~20–35 MB per scene) plus shared neural network weights, from which all children splats and their attributes are predicted on-the-fly during rendering. The representation is designed for on-device deployment, achieving >30 fps on iPhone 14 and Snap AR Spectacles where full 3DGS causes out-of-memory errors.

## Method

The core idea is a hierarchical parent–children tree where each parent splat (initialized from SfM) predicts K children via a shared Instant-NGP hash-grid: the grid's displacement half predicts child positions as offsets from the parent, while the attribute half feeds a permutation-invariant self-attention layer (no positional encoding) that aggregates parent and child features before a shallow MLP regresses scale, rotation, color, and opacity. Children are never stored; only parent positions and scales persist, yielding ~20× storage reduction. Adaptive Tree Manipulation (ATM) dynamically promotes significant children (based on position-gradient threshold) to new parents, prunes insignificant subtrees, and clones entire trees, enabling unbounded tree depth that adapts to geometric complexity. View-dependent color is predicted directly from features and viewing direction via an MLP, eliminating per-splat spherical harmonic coefficients.

## Results

On the Mip-NeRF 360° dataset, the largest configuration (C3) achieves the best PSNR of 29.11 dB with only 35.60 MB storage — a 19.5× reduction compared to 3DGS (693 MB) and 4.4× smaller than the next-smallest compact method (CompactGS, 46.98 MB). On Tank&Temples, C3 reaches 23.82 dB PSNR at 35.32 MB, and on Deep Blending it achieves 29.89 dB PSNR at 35.40 MB. The smallest configuration (C1, D=32) uses only 23.40 MB on Mip-NeRF 360° while matching LightGS's PSNR (28.45 dB), and all three configurations run in real-time (>30 fps) on an iPhone 14, whereas 3DGS and ScaffoldGS produce Out-of-Memory errors on the same device.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023), Ours-C3 achieves higher PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360° (29.11 vs 28.69) and Deep Blending (29.89 vs 29.41) while using only ~4.5% of the storage (35.60 MB vs 693 MB); on Tank&Temples it also wins on PSNR (23.82 vs 23.14) with 11.3× less storage. Against ScaffoldGS (Lu et al., 2023), Ours-C3 wins on PSNR for Mip-NeRF 360° (29.11 vs 29.02) but trails slightly on Tank&Temples (23.82 vs 23.96) and Deep Blending (29.89 vs 30.21), while using 2.4× and 1.9× less storage respectively. Among compact methods, Ours-C3 outperforms CompactGS (Lee et al., 2024) on PSNR across all three datasets (e.g., 29.11 vs 28.60 on Mip-NeRF 360°) with comparable or smaller storage, and beats LightGS (Fan et al., 2024) by 0.66 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360° while using 33% less storage. Eagles (Girish et al., 2023) and CompGS (Navaneet et al.) are clearly outperformed on both PSNR and storage across all datasets. Notably, only Ours, Eagles, and LightGS can run in real-time on an iPhone 14; 3DGS and ScaffoldGS fail with Out-of-Memory errors, and CompactGS/CompGS have unknown on-device capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by replacing the flat collection of independently stored splats with a hierarchical parent-children tree, where only parent splats are stored and children are neural-predicted on-the-fly via a shared hash-grid and self-attention. It is most closely related to ScaffoldGS (depth-1 anchor-based trees) and CompactGS (grid-based neural fields for view-dependent color), but departs by (1) predicting full geometric attributes rather than just color, (2) storing only a subset of splats rather than all, and (3) allowing multi-level tree growth through Adaptive Tree Manipulation. The hash-grid encoding from Instant-NGP provides the spatial feature-sharing backbone, while the attention mechanism over aggregated parent-children features is a novel architectural addition not present in prior 3DGS compression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× storage reduction with equal-or-better PSNR makes 3DGS-based scene representation practically deployable on mobile phones and AR glasses, removing a major barrier to real-world adoption. The finding that ~80% of final parent nodes originate from ATM promotion (children becoming parents) validates the multi-level tree design and suggests that flat or depth-1 structures fundamentally underutilize the scene's spatial hierarchy. Open questions include whether the hash-grid (which occupies half the total storage) can be replaced by more compact encodings, how the method scales to scenes with tens of millions of splats, and whether combining ATM with other compression techniques (quantization, adaptive pruning) yields further gains without quality loss.
