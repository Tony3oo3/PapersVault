## Problem

Conventional activation functions in transformers (ReLU, GELU, SwiGLU) are predominantly linear or piecewise-linear, limiting their capacity to model complex higher-order interactions in data. Pure polynomial activations, while more expressive, suffer from inadequate approximation, exploding values, and oscillatory behavior. The paper asks whether a carefully designed polynomial composition can achieve the expressiveness of higher-order functions while maintaining the stability and optimal approximation guarantees of piecewise-linear activations.

## Input / Output

The method operates on text token sequences of length 4096 within the feed-forward network (FFN) layers of transformer architectures, where the activation is applied element-wise to the intermediate hidden representation (hidden size 2048) between the two fully-connected projections. The output is standard next-token prediction for language modeling, evaluated via perplexity and downstream task accuracy. In the vision ablation, ResNet50 processes 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1K, with PolyNorm replacing ReLU in the residual blocks to produce 1000-class predictions.

## Method

PolyCom composes a degree-r polynomial (with learnable coefficients a_i) with a base function ρ in two ways: Type I (PolyReLU) computes Σ a_i · ReLU^i(x), applying ReLU to each power of x; Type II (PolyNorm) computes Σ a_i · x^i / ‖x^i‖₂, normalizing each power to prevent magnitude explosion. The default 3-order configuration (r=3, a_i=1/3) is dropped directly into the FFN activation slot of LLaMA-2-style dense and OLMoE-style MoE transformers, with intermediate sizes adjusted to keep parameter counts constant across baselines. Theoretically, the paper proves that PolyReLU networks achieve the optimal Sobolev-space approximation rate O(ε^{−d/n})—removing the logarithmic factor present in ReLU networks—and that any ReLU network can be exactly represented by a same-size PolyReLU network, while the reverse requires Ω(ln(1/ε)) more parameters per neuron. Empirically, PolyNorm (which incorporates L₂ normalization for BF16/FP16 stability) consistently outperforms SwiGLU, GELU, and ReLU on both 1B dense and 1B-active/7B-total MoE LLMs, with higher effective weight rank and lower layer-wise cosine similarity indicating richer representations.

## Results

On the 1B dense model (250B tokens), PolyNorm achieves the best average downstream accuracy of 58.68%, outperforming SwiGLU (57.47%), GELU (56.98%), and ReLU (57.18%) by margins of 1.21%, 1.70%, and 1.50% respectively. PolyNorm also attains the lowest training loss (2.17) and validation perplexity (3.17). On the MoE model (1B active / 7B total, 200B tokens), PolyNorm improves average downstream accuracy over SwiGLU from 62.78% to 63.37% and reduces average validation loss from 2.41 to 2.39 across 11 held-out datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolyNorm (the strongest PolyCom variant) consistently outperforms SwiGLU, the standard activation in LLaMA-series models, by +1.21% average downstream accuracy on the 1B dense model and +0.59% on the MoE model, while also achieving lower training loss and PPL throughout training. Against GELU, PolyNorm gains +1.70% average accuracy; against ReLU, +1.50%. In the ablation comparing ReLU variants (Figure 5c), PolyReLU beats both plain ReLU and ReLU² (square ReLU from So et al., 2021) on training loss, confirming that the polynomial composition itself—rather than any single power term—drives the improvement. On the MoE model, PolyNorm wins on 8 of 9 downstream tasks versus SwiGLU (losing only on ARC-Easy by 0.88 points), and achieves lower validation loss on all 11 held-out datasets. The paper also reports that PolyNorm's advantage widens in deeper layers (lower layer-wise cosine similarity), suggesting the gains come from increased representational diversity rather than merely faster convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on polynomial and rectified activation functions (Hornik et al. 1989; Li et al. 2019) by composing polynomials with other nonlinearities (ReLU, normalization) to avoid the known pathologies of pure polynomial activations (oscillation, value explosion). The theoretical analysis of optimal approximation rates in Sobolev spaces directly follows the grid-based Taylor polynomial methodology of Yarotsky (2017) and the exact power-representation technique of Boullé et al. (2020), but applies it to the PolyReLU family to show that the log(1/ε) factor present in ReLU network bounds can be eliminated. Practically, it positions itself as a drop-in replacement for SwiGLU and GELU in transformer FFN layers, building on the LLaMA-2 and OLMoE architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result—that PolyReLU networks achieve the optimal O(ε^{-d/n}) approximation rate in Sobolev spaces without the logarithmic overhead of ReLU networks—provides a principled justification for polynomial composition activations beyond empirical tuning. However, the empirical gains are modest (~1% average downstream accuracy over SwiGLU at 1B scale), and the theory only covers PolyReLU while the empirically best variant (PolyNorm) lacks a corresponding analysis. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at 70B+ scale, whether the normalization in PolyNorm can be theoretically characterized, and whether the higher effective weight rank observed is a cause or consequence of the improved performance.
