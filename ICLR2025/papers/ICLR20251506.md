## Problem

Coordinate-based implicit neural representations (INRs) such as SIREN, FFN, and WIRE overfit to training regions and fail catastrophically on out-of-distribution inputs, making them unsuitable for extrapolation or forecasting of periodic signals. No existing INR architecture effectively captures the sequential periodicity inherent in time series or spatial wave phenomena while maintaining accurate predictions beyond the training domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251506/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a spatio-temporal coordinate pair: a temporal coordinate c_i^t (a scalar time index, spatial coordinate, or min-max-scaled calendar vector) and a feature coordinate c_j^f (a one-hot vector indexing the j-th variable among M features). The output is a scalar signal intensity at that coordinate, internally decomposed into a periodic factor constrained to [-1,1] via sinusoidal activation and an unconstrained real-valued scale factor, whose product yields the prediction. A single trained model simultaneously performs interpolation (imputation) and extrapolation (forecasting) at arbitrary coordinates without sliding windows or retraining.

## Method

NeRT employs an encoder-decoder architecture with a key factorization: the encoder produces three embeddings from the input coordinate — a ReLU-based temporal embedding (ψ_t), a ReLU-based one-hot feature embedding (ψ_f), and a learnable Fourier feature mapping (ψ_F) with trainable frequencies, amplitudes, phase shifts, and biases. The decoder splits into two branches: a periodic decoder (φ_p) using sinusoidal activations that reads only ψ_F ⊕ ψ_f, and a scale decoder (φ_s) using ReLU activations that reads all three embeddings; the final signal is their element-wise product. Training minimizes MSE on the undecomposed signal without explicit supervision of individual factors, optionally augmented by a third-order derivative penalty on the scale component to suppress spurious oscillations. The NTK analysis shows the learnable Fourier mapping yields a stationary, shift-invariant kernel, and the extreme value theorem bounds ψ_F to the min/max defined by the amplitude parameters, enabling principled extrapolation to out-of-distribution coordinates.

## Results

On periodic time series (Table 1), NeRT achieves MSE of 0.0061 on Electricity interpolation and 0.0057 on extrapolation (1 missing block), outperforming SIREN (0.0200/0.0256), FFN (0.0189/0.0144), and WIRE (0.0170/0.0244) by 2–4×. On long-term time series (Table 2), NeRT achieves MSE of 0.0911 on ETTh1 (50% drop) versus 0.2173 (SIREN) and 0.3407 (FFN), and 0.0239 on National Illness (30% drop) versus 0.3502 (SIREN) and 0.1110 (FFN). In the broader time series forecasting comparison (Table 12, m=48), NeRT achieves MSE of 0.0174 on Electricity (n=96) and 0.0086 on Traffic (n=96), beating the best non-INR baselines (NLinear at 0.0329 and RNN at 0.0191) by roughly 2×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRT outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and task setting. Against the three INR baselines (SIREN, FFN, WIRE), NeRT achieves 2–4× lower MSE on periodic time series (e.g., Caiso extrapolation: 0.0131 vs. 0.0596/0.0495/0.0466) and 2.4–3.7× lower MSE on long-term series (ETTh1 50% drop: 0.0911 vs. 0.2173/0.3407). Against the Snake activation baseline (Ziyin et al.), NeRT is the only model that successfully extrapolates on the Minamitorishima atmospheric data, while Snake, SIREN, FFN, and WIRE all fail in the test period. In the comprehensive forecasting comparison (Tables 12–14), NeRT beats all Transformer-based models (Autoformer, Informer, FEDformer), Neural ODE/CDE models (Latent ODE, Neural CDE), RNN/LSTM, and Linear-based models (Linear, DLinear, NLinear) by approximately 2–5× in MSE; for instance, on Traffic (m=48, n=96), NeRT achieves 0.0086 versus the best baseline RNN at 0.0191. A key practical advantage is that NeRT requires only a single training run per dataset to produce predictions at all output window sizes, whereas all window-based baselines must be retrained for each (m, n) combination.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeRT extends the implicit neural representation (INR) paradigm—established for images (SIREN, FFN) and 3D scenes (NeRF)—to the domain of periodic sequential signals, a setting where existing INRs demonstrably fail at extrapolation. Architecturally, it combines two ideas from prior INR work: the sinusoidal activation of SIREN and the Fourier feature mapping of FFN, but makes the Fourier mapping learnable rather than hand-crafted. The factorized periodic×scale decomposition draws from classical time series decomposition (STL) but is realized within a single differentiable INR trained end-to-end on the undecomposed signal, rather than as a separate preprocessing step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and resolves a fundamental gap: despite INRs' success in spatial and 3D domains, they catastrophically fail at extrapolating even simple periodic functions, limiting their applicability to the vast class of temporal signals. NeRT's single-model, coordinate-query-based approach eliminates the need for sliding windows and retraining across prediction horizons, offering a computationally efficient alternative to Transformer and RNN-based forecasters. Key open questions remain: how to incorporate recency weighting without sliding windows, how to efficiently adapt to streaming data (the modulation mechanism is only preliminary), and whether the approach generalizes to strongly non-stationary or non-periodic signals where the inductive bias of periodicity becomes a liability.
