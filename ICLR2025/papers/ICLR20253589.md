## Problem

Existing video benchmarks are inefficient for iterative model development because they are costly to construct (requiring manual annotation, prompt engineering, and data filtering) and conflate multiple video understanding capabilities in each question, making it impossible to isolate specific model weaknesses. Additionally, real-world video benchmarks risk data leakage since the source videos may have appeared in model training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253589/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample consists of a video haystack (10–180 s from MSRVTT, NeXT Videos, or ActivityNet; up to 30 min in VNBench-Long) with one or more 1-second 'needles' inserted at controlled positions, paired with a four-option multiple-choice text query. Needles take the form of overlaid subtitles (intra-frame editing) or static images/short clips inserted between frames (inter-frame insertion). A circular evaluation protocol shuffles the four answer options across four independent trials; a sample counts as correct only if the model selects the right option in all four trials, reducing option-order bias.

## Method

VideoNIAH decouples video content from query-response pairs by embedding unrelated visual 'needles' into arbitrary video 'haystacks' and auto-generating queries via predefined rules, eliminating manual annotation and data-leakage risk. The framework defines three tasks targeting distinct video understanding dimensions: retrieval (single needle, probing temporal perception), ordering (four needles, probing chronological reasoning), and counting (repeated needles, probing spatio-temporal coherence), each further split into intra-frame editing and inter-frame insertion sub-tasks with two difficulty levels. A CLIP-based similarity filter (threshold 0.7) discards candidate needle images that are too visually similar to haystack frames, preventing accidental content overlap. Because needles are independent of the underlying video, the same query-response template can be applied to any video source and duration, enabling scalable evaluation across context lengths from 10 s to 30 min without re-annotation.

## Results

On VNBench (1,350 samples across 9 sub-tasks), Gemini 1.5 Pro achieves the highest overall accuracy at 66.7%, followed by GPT-4o at 64.4% and GPT-4-turbo at 48.9%. Among open-source models, LLaVA-OneVision-72B leads at 58.7%, with a clear 8.0% gap to the best proprietary model. Retrieval tasks are relatively easy for proprietary models (90.7–95.3% average), while ordering shows an enormous proprietary-vs-open-source gap (Gemini 72.9% vs. most open-source models near 0%), and counting is the hardest task for all models (best: Gemini 36.7%, with Counting-E2 at only 7.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini 1.5 Pro (66.7%) outperforms GPT-4o (64.4%) by 2.3% overall, with the largest gap on Counting (36.7% vs. 24.5%) and Counting-E2 (7.3% vs. 0.0%); GPT-4-turbo (48.9%) trails both, particularly on Ordering (29.5% vs. 72.9%/73.4%). The best open-source model, LLaVA-OneVision-72B (58.7%), narrows the proprietary gap to 8.0% overall and is the only open-source model competitive on Ordering (68.7% vs. Gemini's 72.9%), but still lags on Counting (29.3% vs. 36.7%). A stark divide exists in Ordering: GPT-4o (73.4%) and Gemini (72.9%) vastly outperform all open-source models except LLaVA-OneVision-7B/72B (52.4%/68.7%), while ST-LLM, LLaVA-NeXT-Video, VideoChat2, Video-LLaVA, LLaMA-VID, and Video-LLaMA2 score near 0%. On Retrieval, Qwen2-VL-7B (69.1%) and LLaVA-OneVision-7B (77.1%) approach proprietary levels, but the gap widens on harder sub-tasks (Retrieval-I-2: 33.3% and 55.3% vs. 76.0–87.3% for proprietary). Counting-E2 (spatio-temporal coherence with local image regions) is the weakest task across all 12 models, with the best score being Gemini's 7.3%, indicating a universal limitation in fine-grained spatial-temporal tracking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'Needle in a Haystack' (NIAH) paradigm from long-context LLM evaluation (Kamradt, 2023; Hsieh et al., 2024; Song et al., 2024) to the video domain, introducing the first synthetic video benchmark construction framework. It departs from existing video benchmarks (VideoMME, MVBench, MLVU, LongVideoBench) that rely on real-world videos with labor-intensive annotation and assess multiple capabilities simultaneously, by decoupling video content from query-response pairs through automated needle insertion. The framework also builds on CLIP-based similarity filtering to prevent needle-haystack confusion, and positions itself as a complement to rather than replacement for real-world benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VNBench provides a scalable, low-cost evaluation tool that isolates specific video understanding skills (temporal perception, chronological ordering, spatio-temporal coherence), enabling targeted model improvement during iterative development. The finding that even Gemini 1.5 Pro achieves only 7.3% on Counting-E2 (fine-grained spatio-temporal coherence) suggests this remains a fundamental unsolved problem across all current architectures. Open questions include whether synthetic needle-based evaluation fully transfers to real-world video understanding (the paper reports >0.9 correlation with MLVU/VideoMME but on limited model sets), and how to design training objectives that specifically improve spatio-temporal tracking rather than merely increasing frame count or model size.
