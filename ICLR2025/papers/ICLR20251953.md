## Problem

Parameter Efficient Transfer Learning (PETL) methods match or surpass full fine-tuning on classification tasks but consistently underperform on semantic segmentation, where the shift from global object-level perception to pixel-level semantic perception requires larger and broader parameter adjustments that limited tunable parameters cannot accommodate. This performance gap has kept full fine-tuning as the default for segmentation, limiting PETL's broader adoption.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251953/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed at 640×640 resolution) and produces pixel-level semantic segmentation masks with per-pixel class predictions. During the midstream adaptation phase, the input images are paired with coarsely pooled labels derived from the original dense annotations (e.g., a single image-level label vector or patch-level label vectors), while the downstream fine-tuning phase uses the original dense pixel-level ground truth. The segmentation head (UperNet or Mask2Former) is randomly initialized and trained alongside the adaptation module in the downstream phase.

## Method

ProPETL introduces a midstream adaptation phase between pre-training and downstream fine-tuning to progressively bridge the semantic perception granularity gap. Two progressive adaptation strategies are explored: Generalized Parametric Adaptation (GPA), which updates the same bottleneck bypass adapter in both phases, and Decoupled Structured Adaptation (DSA), which freezes the midstream-trained adapter and trains a new randomly initialized adapter on concatenated midstream and original FFN features during downstream fine-tuning, avoiding catastrophic forgetting. The intermediate task is constructed by transforming dense segmentation labels through two operations: perception granularity control (pooling window size s from patch-level to image-level) and supervision diversity modulation (interpolating between one-hot and many-hot labels via a smoothing factor α). Empirically, image-level granularity (s=H×W) with multi-label supervision (α=1) yields optimal performance, and DSA consistently outperforms GPA due to its decoupled parameter design preventing knowledge loss during downstream fine-tuning.

## Results

Using Swin-L (ImageNet-21K pre-trained) with UperNet, ProPETL achieves a mean mIoU of 66.33 and mean mAcc of 75.65 across VOC2012, ADE20k, COCO-Stuff10k, and CityScapes, using only 3.30M learnable parameters (1.7% of full fine-tuning's 195M). Notably, ProPETL surpasses full fine-tuning on COCO-Stuff10k (46.48 vs. 46.30 mIoU) for the first time among PETL methods, and matches or exceeds full fine-tuning on ADE20k in mAcc (63.61 vs. 63.13). The method outperforms the strongest PETL baseline (LoRA) by 0.98 mIoU on mean and by up to 2.26 mIoU on CityScapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProPETL outperforms all seven PETL baselines across all four benchmarks. Against the closest competitor LoRA (4.55M params), ProPETL gains +0.98 mIoU on mean, with the largest margin on CityScapes (+1.13 mIoU) and COCO-Stuff10k (+1.45 mIoU). Compared to AdaptFormer (2.64M params), the primary architectural baseline, ProPETL improves mean mIoU by +1.17 and CityScapes mIoU by +2.26. Against full fine-tuning (195M params), ProPETL matches or exceeds it on COCO-Stuff10k (+0.18 mIoU) and ADE20k mAcc (+0.48), while trailing slightly on ADE20k mIoU (−0.66) and CityScapes mIoU (−0.69). The partial-tuning baselines BitFit and LoRand lag significantly, with mean mIoU gaps of 2.90 and 3.08 respectively, confirming that simple parameter selection or low-rank adapters without progressive adaptation are insufficient for the semantic granularity shift in segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AdaptFormer adapter architecture (bottleneck bypass in FFN layers) by wrapping it in a two-phase progressive training paradigm. While prior progressive transfer learning work (Hsu et al., 2020; Dong et al., 2024b) addressed data-domain shifts via intermediate domains or easy-to-hard pipelines, this paper repurposes the progressive idea to bridge a fundamentally different gap: the shift in semantic perception granularity from object-level classification to pixel-level segmentation. The intermediate task design—converting dense annotations into coarser labels via perception granularity and supervision diversity transformations—is a novel mechanism not seen in prior PETL or progressive adaptation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of perceptual granularity shift as the core bottleneck for PETL in dense prediction provides a clear diagnostic that explains why adapter-based methods plateau on segmentation while excelling at classification. The first PETL method to surpass full fine-tuning on COCO-Stuff10k (using only 1.7% of parameters) is a notable milestone, though the margin is modest (~0.2 mIoU) and the method still trails full fine-tuning on ADE20k. Key limitations include the added training cost of the midstream phase and the reliance on empirical, dataset-specific choices for intermediate task parameters (pooling window size, smoothing factor), leaving open the question of whether a principled or dynamic strategy can replace the current heuristic design.
