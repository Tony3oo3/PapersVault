## Problem

Reconstruction-based unsupervised graph anomaly detectors (e.g., GCN+AE) fail to capture discriminative content because anomalous nodes deliberately camouflage among normal ones, sharing common attributes. This causes the learned latent space to exhibit overlapping distributions for normal and abnormal samples, making reconstruction error an unreliable anomaly signal. The paper asks whether diffusion models can be repurposed from generation to detection to explicitly distill discriminative content into the latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251351/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static attributed graph G = {V, X, E, A} with node features X ∈ R^{n×d} and adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n}, and outputs a per-node anomaly score computed as the L2 reconstruction error between the original graph and its decoded reconstruction. The pipeline operates entirely in a learned latent space: the graph is first projected into k-dimensional embeddings via a GCN encoder, all diffusion-based reconstruction happens on these embeddings, and the final score is obtained by decoding back to graph space. No labels are required at any stage.

## Method

DiffGAD employs a dual diffusion model in latent space to distill discriminative content. A GCN-based Graph AE encodes the graph into latent embeddings; an unconditional DM learns general content (discriminative + common), while a second conditional DM—conditioned on an adaptively-constructed common feature c (iteratively refined via cosine-similarity-weighted aggregation that down-weights potential anomalies)—learns common content alone. Discriminative content is then extracted via a classifier-free-guidance-style linear combination: (1+λ)ε_θ(z_t,t) − λε_θ(z_t,c,t), which is used as the modified score for sampling. Crucially, instead of sampling from pure Gaussian noise, the method adds only t-step noise (t < T) to the original embedding, preserving general content at multiple scales and enabling direct original-vs-reconstructed comparison for anomaly scoring.

## Results

DiffGAD achieves an average ROC-AUC of 68.4 across five datasets (Weibo, Reddit, Disney, Books, Enron), outperforming the second-best method ANOMALOUS (62.7) by approximately 9% relative improvement. The largest gains appear on Enron (71.6 vs. 59.3 for GAAN, a 20.7% absolute gain) and Books (66.4 vs. 62.2 for AnomalyDAE). On the large-scale Dgraph dataset (3.7M nodes), DiffGAD achieves 52.4 ROC-AUC while most deep baselines (DOMINANT, DONE, AdONE, AnomalyDAE, GAAN) fail with out-of-memory or time-limit errors. DiffGAD also demonstrates low variance across runs (e.g., ±0.1–0.3 on Weibo and Reddit), indicating stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffGAD achieves the best ROC-AUC on four of five standard datasets (Reddit 56.3, Disney 54.5, Books 66.4, Enron 71.6) and the best average (68.4), but trails Radar and ANOMALOUS on Weibo (93.4 vs. 98.9), where those classical methods exploit graph-structure-based residual features that happen to align with Weibo's dual structural+contextual outlier pattern. Among deep learning baselines, DiffGAD dominates by large margins: on Enron it surpasses GAAN (59.3) by 20.7 points and DOMINANT (53.7) by 17.9 points; on Books it beats AnomalyDAE (62.2) by 4.2 points. On the 3.7M-node Dgraph dataset, DiffGAD (52.4) is the only deep method that completes without OOM/TLE, though it underperforms the non-graph IF baseline (60.9). The paper attributes the Weibo gap to Radar/ANOMALOUS using domain-specific graph features that do not generalize, while DiffGAD's diffusion-based discriminative distillation provides more transferable anomaly signals across heterogeneous datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffGAD repurposes latent diffusion models—previously used for generation (Ho et al., 2020; Karras et al., 2022; Song et al., 2021)—as a reconstruction mechanism for unsupervised graph anomaly detection, where reconstruction error serves as the anomaly score. The encoder-decoder backbone follows the Graph AE framework of DOMINANT (Ding et al., 2019), while the core novelty lies in adapting classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) to a dual-DM setup: an unconditional DM captures general content (discriminative + common), and a conditional DM (conditioned on an adaptively-constructed common feature) captures common content alone, so their difference isolates discriminative content. A further departure from standard diffusion sampling is the partial-denoising strategy (starting from t < T rather than pure noise), which preserves multi-scale general content to stabilize the reconstruction-based scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete weakness of reconstruction-based GAD—overemphasis on common content that camouflages anomalies—and offers a principled fix via discriminative content distillation, yielding a >9% average AUC gain over the prior best unsupervised method across six datasets. However, the method still trails classical baselines (Radar, ANOMALOUS) on Weibo and underperforms on small graphs (Disney), and the authors acknowledge that latent-space expressiveness is bottlenecked by the GCN encoder, leaving encoder-free or more expressive backbones as an open direction. Whether the dual-DM + classifier-free-guidance paradigm transfers to edge-level, subgraph-level, or temporal anomaly detection remains unexplored.
