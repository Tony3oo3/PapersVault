## Problem

Existing deep generative models for single-cell data operate on pre-processed continuous gene expression approximations, overlooking the inherently discrete nature of scRNA-seq counts and limiting the incorporation of biologically grounded noise models such as the negative binomial. Additionally, controllable multi-modal and multi-attribute generation of cellular data—where users can condition on arbitrary combinations of biological and technical covariates from a single trained model—remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

CFGen takes a Gaussian noise vector, one or more categorical attributes (e.g., cell type, batch, tissue, donor), and a continuous size factor (total transcript count per cell) as inputs, and produces discrete gene expression counts sampled from a gene-specific negative binomial and binary DNA accessibility peaks sampled from a Bernoulli distribution. Unlike prior methods that require normalized or log-transformed counts, CFGen generates raw integer counts directly, with the size factor integrated as a conditioning variable for the latent flow rather than merely a post-hoc scaling factor, ensuring consistency between the sampled latent state and the library size.

## Method

CFGen combines a separately trained autoencoder (with NB and Bernoulli decoders for RNA and ATAC modalities) with a conditional continuous normalizing flow learned via Flow Matching using Gaussian marginal paths, where the flow transports Gaussian noise to latent cell representations conditioned on both the categorical attribute and the size factor. The key theoretical contribution extends classifier-free guidance to compositional multi-attribute generation: Proposition 1 proves that under conditional independence of attributes given the latent variable, the compositional vector field u_t(z) + Σ ω_i [u_t(z|y_i) − u_t(z)] coincides with the probability-flow ODE velocity of a diffusion model using the compositional score, enabling a single trained model to generate cells conditioned on any subset or combination of attributes by adjusting per-attribute guidance weights. The generative process factorizes as p(x,z,l,y) = p(x|z,l)·p(z|y,l)·p(l)·p(y), with the flow approximating p(z|y,l) and modality-specific decoders mapping the latent to discrete likelihood parameters, preserving the discrete count structure end-to-end.

## Results

On conditional single-cell generation, c-CFGen achieves the best RBF-kernel MMD and 2-Wasserstein distance across all four datasets (e.g., MMD 0.85 on PBMC3K vs. 1.27 for scDiffusion and 0.94 for scVI; MMD 0.19 on Tabula Muris vs. 0.24 and 0.26). Unconditionally, u-CFGen outperforms scGAN on three of four datasets (e.g., MMD 0.44 vs. 0.36 on PBMC3K, 0.08 vs. 0.25 on Tabula Muris) but is slightly worse on Dentate gyrus (MMD 0.42 vs. 0.36). In the multi-modal setting, CFGen achieves MMD 0.89/0.92 (RNA/ATAC) and WD 13.90/18.86, surpassing MultiVI (0.86/0.96 MMD, 15.92/21.09 WD) on both modalities. For batch correction, CFGen attains the highest scIB batch score (0.63 NeurIPS, 0.68 C. Elegans) and best biological conservation on C. Elegans (0.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against scDiffusion (diffusion-based), c-CFGen wins on all four datasets in both MMD and WD, with the largest gap on PBMC3K (MMD 0.85 vs. 1.27) and HLCA (MMD 0.54 vs. 0.96). Against scVI (VAE-based), c-CFGen also dominates across all datasets, e.g., MMD 0.19 vs. 0.26 on Tabula Muris and WD 7.39 vs. 9.76. In the unconditional setting, u-CFGen beats scGAN on PBMC3K, Tabula Muris, and HLCA but loses on Dentate gyrus (MMD 0.42 vs. 0.36, WD 21.20 vs. 15.54). In the multi-modal experiment, CFGen outperforms MultiVI on both RNA (WD 13.90 vs. 15.92) and ATAC (MMD 0.92 vs. 0.96, WD 18.86 vs. 21.09), and surpasses PeakVI on ATAC (MMD 0.92 vs. 1.49). For batch correction, CFGen achieves the best scIB batch score on both NeurIPS (0.63 vs. 0.55 scPoli) and C. Elegans (0.68 vs. 0.61 scPoli/scANVI), while scANVI retains a slight edge on biological conservation for NeurIPS (0.68 vs. 0.61).

## Relation to Prior Work

CFGen builds directly on Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) as its generative backbone, replacing the diffusion or GAN frameworks used by prior single-cell generators (scDiffusion, scGAN). It extends the classifier-free guidance formulation for Flow Matching (Zheng et al., 2023) from single-attribute to compositional multi-attribute conditioning, drawing an explicit analogy to compositional CFG in diffusion models (Liu et al., 2022) and proving the equivalence via Proposition 1. The discrete likelihood modeling (negative binomial for RNA, Bernoulli for ATAC) follows the scVI lineage (Lopez et al., 2018), but CFGen departs by conditioning the flow-based prior on both cell type and size factor rather than applying conditioning at the decoder, and by operating on raw counts rather than normalized continuous representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By preserving the discrete, over-dispersed, and sparse structure of single-cell data, CFGen addresses a practical limitation of continuous-space generators that hinders downstream tasks like differential expression and batch correction on raw counts. The compositional guidance framework enables flexible, retraining-free conditional generation across arbitrary attribute combinations, which is directly useful for rare cell type augmentation and multi-batch integration. Key limitations include the conditional independence assumption across attributes and the separate (rather than joint) training of the autoencoder and flow, which the authors acknowledge as inefficient. Open questions include whether joint training can be stabilized, whether the independence assumption holds in complex multi-omics settings, and whether the approach scales to spatial transcriptomics or proteomics.
