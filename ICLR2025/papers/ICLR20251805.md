## Problem

State-of-the-art autoregressive TTS models like VALL-E require vector quantization (VQ/RVQ) to discretize continuous speech before applying autoregressive training, introducing reconstruction artifacts, mispronunciations, prohibitively large codebooks, and a complex two-stage pipeline with specialized multi-codebook handling. This paper asks whether quantization is truly a prerequisite for autoregressive speech modeling, or whether a continuous latent space can be directly modeled autoregressively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251805/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text string (converted to phoneme features) and a prompt speech sample (3–15 seconds of 16 kHz audio) as input, and outputs a synthesized speech waveform. The prompt speech is encoded by the GMM-VAE encoder to provide speaker/style conditioning, while the text is encoded by a Conformer text encoder. The autoregressive decoder generates continuous latent frames (64-dimensional vectors) sequentially, which are then decoded by the GMM-VAE decoder into waveforms. Unlike VQ-based systems, there is no discrete token vocabulary or codebook lookup at inference time.

## Method

The approach replaces the VQ-VAE codec with a GMM-VAE that constrains the latent space to a learned Gaussian Mixture prior (3 components, λ=50) instead of a discrete codebook, preserving multi-modality while keeping representations continuous. The autoregressive model (GMM-LM) is a Conformer encoder-decoder that predicts each next latent frame as a 6-component Gaussian Mixture distribution with diagonal covariances, eliminating the need for embedding layers, softmax output layers, and stop-token classification. A stochastic hard monotonic alignment mechanism enforces strict monotonic text-to-speech alignment by sampling binary alignment decisions from a Bernoulli distribution in the forward pass and using a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation for gradient computation in the backward pass. The entire pipeline uses a single Conformer model for all tokens (no separate AR/NAR stages), halving the dominant computation from 2DL² to DL² and reducing total parameters to 51.5M (10.3% of VALL-E) while achieving lower WER and higher MOS.

## Results

On the LibriSpeech test set with 3-second prompts, the proposed GMM-LM-Mini (51.5M params) achieves WER 2.83%, SIM 0.80, S-MOS 3.84, and Q-MOS 4.11, outperforming VALL-E (496.5M params) which scores WER 6.04%, SIM 0.73, S-MOS 3.65, and Q-MOS 3.54. The larger GMM-LM-Large (315M params) further improves to WER 2.81%, SIM 0.85, S-MOS 4.04, and Q-MOS 4.06. The GMM-VAE codec also surpasses Encodec in reconstruction quality (Mel Distance 1.13 vs. 1.47, SI-SDR 8.89 vs. 5.66). Notably, the Mini model achieves these results with only 10.3% of VALL-E's parameters and half the theoretical computation (DL² vs. 2DL²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VALL-E (the primary AR baseline), the proposed method wins decisively on all metrics: WER drops from 6.04% to 2.83% (3s prompt), SIM rises from 0.73 to 0.80, and Q-MOS improves from 3.54 to 4.11, while using only 10.3% of VALL-E's parameters (51.5M vs. 496.5M). VALL-E's WER degrades with longer prompts (6.04% → 9.68% from 3s to 15s), whereas the proposed method remains stable. Against non-AR baselines StyleTTS-2 and HierSpeech++, the proposed method achieves lower WER (2.83% vs. 3.32% and 3.45%) and higher speaker similarity (SIM 0.80 vs. 0.70 and 0.71) at 3s prompts, with the gap widening for longer prompts where non-AR models show no improvement. In the codec reconstruction comparison (Table 3), GMM-VAE outperforms Encodec on all three metrics (Mel Distance 1.13 vs. 1.47, ViSQOL 3.96 vs. 3.41, SI-SDR 8.89 vs. 5.66), and GMM-LM with teacher forcing also beats VALL-E's teacher-forced reconstruction (Mel Distance 1.82 vs. 2.26, SI-SDR 4.45 vs. −0.15). The proposed method also demonstrates superior noise robustness, with WER and SIM degrading only slightly under −10dB to −20dB prompt noise, while VALL-E's WER nearly doubles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VQ-VAE → autoregressive LM pipeline (VALL-E, SoundStream, Encodec) by demonstrating that vector quantization is not a prerequisite for autoregressive speech modeling. It builds on the GMM-VAE literature (Dilokthanakul et al., 2016; Tomczak & Welling, 2018) to impose a multi-modal continuous prior on the latent space, and on PixelCNN++ (Salimans et al., 2017) for parameterizing the AR output as a Gaussian mixture rather than a categorical distribution. The monotonic alignment mechanism extends Raffel et al. (2017) by replacing soft attention with Bernoulli-sampled binary alignments and a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation for differentiable backpropagation, while the codec architecture is adapted from DAC (Kumar et al., 2024) with all quantization layers removed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core finding—that a 51.5M-parameter continuous AR model outperforms the 496.5M-parameter VALL-E in both WER and MOS—challenges the field's working assumption that discrete quantization is necessary to make AR modeling applicable to speech. This opens a simpler, more parameter-efficient design space for speech language models without codebook management, RVQ complexity, or straight-through estimator instability. However, all experiments are confined to English audiobook speech (LibriLight/LibriSpeech), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to multilingual, spontaneous, or noisy real-world speech. The monotonic alignment contribution also has broader applicability to any AR sequence-to-sequence model where strict alignment is desired.
