## Problem

Solving 3D PDEs (e.g., Navier-Stokes) over irregular CFD geometries with neural operators remains challenging: graph-based methods scale poorly with mesh complexity, transformer-based solvers incur O(N²) cost, and existing neural operator pipelines lack interpretability of intermediate representations. Fengbo addresses this by casting the entire PDE-solution pipeline in 3D Clifford Algebra, where every layer's output carries explicit geometric or physical meaning, enabling a whitebox model with O(N log N) complexity and ~60% fewer parameters than competing architectures.

## Input / Output

Inputs are irregular 3D point clouds (car surface with normals, velocity-field points) and optionally an inlet velocity scalar, which are voxelised into a fixed M×M×M regular grid and embedded as multivectors in G(3,0,0): the scalar slot holds a binary occupancy mask, the vector slot holds 3D coordinates, and the bivector slot holds the dual of surface normals. Outputs are extracted via grade projection from the final physics multivectors—the grade-0 component yields the scalar pressure field φ(x) on the surface, and the grade-1 component yields the 3D velocity vector field ψ(x) over the domain—so both quantities are predicted jointly from a single shared FNO output without additional computational overhead.

## Method

Fengbo is a three-stage pipeline entirely in G(3,0,0) Clifford Algebra: (i) 3D Clifford Geometry blocks apply 3D convolutions with multivector weights via geometric products to mix grades, capture local features, fill sparse voxel volumes, and expand channels; (ii) a 3D Clifford FNO performs spectral convolutions in the Fourier domain on full-grade multivectors (all 8 basis elements), extending Brandstetter et al. (2022) beyond vector/bivector-only processing, to capture global interactions and map geometry to physics; (iii) 3D Clifford Physics blocks downsample channels and extract the target physical quantities via grade projectors. The key design insight is that because every intermediate quantity is a multivector with a fixed geometric interpretation (coordinates, normals, planes, volumes), the network is inherently equivariant under spatial transformations and interpretable at every layer. Training is supervised with a weighted joint L2 loss on pressure and velocity plus an L1 penalty, elastic-net regularisation, and Adam optimisation over 100 epochs on 3 A100 GPUs, yielding 42M trainable parameters.

## Results

On the ShapeNet Car dataset, Fengbo achieves a relative L2 error of 8.86% for pressure and 3.47% for velocity, outperforming GINO encoder-decoder (9.47% pressure, 3.86% velocity) while using 60% fewer trainable parameters (42M total). On the Ahmed Body dataset, Fengbo attains 10.7% pressure testing error with the lowest training error (8.00%) among all compared models. Fengbo is the only architecture in the comparison that jointly estimates both the scalar pressure field and the 3D velocity vector field without additional computational overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ShapeNet Car pressure, Fengbo (8.86%) beats GINO encoder-decoder (9.47%), FNO (9.42%), UNet (12.8%), Geo-FNO (15.9%), GNO (18.8%), PointNet (11.0%), Graph U-Net (11.0%), GraphSage (10.5%), and MLP (13.0%), but trails GINO decoder-only (7.12%) and MeshGraphNet (7.81%). On velocity, Fengbo (3.47%) outperforms GINO encoder-decoder (3.86%), GNO (3.83%), Geo-FNO (16.7%), and all PointNet/Graph-based baselines, with only MeshGraphNet (3.54%) marginally ahead. On Ahmed Body, Fengbo (10.7%) surpasses MeshGraphNet (13.9%), UNet (11.2%), FNO (12.6%), and most GINO configurations, but loses to GINO decoder-only (8.31%). The paper notes that Fengbo's joint estimation of pressure and velocity provides a coupling benefit that constrains both fields simultaneously, and that its O(N log N) complexity is two orders of magnitude lower than Transolver's O(10^8) vs. O(10^6), despite Transolver achieving a 1.4% lower pressure error at that higher cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fengbo extends the Clifford Neural Layers framework of Brandstetter et al. (2022) by upgrading the Clifford FNO to process full-grade 3D multivectors (scalar through trivector) rather than only vector and bivector components, and by wrapping it in a three-stage pipeline (Geometry blocks → FNO → Physics blocks) that explicitly separates geometric encoding from physical regression. It departs from the dominant graph-based paradigm for irregular 3D geometries (GINO, MeshGraphNet, GNO) by instead voxelising the domain into a regular grid, trading graph connectivity for the O(N log N) complexity of spectral convolutions. The architecture is also positioned against Transformer-based PDE solvers (Transolver, Galerkin Transformer) as a lower-complexity alternative, and against Geo-FNO as a simpler route to handling irregular shapes without learned domain deformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a compact (42 M-parameter) pipeline operating entirely in G(3,0,0) Clifford Algebra can match or slightly beat the more complex GINO pipeline on 3D CFD benchmarks while jointly estimating pressure and velocity at no extra computational cost, and that every intermediate layer output retains a clear geometric or physical interpretation (a 'whitebox' property absent in standard neural operators). Key limitations include the restriction to two datasets, the reliance on tensor-based multivector representations that slow training, and the fact that the tested model is capped at 42 M parameters with ablations suggesting deeper/wider variants could improve further. Open questions include whether the Clifford Algebra inductive bias generalises to PDEs with fewer geometric cues, whether the voxelisation step can be replaced by native irregular-grid processing, and whether the interpretability advantage translates into practical debugging or safety guarantees in deployment.
