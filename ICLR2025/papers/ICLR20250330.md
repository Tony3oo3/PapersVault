## Problem

Prior algorithms for learning truncated exponential families (Daskalakis et al. 2018; Lee et al. 2023) suffer from super-polynomial dependence on the inverse survival mass 1/α in both runtime and oracle calls to the truncation set. This paper asks whether one can achieve polynomial dependence on 1/α while retaining state-of-the-art scaling in dimension k and accuracy ε, and provides a positive answer through a new optimization-based analysis of the truncated negative log-likelihood.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes i.i.d. samples from the unknown truncated density p_θ*^S, a membership oracle M_S(x) = 1_S(x) for the survival set, and a β-truncated sample oracle that returns samples from p_θ^S only when p_θ(S) ≥ β (implementable via rejection sampling). It outputs a parameter estimate θ̂ satisfying KL(p_θ*^S ‖ p_θ̂^S) ≤ ε with probability ≥ 99%, using Õ(k/ε² · log(1/α)) samples and Õ(k/ε² · log²(1/α)) oracle calls. The guarantee is on the truncated distributions rather than the full densities, keeping assumptions minimal; parameter recovery follows as a corollary under identifiability conditions.

## Method

The key technical contribution is a simple feasible region for optimization defined directly by the non-truncated loss: D = {θ : L(θ) ≤ L(θ₀) + log(1/α)}, where θ₀ is an empirical estimate of the truncated mean sufficient statistic. Any point in D is guaranteed to assign at least Ω(α) mass to S, which keeps gradients bounded and makes rejection sampling efficient. The algorithm runs projected stochastic gradient descent (PSGD) on the truncated NLL L_S(θ) = L(θ) + log p_θ(S) over D, with smoothness L(1 + log²(1/α)) and gradient variance O(kL log⁶(1/α)). To avoid overshooting into low-mass regions, the method incrementally expands the sublevel threshold from L_min upward in unit steps, stopping when successive constrained minima agree within ε/log(1/α). The truncated sufficient statistic is shown to be sub-exponential with parameter K² = L + log²(1/α), enabling Bernstein-type concentration that yields the poly(log(1/α)) sample complexity for initialization.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1.5) shows that learning a truncated exponential family to KL-divergence ε requires Õ(k/ε² · log(1/α)) samples from the truncated distribution and Õ(k/ε² · log²(1/α)) calls to an Ω(α)-truncated sample oracle, with runtime poly(k, 1/ε, log(1/α)). For truncated Gaussians (Corollary 1.6), the algorithm uses d²/ε² · poly(log(1/α)) samples and M · poly(1/α) membership oracle calls to achieve d_TV ≤ ε. For log-concave exponential families with polynomial sufficient statistics (Corollary 1.7), the same sample complexity holds with the stronger guarantee of parameter recovery ||θ̂ − θ*||₂ ≤ ε and d_TV(p_θ̂, p_θ*) ≤ ε.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central improvement over both Daskalakis et al. (2018) and Lee et al. (2023) is reducing the dependence on the survival mass α from super-polynomial to polynomial. Daskalakis et al. (2018) require M · α^(−poly(log(1/α))) oracle calls and super-polynomial runtime in 1/α for truncated Gaussians, while Lee et al. (2023) require M · poly(α^(−log(1/α))) oracle calls for general log-concave exponential families. In contrast, this paper achieves only M · poly(1/α) oracle calls and poly(k, 1/ε, log(1/α)) runtime, matching the sample complexity of prior work (poly(log(1/α)) in 1/α) while fixing the oracle and runtime dependencies. The improvement stems from requiring only an Ω(α)-truncated sample oracle (versus α^(−log(1/α)) for Lee et al.) and from a simpler feasible region defined directly by the non-truncated NLL sublevel set, which guarantees all parameters in the optimization domain assign at least Ω(α) mass to S.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning from truncated samples, building directly on Daskalakis et al. (2018) for truncated Gaussians and Lee et al. (2023) for general exponential families. The key conceptual departure is in the optimization strategy: rather than designing setting-specific feasible regions (as prior works did for Gaussians, product distributions, permutations, etc.), the authors identify a single, simple constraint—bounding the non-truncated NLL relative to initialization—that uniformly ensures sufficient mass on the survival set across all exponential families. This unified perspective replaces the super-polynomial (1/α)^{poly(log(1/α))} oracle and runtime dependence of prior work with a clean polynomial dependence on 1/α, while preserving the same polynomial dependence on dimension and accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The polynomial-in-1/α guarantee is practically significant when the survival mass is very small (e.g., rare-event detection, selective observation in surveys or clinical trials), where super-polynomial oracle calls become infeasible. The paper also provides a modular framework—decoupling sample complexity (via sub-exponential concentration of truncated sufficient statistics) from optimization complexity (via projected SGD)—that could inform future work on other truncated or censored estimation problems. Open questions include whether the strong convexity and smoothness assumptions can be relaxed, whether the approach extends to non-convex settings such as mixtures of exponentials, and whether the KL-divergence guarantee on truncated densities can be tightened to parameter recovery under weaker identifiability conditions.
