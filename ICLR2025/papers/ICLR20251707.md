## Problem

VecSet (3DShape2VecSet) encodes 3D geometry into a single-scale latent vector set, but its expressiveness is bounded by the number of latent vectors, causing overfitting on small datasets like ShapeNet and making it infeasible to scale to large datasets like Objaverse without prohibitive GPU costs. The quadratic complexity of self-attention over a large number of vectors further limits how much geometric detail can be captured efficiently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251707/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The autoencoder takes a dense 3D point cloud (sampled from a watertight mesh normalized to the unit sphere) and produces a hierarchical set of latent vectors at L resolution levels (e.g., 128×64, 512×32, 2048×16 channels for the Objaverse model). Each latent level is standardized to zero mean and unit variance, and the decoder reconstructs a continuous occupancy function by concatenating cross-attention queries across all levels. For generation, the cascaded diffusion model optionally accepts a category label or text prompt as conditioning and outputs the full hierarchical latent set, which is then decoded into 3D geometry.

## Method

LaGeM introduces a U-Net-style transformer that replaces VecSet's single-scale bottleneck with L hierarchical levels, using cross-attention as both a downsampling operator (encoder) and an upsampling operator (decoder) between levels. A key design choice is the Normalized Bottleneck Autoencoder (NBAE) regularization, which standardizes each latent vector to zero mean and unit variance via a learnable scale-and-shift (analogous to LayerNorm) instead of KL divergence, aligning the latent distribution with the Gaussian noise used in diffusion and eliminating an explicit latent loss term. The final occupancy function is built by concatenating cross-attention features from all levels, so each level contributes a different granularity of geometric detail. For generation, a cascaded diffusion model (using DiT-based denoisers with the EDM formulation) generates latents from the coarsest to the finest level, where each stage conditions on previously generated latents via cross-attention, enabling explicit control over the level of detail in the output geometry.

## Results

On ShapeNet, LaGeM-Objaverse achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 2.42 (×100) and F-Score of 98.93 (×100) across all categories, compared to VecSet's 4.68 and 93.25 respectively — an improvement of roughly 48% in Chamfer. On the generalization benchmark (Table 5), LaGeM-Objaverse outperforms VecSet on all seven external datasets, with the largest gains on GSO (Chamfer 2.35 vs 3.78) and Thingi10k (Chamfer 2.99 vs 4.52). For generative quality on ShapeNet, LaGeM achieves surface-FPD of 0.64 (chair) and 1.12 (table), beating VecSet (0.76 / 1.19) and 3DILG (0.96 / 2.10). Training efficiency is also improved: at 2048 latent vectors, LaGeM uses 0.70× the training time and 0.58× the GPU memory of VecSet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is VecSet (Zhang et al., 2023), the single-level vector-set autoencoder that LaGeM directly extends. LaGeM-ShapeNet (same dataset, similar parameter count) matches or slightly beats VecSet on ShapeNet (Chamfer 4.63 vs 4.68, F-Score 93.47 vs 93.25), while LaGeM-Objaverse (trained on 600k models) substantially outperforms VecSet with a 48% reduction in Chamfer (2.42 vs 4.68) and a 5.68-point F-Score gain (98.93 vs 93.25) across all ShapeNet categories. The advantage is most pronounced on small categories (e.g., lamp: Chamfer 2.37 vs 4.36) where VecSet overfits due to limited training samples. On the generative task, LaGeM's cascaded diffusion beats VecSet on surface-FPD for both chair (0.64 vs 0.76) and table (1.12 vs 1.19), and clearly outperforms 3DILG (Zhang et al., 2022) which uses an irregular latent grid (chair FPD 0.96, table FPD 2.10). Across all seven external generalization datasets (Thingi10k, ABO, EGAD, GSO, pix3d, FAUST, ShapeNet-test), LaGeM-Objaverse consistently achieves lower Chamfer and higher F-Score than VecSet, confirming that the hierarchical design generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaGeM directly extends 3DShape2VecSet (Zhang et al., 2023), which encodes 3D geometry as a single-scale set of latent vectors, by introducing a U-Net-style hierarchical architecture with multiple resolution levels, each controlling a different geometric detail. The multi-scale latent design is inspired by NVAE (Vahdat & Kautz, 2020) for images, and the cascaded generative framework adapts Cascaded Diffusion (Ho et al., 2022) from image grids to unordered vector sets. A key conceptual departure is replacing KL divergence regularization with a simple standardization bottleneck (NBAE), motivated by aligning the latent distribution with the Gaussian noise used in diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical impact is enabling VecSet-style autoencoders to scale to large datasets like Objaverse (600k models) with 0.7× training time and 0.58× memory, removing a major bottleneck for 3D representation learning. The hierarchical latent space also provides a natural mechanism for controllable generation (structure → major details → minor details), which is demonstrated but remains preliminary. A key open question is whether the cascaded diffusion framework can match the quality of single-stage diffusion at equivalent compute budgets, and whether the NBAE regularization holds up under more aggressive generative training regimes.
