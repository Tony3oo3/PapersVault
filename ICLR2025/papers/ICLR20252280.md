## Problem

Existing fake image detectors for latent diffusion models are trained on real/fake datasets where the two classes differ in resolution, aspect ratio, and other image properties beyond the generative model's artifacts, causing detectors to latch onto spurious correlations (e.g., associating downsampling with real images). This paper argues that dataset alignment—ensuring the only difference between real and fake images is the generative model's fingerprint—is a critical but underexplored factor in building robust detectors.

## Input / Output

The detector takes a single RGB image at any resolution as input and outputs a binary real/fake probability score (threshold at 0.5 for accuracy, or calibrated per-dataset for the shader-trained variant). Training images are 96×96 crops sampled from full-resolution real images (COCO/LSUN, ranging from 256×256 to 640×480+) and their VAE reconstructions at matching resolutions. The method is evaluated on images from seven different LDM variants plus post-processed versions with JPEG compression, blur, color jitter, and resizing.

## Method

The core idea is to generate fake training images by passing real images through only the LDM's VAE autoencoder (encoder then decoder) without any denoising step, producing reconstructions that are near-identical to the originals in resolution, aspect ratio, and semantic content but carry the VAE decoder's artifacts. A ResNet-50 (ImageNet-pretrained) is then fine-tuned for binary real/fake classification using the same training recipe as Corvi et al. (2022), including random resized crop, JPEG compression, blur, and other augmentations. Two variants are explored: 'Ours' (random batch mixing of real and fake) and 'Ours-Sync' (each fake image is batched with its corresponding real image under identical augmentations, enforcing alignment at the batch level). Because all LDM-generated images necessarily pass through the same VAE decoder, a detector trained on VAE reconstruction artifacts generalizes to images produced by the full denoising pipeline, even with different U-Net architectures. The approach is ~10× more computationally efficient than iterative denoising-based dataset creation and achieves superior data efficiency, requiring as few as 1k image pairs to learn a strong detector.

## Results

On the main detection benchmark (Table 1), Ours-Sync achieves near-perfect accuracy across all tested generators: 99.57% (SD), 99.37% (Midjourney), 99.57% (Kandinsky), 99.48% (Playground), 100% (PixelArt-α), and 100% (LCM). The most striking improvement over the strongest baseline (Corvi) is on Playground, where Ours-Sync reaches 99.48% versus Corvi's 82.13% (+17.35 points), and on Midjourney (99.37% vs. 96.90%). The method also requires only a single VAE forward pass to generate training fakes, making dataset creation over 10× cheaper than iterative denoising-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Corvi (the strongest ResNet-50-based baseline), Ours-Sync wins decisively on Playground (99.48% vs. 82.13%, +17.35) and Midjourney (99.37% vs. 96.90%, +2.47), while Corvi retains a marginal edge on Real (99.96% vs. 99.76%) and SD (99.73% vs. 99.57%). Against AEROBLADE (training-free, reconstruction-error-based), Ours-Sync dominates on SD (99.57% vs. 74.50%) and Playground (99.48% vs. 14.87%), though AEROBLADE slightly edges out on Midjourney (99.50% vs. 99.37%). Against the CLIP linear-probing baselines (Ojha-LDM and Cozzolino-LDM), the proposed method outperforms by 20–80+ points on nearly every generator, with Ojha-LDM failing catastrophically on Real images (54.16%) due to spurious resizing correlations. The paper also shows that under post-processing (Table 2), Ours-Sync's advantage over Corvi widens further, e.g., +52.09 points on Playground (76.36% vs. 24.27%), confirming that dataset alignment yields substantially greater robustness to real-world corruptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Corvi et al. (2022) training pipeline (ResNet-50, data augmentations, binary cross-entropy) but departs fundamentally in dataset construction: instead of generating fakes via the full iterative denoising process (as Corvi, Ojha, and Cozzolino all do), it reconstructs real images using only the LDM's VAE autoencoder in a single forward pass. Conceptually, it is related to reconstruction-based detection methods like AEROBLADE (Ricker et al., 2024) and DIRE (Wang et al., 2023), but repurposes VAE reconstructions as a *training data generation* strategy rather than an inference-time signal. The paper positions itself as addressing a gap that prior work overlooked: while much effort went into architecture and training recipe choices, the real/fake dataset alignment was left unexamined, allowing spurious correlations (e.g., resolution-dependent resizing artifacts) to leak into the learned decision boundary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 10× reduction in dataset creation cost (skipping the UNet entirely) combined with measurably better robustness to post-processing and architectural changes in the UNet makes this an attractive drop-in improvement for anyone building LDM fake detectors. The shader experiment raises an important open question about what a detector actually needs to learn—whether the VAE decoder's fingerprint is truly content-agnostic—and suggests that future work could explore even more synthetic or domain-shifted training data. Key limitations remain: the method fails on models with substantially different VAE architectures (e.g., FLUX's 16-channel latent space), and it does not address pixel-space diffusion models where no VAE exists. The .webp compression sensitivity also highlights that alignment is necessary but not sufficient for full robustness to arbitrary real-world file properties.
