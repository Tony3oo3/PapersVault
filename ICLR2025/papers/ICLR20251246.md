## Problem

Negative Prompting (NP) in diffusion models uses a constant guidance scale with a flipped sign to repel generation away from undesired features, but this is theoretically flawed: the repulsive field should be strongest near the undesired region and vanish far from it, yet NP's static scale produces large score fields in regions completely unrelated to the negative prompt. The paper shows NP can completely fail to sample the target conditional distribution in one dimension and is suboptimal in higher dimensions, and that the non-stationary, state-dependent nature of the reverse diffusion process makes a fixed guidance scale fundamentally inappropriate.

## Input / Output

The method requires two pre-trained diffusion models — an unconditional model and a 'to-forget' model trained solely on the undesired class or concept — plus a positive text prompt and a negative prompt. During denoising, it tracks the Markov chain transition probabilities between consecutive noisy states to compute a posterior probability p(c₋|x,t) at each step, which in turn determines the dynamic guidance scale. The output is a generated image with the undesired feature suppressed, along with the time-varying guidance scale trajectory; no additional training or fine-tuning of either model is required.

## Method

DNG derives the correct conditional distribution p(x|c₊) ∝ p(x)·(1 − p(c₋|x)), which is always normalizable, and converts it into a score-based guidance equation where the constant NP scale is replaced by a state-dependent factor λ(x,t) = λ₀·p(c₋|x,t)/(1−p(c₋|x,t)). This factor is zero when the posterior is low (the negative prompt is irrelevant) and asymptotically large when the posterior approaches one (the generation is heading toward the undesired region), making the guidance self-regulating. The posterior is estimated without an external classifier by recursively updating log p(c₋|x_t) using the difference in squared distances between the current noisy state and the mean predictions of the unconditional versus to-forget models at each denoising step (Algorithm 2), with a temperature parameter τ to dampen stochastic fluctuations and a small offset δ to bias toward posterior increase. In the static limit (zeroth-order approximation of the posterior), DNG reduces exactly to standard NP, and in the 1D Gaussian mixture setting with the exact posterior, DNG is provably equivalent to positive CFG on the desired modes.

## Results

On class removal from CIFAR-10 and MNIST, DNG outperforms both Negative Prompting (NP) and Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD) in KL-divergence (class balance) and FID (image quality) at high safety levels across all four tested classes. On Stable Diffusion text-to-image, DNG achieves a Q metric of 0.052 ± 0.022 versus NP's 0.007 ± 0.014, indicating that DNG removes related unwanted features while leaving images unaltered when the negative prompt is semantically unrelated. In the 1D Gaussian mixture validation, DNG with the exact posterior is provably equivalent to CFG on the desired modes, whereas NP completely fails to sample the target distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNG is compared against two baselines: Negative Prompting (NP, i.e., CFG with a flipped sign) and Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD). On class removal from CIFAR-10 and MNIST, DNG dominates both baselines in the high-safety regime (where fewest forbidden images are generated), achieving lower KL-divergence and lower FID simultaneously, meaning it removes the target class more completely while preserving image quality and class balance. NP is the weakest baseline: it produces large, uniform repulsive fields even far from the undesired region, leading to unnecessary image distortion and poor class balance. SLD, which uses a pixel-wise heuristic guidance scale, can match or slightly beat DNG on FID at very low safety levels (e.g., ~3% forbidden class remaining) due to its local, less-invasive edits, but DNG clearly surpasses SLD once high safety is required. On Stable Diffusion T2I, DNG's self-regulating guidance scale deactivates (CLIP score ≈ 1) when the negative prompt is semantically unrelated to the positive prompt, whereas NP still modifies the image in that case, resulting in DNG's substantially higher Q metric (0.052 vs. 0.007).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) by Ho & Salimans (2021), replacing the constant guidance scale with a dynamic, state-dependent scale derived from the posterior probability of the forbidden class. The posterior estimation mechanism is inspired by Li et al. (2023), who showed that diffusion models implicitly encode class information trackable via the Markov chain. The key departure is from Negative Prompting (NP), which the paper demonstrates is a theoretically flawed special case of CFG with a sign flip: because score fields are approximately harmonic, inverting them creates large repulsive forces in regions far from the undesired condition, violating the desired property that guidance should be strongest near the forbidden region. Safe Latent Diffusion (Schramowski et al., 2023) is the closest prior work, using a heuristic non-constant scale, but lacks the explicit time-dependence and the principled posterior-based formulation that DNG provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental theoretical flaw in Negative Prompting, a technique underpinning all major text-to-image models (Stable Diffusion, Midjourney), and provides a principled fix that self-regulates: DNG deactivates itself when the negative prompt is semantically unrelated to the positive prompt, preserving image diversity. However, the T2I validation is limited to 5 prompts with no FID or visual classifier verification, and the class-removal experiments, while solid on MNIST/CIFAR10, do not yet demonstrate the approach at the scale of modern T2I models. Open questions include whether prompt-specific hyperparameter tuning can be replaced by a general scheme, how the approach behaves with multi-concept negative prompts, and whether the 1D analytical failure of NP translates to measurable degradation in high-dimensional natural image spaces.
