## Problem

Existing dataset pruning methods for the pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm require training or fine-tuning the full downstream dataset to compute per-sample importance scores, which is prohibitively expensive when the pre-trained model has billions of parameters. Moreover, these methods do not leverage the pre-trained model's existing knowledge, which is already available before fine-tuning and should influence which samples are most informative.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the frozen pre-trained encoder weights (ResNet-18/50, ViT-Small/Base for images; Mistral 7B, Llama3 8B, Gemma2 9B for text) and a labeled downstream dataset, producing a per-sample DLC hardness score and a pruned subset of size M ≤ η·N. For image classification, the loss integral is computed against class-prototype features (mean category embedding); for LLM instruction tuning, it is replaced by a token-level cross-entropy averaged over the output sequence length C. The scoring is entirely training-free, requiring only forward passes through masked versions of the pre-trained model.

## Method

DLC constructs a learning path by masking pre-trained weights at increasing ratios (2%–100%), where weights with the smallest L1 norm are masked first, yielding a sequence of models with monotonically increasing capacity and decreasing expected risk. The learning complexity score for each sample is the Monte Carlo approximation of the integral of its classification loss over this capacity sequence, exploiting the observation that easy samples achieve low loss even at low model capacity. To select the pruned subset, FlexRand replaces the conventional top-K strategy by randomly sampling equal numbers of samples from the easy and hard score intervals, whose boundary is controlled by a splitting hyperparameter γ tuned on validation data. This design adapts sampling preference to the data regime (data-poor vs. data-rich) while avoiding the severe distribution shift that deterministic top-K selection induces, as measured by MMD.

## Results

On the downstream image dataset pruning benchmark (5 datasets × 9 pruning ratios), DLC + FlexRand achieves 60.45% average accuracy across ResNet-18, ResNet-50, ViT-Small, and ViT-Base, outperforming the random baseline (59.21%) by 1.24% and the best training-based baseline Forgetting (59.15%) by 1.30%. The scoring time is only 21.5 s (29.5 s total including hyperparameter tuning), a 35× speedup over one-shot fine-tuning baselines (~1033 s) and a 137× speedup over EL2N (4051 s). For LLM instruction pruning (50% of Alpaca Cleaned or Dolly & HH-RLHF), the method consistently outperforms random on MMLU across Mistral 7B, Llama3 8B, and Gemma2 9B, with gains of 1–3 percentage points per category.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLC + FlexRand outperforms all 11 baselines on average accuracy across the four architectures. The closest competitor is Forgetting (59.15%), which the method beats by 1.30%; uncertainty-based methods (Least Conf 59.06%, Entropy 59.14%, Margin 59.03%) and SSP (58.63%) all fall below the random baseline (59.21%) on average, while geometric methods (Herding 54.31%, kCG 58.34%, CD 57.48%) and gradient-based methods (GraNd 56.88%, EL2N 56.43%) perform substantially worse. The speed advantage is dramatic: DLC requires only 21.5 s for scoring versus ~1033 s for one-shot fine-tuning baselines and 4051 s for EL2N, a 35–137× reduction. On the LLM instruction pruning benchmark (Table 2), the method consistently beats random by 1–3 points per MMLU category across Mistral 7B, Llama3 8B, and Gemma2 9B, confirming that the learning-complexity signal transfers to text generation. Ablations (Figure 6) further show that FlexRand outperforms fixed top-K strategies (T-H, T-E, T-F) by 1.39% over random, and that the L1-based masking principle yields more stable rankings than random masking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of dataset pruning methods (EL2N, Forgetting, GraNd, SSP) that require training or fine-tuning the model on the full dataset to compute scoring functions, which is infeasible for large pre-trained models in the PT-FT paradigm. It repurposes the model-pruning idea of L1-norm-based weight masking (Han et al., 2015) to construct a 'learning path' without any optimization, and formalizes this through a Learning Complexity integral approximated by Monte Carlo sampling. The FlexRand sampling strategy extends the Moderate Coreset observation (Xia et al., 2023) that different data regimes prefer different score intervals, but replaces fixed top-K selection with a randomized two-interval scheme to avoid distribution shift. The work further extends the scoring function to LLM instruction pruning, a setting where prior dataset pruning methods have not been applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 35× reduction in pruning time is practically significant for edge-device fine-tuning and large-model scenarios where even one-shot fine-tuning on the full dataset is prohibitive. A notable finding is that most existing training-based pruning methods perform at or below random in the PT-FT setting, suggesting their scoring functions are miscalibrated when the encoder is already pre-trained. The main limitation is that DLC's effectiveness depends on the pre-trained model providing meaningful representations, and the modest 1.24% average improvement over random (though consistent) leaves room for stronger gains. Open questions include whether the masking-based learning path generalizes to non-ImageNet pre-training, whether the approach extends to multi-modal or generative tasks, and whether the L1-norm masking principle is optimal or whether other distortion schedules could improve the score.
