## Problem

Dense contrastive learning methods struggle to establish pixel/patch correspondence across different views: bilinear interpolation approaches are fragile under random crop augmentation (failing entirely when views don't overlap), while self-contrasting the same view limits input variance. The paper identifies the root cause as the reliance on discrete positional embeddings, which cannot naturally encode the continuous spatial offset between two arbitrarily cropped views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251176/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two randomly cropped views (x_A, x_B) from the same image, each resized to H×W×3, along with their absolute crop coordinates (top, left, height, width). It produces a pretrained ViT encoder (ViT-S/16, ViT-B/16, or ViT-L/16) whose patch-level features serve as dense representations for downstream detection, segmentation, and classification. A lightweight decoder maps the cross-attended query tokens to either RGB pixel values or teacher patch embeddings, depending on the chosen supervision signal.

## Method

CR2PQ constructs a relative coordinate system anchored at view A's top-left corner (unit scale = one feature-map grid of A), then computes continuous (non-integer) relative positions for every patch of view B via a closed-form affine transform of the crop parameters. These continuous coordinates feed into a rotary positional embedding (extending RoPE's discrete index to real-valued inputs), producing a relative rotary matrix R_rela that encodes the spatial offset between views. In the final transformer block, a positional-aware cross-attention module uses R_rela-modulated query tokens (derived from a learnable mask token) to attend over view A's key-value features, extracting view B's patch representations without any explicit correspondence mining. Training combines a patch-level distillation loss (L1 or L2 between student and frozen teacher patch embeddings of view B) with a global alignment loss on mean-pooled features, weighted by λ. The continuous rotary formulation makes the method correspondence-free and integrable into MIM, contrastive, and distillation paradigms, while the cross-attention design avoids the parameter overhead of adding extra query tokens to the backbone.

## Results

On COCO with Mask R-CNN (1x schedule, ViT-S/16), CR2PQ with 300 epochs pretraining achieves 50.5 mAP^bb and 43.4 mAP^mk, outperforming the previous SOTA PQCL (47.7 / 41.1) by 2.8 and 2.3 points respectively. With only 40 epochs pretraining, CR2PQ already reaches 47.2 mAP^bb and 41.1 mAP^mk, surpassing PQCL by 10.4 and 7.9 points. On ADE20K semantic segmentation (UperNet, 160K iters), CR2PQ achieves 47.0 mIoU, exceeding PQCL (45.1) by 1.9 points. For ImageNet-1K fine-tuning classification, CR2PQ reaches 82.2% top-1 (ViT-S, 300 epochs PT) and 83.7% top-1 (ViT-B, 300 epochs PT), matching or exceeding iBOT and MAE with fewer pretraining epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CR2PQ consistently outperforms all baselines on COCO detection and segmentation. Against the previous SOTA PQCL (300 epochs), CR2PQ gains +2.8 mAP^bb (50.5 vs 47.7) and +2.3 mAP^mk (43.4 vs 41.1) under Mask R-CNN. Against iBOT with 800 epochs and 10 extra local views, CR2PQ with only 300 epochs and 2 global views still leads by +1.1 mAP^bb and +0.8 mAP^mk. On ADE20K (UperNet), CR2PQ surpasses PQCL by +1.9 mIoU (47.0 vs 45.1) and DINO (800 epochs, 10 local views) by +2.6 mIoU (47.0 vs 44.4). For ImageNet-1K classification, CR2PQ (ViT-S, 300 epochs) achieves 82.2% top-1, exceeding iBOT (800 epochs, 81.8%) by 0.4 points with fewer pretraining epochs. Notably, CR2PQ with just 40 epochs pretraining already outperforms PQCL (200 epochs) by 10.4 mAP^bb and 7.9 mAP^mk, demonstrating dramatically faster convergence. The paper also shows that vanilla 2D RoPE trained from scratch (31.0 mAP^bb) is far inferior to CR2PQ, confirming that the continuous relative coordinate formulation is essential rather than simply applying RoPE to vision.

## Relation to Prior Work

CR2PQ extends the dense contrastive learning (DCL) line of work (DenseCL, PixPro, SelfPatch, iBOT, PQCL) by replacing the discrete relative positional embeddings used in PQCL with continuous rotary positional embeddings computed from real-valued relative coordinates between two randomly cropped views. Conceptually, it departs from prior DCL methods that either require bilinear interpolation to establish pixel/patch correspondence or input the same view twice (limiting input variance), by making the correspondence problem vanish through the continuous coordinate system. The student-teacher distillation framework with cross-attention for patch-level reconstruction is inherited from DINO and ADCLR, while the rotary positional encoding mechanism is adapted from RoFormer (Su et al., 2024) in the NLP domain, with the key extension from discrete integer indices to continuous real-valued coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The correspondence-free formulation simplifies the DCL pipeline substantially (no GCN or sub-network for correspondence mining) and the method integrates cleanly into MIM, contrastive, and distillation paradigms, making it broadly applicable. The notably fast convergence (40 epochs outperforming 200-epoch baselines by >10 mAP points) has practical implications for compute budgets in pretraining. A stated limitation is sensitivity to background-heavy views, where randomly cropped views may contain little foreground content, potentially degrading downstream performance. Open questions include whether the continuous RoPE formulation generalizes to video or 3D settings, and whether the reliance on a pretrained teacher model (which gives the best results) limits applicability in fully unsupervised settings.
