## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods allocate budgets at the layer level, treating all attention heads within a layer identically, despite evidence that heads play distinct roles in generation. Moreover, prior head-importance estimation (e.g., retrieval heads) captures only the retrieval-and-paste mechanism, ignoring the contextual reasoning ability that tasks like long-context QA actually require. This paper asks whether per-head budget allocation guided by a joint retrieval-and-reasoning importance score can compress KV caches more effectively than layer-level strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253572/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long text context (up to 32K tokens) and a static, pre-computed head importance score distribution obtained once via Needle-in-a-Haystack experiments with retrieval-reasoning examples. During prefilling, it produces a per-head compressed KV cache where each head retains a different number of key-value pairs (determined by its importance score) plus the last α=8 instruction tokens; during decoding, the compressed cache is used to generate text. The importance scores are L1-normalized and fixed at initialization, so no per-input computation is needed for budget allocation.

## Method

HeadKV-R2 operates in three stages. First, it estimates a Retrieval-Reasoning (R2) head importance score by constructing Needle-in-a-Haystack examples that embed a reasoning step r and two candidate answers c¹, c², then scoring each head by summing weighted attention over all tokens of the correct answer c² across decoding steps (Eq. 2), yielding a denser distribution than the sparse retrieval-only baseline. Second, it allocates KV cache budgets per head: each head receives a base budget (b − b/β) plus a share of a shared global pool B proportional to its normalized importance score S_h (Eq. 4), where β controls the pool size. Third, within each head's allocated budget, it selects which specific KV entries to retain using SnapKV's attention-pooling strategy over the last α instruction tokens. The key distinction from prior work is that budget allocation is fully head-level (independent of layer constraints) and the importance signal jointly captures retrieval and reasoning rather than retrieval alone.

## Results

On LongBench (6 QA datasets) and LooGLE (4 long-dependency QA tasks), HeadKV-R2 with KV cache size 128 achieves 32.00 average F1 on Llama-3-8B-Instruct and 31.41 on Mistral-7B-Instruct, retaining only ~1.5% of the full KV cache while reaching 97% of FullKV performance (32.90 / 32.98). At KV size 1024, HeadKV-R2 even surpasses FullKV on Llama-3-8B-Instruct (32.95 vs. 32.90 LongBench average). The advantage is most pronounced in low-resource settings (KV=64 and 128), where HeadKV-R2 outperforms the strongest baseline Ada-SnapKV by 3.5–3.3 F1 points on LongBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HeadKV-R2 consistently outperforms all three baselines (SnapKV, PyramidKV, and Ada-SnapKV) across both models and all KV cache sizes. At the most constrained setting (KV=128), HeadKV-R2 beats Ada-SnapKV by 3.48 F1 on Llama-3 LongBench (32.00 vs. 28.52) and by 3.32 on Mistral (31.41 vs. 28.09), while the gap over SnapKV and PyramidKV is even larger (5.57 and 5.80 on Llama-3). At KV=1024, HeadKV-R2 still leads Ada-SnapKV by 0.64 F1 on Llama-3 (32.95 vs. 32.31) and by 0.89 on Mistral (32.87 vs. 31.98), and marginally exceeds FullKV on Llama-3 (32.95 vs. 32.90). The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the Retrieval-Reasoning distribution (HeadKV-R2) improves over the standard Retrieval distribution (HeadKV-R) by 1.62 F1 on Llama-3 at KV=128, validating that jointly modeling retrieval and reasoning in importance estimation is the key differentiator over prior head-level allocation approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV cache compression line (SnapKV, PyramidKV, Ada-KV) by shifting the allocation granularity from layer-level to head-level, where each attention head receives a distinct budget rather than all heads in a layer sharing a uniform one. The importance scoring mechanism is directly inspired by Wu et al. (2024)'s retrieval-heads identification via Needle-in-a-Haystack tests, but the authors generalize it to a retrieval-reasoning (R2) formulation that accounts for multi-step contextual reasoning, addressing the sparsity and retrieval-only bias of the original distribution. The within-head selection strategy reuses SnapKV's observation-window attention pooling, so the core novelty is the cross-head budget redistribution via a shared pool proportional to R2 importance scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for long-context deployment: retaining only 1.5% of the KV cache while achieving 97% of full-cache QA performance directly reduces memory and latency for production LLM serving. However, the importance scores are computed once offline and are task-agnostic (tuned for contextual QA), limiting generalization to other task types; the authors themselves flag task-specific score estimation (e.g., via gradients) as future work. An open question is whether the head-level allocation advantage persists at larger model scales (70B+) or with multi-GPU parallelism, where per-head synchronization overhead could erode the efficiency gains. The slight exceedance of FullKV at KV=1024 (32.95 vs. 32.90) hints that aggressive compression can act as a regularizer, but this is a single-model observation not yet validated broadly.
