## Problem

Existing 3D molecule generation methods face a dual bottleneck: diffusion-based models require ~1000 sampling steps, and flow-matching methods like EquiFM transport atom coordinates and features along separate, non-unified probability paths that do not guarantee straight trajectories for all modalities. Moreover, none of these methods identify the optimal distribution coupling between the base and target distributions, resulting in suboptimal transport costs and slower generation. The paper addresses both the multi-modal transport cost measurement and the optimal coupling identification problem for geometric distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise z_0 = ⟨z_{x,0} ∈ R^{N×3}, z_{h,0} ∈ R^{N×k}⟩ sampled from a base distribution in a latent space, optionally conditioned on a molecular property s, and produces a 3D molecule geometry g_1 = ⟨x, h⟩ with N atom coordinates in R^{N×3} and categorical features (atom types, charges) in R^{N×d}. All molecules are constrained to the zero center-of-mass space, and the latent dimensionality k is set to 2 for QM9 and 1 for GEOM-DRUG. Generation is performed by solving an ODE with the Dopri5 adaptive-step solver, requiring only 90 steps on QM9 versus 200 for EquiFM.

## Method

GOAT formulates a geometric optimal transport problem with a unified cost c_g that jointly measures coordinate and feature distances, solved in a joint equivariant latent space via an equivariant autoencoder (EGNN-based). Optimal molecule transport is achieved by computing a unified atom-level permutation via the Hungarian algorithm and an optimal rotation via the Kabsch algorithm, yielding a straight-line transport path z_t = t·ẑ_1 + (1−t)·z_0. To obtain the optimal distribution coupling, GOAT trains an initial flow model, samples noise-to-molecule pairs through the learned ODE, purifies the coupling by filtering out invalid molecules via RdKit, and retrains the flow on the purified pairs. The paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that this estimation-and-purification procedure yields a coupling with non-increasing geometric transport cost, guaranteeing Pareto descent and enabling faster generation with improved novelty and significance.

## Results

On QM9, GOAT achieves the best novelty (78.6%) and significance (72.3%) among all methods, while requiring only 90 sampling steps (vs. 200 for EquiFM and 1000 for diffusion-based methods). The S-Time metric (seconds per significant molecule) is 0.12 for GOAT, roughly 3× faster than EquiFM (0.37) and 15× faster than GeoLDM (1.86). On GEOM-DRUG, GOAT achieves 96.2% validity with 90 steps and 0.94 S-Time, compared to EquiFM's 98.9% validity at 200 steps and 2.02 S-Time. The geometric transport cost is reduced by ~89.65% relative to EquiFM's hybrid transport.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOAT outperforms all baselines on novelty and significance on QM9: 78.6% novelty vs. 70.3% (GeoBFN, the closest competitor) and 57.4% (EquiFM), a 37% relative improvement over EquiFM. On efficiency, GOAT requires only 90 ODE steps versus 200 for EquiFM and 1000 for all diffusion-based methods (EDM, GeoLDM, GDM-Aug, EDM-Bridge), yielding the lowest S-Time of 0.12 s. GOAT trails EquiFM on validity (92.9% vs. 94.7%) and GeoLDM (93.8%), and on GEOM-DRUG it falls short of EquiFM (96.2% vs. 98.9%) and GeoLDM (99.3%) in validity, though it still halves the sampling steps. GeoBFN is the closest efficiency competitor (100 steps, 0.16 S-Time) but lags GOAT by ~8% in novelty and ~8.9% in significance. The equivariant autoencoder + joint latent transport is the key differentiator: ablation shows removing ODT drops novelty from 78.6% to 70.4% and increases steps from 90 to 100, while removing OMT drops validity to 85.0% and increases steps to 170.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOAT extends the equivariant flow-matching paradigm for 3D molecule generation initiated by EquiFM (Song et al., 2023a), which transports atom coordinates via optimal transport but atom features via a separate variance-preserving path. The key departure is unifying both modalities into a single equivariant latent space via an equivariant autoencoder, enabling a joint straight-line transport trajectory rather than a hybrid one. Additionally, GOAT adapts the rectified-flow coupling estimation and purification strategy from Liu et al. (2022) to the geometric molecular domain, where variable atom counts and SE(3) symmetries make direct distribution similarity computation intractable. The geometric optimal transport formulation incorporating optimal rotation (Kabsch), translation (zero CoM), and permutation (Hungarian) as a bilevel optimization is a new formalization not present in prior flow-matching or diffusion-based molecule generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× reduction in sampling steps (90 vs. 200 for EquiFM) combined with a ~37% relative improvement in novelty demonstrates that solving the full geometric OT problem—rather than a hybrid approximation—yields simultaneous gains in both speed and generation quality, which is practically significant for large-scale in-silico screening. The theoretical guarantee of non-increasing transport cost (Theorem 3.1) provides a principled foundation absent in prior flow-matching molecule generators. Open questions include whether the coupling purification step (which requires an additional training phase and RdKit-based filtering) can be made more efficient, whether distillation techniques can further reduce the 90-step requirement, and whether the approach generalizes to even larger molecular systems beyond GEOM-DRUG's 181-atom maximum.
