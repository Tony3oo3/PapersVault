## Problem

CTC is the simplest and most computationally efficient end-to-end ASR method, yet it significantly lags behind transducer and CTC/AED systems in recognition accuracy, limiting its practical deployment. This paper addresses that gap by showing that a simple consistency regularization loss on CTC distributions can close the performance gap without adding any architectural complexity or auxiliary decoding heads.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250963/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 80-dimensional log mel-spectrograms (25 ms window, 10 ms shift) and produces per-frame token distributions over 500-class BPE word pieces (English) or 4336-class characters (Mandarin) at a 25 Hz frame rate. Unusually, each training step processes two independently SpecAugmented views of the same utterance through a single shared encoder, and the final output is decoded via prefix search (beam size 4) or greedy search over the CTC lattice.

## Method

CR-CTC generates two augmented views of the input mel-spectrogram by independently applying SpecAugment (with time masking increased 2.5×), passes both through a shared Zipformer encoder, and minimizes a total loss that combines two standard CTC losses with a bidirectional KL-divergence consistency term (α = 0.2) between the per-frame distributions of the two branches. The paper identifies three complementary mechanisms: (1) self-distillation between dropout/stochastic-depth-induced sub-models receiving different augmented inputs, (2) masked prediction at time-masked positions that forces contextual representation learning, and (3) suppression of extremely peaky CTC distributions that reduces overfitting and improves generalization. A stop-gradient operation is applied to the target distributions in the KL term to prevent degenerate solutions, and the approach can also be used as an auxiliary loss to further improve CTC/AED and pruned transducer systems during joint training.

## Results

On LibriSpeech, CR-CTC with Zipformer-M achieves 2.10%/4.61% WER (test-clean/test-other), substantially outperforming the CTC baseline (2.52%/6.02%) and matching or exceeding CTC/AED (2.22%/4.97%) and pruned transducer (2.21%/4.79%) at the same scale. At Zipformer-L, CR-CTC reaches 2.02%/4.35%, and when combined with pruned transducer for joint training, achieves a new state-of-the-art of 1.88%/3.95%, surpassing Conformer-L transducer (2.1%/4.3%) and Stateformer 25L (1.91%/4.36%). On Aishell-1, CR-CTC with Zipformer-S (3.90%/4.12%) outperforms CTC/AED with the larger Zipformer-M (4.0%/4.32%). On GigaSpeech, CR-CTC with Zipformer-XL achieves 10.15%/10.28%, and CR-CTC/AED with Zipformer-XL reaches 9.92%/10.07%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CR-CTC consistently and substantially outperforms vanilla CTC across all three datasets and all Zipformer scales (e.g., 0.42%/1.41% WER reduction on LibriSpeech test-clean/test-other at Zipformer-M). Against CTC/AED, CR-CTC achieves lower WER at Zipformer-M and L on LibriSpeech (2.10/4.61 vs. 2.22/4.97 and 2.02/4.35 vs. 2.09/4.59) while using fewer parameters (64.3M vs. 90.0M and 147.0M vs. 174.3M). Against pruned transducer, CR-CTC wins at Zipformer-M (2.10/4.61 vs. 2.21/4.79) and is comparable at Zipformer-L (2.02/4.35 vs. 2.00/4.38). On Aishell-1, CR-CTC with Zipformer-S (3.90/4.12) surpasses CTC/AED with the larger Zipformer-M (4.0/4.32) and all pruned transducer variants. When used as an auxiliary loss for joint training, CR-CTC further boosts pruned transducer to 1.88/3.95 on LibriSpeech, setting a new state-of-the-art that exceeds Conformer-L transducer (2.1/4.3) and Stateformer 25L (1.91/4.36).

## Relation to Prior Work

CR-CTC extends the consistency regularization paradigm from R-Drop and cosub (which enforce KL-divergence between predictions of randomly sampled sub-models in NLP and vision) to CTC-based speech recognition, with two key departures: it uses different augmented input views rather than identical inputs, and it deliberately increases time masking to amplify a masked-prediction behavior analogous to self-supervised methods like wav2vec 2.0 and HuBERT. The paper also draws on the observation that CTC produces extremely peaky distributions (noted in forced-alignment and distillation literature) but is the first to show that suppressing these peaks via consistency regularization improves generalization. Conceptually, it unifies self-distillation, masked prediction, and peak suppression as three complementary mechanisms of a single auxiliary loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CTC is the most computationally efficient end-to-end ASR architecture and the default choice for production deployment, yet it has consistently lagged behind transducer and CTC/AED systems; closing this gap with a simple regularization term (requiring only two forward passes and a KL-divergence loss) has direct practical impact. The mechanistic decomposition—showing that the gains come from self-distillation between dropout sub-models, contextual learning via masked prediction, and peak suppression—provides actionable understanding for designing future CTC training objectives. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other sequence-to-sequence tasks (e.g., machine translation, TTS), whether the 2.5× time-masking ratio generalizes across languages and noise conditions, and whether the peak-suppression insight can be exploited in decoding or distillation pipelines beyond training.
