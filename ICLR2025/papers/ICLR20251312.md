## Problem

Existing safe RL benchmarks either rely on simplistic 2D grid worlds, focus on proprioceptive robotic manipulation, or limit vision-based 3D tasks to basic collision-avoidance navigation. No benchmark has provided diverse, complex 3D environments that require strategic decision-making, spatial reasoning, and short-term prediction under safety constraints while using egocentric pixel-based perception. HASARD addresses this gap by introducing a suite of six stochastic environments that demand comprehensive safety strategies well beyond mere navigation.

## Input / Output

The agent perceives the environment through a 160×120 pixel 8-bit RGB first-person frame with a 90-degree horizontal field of view, plus a HUD strip at the bottom displaying armor, weapons, keys, ammo, and health. The partially observable state space arises from the limited FOV, meaning the agent cannot see all relevant objects simultaneously. The output is a discrete action selected from a per-environment simplified action space (6–54 actions, e.g., MOVE_FORWARD, TURN_LEFT, JUMP, LOOK_UP) or the full ViZDoom space of 864 discrete actions combined with 2 continuous aiming channels.

## Method

HASARD is built on the ViZDoom platform integrated with Sample-Factory, enabling up to 50,000 environment iterations per second on standard hardware. Each of the six environments is formulated as a CMDP with a single safety constraint and adjustable cost budget, and features three difficulty levels that introduce novel mechanics (e.g., moving platforms, darkness cycles, decoy items, acid pits) rather than merely scaling parameters. The evaluation uses a CNN encoder (Mnih et al. 2015 architecture) feeding into two 512-neuron dense layers and a 512-unit GRU, splitting into actor and critic heads, trained with PPO and its safe variants (PPO-Lag, PPO-PID, PPO-Saute, P3O, PPOCost) over 500M steps with 5 seeds. A key finding is that PPO-PID consistently meets safety constraints while achieving the highest rewards, and that incrementally training across difficulty levels acts as an implicit curriculum, yielding up to 3× reward improvements over direct training on the hardest level.

## Results

Across six HASARD environments at Level 1 (simplified action space, 500M steps, 5 seeds), PPOPID consistently satisfies the safety threshold while achieving the highest reward among compliant methods in 5 of 6 environments (e.g., 43.27 reward / 5.03 cost in Collateral Damage vs. PPOLag's 29.09 / 5.61; 231.53 / 43.91 in Precipice Plunge vs. PPOLag's 147.24 / 44.96). PPO, the unsafe baseline, sets upper bounds on both reward and cost (e.g., 243.42 reward but 475.62 cost in Precipice Plunge). P3O achieves the highest rewards among safe methods in some environments (e.g., 46.52 in Collateral Damage) but exceeds the cost threshold in several settings (e.g., 176.41 cost in Precipice Plunge vs. a budget of ~50). Performance degrades sharply at Level 3, where PPOPID drops to 54.02 reward in Precipice Plunge and PPOCost collapses to 0.04 in Armament Burden.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) serves as the unsafe upper bound, achieving the highest rewards in all environments but incurring costs 2–10× above the safety threshold (e.g., 475.62 cost in Precipice Plunge vs. a budget of ~50). PPOCost (costs as negative rewards) can achieve near-zero cost in some environments (0.69 in Precipice Plunge) but is highly sensitive to the cost scaling factor and provides no general guarantee of constraint satisfaction. PPOLag (Ray et al., 2019) consistently adheres to safety thresholds but yields the lowest rewards among compliant methods in most environments, with the Lagrangian multiplier's continuous adjustment causing fluctuations that prevent consistent constraint satisfaction. PPOSaute (Sootla et al., 2022) exceeds the cost threshold in several environments (e.g., 424.35 in Precipice Plunge, 62.00 in Volcanic Venture) and achieves the lowest rewards overall. P3O (Zhang et al., 2022) achieves the highest rewards among safe methods in Collateral Damage (46.52) and Detonator's Dilemma (29.62) but violates the cost budget in Precipice Plunge (176.41) and Volcanic Venture (46.10). PPOPID (Stooke et al., 2020) is the most effective method on HASARD, consistently meeting constraints while outperforming all other safe baselines in 5 of 6 environments at Level 1, with the largest margin in Precipice Plunge (231.53 vs. 147.24 for PPOLag, a 57% improvement).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the ViZDoom platform (originally designed for general visual RL) and the Sample-Factory training framework to construct the first safe RL benchmark exclusively targeting egocentric vision-based learning in complex 3D environments. It departs from prior safe RL benchmarks (AI Safety Gridworlds, Safety Gym, Safety Gymnasium, Safe-Control-Gym) that rely on 2D toy problems, proprioceptive robotic manipulation, or computationally expensive physics engines, and from vision-based 3D benchmarks (CARLA, MetaDrive) that focus on navigation or driving. The closest prior work, Safety Vision, uses MuJoCo but does not leverage physics for task depth and reduces to 2D navigation, whereas HASARD introduces diverse task types (weapon delivery, target elimination, barrel detonation, cave descent) requiring strategic reasoning, spatial awareness, and short-term prediction beyond collision avoidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HASARD provides a cost-effective (50K env iterations/sec) and open-sourced platform for evaluating safe RL methods under realistic perceptual challenges, revealing that current state-of-the-art methods (PPO-Lag, PPO-PID, PPO-Saute, P3O) struggle to balance reward maximization with safety constraints in complex 3D vision tasks. The implicit curriculum finding—nearly 3× reward improvement in Remedy Rush when training sequentially across difficulty levels—opens a promising but underexplored direction for integrating curriculum learning with safe RL. Key limitations include the ViZDoom engine's simplified physics, discrete actions, single safety constraint per environment, and lack of multi-agent or multi-objective scenarios, all of which constrain direct transfer to real-world safety-critical applications like autonomous driving or robotics.
