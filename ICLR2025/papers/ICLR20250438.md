## Problem

Latent diffusion models train the diffusion process entirely in the autoencoder's latent space with an L2 objective, while the AE decoder is never involved in the training loop. This creates a disconnect: the diffusion model receives no feedback on whether its generated latents will decode to high-quality images, and the irregular latent space means L2 distance in latent space does not correspond to perceptual quality in pixel space. The result is generated images that lack high-frequency detail and appear blurry.

## Input / Output

The method conditions on text prompts (CC12M, S320M) or class labels (ImageNet-1k) and produces 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images via a 796M-parameter DiT denoiser operating in a 4-channel latent space with 8× spatial downsampling. The LPL is applied only during a post-training phase (200k iterations at 256, 120k at 512) and only at timesteps where the noise level σ_t falls below a resolution-scaled threshold τ_σ, meaning it activates exclusively at high signal-to-noise ratios near the clean end of the diffusion trajectory.

## Method

The Latent Perceptual Loss (LPL) decodes both the ground-truth latent z_0 and the model's predicted clean latent ẑ_0 through the frozen AE decoder, extracts intermediate feature maps at L decoder depths, and computes a weighted sum of per-channel L2 distances between the corresponding features after cross-normalization (using the predicted latent's statistics for both tensors to avoid spurious gradients). Depth-specific weights (inverse of the upscaling factor relative to the first layer) balance contributions across resolutions, and a binary outlier-detection mask suppresses unreliable feature regions. The total training objective is L_Diff + w_LPL · L_LPL with w_LPL ≈ 3.0, and the formulation generalizes seamlessly to DDPM-ε, DDPM-v, and Flow-OT by only requiring a closed-form estimate of the clean latent from the model's prediction at each timestep.

## Results

The latent perceptual loss (LPL) consistently improves FID across all three datasets and both resolutions, with boosts ranging from 6% to 20%. On ImageNet-1k at 512 resolution, FID drops from 4.88 to 3.79 (a 22% relative improvement), and on CC12M at 512 resolution, FID drops from 8.79 to 7.27 (a 17% relative improvement). CLIPScore also improves on CC12M (25.06→25.12 at 256, 24.88→25.12 at 512). When applied to flow matching (Flow-OT) on ImageNet-1k 512, LPL achieves the best FID of 3.61, outperforming both DDPM-ε (3.79) and DDPM-v (3.84) with LPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods; the baseline is the identical LDM (same architecture, data, and training schedule) trained without LPL. LPL wins on every metric across all datasets and resolutions. The largest absolute FID gain is 1.59 points on ImageNet-1k 512 (4.88→3.79), followed by 1.52 points on CC12M 512 (8.79→7.27). In the multi-paradigm comparison (Table 3), LPL with Flow-OT achieves the best overall FID (3.61) and also improves density (1.14→1.29), precision (0.75→0.79), and recall (0.52→0.54) over the Flow-OT baseline. Notably, DDPM-ε with LPL (FID 3.79) matches or exceeds Flow-OT without LPL (FID 4.54), indicating that LPL provides a boost comparable to switching training paradigms. The only metric where LPL shows a slight degradation is density under DDPM training (1.14→1.13 for DDPM-ε), while all other distributional metrics (coverage, precision, recall) improve consistently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the perceptual loss paradigm (LPIPS, Johnson et al. 2016; Zhang et al. 2018) from pixel-space image-to-image tasks into the latent diffusion training loop, but with a key departure: instead of using an external pretrained classifier (e.g., Inception) for feature extraction, it leverages the intermediate features of the autoencoder's own decoder, which naturally maps from latent to pixel space. This distinguishes it from Kang et al. (2024), who used a separate classification network over latents for distillation, and from Lin & Yang (2024), whose self-perceptual loss is restricted to UNet denoisers and conflicts with classifier-free guidance. The method is also orthogonal to prior approaches that address the latent-pixel quality gap via cascaded refiners (Podell et al., 2024) or curated high-quality data (Dai et al., 2023), since LPL adds no inference cost and requires no specialized training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identified decoder-diffusion disconnect is a fundamental structural issue in all latent generative models, and LPL offers a drop-in fix that improves FID by 6–20% across datasets, resolutions, and training paradigms (DDPM-ε, DDPM-v, Flow-OT) without altering the architecture or inference pipeline. The practical caveat is the memory overhead from backpropagating through the frozen decoder, which limits batch size or requires gradient checkpointing, and the loss is only applied at high-SNR timesteps, leaving open whether a more principled SNR-dependent weighting could further improve results. Broader open questions include whether the approach transfers to video latent diffusion, to autoencoders with different compression ratios, and whether the depth-specific weighting and outlier-detection heuristics generalize beyond the specific asymmetric VQGAN used in the main experiments.
