## Problem

Diffusion-based planners (diffusers) are widely used in safety-critical robotic tasks but lack any formal safety guarantees; generated trajectories can violate obstacle constraints or joint limits without detection. Existing safety enforcement approaches such as truncation or classifier-based guidance either fail on complex (non-convex) specifications or degrade generative performance, and control barrier functions (CBFs) have never been adapted to the diffusion time domain where the generated data is not directly coupled to robot dynamics.

## Input / Output

At each receding-horizon control step, the method consumes the current robot state, task objectives (e.g., start/goal positions for maze, object locations for manipulation), and a set of differentiable safety specifications b(τ_k) ≥ 0 defined over the planning horizon H. It outputs a noise-free planning trajectory τ = (τ_0, …, τ_H) that is guaranteed to satisfy all specifications at every planning time step, from which a one-step control action is extracted before the diffusion procedure is re-run. The safety specifications must be continuously differentiable functions of the trajectory states, and the method operates on normalized state representations matching the diffusion model's latent space.

## Method

The key insight is to reformulate the denoising diffusion dynamics as a controllable system in diffusion time, where the control is a perturbation Δν^j added to the natural denoising step, and then enforce a novel notion of finite-time diffusion invariance via CBFs applied along diffusion time rather than planning time. Three safe diffuser variants are proposed: RoS (robust-safe) uses a constant robust term γ to guarantee convergence to the safe set within N diffusion steps; ReS (relaxed-safe) introduces relaxation variables with time-varying weights that soften constraints early in denoising and harden them near the end to avoid local traps; TVS (time-varying-safe) directly modifies the specification with a time-varying function σ_k(j) that starts permissive and tightens to the original constraint. Each variant is enforced at every diffusion step by solving a minimum-deviation quadratic program that minimizes the perturbation from the natural diffusion dynamics while satisfying the CBF inequality, with a closed-form solution available when restricting to the two most-violating constraints.

## Results

On maze planning (maze2d-large-v1), all three SafeDiffusers (RoS, ReS, TVS) achieve S-SPEC ≥ 0 and C-SPEC ≥ 0, guaranteeing safety for both simple and complex specifications, while the vanilla Diffuser violates both (S-SPEC = -0.983, C-SPEC = -0.894). The SafeDiffusers preserve generative performance with scores of 1.519–1.557 (vs. 1.598 for the Diffuser) and comparable NLL (4.434–4.619 vs. 4.501). The closed-form (CF) variants reduce per-step computation to 0.007s (vs. 0.106s for full QP), and ReS/TVS reduce the local-trap rate from 100% (RoS) to 36–48% (trap rate ≥1) and 16–21% (trap rate ≥2). On robot locomotion and manipulation, RoS-Diffuser similarly guarantees safety (S-SPEC = 0.000, C-SPEC = 0.010) while maintaining competitive scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The vanilla Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) provides no safety guarantees and violates both simple and complex specifications (S-SPEC = -0.983, C-SPEC = -0.894), while all SafeDiffusers achieve S-SPEC ≥ 0 and C-SPEC ≥ 0 with scores within ~5% of the Diffuser (1.519–1.557 vs. 1.598). Truncation (Brockman et al., 2016) can satisfy simple specifications (S-SPEC ≈ 0) but completely fails on complex ones (C-SPEC = -0.759), and does not provide formal guarantees. Classifier-based guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) fails to satisfy either specification (S-SPEC = -0.789, C-SPEC = -0.979) and severely degrades planning performance (score drops from 1.598 to 0.384, NLL increases from 4.501 to 6.962), because applying gradients at all times disrupts the diffusion process. Invariant Neural ODE (Xiao et al., 2023b) guarantees safety (S-SPEC = 14.0, C-SPEC ≈ 0) but fails catastrophically in closed-loop control (score = -0.025), making it impractical for long-horizon planning. Among the proposed variants, ReS-Diffuser-CF and TVS-Diffuser-CF offer the best trade-off: they guarantee safety, reduce trap rates to 36–48%, maintain scores near the Diffuser baseline, and run at 0.007s per step (comparable to the vanilla Diffuser's 0.006s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two well-developed but previously disconnected lines of work: diffusion-based trajectory planning (Janner et al., 2022) and control barrier functions (CBFs) for safety guarantees in control theory (Ames et al., 2017; Xiao & Belta, 2019). The key conceptual departure is applying CBFs along diffusion dynamics (the denoising time axis) rather than along robot/planning dynamics, which requires a new notion of finite-time diffusion invariance since the initial Gaussian noise almost certainly violates safety constraints. Prior attempts to add safety to diffusion models—classifier-based guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) and truncation (Brockman et al., 2016)—provide no formal guarantees, while the closely related invariant neural ODE work (Xiao et al., 2023b) applies CBFs to a different generative architecture and fails in closed-loop control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide formal safety guarantees for diffusion-based planners, which is critical given their increasing use in safety-critical robotic applications. The identification of the local trap problem—a phenomenon where CBF constraints cause trajectory points to stagnate at obstacle boundaries during denoising—is a genuinely new challenge specific to the diffusion setting, and the three proposed variants (RoS, ReS, TVS) offer a practical menu of trade-offs. Key limitations remain: specifications must be differentiable and known a priori, computation time (solving QPs at each diffusion step) is still too high for real-time deployment on physical robots, and the diffusion dynamics are estimated rather than exact. Open questions include learning specifications from trajectory data, incorporating stochastic differential equation formulations to handle estimation errors, and reducing inference cost for online replanning.
