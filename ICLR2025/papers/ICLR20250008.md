## Problem

Existing fairness benchmarks for LLMs focus almost exclusively on single-turn dialogues, overlooking the more realistic and challenging multi-turn scenarios where bias can accumulate across turns and contextual complexity (pronouns, scattered information, misleading instructions) causes models that appear fair in isolation to generate biased outputs. No comprehensive benchmark existed to systematically evaluate LLM fairness across the full spectrum of multi-turn interaction challenges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250008/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 5-turn multi-turn dialogue prompts as input, where each dialogue is constructed from a source biased statement (stereotype or toxicity) targeting one of six attributes (gender, race, religion, age, disability, appearance). The LLM under evaluation processes all five turns sequentially, with prior turns' outputs fed as dialogue history. The output is a binary bias judgment on the final turn's response, aggregated into a bias ratio per task, per model, and per bias attribute. A distilled FairMT-1K subset (170 samples per task) enables efficient evaluation of up to 15 models at roughly 72.5 H100 GPU hours per model on the full 10K set.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical task taxonomy spanning three stages of multi-turn interaction: Context Understanding (Anaphora Ellipsis, Scattered Questions), Interaction Fairness (Jailbreak Tips, Interference from Misinformation), and Fairness Trade-off (Negative Feedback, Fixed Format). Multi-turn dialogues are generated by GPT-4 using task-specific templates that transform source biased statements from RedditBias, SBIC, and HateXplain into 5-turn conversations where bias is progressively embedded through pronoun references, scattered information, jailbreak tactics, or format conditioning. Evaluation employs GPT-4 as the primary judge (supplied with full dialogue history and the original biased statement to anchor judgment), supplemented by Llama-Guard-3-8B as an open-source auxiliary classifier and human annotators for validation (achieving 93.97% F1 agreement with GPT-4). The key empirical finding is that bias accumulates across turns and models exhibit complementary weaknesses: those strong at comprehension tasks (e.g., Mistral) are vulnerable to bias-resistance tasks, and vice versa (e.g., Llama-2), with no model achieving consistently strong fairness across all six tasks.

## Results

On the challenging FairMT-1K dataset (15 SOTA LLMs), even the best-performing model (Gemma-2-27b-it) exhibits a 25.35% average bias ratio, while GPT-4 achieves 56.67% and Mistral-7B-Instruct reaches 83.43%. On the larger FairMT-10K dataset (6 models), average bias ratios range from 11.38% (Gemma-7b-it, stereotype) to 22.29% (Llama-3.1-8b-it, stereotype). Multi-turn dialogues consistently increase bias ratios compared to single-turn for most models, with the largest increases observed in Scattered Questions, Anaphora Ellipsis, and Jailbreak Tips tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 15 LLMs on FairMT-1K, revealing that no model achieves consistently strong fairness across all six tasks. Gemma-2-27b-it is the overall best performer (25.35% avg. bias), excelling particularly in Negative Feedback (9.70%) and Fixed Format (6.67%), yet still shows 61.82% bias on Interference from Misinformation. GPT-4, despite being the most capable general-purpose LLM, achieves only 56.67% average bias, with a striking 90.91% failure rate on Negative Feedback. Mistral-7B-Instruct performs worst overall (83.43%), struggling across nearly all tasks including Anaphora Ellipsis (95.15%) and Jailbreak Tips (96.36%). Llama-2 models show a distinctive pattern: they are relatively resistant to user-instruction interference (e.g., Llama-2-13b-chat at 33.33% on Jailbreak Tips) but fail on comprehension tasks like Scattered Questions (86.06%). The paper also demonstrates that multi-turn contexts increase bias ratios by 5–10% over single-turn for most models, with Llama-2-7b-chat and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct showing the largest degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-turn LLM fairness evaluation line (BBQ, CEB, TrustLLM, BiasAsker) into the multi-turn dialogue setting, drawing on multi-turn safety and jailbreak literature (CoSafe, S-Eval, CoA framework) for task design. It repurposes established bias datasets (RedditBias, SBIC, HateXplain) as source material but restructures them into six multi-turn dialogue tasks organized by a novel taxonomy spanning context understanding, interaction fairness, and fairness trade-offs. The evaluation methodology follows the LLM-as-judge paradigm (GPT-4 + Llama-Guard-3 + human validation) established in MT-Bench, while the DPO alignment experiment connects to the broader fairness-alignment literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-turn dialogue is the dominant real-world deployment mode for LLMs, yet prior fairness benchmarks have almost exclusively evaluated single-turn interactions, leaving a critical gap in understanding how bias accumulates and manifests across conversational turns. The DPO alignment experiment reveals a significant open trade-off: fairness alignment substantially degrades multi-turn dialogue capabilities (Interactivity drops from 5.17 to 2.60) while increasing over-refusal, suggesting that current alignment paradigms cannot simultaneously achieve fairness and utility in multi-turn settings. Open questions include how to design alignment data and techniques that preserve multi-turn competence, how to handle intersectional and less-represented bias attributes (age, disability, appearance) where all models underperform, and whether the benchmark's English-only, GPT-4-judged evaluation generalizes across languages and model families.
