## Problem

Metal-organic framework (MOF) crystal structure prediction remains intractable for both conventional DFT-based optimization and existing deep generative models, because MOF unit cells contain hundreds to thousands of atoms (up to 2,200), far exceeding the ~52-atom scale of general crystal structure prediction. No prior deep generative model has exploited the modular building-block decomposition of MOFs to reduce this search space, leaving a gap between the complexity of MOF structures and the capabilities of available prediction methods.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of M building blocks (metal nodes and organic linkers), each specified by atom types and canonicalized local coordinates (centroid-subtracted and PCA-aligned to be invariant to initial pose). It outputs the SE(3) roto-translation (q_m, τ_m) for each block and the 6-parameter lattice (a, b, c, α, β, γ), which together reconstruct the full atomic structure via rigid-body composition. This reduces the generative dimensionality from 3N atomic coordinates to 6M block-level degrees of freedom, where M is typically an order of magnitude smaller than N.

## Method

MOFFLOW formulates MOF structure prediction as Riemannian flow matching on the product manifold SE(3)^M × R^6, learning a continuous normalizing flow that transports a simple prior (uniform on SO(3), Gaussian on R^3, log-normal/uniform on lattice) to the target distribution of block roto-translations and lattice parameters. The conditional flow uses geodesic interpolation on SO(3) for rotations and linear interpolation for translations and lattice, with the network trained to predict clean data (q₁, τ₁, ℓ₁) from a noisy intermediate state via a reparameterized regression loss. The architecture is a two-level hierarchy: atom-level GNN layers encode each building block into an embedding, followed by block-level update layers that iteratively refine roto-translations, pairwise features, and lattice using a novel MOFAttention module (a modified invariant point attention that incorporates lattice parameters as an attention offset) and pre-layer-normalized Transformers. SE(3) invariance is enforced through rotation-invariant lattice representation, mean-free translation coordinates, and equivariant message passing, enabling the model to handle structures up to 2,200 atoms per unit cell.

## Results

On the MOF dataset (Boyd et al., 2019) with 30,880 test structures, MOFFLOW achieves a match rate of 31.69% at stol=0.5 with a single sample (RMSE 0.2820), and 44.75% with 5 samples (RMSE 0.2694). At the more lenient stol=1.0 threshold, MOFFLOW reaches 87.46% (1 sample) and 100% (5 samples). Inference time is 1.94 s per structure (1 sample) and 5.69 s (5 samples), substantially faster than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOFFLOW decisively outperforms all three baseline categories. Against the optimization-based CRYSPY methods (Random Search and Evolutionary Algorithm), both of which yield 0% match rate on MOF structures due to the intractable search space of hundreds of atoms per unit cell, MOFFLOW achieves 31.69% MR at stol=0.5 while being 171× faster than RS (1.94 s vs. 332 s) and 1009× faster than EA (1.94 s vs. 1959 s). Against DiffCSP, the state-of-the-art equivariant diffusion model for general crystal structure prediction, MOFFLOW improves match rate from 0.09% to 31.69% (351×) at stol=0.5 with a single sample, and from 0.34% to 44.75% with 5 samples, while also being 2.8× faster (1.94 s vs. 5.37 s). Against the self-assembly algorithm from Fu et al. (2023), which only predicts rotations and requires MOFFLOW's predicted translations and lattice as input, MOFFLOW alone achieves a higher match rate (31.69% vs. 30.04%), lower RMSE (0.2820 vs. 0.3084), and 2.4× faster inference (1.94 s vs. 4.75 s), demonstrating that learned roto-translation prediction surpasses heuristic overlap-based optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOFFLOW extends Riemannian flow matching (Chen & Lipman, 2024) to the SE(3)^M × R^6 space of building-block roto-translations and lattice parameters, drawing architectural patterns from SE(3) flow matching for protein backbones (Yim et al., 2023b) and adapting AlphaFold's invariant point attention (Jumper et al., 2021) into a MOFAttention module that incorporates lattice parameters. The key conceptual departure from general CSP methods (DiffCSP, FlowMM) is the blockwise rigid-body representation, which reduces the generative dimensionality from 3N atomic coordinates to 6M block roto-translations by exploiting the modular structure of MOFs—analogous to how torsional diffusion (Jing et al., 2022) eliminated redundant degrees of freedom in molecular conformer generation. Lattice priors and the Niggli-reduced parameterization follow Miller et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first deep generative model that can predict MOF structures at the scale of hundreds to thousands of atoms per unit cell, a regime where both DFT-based optimization and atom-level generative models completely fail. The rigid-body assumption and blockwise representation are natural for MOFs but introduce a limitation: the method requires known local building-block geometries, which may be unavailable for rare or novel linkers. Open questions include evaluation on experimentally-synthesized MOF databases, handling flexible or partially-unknown building blocks, and incorporating periodic invariance explicitly. The techniques (blockwise Riemannian FM, MOFAttention) may generalize to other modular crystalline materials such as covalent organic frameworks.
