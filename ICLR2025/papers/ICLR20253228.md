## Problem

GFlowNets, like RL agents, struggle to discover high-reward modes when rewards are sparse, low-valued, or separated by long low-reward trajectories, yet prior exploration methods for GFlowNets were limited to small objects, trivially easy reward settings, or extremely sparse zero-reward configurations. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a general-purpose exploration framework that works across diverse task structures, non-zero difficult-to-explore reward densities, and a wide range of trajectory lengths and action-space dimensionalities.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a directed acyclic graph (DAG) state space with a terminal reward function R(x) and an initial state s_0, and produces a stochastic forward policy that generates objects x with probability proportional to R(x). A key structural detail is that the Sibling Network consumes a composite reward combining extrinsic and RND-based intrinsic signals (Eq. 2), while the Behavior Network only ever sees true extrinsic rewards — exploratory trajectories from the Sibling are relabeled with their true terminal rewards before being fed into the Behavior Network's off-policy training loop. The framework is modality-agnostic, demonstrated on 2D-to-8D HyperGrids, 120-length bit sequences with vocabulary sizes up to 2^10, and small-molecule generation over a ~10^16 state space.

## Method

SA-GFN introduces a decoupled dual-network architecture consisting of a Sibling Network (exploratory policy) and a Behavior Network (main policy). The Sibling Network is trained with a composite reward that blends extrinsic rewards with RND-based intrinsic rewards, encouraging it to visit novel and hard-to-predict states. Its generated trajectories are then relabeled with true extrinsic rewards (analogous to Hindsight Experience Replay) and combined with on-policy trajectories from the Behavior Network, which is trained off-policy on this augmented dataset using any standard GFlowNet objective (TB, FM, DB, or SubTB). The critical design insight is that by isolating the non-stationary intrinsic reward signal entirely within the Sibling Network, the Behavior Network's target distribution remains fixed, avoiding the moving-target instability that arises when intrinsic rewards are injected directly into a single GFlowNet's training objective. This decoupling makes the framework composable: any GFlowNet training objective and any intrinsic reward method (RND, NovelD, etc.) can be swapped in without architectural changes.

## Results

SA-GFN consistently outperforms all baselines across four diverse domains. On the Sparse HyperGrid with zero rewards (H up to 128), SA-GFN achieves substantially lower L1 error between the empirical and true reward distribution and faster convergence than GAFN, GFN-TB, and single-network RND. On the harder low-reward HyperGrid (R₀=10⁻⁴ or 10⁻⁵), SA-GFN discovers all four modes of the distribution while other methods find only a subset, and this advantage persists in high-dimensional grids (d=6,8). On Bit Sequence generation (n=120, k∈{1,2,4,6,8,10}), SA-GFN finds the highest number of modes for every k, discovers modes faster (k=1), and achieves the highest Spearman correlation between sampling probability and reward. On sEH protein binder generation (state space ~10¹⁶), SA-GFN achieves the highest average reward for top-10 unique molecules and the lowest Tanimoto similarity, indicating both higher quality and greater diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA-GFN wins against all 11 baselines on every task and metric reported. Against GAFN (Pan et al., 2022) and GFN-TB (Malkin et al., 2022), SA-GFN achieves lower L1 error and discovers more modes on both zero-reward and low-reward HyperGrids, with the gap widening as grid size and dimensionality increase. Against single-network RND (Burda et al., 2018), the decoupled dual-network architecture of SA-GFN provides a clear advantage, confirming that disentangling exploration from learning is critical. On Bit Sequence generation, SA-GFN outperforms A2C with entropy regularization (Williams & Peng, 1991; Mnih & Gregor, 2014), Soft Actor-Critic (Christodoulou, 2019; Haarnoja et al., 2017), and MARS (Xie et al., 2021) in both mode count and Spearman correlation across all vocabulary sizes. On molecule generation, SA-GFN surpasses PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), PPO-RND (Burda et al., 2018), GFN-FM (Bengio et al., 2021a), GAFN, and MARS in both top-10 reward and Tanimoto diversity. Ablations further show that simpler exploration strategies (tempered logits, SAC without large replay buffers) are insufficient for sparse reward settings, while SA-GFN's advantage is robust to the choice of GFlowNet objective (TB, FM, DB) and intrinsic reward method (RND, NovelD).

## Relation to Prior Work

SA-GFN builds on the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021a) by introducing a decoupled dual-network architecture that separates an exploratory Sibling Network (driven by RND-based intrinsic rewards, Burda et al., 2018) from a main Behavior Network trained on true extrinsic rewards. The trajectory relabeling mechanism is adapted from Hindsight Experience Replay (Andrychowicz et al., 2017), leveraging GFlowNets' off-policy training capability to feed exploratory data into the behavior policy. This departs from prior GFlowNet exploration methods—GAFN (Pan et al., 2022) which augments a single network with intrinsic rewards, Thompson Sampling ensembles (Rector-Brooks et al., 2023), and DGFN (Lau et al., 2023)—by explicitly decoupling the nonstationary intrinsic reward signal from the fixed target distribution, avoiding the 'moving target' problem that plagues single-network approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first extensive evaluation of exploration in GFlowNets across diverse reward structures (zero-reward, low non-zero sparse, multi-mode), trajectory lengths, and action-space dimensionalities, extending prior benchmarks that were limited to extremely sparse zero-reward settings. The decoupling principle—keeping the behavior network's target distribution fixed while a separate sibling explores—provides a clean, general-purpose recipe compatible with any GFlowNet objective and intrinsic reward method, making it a practical tool for GFlowNet practitioners. Open questions include whether the dual-network overhead can be eliminated (e.g., via multi-headed architectures), whether multiple sibling networks further improve exploration, and how the approach scales to even larger state spaces beyond the ~10^16 molecules tested.
