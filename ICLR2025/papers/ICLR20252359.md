## Problem

Existing diffusion-based data augmentation for few-shot object detection relies on simple prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLASSNAME]') that produce only typical objects, ignoring fine-grained attributes like color, shape, and size. These methods also fail to generate hard samples near class boundaries and neglect foreground-background relationships, which are critical for object detection but absent in classification-oriented augmentation. As a result, synthetic datasets lack the representativeness needed to prevent overfitting in the few-shot regime.

## Input / Output

The method consumes K real novel-class images (K∈{1,2,3,5,10}) with bounding box annotations, the full base dataset with annotations, and class names fed to ChatGPT for attribute and fine-grained category extraction. Bounding boxes from randomly sampled base images serve as spatial masks for PowerPaint inpainting, and are randomly scaled by factors in {1.25, 1.5, 1.75, 2} to introduce multi-scale diversity. The output is 300 synthetic images per novel class with bounding box annotations (class label replaced by the novel class name), combined with the original few-shot samples to form the augmented fine-tuning dataset.

## Method

MPAD comprises three complementary modules operating on a PowerPaint controllable diffusion backbone. ICOS uses in-context learning with ChatGPT to extract attribute dictionaries (color, shape, parts) and fine-grained subcategories per novel class, then randomly samples key-value pairs to construct diverse prompts that replace the simple 'a photo of a [class]' template. HPAS implements a momentum-based scheduler (Eq. 3) that linearly interpolates between novel and base class CLIP prompt embeddings at each of the 80 diffusion timesteps, with a starting weight w=0.7 and momentum m=0.8, so that early steps emphasize the base class (low-level features like shape and color) while later steps shift toward the novel class (high-level semantic features), producing hard mixed-class samples. BAP selects backgrounds from base images using two criteria: hard backgrounds are chosen by maximum ViT cosine similarity to Stable Diffusion-synthesized novel-class images, and typical clutter backgrounds are chosen by maximum softmax entropy of ViT features, forcing the detector to learn robust foreground-background discrimination. The augmented dataset fine-tunes a DeFRCN detector in the standard two-stage FSOD protocol.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC (G-FSOD, nAP50), MPAD achieves a mean of 66.9% across three novel sets, exceeding the DeFRCN baseline (49.4%) by 17.5% on average and delivering a +31.0% gain in the 1-shot setting. On MS COCO, MPAD reaches 31.2% nAP50 in the 1-shot setting (vs. 9.5% for DeFRCN), improving nAP, nAP50, and nAP75 by over 13%, 21%, and 14% respectively. The method also outperforms traditional augmentation strategies (Cutout, GridMask, AutoAugment, CutMix) by a consistent +9% nAP50 margin on PASCAL VOC Novel Set 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPAD outperforms all 30 compared baselines on both PASCAL VOC and MS COCO. On PASCAL VOC, the closest competitor is Lin et al. (2023) at 61.6% mean nAP50, which MPAD surpasses by 5.3 points; other strong augmentation-based methods like Norm-VAE (59.3%), SFOT (59.3%), and MFD (59.0%) trail by 7–8 points. The DeFRCN baseline (49.4%) is exceeded by 17.5 points on average, with the gap widening to +31.0 points in the 1-shot regime. On MS COCO, MPAD's 31.2% nAP50 in 1-shot nearly doubles the best prior method (FS-DETR at 13.6%) and exceeds the DeFRCN baseline (9.5%) by over 21 points. Against traditional augmentation methods (Cutout 60.1%, GridMask 60.2%, AutoAugment 57.7%, CutMix 60.4% mean nAP50 on Novel Set 1), MPAD (69.4%) leads by a consistent ~9-point margin, confirming that diffusion-based multi-perspective synthesis is substantially more effective than geometric or stochastic perturbations for few-shot detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of diffusion-based data augmentation for few-shot object detection (Lin et al. 2023, SNIDA 2024), which typically relies on simple class-name prompts to generate synthetic novel objects. It departs from this by introducing a multi-perspective framework that explicitly models foreground-foreground relations (via LLM-driven prompt diversification with ICOS and harmonic prompt mixing with HPAS) and foreground-background relations (via entropy-based and similarity-based background selection with BAP). The generation backbone is PowerPaint, a controllable inpainting diffusion model, while the detector remains DeFRCN, positioning the contribution squarely as a data augmentation layer rather than a new detection architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a concrete and underexplored gap in FSOD: synthetic samples generated with simple prompts tend to be typical and lack the hard, boundary-defining examples that are critical for few-shot generalization. The 17.5% average nAP50 gain over DeFRCN (and +31% in 1-shot) is substantial and validated across three novel sets and two benchmarks. However, the authors acknowledge that diffusion hallucinations persist, the starting weight w is fixed across all classes, and the approach has only been validated on PASCAL VOC and MS COCO with a single detector backbone, leaving open whether the multi-perspective augmentation strategy transfers to other architectures or more challenging open-world settings.
