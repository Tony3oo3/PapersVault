## Problem

Deep spiking neural networks suffer from over-parameterization during training and inference, yet existing fully-sparse SNN training methods rely on static pruning ratios or predetermined sparsity levels, leading to under- or over-pruning. No prior approach dynamically adapts the pruning ratio based on the network's actual compressibility while maintaining sparse training from scratch, a capability essential for hardware-constrained neuromorphic deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253346/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spike-encoded RGB images (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32) or event streams (DVS-CIFAR10) processed through LIF neurons over T=2 or T=10 discrete time steps, and outputs a single class label via cross-entropy loss averaged across time steps. The network architectures (ResNet19, VGG-SNN) are initialized with Erdős-Rényi random graph connectivity at 50% density, and the output is a standard softmax classification over the dataset's class set.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage iterative sparse structure learning framework. In Stage I, the sparse SNN (initialized with Erdős-Rényi graph connectivity) undergoes standard LIF-based training with surrogate gradients, after which the PQ index—adapted for SNNs to account for spatiotemporal scaling invariance—is computed to quantify the compressibility of the current weight subnetwork and derive an adaptive rewiring ratio c_i. In Stage II, this ratio drives a dynamic connection rewiring step that prunes the weakest c_i fraction of synapses and regrows c_i fraction of dormant connections using a momentum-based growing rule, executed every Epoch_frequency iterations. The two stages iterate throughout training, so the network remains sparse from initialization while continuously self-adjusting its sparsity level, avoiding the under- or over-pruning pitfalls of fixed-ratio methods.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed two-stage sparse structure learning method (neuron-wise, ResNet19, T=2) achieves 92.48% top-1 accuracy with 40.58% connection density, representing a +1.18% improvement over the non-sparse baseline. On CIFAR-100, the layer-wise variant reaches 70.3% accuracy (+1.07% over non-sparse) at 29.48% connectivity, and on DVS-CIFAR10 it attains 78.4% (+0.08%) at 30% connectivity. Notably, the method maintains sparse training from scratch throughout, with connection density ranging from ~30% to ~41%, while all compared baselines show accuracy degradation relative to their non-sparse counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, the proposed method (92.48%, neuron-wise) slightly trails Grad R (92.54%) and STDS (92.49%) in raw accuracy but substantially outperforms ESLSNN (91.09%), UPR (92.05%), and ADMM (90.19%). The critical distinction is that the proposed method is the only one to *improve* accuracy over its non-sparse counterpart (+1.18%), whereas all five baselines show accuracy loss (ranging from −0.13% to −1.70%). On CIFAR-100, the proposed method (70.3%) falls short of ESLSNN (73.48%) by ~3.2% but edges out UPR (70.45%) and, more importantly, is the only method to report a positive accuracy delta (+1.07%). On DVS-CIFAR10, the proposed method (78.4%) is marginally below STDS (79.8%) but above ESLSNN (78.3%) and UPR (78.3%), again with a positive accuracy gain. Overall, the paper's central claim is not raw accuracy superiority but that sparse-from-scratch training with adaptive PQ-index-driven rewiring can match or exceed non-sparse performance while maintaining ~30–40% connectivity, a property none of the baselines achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the evolutionary structure learning line of work for SNNs (notably ESL-SNNs, Shen et al. 2023) by replacing the static pruning ratio with a dynamically computed one derived from the PQ index, a sparsity/compressibility measure originally developed for ANN pruning (Diao et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is importing data-compression theory into the SNN rewiring loop: rather than fixing a sparsity schedule, the PQ index quantifies how compressible the current sparse subnetwork is at each iteration, thereby adapting the fraction of connections pruned and regrown. The paper also positions itself firmly in the 'fully sparsification from scratch' camp, arguing that this is more hardware-compatible than gradual sparsification methods like gradient rewiring (Chen et al., 2021) or ADMM-based pruning (Deng et al., 2021a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive PQ-index-driven rewiring ratio offers a principled alternative to hand-tuned static sparsity schedules, which is practically relevant for deploying SNNs on neuromorphic hardware where on-chip training demands fixed sparsity from the outset. However, the experimental validation is limited to CIFAR-10/100 and DVS-CIFAR10 with ResNet19 and VGG-SNN at T=2 or T=10, leaving open whether the PQ index generalizes to deeper architectures, longer temporal windows, or larger-scale datasets. A notable open question is whether the marginal accuracy gains over dense baselines (≈0.2–1%) persist at scale, and whether the computational overhead of computing the PQ index each iteration is justified relative to simpler heuristics for setting the rewiring ratio.
