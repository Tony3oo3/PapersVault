## Problem

Simulating incompressible fluid flow on geometric surfaces requires enforcing the divergence-free constraint and preserving energy, but traditional mesh/grid methods suffer from high memory cost, limited robustness across surface representations, and energy dissipation from advection and pressure projection. Existing neural implicit representation simulators (e.g., INSR) cannot guarantee divergence-free fields and still dissipate energy through operator splitting. No prior method has achieved divergence-free incompressible flow simulation directly on implicitly neural-represented surfaces without meshing.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a surface in any of three representations—analytical (sphere, plane), explicit triangle mesh, or implicit neural SDF (DeepSDF, SIREN)—along with an initial vorticity or velocity field. For implicit neural surfaces, points are sampled via x − s(x)∇s(x)/‖∇s(x)‖ and normals from ∇s(x), completely bypassing marching cubes. The output is a continuous parametric neural function (not a discrete grid) that encodes both the divergence-free velocity field v and the scalar vorticity field ω on the surface, with optional conditioning features (e.g., image embeddings) for generation tasks.

## Method

The core construction parameterizes a scalar stream function σ(θ, x) with a neural network and derives the velocity as v(x) = j*((∇(cp*σ) ∘ j(x)) × n(x)), where cp* is the Closest Point Method pullback and n is the surface normal. This follows the exterior calculus identity v = *dμ, guaranteeing div(v) = 0 by construction without any projection step. For time evolution, the paper replaces semi-Lagrangian advection with a covariant-derivative-based Lie transport: the vorticity is advected via the loss L_i = ω_i − ω_{i+1} + (h/2)⟨∇ω_i, v_i⟩ + (h/2)⟨∇ω_{i+1}, v_{i+1}⟩ = 0, derived from the flow map Φ_t^v(ω) = exp(tD_v)ω. This eliminates both velocity advection and pressure projection, the two primary sources of energy dissipation in prior methods. The entire pipeline operates on sampled 3D points in the ambient space, making it meshless and directly applicable to implicit neural surfaces where classical methods fail to converge.

## Results

On the sphere jet flow benchmark (Table 1), NFFS achieves a mean square error of 2.89×10² over 100 time steps, outperforming PINN (1.73×10⁵), INSR (8.63×10⁴), and Functional Fluids on Surfaces (5.34×10³) by approximately 600×, 300×, and 18× respectively, while using comparable storage (~533 KB vs. 516–584 KB). On the inclined-plane Taylor vortices (Table 3), NFFS reaches an MSE of 1.71×10¹, beating HomoLBM (8.92×10¹) and INSR (3.45×10³) at similar memory cost. The method also preserves energy to a relative change of ~10⁻⁵ on the rotating sphere test, matching Functional Fluids on Surfaces and significantly outperforming INSR and RK-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NFFS consistently outperforms all baselines on the primary MSE metric. Against PINN (Raissi et al., 2019), NFFS reduces error by ~600× (2.89×10² vs. 1.73×10⁵) because PINN enforces the divergence-free constraint via a loss term that accumulates projection error each step. Against INSR (Chen et al., 2023), NFFS achieves ~300× lower error (2.89×10² vs. 8.63×10⁴) since INSR's operator-splitting advection causes energy dissipation. Against Functional Fluids on Surfaces (Azencot et al., 2014), NFFS improves MSE by ~18× (2.89×10² vs. 5.34×10³) at the same storage budget, and by ~5× on the Taylor vortices benchmark (1.71×10¹ vs. 3.21×10²). Against classical solvers—Stable Fluid (Stam, 1999) and Elcott et al. (2007)—NFFS achieves 3000× and 44× lower MSE respectively, while using 5× less memory than the high-resolution ground truth. The main trade-off is wall-clock time: NFFS requires 16.5 h per simulation versus 0.8 h for Functional Fluids on Surfaces, due to iterative neural optimization at each time step. On the implicit neural representation robustness test (Table 6), the classical method crashes at 36–89 time steps depending on marching-cubes resolution, whereas NFFS runs stably, demonstrating a qualitative advantage in geometry adaptivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete exterior calculus framework of Azencot et al. (2014) to continuous exterior calculus, combining it with the Closest Point Method (Ruuth & Merriman, 2008) to construct divergence-free vector fields on surfaces without mesh parameterization. The divergence-free construction v = *dμ is inherited from Richter-Powell et al. (2022), but the key departure is applying it to surface flows via CPM pullback operators, enabling simulation on arbitrary surface representations including implicit neural fields (SIREN, DeepSDF). The covariant-derivative-based advection scheme follows Azencot et al. (2014) but is implemented in a fully continuous, meshless neural setting, eliminating the need for pressure projection and associated energy dissipation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework enables incompressible fluid simulation directly on implicit neural surfaces without marching cubes or meshing, a setting where classical methods demonstrably fail to converge (crashing at 36–88 steps across resolutions). This opens the door to integrating fluid dynamics into neural reconstruction pipelines (NeRF, NeuS) for dynamic scene understanding. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to genus-zero surfaces for full accuracy (high-genus cohomology is handled only as a time-invariant term), narrow geometric features challenge the CPM tube-radius assumption, and per-timestep optimization is computationally expensive (~16.5h for 100 steps). Open questions include convergence/stability guarantees, extension to viscous flows and boundary conditions, and whether hybrid representations (hash encoding, neural kernels) can reduce the time cost.
