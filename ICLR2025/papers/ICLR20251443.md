## Problem

Existing recommendation system benchmarks are collected exclusively from the exposure space, creating a fundamental mismatch with the unexposed serving space where models actually operate. This gap prevents research on two critical industrial challenges: the distribution shift between training and serving, and the interplay between multiple stages of the cascade pipeline. No prior dataset captures the filtered (unexposed) items at each stage of the recommendation funnel.

## Input / Output

Each RecFlow instance is a tabular record containing user features (ID, age, gender, province), video features (ID, author, category, duration), context (request_id, timestamp), and stage-specific binary labels (pre-rank_neg, coarse_neg, rank_pos, rank_neg, rerank_pos, rerank_neg) indicating which videos were filtered at each of the 6 funnel stages. The dataset uniquely includes 1.9 billion stage samples alongside 38 million exposure samples, with 7 types of positive feedback (effective_view, long_view, like, follow, forward, comment) and playing_time for implicit feedback modeling. The 6-stage funnel outputs 8000→3000→500→120→10→6 videos per request, and the dataset records which videos survived or were filtered at each transition, enabling evaluation over the full candidate set rather than just exposed items.

## Method

RecFlow is the first industrial dataset capturing unexposed items filtered at each stage of a 6-stage recommendation funnel (retrieval → pre-ranking → coarse ranking → ranking → re-ranking → edge ranking), collected over 37 days from 42K users and 82M videos. The paper demonstrates its utility through several algorithmic explorations: (1) using stage-filtered videos as hard negatives for retrieval (SASRec with BPR loss), (2) FS-LTR, a priority-based ranking loss that forces logits of higher-priority stage samples above lower-priority ones to model inter-stage preferences, (3) auxiliary ranking tasks that add pairwise BPR loss between positive samples and same-request stage samples to pointwise CTR models (DSSM, DIN), and (4) competitive relation modeling in user behavior sequences using rank_pos videos as competing context with hierarchical attention. All experiments use single-epoch training to match industrial practice, evaluated with both classical AUC and a new Recall@K metric computed over the full stage candidate set to better reflect online serving conditions.

## Results

On the RecFlow dataset, using stage samples as hard negatives in retrieval (SASRec) yields up to 49% relative improvement in Recall@100 (0.687 vs. 0.461 baseline) when using rerank_pos samples. The FS-LTR method (Zheng et al., 2024), which models inter-stage preferences, achieves Recall@100 of 0.803 and NDCG@100 of 0.215 in retrieval, a 74% and 117% relative gain over the exposure-space-only baseline. In coarse ranking (DSSM), supplementing all stage samples as extra negatives boosts Recall@200 from 0.535 to 0.728, while FS-LTR reaches Recall@200 of 0.734. In ranking (DIN), FS-LTR achieves Recall@100 of 0.525 versus the 0.319 baseline, a 65% relative improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the standard exposure-space-only baseline and methods leveraging RecFlow's stage samples. FS-LTR (Zheng et al., 2024), which the paper implements to model inter-stage preferences, consistently outperforms the baseline across all three stages: in retrieval it achieves R@100 of 0.803 vs. 0.461 (+74%), in coarse ranking R@200 of 0.734 vs. 0.535 (+37%), and in ranking R@100 of 0.525 vs. 0.319 (+65%). A contrast method, PositiveRank (which only enforces positive > all-other logits without stage-priority ordering), also beats the baseline but is consistently outperformed by FS-LTR on Recall/NDCG metrics (e.g., R@100: 0.457 vs. 0.475 in coarse ranking; R@100: 0.506 vs. 0.525 in ranking), confirming that modeling the full stage-priority hierarchy is more effective than a simple positive-vs-rest ranking. Notably, all methods that incorporate stage samples show degraded AUC/LogLoss on the exposure space, revealing a fundamental tension between exposure-space calibration and serving-space recall that RecFlow makes measurable for the first time.

## Relation to Prior Work

RecFlow departs fundamentally from all prior RS benchmarks (MovieLens, Amazon, Taobao, TenRec, KuaiRec, KuaiRand, KuaiSAR), which exclusively capture the exposure space and thus cannot represent the unexposed items that dominate the serving space. Methodologically, the paper builds on FS-LTR (Zheng et al., 2024) and its Generalized Probability Ranking Principle to formalize how stage-priority information should be incorporated into retrieval and ranking losses, while the experimental models (SASRec, DSSM, DIN) are standard baselines from the sequential recommendation and CTR prediction literature. The dataset's design also draws on the distribution-shift and selection-bias literature (Qin et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2023), providing the first empirical substrate to test those theories at industrial scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RecFlow is the first dataset to make the full multi-stage funnel observable, opening research directions that were previously intractable: studying distribution shift between training and serving spaces, designing multi-stage-consistent objectives, and modeling competitive relations among candidate items. The finding that AUC degrades while Recall@K improves when stage negatives are added challenges the field's reliance on AUC as a proxy for online effectiveness, suggesting that evaluation metrics must be rethought for cascade systems. Limitations include a single recommendation scenario (no cross-domain generalization), absence of multimodal features, and the 1.9B-instance scale imposing significant hardware costs. Open questions include the optimal hardness-to-easy-negative ratio, whether the CN-PN-only FS-LTR configuration generalizes beyond this architecture, and how to extend the framework to re-ranking and edge-ranking stages where the paper provides only preliminary data.
