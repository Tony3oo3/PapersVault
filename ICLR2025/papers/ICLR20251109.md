## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is limited to purely spatial representations and struggles to model view-dependent effects such as specular highlights, anisotropic reflections, and transparency. While N-dimensional Gaussians (N-DG) extend the representation to 6D spatial-angular space, its Gaussian control scheme is suboptimal—over-allocating points in view-dependent scenes and under-utilizing resources in non-view-dependent ones—making it inefficient and less practical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251109/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images with corresponding camera poses as input and produces a 6D Gaussian representation (position μ_p, direction μ_d, 6×6 covariance, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color coefficients) that is sliced into conditional 3D Gaussians at render time. During inference, only the conditional mean μ_cond and conditional opacity α_cond need per-view computation, while the conditional covariance Σ_cond can be pre-computed, enabling real-time rasterization. The method is fully compatible with the 3DGS rendering pipeline, requiring no changes to the rasterizer or downstream applications.

## Method

6DGS enhances the 6D Gaussian representation in two key ways: (1) replacing learnable RGB color with order-3 spherical harmonics to capture view-dependent color, and (2) introducing a scalar λ_opa (hyperparameter or per-Gaussian learnable) that modulates the conditional opacity via f_cond = exp(−λ_opa · (d−μ_d)ᵀΣ_d⁻¹(d−μ_d)), providing finer control over directional opacity decay than N-DG's fixed formulation. At render time, each 6D Gaussian is sliced into a conditional 3D Gaussian by computing the conditional mean (a view-direction-dependent position shift), the conditional covariance (a view-independent Schur complement), and the modulated opacity. To enable the standard 3DGS adaptive densification (cloning/splitting) and pruning, the paper performs SVD on the conditional covariance to extract rotation and scale, replacing N-DG's high-dimensional culling scheme. The entire method uses the same loss, optimizer, and differentiable rasterization as 3DGS, with only the minimum opacity threshold adjusted (τ=0.01 vs. 0.005) to account for the view-dependent opacity decay.

## Results

On the 6DGS-PBR dataset (physically-based ray-traced scenes with strong view-dependent effects), 6DGS achieves an average PSNR of 37.61 dB and SSIM of 0.977, outperforming 3DGS (27.53 dB, 0.950) by +10.08 dB and N-DG (36.10 dB, 0.973) by +1.51 dB, while using only 67,490 Gaussian points on average—a 41.4% reduction over 3DGS and an 82.8% reduction over N-DG. Rendering speed reaches 154.5 FPS (326.3 FPS with FlashGS integration), compared to 114.0 FPS for 3DGS and 49.0 FPS for N-DG. On the Synthetic NeRF dataset (no strong view dependency), 6DGS matches 3DGS in quality (33.26 vs. 33.25 dB PSNR) while using 23.1% fewer points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS, 6DGS wins decisively on the 6DGS-PBR dataset with a +10.08 dB average PSNR gain (up to +15.73 dB on bunny-cloud) while using 41.4% fewer Gaussian points, and also renders faster (154.5 vs. 114.0 FPS). Against N-DG, 6DGS achieves +1.51 dB higher average PSNR with 82.8% fewer points and 3.2× faster rendering (154.5 vs. 49.0 FPS). On the Synthetic NeRF dataset (no strong view dependency), 6DGS essentially ties 3DGS in PSNR (33.26 vs. 33.25) while using 23.1% fewer points, whereas N-DG underperforms both by ~4.25 dB due to its over-allocation of directional capacity. On real-world datasets (Deep Blending, Tanks & Temples, Shiny), 6DGS matches or slightly trails 3DGS in PSNR (e.g., 28.59 vs. 29.48 on Deep Blending) but consistently uses 22–33% fewer points, and on the Shiny dataset (strong view-dependent effects) it gains +2.88 to +2.97 dB over 3DGS on individual scenes like 'cd' and 'lab'.

## Relation to Prior Work

6DGS sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and N-dimensional Gaussians (N-DG), adopting the 6D spatial-angular representation from N-DG while retaining the 3DGS rendering pipeline, spherical harmonics for view-dependent color, and adaptive density control. The key departures from N-DG are: (1) replacing learnable RGB with spherical harmonics for color, (2) introducing a tunable λ_opa parameter to modulate view-dependent opacity via the Mahalanobis distance, and (3) using SVD of the conditional covariance to recover scale/rotation for 3DGS-style densification instead of N-DG's high-dimensional culling. The method is explicitly designed as a drop-in upgrade to the 3DGS ecosystem, requiring only the slicing step (Algorithm 1) before standard 3DGS rasterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving near-physically-based rendering quality at real-time speeds (326 FPS with FlashGS) while remaining fully compatible with the existing 3DGS toolchain, lowering the barrier for adoption in VR/AR, gaming, and film pipelines. The 6DGS-PBR dataset (volumetric clouds, glass, CT scans, subsurface scattering) fills a gap in public benchmarks for view-dependent volumetric rendering. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether CUDA implementation of the slicing step can close the remaining speed gap, and how the method behaves under more complex global illumination (beyond per-Gaussian view-dependent opacity).
