## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees but often yields prediction sets that are too large to be practically useful, especially for high-dimensional classification tasks. Existing methods optimize a single nonconformity score with a scalar threshold, which imposes a rigid partitioning structure that cannot adapt to the geometry of the score distribution. This paper asks whether extending conformal prediction to a multi-dimensional score space can better separate correct from incorrect labels and produce smaller, more informative prediction sets.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet, 224×224 for PathMNIST/ImageNet) or text (20 Newsgroups) as input, processed through a pretrained backbone (ResNet-50, ViT, or BERT-base) with n attached classification heads. The output is a prediction set — a subset of the Q class labels — guaranteed to contain the true label with probability at least 1−α. Unlike standard conformal prediction which returns a threshold-based set, the output here is determined by membership in a union of selected cells in the n-dimensional score space, making the decision boundary unstructured and adaptive to the local score geometry.

## Method

The core mechanism is a three-stage calibration procedure over a multi-dimensional nonconformity score s(x,y) = [s₁(x,y), …, sₙ(x,y)]ᵀ. First, the calibration data is split into D_cells (for cell construction) and D_re-cal (for coverage calibration); the score space is partitioned into k Voronoi-like cells centered at the calibration scores. Second, each cell is scored by D_i = (F_i + T_i)/T_i, the ratio of total to true label scores, and cells are ranked from lowest to highest D_i. Third, the top-ranked cells are selected until the held-out D_re-cal achieves the target coverage ⌈(1−α)(r+1)⌉, and at test time the prediction set includes all labels whose multi-dimensional score falls within the selected region. The multi-dimensional score is generated by a self-ensemble: n classification heads attached to the penultimate layer of a shared backbone, trained with cross-entropy plus a cosine-similarity diversity penalty (β=1) to encourage complementary predictions. The paper proves that this cell-selection procedure is equivalent to a one-dimensional conformal prediction over a derived rank score, preserving finite-sample coverage guarantees, and that it solves a relaxed set-size minimization problem under a coverage constraint without requiring any iterative optimization.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 7 classification heads and RAPS scores (α=0.1), the multi-score method achieves a mean set size of 15.32, compared to 35.29 for the best single head, 22.37 for uniform averaging, 22.47 for optimized weights, and 22.49 for the L2-norm baseline — a 32% reduction over the optimized-weights baseline. On Tiny ImageNet (single-head multi-score, α=0.1), the method achieves 58.08 vs. 62.70 for optimized weights. On PathMNIST (α=0.01), the improvement is more modest (6.26 vs. 6.50 for optimized weights), reflecting the lower difficulty of the 9-class task. All methods maintain the target empirical coverage (≥1−α) across all datasets and α levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baselines are the optimized weighted-score approach (Bai et al., 2021; Luo & Zhou, 2024b), which learns weights to minimize set size under a coverage constraint, and the uniform average of scores. On CIFAR-100 with 7 heads and RAPS (α=0.1), the multi-score method (15.32) outperforms the optimized-weights baseline (22.47) by 32% in mean set size, and the uniform baseline (22.37) by 31%. The advantage grows with the number of heads: at 1 head the gap is small (~3%), but by 7 heads the multi-score method's cell-based region selection clearly exploits the multi-dimensional structure that a single hyperplane (optimized weights) or a single threshold (best head) cannot capture. On Tiny ImageNet, the multi-score method achieves 58.08 vs. 62.70 (optimized) and 62.96 (uniform) at α=0.1, a ~7% improvement. On the easier PathMNIST task (9 classes), the advantage narrows and at α=0.02 the optimized-weights baseline (2.96) slightly edges out the multi-score method (3.31), suggesting the method's benefit is most pronounced in high-dimensional, multi-class settings where the score space has richer structure to exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard conformal prediction framework (Lei et al., 2013) from a one-dimensional nonconformity score to a multi-dimensional score space, where the key departure is replacing the single threshold or weighted-sum hyperplane with a flexible cell-partitioning selection region. The self-ensemble construction draws on the diversity regularization from early-exit ensembles (Qendro et al., 2021), but repurposes it to generate complementary scores from a single backbone rather than from different network depths. Unlike prior multi-score approaches (Bai et al., 2021; Luo & Zhou, 2024b) that impose a rigid hyperplane structure via optimized weights, this method identifies arbitrary-shaped regions with low false-label density, and unlike vector quantile regression, it optimizes for set-size efficiency rather than capturing the central mass of the output distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a parameter-free, optimization-free alternative to weighted-score conformal prediction that is independent of the coverage level and base score, making it broadly applicable without retraining. A key limitation is the need to split calibration data into cell-definition and re-calibration subsets, which reduces effective calibration size and may hurt performance in small-data regimes (though ablations show robustness down to 50% of D_cells). Open questions include how the cell-partitioning approach scales to very high dimensions (n >> 7), whether the marginal coverage guarantee can be strengthened to conditional coverage, and whether the diversity-regularized self-ensemble can be replaced by cheaper score-generation strategies without losing the complementary-information benefit.
