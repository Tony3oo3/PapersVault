## Problem

Tuning-based personalization methods (e.g., DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion) inherently lose compositional ability after fine-tuning: generating 'a <sks> dog wearing a headphone' produces the target dog but drops the headphone. The paper identifies the root cause as semantic drift—the customized concept's embedding moves away from its superclass in CLIP text space—reducing the entropy of joint conditional probability distributions and making it progressively harder to sample from composed conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252492/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of reference images (typically 3–5) of a target concept plus a text prompt containing a special token (e.g., <sks>) and produces a personalized image at 512×512 resolution via Stable Diffusion V1.5. For video, the same fine-tuned model is plugged into AnimateDiff V2 to generate 512×512 videos with 25 inference steps. The SPL is computed using the CLIP ViT-L/14 text encoder from SD v1.5, extracting EOS-token embeddings for both the personalized phrase and the superclass phrase.

## Method

ClassDiffusion builds on Custom Diffusion (fine-tuning only K and V matrices in cross-attention layers) and adds a Semantic Preservation Loss (SPL) that minimizes the cosine distance between the CLIP text embeddings of the personalized phrase (e.g., 'a photo of a V* dog') and the superclass phrase (e.g., 'a photo of a dog'), summed over all special tokens, batch elements, and hidden dimensions. The total training objective is L_recon + λ·L_sp with λ=1.0. The theoretical analysis frames the diffusion model as an implicit Energy-Based Model and shows via Bayes' decomposition that fine-tuning shifts the implicit class classifier p(c_class|x), reducing the entropy of the joint conditional distribution p(x|c_class, c_1, …, c_i) and thus increasing sampling difficulty. By anchoring the personalized token's embedding near its superclass, SPL restores the semantic space needed for compositional generation without sacrificing concept fidelity.

## Results

On the DreamBooth Dataset (20 concepts, single-concept setting), ClassDiffusion achieves the best text-alignment scores: CLIP-T 0.300, BLIP2-T 0.460, and TIFA 0.843, surpassing all baselines on these compositional-generation metrics. However, it trails on concept-similarity metrics (CLIP-I 0.828 vs. DreamBooth's 0.855; DINO-I 0.673 vs. Custom Diffusion's 0.693), reflecting a trade-off between prompt following and subject fidelity. In the multi-concept setting, ClassDiffusion improves CLIP-T from 0.282 to 0.320 and BLIP2-T from 0.380 to 0.477 over Custom Diffusion. In the user study, ClassDiffusion is preferred over all baselines on text similarity (win rates 56.6%–95.4%) but loses on image similarity to DreamBooth (42.1%) and Custom Diffusion (40.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ClassDiffusion outperforms all five baselines on the primary compositional-generation metrics: CLIP-T (0.300 vs. 0.242–0.293), BLIP2-T (0.460 vs. 0.295–0.418), and TIFA (0.843 vs. 0.505–0.835). The largest gains are over DreamBooth (+0.051 CLIP-T, +0.165 BLIP2-T) and Textual Inversion (+0.058 CLIP-T, +0.152 BLIP2-T), which suffer the most severe compositional degradation. Against the closest competitor SVDiff, ClassDiffusion leads by +0.007 CLIP-T and +0.042 BLIP2-T, while SVDiff retains a slight edge on DINO-I (0.606 vs. 0.673 is actually ClassDiffusion winning; SVDiff's DINO-I is 0.606). On concept-similarity metrics (CLIP-I, DINO-I), ClassDiffusion trails DreamBooth and Custom Diffusion by 0.01–0.03, consistent with the user-study finding that humans rate DreamBooth and Custom Diffusion as more faithful to the reference subject. In the multi-concept setting, ClassDiffusion improves over Custom Diffusion by +0.038 CLIP-T and +0.097 BLIP2-T, confirming that the semantic preservation loss scales to multiple personalized tokens. The paper also introduces BLIP2-T as a more reliable text-alignment metric, arguing that CLIP-T underestimates true compositional quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Custom Diffusion's parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy (optimizing K/V matrices in cross-attention layers) atop Stable Diffusion, but departs from the community's standard remedy for post-fine-tuning degradation. While DreamBooth's Class-Specific Prior Preservation Loss addresses diversity collapse, and methods like SVDiff and NeTI target overfitting more broadly, ClassDiffusion identifies a more specific mechanism—semantic drift of the personalized token away from its superclass in CLIP text space—and proposes a Semantic Preservation Loss (SPL) that directly constrains this drift via cosine distance between text embeddings. The theoretical grounding draws on Energy-Based Model formulations of diffusion models and compositional generation literature to formalize how semantic drift reduces the entropy of joint conditional distributions, making multi-attribute sampling harder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a more precise diagnosis than the commonly cited 'overfitting' explanation for why personalized models lose compositional ability, and the SPL is a simple, plug-and-play addition that requires no architectural changes. However, the practical gains are modest: while text-alignment metrics (CLIP-T, BLIP2-T, TIFA) improve to SOTA, image-similarity metrics (CLIP-I, DINO-I) remain below several baselines, suggesting a trade-off between prompt adherence and concept fidelity that the SPL weight only partially resolves. Open questions include whether the semantic-drift diagnosis and SPL generalize beyond the Custom Diffusion fine-tuning paradigm to other personalization methods (e.g., LoRA-based approaches), whether the theoretical entropy argument holds at larger model scales, and whether the BLIP2-T metric the authors introduce will be adopted as a standard evaluation in this subfield.
