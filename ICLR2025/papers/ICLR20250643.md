## Problem

Existing INRs with sinusoidal, Gaussian, or Gabor wavelet activations suffer from an imbalanced space-frequency tradeoff: they either overfit to fine details while introducing noise in smooth regions, or fail to generalize to unseen coordinates in reconstruction tasks like inpainting and denoising. Moreover, these activations require task-specific hyperparameter tuning (frequency, scale) that is heuristic and signal-dependent, limiting practical deployment. The paper asks whether an activation function with provably optimal joint space-frequency energy concentration can resolve these limitations simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The INR receives low-dimensional coordinates (2D pixel indices for images, 3D spatial coordinates plus viewing directions for NeRF) and outputs the corresponding signal values (RGB triplets, binary occupancy, or color-density pairs). Coordinates are first mapped to a higher-dimensional Fourier positional encoding before entering the MLP. For reconstruction tasks, only a subset of coordinates (e.g., 70% random samples or unmasked regions) is provided during training, and the network must generalize to the full coordinate grid at test time.

## Method

PIN replaces the standard MLP activation with the zeroth-order Prolate Spheroidal Wave Function (PSWF), which is the eigenfunction of an integral operator that maximizes energy concentration in both spatial and frequency domains simultaneously. The PSWF is computed numerically via its Legendre polynomial expansion (truncated at a finite order) and converted to a differentiable continuous function using cubic spline interpolation, then instantiated as a learnable activation ψ̂(x) = T·ψ(wx) + b where T, w, and b control amplitude, frequency scaling, and vertical shift. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that the L-layer PIN output is a polynomial of first-layer PSWFs with degree K^(L-1), and since PSWFs are band-limited, the network output is also band-limited with rapid spatial decay, guaranteeing strong space-frequency localization. Unlike Gaussian or Gabor activations whose parameters appear nonlinearly in exponential terms (making them hard to optimize), the PSWF's spline-based parameterization places the scaling parameter in only three polynomial coefficients, enabling faster and more stable convergence during training.

## Results

On the DIV2K dataset (30 images, 5-layer MLP with 300 hidden neurons), PIN achieves 41.46 dB PSNR, substantially outperforming WIRE (30.12 dB), SIREN (38.77 dB), GAUSS (28.13 dB), and ReLU+PE (26.74 dB). On the full Kodak dataset (24 images), PIN reaches 40.17 dB PSNR, surpassing FR-INR (37.91 dB) and INCODE (34.07 dB). PIN also achieves the highest PSNR in image inpainting (both 70% random sampling and text-masked settings), novel view synthesis on the Drums dataset, and 3D occupancy field representation on Stanford shapes, consistently producing the most visually faithful reconstructions with minimal noise in smooth regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIN outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated task. On DIV2K image representation, PIN leads SIREN by 2.69 dB, WIRE by 11.34 dB, GAUSS by 13.33 dB, and ReLU+PE by 14.72 dB. On Kodak, PIN surpasses the more recent FR-INR by 2.26 dB and INCODE by 6.10 dB. In image inpainting, PIN is the only method that maintains high PSNR under both 70% random sampling and text-masked occlusion, while WIRE visibly overfits and SIREN requires highly specific weight initialization. For novel view synthesis (Drums dataset), PIN produces sharper geometry and better-preserved lighting details compared to WIRE, SIREN, and GAUSS. In 3D occupancy field representation, PIN accurately captures high-frequency structural details without the low-pass bias seen in WIRE and SIREN, and without the uniform-area distortions observed in GAUSS. The paper attributes PIN's consistent advantage to the optimal joint space-frequency energy concentration of PSWFs, which avoids the rapid-decay artifacts of Gabor/Gaussian activations and the poor spatial localization of sinusoidal activations.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIN extends the line of INR activation function design that progressed from ReLU (spectral bias) → sinusoidal (SIREN) → Gaussian/Gabor wavelets (GAUSS, WIRE) by replacing the activation with Prolate Spheroidal Wave Functions, which are provably optimal for joint space-frequency energy concentration. The theoretical analysis builds directly on Roddenberry et al.'s polynomial-expansion framework for wavelet-activated INRs and Yüce et al.'s structured dictionary perspective, extending the degree-bound result to PSWFs and showing the network output is band-limited. For positional encoding, PIN adopts the scheme from Factor Fields (Chen et al., 2023a) rather than the original Fourier features of Tancik et al., and the adaptive parameterization (amplitude, frequency, offset) addresses the hyperparameter sensitivity that plagues Gaussian and Gabor-based INRs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the classical signal-processing optimality of PSWFs translates into consistent gains across representation, inpainting, NeRF, and edge-detection tasks, suggesting that the 'wide frequency spectrum challenge' (simultaneously preserving smooth and fine-detail regions) can be addressed at the activation level without architectural changes. A practical advantage is the robustness to parameter initialization (variance up to 25), which reduces the grid-search burden that currently limits adoption of space-frequency compact INRs. Open questions include computational cost of the cubic-spline PSWF evaluation at scale, whether the band-limitation property helps or constrains very high-frequency signals, and whether the approach extends to video or 4D representations where temporal frequency concentration becomes relevant.
