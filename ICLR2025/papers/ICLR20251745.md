## Problem

Existing faithfulness metrics for GNN explanations are not interchangeable—explanations scoring highly under one metric can be arbitrarily unfaithful under another—yet the literature provides little guidance on metric selection or architectural tuning for faithfulness. Moreover, it remains unclear whether optimizing for faithfulness is always a sensible design goal, and the role of faithfulness in out-of-distribution generalization has been entirely neglected.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on annotated graphs G_A = (G, X) with per-node feature vectors x_u ∈ R^d and a corresponding explanation subgraph R_A (identified via thresholding or top-K over soft relevance scores in [0,1]). Faithfulness is measured by perturbing the input—zeroing irrelevant features, deleting edges from the complement or explanation, or replacing the complement with that of another sample—and computing the divergence (KL, L1, or likelihood difference) between the original and perturbed label distributions. The modular GNNs under study take a full annotated graph and jointly output a class prediction and per-element relevance scores that define the explanation subgraph.

## Method

The paper unifies all existing faithfulness metrics under a single template (Definition 2) parameterized by a divergence d and interventional distributions p_R (over supergraphs of the explanation) and p_C (over supergraphs of the complement), then proves they are not interchangeable (Proposition 1) and that standard necessity estimators are systematically blind to the number of truly irrelevant edges (Proposition 2), proposing a fixed-budget deletion scheme to fix this (Proposition 3). For regular injective GNNs, it proves that strictly faithful explanations must coincide with the entire computational graph (the L-hop neighborhood for node classification, the full graph for graph classification), rendering them uninformative (Proposition 4). For modular GNNs, it identifies four architectural desiderata—Hard Scores, Explanation Readout, Local Aggregations, and Classifier Features—whose absence allows information leakage from the complement, and empirically demonstrates that augmenting popular SE-GNNs (GSAT, GISST, RAGE) and DI-GNNs (LECI, CIGA) with missing desiderata significantly improves faithfulness. Finally, it proves that a DI-GNN cannot be domain-invariant unless its extracted subgraph is also strictly sufficient (Proposition 5), and derives a generalization bound (Theorem 1) showing that the ID–OOD likelihood gap is controlled by the detector's plausibility and the explanation's sufficiency.

## Results

The paper's key empirical finding is that existing necessity metrics (RFid+) are insensitive to explanation size: on Motif2, RFid+ assigns nearly constant scores (18±02 vs 21±02) to Top-30% vs Top-90% explanations, while the proposed Nec metric properly penalizes larger explanations (54±04 vs 34±02). For SE-GNNs, adding architectural desiderata (Hard Scores, Explanation Readout, Local Aggregations) to RAGE improves faithfulness on BBBP from 33±04 to 46±02 while maintaining 84% accuracy. For DI-GNNs, augmenting LECI with ER+HS+LA on CMNIST-Color boosts OOD accuracy from 26±10 to 75±06 and faithfulness from 48±11 to 61±01. The paper also proves that for injective regular GNNs, strictly faithful explanations are trivially the full L-hop neighborhood (node classification) or the entire graph (graph classification), rendering them uninformative.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new GNN but augments five existing modular architectures (GSAT, GISST, RAGE, LECI, CIGA) with architectural desiderata they lack. RAGE benefits most consistently from Hard Scores (HS), improving faithfulness on BBBP from 33 to 46 and on BaMS from 33 to 46 while maintaining or improving accuracy. GSAT, in contrast, is severely harmed by HS on Motif2 and Motif-Size (accuracy drops to ~50%), confirming that exact-zero scores are difficult for gradient-based training. For DI-GNNs, LECI+ER on CMNIST-Color nearly triples OOD accuracy (26→58) while CIGA+ER on Motif2 improves faithfulness from 38 to 54. The paper also shows that on LBAPcore and SST2, no augmentation helps, and a standard GIN without OOD regularization matches or exceeds the DI-GNNs, questioning the suitability of these datasets for evaluating local explanations. Overall, the comparisons reveal that architectural choices (not training objectives) are the primary bottleneck for faithfulness in current modular GNNs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: GNN explainability (Agarwal et al. 2023; Pope et al. 2019; Amara et al. 2022), self-explainable and domain-invariant GNN architectures (Miao et al. 2022a; Lin et al. 2020; Gui et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2022), and causal notions of sufficiency/necessity from XAI (Beckers 2022; Watson et al. 2021). Rather than proposing a new architecture or metric, it unifies existing faithfulness metrics under a single parametric template (Definition 2) and then uses that template to prove fundamental limitations: metrics are not interchangeable, necessity estimators are systematically blind to irrelevant edges, and for injective GNNs perfectly faithful explanations are trivial. The domain-invariance connection (Proposition 5, Theorem 1) repurposes the faithfulness/sufficiency framework to show that the DI-GNN literature's focus on plausibility of the extracted subgraph is insufficient without also ensuring the classifier actually relies on it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central practical implication is that faithfulness measurements are meaningless without specifying the perturbation distribution and divergence, which has direct consequences for high-stakes XAI certification where explanation providers could manipulate parameters. The theoretical result that injective GNNs cannot have informative faithful explanations highlights a fundamental expressivity-explainability trade-off that the field has not adequately addressed. The domain-invariance connection opens a new evaluation axis for DI-GNNs (sufficiency of the extracted subgraph) that current benchmarks entirely neglect. Limitations include reliance on relatively small synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets, the architectural desiderata being simple modifications rather than new architectures, and the evaluation of faithfulness being computationally expensive for large graphs. A truly faithful-by-design GNN architecture that avoids the expressivity trade-off remains an open challenge.
