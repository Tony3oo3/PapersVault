## Problem

Generating time series of heterogeneous tabular data (variable-length sequences mixing numerical and categorical fields) remains largely unexplored. Existing autoregressive Transformer approaches (TabGPT, REaLTabFormer) suffer from limited diversity in unconditional generation, and no diffusion-based method can handle the combined challenges of field heterogeneity, variable sequence length, and temporal dependencies among rows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253357/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method starts from a sequence of τ_max random noise vectors in the VAE latent space (792-dim each); for conditional generation, a parent table row is additionally encoded and injected via adaLN-Zero conditioning with classifier-free guidance. The output is a variable-length sequence of heterogeneous tabular rows (e.g., bank transactions with mixed numerical amounts and categorical types), where the length is determined dynamically by the model generating [EoS] padding tokens rather than being fixed or sampled independently. The VAE compresses individual rows (not the full time series), so the DiT operates on a sequence of independent row embeddings that it must combine into a temporally coherent whole.

## Method

TabDiT follows a Latent Diffusion Model paradigm with a key architectural departure: the VAE is non-holistic, compressing each tabular row independently into a 792-dim latent vector rather than encoding the entire variable-length time series, which simplifies variational training and avoids representing variable-length sequences in the latent space. A 12-layer DiT denoising network (140M params) then learns to combine these independent row embeddings into temporally coherent sequences by modeling the joint distribution over the latent sequence. Two additional design choices address heterogeneity and length: (1) an autoregressive VAE decoder where each field's generation is conditioned on previously decoded fields in the same row, paired with a variable-range decimal representation that encodes numerical values as a magnitude-order prefix plus 4 most-significant digits (shorter and less error-prone than fixed-length digit sequences); and (2) explicit padding prediction, where [EoS] tokens in padding rows are generated by the DiT itself, allowing the model to jointly predict both the content and the length of the time series rather than sampling length independently.

## Results

On unconditional generation, TabDiT achieves near-chance-level MLD-TS accuracy (50.43% ± 1.85) on Age2, indicating the discriminator cannot distinguish real from synthetic time series, while the AR Baseline scores 67.53% ± 0.75. On the largest dataset Age1, TabDiT improves MLD-TS by over 27 points (63.93% vs. 91.20%). On conditional generation across four datasets, TabDiT outperforms all baselines (SDV, REaLTabFormer, AR Baseline) on both MLD-TS and LD-SR metrics, often approaching the 50% lower bound. On the large-scale bank dataset (~87M transactions), TabDiT achieves MLD-TS of 56.77 and MLE-TS of 71.38, close to the real-data upper bound of 73.22.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabDiT is compared against three baselines: (1) a strong AR Baseline (a purely autoregressive Transformer merging the hierarchical architecture of TabGPT with the authors' decoding scheme), (2) REaLTabFormer (an AR Transformer for conditional relational tabular generation), and (3) SDV (a Gaussian Copula-based non-deep method). On unconditional generation, TabDiT outperforms the AR Baseline by 17–27 MLD-TS points across all six datasets, with the largest gap on Age1 (63.93% vs. 91.20%). On conditional generation (Table 4), TabDiT beats all three baselines on every dataset and task (child gt-cond, child, merged) for both MLD-TS and LD-SR; in the 'child gt-cond' task the AR Baseline is second-best, while in 'child' and 'merged' REaLTabFormer is second-best. Notably, TabDiT surpasses REaLTabFormer even when conditioned on lower-quality generated parent rows (SR-TabDiT scores below REaLTabFormer on parent generation in Table 5), demonstrating the robustness of the diffusion-based denoising. On the large-scale bank dataset (~87M rows), TabDiT achieves MLD-TS of 56.77 vs. 58.03 for the AR Baseline and MLE-TS of 71.38 vs. 70.41, approaching the real-data ceiling of 73.22.

## Relation to Prior Work

TabDiT extends the Latent Diffusion Model paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022; Peebles & Xie, 2023) to the tabular time series domain by replacing the holistic VAE compression with a non-holistic design: individual rows are compressed independently by a VAE, and a DiT denoising network learns to combine these row embeddings into temporally coherent sequences. This departs from prior LDM-for-tabular work (TabSyn, Zhang et al., 2024), which uses an MLP denoising network and cannot handle variable-length sequences, and from autoregressive tabular time series models (TabGPT, REaLTabFormer), which suffer from limited generation diversity. The variable-length handling adapts FiT's padding mechanism but inverts its logic: rather than masking padding tokens, TabDiT forces the DiT to explicitly generate [EoS] padding rows, thereby jointly predicting sequence length and content. The autoregressive VAE decoder and variable-range decimal representation for numerical fields are novel components not present in prior diffusion-based tabular generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TabDiT is the first deep learning method to perform unconditional generation of heterogeneous tabular data time series, addressing a practical gap in privacy-preserving synthetic data for domains like banking and healthcare where public datasets are scarce. The decomposition strategy (simplified VAE + DiT for temporal modeling) offers a scalable path to handling variable-length sequences that holistic VAEs struggle with, and the near-chance-level discrimination accuracy (50.43% on Age2) suggests the approach can produce highly realistic synthetic series. However, the unconditional evaluation is limited to a single self-implemented AR baseline, and the single-row parent-table generation (needed for the 'child' and 'merged' tasks) underperforms REaLTabFormer, indicating the method is not yet competitive for all sub-tasks. Open questions include whether the non-holistic VAE decomposition generalizes to longer or more complex temporal dependencies, and whether the approach scales to truly large heterogeneous time series beyond the ~100K-account bank dataset tested.
