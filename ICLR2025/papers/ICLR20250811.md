## Problem

Natural gradient descent offers a mathematically principled way to train Physics Informed Neural Networks by respecting the function-space geometry, but its standard formulation requires inverting a P×P Gram matrix at every step, yielding O(P³) cost that is prohibitive for practical PDE solvers. Existing approximations (e.g., K-FAC) sacrifice geometric fidelity, and the connection between natural gradient and the underlying PDE operator's Green's function has not been made explicit. The paper asks whether one can retain the full geometric structure of natural gradient while reducing the per-step cost to O(min(P²S, S²P)).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed batch of collocation points (S_D interior points in Ω and S_B boundary points in ∂Ω) that are never resampled during training, along with the PDE operator D, boundary operator B, source term f, and boundary data g. The neural network is an MLP with tanh activations whose architecture (layer widths, depth) is chosen per problem. The output is the parameter vector θ obtained by iterating SVD-based pseudo-inverse updates with a line-search learning rate, and the trained network u_|θ is evaluated on a separate, larger test set to report L² error against a known analytical solution.

## Method

ANaGRAM reformulates the PINNs loss as a least-squares regression on the composed model (D,B)∘u, then computes the empirical natural gradient by performing an SVD of the S×P Jacobian matrix φ̂ whose entries are ∂_p D[u_|θ](x_i^D) and ∂_p B[u_|θ](x_i^B) evaluated at the fixed batch, rather than forming and inverting the P×P Gram matrix. The update direction is d_θ = V̂ Δ̂† Ûᵗ ∇L̂, where Δ̂† applies a spectral cutoff ε to the singular values, and the learning rate is chosen by line search over [0,1] (η=1 corresponding to a full Green's-function solve in the tangent space). The paper proves that this update is equivalent to applying the generalized Green's function of the operator D on the tangent space T_θM, and that the empirical tangent space is the span of the NNTK kernel sections at the batch points. Two correction terms (E^metric and E^⊥) account for the difference between the empirical and population tangent spaces; in the vanilla algorithm they are neglected, and the paper notes they vanish for linear operators with MLPs.

## Results

On the 2D Laplace equation (P=129), ANaGRAM achieves a median L² error of 2.42e-09 and test loss of 3.85e-13, outperforming E-NGD (1.31e-06 / 2.91e-09) by over three orders of magnitude. On the nonlinear Allen-Cahn equation (P=921), ANaGRAM reaches a median L² error of 2.51e-03, while all baselines stagnate at 0.39–0.74 (Adam, E-NGD, GD, L-BFGS respectively). Per-step CPU cost of ANaGRAM ranges from 0.07 s (2D Laplace) to 0.60 s (Allen-Cahn), comparable to or lower than E-NGD and L-BFGS on most problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANaGRAM outperforms all three baselines (E-NGD, Adam, L-BFGS) on every problem tested. Against E-NGD (Müller & Zeinhofer, ICML 2023), ANaGRAM achieves 3+ orders of magnitude lower L² error on the 2D Laplace (2.42e-09 vs 1.31e-06) and a dramatic 294× improvement on the nonlinear Allen-Cahn (2.51e-03 vs 7.39e-01); E-NGD is slightly better on the 5D Laplace (1.40e-05 vs 3.76e-05) but at 9× the per-step cost. Against Adam, ANaGRAM wins by 5–6 orders of magnitude on linear problems and by ~150× on Allen-Cahn. Against L-BFGS, ANaGRAM is superior on all four problems, most notably on the 5D Laplace (3.76e-05 vs 9.08e-02, a 2400× gap) and Allen-Cahn (2.51e-03 vs 5.40e-01, a 215× gap), while also being faster per step on the 5D Laplace (0.72 s vs 0.30 s per step, though L-BFGS uses fewer total iterations). The paper notes that for linear PDEs, ANaGRAM is theoretically equivalent to E-NGD (Section E), and the observed differences stem from the empirical (finite-batch) formulation and SVD-based pseudo-inverse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the natural gradient framework (Amari, 1998; Ollivier, 2015) to PINNs by reformulating the problem through the lens of RKHS theory and the Neural Tangent Kernel (Jacot et al., 2018; Rudner et al., 2019). It builds directly on Müller and Zeinhofer's (2023) Energy Natural Gradient Descent, proving equivalence for linear operators while introducing a computationally cheaper SVD-based algorithm (ANaGRAM) that scales as O(min(P²S, S²P)) rather than O(P³). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the empirical tangent space and the demonstration that PINN natural gradient corresponds to the Green's function of the differential operator restricted to the tangent space, unifying PINN optimization with classical PDE solution theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Green's function interpretation provides a principled understanding of what natural gradient is actually doing in PINN training, moving beyond ad hoc loss-weighting heuristics that have been proposed to address PINN pathologies. The practical impact is significant for nonlinear PDEs, where ANaGRAM achieves orders-of-magnitude lower error than all baselines (notably on Allen-Cahn), while maintaining per-step costs comparable to L-BFGS. Open questions include: automatic selection of the SVD cutoff factor, principled batch point selection (currently heuristic), extension to mini-batch training, and incorporation of data assimilation into the theoretical framework. The method's reliance on full-batch SVD also raises scalability questions for very high-dimensional or large-parameter problems.
