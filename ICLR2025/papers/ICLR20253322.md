## Problem

Large pretrained video models generate rich visual plans for tasks but are not grounded in any agent's embodiment — they do not specify how to actuate the world to reach the depicted visual states. Existing grounding approaches require expensive embodiment-specific demonstrations with action labels to train inverse dynamics models or goal-conditioned policies, limiting generalization to out-of-distribution settings. This paper asks whether video models can be directly grounded to continuous actions through self-exploration in the environment, without any action annotations, rewards, or segmentation masks.

## Input / Output

The video model takes a single observation image and a natural-language task description as input and outputs a sequence of h intermediate frames (typically 7) representing a visual plan from the start state to task completion. The goal-conditioned policy (a CNN-based Diffusion Policy) takes a current observation image and one of the generated goal frames as input and outputs a chunk of h continuous actions (7-DoF for Libero/Calvin, 4-DoF for MetaWorld) or a single discrete navigation action (4-way for iTHOR). Critically, the video model is trained only on image sequences from demonstrations — no action labels are needed — while the policy is trained entirely from self-collected environment rollouts.

## Method

The method combines three components for unsupervised grounding. First, a frozen video model generates a sequence of goal frames from a start observation and task description; a goal-conditioned policy then attempts to reach each frame sequentially in the environment, and the resulting image-action pairs are stored in a replay buffer and used for training via hindsight relabeling — the actual environment states (not the video frames) become the ground-truth targets, so even imperfect rollouts yield useful training signal. Second, periodic random action bootstrapping injects initial and recurring random-action episodes into the replay buffer to prevent the policy from collapsing into degenerate behaviors and to provide basic world-dynamics coverage. Third, chunk-level action prediction (predicting h actions at once rather than one) produces temporally coherent exploration trajectories, reduces compounding errors, and lowers inference latency. The policy is trained with a standard MSE loss over action chunks sampled from the replay buffer, and the entire pipeline requires no rewards, action labels, or privileged information such as segmentation masks.

## Results

On Libero (8 tasks, 2 scenes), the proposed method achieves 42.2% and 62.0% overall success rate without any action labels, outperforming all action-free baselines (AVDC: 0.0%/0.3%) and most behavior-cloning baselines that require expert demonstrations. On MetaWorld (6 tasks), the method reaches 67.2% overall success, surpassing all baselines including DP BC* (51.7%) and AVDC (50.7%). On Calvin (4 tasks), the method achieves 84.4% overall, beating the best BC baseline (DP BC*: 68.8%) by 15.6 points. On iTHOR visual navigation (12 targets, 4 scenes), the method outperforms AVDC by 36% on average while performing on par with BC-based baselines that require action annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four categories of baselines. Against AVDC (Ko et al., 2023), the only other action-free method, the proposed approach wins decisively: on Libero, AVDC achieves 0.0% and 0.3% overall while Ours reaches 42.2% and 62.0%; on MetaWorld, Ours (67.2%) beats AVDC (50.7%); on iTHOR, Ours outperforms AVDC by 36% on average. Against behavior-cloning baselines (BC, GCBC, DP BC) that require expert action labels, Ours wins on MetaWorld (67.2% vs. best 52.8%), Calvin (84.4% vs. 68.8%), and Libero Scene 2 (62.0% vs. 49.2%), but slightly trails DP BC* on Libero Scene 1 (42.2% vs. 46.0%). Against SuSIE (Black et al., 2023), which uses an image-editing model for subgoal generation and requires action labels, Ours achieves higher success on both Libero scenes (42.2% vs. 29.8% and 62.0% vs. 19.2%). Against the RL baseline DrQ+LIV (Kostrikov et al., 2020; Ma et al., 2023) with zero-shot rewards on MetaWorld, Ours dramatically outperforms (67.2% vs. 1.3%), as the zero-shot reward signals are too ambiguous for effective RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from AVDC (Ko et al., 2023), which directly regresses actions from video via optical flow and depth-based rigid transforms, by instead learning a goal-conditioned policy through self-exploration in the environment, treating each generated video frame as a visual subgoal. It builds on the broader line of work using video models as world simulators or planning priors (Du et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024c) but specifically addresses the embodiment grounding gap that prevents zero-shot video-to-action transfer. The method combines hindsight relabeling from RL, action chunking from Diffusion Policy, and periodic random action bootstrapping into a unified unsupervised exploration pipeline that requires no action labels, rewards, or segmentation masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a policy learned purely through video-guided self-exploration can match or surpass behavior cloning trained on expert action demonstrations across 30 tasks in four environments, removing the need for expensive teleoperation data collection. Key limitations include reliance on simulation for exploration (real-world reset is an open problem), video model hallucinations that mislead the policy, and difficulty with precision manipulation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to real-world robots, whether larger pretrained video models (e.g., Sora-class) would reduce hallucination and improve generalization, and how to handle tasks requiring sub-millimeter precision where random exploration is insufficient.
