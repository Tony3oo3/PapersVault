## Problem

The feed-forward network (FFN) in Vision Transformers relies on simple linear projections that fail to capture rich second-order statistics in token features, yet existing improvements either inflate computational cost (GLU variants, extra CNN modules) or sacrifice performance through slimming (reduced hidden dimensions, block-diagonal projections). No prior method simultaneously improves feature learning quality and reduces complexity, leaving a persistent performance-versus-complexity paradox unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252794/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AFBO is a plug-in module that replaces the FFN in any existing ViT or MLP-Mixer backbone. It consumes d-dimensional token features (N tokens) and produces d-dimensional output features, with the expansion ratio M and group numbers G1/G2 tuned per backbone to match original model complexity. For classification, inputs are 224×224 RGB images yielding class labels; for detection/segmentation on COCO, inputs are resized to 800 on the shorter side and outputs are bounding boxes and instance masks via Mask R-CNN or RetinaNet heads.

## Method

AFBO replaces the first linear projection in FFN with a Spatial-Channel Factorized Bilinear Operation (SCFBO) that approximates second-order statistics via a rank-1 decomposition, splitting the computation into a spatial modeling branch (3×3 depthwise convolution by default) and a channel mapping branch (two point-wise convolutions followed by a dot product). To control complexity at large hidden dimensions, two structured-sparsity channel mappings are introduced: Grouped Cross Channel Mapping (GCCM), which shares a single linear mapping across paired output groups fed by different input groups, and Overlapped Cycle Channel Mapping (OCCM), which uses half-overlapped cyclic input groups to enable inter-group interaction. The final AFBO asymmetrically assigns OCCM to one branch and GCCM to the other, achieving a better accuracy-complexity balance than either strategy alone. The dot product is placed before the activation function (pre-act with SiLU) to avoid the sensitivity of power-function activations to squared bilinear noise.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, AFBO consistently improves 20 ViT/MLP-Mixer backbones: DeiT-T gains +2.4% Top-1 (72.2→74.6%), PoolFormer-S12 gains +1.9% (77.2→79.1%), Swin-T gains +0.9% (81.2→82.1%), and DeiT-B gains +0.7% (81.8→82.5%), all at comparable or lower parameter/FLOP budgets. On out-of-distribution benchmarks, gains are even larger: DeiT-T improves by +5.9% on ImageNet-R (32.6→38.5%) and +4.9% on ImageNet-C (71.1→66.2 mCE). On MS COCO, PoolFormer-S12+AFBO achieves 37.9 AP (RetinaNet) and 35.8 APm (Mask R-CNN), while Swin-T+AFBO reaches 42.1 AP and 41.1 APm, outperforming same-parameter CNN baselines like ResNet-50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFBO outperforms all direct FFN-replacement counterparts across every backbone tested. Against SwiGLU (EVA-02), AFBO gains +2.0% on DeiT-T (74.6% vs 72.6%) and +0.7% on DeiT-S (81.1% vs 80.4%) at matched complexity. Against ConvGLU (TransNext), AFBO leads by +0.4% to +1.0% on IN-1K and +0.8% to +1.0% on IN-R. Against IMLP, AFBO wins by +2.0% on DeiT-T and +1.1% on DeiT-S while using fewer parameters. Against SCHEME, AFBO achieves higher accuracy with fewer parameters (e.g., PoolFormer-S12: 79.1% at 12.0M vs SCHEME-12's 78.5% at 16.7M; Swin-T: 82.1% at 27.5M vs SCHEME-12's 81.7% at 36.9M). Against ConvNeXt block, AFBO leads by +1.0% on DeiT-T (74.6% vs 73.6%). In the appendix, AFBO also beats KAN-modified ViTs by +1.0% to +1.5% on IN-1K and +2.8% to +4.8% on IN-R, and outperforms GLU with M=4 expansion by +0.6% to +1.0% while using 25–30% fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on the second-order statistics modeling tradition from compact bilinear pooling (Gao et al., 2016) and factorized bilinear models (Li et al., 2017), transplanting the rank-1 decomposition of outer-product feature statistics into the FFN of vision transformers. It departs from the dominant line of FFN improvements—GLU variants (Shazeer, 2020; EVA-02) that trade hidden dimension for gating, and CNN-inspired add-ons (DW conv, channel attention, inverted residuals)—by instead modeling second-order channel correlations through a spatial-channel factorized bilinear operation. The structured-sparsity channel mappings (GCCM and OCCM) extend the group convolution idea (Chollet, 2017; Sandler et al., 2018) by reintroducing cross-group information interaction in a parameter-efficient manner, and their asymmetric combination is a novel architectural choice not present in prior FFN or bilinear-pooling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AFBO offers a practical, model-agnostic drop-in replacement for the FFN across a wide range of ViT and MLP-Mixer backbones, consistently improving both in-distribution accuracy and OOD robustness (up to ~6% on ImageNet-R) at comparable or lower complexity—a trade-off that prior slimming or GLU-based methods could not simultaneously achieve. The feature-entropy analysis (Swin-T: 2.7 → 4.7) provides a mechanistic explanation linking denser learned features to improved robustness, which is a useful diagnostic for the field. Open questions include whether the gains persist at foundation-model scale (the authors note future work on this), whether the second-order modeling benefit is specific to the bilinear formulation or generalizes to other higher-order statistics, and how the method interacts with modern pre-training paradigms (e.g., masked autoencoding) rather than supervised ImageNet training.
