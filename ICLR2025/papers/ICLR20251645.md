## Problem

Existing sequence models are predominantly limited to short contexts (4K–128K tokens), making them unable to process and reason over long temporal horizons involving millions of tokens in language or video. Training on such sequences is further hampered by quadratic attention memory costs, the scarcity of long-form training data, and the difficulty of jointly optimizing across heterogeneous modalities (text, image, video) at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251645/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts interleaved sequences of text (BPE tokens) and visual tokens (each 256×256 image or video frame is discretized into 16×16 = 256 VQGAN codes), with special delimiters (<vision>, </vision>, <eof>, <eov>) marking modality boundaries. It supports any-to-any generation: text→text, text→image, text→video, image→text, video→text, and unconditional image/video generation via classifier-free guidance. Videos are processed at 4 FPS with up to 1800 frames (≈1M tokens), and inference on 1M-token sequences requires a minimum TPU v4-128 mesh with 32-way tensor and 4-way sequence parallelism.

## Method

Starting from Llama2-7B, the paper trains a standard autoregressive transformer to 1M context using Blockwise RingAttention (fused with FlashAttention via Pallas) to eliminate the quadratic memory bottleneck while preserving exact attention. Context is extended progressively in powers of two (32K→128K→256K→512K→1M) with RoPE θ scaled proportionally at each stage, and Stage II further adds vision by tokenizing images/frames with a pretrained VQGAN and training on mixed text-image and text-video data from 1K up to 1M tokens. Two key training innovations address practical challenges: (1) masked sequence packing that prevents cross-example attention leakage and re-weights losses to match non-packed training, and (2) synthetic model-generated question-answering data (a short-context model produces QA pairs from book chunks, which are then concatenated into long sequences) to teach the model to attend to arbitrary positions. Loss balancing across modalities and a 25%-per-task batch allocation for the four downstream vision-language tasks preserve short-context accuracy during progressive long-context training.

## Results

On the Video-MME long video benchmark, LWM-1M (7B, ≤1800 frames) achieves 63.7 (medium, 4–15 min) and 60.8 (long, 30–60 min), making it the best-performing 7B model and competitive with much larger 72B models. On the LOFT retrieval benchmarks at 512K context, LWM substantially outperforms GPT-4o (128K) and Claude 3 Opus (200K), scoring 0.72 vs 0.21/0.32 on HotPotQA and 0.38 vs 0.23/0.37 on Quora. On multi-needle retrieval at 1M context, LWM-Text-1M achieves 0.84 (N=4, R=1), far exceeding Llama-3.1-8B (0.32), Qwen2.5-7B (0.0), and Mistral-7B (0.13). Short-context language tasks (ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU, OpenBookQA) show no degradation from context expansion, with scores matching or slightly exceeding the original Llama-2 7B baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On long video understanding (Video-MME), LWM-1M (63.7/60.8) clearly outperforms all 7B baselines—Video-LLaVA (38.1/36.2), Long-LLaVA (51.4/45.4)—and is competitive with 72B models like LLaVA-Video (68.9/61.5), trailing only Gemini 1.5 Pro (74.3/67.4) and GPT-4o (70.3/65.3). On the LOFT retrieval benchmarks, LWM at 512K context decisively beats both GPT-4o (128K) and Claude 3 Opus (200K), e.g., 0.72 vs 0.21 on HotPotQA, demonstrating that longer context enables superior document-level retrieval. At 1M context for multi-needle retrieval, LWM-Text-1M (0.67/0.84/0.69) dramatically outperforms Llama-3.1-8B (0.27/0.32/0.18), Qwen2.5-7B (0.0/0.0/0.0), and Mistral-7B (0.05/0.13/0.10), confirming that these shorter-context models cannot extrapolate to 1M tokens. On short-context image understanding (VQAv2, GQA, SQA), LWM (55.8/44.8/47.7) trails CLIP-based models like LLaVA-1.5 (78.5/62.0/66.8) and InstructBLIP (—/49.2/60.5), which the authors attribute to using discrete VQGAN tokens rather than continuous CLIP embeddings. Similarly, on short video QA (MSVD/MSRVTT/TGIF), LWM (55.9/44.1/40.9) is below Video-ChatGPT (64.9/49.3/51.4). The paper's core advantage is thus in long-context and long-video settings, where its 1M-token window provides a structural edge over all compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the authors' prior Blockwise RingAttention work (Liu et al., 2024; Liu and Abbeel, 2023) for memory-efficient long-sequence training, combined with progressive context extension inspired by GrowLength (Jin et al., 2023a) and RoPE θ-scaling from Code LLaMA (Rozière et al., 2023). It departs from the dominant CLIP-embedding paradigm in vision-language models (LLaVA, InstructBLIP, OpenFlamingo) by using discrete VQGAN tokens in a unified autoregressive 'tokens-in-tokens-out' framework, enabling any-to-any generation alongside understanding. The work is concurrent with Gemini 1.5 in reaching 1M-token context but differs in being fully open-source and in its specific training recipe (masked sequence packing, model-generated QA data, progressive multimodal extension).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By open-sourcing a complete family of 7B models and an optimized training pipeline for 1M-token sequences, this paper provides a practical, reproducible recipe that lowers the barrier for long-context research. Key open questions remain: whether findings at 7B scale transfer to 100B+ models, whether the VQGAN tokenizer's information loss (particularly for OCR and fine-grained detail) can be addressed by combining discrete tokens with continuous CLIP embeddings, and whether the observed trade-off between chat quality and retrieval precision (Table 11) can be resolved with better long-context conversational data. The paper also highlights that multi-needle retrieval degrades with task difficulty, suggesting 1M context utilization is not yet fully solved.
