## Problem

Existing preference alignment methods (RLHF, DPO) require substantial computational resources and large annotated datasets, making them impractical for the diverse and pluralistic nature of human preferences. Tuning-free alternatives like in-context learning, Best-of-N, and Linear Alignment either fail on specification-heavy tasks, incur high inference costs, or rely on first-order gradient approximations that are sensitive to prompt subtlety. The paper asks whether one can directly modulate token-level predictions during inference to enforce adherence to an arbitrary principle without any parameter updates.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the method requires two forward passes through the base LLM: one conditioned on the query x and principle c (constrained), and one conditioned on x alone (unconstrained), to compute the per-token reward. The principle c is a free-form natural language instruction (e.g., 'act as a poet with infectious charm' or 'speak rationally, logically, and rigorously'), and the output is a single aligned text response generated autoregressively. The only tunable hyperparameter is β, which controls the strength of principle adherence versus fidelity to the base model's predictions.

## Method

OPAD's core insight (Proposition 1) is that maximizing the KL divergence between the principle-constrained policy P_c and the unconstrained policy P serves as a tractable surrogate for minimizing the infeasible KL divergence between the true preference distribution P_data and P_c. The reward function r_θ(x, y_<t, c) is defined as the sum of log-ratios of constrained-to-unconstrained token probabilities over two consecutive time steps (t−1 and t), capturing sequential divergence dynamics. Solving the KL-constrained optimization (Eq. 5) yields a closed-form aligned policy p_θ that reweights the constrained distribution by exp(r/β) with a locally computable partition function, requiring no explicit sequence-level summation. This mechanism is mathematically analogous to a residual energy-based model but operates with token-level local normalization, enabling efficient autoregressive generation. The approach inherits the base model's knowledge while injecting a residual alignment signal that reshapes token distributions to reflect the target principle at every step.

## Results

On general alignment (HH-RLHF), OPAD achieves 41.5–42.0% win rates against Direct Prompting and 35.0–42.5% against ICL (GPT4-Turbo judged), while maintaining the lowest perplexity (12.49) among all methods. On personalized alignment (DSP), OPAD substantially outperforms all baselines, reaching 74.8% win rate vs. DP and 74.0% vs. ICL on Vicuna-7B. OPAD also demonstrates strong performance on RLHF-aligned Llama models, winning 70.8% vs. DP on DSP with Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, confirming its utility even for already-aligned models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPAD consistently outperforms Direct Prompting (DP) and Principle Prompting (PP) across all general alignment settings, with win rates of 40–54% vs. DP. Against ICL, OPAD wins decisively on Summarization (53.0% vs. 18.5% lose on Vicuna) but the gap narrows on HH-RLHF. BoN (Best-of-N with a trained reward model) is the strongest competitor: OPAD narrowly beats it on Summarization (Vicuna: 25.5% win vs. 24.0% lose) but loses on HH-RLHF with Vicuna (25.3% win vs. 36.3% lose), reflecting BoN's advantage when a well-trained reward model is available. Against training-based methods PPO and DPO, OPAD wins on Summarization but is competitive or slightly behind on HH-RLHF (e.g., PPO: 35.8% win vs. 42.3% lose on Vicuna HH-RLHF). On personalized alignment (DSP), OPAD dominates all baselines by a wide margin—74.8% win vs. DP, 74.0% vs. ICL, and 56.0% vs. PP on Vicuna-7B—while Self-CD and LA drop to 39.5% and 35.5% win rates respectively, confirming that OPAD's divergence-based reward is particularly effective for out-of-distribution, principle-specific tasks where gradient-approximation methods like LA struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPAD reconceptualizes the KL-constrained policy optimization objective from RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) into a purely inference-time setting, replacing the intractable minimization of KL to ground-truth preference data with a tractable surrogate: maximizing KL divergence between the principle-constrained and unconstrained token distributions. This positions it alongside other tuning-free decoding strategies—Self-Contrastive Decoding, Linear Alignment (Gao et al., 2024), and Best-of-N—but departs from them by deriving a closed-form token-level reward from a principled optimization rather than heuristic contrastive amplification or first-order Taylor approximations. The resulting aligned policy also bears a structural resemblance to residual Energy-Based Models (Parshakova et al., 2019; Deng et al., 2020), though OPAD's local (per-token) normalization makes the partition function tractable without sampling-based corrections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPAD offers a practical middle ground between expensive training-time alignment (RLHF/DPO) and weaker prompting-based approaches, enabling on-the-fly personalization without retraining—valuable given the pluralistic and domain-specific nature of real user preferences. However, the 2× inference cost (two forward passes per token) limits its applicability in latency-sensitive settings, and the authors acknowledge that strict principle adherence can produce formulaic outputs, leaving open the challenge of balancing compliance with creative flexibility. The finding that OPAD is most effective on mid-scale models (7B–13B) and yields diminishing returns on larger, already-well-aligned models suggests a clear niche but also a ceiling on its impact as base models improve.
