## Problem

Finite-particle SVGD convergence rates were a long-standing open problem: the only prior result (Shi & Mackey, 2024) yielded a rate of order 1/√(log log N), a double-exponential gap from the i.i.d.-optimal 1/√N, because the finite-particle dynamics lack the gradient structure that makes the mean-field KL-divergence monotone. This paper closes that gap by establishing O(1/√N) KSD rates and, for the first time, Wasserstein-2 convergence rates for non-Gaussian finite-particle SVGD.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a target potential V: ℝ^d → ℝ (defining π ∝ exp(−V)), a symmetric positive-definite kernel k (or a bilinear-plus-translation-invariant kernel for W2 results), and an initial joint density p₀^N on (ℝ^d)^N with sufficient regularity (C² for continuous time, supported on a sublevel set of V for discrete time). The outputs are theoretical rate bounds: expected KSD² averaged over time is bounded by O(d/N) in continuous time and O(d^{(3−α)/(4(1−α))} / N) in discrete time, while W2 rates for Matérn-type kernels scale as O(N^{−α/d}). The time-averaged particle marginals over [0, N] are shown to converge weakly to π and to become asymptotically i.i.d., yielding multiple approximate samples from the target.

## Method

The central insight is to track the KL-divergence between the joint N-particle density p^N(t) and the product measure π^⊗N, rather than the empirical-measure KL used in mean-field analysis. Differentiating this joint entropy along the N-body Liouville equation reveals a decomposition: the derivative equals −N·E[KSD²(μ^N(t)‖π)] plus a smaller positive term involving a kernel-dependent constant C*(z), yielding the O(1/√N) KSD rate after time-averaging. For discrete time, a Taylor expansion of the joint entropy along the linear interpolation path between successive iterates reproduces the same first-order KSD term, while the second-order remainder is controlled via a priori polynomial growth bounds on N⁻¹ΣV(x_i(n)) (Lemma 3) that handle the non-uniformly-bounded Jacobian of the SVGD map. For Wasserstein-2 convergence, a bilinear term ⟨u,v⟩ is added to the kernel, which provides uniform-in-N second-moment control via a Lyapunov argument; the resulting KSD rate is then translated to W2 using the moment-control framework of Kanagawa et al. (2022), with Matérn kernels giving explicit rates of order N^{−α/d}.

## Results

The paper establishes O(1/√N) KSD convergence rates for both continuous-time (Theorem 1) and discrete-time (Theorem 3) finite-particle SVGD, matching the i.i.d. empirical measure rate and representing a double-exponential improvement over the O(1/√(log log N)) bound of Shi & Mackey (2024). Under mild kernel and potential assumptions, the KSD bound scales linearly in dimension as O(d/√N). For Wasserstein-2 convergence with 'bilinear + Matérn' kernels, the paper derives rates of O(1/N^(α/d)) (Theorem 5), where α > 0 is dimension-independent, exhibiting a curse-of-dimensionality analogous to the i.i.d. setting. The discrete-time result requires T ≈ N^(2/(1−α)) iterations with step-size η ≈ d^{−(1+α)/(2(1−α))} N^{−(1+α)/(1−α)}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Shi & Mackey (2024), which achieved the first finite-particle KSD rate for SVGD at O(1/√(log log N)) due to double-exponentially growing mean-field approximation bounds. This paper's O(1/√N) rate constitutes a double-exponential improvement and matches the minimax-optimal i.i.d. empirical measure rate (Sriperumbudur 2016; Hagrass et al. 2024). Unlike Liu et al. (2024), which handles only the Gaussian SVGD case with bilinear kernels, the present results apply to general non-Gaussian targets under mild regularity. Compared to Das & Nagaraj (2023), who achieve polynomial rates for a modified 'virtual particle' algorithm with added randomness, this paper analyzes the original deterministic SVGD. The W₂ results (Theorems 4–5) are the first for non-Gaussian finite-particle SVGD; the curse-of-dimensionality in the W₂ rate (r(d) ≈ 1/18d) is shown to be unavoidable by analogy with i.i.d. Wasserstein bounds (Dudley 1969; Weed & Bach 2019). No empirical benchmarks are provided, as the contributions are entirely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SVGD convergence analysis lineage from Liu & Wang (2016) and the mean-field non-asymptotic rates of Korba et al. (2020) to the finite-particle setting, which had been a recognized open problem. The key departure is methodological: rather than composing mean-field convergence with finite-particle-to-mean-field error (as in Shi & Mackey, 2024, which yields a slow 1/√(log log N) rate due to double-exponential time dependence), the authors directly track the relative entropy of the joint N-particle density against π⊗N, revealing a clean decomposition into a dominant −N·KSD² term and a manageable positive remainder. For Wasserstein-2 convergence, the paper builds on Kanagawa et al. (2022)'s KSD-to-W2 bridge for bilinear-plus-translation-invariant kernels, applying it for the first time to non-Gaussian finite-particle SVGD dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1/√N) KSD rate is near-optimal (matching i.i.d. sampling rates) and represents a double-exponential improvement over the only prior finite-particle bound, closing a significant gap in the theoretical understanding of SVGD. The propagation of chaos result showing that time-averaged particle trajectories produce asymptotically i.i.d. samples from π is practically meaningful, as it justifies SVGD as a multi-sample generator without the autocorrelation issues of MCMC. Open questions include extending the Wasserstein-2 rates to discrete time (deferred by the authors), understanding whether the curse-of-dimensionality in W2 rates is fundamental or an artifact of the Matérn kernel choice, and whether the joint-density approach can yield sharper rates under stronger structural assumptions on the target.
