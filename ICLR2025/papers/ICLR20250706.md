## Problem

Learning ODEs whose solutions lie on unknown manifolds is challenging because existing neural-network-based approaches (autoencoders, NODEs, LDNets) produce latent-space dynamics that are not guaranteed to respect the manifold geometry, leading to trajectory drift and poor long-term prediction. Standard SINDy dictionaries additionally suffer from the curse of dimensionality, and the geometric constraints (tangent/normal spaces) must be estimated from data without prior knowledge of the manifold. This paper addresses both the representation problem (encoding manifold constraints in a scalable model) and the integration problem (solving the learned ODE while preserving the manifold).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a time series trajectory as a point cloud X = {x_i} in R^n along with an estimated intrinsic dimension d; derivatives are approximated by projecting consecutive-point differences onto locally estimated tangent spaces (y_i = T_i T_i^T (x_{i+1} - x_i)/Δt). The output is a vector field on the tangent bundle parameterized by an intrinsic d×d operator-valued kernel, and a geometry-preserving ODE solver (Euler+NC) that produces predicted trajectories constrained to the manifold. The key structural constraint is that the kernel's matrix-valued output at each point is a d×d matrix (not n×n), making the method scalable to very high ambient dimensions (tested up to n = 20402).

## Method

The core contribution is a geometrically constrained operator-valued kernel k(x,x') = ρ(x,x')P(x)P(x') where P(x) = T_x T_x^T projects onto the locally estimated tangent space, ensuring the learned vector field lies in the tangent bundle. By applying the Woodbury identity to the Gram matrix decomposition K = TRT^T, the ambient-space kernel is converted to an intrinsic d×d matrix kernel r(x,x') = ρ(x,x')T_x^T T_x', reducing the inversion cost from O((nN)^3) to O((dN)^3) regardless of ambient dimension n. Training is standard kernel ridge regression in the induced X(M)-valued RKHS, with the scalar kernel ρ chosen from SE or Matérn families. For time integration, the Euler+NC solver approximates the exponential map exp_{x_i}(f(x_i)Δt) by adding a normal correction term (Δt)²N_i g(T_i^T f(x_i)), where g is estimated via GMLS to capture the second fundamental form. A theoretical error bound (Theorem 3.1) shows the prediction error scales as O(ε + Δt(log N/N)^{ℓ/d}), guaranteeing manifold-constrained solutions in the large-data limit.

## Results

On the 2D cavity flow problem, GMKRR maintains prediction error at roughly 1/6 of Manifold-SINDy's error over the full 30,000-step horizon, while vanilla SINDy produces completely wrong kinetic-energy trends. On the high-dimensional CANDyMan benchmarks (Table 1), GMKRR achieves RMSE of 0.0255 on KS Beating (vs. 0.2744 for CANDyMan, a 10× improvement) with 3,500× faster training (0.014 s vs. 48 s). On KS Travelling, GMKRR-FFT reaches RMSE 0.367 (vs. 2.391 for CANDyMan, 6.7× better), and on Reaction-Diffusion (20,402-dim), GMKRR-Full achieves RMSE 7.4×10⁻⁴ versus CANDyMan's 0.1797 (≈240× improvement). The geometry-preserving Euler+NC integrator keeps trajectories on the manifold where RK2 and NODE visibly deviate, especially on distorted 1D manifolds (Fig. 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla SINDy (Brunton et al. 2016) on the cavity flow, GMKRR is decisively superior: SINDy's kinetic-energy prediction is completely wrong and its RMSE stays at a high, flat level, whereas GMKRR tracks the true fluctuation accurately up to t=150. Against Manifold-SINDy (Callaham et al. 2022), GMKRR reduces the cavity-flow RMSE to about 1/6 of M-SINDy's and avoids the spurious high-frequency oscillations that M-SINDy exhibits. Against CANDyMan (Floryan & Graham 2022), GMKRR wins on all three high-dimensional benchmarks: 10× lower RMSE on KS Beating with 3,500× faster training, 6.5× lower RMSE on KS Travelling (GMKRR-FFT), and ~240× lower RMSE on the 20,402-dimensional Reaction-Diffusion case. Against NODE (Chen et al. 2018) and LDNet (Regazzoni et al. 2024) on KS Travelling, GMKRR-FFT achieves RMSE 0.367 versus 6.690 (NODE) and 7.600 (LDNet), and GMKRR-Full (1.604) still beats both; NODE and LDNet degrade most in the long term because they do not enforce intrinsic-dimension constraints. On the 1D motivating example (Fig. 1), Euler+NC preserves the manifold constraint where RK2 deviates, and both NODE and LDNet drift off the manifold for D=0.1, though NODE matches GMKRR accuracy at D=0.5 at a much higher training cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SINDy framework (Brunton et al., 2016) by replacing its fixed polynomial dictionary with a geometrically constrained operator-valued kernel that projects predictions onto locally estimated tangent spaces, and it kernelizes the resulting regression into an RKHS to avoid the curse of dimensionality. The geometry-preserving integrator (Euler+NC) is positioned as a realization of Hairer's (2011) local coordinate approach, with the local parameterization estimated via GMLS rather than assumed analytically. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant neural-network paradigm (NODE, LDNet, CANDyMan) by showing that a linear kernel method with explicit geometric constraints can match or exceed nonlinear models on long-term prediction, and it generalizes prior scalar-valued kernel regression work (Baddoo et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2024) to the operator-valued setting required by tangent-bundle constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for high-dimensional dynamical systems (fluid mechanics, reaction-diffusion) where ambient dimensions reach 20,000+ and NN-based methods are prohibitively expensive to train; the intrinsic-space formulation via the Woodbury identity makes GMKRR scale with intrinsic dimension d rather than ambient dimension n. The theoretical error bound (Theorem 3.1) provides the first convergence guarantee for a data-driven manifold-constrained ODE solver, though it assumes i.i.d. sampling and leaves the more realistic time-series (non-i.i.d.) setting open. Key limitations include the need for accurate tangent space estimation (which degrades with sparse data), the assumption of a known or estimable intrinsic dimension, and the acknowledged phase drift in long-term predictions that is 'numerically unavoidable.' Future work on noisy data, parameterized dynamics, and pseudo-input strategies for very large N are natural next steps.
