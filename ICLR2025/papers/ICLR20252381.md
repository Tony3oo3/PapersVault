## Problem

Medical LVLMs suffer from factual hallucination that can lead to incorrect diagnoses, and while RAG offers a lightweight remedy, existing RAG-based approaches are dataset-specific (lacking generalizability across radiology, ophthalmology, and pathology) and introduce misalignment issues—both between modalities (the model ignores the input image and copies retrieved text) and between the model output and ground truth (irrelevant retrieved contexts cause previously correct answers to become wrong).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252381/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a medical image (chest X-ray, fundus photograph, or histopathology slide) and a free-form clinical query as input. The image is first routed to a domain-specific retriever that returns top-k similar radiology/pathology/ophthalmology reports from the training set, which are then concatenated with the query as textual context. The Med-LVLM (LLaVA-Med-1.5 7B) autoregressively generates either a binary yes/no answer for VQA or a free-text radiology/ophthalmology report; pathology images are excluded from report generation due to insufficient descriptive text.

## Method

MMed-RAG combines three modules: (1) a domain-aware retrieval mechanism where a fine-tuned BiomedCLIP classifier assigns each image to a modality (radiology, ophthalmology, pathology) and routes it to a domain-specific contrastive retriever (ResNet-50 vision encoder + BioClinicalBERT text encoder trained with CLIP-style InfoNCE loss), avoiding the performance degradation of a single general retriever; (2) an adaptive context selection step inspired by the Gap statistic that computes log-ratios of consecutive similarity scores and truncates retrieval at the first index where the ratio exceeds a threshold γ, dynamically discarding low-relevance contexts; and (3) a RAG-based preference fine-tuning (RAG-PT) stage that constructs DPO preference pairs targeting two misalignment modes—cross-modality pairs penalize correct answers derived from a diffusion-noised unrelated image (forcing reliance on the actual input image), while overall-alignment pairs reward correct use of helpful retrieved contexts and penalize interference from irrelevant ones—trained with a modified DPO loss that conditions the dispreferred response on the noisy input.

## Results

On medical VQA across five datasets (IU-Xray, MIMIC-CXR, Harvard-FairVLMed, Quilt-1M, PMC-OA), MMed-RAG achieves an average accuracy improvement of 18.5% over the LLaVA-Med-1.5 baseline (e.g., 89.54% vs. 75.47% on IU-Xray). On report generation, the improvement is 69.1% (e.g., BLEU 31.38 vs. 9.64 on IU-Xray). MMed-RAG outperforms the strongest RAG baseline (RULE) by 2.8% on VQA and 16.1% on report generation, and surpasses all decoding-based methods by 11.5% and 44.2% respectively. Against other open-source Med-LVLMs, MMed-RAG leads by a wide margin (e.g., 56.94% vs. 35.82% average accuracy in radiology vs. RadFM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMed-RAG outperforms all five decoding-based baselines (Greedy, Beam Search, DoLa, OPERA, VCD) across every dataset and metric; the best decoder (DoLa) reaches only 78.00% on IU-Xray VQA versus MMed-RAG's 89.54%. Among RAG-based methods, MMed-RAG beats RULE (the strongest prior RAG baseline) by 1.70 points on IU-Xray VQA accuracy and by 3.85 BLEU on IU-Xray report generation (31.38 vs. 27.53), with larger gaps on ophthalmology and pathology. Against other open-source Med-LVLMs, MMed-RAG leads by 21+ points in average accuracy (56.94% radiology vs. RadFM's 35.82%; 56.38% ophthalmology vs. miniGPT-Med's 25.28%). MMed-RAG also surpasses commercial models GPT-4o (63.25% on IU-Xray) and Gemini-1.5 (59.73%) by 16–26 points on VQA accuracy. On domain-specific models (RadFM, Quilt-LLaVA, RetinaVLM), MMed-RAG achieves higher scores in each respective domain, and applying RAG-PT alone to these domain-specific models yields significant gains (e.g., RadFM radiology VQA jumps from 26.67% to 48.39%), confirming the method's compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAG paradigm for medical vision-language models by combining three previously separate ideas: domain-specific contrastive retrieval (building on CLIP/BiomedCLIP), adaptive context selection inspired by the gap statistic from clustering, and DPO-based preference fine-tuning. It departs from prior medical RAG work (MedDr, FactMM-RAG, RULE) in two key ways: (1) it introduces a domain identification module to route images to modality-specific retrievers rather than relying on a single general retriever, and (2) it addresses both cross-modality misalignment (model ignoring the image) and overall misalignment (model over-relying on retrieved text) simultaneously through a unified preference fine-tuning objective, whereas RULE only targets over-reliance. The theoretical analysis (Theorems 4.1–4.2) provides formal guarantees that the preference loss increases the model's sensitivity to the image input and to relevant retrieved contexts while decreasing sensitivity to irrelevant ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantified misalignment rates (55% copy-reference, 43% over-reliance) reveal that naively adding RAG to medical VLMs is far more disruptive to alignment than previously acknowledged, and the 43.8% average factual accuracy improvement demonstrates that this disruption can be substantially mitigated. The domain-aware retrieval design is practically important for clinical deployment where radiology, pathology, and ophthalmology images coexist, though it requires retraining the domain classifier and retriever for genuinely new modalities. Open questions include whether a sufficiently large-scale MoE-based general retriever could replace the per-domain architecture, whether the gap-statistic threshold γ can be learned rather than fixed, and whether the preference fine-tuning approach transfers to settings where retrieved contexts are generated (e.g., from a separate LLM) rather than retrieved from a fixed corpus.
