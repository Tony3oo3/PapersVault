## Problem

Tree-Sliced Wasserstein distance on Systems of Lines (TSW-SL) replaces one-dimensional projection lines with tree structures to better capture topological information, but its splitting maps depend only on point positions while ignoring the tree system configuration, and the resulting metric fails to be invariant under Euclidean transformations—a fundamental property expected for any OT distance on Euclidean space. This paper addresses both deficiencies simultaneously by introducing a generalized class of splitting maps that incorporates positional information from both points and tree systems while guaranteeing E(d)-invariance.

## Input / Output

The method takes two empirical probability measures (sets of data points) in R^d and produces a single scalar distance value. The computation is parameterized by the number of sampled tree systems L, the number of concurrent lines k per tree system, and a temperature parameter δ that controls the sparsity of the distance-based splitting. Tree systems are sampled by drawing a common root point x from a distribution on a bounded subset of R^d and k direction vectors from the unit sphere (optionally orthonormalized for the Db-TSW⊥ variant), making the pipeline directly GPU-parallelizable with linear runtime in the number of data points.

## Method

The core contribution is a new class of splitting maps α: R^d × L_k^d → Δ_{k-1} that depend on both the point and the tree system, generalizing the point-only maps of TSW-SL. The key insight is that E(d)-invariance of the splitting map (α(gy, gL) = α(y, L)) is a sufficient condition for injectivity of the associated Radon Transform, which in turn guarantees Db-TSW is a valid metric. Concretely, the splitting map is defined via the Euclidean distance from each point to each line in the tree system: α(x,L)_l = softmax(δ · d(x,L)_l), where larger δ makes the assignment more sparse. The paper further proposes a simplified tree system design—k concurrent lines sharing a single root—that enables a highly parallelizable, GPU-friendly implementation with wall-clock time comparable to vanilla Sliced Wasserstein. Theoretical proofs establish that Db-TSW is an E(d)-invariant metric on P(R^d), and experiments on diffusion-based image generation (CIFAR-10), gradient flows (Swiss Roll, Gaussian 20d), and color transfer demonstrate consistent improvements over SW and TSW-SL baselines.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 unconditional generation (DDGAN), Db-TSW-DD⊥ achieves the best FID of 2.525, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art IWRPSW-DD (2.70) by 0.175, while maintaining comparable training time (162s vs. 152s per epoch). On gradient flow tasks, Db-TSW and Db-TSW⊥ achieve W2 distances of 3.01e-8 and 3.78e-8 on Swiss Roll at iteration 2500, surpassing vanilla SW (1.05e-3) by over three orders of magnitude and SWGG (3.57e-5) by two orders. On Gaussian 20d, Db-TSW reaches 4.60e-3 versus 10.30 for vanilla SW and 6.45 for SWGG at the final iteration, with runtimes (0.006s/iter) matching or beating all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Db-TSW and Db-TSW⊥ consistently outperform all baselines across all three experimental domains. On CIFAR-10 FID, both methods beat the strongest SW variant IWRPSW-DD (2.70) by 0.10 and 0.175 respectively, and surpass TSW-SL-DD (2.83) by a wider margin. On gradient flows (Swiss Roll and Gaussian 20d), Db-TSW achieves the best W2 distance at every evaluated iteration, outperforming SWGG (the next-best method) by roughly two orders of magnitude on Swiss Roll (3.01e-8 vs. 3.57e-5) and by a factor of ~1.4 on Gaussian 20d (4.60e-3 vs. 6.45), while vanilla SW and MaxSW are beaten by 3–4 orders of magnitude. LCVSW, despite competitive runtime, plateaus at 1.36e-3 on Swiss Roll, far behind Db-TSW's 3.01e-8. On color transfer, Db-TSW⊥ (0.12) and Db-TSW (0.21) approach the best QSW variants RRQDSW/RQDSW (0.08) without their randomization overhead, while dramatically outperforming SW and MaxSW (both 9.58). Computationally, Db-TSW matches or beats all baselines in wall-clock time, with the root-concurrent tree system design enabling linear runtime and high GPU parallelizability comparable to vanilla SW.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Tree-Sliced Wasserstein distance on Systems of Lines (TSW-SL) framework by Tran et al. (2025d), which itself combines the Radon Transform projection mechanism from Sliced Wasserstein (Bonneel et al., 2015) with the closed-form OT on tree metric spaces (Le et al., 2019). The key departure is generalizing splitting maps from functions of point positions alone, C(R^d, Δ_{k-1}), to functions of both point positions and tree system configurations, C(R^d × L_k^d, Δ_{k-1}), and imposing E(d)-invariance on these maps. This single invariance requirement simultaneously resolves TSW-SL's failure to be Euclidean-invariant and provides a clean sufficient condition for injectivity of the associated Radon Transform, replacing the more complex injectivity arguments needed for the older class of splitting maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The E(d)-invariance fix is important because Euclidean invariance is a natural and expected property for any OT metric on R^d, and its absence in TSW-SL was a fundamental theoretical gap. The concurrent-line tree system design (all lines sharing a single root) is a practical simplification over the chain-like structures in TSW-SL that enables GPU-parallelizable computation with linear runtime, making the method competitive with vanilla SW in wall-clock time. Open questions include whether techniques developed for SW variants (e.g., optimal projection selection, control variates) can be adapted to the tree-system setting, and whether the framework extends naturally to other geometric domains like spheres or hyperbolic spaces where the authors note concurrent TSW-SL variants are already emerging.
