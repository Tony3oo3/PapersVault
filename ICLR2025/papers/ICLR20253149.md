## Problem

Existing identity-conditioned diffusion models for synthetic face recognition suffer from context overfitting: a fixed identity context constrains not only identity but also identity-irrelevant attributes like expression, pose, and illumination, resulting in poor intra-class diversity and degraded face recognition accuracy. While the generative model inherently possesses the ability to produce diverse images (learned from real data with rich variations), this capability is suppressed when conditioned on a specific identity context.

## Input / Output

The method takes identity context vectors extracted by a pre-trained ArcFace model from real face images, along with a learnable empty context vector, as conditioning signals for a latent diffusion model operating in VAE-encoded latent space. The output is a synthetic face image that simultaneously preserves the target identity and exhibits diverse attribute variations. Non-existent identities are generated by first synthesizing face images with an unconditional model, extracting their identity contexts, filtering for inter-class discrepancy via cosine distance, and then using those contexts as generation conditions.

## Method

UIFace trains a standard latent diffusion model where the identity context is probabilistically (20%) replaced with a learnable empty context during training, enabling the model to generate both identity-preserving and identity-random images. At inference, a two-stage sampling strategy exploits the observation that early timesteps restore identity-irrelevant content while later timesteps recover identity-relevant details: the first stage samples with the empty context to unleash inherent diversity, and the second stage switches to the identity context with classifier-free guidance to lock in identity. An adaptive partitioning strategy determines the per-sample stage boundary by detecting when temporal differences in cross-attention maps exceed a threshold, eliminating the need for a fixed split point. An attention injection module further enhances diversity by normalizing the conditional cross-attention map using statistics from the unconditional cross-attention map, while directly replacing self-attention maps to preserve identity-irrelevant attribute variations without degrading identity consistency.

## Results

On the standard 0.5M synthetic image setting (10k identities × 50 images/ID), UIFace achieves 93.27% average face verification accuracy across LFW, CFP-FP, CPLFW, AgeDB, and CALFW, surpassing the previous best synthetic-based method CemiFace (92.30%) by nearly 1 point. When scaling to 1.0M images (20k×50), UIFace reaches 94.06%, and at 1.5M (30k×50) it attains 94.54%, approaching the 95.05% average of a model trained directly on the real CASIA-Webface dataset. Notably, UIFace achieves these results with fewer training identities (10k vs. 20k used by Arc2Face and DCFace at comparable scales).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UIFace outperforms all seven synthetic-based baselines at the 0.5M image setting, with the largest margins over older GAN-based methods (SynFace: +18.5 pts, DigiFace: +9.8 pts) and diffusion-based methods (IDiff-Face: +5.1 pts, DCFace: +3.7 pts). Against the two strongest recent competitors, CemiFace (92.30%) and Arc2Face (91.73%), UIFace leads by 0.97 and 1.54 points respectively on average accuracy. At the 1.0M scale, UIFace (94.06%) beats both CemiFace (93.07%) and Arc2Face (93.14%) despite using fewer total images (1.0M vs. 1.2M for Arc2Face) and fewer identities (20k vs. 20k+40k). The gap to the real-data reference (CASIA-Real, 95.05%) narrows to just 0.51 points at 1.5M synthetic images, and UIFace even surpasses CASIA-Real on CPLFW (90.67% vs. 90.18%). The paper attributes these gains to the two-stage sampling strategy and attention injection, which increase intra-class diversity (LPIPS 0.5592 vs. 0.5270 baseline; ImRecall 71.96 vs. 53.99) without sacrificing identity consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ID-conditioned latent diffusion framework established by IDiff-Face (Boutros et al., 2023) and the broader synthetic face recognition line (DCFace, ID3, CemiFace, Arc2Face), which condition a diffusion model on identity features extracted from a pre-trained FR encoder. The key departure is an inference-time two-stage sampling strategy: rather than modifying the training objective or network architecture to combat context overfitting (as prior methods do via partial dropout, style decoupling, or paired data), UIFace exploits the observation that identity-relevant details are restored only in later diffusion timesteps, and therefore uses a learnable empty context in early timesteps to unleash the model's inherent intra-class diversity before switching to the identity condition. An attention injection module further transfers spatial attention patterns from unconditional to conditional generation, a mechanism not present in prior ID-conditioned diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that UIFace narrows the gap between synthetic-data-trained and real-data-trained face recognition to within ~0.5% average accuracy (94.54% vs. 95.05%) using only 30k synthetic identities, offering a viable privacy-preserving alternative to scraping real face datasets. The two-stage sampling with adaptive partitioning is a lightweight, training-free inference technique that could potentially generalize to other conditional generation tasks where a fixed condition suppresses natural variation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger identity counts or more diverse attribute spaces, whether the pre-trained FR encoder bottleneck limits ultimate performance, and whether the CASIA-Webface training set (itself a privacy concern) can be replaced entirely.
