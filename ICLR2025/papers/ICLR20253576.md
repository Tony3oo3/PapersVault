## Problem

The state-of-the-art NN-based technology mapping for Boolean networks produces neural networks that are suboptimal in neuron and connection count, and the optimization process is too slow for industrial-scale circuits. Lossless optimization (preserving exact functional equivalence) is NP-hard, and existing compression techniques like pruning and quantization are either lossy or inapplicable to Heaviside threshold networks, leaving the problem of efficiently compressing these NNs while guaranteeing correctness unsolved.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Boolean network (a DAG whose non-PI vertices are labeled with k-input Boolean functions, derived from digital circuit netlists via K-LUT technology mapping) and produces a functionally equivalent neural network with compressed two-layer sub-NNs. The optimization operates on the multilinear polynomial (MP) representation of each vertex's Boolean function, producing minimal MP (MMP) representations that reduce hidden neurons and layer-one connections. Two output architectures are supported: an unmerged NN (depth 2d) optimized without depth constraints, and a layer-merged NN (depth d+1) optimized under a depth-preservation constraint via a leeway-based vertex selection algorithm.

## Method

The core insight is that MPs equivalent under the Heaviside threshold (σ-equivalence) yield functionally identical two-layer NNs, so one can search for a sparser MP within the σ-equivalence class of the original. Theorem 3.1 shows this class is exactly characterized by linear inequalities Aq ≤ b (using the Sierpiński matrix), enabling formulation as an integer linear program with an ℓ₁-relaxed sparsity objective (uniform or degree criterion). To accelerate the per-vertex optimization, the paper introduces an objective-aware NPN classification algorithm: BFs are grouped into NPN equivalence classes, the MMP is computed once for a canonical form, and inverse NPN transformations (with a permuted phase assignment to handle PN-invariance of the criterion) recover MMPs for all class members, reducing redundant ILP calls. Finally, an architecture-aware algorithm (optMaintainDepth) uses a leeway metric on the BN DAG to greedily select which vertices to compress as MMPs while guaranteeing the layer-merged NN depth does not increase, achieving up to 70% connection and 60% neuron reduction versus SOTA.

## Results

On four digital circuits (uart, sha3, ecg, aes), the proposed optAll algorithm reduces connections by up to 70% and neurons by up to 60% relative to the SOTA unmerged NN, while optMaintainDepth reduces connections by up to 50% and neurons by up to 20% on the layer-merged NN without increasing depth. The degree criterion consistently outperforms the uniform criterion in both settings. For optimization speed, the NPN-classification-based algorithm achieves up to 34.3× speedup over the naive per-vertex approach (sha3, K=10, uniform criterion) and up to 5.9× over the per-unique-function caching approach (aes, K=11, degree criterion).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the SOTA NN-based technology mapping of Gavier et al. (2023), which corresponds to 100% connections and 100% neurons in the paper's figures. The proposed optAll algorithm wins decisively on size, reducing connections by up to 70% and neurons by up to 60% while preserving functional equivalence (lossless). Under the depth-constrained optMaintainDepth setting, the method still achieves up to 50% fewer connections and 20% fewer neurons without increasing NN depth. For optimization speed, the paper defines two additional baselines: Naive (solving each vertex's MP/MMP independently) and Cached (solving per unique Boolean function). The NPN-classification algorithm consistently outperforms both for larger circuits (K ≥ 4), achieving 34.3× speedup over Naive on sha3 and 5.9× over Cached on aes. For small BNs or small K, NPN classification is competitive with but not always superior to Cached, as the overhead of computing NPN canonical forms is not amortized.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NN-based technology mapping pipeline of Gavier et al. (2023), which converts Boolean networks into Heaviside threshold neural networks but produces suboptimal representations. The key departure is formulating lossless compression as minimization within σ-equivalence classes of multilinear polynomials, characterized by linear constraints (Theorem 3.1), and solving via integer linear programming with an ℓ₁ relaxation. For acceleration, the paper repurposes NPN classification—a standard technique in FPGA logic synthesis (Zhou et al., 2020)—to exploit shared representations among subproblems, a cross-domain application not previously explored in the NN compression literature. Existing lossless compression results for ReLU and tanh networks do not apply to the Heaviside threshold architecture studied here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 70% connection and 60% neuron reductions, combined with up to 34.3× speedups, make NN-based circuit simulation more practical for industrial-scale Boolean networks and neurosymbolic hardware architectures. However, the underlying ILP remains NP-hard, and the ℓ₁ relaxation may yield suboptimal MMPs (acknowledged in Appendix C.1), leaving a gap between the relaxed solution and the true minimum. Open questions include scalability to circuits with very large K (where the ILP dimension grows as 2^k), whether the NPN acceleration generalizes to non-PN-invariant cost functions, and whether the depth-constrained optMaintainDepth algorithm can be made more aggressive without violating latency requirements.
