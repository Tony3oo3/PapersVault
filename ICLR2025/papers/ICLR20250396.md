## Problem

Existing protein structure generative models (Multiflow, Chroma, RFDiffusion) are limited to unconditional generation or motif scaffolding, offering no mechanism to control the higher-level spatial layout of generated proteins. This results in low diversity, over-representation of conceptually simple helix bundles (73% helicity vs. 42% in PDB), and poor compositional complexity, distinguishing protein generation from image generation where spatial control is commonplace.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250396/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered set of K 3D ellipsoids, each parameterized by a 3D mean, 3×3 covariance matrix, a secondary structure label (α-helix or β-sheet), and a residue count, plus a total protein length L. The ellipsoids can be hand-constructed, extracted from existing PDB structures via a simple spatial+semantic segmentation, or sampled from a lightweight statistical model. The output is a joint protein backbone (N residue frames in SE(3) with translations and rotations) and a 20-residue amino acid sequence, generated via a single flow-matching process over all three modalities simultaneously.

## Method

ProtComposer fine-tunes the pre-trained Multiflow flow-matching model by injecting ellipsoid conditioning through a novel Invariant Cross Attention (ICA) mechanism that performs SE(3)-invariant message passing from ellipsoid tokens to residue tokens using residue local frames, without requiring a canonical frame assignment for ellipsoids. Ellipsoid tokens are initialized from SE(3)-invariant quantities (residue count, squared radius of gyration, secondary structure type) and are mutually updated with residue tokens via concatenation before the transformer stack in each update block. A classifier-free guidance scheme interpolates between the unconditional Multiflow and the ellipsoid-conditioned model across all three flow modalities (translations, rotations, discrete residue types), with a specific self-conditioning interpolation strategy that outperforms alternatives. For unconditional generation, a simple statistical model over ellipsoid layouts (isotropic Gaussian means, Wishart covariances, and an inverse-square Mahalanobis repulsion energy) guarantees novel and diverse layouts, expanding the Pareto frontier between designability, novelty, and diversity far beyond what inference-time parameter tuning of Multiflow can achieve.

## Results

On ellipsoid adherence (Table 1), ProtComposer at λ=1.0 achieves Coverage 0.78, Accuracy 0.90, and Resegment JSD 0.13, approaching the oracle (0.89, 0.92, 0.15) and substantially outperforming Multiflow (0.49, 0.59, 0.43) and Chroma (0.59, 0.58, 0.48). On aggregate protein statistics (Table 2), ProtComposer at λ=2.0 recovers PDB-like helicity (47% vs. PDB 42%) and diversity (48 vs. PDB 41) while Multiflow remains over-helical (73%) and under-diverse (29). Compositionality (effective number of secondary-structure components) improves from 1.9 (Multiflow) to 3.2 (ProtComposer λ=2.0), approaching the PDB value of 4.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Multiflow (the base model being fine-tuned), ProtComposer at λ=1.0 more than doubles Coverage (0.78 vs. 0.49) and Accuracy (0.90 vs. 0.59) while halving Resegment JSD (0.13 vs. 0.43), confirming that ellipsoid conditioning provides strong spatial control absent in the unconditional model. Against Chroma (conditioned via its inference-time ShapeConditioner on a point cloud sampled from the same ellipsoids), ProtComposer wins on every adherence metric—e.g., Accuracy 0.90 vs. 0.58 and JSD 0.13 vs. 0.48—demonstrating that training-time conditioning yields tighter adherence than inference-time projection. Against RFDiffusion, the comparison is primarily on the designability–diversity–novelty Pareto frontier (Figure 4): ProtComposer's ellipsoid statistical model sweeps out a frontier that dominates RFDiffusion's temperature-sweep frontier in diversity and novelty at comparable designability, though RFDiffusion edges out ProtComposer slightly on compositionality (3.3 vs. 3.2 in Table 4). Overall, ProtComposer is the clear winner on controllable generation (ellipsoid adherence) and on the diversity/novelty/designability tradeoff, with the only minor concession being a small compositionality gap to RFDiffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Multiflow (Campbell et al., 2024), a joint sequence-structure SE(3) flow-matching model, by introducing a new conditioning mechanism—Invariant Cross Attention (ICA)—that injects 3D ellipsoid spatial layouts into the generative process. Conceptually, it transplants the spatial conditioning paradigm from image generation (bounding boxes, blobs, semantic maps) to protein structure generation, where no prior method offered comparable control over higher-level spatial arrangement. The classifier-free guidance is adapted from diffusion models to the flow-matching setting with non-Gaussian paths on SO(3) and discrete residue types, and the novel ellipsoid layouts are drawn from a deliberately simple statistical model (isotropic Gaussian means, Wishart covariances, and a pairwise Mahalanobis repulsion energy) rather than a learned generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProtComposer addresses a fundamental limitation of current protein generative models—the inability to control spatial layout—which restricts both diversity and practical design utility. The expanded Pareto frontiers between designability, novelty, and diversity, along with the correction of the helix-fraction bias (73% → ~47%, closer to PDB's 42%), make generated proteins more representative of natural protein space. Open questions include extending ellipsoid annotations beyond secondary structure to functional or binding-site specifications, and whether the simple statistical model for ellipsoids can be replaced by more expressive distributions without sacrificing the guaranteed novelty that motivates its use. The approach is currently limited to secondary-structure-level control and does not address atomic-level or all-atom design.
