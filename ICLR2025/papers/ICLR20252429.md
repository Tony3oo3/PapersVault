## Problem

In noisy partial label learning (NPLL), the ground-truth label of some instances is absent from their candidate label set, yet prior NPLL methods were designed empirically without theoretical guidance on what factors govern generalization. This paper provides the first generalization error bound for NPLL, identifying the noise rate and the mean candidate label set (CLS) size as the two key factors, and then designs a method that simultaneously reduces both.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for fine-grained and real-world datasets) paired with their candidate label sets, and outputs a multi-class classifier. Unusually, the CLS is not fixed: it is iteratively reconstructed during training by pruning low-confidence labels from normal samples and selecting high-confidence labels from the non-CLS for noisy samples. The method operates as a plug-in wrapper around any existing PLL loss (e.g., PaPi, PRODEN, CRDPLL), using BLIP-2 features and Faiss-based KNN (K=5) to compute pseudo-labels for the separation and reconstruction steps.

## Method

The core mechanism has two coupled components driven by the theoretical bound R(f̂)−R(f*) ≤ 2(1−(1−ε)/α)M + complexity terms. First, progressive sample separation computes an ECK metric (cross-entropy between CLS-normalized model predictions and KNN-based pseudo-labels) for each sample, then applies dual thresholds l and u that tighten over training epochs to partition samples into highly reliable normal, highly reliable noisy, and uncertain groups. Second, CLS reconstruction solves a per-instance optimization that minimizes CLS cardinality while maximizing pseudo-label confidence, using an instance-adaptive threshold β_i = β/max(q_i) so that high-confidence samples get more aggressive pruning. For normal samples the reconstructed CLS is drawn from the original CLS; for noisy samples it is drawn from the non-CLS; for uncertain samples one label is swapped. The two components iterate: improved model predictions sharpen the ECK separation, which in turn produces better CLS reconstructions, progressively reducing both ε and α.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with the highest ambiguity level (η=0.1), the proposed method achieves 79.98%, 79.21%, and 76.18% top-1 accuracy at noise levels γ=0.2, 0.3, and 0.4 respectively, outperforming the best baseline (ALIM-Scale) by 4.71%, 8.15%, and 11.57%. On CIFAR-10 with η=0.5, the method reaches 96.55%, 94.54%, and 82.63% accuracy at γ=0.2, 0.3, and 0.4, surpassing the best baseline (PiCO+) by 4.34%, 4.91%, and 7.04%. The method also serves as a plug-in, improving PaPi by up to 15.08% on CIFAR-100 (η=0.1, γ=0.4) and by 12.99% on the fine-grained Flower dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and noise/ambiguity setting tested. Among the PLL-only baselines (PRODEN, CC, CRDPLL, PaPi), the gap is largest under high noise: on CIFAR-100 (η=0.1, γ=0.4), the proposed method achieves 76.18% versus 55.60% for PaPi and 48.50% for CRDPLL. Among the NPLL-specific baselines, ALIM (NeurIPS'23) is the closest competitor, but the proposed method still leads by 4.71–11.57% on CIFAR-100 (η=0.1) and by 2.48–7.04% on CIFAR-10 (η=0.5). PiCO+ (TPAMI'24) is competitive on CIFAR-10 at low noise (92.21% vs. 96.55% at η=0.5, γ=0.2) but degrades sharply at higher noise (75.59% vs. 82.63% at γ=0.4). As a plug-in, the method consistently surpasses ALIM when combined with the same PLL backbone (e.g., PaPi+Ours achieves 81.85% vs. PaPi+ALIM-Onehot's 66.50% on CIFAR-10 at η=0.5, γ=0.4), confirming its generality across different PLL methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Noisy Partial Label Learning (NPLL) line initiated by Lv et al. (2024) and Shi et al. (2023), which relaxed the standard PLL assumption that the ground-truth label must reside in the candidate label set. Prior NPLL methods (PiCO+, UPLLRS, ALIM) address noise empirically—either by treating noisy samples as unlabeled data or by dynamically reweighting labels—but none provide a theoretical characterization of what governs generalization under NPLL. This paper departs from that empirical tradition by deriving the first generalization error bound for NPLL, identifying both noise rate and mean CLS size as controlling factors, and then designing a method (progressive dual-threshold separation plus instance-adaptive CLS reconstruction) that explicitly optimizes both. The plug-in architecture also positions it as a complement to, rather than a replacement for, existing PLL losses like PaPi, PRODEN, and CRDPLL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical bound gives the NPLL community a principled target (reduce ε and α simultaneously) that prior methods lacked, and the plug-in design means the approach can immediately boost any existing PLL method without architectural changes. The 11.57% gain on CIFAR100 under extreme noise, supported by ablations across multiple datasets and fine-grained/real-world benchmarks, suggests the CLS-reconstruction component is not a minor tweak but a substantive contribution. Limitations include the extra per-iteration cost of KNN-based separation and reconstruction (mitigated somewhat by the shrinking CLS accelerating disambiguation), reliance on a strong feature extractor (BLIP-2) for reliable KNN, and the fact that the theoretical bound, while informative, is not tight. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-vision modalities, whether the bound can be made data-dependent, and how the method behaves when the noise rate is very high (e.g., >50%).
