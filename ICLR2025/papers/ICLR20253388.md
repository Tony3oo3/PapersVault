## Problem

Existing 1-bit post-training quantization methods for LLMs produce a significant distribution shift between binarized and full-precision weights, as the binarization parameters are solved only once from the initial weight distribution. Additionally, these methods rely on row-wise scaling factors that fail to capture the pronounced column-wise deviations present in LLM weight matrices, and the integration of calibration data into the binarization parameter update remains underexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253388/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes full-precision LLM weight matrices (e.g., LLaMA-7B/13B/70B, OPT-1.3B–66B, Vicuna-7B/13B) along with a small calibration set of 128 samples from C4 (sequence length 2048, batch size 128). The calibration data is pre-compressed into a 2D second-moment matrix S ∈ R^{m×m} to avoid repeated high-dimensional tensor multiplications during iterative updates. The output is a binarized weight representation at ~1.1 bits per weight, structured as binary matrices with row-wise and column-wise scaling factors, plus a column-group bitmap encoding salient/non-salient and sparse/concentrented partitions.

## Method

ARB-LLM introduces an alternating refined binarization (ARB) algorithm that iteratively updates the mean, scaling factor, and binary matrix by setting partial derivatives of the quantization error to zero, progressively aligning the binarized weight distribution with the full-precision one (Theorem 1 proves monotonic error reduction). Two key extensions follow: ARB-X incorporates calibration data into parameter updates via a reformulated error L₂ = Tr(RSRᵀ) that achieves a ~389× speedup over naive computation (Theorem 2), while ARB-RC replaces the row-wise mean with a column-wise scaling factor to handle LLM column deviations, eliminating the redistribution parameter to reduce storage. A Column-Group Bitmap (CGB) strategy refines the partitioning by extending the salient-column bitmap along the row axis and combining it with the group bitmap, enabling second-order binarization for both salient and non-salient weight groups without extra bitmap storage. The full pipeline (ARB-LLM_RC = ARB-RC + CGB) is the first binary PTQ method to surpass same-size FP16 models on zero-shot QA benchmarks.

## Results

On the OPT family, ARB-LLM_RC (1.11 weight bits) achieves a perplexity of 10.30 on OPT-66B, surpassing the FP16 baseline (9.34) on zero-shot QA and outperforming GPTQ at 3-bit (10.55). On LLaMA-7B, ARB-LLM_RC reduces WikiText2 perplexity to 14.03, a 71.8% improvement over BiLLM (49.79) at the same ~1.09 weight bits. On Vicuna-13B, ARB-LLM_RC achieves 13.38 perplexity versus BiLLM's 43.39, a 69.1% reduction. The paper reports that ARB-LLM_RC is the first 1-bit PTQ method to surpass same-size FP16 models on 7 zero-shot QA datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BiLLM (the SOTA binary PTQ method), ARB-LLM_RC achieves substantial perplexity reductions across all model families: 71.8% on LLaMA-7B (14.03 vs 49.79), 69.1% on Vicuna-13B (13.38 vs 43.39), and 14.5% on OPT-66B (10.30 vs 12.05), all at the same ~1.1 weight bits. Against GPTQ at 3-bit, ARB-LLM_RC outperforms on OPT-66B (10.30 vs 10.55) and LLaMA-7B (14.03 vs 8.63 is a loss, but on LLaMA-65B it achieves 6.56 vs 4.17, also a loss; however on OPT-66B it wins). Against PB-LLM (1.70 bits), ARB-LLM_RC wins by a wide margin on every model, e.g., 10.30 vs 27.66 on OPT-66B and 14.03 vs 82.76 on LLaMA-7B, while using fewer weight bits. The paper also reports that ARB-LLM_RC surpasses same-size FP16 models on 7 zero-shot QA datasets (ARC-c, ARC-e, BoolQ, HellaSwag, OBQA, PIQA, Winogrande), a first for a 1-bit PTQ method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the binary post-training quantization (PTQ) line initiated by PB-LLM and BiLLM, which introduced salient-weight identification and second-order binarization for LLMs. The key departure is the alternating refined binarization (ARB) algorithm, which iteratively updates the mean, scaling factor, and binary matrix to close the distribution gap between binarized and full-precision weights—a refinement step that prior one-shot binarization methods (BWN, XNOR-Net, BiLLM) did not perform. The paper also departs from standard row-wise binarization by introducing column-wise scaling (ARB-RC) motivated by observed column deviations in LLM weight distributions, and incorporates calibration data into parameter updates (ARB-X), a dimension previously underexplored in binary PTQ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 1-bit compression while matching or exceeding FP16 accuracy on zero-shot QA benchmarks is a practical milestone for deploying LLMs on resource-constrained devices, where a 70B model's 150 GB memory footprint is prohibitive. The alternating refinement principle is general and could inform other extreme quantization settings beyond LLMs. However, the perplexity gap to FP16 remains (e.g., 10.30 vs. 9.34 on OPT-66B), and the method's reliance on calibration data and its specific motivation from LLM column-deviation patterns raise open questions about generalizability to other architectures or training regimes. The time overhead of iterative refinement, while modest (~20 min extra for 15 iterations), may become non-trivial at scale or for frequent re-quantization.
