## Problem

Size-based scheduling policies like SRPT can reduce average LLM request completion time, but two obstacles prevent their practical adoption: (1) output length prediction from prompts is either inaccurate (BERT-based) or resource-intensive (separate LLM), so state-of-the-art systems default to FCFS with head-of-line blocking; and (2) preemption in LLM systems incurs substantial KV cache memory overhead for maintaining intermediate states of unfinished requests, a cost that standard SRPT analysis ignores.

## Input / Output

The system consumes user text prompts and, at each decoding iteration, the 4096-dimensional hidden-state embedding from a single intermediate Transformer layer (layer 11 of Llama3-8b-instruct). The initial length estimate comes from a BERT model on the prompt, then the MLP classifier (2.1M parameters, two linear layers with ReLU) maps each layer-11 embedding to a probability vector over 10 equal-width bins spanning 0–512 remaining tokens. A Bayesian smoothing step with a banded transition matrix refines this distribution iteratively, and the resulting predicted remaining length drives the scheduler's preemption and ordering decisions at every token-level iteration.

## Method

TRAIL's key insight is to 'recycle' the LLM's own intermediate layer embeddings as probing features for a lightweight length predictor, eliminating the need for a separate prediction model. At each decoding step, the embedding from layer 11 is fed to a small MLP that classifies remaining length into 10 bins; a Bayesian update with a nearest-neighbor transition matrix smooths the probability distribution across iterations, achieving 2.66× lower MAE than BERT prompt predictions. For scheduling, TRAIL implements SPRPT with limited preemption: a request of predicted size r can only be preempted during its first ⌊c·r⌋ iterations (default c=0.8), so early preemption is cheap (small KV cache) while late-stage preemption is forbidden to avoid memory overflow. The paper derives a closed-form mean response time formula for this policy via the SOAP queueing framework in an M/G/1 model, and integrates the full pipeline into vLLM with chunked prefill, yielding 1.66×–2.01× lower mean latency and up to 24× lower mean TTFT over vanilla vLLM-FCFS.

## Results

TRAIL achieves 1.66x to 2.01x lower mean latency and 1.76x to 24.07x lower mean time-to-first-token (TTFT) compared to vanilla vLLM (FCFS) on the Alpaca dataset with Llama3-8b-instruct. The refined embedding-based length predictions (from layer 11) achieve 2.66x lower mean absolute error than BERT prompt-based predictions. The optimal preemption parameter c=0.8 yields the best latency and TTFT, while c=1 (unlimited preemption, equivalent to standard SRPT) performs worse due to KV cache memory overhead. Under burst workloads, TRAIL maintains its advantage over vLLM by ranking all requests (running and waiting) by predicted length rather than prioritizing new arrivals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRAIL is compared against two vLLM-based baselines: (1) vLLM-FCFS, which uses first-come-first-served scheduling, and (2) vLLM-SJF_BERT, which uses Shortest Job First based on BERT output-length predictions with chunked prefill. TRAIL (c=0.8, refined embedding predictions) dominates both baselines on mean and median latency and TTFT across all request rates, achieving up to 2.01x lower mean latency and up to 24.07x lower mean TTFT versus vLLM-FCFS. Notably, vLLM-SJF_BERT provides only minimal improvement over vLLM-FCFS because vLLM's implementation prioritizes incoming requests over existing running ones, effectively negating the SJF ordering. TRAIL-BERT (using the same BERT predictions but with limited preemption) still outperforms vLLM-SJF_BERT, demonstrating that the limited-preemption scheduling mechanism itself — independent of prediction quality — contributes meaningfully to latency reduction. The gap between TRAIL and TRAIL-BERT further confirms that the embedding-based refined predictions (2.66x lower MAE) translate into additional serving gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRAIL sits at the intersection of two established research threads: LLM inference serving (vLLM, Orca) and prediction-based scheduling (SPRPT/SPJF from Mitzenmacher 2019, Shahout & Mitzenmacher 2024). It departs from prior length-prediction approaches (S3's BERT, Zheng et al.'s separate LLM) by 'recycling' the target LLM's own intermediate layer embeddings as input to a lightweight classifier, drawing on the probing literature (Belinkov 2022, Hewitt & Liang 2019). The scheduling contribution extends SPRPT with a limited-preemption variant (parameter c) specifically motivated by KV cache memory constraints in LLM systems, and the authors derive a closed-form mean response time via the SOAP framework (Scully & Harchol-Balter 2018), which had not been applied to this preemption-limited setting before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The embedding-recycling prediction is a practical, low-overhead technique (≈0.03% FLOPs) that any LLM serving system could adopt without deploying a separate prediction model, and the limited-preemption insight (c=0.8 optimal) directly addresses a real deployment constraint that prior scheduling theory ignored. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (Alpaca), a single model (Llama3-8b), and a single GPU, leaving open whether the optimal layer (11) and c value transfer across architectures, datasets, and multi-GPU settings. The paper also acknowledges that dynamic retraining of the classifier and multi-layer embedding fusion are needed to handle data drift and improve accuracy, and the theoretical model (single-server M/G/1) does not capture the multi-request, memory-constrained reality of LLM serving.
