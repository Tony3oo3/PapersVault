## Problem

Most graph representation learning work targets undirected graphs, while causal/directed graph embedding methods either capture directionality without distance information or rely on fixed spacetime geometries (e.g., Minkowski or De Sitter space) with a single time dimension. No existing framework provides a trainable, differentiable geometry that simultaneously encodes edge weights as spatial distances and edge directionality as causal ordering in a unified continuous representation space with multiple time dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252028/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes node feature vectors in R^N, a binary non-symmetric directed adjacency matrix A (where A_uv=1 implies A_vu=0 for DAGs), and a directed edge weight matrix W_D. It produces node embeddings as events in a product manifold R^{D+T}, a learned quasi-metric D operating on the D spatial dimensions, and a learned partial order T operating on the T temporal dimensions. Training optimizes only over the one-hop neighborhood (local geometry), using the adjacency matrix as a mask in the loss, rather than requiring expensive global shortest-path geodesic distances.

## Method

The Neural Spacetime (NST) is a triplet S=(E, D, T) trained end-to-end: an MLP encoder E maps node features to an intermediate Euclidean space R^{D+T}, a neural quasi-metric D (built from iterative applications of a fractalesque activation σ_{s,l} with separate small-scale and large-scale exponents, combined with invertible positive matrices) learns the spatial distance geometry, and a neural partial order T (using LeakyReLU and σ_{s,s} activations with invertible positive matrices) learns the temporal causal ordering. The key architectural insight is decoupling the representation into a product manifold where space carries a quasi-metric (relaxed triangle inequality) and time carries a partial order, ensuring only valid geometries are learnable. The training loss combines a masked MSE distance loss (matching predicted quasi-metric distances to edge weights for adjacent nodes) and a causality loss using SteepSigmoid activations that penalize violations of the temporal partial order. The main theoretical result guarantees that any k-point DAG embeds into an NST with 1+O(log(k)) distortion while exactly preserving causal structure, improving to O(log(k)+2) spatial and 2 temporal dimensions for posets with planar Hasse diagrams.

## Results

On synthetic weighted DAGs (Table 1), NSTs achieve near-perfect distortion (1.00±0.03 to 1.00±0.05) and perfect directionality (1.0) at 10 embedding dimensions across all tested metrics, with distortion decreasing monotonically as dimension increases from 2 to 10. On real-world networks (Table 2), NSTs achieve distortion of 1.00±0.04 to 1.00±0.05 at 4–10 dimensions on all six datasets (Cornell, Texas, Wisconsin, In silico, E. coli, S. cerevisiae), with directionality ranging from 0.89 to 1.00. The maximum distortion for NSTs stays below 1.73 at 10 dimensions, whereas De Sitter baselines exhibit maximum distortions exceeding 100,000 on several datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares NSTs against two fixed-geometry baselines from Law & Lucas (2023): Minkowski spacetime (one time dimension) and De Sitter spacetime. On the synthetic DAG benchmarks (Table 1), NSTs achieve distortion of 1.00±0.03 to 1.00±0.05 at 10 dimensions with perfect directionality (1.0), while the fixed-geometry baselines are not directly tabulated there but are discussed qualitatively. On real-world networks (Table 2), NSTs consistently outperform De Sitter space, which exhibits catastrophic failures with negative mean distortions (e.g., −129.13±9623.30 on In silico at dim 10) and maximum distortions exceeding 200,000. Against Minkowski space, NSTs match or slightly exceed performance on average distortion (both reach ~1.00 at 10 dimensions on most datasets), but NSTs achieve substantially lower maximum distortion (e.g., 1.32 vs. 3.69 on In silico, 1.17 vs. 1.01 on E. coli) and equal or better directionality. The key advantage of NSTs is their stability: their distortion variance is orders of magnitude smaller than De Sitter's, and they avoid the extreme outliers that plague fixed-geometry approaches, particularly on gene regulatory networks where De Sitter fails completely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural snowflake framework (Borde & Kratsios, 2023) for undirected graph embedding by coupling it with a learnable partial order to handle directed acyclic graphs, and generalizes fixed-geometry spacetime representation learning (Law & Lucas, 2023) by making the spacetime manifold itself trainable rather than choosing a priori from Minkowski or De Sitter spaces. The key architectural departure is the product-manifold decomposition into a neural quasi-metric (space) and a neural partial order (time), with multiple time dimensions (T > 1) enabling the encoding of anti-chains—something impossible with the single-time-dimension Lorentzian geometries used in prior spacetime embedding work. The theoretical grounding draws on fractal geometry, doubling metrics, and poset theory (Dilworth's theorem, Baker et al.'s planar characterization), connecting representation learning to classical metric embedding results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universal embedding theorem (Theorem 1) providing O(log k) distortion with O(log k) spatial dimensions and W time dimensions is a clean theoretical guarantee that no prior spacetime embedding work offered, and the planar-Hasse-diagram refinement (Theorem 2) showing 2 time dimensions suffice for a broad practical class is a useful structural characterization. Practically, the near-zero distortion (1.00±0.04) on real-world WebKB and gene regulatory networks demonstrates that learnable geometries substantially outperform fixed spacetimes, especially in low dimensions where Minkowski/De Sitter baselines degrade severely. Key limitations include restriction to DAGs (not arbitrary digraphs with cycles), the computational difficulty of global optimization for large graphs, and the fact that directionality scores on real-world data (0.89–0.94) fall short of the synthetic 1.00, suggesting the framework's theoretical guarantees are not yet fully realized in practice.
