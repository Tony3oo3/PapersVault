## Problem

Planning with temporally extended actions (options) in reinforcement learning has relied on either predefined options requiring deep environment knowledge or options learned from expert demonstrations unavailable for many environments. Moreover, existing methods that plan with options must recurrently execute each primitive action within an option to obtain intermediate states, inflating computational cost during search. This paper asks whether options can be discovered autonomously through self-play and used efficiently in model-based planning without any human-provided structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4-frame-stacked RGB observation from an Atari game (or a grid map in GridWorld) and produces a search policy over both primitive actions and options, a dominant option sequence of up to L actions, and the composite action (primitive or option) to execute in the environment. The option network outputs L conditional distributions (one per step in the option) plus a virtual 'stop' action, from which a single dominant option is derived per state. The modified dynamics network accepts a composite action encoded as L planes (zero-padded for shorter options) and directly outputs the next hidden state and accumulated discounted reward, bypassing intermediate states.

## Method

OptionZero integrates an option network into MuZero's prediction network, which predicts the 'dominant option'—the longest action sequence whose cumulative probability exceeds 0.5—using L conditional distributions and a stop action to avoid exponential option enumeration. The dynamics network is extended to simulate multi-step transitions given a composite action (option), returning the terminal hidden state and accumulated discounted reward in a single forward pass rather than recurrently unrolling each primitive step. In MCTS, option edges bypass intermediate nodes, and a two-stage selection (primitive PUCT, then option-vs-primitive PUCT with adjusted statistics) determines whether to traverse via the option or the primitive path. Training proceeds via self-play with a modified loss that adds L cross-entropy terms for the option network, where the target is the actual composite action executed in the environment (or 'stop' if the predicted option diverges). The key design choice is restricting to a single dominant option per state, which keeps the option space linear in L while still capturing the most probable temporally extended behavior.

## Results

On 26 Atari games, OptionZero with maximum option length 3 (ℓ₃) achieves a mean human-normalized score of 1054.30% and median of 391.69%, representing a 131.58% improvement in mean over the MuZero baseline (ℓ₁: 922.72%, 328.40%). OptionZero with maximum option length 6 (ℓ₆) achieves 1025.56% mean and 329.77% median. Out of 26 games, ℓ₃ outperforms the baseline in 20 games and ℓ₆ in 17 games, with the largest gains in up n down (+63,810.83) and qbert (+13,592.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is MuZero (ℓ₁, no options), which serves as both the architectural foundation and the direct performance comparison. OptionZero with L=3 (ℓ₃) outperforms MuZero in 20 out of 26 games, achieving a 131.58% improvement in mean human-normalized score (1054.30% vs. 922.72%) and a 63.29% improvement in median (391.69% vs. 328.40%). OptionZero with L=6 (ℓ₆) wins in 17/26 games with a 111.84% mean improvement. The four games where ℓ₃ underperforms (bank heist, gopher, krull, private eye) are attributed to the dynamics network struggling with complex action spaces or visually complex observations. An ablation (n-ℓ₃, options used only in MCTS planning but not executed in the environment) still achieves 1008.15% mean, confirming that the planning benefit of options is independent of their execution in the environment, though full OptionZero (ℓ₃) further improves to 1054.30% by also executing options during self-play.

## Relation to Prior Work

OptionZero extends MuZero by integrating an option network into the prediction head and modifying the dynamics network to simulate multi-step transitions directly, eliminating the need to recurrently unroll each primitive action within an option during MCTS. This departs from prior options-in-MCTS work (de Waard et al., 2016; Gabor et al., 2019; Czechowski et al., 2021) that relied on predefined options or expert demonstration data, and from option-critic architectures (Bacon et al., 2017) that learned options but did not incorporate them into tree search. The 'dominant option' formulation—learning a single most-likely temporally extended action per state via cumulative probability thresholds—avoids the exponential combinatorial explosion of enumerating all option sequences while remaining compatible with MuZero's self-play training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 131.58% mean human-normalized score improvement over MuZero on 26 Atari games demonstrates that autonomous option discovery can meaningfully enhance model-based planning without human-designed abstractions, and the behavioral analysis reveals game-specific strategic skills (e.g., coordinated bomb-planting sequences in Hero) that increase interpretability. However, the method does not improve performance in all games—particularly those with large action spaces or visually complex observations where the dynamics network struggles to learn multi-step transitions—limiting its universality. Open questions include whether dynamically extending the maximum option length, integrating with alternative dynamics models (S4, Dreamer), or applying to two-player strategic games could address these limitations and broaden applicability beyond Atari.
