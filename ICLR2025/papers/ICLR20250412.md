## Problem

Static multi-modal benchmarks for LMMs suffer from test data contamination as training corpora grow, inflating reported performance and obscuring true model capabilities. No existing benchmark is simultaneously fully automated (no human-in-the-loop), contamination-free, multi-modal, and scalable to continuous updates without prohibitive re-evaluation costs for all participating models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250412/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests ArXiv PDFs from predefined scientific domains (cs.CV, eess.SY, q-bio.BM, etc.), parses them with the DeepSearch toolkit to extract figure/table placements, captions, and table contents, then crops the relevant visual elements. GPT-4o receives each figure (or table image plus its markdown-rendered data) along with its caption to generate multiple-choice questions. The first LiveXiv version yields 16,328 questions (7,328 VQA from figures, 9,000 TQA from tables) across 250 papers in 10 domains, growing by ~16K questions monthly. Evaluation outputs are per-model accuracy scores and IRT-based performance estimates for models not directly re-evaluated.

## Method

LiveXiv is a fully automated pipeline with three stages: (1) data acquisition and VQA generation, where ArXiv PDFs are parsed, figures are classified into Block Diagram/Chart/Qualitative via CLIP meta-prompting, and GPT-4o generates questions using a two-step Chain-of-Thought process (figure description followed by ConMe-adapted question prompts); (2) a two-stage filtering phase that first runs a blind test with Llama-3.1-70B (no image provided) to remove ~30% of questions answerable from text alone, then checks GPT-4o–Claude-Sonnet agreement on remaining answers to reduce hallucinated ground truths by 38.5% while retaining 93.85% of valid pairs; (3) an efficient evaluation framework based on the Rasch IRT model, where only 3–5 models are re-evaluated on each new benchmark version (selected via a Fisher information criterion over quantile-spaced difficulty levels), and the performance of all other models is estimated from their skill parameters and the new items' difficulty parameters, reducing evaluation cost by ~75–85%.

## Results

On LiveXiv v1 (16,328 questions from 250 ArXiv papers across 10 domains), Claude-Sonnet achieves the highest accuracy at 75.4% (VQA) and 83.5% (TQA), followed by Qwen2-VL (60.2% VQA, 62.1% TQA) and InternVL2-8B (62.3% VQA, 60.2% TQA). The benchmark is notably challenging: the best open-source model (Qwen2-VL) scores only ~60%, and the weakest model (InstructBLIP-7B) scores just 23.6% VQA / 19.1% TQA. A manually verified 1,000-sample subset confirms the automated pipeline's reliability, with average performance deviation of only 2.3% (VQA) and 2.1% (TQA) compared to the full dataset. The IRT-based efficient evaluation method shows that re-evaluating just 3–5 of 19 models per version yields accurate performance predictions (MAE and Spearman rank correlation validated across LiveXiv v1–v4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between LiveXiv's contamination-free rankings and those from three established static benchmarks (DocVQA, ChartQA, AI2D). On the static benchmarks, IXC2.5-7B and IXC2-4KHD-7B rank 5th and 6th (average rank 5.00 and 6.33), but on LiveXiv they drop to 9th and 10th (average rank 9.50 and 10.50), a decline of 4.5 and 4.17 points respectively — evidence of potential test-data contamination inflating their static-benchmark scores. Conversely, LLaVA-OneVision-7B improves from rank 8 (static) to 6.5 (LiveXiv), and Phi3v improves from 9 to 6, suggesting their true capabilities were underestimated on contaminated benchmarks. Claude-Sonnet maintains rank 1 on both static and LiveXiv, while Qwen2-VL and InternVL2-8B hold ranks 3 and 2–3 consistently. The paper also validates LiveXiv against a manually curated 1,000-sample subset, showing that the automated GPT-4o + Claude-Sonnet generation-and-filtering pipeline introduces negligible error (<2.5% average absolute deviation), confirming the benchmark's reliability without human annotation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiveXiv extends the contamination-free benchmark line (LiveBench, LiveCodeBench, LMMs-Eval LiveBench) by making the pipeline fully automated and multi-modal, eliminating the human-in-the-loop verification that limits LMMs-Eval LiveBench's scalability. For question generation, it adapts ConMe's prompt design to scientific figures and tables, and for efficient re-evaluation it ports the IRT/Rasch-model framework from tinyBenchmarks and PromptEval (which target LLMs) to the multi-modal, continuously-evolving setting. The paper departs from static benchmarks (MME, MMMU, DocVQA, ChartQA, AI2D) by sourcing questions from ever-growing ArXiv content, and from prior efficient-benchmark work by selecting which *models* to re-evaluate (rather than which *samples*) using a Fisher-information criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiveXiv addresses the increasingly critical problem of test-set contamination in LMM evaluation by providing a monthly-updating, fully automated benchmark that exposes genuine capability gaps hidden by saturated static benchmarks. The IRT-based efficient evaluation (saving ~75% of model evaluations per version) makes long-term maintenance economically feasible, a practical contribution for any evolving benchmark. Key open questions include the potential bias introduced by using Claude-Sonnet as the filtering model (which may favor questions it is predisposed to solve), the reliance on proprietary models for generation that limits reproducibility, and whether the multiple-choice format adequately captures generative and complex-reasoning abilities that free-form questions would assess.
