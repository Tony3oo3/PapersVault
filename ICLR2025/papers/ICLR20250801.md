## Problem

While LLMs achieve competitive performance on probabilistic tasks such as time-series forecasting, the internal mechanism by which they build up probability density estimates from in-context data remains opaque. This paper asks what geometric structure LLMs' density estimation trajectories follow in probability space, and whether that structure can be interpreted in terms of known statistical algorithms like kernel density estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250801/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Data points are serialized as comma-delimited 2-digit integers (0–99) forming a text prompt; the LLM's token-level softmax predictions are recursively extracted via the Hierarchy-PDF algorithm to yield a discrete 99-bin PDF at each context length n (up to 200). The sequence of estimated PDFs {p̂_n} is embedded into a 2D InPCA space using pairwise Hellinger distances, producing a trajectory that reveals the learning dynamics. The bespoke KDE model outputs per-step kernel shape s_i and bandwidth h_i schedules that reproduce the LLM's trajectory with high precision.

## Method

The core analysis embeds each LLM's estimated PDF at every context length into a low-dimensional Euclidean space via InPCA (using Hellinger distance), revealing that LLaMA-2, Gemma, and Mistral all trace similar paths geometrically bounded between the geodesic (shortest path from uniform to target) and a 1D Gaussian submanifold. To interpret this geometry, the authors construct a two-parameter bespoke KDE with a flexible kernel K_s(x) = b(s)·exp(−|b(s)x|^s)/Z(s) that interpolates between exponential (s=1), Gaussian (s=2), and tophat (s→∞) shapes, plus an adaptive bandwidth h. Fitting this model to LLaMA-2's trajectory shows the kernel width decays significantly faster than the classical n^(−1/5) schedule, while the shape parameter evolves from ~0.1 to ~1, indicating the LLM implicitly uses a non-Gaussian, adaptive kernel. This leads to the 'dispersive induction head' hypothesis: a kernel-like mechanism that increases predicted probability of similar (not just exact) tokens, with each observation's influence on dissimilar tokens decaying over context length.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative finding is that a 2-parameter bespoke KDE (adaptive kernel shape s and bandwidth h) faithfully reproduces LLaMA-2 13B's in-context density estimation trajectory, with the fitted shape parameter evolving from s ≈ 0.1 to s ≈ 1 and the bandwidth decaying significantly faster than the classical n^{-1/5} schedule. InPCA embeddings capture approximately 90% of pairwise Hellinger distance variance in just 2 dimensions, revealing that all tested LLMs (LLaMA-2 7B/13B/70B, Gemma-2B/7B, Mistral-7B-v0.3) trace strikingly similar low-dimensional trajectories. The bespoke KDE trivially recovers Gaussian KDE parameters (s = 2, h = n^{-1/5}) but fundamentally fails to model the Bayesian histogram, confirming the LLMs' kernel-like mechanism is distinct from histogram-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Gaussian KDE (Wand & Jones, 1994), LLMs exhibit a much faster bandwidth decay and an adaptive kernel shape (s < 2), resulting in longer InPCA trajectories that deviate from the Gaussian submanifold; Gaussian KDE lags behind LLaMA on narrow targets while LLaMA maintains close approximation. Against the Bayesian histogram (Lugosi & Nobel, 1996), LLMs take paths closer to the geodesic rather than the histogram's uniform-prior-driven trajectory, and the bespoke KDE cannot imitate the histogram's behavior (high parameter uncertainties, constant width, near-zero shape), confirming a fundamental algorithmic difference. Across LLM families, LLaMA-2, Gemma, and Mistral produce nearly identical InPCA trajectories despite independent training, with meta-InPCA showing all kernel-like methods clustering together while the Bayesian histogram is isolated. Notably, Gemma-2B is more similar to Mistral-7B and LLaMA-2 70B than to Gemma-7B, suggesting the in-context DE algorithm is a universal property rather than a family-specific artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLM time-series prompting framework of Gruver et al. (2024) and the Hierarchy-PDF extraction algorithm of Liu et al. (2024), but reframes the task explicitly as unconditional density estimation and applies Intensive Principal Component Analysis (InPCA) from the physics/statistics community to visualize the resulting learning trajectories in probability space. Conceptually, it departs from the induction-head literature (Olsson et al., 2022; Akyürek et al., 2024) by arguing that discrete n-gram matching is insufficient for continuous-valued ICL, and instead proposes a kernel-density-estimation interpretation with adaptive shape and bandwidth. The classical KDE and Bayesian histogram baselines are drawn from standard non-parametric statistics (Rosenblat, 1956; Lugosi & Nobel, 1996) and serve as geometric reference points rather than competitive benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that independently trained LLMs (LLaMA-2, Gemma, Mistral) trace strikingly similar low-dimensional DE trajectories, and that a mere two-parameter adaptive KDE reproduces these trajectories with high precision, offers a concrete mechanistic hypothesis for how transformers perform in-context probabilistic reasoning over continuous data. The proposed 'dispersive induction head' concept is a natural bridge between discrete and continuous ICL, but remains speculative and unverified at the circuit level. Key limitations include the restriction to 1D, 2-digit (0–100) representations with at most 200 in-context points, the post-hoc fitting of the bespoke KDE rather than a first-principles derivation, and the absence of an explanation for why the LLMs' optimal kernel shape (s ≈ 0.1–1) differs so dramatically from classical MISE-optimal kernels (Gaussian, Epanechnikov). Open questions include whether the geometric structure persists in higher dimensions, whether the adaptive kernel schedule can be derived from information-theoretic principles (the paper notes no such derivation exists in the f-divergence family), and whether the dispersive induction head can be isolated in transformer circuits.
