## Problem

Human-in-the-loop RL methods that learn reward functions from human preferences or scalar ratings still require thousands of human queries to achieve adequate performance, making them expensive and slow. While offline RL has demonstrated the value of low-quality, reward-free data, its potential for improving feedback efficiency in human-in-the-loop RL remains unexplored. This paper asks whether sub-optimal, unlabeled data can be leveraged to reduce the human interaction burden in scalar- and preference-based reward learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 50,000 sub-optimal state-action transitions (collected via a random policy or partially trained policy) that are pseudo-labeled with the minimum environment reward r_min before any human interaction begins. During the subsequent human-in-the-loop phase, the teacher provides either binary preference comparisons between 50-step trajectory segments or scalar numerical ratings on single segments. The output is a learned reward function (a neural network parameterized by θ) and a trained RL policy (SAC-based) that maximizes the learned reward, evaluated via ground-truth return (DMControl) or success rate (Meta-World).

## Method

SDP is a two-phase plug-and-pretraining tool that can be combined with any off-the-shelf human-in-the-loop RL algorithm. In the reward model pre-training phase, all sub-optimal transitions are pseudo-labeled with r_min and used to train the reward model via standard MSE regression, biasing it toward assigning low rewards to low-quality transitions without any human feedback. In the agent update phase, the RL agent's replay buffer is initialized with the same pseudo-labeled data, and the agent performs gradient updates to shift its policy, generating new transitions that populate the reward model's dataset so the teacher's first feedback covers a different region of state-action space rather than redundantly labeling the original sub-optimal data. The key insight is that pseudo-labeling with r_min introduces minimal statistical bias (since true rewards for sub-optimal transitions are empirically close to r_min) while reducing model variance through increased data volume. After both phases, standard preference-based (Bradley-Terry cross-entropy) or scalar-based (MSE) reward learning proceeds with the pre-trained reward model and updated agent.

## Results

Across 36 preference-based experiments (4 algorithms × 9 environments in DMControl and Meta-World), SDP significantly (p < 0.05) improves learning in 23 experiments and never statistically hurts performance. In scalar feedback on DMControl, SDP achieves final returns of 931.31 (Walker-walk), 862.72 (Cheetah-run), and 777.38 (Quadruped-walk), substantially outperforming R-PEBBLE (362.99, 522.10, 543.92) and Deep TAMER (33.10, 30.71, 73.74), while approaching SAC's oracle performance with as few as 60 feedback queries. In a 16-person human study, SDP significantly outperforms PEBBLE in both Pendulum-swingup and Cartpole-swingup on both AUC and final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDP consistently and significantly outperforms PEBBLE across nearly all environments: e.g., Cartpole-swingup final return improves from 226.4 to 533.9 (p=0.007), and Walker-walk from 173.9 to 490.1 (p=0.001). Against RUNE, SDP+RUNE wins decisively in Cartpole-swingup (561.8 vs 263.5) and Walker-walk (575.7 vs 160.6) but underperforms in Cheetah-run (616.8 vs 681.6, p=0.792), where the base algorithm already performs well. SDP+SURF dramatically improves over SURF in Cartpole-swingup (324.2 vs 83.1) and Walker-walk (436.0 vs 202.1), and SDP+MRN dominates MRN in Cartpole-swingup (644.0 vs 151.1) and Quadruped-walk (733.8 vs 200.1). In scalar feedback, SDP achieves 931.31 on Walker-walk versus R-PEBBLE's 362.99 and Deep TAMER's 33.10, approaching SAC's oracle performance with only 60 feedback queries. In the human study, SDP significantly outperforms PEBBLE in both tested environments, with no significant performance differences across demographic subgroups (gender, age, education, CS background).

## Relation to Prior Work

SDP adapts the pseudo-labeling strategy from Yu et al. (2022), which showed that sub-optimal transitions can be labeled with zero reward and added to an offline RL agent's replay buffer, to the human-in-the-loop setting. The key departures are: (1) using the environment's minimum reward r_min rather than zero for pseudo-labels, (2) introducing a dedicated reward model pre-training phase that biases the reward network toward low values on sub-optimal transitions before any human feedback arrives, and (3) an agent update phase that generates new behaviors so the human teacher's feedback covers a different region of state-action space. The method is deliberately modular, designed to plug into any existing preference- or scalar-based RL algorithm (PEBBLE, RUNE, SURF, MRN, R-PEBBLE) without modifying their core learning procedures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Human feedback is the dominant cost bottleneck in preference- and scalar-based RL, and SDP offers a simple, drop-in way to reduce that cost by exploiting data that is already freely available (random-policy rollouts). The 16-person user study with diverse demographics strengthens the practical case, and the finding that SDP never statistically degrades performance across 36 experiments suggests low adoption risk. Open questions include whether the r_min pseudo-labeling assumption holds in environments with sparse or non-zero minimum rewards, how the approach scales to higher-dimensional or multi-task settings, and whether the benefit persists when the sub-optimal data comes from a very different policy distribution than the target task.
