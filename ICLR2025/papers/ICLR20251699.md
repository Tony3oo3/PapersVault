## Problem

Subquadratic alternatives to transformers (Mamba, Performer, Reformer, etc.) have been proposed to overcome the quadratic attention bottleneck, but no prior work established a fundamental theoretical barrier showing they cannot solve important tasks that standard transformers can. This paper proves that document similarity tasks—finding the most or least similar pair among n documents—require truly quadratic time under SETH/OVC, and that even a single-layer, single-head transformer can solve them, thereby establishing an inherent separation between standard transformers and any subquadratic approach.

## Input / Output

The input is a matrix E ∈ R^{n×ℓ} of n binary bag-of-words document embeddings with ℓ = c·log n (the natural regime where no duplicates are forced). For decision variants (MSD_{n,ℓ,t}, LSD_{n,ℓ,t}), the output is a single scalar: 1 if a pair with cosine similarity ≥ t (or ≤ t) exists, 0 otherwise. For search variants, the output is the pair indices achieving maximum/minimum cosine similarity. The transformer model is a composition φ₂ ∘ A_{Q,K,V} ∘ φ₁ mapping R^{n×d} → R, where the input MLP φ₁ handles normalization and threshold-token insertion, and the output MLP φ₂ applies a continuous piecewise-linear threshold.

## Method

The paper establishes a separation through two complementary results. For hardness, they reduce OV to LSD directly (cosine similarity equals zero iff vectors are orthogonal, so a γ-approximate LSD algorithm detects orthogonal pairs) and use graph-construction techniques from Karthik & Manurangsi (2020) to reduce OV to MSD, proving both require Ω(n²) time under SETH/OVC for ℓ = c·log n. For representational strength, they construct a single attention unit with QK^T = ±3log(n)·I_{d_in} so that the softmax produces exponential weighting: pairs with inner product 0 (or exceeding the threshold) yield attention weights of order 1/(n+1), while all other pairs yield weights of order at most 1/(n+1)^{1.5}. This gap is then exploited by a simple continuous threshold MLP φ₂ that outputs 1 if any entry exceeds 1/(n+1) and 0 otherwise. The input MLP φ₁ normalizes each document vector by its ℓ₁ norm and appends a threshold token (0,…,0,t+1) to encode the decision boundary directly into the attention computation.

## Results

Under SETH (or OVC), the paper proves that document similarity problems require truly quadratic time: LSD_{n,ℓ} cannot be solved in O(n^{2−ε}) time when ℓ = c·log n, and MSD_{n,ℓ} requires quadratic time when ℓ = n^{c/log log n}. Bichromatic MSD achieves the stronger ℓ = c·log n bound. Conversely, a single standard attention unit with input/output MLPs (embedding dimension ℓ+1) provably solves OV, MSD_{n,ℓ,t}, and LSD_{n,ℓ,t} for any threshold t ∈ [0,1], establishing a clean representational separation between standard transformers and any subquadratic-time alternative.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes a theoretical separation rather than empirical benchmark comparisons. Standard transformers (even a single-layer, single-head attention unit with simple sparse low-rank Q, K, V matrices) provably solve document similarity tasks, while any model evaluated in truly subquadratic time O(n^{2−ε}) is provably unable to solve them under SETH/OVC. This applies universally to all subquadratic approaches: heuristic attention approximations (Reformer, Performer, Linformer, SMYRF, KDEformer, Hyperattention), alternative architectures (Mamba, Longformer, Synthesizer, Routing Transformers), and any other method. The hardness extends to all natural variants—bichromatic, approximate (γ-MSD, γ-LSD for any γ ≥ 1), and decision versions (MSD_{n,ℓ,t}, LSD_{n,ℓ,t})—meaning no subquadratic approach can avoid accuracy loss on document similarity tasks, even when compared only to the simplest one-layer transformer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the fine-grained complexity framework (SETH, OVC) and the hardness reductions of Karthik & Manurangsi (2020) for approximate Max-IP, extending them to the cosine-similarity (document similarity) setting. It generalizes the limitation results of Sanford et al. (2024a), who showed that kernel-based subquadratic attention cannot solve k-hop induction heads, by proving that *any* subquadratic-time algorithm—regardless of architecture or heuristic—cannot solve document similarity tasks. On the positive side, it extends the representational-power literature on transformers (Hahn 2020; Merrill et al. 2022; Sanford et al. 2024b) by showing that a single attention head with simple piecewise-linear MLPs suffices to solve OV, MSD, and LSD decision problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a theoretical guarantee that accuracy loss for subquadratic alternatives (Mamba, Performer, Reformer, etc.) on document-similarity-related tasks is not an engineering shortcoming but a fundamental computational barrier, assuming SETH/OVC. This reframes the transformer-vs-alternatives debate: for tasks involving pairwise token interactions, quadratic time may be unavoidable. Open questions include whether the separation extends to richer embedding schemes beyond bag-of-words, whether the parameter regimes (ℓ = Θ(log n) for LSD, ℓ = n^{o(1)} for MSD) are tight, and whether there exist natural NLP tasks where subquadratic models provably match transformers. The conditional nature of the result (SETH/OVC) means a future breakthrough in subquadratic OV algorithms would invalidate the hardness side.
