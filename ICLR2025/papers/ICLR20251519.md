## Problem

Current MLLMs either rely on separate visual encoders (e.g., CLIP) that require costly alignment with LLMs, or simply flatten quantized image tokens into 1D sequences without structural priors. The paper identifies that Transformers fundamentally struggle to learn 2D sequence data in its raw form—failing to even surpass a unigram model on 2D Markov processes—yet no prior work has applied explicit tokenization (merging) to visual tokens the way BPE does for text.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image of arbitrary resolution and a text prompt as input. The image is first quantized by a pretrained VQ-GAN (codebook size 8192) into a 32×32 grid of discrete token IDs, then processed by the BPE Image Tokenizer which merges frequently co-occurring horizontal and vertical token pairs into higher-level tokens from an extended vocabulary (4K–16K additional tokens). The resulting mixed sequence of text and image tokens is fed to Llama-3.1-8B, which produces a free-form text response (answers, descriptions, reasoning).

## Method

The core contribution is a BPE Image Tokenizer that applies Byte-Pair Encoding to 2D quantized image tokens: it iteratively merges the most frequent adjacent token pairs (considering both horizontal and vertical neighbors) to build an extended vocabulary whose tokens inherently encode spatial and semantic structure. Theoretically, the paper proves that for 2D k-th-order Markov data, a Transformer without tokenization is bounded by the unigram entropy H(π), while with a BPE-style tokenizer the achievable loss approaches the optimal H_∞, with the gap between the two being arbitrarily large. Practically, the tokenizer is trained on 2.78M images, and the resulting tokens are fed to Llama-3.1-8B via a two-stage training protocol: (1) image-understanding pretraining with frozen text embeddings on ~1.15M image-caption pairs, and (2) full-parameter SFT on 1.27M multi-task conversation entries. This paradigm eliminates the need for a separate CLIP encoder, letting the LLM acquire all visual comprehension directly from the tokenized image sequence.

## Results

The best Being-VL-0 configuration (LLM+VQ+BPE with PT(freeze)+SFT) achieves 57.1 on VQAv2, 40.9 on MMBench, 1223.5 on MME-perception, 307.1 on MME-cognition, 79.0 on POPE, and 46.0 on VizWiz. With incremental data scaling (adding RefCOCO, AOKVQA, ShareGPT4o, and ALLaVA-Inst), performance further improves to 60.6 on VQAv2, 44.0 on MMBench, 1316.2 on MME-perception, 331.0 on MME-cognition, 81.3 on POPE, and 48.2 on VizWiz. The BPE Image Tokenizer consistently outperforms the VQ-only baseline across all six benchmarks under every training strategy tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external SOTA MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA, Flamingo, Gemini); instead, it ablates the contribution of the BPE Image Tokenizer by comparing LLM+VQ (VQ-GAN tokens fed directly to Llama-3.1-8B) against LLM+VQ+BPE (VQ-GAN tokens further merged by the BPE tokenizer) under identical training protocols. Under the best training strategy (PT(freeze)+SFT), adding BPE improves VQAv2 from 55.4 to 57.1 (+1.7), MMBench from 37.6 to 40.9 (+3.3), MME-perception from 1054.5 to 1223.5 (+169.0), MME-cognition from 277.0 to 307.1 (+30.1), POPE from 76.0 to 79.0 (+3.0), and VizWiz from 45.3 to 46.0 (+0.7). The gains are most pronounced on MME-perception and MME-cognition, suggesting BPE tokenization particularly benefits fine-grained visual understanding and reasoning. The authors explicitly note this is a preliminary implementation with limited data (~1.2M SFT samples vs. 400M used for CLIP pretraining in standard MLLMs) and that the approach has not yet been scaled to directly compete with state-of-the-art foundation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BPE tokenization paradigm from text (Sennrich, 2015) to 2D quantized image tokens, and builds directly on the theoretical framework of Rajaraman et al. (2024) on why tokenization matters for Transformer learning, generalizing their 1D results to 2D image data. It departs from prior token-based MLLMs (Chameleon, Gemini, Unicode, CM3) that simply feed VQ-quantized tokens directly into Transformers, arguing that an explicit merging step is essential rather than optional. The training pipeline follows the LLaVA-style two-stage (PT + SFT) paradigm but replaces the CLIP encoder with a VQ-GAN + BPE tokenizer, positioning the work as a more faithful analogue to how text-only LLMs are trained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that Transformers provably cannot surpass unigram-model performance on 2D Markov data without tokenization is a clean, important insight that reframes why explicit tokenization matters beyond mere compression. Practically, the paper demonstrates competitive multimodal understanding with roughly 0.1% of CLIP's pretraining data, suggesting a potentially more data-efficient path for MLLMs. However, the implementation is explicitly preliminary (8B model, ~2.78M images, limited compute), and open questions remain about whether the BPE approach scales favorably to larger models, whether it generalizes to video or other modalities, and how it compares once CLIP-based methods are given equivalent compute budgets.
