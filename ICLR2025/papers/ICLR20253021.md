## Problem

Self-supervised learning methods designed for iconic, single-subject images (e.g., ImageNet) perform poorly on naturalistic videos with dense, cluttered scenes containing many objects of varying sizes and severely imbalanced class distributions. Dense SSL methods avoid semantic mismatches from multi-subject crops but suffer from spatial imbalance, where large background classes like sky dominate representations while critical small objects like pedestrians (occupying <0.3% of a frame) are underrepresented. The paper addresses how to unify pooled and dense objectives to capture both scene-level semantics and fine-grained small-object understanding from naturalistic video.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired video frames (x_t, x_{t+Δt}) at 720p/30fps along with dense optical flow M_{t→t+Δt}. Two global crops of area [0.16, 0.45] are resized to 512×1024 for the dense objective, while K=6 flow-informed subcrops of area [0.05, 0.3] are resized to 192×192 for the pooled objective. The output is a ResNet-50 backbone with a Spatial Decoder Module producing 4× upsampled dense feature maps, which serve as frozen representations for downstream semantic segmentation (linear readout or UperNet finetuning) and object detection (Faster R-CNN with C4 or FPN).

## Method

PooDLe combines two self-supervised objectives in a unified architecture: (1) a dense flow-equivariance loss (adopted from FlowE) that warps and aligns dense feature projections between paired frames using optical flow, and (2) a pooled invariance loss computed on flow-informed subcrops that act as pseudo-iconic views of individual foreground objects. The flow-informed cropping procedure uses optical flow to identify spatially aligned subcrop pairs across frames, with grid-sampling to ensure spatial diversity. A lightweight Spatial Decoder Module (SDM) with two top-down decoder blocks and UNet-like lateral connections upsamples high-level encoder features while resurfacing fine-grained details from earlier layers, replacing FlowE's dilated convolutions. The key finding is that neither objective alone is sufficient: the pooled loss on subcrops effectively increases the prevalence of small objects in training, while the SDM propagates those learned semantics into the dense loss at full resolution, yielding a synergistic 5.4% mIoU improvement over the dense-only baseline.

## Results

On BDD100K semantic segmentation (linear readout), PooDLe achieves 39.2% mIoU, outperforming the strongest baseline FlowE (35.7%) by 3.5% and supervised ImageNet pretraining (36.7%) by 2.5%. On Cityscapes, PooDLe reaches 47.2% mIoU (linear) and 60.7% mIoU (UperNet), surpassing all baselines. When initialized with supervised ImageNet weights, PooDLe further improves to 44.7% mIoU on BDD100K linear readout and 65.1% mIoU on Cityscapes UperNet. On ADE20K and the new WT-Sem benchmark (pretrained on Walking Tours), PooDLe achieves 16.5% and 11.2% mIoU respectively with a ResNet-50 backbone trained for only 20 epochs, outperforming DoRA (ViT-S, 100 epochs) by 2.6% and 3.4% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PooDLe outperforms all BDD-pretrained baselines across every metric in Table 1. Against the strongest baseline FlowE (dense flow-equivariance SSL), PooDLe gains +3.5% mIoU on BDD100K linear readout, +2.6% on UperNet, +1.7% mAP on C4 detection, and +4.1% mIoU on Cityscapes linear. Against DINO, DenseCL, PixPro, and DoRA (all R50 or ViT-S on BDD), PooDLe leads by 6–26% mIoU on BDD100K linear readout. On the Walking Tours pretraining setting (Table 2), PooDLe (R50, 20 epochs) surpasses DoRA (ViT-S, 100 epochs) by 2.6% mIoU on ADE20K linear readout and 3.4% on WT-Sem linear, despite using a smaller backbone and 5× fewer epochs. PooDLe also beats supervised ImageNet pretraining on BDD100K linear readout (39.2% vs. 36.7% mIoU) and Cityscapes (47.2% vs. 46.8%), though supervised IN1K retains an edge on UperNet finetuning for BDD (55.2% vs. 49.9%). The class-level breakdown (Table 3) reveals that PooDLe's advantage is concentrated on small and rare object classes (e.g., 18.3% vs. 12.2% mIoU for small classes vs. FlowE), confirming that the pooled subcrop objective and SDM specifically address the spatial imbalance problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

PooDLe unifies two previously separate lines of SSL: the dense flow-equivariance objective from FlowE (Xiong et al., 2021) and the pooled invariance objective from BYOL (Grill et al., 2020), combining them within a single architecture for the first time. It departs from multi-crop methods (DINO, iBOT) that use small crops to predict global crops by instead using flow-informed subcrops as pseudo-iconic paired views, which is better suited to dense scenes where global crops contain multiple unrelated subjects. The Spatial Decoder Module draws on UNet lateral connections and FPN top-down upsampling to address the information loss that plagues dense objectives at high resolution, replacing FlowE's dilated convolution approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spatial imbalance problem—where large background classes dominate dense representations while safety-critical small objects (pedestrians, traffic lights) are underrepresented—is particularly dangerous in autonomous driving and remains poorly addressed by existing methods. PooDLe's mechanistic explanation via subcrop hit probability analysis provides a clear design principle for future work on naturalistic video SSL. Open questions include whether the method scales to ViT backbones (all PooDLe results use R50), whether the optical flow dependency can be eliminated, and whether the flow-informed cropping strategy generalizes to non-driving, non-egocentric video domains.
