## Problem

Vision-language models (VLMs) have strong reasoning and task-planning capabilities but exhibit a critical gap in understanding the physical world—object properties, spatial relationships, scene dynamics, and physics-based interactions—limiting their deployment as safe embodied agents. No comprehensive benchmark existed to systematically evaluate this capability across diverse physical phenomena, and the root causes of VLMs' physical reasoning failures remained uncharacterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253531/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PhysBench presents 10,002 interleaved video-image-text entries where each question is a four-option multiple-choice item; inputs range from a single image (18.6%) to a single video (44.8%) to interleaved multi-image/video sequences (20.1%), with up to 48-token questions and 20-token choices. PhysAgent augments the VLM input with foundation-model outputs (depth maps, segmentation masks, bounding boxes) and retrieved physical-knowledge snippets, producing a chain-of-thought explanation followed by the selected answer. In the embodied setting, the VLM receives a visual observation plus a free-form language instruction and outputs key points, waypoints, and affordance attributes that MOKA converts into executable robotic-arm motions.

## Method

The paper contributes two components. PhysBench is a 10,002-entry benchmark spanning four domains (object properties, object relationships, scene understanding, physics-based dynamics) with 19 sub-tasks and 8 capability dimensions, built via a five-step annotation pipeline (4,000 hours) combining web-sourced videos, real-world captures, and simulator-generated data. PhysAgent is a three-step inference framework that (1) classifies the question and activates task-specific prompts with retrieved physical-knowledge memory, (2) invokes vision foundation models—Depth Anything for depth estimation, SAM for segmentation, and GroundingDINO for open-set object detection—to supply perceptual evidence the VLM cannot extract on its own, and (3) performs chain-of-thought reasoning with a self-verification step before committing to an answer. This design targets the two dominant error sources identified in the paper's error analysis: perceptual inaccuracies (37–45% of mistakes) and insufficient physical knowledge (23–35%), yielding an 18.4% zero-shot improvement on GPT-4o without fine-tuning the VLM itself.

## Results

Across 75 VLMs evaluated on PhysBench (10,002 interleaved video-image-text entries), the best model GPT-4o achieves only 49.49% average accuracy, far below the 95.87% human performance. Most open-source models cluster around 35–42%, with the best open-source model (LLaVA-interleave) at 41.00%. PhysAgent, which integrates vision foundation models (Depth Anything, SAM, GroundingDINO) with a physics knowledge memory, improves GPT-4o's zero-shot accuracy by 18.4% overall, with a dramatic 49.5% gain on the Physical Scene Understanding subtask (30.15% → 84.2%). On five embodied manipulation tasks using MOKA, fine-tuning the VLM with PhysBench raises success rates substantially (e.g., Force task: 0.2 → 0.6), while PhysAgent provides consistent zero-shot gains across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o (49.49%) outperforms the best open-source model LLaVA-interleave (41.00%) by 20.7%, confirming a substantial closed-source advantage; Gemini-1.5-pro (49.11%) is a close second. Among prompting baselines on GPT-4o, CoT yields a modest gain on Property (58.6 vs. 56.9) but degrades Dynamics (36.0 vs. 47.0), while Desp-CoT and PLR both underperform the base model on most subtasks. ContPhy, the dedicated physical-reasoning oracle, actually worsens GPT-4o's performance in three of four categories (e.g., Property drops from 56.9 to 52.1) due to rigid logical templates and reliance on R-CNN for visual processing. PhysAgent consistently outperforms all prompting strategies and ContPhy, achieving the highest scores on Property (58.4), Scene (84.2, a 49.5% absolute gain over base GPT-4o), and Dynamics (45.0). On the embodied MOKA tasks, fine-tuning with PhysBench data yields the largest gains (e.g., Force success 0.2→0.6, Affordance 0.6→0.9), while PhysAgent provides smaller but consistent zero-shot improvements (e.g., Affordance 0.6→0.8, Tool 0.4→0.5), validating both the benchmark's utility as training data and PhysAgent's plug-and-play enhancement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior physical reasoning benchmarks (CLEVRER, Physion, ContPhy, SuperCLEVR) that focus on narrow subsets—typically simple geometric primitives, collision events, or single physical properties—by introducing the first comprehensive evaluation spanning four dimensions (object properties, relationships, scene understanding, dynamics) with interleaved video-image-text inputs. Methodologically, PhysAgent repurposes off-the-shelf vision foundation models (Depth Anything, SAM, GroundingDINO) as perceptual augmentations for a general-purpose VLM, contrasting with prior physical oracle models (ContPhy, Physion++) that rely on task-specific rule engines and predefined processing logic. The embodied-agent validation builds directly on MOKA's mark-based visual prompting pipeline, extending it with physical knowledge rather than modifying the underlying VLM architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that physical world understanding does not scale with model size, training data volume, or frame count challenges the prevailing assumption that scaling alone will close capability gaps, pointing instead to a fundamental absence of physical priors in current VLM training corpora. The 18.4% zero-shot improvement from PhysAgent and the consistent gains in MOKA manipulation tasks (e.g., force task success rising from 0.2 to 0.6 with fine-tuning) demonstrate a practical pathway for deploying VLMs in safety-critical embodied settings. Open questions include whether the knowledge-memory approach generalizes beyond the curated physical principles tested here, whether the gap can be closed through pre-training rather than inference-time augmentation, and whether the benchmark's multiple-choice format adequately captures the open-ended reasoning required in real-world manipulation.
