## Problem

Prior work on conditional (selective) classification either assumed realizability or provided no approximation guarantee relative to the optimal classifier-selector pair. This paper addresses the agnostic setting—where no perfect pair exists—by studying conditional classification with homogeneous halfspace selectors under Gaussian x-marginals, delivering the first polynomial-time algorithm with a provable Õ(√opt) approximation guarantee, along with cryptographic hardness results showing that small additive error is intractable for general halfspaces.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes i.i.d. labeled samples (x, y) ∈ R^d × {0,1} drawn from a distribution with standard normal x-marginals, along with a finite (or list-learnable sparse linear) classifier class C and coverage bounds [a, b] constraining the selected halfspace's population mass. It outputs a classifier c′ ∈ C paired with a homogeneous halfspace selector h(w^(c′)) that minimizes the conditional 0-1 loss Pr{y ≠ c′(x) | x ∈ h(w^(c′))}. Because every homogeneous halfspace under a standard normal has exactly 1/2 mass, minimizing the conditional loss is equivalent to minimizing the joint error Pr{x ∈ h(w) ∧ c(x) ≠ y}, which simplifies the optimization target.

## Method

For each classifier c ∈ C, the algorithm relabels y as 1{c(x) ≠ y} to reduce the conditional problem to a one-sided agnostic linear classification task, then runs Projected SGD on the convex surrogate loss L_D(w) = E[y·max(0, ⟨x, w⟩)] using the projected gradient g_w(x,y) = y·x_{w^⊥}·1{x ∈ h(w)}. The key geometric insight is that when the current halfspace h(w) is substantially sub-optimal relative to the optimal h(v), the expected negative projected gradient always has a non-negligible positive projection onto v_{w^⊥}, making the angle θ(v, w) contractive at each step and guaranteeing convergence to an approximately stationary point within Õ(d²/ε⁴) iterations. The algorithm retains all T iterates and selects the halfspace with the smallest empirical joint error on a held-out sample. For sparse linear classifiers, a list-learning subroutine (SPARSELIST) first generates a polynomial-size finite list C containing a near-optimal classifier, after which the finite-class procedure is applied unchanged, yielding a Õ(√opt) guarantee in polynomial time.

## Results

The paper provides the first polynomial-time PAC-learning algorithm for agnostic conditional classification with homogeneous halfspace selectors, achieving an error guarantee of Õ(√opt) where opt is the optimal conditional 0-1 loss, using Õ(d²/ε⁶) examples and running in O(d²|C|/ε⁶) time (Theorem 3.1). This extends to sparse linear classifiers (sparsity s=O(1)) with poly(d, 1/ε, 1/δ) sample and time complexity (Theorem 3.5). On the negative side, under the sub-exponential cLWE assumption, no polynomial-time algorithm can achieve an additive error of o(1/log^{1/2+α} d) for any constant α>0 (Theorem 4.3), establishing that conditional classification is at least as hard as agnostic linear classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The paper positions its contribution against prior theoretical work: previous agnostic selective classification methods (Wiener & El-Yaniv, 2011; 2015; Gelbhart & El-Yaniv, 2019) did not guarantee both computational efficiency and performance relative to the optimal classifier-selector pair, while realizable-setting results (El-Yaniv & Wiener, 2012; Gangrade et al., 2021) assumed a perfect solution exists. The Õ(√opt) guarantee is the first provable approximation factor for agnostic conditional classification. The authors note their bound is likely suboptimal, since O(opt) error was achievable for standard agnostic halfspace learning under Gaussian data (Diakonikolas et al., 2022), and the hardness result (Theorem 4.3) only rules out additive errors smaller than O(1/log^{1/2+α} d), leaving a gap between the upper and lower bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of agnostic linear classification (Kearns et al. 1994; Diakonikolas et al. 2020b, 2022, 2023) and the older selective/conditional classification literature (Chow 1957; El-Yaniv et al. 2010; Wiener & El-Yaniv 2011, 2015). Prior agnostic selective classification works relied on an agnostic learning oracle and did not provide approximation guarantees relative to the optimal classifier-selector pair; this paper is the first to give a polynomial-time algorithm with a provable Õ(√opt) guarantee, using a projected SGD approach on a novel one-sided surrogate loss inspired by Diakonikolas et al. (2020b) but adapted to the conditional setting. The extension to sparse linear classifiers leverages the robust list learning framework of Charikar et al. (2017) and Juba (2017), while the hardness results build on the sub-exponential cLWE assumption (Gupte et al. 2022; Diakonikolas et al. 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish both a positive algorithmic result and a matching hardness barrier for agnostic conditional classification, delineating the computational landscape of the problem. The O(√opt) guarantee is acknowledged as suboptimal (agnostic halfspace learning under Gaussians can achieve O(opt)), and the restriction to homogeneous halfspaces and Gaussian marginals are significant limitations that leave substantial room for improvement. Key open questions include whether the gap to O(opt) can be closed, whether the approach extends to general (inhomogeneous) halfspaces or broader distribution families (e.g., log-concave), and which classifier families are list-decodable. The negative result showing that the distribution-free reduction from conditional to agnostic classification does not transfer to Gaussian data has broader implications for related problems like fairness auditing (Hsu et al. 2024).
