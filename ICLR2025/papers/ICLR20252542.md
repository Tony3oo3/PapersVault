## Problem

Existing contrastive learning for image-text matching relies on fixed margins and pre-defined positive/negative labels, making it unable to handle 'clone negatives'—negative pairs that are semantically consistent with the positive but sub-optimal due to less fine-grained descriptions. Unlike false negatives (which should be re-labeled as positive), clone negatives are genuinely negative yet nearly indistinguishable from positives, causing ambiguous matching outcomes that vanilla TRL or CL cannot resolve because they have already committed to a binary label for every in-batch pair.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252542/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is plug-and-play: it replaces only the loss function in any existing image-text matching architecture (ResNet+BiGRU, Faster R-CNN+BiGRU, Faster R-CNN+BERT, or CLIP ViT-B/32) without modifying the backbone or cross-modal fusion modules. In the weakly-supervised variant, human-annotated captions are replaced by pseudo captions from off-the-shelf tools (BLIP, GIT, BLIP-2, CoCa) that follow a structured subject-verb-object format, thereby increasing the density of potential clone negatives. Training uses a mini-batch of 64 with a momentum memory bank of size 4096 and a 256-dimensional joint embedding space; the maximum sentence length is 32 words and the region count for Faster R-CNN is 36.

## Method

AdaCL modifies the softmax-normalized similarity by introducing two margin parameters—m₁ (scaling) and m₂ (shifting)—applied exclusively to the positive term while leaving all negative terms unchanged, thereby adaptively tightening positive compactness without distorting the negative distribution. A modulating anchor is selected per mini-batch via Gaussian Discriminant Analysis on in-batch similarity scores: the highest- and lowest-salient-score pairs serve as class representatives for salient and potential clone negatives respectively, GDA classifies remaining negatives, and the anchor is set to the median of score differences between positives and identified clone negatives. m₁ and m₂ are then computed in closed form from the anchor's probability and a boundary condition (p̂_u → 1 as the positive score → 1), enabling progressive, training-free tuning that adaptively enlarges the gap between positives and clone negatives. The entire procedure introduces no learnable parameters beyond the base model, making it a direct drop-in replacement for TRL or CL across supervised, weakly-supervised, and noisy-correspondence settings.

## Results

On Flickr30K, AdaCL consistently improves all baselines by 1–5% R@1 across three backbone families. The strongest result is AdaCL-USER (Faster R-CNN+BERT) achieving 86.4 R@1 (I→T) and 69.9 R@1 (T→I), surpassing the original USER by 0.1/0.4 points. On MS-COCO 5K, AdaCL-CORA reaches 67.2 R@1 (I→T) and 47.4 R@1 (T→I), a 2.9/2.0-point gain over CORA. In CLIP pre-training on CC3M, AdaCL yields a dramatic 12.9-point R@1 improvement on Flickr30K zero-shot retrieval (39.5 vs. 26.6 I→T), demonstrating scalability to large-scale contrastive learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaCL achieves consistent improvements over all 17 compared baselines across every backbone type and dataset. The largest relative gains appear on weaker architectures: AdaCL-CMPM improves Flickr30K I→T R@1 by 5.1 points (49.6→54.7) and T→I R@1 by 4.3 points (37.3→41.6), while AdaCL-SCAN gains 4.0/2.3 points on I→T/T→I R@1. On the strongest baselines (Faster R-CNN+BERT), gains are smaller but still positive: AdaCL-USER adds 0.1/0.4 R@1 on Flickr30K, and AdaCL-CORA adds 0.5/0.6 R@1. In CLIP pre-training (Table 2), AdaCL on CC3M dramatically outperforms vanilla CLIP by 12.9 R@1 on Flickr30K I→T (39.5 vs. 26.6) and 7.4 R@1 on T→I (25.5 vs. 18.1), while on CC12M the gap narrows to 1.7/2.9 R@1, suggesting AdaCL is most beneficial when training data is noisier. In noisy correspondence learning (Table 10), AdaCL-CRCL improves I→T R@1 from 77.9 to 81.0 at 20% noise and from 77.8 to 80.3 at 40% noise on Flickr30K, outperforming NCR, BiCro, CREAM, and CRCL. In text-based person retrieval (Table 7), AdaCL boosts domain-generalization R@1 by 14.2 points on CUHK-PEDES (16.3→30.5) and 11.1 points on ICFG-PEDES (15.8→27.9) over vanilla CL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard InfoNCE/contrastive learning objective (Oord et al., 2018) and its large-scale instantiation in CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) by introducing two adaptive margin parameters that replace the fixed-margin paradigm of both triplet ranking loss and vanilla contrastive loss. It departs from prior false-negative elimination strategies (Li et al., 2023a; Zhang et al., 2022), which the authors characterize as 'passive' reweighting, by instead actively propagating clone-negative semantics through a GDA-based anchor that requires no explicit training. The plug-and-play design is validated across CNN and transformer backbones (ResNet+BiGRU, Faster R-CNN+BERT, CLIP ViT-B/32), positioning AdaCL as a drop-in replacement rather than an architectural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'clone negatives'—semantically consistent but sub-optimal matches—as a distinct failure mode from hard negatives and false negatives fills a genuine gap in the contrastive learning literature, where most prior work conflates these categories. The weakly-supervised extension, where AdaCL trained on BLIP-generated pseudo captions rivals ground-truth-annotated baselines at R@10, suggests a practical path toward reducing reliance on expensive crowd-sourced annotations for vision-language pre-training. Open questions include whether the GDA assumption (univariate Gaussian with shared structure) holds under more heterogeneous or larger-scale negative distributions, and whether the per-batch anchor computation introduces meaningful overhead at very large batch sizes beyond the 4096 momentum bank tested here.
