## Problem

MoE-LLMs like Mixtral 8×7b require all experts to be loaded into memory simultaneously (96.8 GB for 16-bit), making deployment on consumer hardware infeasible, while the top-k expert activation during inference remains redundant since many tokens only need a single expert. Prior compression work addressed either static quantization or dynamic pruning in isolation, and uniform bit-width quantization collapses in accuracy at extreme compression ratios (28.6% loss at 2-bit), leaving a gap for a unified, training-free strategy that jointly compresses both storage and computation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full-precision MoE-LLM weights (Mixtral 8×7b or 8×22b), a C4 calibration set of 128 sequences × 2048 tokens for bit-width planning and pruning threshold estimation, and raw input text tokens at inference time. It outputs a mixed-precision quantized model with per-expert bit-widths restricted to {1, 2, 3}-bit (non-expert weights at 4-bit) and a dynamic inference path that skips one of the two activated experts for ~15% of tokens, reducing activated parameters from 26.31 GB to as low as 2.55 GB while fitting on a single A100-80GB GPU.

## Method

MC combines two complementary phases. In the pre-loading phase (PMQ), each expert's significance is scored as φ_i^α · w_i^β (activation frequency raised to α times mean routing weight raised to β), and the per-expert quantization reconstruction error ε_{i,j} (Frobenius norm of output difference when expert i is quantized to j bits) is measured; an Integer Programming formulation then assigns 1/2/3-bit widths to minimize the weighted sum of significance × quantization error subject to a fixed average bit-width, solved in under a second, after which GPTQ performs the actual quantization. In the online inference phase (ODP), for each token the ratio w₁/w₀ of the two selected experts' routing scores is compared against a median threshold μ; if below μ, the lower-weight expert is pruned—except for the top 2% of tokens ranked by a combined importance score (ℓ₁ norm of token features × mean attention score over subsequent positions), which are protected to prevent the 'attention decay' effect where pruning experts for salient tokens degrades downstream attention maps.

## Results

At 2.54-bit average precision, PMQ achieves 67.50% average accuracy across eight zero-shot benchmarks, representing only a 3.8% drop from the 16-bit Mixtral 8×7b (71.29%) while compressing 76.6% of model parameters. When combined with Online Dynamic Pruning (ODP), activated parameters are further reduced by ~15% (from 4.53 GB to 3.96 GB) with less than 0.6% accuracy loss. At the extreme 1.57-bit setting, PMQ still achieves 54.49% average accuracy, outperforming the 2.54-bit BSP baseline (49.07%) by 5.4%. The compressed 2.54-bit MC model (16.24 GB total, 3.96 GB activated) surpasses the 16-bit LLaMA2-13b (26.03 GB) by ~8% on MMLU 5-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMQ decisively outperforms uniform GPTQ quantization ('Uni') at all sub-3-bit settings: at 2.54-bit, PMQ achieves 67.50% vs. Uni's catastrophic 42.67% at 2-bit (a 24.8-point gap), demonstrating that uniform bit-width is fundamentally inadequate for MoE expert compression. Against BSP (Li et al., 2024), the prior MoE-specific mixed-precision method, PMQ leads by 18.4 points at 2.54-bit (67.50% vs. 49.07%) and even surpasses BSP at 2.54-bit while operating at 1.57-bit (54.49% vs. 49.07%). Compared to Hessian-based allocation (HAWQ-V2, Dong et al., 2020), PMQ shows a modest 0.3-point edge at 2.54-bit but a substantial 8.6-point advantage at 1.57-bit (54.49% vs. 45.91%), indicating that the multi-factor significance metric (frequency × routing weight × F-norm) becomes increasingly critical as compression becomes more aggressive. When ODP is layered on top of PMQ, the combined MC pipeline at 2.54-bit retains 66.94% accuracy (only 0.56-point drop from PMQ alone) while reducing activated parameters by 15%, achieving a 1.71× inference speedup over the 16-bit baseline on a single A100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2022) as its quantization backbone and on Lu et al. (2024) for the dynamic expert-skipping idea, but departs from both by unifying static mixed-precision quantization and dynamic token-aware pruning into a single training-free pipeline for MoE-LLMs. Prior MoE compression work addressed either pre-loading quantization (e.g., BSP by Li et al., 2024) or inference-time pruning (Lu et al., 2024) in isolation; MC is the first to co-design both phases, using an integer-programming formulation that jointly considers expert activation frequency, routing weight, and per-expert quantization loss. The token-protection mechanism in ODP also extends the routing-score-only pruning of Lu et al. by incorporating attention scores and feature magnitudes to prevent an 'attention decay' effect that purely weight-based pruning introduces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 2.54-bit Mixtral 8×7b fits on a single A100 (vs. two for FP16) and outperforms the 16-bit LLaMA2-13b on MMLU, demonstrating that extreme MoE compression can beat larger dense models without any retraining. This opens a path for deploying MoE-LLMs on consumer and edge hardware (e.g., 52 tok/s on an RTX 3090). Open questions include generalization beyond Mixtral to other MoE architectures (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, OLMoE), whether the IP-based allocation transfers to multimodal MoE models, and how the approach interacts with emerging codebook-based or QAT quantization methods at even lower bit-widths. The paper also notes that challenging reasoning tasks (GSM8K, HumanEval) still suffer substantial degradation at ultra-low bits, leaving room for future work on task-aware compression.
