## Problem

Conventional Wasserstein-based DRO methods define uncertainty sets as balls in the ambient Euclidean space, which inevitably include unrealistic distributions far from the data manifold. This forces a dilemma: a large radius δ provides robustness but degrades clean accuracy by optimizing over meaningless perturbations, while a small δ offers insufficient protection. The paper addresses this by constraining the uncertainty set to the data manifold and using geodesic (rather than Euclidean) distance, but doing so requires solving optimization on a non-convex manifold without a closed-form representation.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet-200 at 64×64, and BuS ultrasound images) and outputs class labels via a ResNet-18 encoder-classifier. Unusually, the training pipeline requires computing the Jacobian matrix of the encoder with respect to the input at each sample and performing SVD on it to extract approximate tangent directions; the encoder is first pre-trained with a contrastive-learning-plus-Jacobian-regularization objective before the DRO fine-tuning stage begins.

## Method

The paper proposes MWDRO, which replaces the standard Wasserstein uncertainty set with one defined via geodesic Wasserstein distance on the data manifold, and derives a tractable dual formulation (Proposition 1) that avoids explicit optimization over the uncertainty set. The key mechanism is a 'manifold-guided game' between InfoNCE contrastive loss and Jacobian Frobenius-norm regularization: CL amplifies feature variation along semantic-variant tangent directions while Jacobian regularization suppresses variation in all other directions, causing the encoder's principal singular vectors to align with the manifold tangent. During the DRO stage, the algorithm performs Riemannian gradient ascent on the manifold to find worst-case perturbations, using the approximate tangent subspace (top singular vectors above a threshold τ₀) for gradient projection and accumulated step-length as a proxy for geodesic distance. Theoretical guarantees (Theorem 1) show that under geodesic β-smoothness, the accumulated-trajectory approximation yields a bounded suboptimality gap controlled by the condition number κ = (ν+β)/(ν−β).

## Results

The paper evaluates MWDRO against four baselines under Gaussian white noise perturbations (variance 0 to 1) on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet-200, and the medical imaging dataset BuS, using ResNet-18 as the backbone. Results are presented as accuracy-vs-noise-variance curves (Figure 3) rather than single-point numbers; the paper reports that MWDRO 'significantly enhances robustness' across all four datasets compared to DropOut+L2, WDRO, KL-DRO, and Randomized Smoothing. Additional evaluations on adversarial attacks and long-tailed (imbalanced) data distributions are provided in the appendix. The paper also reports that the top five singular vectors of the Jacobian capture over 95% of total variance on CIFAR-10, validating the low-rank tangent approximation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MWDRO outperforms all four baselines (DropOut+L2, WDRO, KL-DRO, and Randomized Smoothing) across all four datasets under increasing Gaussian noise, as shown in Figure 3. The advantage is most pronounced at moderate-to-high noise levels, where conventional WDRO (Sinha et al., 2018) and KL-DRO (Levy et al., 2020) degrade more sharply due to their overly pessimistic uncertainty sets that include unrealistic off-manifold distributions. Randomized Smoothing (Cohen et al., 2019) provides certified robustness but lags behind MWDRO in empirical accuracy under natural noise. DropOut+L2 (He et al., 2016), the standard regularization baseline, shows the weakest robustness across all noise levels. The paper notes in the appendix that MWDRO requires more training time than standard WDRO but achieves better accuracy, and ablation studies confirm that both the manifold-guided game (CL + Jacobian regularization) and the MWDRO objective are necessary for the observed gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Wasserstein-based Distributionally Robust Optimization (WDRO) by replacing the standard Euclidean Wasserstein distance with a geodesic Wasserstein distance defined on a learned data manifold. It builds on the strong duality framework of Gao & Kleywegt (2023) and the ν-fixing strategy of Sinha et al. (2018), but departs from prior WDRO methods (Sinha, Bui, Staib) that operate in the ambient space and cannot incorporate manifold constraints. The key methodological novelty is a 'manifold-guided game' that combines InfoNCE contrastive learning (Chen et al., 2020) with Jacobian regularization (Ross & Doshi-Velez, 2018) to extract approximate tangent spaces from the encoder's Jacobian, a mechanism absent from prior DRO or contrastive learning work. Unlike Liu et al. (2022), which uses discrete graph shortest paths to approximate geodesics, this approach learns a continuous, differentiable manifold representation via neural networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized limitation of standard WDRO—its overly pessimistic uncertainty set that includes unrealistic distributions far from the data manifold—by constraining the robustness optimization to geodesic neighborhoods on the learned manifold. The theoretical guarantees (linear convergence of the inner loop, bounded approximation error for the Riemannian gradient) provide a principled foundation, though the robustness constant in Theorem 1 is relatively loose (5.1× when ν≥17β). Practical limitations include the two-stage training procedure, additional computational cost over standard WDRO, and reliance on the low-dimensional manifold assumption that may not hold for all data. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet), whether the manifold assumption degrades under severe distribution shift, and whether the tangent extraction mechanism generalizes beyond image classification to other modalities.
