## Problem

While scaling up neural network parameters has driven major advances in CV and NLP by leveraging simplicity bias to avoid overfitting, this principle remains largely unexplored in deep reinforcement learning, where the field has focused primarily on algorithmic improvements. Standard MLP architectures in deep RL actually degrade in performance when scaled up, because they lack the architectural components that guide optimization toward simple, generalizable functions. The paper asks whether a purpose-built architecture can embed simplicity bias to make parameter scaling beneficial in deep RL.

## Input / Output

SimBa takes raw observation vectors (state vectors ranging from 3 to 223 dimensions in DMC, up to 8268 in Craftax) and produces either a stochastic action distribution (actor) or a scalar value estimate (critic). A key detail is that the Running Statistics Normalization layer tracks per-dimension running mean and variance online during training, normalizing each observation before it enters the network—this differs from standard batch or layer normalization applied to activations. The architecture is algorithm-agnostic: it replaces the MLP in any actor-critic or encoder module (SAC, DDPG, TD-MPC2, PPO, METRA) while keeping the surrounding loss functions and training loop unchanged.

## Method

SimBa is a residual feedforward architecture with three components that collectively amplify simplicity bias: (i) Running Statistics Normalization (RSNorm) standardizes each input dimension using online-tracked mean and variance, preventing high-variance features from dominating learning; (ii) pre-layer normalization residual feedforward blocks (x_{l+1} = x_l + MLP(LayerNorm(x_l))) maintain a direct linear pathway from input to output, applying non-linearity only through the residual branch with an inverted-bottleneck MLP (4× hidden expansion, ReLU); and (iii) a post-layer normalization before the output layer stabilizes activation magnitudes for reliable policy and value predictions. The core insight, validated via Fourier-based complexity analysis, is that the residual linear pathway and normalization layers bias the network toward low-frequency (simple) functions at initialization, which correlates with better generalization as parameters scale from 0.1M to 17M. Scaling width of the critic network is recommended over depth, since additional depth introduces more non-linear components that reduce simplicity bias. The architecture requires no changes to loss functions, replay strategies, or training protocols—only the network module is swapped and widened.

## Results

On the 51-task continuous control benchmark (DMC, MyoSuite, HumanoidBench), SAC + SimBa achieves a mean return of 706.13 on DMC-Hard, outperforming SAC (135.68) by 5.2×, TD-MPC2 (491.33) by 44%, and DreamerV3 (16.07) by 44×. On MyoSuite, SimBa reaches 74.30% mean success rate vs. 60.70% for SAC and 48.17% for DreamerV3. On HumanoidBench, SimBa scores 736.30 mean return vs. 402.31 for SAC and 165.55 for DreamerV3, while TD-MPC2 (787.64) slightly leads but requires 2.5× more compute. SimBa also improves sample efficiency across on-policy (PPO on Craftax) and unsupervised (METRA state coverage) RL settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimBa (SAC) dominates on DMC-Hard, achieving 706.13 mean return vs. 135.68 for vanilla SAC (5.2×), 491.33 for TD-MPC2 (44% higher), and 16.07 for DreamerV3 (44× higher). Against BRO (the strongest off-policy baseline), SimBa leads on DMC-Hard (706 vs. 693), MyoSuite (74.30 vs. 71.40), and HumanoidBench (736 vs. 623), though BRO slightly edges SimBa on DMC Easy&Medium (834 vs. 823). TD-MPC2 is the only method that beats SimBa on any benchmark (HumanoidBench: 788 vs. 736), but requires 2.5× more compute, making SimBa the most compute-efficient choice overall. On MyoSuite, SimBa (74.30) outperforms TD-MPC2 (65.00), TD7 (35.60), and DreamerV3 (48.17). For on-policy RL, PPO + SimBa learns complex Craftax behaviors (iron sword crafting, Orc Mage defeat) significantly faster than PPO with MLP. For unsupervised RL, METRA + SimBa achieves substantially higher state coverage on Humanoid than METRA alone. In architecture comparisons, SimBa exhibits the highest simplicity bias score and best scaling behavior versus BroNet and SpectralNet, while MLP degrades with increased parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the simplicity bias framework well-established in CV and NLP (Fourier-based complexity analysis, residual connections, layer normalization) into deep RL, where prior scaling efforts (BroNet, SpectralNet, ensembling) relied on computationally intensive components or sophisticated training protocols. It departs from those approaches by arguing that the *placement* of simplicity-inducing components—specifically a direct linear residual pathway with pre-layer normalization and a post-layer normalization before the output—matters more than the individual components themselves. The Running Statistics Normalization layer, while conceptually similar to batch normalization, is positioned as a practical necessity for off-policy settings where replay buffer statistics drift over time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a purely architectural swap (no new loss terms, no planning, no replay ratio tricks) brings SAC to match or surpass TD-MPC2, DreamerV3, and TD7 across 51 continuous control tasks, lowering the barrier for practitioners who lack the infrastructure for model-based or representation-learning methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends to pixel-based (vision) RL, model-based RL beyond the preliminary TD-MPC2 results, and multi-task settings. A key limitation is that all experiments use state-based observations; the high-dimensional Craftax result (8268-dim) is the closest proxy for complex inputs but is still symbolic rather than visual.
