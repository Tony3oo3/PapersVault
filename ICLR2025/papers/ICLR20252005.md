## Problem

Existing PII defense methods for LLMs face a fundamental trade-off: gradient-ascent unlearning effectively erases memorized PII but catastrophically degrades model utility (e.g., infinite perplexity), while gradient-descent methods like Error Injection preserve utility but leave PII vulnerable to extraction attacks. No prior approach simultaneously achieves strong PII protection and comparable model performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252005/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fine-tuned LLM (LLaMA2-7b or LLaMA3-8b) that has memorized PII, along with structured unlearning templates of the form 'name + PII type + PII sequence' (e.g., 'John Griffith phone number (713) 853-6247') and a fabricated substitute PII sequence for memory implanting. It produces an updated model where the identified key tokens within each PII sequence are selectively forgotten, while the model retains comparable perplexity and email-completion quality to the fine-tuned baseline. The number of key tokens to forget (k) is a tunable hyperparameter that controls the defense-utility trade-off.

## Method

PPA is a three-stage pipeline inspired by anterograde amnesia. First, Sensitivity Analysis computes a novel 'memorization factor' D_k = (H_k − H_{k+1})/H_k for each token in the PII sequence, where H_i is the cross-entropy loss at position i; the token with the largest D_k is identified as the key element whose presence most reduces uncertainty in predicting subsequent tokens (formally linked to where Newton's direction approaches zero). Second, Selective Forgetting applies gradient ascent (maximizing negative log-likelihood) exclusively on that single key token rather than the entire PII sequence, minimizing collateral damage to model weights. Third, Memory Implanting applies gradient descent on a fabricated substitute PII sequence to restore the model's generative capacity in the affected context. This targeted, token-level approach—forgetting one critical token and re-anchoring with a substitute—achieves 100% phone number protection and 9.8–87.6% address risk reduction while keeping perplexity and GPT-4o email scores within a few percent of the fine-tuned baseline.

## Results

On the Enron email experiment with LLaMA2-7b, PPA achieves a phone number risk score of 0.0 and exact match score of 0.0 across all three attack types (input rephrasing, probing, soft prompt), while maintaining a perplexity of 16.0 and GPT-4o Email Score of 5.2—nearly identical to the fine-tuned baseline (16.2 / 5.0). For physical address defense, PPA reduces the attack-average risk score to 7.3 (vs. 60.8 for the fine-tuned model) with only a 20% perplexity increase (19.5 vs. 16.2). On LLaMA3-8b, PPA similarly achieves 0.0 phone number risk with perplexity 67.5 (vs. 79.2 fine-tuned), and reduces address risk to 14.9 (vs. 48.9 fine-tuned).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPA achieves perfect phone number protection (0.0 risk score) while maintaining model utility comparable to the fine-tuned baseline, a combination no other method achieves. Unlearning (Jang et al., 2022) also reaches 0.0 risk but catastrophically degrades the model (perplexity 3.2×10¹¹, email score 1.1), rendering it unusable. Empty Response (Patil et al., 2023) preserves utility (perplexity 16.5) but leaves phone number risk at 56.4 and address risk at 59.2. Error Injection (De Cao et al., 2021) reduces phone risk to 21.7 and address risk to 9.9 but still leaks PII under soft prompt attacks. DEPN (Wu et al., 2023) achieves moderate phone protection (risk 5.7) but at the cost of 4.8× higher perplexity and a 60% drop in email score. Among differential-privacy baselines, DP Decoding (Majmudar et al., 2022) and JFT (Shi et al., 2022) both leave phone risk above 28, far worse than PPA's 0.0, while offering only marginal utility gains over PPA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the machine unlearning literature for LLMs, specifically the gradient-ascent approach of Jang et al. (2022) and the gradient-descent defenses (Empty Response, Error Injection) of Patil et al. (2023) and De Cao et al. (2021), as well as the memory-editing method DEPN (Wu et al., 2023). It departs from these by introducing a sensitivity-analysis step grounded in a new 'memorization factor' (D_k) derived from cross-entropy loss dynamics, which identifies a single key token whose forgetting is sufficient to break memorization of an entire PII sequence. The method is inspired by anterograde amnesia in cognitive science, framing the problem as selective forgetting of critical memory elements rather than wholesale erasure, and pairs this with a memory-implanting step borrowed from error injection to restore utility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: existing unlearning methods either destroy model utility (gradient ascent → infinite perplexity) or fail to fully protect PII (gradient descent → residual leakage). PPA's 100% phone-number elimination with near-identical perplexity to the fine-tuned baseline is a strong practical result for deployment scenarios. However, the memorization factor theory is relatively thin (the Newton's-method connection in Proposition 1 is heuristic), the evaluation covers only two PII types and two datasets, and the method requires a priori knowledge of which PII to protect. Open questions include whether the single-key-token insight generalizes to structured PII like SSNs or credit cards, whether it extends to relational privacy (who-knows-what), and whether the approach scales to millions of users without the per-user training loop.
