## Problem

While Haase et al. (2023) proved depth lower bounds for ReLU networks restricted to integer weights, no non-constant lower bound existed for the practically relevant case of rational weights (e.g., decimal fractions used in IEEE floating-point arithmetic). This paper provides the first non-constant depth lower bound for ReLU networks with N-ary fraction weights, partially confirming the Hertrich et al. conjecture that F_n = max{0,x_1,...,x_n} requires Ω(log n) hidden layers.

## Input / Output

The method takes the n-variate function F_n = max{0, x_1, ..., x_n} and a constraint that all network weights belong to the ring of N-ary fractions (numbers of the form z/N^t for z ∈ Z, t ∈ Z_+). It produces a lower bound on the minimum number of hidden layers: at least ⌈log_p(n+1)⌉ where p is any prime not dividing N. For decimal fractions (N=10), this yields ⌈log_3(n+1)⌉; for general N, Theorem 4 gives the weaker but universal bound Ω(ln n / ln ln N). The output is a purely theoretical depth guarantee, not a constructed network.

## Method

The proof extends Haase et al.'s integer-weight technique by replacing the fixed divisor 2 with an arbitrary prime p not dividing N. The key mechanism is a three-step argument: (1) Lemma 16 rescales N-ary fraction weights to integer weights by multiplying the function by M^(k+1), reducing the problem to the integer-weight setting; (2) using Hertrich's polyhedral characterization, representability by k hidden layers is linked to membership in SU^k(P_0(Z^n)), and mixed volumes are used to show that when d = p^t, the normalized volume Vol_d is Minkowski-additive modulo p (Proposition 11), which by induction implies all p^k-dimensional faces of polytopes in SU^k have volume divisible by p (Theorem 14); (3) the standard simplex Δ_n scaled by N^(t(k+1)) has normalized volume N^(nt(k+1))·1, which is not divisible by p, yielding a contradiction. The critical new ingredient over Haase et al. is the use of mixed volumes to establish additivity of volume modulo an arbitrary prime p (not just 2) when the dimension is a power of p.

## Results

The paper proves that any ReLU network with N-ary fraction weights requires at least ⌈log_p(n+1)⌉ hidden layers to exactly represent max{0,x_1,…,x_n}, where p is any prime not dividing N (Theorem 2). For the practically important case of decimal fractions (N=10), this yields a lower bound of ⌈log_3(n+1)⌉ hidden layers (Corollary 3). More generally, for arbitrary N-ary fractions the depth is at least Ω(ln n / ln ln N) (Theorem 4), establishing the first non-constant depth lower bound for practically relevant ReLU networks. These bounds are tight up to a constant factor, since Arora et al. (2018) show ⌈log_2(n+1)⌉ layers suffice with integer weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the integer-weight result of Haase et al. (2023), which proves a ⌈log_2(n+1)⌉ lower bound for ReLU networks with integer weights. The current work generalizes this to N-ary fractions by replacing the divisor 2 with an arbitrary prime p not dividing N, yielding ⌈log_p(n+1)⌉. For decimal fractions (N=10), the bound ⌈log_3(n+1)⌉ is weaker than the integer-weight bound ⌈log_2(n+1)⌉ (since log_3 < log_2), but it covers a strictly larger class of networks. The gap between the lower bound (⌈log_3(n+1)⌉) and the known upper bound (⌈log_2(n+1)⌉) for decimal-weight networks is a factor of ln 2 / ln 3 ≈ 0.63 in the exponent. The paper explicitly notes that its results do not follow by simply scaling integer weights to rationals, requiring new number-theoretic and polyhedral techniques (mixed volumes, prime divisibility of binomial coefficients) beyond Haase et al.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the depth-separation framework of Haase et al. (2023), which proved lower bounds for integer-weight ReLU networks using lattice polytopes and normalized volumes, to the broader and practically more relevant setting of N-ary fraction weights (e.g., binary or decimal floating-point). The key technical generalization replaces the divisor 2 with an arbitrary prime p not dividing N, leveraging mixed volumes to establish additivity of normalized volume modulo p when the dimension is a power of p. The polyhedral characterization of representable functions by Hertrich (2022) and the upper bound of Arora et al. (2018) remain the foundational tools, while the prime-number-theoretic argument in Theorem 4 (using the prime number theorem to bound the smallest prime not dividing N) is new to this line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-constant depth lower bound for ReLU networks with rational weights, which directly covers the floating-point arithmetic used in all practical neural network implementations and quantized inference. It partially confirms Conjecture 1 (Hertrich et al., 2021) for rational weights, showing that even with arbitrary width and arbitrarily large denominators, constant-depth networks cannot exactly represent max{0,x_1,...,x_n}. The main open question remains whether the bound can be tightened to ceil(log_2(n+1)) for general real weights, and whether the choice of subfield (e.g., Q vs. R) fundamentally affects expressiveness (Question 18). The algebraic invariant framework (volume modulo p as a group homomorphism) provides a template for future depth-separation results.
