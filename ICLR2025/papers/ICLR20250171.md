## Problem

Most prior work on Chinese handwriting generation focuses on individual characters, leaving the significantly more challenging task of generating complete text lines largely unexplored. The core difficulty is twofold: ensuring structural correctness of each character while imitating a specific writing style, and arranging the relative positions of characters (especially between Chinese characters and punctuation marks) to produce a natural, coherent layout.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250171/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target text content (a sequence of character categories) and one or more style reference samples (coherent handwritten text lines from a target author) as input, and produces a full online handwritten Chinese text line as a sequence of 3D trajectory points [horizontal, vertical, pen-state], where pen-state distinguishes touching from lifting. Unlike static image outputs, the generated data preserves dynamic stroke-order information, enabling interactive applications. Individual characters are generated at a normalized standard height and then scaled to fit their assigned bounding boxes.

## Method

The paper proposes a hierarchical factorization that decouples layout generation from glyph synthesis. A 2-layer LSTM layout generator autoregressively predicts each character's bounding box (height, width, vertical center, horizontal offset) using in-context-like learning: the true bounding boxes of the reference text line are fed as a prefix, and the LSTM's hidden state implicitly encodes the author's layout style for subsequent planning. For glyph synthesis, a 1D U-Net denoiser within a DDPM framework generates each character conditioned on a character embedding dictionary (encoding structural information) and a multi-scale calligraphy style encoder that extracts features at three scales via continuous downsampling, injected into the U-Net through QKV cross-attention at matching resolutions. The style encoder is trained with a multi-scale contrastive loss that pulls features from the same author together and pushes different authors apart at each scale, while the denoiser is trained with standard diffusion noise-prediction loss.

## Results

On the CASIA-OLHWDB/ICDAR-2013 character generation benchmark, the proposed method achieves a DTW of 0.932, Content Score of 0.891, and Style Score of 0.918, setting the best Style Score among purely data-driven approaches (vs. 0.481 for DiffWriter and 0.432 for DeepImitator). For full text line generation, the in-context layout generator reduces mean geometric feature deviations (e.g., ∇₂ from 0.151 to 0.122) compared to the Gaussian baseline while maintaining AR and CR above 0.85, confirming structurally correct characters. A subjective test with 20 participants shows over 85% of real/synthesized pairs are judged as written by the same person.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among purely data-driven methods, the proposed approach dominates: it improves Style Score from 0.481 (DiffWriter) to 0.918 and DTW from 0.997 to 0.932, while also surpassing DeepImitator on all three metrics. Against style-transfer baselines that require a standard printed font as auxiliary input, the method is competitive on Style Score (0.918 vs. 0.945 for SDT) but trails SDT on DTW (0.932 vs. 0.880) and Content Score (0.891 vs. 0.970), reflecting the trade-off of not using a reference glyph for structural guidance. WriteLikeU achieves a higher Content Score (0.938) but a substantially lower Style Score (0.711), indicating weaker style fidelity. For layout generation, the in-context method outperforms both the Gaussian statistical baseline and unconditional generation on 6 of 8 binary geometric features, with the largest gains on horizontal spacing metrics (∇₂: 0.122 vs. 0.151; ∇₅: 0.129 vs. 0.163), confirming that reference-conditioned layout planning better captures author-specific spacing habits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based online Chinese character generation (DiffWriter, CHIRODIFF) from individual glyphs to full text lines by introducing a hierarchical decomposition into layout and glyph synthesis. The layout generator draws on the in-context learning paradigm from LLMs, repurposing it for spatial arrangement: an LSTM conditioned on reference bounding boxes as a prefix autoregressively predicts character positions. The glyph synthesizer builds on prior stylized character generators (DeepImitator, WriteLikeU, SDT) but replaces their single-scale CNN style encoders with a multi-scale 1D convolutional encoder trained via contrastive learning, fused into a 1D U-Net denoiser through QKV cross-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to address complete online Chinese handwritten text line generation, a task the authors identify as largely unexplored, and the hierarchical layout-glyph decomposition provides a practical and interpretable framework. A key limitation is that independent per-character generation cannot capture cursive stroke connections between characters, which the authors acknowledge requires an end-to-end approach. Open questions include whether synthesized lines can serve as effective augmentation data for recognizers, and whether the hierarchical approach generalizes to more complex handwritten content such as mathematical formulas.
