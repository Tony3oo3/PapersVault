## Problem

Temporal heterogeneous graphs are increasingly central to relational deep learning applications, but their rich structural, feature, and temporal information makes individual nodes highly identifiable, creating significant privacy exposure risks. Existing graph generative models either ignore privacy guarantees, do not jointly handle temporal and heterogeneous structure, or rely on diffusion-based approaches with O(Ln²) complexity that are infeasible at scale. No prior method simultaneously provides differential privacy guarantees, preserves utility, and scales to million-node temporal heterogeneous graphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a temporal heterogeneous graph represented as a sequence of T snapshots, where each snapshot is an adjacency matrix over nodes with types (and edge types determined by node-type pairs), and produces a synthetic temporal heterogeneous graph of the same schema. Internally, the graph is consumed as sampled heterogeneous random walks (sequences of timestamped typed nodes of length l), and the output is assembled from generated walks via a scoring matrix and meta-path frequency table, yielding a full graph with edges assigned to snapshots by node timestamps.

## Method

THEPUFF operates in two stages. First, a differential privacy perturbation mechanism applies edge-add and edge-delete flips to each snapshot with probabilities 1/e^{ε+} and 1/e^{ε-} respectively, where ε+ and ε- are data-driven values chosen to preserve the degree distribution (Eq. 4), yielding a privacy-guaranteed perturbed graph Ĝ. Second, an adversarial training loop uses an LSTM-based Generator that sequentially samples timestamped typed nodes (via Gumbel-Softmax for type and embedding-distance matching for node selection from Ĝ), a Privacy Discriminator and a Utility Discriminator (both tri-level self-attention over time, node, and type levels), where the generator is trained to fool both discriminators simultaneously. The Utility Discriminator is pre-trained separately with random negatives to decouple it from the min-max game, and a final assembler converts generated walks into a graph using edge-count scoring matrices and meta-path frequency sampling.

## Results

THEPUFF is the only method that successfully generates temporal heterogeneous graphs on all four datasets, including large-scale DBLP (17,876 nodes), ML-20M (165,790 nodes, 20M edges), and Taobao (1M nodes), where all diffusion-based baselines (EDGE, DISCO, GraphMaker) either produce empty graphs or run out of memory. On ML-100k, THEPUFF achieves the best Cluster score (2.536e-3) and lowest EO-Rate (2.247e-3), indicating strong structural fidelity and privacy. In the attack experiment on DBLP, THEPUFF reduces the successful attack probability to 0.006%, the lowest among all methods (vs. 0.093% for the original graph, 0.009% for HGEN, and 0.008% for DPGGAN). Training time on ML-100k is ~350s for THEPUFF vs. ~7 hours for EDGE and ~4 hours for GraphMaker.

## Baselines & Comparisons

THEPUFF is the only method that successfully generates non-empty temporal heterogeneous graphs on all four datasets; EDGE produces an empty graph on DBLP, and EDGE, DISCO, and GraphMaker all OOM on DBLP, ML-20M, and Taobao. On ML-100k, THEPUFF achieves the best Cluster (2.536e-3 vs. 3.960e-3 for EDGE) and best EO-Rate (2.247e-3 vs. 5.488e-3 for EDGE), though it trails HGEN on Degree (3.547e-1 vs. 1.395e-1) and Meta-2 (1.664 vs. 1.469). In the degree-based attack experiment on DBLP, THEPUFF achieves the lowest successful attack probability (0.006%) compared to DPGGAN (0.008%), HGEN (0.009%), and the original graph (0.093%), with the smallest standard deviation (9.0e-5). Across all four datasets in the extended attack experiments (Tables 9–10), THEPUFF consistently achieves the lowest attack probability, including in the type-distinguishable setting where it reaches 0.095% on ML-100k (vs. 11.73% original) and 3.6e-4% on Taobao (vs. 0.021% original). In terms of efficiency, THEPUFF trains in ~350s on ML-100k with ~712MB GPU usage, compared to ~7 hours and ~12GB for EDGE, ~4 hours and ~1100MB for GraphMaker, and >32GB for DISCO.

## Relation to Prior Work

THEPUFF sits at the intersection of three prior research threads: (1) walk-based GANs for graph generation (HGEN for heterogeneous graphs, TagGen/TG-GAN for temporal graphs), (2) diffusion-based graph generators (EDGE, DISCO, GraphMaker) that the authors argue are too expensive at O(n²) for large-scale graphs, and (3) differential-privacy graph perturbation (DPGGAN, edge-level DP). The key departure is the dual-discriminator adversarial design: a privacy discriminator trained against a DP-perturbed graph and a utility discriminator trained against the original graph, with the generator sampling nodes from the perturbed graph to inherit privacy. This contrasts with prior privacy-preserving generators (e.g., DPGGAN) that inject noise directly into the generation process rather than using an explicit perturbation-then-adversarial pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly handle temporal, heterogeneous, and privacy-guaranteed graph generation at scale (up to 1M nodes), filling a gap where prior methods address at most two of these three dimensions. The practical efficiency advantage over diffusion baselines (350s vs. hours or OOM) makes it relevant for industrial-scale relational data. However, the privacy guarantee is conditional on discriminator convergence (Claim 4.1), the attack model evaluated is relatively simple (degree-based), and the six proposed utility/privacy metrics are not yet community standards, leaving open whether they capture all relevant aspects of temporal heterogeneous graph fidelity. The non-monotonic EO-Rate curve (privacy degrades at both very small and very large ε) suggests a delicate tuning regime that may not generalize cleanly across graph topologies.
