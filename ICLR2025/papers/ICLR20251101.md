## Problem

Multi-grained video editing—simultaneously modifying objects at class, instance, and part levels—remains unsolved because diffusion models inherently treat different instances of the same class as homogeneous feature segments, causing cross-instance feature coupling and semantic misalignment between text prompts and spatial regions. Existing video editing methods are instance-agnostic, mixing features across distinct objects and failing to apply separate edits to co-occurring instances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251101/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (16–32 frames), a global text prompt, and a set of region-specific prompt–mask pairs (τ_k, m_k) obtained via SAM-Track instance segmentation or K-Means clustering of self-attention features, with optional ControlNet depth/pose maps for structural guidance. It produces an edited video of the same length and resolution, operating entirely in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.5 with 50 DDIM inversion and denoising steps. The ST-Layout Attn modulation is applied only during the first 15 denoising steps, and Latent Blend preserves non-edited background regions by blending inversion latents back into the denoising trajectory.

## Method

VideoGrain introduces Spatial-Temporal Layout-Guided Attention (ST-Layout Attn), a unified modulation of both cross- and self-attention in a frozen diffusion model, requiring no parameter tuning. In cross-attention, each local prompt's query-key scores are amplified within its target spatial mask and suppressed outside it (M_pos = max(QK^T) − QK^T, M_neg = QK^T − min(QK^T)), enabling precise text-to-region control so that, e.g., 'Spiderman' attends only to the left man. In self-attention, the full video is treated as a single spatial-temporal token sequence; positive scores are boosted for token pairs within the same region across all frames while negative scores are suppressed for cross-region pairs, preventing same-class feature coupling. The modulation strength is scaled by λ = ξ(t)·(1−S_i), where ξ(t) = 0.3·t⁵ (self) or t⁵ (cross) controls temporal refinement and S_i is a size-regulation term that increases modulation for smaller regions. The pipeline first performs DDIM Inversion, clusters self-attention features for coarse semantic layout, refines with SAM-Track for instance masks, then applies ST-Layout Attn during denoising.

## Results

On a 76 video-text pair benchmark (DAVIS, TGVE, Internet), VideoGrain achieves the best scores across all automatic and human evaluation metrics: CLIP-T 36.56, Q-edit 25.75, Warp-Err 1.42, and human-rated Overall 83.0. The closest competitor, DMT (T2V-based), trails by 2.47 on CLIP-T, 9.12 on Q-edit, and 18.5 on Overall. In efficiency, VideoGrain is also the fastest (3.83 min) and most memory-efficient (15.94 GB GPU, 4.42 GB RAM) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoGrain outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against T2I-based methods, FateZero (CLIP-T 33.78, Q-edit 10.96) and ControlVideo (CLIP-T 34.41, Q-edit 7.27) suffer from same-class feature coupling, editing both instances identically; TokenFlow (Q-edit 12.28) loses fine details through keyframe interpolation. Ground-A-Video (Edit-Acc 69.0, Overall 63.2) is the strongest T2I baseline thanks to grounding priors but still fails on instance-level separation when bounding boxes overlap. The T2V-based DMT (Q-edit 16.63, Overall 64.5) is the closest competitor, leveraging video generation priors for temporal coherence, yet still blends features across instances (e.g., panda and toy poodle). In the appendix, VideoP2P with SAM-Track masks also fails at joint multi-region editing, confirming that layout masks alone are insufficient without the proposed attention modulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion / DDIM Inversion framework and repurposes SAM-Track for instance-level segmentation and Latent Blend for background preservation, but its core departure is from prior video editing methods (FateZero, ControlVideo, TokenFlow, Ground-A-Video, DMT) that are instance-agnostic and suffer from same-class feature coupling. The key conceptual advance is a unified modulation of both cross-attention (for text-to-region control, extending Kim et al.'s layout-guided attention to video) and self-attention (for inter-region feature separation, drawing on DIFT-style cross-frame correspondence), which prior methods addressed only partially or not at all. Unlike TAV or FateZero that require one-shot tuning, VideoGrain operates entirely zero-shot by modifying attention scores during denoising without any parameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formally defining multi-grained video editing (class, instance, part) and showing that the root cause of failure is feature homogeneity across same-class instances in diffusion self-attention, the paper provides a diagnostic framework that explains why a broad family of prior methods fail. The zero-shot, training-free nature makes it practically deployable, and the attention modulation technique is potentially transferable to other layout-conditioned generation tasks. However, the method is constrained by the T2I base model (SD 1.5), struggles with large shape deformations, and the evaluation on 76 video-text pairs with 5 baselines, while thorough, leaves open whether the approach generalizes to T2V foundation models or longer/more complex videos.
