## Problem

Standard vertical federated learning (VFL) requires all feature partitions to be available during both training and inference, but in practice samples often have missing blocks and clients may leave the federation, rendering the model unusable. Existing remedies either waste data (discarding incomplete samples or ignoring available features), add multi-stage training pipelines with auxiliary modules, or lack convergence guarantees. The paper asks whether an efficient VFL method can be designed that is robust to arbitrary missing features at both training and inference, with provable convergence, without wasting any data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252602/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes feature blocks from K clients where each sample may have only a subset of blocks observed (e.g., four quadrants of a CIFAR image, different sensor channels in HAPT, or different chart events in MIMIC-IV). At inference, any subset of clients that observe their block can participate; each such client independently produces a class label or regression prediction using the available representations. A key design constraint is that mini-batches are selected via a shared seed so that all samples in a batch share the same set of observed blocks, enabling consistent collaborative training across clients.

## Method

LASER-VFL shares a single set of K representation models and K fusion models across an exponential number of predictors (2^(K-1) per client), where each predictor handles a different subset of feature blocks. The fusion model at each client uses a non-parameterized averaging aggregation over the available representations, making it size-invariant so the same weights handle any number of inputs. During training, each client samples K_o tasks (one per subset size) from the power set of observed blocks containing its own block, and the loss is weighted by a_i = (1/i)·C(K_o−1, i−1) to yield an unbiased gradient estimate with only linear per-iteration cost. This task-sampling mechanism effectively regularizes training (analogous to dropout), which explains why LASER-VFL outperforms standard VFL even when all features are present. The paper proves O(1/√T) convergence for nonconvex objectives and linear convergence to an O(σ_b² + K·σ_s²) neighborhood under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with K=4 clients, LASER-VFL achieves 72.3% accuracy when all features are observed (p_miss=0.0/0.0), outperforming Standard VFL (64.7%) and PlugVFL (66.0%). Under heavy missingness (p_miss=0.5/0.5), LASER-VFL retains 51.9% accuracy while Standard VFL collapses to 1.4% and Combinatorial drops to 31.6%. On MIMIC-IV (F1-score), LASER-VFL reaches 92.0 (p_miss=0.0/0.0) and 82.0 (p_miss=0.5/0.5), versus 91.6 and 51.8 for Standard VFL. The method is hyperparameter-free and achieves O(1/√T) convergence for nonconvex objectives, with linear convergence under the PL inequality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Standard VFL (FedBCD, Liu et al. 2022), LASER-VFL wins in every setting: when all features are present it gains 2–8 points (e.g., CIFAR-100: 72.3% vs 64.7%; CIFAR-10: 91.5% vs 89.7%), and under 50% missingness Standard VFL collapses to near-random (CIFAR-100: 1.4%) while LASER-VFL retains 51.9%. Against VFedTrans (Huang et al. 2023), LASER-VFL is substantially better—VFedTrans performs nearly identically to the Local baseline (HAPT: 82.5% vs 82.3%) despite a 22× longer training pipeline, whereas LASER-VFL reaches 94.2% on HAPT. Against PlugVFL (Sun et al. 2024), LASER-VFL consistently outperforms by 1–11 points depending on the dataset and missingness level (e.g., CIFAR-100 p_miss=0.5/0.5: 51.9% vs 41.2%; CIFAR-10 p_miss=0.0/0.0: 91.5% vs 89.4%). Notably, LASER-VFL is never outperformed by more than one standard deviation across all 45 experimental configurations in Table 1, and it also beats the Combinatorial (exhaustive per-subset training) and Ensemble baselines in every setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

LASER-VFL builds directly on the split neural network architecture for vertical federated learning (Ceballos et al., 2020) and the standard VFL training paradigm (Liu et al., 2022), modifying them only through parameter sharing across an exponential family of predictors and a task-sampling mechanism during training. It departs from prior missing-feature VFL methods (self-supervised/semi-supervised approaches, knowledge distillation pipelines, PlugVFL) by simultaneously handling arbitrary subsets of feature blocks during both training and inference without multi-stage pipelines or auxiliary modules. The paper also provides convergence guarantees (O(1/√T) for nonconvex objectives, linear under PL) that prior VFL methods addressing missing features lack entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical practical barrier to VFL deployment: real-world feature partitions are rarely fully available, and standard VFL models become unusable when any client's data is missing. The method's simplicity (no extra hyperparameters, no multi-stage pipeline) and its surprising regularization benefit even in the full-data regime make it a practical drop-in improvement for VFL practitioners. Open questions include extending to settings where different clients hold different labels (multi-task VFL), handling non-random missingness mechanisms, and investigating whether the task-sampling regularization effect persists at larger scale or with deeper architectures.
