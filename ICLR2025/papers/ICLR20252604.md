## Problem

Existing diffusion and flow-based generative models on Riemannian manifolds (e.g., RSGM, RDM, RFM) require multiple approximations—projecting scores to tangent spaces, projecting samples back to the manifold, and simulation-based training data collection—that compound to degrade generation quality. Moreover, prior methods must learn scores in position-dependent tangent spaces, making the learning problem geometrically complex and implementation-heavy. This paper asks whether the group structure of Lie groups can be exploited to eliminate all such approximations while retaining the simplicity of Euclidean score learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes Lie group training data (e.g., 2D/7D torsion angles on tori, n×n rotation matrices in SO(n), or n×n unitary matrices in U(n)) and produces new samples on the same Lie group. During sampling, initialization is drawn from the stationary distribution: the Haar measure on G for the position variable and an isotropic Gaussian on the Lie algebra for the trivialized momentum. The score network is a standard Euclidean-valued neural network whose input is the pair (g, ξ, t) and whose output is a vector in the fixed Lie algebra space, avoiding any geometry-dependent parameterization.

## Method

TDM introduces a left-trivialized momentum ξ ∈ 𝔤 (the Lie algebra, a fixed vector space) so that the true momentum T_e L_g ξ is simply the matrix product gξ. The forward noising process is the trivialized kinetic Langevin dynamics with U(g)=0, converging to Haar×Gaussian; its time reversal (Theorem 1) requires only the score ∇_ξ log p_{T-t}(g,ξ), which lives in the fixed Lie algebra rather than in the position-dependent tangent space T_gG. Training uses Denoising Score Matching for Abelian groups (where the conditional transition is a Wrapped Normal, giving a closed-form target) or Implicit Score Matching for non-Abelian groups. A key implementation choice is the Operator Splitting Integrator, which alternates an exact g-update via g←g·expm(hξ) and an exact ξ-update via exponential integration of the linear OU component, guaranteeing that all iterates remain exactly on G×𝔤 without any projection step. This eliminates the three approximation sources (tangent-space projection, manifold projection, and simulation-based data collection) present in prior Riemannian diffusion methods.

## Results

On the Protein/RNA torsion angle benchmarks, TDM achieves the lowest test NLL across all five datasets, with particularly large gains on Proline (−0.60 vs. RFM's 0.15) and RNA 7D (−6.86 vs. RFM's −5.20). On the SO(3) 32-component mixture, TDM attains a log-likelihood of 0.292±0.07, surpassing RSGM's 0.20±0.03. TDM also outperforms RFM on the challenging Pacman and checkerboard torus datasets, and is the first method to report results on high-dimensional SO(n) (n>3) and U(n) quantum evolution operator generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDM consistently outperforms both RDM (Huang et al., 2022) and RFM (Chen & Lipman, 2024) on all five Protein/RNA torsion angle datasets, with NLL improvements of 0.3–0.7 over RFM and 0.3–3.2 over RDM; the largest gap appears on the 7D RNA dataset where TDM reaches −6.86 versus RFM's −5.20. On the SO(3) 32-component mixture, TDM achieves a log-likelihood of 0.292±0.07, beating RSGM (De Bortoli et al., 2022) at 0.20±0.03. On the torus experiments (checkerboard, Pacman), TDM produces visibly sharper and more faithful multi-modal distributions than RFM, which the authors note degrades on patterns larger than 4×4. TDM is also the first method to report generative results on SO(n) for n>3 and on U(n) quantum evolution operators, where no prior baseline exists for direct comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

TDM builds directly on the trivialized kinetic Langevin dynamics framework of Kong & Tao (2024) and the variational Lie group optimization of Tao & Ohsawa (2020), repurposing them as a forward noising process for generative modeling. It departs from the dominant Riemannian diffusion/flow-matching lineage (RSGM, RDM, RFM) by exploiting the group structure to keep the learned score in a fixed Lie algebra rather than a position-dependent tangent space, thereby eliminating the tangent-space and manifold projections that those methods require. The operator splitting integrator, previously used for sampling (Kong & Tao, 2024), is here adapted as a projection-free, manifold-preserving sampler for the backward generative process. The approach is conceptually closest to FrameDiff (Yim et al., 2023b) in spirit—both achieve fixed-space score learning—but TDM generalizes beyond SO(3)/SE(3) to arbitrary connected compact Lie groups including U(n).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing all three approximation sources (tangent-space projection, manifold projection, and simulation-based training data projection) that plague prior Riemannian generative models, TDM offers a cleaner, more scalable path to high-fidelity generation on Lie groups. The first-ever generative modeling of U(n) data opens a direct route to learning ensembles of quantum time-evolution operators, a problem with immediate relevance to quantum simulation and high-energy physics. The main limitation is that trivialization requires group structure and does not extend to general Riemannian manifolds (though homogeneous spaces are a plausible next step), and the score parameterization and training recipe (e.g., preconditioning, EMA) remain areas for improvement. Open questions include whether the method scales to very high-dimensional Lie groups and whether the simulation-free DSM objective can be extended beyond Abelian groups.
