## Problem

Traditional f-divergences require their generating functions to be convex, which prevents them from capturing certain natural distances between distributions, notably the metric induced by Bayes Hilbert spaces. This convexity constraint also limits the flexibility of divergence-based generative models in learning diverse or heavy-tailed data. The paper asks whether relaxing convexity locally near the origin yields a well-defined divergence framework that can bridge f-divergences and Bayes Hilbert spaces.

## Input / Output

The generative model takes a random noise vector z ~ N(0, I_{100×100}) and produces a sample (a 32×32 RGB image for CIFAR-10 or a 28×28 grayscale image for MNIST). The discriminator maps real or generated samples to a real-valued score, which is then passed through the BHS-specific activation g_f(x) = −1 + exp(x) to constrain the variational function to the domain [−1, ∞) of the mixed conjugate. For the synthetic heavy-tailed experiment, the output is a scalar sample from an estimated lognormal distribution rather than an image.

## Method

The paper introduces a mixed conjugate f*_* that applies the convex Fenchel conjugate on the convex region [a,∞) and the concave conjugate on the concave region [0,a), enabling a valid dual representation for locally non-convex generating functions. The key pseudo-divergence uses f_{BHS}(x) = x·log(x)², which is concave on [0, e^{−1}] and convex on [e^{−1}, ∞), and provably equals the squared Bayes Hilbert space metric plus a mean-log term: D_{f_{BHS}}(μ,ν) = d²_{B²}(p_μ,p_ν) + E_μ[log(μ⊖ν)]². A variational lower bound (Corollary 4.4) is derived that is tight for f_{BHS}, reducing the original sup+inf optimization to a single min-max game over a unified discriminator. In the GAN implementation, the discriminator's raw output is composed with g_f(x) = −1 + exp(x) to respect the mixed conjugate's domain, and the generator loss uses the explicit closed-form mixed conjugate f*_{BHS}(t) = 2(−1±√(1+t))·exp(−1±√(1+t)), with the sign determined by whether t lies in [−1,0) or [0,∞).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, BHSGAN achieves a FID of 31.26±0.08, outperforming all f-GAN variants (KL: 37.50, Pearson: 33.60, GAN: 33.60, Reverse KL: 85.27) and approaching the Wasserstein GAN (30.81±0.12). On the synthetic lognormal distribution estimation task, BHSGAN recovers the true shape parameter most accurately (0.5049 vs. true 0.5), while KL GAN overestimates it nearly 2× (0.9951) and standard GAN severely underestimates it (0.1952). The paper also reports MNIST results with qualitative visual comparisons showing BHSGAN produces the most refined image structures among f-GAN variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the f-GAN family (Nowozin et al., 2016), BHSGAN clearly wins on CIFAR-10 FID, improving over the best f-GAN variant (Pearson/GAN at 33.60) by ~2.3 points and over KL GAN by ~6.2 points. On the lognormal distribution estimation task, BHSGAN achieves the most accurate shape parameter (0.5049 vs. true 0.5), while KL GAN overestimates it by ~2× (0.9951) and standard GAN underestimates it by ~60% (0.1952). Against the Wasserstein GAN (Arjovsky et al., 2017), the comparison is essentially a tie on CIFAR-10 FID (31.26 vs. 30.81, a difference of 0.45 points well within the reported standard deviations), but BHSGAN substantially outperforms WGAN on lognormal shape estimation (0.5049 vs. 0.4220) and scale estimation (1.0937 vs. 1.6670). The paper notes the WGAN FID advantage is 'negligible and unlikely to be conclusive,' while emphasizing that BHSGAN additionally guarantees alignment in the Bayes Hilbert space metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational f-divergence estimation framework of Nguyen et al. (2010) and the f-GAN architecture of Nowozin et al. (2016) by relaxing the convexity constraint on divergence-generating functions, introducing a 'mixed conjugate' that handles locally non-convex functions. It then bridges this generalized framework to Bayes Hilbert spaces (Van den Boogaart et al., 2014), showing that the BHS induced metric corresponds to a pseudo f-divergence with generating function f(x) = x·log(x)². The connection to WGAN (Arjovsky et al., 2017) is primarily as a comparison baseline, though the BHS GAN borrows its gradient penalty for training stability. Conceptually, the paper departs from the f-divergence literature by proving that convexity is not strictly necessary for well-defined divergences, and from the Bayes Hilbert space literature by moving beyond functional regression to finite-sample generative estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical bridge between f-divergences and Bayes Hilbert spaces is the main contribution: it enables sampling from learned BHS distributions in high-dimensional settings, circumventing the curse of dimensionality that limits functional regression approaches in the BHS literature. The tightness of the variational bound for the BHS divergence (unlike general f-divergences) is a clean theoretical result with practical implications for optimization stability. However, the empirical gains are modest—BHSGAN achieves FID 31.26 vs. WGAN's 30.81 on CIFAR-10—and the paper itself acknowledges that more advanced architectures (StyleGAN, VQGAN) and training procedures are needed for competitive performance. Open questions include whether the framework scales to Bayesian posterior sampling in high dimensions and whether the pseudo-divergence perspective generalizes beyond the specific f_BHS function.
