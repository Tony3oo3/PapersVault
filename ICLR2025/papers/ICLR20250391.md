## Problem

While matrix sketching has been applied to least squares with standard convex regularization or convex constraints, no prior work established minimax optimal estimation rates for sparse signal recovery under general convex or nonconvex regularization via sketching. Additionally, existing iterative sketching methods (e.g., IHS) require resampling the projection matrix and recomputing the sketched data at every iteration, incurring substantial computational overhead for large-scale problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250391/cropped_page13_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a design matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} (typically low-rank with r = rank(X) ≪ n), a response vector y ∈ R^n, and a regularization weight λ, and produces an approximate parameter vector β̃* that estimates the true sparse signal β̄ with minimax optimal ℓ² error of order O(√(s̄ log d / n)), where s̄ = ‖β̄‖₀. The sketch size ñ is a small fraction of n (e.g., ñ = O((r + log(1/δ))·(ρ+1)²/ρ²) for Gaussian embeddings), and the iterative procedure requires only a logarithmic number of iterations N in the desired accuracy.

## Method

SRO sketches the data matrix via a single oblivious ℓ²-subspace embedding P (Gaussian or sparse) to form X̃ = PX, then solves the reduced regularized problem. The key innovation is Iterative SRO, which reuses the same projection matrix P across all iterations and applies SRO to the residual at each step, provably reducing the approximation error ‖β⁽ᵗ⁾ − β*‖_X geometrically by a factor ρ per iteration. The theoretical analysis introduces a degree-of-nonconvexity measure based on the Fréchet subdifferential, yielding a universal approximation error bound (Theorem D.2) that unifies convex and certain nonconvex regularizers (SCAD, MCP, capped-ℓ¹). This framework establishes, for the first time, minimax optimal rates for both sparse convex (Lasso) and sparse nonconvex learning via sketching, with the nonconvex case requiring only mild conditions such as RIP or ε = O(√(log d/n)).

## Results

The paper establishes minimax optimal parameter estimation error of O(√(s̄ log d / n)) for both sparse convex (Theorem 4.1) and sparse nonconvex (Theorem 4.5) learning via sketching. In the Lasso signal recovery experiment (n=5000, d=100000, rank ≤ n/100), Iterative SRO with Gaussian subspace embedding achieves ℓ² error of 0.008±0.003 to the true β̄ at sketch size γ=12, nearly matching the oracle solution β* (0.007±0.001), while one-time SRO alone yields 0.067±0.092. For Generalized Lasso (n=80000, d=600), Iterative SRO reduces running time from 11.04s (SRO) to 4.71s while achieving the same approximation error of 0.036, and is faster than the IHS variant (5.11s) that resamples the projection at each iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For subspace clustering on the Extended Yale-B Dataset (1024×2414 face images, 38 subjects), SRO-GSE and SRO-SSE outperform SMCE (Elhamifar & Vidal, 2011), SSC-OMP (Dyer et al., 2013), K-means, and Spectral Clustering by a notable margin in both Accuracy and NMI, and even surpass the original Noisy SSC (Wang & Xu, 2013) for most values of λ, attributed to sketching reducing the adverse effect of noise. For Lasso signal recovery, Iterative SRO with Gaussian subspace embedding achieves error within 0.001–0.005 of the oracle β* across all sketch sizes, while one-time SRO is 5–10× worse (e.g., 0.067 vs 0.007 at γ=12). For Generalized Lasso, Iterative SRO matches the approximation error of the IHS variant (both 0.036) but runs 8% faster (4.71s vs 5.11s) by reusing a single projection matrix, and is 57% faster than one-time SRO (11.04s). The key advantage over IHS (Pilanci & Wainwright, 2016) is that Iterative SRO requires only one sketch computation rather than N separate sketches, reducing time complexity from O(Nnd log ñ + NMñd) to O(nnz(X) + NMñd).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Iterative Hessian Sketch (IHS) framework of Pilanci & Wainwright (2016) from convex constrained least-squares to regularized least-squares with general convex or nonconvex penalties, and departs from IHS by using a single fixed projection matrix throughout all iterations rather than resampling at each step. It also builds on the sketching-for-regularized-optimization line of Yang & Li (2021) but goes substantially further by establishing minimax optimal estimation rates for sparse signal recovery, which prior sketching work had not addressed. The theoretical machinery draws on subspace embedding theory (Johnson-Lindenstrauss, Clarkson & Woodruff) and the degree-of-nonconvexity framework from nonconvex sparse estimation (Zhang & Zhang, 2012; Wang et al., 2014).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central contribution is showing that sketching can achieve the minimax optimal rate O(√(s log d / n)) for sparse *nonconvex* learning (e.g., SCAD, MCP), a problem the authors note was previously considered open and difficult in the sketching literature. This unifies convex and nonconvex sparse estimation under a single sketching framework with sharp guarantees, and the single-projection-matrix design of Iterative SRO offers concrete computational savings over IHS for large-scale problems. Limitations include the requirement n ≫ rank(X) for most convex results, specific smoothness conditions on the nonconvex regularizer, and empirical validation primarily on synthetic data with one real dataset (Extended Yale-B). Open questions include whether the minimax rates extend to higher-order or structured nonconvex penalties, and whether the approach scales to deep learning or kernel methods.
