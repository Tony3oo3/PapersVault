## Problem

Existing non-rigid point cloud registration methods minimize holistic shape deviations via Chamfer Distance, which produces physically implausible collapses and tearing when the target geometry is occluded. This paper identifies the distance metric as the root cause and tackles the under-explored problem of registering a complete source shape to a partially occluded target using coordinate-based neural networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251195/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D point clouds (a complete source and a possibly occluded target), normalizes both to zero-mean unit-variance, and outputs a continuous deformation field parameterized by a coordinate-based MLP. Because the deformation is a smooth neural function rather than per-point displacements, it naturally supports downstream tasks such as shape interpolation at arbitrary timestamps and mesh hole filling without additional annotations. The entire pipeline is a test-time optimization requiring no training data or labels.

## Method

The core contribution replaces Chamfer Distance with the Maximum Correntropy Criterion (MCC), a Gaussian-kernel-based local similarity measure that adaptively down-weights occluded points during optimization, preventing the collapses and tearing that CD induces. To handle source regions with no correspondence in the target, the paper introduces Locally Linear Reconstruction (LLR), which computes fixed reconstruction weights from k-nearest neighbors via a closed-form constrained least-squares solution and uses them as a smoothness regularizer on the displacement field. The deformation is parameterized by a 3-layer MLP (128 neurons per layer) with SIREN periodic activations, optimized unsupervised at test time via Adam for 200 epochs with a combined MCC + LLR objective. The paper further proves that CD is a special case of MCC valid only when both shapes are complete and sufficiently close, positioning correntropy as a more universal point cloud similarity measure.

## Results

On the occluded Open-CAS liver dataset, OAR achieves EPE of 8.66/5.69/12.11 and AccR of 96.8%/97.2%/56.6% across three liver pairs, with zero outlier ratio in all cases—substantially outperforming the next-best method GBCPD (EPE 11.85/9.17/16.24, AccR 65.3%/75.5%/53.2%). On the TOSCA occlusion benchmark (cat, horse, dog), OAR attains the highest AccR (77.2%, 65.3%, 89.8%) and lowest EPE (2.56, 2.25, 2.47) among all neural and optimization-based baselines. On 4DMatch, Lepard+OAR achieves EPE 0.059 and AccR 74.33%, matching or exceeding the supervised Lepard+NDP (EPE 0.077, AccR 74.12%) while remaining fully unsupervised.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OAR decisively outperforms all five primary baselines (GBCPD, AMM_NRR, NSFP, NDP, DPF) on the Open-CAS liver dataset, achieving 2–10× lower EPE and 20–88 percentage-point higher AccR while maintaining zero outliers. The closest competitor, GBCPD, still trails OAR by ~3 EPE and ~30 AccR points on Liver 1, and neural baselines NSFP and NDP suffer severe outlier rates (9–27%) due to collapse/tearing under occlusion. On TOSCA, OAR beats DPF (the strongest neural baseline) by 10–12 AccR points on horse and dog, though DPF is slightly faster (9.5s vs. 13.6s). On 4DMatch, Lepard+OAR matches Lepard+NDP on AccR (74.33% vs. 74.12%) while achieving lower EPE (0.059 vs. 0.077) and fewer outliers (16.41% vs. 17.37%), and on 4DLoMatch it edges out Lepard+NDP in AccS (27.25% vs. 26.59%) and AccR (42.01% vs. 41.05%) despite higher outlier rate. Against the Hyperbolic Chamfer Distance (HCD) metric variant, OAR's MCC achieves 2–3× lower EPE and 20–60 percentage-point higher AccR across all liver pairs, confirming MCC as a more robust similarity measure under occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coordinate-based MLP paradigm for unsupervised non-rigid registration (NSFP, NDP, DPF) by replacing the standard Chamfer Distance loss with the Maximum Correntropy Criterion (MCC), a local and adaptive similarity measure from information-theoretic learning (Liu et al., 2007). The key conceptual departure is that prior methods treat shape deviation holistically, which the authors argue causes collapses and tearing under occlusion; MCC instead assigns per-point adaptive importance, naturally down-weighting occluded regions. For handling the ill-posed deformation of occluded parts, the paper adapts the locally linear embedding principle (Roweis & Saul, 2000) into a Locally Linear Reconstruction (LLR) regularizer, replacing the commonly used AIAP constraint with a geometric prior that propagates deformation from neighboring unoccluded points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap—non-rigid registration under occlusion is critical for applications like computer-assisted surgery where intra-operative scans are inherently partial, yet state-of-the-art methods produce physically implausible results. The theoretical result that Chamfer Distance is a special case of the correntropy-induced metric (Proposition 1) positions MCC as a more universal point cloud similarity measure with potential applicability beyond registration. However, the method remains a test-time optimization with moderate computational cost (LLR matrix inversion), and performance degrades when occlusion is very large, leaving open the question of how to incorporate global shape priors (e.g., symmetry) for severely under-constrained deformations.
