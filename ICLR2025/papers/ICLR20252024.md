## Problem

Stochastic Gradient MCMC (SGMCMC) methods for Bayesian Neural Networks suffer from low sample diversity in practice, producing posterior samples that are too similar to yield reliable uncertainty estimates or out-of-distribution robustness. Existing remedies—tempering, multiple chains, or explicit preconditioning—introduce extra approximations (e.g., Fisher information estimation) or significant memory overhead, making them impractical at scale. The paper asks whether sample diversity can be improved by modifying the parameterization itself rather than the sampler dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 resized to 32×32 for TinyImageNet) and produces class predictions along with calibrated predictive uncertainty (NLL, ensemble ambiguity, OOD detection via predictive entropy). A key design constraint is that during sampling the network carries expanded parameters (decomposed weight matrices), but at inference these are reassembled into the original single weight matrices, so the inference cost and parameter count are identical to the unexpanded network. The sampling procedure uses a ResNet-20 with Filter Response Normalization and Swish activations, with 5,000 steps per cycle (from scratch) or 70,248 steps per cycle (from HMC burn-in).

## Method

PX-SGMCMC reparameterizes each weight matrix W^(l) as a product P_{1:c}^(l) · V^(l) · Q_{1:d}^(l), where P and Q are new square 'expanded' matrices of the same dimension as W, and V retains the original shape. This decomposition induces a preconditioning matrix P_X(t) on the SGLD/SGHMC gradient updates (Lemma 3.1), whose eigenvalues scale with the singular values of the expanded matrices, effectively acting as an implicit step-size scaling that enlarges exploration without destabilizing convergence (Theorem 3.2). For convolutional layers, P and Q operate only on the channel dimensions (not spatial), reducing the parameter overhead from ~3× to (1+2/k²)×. The theoretical bound on consecutive-sample distance grows with the expansion depth (c+d+1) and the maximum singular value M, explaining why deeper expansions yield more diverse samples. The approach requires no tempering, no multiple chains, no explicit preconditioner estimation, and no change to the inference-time model, making it a drop-in modification of any SGMCMC sampler.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, PX-SGHMC achieves 12.1% classification error and 0.388 NLL, outperforming SGHMC (13.3% ERR, 0.422 NLL), SGNHT (13.5% ERR, 0.425 NLL), SGLD (15.2% ERR, 0.477 NLL), and pSGLD (15.8% ERR, 0.501 NLL). The advantage extends to distribution shifts: on CIFAR-10.1, PX-SGHMC reaches 21.8% ERR vs. 23.4% for SGHMC, and on CIFAR-10-C it achieves 28.7% ERR vs. 29.2% for SGHMC. For OOD detection, PX-SGHMC attains 0.832 AUROC on SVHN and 0.884 AUROC on LSUN, surpassing all baselines by 3–5 points in AUROC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PX-SGHMC consistently outperforms all four SGMCMC baselines (SGLD, pSGLD, SGHMC, SGNHT) across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest baseline SGHMC, PX-SGHMC reduces CIFAR-10 ERR by 1.2 points (12.1% vs. 13.3%) and NLL by 0.034 (0.388 vs. 0.422), with similar margins on all distribution-shift benchmarks. Against SGLD and pSGLD, the gap is even larger (3.1 and 3.7 points in ERR on CIFAR-10). In OOD detection (Table 2), PX-SGHMC achieves 0.832 AUROC on SVHN and 0.884 on LSUN, beating SGHMC (0.790/0.860) and SGNHT (0.776/0.864) by 3–5 AUROC points. Compared to the gold-standard HMC (Izmailov et al., 2021), PX-SGHMC matches HMC's functional diversity (AMB 0.339 vs. 0.347; VAR 0.105 vs. 0.107) while achieving a lower ensemble NLL (0.353 vs. 0.381), though individual sample NLL is slightly worse (0.739 vs. 0.800). Against the meta-learning-based L2E method (Kim et al., 2024), PX-SGHMC is competitive on CIFAR-10 (0.069 vs. 0.071 ERR) and CIFAR-100 (0.290 vs. 0.268 ERR, where L2E is slightly better), while matching on TinyImageNet (0.498 vs. 0.488 ERR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SGMCMC family of samplers (SGLD, SGHMC, SGNHT) and the well-studied properties of deep linear networks—specifically their accelerated convergence and implicit bias under gradient flow (Arora et al., 2018; 2019a; 2019b). Prior work addressing SGMCMC's limited sample diversity has focused on modifying the sampling dynamics directly: cyclical step-size schedules (Zhang et al., 2020), learned preconditioners (Gong et al., 2019; Kim et al., 2024), or running multiple chains (Gallego & Insua, 2020; Deng et al., 2020). The key departure here is indirect: rather than altering the dynamics, the paper reparameterizes the network by decomposing each weight matrix into a product of expanded matrices (W = PVQ), which induces a preconditioning on the gradient that acts as an implicit step-size scaling. The authors claim this is the first use of parameter expansion specifically to enhance SGMCMC exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PX-SGHMC achieves HMC-comparable functional diversity (AMB 0.339 vs. 0.347) and even surpasses HMC in ensemble NLL (0.353 vs. 0.381) while remaining a stochastic-gradient method, offering a practical path to principled Bayesian uncertainty without the full-data cost of HMC. The theoretical link between matrix-factorization depth, singular-value dynamics, and exploration bounds provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why the reparameterization works. Limitations include increased training memory (mitigated by a low-rank variant) and evaluation restricted to small-scale image classification (CIFAR, TinyImageNet with ResNet-20); open questions include whether the approach scales to larger architectures, how to optimally choose the expansion depth, and whether the implicit preconditioning interacts favorably with other modern SGMCMC improvements.
