## Problem

FedLAW identified the global weight shrinking effect in federated learning aggregation, showing that scaling the aggregated model by a factor γ < 1 improves generalization. However, FedLAW requires optimizing γ on a proxy dataset assumed to match the global data distribution, raising privacy concerns and limiting real-world applicability. Additionally, FedLAW applies a single shrinking factor uniformly across all layers, ignoring well-documented inter-layer heterogeneity in deep networks during FL training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three quantities already available on the FL server after standard aggregation: the previous global model parameters w_g^t, the newly aggregated model parameters ŵ_g^{t+1} (from which the global gradient is derived as ŵ_g^{t+1} − w_g^t), and each client's local model parameters w_k^t (to compute per-layer gradient variance τ_l). No additional data, proxy datasets, or extra communication rounds are required. The output is a layer-wise weight-shrunk global model where each layer l is independently scaled by its computed factor γ_l^t, producing the final w_g^{t+1} that replaces the standard aggregated model.

## Method

FedLWS derives a closed-form expression for the shrinking factor by hypothesizing that the ratio of the regularization term to the optimization term in the FL update is proportional to the variance of local client gradients (validated empirically with Pearson correlation 0.860). Specifically, γ_l^t = ||w_gl^t|| / (β·τ_l^t·||η_g^t·g_gl^t|| + ||w_gl^t||), where τ_l^t is the mean L1 deviation of layer-l local gradients from their mean, and β is a single hyperparameter (recommended range 0.001–0.1). This computation is performed independently for each layer l of the global model, yielding a vector of layer-specific shrinking factors that are applied as element-wise scaling to the aggregated model. The entire procedure is a post-aggregation, server-side post-processing step that is orthogonal to the choice of aggregation weights, making it a plug-and-play module composable with FedAvg, FedProx, FedDyn, FedDisco, L-DAWA, and other FL baselines without modifying the client-side training loop.

## Results

FedLWS, applied as a plug-and-play post-aggregation step, consistently improves all six baseline FL methods across four image datasets and three heterogeneity levels (α=100, 0.5, 0.1). The best overall average top-1 accuracy reaches 59.32%, compared to 58.41% for FedLAW (which requires a proxy dataset) and 58.02% for FedAvg+LWS. Gains are most pronounced under high heterogeneity (α=0.1): on CIFAR-100, FedLWS variants achieve 36.31–37.22% versus FedLAW's 34.59%, and on CIFAR-10, FedDisco+LWS reaches 62.86% versus FedLAW's 64.76% (where FedLAW benefits from a well-matched proxy). Computationally, FedLWS adds only 0.035 s per aggregation round (CNN) versus 4.83 s for FedLAW, a ~140× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedLWS is evaluated as an additive module on top of six baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedDyn, FedDisco, L-DAWA) and compared directly against FedLAW. Against FedLAW, FedLWS matches or exceeds it on CIFAR-100 (37.22% vs 34.59% at α=0.1) and Tiny-ImageNet (30.02% vs 29.13%), while FedLAW retains an edge on CIFAR-10 (64.76% vs 61.61%) where its 200-sample proxy provides 20 samples per class. Against the base methods without LWS, FedLWS yields consistent gains of 0.5–3.0%: e.g., FedAvg improves from 61.04% to 64.08% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) and from 36.71% to 37.70% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.1). FedProx+LWS achieves the highest FashionMNIST accuracy (88.78%), while FedDisco+LWS leads on CIFAR-10 (62.86%). L-DAWA+LWS shows the largest relative improvement on CIFAR-100 (from ~32% to 36.31%) but remains the weakest overall due to its self-supervised pretraining objective. In text classification (Table 2), FedLWS improves FedAvg by 1.53–2.88% and FedProx by 1.57–2.62% across AG News, Sogou News, and Amazon Review, confirming cross-modality applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FedLAW (Li et al., 2023a), which identified the Global Weight Shrinking (GWS) effect in federated learning and learned the shrinking factor γ via gradient descent on a proxy dataset. FedLWS departs from FedLAW in two key conceptual ways: it derives a closed-form expression for γ from the variance of local client gradients and global model parameters (eliminating the proxy dataset entirely), and it extends the shrinking factor from a single model-wide value to a layer-wise computation. The method is positioned as orthogonal to both client-side drift adjustment (FedProx, FedDyn, SCAFFOLD) and server-side aggregation weight design (FedDisco, L-DAWA, pFedLA), operating as a post-aggregation step that can be layered on top of any existing FL pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedLWS provides a computationally cheap, privacy-preserving plug-and-play module that consistently improves FL generalization across diverse datasets, model architectures, and heterogeneity levels without modifying the local training process or requiring additional data. The 60× reduction in aggregation-time overhead compared to FedLAW makes it practical for real-world deployment where proxy datasets are unavailable or privacy-sensitive. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the requirement for identical client model architectures, leaving heterogeneous-model FL as an open problem. The layer-wise analysis also reveals that the classifier layer demands the strongest regularization early in training, connecting to the broader neural-collapse literature on classifier specificity in FL.
