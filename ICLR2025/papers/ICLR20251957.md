## Problem

Existing 'pre-train, prompt-tuning' methods for pre-trained heterogeneous graph neural networks (HGNNs) suffer from two key limitations: they ignore graph structures during the tuning stage (only modifying node features), which increases training error, and they are constrained by limited labeled data, leading to a large generalization gap. Moreover, these methods lack a unified theoretical framework to formally characterize and improve generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen node representations from both the homogeneous and heterogeneous branches of a pre-trained HGNN, the heterogeneous graph's edge types and neighbor structure, and a small set of labeled target nodes. It outputs a prediction matrix P (dimension n×c) obtained by projecting the final adapted node representations Z through a linear layer, where Z is the concatenation of homogeneous and heterogeneous branch outputs. The prediction vectors are compared against class-subgraph centroids (averaged predictions of nodes sharing the same propagated label) to yield class probabilities for classification or cluster assignments for clustering.

## Method

HG-Adapter introduces dual structure-aware adapters that jointly tune node features (via low-rank MLPs) and graph structures: the homogeneous adapter adaptively learns a sparse adjacency matrix A by computing cosine similarities between projected frozen representations of same-type nodes (restricted to k-nearest neighbors), while the heterogeneous adapter learns per-neighbor-type weights S via a softmax score function over neighbor representations. For optimization, the method designs a label-propagated contrastive loss that first propagates labels through A to unlabeled nodes, then enforces class-subgraph similarity (pulling node predictions toward their class centroid while pushing away from others), thereby bridging the gap between diverse pre-training objectives and downstream tasks. Two additional self-supervised losses treat all nodes as equal supervision: a feature reconstruction loss aligns original features with post-message-passing reconstructions to optimize A, and a margin loss narrows the distance between class-subgraph representations and adapted heterogeneous representations to optimize S. Theoretically, the paper derives a generalization error bound for prompt-tuning methods and proves that HG-Adapter achieves a strictly lower bound by simultaneously reducing training error (via structure-aware adapters approaching optimal parameters) and the generalization gap (via potential labeled data extension through unlabeled nodes).

## Results

On the four main heterogeneous graph datasets (ACM, Yelp, DBLP, Aminer), HERO+HG-Adapter achieves the best Macro-F1 scores of 92.7%, 93.1%, 94.0%, and 78.3% respectively, outperforming the strongest prompt-tuning baseline HetGPT (integrated with HERO) by an average of 0.81% in Macro-F1. On the biomedical dataset HBN-B, HeCo+HG-Adapter reaches 87.7% AUC and 88.9% AUPR, surpassing HGPrompt (86.6%/87.8%) by ~1.3%. On the large-scale Ogbn-mag dataset, HERO+HG-Adapter achieves 52.1% accuracy, improving over HERO+HetGPT (50.6%) by 1.5% and over HERO alone (49.5%) by 2.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HG-Adapter consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across all four main datasets. Against the strongest prompt-tuning method HetGPT (integrated with HERO), HG-Adapter gains +0.3 to +2.6 Macro-F1 points per dataset (e.g., 92.7 vs. 92.4 on ACM, 78.3 vs. 75.7 on Aminer), with the largest gap on Aminer where labeled data is most scarce (only 80 training samples). Against the best fine-tuning baseline HERO alone, HG-Adapter improves by +0.5 to +3.2 Macro-F1 (e.g., 94.0 vs. 93.8 on DBLP, 78.3 vs. 75.1 on Aminer). Against the standalone prompt-tuning method HGPrompt, HG-Adapter (on HERO) leads by +0.6 to +3.5 Macro-F1. Semi-supervised baselines HAN and HGT are consistently 1–10 points below all pre-trained methods, confirming the advantage of the pre-train-then-tune paradigm. On the biomedical HBN-B dataset, HeCo+HG-Adapter (87.7 AUC) beats HGPrompt (86.6) and HeCo+HetGPT (86.7), and on Ogbn-mag, HERO+HG-Adapter (52.1% Acc) surpasses HERO+HetGPT (50.6%) and all other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'pre-train, prompt-tuning' paradigm for heterogeneous graph neural networks (HGPrompt, HetGPT) by proposing a 'pre-train, adapter-tuning' paradigm that additionally tunes graph structures rather than only node features. It draws on the LoRA/low-rank adaptation literature (Hu et al., 2021) for parameter-efficient design and on classical generalization bound theory (Arora et al., 2018; Aghajanyan et al., 2021) to formally characterize why existing prompt-tuning methods are suboptimal. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of dual structure-aware adapters that adaptively learn both homogeneous (intra-type) and heterogeneous (inter-type) graph structures, combined with a label-propagated contrastive loss that bridges pre-training objectives with downstream tasks via class-subgraph similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified theoretical framework (generalization error bound) explaining why prompt-tuning-based methods for HGNNs are limited, and demonstrates that adapter-tuning on both features and structures achieves a provably lower bound. Practically, the method is plug-and-play across different pre-trained HGNNs (HDMI, HeCo, HERO) without requiring task-specific prompt design, which reduces the engineering burden. Open questions include whether the theoretical bound is tight enough to guide hyperparameter selection in practice, whether the approach extends to dynamic or temporal heterogeneous graphs, and whether the label-propagation step remains reliable when the initial homogeneous structure is poorly learned in early training.
