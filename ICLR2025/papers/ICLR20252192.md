## Problem

Preconditioning in consistency distillation (CMs and CTMs) is hand-crafted with no theoretical justification, and despite efforts, no distinct preconditioning has been found to outperform the original one for intermediate trajectory jumps. This paper provides the first theoretical analysis of what makes a good preconditioning and proposes a principled, analytically computable alternative that accelerates multi-step training by 2×–3×.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252192/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model takes a noisy point x_t on the teacher PF-ODE trajectory (at time t), a target time s < t, and optionally a class label, and outputs the corresponding point x_s via the consistency function f_θ(x, t, s) = f(t,s)·x + g(t,s)·D_θ(x, t, s). The preconditioning coefficients f(t,s) and g(t,s) are precomputed from the frozen teacher denoiser D_φ at hundreds of discretized timesteps using automatic differentiation (Hutchinson trace estimator for l_t, JVP for s_t), requiring less than 1% of total training time. The student network mirrors the teacher architecture with an additional timestep input s, and is trained on 64×64 RGB images (CIFAR-10, FFHQ, ImageNet) with EDM's noise schedule (σ_min=0.002, σ_max=80).

## Method

The paper identifies two design criteria for preconditioning coefficients: a boundary condition (f(t,t)=1, g(t,t)=0) and denoiser alignment (the optimal student denoiser at s=t must equal the teacher denoiser D_φ). It then derives a generalized teacher ODE with free parameters {l_t, s_t} that induces a family of valid preconditionings via Euler discretization. The key insight is minimizing the consistency gap ‖D_θ*(x_t, t, s) − D_φ(x_t, t)‖, which yields an analytic bound (Proposition 3.1) whose minimization gives closed-form solutions: l_t = 1 − E[tr(∇D_φ)]/d (Rosenbrock-type gradient restriction) and s_t = E[g_φ^T dg_φ/dλ] / E[‖g_φ‖²] (optimal time reparameterization). A backward Euler rewriting of the discretization is applied for training stability on long jumps. The resulting Analytic-Precond is computed once from the teacher model and plugged into the standard CTM/CM training loop, achieving 2×–3× training acceleration in multi-step generation while improving trajectory alignment with the teacher ODE.

## Results

Analytic-Precond achieves consistent FID improvements over CTM across all four datasets (CIFAR-10 Uncond/Cond, FFHQ 64×64, ImageNet 64×64) at every NFE from 2 to 10, with the largest gains at low NFEs (e.g., FID 5.71 vs. 5.96 on FFHQ at NFE=2, and 5.73 vs. 5.95 on ImageNet at NFE=2). The headline result is a 2× to 3× training acceleration in CTMs for multi-step generation, meaning the student reaches a given FID target in roughly one-third the iterations. For single-step generation (CMs), Analytic-Precond produces FID curves nearly identical to the original CM, confirming that the original preconditioning is already near-optimal for long jumps to ε.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CTM (Kim et al., 2023), Analytic-Precond wins on every dataset and NFE setting in Table 2, with the most pronounced FID gains at NFE=2 (e.g., 0.25 FID improvement on FFHQ, 0.22 on ImageNet) and a 2×–3× reduction in training iterations to reach a target FID in two-step generation. Against CM (Song et al., 2023), Analytic-Precond ties: the preconditioning coefficients f(t,ε) and g(t,ε) are nearly identical to the original CM choice when the jump destination is fixed at ε, so single-step FID curves overlap. Against BCM (Li & He, 2024), Analytic-Precond clearly wins: replacing CTM's preconditioning with BCM's fails to improve either one-step or two-step generation, whereas Analytic-Precond delivers consistent gains. The paper also notes that Analytic-Precond's advantage is concentrated on intermediate (short) trajectory jumps where |t−s|/t is small, while long jumps to ε remain essentially unchanged from the original design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023) and Consistency Trajectory Models (Kim et al., 2023), which introduced hand-crafted preconditionings to enforce boundary conditions in consistency distillation, and on EDM (Karras et al., 2022), which established the denoiser parameterization and preconditioning terminology. The key departure is providing the first theoretical analysis of these preconditionings by connecting them to ODE discretization of the teacher PF-ODE, then deriving a generalized ODE form that induces a family of valid preconditionings. By minimizing a derived bound on the consistency gap (the distance between teacher and optimal student denoisers), the authors obtain Analytic-Precond, an analytically computable preconditioning that replaces the ad-hoc coefficient choices in both CMs and CTMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2×–3× training acceleration in CTMs for multi-step generation is practically valuable, and the theoretical framework (preconditioning as ODE discretization, consistency gap minimization) provides the first principled guidelines for designing trajectory jumpers, potentially transferable to other ODE-based generative or control settings. However, the authors acknowledge that final FID improvements are relatively modest (e.g., 3.83→3.77 on CIFAR-10 at NFE=2), and Analytic-Precond is nearly indistinguishable from the original on long jumps (single-step generation), limiting its impact to the multi-step regime. Open questions include whether the consistency gap framework can yield larger quality gains with different loss designs, and whether the approach extends to other noise schedules or SDE-based trajectories beyond the EDM setting.
