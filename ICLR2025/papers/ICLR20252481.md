## Problem

Existing vision-language models can process either single earth observation images (e.g., GeoChat) or temporal sequences of natural images (e.g., Video-LLaVA), but none can reason over temporal sequences of EO imagery. This gap prevents a unified conversational interface for critical time-dependent tasks such as building damage assessment, semantic change detection, and urban development monitoring, which require comparing satellite images across multiple timesteps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252481/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TEOChat accepts a temporal sequence of up to 8 satellite images (resized to 224×224 via shorter-side resize and center crop) interleaved with natural-language image reference tokens (e.g., 'Image 1', 'Image 2') and a user instruction that may include bounding-box regions in [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max] format. The model produces a free-form natural language response that can contain class labels, axis-aligned bounding boxes, counts, yes/no answers, or references to specific timesteps in the sequence, depending on the task.

## Method

TEOChat adopts a LLaVA-1.5-like architecture with a temporally-shared (Siamese) CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder, a 2-layer MLP projector, and a Llama 2 decoder, initialized from Video-LLaVA weights to leverage its natural-video pre-training. Training freezes the vision encoder and fine-tunes the LLM via LoRA (rank 128) with 8-bit quantization, while also fine-tuning the projector to adapt to EO visual representations. A key prompt design choice interleaves image reference tokens between the visual token blocks, enabling the model to input and output timestep identifiers. The model is trained on TEOChatlas (554,071 examples, 1,244,393 images), which jointly includes 308,861 single-image instruction-following examples from GeoChat_Instruct and 245,210 temporal examples spanning seven task categories (temporal scene classification, change detection, spatial change referring expression, change QA, region-based change QA, temporal referring expression, and region-based temporal QA) drawn from fMoW, xBD, S2Looking, and QFabric.

## Results

TEOChat achieves 75.1% accuracy on fMoW RGB temporal scene classification (vs. 16.6% for Video-LLaVA and 59.2% for GeoChat), 50.0 F1 on xBD damage classification (vs. 7.0 and 11.8), and 34.5 F1 on S2Looking building change detection (vs. 8.0 and 7.7). On change question answering, TEOChat reaches 89.9% accuracy on xBD and 90.0% on S2Looking RQA, far exceeding both baselines. TEOChat also outperforms GPT-4o (+11.7 F1) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (+12.8 F1) on xBD damage classification, and GPT-4o (+14.2 F1) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (+17.1 F1) on S2Looking change detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEOChat substantially outperforms Video-LLaVA (a temporal VLM trained on natural images/videos) on every temporal task, with gaps of 30–73 points (e.g., 75.1% vs. 16.6% on fMoW RGB TSC; 74.9% vs. 1.9% on TRE). Against GeoChat (a single-image EO VLM), TEOChat wins on all temporal tasks by large margins (e.g., +40.8 F1 on xBD damage classification, +55.9% on xBD QA), confirming that single-image instruction-tuning does not transfer to temporal reasoning. TEOChat surpasses specialist models on xBD damage classification (+23.5 F1 over the modified UNet), S2Looking change detection (+8.0 F1 over FC-Siam-Diff), and 5-image RTQA on QFabric (+11.9 F1), but underperforms specialists on xBD localization (−47.0 F1) and QFabric RQA (−14.9 F1 on 5-image), which the authors attribute to the box-vs-segmentation-mask output constraint. On zero-shot change detection (ABCD, CDVQA), TEOChat approaches specialist performance (85.6 vs. 95.2 on ABCD). TEOChat also outperforms proprietary multi-image VLMs GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro on both xBD damage classification and S2Looking change detection by 11–17 F1 points, and beats open-source multi-image models InternVL2 and Qwen2VL by 24–38 F1. On single-image tasks, TEOChat outperforms GeoChat by +3.5 average across scene classification and VQA benchmarks, though it trails fine-tuned specialists like RSGPT and LHRS-Bot on some VQA subtasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEOChat extends the Video-LLaVA architecture (itself a video extension of LLaVA-1.5) from natural image/video understanding to temporal earth observation sequences, while incorporating the single-image instruction-following tasks from GeoChat to preserve EO-specific capabilities. The Siamese (temporally-shared) image encoder design follows the dominant paradigm in specialist change detection literature (FC-Siam-Diff, ChangeMask), but is here embedded within a generalist LLM-based conversational framework. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of interleaved image-reference tokens between visual tokens in the prompt, enabling the model to input and output references to specific timesteps — a capability absent from both Video-LLaVA and GeoChat.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TEOChat establishes the first conversational interface for temporal EO data, opening the door to flexible, multi-task deployment in disaster response and urban monitoring where a single specialist model per task is impractical. The outperformance of GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro by 11–17 F1 points on change detection tasks suggests that domain-specific instruction tuning remains critical even as general-purpose VLMs scale. Key limitations include the 224×224 resolution, 8-image sequence cap, bounding-box-only localization (vs. segmentation masks), and absence of multi-spectral band support — all of which constrain real-world applicability and point to clear future work.
