## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods optimize synthetic data to match training dynamics or distributional similarity of the full training set, but leave unexplored how the distilled dataset generalizes across uncommon subgroups or under distributional shift. A model trained on a distilled set may perform poorly on low-density regions of the sample space because the synthetic data fails to ensure adequate coverage of the full population support, and standard empirical risk minimization inherently biases toward high-prior subgroups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252615/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real training dataset of RGB images with class labels (32×32 for SVHN/CIFAR-10, 128×128 for ImageNet-10) and produces a compact synthetic distilled dataset at a chosen images-per-class (IPC) budget of 1, 10, or 50. The procedure is modular: it wraps around any existing DD matching objective (gradient matching, distribution matching, trajectory matching) by replacing the standard cross-entropy loss during the internal model-update step with a CVaR-based robust objective, so the output synthetic set is compatible with the downstream training protocol of the host DD method.

## Method

The core contribution is a Double-DRO dataset distillation procedure that embeds two layers of distributionally robust optimization into the training loop. At each iteration, the training set is subsampled and clustered by nearest synthetic data point; the model parameters are then updated by minimizing a combined objective of average and maximum Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) of the loss across clusters (Eq. 2), which simultaneously enforces worst-case subgroup performance and tail-risk-aware within-group generalization. The synthetic dataset is subsequently optimized using the host DD method's matching metric (e.g., gradient matching in GLaD or IDC), making the approach plug-and-play. Theoretically, the paper connects the CVaR minimization to a data-driven DRO via Large Deviations Principles, showing that the algorithm satisfies an exponential decay bound on out-of-sample disappointment and approximately solves a bilevel DRO that bounds the population loss uniformly across all subgroups.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (IPC-10), the proposed robust method (GLaD^R) achieves 50.2% top-1 accuracy, improving over GLaD (46.7%) by 3.5 points, while IDC^R reaches 68.6% vs. IDC's 67.5%. On ImageNet-10 (IPC-10), GLaD^R achieves 55.2% vs. GLaD's 50.9% (+4.3%), and IDC^R reaches 74.6% vs. IDC's 72.8%. Under robustness evaluation (worst sub-cluster accuracy on ImageNet-10), GLaD^R achieves 47.1% vs. GLaD's 34.9%, a 12.2-point gain, demonstrating substantially improved group coverage. On subpopulation shift benchmarks, GLaD^R improves worst-group accuracy by 5.7 points on MetaShift (57.0% vs. 51.3%) and 6.4 points on ImageNetBG (38.6% vs. 32.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed robust DD method consistently outperforms all baselines across every dataset and IPC setting. Against GLaD (the strongest latent-level baseline), the gains are most pronounced: +3.5 points on CIFAR-10 IPC-10, +4.3 points on ImageNet-10 IPC-10, and +4.8 points on SVHN IPC-10. Against IDC (the strongest pixel-level baseline), gains are smaller but consistent: +1.1 on CIFAR-10 IPC-10 and +1.8 on ImageNet-10 IPC-10. On robustness metrics (worst sub-cluster accuracy), the advantage over GLaD is dramatically larger — e.g., +12.2 points on ImageNet-10 Cluster-min (47.1% vs. 34.9%) and +6.5 points on CIFAR-10 (46.7% vs. 40.2%). The method also surpasses distribution-matching baselines (DSA, DM, KIP) by wide margins on standard accuracy (e.g., GLaD^R 50.2% vs. DSA 52.1% on CIFAR-10 IPC-10, though DSA is competitive; GLaD^R 55.2% vs. DSA 52.7% on ImageNet-10 IPC-10). On subpopulation shift benchmarks (MetaShift, ImageNetBG), GLaD^R improves worst-group accuracy by 5.7 and 6.4 points respectively over GLaD, confirming the group-robustness objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges distributionally robust optimization (DRO) theory with dataset distillation (DD), a connection the authors note is absent from prior DD literature. It builds on the DRO framework of Duchi & Namkoong (2021) and the data-driven DRO optimality results of Van Parys et al. (2021), repurposing CVaR as a tractable proxy for the DRO objective within the DD training loop. On the DD side, it is explicitly modular: the proposed CVaR-based group-robust loss is injected into the model-updating phase of existing gradient-matching (IDC, GLaD) and distribution-matching (IDM, DREAM, PDD) methods without altering their synthetic-data optimization, positioning it as a drop-in enhancement rather than a new distillation paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in DD: distilled datasets optimized purely for empirical training-error matching can underperform on low-density subgroups or under domain shift, a concern that becomes critical in online or federated deployment. The modular design and modest computational overhead (~30 s per iteration, <50% extra) make it immediately applicable to existing DD pipelines, and the consistent gains across pixel-level, latent-level, and distribution-matching baselines on multiple benchmarks suggest broad utility. Open questions include whether the fixed KMeans clustering in RGB space is optimal for high-dimensional or non-visual data, how the method behaves when the test-time subgroup is entirely unseen during distillation, and whether the theoretical LDP guarantees translate to tighter finite-sample bounds in practice.
