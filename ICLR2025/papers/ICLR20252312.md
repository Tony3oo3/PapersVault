## Problem

Recent concise ConvNets like VanillaNet, ParNet, and RepVGG reduce depth or runtime but often at the cost of inflated parameter counts or increased FLOPs, and none simultaneously optimize for depth, parameters, FLOPs, computational density, and branching. The paper asks whether a single ConvNet macro design can be concise across all these dimensions while remaining architecturally simple and attentionless.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252312/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CoSNet takes standard 224×224 RGB images and produces 1000-class ImageNet labels for the primary classification task. For downstream evaluation, the same backbone feeds DN-DETR (producing COCO detection boxes) and PSPNet (producing pixel-wise segmentation masks). The architecture is explicitly designed for resource-constrained deployment, targeting low memory access cost and minimal branching rather than peak accuracy at scale.

## Method

CoSNet replaces ResNet-style bottleneck blocks with a CoSNet-unit built from Parallel Columnar Convolutions (PCC): M parallel columns of l stacked 3×3 convolutions, each restricted to N output kernels, with no crosstalk between columns. An Input Replication (IR) operation duplicates the squeezed input so every column receives the full channel information (unlike group convolutions that partition channels), and all columns at the same depth level are batched into a single GPU kernel for uniform, high-density computation. Only two 1×1 convolutions per unit (a squeeze L_s and a fuse L_f) minimize depth, while shallow-range projections within columns and a deep-range projection (3×3 pooling + 1×1 conv) bypassing the entire columnar stack improve gradient flow. A Pairwise Frequent Fusion (PFF) variant optionally inserts small 1×1 convolutions between column pairs to recover accuracy at negligible latency cost. The full network stacks five CoSNet-units (no sub-blocks), with M, N, and l as the only three hyperparameters controlling the parameter/FLOP/accuracy tradeoff.

## Results

On ImageNet, CoSNet variants achieve 77.1%–83.7% top-1 accuracy with only 26–38 layers of depth. The mid-range CoSNet-B2 (30.0M params, 5.10B FLOPs) reaches 81.3% top-1 at 9 ms latency (111 FPS), while the largest CoSNet-C2-PFF (44.5M params, 8.27B FLOPs) achieves 83.7% top-1 at 13 ms. CoSNet-B0 surpasses ResNet-50 (76.3%) by 3.2 points while being 50% shallower, 22% fewer parameters, 25% fewer FLOPs, and 40% faster. The PFF variants add 1–2 ms latency overhead but gain 1.2–1.6 points in accuracy over their non-PFF counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSNet-B0 outperforms ResNet-50 by 3.2 points in top-1 accuracy (79.5% vs. 76.3%) while being 50% shallower, 22% fewer parameters, 25% fewer FLOPs, and 40% faster. Against Swin-T, CoSNet-B2 is 72% less deep, 55% faster, and 0.2 points more accurate (81.3% vs. 81.1%); against ConvNeXt-T, it is 55% less deep and 30% faster at a 0.5-point accuracy trade-off (81.3% vs. 81.8%). CoSNet-A0 beats EfficientViT-M4 by 2.8 points (77.1% vs. 74.3%) at the same 6 ms latency with only 0.03B more FLOPs, and CoSNet-B1 matches DeiT-S accuracy (79.9% vs. 79.8%) while being 50% less deep, 23% fewer parameters, and 60% faster. Compared to VanillaNet-8, CoSNet-B1 achieves higher accuracy (79.5% vs. 79.1%) with 47% fewer parameters and 60% fewer FLOPs at similar latency. At the high end, CoSNet-C2-PFF (83.7%) rivals InceptionNeXt-S (83.5%) and UniRepLKNet-S (83.9%) with dramatically fewer layers (38 vs. 48/180) and lower latency (13 ms vs. 18/23 ms), while CoSNet-C2 (82.1%) approaches ConvNextV2-T (82.5%) at 31% fewer parameters and 31% faster inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSNet builds on ResNet's five-stage, channel-doubling structure as its architectural skeleton but departs fundamentally from the residual bottleneck design by replacing multi-block stages with single CoSNet-units that minimize 1×1 convolutions (which constitute ~66% of ResNet's depth). Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the 'simplified ConvNet' line (RepVGG, ParNet, VanillaNet) and the parallel-convolution lineage (Inception, ResNeXt), but distinguishes itself by using input replication instead of channel splitting and by fusing columns only once rather than after every block. Unlike RepVGG, it requires no structural reparameterization at inference; unlike VanillaNet and ParNet, it explicitly controls parameter growth and FLOPs alongside depth reduction, addressing multiple efficiency axes simultaneously rather than optimizing a single metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 26-layer, attentionless, single-branch ConvNet can rival 50–180-layer architectures (ResNet-101/152, ConvNeXt-T, Swin-T, ViTAE-S) across depth, parameters, FLOPs, and latency simultaneously, suggesting that much of the complexity in modern backbones may be unnecessary for competitive accuracy. The parallel columnar convolution with input replication offers a clean, hardware-friendly primitive (batched 3×3 convolutions) that avoids the memory-access penalties of depthwise/group convolutions and the training overhead of multi-branch designs. Open questions include whether the design extends to the mobile regime, whether post-training layer merging (as in VanillaNet) can yield even shallower variants, and whether hybrid attention mechanisms can be integrated without sacrificing the simplicity that defines CoSNet's appeal.
