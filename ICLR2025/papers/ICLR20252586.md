## Problem

Existing retrosynthetic planning methods treat each reaction step in isolation, predicting reactants from a product without leveraging the contextual information from the overall synthetic route. They also require costly per-reaction annotations and ignore the multi-faceted nature of molecular representations, leading to degraded accuracy on longer, more complex routes. The paper addresses the gap of incorporating in-context textual descriptions of the full pathway to guide multi-step retrosynthesis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target molecule (SMILES + 3D conformation), IUPAC-name-derived textual descriptions generated by ChatGPT for each step along the pathway, and a starting material set as input. It outputs ranked reactant SMILES at each expansion step and a fully ranked synthetic pathway. Notably, the textual input is generated per-step from IUPAC names (not SMILES) to serve as a natural-language bridge between molecular structure and the LLM, and at test time only the product's structural description is used to prevent information leakage.

## Method

RetroInText combines three modalities: a fine-tuned MolT5 (T5-based) single-step model that translates product SMILES to reactant SMILES, a 3DInfomax encoder that jointly embeds 2D molecular graphs and 3D conformations via contrastive learning, and a SciBERT text encoder processing ChatGPT-generated pathway descriptions. A cross-attention fusion module treats the textual embedding as query and the molecular embedding as key/value, producing a fused representation scored by an MLP trained with MSE loss. During search (Retro*, Retro*-0, or Greedy DFS), candidate pathways are ranked by a composite score combining Synthetic Complexity Score (ScScore), cumulative reaction cost, and a captioning score that measures textual alignment between intermediate descriptions and the overall route context. The key insight is that in-context textual descriptions of prior steps provide route-level context that stabilizes predictions, yielding up to 5% Top-1 improvement over prior SOTA, with the largest gains at depths 5–8.

## Results

On the RetroBench dataset, RetroInText achieves 41.2% Top-1 exact match accuracy with Retro* search and 42.1% with Retro*-0 (beam search), outperforming the previous SOTA FusionRetro+CREBM (39.4% and 39.6% respectively) by 1.8% and 2.5%. Under Greedy DFS, RetroInText reaches 39.8% Top-1, a 6.0% improvement over FusionRetro+CREBM (33.8%). The advantage is most pronounced at deeper route lengths (depths 5–8), where RetroInText maintains a 3–5% margin over baselines that degrade sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RetroInText outperforms all template-free and semi-template baselines across every search algorithm. Against the prior SOTA FusionRetro+CREBM, RetroInText gains 1.8% Top-1 under Retro* (41.2 vs. 39.4) and 6.0% under Greedy DFS (39.8 vs. 33.8). Among template-based methods, Neuralsym (41.7% Retro* Top-1) and GLN (39.6%) remain competitive, with RetroInText slightly trailing Neuralsym by 0.5% on Retro* but matching or exceeding it on Retro*-0 (42.1 vs. 42.0) and Greedy DFS (39.8 vs. 39.2). The semi-template methods G2Gs (5.4%) and GraphRetro (15.3%) are substantially outperformed, as are the template-free Transformer (31.3%) and Megan (18.8%). The CREBM reranking add-on provides modest gains (e.g., FusionRetro 37.5→39.4) but cannot close the gap to RetroInText, suggesting that in-context textual information captures route-level context that energy-based reranking does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

RetroInText builds directly on MolT5 (Edwards et al., 2022) as its single-step generation backbone, fine-tuning it via a product-to-reactant SMILES translation objective. It departs from prior multi-step planning methods (Retro*, FusionRetro, MCTS-based approaches) that rely exclusively on graph/SMILES representations and per-step cost annotations, by introducing LLM-generated textual descriptions of the entire synthetic route as an in-context signal for the search. The multimodal fusion of 3DInfomax molecular embeddings with SciBERT text embeddings via cross-attention is a natural extension of the in-context learning paradigm from FusionRetro, but applied to a new modality (natural language route descriptions) rather than molecular graph context alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in retrosynthetic planning: the need for expensive per-reaction cost annotations and the inability of purely structural models to capture route-level context. The consistent gains at deeper routes (depth 5–8) suggest that textual context is most valuable when the combinatorial search space is largest, which is where current methods struggle most. However, the approach introduces a dependency on ChatGPT for text generation (API costs, potential hallucinations in chemical descriptions), and evaluation is limited to the USPTO-derived RetroBench dataset, leaving open whether the textual context generalizes to novel reaction types or non-USPTO chemistry. The modest ~1% ablation gain from the text module (41.2 vs. 40.2 Top-1) raises questions about whether the improvement is robust or dataset-specific.
