## Problem

Existing ML methods for solving integer linear programs rely on supervised learning, which requires expensive label generation via traditional solvers and suffers from a fundamental misalignment between the training objective (minimizing prediction loss) and the inference objective (producing feasible, high-quality solutions). This leads to plausible yet infeasible outputs and high training costs. The paper asks whether ILPs can be solved end-to-end with pure ML techniques in an unsupervised, differentiable framework that eliminates the need for solver-generated labels entirely.

## Input / Output

Each ILP instance is encoded as a weighted bipartite graph with variable nodes (featuring normalized objective coefficients, constraint degrees, and coefficient statistics) and constraint nodes (featuring average coefficients, degrees, and normalized RHS), connected by edges carrying constraint coefficients. The GNN predictor outputs a probability vector in (0,1)^n for each binary variable; at inference, 1,000 binary solutions are sampled from this distribution, the best feasible one is selected, and it is fed to a solver (Gurobi or SCIP) as an initial solution within a trust region that limits the Hamming distance from the prediction to fewer than Δ=200 variables.

## Method

DiffILO reformulates a discrete, constrained ILP into a continuous, unconstrained, differentiable-almost-everywhere optimization problem through three steps: (1) a probabilistic merit function that interprets continuous variables as Bernoulli probabilities and transforms hard constraints into expected-violation constraints, (2) a penalty function method that absorbs the expected-violation constraints into the objective with a coefficient μ, and (3) a Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization (relaxed Bernoulli) that enables gradient back-propagation by using the continuous surrogate ξ for gradient flow while the rounded ψ determines actual constraint violations. A GNN with interleaved half-convolutions and Jumping Knowledge aggregation predicts the probability vector, and the entire pipeline is trained via stochastic gradient descent on the differentiable merit function—no solver labels required. An adaptive penalty coefficient (inspired by SAC) and cosine-annealed learning rate stabilize training, and the approach is proven to preserve optimality equivalence with the original ILP under sufficient μ.

## Results

On the Set Covering (SC) dataset, DiffILO+Gurobi achieves an objective of 95.65 at 10s (vs. 131.87 for PS+Gurobi and 1031.39 for standalone Gurobi), reaching 86.48 at 1000s (BKS: 86.45). On Combinatorial Auctions (CA), DiffILO+Gurobi reaches 22272.55 at 1000s, matching the BKS, while PS+Gurobi achieves 22272.47. DiffILO achieves an average training speedup of 13.2× over supervised methods and produces feasible solutions with ratios of 50.8%, 97.1%, and 99.4% on SC, IS, and CA respectively, substantially outperforming PS which struggles to generate feasible solutions on many instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary supervised baseline PS (Han et al., 2023), DiffILO+Gurobi dramatically outperforms PS+Gurobi on SC (95.65 vs. 131.87 at 10s; 86.48 vs. 125.26 at 1000s) and CA (22260.48 vs. 22243.12 at 100s), while matching PS on IS where both reach near-BKS values. Against standalone Gurobi, DiffILO+Gurobi provides a massive 10× improvement at 10s on SC (95.65 vs. 1031.39) and a 1.5× improvement on CA at 10s (22177.82 vs. 22090.76). Against CL-LNS (Huang et al., 2023), DiffILO achieves 95.65 vs. 203.27 at 10s on SC. Against ConPaS (Huang et al., 2024), DiffILO produces higher-quality feasible solutions on SC despite ConPaS failing to generate feasible solutions on most instances. Against DDIM (Zeng et al., 2024), DDIM achieves 100% feasibility on SC but DiffILO produces significantly better objective values, highlighting DiffILO's strength in solution quality over mere feasibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable, unsupervised learning framework for combinatorial optimization initiated by Karalias & Loukas (2020, 'Erdős Goes Neural') and Wang et al. (2022), which relied on problem-specific closed-form penalty functions tailored to particular structures (e.g., graph problems, SAT). The key technical departure is the reformulation of general ILP constraints into an expected-violation form (P2), which eliminates the need for hand-derived penalties and makes the approach applicable to arbitrary ILPs. For the reparameterization step, the paper builds directly on the relaxed Bernoulli / Gumbel-Softmax machinery (Maddison et al., 2016; Jang et al., 2017), but applies it in a novel way: using the rounded sample ψ to determine constraint violation (preserving combinatorial structure) while using the continuous surrogate ξ to flow gradients. This positions DiffILO as the first method to combine probabilistic merit functions with reparameterization for *general* ILPs under a fully unsupervised paradigm, as opposed to the supervised Predict-and-Search or Neural Diving frameworks that require solver-generated labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for traditional solvers during training, DiffILO addresses a fundamental bottleneck in ML-for-ILP research: the 13.2× training speedup is not merely an engineering convenience but removes a structural dependency that has constrained the field's scalability and data diversity. The end-to-end alignment of training and inference objectives (optimizing the merit function directly rather than mimicking solver outputs) yields measurably better feasibility ratios and solution quality, suggesting that supervised imitation of solver behavior is a suboptimal training signal. Open questions include: (1) scalability to very large-scale ILPs (thousands of variables/constraints) where the GNN predictor and K-sample Monte Carlo estimation may become prohibitive; (2) generalization to heterogeneous instance distributions, as evidenced by the weak results on the 'neos' MIPLIB subset; and (3) whether the approach extends naturally to MILPs with continuous variables or to problems with equality constraints and variable bounds beyond {0,1}.
