## Problem

Existing virtual outlier synthesis methods for OOD detection either rely on expensive natural OOD data, apply monotonous Gaussian noise to ID embeddings (producing low-diversity outliers confined near decision boundaries), or use heavy generative models. The key unresolved question is how to efficiently synthesize diverse and representative outliers based solely on in-distribution data, without assuming availability of natural outliers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252076/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and produces two outputs: a class label via a fully connected classification head, and an OOD detection score computed as the KNN distance in a 128- or 256-dimensional unit hyperspherical embedding space. Unusually, during training the model synthesizes virtual outlier embeddings directly in the latent hyperspherical space via Markov chains, which serve as supervision signals alongside real ID data—no pixel-space outlier generation is involved.

## Method

HamOS formulates outlier synthesis as sampling from Markov chains using Spherical Hamiltonian Monte Carlo on the unit hypersphere, where the potential energy is the negative log of an OOD-ness density estimated via averaged KNN distance to pairs of nearby ID clusters. Markov chains are initialized at midpoints between adjacent ID cluster centroids and traverse the feature space over multiple synthesis rounds, producing outliers with a broad spectrum of OOD-ness levels. A hard margin barrier based on von Mises-Fisher kernel density estimation rejects candidates that fall too close to ID clusters, preventing erroneous outlier synthesis. The dual-head architecture (FC classification head + MLP projection head with ℓ₂ normalization) is trained with a combined objective of cross-entropy loss, CIDER contrastive loss for ID compactness, and an OOD discernment loss that minimizes the likelihood of synthesized outliers being assigned to any ID class.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, HamOS achieves a FPR95 of 10.48% (AUROC 97.11%, AUPR 94.94%) while maintaining 94.67% ID accuracy. On CIFAR-100, it reaches FPR95 of 46.68% (AUROC 83.64%, AUPR 75.52%) with 76.12% ID accuracy. On the large-scale ImageNet-1K benchmark, HamOS attains an averaged FPR95 of 44.59% and AUROC of 88.59% across four OOD test sets (SUN, iNaturalist, Places-365, Textures).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HamOS outperforms all 13 baselines on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 in FPR95. Against the strongest contrastive-training baseline CIDER, HamOS reduces FPR95 by 35.63% on CIFAR-10 (10.48 vs. 16.28) and by 5.96% on CIFAR-100 (46.68 vs. 49.64). Against the closest synthesis-based competitor NPOS, HamOS improves FPR95 by 27.17% on CIFAR-10 (10.48 vs. 14.39) and 9.20% on CIFAR-100 (46.68 vs. 51.41). The gap over VOS is even larger (10.48 vs. 42.37 on CIFAR-10), reflecting the diversity advantage of HMC-based sampling over Gaussian noise. On ImageNet-1K, HamOS achieves the best averaged FPR95 of 44.59%, surpassing VOS (45.96%) by 2.98% and PALM (45.62%) by 1.03%, while also leading in AUROC (88.59%) and AUPR (95.24%). Notably, HamOS preserves the highest ID classification accuracy among all regularization-based methods (94.67% on CIFAR-10, 76.12% on CIFAR-100), whereas methods like CSI sacrifice significant ID accuracy (92.03%, 61.75%).

## Relation to Prior Work

HamOS departs from the dominant paradigm of virtual outlier synthesis (VOS, NPOS) that applies Gaussian noise to ID embeddings in low-likelihood regions, instead formulating outlier generation as explicit sampling from Markov chains via Hamiltonian Monte Carlo on the unit hypersphere. It builds directly on Spherical HMC (Lan et al., 2013) for constrained sampling and the OOD-ness density estimation via KNN distance, while integrating with the contrastive training framework established by CIDER (Ming et al., 2023b) for shaping hyperspherical embeddings. The key conceptual departure is treating the synthesis process as a trajectory problem (Leapfrog integration with Metropolis-Hastings acceptance) rather than a one-shot perturbation, enabling outliers to traverse a substantially larger region of feature space with varying OOD-ness levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HamOS provides a principled, assumption-light mechanism for generating diverse virtual outliers using only ID data, removing the dependency on expensive natural OOD collections that constrains outlier exposure methods in domain-specific applications. The framework's modularity—compatible with multiple HMC variants (MALA, RMHMC, mMALA), scoring functions (KNN, Mahalanobis, EBO), and contrastive losses (CIDER, SimCLR, SupCon)—makes it a flexible building block rather than a monolithic pipeline. Open questions include whether the OOD-ness density estimation (based on KNN distance between cluster pairs) can be generalized to non-pairwise or hierarchical OOD structures, and whether the approach transfers to non-visual modalities where hyperspherical geometry may be less natural. The fine-tuning assumption (requiring a pretrained model) also limits applicability in resource-constrained settings.
