## Problem

Conventional image compression optimizes pixel-level rate-distortion, ignoring the semantic information that downstream machine-vision tasks actually require. Existing Video Coding for Machines (VCM) methods either treat each task in isolation or rely on predefined task sets, without systematically identifying which tasks are synergistic versus conflicting, nor modeling the causal dependencies between their latent representations. The paper asks: how can we discern and exploit inter-task interdependencies to achieve efficient multi-task representation compression?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250553/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 512×512 RGB images from the Taskonomy dataset and operates in the Analyze-Then-Compress (ATC) paradigm: a shared Xception encoder first extracts latent features, which are then compressed in feature space rather than pixel space. The compressed bitstream is decoded along a DAG of task groups, producing task-specific outputs (segmentation masks, depth Z-buffers, surface normal maps, 2D keypoint heatmaps, edge texture maps, and reconstructed RGB images) where each group's decoder consumes its own latent plus the decoded latents of its causal parents as cross-task context.

## Method

TAMC has two key components. First, inter-coherent task grouping: during a lookahead phase on downsampled images, the method measures gradient coherence C_{u→v} between every task pair (how much task u's gradient update reduces task v's loss), then uses a branch-and-bound search to partition tasks into k groups sharing a single encoder under a bitrate budget b, maximizing aggregate task performance. Second, causal DAG construction via conditional entropy: pairwise conditional entropies H(Y_i|Y_j) between group latents are estimated with a Kernel Density Estimator, and each node's parent is chosen as the variable minimizing its conditional entropy (subject to lower marginal entropy), yielding a DAG. Compression then traverses this DAG in topological order, extending the standard hyperprior + autoregressive entropy model with a cross-task local context (masked convolution over the parent's decoded latent) and a cross-task global context (non-negative attention between parent and child latents), so that parent representations serve as informative priors that reduce the entropy of child representations.

## Results

On the Taskonomy dataset (6 tasks: Semantic Segmentation, Depth Z-buffer, Surface Normal, Keypoint 2D, Edge Texture, Autoencoder), TAMC achieves state-of-the-art multi-task compression performance, significantly outperforming all baselines in 5 out of 6 tasks at comparable or lower bitrates. At 0.224 bpp, TAMC achieves lower total test loss than MLIC++ (0.289 bpp) and ELIC (0.302 bpp) while using substantially fewer bits. For universal image reconstruction, TAMC is competitive with VTM-17.0, showing slight advantages on semantic metrics (KID, FID, LPIPS) but slightly lower PSNR and MS-SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAMC outperforms all traditional codecs (JPEG, WebP, VTM-17.0) by a wide margin on multi-task performance, as these methods introduce pixel-level distortions that severely degrade semantic task accuracy (e.g., JPEG shows +44.32% loss on Semantic Segmentation in Group 1). Against learned compression baselines ELIC and MLIC++, TAMC achieves superior task performance at a lower bitrate (0.224 vs. 0.302 and 0.289 bpp respectively), with the largest gains on Surface Normal in Group 2 (+12.43% vs. +9.69%/+9.79%) and Edge Texture in Group 3 (+5.38% vs. +2.27%/+2.47%). For universal image reconstruction, TAMC is slightly inferior to VTM-17.0 on MS-SSIM and PSNR but slightly superior on semantic-sensitive metrics (KID, FID, LPIPS), reflecting its task-aware design. Compared to VQGAN-based compression, TAMC achieves better performance on fine-grained tasks (Keypoint 2D, Semantic Segmentation, Depth Z-buffer) at low bitrates, while VQGAN struggles with sparse local feature detection due to its generative nature. TAMC is the most stable method across all task groupings, never significantly degrading any task, whereas JPEG and WebP suffer the most on Semantic Segmentation and Depth Z-buffer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) end-to-end learned image compression with hyperprior and autoregressive entropy models (Ballé et al., Minnen et al., ELIC, MLIC++), (2) the Video Coding for Machines (VCM) paradigm that targets machine-vision fidelity rather than pixel-level reconstruction, and (3) multi-task learning literature on systematic task grouping (Standley et al., Fifty et al., Zamir et al.). Its key departure is repurposing the gradient-coherence grouping criterion from MTL into a compression budget allocation problem, and extending the causal context entropy model (Guo et al. 2021) from intra-representation spatial dependencies to inter-task-group dependencies via a conditional-entropy DAG. Prior VCM methods treated tasks in isolation or with fixed, hand-specified groupings; this is the first to jointly optimize grouping and cross-task causal structure under a bitrate constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled way to answer 'which tasks share a representation and which should be decoded in what order,' a question that becomes critical as multimodal models (CLIP, GPT-4, Sora) consume compressed visual content for diverse downstream analyses. However, several limitations temper the impact: the Xception backbone and simple decoder yield lower PSNR than VTM, experiments are confined to 6 Taskonomy tasks on the Tiny split, and the lookahead module (coherence scoring + branch-and-bound grouping + KDE-based DAG construction) is computationally expensive and not designed for dynamic open-set task addition. Open questions include whether the conditional-entropy DAG generalizes to heterogeneous architectures, how to update the graph incrementally as new tasks arrive, and whether the approach scales beyond the small number of groups tested here.
