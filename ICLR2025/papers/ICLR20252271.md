## Problem

Traditional variational data assimilation (e.g., 3DVar) assumes Gaussian background errors, limiting accuracy when real forecast errors exhibit non-Gaussian structure. Meanwhile, neural network-based DA methods like DiffDA can model complex error distributions but are restricted to grid-aligned observations, failing on the sparse, off-grid observations typical of real-world operational systems. This paper seeks a method that captures non-Gaussian background errors while retaining the flexibility to handle arbitrary observation operators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252271/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a background state from the FengWu forecasting model (69 atmospheric and surface variables on a 721×1440 grid) and a set of observations—either simulated ERA5 values at sparse grid columns or real GDAS prepbufr measurements at arbitrary off-grid locations with 40 vertical levels. It produces analysis states in the same 69-variable, 721×1440 format. The observation operator is auto-differentiable: a simple mask for on-grid observations or a differentiable linear interpolation layer (from 13 physical levels to 40 observation levels) for off-grid data, making the framework compatible with any observation geometry.

## Method

VAE-Var trains a variational autoencoder (using two FengWu/Swin Transformer v2 networks as encoder and decoder) on NMC background error samples to learn the non-Gaussian conditional error distribution p(x|x_b). At assimilation time, the decoder D replaces the linear operator U in 3DVar, defining the non-linear mapping x = D(z) + x_b from a low-dimensional latent space to the physical error space. The variational cost L(z) = ½λzᵀz + L_o(D(z)+x_b, y) is minimized via L-BFGS with auto-differentiation, where λ (≈4.64, rounded to 4) empirically compensates for the omitted Jacobian determinant of the non-linear decoder. This preserves the traditional variational framework—maximizing the posterior p(x|x_b, y)—while the decoder's non-linearity captures complex error structures, and the differentiable observation operator accommodates arbitrary, irregular observation geometries without architectural changes.

## Results

On ERA5-simulated observations (721×1440 grid, 69 predictands), VAE-Var consistently achieves the lowest RMSE across all tested variables (z500, t850, t2m, u10, v10, mslp, z850, u850, v850, u500, v500, t500) and all observation densities (1000–8000 columns), with the largest margin over baselines at the sparsest setting (1000 columns). On real-world GDAS prepbufr observations over a full year (2017), VAE-Var coupled with FengWu outperforms both 3DVar and bilinear interpolation on all eight reported variables in both RMSE and Bias. The z500 analysis RMSE reaches a lower bound of approximately 26 m²/s², and one assimilation cycle takes ~18 seconds on a single A100 GPU (vs. several minutes for CPU-based 3DVar).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAE-Var outperforms DiffDA (Huang et al., 2024) on every reported variable and observation density in both fixed and unfixed ERA5-simulated settings, with the advantage most pronounced at 1000 observation columns where VAE-Var's learned non-Gaussian background model provides the greatest benefit. Against 3DVar (Descombes et al., 2015, GEN_BE implementation), VAE-Var wins in sparse observation regimes (below ~10,000 columns) but slightly lags when observation density exceeds 10,000, likely because the VAE decoder's latent space constrains fine-grained analysis increments. On the one-year GDAS prepbufr experiment, VAE-Var beats both 3DVar and bilinear interpolation on all eight variables in both RMSE and Bias, and also outperforms them under a held-out observation evaluation protocol (East Asia region, 50°E–150°E, 15°N–40°N). The interpolation baseline shows the largest RMSE fluctuations due to varying observation counts over time, while VAE-Var maintains stable performance. A key practical advantage is that VAE-Var handles off-grid, irregular observations natively via a differentiable interpolation observation operator, whereas DiffDA's repaint technique requires grid-aligned observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

VAE-Var sits at the intersection of two DA lineages: traditional variational assimilation (3DVar/4DVar), which handles arbitrary observation operators but assumes Gaussian background errors with expert-constructed covariance (GEN_BE), and diffusion-based neural DA methods (DiffDA, SDA), which capture non-Gaussian structure but are limited to grid-aligned observations. The paper's key conceptual move is to replace the linear GEN_BE mapping U with a VAE decoder as the non-linear map F from latent to error space, while retaining the variational cost function and L-BFGS optimization loop. This distinguishes it from prior latent-space DA work (Amendola 2020, Peyron 2021, Melinc & Zaplotnik 2023), which assimilated in the latent space of the physical field rather than the error field, and from diffusion-based approaches that cannot naturally accommodate off-grid or partial-variable observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-year cyclic forecasting experiment with real GDAS prepbufr observations (off-grid, sparse, partial variable coverage) is the first demonstration of a neural-network-based DA method operating at this practical scale, bridging the gap between research prototypes and operational requirements. The GPU efficiency (~18 s per cycle vs. minutes for CPU-based 3DVar) makes the approach viable for operational deployment. Key limitations remain: the Jacobian determinant of the decoder mapping is empirically dropped (compensated by a heuristic λ), the latent space constraint limits fine-grained analysis increments at high observation density, and the independence assumption between background error and x_b is retained. Open questions include extending to 4DVar, incorporating normalizing-flow constraints for rigorous Jacobian handling, and whether the method scales to higher-resolution or multi-physics systems.
