## Problem

In source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) for time-series data, the target-side adaptation process is typically computationally expensive and prone to overfitting when only a small number of unlabeled target samples are available. Parameter and sample efficiency during adaptation remain largely unexplored, which is critical when a source-pretrained model is deployed to resource-constrained devices with limited target data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253266/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on multivariate time series of shape M×L (M channels, L time steps), where sequence lengths vary substantially across benchmarks (128 for HAR datasets, 3000 for SSC, 5120 for MFD). The source model is pre-trained on labeled source data, and target adaptation uses only unlabeled target samples in a closed-set classification setting, producing a class label from a fixed set of K categories. The decomposed model reduces inference MACs by over 90% compared to the original backbone.

## Method

The framework reparameterizes the 1D-CNN backbone weights during source-model preparation using 2-mode Tucker-style factorization along the input and output channel dimensions, decomposing each convolutional weight tensor into a compact core tensor and two factor matrices via HOOI optimization. During target-side adaptation, only the core tensor is selectively fine-tuned (SFT) while the factor matrices remain frozen, reducing the number of updated parameters by 75–98% depending on the rank factor (RF) hyperparameter. This selective fine-tuning implicitly regularizes adaptation by constraining the layer-wise Frobenius-norm distance between source and target weights, as formalized through a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound. The approach is architecture-agnostic and integrates seamlessly with existing SFDA objectives (SHOT, NRC, AAD, MAPU), consistently improving F1 scores while reducing both fine-tuned parameter counts and inference MACs by over 90%.

## Results

On the SSC dataset, SFT consistently improves average F1 score over all four SFDA baselines: SHOT+SFT (RF=4) reaches 66.14% vs. SHOT's 64.74%, NRC+SFT (RF=4) reaches 64.75% vs. NRC's 62.90%, AAD+SFT (RF=4) reaches 65.22% vs. AAD's 62.10%, and MAPU+SFT (RF=4) reaches 64.94% vs. MAPU's 63.19%. Simultaneously, SFT reduces inference MACs by 84.60% (RF=4) to 93.81% (RF=8) and fine-tuned parameters by 93.60% to 98.34% compared to full-model adaptation. In the low-data regime (0.5% target samples), SFT maintains or improves performance while baselines degrade, demonstrating superior sample efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFT outperforms all four SFDA baselines (SHOT, NRC, AAD, MAPU) on average F1 across the SSC, MFD, HHAR, WISDM, and UCIHAR benchmarks, with the largest gain being +5.02% for AAD+SFT (RF=4) on SSC. The efficiency gains are substantial: at RF=4, SFT reduces fine-tuned parameters from 83.17K to 5.32K (93.6% reduction) and inference MACs from 12.92M to 1.99M (84.6% reduction) while simultaneously improving accuracy. Against LoRA (ranks 2–16) and LoKrA, SFT consistently achieves higher F1 scores on SSC across all target sample ratios (0.5%, 5%, 100%), with the advantage attributed to the structured Tucker decomposition applied at source preparation rather than a residual adapter branch. In the low-data regime (0.5% target samples), SFT's advantage over baselines is most pronounced, particularly on MFD and UCIHAR, where baselines show noticeable F1 drops while SFT maintains stable performance due to its implicit parameter-distance regularization. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that fine-tuning only the core tensor matches or exceeds fine-tuning factor matrices or both, validating the choice of the core tensor as the optimal adaptation subspace.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) for time-series, where methods like SHOT, NRC, AAD, and MAPU adapt a pretrained model to a target domain using only unlabeled target data; (2) tensor decomposition for neural network compression, where Tucker-style factorization has been applied to CNNs (Kim et al., 2016; Lebedev et al., 2015) and transformers (Ma et al., 2019) primarily for inference speedup; and (3) parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA and LoKrA that reduce trainable parameters during adaptation. The key departure from prior work is the *timing and purpose* of the decomposition: rather than introducing a residual low-rank branch at fine-tuning time (as LoRA does), the paper performs Tucker factorization during source-model preparation and then selectively fine-tunes only the core tensor on the target side, which simultaneously reduces inference MACs and acts as an implicit regularizer. The PAC-Bayesian analysis is a standard application of existing bounds (McAllester, 1998; Li & Zhang, 2021) to explain the observed regularization, not a new theoretical contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deploying time-series models on resource-constrained devices (wearables, medical sensors, industrial IoT), where the >90% reduction in both fine-tuned parameters and inference MACs while maintaining or modestly improving F1 scores directly addresses a real deployment bottleneck. The framework's compatibility with multiple SFDA methods makes it a drop-in efficiency layer rather than a competing adaptation strategy, broadening its applicability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to transformer-based time-series backbones (the paper only evaluates 1D-CNNs), whether the rank factor RF can be learned per-layer rather than set globally, and how the method behaves under more severe distribution shifts or open-set settings. The paper also notes that RF > 8 leads to underfitting, suggesting a narrow effective operating range that may not generalize across all architectures.
