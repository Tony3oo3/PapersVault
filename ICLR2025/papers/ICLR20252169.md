## Problem

Despite a wide array of debiasing methods for pretrained language models, there is no principled, unifying understanding of what makes a model fair, and no simple mechanism that can be universally applied across different debiasing approaches. The paper asks whether neural collapse—a geometric phenomenon in deep network training—can provide both an explanatory lens for existing debiasing methods and a practical tool for improving fairness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252169/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained transformer (BERT or RoBERTa) and a curated list of 425 gender-related words (210 female, 215 male) that define the subset of the vocabulary over which the collapse metric is computed. During fine-tuning, the model accumulates per-class token representations (class means) for these gender words and compares their alignment with the corresponding word-embedding vectors. The output is a fine-tuned model evaluated on both intrinsic fairness benchmarks (StereoSet, BEC-Pro) and extrinsic downstream tasks (WinoBias coreference, Bias-in-Bios occupation prediction, Bias-NLI), while GLUE scores confirm that general NLU capability is preserved.

## Method

The key empirical finding is that debiased language models consistently exhibit lower (U)NC3—the standard deviation of cosine similarities between normalized classifier weights (word embeddings) and centered class means (token representations)—specifically on gender-sensitive words, while other NC metrics (NC1, NC2, NC4) diverge. Motivated by this, the paper introduces a simple regularization term L_NC3 that minimizes this standard deviation over the gender-word subset, added to the original fine-tuning loss as L_total = L_original + α·L_NC3. This makes the alignment between each gender word's embedding and its token representation more uniform across classes, effectively collapsing the representations toward a consistent geometric structure. The regularization is agnostic to the underlying debiasing method (Mabel, ASE, BEC) and requires no data augmentation or architectural changes, making it a plug-and-play addition with minimal computational overhead. Ablations confirm that the NC3 term alone, applied to standard masked-language-modeling fine-tuning, already reduces stereotype scores and improves ICAT by 4.29 points.

## Results

On StereoSet (intrinsic fairness), adding the (U)NC3 regularization consistently improves the ICAT score across all three base debiasing methods: Mabel+(U)NC3 reaches 74.55 ICAT (vs. 73.07 for Mabel alone), ASE+(U)NC3 reaches 73.37 (vs. 71.54), and BEC+(U)NC3 reaches 72.38 (vs. 71.73). On WinoBias (extrinsic fairness), Mabel+(U)NC3 achieves the best TPR-1 of 16.93 (a 6.8-point reduction over Mabel's 23.73) and TPR-2 of 1.97 (a >40% reduction over Mabel's 3.41). On the GLUE benchmark, all models with (U)NC3 retain competitive NLU performance (e.g., BEC+(U)NC3 scores 92.5 on SST-2, 84.9 on MNLI), confirming no catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's (U)NC3 regularization is a plug-and-play addition to three representative debiasing methods (Mabel, ASE, BEC) and is compared against BERT, Sent-Debias, Context-Debias, FairFil, and ADEPT. On StereoSet ICAT, Mabel+(U)NC3 (74.55) outperforms all baselines including ADEPT (71.34), Context-Debias (69.45), and BERT (66.86); FairFil achieves the fairest SS (50.93) but at the cost of a severely degraded LM score (44.85), yielding the worst ICAT (44.01). On WinoBias, Mabel+(U)NC3 achieves the lowest TPR-1 (16.93) and TPR-2 (1.97), beating ADEPT (21.54 / 3.85) and Mabel alone (23.73 / 3.41). On Bias-in-Bios, Mabel+(U)NC3 attains the smallest GAP_TPR (0.455) and GAP_RMS (0.126), outperforming ADEPT (0.945 / 0.121) and Context-Debias (0.931 / 0.137). On GLUE, all (U)NC3-augmented models remain within ~1–2 points of their unregularized counterparts, confirming that the fairness gains come without sacrificing general NLU capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: neural collapse (Papyan et al., 2020; Wu & Papyan, 2024), a geometric phenomenon in the terminal phase of classification training, and the large body of LM debiasing work spanning data augmentation (BEC), contrastive fine-tuning (Mabel), and regularization (ASE). The key conceptual departure is showing that these heterogeneous debiasing methods all implicitly drive the same geometric property—increased alignment between word embeddings (classifiers) and token representations (class means)—specifically on gender-sensitive vocabulary. Rather than proposing yet another task-specific debiasing algorithm, the paper repurposes the NC3 alignment metric as a universal, plug-and-play regularization that can be layered on top of any existing fairness method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unifying geometric explanation for why diverse debiasing techniques work, which is valuable for understanding and designing future fairness methods. The NC3 regularization is extremely simple (a single standard-deviation term over cosine alignments) and agnostic to the underlying debiasing pipeline, making it a low-cost add-on. However, practical gains are modest (e.g., +1.5 ICAT on StereoSet for Mabel), experiments are limited to BERT/RoBERTa and gender bias only, and the finding that collapse is specific to gender words rather than the whole vocabulary limits NC as a general debiasing diagnostic. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to large LLMs, other protected attributes, and whether the NC3 signal can be exploited during pretraining rather than only fine-tuning.
