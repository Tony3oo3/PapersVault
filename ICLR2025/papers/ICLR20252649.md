## Problem

Current perturbation response prediction methods reduce textually-rich biological knowledge graphs to adjacency matrices, train on regression objectives (log-fold change) that misalign with downstream biological analyses like differential expression and gene set enrichment, and operate as black boxes that reveal little about the learned biology. The paper asks whether large language models, which natively process the semantic content of biological relationships, can serve as a more faithful and interpretable medium for reasoning over perturbation experiments.

## Input / Output

For differential expression and direction of change, the method takes a perturbation-gene pair (p, g) along with serialized knowledge graph entries (from UniProt, Ensembl, Gene Ontology, CORUM, STRING, Reactome, BioPlex), up to 15 retrieved training-set examples with their discretized binary outcomes, and a cell line context sentence, and produces a binary label (DE or not; up or down). For gene set enrichment, it takes a cluster of gene summaries and produces a short name (≤10 words) and a 1–2 sentence description. Notably, all experimental outcomes are discretized via Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Benjamini-Hochberg correction rather than using continuous log-fold change values, and the knowledge graph is consumed as natural-language text rather than an adjacency matrix.

## Method

SUMMER is a zero-shot, inference-time LLM framework with three steps inspired by message-passing and RAG. First, an LLM (Llama3-70B) summarizes each gene's knowledge graph neighborhood into two directed text descriptions—one as a perturbation (downstream effects) and one as a downstream gene (upstream influences)—with hierarchical application to gene sets. Second, graph-based retrieval selects up to 15 relevant training examples by knowledge graph proximity (top-10 shared neighbors), stratified into pairs where both entities are related, only the perturbation is related, or only the gene is related. Third, a chain-of-thought prompt (Llama3-8B) poses five guiding questions that force the LLM to identify the most similar retrieved examples, summarize their observed effects, check for pathway overlap, and answer the final binary question while citing specific retrieved evidence. The framework requires no finetuning and runs three inference passes per example for uncertainty quantification.

## Results

On differential expression (macro AUROC), SUMMER achieves 0.58–0.61 across five datasets, outperforming all graph-based baselines (GEARS: 0.48–0.54, SCGPT: 0.48–0.52, GAT: 0.54–0.57) and matching or exceeding the strongest non-LLM baseline Retrieval(No LLM) (0.55–0.64) in 4 of 5 settings. On direction of change, SUMMER dominates with AUROC 0.62–0.69, surpassing the best graph baseline GEARS (0.51–0.64) by 3–15 points on four of five datasets. On gene set enrichment, SUMMER(8b) achieves ROUGE-1 recall of 0.57 and BERT F1 of 0.69 on gene clusters, substantially exceeding the best over-representation analysis baseline (GO top-10: ROUGE-1 0.32, BERT F1 0.60). Notably, LLM(No CoT) and LLM(No retrieval) both perform at chance level (~0.50 AUROC), confirming that both retrieval of experimental outcomes and guided chain-of-thought reasoning are essential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUMMER outperforms GEARS (Roohini et al., 2023) on direction of change in 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., 0.65 vs 0.52 on HepG2, 0.66 vs 0.51 on Jurkat), while GEARS is near-random on differential expression (0.48–0.54 AUROC). SCGPT (Cui et al., 2024) performs at or below chance on both tasks (0.48–0.54 AUROC), confirming that single-cell foundation models do not transfer well to discrete perturbation outcome prediction. GENEPT (Chen & Zou, 2024) is the strongest non-LLM baseline on DE (0.54–0.58 AUROC) but trails SUMMER by 2–6 points; on direction of change, GENEPT-PROT reaches 0.55–0.58, well below SUMMER's 0.62–0.69. The GAT baseline (Veličković et al., 2017) shows that graph adjacency alone carries limited signal (0.54–0.57 on DE), while the naive PHYSICAL interaction baseline (0.52–0.55) is competitive, underscoring how hard these tasks remain. On gene set enrichment, SUMMER(8b) achieves ROUGE-1 recall of 0.57 and BERT F1 of 0.69 on gene clusters, roughly doubling the best GSEApy-based baseline (GO top-10: 0.32 / 0.60), and independently, a domain expert rated LLM summaries as equal or more informative than classical enrichment in 92% of cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant perturbation-response prediction paradigm (GEARS, SCGPT, GAT) that formulates the task as continuous regression of log-fold change over knowledge graph adjacency matrices. Instead, it reframes perturbation prediction as discrete question-answering (binary differential expression, binary direction of change, gene set summarization), arguing that this aligns with how biologists actually interpret experiments. Methodologically, SUMMER repurposes standard LLM reasoning strategies—chain-of-thought prompting and retrieval-augmented generation—for a biological setting where the 'retrieval corpus' consists of discretized experimental outcomes rather than text documents, and where knowledge graphs are serialized into natural language summaries rather than fed to GNNs. The paper also contrasts with prior LLM-in-biology work (GenePT, biological QA benchmarks) by emphasizing predictive tasks over knowledge recall.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PERTURBQA provides a benchmark that exposes a fundamental misalignment: state-of-the-art perturbation models (GEARS, SCGPT) perform near-random on the discrete outcomes biologists actually care about, suggesting the regression objective may be optimizing the wrong thing. SUMMER demonstrates that a simple, finetuning-free LLM pipeline (8B model) can match or exceed these SOTA methods, lowering the barrier to entry for computational biology. Key limitations include reliance on a small model for most inference, persistent errors in causal directionality reasoning, and evaluation on only five cell lines. Open questions include whether larger models or structured reasoning (beyond CoT) can resolve the directionality failures, and whether the approach extends to combinatorial perturbations where evaluation data is scarce.
