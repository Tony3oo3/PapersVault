## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods optimize synthetic datasets by matching feature, gradient, or trajectory similarity with the real dataset, but neglect the class-aware complexity inherent in the distilled data. This uncontrolled complexity makes synthetic samples excessively difficult for networks to learn from, degrading generalization across architectures. The paper identifies that no prior method explicitly measures or constrains how the synthetic dataset's per-class structure affects learnability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large real dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32, ImageNet-1K at variable resolution) and a pre-trained surrogate network (e.g., ResNet-18) whose penultimate feature layer (e.g., 512-dim) is used to compute the CMI constraint. It produces a synthetic dataset with IPC ranging from 1 to 100 images per class, optimized via SGD. The CMI is computed in the feature space of the pre-trained network rather than the output probability space, and multiple pre-trained networks with different random initializations are sampled during optimization to keep the constraint dataset-specific and architecture-independent.

## Method

The core contribution is a class-aware conditional mutual information (CMI) regularizer, I(S; Ẑ | Y), that measures how much the softmax of a pre-trained network's features (Ẑ) is influenced by the synthetic input S given the true label Y. Empirically, CMI is computed as the average KL divergence between each synthetic sample's softmax feature distribution and its class centroid in feature space (Eq. 10). The CMI enhanced loss L = L_DD + λ·CMI_emp(S) is added on top of any existing distillation loss (DM, DSA, MTT, IDC, etc.), making it a plug-and-play module. Minimizing CMI concentrates synthetic samples around class centers in feature space, reducing per-class complexity and improving cross-architecture generalization. For memory efficiency, the CMI computation is decomposed by class and embedded into the distillation loop (Algorithm 1), and the surrogate network is randomly resampled each iteration to avoid overfitting to a single architecture.

## Results

The CMI enhanced loss serves as a plug-and-play regularization that consistently improves existing dataset distillation methods across SVHN, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet-1K. The largest single improvement is +5.5% top-1 accuracy for DSA+CMI over DSA on CIFAR-10 at IPC=50 (66.1% vs. 60.6%), and +5.1% for DM+CMI over DM on SVHN at IPC=10 (77.9% vs. 72.8%). On ImageNet-1K, SRe2L+CMI achieves 54.6% top-1 accuracy at IPC=100, surpassing SRe2L (52.8%) by 1.8%. Cross-architecture evaluation shows IDM+CMI gains ~9% over IDM when evaluated on AlexNet, VGG11, and ResNet18, demonstrating strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The CMI enhanced loss is applied as a universal plug-and-play module to six base distillation methods (MTT, DM, IDM, DSA, IDC, SRe2L) and consistently outperforms each base method across all datasets and IPC settings. The most substantial gains appear in feature-matching methods: DM+CMI improves over DM by up to 5.1% (SVHN IPC=10) and 4.0% (CIFAR-10 IPC=10), while DSA+CMI surpasses DSA by 5.5% on CIFAR-10 IPC=50. For gradient-matching methods, IDM+CMI gains 3.3–4.8% over IDM, and MTT+CMI gains 0.8–2.2% over MTT. On ImageNet-1K, SRe2L+CMI improves over SRe2L by 2.9% at IPC=10 and 1.8% at IPC=100. In cross-architecture evaluation (Table 5), IDM+CMI achieves ~9% absolute improvement over IDM on AlexNet, VGG11, and ResNet18, far exceeding the same-architecture gains, indicating that CMI constraint substantially enhances generalization to unseen architectures. The method also combines additively with other techniques (SeqMatch, IID, DREAM), yielding further stacked improvements (e.g., IID+CMI reaches 63.0% on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 vs. IID's 59.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the information-theoretic CMI framework from Yang et al. (2023) and the KL-divergence-based CMI estimation technique from Ye et al. (2024), repurposing them as a complexity regularizer for dataset distillation. It departs from the dominant paradigm in DD—matching gradients, features, or training trajectories between synthetic and real data—by instead constraining the intrinsic class-aware complexity of the synthetic dataset itself, motivated by prior observations (Paul et al., 2021; Zheng et al., 2022) that simpler datasets generalize better. Unlike SDC (Wang et al., 2024a), which uses gradient-based constraints tied to a specific proxy backbone, or IID (Deng et al., 2024), which applies L2-norm feature aggregation, the CMI constraint operates in the feature space of a pre-trained network and is architecture-agnostic, making it a universal plug-and-play module across gradient matching, feature matching, and trajectory matching methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CMI-enhanced loss offers a practical, drop-in regularization that improves accuracy by up to 5.5% and reduces required distillation iterations by 5–10×, which is significant for practitioners who already have DD pipelines in place. The cross-architecture generalization gains (~9% with IDM) suggest the distilled data becomes more robust to backbone changes, addressing a known weakness of DD. Limitations include the infeasibility of CMI computation at IPC=1 (requiring at least one sample per class for empirical estimation), the need to tune λ relative to the base distillation loss, and the dependency on pre-trained surrogate networks. Open questions include whether CMI-based constraints extend to non-image modalities (text, audio) or to generative distillation settings where the synthetic data is produced by a diffusion model rather than optimized end-to-end.
