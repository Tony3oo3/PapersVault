## Problem

Existing MLLM safety benchmarks evaluate whether models correctly reject harmful prompts, but the complementary failure mode—oversensitivity, where models refuse benign queries triggered by specific visual stimuli—remains largely unexplored in the multimodal setting. The only prior oversensitivity benchmark (XSTest) is text-only, leaving no systematic way to measure how visual context causes MLLMs to misclassify safe requests as dangerous.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251168/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark sample is an RGB image (natural web-retrieved or DALL-E 3 synthesized, 79.67% natural) paired with a short text query formatted as a visual question-answering prompt. The evaluation produces a binary refusal/compliance label per sample, aggregated into an Average Refusal Rate (ARR) broken down by stimulus type. Both human raters (97.8% inter-annotator agreement) and GPT-4V automated evaluation are used, with the GPT evaluator achieving 92–99% agreement with human judgments across models.

## Method

MOSSBench is constructed via a two-step pipeline: (1) multiple LLMs (GPT-4, Gemini-pro 1.5, Claude 3 Opus) generate diverse scenario descriptions guided by three identified visual stimuli—Exaggerated Risk, Negated Harm, and Counterintuitive Interpretation—followed by image retrieval via Google Search API or DALL-E 3 synthesis; (2) candidates are filtered through manual review and AMT crowd-sourced harmfulness scoring (mean 1.84/5). The empirical evaluation spans 20 MLLMs (proprietary API, proprietary web, and open-source) under deterministic zero-shot settings, measuring ARR per stimulus type. A key analytical contribution is a three-stage ablation (perception → intent reasoning → safety judgement) that feeds oracle captions and oracle intentions to isolate which reasoning stage each stimulus type primarily disrupts, revealing that Exaggerated Risk errors concentrate in perception while Negated Harm and Counterintuitive Interpretation errors concentrate in intent reasoning and safety judgement.

## Results

On MOSSBench (300 benign multimodal queries), the Average Refusal Rate (ARR) varies dramatically across 20 MLLMs. Claude 3 Opus (web) exhibits the highest oversensitivity at 76.33% ARR (human evaluation), followed by Gemini Advanced (web) at 63.67%, while GPT-4o (web) shows minimal oversensitivity at just 2%. Among open-source models, refusal rates range from 0.67% (mPLUG-Owl2) to 16% (Qwen-VL-Chat), confirming that proprietary models with stricter safety alignment are significantly more prone to rejecting harmless queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, MOSSBench does not propose a new method but evaluates 20 MLLMs on oversensitivity. The key comparison is between proprietary and open-source models: Claude 3 Opus (web) rejects 76.33% of benign queries, while GPT-4o (web) rejects only 2%, a 74-point gap. Among API versions, Claude 3 Sonnet (65.33%) and Claude 3 Haiku (55.33%) are far more oversensitive than GPT-4V (2%) or GPT-4o (8%). Open-source models (mPLUG-Owl2 at 0.67% to Qwen-VL-Chat at 16%) consistently show lower refusal rates than their proprietary counterparts, attributed to less aggressive multimodal safety fine-tuning. The paper further demonstrates that web versions of the same model are significantly more oversensitive than their API versions (e.g., Claude 3 Opus: 76.33% web vs. 30.67% API), and that system prompts emphasizing safety increase refusal rates by up to 16 percentage points (Gemini-Pro 1.5: 29.33% vanilla → 45.33% with safety prompt).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-only oversensitivity evaluation framework of XSTest (Röttger et al., 2024) to the multimodal setting, identifying three visual stimulus types (Exaggerated Risk, Negated Harm, Counterintuitive Interpretation) that trigger unwarranted refusals in MLLMs. It also adapts the three-stage reasoning decomposition (perception, intent reasoning, safety judgement) from Zhang et al. (2024b) to trace where in the response pipeline oversensitivity errors originate. Conceptually, the paper draws an analogy to cognitive distortions in human psychology (mental filtering, catastrophizing, mind reading) to frame and categorize the failure modes, a framing not previously applied to MLLM safety evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the most safety-aligned proprietary models (Claude 3 Opus web, Gemini Advanced) reject up to 76% of benign queries reveals a significant and previously unquantified usability cost of aggressive safety alignment in multimodal systems. The systematic demonstration that different stimulus types implicate different reasoning stages (perception for Exaggerated Risk, intent reasoning and safety judgement for the other two) provides actionable diagnostic structure for future mitigation work. Key limitations include the relatively small benchmark size (300 samples), reliance on LLM-generated scenarios that may introduce bias, and coverage of only three stimulus types. Open questions include whether the safety-oversensitivity trade-off can be broken with better alignment methods, and whether the cognitive-distortion framing generalizes to additional failure modes beyond the three identified.
