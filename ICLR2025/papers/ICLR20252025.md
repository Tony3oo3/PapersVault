## Problem

While Bayesian deep learning offers principled uncertainty quantification, computing inferences such as predictions has been largely overlooked, with Monte Carlo integration over the posterior remaining the standard approach. This is computationally expensive, especially for high-dimensional posteriors, and hinders practical downstream tasks like outlier detection and sensitivity analysis that require many repeated evaluations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data point (image, text sequence, or tabular vector) and a pre-computed posterior approximation over network parameters (Gaussian from Laplace approximation or mean-field VI, with full, diagonal, or KFAC covariance structure). It produces the full posterior predictive distribution in a single forward pass without sampling, yielding predictive means and variances, calibration metrics, OOD entropy scores, and pixel-level sensitivity maps. For transformer architectures, the method operates on the last two (ViT) or four (GPT-2) blocks, treating query and key projections deterministically while propagating uncertainty only through value projections.

## Method

The core mechanism combines two approximations applied layer-by-layer: (1) a local Gaussian approximation at each linear layer, where the product of a random activation and a random weight is approximated as Gaussian under an independence assumption between activations and parameters, and (2) a first-order Taylor expansion (local linearisation) of each non-linear activation function at the mean of its pre-activation. Because Gaussian distributions are closed under linear transformations, the resulting activation distribution at each layer is computed analytically, enabling the entire network to be traversed in one forward pass. For transformers, the softmax in attention is handled by treating Q and K deterministically (using mean inputs) while propagating the distribution through V, and a dedicated block-retrieval algorithm extracts the needed row-pair covariances from KFAC-factorised posteriors without materialising the full covariance matrix. The approach is architecture-agnostic and covariance-structure-agnostic, reducing to O(D_out·D_in) per layer under diagonal covariance.

## Results

On UCI regression (11 datasets), the proposed method achieves better NLPD than Monte Carlo sampling in 11/11 cases under MFVI and 8/11 under full-covariance LA, while matching or exceeding the global linearised model (GLM) in 8/11 cases. On MNIST-like classification, the method achieves comparable accuracy (e.g., 0.975 on MNIST, 0.881 on FMNIST) while substantially reducing NLPD (0.089 vs. 0.210 for LA sampling on MNIST) and ECE (0.004 vs. 0.122 for LA sampling). On ViT fine-tuned on CIFAR-10, the method reaches 0.976 ACC with NLPD of 0.086 and ECE of 0.009, outperforming both LA and MFVI sampling baselines on uncertainty metrics while matching accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LA Sampling (Daxberger et al., 2021), the proposed method consistently wins on NLPD and ECE across all datasets while matching accuracy; e.g., on FMNIST it reduces NLPD from 0.556 to 0.397 and ECE from 0.151 to 0.012. Against MFVI Sampling (Shen et al., 2024, using IVON), the method achieves dramatically lower NLPD (e.g., 0.529 vs. 2.010 on FMNIST) and ECE (0.013 vs. 0.144) while maintaining comparable accuracy. Against the global linearised model GLM (Immer et al., 2021), the method is on par or slightly better on NLPD (e.g., 0.397 vs. 0.548 on FMNIST) and substantially better on ECE (0.012 vs. 0.078), though GLM edges out slightly on OrganCMNIST ECE (0.038 vs. 0.122). Against Moment-Matching (Wu et al., 2019), the method wins on ACC, NLPD, and ECE across all four MNIST-like datasets (e.g., 0.816 vs. 0.771 ACC and 0.733 vs. 0.838 NLPD on OrganCMNIST), while being more general-purpose since it applies to any differentiable activation without case-specific derivations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stable distribution propagation framework of Petersen et al. (2024), which propagated aleatoric input uncertainty through deterministic networks, to the epistemic uncertainty setting where the posterior over parameters is a (Gaussian) distribution. It departs from the global linearised Laplace approximation (GLM, Immer et al., 2021b) by using local linearisation at each activation rather than a single global linearisation, and from moment-matching (Wu et al., 2019) by requiring no per-activation-function derivations. Crucially, the paper disentangles posterior approximation (LA, MFVI) from the inference step, positioning itself as a plug-and-play prediction layer on top of any existing posterior approximation method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing Monte Carlo sampling with a single analytical forward pass, the method removes the dominant computational bottleneck in BDL inference, making uncertainty quantification practical for downstream tasks like sensitivity analysis and OOD detection that were previously prohibitively expensive. Key limitations include the independence assumption between activations and parameters (particularly harmful in residual connections), the approximation error from local linearisation that scales with input distribution width, and the need for a validation set to calibrate predictive variance. Open questions include relaxing the residual-block independence assumption, estimating the induced approximation error to identify failure modes, and scaling to networks with very large output spaces.
