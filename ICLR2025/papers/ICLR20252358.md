## Problem

Existing audio-visual speech recognition (AVSR) research has primarily addressed audio corruptions (background noise) but largely ignores visual corruptions such as lip occlusions, blurred frames, or pixelated faces, which are equally detrimental in real-world settings. More critically, no prior method handles audio-visual *joint* corruption in a general, architecture-agnostic way without relying on external scoring modules or generative reconstruction pipelines, and the cross-modal alignment problem—where a corrupted modality degrades the fusion of both modalities—remains unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 26-dimensional log filter-bank audio features (25 fps, 4 adjacent frames stacked) and 96×96 center-cropped lip video frames (25 Hz, randomly cropped to 88×88 during training), both processed at a unified 25 fps frame rate. During training and evaluation, both modalities are simultaneously corrupted: video frames undergo object occlusion, Gaussian noise, blurring, hands occlusion, or pixelation over 10–50% of the sequence, while audio is mixed with babble, speech, music, or natural noise at SNR levels from −10 dB to +10 dB. The output is a transcribed text sequence produced by a 9-layer Transformer decoder trained with sequence-to-sequence negative log-likelihood.

## Method

CAV2vec is a self-distillation framework (EMA teacher–student) built on a 24-layer Transformer encoder with a ResNet-18 visual front-end, trained as an 'uptraining' phase on top of a pretrained AV-HuBERT checkpoint. Its core contribution is a *unimodal multi-task* corrupted prediction strategy: the ACP task feeds corrupted video (with audio zeroed) into the student and regresses toward clean audio targets from the teacher, while the VCP task does the reverse (corrupted audio → clean video targets), thereby distilling cross-modal knowledge and reducing representation-space dispersion caused by corruption. This contrasts with the straightforward AVCP task (corrupted multimodal input → clean multimodal target), which the ablations show to be inferior because mixed-modality targets obscure per-modality corruption effects. The overall loss combines ACP, VCP, standard masked prediction, and an MLM-style cluster prediction loss (λ_ACP = λ_VCP = λ_MASK = 1.0, λ_MLM = 2.0), with corrupted and masked frames kept disjoint. The approach requires no architectural changes or external modules, and the paper further introduces a robust AVSR benchmark with unseen corruption types (hands occlusion, face pixelation, DEMAND real-world noise) to test generalizability.

## Results

On the LRS3 robust AVSR benchmark with audio-visual joint corruption, CAV2vec (325M params) achieves an average N-WER of 5.1% under object occlusion + noise, 5.2% under hands occlusion, and 5.1% under pixelated face, consistently outperforming all baselines. In noise-dominant scenarios (N≥S, SNR ≤ 0 dB), CAV2vec reaches 7.2% N-WER versus 8.6% for AV-HuBERT and 9.0% for AV-data2vec. On the DEMAND real-world noise benchmark, CAV2vec achieves 4.3% average WER across 8 noisy environments (object occlusion + noise), compared to 5.0% for AV-HuBERT and 5.1% for AV-data2vec. Clean WER is 1.5%, matching the best baseline (AV-data2vec) while substantially improving corrupted-condition performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAV2vec (325M) achieves the lowest N-WER across all three visual corruption types, outperforming the closest same-size baseline AV-data2vec (325M) by 1.1% (obj+noise), 1.3% (hands), and 0.9% (pixelate), and AV-HuBERT (325M) by 0.9%, 1.0%, and 0.7% respectively. Against the larger AV-RelScore (437M), which is specifically designed for audio-visual joint corruption via reliability scoring, CAV2vec still wins by 0.8% (obj+noise), 0.9% (hands), and 0.7% (pixelate) N-WER without requiring additional scoring modules. The advantage is most pronounced against the 673M-parameter RAVEn and BRAVEn, where CAV2vec reduces N-WER by approximately 3–4% (e.g., 5.1% vs. 15.2% for RAVEn and 12.2% for BRAVEn on obj+noise), demonstrating that corrupted representation learning is more effective than simply scaling model size. On the DEMAND real-world noise benchmark, CAV2vec achieves 4.3% average WER versus 5.0% (AV-HuBERT) and 5.1% (AV-data2vec), confirming generalization to unseen noise environments. CAV2vec also maintains competitive clean WER (1.5%) while substantially improving robustness, indicating that the corrupted prediction training does not degrade performance in clean conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAV2vec extends the self-distillation framework of Data2vec (Baevski et al., 2022) and the audio-visual masked prediction paradigm of AV-data2vec (Lian et al., 2023) by replacing random masking with structured corruption (occlusion, noise, blurring) and introducing a corrupted prediction task where the student predicts clean teacher targets from corrupted inputs. The key conceptual departure is the unimodal multi-task learning strategy: rather than the natural multimodal-to-multimodal prediction (AVCP), CAV2vec predicts clean audio targets from corrupted video and clean video targets from corrupted audio, explicitly targeting cross-modal alignment. This positions the work between the noise-robust pretraining line (Shi et al., 2022b) and the visual-corruption modeling line (Hong et al., 2023), unifying both under a single representation learning objective without requiring external scoring modules or generative reconstruction pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in AVSR robustness: while audio noise augmentation is well-studied, visual corruption (occlusion, blurring, pixelation) has received far less attention, yet both occur simultaneously in real-world deployments. The finding that unimodal cross-modal prediction outperforms multimodal prediction for corrupted inputs provides a useful design principle for multimodal robustness that could transfer beyond speech. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-English datasets, whether the corruption types studied (occlusion, Gaussian noise, blurring, pixelation) capture the full diversity of real-world visual degradation, and whether the additional uptraining phase (60K steps) can be reduced or integrated into a single training pipeline. The benchmark with unseen corruption types (hands occlusion, pixelated faces) is a valuable contribution for evaluating generalization.
