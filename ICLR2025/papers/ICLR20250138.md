## Problem

State-of-the-art dense feature matchers like RoMa produce unreliable certainty estimates under image corruptions and adversarial attacks, over-confidently classifying matchable pixels as unmatchable (certainty below 0.05) and thereby failing to sample enough reliable matches for downstream geometry estimation. This robustness gap has not been systematically studied, as prior feature matching benchmarks only evaluate on clean images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250138/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (560×560 at training, 672×672 at inference) and outputs a dense 2D correspondence field (warp) plus a per-pixel certainty score. Unlike standard dense matchers that produce a single sigmoid logit per pixel for certainty, the modified certainty branch outputs a 2-dimensional non-negative evidence vector per pixel, from which a Dirichlet distribution over class probabilities is derived. The final certainty score is the expected probability of the 'reliable match' class under this Dirichlet, and a built-in uncertainty mass (K/Σα) is also available for analysis.

## Method

The paper replaces the binary cross-entropy loss in RoMa's certainty prediction branch with an evidential deep learning (EDL) loss. The certainty head is modified to output a 2D evidence vector e per pixel; Dirichlet concentration parameters are set to α = e + 1 (K=2), and the training loss combines the expected squared error over the Dirichlet distribution with a KL divergence term that shrinks evidence for the non-ground-truth class. At inference, the certainty score is the Dirichlet mean α/S rather than a sigmoid, which naturally prevents over-confidence: under corruption the model predicts high uncertainty (similar evidence for both classes) instead of collapsing to near-zero certainty for matchable regions. The approach is applied at both coarse (global matcher) and fine (warp refiner) scales, with ablations showing that fine-scale EDL is critical for corruption robustness. The overall architecture (DINOv2 + VGG19 features, transformer global matcher, CNN warp refiners) and training setup remain unchanged from RoMa, and the method incurs only 1.4% additional training cost with marginally lower inference time.

## Results

On the proposed MegaDepth-1500-C corruption benchmark, the method achieves 49.8% mean corruption AUC@5° (mCA), a 2.2% improvement over RoMa (47.6%), with up to 10.1% gain on shot noise at severity level 5. On clean MegaDepth-1500, it reaches 63.0% AUC@5° (vs. RoMa's 62.6%) and on clean ScanNet-1500, 32.2% AUC@5° (vs. RoMa's 31.8%), confirming no clean-data performance sacrifice. Under adversarial attacks, the method gains up to 2.9% over RoMa for FGSM and up to 3.4% for PGD on MegaDepth-1500. Inference cost is marginally lower than RoMa (327 ms vs. 329 ms) due to simpler arithmetic in the evidential probability computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline RoMa (CVPR'24), the method wins on every evaluated metric: +0.4% AUC@5° on clean MegaDepth-1500, +2.2% mCA on MegaDepth-1500-C, +0.4% on clean ScanNet-1500, +0.4% mCA on ScanNet-1500-C, and up to +3.4% under PGD adversarial attacks. Against DKM (CVPR'23), the gap is larger under corruptions (+5.5% mCA on MegaDepth-1500-C) and the method also leads on clean data by +2.6% AUC@5°. LoFTR (CVPR'21) is the weakest baseline, losing by 11.2% mCA on MegaDepth-1500-C and dropping to near-zero AUC under adversarial attacks as ε increases. On clean-data SotA comparisons (Tables 3–4), the method edges out all listed baselines including PMatch (61.4%), DKM (60.4%), and CasMTR (59.1%) on MegaDepth-1500, and PATS (26.0%), DKM (29.4%), and PMatch (29.4%) on ScanNet-1500. The method also generalizes to the DKM architecture, improving its mCA by 1.3% on MegaDepth-1500-C, confirming the framework is not specific to RoMa.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes evidential deep learning (Sensoy et al., 2018), originally developed for classification uncertainty, into the certainty prediction branch of dense feature matchers like RoMa and DKM, which previously relied on standard binary cross-entropy. It also extends the corruption-robustness benchmarking paradigm of Hendrycks & Dietterich (2019) from image classification to the feature matching domain, creating the first corruption and adversarial-attack benchmarks for dense matching. Conceptually, the paper departs from the point-estimate probability framing of prior certainty classifiers by modeling second-order probabilities via Dirichlet distributions, directly targeting the over-confidence failure mode observed in SotA matchers under distribution shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduced MegaDepth-1500-C and ScanNet-1500-C benchmarks fill a clear gap: no prior feature matching evaluation considered common corruptions or adversarial attacks, both of which are relevant for real deployment and worst-case robustness analysis. The practical impact is that the modification is minimal (one extra output channel, negligible compute overhead) and plug-and-play across dense matchers, making it easy to adopt. However, the gains are most pronounced on outdoor scenes with smaller overlap (MegaDepth) and less so on indoor scenes (ScanNet), and the method does not improve the underlying warp quality—only the certainty calibration—so it cannot rescue fundamentally wrong correspondences. Open questions include whether combining EDL with adversarial training or data augmentation would yield further robustness gains, and whether the approach transfers to other matching paradigms (detector-based, detector-free).
