## Problem

While preserving target ordinality in the feature space is known to improve deep regression, no theoretical explanation existed for why this helps, and standard regression losses were not understood to be insufficient at compressing representations. This paper shows that ordinality reduces the conditional entropy H(Z|Y) (tightness), but reveals that typical regressors can only move representations along a single direction (the regressor weight θ), leaving them unable to tighten the feature manifold within the solution space.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard regression inputs: face images (AgeDB-DIR), RGB images (NYUD2-DIR), and noisy point samples from low-dimensional manifolds (Mammoth, Torus, Circle). The key structural difference is that the Multiple Targets strategy duplicates the scalar target into M copies (M=8 in experiments), so the regressor head outputs M predictions that are averaged into a single continuous value. The ROT-Reg regularizer additionally requires a batch of samples to compute pairwise transport cost matrices in both target and feature spaces via the Sinkhorn algorithm.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary tightening strategies. First, the Multiple Targets (MT) strategy duplicates the regression target M times, adding M−1 extra linear regressors whose combined constraints reduce the solution space S_{y_i} from a line to a point, tightening the representation globally. Second, the Regression Optimal Transport Regularizer (ROT-Reg) computes self-entropic optimal transport plans on both the target set and the feature set, then minimizes the absolute difference in total transport costs ⟨C^Z, T̃^Y⟩ − ⟨C^Z, T̃^Z⟩, encouraging the feature manifold to locally mirror the target's similarity structure. The final training loss is L_f = L_t + λ·L_ot, where L_t is the standard regression loss. The theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–2) proves that perfect ordinality implies zero conditional entropy and that regression gradients are orthogonal to the solution space, explaining why these additional constraints are necessary.

## Results

On AgeDB-DIR (age estimation), the combined MT + L_ot method achieves 7.28±0.05 MAE (ALL), improving over the baseline (7.80±0.12) by 0.52 and outperforming the strongest baseline PH-Reg (7.32±0.09). On NYUD2-DIR (depth estimation), MT + L_ot reduces RMSE to 1.321 (ALL) from the baseline's 1.477, a 0.156 reduction, while also improving δ₁ accuracy to 0.701. On the coordinate prediction task, L_ot alone (MSE 87±26 on Mammoth, 0.77±0.02 on Torus, 0.010±0.001 on Circle) performs comparably to PH-Reg, which is specifically designed for topology preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed MT + L_ot method outperforms all four baselines on both real-world tasks. On AgeDB-DIR, it beats PH-Reg (the strongest baseline) by 0.04 MAE (7.28 vs. 7.32), RankSim by 0.34 (7.28 vs. 7.62), and OE by 0.37 (7.28 vs. 7.65). On NYUD2-DIR, the advantage is larger: MT + L_ot achieves 1.321 RMSE vs. PH-Reg's 1.450, OE's 1.419, and RankSim's 1.522 (which actually degrades performance). On the coordinate prediction task, L_ot alone is competitive with PH-Reg (49±27 vs. 87±26 on Mammoth; 0.61±0.05 vs. 0.77±0.02 on Torus), while the MT strategy provides limited additional benefit there since the 3D target space already provides sufficient constraints. The Topological Autoencoder baseline is only compared on the coordinate prediction task, where L_ot matches or slightly exceeds it on Torus and Circle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of information bottleneck theory for representation learning (Zhang et al., 2024; Shwartz-Ziv & Tishby, 2017) and the empirical observation that ordinality-preserving regularizers (RankSim, Gong et al. 2022; Keramati et al. 2023) and classification-based reformulations (Farebrother et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2023) improve deep regression. It departs from prior ordinality work by providing the first theoretical link between ordinality and conditional entropy H(Z|Y) (tightness), and from prior classification-helps-regression analyses by identifying the specific gradient-direction constraint that limits regression's tightening ability. The ROT-Reg regularizer repurposes the self-entropic optimal transport formulation (Yan et al., 2024; Landa et al., 2021) from clustering to regression representation learning, while the Multiple Targets strategy is directly inspired by the multi-classifier structure of cross-entropy classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for a long-standing empirical puzzle—why classification-based approaches consistently outperform direct regression—and translates this into two simple, low-cost practical techniques (MT and ROT-Reg) that practitioners can adopt with minimal architectural changes. However, the improvements are modest (0.52 MAE on age estimation, 0.156 RMSE on depth), and the MT strategy shows limited benefit when the target space is already multi-dimensional (coordinate prediction), suggesting the approach's gains are most relevant for scalar-target regression. Open questions include whether the tightness framework extends naturally to multi-dimensional or structured targets, how the methods scale to very large datasets, and whether the theoretical gradient-direction analysis holds for non-linear regressors beyond the linear case analyzed.
