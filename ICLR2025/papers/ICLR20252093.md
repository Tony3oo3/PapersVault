## Problem

Real-world image restoration involves multiple simultaneous degradations (e.g., rain + haze + noise) that no single specialized model can address, yet existing all-in-one models suffer from optimization difficulty, parameter inefficiency, and poor extensibility. While humans naturally solve this by sequentially applying specialized tools with planning, reflection, and adjustment, no prior system has automated this agentic workflow for image restoration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252093/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB image degraded by 2–3 types of corruption drawn from eight categories (low resolution, noise, motion blur, defocus blur, rain, haze, JPEG compression artifacts, low light) and produces a restored high-quality image. The degraded image is first consumed by a fine-tuned VLM (DepictQA) that outputs natural-language severity assessments per degradation type, which then feeds into the LLM's text-based reasoning loop. The final output is the image after potentially multiple rounds of tool application, with intermediate reflections and rollbacks occurring transparently within the pipeline.

## Method

AgenticIR orchestrates a toolbox of 3–6 specialized IR models per degradation type through a five-stage agentic loop: Perception (fine-tuned DepictQA via LoRA assesses degradation severities), Scheduling (GPT-4 determines operation order using retrieved experiential knowledge), Execution (tools are applied sequentially with per-tool reflection), Reflection (DepictQA judges whether each subtask succeeded), and Rescheduling (on failure, the agent rolls back and replans, performing a depth-first tree search over operation sequences). A key technical contribution is a self-exploration and experience-summarization procedure: the agent exhaustively applies all tool-order permutations on a subset of images, collects success/failure statistics, and prompts GPT-4 to distill these into structured knowledge documents that are retrieved during inference to ground scheduling decisions. This addresses the demonstrated failure of LLMs to reliably order operations without concrete empirical evidence, reducing scheduling dispersion from near-random to near-deterministic.

## Results

On the MiO100-based test set with 16 mixed-degradation combinations, AgenticIR achieves the best or near-best performance across all six metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, MANIQA, CLIP-IQA, MUSIQ) in all three difficulty groups. In Group A (2 degradations, seen during exploration), AgenticIR reaches 21.04 PSNR and 0.6818 SSIM, outperforming the strongest all-in-one baseline MiOIR (20.84 PSNR, 0.6558 SSIM). In the more challenging Group C (3 degradations, unseen), AgenticIR attains 18.82 PSNR and 0.4493 LPIPS, surpassing the best baseline AutoDIR (18.61 PSNR, 0.5019 LPIPS) by a notable margin on perceptual quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgenticIR outperforms all six all-in-one baselines (AirNet, PromptIR, MiOIR, DA-CLIP, InstructIR, AutoDIR) on nearly every metric across all three degradation groups. The closest competitor is MiOIR, which leads AgenticIR by a negligible 0.01 dB in PSNR on Group B (20.56 vs. 20.55) but is clearly surpassed on SSIM (0.6905 vs. 0.7009), LPIPS (0.3243 vs. 0.3072), and all no-reference metrics. On the hardest Group C (3 unseen degradations), AgenticIR's advantage is most pronounced: it beats the second-best AutoDIR by 0.21 dB PSNR, 0.055 LPIPS, and 10.82 MUSIQ points, indicating that the agentic tool-orchestration strategy scales better than monolithic models as degradation complexity increases. The paper explicitly notes this comparison is meant to validate feasibility of tool ensembling rather than to claim universal superiority over all-in-one approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM/VLM agent paradigm (HuggingGPT, Visual ChatGPT, Reflexion) to the image restoration domain, proposing a five-stage agentic workflow (Perception, Scheduling, Execution, Reflection, Rescheduling) that dynamically orchestrates a toolbox of single-degradation IR models. It builds directly on DepictQA for VLM-based quality assessment (fine-tuned via LoRA) and departs from prior tool-ensemble approaches—Yu et al. (2018) using reinforcement learning and Chen et al. (2024a) using VLM fine-tuning for plan generation—by introducing a self-exploration and experience-summarization mechanism that compensates for LLMs' lack of IR-specific operational knowledge. The depth-first tree search with rollback positions the system as a deliberate 'System 2' reasoning agent rather than a step-by-step RL policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that composing specialized single-degradation tools under LLM/VLM orchestration can match or exceed all-in-one restoration models, validating a modular and extensible paradigm for complex IR. The self-exploration mechanism—where the agent accumulates empirical success rates and distills them into referenceable knowledge—is a practical and generalizable solution to the LLM knowledge-gap problem that could transfer to other tool-use domains. Key limitations include the restriction to single-degradation tools and synthetic degradations; the authors explicitly note that integrating heterogeneous, parameter-rich, or generative tools would require substantially higher decision-making flexibility, leaving the path to truly general visual processing agents open.
