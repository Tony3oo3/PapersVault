## Problem

Existing edge-level GNNs cannot simultaneously model orientation-equivariant signals (e.g., flow) and orientation-invariant signals (e.g., pipe diameter) on graphs containing both directed and undirected edges. Topological methods like HodgeGNN treat all signals as equivariant and all edges as undirected, while line-graph approaches ignore orientation entirely. No prior architecture distinguishes directed from undirected edges while provably handling both signal modalities with consistent predictions under arbitrary re-orientations.

## Input / Output

EIGN consumes two parallel edge-signal channels—orientation-equivariant (represented relative to a direction-consistent orientation where directed edges have fixed orientation matching their direction) and orientation-invariant—alongside a graph topology that may mix directed and undirected edges. Outputs follow the same dual-modality structure: equivariant predictions flip sign under re-orientation of undirected edges, while invariant predictions remain unchanged. The model is trained in a supervised setting on tasks ranging from flow simulation (predicting edge flows from topology and invariant features alone) to denoising and interpolation, with targets being either equivariant or invariant edge signals.

## Method

EIGN is a multi-layer GNN with parallel equivariant and invariant message-passing pathways, each using novel direction-aware Magnetic Edge Laplacians built from complex-valued boundary operators that encode edge direction via phase shifts of magnitude πq. The equivariant pathway uses B_equ^(q) (which re-orients misaligned adjacent edges and applies complex phases to directed edges), while the invariant pathway uses B_inv^(q) (which never re-orients but still encodes direction through phase shifts). Cross-modality information exchange is achieved through two mechanisms: (i) Laplacian fusion operators L_equ→inv and L_inv→equ that transform signals between modalities via mixed boundary operators, and (ii) a point-wise fusion step using element-wise multiplication (for equivariant outputs) and absolute value (for invariant outputs) to model same-edge interactions. The architecture provably satisfies joint orientation equivariance for equivariant outputs and joint orientation invariance for invariant outputs under arbitrary re-orientations of undirected edges, while remaining sensitive to flipping directed edges.

## Results

On the real-world flow simulation task (Table 5), EIGN achieves the lowest RMSE across all five datasets, with improvements of up to 23.5% over the best baseline (HODGE+DIR) on Winnipeg (0.101 vs. 0.132) and 8.4% on Circuits (0.696 vs. 0.760). On synthetic tasks (Table 4), EIGN dominates: it reaches 0.996 AUC-ROC on LD Cycles (vs. 0.799 for the best baseline) and 0.022 RMSE on Tri-Flow (vs. 0.293), demonstrating its ability to jointly model orientation-equivariant and -invariant signals on partially directed graphs. EIGN also outperforms all baselines on the denoising and interpolation tasks (reported in Appendix E).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EIGN outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and task. Against LINEGRAPH (Bandyopadhyay et al., 2020), EIGN achieves substantially lower RMSE on all real-world datasets (e.g., 0.078 vs. 0.109 on Chicago, 0.696 vs. 1.037 on Circuits) and higher AUC-ROC on synthetic tasks (0.864 vs. 0.758 on RW Comp), confirming that line-graph-based message passing lacks the inductive biases needed for orientation-equivariant signals. Against HODGEGNN (Roddenberry et al., 2021), EIGN is dramatically better on direction-sensitive tasks (0.996 vs. 0.500 AUC-ROC on LD Cycles) and on real-world data with directed edges (0.101 vs. 0.173 RMSE on Winnipeg), as HODGEGNN treats all edges as undirected and all signals as equivariant. Against LINE-MAGNET (Geisler et al., 2023), EIGN wins on all tasks, with the largest gap on LD Cycles (0.996 vs. 0.502 AUC-ROC) where the node-level transformer cannot capture edge-level directionality. Against Dir-GNN (Rossi et al., 2023), EIGN consistently achieves lower RMSE (e.g., 0.078 vs. 0.106 on Chicago, 0.696 vs. 1.029 on Circuits) and higher AUC-ROC (0.864 vs. 0.757 on RW Comp), showing that its principled separation of equivariant and invariant modalities provides stronger inductive biases than separate in/out-neighbor message passing.

## Relation to Prior Work

EIGN generalizes the Magnetic Node Laplacian (Zhang et al., 2021) from node-level to edge-level problems, using complex phase shifts to encode edge direction in boundary operators. It extends the equivariant edge Laplacian from HodgeGNN (Roddenberry et al., 2019/2021) by introducing a parallel invariant signal pathway built on the unsigned boundary operator (Bodnar et al., 2021), and by formally restricting equivariance/invariance requirements to undirected edges only. The key departure from prior topological methods is the joint treatment of both orientation-equivariant and orientation-invariant signals alongside directed and undirected edges—a combinatorial space that neither line-graph approaches nor existing Hodge-based architectures can represent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework with provable guarantees (Theorem 4.1) for the full combinatorial space of edge-level problems, which is practically important for traffic simulation, electrical circuit analysis, and hydraulic network modeling where both signal modalities and edge directions coexist. The ablations confirm that the inductive biases are not merely helpful but necessary: removing direction-awareness or the fusion operators causes dramatic performance drops, and prior methods like HodgeGNN perform at chance level on direction-sensitive tasks. Limitations include restriction to edge-level (no higher-order structures) and reliance on magnetic Laplacians specifically; the authors note that global propagation via the normal-matrix property of these Laplacians is a natural extension left for future work.
