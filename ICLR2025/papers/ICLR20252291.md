## Problem

Existing unsupervised time series anomaly detection methods (density-based, forecasting-based, reconstruction-based) produce high false-alarm rates on non-stationary data because they cannot distinguish genuine anomalies from normal statistical drift. Prior diffusion-based approaches like D³R operate at a single resolution and assume linear trends, failing to exploit multi-scale temporal correlations that could separate non-stationary behavior from true anomalies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252291/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{N×T} and internally generates R resolution scales via average pooling (pool sizes 2^r, r=1..R), padding each to length T. It outputs a per-timestep binary anomaly vector Y ∈ R^T, produced by an ensemble voting scheme that aggregates reconstruction errors across the last 10 diffusion steps at each of the R resolution scales, flagging a timestep as anomalous when the vote count exceeds a fixed threshold (ζ=10).

## Method

MODEM introduces a coarse-to-fine diffusion paradigm where the forward process sequentially corrupts data from lowest to highest resolution over R×K steps, with a lightweight transformer module injecting cross-scale correlations (between x₀^r and x₀^{r+1}) into the diffusion trajectory as prior guidance (Eq. 1–2). The reverse process is adapted to DDIM for deterministic accelerated sampling (R×L steps), where clean predictions at lower resolutions steer the denoising of higher-resolution data. A frequency-enhanced decomposable network parameterizes the denoiser: STFT splits the input into time-invariant (top-m% amplitude) and time-variant components, processed by separate encoders—stacked residual blocks with hierarchical transformers for global shared information, and Dilated ModernTCN Blocks with shared-parameter multi-scale convolutions for complex periodic dynamics. Training is fully unsupervised on normal data using a simplified x₀-prediction loss (Eq. 6) that avoids explicit cross-scale correlation computation at each step.

## Results

MODEM achieves an average F1-score of 93.46% across five real-world anomaly detection datasets (SMD, PSM, MSL, SWaT, SMAP), outperforming the best baseline ImDiffusion (91.35%) by 2.11 points. The largest gains appear on highly non-stationary datasets: SMD (96.01 vs. 94.50 for ImDiffusion) and SWaT (91.21 vs. 87.09). The only dataset where MODEM trails is MSL (89.77 vs. 91.15 for TranAD), which emphasizes inter-variable correlations. MODEM also achieves a competitive average Sequence Detection Delay of 109±9 timesteps, comparable to ImDiffusion (104±14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MODEM outperforms all 16 baselines on 4 out of 5 datasets, with the closest competitor being ImDiffusion (F1*=91.35), which MODEM surpasses by 2.11 points on average. The most notable margins are on SWaT (+4.12 over ImDiffusion) and SMD (+1.51), both characterized by high non-stationarity, confirming the multi-resolution design's effectiveness. On MSL, MODEM (89.77) is outperformed by TranAD (91.15), which the authors attribute to TranAD's stronger inter-variable correlation modeling better matching MSL's characteristics. Among generative-model baselines, DiffAD (90.79) and D3R (87.97) trail MODEM, while forecasting-based methods (LSTM-AD, GDN, MTAD-GAT) and density-based methods (IForest, MSCRED) are substantially weaker, with F1* scores below 66%. MODEM also achieves a competitive average ADD of 109±9 timesteps, slightly behind ImDiffusion (104±14) but far ahead of most other methods (e.g., GDN at 383±1, MSCRED at 365±76). Under affiliation-based metrics (avoiding point-adjustment inflation), MODEM leads on all three high-non-stationarity datasets with an average F1 of 84.20% vs. 80.34% for D3R, the next-best method.

## Relation to Prior Work

MODEM extends the diffusion-model-based anomaly detection line (ImDiffusion, DiffAD, D3R) by introducing a multi-resolution coarse-to-fine paradigm previously explored only in time series forecasting (MR-Diff, MG-TSD). It departs from D3R's moving-average decomposition by instead leveraging cross-resolution correlations learned via cross-attention to guide both forward and reverse diffusion trajectories, and it incorporates a frequency-domain decomposition (STFT) to separate time-invariant from time-variant components—a technique borrowed from spectral analysis literature but applied here within a diffusion denoising network. The denoising backbone combines hierarchical transformers (for inter-variable and temporal dependencies) with Dilated ModernTCN blocks (for multi-scale periodic patterns), drawing on both transformer and modern convolutional architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Non-stationarity remains a persistent practical challenge in real-world anomaly detection (server monitoring, spacecraft telemetry, water treatment), and MODEM provides a principled multi-scale framework that reduces false alarms from normal distributional shifts. The generalization experiments to forecasting and imputation suggest the multi-resolution diffusion paradigm is broadly applicable beyond anomaly detection. However, the method's reliance on ensemble voting across R×L diffusion steps adds inference complexity, and the modest ~2% F1 gain over ImDiffusion (and slight underperformance on MSL) indicates the approach is not a universal solution. Open questions include scalability to very long sequences, applicability to streaming/online settings, and whether the fixed 20% frequency threshold generalizes across domains with different spectral characteristics.
