## Problem

Existing embodied agents in open-world environments like Minecraft either rely on black-box RL (lacking interpretability) or on LLMs with rich prior knowledge and omniscient metadata (GPS, voxel blocks, biome names) that human players cannot observe. This makes them fragile when game rules change and misaligned with human gameplay. The paper asks whether an agent can learn a structured, interpretable causal model of the world from scratch through its own interactions, without any prior knowledge or omniscient data.

## Input / Output

The agent receives first-person screenshots (rendered via prismarine-viewer and described by LLaVA-v1.5-13B), its current inventory, and a task tuple of goal items plus environmental factors. Action names are deliberately hidden and replaced by letters to block prior-knowledge leakage; the agent starts with a single action (gatherWoodLog) and discovers new actions only after acquiring prerequisite items. The primary structured output is a growing causal graph (DAG over items) with near-perfect accuracy (SHD 2±2 vs. ≥30% errors for baselines), alongside the sequence of discrete actions and movements executed to complete the task.

## Method

ADAM's core innovation is a two-stage causal discovery pipeline driven by embodied interventions. First, LLM-based CD uses chain-of-thought prompting (with letter-mapped item names and few-shot examples independent of the technology tree) to propose causal assumptions from N parallel interaction records. Second, intervention-based CD verifies each assumed cause by actually removing that item from the inventory and re-executing the action: if the effect item consistently fails to appear, the edge is retained; otherwise it is pruned. Two optimizations—temporal modeling (eliminating edges that violate event ordering) and subgraph decomposition (processing one action's cause-effect pair at a time)—keep the CD tractable. A controller (LLM planner + actor + memory pool) then decomposes and executes tasks using the learned causal graph, while an MLLM perception module replaces omniscient metadata with visual descriptions, enabling lifelong bootstrapping as newly discovered items unlock further actions and CD cycles.

## Results

On the original Minecraft, ADAM (with parallel sampling) obtains diamonds in 34±7 steps with a 3/3 success rate, achieving a 2.2× speedup over the SOTA (VOYAGER Guided at 75±20 steps, 2/3 success). In the modified Minecraft where crafting recipes are altered, ADAM is the only method that successfully obtains diamonds (61±18 steps, 2/3) and iron tools (54±14 steps, 3/3), while all baselines fail entirely (0/3). On interpretability, ADAM constructs a causal graph with a Structural Hamming Distance (SHD) of 2±2 from the ground-truth technology tree, compared to 10±4 for CDHRL and 24±6–24±9 for non-embodied agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADAM decisively outperforms all three non-embodied baselines (ReAct, Reflexion, AutoGPT, all augmented with the paper's TM and SD optimizations) on every task beyond Stone Tool, as these methods cannot refine causal assumptions without built-in interventions and are confined to the lower levels of the technology tree. Against VOYAGER and VOYAGER Guided (the strongest embodied baselines), ADAM Parallel achieves a 2.2× speedup on the diamond task (34±7 vs. 75±20 steps) with a higher success rate (3/3 vs. 2/3), though VOYAGER Guided is faster on the simpler Wooden and Stone tool tasks (7±2 and 11±2 vs. 12±2 and 18±3 steps). In the modified Minecraft (Table 7), ADAM is the only method that reaches iron tools and diamonds, achieving a 4.6× speedup in obtaining raw_iron over the best baseline, while all prior-knowledge-dependent agents fail completely (0/3). On causal graph accuracy (Table 1), ADAM's SHD of 2±2 substantially outperforms CDHRL (10±4) and non-embodied agents (24±6 to 24±9), demonstrating that intervention-based CD is critical for near-perfect graph recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two well-studied but previously separate lines of work: LLM-based embodied agents in Minecraft (VOYAGER, GITM, JARVIS-1) and causal discovery (CDHRL, PC-style algorithms). Prior embodied agents rely heavily on the LLM's pre-trained prior knowledge of crafting recipes and on omniscient metadata (GPS, biome names, block types) invisible to human players, while prior causal discovery methods operate offline on tabular data without embodied interaction. ADAM departs from both by making causal discovery an online, intervention-driven process embedded within the agent's exploration loop, and by removing dependence on prior knowledge and omniscient metadata in favor of multimodal perception (LLaVA) and discrete action-level interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that an agent can bootstrap a near-complete, interpretable causal model of a complex environment from scratch without any pre-existing knowledge, and that this causal model transfers robustly when the environment's rules change (modified Minecraft). This challenges the prevailing assumption that LLM-based agents must lean on their training-time knowledge to succeed in structured domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to continuous action spaces or environments with hundreds of interacting variables, whether the GPT-4 dependency can be replaced by smaller models without losing the critical inference capability, and whether the discrete 41-action formulation generalizes to richer skill spaces. The reliance on a single game and a single frontier LLM limits the breadth of the evidence.
