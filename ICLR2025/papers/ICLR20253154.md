## Problem

Diffusion transformers (DiT, SiT) learn semantically meaningful but suboptimal internal representations during denoising, exhibiting a significant gap compared to state-of-the-art self-supervised encoders like DINOv2. This representation quality bottleneck limits both training efficiency and final generation quality, as the model must spend extensive training to develop adequate semantics before it can focus on high-frequency detail. The paper asks whether incorporating high-quality external visual representations can make diffusion transformer training substantially easier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253154/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion VAE, consuming a 32×32×4 compressed latent vector (corresponding to a 256×256 RGB image) corrupted by noise at a sampled timestep, along with a class label for conditional generation. During training, REPA additionally requires the clean (unnoised) image to be passed through a frozen pretrained visual encoder (e.g., DINOv2) to produce the target patch-level representation for alignment. At inference, the model generates a 256×256 RGB image by solving the reverse SDE/ODE from pure Gaussian noise, optionally with classifier-free guidance.

## Method

REPA introduces a simple patch-wise alignment regularization that distills pretrained self-supervised visual representations into the diffusion transformer's hidden states. A trainable MLP projection head maps the hidden state at a chosen early layer (e.g., layer 8 of 28) to match the clean-image patch features from a frozen external encoder (DINOv2, MAE, MoCov3, etc.), using either NT-Xent or negative cosine similarity as the similarity function. This alignment loss is added to the standard velocity-prediction or denoising objective with a small weight λ (typically 0.5). A key finding is that aligning only the first few layers is optimal: it provides strong semantic grounding early in the network while freeing later layers to capture high-frequency spatial details, yielding over 17.5× faster convergence (FID=7.9 at 400K steps vs. 7M for vanilla SiT-XL/2) and state-of-the-art FID=1.42 with classifier-free guidance and guidance interval scheduling.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with classifier-free guidance, SiT-XL/2 + REPA achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.42 (with guidance interval, w=1.80, interval [0,0.7]) and FID=1.80 (w=1.35) at 800 epochs, outperforming the vanilla SiT-XL/2 (FID=2.06 at 1400 epochs). Without CFG, REPA accelerates SiT-XL/2 training by over 17.5×, reaching FID=7.9 at 400K iterations—already surpassing the vanilla SiT-XL/2's FID=8.3 at 7M iterations. The method also improves linear probing accuracy from ~65% to ~70% and CKNNA alignment with DINOv2, confirming that better representation alignment translates to both stronger discrimination and generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REPA with guidance interval (FID=1.42) achieves the best FID among all compared methods, beating the closest competitor MDTv2-XL/2 (FID=1.58) by 0.16 and DiffiT (FID=1.73) by 0.31. Without the guidance interval, REPA at 800 epochs (FID=1.80) still outperforms SiT-XL/2 (FID=2.06, 1400 epochs), DiT-XL/2 (FID=2.27, 1400 epochs), MaskDiT (FID=2.28, 1600 epochs), U-ViT-H/2 (FID=2.29), VDM++ (FID=2.40), Simple diffusion (FID=2.77), SD-DiT (FID=3.23), LDM-4 (FID=3.60), ADM-U (FID=3.94), and CDM (FID=4.88). In terms of training efficiency, REPA matches the vanilla SiT-XL/2's 7M-iteration FID (8.3) in just 400K iterations (>17.5× speedup) and reaches FID=5.9 at 4M iterations. REPA also improves recall over vanilla SiT-XL/2 (0.65 vs. 0.59 with guidance interval) while maintaining comparable precision (0.80 vs. 0.82). The only metric where a baseline edges out REPA is precision: DiT-XL/2 achieves 0.83 vs. REPA's 0.80–0.81, though REPA compensates with substantially better FID and recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DiT and SiT diffusion transformer architectures, adding a simple regularization (REPA) that aligns intermediate hidden states with pretrained self-supervised visual encoders (primarily DINOv2). Conceptually, it extends the long-standing observation that denoising score matching implicitly learns representations (Bengio et al., 2013; Xiang et al., 2023) by arguing that the reconstruction-based objective is insufficient for learning strong representations, and that external self-supervised features can serve as a distillation target. It departs from prior knowledge-distillation approaches for diffusion models (RepFusion, DreamTeacher) by targeting the internal representation space rather than the output, and from hybrid discriminative-generative models (Yang et al., 2022; Tian et al., 2024) by keeping the generative architecture unchanged and adding only a lightweight projection head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 17.5× training speedup and the resulting SOTA FID of 1.42 on ImageNet 256×256 make this a practically transformative result for anyone training diffusion transformers, as it dramatically reduces compute costs while improving quality. The core insight—that representation learning, not generative modeling per se, is the bottleneck in diffusion transformer training—reframes how the community should think about scaling these models. Open questions include whether REPA transfers to text-to-image and video generation (the paper reports preliminary results in appendices), whether the benefit persists at much larger scales (e.g., 10B+ parameters), and whether the dependence on a frozen external encoder limits the ceiling of what the diffusion model can learn. The finding that only the first 8 layers need alignment suggests a clean separation between semantic understanding and high-frequency synthesis that may inform future architecture design.
