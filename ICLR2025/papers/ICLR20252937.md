## Problem

Despite the rapid growth of glycan data and individual ML studies on specific glycan properties, no comprehensive benchmark exists to systematically evaluate diverse model architectures across multiple glycan understanding tasks with realistic generalization splits. This gap hinders progress in glycan machine learning by making it impossible to compare sequence-based, graph-based, and all-atom approaches under consistent conditions, and leaves open how multi-task learning affects glycan property prediction.

## Input / Output

Glycans are consumed in two structural forms: tokenized IUPAC-condensed sequences (where each monosaccharide, glycosidic bond, and bracket is a separate token) and planar graphs with monosaccharide nodes and glycosidic-bond edges encoded as one-hot vectors. For protein-glycan interaction prediction, a protein sequence is additionally encoded via ESM-1b and concatenated with the glycan representation. Data splits are non-trivial: single-glycan tasks use motif-frequency-based K-means clustering (K=10, 8:1:1) to ensure test glycans contain unseen structural motifs, while interaction prediction splits by protein sequence similarity (MMseqs2, 50% identity threshold) to simulate generalization to novel lectins.

## Method

GLYCANML provides 11 benchmark tasks (8 taxonomy levels, immunogenicity, glycosylation type, interaction) evaluated with 18 baselines spanning four model families: sequence encoders (shallow CNN, ResNet, LSTM, Transformer), homogeneous GNNs (GCN, GAT, GIN, PNA), heterogeneous GNNs (MPNN, RGCN, CompGCN), and all-atom molecular encoders (Graphormer, GraphGPS, MolFormer, MolCLR, Uni-Mol+, plus pre-trained variants). A dedicated GLYCANML-MTL testbed jointly trains 8 taxonomy tasks under hard parameter sharing, comparing 8 MTL strategies including Nash Bargaining Solution, CAGrad, Temperature Scaling, and Uncertainty Weighting. The key empirical finding is that multi-relational GNNs (especially RGCN) dominate by explicitly modeling different glycosidic bond types as distinct edge relations, while a 2-layer shallow CNN achieves surprisingly competitive performance from condensed sequential information alone. For MTL, Nash Bargaining Solution yields the best mean Macro-F1 (4.28% relative improvement over single-task with CNN backbone), and jointly training taxonomy as auxiliary tasks boosts immunogenicity and glycosylation prediction but not interaction prediction.

## Results

On the GLYCANML benchmark (11 tasks), RGCN achieves the best weighted mean rank of 2.72, winning 6 out of 11 individual tasks including Kingdom taxonomy (Macro-F1 0.647), Species taxonomy (0.146), immunogenicity (AUPRC 0.780), and glycosylation type (Macro-F1 0.948). A 2-layer shallow CNN surprisingly ranks 5th overall (WMR 6.56), achieving competitive scores on 5 of 11 tasks. In the GLYCANML-MTL testbed, the Nash Bargaining Solution method achieves the best mean Macro-F1 of 0.373 with RGCN backbone (vs. 0.360 single-task) and 0.341 with Shallow CNN (vs. 0.327 single-task), a 4.28% relative improvement over single-task learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGCN (Schlichtkrull et al., 2018) dominates the benchmark, achieving the best weighted mean rank (2.72) and top scores on 6/11 tasks, clearly outperforming homogeneous GNNs (GCN WMR 11.09, GAT 9.72, GIN 7.59, PNA 11.09) by leveraging distinct glycosidic bond types as separate relations. CompGCN (Vashishth et al., 2019) ranks second (WMR 5.63), while MPNN (Gilmer et al., 2017) underperforms (WMR 11.97) despite also being heterogeneous. Among sequence encoders, the 2-layer Shallow CNN (WMR 6.56) and ResNet (WMR 5.91) are competitive, but LSTM (WMR 6.53) and Transformer (WMR 10.66) lag on fine-grained taxonomy tasks. All-atom molecular encoders (Graphormer, GraphGPS, MolFormer, MolCLR, Uni-Mol+) and their pre-trained variants (Graphormer* WMR 8.25, MolCLR* 8.78) consistently underperform glycan-specific models, with the best all-atom model (Graphormer*) still ranking 8th overall. For MTL, Nash Bargaining Solution (Navon et al., 2022) outperforms single-task learning by 4.28% relative Macro-F1 (Shallow CNN) and 3.6% (RGCN), while Temperature Scaling (Kendall et al., 2018) is the runner-up; most other MTL methods (GN, DWA, DTP, CAGrad) fail to beat single-task baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground as the first comprehensive benchmark for glycan machine learning, drawing conceptual inspiration from established biomolecule benchmarks like MoleculeNet (small molecules), TAPe/PEER (proteins), and Uni-RNA (nucleic acids). While individual glycan prediction tasks (taxonomy, immunogenicity, glycosylation type, protein-glycan interaction) have been studied in isolation by prior works (Bojar et al., Burkholz et al., Pakhrin et al., Lundstrøm et al.), no prior effort systematically compared diverse model families—sequence encoders, homogeneous/heterogeneous GNNs, and all-atom molecular encoders—under unified evaluation protocols. The MTL testbed (GLYCANML-MTL) repurposes standard multi-task learning algorithms (Nash, CAGrad, GN, TS, UW, DWA, DTP) in a novel glycan-specific context where eight hierarchical taxonomy tasks are jointly learned.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark provides the glycan ML community with a standardized, reproducible evaluation framework that was previously absent, enabling fair comparison of models and accelerating progress in glycoscience. The finding that small molecule encoders (designed for tens of atoms) fail on glycans (hundreds of atoms) highlights a fundamental scale gap and calls for purpose-built glycan architectures. A key open question is why most MTL methods degrade performance on the highly correlated taxonomy tasks—only Nash and TS consistently help—suggesting that the hierarchical structure of taxonomy may require task-specific MTL strategies beyond generic gradient balancing. The modest dataset sizes (13K glycans for taxonomy, 1.3K for immunogenicity) limit generalizability conclusions, and the authors note future work on applying these models to real-world vaccine design.
