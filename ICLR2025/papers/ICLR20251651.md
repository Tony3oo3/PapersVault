## Problem

Despite extensive research on weight initialization for ReLU networks, effective initialization methods for tanh neural networks remain underexplored. The standard Xavier initialization causes activation values to vanish toward zero in deep tanh networks, and its performance degrades as network size increases, with limited relief from Batch or Layer Normalization. This paper addresses the lack of a principled, size-robust initialization strategy specifically designed for tanh activations.

## Input / Output

The initialization method takes the network architecture (number of layers L, nodes per layer N_ℓ, which may vary across layers) and produces initial weight matrices W^ℓ ∈ ℝ^{N_ℓ × N_{ℓ-1}} for each layer. In classification experiments, the initialized tanh FFNNs consume standard images (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/FMNIST, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100) and output class probabilities. In PINN experiments, the network consumes spatial-temporal collocation points (e.g., 20,000 points) and outputs approximate PDE solutions at those points.

## Method

The method is grounded in a fixed-point analysis of the function tanh(ax): when a > 1, iterating x_{n+1} = tanh(ax_n) converges to a non-zero fixed point ξ_a, whereas when a ≤ 1 it converges to zero. The proposed initialization decomposes each weight matrix as W^ℓ = D^ℓ + Z^ℓ, where D^ℓ places 1s on a cyclic diagonal (D_{i,j} = 1 if i ≡ j mod N_{ℓ-1}) and Z^ℓ ~ N(0, σ_z²) with σ_z = α/√N_{ℓ-1} and α = 0.085. This construction ensures the effective per-neuron coefficient a_i^{k+1} in the tanh(a_i x_i) form has a Gaussian distribution centered at 1, keeping activations in a regime where they neither vanish (a ≤ 1) nor saturate (a ≫ 1) as depth increases. The value α = 0.085 was empirically chosen to maintain an approximately normal activation distribution in deep layers, avoiding both the collapse seen with very small σ_z and the saturation seen with very large σ_z.

## Results

On depth-robustness experiments (64 nodes, 3–100 hidden layers, 40 epochs), the proposed initialization consistently outperforms Xavier, with the gap widening at greater depths: on CIFAR-100 at 100 layers, the proposed method achieves 24.41% accuracy vs. Xavier's 20.32% (+4.09 pp), and on CIFAR-10 at 100 layers it reaches 48.96% vs. 46.71% (+2.25 pp). In data-efficiency experiments (10-layer, 64-node FFNN, 100 epochs), the proposed method leads all baselines at every sample size; with only 10 MNIST samples it attains 37.32% accuracy versus 31.13% (Xavier), 22.46% (Xavier+BN), and 28.52% (Xavier+LN). For PINNs (30 hidden layers, 32 nodes), the proposed initialization yields the lowest median loss across all four PDEs (Burgers, Allen-Cahn, Diffusion, Poisson) at every tested depth from 5 to 80 layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms plain Xavier initialization across all four classification datasets, with the advantage growing with depth: at 100 layers on CIFAR-100 the gap is +4.09 pp (24.41% vs. 20.32%), and on CIFAR-10 it is +2.25 pp (48.96% vs. 46.71%). Against Xavier+BN, the proposed method wins decisively in the data-efficiency setting (Table 3): with 100 MNIST samples it achieves 68.44% vs. 57.51% (+10.93 pp), and with 10 samples 37.32% vs. 22.46% (+14.86 pp). Against Xavier+LN the margin is smaller but consistent: 68.44% vs. 65.11% on MNIST (100 samples) and 67.58% vs. 62.01% on FMNIST (100 samples). In the PINN experiments (Table 4), the proposed initialization achieves the lowest median loss at every depth for all four PDEs, whereas Xavier+LN frequently diverges (e.g., Poisson at 80 layers: N/A for Xavier+LN vs. 6.01e-02 for the proposed method). In the non-uniform-width autoencoder experiments (Appendix B.2), the proposed tanh initialization also outperforms ReLU with He initialization + BN, confirming that the advantage is not merely an artifact of the tanh family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the variance-preserving initialization paradigm (Xavier for tanh, He for ReLU) by grounding weight initialization in the fixed-point dynamics of tanh(ax). While prior work on deep ReLU initialization (Lu et al., 2019; Lee et al., 2024; Bachlechner et al., 2021) has addressed dying ReLU and signal propagation, the authors note that analogous robustness guarantees for tanh networks remain largely absent, with Xavier serving as the de facto standard in PINNs and RNNs. The proposed diagonal-plus-noise construction is conceptually distinct from both Xavier and He: rather than matching input/output variance, it ensures the effective per-neuron gain a_i^k stays near 1 so that activations converge to non-zero fixed points rather than vanishing. The method also positions itself as an alternative to normalization layers (BN, LN), which are commonly bolted onto Xavier-initialized tanh networks to mitigate depth-related degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tanh networks remain the default in PINNs and RNNs, where initialization sensitivity and the need for normalization add significant practical overhead; a principled initialization that is robust across widths and depths without normalization could simplify training pipelines and reduce hyperparameter search. The data-efficiency results (outperforming Xavier+BN/LN with as few as 10–100 samples) are practically relevant for PINN settings where collocation points are expensive. Limitations include the empirical determination of α=0.085, the theoretical analysis assuming uniform layer dimensions (though non-uniform cases are tested), and evaluation restricted to FFNNs despite the RNN motivation. Open questions include whether the fixed-point framework generalizes to other saturating activations (sigmoid, ELU) and whether the method extends to recurrent or convolutional architectures.
