## Problem

Existing anti-backdoor learning methods can prevent models from predicting the target label on backdoored samples but fail to recover the original correct labels for those samples, and their multi-stage, non-end-to-end training pipelines are computationally prohibitive for large pre-trained models. This paper asks how to train a clean model end-to-end on a poisoned dataset that both maintains high clean accuracy and recovers the true labels of backdoored images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253382/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes an RGB image (32×32×3 for CIFAR-10, 224×224×3 for ImageNet-subset) alongside its 2D DCT frequency spectrum; during inference, the actual frequency spectrum is replaced by a clean one (the average DCT of a few clean validation images, or the DCT of a single randomly chosen clean image). The output is a class label for classification tasks or a generated text answer for the VQA task with BLIP. Only one clean image is needed at inference time, making the defense practically deployable.

## Method

The core insight is a causal analysis showing that a backdoor attack acts as a confounder A opening a spurious path I←A→Y, and that the backdoor adjustment theorem E[Y|I=do(i)]=E_A[E[Y|i,A]] implies conditioning on both the image and an attack indicator eliminates confounding bias. The MCCI architecture pairs a Semantic Feature Recognition Network (SFRN, e.g. ViT or ResNet-18) that encodes the image into semantic embeddings with a small Attack Indication Network (AIN, a 6-layer CNN) that encodes the DCT frequency spectrum into an attack-presence embedding; the two embeddings are concatenated and passed through a classification head. Training is a single end-to-end supervised pass on the poisoned dataset with cross-entropy loss. At inference, counterfactual outcome estimation is performed by feeding a fake clean frequency spectrum to the AIN, which 'mind-controls' the model into perceiving every input as clean and thereby recovering the original ground-truth label for backdoored samples.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (average across 6 attacks), MCCI-SG achieves 84.70% Clean Accuracy (CA), 3.10% Attack Success Rate (ASR), and 81.36% Attack Recovery Rate (ARR), while MCCI-AVG achieves 83.98% CA, 3.37% ASR, and 81.20% ARR. On ImageNet-subset, MCCI-AVG achieves 70.18% CA, 2.31% ASR, and 65.28% ARR. The ARR metric (recovering poisoned samples to their original correct labels) is the paper's key differentiator: MCCI's ARR is nearly equivalent to its CA, whereas baselines like CBD drop to 47.96% ARR on CIFAR-10 and 36.23% on ImageNet-subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCCI decisively outperforms CBD (Zhang et al., 2023) on all three metrics: on CIFAR-10, MCCI-SG achieves 84.70% CA vs. CBD's 76.73%, 3.10% ASR vs. 30.49%, and 81.36% ARR vs. 47.96%; the gap is even wider on ImageNet-subset where CBD drops to 55.89% CA and 36.23% ARR. Against DBD (Huang et al., 2022), MCCI-SG matches CA on CIFAR-10 (84.70% vs. 84.70%) but substantially improves ARR (81.36% vs. 78.67%) and ASR (3.10% vs. 4.73%); on ImageNet-subset, MCCI-AVG leads on both CA (70.18% vs. 69.19%) and ASR (2.31% vs. 5.20%). Against ASD (Gao et al., 2023), MCCI trades a slightly higher ASR (3.10% vs. 2.10% on CIFAR-10) for a dramatically better ARR (81.36% vs. 63.10%), and on ImageNet-subset MCCI-AVG dominates on all three metrics (70.18% CA, 2.31% ASR, 65.28% ARR vs. 62.62%, 7.27%, 48.81%). The paper's central finding is that while prior methods can suppress ASR, they fail to recover the original correct labels of poisoned samples (low ARR), whereas MCCI's causal-inference-based design achieves near-perfect recovery without sacrificing clean accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCCI reframes anti-backdoor learning through Pearl's backdoor adjustment theorem, treating the attack as a confounder that opens a spurious path I←A→Y, and the defense as conditioning on both the image and an attack indicator to recover the causal effect E[Y|I=do(i)]. For the attack indicator, it repurposes the frequency-spectrum (DCT) representation from Zeng et al. (2021), wrapping it in a small Attack Indication Network (AIN) that is concatenated with the victim model's (SFRN) semantic embedding. This departs from prior multi-stage anti-backdoor methods (ABL, CBD, DBD, ASD) by collapsing isolation, unlearning, and retraining into a single end-to-end optimization loop, and by introducing a counterfactual inference step where a fake clean spectrum 'mind-controls' the model into treating poisoned inputs as clean.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a practical gap by showing that a clean model can recover the original ground-truth labels of poisoned samples (ARR ≈ CA), not merely avoid the target label, and does so with 24–215% less training time than multi-stage baselines while extending naturally to ViT, CLIP, and BLIP. However, the defense's reliance on high-frequency trigger signatures is a significant vulnerability: a bilevel-optimized smooth trigger drives ASR back to 99.34%, and even a simple low-pass blur degrades ARR to ~64–76%. Open questions include whether more general input-level detectors can replace the DCT indicator, how the approach transfers to NLP, and whether the AIN/SFRN capacity asymmetry can be made robust to adaptive adversaries.
