## Problem

PLM-based retrievers exhibit source bias by ranking LLM-generated documents higher than semantically equivalent human-written ones, but the underlying causal mechanism remains unexplored. This paper identifies and quantifies the specific causal pathway: document perplexity acts as a non-causal feature that retrievers inadvertently learn, systematically inflating relevance scores for low-perplexity (i.e., LLM-generated) documents.

## Input / Output

The CDC debiasing method takes as input a small estimation set of 128 query-document pairs (each with both a human-written and LLM-rewritten version) to estimate the bias coefficient β₂ via two-stage IV regression, then at inference time consumes the standard retriever's relevance score and the document's BERT perplexity to produce a calibrated score R̃ = R̂ − β₂·P_d. The output is a debiased relevance score suitable for document ranking, with the correction applied only to the top-10 candidates to balance debiasing strength against ranking performance.

## Method

The paper constructs a causal graph where document perplexity P_d is a treatment on estimated relevance score R̂, with document semantics M_d as a confounder, and uses document source S_d as an instrumental variable in a two-stage least squares regression to isolate the causal effect of perplexity. Theoretically, Theorem 1 proves that under representation collinearity, semi-orthogonal decoder weights, and encoder-decoder cooperation, the retrieval loss gradient is a positive element-wise multiple of the MLM loss gradient, explaining why retrievers inherit perplexity sensitivity from pre-training. The proposed CDC method is an inference-time correction: it estimates β₂ on a small labeled set using 2SLS, then subtracts β₂·P_d from each document's relevance score at test time, requiring no retraining and generalizing across domains (DL19, TREC-COVID, SCIDOCS) and LLMs (Llama-2, GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Mistral).

## Results

CDC reduces source bias (Relative Δ NDCG@3) by 10–40+ points across all six PLM-based retrievers on DL19, TREC-COVID, and SCIDOCS, often reversing the preference from LLM-generated to human-written documents (e.g., BERT on DL19: −23.68 → +5.90; RoBERTa on SCIDOCS: −30.90 → +32.13). Ranking performance (NDCG@3) degrades by less than 2 percentage points in most settings, with a few exceptions (e.g., BERT on TREC-COVID drops from 53.72 to 45.88). The estimated bias coefficient β̂₂ generalizes across out-of-domain datasets and across LLMs (Llama-2, GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Mistral) on SciFact, confirming the method's practical transferability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper applies CDC to six PLM-based retrievers (BERT, RoBERTa, ANCE, TAS-B, Contriever, coCondenser) and compares each model's raw output against its CDC-debiased output. CDC is most effective on simpler architectures: BERT and RoBERTa show the largest bias reversals (e.g., RoBERTa on SCIDOCS shifts from −30.90 to +32.13), while stronger contrastive-learning-based retrievers like Contriever and coCondenser show smaller but still significant bias reductions (e.g., Contriever on DL19: −21.93 → −5.33). On TREC-COVID, where raw bias is most severe (TAS-B at −73.36, coCondenser at −67.95), CDC still halves the bias magnitude but cannot fully eliminate it. Performance costs are minimal for most models (≤2 NDCG@3 points), though BERT on TREC-COVID suffers a larger drop (53.72 → 45.88). The authors note that stronger retrievers trained with sophisticated contrastive learning are harder to debias, suggesting model-specific calibration of β̂₂ could improve results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Dai et al. (2024a,c), who first identified source bias in PLM-based retrievers and established the Cocktail benchmark, and on Mitchell et al. (2023) and Bao et al. (2023), who showed perplexity is a key discriminator between LLM-generated and human-written text. While Ohi et al. (2024) noted a correlation between perplexity and evaluation bias in LLM-as-judge settings, this paper is the first to establish a *causal* effect of perplexity on relevance scores using instrumental variable regression, and the first to provide a theoretical mechanism (positive gradient correlation between MLM and retrieval losses) explaining why this effect arises. The debiasing method CDC repurposes the causal graph structure for inference-time score correction, departing from prior work that only identified the bias without offering a principled fix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AIGC proliferates, source bias threatens to collapse the incentive structure for human content creation, making a mechanistic understanding and practical mitigation urgent. The gradient-correlation explanation unifies several previously unexplained observations (why all PLM retrievers are affected, why better retrievers exhibit more bias) and suggests the problem is inherent to the MLM-pretraining + retrieval-finetuning paradigm rather than a fixable data artifact. Key limitations include the restriction to encoder-only mean-pooling architectures, reliance on simplifying assumptions in the theoretical proof (semi-orthogonal decoder, collinearity), and the open normative question of whether debiasing toward human-written content is always desirable (Goodhart's Law tension). Future work should extend the analysis to CLS-based and autoregressive retrievers and explore whether training-time interventions can eliminate the gradient overlap without sacrificing retrieval quality.
