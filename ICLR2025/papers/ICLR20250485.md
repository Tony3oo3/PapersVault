## Problem

In Federated Domain Adaptation (FDA), clients hold data from unique domains but share a common category space, causing conventional averaging-based aggregation (e.g., FedAvg) to produce suboptimal global models due to divergent gradient directions across heterogeneous feature spaces. This undermines both in-domain accuracy (each client's own domain) and out-of-domain accuracy (generalization to other clients' domains), a trade-off that existing personalized FL methods handle poorly by sacrificing one for the other.

## Input / Output

Each client provides RGB images from its own domain (e.g., Real, Painting, Sketch, Quickdraw, Clipart in DomainNet; Photo, Paint, Sketch, Cartoon in PACS) through a frozen pretrained CLIP image encoder (ViT-B-32 for DomainNet, ConvNeXT-Base for PACS). The method outputs a lightweight global adapter that maps CLIP image embeddings to class labels (up to 345 categories in DomainNet, 7 in PACS), with the classification head derived from CLIP's text encoder via prompt-based label embeddings. Notably, only a small proportion of pretrained embeddings (prototypes) are transmitted per client rather than full model updates, and the entire global training completes in a single communication round.

## Method

MPFT replaces parameter averaging with prototype-based centralized fine-tuning: each client generates domain-specific class prototypes (via mean, cluster, or random sampling of frozen CLIP embeddings) and uploads them to the server, which then trains a global adapter on the aggregated prototype set using cross-entropy loss, effectively simulating centralized training without raw data transfer. A convergence theorem guarantees monotonic loss decrease under smooth non-convex objectives with bounded prototype divergence, and the method converges in one communication round. An optional few-shot local adaptation phase lets clients fine-tune the adapter on a small local subset, regularized by knowledge distillation (KL divergence to the global adapter's outputs) to prevent catastrophic forgetting of cross-domain knowledge. Differential privacy is applied by adding Gaussian noise to prototypes before transmission, and a feature-space hijacking attack experiment confirms that original images remain unrecoverable even after one million gradient descent iterations.

## Results

On DomainNet Subset-50, MPFT (Random, rate=0.3) achieves 82.4% out-of-domain accuracy and 82.9% in-domain accuracy in a single communication round, outperforming the best averaging-based baselines (FedAvg/MOON at 79.0% ood, 73.4% ind) by over 3 percentage points on both metrics. On PACS, MPFT reaches 98.9% ood and 99.2% ind accuracy in 1 round, surpassing FedAvg (97.3% ood, 98.9% ind, 32 rounds) and MOON (97.6% ood, 98.9% ind, 42 rounds). All MPFT variants converge in exactly 1 global communication round, compared to 5–49 rounds required by baselines, reducing total training time from hundreds of seconds to under 50 seconds (e.g., 454.5s vs. 1933.5s for FedAvg on DomainNet-50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPFT (Random, rate=0.3) outperforms all six baselines on both ood and ind accuracy across all four dataset configurations. On DomainNet Subset-50, it beats FedAvg and MOON (the strongest averaging-based methods) by +3.3% ood and +9.5% ind, while also surpassing the personalized methods Ditto (+4.3% ood, +6.7% ind) and DBE (+8.2% ood, +6.7% ind). FedProto, which also uses prototypes but relies on averaging them, is the weakest baseline (73.0% ood, 77.0% ind on DN-50), confirming that prototype averaging distorts the global distribution. On PACS, MPFT achieves 98.9% ood vs. 97.3% for FedAvg and 97.6% for MOON, while Ditto edges out MPFT by 0.1% on ind accuracy (99.3% vs. 99.2%) but trails by 7.2% on ood. Critically, MPFT requires only 1 communication round versus 5–49 rounds for all baselines, reducing total training time by 4–7× (e.g., 454.5s vs. 1933.5s for FedAvg on DN-50) and communication volume by 3–40× (30.9MB vs. 144MB for FedAvg).

## Relation to Prior Work

MPFT departs from the dominant averaging-based aggregation paradigm in federated learning (FedAvg, FedProx, MOON, Ditto) and from personalized FL methods (DBE, FedProto, FedRep) that still rely on parameter or prototype averaging. Rather than treating client models or prototypes as objects to be averaged, MPFT repurposes domain-specific prototypes as a surrogate training dataset for centralized fine-tuning of a global adapter on the server, effectively simulating centralized learning without raw data transfer. The framework builds on CLIP pretrained encoders (ViT-B-32, ConvNeXT-Base) and adapter-based fine-tuning, while borrowing differential privacy mechanisms from DBE and knowledge distillation from standard KD literature for its local adaptation phase.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-communication-round convergence with competitive in-domain and out-of-domain accuracy offers a compelling efficiency-accuracy trade-off for FDA deployments where multi-round FL is impractical. The explicit formalization of domain knowledge preservation vs. adaptation with dedicated evaluation metrics (ind acc, ood acc) provides a clearer optimization target than prior work. Key limitations include dependence on pretrained encoder quality, vulnerability to membership inference attacks beyond the demonstrated feature-space hijacking resistance, and the assumption that prototypes sufficiently capture domain-specific feature distributions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-vision modalities, whether the one-round guarantee holds under more extreme domain shifts, and how to defend against statistical inference attacks on prototype distributions.
