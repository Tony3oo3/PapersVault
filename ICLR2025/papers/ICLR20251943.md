## Problem

Existing static dataset pruning methods for Knowledge Distillation tend to preserve hard samples near the decision boundary, but the student network's limited capacity causes the decision boundary to drift into other classes when learning these hard samples. This paper identifies that medium-difficulty samples constitute a smoothed decision boundary that is both easier for the student to learn and more generalizable, and proposes a logit reshaping technique to mitigate the distributional shift introduced by pruning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard image classification dataset (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224) with ground-truth labels and a pre-trained teacher network downloaded from an open-source repository (no retraining or proxy network required). It produces a pruned training subset of medium-difficulty samples selected in a class-balanced manner, a c-dimensional vector of class-wise logit standard deviations (negligible storage overhead), and a distilled student network trained with reshaped teacher logits. The teacher's cross-entropy loss on each sample serves as the difficulty score, and the reshaping coefficient k_p is computed once over the full dataset before pruning.

## Method

The method scores all training samples by the pre-trained teacher's cross-entropy loss, ranks them, and selects the middle portion (medium-difficulty samples) rather than the hardest or easiest. The key insight is that hard samples cause the student's decision boundary to drift into other classes due to the capacity gap, while easy samples produce boundaries too small to cover the feature distribution; medium samples yield a smoothed, discriminative boundary. To counteract the distributional shift from discarding a large fraction of samples, the method computes the average logit standard deviation per class over the full dataset and rescales each preserved sample's teacher logits by the ratio of its class's global standard deviation to its own (p̃ = p·k_p/STD(p)), effectively aligning individual sample logits with the class-level distribution. The student is then trained with a standard KD loss (temperature-scaled KL divergence plus cross-entropy) using these reshaped logits, requiring no architectural changes to the student or teacher.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet34-MobileNet, the proposed method achieves 70.92% top-1 accuracy at 70% keep ratio, surpassing the full-dataset KD baseline (70.72%) while using only 70% of training data. At 50% keep ratio, the method halves KD training time while improving accuracy by 0.41% over the student trained without distillation. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet50-MobileNetV2, Medium+Reshape achieves 66.18% top-1 accuracy at 70% keep ratio, outperforming all static pruning baselines by 1.5–2.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all static pruning baselines (Random, Herding, EL2N, MoDS) across all keep ratios on both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet. On ImageNet at 70% keep ratio, it surpasses the strongest static baseline EL2N by 0.35% (ResNet34-MobileNet) and 0.77% (ResNet50-MobileNet), and beats MoDS by 0.99% and 1.18% respectively. Notably, the method also outperforms the dynamic pruning method InfoBatch (which requires access to the full dataset) by 0.45% and 0.47% at 70% keep ratio on the two ImageNet pairs, despite being a static method. Against self-distillation methods (Zipf's LS, USKD) on ImageNet at 70% keep ratio, the proposed method achieves 70.92% vs. 69.59% and 70.38% while also reducing training time from >39.86h to 36.98h. The logit reshaping component provides an additional 0.4–0.6% improvement over medium-sample selection alone on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of static dataset pruning (Herding, Forgetting, EL2N, MoDS) and knowledge distillation, departing from the standard assumption that hard samples should be preserved because they are closest to the decision boundary. It builds on MoDS's observation that medium-difficulty samples yield smoother boundaries, but reframes the selection criterion specifically around the student-teacher capacity gap in KD, where hard samples cause decision boundary drift in the student's feature space. The logit reshaping component is distinct from existing logit alignment methods (SKD, NormKD, LSKD) that focus on student-teacher scale matching; instead, it aligns preserved teacher logits with class-wise global distribution statistics to counteract pruning-induced bias. The paper also positions itself against dynamic pruning (InfoBatch) by showing a static method that never accesses the full dataset during training can match or exceed it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners running KD at scale: halving training time at 50% keep ratio while maintaining accuracy, and achieving lossless performance at 70% on ImageNet, directly addresses the I/O bottleneck of large datasets on resource-constrained hardware. The decision boundary drift diagnosis provides a useful conceptual tool for understanding why standard pruning heuristics fail in KD, potentially guiding future work on sample selection for other capacity-constrained learning settings. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification with CNN backbones, reliance on a pre-trained teacher for scoring, and the logit reshaping being a relatively simple heuristic whose optimality is not formally characterized. Open questions include whether the medium-sample principle extends to non-vision modalities, very small keep ratios below 30%, and whether the reshaping can be made adaptive per-epoch rather than fixed.
