## Problem

Defining meaningful divergences on discrete spaces (e.g., sets of vectors) is nontrivial: classical set-operation metrics become uninformative for large ground sets, and the submodular-Bregman divergence of Iyer & Bilmes (2012b) is restricted to submodular generating functions with unclear identifiability guarantees. This paper removes the submodularity restriction via difference-of-submodular (DS) decomposition and introduces a learnable, permutation-invariant neural network formulation that significantly outperforms fixed submodular-Bregman divergences on clustering and retrieval tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unordered sets of D-dimensional vectors (e.g., 500-point 3D point clouds from ModelNet40 or sets of 3–4 MNIST images) and outputs a scalar divergence value between two such sets. Because the input is a set, the architecture must be permutation-invariant; the divergence is then plugged into downstream tasks such as k-means clustering (producing cluster assignments) or top-K set retrieval (producing a ranked list of similar sets). The divergence is guaranteed non-negative and zero iff the two sets are identical, by construction.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that any set function f can be decomposed as f = f¹ − f² with f¹, f² monotone increasing and strictly submodular (strong DS decomposition), and that strict subgradients of f at Y can be constructed as h_Y = h_Y¹ − g_Y² from strict semidifferentials of the components. The divergence is then D_f(X,Y) = D_{f¹}(X,Y) + D^{f²}(X,Y), combining a lower-bound submodular-Bregman term (using a strict subgradient of f¹) and an upper-bound term (using a strict supergradient of f²). For the learnable realization, both f¹ and f² are parameterized by ε-PointNet architectures (log-sum-exp relaxation of max-pooling with an MLP feature map φ and identity aggregation γ), which guarantee submodularity when φ outputs are non-negative. Training uses triplet loss over anchor/positive/negative set triples with the Adam optimizer, and the extreme-point subgradient (via greedy algorithm) and grow/shrink/bar supergradients are used for the semidifferential evaluations.

## Results

On the ModelNet40 set clustering task, the grow-DBD with DS decomposition achieves a Rand index of 0.8015 (ε=0.001), dramatically outperforming classical set-operation metrics (e.g., |X\Y|+|Y\X| at 0.0225). On the set retrieval task, the shrink-DBD with DS decomposition achieves 90.20 mAP, surpassing Densepoint (89.68 mAP) but remaining below the SOTA MVTN (92.9 mAP). The DS decomposition consistently improves over the single-submodular ablation (w/o decomposition) across all supergradient variants and both ε settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical set-operation metrics from Iyer & Bilmes (2012b) (e.g., |X\Y|+|Y\X|, 1−|X∪Y|/|Y|), the learnable DBD achieves a Rand index of ~0.80 versus ~0.023, a roughly 35× improvement, demonstrating that the NN-learned divergence captures structural similarity that simple set operations completely miss on point-cloud data. On set retrieval (Table 4), the shrink-DBD with DS decomposition (90.20 mAP) outperforms Densepoint (Liu et al., 2019) at 89.68 mAP by about 0.5 points, confirming the DBD's competitive retrieval quality. However, the DBD falls short of MVTN (Hamdi et al., 2021) at 92.9 mAP, a gap of ~2.7 points, though the authors note MVTN uses a more complex multi-view architecture. Among the three supergradient choices, grow and shrink supergradients perform comparably (within ~0.1 Rand index), while the bar supergradient is consistently inferior (~0.05 lower), attributed to its independence from the local structure around Y.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the submodular-Bregman divergence framework of Iyer & Bilmes (2012b) by leveraging the strong difference-of-submodular (DS) decomposition of Li & Du (2020) to define Bregman-type divergences from arbitrary set functions, not just submodular ones. It also formalizes the identifiability condition (strict submodularity) that was implicit but unproven in the original work. For the learnable instantiation, the paper adapts the PointNet architecture (Qi et al., 2017) into an ε-PointNet with log-sum-exp to guarantee submodularity, filling a gap the authors note: no prior work learns Bregman divergences over discrete sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded framework for constructing and learning divergences on discrete spaces, which is a natural but underexplored setting for metric learning. However, the practical impact is currently limited: the experiments use a simple two-layer MLP as the set function, the clustering baselines are very weak classical set-operation metrics, and the set retrieval results approach but do not surpass the SOTA (MVTN). Open questions include whether richer permutation-invariant architectures (Set Transformer, Perceiver) or better hyperparameter tuning could close the remaining gap, and whether the exponential complexity of computing DS decompositions in general becomes a bottleneck at scale.
