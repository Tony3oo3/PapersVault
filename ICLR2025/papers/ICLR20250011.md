## Problem

Existing instruction-based image editing models suffer from three critical limitations: (1) biased synthetic data generation from a single method (e.g., Prompt-to-Prompt or inpainting) results in imbalanced editing skills across tasks, (2) quality filtering via CLIP-score has poor correlation with actual data quality, and (3) all models are trained on fixed-resolution, square images, limiting real-world applicability. This paper addresses all three gaps to build a truly generalist editing model.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a source RGB image at any aspect ratio (1:1, 2:3, 3:2, 3:4, 4:3, 9:16, 16:9) with a minimum resolution of 1 megapixel, paired with a free-form text editing instruction specifying one of seven task types (object swap, removal, addition, attribute modification, background swap, environment change, or style transfer). It outputs a single edited image at the same resolution and aspect ratio as the input, preserving unedited regions while applying the requested semantic transformation.

## Method

OMNI-EDIT is built on Stable Diffusion 3 Medium (24 DiT layers) with a novel EditNet architecture that replicates each DiT block as a control branch, enabling intermediate representation interaction between the control and original branches while also updating text token representations—addressing ControlNet's inability to comprehend editing intent like 'removal.' Training data is generated by seven task-specific specialist models (inpainting models for object tasks, Prompt-to-Prompt for attribute/environment edits, CosXL-Edit for style transfer), then filtered via importance sampling using a GPT-4o-distilled InternVL2 scorer that assigns binary weights (score ≥ 9 retained), yielding 1.2M high-quality pairs from ~5M candidates. The generalist model is trained with a weighted diffusion objective where each sample's loss is scaled by its importance weight, and the training set mixes multiple aspect ratios and high resolutions to ensure robustness across diverse real-world inputs.

## Results

On OMNI-EDIT-Bench, OMNI-EDIT achieves the highest scores across all evaluation metrics. Under VIEScore (GPT-4o), it reaches PQ 8.38, SC 6.66, and overall O 6.98, outperforming the best baseline CosXL-Edit (PQ 8.34, SC 5.81, O 6.00) by +0.85 in SC and +0.98 in O. In human evaluation, OMNI-EDIT attains PQ 0.83, SC 0.71, O 0.69, and edit accuracy 0.55, compared to CosXL-Edit's 0.82, 0.56, 0.59, and 0.35 respectively. The largest gains are in semantic consistency (instruction following), where OMNI-EDIT leads the best baseline by +0.15 (human) and +0.85 (GPT-4o VIEScore).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMNI-EDIT outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. The closest competitor is CosXL-Edit, which leads in PQ under Gemini (7.01 vs. 7.06, a marginal gap) but trails OMNI-EDIT substantially in SC (4.90 vs. 5.82) and overall score (4.81 vs. 5.78) under Gemini, and in SC (5.81 vs. 6.66) and O (6.00 vs. 6.98) under GPT-4o. In human evaluation, OMNI-EDIT's edit accuracy of 0.55 nearly doubles CosXL-Edit's 0.35, and its SC of 0.71 exceeds CosXL-Edit's 0.56 by 0.15. Inversion-based methods (DiffEdit, SDEdit) and earlier end-to-end models (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, HIVE) are consistently far behind, with O scores below 4.0 under GPT-4o VIEScore. The paper attributes OMNI-EDIT's advantage in SC to the specialist-to-generalist supervision, which provides task-specific training signals rather than a single biased synthesis pipeline, and to the EditNet architecture's ability to update text representations for correct task intent (e.g., recognizing 'remove' vs. 'add').

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the end-to-end instruction-based image editing line initiated by InstructPix2Pix and advanced by MagicBrush, UltraEdit, CosXL-Edit, and HIVE. It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) replacing a single synthetic data pipeline (P2P, DALLE-2, or SD-Inpaint) with an ensemble of seven task-specific specialist models to ensure balanced editing skills; (2) substituting CLIP-score filtering with LMM-based importance sampling (GPT-4o distilled into InternVL2), building on the VIEScore evaluation framework; and (3) introducing EditNet, a DiT-based architecture that generalizes ControlNet by enabling intermediate-representation interaction and text-branch updates, addressing the representational degradation observed with InstructPix2Pix's channel-wise concatenation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The specialist-to-generalist framework and LMM-based data curation offer a reusable recipe for building balanced editing models, and the OMNI-EDIT-Bench (multi-aspect-ratio, multi-task, high-resolution) fills a gap in evaluation infrastructure that prior benchmarks (MagicBrush, UltraEdit) did not address. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that output quality is bottlenecked by the SD3 base model; they explicitly note that swapping to Flux or stronger backbones could unlock further gains. Open questions include whether the specialist ensemble can be further expanded (e.g., to video editing or 3D), whether the LMM scoring threshold (≥9/10) is optimal, and whether EditNet's intermediate-representation interaction generalizes to other conditional generation tasks beyond editing.
