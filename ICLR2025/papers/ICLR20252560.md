## Problem

MLFF foundation models trained on large, diverse datasets approach DFT-level accuracy but remain prohibitively slow for realistic downstream applications like long-timescale MD simulations. Meanwhile, practitioners typically need fast, specialized models for narrow chemical subsets that also obey physical constraints (e.g., energy conservation) at test time, creating a tension between train-time scalability and test-time physical soundness. No prior method existed to transfer the general-purpose representations of an FM into a smaller, faster, physically-consistent student specialized to a specific region of chemical space.

## Input / Output

The student MLFF takes atomic positions (n×3) and atomic numbers (n) as input and outputs a scalar potential energy and per-atom forces (n×3), identical in form to the teacher FM. The distillation procedure additionally consumes precomputed 3N×3N energy Hessian matrices from the teacher over a specialized data subset, but at training time only s randomly sampled rows of the Hessian are used per iteration (s as low as 1), making the supervision signal sparse yet sufficient. For direct-force student architectures, the Hessian is computed as the negative Jacobian of the force output rather than the second derivative of the energy head.

## Method

The paper introduces Hessian distillation, an architecture-agnostic knowledge distillation objective that trains a small student MLFF to match the energy Hessian (second derivative of energy w.r.t. atomic positions) of a large teacher FM over a specialized chemical subset. The teacher's Hessians are precomputed once via autodifferentiation and stored; during student training, only s uniformly sampled Hessian rows are supervised per iteration, with individual rows extracted efficiently via vector-Jacobian products (VJPs) to avoid forming the full 3N×3N matrix. The total loss combines the standard energy/force regression objective with a weighted Hessian-alignment term, and a dynamic scheduler halves the KD weight once the student's validation loss surpasses the frozen teacher's. For direct-force student models, an additional energy-gradient supervision term (aligning −∇_r U_θ with true forces) is added at no inference-time cost. The approach is demonstrated across four FMs (MACE-OFF, MACE-MP-0, JMP-S/L, EScAIP) and multiple student architectures (GemNet-dT, GemNet-T, PaiNN, eSCN), achieving up to 20× inference speedups while matching or exceeding FM accuracy and improving MD stability and energy conservation.

## Results

On the SPICE Monomers split, Hessian distillation of MACE-OFF into a 0.67M-parameter GemNet-dT student achieves 6.3 meV/Å Force MAE (vs. 6.6 for the 4.7M-parameter FM, 11.3 undistilled, 10.5 n2n, 12.9 a2a) while running 4.3× faster at constant batch and 19.0× faster at throughput-maximizing batch. Across all three SPICE chemical subsets (Monomers, Solvated Amino Acids, Systems with Iodine), the Hessian-distilled students consistently outperform the FM and all baselines on Force MAE, with speedups up to 20.9× (PaiNN on Solvated Amino Acids) and up to 48× at maximum throughput. On MPtrj materials subsets and MD22 large molecules, the same pattern holds: distilled students are up to 10× and 30× faster respectively, with Force MAE improvements of 2–3× over undistilled training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the n2n baseline (Kelvinius et al., 2023), Hessian distillation wins on Force MAE across every chemical subset and student architecture: e.g., on Solvated Amino Acids with PaiNN, Hessian achieves 18.0 vs. n2n's 38.3 meV/Å, and on Monomers with GemNet-dT, 6.3 vs. 10.5. The a2a (atom-embedding projection) baseline is even less competitive, often performing worse than undistilled training (e.g., 12.9 vs. 11.3 on Monomers/GemNet-dT). Against the MACE-OFF FM itself (Kovács et al., 2023), the distilled students frequently match or exceed the teacher's Force MAE (6.3 vs. 6.6 on Monomers; 18.0 vs. 19.4 on Solvated Amino Acids) while being 4–21× faster. On MPtrj (Batatia et al., 2023), Hessian distillation consistently outperforms undistilled training (e.g., 16.9 vs. 15.7 for GemNet-dT on Pm3m, where the undistilled student already beats the FM's 18.1), suggesting a regularizing effect. On MD22 (Shoghi et al., 2023), distilling from the 220M-parameter JMP-L into a 0.67M GemNet-dT student yields 10.6 meV/Å Force MAE vs. 14.3 undistilled and 11.8 for the FM, with 30× speedup; the GemNet-T student with conservative forces also conserves energy over 100 ps NVE simulations while JMP-L drifts. Distilling from EScAIP (Qu & Krishnapriyan, 2024) further improves student performance over MACE-OFF distillation (e.g., 4.8 vs. 5.1 meV/Å on Monomers/GemNet-T) and eliminates the occasional energy-MAE degradation seen with MACE-OFF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation framework (Hinton et al., 2015) and Sobolev training (Czarnecki et al., 2017, which used first-order derivatives as supervision) to second-order energy Hessian alignment for MLFF specialization. It departs from the prior MLFF KD work of Kelvinius et al. (2023), which aligned internal node features and required architectural compatibility, by using the energy Hessian as an architecture-agnostic distillation signal that can be precomputed and applied to any student-teacher pairing. The paper also builds on the recent wave of MLFF foundation models (MACE-OFF, MACE-MP-0, JMP, EScAIP) trained on large diverse datasets, positioning Hessian distillation as a mechanism to extract fast, specialized 'engines' from these general-purpose models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× inference speedup while matching or exceeding FM accuracy on specialized chemical subsets addresses a critical bottleneck for practical MD simulations and geometry optimization, where FMs are currently prohibitively expensive. The demonstration that distilling from a direct-force teacher into a conservative-force student preserves energy conservation in NVE simulations while leveraging the teacher's representations opens a recipe for combining scalable, minimally-constrained FM training with task-specific physical inductive biases. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even larger systems (beyond ~370 atoms), whether Hessian-based KD can specialize along axes other than chemical composition (e.g., temperature, pressure), and whether the observed student-outperforms-teacher effect reflects a fundamental limitation of current FM training recipes or is an artifact of the small specialized subsets used here.
