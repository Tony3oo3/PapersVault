## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks rely on isolated single-turn QA pairs tied to individual clips, failing to evaluate whether LVLMs can sustain temporally sequential multi-turn reasoning across the full duration of a streaming video. No prior benchmark constructs dialogue chains with explicit temporal linkages between successive video segments, which is essential for assessing real-world streaming scenarios where users ask contextually connected questions over time without access to future content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251525/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives video segments sequentially at 1 FPS, each split by scene detection (5–15 scenes per video, 1-second overlap between consecutive clips) and accompanied by a growing dialogue history of prior QA pairs. In streaming evaluation, when a question has a temporal linkage to a subsequent QA chain, the model jumps to that related question with 80% probability, simulating non-linear temporal reasoning across segments. Outputs are free-text answers scored across six LLM-based dimensions (Semantic Accuracy, Contextual Coherence, Logical Consistency, Temporal Understanding, Informational Completeness, Overall Score) and nine skill-specific categories ranging from Intention Inference to Spatio-Temporal Speculation.

## Method

SVBench is constructed via a semi-automated pipeline: GPT-4o generates 5–6 consecutive QA pairs per video clip (QA chains), GPT-4 identifies temporal linkages between adjacent chains across six relationship types (Action, Quantity, Person, Object, Event, Environment), and human annotators manually modify subsequent-chain questions to create cross-clip reasoning dependencies. The benchmark contains 1,353 videos and 49,979 QA pairs (avg. 36.94 per video) from six streaming platforms, split into training (42,605 QA) and evaluation (7,374 QA) sets. For the StreamingChat model, the authors fine-tune InternVL2 (InternViT vision encoder + MLP projector + InternLM2 LLM) with LoRA on the SVBench training data, using a static-resolution strategy to fit several minutes of video within a 32k context window, with video segments and multi-turn dialogues interleaved in a sequential format that mirrors real-time streaming consumption.

## Results

On SVBench, GPT-4o achieves the highest Overall Score of 66.29 (dialogue) and 58.17 (streaming), while the proposed StreamingChat leads all open-source models with 59.41 (dialogue) and 53.90 (streaming). StreamingChat improves over its base model InternVL2 by 28.79% in dialogue evaluation and 26.20% in streaming evaluation. Across the 9 streaming video understanding skills, nearly all models score below 60, with Spatio-Temporal Speculation (STS) being the weakest skill (GPT-4o: 44.58, StreamingChat: 37.99) and Potentiality Assessment (PA) the strongest (StreamingChat: 71.96, GPT-4o: 59.47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamingChat outperforms all open-source LVLMs on SVBench, achieving 59.41 OS in dialogue evaluation vs. MiniCPM-V 2.6 (54.30), InternLM-XComposer2.5 (52.31), and VILA (48.51), and 53.90 in streaming evaluation vs. MiniCPM-V 2.6 (52.19) and InternLM-XComposer2.5 (51.46). Compared to its base model InternVL2 (46.13 dialogue / 42.71 streaming), StreamingChat gains +13.28 and +11.19 OS points respectively. Among closed-source models, GPT-4o (66.29/58.17) and GPT-4V (65.19/57.35) still lead, but StreamingChat surpasses both on three of nine skills (PA: 71.96 vs. 59.47/61.57; CST: 59.26 vs. 56.63/58.57; ECA: 47.68 vs. 47.47/47.44). On conventional benchmarks (MMBench, MMBench-Video, VideoMME, MVBench), fine-tuning to StreamingChat causes only slight decreases on MMBench and MMBench-Video while yielding modest gains on VideoMME and MVBench, confirming that streaming-specific training does not degrade general video understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the InternVL2 architecture (InternViT + MLP projector + InternLM2) as the backbone for its StreamingChat model, applying LoRA fine-tuning on the proposed SVBench training data. Conceptually, it departs from existing video QA benchmarks (NExT-QA, MVBench, LVBench, MovieChat) that evaluate single-turn, isolated questions on short clips, and from streaming video understanding work (VideoLLM-Online, Flash-VStream) that processes video sequentially but without multi-turn temporal reasoning. The key conceptual contribution is the formalization of 'temporal multi-turn dialogue' with explicit temporal linkages between QA chains across consecutive video segments, a task formulation absent from prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVBench is the first benchmark to systematically evaluate long-context streaming video understanding through temporal multi-turn dialogues, and its real-time leaderboard positions it as a likely standard reference for the emerging streaming video understanding subfield. The finding that even GPT-4o achieves only ~58–66 overall scores (vs. ~80–84 for humans) quantifies a substantial gap that motivates future research. Open questions include whether the 80% probabilistic jump mechanism in streaming evaluation adequately captures real-time interaction dynamics, how the benchmark scales to truly unbounded video lengths beyond the 32k context window, and whether the LLM-based evaluation (GPT-4 scoring) introduces circularity despite the human validation study.
