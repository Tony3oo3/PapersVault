## Problem

Existing auto-regressive mesh generation methods are limited to fewer than 1,600 faces at 128³ vertex resolution due to inefficient tokenization (lossy VQ-VAEs or zero-compression), and they struggle to generalize when conditioned on single-view images. The paper addresses these three gaps—face count, surface resolution, and conditioning generalization—simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251284/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a point cloud (surface-sampled coordinates only, no normals) or a single-view RGB image as input, and produces a 3D triangular mesh with up to 4,000 faces at 512³ vertex quantization. A bucketed face-count token (≤1000, 1000–2000, 2000–4000, >4000) provides coarse control over output complexity. The generated mesh has consistent face orientation within each sub-sequence, enabling correct back-face culling without post-processing.

## Method

EdgeRunner introduces a modified EdgeBreaker tokenization that traverses triangles via half-edges, achieving ~50% compression (4–5 tokens/face vs. 9) while eliminating long-range dependencies by duplicating vertices at sub-sequence boundaries and using only three face-type tokens (B, N, P). The core ArAE architecture pairs a lightweight cross-attention encoder (8,192 points → 2048×64 latent code) with a 24-layer OPT auto-regressive decoder (~0.7B params) trained with cross-entropy loss plus L2 latent regularization. Because the latent code is fixed-length, a separate 24-layer DiT (~0.5B params) can be trained with DDPM and min-SNR weighting to map CLIP image features into this latent space, decoupling the difficult image-conditioning problem from the auto-regressive decoding. This two-stage design—simple point-cloud-conditioned ArAE for the latent space, then diffusion for complex conditioning—yields substantially better generalization than directly prepending image tokens to the auto-regressive decoder.

## Results

In the user study (Table 1), EdgeRunner achieves 4.83/5 on input consistency, 4.54/5 on triangle aesthetic, and 4.58/5 on overall quality, substantially outperforming MeshAnything (2.93/2.43/2.43) and MeshAnythingV2 (2.64/2.36/2.14). On reconstruction quality (Table 2), EdgeRunner achieves the best Chamfer Distance (0.034 on AI-Mesh, 0.036 on Artist-Mesh) and Hausdorff Distance (0.079 on AI-Mesh, 0.112 on Artist-Mesh), roughly halving the error of both baselines. The model generates meshes with up to 4,000 faces at 512³ vertex resolution, running at ~100 tokens/sec on an A100 GPU (≈45 s for 1,000 faces, ≈3 min for 4,000 faces).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EdgeRunner outperforms both MeshAnything and MeshAnythingV2 across every evaluated metric. In the user study, EdgeRunner scores roughly 2 points higher than MeshAnything and 2.2 points higher than MeshAnythingV2 on overall quality (4.58 vs. 2.43 and 2.14). On reconstruction quality, EdgeRunner achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.034 on AI-Mesh, less than half that of MeshAnything (0.072) and MeshAnythingV2 (0.081), and a Hausdorff Distance of 0.079 versus 0.170 and 0.241 respectively. Notably, EdgeRunner uses only point coordinates as encoder input, whereas MeshAnything additionally leverages surface normals, yet still achieves superior results. For image-conditioned generation, EdgeRunner is compared qualitatively against Unique3D (an optimization-based method); EdgeRunner produces artistic meshes that better capture input semantics and generalizes to 2D-style and realistic-lighting images despite being trained only on simple-shaded renders. MeshAnythingV2 performs particularly poorly on the AI-Mesh dataset, often failing to generate complete surfaces, while EdgeRunner maintains stability on these challenging cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the auto-regressive mesh generation line initiated by MeshGPT and MeshAnything, which tokenized meshes via VQ-VAE or zero-compression and used transformer decoders to predict token sequences. The key departure is a modified EdgeBreaker-based tokenization that achieves 50% compression with reduced long-range dependencies, and the introduction of an Auto-regressive Auto-encoder (ArAE) that compresses variable-length mesh sequences into a fixed-length latent space. This latent space then enables training a DiT-based latent diffusion model (following PixArt-α's architecture) for image-conditioned generation, borrowing the cross-attention encoder design from 3DShape2VecSet for point-cloud encoding. The paper thus bridges two previously separate paradigms—auto-regressive sequence modeling and latent diffusion—within a unified pipeline for artistic mesh generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EdgeRunner pushes auto-regressive mesh generation to 4,000 faces at 512³ resolution, roughly doubling the face count and quadrupling the resolution of prior methods, making it practical for generating artist-quality meshes rather than low-poly approximations. The ArAE architecture—using an auto-regressive decoder as the 'decoder' of an auto-encoder whose latent space feeds a diffusion model—offers a general recipe for handling variable-length structured outputs in generative models. However, the paper acknowledges that success rates degrade with sequence length, the compression ratio remains insufficient for complex game-character meshes, and precise topology control from a single input remains an open challenge. The latent diffusion approach for image conditioning also raises the question of whether this two-stage design can be replaced by a more direct end-to-end formulation as model capacity grows.
