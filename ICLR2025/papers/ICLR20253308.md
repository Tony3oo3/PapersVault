## Problem

Serving thousands of LoRA-based customized models simultaneously incurs prohibitive GPU memory costs (e.g., 3.36 TB for 10,000 users on a 70B model). Existing parameter-sharing methods (VeRA, Tied LoRA, PRoLoRA) each address only inter-layer or intra-layer sharing and suffer from limited capacity, dimension constraints, or restricted efficiency potential, while the fundamental principles governing when sharing helps versus hurts performance remain unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253308/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (LLaMA2-7B/13B, LLaMA3.2-3B) and instruction-tuning datasets (SuperNI, Flan V2, CoT, CodeAlpaca) as input, and produces a fine-tuned model that generates text responses. Unusually, the per-layer low-rank matrices are not directly trainable parameters but are constructed at runtime by indexing into shared global shard pools, meaning the actual trainable parameters are the pool vectors plus fixed index matrices, yielding roughly 8× fewer trainable parameters than standard LoRA at equivalent performance.

## Method

MoS maintains a global pool of vector shards for each linear layer type, shared across all Transformer blocks (inter-layer) and within each layer (intra-layer), and uses a MoE-like index-based routing mechanism to select a designated number of shards that are concatenated to form the low-rank matrices A and B. Four nearly cost-free differentiation strategies counteract the performance degradation of pure sharing: subset selection (boolean masking of vector pairs per block), pair dissociation (separating A and B vectors into independent pools with ordered indices to boost combinatorial diversity), vector sharding (splitting vectors into smaller shards for finer-grained recombination), and shard privatization (partitioning the pool into public and private segments for exclusive per-block access). The key insight is that parameter efficiency requires a careful balance: sharing reduces parameter count, but differentiation is indispensable to prevent the detrimental effects of excessive sharing, with pair dissociation and shard privatization contributing most significantly to performance.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B with a fixed 5.00M trainable parameter budget, MoS (rank 4/8) achieves an average score of 36.39/37.63 across MMLU, BBH, GSM8K, TyDi QA, and HumanEval, surpassing LoRA (rank 2, 34.98), Tied LoRA (rank 280, 35.26), and PRoLoRA (rank 4/8, 36.03). In a performance-targeted setting, MoS at 19.99M parameters matches LoRA at rank 64 (159.91M parameters, avg 37.53), demonstrating approximately 8× parameter savings. On LLaMA2-13B, MoS (rank 4/8, 5.00M) achieves an average of 45.98, outperforming LoRA (43.92) and PRoLoRA (45.04) at the same parameter budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the 5.00M parameter budget, MoS outperforms all four baselines on average and on every individual benchmark: it beats LoRA (rank 2) by +1.41 avg points, Tied LoRA (rank 280) by +1.13, PRoLoRA (rank 4/8) by +0.36, and VeRA (rank 256) by +2.39. The most notable margin is over LoRA on GSM8K (+2.15 EM) and BBH (+1.07 EM), where the higher effective rank from global sharing provides clear gains. In the performance-targeted comparison, MoS at 19.99M parameters (rank 16/32) matches LoRA at rank 64 (159.91M parameters) on four of six metrics, confirming the ~8× parameter reduction claim. On LLaMA2-13B, MoS extends its lead over PRoLoRA by +0.94 avg points and over LoRA by +2.06 at the same 5.00M budget. VeRA, despite using only 1.42M parameters, underperforms all other methods and could not be scaled to match the 5.00M budget due to OOM, indicating a very low performance-to-rank ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the parameter-sharing line of work in LoRA-based PEFT: VeRA and Tied LoRA share matrices across Transformer blocks (inter-layer), while PRoLoRA reuses submatrices within a single layer (intra-layer). MoS departs from all three by treating fine-grained vector shards (rather than full vector pairs) as the atomic unit, pooling them globally across both inter- and intra-layer dimensions, and selecting subsets via a MoE-like index-based routing mechanism. A key conceptual departure is the explicit identification of a sharing–differentiation trade-off: the paper shows empirically that pure sharing degrades performance and that four nearly cost-free differentiation strategies (subset selection, pair dissociation, vector sharding, shard privatization) are essential to recover and exceed LoRA-level quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated ~8× reduction in trainable parameters (matching LoRA rank-64 performance with only rank-8-equivalent parameters) directly addresses the GPU-memory bottleneck when serving thousands of user-specific LoRA adapters simultaneously, a growing concern as LLM providers scale customization. The sharing-versus-differentiation principle offers a reusable design guideline for future parameter-sharing methods beyond LoRA. Limitations include slightly higher training-time GPU memory (due to the inflated effective rank) and the need for index-based (rather than activation-based) routing to avoid inference latency, which constrains the routing to be static. Open questions include whether the MoE-like selection can be made learnable or task-adaptive, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-task or multi-tenant LoRA serving scenarios.
