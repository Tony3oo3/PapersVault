## Problem

Existing RLHF models assume a specific linear reward structure and only receive feedback at episode end, failing to capture neuroscience-backed partially-observed 'internal states' (e.g., a human's evolving emotional state while reading) that shape feedback, nor do they accommodate intermediate snippet-level feedback. This paper asks how to generalize RLHF to incorporate both internal states and intermediate feedback, and what algorithms can achieve sublinear regret without the exponential blowup of naive history-summarization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250816/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on an episodic MDP where at each step the agent observes a state s_h and takes action a_h, but rewards r_h(s_h, u_h, a_h) depend on unobserved internal states u_h that evolve via a history-dependent generator w_h. Feedback o_h is received only at a subset of timesteps H_p (size p) and is a stochastic function of the reward (e.g., Bernoulli), not the reward itself. The output is a history-dependent policy π(τ[h-1], s_h) with explicit regret and sample complexity bounds; for dueling feedback, the agent runs two trajectories in parallel and receives a preference signal over the pair.

## Method

The paper introduces the PORMDP model, which augments a standard MDP with unobserved internal reward-states and a set of feedback timesteps, then provides three algorithmic contributions. First, two model-based optimistic algorithms (POR-UCRL using confidence sets, POR-UCBVI using trajectory-dependent bonuses) that separately learn the Markovian transition kernel and the non-Markovian decoder-induced reward functions, achieving Õ((poly(H,S,A) + p√(d_E·d_C))√T) regret—avoiding the (SA)^Ω(H) dependence of naive history-summarization. Second, a model-free analysis of GOLF using a new α-history-aware Bellman-eluder (HABE) dimension that captures recursive structure in internal states, yielding polynomial regret on the combination-lock example where both the standard BE dimension and the model-based guarantees are exponential. Third, a white-box reduction from dueling to cardinal PORRL that restricts policy selection to candidates that could be optimal under the confidence set, proving that the naive black-box reduction always incurs linear dueling regret.

## Results

The paper provides theoretical regret and sample complexity guarantees rather than empirical results. POR-UCRL achieves cardinal regret Õ((pS√(HA) + Σ√(d_{E,h}·d_{C,h}))√T) and sample complexity Õ(p²HS²A/ε² + p²d_E·d_C/ε²). GOLF with the new α-HABE dimension achieves regret Õ(pH√(d_HABE·d_C·T)), which can be polynomial (e.g., A for the combination lock) when the standard Bellman-eluder dimension is exponential (A^H). The dueling-to-cardinal reduction yields dueling regret Õ((pS√(HA) + Σ√(d_{E,h}·d_{C,h}))√T) via Algorithm 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's comparisons are entirely theoretical. Against naive history-summarization (treating the full trajectory as state), POR-UCRL's transition-estimation term is poly(H,S,A) rather than (SA)^Ω(H), an exponential improvement. Against Chatterji et al. (2021) in the linear RLHF setting (U = φ(τ)ᵀw, H_p = {H}), both POR-UCRL and POR-UCBVI improve the regret guarantee since d_{E,H} = d_{C,H} = d. Against Wang et al. (2023b), which uses dueling feedback with P-OMLE achieving Õ(H²S²A/ε² + H²d_{E,H}d_{C,H}/ε²) dueling oracle queries, POR-UCRL and POR-UCBVI achieve smaller cardinal-feedback sample complexity. For the combination lock example, GOLF with α-HABE dimension A is exponentially better than both the standard GOLF guarantee (BE dimension ≥ A^H − 2) and the model-based POR-UCRL/POR-UCBVI guarantees (eluder dimension ≥ A^h), but only under dense intermediate feedback (H_p = [H]); under sparse feedback (H_p = {H}), all methods face Ω(√(A^H·T)) regret.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces PORMDPs as a unifying framework that subsumes and extends prior RLHF models (Chatterji et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2023b; Saha et al., 2023) by incorporating partially-observed internal reward states and intermediate feedback, going beyond the linear reward models assumed in existing work. The model-based algorithms POR-UCRL and POR-UCBVI directly adapt the confidence-set and bonus-based optimism frameworks of UCRL (Jaksch et al., 2010) and UCBVI (Azar et al., 2017) to handle non-Markovian rewards with Markovian transitions. The model-free analysis builds on GOLF (Jin et al., 2021) but introduces a new history-aware Bellman-eluder dimension (HABE) to capture recursive structure in internal states, and the dueling-to-cardinal reduction generalizes the specific algorithm of Pacchiano et al. (2021) into a generic template applicable to any optimistic cardinal PORRL algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical framework showing that internal states in human feedback are not merely a modeling convenience but fundamentally change the statistical complexity of RLHF, with naive approaches incurring exponential regret. The key practical insight—that model-free methods with shared Q-function representations can be exponentially more sample-efficient than model-based methods when internal states have recursive structure—directly informs architecture choices for LLM alignment (e.g., using a single critic network across timesteps). Open questions include the computational tractability of the optimistic policy search over history-dependent policies, whether the theoretical gains from HABE translate to practical LLM training, and how to handle the overcomplete POMDP structure that PORMDPs exhibit. The dueling-to-cardinal reduction, while theoretically clean, requires well-designed confidence sets and may be hard to instantiate in practice.
