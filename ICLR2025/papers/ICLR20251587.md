## Problem

Understanding how PDE parameters induce qualitative behavioral changes (bifurcations) is a central challenge in the physical and life sciences, but existing data-driven methods either focus on fine-grained spatial reconstruction rather than low-dimensional effective dynamics, or learn effective dynamics without any parametric structure. This leaves no tool to extract parametrically interpretable reduced-order models from noisy, limited PDE data and use them to predict bifurcation locations in unseen regions of parameter space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatiotemporal PDE solutions u(x,y,t) sampled on a 64×64 square domain with periodic boundaries, along with known system parameters θ, and produces a low-dimensional effective trajectory a(t) consisting of the top d (typically 2, 5, or 10) most active scattering coefficients. From this learned effective dynamics, one can extract bifurcation locations via numerical continuation and decode pattern class labels (e.g., spots vs. stripes) from the steady state. The scattering transform is lossy by design, so the reduced representation is not a full reconstruction but a compact, interpretable summary of multiscale spatial structure.

## Method

TRENDy's key insight is that for any Fréchet-differentiable measurement operator Φ, the image a(t)=Φ[u](t) satisfies an ODE ȧ=g(a;θ) sharing the same parameter space as the original PDE. The operator Φ is fixed to the scattering transform—a cascade of Morlet wavelet band-pass filters interleaved with modulus nonlinearities and low-pass averaging—which provides stable, multiscale, interpretable features that strongly regularize the NODE's phase space. The unknown effective dynamics g is approximated by a 4-layer MLP (64 hidden units, ReLU) whose input is the concatenation [a:θ], trained via supervised regression against true effective trajectories with a loss combining state and derivative terms. Because the scattering features are hardwired rather than learned, the approach avoids costly self-supervised feature learning and achieves significantly better noise robustness than competing methods like SINDyCP, which degrades sharply under boundary or patch noise.

## Results

On the Gray Scott Turing bifurcation task, TRENDy with 10 scattering coefficients (T10) estimates the critical parameter k* = 0.0624 (true value: 0.062), outperforming SINDyCP (k* = 0.0651) and degrading gracefully under boundary and patch noise, whereas SINDyCP degrades sharply. On the Brusselator Hopf bifurcation task, TRENDy achieves a forecasting MSE of 4.34×10⁻⁴ in the best condition (no noise, holdout ε=0.15), rising to 1.05×10⁻² under the most challenging condition (patches, ε=1.0). On the ocellated lizard development data, an SVM trained on TRENDy's 10-dimensional final state achieves 95% accuracy and 95% weighted F1 in classifying anatomical quadrant labels, demonstrating that the learned effective dynamics encode spatial geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SINDyCP (Nicolaou et al., 2023), a sparse regression method for fitting parametric PDEs. On the Gray Scott bifurcation detection task, TRENDy T10 estimates k* = 0.0624 (error: 0.0004 from true 0.062), while SINDyCP estimates k* = 0.0651 (error: 0.0031) in the noiseless condition. The critical difference emerges under noise: TRENDy degrades gracefully across boundary and patch corruption conditions, whereas SINDyCP's performance drops sharply, making it unreliable in realistic noisy settings. TRENDy also outperforms simpler feature baselines (spatial gradient, Fourier vector) on the pattern classification subtask, where T10 achieves the highest F1 score in decoding four pattern classes (dense spots, homogeneity, sparse spots, stripes) from the learned effective steady state. The spatial gradient feature can detect general heterogeneity but fails to distinguish between different patterning regimes, confirming that TRENDy's multiscale scattering features carry substantially more discriminative information.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRENDy synthesizes two previously separate lines of work: the effective dynamics framework of Vlachas et al. (2022), which used multiscale features and RNNs to model reduced-order PDE behavior but without any parametric connection to the underlying system, and the parametric PDE fitting approach of SINDyCP (Nicolaou et al., 2023), which learned explicit governing equations but operated in the full observable space rather than a reduced effective space. The key architectural departure is the use of the scattering transform (Mallat, 2012) as a fixed, interpretable measurement operator that maps PDE solutions to low-dimensional trajectories, combined with a Neural ODE (Chen et al., 2018) whose dynamics explicitly depend on the known PDE parameters. This positions TRENDy as the first framework to learn a parametric, low-dimensional effective dynamics model whose phase space is both interpretable and directly connected to the original PDE's parameter space, enabling bifurcation prediction without fine-grained reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to predict qualitative behavioral changes (bifurcations) from limited, noisy spatiotemporal data is critical for understanding pattern formation in biology and physics, where governing equations are often unknown and experimental data is sparse. TRENDy's graceful degradation under noise—where SINDyCP fails sharply—makes it practically relevant for real-world settings like developmental biology. Key limitations include its current restriction to equilibrium dynamics for real data (oscillatory systems remain challenging), the fixed (non-learned) nature of scattering coefficients which trades expressiveness for interpretability, and the lack of explicit noise modeling. Open questions include whether learned measurements with reconstruction losses could improve performance, and whether the framework extends to genuinely stochastic or high-frequency oscillatory data.
