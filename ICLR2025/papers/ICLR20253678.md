## Problem

Continual personalization of text-to-image diffusion models requires sequentially acquiring new user-defined concepts while retaining previously learned ones, without access to prior task data (replay-free setting). The state-of-the-art C-LoRA suffers from a degenerate solution where its self-regularization penalty drives all LoRA weights toward zero, causing catastrophic forgetting of early tasks and loss of plasticity on later ones. The paper identifies that the class-conditional density estimates (diffusion classifier scores) inherent in pretrained diffusion models remain underexploited as a regularization signal for this problem.

## Input / Output

For each sequential task, the method takes a text prompt containing a learned custom concept token (e.g., 'a photo of [V*]') and 4–20 reference images of that single concept. The backbone is Stable Diffusion v1.4 or v2.0 with LoRA adapters (rank 16) on the cross-attention key/value projection layers of the U-Net. Output is 512×512 photorealistic images generated via 50-step DDPM sampling. A single consolidated LoRA per task handles inference for all seen concepts and sequentially initializes the next task's LoRA, incurring zero additional storage or parameter overhead over C-LoRA.

## Method

The paper proposes two consolidation phases that exploit diffusion classifier (DC) scores—softmax-normalized denoising losses over a subset of class-conditional prompts—as regularization priors. For parameter-space consolidation, it replaces C-LoRA's self-regularization with Elastic Weight Consolidation in LoRA space, where DC scores are incorporated into the loss used to compute the Fisher Information Matrix, yielding more informative parameter-importance estimates (Eq. 5). For function-space consolidation, it adapts Deep Model Consolidation into Diffusion Scores Consolidation (DSC): a student LoRA (initialized from the current task LoRA) is distilled to match both the DC score distributions and noise-prediction outputs of two teacher LoRAs—the current task and a randomly sampled previous task—over the full timestep range. DC scores are computed efficiently over a fixed-size subset of k=5 concepts (current task, a shared prior concept, and k−2 randomly sampled previous concepts) rather than all seen concepts, keeping the forward-pass cost constant regardless of task sequence length.

## Results

On the Custom Concept setup (6 tasks), the best variant DSC EWC DC achieves KID of 140.18×10⁵, A_MMD of 94.1×10³, BwT_MMD of −92.44×10³, and CLIP I2I of 73.17×100, outperforming C-LoRA (KID 173.8, A_MMD 117.2, BwT_MMD −107.47) across all generation-quality and forgetting metrics. On the more challenging Landmarks setup (10 tasks), DSC EWC DC further reduces KID to 51.22×10⁵ and A_MMD to 36.09×10³, with BwT_MMD of −3.85×10³ (vs. C-LoRA's −16.47). All proposed variants require only 0.09% trainable parameters and 100.54% storage overhead relative to the U-Net, matching C-LoRA's parameter budget exactly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against C-LoRA (the state-of-the-art continual personalization baseline), DSC EWC DC improves KID by 19.5% (140.18 vs. 173.8), A_MMD by 20% (94.1 vs. 117.2), and BwT_MMD by 14.9% (−92.44 vs. −107.47, where higher is better), while also boosting CLIP I2I from 64.89 to 73.17. Against Custom Diffusion (CD), the gap is even larger: DSC EWC DC reduces KID by 22% and A_MMD by 23%, while CD suffers severe forgetting (BwT_MMD = −273.41 vs. −92.44). Textual Inversion achieves zero forgetting by design (frozen backbone) but is the worst at acquiring new concepts (KID 205.69, A_MMD 185.74), confirming the plasticity–stability trade-off. LoRA merge (task arithmetic) is the weakest baseline overall (KID 261.74, A_MMD 312.44), demonstrating that simple parameter merging is insufficient for retaining generation quality. On the 10-task Landmarks setup, DSC EWC DC again leads on 3 of 5 metrics (KID 51.22, A_MMD 36.09, BwT_MMD −3.85), with EWC DC achieving the best CLIP I2I (82.86) and F_MMD (0.035), confirming consistent gains across task sequence lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual personalization framework established by C-LoRA (Smith et al., 2024b), which trains sequential LoRA adapters on cross-attention KV layers with a self-regularization penalty. The key departure is identifying C-LoRA's self-regularization as leading to a degenerate solution (all LoRA weights driven toward zero), and replacing it with EWC in parameter-space and a double-distillation scheme (DSC) in function-space, both enriched by diffusion classifier (DC) scores. The DC scores, previously exploited only for test-time zero-shot classification (Li et al., 2023; Clark & Jaini, 2024), are repurposed here as a training-time regularization signal that encodes class-specific semantic knowledge from the pretrained diffusion model. The function-space consolidation adapts Deep Model Consolidation (Zhang et al., 2020) to the diffusion setting by distilling both DC score distributions and noise prediction scores from multiple teacher LoRAs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important scenario—sequential personalization of diffusion models without replay—while maintaining zero storage and parameter overhead over C-LoRA, making it suitable for edge deployment. The identification of C-LoRA's degenerate solution and the proposed BwT_MMD metric (absolute forgetting vs. relative) are useful corrections to the field's evaluation methodology. However, the method incurs additional training-time cost from multiple class-conditional forward passes for DC score computation, and the performance gains over plain EWC are modest (1–3 points on BwT_MMD). Open questions include whether the DC-score-based regularization generalizes to video/3D diffusion models, whether the constant-factor concept sampling (k=5) remains sufficient for very long task sequences, and whether the approach transfers to architectures beyond U-Net-based Stable Diffusion.
