## Problem

Existing text-to-image alignment benchmarks evaluate models on a single slice of data—one prompt set, one human annotation template, or one evaluation task—leading to conclusions that do not generalise across conditions. The systematic impact of prompt coverage, annotation template design, and evaluation task choice on model and metric rankings has not been measured, making it unclear whether a given metric or model truly outperforms another or merely wins on a narrow subset.

## Input / Output

The Gecko metric takes a free-form text prompt (up to 570 characters in Gecko(R)) and a generated RGB image, and produces a scalar alignment score in [0,1] alongside the full set of interpretable QA pairs that contributed to the score. The Gecko2K benchmark itself comprises 2K prompts spanning 12 skills and 36 sub-skills, with ~108K human annotations collected across four templates (Likert, Word Level, DSG(H), and side-by-side) for four T2I models (SD1.5, SDXL, Muse, Imagen Vermeer). For the text-to-video extension, the same pipeline is applied to video clips using Gemini Flash as the multimodal VQA backend.

## Method

Gecko is a zero-shot, two-stage QA/VQA alignment metric built on the TIFA pipeline with three targeted improvements. First, for groundedness and coverage, QA generation is split into two LLM calls: the LLM first annotates visually groundable words in the prompt (e.g., 'A {1}[red colored] {2}[dog]'), then generates a binary QA pair for each annotated word, ensuring comprehensive coverage of prompt elements. Second, an NLI model (T5-11B) scores factual consistency between the prompt and each generated QA pair, and pairs below a threshold (r=0.005) are discarded to remove hallucinated questions. Third, instead of binary answer matching, the final alignment score normalises each answer's negative log-likelihood by the sum over all candidate answers, preserving the VQA model's uncertainty signal. The Gecko2K benchmark pairs this metric with a statistically grounded model-ordering protocol (Wilcoxon signed-rank test at p<0.001) and evaluates across three tasks—model ordering, pair-wise instance scoring, and point-wise instance scoring—to expose setting-dependent metric rankings.

## Results

On the Gecko2K benchmark, the Gecko metric (with Gemini Flash backend) achieves the highest Spearman correlation with human ratings across all four annotation templates: 0.54 (WL), 0.59 (Likert), 0.56 (DSG(H)) on Gecko(S) and 0.43, 0.58, 0.48 on Gecko(R), with 78.8% SxS accuracy on Gecko(S). On TIFA160, Gecko achieves Spearman ρ = 0.64 and Kendall's τ = 0.50, outperforming DSG (0.57/0.46) and TIFA (0.43/0.32) with the same backend models. On the VBench text-to-video extension, Gecko (Gemini Flash) reaches 55.8% SxS accuracy versus 52.0% for VQAScore and 28.0% for VideoCLIP. The paper reports that Gecko performs on average 40.5% better than interpretable baselines on pair-wise instance scoring and 22% better on point-wise instance scoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gecko (PALM-2/PaLI) outperforms TIFA across all templates and both prompt sets, e.g., 0.47 vs 0.39 Spearman on Gecko(S) WL and 74.6% vs 53.2% SxS accuracy. Against DSG, Gecko leads on most metrics (0.55 vs 0.47 Likert on Gecko(R); 74.6% vs 58.1% SxS on Gecko(S)), though DSG is competitive on Gecko(S) WL (0.45 vs 0.47). CLIP and PyramidCLIP are substantially weaker on point-wise scoring (Spearman ≤ 0.26) but PyramidCLIP is surprisingly competitive on SxS (64.3–70.7%), illustrating that single-score metrics can excel at pair-wise comparison despite poor instance-level correlation. VQAScore (Gemini Flash) is the strongest single-score baseline, slightly beating Gecko (PALM-2/PaLI) on Gecko(S) SxS (76.5% vs 74.6%), but Gecko with Gemini Flash surpasses it (78.8%). VNLI, a supervised baseline fine-tuned on COCO, is outperformed by Gecko on most metrics but edges ahead on Gecko(S) Likert (0.55 vs 0.52). On the text-to-video VBench extension, Gecko (Gemini Flash) outperforms VideoCLIP by a wide margin (55.8% vs 28.0% SxS accuracy) and VQAScore by 3.8 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the QA/VQA evaluation pipeline introduced by TIFA and DSG, which decompose alignment scoring into LLM-generated question-answer pairs scored by a VLM. The Gecko metric simplifies this pipeline (removing scene-graph generation) while adding three targeted improvements: two-step grounded question generation for coverage, NLI-based hallucination filtering, and probability-normalized VQA scoring to capture uncertainty. On the benchmarking side, the paper departs from prior datasets (DrawBench, DSG1K, TIFA160, HEIM) that each collect annotations under a single template and limited skill coverage, by systematically curating 2K prompts across 12 skills and 36 sub-skills and gathering ~100K annotations across four distinct human annotation templates (Likert, Word Level, DSG(H), and side-by-side).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central methodological finding—that model rankings and metric rankings are not stable across annotation templates, prompt subsets, or evaluation tasks—challenges the common practice of drawing conclusions from a single evaluation slice and argues for multi-template, multi-task evaluation as a standard. The Gecko2K benchmark, with its fine-grained skill/sub-skill taxonomy and reliable-prompt filtering, provides a more discriminative and reproducible evaluation resource than prior benchmarks. Open questions include whether the conclusions generalize to newer T2I architectures (e.g., autoregressive or flow-matching models), whether the Gecko metric's reliance on PaLM-2/PaLI or Gemini Flash backends limits its portability, and how the evaluation framework should adapt as T2I models saturate on current benchmarks.
