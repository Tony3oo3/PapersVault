## Problem

Existing prompt learning methods for pre-trained multimodal models optimize prompts through direct loss backpropagation, which constrains the richness and specificity of prompt representations. This goal-driven update mechanism limits prompting capability on complex, fine-grained tasks such as referring expression comprehension, where prompts must encode detailed cross-modal spatial reasoning rather than merely shifting a global representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252186/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image and a free-form text caption as input and outputs a bounding box for the referred object via GLIP's region proposal network. During training, ground-truth binary masks (1×224×224) are additionally required to supervise the Mask-VAE and diffusion prompt generator, but at inference only the image and caption are needed. The generated prompts are image-sized saliency maps decoded from a 4×28×28 latent space, which are then dimensionally reduced via convolutional adapters before being injected into the encoder.

## Method

Diff-Prompt is a three-stage pipeline that uses a diffusion model as a prompt generator. Stage 1 trains a Mask-VAE (368 kB) to compress binary masks into a 4×28×28 latent code. Stage 2 trains a compact DiT-based prompt generator (12 blocks, hidden size 512, 309 MB) conditioned on GLIP's image and text embeddings to denoise the mask latent over 100 forward steps, sampled via DDIM in 25 reverse steps. Stage 3 aligns the denoising trajectory with the encoder: at each of the first D attention layers, the latent feature at step 25−2i is decoded back to a 224×224 saliency map, passed through a modality-specific adapter to produce input-specific prompts, and concatenated with learnable global prompts before being fed into both the vision and language encoders. The reverse-order alignment (richer cross-modal interaction in shallow layers) is shown to outperform sequential alignment, and only the adapters and global prompts are trained in this final stage, keeping the backbone, Mask-VAE, and prompt generator frozen.

## Results

On RefCOCO val, Diff-Prompt achieves R@1=37.94, R@5=90.55, and UB=99.37, surpassing the GLIP-T(A) foundation model by +6.12 R@1 and +10.55 R@5. On RefCOCO testA, the improvement over the foundation model reaches +8.87 R@1 and +14.05 R@5. On Flickr30k val, Diff-Prompt scores R@1=57.39 and R@5=81.20, outperforming the next-best method (FedTPG_d9 at R@1=56.08) by 1.31 points. The method introduces 5.5M tunable parameters (4.83% of total) with an inference time of 2.29s, compared to 1.77–1.94s for lighter prompt methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-Prompt outperforms all 17 baselines across every metric on both RefCOCO and Flickr30k. On RefCOCO val, it leads the strongest prompt-based competitor MaPLe (R@1=34.81) by 3.13 points and the best adapter method Meta-adapter (R@1=36.54) by 1.40 points in R@1. On Flickr30k val, it surpasses FedTPG_d9 (R@1=56.08) by 1.31 R@1 and MaPLe (R@1=54.96) by 2.43 R@1. Notably, VPT actually underperforms the GLIP-T(A) foundation model (R@1=28.80 vs. 31.82 on RefCOCO val), confirming the paper's argument that single-modality prompts are insufficient for fine-grained grounding. In cross-dataset generalization (Table 2), Diff-Prompt achieves an average of 68.85, trailing CoCoLe (68.91) by 0.06 but leading CPL (68.07) and CoPrompt (67.00), with particular strength on Flowers102 (75.57) and Aircraft (29.07). The method uses 5.5M tunable parameters—more than MaPLe (0.7M) and CoOp (55.3K) but fewer than FedTPG_d9 (39.1M)—at the cost of slightly higher inference time (2.29s vs. ~1.8s for others) due to the multi-step diffusion sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of prompt learning for vision-language models and latent diffusion models. It departs from the dominant paradigm of directly optimizing prompt parameters via backpropagation (as in VPT, CoOp, MaPLe, FedTPG) by instead training a diffusion model to *generate* input-specific prompts, using mask supervision to ground the generation in spatial semantics. Architecturally, it builds on DiT for the prompt generator backbone and on LDM's latent-space diffusion strategy, repurposing both for a fundamentally different role: not image synthesis, but producing intermediate representations that are injected into a frozen encoder via deep prompting. The Mask-VAE compresses binary masks into a compact latent space, enabling the diffusion process to operate efficiently while preserving spatial information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a generative model, when properly supervised, can produce richer and more input-specific prompt information than directly learned prompts, particularly for fine-grained spatial tasks like referring expression comprehension. However, the approach carries notable practical costs: the 224×224 input constraint, multi-step DDIM sampling at inference, and dependency on the pretrained VAE/DiT quality limit immediate adoption. Open questions include whether one-step or lightweight generators could match the quality, whether the mask-supervision strategy generalizes to tasks without dense spatial annotations, and whether the diffusion-based prompt generation can be made resolution-agnostic. The moderate parameter overhead (5.5M, ~4.8%) and modest inference-time increase (~0.4s) make it more practical than full fine-tuning but less efficient than standard prompt tuning.
