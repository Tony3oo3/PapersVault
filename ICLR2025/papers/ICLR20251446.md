## Problem

Prior work on knowledge localization in LLMs (KN, ROME, KC) proposed that individual facts reside in distributed parameters, specific layers, or parameter chains, but these claims lack a unified and verifiable basis. This paper demonstrates through fidelity and reliability experiments that none of the existing individual-knowledge localization methods are faithful or reliable, and instead shows that what *can* be localized is not individual knowledge but the commonalities shared across a dataset—i.e., model capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251446/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of (prompt, answer) text pairs (e.g., 1600 GSM8K math problems, 2400 Emotion sentiment samples, 1200 Code25K code snippets) together with a frozen pretrained LLM (GPTJ-6B, Llama2-7B/13B). It produces a binary mask over all FFN intermediate neurons, where a neuron is marked as a 'commonality neuron' if its dataset-averaged integrated-gradient score deviates by more than σ (default 6) standard deviations from the mean. The resulting mask is extremely sparse—covering only 0.08%–0.28% of MLP parameters—and can be directly used to fine-tune or zero out specific neurons to enhance or erase a capability.

## Method

CNL (Commonality Neurons Locating) extends the KN integrated-gradients attribution to the dataset level: for each FFN neuron ω^{l,j}, it computes the expected integrated gradient of the output probability over all (x, y) pairs in the dataset, averaging over output positions and gradient steps (S=19). Neurons whose scores fall outside σ standard deviations of the dataset-wide mean are flagged as commonality neurons. The paper first establishes the negative result—individual knowledge cannot be reliably localized—via fidelity experiments (rewriting the same fact 5× yields only 7–37% neuron overlap across methods) and reliability experiments (zeroing/doubling activations or editing non-localized layers still changes knowledge). It then validates the positive result through decoupling experiments (same math expression in different task formats shares only 15.6% of response parameters, while same-capability samples share 8.6%), enhance experiments (fine-tuning only the 0.15% located neurons outperforms random and full-model baselines), erase experiments (zeroing located neurons drops accuracy by up to 18.7%), and cross-dataset transfer (GSM8K-located neurons boost Meta_Math by 22%), collectively showing that commonality neurons form a sparse, transferable collection of capability neurons.

## Results

CNL achieves a neuron overlap rate of 96.42% on GSM8K (Llama2-7B) and 98.32% on Imdb (Llama2-13B), demonstrating that data commonalities map to a consistent, small set of FFN neurons (only 0.08–0.28% of MLP parameters). In the enhancement experiment, fine-tuning only the located neurons (0.15% of total parameters) yields a 5.17% average improvement over random at epoch 1 (Llama2-7B, σ=6) and up to 9.87% on Emotion at epoch 10 (GPTJ-6B). In the erase experiment, zeroing located neurons causes an 18.68% average performance drop (Llama2-13B, σ=3) versus only 0.21% for random neurons, confirming the located set is causally linked to model capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CNL dominates all three individual-knowledge localization baselines across every unified metric. Against KN (Dai et al., 2021), CNL more than doubles the fidelity overlap (96.4% vs. 37.3%) and nearly doubles the Impact on Performance (IPP 20.0 vs. 11.4), while matching its parameter granularity (0.2%). Against ROME (Meng et al., 2022), CNL is superior on all three axes: overlap (96.4% vs. 32.7%), granularity (0.2% vs. 16.7% of parameters), and IPP (20.0 vs. 0.0, meaning ROME's layer-level edits produce no net performance gain over random). Against KC (Yao et al., 2024), CNL achieves a 13× higher overlap (96.4% vs. 7.2%), a smaller parameter footprint (0.2% vs. 1.6%), and a higher IPP (20.0 vs. 10.5). The paper further shows that in the enhancement experiment, fine-tuning only CNL-located neurons outperforms both random selection and the 'without-located' ablation across all three datasets and both model families (Llama2-7B/13B, GPTJ-6B), with the gap widening at later epochs (e.g., 44.65 vs. 34.67 avg at epoch 10 for Llama2-7B, σ=3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly challenges the dominant line of work on individual knowledge localization in transformers (KN, ROME, KC), arguing through fidelity and reliability experiments that none of these methods can consistently identify the parameters storing a specific fact. It repurposes the integrated-gradients attribution formula from KN (Dai et al., 2021) but shifts the unit of analysis from a single factual prompt to an entire dataset, computing expected neuron contributions across many samples to identify 'commonality neurons.' Conceptually, it reframes the interpretability question from 'where is this fact stored?' to 'which parameters implement this capability?', positioning itself as a corrective to what it views as a misdirected research agenda.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the core claim holds—that individual knowledge is not localizable but capabilities are—this would redirect a substantial body of interpretability and knowledge-editing research toward capability-level interventions rather than fact-level ones. However, several open questions remain: the 'capability' concept is somewhat circularly defined by the dataset used for localization, experiments are limited to 6–13B models and FFN neurons only, and the enhancement gains (a few percentage points) are modest relative to the strong conceptual claim. Future work should test whether this framework scales to larger models, extends to attention layers, and can support targeted capability editing analogous to ROME-style knowledge editing.
