## Problem

Existing Graph Transformers face two intertwined limitations: the quadratic time and space complexity of node-level self-attention makes them unscalable to large graphs, and the integration of local GNN blocks with global SA modules in the same (node) dimension creates an inherent tradeoff where fine-grained structural details are lost when capturing long-range dependencies. No prior architecture achieves both linear complexity and full global expressivity without sacrificing local fidelity or requiring auxiliary components like positional encodings or augmented losses.

## Input / Output

DUALFormer consumes a node attribute matrix X ∈ R^{n×f} and an edge list (or adjacency matrix) describing graph topology, and outputs per-node class labels (for node classification) or per-node property scores (for node property prediction). A distinctive aspect is that the self-attention score matrix is computed over the feature dimension (d×d, where d is the hidden dimension, typically 32–512) rather than the node dimension (n×n), so the attention mechanism operates on correlations between feature dimensions rather than between node pairs. The model is trained in a semi-supervised setting with standard train/val/test splits, using negative log-likelihood loss for classification and ROC-AUC or accuracy for property prediction.

## Method

DUALFormer is a dual-dimensional Graph Transformer that decouples local and global information processing into two distinct dimensions of the feature matrix. The Global Attention Module exploits the linearized-attention identity (QK⊤)V ≈ Q(K⊤V) to compute a d×d attention score matrix M = σ((Q⊤K)/√n) over feature dimensions, then applies it as V·M to propagate query features along feature-to-feature correlations, reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(n+e). The Local Graph Convolution Module (defaulting to SGC) is applied after the attention layers to inject structural bias via sparse adjacency-matrix diffusion in the node dimension. This separation means the GNN captures locality in the node dimension while the SA captures globality in the feature dimension, eliminating the tradeoff that plagues same-dimension GTs. Theoretically, Theorem 1 proves the global attention module reduces intra-class variance while preserving inter-class variance, improving representation discriminability. The architecture requires no positional encodings, no augmented training losses, and no additional hyperparameters beyond standard Transformer components, making it both simpler and more scalable than competing GTs.

## Results

On the primary node classification task across seven standard benchmarks, DUALFormer achieves the best accuracy on 5 of 7 datasets, with the largest margins on Cora (85.88%, +1.17% over runner-up CoBFormer at 84.71%) and PubMed (83.97%, +2.55% over CoBFormer at 81.42%). On large-scale node property prediction, DUALFormer leads on 3 of 4 datasets, achieving 82.98% ROC-AUC on ogbn-proteins (+3.45% over SGFormer) and 83.91% accuracy on ogbn-products. DUALFormer also attains the shortest per-epoch training time (e.g., 0.034s on ogbn-arxiv) and among the lowest GPU memory usage (1.17 GB) across all compared Graph Transformers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUALFormer outperforms all five GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, APPNP, SGC) by 2–5% on every node classification dataset, confirming that the dual-dimensional attention+GNN design substantially exceeds pure message-passing. Among the eight GT baselines, DUALFormer wins on 6 of 7 datasets against the strongest competitors: it beats CoBFormer (the prior best) by 1.17% on Cora and 2.55% on PubMed, and surpasses SGFormer by 1.38% on Cora. The two exceptions are Computers (Polynormer 93.68% vs. DUALFormer 93.16%) and CS (NAGphormer 95.75% vs. DUALFormer 95.62%), where the gaps are within 0.6%. On large-scale node property prediction, DUALFormer leads on ogbn-proteins (82.98% ROC-AUC, +3.45% over SGFormer), ogbn-arxiv (73.71%), and ogbn-products (83.91%), but trails Polynormer on pokec (82.97% vs. 86.10%). On heterophilic graphs (Roman-Empire, Question) and the 100M-node ogbn-papers100M, DUALFormer also outperforms spectral GNNs (ChebNetII, OptBasisGNN) and linear-complexity GTs (NodeFormer, NAGphormer, SGFormer), with the largest gain on Question (78.62% vs. SGFormer's 77.06%). In terms of efficiency, DUALFormer matches or beats SGFormer in training time on all large graphs while using comparable or less GPU memory, and avoids the O(n²) attention cost that limits GraphTrans and SAT.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUALFormer builds directly on the linearized attention mechanism of Katharopoulos et al. (2020), which showed that the standard (Q×K)×V computation can be reordered to Q×(K×V) at linear cost. The key conceptual departure from existing Graph Transformers (GraphTrans, NAGphormer, GOAT, SGFormer, Polynormer, CoBFormer, etc.) is that instead of performing self-attention over the node dimension with value V as the agent representation, DUALFormer performs attention over the feature dimension with query Q as the agent representation, thereby decoupling local GNN message-passing (node dimension) from global attention (feature dimension). This dual-dimensional separation eliminates the need for positional encodings, augmented training losses, or additional hyperparameters that most prior GTs require to integrate structural information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DUALFormer achieves O(n+e) training complexity while retaining full (not approximate) global expressivity, a combination that prior GTs could not simultaneously deliver—NAGphormer sacrifices global expressivity for linearity, while GOAT and Exphormer only approximate it. The practical simplicity (no PE, no ATL, no extra parameters) makes it an attractive lightweight baseline for large-scale graph learning. However, the paper acknowledges that DUALFormer does not outperform well-designed spectral GNNs (ChebNetII, OptBasisGNN) on heterophilic graphs, and the GNN module becomes a bottleneck on extremely edge-dense graphs (Amazon2M). Open questions include designing a universal GT architecture that handles both homophilic and heterophilic graphs, and extending the dual-dimensional design to edge-level tasks.
