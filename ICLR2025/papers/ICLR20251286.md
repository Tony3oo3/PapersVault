## Problem

Adversarial training on long-tailed (imbalanced) datasets produces disproportionately poor robustness on tail classes, yet prior work has not provided a theoretical explanation for this failure. Existing long-tailed adversarial training methods (RoBal, REAT, AT-BSL) combine balanced softmax with standard adversarial training but still exhibit a large gap between head and tail class robustness, leaving tail classes significantly more vulnerable than under natural training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from long-tailed datasets (CIFAR-10-LT at 32×32, CIFAR-100-LT at 32×32, TinyImageNet-LT at 64×64) with imbalance ratios ranging from 5 to 50, and outputs a single class label per image. A key structural detail is that the training pipeline operates on two different datasets simultaneously: the full long-tailed dataset D for the student model and a resampled balanced sub-dataset D_B (where each class contains γ·n₁ samples) for the self-teacher, with γ = IR/2.

## Method

The proposed two-step framework first constructs a balanced sub-dataset D_B by up-sampling tail classes and down-sampling head classes, then adversarially trains a balanced self-teacher on D_B using 10-step PGD with cross-entropy loss for 30 epochs. In the main training phase, the student model is trained on the full long-tailed dataset D for 100 epochs using PGD adversarial training with a combined loss: balanced softmax (BSL) on adversarial inputs plus a self-distillation term (KL divergence) that matches the student's adversarial logits to the self-teacher's clean-input logits, weighted by hyperparameter α. The key insight, supported by a theoretical analysis on Gaussian mixture models, is that adversarial training inherently shifts the decision boundary in a way that disproportionately harms tail classes (Corollary 1), and that a teacher trained on balanced data naturally develops stronger tail-class robustness that can be distilled into the student. This self-distillation mechanism transfers tail robustness without requiring any external pre-trained model, using only the same architecture trained on a resampled subset of the available data.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (IR=50, ResNet-18), the proposed method achieves 70.81% clean accuracy, 38.85% PGD-20 robust accuracy, and 34.32% AutoAttack accuracy at the best checkpoint, with tail-class PGD accuracy of 36.9%—a 20.3 pp improvement over the best long-tailed baseline (RoBal, 15.9% at last checkpoint). On CIFAR-100-LT (IR=10), it reaches 46.13% clean and 22.42% PGD accuracy with 17.9% tail-class PGD accuracy, outperforming AT-BSL (12.4%) by 5.5 pp. On TinyImageNet-LT (IR=10), it achieves 38.44% clean and 17.02% PGD accuracy with 16.0% tail-class PGD accuracy, surpassing AT-BSL (13.0%) by 3.0 pp. The method consistently achieves the highest overall robust accuracy (PGD and AutoAttack) across all three datasets while also delivering the largest tail-class gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines on both overall and tail-class metrics across all three datasets. Against standard adversarial training methods (PGD-AT, TRADES, MART, AWP), the gains are substantial: on CIFAR-10-LT, tail-class PGD accuracy jumps from 0.2–2.2% (AWP/PGD-AT) to 36.9%, while overall PGD accuracy improves from 29.3–32.4% to 38.85%. Against long-tailed adversarial training baselines, the advantage is most pronounced on tail classes: on CIFAR-10-LT, Ours achieves 36.9% T-PGD vs. RoBal's 33.1% and AT-BSL's 22.0%; on CIFAR-100-LT, 17.9% vs. AT-BSL's 12.4% and RoBal's 8.0%; on TinyImageNet-LT, 16.0% vs. AT-BSL's 13.0% and RoBal's 4.4%. The method also leads on overall robust accuracy (PGD and AutoAttack) in every setting, e.g., 38.85% PGD on CIFAR-10-LT vs. 35.98% (REAT) and 35.87% (AT-BSL), and 22.42% PGD on CIFAR-100-LT vs. 21.85% (AWP) and 19.67% (RoBal). Notably, the gap widens at the last checkpoint, where baselines like RoBal and REAT degrade significantly on tail classes (e.g., RoBal T-PGD drops to 15.9% on CIFAR-10-LT) while the proposed method remains stable at 36.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of long-tailed adversarial training initiated by RoBal (Wu et al., 2021), REAT (Li et al., 2023), and AT-BSL (Yue et al., 2024), which primarily combined balanced softmax or class-aware margins with standard adversarial training losses. It departs from this lineage by providing a theoretical analysis (via Gaussian mixture models) showing that adversarial training inherently degrades tail-class robustness below natural-training levels, and by introducing a self-distillation mechanism where a balanced-subset-trained teacher transfers tail robustness to the main model. The distillation component builds on adversarial knowledge distillation techniques (Lee et al., 2023), while the balanced-subset construction draws on standard resampling strategies from the long-tailed recognition literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that adversarial training makes tail classes less robust than under natural training (Corollary 1) reframes how practitioners should think about robustness under class imbalance, suggesting that simply adding adversarial training to long-tailed pipelines is insufficient. The practical impact is significant—20.3 percentage point tail-class PGD improvement on CIFAR-10-LT—but the scope is limited to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet) and modest architectures (ResNet-18, WRN-34-10), leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-level problems. The authors note that the self-teacher uses a simple PGD-AT procedure and that class-level fairness beyond data quantity remains unaddressed, pointing to future work on more sophisticated teacher training and intrinsic class difficulty.
