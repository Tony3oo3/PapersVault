## Problem

Existing hallucination benchmarks for multimodal LLMs evaluate only within a single modality (visual or audio), leaving cross-modal driven hallucinations—where one modality causes the model to perceive non-existent content in the other—entirely unmeasured. This paper identifies that audio-visual LLMs are systematically vulnerable to such cross-modal hallucinations and introduces the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate this phenomenon.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250876/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes paired 10-second video clips and audio clips (1,106 real videos from VALOR/AudioCaps plus 1,030 synthetic videos with swapped audio) alongside a text question. Three judgment tasks (Audio-driven Video Hallucination, Video-driven Audio Hallucination, Audio-visual Matching) require binary Yes/No answers, while the Audio-visual Captioning task requires a free-form sentence describing both what is seen and heard. The full dataset comprises 5,302 QnA pairs (balanced 50/50 yes/no) and 1,106 ground-truth audio-visual captions.

## Method

The core contribution is a semi-automatic two-stage annotation pipeline: Stage 1 disentangles audio-visual information into five categories (in-view sound source, in-view silent object, out-of-view sound source, in-view sound, out-of-view sound) by feeding RAM++ visual tags and dataset captions into GPT-4 with few-shot examples; Stage 2 generates QnA pairs via rule-based templates for judgment tasks and GPT-4 for caption synthesis, with minimal human verification at each stage. For model improvement, the paper applies two interventions to Video-LLaMA: (1) audio feature alignment by pre-training a Q-Former and linear projection layer on 173K audio-text pairs to strengthen the audio-to-LLM mapping, and (2) LoRA fine-tuning (rank 16, alpha 32) on an annotation-enriched dataset of 10,327 videos and 87,624 QnA pairs generated by the same pipeline without human verification. The key empirical finding is that multimodal inputs actually degrade performance relative to unimodal or text-only inputs, indicating the LLM's limited capacity to perceive and bind complex multimodal signals is the root cause of cross-modal hallucinations.

## Results

On AVHBench, most audio-visual LLMs perform near random chance (50%) on cross-modal hallucination tasks: PandaGPT achieves the best accuracy on Audio-driven Video Hallucination (58.5%) and Video-driven Audio Hallucination (61.3%), while OneLLM leads on Audio-Visual Matching (60.1%). For Audio-Visual Captioning, ChatBridge achieves the highest CIDEr (33.1) and GAVIE-A (4.69). Critically, feeding unimodal inputs instead of multimodal inputs improves performance (e.g., Video-LLaMA: 50.1% → 50.0% on A→V, but OneLLM: 53.7% → 76.5%), and converting inputs to text captions further boosts accuracy (OneLLM reaches 76.6% on A→V), revealing that multimodal signals actively confuse these models. After LoRA fine-tuning plus audio alignment, Video-LLaMA improves from 50.1% to 83.9% on A→V (multimodal) and from 50.2% to 77.3% on V→A (multimodal).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All six primary audio-visual LLMs (X-InstructBLIP, ImageBind-LLM, Video-LLaMA, ChatBridge, PandaGPT, OneLLM) perform at or near random chance on cross-modal hallucination tasks, with PandaGPT being the strongest on hallucination discrimination (58.5% A→V, 61.3% V→A) and OneLLM best on matching (60.1%). Unimodal LLMs substantially outperform their audio-visual counterparts on single-modality tasks: PLLaVA (video-only) reaches 81.5% on Video Hallucination vs. 50.0–76.5% for audio-visual models, and LTU (audio-only) reaches 67.2% on Audio Hallucination vs. 34.4–64.1%. Among more recent models evaluated in the appendix, Gemini-Flash achieves the highest A→V accuracy (83.3%) and Video-LLaMA2 the best V→A accuracy (74.2%), indicating gradual improvement but still far from reliable. The paper's improved Video-LLaMA (LoRA + audio alignment) jumps from 50.1% to 83.9% on A→V and 50.2% to 77.3% on V→A, surpassing all original baselines and approaching unimodal LLM performance. On generalization benchmarks (VAST, AVinstruct), the combined LoRA + alignment variant also achieves the best scores (CIDEr 47.6 on VAST; 57.8% accuracy on AVinstruct), confirming that the anti-hallucination training transfers to broader audio-visual understanding tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hallucination evaluation paradigm from unimodal (visual-only or audio-only) settings to cross-modal audio-visual scenarios. Prior benchmarks such as POPE, CIEM, NOPE, and GAVIE assess whether models hallucinate objects within a single modality, but none evaluate whether one modality drives spurious perception in the other. The training experiments build directly on Video-LLaMA's dual-branch architecture (vision-language and audio-language branches via ImageBind), applying BLIP-2's Q-Former for audio alignment and LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, while the dataset construction pipeline repurposes existing datasets (VALOR, AudioCaps) with GPT-4-assisted disentanglement and RAM++ visual tagging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVHBench is the first benchmark to systematically evaluate cross-modal hallucinations in audio-visual LLMs, filling a clear gap as these models gain traction for video understanding. The finding that multimodal inputs degrade performance below unimodal baselines challenges the core assumption that more modalities should improve perception, pointing to a fundamental alignment and binding problem in current architectures. Key limitations include the focus on environmental sound (not speech), 10-second clips, and limited scenario diversity, leaving open questions about temporal reasoning, multi-object interactions, and whether the observed improvements from LoRA + audio alignment generalize to newer, larger models. The benchmark is likely to become a standard diagnostic tool as the audio-visual LLM landscape evolves rapidly.
