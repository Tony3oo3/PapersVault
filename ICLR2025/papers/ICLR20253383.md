## Problem

LoRA achieves parameter-efficient fine-tuning by re-parameterizing weight changes as a product of two low-rank matrices, yet consistently underperforms full fine-tuning. The paper identifies the root cause: LoRA optimization is mathematically equivalent to full fine-tuning with a low-rank 'equivalent gradient' that loses information present in the true full gradient, leading to divergent optimization trajectories and sub-optimal convergence.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen pretrained model (T5-base, Llama-2-7B, or CLIP-ViT-B/16) with LoRA adapters of rank r (default 8, scaling factor s=α/r or α/√r) inserted into all linear layers except embeddings, normalization, and classification heads. During each training step, the standard LoRA forward/backward pass produces gradients g_lora^A and g_lora^B, which are then algebraically adjusted before the parameter update. For image classification, the CLIP text classifier (prompt-based) remains frozen while only the visual backbone is adapted; for LLM tasks, sequence lengths are 1024 with macro batch size 32.

## Method

LoRA-Pro derives a closed-form correction to the gradients of LoRA's low-rank matrices A and B so that the resulting equivalent gradient g̃ = sBg^A + sg^B A best approximates the full fine-tuning gradient g in Frobenius norm. The optimal adjusted gradients (Theorem 2.1) are expressed entirely in terms of the standard LoRA gradients g_lora^A and g_lora^B plus an arbitrary r×r matrix X, avoiding any need to compute the full m×n gradient. The matrix X is selected by solving a Sylvester equation (Theorem 2.3) that minimizes the deviation of the adjusted gradients from the original LoRA gradients, and a separate theorem (Theorem 2.2) guarantees the loss strictly decreases at each step. For AdamW, the method computes the equivalent gradient, applies AdamW's momentum and variance updates to it, then decomposes the resulting AdamW gradient back into adjusted A and B gradients, with weight decay applied to W₀, A, and B separately to mimic full fine-tuning.

## Results

On GLUE (T5-base, rank=8), LoRA-Pro achieves an average score of 88.44, surpassing both standard LoRA (82.08) and full fine-tuning (87.91). On Llama-2-7B tasks (rank=8), LoRA-Pro reaches 57.57 on GSM8K and 22.97 on HumanEval, improving over LoRA by +15.49 and +8.21 respectively. On image classification (CLIP-ViT-B/16), LoRA-Pro attains 89.20 average accuracy across 7 datasets, exceeding full fine-tuning (88.06) and LoRA (83.46). At rank=128, LoRA-Pro further narrows the gap to full fine-tuning, reaching 61.08 on GSM8K and 30.28 on HumanEval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA-Pro consistently outperforms standard LoRA across all task domains: +6.36 average on GLUE, +15.49 on GSM8K, +8.21 on HumanEval, and +5.74 average on image classification. Against the strongest PEFT baseline LoRA-GA, LoRA-Pro wins on GSM8K (+3.97) and HumanEval (+3.16) at rank 8, with the gap widening to +6.01 and +7.23 at rank 128, but trails slightly on MT-Bench (5.72 vs 5.95). LoRA-Pro also beats DoRA, AdaLoRA, PiSSA, rsLoRA, and LoRA+ on GSM8K and HumanEval, with the largest margins over AdaLoRA (+6.85 GSM8K, +5.17 HumanEval) and PiSSA (+13.03 GSM8K, +6.95 HumanEval). Notably, LoRA-Pro surpasses full fine-tuning on GLUE (88.44 vs 87.91) and image classification (89.20 vs 88.06), though it still lags behind full fine-tuning on GSM8K (57.57 vs 59.36) and HumanEval (22.97 vs 35.31) at rank 8, with the gap narrowing substantially at higher ranks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA's low-rank reparameterization (W = W₀ + sBA) and positions itself within the broader family of LoRA variants (LoRA+, rsLoRA, DoRA, AdaLoRA, PiSSA, LoRA-GA). Its key conceptual departure is the 'equivalent gradient' perspective: LoRA optimization is shown to be mathematically equivalent to full fine-tuning with a virtual low-rank gradient, reframing the LoRA-vs-full-FT performance gap as a gradient approximation problem. Rather than approximating the full gradient from the outside (as LoRA-GA does), LoRA-Pro adjusts the internal gradients of A and B via a closed-form solution derived from a Sylvester equation, making the equivalent gradient match the true full gradient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LoRA-Pro provides a principled, theoretically grounded way to close the well-known performance gap between LoRA and full fine-tuning with modest overhead (~4 GB extra memory, ~10 min extra training time), and it consistently outperforms all LoRA variants across NLU, dialogue, math, code, and vision tasks. The equivalent-gradient framework offers a new diagnostic lens for understanding *why* low-rank adapters underperform, potentially guiding future PEFT designs. Key limitations remain: the method still assumes ΔW is low-rank (which may break in pre-training or with very large fine-tuning data), and it cannot be applied to structurally different variants like DoRA or FLoRA. Open questions include whether the gradient-adjustment principle generalizes to non-LoRA PEFT methods and how it behaves at pre-training scale.
