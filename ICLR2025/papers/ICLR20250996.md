## Problem

Existing decoding strategies for MLLM hallucination mitigation penalize summary tokens but lack a mechanistic understanding of how the LLM's inherent summarization mechanism causes hallucinations. This paper identifies that over-propagation of anchor tokens—driven by a polarized eigenspectrum variance in the query-key attention matrix—causes the model to neglect visual information, and shows that merely intervening in this variance (without penalizing any specific tokens) is sufficient to alleviate hallucinations at zero extra inference cost.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard MLLM inputs: an RGB image (processed via CLIP/EVA vision encoder into 32–576 image tokens depending on architecture) and a text prompt such as 'Please describe this image in detail.' Outputs are standard autoregressive text—image captions for CHAIR/GPT-4 evaluation or binary yes/no answers for POPE VQA. TAME is applied internally at the self-attention W_QK matrix level during each decoding step, so the external I/O interface is unchanged; it is a drop-in replacement for the attention weight computation that adds no additional forward passes or inference latency.

## Method

The paper defines a token propagation probability ρ_i that quantifies the likelihood of a token's information propagating to other tokens, and proves (via Propositions 1–3) that ρ is governed by two eigenspectrum parameters of W_QK: the eigenvalue mean (tr(W)) and scale (tr(W²)). Over-propagation of anchor tokens—and thus hallucinations—occurs when the eigenspectrum variance is polarized (large tr(W²) relative to tr(W)), reducing propagation entropy. TAME reparameterizes the attention weight as Ŵ_QK = (1 + γ/log(η+ξ))·W_QK where η = tr(W_QK²), dynamically scaling the eigenspectrum variance back toward the high-entropy regime during autoregressive generation. The logarithmic form prevents excessive scaling when η is near zero and reduces scaling when η is large, effectively constraining the variance without altering the mean. The method is training-free, adds no extra inference time (η is estimated via a Monte Carlo power-method approximation), and is plug-and-play: it can be stacked on top of OPERA, VCD, ICD, beam search, RAG, or RLHF pipelines.

## Results

On the POPE hallucination benchmark (average F1 across random, popular, and adversarial splits), TAME as a plug-in consistently improves all baselines across four MLLMs: e.g., OPERA+TAME reaches 84.9 F1 on InstructBLIP (vs. 82.7 for OPERA alone) and 83.8 on LLaVA-1.5 (vs. 83.4). On CHAIR evaluation, TAME achieves approximately 27.1% relative improvement over SID on LLaVA-1.5. On the GPT-4-assisted HalluBench evaluation, TAME yields a 34.3% reduction in hallucinated sentence ratio (HSR) over greedy decoding and a 12.7% reduction in hallucinated words per image (HWPI) compared to OPERA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAME is evaluated as a training-free, zero-overhead plug-in on top of seven decoding baselines (Beam, VCD, ICD, OPERA, SID, and their combinations) across four MLLMs. On POPE, TAME+OPERA achieves the highest InstructBLIP score (84.9 vs. 82.7 for OPERA alone, +2.2 F1), while TAME+SID leads on LLaVA-1.5 (84.1 vs. 83.5) and Shikra (83.9 vs. 82.2). TAME+VCD delivers the strongest MiniGPT-4 result (79.4 vs. 74.7 for VCD alone, +4.7 F1), indicating the largest gains on models with Q-former-based 32-token image representations. On the broader GQA/SEED/VisWiz/MME/POPE suite (Table 4), TAME consistently adds 0.5–1.5 points over each base method (e.g., SURf-RAG+TAME reaches 87.1 POPE-Random vs. 85.6 for SURf-RAG; OPERA+TAME reaches 62.5 GQA vs. 62.0 for OPERA on LLaVA-1.5-7B). TAME also complements retrieval-augmented (Vanilla-RAG, SURf-RAG) and RLHF-based (RLHF-V, CSR) pipelines, improving POPE by 1.2–1.8 F1 in each case, confirming its generality as an orthogonal enhancement rather than a competing decoding strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Bao et al. (2024), who showed that self-attention localizes when the QK eigenspectrum concentrates, and extends that spectral theory to explain hallucinations in MLLMs through a new 'token propagation probability' framework. It departs from the dominant hallucination-mitigation paradigm (OPERA, VCD, ICD, SID) that explicitly penalizes summary/anchor tokens during decoding, arguing instead that the root cause is the polarized variance of the QK eigenspectrum and that a simple reparameterization of W_QK suffices. The anchor-token literature (Wang et al. 2023; Pang et al. 2024) is unified under a single spectral characterization, resolving what the authors call the 'blessing and curse' of anchor tokens: moderate propagation aids expressivity while over-propagation triggers hallucination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical link between the eigenspectrum of the attention weight matrix and MLLM hallucinations, offering a mechanistic explanation (over-propagation due to polarized variance) rather than a purely empirical one. Practically, TAME is a zero-overhead plug-and-play reparameterization that can be stacked on top of any existing decoding strategy (OPERA, SID, RAG, RLHF), making it attractive for deployment on resource-constrained devices where contrastive decoding's 2–3× inference cost is prohibitive. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include inability to address all hallucination types (attribute, spatial, relational) and the still-elusive causal mechanism by which anchor tokens influence generation. An open question is whether the eigenspectrum intervention generalizes to architectures without standard self-attention (e.g., Mamba-based MLLMs) or to longer-context settings where the eigenspectrum dynamics may shift.
