## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for diffusion models map all undesirable concepts to a single fixed generic target (e.g., an empty prompt or 'a photo'), ignoring how the target choice affects preservation of unrelated concepts. This paper shows that the concept space has a geometric locality structure—erasing one concept primarily impacts only semantically nearby concepts—and that the fixed-target strategy is suboptimal because it fails to exploit this structure to minimize collateral damage.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained Stable Diffusion model (v1.4), a set of concepts to erase, and a concept space vocabulary (default: 1000 ImageNet classes, filtered to the k=100 most textually similar concepts per query to limit computational cost). It outputs a fine-tuned sanitized diffusion model that no longer generates the erased concepts while retaining generation capability for all other concepts. The target concept is not a single discrete label but a learned continuous mixture over the concept space, represented via Gumbel-Softmax weights over precomputed CLIP text embeddings.

## Method

AGE formulates target selection as a minimax optimization: the outer min updates model parameters θ' to map the erased concept's output toward the target concept's output (L1) while keeping the target concept's own output unchanged (L2), and the inner max over a learnable weight vector π selects the target concept that is most sensitive to parameter changes (identifying locally related concepts) yet not a synonym of the query (ensuring effective erasure). The Gumbel-Softmax operator with low temperature (γ=0.1) parameterizes the target as a sparse mixture of a few concepts from the vocabulary, enabling gradient-based search in continuous space rather than discrete enumeration. A dictionary stores per-concept target weights across iterations for stable, continuous learning. The approach is validated on object erasure (Imagenette), NSFW attribute removal (Nudenet detector), and artistic style removal (CLIP/LPIPS), consistently outperforming ESD, UCE, CA, MACE, and AP on preservation metrics while maintaining competitive erasure rates.

## Results

On the object-erasure task (Imagenette, 5 classes erased / 5 preserved), AGE achieves 98.1% ESR-1 and 95.7% ESR-5 while dramatically outperforming all baselines on preservation: 73.6% PSR-1 and 95.6% PSR-5, versus 55.2% and 79.9% for the strongest baseline (AP). AGE also attains the best FID (16.1) and near-best CLIP score (26.0) on COCO 30K. On the NSFW nudity-erasure task, AGE achieves the lowest NER-0.5 (1.53%) and best FID (14.20), surpassing AP (1.70%, 15.52) and UCE (3.42%, 15.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGE trades a small amount of erasure performance (≈1–2% lower ESR-5) for a large gain in concept preservation across all five baselines. Against the strongest preservation baseline AP, AGE improves PSR-1 by 18.4 points (73.6% vs. 55.2%) and PSR-5 by 15.7 points (95.6% vs. 79.9%), while matching AP's FID (16.1 vs. 16.3) and CLIP (26.0 vs. 26.1). Against UCE, which achieves perfect 100% ESR but severely degrades preservation (PSR-5 = 49.5%), AGE sacrifices only 4.3% ESR-5 to recover 46.1 points of PSR-5. Against ESD, CA, and MACE, AGE wins on every preservation and quality metric (PSR-1, PSR-5, FID, CLIP) while remaining within 1–2% on ESR. On the NSFW task, AGE achieves the best NER at every threshold (e.g., 1.53% at 0.5 vs. AP's 1.70% and UCE's 3.42%) and the best FID (14.20 vs. AP's 15.52), confirming the advantage generalises beyond object erasure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept erasure line of work (ESD, UCE, CA, MACE, AP) by challenging their shared assumption that a fixed generic target (empty prompt or 'a photo') is appropriate for all erasure queries. It builds most directly on AP (Bui et al., 2024b), which introduced adversarial concepts for preservation, but reframes the problem around target concept selection rather than preservation, and introduces a minimax optimization with Gumbel-Softmax to find per-concept optimal targets in continuous space. The empirical concept-graph analysis (locality, asymmetry, abnormal concepts) is a new contribution not present in prior erasure work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: adaptive target selection nearly closes the preservation gap (PSR-5 from 79.9% to 95.6% on object erasure) while maintaining competitive erasure rates, which is critical for deploying sanitized models without degrading general generation quality. Key limitations include the need for a predefined concept space (k=100 nearest concepts), the computational overhead of the inner maximization, and the observation that concept mixtures are not always smooth transitions. Open questions include whether the concept-graph locality property holds for non-ImageNet domains, very large erasure sets, or different diffusion architectures, and whether the Gumbel-Softmax continuous relaxation can be replaced by more efficient discrete search.
