## Problem

Dynamic scene reconstruction from sparse multi-view inputs requires incorporating deformation priors (e.g., rigidity, volume preservation) to generalize to unseen viewpoints and timestamps, but existing methods face an inherent trade-off: priors improve generalization yet compromise reconstruction fidelity by failing to exactly match the given input. No prior work provides a principled mechanism to integrate velocity-field-based deformation priors into simulation-free dynamic reconstruction models while preserving high-fidelity reconstructions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a collection of RGB images F_t = {I_i^t} captured at T time steps from M viewing directions, along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces rendered images from any novel viewing direction d ∈ S² at any continuous timestamp t ∈ [t₁, t_T]. The ReMatching loss can be applied at two levels: on the time-dependent 3D Gaussian center positions (particle-level, V = R^{n×d}) or on rendered image pixel colors (image-level, V = C¹(R²)), with the image-space variant evaluated at a fixed viewpoint through time. The model is initialized with 100K 3D Gaussians and trained for 40K iterations at 800×800 resolution on D-NeRF.

## Method

The ReMatching framework formulates deformation priors as classes of velocity fields (divergence-free, rigid, directionally restricted, and adaptive combinations thereof) and introduces a two-step alternating projection loss that mimics the Alternating Projections Method. First, it solves a matching problem to find the closest velocity field u_t in the prior class P to the reconstruction flow, using the continuity equation as the matching criterion (avoiding explicit ODE integration). Second, it applies a flow-matching loss (L_RM) that drives the reconstruction function ψ_t to match u_t, effectively projecting back onto the reconstruction flow set. All prior classes use linear parameterizations (e.g., skew-symmetric matrices for rigid motion, curl-based divergence-free basis functions for volume preservation), reducing the matching sub-problem to a closed-form least-squares solve. An adaptive-combination scheme with learnable spatial weights w_j(x,t) allows piece-wise assignment of different prior classes to different scene regions, with an entropy regularization to encourage clean part segmentation. The framework is instantiated on a Gaussian Splatting model with MLP-based time-dependent deformation (following D3G), and the total training loss is L = L_REC + λ·L_RM with λ = 0.001.

## Results

On the D-NeRF synthetic benchmark (8 scenes), the ReMatching framework with the adaptive-combination prior P_IV achieves the best PSNR across all 8 scenes, e.g., 41.61 on Bouncing Balls, 41.69 on Hell Warrior, and 38.38 on T-Rex, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline D3G by 0.1–0.5 dB. On the HyperNeRF real-world dataset (5 scenes), Ours(P_IV) achieves the best PSNR on 4 of 5 scenes (e.g., 25.28 on Slice Banana, 26.80 on Chicken, 32.67 on Split Cookie), with a small loss on Lemon (28.30 vs. D3G's 28.65). On the Dynamic Scenes dataset, Ours-P_IV improves PSNR over D3G on all 4 scenes (e.g., 26.66 vs. 26.01 on Truck, 23.63 vs. 23.12 on Jumping).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against D3G (the strongest Gaussian-splatting baseline), Ours(P_IV) wins on all 8 D-NeRF scenes in PSNR, with the largest gains on T-Rex (+0.49 dB), Stand Up (+0.46 dB), and Hell Warrior (+0.41 dB), and a near-tie on Lego (+0.02 dB). Against GA3D, Ours(P_IV) wins on 7 of 8 D-NeRF scenes but loses slightly on Hook (37.39 vs. 37.78). On HyperNeRF, Ours(P_IV) beats D3G on 4 of 5 scenes (Slice Banana +0.41, Chicken +0.14, Split Cookie +0.06) but trails on Lemon (28.30 vs. 28.65); Ours(P_III) recovers the Torch scene with a +0.59 dB gain. K-Planes and NPG are clearly outperformed across all reported scenes, with PSNR gaps of 2–9 dB. On the Dynamic Scenes dataset, Ours-P_IV improves over D3G on all 4 scenes, with the largest gain on Truck (+0.65 dB) and Jumping (+0.51 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of flow-based 3D dynamics (Niemeyer et al., Du et al., Eisenberger et al.) and dynamic novel-view rendering (D-NeRF, Deformable 3D Gaussians, GA3D). Its key conceptual departure is reframing the integration of deformation priors as an Alternating Projections problem: rather than enforcing priors as hard constraints or soft regularization (as in prior D3G/GA3D pipelines), it introduces a ReMatching loss that projects the reconstruction flow onto a velocity-field prior class via the continuity equation, then re-projects back—mimicking APM without requiring numerical ODE integration. The framework also unifies two previously separate settings (particle-position geometry and rendered image pixels) under a single loss formulation, and introduces adaptive-combination prior classes that learn spatially-varying part assignments, going beyond the single global prior used in earlier work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent trade-off in dynamic reconstruction: priors improve generalization to unseen timestamps but often degrade fidelity on training views. The ReMatching loss offers a principled mechanism to balance this, and the framework's model-agnostic design (demonstrated on both D3G and GA3D backbones) suggests broad applicability. However, the prior classes explored (rigid, volume-preserving, directional-restricted) remain relatively simple, and the paper itself identifies richer physical phenomena (liquids, gases) and video-generative-model-derived priors as open directions. The modest but consistent gains (~0.1–0.5 dB PSNR) across synthetic and real-world benchmarks suggest the approach is sound but not yet transformative; its larger value may lie in providing a general template for incorporating future, more expressive priors into dynamic reconstruction pipelines.
