## Problem

Continuous-discrete state space models (CD-SSMs) for irregular time series typically rely on sequential Bayesian recursion (filtering/smoothing) to approximate the posterior path measure, incurring computational cost that scales linearly with the number of observations. Moreover, the Doob's h-transform that characterizes the conditioned SDE dynamics is intractable in general, and prior work only handles a single terminal constraint rather than multiple marginal constraints from irregularly sampled observations. The paper asks: can one directly approximate the posterior path measure for a continuous-time SSM conditioned on multiple irregular observations, with a tight variational bound and without sequential recursion?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of discrete observations {o_{t_i}} at irregularly spaced time stamps {t_i} over an interval [0,T], where the number and spacing of observations vary per sequence (e.g., 48-hour clinical records with 41 features sampled at non-uniform intervals). An encoder maps these into low-dimensional auxiliary variables y_{t_i}, which drive the latent dynamics. The output is a full latent trajectory X_{0:T}^α over the entire interval, from which per-time-point predictions (class labels, regression values) or reconstructed values at unseen time stamps (for interpolation/extrapolation) are decoded. The model supports two assimilation modes: history-only (causal, for extrapolation) and full-sequence (non-causal, for interpolation/classification).

## Method

ACSSM constructs the posterior path measure via a multi-marginal Doob's h-transform, which extends the classical single-terminal h-transform to multiple marginal constraints from irregular observations, yielding a class of conditioned SDEs with drift b + ∇_x log h. Since h is intractable, the paper reformulates its estimation as a stochastic optimal control (SOC) problem: a control-affine SDE with a cost function combining control energy and negative log-potentials, whose optimal control α* = ∇_x log h recovers the conditioned dynamics. A tight ELBO is derived via the Hopf-Cole transformation linking the HJB equation to the linear PDE for h. For practical scalability, the drift is locally linearized as a convex combination of trainable SPD base matrices weighted by a transformer's attention output, enabling closed-form Gaussian marginal moments (simulation-free) and O(log k) parallel scan computation of all intermediate states. An encoder-decoder pair with auxiliary variables amortizes the control, allowing inference on novel sequences without re-optimization.

## Results

ACSSM achieves 91.4% accuracy on Human Activity classification (vs. 91.1% for mTAND, 90.6% for Latent-SDE_H) and 2.98×10⁻³ MSE on Pendulum regression (vs. 3.20×10⁻³ for mTAND, 3.84×10⁻³ for Latent-SDE_H). On interpolation, ACSSM achieves 0.006×10⁻² MSE on USHCN and 0.116×10⁻² on Physionet, outperforming all 9 baselines. On extrapolation, ACSSM reaches 0.941×10⁻² MSE on USHCN (vs. 1.273 for CRU) and 0.627×10⁻² on Physionet. Training runtime is 2.3 sec/epoch on USHCN and 3.8 sec/epoch on Physionet, more than 16–25× faster than CRU (122 and 114 sec/epoch respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACSSM outperforms all 9 baselines across every task and dataset. On USHCN interpolation, ACSSM (0.006×10⁻²) beats the second-best RKN-Δt (0.009×10⁻²) by 33%, and on Physionet interpolation it leads CRU (0.182×10⁻²) by 36%. For USHCN extrapolation, ACSSM (0.941×10⁻²) improves over CRU (1.273×10⁻²) by 26%, while on Physionet extrapolation it matches CRU (0.627 vs. 0.629×10⁻²). On Human Activity classification, ACSSM (91.4%) edges out mTAND (91.1%) and Latent-SDE_H (90.6%), and on Pendulum regression it achieves 2.98×10⁻³ MSE, beating mTAND (3.20×10⁻³) and Latent-SDE_H (3.84×10⁻³). The most striking advantage is computational: ACSSM trains in 2.3 sec/epoch on USHCN versus 122 sec for CRU (53× faster) and 389 sec for GRU-ODE-B (169× faster), thanks to its simulation-free linear dynamics and parallel scan computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACSSM extends the classical Doob's h-transform from single terminal constraints (Doob, 1957; Chopin et al., 2023) to multi-marginal conditioning on irregularly sampled observations, and reformulates the intractable h-function estimation as a stochastic optimal control problem via the Hopf-Cole transformation linking HJB equations to linear PDEs. This departs from prior CD-SSM approaches (CRU, Schirmer et al. 2022; Ansari et al. 2023) that rely on sequential Bayesian recursion or numerical SDE solvers, and from neural ODE/SDE methods (Latent-ODE, Latent-SDE_H) that require expensive gradient-through-simulation training. The practical design—locally linear drift with convex combinations of base matrices, transformer-based masked attention for information assimilation, and parallel scan for O(log k) marginal computation—synthesizes ideas from structured state space models (S4, S5) and variational Schrödinger diffusion (Deng et al., 2024) into a unified simulation-free framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded VI framework with a tight ELBO for continuous-time state space models under irregular observations, avoiding both the sequential bottleneck of Bayesian filtering and the numerical instability of gradient-through-SDE training. The 16–25× speedup over CRU while matching or exceeding its accuracy is practically significant for long-horizon clinical and climate time series. Open questions remain: the variational gap introduced by the locally linear drift approximation may accumulate over very long sequences (e.g., LLM-scale), and the method's reliance on high-capacity neural networks to approximate the optimal control—rather than particle-based importance weighting—limits robustness guarantees compared to SMC variants (Heng et al., 2020; Lu & Wang, 2024).
