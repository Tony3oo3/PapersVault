## Problem

Coarse segmentation masks from incomplete supervision, model predictions, or human annotation are noisy and lack fine boundary detail, making them unreliable for training downstream segmentation models. Existing refinement techniques are either model-dependent, task-specific, category-limited, or time-inefficient. While SAM is a powerful promptable segmentation foundation model, adapting it to mask refinement is non-trivial because coarse mask defects (false positives, false negatives) corrupt the prompts that SAM requires for accurate segmentation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image at its original resolution (no multi-scale strategy) and a coarse binary segmentation mask as input, producing a refined mask with improved boundary quality. For semantic segmentation, the input mask is category-wise and may contain multiple objects, which the method handles via a split-then-merge pipeline. In the SAMRefiner++ variant, the output additionally includes IoU prediction scores used to select the best mask from SAM's three candidate predictions, with the IoU head adapted via a lightweight LoRA module trained on coarse-mask IoU as a self-supervised signal.

## Method

The core contribution is a noise-tolerant multi-prompt excavation strategy that mines three complementary prompt types from the coarse mask: (1) distance-guided points selected by maximum distance to the opposite region, (2) context-aware elastic bounding boxes (CEBox) that conditionally expand the tight box based on feature similarity in SAM's latent space, and (3) Gaussian-style masks that convert the binary coarse mask into continuous-valued logits compatible with SAM's dense prompt input. A split-then-merge (STM) pipeline decomposes multi-object semantic masks into instance-level regions before prompt extraction, then re-merges adjacent regions based on box area variation and mask occupancy. SAMRefiner++ adds a minimal LoRA adaptor on SAM's IoU head trained with a pairwise ranking loss supervised by coarse-mask IoU, improving top-1 mask selection without any additional annotations. The entire framework is training-free (or minimally adapted) and operates as a generic post-processing step compatible with any segmentation model.

## Results

On DAVIS-585, SAMRefiner achieves 87.1 IoU, outperforming the best baseline CRM (82.9) by 4.2 points and the coarse mask (81.4) by 5.7 points. On COCO with PointWSSIS pseudo masks, SAMRefiner reaches 35.3 IoU versus 29.7 for SegRefiner and 28.9 for CascadePSP. On PASCAL VOC with CLIP-ES, it attains 79.3 mIoU, surpassing CRM (78.7) and CascadePSP (76.9). SAMRefiner is also the fastest method at 0.6 hours for COCO train5K, less than half the time of CRM (1.5h) and CascadePSP (3.4h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMRefiner decisively outperforms all four SOTA baselines on DAVIS-585 (87.1 vs. 82.9 CRM, 81.9 PSP, 81.0 CRF, 80.3 SegRefiner) and on all three COCO settings, with the largest margin on PointWSSIS (+5.6 over SegRefiner). On PASCAL VOC, SAMRefiner wins on BECO (+2.8 over CRM) and CLIP-ES (+0.6 over CRM) but trails SegRefiner on MaskCLIP (57.3 vs. 58.5) and DeepLabV2 (78.8 vs. 83.1), suggesting that for already high-quality full-supervision masks, diffusion-based refinement retains an edge. In the LVIS-annotated COCO val evaluation (Table 6), SAMRefiner consistently beats SegRefiner, BRP, and SegFix across all eight segmentation backbones (e.g., +3.4 APmask over SegRefiner on Mask R-CNN RN50, +3.6 on SOLO, +1.6 on Mask2Former). Dense CRF is the weakest baseline overall, often performing below the coarse mask itself on COCO (13.9 vs. 15.2 for NB), confirming its inability to handle complex instance-level defects. SAMRefiner is also the fastest method (0.6h vs. 1.0–3.4h for others) due to SAM's batch processing capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023) as its segmentation backbone but departs from prior mask refinement techniques in a fundamental way: rather than training task-specific refinement modules (PointRend, RefineMask) or dataset-specific cascade networks (CascadePSP, CRM), it repurposes SAM's prompt-driven architecture as a universal, training-free refiner. The key conceptual departure is the multi-prompt excavation strategy—distance-guided points, context-aware elastic boxes, and Gaussian-style masks—which addresses the previously unexplored challenge of extracting noise-tolerant prompts from defective coarse masks. The optional IoU adaption step (SAMRefiner++) introduces a LoRA adaptor placed specifically in the IoU head rather than the backbone, a minimal adaptation strategy that preserves SAM's zero-shot mask generation while improving mask selection on target datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAMRefiner provides a practical, drop-in post-processing tool that consistently improves pseudo-mask quality across diverse supervision regimes (unsupervised, weakly-supervised, semi-supervised) and segmentation tasks, with the potential to reduce annotation costs in the pseudo-labeling paradigm. Its batch-processing efficiency (5× faster than CascadePSP) makes it viable for large-scale dataset refinement. Key limitations remain: the method fails on extremely noisy initial masks (ill-posed problem), struggles with multi-object semantic segmentation despite the STM pipeline, and inherits SAM's sensitivity to intricate object structures. Open questions include whether fine-tuning SAM for multi-object cases would resolve remaining failures, and whether the prompting strategy generalizes to video or 3D segmentation refinement.
