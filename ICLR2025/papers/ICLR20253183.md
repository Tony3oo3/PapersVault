## Problem

Perturbation-based forward learning methods (SPSA, ES, LR) estimate gradients via Monte Carlo queries, but existing approaches allocate an equal number of queries to every data point regardless of estimation difficulty. This uniform allocation wastes computational budget on easy-to-estimate samples while under-allocating to high-variance ones, severely limiting scalability to large models and datasets. The paper asks: how to minimize gradient estimation variance under a fixed total query budget by adaptively allocating queries across data within a mini-batch?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253183/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For ViT fine-tuning, the method consumes 224×224 RGB images and produces class labels, operating only on a targeted module (e.g., a group of transformer layers) while treating the rest of the network as a frozen black box. For black-box prompt tuning on CLIP, it takes image-text pairs and outputs low-dimensional prompt tokens (intrinsic dimension 1000) projected into the frozen encoder spaces. For multimodal alignment, it takes image-text pairs and trains a linear adapter projecting CLIP visual features into the LLM token space, requiring only forward passes and scalar loss feedback—no gradient or computation-graph access to the foundation model.

## Method

The paper unifies all perturbation-based estimators (LR, SPSA, ES) under a single Monte Carlo framework and formulates query allocation as an optimization over a Gaussian Allocator whose parameters are reparameterized into just four scalars (β₀, β₁, σ, γ) using loss values and CLS-token cosine similarities as features. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that maximizing the expected performance improvement is equivalent to minimizing the expected trace of the per-data gradient covariance divided by allocated queries, making the allocator optimization independent of the unknown mean and off-diagonal covariance entries. The allocator is trained via a likelihood-ratio gradient estimator with a small number of initial probe queries per data point, and Theorem 2 provides a closed-form lower bound on the improvement over equal allocation that scales with the heterogeneity of per-data variance. The resulting OPS-LR and OPS-SPSA methods are plug-and-play accelerators that compress the effective query budget to as few as 20 per data point while outperforming equally-allocated baselines across ViT fine-tuning, black-box prompt tuning, and multimodal alignment.

## Results

On ImageNet with ViT-base, OPS-LR achieves 65.8% top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming the equally-allocated LR baseline (57.3%), DeepZero (55.6%), and Mezo with n-SPSA (54.8%), while closing the gap to the BP upper bound (80.5%). With ViT-Large, OPS-LR reaches 67.6% on ImageNet and 95.5%/90.8% on CIFAR-10/100, consistently leading all forward-learning baselines across all six datasets. The OPS-SPSA variant also improves over its equally-allocated counterpart (e.g., 60.8% vs. 55.6% on ImageNet for ViT-base), demonstrating the allocator's universality across perturbation-based estimators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPS-LR outperforms the equally-allocated LR baseline (Jiang et al., 2023) by 8.5 points on ImageNet (65.8% vs. 57.3%) and by 4.5–7.4 points on other datasets for ViT-base, confirming that adaptive query allocation is the key differentiator. Against DeepZero (Chen et al., 2023), which uses SPSA with 192 queries, OPS-LR gains 10.2 points on ImageNet (65.8% vs. 55.6%) while OPS-SPSA gains 5.2 points (60.8% vs. 55.6%), showing the allocator works across estimator families. Mezo (Malladi et al., 2023) with only 2 queries per data point collapses to near-random performance (9.3% on ImageNet), while its n-SPSA variant (54.8%) is still 11 points below OPS-LR. The BP upper bound (Rumelhart et al., 1986) remains 14.7 points above OPS-LR on ImageNet for ViT-base, indicating a remaining gap, though OPS-LR narrows this to 14.1 points for ViT-Large. In the black-box prompt tuning setting (Table 2), OPS-LR achieves 65.1% on ImageNet, surpassing Manual Prompting (Radford et al., 2021) by 7.2 points and the equally-allocated LR by 2.6 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the two main families of perturbation-based forward learning—likelihood ratio (LR) estimators and SPSA/evolutionary strategy (ES) estimators—under a single Monte Carlo sampling framework, then introduces a new sub-problem: how to allocate a fixed query budget across data points in a mini-batch. Prior work (DeepZero, Mezo) assigns queries uniformly, ignoring data-specific estimation difficulty; here the authors formulate the allocation as a constrained optimization problem and solve it with a reparameterized Gaussian allocator whose gradient is itself estimated via the likelihood ratio trick. The reparameterization (linear mean in loss, RBF kernel covariance in embedding similarity) draws on data-pruning and Bayesian optimization literature, reducing the allocator to just four learnable parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Forward learning is the only viable training paradigm when the model is a black box (third-party APIs, hardware with no backward pass, non-differentiable physics), yet its scalability has been bottlenecked by the large number of Monte Carlo queries required per data point. By adaptively concentrating queries on high-variance data, the method compresses the per-data budget to as few as 20 queries while outperforming equal-allocation baselines across ViT fine-tuning, CLIP prompt tuning, and multimodal adapter alignment. The gap with backpropagation remains substantial (e.g., 65.8% vs 80.5% on ImageNet for ViT-base), and the authors acknowledge closing it as an open challenge; integrating efficient attention (Flash Attention) to reduce the O(n²) memory cost of many queries is also left for future work.
