## Problem

In model-based reinforcement learning, accumulated model errors during rollouts distort the synthetic data distribution, degrading policy learning and limiting rollout horizons. The standard Trajectory Sampling mechanism does not explicitly separate aleatoric process noise from epistemic parameter uncertainty, causing overestimated variance that compounds over time. The paper addresses three questions: how to propagate rollouts so the data distribution matches the environment, how to quantify the resulting data corruption, and when to terminate rollouts.

## Input / Output

At each rollout step, the mechanism consumes the current state s_t, an action a_t sampled from the policy, and E ensemble model members (AES models) that each produce a mean and covariance prediction. It outputs a corrected Infoprop state whose marginal distribution provably equals the estimated environment distribution, a binary termination signal triggered when per-step entropy exceeds λ₁ or accumulated entropy exceeds λ₂, and the running sum of marginal entropies as a corruption metric. The thresholds λ₁ and λ₂ are n_S-dimensional vectors computed as quantiles of single-step Infoprop entropies evaluated on the environment replay buffer, allowing per-dimension termination.

## Method

Infoprop decomposes the model state into a signal fraction (environment dynamics) and a noise fraction (epistemic uncertainty) by fusing E ensemble predictions via covariance intersection to obtain the estimated environment state, and computing the epistemic variance as the scatter of ensemble means around the fused mean. The Infoprop state is then constructed as a Kalman-style conditional expectation of the signal given a noisy model observation, which by construction yields a state distribution identical to the estimated environment distribution (Theorem 1i). Data corruption along a rollout is quantified as the accumulated marginal entropy of Infoprop states, which equals the conditional entropy of the full environment trajectory given the observed model trajectory (Theorem 1ii). Rollouts are terminated when either the single-step entropy exceeds a per-dimension threshold λ₁ (restricting usage to a sufficiently accurate subset) or the accumulated entropy exceeds λ₂ (restricting to sufficiently accurate paths). In Infoprop-Dyna, this mechanism is plugged into a Dyna-style architecture with probabilistic ensembles and a SAC agent, using pink noise exploration to expand the model's accurate region.

## Results

Infoprop-Dyna achieves state-of-the-art performance in Dyna-style MBRL across MuJoCo HalfCheetah, Walker2d, Hopper, and Ant, substantially outperforming MBPO in both data efficiency and asymptotic return while matching or exceeding MACURA. A key quantitative advantage is rollout length: Infoprop-Dyna propagates rollouts up to 100 steps (vs. 11 for MBPO and 10 for MACURA) while maintaining data consistency, as confirmed by the close match between model-generated data (D_mod) and environment data (D_env) distributions. On the 11th state dimension of Hopper, Infoprop rollouts show substantially tighter distributions around the ground truth compared to the widely spread TS-based rollouts of MBPO and MACURA, which exhibit severe outliers even at their shorter horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MBPO (Janner et al., 2019), Infoprop-Dyna substantially outperforms in both data efficiency and asymptotic performance on all four MuJoCo tasks; MBPO's fixed 11-step branched rollouts produce widely spread data distributions that degrade policy learning. Against MACURA (Frauenknecht et al., 2024), Infoprop-Dyna performs on par or slightly better in return while achieving roughly 10× longer rollouts (up to 100 steps vs. MACURA's 10-step cap) with comparable or superior data consistency, since MACURA's single-step termination criterion cannot prevent error accumulation over longer horizons. Against the model-free SAC baseline (Haarnoja et al., 2018), Infoprop-Dyna consistently achieves higher returns using only a fraction of the environment interactions, demonstrating the data-efficiency benefit of the Dyna-style architecture augmented with Infoprop rollouts. The one exception is MuJoCo Ant, where Infoprop-Dyna's average rollout length decreases over training (attributed to primacy bias in model learning), and MuJoCo Humanoid, where critic overfitting and plasticity loss cause late-training instabilities despite consistent rollout data quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Dyna-style MBRL framework of MBPO (Janner et al., 2019) and the probabilistic ensemble (AES) model architecture of Lakshminarayanan et al. (2017), while adopting the sufficiently-accurate-subset concept from MACURA (Frauenknecht et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is from the standard Trajectory Sampling (TS) rollout mechanism (Chua et al., 2018), which naively samples a single ensemble member per step and thereby conflates aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty. Infoprop reframes model rollouts as communication through a Gaussian noise channel, using covariance intersection to fuse ensemble predictions into a signal estimate and a Kalman-style conditioning step to construct the Infoprop state, with information-theoretic entropy providing principled termination criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to extend model rollouts from ~10 steps (MBPO/MACURA) to ~100 steps while maintaining data consistency with the environment distribution is a practical advance for Dyna-style MBRL, potentially reducing the need for environment interaction. However, the paper itself highlights significant open questions: naively integrating Infoprop into standard Dyna architectures leads to instabilities (especially on humanoid), critic overfitting, and primacy bias when high-quality synthetic data is used at high update-to-data ratios. Whether the information-theoretic termination framework and signal-noise decomposition transfer cleanly to other MBRL paradigms (planning, analytic gradient methods, value expansion) remains unexplored, and the authors explicitly defer regularization strategies for the Dyna integration to future work.
