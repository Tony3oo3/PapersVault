## Problem

Existing weakly supervised affordance grounding methods rely on class activation maps (CAMs) to localize action regions, but CAMs only highlight the most discriminative area and struggle when the same affordance manifests very differently across objects (e.g., 'open a bottle' vs. 'open a refrigerator'). Additionally, prior cross-view alignment strategies that pool exocentric features globally or via CAM-guided masks introduce noise from backgrounds and human bodies, preventing effective transfer of interaction knowledge to static object images.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single egocentric RGB image of a static object (224×224) and a free-form text affordance query (open-vocabulary, not restricted to a fixed label set) and produces a 224×224 affordance heatmap indicating the region on the object that supports the queried action. During training, each egocentric image is paired with one exocentric human-object interaction image sharing the same object and affordance class, but only image-level category labels are provided—no pixel-level annotations are used.

## Method

The core pipeline converts the weakly supervised problem into a pseudo-fully supervised one by mapping each (object, affordance) pair to a part name (e.g., 'hold knife' → 'handle of the knife'), then using VLpart for open-vocabulary part detection and SAM for segmentation to generate pixel-level pseudo labels on egocentric images. The model architecture combines a CLIP ViT-B/16 visual encoder, a frozen CLIP text encoder, a 4-block cross-modal fuser (text as query, image as key/value), and a SAM-style mask decoder with two-way transformer blocks and deconvolutions. Three enhancements refine the baseline: (1) a label refinement stage that exploits human occlusion in exocentric images via a mask-guided semantic similarity loss to correct sub-optimal pseudo labels, followed by SAM auto-mask post-processing; (2) fine-grained cross-view feature alignment using masked average pooling restricted to the target object (localized by VLpart+SAM) rather than global pooling, with a stop-gradient on the exocentric branch; and (3) a lightweight reasoning module (two MLPs) that predicts the object class and target part from the visual class token and text query, embedding part-level semantic priors into the grounding feature to improve generalization to unseen object categories.

## Results

On AGD20K, the full model achieves KLD 0.890, SIM 0.510, and NSS 1.547 on the seen split, and KLD 1.153, SIM 0.437, and NSS 1.418 on the unseen split, setting new state-of-the-art among weakly supervised methods on all six metrics. The baseline (pseudo-label supervision only) already surpasses the best prior method (WSMA) by a wide margin: e.g., KLD drops from 1.176 to 0.938 (seen) and 1.335 to 1.256 (unseen). The full model further improves over the baseline by roughly 5–10% on each metric, with the largest gains on the unseen split (KLD 1.256→1.153, NSS 1.346→1.418).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six weakly supervised baselines (Cross-View-AG, Cross-View-AG+, LOCATE, WSMA, Strategies, WorldAfford) on every metric for both seen and unseen splits. Against the strongest prior method WSMA, the full model reduces KLD by 24% (1.176→0.890) and increases SIM by 23% (0.416→0.510) on the seen split, and reduces KLD by 14% (1.335→1.153) and increases NSS by 16% (1.220→1.418) on the unseen split. The gap is even larger versus the original Cross-View-AG: KLD drops from 1.538 to 0.890 (seen) and 1.787 to 1.153 (unseen), while NSS nearly doubles from 0.927 to 1.547 (seen). The method also beats zero-shot approaches (AffordanceCLIP, OVAL-Prompt) and the fully supervised AffordanceLLM on the hard split (KLD 1.395 vs. 1.661, NSS 1.151 vs. 0.947), though it remains below the few-shot OOAL method (KLD 0.740 seen / 1.070 unseen), which uses one ground-truth heatmap per object category during training. The paper notes that OOAL's modeling techniques (prompt tuning, DINOv2 multi-layer fusion) are orthogonal and could be combined with the proposed pipeline for further gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CAM-based paradigm in weakly supervised affordance grounding (WSAG), where prior methods (Cross-View-AG, LOCATE, WSMA) train an action classifier and rely on class activation maps as spatial predictions. Instead, it repurposes the pseudo-labeling strategy well-established in weakly supervised semantic segmentation (WSSS) to the affordance domain, using VLpart and SAM to generate part-level masks guided by a manually defined affordance-to-part mapping. Architecturally, it builds on SAM's two-way Transformer mask decoder and CLIP's ViT-B/16 encoder, with a cross-modal fuser adapted from CAE's regressor block. The key conceptual contribution is bridging the gap between action semantics (verbs) and object semantics (nouns/parts) that foundation models understand natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from CAM-based to pseudo-fully supervised training yields a substantial performance gap (e.g., KLD 0.890 vs. 1.176 for the best prior method on the seen split), suggesting that the CAM bottleneck was a fundamental limitation rather than a minor one in WSAG. However, the work remains confined to the AGD20K dataset (~30K images, 36 affordance classes, 50 object classes), and the pseudo labels still require manual part-name mapping for each object-affordance pair, limiting scalability. The gap with few-shot supervised methods like OOAL (KLD 0.740) remains, and the simple two-MLP reasoning module may not generalize to truly novel object structures. Open questions include whether the part-level prior approach scales to open-vocabulary affordance-object combinations without manual mapping, and whether the cross-view alignment strategy transfers to video or 3D settings.
