## Problem

Current interactive segmentation methods lack an understanding of relative depth (order) between objects in a scene, making it difficult to separate a target from background objects at different 3D positions. Additionally, existing approaches either use dense prompt fusion (computationally expensive) or sparse prompt fusion (poor spatial alignment), and they do not explicitly separate foreground and background prompt attention, causing intermingling of target and background features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252923/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed at 1024×1024) and sequential user clicks (positive for target, negative for background) as input, along with a depth map generated by a frozen DepthAnythingV2 decoder. The image is encoded once by a frozen ViT-Base encoder and reused across all interaction rounds. Each click produces both a dense 2-channel embedding (added to image features) and a sparse C=128-dimensional embedding (used in cross-attention), with up to 48 clicks supported (24 positive, 24 negative). The output is a binary segmentation mask for the target object.

## Method

OIS introduces two novel attention modules operating on sparse click embeddings. First, order-aware attention computes an order map (absolute depth difference between each pixel and the prompt's depth location) and uses it as a soft mask in cross-attention, so that regions at similar depth to the prompt receive high attention while distant regions are suppressed via a learnable scale parameter σ. Second, object-aware attention performs foreground-background separated masked cross-attention: positive click embeddings attend only to previously predicted foreground pixels, while negative click embeddings attend only to background pixels, introducing an explicit object notion. The framework combines dense fusion (adding click embeddings directly to image features for spatial alignment) with sparse fusion (efficient cross-attention between N click embeddings and HW image tokens), replacing SegNext's costly self-attention over spatial features. The architecture uses three stacked blocks of order-level and object-level understanding modules, each containing the two attention mechanisms followed by a 2-layer MLP and LayerNorm, trained with normalized focal loss on HQSeg44K.

## Results

On HQSeg44K, OIS achieves 89.40% 1-mIoU (vs. 81.79% for SegNext, a +7.61 improvement) and 93.78% 5-mIoU, while requiring only 3.95 clicks to reach 90% mIoU (NoC90) and 7.50 clicks for 95% (NoC95). On DAVIS, OIS reaches 87.29% 1-mIoU and 92.76% 5-mIoU with NoC90 of 3.80 and NoC95 of 8.59, outperforming SegNext by 1.32 mIoU after one click. In terms of efficiency, OIS achieves 31 ms per click and 9.2 s SAT latency, roughly 2× faster than SegNext (58 ms, 20.6 s) while delivering superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OIS outperforms all 11 baselines across every reported metric on both HQSeg44K and DAVIS. Against the closest competitor SegNext (Liu et al., 2024a), OIS improves 1-mIoU by 7.61 points on HQSeg44K (89.40 vs. 81.79) and 1.32 points on DAVIS (87.29 vs. 85.97), while reducing NoC95 by ~2 clicks on both datasets and halving inference latency (31 ms vs. 58 ms SPC). Compared to SAM-based methods (SAM, HQ-SAM, MobileSAM, EfficientSAM), OIS achieves substantially higher 1-mIoU (89.40 vs. 42–53 on HQSeg44K) and fewer clicks, demonstrating that sparse prompt encoding alone is insufficient without order and object awareness. Against dense-fusion methods (RITM, FocalClick, SimpleClick, InterFormer), OIS requires 1–6 fewer clicks to reach 90% mIoU and achieves 20–53 points higher 1-mIoU. The 3D-integration baseline MM-SAM (Xiao et al., 2024), which naively adds a depth encoder, is outperformed by OIS on all DAVIS metrics (NoC90: 3.80 vs. 6.22; 1-mIoU: 87.29 vs. 51.41), confirming that order-aware attention is a more effective use of depth than direct depth fusion. HR-SAM/HR-SAM++ (Huang et al., 2024) report competitive 5-mIoU (91.84–93.32) but do not report 1-mIoU or NoF95; OIS still leads on NoC90 and NoC95 where comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

OIS builds on the SAM family of sparse prompt encoding (Kirillov et al., 2023) and SegNext's dense prompt fusion (Liu et al., 2024a), but departs from both by combining dense and sparse integration in a single framework. The object-aware attention module adapts the foreground-background separated cross-attention from Cutie (Cheng et al., 2024, video object segmentation) to interactive segmentation for the first time, while the order-aware attention draws on the learnable-scale masking idea from HODOR (Athar et al., 2022). The key conceptual departure is introducing relative depth ('order') as an explicit signal in interactive segmentation, a modality that prior methods (including MM-SAM) either ignored or fused naively without structured attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.61 mIoU gain after a single click on HQSeg44K combined with a 2× speedup over SegNext is practically significant for interactive tools where both first-click quality and latency matter. The robustness to depth-map quality (Table 6) and the finding that object-aware attention can compensate for erroneous depth predictions suggest the approach is deployable without a perfect depth estimator. Open questions include whether order information extends naturally to video interactive segmentation, whether the method generalizes to multi-object or panoptic settings, and whether the reliance on a frozen depth encoder limits adaptability to out-of-distribution scenes. Failure cases with thin occluders (Fig. 14) indicate that order-based reasoning has limits when occlusion geometry is complex.
