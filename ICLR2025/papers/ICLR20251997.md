## Problem

The arc-cosine kernel, recently adopted for kernel-based implicit surface reconstruction (Neural Splines, NKF), lacks adjustable parameters, is non-stationary (not translation invariant), and offers no mechanism to tune reconstruction smoothness or frequency content. This paper asks whether a family of stationary, parameterized kernels can replace it while enabling tunable, scalable, and theoretically grounded surface reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251997/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an oriented point cloud of m points with per-point normals (and optionally per-point RGB colors for texture reconstruction), where m ranges from 1,000 (sparse ShapeNet) to 100,000 (SRB). It solves a Kernel Ridge Regression problem to produce a scalar SDF f: R^d → R (or a 4D function f': R^d → R^4 for textured output), whose zero-level set is extracted as a mesh via Marching Cubes. Gradients are approximated with finite differences using a fixed offset ε=0.005 along normals, doubling the effective point set to 2m.

## Method

The core contribution is substituting the arc-cosine kernel with the Matérn kernel family k_ν(x,y) = Φ_ν(‖x−y‖), parametrized by smoothness ν (controlling differentiability) and bandwidth h (controlling spatial scale), within the standard KRR surface reconstruction framework. Matérn kernels are stationary, spatially decaying (enabling sparse Gram matrices and scalable solvers like FALKON), and unify the Laplace (ν=1/2) and Gaussian (ν→∞) kernels. Theoretically, the paper proves that the NTK of an infinite-width two-layer SIREN network equals a Matérn kernel when bottom-layer weights are drawn from the kernel's spectral density, and that the Laplace kernel equals the arc-cosine kernel up to an affine transformation on the hypersphere. Practically, the bandwidth h acts as a tunable spectral-bias control analogous to Fourier feature mappings, and the paper derives a closed-form optimal h* from a new L₂ reconstruction error bound. For the learnable variant, Matérn kernels are plugged into the NKF framework (input-conditioned neural network before kernel evaluation), outperforming the arc-cosine NKF while training 4–5× faster.

## Results

On the Surface Reconstruction Benchmark (SRB), Matérn 3/2 achieves the best Chamfer distance (0.18) and near-best Hausdorff distance (2.93), matching NS (CD 0.17, HD 2.85) while being ~2x faster (12.47s vs 19.89s). On ShapeNet (sparse, 1,000 points), Matérn 3/2 reaches an F-Score of 94.9 and CD of 4.00, outperforming the arc-cosine kernel (92.8 / 4.67) and SPSR (84.3 / 6.26). For learned kernels, Matérn 1/2 achieves IoU 95.3 vs. NKF (94.7) and NKSR (95.6), with a >4x faster training time. For texture reconstruction on GSO, Matérn 3/2 attains PSNR 19.61 vs. arc-cosine 19.34 at 10,000 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Matérn 3/2 outperforms the arc-cosine kernel (NS) on ShapeNet by ~2 F-Score points (94.9 vs. 92.8) and ~0.67 CD (4.00 vs. 4.67), and matches NS on SRB (CD 0.18 vs. 0.17) while being significantly faster (12.47s vs. 19.89s). Against SPSR, Matérn 3/2 improves CD by ~0.03 on SRB and F-Score by ~10.5 points on ShapeNet. Among kernel-free methods, Matérn 3/2 beats SIREN (CD 0.18 vs. 0.19, HD 2.93 vs. 3.86), VisCo (HD 2.93 vs. 2.95), DiGS (HD 2.93 vs. 3.55), FFN, SAP, and OG-INR on both CD and HD. For learned kernels, Matérn 1/2 surpasses NKF (IoU 95.3 vs. 94.7) and approaches NKSR (95.3 vs. 95.6) with >4x faster training; in the extreme out-of-category generalization setting (train on chairs only), Matérn 1/2 achieves the best IoU (93.4) vs. NKF (92.8) and NKSR (89.6). Matérn 5/2 and the Gaussian kernel (ν→∞) perform substantially worse, confirming that excessive smoothness hurts reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of kernel-based surface reconstruction and neural network theory. It builds directly on Neural Splines (Williams et al., 2021), which introduced the first-order arc-cosine kernel for implicit surface reconstruction, and Neural Kernel Fields (Williams et al., 2022), which made that kernel learnable via an input-conditioned network. The key departure is replacing the non-stationary, parameter-free arc-cosine kernel with the Matérn family—stationary, spatially decaying kernels with explicit smoothness (ν) and bandwidth (h) parameters—and establishing, for the first time, that Matérn kernels are the Neural Tangent Kernels of infinite-width SIREN networks, thereby unifying two previously separate lines of work on overcoming spectral bias in coordinate-based representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a two-line PyTorch implementation of the Laplace kernel (Matérn ν=1/2) nearly matches the highly engineered NKSR in noise-free reconstruction while training over five times faster, and the bandwidth parameter h provides a principled, theoretically grounded knob for controlling reconstruction detail. The theoretical connections—Matérn kernels as SIREN NTKs, and the Laplace/arc-cosine equivalence on the sphere—give the field a cleaner conceptual framework for understanding why these kernels work. Open questions include whether the optimal bandwidth h can be learned automatically per-shape (the paper only does a small manual sweep), whether the SIREN-NTK connection can be exploited to design better hybrid architectures, and how well these results extend to unoriented or extremely sparse point clouds beyond the 1,000-point regime tested here.
