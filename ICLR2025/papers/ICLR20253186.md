## Problem

While it is empirically known that different features (frequency modes, class labels) emerge at different times during generative diffusion, there has been no precise theoretical characterization of the spectral structure of the score function's Jacobian that reveals the dimensionality and geometry of the underlying data manifold. In particular, the temporal evolution of spectral gaps—separations in the eigenvalue spectrum that mark sub-manifold boundaries—remained unexplained, and it was unclear how this geometry relates to the manifold overfitting pathology that limits likelihood-based generative models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained diffusion score network, a data point sampled from the dataset, and a diffusion time parameter t. It produces the ordered singular value spectrum of the score's Jacobian at that (x, t) pair, from which spectral gaps are read off to estimate local manifold dimensionality and identify which of three geometric phases (trivial, coverage, consolidation) is active. A key implementation detail is that perturbations for the finite-difference Jacobian estimate are drawn from a Gaussian and then orthogonalized, which suppresses the anomalous slope in the ordered spectrum that can obscure intermediate gaps.

## Method

The paper derives the full eigenvalue distribution of the score Jacobian J_t for diffusion on random linear manifolds using the replica method from statistical physics, treating both an isotropic single-variance case (yielding a Marchenko-Pastur-derived spectrum with an explicit gap formula) and a multi-variance case with hierarchically separated subspaces. From these spectral densities, closed-form expressions are obtained for the opening, maximum-width, and closure times of both intermediate gaps (between subspaces of different variance) and the final manifold gap, with the maximum-visibility time scaling as t_max = O(σ_k · σ_{k+1}). This spectral analysis reveals three qualitatively distinct phases of generation: a trivial isotropic phase at large t, a manifold coverage phase where intermediate gaps are open and the score is sensitive to the internal density ρ(x), and a consolidation phase at small t where the score becomes orthogonal to the manifold and projects particles onto its support. The theory is validated by training diffusion networks (PixelCNN++ backbone for images, residual MLP for linear data) on MNIST, CIFAR-10, CelebA, and synthetic linear manifolds, then measuring Jacobian spectra at multiple diffusion times using an improved orthogonalized SVD procedure that resolves intermediate gaps invisible to the standard method of Stanczuk et al. (2022).

## Results

The paper derives closed-form spectral gap formulas for diffusion on linear manifolds, showing the final gap width evolves as Δ_GAP(t) = σ²(1+α_m^{-1/2})² / (t + σ²(1+α_m^{-1/2})²) and the intermediate gap is maximally visible at t_max = √(f(1-f))(1-√(1/(fα_m)))(1+√(1/((1-f)α_m)))σ₁σ₂. Experiments on linear synthetic data (d=100, m=40) confirm that higher-variance subspaces open their spectral gaps first, with good quantitative agreement between replica-method predictions and trained-network singular values. On natural images, all three geometric phases are observed: a flat spectrum at large t (trivial), multiple simultaneous gap openings at intermediate t (coverage), and a single sharpening manifold gap at small t (consolidation), with MNIST yielding a latent dimension m≈100 consistent with prior estimates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Stanczuk et al. (2022), who introduced the use of Jacobian singular values to estimate manifold dimensionality but only analyzed the consolidation phase (small t). This paper extends the analysis to all diffusion times and introduces an orthogonalized perturbation method (Algorithm 1) that replaces the standard SVD procedure. As shown in Fig. 9, the standard method produces an anomalous slope in the ordered singular value plot that obscures intermediate gaps, while the orthogonalized version clearly reveals them on both CelebA and MNIST. The paper's theoretical framework (random matrix theory + replica method) provides quantitative predictions for gap opening/closing times that are validated against trained networks, going beyond the purely empirical dimensionality estimation of Stanczuk et al. The authors note a residual discrepancy in the spectral shape near the Dirac-delta spike at r=-1, attributing it to the final configuration of the trained network rather than a failure of the theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral-gap analysis of Stanczuk et al. (2022), who first showed that the final gap in the Jacobian eigenvalue spectrum of a trained diffusion model reveals the data manifold dimensionality, by providing a full random-matrix-theory derivation of both the final and intermediate spectral gaps under the hidden manifold model of Goldt et al. (2020). It positions itself as a theoretical complement to recent empirical studies of the local linear structure of trained diffusion models (Kadkhodaie et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024b), which identified geometry-adaptive harmonic decompositions and frequency-mode separation without a closed-form spectral prediction. The use of the replica method and Marchenko-Pastur distributions from statistical physics to derive exact gap-opening timescales is a methodological departure from the purely empirical or fixed-point analyses in the diffusion literature (Biroli et al., 2024; Raya and Ambrogioni, 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-phase decomposition (trivial, manifold coverage, manifold consolidation) provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why diffusion models avoid the manifold overfitting pathology that degrades likelihood-based generators: the internal density is encoded during the coverage phase while the manifold geometry is locked in during the consolidation phase, so the two are never simultaneously optimized. A key limitation is that the theory is restricted to linear manifolds with Gaussian internal distributions; the extension to curved manifolds is argued via local linearity of the diffusive shell but remains a conjecture, and the observed discrepancy between the theoretical Dirac spike and the empirical bulk in the trained-network spectra is left unexplained. Open questions include whether the gap-timing formulas transfer to high-dimensional non-linear manifolds (e.g., natural images beyond the qualitative agreement shown), and whether the intermediate-gap structure can be exploited for controllable generation or manifold-aware sampling.
