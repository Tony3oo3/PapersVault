## Problem

Full conformal prediction (full-CP) provides distribution-free coverage guarantees while using all data for both training and calibration, but it is computationally infeasible for neural networks because it requires retraining the model for every test point and candidate label. Split conformal prediction (split-CP) avoids retraining but sacrifices statistical efficiency by withholding data from model fitting, leading to wider and less adaptive prediction intervals. The paper asks whether full-CP can be approximated at scale for neural network regression without retraining or grid search over the label space.

## Input / Output

The method operates post-hoc on a single trained neural network (fully-connected or VGG-19 CNN) and the full training dataset, requiring no held-out calibration split. Given a test feature vector, it produces a prediction interval (scalar regression) or a hyperrectangular confidence region (multi-output bounding box localization) whose width adapts to the local difficulty of the test point. The intervals are constructed in closed form from changepoints derived via Gauss-Newton influence, with no grid over candidate labels.

## Method

The core mechanism approximates the full-CP retraining step with a single Gauss-Newton influence update: starting from the trained parameters θ*, the add-one-in solution θ*⁺(y) is approximated by a rank-one perturbation involving the Gauss-Newton Hessian inverse and the test-point Jacobian. Combined with first-order network linearization, this expresses each residual as a piecewise linear function of the candidate label y, recovering the exact conformalized ridge regression (CRR) coefficient structure. The prediction set is then computed in closed form by sorting changepoints (where the test residual crosses a training residual), eliminating the need for a grid search. The method is shown to be equivalent to conformalizing the Linearized Laplace posterior, and uses studentized nonconformity scores (normalized by the square root of 1 − leverage) for best empirical performance. A 'split+refine' variant, which pre-trains on a subset and solves the linearized objective on the calibration set, provides a guaranteed-coverage fallback.

## Results

On small UCI regression datasets (yacht, boston, energy), ACP-GN produces the tightest prediction intervals among all well-calibrated methods, e.g., average width of 1.594 vs. 2.553 (SCP) and 1.690 (LA) on yacht at 90% coverage, while maintaining valid coverage (87.4% vs. target 90%). On the bounding box localization task (Oxford-IIIT Pet, VGG-19), ACP-GN (split+refine) achieves the smallest confidence region volume (0.311×10⁻² at 85% target) compared to SCP (0.809×10⁻²) and CRF (0.713×10⁻²), a ~2.6× reduction. On larger datasets (bike, protein, facebook_2), ACP-GN remains competitive on width but occasionally miscovers; the split+refine variant restores valid coverage at a modest efficiency cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Linearized Laplace (LA), ACP-GN produces tighter intervals on all small datasets (e.g., 1.594 vs. 1.690 width on yacht at 90%) while LA frequently miscovers (marked ✗ at 95% and 99% on yacht, energy, bike, protein, facebook_2). Against split conformal prediction (SCP), ACP-GN consistently achieves 20–40% narrower intervals on small datasets (e.g., 2.385 vs. 4.001 on yacht at 95%) and remains competitive on large datasets (98.8 vs. 131.1 on bike at 90%), though ACP-GN occasionally miscovers on the larger sets. Against conformalized residual fitting (CRF), ACP-GN is tighter across all settings, and the proposed SCP-GN variant also outperforms CRF (e.g., 3.349 vs. 3.947 on yacht at 95%) without requiring an additional residual-prediction network. Against conformalized quantile regression (CQR), ACP-GN is substantially tighter (e.g., 2.385 vs. 5.845 on yacht at 95%; 130.9 vs. 167.7 on bike at 95%). Against the prior approximate full-CP method (ACP, Martinez et al. 2023), ACP-GN achieves tighter intervals (e.g., 1.467 vs. 1.706 on energy at 90%) while eliminating the need for a target-space grid search entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformalized ridge regression (CRR) framework from exact linear models to neural networks by replacing the exact rank-1 Hessian update with a Gauss-Newton influence approximation, thereby recovering the same piecewise-linear residual structure that eliminates the need for grid search over candidate labels. It builds directly on Martinez et al. (2023), who used influence functions to approximate full-CP retraining but were restricted to classification (requiring label discretization); here, the combination of Newton-step influence with network linearization extends the approach to continuous regression targets. The method is further unified with Linearized Laplace (Khan et al., 2019; Immer et al., 2021b), showing that ACP-GN is equivalent to conformalizing the Laplace-GGN posterior, placing it within the Conformal Bayes framework for recalibrating Bayesian intervals under misspecification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that full-CP—long considered infeasible for deep learning due to per-label retraining—becomes tractable with a single training run and O(N log N) per-test-point cost, yielding prediction intervals that are both statistically efficient (using all data) and locally adaptive. However, the coverage guarantee is only approximate (not distribution-free), and the paper acknowledges miscoverage on larger datasets, motivating the 'split+refine' variant that trades some efficiency for guaranteed validity. Open questions include tightening theoretical bounds on the Gauss-Newton approximation error, scaling to architectures where even KFAC is prohibitive, and extending to tasks like pose estimation and tracking where multi-output structure is more complex.
