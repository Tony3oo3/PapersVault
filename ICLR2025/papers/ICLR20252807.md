## Problem

Existing satisficing bandit algorithms (e.g., SAT-UCB) derive regret bounds that scale inversely with the satisficing gap—the separation between the threshold and the best non-satisficing arm. In continuous or large arm spaces such as Lipschitz and concave bandits, this gap can be zero, rendering the bounds vacuous. The paper asks whether constant satisficing regret is achievable for general bandit optimization problems without relying on this gap.

## Input / Output

SELECT is a template that wraps any black-box learning oracle ALG with sub-linear standard regret (e.g., UCB, Thompson Sampling, or discretized UCB for Lipschitz bandits). The learner specifies the arm set X, reward class R, satisficing threshold S, and time horizon T; the algorithm outputs a sequence of arm pulls and guarantees either a constant satisficing regret (when a satisficing arm exists) or the same standard regret as the oracle (when no satisficing arm exists). The key design parameter is the oracle's regret exponent α ∈ [1/2, 1), which controls the round schedule and the rate at which the algorithm converges to satisficing arms.

## Method

SELECT operates in rounds indexed by i, with a shrinking scale γ_i = 2^{-i(1-α)/α}. In each round, it (1) runs the oracle for t_i = ⌈2^{i/α}⌉ steps and uniformly samples an arm from the oracle's trajectory as a candidate; (2) performs forced sampling by pulling that candidate for T_i = ⌈γ_i^{-2}⌉ additional steps to tighten the confidence interval; and (3) monitors the lower confidence bound (LCB) of the candidate's mean reward, terminating the round the moment the LCB drops below S. The critical design choice is using LCB rather than UCB or the empirical mean for the test: this ensures that a non-satisficing arm is rejected within one additional pull in expectation, eliminating the 1/Δ_S dependence that plagues prior methods. The resulting satisficing regret scales with the exceeding gap Δ_S* = r(X*) − S (the margin by which the optimal arm exceeds the threshold) rather than the satisficing gap, yielding a constant bound in the realizable case and oracle-matching standard regret in the non-realizable case.

## Results

Theoretically, SELECT achieves a constant satisficing regret of O((1/Δ*_S)^{α/(1-α)} · polylog(C₁/Δ*_S)) in the realizable case (Theorem 1), independent of the time horizon T, and the same sub-linear standard regret O(C₁T^α · polylog(T)) as the oracle in the non-realizable case (Theorem 2). Numerically, across finite-armed (4 arms), concave (1D), and Lipschitz (2D) bandit settings with T ranging from 500 to 5000, SELECT consistently achieves constant satisficing regret in the realizable cases (S=0.93, 0.3, 0.5 respectively) while matching the oracle's standard regret in the non-realizable cases (S=1.5). The key practical advantage is that the satisficing regret bound depends on the exceeding gap Δ*_S = r(X*)−S rather than the satisficing gap Δ_S, which can be zero for continuous-arm problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAT-UCB (Michel et al., 2023), SELECT achieves lower satisficing regret in the realizable case across all three bandit settings (finite-armed, concave, Lipschitz), and also lower standard regret in the non-realizable case. Against SAT-UCB+ (a heuristic with no regret guarantee from the same paper), SELECT achieves comparable satisficing regret in the finite-armed realizable case but clearly outperforms it in the concave and Lipschitz realizable settings where SAT-UCB+ fails to converge due to repeatedly pulling near-threshold non-satisficing arms. Against Thompson Sampling (Agrawal & Goyal, 2017) used as the oracle in finite-armed bandits, SELECT matches the standard regret in the non-realizable case while achieving constant (vs. growing) satisficing regret in the realizable case. Against the convex bandit algorithm of Agarwal et al. (2011) in the concave setting, SELECT converges to satisficing arms faster and incurs smaller satisficing regret, while reaching a comparable distribution in the non-realizable case. Against the uniformly discretized UCB of Bubeck et al. (2011) in the Lipschitz setting, SELECT again converges faster with smaller satisficing regret in the realizable case and performs best in the non-realizable case where the other baselines show similar performance to each other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the satisficing bandit framework of Michel et al. (2023) and Garivier et al. (2019), which were limited to finite-armed settings and relied on uniform exploration to identify satisficing candidates. SELECT departs from these works in two key ways: it replaces uniform exploration with oracle-based trajectory sampling (making it applicable to large or infinite arm spaces), and it replaces UCB/empirical-mean testing with LCB testing, which is shown to be essential for achieving constant satisficing regret without dependence on the satisficing gap Δ_S. The introduction of the 'exceeding gap' Δ_S* as the relevant problem parameter, rather than Δ_S, is what enables non-vacuous bounds for continuous-arm problems like concave and Lipschitz bandits where Δ_S = 0.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SELECT provides the first general template for satisficing regret minimization that extends beyond finite-armed bandits to continuous-arm optimization problems, filling a gap where prior satisficing bounds were vacuous. The LCB-based testing mechanism is a clean conceptual contribution: it shows that the standard UCB intuition from bandit literature is actually the wrong tool for satisficing, and that a more conservative lower-bound test is what enables gap-free constant regret. Open questions include extending to non-stationary settings and understanding the interplay between satisficing regret and exploration in limited-adaptivity regimes, both of which the authors flag as future directions.
