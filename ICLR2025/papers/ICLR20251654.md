## Problem

Linearizing LLMs—swapping softmax attention for subquadratic alternatives—has shown promise for efficient inference, but prior methods (SUPRA, Mamba-in-Llama) degrade quality by 23–28 points on 5-shot MMLU, require 20–100B training tokens, update all model parameters, and have only been validated up to 8B models. The paper asks whether linearizing can be made quality-preserving, parameter- and token-efficient, and scalable to 70B+ LLMs by simply reducing architectural differences rather than performing full-model retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained decoder-only Transformer LLM (Llama 3 8B, Mistral 7B, Llama 3.1 70B/405B) and produces a linearized variant with subquadratic attention layers. During training, natural language sequences (Alpaca 50K samples or RedPajama, packed to 1024 tokens) are fed through the frozen original model to extract per-layer attention inputs and outputs, which supervise the feature-map learning. At inference, the linearized model consumes text tokens and generates text tokens with O(n) time and O(1) memory per generated token (fixed-size KV state instead of a growing KV cache), supporting 3× throughput and 64× batch sizes over FlashAttention-2 at 4096-token generation.

## Method

LoLCATs is a two-step procedure. Step 1 (Attention Transfer) trains learnable feature maps φ_q, φ_k for a hybrid sliding-window + linear attention layer to match the original softmax attention outputs via a per-layer MSE loss, using teacher-forced hidden states so errors do not propagate; for large models, layers are split into blocks of size b and trained independently to address the finding that jointly trained later layers accumulate 200× the MSE of earlier ones. Step 2 (Low-rank Adjusting) swaps in the trained linear attentions and applies LoRA (rank 8, α=16) only to the W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o projection matrices, updating <0.09% of parameters via next-token prediction. The key architectural insight is that combining a short sliding window (size 64) of softmax attention with linear attention over all prior tokens consistently reduces attention-matching MSE compared to pure linear attention, while remaining subquadratic overall. This combination of attention transfer + LoRA with the hybrid architecture closes 79.8–86.6% of the Transformer quality gap on 7–8B models and 77.8–78.1% on 70B/405B models, using only 40M tokens and 0.2% of prior methods' trainable parameters.

## Results

On LM Eval (6 tasks), LoLCATs-linearized Llama 3 8B achieves 70.7 average accuracy (74.2 excluding MMLU), closing 86.6% of the gap to the original Transformer (72.0/73.1), while using only 40M training tokens and updating <0.2% of parameters. On 5-shot MMLU, LoLCATs Llama 3 8B reaches 52.8%, a +9.6 point improvement over the next-best fully subquadratic model (Mamba2-Llama3 at 43.2%) and +28.6 points over prior linearizing with Hedgehog (24.2%). At scale, LoLCATs produces the first linearized 70B and 405B LLMs, closing 77.8% and 78.1% of the 5-shot MMLU gap to the original Llama 3.1 Transformers respectively, using under half the GPU-hours of prior methods for 8B linearizing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoLCATs outperforms all 14 baselines on average LM Eval accuracy at the 7B scale. Against prior linearizing methods, LoLCATs Llama 3 8B (70.7 avg) beats Mamba2-Llama3 (61.9, Wang et al.) by 8.8 points and Hedgehog (55.7, Zhang et al.) by 15.0 points, while using 500–2500× fewer tokens (40M vs. 20–100B). Against pretrained subquadratic LLMs, LoLCATs Mistral 7B (70.7 avg) surpasses Zamba 7B (69.5, Glorioso et al.) by 1.2 points and Griffin 7B (65.8, De et al.) by 4.9 points, despite training on only 0.04B tokens versus 300–1420B. On 5-shot MMLU specifically, LoLCATs Llama 3 8B (52.8) leads Zamba (57.7) by a smaller margin but still exceeds Mamba 7B (33.3, Gu & Dao), RWKV-6 (–), TransNormer (43.1, Qin et al.), StripedHyena (26.0, Poli et al.), and SUPRA (34.2, Mercat et al.) by 18.5–26.8 points. At the 1B scale, LoLCATs-linearized Phi 1.5 1.3B (67.2 avg no-MMLU) matches MOHAWK-distilled Phi-Mamba 1.5 (66.0, Bick et al.) while using 1.33% of its training tokens, and outperforms xLSTM 1.4B (58.6, Beck et al.), GLA 1.3B (51.9, Yang et al.), and DeltaNet 1.3B (52.1, Yang et al.) by 5.6–15.3 points. The only near-tie is with Zamba 7B on average (70.7 vs. 69.5), where Zamba retains a 4.9-point edge on 5-shot MMLU (57.7 vs. 52.8) but LoLCATs leads on all other tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2024 line of work on linearizing existing Transformer LLMs (Mercat et al., Wang et al., Bick et al.) by replacing their full-parameter fine-tuning stage with a two-step procedure: (1) 'attention transfer,' where learnable linear attention feature maps (building on Hedgehog and T2R) are trained via MSE to match the original softmax attention outputs, and (2) minimal LoRA adjustment on attention projections only. Architecturally, it introduces a hybrid sliding-window + linear attention layer (inspired by Longformer and Griffin) that remains subquadratic while better approximating softmax. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods assumed full-model updates were necessary to recover quality after swapping attention mechanisms, whereas LoLCATs shows that reducing the architectural mismatch upfront (via attention transfer) makes low-rank adaptation sufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LoLCATs makes LLM linearizing dramatically more accessible: 0.2% of prior methods' parameters and 0.04–0.2% of their tokens, enabling 7B linearization on a single 40GB GPU and, for the first time, viable 70B and 405B linearizations (50× the scale of prior work). The remaining ~14-point MMLU gap at 8B and the open question of whether linearizing preserves safety guardrails in instruction-tuned models are important limitations. The block-by-block training strategy and the finding that later layers accumulate 200× the attention-matching MSE of earlier layers suggest that layer-wise distillation dynamics in deep models remain an active area for further study.
