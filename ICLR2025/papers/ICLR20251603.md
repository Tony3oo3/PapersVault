## Problem

Prior work demonstrated that universal text jailbreaks transfer between language models (GCG) and adversarial images transfer between image classifiers, raising the question of whether gradient-based universal image jailbreaks would similarly transfer between vision-language models. No systematic large-scale study had assessed this transferability across a diverse set of VLMs with varying vision backbones, language models, and training recipes, leaving the threat landscape for black-box VLMs unclear.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512×3 RGB image (initialized as uniform random noise or a natural photograph) and a set of harmful-yet-helpful prompt-response text pairs from one of three datasets (AdvBench, Anthropic HHH, or a newly generated 48k-pair dataset). Gradient descent optimizes the image pixels to minimize the joint negative log-likelihood of the target responses across one or more frozen VLMs. The output is a single optimized adversarial image that, when fed alongside diverse harmful prompts, elicits harmful-yet-helpful text responses; success is measured via cross-entropy loss and a Claude 3 Opus Likert-scale harmfulness-yet-helpfulness score.

## Method

The paper conducts a large-scale empirical study using 40+ open-parameter VLMs from the Prismatic suite plus 18 newly constructed VLMs spanning 6 language backbones (Vicuna, Llama 2/3, Gemma, Mistral, Phi 3) and 3 vision backbones (CLIP, SigLIP, DINOv2+SigLIP). A single image is optimized via Adam (lr=1e-3, 50k steps, effective batch size 8) against one or more frozen VLMs using a product-of-probabilities loss over prompt-response pairs. The key experimental design sweeps over attack ensemble size (1, 2, 8 VLMs), shared vs. distinct vision/language backbones, instruction-following and safety-alignment status, training stages (1-stage vs. 2-stage), training data overlap, and training checkpoint proximity. The central finding is that image jailbreaks are universal against attacked VLMs but exhibit essentially zero transfer to non-attacked VLMs, with partial transfer only between identically-initialized VLMs differing in training data or optimizer steps, and strong transfer only when attacking large ensembles of 'highly similar' VLMs.

## Results

Across 40+ open-parameter VLMs, gradient-optimized image jailbreaks consistently achieve ~87.5% harmful-yet-helpful scores (Claude 3 Opus metric) on the attacked VLM(s) but exhibit little-to-no transfer to any other VLM, regardless of shared vision backbones, language models, or safety-alignment training. Partial transfer (40–60% vs. ~15% baseline) is observed only between identically-initialized VLMs with overlapping training data or between nearby training checkpoints of the same VLM (3-epoch checkpoint drops to ~40%). Attacking ensembles of 8 'highly similar' VLMs achieves near-ceiling transfer to a target VLM, while attacking 8 diverse VLMs yields zero transfer outside the ensemble. Jailbreaking an ensemble of 8 VLMs requires approximately the same number of gradient steps (<500) as jailbreaking a single VLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new attack method to benchmark against baselines; rather, it is a large-scale empirical study that systematically tests transferability across 40+ VLMs. Its findings directly contradict Niu et al. (2024), who claimed successful transfer to black-box VLMs; the authors argue Niu et al.'s success is an artifact of their scoring methodology (counting non-refusal as success, including nonsense outputs, and lacking baseline refusal rates). The results align with Bailey et al. (2023) and Qi et al. (2024a), who also found limited or no transfer, and with Chen et al. (2024b), who independently confirmed low transferability. The paper's key differentiator is scale and systematicity: it sweeps over vision backbones (CLIP, SigLIP, DINOv2+SigLIP), language backbones (Vicuna, Llama 2/3, Gemma, Mistral, Phi 3), safety-alignment status, training stages, and ensemble sizes, identifying the narrow conditions (highly similar VLMs, overlapping training data, nearby checkpoints) under which partial transfer occurs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: (1) universal and transferable text jailbreaks against language models (GCG, Zou et al., 2023) and (2) transferable adversarial examples against image classifiers (Papernot et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2016). It also builds on prior VLM jailbreaking work (Qi et al., 2024a; Bailey et al., 2023; Niu et al., 2024) that demonstrated gradient-based image attacks can jailbreak individual VLMs, but which produced conflicting and often under-specified transferability claims. The key departure is the systematic, large-scale negative result: despite the strong prior evidence from both unimodal settings, image jailbreaks against VLMs do not transfer, and the paper uses the Prismatic VLM suite (Karamcheti et al., 2024) plus 18 newly released VLMs to isolate which architectural and training factors (if any) enable partial transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that VLMs are substantially more robust to gradient-based transfer attacks than either LMs or image classifiers has direct implications for the threat model of multimodal AI systems: an attacker with white-box access to one VLM cannot trivially obtain a universal image that jailbreaks other VLMs. The degrees-of-freedom argument (images have ~10^200000 DOF vs. ~10^100 for text) offers a mechanistic explanation but leaves open whether regularization or constrained optimization could recover transferability. Open questions include whether this robustness persists against more sophisticated attacks, how to formally define 'highly similar' VLMs, and how to build detection systems for image-based jailbreaks in deployment.
