## Problem

Existing removal-based explainability methods for Vision Transformers, particularly ViT-Shapley, approximate Shapley values using a least-squares (MSE) objective that poorly captures the probabilistic nature of classifier outputs, and rely on random mask sampling that is sample-inefficient when important and unimportant patches are heavily imbalanced. This paper addresses both limitations and further explores whether a trained explainer can double as a fully explainable classifier with consistent predictions and explanations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250631/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images tokenized into 16×16 patches (ViT-B/16 with DINOv2 registers) and, during training, operates on randomly or informatively masked subsets of these patches. At inference, it produces a per-patch Shapley attribution map where each patch's importance score is φ_i = φ_i⁺ − φ_i⁻. In explainable-classifier mode, the same model outputs a class label by applying softmax (multiclass) or sigmoid (binary) to the sum of all positive Shapley values over the full unmasked image, guaranteeing prediction–explanation consistency.

## Method

Salvage trains a Segmenter-based explainer to learn separate positive (φ_i⁺) and negative (φ_i⁻) Shapley values per patch, then maps their weighted sum through softmax/sigmoid to form a Shapley probability distribution u(S) for each masked subset S. Training minimizes the Jensen-Shannon divergence between u(S) and the target classifier's actual prediction v(S), replacing ViT-Shapley's MSE-on-scalars with a proper distributional objective. To counter the imbalance between high- and low-likelihood masks, the method employs attribution-guided sampling: current importance estimates are used as weights to draw two balanced mask subsets per batch—one favoring the most informative patches and one the least—yielding a more uniform spread of prediction likelihoods. At test time, per-patch Shapley values are recovered as φ_i⁺ − φ_i⁻, and the explainer can itself classify by aggregating all φ_i⁺ over the full image, producing a fully transparent classifier whose explanation is the very basis of its prediction.

## Results

Salvage achieves the highest SRG and R-SRG scores across all four datasets: Pet (SRG 68.5, R-SRG 26.3), ImageNette (SRG 51.3, R-SRG 14.9), MURA (SRG 68.6, R-SRG 25.3), and WBC (SRG 69.7, R-SRG 22.6). On the Pet dataset with human-annotated regions, Salvage also attains the best RMA (64.9), though Attn. Rollout (74.6) and Saliency Maps (76.3) marginally exceed its RRA (73.5). The closest competitor, ViT-Shapley, trails Salvage by 7.4 SRG points on Pet and by 11.6 R-SRG points on WBC, with the gap widening substantially on the relative metrics (R-SRG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Salvage outperforms all 10 baselines on SRG and R-SRG across every dataset, with the largest margins on the relative metric R-SRG (e.g., +11.6 over ViT-Shapley on WBC, +10.1 over RISE on ImageNette). The closest competitor is ViT-Shapley, which leads Salvage by only 3.3 SRG on MURA (65.3 vs. 68.6) but trails by 12.3 R-SRG on WBC (7.3 vs. 22.6), indicating Salvage better captures relative importance differences. RISE is the second-best on Pet SRG (63.7) but produces highly noisy attribution maps and scores only 30.1 RMA, far below Salvage's 64.9. On Pet RRA, Attention Rollout (74.6) and Saliency Maps (76.3) slightly exceed Salvage (73.5), yet both methods score substantially lower on SRG and R-SRG, suggesting they rank the correct region but fail to distinguish the most decision-relevant sub-regions. CAM-based methods (GradCam, EigenCam) and gradient-based methods (IntGrad, Sal. Maps) consistently underperform, with GradCam even yielding negative SRG on ImageNette and WBC, confirming the paper's observation that these architecture-specific techniques are poorly suited to ViT-based classifiers.

## Relation to Prior Work

Salvage directly extends the removal-based explanation lineage of FastShap (Jethani et al., 2022) and ViT-Shapley (Covert et al., 2023), which train an explainer to approximate Shapley values of image patches. The paper departs from ViT-Shapley in two key ways: replacing the MSE-based scalar approximation with a Jensen-Shannon divergence loss over full probability distributions (the 'Shapley-distribution'), and substituting random mask sampling with an attribution-guided sampling strategy that rebalances the distribution of high- and low-likelihood masks. It also introduces the 'classifying by explaining' concept, where the explainer's aggregated Shapley scores directly produce a classification prediction, a framing absent from prior removal-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a Shapley-distribution explainer can serve as a fully explainable classifier with only a ~2% accuracy drop (while the MSE-based ViT-Shapley explainer collapses to near-zero accuracy) opens a path toward inherently transparent classifiers for safety-critical vision applications. The attribution-guided sampling addresses a practical training bottleneck (imbalance between informative and uninformative patches) that likely affects other removal-based methods. Open questions include whether the linear-independence assumption of Shapley values limits expressiveness for feature interactions, whether the approach generalizes beyond the four tested datasets to larger-scale benchmarks like ImageNet, and whether the explainer architecture (Segmenter) can be further refined to capture finer spatial structure.
