## Problem

Standard feedforward CNNs model V1 contextual modulation through successive convolutions and a fully connected readout, but it remains unclear whether non-local mechanisms like self-attention can further improve neural response prediction, and what distinct roles each context mechanism plays. In particular, standard evaluation metrics (e.g., Pearson correlation) fail to capture a neuron's peak tuning preference, leaving a gap in assessing how well models identify the strongest excitatory stimuli.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252173/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single grayscale natural image (50×50 px, downsampled from 100×100 px at 15 px/degree visual angle) and produces a scalar predicted calcium response (dF/F) for one macaque V1 neuron. Each neuron is modeled by its own dedicated network, and the receptive field of the center hypercolumn after the initial convolutional blocks covers approximately 13×13 px (~0.8°), matching the experimentally measured neuron receptive field size of ~0.5–0.75°.

## Method

The paper augments a baseline feedforward CNN (two α-CPBs with 5×5 kernels, two β-CPBs with 3×3 kernels, and a fully connected readout) with a single-headed self-attention layer inserted between the α- and β-CPB blocks, with parameter counts matched by reducing channels from 32 to 30. To dissect the contributions of each context mechanism, the authors construct factorized variants: rf-CNN (1×1 β-CPBs + center-only readout, no context) and rf+sa-CNN (self-attention as the sole context source). A key finding is that self-attention alone fails when trained simultaneously with the receptive field, but an incremental learning schedule—first learning the α-CPB receptive fields, then the self-attention layer, then the fully connected readout—recovers and even surpasses the full model's performance, revealing that the three context mechanisms (convolutions, self-attention, FCL) are complementary and that the FCL is critical for peak tuning while the receptive field dominates overall correlation. The authors also introduce a Peak Tuning Index (PT) metric that measures the fraction of a neuron's top-1% responses correctly captured by the model, addressing the limitation of correlation-based metrics in evaluating peak response prediction.

## Results

On macaque V1 neural response prediction (M1S1 and M2S1), ff+sa-CNN improves Pearson correlation over the parameter-matched ff-CNN baseline by +6.6% (0.393→0.416) on M1S1 and +3.3% (0.477→0.491) on M2S1. More strikingly, peak tuning improves substantially: PT_J rises from 3.3 to 5.6 (M1S1) and from 8.6 to 11.5 (M2S1), while PT_S rises from 5.6 to 10.5 and from 16.2 to 23.5, respectively. Notably, the receptive-field-only model (rf-CNN) achieves the highest overall correlation (0.420 on M1S1, 0.496 on M2S1) but the worst peak tuning (PT_J = 1.1 and 4.4), revealing a trade-off between overall tuning fit and peak capture that contextual modulation resolves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 'what and where' shared-core factorized model (Klindt et al., NeurIPS 2018) and the goal-driven VGG transfer-learning model (Yamins & DiCarlo, 2016), the paper's baseline ff-CNN achieves comparable or superior Pearson correlation and CC_norm² on both monkey datasets (Appendix A.3, Figure 8), justifying its use as the reference architecture. Against the recurrent CNN of Zhang et al. (2022), ff+sa-CNN outperforms on both correlation (0.498 vs. 0.435) and peak tuning (PT_S 19.7 vs. 5.7; PT_J 11.0 vs. 3.3) at full data (100% of M2S1), but the recurrent CNN is more data-efficient, surpassing both ff-CNN and ff+sa-CNN when training data is reduced to 25% or less (Appendix A.14). The paper's key internal comparison shows that self-attention alone (rf+sa-CNN) actually degrades performance relative to rf-CNN when trained simultaneously, but incremental learning recovers and exceeds the baseline, with ff+sa-CNN*(Incr.FC₁) reaching the highest correlation (0.430 on M1S1) while ff+sa-CNN* (Incr.FC₂) achieves the best peak tuning among incremental variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feedforward CNN paradigm for macaque V1 neural response prediction (Cadena et al., 2017; Klindt et al., 2018; Kindel et al., 2019) by inserting a single self-attention layer to model horizontal cortical connections, drawing on the non-local/self-attention literature from vision (Pan et al., 2022; Bello et al., 2020) and the observation that recurrent/horizontally-connected models improve neural prediction (Zhang et al., 2022; Nayebi et al., 2018). It deliberately departs from the large-scale transformer approach (Li et al., 2023, V1T for mouse V1) by isolating the contribution of one attention head within a compact CNN, enabling mechanistic factorization of three contextual modulation pathways (successive convolutions, self-attention, fully connected readout). The incremental learning paradigm, where receptive fields are learned before contextual modulation, is inspired by neurodevelopmental evidence that horizontal connections mature after feedforward receptive fields (Felleman & van Essen, 1991) and by continual learning literature (van de Ven et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the peak tuning index (PT) addresses a genuine gap: standard metrics like Pearson correlation and FEV are dominated by the ~99% of weakly-responding stimuli and systematically underweight the neuron's most preferred features, which are the functionally critical part of the tuning curve. The finding that the simplest model (rf-CNN, no context) achieves the highest correlation yet the worst peak tuning reframes how the field should evaluate neural models and suggests that 'better fit' in the standard sense may mask failure to capture the neuron's true selectivity. Open questions include whether the incremental learning advantage generalizes to larger architectures or other cortical areas, how the self-attention weights map onto specific biological horizontal/feedback circuits, and whether the center-surround trade-off identified here persists with multi-head attention or deeper networks. Limitations include grayscale-only inputs, single-headed attention, small model sizes, and evaluation on only two macaque V1 sites.
