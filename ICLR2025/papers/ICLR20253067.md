## Problem

When LLMs are adapted to non-linguistic domains like decision making or time series forecasting, the standard linear classification head treats all token IDs as independent, ignoring the natural continuous structure among nearby tokens (e.g., 'move left' and 'move up-left' should have similar probabilities). Parameterized distributions like Gaussians or GMMs incorporate continuity but are too rigid to capture the multi-modal distributions needed for high-quality generation. The paper asks whether a head can be both expressive enough for complex multi-modal densities and structurally biased toward smoothness over the continuous token space.

## Input / Output

The Fourier head takes a hidden state vector x ∈ R^n from any upstream encoder (transformer, MLP, etc.) and produces a categorical distribution over m bins in R^m, where the bins represent quantized values over a continuous interval (e.g., 18 discrete actions in Atari, 4096 bins over [-15,15] in time series). The output is guaranteed non-negative and sums to 1 by construction, since it is obtained by evaluating a learned continuous PDF at m bin centerpoints and normalizing. A key design detail is that the m bins need a semantically meaningful ordering (nearby bins encode similar values), which is what makes the continuity inductive bias appropriate.

## Method

The Fourier head is a drop-in replacement for the linear classification layer that constructs a categorical distribution as the discretization of a learned continuous Fourier-series PDF over [-1,1]. Concretely, a linear layer maps the input to 2(N+1) autocorrelation coefficients, which are converted to Fourier coefficients via a specific product formula; these define a 2-periodic PDF that is guaranteed to integrate to 1 and be non-negative. The PDF is evaluated at m bin centerpoints and normalized to yield the output distribution. The paper proves a scaling law (Theorem 3.3) showing that increasing the number of frequencies N improves modeling capacity but degrades output smoothness at a rate governed by the decay of the true distribution's Fourier coefficients. Practical training strategies include Fourier weight-decay regularization to penalize high-frequency content, weight initialization near uniform (dividing He-initialized weights by 1000), and mixed-precision binning that allocates more bins to data-dense regions to accelerate spectral decay.

## Results

On the Decision Transformer Atari benchmark, the Fourier head improves normalized returns by up to 377% (Gravitar: 4.98 vs 1.32) and 46% (Seaquest: 3.70 vs 2.53) over the linear-head baseline, with consistent gains across all four games. On the Chronos time series forecasting benchmark (20 zero-shot datasets), the best Fourier model (N=550) reduces MASE from 0.883 to 0.852 (−3.5%) and smoothness from 0.1689 to 0.0283 (≈6× smoother) while matching WQL. On synthetic density-estimation tasks, the Fourier head reduces average KL divergence by ~40% over the linear head across Gaussian, GMM-2, and Beta datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Decision Transformer linear-head baseline (Chen et al., 2021), the Fourier head wins decisively on all four Atari games: Seaquest returns rise from 2.53 to 3.70 (+46%), Gravitar from 1.32 to 4.98 (+377%), BankHeist from −0.09 to 0.92, and DoubleDunk from −72.72 to 45.45, all with identical training hyperparameters. Against the Chronos linear-head baseline (Ansari et al., 2024), the Fourier-550 head reduces MASE from 0.883 to 0.852 and smoothness from 0.1689 to 0.0283 (≈6× smoother) while slightly improving WQL (0.750→0.749), using fewer parameters than the linear head. Against the base Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct model (Dubey et al., 2024) on Gaussian sampling, LoRA fine-tuning alone reduces Total Variation Distance by a factor of ≈2.07, while LoRA + Fourier head reduces it by a factor of ≈4.86, showing the continuity inductive bias provides a substantial additional gain beyond standard fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

The Fourier head extends the Fourier Basis Density Model (De la Fuente et al., 2024), which learned marginal univariate densities via a Fourier basis, to the conditional/multivariate setting by making the Fourier coefficients input-dependent through a linear layer. It departs from the standard practice in LLM-based sequence modeling (Decision Transformer, Chronos) of using a generic linear classification head, arguing that such heads ignore the natural continuous structure among adjacent tokens. The approach is conceptually related to sinusoidal activation functions (Sitzmann et al., 2020), but fixes frequencies to the canonical set {1,...,N} and learns amplitudes, enabling direct use of classical Fourier approximation theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Fourier head offers a simple, drop-in architectural modification that injects a continuity inductive bias into LLM output layers, with a proven scaling law (Theorem 3.3) guiding the frequency choice. Its practical impact is demonstrated across offline RL (up to 377% return improvement on Gravitar) and zero-shot time series forecasting (3.5% MASE reduction), suggesting broad applicability wherever LLMs model continuous-valued tokens. Open questions include whether the discretization-based training objective can be replaced with direct continuous MLE, whether the head can be adaptively employed within general-purpose LLM training, and whether the continuity assumption (semantically meaningful bin ordering) limits its applicability to domains without natural token adjacency.
