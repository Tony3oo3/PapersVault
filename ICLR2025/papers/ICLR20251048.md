## Problem

Estimating the energy landscape of stochastic dynamical systems is critical for understanding long-term dynamics, but existing approaches either rely on costly manual experiments (e.g., 50,000 generations of bacteria) or require high-cost energy annotations as supervisory signals. Classical Markov state models unrealistically assume thermodynamic equilibrium sampling, while self-supervised methods treat networks as black boxes that ignore physical knowledge. No prior method organically integrates AI techniques with physical priors to estimate energy landscapes purely from evolution trajectories without any energy supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes N-step evolution trajectories X_N = {x_{t_i}} in a D-dimensional observation space, where the observation x_t = g(s_t) can be a linear or nonlinear (even continuous-to-discrete) mapping of the hidden state. The output is twofold: an estimated energy Ê(E(x)) that is only required to be a linear transformation of the true energy E(g^{-1}(x)), and conditional probability distributions p(c_{t+Δt} | c_t) over discrete codeword states for evolution prediction. Notably, no ground-truth energy values are provided during training; the self-supervision signal comes entirely from the prediction error of future system states.

## Method

PESLA first maps the observed space to a discrete latent landscape via an adaptive codebook learned with vector quantization (VQ-VAE style), where KNN-style assignment partitions the state space into codeword regions with non-uniform density that adapts to data coverage rather than using equidistant grid binning. On this discrete landscape, a graph neural Fokker-Planck equation (extending Chow et al.'s graph Fokker-Planck theory) models the time evolution of probability distributions, explicitly incorporating energy differences E_ji between neighboring codewords as drift and a learnable noise coefficient β_ξ for diffusion, with a GCN-based probability encoder Φ lifting one-hot vectors into high-dimensional representations for richer relational modeling. A physics-inspired regularization term L_phy = D_KL(p || q) enforces convergence to the Boltzmann distribution in long-term predictions, eliminating the need for thermodynamic equilibrium sampling. Training is fully self-supervised: the total loss L = L_reconstruct + L_vq + L_latent + L_code + L_phy jointly optimizes the encoder, decoder, codebook, energy function, and graph neural ODE parameters, with a two-phase strategy that first learns the landscape topology then freezes it to train the dynamics model.

## Results

On the 2D Prinz Potential, PESLA achieves a trajectory energy correlation ρ_T of 0.9290 and full-space energy correlation ρ_F of 0.7419, substantially outperforming MSM (ρ_T = 0.7762, ρ_F = 0.4354) and APE (ρ_T = 0.5755, ρ_F = 0.4449). For evolution prediction, PESLA attains MJS = 0.1031 and TJS = 0.1796, beating the best baseline NeuralMJP (MJS = 0.1463, TJS = 0.2282) by approximately 30% and 21% respectively. Across all three systems (2D Prinz, Ecological Evolution, Protein Folding), PESLA consistently achieves the lowest JS divergence, with an average 17.65% improvement in evolution prediction accuracy over state-of-the-art baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PESLA dominates all six baselines across both tasks and all three systems. In energy estimation, PESLA's ρ_T of 0.9290 on the 2D Prinz Potential exceeds MSM (0.7762) by 20% and APE (0.5755) by 61%; on Ecological Evolution, PESLA reaches ρ_T = −0.9067 versus MSM (−0.8104) and APE (−0.7063). In evolution prediction, PESLA's MJS of 0.1031 on the 2D Prinz Potential is 30% lower than the best baseline NeuralMJP (0.1463), and its TJS of 0.1796 is 21% lower than NeuralMJP (0.2282). On Ecological Evolution, PESLA (MJS = 0.3111, TJS = 0.3277) outperforms T-IB (MJS = 0.3376, TJS = 0.3720), which is the strongest baseline in that setting, while NeuralMJP, VAMPNet, and SDE-Net all perform worse. On the BBA protein folding task, PESLA achieves TJS = 0.2468, a 32% improvement over NeuralMJP (0.3637) and 43% over SDE-Net (0.5502). Notably, PESLA is the only method that simultaneously excels at both energy estimation and evolution prediction, whereas baselines like MSM and APE are limited to energy estimation and NeuralMJP/T-IB/VAMPNet/SDE-Net are limited to evolution prediction without explicit energy recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

PESLA sits at the intersection of three research threads: vector-quantized autoencoders (VQ-VAE) for discrete state-space representation, graph neural Fokker-Planck equations (extending Chow et al.'s graph Markov process theory and Chamberlain et al.'s graph neural diffusion), and physics-informed learning for stochastic dynamical systems. It departs from prior energy estimation methods (MSM, APE) that either require thermodynamic equilibrium sampling or explicit energy labels, and from black-box self-supervised approaches that ignore physical structure. The key conceptual departure is using the Fokker-Planck equation not merely as a generative model but as a differentiable bridge that couples energy estimation to evolution prediction, with a Boltzmann-distribution KL regularization that relaxes the equilibrium assumption without requiring ground-truth energy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for energy annotations, PESLA makes energy landscape estimation feasible in domains where experimental measurement is prohibitively expensive (protein fitness landscapes, ecological adaptation), and the physics-informed architecture provides interpretability that pure black-box models lack. The ablation revealing that the physics-inspired regularization (L_phy) is essential—its removal collapses energy correlation from 0.93 to 0.06—highlights that self-supervised signals alone are insufficient without physical priors. Open questions include extending to time-varying landscapes (e.g., climate-driven fitness shifts), non-conservative forces where no energy landscape exists, and scaling to very high-dimensional systems where the codebook size must grow substantially.
