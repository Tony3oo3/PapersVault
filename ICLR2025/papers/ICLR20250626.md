## Problem

CLIP-based zero-shot classifiers are extremely vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, dropping to near-zero accuracy under AutoAttack. Existing defenses either require adversarial training (which degrades zero-shot generalization and fails against unseen attacks) or perform purification in high-dimensional pixel space, which is computationally expensive and less effective due to sparsity. No prior work has explored purification in CLIP's multi-modal latent space or proposed a purification method that avoids generative models entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250626/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image, encodes it via CLIP's image encoder into a latent embedding, and outputs a class label by matching the purified embedding against text embeddings of candidate class names (using 80 diverse prompt templates). No dataset-specific training or fine-tuning is required; the entire defense operates at inference time on the pre-trained CLIP model. The purification modifies only the direction of the normalized embedding vector (polar coordinates), preserving the vector magnitude, which is critical since CLIP's alignment relies on cosine similarity where magnitude is irrelevant.

## Method

CLIPure performs adversarial purification in CLIP's multi-modal latent space by maximizing the log-likelihood of the image embedding, formalized through bidirectional SDEs where the attack is a forward SDE and purification is the reverse SDE. The derived purification risk lower bound shows effectiveness depends on the KL divergence between adversarial and benign distributions and the smoothness of the adversarial distribution, both of which are more favorable in CLIP's denser multi-modal latent space than in pixel space. Two variants model likelihood differently: CLIPure-Diff uses DaLLE-2's DiffusionPrior (a diffusion model over CLIP embeddings conditioned on a blank template), while CLIPure-Cos uses the cosine similarity between the image embedding and the text embedding of 'a photo of a.' as a discriminative likelihood proxy. A key design choice is decomposing the embedding into direction (unit vector) and magnitude, then performing gradient ascent only on the direction, since CLIP's cosine-similarity-based alignment makes vector length irrelevant and direct optimization over the full vector fails. CLIPure-Cos is the first purification method not based on generative models, achieving inference times only 1.14× vanilla CLIP versus 6166× for DiffPure.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under ℓ∞=8/255, CLIPure-Cos achieves 91.1% robust accuracy (95.6% clean), surpassing the previous SOTA purification method LM-EDM (71.7%) by 27.1% and DiffPure (71.3%) by 27.8%. On ImageNet under ℓ∞=4/255, CLIPure-Cos reaches 72.6% robust accuracy (76.3% clean), outperforming the best adversarial training method AT-Swin-L (59.6%) by 13% and the best CLIP-based adversarial training method TeCoA (44.3%) by 28.3%. Across 13 zero-shot classification datasets, CLIPure-Cos achieves a 108% relative improvement in average robustness over the previous SOTA (FARE) at ℓ∞=4/255, and a 45.9% improvement at ℓ∞=2/255. Inference efficiency is dramatically improved: CLIPure-Cos runs at only 1.14× the speed of vanilla CLIP, versus 6167× for DiffPure and 694× for LM-EDM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIPure-Cos decisively outperforms all baselines on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Against pixel-space purification methods, it beats LM-EDM by 27.1% (CIFAR-10, ℓ∞=8/255) and 288% (ImageNet, ℓ∞=4/255), and DiffPure by 27.8% and 63.5% respectively. Against adversarial training methods, it surpasses AT-Swin-L (59.6%) and AT-ConvNeXt-L (57.7%) on ImageNet by 13% and 15%, and AT-EDM-ℓ∞ (70.9%) on CIFAR-10 by 20.2%. Against CLIP-based adversarial training (FARE at 33.3% and TeCoA at 44.3% on ImageNet), CLIPure-Cos leads by 39.3% and 28.3% while also maintaining higher clean accuracy (76.3% vs. 70.4%/75.2%). The uni-modal latent space purification (LM-StableDiffusion) performs poorly at only 6.9% robust accuracy on CIFAR-10, confirming the advantage of multi-modal latent space. Under the BPDA+EOT attack (Table 4), CLIPure-Cos (93.5%) still outperforms all pixel-space purification baselines including GDMP (76.2%), ADP (70.0%), and Purify-EBM (54.9%). The only methods that approach CLIPure's performance are the ℓ2-trained adversarial training variants (AT-EDM-ℓ2 at 84.8% on CIFAR-10 ℓ2=0.5), but these sacrifice clean accuracy and do not generalize to ℓ∞ attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial purification paradigm (originally formulated in pixel space via generative models like DiffPure and LM-EDM) into CLIP's multi-modal latent space, leveraging the SDE-based theoretical framework from Song et al. (2020) to formalize purification risk as KL divergence between attack and denoising processes. It departs from prior CLIP-based defenses (FARE, TeCoA) that rely on dataset-specific adversarial training by operating entirely in the pre-trained latent space without any fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) purification in a multi-modal (vision-language) latent space rather than pixel or uni-modal latent space, and (2) the discovery that a simple cosine-similarity-based likelihood (CLIPure-Cos) can replace the generative diffusion model entirely, making it the first non-generative purification method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLIPure-Cos offers a practically transformative efficiency gain (1.14× vs. 6167× inference overhead relative to vanilla CLIP) while achieving the strongest zero-shot adversarial robustness reported, making adversarial defense feasible in latency-sensitive deployment scenarios. The finding that a discriminative cosine-similarity objective outperforms a diffusion-based generative likelihood for purification challenges the prevailing assumption that purification requires a generative model, potentially reshaping how the field approaches adversarial defense. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond image classification (e.g., to segmentation or detection), whether it transfers to other VLMs beyond the CLIP family, and whether the gap between CLIPure-Diff and CLIPure-Cos indicates that the DiffusionPrior's noise-injection process is fundamentally misaligned with CLIP's cosine-similarity geometry.
