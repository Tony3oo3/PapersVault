## Problem

Existing graph OOD detection methods like GNNSafe derive node energy from classification logits without explicit data-distribution modeling, and rely on energy propagation that assumes homophily—causing severe degradation on heterophilic graphs where neighbors share dissimilar distributions. Additionally, training energy-based models via MLE on graphs requires MCMC sampling over both node features and discrete neighbor sets, which is computationally intractable for large graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252824/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d0} and adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N}, and produces a scalar OOD energy score per node (higher = more OOD) plus a class label prediction. Crucially, the energy score is computed entirely in a learned latent space after GNN encoding, so the energy head never operates on the raw adjacency structure. No OOD samples are required during training, distinguishing DeGEM from baselines like OE, Energy-FT, and GNNSafe++ that use OOD exposure.

## Method

DeGEM decouples the EBM into a graph encoder g_α (trained by DGI contrastive learning plus classification loss) that maps (x, N(x)) to a latent representation h, and an energy head f_ω (an MLP trained by MLE) that scores h in latent space. The key insight is that once topology is encoded into h, K-step MCMC sampling for EBM training operates only on the low-dimensional latent space, avoiding the intractable problem of sampling adjacency matrices. A Multi-Hop encoder with weighted self-loops fuses multi-scale features to handle heterophily, while Conditional Energy (CE) conditions the energy function on a global readout summary and Energy Readout (ERo) computes that summary via energy-weighted averaging. A Recurrent Update mechanism alternates between updating the energy head (given fixed encoder) and the encoder (given fixed energy head), enabling the two components to promote each other across training epochs.

## Results

On four homophilic datasets (Cora, Amazon-Photo, Twitch, Arxiv), DeGEM achieves an average AUC of 97.08% and Acc of 83.56%, improving over the best baseline GNNSafe (88.73% AUC) by 8.35 points. On three heterophilic datasets (Chameleon, Actor, Cornell), DeGEM reaches 97.55% average AUC and 52.53% Acc, surpassing the best baseline GPN (76.92% AUC) by 20.63 points. Notably, DeGEM is trained without any OOD exposure yet outperforms methods that use OOD samples during training (OE, Energy-FT, GNNSafe++), and maintains high performance even when only 10% of labels are available (96.69% avg AUC vs. 64.80% for GNNSafe++).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeGEM decisively outperforms all 12 baselines on both homophilic and heterophilic graphs. On homophilic graphs, it beats the strongest graph-specific baseline GNNSafe (88.73% avg AUC) by 8.35 points and the i.i.d. Energy method (86.46%) by 10.6 points; on heterophilic graphs, the gap widens dramatically—DeGEM (97.55%) surpasses GPN (76.92%) by 20.6 points and GNNSafe (55.23%) by 42.3 points, exposing GNNSafe's energy propagation as severely harmful under heterophily. Among i.i.d. baselines, Energy and MSP are the strongest on homophilic graphs but collapse on heterophilic ones (e.g., ODIN drops to 51.66% avg AUC on heterophilic vs. 82.01% on homophilic). Critically, DeGEM trained without any OOD exposure outperforms all three OOD-exposure baselines (OE, Energy-FT, GNNSafe++) on every dataset, and this advantage grows under label scarcity: at 10% label rate, DeGEM maintains 96.69% avg AUC while GNNSafe++ drops to 64.80%. The ablation confirms that the combination of MLE-trained energy and DGI-based graph contrastive learning is essential—neither component alone (72.24% or 73.90% avg AUC) approaches the full model (96.88%), and replacing DGI with GRACE or SUGRL degrades performance to 79.38% and 75.24% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the energy-based OOD detection framework (Liu et al., 2020) and its graph adaptation in GNNSafe (Wu et al., 2023), but departs from both in two key ways: it replaces classification-logit-derived energy with a properly MLE-trained energy head, and it removes the energy propagation mechanism that GNNSafe relies on. The graph encoder is trained via DGI (Veličković et al., 2018), which the authors formally connect to EBM training via noise contrastive estimation, establishing a theoretical bridge between contrastive graph representation learning and energy-based density modeling. The decoupling of topology extraction (GCL) from density estimation (MLE in latent space) is the central architectural departure, making MCMC sampling tractable without sampling adjacency matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic evaluation of node OOD detection on heterophilic graphs, revealing that existing graph-based methods (particularly those using energy propagation) suffer severe degradation in this common real-world setting. The decoupling strategy offers a practical path to scaling EBM-based OOD detection to large graphs, since MCMC operates in low-dimensional latent space rather than on graph topology. However, the near-perfect AUC scores (≥99.9) on several datasets raise concerns about benchmark saturation, and the method still inherits the MCMC sampling cost of EBM training. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic or multi-graph settings and whether the DGI-specific inductive bias is truly necessary or whether other GCL variants could work with different architectural choices.
