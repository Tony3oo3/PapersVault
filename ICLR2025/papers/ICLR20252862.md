## Problem

Decoder-only LLMs were long believed to underperform bidirectional models for general-purpose text embedding due to unidirectional attention and high-dimensional representations. While E5-Mistral demonstrated that decoder-only LLMs could be competitive, it retained the causal attention mask and last-token pooling, and relied on proprietary synthetic data. This paper addresses how to build a fully reproducible, generalist embedding model from a decoder-only LLM that excels across all embedding task types—retrieval, classification, clustering, STS, and reranking—without depending on other embedding models or closed-source data.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text query prefixed with a task-specific instruction template (e.g., 'Given a question, retrieve passages that answer the question') and a text document, both truncated to 512 tokens with <BOS>/<EOS> tokens appended. Instruction tokens are masked out from the final embedding vector but still influence it through self-attention; no instruction prefix is added to documents. The output is a single dense vector produced by mean-pooling the latent attention layer's output (512 latents, 4096 hidden dimension, 8 attention heads) after an MLP, yielding a 4096-dimensional embedding per input.

## Method

NV-Embed replaces the causal attention mask with bidirectional attention during contrastive training and introduces a latent attention pooling layer where the LLM's last-layer hidden states serve as queries attending to a trainable latent array (K=V, 512 latents) in a cross-attention formulation, followed by an MLP and mean pooling—outperforming both mean pooling and last-<EOS> token embedding. Training uses a two-stage contrastive instruction-tuning scheme on Mistral-7B with LoRA (rank 16): Stage 1 trains on retrieval datasets with in-batch negatives and positive-aware hard-negative mining (using E5-Mistral as a teacher to set score thresholds at 95% of the positive score), while Stage 2 blends in non-retrieval datasets (classification, clustering, STS) with in-batch negatives disabled to avoid misleading false negatives. For multi-class classification and clustering, the method uses example-based labeling (sampling a same-class example as the positive document) rather than label-based, and generates 120K synthetic instruction examples across 60K tasks via Mixtral-8x22B to broaden task generalization.

## Results

NV-Embed-v2 achieves a record MTEB average score of 72.31 across 56 embedding tasks, ranking #1 on the MTEB leaderboard (as of Aug 30, 2024). It leads in 15 retrieval tasks (nDCG@10: 62.65), 11 clustering tasks (V-Measure: 58.46), and 12 classification tasks (Accuracy: 90.37). On the out-of-domain AIR-Benchmark, NV-Embed-v2 attains the highest Long-Doc score (Recall@10: 74.78) and the second-highest QA score (nDCG@10: 52.28). The earlier NV-Embed-v1 model scored 69.32 and held the #1 MTEB position as of May 24, 2024.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NV-Embed-v2 (72.31 MTEB avg) outperforms all 8 compared baselines on the full MTEB benchmark. The closest competitor is Bge-en-icl zero-shot (71.24), which NV-Embed-v2 surpasses by 1.07 points; notably, Bge-en-icl requires in-context examples at inference time, whereas NV-Embed-v2 operates purely zero-shot. Against SFR-Embedding-2R (70.31), which fine-tunes E5-Mistral, NV-Embed-v2 gains 2.0 points by training from Mistral-7B with bidirectional attention and latent-attention pooling. The gap widens substantially against E5-Mistral (66.63, +5.68), GritLM-7B (66.76, +5.55), and OpenAI's Text-embed-3-large (64.59, +7.72). On the out-of-domain AIR-Benchmark, NV-Embed-v2 achieves the highest Long-Doc score (74.78) but trails Bge-en-icl (52.93 vs. 52.28) on the QA sub-task by 0.65 points. Linq-Embed-Mistral is outperformed on both AIR-Bench sub-tasks (QA: 52.28 vs. 49.69; Long-Doc: 74.78 vs. 72.11).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the E5-Mistral paradigm of contrastive instruction-tuning on decoder-only LLMs for text embedding, but departs in three key ways: (1) it replaces the causal attention mask with bidirectional attention during contrastive training (a simpler alternative to LLM2Vec's masked-token-prediction warm-up or GRIT's hybrid generative-representational objective), (2) it introduces a latent attention layer for sequence pooling that outperforms both mean pooling and last-<EOS> token embedding, and (3) it employs a two-stage training schedule that prioritizes retrieval with in-batch negatives before blending non-retrieval tasks without them. For hard-negative mining, it adopts the positive-aware technique from NV-Retriever (Moreira et al., 2024) rather than proposing a new mining strategy. Unlike SFR-Embedding-2R, which fine-tunes E5-Mistral with task-homogeneous batching, NV-Embed is trained from scratch on Mistral-7B using only public data with well-blended multi-task batches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NV-Embed provides a fully reproducible recipe (architecture, training procedure, data curation, hyperparameters) for building state-of-the-art LLM-based embedding models, which is rare among top MTEB entries that often withhold training data details. The model compression study demonstrating that a pruned and quantized 8B model outperforms natively smaller 3B–4B models (Llama3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-3B, Minitron-4B) offers a practical pathway to deploying high-quality embeddings at reduced cost. Open questions include whether the latent attention layer and two-stage schedule generalize to larger base models or multilingual settings, and whether the 512-token context limit constrains real-world long-document retrieval beyond what AIR-Bench captures.
