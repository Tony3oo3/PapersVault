## Problem

Progressive compression with diffusion models, as proposed by Theis et al. (2022), requires communicating Gaussian samples at each denoising step via relative entropy coding (REC), whose runtime scales exponentially with the number of bits transmitted. This makes Gaussian-diffusion-based progressive codecs computationally intractable for practical image compression, leaving a gap between the theoretical rate-distortion performance of diffusion models and their deployability as real-world codecs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251361/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10 or 64×64 ImageNet) and produces a single progressive bitstream structured as a sequence of entropy-coded quantization indices, one per diffusion step. Decoding can be halted at any intermediate step to yield a lossy reconstruction of increasing quality, or run to completion for a lossless reconstruction. The bitstream is transmitted from most-significant (coarsest, t=T) to least-significant (finest, t=0) step, with the final step transmitting the residual x given z_0 via a categorical likelihood model.

## Method

UQDM replaces the Gaussian noise channels in the forward process of VDM with uniform noise channels: q(z_T|x) remains Gaussian, but each conditional q(z_{t-1}|z_t,x) is a uniform distribution whose mean and variance match the Gaussian forward posterior. This makes each step's communication a uniform-noise channel, which can be simulated in polynomial time via universal quantization (UQ) instead of intractable Gaussian REC. The reverse process uses a learned logistic density convolved with uniform noise, with a per-coordinate learned variance (inspired by Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021) that significantly improves the NELBO. The forward process is non-Markovian (DDIM-style factorization), and the paper proves that as T→∞ the sum of uniform noise terms converges to Gaussian by the Lindeberg-Feller CLT, recovering the VDM continuous-time limit. Training optimizes the full NELBO end-to-end with a small number of steps (T≈4–5), using a standard U-Net denoiser with doubled output channels for the learned variance.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32), UQDM with T=4 consistently outperforms JPEG and JPEG2000 in both PSNR and FID across all bit-rates, and matches or exceeds BPG in FID while surpassing BPG in PSNR at higher bit-rates. On ImageNet 64×64, UQDM beats JPEG, JPEG2000, and CTC across all bit-rates, and achieves the best PSNR and FID among all baselines in the high bit-rate regime where other codecs plateau. On the swirl toy dataset, UQDM achieves ~8 bpd lossless compression at an optimal T≈5, compared to VDM's theoretical 5.8 bpd at T=1000 (which is computationally intractable). The actual file size is within 3% of the theoretical NELBO, confirming efficient entropy coding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BPG (Bellard, 2018), UQDM matches BPG in realism (FID) on CIFAR-10 and surpasses it in PSNR at higher bit-rates; on ImageNet 64×64, UQDM continues to improve quality at higher bit-rates while BPG plateaus. Against CTC (Jeon et al., CVPR 2023), the only other progressive neural codec evaluated, UQDM achieves comparable or slightly better PSNR and FID across all bit-rates on ImageNet 64×64. Against CDC (Yang & Mandt, NeurIPS 2023), a non-progressive conditional diffusion codec, UQDM outperforms it on both fidelity and realism metrics on ImageNet 64×64, while also offering the practical advantage of progressive decoding with a single model. Against the theoretical VDM/Gaussian diffusion baseline (Theis et al., 2022), UQDM achieves a performance gap (e.g., ~8 bpd vs. 5.8 bpd on swirl), but unlike VDM's Gaussian channel simulation which has exponential runtime complexity, UQDM's universal quantization approach is computationally tractable, making it the first practical unconditional diffusion-based progressive codec.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the progressive compression framework of Theis et al. (2022), which showed that the NELBO of a Gaussian diffusion model (VDM) corresponds to the lossless coding cost of a progressive codec. The key departure is replacing Gaussian noise channels in the forward process with uniform noise channels, enabling the use of Universal Quantization (Zamir & Feder, 1992) instead of intractable Gaussian channel simulation (Relative Entropy Coding). The forward process follows the non-Markovian DDIM factorization, and the reverse process adapts the VDM denoising architecture with a learned per-coordinate variance inspired by Nichol & Dhariwal (2021). The paper also proves that the uniform forward process converges to the Gaussian VDM forward process in the continuous-time limit, establishing a theoretical bridge between the two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first computationally tractable progressive codec based on an unconditional diffusion model, achieving competitive rate-distortion and rate-realism results across all bit-rates (up to lossless) with a single model — a practical advantage over methods requiring separate models per bitrate. However, a notable gap remains to the theoretical performance of Gaussian diffusion (VDM), and the optimal number of steps is small (T≈4–5), suggesting the uniform-noise approximation introduces a constant per-step overhead that limits scaling. Open questions include whether more sophisticated reverse-process densities or flow-based reconstructions can close this gap, and whether combining UQDM with latent diffusion, distillation, or consistency models can improve both efficiency and quality at scale.
