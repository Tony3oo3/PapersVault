## Problem

Existing novel view synthesis methods rely on 3D inductive biases—ranging from explicit representations (NeRF, 3DGS) to architectural priors (epipolar projections, plane-sweep volumes)—that constrain flexibility and limit generalization to diverse, complex scenes. Even recent Large Reconstruction Models that removed architecture-level biases still depend on representation-level biases and their associated rendering equations. This paper asks whether a fully data-driven transformer can achieve state-of-the-art view synthesis quality while minimizing these 3D inductive biases.

## Input / Output

The model takes N sparse posed RGB images (256×256 or 512×512) with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics, along with a target camera pose. Each input image is patchified into non-overlapping 8×8 patches, and per-pixel 6D Plücker ray embeddings are computed from the camera parameters and concatenated with RGB patches before a linear projection into token space. The target view is represented solely by its Plücker ray embedding patches (no RGB input). The model outputs a novel-view RGB image at the target pose, regressed per-patch through a linear layer and sigmoid. Models are trained with 2–4 input views but generalize zero-shot to 1–10+ views at test time.

## Method

LVSM introduces two pure-transformer architectures that eliminate all handcrafted 3D representations and rendering equations. The encoder-decoder variant uses a transformer encoder to compress input image tokens into a fixed-length set of 3072 learnable latent tokens (a fully learned scene representation), which a transformer decoder then combines with target-view Plücker ray tokens to produce output image tokens. The decoder-only variant removes even this intermediate representation, using a single-stream transformer with full bidirectional self-attention to directly map concatenated input and target tokens to output tokens, treating view synthesis as a sequence-to-sequence translation. Both architectures use dense full self-attention (no causal or cross-attention masks), QK-Norm for training stability, and are trained end-to-end with MSE plus perceptual loss. The decoder-only model consistently outperforms the encoder-decoder variant and surpasses prior SOTA (GS-LRM) by 1.5–3.5 dB PSNR, while the encoder-decoder variant offers faster inference due to its fixed-length latent bottleneck.

## Results

On object-level benchmarks at 512 resolution, the decoder-only LVSM achieves 32.10 dB PSNR on ABO and 32.36 dB PSNR on GSO, surpassing the previous best (GS-LRM) by 3.0 dB and 1.8 dB respectively. At 256 resolution, the decoder-only LVSM reaches 32.47 dB PSNR on ABO and 31.71 dB PSNR on GSO, a 3.5 dB and 2.2 dB gain over GS-LRM. On the scene-level RealEstate10K benchmark (256 resolution), the decoder-only LVSM achieves 29.67 dB PSNR, outperforming GS-LRM (28.10 dB) by 1.6 dB. The encoder-decoder variant achieves competitive results (e.g., 29.81 dB on ABO at 512) with faster inference due to its fixed-length latent representation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The decoder-only LVSM outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric. Against GS-LRM (the strongest prior method), the decoder-only LVSM gains 3.0 dB PSNR on ABO (32.10 vs. 29.09 at Res-512), 1.8 dB on GSO (32.36 vs. 30.52), and 1.6 dB on RealEstate10K (29.67 vs. 28.10). The encoder-decoder LVSM also beats GS-LRM on ABO (29.81 vs. 29.09) and RealEstate10K (28.58 vs. 28.10) but is slightly behind on GSO at Res-512 (29.32 vs. 30.52). Against Triplane-LRM, the gap is even larger (e.g., 4.6 dB on ABO at Res-512). LGM, MVSplat, and pixelNeRF are substantially outperformed, with the decoder-only LVSM exceeding LGM by over 11 dB on ABO and MVSplat by 3.3 dB on RealEstate10K. Notably, even a reduced 1-GPU decoder-only model (27.66 dB on RealEstate10K) surpasses MVSplat (26.39 dB) by 1.3 dB, demonstrating that LVSM's quality advantage persists under limited compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

LVSM extends the line of Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs) such as GS-LRM, Instant3D, and MeshLRM, which removed architecture-level 3D biases (epipolar projections, plane-sweep volumes) but retained representation-level biases (NeRF, 3DGS, meshes) and their rendering equations. LVSM departs from this by eliminating both levels of 3D inductive bias: the encoder-decoder variant replaces explicit 3D representations with a fully learned 1D latent token set, while the decoder-only variant removes even the intermediate representation, treating NVS as a pure sequence-to-sequence translation. Conceptually, the encoder-decoder design is closest to SRT (Sajjadi et al., 2022), but LVSM replaces SRT's CNN tokenizer with ViT-style patchification and its cross-attention decoder with bidirectional self-attention, yielding substantial quality gains. The Plücker ray embedding for camera conditioning follows prior work but is applied at the patch level within a fully transformer-based pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.5–3.5 dB PSNR improvement over GS-LRM across object and scene benchmarks, combined with strong zero-shot generalization to unseen numbers of input views, provides compelling evidence that 3D inductive biases are not necessary for high-quality novel view synthesis—a finding that challenges a core assumption in the field. The practical result that a 1–2 GPU model outperforms prior methods trained on 64 GPUs significantly lowers the barrier for academic research. Key limitations remain: the deterministic model cannot hallucinate unseen regions (producing noisy/flickering artifacts), and performance degrades on aspect ratios outside the training distribution. Open questions include whether combining LVSM's learned rendering function with generative priors can resolve the unseen-region problem, and whether the decoder-only architecture scales further with model size and data.
