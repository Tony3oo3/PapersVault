## Problem

Speculative decoding suffers from a mutual waiting problem: the target model idles while the draft model generates tokens, and the draft model idles while the target model verifies them. This is caused by the asynchronous execution of drafting and verification phases, and is exacerbated by a fixed draft length that cannot adapt to varying acceptance rates across decoding steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250044/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PEARL takes a text prompt, a small draft model M_q, a large target model M_p, and a window size gamma (set to the rounded speed ratio c = T(M_p)/T(M_q) between the two models). It produces the same lossless generated text tokens as standard speculative decoding, but with both models running simultaneously at every timestamp. The draft length is no longer fixed at gamma; it adapts dynamically, shorter when tokens are likely rejected and longer when they are likely accepted, so the number of tokens produced per step ranges from 1 up to 2*gamma or more.

## Method

PEARL introduces two complementary strategies that are switched based on the previous step's acceptance outcome. Pre-verify runs the target model on the original prefix in parallel with the draft model's first forward pass, verifying the first draft token before the full verification phase; if rejected, all subsequent draft tokens are discarded and the next drafting phase begins immediately, saving an entire target-model forward. Post-verify assumes all gamma draft tokens will be accepted and uses the draft model to generate an additional gamma tokens during the target model's verification forward; if all are accepted, the next drafting phase is skipped entirely and the last target-model logit serves as a pre-verify for the next token. Together, these strategies ensure both models are active at every timestamp (eliminating mutual waiting) and make the effective draft length adaptive: short when the draft model is misaligned, long when it is well-aligned.

## Results

On HumanEval (code generation), PEARL achieves walltime speedups of 2.48×–4.43× over auto-regressive decoding across six model configurations (CodeLlama 7&34B/70B, Llama 2 7&70B, Llama 3.1 8&70B, DeepSeek 1.3&33B, DeepSeek 6.7&33B), with the peak 4.43× on CodeLlama 7&70B. Compared to vanilla speculative decoding, PEARL yields a 1.36×–1.50× additional speedup. On multilingual arithmetic (GSM8K/MGSM) and multi-round dialogue (MT-bench) with Llama 2 7&70B, PEARL reaches 3.95× and 2.64× average speedup respectively, outperforming all baselines by 1.36×–1.55× over vanilla SD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEARL outperforms all four baselines across every model configuration and task. Against vanilla Speculative Decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023), PEARL achieves 1.36×–1.50× higher speedup (e.g., 4.43× vs. 3.03× on CodeLlama 7&70B; 3.48× vs. 2.32× on DeepSeek 1.3&33B). Against Ouroboros (Zhao et al., 2024), PEARL leads by 0.15×–1.15× (e.g., 4.43× vs. 3.28× on CodeLlama 7&70B; 3.48× vs. 3.25× on DeepSeek 1.3&33B). Against Lookahead Decoding (Fu et al., 2024), PEARL's advantage is the largest, reaching up to 2.86× higher speedup (4.43× vs. 1.57× on CodeLlama 7&70B). Against Assisted Generation (Gante, 2023), PEARL consistently wins by 0.27×–1.94× (e.g., 3.87× vs. 2.72× on Llama 3.1 8&70B). The gap is most pronounced for larger target models (70B) where the mutual waiting problem is more severe, confirming PEARL's parallelism and adaptive draft length as the key differentiators.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEARL builds directly on the speculative decoding framework of Leviathan et al. (2023), which introduced the draft-then-verify paradigm for lossless LLM inference acceleration. It departs from all existing SD methods (Ouroboros, Lookahead Decoding, Medusa, EAGLE, etc.) by identifying and addressing the 'mutual waiting problem'—the idle time incurred by the asynchronous, sequential execution of the draft and target models. The two proposed strategies (pre-verify and post-verify) parallelize the drafting and verification phases and introduce adaptive draft length, a dimension absent from prior fixed-window SD approaches. The authors explicitly position PEARL as orthogonal to other SD improvements, suggesting it can be combined with them for further gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mutual waiting problem is a fundamental bottleneck in all existing speculative decoding implementations, and PEARL's parallel execution framework addresses it in a general, training-free manner that is orthogonal to draft model quality or verification strategy. The 1.5× improvement over already-optimized SD (and up to 4.43× over auto-regressive) is practically significant for deployment latency. Key open questions include: integrating PEARL with tensor parallelism (discussed but left as future work), extending pre-verify to multiple tokens, and reducing the increased power consumption from running both models simultaneously. The resource-competition analysis (89–99% retention under co-location) is a useful but incomplete treatment of the hardware scheduling challenge.
