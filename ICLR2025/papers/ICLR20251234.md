## Problem

Auto-regressive Transformers exhibit two persistent anomalies: the first token receives maximal attention in up to 98% of attention heads, and specific feature channels in the hidden states contain outlier activations orders of magnitude larger than others. These phenomena force practitioners to use complex attention masking schemes for long-context tasks and sophisticated quantization methods (e.g., SmoothQuant, LLM.int8) to deploy models, yet their root causes remained unexplained and no training-time fix existed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text sequences (C4 en split, 256-token context) for next-token prediction training, identical to vanilla GPT-2 or Llama-2 pretraining. The distinguishing output property is that the resulting model weights are robust to basic quantization: per-tensor 8-bit absmax and per-channel 4-bit zeropoint weight quantization incur negligible perplexity penalty (≤0.8 PPL) without any calibration or mixed-precision decomposition, whereas standard models suffer catastrophic degradation (up to 3565 PPL penalty at 4-bit).

## Method

The paper proposes two independent, training-time fixes. First, softmax-1 replaces the canonical softmax denominator Σⱼ exp(xⱼ) with 1 + Σⱼ exp(xⱼ), allowing attention scores to sum to less than 1 so that heads can effectively attend to nothing when no relevant key is present; this removes the causal-masking privilege of the first token and reduces first-token attention from 65% to 3.3%. Second, OrthoAdam is an optimizer that multiplies each gradient by a fixed random orthogonal matrix Q before computing the exponential moving averages of first and second moments, then transforms the resulting update back via Qᵀ; this decouples the adaptive per-parameter scaling from the model parameter basis, preventing the small second-moment values in specific dimensions from producing disproportionately large weight updates that create outlier activations. Together, these reduce hidden-state kurtosis from 1657 to 3.1 (near-Gaussian) with no perplexity penalty, and the paper provides theoretical analysis showing that orthogonal rotation of a vector with one dominant component reduces the ℓ∞/ℓ₂ norm ratio from ≈1 to O(1/D) and kurtosis from O(D) to 3.

## Results

Across GPT2 (60M–1.4B) and Llama2-130M models trained on C4, softmax-1 + OrthoAdam matches or slightly improves validation perplexity (e.g., GPT2-1.4B: 13.3 vs. 13.4 vanilla) while reducing hidden-state kurtosis from 351.9 to 3.0 and first-token attention from 64.8% to 3.3%. Under 4-bit zeropoint weight quantisation, the perplexity penalty drops from 3564.3 (GPT2-1.4B vanilla) to 0.2 (GPT2-1.4B Ours), and from 118490.7 (GPT2-350M vanilla) to 0.8 (GPT2-350M Ours). Pretrained Llama3.1-8B suffers a 4-bit PPL of 2,087,638, whereas the authors' GPT2-1.4B (Ours) achieves a 4-bit PPL of only 13.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: (1) vanilla GPT2 trained with canonical softmax and Adam, (2) vanilla Llama2 trained with canonical softmax and Adam, and (3) publicly pretrained models (GPT2-Small through GPT2-XL, Llama2-7B, Llama3.1-8B) for quantisation robustness. On language modelling perplexity, softmax-1 + OrthoAdam ties or marginally beats the vanilla baselines (e.g., GPT2-350M: 16.3 vs. 16.4; Llama2-130M: 17.3 vs. 17.4), confirming no performance cost. On the two target anomalies, the method wins decisively: kurtosis drops by 95–99.8% (e.g., 1657→3.1 for GPT2-1.4B) and first-token attention drops from 53–65% to 2–3%. Under 4-bit zeropoint weight quantisation, vanilla GPT2 models become essentially unusable (PPL 4456–118507), while the authors' models retain near-full-precision performance (PPL 17.1 and 13.6, penalties of 0.8 and 0.2 respectively). Compared to pretrained Llama3.1-8B (4-bit PPL 2,087,638), the authors' GPT2-1.4B (Ours) achieves a 4-bit PPL of 13.6, demonstrating that the quantisation fragility of modern LLMs is an artefact of training rather than an inherent property of the architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Transformer attention mechanism (Vaswani et al., 2017) and the Adam optimizer (Kingma & Ba, 2015), proposing targeted modifications to each: softmax-1 reformulates the attention normalization, while OrthoAdam inserts orthogonal matrix transformations into Adam's gradient moment tracking. It departs from prior work on attention dominance (Bondarenko et al., 2023; Xiao et al., 2024) that proposed workarounds like clipping, gating, or attention sinks, by identifying softmax combined with causal masking as the root cause and eliminating the need for such patches. It also departs from prior outlier-activation literature (Dettmers et al., 2022; Xiao et al., 2023) that addressed the symptom via complex quantization schemes, by showing the optimizer itself is the cause and fixing it at the source. Critically, the paper refutes the prior assumption (Xiao et al., 2023; Bondarenko et al., 2023) that first-token attention dominance and outlier activations are linked, demonstrating they are independent phenomena requiring separate solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: models trained with softmax-1 and OrthoAdam tolerate basic 4-bit zeropoint weight quantization with a perplexity penalty of 0.3 versus 3565 for standard models, potentially eliminating the need for calibration-dependent quantization schemes like SmoothQuant or LLM.int8() in deployment pipelines. The conceptual contribution—that Adam's second-moment tracking, not the architecture, is responsible for outlier activations—reframes how practitioners think about training dynamics and may influence future optimizer design. Limitations include evaluation only up to 1.4B parameters (though observational checks on Llama-3.1-8B confirm the phenomena persist at scale), a modest computational overhead from the orthogonal matrices, and training exclusively on C4. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-autoregressive or multimodal architectures, whether the orthogonal basis can be learned rather than random, and whether the softmax-1 modification interacts with other attention variants (e.g., grouped-query or sliding-window attention).
