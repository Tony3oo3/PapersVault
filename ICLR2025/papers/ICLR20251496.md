## Problem

LLMs struggle with molecular design because molecules are graph-structured data that cannot be faithfully represented as sequential text (e.g., SMILES), leading to poor generation quality and near-zero retrosynthetic planning success. Existing approaches either use text-only LLMs that hallucinate invalid structures and reactions, or graph-only methods that cannot handle the complex, language-specified multi-property constraints and synthesis planning that realistic molecular design demands. No prior framework unifies interleaved text and graph generation within a single autoregressive model for inverse molecular design with retrosynthetic planning.

## Input / Output

The input is a natural-language question specifying multi-property constraints (e.g., HIV inhibition, CO2 permeability), structural requirements (e.g., molecular weight, number of aromatic rings), and synthesizability. The output is an interleaved sequence of text paragraphs, molecular graphs (as <molecule> tokens decoded by a GIN graph encoder), and reaction templates, forming a complete multi-step retrosynthetic route from the target molecule down to purchasable building blocks from a ~1.3M Enamine inventory. Each synthesis step includes reaction condition text, the predicted reaction template, and the corresponding reactant molecules, with the entire route validated against AND-OR tree constraints.

## Method

Llamole augments a base LLM (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, or Qwen2-7B) with two frozen pre-trained graph modules: a Graph Diffusion Transformer for multi-conditional molecule generation and a GIN-based GNN for one-step reaction template prediction among ~300K USPTO templates. A trigger-query-prediction mechanism uses special tokens (<design_start>/<retro_start>) to switch the active module, followed by eight query tokens whose averaged hidden states are projected via linear layers into conditioning vectors for the graph modules. A GIN graph encoder, pre-trained with CLIP-style contrastive loss on ~600K graph-text pairs, replaces the word encoder for molecule tokens so the LLM can attend to generated structures. For retrosynthetic planning, Llamole performs A* search on an AND-OR tree where the LLM itself computes the heuristic J_heuristic as a multi-choice probability over remaining synthesis steps, replacing domain-specific cost models. Training freezes all graph module parameters and fine-tunes only the LLM via LoRA plus the query projection layers and a linear adapter on the graph encoder, using the unified autoregressive loss L_Llamole = L_LM + L_DM + L_predictor.

## Results

On the MolQA benchmark (9,986 drug + 750 material QA pairs), Llamole (best variant: Llama-3.1-8B) achieves 0.913 chemical validity, 0.142 Morgan fingerprint similarity, 0.427 ROUGE-L, and 0.623/0.629/0.713 balanced accuracy on HIV/BBBP/BACE drug properties, while reducing material MAE to 0.653/1.344/0.549/0.021/0.030 for CO₂/N₂/O₂ permeability, FFV, and thermal conductivity. Llamole improves over the best LLM baselines by up to 80.9% (FFV MAE) and 72.6% (O₂ permeability MAE) across 12 controllability metrics. For retrosynthetic planning, Llamole increases the drug success rate from 5.5% (best ICL) to 35.1% and the material success rate from 4.8% to 17.9%, surpassing BioNavi+DiGress (18.0% drug, 15.4% material).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Llamole outperforms GraphGA (Gao et al., 2022) on all 12 controllability metrics, most dramatically on material properties (e.g., CO₂ permeability MAE 0.593–0.653 vs. 0.847) and drug balanced accuracy (0.596–0.623 vs. 0.536 for HIV), while matching or exceeding its 0.885 validity. Against DiGress (Vignac et al., 2022), Llamole achieves substantially higher validity (0.888–0.913 vs. 0.375) and similarity (0.135–0.142 vs. 0.046), though DiGress has a slight edge on O₂ permeability MAE (0.680 vs. 0.549–0.581). For retrosynthesis, Llamole (35.1% drug, 17.9% material) surpasses BioNavi+DiGress (18.0% drug, 15.4% material) by roughly 2× on drug success. Among the 14 LLM baselines (Llama-2/3, Mistral, Qwen2, Granite-13B, Flan-T5-XXL), Llamole's 7–8B variants beat even the 70B ICL models across all metrics; for instance, Llamole (Llama-3.1-8B) achieves 0.913 validity vs. Llama-3-70B ICL's 0.706 and 0.623 HIV BA vs. 0.415. Llamole also outperforms MolT5 (Edwards et al., 2022) on drug design (0.662 avg BA vs. 0.264 for MolT5-large) and is the only method that generates valid polymer structures for material design, where MolT5 fails entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

Llamole integrates two established graph-based components — the Graph Diffusion Transformer (Graph DiT) for multi-conditional molecular generation and a GIN-based predictor for reaction template prediction — into a unified multimodal autoregressive framework with a base LLM. It departs from prior LLM-for-chemistry work (Guo et al., 2023; Jablonka et al., 2023), which treated molecules as sequential text (SMILES) and showed poor generation quality, by instead using trigger-query tokens to switch between text and graph generation spaces. For retrosynthetic planning, it adapts the AND-OR tree A* search formulation from Retro* (Chen et al., 2020) but replaces the learned cost function with an LLM-based multi-choice heuristic, and differs from prior multimodal molecular models (Edwards et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023b) by treating molecules as graphs and jointly addressing synthesizability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even 70B-parameter LLMs with in-context learning or supervised fine-tuning cannot match a simple graph-based method (GraphGA) on controllable molecular design, establishing that text-only autoregression is fundamentally insufficient for graph-structured chemical data. The MolQA benchmark (139K synthesis routes, 8 properties, drug and material domains) provides the first systematic evaluation suite for multimodal molecular design with retrosynthetic planning, filling a gap left by existing PubChem-based datasets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to polymerization reactions (currently only monomer structures are handled), whether the 30-second A* time limit is sufficient for complex multi-step syntheses, and whether the LLM-based heuristic can be made reliably superior to domain-specific cost models rather than merely competitive.
