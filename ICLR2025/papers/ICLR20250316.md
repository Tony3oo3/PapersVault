## Problem

Score distillation methods for one-step diffusion generation suffer from a score mismatch issue: the student generator's initial distribution is far from the teacher's training distribution, so the teacher's predicted scores on generated samples are unreliable, leading to inaccurate gradient guidance and slow convergence to suboptimal solutions. Existing methods only use the endpoint of the pre-trained diffusion model as the teacher, ignoring the intermediate convergence trajectory between the student and teacher that could provide more reliable guidance at each training step.

## Input / Output

The distilled student generator takes a random noise vector (and optionally a class label for conditional generation or a text prompt for text-to-image) and produces a single image in one forward pass, eliminating the iterative denoising of the teacher. Experiments span resolutions from 64×64 (FFHQ, AFHQv2, CIFAR-10, ImageNet) to 256×256 (LSUN) and up to 1024×1024 (SDXL text-to-image). The method requires no training data during distillation—only the pre-trained teacher model and the initial student generator are needed.

## Method

DisBack introduces the full convergence trajectory of the teacher model into score distillation via two stages. In Degradation Recording, an auxiliary diffusion model initialized from the teacher is fine-tuned on the initial student generator's outputs, and intermediate checkpoints are saved to form a degradation path from the teacher distribution to the student's initial distribution. In Distribution Backtracking, this path is reversed to form a convergence trajectory, and the student generator is sequentially distilled against each intermediate checkpoint—starting from the one closest to the student's current distribution and progressing toward the teacher—using the standard score-difference gradient. This ensures that at every training step, the target score function is close enough to the student's current distribution to provide reliable guidance, mitigating the mismatch issue. The degradation recording stage requires only ~200 iterations, adding negligible overhead, and the approach is orthogonal to existing distillation methods like Diff-Instruct or DMD2.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64 conditional generation, DisBack achieves an FID of 1.38 with 1 NFE, outperforming DMD2 (1.51), Sid (1.52), CTM (2.06), and DDDM (3.47), and approaching the multi-step EDM baseline (1.36 at 511 NFE). On unconditional generation, DisBack achieves FID 10.88 on FFHQ 64×64, 9.97 on AFHQv2, 6.99 on LSUN-bedroom, and 10.30 on LSUN-cat, all with 1 NFE. DisBack also converges 1.79×–5.67× faster than Diff-Instruct (the closest baseline without convergence trajectory constraint) across datasets. For text-to-image generation from SDXL, DisBack achieves FID 18.96 at 1 NFE, beating DMD2 (19.01), SDXL-Turbo (24.57), and SDXL Lightning (23.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisBack outperforms all one-step distillation baselines on ImageNet 64×64: it beats DMD2 (1.51) by 0.13 FID, Sid (1.52) by 0.14, CTM (2.06) by 0.68, DDDM (3.47) by 2.09, Diff-Instruct (5.57) by 4.19, and CD (6.20) by 4.82. On unconditional generation (Table 1), DisBack (10.88 FFHQ, 9.97 AFHQv2, 6.99 LSUN-bedroom, 10.30 LSUN-cat) consistently outperforms CD (12.58, 10.75, 7.80, 11.00) and Diff-Instruct (19.93 FFHQ), while also surpassing multi-step EDM at 15 NFE (15.81 FFHQ, 13.67 AFHQv2). For text-to-image from SDXL, DisBack (FID 18.96, CLIP 0.335) edges out DMD2 (19.01, 0.336) and substantially outperforms SDXL-Turbo (24.57), SDXL Lightning (23.92), and LCM-SDXL (81.62). The ablation (Table 3) confirms that removing the convergence trajectory constraint degrades ImageNet FID from 1.38 to 5.96, validating the core contribution. DisBack also achieves 1.79×–5.67× faster convergence than Diff-Instruct, the closest method without trajectory constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisBack extends the score distillation framework established by Variational Score Distillation (Wang et al., 2023) and Diff-Instruct (Luo et al., 2023), which optimize a student generator by minimizing the KL divergence between generated and training distributions using two score functions. The key departure is that prior methods treat only the endpoint of the pre-trained diffusion model as the teacher, while DisBack introduces the entire convergence trajectory by constructing a degradation path from teacher to student and reversing it as a sequence of intermediate distillation targets. This trajectory constraint is orthogonal to existing distillation methods (DMD, DMD2, Consistency Models), meaning it can be layered on top of them without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DisBack achieves FID 1.38 on ImageNet 64×64 with a single NFE, surpassing both multi-step EDM (1.36 at 511 NFE) and prior one-step methods like DMD2 (1.51) and Sid (1.52), while also delivering 1.79–5.67× faster convergence than Diff-Instruct. The degradation recording stage requires only ~200 iterations, making the additional cost negligible. However, performance remains bounded by the teacher model's quality, and the authors acknowledge that the number of intermediate checkpoints and per-node training steps require careful tuning (N=11 checkpoints actually degrades performance to FID 9.15). An open theoretical question the authors raise is whether the sequence of score functions along the degradation path truly forms a valid gradient field, since neural network approximations may violate the conditions (path independence, zero curl) required for a potential function to exist.
