## Problem

While Sora and Stable Diffusion 3 demonstrated that Diffusion Transformers (DiT) can scale to generate content at arbitrary resolutions, they provide no implementation details, public checkpoints, or cross-modality flexibility. The original DiT architecture is capped at 600M parameters, relies on absolute positional embeddings that prevent resolution generalization, and diverges under mixed-precision training, making it impractical to scale to the multi-billion-parameter regime needed for high-fidelity generation. This paper addresses how to build a stable, scalable, and modality-agnostic diffusion transformer that supports arbitrary resolutions, aspect ratios, and temporal durations within a single unified framework.

## Input / Output

Lumina-T2X takes a text prompt (encoded by LLaMA-7B or SPHINX-13B) and produces a 1D token sequence that can be decoded into images at arbitrary resolutions (768×768 to 1792×1792 pixels), 720p videos up to 128 frames, multi-view 3D object images, or speech spectrograms. The key structural detail is that all modalities are flattened into a single 1D sequence using 2×2 latent patches with learnable [nextline] and [nextframe] tokens as spatial/temporal delimiters, enabling the same architecture to handle any resolution or duration without architectural changes. For resolution extrapolation, the model can generate images at resolutions up to ~1.75× beyond its 1024×1024 training resolution using NTK-aware scaled RoPE, time shifting, and proportional attention at inference time.

## Method

Flag-DiT modifies the DiT backbone with four key architectural changes: (1) replacing absolute positional embeddings with RoPE to enable resolution extrapolation via translational invariance, (2) substituting LayerNorm with RMSNorm and adding KQ-Norm before attention to prevent loss divergence under mixed-precision training at scale, (3) switching from DDPM to a linear-interpolation flow matching formulation that regresses a constant velocity field, and (4) injecting text conditioning through a zero-initialized gated cross-attention mechanism that learns sparse gating (turning off ~90% of text embeddings). The model is scaled from 600M to 7B parameters and extended to 128K-token context windows using FSDP and sequence parallelism. A critical empirical finding is that larger Flag-DiT models converge substantially faster than smaller ones, so the default 5B-parameter Lumina-T2I requires only 35% of the training compute of the 600M-parameter PixArt-α while achieving superior quality, and training from scratch outperforms ImageNet-pretrained initialization.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 label-conditioned generation, Flag-DiT-D-7B achieves FID 6.09 without classifier-free guidance, substantially outperforming DiT-XL/2 (FID 9.62) and SiT-XL/2 (FID 8.60) while using only 256M training images versus 1792M. With CFG, Flag-DiT-3B-G reaches FID 1.96 and IS 284.80, surpassing DiT-XL/2-G (FID 2.27) and SiT-XL/2-G (FID 2.06) with just 14% of the training iterations. On ImageNet 512×512, Flag-DiT-D-3B-G achieves FID 2.52 and IS 303.70, beating DiT-XL/2-G (FID 3.04, IS 240.82) by a wide margin. The default Lumina-T2I (5B Flag-DiT + 7B LLaMA) requires only 35% of the training computational costs of PixArt-α (600M DiT + 3B T5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flag-DiT-D-7B decisively outperforms DiT-XL/2 (FID 6.09 vs 9.62) and SiT-XL/2 (FID 6.09 vs 8.60) on ImageNet 256×256 without CFG, using only 14% of the training images (256M vs 1792M). With CFG, Flag-DiT-3B-G achieves the best FID (1.96) among all compared methods, beating DiT-XL/2-G (2.27), SiT-XL/2-G (2.06), and U-ViT-H/2-G (2.29) while also achieving the highest Recall (0.61). On 512×512, Flag-DiT-D-3B-G reduces FID from 3.04 (DiT-XL/2-G) to 2.52 and boosts IS from 240.82 to 303.70, demonstrating superior high-resolution detail capture. Against GAN-based baselines, Flag-DiT-3B-G with CFG (FID 1.96) surpasses StyleGAN-XL (FID 2.30) and BigGAN-deep (FID 6.95), while also achieving much higher Recall (0.61 vs 0.53 and 0.28). Compared to PixArt-α, the default Lumina-T2I configuration (5B Flag-DiT + 7B LLaMA) requires only 35% of the training computational costs while also enabling training-free resolution extrapolation that PixArt-α cannot match due to its use of absolute positional embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) by transplanting LLM design choices—RoPE, RMSNorm, and KQ-Norm—to stabilize and scale the diffusion transformer backbone to 7B parameters, while replacing the DDPM objective with flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2024) for a simpler linear-interpolation training pipeline. It positions itself as an open-source, implementation-detailed counterpart to Sora's closed demonstration of DiT scaling, and builds on PixArt-α/Σ's progressive training recipe for text-to-image generation but with a substantially larger backbone. The key conceptual departure is the use of learnable [nextline] and [nextframe] tokens combined with RoPE to unify image, video, multi-view, and audio generation into a single 1D sequence modeling framework, avoiding modality-specific architectural modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 5B Flag-DiT requires only 35% of PixArt-α's training compute challenges the prevailing assumption that smaller diffusion backbones with more iterations are more efficient, suggesting that the field should invest in larger DiT models rather than longer training schedules. The training-free resolution extrapolation (up to 1.8K from 1K training) via NTK-aware RoPE, time shifting, and proportional attention offers a practical path to ultra-high-resolution generation without additional data or fine-tuning. However, the current system trains each modality independently, leaving open whether joint multi-modal training can yield cross-modal transfer benefits, and video quality still lags significantly behind Sora. Data coverage limitations (hands, complex scenes) remain a shared bottleneck across all current generative models.
