## Problem

Existing action-driven video generation models rely solely on slow learning (large-scale pre-training) to build world models, lacking a fast learning mechanism for episodic memory storage. This causes inconsistencies across temporally distant frames in long videos, as the model cannot recall scenes beyond its context window. The paper addresses this by introducing a dual-speed learning system that mimics the complementary learning system in human brains, combining slow world-model learning with rapid episodic memory consolidation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a first-frame image (VAE-encoded to latent space), a stream of free-text action prompts (e.g., 'go left', 'turn right'), and the previous video chunk as conditioning, operating within a 32-frame context window. It produces a continuous long video sequence by iteratively generating new chunks in latent space without pixel-space decode-re-encode between iterations, extending up to 1000+ frames. For planning tasks, the output video is converted to executable actions via an inverse dynamics model that maps consecutive frame pairs to control signals.

## Method

SLOWFAST-VGEN combines a masked conditional video diffusion model (built on ModelScopeT2V) for slow learning with an inference-time TEMP-LORA module for fast learning. The slow learning model conditions on clean past-frame latents and text actions to denoise the next chunk, with loss masked to only the generated frames. The key innovation is the fast learning strategy: after each chunk is generated, the input and output latents are concatenated into a temporal continuum, noise is added to the entire sequence, and the LoRA parameters are updated via denoising on this action-agnostic sequence—emphasizing memorization of full trajectories rather than input-to-output transitions. A slow-fast learning loop further integrates frozen TEMP-LORA parameters from multiple episodes into the outer slow learning update, enabling context-aware skill acquisition across episodes for long-horizon planning tasks.

## Results

SLOWFAST-VGEN achieves an FVD of 514, substantially outperforming the best baseline AnimateDiff (782) and all other methods (AVDC: 1408, Streaming-T2V: 990, Runway Gen-3 Turbo: 1763, SEINE: 919, iVideoGPT: 1303). On long-video consistency, SLOWFAST-VGEN records only 0.37 scene cuts (SCuts) versus 0.89 for Streaming-T2V and 2.94 for AnimateDiff, and achieves the highest Scene Revisit Consistency (SRC) score of 93.71. The method also leads on PSNR (19.21), SSIM (60.53), LPIPS (25.06), and human evaluation (0.897), demonstrating superior quality and coherence across all evaluated metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLOWFAST-VGEN outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor AnimateDiff (FVD 782), SLOWFAST-VGEN reduces FVD by 34% (514 vs. 782) and cuts scene changes by nearly 8x (0.37 vs. 2.94 SCuts). Streaming-T2V is the strongest baseline on long-video consistency (0.89 SCuts, 91.02 SRC), but SLOWFAST-VGEN still leads by 2.4x on SCuts and by 2.69 points on SRC, which the authors attribute to TEMP-LORA storing the full trajectory versus Streaming-T2V's single anchor frame. Runway Gen-3 Turbo performs worst overall (FVD 1763, Human 0.205), failing to comprehend text action inputs. AVDC and iVideoGPT suffer from image-space diffusion instability and limited context, yielding FVDs of 1408 and 1303 respectively. SEINE achieves the second-best PSNR (18.04) but still trails SLOWFAST-VGEN on FVD (919 vs. 514) and SCuts (1.03 vs. 0.37). The ablation row (wo/ Temp-LoRA) confirms that the fast-learning module contributes 1.51 fewer scene cuts and 4.67 higher SRC over the slow-learning-only variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends action-driven video generation (Du et al. 2023; Yang et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2024a) and long video generation (Streaming-T2V, SEINE, iVideoGPT) by introducing a dual-speed learning architecture inspired by the complementary learning system in cognitive science. It builds on masked conditional video diffusion (MCDV) for the slow-learning component and adapts TEMP-LORA from long text generation (Wang et al. 2024b) to video, modifying the update mechanism to store full trajectories rather than input-output pairs. The key departure from prior long video generation methods (which condition only on the most recent chunk or a single anchor frame) is the explicit storage of episodic memory in LoRA parameters during inference, enabling consistency across arbitrarily long sequences without expanding the context window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental limitation in current video generation: the inability to maintain scene consistency beyond the model's context window, which is critical for applications like autonomous driving, robotics, and interactive simulation. The 200k-video dataset with language action annotations across five domains (Unreal, Minecraft, EPIC-KITCHENS, robotics, driving) is a practical resource for the community. However, the approach introduces additional inference-time computation, the model is not a complete world model (limited to trained scenarios), and the slow-fast learning loop is computationally intensive for full pre-training. Open questions include whether the TEMP-LORA mechanism scales to much longer sequences, whether it generalizes to unseen domains without retraining, and whether the complementary learning system framing can be extended to other generative tasks beyond video.
