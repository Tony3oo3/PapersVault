## Problem

Offline RL performance degrades sharply when high-return trajectories are scarce in the dataset, a common situation in both simulation and real-world settings. Existing reweighting methods focus exclusively on amplifying high-return samples, leaving low-return trajectories unused and providing no mechanism for an agent to escape from low-return regions where no high-return path exists.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes an offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') transition tuples, pre-computing per-state returns to partition states into high-return and low-return groups for contrastive sampling. At inference, given a current state s_t, the Planning Module generates an H-step trajectory of state-action pairs via iterative denoising, from which the immediate action â_t is extracted. The contrastive module operates on intermediate noisy reconstructions during training, requiring no additional input at test time.

## Method

ContraDiff couples a diffusion-based trajectory generator (U-Net denoiser with return-guided sampling) with a contrastive constraint on the generated states. The Planning Module produces H-step trajectories by denoising from Gaussian noise conditioned on the current state, guided by a learned return predictor J_φ. The Contrastive Module then applies a modified InfoNCE loss (positive terms removed from the denominator, following Schroff/Sohn) that pulls each generated state toward κ sampled high-return states and away from κ sampled low-return states, with sampling probabilities governed by sigmoid functions of state returns (optionally restricted to dynamically consistent neighbors via MiniBatch K-Means clustering). The total objective is L_d + L_v + λ_c·L_c, where the contrastive loss is weighted by 1/(h+1) to emphasize near-horizon states, and the return predictor is optimized independently from the diffusion denoiser.

## Results

ContraDiff achieves the best or second-best normalized score on 25 out of 27 sub-optimal D4RL datasets (3 locomotion environments × 3 dataset mixes × 3 expert ratios). The advantage is most pronounced in the hardest setting (Rand-Exp with 10% expert data): on Halfcheetah-Rand-Exp-0.3, ContraDiff-SRD scores 88.7 vs. the best baseline (Diffuser) at 75.8, and on Hopper-Rand-Exp-0.2 it scores 75.3 vs. CDE at 66.5. In the easier M-Exp setting, gains are smaller but consistent, e.g., ContraDiff-SRD reaches 100.1 on Hopper-M-Exp-0.2 vs. DD at 90.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ContraDiff outperforms all 8 regular offline RL baselines (CDE, HD, Decision Stacks, ReDiffuser, DT, TT, Diffuser, DD) on 25 of 27 sub-optimal datasets, with the largest margins in the Rand-Exp setting where high-return trajectories are scarcest (e.g., +12.9 over Diffuser on Halfcheetah-Rand-Exp-0.3, +8.8 over CDE on Hopper-Rand-Exp-0.2). Against the closest competitor Diffuser (its own backbone), ContraDiff-SR wins on 14/27 and ContraDiff-SRD on 13/27, with the two variants complementing each other across environments. Decision Stacks and DT are consistently the weakest baselines, often scoring below 20 on Rand-Exp datasets where ContraDiff exceeds 50. In the resampling-method comparison (Table 2), ContraDiff surpasses AW+Diffuser, RW+Diffuser, AW-DW+Diffuser, and U-DW+Diffuser in the majority of settings, confirming that contrastive exploitation of low-return samples is more effective than reweighting high-return samples alone. The ablation (ContraDiff-C without the contrastive loss) underperforms full ContraDiff on all 9 tasks, and ContraDiff-N (high-return samples only) also loses, validating that both the contrastive mechanism and the use of low-return data are necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

ContraDiff builds directly on Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) as its trajectory-generation backbone and adapts the soft contrastive sampling strategy from Thoma et al. (2020) to the RL setting. It departs from prior contrastive learning in RL (CURL, Qiu et al., Agarwal et al.), which used CL to learn state or task representations, by instead applying CL to directly constrain the states within a diffusion-generated planned trajectory toward high-return and away from low-return states. It also contrasts with prior work on imbalanced offline datasets (Hong et al., 2023a,b) that only reweight high-return samples, arguing that low-return trajectories carry valuable 'avoidance' information that reweighting discards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically common scenario—offline datasets dominated by low-return trajectories—where existing reweighting methods fail when the agent enters low-return regions with no learned escape path. The contrastive mechanism provides a principled way to extract negative information from abundant low-quality data, and the 27-dataset evaluation with 50 seeds gives strong empirical support. However, the method is currently limited to trajectory-generation-based diffusion methods (Diffuser-style), and the authors acknowledge it cannot yet be applied to action-level or state-action-pair diffusion policies. An open question is whether the contrastive constraint can be generalized to non-diffusion offline RL methods or to latent trajectory representations.
