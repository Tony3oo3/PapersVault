## Problem

Existing strategies for mitigating catastrophic forgetting in continual learning—such as experience replay—rely on pre-defined heuristics with no theoretical guarantee of optimality. Prior theoretical analyses of learning curves in teacher-student frameworks characterised performance under fixed protocols but did not derive the protocols themselves. This paper asks: what is the provably optimal task-selection and learning-rate schedule that minimises forgetting while maximising performance across all tasks?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252135/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a teacher-student setup where inputs are i.i.d. N-dimensional Gaussian vectors, labels are generated by single-layer teacher networks with a tunable pairwise similarity parameter γ, and the student is a two-layer network with K=T hidden units and task-specific readout heads. Given a fixed training time budget α_F for the second phase (after task 1 is fully learned), the method outputs a continuous-time task-selection protocol t_c(α) specifying which task to train on at each step, and a continuous learning-rate schedule η(α). For real-data deployment, the derived 'pseudo-optimal' strategy reduces to a simple rule: train exclusively on the new task until its loss matches the old task's loss, then switch to interleaved replay—requiring only online monitoring of both tasks' generalisation error.

## Method

The core technique combines statistical physics dimensionality reduction with Pontryagin's maximum principle. First, the high-dimensional stochastic SGD dynamics of the two-layer student network are reduced in the N→∞ limit to a low-dimensional system of ODEs governing order parameters (student-teacher overlaps M, student-student overlaps Q, and readout weights V), following the Saad–Solla / Biehl–Schwarze framework. Second, Pontryagin's principle is applied to these ODEs: a cost functional (weighted sum of final generalisation errors) is augmented with conjugate (backward) dynamics for the order parameters, and the optimal control is obtained by pointwise minimisation of the Hamiltonian over the discrete task index and continuous learning rate. The resulting optimal protocol exhibits a structured 'focus-then-revision' form—an initial phase training only on the new task, followed by interleaved replay—whose transition point is determined by the conjugate variables. Jointly optimising the learning rate reveals a nontrivial schedule that 'jumps' between two task-dependent curves, and the theory predicts an inverted non-monotonic dependence of forgetting on task similarity (minimal at intermediate γ), explained by disentangling first-layer alignment effects from readout-layer convergence timescales that diverge as γ→1.

## Results

On synthetic teacher-student data (K=T=2, α_F=25), the optimal replay protocol achieves the lowest average loss across all task similarity values γ, and critically reverses the non-monotonic forgetting pattern: minimal forgetting occurs at intermediate γ (~0.3) rather than maximal, unlike the no-replay baseline. The pseudo-optimal strategy (focus phase + randomised revision) performs comparably to the full optimal protocol on synthetic data. On Fashion-MNIST (γ=0.5, 10k steps per task), the pseudo-optimal strategy achieves the lowest average loss over the full training trajectory, outperforming both no-replay and interleaved replay. On CIFAR-10, the pseudo-optimal strategy yields up to 3% improvement in loss over the best heuristic, with only a narrow region (<0.5% loss increase) where it slightly underperforms interleaved replay.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the interleaved replay strategy from Lee et al. (2022), which alternates one sample from the new task with one from the old task. On synthetic data (Fig. 3a), the optimal protocol clearly outperforms interleaved replay across all γ values, with the gap most pronounced at intermediate similarity. The pseudo-optimal variant (retaining the focus phase but randomising the revision phase) closes most of this gap, confirming that the structured ordering of the revision phase is less critical than the initial focus on the new task. On Fashion-MNIST (Fig. 6–7), the pseudo-optimal strategy dominates: for small downstream tasks (500–1000 samples) it approaches no-replay performance, while for larger tasks (4000–8000 samples) it approaches interleaved performance, effectively interpolating between the two heuristics. On CIFAR-10 (Fig. 11–12), the pseudo-optimal strategy achieves up to 3% lower loss than interleaved replay during training, though a small region exists where it is ~0.5% worse than interleaved. The jointly optimal learning-rate-and-task-selection protocol (Fig. 9) further outperforms all constant-rate strategies and hand-tuned annealing schedules (exponential, power-law), confirming the value of the full optimal control formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed but previously unconnected lines of work: statistical physics analyses of neural network training dynamics (Saad & Solla, 1995; Biehl & Schwarze, 1995; Lee et al., 2021, 2022) and optimal control theory (Pontryagin, 1957; Saad & Rattray, 1997). While Lee et al. derived exact ODEs for continual learning in teacher-student setups and identified the non-monotonic task-similarity effect, and Saad & Rattray applied control theory to optimise learning rates in committee machines, no prior work had combined these tools to derive optimal *task-selection* protocols. The paper departs from the heuristic replay strategies (no-replay, interleaved) used in all prior continual learning analyses by formulating task selection as a genuine optimal control problem over the mean-field ODEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, non-heuristic derivation of optimal training protocols for continual learning, revealing that the optimal strategy has a structured 'focus-then-revise' form and that optimal replay inverts the previously observed non-monotonic task-similarity effect (forgetting becomes minimal rather than maximal at intermediate similarity). The pseudo-optimal strategy extracted from the theory is simple to implement and transfers to real datasets (Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10), offering a practical tool for practitioners. Key limitations include the restriction to two-layer networks, i.i.d. Gaussian inputs, and the online setting; Pontryagin's principle yields necessary but not sufficient conditions for global optimality. Open questions include extension to deeper architectures, batch learning, structured data, and task difficulty (curriculum learning).
