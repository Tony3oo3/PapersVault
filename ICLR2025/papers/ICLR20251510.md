## Problem

Existing multimodal models (CLIP, ImageBind, LanguageBind, VAST) align modalities pairwise via cosine similarity to a single anchor, which does not guarantee that non-anchor modalities are mutually aligned. This pairwise limitation leads to suboptimal performance in tasks requiring joint understanding of three or more modalities, and no direct mathematical formulation exists for computing similarity among more than two embedding vectors at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251510/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method encodes video clips (8 frames), audio clips, text captions, subtitles, and optionally depth maps into unit-norm embedding vectors in a shared n-dimensional latent space (n ≈ 1024). The GRAM volume is computed as the square root of the determinant of the k×k Gram matrix formed by the k modality vectors (k = 3–5, where k ≪ n), yielding a scalar alignment measure. At inference, this volume replaces cosine similarity for ranking, and the model also produces binary matched/unmatched predictions via a data-anchor matching head.

## Method

GRAM replaces cosine similarity with the volume of the k-dimensional parallelotope spanned by all modality vectors, computed as √det(G) where G is the Gram matrix of inner products. In the two-modality case this reduces to sin(θ), the complement of cosine similarity, but for k > 2 the determinant expands to include all pairwise cross-products (e.g., for T-V-A: 1 − VA² − TA² − TV² + 2·TA·AV·VT), ensuring all modalities are jointly aligned rather than only to an anchor. The GRAM-based contrastive loss (L_D2A and L_A2D) minimizes volume for matched samples and maximizes it for negatives within a batch, using a learnable temperature τ. The model uses VAST's encoders (BERT-B, BEATs, EVA-CLIP-ViT-G; ~1B parameters) with VAST's fusion layers removed, is further pretrained on 150k VAST27M samples with batch size 256, and combines the volume-based losses with a data-anchor matching loss (λ=0.1) for binary prediction.

## Results

In zero-shot text-to-video retrieval, the GRAM model with T-VA modalities achieves R@1 of 54.2 on MSR-VTT (+4.9 over VAST), 59.0 on ActivityNet (+7.6), and 83.9 on VATEX (+3.9), while the T-VAS variant reaches 54.8 on MSR-VTT and 83.5 on VATEX. In fine-tuning, the T-VAS GRAM model achieves R@1 of 64.0 on MSR-VTT (+7.4 over VAST) and 64.8 for V2T (+7.2). On audio tasks, GRAM T-AV reaches R@10 of 75.3 on AudioCaps (+9.9 over VAST) and Acc@10 of 78.1 on VGGSound (+3.6). The GRAM volume measure also correlates strongly (ρ=0.923) with downstream model performance, validating it as a useful evaluation metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The GRAM model is built on the same VAST backbone (BERT-B, BEATs, EVA-CLIP-ViT-G, ~1B params) but replaces VAST's cosine-based pairwise contrastive loss with the proposed volume-based GRAM loss, removing VAST's fusion layers entirely. Against VAST (the primary same-architecture baseline), GRAM T-VA improves zero-shot T2V R@1 by 4.7–7.6 points across MSR-VTT, DiDeMo, and ActivityNet, and finetuning T2V R@1 by up to 7.4 points on MSR-VTT (T-VAS: 64.0 vs. 56.6). GRAM also outperforms all other baselines: LanguageBind (T-V only) by 9.4 points on MSR-VTT T2V zero-shot, VideoPrism-b by 2.8 points, and mPLUG-2 by 7.1 points. On audio retrieval (AudioCaps), GRAM T-AV surpasses VAST T-AV by 9.9 points on R@10 (75.3 vs. 65.4) and beats LanguageBind T-A by 13.5 points on R@1 (33.2 vs. 19.7). The GRAM T-V configuration (without audio) already exceeds VAST T-VA on MSR-VTT (52.8 vs. 49.3), demonstrating that the volume-based alignment is more effective even with fewer modalities. GRAM additionally scales to five modalities (adding depth via ChronoDepth), reaching 55.3 R@1 on MSR-VTT, confirming that the parallelotope volume computation generalizes beyond the typical 2–3 modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pairwise cosine-similarity paradigm established by CLIP and extended by ImageBind, LanguageBind, and VAST, where each modality is aligned to a single anchor without guaranteeing mutual alignment among non-anchor modalities. It builds directly on VAST's encoder architecture (BERT, BEATs, EVA-CLIP) but replaces VAST's MLP-based fusion layers with a geometric volume computation over the Gram matrix of all modality vectors. The contrastive loss structure follows CLIP's InfoNCE formulation, and the data-anchor matching component borrows the hard-negative mining strategy from ALBEF, but the core novelty is the parallelotope-volume measure that simultaneously captures inter-modal relationships in the full embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRAM provides a mathematically grounded, drop-in replacement for cosine similarity that naturally generalizes from 2 to n modalities, addressing a structural limitation that has constrained multimodal models to anchor-based pairwise alignment. The finding that GRAM volume strongly correlates (ρ=0.923) with downstream retrieval performance positions it as a useful diagnostic metric for evaluating latent-space quality independent of task-specific benchmarks. Open questions include whether the k ≪ n assumption holds as embedding dimensions shrink or modality counts grow, how the method interacts with the modality gap phenomenon (which the paper acknowledges but does not resolve), and whether the gains persist when the backbone is not already a strong pre-trained multimodal model.
