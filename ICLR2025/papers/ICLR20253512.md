## Problem

Existing native 3D generation methods face three intertwined design challenges: VAE inputs (point clouds lack texture, multi-view images lack explicit 3D structure), latent space structure (permutation-invariant set latents sacrifice editability, tri-planes don't map changes to specific object parts), and output representations (texture-less SDFs need post-processing, volumetric tri-planes are memory-prohibitive at high resolution). No prior method provides a 3D latent space that is simultaneously compact for efficient diffusion, explicitly structured for interactive editing, and decodable into high-fidelity renderable geometry.

## Input / Output

The 3D VAE consumes 8 posed views of 512×512 RGB-D-N renderings, channel-concatenated into 15 channels (RGB, unprojected 3D positions, surface normals, and Plücker camera embeddings), and produces a point-cloud structured latent z = [z_x ⊕ z_h] ∈ R^((3+10)×768) where z_x is an FPS-sampled sparse point cloud and z_h is a 10-dim feature per point. The decoder outputs 73,768 surfel Gaussians (13-dim attributes each) uniformly distributed on the object surface with ~97% opacity utilization, compared to ~50% for pixel-aligned methods. Conditional generation accepts text (CLIP penultimate tokens), single RGB images (DINOv2 global+patch features), or point clouds, and the cascaded design allows stage-1 point cloud outputs from external models (e.g., Point-E) to be fed directly into stage-2 for texture synthesis.

## Method

The core contribution is a point-cloud structured 3D latent space: an SRT-like transformer encoder processes multi-view RGB-D-N renderings into an unstructured set latent z_z, which is then projected onto a sparse FPS-sampled point cloud via cross-attention (query = PE(z_x), key/value = z_z), yielding a compact N=768-point latent that is both permutation-invariant for diffusion and spatially anchored for editing. The decoder uses a DiT-B/2 transformer followed by K=3 cascaded attention-based upsamplers (ratios 8×4×3) to progressively densify the latent into 73,768 surfel Gaussians, trained end-to-end with rendering, geometry (depth distortion + normal alignment), KL, and GAN losses. For generation, a two-stage cascaded flow-matching DiT (24 layers, 1024 hidden dim, 458M params) first generates the geometry z_x via a point cloud diffusion model, then generates texture features z_h conditioned on z_x, enabling geometry-texture disentanglement and interactive editing by directly manipulating the sparse point cloud latent before regenerating features.

## Results

On image-to-3D generation (Table 2), GAUSSIANANYTHING achieves the best CLIP-I (89.06), MUSIQ (65.17), P-FID (8.72), P-KID (3.22), and MMD (15.48) among all baselines, with a particularly large margin on 3D shape metrics (P-FID 8.72 vs. next-best LN3Diff at 27.17). On text-to-3D generation (Table 1), the method leads all baselines on CLIP ViT-B/32 (31.80), ViT-L/14 (29.38), MUSIQ-AVA (4.99), and Q-Align (3.13). The method trails LGM on 2D rendering FID (24.21 vs. 19.93) and KID (0.76 vs. 0.55), which the authors attribute to LGM's reliance on a pre-trained multi-view diffusion model for texture quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAUSSIANANYTHING outperforms all native 3D diffusion baselines (LN3Diff, Shape-E) on every 3D shape metric: P-FID improves from 27.17 (LN3Diff) to 8.72, and P-KID from 10.02 to 3.22, reflecting the benefit of the point-cloud-structured latent and surfel Gaussian decoder. Against multi-view-to-3D methods (LGM, CRM, Lara, One-2-3-45), the proposed method wins on CLIP-I, MUSIQ, and all 3D shape metrics, while LGM retains an edge on 2D FID/KID due to its pre-trained MVDream texture prior. Among single-image-to-3D feed-forward methods (OpenLRM, Splatter-Image), GAUSSIANANYTHING leads on CLIP-I, MUSIQ, P-FID, P-KID, and MMD, with the largest gap on P-FID (8.72 vs. 35.74 for OpenLRM). For text-to-3D, the method surpasses 3DTopia (30.10→31.80 CLIP ViT-B/32) and LN3Diff (29.12→31.80) on all four metrics, with the most notable gain on Q-Align (3.13 vs. 2.22 for LN3Diff). The only metrics where a baseline wins are FID/KID (LGM) and COV (Shape-E at 61.33% vs. 59.50%), which the authors note reflect LGM's stronger 2D texture fidelity and Shape-E's implicit SDF coverage rather than overall 3D quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the native 3D diffusion model lineage (Point-E, Shape-E, LN3Diff, 3DTopia) by redesigning three key components: the VAE input (multi-view RGB-D-N renderings instead of raw point clouds), the latent space structure (point-cloud-structured tokens obtained by cross-attending SRT-style set latents onto a sparse FPS-sampled point cloud), and the output representation (surfel Gaussians via cascaded upsampling instead of textureless SDFs or volumetric tri-planes). The cascaded flow-matching framework, where a geometry model precedes a texture model, draws on the cascaded diffusion paradigm from 2D image generation and prior 3D point cloud diffusion work (Lyu et al., 2023; Schroppel et al., 2024), but applies it to a structured latent that enables interactive editing. The DiT backbone, flow matching objective, and SRT-style multi-view encoder are all adopted from prior work; the novelty lies in their specific composition into a point-cloud-structured latent space that bridges the flexibility of set representations with the editability of structured ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The point-cloud-structured latent space provides a practical path to interactive 3D editing with diffusion models—analogous to drag-and-drop editing in 2D—by allowing direct manipulation of the sparse geometry tokens before texture regeneration. This addresses a key limitation of prior native 3D diffusion models that used unstructured set latents, where editing was not naturally supported. However, the authors acknowledge that texture quality still lags behind multi-view generation pipelines (e.g., LGM), and the method does not yet leverage 2D pre-training priors as effectively as those approaches. Open questions include whether the latent design scales to scene-level generation, whether physics-based rendering (e.g., 3DGRT) can be integrated, and whether the cascaded framework generalizes to more complex multi-object or articulated scenarios.
