## Problem

Traditional risk-sensitive offline RL minimizes the risk estimated by a single critic, but because risk measures like CVaR emphasize rare tail events, even small errors in the estimated return distribution can cause large inaccuracies in the computed risk. These errors are amplified by the distribution shift inherent in offline settings where the agent cannot receive corrective feedback. The paper introduces model risk—the worst-case risk over a set of plausible alternative return distributions—into offline RL for the first time, shifting the optimization target from minimizing estimated risk to minimizing the worst-case risk across model uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations (trading features for finance, lidar/kinematic/goal data for Airsim, or standard D4RL state vectors) and produces a deterministic action via a TD3+BC-style actor. Unusually, the actor's loss requires computing a model risk value at each step, which in turn demands accurate estimates of the return distribution's mean and standard deviation from an ensemble of quantile-regression critics, plus a Wasserstein-2 tolerance ε derived from critic disagreement. The Fourier feature network maps the raw state-action pair into a high-dimensional sinusoidal embedding before quantile regression, specifically to overcome spectral bias that would otherwise corrupt these critical statistics.

## Method

MR-IQN integrates the TD3+BC actor-critic framework with a model risk objective, using an ensemble of IQN/TQC critics trained via quantile regression. The key mechanism is the critic-ensemble criterion: μ is set to the minimum mean across critics, σ to the maximum standard deviation, and ε to the maximum squared Wasserstein-2 distance between each individual critic and a quantile-mixture ensemble (constructed by sorting and interpolating all critics' quantile estimates), requiring no additional hyperparameters. The model risk is then computed in closed form via Theorem 1 from Bernard et al. (2023), which gives the worst-case spectral risk over a Wasserstein-2 ball parameterized by (μ, σ, ε). To ensure the mean and standard deviation estimates are accurate—critical because they define the plausible scenario set—the critics employ a learned Fourier feature network that maps inputs through random sinusoidal projections, addressing spectral bias that causes conventional MLPs to fail at capturing high-frequency components of the quantile function. The actor minimizes a weighted sum of the computed model risk and a behavior-cloning L2 loss, with a stop-gradient trick applied to μ to prevent the policy gradient from collapsing to a mean-deviation-only signal.

## Results

In the high-uncertainty Forex trading environment, MR-IQN achieves a negative CV@R(50%) risk of 556.94±172.92 and Wang(-0.5) risk of 1639.79±57.88, outperforming the closest baseline IQN-TD3+BC (454.16±11.76 and 1307.52±90.05) by 18.5% and 25.4% respectively. In the self-driving (Airsim) scenario, MR-IQN achieves the highest success rate (87.4%–88.8%) and lowest collision rate (10.6%–11.6%) across both risk measures. On D4RL Mujoco-v2, MR-IQN achieves an overall normalized score of 68.59, marginally outperforming 1R2R (67.35–67.78) and substantially exceeding ORAAC (31.83) and CODAC (61.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MR-IQN consistently outperforms IQN-TD3+BC (the closest non-model-risk baseline) in the high-uncertainty Forex environment, reducing CV@R(50%) risk by 18.5% and Wang(−0.5) risk by 25.4%, while also achieving higher mean returns. In the lower-uncertainty Stock environment, MR-IQN matches or slightly exceeds IQN-TD3+BC on risk metrics (31.16 vs. 30.92 for CV@R(50%)) at a small cost in mean return, illustrating a conservatism–performance tradeoff. Against CODAC and ORAAC, MR-IQN dominates across all finance and self-driving settings; CODAC and ORAAC suffer from hyperparameter sensitivity and overly restrictive behavior-policy constraints, respectively, leading to substantially worse risk scores (e.g., ORAAC's CV@R(50%) risk in Forex is −99.90 vs. MR-IQN's 556.94). On D4RL, MR-IQN (68.59 overall) marginally beats 1R2R (67.35–67.78) and clearly outperforms ORAAC (31.83) and CODAC (61.34), though 1R2R retains an edge on individual Random-dataset environments (e.g., HalfCheetah Random: 35.5 vs. 20.48). In the self-driving Airsim scenario, MR-IQN achieves the best success rate (88.0% for CV@R(50%), 87.4% for Wang(−0.5)) and lowest collision rate (11.6% and 10.6%), surpassing IQN-TD3+BC by 6.2 and 3.6 percentage points in success rate respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends risk-sensitive offline RL (ORAAC, CODAC, 1R2R) by replacing the standard objective of minimizing estimated risk H_φ(Z^π) with minimizing *model risk*—the worst-case risk over a Wasserstein-2 ball of plausible alternative return distributions—a concept borrowed from robust mathematical finance (Bernard et al., 2023; Breuer & Csiszár, 2016). The implementation builds on the IQN/TQC distributional critic architecture and the TD3+BC offline RL framework, while introducing a critic-ensemble criterion that sets the model-risk tolerance ε from inter-critic disagreement without extra hyperparameters. A secondary technical contribution addresses spectral bias in quantile regression via Fourier feature networks (Tancik et al., 2020; Li & Pathak, 2021), which the authors show is necessary for accurate mean and standard deviation estimation that the model-risk formula requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper is the first to formalize model risk minimization in offline RL, addressing a genuine vulnerability: in offline settings the agent cannot verify its risk estimates, so small distributional errors can propagate into large risk miscalculations. The 11.2%–18.5% risk reduction over the strongest baseline (IQN-TD3+BC) is most pronounced in high-uncertainty environments (Forex with 100× leverage), supporting the claim that model risk matters most when model error is likely. Limitations include restriction to spectral risk measures (excluding EV@R, CMV), dependence on accurate μ/σ estimation, and evaluation limited to two domain-specific environments plus D4RL. Open questions include extension to POMDPs, whether the critic-ensemble criterion generalizes to non-IQN distributional critics, and how the approach scales to high-dimensional continuous control beyond the tested settings.
