## Problem

While numerous factuality enhancement methods (contrastive decoding, representation editing) have been proposed to reduce hallucinations in LLMs, no prior work had systematically evaluated whether these methods undermine context-faithfulness—the ability of LLMs to follow external context that contradicts their parametric knowledge. This paper asks whether improved factuality comes at the cost of making LLMs overconfident in their internal knowledge, thereby ignoring or contradicting injected contextual information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251476/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For context-faithfulness evaluation, the framework takes multi-hop questions from MQUAKE paired with counterfactual edit context and k-shot contextual demonstrations (3, 5, or 10 shots, with optional COT reasoning) as input, producing generated text answers scored for editing accuracy. For factuality evaluation, inputs are TruthfulQA multiple-choice questions or FACTSCORE open-ended prompts, yielding answer selections and atomic fact extractions. For interpretability analysis, the method captures token-level logits and hidden states specifically at positions where context-injected knowledge diverges from parametric knowledge, enabling fine-grained comparison across methods.

## Method

This empirical study systematically evaluates eight factuality enhancement methods (DoLa, ICD, CD, ITI, TruthX, RECITE, Conf-Num/Verb, Self-Eval) on both factuality benchmarks (TruthfulQA, FACTSCORE) and context-faithfulness benchmarks (MQUAKE ICE, MeLLo) using LLAMA2-7B/13B-CHAT, revealing that factuality gains are modest and inconsistent while context-faithfulness declines are severe and universal (up to 69.7% for TruthX). To explain these declines, the authors design a Knowledge Token Capturing algorithm that identifies the specific tokens where context-injected knowledge diverges from parametric knowledge, then analyzes logit distributions (showing decreased probability for new knowledge tokens and increased probability for parametric tokens) and t-SNE-projected hidden states (showing post-injection convergence toward pre-injection parametric distributions for representation editing methods). Case studies further categorize context-unfaithful outputs into three failure modes: ignoring context, denying context, and misformatting context, providing concrete evidence of the overconfidence mechanism.

## Results

On TruthfulQA (MC2), factuality enhancement methods yield modest and inconsistent gains: TruthX improves LLAMA2-7B-CHAT from 54.6% to 73.9%, while ICD reaches 69.1%, but several methods (e.g., DoLa at 60.8%, ITI at 54.7%) show only marginal or even negative improvements on other metrics. On the MQUAKE ICE task (context-faithfulness), all methods suffer severe declines: TruthX drops LLAMA2-7B-CHAT from 68.5% to 26.7% (↓61.1%) at 3-shot and to 26.5% (↓67.9%) with COT(10-shot), while ICD drops from 68.5% to 50.1% (↓26.9%). The largest observed context-faithfulness decrease reaches 69.7%, far exceeding the modest factuality gains, confirming that factuality enhancement is not a free lunch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new method but systematically evaluates eight existing factuality enhancement approaches against unmodified LLAMA2-CHAT baselines. On factuality (TruthfulQA MC2), TruthX achieves the largest gain (+19.3 pp on 7B) but with inconsistent improvements across metrics; ICD and DoLa show moderate gains (+14.5 and +6.2 pp respectively on 7B), while ITI barely improves (54.7% vs. 54.6% baseline). On context-faithfulness (MQUAKE ICE), every method loses substantially: TruthX is the worst offender (↓61.1–67.9 pp), CD and ICD show moderate declines (↓13.7–29.5 pp), DoLa is in between (↓14.8–22.4 pp), and ITI is the mildest (↓11.9–20.6 pp). The paper's central finding is that the context-faithfulness cost (average >20 pp decline) far outweighs the factuality benefit (average <10 pp gain), and this trade-off holds across both 7B and 13B models, both contrastive decoding and representation editing families, and both simple ICE and the more realistic MeLLo evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established research threads: factuality enhancement (contrastive decoding methods like DoLa, ICD, CD; representation editing methods like ITI, TruthX) and knowledge editing / context-faithfulness evaluation (MQUAKE, MeLLo, in-context editing). Rather than proposing a new method, it repurposes knowledge editing benchmarks as a diagnostic tool to measure whether factuality-enhanced LLMs can still follow counterfactual context. The paper departs from the prior work's implicit assumption that reducing the 'knowing vs. telling' gap is a free improvement, by systematically showing that the same mechanisms that sharpen parametric recall also suppress the model's willingness to adopt externally provided knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art factuality methods (especially TruthX) can degrade context-faithfulness by up to ~68% is a critical warning for the RAG and knowledge-editing communities: methods deployed to reduce hallucinations may simultaneously make models *less* responsive to retrieved or user-supplied context, introducing a new class of failure. The interpretability analysis (logit shifts toward parametric tokens, hidden-state convergence toward pre-injection distributions) provides a mechanistic explanation that points toward concrete remediation strategies (e.g., boosting context-relevant token probabilities, allowing broader hidden-state dispersion). Open questions include whether the trade-off is fundamental to the 'amplify parametric confidence' paradigm or specific to the tested methods, whether it persists at larger scales, and how to design enhancement that is context-aware rather than uniformly pro-parametric.
