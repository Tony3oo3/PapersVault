## Problem

Current multi-instance generation (MIG) methods train a single unified adapter to handle both instance positioning and fine-grained attribute rendering simultaneously, requiring large amounts of detailed instance-level annotations and making it difficult to build robust renderers. This 'unified adapter challenge' has prevented MIG from being widely adopted in state-of-the-art foundational models like SD2 and SDXL, with most existing methods relying on the less capable SD1.5.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-specified layout (bounding boxes for each instance) and per-instance text descriptions (category, color, material, etc.) as input. It first produces a coarse scene depth map at 512×512 resolution, which can be upsampled to 768×768 or 1024×1024 to match higher-resolution base models, and then renders a final multi-instance RGB image. The detail rendering stage is entirely training-free, meaning the same trained adapter can be paired with any foundational model that has a pretrained depth ControlNet without retraining.

## Method

3DIS decouples MIG into two stages: (1) a Layout-to-Depth model that generates a scene depth map, and (2) a training-free detail renderer that paints fine-grained attributes. For stage 1, LDM3D is fine-tuned with an augmented pyramid noise strategy to emphasize low-frequency depth recovery, and a MIGC-style adapter is trained on COCO with instance descriptions stripped of fine-grained adjectives so the model focuses on structure and scene composition. For stage 2, a pretrained ControlNet injects the depth map into the target model, with FFT-based low-pass filtering applied to ControlNet feature maps to suppress high-frequency artifacts. The detail renderer replaces standard Cross-Attention with per-instance rendering: each instance is independently rendered using its own text prompt, SAM segments precise instance masks from the depth map to prevent attribute leakage between overlapping boxes, and all per-instance feature maps are merged via softmax-weighted spatial masks at every attention layer.

## Results

On the COCO-Position benchmark (complex layouts with ≥5 instances), 3DIS (SD1.5) achieves AP=56.83, AP75=62.40, and AP50=82.29, surpassing the previous SOTA MIGC by +11.8 AP and +16.3 AP75. On the COCO-MIG benchmark, the training-free detail renderer of 3DIS (SD1.5) reaches an average Instance Attribute Success Ratio (IASR) of 53.0, improving by ~33 points over MultiDiffusion (22.3) and by ~2.5 points over the adapter-based InstanceDiffusion (50.5). When paired with SDXL, 3DIS further improves to IASR=56.0 and MIoU=47.0, demonstrating that the framework transfers seamlessly to higher-quality base models without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DIS decisively outperforms all five baselines on both benchmarks. Against the training-free methods BoxDiffusion and MultiDiffusion, 3DIS leads by a wide margin: on COCO-Position, AP75 jumps from 2.12 (BoxDiff) and 4.24 (MultiDiff) to 62.40; on COCO-MIG, average IASR rises from 15.7 and 22.3 to 53.0. Against the adapter-based GLIGEN, 3DIS improves AP75 from 40.75 to 62.40 and IASR from 31.3 to 53.0, while also achieving better FID (23.24 vs. 26.80). The most direct comparison is with MIGC, the previous SOTA: 3DIS gains +16.3 AP75 on COCO-Position and +2.6 IASR (69.7 vs. 67.1) on COCO-MIG when combined as 3DIS+MIGC. Compared to InstanceDiffusion, 3DIS (SD1.5) achieves a comparable IASR (53.0 vs. 50.5) without any training for the rendering stage, and extends naturally to SD2.1 and SDXL for higher visual quality (IASR up to 56.0 with SDXL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MIGC adapter architecture for layout conditioning and the LDM3D text-to-depth model, but departs fundamentally from prior MIG methods (GLIGEN, MIGC, InstanceDiffusion) by decoupling instance positioning from attribute rendering. Where previous approaches train a single unified adapter to handle both spatial placement and fine-grained attribute synthesis, 3DIS introduces a scene depth map as an intermediate representation: a layout-to-depth adapter handles only coarse scene composition, while a training-free cross-attention renderer (augmented with SAM segmentation and FFT low-pass filtering on ControlNet features) handles per-instance detail. This separation is motivated by the observation that unified adapters require expensive instance-level annotations and are difficult to port across foundational models, which is why MIG has not been adopted in SD2 or SDXL despite ControlNet's widespread integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 3DIS enables multi-instance generation on any foundational model with a pre-trained depth ControlNet (SD2, SDXL, and stylistic LoRA variants) without retraining the detail renderer, lowering the barrier to adopting MIG in production pipelines. The 16.3% AP75 gain over MIGC on COCO-Position and the 35% IASR improvement over MultiDiffusion on COCO-MIG demonstrate that the depth-map intermediate representation genuinely improves scene coherence in complex, overlapping layouts. Open questions include whether the approach extends to DIT-based architectures (the authors mention Flux as future work), how it scales beyond 6 instances, and whether the coarse depth map bottleneck limits fidelity for highly textured or transparent objects.
