## Problem

Existing VFM-assisted 3D instance segmentation methods (e.g., SAM3D, SAI3D) are either offline or rely on slow hand-crafted mask merging, making them impractical for embodied tasks that demand online, real-time, fine-grained 3D perception from streaming RGB-D video. The core challenge is that future frames are unavailable, instances span multiple frames requiring efficient cross-frame matching, and naive 2D-to-3D projection produces geometrically inconsistent masks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251362/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming RGB-D video where each frame provides a color image and a depth image with known camera poses; point clouds are obtained by projecting depth to 3D. At each time step t, the model predicts instance masks only for the current frame's point cloud and incrementally merges them into the accumulated scene, producing temporally consistent 3D point-wise masks plus a fixed-size query vector per instance. The output is the union of all observed point clouds segmented into instances, updated online without access to future frames.

## Method

ESAM lifts SAM-generated 2D masks to 3D queries via a geometric-aware pooling module: 2D masks serve as superpoint indices over point cloud features extracted by a sparse U-Net with memory-based adapters, and weighted pooling (using normalized relative point positions within each superpoint) produces 3D queries with one-to-one correspondence to SAM masks. A dual-level query decoder then iteratively refines these queries over three transformer layers, using superpoint features for efficient cross-attention (M << N) while predicting fine-grained point-wise masks from point features. For online merging, three auxiliary tasks (axis-aligned bounding box regression, contrastive instance features trained across adjacent frames, and per-category semantic classification) yield fixed-size vectors per mask, enabling the similarity matrix between current and previous masks to be computed via a single matrix multiplication followed by bipartite matching—replacing the slow per-pair geometric comparison used by prior methods.

## Results

On ScanNet200 (class-agnostic 3D instance segmentation), ESAM achieves 42.2 AP / 63.7 AP50 / 79.6 AP25, surpassing the best offline VFM-assisted method Open3DIS (34.6 AP) by 7.6 AP and the best online method SAM3D (20.2 AP) by 22.0 AP. The real-time variant ESAM-E (using FastSAM) reaches 43.4 AP at ~10 FPS (100 ms/frame total). On ScanNet, ESAM-E+FF achieves 42.6 AP / 61.9 AP50 at 9.8 FPS, outperforming the previous best online method TD3D-MA (39.0 AP at 3.5 FPS) by 3.6 AP while being nearly 3× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESAM outperforms all VFM-assisted baselines on ScanNet200: it beats SAM3D (20.2 AP) by 22.0 AP, SAI3D (28.2 AP) by 14.0 AP, Open3DIS (34.6 AP) by 7.6 AP, and SAMPro3D (18.0 AP) by 24.2 AP, while also being 19× faster than SAM3D in the 3D pipeline (80 ms vs. 1518 ms). In the zero-shot transfer setting (Table 2), ESAM leads all methods on both SceneNN (28.8 AP vs. 18.6 for SAI3D) and 3RScan (14.1 AP vs. 9.5 for Open3DIS), confirming strong generalization. Among online 3D instance segmentation methods (Table 3), ESAM-E+FF surpasses TD3D-MA by 3.6 AP on ScanNet and 7.3 AP on SceneNN at nearly 3× the speed (9.8 vs. 3.5 FPS). ESAM-E+FF also outperforms INS-Conv (57.4 AP50) on ScanNet AP50 (61.9 vs. 57.4). However, ESAM still trails the strongest offline methods Oneformer3D (59.3 AP) and TD3D (46.2 AP) on ScanNet, which have access to the full reconstructed point cloud rather than streaming frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VFM-assisted 3D instance segmentation line (SAM3D, SAI3D, SAMPro3D, Open3DIS) by replacing their hand-crafted 2D-to-3D projection and geometric-similarity-based mask merging with a learnable, query-based pipeline. It builds directly on SAM for 2D mask generation and on the memory-based adapter architecture from Xu et al. (2024) for temporal-aware 3D feature extraction via sparse U-Net. The key conceptual departure is representing each 3D mask as a fixed-size query vector, which enables efficient matrix-operation-based merging across frames rather than expensive per-point geometric comparisons, and makes the entire process compatible with online streaming input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESAM addresses a practical bottleneck for embodied AI: prior VFM-assisted 3D segmentation methods are either offline or too slow for real-time robot control, and this paper closes that gap with a 20× speedup over SAM3D while substantially improving accuracy. The query-based merging paradigm is a reusable design pattern that could transfer to other online 3D perception tasks. Open questions include whether the heavy 3D sparse U-Net backbone can be further accelerated (e.g., via pruning or distillation), whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, and how it performs when paired with next-generation faster VFMs beyond FastSAM.
