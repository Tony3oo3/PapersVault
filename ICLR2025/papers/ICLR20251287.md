## Problem

Existing state-entropy maximization (SEM) methods for unsupervised RL pre-training are either on-policy and sample-inefficient, or off-policy but biased because they estimate the entropy of the replay buffer's state distribution rather than the target policy's stationary distribution. No principled off-policy SEM algorithm existed that could provably converge to an optimal SEM policy from arbitrary off-policy data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251287/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an arbitrary off-policy replay buffer of (s, a, s') transitions and the initial state distribution p₀, with no requirement that the data was collected by the current policy. It outputs a single stationary Markov policy π(a|s) parameterized as a tanh-Gaussian MLP, obtained by first computing stationary distribution correction ratios w*(s,a) and then performing i-projection (KL minimization against the data policy). In continuous domains, ν and μ are parameterized by scalar-output MLPs, and a separate e-network is trained to evaluate the Bellman residual for every action at extraction time.

## Method

SEMDICE formulates SEM as a concave program over the joint state-action stationary distribution d(s,a) and state stationary distribution d̄(s), subject to Bellman flow and marginalization constraints, with an f-divergence regularizer toward the data distribution d^D. Via Lagrange duality and Fenchel conjugate, the inner maximization over d and d̄ is eliminated in closed form, reducing the problem to a single convex minimization over dual variables (ν, μ). A numerically stable log-sum-exp variant replaces the intractable sum-exp term, and a single-sample Monte Carlo estimate of the Bellman operator makes the objective fully model-free and optimizable by SGD on (s,a,s') samples. The optimal policy is extracted via i-projection: minimizing KL(d^D(s)π(a|s) ‖ d^D(s)π*(a|s)) using the computed correction ratios w*(s,a), which avoids directly parameterizing the policy during the main optimization.

## Results

On randomly generated 20-state tabular MDPs, SEMDICE is the only method that converges to the optimal SEM policy, while on-policy policy-gradient SEM requires approximately 100× more samples to reach near-optimal entropy. On the URLB benchmark (12 tasks across Walker, Quadruped, and Jaco Arm domains), SEMDICE achieves maximum state entropy in Walker and Jaco Arm and near-maximum in Quadruped, reaching a proper SEM policy with as few as 100K environment interactions versus ~2M for RND, Disagreement, and ProtoRL. In the fine-tuning phase (100K steps with task-specific rewards), SEMDICE significantly outperforms all data-based (SEM-based) and knowledge-based baselines in adaptation efficiency, though it underperforms the competence-based CIC method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEMDICE outperforms all data-based SEM baselines (ProtoRL, APT, MEPOL) on both state entropy maximization and downstream adaptation: these methods fail to converge to a single stationary SEM policy, exhibiting oscillatory non-stationary behavior, while SEMDICE converges to a stable optimal SEM policy. Against knowledge-based methods (RND, Disagreement), SEMDICE achieves comparable peak state entropy but with dramatically better sample efficiency (~100K vs. ~2M environment steps to converge). In the URLB fine-tuning evaluation, SEMDICE significantly outperforms RND, Disagreement, ProtoRL, APT, and DIAYN across all three domains in rapid adaptation to downstream reward functions. The sole exception is CIC (contrastive intrinsic control), a competence-based method that outperforms SEMDICE in fine-tuning, which the authors attribute to CIC's additional representation learning component. In tabular MDPs, SEMDICE also outperforms the on-policy MaxEnt method (Hazan et al., 2019) in learning efficiency, while computing a single stationary Markov policy rather than maintaining a growing set of policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEMDICE extends the DICE (Distribution Correction Estimation) family—particularly OptiDICE (Lee et al., 2021)—from reward maximization to state entropy maximization by introducing a marginalization constraint and an additional dual variable μ that encodes the state stationary distribution. It departs fundamentally from existing SEM methods (Hazan et al., 2019; Liu & Abbeel, 2021b; Yarats et al., 2021; Mutti et al., 2021), which rely on policy gradients with particle-based entropy estimators and are either on-policy or biased when applied off-policy. By operating directly in the space of stationary distributions and leveraging Fenchel conjugates with f-divergence regularization, SEMDICE reduces the SEM problem to a single convex minimization solvable from arbitrary off-policy data, a formulation not previously available in the SEM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEMDICE provides the first principled, unbiased off-policy algorithm for state entropy maximization, addressing a long-standing gap where existing methods either required on-policy samples or optimized a biased proxy (replay buffer entropy rather than target policy entropy). The convex formulation guarantees stable optimization and a unique solution, and the empirical demonstration that prior off-policy SEM methods (ProtoRL, APT) exhibit non-stationary oscillatory behavior rather than converging to a single SEM policy is a useful diagnostic for the field. Open questions include formal convergence guarantees for the practical biased objective (16), scaling to high-dimensional pixel-based domains (the authors note representation learning integration as future work), and whether the DICE-based approach can be extended to competence-based objectives like mutual information maximization.
