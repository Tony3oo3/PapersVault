## Problem

Existing graph prompt tuning methods focus exclusively on bridging the gap between pre-training pretexts and downstream tasks, neglecting adversarial robustness. The paper demonstrates that even simple structural perturbations (e.g., adding a few edges) can render carefully designed prompts highly misleading, causing catastrophic accuracy drops of over 40% in few-shot node classification. No prior work provides a systematic defense mechanism for graph prompts under adversarial attack scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251034/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained GNN (GraphMAE or GraphCL), a potentially attacked graph with node feature matrices, and k few-shot labeled subgraphs per class (5-shot or 10-shot). Each input node is represented by its multi-hop neighbor subgraph (structure + features). The output consists of node class predictions from the frozen model and the tuned defense prompt vectors (one per filtering scenario, fused per-node). The prompts operate in the feature space rather than the structural space, allowing real-time adaptation to the current perturbation state during tuning.

## Method

GPromptShield introduces a two-pronged shield defense system. The Direct Handling component (MD-PT) designs a hybrid multi-defense prompt that assigns node-specific prompt subsets based on three filtering criteria: low-degree nodes receive a degree prompt p_d, low-centrality-similarity nodes receive a similarity prompt p_s, and out-of-distribution-detected nodes receive an OOD prompt p_o; these are fused per-node via weighted average or self-attention. Training optimizes the downstream loss plus three auxiliary regularizers: hidden feature smoothness (L_s), KL-divergence alignment between biased and unbiased node embedding distributions (L_kl), and a multi-prompt overlap constraint (L_o) that enforces similarity among prompts sharing overlapping node sets. The Indirect Amplification component (IA-PT) is a plug-in tuning strategy that trains a structure-free MLP on few-shot samples, generates pseudo-labels via softmax confidence, and uses them to re-tune any existing prompt template while keeping the pre-trained model frozen. A theoretical proof (Theorem 1) establishes that under the GPF linear model assumption, a prompt matrix M exists satisfying f(Ã, X+P) = f(A, X) when BN is invertible and W has full column rank.

## Results

Under 5-shot non-adaptive attacks with GraphMAE pre-training on Cora, IA-PT maintains 68.30% accuracy under MetaAttack (25%) and 66.62% under Heuristic (50%), a drop of less than 3% from its 68.93% clean accuracy. In contrast, GPF-plus drops from 63.40% to 39.46% (−24%) under the same MetaAttack, and GPPT drops from 67.79% to 43.17% (−25%). Across all attack types and datasets, the proposed MD-PT and IA-PT keep performance within ±10% of clean accuracy, while baselines degrade by 20–45%. Under adaptive attacks (Table 2), IA-PT with GraphMAE on Cora-ML achieves 66.17% (GCNSVD) and 63.49% (GCNJaccard), outperforming the best baseline GPF-plus (47.21% and 46.26%) by roughly 17–19 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPF/GPF-plus (Fang et al., 2024), the proposed methods show the largest gains: on Cora with GraphMAE, IA-PT retains 68.30% under MetaAttack while GPF-plus collapses to 39.46% (a 29-point gap), and MD-PT holds 62.49% versus GPF-plus's 39.46%. GPPT (Sun et al., 2022) starts strong on clean data (67.79% on Cora/GraphMAE) but degrades to 43.17% under MetaAttack, whereas IA-PT stays at 68.30%. All-in-one (Sun et al., 2023a) is the weakest baseline overall, dropping to 29.75% on Cora under MetaAttack. GraphPrompt (Liu et al., 2023b) and MultiGPrompt (Yu et al., 2024) also suffer 20–30 point drops under attacks, confirming that compatibility-focused prompts are highly vulnerable. Under adaptive attacks (Table 2), IA-PT with GraphMAE on Cora-ML reaches 66.17% (GCNSVD) versus GPF-plus's 47.21% and ProG's 17.05%, demonstrating that the indirect amplification strategy generalizes to white-box targeted attacks. Notably, on clean data the proposed methods remain competitive (IA-PT: 68.93% vs. GPPT's 67.79% on Cora/GraphMAE), showing that robustness is achieved without sacrificing accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the graph prompt tuning lineage (GPF, GPPT, GraphPrompt, MultiGPrompt, All-in-one), which has focused exclusively on bridging the compatibility gap between pre-training pretexts and downstream tasks. It departs from this by reframing the core question from 'how do we make prompts compatible?' to 'how do we make prompts robust when the input graph is adversarially perturbed?' The design draws on OOD-based adversarial detection (Li et al., 2022b) for the out-of-distribution filtering tip, and on language prompt robustness benchmarks (PromptBench, Zhu et al., 2023) as conceptual motivation. The indirect amplification strategy is a direct adaptation of the distribution-consistency training idea from Li et al. (2022b) to the prompt-tuning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies a previously overlooked security vulnerability in graph prompt tuning: even well-designed compatibility prompts can be catastrophically misleading under modest structural perturbations, a risk that becomes critical as prompt-tuned GNNs are deployed in safety-sensitive applications. The extensible shield framework (new filtering tips can be added as attacks evolve) and the theoretical existence proof provide a principled foundation for future robust prompt design. However, the evaluation is confined to small citation networks (≤2,810 nodes), and the theoretical proof relies on strong invertibility assumptions (W and BN must be invertible) that may not hold for deeper or more complex GNNs. Open questions include scalability to large-scale graphs (ogbn-arxiv results are notably weak), applicability to structure-based prompts, and whether the OOD detector's training cost is acceptable in practice.
