## Problem

Unsupervised Concept-based Interpretable Networks (CoINs) learn dictionaries of high-level concepts for prediction, but visualizing what each concept encodes remains a major bottleneck for large-scale images. Existing visualization approaches—selecting most-activating training samples or running activation maximization in pixel space—produce unnatural, repetitive patterns that are difficult for users to interpret, and the visualization process is entirely decoupled from the training pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252911/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes 256×256 RGB images and produces a class prediction, a set of K concept activation feature maps (each 3×3, non-negative), a high-quality reconstructed image via the generative model, and per-concept relevance scores. During interpretation, a user can selectively scale any single concept activation by a factor λ while holding all others fixed, and the generative model renders the visual effect of that concept in isolation, enabling granular and interactive exploration of what each concept encodes.

## Method

VisCoIN builds an interpretable classifier g on top of a frozen pretrained classifier f (ResNet50) and a frozen pretrained generator G (StyleGAN2-ADA), learning three lightweight modules: a concept extractor Ψ that reads hidden layers of f to produce K concept feature maps, a linear predictor Θ that pools and classifies, and a concept translator Ω that maps concept activations into the W+ latent space of G. The training loss enforces four properties simultaneously: output fidelity (cross-entropy between g and f), input fidelity and viewability (L1, L2, LPIPS, and a reconstruction-classification term through G), sparsity (ℓ1 penalty on activations), and dictionary diversity (kernel orthogonality). The key architectural insight is that routing reconstruction through a pretrained generative model—rather than a standard pixel decoder—yields perceptually faithful reconstructions and, crucially, makes individual concept activations directly controllable: modifying φ_k(x) by a factor λ and passing the result through Ω and G produces a natural image where the visual effect of that single concept is clearly visible, enabling interactive interpretation without pixel-space optimization.

## Results

On the three main benchmarks, VisCoIN achieves 87.71% accuracy on CelebA-HQ, 79.44% on CUB-200, and 79.89% on Stanford Cars, matching the pretrained ResNet-50 classifier (f) on CelebA-HQ and coming within 1–2.4% on the fine-grained tasks. Reconstruction quality is substantially improved over baselines: FID drops from 30.45/53.16/64.16 (FLINT) to 8.55/15.85/6.77 (VisCoIN) across the three datasets. Faithfulness (median FF_x at τ=0.2) reaches 0.171/0.146/0.118 for VisCoIN versus 0.201/0.002/<10⁻³ for FLINT, showing a dramatic advantage on complex datasets. Concept visualization consistency (CC_k) averages 85.5±13.9, 85.0±8.4, and 82.7±8.3% for VisCoIN, outperforming FLINT (82.6±22.7, 72.6±18, 70±16.3) and FLAEM (57±17.3, 55.6±13.6, 54.9±13.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLINT (Parekh et al., 2021), VisCoIN achieves comparable accuracy on CelebA-HQ (87.71% vs 87.25%) and a clear advantage on CUB-200 (79.44% vs 77.20%) and Stanford Cars (79.89% vs 75.95%). The most striking gap is in faithfulness: on CUB-200 and Stanford Cars, FLINT's faithfulness drops to near-zero (0.002 and <10⁻³ at τ=0.2), while VisCoIN maintains 0.146 and 0.118, indicating that FLINT's concepts encode little classification-relevant information on complex datasets. VisCoIN also substantially outperforms FLINT on reconstruction FID (e.g., 6.77 vs 64.16 on Stanford Cars) and consistency (82.7±8.3 vs 70±16.3 on Stanford Cars). Against FLAEM (Sarkar et al., 2022), VisCoIN dominates across all metrics: accuracy is 27–30 points higher on CUB-200 and Stanford Cars (79.44% vs 51.76%, 79.89% vs 50.02%), faithfulness is orders of magnitude higher, and consistency is roughly 30 points better. FLAEM's near-random faithfulness (<10⁻³) on both fine-grained datasets suggests its learned concepts are largely uninformative for prediction in those settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised Concept-based Interpretable Network (CoIN) lineage, building directly on FLINT (Parekh et al., 2021) for the concept extraction architecture Ψ and the overall two-stage (extract-then-predict) design. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a concept translator module Ω that maps learned concept activations into the latent space of a pretrained generative model (StyleGAN2-ADA), drawing on GAN-inversion techniques (Abdal et al., 2019; Yao et al., 2022) for the reconstruction pipeline. Unlike prior unsupervised CoINs (SENN, FLINT, FLAEM) that treat visualization as a post-hoc probing step, VisCoIN integrates a 'viewability' property into training via perceptual and classification-based reconstruction losses, making the generative model an active component of the learning objective rather than a separate analysis tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in unsupervised CoINs: for large-scale images, activation-maximization visualizations produce unnatural, hard-to-interpret outputs that obscure what a concept actually encodes. By coupling concept learning with a pretrained GAN through a lightweight translator, VisCoIN enables interactive, granular concept visualization (imputing activations and observing generated modifications) that is both higher quality (FID ~8–16 vs. ~50–70 for baselines) and more faithful to classification-relevant information. Open questions include whether the approach extends to diffusion-model latent spaces (where meaningful traversal is still unclear), whether concepts can be made more unimodal (the paper notes single concepts sometimes modify multiple features), and how the method scales to supervised CoINs or multimodal settings.
