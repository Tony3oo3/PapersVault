## Problem

Current MLLMs for chart understanding rely on a single MLP connector trained primarily on chart-to-table alignment, which captures only basic numerical values while missing semantic attributes like colors, shapes, and rendering details. Heavy alignment on a single connector can erode general capabilities such as instruction following and visual counting, and random or co-upcycle MoE initialization leads to expert homogenization or training instability. The paper addresses how to bridge the modality gap with a connector that captures diverse chart representations while preserving general MLLM performance.

## Input / Output

The model takes a chart image at 490×490 resolution and a text instruction as input. The CLIP ViT-Large encoder produces 1225 visual tokens, which are routed through a top-2-of-4 MoE connector before being concatenated with text tokens for the LLM. The output is a text answer, or in Program-of-Thought (PoT) mode, the model generates Python variables and operation code that are executed to produce the final numerical answer, addressing the mathematical reasoning weakness of open-source MLLMs.

## Method

ChartMoE replaces the standard 2-layer MLP projector with a Mixture-of-Experts connector (4 experts, top-2 activated) while keeping the vision encoder and LLM frozen during alignment. The key insight is diversely initializing each expert through distinct alignment pre-training tasks: chart→table (numerical extraction), chart→JSON (semantic attributes like colors and layout), chart→code (rendering details with explicit values and hex codes), plus the vanilla connector to preserve general capabilities. A new ChartMoE-Align dataset of ~1M chart-table-JSON-code quadruples is generated via a pipeline using fine-tuned DePlot for attribute extraction and matplotlib code templates. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) alignment pre-training of individual connectors, (2) high-quality knowledge learning on MMC instructions with LoRA on the LLM, and (3) chart-specific annealing tuning on ChartQA and ChartGemma. Notably, the standard MoE load-balancing loss is removed because the diverse initialization naturally produces specialized expert preferences over visual tokens (e.g., background vs. data points vs. legends), and forcing uniformity degrades performance.

## Results

On ChartQA (the primary benchmark), ChartMoE+PoT achieves 84.64% relaxed accuracy at margin 0.05 (average of Human and Augmented splits), improving the previous SOTA TinyChart+PoT from 80.48% to 84.64% (+4.16%). On ChartBench (zero-shot, no fine-tuning on train set), ChartMoE reaches 51.67% overall accuracy, surpassing the base model InternlmXC-v2 (48.41%) and ChartGemma (38.46%). On ChartFC and ChartCheck (fact-checking), ChartMoE achieves 74.73% and 78.87%/79.20% (T1/T2) respectively, outperforming ChartGemma by +4.4%, +7.4%, and +4.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChartMoE+PoT (84.64% @0.05) outperforms the previous SOTA TinyChart+PoT (80.48%) by +4.16% on ChartQA, and surpasses ChartAst (79.00%), ChartLlama (75.76%), and ChartGemma+PoT (76.56%) by 5.6–8.9 points. Compared to the base model InternlmXC-v2 (72.00%), ChartMoE gains +9.20% without PoT and +12.64% with PoT. On ChartBench (zero-shot), ChartMoE (51.67%) beats ChartGemma (38.46%), OneChart (29.93%), and all specialist chart models (<10%), with the largest gain on extra chart types (+15.86% over baseline). On ChartFC and ChartCheck, ChartMoE leads ChartGemma by +4.4% and +7.4%/+4.9% respectively, without any fine-tuning on those benchmarks. On general VQA benchmarks (MME, MMBench), ChartMoE preserves the base model's capabilities nearly intact, whereas directly fine-tuned InternlmXC-v2 shows significant degradation, validating the benefit of retaining the vanilla connector as one MoE expert.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChartMoE extends the LLaVA-style MLLM architecture (built on InternLM-XComposer2) by replacing the standard two-layer MLP projector with a Mixture-of-Experts connector, drawing on the ST-MoE router-and-expert design. Unlike prior MoE-in-MLLM work (MoE-LLaVA, DeepSeek-VL, CuMo) that applies MoE to the LLM or vision encoder to scale model capacity, ChartMoE applies MoE exclusively to the lightweight connector, with the key departure being a diverse-alignment initialization strategy (chart-to-table, chart-to-JSON, chart-to-code) instead of random or co-upcycling initialization. The alignment tasks build on and extend prior chart alignment efforts (ChartAst's chart-to-table, ChartReformer's chart-to-JSON, OneChart's chart-to-dictionary), while the ChartMoE-Align dataset synthesizes quadruples from ChartQA, PlotQA, and ChartY sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MoE connector adds only ~63M parameters and negligible inference overhead, making it a practical drop-in replacement for the standard MLP projector in LLaVA-like MLLMs, and the approach generalizes to other base models (demonstrated on LLaVA-v1.5-7B). The diverse initialization yields interpretable expert specialization (background vs. data-point vs. legend tokens), and retaining the vanilla connector as one expert preserves general VQA capabilities without additional retraining. Key limitations include the dependency on chart-specific alignment tasks for initialization (non-chart domains would need new alignment designs) and the non-plug-and-play nature of the multi-expert projector. An open question is whether the balance-loss removal generalizes beyond chart scenarios where visual token distributions are inherently imbalanced.
