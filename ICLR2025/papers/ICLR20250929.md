## Problem

Existing benchmarks for interleaved multimodal comprehension and generation are constrained in scale (e.g., InterleavedBench has only 815 samples), narrow in query format, and lack reliable automated evaluation metrics. Current scoring approaches either assess single-modality quality (BLEU, FID), rely on potentially biased GPT-4-based judging, or measure only text-image alignment (CLIPScore) without capturing the holistic quality of interleaved outputs. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a 20K-sample benchmark and a dedicated fine-tuned scoring model.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 20,103 interleaved text-and-image queries (average 1.32 images per question) spanning 3 categories, 12 fields, and 102 subfields, with 96.6% open-ended and 3.4% multiple-choice formats. Models must produce interleaved text-and-image responses in arbitrary sequences; the scoring model then takes the original query, the model's full interleaved response, and ground-truth references to emit a single percentage score derived from a 1–6 rubric. Category-specific criteria cover dimensions such as text quality, image quality, text-image coherence, method practicality, creativity, and stylistic consistency, with an additional penalty for harmful content.

## Method

MMIE is curated from four source datasets (WikiHow, VIST, MathVista, ReMI) through a two-stage pipeline: (1) extraction and restructuring into a unified interleaved text-and-image instruction format, and (2) multi-step quality control including lexical-overlap deduplication, formatting standardization, and manual review. For automated evaluation, the authors fine-tune InternVL-2-4B as a scoring model on 800 human-annotated examples scored on a six-point scale using detailed, category-specific rubrics with reference answers for each score level. The scoring model is validated on 200 held-out human-scored samples, achieving the highest cosine similarity (0.873) and lowest MAE (1.444) among all compared scorers (GPT-4o, CLIPScore, Anole, base InternVL-2.0-4B). The paper evaluates eight LVLMs—four open-source interleaved models (MiniGPT-5, EMU-2, GILL, Anole) and four integrated LVLM+T2I pipelines (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5, LLaVA-34b, Qwen2-VL-72b each paired with Openjourney, SD-3, SDXL, or Flux)—revealing that even the best configuration (GPT-4o + SDXL) scores only 65.47%.

## Results

On the MMIE benchmark (20,103 interleaved multimodal queries), the best open-source interleaved LVLM, Anole, achieves only 55.22% average score, while the best integrated LVLM (GPT-4o + SDXL) reaches 65.47%. All four open-source interleaved models (MiniGPT-5, EMU-2, GILL, Anole) average just 50.80%, highlighting substantial room for improvement. The proposed MMIE-Score metric achieves the highest correlation with human annotations among all evaluated scoring methods, with a cosine similarity of 0.873, MSE of 3.300, MAE of 1.444, and Pearson coefficient of 0.113.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four open-source interleaved LVLMs, Anole (55.22%) outperforms MiniGPT-5 (50.92%), GILL (51.58%), and EMU-2 (45.33%) by 8.4%, 7.0%, and 21.8% respectively in average score. Integrated LVLMs (non-interleaved LVLM + T2I model) significantly outperform all open-source interleaved models by an average of 25.2%, with GPT-4o + SDXL achieving the best overall score of 65.47%. For the scoring metric, MMIE-Score (fine-tuned InternVL-2-4B) outperforms GPT-4o (cosine 0.733, MAE 1.573), Anole (cosine 0.805, MAE 1.600), InternVL-2.0-4B (cosine 0.736, MAE 3.165), and CLIPScore (cosine 0.639, MAE 2.251) on all four correlation metrics, demonstrating superior alignment with human annotations. On the OOD COCO generalization test, MMIE-Score (cosine 0.673, Pearson 0.104) ranks second only to GPT-4o (cosine 0.745, Pearson 0.097), confirming reasonable cross-task generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nascent line of interleaved multimodal evaluation, building on the small-scale InterleavedBench (Liu et al., 2024, 815 samples) and the broader LVLM benchmarking tradition (MMMU, MME, MMBench) that has so far assessed only single-modality outputs. It draws on four source datasets (VIST, WikiHow, MathVista, ReMI) to construct a 20K-sample benchmark supporting both interleaved inputs and outputs across 12 fields, and departs from prior evaluation practice by replacing GPT-4o-based scoring with a fine-tuned InternVL-2-4B scoring model trained on human-annotated rubrics. The work also sits at the intersection of interleaved generation architectures (Chameleon, Show-o, Anole) and text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, Flux), evaluating both native interleaved LVLMs and integrated LVLM+T2I pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial gap: even the strongest integrated system (GPT-4o + SDXL) achieves only 65.47%, while native interleaved LVLMs average around 50%, suggesting that current architectures are far from solving coherent interleaved generation. The finding that integrated LVLM+T2I pipelines outperform native interleaved models by ~25% on average raises an open question about whether native interleaved training can close this gap or whether hybrid architectures will remain the practical path forward. The proposed scoring model's demonstrated alignment with human annotation (outperforming GPT-4o as a judge) offers a more reproducible and less biased evaluation protocol, though the low Pearson correlations across all methods (≤0.11) hint at residual disagreement that warrants further investigation. As interleaved generation becomes central to next-generation multimodal systems, MMIE is likely to become a standard reference benchmark.
