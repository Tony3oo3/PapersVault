## Problem

Current long-context LLMs can process inputs exceeding 100,000 tokens yet consistently fail to generate outputs beyond ~2,000 words, despite over 1% of real user prompts explicitly requesting longer responses. Through controlled experiments, the paper identifies the root cause: the model's maximum generation length is capped by the longest output present in its SFT dataset, not by its pretraining exposure or architectural context window.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251306/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a user writing instruction containing an explicit word-count target (ranging from 0 to 20,000+ words) and produces a single coherent, well-structured long-form text in one generation pass. During the AgentWrite data-construction phase, the instruction is first decomposed into a paragraph-level plan (each paragraph 200–1,000 words), then each paragraph is generated sequentially with all previously written content fed as context to maintain coherence. The resulting SFT pairs span output lengths from 2k to 32k words, with the trained model achieving 10k–20k word generations in a single forward pass.

## Method

AgentWrite is a two-stage divide-and-conquer agent pipeline: Step I prompts an LLM to produce a detailed writing outline specifying the main content and target word count for each paragraph; Step II serially generates each paragraph, conditioning on the original instruction, the full plan, and all previously generated paragraphs to preserve coherence. Using GPT-4o as the backbone, AgentWrite constructs LongWriter-6k—6,000 instruction-response pairs with outputs of 2k–32k words—by selecting long-output instructions from GLM-4 SFT data and WildChat logs. The trained model (LongWriter-9B/8B) is fine-tuned on LongWriter-6k mixed with 180k general SFT examples using token-averaged loss weighting, then further refined via DPO on 4k preference pairs specifically targeting long-form writing. The key insight is that the output-length ceiling is a data artifact of SFT, not a model-capacity limit, so simply adding long-output examples during alignment unlocks the latent generation capacity already present in long-context pretraining.

## Results

On LongBench-Write, LongWriter-9B-DPO achieves the best overall score (S̄=84.0) with Sl=82.6 and Sq=85.4, surpassing all proprietary models including Claude 3.5 Sonnet (S̄=80.7) and GPT-4o (S̄=78.6). In the critical [4k, 20k) word range, LongWriter-9B-DPO achieves Sl=90.3, while all other models score below 28.5 (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) or near 0 (GPT-4 Turbo, Llama-3.1-8B). DPO further improves both length adherence (+4%) and quality (+3%) over the SFT-only LongWriter-9B. The LongWrite-Ruler test confirms the trained models extend maximum generation length from ~2k to 10k–20k words.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongWriter-9B-DPO (9B params) outperforms all proprietary baselines on overall score: Claude 3.5 Sonnet (80.7), GPT-4o (78.6), GPT-4o mini (77.6), and GPT-4 Turbo (67.3). The gap is most dramatic in the [4k, 20k) word range, where LongWriter-9B-DPO scores Sl=90.3 versus at most 26.0 for Claude 3.5 Sonnet and 0 for GPT-4 Turbo and Llama-3.1-8B. Among open-source baselines, Mistral-Large-Instruct (77.0) and GLM-4-9B-chat (68.3) are the strongest, but both are surpassed by LongWriter-9B-DPO by 7+ points overall. Suri-I-ORPO, the only prior model explicitly aligned for long-form generation, scores lowest (56.6) due to poor quality (Sq=53.5). Notably, LongWriter-9B-DPO's quality score (85.4) is competitive with proprietary models (GPT-4o: 91.8, Claude 3.5: 87.7) while its length adherence (82.6) far exceeds all baselines, and human evaluation shows it ties GPT-4o in pairwise preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of long-context LLM research (which has focused on extending input windows to 100k+ tokens) and LLM alignment, identifying that the output window—not the input window—is the binding constraint for long-form generation. The AgentWrite pipeline repurposes the agent-based divide-and-conquer paradigm (as in AutoGen, MetaGPT, Branch-Solve-Merge) for the novel purpose of synthetic data construction rather than task execution. It explicitly departs from the instruction backtranslation approach used by Suri (Pham et al., 2024), the only prior work targeting output length extension, showing through ablation that backtranslation degrades both length adherence and quality. The DPO alignment stage builds on standard preference optimization literature but applies it specifically to long-writing instruction following.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core empirical finding—that a model's maximum output length is directly bounded by the maximum output length in its SFT data—is actionable and broadly applicable: practitioners can unlock longer generation simply by adding long-output examples during alignment, without architectural changes. However, the current ceiling of 10k–20k words (vs. 100k+ input context) and the observed quality trade-offs (coherence and clarity drop for creative writing) leave open the question of whether truly 100k-word outputs are achievable. Inference efficiency for very long generations (~55s for 10k tokens on a single H800) remains a practical bottleneck, and the AgentWrite pipeline's segmentation artifacts suggest the data construction method itself may need refinement for narrative and creative genres.
