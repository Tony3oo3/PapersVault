## Problem

No finite-time regret bounds existed for online adaptive control of the risk-sensitive Linear Exponential-of-Quadratic Regulator (LEQR) in the finite-horizon episodic setting. While risk-neutral LQR had well-developed regret theory and risk-sensitive RL had bounds for tabular MDPs, the continuous-state/action risk-sensitive LEQR with unknown system matrices remained unexplored, leaving a gap for applications like portfolio liquidation where risk aversion and episodic resets are natural.

## Input / Output

The agent observes state vectors x_t ∈ R^n and executes continuous control actions u_t ∈ R^m over T time steps per episode, with the system resetting to a fixed initial state x_0 at the start of each of N episodes. The system matrices (A, B) are unknown and must be estimated online from observed trajectories; the agent also knows the cost matrices Q, Q_T, R and the risk-sensitivity parameter γ. The output is a sequence of linear feedback gains K_t computed from the current system estimate via the modified Riccati equation, yielding controls u_t = K_t x_t (plus optional exploration noise).

## Method

The paper proposes two algorithms. Algorithm 1 is an epoch-based least-squares greedy method that doubles the number of episodes per epoch, estimates (A, B) via l2-regularized least squares, and applies the certainty-equivalent optimal controller from the modified Riccati equation; it achieves Õ(log N) regret under a self-exploration identifiability condition (Assumption 1) ensuring the state-action covariance is positive definite. Algorithm 2 updates the estimate after every episode and injects Gaussian exploration noise with variance 1/√k, achieving Õ(√N) regret without the identifiability assumption. The key technical contributions are (a) a recursive perturbation analysis of the LEQR Riccati equation, which involves extra P̃_t = (I − γP_t)^{-1}P_t terms absent in the risk-neutral case, and (b) a decomposition of the risk-sensitive performance gap into log-determinant terms of controller mismatch matrices D_t, leveraging Jacobson's (1973) identity for conditional expectations of exponentials of quadratic forms.

## Results

The paper establishes the first regret bounds for episodic risk-sensitive LEQR. Under an identifiability condition (Assumption 1), the least-squares greedy algorithm (Algorithm 1) achieves Õ(log N) regret (Theorem 1), where N is the total number of episodes. Without this assumption, a least-squares algorithm with injected Gaussian exploration noise (Algorithm 2) achieves Õ(√N) regret (Theorem 2). In simulations across three LEQR systems, Algorithm 1 incurs large initial regret due to infrequent parameter updates, and using a misspecified risk parameter γ (particularly γ=0, corresponding to the risk-neutral case) leads to substantially worse regret than the correctly specified γ=0.1. Regret increases monotonically with the time horizon T, consistent with the theoretical dependence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is the first to establish regret bounds for episodic risk-sensitive LEQR, and the authors explicitly note that 'there are currently no other existing algorithms with sublinear regret for this problem.' Consequently, no direct algorithmic baselines are compared in the experiments. The closest related work is Basei et al. (2022), which provides logarithmic regret for episodic continuous-time risk-neutral LQR under a similar identifiability assumption; however, that result does not apply to the risk-sensitive (LEQR) setting because the modified Riccati equation introduces additional complexity (the extra P̃_t matrices and the risk parameter γ). The simulation in Figure 1b indirectly illustrates the cost of ignoring risk sensitivity: running Algorithm 1 with γ=0 (the risk-neutral controller from Basei et al.) yields significantly higher regret than using the correct γ=0.1, confirming that risk-neutral methods are suboptimal for risk-sensitive LEQR. The two proposed algorithms are compared against each other in simulation, with Algorithm 2 (per-episode updates with exploration noise) outperforming Algorithm 1 (epoch-based updates) in the initial learning phase due to more frequent parameter estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the episodic risk-neutral LQR regret analysis of Basei et al. (2022) to the risk-sensitive LEQR setting, where the modified Riccati equation involves additional matrices (P̃_t) and the risk parameter γ explicitly affects the solution structure. The estimation framework adapts the epoch-based least-squares greedy approach from Basei et al. (2022) and the self-normalized martingale analysis from Mania et al. (2019) and Simchowitz & Foster (2020), while the performance-loss analysis relies on a key identity from Jacobson (1973) that expresses the entropic risk gap in terms of controller mismatch. The paper also connects to the rapidly growing risk-sensitive RL literature (Fei et al. 2020, 2021; Bastani et al. 2022) but addresses the continuous state/action space setting rather than tabular MDPs, where the nonlinearity of the log-exp objective creates fundamentally different analytical challenges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first set of regret bounds for finite-horizon episodic LEQR, filling a notable gap between the well-studied risk-neutral episodic LQR and the tabular risk-sensitive MDP literature. The two complementary results (logarithmic regret under a self-exploration/identifiability condition, and √N regret without it) provide a complete picture analogous to the risk-neutral case. Key open questions include extending to the infinite-horizon average-reward setting (where the generalized algebraic Riccati equation is harder to perturb), studying other risk measures such as coherent risk measures, establishing lower bounds, and handling partially observable states. The practical motivation—e.g., risk-sensitive asset liquidation in finance—suggests these bounds could inform real-world adaptive control systems where risk aversion is essential.
