## Problem

Existing multimodal retrieval methods for embodied agents select reference trajectories based on surface-level textual or visual similarity, which does not correlate with actual task effectiveness. A trajectory with a similar instruction but a different scene layout, or one in the same scene with a different object arrangement, can mislead the agent despite high embedding similarity. The paper asks whether a retriever can be trained to directly assess trajectory effectiveness for a given task rather than relying on similarity heuristics.

## Input / Output

The retriever takes a current task instruction, the agent's current egocentric observation image, and a trajectory abstraction (a GPT-4o-compressed subset of milestone images, feedback strings, and action sequences from an expert trajectory) as input, and outputs a single scalar effectiveness score via a Bradley-Terry score head (one-layer MLP on the last hidden state). The downstream MLLM agent then consumes the highest-scored trajectory abstraction alongside its own observation to produce fine-grained atomic actions (e.g., RotateLeft_90, Take mug 1, Put cup 1 on table 2). Trajectory Abstraction reduces an average of 11.51 trajectory steps to 3.13 milestones, keeping only observations where key decisions are made, goals are achieved, or the current task's target object appears in feedback.

## Method

MART fine-tunes LLaVA-7B as a trajectory retriever through interactive preference learning: for each training task, K candidate trajectories are sampled from memory and fed individually to the MLLM agent, which executes the task in the simulator; the resulting success rates form preference pairs (higher-success trajectory = positive, lower = negative) used to train a Bradley-Terry reward-modeling loss. Architecturally, the standard language-model head is replaced with a single-layer MLP score head that maps the last hidden state to a scalar, and training uses LoRA (r=16, α=32) for efficiency. A separate Trajectory Abstraction module, powered by GPT-4o in zero-shot, compresses full multimodal trajectories into a small set of task-relevant milestones (description, image, feedback, inter-milestone actions), reducing token count while preserving grounding information. At inference, the retriever scores all candidate trajectories, the top-scoring one is abstracted, and the MLLM agent executes the task using the abstracted trajectory as a reference prompt, with a self-reflection step after each action to detect failures and adjust.

## Results

On AI2-THOR, MART achieves a 40% full-task Success Rate (SR) and 75% sub-task SR, surpassing all baselines by at least 10 percentage points (vs. 18% for Plain-Agent, 26% for LLaVA-Plain, 24% for Similarity+LLaVA, and 22% for RAP). On LEGENT, MART reaches 87% SR with an average of 13.81 steps, compared to 70–75% SR and 20.6–25.0 steps for baselines. On ReALFRED, MART achieves 37% SR and 58% sub-task SR, outperforming the best baseline (RAP at 27% SR) by 10 points. MART also consistently reduces average steps across all environments, e.g., 123.19 vs. 144–160 steps on AI2-THOR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MART outperforms LLaVA-Plain (LP) [Liu et al., 2023] across all three environments: on AI2-THOR, MART achieves 40% SR vs. LP's 26% (a 14-point gap), and on LEGENT, 87% vs. 69% (18 points). The key difference is that LP uses raw token-generation probability as a score without any fine-tuning, while MART's Bradley-Terry fine-tuned score head learns to assess trajectory effectiveness from interactive feedback. Against RAP [Kagaya et al., 2024], which retrieves based on per-modality similarity, MART wins by 18 points on AI2-THOR (40% vs. 22%) and 12 points on LEGENT (87% vs. 75%), demonstrating that surface-level similarity is a poor proxy for trajectory usefulness. The Similarity+LLaVA (SL) baseline, which combines similarity-based retrieval with LLaVA ranking, also underperforms MART (24% vs. 40% on AI2-THOR), confirming that the unified interactive-learning retriever is superior to a decoupled retrieve-then-rank pipeline. MART additionally reduces average steps by 15–25% over all baselines, indicating not just higher success but more efficient task completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

MART builds on LLaVA as its base MLLM and repurposes the Bradley-Terry paired-comparison framework (originally from psychometrics) as a training objective for a multimodal trajectory scorer. It departs from the dominant similarity-based retrieval paradigm used in embodied agent memory systems (e.g., RAP, JARVIS-1, Groot), which compute weighted sums of image and text embedding similarities, by instead training the retriever to assess trajectory *effectiveness* through interactive feedback. The Trajectory Abstraction mechanism draws on MLLM summarization capabilities (using GPT-4o in zero-shot) to compress long trajectories, extending the line of work on memory compression in LLM agents (e.g., Voyager, Synapse) to the multimodal setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that similarity-based retrieval is fundamentally misaligned with task effectiveness in embodied settings, and demonstrates that a preference-learning fine-tuned MLLM can close this gap with a >10% success rate improvement across environments. However, the approach is currently limited to the household domain, single-trajectory retrieval (one-shot), and small memory pools (40–170 trajectories), leaving open whether the paradigm scales to larger, more diverse memory banks or transfers across domains (web, sandbox games). The reliance on GPT-4o for Trajectory Abstraction and the need for planner-collected expert trajectories also raise practical deployment questions about cost and data availability.
