## Problem

Existing ASR robustness benchmarks are fragmented, each measuring only one or a few corruption types, and collectively overlook important scenarios such as special effects, computer-generated speech, and adversarial attacks. This forces model developers to evaluate on disparate benchmarks, making cross-model comparisons unreliable and preventing identification of specific model weaknesses under controlled conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251385/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes clean speech recordings (LibriSpeech test-clean, TEDLIUM 3 test, Multi-Lingual LibriSpeech test in English and Spanish) and produces per-scenario WER, WERD (WER degradation relative to clean baseline), and NWERD (difficulty-normalized WERD weighted by DNSMOS/PESQ speech quality scores). For adversarial scenarios, raw WERD is used instead of NWERD because perturbation quality is model-specific. The benchmark also accepts inherently noisy recordings (CHiME-6, AMI, Common Voice accented speech) and synthetic speech (YourTTS, Bark) as additional evaluation inputs, and is extensible to other languages via provided scripts.

## Method

SRB evaluates ASR robustness through a three-step pipeline: (1) simulating 114 challenging scenarios across six categories—clean speech, social gatherings, speech variations, environmental effects, digital augmentations, and adversarial attacks—by applying controlled perturbations (e.g., RIR convolution, SNR-controlled noise mixing, SoX special effects, resampling) to clean recordings or using inherently noisy datasets with known, limited noise sources; (2) transcribing all recordings with the target ASR model; and (3) computing WER, WERD, and difficulty-normalized NWERD using DNSMOS and PESQ quality scores to weight scenarios by estimated difficulty, following the Hendrycks & Dietterich (2019) normalization approach. The adversarial component includes both utterance-specific PGD attacks (Madry et al., 2018) and utterance-agnostic universal perturbations (Neekhara et al., 2019) at four SNR levels (10–40 dB). The benchmark is publicly released with code and pre-computed perturbed data on HuggingFace, enabling reproducible, fine-grained, and extensible robustness evaluations across model families and demographic subgroups.

## Results

On SRB's non-adversarial scenarios, Whisper large-v2 (wsp-lg) achieves the lowest average NWERD (13.0) among English models, followed by Parakeet RNN-T 1.1B (10.9) and Whisper medium (3.7), while Canary-1B—which tops the Open ASR Leaderboard—ranks only 5th (NWERD 14.8). For adversarial robustness, Wav2Vec-2.0 large achieves the lowest utterance-specific WERD (23.8), substantially outperforming Whisper large (53.6) and Canary-1B (38.3). On Spanish, wsp-lg remains the most robust non-adversarial model (AVG NWERD 11.9), while Canary-1B suffers a catastrophic NWERD of 699.9 on accented speech. The benchmark also reveals gender disparities: adversarial perturbations increase Whisper's WER significantly more for female than male speakers on LibriSpeech.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRB evaluates 12+ ASR model families, revealing that robustness rankings differ substantially from standard accuracy leaderboards. Canary-1B (cnry-1b) tops the Open ASR Leaderboard but ranks only 5th on SRB's non-adversarial robustness (AVG NWERD 14.8 vs. wsp-lg's 13.0), with particular vulnerabilities to echo, real RIRs, and speed/pitch modifications. Whisper large-v2 (wsp-lg) is the most robust non-adversarial model for both English and Spanish, despite using only SpecAugment during training, suggesting its 680K hours of weakly supervised data exposed it to diverse real-world conditions. Conversely, Wav2Vec-2.0 large (w2v2-lg) is the most robust against utterance-specific adversarial attacks (WERD 23.8 vs. wsp-lg's 53.6 and cnry-1b's 38.3), indicating that CTC-based encoder-only architectures resist adversarial perturbations better than seq2seq models. Parakeet RNN-T 1.1B (prkt-rnnt-1.1b) achieves the best clean WER (5.9) and second-best non-adversarial robustness (10.9), confirming RNN-T's advantage for streaming scenarios. For Spanish, wsp-lg dominates non-adversarial robustness (AVG NWERD 11.9) while w2v2-bs-es leads adversarial robustness (WERD 21.9); Canary-1B's NWERD of 699.9 on accented Spanish speech exposes a critical fairness gap. The paper further shows that model size correlates positively with robustness (larger Whisper/Parakeet/Wav2Vec variants outperform smaller ones), while training data size has only a minor effect, and that multilingual models are consistently less robust on Spanish than English.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the fragmented landscape of ASR robustness benchmarks (CHiME, REVERB, MUSAN, WHAM!, Aurora) into a single comprehensive benchmark with 114 scenarios across six categories. It borrows the difficulty-normalized metric design from ImageNet-C (Hendrycks & Dietterich, 2019) and adapts PGD-based adversarial attack formulations from Madry et al. (2018) and Neekhara et al. (2019) to the speech domain. The key departure from prior work is the inclusion of digital augmentations (special effects, synthetic speech) and adversarial attacks, which existing ASR benchmarks largely overlooked, alongside a fairness evaluation dimension (gender, language) that prior robustness benchmarks did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SRB provides the first unified, reproducible benchmark for comprehensive ASR robustness evaluation, enabling direct cross-model comparisons that were previously impossible due to fragmented evaluation protocols. The finding that Canary-1b (top on Open ASR Leaderboard) is substantially less robust than Whisper, and that Wav2Vec models are paradoxically more robust to adversarial attacks despite weaker non-adversarial robustness, challenges prevailing assumptions about what drives robustness. Open questions include whether the English/Spanish-only scope generalizes to low-resource languages, whether the observed gender disparities in adversarial robustness are artifacts of the attack methodology or reflect genuine model vulnerabilities, and whether the 114 scenarios adequately capture the full diversity of real-world deployment conditions.
