## Problem

Prior work has developed separate machine learning models for image-based and DNA-based taxonomic classification, but no method had fused barcode DNA with images in a shared embedding space. BioCLIP aligned images to text-encoded taxonomic labels, but species-level labels are scarce (only ~9% in BOLD-derived datasets), limiting scalability. The paper asks whether DNA barcodes—available at scale without expert annotation—can serve as a superior alignment target for learning image representations suited to fine-grained taxonomic classification.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a biological image (resized to 256×256 with a 224×224 center crop) as a query and computes cosine similarity against a reference database of labelled image and DNA embeddings (keys). DNA inputs are capped at 660 base pairs and tokenized into non-overlapping 5-mers; text inputs concatenate the available Linnean hierarchy (order, family, genus, species). The output is a taxonomic label at any rank (order through species) taken from the nearest key, enabling zero-shot classification of species never seen during training, as well as cross-modal image-to-DNA retrieval.

## Method

CLIBD initializes three pretrained encoders—ViT-B for images, BarcodeBERT (5-mer tokenization) for DNA barcodes, and BERT-Small for taxonomic text—and fine-tunes them jointly with a symmetric NT-Xent contrastive loss applied to each modality pair (image↔DNA, DNA↔text, image↔text), using a learnable temperature initialized to 0.07. The key design choice is using DNA barcodes as the primary alignment signal rather than text labels, since barcodes are abundant and precise while species-level annotations are rare (only 3.36% of pretraining data). At inference, a query image embedding is matched via cosine similarity to a reference database of labelled image and DNA keys, and the taxonomic label of the nearest key is returned. The trimodal (I+D+T) model yields the most visually interpretable attention patterns, while the I+D model can match or exceed it at the species level because text labels are too sparse to provide a strong supervisory signal at fine-grained ranks.

## Results

On BIOSCAN-1M, the full CLIBD model (I+D+T) achieves 51.2% top-1 macro-accuracy (harmonic mean of seen/unseen) for species-level image-to-image retrieval, compared to 5.9% for unaligned encoders and 10.9% for SimCLR unimodal contrastive training. For the cross-modal image-to-DNA retrieval task, CLIBD achieves 14.7% H.M. at the species level versus 0.0% without alignment. On the INSECT dataset for Bayesian zero-shot learning, combining CLIBD-D (DNA encoder) with CLIBD-I (image encoder) and fine-tuning yields 35.0% species-level H.M. accuracy, surpassing the original BZSL baseline of 27.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIBD substantially outperforms BioCLIP [61] on species-level image-to-image retrieval: 51.2% H.M. (I+D+T) vs. 17.1% for BioCLIP, even when CLIBD is trained only on image+text (41.5% vs. 17.1%). Against the SimCLR unimodal contrastive baseline [11], CLIBD's cross-modal alignment yields a 4.7× improvement in species-level image-to-image H.M. (51.2% vs. 10.9%), confirming that the gain comes from cross-modal information flow rather than domain-specific data alone. For the BZSL task on INSECT [4], CLIBD-D (DNA encoder pretrained via CLIBD contrastive alignment) outperforms both BarcodeBERT [2] (26.4% vs. 23.1% H.M.) and DNABERT-2 [79] (26.4% vs. 16.2% H.M.) without any task-specific fine-tuning, and the full CLIBD-D + CLIBD-I pipeline with fine-tuning reaches 35.0% H.M. versus the original BZSL baseline of 27.0%. On BIOSCAN-1M, the simpler IS+DU strategy (image keys for seen species, DNA keys for unseen) with a linear classifier achieves 15.3% macro H.M. on unseen species, outperforming BZSL [4] at 12.9%, suggesting the Bayesian complexity is not necessary when good aligned embeddings are available.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLIBD extends the CLIP-style contrastive learning framework to three modalities (image, DNA, text) following the trimodal loss formulation of TriCoLo, but departs from BioCLIP's image-text alignment by substituting DNA barcodes as the primary alignment target. This is motivated by the practical observation that DNA barcodes are far more abundant (~19M in BOLD) and require no expert annotation, whereas taxonomic labels are scarce (only 9% species-level in BIOSCAN-1M). The method also supersedes the Bayesian zero-shot learning (BZSL) approach of Badirli et al., which used DNA embeddings as surrogate priors in a hierarchical Bayesian model, by learning the image-DNA alignment end-to-end via contrastive loss. For DNA encoding, it builds on BarcodeBERT's 5-mer tokenization and masked language model pretraining, and for images on ViT-B pretrained on ImageNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLIBD addresses a critical bottleneck in biodiversity monitoring: the scarcity of expert taxonomic annotations relative to the abundance of DNA barcodes and images. By demonstrating that DNA barcodes serve as a superior alignment target to text labels (I+D outperforms I+T at species level), the paper provides a scalable pathway to improve image-based species identification without requiring expensive expert annotation. Key limitations include the current restriction to insects, the still-modest cross-modal (image-to-DNA) retrieval accuracy (~15% H.M. at species level), and the dependence on DNA barcode availability which lags behind image-only datasets. Open questions include extending the approach beyond insects, integrating with larger natural-image datasets like BioTrove, and exploring whether other multimodal learning schemes beyond CLIP-style contrastive loss could further improve alignment.
