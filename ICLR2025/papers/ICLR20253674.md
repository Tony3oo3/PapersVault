## Problem

Offline RLHF methods are constrained by the fixed coverage of a static preference dataset, causing the learned reward model to fail on out-of-distribution responses. While iterative online RLHF can collect new prompt-response pairs, the central open question is how to explore the vast token-sequence space systematically in each iteration. Existing online exploration approaches (SELM, VPO, XPO) rely on the LLM's own likelihood to encourage novelty but lack theoretical guarantees tied to the confidence of the learned reward model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference LLM (Zephyr-7B-SFT or Llama-3-8B-Instruct), a preference dataset of prompt-response pairs (UltraFeedback 60K), and a small 0.4B PairRM reward model for ranking. In each of 3 online iterations, the current policy generates one response per prompt, PairRM ranks the generated response against the dataset's chosen/rejected responses, and the best/worst pair forms the training set. The output is a LoRA-adapted LLM policy (rank 128, targeting q/k/v/o/gate/up/down projections) that balances preference alignment with exploration, plus a lightweight CFN head (4096→32→20 FC layers) that estimates pseudo-counts from the LLM's last hidden state.

## Method

COPO derives a provably efficient online RLHF objective by showing that, under a linear reward assumption, maximizing an optimistic value function with a UCB bonus yields an Õ(√T) regret bound. The UCB term is shown to reduce to a count-based exploration bonus in the tabular case, motivating the practical objective: standard DPO loss plus α·E[1/√(N(x,y)+λ)], where N is a pseudo-count of how often a prompt-response pair has been visited. To estimate pseudo-counts in the enormous discrete token space, COPO trains a Coin Flipping Network (CFN) via simple MSE regression on random ±1 vectors, so that ‖f_θ(s)‖²/d approximates 1/N(s) without requiring a density model. The iterative loop is: (1) sample responses from the current policy, (2) rank with PairRM to form preference pairs, (3) train the CFN on the accumulated data, (4) update the LLM with the combined DPO + count-based exploration objective, then reset the reference model. The gradient of the exploration term explicitly increases log-likelihood for responses in under-visited regions, implementing optimism in the face of uncertainty.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2.0, COPO achieves an LC Win Rate of 26.91 (Zephyr-7B) and 35.54 (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) after 3 online iterations, outperforming online DPO (22.19 / 33.17) and SELM (24.25 / 34.67). On MT-Bench, COPO reaches 7.79 (Zephyr) and 8.32 (Llama-3-8B), surpassing both baselines. Notably, COPO with Llama-3-8B (35.54 LC Win Rate) exceeds the much larger Llama-3-70B-Instruct (33.17) and Yi-34B-Chat (27.19) on AlpacaEval 2.0. On academic benchmarks (GSM8K, HellaSwag, ARC, TruthfulQA, EQ-Bench, OBQA), COPO achieves the best average score of 65.2 (Llama-3-8B) vs. 65.0 (SELM) and 64.8 (DPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COPO consistently outperforms online DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) on both AlpacaEval 2.0 LC Win Rate (35.54 vs. 33.17 for Llama-3-8B; 26.91 vs. 22.19 for Zephyr-7B) and MT-Bench (8.32 vs. 8.18; 7.79 vs. 7.46), with the gap widening over iterations. Against SELM (Zhang et al., 2024), COPO wins on LC Win Rate for both models (35.54 vs. 34.67; 26.91 vs. 24.25) and on MT-Bench for Zephyr (7.79 vs. 7.61), while SELM slightly edges COPO on MT-Bench 2nd Turn for Llama-3 (7.98 vs. 8.11 is actually COPO winning). In the limited-data adversarial setting (20% UltraFeedback), COPO's advantage over SELM grows to 2.55 points (31.80 vs. 29.25 LC Win Rate). COPO with Llama-3-8B surpasses the 4× larger Llama-3-70B-Instruct on AlpacaEval LC Win Rate (35.54 vs. 33.17) but remains below it on MT-Bench (8.32 vs. 9.01), and clearly beats Yi-34B-Chat (35.54 vs. 27.19). Against D2PO (Singhal et al., 2024), COPO achieves 35.54 vs. 22.03 LC Win Rate, though D2PO uses a self-trained discriminator rather than an off-the-shelf reward model, making the comparison partially confounded.

## Relation to Prior Work

COPO sits at the intersection of two well-developed threads: (1) online RLHF methods that iteratively collect preference data to overcome the coverage limitations of offline datasets (SELM, XPO, VPO), and (2) count-based exploration in RL, which is provably efficient in tabular and linear MDPs (Strehl & Littman, 2008; Bellemare et al., 2016). The key departure from prior online RLHF work is the source of optimism: rather than relying on the LLM's own likelihood or a KL-regularized value function (as in SELM/VPO), COPO constructs optimism from a confidence set of the reward MLE, yielding a UCB bonus that provably reduces to a count-based exploration term in the tabular case. For practical pseudo-count estimation, the paper repurposes the Coin Flipping Network (Lobel et al., 2023) from deep RL, replacing the heavier density-model-based pseudo-counts with a lightweight two-layer FC regressor trained via simple MSE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COPO provides the first provably efficient (Ō(√T) regret) exploration strategy specifically tailored to online RLHF, giving practitioners a theoretically grounded alternative to heuristic exploration in iterative preference alignment. The practical recipe—DPO loss plus a small CFN-based count bonus—is lightweight enough to run with LoRA on 4×A100 GPUs, making it accessible. Open questions include the length-bias side effect of the exploration term (which inflates response length in later iterations), the need for a changing prompt set to avoid being bounded by the initial dataset's coverage, and automatic scheduling of the exploration coefficient α. The method's model-agnosticism (demonstrated with both DPO and KTO) suggests it could serve as a general exploration plug-in for future online alignment algorithms.
