## Problem

Existing game-theory-based evaluations of LLMs are largely confined to two-player, two-action settings (e.g., Prisoner's Dilemma, Ultimatum Game) and rely on fixed, static game parameters, creating a risk of test-set leakage from training data. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a multi-agent, multi-round, multi-action benchmark with dynamically adjustable game parameters to assess LLMs' strategic reasoning, robustness, and generalizability in more realistic decision-making environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252360/cropped_page15_idx10.webp)

## Input / Output

Each game instantiates 10 LLM-based agents that interact over 20 rounds (simultaneous games) or until a terminal condition (sequential games), receiving prompt templates that encode game rules, historical round outcomes, and a JSON response format. Game parameters (number of players N, rounds K, multiplier R, total golds G, hit rates, etc.) are configurable, and the scoring scheme dynamically rescales raw per-game metrics to a 0–100 scale where higher always indicates better performance, adapting to whatever parameter values are chosen. The final output is a per-game score, an overall aggregate score, and a leaderboard ranking across 13 LLMs from 6 model families.

## Method

γ-Bench organizes eight classical game-theory scenarios into three categories—Cooperative (Guess 2/3 of the Average, El Farol Bar, Divide the Dollar), Betraying (Public Goods Game, Diner's Dilemma, Sealed-Bid Auction), and Sequential (Battle Royale, Pirate Game)—each targeting distinct cognitive abilities such as K-level reasoning, mixed-strategy adoption, Theory of Mind, and sequential planning. The key technical contribution is a dynamic scoring scheme (Eq. 1) that rescales raw game-specific metrics (e.g., L1 distance from Nash equilibrium, deviation from optimal probability, bid-valuation gaps) into a unified 0–100 scale that automatically adapts to arbitrary game parameters, enabling fair comparison across settings and minimizing memorization risk. Beyond the core benchmark, the paper conducts a systematic ablation across four research questions: robustness (five repeated runs, six temperature levels, five GPT-4-rephrased prompt templates), reasoning strategies (Chain-of-Thought prompting and persona assignment), generalizability (varying game parameters like R, G, and hit rates to probe data contamination), and a cross-model leaderboard spanning GPT-3.5/4, Gemini, LLaMA-3.1, Mixtral, and Qwen-2.

## Results

On γ-Bench (8 classical game theory scenarios, 10 agents per game, 20 rounds, 5 runs), Gemini-1.5-Pro achieves the highest overall score of 69.8/100, followed by GPT-4o (66.7), LLaMA-3.1-70B (65.9), and Mixtral-8x22B (62.4). GPT-3.5 (0125) scores 44.4±2.1 overall, with Chain-of-Thought prompting improving it to 57.9. Performance varies dramatically across games: GPT-4 Turbo scores 91.6 on 'Guess 2/3 of the Average' but only 0.9 on 'Diner's Dilemma', while Gemini-1.5-Pro achieves 100.0 on 'Public Goods Game' and 95.4 on 'Guess 2/3 of the Average'.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-1.5-Pro (69.8) outperforms all 12 other models, with particularly strong margins on 'Public Goods Game' (100.0 vs. next-best 97.0 for LLaMA-3.1-405B) and 'Diner's Dilemma' (35.9 vs. 28.6 for GPT-3.5-0613). Among closed-source models, GPT-4o (66.7) edges out GPT-4 Turbo (62.4) by 4.3 points, largely due to better performance on 'El Farol Bar' (70.0 vs. 23.0) and 'Diner's Dilemma' (10.7 vs. 0.9). Among open-source models, LLaMA-3.1-70B (65.9) surpasses GPT-4 Turbo (62.4) by 3.5 points, while Mixtral-8x22B (62.4) ties GPT-4 Turbo exactly. Notably, LLaMA-3.1-405B (61.8) underperforms its 70B counterpart (65.9), attributed to an overly conservative strategy in 'El Farol Bar' (20.5 vs. 59.7). All GPT-3.5 variants cluster tightly (42.7–45.1), and Gemini-1.0-Pro (45.7) performs comparably to GPT-3.5, suggesting a significant generational gap to Gemini-1.5-Pro. Mixtral-8x7B (43.4) is the weakest model overall, with near-zero scores on 'Divide the Dollar' (1.2) and 'Sealed-Bid Auction' (3.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work evaluating LLMs through game-theoretic scenarios, which has been dominated by two-player, two-action settings (Prisoner's Dilemma, Ultimatum Game) with static parameters. It departs from prior benchmarks by introducing multi-player, multi-round, multi-action games with dynamically adjustable parameters, explicitly addressing the test-set-leakage problem that afflicts fixed-setting evaluations. The framework also integrates robustness analyses (temperature, prompt templates, multiple runs) and reasoning-strategy ablations (CoT, persona prompting) that prior game-theory evaluations of LLMs largely omitted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

γ-Bench provides a practical, extensible testbed for assessing LLMs' strategic reasoning in multi-agent contexts, with the dynamic parameterization enabling ongoing evaluation as models improve. The finding that even the best model (Gemini-1.5-Pro) scores only 69.8/100, and that the 405B LLaMA underperforms its 70B counterpart, suggests that scale alone does not resolve strategic reasoning deficits. Open questions include whether the observed cooperative biases (e.g., GPT-3.5 choosing cheap dishes in Diner's Dilemma) reflect genuine understanding or surface-level pattern matching, and how performance changes when LLMs play against humans or fixed strategies rather than homogeneous agent populations.
