## Problem

The state-of-the-art OSG algorithm for multi-agent online submodular maximization suffers from two coupled limitations: it only guarantees a sub-optimal (1/(1+c))-approximation (where c is the joint curvature) rather than the tight (1-e^{-c})/c ratio, and it requires a fully connected directed communication graph whose overhead scales prohibitively with the number of agents. This paper asks whether an online algorithm can simultaneously achieve the tight approximation guarantee and operate over an arbitrary connected (possibly sparse) undirected graph.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the environment reveals a monotone submodular function f_t over the union of all agents' discrete action sets, but each agent i is restricted to a local marginal gain oracle that only evaluates f_t(A∪{a})−f_t(A) for a in its own action set V_i. The algorithm outputs, per agent, a stochastically rounded action a_{t,i} drawn from its local probability vector, and updates a full n-dimensional probability vector over all actions in V (not just its own) via consensus with neighbors. The curvature parameter c∈[0,1] and the spectral gap β of the weight matrix W are required as inputs to set the step size.

## Method

The core mechanism converts the discrete submodular maximization into a continuous problem via the multilinear extension F(x), then constructs a surrogate function F^s whose gradient is the weighted integral ∇F^s(x)=∫₀¹ e^{c(z−1)}∇F(z·x)dz. The key theoretical insight (Theorem 2) is that stationary points of F^s achieve the tight (1-e^{-c})/c approximation, closing the gap left by stationary points of F itself which only yield 1/(1+c). MA-OSMA integrates this surrogate gradient with consensus-based information aggregation (weighted averaging over neighbors) and a Bregman-divergence mirror ascent step, using a divide-and-conquer update where each agent only computes local gradient estimates for its own actions while copying consensus values for others. MA-OSEA replaces the constrained projection with a closed-form exponential update derived from KL divergence, mixing in a uniform distribution (parameter γ) to avoid the infinite-gradient boundary issue, yielding a fully projection-free algorithm. Both achieve a dynamic regret bound of Õ(√(C_T·T/(1−β))) against the (1-e^{-c})/c-approximation to the best hindsight comparator.

## Results

Both MA-OSMA and MA-OSEA achieve a dynamic regret bound of $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{C_T T/(1-\beta)})$ against a tight $(\frac{1-e^{-c}}{c})$-approximation to the best comparator in hindsight, where $C_T$ is the deviation of the maximizer sequence, $\beta$ is the spectral gap of the communication network, and $c \in [0,1]$ is the joint curvature. This improves upon the state-of-the-art OSG algorithm's $(\frac{1}{1+c})$-approximation (e.g., at $c=1$, the ratio improves from $1/2$ to $1-e^{-1} \approx 0.632$). In simulation-based multi-target tracking (20 agents, 30 targets, $T=2500$), MA-OSMA and MA-OSEA significantly outperform OSG in average cumulative utility, average number of targets within 5 units, and average distance to top-5 nearest targets across all tested target-mix scenarios. Notably, algorithms over a sparse Erdős–Rényi random graph (average degree 4) perform comparably to those over a complete graph, confirming communication efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is OSG (Xu et al., 2023), the online sequential greedy algorithm for multi-agent submodular coordination. MA-OSMA and MA-OSEA strictly dominate OSG on two axes: (1) approximation quality — they achieve the tight $(\frac{1-e^{-c}}{c})$-approximation versus OSG's sub-optimal $(\frac{1}{1+c})$, a gap that is non-negligible for moderate-to-high curvature (e.g., 0.632 vs 0.5 at $c=1$); and (2) communication topology — they operate over any connected graph (including sparse ones) while OSG requires a complete directed acyclic graph. In the multi-target tracking simulations (Figure 3), MA-OSMA and MA-OSEA consistently outperform OSG in average cumulative utility, average number of targets within 5 units, and average distance to the top-5 nearest targets across three target-mix ratios (R:A:P = 8:1:1, 6:3:1, 4:5:1). Additionally, the sparse-graph variants (MA-OSMA-r, MA-OSEA-r on an Erdős–Rényi graph with average degree 4) perform comparably to their complete-graph counterparts, whereas OSG's approximation degrades with graph sparsity (scaling with $\alpha(G)$). MA-OSEA further offers a projection-free update via KL divergence with uniform mixing, eliminating the constrained mirror projection step in MA-OSMA at the cost of an extra $\log(T)$ factor in the regret bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the online sequential greedy (OSG) algorithm of Xu et al. (2023) for multi-agent online submodular maximization, addressing its two key limitations: a sub-optimal (1/(1+c))-approximation and a rigid complete directed acyclic communication graph requirement. The methodological core combines three previously separate ideas: the multilinear extension (Calinescu et al., 2011) for continuous relaxation, a curvature-aware surrogate gradient (Zhang et al., 2022; Wan et al., 2023) that tightens the approximation to (1-e^{-c})/c, and consensus-based distributed optimization (Shahrampour & Jadbabaie, 2017) to replace the complete graph with any connected topology. The projection-free variant (MA-OSEA) further draws on KL-divergence-based mirror ascent to eliminate costly projection steps, building on the entropic ascent framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving the tight (1-e^{-c})/c approximation in an online multi-agent setting over sparse graphs closes a meaningful theoretical gap over OSG and makes the algorithm practical for large-scale deployments where complete communication is infeasible. The projection-free variant reduces per-agent computational cost, which is critical for resource-constrained platforms like UAVs. Open questions highlighted by the authors include extending the analysis to noisy marginal evaluations, time-varying and directed network topologies, and designing parameter-free algorithms that eliminate the need to know the curvature c a priori — all of which would broaden applicability to real-world sensing and tracking scenarios.
