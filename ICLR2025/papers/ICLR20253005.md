## Problem

Conventional early stopping halts training for the entire dataset simultaneously, ignoring that individual instances are learned at different rates. This causes redundant backpropagation on already-mastered instances, wasting computational resources. The paper asks whether early stopping can be refined to the instance level, adaptively excluding mastered samples from further training without degrading performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images with class labels (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k and PASCAL VOC) and produces trained CNN parameters. A key operational detail is that every epoch requires a forward pass over the full training set to compute per-instance second-order loss differences, but backpropagation is restricted to the shrinking subset of not-yet-mastered instances. The output model achieves comparable or slightly better test accuracy and transfer performance while saving 10–50% of backpropagation mini-batches.

## Method

IES defines an instance as 'mastered' when the absolute second-order difference of its loss across three consecutive epochs, |L_i(w^(t)) − 2L_i(w^(t−1)) + L_i(w^(t−2))|, falls below a unified threshold δ. The second-order difference is chosen over raw loss because it exhibits a lower coefficient of variation across instances in later training stages, enabling a single threshold to work uniformly regardless of each instance's optimal loss value. Each epoch, the algorithm performs a forward pass on all instances, identifies mastered ones, and backpropagates only on the remaining subset. The paper shows this exclusion increases the average gradient norm of the retained mini-batch, accelerating training loss reduction and more rapidly decreasing the maximum Hessian eigenvalue and SAM value, which together explain the maintained or improved generalization and transfer performance.

## Results

IES reduces backpropagation instances by 10%–50% while maintaining or slightly improving test accuracy across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-1k, achieving ~1.2×–1.4× wall-time speedup. At a target 2.0× computational speedup, IES achieves 93.7% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 74.9% on CIFAR-100, outperforming all compared data-efficiency baselines. For transfer learning, models pretrained with IES on ImageNet-1k surpass the no-removal baseline by 0.9%, 1.9%, and 2.5% on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Caltech-101 after 1-epoch fine-tuning. On high-level tasks, IES saves 20.0% mini-batches on Faster R-CNN (mAP 70.2%) and 14.0% on DeepLab v3 (mIoU 76.1%) with no accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 2.0× speedup, IES (93.7% / 74.9%) clearly outperforms Conventional Early Stopping (90.4% / 68.7%) by 3.3 and 6.2 points on CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100, and beats SB (Jiang et al., 2019) by 0.7 and 4.3 points. IES also surpasses DIHCL (Zhou et al., 2020) by 0.3 and 0.6 points, and EfficientTrain (Wang et al., 2024) by 2.2 points on CIFAR-10, while matching it within 0.1 point on CIFAR-100 (74.9% vs 75.0%). At the more aggressive 3.0× speedup, IES extends its lead: 93.2% vs 92.5% (EfficientTrain) on CIFAR-10 and 73.0% vs 70.6% on CIFAR-100. In the direct comparison with InfoBatch's Small Loss & Rescale (Qin et al., 2023) at matched exclusion rates (Figure 5), IES with second-order differences consistently achieves higher accuracy, while Random Remove degrades performance substantially. Additionally, IES pretrained models transfer better than no-removal pretrained models across all three downstream tasks, suggesting the method produces flatter, more transferable loss landscapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

IES extends the classical early stopping paradigm (Prechelt, 2002) from the dataset level to the instance level, asking when individual samples can be excluded from backpropagation. It is positioned within the broader sample-selection literature (data pruning, importance sampling, curriculum learning) but distinguishes itself by using the second-order difference of per-instance loss as a 'mastered' criterion rather than loss magnitude, gradient norm, or prediction uncertainty. Unlike dynamic pruning methods that sample a fixed proportion of 'important' instances each epoch, IES adaptively determines the removal set from the model's own learning progress, eliminating the need for pre-set schedules or removal rates. The authors frame IES as a curriculum-learning strategy for the end of training, where the curriculum is defined by mastery rather than difficulty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is clear: a 10–50% reduction in backpropagation with no accuracy loss (and a ~1.5% transfer-learning gain) is a meaningful efficiency win for large-scale vision training, and the method is trivially implementable since the criterion requires only forward passes. The observation that IES-trained models exhibit lower loss-landscape sharpness and better transferability suggests a mechanistic link between avoiding redundant training on mastered samples and finding flatter minima, though the authors acknowledge that a rigorous theoretical analysis of why the second-order difference is the right criterion remains open. Fairness implications are only preliminarily assessed, and the method's behavior under more extreme noise or distribution shift is not fully explored.
