## Problem

Dynamical systems reconstruction (DSR) methods have advanced significantly for single-domain data, but integrating observations from multiple dynamical regimes—systems with fundamentally different attractor topologies and bifurcation structures—into a unified generative framework remains largely unexplored. Existing hierarchical approaches for dynamical systems either consider only systems in similar regimes (e.g., all periodic) or focus on short-term forecasting rather than faithful long-term generative reconstruction. This gap is particularly acute when individual time series are short, making group-level information essential for accurate reconstruction of each subject's dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series from S subjects, each potentially from a different dynamical regime, with sequences as short as T_max=1000 (or even T_max=100 in few-shot mode). In few-shot settings, only a single short sequence from a new parameter regime is provided alongside a pre-trained hierarchical model, and only the low-dimensional subject feature vector (as few as 1 scalar) is fine-tuned. Outputs include subject-specific generative DSR models that produce freely generated trajectories matching ground-truth attractor geometry and power spectra indefinitely beyond training length, plus interpretable low-dimensional feature vectors that cluster subjects by dynamical regime and linearly encode the underlying control parameters.

## Method

The framework decomposes each subject's DSR model parameters into a low-dimensional subject-specific feature vector l^(j) ∈ R^{N_feat} and high-dimensional group-level parameters θ_group shared across all subjects, connected by a simple linear projection G that maps features to the weights of a shallow piecewise-linear RNN (shPLRNN). All parameters are trained end-to-end via Generalized Teacher Forcing (GTF), which linearly interpolates between model-generated and data-inferred latent states to stabilize training on chaotic dynamics, with a critical differential learning rate (10^{-3} for features vs. 10^{-4} for group matrices) that prioritizes subject-specific information incorporation. The observation model is the identity map, forcing the DSR model to capture the actual dynamics rather than offloading complexity to a decoder. Ablations show that the simple linear group-level mapping outperforms MLPs and outer-product parameterizations, and that the learned feature spaces spontaneously organize along the ground-truth bifurcation parameters of the underlying ODEs, yielding a dynamically interpretable embedding where different attractor topologies occupy distinct regions.

## Results

On the Lorenz-63 benchmark (64 subjects, T_max=1000), hier-shPLRNN achieves a state space divergence D_stsp of 0.394±0.014 and Hellinger distance D_H of 0.097±0.005, substantially outperforming the shPLRNN ensemble (D_stsp=1.82, D_H=0.118), CoDA (D_stsp=1.4, D_H=0.337), and LEADS (D_stsp=9.7, D_H=0.58). On the higher-dimensional Lorenz-96 benchmark (20 subjects, N=10), hier-shPLRNN achieves D_stsp=0.580±0.002 and D_H=0.0527±0.0010, again the best across all methods. For unsupervised EEG classification (Epilepsy2), GMM clustering in the learned DS feature space recovers ground-truth labels with 92.6±1.2% accuracy, outperforming all conventional time-series feature extractors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's hierarchical shPLRNN dominates all four DSR baselines on both Lorenz-63 and Lorenz-96. Against the shPLRNN ensemble (its own ablation without the hierarchical component), hier-shPLRNN reduces D_stsp by ~4.6× on Lorenz-63 (0.394 vs. 1.82) and ~2.2× on Lorenz-96 (0.580 vs. 1.291), while using roughly half the parameters. CoDA (Kirchmeyer et al., 2022) is outperformed by 3.5× on D_stsp for Lorenz-63 (0.394 vs. 1.4) and 3.5× for Lorenz-96 (0.580 vs. 2.05), and by 3.5× on D_H for Lorenz-63 (0.097 vs. 0.337); CoDA also uses ~14× more parameters. LEADS (Yin et al., 2021) is the weakest competitor, with D_stsp of 9.7 on Lorenz-63 (25× worse) and 3.2 on Lorenz-96 (5.5× worse), and D_H values roughly 6× higher. For the EEG classification task, the DS feature space from hier-shPLRNN (92.6% GMM accuracy) substantially outperforms tsfresh, Catch-22, ROCKET, MiniRocket, and the attention-based convolutional autoencoder of Wang et al. (2023), all of which produce much less distinct class separations in their respective embedding spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DSR literature (shPLRNN, GTF training from Hess et al. 2023) by wrapping individual DSR models in a hierarchical structure where low-dimensional subject-specific feature vectors are mapped to model parameters via shared group-level matrices. It departs from prior hierarchical DS work (LEADS, CoDA) which decomposed dynamics into shared and environment-specific components but only considered systems in similar dynamical regimes (e.g., all periodic orbits) and focused on forecasting rather than generative long-term performance. The hypernetwork-style parameterization (feature vector → model weights) is conceptually related to hypernetworks and multi-task learning, but is specifically designed to preserve the generative, attractor-preserving properties required for DSR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical path toward DSR foundation models that can generalize across fundamentally different dynamical regimes with minimal new data (few-shot: 100 time steps, ~6s convergence). The interpretability finding—that learned features linearly encode bifurcation control parameters—opens a new application axis for DSR in neuroscience and medicine, where identifying which dynamical parameters differentiate patient groups could be clinically relevant. Key open questions include the mathematical basis for the interpretability, how to handle systems on widely different time scales, and whether incorporating physics-informed priors (SINDy/PINN-style) can strengthen mechanistic links. The paper also notes that the approach currently requires systems to evolve on similar time scales, limiting its applicability to multi-scale biological processes.
