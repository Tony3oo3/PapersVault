## Problem

Existing safety interpretability research attributes LLM safety to representations, neurons, and layers, but largely overlooks the multi-head attention mechanism despite its confirmed critical role in other model capabilities. This paper asks: which specific attention heads are responsible for safety, and what is their functional role in enabling rejection of harmful queries?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253029/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes harmful query text (from AdvBench, JailbreakBench, or Malicious Instruct) and the full parameter set of an aligned LLM (Llama-2-7b-chat or Vicuna-7b-v1.5) as input, using either direct input or a minimal chat template without system prompts to isolate inherent safety. It produces per-head Ships importance scores (KL divergence at query level, or SVD principal angles at dataset level), a greedily selected group of safety-critical heads via Sahara, and the resulting ablated model evaluated by attack success rate over 128-token generations with greedy or top-k sampling.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level attribution framework. At the query level, Ships computes the KL divergence between the original and ablated model's output distribution for a specific harmful query, where ablation is achieved via two distinct mechanisms: Undifferentiated Attention (scaling W_q or W_k by ε≈1e-5, collapsing attention weights to a uniform lower-triangular matrix) and Scaling Contribution (scaling W_v by ε, linearly reducing the head's output magnitude). At the dataset level, generalized Ships applies SVD to stacked top-layer activations across all harmful queries and measures principal angles between the left singular matrices of the original and ablated models. The Sahara algorithm then greedily selects a small group (typically ≤5) of heads whose joint ablation maximizes the dataset-level representation shift. A key mechanistic finding is that Undifferentiated Attention dramatically outperforms Scaling Contribution, revealing that safety heads function primarily as feature extractors rather than output modulators, and that safety heads overlap substantially across models fine-tuned from the same base model.

## Results

Ablating a single safety attention head (constituting only 0.006% of all parameters) via Undifferentiated Attention increases the attack success rate (ASR) of Llama-2-7b-chat from 0.04 to 0.64 (16× improvement) and Vicuna-7b-v1.5 from 0.27 to 0.55. Using the Sahara algorithm to identify a group of 3 safety heads (~0.018% of parameters) achieves an ASR of 0.72±0.05 on Llama-2-7b-chat, matching the best prior method (ActSVD at 0.73±0.03) while modifying 278× fewer parameters. The method requires only 6 GPU hours versus ~850 GPU hours for prior full-generation-based approaches, a 142× efficiency gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ActSVD (Wei et al., 2024b), the proposed method achieves a comparable ASR (0.72 vs. 0.73) while modifying only 0.018% of parameters versus ~5%, representing a three-orders-of-magnitude improvement in attribution precision at the Head level rather than Rank level. Compared to GTAC & DAP (Chen et al., 2024), the method achieves a higher ASR (0.72 vs. 0.64) with far fewer modified parameters (0.018% vs. ~5%) and finer granularity (Head vs. Neuron). Against LSP (Zhao et al., 2024b), the method substantially outperforms on ASR (0.72 vs. 0.58) while modifying 167× fewer parameters (0.018% vs. ~3%). In terms of computational efficiency, the method requires only 6 GPU hours without full text generation, compared to ~850 GPU hours for both Masking Head (Michel et al., 2019) and ACDC (Conmy et al., 2023), a 142× speedup. For helpfulness preservation, safety head ablation via Undifferentiated Attention causes less zero-shot task degradation than SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023) and Wanda (Sun et al., 2024) pruning, confirming that safety heads can be targeted independently of general capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the safety interpretability line of work (ActSVD, GTAC&DAP, Layer-Specific Pruning) that has attributed safety to representations, neurons, and layers, by specifically targeting multi-head attention heads—a component confirmed crucial for other capabilities but largely overlooked in safety analysis. It builds on Michel et al.'s head ablation paradigm but introduces a finer-grained distinction between modifying attention weights (Wq/Wk, 'Undifferentiated Attention') and attention outputs (Wv, 'Scaling Contribution'), going beyond the standard zero-out approach. The dataset-level generalization via SVD principal angles draws on Wei et al.'s (2024b) use of left singular matrices for safety feature identification, but repurposes it to score individual attention heads rather than rank residual-stream directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single attention head (0.006% of parameters) can be ablated to increase ASR by 16×, compared to ~5% parameter modification in prior methods, offers a dramatically more precise localization of safety mechanisms. The conceptual insight that safety heads function primarily as feature extractors (modifying Wq/Wk is critical, modifying Wv is not) reframes how we think about the attention mechanism's role in alignment. Limitations include evaluation on only two 7B models, a heuristic (non-guaranteed) search algorithm, and rule-based ASR evaluation; open questions include whether the same head-level granularity holds at 70B+ scale, whether safety heads are architecture-specific, and whether identifying these heads enables targeted safety hardening without the helpfulness trade-off that broader pruning methods incur.
