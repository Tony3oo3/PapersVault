## Problem

Existing 3D human motion generation methods require extensive task-specific training, are limited to short atomic motions, and lack the ability to support multi-turn conversational interaction for iterative refinement and editing. No prior framework efficiently bridges the gap between natural language and 3D motion while supporting complex, long-sequence generation through dialogue without additional instruction-tuning data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251720/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts free-form text prompts (potentially describing multiple sequential actions, abstract scenarios, or editing requests) and motion sequences in HumanML3D joint-position format (R^{T×D}) as input. GPT-4 decomposes complex prompts into sub-prompts, each fed to MotionLLM, which produces variable-length motion token sequences that are concatenated and decoded into a single continuous 3D motion. For captioning, motion token sequences are input and natural language descriptions are output. The autoregressive generation means no target motion length needs to be specified, and the detokenizer smooths transitions between concatenated segments.

## Method

Motion-Agent is a compositional framework with three components: a VQ-VAE motion tokenizer/detokenizer (downsampling rate 4, codebook size 512, hidden dim 512, trained with reconstruction + commitment + joint-position regularization losses), a translation agent MotionLLM built on Gemma2-2b-it with 512 motion tokens appended to its vocabulary and fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 64 for generation, 32 for captioning, ~1-3% of parameters), and GPT-4 as an untrained conversational coordinator. GPT-4 parses user requests, decomposes them into structured JSON sub-prompts, invokes MotionLLM multiple times, and concatenates the resulting motion token sequences before universal decoding. The key insight is that the LLM's inherent multi-turn conversational ability and instruction-following capacity replace the need for task-specific instruction tuning, enabling long-sequence generation, multi-turn editing, and motion understanding through a decompose-and-integrate strategy that maps known short-motion distributions to arbitrarily long sequences.

## Results

On the HumanML3D test set, MotionLLM achieves R-Precision Top-1 of 0.515 and Top-3 of 0.801 for text-to-motion generation, with a multimodal distance of 2.967 and diversity of 9.908, using only 83.1M trainable parameters (LoRA rank 64 on Gemma2-2B). For motion captioning, MotionLLM achieves Bleu@1 of 54.53, Rouge of 48.7, and Bert Score of 42.63, substantially outperforming all prior bidirectional models. The FID of 0.230 is competitive with autoregressive baselines (MotionGPT: 0.232, MotionChain: 0.248) though higher than non-autoregressive MoMask (0.045), which requires ground-truth motion lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For motion generation, MotionLLM outperforms all autoregressive baselines: it beats MotionGPT (Jiang et al., 2024b) by +0.023 R-Precision Top-1 and −0.129 MM-Dist, and MotionChain (Jiang et al., 2024c) by +0.011 R-Precision Top-1 and −0.066 MM-Dist, while using roughly one-third of their trainable parameters. Against the non-autoregressive MoMask (Guo et al., 2024), MotionLLM trails slightly on R-Precision (0.515 vs. 0.521) and FID (0.230 vs. 0.045), but MoMask requires ground-truth motion lengths at inference, whereas MotionLLM generates variable-length motions autoregressively without length specification. T2M-GPT (Zhang et al., 2023b) achieves a lower FID (0.116) but is outperformed by MotionLLM on R-Precision (0.515 vs. 0.491) and MM-Dist (2.967 vs. 3.118). For motion captioning, MotionLLM achieves a decisive margin over all baselines, improving Bleu@1 by +5.6 over TM2T (54.53 vs. 48.90) and Bert Score by +5.7 over MotionChain (42.63 vs. 36.9), demonstrating the advantage of leveraging a pre-trained LLM's language generation capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on T2M-GPT's VQ-VAE motion tokenization (codebook-based discretization of motion sequences) but departs fundamentally in how those tokens are consumed: rather than training a dedicated autoregressive transformer from scratch, it fine-tunes a pre-trained LLM (Gemma2-2b) with LoRA adapters to serve as a text-motion translation agent. It further distinguishes itself from MotionChain, which achieves multi-turn conversational control through extensive instruction tuning on synthesized multi-turn data, by composing a lightweight MotionLLM with GPT-4 as an untrained coordinator that decomposes complex prompts into sub-calls and concatenates results. The overall design follows the compositional LLM paradigm (Chameleon, Chameleon-style plug-and-play) applied to the motion domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that 1–3% parameter fine-tuning of a pre-trained LLM can match or exceed from-scratch motion generation models (T2M-GPT, MotionGPT) on R-Precision and Diversity while achieving clear SOTA on captioning, suggesting that pre-trained linguistic priors transfer meaningfully to motion-language alignment. The compositional GPT-4 + MotionLLM architecture enables multi-turn editing, long-sequence generation, and motion reasoning without any task-specific training data, lowering the barrier to building interactive motion systems. Open questions remain around human-environment interaction, detailed hand/face articulation, and full multi-human generation (only preliminary results are shown), and the reliance on GPT-4 as a paid API coordinator limits full reproducibility and deployment flexibility.
