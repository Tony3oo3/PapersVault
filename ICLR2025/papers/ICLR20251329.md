## Problem

Standard alignment methods like PPO and DPO require either expensive reward model training or large paired preference datasets, and their effectiveness diminishes on already-well-aligned strong models. The paper asks whether the alignment behavior captured by a weaker model can be transferred to a stronger one, leveraging the weak-to-strong generalization phenomenon where strong models fine-tuned on weak supervision outperform their weak teachers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251329/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

WSPO consumes an offline dataset of single query-response pairs (no chosen/rejected pairs required), a weak model in its pre-alignment state (e.g., SFT or base checkpoint), the same weak model after alignment (e.g., DPO or PPO checkpoint), and a strong reference model. The output is a single aligned strong language model. Notably, the method works even with rejected or non-preferred data subsets, and the only tunable hyperparameter is γ, which controls the regularization ratio between strong and weak models.

## Method

WSPO exploits the DPO reparameterization (Theorem 1) that any reward function consistent with Bradley-Terry preferences can be written as β·log(π_r(y|x)/π_ref(y|x)), i.e., the log-ratio of post- and pre-alignment distributions. The weak model's alignment shift — log(π_r^weak(y|x)/π_ref^weak(y|x)) — is treated as an implicit reward signal, eliminating the need for a separate reward model or paired preference data. The strong model is then trained to minimize the per-token L2 distance between γ·log(π_θ^strong(y|x)/π_ref^strong(y|x)) and log(π_r^weak(y|x)/π_ref^weak(y|x)), where γ = λ/β balances reward maximization against KL-regularization. Because the gradient direction is governed by the distributional gap rather than the model's own likelihood on the data, WSPO avoids the overfitting and reward-hacking issues common to DPO, and the strong model can amplify the weak model's alignment beyond what the weak model achieves on its own.

## Results

On Qwen2-7B-Instruct, WSPO improves Arena-Hard win rate from 39.70% (SFT) to 49.60%, achieves a 47.04% length-controlled win rate on AlpacaEval 2, and scores 7.33 on MT-Bench. On Qwen2-7B-Base, WSPO lifts Arena-Hard from 5.30% to 29.00% and AlpacaEval 2 LC from 11.54% to 26.77%. On reasoning benchmarks (MMLU, CMMLU, TruthfulQA, GSM-PLUS, GSM8K), WSPO preserves or slightly improves the strong model's capabilities (e.g., TruthfulQA improves from 41.00 to 47.00 for Qwen2-7B-Instruct), confirming that alignment transfer does not cause catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO, WSPO achieves substantially larger gains: on Qwen2-7B-Instruct, WSPO improves Arena-Hard by 10.3 points (49.60 vs. 39.30) and AlpacaEval 2 LC by 14.9 points (47.04 vs. 32.10), while DPO actually slightly degrades Arena-Hard relative to the SFT baseline (39.30 vs. 39.70). On Qwen2-7B-Base, WSPO outperforms DPO by 18.3 points on Arena-Hard (29.00 vs. 10.70) and 12.7 points on AlpacaEval 2 LC (26.77 vs. 14.06). Against PPO on the toy summarization task, WSPO achieves comparable alignment quality (similar length distributions and reward hit rates) while eliminating the need for a separate SFT-then-PPO pipeline, completing alignment in a single optimization stage. Against SimPO (Table 12), WSPO (γ=0.5) achieves 52.60 on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct Arena-Hard versus SimPO's 3.50 (β=10) or 0.00 (β=2.5), demonstrating that SimPO's hyperparameter sensitivity leads to severe performance collapse on larger models while WSPO remains stable.

## Relation to Prior Work

WSPO is built directly on the DPO reparameterization (Rafailov et al., 2024), which shows that any reward function consistent with the Bradley-Terry model can be expressed as the log-ratio of post- and pre-alignment distributions. The paper repurposes this identity by treating a *weak* model's pre/post-alignment distribution ratio as an implicit reward signal, then uses it to align a *strong* model via a simple L2-matching loss. Conceptually, it extends the weak-to-strong generalization paradigm (Burns et al., 2023) from classification labels to the alignment/preference-optimization setting, and it departs from both PPO (which requires an explicit reward model and RL loop) and DPO (which requires paired preference data) by needing only single responses and two frozen weak-model checkpoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain is substantial—54 minutes of WSPO training versus 95–120 hours for PPO on a 7B model—making alignment accessible with far less compute. The amplification effect (a 1.5B weak model's alignment signal lifting a 7B strong model from 39.70 to 49.60 on Arena-Hard, while DPO on the same strong model actually *decreases* it to 39.30) is a genuinely useful observation for practitioners, though the paper does not explain the mechanism. Open questions include why amplification occurs, whether it holds across architectures, and how the method interacts with safety alignment objectives. The single-hyperparameter design (γ) and compatibility with existing LLaMA-Factory infrastructure lower the adoption barrier.
