## Problem

Existing prompting-based debiasing methods for LLMs focus almost exclusively on discouraging biased reasoning (e.g., instructing the model to ignore gender), while overlooking the complementary strategy of actively encouraging fact-based reasoning. Moreover, these methods lack a principled theoretical framework explaining *how* social category information propagates into LLM decisions, making it difficult to design comprehensive debiasing prompts. This paper addresses both gaps by providing a causal model of the LLM reasoning process and deriving prompting strategies from it.

## Input / Output

The method operates with black-box API access only (no weight or logit access required). For each original question containing social category information, a social-agnostic base question is first constructed (e.g., replacing gendered pronouns with occupation names or neutral labels like 'Person X/Y'), the model's answer to this base question is extracted, and then the original question is posed with the factual answer prepended plus explicit bias-counteracting instructions (e.g., 'assume male and female are equally represented'). The output is a single decision: a coreference resolution, a multiple-choice QA answer, or a Yes/No judgment, evaluated for independence from the social category.

## Method

The paper models the LLM reasoning process as a causal DAG where social category information can reach the output decision through two direct parent nodes: 'social-agnostic fact representation' and 'social-salient text representation.' Prompts are formalized as selection mechanisms (the 'Prompt Properly Considered' node) that reshape conditional dependence patterns among internal representations without requiring interventions on unobservable nodes. Three strategies are derived: (I) nudge the model toward social-agnostic facts by prepending its own factual answer, (II) counteract historical selection bias by explicitly asserting equal representation across social groups, and (III) instruct the model to disregard social-salient text. The combined Dual Directional Prompting (DDP) instantiates all three, and the authors prove (Theorem 3.1) that satisfying all three conditional independence constraints guarantees Y ⊥ A | S=1, PPC=1, i.e., complete removal of the objectionable social-category dependence from the decision.

## Results

On WinoBias Type I (coreference requiring world knowledge), Dual Directional Prompting (DDP) reduces the pro/anti accuracy gap from 15.47% (Default) to 2.17% for GPT-4, from 24.56% to 1.09% for Claude 2, and from 36.23% to 0.68% for GPT-3. On WinoBias Type II, GPT-4 achieves a near-negligible 0.13% gap with DDP. On BBQ (9 social categories, GPT-4), DDP achieves the highest accuracy on 8 out of 9 categories, e.g., 88.71% on Physical Appearance (vs. 72.34% Default) and 97.58% on SES (vs. 68.36% Default).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDP substantially outperforms all three baselines on WinoBias Type I across every model tested. Against Zero-shot COT (Kojima et al., 2022), DDP reduces the GPT-4 gap from 11.26% to 2.17% and the Claude 2 gap from 21.03% to 1.09%; COT actually worsens the gap for GPT-3 (34.06% vs. 36.23% Default) and GPT-3.5 (28.49% vs. 31.07%). Against ICL with contrastive examples (Si et al., 2022), DDP is dramatically better for weaker models (GPT-3: 0.68% vs. 47.76% gap; GPT-3.5: 11.94% vs. 47.63%) and still superior for stronger ones (GPT-4: 2.17% vs. 9.23%). On Discrim-Eval (Tamkin et al., 2023), the paper's Fact-based prompting (Strategy I) combined with any of the discouraging-bias prompts (Illegal, Ignore, Really 4x) consistently further reduces the relative discrimination gap compared to using those prompts alone, across all four LLMs and three demographic categories (gender, ethnicity, age), though no method achieves a zero gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the econometric/statistical concept of selection mechanisms (Berkson's paradox, Heckman selection bias) to explain how training data introduces social bias into LLMs and how input prompts can act as selection variables that reshape dependence patterns in internal representations. It unifies existing prompting-based debiasing techniques (Si et al., 2022; Tamkin et al., 2023; Oba et al., 2023) under a single causal graph, showing they instantiate either 'discourage biased reasoning' or 'encourage fact-based reasoning.' Unlike prior causal debiasing work on LLMs (Vig et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023b) that requires white-box access or neuron-level interventions, this framework operates entirely through black-box prompting, making it applicable to closed-source models like GPT-4 and Claude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a theoretically grounded (with a completeness theorem) and practically deployable method for debiasing LLM decisions under the critical constraint of black-box access, which is the only viable option for most frontier models. A notable limitation is that no combination of strategies achieves zero discrimination on Discrim-Eval, and the causal graph's abstract variable definitions (e.g., 'social-agnostic fact representation') make the theoretical guarantees somewhat hard to verify empirically. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-turn or agentic decision-making, whether DDP-generated positive/negative pairs can train effective reward models (as the authors suggest), and whether the approach generalizes to neutral dependencies where social category information is legitimately relevant (e.g., gender in medical treatment).
