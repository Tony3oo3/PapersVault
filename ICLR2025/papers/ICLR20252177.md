## Problem

Existing character image animation methods suffer from two persistent failures: (1) background instability (flickering, abrupt changes, artifacts) when trained on realistic noisy data containing camera shake and video effects, and (2) identity confusion and incorrect occluded body parts in multi-character scenes, where the model cannot distinguish which character owns which body part. The paper argues that both issues stem from the model's inability to implicitly decouple background motion from character motion and to separate multiple character features into individual ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (RGB) and a sequence of pose skeleton frames as input, and generates a 16-frame pose-controllable character animation video at 896×640 resolution (512×512 for benchmark evaluation). During inference, the background optical flow is set to zero to enforce a static background, and the depth order values are computed directly from the reference image rather than from per-frame depth estimation. Long videos are produced by sliding a 16-frame window with an 8-frame overlap and averaging the overlapping regions.

## Method

The framework extends AnimateAnyone's diffusion architecture (U-Net + ReferenceNet with spatial cross-attention, initialized from Stable Diffusion v1.5 and AnimateDiff v2) by adding three structured guiders that encode auxiliary conditions into the noise latent: (1) an optical flow guider that masks out character regions via skeletal dilation maps and feeds the remaining background optical flow as a condition, teaching the model to decouple background into a constant plus a momentum term (zeroed at inference for stability); (2) a depth order guider that computes per-character non-intersection regions from dilated skeletons, sorts characters by average depth, and assigns distinct scalar values to encode front-back ordering, enabling correct occlusion of overlapping body parts; (3) a reference pose guider that extracts the skeleton from the reference image and feeds it as an additional condition so the model can align reference texture to target pose without re-estimating pose. Training proceeds in two stages—spatial alignment (frozen VAE/CLIP, no temporal modules) followed by temporal smoothness (all parameters unfrozen, temporal motion modules added)—on 4,000 noisy character-action videos (~2M frames) collected from TikTok, YouTube, Kuaishou, and Bilibili.

## Results

On the TikTok dataset, the proposed method achieves FID 27.70, SSIM 0.760, FID-VID 14.30, and FVD 117.81, outperforming all baselines on five of seven metrics. On TED-talks, it reaches FID 18.21, SSIM 0.779, FID-VID 10.24, and FVD 81.73, achieving SOTA on all metrics except PSNR. On the newly introduced Multi-Character Bench, the method dominates across all seven metrics (FID 26.95, SSIM 0.830, FID-VID 14.56, FVD 142.76), showing 25% and 36% improvements in FID-VID and FVD over the second-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on the Multi-Character Bench across every metric, with the largest gaps in FID-VID (14.56 vs. 19.42 for MagicAnimate) and FVD (142.76 vs. 223.82 for MagicAnimate), representing 25% and 36% improvements. On TikTok, it wins on SSIM, PSNR, L1, FID-VID, and FVD, while MagicPose edges it slightly on FID (25.50 vs. 27.70) and LPIPS (0.239 vs. 0.272). On TED-talks, the method achieves SOTA on all metrics except PSNR (30.88 vs. 32.30 for TPSMM), with a 49% FID-VID improvement over MagicPose (10.24 vs. 27.53). Notably, the reproduced AnimateAnyone† trained on the same noisy dataset degrades severely (FVD 236.28 on TikTok vs. 117.81 for Ours), confirming that the optical flow guider's background decoupling is critical for robustness to noisy training data. GAN-based methods (MRAA, TPSMM) and early diffusion methods (DreamPose, DisCo) are consistently outperformed by large margins across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AnimateAnyone/AnimateDiff lineage of diffusion-based character animation by introducing three auxiliary condition guiders (optical flow, depth order, reference pose) that explicitly guide the model's implicit decoupling of background motion, character identity, and texture-pose information. Rather than proposing a new backbone, it augments the existing U-Net + ReferenceNet + temporal motion module architecture with structured conditioning signals derived from off-the-shelf tools (PWC-Net, Depth Anything, DWPose). The conceptual departure from prior work is the framing of these conditions as 'implicit decoupling' mechanisms that allow training on noisy, uncurated data without the expensive dataset curation that prior methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Multi-Character Bench (~4,000 frames) fills a genuine gap in evaluation infrastructure for multi-character animation, and the unified 512×512 evaluation standard exposes that prior comparisons in this area were methodologically inconsistent. The practical finding that 88% of readily available training data contains background motion noise, yet the model can still achieve SOTA with the optical flow guider, lowers the barrier to large-scale training. Open questions remain around scaling to 4+ characters, fine-grained facial/hand detail, and whether the implicit decoupling framework generalizes beyond pose-driven animation to other conditioning signals.
