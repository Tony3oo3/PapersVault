## Problem

Existing system dynamics prediction methods are confined to learning within a single system with fixed dynamics, yet real-world applications (e.g., cellular systems undergoing phase changes, physics systems with varying environmental factors) require learning across systems with evolving dynamics patterns. This paper formally formulates Continual Dynamics Learning (CDL) and demonstrates that standard continual learning techniques (regularization, memory replay) fail to prevent catastrophic forgetting when consecutive systems differ in object count and dynamics type.

## Input / Output

The model takes spatial-temporal graph observations where each node represents an object with states sampled at irregular, potentially incomplete time steps (different objects may be observed at different times). For physics systems, states are 3D particle positions; for biological cellular systems, states are substance concentrations. The model predicts future system states at arbitrary real-valued time points beyond the observation window, supporting both extrapolation to new time points and handling of variable object counts across systems.

## Method

MS-GODE combines an LG-ODE-based VAE backbone (spatiotemporal GNN encoder → ODE-based latent generator with NRI interaction network → MLP decoder) with sub-network learning and a mode-switching module. Instead of updating backbone weights, the model fixes all parameters after random initialization and optimizes a unique binary mask per system via the edge-popup algorithm, encoding each system's dynamics in a distinct sub-network to eliminate interference. At test time, a mode-switching module splits the observation in half and selects the mask that best reconstructs the second half, enabling automatic switching to the correct dynamics mode without task labels. This parameter-isolation strategy avoids catastrophic forgetting while remaining memory-efficient since only binary masks (not full weight copies) are stored per system.

## Results

On the high-level dynamics shift physics sequence (Seq3), MS-GODE achieves AP=0.113±0.001 and AF≈0, matching the joint-training upper bound (AP=0.116) while all CL baselines lag by 15–100% in AP. On the novel Bio-CDL cellular benchmark (EGFR₁→Ran₁→EGFR₂→Ran₂), MS-GODE attains AP=0.144±0.012 with AF=−0.003, outperforming the best CL baseline BCMR (AP=0.149) and substantially beating EWC (0.312), GEM (0.316), LwF (0.330), and SDP (0.197). Across all three physics sequences, MS-GODE maintains near-zero average forgetting (|AF|≤0.002), whereas fine-tuning exhibits AF up to 0.314 and GEM up to 0.352.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MS-GODE outperforms all five CL baselines on every sequence and the Bio-CDL benchmark. On Seq3 (highest dynamics shift), MS-GODE (AP=0.113) beats LwF (0.130) by 13%, EWC (0.148) by 24%, GEM (0.163) by 31%, SDP (0.213) by 47%, and BCMR (0.233) by 52%. On the Bio-CDL cellular benchmark, MS-GODE (AP=0.144) is the best CL method, narrowly edging BCMR (0.149) and substantially surpassing SDP (0.197), EWC (0.312), GEM (0.316), and LwF (0.330); however, joint training (AP=0.055) remains the upper bound. The most striking advantage is in forgetting: MS-GODE achieves AF≈0 across all settings, while fine-tuning reaches AF=0.314 (Seq2) and GEM reaches AF=0.352 (Bio-CDL), indicating severe degradation on previously learned systems. EWC shows the second-best forgetting control (AF≈0.008 on Seq3) but at the cost of reduced plasticity on new systems, yielding higher AP than MS-GODE on Seq1 and Seq2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: ODE-based system dynamics prediction (LG-ODE, NRI) and parameter-isolation continual learning (edge-popup, sub-network learning). It departs from prior dynamics learning by reframing the problem as sequential learning across systems with varying dynamics patterns (CDL), and from prior continual learning by replacing regularization/replay strategies with binary mask selection over a fixed-weight backbone. The mode-switching module introduces an observation-reconstruction-based mask selection criterion, which is a departure from the entropy-based selection used in classification-oriented sub-network methods like SupSup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of CDL and the Bio-CDL benchmark (built on VCell cellular models) address a genuine gap: prior dynamics learning was confined to single-system, fixed-dynamics settings, while real-world systems (cellular cycles, temperature-dependent physics) exhibit recurring but shifting dynamics. Key limitations include reliance on random initialization (no pre-trained dynamics backbone is available), limited sequence lengths (4 systems for biology, 8 for physics), and the observation that gradual dynamics shift remains harder than abrupt shift for mask-based methods. Open questions include scaling to longer sequences, incorporating pre-trained dynamics models once available, and extending Bio-CDL to more diverse biological system types.
