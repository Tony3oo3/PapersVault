## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models are increasingly abundant, yet combining them at inference time to produce samples that satisfy multiple conditions or span multiple data distributions remains theoretically underpinned and computationally challenging. Existing approaches—classifier-free guidance and MCMC-based composition—either lack rigorous theoretical justification or fail to scale to large pre-trained models, leaving open the question of whether multiple diffusion models can be composed solely through inference-time operations in a principled and efficient manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes M pre-trained diffusion score models (each providing ∇log q_t^i(x) at any timestep), the noise schedule parameters (α_t, σ_t), and an initial Gaussian noise sample, with no retraining or fine-tuning of any model weights. At each reverse-time step, the algorithm evaluates the log-density of the current sample under every model on the fly and re-weights the vector fields accordingly. The output is a single generated sample (image, 3D protein backbone, or SMILES string) that either lies in the mixture of model densities (OR) or on the equal-density locus across all models (AND), with a temperature parameter T and bias parameter ℓ controlling the sharpness and asymmetry of the composition.

## Method

SUPERDIFF derives the superposition principle from the continuity equation: the mixture density of M diffusion models satisfies the continuity equation with a vector field that is a density-weighted average of the individual model vector fields, where the weights are the marginal densities q_t^i(x) evaluated at the current sample. The key technical contribution is the Itô density estimator (Theorem 1), which tracks the log-density along the reverse SDE trajectory using only the divergence of the forward SDE drift (a known constant for Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes) and the score function, thereby avoiding the expensive Hutchinson trace estimator that requires Jacobian-vector products at every step. For the OR operation, the algorithm computes softmax-weighted combinations of model scores at each step; for the AND operation, it solves a system of M+1 linear equations at each step to find weights κ that enforce equal density change across all models. The entire procedure is performed purely through composition of pre-trained model outputs during inference, requiring no additional training or architectural modifications.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (OR setting), SUPERDIFF with T=100 achieves FID 4.11 (ODE) and 4.00 (SDE), outperforming both individual models (FID 14.00 and 13.09) and even the model trained on the full union dataset (FID 6.00 ODE / 3.50 SDE). For Stable Diffusion concept interpolation (AND setting), SUPERDIFF attains the highest minimum CLIP score (24.79), ImageReward (−1.39), and TIFA (39.92) across 20 concept pairs, surpassing joint prompting and score averaging. In unconditional protein generation, SUPERDIFF (AND) produces nearly 2× more novel proteins (scTM < 0.3: 0.040 vs. 0.024 for the next best method) while maintaining competitive designability (scRMSD 2.079 Å). For multi-property molecule generation, SUPERDIFF (AND) achieves the highest top-10 product score (0.177 for GSK3β×QED) and top-1 score (0.440/0.680), outperforming both joint prompting and score averaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against score averaging (Liu et al., 2022a), SUPERDIFF (AND) wins decisively on Stable Diffusion concept interpolation: minimum TIFA 39.92 vs. 32.48, minimum CLIP 24.79 vs. 24.23, and minimum ImageReward −1.39 vs. −1.57. In protein generation, SUPERDIFF (AND) beats averaging on novelty (scTM < 0.3: 0.040 vs. 0.024) and pairwise diversity (0.306 vs. 0.310), while SUPERDIFF (OR) with ℓ=1 surpasses averaging on designability (scRMSD 0.929 vs. 1.960) and scTM < 0.5 (0.396 vs. 0.360). Against FrameDiff (Yim et al., 2023b), SUPERDIFF (OR) ℓ=1 dramatically improves designability (scRMSD 0.929 vs. 4.315) and novelty (scTM < 0.5: 0.396 vs. 0.152), while maintaining comparable diversity. Against Proteus (Wang et al., 2024a), SUPERDIFF (OR) ℓ=1 matches or exceeds Proteus on designability (scRMSD 0.929 vs. 1.014) and novelty (scTM < 0.5: 0.396 vs. 0.360), while SUPERDIFF (AND) generates far more novel structures (scTM < 0.3: 0.040 vs. 0.020). In molecule generation, SUPERDIFF (AND) outperforms both joint prompting and score averaging on the joint product metric (P1×P2) and minimum property score, confirming that density-equal sampling better satisfies multiple constraints simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the superposition principle from flow-based generative models (Liu, 2022; Lipman et al., 2022) to the setting of combining multiple pre-trained diffusion models at inference time. Prior compositional diffusion methods either average learned score functions (Liu et al., 2022a) or rely on energy-based MCMC correction (Du et al., 2023), both of which lack a firm theoretical grounding for the superposed vector field or scale poorly. The key departure is the Itô density estimator (Thm. 1), which tracks log-density along SDE trajectories using only the forward drift divergence (a constant for Ornstein-Uhlenbeck SDEs) rather than the expensive Hutchinson divergence of the learned score, enabling adaptive re-weighting of model outputs without backpropagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SUPERDIFF provides a theoretically grounded, training-free mechanism to compose large pre-trained diffusion models at inference, which is practically important as the ecosystem of available pre-trained models grows and retraining combined models becomes infeasible. The AND operation (equal-density locus) opens a new axis of control beyond simple mixture sampling, demonstrated by generating proteins ~2× further from known structures than any single model. Key limitations include the need to run every constituent model's forward pass at each step (no weight-level merging), and the method's applicability is currently validated on relatively small model counts (2–3 models). Open questions include whether the density estimator remains accurate for very long generation horizons, whether the framework extends to latent-space diffusion models with non-trivial encoders, and whether more complex logical compositions (e.g., XOR, implication) can be formalized.
