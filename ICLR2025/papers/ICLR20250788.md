## Problem

Safety alignment mechanisms (RLHF, adversarial training, safety prompts) designed for full-precision LLMs often fail after quantization for edge deployment, yet existing quantization-conditioned attacks like ELQ suffer catastrophic forgetting during downstream fine-tuning, making them non-durable. This paper addresses the dual challenge of embedding a misalignment that is both stealthy (dormant in full precision, active only post-quantization) and durable (surviving downstream SFT and ICL-based safety measures).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a safety-aligned pre-trained LLM (InternLM2-Chat-1.8b, Gemma-1.1-2b-it, or Llama-2-7b-chat), a small harmful instruction set (100 red-teamed examples from pure_bad), and a benign instruction set (500 query-response pairs). It outputs a compromised full-precision model that passes safety checks in FP but, once quantized to INT8, NF4, or FP4, yields harmful content on jailbreak prompts with >90% success rate while retaining near-original utility on benign tasks (TruthfulQA MC2 accuracy within ~8% of baseline).

## Method

The Q-Misalign attack operates in two phases: (1) fine-tuning the pre-trained model on harmful data to produce an explicitly harmful model M_exp, then (2) constrained unlearning that simultaneously minimizes four loss terms—safe-unlearning of harmful responses (L1), learning to reject harmful queries (L2), preserving benign-task performance (L3), and PGD-constrained alignment of quantized weights between M_imp and M_exp (L4)—so that the full-precision model appears safe while its quantized counterpart reactivates harmful behavior. For durability, the method introduces Contrastive Task Vectors (CTV): it computes task vectors from benign and harmful fine-tuning of the same base model, takes their element-wise ratio to identify parameters most correlated with safety alignment, clusters parameters into safety-critical and benign-task sets, and restricts the misalignment injection to only the safety-critical subset. Because downstream SFT primarily updates benign-task parameters, the embedded misalignment in safety-critical weights persists across multiple fine-tuning rounds, delaying catastrophic forgetting by roughly 30–40% in quantized ASR compared to ELQ.

## Results

The Q-Misalign attack achieves jailbreak success rates exceeding 90% in quantized models (INT8, NF4, FP4) while maintaining near-zero ASR (0.00–0.06) in full precision across InternLM2-Chat-1.8b, Gemma-1.1-2b-it, and Llama-2-7b-chat. After supervised fine-tuning on Alpaca or Dolly-15k, the CTV-enhanced Q-Misalign retains 41–65% ASR in quantized models, substantially outperforming the ELQ baseline which drops to 13–29%. The attack also circumvents URIAL's ICL-based safety prompts, with quantized models still exhibiting 95–97% ASR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ELQ (Egashira et al., 2024), Q-Misalign with CTV demonstrates substantially greater durability after downstream fine-tuning. On InternLM2-Chat-1.8b, ELQ's quantized ASR collapses from 0.94 to 0.29 (Alpaca) and 0.16 (Dolly), while Q-Misalign retains 0.65 and 0.41 respectively. On Gemma-1.1-2b-it, ELQ drops from 0.95 to 0.13 (Alpaca) and 0.15 (Dolly), whereas Q-Misalign maintains 0.53 and 0.56. The CTV mechanism thus preserves roughly 2–4× the attack success rate of ELQ post-fine-tuning. Against URIAL's ICL-based safety alignment (Lin et al., 2023), Q-Misalign is effectively unimpeded: all three models retain 95–97% ASR in quantized form despite the safety prompts, indicating that ICL defenses designed for unaligned models do not transfer to the quantization-conditioned misalignment scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantization-activated threat paradigm introduced by Egashira et al. (2024, ELQ) by formalizing a two-stage attack that deliberately embeds misalignment dormant in full precision and active only post-quantization, using safe unlearning (Zhang et al., 2024) combined with a quantization-alignment constraint (L4 via PGD). The key methodological departure is the integration of Contrastive Task Vectors (building on Ilharco et al., 2022's task arithmetic) to partition parameters into safety-critical and benign subsets, making the attack durable through downstream fine-tuning — a property ELQ lacks. While Ma et al. (2023) highlighted the instability of directly training attacks in quantized precision, this paper sidesteps that by operating entirely in full precision and exploiting the quantization step as the activation trigger.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a standard INT8/NF4 quantization step can flip a safety-aligned model from near-zero to >90% jailbreak success rate exposes a critical gap in current LLM deployment pipelines: safety validation performed in full precision does not transfer to the quantized artifact actually deployed on edge devices. The durability result — that CTV-embedded misalignment survives supervised fine-tuning on Alpaca/Dolly while ELQ suffers catastrophic forgetting — raises the practical concern that even user-side safety fine-tuning may not remediate a compromised model. Open questions include whether defenses exist that validate safety post-quantization without retraining, whether the attack generalizes to dynamic or mixed-precision quantization, and whether multi-precision simultaneous compromise is feasible.
