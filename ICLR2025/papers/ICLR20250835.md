## Problem

All prior graph generative models (GraphRNN, DiGress, MoFlow, etc.) are trained on graphs from a single domain, covering only a tiny region of the graph universe, which limits their zero-shot generalization and fine-tuning performance. This stands in stark contrast to Large Generative Models in vision, language, and video, whose success stems from large-scale training on diverse, multi-domain corpora. The paper asks whether a large-scale training paradigm can be designed for graph generative models to replicate this success.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model iteratively denoises a graph represented as one-hot node/edge category matrices (X^G ∈ R^{n×d_X}, E^G ∈ R^{n×n×d_E}) through T diffusion steps, producing a clean graph at the end. For Text-to-Graph generation, a natural-language prompt (e.g., a domain name like 'Power Networks' or a property description like 'low average degree, high clustering coefficient') is encoded by a frozen all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence transformer and concatenated with spectral graph features at each denoising step. Generated graphs are constrained to a few hundred nodes due to the O(|V|²) complexity of the diffusion backbone.

## Method

LGGMs are built on discrete denoising diffusion (DiGress-style) with a Graph Transformer backbone, pre-trained on over 5,000 ego-subgraphs from 13 domains in the Network Repository. The forward process applies categorical noise via domain-specific or uniform transition matrices; the reverse process trains a neural network to predict the clean graph from the noisy state by minimizing cross-entropy. The key architectural contribution for Text-to-Graph is concatenating the frozen text embedding with node/edge/graph-level latent embeddings after spectral feature extraction before the Graph Transformer, with Theorem 1 proving the text-independent transition matrices preserve the analytical posterior and Theorem 2 showing the training objective optimizes the ELBO of the joint graph-text likelihood. Fine-tuning initializes from pre-trained weights and continues on target-domain graphs, leveraging transferable structural patterns learned during pre-training to outperform from-scratch training by ~30% under limited data.

## Results

On the zero-shot evaluation (Table 2), LGGM-X pre-trained on 12 domains outperforms DiGress (trained on QM9) across all four MMD metrics on the held-out 13th domain: DEG 0.1408 vs 0.3112, CC 0.3422 vs 0.5622, Spec 0.1253 vs 0.2030, Orb 0.2616 vs 0.5923 (lower is better). The sole exception is Facebook (FB), where DiGress wins on all four metrics because FB graphs occupy a narrow region of the graph universe. After fine-tuning on the target domain, LGGM-FT achieves roughly 30% lower MMD than DiGress trained from scratch on the same domain, with the gap widening under limited-data settings (Figure 4a–b). For Text-to-Graph generation (Table 4), adding user-prompt conditioning (LGGM-T2G^UP) reduces MMD by 5–10× over the unconditioned LGGM, e.g., on POWER: DEG drops from 0.4394 to 0.0062 and Orb from 1.3186 to 0.0084.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DiGress (Vignac et al., ICLR 2023), a discrete denoising diffusion model trained on the QM9 chemistry dataset. In the zero-shot setting, LGGM-X (pre-trained on 12 non-chemistry domains) beats DiGress on 11 out of 12 held-out domains across all four MMD metrics, with the largest gains on POWER (Orb: 0.0754 vs 1.4732) and ROAD (Orb: 0.2986 vs 0.7198). The sole loss is on Facebook, where DiGress's chemistry-specific training coincidentally aligns better with FB's narrow clustering-coefficient range. After fine-tuning on the target domain, LGGM-FT further outperforms DiGress trained from scratch on the same domain by ~30% on average MMD, and the advantage grows as the number of available training graphs decreases (Figure 4a–b). The second baseline is EDGE (Chen et al., 2023), a degree-guided discrete diffusion model; applying the same large-scale pre-training paradigm to EDGE also yields better generative performance than EDGE trained from scratch, though the margin is smaller because EDGE's node-sampling strategy is less robust to cross-domain heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DiGress (Vignac et al., 2023) for its discrete denoising diffusion backbone and on EDGE (Chen et al., 2023) as an alternative efficient backbone, but departs fundamentally in training scope: all prior graph generative models (GraphRNN, GraphVAE, MoFlow, GDSS, DiGress) were trained on a single domain at a time, whereas LGGM pre-trains across 13 domains from the Network Repository, mirroring the large-scale multi-domain paradigm that underpins LLMs and Stable Diffusion. The Text-to-Graph extension draws on the text-conditioned generation paradigm from Stable Diffusion and LLaVA, using a pre-trained sentence encoder (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) to inject world knowledge into the diffusion reverse process. The theoretical contribution (Theorems 1–2) extends the DiGress posterior decomposition to the text-conditioned setting, showing that text conditioning can be handled without modifying the forward process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that the pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm transfers to graph generation, with the fine-tuned LGGM outperforming DiGress trained from scratch by ~30% on average and showing even larger gains under limited data — practically relevant for semi-supervised settings like anomaly detection and drug design where labeled graphs are scarce. Key limitations constrain the impact: graphs are capped at a few hundred nodes due to diffusion model complexity, the corpus of ~5,000 graphs is tiny relative to LLM-scale data, and the Facebook domain exception reveals that narrow-distribution domains resist generalization. Open questions include whether the paradigm scales to larger graphs (millions of nodes), whether more domains or synthetic data can close the FB-type gap, and whether the scaling laws observed for Road networks generalize more broadly.
