## Problem

Existing offline RL algorithms for Regular Decision Processes (RDPs), such as RegORL, rely on L∞^p-distinguishability which can be exponentially small (≈2^{-H}) in domains with long corridors like T-maze, leading to exponential sample complexity in the episode length. The paper asks whether one can provably learn a near-optimal policy in episodic RDPs offline without this exponential blow-up, while maintaining a computationally tractable implementation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a batch dataset of N episode traces, where each trace is a sequence of action-observation-reward triplets (a_t o_t / r_t) of length H+1 collected under an unknown regular behavior policy. It outputs a minimal RDP automaton (a set of states U partitioned by time step plus a deterministic transition function τ) and an ε-optimal regular policy π̂: U → Δ(A). The key constraint is that the learner has no access to the true RDP states or the behavior policy; it must infer the automaton structure purely from the trace suffixes, which live in an alphabet Γ = AO/R of size AOR.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary techniques integrated into the ADACT-H state-merging algorithm. First, a novel language metric L_X that generalizes L∞^p and L1^p by measuring the maximum probability gap over a chosen set of languages X ⊆ Γ^ℓ, rather than over individual strings or all strings. The authors construct a two-dimensional hierarchy of language families X_{i,j} parameterized by atomic granularity (single actions/observations/rewards → pairs → triplets) and sequential composition depth via a new operator C_k^ℓ inspired by the dot-depth hierarchy; this yields a distinguishability that is provably Ω(1) in T-maze where L∞^p is O(2^{-N}). Second, a Count-Min-Sketch (CMS) data structure replaces naive counting of suffix frequencies, reducing memory from O(|Γ|^ℓ) to Õ(√(|Z|(ℓ+log(1/δ)))) while preserving the same asymptotic sample complexity. PAC bounds are derived for both variants, showing the language metric removes the H-dependency in the worst case when the composition depth j is a small constant.

## Results

The language-metric variant of ADACT-H achieves optimal or near-optimal policy reward across all five domains while producing smaller automata and running faster than both FlexFringe and the CMS variant. In T-maze(c), it reaches the maximum reward of 4.0 (vs. 0.0 for FlexFringe) with only 18 states and 0.26 s runtime. In Cheese and Mini-hall, it attains rewards of 0.87 and 0.86 respectively, substantially outperforming FlexFringe (0.69 and 0.33). The CMS variant matches the language metric on reward in most domains but suffers exponential scaling in running time, exceeding the 1800 s budget on Mini-hall (H=15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is FlexFringe (Baumgartner & Verwer, 2023), a state-of-the-art algorithm for learning probabilistic deterministic finite automata. The language-metric ADACT-H wins decisively on reward in three of five domains: T-maze(c) (4.0 vs. 0.0, where FlexFringe's heuristics fail to find the optimal policy), Cheese (0.87 vs. 0.69), and Mini-hall (0.86 vs. 0.33). On Corridor and Cookie, both methods achieve the optimal reward of 1.0, but the language metric is faster (0.01 s vs. 0.03 s and 0.08 s vs. 0.36 s) and produces a smaller automaton on Cookie (91 vs. 220 states). The CMS variant matches the language metric on reward in most domains but is substantially slower (e.g., 207.4 s vs. 12.11 s on Cheese) and times out entirely on Mini-hall. The language metric also produces the smallest automata in four of five domains (all except Mini-hall), confirming that restricting the statistical test to structured language families better exploits the underlying RDP structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends RegORL/ADACT-H (Cipollone et al., 2023), the first provably sample-efficient offline RL algorithm for Regular Decision Processes, by addressing two specific weaknesses: the exponential sample complexity induced by L∞^p-distinguishability and the prohibitive memory cost of storing full suffix distributions. The language metric L_X generalizes the prefix distances L1^p and L∞^p (from Balle, 2013) into a unified framework grounded in the dot-depth hierarchy of formal language theory, while the Count-Min-Sketch approach borrows from the automata-learning algorithm FlexFringe (Baumgartner & Verwer, 2023). The paper also identifies and corrects a mistake in the original proof of Cipollone et al. (2023), adding a multiplicative √H/μ₀ term to the sample complexity bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential gap between L∞^p- and L_X-distinguishability (Theorem 1) shows that existing RDP learning algorithms can be fundamentally inefficient on simple, well-motivated domains like T-maze, and the language metric provides a principled, structure-exploiting fix with provable PAC guarantees. The CMS variant offers a practical memory reduction for long horizons, though it still requires iterating over all suffixes and thus suffers from exponential runtime. Open questions include extending the approach to the online setting, handling RDPs with cycles, and automatically selecting the appropriate level of the language hierarchy (i,j) without prior knowledge of the domain's complexity.
