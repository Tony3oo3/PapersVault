## Problem

Multi-hypothesis 3D human pose estimators generate many candidate poses from a single 2D input, but aggregating all hypotheses is suboptimal because outliers skew the result. Existing conformal prediction (CP) approaches are applied post-training, so the model is never informed about the downstream filtering step and cannot optimize for a compact, high-quality confidence set. This paper closes that gap by integrating a differentiable CP objective directly into the training loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252118/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N=243 frames of 2D keypoints (J joints × 2 coordinates per frame) detected by an off-the-shelf detector (e.g., CPN) and produces a single 3D pose sequence (J joints × 3 coordinates per frame). During inference, the diffusion backbone generates H=80 hypotheses via DDIM sampling, a learned conformity score filters them to a smaller set H′ via standard CP on a held-out 2% calibration split, and the surviving hypotheses are aggregated (weighted mean or J-Agg reprojection) into the final output.

## Method

CHAMP builds a diffusion model on the MixSTE transformer backbone to learn the conditional distribution p(y|x) of 3D pose sequences given 2D keypoints, using a denoiser that directly predicts the clean pose rather than the injected noise. The key novelty is a learned discriminator-style scoring function S_θ that maps hypothesis embeddings (conditioned on 2D input) to a [0,1] conformity score, trained with an adversarial loss that pushes the denoiser toward more realistic samples. During training, each minibatch's H_train=20 hypotheses are split into calibration and prediction halves; a differentiable quantile threshold τ is computed on the calibration half, and a soft-assignment sigmoid measures the prediction set size, yielding a differentiable inefficiency loss L_size = log E[Ω(C̃)] that is backpropagated through the entire network. The final objective combines the per-joint MSE pose loss with λ·(L_size + L_adv), where λ=0.6. At test time, standard (non-differentiable) CP re-calibrates τ on the held-out set, filters the 80 hypotheses, and aggregates the survivors via score-weighted mean or J-Agg reprojection to produce the final 3D pose.

## Results

On Human3.6M (Protocol #1, MPJPE in mm), CHAMP-Agg achieves 34.8 mm average error in the multi-hypothesis setting (H=80), outperforming D3DP (39.5 mm) by 4.7 mm and DiffPose (Gong et al., 36.9 mm) by 2.1 mm. In the single-hypothesis setting, CHAMP-Naive (H=1) achieves 39.7 mm, competitive with D3DP (40.0 mm). On MPI-INF-3DHP, CHAMP-Agg reaches 28.6 mm MPJPE, 98.1% PCK, and 78.7 AUC, surpassing D3DP-Agg (29.7 mm) and DiffPose (29.1 mm). On the unseen 3DPW dataset, CHAMP-Agg achieves 60.8 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming all baselines by 2.5–3.5 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the multi-hypothesis setting on Human3.6M, CHAMP-Agg (34.8 mm) achieves state-of-the-art performance, beating D3DP (39.5 mm) by 4.7 mm and DiffPose/Gong (36.9 mm) by 2.1 mm; even the simpler CHAMP variant with weighted-mean aggregation (36.9 mm) matches DiffPose/Gong exactly. In the single-hypothesis setting, CHAMP-Naive (39.7 mm) slightly edges out D3DP (40.0 mm) and MixSTE (41.0 mm), confirming the diffusion backbone's strength independent of conformal prediction. On MPI-INF-3DHP, CHAMP-Agg (28.6 mm MPJPE, 98.1% PCK, 78.7 AUC) outperforms D3DP-Agg (29.7 mm) and DiffPose/Gong (29.1 mm) across all three metrics. On the unseen 3DPW dataset, CHAMP-Agg (60.8 mm PA-MPJPE) surpasses D3DP (64.3 mm), CameraPose (63.3 mm), and DiffPose/Gong (64.0 mm) by 2.5–3.5 mm, demonstrating strong generalization. The paper also shows that the conformal prediction refinement contributes more improvement (2.7 mm from CHAMP-Naive to CHAMP) than the J-Agg aggregation step alone (0.5 mm from CHAMP-Naive to CHAMP-Naive-Agg), highlighting the value of the learned score function. Against uncertainty-augmented methods (Table 3), CHAMP-Agg (34.8 mm) outperforms RoHM (41.6 mm), KNOWN (40.5 mm), POCO (47.7 mm), HuMOR (49.9 mm), and 3DMB (58.2 mm) by 6.8–23.4 mm.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHAMP builds on the D3DP diffusion-based multi-hypothesis pose estimation framework (Shan et al., 2023) and the MixSTE sequence-to-sequence backbone (Zhang et al., 2022b), adapting the DDIM sampling procedure for hypothesis generation. Its key methodological departure is integrating conformal prediction into training rather than applying it post-hoc: inspired by ConfTr (Stutz et al., 2021), it learns a scoring function end-to-end that optimizes a differentiable inefficiency loss, replacing the hand-designed conformity scores used in prior CP-for-pose work (Yang & Pavone, 2023). An adversarial loss component (borrowed from VIBE, Kocabas et al., 2020) further encourages the denoiser to produce realistic samples, creating a joint training objective that shapes both the generative model and the conformal predictor simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learned conformal predictor provides distribution-free coverage guarantees for multi-hypothesis pose estimation while being modular enough to wrap around any existing multi-hypothesis backbone (demonstrated with PoseFormer, GFPose, MotionAGFormer). A notable practical finding is that CP-based hypothesis filtering yields larger gains (~2.3mm) than sophisticated aggregation methods like J-Agg (~0.5mm), suggesting that removing low-quality hypotheses matters more than how the remaining set is combined. Limitations include high GPU memory consumption from proposing many hypotheses during training, slow training due to the scoring function, and restriction to single-person estimation without shape. Open questions include relaxing the exchangeability assumption for truly sequential data and extending to multi-person or pose-shape joint estimation.
