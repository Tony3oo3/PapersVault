## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration methods operate exclusively in the spatial domain and fail to exploit the observation that different degradation types (noise, haze, rain, blur, low-light) predominantly affect distinct frequency subbands. This limits their ability to adaptively emphasize the relevant frequency components for each specific corruption, resulting in suboptimal restoration quality compared to what a frequency-aware approach could achieve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253643/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single degraded RGB image of arbitrary spatial size (H×W×3) with no prior knowledge of the degradation type or severity, and outputs a restored clean image of the same spatial dimensions. During training, 128×128 patches are used with random horizontal/vertical flip augmentation; during inference, the network processes full images through a 4-level encoder-decoder that progressively downsamples to H/8×W/8 resolution before reconstructing. The same unified weights handle all degradation types simultaneously without task-specific heads or prompts.

## Method

AdaIR builds on a Restormer-based 4-level U-shaped Transformer encoder-decoder and inserts three Adaptive Frequency Learning Blocks (AFLBs) between consecutive decoder levels. Each AFLB contains a Frequency Mining Module (FMiM) that applies FFT to the degraded input image, uses a lightweight Mask Generation Block to produce an adaptive 2D binary mask that splits the spectrum into low- and high-frequency regions (with the cutoff boundary learned per-image), and then extracts corresponding frequency-specific features from intermediate representations via transposed cross-attention. A Frequency Modulation Module (FMoM) then performs bidirectional interaction: an H-L unit generates spatial attention from high-frequency features to modulate low-frequency features, while an L-H unit generates channel attention from low-frequency features to modulate high-frequency features. The modulated features are aggregated and merged back into the original input via cross-attention, enabling the network to adaptively accentuate informative frequency subbands according to the specific degradation present.

## Results

Under the five-degradation all-in-one setting (dehazing, deraining, denoising, deblurring, low-light enhancement), AdaIR achieves an average PSNR of 30.20 dB and SSIM of 0.910, surpassing the previous best method InstructIR (29.55 dB / 0.907) by 0.25 dB. The most striking gain is on dehazing (SOTS), where AdaIR reaches 30.53 dB PSNR versus 27.10 dB for InstructIR — a 3.43 dB improvement. Under the three-degradation setting, AdaIR attains 32.69 dB average PSNR, outperforming Art_PromptIR (32.49 dB) by 0.2 dB and PromptIR (32.06 dB) by 0.63 dB, with a notable 0.7 dB gain on deraining (Rain100L).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaIR outperforms all 25 compared baselines on the five-task all-in-one benchmark, with the largest margin on dehazing: 3.43 dB over InstructIR (30.53 vs. 27.10 dB on SOTS) and 3.74 dB over PromptIR (26.54 dB). On deraining (Rain100L), AdaIR leads PromptIR by 1.65 dB (38.02 vs. 36.37) and InstructIR by 1.18 dB (36.84). On denoising (BSD68, σ=25), AdaIR is competitive but slightly trails PromptIR (31.35 vs. 31.47 dB) and IDR (31.60 dB), while on deblurring (GoPro) it lags InstructIR by 1.28 dB (28.12 vs. 29.40) and GridFormer by 1.10 dB (29.22). On low-light enhancement (LOL-v1), AdaIR matches InstructIR at 23.00 dB PSNR but achieves a higher SSIM (0.845 vs. 0.836). In the three-task setting, AdaIR beats Art_PromptIR by 0.7 dB on deraining and 0.23 dB on dehazing, while maintaining comparable denoising performance (28.19 vs. 28.14 dB at σ=50). Generalization to unseen degradations (desnowing, defocus deblurring, raindrop removal) further confirms AdaIR's advantage, e.g., 20.54 dB on CSD desnowing vs. 20.47 for PromptIR and 19.32 for AirNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaIR builds directly on the Restormer U-shaped Transformer encoder-decoder as its backbone, inserting novel Adaptive Frequency Learning Blocks (AFLBs) between decoder levels. It departs from prior all-in-one restoration methods (AirNet, PromptIR, IDR, InstructIR) that operate exclusively in the spatial domain by introducing an explicit frequency-domain pipeline: an adaptive mask-guided FFT spectral decomposition (FMiM) followed by bidirectional spatial/channel attention modulation (FMoM). The H-L unit adapts CBAM's spatial attention, and the feature extraction step reuses the multi-dconv head transposed cross-attention from Restormer/CrossViT, but the key conceptual departure is using the raw degraded image's adaptively decoupled spectra to guide which frequency subbands to mine from intermediate features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The all-in-one restoration task is practically important for edge deployment where maintaining separate models per degradation is infeasible, and AdaIR's AFLB is designed as a lightweight plugin (2.64M params, 6.21 GFLOPs overhead) that can be dropped into existing restoration architectures. The frequency-domain framing provides a principled inductive bias—noise and rain contaminate high frequencies while haze and low-light affect low frequencies—making the model's degradation-agnostic behavior more interpretable. Open questions include whether the adaptive mask generalizes to degradation types with overlapping spectral signatures, whether the approach extends to video restoration, and whether the benefit persists at higher resolutions or with more than five degradation types.
