## Problem

Existing strategy-based jailbreak attacks rely on human-designed strategies, limiting their scope to the imagination of the designer, and typically employ only a single strategy at a time, leaving the potential for autonomously discovering, combining, and evolving diverse strategies largely unexplored. This paper asks whether an LLM agent can automatically discover jailbreak strategies from scratch, store them in a structured library, and iteratively evolve them without any human intervention or predefined candidate set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250368/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a malicious request text and interacts with the target LLM purely through its textual API (black-box), requiring no gradient or internal access. It orchestrates three LLMs in a loop—an attacker LLM that generates jailbreak prompts, a target (victim) LLM that produces responses, and a scorer LLM (Gemma-1.1-7B-it) that assigns a 1–10 compliance score—plus a summarizer LLM that extracts strategy descriptions. The primary outputs are the generated jailbreak prompt and a growing strategy library where each entry is a (key, value) pair: the key is a text-embedding vector of a prior response, and the value contains the strategy name, a concise definition, an example prompt, and the score differential that validated it.

## Method

AutoDAN-Turbo is a three-module lifelong-learning agent: (1) an Attack Generation and Exploration Module that iteratively generates prompts, collects target responses, and scores them; (2) a Strategy Library Construction Module that, by comparing consecutive attack logs where a higher score indicates an improvement, uses a summarizer LLM to extract and name the textual modification responsible for the score gain, storing it as a structured JSON entry; and (3) a Jailbreak Strategy Retrieval Module that embeds the current response and retrieves the top-k strategies with the highest score differentials via cosine similarity over response embeddings. The framework operates in two phases—a warm-up exploration stage that builds an initial library from scratch, followed by a running-time lifelong learning stage where the library is continuously augmented across multiple rounds over the full dataset. A key design choice is using the *response* embedding (not the prompt) as the retrieval key, so that strategies are matched to the type of refusal or partial compliance the target model exhibits. The library also supports plug-and-play injection of human-designed strategies, which then participate in the same retrieval-and-evolution loop.

## Results

On the Harmbench benchmark (400 malicious requests), AutoDAN-Turbo (Llama-3-70B attacker) achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 57.7 across 8 victim models, surpassing the runner-up Rainbow Teaming (33.1) by 74.3%. On the StrongREJECT metric, it scores 0.25 vs. 0.13 for Rainbow Teaming (+92.3%). Notably, AutoDAN-Turbo achieves 88.5% ASR on GPT-4-1106-turbo, and 93.4% when augmented with 7 human-designed strategies. In query efficiency, AutoDAN-Turbo requires only 6.72 average queries per successful jailbreak vs. 52.56 for PAIR and 51.70 for TAP (87% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoDAN-Turbo outperforms all five black-box baselines across every victim model. Against Rainbow Teaming (the strongest baseline, avg 33.1% ASR), AutoDAN-Turbo (Llama-3-70B) leads by 74.3% on average, with the largest gap on GPT-4-1106-turbo (88.5 vs. 51.7). TAP (avg 26.0%) and PAIR (avg 22.8%) are both substantially outperformed, with TAP particularly weak on Llama-2-7B (8.3 vs. 34.3). GCG-T (avg 18.6%) and PAP-top5 (avg 11.1%) trail even further, with PAP-top5 performing worst on GPT-4-1106-turbo (8.4 vs. 88.5). Compared to the original AutoDAN (avg 52.8% in Table 2), AutoDAN-Turbo improves by ~5 points on average, with the most dramatic gains on stronger models like GPT-4-1106-turbo (83.8–88.5 vs. 58.5 for TAP-T, the closest prior method in Table 2). The method also reduces query cost by 87% relative to PAIR and TAP while achieving higher ASR.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoDAN-Turbo sits at the intersection of two existing lines of jailbreak research: optimization-based attacks (GCG, PAIR, TAP) that iteratively refine prompts via feedback signals, and strategy-based attacks (DAN, Rainbow Teaming, PAP) that rely on human-designed jailbreak schemes. It departs from both by replacing human strategy design with an autonomous lifelong-learning agent that discovers, stores, and combines strategies from scratch, and by replacing gradient/loss-based optimization with embedding-based retrieval over a growing strategy library. The closest prior work is Rainbow Teaming (8 predefined strategies) and PAP (40 persuasion schemes), but AutoDAN-Turbo removes the fixed strategy set entirely and adds a retrieval-augmented, self-evolving loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 88.5% ASR on GPT-4-1106-turbo and the <4% ASR drop when transferring the strategy library across unseen datasets make this a practically significant red-teaming tool for LLM safety evaluation. The plug-and-play compatibility with human-designed strategies and the 87% query-efficiency gain over PAIR/TAP suggest the strategy library is a reusable, transferable asset rather than a one-off artifact. Key open questions include whether defenders can mirror the lifelong-learning loop to continuously patch discovered vulnerabilities, and whether the high computational cost of building the library from scratch limits real-world deployment. The paper acknowledges the multi-LLM loading overhead as a practical limitation.
