## Problem

Existing online time series forecasting (OTSF) frameworks suffer from information leakage, where models are evaluated on time steps already used in backpropagation, inflating performance estimates. Additionally, the standard OTSF setting only forecasts one truly future step while the remaining output positions correspond to already-observed data, misaligning with the practical goal of predicting unknown future values. This paper redefines the OTSF setting to eliminate leakage and addresses the resulting challenge of training with incomplete ground truth sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251029/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a multivariate time series window of length L (default 96) ending at the current timestamp and produces H future steps (H ∈ {1, 24, 48, 96}). A critical constraint distinguishes this from prior OTSF: at time t=i, the model may only be updated with ground truth up to t=i+1, and the full prediction sequence is evaluated only at t≥i+H when all H ground truth values are available. Datasets span 7–862 features (Electricity, ETTh2, ETTm1, Exchange, Traffic, Weather) with 7,396–57,600 timesteps, split 20%/5%/75% for train/validation/test.

## Method

DSOF employs a teacher-student architecture with residual learning: a robust backbone (e.g., DLinear, PatchTST, FSNet) generates coarse predictions, and a lightweight 3-layer MLP student refines them by learning the residual error. Two data streams update the model: a slow stream uses experience replay with a FIFO buffer of complete input-output pairs (waiting L+H steps for full ground truth) to stabilize training and prevent overfitting to noise, while a fast stream applies a temporal-difference-inspired loss that constructs pseudo-labels from the most recent ground truth point(s) plus teacher predictions for remaining steps, weighted by a geometric decay factor γ to down-weight uncertain distant predictions. During fast-stream updates, the teacher is frozen and only the student is updated, balancing rapid adaptation to distribution shifts with training stability. The framework is model-agnostic and can be applied to any forecasting backbone.

## Results

DSOF consistently outperforms batch learning across all six datasets (ECL, ETTh2, ETTm1, Exchange, Traffic, Weather) and all seven backbone architectures at horizons H=1, 24, and 48. The most dramatic gains appear on ECL, where FSNet as teacher reduces MSE from 380 to 2.33 (H=1) and from 480 to 7.0 (H=48), and NSTransformer reduces MSE from 39 to 2.70 (H=1), representing over 50% MSE reduction. Against other online frameworks (DGrad, TFCL, DER++) in the leakage-free setting, DSOF achieves the lowest or second-lowest MSE in nearly all configurations, with a notable 15%+ MSE reduction on ECL at H=48 regardless of teacher model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against batch learning baselines, DSOF wins in the vast majority of configurations: DLinear batch MSE of 2.842 on ECL (H=1) drops to 2.065 with DSOF, FSNet batch MSE of 380 drops to 2.33, and NSTrans batch MSE of 39 drops to 2.70, all representing >50% reductions. FITS, OneNet, and iTransformer as batch baselines are similarly outperformed by DSOF across all datasets. In the online-framework comparison (Table 3), DSOF consistently beats DGrad (the leakage-free version of FSNet/OneNet online training), TFCL, and DER++: on ECL at H=48 with DLinear, DSOF achieves 6.181 vs. DGrad's 10.0, TFCL's 9.746, and DER++'s 7.339. The one exception is Traffic at H=1 with DLinear, where DSOF (0.302) ties batch learning exactly, and in a few ETTh2 H=48 configurations DSOF (3.082) is marginally worse than DSOF without the student model (3.015), though both still beat all other online baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper redefines the online time series forecasting (OTSF) setting by identifying and eliminating information leakage present in all prior OTSF formulations (Pham et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023), where models were evaluated on time steps already used for backpropagation. Methodologically, it repurposes experience replay (Rolnick et al., 2019) as a slow stream with a FIFO buffer for stable generalization, and adapts temporal difference learning (Sutton & Barto, 1999) as a fast stream using pseudo-labels from a frozen teacher model for immediate adaptation. The teacher-student residual learning architecture is model-agnostic, wrapping existing backbones (DLinear, PatchTST, FSNet, etc.) with a lightweight MLP student, distinguishing it from prior OTSF methods that modify the backbone itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The information leakage finding is a methodological correction that affects the validity of all prior OTSF evaluations, making this redefinition important for the subfield's credibility. However, the framework's practical adoption is limited by notably higher computational cost (2–6× slower than baselines per iteration) and sensitivity to the TD regularization parameter γ. Open questions include whether more advanced semi-supervised pseudo-label generation, improved ER strategies, or architectures specifically designed for online forecasting could close the efficiency gap while maintaining the accuracy gains.
