## Problem

Existing text-to-audio diffusion models generate mono audio without spatial control, and two-stage pipelines that convert mono to stereo via ITD/ILD filters suffer from error accumulation and high computational cost. The field also lacks a large-scale stereo audio dataset with explicit spatial descriptions (direction, movement, multi-source) and appropriate evaluation metrics for spatial fidelity, making controllable spatial audio generation from text or images an unsolved problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251528/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts containing spatial descriptions (e.g., 'a motorcycle moving from front to left'), RGB images from COCO-2017, or interactive bounding boxes/click points, and produces 10-second dual-channel stereo audio at 16 kHz. A key unusual input is the azimuth state matrix S ∈ R^{K×L_azi×d_time}, which encodes the angular position of each of K sound sources across time slots; during training this is derived from simulation ground truth, while at inference it is inducted by GPT from the text or image. The image encoder operates at the regional level (N detected objects) rather than the global level, producing per-region embeddings that are concatenated with coordinates and class IDs before feeding into the diffusion backbone.

## Method

SpatialSonic is a one-stage latent diffusion model built on Stable Audio Open's autoencoder and DiT backbone, fine-tuned on the newly constructed BEWO-1M dataset (1M+ simulated stereo clips with GPT-4-assisted spatial captions). The core architectural contribution is an Azimuth Fusion Module (AFM) that cross-attends the azimuth state matrix against the text or image embedding, injecting explicit spatial guidance that neither modality alone provides. The azimuth matrix comes in two forms: a coarse Gaussian distribution N(μ(t), σ²) over azimuth bins (suitable for T2A where exact angles are ambiguous) and a fine one-hot discrete matrix (suitable for I2A where object positions are known). For image conditioning, a Mask-RCNN detector extracts regional features that are combined with CLIP embeddings and pre-trained in a shared encoder-decoder space with T5, enabling the image encoder to produce spatially-aware regional representations aligned with the text encoder. Training uses v-prediction parameterization with a cosine noise schedule and classifier-free guidance (scale 6.0), optimized with AdamW at lr 2e-5 over 500K steps on 8× RTX 4090 GPUs.

## Results

On the 1-C AudioCaps test set, SpatialSonic achieves the best Inception Score (13.79) and competitive CLAP (0.672) and FD (14.03) among all T2A baselines. On the core 2-C spatial audio evaluation (BEWO-1M test set), SpatialSonic reduces FSAD to 0.17–0.31 across subsets (SS, SD, DS, M, RW), compared to 0.53–2.48 for the strongest baseline (Stable Audio Open), and lowers CRW MSE by up to 70% (e.g., 13.75 vs 36.30 on DS-set). For I2A generation, SpatialSonic achieves FSAD of 0.52 versus 2.08 (S&H†) and 2.49 (See2Sound), with MOS-Direction of 3.79. The paper reports an overall 70% reduction in ITD error relative to popular models with minor adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialSonic consistently outperforms all three T2A baselines (AudioLDM2†, Make-An-Audio2†, Stable Audio Open) on the spatial metrics FSAD and CRW MSE across every BEWO-1M subset, with the largest gains on the multi-source DS-set (FSAD 0.31 vs. 0.87 for Stable Audio Open; CRW MSE 13.75 vs. 36.30). On GCC MSE, SpatialSonic leads on SS-set (27.20 vs. 38.73) and DS-set (22.51 vs. 32.63) but is slightly behind Stable Audio Open on SD-set (44.36 vs. 45.76) and M-set (33.32 vs. 34.20), which the authors attribute to the global-average nature of GCC in complex moving-source scenarios. For I2A, SpatialSonic achieves FSAD 0.52 versus 2.08 (S&H†) and 2.49 (See2Sound), a 1.56-point improvement over the best prior method, while also leading on CRW MSE (57.37 vs. 62.55/60.73) and MOS-Direction (3.79 vs. 3.47/3.17). In the interactive I2A setting, SpatialSonic wins on both MOS-Events and MOS-Direction for both BBox and Point prompts, and achieves substantially higher direction clarity (CRW MA 17.32–18.11 vs. 7.11–10.37 for baselines). On the 1-C AudioCaps benchmark, SpatialSonic is competitive but not uniformly best: it leads on IS (13.79) and FD (14.03) but trails TANGO2 on KL (1.37 vs. 1.12) and CLAP (0.672 vs. 0.675), indicating that the spatial guidance does not degrade mono quality but does not fully dominate on all standard audio metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Audio Open (Evans et al., 2024b) as its diffusion backbone but departs fundamentally from the two-stage paradigm that dominates spatial audio work—where a mono audio generator is cascaded with a mono-to-stereo filter or room simulator (Zhou et al., 2020; Parida et al., 2022; Dagli et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an explicit azimuth state matrix as a continuous spatial guidance signal fed into the diffusion process, drawing inspiration from layout-controllable image generation (Inoue et al., 2023; Qu et al., 2023) but adapted to the temporal-angular structure of sound. The regional image perception module repurposes Mask-RCNN and CLIP features in a detection-to-captioning pre-training loop, extending the global-semantic alignment of CLIP-based I2A models (Sheffer & Adi, 2023; Xing et al., 2024) to positional and relational context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEWO-1M is the first large-scale (1M+ samples, 2.8k hours) stereo audio dataset with explicit spatial captions, filling a critical data gap that has constrained spatial audio research to small, manually-annotated corpora. The azimuth state matrix provides a generalizable mechanism for encoding arbitrary source trajectories, potentially applicable beyond this specific architecture. However, the evaluation relies heavily on simulated ground truth (Pyroomacoustics/gpuRIR), the real-world subset is only 200 samples, and the image encoder's limited size restricts open-world generalization. Open questions include whether the simulation-to-real gap can be closed with fine-tuning, whether the approach extends to 5.1-channel or higher-order ambisonics, and whether the coarse-vs-fine guidance trade-off holds at larger model scales.
