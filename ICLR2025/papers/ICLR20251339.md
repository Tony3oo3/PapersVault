## Problem

Existing unsupervised curve-adjustment methods for low-light image enhancement (e.g., ZeroDCE, ReLLIE) rely on discrete iterative updates with a fixed number of steps, which cannot guarantee convergence of the optimization and makes performance sensitive to the chosen iteration count. This paper addresses the gap by reformulating the iterative curve-adjustment process as a continuous dynamical system, enabling guaranteed convergence and input-specific adaptation of the number of update steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251339/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-light RGB image with pixel values normalized to [0, 1] and produces a well-exposed enhanced image of the same spatial dimensions. During inference, the adaptive dopri5 ODE solver determines an input-specific number of integration steps (up to 30) and terminates early when the solution error falls within a tolerance of 1e-5. Users can further adjust the final integration state T (from −(T+Δt) to +(T+Δt)) to control the output exposure level, including producing images darker than the input. A final Noise Removal pass is applied to suppress noise amplified during enhancement.

## Method

CLODE reformulates the standard pixel-wise curve-adjustment update I_{n+1} = I_n + A_n ⊗ I_n ⊗ (1−I_n) as a Neural ODE dI_t/dt = f_θ(I_t, t), where the ODE function f_θ computes the continuous dynamics of the latent image. The ODEfunc consists of a lightweight 3-layer CNN Noise Removal module (trained with a Zero-shot Noise2Noise loss) that denoises the current state, followed by a Curve Parameter Estimation module (a shallow two-branch CNN with layer normalization and time-conditioned convolutions) that predicts the pixel-wise curve parameter map A_t. The system is solved with the adaptive Dormand-Prince 5th-order Runge-Kutta (dopri5) solver, which dynamically adjusts step size and terminates when the solution converges within tolerance, replacing the fixed iteration count of prior methods. Training is fully unsupervised using five zero-reference losses: spatial consistency, exposure, color constancy (Gray-World), parameter regularization, and the self-supervised noise removal loss, all accumulated across the continuous integration trajectory.

## Results

On the LSRW and LOL benchmark datasets, CLODE (unsupervised) achieves 17.28/19.61 dB PSNR and 0.533/0.718 SSIM, outperforming the best prior unsupervised method PairLIE (16.97/19.51 dB) and ZeroDCE (14.50/16.49 dB). With user-controlled final state (CLODE†), the method reaches 20.77/23.58 dB PSNR, surpassing the best supervised method RetinexFormer (17.76/25.15 dB) by 0.73 dB in average PSNR (22.18 vs. 21.45). On SICE Part2 with varied exposure conditions, CLODE† achieves the best PSNR (16.18) and SSIM (0.707) among all compared methods, including supervised approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLODE outperforms all unsupervised baselines across both LSRW and LOL: it gains +2.78 dB PSNR over ZeroDCE (17.28 vs. 14.50 on LSRW), +0.31 dB over PairLIE (17.28 vs. 16.97), and +2.44 dB over Night-Enhancement (17.28 vs. 14.24). With user-controlled integration (CLODE†), the method surpasses the strongest supervised baselines—RetinexFormer (21.45 dB avg), LLFlow (19.34 dB), and GSAD (20.19 dB)—achieving 22.18 dB average PSNR without any paired ground-truth data. On SICE Part2 (varied exposure), CLODE† leads all methods in PSNR (16.18) and SSIM (0.707), beating supervised RetinexFormer (19.12 PSNR on SICE-trained variant) and unsupervised ZeroDCE (12.67). The ablation in Table 4 confirms the NODE reformulation is the key driver: continuous adaptive stepping (≤30 steps, dopri5) yields 17.28/19.61 dB PSNR versus 12.87–16.12 dB for fixed discrete steps of 1–30 iterations. CLODE also achieves the best PI (Perceptual Index) scores among all methods including supervised ones (2.914 on LOL vs. 3.102 for RetinexFormer), and the lowest color-matching histogram loss (0.3848 for CLODE†), indicating superior color fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLODE reformulates the iterative pixel-wise curve-adjustment paradigm established by ZeroDCE (Guo et al., 2020) and ReLLIE (Zhang et al., 2021) as a Neural Ordinary Differential Equation, drawing on the NODE framework of Chen et al. (2018) and the original S-curve formulation of Yuan & Sun (2012). The key conceptual departure is replacing fixed-step discrete updates with an adaptive continuous-time integration (dopri5 solver), which guarantees convergence within a tolerance rather than relying on empirically chosen iteration counts. The Noise Removal module borrows the Zeroshot-N2N loss (Mansour & Heckel, 2023) to train a lightweight denoiser concurrently with the enhancement process, a self-supervised strategy not previously integrated into curve-adjustment pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing convergence guarantees and input-specific adaptive step sizes, CLODE addresses a fundamental limitation of all prior discrete curve-adjustment methods, and the resulting unsupervised performance (CLODE†) surpasses state-of-the-art supervised methods by 0.73 dB average PSNR on LSRW/LOL. The user-controllable integration interval offers a practical advantage for exposure tuning without retraining. Open questions include whether stiff ODE solvers could reduce inference cost for normal-exposed images (where the dynamics are stiff), whether dynamically selecting solvers based on input conditions would improve efficiency, and whether the NODE reformulation generalizes to other iterative image restoration tasks beyond exposure adjustment.
