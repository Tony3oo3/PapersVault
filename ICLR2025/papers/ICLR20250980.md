## Problem

In Federated Prompt Learning for multimodal LLMs, balancing personalization, generalization, and privacy remains unsolved: over-personalization causes local overfitting, while differential privacy degrades both personalization and generalization under non-IID data. Existing FPL methods either ignore privacy entirely or apply it without accounting for the personalization-generalization tradeoff, and prior private multi-task learning approaches are designed for full model training and cannot be directly applied to the prompt parameter space.

## Input / Output

Each client feeds RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, variable resolution for other datasets) through a frozen CLIP model (ViT-B16 or ResNet50 backbone) alongside a learned soft prompt of 16 tokens at dimension 512, appended to class label text. The output is a probability distribution over C classes computed via cosine similarity between the prompt-conditioned text embedding and the image embedding, scaled by a temperature parameter. The trained prompt (global + local components) is the distributable artifact, subject to membership inference attacks if unprotected.

## Method

DP-FPL decomposes each client's prompt into a global prompt (aggregated at the server) and a local prompt (retained client-side), then factorizes the local prompt at every training iteration into two low-rank components u_i, v_i plus a residual r_i = p_{L,i} − u_i v_i using a single-step power method. LDP Gaussian noise is injected only into the low-rank components (reducing the noise dimensionality versus full-rank perturbation), while GDP noise is applied to the server-aggregated global prompt gradient. The residual term, used in the forward pass but excluded from gradient recomputation, compensates for expressiveness lost during factorization and acts as a counterbalance to the regularization-like error introduced by low-rank constraints and DP noise. A privacy analysis via the advanced composition theorem yields explicit bounds on σ_L and σ_G in terms of sensitivity, number of rounds T, and target (ε,δ) guarantees.

## Results

On local classes (personalization), DP-FPL achieves 92.71% on Caltech101, 85.25% on OxfordPets, 72.11% on OxfordFlowers, and 80.57% on Food101 at ε=0.1, consistently outperforming all baselines by 5–34 percentage points. On neighbor classes (generalization), DP-FPL reaches 90.02% on Caltech101 and 77.12% on OxfordPets at ε=0.1, again leading all competitors. On CIFAR-100 with 25 clients under Dirichlet split (α=0.3), DP-FPL achieves 59.53% at ε=0.1 versus 55.76% (FedPGP), 50.86% (PromptFL), and 46.95% (FedOTP). Under the strictest privacy setting (ε=0.01), DP-FPL still maintains 85.21% on Caltech101 local classes, while baselines drop to 77–79%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-FPL is compared against three baselines: PromptFL (single shared prompt, no personalization), FedOTP (full-rank global + local prompts via optimal transport), and FedPGP (low-rank factorization of local prompt, factorized once at initialization). Under any DP noise level (ε ≤ 0.4), DP-FPL dominates all three baselines on every dataset for both local and neighbor class accuracy. The margin is most dramatic on OxfordPets at ε=0.1, where DP-FPL achieves 85.25% local accuracy versus 72.77% (PromptFL), 59.54% (FedOTP), and 63.21% (FedPGP) — a gap of 12–26 points. FedPGP, the closest competitor (also using low-rank factorization), degrades sharply under strict privacy because it factorizes only once and lacks the residual term; at ε=0.01 on OxfordFlowers it drops to 35.23% while DP-FPL retains 51.55%. In the non-private setting, FedOTP occasionally leads on local classes (e.g., 99.63% on OxfordPets), but DP-FPL is competitive (96.91%) and pulls ahead as soon as any noise is introduced. On CIFAR-100 with 50 clients and Dirichlet split, DP-FPL leads by 4–12 points over all baselines at ε=0.1 (60.49% vs. 53.39% FedPGP, 53.17% PromptFL, 57.05% FedOTP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the personalized federated prompt learning (FPL) line of work by integrating differential privacy into the prompt learning pipeline. It builds directly on the Reparametrized Gradient Perturbation (RGP) low-rank factorization scheme from Yu et al. (2021) and the global/local prompt decomposition with low-rank factorization from Cui et al. (2024, FedPGP), but departs from both in two key ways: performing factorization at every training iteration rather than once at initialization, and introducing a residual term to preserve expressiveness lost during factorization. The selective application of LDP to low-rank components and GDP to the global prompt is a novel privacy mechanism design that differs from prior work (e.g., Zhao et al. 2023) that applies noise uniformly to the entire prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important trilemma—balancing personalization, generalization, and privacy—in the increasingly relevant setting of deploying multimodal LLMs via federated prompt learning with a prompt-as-a-service paradigm. The finding that moderate DP noise can act as beneficial regularization for generalization, and that the residual term is critical under strict privacy, provides actionable design guidance. However, the theoretical analysis of the residual term is left as future work, the MIA evaluation uses a relatively standard shadow-model approach, and experiments are limited to image classification with CLIP/ResNet backbones, leaving open whether the approach transfers to other modalities or larger foundation models.
