## Problem

Exact machine unlearning systems like SISA guarantee complete removal of deleted data influence but require retraining affected model components, causing production downtime and service failure. In the worst case, deletion requests hitting the first slice of every shard force a full retrain from scratch. The paper addresses how to perform exact unlearning without any retraining at deletion time while maintaining model performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253054/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a pre-trained model (ViT, RoBERTa, Llama2/3), a private fine-tuning dataset partitioned into m shards of L slices each, and a training budget B specifying how many slice-sequence permutations to train per shard. At inference, it accepts standard image or text inputs and produces task predictions via an ensemble of the best-performing model per shard. At deletion time, it accepts a data instance identifier and outputs the same model checkpoint with specific LoRA layers zeroed out—no retraining or checkpoint swapping is required, only a layer deactivation flag.

## Method

S³T introduces sequential slice-wise LoRA training where the i-th LoRA layer (from the top) is trained on the cumulative data of slices {1,…,i} while all other LoRA layers remain frozen, creating strict parameter isolation between slices. To handle deletions affecting early slices, the framework trains B different models per shard using diverse permutations of the same L slices, selected via iterative cyclic rotation (uniform prior) or a bipartite-matching-based algorithm (BMS) that maximizes expected surviving slices under non-uniform deletion probabilities. Upon a deletion request for slice i, the system simply deactivates LoRA parameters for layers {i,…,L} in the affected shard's models, reducing storage by a factor of L compared to SISA's checkpoint approach. Theoretically, S³T achieves a deletion rate of O(mL log(m·min(B,L))) versus SISA's O(mL log m), and a performance-retention probability gap of ζ(1−ζ^{B′−1}) over SISA, where B′=min(B, L!/(L−k)!).

## Results

S³T achieves performance comparable to full fine-tuning (FT) across all evaluated settings: on LLM instruction tuning (Table 1), S³T matches or slightly exceeds FT on most benchmarks (e.g., Llama2-7B S³T scores 43.6 on MMLU vs. 43.3 for FT; Llama3-8B S³T scores 82.4 on PIQA vs. 80.9 for FT). For deletion performance, SISA and ProtoSISA fail after approximately 40 deletion requests, while S³T (B=4) continues functioning with high performance and handles significantly more requests. S³T reduces total deletion time by 2.8× compared to SISA and 25× compared to full retraining after 1000 requests, and achieves up to 2.3× and 3× deletion rate gains over SISA when varying shard and slice counts respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SISA (Bourtoule et al., 2021), S³T achieves a 1.6× improvement in deletion rate with a modest budget (B=8, m=5 shards, L=32 slices), and up to 2.3× and 3× gains when varying shard and slice counts respectively. SISA and ProtoSISA (Yan et al., 2022) both fail after approximately 40 deletion requests on CIFAR-10/100 (m=5, L=4), while S³T with B=4 continues to function with high performance well beyond that point. In terms of deletion time, S³T reduces total time by 2.8× compared to SISA and 25× compared to full retraining after 1000 requests. Against sequential training (Kumar et al., 2023), S³T achieves similar or superior performance on CIFAR-10/100 while training L times fewer parameters per slice, demonstrating that the parameter isolation strategy does not sacrifice model quality. S³T with B=4 achieves performance very close to the full budget B=24, indicating diminishing returns beyond L sequences for deletion rate (consistent with Lemma 1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SISA (Sharded, Isolated, Sliced, and Aggregated) exact unlearning framework of Bourtoule et al. (2021), which partitions data into shards and slices and retrains only affected components upon deletion. The key departure is the introduction of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) with a sequential slice-wise training strategy that enables parameter isolation at the layer level, allowing unlearning via layer deactivation rather than checkpoint swapping. The sequence-aware training (multiple slice permutations) and the bipartite-matching-based sequence selection algorithm (BMS) are novel contributions that go beyond prior SISA variants like ProtoSISA (Yan et al., 2022) or adapter-based retraining (Kumar et al., 2023), which modify SISA components without changing its fundamental operating logic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a critical practical gap: production ML systems cannot tolerate downtime even for single-component retraining, and SISA's worst-case failure (all first slices deleted) forces full retraining. The theoretical result that only L sequences (not L!) are needed for optimal deletion rate provides a clean, actionable design principle. Limitations include the assumption that deletions affect only fine-tuning data (not pre-training), the additional offline training cost that scales with budget B, and the reliance on PEFT methods that may struggle with very small data slices. Open questions include finer-grained parameter isolation, sample-efficient PEFT for small slices, and defending against adversarial deletion requests designed to degrade model performance.
