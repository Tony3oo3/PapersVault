## Problem

Pretrained multimodal models for egocentric video recognition assume all modalities (audio, video) are available at test time, but real-world deployments frequently encounter missing modalities due to privacy redaction, hardware limitations, or cost constraints. Existing solutions to handle missing modalities require expensive retraining of the entire model, rendering pretrained checkpoints obsolete and making them impractical for large-scale or resource-constrained settings. This paper asks whether a pretrained multimodal model can be adapted at test time—using only unlabeled incoming data—to remain robust when modalities are missing, without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous online stream of unlabeled audio-visual egocentric video clips where each sample may contain audio only, video only, or both; missing modality channels are zero-padded to maintain a fixed input dimensionality. At each time step the model must emit an action class prediction before the next sample arrives, and parameter updates are triggered only when a modality-complete (audio-visual) sample is encountered. The output is the predicted label plus updated normalization-layer parameters that render the model's predictions invariant to which modality subset is present.

## Method

MiDl (Mutual information with self-Distillation) is a self-supervised, online test-time adaptation method that updates only the learnable parameters of normalization layers in a pretrained multimodal model. The objective combines two terms: (1) mutual information minimization between the model's output distribution and the available modality type, which drives the prediction to be invariant to whether audio, video, or both are present, and (2) KL-divergence self-distillation between the adapted model and the frozen original model on complete-modality inputs, which preserves accuracy when all modalities are available. The MI term is decomposed into the average entropy of per-modality predictions minus the entropy of the modality-averaged prediction, requiring three forward passes (audio-only, video-only, audio-visual) plus one pass through the original model per adaptation step. Adaptation is performed exclusively on modality-complete samples because the MI loss vanishes for incomplete inputs; for incomplete samples the most recently adapted model predicts without further updates. The method is agnostic to backbone architecture (MBT, vanilla self-attention), pretraining strategy (masked autoencoder, Omnivore), dataset, and which specific modality is missing.

## Results

On Epic-Kitchens, MiDl achieves 52.4% action accuracy at 50% missing rate and 46.4% at 75% missing rate, improving over the non-adapted MBT baseline (46.8% and 37.9%) by 5.6% and 8.5% respectively. On Epic-Sounds, MiDl reaches 38.8% and 29.8% at 50% and 75% missing rates, outperforming the baseline (37.1% and 28.3%). Critically, MiDl preserves the full-modality performance (63.7% on Epic-Kitchens, 55.0% on Epic-Sounds at 0% missing), while under Long-Term Adaptation it further boosts 100%-missing accuracy to 41.4% on Epic-Kitchens (+11.9% over baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiDl consistently outperforms all three TTA baselines (Shot, Tent, ETA) at every missing rate above 0% on both datasets. On Epic-Kitchens at 75% missing, MiDl achieves 46.4% vs. Shot's 40.6%, Tent's 24.2%, and ETA's 20.6%—a 5.8-point margin over the closest competitor (Shot) and a 22.2-point margin over Tent. Tent and ETA actually degrade below the non-adapted baseline at high missing rates (e.g., Tent drops to 9.9% and ETA to 7.9% at 100% missing on Epic-Kitchens), whereas MiDl maintains the baseline's 29.5% floor. At 0% missing rate, MiDl matches the baseline (63.7% vs. 63.9%), confirming that the KL self-distillation term prevents performance loss when all modalities are present. The paper attributes the baselines' failure to their design for simple covariate shifts (weather, illumination) rather than the structural domain shift caused by an entire modality being absent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the missing modality problem—previously addressed exclusively through training-time strategies (architectural modifications, learnable tokens, regularizers)—as a test-time adaptation (TTA) problem, building on the online TTA evaluation protocol from Alfarra et al. (2023). It departs from prior TTA methods (TENT, SHOT, ETA), which target covariate shifts like illumination or weather changes, by introducing a modality-specific domain shift and a dedicated loss (mutual information minimization plus self-distillation) that encourages prediction invariance to the available modality source. The approach is architecturally agnostic and requires no access to training data, distinguishing it from all prior missing-modality methods that necessitate full retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MiDl offers a practical, retraining-free solution for real-world egocentric video deployments where modalities are intermittently unavailable due to privacy, cost, or hardware constraints—a scenario increasingly common with wearable devices. Key limitations include validation only on audiovisual egocentric datasets (Epic-Kitchens, Epic-Sounds), a 3× forward-pass overhead per adaptation step, and the requirement that some modality-complete samples appear in the stream (p_AV ≠ 0) for adaptation to occur. Open questions include generalization to other modality combinations (e.g., IMU, text), other domains beyond egocentric video, and whether the approach can be extended to fully unimodal streams where no complete-modality samples exist.
