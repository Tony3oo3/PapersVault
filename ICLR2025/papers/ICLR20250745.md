## Problem

SAM produces high-quality pixel patches but provides no semantic labels, and existing adaptations either delegate detection to external models (undermining SAM's foundation-model role) or label patches without resolving over-segmentation where a single instance is split into multiple sub-patches. The paper asks how to equip SAM with both semantic and instance-level recognition in a single, unified framework that handles open and closed domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250745/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image, a set of text class labels, and pre-generated SAM patches (binary masks from segment-everything mode with 48 grid points per axis, 0.8 IoU threshold, 0.9 stability score). SAM patches are fixed and never refined by regression or segmentation heads; all computation operates at the patch level. The output is an M×N affinity matrix (queries × patches) from which semantic masks, instance masks, and panoptic masks are directly read off. For open-vocabulary inference, a frozen CLIP image encoder (ConvNeXt-L + FPN) supplements the learned classifier via a geometric average of scores (κ=0.4).

## Method

SAM-CP defines two composable prompts—Type-I (does a patch match a text label?) and Type-II (do two same-class patches belong to the same instance?)—and implements them through a unified affinity framework. Semantic queries are derived from CLIP text embeddings and instance queries are initialized from patch visual features (patch-as-query); both attend to SAM patch features as keys via six layers of dynamic cross-attention (DCA) where the affinity matrix A serves as a binary mask, affinity refinement (AR) updates A via cosine similarity, and query enhancement (QE) fuses RoI features of high-affinity regions back into the query. Patches are merged on-the-fly during affinity propagation, eliminating the O(N²) pairwise comparison and post-processing conflicts of a naive pipeline. Training uses focal loss for classification, mask focal loss plus Dice loss for instance masks, and Hungarian matching (with cls, mfl, dice, bbox, and gIoU costs) to assign instance queries to ground-truth instances.

## Results

On open-vocabulary segmentation (COCO→ADE20K), SAM-CP achieves 27.2 PQ, 77.7 SQ, 32.9 RQ, 17.0 AP, and 31.8 mIoU, surpassing the previous SOTA FCCLIP (26.8 PQ, 16.8 AP) and FrozenSeg (25.9 PQ, 16.4 AP). On ADE20K→COCO, it reaches 28.6 PQ and 21.9 AP, and on COCO→Cityscapes it achieves 41.0 PQ, 29.3 AP, and 47.9 mIoU. In closed-domain COCO-Panoptic with Swin-L, SAM-CP reports 52.7 PQ, 45.2 AP, and 61.8 mIoU, which is competitive but below Mask2Former (57.8 PQ, 48.6 AP, 67.4 mIoU) due to SAM's inherent patch-quality limitations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In open-vocabulary segmentation, SAM-CP outperforms all 11 baselines in Table 1 on PQ and AP across all three cross-dataset settings. The most notable gains are over FCCLIP (27.2 vs. 26.8 PQ, 17.0 vs. 16.8 AP on COCO→ADE20K) and FrozenSeg (27.2 vs. 25.9 PQ, 17.0 vs. 16.4 AP), the two strongest prior methods. SAM-CP also achieves the best SQ (77.7 vs. FCCLIP's 71.5) and RQ (32.9 vs. FCCLIP's 32.3), indicating better segmentation quality and recall. However, SAM-CP trails FCCLIP on mIoU (31.8 vs. 34.1 on COCO→ADE20K) and FrozenSeg on mIoU (31.8 vs. 34.4), suggesting its semantic segmentation is slightly weaker than its instance/panoptic performance. In closed-domain COCO (Table 2), SAM-CP with Swin-L (52.7 PQ, 45.2 AP, 61.8 mIoU) is competitive with Mask2Former (57.8 PQ, 48.6 AP, 67.4 mIoU) but falls short on all three metrics, and it also trails Mask DINO (58.3 PQ, 50.6 AP, 67.3 mIoU). The paper attributes this gap to SAM's higher missing rate (16.7% vs. 10.1% for Mask DINO at IoU>0.5), meaning SAM fails to generate patches for some objects that SAM-CP cannot recover. In the supplementary SAM-based comparison (Table 13), SAM-CP (41.7 mAP, 59.4 AP50) outperforms OVSAM (35.8–36.7 mAP) and Prompt Segment Anything (41.5 mAP) without requiring a separate pre-trained detector.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM-CP positions itself against two existing lines of SAM adaptation: (1) pipeline methods like Grounded-SAM that delegate semantic understanding to a separate detector and use SAM only for mask refinement, and (2) label-assignment methods (SSAM, Semantic-SAM, SAM-CLIP) that classify individual SAM patches but struggle with over-segmentation. The paper departs from both by introducing two composable prompts (semantic alignment and instance merging) unified into a single DETR-style affinity propagation decoder, where CLIP text embeddings serve as semantic queries and patch features serve as both instance queries and keys. This unification draws on DETR's query mechanism, GLIP's vision-language scoring, and FCCLIP's frozen-CLIP inference, but the key architectural departure is treating semantic and instance segmentation as two query types within one forward pass rather than sequential stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The composable-prompt framing offers a clean, modular recipe for equipping any patch-generating foundation model with multi-grained semantic perception, and the demonstrated swappability (SAM → SAM2 with zero retraining) validates this modularity. However, the method's ceiling is fundamentally bounded by SAM's patch quality: the paper itself shows SAM's 16.7% missing rate (vs. Mask DINO's 10.1%) directly limits closed-domain accuracy, and inference speed is coupled to SAM's latency. Open questions include whether the affinity framework can incorporate learned patch refinement (beyond the ad-hoc Mask DINO proposal addition), whether the composable-prompt idea extends to video or 3D foundation models, and whether the modest open-vocabulary gains over FCCLIP (~0.4 PQ) will widen as SAM's successor models improve patch recall.
