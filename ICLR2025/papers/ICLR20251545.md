## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods that adjust the loss function either require retain data (which may be unavailable in practice) or a reference LLM (incurring additional training cost) to calibrate responses and preserve model utility. Moreover, leveraging explicit retain data or implicit knowledge from a reference model during fine-tuning blurs the boundary between forget and retain data, creating an inherent trade-off between unlearning quality and overall model performance. This paper asks whether effective unlearning can be achieved using only the forget data itself, without any external calibration signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only forget data (prompt-response pairs from the target unlearning set) and a set of template/example responses (e.g., a reject-based 'I don't know' answer or an irrelevant response devoid of the unlearning target), which can be generated by an open-source LLM or hand-defined. It produces updated LLM parameters that, when queried with forget prompts, generate the template response rather than the original forget content. No retain dataset, reference model, or data-mixing strategy is required at any point during training.

## Method

FLAT formulates unlearning as maximizing the f-divergence between the distribution of template responses and the distribution of forget responses, both conditioned on the forget prompts. Via Fenchel duality, the variational form of this f-divergence yields a per-sample loss that assigns distinct activation functions g* and f* to the template and forget terms, providing theoretically optimal importance weights that balance what to learn (template) versus what to forget. Empirically, the loss operates on the average next-token probability (not log-probability) across all tokens in each response, with the f-divergence-specific conjugate functions (e.g., tanh/2 for TV, identity for KL) shaping the gradient signal. This distinguishes FLAT from DPO in three concrete ways: the re-weighting activation function, averaging rather than summing token-level losses, and omitting the logarithm on output probabilities. A convergence theorem (Theorem 3.4) establishes that the empirical f-divergence estimator achieves the optimal non-parametric rate N^{-1/(r_Φ+2)} toward the true distributional divergence under bounded density ratio and regularity assumptions.

## Results

On the TOFU entity unlearning benchmark (Llama2-7B), FLAT(KL) achieves the best Model Utility (MU = 0.6393) while attaining a Forget Quality (FQ) p-value of 0.0286, outperforming all baselines on the utility metric. On the Harry Potter copyrighted content task (OPT-2.7B), FLAT(KL) achieves a Forget Quality Gap of 1.3238 (close to the retained model's 0.0) with PPL of 15.53 and average zero-shot accuracy of 0.4694, matching the retained model's utility. On the MUSE-News benchmark, FLAT(TV) achieves the lowest VerbMem (1.7) and KnowMem on D_f (13.6) among methods that also preserve retain-set knowledge (KnowMem on D_r = 31.8), while all methods fail to achieve satisfactory privacy leakage scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLAT consistently achieves the best Model Utility across all three base LLMs on TOFU (e.g., 0.6393 vs. 0.6359 for DPO and 0.6337 for LLMU on Llama2-7B), while also attaining top-two Forget Quality p-values. On the Harry Potter task, FLAT(KL) reduces the FQ Gap to 1.3238, substantially outperforming GA (2.7301), KL (2.7301), GD (2.3439), PO (2.1601), LLMU (2.4639), and DPO (2.2152), while NPO achieves a slightly lower FQ Gap (1.2611) but at the cost of significantly degraded PPL (19.66 vs. 15.53 for FLAT). On MUSE-News, FLAT(TV) is one of only three methods (alongside GD and WHP) to satisfy all memorization criteria simultaneously, achieving the highest retain-set KnowMem (31.8) among those three. The ablation against DPO and SimPO (Table 6) shows FLAT(KL) wins on 10 of 12 sub-metrics, confirming that the f-divergence re-weighting mechanism is the key differentiator rather than the data formulation. Overall, FLAT trades a small amount of forget quality (vs. NPO or PO) for substantially better utility preservation, and does so without requiring retain data or a reference model.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLAT builds on the variational form of f-divergence (via Fenchel duality) established in Nowozin et al. (2016) and Wei & Liu (2021), repurposing it as a principled loss-adjustment mechanism for LLM unlearning. It departs from the dominant loss-adjustment paradigm (forget loss + retain loss + custom loss) by eliminating both the retain data term and the reference-model calibration term, instead deriving the optimal re-weighting between template-response learning and forget-response suppression from the f-divergence conjugate functions. The method is closely related to DPO and NPO in its use of paired (preferred, dispreferred) responses, but differs in three concrete ways: no reference model, averaging rather than summing token probabilities, and a non-logarithmic re-weighting via f* and g* activation functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependency on retain data and a reference LLM, FLAT addresses a practical bottleneck in real-world unlearning where retain data may be unavailable and maintaining a reference model is computationally expensive for large-scale LLMs. The theoretical convergence guarantee (Theorem 3.4) for the empirical f-divergence estimator provides a non-parametric rate of convergence, which is a clean contribution to the unlearning literature. However, the method still fails to achieve satisfactory privacy-leakage scores on the MUSE benchmark (a limitation shared by all tested baselines), and the empirical gains over strong methods like NPO are modest and inconsistent across metrics and datasets, leaving open whether the f-divergence framework offers a fundamental advantage over simpler preference-based objectives at scale.
