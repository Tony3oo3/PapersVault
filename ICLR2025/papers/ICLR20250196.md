## Problem

In imbalanced regression, deep learning models bias predictions toward many-shot regions to minimize overall error, causing significantly higher errors in few-shot regions where rare but clinically critical samples reside. While distributional information has proven effective in imbalanced classification, the distribution discrepancy between model predictions and true labels in imbalanced regression has been largely unexplored, leaving a gap in how to align prediction distributions with label distributions in a differentiable manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250196/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is architecture-agnostic and operates at the loss-function level: it takes standard CNN outputs (ResNet-50 for facial images, Net1D for single-lead ECG signals) and the corresponding continuous labels, then computes an additional distribution-alignment term alongside the standard per-sample loss. The continuous label space is discretized into equal-width bins solely for distribution estimation (e.g., bin width of 1 year for age, or clinically relevant intervals for potassium), while the model still predicts continuous values. The distribution loss weight is fixed at 1, and the method is applied as a fine-tuning stage on pre-trained model parameters.

## Method

Dist Loss jointly optimizes two objectives: (1) distribution alignment, which minimizes the distance between the model's prediction distribution and the label distribution, and (2) sample-wise prediction error. The distribution alignment is computed in three steps: KDE estimates the label probability distribution to generate pseudo-labels (a sequence where each bin appears proportional to its expected frequency in a batch of size M); model predictions are sorted via fast differentiable sorting (Blondel et al., 2020, using projection onto permutation polytopes with O(n log n) forward and O(n) backward complexity) to form pseudo-predictions; and the distance between these two sequences approximates the distribution discrepancy. The sequence difference is measured using inverse-probability-weighted MSE, which upweights rare label values. This dual-objective loss is applied during fine-tuning of pre-trained models, requiring no architectural changes and incurring minimal additional computational cost compared to vanilla training.

## Results

On the few-shot region, Dist Loss achieves SOTA MAE of 22.550 (IMDB-WIKI-DIR), 9.122 (AgeDB-DIR), and 1.329 (ECG-K-DIR), and SOTA GM of 5.453 (AgeDB-DIR) and 0.978 (ECG-K-DIR). It outperforms the vanilla model by 4.380/3.772/0.442 in MAE and 6.966/4.336/0.600 in GM across the three datasets, respectively. Dist Loss wins 5 out of 6 headline metrics, losing only on IMDB-WIKI-DIR GM (14.288 vs. Balanced MSE's 12.603). Training time is comparable to Balanced MSE (~154s vs. 153s per epoch on IMDB-WIKI-DIR), far cheaper than feature-level methods like ConR (~1169s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dist Loss outperforms all five baselines (LDS, FDS, Ranksim, ConR, Balanced MSE) on MAE across all three datasets, with the largest gains over Ranksim (+3.449 MAE on IMDB-WIKI-DIR) and FDS (+2.358 MAE on IMDB-WIKI-DIR). On GM, Dist Loss wins on AgeDB-DIR (5.453 vs. 6.248 for Balanced MSE) and ECG-K-DIR (0.978 vs. 1.046 for Balanced MSE), but trails Balanced MSE (12.603) and LDS (12.803) on IMDB-WIKI-DIR GM (14.288), which the authors attribute to the geometric mean's sensitivity to small frequent errors. Compared to the closest competitor Balanced MSE, Dist Loss improves few-shot MAE by 0.992/0.491/0.088 on the three datasets. The paper further shows Dist Loss is complementary: when stacked on top of each baseline (Table 2), it improves 26 out of 30 metrics, e.g., reducing LDS's IMDB-WIKI-DIR few-shot MAE from 22.753 to 22.331 and Balanced MSE's AgeDB-DIR few-shot GM from 6.248 to 5.585.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distribution-alignment paradigm from imbalanced classification (e.g., KL-divergence balancing, posterior recalibration) to imbalanced regression, a setting where such strategies had been largely unexplored. It builds directly on Blondel et al.'s fast differentiable sorting to make the distribution distance computation end-to-end trainable, and on the imbalanced regression framework established by Yang et al. (LDS, FDS) and Ren et al. (Balanced MSE), which already leveraged label distribution priors for reweighting. The key conceptual departure is that rather than reweighting individual samples or smoothing feature spaces, Dist Loss explicitly minimizes a differentiable distance between the model's prediction distribution and the true label distribution, treating the batch as a Monte Carlo sample from each distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is practically attractive because it is a drop-in loss addition that complements (rather than competes with) existing imbalanced regression techniques, consistently improving few-shot accuracy across all tested baselines. Its clinical motivation—accurately detecting rare but life-threatening potassium abnormalities from ECGs—gives it concrete real-world relevance beyond academic benchmarks. Open questions include whether the KDE-based discretization of the continuous label space introduces bias in highly multimodal distributions, whether the approach generalizes to multi-output or structured regression, and why the GM metric reveals a localized weakness on IMDB-WIKI-DIR that MAE masks. The batch-size insensitivity suggests the method is robust, but the reliance on a single pre-trained backbone (ResNet-50/Net1D) limits architectural generality claims.
