## Problem

Traditional masked graph modeling cannot be directly applied to circuit representation learning because masking gates in an AIG admits multiple logically valid reconstructions, destroying the strict logical equivalence that circuits require. Additionally, existing self-supervised circuit representation methods capture functional behaviors (e.g., truth tables) but fail to extract fine-grained structural details, while GNNs trained purely on structure overlook abstract circuit functions that are only implicitly encoded in gate connectivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253108/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes AIGs (directed acyclic graphs with PI, PO, AND, and NOT gate nodes) as primary input, with the adjacency matrix kept unmasked throughout training. For the VGA component, equivalent behavior-level Verilog code is additionally consumed, processed by a bidirectional LLM (gte-Qwen2-7B-instruct) into token embeddings that are adaptively pooled to 16 tokens. The output is a per-node latent representation (d-dimensional) from the GNN encoder, which is then used without fine-tuning for downstream tasks: QoR ranking over 1500 synthesis sequences, logic equivalence scoring via cosine similarity, and SAT solver parameter regression (node/level counts for Boolean fences).

## Method

MGVGA is a constrained masked modeling paradigm built on an AIG autoencoder (DeepGCN encoder/decoder) with two complementary masking strategies. MGM masks gate representations in the latent space after full encoding, so unmasked gate embeddings already aggregate features from masked gates and serve as logical-equivalence constraints during reconstruction; reconstruction targets gate type (4-class classification) and in/out-degree (regression) for masked nodes only. VGA masks gate types in the original AIG before encoding, then constrains reconstruction via a cross-attention block that aligns the masked AIG representation (as queries) with LLM-extracted Verilog code embeddings (as keys/values), injecting abstract functional knowledge from the LLM into the GNN. The total loss is the sum of MGM and VGA reconstruction losses, and the adjacency matrix is never masked to maintain a unique reconstruction solution and avoid NP-hard ambiguity.

## Results

On QoR prediction across 10 circuit designs, MGVGA achieves an average NDCG@3 of 0.540 and NDCG@5 of 0.537, substantially outperforming DeepGate2 (0.334 and 0.324 respectively), with Top-10% Commonality of 0.301 vs. 0.226. On logic equivalence identification, MGVGA achieves an average F1-score of 0.470 and AUC of 0.862, compared to DeepGate2's 0.424 and 0.804. In SAT solving (exact synthesis), MGVGA achieves a 53.09% average runtime reduction over the baseline, versus 18.24% for DeepGate2 and 21.31% for DeepGate3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepGate2 (Shi et al., ICCAD 2023), MGVGA wins decisively on both primary tasks: QoR prediction (NDCG@3: 0.540 vs. 0.334, a 62% relative improvement) and logic equivalence identification (F1: 0.470 vs. 0.424; AUC: 0.862 vs. 0.804). Against DeepGate3 (Shi et al., ICCAD 2024), MGVGA outperforms on small-design logic equivalence (F1: 0.474 vs. 0.390; AUC: 0.859 vs. 0.834) and achieves a 53.09% average SAT-solving runtime reduction versus DeepGate3's 21.31%. Against the exact synthesis baseline (Haaswijk et al., TCAD 2019), MGVGA reduces SAT solver runtime by 53.09% on average, more than doubling the reduction achieved by DeepGate2 (18.24%) and DeepGate3 (21.31%). MGVGA also generalizes across GNN backbones: when applied to GraphSAGE and Graph Transformer, both still outperform the DeepGate2 baseline, confirming the paradigm's architecture-agnostic benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the masked graph autoencoder paradigm (GraphMAE, GraphMAE2) to circuit representation learning, but departs from prior work by masking in the latent space rather than the original graph to preserve logical equivalence—a constraint unique to AIGs that standard masked modeling violates. It extends the DeepGate2/DeepGate3 line of circuit representation learning by introducing a Verilog-AIG alignment component that uses a bidirectional LLM (gte-Qwen2-7B-instruct) as a teacher to inject functional knowledge into GNNs via cross-attention, a combination not previously explored in the EDA literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The logical equivalence constraint is a genuine structural challenge in circuit representation learning that prior masked modeling approaches overlooked, and the latent-space masking solution is a clean, minimal fix. The LLM-to-GNN knowledge transfer for circuit function is a promising direction, though the paper only tests one LLM and a limited set of Verilog-AIG pairs (64,826), leaving open whether the functional gains generalize across HDL styles or scale to sequential/mixed-signal designs. The 0.12M-parameter model outperforming 8M-parameter DeepGate3 suggests the constrained training signal is more important than model capacity for this task, but the evaluation is confined to combinational AIGs on specific benchmarks, and the practical integration into industrial EDA flows remains untested.
