## Problem

Existing vision context sparsification methods (e.g., FastV) reduce image tokens during the prefill stage of MLLMs, but the efficiency gains progressively diminish during decoding as the autoregressive generation of output text tokens dominates computation and memory. No prior framework addresses the growing redundancy in the language context (generated output tokens) alongside the vision context, leaving a gap in achieving consistently efficient inference throughout the entire MLLM generation process.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed at 336×336 resolution into 576 image tokens via a vision encoder and MLP projector) and a text prompt, producing a generated text response. Sparsification is applied at a single decoder layer (default: layer 2), where the image predictor reduces 576 image tokens to ~115 (80% reduction) during prefill, and the output text predictor dynamically prunes generated tokens during decoding. The framework operates across three inference modes—prefill, decoding without KV cache, and decoding with KV cache—each with a tailored sparsification strategy, and supports batch-parallel inference via padded token sets.

## Method

Dynamic-LLaVA introduces two lightweight learnable predictors (an image predictor with ViT blocks and an output text predictor with MLPs) that generate binary masks to select which tokens to retain, applied once at a chosen decoder layer with decisions propagated to all subsequent layers. For prefill, only image tokens are pruned; for decoding without KV cache, both image and output text tokens are reduced; for decoding with KV cache, the output predictor performs 'online KV cache compression' by deciding whether the current token's K/V activations should be appended to the cache. End-to-end training uses a MaskedSoftmax operation to isolate non-essential tokens' influence on essential tokens without breaking autoregressive loss computation, combined with Gumbel-Softmax and Straight-Through Estimator to make the non-differentiable argmax selection trainable. A Frobenius-norm regularization term enforces predefined keep rates for vision and language contexts, with language sparsification applied only to samples exceeding a minimum output length to avoid training instability.

## Results

Dynamic-LLaVA reduces image tokens by ~80% (576→115) and prefill FLOPs by ~75% (10.1→2.5 TFLOPs) while matching or exceeding the full-context LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline on most vision understanding benchmarks (e.g., SciQA +2.3%, MMBench +1.1%, POPE +0.4%). During decoding, the combined vision-language sparsification reduces computation by ~50% (without KV cache) and GPU memory by ~50% (with KV cache), while maintaining generation fluency (PPL increase < 0.3) and quality (METEOR slightly improved). In practical inference on A100 (batch=8), Dynamic-LLaVA-13B completes 4K-token generation in 6184s vs. 13368s for LLaVA-1.5-13B (which OOMs), and 2382s vs. 4117s for 2K tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full-context LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline, Dynamic-LLaVA-7B_I matches or exceeds performance on 8 of 12 reported benchmarks (gains on SciQA +2.3%, MMBench +1.1%, POPE +0.4%) while using only 20% of the image tokens. Compared to FastV (144 tokens, 3.2 TFLOPs), Dynamic-LLaVA uses 20% fewer tokens and 22% fewer FLOPs while achieving substantially better scores (e.g., VQAv2 78.0 vs. 75.1, GQA 61.4 vs. 57.5, POPE 85.0 vs. 81.0). Against HiRED (same 115 tokens), Dynamic-LLaVA dramatically outperforms on SciQA (69.1 vs. 44.2) and VQAv2 (78.0 vs. 74.7), indicating that learnable predictors are far more effective than attention-guided heuristics. For generation tasks, Dynamic-LLaVA_I|T achieves PPL 4.90 and METEOR 0.3108 on LVIS-VQA (single-round) vs. 4.59/0.3103 for the full model, while reducing decoding FLOPs by ~45% and KV cache memory by ~39–49%; in contrast, H2O KV cache compression causes catastrophic degradation (PPL 78.95, METEOR 0.0388). When combined with TokenPacker, Dynamic-LLaVA reduces tokens from 144 to 57 (−60% additional) with only 0.3% average performance loss, outperforming the standalone TokenPacker-64Token baseline on MMBench (65.1 vs. 64.1) and MM-Vet (32.1 vs. –).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA paradigm and the vision context sparsification line of work (FastV, PruMerge, VoCo, HiRED) by adding a second axis of sparsification: the generated language context during decoding. Prior methods reduce image tokens after a fixed layer to accelerate the prefill stage, but the paper's central observation is that these gains diminish as autoregressive text generation dominates computation. It also positions itself against LLM KV cache compression methods (H2O, SnapKV, PyramidKV) by framing its decoding-with-cache strategy as 'online KV cache compression'—deciding whether to *add* a token's activations to the cache rather than evicting historical entries—enabling unified efficiency across all three inference modes (prefill, decoding w/o cache, decoding w/ cache).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50% GPU memory savings during KV-cache decoding and ~50% computation reduction during cache-free decoding are practically significant for deploying MLLMs on consumer or edge hardware, and the framework composes with existing efficient projectors (e.g., TokenPacker) for compounding gains. However, the evaluation is limited to LLaVA-1.5 (7B/13B) with a fixed sparsification layer (l=2), and the paper acknowledges that text-token sparsification during prefill degrades performance, leaving the optimal sparsification schedule across layers and stages underexplored. Whether the learnable predictors transfer across different MLLM backbones or higher-resolution settings without retraining remains an open question.
