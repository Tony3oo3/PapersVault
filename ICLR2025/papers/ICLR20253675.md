## Problem

Despite the empirical success of transformers, the theoretical mechanisms by which self-attention performs variable selection in classification tasks remain poorly understood. Prior theoretical analyses either relied on impractical initializations, fixed certain parameters during training, or focused on regression rather than classification. This paper addresses the gap by providing a tight global convergence analysis for a one-layer transformer learning a group-sparse binary classification problem from zero initialization, revealing exactly how each component of the attention mechanism contributes to optimal variable selection.

## Input / Output

The input is a matrix X ∈ R^{d×D} whose D columns each contain d Gaussian-distributed variables, concatenated with sinusoidal positional encodings to form Z ∈ R^{(d+D)×D}. The label y ∈ {−1,+1} depends only on the inner product of one designated group x_{j*} with a fixed unit vector v*, making the problem group-sparse. The model outputs a scalar logit via a weighted sum of attended tokens, and the attention score matrix S ∈ R^{D×D} reveals which group the transformer focuses on; theoretically, S_{j*,j} → 1 for all j while all other entries vanish exponentially in D.

## Method

The architecture is a one-layer self-attention model f(Z,W,v) = v^T Z S(Z^T W Z) 1_D, where W ∈ R^{(d+D)×(d+D)} is a combined query-key matrix and v ∈ R^{d+D} is a single value vector, both initialized to zero and trained jointly by gradient descent on the population cross-entropy loss. The key mechanism is that W decomposes into blocks where W_{2,2} (position-position interaction) learns a low-rank structure that makes the positional encoding of the label-relevant group j* dominate the softmax, effectively selecting that group, while W_{1,1} (feature-feature interaction) aligns with v* v*^T and the value vector v_1 aligns with v*. The paper establishes a tight convergence rate of T* = Θ(D³ ∨ 1/(D³ε³)) iterations with matching upper and lower bounds on the loss, and further shows that the pre-trained model transfers to downstream tasks with a generalization error of Õ((d+D)/(γ²n)), improving upon the Ω(dD/ε) sample complexity of linear logistic regression on vectorized features.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2.2) establishes a tight convergence bound: a one-layer transformer trained by gradient descent from zero initialization reaches population cross-entropy loss ε in T* = Θ(D³ ∨ 1/(D³ε³)) iterations, with matching upper and lower bounds. For downstream transfer (Theorem 3.2), fine-tuning the pre-trained model via online SGD achieves a generalization error of Õ((d+D)log²n / (γ²n)), yielding a sample complexity of Õ((d+D)/ε) that strictly improves over the Ω(dD/ε) lower bound for linear logistic regression on vectorized features. Numerical experiments on synthetic group-sparse data (D=6, d=4 and D=4, d=2) confirm that training loss converges to 0, the attention matrix concentrates on the label-relevant group (j*=2), and downstream test accuracy converges to 1 after ~400 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper without a standard benchmark comparison table. The key quantitative comparison is between the pre-trained transformer's sample complexity of Õ((d+D)/ε) for the downstream task and the classical PAC lower bound of Ω(dD/ε) for linear logistic regression on the full vectorized input, showing a factor-of-D improvement when d ≫ 1. The paper distinguishes itself from Wang et al. (2024), which studies a similar sparse token selection problem but uses an l₂ (regression) loss and assumes the positional encoding of the next token equals the average of target tokens. Compared to Jelassi et al. (2022), this work avoids the impractical assumption that the value vector is initialized aligned with the ground truth direction, instead using zero initialization, and provides matching upper and lower bounds rather than only an upper bound on iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the growing line of work analyzing training dynamics of transformers (Zhang et al. 2024; Huang et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024b) from regression to classification, and departs from prior classification analyses (Jelassi et al. 2022; Tarzanagh et al. 2023) by requiring only zero initialization with all parameters jointly trained, rather than infeasible structured initializations or frozen sub-parameters. The 'group sparsity' data model connects to classical high-dimensional statistics (Huang & Zhang 2010; Li et al. 2023b), and the transfer-learning analysis draws on PAC-learning sample complexity bounds for logistic regression (Long 1995). Unlike Wang et al. (2024), which studies sparse token selection under L2 loss with a simplified positional-encoding assumption, this paper tackles cross-entropy classification with a more standard attention formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first tight (matching upper and lower) convergence bound for a one-layer transformer learning group-sparse classification from zero initialization, and gives a clean mechanistic decomposition showing that positional encodings serve purely as attention-routing signals while the value vector aligns with the ground-truth direction. The transfer-learning result demonstrating improved sample complexity over linear logistic regression on vectorized features suggests transformers can exploit group structure for data-efficient downstream adaptation. Key limitations include the restriction to one-layer architectures, population (infinite-data) loss, Gaussian features, and a specific sinusoidal positional encoding; extending these results to deeper transformers, finite-sample regimes, and integration with MLPs or normalization layers remains open.
