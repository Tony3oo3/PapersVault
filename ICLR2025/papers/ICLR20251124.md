## Problem

The Sinkhorn algorithm for entropic optimal transport is restricted to bipartite graphs with fixed marginal constraints, making it inapplicable to general graph transportation where flow must pass through transshipment nodes and satisfy flow balance rather than marginal constraints. Existing graph-based OT methods (e.g., Sobolev transport) precompute shortest paths and cannot impose capacity constraints on nodes or edges, while classical minimum-cost flow solvers are CPU-bound and assume integer data. No GPU-friendly matrix iterative algorithm existed for solving minimum-cost flow on general graphs with flow balance and capacity constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes an N×N distance matrix D (with D_ii=0), an N-dimensional net supply vector s = a−b (where positive entries are supply nodes, negative are demand nodes, zero are transshipment), and optionally an N×N edge capacity matrix S and N-dimensional node capacity vector r. It outputs an N×N directed flow matrix P* satisfying P*1_N − P*ᵀ1_N = s, with a final backflow-removal step P* ← max{0, P* − P*ᵀ} to eliminate bidirectional flow between node pairs. The algorithm operates on general directed graphs (not restricted to bipartite structure) and handles both double-precision and integer-precision data, scaling from 100 to over 3.8 million nodes.

## Method

The key reformulation introduces virtual self-flows (Diag(P) = d) so that isolated nodes participate in iterations, and an auxiliary outflow variable q that converts flow balance constraints into marginal-like constraints (P1_N = q+d, Pᵀ1_N = q−s+d), enabling a Sinkhorn-style alternating update. The solution has the form P = Diag(u)K Diag(v) with u⊙v = 1, but unlike vanilla Sinkhorn, the Gibbs kernel K is not fixed—its diagonal is updated each iteration via Diag(K) = d/(u⊙v) to enforce the virtual flow constraint, while off-diagonal entries remain exp(−D_ij/ε). The outflow q is updated in closed form as q = s/2 + ((Kv)⊙(Kᵀu) + s²/4)^{1/2} − d. For capacity constraints, node limits are enforced by truncating q, and edge limits modify the K update to K ← min(K, Diag(u)⁻¹ S Diag(v)⁻¹). Linear convergence is proven via the Hilbert projective metric, and all operations reduce to matrix-vector multiplications, making the algorithm GPU-friendly.

## Results

On Uniform-MCF (double-precision, no constraints), EOFT-Sinkhorn achieves objective values of 10.11 (500 nodes), 10.311 (5k nodes), and 10.728 (10k nodes) in 4.18s, 33s, and 47s respectively, compared to exact solvers Real (10.02/10.059/10.08) and ZKW (10.02/10.059/10.08) which take 14.8s/1843s/3363s and 18.4s/2034s/3781s. Gurobi fails to complete 5k and 10k instances within 3 hours. On very large sparse graphs (~1M nodes), EOFT-Sinkhorn solves Netgen_8 in 50.4s (obj 18.83) versus 335s for pns and 186s for lemon-ns (both obj 18.33), and Vision (3.9M nodes) in 1802s (obj 32.04) versus 2805s for lemon-ns (obj 30.16). The objective gap to exact solutions is typically 0.5–7%, while speedups range from 3× to over 100× depending on problem size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EOFT-Sinkhorn consistently outperforms all baselines in wall-clock time, with speedups of ~3.5× over Real at 500 nodes, ~56× at 5k nodes, and ~71× at 10k nodes on the no-constraint setting. Gurobi is the slowest CPU-based solver (768s at 500 nodes) and fails to finish 5k/10k instances within 3 hours, while EOFT-Sinkhorn solves 10k nodes in under 1 minute. On the NETGEN integer-precision benchmark (Table 2), EOFT-Sinkhorn is slower than Real and ZKW on small instances (100 nodes: 22.2s vs 0.42s) but becomes faster at 500+ nodes (82s vs 706–1028s at 500 nodes; 1261s vs 7491–18592s at 5k nodes). On very large sparse graphs (Table 3), EOFT-Sinkhorn achieves the fastest solve time on Netgen_8 (50.4s vs 186s for lemon-ns and 335s for pns) but is slightly slower than lemon-ns on Netgen_lo_8 (59.3s vs 51s) and Vision (1802s vs 2805s for lemon-ns, though pns is faster at 1774s). The trade-off is a small objective gap: EOFT-Sinkhorn's approximate solutions are 0.5–7% above the exact optimum, whereas Real, ZKW, pns, and lemon-ns all achieve the exact optimal objective on their respective benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Sinkhorn algorithm for entropic optimal transport (Cuturi, 2013; Benamou et al., 2015) from bipartite graphs with marginal constraints to general directed graphs with flow balance constraints, introducing the Optimal Flow Transport (OFT) formulation. It departs from prior graph-OT work (Sobolev transport by Le et al., 2022/2024) that relies on precomputed shortest paths and cannot handle node/edge capacity constraints, and from classical MCF algorithms (Fulkerson, Klein, Goldberg) that are CPU-bound and often assume integer data. The key technical departure is the introduction of virtual self-flows and a reformulation of flow balance into marginal-like constraints, enabling a Sinkhorn-style matrix iteration that was previously inapplicable to the flow balance setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first GPU-friendly matrix iterative solver for the minimum-cost flow problem, achieving significant speedups (e.g., 33s vs. 1843s on 5k-node instances) over CPU-based exact algorithms while handling high-precision (non-integer) data that traditional MCF methods cannot. The approximate nature of the solution (2–3% objective gap on larger instances) and the need to tune the virtual-flow parameter d and regularization ε are practical limitations. Open questions include combining OFT with partial transport, robust optimization, and parallel computation, as well as whether the entropic regularization bias can be further reduced for near-exact solutions at scale.
