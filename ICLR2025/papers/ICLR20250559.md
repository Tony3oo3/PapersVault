## Problem

In parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), each downstream task is typically solved independently, ignoring the growing body of related tasks that could inform adaptation. Existing cross-task strategies—LoRA zoo mixing, retrieval-based composition, and model-based clustering—are ad-hoc library-based approaches that scale poorly and lack a principled mechanism for extracting shared structure. The paper asks whether a Bayesian meta-learning framework can learn a compact, generative prior over PEFT modules that captures task-agnostic knowledge and regularizes adaptation to unseen tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained backbone (BART-base, ViT-B/16, or FLAN-T5-XL) with rank-4 LoRA adapters, training data from N meta-training tasks (32 examples per task on Cross-Fit, 800 per task on VTAB-1K), and at test time a small support set from an unseen task. During meta-training it produces a K-component Gaussian mixture posterior over the shared latent variable φ; at test time it produces fine-tuned LoRA parameters for the target task, task predictions, and calibrated uncertainty estimates (ECE, NLL). The mixture order K (1–5) controls the number of prototypical task templates retained.

## Method

LiFT introduces a hierarchical Bayesian model where task-specific LoRA parameters θ_i^a are random variables governed by a higher-level latent variable φ, with Gaussian priors p(φ)=N(0,σ²I) and p(θ_i^a|φ)=N(φ,β²I). Posterior inference uses a novel SGLD-Gibbs sampler that cycles through tasks in round-robin fashion (updating one θ_i^a per step while always updating φ), with an auxiliary variable J that asynchronously maintains the gradient sum for φ to avoid O(N) memory. A novel online EM algorithm then distills the SGLD-Gibbs posterior samples into a K-component Gaussian mixture in a single pass, without storing all samples. At test time, this mixture serves as a prior over the target task's LoRA parameters, and SGLD sampling finds the posterior p(θ_*^a | D_*, {D_i}), effectively regularizing adaptation toward the most relevant learned prototypes.

## Results

On the Cross-Fit NLP benchmark (160 tasks, BART-base + rank-4 LoRA), LiFT with K=5 mixture components achieves an average score of 51.90 across 7 task splits, outperforming the best meta-learning baseline i-MAML (48.02) by ~3.9 points and the best LoRA mixture method (i-MAML-Mix K=5, 49.23) by ~2.7 points. On VTAB-1K vision (ViT-B/16), LiFT K=3 achieves 80.6/86.3/59.9 average accuracy on Natural/Special/Structured groups, surpassing ProtoNet (79.8/84.3/55.2) and all MAML variants. On Shakespeare next-word prediction, LiFT K=3 achieves 10.99 average perplexity versus 11.28 for Reptile and 11.27 for Union Train.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiFT outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every benchmark. On Cross-Fit, LiFT (K=5, avg 51.90) beats the strongest meta-learning baseline i-MAML (48.02) by 3.9 points, the best Bayesian meta-learner BMAML (47.89) by 4.0 points, and the best LoRA mixture method i-MAML-Mix (49.23) by 2.7 points. Against LoRA zoo approaches, LiFT surpasses LoRA Retriever (46.34) by 5.6 points and model-based clustering MBC-μ (45.95) by 6.0 points. On VTAB-1K, LiFT K=3 leads ProtoNet (Snell et al., 2017) by 0.8/2.0/4.7 points on Natural/Special/Structured groups respectively, and outperforms ProtoNet-LITE (Bronskill et al., 2021) by 1.4/1.9/4.6 points. On Shakespeare, LiFT K=3 (10.99 perplexity) improves over Reptile (11.28) and Union Train (11.27) by ~0.3 perplexity. In the appendix, LiFT also beats Bootstrapped-MAML (Flennerhag et al., 2022) by 9.1 points on CLS-45 (64.12 vs 55.02), and statistical significance tests (5 seeds) confirm all improvements are significant (p < 10⁻⁵).

## Relation to Prior Work

LiFT sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) gradient-based meta-learning (MAML, Reptile, i-MAML), which learns shared initialization via hypergradient optimization; (2) Bayesian meta-learning (BMAML, ABML), which introduces stochasticity but without a hierarchical structure over task-specific parameters; and (3) LoRA zoo/mixing approaches (LoRA-Retriever, MBC-μ), which train task-wise adapters and recombine them at test time. LiFT departs from all three by jointly modeling all task-specific LoRA parameters under a shared latent variable φ with a Gaussian mixture prior, and by performing posterior inference via a novel SGLD-Gibbs sampler combined with an online EM algorithm to maintain the mixture representation. The SGLD-Gibbs combination and the online EM for streaming posterior samples are the key technical departures from prior Bayesian meta-learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiFT provides a principled, uncertainty-aware framework for cross-task PEFT that outperforms both gradient-based meta-learners and recent LoRA library-mixing methods by substantial margins across NLP and vision benchmarks, while matching the per-iteration computational cost of vanilla SGD. The hierarchical Bayesian formulation naturally yields calibrated uncertainty estimates (significantly better ECE and NLL than BMAML/ABML), which is valuable for downstream decision-making. A key limitation is the requirement for joint training across all source tasks, though the 'Fixed θ_i^a' variant partially addresses this by accepting pre-fine-tuned LoRAs. Open questions include whether the Gaussian mixture posterior adequately captures highly heterogeneous task distributions at scale, how the method behaves when the number of training tasks grows to thousands, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-LoRA PEFT architectures or full fine-tuning settings.
