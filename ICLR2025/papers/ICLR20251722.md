## Problem

Existing 3D shape representation methods predominantly rely on point clouds, which suffer from limited resolution, a domain gap with real-world images, and an inability to effectively leverage 2D priors from pre-trained CLIP models. These point cloud approaches also demand substantial compute (e.g., 480 A100 hours for Uni3D's 1B-parameter model) and require a complete 3D scan or mesh of the object. The paper asks whether multi-view images can serve as a more efficient, flexible, and generalizable 3D representation that naturally inherits 2D visual priors.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a variable number (1 to M) of 224×224 rendered multi-view images of a 3D object, with no assumptions on camera pose or view ordering (permutation-invariant). During training, 1–6 views are randomly sampled per step; at inference, typically 12 views are used. The output is a single shape embedding obtained by averaging the [CLS] token embeddings across all views, aligned to a frozen CLIP text-image space, enabling downstream tasks including shape classification, fine-grained text-to-shape retrieval, and image-to-shape retrieval.

## Method

Duoduo CLIP replaces the point cloud encoder in a CLIP-style contrastive learning framework with a multi-view ViT-B/32 image encoder initialized from the same OpenCLIP checkpoint used for the frozen image and text encoders. The key architectural innovation is cross-view attention (MVA): in the trainable layers, self-attention is modified to operate over the concatenated token sequences of all M views (Q,K,V ∈ R^{(L×m)×k}), enabling geometric correspondence learning across views without adding parameters. For efficiency, only the attention layers of the last 6 of 12 ViT layers are trained (MLPs remain frozen), halving VRAM usage and preserving CLIP priors for better generalization. Training uses an asymmetric contrastive loss (InfoNCE) across shape-text, shape-image, and image-text pairs with a batch size of 1600, requiring only 87M parameters and 57 A5000 hours—dramatically less than point cloud SOTA methods.

## Results

On Objaverse-LVIS (Ensembled dataset), Duoduo CLIP with 12 multi-views achieves 55.2% Top-1, 77.1% Top-3, and 83.4% Top-5 accuracy, matching the 1B-parameter Uni3D (55.3% Top-1) while using only 87M parameters and 57 A5000 GPU-hours versus Uni3D's 480 A100 GPU-hours. On ScanObjectNN, Duoduo CLIP (12F) reaches 66.3% Top-1, surpassing Uni3D (65.3%) and ShapeLLM/RECON++ (65.4%). For fine-grained text-to-shape retrieval on Text2Shape, Duoduo CLIP achieves RR@1=15.90 and RR@5=34.08, substantially outperforming Uni3D-G (10.78/26.37) and OpenShape† (10.53/25.94) trained on the same Ensembled dataset, and approaching the SOTA MXM-CLR (16.83/39.06) trained directly on Text2Shape.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Duoduo CLIP (12F) matches Uni3D on Objaverse-LVIS Top-1 (55.2 vs 55.3) while using ~12x fewer parameters (87M vs 1B) and ~8x less GPU time (57 A5000-hrs vs 480 A100-hrs), and surpasses Uni3D on ScanObjectNN Top-1 (66.3 vs 65.3). With only 5 views, Duoduo CLIP already outperforms all point cloud baselines except Uni3D on Objaverse-LVIS (54.1 vs 53.7 for ShapeLLM, 52.5 for MixCon3D, 52.0 for VIT-LENS). On text-to-shape retrieval, Duoduo CLIP (RR@1=15.90) significantly outperforms Uni3D-G (10.78) and OpenShape† (10.53) by 5+ points, and approaches the Text2Shape-specific SOTA MXM-CLR (16.83). The paper also shows that Duoduo CLIP generalizes better on unseen data: when trained without LVIS shapes, it achieves 52.7% Top-1 on O-LVIS versus 46.8% for OpenShape and 47.2% for Uni3D, indicating that fine-tuning CLIP preserves its generalization better than training point cloud encoders from scratch. On real-world MVPNet data, Duoduo CLIP trained with MVImgNet reaches 66.06% Top-1 (12F), substantially outperforming OpenShape†+MVPNet (54.59%) and even zero-shot CLIP B-32 (52.68%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the OpenShape contrastive learning framework (Liu et al., 2023a) by replacing the point cloud shape encoder with a multi-view image encoder initialized from CLIP's ViT-B/32 image encoder (Radford et al., 2021). The key architectural departure is the introduction of cross-view attention (inspired by MVDream) that allows tokens from different views to attend to each other, enabling information flow across frames while maintaining permutation invariance. Unlike prior 3D-text alignment methods (ULIP, Uni3D, TAMM, VIT-LENS) that all rely on point cloud encoders, this work demonstrates that rendered multi-view images are a sufficient and more efficient 3D representation, leveraging 2D priors from CLIP more naturally than point cloud approaches can.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic efficiency gap (57 A5000 hours / 87M params vs. 480 A100 hours / 1B params for Uni3D) makes 3D shape representation learning accessible to much smaller labs, and the multi-view image representation offers practical advantages for robotics and real-world applications where point cloud reconstruction introduces errors. The finding that point cloud methods generalize worse on unseen shapes (zero-shot CLIP BigG-14 beats OpenShape on Ensembled-no-LVIS) challenges the assumption that explicit 3D geometry is necessary for good generalization. Open questions include whether hybrid point cloud + multi-view approaches can capture both geometric and textural information, whether incorporating depth or normal maps closes the remaining geometric gap, and whether the approach scales to larger CLIP variants or alternative pretraining objectives beyond contrastive learning.
