## Problem

Prior LLM-based longitudinal health prediction methods are validated only on homogeneous numerical sensor time series and ignore the substantial computing, time, and monetary costs of invoking LLMs for multiple related outcomes. Real-world ubiquitous health data from ecological momentary assessment protocols are heterogeneous (categorical, mixed-type), have high missing rates, and small sample sizes, making it unclear whether existing approaches translate to practical settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252929/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous time-series health data (passive sensor readings and self-reported survey responses, including categorical and mixed-type features with missing values) along with auxiliary survey data (demographics, background questionnaires), all converted into natural-language data descriptions capped at 200 words. It produces simultaneous binary predictions for Q health or well-being outcomes (Q ranges from 2 to 6 across datasets), with only positive labels explicitly concatenated to reduce negation-induced hallucinations. The boosting pipeline operates on these text descriptions rather than raw numerical features, leveraging the LLM's ability to reason over heterogeneous modalities without retraining.

## Method

MuHBoost adapts SummaryBoost's framework—where LLM-generated summaries serve as weak learners in an AdaBoost pipeline—to multi-label classification via the Label Powerset approach, with a modified ClusterSampling subroutine that handles the degenerate case where 2^Q approaches the sample size N. A two-step data conversion procedure (extractive then abstractive summarization) handles heterogeneous inputs and mitigates hallucination. Two variants address LLM hallucination under multi-label inference: MuHBoost[LP+] reformulates the LP problem to identify only positive labels (simplifying the prompt), while MuHBoost[CC] employs AdaBoost.C2 to run independent per-label boosting paths in a fixed chain order, allowing asynchronous early stopping and label-wise weight updates. The entire pipeline uses GPT-3.5 via API with up to 100 boosting rounds and a representative subset size of 10, requiring no LLM finetuning.

## Results

Across 13 multi-label prediction tasks on four ubiquitous health datasets (LifeSnaps, GLOBEM, CoSt, PWUD), MuHBoost and its variants achieve the best or near-best rankings (1–7 out of 30) on all three MLC metrics (Hamming accuracy, micro-F1, macro-F1), while all baselines rank 7 or worse. With auxiliary data incorporated, MuHBoost[CC] attains HA of 0.739, miF1 of 0.843, and maF1 of 0.830 on LifeSnaps+; MuHBoost[LP+] leads on the larger-Q PWUD+ task (Q=6) with HA 0.688, miF1 0.784, maF1 0.771. Traditional ML baselines (RF, XGBoost, MLkNN, MLTSVM) consistently rank in the bottom half (18–30), and even zero-/few-shot LLM prompting ranks 7–19, confirming the advantage of the boosting-based aggregation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuHBoost and both variants (LP+, CC) dominate all 12 baselines across every dataset and metric. Among LLM-based baselines, 10-shot[LP+] is the strongest (ranks 6–14), but still trails MuHBoost[CC] by 3–6 rank positions on most metrics; zero-shot methods rank 10–19, confirming that single-pass LLM inference is insufficient for these heterogeneous, small-sample health tasks. Classical ML baselines (RF, XGBoost, MLkNN, MLTSVM) consistently rank 18–30, with RF[LP] and XGBoost[LP] performing worst on PWUD (ranks 19–30), likely due to the extreme class imbalance (e.g., 58/10 negative/positive ratio for opioids). The CC variant excels for small-to-moderate Q (LifeSnaps Q=2, GLOBEM Q=3, CoSt Q=2), achieving rank 1 on all three metrics with auxiliary data, while LP+ takes rank 1 on the high-Q PWUD task (Q=6), suggesting that the simpler positive-label-only inference directive better mitigates LLM hallucination when many labels must be predicted simultaneously. Incorporating auxiliary data improves all methods, but the gain is most pronounced for the MuHBoost family (e.g., MuHBoost[CC] on LifeSnaps improves from rank [4,5,4] to [1,1,1]), whereas classical ML methods see little or no benefit, consistent with overfitting on small, high-dimensional data.

## Relation to Prior Work

MuHBoost extends SummaryBoost (Manikandan et al., 2023), which uses LLM-generated summaries as weak learners in an AdaBoost pipeline for tabular data, to the multi-label classification setting for longitudinal health data. It departs from prior LLM-based health prediction works (Liu et al. 2023; Englhardt et al. 2024; Kim et al. 2024) by addressing two practical gaps: handling heterogeneous data types (categorical, mixed-type, high missingness) rather than only numerical time series, and jointly predicting multiple related outcomes via MLC to reduce API call costs. The two variants draw on established MLC literature—subset 0/1 loss from Nam et al. (2017) for the LP+ variant and AdaBoost.C2 from Li et al. (2023) for the CC variant—while the data conversion step incorporates the extract-then-generate pipeline from Zhang et al. (2023) to mitigate LLM hallucination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a resource-efficient, interpretable framework for multi-outcome prediction from real-world ubiquitous health data, which prior LLM-based approaches could not handle due to their restriction to numerical time series and per-task model construction. The practical implication is significant for just-in-time intervention systems (e.g., for PWUDs or at-risk students) where multiple comorbid outcomes must be predicted simultaneously under budget constraints. However, the evaluation is limited to four small datasets (N=48–130), the methods depend on commercial LLM APIs with nontrivial per-call costs, and the authors explicitly caution against on-the-fly clinical use, positioning the system as a decision-aid complementing human experts. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger cohorts, whether the specific LLM (GPT-3.5) is critical, and how performance degrades as missingness rates increase beyond what was observed.
