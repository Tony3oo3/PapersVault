## Problem

Existing model-merging methods (e.g., Task Arithmetic, TIES-merging) optimize a single scalar objective—typically the average accuracy across tasks—thereby ignoring the inherent trade-offs and conflicts between task objectives. In practice, practitioners need a *set* of solutions with varying trade-offs (a Pareto front) to match diverse preferences, but exhaustively evaluating merged models to find this front is computationally prohibitive, especially as the number of tasks grows.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained model θ_pre and N fine-tuned models θ_ft^n (same architecture), from which it computes task vectors v_n = θ_ft^n − θ_pre. It requires only K expensive forward-pass evaluations of merged models (K ranges from 30 to 250 depending on N) to fit the surrogate, rather than the exponential grid search a brute-force approach would need. The output is a Pareto front: a set of scaling-coefficient vectors c ∈ ℝ^N, each corresponding to a merged model θ_m(c) = θ_pre + Σ c_n v_n that is non-dominated in task performance. Users can then select any point on the front matching their preference, or use the front to understand the trade-off landscape without pre-specifying preferences.

## Method

MAP approximates each task's evaluation metric M_n(c) with a quadratic surrogate M̃_n(c) = ½cᵀA_n c + b_nᵀc + e_n, justified by a second-order Taylor expansion around θ_pre whose remainder is negligible because task-vector norms are only ~1–2% of pretrained parameter norms. Crucially, the surrogate parameters (A_n, b_n, e_n) are *not* obtained from gradients or Hessians; instead they are fitted by ordinary least squares on K sampled (c, M_n) pairs, reducing the problem to a small linear regression with (N+1)(N+2)/2 parameters per task. A many-objective optimizer (NSGA-III) is then applied to the N quadratic surrogates to extract the amortized Pareto front. Two variants further reduce cost: Bayesian MAP uses UCB-guided adaptive sampling over angular bins of the coefficient space for N ≤ 3, and Nested-merging MAP merges models pairwise in a tree structure, cutting total evaluations from O(N·2^N) to O(N log N) for large N.

## Results

On the preference-weighted sum of accuracies metric across 8 zero-shot image classification tasks (ViT-B/32/CLIP), MAP achieves 72.96±2.52, outperforming all model-merging baselines including TIES-merging (72.24), Ada-Merging++ (72.55), and MOEA/D (68.59). The quadratic surrogate model achieves R² values of 0.87–0.98 across 2–8 tasks, and MAP's win rate over brute-force direct search grows from ~50% (N=2, where direct search is ground truth) to 65.58% (N=8, where direct search is no longer ground truth). MAP requires only 30–250 scaling coefficient evaluations versus 200–1000 for direct search, achieving a >95% reduction in evaluations compared to MOEA/D (60 vs. 2500 evaluations for 2 tasks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAP consistently outperforms all 9 model-merging baselines across all task counts (2–8). Against the strongest merging baselines, MAP leads TIES-merging by 0.72 points (8 tasks: 72.96 vs. 72.24), Ada-Merging++ by 0.41 points (72.96 vs. 72.55), and MOEA/D by 4.37 points (72.96 vs. 68.59). The gap widens substantially for Task Arithmetic and DARE-TA, which degrade severely at higher task counts (e.g., TA drops to 39.79 at 7 tasks vs. MAP's 73.40). MAP also outperforms Model soups by 8.6 points at 8 tasks (72.96 vs. 64.36). The only methods that exceed MAP are single-task models and joint MTL training, which serve as upper bounds since they do not face the merging constraint. In terms of computational efficiency, MAP uses only 60 evaluations for 2 tasks versus 2500 for MOEA/D (>95% reduction), and its nested-merging variant reduces complexity from O(N·2^N) to O(N log N), achieving a 250× speedup at N=8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022) and its extensions (TIES-merging, DARE, SLERP), which compute weighted averages of task vectors but optimize a single scalar objective (e.g., average accuracy). It departs from this by reframing model merging as a multi-objective optimization problem over scaling coefficients, drawing on Pareto front estimation from multi-task learning (Sener & Koltun, 2018; Lin et al., 2019) and many-objective evolutionary algorithms (NSGA-III, MOEA/D). The key methodological departure is the use of a quadratic surrogate model—motivated by the lazy-training observation that fine-tuned weights remain close to pretrained weights—to amortize the expensive evaluation cost, combined with Bayesian adaptive sampling and a nested-merging scheme to further reduce compute. Unlike prior Pareto multi-task learning methods that require gradient-based optimization of full network parameters, MAP operates entirely in the low-dimensional scaling-coefficient space post-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAP provides practitioners with a flexible set of trade-off solutions rather than a single merged model, which is more aligned with real-world deployment where preferences across tasks vary (e.g., hospital specialties, multilingual LLMs). The computational efficiency is notable: only 30–250 expensive model evaluations are needed regardless of the number of tasks, and the surrogate model has just (N+1)(N+2)/2 parameters (45 for 8 tasks) versus millions in the underlying DNNs. Key limitations include the absence of a detector for whether tasks exhibit meaningful trade-offs (degenerate single-point Pareto 'fronts' can occur), and the quadratic approximation may break down for more aggressive fine-tuning where task vectors are no longer small relative to pretrained weights. Open questions include whether the approach extends to merging models with different architectures or to settings where the evaluation metric is stochastic and noisy.
