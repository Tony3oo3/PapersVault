## Problem

Existing representation similarity metrics like CKA rely on statistical properties of entire datasets, making them sensitive to outliers and unable to capture how individual samples' representations evolve across layers. The paper asks whether a simple per-sample metric can capture layer-wise representational similarity in transformers, and whether this similarity can be actively enhanced during training to make shallow layers more effective for early prediction and efficient inference.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard transformer inputs—32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10), 224×224 images (ImageNet-1K), or text sequences of length 256 (GLUE, WikiText-103)—and produces class labels or generated tokens. Unusually, the single last-layer linear classifier is applied to every hidden layer's representation, yielding layer-wise predictions and cosine similarity scores for each sample at each depth. For multi-exit inference, the model exits early when the max softmax confidence exceeds a threshold, producing a variable-depth computation per sample.

## Method

The paper introduces a sample-wise cosine similarity metric that, due to residual connections eliminating rotation ambiguity in transformers, aligns with the more complex CKA and is sufficient to capture layer-wise representational similarity. Under a geodesic curve assumption in Wasserstein space (justified by weight-decay training of residual networks), Theorem 1 proves that cosine similarity to the last layer increases monotonically across layers, and Theorem 2 shows this implies monotonically increasing predicted probability, providing a theoretical justification for saturation events. The key training contribution is an aligned loss that replaces the standard last-layer-only CE loss with a weighted sum of CE losses computed by a single shared classifier applied to every layer's features, with linearly increasing weights (λ_ℓ = 2ℓ/(L(L+1))) to emphasize deeper layers. This aligned training enhances layer-wise similarity, promotes earlier saturation events, reduces the ε-effective depth, and enables multi-exit inference with a single classifier—achieving on-par accuracy and speedup with multi-classifier baselines while saving up to 78% of classifier parameters for large-vocabulary models.

## Results

On ImageNet with DeiT-S, the aligned training multi-exit model achieves 77.96% top-1 accuracy with a 1.36× speedup, comparable to the multi-exit/classifiers baseline (78.32% accuracy, 1.42× speedup) while using a single shared classifier instead of 12 separate ones. On CIFAR-10, aligned training reduces the ε-effective depth from 12 (standard training) to approximately 7, meaning the model reaches near-full accuracy at layer 7 rather than requiring all 12 layers. For NLP, AlignedBERT outperforms standard BERT in layer-wise F1/accuracy across all GLUE tasks (SST-2, MRPC, QQP, MNLI, QNLI, RTE), and AlignedGPT achieves higher prediction accuracy and lower perplexity than standard GPT-2 at every intermediate layer on WikiText-103.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the multi-exit/classifiers baseline (BerXit-style, Xin et al. 2021), aligned training with a single shared classifier achieves on-par layer-wise accuracy (77.96% vs. 78.32% on ImageNet) and a comparable 1.36× speedup (vs. 1.42×), while eliminating the need for 11 additional classifiers and the KL-divergence regularization term. Aligned training also produces higher layer-wise cosine similarity than the multi-exit/classifiers approach. Against standard BERT fine-tuning, AlignedBERT consistently achieves higher layer-wise F1/accuracy at every layer across all six evaluated GLUE tasks (SST-2, MRPC, QQP, MNLI, QNLI, RTE), with the gap most pronounced in shallow layers. Against standard GPT-2 fine-tuning, AlignedGPT achieves higher prediction accuracy and lower perplexity at every intermediate layer on WikiText-103, and generates text with higher coherence and diversity when exiting from shallower layers, improving inference efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) representation similarity analysis, where it departs from statistical metrics like CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019) by showing that per-sample cosine similarity suffices for transformers due to residual connections eliminating rotation ambiguity; (2) Neural Collapse (Papyan et al., 2020), whose self-duality property (NC3) directly motivates deploying a single shared classifier across all layers; and (3) multi-exit inference (Xin et al., 2021; Geng et al., 2021), where prior work requires separate classifiers per exit. The theoretical grounding draws on the geodesic curve characterization of ResNet training in Wasserstein space (Gai & Zhang, 2021), extended here to transformers via the residual update structure. The key conceptual departure is replacing the multi-classifier multi-exit paradigm with a single-classifier design, justified by the observed monotonic alignment of layer features with the last-layer classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-classifier multi-exit result has direct practical impact: it eliminates the parameter overhead of per-layer classifiers (up to 78% savings for GPT-2 with 50K vocabulary), making early-exit inference more deployable for large language models. The aligned training method is remarkably simple—just a weighted sum of cross-entropy losses across layers—yet it simultaneously improves layer-wise accuracy, promotes early saturation events, and reveals the minimal effective depth for a task. Open questions include whether the geodesic curve assumption holds precisely in practice (the authors acknowledge it may not), whether the approach extends to architectures with varying feature dimensions across layers, and how it integrates with nested-width approaches like MatFormer. The modest speedup (1.36× vs. 1.42× for multi-classifier) suggests the practical benefit is primarily in parameter efficiency rather than raw latency reduction.
