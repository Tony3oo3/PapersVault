## Problem

Existing dense self-supervised methods provide only binary attract/repel signals (contrastive) or rely on pooled object-level representations, failing to capture the fine-grained ordering of patch-level nearest neighbors that is critical for in-context scene understanding. While nearest-neighbor retrieval has proven effective as an evaluation protocol for scene understanding, it has not been leveraged as a training objective to shape the feature space itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253651/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image, from which two augmented views (global 518×518 and local 98×98 crops for DINOv2-based models, or 224×224 and 96×96 for others) are generated. These views are processed by a student-teacher ViT pair, and the resulting spatial tokens are aligned via ROI-Align to a common grid. The output is the improved dense patch-level spatial tokens from the ViT backbone (projection head discarded), which can be directly used for frozen clustering, linear segmentation, in-context retrieval, or end-to-end fine-tuning. Reference patches for distance computation are sampled from other images in the batch, making the method batch-dependent.

## Method

NeCo enforces patch-level nearest-neighbor ordering consistency between two augmented views of the same image. For each aligned patch, pairwise cosine distances to a set of reference patches (sampled from other batch images) are computed for both student and teacher features. These distance matrices are then sorted using a differentiable sorting network (Bitonic sort with arctan-based soft min/max relaxation), producing approximate permutation matrices that encode the probability of each reference patch being the k-th nearest neighbor. The training loss is a cross-entropy between the teacher's and student's permutation matrices, forcing the ordering of nearest neighbors to be consistent across views. This is applied as a lightweight 'dense post-pretraining' step on top of existing pretrained backbones (DINOv2-registers, DINO, iBOT, CrIBo, etc.), requiring only ~19 GPU hours on a single A6000, and operates strictly in the deep feature space to avoid the pitfalls of pixel-level reconstruction.

## Results

NeCo (ViT-B/14, DINOv2R init) achieves 86.4 mIoU on COCO-Things, 64.1 on COCO-Stuff, 84.4 on Pascal VOC, and 46.5 on ADE20K for linear segmentation, outperforming DINOv2R by up to 4.8% and CrIBo by up to 10%. For in-context scene understanding, NeCo surpasses CrIBo and DINOv2R by 4–13% on Pascal VOC and ADE20K across all data fractions. On unsupervised semantic segmentation (Pascal VOC), NeCo reaches 55.6 mIoU, exceeding COMUS (50.0) and DINOv2R (35.1). For 3D understanding on SPair-71k, NeCo improves DINOv2R-B14 multiview feature consistency by ~10% on average (32.5→34.3 Recall@0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeCo consistently outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every evaluation protocol. Against the strongest dense self-supervised baselines, NeCo beats CrIBo by 3.4–14.5% (clustering, linear segmentation, and in-context) and DINOv2R by 1.5–4.8% (linear segmentation, full fine-tuning, and in-context). On unsupervised semantic segmentation (Pascal VOC), NeCo (55.6 mIoU) surpasses COMUS (50.0), DINOSAUR (37.2), DeepSpectral (37.2), MaskContrast (35.1), and DINOv2R (35.1) by 5.6–20.5 points. For in-context scene understanding, NeCo outperforms Hummingbird (Balazevic et al., 2023) by ~13% on Pascal VOC at 1/128 fraction and CrIBo by 4–13% across all data fractions. NeCo also improves upon image-level pretraining baselines (DINO, iBOT, MAE) by 10–30% in clustering and 15–40% in linear segmentation. Notably, NeCo extends to vision-language models, improving CLIP by 12–37% and SigLIP by 12–37% on linear segmentation and in-context tasks, and boosts DINOv2R's 3D multiview consistency on SPair-71k by ~10% on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeCo extends the dense self-supervised learning line (DINO, iBOT, CrIBo, Leopart, TimeT) by introducing a patch-level differentiable sorting loss that enforces nearest-neighbor ordering consistency between student and teacher views. It departs from CrIBo and Hummingbird, which enforce nearest-neighbor consistency at the pooled image or object level, by operating directly on individual patch features and adding the richer ordering signal from differentiable sorting networks (Petersen et al., 2021). The 'dense post-pretraining' framing—starting from an already image-level pretrained model and adapting it with a dense loss in ~19 GPU hours—is a practical departure from prior work that trains dense objectives from scratch or via learnable pooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's practical efficiency (19 GPU-hours on a single GPU to improve upon DINOv2R, which was trained on 142M images) makes dense post-pretraining accessible to groups without large compute budgets, and its generality across six vision backbones plus CLIP/SigLIP suggests broad adoption potential. Open questions include whether the gains persist at ViT-Large or larger scales, whether the sorting-based loss can be integrated into the original pretraining phase rather than applied as a post-hoc step, and whether the modest full-finetuning gains over DINOv2R (≤1.7%) indicate a ceiling for this approach on supervised downstream tasks. The method's reliance on multi-crop augmentations and ROI-Align for spatial alignment also constrains its applicability to architectures without natural patch-grid structure.
