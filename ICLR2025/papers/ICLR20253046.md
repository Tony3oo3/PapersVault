## Problem

While commercial image and video generation services have begun deploying copyright safeguards (e.g., DALL-E's prompt rewriting), no prior work has systematically quantified how easily users can trigger generation of copyrighted characters—especially through indirect prompts that omit the character's name—or measured how effectively existing mitigations suppress such outputs while preserving user intent. This gap is critical because at least one lawsuit has already awarded damages for AI-generated copyrighted characters, yet the robustness of deployed defenses remains untested.

## Input / Output

The evaluation framework takes text prompts in three forms—explicit character names, indirect keyword sets (e.g., 5 LAION co-occurrence keywords), or 60-word GPT-4-generated descriptions—along with optional negative prompts (character name plus ranked anchoring keywords), and feeds them to five image models (Playground v2.5, SDXL, PixArt-α, DeepFloyd IF, DALL-E 3) and one video model (VideoFusion). Each generated output is scored on two axes: DETECT, a binary GPT-4V judgment of whether a specific copyrighted character is recognizable in the image (82.5% accuracy, Cohen's κ=0.648 vs. human majority), and CONS, a VQAScore (CLIP-FlanT5 backbone) probability that the image exhibits the character's key general characteristics (e.g., 'cartoon mouse' for Mickey Mouse). The evaluation set comprises 50 characters from 18 studios spanning U.S. and international franchises.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-stage indirect-anchor identification pipeline: GPT-4 generates 50 candidate keywords and 60-word descriptions per character, which are then ranked by three methods—EMBEDDINGSIM (cosine similarity in the image model's text encoder space), CO-OCCURRENCE (frequency of co-occurrence with the character name in LAION-2B, C4, OpenWebText, and The Pile), and LM-RANKED (greedy-decoded keyword list). The key empirical finding is that as few as 2–5 LAION co-occurrence keywords (e.g., 'videogame, plumber' for Mario) reliably trigger character generation across all tested models, bypassing name-based content filters. For mitigation, the paper combines DALL-E-style prompt rewriting (simulated via GPT-4 with the extracted system prompt) with negative prompting that includes the target character's name plus 5 EMBEDDINGSIM and 5 CO-OCCURRENCE-LAION keywords, reducing DETECT from 30.3 to 4.3 on Playground v2.5 while keeping CONS stable at ~0.81. Statistical significance is confirmed via one-tailed t-tests (p<0.05), and the combined strategy generalizes across all five open-source models, with DeepFloyd IF showing a 94% reduction in detected characters.

## Results

The proposed combination of prompt rewriting and negative prompts (target's name + 5 EMBEDDINGSIM + 5 CO-OCCURRENCE-LAION keywords) reduces DETECT from 30.33 to 4.33 on Playground v2.5 (a 86% reduction) while slightly improving CONS from 0.75 to 0.81. Across all five models, DETECT drops to as low as 1.67 (SDXL) and 2.00 (DeepFloyd IF), representing a 95% reduction, with CONS remaining stable (0.72–0.81). Prompt rewriting alone only reduces DETECT from 30 to 14 on Playground v2.5, confirming it is insufficient without negative prompting. As few as 2 generic keywords (e.g., 'videogame, plumber') can trigger generation of a specific copyrighted character like Mario, and 5 LAION co-occurrence keywords nearly match the effectiveness of 60-word descriptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three mitigation strategies on Playground v2.5: (1) prompt rewriting alone, (2) negative prompts alone, and (3) their combination. Prompt rewriting alone reduces DETECT from 30.33 to 14.33 but leaves 14 out of 50 characters still generated; the failure analysis shows that rewritten prompts retaining LAION co-occurrence keywords (avg. 0.667 vs. 0.387 in success cases) are more likely to fail. Negative prompts alone (target's name + 5 EMBEDDINGSIM + 5 CO-OCCURRENCE-LAION keywords) reduce DETECT from 30.33 to 20.67 with the character name as prompt, but the combination with prompt rewriting achieves the strongest result at 4.33. Across models, the combination strategy is most effective on SDXL (DETECT 1.67) and DeepFloyd IF (DETECT 2.00), while VideoFusion shows the weakest mitigation (DETECT 11.33), suggesting video models retain stronger character associations. The CONS metric remains stable or slightly improves across all configurations, indicating the mitigation does not significantly degrade user experience.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of work studying memorization and copyright in generative models (Carlini et al. 2023; Somepalli et al. 2023) but shifts focus from verbatim data-point regurgitation to the legally distinct challenge of character-level substantial similarity. It draws on legal scholarship (Sag 2023; Henderson et al. 2023; Lee et al. 2024) to motivate the evaluation metrics, and leverages VQAScore (Lin et al. 2024) for consistency scoring and WIMBD-style co-occurrence indexing (Elazar et al. 2023) for identifying indirect anchors. Unlike prior concept-erasure or unlearning approaches (Kumari et al. 2023; Li et al. 2024b; Zhang et al. 2023) that modify model weights, this work focuses exclusively on inference-time interventions (prompt rewriting and negative prompting), making it directly deployable without retraining. It also extends the jailbreaking analyses of Kim et al. (2024) by systematically studying indirect anchoring as a phenomenon and pairing it with mitigation evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, dual-metric evaluation framework (DETECT + CONS) for copyrighted character generation, giving model deployers a concrete tool to measure the trade-off between copyright protection and user experience. The finding that industry-standard prompt rewriting reduces detected characters only from 30 to 14, while adding negative prompts drops it to 4–5 with minimal consistency loss, offers an immediately actionable deployment recipe. Open questions remain: whether optimization-based methods can discover more complex indirect anchors, how to handle compound prompts that implicitly reference characters, and whether these metrics generalize to trademarks and less-popular characters. The 82.5% accuracy of the GPT-4V-based detector and the 50-character evaluation set are acknowledged limitations that future work should address.
