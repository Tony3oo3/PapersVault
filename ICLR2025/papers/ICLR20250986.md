## Problem

While virtual nodes (VNs) are widely used to augment MPNNs with global information, no formal analysis exists on how and to what extent VNs mitigate oversquashing. Additionally, unlike Graph Transformers, standard VN formulations assign uniform sensitivity to all nodes, preventing them from learning heterogeneous node importance—a key capability that explains the empirical gap between VNs and GTs on certain tasks.

## Input / Output

The method operates on unweighted, undirected graphs with node feature vectors (molecular graphs with ~25 nodes, image graphs with ~70–120 super-pixel nodes, or function call graphs with up to 5,000 nodes) and produces a single graph-level prediction via mean pooling. The VN is connected to all n nodes, giving the architecture O(|E|+n) complexity per layer—linear in graph size—compared to the O(n²) cost of full attention in Graph Transformers. The framework is evaluated on both classification (e.g., Peptides-Func, MolHIV, PPA) and regression (Peptides-Struct) graph-level tasks.

## Method

The paper provides a spectral characterization (Theorem 3.1) showing that adding a VN changes the commute time between nodes i and j by a quantity determined by the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian, proving that VNs reduce commute time (and hence oversquashing) on real-world graphs. A sensitivity analysis (Proposition 4.1) then reveals that standard MPNN+VN layers are homogeneous: the Jacobian ∂h_i/∂h_k is independent of k for nodes beyond 2 hops, unlike GTs where attention weights vary per node pair. To close this gap, the authors propose MPNN+VN_G, which asynchronously interleaves a local MPNN update with a global VN update computed over the local representations, so that the global aggregator inherits heterogeneous node scores from the message functions ψ_ij. This retains the O(|E|+n) complexity of standard VNs while enabling node-specific relevance in the global update, and consistently outperforms MPNN+VN—most notably on CIFAR-10 (8.25% improvement) where node heterogeneity is empirically critical.

## Results

GatedGCN+PE+VN_G achieves the best performance on Peptides-Struct (0.2458 MAE), MNIST (98.626% acc.), ogbg-molhiv (79.10% acc.), and ogbg-ppa (81.17% acc.) among all compared methods. It improves over the standard GatedGCN+PE+VN baseline on every task, with the largest relative gain on CIFAR-10 (76.080% vs. 70.280%, an 8.25% improvement). On the OGB benchmarks, VN_G also leads on MolPCBA (29.17% acc.), surpassing GPS (29.07%) and CRaWL (29.86% on MolPCBA only). The method maintains O(|E|+n) computational complexity while matching or exceeding quadratic-complexity Graph Transformers on most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VN_G consistently outperforms the standard GatedGCN+PE+VN on all eight benchmarks, with the most dramatic gain on CIFAR-10 (76.08% vs. 70.28%), confirming the paper's theory that tasks requiring heterogeneous node scoring benefit most from the VN_G formulation. Against the GPS Graph Transformer, VN_G wins on Peptides-Struct (0.2458 vs. 0.2509), MNIST (98.63% vs. 98.05%), CIFAR-10 (76.08% vs. 72.30%), MalNet-Tiny (93.67 vs. 93.50), and all three OGB tasks (MolHIV 79.10 vs. 78.80; MolPCBA 29.17 vs. 29.07; PPA 81.17 vs. 80.15), while trailing on Peptides-Func (0.6822 vs. 0.6534—actually VN_G wins here too). The only clear losses are to DRew on Peptides-Func (0.6822 vs. 0.7150) and to GRIT on CIFAR-10 (76.08% vs. 76.47%), both narrow gaps. Compared to Exphormer, VN_G leads on Peptides-Struct, CIFAR-10, and all OGB tasks but trails slightly on MNIST (98.63% vs. 98.41%—actually VN_G wins) and MalNet-Tiny (93.67 vs. 94.02). Overall, VN_G matches or exceeds the strongest Graph Transformer baselines at linear rather than quadratic complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the commute-time/mixing framework of Di Giovanni et al. (2024) to provide the first systematic characterization of how virtual nodes affect oversquashing, showing the improvement is governed by the Laplacian spectrum of the input graph. It departs from Cai et al. (2023), who showed MPNN+VN can approximate self-attention in a non-uniform regime, by revealing a fundamental homogeneity limitation of standard VN (Proposition 4.1) that their analysis did not capture. The proposed VN_G formulation sits between the fully homogeneous MPNN+VN and the fully heterogeneous Graph Transformer, using asynchronous interleaving of local and global updates to learn node-dependent sensitivity at the same O(|E|+n) cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The homogeneity result provides a clear diagnostic for when VN will underperform GTs: tasks requiring heterogeneous node scoring in the global update. VN_G offers a practical, drop-in improvement that closes this gap (e.g., 8.25% on CIFAR10) without the quadratic cost of attention, making it a strong default for graph-level tasks. The spectral characterization of commute-time reduction gives practitioners a principled way to predict whether adding a VN will help on a given graph. Open questions include whether the linear-setting expressivity results (Appendix B) extend to nonlinear MPNNs, and how VN_G compares to more sophisticated rewiring or attention-based approaches on tasks where heterogeneity is extreme.
