## Problem

Existing RL-based recommender systems rely on standard exploration strategies (e.g., ε-greedy, entropy-based) that are ineffective in large item spaces with sparse reward signals, causing them to overfit to users' short-term interests and fail to discover items reflecting long-term preferences. This leads to suboptimal policies that cannot maximize expected reward over the long term, as the agent cannot systematically identify informative, uncertain items beyond current observations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes user interaction history as Word2Vec item embeddings, a sliding window of length 2N that advances N items per step (replacing the first N slots with the previous recommendation list and filling the rest with newly observed items), and a full item pool. It produces a top-N ranked recommendation list scored by a combined exploitation-exploration score (predicted rating plus vacuity weighted by log-position), along with per-item Dirichlet evidence vectors that yield both a rating prediction and a vacuity uncertainty score. During testing, items absent from the user's history receive a neutral rating equal to the positive threshold τ.

## Method

ECQL integrates two components: a Sequential State Encoder (SSE) that recursively aggregates the previous state, sliding-window items, and newly recommended items into a dynamic state vector, and a Conservative Evidential Actor-Critic (CEAC) module with three networks—an action network (Gaussian policy), a critic network (double-DQN-style conservative evidential Q-value), and an evidence network (Dirichlet rating prediction). The key exploration mechanism replaces ε-greedy or entropy with vacuity from Subjective Logic: items with high vacuity (low evidence) are prioritized as informative for discovering long-term interests, while a conservative regularizer penalizes the target policy's Q-value and boosts the behavior policy's Q-value to prevent deviation from confirmed user preferences. The evidential reward augments the standard rating reward with a vacuity-based information-gain term, and the paper proves that the resulting conservative Q-value lower-bounds the true state value and that the uncertainty regularizer acts as an importance weight adjusting optimism toward the behavior policy, yielding a ζ-safe policy improvement guarantee.

## Results

ECQL achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four datasets, with P@5 of 0.6313 (MovieLens-1M), 0.6379 (MovieLens-100K), 0.6336 (Netflix), and 0.6232 (Yahoo! Music), outperforming the best RL baseline ResAct (0.6188, 0.6195, 0.6175, 0.6215) and the best non-RL baseline DCNv2 (0.6152, 0.6158, 0.5784, 0.5715). On the test return metric (R@T), ECQL reaches 68.54 (ML-1M), 42.31 (ML-100K), 70.19 (Netflix), and 43.59 (Yahoo), surpassing the next-best ResAct by 6.2, 3.35, 5.52, and 3.65 points respectively. On the offline NCIS metric, ECQL scores 4.124 (ML-1M) and 4.223 (Netflix), exceeding ResAct (4.035, 4.126) by approximately 2–2.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECQL outperforms all 25 baselines across all four datasets on every reported metric. Among RL-based methods, ECQL surpasses the strongest competitor ResAct by 1.25% P@5 and 6.2 points in test return on MovieLens-1M, and by 1.61% P@5 and 5.52 points R@T on Netflix. Against the best bandit baseline CoLin, ECQL gains 1.01% P@5 and 9.67 points R@T on ML-1M. Compared to the top sequential model CL4SRec (0.6135 P@5 on ML-1M), ECQL improves by 1.78% P@5, and versus the best deep learning model DCNv2 (0.6152 P@5), by 1.61%. On the offline NCIS metric, ECQL leads ResAct (the next-best) by 0.089 on ML-1M (4.124 vs. 4.035) and by 0.097 on Netflix (4.223 vs. 4.126), confirming superior long-term policy quality. The gap widens in later time steps (Figure 3), indicating that ECQL's vacuity-guided exploration accumulates knowledge more effectively than ε-greedy, SAC (entropy-based), or bandit-based exploration strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Conservative Q-Learning (CQL, Kumar et al. 2020) to an evidential setting by replacing the standard reward with an evidential reward that incorporates vacuity (a second-order uncertainty from Subjective Logic) as an exploration signal, and by using Double DQN (Hasselt 2010) for the critic. It departs from prior RL-based recommendation work (ε-greedy, SAC entropy-based exploration) by arguing that vacuity—measuring lack of evidence rather than distributional spread—is the appropriate uncertainty for discovering items beyond current observations that reflect long-term user interests. The Sequential State Encoder (SSE) draws on sliding-window architectures from sequential recommendation but repurposes them to maintain a dynamic MDP state that blends past, current, and RL-explored items.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled alternative to entropy-based exploration in RL recommendation, with theoretical guarantees (lower-bounded policy value, ζ-safe policy improvement) that are absent in most prior RL-RS work. The key open question is scalability: experiments use pre-trained Word2Vec embeddings and datasets up to ~10M interactions, leaving unclear whether the evidential framework and its concentration-based theoretical bounds hold at web-scale item pools. Additionally, the reliance on ground-truth ratings as reward during training (with a neutral rating for unseen items at test time) limits direct applicability to implicit-feedback settings where no explicit rating signal exists.
