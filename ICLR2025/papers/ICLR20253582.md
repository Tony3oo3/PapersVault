## Problem

Existing optimal second-order methods for convex-concave minimax problems (NPE, OGDA-2, Perseus) achieve the best-known iteration complexity of O(ε^{-2/3}) but require computing a fresh Hessian and solving a d×d linear system at every iteration, incurring O(d³) cost per step. This paper asks whether the Hessian can be reused across multiple iterations to reduce the total computational cost while preserving the optimal convergence rate.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a twice-differentiable minimax objective f(x,y) with ρ-Lipschitz continuous Hessians, an initial point z_0, and three hyperparameters: total iterations T, Hessian-reuse interval m (the snapshot frequency), and cubic regularization parameter M. The Hessian ∇F(z) is evaluated only at snapshot points every m iterations, where a Schur decomposition is precomputed; between snapshots, only O(d²) back-substitution is needed per step. The output is an ergodic (γ-weighted) average of intermediate iterates z_{t+1/2}, which constitutes an ε-saddle point in the C-C setting or, with restarts, an ε-root in the SC-SC setting.

## Method

LEN (Lazy Extra Newton) performs two sub-steps per iteration: an implicit cubic-regularized Newton step that solves (∇F(z_{π(t)}) + γ_t I)(z_t − z_{t+1/2}) = F(z_t) using the Hessian cached at the most recent snapshot z_{π(t)}, followed by an explicit extra-gradient step z_{t+1} = z_t − γ_t^{-1} F(z_{t+1/2}). The key technical challenge is bounding the accumulated error from the stale Hessian, which cannot be handled by the simple triangle-inequality argument used in prior lazy-Hessian work because the extra-gradient step decouples the Newton progress from the Hessian staleness distance. The authors introduce a summation inequality (Lemma 4.2) that absorbs the accumulated lazy-Hessian error into the negative descent terms when M ≥ 3ρm. For SC-SC problems, LEN-restart wraps LEN in a restart loop with T = O(m^{2/3}κ^{2/3}) inner iterations per epoch, yielding superlinear (3/2)^s contraction in the distance to the saddle point. With the optimal choice m = Θ(d), the total computational complexity improves from Õ((N+d²)d·ε^{-2/3}) to Õ((N+d²)(d + d^{2/3}ε^{-2/3})), a factor of d^{1/3} speedup over all prior second-order methods.

## Results

For convex-concave (C-C) minimax problems, LEN with m=Θ(d) achieves a total computational complexity of O~((N+d²)(d+d^(2/3)ε^(-2/3))), improving over the state-of-the-art NPE and other optimal second-order methods (which require O~((N+d²)dε^(-2/3))) by a factor of d^(1/3). For strongly-convex-strongly-concave (SC-SC) problems, LEN-restart achieves O~((N+d²)(d+d^(2/3)κ^(2/3))) where κ=ρ/μ is the condition number, again improving prior methods by d^(1/3). Numerical experiments on a regularized bilinear min-max problem (n∈{10,100,200}) and fairness-aware learning tasks (heart, adult, law school datasets) confirm that LEN with m=10 consistently outperforms both EG and NPE in wall-clock running time to reach a given gradient norm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against NPE (Monteiro & Svaiter, 2012) and its variants (search-free NPE, OGDA-2, Perseus), all of which achieve the same optimal iteration complexity of O(ε^(-2/3)) but require a fresh Hessian at every iteration, yielding O~((N+d²)dε^(-2/3)) total cost. LEN strictly improves this by a factor of d^(1/3) by reusing the Hessian every m=Θ(d) iterations, reducing the Hessian oracle calls from O(ε^(-2/3)) to O(ε^(-2/3)/d + 1). Against the first-order baseline EG (Korpelevich, 1976), LEN retains the superior O(ε^(-2/3)) iteration rate versus EG's O(ε⁻¹), while also being computationally cheaper per iteration than NPE. In the SC-SC setting, LEN-restart matches the O((ρ/μ)^(2/3)) iteration rate of OGDA-2, ARE-restart, and Perseus-restart but again saves a d^(1/3) factor in total computational cost. Empirically (Figures 1–2), LEN with m=10 consistently reaches lower gradient norms faster than both EG and NPE across all tested problem sizes and datasets, with the gap widening as dimension increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEN extends the Newton Proximal Extragradient (NPE) framework of Monteiro & Svaiter (2012) by incorporating the lazy Hessian update strategy previously developed for minimization problems by Doikov et al. (2023) and Chayti et al. (2023). The key conceptual departure is that, unlike prior second-order minimax methods (NPE, OGDA-2, Perseus) which compute a fresh Hessian at every iteration, LEN reuses the Hessian from a snapshot point updated every m iterations while still performing an extra-gradient step. This introduces a non-trivial analytical challenge: the progress from Newton sub-steps no longer directly bounds the accumulated lazy-Hessian error, and the non-symmetric Jacobian in the minimax setting requires Schur decomposition rather than eigendecomposition for efficient sub-problem solving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The d^{1/3} computational speedup over state-of-the-art second-order minimax methods is a clean and meaningful improvement for high-dimensional problems, reducing the dominant O(d) per-iteration Hessian cost to an amortized O(d^{2/3}) cost. However, the practical impact is tempered by the fact that each snapshot still requires an O(d^3) Schur decomposition, and the empirical validation is limited to small-scale bilinear and fairness-aware problems. Open questions include extending the lazy Hessian idea to nonconvex-nonconcave minimax, stochastic settings with sub-sampled Hessians, and adaptive second-order methods, all of which the authors identify as future directions.
