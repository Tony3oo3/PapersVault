## Problem

Long-context LLM inference is bottlenecked by KV cache memory that scales linearly with sequence length, making million-token contexts infeasible on a single GPU (e.g., Llama-3-8B at 1M tokens requires ~137 GB in FP16). Existing KV cache pruning methods (H2O, TOVA, StreamingLLM, FastGen) either degrade long-context retrieval accuracy or offer only modest efficiency gains, because they apply uniform compression across all attention heads without distinguishing which heads actually require full context access.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B/70B, Mistral-7B) and a long context text prompt as input, and produces generated text tokens while maintaining a compressed KV cache. A one-time offline optimization phase (2,000 steps on 8×A100) uses synthetic passkey-retrieval sequences embedded in BookSum texts to learn per-head gate values; at inference, each Transformer layer maintains two separate KV caches—a full-length cache for retrieval heads and a constant-size cache (16 sink + 64 recent tokens) for streaming heads—so memory usage becomes O(1) for the majority of heads regardless of context length.

## Method

DuoAttention's key insight is that only a small fraction of attention heads (retrieval heads) attend to contextually relevant tokens across the full sequence, while the majority (streaming heads) focus on attention sinks and recent tokens. To identify retrieval heads, the paper assigns a trainable scalar gate α_{i,j} ∈ [0,1] to each KV head that blends full-attention and streaming-attention (Λ-mask) outputs, then optimizes these gates via a distillation loss (L2 difference from the full-attention model's hidden states on passkey tokens) plus L1 regularization to encourage sparsity, with all other model parameters frozen. At deployment, gates are binarized at a threshold τ to assign each head to full or streaming attention, and Q/K/V projection channels are reordered so retrieval and streaming heads form contiguous blocks for efficient cache slicing. For chunked pre-filling, streaming heads attend only to a constant number of contextual tokens per chunk, reducing time complexity from O(L²) to O(LK) and memory from O(L) to O(K), while retrieval heads use standard FlashAttention-2.

## Results

On LongBench with Llama-3-8B-Instruct-1048K at a 50% KV cache budget, DuoAttention achieves an average score of 40.21, matching or exceeding full attention (40.08) while using half the KV memory. On Llama-2-7B-32K at a 25% KV budget, DuoAttention scores 34.49 vs. full attention's 37.52, still far ahead of all baselines. In efficiency terms, DuoAttention reduces decoding memory by up to 2.55× (MHA) and 1.67× (GQA), speeds up decoding by up to 2.18× and 1.50×, and accelerates pre-filling by up to 1.73× and 1.63×. Combined with 8-bit weight and 4-bit KV quantization, DuoAttention enables Llama-3-8B to process 3.3 million tokens on a single A100-80G GPU, a 6.4× capacity increase over full-attention BF16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuoAttention decisively outperforms all KV-cache compression baselines on both long- and short-context benchmarks. On LongBench (Llama-3-8B, 50% budget), DuoAttention (40.21 avg) beats H2O (35.76), TOVA (35.55), and StreamingLLM (32.26) by 4.5–8.0 points, and on Llama-2-7B (25% budget) it leads H2O (26.84), TOVA (29.78), and SLLM (27.80) by 4.7–7.7 points. On Needle-in-a-Haystack, all of H2O, TOVA, and StreamingLLM fail to retrieve the needle at most depths, while DuoAttention maintains near-perfect accuracy up to 1048K tokens. FastGen, which profiles attention heads to discard tokens, scores 32.82 (Llama-3-8B) and 19.01 (Llama-2-7B) on LongBench subsets versus DuoAttention's 40.01 and 32.81, and suffers OOM beyond 24K–32K context. Among the most recent baselines, DuoAttention edges out SnapKV (38.47), PyramidKV (38.39), and AdaKV (38.67) on LongBench, and critically, while SnapKV and PyramidKV degrade severely when the query is not at the end of the context (e.g., multi-turn dialogue), DuoAttention remains robust. On short-context tasks (MMLU, MBPP, MT-Bench), DuoAttention at 50% budget is near-lossless (e.g., Llama-3-70B: MMLU 79.35% vs. 79.38% full), whereas H2O drops MBPP from 47.85% to 32.12% and SLLM to 5.57%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the retrieval-heads concept introduced by Wu et al. (2024) and the attention-sinks/streaming paradigm from StreamingLLM (Xiao et al., 2023b), but departs from prior KV cache compression methods (H2O, TOVA, FastGen, SnapKV, PyramidKV) that apply uniform or query-driven token eviction across all heads. The key methodological departure is replacing attention-score profiling (used by FastGen and RazorAttention) with an optimization-based gate-learning procedure on synthetic passkey data, drawing inspiration from CNN filter pruning (Liu et al., 2017). Unlike architectural approaches (GQA, MQA) that require pre-training, DuoAttention is a post-hoc, plug-and-play framework that operates on frozen model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DuoAttention provides a practical, drop-in solution for the memory bottleneck in long-context LLM deployment, enabling 3.3M-token contexts on a single A100 when combined with quantization—a 6.4× capacity increase over standard FP16 full attention. Its compatibility with GQA, FlashAttention, chunked pre-filling, and quantization makes it immediately deployable in existing inference stacks. Open questions include how the retrieval/streaming head partition generalizes to new model architectures trained from scratch, whether the synthetic-data identification procedure remains effective for multi-modal or multi-turn dialogue settings, and whether the fixed 25%/50% head ratios can be further optimized per-layer or per-task.
