## Problem

Knowledge-distillation-based federated learning (KD-FL) methods, which communicate predictions on a public dataset instead of model parameters, have received growing attention for their privacy and communication advantages, yet their behavior under Byzantine attacks remains largely unexplored. It is unclear whether the prediction-based communication modality inherently limits adversarial impact compared to parameter-based methods like FedAVG, and whether existing Byzantine-resilient aggregation techniques transfer effectively to this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251546/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Clients transmit per-sample probability vectors (soft predictions over c classes) on a shared public unlabeled dataset (e.g., 100k STL-10 images for CIFAR-10, 90k CINIC-10 validation images, 1M Clothing1M images); no model parameters are ever sent to the server. The server additionally maintains per-client reliability weights p_i^t updated across communication rounds via an exponential-weights scheme. The output is a re-initialized server classifier trained each round on the public dataset using the aggregated soft predictions as distillation targets, plus the updated weight vector used for the next round's weighted aggregation.

## Method

The paper first establishes theoretically that FedDistill is inherently more Byzantine-resilient than FedAVG: the threat vector is confined to the bounded c-dimensional probability simplex (max ℓ₂ distance √2) and acts only indirectly through the distillation loss, yielding an O(C²α²) gradient-error bound between the perturbed and honest objectives. Building on this, two FedDistill-specific attacks are introduced—LMA (assigns probability 1 to the least-likely class under the honest mean) and CPA (uses a class-similarity covariance matrix to target the least similar class)—both of which outperform naive label-flipping and break existing defenses like Cronus and geometric median. To counter these, ExpGuard is proposed as a meta-algorithm that computes per-client outlier scores from a robust aggregation method over the entire public dataset, then updates client weights via the exponential-weights algorithm across rounds; its best variant EG+F skips explicit filtering and uses the eigenvector-projection distance as the score. Finally, HIPS (Hiding In Plain Sight) obfuscates attacks by restricting the byzantine prediction space from the full simplex to the convex hull of honest predictions, trading raw attack strength for evasiveness against detection-based defenses.

## Results

On CINIC-10 (ResNet-18, baseline 80.2%), EG+F achieves 79.2% under the CPA attack with 45% byzantine clients, compared to 45.9% for mean aggregation, 71.2% for geometric median, and 65.9% for Cronus. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, baseline 87.7%), EG+F reaches 85.5% under CPA versus 68.4% (Mean), 78.4% (GM), and 74.5% (Cronus). On Clothing1M (ResNet-50, baseline 69.0%), EG+F maintains 68.5% under CPA while mean aggregation collapses to 33.7%. In the benign setting, FedDistill matches or exceeds FedAVG (e.g., 80.2% vs 75.1% on CINIC-10, 69.0% vs 61.2% on Clothing1M), while under byzantine attacks FedDistill experiences only a 1.1-fold accuracy reduction versus a >4-fold reduction for state-of-the-art FedAVG defenses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cronus (Chang et al., 2019), the proposed EG+F consistently outperforms across all four datasets and all three attacks: on CINIC-10 under CPA, EG+F achieves 79.2% vs Cronus's 65.9% (+13.3 pp), and on CIFAR-10 under CPA, 85.5% vs 74.5% (+11.0 pp). Against the state-of-the-art FedAVG defense (D-SGD with NNM+CWMED from Allouah et al., 2023), FedDistill with EG+F is dramatically more resilient: with 9 byzantine clients and attack magnitude η=1, the FedAVG defense drops to 19.8% accuracy on CINIC-10, while FedDistill+EG+F retains 77.4% under LMA (a >4-fold vs 1.1-fold reduction from baseline). Against vanilla FedAVG (McMahan et al., 2017), FedDistill is competitive or superior in the benign setting (80.2% vs 75.1% on CINIC-10; 69.0% vs 61.2% on Clothing1M) and far more byzantine-resilient, as FedAVG collapses to near-chance accuracy with only 10% byzantine clients under naive attacks while FedDistill remains functional even at 45%. The HIPS obfuscation framework further demonstrates that while EG+F is the best defense against non-obfuscated attacks, the mean aggregation is slightly more robust against HIPS-obfuscated attacks (e.g., 74.2% vs 72.9% on CINIC-10 for HIPS+CPA), making EG+F the preferred worst-case choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the byzantine-resilient federated learning literature from the parameter-space setting (FedAVG and its robust-aggregation variants) to the prediction-space setting of knowledge-distillation-based FL (FedDistill). It builds directly on Chang et al.'s Federated Ensemble Distillation and its Cronus defense, as well as the exponential weights algorithm of Littlestone & Warmuth, but departs from prior byzantine FedDistill work by isolating the threat vector to the bounded probability simplex rather than the unbounded parameter space, and by introducing attacks (LMA, CPA) and a defense (ExpGuard) specifically designed for this modality. The theoretical analysis connecting the gradient error to the byzantine fraction α via the simplex geometry is a new contribution not present in the FedAVG byzantine literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic characterization of why KD-based FL is inherently more byzantine-resilient than parameter-sharing FL, with a clean O(C²α²) approximation guarantee, giving practitioners a principled reason to prefer prediction-based communication in adversarial settings. The HIPS obfuscation framework reveals a non-trivial tradeoff: attacks that are weaker in raw disruption but harder to detect can be more effective against defenses, suggesting that current robust-aggregation defenses may be insufficient against adaptive adversaries. Open questions include whether the i.i.d. assumption (which the paper acknowledges is restrictive) can be relaxed without degrading the theoretical guarantees, whether HIPS generalizes to multi-round adaptive attacks, and whether FedDistill-specific attacks can be ported back to FedAVG via gradient-based formulations as the authors speculate.
