## Problem

DiT-based video generation models incur substantial computational cost from three distinct attention mechanisms (spatial, temporal, and cross), making real-time generation impractical. Training-free acceleration methods for video DiTs remain unexplored, and existing caching techniques designed for CNN-based diffusion models are not directly applicable due to the shift to transformer architectures and the complex interplay among multiple attention types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250119/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated video, operating as a drop-in acceleration layer on top of existing pretrained DiT video models (Open-Sora at 2s/480p, Open-Sora-Plan at 2.7s/512×512, Latte at 2s/512×512). With 8 H100 GPUs and broadcast sequence parallelism, it achieves real-time generation (up to 35.6 FPS) for 480p video and extends to 720p on 16 GPUs. The method is entirely training-free and requires no modification to model weights or architecture.

## Method

PAB exploits the observation that attention output differences between adjacent diffusion steps follow a U-shaped pattern, with ~70% of middle steps exhibiting minimal variation. It broadcasts the full attention module output (rather than attention scores) from a computed step to subsequent steps within this stable segment, completely bypassing redundant attention computation. Different broadcast ranges are assigned per attention type in a pyramid style—spatial attention (most variable) gets the shortest range, temporal gets a medium range, and cross-attention (most stable) gets the longest—tailoring efficiency to each component's redundancy profile. A similar strategy is applied to selected MLP modules. Additionally, broadcast sequence parallelism leverages the fact that when temporal attention is broadcast, all associated inter-GPU communication is eliminated, enabling near-linear scaling across multiple GPUs with minimal communication overhead.

## Results

PAB achieves up to 1.58× speedup on a single GPU and up to 10.5× speedup with 8 H100 GPUs (reaching 35.6 FPS for real-time 720p video generation), while maintaining near-original quality. On Open-Sora (2s, 480p), PAB₂₄₆ scores 78.51 VBench (vs. 79.22 original) with 19.87s latency (vs. 26.54s original). On Open-Sora-Plan (2.7s, 512×512), PAB₂₄₆ scores 80.30 VBench (vs. 80.39 original) with 33.83s latency (vs. 46.49s original). On Latte (2s, 512×512), PAB₂₃₅ scores 76.32 VBench (vs. 77.40 original) with 8.91s latency (vs. 11.18s original).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAB consistently outperforms both baselines (Δ-DiT and T-GATE) on all quality metrics and speedup across all three models. On Open-Sora, PAB₂₄₆ achieves 78.51 VBench vs. 78.21 (Δ-DiT) and 77.61 (T-GATE), while also delivering 1.34× speedup vs. 1.03× and 1.19× respectively. The gap is most dramatic on Latte, where Δ-DiT collapses to 52.00 VBench (a 25-point drop from the original 77.40) due to its block-skipping strategy being incompatible with the spatial-temporal architecture, whereas PAB₂₃₅ retains 76.32 VBench. T-GATE performs reasonably on Open-Sora-Plan (80.15 VBench) but still trails PAB₂₄₆ (80.30) in quality while offering less speedup (1.18× vs. 1.37×). In terms of pixel-level fidelity, PAB achieves substantially higher PSNR (27.04 on Open-Sora) and lower LPIPS (0.0925) than both baselines, indicating that broadcasting full attention outputs preserves far more detail than caching feature offsets (Δ-DiT) or selectively reusing self-attention scores (T-GATE).

## Relation to Prior Work

PAB extends the training-free caching paradigm established for CNN-based diffusion models (DeepCache, T-GATE, Δ-DiT) to the DiT-based video generation setting, where three distinct attention types (spatial, temporal, cross) introduce more complex redundancy patterns than the single self-attention in image models. The key departure is the 'pyramid' strategy: rather than applying a uniform cache interval, PAB assigns different broadcast ranges per attention type based on empirically observed variance, and broadcasts full attention-module outputs rather than intermediate scores. For distributed inference, it builds directly on DSP (Dynamic Sequence Parallelism), exploiting the fact that broadcasting temporal attention eliminates all inter-GPU communication for that component, which prior sequence-parallel methods (Megatron-SP, DS-Ulysses) cannot achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAB demonstrates that real-time video generation (up to 35.6 FPS at 720p on 16 H100s) is achievable with a simple, training-free, plug-and-play technique, lowering the barrier for deploying DiT-based video models in interactive applications. Its generality across three architecturally distinct models (Open-Sora, Open-Sora-Plan, Latte) suggests broad applicability to future video DiTs. However, the fixed broadcast strategy may degrade on highly dynamic scenes, the method only addresses attention/MLP redundancy (not other components), and the quality drop at aggressive broadcast ranges (e.g., Open-Sora-Plan PAB₅₇₉ losing ~8.6 VBench points) indicates a quality-speed tradeoff that remains unresolved for production use.
