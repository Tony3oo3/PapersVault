## Problem

Fine-grained spuriosity ranking of training data has been shown to significantly improve bias mitigation over binary biased/unbiased partitioning, but existing ranking methods (e.g., Moayeri et al., 2023) require human supervision or auxiliary biased models to identify spurious features, limiting their applicability. This paper asks whether spuriosity rankings can be derived automatically from the training dynamics themselves, without any human annotation of bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250023/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (UrbanCars, CelebA, BAR, ImageNet-1K) or sentence pairs (MultiNLI) and produces two outputs: a per-class integer spuriosity rank ρ(x) ordering data points from highest to lowest spuriosity, and a debiased class prediction. The ranking is fine-grained (up to N distinct ranks per class) rather than a binary label, and is obtained as a byproduct of the training trajectory rather than a separate annotation step. For contrastive debiasing, the rank determines pair construction: same-rank samples form negative pairs while higher-rank samples form positive pairs.

## Method

Sebra exploits the Hardness-Spuriosity Symmetry (Assumption 1): in ERM training, the ease of learning a sample is inversely correlated with its spuriosity. It steers ERM along a high-to-low spuriosity pathway using two controller variables: a binary selection variable v_i that progressively removes already-learned (high-spuriosity) samples to prevent interference, and a continuous upweighting variable u_i = p_y^(1/β) that counteracts non-uniform gradient magnitudes across samples of different spuriosity levels. Theorem 1 establishes a conservation law u_i·L_CE + β(u_i ln u_i − u_i) = c that guarantees the upweighting has the correct exponentially-decreasing form. The spuriosity rank of each sample is the epoch at which its predicted probability crosses a critical threshold p_critical (derived via the Lambert W-function), with fine-grained collision resolution within the same epoch achieved by sorting on u_i. The resulting rankings feed into a supervised contrastive loss that pulls same-rank (similarly spurious) representations apart and pushes higher-rank (less spurious) representations closer, simultaneously optimizing cross-entropy for classification.

## Results

On UrbanCars, Sebra achieves a worst-group accuracy of 73.8% and an average gap of −10.57, approaching the supervised Group DRO (−10.30) while requiring no bias annotations. On CelebA, Sebra reaches 65.3% worst-group accuracy with an average gap of −9.82, substantially outperforming all unsupervised baselines (next best: DFR at −19.16). On BAR, Sebra achieves 75.36% test accuracy on bias-conflicting samples, surpassing the best unsupervised baseline (Debian, 69.88%) by over 5 points. For ranking quality, Sebra attains Kendall's τ of 0.85 on UrbanCars and 0.69 on CelebA, far exceeding the human-supervised Spuriosity Ranking method (0.40 and 0.38 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sebra outperforms all five unsupervised baselines (ERM, LfF, JTT, Debian, DFR) across every dataset and metric. On UrbanCars, Sebra's worst-group accuracy of 73.8% exceeds the next-best unsupervised method (JTT, 33.3%) by over 40 points, and its average gap of −10.57 is nearly on par with the supervised Group DRO (−10.30). On CelebA, Sebra's worst-group accuracy of 65.3% nearly doubles that of the best unsupervised competitor (Debian, 41.1%), and its average gap of −9.82 is roughly half the gap of DFR (−19.16). On BAR, Sebra achieves 75.36% test accuracy, beating Debian (69.88%) by 5.5 points. For ranking quality (Table 1), Sebra's Kendall's τ of 0.85 on UrbanCars and 0.69 on CelebA substantially surpasses the human-supervised Spuriosity Ranking method (0.40 and 0.38), demonstrating that self-guided ranking without human labels can exceed supervised approaches. The only comparison where Sebra trails is against the fully supervised Group DRO on CelebA's average gap (−9.82 vs. −5.79), though Sebra requires no bias annotations whatsoever.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sebra extends the line of unsupervised debiasing work (LfF, JTT, Debian) that identifies biases via discrete group partitioning, and the spuriosity-ranking line (Singla & Feizi 2022; Moayeri et al. 2023) that orders data by bias strength but requires human supervision or adversarially trained interpretable encoders. The key conceptual departure is deriving fine-grained spuriosity rankings directly from ERM training dynamics via a formalized Hardness-Spuriosity Symmetry and its associated conservation law (Theorem 1), eliminating the need for bias labels or interpretability tools. For mitigation, Sebra builds on contrastive debiasing (CorrectN-Contrast, Zhang et al. 2022) but refines pair selection using the derived rank ordering rather than binary biased/unbiased labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sebra provides the first fully unsupervised fine-grained bias ranking that outperforms its supervised counterpart (Kendall's τ 0.85 vs. 0.40 on UrbanCars), suggesting that the training dynamics of ERM already encode rich spuriosity information that prior methods failed to exploit. The conservation-law formulation gives a clean theoretical handle on why sequential learning under controlled ERM yields valid rankings, which could inform future work on curriculum-based robustness. Limitations include sensitivity to label noise and potential collapse under class imbalance, and the paper acknowledges the need for unsupervised evaluation metrics for spuriosity rankings beyond the proxy metrics (Kendall's τ, PD) used here.
