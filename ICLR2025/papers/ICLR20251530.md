## Problem

Existing trajectory inference methods (Lavenant et al., 2024; Chizat et al., 2022) assume all state variables relevant to the dynamics are directly observed, ignoring the well-established principle from control theory that unobserved states like velocity are critical for accurate dynamics modeling. This paper addresses the partially observed setting, where only a subset of latent states is measured (e.g., position without velocity), and shows that incorporating a known dynamics prior on the hidden states dramatically improves trajectory reconstruction and enables data association in ambiguous scenarios like crossing paths.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired empirical distributions of observations in a lower-dimensional observation space Y at T time points (with potentially varying sample counts N_i^T per time), a known divergence-free drift field Ξ encoding the dynamics model, and a smooth observation function g: X → Y. It produces m particles at each time step representing the latent space marginals in X (higher-dimensional than Y), along with entropic transport plans γ_i coupling adjacent time points from which full trajectories can be sampled via composition. The optimization operates entirely in the latent space X, with the data-fitting term computed in observation space through the pushforward g_#.

## Method

PO-MFL formulates trajectory inference as a minimum entropy estimation problem in path space relative to a reference SDE measure W^{Ξ,τ} that encodes the known dynamics, then reduces this infinite-dimensional problem to a finite-dimensional objective over P(X)^T using entropic OT (Schrödinger bridges) between adjacent time marginals. The key technical innovation is incorporating the known drift Ξ into the OT cost via an Euler-Maruyama approximation of the transition kernel, yielding a tractable quadratic cost c̃(x,y) = ½‖y − x + Δt·Ξ(t,x)‖² that acts as a first-order dynamics prior (versus the zeroth-order Brownian kernel of the fully-observed baseline). The reduced objective is minimized via Mean-Field Langevin dynamics implemented as noisy particle gradient descent with Sinkhorn iterations for the entropic OT subproblems. The paper proves consistency (convergence to ground truth as observations become dense), exponential convergence of the MFL dynamics, and introduces a C_Ψ-ensemble observability condition ensuring identifiability of the latent marginals from observation-space marginals alone.

## Results

On the Wikipedia page traffic dataset (500-day trajectories, 3 pages), PO-MFL achieves a normalized W2 distance of 1.01±0.04 (m=3), 1.07±0.07 (m=6), and 1.05±0.10 (m=15) between true and sampled trajectory distributions, consistently outperforming the FO-MFL baseline (1.61±0.21, 1.76±0.30, 1.91±0.44 respectively). In synthetic constant-velocity experiments, PO-MFL successfully reconstructs trajectories and recovers per-particle velocities matching ground truth, while FO-MFL fails to converge. In a crossing-paths scenario, PO-MFL correctly separates two moving groups using the velocity prior, whereas FO-MFL collapses to the population centroid.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is FO-MFL (fully-observed Mean-Field Langevin) from Chizat et al. (2022), which assumes the entire state vector is observed and uses a zeroth-order (Brownian) transition kernel. PO-MFL wins decisively across all settings: on the Wikipedia dataset, it reduces normalized W2 distance by 37–45% (e.g., 1.01 vs. 1.61 at m=3) while also reducing variance by roughly 5× (0.04 vs. 0.21). In the synthetic constant-velocity model, FO-MFL fails to converge entirely, while PO-MFL recovers both positions and hidden velocities. In the crossing-paths experiment, FO-MFL collapses to the centroid because it lacks a hidden velocity state and prefers V-shaped trajectories over straight lines; PO-MFL leverages the constant-velocity dynamics prior to correctly separate the two groups. The paper also shows via Figure 3 that PO-MFL's advantage persists as the number of time points, number of observations, and initial velocity are varied, whereas FO-MFL's W2 error degrades sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimum-entropy trajectory inference framework of Lavenant et al. (2024) and the Mean-Field Langevin (MFL) algorithm of Chizat et al. (2022) from the fully observed setting to a partially observed (latent state) setting by introducing a known dynamics model Ξ into the SDE. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the observable state space model structure from target tracking (e.g., the constant velocity model), where only position is observed but velocity is a hidden state, and formalizing identifiability via a new C_Ψ-ensemble observability condition that generalizes Zeng et al. (2015). The entropic OT cost is modified via an Euler-Maruyama approximation of the transition kernel, making the Schrödinger bridge tractable while incorporating the known drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to incorporate simple, non-informative dynamics priors (constant velocity, autoregressive) into trajectory inference is practically significant: it makes the problem more robust to sparse observations and enables disambiguation in crossing-path scenarios where the fully observed baseline collapses. The paper provides the first consistency and exponential convergence guarantees for the partially observed setting, closing a theoretical gap. Open questions include stability under dynamics model misspecification (the unknown Ψ potential provides some flexibility but its limits are unexplored), extension of ensemble observability to fully stochastic systems, and validation on real single-cell genomic data where the motivation is strongest.
