## Problem

Most fairness-aware RL work addresses only immediate bias in static settings, yet real decision systems operate dynamically where decisions compound over time and can entrench long-term disparities. A key open question is how long-term fairness (equity in cumulative qualification gains) relates to and potentially conflicts with short-term fairness notions like benefit fairness, and how to balance both during policy optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250121/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a tabular MDP where each individual is characterized by a binary sensitive attribute, a qualification state (credit score), and an unobserved credit drift term; the policy is a feedforward neural network mapping (state, group) to a binary decision. Beyond the learned policy, the framework outputs a qualification gain disparity metric C_π(θ), its causal decomposition into DPE, IPE, and SPE components, and a Gini-inspired benefit fairness score Λ that measures pairwise decision-rate discrepancies weighted by inverse benefit distance.

## Method

The paper introduces a qualification gain parity metric defined as the difference in expected cumulative qualification gain across groups, expressed via Bellman's equation under a soft intervention do(π). It then performs a path-specific causal decomposition using two hypothetical policies—a baseline π₀ that always denies treatment and a virtual policy π^PS that transitions states under π but computes gains under π₀—yielding three components: Direct Policy Effect (instantaneous impact), Indirect Policy Effect (delayed impact through environment transitions), and Spurious Policy Effect (environment-only disparity). A key theoretical result (Proposition 1) shows the DPE equals the expected product of the treatment probability and the conditional average treatment effect (benefit Δ), directly linking long-term fairness to the short-term notion of benefit fairness. For optimization, the authors extend PPO with a squared qualification gain disparity constraint and a Gini-inspired benefit fairness regularizer Λ, producing the PPO-C and PPO-Cb algorithms.

## Results

On a loan-disbursement simulation environment (three settings varying initial credit distributions, repayment probabilities, and credit drift), PPO-C and PPO-Cb both substantially outperform all baselines in reducing qualification gain disparity (the long-term fairness metric C_π(θ)), while PPO-C maintains utility competitive with the baselines. PPO-Cb achieves the best qualification gain disparity in Setting 1 and comparable performance in Settings 2–3, at a modest utility cost. The causal decomposition reveals that the Direct Policy Effect (DPE) is far more responsive to policy optimization than the Indirect Policy Effect (IPE), and increasing the benefit-fairness penalty β^Λ in PPO-Cb further reduces DPE while improving the benefit-fairness metric Λ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla PPO (utility-only optimization), both PPO-C and PPO-Cb achieve markedly lower qualification gain disparity across all three settings while retaining competitive utility, confirming that the fairness constraint does not come at a large performance cost. Compared to A-PPO (Yu et al., 2022), which regularizes the advantage function for equalized opportunity, PPO-Cb produces a 'respectable profit in Setting 2, where A-PPO struggles,' indicating that A-PPO's advantage-regularization approach degrades utility more severely under that setting. Relative to F-PPO-L (Hu et al., 2023), which penalizes the 1-Wasserstein distance between group feature distributions, both PPO variants achieve lower qualification gain disparity, suggesting that directly constraining the expected qualification gain is more effective than matching marginal feature distributions. The key differentiator is that PPO-Cb additionally enforces benefit fairness via the Gini-inspired metric Λ, yielding a more pronounced reduction in DPE than PPO-C, particularly in Setting 1, without significantly altering IPE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fair reinforcement learning literature by introducing a qualification-gain-parity metric for long-term fairness, departing from prior approaches that regularize the advantage function (Yu et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2023) or constrain action rewards (Jabbari et al., 2017). The causal decomposition into DPE, IPE, and SPE draws on path-specific effect techniques (Avin et al., 2005) but applies them to a qualification-gain value function rather than the advantage function, distinguishing it from Pan & Schölkopf (2024). The paper's key conceptual contribution is linking the DPE component to benefit fairness (Plecko & Bareinboim, 2023), thereby bridging long-term and short-term fairness notions that prior work (e.g., Hu et al., 2024) had shown to be in tension under sensitive-attribute-unconsciousness constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that benefit fairness and long-term fairness need not conflict—because benefit fairness directly reduces the DPE component—offers a practical path to reconciling short-term and long-term fairness objectives, which prior work suggested was inherently impossible. The DPE/IPE/SPE decomposition provides a diagnostic lens for practitioners to identify whether bias stems from the policy's direct decisions, delayed environmental feedback, or pre-existing environmental disparities. However, the analysis is limited to a single loan-approval simulation with a binary sensitive attribute, and the authors acknowledge that designing qualification gain functions that decouple benefit from the sensitive feature remains an open problem. Scaling to multi-group, multi-dimensional settings and validating the theoretical alignment in more complex dynamic environments are important next steps.
