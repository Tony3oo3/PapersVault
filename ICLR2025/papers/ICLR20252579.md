## Problem

Existing video MLLMs are poorly suited for egocentric video understanding due to three gaps: (1) no large-scale egocentric QA training corpus exists, (2) no benchmark covers egocentric videos spanning 30 seconds to 1 hour, and (3) current long-video strategies either uniformly sample a limited number of frames (losing visual detail) or feed millions of visual tokens into long-context transformers (suffering from slow training and small batch sizes). The paper argues that, analogous to human cognition, a more effective approach is progressive understanding: first get a global overview, then focus on question-relevant details.

## Input / Output

The model takes an egocentric video (uniformly sampled into up to 300 frames) and a text question as input. During inference, the video is first processed in a compressed form (one embedding per frame via average pooling) for the global glimpse step, then only the top-32 selected high-resolution frame embeddings (each ~729 tokens from SigLIP-so400m) are fed to the LLM in the fallback step. The output is a free-form text answer; on the EgoMemoria benchmark, answers are evaluated as multiple-choice selections (4 options) with a debiased accuracy metric that excludes questions answerable without visual input.

## Method

MM-Ego introduces a Memory Pointer Prompting mechanism with two inference steps. In the global glimpse step, compressed per-frame visual embeddings are concatenated with the question embedding and a learnable memory pointer embedding, then passed through the LLM; the pointer's output embedding is dot-producted against all compressed visual embeddings to produce per-frame correlation scores, with a uniform-sampling perturbation (parameter α=0.1) to balance exploration and exploitation. In the fallback step, the top-k high-resolution visual embeddings corresponding to the highest-scoring frames are assembled with the question embedding and fed to the LLM for answer generation. Training uses a binary cross-entropy loss on the correlation scores (when ground-truth key-frame indices are available from the data engine) plus standard language cross-entropy on the answer tokens. The model is initialized from LLaVA-OV 7B (SigLIP-so400m ViT + Qwen2-7B) and fine-tuned on a 7M-sample egocentric QA dataset generated by a 'narration-to-QA' data engine that uses GPT-4o to convert human-annotated Ego4D narrations into question-answer pairs with key-frame annotations.

## Results

On the EgoMemoria benchmark (Mean Debiased Accuracy), MM-Ego achieves 61.27% average, outperforming GPT-4o (60.48%), LLaVA-OV 7B (47.32%), and LLaVA-NeXT-Video-32B-Qwen (36.19%). On EgoSchema, MM-Ego scores 69.03% (Full), surpassing the previous best open-source model LLaVA-NeXT-Video-32B-Qwen (60.85%) by +8.18 points, though trailing GPT-4o (72.2%). On the general Internet-video benchmark Video-MME (w/o subtitles), MM-Ego achieves 57.00% (Entire), slightly below LLaVA-OV 7B (58.30%) and LLaVA-NeXT-Video-32B-Qwen (60.20%), but competitive given its narrower training data mixture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-Ego outperforms all open-source baselines on both egocentric benchmarks: it leads EgoMemoria by +13.95 over LLaVA-OV 7B (61.27 vs. 47.32 MDA) and by +25.08 over LLaVA-NeXT-Video-32B-Qwen (61.27 vs. 36.19), and leads EgoSchema by +8.93 over LLaVA-OV 7B (69.03 vs. 60.10) and +8.18 over LLaVA-NeXT-Video-32B-Qwen (69.03 vs. 60.85). Against GPT-4o, MM-Ego edges out on EgoMemoria (61.27 vs. 60.48) but trails on EgoSchema (69.03 vs. 72.2) and Video-MME (57.00 vs. 71.90). On the general Video-MME benchmark, MM-Ego is slightly below LLaVA-OV 7B (57.00 vs. 58.30) and LLaVA-NeXT-Video-32B-Qwen (57.00 vs. 60.20), which the authors attribute to a less diverse training data mixture. The LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B-DPO model is substantially weaker across all egocentric metrics (25.94 MDA on EgoMemoria), confirming that egocentric-specific training data and architecture are critical for first-person video understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLaVA-OV as its base architecture and introduces a Memory Pointer Prompting mechanism inspired by Pointer Networks (Vinyals et al., 2015; Merity et al., 2016), repurposing the pointer concept for question-aware frame selection in long egocentric videos. It departs from the two dominant long-video MLLM strategies—uniform frame sampling and long-context transformers with parallelism—by proposing a two-step 'global glimpse + fallback' approach that first compresses all frames for overview, then retrieves high-resolution embeddings for key frames. The data engine is conceptually related to LLaMA-VID and GroundVQA but differs in using human-annotated narrations (rather than VLM-generated labels) to avoid the chicken-and-egg problem of VLM-limited data quality, and scales to 7M QA samples across videos up to one hour long.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a critical gap in egocentric video understanding by providing the first large-scale (7M) egocentric QA training corpus and the EgoMemoria benchmark spanning 30 seconds to 1 hour, which is significantly more diverse in duration than prior egocentric benchmarks (EgoSchema ~3 min, QaEgo4D ~8 min). The debiasing evaluation methodology (Mean Debiased Accuracy) is a practical contribution that reveals language bias inflates scores by ~18 points, a concern applicable to all video QA evaluation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to thousands of frames for always-on egocentric videos, and whether the pointer-based retrieval generalizes beyond the 300-frame limit tested here.
