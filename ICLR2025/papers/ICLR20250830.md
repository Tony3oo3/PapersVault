## Problem

Existing decentralized optimization algorithms with communication compression suffer from a combination of limitations: they require strong assumptions such as bounded data heterogeneity or large batch sizes, fail to achieve linear speedup with the number of clients, or have suboptimal dependence on noise variance. No prior method achieves the optimal asymptotic rate O(σ²/nε⁴) matching distributed SGD under standard assumptions (L-smoothness, bounded gradient variance) without any of these impractical restrictions.

## Input / Output

Each client maintains local parameters and receives compressed messages from its neighbors over an undirected communication graph defined by a doubly stochastic mixing matrix W. The method operates with arbitrary contractive compressors (e.g., Top-K, quantization) applied column-wise, and assumes only batch size one for stochastic gradients—no large-batch requirement. The output is a uniformly random iterate from the sequence {x̄₀, …, x̄_{T−1}} whose expected squared gradient norm is bounded by ε², with convergence guarantees that scale linearly with the number of clients n.

## Method

MoTEF integrates three mechanisms: (1) EF21-type Error Feedback on both the parameter and gradient-tracking channels to correct compression bias, (2) a Gradient Tracking step that performs gossip averaging on a momentum term rather than raw gradients, and (3) Polyak momentum applied to the tracked quantity. The key insight is that tracking momentum (M^{t+1} = (1−λ)M^t + λ·∇̃F(X^{t+1})) instead of gradients, combined with EF, simultaneously eliminates data heterogeneity dependence and enables linear speedup with n—properties that prior methods like BEER or Choco-SGD cannot achieve together. A variance-reduced variant, MoTEF-VR, replaces the simple momentum with a STORM-type estimator under the mean-squared-smoothness assumption, improving the stochastic term from O(σ²/nε⁴) to O(σ/nε³). Convergence is established via a six-term Lyapunov function whose descent is verified through a system of linear inequalities solved symbolically.

## Results

MoTEF achieves the optimal asymptotic convergence rate of O(σ²/(nε⁴)) for general non-convex functions, matching the rate of distributed SGD and being the first decentralized algorithm with contractive compressors to do so under arbitrary data heterogeneity without large-batch requirements. In the PL regime, MoTEF achieves Õ(Lσ²/(μ²nε)), also with linear speedup in n. Empirically, on logistic regression with non-convex regularization (n=100, ring topology, gsgd_b compressor), MoTEF outperforms BEER, Choco-SGD, DSGD, and D2 in both gradient norm and test accuracy as a function of communication rounds. On MNIST MLP training, MoTEF and BEER reach similar gradient norms, but MoTEF converges significantly faster in test accuracy due to momentum tracking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoTEF dominates all compared baselines on the key theoretical axis: it is the only method achieving the optimal O(σ²/(nε⁴)) rate with linear speedup in n, no large-batch requirement, no bounded-gradient or bounded-heterogeneity assumptions, and support for arbitrary contractive compressors. Against BEER (Zhao et al., 2022), MoTEF removes the large-batch requirement (BEER needs batch size Ω(σ²/(αε²))) and achieves linear speedup with n, whereas BEER's stochastic term does not improve with n; experiments in Figures 1–2 confirm that MoTEF's error decreases with more clients while BEER's does not. Against Choco-SGD (Koloskova et al., 2019), MoTEF eliminates the bounded-gradient assumption and is robust to data heterogeneity (Figure 2 shows Choco-SGD degrading as ζ increases while MoTEF does not). Against DoCoM (Yau & Wai, 2022), MoTEF achieves a better dependency on noise variance (σ² vs. σ³) and does not require the stronger smoothness assumptions DoCoM needs. Against CEDAS (Huang & Pu, 2023), MoTEF avoids the need for an additional unbiased compressor that doubles per-iteration communication cost. In the empirical logistic regression and MLP experiments (Figures 3, 5), MoTEF consistently outperforms DSGD and D2 in communication complexity for both gradient norm and test accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoTEF synthesizes three established mechanisms—EF21-type error feedback (Richtárik et al., 2021), gradient tracking (Koloskova et al., 2021), and Polyak momentum—into a unified decentralized algorithm, departing from prior work (BEER, Choco-SGD, DeepSqueeze, DoCoM) that either required bounded data heterogeneity, large batches, or failed to achieve linear speedup with the number of clients. The EF-enhanced gossip step is directly adapted from BEER (Zhao et al., 2022) and Choco-SGD (Koloskova et al., 2019), while the momentum tracking on the gradient estimator is the key architectural novelty that removes heterogeneity dependencies. The variance-reduced variant MoTEF-VR extends the STORM estimator (Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019) to the decentralized compressed setting, a combination not previously analyzed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoTEF resolves a long-standing gap by being the first decentralized algorithm with contractive compressors to provably match the optimal O(σ²/nε⁴) rate of distributed SGD under arbitrary data heterogeneity without large-batch or bounded-gradient assumptions. The suboptimal 1/ρ³ spectral gap dependency in the deterministic regime remains an open question—experiments suggest the practical sensitivity is much weaker than theory predicts, but it is unclear whether this is an artifact of the analysis or inherent to gradient tracking. Future directions include combining MoTEF with local steps, asynchronous communication, or per-client smoothness constants to further tighten the rate.
