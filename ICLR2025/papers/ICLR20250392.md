## Problem

Existing computational methods for analyzing molecular dynamics (MD) trajectories struggle with the high-dimensional, non-smooth interatomic interactions in large-scale biological systems. Current approaches either oversimplify dynamics with biophysical priors, scale linearly with the number of time stamps, or fail to capture high-order interatomic interactions that are critical for understanding protein function and drug resistance. No prior framework efficiently combines structural bioinformatics (bond-mediated interatomic interactions) with learned coarse graining for end-to-end characterization of complex protein dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a full MD trajectory — a sequence of T frames each containing N×3 atomic 3D coordinates (up to ~5,000 atoms and 2,500 frames in the largest benchmark) — together with the molecular bond adjacency matrix defining the molecular graph. It outputs a binary property label (e.g., drug-sensitive vs. drug-resistant, active vs. inactive receptor state, degradation vs. dimerization type). The entire pipeline is fully differentiable, enabling end-to-end supervised training with a combined loss of spectral clustering, temporal consistency, and cross-entropy terms.

## Method

Deep Signature comprises three differentiable modules: (1) a deep spectral clustering module that uses a stack of graph pooling layers (GCN + MLP with softmax) to learn soft cluster assignments via a normalized-cut relaxation objective, progressively coarsening the atomic trajectory from N nodes down to M≪N nodes while enforcing temporal consistency with the original dynamics; (2) a path signature transform module that partitions the coarse-grained trajectory into segments and computes depth-D log-signatures (iterated integrals) over each segment using the Signatory library, capturing interatomic temporal correlations as geometric areas while eliminating redundancy via the shuffle product identity; and (3) an LSTM layer that models nonlinear interactions across the log-signature sequence, followed by a two-layer MLP classifier. The key insight is that log-signatures provide a compact, mathematically principled encoding of non-smooth interactive dynamics that is provably invariant to translation, near-invariant to rotation, equivariant to atomic permutation, and invariant to time reparameterization — properties that enhance generalization and interpretability.

## Results

On the Gene Regulatory Dynamics benchmark, Deep Signature achieves 99.12% accuracy and 98.65% recall, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline GraphLSTM (96.40% accuracy, 93.14% recall). On the large-scale EGFR Mutation Dynamics (~5,000 atoms, 1,000 frames per trajectory), Deep Signature reaches 69.33% accuracy and 21.27% recall, surpassing GraphLSTM (64.93% / 11.60%) and dihedral-angle descriptors (67.47% / 2.40%). On GPCR Dynamics (2,500 frames, ~285 amino acids), Deep Signature attains 58.00% accuracy and 43.30% recall, again leading all baselines including GraphLSTM (53.47% / 38.13%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five deep-learning baselines (Head, Tail, Head&Tail, GraphLSTM, GraphTrans) and two classical geometric-descriptor baselines (dihedral angle + PCA + SVM, Cα-dihedral angle + PCA + SVM) across all three benchmarks. Deep Signature consistently wins on both accuracy and recall in every setting. The closest competitor is GraphLSTM, which aggregates atomic dynamics by averaging and applies an LSTM over time; Deep Signature outperforms it by ~2.7 points in accuracy on Gene Regulatory Dynamics, ~4.4 points on EGFR, and ~4.5 points on GPCR. The recall gap is even more pronounced on EGFR (21.27% vs. 11.60%), where Deep Signature's log-signature features better handle class imbalance. Classical dihedral-angle descriptors achieve high accuracy but extremely low recall on EGFR (2.40%), indicating they exploit spurious correlations rather than capturing true dynamic patterns. GraphTrans underperforms GraphLSTM on all three benchmarks, suggesting that self-attention over temporal frames is less effective than recurrent aggregation for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate threads: GNN-based soft spectral clustering for graph pooling (Bianchi et al., 2020) and path signature transforms from rough path theory (Lyons, 2014), applying their composition to molecular dynamics trajectory analysis. It departs from prior MD analysis methods that either rely on biophysical priors (dihedral angles, RMSD) or discretize molecules into voxel grids for CNNs, by instead preserving the molecular graph structure and extracting interatomic interaction dynamics through iterated integrals. The paper also distinguishes itself from recent work combining path signatures with GNNs for traffic forecasting (Choi & Park, 2023; Wang & Zang, 2024), arguing that those approaches process temporal information per-node and fail to capture cross-node temporal interactions essential for protein function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a recognized gap in MD analysis: the inability of existing methods to efficiently characterize high-order interatomic interactions along trajectories without quadratic complexity in system size. The theoretical guarantees (translation invariance, near rotation invariance, permutation equivariance, time-reparametrization invariance) provide physical interpretability that purely data-driven approaches lack. However, the moderate accuracy gains on real protein systems (69% on EGFR, 58% on GPCR) and the 'near' rather than exact rotation invariance suggest the approach has not yet fully solved the problem of large-scale dynamic characterization. Open questions include whether the method scales to systems with tens of thousands of atoms, whether the coarse-graining level can be learned end-to-end without manual specification, and whether the framework extends to regression tasks (e.g., binding affinity prediction) beyond binary classification.
