## Problem

Learning-based solvers for combinatorial optimization face scalability challenges due to large numbers of learnable parameters and sensitivity to out-of-distribution instances, while traditional sampling methods like simulated annealing rely on sequential local updates that cannot be parallelized. The recent iSCO solver improves on this with discrete Langevin dynamics but still operates in discrete space without continuous relaxation and lacks communication between parallel chains, limiting its ability to fully exploit GPU parallelism for large-scale instances.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,E) with problem-specific parameters C (e.g., edge weights, adjacency matrix) and penalty coefficients λ that encode constraints via a penalty method, producing a binary solution vector x ∈ {0,1}^N by thresholding the relaxed continuous variables at 0.5. Internally, the method operates on S parallel continuous variable vectors p ∈ [0,1]^N (or probability vectors in [0,1]^K for general discrete variables), updated via gradient-based Langevin dynamics on a single GPU. The solution is guaranteed feasible in practice, with the soft solution converging to exact 0/1 values within 32-bit floating-point precision.

## Method

PQQA reformulates the discrete CO problem via continuous relaxation, introducing an α-entropy term s(σ(w)) that is convex, minimized at binary values {0,1}^N, and maximized at the half-integral point 1_N/2. Quasi-Quantum Annealing (QQA) anneals a penalty parameter γ from negative to positive: at γ<0 the entropy dominates, pushing variables toward 0.5 (a 'quasi-quantum' superposition state that smooths non-convexity), while at γ>0 the entropy vanishes and variables are driven to discrete 0/1 values (a 'classical' state), with a theorem guaranteeing convergence to the optimal discrete solution. Gradient-based Langevin updates (using AdamW with a sensitive two-step transition that updates p directly then clamps) replace local Metropolis flips, enabling simultaneous updates of all N variables per step. A key novelty is the extended Boltzmann distribution over S parallel runs that includes a standard-deviation-based communication term, encouraging diversity across parallel chains and implicitly reinforcing binary convergence, all computed efficiently in a single GPU batch.

## Results

On the primary MIS benchmark (Table 1), PQQA with S=1,000 parallel runs and 30,000 steps achieves an approximation ratio (ApR) of 1.043 on large Erdős-Rényi graphs (9,000–11,000 nodes), surpassing iSCO (1.008) and KaMIS (1.000) while running in 25.5 min/graph. On the particularly difficult Regular Random Graphs with d=100 and 10⁶ nodes (Table 2), PQQA reaches ApR 0.966 versus 0.850 for iSCO and 0.194 for SA, demonstrating a clear scalability advantage. On max clique (Table 3), PQQA achieves ApR 1.000 on Twitter and 0.868 on RBtest without any training, outperforming all learning-based baselines. On graph coloring (Table 6), PQQA achieves zero conflicts on 10 of 12 COLOR instances, matching or exceeding Tabucol and all GNN-based solvers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PQQA clearly outperforms iSCO on large-scale instances: on ER-[9000-11000] MIS, PQQA achieves ApR 1.043 vs. iSCO's 1.008, and on RRG(d=100, 10⁶ nodes) it reaches 0.966 vs. iSCO's 0.850, with the gap widening as problem size grows. Against the specialized OR solver KaMIS, PQQA surpasses it on large ERGs (1.043 vs. 1.000) while matching it on SATLIB (0.996 vs. 1.000). All learning-based methods (Intel, DGL, LwD, DIMES, EPM, AFF, RUN-CSP) are outperformed by PQQA on both MIS and max clique, with PQQA requiring no training data. On max cut (Optsicom), PQQA ties iSCO at ApR 1.000. On balanced graph partition, PQQA achieves the lowest edge cut ratio (0.03–0.04) across all five DNN computation graphs, beating GAP, hMETIS, and iSCO. On graph coloring, PQQA achieves zero conflicts on 10/12 COLOR instances, outperforming PI-GCN, PI-SAGE, and GNN, and matching or beating Tabucol on the hardest queen graphs (11 and 14 conflicts vs. 20 and 35 for Tabucol).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Ichikawa (2023)'s continuous relaxation framework with α-entropy by introducing Quasi-Quantum Annealing (QQA), which anneals the entropy penalty γ from negative (quasi-quantum, half-integral) to positive (classical, binary) states, and adds a parallel-run communication term inspired by the Sum-Hamming distance from Ichikawa & Iwashita (2024). Conceptually, it departs from iSCO (Sun et al., 2023b), which operates in discrete space via discrete Langevin dynamics, by instead working in continuous space with gradient-based Langevin updates that simultaneously update all variables. The parallel communication mechanism, which encourages diversity across S parallel chains via an STD-based term, is a novel addition not present in iSCO or traditional parallel tempering, and is specifically designed to exploit GPU batch parallelism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PQQA provides a training-free, GPU-accelerated general-purpose CO solver that matches or exceeds learning-based methods and iSCO, with a particularly notable speed-quality advantage on large-scale instances (up to 10^6 nodes) where learning-based solvers and even iSCO degrade or fail entirely. The practical implication is that practitioners may not need to invest in training problem-specific neural networks when a well-tuned gradient-based annealing approach on a single GPU suffices. Key limitations include reliance on the penalty method (which can fail when feasible solutions are hard to find) and the need for hyperparameter tuning (γ range, learning rate, S). Open questions include extension to mixed-integer problems, whether the parallel communication term can be made adaptive, and how PQQA compares to emerging quantum annealing hardware on real quantum devices.
