## Problem

Despite the success of ViT-based vision foundation models in 2D understanding, their ability to capture 3D spatial relationships—specifically, whether they produce consistent (view-equivariant) features for the same 3D point across different camera viewpoints—remains poorly understood and unexploited. This paper asks whether 3D equivariance quality correlates with downstream 3D task performance and whether it can be cheaply improved through targeted finetuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252097/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (512×512) rendered from 3D objects or captured in the wild, extracting final-layer ViT features with L2 normalization. A single 3×3 convolutional layer (stride 1) is appended to the ViT to exchange information between neighboring patches before bilinear upsampling, producing dense per-pixel feature maps at full image resolution. These equivariant features are then used for nearest-neighbor correspondence matching, which feeds into RANSAC PnP for 6D pose estimation, softmax-based neighborhood search for point tracking, and cosine-similarity keypoint matching for semantic correspondence.

## Method

The finetuning strategy applies LoRA adapters to the last four transformer blocks and appends a single convolutional head to a frozen ViT (e.g., DINOv2-base). During training, two random views of the same object from a 10K Objaverse subset are sampled, corresponding pixels are identified via dense 3D correspondences, and the SmoothAP ranking loss is applied to enforce feature similarity between matched pixels while pushing apart non-matched ones. Training runs for only 10K iterations with AdamW (lr=1e-5, weight decay=1e-4). The key insight is that SmoothAP directly optimizes retrieval precision for correspondence matching (unlike contrastive loss with fixed margins), and that even a single multi-view pair from one object for one iteration yields substantial 3D equivariance gains, demonstrating that the pretrained ViT already encodes most 3D structure and needs only minimal calibration to express it consistently across views.

## Results

After finetuning on Objaverse with SmoothAP loss and a single conv layer, DINOv2 achieves 58.03% (3cm-3deg) on OnePose-LowTexture, 46.85% Average Jaccard on TAP-Vid-DAVIS, and 47.25% PCK@0.05 on PF-PASCAL (different view), representing gains of +9.58, +5.0, and +5.06 over the pretrained model respectively. Remarkably, finetuning on a single object for just one iteration still yields +4.85, +3.55, and +3.47 improvements on the same metrics. Weaker models benefit more: DeiT gains +16.39 (3cm-3deg) and MAE shows substantial improvement from its initially weakest position. On WildGaussians novel view synthesis, the finetuned DINO features improve PSNR by 0.19 dB and reduce LPIPS across all six scenes compared to the original W-G pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is DINOv2 before vs. after the proposed finetuning: the method improves 3cm-3deg pose accuracy by 9.58 points, Average Jaccard tracking by 5.0, and PCK@0.05 semantic correspondence by 5.06. DINOv2-Reg (the registered variant) also benefits, gaining 3.46/6.67/6.92 across the three pose thresholds, while weaker baselines like DeiT (+16.39 on 3cm-3deg) and MAE show even larger relative gains. Against FiT (Yue et al., 2024) and DUSt3R (Wang et al., 2024), the paper reports that both methods perform 'much worse' than the proposed approach on all three downstream tasks (detailed numbers in supplementary). On the WildGaussians novel view synthesis task (Table 1), the finetuned DINO features marginally outperform the original W-G pipeline, e.g., PSNR 21.01 vs. 20.82 on Mountain and 24.01 vs. 23.96 on Patio, with consistently lower LPIPS. Among the five pretrained ViTs evaluated (DINOv2, DINOv2-Reg, MAE, CLIP, DeiT), DINOv2 consistently ranks first in both 3D equivariance quality (lowest APE) and downstream task performance, confirming the strong correlation the paper establishes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging line of work probing 3D awareness in vision foundation models (DietNeRF, LeRF, El Banani et al.) by making the evaluation systematic and task-agnostic: rather than tying multi-view consistency to a specific reconstruction or distillation pipeline, it directly measures feature equivariance across views and correlates it with three canonical correspondence tasks. Methodologically, it departs from FiT (which lifts 2D features into 3D and projects back) and DUSt3R (which predicts per-pixel 3D coordinates) by operating entirely in the existing ViT feature space, using a SmoothAP ranking loss to align corresponding pixels across views with minimal architectural modification (LoRA + one conv layer). The benchmark itself builds on Objaverse and MVImgNet correspondences but introduces the APE/PCDP metrics as a standardized way to quantify equivariance quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe—10K iterations of LoRA finetuning with a single conv head and SmoothAP loss on synthetic Objaverse data—offers a low-cost, broadly applicable upgrade to any ViT-based pipeline for 3D correspondence tasks, with demonstrated sim-to-real transfer to MVImgNet and downstream gains in pose estimation, tracking, and semantic correspondence. The finding that even one iteration on one object yields substantial equivariance gains suggests that 3D spatial alignment is a shallow, quickly-learnable property in pretrained ViT features, which reframes how much data is truly needed to 'unlock' 3D awareness. Open questions include whether the gains persist under more challenging conditions (articulated objects, severe occlusion, category-level generalization beyond PF-PASCAL), whether the single-iteration effect generalizes to larger or differently-architected models, and whether the approach can be extended to video or 4D correspondence.
