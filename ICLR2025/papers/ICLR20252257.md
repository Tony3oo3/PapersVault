## Problem

Existing multimodal LLM benchmarks predominantly evaluate single-image understanding, overlooking the richer task of integrating and reasoning across multiple images from varied perspectives, settings, and moments. While a few recent benchmarks include multi-image questions, they cover only limited relations and lack controlled, robust evaluation—particularly the ability to recognize unanswerable queries. MUIRBENCH addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive, pairwise-designed benchmark spanning 12 multi-image tasks and 10 relation categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252257/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each instance presents 2–9 images (average 4.3) interleaved with a text question and 4–5 multiple-choice options; images may appear at the beginning, middle, or end of the question, within the options, or in mixed positions. For models that do not natively accept multiple images, all images are concatenated into a single input. Evaluation produces accuracy scores decomposed by task (12), multi-image relation (10), answerable vs. unanswerable split, image position, and unanswerable-creation strategy, enabling fine-grained diagnostic analysis.

## Method

MUIRBENCH is built around a pairwise design: every answerable multiple-choice instance is paired with an expert-annotated unanswerable counterpart created via one of three minimal-difference strategies—replacing/reordering images (24.2%), modifying the question to be incompatible with the images and options (35.3%), or replacing options so no correct answer exists (40.5%). The 2,600 instances (1,300 answerable + 1,300 unanswerable) are sourced from existing datasets (40.8%), reformatted derivations of binary/open QA (21.7%), and newly curated data for underrepresented tasks like geographic understanding and visual retrieval (37.5%). Fine-grained metadata (image relation, type, position, count) is annotated for each instance to support diagnostic analysis. Evaluation follows the VLMEvalKit protocol (temperature 0, 10 retries, rule-based answer extraction) across 20 multimodal LLMs, comparing multi-image-trained models (GPT-4o, Gemini Pro, Mantis, VILA, Idefics, Emu2, OpenFlamingo) against single-image-trained models (LLaVA variants, CogVLM, MiniGPT-4, Yi-VL) to isolate the effect of multi-image training.

## Results

On MUIRBENCH (2,600 multiple-choice questions, 11,264 images, 12 tasks), GPT-4o achieves the highest overall accuracy at 68.0%, followed by GPT-4-Turbo (62.3%) and Gemini Pro (49.4%), while human performance reaches 93.2%. Open-source multi-image-trained models (Mantis, Idefics, VILA, Emu2, OpenFlamingo) cluster between 23.7% and 44.5%, and single-image-trained models (LLaVA variants, CogVLM, MiniGPT-4) fall below 33.3%. All models show a significant accuracy drop on unanswerable instances: GPT-4o loses 26.8% and Gemini Pro loses 21.5% relative to their answerable performance, indicating a persistent inability to recognize when a question cannot be answered.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o (68.0%) leads all 20 evaluated models but remains 25.1 points below human performance (93.2%). Among open-source multi-image models, Mantis-8B-Idefics2 (44.5%) is the strongest, outperforming Idefics-9B-Instruct (35.4%), Emu2-Chat 37B (33.6%), VILA 1.5-13B (33.1%), Idefics2-8B (26.1%), and OpenFlamingo-v2-9B (23.7%). Notably, single-image-trained LLaVA-NeXT-34B (33.3%) underperforms the smaller multi-image-trained Mantis-8B (44.5%) by over 11 points, demonstrating that multi-image training is critical. On the hardest tasks (Ordering, Visual Grounding), even GPT-4o drops to 23.4% and 36.9% respectively, while on easier tasks (Image-Text Matching, Diagram Understanding) it reaches 86.9% and 88.7%. Among the most recent models evaluated post-benchmark (Table 4), Gemini 1.5 Pro (64.9%) and MAmmoTH-VL-8B (55.1%) close the gap but still fall well short of GPT-4o. The consistent pattern across all models is a large accuracy drop on unanswerable instances, with no model reliably abstaining from answering.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUIRBENCH extends the multi-image evaluation space beyond prior benchmarks like Mantis-Eval (207 examples), BLINK, and MileBench, which cover only limited task or relation subsets. Conceptually, it repurposes the unanswerable-question paradigm from SQuAD (Rajpurkar et al., 2018) into a pairwise answerable/unanswerable design for multi-image understanding, a robustness mechanism absent from all prior multi-image benchmarks. The data curation draws on existing single- and multi-image datasets (GeneCIS, NLVR2, HallusionBench, MMBench, ISVQA) but systematically reformats and augments them to cover 12 tasks across 10 relation categories, with fine-grained metadata (image positions, types, relations) that no prior benchmark provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even GPT-4o (68%) and Gemini Pro (49.3%) fall 25–44 points below human accuracy, and that single-image-trained models (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-34B) cannot meaningfully generalize to multi-image inputs, hovering near random. The large unanswerable-instance gap (26.8% for GPT-4o) exposes a fundamental inability to recognize insufficient context, a critical reliability concern for real-world deployment. Open questions include how to architect models that natively process multiple images without lossy concatenation, how to train for abstention on unanswerable queries, and whether the lack of size-performance correlation reflects a data or training-process bottleneck. Limitations include English-only content, multiple-choice format, 2D images only, and a restricted set of unanswerable-creation strategies.
