## Problem

Existing embodied AI simulators target either indoor household spaces (AI2-THOR, Habitat, iGibson) or vehicle-centric driving roadways (CARLA, MetaDrive, SUMO), leaving a critical gap for urban micromobility on sidewalks, plazas, and public spaces. Micromobility presents unique challenges absent in both domains: large-scale multi-block scenes, multifarious terrains (curbs, tree roots, ramps), diverse cluttered obstacles, and dense interactions with pedestrians and vulnerable road users. No prior simulator was designed to support training and safety evaluation of AI-driven mobile machines (delivery bots, electric wheelchairs, robot dogs) in these environments.

## Input / Output

The ego agent observes a LiDAR vector encoding distance and direction to the nearest object within a 50 m radius, a state vector summarizing its kinematics, and navigation information pointing toward the goal; the action is a 2-D continuous vector in [-1, 1] controlling acceleration and steering rate. The platform itself consumes street block category/order/width specifications, sidewalk template indices, object and dynamic-agent density parameters, and terrain primitive selections to procedurally emit full 3-D interactive scenes (mean 20,000 m², 410 m episode length) with 10,000 urban-specific obstacles, 1,100 rigged SMPL-X humans (2,314 motions each), VRUs, and mobile machines. Evaluation outputs include Success Rate, SPL, Social Navigation Score, and Cumulative Cost (crash count) on both seen and unseen scene splits.

## Method

MetaUrban is a compositional procedural-generation pipeline with three coupled modules. (1) Hierarchical Layout Generation samples street block categories (straight, intersection, roundabout, circle, T-junction), assigns one of seven sidewalk templates with four functional zones (building, frontage, clear, furnishing), places objects in three tiers (standard infrastructure, non-standard infrastructure, clutter) conditioned on zone geometry, and synthesizes terrain via Wave Function Collapse over five primitives (slopes, steps, stairs, ramps, rough) with per-material friction textures. (2) Scalable Obstacle Retrieval builds a 1,215-item description pool from CityScape/Mapillary annotations, GPT-4o + Grounded-SAM segmentation of 25,000 street images across 50 countries, and urban-design handbooks, then performs BLIP-2-based open-vocabulary image-text retrieval over Objaverse/Objaverse-XL to select 10,000 high-quality urban-specific 3-D assets. (3) Cohabitant Populating instantiates 1,100 SMPL-X humans (68 garments × 32 hairs × 13 beards × 46 accessories × 1,038 textures) with 2,314 BEDLAM motions, adds VRUs and six mobile-machine types, plans collision- and deadlock-free trajectories via ORCA + Push-and-Rotate, and enforces traffic rules. Benchmarks evaluate seven baselines (PPO, PPO-Lag, PPO-ET, IQL, TD3+BC, BC, GAIL) on PointNav and SocialNav across the 12,800-scene MetaUrban-12K dataset, with ablations on scene scaling, object/agent density, and cross-machine mechanical-structure effects.

## Results

On the MetaUrban-12K benchmark, PPO achieves the highest PointNav success rate of 66% (SPL 0.64) on the test set, while IQL leads on SocialNav with 36% SR (SNS 0.67). The tasks remain far from solved, with maximum success rates of only 66% (PointNav) and 36% (SocialNav), indicating substantial difficulty in urban micromobility navigation. Models trained on MetaUrban-12K demonstrate strong generalizability, maintaining 49% and 26% average success rates on the unseen test set (MetaUrban-unseen) with novel sidewalk templates, objects, and agent dynamics. Safe RL methods (PPO-Lag, PPO-ET) substantially reduce collision cost (e.g., PPO-ET drops SocialNav cost from 0.66 to 0.26) but at a significant effectiveness penalty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among online methods, PPO dominates PointNav on the test set (66% SR vs. 60% for PPO-Lag and 57% for PPO-ET), but PPO-Lag surprisingly generalizes best to unseen scenes (60% SR vs. PPO's 49%), suggesting its safety constraints act as a regularizer. On SocialNav, IQL (offline RL) achieves the highest test SR (36%) and unseen SR (27%), outperforming PPO (34% test, 24% unseen) and all other baselines, indicating that offline learning from expert demonstrations is particularly effective in dynamic multi-agent settings. Safe RL methods (PPO-Lag, PPO-ET) dramatically reduce collision cost—PPO-ET cuts SocialNav test cost from 0.66 (PPO) to 0.26—but at a severe effectiveness cost (SR drops from 34% to 5%). Among imitation learning baselines, GAIL (47% PointNav SR) substantially outperforms BC (36%), yet both trail PPO by 19–30 points on PointNav. TD3+BC is the weakest overall performer on PointNav (29% test SR, 20% unseen SR), suggesting that its conservative offline policy struggles with the long-horizon, high-density urban scenes. Overall, no single method dominates across all settings, and the gap between best and worst methods (e.g., 66% vs. 29% on PointNav test) highlights that urban micromobility navigation remains an open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaUrban carves out a new domain between indoor household simulators (AI2-THOR, Habitat, iGibson) and driving simulators (CARLA, MetaDrive, SUMO), none of which target the public urban spaces (sidewalks, plazas) where micromobility vehicles operate. It inherits MetaDrive's Block Incremental Generation for road networks and PyBullet physics, ORCA and Push-and-Rotate for multi-agent trajectory planning, and Wave Function Collapse for terrain synthesis, but recombines them with VLM-based open-vocabulary 3D asset retrieval (BLIP over Objaverse) and digital-human populating (SMPL-X + BEDLAM) to create a compositional urban micromobility environment. The key conceptual departure is treating the sidewalk and its functional zones as the primary simulation substrate rather than the roadway, and explicitly modeling vulnerable road users and heterogeneous mobile machines as first-class agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first dedicated platform for AI-driven urban micromobility, MetaUrban-12K provides a much-needed benchmark where the tasks are demonstrably far from solved (≤66% SR for PointNav, ≤36% for SocialNav), giving the community a clear target. The cross-machine evaluation revealing that mechanical specifications (speed, steering, engine force) systematically trade off mobility against safety offers actionable guidance for hardware designers before deployment. Open questions include scaling from multi-block to city-level scenes, incorporating multi-agent learning, extending beyond LiDAR-only observations, and validating sim-to-real transfer for the diverse vehicle classes. The platform's compositional architecture and extensible object repository position it as potential long-term infrastructure for the field.
