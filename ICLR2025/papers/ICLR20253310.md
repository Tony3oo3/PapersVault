## Problem

Partial graph matching in keypoint alignment is complicated by two distinct failure modes—point occlusion and annotation errors—that prior work has conflated. Occlusion-caused missing points may still find correspondences in other graphs, while annotation errors are inherently meaningless and have no valid match. Existing methods either handle only one of these challenges (e.g., URL for occlusion) or apply post-hoc filtering (e.g., AFAT) that is too late to correct already-disrupted matching.

## Input / Output

The method takes pairs of 256×256 RGB images with keypoint annotations, extracts keypoint features via a ResNet50 backbone (concatenating relu3_5 and relu4_1 outputs), refines them with Spline Conv over a Delaunay-triangulation adjacency, and appends a class embedding derived from global image features. It produces a pairwise keypoint matching matrix X_ij = X_iu · X_ju^T, where nodes identified as annotation outliers (energy score above threshold τ) are excluded from matching and reintroduced as unmatched in the final output.

## Method

UGM decomposes ambiguous pairwise partial matching into two well-defined subgraph matching problems via a shared latent universe graph U (a complete directed graph with node and edge embeddings shared across all categories). Each input graph is matched to U using node affinity K_n = N_i N_u^T and edge affinity K_e = E_i E_u^T solved by LPMP, and the pairwise result is recovered as X_ij = X_iu X_ju^T. Training uses cross-entropy loss on the affinities with ground-truth universe matching as supervision, plus a margin-based energy loss that pushes inlier energy scores below m_in and outlier scores above m_out. At inference, an energy score E(v_i) = −T·ln(Σ_j exp(K_n(i,j)/T)) identifies annotation-error outliers, which are filtered before the graph matching solver runs, reducing the problem to a cleaner subgraph matching task.

## Results

On Pascal VOC (unfiltered setting), UGM achieves 73.9% average F1 score, outperforming the next-best method URL (71.7%) by 2.2% and the previous SOTA DLGM (64.8%) by 9.1%. Under the random outlier setting (two outliers per image), UGM reaches 65.1% F1, exceeding GCAN (60.3%) by 4.8%. On Willow Object Class, UGM leads across all three settings: 91.9% (occlusion), 85.4% (random outlier), and 71.1% (occlusion + random outlier), with the largest margin of 9.7% over GCAN in the occlusion setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGM outperforms all 10 baselines on Pascal VOC unfiltered (Table 1) and random outlier (Table 2) settings, as well as all three Willow settings (Table 3). The closest competitor is URL (Nurlanov et al., AAAI 2023), which UGM surpasses by 2.2% F1 on Pascal VOC unfiltered; however, URL's code is unavailable so it is excluded from the random outlier and Willow comparisons. Among learning-based methods, UGM beats GCAN (Jiang et al., CVPR 2022) by 9.3% on Pascal VOC unfiltered and by 4.8% under random outliers, and beats AFAT (Wang et al., CVPR 2023) by 11.9% and 12.0% respectively. On Willow, UGM's advantage is most pronounced in the occlusion setting (91.9% vs. GCAN's 82.2%, a 9.7% gap), attributed to the structured universe graph decomposing partial matching into subgraph matching. Traditional solvers (GAGM, RRWM, BPF, ZAC) using pre-learned affinities lag significantly, with UGM exceeding the best solver ZAC by 16.9% on Pascal VOC unfiltered. Under the combined occlusion + random outlier setting on Willow, UGM maintains 71.1% F1 while AFAT drops to 65.2% and GCAN to 51.7%, demonstrating the synergy between the universe graph and OOD filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends URL's (Nurlanov et al., 2023) universe point representation into a structured universe graph with both node and edge embeddings, and adapts energy-based OOD detection (Liu et al., 2020) for pre-matching annotation error filtering. Conceptually, it departs from prior partial matching approaches (BBGM, GCAN, AFAT) by explicitly distinguishing point occlusion from annotation errors—treating the former as a structural matching problem solvable via the universe graph and the latter as a distributional anomaly detectable via energy scores. The decomposition X_ij = X_iu · X_ju^T reframes ambiguous pairwise partial matching as two well-defined subgraph matching problems, building on the non-negative factorization equivalence established by Bernard et al. (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of occlusion handling (universe graph) from annotation error removal (OOD detection) provides a principled framework that prior methods conflated, and the 9.7% F1 gap over GCAN on Willow occlusion demonstrates the practical value of structured graph matching over flat point representations. However, the approach remains limited to closed-set problems, the complete universe graph creates edge embedding sparsity issues, and errors in X_iu propagate to all pairwise matches. Open questions include whether few-shot adaptation can extend the universe graph to unseen categories, and whether the OOD detection generalizes beyond the specific energy-score formulation used here.
