## Problem

Dynamic Gaussian splatting methods struggle to reconstruct scenes with minimal camera movement because they lack sufficient parallax to constrain Gaussian placement. Existing approaches regularize motion using data-driven depth and optical flow priors applied directly to rendered outputs, but these fail to regularize motion *through* the warp field itself, producing sub-optimal trajectories and overfitting to training viewpoints. The paper asks whether an analytical, physics-grounded velocity field can be derived directly from the forward warp field to enforce continuous motion constraints without additional learnable parameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video sequence, an SfM-initialized point cloud (from COLMAP or Reality Capture), fixed camera intrinsics/extrinsics, per-frame optical flow maps from RAFT, and monocular depth maps from Marigold. It outputs a dynamic 3D Gaussian scene with a learned MLP warp field that maps canonical Gaussians to their deformed states at any time t, enabling novel view synthesis, analytically derived scene flow, and per-Gaussian motion trajectories. Notably, the optical flow and depth are not used as direct pixel-level supervision on rendered frames; instead they serve as targets for scene flow computed by time-integrating the warp field's analytical velocity, decoupling in-plane (flow) and out-of-plane (depth) motion losses.

## Method

The core contribution is a Lucas-Kanade-inspired analytical velocity field derived from the forward warp field. Given a warp field W_c→(G, t) that deforms canonical Gaussians to time t, the velocity v(G; t) is computed as J_G⁺ · ∂W/∂t (Eq. 10–11), where J_G⁺ is the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the warp Jacobian with respect to Gaussian parameters, avoiding numerical instabilities. This velocity is time-integrated via a Runge-Kutta solver with Monte Carlo sampling (10 points) to analytically obtain Gaussian positions at t+Δt. The rendered optical flow and depth from these analytically derived Gaussians are then compared to reference flow/depth, yielding a continuous-time motion loss L_motion = α·L_flow + β·L_depth that regularizes the warp field without extra network modules. This replaces discrete frame-to-frame offset supervision with a differentiable, time-domain constraint that enforces physically consistent trajectories across the entire sequence.

## Results

On the Dynamic Scenes dataset, the proposed method achieves PSNR of 26.34 (Playground), 26.35 (Balloon1), 25.89 (Balloon2), and 23.02 (Umbrella), outperforming Deformable GS and 4DGaussians on most scenes. On Plenoptic Videos, it reaches a mean PSNR of 33.44 (SSIM 0.954, LPIPS 0.068), surpassing Deformable GS (32.46) and 4DGaussians (31.58). On HyperNeRF, it achieves PSNR 27.38 (SSIM 0.81, LPIPS 0.26), improving over 3DGS (20.84) and Deformable GS (26.47). On DyCheck with pose refinement, gains of 4–6 dB over NSFF and Marbles are observed (e.g., 22.61 vs. 17.54 on Apple).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms Deformable GS (Yang et al. 2023) by 1.5–4.0 dB PSNR across Dynamic Scenes and by ~1 dB on Plenoptic Videos and HyperNeRF, confirming that analytical scene flow regularization improves over unregularized warp fields. Against 4DGaussians (Wu et al. 2024), gains of 2–5 dB PSNR are observed on Dynamic Scenes and ~1 dB on Plenoptic Videos/HyperNeRF. On the low-EMF DyCheck benchmark, the method with pose refinement surpasses NSFF (Li et al. 2021) and Marbles (Stearns et al. 2024) by 4–6 dB, though without pose refinement it is competitive but not uniformly superior. Notably, NSFF still outperforms the proposed method on Balloon2 (30.59 vs. 25.89 PSNR) and Umbrella (24.40 vs. 23.02), suggesting the analytical flow regularization is less effective when scene motion is highly structured and the camera is fully static. Compared to Flow-supervised NeRF (Wang et al. 2023), the explicit Gaussian representation yields substantially better results (e.g., 26.34 vs. 16.70 PSNR on Playground), demonstrating the advantage of explicit over implicit scene representations for flow-based regularization. On Plenoptic Videos, MA-GS (Guo et al. 2024) is outperformed by ~1.4 dB mean PSNR, and 3DGS (Kerbl et al. 2023) is surpassed by 6.5 dB on HyperNeRF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical Lucas-Kanade small-motion decomposition to the forward warp field of deformable Gaussian Splatting, drawing on the twist Jacobian formulation from PointNetLK (Li et al., 2021) and the analytical flow supervision pioneered for implicit NeRFs by Wang et al. (2023). It builds directly on Deformable GS (Yang et al., 2023) as the base dynamic Gaussian framework, modifying its MLP-based warp field to enable analytical velocity derivation via time differentiation. The key conceptual departure is from the prevailing practice of using external data-driven priors (monocular depth, optical flow) as regularizers: instead, the paper shows the warp field itself encodes sufficient structure to derive scene flow analytically, shifting the regularization from discrete frame-to-frame supervision to continuous-time constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method enables high-quality dynamic scene reconstruction from near-static cameras (low EMF), a setting where both NeRF and Gaussian Splatting methods have struggled due to insufficient parallax. The finding that direct depth/flow supervision on Deformable GS can actually degrade performance (Table 3) challenges the field's assumption that more external priors always help, suggesting that analytical constraints on the warp field are a more principled alternative. Open questions include sensitivity to SfM initialization quality (dynamic regions with sparse point clouds remain problematic), computational cost of per-Gaussian pseudoinverse operations at scale, and whether the approach extends to topologically changing scenes or long sequences where canonical space assumptions break down.
