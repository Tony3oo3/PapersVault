## Problem

Existing tabular data generation methods struggle with the inherent heterogeneity of mixed-type data: prior diffusion-based approaches either discretize time (yielding looser ELBOs), encode all features into a latent continuous space (introducing encoding overhead and indirect modeling), or apply uniform noise schedules that ignore the vastly different marginal distributions across columns. No prior method combines a principled continuous-time mixed-type diffusion framework with explicit per-feature distribution adaptation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a mixed-type tabular dataset where numerical columns are normalized via QuantileTransformer and categorical columns are one-hot encoded with an additional [MASK] token appended to each category set. During unconditional generation, the output is a full synthetic table row with numerical values recovered through the inverse QuantileTransformer and categorical values decoded from the one-hot representation. For conditional imputation, known columns are held fixed throughout sampling while only the target (missing) columns are denoised, with classifier-free guidance interpolating between conditional and unconditional predictions.

## Method

TABDIFF defines a joint continuous-time forward process where numerical features follow a Variance-Exploding SDE (Gaussian noise) and categorical features follow a masked absorbing diffusion (progressive replacement by [MASK]), both parameterized by per-feature learnable noise schedules: a power-mean schedule (σ_ρ(t)) for numerical columns and a log-linear schedule (α_k(t)) for categorical columns. A single transformer-based denoising network processes the concatenated noisy numerical and one-hot categorical inputs, trained end-to-end with a combined denoising MSE loss (numerical) and continuous-time ELBO (categorical). A key innovation is the stochastic backward sampler, which at each reverse step first perturbs the current state forward by a small time increment before denoising, enabling the model to re-correct previously decoded categorical values and reduce accumulated error. For conditional generation, classifier-free guidance is applied by interpolating the model's conditional and unconditional predictions in log-space for both numerical and categorical outputs.

## Results

On seven tabular datasets, TABDIFF achieves the best average performance across all eight evaluation metrics. On the headline Trend metric (pair-wise column correlation error), TABDIFF reaches 1.80% average error, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art TABSYN (2.33%) by 22.6%. On Shape (marginal distribution error), TABDIFF achieves 1.17% vs. TABSYN's 1.35% (13.3% improvement). On downstream Machine Learning Efficiency, TABDIFF closes the gap to real data to 5.76% average, outperforming TABSYN (6.78%) by 15.0%, and on the C2ST detection score TABDIFF reaches 0.9787 vs. TABSYN's 0.9156.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TABDIFF outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all seven datasets. Against the closest competitor TABSYN (latent-space score-based diffusion), TABDIFF wins on Shape (1.17% vs 1.35%), Trend (1.80% vs 2.33%), α-Precision (98.22 vs 97.86), β-Recall (49.40 vs 46.77), C2ST (0.9787 vs 0.9156), and MLE gap (5.76% vs 6.78%), with the largest margin on Trend (22.6%) and C2ST (6.89%). Among diffusion baselines, TabDDPM fails catastrophically on high-cardinality datasets (News: 78.75% Shape error; Diabetes: 51.54% Trend error) due to training loss explosion, while CoDi and STaSy show moderate performance but are consistently outperformed by TABDIFF. GAN-based CTGAN and VAE-based TVAE/GOGGLE are substantially weaker, with average Shape errors of 15.99%, 15.97%, and 17.91% respectively. The autoregressive GReaT model hits OOM on News and Diabetes and shows highly inconsistent performance (e.g., 70.02% Trend error on Default). Notably, TABDIFF's advantage is most pronounced on the Diabetes dataset (27 categorical columns, max 716 categories), where it surpasses TABSYN by over 35% on both Shape and Trend, demonstrating superior handling of high-cardinality categorical features.

## Relation to Prior Work

TABDIFF unifies two previously separate diffusion paradigms—Gaussian (variance-exploding SDE) diffusion for numerical features and masked (absorbing) diffusion for categorical features—into a single continuous-time joint process operating in native data spaces. This departs from prior tabular diffusion methods like TabDDPM and TABSYN, which encode all features into a continuous latent space before applying Gaussian diffusion, and from CoDi, which uses discrete-time steps for categorical features. The feature-wise learnable noise schedules (power-mean for numerical, log-linear for categorical) are a novel mechanism not present in any prior mixed-type tabular generator, and the stochastic sampler extends restart-sampling ideas from image diffusion to the mixed-type tabular setting. Classifier-free guidance is adapted from the continuous-diffusion literature to handle both numerical and categorical conditional generation simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By modeling mixed-type tabular data in its native space with per-feature adaptive noise schedules, TABDIFF addresses a fundamental limitation of latent-space approaches: encoding overhead and loss of discrete co-occurrence structure. The consistent 13–22% improvements across all eight evaluation metrics, particularly the 22.5% gain on pairwise correlation (Trend), suggest that feature-wise heterogeneity is a first-order factor in tabular generation quality that prior methods under-addressed. Practical limitations include the reliance on QuantileTransformer preprocessing for numerical columns, the relatively small embedding dimension (d=4), and the 8000-epoch training cost. Open questions include scalability to very high-cardinality or very high-dimensional tables, whether the learnable schedules transfer across domains without retraining, and whether the stochastic sampler's benefit persists at very low step counts (e.g., 5 steps).
