## Problem

Existing DM-based recommenders face a fundamental tension in their training objectives: generative losses like MSE model only positive items and ignore negative samples, causing predicted embeddings to drift toward disliked items, while standard recommendation objectives (BPR, cross-entropy) treat DMs merely as noise-robust classifiers and fail to exploit their generative and generalization capabilities. No personalized ranking loss had been specifically designed to integrate negative-sample information into the diffusion generative framework while preserving the model's ability to maximize data log-likelihood.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user's chronological interaction sequence (last 10 items), encoded by a pluggable sequence encoder (GRU, Transformer, or BERT) into a conditioning vector, along with item embeddings (ID-based at dimension 64+ or semantic text embeddings from OpenAI text-embedding-3-large) and in-batch negative samples. During inference, starting from pure Gaussian noise, the denoising network iteratively generates a next-item embedding over 20 DDIM steps, which is then ranked against the full candidate set via maximum inner product search to produce top-K recommendations. The embedding dimension is a critical factor—DM-based recommenders require substantially higher dimensions than non-diffusion baselines to maintain stable feature spaces.

## Method

PreferDiff reformulates the classical BPR objective from a rating-margin loss into a log-likelihood ranking loss by applying softmax normalization, yielding L_BPR-Diff which operates on the log-probability ratio of positive versus negative items under the diffusion model. Because DMs are latent variable models making direct optimization intractable, the paper derives a variational upper bound via Jensen's inequality and ELBO decomposition, reducing the objective to a per-timestep comparison of denoising errors for positive and negative items. The MSE term is replaced with cosine error to align training with the inner-product retrieval used at inference, and multiple negatives are efficiently incorporated by diffusing only their centroid rather than each sample individually, avoiding redundant denoising steps. The final loss balances a generative term (L_Simple) and a preference term (L_BPR-Diff-C) via hyperparameter λ, and gradient analysis reveals that PreferDiff automatically assigns higher weights to hard negatives where the DM incorrectly favors negatives over positives. The paper further establishes that L_BPR-Diff is a special case of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with β=1 and a uniform reference distribution, connecting generative recommendation to the preference-alignment literature.

## Results

PreferDiff achieves the best performance across all metrics on three primary benchmarks (Sports, Beauty, Toys from Amazon). On Sports, it reaches R@5=0.0185 and N@5=0.0147, improving 19.35% over the best baseline (DreamRec, R@5=0.0155). On Beauty, it attains R@5=0.0429 and N@5=0.0323 (5.66% and 9.36% improvements), and on Toys, R@5=0.0473 and N@5=0.0367 (7.50% and 13.62% improvements). All improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.05). Additionally, PreferDiff converges in ~35 epochs (8 min) versus DreamRec's ~65 epochs (15 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreferDiff outperforms all 10 baselines across every metric on all three primary datasets. The closest competitor is DreamRec (the DM-based method it builds upon), which PreferDiff beats by 19.35% on Sports R@5 (0.0185 vs. 0.0155), 5.66% on Beauty R@5 (0.0429 vs. 0.0406), and 7.50% on Toys R@5 (0.0473 vs. 0.0440). Among other DM-based recommenders, DiffRec (Wang et al., 2023) and DiffuRec (Li et al., 2024) trail significantly—DiffRec scores only 0.0125 R@5 on Sports and 0.0195 on Beauty, while DiffuRec reaches 0.0093 and 0.0286 respectively. Traditional sequential recommenders (GRU4Rec, SASRec, BERT4Rec) and contrastive/generative methods (CL4SRec, TIGER) are further behind, with SASRec achieving only 0.0047 R@5 on Sports. Text-based recommenders MoRec and LLM2BERT4Rec perform competitively on Beauty (0.0259 and 0.0379 R@5) but still fall short of PreferDiff's 0.0429. In the zero-shot general recommendation setting (Table 3), PreferDiff-T outperforms UniSRec and MoRec by 2–10% across in-domain, out-domain, and cross-platform (Steam) scenarios without any additional architectural components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DreamRec framework (Yang et al., 2023b) for DM-based sequential recommendation and the DDPM paradigm (Ho et al., 2020) for the forward/reverse diffusion process, but departs from both by replacing their standard MSE or unmodified BPR objectives with a novel log-likelihood ranking loss (L_BPR-Diff) derived through variational inference. The key conceptual departure is reframing BPR's rating-margin objective as a log-likelihood ratio, which the authors then show is a special case of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from the LLM alignment literature, thereby bridging recommendation ranking with generative modeling. The extension to multiple negatives via a centroid strategy and the substitution of MSE with cosine error are practical refinements that distinguish PreferDiff from prior DM-based recommenders like DiffRec and DiffuRec.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PreferDiff provides the first principled ranking loss tailored to DM-based recommenders, addressing a gap where prior work either underutilized the generative capacity of diffusion models (by applying ranking losses) or neglected negative items (by using pure MSE). The theoretical connection to DPO opens a path for preference alignment in generative recommenders, and the zero-shot generalization via text embeddings suggests DM-based recommenders could serve as universal sequential recommenders without retraining. However, the method's strong dependence on high embedding dimensions and the hyperparameter λ limits its practical deployability, and it remains open whether the DPO connection can be exploited for more sophisticated preference alignment (e.g., with a learned reference policy) or whether the dimension sensitivity can be mitigated through architectural changes.
