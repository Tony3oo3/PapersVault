## Problem

Multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) face concept drift in real-world streaming data—both gradual drift from long-tailed class imbalance and sudden drift from out-of-distribution (OOD) categories—but their combined impact on vision-language models, particularly on image-text alignment during pre-training, remains largely unexplored. Existing long-tailed and OOD detection methods for VL models focus almost exclusively on fine-tuning with pre-trained CLIP weights, leaving the pre-training stage and the unified treatment of both drift types unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (384×384, patch size 16×16) and text captions (sequence length up to 512 tokens with a [CLS] token) as paired inputs. During pre-training, it produces L2-normalized image and text feature vectors in a shared hyperspherical space (d=768 for ViT-Base) used for contrastive alignment. During fine-tuning, it outputs a class label via a two-layer linear classifier, a binary OOD/ID decision via non-parametric KNN over the T-hp metric, and a generated caption from the image-grounded text decoder. The authors also introduce OpenMMlo, a ~740k image-caption pair dataset with category annotations spanning ImageNet-LT, iNaturalist 2018, and Places-LT, where captions are generated by InstructBLIP with category-related words masked for evaluation.

## Method

The paper extends concept drift theory to the multi-modal domain by treating each modality as a distinct data stream, then proposes a T-distributed spherical adapter (Thp) that replaces cosine similarity in contrastive pre-training and serves as a router in a mixture-of-experts fine-tuning head. The Thp metric, p(x) ∝ 2/(κ(1−μᵀx)), is derived from directional statistics on the hypersphere and exhibits a light-tailed, high-kurtosis property: its gradient with respect to tail-category centers is provably smaller than the vMF metric's for head-category samples, preventing the feature-space compression that causes tail drift bias. In pre-training, the adapter computes an N×N Thp similarity matrix over image-text pairs (with ALBEF-style soft labels from a momentum encoder) to drive contrastive alignment, while a language modeling loss activates the BLIP-style image-grounded text decoder. In fine-tuning, the T-distributed adapter routes features to FFN experts and explicitly models the feature space with concrete category centers, enabling a simple non-parametric KNN (using the Thp distance to the k-th nearest neighbor) to partition samples into ID and OOD without any additional OOD-specific module or external data.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT (All split), the proposed method achieves 67.6% top-1 accuracy when trained from scratch and 77.2% when fine-tuning from CLIP, outperforming the strongest baselines LiVT† (63.8%) and LIFT (77.0%) respectively. On iNaturalist 2018 (All split), it reaches 78.9% (from scratch) and 81.7% (fine-tuning), surpassing LiVT† (81.0%) and LIFT (79.1%). For OOD detection on CIFAR100-LT, the method achieves the best FPR95 and AUROC across all four external datasets (e.g., 8.3% FPR / 98.7% AUROC on SVHN vs. CIDER's 12.6% / 97.8%). On domain generalization (ImageNet-Sketch), it attains 50.2% accuracy, exceeding DAPT (48.3%), VPT (47.7%), and zero-shot CLIP (46.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the training-from-scratch setting, the proposed method outperforms LiVT† by 3.8 points on ImageNet-LT (All) and matches it on iNaturalist 2018, while substantially exceeding ViT (31.6%), MAE (54.5%), and DeiT (48.4%) on ImageNet-LT, confirming that the T-distributed adapter mitigates pre-training bias from long-tailed data. In the fine-tuning setting (CLIP backbone), the method surpasses LIFT by 0.2 points on ImageNet-LT (All) and by 2.6 points on iNaturalist 2018 (All), and outperforms VL-LTR by 0.5 points on ImageNet-LT while achieving 81.7% vs. 81.0% on iNaturalist. Against zero-shot CLIP, the gap is dramatic on iNaturalist 2018 (81.7% vs. 5.5%), demonstrating that even a model pre-trained on 40M balanced pairs cannot overcome long-tail drift without explicit adaptation. For OOD detection, the method beats the strongest prior CIDER on all four datasets (e.g., 8.3% FPR vs. 12.6% on SVHN; 97.5% AUROC vs. 92.8% on LSUN) without requiring any external OOD data or specialized modules. On domain generalization, it leads DAPT by 1.9 points and zero-shot CLIP by 4.1 points on ImageNet-Sketch, validating the robustness of the T-distributed hyperspherical embeddings under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the T-distributed spherical metric (Thp) from the first author's prior single-modal work on imbalanced classification (Yang et al., AAAI 2023) into the multi-modal vision-language domain, embedding it within a BLIP-style encoder-decoder architecture. It departs from the dominant line of VL long-tail methods (LPT, BALLAD, VL-LTR, LIFT) that operate exclusively in the fine-tuning stage on CLIP-pretrained models, by explicitly addressing drift from pre-training onwards. The concept drift theory from Lu et al. (2019) is generalized from single-stream to multi-stream (multi-modal) settings, and the KNN-based OOD detection follows Sun et al. (2022), while the contrastive pre-training with soft labels follows ALBEF (Li et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical claim—that pre-training on imbalanced data causes fundamental, persistent damage to image-text alignment that fine-tuning alone cannot repair (and can even worsen, as shown by CLIP's few-shot accuracy dropping from 69.6% zero-shot to 39.9% after fine-tuning)—challenges the common practice of simply fine-tuning large pre-trained models on long-tailed data. The OpenMMlo dataset (~740k image-caption pairs) fills a gap in multi-modal long-tailed open-world benchmarks. However, the experiments are conducted at a relatively modest scale (ViT-Base, 115.8K training pairs) compared to modern MLLMs trained on tens of millions of pairs, leaving open whether the T-distributed adapter's benefits persist at the scale of GPT-4V or LLaVA-class models, and whether the BLIP architecture choice limits generalizability of the findings.
