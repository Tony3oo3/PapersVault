## Problem

Standard learning-to-defer (L2D) requires every human expert to annotate the entire training dataset, making annotation prohibitively expensive and limiting dataset scale and diversity. Moreover, existing L2D methods lack a principled mechanism to control workload distribution among experts and the AI classifier, leading to imbalanced workloads where high-performing experts are overloaded while others remain idle. This paper addresses both limitations simultaneously within a unified probabilistic framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251737/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes image samples (RGB or medical, resized to 224×224) along with partially observed expert annotations, where each expert annotates only a subset of the training set at a configurable missing rate. It produces a class label prediction and a deferral decision selecting one of M human experts or the AI classifier, with the gating model outputting a probability over the simplex Δ_M so that deferral probabilities are mutually coupled rather than independent. Workload constraints (per-expert lower and upper bounds ε_l, ε_u) are set as hyperparameters to control the fraction of samples routed to each decision-maker during both training and inference.

## Method

The paper models L2D as a mixture-of-experts latent variable model where the expert selection index z and missing expert annotations t^(j) are treated as latent variables, optimized via the EM algorithm. The E-step uses mean-field variational inference with fixed-point iteration to jointly infer the posterior over z and the missing annotations, while the M-step maximizes the completed-data log-likelihood to update the gating model and AI classifier (ResNet-18) parameters. The key novelty is a workload constraint integrated into the E-step as an I-projection in information geometry: the unconstrained posterior q*(z_i) is projected onto the set of distributions satisfying ε_l ≤ (1/N)Σq̃(z_i) ≤ ε_u, solved efficiently via Lagrange duality with a projected gradient solver. This avoids the brittleness of post-hoc thresholding or cost-based approaches that require extensive hyperparameter tuning and fail to converge for certain coverage values. The full procedure uses online (stochastic) EM with mini-batches, where the constrained E-step operates on each batch to enforce the global workload constraint without additional training overhead.

## Results

The primary metric is the area under the coverage-accuracy curve (AUC-CAC, ×100), where higher is better. The proposed probabilistic L2D achieves the best AUC-CAC across all four datasets and all missing rates. For example, on Cifar-100 (2 experts, 70% missing) it scores 71.19 vs. 67.68 (Naive L2D), 65.52 (MixMatch), and 66.52 (FixMatch); on MiceBone (8 experts, 30% missing) it reaches 83.48 vs. 79.67, 81.02, and 81.87 respectively. On the single-expert NIH-AO dataset (20% missing), the method attains 93.40 AUC-CAC, surpassing all baselines by 1.1–2.0 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two families of baselines: (1) Naive L2D (Madras et al., 2018), modified to handle missing annotations by repeatedly sampling an expert until one with an available annotation is found, and (2) SSL-based L2D (Hemmer et al., 2023), which trains MixMatch or FixMatch classifiers per expert to generate pseudo-labels for missing annotations before training a standard L2D system. The proposed probabilistic method wins on every dataset and missing-rate setting where annotations are incomplete. The gap over Naive L2D is most pronounced on Cifar-100 (up to +3.5 AUC-CAC at 70% missing) and MiceBone (up to +3.8 at 30% missing), while on NIH-AO the margin is smaller (+1.6) due to the single high-accuracy expert. The SSL baselines (MixMatch/FixMatch) degrade sharply on Cifar-100 (dropping ~5–6 points below Naive L2D at 30–70% missing) because the synthetic asymmetric label noise violates the smooth-manifold assumption underlying semi-supervised learning, but they remain competitive on real-world medical datasets where natural label noise is more structured. At zero missing rate, all methods collapse to the same algorithm and share identical scores, confirming the comparison is meaningful only under partial annotation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learning to Defer (L2D) paradigm (Madras et al., 2018) by reformulating it as a mixture-of-experts probabilistic model and applying the EM algorithm to handle missing expert annotations as latent variables. It departs from the only prior work addressing missing annotations (Hemmer et al., 2023), which uses a two-phase semi-supervised learning approach (MixMatch/FixMatch) to generate pseudo-labels before training a standard L2D system; here, the missing annotations are handled end-to-end within a single variational EM loop. The workload control mechanism, formulated as an I-projection in information geometry, replaces the post-hoc thresholding strategy of Mozannar et al. (2023) and the cost-based balancing of Narasimhan et al. (2022) with a principled constraint solved efficiently during the E-step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the requirement that every expert annotate every training sample addresses a major practical bottleneck in deploying L2D systems, particularly in medical imaging where expert time is scarce and expensive. The integrated workload control enables train-test consistent deferral without post-hoc intervention, which is critical for real-world deployment. However, scalability remains a significant limitation: the method requires one model per expert, making it impractical for datasets with hundreds of annotators (the authors acknowledge this and suggest clustering or conditional expert models as future directions). The MiceBone results also reveal that inconsistent expert performance between training and test sets can produce counterintuitive behavior, highlighting the need for more robust handling of expert variability.
