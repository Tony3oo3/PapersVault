## Problem

Standard dense retrieval models optimize a single query-passage similarity score and lose the instruction-following capability of their backbone LMs after IR training. While prior work prepends a fixed, dataset-wide template instruction to every query, no bi-encoder retriever can adapt its notion of relevance per-query via free-form, instance-level natural language instructions the way a prompted LM can.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250637/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the bi-encoder encodes a concatenated query+instruction string (up to 304 tokens) and each passage (256 tokens) independently, then ranks via dot-product similarity. The instruction is free-form natural language that can vary in length (one sentence to two paragraphs) and style (persona, negation, background context), defining per-query relevance criteria such as temporal constraints, exclusions, or additional information requirements. Output is a ranked list of passages scored by the single similarity value.

## Method

Promptriever is a LLaMA-2 7B bi-encoder trained with LoRA on a newly curated ~500K-instance dataset built from MS MARCO. The dataset has two key components: (1) instance-level instructions generated by Llama-3-70B that add diverse, free-form relevance criteria to each query while preserving the original positive passage's relevance, and (2) instruction negatives — passages that are query-relevant but become instruction-irrelevant, generated by GPT-4o and filtered by the FollowIR-7B cross-encoder. The instruction negatives are the critical mechanism: because a passage can be topically relevant yet instruction-irrelevant, the model must condition on both query and instruction to minimize training loss, forcing sensitivity to fine-grained relevance details. Training uses the same contrastive recipe and hyperparameters as RepLLaMA (temperature 0.01, 30 hard negatives per query, one epoch) but with the instruction-augmented data, and the final model jointly trains on both standard and instruction-augmented MS MARCO data.

## Results

On FollowIR, Promptriever achieves an average score of 26.1 with +11.2 p-MRR, outperforming RepLLaMA (23.0, −3.1 p-MRR) by +3.1 nDCG/MAP and +14.3 p-MRR. On InstructIR, it reaches 92.1 nDCG@10 and 63.1 Robustness@10, a +12.9 gain in robustness over RepLLaMA (50.2). On BEIR, zero-shot prompting yields a +1.4 nDCG@10 average improvement (55.0→56.4), while RepLLaMA and BM25 degrade under the same prompts. On in-domain MS MARCO, Promptriever remains comparable to RepLLaMA (73.2 vs. 74.5 DL19 nDCG@10; 42.0 vs. 42.5 Dev MRR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RepLLaMA (same LLaMA-2 backbone and training recipe), Promptriever gains +14.3 p-MRR on FollowIR (−3.1→+11.2) and +12.9 Robustness@10 on InstructIR (50.2→63.1), confirming that the instruction data—not the backbone—drives the improvement. Among bi-encoders, Promptriever outperforms E5-Mistral (p-MRR −3.5 vs. +11.2), Google Gecko (p-MRR +2.3 vs. +11.2), Instructor XL (−2.8 vs. +11.2), and BM25 (−2.1 vs. +11.2) on instruction sensitivity, while also achieving the highest InstructIR nDCG@10 (92.1). Against the strongest cross-encoder FollowIR-7B (p-MRR +12.2, Robust@10 71.5), Promptriever is competitive on p-MRR (+11.2) and leads on InstructIR nDCG (92.1 vs. 81.3), despite using a far more efficient bi-encoder architecture. On BEIR, Promptriever with the best prompt averages 56.4 nDCG@10 versus 54.8 for RepLLaMA with its best prompt and 35.9 for BM25, and the selected-prompt setting (using only 10 dev queries) recovers nearly all of the best-prompt gain. On in-domain MS MARCO, Promptriever is statistically comparable to RepLLaMA (73.2 vs. 74.5 DL19; 72.3 vs. 71.8 DL20), showing that instruction training does not sacrifice standard retrieval quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

Promptriever extends the RepLLaMA training recipe (same LLaMA-2 7B backbone, LoRA, MS MARCO data, hyperparameters) by augmenting queries with instance-level natural language instructions and introducing 'instruction negatives'—passages that are query-relevant but instruction-irrelevant. This departs from prior instruction-tuned retrievers (Instructor, TART, E5-Mistral, GritLM) that prepend a single templated instruction to all queries in a dataset, rather than defining relevance conditions per-instance. Conceptually, the paper bridges the LM prompting literature (InstructGPT, FLAN, T0) with dense retrieval, arguing that the per-instance instruction-following capability of base LMs is lost during standard IR training and can be restored with targeted synthetic data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first bi-encoder retriever that can be zero-shot prompted in free-form natural language to adjust its relevance notion per query, opening the door to applying the full toolkit of LM prompt engineering (chain-of-thought, few-shot selection, persona prompts) to retrieval systems. The released ~500K instruction-augmented dataset and the instruction-negatives data augmentation strategy are reusable components for future work. Key open questions include whether in-context learning (few-shot examples in the prompt) transfers to retrievers, why certain prompts outperform others (the error analysis found no query-level signal), and whether the approach scales to BERT-sized models (it does not, per Appendix G). The paper also acknowledges potential biases in LM-generated training data.
