## Problem

While the individual arithmetic operations of transformer layers are known by design, the collective behavior of token embeddings as they traverse the latent space across successive layers remains poorly characterized. This paper asks whether the vast complexity of LLM internal computation can be captured by a small number of ensemble-level statistical parameters, treating the transformer stack as a complex dynamical system whose macroscopic properties are accessible without understanding microscopic details.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large text corpus (primarily Thoreau's Walden), tokenizes it, and splits it into non-overlapping 50-token pseudo-sentences. For each pseudo-sentence, the hidden state of the last ('pilot') token is extracted at every transformer layer (24 for GPT-2), forming a trajectory in the 1024-dimensional latent space. The output is a parametric stochastic model: a rotation-stretch linear map derived from per-layer SVD, a Gaussian noise term with variance scaling as exp(λ(t+τ)), and a continuous-time Langevin/Fokker-Planck equation describing the ensemble probability density evolution.

## Method

The core approach collects an ensemble of pilot-token trajectories and applies SVD at each layer to identify intrinsic bases that reveal trajectories cluster on a low-dimensional (~256-dimensional) curved manifold rather than filling the full latent space isotropically. Trajectory evolution between layers is then approximated by a linear transformation composed of rotation (by the singular vector basis) and stretch (by the ratio of singular values), plus a residual noise term. The noise is empirically characterized as zero-mean, Gaussian, isotropic, with variance growing exponentially in the sum of source and target layer indices (α≈0.64, λ≈0.18 for GPT-2). This discrete model is extended to continuous time by interpolating the rotation and stretch matrices, yielding a Langevin SDE with a linear drift and multiplicative noise, and an equivalent Fokker-Planck PDE for the probability density. Validation includes simulating the SDE to reproduce true trajectory distributions, null tests with gibberish inputs and untrained/ablated models (where the structure collapses), and replication on Llama 2 7B, Mistral 7B, and Llama 3.2 models.

## Results

Token trajectories in GPT-2 (1024-dim latent space, 24 layers) cluster on a low-dimensional manifold: keeping only K₀=256 principal components (25% of dimensions) recovers the next-token output distribution to within ~10% of the uncorrelated-baseline KL divergence. The linear approximation x(t+τ) ≈ R(t+τ)Λ(t,τ)R(t)ᵀx(t) + w(t,τ) is validated by a linear classifier that can barely separate true from extrapolated positions (50–61% accuracy vs. 50% chance). The residual noise is Gaussian, isotropic, zero-mean, with variance scaling as exp(λ(t+τ)), yielding α≈0.64 and λ≈0.18 for GPT-2. The same stochastic structure (with model-specific α, λ) is confirmed on Llama 2 7B (α≈−5.4, λ≈0.27), Mistral 7B, and Llama 3.2 (1B/3B), though the last layer in the three most recent models shows anomalous variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing interpretability methods; instead it validates its stochastic trajectory model against null baselines. An untrained (reinitialized) GPT-2 produces straight, quasi-parallel trajectories that violate the exponential-variance and Gaussianity assumptions, confirming that the observed manifold structure is a product of training. Gibberish (random-token) inputs cluster on a distinct but adjacent manifold, linearly separable from language at every layer, showing the model encodes linguistic structure in trajectory geometry. Across four trained architectures (GPT-2 355M, Llama 2 7B, Mistral 7B, Llama 3.2 1B/3B), the same rotation-stretch-diffusion pattern holds, with only the noise parameters (α, λ) changing; however, the final layer of Mistral 7B and both Llama 3.2 variants exhibits an out-of-distribution variance spike, suggesting fine-tuning or re-alignment effects that the current model does not capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM interpretability and statistical physics. While prior interpretability work has focused on attention patterns (Vig, 2019), monosemantic features (Bricken et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024), geometric concept relationships (Jiang et al., 2024), or cluster emergence in self-attention (Geshkovski et al., 2024), this work instead treats the transformer stack as a complex dynamical system and characterizes the ensemble statistics of token trajectories ('lines of thought') through successive layers. It repurposes tools from stochastic process theory (Langevin equations, Fokker-Planck equations, Wiener processes) and differential geometry (Stiefel manifold interpolation) to the LLM context, building conceptually on the Neural ODE paradigm (Chen et al., 2019) and the transformer alignment observations of Aubry et al. (2024), but applying them to a fundamentally different question: the statistical geometry of trajectory ensembles rather than individual feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~256 dimensions (25% of the 1024-dim latent space) suffice for next-token prediction, and that the full trajectory dynamics reduce to a two-parameter stochastic model (α, λ) plus SVD-derived rotation/stretch, offers a compact, physically interpretable description of what billions of parameters accomplish. The consistent last-layer anomaly across Llama 2, Mistral, and Llama 3.2 is an open question that may reveal how fine-tuning or re-alignment distorts the otherwise smooth dynamical flow. Key limitations include reliance on a single corpus (Walden) for the primary quantitative results, the inability to connect latent-space positions to output distributions due to the final layer normalization, and the fact that the model is only a first-order approximation. The paper opens a path toward hybrid architectures where the deterministic drift is handled by a small ODE system and the 'meaning' resides in the stochastic component, potentially enabling significant compression.
