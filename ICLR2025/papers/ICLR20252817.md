## Problem

Score distillation-based text-to-3D generation suffers from the Multi-Face Janus problem, where generated 3D assets exhibit repeated patterns across different camera poses because the underlying text-to-image diffusion models are biased toward canonical (frontal) poses. Existing remedies—prompt engineering, negative prompts, or entropy constraints—address symptoms rather than the root distributional bias, leaving the guidance signal skewed during optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (augmented with auxiliary directional phrases like 'from side view, from back view' to ensure all pose sub-distributions are non-zero) and user-provided template images for each discrete pose category (front, back, left, right), which can be manually curated from Stable Diffusion outputs or obtained via Zero-1-to-3. It produces an Instant-NGP hash-grid 3D scene representation optimized over three 15k-iteration stages (USD → SDS → USD), with a fine-tuned U-Net (via LoRA) serving as the particle denoiser. The pose classifier operates on DINOv2 ViT-S/14 features at 16×16 spatial resolution, requiring no additional training data.

## Method

RecDreamer rectifies the text-to-image prior so that the marginal pose distribution becomes uniform, proving (Theorem 1–2) that the rectified noisy density equals the original density multiplied by an auxiliary function r(x_t|y) = ∫ f(c|y)/p_t(c|y)·p(c|x_t,y) dc. This yields Uniform Score Distillation (USD), whose gradient augments the standard VSD gradient with an additional term −E[ω(t)(σ_t/α_t)∇_θ log r(x_t|y)], seamlessly integrating into the existing particle-based optimization without altering the sampling process. To compute r in practice, a training-free pose classifier combines orientation similarity (a patch-matching L2 distance over DINOv2 features masked by PCA-based foreground segmentation) and texture similarity (cosine similarity of [cls] tokens) in a differential AND-gate, followed by low-temperature softmax. The noisy posterior p(c|x_t,y) is approximated via Tweedie's formula as p(c|x̂_0,y), and the marginal p_t(c|y) is tracked online using a time-interval exponential moving average updated during optimization, avoiding any fixed distribution assumption.

## Results

On 22 text prompts, USD (RecDreamer) achieves the best FID (165.97) and unbiased FID (165.25), marginally outperforming VSD (168.19 / 169.66). More notably, USD substantially improves pose consistency: it attains the highest categorical entropy (cEnt = 1.0375) and pose entropy (pEnt = 1.2488), with pEnt exceeding the next-best baseline (SDS at 1.1542) by over 8%. Text-image alignment (CLIP score) is slightly lower than VSD (0.6842 vs. 0.6807), reflecting the trade-off of incorporating back and side views that may not align with predominantly frontal text descriptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USD outperforms all six baselines on both FID and uFID, with the closest competitor being VSD (168.19 vs. 165.97 FID). The most striking advantage is in pose consistency: USD's pEnt of 1.2488 exceeds VSD (1.0676) by ~17% and ESD (1.0928) by ~14%, directly quantifying the reduction of the Multi-Face Janus problem. On cEnt, USD (1.0375) also leads all baselines, though the margin over PerpNeg (1.0348) is modest. The only metric where USD trails is CLIP score (0.6842 vs. VSD's 0.6807), a small gap the authors attribute to multi-view content (back/side views) that deviates from front-oriented text prompts. Among the dedicated Janus-mitigation methods (Debiased-SDS, PerpNeg, ESD), USD dominates on every metric, confirming that distribution rectification is more effective than prompt engineering or entropy regularization for addressing pose bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

RecDreamer extends the Variational Score Distillation (VSD/ProlificDreamer) framework by introducing a distribution rectification term derived from importance sampling, ensuring the target distribution has a uniform pose marginal. It departs from prior Multi-Face Janus mitigations (Debiased-SDS, PerpNeg, ESD, DreamControl) that modify prompts, gradients, or view sampling strategies, instead reshaping the underlying data density itself so that standard score distillation naturally produces pose-consistent geometry. The noisy-state approximation borrows the DPS/Tweedie formulation, and the training-free pose classifier repurposes DINOv2 features with a patch-matching orientation metric, avoiding any additional fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distribution-rectification perspective offers a principled, general mechanism for correcting any marginal bias in 2D diffusion priors used for 3D generation, not just pose, and the unified form of the rectified density across all noise levels (Theorem 2) makes the approach cleanly integrable into existing score distillation pipelines. However, the method is limited by its coarse 4-category pose discretization, its dependence on user-provided template images, and the fact that reweighting cannot generate content absent from the original distribution. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-view 3D priors, whether finer-grained or continuous pose control is feasible, and whether the rectification framework generalizes to other bias dimensions (e.g., lighting, scale) in text-to-3D generation.
