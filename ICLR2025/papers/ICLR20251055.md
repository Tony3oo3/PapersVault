## Problem

Gaussian variational inference on the Bures-Wasserstein manifold requires computing the BW gradient of the potential energy in the forward step of the forward-backward Euler scheme, but this gradient involves intractable expectations that prior methods approximate with single-sample Monte Carlo estimators. These estimators are excessively noisy, particularly in high dimensions, forcing small step sizes and producing slow, unstable convergence that severely limits the practical accuracy of BW-based VI.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a target potential V(x) with access to its gradient and Hessian (typically via automatic differentiation), an initial Gaussian N(m₀, Σ₀), a step size η, and a control variate coefficient c ∈ (0,1]. It outputs a sequence of Gaussian approximations μ_k = N(m_k, Σ_k) converging to the optimal BW projection of the target. The approximation is structurally constrained to non-degenerate Gaussians, and the per-iteration cost is dominated by the O(d³) Cholesky decomposition already needed for sampling; the variance-reduced estimator adds only O(d²) overhead by reusing that factorization.

## Method

The core contribution is a control variate estimator that exploits the fact that, as the VI distribution μ_k approaches the target π, the score function Σ_k^{-1}(X_k − m_k) becomes highly correlated with ∇V(X_k). The variance-reduced estimator b̃_k = ∇V(X_k) − c_k Σ_k^{-1}(X_k − m_k) replaces the standard single-sample MC estimator in the forward step of the FB Euler scheme, while the backward (proximal/JKO) step retains its closed-form solution. The paper proves strictly smaller variance than the MC estimator in a neighborhood of the optimal solution (under mild smoothness of ΔV) and at all large-variance iterates for strongly convex V, and shows this reduction propagates into improved convergence bounds for both convex and strongly convex objectives. An adaptive choice c_k = Tr(∇²V(X_k))/Tr(Σ_k^{-1}) achieves near-optimal variance reduction at negligible extra cost, yielding order-of-magnitude accuracy improvements over prior BW methods in experiments.

## Results

On 200-dimensional Gaussian targets, SVRGVI achieves a final KL divergence of approximately 10⁻², compared to ~10³ for both SGVI and BWGD — a 5-order-of-magnitude improvement. This gap widens with dimensionality: at d=10 the improvement is modest, but by d=200 the previous BW methods are effectively unusable while SVRGVI remains stable and accurate. On the Student's t (d=200) and Bayesian logistic regression (d=200) experiments, SVRGVI is again the most accurate method, with BWGD being unstable and even worse than the Laplace approximation on the Student's t target. In a minibatch comparison, SGVI requires approximately 100 i.i.d. Monte Carlo samples per iteration to match the performance of SVRGVI using a single sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVRGVI dominates both BW-space baselines (BWGD and SGVI) across all experiments by orders of magnitude in final KL divergence, with the gap growing sharply in high dimensions (d=200). BWGD (Lambert et al., 2022) is particularly unstable and on the Student's t target performs worse on average than the non-iterative Laplace approximation. SGVI (Diao et al., 2023) is the closest competitor since it shares the same FB Euler structure, but its single-sample Monte Carlo estimator introduces too much noise, requiring ~100 samples per iteration to approach SVRGVI's single-sample accuracy. Against the Euclidean-geometry baseline EVI (using Roeder et al.'s reparameterization trick with ADAM), SVRGVI clearly outperforms in higher dimensions (d=50, 200), whereas previous BW methods did not. The Laplace approximation, which does not optimize KL divergence but fits a Gaussian at the target mode, is outperformed by SVRGVI on non-Gaussian targets (Student's t, Bayesian logistic regression) but is by definition optimal for Gaussian targets and thus omitted there.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Bures-Wasserstein geometry framework for Gaussian variational inference established by Lambert et al. (2022) and Diao et al. (2023), who introduced forward-backward Euler (JKO) schemes in the BW space with closed-form proximal steps. The specific gap addressed is the high variance of single-sample Monte Carlo estimators for the intractable expectations in the forward step, which Diao et al. identified but did not resolve. The variance-reduced estimator draws on the control variates principle from Roeder et al. (2017) in Euclidean VI, with the key insight that the VI distribution's Stein/Hyvärinen score is a natural correlated control for the target gradient. The paper also notes the recently discovered connection (Yi & Liu, 2023) that Roeder et al.'s Euclidean method and BW methods are different discretizations of the same Särkkä ODE dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes BW-based Gaussian VI practically viable in high dimensions, where previous methods (BWGD, SGVI) degraded to unusable accuracy (5 orders of magnitude gap at d=200). The theoretical guarantees—variance reduction near the optimum for arbitrary targets and globally for strongly log-concave targets—provide clear conditions under which the method should always be preferred. Limitations include the restriction to Gaussian approximations, the cubic cost of Hessian computation, and the fact that the variance reduction is a single-sample technique that does not address the fundamental approximation error of the Gaussian family. Open directions include porting more advanced variance reduction techniques from Euclidean VI (Kim et al. 2023, Domke et al. 2024) to the BW setting and extending beyond Gaussian approximating families.
