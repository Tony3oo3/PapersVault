## Problem

Diffusion-based audio waveform reconstruction models suffer from two compounding latency bottlenecks: they require many sampling steps (50–200) and operate at the individual sample-point level, necessitating multiple upsampling layers that inflate sequence length and GPU memory. This makes them an order of magnitude slower than GAN-based vocoders, limiting real-time applicability, while GANs themselves suffer from training instability and mode collapse. The paper asks whether a diffusion-type model can match GAN-level inference speed without sacrificing reconstruction quality.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a 100-bin Mel-spectrogram or discrete EnCodec tokens (at 1.5, 3.0, 6.0, or 12.0 kbps bandwidths) and produces a full-band audio waveform at 22.05/24 kHz or 44.1 kHz. Internally, the full-band complex spectrogram is split into 8 equally-spaced subbands that are processed in a single parallel batch, and the neural network operates exclusively at the STFT frame level (128-frame crops), enabling 177-second clips within 30 GB of GPU memory versus 6 seconds for sample-point-level baselines like PriorGrad.

## Method

RFWave combines Rectified Flow (straight-line transport between noise and data) with a multi-band ConvNeXtV2 backbone that predicts velocity fields for all 8 frequency subbands simultaneously in one forward pass, avoiding the error accumulation of sequential band conditioning. The model offers two operating modes—time-domain (requiring STFT/ISTFT at each Euler step but yielding slightly better high-frequency detail) and frequency-domain (requiring only a single ISTFT after sampling)—and is trained with three enhanced losses: an energy-balanced loss that reweights MSE by per-frame subband energy to suppress silent-region noise, an overlap loss that enforces consistency across subband boundaries via 8-bin circular padding, and an STFT magnitude loss that reduces background-noise artifacts. A novel 'equal straightness' scheduling strategy selects non-uniform Euler time points so that each step covers an equal increment of trajectory curvature, improving sample quality at the same 10-step budget. The result is a 18.1 M-parameter model achieving 162× real-time inference on an RTX 4090 with quality on par with or exceeding GAN vocoders, and notably superior out-of-domain generalization.

## Results

On the Mel-spectrogram input benchmark (averaged across LibriTTS, Opencpop, and MTG-Jamendo), RFWave achieves a MOS of 3.95±0.09, PESQ of 4.202, and ViSQOL of 4.456 with only 10 sampling steps, substantially outperforming PriorGrad (MOS 3.75) and FreGrad (MOS 2.99). On the out-of-domain MUSDB18 test set, RFWave achieves an average MOS of 3.67, surpassing BigVGAN (3.51) and Vocos (3.10). Inference speed reaches 162.59× real-time on an RTX 4090 with 780 MB GPU memory, more than 2× faster than BigVGAN (72.68×RT) and ~10× faster than PriorGrad (16.67×RT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against diffusion-based vocoders, RFWave dominates: it outperforms PriorGrad by +0.20 MOS and +0.59 PESQ, and FreGrad by +0.96 MOS and +0.56 PESQ, while also achieving 10× and 22× faster inference (162.59×RT vs 16.67×RT and 7.50×RT). Against GAN-based models on the in-domain LibriTTS set, RFWave is broadly competitive with BigVGAN (winning on MOS 3.82 vs 3.78 and PESQ 4.304 vs 4.240, but trailing on ViSQOL 4.579 vs 4.712 and Periodicity 0.091 vs 0.067), and clearly beats Vocos on MOS, PESQ, and Periodicity. On the out-of-domain MUSDB18 set, RFWave's advantage over GANs becomes decisive, with an average MOS of 3.67 vs 3.51 (BigVGAN) and 3.10 (Vocos), attributed to GANs producing horizontal-line artifacts in high-frequency regions. For discrete EnCodec token decoding, RFWave (with CFG 2.0) surpasses both EnCodec's GAN decoder and MBD on MOS at every bandwidth (e.g., 3.69 vs 3.10 vs 3.43 at 6 kbps), though EnCodec retains a slight edge on ViSQOL, which the authors attribute to a metric bias toward GAN waveform characteristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

RFWave builds directly on Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2023) as its generative backbone, replacing the curved-trajectory ODE of standard diffusion with straight-line transport to reduce sampling steps from dozens to 10. It adapts the multi-band decomposition strategy from Multi-band Diffusion (Roman et al., 2023) but departs in two key ways: subbands are extracted by directly slicing complex spectrogram dimensions rather than applying band-pass filters, and all subbands are processed in parallel by a single unified ConvNeXtV2 model rather than sequentially conditioned on lower bands. The frame-level (STFT-frame) operation follows the efficiency philosophy of Vocos and APNet2, while the three enhanced loss functions (energy-balanced, overlap, STFT) and the equal-straightness sampling schedule are novel contributions specific to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 160× real-time inference on a single GPU with only 10 sampling steps, RFWave closes the speed gap between diffusion-type and GAN-based vocoders, making flow-matching vocoders practically viable in real-time pipelines (e.g., streaming TTS, live audio generation) where GANs have been the default. The demonstrated out-of-domain generalization advantage over BigVGAN and Vocos on MUSDB18 suggests diffusion-type vocoders may be more robust for production systems handling diverse audio content. Open questions include whether the 10-step budget can be further reduced, whether the multi-band parallel strategy scales to very high sample rates (96 kHz+), and whether the frame-level formulation can be extended to non-stationary or spatial audio.
