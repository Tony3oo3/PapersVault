## Problem

Speculative Decoding (SD) accelerates LLM inference by drafting tokens with a small model and verifying them in parallel, but it has only been applied to N-to-1 verification (single response) in NLP tasks. LLM-based generative recommendation requires generating top-K distinct item sequences via beam search, creating a far more stringent N-to-K verification where all K sequences must be successfully drafted at every step to skip an LLM call. This paper is the first to formulate and solve SD under N-to-K verification for recommendation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253300/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user's historical interaction sequence (encoded as token sequences) and operates with a small draft model (LLaMA-68M) and a large target LLM (LLaMA-7B). Items are represented as learnable token identifiers of fixed length L=4 generated via RQ-VAE, not natural language titles. The output is K distinct item token sequences (K ∈ {1,3,5,10,20}) produced with the same distribution as the target LLM's beam search, but with roughly 2× fewer target LLM forward passes. The draft model generates N=40 beam candidates per step, which are verified in a single parallel LLM call using tree-based attention.

## Method

AtSpeed proposes two alignment objectives tailored to two verification strategies. Under strict top-K verification (AtSpeed-S), the draft model is trained to minimize Reverse KL Divergence (RKLD) plus a density regularization term over constrained valid tokens, directly optimizing the probability that all K target sequences appear among the N drafted candidates. Under a novel relaxed sampling verification (AtSpeed-R), non-top-K drafted sequences with high draft probability can be accepted with probability p(y)/q(y), and the alignment objective minimizes Total Variation Distance (TVD) on top-K sequence generation probabilities, provably preserving the output distribution. Both variants use constrained alignment (restricting to valid item tokens via ConstrainedTopK) to avoid noise from high-probability but invalid tokens, and employ tree-based attention to verify all γN drafted beam sequences in a single flattened LLM call, eliminating redundant prefix computations.

## Results

AtSpeed achieves near 2× walltime speedup under strict top-K verification (1.97× on Beauty, 1.83× on Games) while producing identical recommendation results to the target LLM. Under the proposed relaxed sampling verification, AtSpeed-R further boosts speedup to 2.11× (Beauty) and 2.05× (Games) with only marginal drops in Recall@5 and NDCG@5. The accept steps (average accepted beam search steps per user) reach 1.80 (Beauty) and 1.72 (Games) under strict verification, and 2.10 and 2.02 under relaxed verification, confirming that the N-to-K verification challenge is effectively addressed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AtSpeed-S outperforms all five baselines (DARE, SFT, WordKD, TVDKD, SeqKD) on both datasets under strict top-K verification, achieving the highest average walltime speedup (1.97× Beauty, 1.83× Games) and accept steps. The strongest baseline, SeqKD, trails AtSpeed-S by 0.14× (Beauty) and 0.12× (Games) in average speedup under strict verification. Under relaxed sampling verification, AtSpeed-R achieves the best speedup (2.11× Beauty, 2.05× Games), surpassing SeqKD by 0.10× and 0.10× respectively. Logit-level KD methods (WordKD, TVDKD) underperform SFT in several settings, attributed to the difficulty of aligning across the full vocabulary with a small draft model and the noise from invalid high-probability tokens. DARE, a retrieval-based drafting method, consistently lags behind all alignment-based methods, particularly under strict verification where it achieves near-zero accept steps on Games (0.03), indicating poor top-K sequence alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the speculative decoding (SD) paradigm from Leviathan et al. (2023), which operates under N-to-1 verification for NLP tasks, to the substantially harder N-to-K verification required by beam search in LLM-based generative recommendation. It also leverages tree-based attention from SpecInfer (Miao et al., 2024) to make parallel verification of multiple drafted beam sequences computationally efficient. Conceptually, the paper bridges two research threads—speculative decoding and knowledge distillation for LLMs—by proposing alignment objectives (RKLD-based AtSpeed-S and TVD-based AtSpeed-R) that are specifically designed to maximize top-K sequence recall rather than single-token or single-sequence agreement, departing from prior KD-for-SD work (e.g., DistillSpec) that targets N-to-1 settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLM-based generative recommendation has shown strong accuracy but remains impractical for real-world deployment due to autoregressive decoding latency; a near-lossless ~2× speedup (up to 2.5× with relaxed verification) directly addresses this bottleneck. The N-to-K verification formulation is a genuine generalization that prior SD literature has not addressed, and the relaxed sampling verification strategy offers a principled accuracy-speed tradeoff for beam search settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond LC-Rec/LLaMA-7B to other LLM-based recommenders and item tokenization schemes, whether non-LLM draft models (e.g., traditional recommenders) could further reduce drafting overhead, and how the method interacts with other acceleration techniques like quantization or KV-cache optimization.
