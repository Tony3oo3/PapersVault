## Problem

Existing pretext tasks for patch-based time series models, such as mask-and-reconstruct or complementary contrastive learning, fail to explicitly capture the sequential order dependencies between patches in both time and channel dimensions. This omission means models do not learn the intrinsic structural ordering that is critical for accurate time series classification, leading to subpar performance on tasks where patch sequence arrangement carries discriminative information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251850/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×D} and segments each channel into N non-overlapping patches of size s (typically 10–40 patches per channel), which are encoded into latent patch representations p_{(i,d)} ∈ R^{d_h} by a backbone model (PatchTST or PITS). Channel-wise permutations with controlled intensity (weak: γ=1,2,3; strong: γ=10,40) generate three sequence variants—Original, Weak, and Strong—that feed into the pretext losses. In supervised mode the output is a class label; in self-supervised mode the learned patch embeddings are evaluated via linear probing, and the pretext task itself produces binary consistency predictions and contrastive context vectors from single-layer unidirectional LSTMs.

## Method

PPT introduces channel-wise patch permutation as the core augmentation: within each channel, pairs of patches are randomly swapped with frequency γ, producing weak (few swaps) and strong (many swaps) permuted sequences. Two complementary losses exploit these sequences: (1) a consistency loss where autoregressive LSTMs summarize time-order and channel-order patch sequences into context vectors, which are then classified as original (label 1) vs. strongly permuted (label 0) via binary cross-entropy; and (2) a contrastive InfoNCE loss where the original context vector is the anchor, the weakly permuted vector is the positive, and strongly permuted vectors from all channels serve as negatives. Both losses operate in time and channel dimensions and are weighted by learnable parameters (homoscedastic uncertainty), allowing PPT to function as a standalone SSL objective or as an additive term to supervised cross-entropy. The permutation is implemented efficiently via a pre-built bank of 1000 index permutations, and the paper additionally proposes the ACF-CoS metric—cosine similarity between autocorrelation functions of original and patch-shuffled series—to pre-assess whether a given dataset's orderness justifies applying PPT.

## Results

In self-supervised linear probing, PatchTST+PPT achieves the best accuracy on EMO (81.92%) and GL (92.33%), while PITS+PPT achieves the best on PTB (86.48%). PPT consistently outperforms the original pretext tasks of both backbones: PatchTST+PPT improves over PatchTST by +3.2% on EMO and +3.9% on GL, while PITS+PPT improves over PITS by +5.9% on EMO, +1.9% on PTB, and +4.8% on GL. In supervised training, PPT yields up to 7% accuracy gain on PTB ECG (PITS: 89.5% vs 82.0% baseline) and increases the Original-vs-Permuted accuracy gap (Diff) by up to 23.3 points on GL, confirming enhanced order awareness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPT (applied to PatchTST or PITS) outperforms all 11 non-patch and patch-based baselines on at least two of the three main datasets. Against the strongest non-patch SSL methods, PatchTST+PPT beats SimMTM (81.92% vs 81.75% on EMO) and TS-GAC (92.33% vs 92.32% on GL, a near-tie), while PITS+PPT surpasses UniTS on PTB (86.48% vs 84.20%). Against the original pretext tasks of the two backbones, PPT wins on EMO and GL for both models but slightly underperforms PatchTST's mask-reconstruct on PTB (78.50% vs 79.89%), suggesting order learning is less beneficial when the task's ACF-CoS is moderate. PITS+PPT shows the largest relative gains over PITS's original contrastive pretext (+5.9% on EMO, +4.8% on GL), indicating that PPT's order-aware signals complement and exceed the complementary-patch contrastive objective. In supervised training (Table 3), PPT consistently improves Original accuracy and increases the Original-vs-Permuted gap across all three datasets, with the largest Diff gain of 24.6 points for PITS on PTB, confirming that the learned representations are more order-sensitive than those from cross-entropy alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPT is conceptually inspired by TS-TCC's weak/strong augmentation framework but inverts its contrastive objective: rather than pulling weak and strong representations together, PPT pushes them apart while anchoring the weak view to the original sequence. It departs from existing patch-based pretext tasks (mask-reconstruction in PatchTST, complementary contrastive learning in PITS) by explicitly targeting patch order dependencies across both time and channel dimensions via controlled channel-wise permutations. The design also draws on order-aware pretext tasks from vision (jigsaw, position prediction) and video (temporal self-supervision), but is the first to formalize this for patch-based time series with a dual consistency-plus-contrastive loss structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PPT offers a lightweight, plug-in pretext task that consistently improves patch-based time series classifiers without architectural changes, and the ACF-CoS metric gives practitioners a cheap diagnostic to predict whether order learning will help on a given dataset. Key limitations include minimal gains on forecasting tasks (where permutation disrupts trend structure), sensitivity of ACF-CoS to patch size, and the added computational overhead of autoregressive LSTM modules during training. Open questions include whether a universal pretext task can handle both classification and forecasting, and whether more robust orderness metrics can replace the patch-size-dependent ACF-CoS.
