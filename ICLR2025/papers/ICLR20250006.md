## Problem

Standard Transformer softmax attention over-allocates scores to irrelevant context, creating 'attention noise' that drowns out the correct answer. This leads to poor key information retrieval, contextual hallucination, and sensitivity to in-context example ordering. The paper asks whether a simple architectural modification to the attention mechanism can cancel this noise while preserving the macro Transformer layout.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250006/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a tokenized text sequence (up to 64K tokens in long-context experiments) and produces next-token predictions via a standard decoder-only LM head. Internally, the attention mechanism projects the input into two query groups (Q1, Q2), two key groups (K1, K2), and a single value matrix V of dimension 2d, then computes the output as (softmax(Q1K1ᵀ/√d) − λ·softmax(Q2K2ᵀ/√d))·V, where λ is a learnable scalar initialized per-layer. The number of attention heads is halved relative to a same-size Transformer to keep total parameters and FLOPs matched.

## Method

Differential Transformer replaces standard softmax attention with a differential attention operator that subtracts two independently computed softmax attention maps, analogous to a differential amplifier canceling common-mode noise. Query and key projections are split into two groups of dimension d each (so W^Q, W^K ∈ R^{d_model×2d}), while the value projection has dimension 2d; a learnable scalar λ (re-parameterized via exponentials of learnable vectors for stable optimization) controls the subtraction weight. In the multi-head variant, each head applies its own differential attention, and per-head GroupNorm with a fixed multiplier (1−λ_init) is used to normalize the sparser, more diverse head statistics and align gradient magnitudes with standard Transformer, enabling direct reuse of LLaMA-style hyperparameters. The rest of the architecture (pre-RMSNorm, SwiGLU FFN, RoPE positional encoding) is unchanged, and the mechanism is compatible with FlashAttention for efficient training and inference.

## Results

On the LM Eval Harness benchmark (1T training tokens), DIFF-3B achieves an average zero-shot accuracy of 60.6%, outperforming OpenLLaMA-3B-v2 (57.5%) and StableLM-base-alpha-3B-v2 (56.8%). In scaling experiments, DIFF Transformer requires only ~65% of the model size or training tokens to match Transformer's language modeling loss: a 6.8B DIFF model matches an 11B Transformer (62.2% parameters), and 160B training tokens for DIFF match 251B tokens for Transformer (63.7%). In the controlled 350B-token comparison (Appendix B), DIFF-3B achieves 56.2% zero-shot and 58.0% 5-shot average accuracy versus 55.4% and 56.4% for Transformer-3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard Transformer (with LLaMA-style RMSNorm, SwiGLU, no bias), DIFF Transformer wins on every evaluated metric: in the controlled 350B-token comparison, DIFF-3B gains +0.8% zero-shot and +1.6% 5-shot average accuracy; in needle-in-a-haystack retrieval at N=6, R=2, DIFF achieves 0.85 vs 0.55 accuracy (30% gap); and in hallucination evaluation, DIFF improves accuracy by 9–19 points across summarization and QA datasets. Against OpenLLaMA-3B-v2 (Geng & Liu, 2023), DIFF-3B leads by +3.1 points in average accuracy (60.6 vs 57.5), with the largest gap on ARC-C (+3.9) and WinoGrande (+4.2). Against StableLM-base-alpha-3B-v2 (Tow, 2023), the margin is +3.8 points in average accuracy (60.6 vs 56.8). Against StableLM-3B-4E1T (Tow et al., 2023), only partial metrics are available, but DIFF-3B exceeds it on ARC-E (72.9 vs 66.6) and WinoGrande (67.1 vs 63.2) while matching PIQA (76.8). The scaling experiments further show that DIFF Transformer consistently requires fewer parameters or tokens to reach the same loss, indicating a systematic architectural advantage rather than a single-task win.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the standard Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) with LLaMA-style modifications (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, no bias), replacing only the softmax attention computation with a differential attention mechanism that subtracts two independently computed softmax attention maps. The design draws an explicit analogy to differential amplifiers and noise-canceling headphones from electrical engineering, and is supported by spectral analysis from Naderi et al. (2024) showing that differential attention resolves rank collapse. The paper positions itself as a drop-in 'foundation architecture' that inherits Transformer's macro layout, hyperparameters, and FlashAttention compatibility while addressing the well-documented attention noise problem (Kamradt, 2023; Liu et al., 2024b) where models over-allocate attention to irrelevant context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a ~35% reduction in required model size or training tokens (if the scaling trend holds), an ~8× reduction in activation outliers that directly enables lower-bit quantization of attention logits, and improved robustness to in-context example ordering—a chronic issue in LLM deployment. Key open questions include whether the 65% efficiency advantage persists at 100B+ scale (current evidence tops out at 13B with only 10B tokens), how differential attention interacts with other emerging architectural innovations (e.g., MoE, sliding window attention), and whether the sparser attention patterns can be exploited for KV-cache compression as the authors suggest. The ~9–12% throughput overhead is a minor cost, but the long-term impact depends on whether the community adopts this as a default attention variant.
