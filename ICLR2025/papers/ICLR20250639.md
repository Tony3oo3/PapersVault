## Problem

Existing protections against unauthorized diffusion customization (e.g., LoRA/DreamBooth, SDEdit) rely on untargeted adversarial attacks that maximize score-function error, but under the small perturbation budgets needed to preserve visual quality, they only add chaotic patterns to parts of the output image while leaving the main content intact. The paper asks whether a targeted attack—previously attempted but unsuccessful (e.g., ASPL-T)—can be redesigned to produce uniformly degraded, unusable customization outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250639/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (512×512 for SD1.x, 768×768 for SD2.x) and the parameters of a Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion 1.5 as the default backbone) as input, and produces a single adversarially perturbed image x′ = x + η with element-wise perturbation bounded by ζ = 4/255 (L2-constrained). The protected image is visually nearly indistinguishable from the original but, when used as a reference for LoRA fine-tuning (20 images per subject) or SDEdit (strength 0.3), causes the customized model to generate images covered in high-contrast chaotic patterns that render them unusable.

## Method

ACE (Attacking with Consistent score-function Errors) replaces the untargeted objective of maximizing score-function error with a targeted objective that minimizes the L2 distance between the predicted score function s_θ(z′(t), t) and a fixed chaotic-pattern target T in the LDM latent space, applied consistently across all protected images. ACE+ augments this with an additional term minimizing ‖z′(0) − T‖² to directly steer the latent representation toward the target, improving SDEdit protection. The target T is a high-contrast, repetitive motif (Moire-like pattern) designed to be visually chaotic to human perception. Optimization uses PGD (50 steps, step size 5×10⁻³) interleaved with 5-step LoRA fine-tuning per iteration (4 total iterations), following ASPL's poisoning pipeline. The key mechanistic insight is that a unified target forces the customized model to learn a single reversal bias during fine-tuning, which then propagates as a consistent chaotic texture across all generated images—something untargeted attacks cannot achieve because their per-image error patterns conflict during training.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ with LoRA, ACE achieves a Face Detection Failure Rate (FDFR) of 1.00, meaning 100% of customized images fail face detection, compared to 0.33 for the strongest baseline ASPL. ACE+ attains the best CLIP-IQA scores of 35.68 (CelebA-HQ LoRA) and 48.53 (WikiArt LoRA), while ACE* reaches 76.50 CLIP-IQA on WikiArt LoRA. For SDEdit, ACE achieves the lowest MS-SSIM (0.73 on CelebA-HQ, 0.23 on WikiArt) and ACE+ the lowest CLIP-SIM (67.47 on CelebA-HQ), indicating the strongest structural and semantic degradation of output images. All experiments use a perturbation budget of 4/255, smaller than the 8/255 or 16/255 budgets used in prior work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACE/ACE+ outperform all three baseline families (PhotoGuard, AdvDM, and ASPL) across both CelebA-HQ and WikiArt on both SDEdit and LoRA. Against the strongest baseline ASPL, ACE achieves a dramatically higher FDFR of 1.00 vs. 0.33 on CelebA-HQ LoRA, and ACE+ improves CLIP-IQA from 33.62 to 35.68 (CelebA-HQ) and from 46.74 to 48.53 (WikiArt). On SDEdit, ACE reduces MS-SSIM from 0.82 (ASPL) to 0.73 on CelebA-HQ and from 0.30 to 0.23 on WikiArt, while ACE+ reduces CLIP-SIM from 84.12 to 67.47 on CelebA-HQ. PhotoGuard (PG/PG+) and AdvDM are consistently the weakest baselines, with FDFR values near 0.02–0.04 and CLIP-IQA scores 5–10 points below ASPL. The user study with 1,304 artists confirms ACE produces the worst-quality customization images in 55% of head-to-head comparisons against ASPL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial-attack-based protections against unauthorized diffusion customization (PhotoGuard, AdvDM, ASPL/Anti-DreamBooth), all of which rely on untargeted attacks that maximize score-function error. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the targeted-attack paradigm from classifier adversarial robustness—replacing the ground-truth score function with a fixed chaotic-pattern target and flipping maximization to minimization—yielding ACE/ACE+. It also incorporates the LDM-specific targeted objective from MIST (Liang & Wu, 2023) as an auxiliary term in ACE+, and follows ASPL's poisoning pipeline with fine-tuning steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACE is the first targeted-attack-based protection to consistently outperform all untargeted baselines across two mainstream customization methods (LoRA+DreamBooth and SDEdit), achieving 100% face-detection failure under a small perturbation budget. The proposed hypothesis—that targeted attacks unify the score-function error pattern so the fine-tuned model learns a single reversal bias—offers a mechanistic explanation that could guide future protection design. Open questions include a rigorous theoretical treatment of this mechanism, robustness against advanced purification (e.g., IMPRESS, GrIDPure), and whether the different failure mode observed on Stable Diffusion 3 (semantic deviation rather than quality degradation) generalizes to other DiT-based architectures.
