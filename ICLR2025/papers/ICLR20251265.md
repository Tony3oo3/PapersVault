## Problem

Existing black-box defenses for VLMs against jailbreak attacks are either unimodal (protecting only the visual or textual modality) or bimodal but fail to fully exploit cross-modal information, often degrading performance on benign inputs. Additionally, no prior defense can handle universal adversarial perturbation (UAP)–based jailbreaks, and detection-based approaches (e.g., Jailguard, CIDER) merely reject malicious prompts rather than enabling the model to respond correctly.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair consisting of a potentially adversarial RGB image and a malicious text prompt. It produces a purified image (via iterative diffusion denoising), a semantically preserved rewritten text prompt (via an LLM template that adds clarifying questions around key concepts), and a fixed-length blue-team suffix generated by a lightweight GPT-2 model. The final input to the target VLM is the purified image concatenated with the rewritten text plus the appended suffix, so the VLM receives a single augmented textual prompt alongside a clean image.

## Method

BlueSuffix comprises three plug-and-play components: a DiffPure-based image purifier that removes adversarial perturbations via forward-and-reverse diffusion, an LLM-based text purifier (GPT-4o or Llama-3-8B-Instruct) that rewrites jailbreak prompts into more explicit, safety-aware formulations without changing their meaning, and a GPT-2-based blue-team suffix generator fine-tuned with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The key innovation is the cross-modal optimization strategy: the suffix generator's reward signal is the safety score (0 or 1) assigned by an LLM judge to the target VLM's response, which inherently depends on both the purified image and the rewritten text, thereby encoding cross-modal interactions into the suffix. A KL-divergence penalty against a frozen reference policy prevents mode collapse while keeping the suffix semantically coherent. The generator is trained on hard BAP-attack prompts from MM-SafetyBench yet generalizes to unseen attacks (GCG, AutoDAN, VAA, imgJP), unseen datasets (AdvBench, RedTeam-2K, Harmful Instructions), and unseen target models (including the commercial Gemini API), reducing ASR by ~70% on open-source VLMs and ~50% on Gemini.

## Results

BlueSuffix achieves 0.00% Attack Success Rate (ASR) against all unimodal attacks (VAA, ImgJP, GCG, AutoDAN) across all four target VLMs (LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, InstructionBLIP, Gemini). On the challenging bimodal BAP attack (MM-SafetyBench), BlueSuffix reduces ASR by ~70% on open-source models (LLaVA: 61.02%→4.65%, MiniGPT-4: 62.26%→9.37%, InstructionBLIP: 58.48%→6.78%) and by ~50% on the commercial Gemini model (~41%→~0.4%). On benign prompts (AlpacaEval), BlueSuffix maintains a Benign Passing Rate of 78.00%, only 3.60% below the undefended baseline of 81.60%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlueSuffix outperforms all five baselines on the BAP attack across every model. Against the strongest unimodal baseline DiffPure, BlueSuffix reduces ASR from 32.47% to 4.65% on LLaVA (a 27.8-point gap) and from 22.83% to 9.37% on MiniGPT-4. The combined DiffPure+Safety Prompt baseline (the best unimodal combination) still trails BlueSuffix by 7.19 points on LLaVA (11.84% vs 4.65%) and 6.70 points on MiniGPT-4 (16.07% vs 9.37%). The white-box baselines R2D2 (46.55%) and CAT (41.85%) are substantially weaker than BlueSuffix on LLaVA, confirming that black-box purification plus a learned suffix can surpass parameter-level adversarial training. VLGuard, the only other bimodal baseline tested, achieves 21.67% ASR on LLaVA—nearly 5× worse than BlueSuffix's 4.65%. On the commercial Gemini model, BlueSuffix (0.30–0.47%) marginally improves over DiffPure+Safety Prompt (2.10–2.92%), reflecting Gemini's already-strong built-in safety mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

BlueSuffix builds on DiffPure (Nie et al., 2022) for diffusion-based image purification and extends the black-box defense paradigm for VLMs beyond detection-based approaches like Jailguard and CIDER. While prior bimodal defenses (Jailguard, CIDER, VLGuard) focus on detecting and rejecting malicious inputs, BlueSuffix departs by adopting a 'blue-team' strategy: it purifies both modalities and appends an RL-fine-tuned suffix (via PPO on a GPT-2 backbone) that actively guides the target VLM toward safe responses, rather than merely flagging threats. The cross-modal optimization objective, which uses the target VLM's own output as reward signal through an LLM judge, is the key conceptual departure from unimodal purification or static safety prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BlueSuffix provides a practical, plug-and-play, model-agnostic defense that achieves near-zero ASR on unimodal attacks and ~70% reduction on the hardest bimodal attack (BAP) across open-source VLMs, with strong transferability to a commercial model (Gemini) despite the suffix being trained only on LLaVA responses. The residual ~5–9% ASR on BAP and the reliance on an LLM judge (GPT-4o) for both text purification and reward signal are practical limitations that constrain deployment. Open questions include whether the RL-fine-tuned suffix generalizes to future VLM architectures with different fusion mechanisms, and whether more sophisticated adaptive attacks (beyond the simple re-optimization tested) could erode the defense further.
