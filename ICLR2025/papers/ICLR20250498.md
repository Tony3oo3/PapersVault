## Problem

Timestep-distilled diffusion models (e.g., SDXL-DMD2, SDXL-Turbo) generate high-quality images in 1–4 steps, but naively fine-tuning them with the standard diffusion loss produces blurry, degraded outputs because the distilled models lose the ability to predict local scores. The alternative of fine-tuning the multi-step teacher and re-running the full distillation pipeline is prohibitively expensive (~4000 A100 GPU hours for DMD2), making it impractical for lightweight customization tasks like preference alignment, style transfer, or concept injection.

## Input / Output

The method operates on latent diffusion models (SDXL family) conditioned on text prompts, producing RGB images in 1–4 denoising steps. For preference tuning, it consumes pre-collected win-lose image pairs (Pick-a-Pic v2, ~850K pairs) or online-sampled pairs labeled by a reward model (PickScore). For style transfer and concept customization, it takes small sets of target images (e.g., ~5 Dreambooth images or Pokemon-style pairs) and samples reference images on-the-fly from the current model. The output is a LoRA-adapted distilled model (rank 16–32) that retains its few-step inference schedule while shifting its output distribution toward the target.

## Method

PSO formulates the few-step denoising process as a Markov Decision Process where each timestep is a state-action pair, then applies a DPO-inspired margin objective that maximizes the log-likelihood ratio between a target trajectory (from the desired data distribution, obtained via forward diffusion) and a reference trajectory (from the current model's generative process). The key insight is that by optimizing the *relative* likelihood gap rather than the absolute diffusion loss, the model preserves its few-step generation capability while steering its output distribution. The final loss (Eq. 5) compares ε-prediction errors on the data trajectory against x₀-prediction errors on the reference trajectory, each regularized by a frozen pretrained copy of the model. PSO generalizes to offline settings (pre-sampled reference images, unifying DiffusionDPO) and online settings (reward-model-labeled pairs, unifying D3PO), and is applied via LoRA fine-tuning with a regularization weight β that controls deviation from the pretrained model.

## Results

On the Pick-a-Pic test set, Online-PSO fine-tuned SDXL-DMD2 (4 steps) achieves a PickScore of 22.73, ImageReward of 0.9773, and Aesthetic Score of 6.077, surpassing the 50-step SDXL-DPO baseline (PickScore 22.60, ImageReward 1.0075) in PickScore and Aesthetic Score while remaining competitive in ImageReward. On PartiPrompts, Online-PSO reaches PickScore 23.29 and ImageReward 1.1702, outperforming SDXL-DPO (22.92, 1.1180) across both metrics. The naive baseline of applying DPO LoRA to the distilled model (SDXL-DPO+DMD2-LoRA) actually degrades performance below the unmodified SDXL-DMD2 (PickScore drops from 22.35 to 22.20 on Pick-a-Pic), confirming that standard diffusion fine-tuning is incompatible with timestep-distilled models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSO consistently outperforms the base distilled model (SDXL-DMD2) across all metrics on both Pick-a-Pic and PartiPrompts, with Online-PSO achieving the largest gains (e.g., +0.38 PickScore, +0.10 ImageReward on Pick-a-Pic). The naive approach of applying DPO LoRA to the distilled model (SDXL-DPO+DMD2-LoRA) actually degrades quality below the unmodified base, dropping PickScore from 22.35 to 22.20 and Aesthetic Score from 5.937 to 5.759, validating the paper's core motivation. Among 50-step multi-step baselines, Online-PSO (4 steps) surpasses SDXL-DPO in PickScore (22.73 vs. 22.60) and Aesthetic Score (6.077 vs. 6.040) on Pick-a-Pic, and in both PickScore (23.29 vs. 22.92) and ImageReward (1.1702 vs. 1.1180) on PartiPrompts. The other 50-step preference optimization baselines (SDXL-RPO, SDXL-SPO, SDXL-MaPO) are not directly comparable to the 4-step PSO models but serve as references for the multi-step setting; notably, SDXL-SPO achieves the highest Aesthetic Score (6.283) among 50-step methods on Pick-a-Pic. For SDXL-Turbo (Table 2), both Offline-PSO and Online-PSO improve all metrics over the base 4-step model, and the fine-tuned model generalizes to 1-step inference with consistent gains. For SDXL-LCM (Table 3), PSO outperforms both the base model and SDXL-DPO+LCM-LoRA across all four metrics on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) framework from Rafailov et al. to the specific setting of timestep-distilled diffusion models, where vanilla diffusion loss fails due to the altered sampling dynamics. It unifies and generalizes prior diffusion preference optimization methods—Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) as the offline special case and D3PO (Yang et al., 2024a) as the online special case—under a single pairwise sample optimization objective. The key departure from all prior work is the MDP formulation tailored to few-step distilled models (1–4 steps), which enables direct fine-tuning without the prohibitively expensive re-distillation pipeline that would otherwise be required after any preference or style adjustment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSO addresses a critical deployment bottleneck: fine-tuning distilled models for specific applications (style, concept, preference) previously required re-running the full distillation process (~4000 A100 GPU hours for DMD2), making per-application customization economically infeasible. By showing that a lightweight LoRA-based PSO fine-tune can match or exceed re-distillation quality across DMD2, Turbo, and LCM, the paper opens the door to rapid, low-cost adaptation of fast diffusion models. The authors acknowledge that style transfer and concept customization results are preliminary and require further loss design and regularization work, and the method has not yet been tested on video generation or controllable generation settings.
