## Problem

Existing multi-modal learning methods require instance-level correspondence (paired data from the same object across modalities), which is extremely resource-consuming and often impossible for novel modalities such as tactile sensing or bio-sensing. This paper formalizes Out-of-Modal (OOM) Generalization—the task of learning a new modality using knowledge from known modalities without (or with only scarce) instance-level pairing—and provides the first systematic treatment of both semi-supervised and unsupervised variants of this problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250969/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen pre-trained IM perceptors (ImageBind or LanguageBind) that encode known modalities (RGB, language, thermal, depth, audio, video) into a shared feature space, plus an OOM learner (ViT-T/16 initialized from scratch) that processes the novel modality. In the semi-supervised setting, 10% of OOM data has labels and correspondence to IM data; in the unsupervised setting, no labels or correspondences exist at all. The output is a class label for OOM data (classification accuracy) and a cross-modal retrieval ranking (R@1, R@5) between OOM and IM features.

## Method

COX (Connect&Explore) operates in two stages grounded in a variational information bottleneck framework. First, it learns a connection by mapping IM features into the OOM feature space via an MLP decoder, maximizing the information commonality I(X^O; X^I; Y) through a reconstruction loss (reconstructing OOM data from the IM latent) and a classification loss, effectively transferring task-relevant knowledge across modalities. Second, it explores OOM uniqueness by identifying data where IM classifiers disagree (high uniqueness) versus agree (anchor points with high commonality), then minimizing the KL divergence between the OOM posterior on disagreement data and the anchor posterior. For the unsupervised case, a warm-up phase uses modality agreement to bootstrap initial anchors, followed by creating emergent correspondences by matching IM data mapped into OOM space to nearby OOM features, which are then treated as pseudo-labeled pairs. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that modality disagreement is upper-bounded by the OOM uniqueness term in the information decomposition, justifying the exploration strategy.

## Results

On classification (Table 2), COX with LanguageBind achieves 66.4% accuracy on LLVIP (thermal OOM), 58.8% on NYU-D (RGB OOM), and 33.5% on TVL (RGB OOM) in the semi-supervised setting, outperforming EntMin by 4–8 points across all datasets. In the unsupervised setting, COX with LanguageBind reaches 55.7% on LLVIP (thermal OOM) and 24.5% on NYU-D (RGB OOM), improving over the Random baseline by over 10 points. On cross-modal retrieval (Table 3), COX with LanguageBind achieves R@1 of 28.7 on MSR-VTT (video OOM) and 39.2 on MOSEI (video OOM) in the semi-supervised setting, and R@1 of 15.4 and 20.4 respectively in the unsupervised setting, consistently outperforming MoCo and SSL baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COX consistently outperforms EntMin (Grandvalet & Bengio, 2004) across all datasets and settings: in the semi-supervised case with LanguageBind, COX gains +7.8 points on TVL-RGB (33.5 vs. 25.7), +4.2 on LLVIP-The (66.4 vs. 62.2), +5.7 on NYU-D-Lan (48.4 vs. 42.7), and +7.6 on VGGS-Vid (22.1 vs. 14.5). Against MoCo (He et al., 2020) on cross-modal retrieval, COX with LanguageBind achieves R@1 of 25.2/24.1/28.7 on MSR-VTT (Aud/Lan/Vid) versus MoCo's 21.1/20.9/24.5, a consistent 3–4 point improvement. In the unsupervised retrieval setting, COX (R@1 = 14.8/18.4/15.4 on MSR-VTT) also surpasses MoCo (9.6/11.1/11.0) and SSL (9.2/11.0/10.3) by 4–7 points. On the unimodal comparisons in Table 5, COX beats FreeMatch (Wang et al., 2022) by 7.1 points on MSR-VTT vision (52.3 vs. 45.2) and MixText (Chen et al., 2020) by 2.2 points on NYU-D language (23.4 vs. 21.2), confirming that cross-modal knowledge transfer provides gains even over strong unimodal semi-supervised methods. COX does not match the fully-aligned upper bound (e.g., 87.2% on LLVIP-The vs. COX's 66.4%), which the authors acknowledge as a limitation of the correspondence-free setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem setting—Out-of-Modal (OOM) Generalization—that departs from cross-modal fine-tuning (which requires labeled target data), cross-modal generalization (which requires instance-level correspondence), and MML without labeled multi-modal data (which still provides paired unlabeled data). Methodologically, it repurposes the Variational Information Bottleneck framework (Alemi et al., 2016) for cross-modal connection learning and draws on the Williams & Beer (2010) information decomposition (commonality/uniqueness/synergy) to justify why modality disagreement identifies OOM-specific knowledge. The frozen IM perceptors (ImageBind, LanguageBind) serve as off-the-shelf knowledge sources, positioning COX as a lightweight adapter rather than a new multi-modal architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: novel modalities like tactile sensing or bio-signals are extremely difficult to pair instance-by-instance with vision or language, yet the paper shows that even zero correspondence allows meaningful knowledge transfer. The theoretical result (Theorem 1) providing an information-theoretic upper bound on modality disagreement in terms of OOM uniqueness is a clean contribution that could inform future multi-modal theory. However, the performance gap to fully-aligned models remains large (e.g., ~19% vs ~82% on TVL unsupervised), and the evaluation is confined to within-dataset modalities, leaving cross-dataset OOM generalization as a significant open question.
