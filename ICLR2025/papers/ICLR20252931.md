## Problem

Aligning generative AI to multiple human values simultaneously (e.g., harmlessness, helpfulness, humor) is challenging because existing RLHF/DPO/MORL approaches rely on arbitrary linear scalarization weights with no principled way to specify target levels per value, no feasibility check, and no guarantee of Pareto improvement across all values. The paper asks: how can a user define desired levels for each value and obtain a provably optimal alignment that achieves exactly those targets without degrading others?

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, m multi-dimensional reward functions (e.g., humor classifier, GPT-2 value heads for harmlessness/helpfulness, SimCSE for coherence), and a user-specified value palette c — typically set as quantiles (e.g., 80th percentile) of the original model's reward distributions estimated from a pilot set of n=2000 Monte Carlo samples. It outputs a weight vector λ (one-to-one with c) and aligned text generation, implemented either via decoding-stage importance sampling over k candidates or via PPO finetuning with the MAP-guided reward R = λ^T r. The pilot dataset is pre-computed once and reused across different palette configurations, decoupling the optimization cost from model size.

## Method

MAP reformulates alignment as a constrained optimization: minimize KL divergence from the original model p0 subject to expected reward constraints E[r_i(x,y)] ≥ c_i for each value i. The solution has a closed-form exponential-family structure p_λ(y|x) ∝ p0(y|x) exp(λ^T r(x,y)), where λ is obtained by solving a strictly concave dual problem via gradient ascent on a finite-sample approximation. Key theoretical results prove that linear combinations of individual rewards are sufficient to span the entire Pareto frontier (Theorem 3), that sequential single-value alignment over multiple cycles converges to the simultaneous MAP solution (Theorem 5), and that the method is robust to reward mis-specification under non-degenerate covariance. The approach is agnostic to model architecture and alignment stage, and includes automatic palette adjustment (interpolation or greedy search) when a user-specified palette is infeasible.

## Results

MAP achieves a Navigation Efficiency (NE) of 55% (decoding) and 52% (finetuning) for expected reward, and 60% and 70% for win rate, respectively, meaning over half of MAP-aligned models simultaneously improve on both target values. In contrast, MORL achieves only 21–35% NE, DPO achieves 9–27%, and DPO-Soup achieves 36–45%. On the OPT-1.3B conversational task, MAP with the HHH-70% palette simultaneously improves Humor (2.07→2.49), Helpfulness (−1.47→−1.29), and Harmlessness (0.25→0.66) while preserving Diversity, Coherence, and Perplexity, whereas single-value alignment (e.g., Humor-80%) degrades Harmlessness from 0.25 to 0.01.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAP decisively outperforms all three baseline families on both Navigation Efficiency metrics. Against MORL (random linear scalarization), MAP-D improves expected-reward NE from 21% to 55% (+34 pp) and win-rate NE from 21% to 60% (+39 pp); MAP-F improves from 35% to 52% and 55% to 70% respectively. Against DPO with mixed preference data, the gap is even larger: MAP-D's 55% NE versus DPO(0.1)'s 27% and DPO(0.5)'s 9%, indicating that DPO's Bradley-Terry objective struggles to balance multiple values simultaneously. Against DPO-Soup (linear interpolation of value-specific DPO models), MAP-D leads by 10 pp on expected-reward NE (55% vs 45%) and 15 pp on win-rate NE (60% vs 45%), suggesting that weight-space interpolation cannot fully capture the Pareto frontier that MAP's constrained optimization directly targets. The paper also notes that MAP's feasibility check (Algorithm 1, Step 2) provides a principled way to reject infeasible palettes, a capability absent from all three baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAP builds directly on the RLHF objective (Bai et al., 2022) but reframes multi-value alignment as a constrained optimization problem with user-specified 'value palettes' rather than the standard approach of randomly sampling linear scalarization weights. Prior multi-value alignment methods (MORL, Rewarded Soup, DPO-Soup) combine reward functions or preference data with arbitrary weights and lack guarantees on feasibility or Pareto optimality. MAP departs from this by providing a primal-dual solution that certifies whether a target is achievable and, if so, computes the exact weight vector, while proving that linear reward combinations suffice to span the full Pareto frontier (Theorem 3) and that sequential single-value alignment converges to the simultaneous solution (Theorem 5).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAP offers the first principled, interpretable framework for multi-value alignment where users can specify exact target levels and receive a feasibility certificate, addressing a practical gap in current RLHF/DPO pipelines where weight selection is ad hoc. The sequential-alignment equivalence (Theorem 5) is particularly valuable for memory-constrained deployments where loading all reward models simultaneously is infeasible. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small models (OPT-1.3B, Llama2-7B-chat), reliance on proxy reward models whose quality bounds the alignment, and the assumption that reward functions are well-specified. Open questions include scaling to frontier-scale models, handling dynamically personalized values, and extending the framework to data-mixing formulations rather than reward-weighting.
