## Problem

Computing tight privacy parameters for differentially private training that combines privacy amplification via random batching with correlated noise (the matrix mechanism) has been limited by reliance on composition theorems. Prior work either restricted the correlation matrix C to banded structure or introduced significant slack for general C at practical ε values, preventing simultaneous optimization of C and amplification. This paper provides near-exact privacy analysis for any lower-triangular, non-negative C, enabling direct optimization of the correlation matrix under amplification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a lower-triangular, non-negative correlation matrix C (restricted to Toeplitz or BLT families for optimization), a dataset D, target privacy parameters (ε, δ), and balls-in-bins batching parameters (b batches per epoch, E epochs, total n = b·E iterations). It produces a calibrated noise multiplier σ via bisection on the Monte Carlo-estimated δ, an optimized C minimizing the RMSE objective σ·‖A⁻¹C‖ (Frobenius norm, A the lower-triangular all-ones matrix), and a formal (ε, δ)-DP guarantee certified by the Estimate-Verify-Release framework with a user-specified failure probability. The dominating pair for the PLD is a b-mode Gaussian mixture, making sampling efficient regardless of n.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a two-part framework: (1) a 'balls-in-bins' batching scheme where each example is independently assigned to exactly one of b batches uniformly at random and batches are consumed in round-robin order across epochs, which yields a clean b-mode Gaussian mixture dominating pair amenable to Monte Carlo sampling; and (2) Monte Carlo accounting that bypasses composition entirely by sampling from the privacy loss distribution of this dominating pair and estimating δ as the empirical average of max{1 − e^{ε−Y}, 0}. For optimization, since σ is recovered via bisection (non-differentiable), the paper uses implicit differentiation of the constraint δ̂(σ, C) = δ to obtain gradients of σ with respect to C, enabling gradient descent over the Toeplitz or BLT parameterization. The final privacy guarantee is made rigorous via the Estimate-Verify-Release framework with a Bernstein-based tail bound (Theorem 2.2) controlling the Monte Carlo estimation error.

## Results

On the prefix-sum RMSE benchmark (2048 iterations, 16 epochs), the optimized correlation matrices with balls-in-bins batching achieve up to ~10% RMSE reduction over the prior SOTA (banded MF + Poisson sampling of Choquette-Choo et al., 2024a). On CIFAR-10 (VGG, 20 epochs × 100 iterations, batch size 500), balls-in-bins amplified matrices Pareto-dominate the SOTA baseline, achieving equal accuracy at smaller ε and up to 1% absolute accuracy improvement at larger ε (e.g., ε=8). The optimized BLT matrices (4 buffers, 3× model memory overhead) also match or exceed the baseline while being up to 4× more memory efficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA baseline of banded matrix factorization + Poisson sampling (Choquette-Choo et al., 2024a), the paper's balls-in-bins amplified methods consistently win or tie: banded MF + balls-in-bins is always at least as good and gives up to 1% absolute accuracy improvement at larger ε, while the baseline assumes the stronger (less practical) Poisson sampling. The optimized BLT matrices + balls-in-bins also always match or exceed the baseline, with the added benefit of up to 4× lower memory overhead (4 buffers vs. up to 16 bands). Against the unamplified MEMF baseline (Choquette-Choo et al., 2022), the practical implementation of balls-in-bins batching (with padding/truncation to fixed batch size) still shows clear amplification benefits that far exceed the utility loss from suboptimal batching. The optimized Toeplitz matrices are incomparable to the baseline, with weaker performance attributed to the difficulty of the higher-dimensional optimization problem. In the RMSE setting, balls-in-bins consistently outperforms Poisson sampling for banded matrices with b > 1 (Fig. 2b), and the optimized C matrices achieve up to 10% RMSE reduction over the best banded + Poisson configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active lines of work in differentially private machine learning: privacy amplification via random batching and correlated noise via the matrix mechanism (DP-FTRL). Prior work combining these two ideas was limited to either banded correlation matrices (Choquette-Choo et al., 2024a) or general C with composition-based analysis that introduces significant slack (Choquette-Choo et al., 2024b). The key departure here is using Monte Carlo accounting (Wang et al., 2023) to bypass composition entirely, paired with a new 'balls-in-bins' batching scheme whose structure makes the required privacy-loss-distribution sampling tractable, enabling near-exact privacy analysis for arbitrary lower-triangular non-negative C.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to optimize the correlation matrix C while directly accounting for amplification (rather than requiring an expensive post-hoc grid search) is a practical unlock for the DP-ML community, yielding ~10% RMSE reductions and up to 1% CIFAR-10 accuracy gains over the prior SOTA. Open questions include whether Monte Carlo accounting can be extended to Poisson sampling (whose 2^n-mode Gaussian mixture is a major technical barrier), whether the sample complexity of MC verification (scaling as 1/δ) can be improved, and whether the practical balls-in-bins variant scales to large-model training. The paper also highlights that the current practice of assuming Poisson sampling in analysis while implementing shuffling in practice remains a gap that this work partially addresses.
