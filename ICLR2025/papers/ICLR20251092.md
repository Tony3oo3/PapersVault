## Problem

While DNNs trained on static images exhibit emergent 3D perception, it remains unknown whether these capabilities are sufficient for Visual Perspective Taking (VPT)—the ability to reason about what another viewpoint can see. No prior benchmark systematically compared human and machine VPT on identical stimuli, leaving open whether DNNs' 3D representations support the spatial reasoning humans use to anticipate others' behavior.

## Input / Output

Each stimulus is a 256×256 RGB image rendered from a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (built from Co3D videos) containing a green camera and a red ball in natural object scenes. DNNs consume penultimate-layer embeddings via linear probes or full images via fine-tuning, while VLMs receive the image plus a text prompt with 20 in-context training examples. The output is a binary decision (which object is closer, or whether the green camera can see the red ball), and critically, the same 94 test images carry both a depth-order label and a VPT label with counterbalanced ground truth, enabling direct task comparison on identical visual content.

## Method

The 3D-PC is constructed by training 3D Gaussian Splatting models on Co3D videos, importing them into Unity, and placing a green camera and red ball into each scene to derive ground-truth labels for depth order and VPT. Random viewpoint trajectories are rendered to produce unlimited stimuli (7,480 training + 94 test images for depth order/VPT-basic; 100 images for VPT-Strategy), with labels counterbalanced across tasks on the same images to control for visual-statistic confounds. The paper evaluates 327 DNNs spanning CNNs, ViTs, foundation models (MAE, DINOv2, SAM, Depth Anything), Stable Diffusion 2.0, and VLMs (GPT-4, Gemini, Claude 3) via linear probing, fine-tuning, and text prompting, alongside 33 human participants. A key design element is the VPT-Strategy task, which fixes the scene camera while incrementally moving the green camera and red ball past an occluder, specifically designed to distinguish line-of-sight reasoning from brittle feature-based shortcuts. Strategy analysis employs SmoothGrad attribution maps (quantified via Dice similarity to target objects) and Cohen's κ error-pattern correlations between humans and DNNs.

## Results

On the 3D-PC benchmark, humans achieve 74.73% accuracy on depth order and 86.82% on VPT-basic, while the best linearly-probed DNN (BEiT, ImageNet-21k) reaches only 53.82% on VPT-basic—near chance. After fine-tuning, 97% of DNNs exceed human accuracy on depth order and four DNNs reach human-level VPT-basic, but the best fine-tuned DNN (Swin Transformer) drops to 66% on VPT-Strategy versus 87% for humans. DNN depth-order accuracy strongly correlates with ImageNet classification accuracy (ρ=0.66, p<0.001), while the VPT-basic correlation is weaker (ρ=0.34, p<0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 327 DNNs against human performance on three 3D perception tasks. On depth order, state-of-the-art classifiers (EVA-02, ViT variants) match or exceed the 74.73% human accuracy after linear probing, and 97% surpass humans after fine-tuning. On VPT-basic, all DNNs fail dramatically: the best linearly-probed model (BEiT) reaches only 53.82% versus 86.82% for humans, and commercial VLMs (ChatGPT4 at 52%, Gemini at 52%, Claude 3 at 50%) perform at chance. Foundation models (MAE, DINOv2, SAM, MiDas, Depth Anything) and Stable Diffusion 2.0 also fail on VPT-basic, confirming the gap is not architecture-specific. After fine-tuning, four DNNs reach human-level VPT-basic, but all collapse to 66% or below on VPT-Strategy (vs. 87% human), revealing that DNNs rely on brittle object-size/location shortcuts rather than the line-of-sight strategy humans use. Swin Transformer (ImageNet-21k) is the strongest fine-tuned DNN on VPT-Strategy at 66%, still 21 points below humans.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges developmental psychology's long-standing study of Visual Perspective Taking (VPT) — from Piaget's Three Mountains Task through Michelon & Zacks' VPT-1/VPT-2 taxonomy — with the growing evidence that DNNs trained on static images develop emergent 3D scene representations (Ranftl et al., Saxena et al., El Banani et al.). It departs from prior psychology paradigms by using 3D Gaussian Splatting on Co3D videos to generate unlimited, counterbalanced natural-scene stimuli, and from prior CV benchmarks by posing multiple 3D tasks (depth order, VPT-basic, VPT-Strategy) on the exact same images to control for confounds. The VPT-Strategy task is explicitly motivated by the shortcut-learning literature (Geirhos et al.) and the finding that DNNs increasingly diverge from human visual strategies as they improve on standard benchmarks (Linsley et al., Fel et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3D-PC reveals a fundamental and well-evidenced dissociation: DNNs match or exceed humans on basic depth ordering yet remain near chance on VPT even after fine-tuning, and their fine-tuned performance collapses on VPT-Strategy while humans maintain 87% accuracy — indicating DNNs learn brittle object-feature shortcuts rather than the line-of-sight reasoning humans use. This challenges the assumption that large-scale static image training (or even task-specific fine-tuning) is sufficient for human-like 3D spatial reasoning, and points toward the need for new training paradigms or architectures that incorporate embodied or multi-view 3D experience. Key open questions include what specific training signals would enable true line-of-sight estimation in DNNs, whether the ImageNet-accuracy correlation with 3D perception will persist at larger scales, and how to extend the benchmark beyond the most basic form of VPT to the full range of human perspective-taking abilities.
