## Problem

Jin et al. (2021) showed that the dual of non-convex DRO with ψ-divergence satisfies generalized (L0,L1)-smoothness and affine variance noise, requiring a normalized-momentum algorithm and involved convergence analysis. This paper asks whether these conditions are an overkill: by studying the model parameter x and dual variable η separately, it reveals that the dual is actually standard L-smooth in η and only partially generalized-smooth in x, with the Lipschitz constant depending on |∇_η L| rather than the full gradient norm. This more precise structure enables dramatically simpler algorithms and proofs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N training samples from P0, a non-convex G-Lipschitz and L-smooth loss ℓ(x,S), and a fixed ψ-divergence penalty parameter λ. It outputs a pair (x, η) where x ∈ R^d is the model parameter and η ∈ R is the scalar dual variable, jointly forming an ε-stationary point of the dual objective. A key practical property is that per-iteration gradient complexity is independent of N, making the algorithms scalable to very large training sets; the stochastic variants use mini-batches of sizes N1, N2 (and N3, N4 for the Spider variant) drawn i.i.d. from P0 at each step.

## Method

The central insight is a 'partially generalized (L0, L1, L2)-smoothness' characterization: the dual is standard L2-smooth in η and (L0, L1)-smooth in x with the Lipschitz constant linear in |∇_η L| rather than ‖∇_{x,η} L‖. This enables an alternating-update strategy (D-GD, D-SGD-C): first update η with plain gradient descent at step 1/L2, which bounds |∇_η L| and thereby bounds the effective Lipschitz constant in x, reducing the problem to standard smooth optimization for the x-update. The stochastic version (D-SGD-C) adds mini-batch gradient estimation with an adaptive clipping step size β_t = min(1/(2L0), ε/(L0‖v_t‖)) on the x-update, achieving O(ε^{-4}) gradient complexity. A variance-reduced variant (D-Spider-C) replaces unbiased mini-batch gradients with Spider-style differences computed on small batches between periodic large-batch refreshes every q iterations, reducing complexity to O(ε^{-3}) while requiring only bounded loss variance (not bounded loss) as a noise assumption.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with headline results being complexity bounds: D-GD achieves O(ε^{-2}) iteration complexity in the deterministic setting, D-SGD-C achieves O(ε^{-4}) gradient complexity, and D-Spider-C improves this to O(ε^{-3}) gradient complexity. Numerically, on the Life Expectancy dataset (N=2413, 34 features, non-convex loss with λ=0.01), results are presented as training curves (Figures 1–2) rather than a numerical table. The key empirical finding is that the proposed D-SGD-C and D-Spider-C algorithms outperform the prior DRO method (Jin et al., 2021) in the stochastic setting, while D-GD matches standard GD performance in the deterministic setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the stochastic setting (Figure 2), both D-SGD-C and D-Spider-C outperform Normalized-SGD with momentum (Jin et al., 2021) and standard SGD, with D-Spider-C achieving performance comparable to Normalized-SPIDER (Chen et al., 2023). In the deterministic setting (Figure 1), D-GD matches standard GD in convergence, confirming that alternating updates of x and η are empirically equivalent to joint optimization, and D-GD-C performs comparably to Clipped GD (Zhang et al., 2019) and Normalized GD (Chen et al., 2023). The main practical advantage over Jin et al. (2021) is that the proposed methods use simpler SGD-type updates (with clipping) rather than a normalized momentum scheme, while achieving the same or better empirical convergence. The theoretical advantage is that the O(ε^{-3}) complexity of D-Spider-C improves upon the O(ε^{-4}) of both D-SGD-C and the prior method, with a simpler proof structure due to the partially generalized smoothness characterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on Jin et al. (2021), who first analyzed non-convex DRO with ψ-divergence uncertainty sets by showing the dual objective satisfies generalized (L₀,L₁)-smoothness and affine variance noise, then designed a normalized momentum method achieving O(ε⁻⁴) gradient complexity. The key departure is a structural recharacterization: by studying the model parameter x and dual variable η separately, the authors show the dual is actually *partially* generalized smooth (standard L-smooth in η, generalized smooth in x with Lipschitz constant depending only on ∇_η L) and the noise is *partially* affine variance (bounded in η, affine in (∇_η L)² for x). This tighter characterization eliminates the need for adaptive/normalized methods, allowing plain gradient descent (deterministic) and SGD with clipping (stochastic) to match the O(ε⁻⁴) complexity, and a Spider-style variance reduction to improve it to O(ε⁻³).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual insight that generalized (L₀,L₁)-smoothness is an overkill for DRO duals simplifies both the theory and the algorithms, making the problem more accessible and the proofs substantially shorter than prior analyses. The O(ε⁻³) gradient complexity via D-Spider-C is a genuine improvement over the O(ε⁻⁴) of Jin et al. (2021) and prior Spider-based approaches for generalized smooth problems. However, empirical validation is limited to a single small-scale regression dataset (N=2413 life expectancy samples), leaving open whether the practical advantages of simpler algorithms (vs. normalized momentum or Adam) persist at the scale of modern deep learning. The partially generalized smoothness framework could potentially transfer to other minimax or robust optimization problems where variables have heterogeneous smoothness structure.
