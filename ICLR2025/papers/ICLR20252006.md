## Problem

Training LLMs for autoformalization (translating natural language mathematics into formal Lean 4) is bottlenecked by the scarcity of high-quality parallel NL-FL datasets. Existing annotation pipelines fail to exploit the rich structural metadata available from the Lean 4 compiler—particularly theorem dependency graphs and tactic states—leading to fabricated translations of dependent definitions and rigid, distributionally unnatural informal statements that hinder generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252006/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Herald translator takes a natural language mathematical statement (e.g., from undergraduate or graduate textbooks, multilingual sources) and produces a syntactically valid Lean 4 theorem statement. Validation is two-stage: the generated Lean code must pass the Lean 4 compiler, and a back-translation via InternLM2-Math-Plus-7B followed by an NLI check (DeepSeek Chat v2.5) must confirm semantic equivalence with the original. The underlying dataset comprises 580k NL-FL statement pairs and 44k NL-FL proof pairs derived from Mathlib4, with the statement corpus augmented from 110k original theorems via tactic-state decomposition and LLM-based paraphrasing.

## Method

The pipeline uses Lean-Jixia, a static analyzer for Lean 4, to extract structured context (head statements, theorem kind, docstrings, neighbor statements, and dependent theorems) for each formal statement. Translation proceeds in dependency-level order over a DAG of theorem dependencies, ensuring that natural language annotations for all prerequisite theorems are available before translating the target. A RAG component retrieves the most semantically similar human-annotated theorem from a vector database of 1,000 examples to anchor the LLM's translation. Two augmentation strategies expand the corpus: (1) tactic-based augmentation extracts intermediate proof states from tactic-mode proofs to generate 580k localized, compiler-validated statements, and (2) LLM-based informal augmentation produces paraphrases via logical equivalence rewriting, abstract concept substitution, omission of implicit conditions, and multi-linguistic translation (Chinese, French, Russian). The final model is DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-Base 7B fine-tuned via SFT on a 2:2:1 mix of NL→FL, FL→NL, and general-domain (OpenHermes2.5) data over 5 epochs.

## Results

The Herald translator achieves 96.7% Pass@128 accuracy on miniF2F-test and 96.3% on miniF2F-valid, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the more challenging graduate-level datasets, it reaches 23.5% on Extract Theorem and 16.0% on College CoT (both 200-sample shuffled subsets). The model is fine-tuned on 580k NL-FL statement pairs and 44k NL-FL proof pairs from the Herald dataset, with a 2:2:1 training ratio of NL→FL, FL→NL, and general-domain (OpenHermes2.5) examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Herald decisively outperforms both baselines across all four evaluation datasets. Against TheoremLlama (Wang et al., 2024), Herald gains +46.6 points on miniF2F-test (96.7% vs 50.1%), +19.5 points on Extract Theorem (23.5% vs 4.0%), and +13.1 points on College CoT (16.0% vs 2.9%). Against InternLM2-Math-Plus-7B (Ying et al., 2024b), the margins are +23.7 points on miniF2F-test (96.7% vs 73.0%), +16.0 points on Extract Theorem (23.5% vs 7.5%), and +9.5 points on College CoT (16.0% vs 6.5%). The largest relative gains appear on the harder graduate-level datasets, suggesting that the hierarchical dependency-aware translation and dual augmentation strategy are particularly effective for complex, less-common mathematical content. A human evaluation on ProofNet (185 statements) further confirms that Herald's validation-passed outputs have a comparable false-positive rate to InternLM2-Math-Plus-7B, making the automated comparison fair.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoformalization pipeline established by TheoremLlama (Wang et al., 2024) and Lean Workbook (Ying et al., 2024a), which both use LLMs to generate NL-FL pairs from Lean corpora, by introducing a structural-information-aware, dependency-ordered translation process powered by the Lean-Jixia static analyzer. Where prior methods treat each theorem in isolation, Herald exploits the DAG of theorem dependencies to ensure dependent statements are informalized first, and augments the LLM's context with tactic states, proof states, and semantically retrieved related theorems (building on Gao et al., 2024's Mathlib4 semantic search engine). The dual augmentation strategy—tactic-state decomposition and LLM-based informal rewriting—addresses data scarcity and distribution shift in ways that earlier bootstrapping or expert-iteration approaches did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 580k NL-FL statement pairs and 44k proof pairs constitute one of the largest open resources for training autoformalization models, and the 96.7% miniF2F-test accuracy (vs. 73% for the previous best) represents a substantial practical improvement. However, the 23.5% accuracy on graduate-level theorems and the fact that DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5 could only prove one trivial theorem in the Stacks Project section underscore that the formalization-to-proof gap remains wide. Open questions include whether the dependency-order and RAG components generalize beyond Mathlib4 to other Lean libraries, how to handle highly abstract domains (category theory, diagram-based theorems) where current translations degrade, and whether a more capable prover can close the loop from autoformalized statements to verified proofs at scale.
