## Problem

Vision foundation models like DINOv2, CLIP, and SynCLR achieve remarkable generalization but are trained on massive private datasets (142M–2B images) with enormous computational costs, making them inaccessible for most researchers to reproduce or compress. The paper asks whether the generalization ability of these foundation models can be replicated on a much smaller, publicly available dataset like ImageNet-1K (1.2M images) without sacrificing downstream performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252087/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images from ImageNet-1K (224×224, 1.2M images across 1000 classes) as the sole training data, with a randomly initialized ViT student (patch size 14) and a pre-trained foundation model as the frozen teacher. For the patch-level objective, an additional masked view of each image is generated by randomly masking patches. The student produces general-purpose visual representations (CLS token, patch tokens, and full spatial feature maps) that can be directly applied to linear classification, semantic segmentation via UperNet, or depth estimation via DPT without task-specific retraining.

## Method

Proteus removes two components from conventional knowledge distillation that introduce dataset bias: the Cross-Entropy loss on one-hot labels and the projection onto a fixed class dimension (FC layer), instead distilling on intermediate features before the classification head. It then constructs a proxy task with three complementary MSE-based objectives at different granularities: token-level (aligning the CLS token), feature-level (aligning the full spatial feature map for dense prediction), and patch-level (predicting unmasked patch tokens from a randomly masked input, inspired by iBOT). Each objective uses a lightweight projection head (LayerNorm + Linear) to bridge the dimension gap between teacher and student. The student is randomly initialized rather than weight-inherited, and the framework is teacher-agnostic, successfully transferring knowledge from DINOv2, CLIP, and SynCLR with only 1.2M training images and ImageNet-level training costs.

## Results

Proteus-L/14, trained on only 1.2M ImageNet-1K images, achieves 86.2% ImageNet linear probing accuracy and 91.0% average fine-grained classification accuracy, matching the Oracle DINOv2-L/14 (142M images) at 86.3% and 91.0% respectively. On dense prediction, Proteus-L/14 reaches 57.7 mIoU on ADE20K (vs. 58.1 for DINOv2-L/14) and 0.240 RMSE on NYU-Depth V2, actually outperforming DINOv2-L/14 (0.243). At the ViT-S scale, Proteus-S/14 (81.8% ImageNet, 85.8% fine-grained avg) outperforms CLIP-B/32 (74.7%, 82.5%) and OpenCLIP-B/32 (74.4%, 83.4%) trained on 400M images, using only 0.3% of their training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proteus-L/14 (1.2M images) essentially matches the Oracle DINOv2-L/14 (142M images) on both ImageNet (86.2 vs 86.3) and fine-grained average (91.0 vs 91.0), and even outperforms it on NYU-Depth V2 depth estimation (0.240 vs 0.243 RMSE). Against CLIP-L/14 (400M images), Proteus-L/14 wins on ImageNet by 2.8 points (86.2 vs 83.4) and fine-grained average by 1.9 points (91.0 vs 89.1). Proteus-L/14 also surpasses SynCLR-L/14 (600M synthetic images) on ImageNet (86.2 vs 82.8) and fine-grained average (91.0 vs 90.5). At the ViT-B scale, Proteus-B/14 outperforms DINOv2-B/14 on ImageNet (84.9 vs 84.5) but trails on fine-grained average (88.6 vs 90.0), while clearly beating CLIP-B/16 (78.7) and OpenCLIP-B/16 (78.7) on ImageNet. Compared to the supervised distillation baseline DeiT, Proteus consistently wins across all scales on ImageNet accuracy, robustness (ImageNet-A, -R, -S, -C), fine-grained classification, and dense prediction (ADE20K mIoU and NYU-Depth RMSE), with the gap widening as model size increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

Proteus repositions knowledge distillation from a task-specific compression tool into a general mechanism for transferring foundation-model representations under severe data constraints. It departs from conventional KD (Hinton; DeiT) by removing the Cross-Entropy loss and FC-layer projection that the authors identify as sources of dataset bias, and instead adopts intermediate-feature distillation in the spirit of FitNets. The three-level objective (token, patch, feature) combines discriminative alignment with a masked-image-modeling-inspired patch recovery step borrowed from iBOT, creating a unified proxy task that emulates the teacher's self-supervised training behavior. Unlike TinyCLIP, which still requires the original 400M-image dataset, Proteus operates entirely on ImageNet-1K (1.2M images), making it orthogonal to prior data-reduction or structure-pruning approaches for foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: any researcher with 8 A100 GPUs and ImageNet-1K can now reproduce DINOv2-, CLIP-, or SynCLR-level representations at a fraction of the original training cost, democratizing access to foundation-model knowledge. The finding that a 100× reduction in training data (1.2M vs. 142M) preserves generalization across 19 benchmarks challenges the assumption that massive diverse data is strictly necessary for transferable visual features. Key limitations include the requirement to match the teacher's patch size, validation only at ImageNet-1K scale, and the observation that performance on certain fine-grained datasets (e.g., FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford Cars) still lags behind the oracle when the proxy dataset lacks relevant classes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger proxy datasets, extends to multi-modal foundation models (VLMs, LMMs), and whether the surprising robustness to extreme data reduction (single-image proxy) reveals a fundamental property of distillation that can be further exploited.
