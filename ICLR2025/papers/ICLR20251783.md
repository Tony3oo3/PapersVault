## Problem

Vision-language large models (VLLMs) suffer from modality misalignment that causes hallucinations and unsafe content, yet existing preference tuning methods rely on coarse sentence-level rewards, external datasets, or costly human annotations. The paper demonstrates empirically that sentence-level CLIP scores show near-zero correlation (r = -0.01) with standard quality metrics like BLEU and ROUGE, and that hallucinated and correct objects are nearly indistinguishable at the sentence level, while token-level scores from the vision encoder clearly separate them.

## Input / Output

FiSAO takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input and produces an aligned text response, trained on the first 8k samples of LLaVA-Instruct 150k with no additional data. A key constraint is that fine-grained rewards are only assigned to tokens belonging to a pre-constructed entity set C of 5,678 object words (expanded from 1,204 Detic/COCO labels via WordNet synonyms and inflection), so non-object tokens receive zero reward. The vision encoder (CLIP-ViT-L-334 for LLaVA-1.5, CLIP-ViT-L for InstructBLIP) computes a dot-product score between each candidate object token's text embedding and the image embedding to determine the reward.

## Method

FiSAO reframes preference tuning as a sequential decision-making (MDP) problem where the VLLM policy selects next tokens and the model's own CLIP vision encoder acts as a fine-grained verifier. It first estimates baseline scores μ_gt and μ_hal by averaging dot-product scores over ground-truth and hallucinated object tokens across the training set, then assigns a normalized reward in [-1, 1] to each generated token in the entity set C: positive if its score exceeds μ_gt + λ, negative if below μ_hal − λ, and zero otherwise, with a KL-divergence penalty against the reference policy. Policy optimization uses clipped PPO (4 epochs per sample) with LoRA (r=128) fine-tuning, requiring no separate reward model, no external preference data, and no human annotation. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.1) proves that under a linear generative model, incorporating vision-encoder feedback with a positive weight λ strictly reduces the expected regression loss compared to SFT alone, because the feedback term partially recovers a component of the ground-truth noise that is correlated with the visual input.

## Results

On the LLaVA-1.5 backbone, FiSAO achieves 1522.6 MME-Perception, 60.6 SEED-Bench, 69.3 ScienceQA, 61.2 COCO-Cap, 39.9 CHAIR_S, and 9.9 CHAIR_I, delivering an average performance improvement of 8.7% over the unmodified baseline. On InstructBLIP, FiSAO reaches 1305.3 MME-Perception, 40.0 SEED-Bench, 45.4 ScienceQA, 66.7 COCO-Cap, 42.2 CHAIR_S, and 8.8 CHAIR_I, with the largest gains in hallucination reduction (CHAIR_S drops from 60.3 to 42.2). FiSAO also outperforms all other preference tuning methods (Vlfeedback, Human-Prefer, POVID) on the majority of benchmarks without requiring any additional data, reward model training, or GPT-assisted annotation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiSAO outperforms all three preference tuning baselines (Vlfeedback/Silkie, Human-Prefer/LLaVA-RLHF, POVID) on the LLaVA-1.5 backbone across most benchmarks, achieving the best MME-Perception (1522.6 vs. 1432.7/1490.6/1452.8), SEED (60.6 vs. 59.3/58.1/60.2), ScienceQA (69.3 vs. 66.2/65.8/68.8), and COCO-Cap (61.2 vs. 54.8/50.4/57.3). On hallucination metrics, FiSAO achieves 39.9 CHAIR_S and 9.9 CHAIR_I, beating Vlfeedback (40.3/13.2) and Human-Prefer (38.7/11.3) on CHAIR_I, though POVID achieves slightly lower CHAIR_S (35.2) and CHAIR_I (8.3). On the InstructBLIP backbone, FiSAO shows the most dramatic gains, improving CHAIR_S from 60.3 to 42.2 and CHAIR_I from 11.9 to 8.8, while also leading on MME-Perception (1305.3), SEED (40.0), ScienceQA (45.4), and COCO-Cap (66.7). Against open-source VLLMs in Table 3, FiSAO(LLaVA-1.5) slightly edges out the base LLaVA-1.5 on MME-P (1522.6 vs. 1510.7), SEED (60.6 vs. 58.6), and ScienceQA (69.3 vs. 66.8), while remaining competitive with Qwen-VL-Chat and mPLUG-Owl2 on most metrics. Notably, FiSAO requires no additional data, no reward model training, and no GPT-assisted annotation, unlike all compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

FiSAO extends the standard preference tuning pipeline (SFT → Reward Modeling → Policy Optimization) established by Ouyang et al. and adapted to VLLMs by Yu et al. and Sun et al., but departs fundamentally from prior methods by replacing external reward models or GPT-assisted labeling with the model's own pre-trained vision encoder as a token-level verifier. Where prior approaches (Silkie/Vlfeedback, LLaVA-RLHF, POVID) rely on sentence-level or output-level rewards and require additional annotated data, FiSAO reframes preference tuning as a sequential decision-making problem (MDP) where each generated token receives a fine-grained reward computed via dot-product with the visual embedding. The optimization leverages clipped-PPO (Schulman et al.) but applies it at token granularity rather than sequence granularity, which the authors argue is the first instance of token-level rewards in VLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal of FiSAO is its data-free self-alignment: no external reward model training, no human annotations, and no GPT-assisted labeling are needed, making it more scalable than competing preference tuning methods. The empirical finding that sentence-level CLIP scores have near-zero correlation (r = −0.01) with BLEU/ROUGE challenges the common assumption that CLIP sentence scores are reliable reward signals, motivating the shift to token-level feedback. However, the method's effectiveness is bounded by the quality of the pre-trained vision encoder (biases propagate), and the performance gains, while consistent across two backbones, are modest and FiSAO does not uniformly dominate all baselines (e.g., POVID still achieves lower CHAIR scores on LLaVA). Open questions include whether token-level self-rewards generalize beyond object tokens to reasoning or compositional content, and whether the approach transfers to video or multi-image settings.
