## Problem

Reconstruction-based multivariate time series anomaly detection (MTSAD) suffers from over-generalization, where anomalies are reconstructed as well as normal points and thus cannot be flagged by high reconstruction errors. Existing memory-bank solutions like MEMTO learn only point-level prototypes, which fail to capture local temporal dependencies needed to detect interval anomalies and cannot model periodic regularities needed to identify long-term period anomalies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251933/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series window of length 100 with C variables, which is internally split into non-overlapping patches of m different sizes (via average pooling) and into period segments derived from the top-K FFT frequencies per variable. It produces a per-time-point anomaly score that is the product of a softmax-weighted sum of feature-space distances to the nearest patch and period prototypes and the L2 reconstruction error, from which a binary label is assigned by thresholding. The reconstructed series in the original C-dimensional space is also available as an auxiliary output.

## Method

H-PAD learns two complementary prototype banks: (1) multi-scale patch prototypes, where the series is average-pooled at m different granularities, encoded by separate Transformer encoders, and stored in M-prototype memory banks updated via a sigmoid gating mechanism that balances retention of history with incorporation of new information; reconstruction at each scale uses attention-weighted prototype lookup, and the m scale-specific reconstructions are fused by a two-layer MLP; (2) period prototypes, where FFT identifies the top-K dominant frequencies, the series is segmented into period-length chunks per variable, and one prototype per variable per period is learned with the same gated update rule. The final reconstruction is a weighted sum of the patch-branch and period-branch outputs, and the anomaly score multiplies the reconstruction error by a softmax of the summed distances from each query to its nearest patch prototype (inverse-scaled by patch size) and to the corresponding period prototype. Training minimizes a weighted combination of Frobenius reconstruction loss, entropy-based sparsity on patch reconstruction weights, and L2 distance between period prototypes and their encoded segments.

## Results

H-PAD achieves the best average F1-score of 96.86% across five benchmark datasets (MSL, SMAP, PSM, SMD, SWaT), exceeding 95% F1 on every individual dataset. Under the more rigorous AUC-ROC/AUC-PR evaluation (avoiding the point-adjusted metric), H-PAD attains the highest average AUC-ROC of 72.83 and AUC-PR of 33.12 across all seven datasets, outperforming the next-best method DMamba (67.69 / 30.61) by a substantial margin. The ablation confirms that removing the patch-prototype branch causes the largest performance drop (avg F1 falls from 96.86% to 83.08%), while removing the period branch drops it to 96.11%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-PAD outperforms all 17 baselines on average F1 across the five primary datasets. The closest competitor is GSC_MAD (95.10% avg F1), which H-PAD surpasses by 1.76 points; MEMTO (94.99%) and DCdetector (94.37%) trail by 1.87 and 2.49 points respectively. On the AUC-ROC/PR evaluation (Table 2, seven datasets), H-PAD leads by a wide margin: its average AUC-ROC of 72.83 exceeds DMamba (67.69) by 5.14 points and MEMTO (55.72) by 17.11 points, while its AUC-PR of 33.12 beats DMamba (30.61) and all other baselines (≤19.56). Notably, DMamba, despite strong AUC-ROC on NIPS_TS_Water (96.93) and NIPS_TS_Swan (77.84), performs poorly on MSL (61.54) and SMAP (38.99), whereas H-PAD maintains more consistent performance across all seven datasets. Classical methods (Isolation Forest, LOF, OC-SVM) and early deep methods (LSTM-VAE, BeatGAN) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 15–35 F1 points. The paper also reports that under point-adjusted (PA) evaluation, several baselines (e.g., DCdetector, MEMTO) achieve inflated F1 scores, which is why the authors emphasize AUC-ROC/PR as the fairer comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

H-PAD extends the memory-guided reconstruction paradigm introduced by MEMTO (Song et al., 2024), which learned point-level normal prototypes with a gating-based update mechanism to combat over-generalization. The key departure is replacing single-point prototypes with multi-scale patch prototypes (via average pooling at different kernel sizes) to capture local temporal dependencies, and adding per-variable period prototypes derived from FFT-based frequency decomposition (inspired by TimeNet and FEDformer) to model global periodic structure. The broader reconstruction-based MTSAD lineage—including Anomaly Transformer, DCdetector, and D3R—provides the encoder-decoder backbone, while the memory-network update strategy traces back through MemAE and MNAD to MEMTO's gating unit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: point-prototype memory banks cannot distinguish interval anomalies (brief multi-point deviations) or periodic anomalies (shifts in recurring patterns), which are common in real-world monitoring. However, the practical impact is tempered by a significant computational overhead (~10× MACs and ~6× parameters vs. MEMTO) and by the fact that the headline F1 scores rely on point-adjusted evaluation, which the authors themselves flag as unreliable; the more rigorous AUC-ROC/PR results show solid but modest gains. Open questions include whether the multi-scale patch mechanism is truly necessary versus a single well-chosen patch size, how the method scales to very long series or high-dimensional settings, and whether the FFT-based period detection is robust to non-stationary or multi-periodic signals with overlapping frequencies.
