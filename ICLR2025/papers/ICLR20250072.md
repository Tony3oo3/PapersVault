## Problem

Conformal prediction for time-series data is fundamentally challenged by the violation of exchangeability, which is inherent in sequentially dependent observations. While weighted quantile approaches (e.g., Barber et al., 2023) can restore coverage for non-exchangeable data, they rely on pre-specified data-independent weights with no principled mechanism for optimization. This paper addresses the open question of how to learn optimal, data-adaptive weights for conformal prediction intervals on dependent time-series data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained point predictor f̂ (random forest in experiments), a sliding window of T past non-conformity scores (residuals ε̂_t = Y_t − f̂(X_t)), and a target significance level α. The window is broken into n = T − w overlapping segments of length w, where the last segment serves as the test point and the preceding n segments form training pairs for the RNW estimator. The output is an asymmetric prediction interval [f̂(X_t) + Q̂_β*(X̃_{n+1}), f̂(X_t) + Q̂_{1−α+β*}(X̃_{n+1})], where β* is chosen to minimize interval width, along with the data-adaptive RNW weights that govern the weighted empirical CDF.

## Method

KOWCPI embeds the Reweighted Nadaraya-Watson (RNW) estimator as the quantile regression engine within a sequential conformal prediction loop. The RNW estimator introduces probability-like adjustment weights p_i(X̃) that correct for the dependent structure of time-series residuals; these are computed by solving a one-dimensional convex minimization over a scalar λ (Lemma 3.1), making the computation tractable. The final weights Ŵ_i combine the adjustment weights with kernel similarity K_h(X̃_i − X̃), producing a weighted empirical CDF from which conditional quantiles are extracted as generalized inverses. At each time step, the sliding window of residuals is updated, the RNW weights are recomputed for the new test point, and an asymmetric interval is constructed by optimizing β* over [0, α] to minimize width. The bandwidth h is selected via a nonparametric AIC criterion (Cai & Tiwari, 2000), and the window length w is chosen by cross-validation or adaptively via a two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Theoretically, the paper proves asymptotic conditional coverage under strong mixing of the residual process, with the coverage gap bounded by O_p(h² + (nh^w)^{−1/2}) + 2Δ(X̃_{n+1}).

## Results

On three real time-series datasets (Electric, Wind, Solar) with a target coverage of 90%, KOWCPI achieves valid marginal coverage (0.90, 0.91, 0.90) while producing the narrowest average prediction interval widths: 0.22 on Electric, 2.41 on Wind, and 48.8 on Solar. On the AAPL stock price dataset, KOWCPI achieves 91.2% coverage with a width of 15.74, compared to SPCI's 19.39 and ACI's 44.84. The method's advantage is most dramatic on the Solar dataset, where KOWCPI's width (48.8) is roughly 3× narrower than SPCI (84.2) and ~3× narrower than ACI/FACI/AgACI (~142–145).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KOWCPI outperforms all baselines that achieve valid coverage on the width metric across all three main datasets. The closest competitor is SPCI (Xu & Xie, 2023b), which is narrower than ACI/FACI/AgACI but still 30–70% wider than KOWCPI (e.g., 0.29 vs 0.22 on Electric, 84.2 vs 48.8 on Solar). ACI, FACI, and AgACI all produce substantially wider intervals, particularly on Wind (7.5–8.3 vs 2.41) and Solar (~142–145 vs 48.8), because they rely on a single adaptive threshold rather than data-adaptive kernel weights. SF-OGD and SAOCP fail to achieve valid coverage on most datasets (e.g., SF-OGD drops to 0.11 on Wind and 0.00 on Solar), while Plain NW (the un-reweighted Nadaraya-Watson variant) collapses to 0.41 coverage on Solar, confirming the importance of the reweighting step. On the AAPL stock price dataset (Table A.1), KOWCPI again achieves the narrowest width (15.74) among methods with valid coverage, beating SPCI (19.39) and all ACI variants (43–45).

## Relation to Prior Work

KOWCPI sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the classical Reweighted Nadaraya-Watson (RNW) estimator for nonparametric conditional distribution estimation (Hall et al., 1999; Cai, 2002), (2) sequential conformal prediction for time series via quantile regression on non-conformity scores (Xu & Xie, 2023b), and (3) weighted conformal prediction for non-exchangeable data (Barber et al., 2023). The key departure is that while Barber et al. required pre-specified data-independent weights and SPCI used a simpler quantile regression, KOWCPI embeds the full RNW estimator to learn data-adaptive weights in a principled, data-driven manner, directly addressing the open weight-selection problem. The theoretical analysis leverages classical nonparametric regression consistency results under strong mixing, connecting conformal prediction theory to the well-developed literature on kernel estimation for dependent data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first asymptotic conditional coverage guarantee for a kernel-based conformal method under strong mixing of residuals (a weaker assumption than stationarity of the full time series), filling a theoretical gap in the time-series conformal prediction literature. The empirical results consistently show KOWCPI achieves the narrowest prediction intervals while maintaining valid coverage across diverse real-world datasets, with the Plain NW ablation confirming that the reweighting mechanism is essential for handling volatility shifts. Open questions include the precise convergence rate of the conditional coverage gap, the practical behavior of the adaptive window selection (Appendix E), and whether the approach extends cleanly to high-dimensional or multivariate settings. For practitioners needing distribution-free uncertainty quantification on non-stationary time series, KOWCPI offers a theoretically grounded and empirically competitive option.
