## Problem

Prior Bayesian Pseudo-Coreset (BPC) methods that learn small synthetic datasets to approximate BNN posteriors suffer from three key limitations: they require storing expert weight trajectories across multiple seeds and epochs, they rely on stop-gradient during training which yields sub-optimal pseudo-coresets, and they still depend on sequential SGMCMC sampling at inference for Bayesian Model Averaging. This paper addresses all three issues by reformulating BPC within a variational inference framework that admits closed-form solutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full training dataset of RGB images with class labels (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) and produces a small synthetic pseudo-coreset of size m×k images (m images per class for k-way classification, with m typically 1–50). At inference, a single test image is passed through a trained feature extractor and the variational posterior over last-layer weights yields a predictive distribution (class probabilities with calibrated uncertainty) in one forward pass, eliminating the need for multiple SGMCMC samples. The pseudo-coreset is trained with a 3-layer CNN + BatchNorm backbone, and the learned images are optimized end-to-end via bilevel optimization.

## Method

VBPC treats only the last linear layer of a CNN as random (Gaussian variational posterior) while keeping the feature extractor deterministic, and formulates coreset learning as a bilevel optimization. The critical design choice is using a Gaussian likelihood for the inner (coreset VI) problem, which yields a closed-form solution for the variational posterior via the Woodbury formula, thereby eliminating the need for stop-gradient or unrolled computation graphs. The outer (dataset VI) problem uses a softmax categorical likelihood and is optimized with SGD, where the expected log-softmax is approximated via a mean-field Gaussian-softmax integral (Lu et al., 2020). Memory efficiency is achieved by never materializing the O(h²) covariance matrix V*, instead computing determinants and traces through the Weinstein-Aronszajn identity and storing only the O(n̂²) kernel matrix (I + γ/(ρβ) ΦΦᵀ)⁻¹. A model pool of P randomly initialized feature extractors is cycled during training to prevent the pseudo-coreset from overfitting to a single feature map, and at inference the trained pseudo-coreset is used to fine-tune a fresh network before computing the closed-form variational posterior for BMA.

## Results

VBPC achieves the best ACC and NLL across all five benchmark datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet) at every ipc setting, requiring only a single forward pass for Bayesian Model Averaging. On CIFAR-10 (10 ipc), VBPC reaches 69.8% ACC and 0.89 NLL, compared to the best BPC baseline FBPC at 62.3% ACC and 1.31 NLL. On CIFAR-100 (10 ipc), VBPC achieves 49.4% ACC and 2.07 NLL versus FBPC's 39.7% ACC and 2.67 NLL. The NLL improvements are particularly large, with VBPC reducing NLL by 0.4–1.5 points over the strongest baseline across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VBPC outperforms all four BPC baselines (BPC-rKL, BPC-fKL, FBPC, BPC-CD) on every dataset and ipc setting for both ACC and NLL. The margin is largest on CIFAR-10 (10 ipc), where VBPC achieves 69.8% ACC and 0.89 NLL versus the best BPC baseline FBPC at 62.3% ACC and 1.31 NLL — a 7.5-point ACC gain and 0.42 NLL reduction. VBPC also surpasses dataset distillation baselines FRePo (53.9%) and RCIG (69.1%) on CIFAR-10 (10 ipc) ACC, reaching 69.8%. A key practical advantage is that VBPC requires only a single forward pass for BMA, whereas SGMCMC-based baselines require multiple forward passes (one per weight sample) to compute the predictive distribution. On OOD corruptions (CIFAR-10-C), VBPC maintains the largest accuracy and NLL margins across all seven corruption types, e.g., 59.3% ACC under Gaussian Blur vs. 41.4% for BPC-CD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian Pseudo-Coreset (BPC) line of work (Kim et al., 2022; 2023; Tiwary et al., 2024) by replacing SGMCMC-based posterior approximation with last-layer variational inference (Fiedler & Lucia, 2023; Harrison et al., 2024b), building on the Bayesian Learning Rule framework (Khan & Rue, 2023). The bilevel optimization formulation with the implicit function theorem follows the dataset distillation approach of Loo et al. (2023), while the mean-field approximation for the Gaussian-softmax integral (Lu et al., 2020) handles the outer optimization. The key departure from prior BPC work is the use of a Gaussian likelihood for the inner (coreset) VI problem, which yields a closed-form solution and eliminates the need for expert trajectories, stop-gradient, and iterative SGMCMC sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VBPC reduces Bayesian Model Averaging from multiple forward passes (one per SGMCMC sample) to a single forward pass, while cutting training memory by ~50% and training time to under 20% of the naive approach. The closed-form inner solution is a clean theoretical contribution that makes the bilevel optimization tractable without unrolling computation graphs. A key limitation is the restriction to last-layer VI, which the authors acknowledge may underperform for very deep or complex models; extending to full-weight variational inference is identified as future work. The Gaussian likelihood choice for the inner problem, while enabling closed-form solutions, represents a modeling compromise that may limit expressiveness for highly non-Gaussian posteriors.
