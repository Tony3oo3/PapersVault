## Problem

Filter-based Concept Activation Vectors (CAVs), computed as weight vectors of linear classifiers such as SVMs, optimize class separability rather than precisely capturing the concept signal direction. This causes directional divergence because distractor signals—unrelated features in the data—are picked up by the filter weights to improve separability, producing inaccurate concept directions that degrade downstream applications like TCAV sensitivity testing and ClArC shortcut removal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251934/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes m-dimensional latent activations from a chosen DNN layer and binary concept labels (+1/−1) as input, producing an m-dimensional concept activation vector pointing from samples without the concept to samples with it. Unlike filter-based CAVs that require selecting a linear model type (SVM, lasso, logistic, ridge) and tuning regularization strength, the pattern-CAV is computed in closed form with no hyperparameters. The resulting CAV is then consumed downstream either as the direction for TCAV sensitivity scoring (dot product with the model gradient) or as the artifact direction to penalize in ClArC model correction.

## Method

The core contribution reverses the modeling direction: instead of regressing concept labels on activations (classification, as in filter-CAVs), the pattern-CAV regresses activations on concept labels, minimizing the Frobenius norm ‖A − thᵀ − 1bᵀ‖_F (Eq. 2). This yields a closed-form solution (Eq. 3) equal to the sample covariance between activations and concept labels divided by the concept label variance, which for binary labels simplifies to the difference of cluster means Ā⁺ − Ā⁻. The paper provides theoretical proofs (Theorems B.1–B.3) showing that filter-CAVs diverge under feature scaling (the weight entry scales anti-proportionally to the scaling factor) and under noise rotation (the filter prefers directions that minimize projection variance), while the pattern-CAV remains invariant to both. Empirically, pattern-CAVs achieve significantly higher cosine similarity with ground-truth concept directions across all convolutional layers and datasets, and improve both TCAV scores and ClArC correction effectiveness on VGG, ResNet, EfficientNet, and Vision Transformer architectures.

## Results

Pattern-based CAVs achieve significantly higher cosine similarity with the ground-truth concept direction across all 13 Conv layers of VGG16 on ISIC2019, Bone Age, and FunnyBirds, while filter-based CAVs (SVM, lasso, logistic, ridge) exhibit higher class-separability (AUC) but diverge from the true direction. On the FunnyBirds TCAV experiment, pattern-CAVs achieve a perfect ΔTCAV of 0.5 for VGG16 and EfficientNet-B0, whereas filter-CAVs yield lower scores. In ClArC model correction (Table 1), pattern-CAVs produce the highest biased-test accuracy (e.g., 0.75 for VGG16 on ISIC2019-controlled vs. 0.50–0.70 for filter-CAVs) and the lowest artifact relevance (0.26 vs. 0.54–0.62) and ΔTCAV (0.03 vs. 0.07–0.29), with statistical significance at p<0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares pattern-based CAVs against four filter-based CAV baselines: SVM (Cortes & Vapnik, 1995), Lasso (Tibshirani, 1996), Ridge regression (Hoerl & Kennard, 1970), and Logistic regression. On CAV alignment, pattern-CAVs consistently achieve higher cosine similarity with the ground-truth concept direction across all layers and all three datasets, while filter-CAVs trade alignment for higher class-separability AUC. In the TCAV experiment on FunnyBirds, pattern-CAVs achieve a perfect ΔTCAV of 0.5 for VGG16 and EfficientNet-B0, whereas SVM-CAVs and other filter-CAVs produce lower scores that fail to fully reflect the model's true concept dependence. In ClArC model correction, pattern-CAVs outperform all filter-CAVs on biased-test accuracy (e.g., VGG16: 0.69 vs. 0.28–0.37 for filter-CAVs on ISIC2019-controlled) and reduce artifact relevance by 40–60% (e.g., 0.26 vs. 0.54–0.62 for VGG16). The advantage is most pronounced for VGG16 and EfficientNet-B0; for ResNet50 and ViT, the gap narrows but pattern-CAVs still achieve the best or near-best biased accuracy and lowest artifact relevance. Notably, pattern-CAVs require no hyperparameter tuning (no regularization strength), unlike all filter-based baselines, making them more computationally efficient and less sensitive to random seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the CAV framework introduced by Kim et al. (2018) for concept sensitivity testing, but departs from the standard practice of computing CAVs as weight vectors of linear classifiers (SVM, logistic, ridge, lasso). The core idea is borrowed from the neuroimaging literature (Haufe et al., 2014), which showed that linear model weights conflate the true signal with distractor components, and from PatternNet (Kindermans et al., 2018), which applied this decomposition to local explainability. The authors transplant this signal/distractor separation to the global CAV setting, replacing the separability-optimizing filter with a regression-based pattern that isolates the concept direction. The paper also builds on the ClArC framework (Anders et al., 2022; Dreyer et al., 2024) for CAV-based model correction, showing that directional precision matters for shortcut removal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that filter-based CAVs produce arbitrary TCAV scores depending on distractor geometry undermines the reliability of concept sensitivity testing, a widely adopted interpretability tool. Pattern-CAVs offer a simpler, hyperparameter-free alternative that is provably invariant to feature scaling and robust to noise, with clear benefits across multiple architectures and datasets. However, the paper acknowledges that for applications prioritizing class separability (e.g., post-hoc concept bottleneck models), filter-CAVs remain preferable, so the choice of CAV computation must be task-dependent. Open questions include extending the approach beyond binary concept labels, incorporating prior knowledge (e.g., sparsity), semi-supervised concept discovery, and disentangling correlated concept directions.
