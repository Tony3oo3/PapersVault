## Problem

Black-box model inversion attacks, where adversaries only query model outputs, have outperformed state-of-the-art white-box attacks, yet existing defenses modify model weights or structure and thus fail to resist them. No prior work has leveraged conditional mutual information (CMI) as a defense objective, and no training-free post-processing method exists that can effectively reduce the information leakage in model outputs against black-box MIAs.

## Input / Output

The method takes the raw softmax output f(x) ∈ Δ^Y of a pre-trained classifier and a set of pre-computed class-conditional mean vectors q^y (the average softmax output over validation samples of class y), along with a temperature T for sampling the target class. It produces a modified probability vector p* on the same simplex, constrained to stay within an adaptive L1 ball of radius ε·H(Ŷ|X=x) around f(x). The entire pipeline is a post-processing step applied at inference time—no model retraining is required, and the defender only needs the class-conditional means D' rather than the full private dataset.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) proves that CMI I(X;Ŷ|Y) = I(X;Ŷ) − I(Ŷ;Y), making it a special case of the Information Bottleneck with Z=Ŷ and β=1; minimizing CMI therefore simultaneously compresses input–output dependence (improving MIA resistance) and maximizes prediction–ground-truth dependence (preserving utility). Operationally, for each query the algorithm samples a target class y with probability softmax(f(x)/T), then solves a convex problem: min KL(p‖q^y) subject to ‖p−f(x)‖₁ ≤ ε·H(Ŷ|X=x) and p ∈ Δ^Y, where the distortion bound is adaptively scaled by the Shannon entropy of the output (adaptive rate-distortion). The closed-form solution is obtained via a water-filling procedure in O(|Y| log |Y|) time, with a GPU-parallel variant (Algorithm 3) that eliminates branching and processes batches in ~0.5 s for 512 images, adding only 2.5–7.4% inference overhead.

## Results

Under soft-label attacks (Mirror, C2FMI), SSD reduces attack top-1 accuracy from 18.0% to 3.2% on IR-152/CelebA and from 55.2% to 32.2% on IR-152/FaceScrub, while maintaining the highest test accuracy (87.1% and 97.0%) and lowest output distortion (0.191 and 0.055) among all defenses. Under hard-label attacks (BREP, LOKT), SSD achieves 1.8% and 1.2% Acc1 on IR-152/CelebA (vs. 26.8% and 50.2% without defense), and 9.2% and 15.8% on IR-152/FaceScrub (vs. 50.6% and 90.2%). The additional inference overhead is only 2.5–7.4% (0.5 s for 512 images × 100 inferences on an RTX 3090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSD outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across both soft-label and hard-label attacks. Against MID (Wang et al., 2020), SSD achieves substantially lower Acc1 (e.g., 3.2% vs. 17.8% on Mirror/IR-152/CelebA) and higher feature distances, though MID marginally edges SSD on C2FMI Acc1 for IR-152/FaceScrub (0.2% vs. 1.6%). Against BiDO (Peng et al., 2022) and LS (Struppek et al., 2023), SSD consistently wins on both attack accuracy and feature distance across all model-dataset-attack combinations. Against TL (Ho et al., 2024), SSD dominates on hard-label attacks (e.g., 1.8% vs. 20.2% Acc1 under BREP on IR-152/CelebA) and also leads on soft-label metrics. Against Purifier (Yang et al., 2023), SSD achieves dramatically lower Acc1 under hard-label attacks (1.8% vs. 22.8% for BREP; 1.2% vs. 40.2% for LOKT) while also maintaining higher test accuracy (87.1% vs. 84.5%) and lower distortion (0.191 vs. 0.362). Notably, several baselines (MID, BiDO, LS, TL) perform worse than no defense on certain indicators, confirming that weight/structure-based defenses are ineffective against output-exploiting black-box attackers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes conditional mutual information (CMI), previously used as a training-time regularizer or performance metric (Yang et al., ISIT 2024), as a post-processing defense objective against black-box model inversion attacks. It extends the Information Bottleneck framework of Wang et al. (AAAI 2020, MID) by proving CMI is a special case of IB (Theorem 1), thereby inheriting IB's dual benefit of compressing input-prediction dependence while preserving prediction-label dependence. The key departure from prior MI-based defenses (MID, BiDO, LS, TL) is moving from training-time weight/structure modification to inference-time output modification, making the defense plug-and-play and specifically targeted at the black-box setting where attackers only observe outputs. Temperature scaling from Guo et al. (ICML 2017) is adapted to calibrate the sampling probability in the CMI minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that several existing defenses (MID, BiDO, LS, TL) perform worse than no defense against modern black-box attacks is a useful negative result that highlights a critical gap: defenses modifying model internals are largely ineffective when attackers only exploit outputs. SSD's plug-and-play nature (2.5–7.4% overhead, no retraining) makes it immediately deployable, and the adaptive rate-distortion constraint provides a principled way to balance privacy and utility. Limitations include evaluation restricted to face recognition (CelebA, FaceScrub) at relatively low resolutions (64×64, 224×224), and the method requires access to a validation set to compute class-conditional mean outputs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other modalities (text, audio), scales to very high-dimensional output spaces, and remains effective against adaptive attackers who are aware of the post-processing.
