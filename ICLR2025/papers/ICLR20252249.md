## Problem

Federated generative models face a triple challenge: GAN-based FL methods are notoriously unstable under non-IID data heterogeneity, prior work is limited to simple datasets (MNIST, FMNIST) and fails on complex ones like CelebA and CIFAR-10, and full-model weight communication incurs prohibitive overhead. No existing method simultaneously addresses communication efficiency, differential privacy, training stability, and generation quality in federated image generation.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a random noise vector as input and produces images at dataset-specific resolutions (28×28 for MNIST/FMNIST, 64×64 for CelebA, 32×32 for CIFAR-10). Rather than transmitting full model weights, each client uploads only a stochastic binary mask (~5.75 MB vs. 14–15 MB for baselines), and the final output is a lightweight generator W* = W_init ⊙ M* whose weights are already 1-bit quantized via Kaiming Normal initialization (each parameter is ±√(2/n_{l-1})), reducing storage to ~7.25 MB. A VGG-19 network is used solely for computing MMD loss during training and is discarded afterward.

## Method

PRISM reframes federated generative training as a strong lottery ticket search: instead of updating weights, each client iteratively refines a score vector by minimizing an MMD loss (matching mean and covariance of real vs. fake images in VGG-19 embedding space) over a frozen randomly-initialized ResNet generator. Scores are mapped through sigmoid to Bernoulli probabilities, and stochastic binary masks are sampled and uploaded to the server, which aggregates them into a global probability vector. A key stability mechanism, MADA (Mask-Aware Dynamic Moving Average Aggregation), interpolates the new global mask with the previous one using the Hamming distance between consecutive global masks as the interpolation coefficient λ, automatically dampening client drift without extra regularization or hyperparameter tuning. Differential privacy is enforced by injecting Gaussian noise into the probability vector before Bernoulli sampling, with the overall budget tracked via a subsampled moments accountant. A hybrid variant PRISM* selectively communicates float scores for a fraction α of layers (chosen from deeper layers) while sending binary masks for the rest, enabling a tunable trade-off between generation quality and communication cost.

## Results

Under the non-IID, privacy-free setting (Table 3), PRISM achieves FID of 9.70 (MNIST), 32.75 (FMNIST), 21.86 (CelebA), and 61.12 (CIFAR-10), while the hybrid PRISM* variant further improves to 6.96, 29.01, 13.02, and 35.53 respectively. Under the challenging non-IID + (9.8, 10⁻⁵)-DP setting (Table 2), PRISM achieves FID of 34.20 (MNIST), 67.16 (FMNIST), and 39.80 (CelebA), compared to 338.67, 131.62, and 228.97 for GS-WGAN. PRISM requires only 5.75 MB per-round uplink communication (vs. 14–15 MB for GAN baselines) and produces a final model of 7.25 MB (vs. 14–15 MB), thanks to its 1-bit Kaiming initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM decisively outperforms both privacy-preserving GAN baselines: under non-IID + DP, PRISM achieves FID 34.20/67.16/39.80 on MNIST/FMNIST/CelebA versus 338.67/131.62/228.97 for GS-WGAN and 153.93/146.63/222.83 for DP-FedAvgGAN, while also using ~60% less communication per round. Against the privacy-free MD-GAN, PRISM wins on MNIST (9.70 vs. 37.80 FID), FMNIST (32.75 vs. 55.51), and CIFAR-10 (61.12 vs. 52.72 for PRISM, but 35.53 for PRISM*), though MD-GAN retains a slight edge on CelebA (18.91 vs. 21.86); PRISM* closes this gap with FID 13.02. Multi-FLGAN is the weakest baseline, completely failing on CelebA (FID 314.84, P&R 0/0) and CIFAR-10 (FID 163.05) under non-IID, and requiring 9× more communication (52 MB) than PRISM. The ablation PRISM† (without MADA) consistently underperforms full PRISM across all settings, confirming that the mask-aware dynamic moving average aggregation contributes meaningfully to stability under heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the Strong Lottery Ticket hypothesis, which has been applied to classification (Frankle & Carbin, 2018) and recently to centralized generative models (Yeo et al., 2023); (2) federated learning with binary mask communication for classification tasks (FedMask, FedPM); and (3) federated GANs with or without differential privacy (MD-GAN, Multi-FLGAN, DP-FedAvgGAN, GS-WGAN). The key departure is replacing adversarial training with MMD-based moment matching and shifting the optimization target from weight updates to a stochastic binary mask over frozen Kaiming-initialized weights, combined with a novel mask-aware dynamic moving average aggregation (MADA) that has no direct analogue in prior FL aggregation schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRISM is the first framework to produce coherent images on complex datasets (CelebA, CIFAR-10) under simultaneous non-IID and differential-privacy constraints in FL, a setting where all prior GAN-based methods collapse entirely. The 48% communication reduction and the inherently lightweight final model (1-bit quantized weights via Kaiming initialization) make it practically relevant for edge-device deployment. However, the generator remains a modest ResNet, the VGG19 feature extractor (discarded post-training) introduces a dependency on a large pretrained network during training, and the gap to centralized training (e.g., FID 34 vs. 5.8 on MNIST non-IID) is still substantial. Open questions include whether the SLT+MMD approach scales to diffusion models (only a preliminary, noisy result is shown) and to larger architectures or datasets like ImageNet.
