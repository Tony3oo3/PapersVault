## Problem

Self-supervised MRI denoising methods (e.g., Noise2Void, Noise2Self) rely on masking or blind-spot strategies that limit receptive fields and cause oversmoothing of fine spatial features, yielding inferior performance to supervised approaches. Supervised methods require high-SNR ground-truth labels that are impractical to acquire in clinical MRI. The paper asks whether a score-based formulation can learn effective denoising directly from noisy observations without clean targets while preserving diagnostically relevant detail.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy 2D MRI slice (or a noisy target slice plus one or more auxiliary contrast slices for multi-contrast denoising) along with an estimated noise level σ_t_data, and produces a denoised 2D slice approximating E[X_0 | X_t_data]. The noise is modeled as additive Gaussian (valid when SNR > 2; Rician noise can be pre-processed via variance-stabilizing transform). During training, additional synthetic Gaussian noise is injected on top of the observed noise to create a continuum of corruption levels, so the network never sees clean data.

## Method

C2S introduces a Generalized Denoising Score Matching (GDSM) loss that extends standard DSM to the ambient-noise setting: given a noisy observation X_t_data, a forward process adds further Gaussian noise to produce X_t, and the network h_θ is trained so that a weighted combination γ·h_θ(X_t,t) + δ·X_t matches X_t_data, where γ and δ are closed-form coefficients depending on the noise schedule. The optimal solution provably recovers E[X_t_target | X_t], unifying DSM, ADSM, and Noisier2Noise as special cases. A reparameterization of the noise level (σ_τ² = σ_t² − σ_t_data²) enables uniform sampling over the corruption range for stable convergence, and a detail-refinement stage trains with a non-zero target noise level to retain fine textures. The backbone is a DDPM-style U-Net with time conditioning and a Noise Variance Conditioned Multi-Head Self-Attention (NVC-MSA) module that injects noise-level information into attention queries, keys, and values.

## Results

On the M4Raw test dataset (higher-SNR labels), C2S with detail refinement achieves 32.77 dB PSNR / 0.919 SSIM (T1), 32.33 / 0.890 (T2), and 32.51 / 0.876 (FLAIR), outperforming all self-supervised baselines by 0.8–2.2 dB PSNR and matching or exceeding supervised methods (Noise2Noise, SwinIR, Restormer) on most contrasts. On fastMRI with simulated noise (σ=13/255), C2S achieves 33.48 / 0.832 (PD) and 30.91 / 0.756 (PDFS), the best self-supervised results, though supervised Noise2True (U-Net) still leads by ~1 dB. Extending to multi-contrast inputs on M4Raw, C2S reaches 33.89 / 0.922 (T1, using T1+FLAIR), surpassing even the best supervised baseline (Noise2Noise: 32.59 / 0.911).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C2S with detail refinement decisively outperforms all self-supervised baselines on M4Raw: it beats Noise2Self by ~1.0 dB PSNR, Noisier2Noise by ~1.1 dB, Recorrupted2Recorrupted by ~0.9 dB, and PUCA by ~2.2 dB across contrasts. Against supervised methods, C2S matches or exceeds SwinIR and Restormer on all three contrasts (e.g., T1: 32.77 vs. 32.53 for SwinIR), and is competitive with Noise2Noise (T1: 32.77 vs. 32.59; T2: 32.33 vs. 32.37; FLAIR: 32.51 vs. 32.70, where Noise2Noise retains a small edge). On fastMRI, C2S is the best self-supervised method (e.g., PD σ=13: 33.48 vs. 33.29 for Recorrupted2Recorrupted), but supervised Noise2True (U-Net) still leads by ~1 dB (34.54 vs. 33.48), indicating that when clean labels are available, supervised training retains an advantage. Classical methods (NLM, BM3D) are consistently outperformed by 0.5–1.7 dB across all settings. The multi-contrast extension further widens the gap, with C2S (T1+FLAIR) reaching 33.89 / 0.922 on T1, surpassing even Noise2Noise by 1.3 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

C2S extends Denoising Score Matching (DSM) from Vincent (2011) to the ambient noise setting, where only noisy observations are available, by introducing the Generalized Denoising Score Matching (GDSM) loss that unifies DSM, Ambient DSM (Daras et al., 2024), and Noisier2Noise (Moran et al., 2020) under a single framework parameterized by a target noise level. The key conceptual departure is modeling the conditional expectation of a higher-SNR image given a further corrupted observation, rather than requiring clean labels or repeated noisy measurements. The architecture builds on the DDPM U-Net with time conditioning and the NVC-MSA module from DiffiT, adapted for denoising rather than generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C2S demonstrates that self-supervised score-based denoising can close the gap with supervised methods on MRI, which is practically significant because acquiring high-SNR ground truth requires longer scan times and is often infeasible. The robustness to ±50% noise level estimation errors and the multi-contrast extension make the approach broadly applicable in clinical settings where exact noise characteristics are unknown. Open questions include whether the Gaussian noise assumption (valid for SNR > 2) limits applicability in very low-SNR regimes, whether the framework extends naturally to 3D volumetric denoising, and whether more expressive backbones (e.g., Vision Transformers) would further improve performance as suggested but not fully explored in the paper.
