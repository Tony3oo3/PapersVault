## Problem

Self-supervised goal-conditioned RL (GCRL) agents have failed to achieve the same transformative impact as self-supervised learning in NLP and vision, due to two bottlenecks: slow CPU-based environment simulations that limit data collection to small datasets, and unstable training algorithms that prevent reliable scaling. This has prevented the field from exploring emergent properties of self-supervised RL on large-scale data and has made iterative research prohibitively expensive.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state vector from a continuous-control physics environment (e.g., Ant, Humanoid, Pusher) along with a goal state sampled from the environment's state space, and outputs a continuous action via a goal-conditioned policy π(a|s,g). Critically, no reward signal is provided during training; the agent learns purely from the structure of state transitions. Goals are sampled from the same distribution as training states (e.g., a disk of radius 10m for Ant, a maze-specific set for Ant Maze), and the policy must generalize to arbitrary goals within that distribution at evaluation time.

## Method

JaxGCRL is a fully JIT-compiled, GPU-resident implementation of Contrastive RL (CRL) that eliminates the CPU-GPU data transfer bottleneck by running all 1024 parallel environments, the replay buffer, and gradient updates on a single GPU with one CPU thread, achieving a 22× speedup over the original CRL codebase. The CRL algorithm uses a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss with an L2-norm energy function and a logsumexp regularizer (coefficient 0.1) to train a state-action-goal critic f(s,a,g) that approximates the discounted state visitation probability p_γ^π(g|s,a), which is equivalent to the goal-conditioned Q-function. A DDPG-style actor loss then extracts the goal-conditioned policy by maximizing the critic over actions. The paper also introduces a benchmark of 8 GPU-accelerated BRAX/MJX environments spanning simple manipulation (Reacher) to complex long-horizon locomotion (Humanoid, Ant Push), and systematically evaluates design choices including energy functions (L1, L2, dot product, cosine), contrastive objectives (InfoNCE, FlatNCE, DPO, IPO, SPPO, Forward-Backward), architecture scaling (width/depth up to 4×1024 with Layer Normalization), and UTD ratios up to 1:48.

## Results

JaxGCRL achieves a 22× speedup over the original CRL implementation, reaching over 16,500 environment steps per second on a single GPU (10M steps in ~10 minutes, 50M steps in under an hour for most environments). CRL with L2 energy function and symmetric InfoNCE objective outperforms all baselines (SAC, SAC+HER, TD3, TD3+HER, PPO) across the 8-environment benchmark, producing non-trivial policies even in the hardest tasks (Humanoid, Pusher, Ant Push). In the data-rich 300M-step setting, L2+InfoNCE dominates locomotion tasks while dot product performs best in Ant Soccer, and larger architectures (4×1024 with Layer Normalization) yield further gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRL clearly outperforms all four baseline families across the JaxGCRL benchmark. Against SAC and SAC+HER, CRL achieves higher success rates in most environments, with the gap being most pronounced in exploration-heavy tasks like Pusher and Ant Push where HER-based methods struggle (SAC+HER in particular suffers because relabeling the puck goal to its final position often trivializes the task). Against TD3 and TD3+HER, CRL again leads, with HER providing modest improvements to TD3 but not closing the gap to CRL. PPO performs worst across all tasks, likely due to the sparse reward setting (r=1 only at goal proximity), making it an unsuitable baseline for GCRL. Notably, CRL is the only method that learns effectively in all 8 environments without requiring a high UTD ratio, whereas other methods show significant degradation in high-dimensional (Humanoid) or exploration-intensive (Ant Push, Pusher) settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a systems and empirical contribution that builds directly on the Contrastive RL (CRL) algorithm of Eysenbach et al. (2022) and the classification-based critic framework of C-Learning (Eysenbach et al., 2021), re-implementing and stabilizing them in a fully JIT-compiled JAX pipeline. The GPU acceleration leverages BRAX (Freeman et al., 2021) and MuJoCo MJX for physics simulation, shifting the bottleneck from CPU-bound data collection to GPU-resident replay buffers and training. Conceptually, the paper does not propose a new learning algorithm but rather identifies which design choices (energy functions, contrastive objectives, architecture scaling, UTD ratios) matter most for CRL in data-rich online settings, filling a gap left by prior work that was limited by slow simulation and small-scale experiments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22× speedup and the 8-environment benchmark dramatically lower the barrier to GCRL research, enabling multi-seed experiments in minutes rather than days and making self-supervised RL accessible to under-resourced labs. Key empirical findings—L2 + symmetric InfoNCE dominating locomotion tasks at scale, dot product winning for manipulation, Layer Normalization enabling continued gains at width 1024, and the surprising inability of agents to stabilize at goals (Humanoid falls, Ant Soccer overshoots)—provide concrete guidance for future CRL development. Open questions include whether these findings transfer to pixel-based or partially observable settings, whether the goal-as-single-state assumption can be relaxed, and whether the identified architecture and loss preferences hold in offline or multi-task transfer regimes.
