## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for diffusion models assume the target distribution matches the training distribution, but practical zero-shot conditional samplers (e.g., image super-resolution, inpainting) inherently use unconditional scores to generate from a different conditional target, creating a score mismatch at every diffusion step. This mismatched regime remained largely unexplored theoretically, with no performance guarantees for widely used zero-shot samplers like CCDF or DDNM, and no explicit characterization of how the mismatch affects convergence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained unconditional score estimates s_t(x_t) (no retraining required), a conditioning vector y ∈ ℝ^p, and a measurement matrix H ∈ ℝ^{p×d} defining the linear model y = Hx₀ + n with Gaussian noise n ~ N(0, σ_y²I_p). It produces conditional samples x₀ ~ Q_{0|y} along with a KL divergence upper bound that explicitly decomposes into an oracle term, an asymptotic bias term (proportional to the accumulated Fisher divergence between target and sampling distributions), and a vanishing term. The bounds carry explicit dependencies on dimension d, the conditioning y, and the noise level σ_y², which is unusual compared to prior work that omits dimensional scaling.

## Method

The paper establishes the first non-asymptotic KL divergence bound for general score-mismatched DDPMs by decomposing the reverse-step error via a tilting-factor analysis under a mean-perturbed Gaussian, showing that score mismatch introduces a non-vanishing asymptotic distributional bias equal to the sum of step-wise Fisher divergences between the target and sampling distributions. Guided by this bias characterization, the authors design a bias-optimal zero-shot sampler (BO-DDNM) that selects the rectification function f*_{t,y}(x_t) = Σ_{t|0,y}^{-1}(√ᾱ_t H†y − H†Hx_t) to minimize the asymptotic bias for linear conditional models, where Σ_{t|0,y} incorporates the measurement noise covariance. Unlike vanilla DDNM, which ignores noise in the rectification, BO-DDNM accounts for σ_y² through the full covariance, yielding a δ^{-2} bias scaling versus DDNM's δ^{-4} under the early-stopping noise schedule. The technical core involves bounding all non-centralized Gaussian moments of the tilting factor to control higher-order Taylor terms, and the results are instantiated for bounded-support and Gaussian mixture targets with explicit dependencies on the conditioning y and inter-component correlation.

## Results

The paper establishes the first non-asymptotic KL divergence bound for general score-mismatched DDPMs, showing the error decomposes into an oracle term (decaying as Õ(T⁻¹)), a bias term proportional to the accumulated score mismatch, and a vanishing term. For the proposed BO-DDNM sampler on bounded-support targets, the asymptotic bias scales as δ⁻² (where δ is the early-stopping parameter), compared to δ⁻⁴ for vanilla DDNM (Theorem 4). For Gaussian mixture targets, the asymptotic KL divergence is of order d + Σₙ πₙ‖H†y − H†Hμ₀,ₙ‖², with explicit dependence on the conditioning y, measurement noise variance σ_y², and inter-component correlation ρ (Theorem 5, Proposition 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed BO-DDNM sampler against DDNM and DDNM⁺ (Wang et al., 2023) for linear conditional models with measurement noise. Theoretically, BO-DDNM achieves a strictly smaller asymptotic bias: its bias scales as δ⁻² while DDNM's scales as δ⁻⁴ under the early-stopping noise schedule (Theorem 4), meaning BO-DDNM converges to a tighter distributional bound as T→∞. Numerically (Figure 1), BO-DDNM achieves substantially lower KL divergence than both DDNM and DDNM⁺ for both Gaussian and Gaussian mixture targets, with the advantage growing as the measurement noise variance σ_y² increases. When σ_y²=0 (no measurement noise), BO-DDNM coincides exactly with DDNM, confirming that the improvement is specifically due to the optimal handling of noisy observations. The CCDF sampler (Chung et al., 2022b) is also shown to fit within the general score-mismatched framework (Appendix D.1), but no direct numerical comparison is provided against it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tilting-factor analysis framework of Liang et al. (2024) from the score-matched DDPM setting to the score-mismatched case, where the sampling scores differ from the target scores at every diffusion step. It builds directly on the DDPM formulation (Ho et al., 2020) and the DDNM zero-shot conditional sampler (Wang et al., 2023), providing the first convergence guarantees for widely-used zero-shot samplers like CCDF and DDNM that prior theory (Xu & Chi, 2024) could not cover due to its reliance on plug-and-play-specific Markov kernel properties. The noise schedule design from Li et al. (2024c) is adopted to obtain explicit dimensional dependencies. Conceptually, the paper departs from the extra-guided-training paradigm (classifier guidance, CFG) by focusing entirely on training-free score rectification, and identifies the asymptotic bias as a projected Fisher divergence between target and sampling distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a significant theoretical gap: zero-shot conditional diffusion samplers (CCDF, DDNM) are widely deployed in image restoration, super-resolution, and inpainting, yet lacked any convergence guarantees with explicit dimensional scaling. The identification of a non-vanishing asymptotic bias (rather than merely slower convergence) under score mismatch reframes how practitioners should think about the fundamental limitations of training-free conditional generation. The BO-DDNM sampler offers a concrete, drop-in improvement over DDNM under measurement noise, though its practical impact is currently validated only on small-scale Gaussian and Gaussian mixture targets (d=2–4). Open questions include extending the analysis to non-linear conditional models, incorporating higher-order score derivatives, and understanding whether the asymptotic bias can be eliminated entirely or only minimized.
