## Problem

Current computational methods for designing cis-regulatory elements (CREs) rely on iterative mutation-based optimization that is prone to local optima and produces low-diversity sequences, and they lack explicit incorporation of biological prior knowledge about transcription factor binding sites (TFBSs). This paper addresses both limitations by proposing a generative reinforcement learning framework that creates novel sequences from scratch while using TFBS regulatory roles as domain-informed rewards.

## Input / Output

The method consumes CRE sequences with MPRA-derived fitness labels (80 bp yeast promoters, 200 bp human enhancers), TFBS position frequency matrices from the JASPAR database, and an Enformer-based reward model (oracle or surrogate). It outputs 256 synthetic CRE sequences per optimization round, generated autoregressively nucleotide-by-nucleotide. TFBS occurrences are identified via FIMO scanning during generation, and the reward signal combines step-level TFBS rewards (positive for activators, negative for repressors, inferred per cell type via LightGBM + SHAP) with a terminal fitness reward from the reward model.

## Method

TACO fine-tunes a HyenaDNA state-space model (linear-complexity AR DNA language model) pre-trained on low-fitness CRE sequences, using REINFORCE with a composite reward: intermediate TFBS rewards assigned when a generated nucleotide completes a known TFBS (magnitude determined by mean SHAP values from a LightGBM model trained on TFBS frequency features), plus a terminal fitness reward from an oracle or surrogate. The key insight is that TFBS frequency features alone capture substantial predictive power for CRE fitness (Pearson r ≈ 0.65 vs. 0.83 for full-sequence Enformer), and SHAP values on this interpretable model reveal cell-type-specific activator/repressor roles that can be injected as dense step-level rewards during autoregressive generation. Auxiliary techniques include a hill-climbing replay buffer storing high-fitness sequences and entropy regularization to prevent premature convergence, enabling the policy to maintain high sequence diversity while climbing the fitness landscape.

## Results

On the human enhancer hard difficulty (most challenging setting), TACO achieves Top fitness of 0.76 (HepG2), 0.93 (K562), and 0.92 (SK-N-SH), matching or exceeding all baselines on K562 and SK-N-SH. The headline advantage is diversity: TACO achieves 131.8–137.4 median pairwise distance, roughly 1.7× higher than CMAES (the best diversity baseline at ~76–79) and 6–9× higher than PEX (~16–23). In the offline MBO setting (Table 4, K562), TACO achieves Top=0.75 and Medium=0.72, comparable to PEX (0.76/0.73), while maintaining Diversity=102.6 versus PEX's 15.8. On yeast promoters (hard), TACO reaches maximum fitness (Top=1) with diversity of 49.6–52.8, far exceeding all baselines (next best: CMAES at ~30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TACO dominates on diversity across all five datasets, achieving 124–139 median pairwise distance versus 75–79 for CMAES (the best diversity baseline) and 15–23 for PEX. On Top fitness for the hard difficulty, TACO matches or beats all baselines on K562 (0.93 vs. PEX 0.95, DNARL 0.89) and SK-N-SH (0.92 vs. PEX 0.90), while PEX retains a slight edge on HepG2 (0.85 vs. 0.76). In the offline MBO setting (Table 4), TACO's Top (0.75) and Medium (0.72) are within 0.01 of PEX (0.76/0.73), but TACO's diversity (102.6) is 6.5× higher than PEX (15.8) and 1.3× higher than CMAES (79.2). AdaLead and BO consistently underperform on both fitness and diversity, with AdaLead's greedy strategy causing rapid diversity collapse. The generative baselines regLM and DDSM maintain high diversity (149.6 and 93.4) but achieve substantially lower fitness (Top 0.69 and 0.43 respectively), confirming that pure generative modeling without optimization cannot match RL-guided approaches on fitness.

## Relation to Prior Work

TACO builds on the autoregressive DNA generation paradigm established by HyenaDNA and regLM, repurposing them as a policy network for RL fine-tuning rather than as conditional generators. It departs from prior CRE optimization methods (AdaLead, PEX, CMAES, GAs) that iteratively mutate existing sequences and are prone to local optima, instead generating novel sequences from scratch via a generative policy. The key conceptual departure is the explicit incorporation of TFBS regulatory knowledge—derived via SHAP values on a LightGBM model—as step-level rewards in the RL loop, inspired by de Almeida et al.'s observation that successful evolution first removes repressor motifs then adds activator motifs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TACO is among the first to explicitly integrate TFBS motif information into ML-based CRE design, bridging the gap between data-driven optimization and biological prior knowledge. The substantial diversity advantage (3–6× over baselines) while maintaining competitive fitness suggests that generative RL policies can explore the sequence space more broadly than mutation-based methods, which is critical for discovering truly novel regulatory elements. Open questions include whether the fixed JASPAR TFBS vocabulary limits the approach, whether TF orientation and pairwise interactions (currently ignored) would further improve performance, and how to properly evaluate biological plausibility of generated DNA sequences beyond fitness scores.
