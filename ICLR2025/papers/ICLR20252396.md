## Problem

Existing diffusion-based virtual try-on methods rely on heavy auxiliary components—additional image encoders (CLIP, DINOv2), duplicated ReferenceNet structures, text encoders with cross-attention, and multi-step preprocessing (pose estimation, human parsing, captioning)—which inflate parameter counts, training cost, and inference memory. The paper asks whether a single UNet can handle both garment and person images simultaneously through simple spatial concatenation, and which internal modules are truly necessary to adapt a pre-trained diffusion model to try-on.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252396/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The mask-free variant takes only a person image and a garment reference (in-shop or worn on another person) at 512×384 or 1024×768 resolution; the mask-based variant additionally requires a binary cloth-agnostic mask. No pose maps, parsing maps, or text prompts are needed at inference. The garment and person are concatenated along the width dimension before VAE encoding, and after denoising the output latent is split spatially to extract only the person half, which is decoded into the final try-on image.

## Method

CatVTON reduces the architecture to just a VAE and a simplified denoising UNet (899.06M params) by removing the text encoder, cross-attention layers, all image encoders, and any ReferenceNet. The core mechanism is spatial concatenation: the garment and person images are stacked along the width axis, jointly encoded by the VAE into a single latent, and fed to one UNet so that garment and person features interact within a shared latent space without any separate encoding pathway. For parameter-efficient training, ablation experiments show that only the self-attention layers (49.57M params) need to be fine-tuned—ResNet blocks and cross-attention contribute negligibly—preserving the pre-trained prior while enabling garment-person interaction. Training uses MSE loss with a 10% conditional dropout for classifier-free guidance (CFG strength 2.5) and the DREAM strategy (λ=10) to balance detail fidelity against distortion. Two model variants are trained: a mask-based model initialized from the inpainting version of Stable Diffusion v1.5 and a mask-free model initialized from InstructPix2Pix, the latter trained on pseudo-data generated by the former.

## Results

On VITON-HD (paired), CatVTON (Inpainting) achieves SSIM 0.8704, FID 5.425, KID 0.411, and LPIPS 0.0565, outperforming the closest diffusion-based competitor IDM-VTON (FID 5.762, KID 0.732) and matching GP-VTON's SSIM while substantially improving FID (5.425 vs. 8.726) and KID (0.411 vs. 3.944). On DressCode (paired), CatVTON (Inpainting) reaches SSIM 0.8922, FID 3.992, KID 0.818, and LPIPS 0.0455, beating OOTDiffusion (FID 4.610, KID 0.955) and IDM-VTON (FID 6.821, KID 2.924). In unpaired settings, CatVTON also leads on both datasets, e.g., FID 9.015 / KID 1.091 on VITON-HD vs. IDM-VTON's 9.842 / 1.123. The model achieves these results with only 49.57M trainable parameters and 4.00 GB inference memory, roughly 2× fewer total parameters and 10×+ fewer trainable parameters than competing diffusion-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CatVTON outperforms all eight quantitative baselines on every reported metric across both VITON-HD and DressCode. The closest competitor is IDM-VTON, which uses a large ReferenceNet (2.57B params) and text conditioning; CatVTON beats it by 0.34 FID and 0.32 KID on VITON-HD paired, and by 2.83 FID and 2.11 KID on DressCode paired, while using only 49.57M trainable parameters vs. IDM-VTON's 2.87B. OOTDiffusion is the strongest DressCode competitor (FID 4.610, KID 0.955), but CatVTON (Inpainting) improves FID to 3.992 and KID to 0.818. GP-VTON, a warping-based method, matches CatVTON on SSIM (0.8701 vs. 0.8704) and is slightly better on LPIPS (0.0585 vs. 0.0565), but lags substantially on FID (8.726 vs. 5.425) and KID (3.944 vs. 0.411), indicating weaker perceptual realism. StableVTON, StableGarment, MV-VTON, LaDI-VTON, and DCI-VTON are all clearly outperformed on FID and KID by margins of 1–12 points. OutfitAnyone is compared only qualitatively in in-the-wild scenarios (Figure 6), where CatVTON produces more natural background–person–garment fusion and better preservation of fine garment patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

CatVTON builds directly on Latent Diffusion Models (Stable Diffusion v1.5) and the DREAM training strategy, but departs sharply from the prevailing diffusion-based try-on paradigm that relies on ReferenceNet/Dual-UNet architectures, auxiliary image encoders (CLIP, DINOv2), and multi-stage preprocessing (pose estimation, human parsing, captioning). The core conceptual move is replacing all of these with a single spatial concatenation of garment and person images fed into one simplified UNet, motivated by the observation that pre-trained encoders fail to preserve fine garment detail while ReferenceNet's shared-latent-space mechanism can be replicated more simply through direct concatenation. The paper also contributes an empirical study identifying self-attention layers as the sole critical component for adapting a pre-trained diffusion model to try-on, a finding that reframes the fine-tuning strategy for this task family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing trainable parameters by over 10× and inference memory by 49%+ while matching or exceeding SOTA quality makes diffusion-based try-on deployable on consumer-grade GPUs (<8 GB VRAM), opening the door to real-time e-commerce applications. The finding that text conditioning actively degrades try-on performance and that a single UNet with spatial concatenation suffices challenges the field's momentum toward ever-larger multi-encoder architectures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher resolutions without VAE detail loss, how it performs on non-Western garment categories, and whether the self-attention-only training strategy transfers to other conditional generation tasks beyond try-on.
