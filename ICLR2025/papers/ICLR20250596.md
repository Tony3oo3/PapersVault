## Problem

Personalized image generation models (e.g., DreamBooth, AnyDoor, IDM-VTON) frequently produce localized artifacts such as incorrect logos, distorted textures, and blurriness that degrade fine-grained identity fidelity. Existing artifact refinement methods (PAL, RealisHuman, SynArtifact) lack controllability, cannot handle arbitrarily-shaped artifact regions, and fail to precisely preserve identity details from a reference image. This paper introduces the task of reference-guided artifacts refinement, providing a controllable, identity-preserving solution that was previously unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a generated image I_a containing artifacts, a user-provided free-form binary mask M_a delineating the artifact region (arbitrarily shaped, not restricted to rectangles or regular shapes), and a segmented reference object image I_r containing the correct identity details (logo, font, texture). The output is a refined image I_a* where only the masked region is corrected while the unmasked background and surrounding content remain unchanged. The pipeline is model-agnostic, requiring no test-time tuning or optimization, and operates on RGB images at standard resolutions processed through a VAE latent space.

## Method

Refine-by-Align is a two-stage diffusion model (U-Net backbone from Stable Diffusion with DINOv2 replacing the text encoder) where both stages share weights of a single network. The Alignment Stage exploits the spatial correspondence encoded in cross-attention maps: it computes a 2D correspondence mask by performing a Hadamard product between the artifact mask and the cross-attention map at each (timestep, layer) pair, then selects the optimal combination (t=0, l=9 at inference) to identify the matching region in the reference. The Refinement Stage feeds the extracted reference region (I_r ⊗ M*) through DINOv2 as visual guidance to inpaint the artifact region via a zoom-in strategy. Training is fully self-supervised in two modes: Alignment Mode uses complete objects as references to teach the model to locate relevant sub-regions, while Refinement Mode provides partial references to teach inpainting, both using Pixabay and MVObj datasets with color perturbations and affine transformations to simulate real-world variation.

## Results

On GenArtifactBench, Refine-by-Align achieves the best identity preservation scores among all methods: CLIP-I of 86.63 and DINO-I of 75.31, outperforming the strongest baselines (MimicBrush: 85.06 / 67.62; ObjectStitch: 83.94 / 72.62). On CLIP-T (text-semantic alignment), PAL edges ahead slightly (25.89 vs. 25.41), but the authors note CLIP-T captures only high-level semantics and cannot measure fine-grained detail preservation. In the user study (720 votes, 150+ workers), the method is preferred over every baseline on both identity (55.8–74.2% win rate) and realism (56.7–83.3% win rate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Refine-by-Align wins on both identity metrics (CLIP-I and DINO-I) against all six baselines. The largest margins are over PAL (DINO-I +21.5) and Cross-Image Attention (DINO-I +18.5), which lack reference-guided detail transfer. Against the closest competitor MimicBrush (a concurrent reference-guided editing method), the advantage is more modest: +1.56 on CLIP-I and +7.69 on DINO-I, but the user study shows a clear 60/40 preference for Refine-by-Align on identity and 59.2/40.8 on realism. Against ObjectStitch and AnyDoor (which receive the exact local reference region as input, reducing their difficulty), the method still leads by +2.7 and +4.0 on DINO-I respectively. The only metric where Refine-by-Align trails is CLIP-T, where PAL leads by 0.48 points; however, the authors argue CLIP-T measures only high-level text-image alignment and is insensitive to the fine-grained logo/text details that are the focus of artifact refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: reference-guided image editing (AnyDoor, ObjectStitch, Paint-by-Example) and artifact detection/refinement (PAL, RealisHuman, SynArtifact). It departs from prior artifact-refinement methods by introducing explicit reference-image guidance and a free-form mask, making the output controllable rather than purely learned. It departs from prior reference-guided editing methods by adding a dedicated alignment stage that localizes the correct sub-region in the reference via cross-attention maps of the diffusion backbone, rather than feeding the entire reference image. The architecture reuses the AnyDoor/IMPRINT U-Net with a DINOv2 vision encoder replacing the text encoder, and the alignment algorithm draws on the known property (Hertz et al., 2022) that cross-attention encodes spatial correspondence between generated pixels and conditioning tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first benchmark (GenArtifactBench) and the first method specifically for reference-guided artifact refinement, filling a gap between uncontrolled inpainting-based artifact removal and untargeted reference-guided editing. Its practical value lies in being a plug-and-play, model-agnostic post-processing step for any personalized generation pipeline (DreamBooth, virtual try-on, view synthesis) without test-time optimization. Key limitations include sensitivity to large appearance disparity between the generated and reference objects, and the 16×16 token resolution of DINOv2 capping spatial precision. Open questions include whether artifact detection can be automated end-to-end, whether multiple references or text descriptions can be blended, and whether the alignment strategy generalizes to higher-resolution or video settings.
