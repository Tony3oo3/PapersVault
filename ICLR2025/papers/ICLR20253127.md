## Problem

Existing 3D molecular representation pre-training methods model molecular energy states solely through classical mechanics (continuous potential energy functions via denoising), overlooking the quantized discrete energy level structures described by quantum mechanics. These quantized structures are experimentally measurable through molecular energy spectra (UV-Vis, IR, Raman) and encode critical information about electronic, vibrational, and rotational transitions that directly influence molecular properties and dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253127/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes 3D atomic coordinates (x ∈ R^{3N}), atomic numbers, and three spectral modalities: UV-Vis (601 points, 1.5–13.5 eV), IR (3501 points, 500–4000 cm⁻¹), and Raman (3501 points, same range), with absorption intensities log₁₀-transformed to mitigate peak-height variance. At downstream inference, only the 3D conformation and atomic numbers are required—the pre-trained 3D encoder produces molecular representations for property or force-field prediction without any spectral input, having internalized spectral knowledge during contrastive alignment.

## Method

MolSpectra introduces SpecFormer, a single-stream Transformer encoder that independently patches each spectrum (patch length P=20, stride D=10 for 50% overlap), projects patches to a shared latent space, concatenates all spectral patch embeddings, and processes them jointly via multi-head self-attention to capture both intra-spectrum (e.g., multiple peaks from the same functional group) and inter-spectrum (e.g., IR and UV-Vis peaks from the same aromatic ring) dependencies. SpecFormer is trained with a Masked Patches Reconstruction (MPR) objective that zeros out 10% of patches and reconstructs them via spectrum-specific heads. A contrastive InfoNCE loss then aligns the 3D encoder output (TorchMD-Net trained with Coord denoising) and the SpecFormer output for the same molecule, distilling spectral knowledge into the 3D representation. The full pipeline uses a two-stage schedule: stage 1 performs denoising-only pre-training on PCQM4Mv2 (~4M molecules, no spectra), and stage 2 applies the combined denoising + MPR + contrastive objective on QM9S (~134K molecules with computed spectra).

## Results

On QM9 (12 quantum chemistry properties), MolSpectra achieves state-of-the-art MAE in 8 out of 12 properties, including homo (15.5 meV), lumo (13.1 meV), gap (26.8 meV), and dipole moment μ (0.011 D), outperforming the primary denoising-only baseline Coord in 10 out of 12 properties. On MD17 force prediction, MolSpectra achieves the best MAE on 5 of 8 molecules (e.g., Aspirin 0.099 vs. Coord 0.211 kcal/mol/Å), demonstrating improved modeling of non-equilibrium molecular dynamics. The method also surpasses all training-from-scratch baselines (SchNet, EGNN, DimeNet++, PaiNN, SphereNet, TorchMD-Net) and other pre-training methods (Transformer-M, SE(3)-DDM, 3D-EMGP) on most QM9 properties.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary denoising-only baseline Coord (Zaidi et al., ICLR 2023), MolSpectra improves MAE on 10 of 12 QM9 properties, with the largest gains on gap (26.8 vs. 31.8 meV, −15.7%), U (5.45 vs. 6.11 meV, −10.8%), and G (6.18 vs. 6.91 meV, −10.6%), while slightly underperforming on R² (0.410 vs. 0.450) and Cᵥ (0.021 vs. 0.020). Compared to other pre-training methods, MolSpectra substantially outperforms SE(3)-DDM (e.g., homo 15.5 vs. 23.5 meV), 3D-EMGP (homo 15.5 vs. 21.3 meV), and Transformer-M (homo 15.5 vs. 17.5 meV) on electronic properties. Among training-from-scratch methods, MolSpectra matches or beats the strongest baselines TorchMD-Net (μ: 0.011 vs. 0.011) and PaiNN (homo: 15.5 vs. 27.6 meV) across most properties. On MD17 force prediction, MolSpectra achieves the best MAE on 5 of 8 molecules (Aspirin 0.099, Benzene 0.097, Ethanol 0.052, Malonaldehyde 0.077, Salicylic Acid 0.093) but trails Coord on Naphthalene (0.085 vs. 0.053), Toluene (0.075 vs. 0.058), and Uracil (0.095 vs. 0.074), suggesting the spectral knowledge is most beneficial for molecules with richer vibrational/electronic structure. The paper also validates generality by showing MolSpectra improves upon SliDe (Ni et al., ICLR 2024) as well, confirming compatibility with different denoising objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the denoising-based pre-training paradigm for 3D molecular representations (Coord, Frad, SliDe), which has been shown to be equivalent to learning molecular force fields within classical mechanics. The key departure is the introduction of quantum mechanical knowledge through multi-modal energy spectra (UV-Vis, IR, Raman), which capture quantized energy level structures that classical potential energy functions cannot represent. The authors propose SpecFormer, a single-stream Transformer encoder that jointly processes multiple spectra to capture intra- and inter-spectrum peak correlations, trained with a masked patch reconstruction objective, and aligned to the 3D encoder via a contrastive (InfoNCE) loss. This positions the work at the intersection of molecular spectroscopy and geometric deep learning, repurposing the masked autoencoder paradigm from NLP/vision for spectral signal reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that infusing quantum mechanical spectral knowledge into 3D molecular pre-training yields consistent improvements in property prediction (QM9) and force field prediction (MD17) over denoising-only baselines, suggesting that classical mechanics alone is insufficient for capturing full molecular behavior. However, practical adoption is limited by the scarcity of multi-modal spectral data (only ~134K molecules with three spectra at B3LYP/def-TZVP level), and SpecFormer currently handles only 1D spectra, leaving higher-dimensional modalities (2D NMR, correlation spectra) unaddressed. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger and more diverse spectral datasets, whether the pre-trained spectral encoder can be repurposed for spectrum-related downstream tasks (e.g., de novo structure elucidation), and whether the gains persist on larger molecules beyond the 9-heavy-atom QM9 regime.
