## Problem

Existing UI understanding models like Ferret-UI are constrained to a single platform type (mobile), a fixed grounding resolution, and text-only GPT-4 prompting for training data generation that lacks visual spatial context. This limits their applicability across the diverse platform landscape (smartphones, tablets, web, smart TVs) where resolution, layout, and interaction paradigms vary significantly.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a UI screenshot at any native resolution (ranging from 540×960 Android to 2048×2732 Web) paired with a free-form user text query, and produces grounded responses that include bounding box coordinates in the original image space, UI widget type classifications from a unified 13-class label space, user-centered interaction instructions (e.g., 'drag the slider left' rather than 'click [x,y]'), and multi-round perception/interaction Q&A. The adaptive N-gridding mechanism (with size limit N=8, bounding total grids to 16) ensures the model processes the screenshot at its original resolution without fixed-size cropping.

## Method

Ferret-UI 2 builds on a CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder + LLM backbone (Vicuna-13B, Llama3-8B, or Gemma-2B) architecture with three key innovations. First, it introduces adaptive N-gridding: rather than using a fixed grid configuration, it searches over all (Nw, Nh) pairs with Nw+Nh≤N to find the grid that minimizes the product of aspect-ratio distortion and relative pixel change, preserving original resolution while bounding inference cost. Second, it unifies five platforms (iPhone, Android, iPad, Web, AppleTV) under a common 13-class widget label space, using human-annotated or HTML-parsed bounding boxes instead of model-detected ones. Third, for advanced task data generation, it replaces Ferret-UI's text-only GPT-4 prompting with GPT-4o augmented by Set-of-Mark visual prompting (corner-style bounding boxes with unique number tags and color-coded widget classes), enabling the model to perceive spatial relationships and generate higher-quality, user-centered interaction training data across 529K samples.

## Results

On the constructed multi-platform benchmarks (averaged over iPhone, Android, iPad, Web, and AppleTV), Ferret-UI 2 with Llama3-8B achieves the best overall performance: 80.28 on referring, 82.79 on grounding, 89.73 GPT-4o score on advanced tasks, and 55.78 IoU on the GUIDE next-action prediction benchmark. The Vicuna-13B variant achieves the highest Multi-IoU (41.71) on advanced tasks, while the 2B Gemma variant remains competitive at 80.25 GPT-4o score. On the zero-shot GUI-World benchmark, Ferret-UI 2 attains an average GPT-4 score of 2.948, outperforming Ferret-UI (2.638) and GUI-Vid (2.767), though trailing GPT-4o (3.619) and Gemini-Pro1.5 (3.295).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ferret-UI 2 substantially outperforms its predecessor Ferret-UI across all metrics: the Llama3-8B variant improves the advanced-task GPT-4o score from 45.81 to 89.73 (+43.92), Multi-IoU from 18.75 to 41.15, and GUIDE IoU from 26.91 to 55.78. Against SeeClick (QWen-VL-9.6B), Ferret-UI 2 wins on referring (80.28 vs. 51.58), advanced GPT-4o score (89.73 vs. 67.49), Multi-IoU (41.15 vs. 21.56), and GUIDE IoU (55.78 vs. 39.51), though SeeClick edges ahead on grounding (62.82 vs. 82.79 is actually Ferret-UI 2 winning; SeeClick's grounding is 62.82). GPT-4o, despite strong general reasoning, struggles with fine-grained UI grounding (12.14) and achieves only 9.64 IoU on GUIDE, far below Ferret-UI 2's 55.78; even with SoM prompting, GPT-4o's Multi-IoU remains at 7.36. On the GUI-World zero-shot benchmark, Ferret-UI 2 (2.948 avg) beats Ferret-UI (2.638), GUI-Vid (2.767), SeeClick (2.704), and QWen-VL-MAX (2.580), but falls short of Gemini-Pro1.5 (3.295) and GPT-4o (3.619). On ScreenSpot, Ferret-UI 2 achieves 54.0% average grounding accuracy, surpassing SeeClick (53.4%) and CogAgent (47.4%), with particularly strong Mobile (80.3%/55.4%) and Web (81.2%/33.5%) performance, though underperforming on the unseen Desktop domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ferret-UI 2 directly extends Ferret-UI (You et al., 2024), which established grounded mobile UI understanding via multimodal LLMs but was limited to iPhone and Android with a fixed grounding resolution. It also builds on the Any-Resolution (AnyRes) encoding from LLaVA-Next (Liu et al., 2024a), replacing its unbounded grid expansion with a bounded adaptive N-gridding algorithm that minimizes aspect-ratio and pixel distortion under a fixed inference cost. The key conceptual departure from Ferret-UI is the shift from text-only GPT-4 prompting for data generation to GPT-4o with Set-of-Mark visual prompting, which enables spatially-aware training data, and the expansion from two mobile platforms to five heterogeneous platforms (iPhone, Android, iPad, Web, AppleTV).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in UI understanding: most prior agents are single-platform or mobile-only, while real-world ecosystems span phones, tablets, web, and smart TVs with vastly different resolutions and aspect ratios. The adaptive N-gridding mechanism and the use of human-annotated (rather than model-detected) bounding boxes offer concrete, reusable techniques for high-fidelity multi-platform UI grounding. Limitations include the heavy data imbalance across platforms (AppleTV has only ~16k images), the restriction to single-step interactions rather than multi-step task execution, and the absence of desktop environments. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to even more platform types, whether multi-step interaction can be layered on top, and whether the cross-platform transfer patterns (e.g., landscape vs. portrait domain gaps) can be mitigated with targeted data augmentation.
