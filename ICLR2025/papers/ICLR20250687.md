## Problem

Intrinsic motivation and reward shaping pseudo-rewards guide RL exploration but can cause degenerate behaviors (e.g., agents fixating on noisy TV screens) and reward-hacking, with no unified theory explaining why these failures occur or providing general criteria for when pseudo-rewards are safe. This paper asks: can we characterize all pseudo-rewards within a single framework that both explains their mechanisms and guarantees they cannot be exploited?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250687/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full BAMDP state ⟨s_t, h_t⟩ (current MDP state plus the entire observed history of states, actions, and rewards) and a user-specified potential function φ over BAMDP states, producing a pseudo-reward F(h_t) = γφ(h_t) − φ(h_{t−1}) added to the true reward at each step. Unlike classic PBSF which is restricted to functions of the current MDP state, the potential here can depend on the entire training history, enabling it to encode cumulative information value (e.g., count of unique states visited, maximum displacement achieved). In the meta-RL setting, only the meta-learner receives the pseudo-reward while the inner RL agent observes only true rewards, keeping the posterior over MDPs unaffected by shaping.

## Method

The paper formulates RL as a BAMDP where the state is the agent's full knowledge (history h_t), decomposing optimal BAMDP value into Value of Information (VOI, the return gain from accumulated knowledge) and Value of Opportunity (VOO, the prior expected value of the physical state). It then defines BAMPFs as potential-based shaping over BAMDP states and proves (Theorem 4.2) that BAMPFs are necessary and sufficient to preserve Bayes-optimality in meta-RL: the telescoping sum of γφ(h_{t+1}) − φ(h_t) reduces to a constant, so the optimal shaped policy equals the optimal unshaped policy. For standard RL, Theorem 4.3 shows that BAMPFs with bounded, monotone-increasing potentials preserve approximate MDP optimality at convergence, since the residual potential term at episode end becomes arbitrarily small. Empirically, a '1st Winner Pulls' BAMPF accelerates A2C meta-learning on Bernoulli Bandits without causing over-exploitation, and a 'Max Displacement' BAMPF (exponentially smoothed maximum x-displacement) helps PPO solve Mountain Car faster while avoiding the reward-hacking that plain displacement rewards produce.

## Results

The paper's empirical results are presented as learning curves rather than single-point metrics. In the Bernoulli Bandits meta-RL experiment, the 1st Winner Pulls BAMPF enables A2C to learn lower-regret RL agents faster than no shaping, while still converging to the optimal strategy; the same pull-count used as a raw (non-BAMPF) pseudo-reward causes the meta-learner to converge on an over-exploiting agent that never reaches optimal regret. In Mountain Car with PPO, the Max Displacement BAMPF accelerates goal-reaching compared to no shaping or classic PBS, while the raw Displacement reward produces a reward-hacking policy that avoids the goal. In a 100-lever MDP with DQN, the bounded monotone BAMPF (unique-lever count) increases exploration and still converges to the optimal policy, whereas an entropy bonus causes the agent to oscillate between the correct lever and others, achieving higher shaped but lower true return.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but rather contrasts different pseudo-reward formulations under a common framework. In Bernoulli Bandits meta-RL, the BAMPF (green) outperforms no shaping (grey) in convergence speed to low regret and matches it at the optimum, while the non-BAMPF '1st Winner Pulls' (purple) loses by converging to a suboptimal over-exploiting policy. In Mountain Car, the Max Displacement BAMPF (green) wins on both exploration speed (higher max displacement earlier) and final performance (reaches goal sooner) compared to no shaping, Displacement PBS (which preserves optimality but provides no exploration benefit), and raw Displacement (which reward-hacks by avoiding the goal). In the 100-lever DQN experiment, the BAMPF wins on both exploration breadth (more unique levers tried) and final policy quality (converges to always pulling the correct lever), while the entropy bonus loses by producing a reward-hacking oscillation policy. Across all three experiments, the consistent finding is that BAMPF formulations improve exploration efficiency without sacrificing final optimality, whereas non-BAMPF pseudo-rewards either fail to help or actively cause reward-hacking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Ng et al.'s (1999) potential-based shaping theorem from MDPs to BAMDPs, proving that BAMPFs preserve Bayes-optimality in meta-RL and (under bounded monotone potentials) MDP optimality in standard RL. It builds on Duff's (2002) formulation of RL as a BAMDP and unifies the previously separate literatures on intrinsic motivation (Pathak et al., 2017; Houthooft et al., 2016) and reward shaping under a single value-decomposition lens (Value of Information + Value of Opportunity). Unlike prior extensions of PBS to time-varying or history-dependent potentials (Devlin & Kudenko, 2012; Forbes et al., 2024), this work explicitly handles the fact that rewards influence the BAMDP state, making the necessity proof non-trivial, and provides a principled typology for categorizing all pseudo-rewards by which value component they signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BAMDP value decomposition gives practitioners a diagnostic tool for understanding why a given pseudo-reward helps or hurts in a specific domain, and the BAMPF form provides a concrete recipe for retrofitting existing pseudo-rewards to be immune to reward-hacking. However, the conversion process is manual and the monotonicity condition for MDP-level optimality is restrictive (excluding non-cumulative terms like raw prediction error or entropy bonuses without additional decomposition). Empirical validation is limited to small-scale environments (Mountain Car, Bernoulli Bandit, 100-lever MDP), leaving open whether the framework scales to complex continuous-control or multi-task settings where pseudo-reward design is most challenging. A key open question is whether BAMPF potentials can be learned automatically rather than hand-crafted.
