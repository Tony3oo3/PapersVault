## Problem

Medical imaging datasets suffer from severe data scarcity and long-tail imbalances, with pathological subjects far outnumbered by normal ones. Existing generative lesion synthesis methods either waste model capacity on background generation (risking anatomical integrity) or rely on hand-crafted heuristics that fail on complex multi-peak or multi-class lesions, and none offer fine-grained control over lesion texture, size, location, or mask alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a lesion-free 3D scan (CT at 64×64×32 or MRI at 72×72×10), a binary lesion mask, and optionally a texture histogram and control sphere as conditioning signals. It outputs a synthetic lesion-containing image with the original background perfectly preserved, plus a generated lesion mask. For multi-class cardiac lesions, the diffusion model operates on n channels (one per lesion class) and combines them via mask-weighted summation into a single grayscale image.

## Method

LeFusion redesigns the diffusion training objective with a lesion-focused loss (Eq. 4) that applies the foreground mask to the noise-prediction error, so the 3D U-Net learns to denoise only within the lesion region while the background is preserved by directly substituting forward-diffused real background pixels at each reverse step (Eq. 3). For multi-peak lesions (e.g., lung nodules with distinct attenuation types), a histogram-based texture control is injected via cross-attention, allowing the user to steer the generated texture type at inference without requiring lesion-type annotations. For multi-class lesions (e.g., cardiac MI and PMO), a multi-channel decomposition strategy generates each class in a separate channel and combines them, capturing inter-class correlations. A companion DiffMask module—a separate diffusion model conditioned on a bounding sphere and boundary mask—generates diverse lesion masks with controllable size, location, and shape, further expanding synthetic data diversity.

## Results

On the LIDC lung nodule CT dataset, LeFusion-H+DiffMask (with the most synthetic data, P+P'+N") achieves 83.44% Dice and 93.35% NSD with nnUNet, and 83.13% Dice and 93.20% NSD with SwinUNETR, improving over the real-data-only baseline (78.26% Dice / 88.90% NSD) by +5.18% Dice and +4.45% NSD for nnUNet. On the Emidec cardiac MRI dataset, LeFusion-J-H+DiffMask reaches 71.28% Dice for MI and 43.41% Dice for PMO with nnUNet, a +2.67% and +7.09% improvement over the baseline respectively. LeFusion consistently outperforms all four baselines (Hand-Crafted, Cond-Diffusion, Cond-Diffusion(L), and RePaint) across both datasets and both segmentation architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four baselines underperform the real-data-only baseline on LIDC when using real masks (P+P'), confirming that poor synthetic quality actively harms downstream segmentation. Hand-Crafted (Hu et al., 2023) drops nnUNet Dice to 76.80% (−1.46% vs. baseline) due to unrealistic Gaussian-noise textures. Cond-Diffusion (Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al., 2022) and Cond-Diffusion(L) (Chen et al., 2024) both disrupt background anatomy, yielding 77.05% and 76.66% Dice respectively. RePaint (Lugmayr et al., 2022) performs best among baselines at 77.57% Dice but still falls short of the baseline because its global loss cannot focus on lesion-specific textures. LeFusion-H (with histogram control) surpasses all baselines by 2.1–3.8% Dice, and adding DiffMask further pushes nnUNet Dice to 83.44% (+5.18% over baseline). On Emidec, the multi-class setting amplifies the gap: RePaint drops PMO Dice to 28.93% (−7.39% vs. baseline) while LeFusion-J+DiffMask achieves 43.54% (+7.22% over baseline), demonstrating that joint multi-channel modeling is critical for correlated lesion classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

LeFusion builds directly on the inpainting mechanism from RePaint and Blended Diffusion, which combine forward-diffused backgrounds with reverse-diffused foregrounds during inference, but repurposes it for a specialized medical task: generating only lesion content while preserving the anatomical background exactly. It departs from conditional diffusion approaches (Chen et al. 2024, Rombach et al. 2022) that use the background as a conditioning input and still require the model to reconstruct it, instead redesigning the training loss to focus exclusively on the lesion region. The background-preserving lesion synthesis paradigm (Hu et al. 2023, Yao et al. 2021) is retained, but the hand-crafted texture and mask generation is replaced with data-driven diffusion models augmented by histogram-based texture control and multi-channel decomposition for multi-class scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that synthetic lesion data generated with fine-grained control (texture type, size, location, boundary) can substantially improve downstream segmentation, addressing a persistent data-scarcity bottleneck in medical AI. A key practical insight is that without explicit histogram control, diffusion models on normal scans bias toward healthy-appearing textures, silently degrading downstream performance—a failure mode that is easy to miss without careful evaluation. The approach is framed as generalizable to any domain with abundant normal data and scarce anomalies (industrial, environmental, materials science), though validation is limited to two 3D medical datasets. Open questions include whether the method scales to larger 3D volumes, whether the recurrent inpainting mechanism is necessary or an artifact of the small crop sizes used, and how the approach performs when the normal background itself is heterogeneous or pathologically altered.
