## Problem

Fine-tuned LLMs on small datasets exhibit severe overconfidence, producing poorly calibrated probability estimates. While Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) offers single-pass uncertainty quantification, its training objective drives the model to generate excessively large evidence values, resulting in over-concentrated Dirichlet distributions that degrade calibration. Existing EDL regularizers (e.g., suppressing non-target evidence) do not fully address this overconfidence issue.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sequence (question plus multiple-choice options A/B/C/D) as input to a LoRA-fine-tuned LLM (Llama2-7B, Llama3-8B, or Mistral-7B). The LLM's output logits for the option tokens serve as pre-evidences, which are modeled as a Gaussian distribution with learned mean and variance. At inference, K=20 samples are drawn from this Gaussian, averaged, and converted through SoftPlus into Dirichlet parameters, yielding a predicted token, per-token belief masses, and a scalar uncertainty mass u = C/α₀. The total computational overhead is under 2% of the pretrained LLM's FLOPs.

## Method

IB-EDL regularizes the EDL pipeline by imposing an information bottleneck on the pre-evidence (the LLM's raw output logits), chosen specifically so that p(y|z) is exactly computable from the Dirichlet-Categorical pipeline without a learned decoder, thereby guaranteeing a valid lower bound on I(Z,Y). The LLM's linear head is doubled: one branch predicts the Gaussian mean μ and the other the variances σ of the pre-evidence distribution, both trained jointly with LoRA adapters. The IB-Info loss (KL divergence between the learned Gaussian p(z|x) and a standard normal prior) naturally imposes an ℓ₂ penalty on μ, directly discouraging the generation of disproportionately large evidence that causes overconfidence. Training minimizes L_IB-MSE + β·L_IB-Info using the reparameterization trick with K Monte Carlo samples per input. The paper also identifies a previously overlooked theoretical issue: substituting a learned q(y|z) for p(y|z) at intermediate layers can invalidate the IB lower bound, a problem avoided by the pre-evidence choice of Z.

## Results

On fine-tuned Llama2-7B, IB-EDL achieves the lowest ECE across all six datasets, reducing it by 4.7× on ARC-C (6.38 vs. 30.08 for MAP), 5.3× on ARC-E (2.57 vs. 13.66), and 3.9× on CSQA (4.90 vs. 19.06). NLL is also consistently the lowest, e.g., 1.03 on ARC-C and 0.29 on SciQ. On Llama3-8B, IB-EDL further reduces ECE to 2.78 (ARC-C), 2.34 (OBQA), and 4.34 (CSQA), while maintaining the highest or near-highest accuracy (81.14% on ARC-C, 89.00% on OBQA). The method introduces less than 2% computational overhead over the pretrained LLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IB-EDL outperforms all seven baselines on ECE across all six datasets for both Llama2-7B and Llama3-8B. Compared to MAP (the standard fine-tuning baseline), IB-EDL reduces ECE by 3–5× (e.g., 6.38 vs. 30.08 on ARC-C, 2.57 vs. 13.66 on ARC-E for Llama2-7B), while maintaining comparable or slightly higher accuracy. Against the multi-forward-pass methods MCD and Deep Ensemble, IB-EDL achieves substantially lower ECE (e.g., 6.38 vs. 30.08/28.26 on ARC-C) in a single forward pass. Among EDL-family baselines, IB-EDL beats vanilla EDL (6.38 vs. 14.26 ECE on ARC-C), VID (6.38 vs. 13.94), I-EDL (6.38 vs. 12.39), and R-EDL (6.38 vs. 15.34) on ARC-C, with similar margins on other datasets. The Laplace-LoRA (LA) baseline is competitive on some datasets (e.g., 8.80 ECE on ARC-C) but degrades sharply on others (25.01 on ARC-E), whereas IB-EDL remains consistently strong. On OOD detection (Table 3), IB-EDL achieves the highest AUROC using both max-probability and uncertainty-mass scores, e.g., 87.47/88.34 on OBQA→ARC-C for Llama2-7B, surpassing VID (86.47/81.24) and all non-EDL methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

IB-EDL sits at the intersection of Evidential Deep Learning (Sensoy et al., 2018) and the Deep Variational Information Bottleneck (Alemi et al., 2017), using the X-Z-Y Markov chain bounds from Wieczorek & Roth (2020). The paper identifies a previously overlooked theoretical issue: when IB is applied at an intermediate layer and p(y|z) is replaced by a learned q(y|z), the resulting objective may no longer be a valid lower bound on I(Z,Y). By choosing the pre-evidence as the hidden variable, IB-EDL resolves this issue and naturally induces an ℓ₂ penalty on evidence magnitudes. The authors further show that VB-based EDL methods (e.g., VID) are a special case of their framework, unifying several prior approaches under a single information-theoretic lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Overconfidence in fine-tuned LLMs is a critical barrier to deploying these models in safety-sensitive domains, and IB-EDL addresses it with less than 2% computational overhead over the base model—far cheaper than Deep Ensembles or MC-Dropout. The practical impact is substantial: ECE drops by several factors compared to MAP across six benchmarks and three 7B–8B architectures, and the method remains robust to 30% label noise. Open questions include whether the diagonal-covariance assumption on pre-evidences limits expressiveness, whether IB-EDL extends to generative tasks where uncertainty metrics are still immature, and how the β hyperparameter scales with model size or dataset difficulty.
