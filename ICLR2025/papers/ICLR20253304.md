## Problem

Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models for robot manipulation are fragile under inevitable external perturbations (lighting shifts, video noise, occlusions) encountered during real-world deployment, because they lack a mechanism to explicitly model and offset disturbances. While image augmentation provides limited robustness, it cannot generalize beyond a narrow task distribution. The paper identifies that the classical Internal Model Control (IMC) principle—which uses an internal model to simulate perturbations and reference inputs for closed-loop stability—has never been instantiated within a VLA framework for general visual-language manipulation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes only third-person RGB images from a static camera (256×256, 51-frame sequences) and a natural language instruction; proprioceptive observations and gripper-view images are deliberately excluded to increase difficulty. The behavior planner outputs future goal video frames (17 micro-frames per chunk, decoded from a 2D+3D VAE latent space), while the goal-guided policy outputs a 7-DoF action vector (6D end-effector position + binary gripper state). Training is decoupled: the planner requires only text-video pairs (no action labels), and the policy requires only video-action pairs (no language labels), reducing data dependency.

## Method

GEVRM instantiates the IMC principle in three coupled components. (1) A robot behavior planner built on a pre-trained Open-Sora DiT backbone, compressed via cascaded 2D VAE (8×8 spatial) and 3D VAE (4× temporal) with causal convolutions, trained with Rectified Flow and a random mask mechanism (75% weight on first-h-frames unmasking) to enforce physical-law understanding. (2) A state alignment module using twin ResNet-34 encoders for current and goal states, optimized via DINO-style prototypical contrastive learning (3000 prototypes, Sinkhorn-Knopp targets) to align latent representations and implicitly simulate robot responses to perturbations. (3) A goal-guided diffusion policy (DDPM, 20 steps) conditioned on the L2-normalized internal embeddings (z, ẑ) to decode multi-modal actions. The joint objective J = J_φ + λJ_ψ co-optimizes action prediction and state alignment, and at test time the planner re-samples goals every 20 control steps in a closed loop.

## Results

On the CALVIN zero-shot generalization benchmark (train A,B,C → test D), GEVRM achieves 0.92/0.70/0.54/0.41/0.26 success rates for 1–5 chained instructions, outperforming the strongest baseline SuSIE (0.87/0.69/0.49/0.38/0.26) and GR-1 (0.75/0.45/0.20/0.15/0.10). Under five external perturbations (image shift, rotation, color jitter, occlusion, noise), GEVRM maintains an average chain length of 1.62 versus 1.11 for SuSIE and 1.44 for GR-1. For goal generation quality, GEVRM achieves FID 35.70 and FVD 4.16 on Bridge data, substantially surpassing AVDC (FID 246.45, FVD 22.89) and SuSIE (FID 114.79).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEVRM outperforms all 11 baselines across both standard and perturbed CALVIN evaluations. Against the closest competitor SuSIE (Black et al.), GEVRM gains +0.05 on 1-instruction success (0.92 vs 0.87) and a substantially larger +0.51 average chain length under perturbations (1.62 vs 1.11), with the gap widening to +0.21 on 2-instruction perturbed success (0.47 vs 0.26). GR-1 (Wu et al.) is beaten by a wide margin on both goal generation (FID 94.47 vs 236.75 on CALVIN) and action execution (0.92 vs 0.75 for 1 instruction). UniPi (Du et al.) and HiP (Ajay et al.) are significantly outperformed, with GEVRM achieving 0.92 vs 0.56 and 0.08 respectively on 1-instruction success. Among language-conditioned baselines (HULC, MCIL, MdetrLC, AugLC, LCBC), GEVRM leads by 0.23–0.72 on 1-instruction success. RoboFlamingo (Li et al.) is the strongest perturbation baseline but still trails GEVRM by +0.07 on 1-instruction and +0.31 average chain length under perturbations. On goal generation quality, GEVRM dominates AVDC (Ko et al.) by a factor of ~7 in FID on Bridge data (35.70 vs 246.45), confirming superior video expressiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of video-generation-based robot planning (UniPi, SuSIE, ADVC, GR-1) and classical Internal Model Control (IMC) from process control theory. While prior video-generation VLA methods treat future state prediction as an open-loop planning step and rely on data augmentation for robustness, GEVRM explicitly closes the loop by using prototypical contrastive learning to align current and goal state representations, thereby simulating robot responses and implicitly estimating external perturbations. The video generation backbone is derived from Open-Sora's DiT with Rectified Flow training, and the action decoder adapts Diffusion Policy with goal-state conditioning, but the key architectural departure is the IMC-inspired state alignment module that has no direct analogue in prior VLA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: VLA models deployed in the wild face lighting changes, sensor noise, and occlusions that degrade performance, yet most prior work evaluates only in clean, controlled environments. The IMC-inspired closed-loop formulation offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc data augmentation for robustness, and the decoupled training (video planner needs only text-video pairs; policy needs only video-action pairs) reduces data requirements. However, real-world validation is limited to three simple pick-and-place tasks on a UR5 with moderate success rates (0.6–0.8), and the perturbation robustness gains over the strongest baseline (SuSIE) are consistent but modest (e.g., 1.62 vs. 1.11 average chain length). Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex multi-object manipulation, whether larger video generation models further improve goal expressiveness, and how the method handles perturbations not seen during training.
