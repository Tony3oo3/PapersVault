## Problem

High-dimensional asymptotic analyses of classification have largely relied on Gaussian data assumptions, with subsequent work establishing Gaussian universality principles that claim performance depends only on first and second moments. However, the conditions under which these universality principles hold for mixture data were never precisely specified. This paper identifies and characterizes the exact conditions under which Gaussian universality breaks down for classification of linear factor mixture models, showing that the data distribution can affect learning performance beyond class-conditional means and covariances.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. training samples consisting of p-dimensional feature vectors drawn from a linear factor mixture model (LFMM) with binary labels, a convex continuously differentiable loss function (e.g., logistic, square, or square hinge), and a ridge regularization parameter λ > 0. The output is a self-consistent system of q+3 deterministic constants (θ, η, γ, ω₁,…,ω_q) that fully characterize the asymptotic distribution of both in-sample and out-of-sample predicted scores, from which training and generalization errors are computed as probabilities involving a scalar random variable r whose distribution depends explicitly on the noise factor distributions e₁,…,e_q rather than just their moments.

## Method

The paper develops a leave-one-out analysis (in the spirit of El Karoui et al. 2013) that bypasses the conditional one-directional CLT required by prior Gaussian universality arguments. By introducing leave-one-out and leave-one-variable-out classifier variants, the authors derive a self-consistent system of q+3 equations whose solution determines the high-dimensional equivalent classifier β̃, whose distribution captures the non-Gaussian structure of informative factors through explicit dependence on the noise variables e₁,…,e_q. The key insight is that the mapping h_κ(r,y) = (prox_{κ,ℓ(·,y)}(r) − r)/κ enters the system in a way that retains distributional information about the informative factors, causing the breakdown of Gaussian universality. The authors then derive precise conditions for when universality is restored: in-distribution performance is universal iff the informative factor noises are Gaussian (Corollary 2), and classifier-level universality additionally holds when the loss is square (Corollary 3), since square loss makes h_κ linear in r and thus insensitive to higher-order moments. This reveals that square loss, while provably optimal under GMM, becomes suboptimal under LFMM because it cannot adapt to non-Gaussian informative factor distributions.

## Results

The paper provides a self-consistent system of q+3 equations (Theorem 1) that precisely characterizes the asymptotic training and generalization performance of ridge-regularized ERM under a linear factor mixture model (LFMM), extending prior GMM analyses. Corollary 2 establishes that Gaussian universality of in-distribution performance holds if and only if all informative factors are Gaussian; Corollary 3 shows that Gaussian universality of the classifier holds if either the informative factors are Gaussian or the square loss is used. Numerical experiments (Figures 1–3) confirm close agreement between the theoretical predictions and empirical performance on moderately sized datasets (n=600, p=200), and Figure 3 demonstrates that on an LFMM with Rademacher informative factors, the logistic loss yields strictly better classification accuracy than the square loss with optimally chosen regularization, while under the equivalent GMM the square loss is at least as good.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing ML methods but instead compares the LFMM framework against its equivalent GMM (matching class-conditional means and covariances) and compares different loss functions (square, logistic, square hinge) under both settings. Under the equivalent GMM, the square loss is optimal and the logistic loss fails to outperform it (left panel of Figure 3), consistent with prior results by Taheri et al. (2021b) and Mai & Liao (2019). Under the LFMM with non-Gaussian (Rademacher) informative factors, the logistic loss achieves higher classification accuracy than the square loss with optimally tuned λ (right panel of Figure 3), revealing the suboptimality of square loss beyond the Gaussian setting. The square loss exhibits Gaussian universality of the classifier (Corollary 3): classifiers trained on LFMM and its equivalent GMM produce asymptotically identical test errors, meaning the square-loss classifier cannot adapt to non-Gaussian informative factors. In contrast, the square hinge loss and logistic loss break this universality, producing measurably different predicted-score distributions on LFMM versus GMM (Figure 2), and thereby capturing the non-Gaussian structure that the square loss ignores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional asymptotic analysis of ridge-regularized ERM for classification from Gaussian mixture models (GMMs) to the more general linear factor mixture model (LFMM), where class-conditional data are generated by linear mappings of independent non-Gaussian factors. It builds directly on the conditional Gaussian equivalent principle (CGEP) of Dandi et al. (2024), which assumed a conditional one-directional CLT without specifying verifiable conditions, and on the leave-one-out analysis technique of El Karoui et al. (2013) for robust regression. The paper departs from the GMM analysis lineage (Mai & Liao 2019, Mignacco et al. 2020, Liao & Couillet 2019) by showing that the self-consistent equations governing asymptotic performance acquire additional terms (ω_k) that capture non-Gaussian variation in informative factors, breaking the Gaussian universality that held under GMM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first precise, verifiable conditions under which Gaussian universality holds for mixture classification, resolving a gap left by Dandi et al. (2024) whose CGEP relied on an unverifiable CLT assumption. A key practical implication is that the square loss—proven optimal for high-dimensional GMM classification—becomes suboptimal under LFMM with non-Gaussian informative factors, while the logistic loss can outperform it, opening a concrete direction for distribution-aware loss design. Open questions include whether the optimal loss depends on the sample ratio n/p (as in high-dimensional regression), whether an explicit optimal loss can be designed for arbitrary data distributions, and how these findings extend to multi-class settings and non-smooth losses.
