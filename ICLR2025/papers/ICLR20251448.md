## Problem

GPU memory capacity and bandwidth have not kept pace with computational power, making model size (in bits) the primary bottleneck for LLM inference. Post-training quantization (PTQ) is the standard remedy but degrades significantly below 4-bit precision, and the relative performance of models *pretrained* at low bitwidths versus their PTQ and full-precision counterparts at matched bit sizes remains unexplored. This paper asks whether ternary (1.58-bit) language models pretrained from scratch can match or exceed FloatLM and QuantLM performance at equivalent bit budgets, and how their scaling laws compare.

## Input / Output

The models consume raw text sequences (tokenized with the GPT-NeoX 20B tokenizer at 2048-token context length) and produce next-token predictions via standard autoregressive decoding. During inference, each linear layer stores only ternary states {−1, 0, +1} plus a single per-matrix scale scalar, yielding ~1.58 effective bits per parameter; the embedding table and LM head remain in FP16. This contrasts with FloatLM (16 bits/param) and QuantLM (3–8 bits/param with group-wise scales), making TriLM 5.9× smaller in bits than a same-parameter FloatLM at the 3.9B scale.

## Method

TriLM is a LLaMA-style transformer (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, multi-head attention, no biases) in which every linear-layer weight is ternarized on-the-fly during the forward pass: a per-matrix scale γ is computed as the mean absolute value of the FP16 latent weights, each weight is clamped to [−1, 1] after scaling and rounded to the nearest ternary state, and gradients flow back to the latent weights via a straight-through estimator. Two training-schedule interventions are critical: the peak learning rate is reduced at the halfway point of training, and L2 weight decay is removed at the two-thirds mark (since ternarization itself regularizes). The authors train the Spectra suite—54 models spanning 99 M to 3.9 B parameters and 1.58 to 16 bits, all on 300 B SlimPajama tokens—enabling controlled comparison of TriLM, FloatLM, and GPTQ-quantized QuantLM (3/4/6/8-bit) at matched parameter counts and bit sizes. The key empirical finding is that TriLMs exhibit a lower validation-loss offset in the power-law scaling fit (ε = 1.76 vs 1.67 for FloatLM, same exponent α = 0.26), scale more favorably when plotted against effective bit size, and at 3.9 B parameters match FloatLM 3.9B on commonsense, reasoning, and knowledge benchmarks despite being 5.9× smaller in bits.

## Results

At 3.9B parameters, TriLM matches FloatLM 3.9B across all commonsense, reasoning, and knowledge benchmarks despite being 5.9× smaller in effective bitsize (10.76 vs 63.83 ×10⁹ bits). TriLM 3.9B's validation perplexity matches FloatLM 1.1B (18.39 ×10⁹ bits), which is 1.7× larger in bits. The scaling law fit shows both TriLM and FloatLM share exponent α=0.26, with offset terms ε_TriLM=1.76 and ε_FloatLM=1.67, indicating the validation loss gap closes as parameter count increases. At 2.4B+ scales, TriLMs consistently outperform 4-bit QuantLMs of equivalent parameter count on all evaluated benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FloatLM (same parameter count): TriLM 3.9B achieves parity with FloatLM 3.9B on all commonsense/reasoning and knowledge benchmarks (ARC, BoolQ, HellaSWAG, WinoGrande, PIQA, LAMBADA, SciQ, TriviaQA, MMLU), while using only 10.76×10⁹ bits vs 63.83×10⁹ bits — fewer bits than FloatLM 830M (13.34×10⁹). Against 4-bit QuantLM (same parameter count): TriLMs consistently outperform QuantLM 4-bit at 2.4B and 3.9B scales across all benchmarks, confirming that post-training quantization is sub-optimal compared to direct ternary pretraining. Against BitNet b1.58: TriLM 1.1B outperforms both the authors' replication of BitNet b1.58 1.1B and the reported BitNet b1.58 1.3B on averaged commonsense and reasoning scores (Figure 10), attributed to architectural differences (Pre-Norm vs activation normalization, RMSNorm with scale parameter). Against Pythia: The Spectra suite includes Pythia scores in Tables 7, 8, and 10 for external reference, but the primary within-suite comparison (TriLM vs FloatLM vs QuantLM at matched parameter counts) is the paper's core evaluation axis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ternary-weight training paradigm introduced by Ternary Weight Networks (Li et al., 2016) and operationalized for LLMs by BitNet b1.58 (Ma et al., 2024), but departs from BitNet's architecture by adopting GPT-3-style Pre-Norm with RMSNorm-with-scale rather than BitNet's activation-level 8-bit quantization between linear layers. It positions itself against the dominant post-training quantization (PTQ) approach (GPTQ and variants) by arguing that pretraining directly in ternary precision avoids the representation mismatch that degrades QuantLMs below 4-bit. The Spectra suite draws methodological inspiration from Pythia, OLMo, and LLM360 for systematic multi-scale comparison, but is the first to span bit-widths (1.58 to 16 bits) alongside parameter counts in a single open suite.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 3.9B-parameter ternary model matches its full-precision counterpart across commonsense, reasoning, and knowledge benchmarks—while being 5.9× smaller in bits—challenges the field's default assumption that post-training quantization is the primary route to efficient LLM deployment. The Spectra suite (54 models, 99M–3.9B params, 1.58–16 bits) is a valuable open resource for studying low-bitwidth scaling. Key open questions remain: the persistent perplexity gap on web corpora (Dolma, RefinedWeb, C4), the fact that TriLM 3.9B exhibits the same toxicity/stereotyping as FloatLM 3.9B (far worse than a bit-equivalent FloatLM 830M), and whether the validation-loss convergence predicted by the scaling law holds beyond 4B parameters or across different architectures and data regimes.
