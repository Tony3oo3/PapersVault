## Problem

Existing urban scene generation methods primarily produce static, single-frame 3D occupancy, failing to capture the inherently dynamic nature of real-world driving environments with moving vehicles, pedestrians, and long temporal sequences. Generating high-quality 4D occupancy scenes at large spatial scales (80×80×6.4 m³) and long temporal horizons (up to 128 frames) remains an open and challenging problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251455/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes Gaussian noise for unconditional generation, or conditions such as a prior HexPlane (for autoregressive extension), ego-vehicle trajectory coordinates (T×2), discrete text commands (STATIC/FORWARD/TURN LEFT/TURN RIGHT), a bird's-eye binary layout tensor (T×H×W), or a 2D spatial mask for inpainting. It outputs a sequence of 3D semantic occupancy volumes at 200×200×16 (Occ3D-Waymo/nuScenes) or 128×128×8 (CarlaSC) resolution, with up to 11 semantic classes and up to 128 frames per generated scene.

## Method

DynamicCity is a two-stage framework: (1) a VAE that encodes 4D occupancy into a compact HexPlane representation using a novel Projection Module—transformer-based networks that compress the 4D feature volume into six 2D feature planes—followed by an Expansion & Squeeze Strategy (ESS) that decodes by duplicating each plane to full 4D shape and fusing them via Hadamard product, yielding up to 7.05% mIoU gain, 2.06× training speedup, and 70.84% memory reduction over point-wise querying; (2) a DiT-based diffusion model that generates HexPlane latents, where a Padded Rollout Operation (PRO) reorganizes all six planes into a single square 2D feature map (with zero-padding in uncovered corners) so that patchified tokens naturally encode spatial and temporal adjacency, enabling the transformer to model 4D relationships without explicit 4D attention. Conditional generation is achieved via adaLN-Zero injection and cross-attention branches, supporting trajectory-, command-, layout-, and mask-driven generation as well as autoregressive long-sequence extension.

## Results

On 4D scene reconstruction, DynamicCity achieves mIoU of 74.22% on CarlaSC (16 frames), 68.18% on Occ3D-Waymo (16 frames), and 56.93% on Occ3D-nuScenes (16 frames), outperforming OccSora by 40.8%, 31.8%, and 43.2% respectively. For 4D scene generation, DynamicCity reduces FID from 28.55 to 10.96 (2D) and from 1559 to 354.2 (3D) on CarlaSC, and from 3140 to 1806 (3D) on Occ3D-Waymo. The VAE also achieves up to 2.06× training speedup and 70.84% memory reduction over point-wise querying via the Expansion & Squeeze Strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynamicCity is compared against three baselines. Against OccSora (Wang et al., 2024), the primary 4D occupancy generation baseline, DynamicCity wins decisively on all metrics: mIoU improvements of 30–43% across all datasets and frame counts, FID reductions of 60–78% in 3D space, and higher Precision/Recall in both 2D and 3D evaluations. Against SemCity (Lee et al., 2024), a triplane diffusion method for static 3D scene generation, DynamicCity achieves substantially lower FID (12.94 vs 35.40 in 2D; 427.5 vs 1113 in 3D) and higher Precision (0.307 vs 0.213 in 2D; 0.460 vs 0.253 in 3D), while also generating dynamic scenes rather than single frames. Against OccWorld (Zheng et al., 2024) on the occupancy forecasting task (Occ3D-nuScenes, 2-second context), DynamicCity achieves 80.52 mIoU at T=0 versus 66.38 for OccWorld, and slightly better scores at T=1 and T=2 (26.18 vs 25.78 and 16.94 vs 15.14 mIoU respectively). DynamicCity wins all head-to-head comparisons across every reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynamicCity builds on two key prior contributions: HexPlane as a compact 4D spacetime representation (Cao & Johnson, 2023; Fridovich-Keil et al., 2023) and DiT as a scalable diffusion backbone (Peebles & Xie, 2023). It departs from prior 4D occupancy generation work (OccSora, OccWorld) that relied on voxel-based or point-query decoding by introducing a transformer-based Projection Module for HexPlane encoding and an Expansion & Squeeze Strategy for parallel decoding, replacing naive averaging and per-point querying. The Padded Rollout Operation is a novel reorganization of HexPlane planes into a 2D square token layout specifically designed to make the structured 4D representation compatible with DiT's patch-based attention, filling a gap between structured 4D representations and transformer-based diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DynamicCity is among the first frameworks to generate large-scale (80×80×6.4 m³), multi-frame (up to 128 frames) dynamic 4D occupancy with semantics, supporting diverse conditional generation (trajectory, command, layout, inpainting) that prior 4D methods lacked. The 30–40% mIoU reconstruction gains over OccSora and the 70% memory reduction from ESS make dynamic scene generation substantially more practical. Open questions remain around generalization to unseen object categories, quality degradation in very long autoregressive sequences, and handling highly congested traffic with rapid multi-object interactions—limitations the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
