## Problem

Activation editing (steering vectors) has emerged as an efficient, training-free method to align LLM behavior at inference time, but its privacy implications remain entirely unexplored. When steering vectors are computed from private datasets (e.g., patient histories or financial records), the resulting aligned LLM can leak information about individual samples in that dataset. This paper is the first to study and address the privacy risk of activation editing, asking whether LLM alignment via steering can be made differentially private without significant utility loss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a private dataset of paired demonstrations, each consisting of a prompt with a positive (desirable) and negative (undesirable) completion, and produces differentially private steering vectors for a selected subset of middle transformer layers (e.g., layers 11–15 of a 7B model). During inference, these vectors are added to the LLM's hidden activations at every token position in the selected layers, producing privately steered text. The privacy guarantee is (|S|ε, |S|δ)-DP where |S| is the number of steered layers, and the guarantee holds for an unbounded number of subsequent queries at zero additional privacy cost.

## Method

PSA computes steering vectors by extracting last-token activations for each positive/negative demonstration pair at selected layers, forming difference vectors, then clipping each vector to an L₂ ball of radius C_l and scaling by C_l to bound the L₂ sensitivity. Calibrated Gaussian noise with σ = 2√(2ln(1.25/δ))/(nε) is added to the averaged, clipped difference vectors, yielding a (ε,δ)-DP steering vector per layer via the Gaussian mechanism. The overall (|S|ε, |S|δ)-DP guarantee follows from the basic composition theorem across layers and the post-processing theorem, which ensures that any number of subsequent inference-time queries incurs no additional privacy cost. The clipping-and-scaling step is critical: it controls the sensitivity (and thus the required noise magnitude) while avoiding the extra privacy leakage that would arise from using the empirical maximum norm. Empirically, PSA achieves alignment accuracy and text-generation quality comparable to non-private mean steering across seven behavioral benchmarks and four LLM families (0.5B–7B), with a novel canary-based Membership Inference Attack confirming that empirical ε values are tighter than the theoretical bounds.

## Results

On GPT-4-evaluated text generation (Llama-2-Chat 7B), PSA achieves scores within ~0.5 of non-private Mean Steering across all seven alignment behaviors (e.g., Hallucination: 3.94 vs. 4.04; Refusal: 7.88 vs. 7.98), while consistently outperforming zero-shot. PSA actually surpasses Mean Steering on Myopic Reward (3.56 vs. 2.22) and Corrigibility (5.42 vs. 4.94). MMLU general-capability scores remain within 1–2 points of zero-shot (e.g., 63.0 vs. 63.6 on Sycophancy), confirming minimal utility degradation. Empirical privacy (ε_emp = 0.6 for Llama-2-7B, 1.6 for Qwen-2.5-7B) is well below the theoretical bound (ε_th = 2.0), indicating stronger real-world protection than the worst-case guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSA is compared against two non-private activation editing baselines: Mean Steering (Rimsky et al., 2024) and PCA Steering / In-Context Vectors (Liu et al., 2024). Against Mean Steering, PSA achieves nearly identical alignment accuracy on most behaviors (within 0.1–0.2 on GPT-4 scores) and actually outperforms it on Myopic Reward (3.56 vs. 2.22) and Corrigibility (5.42 vs. 4.94), likely because the DP noise occasionally aligns the steering direction more favorably. Against PCA Steering, PSA is consistently superior across all seven datasets (e.g., Hallucination 3.94 vs. 3.88; Corrigibility 5.42 vs. 4.12), confirming that the mean-difference formulation is a stronger base to privatize. Both baselines lack any formal privacy guarantee (ε_th = ∞), whereas PSA provides (ε, δ)-DP with ε_total ≈ 2.0 for 5 layers. On the MIA audit, non-private Mean Steering is trivially attacked (ε_emp = 4.0–6.0), while PSA reduces empirical ε to 0.6 (Llama) and 1.6 (Qwen), demonstrating a concrete privacy improvement of 4–10×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation editing paradigm (Rimsky et al., 2024; Turner et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024) by applying the Gaussian mechanism from differential privacy (Dwork et al., 2006) to the steering vector computation, adding calibrated noise after a clipping-and-normalization step. It departs from prior DP-for-LLMs work, which has focused on training-time DP-SGD or inference-time in-context learning, by targeting the less-explored inference-time activation editing setting. The paper also introduces the first Membership Inference Attack specifically designed for steering vectors, building on canary-based MIA techniques from fine-tuning and ICL literature but adapting them to the unique challenge that activation editing does not lower loss on training samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal DP guarantee for LLM alignment via activation editing, which is practically important for organizations (healthcare, finance) that must align models on sensitive private data without leaking individual records. The finding that a simple clip-and-noise mechanism preserves alignment utility across seven behaviors and four model families (0.5B–7B) makes the approach immediately deployable. Open questions include whether the guarantees extend to more complex steering methods (e.g., spectral editing, multi-step optimization), how the privacy-utility tradeoff behaves under adaptive or repeated queries beyond the single-vector setting, and whether the MIA framework generalizes to multi-sample or multi-turn inference scenarios.
