## Problem

Existing adversarial perturbation methods for protecting images from diffusion model manipulation focus on whole-image tasks (image-to-image, text-to-image) and apply a single perturbation across the entire image. In inpainting tasks, the adversary's mask removes perturbations from the target region, leaving it vulnerable, and prior latent-space objectives fail to disrupt the denoiser's generation process within the masked area. This paper identifies inpainting as an under-addressed threat and shows that state-of-the-art defenses (PhotoGuard, AdvDM, Mist, CAAT, SDST) still allow adversaries to successfully inpaint protected images.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and produces an imperceptible adversarial perturbation bounded by η=0.06 in L∞ norm, optimized via PGD over 250 iterations at the final diffusion timestep T. Grounded SAM identifies target objects to generate an enlarged bounding box (ρ=1.2) that partitions the image into two regions for separate perturbation optimization. The protected image is then evaluated against foreground and background inpainting using segmentation masks, bounding-box masks, and their inverses, with 50 randomly generated prompts from ChatGPT.

## Method

ADVPAINT introduces a novel adversarial objective that directly targets the attention mechanisms of the inpainting U-Net denoiser rather than operating in latent space. For cross-attention blocks, it maximizes the L2 difference in query vectors between the clean and adversarial images across all layers, breaking the alignment between image features and prompt tokens. For self-attention blocks, it simultaneously maximizes differences in all three components (query, key, value) to disrupt the model's internal semantic and spatial understanding. A two-stage perturbation strategy then divides the image using an enlarged bounding box around the target object, optimizing distinct perturbations for the interior and exterior regions, which provides robustness across diverse mask shapes and sizes that may exceed the optimization boundary.

## Results

ADVPAINT achieves FID scores of 347.88 (foreground, m^seg) and 303.90 (background, m^seg-inverse), exceeding the best baseline (Mist) by over 100 points in FID. Precision drops to 0.0570 and 0.0936 respectively, compared to 0.4590 and 0.1602 for Mist, indicating far fewer generated images fall within the real-image distribution. LPIPS also improves to 0.6731 and 0.6105, confirming greater perceptual distortion of inpainted outputs. The two-stage perturbation strategy (Table 2) further boosts FID from 345.76 to 347.88 (foreground, m^seg) and from 266.00 to 303.90 (background, m^seg-inverse) over single-stage optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADVPAINT outperforms all five baselines across every metric and mask configuration. Against Mist (the strongest prior method), ADVPAINT gains +112 FID on foreground m^seg (347.88 vs. 235.81) and +148 FID on background m^seg-inverse (303.90 vs. 155.57), while reducing Precision from 0.4590 to 0.0570 and from 0.1602 to 0.0936 respectively. PhotoGuard and CAAT, which rely on latent-space alignment or cross-attention fine-tuning, achieve FID scores in the 210–235 range for foreground tasks, roughly 110–135 points below ADVPAINT. AdvDM and SDST, which maximize noise-prediction loss or combine SDS with latent alignment, show the weakest protection (FID 212–232), confirming that latent-space objectives lose effectiveness when perturbations are masked out. The paper also shows that even when prior objectives (Eq. 2, 3, 4) are re-optimized with the SD inpainter, they yield FID scores of only 182–225, far below ADVPAINT's attention-disruption approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-problem within the broader line of adversarial image protection for diffusion models: specifically, defending against mask-based inpainting rather than whole-image manipulation. Prior methods (PhotoGuard, AdvDM, Mist, CAAT, SDST) optimize latent-space objectives that align or misalign encoded representations, but the paper argues these are fundamentally mismatched to inpainting because the mask strips perturbations from the target region. ADVPAINT departs by directly targeting the U-Net denoiser's cross- and self-attention blocks, disrupting the query-key-value interactions that govern prompt-image alignment and intra-image semantics. The two-stage perturbation strategy (separate perturbations inside and outside an enlarged bounding box) is a novel practical addition not present in prior single-perturbation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of inpainting-specific image protection, filling a gap that prior adversarial perturbation work left open. The >100-point FID gap over prior methods, while partly attributable to baselines not being designed for inpainting, highlights a real vulnerability in current protection schemes. Limitations include evaluation on a single inpainting model (SD inpainter), a modest 100-image test set, and the acknowledged computational overhead for multi-object images. Open questions include whether attention-disruption perturbations transfer to newer architectures (e.g., diffusion transformers like SD3 or PixArt), whether adaptive adversaries can reverse-engineer the attention targets, and whether the protection survives common image transformations (compression, resizing) that the paper does not thoroughly test.
