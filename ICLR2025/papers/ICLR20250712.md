## Problem

While the expressiveness of transformer encoders has been extensively studied, the fundamental question of whether sound and complete automatic formal reasoning (verification or interpretation) is possible for them remained open. This paper establishes that the satisfiability problem (TRSAT) for transformer encoders is undecidable for classes commonly considered in the expressiveness literature, and identifies the precise restrictions under which it becomes decidable, along with tight complexity bounds.

## Input / Output

The input is a transformer encoder T specified by its positional embedding, per-layer attention heads (scoring and pooling functions), combination functions, and output function, together with an alphabet Σ. In the bounded variant, an additional length bound n is provided, encoded either in binary or unary, which critically affects the complexity class (NEXPTIME vs. NP). The output is a binary decision: whether there exists some word w over Σ (with |w| ≤ n in the bounded case) such that T(w) = 1, i.e., T accepts w.

## Method

The paper proves undecidability of TRSAT for a minimally expressive class T_udec (hardmax normalisation, FNN-based scoring, positional embeddings) via a reduction from the unbounded octant tiling-word problem, showing that two encoder layers suffice to decode tiling coordinates and three attention heads verify horizontal/vertical matching constraints. Undecidability persists under log-precision arithmetic. For decidability, the paper introduces a bounded variant BTRSAT (decidable for all computable TE, NP- or NEXPTIME-complete depending on encoding of n) and, more substantially, shows that restricting TE to fixed-width arithmetic with periodic positional embeddings (T_o^FIX) yields NEXPTIME decidability via a 'small-word property': if T accepts any word, it accepts one of length at most 2^{poly(|T|)}, enabling a nondeterministic guess-and-certify procedure. A matching NEXPTIME-hardness result is established for T^FIX (arbitrary embeddings, fixed-width arithmetic) by reducing from the binary-encoded bounded octant tiling problem, where the fixed-width constraint forces periodicity in the embedding and enables the TE to enforce exact word-length bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes that the satisfiability problem TRSAT for transformer encoders is undecidable for classes using hardmax normalization with expressive (FNN-based) scoring (Theorem 1), and remains undecidable even under log-precision arithmetic (Theorem 2). Bounding the input word length to n makes the problem decidable: BTRSAT is NP-complete when n is unary-encoded and NEXPTIME-complete when n is binary-encoded (Theorem 3). For quantized TE with periodic positional embeddings over fixed-width arithmetic, TRSAT is in NEXPTIME (Theorem 4), and this bound is tight — NEXPTIME-hard — when arbitrary embeddings are permitted (Theorem 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the 'comparisons' are between progressively restricted TE classes and their resulting complexity. The undecidability results (Theorems 1–2) show that the combination of hardmax normalization and expressive scoring is sufficient to make formal reasoning impossible, even under log-precision constraints. The bounded variant (Theorem 3) demonstrates that simply capping input length restores decidability but at NEXPTIME cost for binary-encoded bounds. The fixed-width arithmetic results (Theorems 4–5) show that quantization — a practical necessity for deploying large TE — provides an alternative path to decidability, with the periodic-embedding restriction yielding an NEXPTIME upper bound and the relaxation to arbitrary embeddings matching it with a NEXPTIME-hardness lower bound. The key trade-off identified is between embedding expressiveness (periodic vs. arbitrary) and the feasibility of constructing polynomial-size reductions, since periodic embeddings of period p require size linear in p, causing exponential blow-up when p must encode large bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of transformer expressiveness theory and formal verification. It builds on the expressiveness results of Pérez et al. (2021) showing encoder-decoder transformers are Turing-complete, and on circuit-complexity analyses of encoder-only transformers (Hahn 2020; Hao et al. 2022), but departs from both by establishing fundamental computability and complexity bounds for a specific decision problem (satisfiability) rather than characterizing language classes. The reduction from octant tiling problems (Berger 1966; van Emde Boas 1997) is repurposed from classical undecidability theory to the transformer setting, and the fixed-width arithmetic analysis connects to practical quantization literature (Bondarenko et al. 2021) in a way that prior expressiveness work had not explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The undecidability result for encoder-only transformers is a fundamental negative: it means no fully automatic, sound, and complete method exists for verifying safety properties or generating formal explanations for a broad class of practically relevant architectures. The positive results (decidability under input-length bounds or fixed-width arithmetic, with NEXPTIME bounds) provide a concrete framework for where formal reasoning is possible but computationally intractable. Key open questions include whether the undecidability persists when replacing hardmax with the more commonly used softmax, how the interplay between embedding expressiveness and attention mechanism affects the boundary between decidable and undecidable cases, and whether tighter complexity bounds can be obtained for specific fixed-width arithmetics used in practice.
