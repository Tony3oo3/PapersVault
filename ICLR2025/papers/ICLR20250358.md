## Problem

The Mechanistic Neural Network (MNN) learns explicit ODE representations from noisy time series, enabling both forecasting and scientific analysis like parameter identification. However, its dense solver requires cubic time and quadratic space complexity in sequence length, and its sparse solver suffers from slow convergence and poor GPU utilization, making MNN impractical for long temporal sequences such as multi-year climate recordings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250358/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multidimensional discretized time sequence x_{1:T} and produces a discretized ODE solution trajectory y_{1:T} of the same length. A neural encoder maps the input to a semi-symbolic ODE representation (coefficients c, forcing d, initial conditions u, and step sizes s), which the solver then integrates. The sequence length T is the critical scaling dimension: S-MNN handles T up to 1,727 weeks (SST data) where the original MNN dense solver fails beyond T=104 and the sparse solver beyond T=416 due to memory limits.

## Method

S-MNN reformulates the MNN's linear system by (1) removing slack variables from smoothness constraints, (2) replacing central-difference constraints with forward and backward Taylor expansions extended to the highest derivative order, and (3) converting the quadratic program to a weighted least-squares regression. These changes cause the normal-equations matrix M = A^T W A to exhibit a block-banded structure where each time step couples only to its immediate neighbors. The solver exploits this via a blocked Cholesky/LDL decomposition (Algorithm 3) followed by forward and backward substitution (Algorithm 4), reducing time complexity from O(T^3 V^3 R^3) to O(T V^3 R^3) and space from O(T^2 V^2 R^2) to O(T V^2 R^2). The backward pass uses analytic gradient formulas (∂ℓ/∂β = M^{-1} ∂ℓ/∂y and ∂ℓ/∂M = -∂ℓ/∂β · y^T) to avoid redundant solves, and the entire pipeline is optimized for GPU parallelism across the batch and variable dimensions.

## Results

On the Lorenz discovery task (default: batch 512, sequence length 50), S-MNN achieves a 4.9× speedup over MNN Dense (7.4 ms vs. 36.4 ms per step) and a 50% GPU memory reduction (1.38 vs. 2.77 GiB). On the KdV PDE benchmark, S-MNN attains a rollout-averaged NMSE of 0.00005 (20 s) and 0.00037 (40 s), slightly outperforming MNN Dense (0.00006 / 0.00032) while reducing training time from 38.0 h to 10.1 h. For long-sequence SST forecasting, S-MNN maintains low relative MSE at sequence lengths up to 1,727 weeks where MNN Dense runs out of memory beyond 104 weeks and MNN Sparse beyond 416 weeks, with runtime and memory scaling linearly in sequence length as predicted by theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MNN Dense, S-MNN achieves a 4.9× speedup and 50% memory reduction at default settings while matching or slightly improving accuracy (KdV NMSE 0.00005 vs. 0.00006; Lorenz coefficients within 0.005 of ground truth). Against MNN Sparse, S-MNN is consistently faster (5–22×) and more accurate, as the sparse solver frequently fails to converge (no convergence on KdV; loss stagnation at 200 CG iterations for long sequences). On the KdV benchmark, S-MNN substantially outperforms ResNet (NMSE 0.0223) and FNO (0.0276) from Brandstetter et al. (2022), confirming the mechanistic bottleneck's advantage for PDE prediction. For equation discovery, S-MNN matches SINDy (Kaheman et al., 2020) on Lorenz coefficient recovery (e.g., a₃ = 27.9915 vs. SINDy's 27.998 vs. ground truth 28). In standalone ODE validation, S-MNN achieves relative MSE below 10⁻⁶, comparable to RK45 and LSODA, though with higher CPU runtime (~22 ms vs. ~1–7 ms) due to its GPU-oriented design. For SST forecasting, S-MNN maintains low relative MSE at sequence lengths where both MNN solvers exceed GPU memory limits, and outperforms Ada-GVAE (Locatello et al., 2020) on longer horizons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct algorithmic reformulation of the Mechanistic Neural Network (MNN, Pervez et al., 2024), which introduced a unified framework for ODE-based prediction and equation discovery in scientific ML. The key departure is eliminating slack variables and central difference constraints from the original MNN's quadratic programming formulation, replacing them with forward/backward Taylor smoothness constraints solved via least squares regression. This reformulation exposes a banded matrix structure in the normal equations that the original MNN's unstructured sparse matrices could not exploit, enabling a blocked Cholesky/LDL solver with linear complexity. In the broader scientific ML landscape, S-MNN occupies the same niche as MNN—bridging Neural ODEs (prediction), SINDy (discovery), and Neural Operators (PDEs)—while addressing the scalability bottleneck that limited MNN to short sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from O(T³) time and O(T²) space to O(T) for both is the critical enabler for long-horizon scientific applications: the paper demonstrates 4-year sea surface temperature forecasting on 1,727-week sequences that the original MNN simply cannot handle (OOM beyond ~104 weeks for dense, ~416 for sparse). As a drop-in replacement, S-MNN immediately benefits all existing MNN applications without architectural changes. The main open limitation is the sequential dependency in the Cholesky decomposition and substitution steps along the time axis, which underutilizes GPU parallelism for small batch/block sizes; the authors identify developing fully parallel linear-complexity algorithms as future work. Whether the banded-structure trick generalizes to other mechanistic or physics-informed solvers with similar over-determined linear systems remains an open question.
