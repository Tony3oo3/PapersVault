## Problem

Existing video guardrails are either simplistic classifiers trained on a fixed set of unsafe categories with no explanatory output, or MLLM-based approaches that prompt with lengthy safety guidelines autoregressively, suffering from high latency, policy positional bias, vague explanations, and poor adaptability to new policies or taxonomies. No prior work simultaneously addresses all four of these limitations in a single efficient video guardrail model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252761/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SAFEWATCH takes a video clip, a set of n natural-language safety policies, and a guardrail query as input. The video is first segmented into safety-aware events by a lightweight TransnetV2-based network, and one representative frame per event is sampled and encoded into patch tokens via a pre-trained video encoder and MLP projector. The output is a structured response containing (i) a video description highlighting potential unsafe elements, (ii) a set of n binary guardrail flags (one per policy), and (iii) a chain-of-thought explanation justifying each flagged violation with content-specific rationale.

## Method

SAFEWATCH builds on InternVL2-8B and introduces two plug-and-play modules. Parallel Equivalent Policy Encoding (PEPE) decomposes lengthy safety guidelines into independent policy chunks separated by anchor tokens, masks cross-policy attention so each chunk attends only to its own tokens plus the query and video, and applies equivalent RoPE position embeddings to all chunks so that no policy receives preferential attention based on its input position. Policy-Aware Adaptive Pruning (PAP) computes a cross-attention relevance score between each policy chunk and every video token, then retains only the top-k most relevant tokens per policy, adaptively discarding noisy or irrelevant visual information to reduce inference cost by up to 90% with less than 1% accuracy loss. Training proceeds in three stages: multi-task guardrail fine-tuning (with PEPE only, on unsafe and normal video data), adaptive-pruning training (enabling PAP to stabilize behavior under dynamic token selection), and DPO preference post-tuning to produce concise, specific explanations and reduce false positives. The model is trained on SAFEWATCH-BENCH, a 2M-video benchmark across six safety categories, annotated via a multi-agent propose-discuss consensus pipeline using multiple MLLMs and a GPT-4o judge.

## Results

On SAFEWATCH-BENCH-Real, SAFEWATCH-8B achieves 72.6% average accuracy and 86.7 F1 across six safety categories, outperforming the strongest baseline GPT-4o (43.4% ACC, 76.5 F1) by 29.2%. On SAFEWATCH-BENCH-GenAI, SAFEWATCH reaches 72.0% average accuracy vs. GPT-4o's 44.8% (27.2% improvement). SAFEWATCH also achieves 98.8 AUPRC, 7.17/10 GPT-4o-rated explanation quality, and 3.9s per-video inference time, while reducing inference cost by ~10% over the InternVL2-8B backbone. Across five existing benchmarks (LSPD, XD-Violence, UCF-Crime, FakeSV, FVC), SAFEWATCH delivers an average 13.6% accuracy improvement over prior SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFEWATCH-8B decisively outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest closed-source model GPT-4o, SAFEWATCH gains +29.2 ACC (72.6 vs 43.4), +10.2 F1 (86.7 vs 76.5), and +0.65 GPT-4o explanation score (7.17 vs 6.52), while also being 38% faster (3.9s vs 6.3s per video). Against Gemini-1.5-pro, the gap is even wider at +32.1 ACC. Among open-source guardrail models, SAFEWATCH surpasses LlavaGuard-34B by +52.5 ACC and is 6.1× faster (3.9s vs 23.9s), outperforms Holmes-VAD by +54.5 ACC, and beats LlamaGuard3V-11B by +48.0 ACC. Compared to its own backbone InternVL2-8B (29.1 ACC), the PEPE+PAP modules and multi-stage training yield a +43.5 ACC gain, while the larger InternVL2-26B (37.8 ACC) is still 34.8 points behind SAFEWATCH-8B, confirming that architecture and training recipe matter more than raw parameter scale. On the Azure Mod API (35.0 ACC), SAFEWATCH leads by +37.6 ACC while also providing detailed explanations that the API does not offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based guardrail paradigm (LlamaGuard, NeMo) from text and image domains to video, departing from prior video safety work that relied on fixed-category classifiers (Microsoft, Amazon APIs) or frame-by-frame image guardrails (LlavaGuard, Holmes-VAD). Architecturally, it builds on InternVL2-8B as a backbone and adapts two known ideas—sparse autoencoder-inspired independent representations (motivating PEPE's parallel policy encoding) and cross-attention-based token pruning (PUMER, Token Merging)—into a unified video guardrail pipeline. The TransnetV2-based safety-aware event segmentation repurposes shot boundary detection for safety-specific temporal sampling, and the multi-agent propose-discuss annotation pipeline draws on LLM-as-a-judge consensus methods for dataset curation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As generative video models (Sora, CogVideoX, Movie Gen) become more capable, the need for scalable, policy-adaptive video moderation is urgent, and SAFEWATCH-BENCH (2M videos, 6 categories, 30+ tasks) fills a critical gap left by small, single-category benchmarks. The finding that even 26B MLLMs exhibit strong policy positional bias (PCC ≈ −0.82) that a simple equivalent-RoPE fix resolves is practically useful for anyone deploying multi-policy guardrails. Limitations include reliance on a single 8B backbone, the GenAI subset's quality being constrained by current generative models, and the evaluation set being withheld (only eval split released), which limits independent reproduction. Open questions include whether PEPE/PAP generalize to longer videos, audio-visual content, or substantially larger model scales, and how the system performs under adversarial or obfuscated unsafe content.
