## Problem

Training rectified flow and diffusion models is slow because random noise-sample pairings produce messy, intersecting paths in the high-dimensional noise-sample space, creating conflicting gradient signals at overlapping points. While well-trained models implicitly discover well-organized 'preferable' paths with greater inter-path separation, no prior method explicitly leverages this structural difference to accelerate training without sacrificing generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251013/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

DANSM operates on batches of n Gaussian noise vectors and n data samples (RGB images at 32×32, 64×64, or 256×256, or latent variables at 4×32×32 or 4×64×64 from Stable Diffusion's autoencoder). It produces a one-to-one noise-sample matching that replaces the standard random pairing during training; the matched pairs are then fed into the standard RFM or DM training loop. The match-size n is the sole hyperparameter, and all noises are guaranteed to be used exactly once, ensuring full coverage of the noise space.

## Method

DANSM (Distance-Aware Noise-Sample Matching) maximizes the inter-path distance among n noise-sample paths by exploiting the straight-line geometry of rectified flow. The authors first derive a closed-form expression for the minimal inter-path distance between two linear interpolation paths as a function of timestep t, then prove a negative correlation between inter-path distance and path length (shorter paths yield greater separation). This allows replacing the expensive inter-path distance maximization with a simpler path-length minimization surrogate. A greedy algorithm (Algorithm 1) assigns each sample to its nearest available noise in O(n²) time, which is GPU-parallelizable and avoids the O(n³) Hungarian algorithm used by Immiscible Diffusion. The method is model-agnostic and generalizes to standard diffusion models without modification, achieving 30–40% training time reduction on CIFAR-10, ImageNet64, LSUN Bedroom, and FFHQ.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (10-step sampling), DANSM with match-size 10,000 reduces FID from 16.5 (baseline) to 9.7 at epoch 500, and on ImageNet64 (5-step sampling) from 57.1 to 52.2 at epoch 200. On the LSUN Bedroom latent space, DANSM with match-size 10K cuts training time from 25.5 to 15.4 hours (39.6% savings) to reach FID 15.3. When generalized to diffusion models on CIFAR-10 (3-step sampling), DANSM with match-size 1,000 reduces FID from 93 to 58 at epoch 1,000, and on Bedroom latent space achieves a 37.2% training time reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard RFM baseline (Liu et al., 2023a), DANSM consistently achieves lower FID at every evaluated epoch across all three image-space datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet64, Bedroom) and the Bedroom latent space, with the largest gap on CIFAR-10 where FID drops from 16.5 to 9.7 at epoch 500. In terms of training efficiency, DANSM with match-size 10K reaches FID 15.3 in 15.4 hours versus 25.5 hours for the baseline, a 39.6% time saving. Compared to Immiscible Diffusion (Li et al., 2024), which uses the Hungarian algorithm (O(n³)) for noise-sample assignment, DANSM's greedy O(n²) approach achieves comparable FID scores but with substantially less computational overhead, especially at larger match-sizes (e.g., 10,000), where the CPU-bound Hungarian solver becomes a bottleneck. Against the standard diffusion model baseline (Song et al., 2021a), DANSM generalizes without modification, reducing CIFAR-10 FID from 93 to 58 (3-step sampling) and cutting Bedroom latent training time by 37.2% to reach FID 27.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the straight-line path property of rectified flow models (Liu et al., 2023a) to derive a closed-form inter-path distance formula, and connects to the 'consistent model reproducibility' phenomenon identified by Zhang et al. (2024) and Song et al. (2021b), which shows that well-trained models converge to similar noise-to-sample mappings. It is closely related to Immiscible Diffusion (Li et al., 2024), which also assigns noises to samples to shorten path lengths, but DANSM provides the missing theoretical justification (a proof of negative correlation between inter-path distance and path length) and replaces the O(n³) Hungarian algorithm with an O(n²) greedy approach suitable for GPU parallelization. The paper thus positions itself as the rigorous, computationally efficient counterpart to prior heuristic noise-assignment methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 30–40% reduction in training time without quality loss is practically significant for large-scale generative model training, and the method's simplicity (a greedy nearest-neighbor matching step) makes it easy to integrate into existing RFM and DM training pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet64, LSUN Bedroom 256×256, FFHQ 256×256) and does not address conditional generation, text-to-image, or very large-scale training where the match-size overhead and its interaction with batch size may behave differently. Open questions include whether the speedup persists at the scale of billion-parameter models, how the method interacts with classifier-free guidance or other conditioning mechanisms, and whether the inter-path distance framework can inform sampling strategies beyond training.
