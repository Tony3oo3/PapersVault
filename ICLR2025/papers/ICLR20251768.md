## Problem

Existing concept erasing methods for text-to-image diffusion models target individual high-risk visual concepts (e.g., nudity, violence) but overlook that inappropriate themes can emerge from combinations of individually harmless concepts (e.g., {Kids, Drink, Wine} → 'underage drinking'). This introduces two unsolved challenges: (1) theme consistency analysis, where multiple distinct concept combinations express the same harmful theme and no fixed list of high-risk combinations exists, and (2) concept combination disentanglement, where erasing the combination without degrading the generative quality of its constituent concepts is difficult due to semantic entanglement in the model's features.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single textual concept combination (e.g., 'kids drink wine') as a seed prompt and a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (v1.5 or SD XL) as the base. The seed is consumed by a GPT-4-based multi-agent pipeline (via AutoGen) to iteratively expand a concept logic graph over K iterations, and the resulting graph guides fine-tuning of only the cross-attention layer parameters. The output is a modified diffusion model that suppresses generation of all theme-consistent concept combinations while retaining the ability to generate each individual concept in isolation.

## Method

CoGFD (Concept Graph-based high-level Feature Decoupling) combines two modules. First, an LLM-based concept logic graph generation strategy uses two GPT-4 agents (a Generator and a Reviewer) in an iterative loop to build a graph with three logical relations—Conjunction (decomposing a combination into its constituent concepts), Equivalence, and Inclusion (linking semantically similar combinations and concepts)—thereby identifying all theme-consistent concept combinations from a single seed. Second, a gradient adversarial loss operates on early denoising timesteps (t ∈ [750, 1000] for SD v1.5, where high-level structural features are formed) to decouple co-occurring high-level features: a gradient-ascent term increases the noise-prediction distance between the fine-tuned and frozen models for the combination prompt, while a gradient-descent term decreases that distance for each individual concept prompt, weighted by α = 0.1. This preserves individual concept generation while breaking the co-occurrence pattern that produces the harmful combination.

## Results

On the UnlearnCanvas dataset (object + style concept combinations), CoGFD achieves 93.7% object classification accuracy and 92.2% style classification accuracy for overlapped concept combinations, substantially outperforming all baselines (e.g., ESD drops to 47.5% object / 41.1% style). On non-overlapped combinations, CoGFD reaches 94.6% object and 97.5% style accuracy, closely matching the original SD v1.5 (98.8%). On COCO30K, CoGFD-fine-tuned model achieves FID-SD of 1.56 and CLIP score of 30.74, the closest to the original SD v1.5 (FID-SD 14.55, CLIP 30.90) among all methods, indicating minimal degradation of general generative capability. The Erase-Retain Score of 8.19 (at t∈[750,1000]) confirms effective erasure of concept combinations while preserving individual concept generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoGFD outperforms all five baselines on both object and style classification accuracy for overlapped concept combinations, with the largest margins over ESD (47.5% object, 41.1% style) and CA (80.9% object, 71.8% style). On non-overlapped combinations, CoGFD (94.6% object, 97.5% style) is competitive with SALUN (91.4% object, 89.1% style) and FMN (90.1% object, 96.1% style), while ESD and UCE lag further behind. An interesting asymmetry emerges: SALUN preserves object accuracy well (91.6%) but severely degrades style accuracy (42.8%), while FMN shows the opposite pattern, suggesting these methods erase combinations by removing one concept type rather than decoupling features. On the COCO30K generalization test, CoGFD achieves the best FID-SD (1.56 vs. 2.74 for UCE and 2.98 for ESD) and highest CLIP score (30.74 vs. 30.15 for UCE), confirming it causes the least collateral damage to overall image generation quality. On the HarmfulCmb theme-consistency evaluation (Figure 5), CoGFD significantly reduces inappropriate image generation across all 10 high-risk themes compared to baselines, which cannot generalize erasure to semantically equivalent concept combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept-erasing line of work for text-to-image diffusion models (ESD, CA, UCE, FMN, SALUN), which targets individual high-risk visual concepts, by identifying and formalizing the distinct problem of erasing *combinations* of benign concepts that jointly produce inappropriate themes. It draws on Ho et al.'s (2020) observation that high-level image features are generated in the early denoising stages to design a gradient adversarial loss that decouples co-occurring high-level features rather than removing them outright. The theme-consistency analysis component repurposes multi-agent LLM interaction (GPT-4 via AutoGen) to iteratively construct a concept logic graph, a technique borrowed from knowledge-graph generation but applied here to decompose semantically related concept combinations. The paper explicitly positions itself apart from machine unlearning (which targets memorized data) and knowledge editing (which targets LLM FFN layers), arguing that CCE requires a fundamentally different formulation centered on compositional semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CCE problem addresses a genuine and underexplored safety gap: diffusion models can generate harmful content from combinations of individually benign concepts (e.g., 'kids' + 'wine' → underage drinking), and no prior method systematically handles this. The practical impact is significant for model providers who need to mitigate ethical and legal risks without degrading the model's overall generative utility. Key limitations include the HarmfulCmb dataset covering only minor-related themes, reliance on GPT-4 for concept graph generation (introducing a dependency and potential instability), and evaluation limited to Stable Diffusion v1.5 and SD XL. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other diffusion architectures, how robust it is to adversarial prompt rephrasing, and whether the LLM-based graph generation can scale to thousands of themes without manual curation.
