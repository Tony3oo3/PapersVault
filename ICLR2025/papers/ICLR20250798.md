## Problem

Existing denoising-based 3D molecular pre-training methods apply a single hand-crafted noise strategy (e.g., fixed-scale Gaussian or hybrid dihedral/coordinate perturbations) uniformly across all molecules. This ignores both the anisotropy of molecular force fields within a molecule and the molecule-specific Potential Energy Surface (PES) differences across molecules, leading to inaccurate force field learning and suboptimal representations for downstream property prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250798/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes equilibrium 3D conformations (N×3 atomic coordinate matrices) along with molecular graph topology as input. The Noise Generator outputs a per-atom Gaussian distribution N(x_i, σ²_{x_i}) for each atom, where the mean is the atom's original position and the variance is learned and atom-specific. The Denoising Module then takes the reparameterized noisy conformation and predicts per-atom noise vectors; after pre-training, only the Denoising Module is retained, and its intermediate hidden representations serve as the molecular embedding for downstream tasks.

## Method

DenoiseVAE introduces a learnable Noise Generator (a 4-layer EGNN) that maps the original conformation to atom-specific Gaussian noise distributions, replacing fixed hand-crafted noise schedules. Noisy conformations are sampled via the reparameterization trick (x̃_i = x_i + ε·σ_{x_i}) and fed into a 7-layer EGNN Denoising Module that predicts the added noise. The two modules are jointly trained under a VAE objective: a variance-weighted reconstruction loss (L_Denoise) that enforces physically meaningful denoising, plus a KL divergence term (L_KL) that regularizes the generated distributions toward a fixed Gaussian prior N(0, σ²) to prevent collapse to trivial zero-noise solutions. Theoretically, the paper proves that minimizing this objective is equivalent to maximizing the ELBO of the conformation log-likelihood, and that the learned adaptive distribution achieves a strictly higher ELBO than any fixed-Gaussian baseline, providing a formal guarantee of superior force field approximation.

## Results

On QM9, DenoiseVAE achieves the best or second-best MAE in 11 out of 12 molecular properties, with standout results on dipole moment (μ = 0.0079 D), HOMO-LUMO gap (Δε = 26.0 meV), and heat capacity (Cv = 0.015 cal/mol·K, a 21% improvement over SliDe). On MD17 force prediction, it achieves the best MAE in 5 of 8 molecules, including Naphthalene (0.0219) and Aspirin (0.0567). On PDBBind LBA prediction at the 30% sequence identity split, DenoiseVAE attains the best RMSE (1.401) and Pearson correlation (0.588) among all pretraining-based methods, and the best Pearson (0.769) at the 60% split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest denoising baseline SliDe (Ni et al., 2023), DenoiseVAE wins on 10 of 12 QM9 properties, with the largest gains on ZPVE (1.028 vs 1.521 meV, −32.4%) and Cv (0.015 vs 0.019 cal/mol·K, −21.1%), while losing on polarizability α (0.0650 vs 0.0366 a₀³) and ε_HOMO (14.2 vs 13.6 meV). Compared to Frad (Feng et al., 2023), which uses hand-crafted hybrid dihedral+coordinate noise, DenoiseVAE outperforms it on 11 of 12 QM9 properties and achieves 20.9% and 16.6% MAE reductions on Naphthalene and Aspirin in MD17, respectively. On MD17, DenoiseVAE is best in 5/8 molecules but underperforms Frad on Malonaldehyde (0.1012 vs 0.0427) and Salicylic Acid (0.1478 vs 0.0410), suggesting the learned noise distribution is less effective for certain rigid or highly symmetric systems. On PDBBind LBA at the 30% identity split, DenoiseVAE achieves the best RMSE (1.401) and Pearson (0.588) among all pretraining-based methods, surpassing EGNN-PLM (RMSE 1.403, Pearson 0.565) and Uni-Mol (RMSE 1.520, Pearson 0.558); at 60% identity it leads in Pearson (0.769) but trails ProNet (0.765) by a narrow margin in Spearman (0.746 vs 0.761). On PCQM4Mv2, DenoiseVAE achieves a validation MAE of 0.0777 with only 1.44M parameters, matching or beating much larger models like GPS++ (44.3M params, 0.0778) and Graphormer (47.1M, 0.0864).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the denoising-based 3D molecular pre-training line (Coord, 3D-EMGP, Frad, SliDe) by replacing hand-crafted, molecule-agnostic noise strategies with a learnable Noise Generator that produces atom-specific, molecule-adaptive noise distributions. The key conceptual departure is framing the noise generation and denoising jointly within a Variational Auto Encoder (Kingma & Welling, 2013), where the reparameterization trick enables end-to-end training and a KL regularization prevents trivial solutions. The theoretical foundation rests on the score-matching/denoising equivalence (Vincent, 2011), which the paper leverages to show that optimizing their objective maximizes the ELBO of the conformation log-likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation: shared hand-crafted noise ignores the molecule-specific Potential Energy Surface, leading to suboptimal force field learning. The VAE formulation provides a principled training recipe and theoretical guarantee, and the resulting model is remarkably lightweight (1.44M parameters) yet competitive with much larger architectures on PCQM4Mv2. However, the method is based on classical force field assumptions, only models independent per-atom noise (diagonal covariance), and shows notably weaker results on 3 of 8 MD17 molecules compared to Frad, suggesting the approach may not uniformly capture all molecular dynamics. Open questions include whether integration with more accurate (e.g., DFT-level) force fields or full covariance modeling would yield further gains.
