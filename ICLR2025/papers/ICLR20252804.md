## Problem

Many important machine learning problems (min-max optimization, projected Bellman error minimization) are variational inequalities (VIs) rather than scalar loss minimization, causing naive gradient methods to diverge or cycle. Existing surrogate loss approaches were only analyzed for scalar minimization and do not exploit the hidden monotone structure present in VI settings. This paper shows that VIs are strictly harder than scalar minimization—divergence is possible even under strong monotonicity—and proposes a principled surrogate-based framework with convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes initial parameters θ₁ ∈ R^d, a VI operator F (or unbiased stochastic estimates F_ξ in the stochastic case), and a differentiable model mapping g: R^d → R^n whose image defines the constraint set Z. It produces a sequence of parameters {θ_t} whose predictions z_t = g(θ_t) converge linearly to the VI solution z*. The inner loop accepts any black-box optimizer (ADAM, Gauss-Newton, Levenberg-Marquardt, etc.) that can descend the surrogate loss, making the approach optimizer-agnostic. In the stochastic RL setting, the squared-error term in the surrogate can be approximated with a fixed batch (Surr-GD) or resampled per gradient step (double sampling) to trade bias against variance.

## Method

At each outer iteration t, the method constructs a squared-error surrogate loss ℓ_t(θ) = ½‖g(θ) − (g(θ_t) − ηF(g(θ_t)))‖² whose exact minimizer recovers the projected gradient step in prediction space. A novel α-descent condition (ℓ_t(θ_{t+1}) − ℓ_t* ≤ α²(ℓ_t(θ_t) − ℓ_t*)) is imposed on the inner-loop optimization, guaranteeing linear convergence to the VI solution when α < μ/L and η is sufficiently small, without requiring errors to be summable or globally bounded. The paper proves that α < 1 alone is insufficient for VI convergence (unlike scalar minimization), demonstrating a fundamental separation in difficulty. The framework unifies existing preconditioning methods—PHGD, Bertsekas's stochastic least-squares update—as special cases corresponding to one step of Gauss-Newton on the surrogate, and extends them with damped Gauss-Newton and Levenberg-Marquardt variants that show improved empirical robustness. In the RL setting, the approach yields novel TD(0) variants that are more data- and compute-efficient than standard TD(0) in Mujoco environments.

## Results

In the nonlinear PBE experiments on Ant and HalfCheetah (Mujoco), surrogate-based methods (Surr-GD and double-sampling surrogate) significantly outperform TD(0) in both data efficiency (outer loop iterations) and wall-clock time, and converge to a lower value prediction error than TD(0) (indicated by the red dashed line in Figure 4). In the hidden matching pennies game, LM and GD with 10 inner steps outperform PHGD (single-step GN), with LM being the most stable method under larger step sizes. In the linear PBE setting (100-state slow-mixing Markov chain), Surr-GD with multiple inner steps converges faster to the exact Bertsekas preconditioned update than a single-step approximation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PHGD (Sakos et al., 2024), the proposed surrogate framework with LM or multi-step GD achieves faster and more robust convergence in both hidden matching pennies and hidden rock-paper-scissors games; PHGD (equivalent to single-step GN) converges linearly but diverges when the step size η is increased by an order of magnitude, whereas DGN and LM maintain convergence under the same conditions. Against TD(0) (Sutton, 1988) in the nonlinear PBE setting on Ant and HalfCheetah, the surrogate methods (Surr-GD with 50 inner steps and the double-sampling variant) achieve substantially lower value prediction error in fewer outer-loop iterations and less wall-clock time, and converge to a lower asymptotic error than TD(0). Against Bertsekas's stochastic preconditioned update (2009), Surr-GD with multiple inner steps converges to the same fixed point but does so faster, while avoiding the potentially intractable matrix inversion in the exact update. Overall, the paper's method wins on all three comparisons, with the most dramatic gains over TD(0) in the deep RL setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the surrogate loss framework from scalar minimization (Johnson & Zhang 2020; Vaswani et al. 2021; Lavington et al. 2023) to variational inequality problems, introducing an α-descent condition that is optimizer-agnostic and avoids requiring summable or bounded errors. It provides a unifying lens on preconditioning methods for VIs—showing that PHGD (Sakos et al. 2024), Bertsekas's stochastic least-squares policy evaluation (2009), and natural hidden gradient dynamics (Mladenovic et al. 2022) are all equivalent to one step of Gauss-Newton on the surrogate loss. The key departure from prior surrogate work is the demonstration that VI surrogates are strictly harder than scalar surrogates: even under strong monotonicity, α < 1 can still cause divergence due to rotational dynamics inherent to adversarial problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives practitioners a principled way to plug standard deep learning optimizers (ADAM, etc.) into VI problems while retaining convergence guarantees, and the unification of preconditioning methods under the surrogate/Gauss-Newton lens suggests natural extensions (DGN, LM) with better empirical robustness. The novel TD(0) variants with double sampling offer a practical data-efficiency improvement for policy evaluation. Key limitations include the requirement that Z be convex and F be strongly monotone, the difficulty of verifying the α-descent condition in practice (ℓ*_t is generally unknown), and the fact that the stochastic analysis requires Z = R^n. Open questions include relaxing the convexity assumption on Z, handling non-strongly-monotone operators, and whether the α-descent condition can be automatically enforced by adaptive optimizers without explicit inner-loop control.
