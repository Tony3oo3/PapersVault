## Problem

Existing 3D hair modeling methods neglect the inherent biscale nature of hair—where global structure defines flow, volume, and length while local structure defines curl patterns—limiting both generation quality and multi-scale editing. Moreover, most prior models are heavy and task-specific, lacking the lightweight, disentangled parametric design (analogous to SMPL for bodies) needed to serve as a generic prior across diverse downstream applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253047/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is parameterized by a 512-dim guide strand vector θ (global structure), a 512-dim styling vector β (curl patterns), and a pre-defined root set R controlling hair density. Internally, hair is stored as a 256×256×64 UV texture of strand PCA coefficients on the scalp, with each strand represented as a 100-point 3D polyline. For reconstruction, a single 2D image with estimated camera parameters drives differentiable rendering; for generation, Gaussian noise samples θ and β; for the T2I application, depth and edge maps extracted from the 3D hair geometry condition Adobe Firefly.

## Method

PERM's core insight is a PCA-based strand representation computed in the frequency domain via DFT, where the first 10 principal components capture low-frequency global structure (direction, length) and the remaining 54 encode high-frequency curl details—enabling natural decomposition into guide and residual textures. The architecture decomposes hair generation into three stages mirroring the grooming pipeline: (1) a StyleGAN2 generator synthesizes a 32×32 guide texture (10 PCA coefficients + baldness mask) from θ; (2) a U-Net deterministically upsamples to 256×256 by predicting blending weights and residual corrections over 4 neighboring guide strands; (3) a VAE (pSp-style encoder, StyleGAN2-style decoder) synthesizes the 256×256×54 residual texture from β. Notably, the frequency-domain PCA strand decoder (19.5K parameters) achieves lower position error than all deep-learning VAE alternatives (759K–15.67M parameters) while requiring no learned decoder, and the full model is trained on 21K augmented USC-HairSalon samples with each module trained separately.

## Results

On the strand representation benchmark (Table 1), Freq. PCA (Ours) achieves a mean position error of 0.020 and curvature error of 2.150 with only 19.50K parameters, substantially outperforming all deep-learning VAE alternatives (e.g., MLP VAE: pos. err. 0.158, cur. err. 1.594; Freq. VAE: pos. err. 1.211, cur. err. 0.910) while using 1–3 orders of magnitude fewer parameters. For full-hair 3D reconstruction on 17 test hairstyles, PERM achieves a position error of 1.658 and curvature error of 0.769, compared to GroomGen's 2.570 and 6.199 respectively. On a second, curlier private dataset (Table 2), Freq. PCA again achieves the lowest position error (0.026) with competitive curvature error (2.364).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GroomGen (Zhou et al., 2023), PERM achieves substantially lower full-hair reconstruction errors (pos. err. 1.658 vs. 2.570; cur. err. 0.769 vs. 6.199), and the authors demonstrate that GroomGen's GAN-based neural upsampler is highly unstable, frequently collapsing to implausible curls and flyaway strands. For single-view hair reconstruction, PERM qualitatively outperforms HairStep (Zheng et al., 2023) by better preserving local curl patterns and avoiding bald-area artifacts on tilted head poses, and also surpasses Yang et al. (2019) on dynamic hair sequences. Compared to HAAR (Sklyarova et al., 2023b), PERM produces more faithful reconstructions because HAAR's text-conditioned pipeline cannot capture intricate hairstyle details. For hairstyle interpolation, PERM matches Zhou et al. (2018) and both outperform Weng et al. (2013), with PERM additionally avoiding head-mesh intersection artifacts. At the strand level, Freq. PCA dominates all VAE-based alternatives (Freq. VAE, CNN VAE, Transformer VAE, MLP VAE) on position error by 1–2 orders of magnitude while using 19.5K parameters versus 0.76M–15.67M.

## Relation to Prior Work

PERM extends the strand-based hair representation lineage from HairNet (Zhou et al., 2018) and GroomGen (Zhou et al., 2023), which organized strand features as 2D scalp UV textures, but departs fundamentally in two ways: it replaces the VAE-based strand encoder with a PCA decomposition in the frequency domain (DFT), and it explicitly disentangles global hair structure (guide textures, low-rank PCA coefficients) from local curl patterns (residual textures, high-rank coefficients) using separate generative models (StyleGAN2 and VAE respectively). The overall design philosophy mirrors SMPL/MANO parametric body models, positioning hair as a lightweight, disentangled prior for downstream tasks rather than a task-specific heavy network. The frequency-domain PCA choice is motivated by biological hair growth literature (Hoover et al., 2023; De La Mettrie et al., 2007), transferring insights from hair-type classification into a learned generative framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PERM provides the first lightweight, disentangled parametric hair prior analogous to SMPL for bodies, enabling task-agnostic applications (single-view reconstruction, hairstyle editing, hair-conditioned image generation) without retraining for each task. Key limitations include reliance on limited 3D hair data (USC-HairSalon + augmentation), underrepresentation of afro and braided styles, and an optimization-based single-view pipeline that struggles with extreme occlusions or tied hairstyles. Open questions include developing 3D generative models directly from 2D in-the-wild images, integrating diffusion-based full-head synthesis, and building systematic data capture pipelines to address the fundamental scarcity of diverse 3D hair assets.
