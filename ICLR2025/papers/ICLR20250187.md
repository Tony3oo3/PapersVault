## Problem

Existing multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) algorithms are validated only on benchmarks with 2–4 objectives, yet practical applications (jet engine control, traffic management, military planning) demand simultaneous optimization of many correlated objectives. Naively sampling preference vectors in high-dimensional simplex spaces becomes computationally intractable, and standard dimension reduction techniques (PCA, autoencoders) are designed for static datasets and fail to preserve Pareto-optimality under online learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a K-dimensional instantaneous reward vector r(s,a) ∈ R^K (K > m ≥ 2) and a state observation from a MOMDP, and produces an m-dimensional reduced reward f(r) = Ar used for online Q-network training. At evaluation time, the learned preference-parameterized policy π_m*(·|·, ω_m) is tested on evenly distributed points in the (m−1)-simplex Δ^m, and performance is measured in the original K-dimensional reward space via hypervolume and sparsity. The reduced dimensionality m is a hyperparameter (e.g., m=4 for 16-objective traffic, m=3 for 5-objective LunarLander), and the transformation matrix A is constrained to be positive and row-stochastic so that every reduced preference ω_m ∈ Δ^m maps to a valid original-space preference A^T ω_m ∈ Δ^K.

## Method

The core contribution is an online affine reward projection f(r) = Ar where A ∈ R_+^{m×K} is positive and row-stochastic, proven (Theorem 1) to preserve Pareto-optimality: any policy optimal in the reduced space remains Pareto-optimal in the original space. The matrix A is learned jointly with a reconstruction network g_φ by minimizing ||r − g_φ(Ar)||², using a softmax parameterization on each row of A to enforce positivity and row-stochasticity throughout stochastic gradient descent. This reconstruction objective is trained simultaneously with the multi-objective Q-network Q_θ(s, a, ω_m) (following Yang et al., 2019) that approximates the optimal action-value function over all reduced preferences ω_m ∈ Δ^m. Dropout is applied to g_φ as implicit L2 regularization to prevent the reconstructor from over-focusing on a subset of objectives, which empirically reduces sparsity. The paper also introduces a dedicated training-and-evaluation framework: the Q-network is trained online with reduced rewards f(r) from a replay buffer, while evaluation computes expected cumulative returns in the original K-dimensional space to fairly compare dimension reduction methods.

## Results

In the 16-objective Traffic Light Control environment, the proposed method achieves a hypervolume of 166.9±48.1 (×10⁶¹) and sparsity of 2.3±1.0 (×10⁵), improving the base algorithm's hypervolume by a factor of 37.9 and reducing sparsity by a factor of ~800. In the 5-objective LunarLander-5D environment, the method achieves HV of 25.6±6.9 (×10⁷) and SP of 1.1±1.2 (×10²), an 8.3× hypervolume improvement over the base. The method also outperforms the closest competitor (NPCA) by 8.6× in hypervolume in the traffic setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all four baselines on both hypervolume and sparsity in both environments. Against the base algorithm (Yang et al. 2019) without dimension reduction, the method achieves 37.9× higher hypervolume and ~800× lower sparsity in the 16-objective traffic setting, and 8.3× higher hypervolume with 28.4× lower sparsity in LunarLander-5D. Incremental PCA (Cardot & Degras 2018) completely fails in the traffic environment (HV = 0) because its eigenvector matrix violates the positivity condition required for Pareto-optimality preservation, and it also worsens sparsity in LunarLander-5D. The online autoencoder (Bank et al. 2020) also produces near-zero hypervolume in traffic (0.007) due to its nonlinear transformation violating the linearity requirement, and shows no improvement over the base in LunarLander-5D. NPCA (Zass & Shashua 2006) is the strongest baseline, achieving 19.4 HV in traffic, but still falls 8.6× short of the proposed method; its online variant suffers from instability where the PCA loss and orthonormality constraint conflict, causing the learned matrix to collapse to rank 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the multi-policy MORL framework of Yang et al. (2019), which trains a single preference-parameterized Q-network to cover the convex coverage set, and applies a reward dimension reduction layer on top of it. It departs from existing online dimension reduction methods (incremental PCA, online autoencoders, online NPCA) by imposing the specific structural constraints of positivity and row-stochasticity on the projection matrix, which the authors prove (Theorem 1) are sufficient to preserve Pareto-optimality after transformation. The paper also introduces a new training and evaluation framework for this setting, since prior dimension reduction work in MORL (e.g., Giuliani et al., 2014) was limited to static, batch-based analyses without online interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine scalability bottleneck: most MORL algorithms are validated on 2–4 objectives, yet practical applications (jet engine control, military planning, traffic management) demand 8–16+ objectives, and naive preference sampling in high-dimensional simplex becomes intractable. The clean theoretical guarantee (affine + positive + row-stochastic ⇒ Pareto-optimality preserved) gives practitioners a principled, simple tool rather than a heuristic. Key open questions remain: Theorem 1 provides only sufficient conditions, the test-time reduction of high-dimensional preference vectors to the learned lower-dimensional space is not addressed, and validation is limited to two environments (one custom), leaving the method's robustness across diverse high-dimensional MORL tasks unestablished.
