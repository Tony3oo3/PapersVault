## Problem

Existing human motion generation methods rely on clean MoCap data with limited scene diversity and ignore critical context factors like surrounding environment, individual characteristics, and route goals. Web videos offer natural, diverse pedestrian movements but produce noisy pseudo-labels when annotated with pre-trained predictors, creating an unexplored challenge of learning realistic context-aware pedestrian movements from large-scale noisy data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252361/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 2D urban scene image, monocular depth map, semantic segmentation map, SMPL body shape parameter β (10-dim), initial 3D position, and goal 3D position as conditioning. It outputs a 2-second (60-frame) 3D pedestrian motion sequence parameterized by SMPL root translation, root orientation (6D rotation), and body pose (23 joints). The motion is internally represented as per-timestep velocity (Δt) and rotation tokens rather than absolute positions, and the model supports generating arbitrarily long sequences by concatenating short intervals.

## Method

PedGen is a conditional diffusion model with a transformer denoiser that treats velocity and rotation as separate tokens per timestep, trained with a combined reconstruction, trajectory, and forward-kinematics geometric loss. To handle noisy pseudo-labels, it employs two strategies: (1) an iterative reconstruction-based anomaly detection loop that partially denoises samples and filters those exceeding a reconstruction error threshold, and (2) a learnable motion mask embedding that replaces missing timesteps in partial labels, with the loss computed only on available frames. A novel Context Encoder unprojects 2D depth and semantic maps into a 3D point cloud, extracts a local neighborhood around the starting position, voxelizes it with majority-voted semantic labels, and processes it through a cross-attention layer to produce a scene embedding that is combined with body shape and goal position embeddings to form the final conditioning vector.

## Results

On the CityWalkers validation set without goal conditioning, PedGen achieves mADE 1.13 and aADE 4.08, outperforming HumanMAC (mADE 1.31, aADE 4.67) and MDM (mADE 1.33, aADE 4.55). With goal conditioning, PedGen reaches mADE 0.59 and aADE 0.96 on CityWalkers, surpassing TRUMANS (mADE 0.73, aADE 1.26). On the zero-shot Waymo test set, PedGen achieves mFDE 0.78 with goal (vs. TRUMANS 1.41), and on the CARLA simulator it attains 0% collision rate and 0% foot floating rate with goal conditioning, compared to TRUMANS's 0.6% for both.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PedGen consistently outperforms all three baselines across all three evaluation datasets. Without goal conditioning, PedGen reduces aADE by 12.4% over HumanMAC (4.08 vs. 4.67) and 10.3% over MDM (4.08 vs. 4.55) on CityWalkers, while also achieving the lowest collision rate (1.6% vs. 2.5% and 2.1%) and foot floating rate (2.6% vs. 10.2% and 3.2%) on CARLA. With goal conditioning, PedGen beats TRUMANS by 19.2% in mADE (0.59 vs. 0.73) and 14.3% in aADE (1.08 vs. 1.26) on CityWalkers, and more dramatically on Waymo where mFDE drops from 1.41 to 0.78 (a 44.7% reduction). On the CARLA simulator with goal, PedGen achieves perfect 0% collision and 0% foot floating rates, whereas TRUMANS still exhibits 0.6% for both. The zero-shot generalization gap is particularly notable on Waymo, where PedGen's mFDE of 0.78 nearly halves TRUMANS's 1.41, demonstrating the benefit of learning from diverse real-world web video data with context factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

PedGen builds on the conditional diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020; Ho & Salimans, 2022) and the transformer-based denoising architecture popularized by MDM (Tevet et al., 2022), but departs from prior human motion generation in two key ways. First, instead of training on clean MoCap or controlled-environment data (AMASS, SLOPER4D, EMDB), it learns from noisy pseudo-labels extracted from web videos via WHAM, ZoeDepth, and SegFormer. Second, it introduces a Context Encoder that lifts 2D depth/semantic maps into a local 3D voxel representation, departing from prior scene-conditioned motion models (TRUMANS, Huang et al. 2023) that operate on indoor scenes or 2D features. The paper positions itself against both the pedestrian prediction literature (Waymo-style trajectory forecasting) and the human motion generation literature (MoCap-based diffusion), arguing that neither adequately captures natural urban pedestrian behavior with its full context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CityWalkers (30.8 hours, 120K pedestrians, 227 cities, 49 countries) is the first large-scale dataset of real-world pedestrian movements paired with multi-factor context labels (scene geometry/semantics, body shape, goal), filling a gap left by controlled MoCap datasets and sensor-dependent outdoor datasets. The demonstration that noisy pseudo-labeled web video data at scale outperforms clean but small-scale LiDAR-annotated data (SLOPER4D) is a practical signal for the field. However, PedGen only models static scene context at the starting frame and generates single-pedestrian movements, leaving dynamic interactions (other pedestrians, moving vehicles) and group behaviors as important open problems. The zero-shot transfer to both Waymo and CARLA suggests the learned representations generalize, but the gap between simulated and real-world deployment in safety-critical settings (autonomous driving) remains unaddressed.
