## Problem

IRM-TV, which reinterprets Invariant Risk Minimization as a total variation model, cannot achieve full out-of-distribution generalization because its TV penalty hyperparameter is fixed and training environments are often insufficiently diverse. Lai & Wang (2024) showed that the penalty must vary with the feature extractor but left the construction and optimization of this variable penalty unspecified. This paper identifies the missing mechanism: the TV penalty is exactly a Lagrangian multiplier, enabling a tractable primal-dual formulation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes data samples drawn from multiple environments, where each sample carries an input x and target y; when explicit environment labels are unavailable, auxiliary variables z (e.g., RGB channel means, geographic coordinates, or demographic attributes) are fed into a separate environment-inference network that outputs a probability vector over E fundamental environments. The feature extractor (MLP, 1D-CNN, 2D-CNN, or ResNet-18 depending on the task) maps x to a latent feature, and a linear classifier w produces the final class label or regression value. The TV penalty strength is itself a small neural network parameterized by a separate set of parameters that takes the flattened feature-extractor parameters as input, so the penalty adapts dynamically during training rather than being a fixed scalar.

## Method

The key insight is that the autonomous TV penalty hyperparameter in IRM-TV is precisely a Lagrangian multiplier, which converts the problem into a primal-dual (minimax) optimization: the primal variable minimizes the entire invariant risk to extract invariant features, while the dual variable maximizes the TV penalty to adversarially stress-test against spurious features. The training objective is a semi-Nash equilibrium where the feature extractor is optimal only conditional on the dual variable being at its worst-case response, formalized in Definition 1 and guaranteed to exist under mild continuity and boundedness assumptions (Theorem 2). A convergent primal-dual algorithm (Theorem 3) alternates subgradient descent on the feature extractor (handling the non-differentiable absolute-gradient term) with gradient ascent on the dual parameters, using a 1/k^p learning-rate schedule that yields O(((p-1)*epsilon)^(-1/(p-1))) complexity. In practice, the penalty function is instantiated as a small MLP with SoftPlus output, and the adversarial loop is run after an initial ERM warm-up phase, allowing the penalty to dynamically strengthen as the feature extractor evolves.

## Results

On the Simulation benchmark (most challenging setting), OOD-TV-IRM-ℓ₁ achieves the highest mean accuracy of 89.44% and worst-case accuracy of 88.77%, outperforming IRM (86.70%/85.59%) and IRM-TV-ℓ₁ (87.13%/86.18%). On CelebA, OOD-TV-IRM-ℓ₁ reaches 79.45% mean accuracy vs. 76.30% for IRM and 77.51% for IRM-TV-ℓ₁. On the regression task (House Price), OOD-TV-Minimax-ℓ₁ achieves the lowest MSE of 0.2621 (mean) and 0.3706 (worst), compared to 0.3203/0.4465 for ZIN and 0.4589/0.6378 for IRM. Across all 7 datasets, the proposed OOD-TV methods outperform their direct IRM-TV counterparts in every mean and worst-case metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed OOD-TV-IRM-ℓ₁ consistently outperforms its direct predecessor IRM-TV-ℓ₁ across all datasets: on Simulation (89.44% vs. 87.13% mean), CelebA (79.45% vs. 77.51%), Adult (84.35% vs. 82.98%), and House Price (0.3383 vs. 0.4009 MSE). Similarly, OOD-TV-Minimax-ℓ₁ beats Minimax-TV-ℓ₁ on Simulation (83.79% vs. 75.55%), CelebA (70.44% vs. 68.76%), and NICO Animal (91.12% vs. 82.89%). Against the original IRM baseline, OOD-TV-IRM-ℓ₁ gains 2.74% mean accuracy on Simulation and 3.15% on CelebA, while OOD-TV-Minimax-ℓ₂ surpasses ZIN by 1.65% on Simulation and 1.70% on Colored MNIST (96.71% vs. 95.02%). The OOD-TV methods also dominate TIVA, EIIL, and LfF across nearly all benchmarks, with the largest margins observed on Landcover (OOD-TV-IRM-ℓ₂ at 67.05% vs. TIVA at 53.88%) and NICO Vehicle (OOD-TV-Minimax-ℓ₂ at 78.72% vs. EIIL at 69.02%). The only notable exception is House Price, where OOD-TV-IRM-ℓ₁ (0.3383 MSE) trails OOD-TV-Minimax-ℓ₁ (0.2621 MSE), indicating the Minimax variant is better suited for regression tasks without explicit environment labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Lai & Wang (2024), who showed that IRM is mathematically equivalent to a total variation model and argued that the TV penalty hyperparameter λ_Φ must be variable with the feature extractor Φ to achieve OOD generalization, but left the implementation unspecified. The key conceptual leap here is recognizing that λ_Φ is precisely a Lagrangian multiplier, which transforms the single-level IRM-TV optimization into a primal-dual (adversarial) game between the feature extractor (primal, minimizing invariant risk) and the penalty strength (dual, maximizing the TV penalty to stress-test against spurious features). The OOD-TV-Minimax variant further builds on ZIN (Lin et al., 2022) by replacing the fixed environment partition with a learned environment inference operator ρ, making it the TV-ℓ₁ analogue of ZIN with an autonomous penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a concrete gap left by Lai & Wang (2024) by providing a tractable, convergent primal-dual algorithm for the variable λ_Φ, making the TV-based IRM framework practically deployable for OOD generalization. The semi-Nash equilibrium framing gives a principled stopping criterion and explains the adversarial dynamics between training loss and robustness. Open questions remain: whether the approach benefits from richer environment diversity (the paper acknowledges insufficient training environment diversity as a fundamental limitation), whether the TV penalty is optimal compared to other variation measures, and whether the semi-Nash equilibrium is sufficient or whether a full Nash equilibrium would yield further gains. The consistent but moderate improvements across 7 diverse datasets suggest the method is robust but not transformative.
