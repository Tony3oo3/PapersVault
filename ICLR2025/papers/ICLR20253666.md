## Problem

Transformer-based LLMs face quadratic attention cost and enormous KV cache memory when processing long contexts, and existing context compression methods (soft-token summarization, token deletion) suffer from information bottlenecks, re-encoding overhead, fixed compression ratios, or query-dependency that limits multi-turn efficiency. This paper addresses the gap of achieving simultaneously effective, efficient, and flexible long-context compression that generalizes beyond the training length.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253666/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long text context (up to 128K tokens, far exceeding the 20K training length) and progressively compresses it into beacon token KV activations at every layer, reducing the effective sequence length by a configurable ratio (2×–32×). The compressed activations are accumulated across chunks and reused as a proxy for the full context during generation, enabling the LLM to produce next-token predictions conditioned on a much shorter representation. The compression is query-independent, so the compressed cache can be incrementally updated across multi-turn conversations without recomputation.

## Method

Activation Beacon introduces a special beacon token ⟨b⟩ whose per-layer KV activations serve as the compressed representation, replacing the raw context tokens. The long context is partitioned into fixed-size chunks, each further split into fine-grained units of size α (the compression ratio), with one beacon token appended at the end of every unit so that each beacon attends to a progressively larger scope, enabling fine-grained distillation. During encoding, the LLM processes one chunk at a time; after each chunk, raw token activations are discarded while beacon activations are accumulated and fed as cached context to subsequent chunks, avoiding re-encoding. Training uses compression-based auto-regression (next-token prediction conditioned on compressed + local context) with the backbone LLM frozen, optimizing only the beacon-specific projection matrices and a shared beacon embedding, while randomly sampling the compression ratio per chunk to support flexible downstream configurations.

## Results

On LongBench (32K context), Activation Beacon achieves 34.9/27.5/25.0/61.4/57.8 on Llama-2-7B and 40.5/40.3/26.8/68.4/66.4 on Qwen-2-7B across Single-Doc, Multi-Doc, Summarization, Few-Shot, and Code tasks, matching the uncompressed fine-tuned baseline (Full-FT) within 0.5 points on nearly all categories. On Multi-Needle-in-a-Haystack (128K, x8 compression), it achieves 9.70/9.35/9.10 accuracy over 1/2/3 turns with 2.445/2.773/2.981 s latency, versus 4.399/5.254/6.153 s for Full-FT, yielding ~2× end-to-end speedup and 8× KV cache reduction. The method generalizes to 128K contexts despite being trained only on ≤20K inputs, and preserves short-context capabilities (e.g., MMLU 69.1 vs. 70.1 for Qwen-2-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Activation Beacon decisively outperforms all four compression baselines on LongBench. On Llama-2-7B (32K, adaptive x2–x8 compression), it leads ICAE by +15.4 on Single-Doc (34.9 vs. 19.5) and AutoCompressor by +22.0 (34.9 vs. 12.9), confirming that a handful of soft tokens cannot capture the information in long contexts. LongLLMLingua also lags substantially (21.5 Single-Doc) because aggressive token deletion at x4–x8 ratios destroys context coherence. SnapKV is the strongest baseline (24.2 Single-Doc on Llama-2; 38.7 on Qwen-2) but is fundamentally limited to the backbone's native window (4K for Llama-2), so it cannot compress 32K inputs. On Qwen-2-7B (uniform x4), Activation Beacon matches Full-FT within 0.5 points on every task while SnapKV trails by 2.3 on Single-Doc and 6.0 on Code. In multi-turn NeedleBench (128K, x8), Activation Beacon achieves 9.10 accuracy at 2.981 s over 3 turns, versus 1.50 at 27.751 s for LongLLMLingua and 8.85 at 10.659 s for SnapKV, demonstrating both superior quality and a 3.6× latency advantage in realistic multi-turn settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant soft-token compression paradigm (Gisting, AutoCompressor, ICAE) by directly compressing per-layer KV activations into beacon tokens rather than distilling context into a small number of learned soft prompts. It also contrasts with query-dependent token-deletion methods (LLMLingua, SnapKV) by performing query-independent compression, enabling multi-turn reuse without recomputation. The progressive, fine-grained chunk-by-chunk workflow with interleaved beacon tokens is a new architectural design within the context compression family, and the plug-in formulation (freezing the LLM and training only new Q/K/V projection matrices for beacon tokens) positions it as a lightweight add-on rather than a retraining of the base model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-lossless compression at 8× ratio with 2× inference speedup and 8× KV cache reduction, while generalizing from 20K training to 128K inference, makes long-context deployment substantially more practical on consumer hardware. The query-independent, incrementally-updatable compression is particularly valuable for multi-turn and streaming scenarios where prior methods require full recomputation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to MoE or multimodal architectures, whether the RULER reasoning/aggregation gaps (VT, CWE) can be closed without task-specific fine-tuning, and how the method interacts with orthogonal KV compression techniques (quantization, GQA, cross-layer sharing) that the authors note as future work.
