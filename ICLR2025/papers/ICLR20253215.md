## Problem

Most functional causal models for causal discovery assume homoscedastic (constant-variance) noise, limiting their applicability to real-world data where noise variance often depends on the cause. Existing methods for handling heteroscedastic noise typically require computationally intensive noise-extraction steps (nonlinear regression followed by independence tests on residuals) or impose restrictive distributional assumptions such as Gaussianity. This paper asks whether causal direction can be identified under heteroscedastic symmetric noise without explicitly extracting the exogenous noise terms.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an observational data matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} (n=5000 samples in experiments) and a conditional independence test oracle with a significance level α. It first estimates the score function ∇log p via sliced score matching with a 3-layer MLP, then outputs a topological order π (a permutation of the d variables) and a binary DAG adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{d×d}. The two-phase design means the expensive score estimation is performed d times (once per sink-node removal), while the CI-test phase runs in O(d²) tests, reducing worst-case complexity from exponential to polynomial in d.

## Method

The core insight is a skewness-of-score criterion: in a heteroscedastic symmetric noise model Y = f(X) + σ(X)N with symmetric noise N, the score component along the effect coordinate (∂_y log p) is unskewed (SkewScore_y = 0) while the cause coordinate (∂_x log p) is generically skewed (SkewScore_x ≠ 0), as formalized in Theorem 1 under a mild non-degeneracy assumption (Assumption 1). This asymmetry arises because the conditional p(y|x) is symmetric for each x, whereas p(x|y) is generally asymmetric due to the heteroscedastic variance σ(x). The SkewScore algorithm (Algorithm 1) exploits this by iteratively selecting the variable with minimum |E[(∂_{x_j} log p)³]| as the current sink node, removing it, and repeating to recover the full topological order; edges are then added via conditional independence tests respecting that order. Corollary 2 extends the criterion to the multivariate setting, showing a node is a sink if and only if its score coordinate is unskewed, under a genericity condition (Eq. 6) that holds for almost all model parameters.

## Results

SkewScore is evaluated on synthetic HSNM data (5000 samples) across bivariate, latent-confounded triangular, and multivariate settings with Gaussian and Student's t noise. In the bivariate setting, SkewScore consistently outperforms or matches HNM-specific baselines (HECI, LOCI, HOST) and substantially outperforms general-purpose methods (NoTEARS, DAGMA, DiffAN). In the multivariate setting, SkewScore achieves the lowest topological order divergence and SHD, with the advantage growing as dimensionality increases (d up to 50). In the Gumbel (non-symmetric) noise ablation, SkewScore retains 89% accuracy versus 33% for HOST and 27% for DAGMA, demonstrating robustness beyond the symmetric noise assumption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HNM-specific baselines, SkewScore matches or slightly trails LOCI and HECI in the bivariate Gaussian/Student-t settings, but clearly outperforms them in the latent-confounded triangular setting (especially under the GP-sig data generation) and in multivariate scenarios as dimensionality grows. HOST, which relies on noise extraction and normality tests, degrades significantly under Student's t noise and in the presence of confounders, and drops to 33% accuracy under Gumbel noise. DiffAN performs competitively at low dimensions (d ≤ 10) but its accuracy and SHD deteriorate as d increases, likely due to error accumulation in its iterative non-retraining approach. NoTEARS and DAGMA, designed for linear or general DAG learning without HNM assumptions, show the weakest performance across all settings, with DAGMA scoring only 27% under Gumbel noise. Overall, SkewScore's key advantage is its consistent performance across noise types, confounding structures, and dimensions without requiring exogenous noise extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-matching-based causal discovery framework of Rolland et al. (2022) and the ordering-based search paradigm of Teyssier & Koller (2005) to the heteroscedastic noise setting. Unlike prior HNM methods (Immer et al. 2023, Duong & Nguyen 2023, Strobl & Lasko 2023) that require iterative noise extraction via nonlinear regression followed by independence or normality tests, SkewScore uses the third moment of the score function (skewness of the gradient of log density) as a direct, noise-extraction-free criterion. It also departs from earlier score-matching approaches (Rolland et al. 2022, Sanchez et al. 2023) that rely on second-order derivatives (Hessian diagonal) and assume homoscedastic noise, by showing that first-order score skewness suffices under symmetric heteroscedastic noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Heteroscedastic noise is prevalent in real-world domains (environmental science, robotics, finance), yet most functional causal model methods assume constant variance, limiting practical applicability. SkewScore offers a computationally simpler alternative that avoids the expensive per-variable noise extraction loop, and its theoretical robustness guarantee for a bivariate latent-confounded triangular model (when the causal effect dominates) is a useful step toward handling confounding without full FCI-style machinery. Key limitations include the symmetric noise assumption, the restriction to additive latent confounding effects, and the potential for spurious direct edges in multivariate DAG recovery under latent confounders. Open questions include extension to multiple latent confounders, non-symmetric noise, and scalability to very high-dimensional settings.
