## Problem

Existing prompt-based and LoRA-based continual learning methods with foundation models require either expanding a prompt/LoRA pool or retaining samples from previous tasks, creating scalability bottlenecks as the number of sequential tasks grows. No prior method simultaneously achieves rehearsal-free learning, inference efficiency without additional computational overhead, and end-to-end optimization of all parameters for CL objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252105/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and outputs class labels over an ever-growing label set. LoRA components (rank-10 by default) are inserted into the query and value projections of all Transformer blocks in a ViT-B/16 backbone, with a shared set of scalar magnitudes across all projections. At inference, the final trained model is evaluated directly on any test image without task-specific component selection or prompt retrieval, making inference cost identical to the base model plus a fixed low-rank overhead.

## Method

SD-LoRA incrementally decouples the magnitude and direction of LoRA updates: for each new task t, a new LoRA direction ĀtBt is learned while all previously learned directions {ĀkBk}k<t are frozen, and only the scalar magnitudes {αk}k=1..t are jointly optimized. This design causes the model to trace a low-loss trajectory that converges to an overlapping low-loss region shared by all tasks, eliminating catastrophic forgetting without rehearsal. Two parameter-efficient variants are introduced: SD-LoRA-RR applies stepwise rank reduction to later-task LoRA components, and SD-LoRA-KD uses least-squares fitting to absorb a new direction into the span of existing directions when the residual falls below a threshold, preventing unbounded parameter growth. A theoretical analysis based on matrix factorization convergence shows that gradient descent with small initialization sequentially recovers principal components of the optimal update, explaining why early-task directions dominate and later ones contribute diminishing refinements.

## Results

On ImageNet-A (N=10), SD-LoRA achieves 55.96% average accuracy (Acc) and 64.95% average anytime accuracy (AAA), outperforming the strongest baseline InfLoRA (49.20% Acc, 60.92% AAA) by 6.76% and 4.03% respectively. On DomainNet (N=5), SD-LoRA reaches 72.82% Acc and 78.89% AAA, edging out InfLoRA (71.59%, 78.29%). On ImageNet-R (N=20), SD-LoRA attains 80.30% Acc, surpassing InfLoRA by 7.68% in Acc and 4.62% in AAA. The parameter-efficient variants SD-LoRA-RR and SD-LoRA-KD show only marginal degradations (≤1.7% Acc) relative to full SD-LoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD-LoRA consistently outperforms all five baselines across both ImageNet-A and DomainNet. On ImageNet-A, the gap over the strongest baseline InfLoRA is 6.76% in Acc (55.96 vs. 49.20) and 4.03% in AAA (64.95 vs. 60.92); over prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, HiDe-Prompt), the Acc margin ranges from 10.0% to 13.3%. On DomainNet, SD-LoRA leads InfLoRA by 1.23% Acc and 0.60% AAA, and surpasses HiDe-Prompt (the best prompt-based method) by 0.62% Acc and 1.88% AAA. Notably, SD-LoRA is the only method that is simultaneously rehearsal-free, inference-efficient (no prompt selection overhead), and end-to-end optimized, whereas HiDe-Prompt and InfLoRA both require sample storage and prompt-based methods incur additional inference cost for component selection. The two variants (SD-LoRA-RR, SD-LoRA-KD) retain within 1.7% Acc of full SD-LoRA while reducing learnable parameters from 0.37M to 0.23M (RR) or eliminating parameter growth entirely (KD).

## Relation to Prior Work

SD-LoRA builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) by decomposing the low-rank update ΔW = AB into its Frobenius-norm magnitude and normalized direction, then incrementally learning these components separately across sequential tasks. It departs from the dominant prompt-based CL paradigm (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, HiDe-Prompt) and the rehearsal-based LoRA approach (InfLoRA) by eliminating both prompt-pool expansion and sample storage, instead relying on a fixed set of previously learned directions with adaptively rescaled magnitudes. The theoretical analysis draws on matrix factorization convergence results from Jiang et al. (2023), while the empirical observation that task-specific fine-tuned weights cluster near each other extends prior findings from Entezari et al. (2022) and Gueta et al. (2023) into the CL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SD-LoRA simultaneously satisfies three properties (rehearsal-free, inference-efficient, end-to-end optimized) that no prior foundation-model CL method achieves together, making it a practical choice for scalable, resource-constrained continual learning deployments. The finding that early LoRA directions dominate while later ones serve as minor refinements opens the door to more aggressive parameter compression strategies beyond the two variants proposed here. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ViT backbones to other foundation model architectures (e.g., diffusion models, LLMs), whether the rank-reduction and knowledge-distillation heuristics can be made theoretically principled, and how the method behaves under more extreme task distributions or very long task sequences.
