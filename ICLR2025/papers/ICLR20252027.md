## Problem

Existing multi-objective optimization methods (MGDA, linearization, reference-vector approaches) are designed for 2–3 objectives and fail to scale when the number of objectives n far exceeds the number of trainable solutions m (n ≫ m). In such settings, no single model can be Pareto-optimal for all objectives, and prior methods either collapse to a small subset of objectives or become computationally infeasible, leaving many objectives uncovered or poorly served.

## Input / Output

The method takes n empirical loss functions (one per objective, e.g., per client, task, or training instance), m model parameter vectors to optimize, and two tunable marginal distributions α ∈ Δⁿ and β ∈ Δᵐ that control the transport constraints. It produces m diverse Pareto-stationary models along with an optimal transport matrix Γ ∈ ℝ₊^{n×m} that encodes which objectives each model specializes in. Unlike single-solution MOO, the output is a set of complementary experts: at inference, each objective (client/task/instance) selects its best-performing model from the m solutions, and the sparsity of Γ ensures each model focuses on a disjoint subset of objectives.

## Method

MosT formulates the problem as a bi-level optimization: the lower level solves an optimal transport problem to find a balanced matching Γ between n objectives and m solutions, subject to marginal constraints Γ1_m = α and Γᵀ1_n = β plus a diversity regularizer R(Γ) = −Σᵢ maxⱼ Γ_{i,j} that encourages sparse, non-overlapping assignments. The upper level runs a Γ-reweighted MGDA for each model θⱼ, where the OT weights rescale objective gradients so that well-optimized objectives do not dominate the common descent direction. The algorithm alternates between solving the OT (via IPOT) and performing K steps of reweighted MGDA (via a Frank-Wolfe min-norm solver), converging at O(ν/T) in the non-convex case and linearly in the strongly-convex case under a stability assumption on the matching. A practical curriculum progressively shifts from enforcing uniform β (models receive balanced training) to enforcing uniform α (all objectives are covered), preventing the collapse phenomenon where one model dominates all objectives. For the n ≪ m regime, MosT-E augments the n objectives with n′−n Dirichlet-sampled convex combinations to create n′ ≫ m synthetic objectives, enabling diverse coverage of the Pareto front.

## Results

On federated learning with 30 synthetic clients, MosT achieves 84.25% mean accuracy, outperforming MGDA (77.22%), FedMGDA+ (75.26%), and FedAvg (75.71%) by 6–9 percentage points. On the real-world FEMNIST dataset (206 clients), MosT reaches 81.16% vs. 78.86% for MGDA and 80.08% for FedMGDA+. In multi-task learning, MosT attains 82.98% on Office-Caltech10 and 67.65% on DomainNet, surpassing the strongest baselines (MGDA at 80.74% and 65.81%) while methods like EPO, COSMOS, and TAG lag by 10–30 points. Runtime is comparable to baselines (e.g., 217.6s vs. 219.9s for MGDA on Syn(0.0)), with OT computation accounting for <1% of total time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MosT consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every application. Against MGDA (run independently m times), MosT gains 6–9 points on synthetic FL, 2.2 points on FEMNIST, 2.2 points on Office-Caltech10, and 4.3 points on BoolQ. Among federated learning baselines, FedMGDA+ is the strongest competitor (80.08% on FEMNIST) but still trails MosT by 1.1 points; FedAvg and FedProx are 8–9 points behind. In multi-task learning, EPO (61.05%), COSMOS (63.83%), and TAG (49.38%) on Office-Caltech10 are all substantially outperformed by MosT (82.98%), with TAG showing particularly poor performance. On ZDT bi-objective benchmarks, MosT achieves the highest hypervolume (5.87 on ZDT-1, 6.88 on ZDT-2, 6.39 on ZDT-3) versus SVGD (5.54, 6.65, 5.77) and EPO (4.40, 6.65, 4.53), and even outperforms SVGD and EPO when those methods are given 20× more solutions (m=100 vs. m=5). Linearization-based MOO is the weakest baseline in most settings, confirming that simple weighted averaging cannot capture diverse Pareto trade-offs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-gradient descent algorithm (MGDA) from Désidéri (2012) from a single-solution to a multi-solution setting, introducing optimal transport as the mechanism for matching n objectives to m solutions. Unlike prior multi-solution MOO methods (EPO, SVGD, COSMOS, TAG) that rely on fixed preference vectors or stochastic exploration of the Pareto front, MosT formulates the objective-solution assignment as a bi-level optimization where the transport plan Γ is jointly optimized with model parameters. The approach also draws on mixture-of-experts routing ideas, but replaces learned gating with a principled OT-based matching that enforces balanced coverage via marginal constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MosT addresses a practical scaling gap: existing MOO methods degrade or become infeasible when objectives vastly outnumber solutions (n >> m), a regime common in federated learning, multi-task learning, and prompt learning. The convergence guarantees (linear in the strongly-convex case, O(ν/T) in the non-convex case) and the negligible OT overhead make the method deployable without extra compute. Open questions include whether the full-gradient assumption in the convergence proof can be relaxed for large-scale stochastic settings, whether the MosT-E extension (Dirichlet-sampled convex combinations) is the right way to handle n << m, and how the method behaves when objectives are highly correlated or adversarially structured.
