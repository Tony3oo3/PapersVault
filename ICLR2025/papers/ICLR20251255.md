## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks either rely on fixed-form VQA (short answers, multiple choice) that is easy to score but unrealistic, or use open-ended responses that loosely gauge task completion without testing precise compliance with layered, compositional instructions. The ability of MLLMs to strictly adhere to detailed, multi-constraint prompts—such as answering in exactly two sentences while writing a poem from a specific perspective—remains largely unmeasured in the multimodal setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251255/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item pairs an image (sourced from COCO 2017, SBU, TextVQA, and Flickr, covering animals, food, landmarks, art, text, etc.) with a manually crafted prompt containing 2–5 sub-instructions drawn from 8 categories (description, length limit, mention, genre, grammar, math, perspective, OCR). The model produces a free-form text response, which is scored 0–10 by GPT-4o as judge using a weighted rubric where the description sub-instruction receives the largest weight (6 points for two sub-instructions, 4 for three or more) and all weights sum to 10.

## Method

MIA-Bench comprises 400 image-prompt pairs constructed under three design principles (correctness, no answer leakage, image-dependence) and spanning five complexity levels (basic through complex). Evaluation decomposes each prompt into individual sub-instructions, assigns per-component weights, and uses GPT-4o with a structured grading prompt to produce per-component and total scores; ranking stability is verified across four different GPT-4o judge versions. To improve instruction adherence, the authors construct a Diverse Instruction-Tuning (DIT) dataset of 5,000 image-prompt pairs by prompting GPT-4v to generate five difficulty-graded instructions per image from 1,000 COCO images, manually cleaning the output, and using GPT-4v to produce ground-truth responses (90% verified compliant). Supervised fine-tuning LLaVA-NeXT-13b on this DIT data for one epoch yields a ~10-point gain on MIA-Bench (69.16 → 78.85) with only minor regressions on other benchmarks, demonstrating that targeted instruction-adherence data can close the gap without sacrificing general multimodal capability.

## Results

GPT-4o achieves the highest overall MIA-Bench score of 88.58, leading in 7 of 8 sub-instruction categories (Description 90.82, Length Limit 92.73, Genre 94.29, Grammar 85.70, Mention 90.66, Math 87.07, Perspective 92.50, OCR 86.54). Among open-source models, LLaVA-NeXT-110b scores 79.84, MiniCPM-Llama3-v2.5 scores 76.27, and Phi-3-vision scores 76.02, while the weakest model (Fuyu-8b) scores only 24.52. Supervised fine-tuning with 5,000 diverse instruction-tuning (DIT) pairs improves LLaVA-NeXT-13b from 69.16 to 78.85 on MIA-Bench (+9.69 points) with only minor regressions on other benchmarks (e.g., MMBench drops from 70.6 to 68.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o (88.58) outperforms GPT-4v (86.11) by 2.47 points and Claude-3-Opus (84.50) by 4.08 points overall, with the largest gap in Perspective (92.50 vs. 59.17 for GPT-4v and 65.00 for Claude-3-Opus). Reka-Core achieves the highest Description score (91.05) but trails GPT-4o in Length Limit (79.91 vs. 92.73) and Perspective (77.50 vs. 92.50). Among open-source models, LLaVA-NeXT-110b (79.84) leads, followed by MiniCPM-Llama3-v2.5 (76.27) and Phi-3-vision (76.02), while smaller models like Fuyu-8b (24.52) and Kosmos-2 (26.06) struggle significantly across all categories. A key finding is that MIA-Bench rankings diverge sharply from other benchmarks: InternVL-Chat-V1.5 holds the highest meta-ranking on MME/MMMU/MMBench/MMVet/HallusionBench/MathVista but ranks last among the top-5 on MIA-Bench (75.42), while Claude-3-Opus, the lowest on those benchmarks, ranks 3rd on MIA-Bench (84.50). SFT with 5K DIT pairs lifts LLaVA-NeXT-13b from 69.16 to 78.85 on MIA-Bench, surpassing LLaVA-NeXT-34b (75.61) without the 34B parameter scale, though at a small cost on MMBench (70.6→68.6) and TextVQA (64.26→63.20).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-following evaluation paradigm established for text-only LLMs (IFEval, InfoBench) to the multimodal setting, where prior multimodal benchmarks (MME, MMMU, MMBench, LLaVA-Bench) have only loosely assessed instruction compliance through open-ended or multiple-choice formats. The authors explicitly position MIA-Bench as the first known effort to rigorously benchmark strict, layered, and compositional instruction adherence in MLLMs, departing from the broader 'visual understanding' framing of existing benchmarks. The SFT component builds on the visual instruction tuning lineage (LLaVA, MiniGPT-4) but targets a narrower objective: compliance with explicit format and content constraints rather than general multimodal capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical finding is that strong performance on established multimodal benchmarks does not predict strict instruction adherence — InternVL-Chat-V1.5, the top meta-ranked model on MME/MMMU/MMBench, ranks last on MIA-Bench, while Claude-3-Opus (lowest meta-ranking) ranks third. This suggests that instruction fidelity is a distinct axis of capability that current evaluation suites miss. The SFT experiment showing ~10-point improvement with only 5,000 targeted pairs is encouraging but was validated on a single model family (LLaVA-NeXT-13b), leaving open whether the approach generalizes. The benchmark's small size (400 pairs) and reliance on GPT-4o as judge (despite cross-validation with Claude-3) are acknowledged limitations that constrain its comprehensiveness.
