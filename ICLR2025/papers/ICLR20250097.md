## Problem

Existing approximate machine unlearning methods lack formal guarantees on the utility-complexity trade-offs, and the theoretical understanding of how many samples can be deleted under fixed utility and computation budgets remains incomplete. In particular, the out-of-distribution case—where forget data may arbitrarily deviate from the test distribution—was entirely unexplored, and it was an open question whether unlearning could achieve dimension-independent deletion capacity, separating it from differential privacy-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The unlearning procedure takes a trained model θ_S^A (produced by an optimization oracle on the full dataset S), a forget set S_f of size f, and the retain set S \ S_f, and outputs an unlearned model θ^U that is (q, qε)-indistinguishable in Rényi divergence from a model trained from scratch on the retain set. A key structural detail is that the unlearning phase is initialized at the trained model rather than from scratch, and the final output includes additive Gaussian noise N(0, α_emp/(2Ld) I_d) to certify the unlearning guarantee. In the OOD variant, the training phase itself is modified to use robust gradient estimation, making the initialization insensitive to the forget data.

## Method

For in-distribution unlearning, Algorithm 1 combines any approximate empirical risk minimizer (e.g., gradient descent) with output perturbation: the model is refined to precision O(α_emp·ε/(Ld)) on the retain set, then Gaussian noise is added, yielding (q, qε)-approximate unlearning with dimension-independent utility deletion capacity Ω(n√α). For out-of-distribution unlearning, Algorithm 2 introduces a robust training phase using the coordinate-wise trimmed mean of per-sample gradients (trimming f values per coordinate), which provably converges to the retain-set minimizer up to an error proportional to the interpolation error and f/n, regardless of the forget data. This robust initialization ensures the unlearning phase has near-linear time complexity O(nd·log(1 + d·f·E(S_r)/(αε·n))) independent of the forget data, providing the first theoretical defense against slow-down (denial-of-service) attacks on certified unlearning.

## Results

For in-distribution unlearning, Algorithm 1 (gradient descent + output perturbation) achieves a utility deletion capacity of Ω(n√α), which is dimension-independent and matches the lower bound from robust mean estimation, resolving an open question by Sekhari et al. (2021). The computational deletion capacity scales as Ω(n·αε·exp(T/2nd)/(Rd‖θ₀−θ_S*‖)), growing exponentially with the time budget T. For out-of-distribution unlearning, Algorithm 2 (robust trimmed-mean training + output perturbation) achieves a utility deletion capacity of Ω(n) and a computational deletion capacity of Ω(n·α²ε²·exp(T/nd)/(E(S_r)·d²‖θ₀−θ_S*‖²)), which is provably independent of the forget data, providing the first theoretical defense against slow-down attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Newton-step method of Sekhari et al. (2021), Algorithm 1 with gradient descent strictly dominates on both utility and computational deletion capacity: the utility capacity is dimension-independent (Ω(n√α)) versus Sekhari's Ω̃(n·d^{−1/4}·α^{1/4}·ε^{1/4}), and the computational capacity is near-linear in n versus Sekhari's zero unless T ≥ Ω(nd² + d^{2.38}). Against 'lazy' differential privacy (Huang and Canonne, 2023), Algorithm 1 removes the 1/√d penalty in utility capacity (Ω(n√α) vs. Θ̃(n√(αε/d))) and reduces the computational threshold from Ω(n²d) to near-linear, establishing a tight separation between unlearning and DP. Figure 1a empirically confirms this separation on a least-squares regression task: DP-SGD error grows near-linearly with dimension d, while Algorithm 1's error remains flat. For out-of-distribution data (Table 2), Algorithm 2 is the only method with a non-zero computational deletion capacity in the non-Lipschitz setting, whereas Algorithm 1's capacity drops to zero because the unlearning time can be unbounded for a single adversarial forget sample (Proposition 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the certified unlearning framework of Neel et al. (2021) and Guo et al. (2020) by showing that the simpler gradient descent + output perturbation approach (rather than the Newton step of Sekhari et al., 2021) achieves dimension-independent in-distribution deletion capacity, resolving an open theoretical question. It introduces a computational deletion capacity dimension to the utility-based formalism of Sekhari et al. (2021), and for the out-of-distribution setting, it repurposes the coordinate-wise trimmed mean from robust statistics (Lugosi and Mendelson, 2019) as a training-time mechanism to ensure robust initialization for unlearning. The OOD unlearning objective itself is explicitly framed as previously unstudied in the theoretical unlearning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dimension-independent deletion capacity result establishes a tight separation from differential privacy-based unlearning (which degrades polynomially in model dimension), providing practitioners with a clear theoretical justification for preferring unlearning-specific methods over DP for data deletion. The OOD robustness result is the first theoretical guarantee against slow-down/denial-of-service attacks on certified unlearning (Marchant et al., 2022), addressing a practical vulnerability. Key limitations include the strong convexity and unique minimizer assumptions, which restrict applicability to deep learning; open questions include unified upper bounds on deletion capacities and extension to non-convex, high-dimensional models.
