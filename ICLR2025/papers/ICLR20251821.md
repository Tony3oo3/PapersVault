## Problem

Continuous-time consistency models (CMs) eliminate the discretization errors inherent in discrete-time variants, but prior work found them to suffer from severe training instability that prevented scaling beyond small model sizes. This paper identifies the root causes of that instability—primarily in the time-derivative component of the tangent function used for supervision—and provides a complete, theoretically motivated recipe to fix them, enabling continuous-time CMs to be trained at an unprecedented 1.5B parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251821/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a noisy sample x_t = cos(t)·x_0 + sin(t)·z (with z ~ N(0, σ_d²I)) and a continuous time value t ∈ [0, π/2], optionally conditioned on a class label, and directly predicts the clean image x_0 in one or two forward passes. For ImageNet 512×512, generation operates in the latent space of a pretrained VAE (following EDM2), while CIFAR-10 (32×32) and ImageNet 64×64 work in pixel space. Two-step sampling uses a fixed intermediate timestep t=1.1, and conditional ImageNet 512×512 models additionally take a guidance scale s ∈ [1,2] as input to incorporate classifier-free guidance during distillation.

## Method

The paper introduces TrigFlow, a diffusion formulation x_t = cos(t)·x_0 + sin(t)·z that unifies EDM's unit-variance principle with flow matching, yielding simple closed-form expressions for the PF-ODE, diffusion objective, and CM parameterization f_θ(x_t,t) = cos(t)·x_t − sin(t)·σ_d·F_θ(x_t/σ_d, t). To stabilize continuous-time CM training, the paper identifies that instability originates from the time-derivative term sin(t)·∂_t F_θ and addresses it through: identity time conditioning c_noise(t)=t (avoiding the 1/cos(t) blowup near t=π/2), positional embeddings instead of high-frequency Fourier embeddings, adaptive double normalization replacing AdaGN, tangent normalization (dividing df/dt by ‖df/dt‖+0.1), and a jointly trained adaptive weighting function. For large-scale FP16 training, they rearrange the JVP computation to prevent intermediate overflow and implement a Flash-Attention-compatible JVP algorithm. The resulting sCM is trained via consistency distillation (sCD) from a pretrained EDM2 teacher or consistency training (sCT) from scratch, scaling predictably across model sizes from 280M to 1.5B parameters.

## Results

The 2-step sCD-XXL model (1.5B params) achieves FID 1.88 on ImageNet 512×512, narrowing the gap to the teacher EDM2-XXL (FID 1.73, 63×2 NFE) to within 10%. On CIFAR-10, 2-step sCT achieves FID 2.06 (teacher EDM FID 2.01), and on ImageNet 64×64, 2-step sCD achieves FID 1.48 (teacher FID 1.33). The 1-step sCD-XXL (FID 4.29) already surpasses StyleGAN-XL (FID 2.41) and VAR (FID 2.63) on ImageNet 512×512, and the 2-step result outperforms all non-diffusion generative models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, 2-step sCT (FID 2.06) outperforms all previous consistency training methods including iCT-deep (2.24) and ECT (2.11), and 2-step sCD (FID 2.52) beats CD (2.93), TRACT (3.32), and Consistency-FM (5.34). On ImageNet 512×512, 2-step sCD-XXL (FID 1.88) surpasses all GAN and masked-model baselines (StyleGAN-XL 2.41, VAR 2.63, MAR 1.73) and approaches the teacher diffusion model EDM2-XXL (1.81 at 63×2 NFE) within 10%. The 1-step sCD-XXL (FID 4.29) already exceeds StyleGAN-XL and VAR, demonstrating that even single-step generation is competitive. In the VSD comparison (Figure 7), sCD maintains comparable precision and recall to the teacher diffusion model across guidance scales, while VSD suffers from mode collapse (decreasing recall) at higher guidance levels. sCD scales predictably with model size, maintaining a constant FID ratio offset relative to the teacher, whereas sCT shows increased variance at larger scales (particularly in latent spaces).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the consistency model framework (Song et al., 2023; Song & Dhariwal, 2023) and the EDM parameterization (Karras et al., 2022), proposing TrigFlow as a unifying reformulation that subsumes both EDM and Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) under a single trigonometric parameterization. It departs from prior continuous-time CM work (Song et al., 2023; Geng et al., 2024) by diagnosing the root cause of training instability as the time-derivative term in the tangent function, then providing a complete stabilization recipe (identity time transformation, positional embeddings, adaptive double normalization, tangent normalization, adaptive weighting). The adaptive weighting mechanism is adopted from EDM2 (Karras et al., 2024), and the JVP computation extends Flash Attention (Dao et al., 2022) to support forward-mode differentiation in attention layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to train continuous-time consistency models at 1.5B parameter scale, narrowing the FID gap with state-of-the-art diffusion models (requiring 63+ sampling steps) to within 10% using only 2 steps. The TrigFlow formulation is likely to become a standard reference for diffusion model parameterization due to its simplicity and unification of EDM/Flow Matching. Key open questions remain: sCT underperforms sCD in latent spaces (suggesting encoder/decoder design matters for CMs), sCD still depends on a pretrained teacher, and the paper acknowledges that FID may not capture all semantic quality at high resolution. The finding that VSD suffers mode collapse at high guidance while sCD does not has practical implications for guided generation pipelines.
