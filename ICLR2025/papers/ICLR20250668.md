## Problem

Entropy minimization (EM), the dominant learning objective in test-time adaptation, greedily drives all test samples toward near-zero entropy, causing overconfident predictions and frequent model collapse when the model encounters unreliable or out-of-distribution samples. Prior work has patched this with sample filtering, constrained optimization, or model recovery heuristics, but the inherent overconfidence of the EM objective itself remains unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250668/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled test images in mini-batches (size 64) alongside a pretrained classifier (ViT-Base or ResNet-50) whose normalization-layer parameters are updated online. Instead of a single softmax probability vector, the output is a subjective opinion: K belief masses plus one uncertainty mass that sums to 1, allowing the model to explicitly express 'I do not know' by concentrating mass on the uncertainty component. The adapted model parameters are updated per batch without access to any training data.

## Method

COME replaces the standard softmax-entropy loss with the entropy of a Dirichlet-based subjective opinion, where model logits are transformed via exp(ReLU(·)) into Dirichlet concentration parameters, yielding per-class belief masses and a global uncertainty mass u = K/S. Minimizing the opinion entropy gives the model an additional degree of freedom: it can reduce entropy by either sharpening beliefs or by increasing the uncertainty mass, thereby rejecting unreliable samples rather than forcing a confident (potentially wrong) prediction. To prevent the pretrained model's overconfidence from dominating, COME constrains the uncertainty mass to stay within δ of the pretrained model's value, implemented by factorizing the logits as f(x)/‖f(x)‖_p · ‖f(x)‖_p^{no_grad} · τ so that gradient descent on the opinion entropy cannot alter the p-norm. Theoretically, this yields a data-adaptive upper bound on model confidence that tightens as the pretrained model's uncertainty u₀ increases, provably preventing the collapse-to-trivial-solution behavior of vanilla EM.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity level 5), COME consistently improves all EM-based TTA baselines, with the most dramatic gains on weather corruptions: SAR+COME achieves 65.3% on Snow vs. 22.2% for SAR+EM (a 43.1-point improvement), and Tent+COME reaches 58.4% on Snow vs. 10.5% for Tent+EM. Across all 15 corruptions, COME-equipped SAR averages 64.2% accuracy versus 54.1% for SAR+EM. In open-world TTA, COME reduces false positive rate by up to 16.3 points (EATA+COME on Places: 45.6% vs. 61.9%), and in lifelong TTA, SAR+COME recovers from near-zero accuracy (0.1%) on several digital corruptions to 66–72%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COME is a drop-in replacement for the EM loss, so the primary comparisons are each EM-based method with vs. without COME. The largest gains appear with SAR (up to +43.1 pts on Snow) and Tent (up to +47.9 pts on Snow), where EM-based methods suffer severe model collapse; EATA and CoTTA see more modest but consistent gains of 0.1–5.4 pts. Against non-EM baselines, COME-equipped methods dominate: SAR+COME (64.2% avg) outperforms FOA (57.5%), TEA (48.5%), T3A (43.5%), LAME (42.5%), and PL (53.5%) on ImageNet-C level 5. In the open-world setting, COME-equipped EATA achieves the lowest FPR (45.6% on Places), beating FOA (61.4%), TEA (67.8%), and T3A (69.0%). In the source-free domain adaptation ablation (Table 10), COME-equipped methods (Tent, EATA, SAR) outperform IM (65.1% at epoch 3) and PL/EM which collapse entirely. Against the Bayesian TTA method BACS (Zhou & Levine, 2021), COME-equipped EATA achieves 58.1% average accuracy vs. BACS's 56.1% on ImageNet-C averaged over all corruptions and levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

COME positions itself as a principled drop-in replacement for entropy minimization (EM), the dominant learning objective in TTA methods like Tent, EATA, SAR, and CoTTA. It draws on two distinct lineages: evidential deep learning (Sensoy et al., 2018; Malinin & Gales, 2018) for Dirichlet-based subjective opinion uncertainty modeling, and logit normalization (Wei et al., 2022) for the norm-constrained optimization that regularizes the uncertainty mass. Unlike prior TTA work that patches EM's instability with sample filtering, model recovery, or constrained parameter updates, COME addresses the root cause by reformulating the objective itself to explicitly represent 'I do not know' as a valid prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a few lines of code change (replacing softmax entropy with opinion entropy plus a detached norm constraint) consistently prevents model collapse across standard, open-world, and lifelong TTA settings, with the largest gains on severe corruptions where EM catastrophically fails. The ablation revealing that the uncertainty constraint (norm regularization) contributes far more than the subjective-logic reformulation alone is a useful diagnostic for practitioners. Key limitations include reliance on the pretrained model's uncertainty being reasonably calibrated (the constraint anchors to u_θ₀), and the method has not been validated on modern vision foundation models like DINOv2, which the authors identify as a promising future direction.
