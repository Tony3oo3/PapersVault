## Problem

Existing Text-to-3D methods either rely on slow iterative optimization (minutes to hours per object) or require complex 3D-aware architectures trained from scratch on expensive 3D data, while also necessitating lossy post-processing steps like iso-surface extraction and UV unwrapping. The scarcity of high-quality 3D training data and the irregular graph structure of meshes make it difficult to achieve both fast generation and strong generalization simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt and produces a triangulated 3D mesh (up to 589,824 vertices at 768×768 geometry image resolution) with a jointly generated UV atlas and albedo texture in under 10 seconds. Unlike methods that output implicit representations requiring marching cubes, the output is a direct quad-mesh triangulated from the geometry image, with the UV layout learned from training data rather than computed by a separate unwrapping algorithm. The albedo textures are free of baked lighting, making them directly usable in PBR graphics pipelines.

## Method

GIMDiffusion represents 3D surfaces as multi-chart geometry images (2D images encoding 3D vertex positions) and applies a Collaborative Control scheme with two parallel diffusion models: a frozen zero-terminal-SNR Stable Diffusion v2.1 generates UV-space albedo textures, while a new architectural clone (omitting cross-attention layers) generates geometry images, with the two connected by linear cross-network communication layers for bidirectional information sharing. A custom VAE with an additional multi-chart mask channel, 8-channel latent space, and no GAN/LPIPS losses handles geometry image compression to preserve sharp chart boundaries. Training uses ~100K curated Objaverse objects with existing UV maps providing semantically meaningful chart boundaries, and the cylindrical coordinate transform resolves rotational ambiguity. The key insight is that handcrafted UV maps from training data naturally partition objects into editable parts, and the 2D image-based representation allows full reuse of powerful pre-trained Text-to-Image priors without any 3D-specific architecture.

## Results

On T³ Bench (Single Object track, 100 prompts), GIMDiffusion achieves a score of 35.2, competitive with CRM (39.6) but below SF3D (48.2) and MVDream (47.8). Generation time is 10 seconds per object, comparable to CRM (13 s) and SF3D (3.5 s), and dramatically faster than optimization-based methods (7–30 minutes). For intrinsic mesh quality, GIMDiffusion reports AR>4 = 0.038, RR>4 = 0.042, and MA<10 = 0.035, outperforming LGM and SF3D but trailing CRM (0.012/0.017/0.013). Crucially, GIMDiffusion is the only evaluated method that simultaneously produces relightable albedo textures, generative UV unwrapping, and semantically separable parts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIMDiffusion trails the top T³ Bench scorers—SF3D (48.2) and MVDream (47.8)—by 13 and 13 points respectively, and CRM (39.6) by 4.4 points, indicating that its reliance on a frozen 2D prior limits prompt-alignment quality relative to methods with stronger 3D-aware training. However, GIMDiffusion is the only method that is both relightable and produces separable parts, capabilities that no other baseline offers. On intrinsic mesh quality, GIMDiffusion (AR>4 = 0.038) substantially outperforms SF3D (0.112) and LGM (0.068) but is outperformed by CRM (0.012), suggesting CRM's convolutional reconstruction yields cleaner triangulation. In terms of speed, GIMDiffusion (10 s) is competitive with CRM (13 s) and SF3D (3.5 s) while being orders of magnitude faster than optimization-based baselines like MVDream (30 min) and GaussianDreamer (15 min). The paper also notes that Image-to-3D baselines (LGM, CRM, SF3D) were adapted to Text-to-3D by prepending a 3-second image generation step, making the effective comparison fairer than raw generation times suggest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes geometry images—a 2002 surface representation largely overlooked in deep learning—as the core 3D encoding for a diffusion-based Text-to-3D pipeline, avoiding the 3D-aware architectures (neural implicit fields, triplanes, Gaussian splats) that dominate prior feed-forward methods. The key architectural dependency is Collaborative Control (Vainer et al., 2024), a bidirectional communication scheme originally designed for PBR texture generation, here extended to jointly produce albedo textures and geometry images from a frozen Stable Diffusion base model. Unlike optimization-based approaches (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer) that iteratively refine 3D representations via score distillation, or feed-forward methods (LGM, CRM, SF3D) that require expensive 3D training data and post-processing (marching cubes, XAtlas unwrapping), GIMDiffusion operates entirely in 2D image space and jointly outputs the UV layout, eliminating both the 3D architecture and the unwrapping step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for game and graphics pipelines: generating relightable, UV-unwrapped, semantically-separable 3D assets in ~10 seconds addresses a real production bottleneck, and the joint UV map generation is, to the authors' knowledge, the first in Text-to-3D. However, the T³ Bench quality score (35.2) trails several baselines (SF3D: 48.2, MVDream: 47.8), and visible cracks at chart boundaries remain a persistent artifact stemming from the lack of inter-chart connectivity in multi-chart geometry images. Open questions include whether the crack problem can be solved via generalized zippering or wavelet-based VAEs, how to establish canonical chart orientations to align with the 2D prior's rotational biases, and whether topology prediction and polygon-budget conditioning can make the output directly production-ready.
