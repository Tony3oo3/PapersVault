## Problem

Existing training-free guidance methods for generative models operate exclusively on continuous data (e.g., images), while many scientific applications such as molecular design require joint modeling of discrete variables (atom types) and continuous variables (3D coordinates). Additionally, guided generation within the flow matching framework—now a dominant paradigm for generative foundation models—remains largely unexplored, with no principled way to steer multimodal flows toward target properties without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained unconditional Multimodal Flow model (Multiflow with EGNN backbone) and an off-the-shelf, time-independent target property predictor f_c(G_1) (e.g., an EGNN-based classifier or regressor for molecular properties), along with a scalar or vector target value c. At inference, it produces a 3D molecule G_1 = (X_1, a_1) where X_1 ∈ R^{3×n} are atomic coordinates (projected to the translation-invariant simplex Γ) and a_1 ∈ A^n are atom types drawn from a finite alphabet including a mask state. No additional training or fine-tuning of either the flow model or the predictor is required; the predictor is used purely as a scoring function during the iterative flow simulation.

## Method

TFG-Flow constructs a guided flow p_t(G_t|c) that provably preserves the marginals of the original unguided flow while aligning the terminal distribution with the target predictor (Theorem 3.1). For the discrete component (atom types), it estimates the guided Kolmogorov rate matrix via a weighted Monte Carlo scheme: K samples are drawn from the flow model's posterior p_{1|t}(G_1|G_t), and the rate matrix is computed as a ratio of expectations weighted by f_c(G_1), reducing the exact computation from O(|A|^n) to O(log(n|A|)) samples with high-probability guarantees (Theorem 3.4). For the continuous component (coordinates), it performs N_iter steps of gradient ascent on log f_c(E[X_1|X_t, a_t]) with respect to X_t, projecting back to the simplex Γ after each step to maintain translation invariance, then uses the updated X_t to estimate the guided Fokker-Planck velocity. SO(3)-invariance of the entire simulated flow is guaranteed by combining an equivariant EGNN backbone with an invariant target predictor (Theorem 3.5). A temperature parameter τ modulates the sharpness of the discrete guidance by softening the predictor's softmax output.

## Results

On QM9 quantum property guidance, TFG-Flow achieves the best MAE among all training-free methods across all six properties (e.g., 1.75 for Cv, 0.817 for μ, 2.32 for α), with an average relative improvement of +20.3% over the best continuous-diffusion training-free baseline TFG. On combined property guidance (Table 2), TFG-Flow significantly outperforms the conditional flow baseline Cond-Flow (e.g., MAE 2.36 vs 4.96 for Cv+μ; 4.62 vs 9.33 for α+μ). For structure guidance, TFG-Flow improves Tanimoto similarity over unconditional generation by +76.83% on QM9 and +22.35% on GEOM-Drug. On pocket-targeted drug design (CrossDocked2020), TFG-Flow achieves the best Vina score (−7.65) among all methods, surpassing TargetDiff (−7.32) and unguided Multiflow (−7.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFG-Flow outperforms all six continuous-diffusion training-free baselines (DPS, LGD, FreeDoM, MPGD, UGD, TFG) on every quantum property, with the largest gains over DPS (which fails catastrophically on α with MAE 51169) and consistent ~30–50% MAE reductions over the strongest baseline TFG. Against the training-based conditional flow (Cond-Flow), TFG-Flow is comparable on single-property guidance (e.g., μ: 0.817 vs 0.962) but substantially better on combined properties (e.g., α+μ: 4.62 vs 9.33 MAE), despite requiring no conditional training data. TFG-Flow still trails the training-based EEGSDE (which uses both conditional training and energy-guided SDE) on most properties, confirming a remaining gap between training-free and training-based guidance. For pocket-targeted drug design, TFG-Flow achieves the best Vina score (−7.65) over TargetDiff (−7.32), Pocket2Mol (−7.23), AR (−6.20), and unguided Multiflow (−7.01), though autoregressive methods (AR, Pocket2Mol) retain an edge on QED and SA scores. On structure guidance, TFG-Flow improves Tanimoto similarity by +76.83% (QM9) and +22.35% (GEOM-Drug) over unconditional generation, though absolute similarity values (0.290, 0.208) remain modest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free guidance paradigm from continuous diffusion models (DPS, LGD, FreeDoM, MPGD, UGD, TFG) to the multimodal flow matching setting, where data comprises both discrete (atom types) and continuous (3D coordinates) variables. It builds directly on Multiflow (Campbell et al., 2024) as the underlying generative model and adapts the gradient-ascent continuous guidance strategy from TFG (Ye et al., 2024), while introducing a novel Monte Carlo-based discrete guidance mechanism that avoids the curse of dimensionality. The rectified flow formulation (Liu et al., 2022) provides the straight-line conditional paths that make the velocity and rate matrix derivations tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TFG-Flow provides the first principled framework for training-free guidance in multimodal generative flow, unlocking plug-and-play property steering for any flow model with mixed discrete-continuous data (molecules, materials, proteins). The theoretical guarantees—particularly that O(log(n|A|)) Monte Carlo samples suffice for discrete guidance and that SO(3)-invariance is preserved—give practitioners confidence in the method's correctness. However, a significant performance gap with training-based methods (e.g., EEGSDE) persists, and structure-guidance results (Tanimoto ~0.21–0.29) remain unsatisfactory, suggesting that training-free guidance for multimodal flow is still in its early stages and will benefit from stronger foundation models.
