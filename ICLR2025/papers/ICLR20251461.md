## Problem

Neural displacement priors for IMU-only odometry must generalize across arbitrary IMU mount orientations and motion directions, but existing methods (TLIO, RONIN, RIO) enforce only approximate equivariance via data augmentation or auxiliary consistency losses, leaving residual trajectory variations under rotated inputs. No prior work achieves strict equivariance by design, and none addresses reflection equivariance, which requires special handling of angular rates that must invert under reflection to preserve the right-hand rule.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of gravity-aligned IMU measurements (accelerations a_i and angular velocities ω_i, each in R³) expressed in a local gravity-aligned frame, with the z-axis aligned to gravity. For the TLIO variant, it outputs a 3D displacement vector and a full 3×3 covariance matrix (constructed from a diagonal covariance in the canonical frame mapped back via the learned frame); for the RONIN variant, it outputs a 2D velocity. A key preprocessing step decomposes each angular rate ω into two perpendicular vectors v₁, v₂ via a bijection so that all three vector quantities (a, v₁, v₂) transform identically under roto-reflections, enabling unified equivariant processing.

## Method

EqNIO introduces a canonicalization scheme that maps IMU data into a learnable canonical frame F ∈ O_g(3) (a gravity-preserving roto-reflection) before passing it to a conventional displacement-prior network, then maps outputs back to the original frame. The frame network Ψ is designed to be strictly O_g(3)-equivariant (equivalently O(2)-equivariant, since O_g(3) ≅ O(2)), ensuring that canonical inputs are invariant under any roto-reflection of the trajectory. To achieve this, the paper tailors O(2)-equivariant linear layers (2D vector neurons with weights W₁I for O(2) or W₁I + W₂R₉₀ for SO(2)), equivariant 1D convolutions over time, and a gated nonlinearity that mixes scalar (z-components, norms, dot-products) and vector (xy-components) features. The angular-rate decomposition ω = v₁ × v₂ resolves the representation mismatch between accelerations and angular rates under reflections. The framework is architecture-agnostic and is demonstrated by plugging into both the TLIO (EKF + NN) and RONIN (end-to-end NN) pipelines without modifying their backbones.

## Results

On the TLIO architecture, EqNIO with O(2) equivariant frame achieves 2.982 MSE* (×10⁻² m²) and 2.382 ATE* (m) on the TLIO dataset, outperforming baseline TLIO (3.333 MSE*, 3.079 ATE*) by 57% and 12% respectively. On the Aria dataset, the improvement is even more dramatic: 2.304 MSE* vs. 15.248 for TLIO (85% reduction). On the RONIN architecture, O(2) Eq. Frame achieves 4.42 ATE* and 3.95 RTE* on RONIN-U (vs. 5.57/4.38 for RONIN), and 1.28 ATE* / 2.10 RTE* on OxIOD, outperforming RIO's best variant (+J+TTT: 2.96 ATE*, 3.74 RTE*) by 56% and 43%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EqNIO consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and metric. Against TLIO (Liu et al., 2020), the O(2) equivariant frame reduces MSE* by 57% (2.982 vs. 3.333) and ATE* by 12% (2.382 vs. 3.079) on the TLIO dataset, and by 85% on MSE* (2.304 vs. 15.248) on the Aria dataset, demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization. Against RONIN (Herath et al., 2020), EqNIO improves ATE* by 14% (4.42 vs. 5.57) and RTE* by 9% (3.95 vs. 4.38) on RONIN-U, and by 64% on ATE* (1.28 vs. 3.52) on OxIOD. Against RIO (Cao et al., 2022), which uses auxiliary equivariance losses and adaptive test-time training, EqNIO achieves 56% lower ATE* (1.28 vs. 2.96) and 43% lower RTE* (2.10 vs. 3.74) on OxIOD, while also being the only method that reports results on RIDI-C (0.70 ATE*, 1.41 RTE*). Notably, EqNIO requires no data augmentation, no auxiliary losses, and no test-time optimization, yet converges in 38 epochs versus over 100 for RONIN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural inertial odometry line (TLIO, RONIN, RIO) by replacing their approximate equivariance strategies—yaw data augmentation and auxiliary equivariance losses—with strict O(2) equivariance by design, using a learned canonicalization scheme inspired by Kaba et al. (2023). The frame-prediction network draws on the scalar/vector decomposition of Villar et al. (2021) and equivariant linear layer construction from Finzi et al. (2021), adapted to a 2D setting via the O_g(3) ≅ O(2) isomorphism. A key technical departure is the bijective decomposition of angular rates into two vectors that transform identically to accelerations under roto-reflections, resolving the right-hand-rule incompatibility that prevents joint processing of accelerometer and gyroscope data in an equivariant framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is architecture-agnostic (demonstrated on both filter-based TLIO and end-to-end RONIN), making it a drop-in improvement for existing neural inertial odometry pipelines, and the strict equivariance eliminates the need for costly data augmentation, auxiliary losses, or test-time ensembles. A notable finding is that a fully equivariant CNN underperforms the canonicalization-based approach, suggesting that leveraging invariant scalars through conventional backbones is more effective than enforcing equivariance throughout. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full 3D equivariance (beyond the gravity-aligned subgroup), how it behaves under significant IMU bias drift or non-gravitational accelerations, and whether the computational overhead of the equivariant frame network is acceptable on resource-constrained edge devices.
