## Problem

The Balanced Stable Marriage (BSM) problem seeks a stable matching that minimizes the maximum collective dissatisfaction across two sides of a market, but is NP-hard. Existing approaches either use heuristics (e.g., PowerBalance, DACC, BiLS) that find 'fair' matchings without guaranteeing optimality, or brute-force enumeration (ENUM) that becomes infeasible when the number of stable matchings grows exponentially. No prior method efficiently returns the exact optimal balanced stable marriage for practical instance sizes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a two-sided market instance with n agents per side and strict preference lists, from which a rotation graph G (a DAG with |R| rotation nodes and |E| edges) is constructed in O(n²) preprocessing. It outputs the exact balanced stable marriage μ* minimizing max{C_M(μ), C_W(μ)}, along with the optimal balance cost. The algorithm is also extended to the sex-equal stable marriage objective min|C_M − C_W|, returning the exact solution for that variant as well.

## Method

ISORROPIA performs local search on a pruned subset of the rotation graph's antichain space. It first partitions all rotations into R_M and R_W based on which side is disadvantaged in each rotation's r-related stable marriage, then defines two candidate rotation sets: R_◁ (rotations in R_W beyond the largest W-disadvantaged layer ℓ_W) and R_▷ (rotations in R_M whose parents are all in R_W). Three theorems prove that the exact optimum lies within three small antichain sets—A_I and A_II (antichains in A_W containing at least one rotation from R_◁) and A_III (single-rotation antichains from R_▷)—while the mixed set A_MW and the remaining portions of A_M and A_W can be safely discarded. A modified reverse-enumeration procedure (ENUM⁻) then searches only these three sets, reducing the explored search space from N total stable marriages to N_◁ + |R| + ℓ_W, yielding up to three orders of magnitude speedup over exact baselines while preserving optimality.

## Results

ISORROPIA finds the exact optimal balanced stable marriage on all tested instances, reducing the search space by a factor of 10¹ to 10⁴ compared to brute-force enumeration (ENUM⁻). On the Taxi dataset, ISORROPIA achieves up to a 10³× speedup over ENUM⁻; on Adm, the speedup is ~10¹×; on Hard instances, the speedup is modest (factor of ~1) due to the exponentially growing number of stable matchings. In terms of balance cost, ISORROPIA always matches the exact optimum, while heuristics (BILS, DACC, POWERBALANCE) exhibit gaps of up to 9× from the optimal balance cost as instance size grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ENUM⁻ (the only other exact method), ISORROPIA is faster by up to three orders of magnitude on Taxi (up to 10³×) and by ~10¹× on Adm, while matching ENUM⁻ exactly on balance cost whenever ENUM⁻ terminates within its 10⁷ enumeration cap. Against the three heuristics (BILS, DACC, POWERBALANCE), ISORROPIA always achieves the optimal balance cost, whereas the heuristics can be off by up to 9×; for example, on Adm with n=2500, ISORROPIA achieves balance cost 273,353 versus BILS 300,159 (+26,806), DACC 718,918 (+445,565), and POWERBALANCE 556,764 (+283,411). In raw runtime, ISORROPIA is faster than DACC and BILS but can be slower than the lightweight heuristics POWERBALANCE and HMS, which run in O(n²) or O(tn+kmn²) time without guaranteeing optimality. HMS performs very similarly to POWERBALANCE and is not separately reported in the figures. Overall, ISORROPIA is the only method that simultaneously guarantees the exact optimal balance cost and scales to instances with up to ~10⁶ stable matchings within a 600-second time limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the rotation graph representation of all stable marriages (Gusfield & Irving, 1989; Irving & Leather, 1986) and the Deferred Acceptance algorithm (Gale & Shapley, 1962), which together provide the structural foundation for navigating the space of stable matchings. It departs from prior work by exploiting local optimality properties of the balance cost under the domination relationship to prune the rotation graph into three small sets of promising antichains, enabling a local search that nonetheless guarantees the exact optimal solution. This contrasts with prior exact methods (brute-force enumeration) that scale poorly, and with heuristic approaches (DACC, PowerBalance, BILS, HMS) that offer no optimality guarantees for the balance cost objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing practical gap: BSM is NP-hard and no prior method efficiently returned exact solutions for realistic instance sizes (e.g., 10k agents in university admissions). ISORROPIA's up-to-three-orders-of-magnitude speedup over exact baselines, combined with its extension to sex-equal stable marriage, makes fair two-sided matching tractable for real-world deployment. Limitations include the still-potentially-exponential worst-case search (though empirically reduced to <50% of all stable marriages) and the restriction to strict preference lists. Open questions include whether the local-optimality pruning strategy generalizes to other fairness objectives (e.g., egalitarian cost) or to many-to-many matching variants.
