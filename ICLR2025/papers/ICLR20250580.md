## Problem

Existing symbolic regression methods rely on expression-tree representations and evolutionary search without incorporating the domain-specific scientific prior knowledge that human scientists use to formulate hypotheses, leading to inefficient exploration of the vast combinatorial equation space. While LLMs encode extensive scientific knowledge and code generation ability, prior LLM-based optimization frameworks lack mechanisms for integrating domain priors and maintaining diverse exploration, making them unsuitable for equation discovery.

## Input / Output

The method takes a tabular dataset of input-output observations (x_i, y_i) together with a natural language problem specification describing the scientific context, variable meanings, and physical constraints. The LLM generates equation hypotheses as executable Python program skeletons (functions with a params vector of placeholder coefficients), which are then fitted to data via differentiable optimization. The final output is a symbolic equation expressed as a Python program with optimized numeric parameters, evaluated by normalized MSE on both in-domain and out-of-domain test splits.

## Method

LLM-SR is an iterative hypothesis-refinement loop with three stages: (1) Hypothesis Generation, where the LLM samples equation program skeletons conditioned on a structured prompt containing the problem description, an evaluation function, and in-context examples from a dynamic experience buffer; (2) Data-driven Evaluation, where each skeleton's placeholder parameters are optimized via BFGS or Adam and scored as negative MSE; and (3) Experience Management, where a multi-island buffer (m=10 islands) stores high-scoring programs, periodically resets stagnant islands, and samples in-context examples via Boltzmann cluster selection favoring higher scores and shorter programs. The key design choice is decoupling discrete equation-structure search (handled by the LLM as a mutation/crossover operator) from continuous parameter fitting (handled by standard optimizers), with the program representation enabling direct differentiable optimization. Stochastic temperature-based sampling (τ=0.8) balances exploration of novel structures against exploitation of the LLM's scientific priors.

## Results

LLM-SR (GPT-3.5) achieves NMSE of 4.65e-7 (ID) and 0.0005 (OOD) on Oscillation 1, and 2.12e-7 (ID) and 3.81e-5 (OOD) on Oscillation 2, outperforming the best SR baseline (PySR: 0.0009/0.3106 and 0.0002/0.0098 respectively). On E. coli Growth, LLM-SR (Mixtral) reaches 0.0026 (ID) and 0.0037 (OOD), while the best baseline (PySR) scores 0.0376 (ID) and 1.0141 (OOD). On Stress-Strain, LLM-SR (GPT-3.5) achieves 0.0210 (ID) and 0.0516 (OOD) versus PySR's 0.0331 (ID) and 0.1304 (OOD). Notably, LLM-SR runs for only ~2.5K iterations versus 2M+ for search-based baselines, yet still achieves substantially lower error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-SR outperforms all eight baselines across all four benchmarks in both ID and OOD settings. The closest SR competitors are PySR and uDSR: on Oscillation 2, LLM-SR (GPT-3.5) achieves OOD NMSE of 3.81e-5 versus PySR's 0.0098 (a ~257× improvement) and uDSR's 0.0015 (~40× improvement). The OOD gap is most dramatic on E. coli Growth, where LLM-SR (Mixtral) scores 0.0037 OOD while the best baseline (PySR) scores 1.0141, indicating that LLM-SR's equations generalize far better to unseen conditions. Transformer-based SR models (NeSymReS, E2E) perform poorly, likely due to limited generalization from their pretraining distributions. Among LLM-based optimization baselines, both LMX (0.5031 ID / 48.93 OOD on Osc. 1) and FunSearch (0.4840 ID / 8.059 OOD) are substantially outperformed by LLM-SR, confirming that the skeleton+optimizer decomposition and multi-island experience management are critical design choices beyond generic LLM program search.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLM-SR adapts the LLM-as-program-search paradigm from FunSearch (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024) and the LLM crossover mechanism from LMX (Meyerson et al., 2023) to the specific domain of scientific equation discovery. It departs from traditional symbolic regression methods (GP-based PySR/GPlearn, RL-based DSR/uDSR, Transformer-based NeSymReS/E2E) by replacing blind evolutionary search over expression trees with LLM-generated equation program skeletons guided by natural-language scientific priors. The key methodological departure is the two-stage decomposition: the LLM proposes structural skeletons with placeholder parameters, and off-the-shelf optimizers (BFGS/Adam) handle continuous parameter fitting, a separation not present in prior LLM-optimization frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical evaluation flaw: standard Feynman benchmark problems are likely memorized by LLMs, invalidating them for assessing LLM-based discovery methods. The demonstrated OOD generalization gap (e.g., NMSE ~0.004 vs. >1 for baselines on E. coli growth) suggests that embedding scientific priors in the search process is not merely an efficiency gain but a fundamental quality improvement for extrapolation. Open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional or multi-equation systems, reducing the computational cost of iterative LLM queries, and whether the approach transfers to domains where no clean multiplicative or additive structure exists. The reliance on GPT-3.5/Mixtral as backbones also leaves open how much further gains are possible with stronger models.
