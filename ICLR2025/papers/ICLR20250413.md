## Problem

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting improves language model performance by generating intermediary reasoning tokens, but its mechanism is often conflated with simply re-applying a model with weight-tying (as in Universal Transformers). The paper identifies a critical distinction: in CoT, newly generated tokens can attend to all previous thought tokens, whereas weight-tying schemes do not grant this access. Additionally, prior adaptive-depth methods struggle with attending to tokens that halted early and with efficient batch processing, limiting their practicality for compute-constrained inference.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text sequence (context tokens) and, at inference time, a compute budget that determines the maximum number of model passes (repeats) per token. It produces standard next-token predictions for language modeling. Unusually, the compute budget is adjustable at inference without retraining: the user can choose how many repeats to activate or let the learned router allocate compute per-token, enabling a continuous accuracy-versus-computation trade-off curve from a single trained model.

## Method

CoTFormer repeats the same set of n_layer transformer blocks n_repeat times with weight-tying, but unlike Block Universal Transformers, it concatenates intermediate representations from all previous repeats into the attention context, so each new 'thought' token can attend to all prior thought tokens—mimicking CoT's attention pattern. Architecture tweaks (reserved beginning/end layers and a layer norm after each repeat, yielding LN-CoTFormer) further improve performance, allowing a 24-layer CoTFormer with 5 repeats to match a 48-layer standard Transformer. For adaptive computation, the paper introduces Mixture of Repeats: after each pass, a learned router scores every token via a dot product with a per-repeat embedding, and only the top-k tokens (determined by a randomly sampled capacity during training) proceed to the next repeat. An interpolation between the previous and new representation ensures that increasing capacity never degrades performance, while random capacity sampling during training enables the model to function at any compute budget at inference. A depth embedding conditions the model on the number of remaining repeats, and the approach avoids the early-halting attention problem inherent to prior adaptive-depth methods since halted tokens remain accessible through the accumulated attention context.

## Results

On OpenWebText2, CoTFormer with 24 base layers and 5 repeats achieves a perplexity of 24.48, approaching the 48-layer Standard Transformer (24.17) with half the parameter count. With architecture tweaks (reserved layers + layer norm after each repeat, i.e. LN-CoTFormer 2→21×5→1), the model reaches 24.11 perplexity, surpassing the 48-layer Standard Transformer. At the 12-layer scale, CoTFormer (12×5) achieves 26.64 perplexity versus 27.15 for the Block Universal Transformer, a consistent gap that widens with more repeats.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoTFormer consistently outperforms the Block Universal Transformer (Dehghani et al., 2019) at every scale and repeat count: at 12×5, 26.64 vs. 27.15 perplexity; at 24×5, 24.48 vs. 24.95. The gap widens with more repeats, suggesting better scaling properties. Against the 48-layer Standard Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017), the base CoTFormer (24×5) is close (24.48 vs. 24.17), and the LN-CoTFormer variant (2→21×5→1) surpasses it at 24.11. In the adaptive setting, the proposed Mixture of Repeats training method outperforms both PonderNet (Banino et al., 2021; 41.37 perplexity) and Stick Breaking from Sparse Universal Transformer (Tan et al., 2023; 33.82) at 33.08. Finally, CoTFormer (24.48) significantly outperforms Pause Tokens (Goyal et al., 2024; 25.05) at the same 24-layer, 5-repeat configuration, confirming that iterative model application with access to intermediate representations is more effective than static placeholder tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoTFormer is positioned at the intersection of Chain-of-Thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022b) and Universal Transformers (Dehghani et al., 2019), identifying a critical distinction: in CoT, newly generated thought tokens can attend to all previous intermediary tokens, whereas in Block Universal Transformers (weight-tied recurrent application) such cross-repeat attention is not available. The architecture inherits the weight-tying framework from Universal Transformers and the capacity-based routing idea from Mixture-of-Depth (Raposo et al., 2024), but departs from both by treating each repeat as producing a new implicit token that enters the attention context. The adaptive training method (Mixture of Repeats) is contrasted with PonderNet's halting mechanism and Sparse Universal Transformer's Stick Breaking, with the paper arguing for more stable and less hyperparameter-sensitive training dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 24-layer CoTFormer with 5 repeats matches a 48-layer standard transformer in perplexity is practically significant for memory-constrained deployment, and the adaptive compute allocation enables navigating an accuracy-cost trade-off at inference without retraining. However, the evidence is limited to small-scale models (12–48 layers, 768 hidden dim, 256 sequence length, 40–60k steps on OpenWebText2), and a notable gap remains between the adaptive model (23.83) and the non-adaptive CoTFormer (23.19) at the same compute budget, suggesting the adaptive training is not yet fully mature. Open questions include whether the CoT-mimicking mechanism scales to LLM-sized models, whether better sampling strategies can close the adaptive gap, and whether the required number of repeats (5 for 2× depth) can be reduced toward the theoretical optimum (2).
