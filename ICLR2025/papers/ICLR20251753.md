## Problem

Global self-attention in diffusion transformers scales quadratically with sequence length, creating prohibitive computational costs for high-resolution image and long video generation. The paper identifies that visual information is inherently sparse and redundant: attention maps of tokens within the same spatial window are nearly identical for distant tokens, meaning dense long-range attention performs largely redundant computation. This redundancy limits the scalability of DiT-based architectures to higher resolutions and longer video durations.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt encoded by T5 (120 extracted tokens) and produces images up to 2048×2048, videos up to 512×512×288 frames, or multi-view image sequences of 512×512×24. Inputs are processed through a 3D VAE (8× spatial, 4× temporal downsampling) followed by 2× patch embedding to form latent token sequences. Generation uses v-prediction with 50 denoising steps and classifier-free guidance at scale 6.0, trained via a low-to-high-resolution curriculum (256→512→1024 for images; 256→512 for video).

## Method

PT-DiT replaces global self-attention with a two-module design within each transformer block. The Global Information Interaction Module (GIIM) reshapes latent tokens to recover spatial-temporal structure, computes one averaging proxy token per spatial-temporal window (size p_t×p_h×p_w), performs self-attention among these far fewer proxy tokens to capture global semantics, then uses cross-attention (latent tokens as Query, proxy tokens as Key/Value) to broadcast global information back to all tokens. The Texture Complement Module (TCM) adds window attention and shift-window attention (analogous to Swin Transformer) to model fine-grained local spatial details that sparse proxy attention cannot capture, while also preventing grid artifacts. Compression ratios scale with resolution—(1,2,2) at 256 up to (1,16,16) at 2048 for images, with p_f=4 fixed for video—reducing self-attention cost to as low as 2.3% of full attention at 2048 resolution while retaining all latent tokens to preserve texture fidelity.

## Results

On MS-COCO 256×256, PT-T2I achieves a zero-shot FID-30K of 15.70, competitive with PixArt-α (10.65) but trailing top methods like RAPHAEL (6.61) and Imagen (7.27). On video generation, PT-T2V achieves FVD of 384.03 on UCF-101 and 375.23 on MSR-VTT, outperforming DiT-based baselines CogVideoX-2B (680.11/418.14) and EasyAnimateV4 (694.80/568.99) while remaining competitive with U-Net-based methods. In terms of efficiency, PT-DiT reduces computational complexity to 51% of DiT and 66% of PixArt-α at the same parameter scale for images, and to 82% of EasyAnimateV4 and 77% of CogVideoX for video generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For text-to-image on MS-COCO, PT-T2I (FID-30K 15.70) underperforms PixArt-α (10.65), DALL-E 2 (10.39), SD (8.73), Imagen (7.27), and RAPHAEL (6.61), but outperforms Kolors (23.15) and Flux.1-dev (22.76); the authors attribute the gap to distribution mismatch between their training data and MS-COCO. For text-to-video, PT-T2V substantially outperforms the two DiT-based baselines: it reduces UCF-101 FVD from 680.11 (CogVideoX-2B) and 694.80 (EasyAnimateV4) to 384.03, and MSR-VTT FVD from 418.14 and 568.99 to 375.23. Among U-Net-based methods, PT-T2V trails PixelDance (FVD 242.82) and DynamiCrafter (FVD 404.50 on UCF-101; 219.31 on MSR-VTT) but beats AnimateDiff (584.85/628.57) and FancyVideo (412.64/333.52). On CLIPSIM, PT-T2V (0.2349) is slightly below U-Net methods using CLIP encoders (0.27–0.31) but edges out CogVideoX-2B (0.2318) and EasyAnimateV4 (0.2285), which share the same T5 encoder. In efficiency comparisons (Appendix A.3–A.4), PT-DiT/S-Class achieves FID 65.86 vs. DiT-S (68.40) and SiT-S (68.61) at 37% lower GFLOPs, and PT-DiT/H reduces GFLOPs by 82% relative to Lumina-Next at 512 and 2048 resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

PT-DiT is a direct architectural modification of the DiT/PixArt-α lineage, replacing the quadratic global self-attention with a two-stage proxy-token mechanism: self-attention over a small set of window-averaged proxy tokens, followed by cross-attention to propagate global context back to all latent tokens. The Texture Complement Module borrows window and shift-window attention from Swin Transformer to recover local detail that the sparse proxy mechanism cannot capture. This positions the work between full-attention DiTs (DiT, PixArt-α, CogVideoX) and aggressive token-compression methods (ToMe, DAM), which the authors argue lose either fine-grained detail or spatially-adjacent token relationships critical for generation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 59% computational reduction over DiT and 34% over PixArt-α at matched parameter scale is practically significant, enabling 2048×2048 image and 512×512×288 video training on a single 64GB GPU—a meaningful step toward democratizing high-resolution diffusion transformer training. However, the MS-COCO FID of 15.70 trails PixArt-α (10.65) and the video benchmarks show competitive but not clearly superior results, leaving open whether the efficiency gains hold at much larger scales (the paper only trains up to 1.1B) or for substantially longer/higher-resolution videos. The resolution-dependent compression ratios also introduce a practical complexity that may complicate multi-resolution training pipelines.
