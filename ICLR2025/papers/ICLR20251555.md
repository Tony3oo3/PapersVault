## Problem

Existing diffusion model-based illumination degradation image restoration (IDIR) methods apply denoising directly at the image level, incurring high computational costs from many inference steps and producing pixel misalignment in restored details and local consistency. No prior work has leveraged latent diffusion models to generate compact, domain-specific guidance priors tailored to the Retinex decomposition of illumination degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251555/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-quality RGB image (H×W×3) as input and produces a restored high-quality RGB image of the same resolution. Internally, the diffusion process operates on compact latent vectors of size 3C' (reflectance) and C' (illumination) with C'=64, requiring only T=4 denoising iterations rather than the hundreds used by image-level diffusion models. The conditional signal for the denoising network is extracted from the low-quality image's reflectance or illumination component via a separate RPE module, so the model is fully self-contained at inference time.

## Method

Reti-Diff comprises two components trained in two phases. Phase I pretrains a Retinex-guided transformer (RGformer) with a Retinex prior extraction (RPE) module that compresses ground-truth reflectance and illumination into compact priors, which then serve as dynamic modulation parameters in RGformer's Retinex-guided multi-head cross-attention (RG-MCA) and dynamic feature aggregation (DFA) blocks. Phase II introduces a Siamese pair of Retinex-based latent diffusion models (RLDMs) that learn to generate these priors from the low-quality input alone, operating separately in the reflectance and illumination domains rather than the RGB domain. In RG-MCA, the reflectance prior modulates query features while the illumination prior modulates key and value features, implicitly modeling the Retinex product relationship through cross-attention. An auxiliary decoder enforces that the split features within RG-MCA genuinely capture reflectance and illumination knowledge, and the overall loss combines reconstruction, Retinex decomposition, and diffusion consistency terms.

## Results

On the primary low-light image enhancement (LLIE) task, Reti-Diff achieves the best performance across all four benchmark datasets: PSNR 25.35 / FID 49.14 on LOL-v1, PSNR 22.97 / FID 43.18 on LOL-v2-real, PSNR 27.53 / FID 13.26 on LOL-v2-synthetic, and PSNR 25.53 / FID 51.66 on SID, surpassing the second-best method (Diff-Retinex) by 13.2% on average. In terms of efficiency, Reti-Diff requires only 156.55G MACs and achieves 12.27 FPS at 256×256 resolution, compared to 396.32G / 4.25 FPS for Diff-Retinex and 1340.63G / 2.33 FPS for GSAD. On downstream tasks, Reti-Diff improves mean AP by 4.72% over Retformer on ExDark detection and mean IoU by 6.55% over SNR-Net on VOC semantic segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reti-Diff outperforms all 17 compared methods on every LLIE dataset (Table 1), with the largest margins over diffusion-based baselines: it beats Diff-Retinex by +3.37 dB PSNR on LOL-v1 and +3.23 dB on LOL-v2-synthetic, while also achieving substantially lower FID (49.14 vs. 51.33 on LOL-v1; 13.26 vs. 19.24 on LOL-v2-synthetic vs. GSAD). Among transformer-based methods, Reti-Diff surpasses Retformer by +0.19 dB PSNR on LOL-v1 and +1.86 dB on LOL-v2-synthetic, and outperforms MambaIR (ECCV24) by +3.12 dB on LOL-v1 and +1.78 dB on LOL-v2-synthetic. On underwater enhancement (Table 3), Reti-Diff leads MambaIR by 2.30% in UIQM on LSUI; on backlit enhancement (Table 4), it achieves the best LPIPS (0.147) and FID (27.47) on BAID, beating MambaIR by 0.006 LPIPS and 1.66 FID. On real-world IDIR (Table 5), Reti-Diff surpasses DCC-Net by 13.39% in PI across five datasets. For downstream tasks, Reti-Diff improves mean AP from 72.5 (Retformer) to 75.8 on ExDark detection and mean IoU from 58.0 (SNR-Net) to 61.8 on VOC segmentation, confirming that its restored images better support high-level vision tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Retinex decomposition framework from URetinex (Wu et al., 2022) for separating reflectance and illumination, and borrows the slot-attention-style auxiliary decoder from Locatello et al. (2020) to enforce structural constraints on decomposed features. It departs from prior diffusion-based IDIR methods (Diff-Retinex, GSAD) that apply denoising directly at the image level, instead operating in a compact latent space conditioned on Retinex-domain priors. Unlike general-purpose LDM approaches such as DiffIR that extract priors from the RGB domain, Reti-Diff trains separate Siamese diffusion models in the reflectance and illumination domains, explicitly encoding Retinex theory into the generative prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a ~4× speedup over image-level diffusion baselines (12.27 FPS vs. 4.25 FPS for Diff-Retinex) while simultaneously improving quality, making diffusion-based restoration more deployable. The Retinex priors demonstrate strong generalizability—plugging RLDM into Uformer, Restormer, or Retformer yields consistent gains (1.3–8.3% PSNR), suggesting the priors are a reusable component beyond the full pipeline. The authors acknowledge a limitation: under severe illumination degradation, texture information is lost during illumination recovery, and they propose frequency-domain priors as a future direction. Open questions include whether the two-phase training strategy and the specific Retinex-domain factorization generalize to other degradation types (haze, rain) or whether the compact latent bottleneck becomes a ceiling for very high-resolution restoration.
