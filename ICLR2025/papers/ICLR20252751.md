## Problem

Existing landmark-driven portrait animation methods depend on supervised facial landmark detectors that fail on non-human characters (emojis, toys, animals) due to domain gap, and 3DMM-based retargeting cannot capture subtle motions like eye closure. This limits portrait animation to realistic human faces and prevents bringing arbitrary stylized characters to life from arbitrary driving videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252751/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference character image (any domain: human, anime, emoji, toy, animal) at 512×512 resolution and a driving video (human or non-human, 110–127 frames) as input, and produces a 64-frame animated character video at 512×512. The intermediate reposed landmark sequence (68 keypoints per frame) is also an explicit output, making FaceShot a drop-in plugin for any landmark-driven animation model. Notably, the driving video need not be human, enabling open-domain animation from any video source.

## Method

FaceShot is a training-free framework with three components. First, an appearance-guided landmark matching module extracts diffusion features via DDIM inversion at U-Net layer 6 and time step 301, using IP-Adapter image prompts to inject appearance priors and an appearance gallery (five facial parts across multiple domains) to reduce cross-domain mismatch; landmarks are matched by minimizing cosine distance between averaged target features and reference features. Second, a coordinate-based landmark retargeting module transfers motion in two stages: global face translation/rotation via rectangular coordinate systems defined by face-boundary endpoints, then local per-part motion (eyes, mouth, nose, eyebrows, face boundary) combining relative part motion scaled by boundary ratio and per-point coordinate scaling. Third, the resulting landmark sequence is fed as a ControlNet condition into MOFA-Video (a frozen SVD-based animation model) to synthesize the final video, requiring no fine-tuning of any component.

## Results

On CharacBench (46 diverse non-human characters), FaceShot achieves the best user preference scores across all three dimensions: Motion (8.14), Identity (8.32), and Overall (8.27), outperforming the next-best method LivePortrait (7.33/7.08/7.11). FaceShot also attains the lowest Point-Tracking error (6.935) and the highest ArcFace identity score (0.848) among methods without failure cases, while its base model MOFA-Video scores 14.985 on Point-Tracking. Notably, FaceShot has zero failure cases, whereas AniPortrait, MegActor, and LivePortrait fail on 39.13%, 36.50%, and 16.67% of characters respectively. In the landmark matching ablation (Table 2), FaceShot achieves the lowest NME (8.569) compared to DIFT (11.448), Uni-Pose (13.731), and STAR (24.530).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the eight portrait animation baselines in Table 1, FaceShot wins on all user preference metrics (Motion, Identity, Overall) and Point-Tracking, with the largest margin over its base model MOFA-Video (Point-Tracking 6.935 vs. 14.985; Overall 8.27 vs. 3.18). LivePortrait achieves a slightly higher ArcFace (0.893 vs. 0.848) but only on the 83.33% of characters it successfully processes, and trails FaceShot on all user preference scores. Follow Your Emoji and MegActor are the closest competitors on user preference (Overall 6.74 and 6.26) but still fall 1.5–2.0 points behind FaceShot. In the landmark detection ablation (Table 2), FaceShot's appearance-guided matching outperforms the unsupervised DIFT (NME 8.569 vs. 11.448) and both supervised detectors Uni-Pose (13.731) and STAR (24.530). For retargeting (Table 3), FaceShot achieves the lowest Point-Tracking (6.935) versus Deep3D/3DMM (8.282), Everything's Talking (8.382), and FreeNet (8.272), confirming that coordinate-based retargeting captures subtle motions (eye closure, mouth opening) that 3DMM and Bezier-curve approaches miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

FaceShot sits at the intersection of two research threads: the emergent semantic correspondence in diffusion model features (demonstrated by DIFT, Tang et al. 2023) and landmark-driven portrait animation (MOFA-Video, Niu et al. 2024). It departs from the dominant paradigm of supervised landmark detectors (STAR, Uni-Pose) by repurposing diffusion features as a training-free cross-domain matching mechanism, guided by appearance priors injected via IP-Adapter. For motion retargeting, it replaces the standard 3DMM-based pipeline (Deep3D, Everything's Talking, FreeNet) with a simple coordinate-system transformation, arguing that parametric 3D face models cannot generalize to non-human geometries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: FaceShot provides a drop-in plugin that makes any existing landmark-driven animation model work on non-human characters (emojis, toys, animals) without retraining, and the CharacBench benchmark fills a clear gap in evaluation infrastructure for character animation. The coordinate-based retargeting outperforming 3DMM methods suggests that for stylized or non-human faces, geometric transformations may be more robust than learned parametric models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full-body or multi-character animation, how the appearance gallery scales to truly open-domain characters, and whether the diffusion-feature matching degrades for extreme domain gaps (e.g., 3D-rendered vs. 2D hand-drawn).
