## Problem

Existing visual counterfactual explanations (VCEs) modify images in an entangled and widely dispersed manner, simultaneously altering multiple unrelated regions (e.g., a bird's head, feathers, branch texture, and even text captions). This lack of a region constraint makes it virtually impossible to draw explicit causal conclusions about which specific factor drove the classifier's decision change, and can lead to faulty reasoning through confirmation bias. The paper argues that VCEs must be synthesized with a hard constraint on where changes are permitted to enable principled, interpretable explanations.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a binary region mask R (defining the only area where modifications are allowed), a target class label y, a pre-trained classifier f (e.g., ResNet50 on ImageNet), and a pre-trained I²SB inpainting model as inputs, and produces a single region-constrained counterfactual image where pixels outside R are preserved exactly while R is infilled to flip f's prediction to y. The region mask can be obtained via three routes: automated extraction (Integrated Gradients with grid-cell postprocessing), text-to-object segmentation via LangSAM using the class name as prompt, or manual user-defined segmentation through a brush-based interface. The I²SB model is trained on 20–30% freeform masks from the Palette dataset and generalizes to masks up to ~40% of image area.

## Method

RCSB formulates region-constrained VCE generation as conditional inpainting: sampling from p(x | argmax f(y|x)=y, (1−R)⊙x=(1−R)⊙x*), and uses the OT-ODE variant of I²SB as the generative prior, whose trajectory stays much closer to the data manifold than standard SGMs, thereby reducing gradient misalignment with the classifier. The classifier f replaces the auxiliary classifier in the classifier-guidance scheme, but its gradients are progressively aligned through four incremental improvements: (1) Tweedie's formula to feed denoised estimates to f instead of noisy intermediate samples, (2) ADAM stabilization to smooth noisy gradient norms, (3) adaptive normalization that scales each gradient by the norm of the first encountered gradient, and (4) trajectory truncation (starting inpainting from an intermediate timestep τ) to preserve original structure. No auxiliary losses (LPIPS, l2) are used; the classifier's signal alone drives the conditional inpainting. For automated region extraction, Integrated Gradients attributions are aggregated into c×c grid cells and thresholded to cover a fraction a of the image, amplifying focus on semantically coherent concepts.

## Results

On ImageNet, RCSB (best config A: a=0.1, c=4, s=3, τ=0.6) achieves FID of 8.0, 17.2, and 23.0 on the Zebra-Sorrel, Cheetah-Cougar, and Egyptian Cat-Persian Cat tasks respectively, representing up to 4× lower FID and 3× lower sFID compared to the best prior method (DVCE). RCSB also achieves near-perfect Flip Rate (94.7–100%) and the highest sparsity (COUT 0.74–0.92), where all previous methods fail to reach non-negative COUT on Zebra-Sorrel. On representation similarity (S³), RCSB matches or exceeds the best prior values (0.88–0.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCSB dominates all six baselines on realism (FID/sFID) by a large margin: on Zebra-Sorrel, RCSB^A achieves FID 8.0 vs. 33.1 (DVCE, the best prior), 67.7 (ACE_l2), and 82.4–84.5 (LDCE/ACE_l1). On sparsity (COUT), RCSB is the only method to achieve strongly positive values (0.74–0.92), while all baselines are negative or near-zero on Zebra-Sorrel (−0.45 to −0.06) and at most 0.60 on Egyptian Cat-Persian Cat. On Flip Rate, RCSB matches or exceeds all baselines (94.7–100%), whereas ACE_l1 drops to 47.0% and DVCE to 57.8% on Zebra-Sorrel. The only metric where a baseline edges out RCSB is S³ on Zebra-Sorrel, where ACE_l1 reaches 0.92 vs. RCSB^A's 0.88, though RCSB^B and ^C close the gap (0.86, 0.82). In the ablation (Table 2C), adapting the RCSB guidance scheme to RePaint, MCG, and DDRM still yields competitive COUT and FR but with substantially worse FID (42–149 vs. 8–23 for RCSB), confirming the I²SB prior's critical role in realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Image-to-Image Schrödinger Bridge (I²SB) framework of Liu et al. (2023) and the score-based generative model theory of Song et al. (2021), repurposing I²SB's direct corrupted-to-clean mapping as a conditional inpainting prior guided by a classifier's gradients. It departs from the prior VCE lineage (DiME, DVCE, ACE, LDCE, FastDiME) by arguing that their unconstrained or classifier-dependent region modifications are fundamentally insufficient for causal reasoning, and instead introduces a hard, user- or model-independent region constraint as a first-class requirement. The gradient alignment techniques (Tweedie's estimate, ADAM stabilization, adaptive normalization, trajectory truncation) are novel contributions not present in prior diffusion-based VCE or inpainting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual argument that unconstrained VCEs cannot support causal attribution because changes are entangled across the image is a strong reframing that could reshape how the XAI community designs and evaluates counterfactual explanations. The demonstration that I²SB's trajectory proximity to the data manifold enables far more effective classifier guidance than standard SGMs (starting from pure noise) provides a practical architectural insight for any guided generative task. Open questions include whether I²SB can handle regions exceeding ~40% of image area, how to verify that RVCEs capture the model's true reasoning rather than spurious correlations, and whether the evaluation metrics (FID, COUT) remain meaningful when large portions of the image are preserved by construction.
