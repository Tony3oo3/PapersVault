## Problem

ControlNet requires independent training from scratch for every condition type, consuming millions of data pairs and hundreds of A100 GPU hours per condition, which makes it prohibitively expensive for ordinary users to develop new condition types. This high barrier has stunted the growth of the ControlNet community relative to the flourishing Stable Diffusion + LoRA ecosystem, where users can fine-tune with minimal data and a single GPU.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250705/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a condition image (e.g., canny edge, depth map, skeleton, segmentation mask, or any novel condition) encoded through the pretrained Stable Diffusion VAE, combined with a text prompt, and produces a generated RGB image that follows the spatial structure of the condition. For multi-conditional generation, multiple condition images are each processed through the Base ControlNet with their respective LoRAs, and the outputs are summed before being fed to the UNet decoder. The framework operates on Stable Diffusion v1.5 latent space with DDIM sampling at 50 steps and classifier-free guidance weight of 7.5.

## Method

CtrLoRA decomposes the ControlNet into a shared Base ControlNet (360M params) trained jointly on 9 base conditions with condition-specific LoRA layers (rank 128) that capture each condition's unique characteristics, allowing the shared backbone to learn general I2I generation knowledge. For adapting to a novel condition, the Base ControlNet is frozen and only a new 37M-parameter LoRA is trained, requiring as few as 1,000 data pairs and under one hour on a single RTX 4090 GPU. A key design choice is replacing the original ControlNet's randomly initialized condition embedding network with the pretrained Stable Diffusion VAE, which aligns the condition embedding space with the Base ControlNet's input space and eliminates the sudden convergence phenomenon. Multi-conditional generation is achieved by summing the outputs of the Base ControlNet equipped with different LoRAs, enabling composable control without additional training.

## Results

On novel conditions with only 1,000 training images, CtrLoRA achieves LPIPS/FID of 0.305/16.12 (Lineart), 0.159/35.18 (Densepose), 0.326/9.97 (Inpainting), and 0.255/15.44 (Dehazing), substantially outperforming ControlNet (e.g., 0.622/22.29 on Lineart) and ControlNet-XS (0.623/22.36). With 100k images, CtrLoRA maintains or improves its lead (e.g., 0.247/13.47 on Lineart vs. ControlNet's 0.264/14.10). On base conditions, CtrLoRA performs on par with UniControl (e.g., Canny: 0.388/16.65 vs. 0.273/18.58; Depth: 0.222/19.34 vs. 0.216/21.29). CtrLoRA also converges dramatically faster, following the condition after ~500 training steps versus >10,000 steps for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet and its lightweight variants (ControlNet-LITE, ControlNet-XS), CtrLoRA wins decisively with 1k training images across all four novel conditions (e.g., Lineart LPIPS drops from 0.622 to 0.305; Dehazing FID drops from 54.07 to 15.44), and remains competitive or superior at 100k images. Against UniControl on base conditions, CtrLoRA is roughly on par: it achieves better FID on most conditions (e.g., Canny 16.65 vs. 18.58, Depth 19.34 vs. 21.29) but slightly worse LPIPS (e.g., Canny 0.388 vs. 0.273). Against T2I-Adapter (evaluated in Appendix Tables 5–6), CtrLoRA outperforms on both base and novel conditions (e.g., Lineart 0.247/13.47 vs. 0.498/20.53). Against ControlLoRA with 1,000 images (Table 7), CtrLoRA achieves substantially better results on all four conditions (e.g., Inpainting LPIPS 0.326 vs. 0.614; Dehazing FID 15.44 vs. 41.96), demonstrating that the shared Base ControlNet provides critical general I2I knowledge that direct LoRA-on-SD lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two well-established paradigms—the 'Base + PEFT' workflow popular in the Stable Diffusion community and the ControlNet architecture for spatially conditioned generation—into a unified framework. It departs from ControlNet's per-condition independent training by introducing a shared Base ControlNet trained across multiple conditions simultaneously, with condition-specific LoRAs capturing task-unique characteristics. The use of a pretrained VAE as the condition embedding network (replacing ControlNet's randomly initialized convolutional encoder) is a targeted design choice that addresses slow convergence and the 'sudden convergence' artifact. Compared to unified multi-condition models like UniControl, CtrLoRA adds a straightforward LoRA-based extension path for novel conditions that those methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing the cost of creating a new ControlNet from ~600 A100 GPU hours and millions of images to under one hour on a single RTX 4090 with ~1,000 pairs dramatically lowers the barrier for community-driven exploration of new condition types. The 90% parameter reduction per new condition also eases distribution and deployment. Key limitations include slower convergence for color-related conditions (attributed to VAE/UNet architectural constraints) and the current restriction to SD1.5's UNet backbone; the authors identify migration to DiT-based backbones (SD3, Flux.1) as the natural next step. An open question is whether the Base ControlNet's general I2I knowledge transfers effectively to fundamentally different condition modalities beyond the spatial/structural types tested here.
