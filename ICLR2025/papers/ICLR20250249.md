## Problem

Recent works have shown that fine-tuning text-to-image diffusion models can yield strong dense perception results, but critical design choices—whether multi-step denoising is necessary, how to train the U-Net (full fine-tuning vs. LoRA vs. freezing), what noise form and proportion to use, and how fine-tuning data quality affects outcomes—remain unjustified. This paper systematically ablates these decisions to identify what actually drives transfer efficiency and performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250249/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image, encodes it through the frozen VAE encoder of Stable Diffusion v2.1 into a 4-channel latent at 1/8 spatial resolution, and produces a dense prediction map (depth, surface normal, segmentation, or alpha matte) in the same latent space. Optionally, a lightweight DPT or UperNet decoder replaces the VAE decoder to output pixel-space predictions at full resolution, enabling task-specific pixel-wise losses (e.g., scale-shift-invariant loss, angular loss) that supervise fine-grained details the 1/8-resolution latent cannot capture.

## Method

GenPercept reduces the multi-step diffusion denoising process to a single deterministic forward pass by setting the DDPM scheduler hyperparameters (β_start, β_end) to (1, 1), which makes the noise proportion √(ᾱ_t) equal to zero and collapses the iterative denoiser into a one-step regressor from the observation latent to the target latent. The U-Net of Stable Diffusion v2.1 is fully fine-tuned (not frozen, not LoRA) on ~90K synthetic samples for 30K iterations at 768×768 resolution, with the input RGB latent concatenated as conditioning. Ablations show that the U-Net encodes the bulk of the perceptual prior (training from scratch degrades performance dramatically), that timesteps and text prompts are negligible for deterministic perception tasks, and that high-quality synthetic annotations (Hypersim + Virtual KITTI) outperform real-data alternatives (Taskonomy + Cityscapes) due to cleaner ground truth. The one-step paradigm also enables plugging in customized decoders and task-specific losses (MSE + SSI + gradient for depth, angular loss for normals) that improve fine-grained detail without the overhead of multi-step inference.

## Results

On monocular depth estimation, GenPercept (Depth) trained on only 90K synthetic samples achieves AbsRel of 0.094 (KITTI), 0.052 (NYU), 0.056 (ScanNet), 0.302 (DIODE), and 0.066 (ETH3D), with corresponding δ1 values of 0.923, 0.966, 0.965, 0.767, and 0.957. For surface normal estimation, GenPercept with image angular loss achieves mean angular error of 16.4° on NYU v2 and 15.2° on ScanNet, outperforming DINSE (16.4°/16.2°) and GeoWizard (19.8°/21.1°) on NYU. On dichotomous image segmentation (DIS5K), GenPercept at 1024px inference achieves maxFβ of 0.875 and Sα of 0.885, surpassing IS-Net (0.799/0.819) and HyV2 (0.757/0.792) but trailing MVANet (0.916/0.905).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenPercept (Depth) outperforms Marigold (74K samples) on 4 of 5 depth datasets (e.g., NYU AbsRel 0.052 vs 0.055, ScanNet 0.056 vs 0.064) while using a comparable amount of training data, and significantly surpasses DMP Official across all datasets (e.g., KITTI AbsRel 0.094 vs 0.240). Against GeoWizard (280K samples), GenPercept wins on KITTI (0.094 vs 0.097) and ScanNet (0.056 vs 0.061) but trails on DIODE (0.302 vs 0.297) and ETH3D (0.066 vs 0.064). For surface normal estimation, GenPercept with image angular loss beats DINSE on NYU (mean 16.4° vs 16.4°, but 5.0% threshold 33.3% vs 32.8%) and GeoWizard on NYU (16.4° vs 19.8°), though it lags behind Metric3Dv2 (13.5°) which uses 8.8M training samples. On dichotomous segmentation, GenPercept at 1024px surpasses IS-Net and HyV2 on all DIS5K metrics but remains below MVANet (maxFβ 0.875 vs 0.916). For image matting, GenPercept underperforms ViTAE-S on P3M-500-NP (SAD 12.77 vs 7.59) but substantially outperforms it on zero-shot AIM500 (SAD 75.5 vs 112.52), demonstrating stronger generalization from diffusion priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic empirical investigation into the design space of repurposing pre-trained diffusion models (specifically Stable Diffusion's U-Net) for dense perception tasks, building directly on two representative prior approaches: Marigold (stochastic multi-step) and DMP (deterministic multi-step). Rather than proposing a new task formulation, it dissects and re-derives the training and inference pipelines of these methods, showing that the multi-step diffusion mechanism can be collapsed into a single deterministic forward pass by setting the scheduler's (β_start, β_end) to (1,1). The resulting GenPercept paradigm also departs from prior work by demonstrating that the VAE decoder, text conditioning, and timestep embeddings are dispensable, and that full U-Net fine-tuning outperforms LoRA-based adaptation for this task family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: replacing a 10-step (or more) iterative denoising pipeline with a single U-Net forward pass yields roughly an order-of-magnitude inference speedup with no measurable performance loss, making diffusion-prior-based perception far more deployable. The finding that the perceptual prior resides almost entirely in the U-Net (not the VAE) and that text prompts and timesteps are negligible reframes 'diffusion models for perception' as essentially 'pre-trained U-Nets for regression,' which could broaden the pool of usable backbones. Open questions remain about whether the rich visual prior comes from the LAION-5B pre-training data or the diffusion objective itself, and whether scaling fine-tuning data further would close the remaining gap to task-specific SOTA methods like DepthAnything or MVANet.
