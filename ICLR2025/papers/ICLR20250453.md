## Problem

Existing quantization error reconstruction methods (LoftQ, ZeroQuant-V2, LQER) compute low-rank compensation terms via SVD of the raw weight quantization error or hand-crafted activation-scaled variants, but lack a theoretical justification for the optimization objective. The paper shows that minimizing per-layer weight approximation error does not guarantee reduced model output error, and that no prior work provides a closed-form analytical solution for the optimal low-rank terms under an output-error objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n}, its quantized counterpart W̃ (from any quantization function q(·)), a calibration set of input activations x ∈ R^m (typically a subset of pretraining data), and a target rank k ≪ min(m,n). It produces two high-precision low-rank matrices A_k ∈ R^{m×k} and B_k ∈ R^{k×n} such that W ≈ W̃ + A_k B_k. For QERA-exact, the full m×m autocorrelation matrix R_XX = E{x^T x} must be computed from calibration samples; for QERA-approx, only per-dimension second moments E{x_i²} are needed, reducing the calibration to a simple diagonal scale vector.

## Method

QERA reframes quantization error reconstruction as minimizing the expected squared layer output error E_x{‖x(W̃ + C_k) − xW‖₂²} rather than the weight Frobenius norm. The key derivation shows this objective equals ‖R_XX^{1/2}(W̃ + C_k − W)‖_F², which by the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem yields the closed-form solution C_k = (R_XX^{1/2})^{−1} · SVD_k(R_XX^{1/2}(W − W̃)) (QERA-exact). Under the empirically validated assumption that input dimensions are uncorrelated (R_XX is diagonal), the solution simplifies to C_k = S^{−1} · SVD_k(S(W − W̃)) where S = diag(√E{x_i²}), giving QERA-approx — a computationally efficient variant that explains why LQER's heuristic scale matrix works. The method is agnostic to the underlying quantization function and introduces no additional inference-time overhead, since the low-rank terms are pre-computed offline and the forward pass retains the same y = x(W̃ + A_k B_k) structure.

## Results

On QPEFT, QERA-approx achieves 76.23% average accuracy on GLUE for 2-bit RoBERTa-base (rank 64), outperforming LoftQ (70.18%) by 6.05% and QLoRA (67.62%) by 8.61%. On PTQ, QERA-exact achieves 71.42% average accuracy on six downstream tasks for 4-bit LLaMA-3.1-70B (rank 32), surpassing ZeroQuant-V2 (68.45%) by 2.97% and LQER (70.94%) by 0.48%, while matching HQQ (71.31%) which does not use error reconstruction. On WikiText2 perplexity, QERA-exact achieves 3.82 for 4-bit LLaMA-3.1-70B vs. 4.48 for ZeroQuant-V2 and 4.10 for LQER (Δ_ppl = −0.28 vs. LQER). At 3-bit, QERA-exact's advantage over QERA-approx becomes pronounced, e.g., 6.68 vs. 6.99 perplexity on LLaMA-3.1-70B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoftQ (the primary QPEFT baseline), QERA-approx consistently wins: at 4-bit RoBERTa-base on GLUE, QERA achieves 83.71% vs. LoftQ's 82.92%; at 3-bit, 77.43% vs. 73.31% (+4.12%); and at 2-bit, 76.23% vs. 70.18% (+6.05%). Against QLoRA, QERA also wins at every bit width, closing the gap to full-precision LoRA (84.00% at 16-bit). In PTQ, QERA-exact outperforms ZeroQuant-V2 across all models and bit widths (e.g., 71.42% vs. 68.45% on LLaMA-3.1-70B at 4-bit; 6.68 vs. 10.12 perplexity on LLaMA-3.1-70B at 3-bit). QERA-exact also beats LQER on most models, with the clearest margin at 3-bit (e.g., 6.68 vs. 7.05 perplexity on LLaMA-3.1-70B), while QERA-approx matches or slightly edges LQER at 4-bit. Notably, QERA-exact at 4-bit achieves accuracy on par with HQQ (a leading method that does not use error reconstruction at all), e.g., 71.42% vs. 71.31% on LLaMA-3.1-70B, demonstrating that the analytical solution recovers nearly all quantization loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

QERA provides the first closed-form analytical solution to the quantization error reconstruction problem that prior methods (LoftQ, ZeroQuant-V2, LQER) addressed only heuristically. It departs from the standard formulation by showing that minimizing the weight approximation error ‖W−W̃−C_k‖_F — the objective used by LoftQ and ZeroQuant-V2 — is the wrong target, and instead derives the optimal low-rank terms by minimizing the expected layer output error. The resulting QERA-approx solution (diagonal scale matrix + SVD + un-scale) subsumes LQER's heuristic as a special case, while QERA-exact (full autocorrelation matrix square root) generalizes it. The paper also establishes equivalence with the concurrent CALDERA work's Lemma 4.2 under a different problem setup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that per-layer weight error reduction does not guarantee model output error reduction (and can even increase it with more LoftQ iterations) corrects a foundational assumption underlying several popular quantization methods. Practically, QERA offers a drop-in replacement that improves both QPEFT (up to 6.05% accuracy at 2-bit) and PTQ (nearly lossless at 4-bit, 2.97% gain at 70B scale) without changing the inference-time computation. Limitations include the computational cost of QERA-exact's matrix square root (currently CPU-bound), the uncorrelated-dimension assumption failing in some attention layers, and diminishing gains over QERA-approx for larger models. Open questions include GPU-accelerated matrix square root, relaxing Assumption 1 for correlated layers, and whether the framework extends to activation quantization or mixed-precision settings.
