## Problem

Larger classifiers achieve higher accuracy but incur substantially higher inference costs, while smaller classifiers are cheaper but less accurate. Prior hybrid inference approaches either rely on learned routers without statistical guarantees or cannot deterministically satisfy user-specified cost budgets. This paper asks: how can one compute the optimal assignment of diverse pre-trained classifiers to classification queries that maximizes aggregate accuracy under a hard cost budget, with provable estimation guarantees?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251259/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a batch of N image queries (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet, variable for ImageNet-1K), a set of M pre-trained classifiers with known per-inference USD costs, a user-specified total cost budget B, and K pre-computed labelled samples of size s drawn from the validation split. It outputs an optimal model portfolio—a deterministic mapping from each query to exactly one classifier—computed by solving an ILP; the assigned classifiers then produce the final class labels. Feature extraction costs are deducted from the budget before portfolio computation, and the ILP is regularized by a variance penalty term to mitigate overestimation risk.

## Method

OCCAM exploits the r-separation property of image classification to show that the success probability function SP_i(x) is Lipschitz continuous, enabling nearest-neighbor-based estimation from pre-computed labelled samples. For each test query, the method finds its nearest neighbor in K i.i.d. samples to obtain an asymptotically unbiased, low-variance estimate of each classifier's success probability on that query. These estimates feed into an integer linear program that assigns exactly one classifier per query to maximize total estimated accuracy subject to the cost budget, with a variance-based regularization term (λ·σ_i) to penalize high-variance estimators. The entire pipeline is training-free—no router or accuracy predictor is learned—distinguishing it from prior hybrid inference methods that require separate training phases and offer no guarantees. The ILP is solved with the HiGHS branch-and-cut solver, and the approach achieves up to 40% cost reduction with less than 1% accuracy drop across four image classification benchmarks.

## Results

OCCAM achieves up to 40% cost reduction with minimal accuracy drop across four image classification benchmarks. On CIFAR-10 (10 classes), OCCAM incurs only 0.51% accuracy drop at 40% cost reduction, compared to 1.22% for FrugalMCT and 2.22% for Single Best. On the harder Tiny ImageNet (200 classes), OCCAM achieves 2.75% accuracy drop at 40% cost reduction, outperforming FrugalMCT (3.88%) by 1.13 percentage points. On ImageNet-1K (1000 classes), OCCAM still leads all baselines at every budget level, with 2.24% accuracy drop at 40% cost reduction versus 2.35% for FrugalMCT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCCAM is compared against three baselines: Single Best (always uses the most expensive model), Random (uniform random accuracy estimates fed into the same ILP), and FrugalMCT (Chen et al., 2022, which trains a random-forest accuracy predictor on model-extracted features). OCCAM consistently outperforms FrugalMCT across all four datasets and all three cost-reduction levels (10%, 20%, 40%). The margin is largest on Tiny ImageNet at 40% cost reduction, where OCCAM achieves 2.75% accuracy drop versus 3.88% for FrugalMCT (a 1.13 pp advantage), and on CIFAR-10 at 40% reduction (0.51% vs. 1.22%, a 0.71 pp advantage). On ImageNet-1K, OCCAM edges out FrugalMCT by 0.11 pp at 40% cost reduction (2.24% vs. 2.35%). Single Best and Random are substantially weaker: Random suffers 7.03% accuracy drop on Tiny ImageNet regardless of budget, and Single Best's performance is fixed (it cannot adapt to the budget), yielding 4.01% drop on Tiny ImageNet. In the large-scale 101-classifier experiment on ImageNet-1K (Appendix A.6), FrugalMCT does not finish within 5 hours, while OCCAM with only 12 Pareto-efficient models still achieves less than 1% accuracy drop at all cost levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid ML inference paradigm (Kag et al. 2022; Ding et al. 2022, 2024) by moving from a simple one-large/one-small model setting to a multi-model portfolio with explicit user-specified cost budgets, and departs from the closest prior work FrugalMCT (Chen et al. 2022) by replacing its trained neural accuracy predictor with a training-free, nearest-neighbor-based estimator grounded in the r-separation and Lipschitz continuity assumptions from Yang et al. (2020). The optimal assignment is then computed via integer linear programming rather than learned routing, positioning OCCAM as a principled, guarantee-backed instance of the classical Algorithm Selection problem (Rice, 1976) specialized to classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40% cost reduction with under 1% accuracy drop is practically significant for production ML services where inference cost dominates operational expenses, and the training-free estimator with asymptotic unbiasedness/low-variance guarantees offers a transparent alternative to black-box routers that lack statistical guarantees. However, the approach is constrained to well-separated classification tasks (primarily image classification), assumes IID data, and optimizes average rather than worst-case accuracy, leaving open how to adapt it to tasks like sentiment analysis, to data drift, or to safety-critical settings requiring min-accuracy guarantees. The finding that a simple nearest-neighbor estimator outperforms a trained random forest (FrugalMCT) across all tested settings suggests that the structure of well-separated problems may be underexploited by learned routing approaches.
