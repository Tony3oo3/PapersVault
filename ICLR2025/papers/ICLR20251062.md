## Problem

Current LLM reasoning approaches predominantly rely on deductive reasoning (step-by-step from given conditions), neglecting the diversity of reasoning types. The paper shows that certain problems are exclusively solvable through inductive, abductive, or analogical reasoning, yet existing methods neither identify the appropriate reasoning type for a given problem nor ensure the model actually follows it during generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251062/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single problem text (logical multiple-choice or mathematical word problem) as input. The meta-thinker outputs an effectiveness score in [0,1] for each of five reasoning types (deductive, inductive, abductive, analogical, none), from which the optimal type f* and the effective set F are derived. The reasoner then produces a detailed reasoning path and predicted answer, guided by up to 3 retrieved demonstrations matched via cosine similarity (threshold δ=0.5) from the explicit collection. Final answers can be aggregated via self-consistency over 5 samples on f* or weighted voting across all types in F.

## Method

TypedThinker comprises three components: a meta-thinker that predicts per-type effectiveness scores, an explicit collection of demonstrations (one correct solution per reasoning type per training problem, verified by a reverse-check that the model can identify the reasoning type from the solution), and a fine-tuned LLM reasoner that applies the selected reasoning type. Training uses a self-training pipeline: for each training problem, 10 solutions are sampled per reasoning type at temperature 1, filtered by answer correctness and reverse-checked for type fidelity, yielding empirical effectiveness scores (n_k/m) used to fine-tune the meta-thinker via instruction-following pairs. At inference, the meta-thinker selects the optimal reasoning type, the system retrieves the top-3 most similar demonstrations via SentenceTransformer embeddings, and the reasoner generates a solution following that type. The approach is orthogonal to the backbone LLM and can be transferred to larger models (GPT-4o) or domain-specific models (MetaMath) without retraining the reasoner.

## Results

TypedThinker achieves the best average accuracy across four benchmarks (LogiQA, BBH, GSM8K, MATH) for all three backbone LLMs. With self-consistency over 5 responses (+SC@5), TypedThinker reaches 42.2% average accuracy on Mistral 7B (vs. 38.8% for Few-shot +SC@5), 56.2% on LLaMA3 8B (vs. 49.7%), and 66.8% on Qwen 2-7B (vs. 59.8%), corresponding to absolute gains of 3.4%, 6.5%, and 7.0% respectively. The largest single-benchmark improvement is on LogiQA, where LLaMA3 8B +SC@5 reaches 63.7% (vs. 57.9% Few-shot +SC@5). On the unseen Contexthub benchmark, TypedThinker +SC@5 also outperforms all baselines (45.2% for Mistral 7B, 42.3% for LLaMA3 8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TypedThinker outperforms all baselines in the +SC@5 setting across both Mistral 7B and LLaMA3 8B. Compared to Self-Discover (Zhou et al., 2024), which generates a shared task-level reasoning structure, TypedThinker +SC@5 achieves 42.2% vs. 28.9% average on Mistral 7B and 56.2% vs. 51.6% on LLaMA3 8B; Self-Discover struggles particularly on GSM8K (20.8% vs. 50.0% for Mistral) because a single shared structure cannot accommodate diverse problem types. Against the Mixture of Reasoning baselines (Zero-shot MoR@5 and Few-shot MoR@5), which apply all reasoning types and majority-vote, TypedThinker +SC@5 wins on Mistral 7B (42.2% vs. 34.1% and 38.8%) and LLaMA3 8B (56.2% vs. 46.8% and 48.1%), because indiscriminate mixing dilutes the correct answer when only one or two reasoning types are effective. The CoT Selection baseline (prompting the LLM to pick a reasoning type) underperforms Few-shot on Mistral 7B (32.5% vs. 31.8% single, 38.2% vs. 38.8% +SC@5) because the untrained model selects 'None' over 60% of the time and defaults to deductive reasoning when it does choose a type. The improvement is more pronounced for the stronger LLaMA3 8B backbone, suggesting that models with better instruction-following benefit more from TypedThinker's typed reasoning guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work that improves LLM reasoning by elaborating chain-of-thought paths (Complex CoT, Tree of Thought, Graph of Thought) or increasing output diversity through repeated sampling and multi-agent debate. Instead, it repurposes the cognitive-science taxonomy of reasoning types (deductive, inductive, abductive, analogical) as a first-class selection variable, combining a fine-tuned meta-thinker (an implicit policy) with a retrieval-based demonstration collection (an explicit memory) to guide the LLM reasoner. It builds on self-consistency for answer aggregation, rejection sampling for experience collection, and SentenceTransformer-based retrieval, but the key conceptual departure is treating reasoning-type identification as a learnable, per-instance decision rather than a fixed prompt or a single shared structure (as in Self-Discover).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that certain problems are exclusively solvable by a specific reasoning type, establishing reasoning-type selection as a meaningful and underexplored axis for improving LLM problem-solving. Its practical impact is broad: the framework requires no distillation from larger models and can be bolted onto GPT-4o or domain-specific models like MetaMath, suggesting a lightweight, transferable mechanism for diversifying reasoning. Key limitations include applying a single reasoning type to the entire problem (rather than per-step), focus on logic and math benchmarks, and the finding that retrieved demonstrations can mislead on mathematical tasks due to numeric interference. Open questions include whether multi-step typed reasoning (different types at different steps) yields further gains and whether the approach extends to code generation, creative tasks, or commonsense reasoning without task-specific fine-tuning.
