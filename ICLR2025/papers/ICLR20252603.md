## Problem

Full fine-tuning of large pre-trained convolutional models is computationally expensive and prone to overfitting on limited downstream data, while existing parameter-efficient methods like LoRA still risk degrading the model's generalization capability. This paper asks whether one can fine-tune large ConvNets by adjusting only a tiny subset of parameters that capture spatial adaptation, while preserving the spatially-invariant channel-mixing knowledge acquired during pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252603/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For discriminative tasks, the method takes 224×224 RGB images and outputs class labels, evaluated on VTAB-1k (1,000 training samples per task) and full CIFAR-100/ImageNet-1K. For generative tasks, it takes text prompts (and 4–9 reference images for few-shot concept learning) and produces 512×512 images via Stable Diffusion, with fidelity, diversity (Vendi score), and CLIP-based text-to-image alignment as evaluation metrics. The decomposition is applied per convolutional layer, where each k×k spatial filter is replaced by m filter atoms of the same spatial size plus a 1×1 coefficient tensor, and linear/1×1 layers are handled via Kronecker decomposition into atom and coefficient tensors.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes each convolutional layer F ∈ R^{c'×c×k×k} into a filter atom tensor D ∈ R^{m×k×k} (spatial-only kernels) and an atom coefficient tensor α ∈ R^{c'×c×m} (1×1 channel-mixing weights) via sparse coding (ISTA), such that F = α × D. During fine-tuning, only D is updated while α is frozen, so the model adapts spatial receptive fields to the target task without altering the learned cross-channel combination rules. To expand the tunable parameter space when needed, each filter atom is recursively decomposed into m₁ sub-atoms D₁ with intra-channel mixing coefficients β, yielding an overcomplete basis that increases capacity without touching α. Linear and 1×1 convolutional layers are handled by first expressing W = Σᵢ Aᵢ ⊗ Bᵢ via Kronecker decomposition, then treating {Bᵢ} as atoms D_c and {Aᵢ} as coefficients α_c, with only D_c fine-tuned. This yields as few as 0.05M–2.4M tunable parameters (vs. 22.67M for LoRA) while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy on both classification and image generation.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k discriminative benchmark with ConvNeXt-B, the proposed filter subspace method achieves 73.59% mean accuracy using only 0.45M tunable parameters, outperforming LoRA (67.53%, 17.4M params) by 6.06 points and full fine-tuning (72.14%, 87.67M params) by 1.45 points. On the generative VTAB benchmark with Stable Diffusion, the method attains the lowest mean FID of 54.7 with just 1.11M parameters, compared to LoRA's 57.5 FID with 22.67M parameters and full fine-tuning's 61.3 FID with 860M parameters. In few-shot concept learning (Dreambooth), configuration C3 achieves 0.707 fidelity while preserving 6.92 diversity, balancing target-concept alignment with generative diversity better than LoRA (0.697 fidelity, 4.84 diversity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all parameter-efficient baselines on both task types. On VTAB-1k discriminative tasks, it beats LoRA by 6.06 points (73.59% vs. 67.53%) while using 39× fewer parameters (0.45M vs. 17.4M), and surpasses BitFit (71.77%) and Linear Probing (61.21%) by 1.82 and 12.38 points respectively. It also exceeds full fine-tuning (72.14%) by 1.45 points with 195× fewer tunable parameters. On the generative VTAB benchmark, the method achieves the best mean FID (54.7) with 1.11M parameters, beating LoRA (57.5, 22.67M), LoHa (58.4, 33.86M), LoKr (61.8, 2.12M), and full fine-tuning (61.3, 860M). In few-shot concept learning, configurations C1–C3 (which keep atom coefficients α fixed) maintain high diversity (20.42, 9.37, 6.92) and text-to-image alignment (0.301, 0.279, 0.236), whereas C4 (which also tunes α) overfits to the target concept, dropping diversity to 3.17 and alignment to 0.201—confirming that preserving spatially-invariant channel weights is critical for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the filter decomposition lineage from DCFNet (Qiu et al., 2018), Li et al. (2019), and Papyan et al. (2017), which represent convolutional filters as linear combinations of spatial filter atoms, by repurposing this decomposition as a fine-tuning strategy rather than a compression or continual-learning tool. It departs from the dominant LoRA-style paradigm (low-rank updates on the full weight matrix) by operating exclusively in the spatial filter subspace, tuning only the spatial-only filter atoms while freezing the spatially-invariant channel-mixing coefficients. The Kronecker-product formulation for linear layers follows Edalati et al. (2022) and YEH et al. (2023), and the overcomplete recursive decomposition of filter atoms is a novel extension that expands the tunable parameter space without touching atom coefficients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is achieving competitive or superior accuracy with 1–2 orders of magnitude fewer tunable parameters than LoRA (0.45M vs. 17.4M on VTAB-1k; 1.11M vs. 22.67M on Stable Diffusion), while the conceptual finding that freezing spatially-invariant channel weights (α) is essential for preserving generative diversity and text-to-image alignment offers a principled guideline for what to freeze during adaptation. The main limitation is that the method is most natural for convolutional architectures; the linear-layer extension via Kronecker decomposition is acknowledged as under-explored, and the approach has not been validated on purely transformer-based backbones without convolutional components. Open questions include whether the filter-subspace view transfers to attention-dominated architectures and whether the overcomplete decomposition can be made adaptive per-layer or per-task.
