## Problem

Existing regularization functions for promoting discrete-valued solutions, eigenvectors, or orthogonal matrices in optimization problems either require additional trainable scale parameters, are non-differentiable, or are limited to a single structural property. This paper asks whether a single, unified recipe can yield differentiable, scale-adaptive regularizers that are free of spurious stationary points and applicable to a broad range of structural constraints.

## Input / Output

For discrete-vector recovery, the method takes a measurement vector b and an underdetermined matrix A (M×N, M<N) and outputs a binary (±α) or ternary (−α,0,α) vector x satisfying b=Ax, with the scale α determined automatically. For neural network quantization, it takes full-precision ResNet weights initialized from pretrained checkpoints and RGB training images (ImageNet 224×224, CIFAR-10 32×32), and produces binary or ternary quantized weights with one shared scale factor per output channel or row. The eigenvector and orthogonal-matrix variants take a fixed matrix C (or A,B pair) plus a measurement vector and return the corresponding structured solution.

## Method

The core mechanism is Proposition 1: given two vector-valued functions g and h, the function ℓ(x)=‖g(x)‖²‖h(x)‖²−|⟨g(x),h(x)⟩|² is nonnegative and vanishes iff g(x) and h(x) are linearly dependent, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Choosing g and h as entry-wise polynomials (e.g., g(x)=[x₁²,…,x_N²]ᵀ, h(x)=1_N for binary) yields simple, differentiable regularizers that are provably invex (no spurious stationary points). Lemma 1 reveals the auto-scale property: ℓ(x)=‖g(x)‖² min_β ‖βg(x)−h(x)‖², so the scale β is implicitly optimized without being an explicit parameter. For neural network quantization, the procedure is three-step: (i) train with the CS regularizer added to the task loss, (ii) hard-quantize weights to the nearest discrete set with the learned scale, and (iii) fine-tune only the scale factors and biases with quantized weights fixed, requiring no extra storage during training.

## Results

On ImageNet (ResNet-18), CS-regularized binary weight quantization outperforms BWN, SQ-BWN, HWGQ, PCCN, and TWN, achieving top-1 accuracy 4.9% below the SOTA ProxyBNN; ternary quantization outperforms TWN and SQ-TWN but trails QIL by 2.8%. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-20), binary quantization matches LQ-Net and slightly beats DoReFa-Net, while trailing DAQ and LCR-BNN by 0.9%; ternary quantization is outperformed only by DAQ and LCR-BNN by a mere 0.2%. For underdetermined linear system recovery (N=100), the CS binarizer ℓ_bin achieves higher success probability than ℓ^∞-norm minimization at every undersampling ratio M/N ∈ [0.3, 0.9], and ℓ_osb/ℓ_ter significantly outperform ℓ^1-norm minimization for one-sided binary and ternary recovery respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For underdetermined linear system recovery, the CS binarizer ℓ_bin achieves strictly higher success probability than ℓ^∞-norm minimization (Mangasarian & Recht, 2011) at all undersampling ratios, and ℓ_osb/ℓ_ter significantly outperform ℓ^1-norm minimization (Cai et al., 2009) for one-sided binary and ternary recovery. Against the learnable-scale regularizer ℓ_{bin,β} = Σ(x_n²−β)² (Tang et al., 2017; Darabi et al., 2019), the CS regularizer matches or exceeds recovery performance while eliminating the need for an additional optimization parameter β. On ImageNet, the CS-regularized approach outperforms BWN (Rastegari et al., 2016), SQ-BWN (Dong et al., 2017), HWGQ (Cai et al., 2017), PCCN (Gu et al., 2019), and TWN (Li et al., 2016) for both binary and ternary quantization, but trails ProxyBNN (He et al., 2020) by 4.9% (binary) and QIL (Jung et al., 2019) by 2.8% (ternary). On CIFAR-10, the method matches LQ-Net (Zhang et al., 2018) and slightly beats DoReFa-Net (Zhou et al., 2016) for binary weights, while DAQ (Kim et al., 2021) and LCR-BNN (Shang et al., 2022) lead by 0.9% (binary) and 0.2% (ternary). The paper also generalizes the CS regularizer from Ehrhardt & Arridge (2014) as a special case, and demonstrates that the eigenvector regularizer ℓ_eig outperforms the baseline ℓ_μ = ‖Cx − μx‖² which requires learning an additional eigenvalue parameter μ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes a single CS-based regularizer from Ehrhardt & Arridge (2014) into a systematic recipe (Proposition 1) for constructing a broad family of differentiable regularizers from any pair of vector-valued functions. It departs from existing binarization regularizers (e.g., Tang et al. 2017, Darabi et al. 2019) that require either a fixed scale or an additional trainable parameter β, and from trigonometric regularizers (Naumov et al. 2018, Solodskikh et al. 2022), by showing that the CS inequality alone yields auto-scale, parameter-free regularizers that are often invex. The framework also subsumes prior ℓ^∞-norm and ℓ^1-norm recovery approaches as baselines rather than building on them, positioning CS regularizers as a unifying alternative that extends naturally to eigenvector promotion, orthogonal matrix recovery, and multi-bit discretization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The auto-scale property (no additional optimization parameters) and the invex guarantee (no spurious stationary points) together offer a clean, theoretically grounded alternative to the ad-hoc regularization functions currently used in neural network quantization, potentially simplifying training pipelines and reducing storage overhead. However, the empirical gains on ImageNet and CIFAR-10 are competitive rather than state-of-the-art (4.9% below ProxyBNN for binary, 2.8% below QIL for ternary), and the higher-order polynomial structure of some regularizers (e.g., eighth-order for ternary) raises numerical stability concerns. Open questions include whether CS regularizers can be combined with sophisticated SOTA quantization procedures (proxy matrices, learned quantization intervals), whether the framework extends cleanly to complex-valued or mixed-precision settings, and whether the invex property can be exploited by dedicated convergence-accelerating algorithms beyond standard FISTA.
