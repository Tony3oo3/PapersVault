## Problem

Vision-language learning demands strong visual perception (fine-grained recognition, spatial reasoning), yet current efficient VLMs built on CLIP suffer from limited visual perception and object hallucination, especially when trained on small datasets. The paper asks whether the rich, diverse visual knowledge already encoded in numerous pre-trained vision experts (depth, edge, surface normal, self-supervised embeddings) can be explicitly transferred to a frozen CLIP encoder to boost VL performance without the massive data and model scale typically required.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251978/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (256×256 during pre-training, 480×480 during fine-tuning) and a text prompt or question, producing a generated caption or short text answer. The image is first encoded by a frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP encoder into 256 patch-level vision tokens; these tokens are then augmented with expert knowledge before being injected into a frozen RoBERTa-base language decoder via cross-attention. In the merged variant (ToVE-Lite), only the single knowledge-merged CLIP encoder is needed at inference, eliminating all expert forward passes.

## Method

ToVE builds a hub of five pre-trained vision experts (DPT for depth, NLL-AngMF for surface normal, DexiNed for edges, DINO-v2, and EVA-CLIP-02) whose patch tokens are projected into a shared embedding space via expert-specific + shared MLP projectors. A token-aware gating network computes per-token routing scores over all K experts, and the resulting softmax weights (with small Gaussian noise for exploration) produce a weighted ensemble of expert tokens for each vision token. Expert knowledge is injected via a residual addition (t̃_vis = t_vis + λ·MLP(t_exp)) with a small λ, preserving CLIP's generalizability while complementing it with domain-specific visual priors. During training, a load-balancing auxiliary loss (importance + load variance) with a cosine-decaying coefficient prevents the gate from collapsing onto a single expert. For deployment efficiency, low-contributing experts can be detached by zeroing their routing scores, or all expert knowledge can be merged into the CLIP encoder itself via a joint language-modeling + L2-norm distillation loss, yielding a single-encoder model (ToVE-Lite) that requires no expert inference.

## Results

ToVE (3M pre-training pairs) achieves 113.1 CIDEr on NoCaps and 40.3 BLEU-4 / 24.5 SPICE on COCO, outperforming Prismer (12.7M pairs) which scores 111.7 CIDEr on NoCaps, with approximately 4× less data. On VQAv2, ToVE reaches 75.8% test-standard accuracy, competitive with BLIP (78.2%) trained on 129M pairs. On visual perception benchmarks, ToVE achieves 67.7% on VSR and 82.5 F1 on POPE-A, surpassing Prismer-Z (63.2% VSR, 81.2 POPE-A) trained on 12.7M pairs and significantly outperforming Prismer-Z* (55.3% VSR) trained on the same 3M data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToVE (3M pairs) outperforms Prismer (12.7M pairs) on NoCaps CIDEr (113.1 vs. 111.7) and COCO SPICE (24.5 vs. 24.1) while using ~4× less data, and surpasses Prismer-Z on VSR (67.7 vs. 63.2) and POPE-A (82.5 vs. 81.2). Against BLIP (129M pairs), ToVE achieves comparable COCO BLEU-4 (40.3 vs. 39.4) and higher NoCaps CIDEr (113.1 vs. 105.7), but trails on VQAv2 (75.8 vs. 78.2). ToVE clearly beats smaller-data baselines: OSCAR (80.9 CIDEr), VinVL (95.5), and GIT (89.6) on NoCaps, and ALBEF (74.5), Triple (74.9), and MaskVLM (75.5) on VQAv2. On visual perception, ToVE surpasses MLLMs including LLaVA (56.3 VSR, 67.0 POPE-A), MiniGPT-4 (50.7 VSR, 71.4 POPE-A), and InstructBLIP (54.3 VSR), though InstructBLIP retains an edge on POPE-R (89.3 vs. 87.4). ToVE slightly underperforms BEiT3 (77.7) and MAMO (76.1) on VQAv2, and BLIP-2_OPT2.7b edges ahead on zero-shot NoCaps CIDEr (111.9 vs. 110.2), but ToVE uses 1.3× less data than BLIP-2 and 4× less than Prismer.

## Relation to Prior Work

ToVE builds directly on the frozen-CLIP-encoder-plus-language-decoder architecture popularized by BLIP-2 and Prismer, but departs from both in a key way: rather than relying on massive image-text pre-training or a learned Resampler to implicitly synthesize expert knowledge, it explicitly transfers knowledge from a hub of pre-trained, domain-specific vision experts (depth, edge, surface normal, DINO, EVA) via a token-aware gating network and residual addition. This positions it between classical MoE (which uses randomly-initialized FFNs) and knowledge distillation (which typically operates at the task level), introducing a token-level, residual-based transfer mechanism that preserves CLIP's generalizability. The knowledge merging step, which distills expert contributions back into a single CLIP encoder, extends the model-merging literature (task arithmetic, model soup) to the vision-token level for VL tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving competitive zero-shot captioning and VQA with 3M pairs (vs. 12.7M–129M for prior efficient VLMs) makes VL learning feasible in data-scarce domains like medical imaging. The knowledge merging approach (ToVE-Lite) eliminates all expert inference at deployment while retaining ~95.6% of full ToVE performance, and the pluggable expert design allows practitioners to trade inference cost for accuracy without retraining. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to truly unseen domains, how it scales beyond five experts, and whether the preliminary LLaVA-1.5 extension (ToVE_Vicuna) can be made competitive with full-scale LVLM pre-training. The dependence on the availability and quality of pre-trained vision experts remains a practical constraint.
