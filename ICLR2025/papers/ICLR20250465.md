## Problem

Stochastic diffusion samplers cannot track the likelihood of the samples they produce, unlike the Probability-Flow ODE, making it impossible to study what images occupy the high-density regions of the learned distribution. While guided diffusion models are known to suppress low-likelihood outliers, it remains unclear whether they also avoid very high-likelihood regions, and what those regions actually contain. This paper asks: what does the highest-density part of a diffusion model's probability landscape look like, and can we deliberately sample from it?

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained score-based diffusion model with score function s(t,x) and a user-chosen threshold time t ∈ (0,T]. Starting from a noisy sample x_t drawn at time t (via the forward SDE or PF-ODE), it produces a high-density image y_0 by integrating the HD-ODE from t to 0, along with a log-likelihood estimate tracked via the augmented SDE at no additional NFE cost. The density estimate r_0 from the augmented reverse SDE is shown to form an upper bound on log p_0^ODE(x_0) and correlates with it at >0.99, while the ELBO provides a lower bound, giving a practical sandwich for the true SDE likelihood.

## Method

The paper introduces augmented stochastic differential equations (Theorems 1–2) that jointly evolve the sample x_t and its log-density log p_t(x_t), requiring only the first-order score (no Hessian or divergence terms) and incurring zero extra network evaluations per step. For the theoretical mode of the denoising distribution p(x_0|x_t), Theorem 5 derives an exact mode-tracking ODE involving Hessian inversion and third-order derivatives, which is intractable in high dimensions. The practical HD-ODE (Equation 18) replaces this with the reverse SDE drift alone, which is provably exact for Gaussian data and empirically yields samples with strictly higher likelihood than regular stochastic samples across all tested noise levels. A novel general instantaneous change-of-variables lemma (Lemma 1) enables density tracking along the HD-ODE path without reverting to the more expensive PF-ODE. Algorithm 1 combines these: sample x_t at threshold t, integrate the HD-ODE to obtain y_0, and read off its likelihood from the augmented dynamics.

## Results

The augmented reverse SDE (Theorem 1) tracks log-density during stochastic sampling at approximately 1 NFE per step, matching the cost of the standard reverse SDE. On CIFAR-10, the r_0 likelihood estimate correlates at 0.996 (likelihood-optimized model) and 0.999 (sample-quality-optimized model) with the exact PF-ODE log-likelihood log p_0^ODE(x_0). On FFHQ-256, the model's likelihood estimates show a 97% correlation with PNG file size (i.e., information content), explaining why blurry and cartoon-like images receive the highest densities. Algorithm 1 (high-density sampling) produces images y_0 that have strictly higher likelihood than all 512 regular stochastic samples x_0 across every tested noise level t (0% of regular samples exceed y_0 in likelihood).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the PF-ODE (Song et al., 2021c), the proposed augmented reverse SDE (Theorem 1) is strictly superior in capability: it supports stochastic sampling (which PF-ODE cannot) while simultaneously tracking log-density at the same ~1 NFE/step cost, whereas PF-ODE requires second-order derivatives (div∇) roughly doubling the per-step cost and still cannot perform stochastic sampling. The r_0 estimate from the augmented SDE empirically forms an upper bound on log p_0^ODE(x_0) for every individual sample tested, with correlations of 0.996–0.999. Compared to the ELBO (Kingma et al., 2021), the paper establishes the strict inequality r_0 > log p_0^ODE(x_0) > ELBO(x_0) for all 512 samples on CIFAR-10 under both a likelihood-optimized and a sample-quality-optimized model. The tractable gap r_0 − ELBO(x_0) provides a practical upper bound on the estimation bias of both r_0 and ELBO, and the sample-quality-optimized model yields a smaller gap (lower R(s)), suggesting p_0^SDE and p_0^ODE are closer for that model. The paper does not report a single standard benchmark metric (e.g., FID, IS) since its contribution is analytical rather than a new generative model; the 'win' is in enabling likelihood tracking for stochastic samplers at zero additional cost, a capability previously unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based diffusion framework of Song et al. (2021c) by deriving augmented SDEs that track log-density evolution for stochastic samplers, filling a gap where only the PF-ODE (a CNF from Chen et al., 2018) could provide likelihood estimates. The reverse-time SDE structure builds on Anderson (1982), while the general β-parameterized SDE analysis extends Karras et al. (2022). Conceptually, the paper departs from Karras et al. (2024a), who attributed guided diffusion's success to suppressing low-density outliers, by showing that high-density regions are equally problematic and should be avoided for quality generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical discovery that the highest-likelihood images in diffusion models are cartoons, blurry images, or blank images — despite training data containing none of these — reveals a fundamental disconnect between likelihood and perceptual quality that has practical implications for training objectives and evaluation. The augmented SDE provides a zero-cost likelihood tracker for any pretrained stochastic sampler, and the 97% correlation between likelihood and PNG file size (information content) on FFHQ-256 suggests a concrete mechanism for the phenomenon. Open questions include why the model assigns highest density to information-poor images, whether non-linear drift SDEs (Bartosh et al., 2024) can resolve the bias in likelihood estimation, and whether avoiding high-density regions can be incorporated into training or sampling to improve generation quality.
