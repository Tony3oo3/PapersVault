## Problem

EMCCD cameras produce images with complex, device-specific noise (multiplicative noise, blooming streaks, fixed-pattern noise) that existing physics-based noise models for ordinary CMOS sensors cannot capture, and no unified deep-learning denoising paradigm has been established for EMCCD. Prior EMCCD noise analyses remain purely theoretical and do not produce the calibrated, tractable noise parameters needed to synthesize training data for neural networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250156/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 16-bit noisy EMCCD raw image (EM gain 300, exposure 1×10⁻³ s to 0.1 s) and outputs a denoised monochrome image. Before network inference, the input undergoes algorithmic blooming removal (column-mean subtraction scaled by a calibrated blooming factor) and FPN subtraction, so the network only needs to recover shot, multiplicative, and readout noise. Training crops are 512×512 patches from 4288×2848 SID raw images; the first-ever EMCCD paired testset contains 224 macroscopic noisy–clean pairs with exposure ratios spanning 30–1600, and a 24-image microscopic finetuning set of 512×512 fluorescence frames.

## Method

The core contribution is an EMCCD-specific physics-based noise model D = B[K(I + N_P·f) + FPN] + N_r + N_q that incorporates three elements absent from prior models: a noise factor f ∈ [1.3, √2] capturing multiplicative amplification, a log-logistic regression linking FPN pixel values to exposure time (r² > 0.95), and a closed-form blooming factor x derived from calibration frames. All noise parameters (system gain K via gain-series MLE, pixel-wise readout-noise std maps, FPN coefficients, blooming factor) are calibrated empirically on an iXon Ultra 897 across 52 exposure levels. Blooming and FPN are removed as deterministic preprocessing outside the network, leaving only stochastic components for the Uformer transformer to learn. The Uformer is trained supervised on synthetic noisy–clean pairs generated by injecting the calibrated noise into SID ground-truth images, then finetuned on a small microscopic dataset to transfer from macroscopic to fluorescence-microscopy scenes.

## Results

On the first-ever real-world EMCCD testset (224 noisy-clean image pairs with exposure ratios from 30 to 1600), the proposed method achieves 48.59 dB PSNR, 0.9854 SSIM, and 0.0742 LPIPS, outperforming all baselines by at least 2 dB in PSNR. The closest competitor, the theoretical EMCCD noise model (Ryan et al., 2021), reaches 46.44 dB PSNR and 0.9788 SSIM. An ablation study confirms that each noise component (multiplicative, readout, FPN, blooming) contributes to the final performance, with blooming removal being the most impactful single component (dropping to 46.52 dB when removed).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins against all four baselines on every metric. Against BM3D (43.69 dB PSNR), the gain is 4.90 dB, reflecting the advantage of learned denoising over classical collaborative filtering. Against ELD (43.90 dB), the physics-based CMOS noise model introduces unnatural row-banding artifacts absent in EMCCD, yielding a 4.69 dB gap. Against the theoretical EMCCD noise model from Ryan et al. (46.44 dB), which uses the full Poisson-gamma-normal distribution, the proposed method gains 2.15 dB by incorporating exposure-dependent FPN modeling, blooming removal, and pixel-heterogeneous readout noise. Against SRDTrans (44.48 dB), a self-supervised fluorescence denoising transformer, the supervised physics-based approach leads by 4.11 dB in PSNR and 0.137 in LPIPS, suggesting that accurate noise synthesis from calibrated parameters is more effective than self-supervised learning for this specific sensor. Qualitatively, the method also handles blooming streaks and preserves fine microscopic detail better than all competitors after finetuning on fluorescence images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the physics-based noise modeling paradigm established for CMOS sensors (ELD, PMN) to the fundamentally different noise physics of EMCCD cameras, where electron multiplication registers introduce a multiplicative noise factor and the absence of a mechanical shutter produces blooming artifacts. It draws on Ryan et al.'s gain-series calibration for accurate system gain estimation and Feng et al.'s preprocessing philosophy of handling fixed-pattern noise outside the network. The key departure is that existing CMOS noise models (which correlate parameters with ISO/gain) cannot be transplanted to EMCCD, motivating a new model parameterized by exposure time and incorporating EMCCD-specific components like the noise factor f ∈ [1.3, √2] and a log-logistic FPN-exposure relationship.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic physics-based noise model and the first real-world paired test dataset (224 macroscopic + 24 microscopic images) for EMCCD denoising, filling a gap that has left the field relying on either generic image processing (BM3D) or theoretical noise models not designed for deep learning. The practical impact is significant for fluorescence microscopy, astronomy, and quantum imaging where photodamage limits exposure time. Open questions include whether the calibration pipeline generalizes across EMCCD manufacturers, whether self-supervised or few-shot approaches can reduce the heavy calibration burden, and whether diffusion models can better handle the residual over-smoothing that persists even with the full noise model.
