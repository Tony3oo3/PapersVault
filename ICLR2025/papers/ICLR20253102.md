## Problem

Gap-dependent regret bounds for Q-learning had only been established for algorithms using simple Hoeffding-type bonuses (e.g., UCB-Hoeffding, AMB), leaving it open whether the more sophisticated variance-estimator bonuses and reference-advantage decomposition used by UCB-Advantage and Q-EarlySettled-Advantage could also yield logarithmic-in-T regret under a positive suboptimality gap. Additionally, no gap-dependent analysis of policy switching cost existed for any Q-learning algorithm.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate on finite-horizon episodic tabular MDPs with S states, A actions, and H steps per episode, where the agent sequentially observes states, selects actions, receives deterministic rewards in [0,1], and transitions to the next state. The theoretical outputs are regret bounds that are logarithmic in total steps T (rather than the worst-case √T) and scale with the maximum conditional variance Q*, the reference-settling hyperparameter β, and the minimum suboptimality gap Δ_min, plus a high-probability policy switching cost bound that separates optimal and suboptimal state-action-step triples. The bounds also provide practical guidance for tuning β based on prior knowledge of Q* and Δ_min.

## Method

The paper develops a novel error decomposition framework that separates estimation errors into advantage estimation, reference estimation, and reference settling components. The key technical tool is the construction of surrogate reference functions V̂_h^{R,k}(s) = max{V_h*(s), min{V_h*(s)+β, V_h^{R,k}(s)}} that are determined before each episode, resolving the non-martingale difficulty caused by the actual settled reference depending on the entire learning process and enabling concentration-inequality-based control of the martingale sum terms. The variance estimator in the bonus is exploited to bound errors in terms of the maximum conditional variance Q* rather than the trivial upper bound Θ(H²), yielding the gap-dependent term Õ((Q*+β²H)H³SA/Δ_min). For the policy switching cost, the proof exploits the unbalanced visit counts between optimal and suboptimal actions, applying Jensen's inequality separately to each group to achieve a log-log T improvement over prior worst-case bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes the first gap-dependent regret bounds for Q-learning with variance estimators and reference-advantage decomposition. UCB-Advantage achieves a gap-dependent expected regret of Õ((Q*+β²H)H³SA/Δ_min) + H⁸S²A log(SAT) log(T)/β², and Q-EarlySettled-Advantage achieves Õ((Q*+β²H)H³SA/Δ_min) + H⁷SA log²(SAT)/β, both logarithmic in T. Under worst-case variance Q*=Θ(H²), the gap-dependent term reduces to Õ(H⁵SA/Δ_min), improving upon Yang et al. (2021) and Xu et al. (2021) by a factor of H. Under best variance Q*=0 (deterministic MDPs), Q-EarlySettled-Advantage's regret can depend on Δ_min^(−1/3) rather than Δ_min^(−1). The policy switching cost for UCB-Advantage is shown to be O(H|D_opt| log(T/(H|D_opt|)+1) + H|D_opt^c| log(·/Δ_min)), removing a factor of A compared to the worst-case O(H²SA log T) when each state has a unique optimal action.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior gap-dependent Q-learning baselines: UCB-Hoeffding (Jin et al. 2018, analyzed by Yang et al. 2021) and AMB (Xu et al. 2021). Under worst-case variance Q*=Θ(H²), the paper's gap-dependent regret term Õ(H⁵SA/Δ_min) improves upon Yang et al.'s Õ(H⁶SA/Δ_min) by a factor of H, and matches or improves upon Xu et al.'s bound. Under best variance Q*=0 (deterministic MDPs), Q-EarlySettled-Advantage achieves a regret depending on Δ_min^(−1/3), which is intrinsically better than the Δ_min^(−1) dependency of both baselines—a gap that cannot be closed by Hoeffding-type bonuses. Numerical experiments (Figures 1–2) confirm that both UCB-Advantage and Q-EarlySettled-Advantage exhibit lower regret than UCB-Hoeffding and AMB across both general and deterministic MDPs, with regret growing logarithmically in the number of episodes K. For policy switching cost, the paper improves upon Zhang et al. (2020)'s O(H²SA log T) by removing a factor of A when each state has a unique optimal action (|D_opt|=HS), and by replacing log T with log log T in the suboptimal-action term.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gap-dependent regret analysis for model-free Q-learning from Yang et al. (2021) and Xu et al. (2021), which were limited to algorithms using simple Hoeffding-type bonuses, to the more sophisticated UCB-Advantage (Zhang et al., 2020) and Q-EarlySettled-Advantage (Li et al., 2021) that employ variance estimators and reference-advantage decomposition for variance reduction. The key technical departure is the introduction of surrogate reference functions that resolve a non-martingale difficulty arising from the settled reference functions depending on the entire learning process — a challenge that blocked all prior optimism-based analyses. The paper also introduces the first gap-dependent policy switching cost analysis for Q-learning, building on the stage-based update design of UCB-Advantage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work answers two open questions in model-free RL: whether variance-reduction techniques (variance estimators + reference-advantage decomposition) can improve gap-dependent regret, and whether gap-dependent switching cost bounds exist for Q-learning. The surrogate reference function construction is a technical tool of independent interest that the authors note could extend to off-policy, offline, and stochastic settings. A notable practical implication is that under deterministic MDPs (Q*=0), the regret can scale as Δ_min^{-1/3} rather than Δ_min^{-1}, providing new guidance on hyperparameter β selection. The analysis is restricted to finite-horizon tabular MDPs with on-policy learning, leaving open whether the techniques transfer to function-approximation or infinite-horizon settings.
