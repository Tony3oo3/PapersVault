## Problem

Training two-layer ReLU neural networks is a non-convex, NP-hard optimization problem that yields black-box models with unclear inner workings. While convex formulations have been applied to neural network verification and robustness certification, their use for actually *training* networks—especially with non-linear ReLU activations—remains largely unexplored. This paper bridges that gap by providing an exact convex reformulation of the training problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and one-hot encoded label matrix Y ∈ R^{n×c} as input, along with a regularization parameter γ ≥ 0. It produces a lifted matrix Λ ∈ R^{p×p} (where p = c + d + 2n) whose factorization yields the first-layer weights u_j ∈ R^d and second-layer weights v_j ∈ R^c for each hidden neuron. Notably, the formulation's dimension is independent of the network width m, depending only on data size n, input dimension d, and output dimension c. A TOS-based rounding step then extracts concrete network weights from the lifted solution for making class predictions.

## Method

The key insight is to decompose each ReLU pre-activation Xu_j into positive and negative parts (α_j, β_j) with the complementarity constraint trace(α_j β_j^T) = 0, then lift the problem by introducing the concatenated vector λ_j = [α_j^T, β_j^T, u_j^T, v_j^T]^T ∈ R^p and the matrix variable Λ = Σ_j λ_j λ_j^T. This yields an exact convex completely positive (CP) program where the constraint P_{αβ}ΛP_{αβ}^T ∈ CP encodes the non-negativity of α and β, and the PSD constraint on Λ captures the rank structure. Since solving CP programs is NP-hard, the paper proposes a semidefinite relaxation that replaces the CP cone with the doubly non-negative cone (PSD ∩ entrywise non-negative), yielding a polynomial-time solvable SDP. A three-operator splitting (TOS) rounding heuristic then projects the SDP solution back onto the feasible set of the original CP formulation to recover interpretable network weights.

## Results

On five real classification datasets (Iris, Ionosphere, Pima Indians, Bank Notes, MNIST), SDP-NN with TOS rounding achieves weighted-average F1 scores of 0.987 (Iris), 0.924 (Ionosphere), 0.679 (Pima Indians), 0.930 (Bank Notes), and 0.862 (MNIST) at γ=0.1, competitive with NTK and NNGP baselines. Adding a bias term (SDP-NN-bias) improves performance, reaching 0.714 F1 on Pima Indians and 0.991 on Bank Notes. The SDP relaxation achieves an empirical approximation ratio above 76.90% relative to SGD training loss on synthetic datasets, confirming the relaxation's tightness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDP-NN is compared against NNGP (Lee et al., 2018) and NTK (Jacot et al., 2018) kernel methods, as well as SGD with 300 hidden neurons. On Ionosphere, SDP-NN outperforms both NNGP (0.920 vs 0.886 accuracy) and NTK (0.920 vs 0.886), and also beats SGD (0.920 vs 0.891). On Pima Indians, SDP-NN-bias achieves the best accuracy (0.672/0.703) among all methods, surpassing NTK (0.586/0.620) and NNGP (0.534/0.602). However, SDP-NN without bias underperforms on Bank Notes (0.860 vs 0.983 for NTK) and MNIST (0.794 vs 0.863 for NTK), though the bias variant recovers most of the gap on Bank Notes (0.991 vs 0.983). Overall, the SDP relaxation is competitive but not uniformly superior; its primary advantage is providing a convex, globally-optimal formulation with theoretical guarantees rather than matching the best empirical accuracy across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of convex reformulations for neural network training initiated by Pilanci & Ergen (2020) and Sahiner et al. (2021), but departs from them in a key way: rather than requiring enumeration or sampling of ReLU sign patterns (which grows exponentially with data rank), it uses a completely positive (CP) cone constraint to capture the ReLU nonlinearity exactly in a finite-dimensional lifted space. The lifting technique connects to Burer (2009) and Brown et al. (2022) for neural network verification, but the objective here is convex (not linear) in the lifted space, requiring a different equivalence argument. The SDP relaxation replaces the CP cone with the doubly non-negative cone, making it the zeroth-order Sum-of-Squares relaxation, and the infinite-width extension via Lebesgue integrals over probability measures parallels Bach (2017) and Mhaskar (2004) but within a convex optimization framework rather than a kernel/GP one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact CP reformulation provides a clean theoretical lens on ReLU network training, revealing that network width corresponds to the CP-rank of the lifted matrix and establishing a finite, data-dependent critical width R that scales quadratically with the number of data points. However, the practical impact is currently limited: the CP problem is NP-hard, and the SDP relaxation, while polynomial-time, scales quadratically in data size (reaching ~4.1M variables for 1000 MNIST samples), making it infeasible for real-world scale. Open questions include whether tighter SoS hierarchy relaxations close the gap, whether quantum computing can solve the CP problem efficiently, and whether the critical width bound connects to generalization theory or minimal-width requirements for ReLU networks.
