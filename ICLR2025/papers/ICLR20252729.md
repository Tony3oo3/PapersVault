## Problem

While diffusion models have been adopted in offline RL as behavior samplers or trajectory planners, it remains unclear how to directly represent a target policy as a diffusion model while enforcing KL-divergence constraints relative to the behavior policy. Existing approaches either require explicit density estimation (infeasible for diffusion models), use biased diffusion samplers that fail to prevent out-of-distribution actions, or necessitate backpropagation through the denoising path, making training slow and inference expensive.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous environment state vector as input and produces a continuous action vector as output via a state-conditional diffusion denoising process. At evaluation, the policy samples N_a=10 candidate actions through the denoising chain (using only T=5 diffusion steps) and selects the one maximizing the Q-ensemble mean, reducing inference cost compared to prior diffusion-based methods that require 32–128 samples. The actor network is a 3-layer MLP with 256 hidden units and Mish activations, conditioned on state and diffusion timestep.

## Method

DAC formulates KL-constrained policy iteration as a diffusion noise regression problem by showing that the optimal policy's score function decomposes into the behavior policy's score plus a Q-gradient term (Eq. 11). Working in the noisy perturbation space, this yields a supervised training target where the noise predictor ε_θ(x_t, s, t) regresses toward ε − (1/η)√(1−ᾱ_t)∇_{x_t}Q(s, x_t), with the √(1−ᾱ_t) factor constituting 'soft Q-guidance' that fades guidance strength as denoising progresses, keeping generated actions within the behavior support. The critic is trained with a lower confidence bound (LCB) of a 10-network Q-ensemble to balance over- and under-estimation, and the Q-gradient for guidance is estimated by averaging target Q-networks. Training avoids backpropagation through the denoising path entirely, and the Lagrangian multiplier η is either fixed or learned via dual gradient ascent to enforce the behavior-cloning constraint ‖ε_θ − ε‖² ≤ b.

## Results

On D4RL locomotion tasks (9 environments), DAC achieves a total normalized score of 836.4, surpassing the best prior method (DTQL at 798.3) by approximately 5%. On the more challenging antmaze tasks (6 environments), DAC scores 459.9, competitive with but slightly below the best prior methods (AlignIQL 474.8, IDQL-A 474.6), with the gap concentrated on the 'large' antmaze tasks where DAC does not apply reward-shifting. Notably, on 'medium' locomotion datasets containing many sub-optimal trajectories, DAC consistently improves over prior methods by more than 10% (e.g., walker2d-medium: 96.8 vs. 89.4 for DTQL). DAC also requires only N_a=10 action samples at evaluation time, compared to 32–128 for competing diffusion-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAC outperforms all 11 baselines on 13 of 15 locomotion tasks, with the largest margins against diffusion-based methods: over DTQL (e.g., walker2d-medium 96.8 vs. 89.4, +8.3) and DQL (e.g., halfcheetah-medium 59.1 vs. 51.1, +8.0). Against value-based baselines (CQL, IQL, IVR, EQL), DAC leads by 5–15 points on medium and medium-replay tasks. On antmaze, DAC achieves near-perfect scores on umaze (99.5) and medium-play (85.8), but trails IDQL-A on the two 'large' antmaze tasks (50.3 vs. 63.5 on large-play; 55.3 vs. 67.9 on large-diverse), which the authors attribute to not applying the reward-shifting trick used by prior methods. DAC also slightly underperforms SfBC on antmaze-umaze-diverse (85.0 vs. 85.3) and AlignIQL on antmaze-medium-diverse (84.0 vs. 85.5). On Adroit tasks (Appendix), DAC achieves 72.6 average, matching DTQL (72.7) and beating Diffusion Q-learning (65.1) and IQL (54.4). Importantly, DAC reports post-convergence performance without online model selection, whereas DQL and DTQL report best-checkpoint scores, making DAC's advantage more conservative in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAC builds on the KL-constrained policy iteration framework (Schulman et al., 2015; Peng et al., 2019; Nair et al., 2020) and the DDPM noise regression objective (Ho et al., 2020), but departs from prior diffusion-based offline RL methods (Diffusion Q-learning, SfBC, IDQL) that use diffusion models as behavior samplers for Q-target estimation. The key conceptual shift is formulating the KL-constrained policy improvement step directly as a diffusion noise regression problem, yielding a soft Q-guidance term (noise-scaled Q-gradient) that eliminates the need for density estimation of either the behavior or target policy. The critic component draws on LCB-based Q-ensembles (Ghasemipour et al., 2022) to avoid the over-pessimistic bias of ensemble-minimum targets used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAC resolves a previously open question—how to theoretically and practically regularize a target policy when the behavior policy is represented by a diffusion model—by showing the KL constraint maps naturally onto a noise regression target. The avoidance of backpropagation through the denoising path yields a substantial training speedup (5.4× faster than Diffusion Q-learning at T=100), making diffusion-based offline policies more practical for real-world deployment. Limitations include weaker performance on antmaze-large tasks (attributed to reward sparsity without negative reward tuning) and the continued need for a Q-ensemble of size 10 for stable Q-gradient estimation. Open questions include whether the soft Q-guidance formulation extends to discrete action spaces or alternative divergence constraints beyond KL.
