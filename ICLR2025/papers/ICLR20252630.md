## Problem

Existing LLM-based re-ranking methods (e.g., RankGPT) rely on autoregressive generation, requiring at least O(N) forward passes for N documents and strong instruction-following capabilities that open-weight models often lack. The paper asks whether generation is truly necessary, hypothesizing that abundant relevance signals already exist in LLM attention patterns during context encoding but are underutilized when routed through a generative bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252630/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ICR takes a search query and N retrieved documents as input, formatted into a single prompt with documents in reverse order and the query appended at the end (exploiting the autoregressive nature of decoder-only LLMs). It produces a per-document scalar ranking score computed from token-level attention weights, yielding a guaranteed well-formed ranking without any text generation. The entire re-ranking of N documents requires exactly two forward passes (one with the real query, one with the calibration query 'N/A'), sharing the KV cache of document tokens between them, with calibration adding only ~30% overhead.

## Method

ICR computes a token-level ranking score for each document token by averaging the attention weights it receives from all query tokens across every layer and head (Eq. 1), which naturally avoids length bias since each query token's attention distribution sums to 1. To mitigate intrinsic LLM biases (position bias, bias toward punctuation/titles/entities), a calibration score is computed identically using a content-free query 'N/A' and subtracted from the real-query score (Eq. 2). Tokens with calibrated scores below mean − 2σ are filtered out, and the remaining calibrated scores are summed per document to produce the final ranking score (Eq. 3). Because no generation is involved, ICR works with any pretrained LLM without fine-tuning and guarantees a valid ranking, reducing complexity from O(N) to O(1) forward passes. The prompt is framed as either a QA or IE instruction depending on query type, leveraging the LLM's existing instruction-following abilities during the prefill stage.

## Results

On the BEIR single-hop benchmark (nDCG@10), ICR with Llama-3.1 8B achieves a micro-average of 60.4, outperforming RankGPT with the same model (52.0) by 8.4 points and matching RankGPT with GPT-4o mini (60.5). ICR with Mistral 7B reaches 57.3 micro-average, surpassing RankGPT with GPT-3.5-turbo (54.0) by 3.3 points. On multi-hop re-ranking (Recall@2/Recall@5), ICR (Llama-3.1 8B) achieves 62.9/71.6, outperforming RankGPT (Llama-3.1 8B) by 6.3/2.2 points and approaching RankGPT (GPT-4o mini) at 66.0/73.6. ICR requires only 2 forward passes (O(1)) versus at least O(N) for generative methods, cutting latency by over 60%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RankGPT (Sun et al., 2023), the primary zero-shot generative baseline, ICR wins decisively: with Llama-3.1 8B it gains 8.4 points in micro-average nDCG@10 (60.4 vs. 52.0) and 6.3 points in multi-hop Recall@2 (62.9 vs. 56.6), while with Mistral 7B the gap widens to 13.3 points (57.3 vs. 44.0) due to Mistral's weaker instruction-following. ICR also guarantees 100% ranking success rate versus 34.3% for RankGPT (Mistral 7B) and 99.2% for RankGPT (Llama-3.1 8B). Compared to BM25 (Robertson & Zaragoza, 2009), ICR improves micro-average nDCG@10 by 15.7 points (60.4 vs. 44.7) with Llama-3.1 8B. Against ColBERT v2 (Santhanam et al., 2022) on multi-hop tasks, ICR (Llama-3.1 8B) improves Recall@2 from 53.9 to 62.9 and All-Recall@5 from 33.5 to 47.9. In Appendix F, ICR (Llama-3.1 8B) outperforms the supervised RankVicuna (Pradeep et al., 2023a) on SciFact (76.1 vs. 71.3) without any fine-tuning, while RankVicuna leads on TREC-COVID (80.7 vs. 72.8) and DBPedia-Entity (44.4 vs. 35.3); ICR also beats the setwise T5-XL method (Zhuang et al., 2024) on SciFact (76.1 vs. 69.1) and TREC-COVID (72.8 vs. 75.7, a narrow loss).

## Relation to Prior Work

ICR extends the attention-sorting idea of Peysakhovich & Lerer (2023), which ranked documents by attention received at the last generated token, by aggregating attention over all query tokens and all layers/heads, and by adding a calibration step adapted from Zhao et al. (2021) that subtracts scores obtained with a content-free query. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant generative re-ranking paradigm (RankGPT, pointwise, pairwise, listwise methods) that treats LLMs as text generators producing rankings or scores, instead treating the prefill/attention stage as a sufficient signal for relevance. This positions ICR as a non-generative alternative that leverages the same pretrained models but extracts signal from a fundamentally different part of the forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1) forward-pass complexity (two passes regardless of document count) versus O(N) for generative methods is a substantial practical efficiency gain for production IR pipelines, and the guaranteed well-formed output eliminates the format-failure problem that plagues open-weight LLMs used with RankGPT. The finding that attention-based re-ranking is particularly strong on tasks requiring contradiction reasoning, query-passage contextualization, and multi-hop information integration suggests that generative approaches may be actively suppressing useful internal signals. Key limitations include a persistent lexical bias that hurts entity-centric retrieval (DBPedia-Entity), restriction to decoder-only open-weight models with accessible attention weights, and the fact that only 7–8B models were tested, leaving open whether the approach scales favorably to larger or proprietary architectures.
