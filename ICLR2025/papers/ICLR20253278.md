## Problem

Computing RNA first passage time distributions via kinetic simulators like Kinfold requires thousands of stochastic Monte-Carlo trajectories, making the analysis computationally intractable for real-time applications such as kinetic RNA design and high-throughput drug discovery. No deep learning approach had previously been applied to RNA folding kinetics, leaving a gap between the accuracy of existing simulators and the speed needed for practical use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253278/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

KinPFN takes only N context first passage times (typically 25–100) extracted from a kinetic simulator as input, with no dependence on RNA sequence, length, structure, or energy parameters. It produces the full posterior predictive distribution of folding times in a single forward pass, discretized into 1,000 equal-width buckets forming a Riemann distribution, from which the CDF is directly computed. This replaces the need for 1,000+ simulator trajectories with just a few percent of the original compute budget.

## Method

KinPFN is a transformer-based prior-data fitted network (PFN) trained entirely on synthetic data sampled from a family of multi-modal Gaussian distributions (k=2–5 components) over log-encoded first passage times in [10⁻⁶, 10¹⁵], with bounded uniform base means, uniform standard deviations, and a global shift parameter to capture the plateaus and multi-modal structure observed in real folding CDFs. During training, M synthetic times per example are split at a random cutoff N into context and held-out targets, and the model minimizes the prior-data NLL, which directly minimizes KL divergence to the true posterior predictive distribution. The architecture deliberately omits positional encoding for permutation invariance and uses masked attention so that test positions attend only to training positions, ensuring independent predictions. Hyperparameters were tuned via two rounds of NePS Hyperband over 261 configurations, yielding a final 4.86M-parameter model (8 layers, 512 hidden size, 4 heads, 256 embedding) trained for ~5 hours on a single A40 GPU.

## Results

On the 635-sequence test set, KinPFN achieves a prior-data NLL of 1.2435 with only 25 context first passage times, outperforming the best GMM (GMM2: 1.3612), best DP-GMM (DP-GMM2: 1.3529), and KDE (1.2559). At N=100 context times, KinPFN reaches 1.1858 NLL, maintaining its lead over all baselines. The method requires only 2.5% of the original simulator compute budget (25 vs. 1,000 Kinfold simulations) to achieve comparable CDF approximations, and in the tRNA case study, 50 context times (5% compute) reduce CPU time from ~2,686 minutes to ~170 minutes with nearly identical results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KinPFN consistently outperforms all three baseline families (GMM, DP-GMM, KDE) on NLL from N=25 context times onward, with the gap being largest at smaller context sizes (e.g., at N=25, KinPFN's 1.2435 NLL beats GMM2's 1.3612 by ~0.12 and KDE's 1.2559 by ~0.01). At N=10, KinPFN still leads on NLL (1.3739 vs. KDE's 1.4370) but is slightly worse than KDE on MAE and KS, indicating less reliable distributional matching with very few context points. The GMM baselines degrade sharply with increasing assumed modality k (GMM5 reaches 37.58 NLL at N=10), while DP-GMMs are more robust to the modality choice but remain consistently worse than KinPFN across all N. On MAE (Table 6), KinPFN achieves 0.0561 at N=25 versus KDE's 0.0690 and DP-GMM5's 0.0768, and on KS statistic (Table 7), KinPFN reaches 0.1098 at N=25 versus KDE's 0.1344. At larger context sizes (N≥250, Table 8), GMMs with k=4,5 actually surpass KinPFN on NLL, suggesting KinPFN's advantage is most pronounced in the few-shot regime where it was designed to operate.

## Relation to Prior Work

KinPFN directly extends the prior-data fitted network (PFN) framework of Müller et al. (2022), which was previously applied to few-shot classification, tabular data, learning curve extrapolation, and time series forecasting, to the domain of biological distribution prediction. Conceptually, it departs from both traditional kinetic RNA simulators (Kinfold, Kfold, treekin) that rely on expensive Monte-Carlo sampling, and from standard density estimators (GMM, KDE, DP-GMM) that require the number of modes or bandwidth to be specified a priori. The key architectural departure is the use of a synthetic prior over multi-modal Gaussian distributions of first passage times, deliberately excluding all molecular features (sequence, structure, energy), which makes the model agnostic to the underlying data-generating process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the computational cost of RNA first passage time analysis to ~2.5% of the original simulator budget while maintaining comparable accuracy, KinPFN makes kinetic RNA design—a task previously intractable due to exponential simulator runtimes—practically feasible for drug discovery and synthetic biology. The demonstrated generalization to smFISH gene expression data suggests PFNs trained on synthetic priors could accelerate a broader class of biological distribution analyses. Key open questions include whether incorporating molecular features (sequence, structure, energy) into the synthetic prior would improve accuracy, whether the approach scales to longer RNAs (>150 nt) where simulator costs become prohibitive, and how to handle the bounded time range limitation for extreme folding processes.
