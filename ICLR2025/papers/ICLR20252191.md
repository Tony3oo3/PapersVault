## Problem

In information retrieval games where publishers strategically modify content to maximize exposure, it was unclear which ranking functions guarantee convergence of no-regret learning dynamics—the dynamics most relevant to real-world SEO behavior. While better-response dynamics convergence had been studied under specific ranking schemes, no general characterization existed for the broader class of no-regret dynamics, leaving platform designers without principled criteria for choosing ranking mechanisms that ensure ecosystem stability.

## Input / Output

The framework takes k-dimensional embedding vectors in [0,1]^k for each publisher's initial document and for user information needs, a proportional ranking function (PRF) uniquely determined by a single one-variable scalar activation function g, a demand distribution P*, and game parameters (penalty factor λ, number of publishers n, embedding dimension k). The vector-valued ranking function that assigns exposure probabilities to all n publishers is fully specified by this one scalar function, which is the key structural simplification. Outputs include a Nash equilibrium strategy profile with a formal convergence guarantee, publishers' welfare (sum of utilities), users' welfare (expected relevance of ranked documents), and the number of rounds to convergence.

## Method

The paper defines Proportional Ranking Functions where publisher i's exposure is g(d(x_i, x*)) / Σ_j g(d(x_j, x*)) for a twice-differentiable strictly decreasing activation g, then proves (Theorem 2) the equivalence of three conditions: g is concave, the induced games are concave (Rosen, 1965), and the induced games are socially-concave (Even-dar et al., 2009). The proof proceeds via circular implications: concavity of g implies social concavity through convexity composition arguments on the ratio structure, while non-concavity of g is shown to yield a non-concave game via a counterexample with all initial documents at one corner and the information need at the opposite. Combined with Even-dar et al.'s convergence theorem, this guarantees that any no-regret dynamics converges in average to a Nash equilibrium whenever g is concave. Empirically, three PRF families (linear, root, logarithmic) are compared using the LRL-OFTRL algorithm, revealing a fundamental trade-off: activation functions that make the ranking closer to uniform increase publishers' welfare but decrease users' welfare and slow convergence, a relationship robust to changes in λ, n, s, and k.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution (Theorem 2) establishes that for proportional ranking functions (PRFs), the concavity of the activation function g is equivalent to the induced games being concave and to them being socially-concave, thereby guaranteeing convergence of any no-regret dynamics. Empirically, using the LRL-OFTRL algorithm with n=3, s=3, k=3, the three concave PRF families (linear, root, logarithmic) exhibit a clear trade-off: the linear PRF (g(t)=b−t, b=1+10⁻⁵) yields the highest publishers' welfare but lowest users' welfare and slowest convergence, while the logarithmic PRF (g(t)=ln(c−t), c=2+10⁻⁵) yields the lowest publishers' welfare but highest users' welfare and fastest convergence. The root PRF (g(t)=(1−t)^a, a=1/2) sits between the two. The penalty factor λ has a non-monotonic effect on publishers' welfare (decreasing then increasing) while monotonically decreasing users' welfare, and the embedding dimension k has negligible welfare impact but substantially slows convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods but instead compares three families of concave PRFs (linear, root, logarithmic) and their hyperparameters against each other under identical game settings. Across all ecosystem configurations (varying λ, n, s, k), the ordering is consistent: less uniform ranking functions (logarithmic > root > linear in non-uniformity) produce higher users' welfare, lower publishers' welfare, and faster convergence. Within each family, hyperparameters that push the ranking toward uniform (increasing b in linear, decreasing a in root, increasing c in logarithmic) consistently increase publishers' welfare at the cost of users' welfare and convergence speed. The softmax (exponential) PRF, which is non-concave, was tested but excluded from comparisons because LRL-OFTRL produces linear (not sub-linear) regret growth under it, violating the no-regret condition. The trade-off ordering is shown to be robust to changes in the ecosystem distribution (truncated multivariate normal with varying correlation parameters ρ₁, ρ₂ ∈ {−0.5, 0, 0.5}).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the game-theoretic analysis of strategic publishers in information retrieval from better-response dynamics (Madmon et al., 2023; Ben-Porat et al., 2019b) to no-regret learning dynamics, introducing proportional ranking functions (PRFs) as a unifying class of ranking mechanisms. The theoretical core builds directly on Rosen's (1965) concave games and Even-dar et al.'s (2009) socially-concave games, establishing a full characterization (necessary and sufficient) of which PRFs guarantee convergence, rather than merely sufficient conditions. Empirically, it departs from prior work by using the state-of-the-art LRL-OFTRL algorithm (Farina et al., 2022) designed for concave games, and by studying the softmax ranking function as a non-concave counterexample where no-regret guarantees break down.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean equivalence between activation concavity, social concavity, and game concavity gives platform designers a simple, actionable criterion for choosing ranking functions that guarantee ecosystem stability, with the practical implication that publishers can learn a deterministic equilibrium strategy. Key limitations include the single-document-per-publisher assumption, the specific squared-Euclidean cost function, and the gap between the theoretical guarantee of average-profile convergence and the empirically observed last-iterate convergence. Open questions include whether convergence holds for non-concave activations (e.g., softmax), how the results extend to multi-document publishers, and whether theoretical convergence rates can be established.
