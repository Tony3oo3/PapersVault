## Problem

Offline RL relies on large pre-collected datasets, yet additional data can degrade performance by exacerbating distribution shift, and no prior work has systematically studied how to select a reduced subset that improves both algorithm performance and training efficiency. The paper asks: how do we determine the subset of the offline dataset that enhances policy learning while reducing computational cost?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a complete offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples organized into coherent trajectories, along with pre-stored Q-network parameters θ_t from T training rounds. It outputs a reduced trajectory-level subset S with per-element weights w, which is then used to train a standard offline RL algorithm (TD3+BC or IQL) for 1M steps. The selection operates at the trajectory granularity rather than individual transitions, and the top-m% return constraint filters out low-quality trajectories before gradient matching.

## Method

ReDOR formulates dataset selection as a gradient approximation problem: find the smallest weighted subset whose combined TD-loss gradients closely match the full-dataset gradient. The key theoretical insight is proving that the actor-critic objective is weakly submodular, enabling the use of Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) for efficient greedy selection. Three modifications adapt OMP to offline RL's dynamic nature: (I) replacing TD targets with empirical trajectory returns to stabilize gradients across policy updates, (II) selecting entire trajectories (using mean gradient as the basis vector) rather than individual transitions to preserve coherence, and (III) imposing a top-m% return constraint that biases selection toward high-quality data. A multi-round strategy (T=50 rounds) re-runs OMP with updated Q-values at each round, and the final reduced dataset is the union across all rounds.

## Results

On the D4RL (Hard) benchmark, ReDOR achieves substantial improvements over all baselines across all nine Mujoco tasks. The most dramatic gains appear on Walker2d-medium-v0 (80.5 vs. 53.3 for BlockGreedy, the strongest baseline) and Halfcheetah-medium-v0 (41.0 vs. 27.5 for BlockGreedy), representing relative improvements of ~51% and ~49% respectively. On the Antmaze suite, ReDOR also outperforms the complete dataset (e.g., 84.9 vs. 70.0 on Antmaze-medium-diverse-v0), demonstrating that a well-selected subset can surpass the full dataset. Computational overhead remains low at 8–14 minutes even on datasets with ~2 million data points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReDOR consistently outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated task. Against the supervised-learning data selection methods, the gap is largest on medium-difficulty tasks: on Walker2d-medium-v0, ReDOR (80.5) exceeds BlockGreedy (53.3) by 27.2 points and KRLS (49.1) by 31.4 points; on Halfcheetah-medium-v0, ReDOR (41.0) surpasses BlockGreedy (27.5) by 13.5 points. Log-Det is the weakest baseline overall, trailing ReDOR by 20–37 points across tasks. The Prioritized baseline (TD-loss-based selection from online RL) also underperforms ReDOR on all Antmaze tasks (e.g., 15.0 vs. 46.0 on Antmaze-large-play-v0), confirming that loss-priority selection is insufficient for offline RL where targets evolve. Notably, ReDOR also beats the Complete Dataset baseline on several Antmaze tasks (e.g., 90.7 vs. 87.5 on umaze-v0; 84.9 vs. 70.0 on medium-diverse-v0), indicating that removing suboptimal trajectories actively improves policy quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data subset selection / coreset literature from supervised learning (Grad-Match, submodular set cover formulations) to the fundamentally different setting of offline RL, where learning targets evolve with policy updates rather than remaining fixed. It adapts Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (Elenberg et al., 2018) and the gradient-matching objective (Killamsetty et al., 2021) with three key modifications: empirical-return-based gradient stabilization, trajectory-level selection, and a return-threshold constraint. The submodularity proof for the actor-critic objective (building on Mirzasoleiman et al., 2020) provides the theoretical justification for greedy selection, but the paper explicitly positions itself as the first work to address reduced dataset selection in offline RL, noting that supervised learning methods fail due to the dynamic nature of RL targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: offline RL practitioners can reduce dataset size while simultaneously improving policy performance and cutting training time, which matters for real-world deployments where data collection is expensive and noisy. The paper's theoretical analysis (convergence bounds, approximation guarantees) provides confidence that the method is not merely heuristic. Key limitations include the assumption of bounded gradients (which current offline RL methods only partially guarantee) and the gap between the theoretical TD-loss analysis and the practical use of fixed empirical returns. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to real-world robot tasks, model-based offline RL (MOPO, MoERL), and whether the multi-round selection strategy scales to very long horizons.
