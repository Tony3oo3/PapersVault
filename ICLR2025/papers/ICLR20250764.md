## Problem

Existing identity-control initialization methods (Fixup, ZerO) preserve identity only in the main branch by zeroing sub-stem weights, while initializing remaining weights with random or Hadamard values that can undermine the inductive bias. Moreover, directly using identity matrices faces two unresolved obstacles: rank constraints when output dimensions exceed input dimensions in non-square weight matrices, and convergence failure when target eigenvalues contain negative values. IDInit addresses both obstacles to achieve identity preservation in both main and sub-stem layers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes weight matrices of arbitrary shape (square, rectangular, or 4D convolutional kernels of size k×k×c_in×c_out) along with the activation function type to determine the scaling factor τ (√2 for ReLU first layer, 1 otherwise). It produces initialized weight matrices where the identity-preserving component IDI_τ fills entries with τ when m ≡ j (mod D_i^I) and 0 otherwise, while the zero-preserving component IDIZ_ε uses a subtraction scheme with ε=1e-6 to generate near-zero outputs without exact zeros. For convolutions, the identity matrix is reshaped into the 4D kernel tensor (IDIC_τ) to fuse spatial information and increase channel diversity.

## Method

IDInit consists of two complementary components: (1) IDI_τ, which pads an identity matrix adjacent to itself to handle non-square weight matrices, theoretically proven to break the rank constraint (rank ≥ D_0) that plagues zero-padding schemes, and (2) IDIZ_ε, which replaces exact zeros in the last sub-stem layer with a small-value subtraction scheme (ε=1e-6) to eliminate dead neurons while maintaining near-zero output variance. For convolutional layers, a patch-maintain strategy reshapes the identity matrix into the 4D kernel tensor to shift spatial features and increase diversity, avoiding the performance degeneration of channel-maintain convolution. The paper further proves that SGD with momentum breaks the persistent symmetry in identity-initialized layers (which Bartlett et al. identified as causing convergence failure), and introduces a loose condition (τ ~ N(τ, ε_τ) with ε_τ=1e-6) to add feature diversity and further increase rank. The framework applies uniformly to residual and non-residual networks, CNNs, and Transformers, with the zero-preserving component applied to the final layer of each residual block and the classification head.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-56 (SGD), IDInit achieves 93.41% top-1 accuracy and reaches 80% accuracy in 26±1 epochs, outperforming Kaiming (93.36%, 34±3 epochs), Fixup (93.24%, 31±3 epochs), and ZerO (90.57%, 57±3 epochs). On ImageNet, IDInit delivers an average 0.55% accuracy improvement over default initialization (e.g., 76.72% vs 75.70% on RN-50) while reducing convergence time by 7.4 epochs on average. For BERT-Base pre-training, IDInit achieves an 11.3% reduction in computational cost (FLOPs) to reach comparable loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDInit outperforms all six baselines on convergence speed across every setting: it reaches 80% accuracy in 26±1 epochs (ResNet-56, SGD) versus 31±3 for Fixup, 33±2 for ReZero, 34±3 for Kaiming, and 57±3 for ZerO. In terms of final accuracy, IDInit achieves the best or near-best results in most configurations—93.41% on ResNet-56 (SGD) vs. Kaiming's 93.36% and Fixup's 93.24%, and 90.01% on ResNet-56 (Adam) vs. Fixup's 89.50% and Kaiming's 87.55%. On ImageNet, IDInit consistently beats default initialization by 0.3–1.0% across ViT-B/32, RN-50, SRN-50, and RN-152, and also outperforms Fixup (75.83%) and ZerO (75.64%) on RN-50 (76.72%). On text classification (SST2/TREC-6), IDInit achieves the highest average accuracy (82.58/85.22 on SST2; 88.53/91.20 on TREC-6) with the smallest standard deviations, surpassing Default, Orthogonal, Fixup, and ZerO. ZerO performs notably worse than other identity-control methods (e.g., 90.57% vs. 93.41% on ResNet-56 SGD), which the paper attributes to dead neurons caused by zero down-sampling. Kaiming remains the closest competitor in final accuracy on ResNet-110 (94.06% vs. 94.04%) but is significantly slower to converge (33±4 vs. 27±1 epochs to 80%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identity-control initialization paradigm established by Fixup (Zhang et al., 2019) and ZerO (Zhao et al., 2022), which set residual-stem weights to zero to enforce identity transition in the main branch. The key departure is that IDInit also initializes the remaining (non-zero) weights with identity-like matrices rather than random values (Xavier in Fixup, Hadamard in ZerO), thereby preserving identity in both the main and sub-stem layers simultaneously. It directly addresses two identified limitations: the rank constraint problem from ZerO (resolved by padding identity matrices instead of zeros, with a theoretical proof) and the convergence difficulty of identity initialization identified by Bartlett et al. (2019) (resolved by showing that standard SGD with momentum breaks the symmetry that causes all layers to remain identical). Additional practical extensions—patch-maintain convolution for 4D kernels and IDIZ for dead neurons—broaden applicability beyond the square-matrix, single-layer setting of prior identity-control work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IDInit offers a drop-in initialization that consistently improves convergence speed (7.4 fewer epochs on ImageNet, 11.3% FLOP reduction for BERT-Base) and accuracy across CNNs, Transformers, and RNNs, making it practically attractive as a one-line change to any training pipeline. The demonstration that the identity-matrix convergence limitation identified by Bartlett et al. is resolved by the standard SGD-with-momentum setup removes a perceived theoretical barrier to identity-based initialization. Open questions remain: the exact mechanism by which identity-based initialization outperforms random initialization is not fully explained, and a rigorous convergence-rate analysis is deferred to future work. The method's reliance on momentum (though nearly universal in practice) is noted as a mild limitation for ground truths with negative eigenvalues.
