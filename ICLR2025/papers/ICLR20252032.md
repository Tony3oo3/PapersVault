## Problem

Existing safe generation methods for diffusion models either require expensive fine-tuning to remove harmful concepts (unlearning/editing) or rely on static training-free filtering that cannot adapt to new or implicit toxic triggers at inference time. Training-based approaches degrade quality for unrelated content and lack flexibility, while prior training-free methods suffer from distribution shifts when hard-filtering prompts and fail to guard against adversarial or indirect triggers. SAFREE addresses these gaps by providing an instant, adaptive, and training-free safeguard that filters user-defined toxic concepts without modifying model weights.

## Input / Output

SAFREE takes a free-form text prompt and a set of user-defined toxic concept keywords (e.g., 'nudity', 'Van Gogh', 'animal abuse') as input. The toxic keywords are encoded into a subspace matrix C in the CLIP text embedding space, and the prompt is processed at the token-embedding level. The output is a generated image or video that preserves the safe semantics of the original prompt while suppressing the targeted toxic concept. The method is plug-and-play across backbones (SD-v1.4, SDXL, SD-v3, ZeroScopeT2V, CogVideoX) and requires no model weight updates, with only a small inference-time overhead (~0.03s/sample over baseline).

## Method

SAFREE operates in two complementary spaces. In the text embedding space, it first constructs a toxic concept subspace C by concatenating CLIP embeddings of user-defined toxic keywords, then detects trigger tokens by masking each token and measuring the residual distance of the pooled embedding to C; tokens whose removal increases the distance beyond a threshold are flagged as toxic. Rather than deleting or nulling these tokens, SAFREE projects them into a subspace orthogonal to C while constraining them to remain within the original input embedding space, preserving prompt coherence. A self-validating filtering mechanism then dynamically determines how many denoising steps to apply the filtered embeddings, using the cosine distance between the original and projected prompt embeddings as an adaptive threshold. In the visual latent space, an adaptive re-attention mechanism applies a Fourier-domain re-weighting that selectively attenuates low-frequency components amplified by the filtered prompt, reducing toxic content at the pixel level without over-smoothing safe regions.

## Results

SAFREE achieves the lowest Attack Success Rate (ASR) among all training-free methods across all five adversarial prompt benchmarks: 0.034 (I2P), 0.384 (P4D), 0.114 (Ring-A-Bell), 0.585 (MMA-Diffusion), and 0.282 (UnlearnDiff), reducing unsafe content by up to 47% compared to the best training-free baseline (RECE). On generation quality (COCO-30k), SAFREE maintains competitive FID (36.35), CLIP score (31.1), and TIFA (0.790), closely matching the unmodified SD-v1.4 baseline (FID 31.3, CLIP 31.3, TIFA 0.803). The method also reduces unsafe outputs by 48% on SDXL and 47% on SD-v3, and significantly lowers unsafe video generation rates on both ZeroScopeT2V and CogVideoX-5B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free methods, SAFREE outperforms SLD-Max (the strongest no-weight-modification baseline) on all five ASR metrics, with the largest gap on UnlearnDiff (0.282 vs. 0.479, a 41% reduction) and Ring-A-Bell (0.114 vs. 0.570). Against RECE, the best training-free method that does modify weights, SAFREE achieves 47% lower ASR on I2P (0.034 vs. 0.064) and 13% lower on MMA-Diffusion (0.585 vs. 0.675), while P4D is nearly tied (0.384 vs. 0.381). UCE is consistently outperformed by SAFREE across all five adversarial benchmarks. Against training-based methods, SAFREE is competitive with SA on I2P (0.034 vs. 0.062) and P4D (0.384 vs. 0.623), but MACE achieves substantially lower ASR on most benchmarks (e.g., 0.146 on P4D vs. SAFREE's 0.384) at the cost of severe quality degradation (FID 52.24 vs. SAFREE's 36.35). ESD and CA show minimal or no improvement over the base model on several attacks, while SDID actually increases ASR on I2P (0.270 vs. 0.178). Overall, SAFREE provides the best trade-off between safety and generation quality among all methods that do not require retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFREE positions itself within the safe T2I generation literature by departing from both training-based concept erasure (ESD, SA, CA, MACE) and training-free weight-editing methods (UCE, RECE), instead operating entirely in the text embedding and latent spaces without any weight modification. Conceptually, it repurposes the subspace projection idea from ESD/UCE (identifying a toxic concept subspace in CLIP embedding space) but applies it at inference time via orthogonal projection of individual token embeddings, combined with a self-validating mechanism inspired by the observation that different denoising timesteps contribute unevenly to toxic content. The Fourier-domain latent re-attention draws on recent findings about oversmoothing in diffusion (FREEU) and the role of initial noise, extending these ideas into a safety-filtering context. Unlike prior filtering methods like SLD that rely on fixed negative prompts, SAFREE adaptively selects which tokens to project and how many denoising steps to apply, making it more targeted and input-dependent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAFREE offers a practical, zero-cost safety guard that can be plugged into any diffusion-based T2I or T2V model without retraining, which is increasingly important as generative models scale and the definition of 'unsafe' content varies across users and contexts. Its architecture-agnostic design (working across UNet and DiT backbones, T2I and T2V) makes it a versatile baseline for the safety community. However, the authors acknowledge a key limitation: implicit or chain-of-thought-style toxic prompts can still jailbreak the method, leaving open the question of how to handle semantically distributed or obfuscated triggers. The broader open problem of perfect safeguarding in generative models remains unresolved, and SAFREE's filtering-based approach may face diminishing returns as adversarial prompt generation becomes more sophisticated.
