## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for steady-state Fokker-Planck (SFP) equations face a fundamental trade-off: soft normalization constraints require delicate balancing of multi-objective losses and still risk collapse to the trivial zero solution, while hard constraints (normalizing flows, GMMs) restrict the trial solution space and compromise representation capacity. No prior approach can strictly enforce the normalization condition while simultaneously permitting free-form network architectures to model complex, high-dimensional probability densities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes d-dimensional spatial coordinates x, a known drift vector field μ(x), and a diffusion matrix D(x) = ½σ(x)σ(x)ᵀ, with no labeled density data required. Training samples are generated by simulating the underlying SDE via a strong-order-1.5 stochastic Runge-Kutta scheme, which also adaptively determines the integration domain Ω. The network outputs an unnormalized density p̃_θ(x) (strictly positive via softplus activation); the final normalized solution p_θ(x) = p̃_θ(x)/Z_θ is obtained by a single post-processing step that computes the partition function Z_θ via Gauss-Legendre quadrature (TNN) or Monte Carlo sampling (MLP).

## Method

The core contribution is a score-based PDE loss (Theorem 1) that reformulates the plain Fokker-Planck residual into a form depending only on the score s_θ = ∇log p̃_θ, specifically J_score = E_{x~p}[|s_θ·μ̃(x) + ∇·μ̃(x)|] where μ̃(x) = μ(x) − ∇·D(x) − D(x)s_θ(x). Because the score is invariant to the additive constant log Z_θ, the network can freely model the unnormalized density at any scale, and the normalization condition is entirely decoupled from training. This eliminates the zero-solution collapse, removes the need for multi-objective loss balancing, and permits a large learning rate (0.01) with stable convergence. Free-form architectures (TNN with power embedding, or standard MLP) are used to parameterize p̃_θ, and the partition function is computed once after training—via decomposed 1D Gauss-Legendre quadrature for TNN or Monte Carlo estimation for MLP—yielding the normalized solution without any in-loop integral evaluation.

## Results

On the 4D Ring problem, FPNN achieves a MAPE of 3.84% (MAE 5.56×10⁻⁴) versus TFFN's 49.25% (MAE 3.61×10⁻³). On 6D Unimodal, FPNN reaches 4.33% MAPE (MAE 1.48×10⁻³) while TFFN fails with 293% MAPE. On 6D Multi-modal, FPNN attains 12.18% MAPE (MAE 1.98×10⁻⁴) compared to TFFN's 92.90%. Extending to higher dimensions, FPNN achieves 18.30% MAPE on 10D Multi-modal, 18.38% on 10D Gaussian mixture, and 12.79% on 20D Gaussian, with over 20× training speedup and as few as 256 parameters for the 4D Ring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is TFFN (Wang et al., 2024), a tensor neural network that enforces normalization via a hard constraint through its low-rank structure. FPNN decisively outperforms TFFN on every problem and metric: on 4D Ring, FPNN reduces MAPE from 49.25% to 3.84% (a 13× improvement) while using 35,584 parameters (TNN) or as few as 256 (MLP) versus TFFN's 33,792. On 6D Unimodal, TFFN's MAPE exceeds 293% (indicating a near-failure), whereas FPNN achieves 4.33% with only 980 MLP parameters. On 6D Multi-modal, FPNN cuts MAPE from 92.90% to 12.18%. Beyond 6D, TFFN becomes inapplicable, while FPNN extends to 20D with 12.79% MAPE. FPNN also achieves over 20× training speedup, completing the 4D Ring in 4.8 minutes (MLP, 256 params) versus TFFN's 27.6 minutes (33,792 params) without matching accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: score matching (Hyvärinen & Dayan, 2005), which provides the mathematical foundation for learning unnormalized densities; PINNs (Raissi et al., 2019), which supply the neural-network-approximates-PDE-solution paradigm; and Tensor Neural Networks (Wang et al., 2022; Cho et al., 2024), which offer a low-rank architecture amenable to efficient high-dimensional integration. The key departure is from existing SFP solvers that either require labeled data (Zhai et al., 2022), impose soft normalization penalties with delicate multi-objective balancing (Alhussein et al., 2023), or restrict architecture to normalizing flows/GMMs for hard constraints (Tang et al., 2022; Anderson & Farazmand, 2024). FPNN's score PDE loss (Theorem 1) reformulates the plain PDE residual into a form depending only on the score ∇log p̃_θ, thereby decoupling density-shape learning from the intractable partition function and enabling arbitrary network architectures while still strictly satisfying normalization via a one-time post-processing step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean decoupling of score learning from normalization resolves a fundamental tension in SFP equation solving: existing methods must either sacrifice representation capacity (hard constraints) or endure tortuous optimization dynamics (soft constraints). The demonstrated 20× speedup over TFFN and the ability to solve 20D problems with as few as 256 parameters (4D Ring) suggest a practical path toward universal fast solvers for high-dimensional stochastic systems in physics, finance, and mean-field games. However, the framework is limited to steady-state equations; extending it to time-dependent FP equations—where the integration domain and normalizing constant are time-varying—remains an open challenge. The single external baseline (TFFN) and the absence of comparison against recent normalizing-flow or GAN-based density estimators leave some questions about relative positioning unanswered.
