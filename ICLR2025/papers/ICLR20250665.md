## Problem

Previous online learning work on bilateral trade focused exclusively on maximizing cumulative gain-from-trade (sum of traders' net utilities), which can be highly unfair to traders with small profit margins and misaligned with the broker's actual revenue, which depends on the number of trades executed. This paper studies the alternative objective of maximizing trading volume (total number of trades) and provides a complete picture of achievable regret rates under different feedback models and regularity assumptions on the unknown valuation distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250665/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round, two traders arrive with private valuations drawn i.i.d. from an unknown distribution on [0,1], and the broker must post a single trading price in [0,1]. A trade occurs if the price falls between the two valuations. The broker receives either full feedback (both valuations revealed) or 2-bit feedback (only indicators of whether each valuation is below the posted price). The output is a sequence of prices minimizing regret against the best fixed price in hindsight, with the reward being the indicator of a successful trade rather than the gain-from-trade utility.

## Method

The central technical contribution is the Median Lemma, which shows that the expected trade probability at price p equals 2F(p)(1−F(p)), so the optimal price is the median of the valuation cdf and the instantaneous regret is proportional to (1/2 − F(p))². In the full-feedback case with a continuous cdf, the Follow the Empirical Median (FEM) algorithm achieves O(ln T) regret by tracking the sample median, matching a lower bound that holds even under 2-Lipschitz cdfs. In the 2-bit feedback case with an M-Lipschitz cdf, the Median Binary Search (MBS) algorithm exploits the two Bernoulli observations per round to perform a confidence-based binary search toward the median, achieving O(ln(MT) ln T) regret without requiring knowledge of M. The paper further shows that dropping continuity degrades full-feedback regret to Θ(√T) (via a Follow the Empirical Ψ algorithm), while dropping Lipschitzness makes the 2-bit problem unlearnable with linear regret.

## Results

In the full-feedback setting with a continuous cdf, the Follow the Empirical Median (FEM) algorithm achieves O(ln T) regret (Theorem 1), matching a worst-case Ω(ln T) lower bound that holds even under a 2-Lipschitz cdf (Theorem 2). In the 2-bit feedback setting with an M-Lipschitz cdf, the Median Binary Search (MBS) algorithm achieves O(ln(MT) ln T) regret (Theorem 3), with a near-matching Ω(ln(MT)) lower bound (Theorem 4). Dropping the continuity assumption degrades full-feedback regret to Θ(√T) (Theorems 5–6), while dropping the Lipschitz assumption makes the 2-bit feedback problem unlearnable with Ω(T) worst-case regret (Theorem 7).

| Feedback | M-Lipschitz cdf | Continuous cdf | General (no assumption) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full | Ω(ln T) (Thm 2) | O(ln T) (Thm 1) | Θ(√T) (Thm 5+6) |
| 2-Bit | O(ln(MT) ln T), Ω(ln(MT)) (Thm 3+4) | Ω(T) (Thm 7) | Ω(T) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are made against prior theoretical results. The closest prior work is Bolić et al. (2024), which studies the same i.i.d. brokerage problem but with a gain-from-trade reward. Under the M-Lipschitz cdf assumption, Bolić et al. obtain Θ(M ln T) regret in full-feedback, whereas this paper achieves Θ(ln T) even with only a continuous cdf—showing that Lipschitz regularity provides no speedup for the trading-volume objective. In the 2-bit feedback case, Bolić et al. prove a sharp Θ(√(MT)) rate, while this paper achieves the exponentially faster O(ln(MT) ln T) upper bound (tight up to a ln T factor). When the Lipschitz assumption is removed, both problems degrade to Θ(√T) in full-feedback and become unlearnable (Ω(T)) in 2-bit feedback, confirming that the regularity requirements are essential. The paper also provides a concrete example (Section 3) showing that a gain-from-trade maximizing broker can sacrifice the vast majority of traders (probability Θ(ε) of trading) in favor of a tiny high-valuation cluster, while a trading-volume maximizing broker guarantees Θ(1) trade probability for all traders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online learning framework for bilateral trade brokerage established by Bolić et al. (2024), which studied the same i.i.d. setting with flexible buyer/seller roles but targeted gain-from-trade maximization. The key conceptual departure is replacing the gain-from-trade reward with trading volume (number of trades), which shifts the optimal pricing strategy from posting the mean valuation to posting the median. The Median Lemma (Lemma 1) is the central technical tool that enables this shift, revealing that the expected trade probability is a quadratic function of the cdf deviation from 1/2, a structure absent in the gain-from-trade objective. The paper also situates itself within the broader bilateral trade online learning literature (Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2021, 2023, 2024a,b; Azar et al. 2022; Bernasconi et al. 2024), but those works either fix buyer/seller roles or use different reward structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a complete regret characterization (matching upper and lower bounds up to log factors) for trading volume maximization across all combinations of feedback type (full vs. 2-bit) and cdf regularity (Lipschitz, continuous, general), filling a gap left by prior work that only addressed gain-from-trade. The practical motivation is compelling: in OTC markets where brokers earn per-trade fees and most traders operate on thin margins, volume maximization is arguably the more realistic and fairer objective than gain-from-trade. Open questions include non-stationary variants, definite seller/buyer roles, contextual extensions, 1-bit feedback (full privacy), and whether α-regret can restore learnability in the unlearnable 2-bit feedback regime.
