## Problem

Current text-to-3D generation methods struggle to produce meshes with fine details and delicate structures such as fingers, hair, and facial features. Implicit representations (NeRF, Triplanes, 3D Gaussians) introduce floating-point artifacts that require aggressive smoothing, erasing detail, while dense cube-based methods (DMTet, FlexiCubes) scale cubically in memory and compute, making high-resolution generation impractical. This gap is especially acute for anime character generation, where these intricate features are essential to the character's identity.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a text prompt, converts it to four 512×512 multi-view images via a fine-tuned PIXART-Σ text-to-image model and a multi-view diffusion model, then encodes these with DINO into pose-aware tokens. The output is a textured 3D mesh at 256³ resolution (~170K faces) where both geometry (SDF-based) and texture (30-dim per-cube features) are optimized simultaneously within the same representation. The sparsification step reduces active cubes from 64³ to ~12K (<5%), enabling this high-resolution output without cubic memory blowup.

## Method

The core contribution is SparseCubes, a sparse differentiable mesh representation that embeds 25-dimensional geometric features (SDF, vertex deformation, and edge/face/midpoint weights derived from FlexiCubes) and 30-dimensional texture features directly into each cube, enabling simultaneous geometry and texture optimization. A sparsification criterion based on SDF sign changes at cube corners, with two rounds of neighbor propagation, retains only ~5% of cubes, cutting memory by 50% and compute by over 95%. The Sparse Cube Transformer processes these cubes in a two-stage pipeline: a Coarse Proposal Network at 32³ resolution predicts valid cube locations, then the SCT refines them through three resolution stages (64³→128³→256³) using local self-attention over minimal 2³ and 4³ regions instead of global attention, achieving over 1000× compute reduction in the self-attention step. Training is staged—first the CPN for 30K iterations on 32 A100 GPUs, then the SCT with a warm-up phase and fine-tuning—using a composite loss of image L2, LPIPS, mask L1, depth L2, and normal L2. A random displacement data augmentation addresses off-center characters (e.g., large tails or wings) that would otherwise receive under-optimized positional embeddings.

## Results

On the anime avatar dataset (Table 1), the proposed method achieves the best PSNR (24.99), SSIM (0.9283), and Chamfer Distance (5.90×10⁻⁵) among all compared methods, with a generation time of 20 seconds. The CD error is reduced by approximately 70% compared to the second-best method (Instant3D at 19.42×10⁻⁵). On the Objaverse LVIS subset (Table 2), the method also leads all metrics: PSNR 26.78, SSIM 0.9452, and CD 36.86×10⁻⁵. On the realistic RenderPeople dataset (Table 5), it achieves PSNR 25.93 and CD 9.60×10⁻⁵, outperforming LGM (PSNR 20.02, CD 11.78) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines across every quantitative metric tested. Against the closest competitor LGM (PSNR 24.05, CD 28.49), the proposed method improves PSNR by ~0.9 dB and reduces Chamfer Distance by ~79% (5.90 vs. 28.49), while also being 2.6× faster (20s vs. 52s). Compared to Instant3D (PSNR 23.19, CD 19.42), the CD error drops by ~70%, confirming substantially better mesh geometry. DreamGaussian and InstantMesh (both used as pre-trained models without fine-tuning) lag far behind on all metrics, with CD errors of 184.96 and 492.48 respectively. The SMPL-based TeCH and ImageDream are only compared qualitatively (Appendix Fig. 9), where the paper notes TeCH's reliance on the SMPL body model produces poor results for diverse hairstyles and outfits. On the general 3D object benchmark (Objaverse LVIS), the method extends its lead, achieving PSNR 26.78 vs. LGM's 24.70 and CD 36.86 vs. LGM's 116.14, demonstrating that the SparseCubes advantage generalizes beyond anime characters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FlexiCubes/DMTet line of differentiable cube-based mesh representations by introducing a sparse variant (SparseCubes) that retains only the small fraction of cubes intersecting the surface, reducing computation by over 95%. Architecturally, it follows the large reconstruction model paradigm established by Instant3D and LGM (DINO-encoded multi-view tokens feeding a transformer decoder), but replaces their 2D Triplane or 3D Gaussian implicit representations with explicit 3D SparseCubes processed by a Sparse Cube Transformer. The key conceptual departure is the use of local self-attention over minimal 3D neighborhoods (2³ or 4³ cubes) instead of global self-attention, which the authors argue both enables the efficiency gains and helps preserve fine local geometric detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: enabling 256³-resolution mesh generation at 5 seconds per iteration (vs. 2850s for dense cubes) makes high-detail 3D character generation feasible on standard hardware, and the 70% Chamfer Distance reduction over the second-best method indicates meaningfully better geometric fidelity for delicate structures like fingers and hair. However, the approach is currently validated primarily on 20K anime characters, and the text-to-image front-end (fine-tuned PIXART-Σ) still struggles with prompt alignment for uncommon character types. Open questions include whether SparseCubes scale to 1k³ resolution as the authors envision, whether the method generalizes well beyond stylized characters to arbitrary detailed objects, and whether the local-attention design transfers to other 3D generation tasks.
