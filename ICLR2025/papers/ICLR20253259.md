## Problem

Variational inference objectives face a fundamental trade-off: the ELBO (reverse KL) is mode-seeking and underestimates uncertainty, while mass-covering alternatives like SNIS-fKL introduce bias and suffer from high gradient variance that does not vanish at convergence. This paper asks whether a contrastive estimation framework can yield variational objectives that are simultaneously mass-covering, stable to train, and possess zero-variance gradients when the approximation is exact.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the unnormalized joint density p(θ, x_obs) evaluated at sampled points and a choice of negative distribution p⁻(θ) (which is never actually sampled from, only evaluated). It produces a parameterized variational distribution q_φ(θ) — ranging from simple factorized normals to masked autoregressive flows with rational quadratic splines — that can be sampled to obtain posterior approximations. A key constraint is that the proposal, variational, and true posterior must share support to guarantee exact recovery at optimality.

## Method

SoftCVI reframes posterior approximation as a K-way classification task: at each step, K samples are drawn from the variational distribution (serving as the proposal), and ground truth soft labels are computed analytically as softmax of log-ratios p(θ_k, x_obs)/p⁻(θ_k). A classifier parameterized as softmax of log-ratios q_φ(θ_k)/p⁻(θ_k) is trained via cross-entropy loss, so the optimal classifier recovers the true posterior. The negative distribution is set to p⁻(θ) = q_φ(θ)^α with a tempering hyperparameter α ∈ [0,1], where lower α favors more mass-covering solutions. When α=1, the SoftCVI objective equals the SNIS-fKL objective plus a normalization term whose gradient acts as a control variate, provably driving gradient variance to zero at convergence — a property absent from standard SNIS-fKL and ELBO estimators.

## Results

Across four Bayesian inference tasks (Eight Schools, Linear Regression, SLCP, GARCH(1,1)), SoftCVI with α=0.75 achieves the best-calibrated posteriors and highest average log-probability of reference posterior samples (a proxy for lower forward KL divergence) compared to ELBO and SNIS-fKL. On the multimodal SLCP task, only SoftCVI objectives successfully capture all four symmetric modes; ELBO frequently misses modes entirely while SNIS-fKL approximates individual modes poorly. The advantage of SoftCVI over SNIS-fKL is most pronounced at smaller sample sizes (K=2), where the built-in control variate in SoftCVI's gradient estimator provides substantially lower variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SoftCVI against two baselines: the ELBO (reverse KL) and SNIS-fKL (forward KL via self-normalized importance sampling). Against ELBO, SoftCVI clearly wins on all tasks: ELBO's mode-seeking behavior causes it to miss posterior modes on SLCP and produce overconfident, poorly calibrated posteriors across all tasks. Against SNIS-fKL, the comparison is more nuanced: the paper proves that SoftCVI with α=1 and SNIS-fKL produce identical gradients in expectation, but SoftCVI's objective naturally includes a control variate (the normalization term) that drives gradient variance to zero as the variational distribution converges to the true posterior. Empirically, SoftCVI (α=1) outperforms SNIS-fKL on log-probability of reference samples and calibration on all tasks except Eight Schools, where they perform similarly. The gap widens significantly at K=2, where SNIS-fKL's gradient variance (scaling as 1/K) degrades its performance substantially while SoftCVI maintains a reasonable signal-to-noise ratio. SoftCVI with α=0.75 further improves over both baselines by increasing mass-covering pressure, though α=0 (flat negative) leads to mass leakage into low-density regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SoftCVI bridges two previously separate lines of work: contrastive estimation for density ratio learning (Gutmann & Hyvärinen, 2010) and variational inference for posterior approximation (Jordan et al., 1999). The key conceptual departure from standard contrastive learning is that no explicit positive or negative samples are needed—soft labels are computed analytically from the unnormalized posterior, and the classifier is parameterized directly by the variational distribution rather than a separate network. The paper also reveals that the SNIS-fKL objective (Jerfel et al., 2021) is a special case of SoftCVI (α=1) that lacks a natural control variate, and connects to InfoNCE (Oord et al., 2018) as the hard-label limit. Unlike adversarial VI (Huszár, 2017; Makhzani et al., 2015), SoftCVI does not require a separate discriminator but does require an explicitly parameterized variational family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-variance gradient at the optimum (even for finite K) addresses a practical pain point in variational inference where gradient noise near convergence destabilizes training, and the derived lower-variance SNIS-fKL estimator (eq. 15) is immediately useful to practitioners already using that objective. The mass-covering behavior is particularly relevant for scientific applications requiring calibrated uncertainty quantification, where the mode-seeking ELBO is known to understate uncertainty. Open questions include the principled choice of the tempering hyperparameter α (currently set heuristically), whether alternative f-divergences or label smoothing/temperature scaling can further improve calibration, and whether the approach extends to implicit variational families. A limitation is that SoftCVI requires an explicit density for q_φ, precluding the use of implicit models that adversarial VI enables.
