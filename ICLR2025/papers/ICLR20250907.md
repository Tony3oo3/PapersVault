## Problem

Regression uncertainty quantification typically assumes a parametric distribution (usually Gaussian) around the predicted mean, which cannot capture skewed, multi-modal, or otherwise complex aleatoric target distributions. While classification expresses data uncertainty naturally through class probabilities, no practical distribution-free method existed for neural network regression that could learn arbitrary output distributions without committing to a specific density form.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250907/cropped_page7_idx16.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a feature vector X and, at each evaluation, concatenates a freshly sampled standard normal noise vector ε to the input (or a hidden-layer activation). The nondeterministic portion is evaluated n times per forward pass, producing n samples (and optionally n importance weights in the weighted variant) of shape [b×n×k] for batch size b and target dimensionality k. For multivariate regression (e.g., 784-dimensional MNIST pixels), the output is a joint distribution over all k variables, sampled without any locality or factorization assumptions.

## Method

The paper introduces nondeterministic neural network architectures where randomness is injected via concatenated Gaussian noise, and the network is trained by backpropagating a novel unbiased O(n log n) sample-based CRPS loss over all n samples simultaneously. The key derivation is an unbiased modification of the sorted-sample CRPS formula (replacing 1/(2n²) with 1/(2n(n−1)) and adjusting the rank coefficients), which corrects the known underestimation of prediction variance. A weighted-sample CRPS variant is also derived, enabling importance-weighted sampling that supports multi-head and layered-multi-head architectures where different heads model different regions of the output distribution. For multivariate targets, the Energy Score (multivariate CRPS) is used in its O(n²) form. Epistemic uncertainty is handled by pooling samples from a deep ensemble of independently trained networks, combining the distribution-free aleatoric modeling with standard ensemble-based epistemic quantification.

## Results

On the UCI regression uncertainty benchmark (9 datasets), the best variant WCRPS_e (weighted CRPS with deep ensembles) achieves the best NLL on 6/9 datasets and best RMSE on 8/9 datasets. Headline NLL scores include 0.31±0.03 on energy, -1.38±0.01 on kin8nm, 2.57±0.01 on power, and 2.05±0.01 on protein. In RMSE, WCRPS_e dominates with scores like 2.91±0.18 (boston), 4.94±0.17 (concrete), 0.41±0.01 (energy), and 3.47±0.02 (protein). The method also demonstrates strong calibration on most datasets, with underconfidence only on yacht and naval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016), WCRPS_e wins on 8/9 datasets in NLL, with the only loss being a marginal 0.01 gap on concrete (2.95 vs 2.94). Against PBP-MV (Sun et al., 2017), WCRPS_e wins on all 9 datasets in NLL, with the largest margin on energy (0.31 vs 1.01) and protein (2.05 vs 2.77). Against Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), WCRPS_e also wins on all 9 datasets in NLL, most notably on energy (0.31 vs 0.62) and protein (2.05 vs 2.66). The closest competition is MDN_bnn (MDN with BNN-style epistemic uncertainty), which beats WCRPS_e on naval (-6.72 vs -6.51), protein (1.96 vs 2.05), and wine (-5.11 vs 0.80, though wine is a discrete classification task where NLL is unreliable). In RMSE, WCRPS_e dominates all baselines on 8/9 datasets, with the sole exception being yacht where MDN_bnn edges ahead by 0.01 (0.77 vs 0.78). The paper notes that the single-head weighted architecture's implicit bias toward smooth distributions is an advantage in most practical settings, while multi-head variants underperform WCRPS_e on many datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on nondeterministic neural networks for probabilistic regression, building most directly on DISCO Nets (Bouchacourt et al., 2016), which similarly inject random noise for nondeterminism and optimize a dissimilarity-based loss. The key departure is the use of the CRPS (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007) as the training objective, with the authors deriving the first unbiased O(n log n) sample-based formula and the first weighted-sample variants in both O(n²) and O(n log n) forms. Unlike BNNs that model epistemic uncertainty through weight distributions and MDN that restricts aleatoric uncertainty to Gaussian mixtures, this approach learns arbitrary output distributions purely from samples, positioning itself as a truly distribution-free alternative to both families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a conceptually simple, implementation-friendly way to capture complex (multi-modal, skewed) aleatoric distributions without parametric assumptions, and the multivariate extension to 784-dimensional outputs (MNIST inversion) demonstrates that the framework scales beyond the univariate setting where most prior distribution-free regression work operates. Limitations include the O(n²) energy score for multivariate targets (no O(n log n) analogue exists), the need for KDE-based likelihood estimation for comparison with prior work, and underconfidence on some UCI datasets. Open questions include whether other nondeterminism injection strategies (beyond concatenated noise) could improve expressivity, and whether the multivariate case can be accelerated with techniques from sliced MMD or Riesz kernel literature.
