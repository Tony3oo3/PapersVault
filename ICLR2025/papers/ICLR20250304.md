## Problem

Noisy linear SCMs with confounders are known to be identifiable when all confounders and noise are non-Gaussian, and unidentifiable when all are Gaussian, but the identifiability status when only the noise is Gaussian while confounders remain non-Gaussian was previously unresolved. This paper closes that gap by establishing precise finite identifiability conditions and characterizing the equivalence class of SCMs that share the same observed PDF.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the joint PDF of observed variables (equivalently, the SCM parameter pair (M, Q) derived from the structural equations) and iterates over all n! permutation matrices to enumerate the equivalence class. The output is a finite set of at most n! distinct SCMs (each specified by matrices A, B, C), an analytical expression for the confounder characteristic function valid across all equivalent SCMs, and the causal structure order of each member. The key structural constraint is that Q must satisfy Q_ij*Q_ji = 0 for all i != j, encoding the acyclicity of the causal DAG.

## Method

The core technique uses characteristic functions to decompose the observed PDF into its Gaussian (noise) and non-Gaussian (confounder) components, leveraging the fact that under Assumption 2 (confounders have no Gaussian factor), the non-Gaussian part must match across equivalent SCMs, which forces M = M' via a Darmois-Skitovich-type argument (Lemma 5) combined with the linear independence of M's columns. The Gaussian part is then handled through the covariance matrix Sigma = QQ^T, where the causal-structure constraint Q_ij*Q_ji = 0 restricts valid Q matrices to those expressible as P^T Q_p P with Q_p the Cholesky factor of P Sigma P^T, yielding at most n! candidates (one per permutation matrix). Algorithm 1 systematically applies this construction for each permutation P, checking via Lemma 1 whether PQP^T is lower triangular to determine if P yields a distinct SCM or merely reorders the same one. The confounder PDF is recovered analytically by inverting the informative sub-matrix M-tilde and dividing the observed CF by the Gaussian noise CF (Theorem 2, Part 3).

## Results

The paper establishes that a linear SCM with Gaussian noise and non-Gaussian confounders is finitely identifiable with an equivalence class of at most n! SCMs (Theorem 2), where n is the number of observed variables. Each SCM in the equivalence class shares the same M matrix and the confounders' joint characteristic function (given analytically in Eq. 3), differing only in the Q matrix via a permutation-based Cholesky decomposition. Unique identifiability is achieved if the causal order is specified (Corollary 1), or if all observed variables are isolated (Corollary 3). In contrast, when both noise and confounders are Gaussian, the SCM is not finitely identifiable—infinitely many SCMs share the same observed PDF (Theorem 1), and every such SCM can be represented as a confounder-free SCM with the same joint PDF (Proposition 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or dataset benchmarks. The key comparison is against prior identifiability results: Salehkaleybar et al. (2020) require both noise and confounders to be non-Gaussian for finite identifiability, whereas this paper shows non-Gaussian confounders alone suffice when noise is Gaussian. Li et al. (2024) allow some Gaussian noise but require restrictive graphical conditions (e.g., generalized pure pairs) for unique identifiability; here, unique identifiability follows simply from specifying the causal order without extra graphical assumptions. The ICA-based result (Pavan & Miranda, 2018) gives identifiability up to column permutations and scalings of [M,Q] for fully non-Gaussian sources; this paper refines that by showing the scaling ambiguity disappears under the unit-covariance assumption and that the equivalence class is bounded by n! rather than being unbounded. The paper also extends the Gaussian unidentifiability result beyond counter-example arguments (Eriksson & Koivunen, 2004; Hoyer et al., 2008) by proving a general equivalence to confounder-free models (Proposition 1) and showing the result holds even when the number of confounders is less than n (Proposition 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability analysis of linear SCMs from the well-studied extremes (all non-Gaussian, where ICA/Darmois-Skitovich yields finite identifiability up to permutations, and all Gaussian, where unidentifiability is known via counterexamples) to the previously unresolved mixed case of Gaussian noise with non-Gaussian confounders. It builds directly on the characteristic-function framework of Eriksson & Koivunen (2003) and the Darmois-Skitovich-based results of Pavan & Miranda (2018), while leveraging the canonical-model reduction from Hoyer et al. (2008) to handle non-root confounders. The key methodological departure is the use of Cholesky decomposition structure of the noise covariance to bound the equivalence class at n! and to derive the confounder PDF analytically, rather than relying on higher-order cumulants or overcomplete ICA as in prior latent-variable causal discovery work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that Gaussian noise does not destroy finite identifiability when confounders remain non-Gaussian is practically significant, since measurement noise in many physical and engineering systems is well-approximated by Gaussians while latent confounders often are not. The clean n! bound and the algorithmic procedure for enumerating the equivalence class provide a concrete, computable characterization rather than a mere existence argument. However, the analysis is restricted to linear SCMs with known causal structure (or at most a known partial order), and the paper does not address how to estimate the 'first' SCM from data, leaving the full causal discovery pipeline open. Future work would need to relax linearity, handle mixed Gaussian/non-Gaussian confounders, and develop data-driven estimation of the equivalence class.
