## Problem

Graph Transformers require effective tokenizers to process graph-structured data, but existing approaches either rely on heuristic structural encodings or co-train GNNs with the Transformer, coupling the two training stages. This paper asks whether a standalone, self-supervised graph tokenizer can produce discrete tokens that are both memory-efficient and generalizable enough for a vanilla Transformer to capture long-range graph dependencies without architectural modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253505/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d_x} and edge structure E. The pretrained tokenizer maps each node to c discrete integer tokens (one per RVQ codebook level), which are then serialized into per-node sequences of length c×(k+1) using Personalized PageRank over the original edges augmented with KNN semantic edges. At inference, only the discrete token indices and a small codebook table (c×K×d_c) are needed rather than the full feature matrix, yielding 23–270× memory reduction depending on graph size. The Transformer encoder outputs node-level, graph-level, or edge-level predictions for classification, regression, or link prediction tasks.

## Method

GQT decouples tokenizer training from Transformer training in two stages. First, a GAT encoder is trained with a multi-task self-supervised loss combining Deep Graph Infomax (node-graph mutual information), GraphMAE2 (masked reconstruction plus teacher-student distillation), and a VQ commitment loss; its output representations are quantized via Residual Vector Quantization into c hierarchical discrete tokens per node, with codebooks updated by K-Means initialization and EMA. Second, a vanilla Transformer encoder with flash attention processes per-node sequences constructed by concatenating the target node's tokens with those of its top-k PPR-ranked neighbors (over original + semantic edges), modulated by structural gating (softmax-normalized PPR scores), aggregated codebook embeddings, and two trainable positional encodings (node-level and hierarchy-level). The key design insight is that the GNN captures local interactions while the Transformer focuses exclusively on long-range dependencies, and RVQ provides both memory compression and a regularization effect that improves robustness to adversarial attacks compared to reconstruction-based RQ-VAE.

## Results

GQT achieves state-of-the-art performance on 20 out of 22 benchmarks spanning homophilic, heterophilic, long-range, and large-scale graph tasks. On homophilic node classification, GQT leads on 7/8 datasets (e.g., 71.81% on CoraFull, 97.53% on Physics, 80.14% on WikiCS), outperforming the best GT (NAGphormer/Exphormer) and GNN (GRAND+) baselines. On large-scale datasets, GQT achieves 82.13% ROC-AUC on ogbn-proteins, 73.14% accuracy on ogbn-arxiv, 82.46% on ogbn-products, and 83.76% on pokec, surpassing all baselines including those that run out of memory (GraphGPS, Exphormer). The method also delivers substantial memory reductions: 270× for a 10⁶-node graph with 1024-dim features, 23× on Physics, and 30× on ogbn-products.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GQT outperforms all 8 GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, APPNP, GPRGNN, GraphSAINT, GraphSAGE, PPRGo, GRAND+) on 7 of 8 homophilic datasets, with the largest margin over GRAND+ on WikiCS (80.14% vs. no reported result) and Computer (93.37% vs. 88.74%). Among the 10 GT baselines, GQT surpasses the strongest competitors NAGphormer and Exphormer on all 8 homophilic datasets (e.g., +0.30 on CoraFull, +0.62 on CS, +1.60 on WikiCS), while GraphGPS and Exphormer suffer OOM on several datasets. On heterophilic benchmarks, GQT wins 5/6 datasets, with the most dramatic gain on Minesweeper (97.36% ROC-AUC vs. 90.89% for SGFormer, a 6.5-point improvement) and Roman-Empire (90.98% vs. 89.03% for Exphormer). On all four large-scale datasets, GQT achieves the best results, including 82.46% on ogbn-products where GraphGPS and Exphormer fail with OOM, and 83.76% on pokec where the next best (LINKX) reaches only 82.04%. The ablation study confirms that the multi-task SSL objectives (GraphMAE2 + DGI) contribute more to performance than vector quantization itself, and that semantic edges and structural gating are particularly critical for heterophilic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

GQT builds on Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) from image generation (Lee et al., 2022) and the VQ-VAE commitment mechanism (Van Den Oord et al., 2017), adapting both to the graph domain for the first time in a tokenizer context. It departs from prior graph tokenizers (TokenGT, NAGphormer, GraphiT, VCR-Graphormer) that either use hand-crafted structural encodings or co-train GNN encoders jointly with the Transformer, by fully decoupling tokenizer training via multi-task self-supervised objectives (DGI + GraphMAE2). The serialization strategy—combining PCA-based semantic edges with Personalized PageRank sequences—draws on structural encoding ideas from GraphGPS and Exphormer but repurposes them as a pre-processing step rather than a learned architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling tokenizer training from Transformer training and using RVQ to compress node representations into discrete tokens, GQT enables vanilla Transformers to process large-scale graphs (2.4M+ nodes) with 30–270× memory reduction while achieving SOTA on 20/22 benchmarks spanning homophilic, heterophilic, and long-range settings. This opens a practical path toward Graph Foundation Models where a shared discrete token space could be reused across datasets and coupled with LLMs. Key open questions include whether the pre-trained tokenizer can be fine-tuned end-to-end with the Transformer, how the approach extends to generative graph modeling, and whether the PPR-based serialization generalizes to dynamic or temporal graphs.
