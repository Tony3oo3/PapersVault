## Problem

Text-to-image generation excels at creative content but hallucinates on knowledge-intensive concepts (landmarks, species, logos), while text-to-image retrieval grounds answers in real images but is limited to a fixed database and lacks creativity. No prior work unifies both tasks within a single Large Multimodal Model; existing ensemble approaches like GILL rely on an external CLIP retriever and diffusion decoder, incurring extra alignment costs and the inefficiency of dense retrieval at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250136/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a single RGB image as output, which is either a newly generated image (decoded from discrete visual tokens via a diffusion decoder) or a retrieved image from a pre-tokenized database. Database images are compressed into discrete visual token sequences (32 tokens for SEED-LLaMA; variable-length 37–130 tokens for LaVIT) and stored in a Trie structure that constrains the autoregressive search space. The system autonomously decides per prompt whether to return the generated or retrieved image, with no user-specified mode selection.

## Method

TIGeR-ONE is a training-free, model-agnostic framework built on top of existing autoregressive LMMs (SEED-LLaMA, LaVIT) that unifies generation and retrieval under a single next-token-prediction objective. For retrieval, it introduces Forward Beam Search (FBS) that performs Trie-constrained autoregressive decoding over pre-tokenized database images, reducing retrieval from |G| forward passes to N (sequence length) passes, followed by Reverse Re-Ranking (RRR) that re-scores the top-B candidates using the reverse conditional likelihood log p(X|Y) to break through the recall ceiling of forward-only proxies. For generation, it runs unconstrained autoregressive decoding in parallel with FBS, requiring only N forward propagations total. An autonomous decision mechanism then compares the prompt-image similarity of the generated and retrieved candidates (using the debiased PMI or reverse proxy) and selects the higher-scoring image, incurring no additional computational cost since similarities are already computed during FBS and RRR.

## Results

On TIGeR-Bench (unified generation + retrieval), Ours (LaVIT) achieves CLIP-T 28.45 and CLIP-I 61.37, outperforming the strongest agent baseline (SDXL+CLIP+Qwen2-VL*) at 27.91/60.65 and all LMM baselines. On Flickr30K, Ours (SEED-LLaMA) reaches R@1 71.70 / R@5 91.82 / R@10 95.44, surpassing CLIP (68.70/90.60/95.20) and generative retrieval baselines GRACE (37.40) and IRGen (49.00) in a training-free manner. On MS-COCO, Ours (SEED-LLaMA) achieves R@1 46.11 vs. CLIP's 37.80. On VisDial multi-turn chat, Ours (SEED-LLaMA) reaches CLIP-I 70.9 at 10 rounds, improving +3.0 over vanilla SEED-LLaMA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the unified TIGeR-Bench task, Ours (LaVIT) (CLIP-T 28.45, CLIP-I 61.37) outperforms the best agent baseline SDXL+CLIP+Qwen2-VL* (27.91/60.65) and all single-task experts: SDXL for generation (26.79/46.71) and CLIP for retrieval (25.22/53.95). Against GILL, the only prior LMM supporting both tasks, Ours (LaVIT) improves CLIP-T by 16.3 points and CLIP-I by 46.1 points, reflecting GILL's reliance on an external CLIP retriever. On Flickr30K retrieval, Ours (SEED-LLaMA) achieves R@1 71.70, beating CLIP (68.70) by 3.0 points and substantially outperforming trained generative retrieval baselines GRACE (37.40) and IRGen (49.00) without any task-specific training. On MS-COCO, Ours (SEED-LLaMA) reaches R@1 46.11 vs. CLIP's 37.80 (+8.3) and IRGen's 29.60 (+16.5). On VisDial multi-turn chat, Ours (SEED-LLaMA) surpasses GLIDE (58.7) and SD-v1.5 (62.2) at 10 rounds with CLIP-I 70.9, while Ours (LaVIT) improves over vanilla LaVIT by +6.5 at 10 rounds, showing the unified framework's advantage grows with dialogue complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the discrete visual tokenization paradigm of SEED-LLaMA and LaVIT, which encode images into sequences of discrete codes for autoregressive generation, and on the generative retrieval framework of Tay et al. (2022) and Li et al. (2024b), which use Trie-constrained beam search to retrieve items via token decoding. It departs from GILL (Koh et al., 2023), the closest prior work, by eliminating the external CLIP retriever and diffusion decoder ensemble in favor of a single LMM that handles both generation and retrieval natively through likelihood-based similarity proxies. The key conceptual departure is identifying and exploiting the intrinsic discriminative ability of LMMs—particularly the reverse conditional likelihood log p(X|Y)—as a training-free retrieval signal, and introducing a PMI-based debiasing strategy to address the visual prior problem that degrades forward-direction discrimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, model-agnostic framework offers a practical path to unified image acquisition without additional alignment training or iterative denoising, with the notable efficiency property that retrieval cost remains constant regardless of database size (unlike dense retrieval). The TIGeR-Bench provides a much-needed evaluation protocol spanning creative and knowledge-intensive domains, exposing the complementary weaknesses of pure generation and pure retrieval. However, the decision mechanism suffers from a generation preference bias (especially in LaVIT), and the approach is restricted to discrete-tokenization LMMs; the root causes of the observed modality asymmetry between captioning and generation remain unexplained, and whether the framework extends to continuous-representation LMMs or more complex interleaved multimodal contexts is an open question.
