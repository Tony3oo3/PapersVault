## Problem

Neural transform coding (NTC) compresses sources by transforming to a latent space, applying scalar quantization (integer rounding), and entropy coding. The paper demonstrates that for sources with latent dimensionality greater than one, this scalar quantization is fundamentally sub-optimal: the induced quantization regions remain square-like in source space and cannot match the hexagonal (or more general) regions of optimal vector quantization, regardless of how the learned transforms are designed. The gap is attributed to the quantizer choice, not the transform architecture.

## Input / Output

The method accepts source vectors x of varying types: 2-D or n-dimensional i.i.d. sequences (Gaussian, Laplacian, Banana marginal), 16-D physics signals, 33-D speech vectors, and variable-resolution Kodak images. For images, a product lattice is applied along the channel dimension of the latent tensor (C×H×W), with C/n independent lattice quantizers applied H·W times. The output is a compressed bitstream whose rate is estimated via cross-entropy of a normalizing-flow density model over lattice Voronoi cells, plus a reconstruction x̂. For block coding (BLTC), the input is a sequence of n i.i.d. source vectors, and the output rate is measured per-sample across the block.

## Method

Lattice Transform Coding (LTC) replaces NTC's integer-lattice scalar quantizer in the latent space with a general lattice quantizer Q_Λ, using best-known lattices per dimension (A₂ hexagonal, D₄*, E₈, Leech Λ₂₄) that have efficient closest-vector-problem solvers. The rate term is computed via Monte-Carlo integration of a multivariate normalizing-flow density (RealNVP) over each Voronoi cell, replacing the factorized CDF-based likelihood used in NTC; backpropagation through the non-differentiable quantizer uses either the straight-through estimator or dithered quantization with uniform noise over the Voronoi region. For fixed-rate coding, nested lattice quantization (coarse lattice Λ_c defining a bounded region of fine lattice Λ_f) yields O(n) encoding complexity with a fixed alphabet. Block-LTC (BLTC) extends LTC to compress n i.i.d. source vectors simultaneously by applying separate LTC models across the block in the latent space, enabling approach to the asymptotic rate-distortion function R(D) rather than the one-shot ECVQ bound.

## Results

On the Kodak image benchmark, LTC with the Leech lattice (Λ₂₄) achieves a −16.708% BD-rate gain over the Cheng2020-NTC baseline, and −11.895% relative to the VTM standard codec, outperforming ELIC (−10.607% vs. NTC) and NVTC (−4.953% vs. NTC). On 2-D Gaussian and Banana synthetic sources, LTC recovers the quantization regions and rate-distortion performance of optimal ECVQ, whereas NTC is stuck at ECSQ performance. On n-length i.i.d. sequences (Gaussian, Laplacian, Banana-1D), LTC with best-known lattices (A₂, D₄*, E₈, Λ₂₄) approaches the rate-distortion function R(D), bridging up to 30% of the gap to R(D) on real-world sources. Inference runtime increases modestly: 4.002 s/image for E₈ vs. 3.241 s for NTC, and 8.634 s for Λ₂₄ on single-core CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTC consistently outperforms NTC (Cheng2020) across all lattice choices on Kodak, with gains scaling with lattice dimension: −2.337% (Dₙ*), −5.274% (E₈), and −16.708% (Λ₂₄). Against the standard VTM codec, LTC-Λ₂₄ achieves −11.895% BD-rate, substantially beating VTM's own −7.879% over NTC. LTC also surpasses ELIC (−10.607% vs. NTC) and NVTC (−4.953% vs. NTC) on Kodak, though the authors note that NVTC and ELIC use different transforms and entropy models, so the comparison is not purely isolating quantization. On synthetic sources (2-D Gaussian, Banana, i.i.d. sequences), LTC matches or approaches ECVQ (the optimal one-shot code), while NTC is provably sub-optimal and stuck at ECSQ performance. Compared to ECLQ (lattice quantization without learned transforms), LTC performs much better at low and moderate rates because the synthesis transform warps reconstructions to match source statistics. The Dₙ* lattice used by LVQAC (Zhang & Wu, 2023) yields only −2.337% BD-rate, confirming that sub-optimal lattice choice limits gains, whereas best-known lattices unlock the full packing-efficiency benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Nonlinear Transform Coding (NTC) by Ballé et al. (2021) and the specific Cheng et al. (2020) image compression architecture, identifying a fundamental limitation: scalar (integer) quantization in the latent space is sub-optimal whenever the source's latent dimensionality exceeds one. The proposed Lattice Transform Coding (LTC) framework replaces integer rounding with general lattice quantization, drawing on classical lattice theory (Conway & Sloane, 1999) and the companding literature (Gersho, 1979; Linder et al., 1999) which predicted that the choice of lattice in the latent space determines performance. Unlike concurrent works LVQAC (Zhang & Wu, 2023) and LVQ-VAE (Kudo et al., 2023) that use lattice quantization primarily to exploit latent correlations, this paper provides a principled diagnosis of *why* lattice quantization is needed and demonstrates that the NTC transforms themselves cannot compensate for a sub-optimal quantizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that NTC's learned transforms are fundamentally unable to overcome scalar quantization's sub-optimality (quantization regions remain square-like regardless of architecture) challenges a working assumption in the neural compression community and redirects design focus toward the quantizer. LTC provides a practical, low-complexity path to approaching the rate-distortion function R(D) without the exponential cost of vector quantization, with a -16.7% BD-rate gain on Kodak images. Open questions include scaling to lattice dimensions beyond 24 (where efficient CVP solvers are lacking), integrating LTC with more advanced NTC architectures like ELIC, and whether the residual gap to R(D) can be fully closed with better entropy models or higher-dimensional lattices.
