## Problem

In multi-node distributed learning, both inter-node and intra-node label shifts cause standard ERM-based methods (FedAvg, FedBN, SCAFFOLD) to fail because they assume identical train/test distributions. The core technical bottleneck is accurate estimation of test-to-train importance ratios, where existing MLLS-based approaches suffer from predictor miscalibration that propagates into ratio estimation error. This paper tackles the gap by improving the predictor's calibration through entropy regularization, enabling more robust ratio estimation under label shift while keeping data confined to each node.

## Input / Output

Each node provides labeled training images (e.g., 28×28 MNIST, 32×32 CIFAR-10) and unlabeled test images from a shifted label distribution. The method outputs per-class importance ratios r(y) = p^te(y)/p^tr(y) as a vector in R_+^m, which are then used to weight the global model's training loss. In the multi-node setting, only these low-dimensional ratio vectors (one per class) are communicated between nodes, preserving data privacy; no raw images or gradients are shared. The final output is a global classifier (LeNet or ResNet-18) that predicts class labels for new test samples.

## Method

VRLS augments the standard cross-entropy training objective for the predictor f_θ with a Shannon entropy regularizer Ω(f_θ) = Σ_c softmax(f_θ(x))_c · log(softmax(f_θ(x))_c), which penalizes over-confident softmax outputs and directly targets the miscalibration error identified in Garg et al. (2020). The calibrated predictor is then plugged into the MLLS objective to estimate importance ratios via convex optimization over r ∈ R_+^m. In the multi-node IW-ERM framework, each node independently runs VRLS on its local data (Phase 1), the resulting per-node ratios are aggregated into a global ratio vector (Phase 2), and a shared global model is trained with the importance-weighted loss (Phase 3). Theoretical analysis establishes high-probability estimation error bounds (Theorem 5.1) and shows that importance weighting scales convergence rates by at most a factor of r_max without degrading communication or privacy guarantees (Theorem 5.2, 5.3–5.5).

## Results

On Fashion MNIST with 5 nodes, IW-ERM with VRLS achieves 75.20% average accuracy, outperforming FedAvg (54.72%), FedBN (53.59%), FedProx (56.06%), and SCAFFOLD (57.74%) by over 17–22 percentage points, while approaching the upper bound of 82.73% (true ratios). On CIFAR-10 with 5 nodes, IW-ERM reaches 56.40% accuracy versus FedAvg's 45.15% and FedBN's 42.63%, closely matching the upper bound of 57.90%. At scale (100 and 200 nodes on CIFAR-10), IW-ERM achieves 53.54% and 62.16% respectively, substantially exceeding FedAvg (39.15%, 59.42%) and FedBN (15.37%, 17.53%). In single-node ratio estimation, VRLS consistently achieves lower MSE than MLLS_L2 and MLLS_EM across MNIST and CIFAR-10 under varying shift intensities and sample sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IW-ERM with VRLS decisively outperforms all federated learning baselines. On Fashion MNIST (5 nodes), it achieves 75.20% accuracy versus FedAvg (54.72%), FedBN (53.59%), FedProx (56.06%), and SCAFFOLD (57.74%)—gains of 17–22 percentage points while using only one-third the iterations (5,000 vs. 15,000). On CIFAR-10 (5 nodes), IW-ERM reaches 56.40% compared to FedAvg's 45.15% and FedBN's 42.63%, approaching the true-ratio upper bound of 57.90%. At 100 nodes, IW-ERM (53.54%) surpasses FedAvg (39.15%) by 14.4 points and FedBN (15.37%) by 38.2 points; at 200 nodes it leads FedAvg (59.42%) by 2.7 points while FedBN collapses to 17.53%. In ratio estimation (Figures 1–2), VRLS achieves consistently lower MSE than both MLLS_L2 (Garg et al., 2020) and MLLS_EM (Saerens et al., 2002) across all tested shift parameters (α) and sample sizes on MNIST and CIFAR-10, with the gap widening under stronger shifts and smaller test sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLS framework (Garg et al., 2020) for label shift importance ratio estimation by replacing post-hoc calibration (e.g., BCT) with an in-training Shannon entropy regularizer that directly penalizes miscalibrated softmax outputs. It then embeds this improved ratio estimator into an Importance-Weighted ERM (IW-ERM) framework, building on the single-node IW-ERM of Shimodaira (2000) and the multi-node IW-ERM formulation of Fang et al. (2023), to handle both intra-node and inter-node label shifts in distributed learning. The key departure from prior distributed learning work (e.g., Ramezani-Kebrya et al., 2023b for covariate shift) is the explicit treatment of label shifts with privacy-preserving ratio sharing, and the provision of Rademacher-complexity-based estimation error bounds that account for the entropy-regularized predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: standard federated/distributed learning methods (FedAvg, FedBN, SCAFFOLD) assume i.i.d. data across nodes and ignore test-time distribution shifts, which are pervasive in real deployments. The entropy-regularized VRLS estimator is a simple, drop-in modification to the MLLS pipeline that consistently reduces ratio estimation MSE, and the multi-node IW-ERM framework achieves near-upper-bound performance while sharing only low-dimensional ratio vectors (preserving data privacy). Key limitations include the strict assumption that p(x|y) is identical across all nodes and between train/test, which the authors acknowledge may not hold in practice (their 'relaxed' experiments show degradation). Open questions include relaxing the class-conditional invariance assumption, scaling to hundreds of nodes with heterogeneous architectures, and validating on real-world non-synthetic label shift scenarios.
