## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models struggle to simultaneously maintain high image fidelity and strong prompt-image alignment, especially for challenging prompts involving counting, spatial relations, and multi-object composition. Strong classifier-free guidance improves alignment but causes mode collapse and artifacts, while existing remedies (manifold constraints, varying guidance scales) compromise diversity or produce unnatural outputs. No self-reflection mechanism had been formulated for diffusion sampling that could iteratively self-improve generation quality without retraining.

## Input / Output

Z-Sampling is a training-free, plug-and-play sampling procedure that takes a text prompt and a standard Gaussian noise latent as input and outputs a generated image, operating entirely within the existing inference pipeline of pretrained diffusion models (SDXL, SD-2.1, Hunyuan-DiT, DreamShaper-xl-v2-turbo). It requires two hyperparameters beyond standard sampling: a denoising guidance scale γ1 (typically 5.5) and an inversion guidance scale γ2 (typically 0), plus a zigzag step count λ controlling how many early timesteps use the self-reflection operation. The method requires a deterministic sampler (e.g., DDIM) for accurate inversion and adds 2λ forward passes per image, though experiments show it can match or exceed standard sampling quality with 36% less total compute by halving the denoising steps.

## Method

The core insight is that the guidance gap δγ = γ1 − γ2 between a strongly-guided denoising step and a weakly-guided inversion step injects prompt-relevant semantic information into the latent, as a latent that can generate prompt-aligned images under weak guidance inherently carries that semantic content. Z-Sampling exploits this by performing a zigzag self-reflection operation at each timestep: denoise x_t → x_{t−1} with guidance γ1, then invert x_{t−1} → x̃_t with guidance γ2, and use x̃_t as the next denoising input, accumulating semantic information step-by-step along the sampling path. Theoretically, the paper proves (via Jensen's inequality) that the step-by-step cumulative semantic gain ∑(τ₁(t))² strictly exceeds the end-to-end gain (∑τ₁(t))², explaining why local zigzag injection outperforms a single end-to-end inversion. The method is architecture-agnostic, requires only ~10 lines of code to implement, and composes additively with orthogonal techniques such as Diffusion-DPO and AYS-Sampling.

## Results

On Pick-a-Pic and DrawBench, Z-Sampling consistently outperforms both standard sampling and Resampling across all four tested diffusion models (SD-2.1, SDXL, DreamShaper-xl-v2-turbo, Hunyuan-DiT). For SDXL, Z-Sampling achieves HPSv2 of 31.28 (vs. 29.89 standard, 30.54 Resampling) and IR of 79.22 (vs. 58.65, 78.60) on Pick-a-Pic. On the challenging GenEval benchmark, Z-Sampling improves overall alignment from 52.52% (standard) to 57.04%, with particularly large gains in counting (33.75%→46.25%) and color attribution (18.00%→24.00%). The HPSv2 winning rate of Z-Sampling over standard sampling reaches up to 94% on DreamShaper-xl-v2-turbo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Z-Sampling outperforms Resampling (the primary baseline in Table 1) on nearly all metrics across all four models, with the largest gains on DreamShaper-xl-v2-turbo (HPSv2 +0.96, IR +8.44 on Pick-a-Pic). Against Semantic-aware CFG (Shen et al., 2024), Z-Sampling achieves substantially higher IR (36.89 vs. 2.03 on Pick-a-Pic) and HPSv2 (27.05 vs. 26.02) with SD-2.1. Compared to AYS-Sampling (Sabour et al., 2024), Z-Sampling improves HPSv2 from 32.78 to 33.53 and IR from 91.88 to 103.95 on DreamShaper-xl-v2-turbo, and the two methods are composable (AYS+Z-Sampling reaches 33.57 HPSv2). Against Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024), Z-Sampling alone slightly trails DPO on Pick-a-Pic HPSv2 (31.28 vs. 31.41) but wins on DrawBench (30.50 vs. 29.80); combining DPO+Z-Sampling yields the best overall (31.60 HPSv2, 94.48 IR). Z-Sampling also beats SEG (Hong, 2024) by +0.75 HPSv2 and +16.65 IR on SDXL Pick-a-Pic, and CFG++ (Chung et al., 2024) by +0.96 HPSv2 and +11.25 IR. On video generation (ChronoMagic-Bench-150), Z-Sampling with AnimateDiff outperforms FreeInit (Wu et al., 2025) on UMT-FVD (243.26 vs. 268.31) and UMT-SCORE (2.97 vs. 2.82).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDIM denoising/inversion framework (Song et al., 2020) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), repurposing the inversion step not for image editing (as in Mokady et al., 2023) but as a semantic injection mechanism. It departs from prior resampling approaches (lug, 2022) that add random noise for manifold correction, and from observations that latents carry implicit semantic information (Mao et al., 2023b; Xu et al., 2024b), by formally showing that a guidance gap between denoising and inversion steps can be exploited to accumulate prompt-relevant semantics step-by-step. The conceptual framing borrows from LLM self-reflection (Shinn et al., 2024), but the mechanism is entirely diffusion-specific: alternating denoise-invert cycles with asymmetric CFG scales, with a Jensen's inequality argument proving step-by-step accumulation dominates end-to-end injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Z-Sampling is a training-free, plug-and-play sampling method that consistently improves prompt alignment across U-Net, DiT, and 4-step distilled architectures, and composes with orthogonal methods like Diffusion-DPO and AYS-Sampling. Its main limitation is reliance on deterministic samplers (DDIM), as stochastic samplers amplify the approximation error term τ₂ and degrade results; extending to SDE-based frameworks is left open. The method also remains untested beyond text-to-image (video, 3D, molecular), and the extra zigzag computation, while manageable, precludes it from being a drop-in replacement in latency-critical settings. The clean theoretical decomposition (semantic gain vs. approximation error) provides a useful lens for future sampling design.
