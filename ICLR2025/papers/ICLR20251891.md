## Problem

Vanilla response-based knowledge distillation for image super-resolution yields only marginal or even negative improvements because the teacher's output is merely a noisy approximation of the ground-truth HR image, so the 'dark knowledge' mechanism that works in classification does not transfer. The teacher's guidance is effectively shaded by the GT labels, and existing feature-based KD methods are impractical when teacher architectures are inaccessible due to commercial or privacy constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (H×W×3) and produces a high-resolution RGB image (s_c·H × s_c·W × 3) at scale factors ×2, ×3, or ×4. During training, auxiliary distillation samples are generated on-the-fly: zoom-in samples are random patches cropped from the HR image at LR resolution, and zoom-out samples are further down-sampled versions of the LR image. The approach is architecture-agnostic, operating on the final pixel outputs of both teacher and student regardless of whether they are CNNs (EDSR, RCAN) or Transformers (SwinIR).

## Method

AugKD introduces two modules that reframe KD for SR as a data-centric rather than knowledge-type-centric problem. First, auxiliary distillation sample generation creates extra LR inputs via zoom-in (randomly cropping HR patches at LR size) and zoom-out (further down-sampling the LR image), on which the teacher provides supervision unshaded by GT labels, allowing genuine knowledge transfer. Second, label consistency regularization applies invertible augmentations (horizontal/vertical flips, 90°/180°/270° rotations, and color inversion) to the student's input while keeping the teacher's input unperturbed, then inverse-augments the student's output before computing the L1 distillation loss, forcing the student to learn transformation-invariant representations. The total training objective combines the standard reconstruction loss, the conventional KD loss on original pairs, and the auxiliary distillation loss on augmented samples, weighted by λ_kd and λ_augkd.

## Results

On EDSR ×4 SR, AugKD achieves 26.45 dB PSNR / 0.7963 SSIM on Urban100, outperforming the best prior method CSD (26.34/0.7948) and vanilla KD (26.21/0.7897). Across all three scales (×2, ×3, ×4) and four benchmark datasets, AugKD consistently yields the highest PSNR and SSIM among all compared distillation methods. The average PSNR gain over response-based KD on Urban100 is 0.43 dB for EDSR, 0.31 dB for RCAN, and 0.31 dB for SwinIR. On real-world SR (NIQE, lower is better), AugKD achieves 5.398 on RealSR and 3.494 on OST300, improving over KD (5.425 and 3.652) with a 1.24M-parameter SwinIR student.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AugKD wins against all seven baselines across every scale and dataset. Compared to vanilla response-based KD (Hinton et al.), AugKD gains 0.43 dB average PSNR on Urban100 for EDSR (e.g., 26.45 vs. 26.21 at ×4), 0.31 dB for RCAN, and 0.31 dB for SwinIR. Feature-based methods FitNet and AT actually degrade performance below scratch training in most settings (e.g., FitNet drops to 25.40 dB on Urban100 ×4 vs. 26.21 for scratch), confirming that intermediate feature alignment is poorly suited to pixel-level SR. RKD and FAKD offer only marginal or no improvement over scratch, while CSD (contrastive self-distillation) is the strongest prior SR-specific method but still trails AugKD by 0.11 dB on Urban100 ×4. CrossKD (Fang et al.) achieves 26.52 dB on Urban100 ×4 for RCAN, slightly below AugKD's 26.62 dB, and is inapplicable to SwinIR due to architectural constraints. AugKD's key advantage is universality: as a response-based method it works across CNN and Transformer backbones and even in heterogeneous teacher-student settings where feature-based methods cannot be applied.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on response-based knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) and repurposes consistency regularization from semi-supervised learning (Oliver et al., 2018; Englesson & Azizpour, 2021) into the SR distillation setting. It departs from the dominant feature-based KD line for SR (FAKD, CSD, CrossKD) by arguing that the teacher's 'dark knowledge' is inherently limited in pixel-reconstruction tasks, and instead shifts the paradigm to data-centric training: generating auxiliary inputs via zoom-in/zoom-out so the teacher's supervision is no longer 'shaded' by ground-truth labels. The approach is conceptually closer to data-free KD (Zhang et al., 2021b) in that the teacher provides the sole supervision signal on auxiliary samples, but avoids discarding available GT labels entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AugKD offers a practical, architecture-agnostic KD recipe for SR that works across CNN and Transformer backbones and even in heterogeneous teacher-student settings where feature-based methods are inapplicable. The clean diagnosis of why vanilla KD underperforms in SR (teacher outputs are noisy approximations of GT, not rich 'dark knowledge') is a useful conceptual contribution for the broader image-restoration community. However, the absolute gains remain modest (0.2–0.5 dB over the best baselines), and the method still requires access to a pre-trained teacher at inference-training time. Open questions include whether the zoom-based auxiliary sample strategy generalizes to other pixel-level tasks (denoising, inpainting, deblurring) and whether more sophisticated augmentation schedules could yield larger improvements.
