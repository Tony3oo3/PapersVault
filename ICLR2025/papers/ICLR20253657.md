## Problem

Current math reasoning benchmarks like GSM8K evaluate models solely on final-answer accuracy, ignoring the quality of intermediate reasoning steps, and have become saturated (SOTA models exceed 80%) and vulnerable to data contamination. This paper asks whether shifting the evaluation role from 'student answering questions' to 'teacher scoring solutions'—a meta-reasoning task requiring deliberate, system-2-style examination of assumptions, calculations, and logic—can produce a more differentiating and contamination-robust benchmark.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253657/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a grade-school math question (in one of three formats: original GSM8K, Program-of-Thought code, or reversed reasoning where an input is hidden) with a step-by-step solution generated by MetaMath-7B at temperature 1, deliberately targeting ~50% result accuracy to ensure a balance of correct and subtly flawed solutions. The evaluated model must produce a structured three-part output: a binary correctness judgment, the index of the first erroneous step (solutions are segmented by newlines and indexed), and a free-text explanation of why that step is wrong. The MR-Score aggregates these via a weighted combination of MCC (correctness), step-identification accuracy, and reason-quality accuracy, with weights chosen by grid search to maximize inter-model differentiation.

## Method

The core contribution is a benchmark-construction pipeline that transforms GSM8K into a meta-reasoning evaluation: MetaMath-7B generates solutions at temperature 1 to produce nuanced errors, then a panel of expert annotators (recruited via trial labeling and supervised by four QC leads) labels solution correctness, first error step, and detailed error reasons, with double-annotation, 50% secondary QC, and 10% author inspection. The MR-Score metric uses MCC rather than F1 or balanced accuracy for the correctness sub-task specifically to penalize degenerate strategies (e.g., always predicting 'correct') that would otherwise score ~50%. The paper's key empirical finding is that specialized math models (WizardMath, MetaMath, MAmmoTH) trained on GSM8K-style data systematically fail at this scoring paradigm—reverting to question-answering behavior under few-shot prompts—while OpenAI's o1 models, which exhibit system-2 deliberative reasoning, outperform all other SOTA models by over 20 absolute MR-Score points, supporting the hypothesis that current training paradigms produce superficial rather than deep reasoning.

## Results

On MR-GSM8K, the o1-preview model achieves the highest MR-Score of 70.7 (zero-shot) and 70.3 (few-shot), with an MCC of 86.6, ACC_step of 68.3, and ACC_reason of 65.7. GPT-4-Turbo follows at 50.5 (zero-shot) and 53.0 (few-shot) MR-Score. Among open-source models, Llama3-70B leads with 38.3 (zero-shot) MR-Score, while specialized math models (WizardMath-7B, MetaMath-70B, MAmmoTH-70B) score near zero (0.0–5.0), revealing a stark gap between general and math-specialized models on meta-reasoning tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The o1-series models (o1-preview: 70.7, o1-mini: 69.2 MR-Score) outperform all other models by more than 20 absolute points over GPT-4-Turbo (50.5/53.0), supporting the paper's hypothesis that system-2 deliberative thinking is critical for meta-reasoning. Among open-source models, Llama3-70B (38.3) is the strongest but still trails o1-preview by over 30 points; notably, it outperforms the larger Deepseek-v2-236B (29.8) in both zero-shot and few-shot settings. Specialized math models collapse dramatically: WizardMath-7B (0.2), MetaMath-70B (0.0), and MAmmoTH-70B (5.0) score near zero despite being fine-tuned on GSM8K data, while the small Phi3-3.8B (22.9) surpasses all of them. Few-shot demonstrations (k=3) generally degrade performance for specialized models (e.g., Deepseek-Math-7B-RL drops from 11.6 to 0.1) and show inconsistent effects on general chat models, with GPT-4-Turbo slightly improving (50.5→53.0) while Claude3-Haiku drops sharply (15.3→4.9). The paper attributes the specialized models' failure to overfitting on GSM8K's input/output distribution, which prevents them from adapting to the solution-scoring paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the GSM8K dataset (Cobbe et al., 2021) and the step-level verification approach of Lightman et al. (2023) into a fundamentally different evaluation paradigm: rather than asking models to solve problems, it asks them to score, diagnose, and explain errors in given solutions. Conceptually, it draws on the chain-of-thought and program-of-thought literatures for solution generation, on reversed-reasoning work (Yu et al., 2023) for question construction, and on Kahneman's system-2 framework to characterize the cognitive demands of 'reasoning about reasoning.' Unlike prior LLM-as-judge work (Zheng et al., 2023) that evaluates open-ended responses, this paper introduces a structured, progressive three-part metric (MR-Score) that decomposes meta-reasoning into correctness judgment, first-error-step localization, and error-reason explanation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple transformation of a saturated benchmark (GSM8K, where SOTA models exceed 80%) into a meta-reasoning variant (MR-GSM8K) restores strong model differentiation and robustness against data contamination, offering a general recipe for revitalizing other benchmarks. The finding that specialized math models fine-tuned on GSM8K data score near zero on MR-GSM8K, and that few-shot demonstrations often degrade performance, challenges the assumption that domain-specific fine-tuning yields robust reasoning. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends beyond math to holistic or creative domains, whether training on meta-reasoning data can close the gap (the in-domain fine-tuning experiment shows only modest gains), and whether the o1 models' dominance reflects a genuinely different cognitive architecture or simply more compute for iterative self-reflection.
