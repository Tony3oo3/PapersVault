## Problem

Low-rank decomposition methods like LoRA constrain the hypothesis space of weight updates, limiting parameter efficiency. While FourierFT introduced frequency-domain PEFT, it randomly selects frequency components and its theoretical advantages over low-rank methods remain unanalyzed. This paper provides a rigorous comparison showing that random frequency selection is actually inferior to low-rank methods, and that carefully selected frequency components can surpass them in expressivity.

## Input / Output

The method operates on individual pre-trained weight matrices (query and value projections in attention layers) of Transformer-based models, reparameterizing the weight update ΔW as a sparse combination of DCT basis vectors. For language tasks, inputs are text sequences (up to 512 tokens for GLUE, variable-length for instruction tuning) producing classification scores, generated text, or LLM-judged quality ratings. For vision tasks, inputs are RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for ViT) producing class labels across 8 fine-grained and coarse-grained datasets. The trainable parameters consist of B scalar coefficients and B discrete 2D location indices on the DCT spectrum, with B typically ranging from 1,000 to 150,000 depending on task and model size.

## Method

LoCA reparameterizes the weight update ΔW as α·Cᵀ·S(a,l,1)·D, where C and D are pre-computed DCT matrices, a are learnable scalar coefficients, and l are learnable discrete locations on the DCT spectrum. The key insight is that individually selecting real/imaginary Fourier coefficients is equivalent to selecting locations on the real-valued DCT spectrum (Theorem 2), eliminating the need for complex-valued optimization. To handle the discrete nature of location variables, LoCA uses central finite-difference approximation to estimate gradients: the location gradient is computed by applying a DCT to the backpropagated loss gradient and extracting two adjacent entries, introducing negligible computational overhead. Training follows an alternating optimization schedule: coefficients are updated for B_a steps, then locations for B_l steps, repeated for B_s iterations before switching to coefficient-only optimization. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) proves that this location-aware frequency-domain approach achieves strictly lower expected reconstruction error than low-rank decomposition under the same parameter budget, given the asymptotic Gaussian structure of fine-tuning weight updates.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-base, LoCA achieves an average score of 86.0 using only 0.024M trainable parameters, matching LoRA (86.1, 0.3M params) and AdaLoRA (86.3, 0.3M params) with ~12× fewer parameters, while outperforming FourierFT (85.4) at the same parameter budget. On instruction tuning with LLaMA-2-13B, LoCA (12M params) scores 5.52 on MT-Bench and 8.11 on Vicuna, surpassing LoRA (52.4M params) which scores 5.48 and 8.03. On image classification with ViT-Large (480K params), LoCA achieves 88.42% average accuracy, approaching LoRA (88.81%, 1.57M params) with ~3× fewer parameters and clearly outperforming FourierFT (87.39%, same budget).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoCA consistently outperforms FourierFT at the same parameter budget across all four experimental domains: on GLUE (86.0 vs 85.4 for RoBERTa-base), E2E NLG (72.1 ROUGE-L vs 71.8 for GPT-2-Large), instruction tuning (5.52 vs 5.37 MT-Bench for LLaMA-2-13B), and image classification (88.42% vs 87.39% avg for ViT-Large at 480K params). Against LoRA, LoCA matches or approaches performance with roughly 12× fewer parameters on GLUE (0.024M vs 0.3M) and 4× fewer on instruction tuning (12M vs 52.4M for LLaMA-2-13B), where it actually surpasses LoRA on both MT-Bench (5.52 vs 5.48) and Vicuna (8.11 vs 8.03). On image classification, LoCA at 239K params (86.66% avg) approaches LoRA at 581K params (86.76%) with ViT-base. LoCA also outperforms VeRA on GLUE (86.0 vs 85.7 for RoBERTa-base) despite using fewer parameters (0.024M vs 0.043M). The main gap remains on challenging tasks like QQP, where LoRA's larger parameter budget (90.8 vs 88.7 for RoBERTa-base) still provides an advantage, though the paper shows this gap closes when LoCA's budget is increased.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends FourierFT (Gao et al., 2024), which first proposed reparameterizing weight updates via inverse Discrete Fourier Transform with randomly selected frequency components. LoCA departs from FourierFT in three key ways: (1) it provides the first rigorous theoretical analysis showing that random frequency selection actually has *lower* expressivity than low-rank decomposition, (2) it replaces iDFT with iDCT to operate entirely in the real domain, and (3) it introduces location-aware selection of frequency components via finite-difference gradient estimation. The paper also positions itself against the broader LoRA family (LoRA, AdaLoRA, DoRA, VeRA) by arguing that the low-rank decomposition form inherently constrains the hypothesis space, and uses random matrix theory (Marchenko-Pastur law, asymptotic normality of M-estimators) to formalize this comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framework establishing when frequency-domain PEFT can or cannot outperform low-rank methods fills a significant gap in the PEFT literature, providing practitioners with principled guidance on method selection. The finding that random frequency selection is inferior to low-rank decomposition challenges the implicit assumption that frequency-domain methods are inherently more expressive. Open questions include whether the asymptotic Gaussian assumption on weight updates holds for all architectures and training regimes, whether the alternating optimization strategy scales reliably to very large models (100B+), and whether the location-selection mechanism can be further accelerated for real-time adaptation scenarios.
