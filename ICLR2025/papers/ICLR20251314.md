## Problem

Training Neural SDEs as generative models for continuous stochastic processes has relied on either adversarial training (SDE-GANs), which is unstable and prone to mode collapse, or signature kernel methods, which require solving Goursat PDEs with O(D²) computational complexity per epoch. No existing method provides a strictly proper, non-adversarial training objective for Neural SDEs that avoids this quadratic overhead.

## Input / Output

The method consumes time series data paths (e.g., 64- or 256-timestamp sequences of financial instruments) where each path only needs to be observed at two randomly sampled timestamps, while generated paths from the Neural SDE can be evaluated at arbitrary timestamps. The model is a multi-dimensional Neural SDE with fully connected networks parameterizing drift and diffusion, initialized from a standard Gaussian, and produces full stochastic paths over [0,T] as output. All features in a dataset are modeled jointly in a single multi-dimensional SDE.

## Method

The paper proves that any strictly proper scoring rule on finite-dimensional space (e.g., an RBF-kernel-based score on R^{2d}) can be lifted to a strictly proper scoring rule for continuous Markov processes by averaging over the two-time joint distributions (X_{t1}, X_{t2}) with (t1,t2) drawn uniformly from [0,T]². This yields the Finite Dimensional Matching (FDM) algorithm: at each training step, a batch of generated paths and a batch of data paths are sampled, two random timestamps are drawn per sample, and the empirical MMD-style score is maximized via standard gradient-based ERM. Because the Markov property ensures the two-time joint distributions fully characterize the process law, this finite-dimensional objective is sufficient for full path-distribution matching. The approach reduces per-epoch complexity from O(D²) (signature kernels) to O(D), and avoids adversarial training entirely.

## Results

FDM achieves average KS test scores of approximately 0.11–0.13 with null-hypothesis rejection rates of 5–16% across all six datasets (metal prices, stock indices, exchange rates, energy prices, bonds, and Rough Bergomi) at 64 timestamps, consistently outperforming SigKer (0.12–0.35 KS, 8–94% rejection), TruncSig (0.17–0.46 KS, 47–100% rejection), and SDE-GAN (0.21–0.98 KS, 70–100% rejection). On the 16-dimensional Rough Bergomi model, FDM matches SigKer (0.113 vs. 0.112 KS at t=6) while dramatically outperforming TruncSig (0.450) and SDE-GAN (0.308). At 1024 timestamps, SigKer crashes due to a thread-limit error in its PDE solver, while FDM remains functional with KS scores of 0.117–0.260.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDM outperforms all three baselines on the vast majority of dataset-timestamp combinations. Against SigKer (Issa et al., 2023), FDM achieves lower KS scores and rejection rates on all real-world financial datasets (e.g., 0.112 vs. 0.130 KS on Silver at t=32; 0.117 vs. 0.287 on USA500 at t=32), though SigKer is competitive on the 16-dim Rough Bergomi model (0.112 vs. 0.113 at t=6) and slightly better at early timestamps on some exchange-rate dimensions. Against TruncSig (Bonnier & Oberhauser, 2024), FDM wins decisively everywhere, with TruncSig showing near-100% rejection rates on most datasets and running out of GPU memory on the 32-dim Rough Bergomi model. Against SDE-GAN (Kidger et al., 2021), FDM dominates across all settings, with SDE-GAN frequently exceeding 0.5 KS and 90%+ rejection rates. A critical practical advantage emerges at longer sequence lengths: at 1024 timestamps, SigKer crashes entirely due to a thread-limit error in its Goursat PDE solver, while FDM continues to produce usable results, underscoring the O(D) vs. O(D²) complexity advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two research threads: the theory of strictly proper scoring rules for distribution comparison (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007) and the training of Neural SDEs as generative models (Kidger et al., 2021; Issa et al., 2023). Prior Neural SDE training methods rely on either adversarial objectives (SDE-GAN) or signature kernel scores that require solving Goursat PDEs with O(D²) complexity. The key conceptual departure is showing that the Markov property of SDEs reduces the path-space comparison to matching two-time joint distributions, allowing any finite-dimensional strictly proper scoring rule to be lifted to a process-level scoring rule without PDE solvers or adversarial training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FDM provides a theoretically grounded (strictly proper) training objective that is both simpler and faster than existing methods, reducing per-epoch complexity from O(D²) to O(D) and eliminating the need for PDE solvers or adversarial dynamics. This makes long-horizon time series generation (e.g., 1024+ timestamps) tractable where signature kernel methods fail outright. The main limitation is the reliance on the Markov property and path continuity, though the authors sketch an extension to Càdlag processes. An open direction is relaxing the Markov assumption to accommodate hidden state or non-Markovian dynamics, which would broaden applicability beyond SDE-based models.
