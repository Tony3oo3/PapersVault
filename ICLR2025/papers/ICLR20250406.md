## Problem

No prior work has systematically studied the task of explaining differences between two audio recordings in natural language, despite its importance in audio forensics, quality assessment, and audio generation. Current approaches require human experts with specialized knowledge of phonetics and spectrogram analysis, and no annotated datasets or benchmarks exist for this comparative reasoning task.

## Input / Output

The model accepts two audio files (10-second clips for AudioCaps-derived data, 15–30 seconds for Clotho-derived data, sampled at 32 kHz and converted to 64-bin log-Mel spectrograms) plus a user text prompt specifying the desired explanation tier. It produces a free-form natural language explanation of the differences, structured into three tiers: Tier 1 (concise, ~27 words, audio events/objects), Tier 2 (brief, ~52 words, events + scenes + signal properties), and Tier 3 (detailed, ~155 words, semantics + listener emotions + acoustic analysis). The prompt database contains ~100 paraphrased instructions per tier to support flexible user phrasing.

## Method

ADIFF employs prefix-tuning to prompt a frozen GPT-2 (128M) decoder, with the key architectural addition being a cross-projection layer (4-layer transformer) that processes the concatenated prefix of [audio-1 latent, separator token, audio-2 latent, text prompt] to explicitly learn inter-audio differences. The audio encoder is HTSAT (frozen, pretrained on AudioSet), and audio embeddings are projected into the LM's latent space via 8-layer transformer mappers. Training follows a three-stage protocol: (1) unimodal pretraining (skipped, using pretrained HTSAT and GPT-2), (2) multimodal grounding where only projection and cross-projection layers are trained with both encoder and LM frozen, and (3) full finetuning at a low learning rate (~1e-6) for 10 epochs to steer the LM toward acoustic terminology without catastrophic forgetting. Position captioning (training the model to caption 'the first audio' or 'the second audio' alongside difference explanations) is incorporated to improve grounding and reduce confusion between perceptually similar sounds.

## Results

On the ACD test set, ADIFF achieves SPIDEr scores of 0.303 (Tier 1), 0.345 (Tier 2), and 0.183 (Tier 3), outperforming the naive prefix-tuning baseline (0.220/0.225/0.123) and QwenAC-LoRA (0.235/0.212/0.173) across all three explanation tiers. On CLD, ADIFF reaches 0.652/0.692/0.417 SPIDEr, with the largest gain over baseline on Tier 3 (0.417 vs. 0.166, a 2.5× improvement). In human evaluation, ADIFF scores 3.47/3.53/3.57 (Correctness/Granularity/Readability out of 5) on average across Studio, FSD50K, and GTZAN scenarios, surpassing the baseline (3.26/3.35/3.39) and zero-shot QwenAC (2.81/2.56/3.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADIFF (128M GPT-2) outperforms the naive prefix-tuning baseline across all three tiers on both ACD and CLD, with the most dramatic gain on CLD Tier 3 (0.417 vs. 0.166 SPIDEr, +151%). Against QwenAC-LoRA (7B), ADIFF wins on 5 of 6 tier-dataset combinations, losing only on CLD Tier 2 (0.692 vs. 0.756). Against QwenAC-Full (7B), ADIFF wins on 5 of 6 combinations, again losing only on CLD Tier 2 (0.692 vs. 0.958), which the authors attribute to Tier 2's linguistic simplicity favoring a larger LLM. In human evaluation, ADIFF achieves the highest average scores across all three dimensions (Correctness 3.47, Granularity 3.53, Readability 3.57) compared to QwenAC-Full (3.28/3.45/3.49) and the baseline (3.26/3.35/3.39), with the largest margin in Granularity, confirming ADIFF's strength in producing detailed, fine-grained difference descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prefix-tuning architecture from Pengi (Deshmukh et al., 2023) to a comparative audio task, adding a cross-projection layer with a latent separator token to enable the model to reason about differences between two audio inputs. It draws on audio captioning datasets (AudioCaps, Clotho) as data sources and positions itself against general-purpose Audio-Language Models (SALMONN, GAMA, LTU, Qwen-Audio) that handle single-audio tasks but lack comparative reasoning. The task is conceptually analogous to Image Difference Captioning in vision, but the authors explicitly distinguish their formulation from prior audio difference work (Komatsu et al. 2024, Takeuchi et al. 2023), which focuses on single-audio captioning or simple add/remove/change operations rather than multi-tier natural language explanations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive benchmark for audio difference explanation, providing two datasets with three tiers of explanation granularity and establishing evaluation protocols (objective metrics plus human evaluation across correctness, granularity, and readability). The finding that a 128M-parameter GPT-2 outperforms a 1.5B GPT-2-XL under matched compute budgets for prefix-tuning audio-language models is a practical and somewhat counterintuitive result for the field. Key limitations include reliance on LLM-generated training data (only test set is human-verified), restriction to 10-second audio clips, and the modest absolute performance (average ~3.5/5 in human evaluation), suggesting the task remains far from solved. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer recordings, whether the cross-projection mechanism generalizes beyond GPT-2, and how to reduce hallucination without sacrificing explanation richness.
