## Problem

Existing neural PDE solvers either require large training datasets to learn mappings between function spaces (neural operators, standard GNNs) or cannot handle irregular computational domains (PeRCNN). Accurate prediction of spatiotemporal dynamics on coarse unstructured meshes with small training sets, complex hybrid boundary conditions, and generalization across PDE parameters (e.g., Reynolds number) remains an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252670/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an initial condition (state variable values at sparse nodes on a coarse unstructured mesh, e.g., 503–4225 nodes constructed via Delaunay triangulation) along with specified boundary conditions (Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, or Periodic) and produces the full spatiotemporal trajectory by rolling out a second-order Runge-Kutta integrator step-by-step. The mesh is deliberately coarse and non-uniform, and the model must handle hybrid BCs on irregular domains (e.g., circular or dolphin-shaped cutouts) rather than only regular grids with uniform BCs.

## Method

PhyMPGN embeds a message-passing GNN (Encode-Process-Decode framework) inside a numerical ODE integrator (RK2) to learn the nonlinear PDE operator F, decomposing it as F ≈ GNN_block + Laplace_block. The Laplace block encodes the discrete Laplace-Beltrami operator (cotangent weights, Voronoi mass) for the diffusion term and adds a lightweight correction network (MLP encoder/decoder + MPNN processor) to fix accuracy loss on coarse meshes. A novel BC padding strategy is applied in both physical space (ghost nodes for Neumann/Robin, node flipping for periodic) and latent space (after each MPNN layer), ensuring boundary constraints are satisfied at every rollout step. Training uses segmented backpropagation (loss only on first and last frames of each M-step segment) combined with input noise injection to stabilize long-horizon predictions.

## Results

On the three PDE systems (Burgers', FitzHugh-Nagumo, Gray-Scott), PhyMPGN achieves MSEs of 2.99e-4, 2.82e-3, and 4.05e-4 respectively, outperforming the best baseline (MGN) by 67.5%, 90.2%, and 90.9% in relative MSE reduction. On the cylinder flow (Navier-Stokes) problem, PhyMPGN generalizes across Reynolds numbers with MSEs of 2.14e-4 (Re=[200,280,360]), 3.56e-2 (Re=[440,480,520]), and 1.25e-1 (Re=[600,800,1000]), leading the best baseline by 97.0%, 83.4%, and 79.0% respectively. The model uses only 3–6 training trajectories per PDE system and operates on coarse unstructured meshes with ~500–4200 nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyMPGN outperforms all four baselines across every PDE system and every Reynolds number group tested. MGN (Pfaff et al., 2021) is the strongest baseline, achieving the second-best MSE in most settings (e.g., 9.19e-4 on Burgers', 4.44e-3 on GS), but PhyMPGN still leads by 67–91% in relative MSE. MP-PDE (Brandstetter et al., 2022) is competitive on Burgers' (5.88e-3) and small-Re cylinder flow (7.23e-3) but degrades sharply on GS (2.82e+0) and large-Re flow (5.96e-1). DeepONet (Lu et al., 2021) consistently underperforms, with MSEs 1–2 orders of magnitude worse than PhyMPGN. PA-DGN (Seo et al., 2020) is the weakest baseline, producing NaN results on several irregular-domain and high-Re scenarios. Notably, PhyMPGN achieves these gains while using only 3–6 training trajectories and a minimal Laplace block (~1% of total parameters), whereas the baselines require comparable or larger parameter budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhyMPGN sits at the intersection of three research threads: message-passing GNNs for PDE simulation (MGN, MP-PDE), physics-encoded learning on regular grids (PeRCNN), and numerical ODE integration for temporal marching. The key departure from PeRCNN is the replacement of finite-difference convolutional layers with a learnable discrete Laplace-Beltrami operator, enabling the physics encoding to operate on unstructured meshes where finite differences are inapplicable. Unlike MGN and MP-PDE, which treat the PDE residual as a purely data-driven black box, PhyMPGN explicitly decomposes the nonlinear operator F into a GNN-learned component and a Laplace-encoded diffusion component, constraining the solution space with a priori physics. The Encode-Process-Decode GNN architecture and the BC padding strategy are adapted from Battaglia et al. and PeRCNN respectively, but the combination with a correction network for the coarse-mesh Laplacian and the dual-space (physical + latent) padding for four BC types on irregular domains is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically important gap: most neural PDE solvers either require regular grids (PeRCNN, FNO) or large training datasets (neural operators), while many real-world problems involve irregular geometries and limited observations. The finding that a 5.6k-parameter Laplace block (≈1% of total model size) contributes the largest single ablation gain (4.4× MSE increase when removed) suggests that encoding the diffusion physics is far more critical than increasing GNN capacity, a principle that could guide future physics-informed architecture design. Open challenges acknowledged by the authors include extending to 3D, handling higher-order diffusion terms (∇⁴), and properly encoding Neumann/Robin BCs in latent space. The approach's reliance on knowing the diffusion term a priori limits its applicability to fully unknown PDEs, positioning it as a semi-supervised physics-informed method rather than a fully data-driven one.
