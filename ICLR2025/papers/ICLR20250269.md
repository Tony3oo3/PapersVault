## Problem

LLMs exhibit a 'lost-in-the-middle' positional bias, failing to retrieve information from the middle of long contexts, and their reasoning degrades as context length grows. Existing remedies—finetuning on long-context augmentation data containing factual content—either underperform or introduce hallucinations, as finetuning on previously unseen knowledge is known to encourage the model to fabricate answers. The paper asks whether a purely synthetic, fact-free training signal can improve long-context retrieval and reasoning without these side effects.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a long context (e.g., 20 documents of ~4K tokens, or up to 120 documents at 24K tokens) paired with a question, and must produce a short retrieved answer (MDQA) or a True/False classification with optional chain-of-thought reasoning (FLenQA). During training, the input is a prompt listing 49–85 numerical dictionaries (each with 3–4 integer key-value pairs or multi-subkey tuples) plus a query for a specific gold key, and the target output is a single integer value, optionally wrapped in a fixed answer template so that token-level loss concentrates on the retrieval skill rather than phrasing.

## Method

The paper designs two synthetic retrieval tasks—simple key-value lookup and a harder multi-subkey variant where keys are tuples sharing partial subkeys with the gold key in randomized order—and finetunes LLMs (Mistral 7B, GPT-3.5 Turbo) on only ~150–350 samples of these tasks. A key design choice is providing an answer template in the prompt, which suppresses loss on formatting tokens and forces the model to learn the retrieval mechanism rather than output style. Because the data is entirely numerical and contains no factual content, it avoids the hallucination-inducing effect documented by Gekhman et al. (2024) that plagues baselines like MultidocQA, IN2, and Needle-in-a-haystack. The learned retrieval skill transfers to natural-language benchmarks: the U-shaped positional bias on MDQA flattens, FLenQA reasoning accuracy improves even without explicit chain-of-thought, and general-capability scores (MMLU, HellaSwag, GSM8K, TriviaQA, NQ-Open) remain essentially unchanged.

## Results

On 20-document MDQA, finetuning GPT-3.5 Turbo on the synthetic key-value retrieval dataset yields a 10.5% accuracy improvement at position 10 (the worst position for the original model) and flattens the characteristic U-shaped performance curve. On FLenQA, finetuning with answer templates significantly improves chain-of-thought reasoning accuracy across context lengths from 250 to 3000 tokens. Crucially, general benchmark performance remains nearly unchanged (e.g., Mistral 7B MMLU: 53.42→53.44, TriviaQA: 47.63→47.74), while all factual-information baselines suffer notable degradation on knowledge-based benchmarks (TriviaQA drops of 2.19–6.33%, NQ-Open drops of 1.81–6.73%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four long-context augmentation baselines (MultidocQA, IN2, Needle-in-a-haystack, and MDQA) against the proposed synthetic key-value retrieval data, all finetuning Mistral 7B with roughly equal training tokens. On MDQA retrieval, the synthetic data approach outperforms direct MDQA finetuning (Finding 2), and is competitive with or slightly below MultidocQA and IN2 on FLenQA. However, the decisive advantage is on general benchmarks: the synthetic data preserves TriviaQA accuracy (47.74% vs. 47.63% baseline) while all four baselines degrade substantially—Needle-in-a-haystack causes the largest drop (TriviaQA −6.33%, NQ-Open −6.73%), followed by MDQA (−2.85%, −3.96%), MultidocQA (−2.43%, −2.91%), and IN2 (−2.19%, −1.81%). The authors attribute this to the baselines containing factual information that encourages hallucination (per Gekhman et al., 2024), whereas the purely numerical synthetic data avoids this failure mode entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of long-context LLM research (Liu et al.'s 'lost-in-the-middle' finding, Kamradt's needle-in-a-haystack test) and data-centric AI, proposing that the quality of finetuning data matters more than its domain specificity. It departs from prior long-context augmentation approaches (Yu 2024, An et al. 2024) that finetune on real factual QA data by instead using purely synthetic numerical key-value retrieval tasks, motivated by Gekhman et al.'s finding that finetuning on unseen factual knowledge encourages hallucinations. The answer-template mechanism draws on the broader observation that loss masking on formatting tokens lets the model focus on the substantive skill.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is clear: practitioners finetuning LLMs for long-context retrieval can use small, cheap, purely synthetic datasets (350 samples) to improve retrieval and reasoning without the hallucination risk that accompanies factual augmentation data. A key open question is whether this approach generalizes to relevant-distractor settings (where it currently fails, per Figure 10) and to even longer contexts or other model families. The finding that synthetic data outperforms finetuning on the target benchmark itself (MDQA) suggests that skill transfer from abstract tasks may be more effective than domain-specific memorization, but this needs validation at scale.
