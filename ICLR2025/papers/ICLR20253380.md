## Problem

Protein language models like ESM2 extract evolutionary representations from sequences but underperform on structure-dependent tasks (contact prediction, structure prediction, mutation stability) because they lack explicit structural context. While structure-based models capture 3D geometry, they require expensive structure inputs (MSAs, PDB coordinates) that are often unavailable. The open problem is to build a single-sequence model whose representations implicitly encode structural information without any structure input at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253380/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, ISM takes only a protein amino acid sequence (up to 512 residues) and produces per-residue 650M-parameter ESM2 backbone representations enriched with structural information. During training, the supervision signal comes from structure tokens generated from 3D structures: the Atomic Autoencoder operates on masked microenvironments (all atoms within 10 Å of a residue's α-carbon, excluding the masked residue's own atoms) and produces K=64 discrete tokens via K-means, while MutRank produces K=512 tokens. The final model requires no structure, MSA, or coordinate input—only the sequence string.

## Method

ISM introduces a two-stage pipeline: (1) an Atomic Autoencoder with a Graph Transformer encoder and Transformer decoder is trained on masked microenvironments to reconstruct all-atom coordinates using a Kabsch-aligned MSE loss, a distogram classification loss, and an amino-acid prediction loss; (2) the learned residue-level features are discretized via K-means into structure tokens, which supervise a 'structure-tuning' fine-tuning of pre-trained ESM2. The key training objective replaces standard MLM with a combined loss where the model predicts structure tokens at ALL positions (not just masked ones) alongside the original MLM loss, which the authors show better distills structural representations. The model is initialized from ESM2 (not trained from scratch), and structure tokens from both the Atomic Autoencoder and MutRank are used jointly. At inference, the structure-tuned ESM2 backbone produces structurally-enriched per-residue embeddings from sequence alone, outperforming ESM2 on long-range contact prediction (0.49 vs 0.35) and matching structure-input models like SaProt on CAMEO structure prediction.

## Results

On the CAMEO structure prediction benchmark, ISM achieves a GDT-TS of 0.67, outperforming ESM2 (0.64) by 5% and all other sequence-only baselines. On long-range contact prediction, ISM reaches a P@L of 0.49, a 40% improvement over ESM2 (0.35) and matching SaProt (0.48) which explicitly requires 3D structure as input. On S669 ΔΔG prediction, ISM achieves an AUC of 0.76 and Spearman ρ of 0.53, surpassing ESM2 (AUC 0.72) and matching structure-input models like MutateEverything(AF) (AUC 0.76) and Stability Oracle (AUC 0.75) while using only sequence input and running 20× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISM outperforms ESM2 (Lin et al., 2022) across all structure-based metrics: GDT-TS 0.67 vs 0.64, long-range contact 0.49 vs 0.35, and binding F1 0.35 vs 0.31. Against structure-aware sequence models ESM2-S (Zhang et al., 2024) and S-PLM (Wang et al., 2023), ISM achieves substantially higher GDT-TS (0.67 vs 0.61) and long-range contact (0.49 vs 0.36/0.36). ISM matches or slightly exceeds SaProt (Su et al., 2023) on long-range contact (0.49 vs 0.48) and binding F1 (0.35–0.37 vs 0.36), despite SaProt requiring explicit 3D structure input at inference. On S669 ΔΔG prediction, ISM (AUC 0.76, ρ 0.53) surpasses ESM2-fine-tuned (AUC 0.72), MutateEverything-ESM (AUC 0.72), and ESM3 (AUC 0.70), while matching MutateEverything-AF (AUC 0.76) and exceeding Stability Oracle (AUC 0.75) — all of which use structure or MSA inputs. On functional benchmarks (PEER/FLIP), ISM remains competitive with ESM2 and SaProt (e.g., GO-MF Fmax 0.67 vs 0.67/0.65), confirming that structure-tuning does not degrade evolutionary representations. ISM also outperforms the older ESM1b (Rives et al., 2019) on most functional tasks and the physics-based FoldX (Schymkowitz et al., 2005) on stability prediction (AUC 0.76 vs 0.62).

## Relation to Prior Work

ISM builds directly on ESM2 as its backbone and on the Graph Transformer architecture from MutComputeX-GT (Diaz et al., 2024) and the evolutionary loss from MutRank (Gong et al., 2024). It departs from prior structure-augmented pLMs (SaProt, ProstT5, MULAN, ProSST) that require 3D structure as an explicit input at inference time, and from coarse-grained structure-loss approaches (ESM2-S, S-PLM) that operate at the fold or global level. The key conceptual departure is the 'structure-tuning' objective: a sequence-only model is fine-tuned to predict discrete microenvironment tokens at every residue position, effectively distilling structure-model knowledge into the sequence model's weights so that no structure is needed at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ISM provides a practical, drop-in upgrade to ESM2 (one line of code) that substantially improves structural tasks—40% gain in long-range contact prediction and matching structure-input models on ΔΔG—while preserving ESM2's competitive performance on functional benchmarks. The finding that a small PDB dataset (35K) outperforms the much larger Uniclust30 (5.8M) for stability prediction is counterintuitive and suggests data curation may matter more than scale for structure-sensitive tasks, though the authors attribute this partly to S669 overlap. Open questions include whether the distillation approach generalizes to other modalities (e.g., ligand binding, post-translational modifications) and whether the reliance on AlphaFold-generated structures for token generation introduces systematic biases that limit performance on truly novel folds.
