## Problem

Joint finetuning of a pretrained encoder and a randomly initialized decoder—the de facto standard in semantic segmentation—causes the encoder to overfit to the source domain, corrupting its generalizable features and degrading performance on unseen target domains. Existing domain generalization methods for segmentation (augmentation-based, feature-invariance-based) have not addressed this fundamental vulnerability of the joint finetuning procedure itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a single source domain (e.g., GTAV or Cityscapes) at 768×768 crop resolution and produces pixel-wise semantic segmentation masks over 19 classes. The final inference model is a single encoder-decoder pair (EMA encoder ∘ EMA decoder) with no ensemble at test time, using standard DeepLab v3+ with ResNet-50/101 or MiT-B5 backbones pretrained on ImageNet.

## Method

DeFT operates in two stages. Stage 1 warms up the randomly initialized decoder while keeping the pretrained encoder frozen, so the decoder learns task-relevant knowledge without distorting the encoder's generalizable features. Stage 2 decouples finetuning into two parallel pathways with opposing AC/RC configurations: f₁ = d̃₁∘e₁ (RC decoder + AC encoder) and f₂ = d₂∘ẽ₂ (AC decoder + RC encoder). ACs are updated by standard gradient descent on the source-domain cross-entropy loss, while RCs are updated via an exponential moving average biased toward their initialization (β = 0.9999), performing a gradual temporal ensemble in parameter space that retains generalization capability. The final model is the composition of the two RCs (d̃₁∘ẽ₂), whose feature distributions are implicitly aligned through the cross-pathway EMA coupling, yielding fewer effective parameters per pathway and a shorter distance from initialization than joint finetuning.

## Results

Trained on GTAV with ResNet-50, DeFT achieves 47.91% average mIoU across Cityscapes (50.06%), BDD-100K (43.17%), and Mapillary (50.51%), outperforming the strongest baseline TLDR (45.09%) by 2.82 points. With ResNet-101, DeFT reaches 50.15% average mIoU, again leading all competitors. In the reverse setting (trained on Cityscapes, evaluated on BDD-100K, SYNTHIA, GTAV), DeFT achieves 43.57% average mIoU, surpassing the next-best SAN-SAW (42.85%) by 0.72 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeFT outperforms all 12 compared baselines in every evaluation setting. On the GTAV→{Cityscapes, BDD-100K, Mapillary} transfer with ResNet-50, DeFT (47.91% avg) beats the closest competitor TLDR (45.09%) by 2.82 points, BlindNet (44.71%) by 3.20 points, and PASTA (43.81%) by 4.10 points. The margin over older methods is even larger: WildNet (43.04%), SHADE (42.42%), SAN-SAW (39.65%), DIRL (40.60%), ISW (37.37%), DRPC (34.56%), and IBN-Net (34.63%) all trail by 3–13 points. With ResNet-101, DeFT extends its lead to 3.06 points over TLDR (50.15% vs. 47.09%) and 4.73 points over PASTA (50.15% vs. 45.42%). In the reverse Cityscapes→{BDD-100K, SYNTHIA, GTAV} setting, DeFT (43.57%) edges out SAN-SAW (42.85%) by 0.72 points and BlindNet (42.77%) by 0.80 points, with larger gaps over WildNet (41.97%), SHADE (42.39%), and DRPC (40.69%). On the MiT-B5 transformer backbone (Table 10), DeFT (55.49% avg) surpasses DAFormer+DGInStyle (54.25%) by 1.24 points and DAFormer alone (51.73%) by 3.76 points, confirming versatility across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeFT builds on the two-stage warm-up strategy from LP-FT (Kumar et al., 2022), which showed that warming up a randomly initialized decoder with a frozen pretrained encoder preserves generalizable features. It also draws on EMA-based weight averaging (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) as a mechanism for temporal ensembling in parameter space. The key departure is that, rather than jointly finetuning encoder and decoder after warm-up as LP-FT and all prior domain-generalization methods do, DeFT decouples their optimization into two parallel pathways with opposing adaptive/retentive configurations, using an initialization-biased EMA to update the retentive components. This positions DeFT as a training-strategy contribution orthogonal to the data-augmentation and feature-manipulation approaches (WildNet, SHADE, PASTA, IBN-Net, etc.) that dominate the domain-generalizable segmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the de facto standard of joint encoder-decoder finetuning actively degrades domain generalization challenges a widely-adopted assumption and provides a clear, simple remedy that can be layered on top of existing DG methods. The approach is architecture-agnostic (validated on ResNet and MiT-B5 transformer backbones) and orthogonal to augmentation-based or feature-invariance-based strategies, suggesting broad practical utility. Open questions include whether EMA is the optimal RC update scheme, whether the theoretical analysis (currently limited to a two-layer linear network) extends to deep nonlinear architectures, and whether the decoupling principle generalizes beyond the encoder-decoder split to other multi-module architectures.
