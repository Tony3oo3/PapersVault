## Problem

DP-BANDMF, the state-of-the-art correlated-noise mechanism for differentially private training, suffers from two severe scalability bottlenecks: strategy optimization requires O(n³) time and O(n²) memory (limiting it to ~10⁴ iterations), and training incurs an O(b·d) memory overhead that becomes prohibitive for large models. These limitations prevent DP-BANDMF from being used in the large-scale training regimes (billions of parameters, hundreds of thousands of iterations) where it would be most impactful.

## Input / Output

The method takes a training dataset, privacy budget (ε, δ), number of iterations n, number of epochs k, clipping norm R, and initial model parameters, and produces an optimized b-banded lower-triangular strategy matrix C (or equivalently, banded Toeplitz coefficients θ ∈ R^b for the most scalable variant) along with differentially private model parameters. The strategy is parameterized either as a full b×n matrix Θ (general banded) or a single b-vector θ (banded Toeplitz), with column normalization applied as a post-processing step. At training time, the correlated noise is generated in a distributed fashion where each accelerator handles a shard of the d-dimensional noise vectors, requiring only O(b·d/M) memory per machine for M machines.

## Method

The paper introduces three techniques to scale DP-BANDMF. First, it replaces the explicit n×n matrix operations in strategy optimization with a streaming algorithm (Algorithm 3) that computes rows of AC⁻¹ one at a time, reducing complexity from O(n³)/O(n²) to O(n²·b)/O(n·b) for general banded strategies, enabling optimization up to n ≈ 10⁵. Second, it restricts the strategy space to banded Toeplitz matrices, exploiting the fact that the inverse of a lower-triangular Toeplitz matrix is also Toeplitz (Proposition 3.1), which reduces the objective evaluation to O(n·b) time and O(n) space, scaling beyond n > 10⁷ with <2% suboptimality. Third, it distributes the correlated noise generation across machines by sharding the d-dimensional noise vectors, so each machine stores only its shard of the b most recent noise vectors, making the O(b·d) overhead manageable in multi-accelerator settings. The optimization uses L-BFGS with JAX auto-differentiation, and the banded Toeplitz variant further benefits from a fixed-point approximation (App. I) that reduces loss evaluation to O(b³) for extremely large n.

## Results

At n=16384 iterations and k=8 epochs, Amplified DP-BANDMF achieves ~2× lower RMSE than DP-SGD at ε=1, and still maintains 19% lower RMSE than the best competitor (DP-SGD or Buffered Toeplitz) at ε=8. Unamplified DP-BANDMF achieves ~5% lower RMSE than Buffered Toeplitz (BLT-DP-MF) across all ε values. The banded Toeplitz approximation is ≤0.25% suboptimal relative to the full banded strategy for n≥16384 and b≤32, while scaling strategy optimization to n>10⁷. At n=10⁵, DP-BANDMF enjoys 25% smaller RMSE than the next best scalable competitor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016), Amplified DP-BANDMF achieves ~2× lower RMSE at ε=1, with the gap narrowing to 19% at ε=8; DP-SGD remains competitive only at very small ε. Against Tree Aggregation (Kairouz et al., 2021b), Stamping (Denisov et al., 2022), and FHU (Fichtenberger et al., 2023), DP-BANDMF consistently achieves lower RMSE across all tested ε values, with the largest margins at small ε. Against the closest concurrent competitor, BLT-DP-MF / Buffered Toeplitz (McMahan et al., 2024a), Unamplified DP-BANDMF achieves ~5% lower RMSE for all ε, while Amplified DP-BANDMF achieves substantially larger margins. Against BANDED SQRT (Kalinin & Lampert, 2024), DP-BANDMF achieves up to 25% lower expected error, with the gap growing for larger n. Against the original DP-BANDMF (Choquette-Choo et al., 2024), the scalable version converges to the same optimal solution for all settings where the original could run (n<10⁴), while extending scalability to n>10⁷ via banded Toeplitz strategies with ≤2% suboptimality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DP-BANDMF (Choquette-Choo et al., 2024), the state-of-the-art banded matrix factorization mechanism that unifies DP-SGD and dense DP-MF through a tunable number of bands. The core departure is replacing the explicit n×n matrix materialization in strategy optimization with implicit streaming computation (Algorithm 3) and a further restriction to banded Toeplitz strategies, reducing complexity from O(n³) to O(n²·b) and O(n·b) respectively. Concurrent work by McMahan et al. (2024a,b) and Kalinin & Lampert (2024) also addresses scalability of DP-MF variants, but this paper is the first to combine efficient strategy optimization with distributed noise generation to handle both large iteration counts and large model dimensions simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes the primary practical barriers to deploying DP-BANDMF in realistic large-scale training (billions of parameters, 10⁵+ iterations), making the best-known correlated-noise DP mechanism accessible beyond toy settings. The finding that the optimal number of bands follows the simple rule b* ≈ ε√n/k and is typically small (≤128 in most practical regimes) means the training-time memory overhead is manageable even for large models. Key open questions remain: RMSE on the prefix workload is an imperfect proxy for learning performance (especially with adaptive optimizers, where fewer bands paradoxically help at fixed RMSE), Poisson sampling is awkward to integrate into modern training infrastructure, and the distributed noise generation assumes worker machines are not compromised.
