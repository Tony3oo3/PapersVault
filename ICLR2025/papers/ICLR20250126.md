## Problem

Neural implicit surface reconstruction from multi-view images struggles to simultaneously recover fine geometric details in complex structures and maintain smoothness in textureless regions like walls and floors. Existing methods that incorporate monocular geometric priors apply them uniformly across all regions, but these priors are highly accurate in simple areas yet unreliable in complex ones, leading to visible detail loss and erroneous surfaces. The paper addresses this by learning the angular deviation between true scene normals and prior normals rather than rigidly enforcing prior alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view RGB images at 384×384 resolution along with monocular depth and normal cues generated by a pretrained model (Omnidata by default, though GeoWizard is also tested). The SDF is parameterized via multi-resolution hash grids (Instant-NGP, 16 levels from 2^5 to 2^11) feeding a two-layer 256-node MLP, and the output is a dense watertight 3D surface mesh extracted from the zero level-set of the learned SDF. Training runs for 128,000 steps on a single A100 GPU (~14 hours), sampling 4×1024 rays per iteration.

## Method

ND-SDF introduces a Normal Deflection Field represented as quaternions predicted by a dedicated MLP f_d(x, v, n, z), which learns the SO(3) rotation needed to align rendered scene normals with monocular normal priors. An adaptive deflection angle prior loss uses shifted logistic modulation functions to dynamically weight the deflected normal loss (for complex regions with large deflection angles) against the original normal loss (for smooth regions with small angles), achieving a balance between detail and smoothness without manual region segmentation. Three deflection-angle-guided optimizations further enhance fine structure recovery: (1) ray sampling is biased toward pixels with large accumulated deflection angles, (2) the photometric color loss is re-weighted by the deflection angle to emphasize complex areas, and (3) a TUVR-style unbiased SDF-to-density transformation is applied only to thin structures identified by large deflection angles, avoiding the perspective-inconsistency convergence issues that arise from global unbiasing. A progressive warm-up schedule gradually activates the deflection field from zero rotation to prevent early-training instability.

## Results

On ScanNet, ND-SDF achieves an F-score of 82.0, outperforming the previous best (H2O-SDF, 79.9) by 2.1 points, with the highest Precision (84.0) and Recall (80.3) among all methods. On ScanNet++, it reaches an F-score of 0.721 with a notably high Recall of 0.785, surpassing DebSDF (0.696) and MonoSDF (0.675). On Replica, ND-SDF attains the lowest Chamfer distance (2.57) and highest F-score (91.60). On Tanks and Temples, it achieves a mean F-score of 14.46, substantially exceeding DebSDF (9.66) and MonoSDF (10.11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ND-SDF outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset. Against the closest competitor H2O-SDF on ScanNet, it gains +2.1 F-score (82.0 vs 79.9) and +3.4 Recall (80.3 vs 76.9), indicating superior recovery of fine structures. DebSDF, the prior state-of-the-art, is surpassed by +3.5 F-score on ScanNet (82.0 vs 78.5) and +2.5 on ScanNet++ (0.721 vs 0.696), with the gap widening on Recall (80.3 vs 76.5; 0.785 vs 0.727). MonoSDF and HelixSurf trail by 8.7 and 6.5 F-score points respectively on ScanNet. On Tanks and Temples, ND-SDF's mean F-score of 14.46 nearly doubles that of MonoSDF (10.11) and DebSDF (9.66), while NeuRIS collapses to 1.13. On Replica, ND-SDF achieves the best Chamfer distance (2.57 vs 2.90 for DebSDF) and F-score (91.60 vs 88.36), though DebSDF retains a marginal edge in Normal Consistency (93.23 vs 92.85). Traditional MVS (COLMAP) and basic neural implicit methods (VolSDF, NeuS) are far behind, confirming the necessity of prior-guided approaches for indoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

ND-SDF builds directly on the SDF-induced volume rendering framework established by VolSDF and NeuS, adopts the monocular prior supervision strategy from MonoSDF, incorporates numerical gradient and curvature regularization from Neuralangelo, and integrates the TUVR unbiased density transformation. Its key departure from prior indoor reconstruction methods (MonoSDF, NeuRIS, H2O-SDF, DebSDF) is the introduction of a quaternion-based normal deflection field that explicitly models the SO(3) angular residual between scene normals and prior normals, rather than applying priors uniformly or re-weighting them via uncertainty or visual-feature heuristics. The deflection angle then serves as a geometrically grounded signal to adaptively modulate prior loss, guide ray sampling, and selectively apply unbiased rendering to thin structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent tension in indoor neural surface reconstruction: monocular priors are highly reliable on smooth textureless surfaces (walls, floors) but unreliable on complex fine-grained structures, and prior methods lack a principled mechanism to distinguish these regimes. The deflection field provides a clean, geometry-meaningful solution that naturally separates high- and low-frequency regions without requiring additional semantic or probabilistic models. However, the method is confined to prior-assisted settings, struggles with poorly covered regions, and may converge to local optima in ambiguous areas; the authors suggest multi-view consistency constraints as a future direction. The robustness experiment showing only a 0.095 F-score drop under 60° normal bias (vs. 0.493 for MonoSDF) is practically significant for real-world deployment where prior quality varies.
