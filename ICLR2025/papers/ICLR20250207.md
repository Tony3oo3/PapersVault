## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting relies on non-commutative alpha blending that mandates depth sorting before rendering, introducing significant computational overhead on resource-constrained mobile GPUs and causing visible 'popping' artifacts during camera motion. The tile-based radix sort used by 3DGS also forces a fully compute-based rasterization pipeline, preventing the use of efficient hardware rasterizers. This paper asks whether the sorting requirement can be eliminated entirely while preserving competitive image quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250207/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained set of 3D Gaussian parameters (center positions, scales, rotations, spherical harmonic coefficients for both color and view-dependent opacity) along with a camera pose, and outputs a rendered novel-view image at 1920×1080. Unlike standard 3DGS where opacity is a fixed scalar per Gaussian, here opacity is an unconstrained view-dependent quantity computed from spherical harmonics evaluated at the camera direction. On mobile, all Gaussians are submitted via a single instanced draw call to the Vulkan graphics pipeline, with an additional subpass performing the final per-pixel normalization of accumulated color and weight.

## Method

The core contribution is Weighted Sum Rendering (WSR), which replaces 3DGS's sequential alpha-blending equation with a commutative weighted sum: each Gaussian's color is multiplied by its opacity and a depth-dependent weight, and all contributions (plus a learnable background term) are accumulated and normalized per pixel. Three weight variants are explored—DIR-WSR (constant weight), EXP-WSR (exponential decay in depth with learnable σ and β), and LC-WSR (linear correction w(d)=max(0,1−d/σ)·vᵢ with learnable σ and per-Gaussian vᵢ)—with LC-WSR achieving the best quality because it can zero out distant Gaussians to model occlusion. A second key component is view-dependent opacity, where the per-Gaussian opacity is replaced by an unconstrained spherical-harmonic function of viewing direction, allowing the network to suppress occluded Gaussians from specific viewpoints. Because the rendering equation involves only commutative addition, no depth sorting is required, enabling direct use of the GPU's fixed-function rasterizer and hardware blending. The model is trained end-to-end with the standard 3DGS loss (L1 + 0.2·D-SSIM) using custom CUDA kernels in PyTorch, and the resulting models contain fewer Gaussians on average (2.88M vs. 3.98M for 3DGS), further reducing runtime.

## Results

On the Mip-NeRF360 dataset, LC-WSR achieves 27.19 dB PSNR, 0.804 SSIM, and 0.211 LPIPS, matching 3DGS (27.21 dB) within 0.02 dB. On Tanks & Temples, LC-WSR outperforms 3DGS by 0.47 dB PSNR (23.61 vs. 23.14) and 0.006 LPIPS (0.177 vs. 0.183). On Deep Blending, LC-WSR leads 3DGS by 0.22 dB PSNR (29.63 vs. 29.41) and 0.014 LPIPS (0.229 vs. 0.243). On mobile (Snapdragon 8 Gen 3, 1920×1080), the method is on average 1.23× faster than 3DGS-Graphics and uses ~63% of its runtime memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS (the primary baseline), LC-WSR achieves near-identical quality: it trails by only 0.02 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 but leads by 0.47 dB on Tanks & Temples and 0.22 dB on Deep Blending, while also improving LPIPS on all three datasets. Compared to Compact 3DGS, LC-WSR wins on PSNR for Tanks & Temples (23.61 vs. 23.71 is a 0.10 dB loss) and Deep Blending (29.63 vs. 29.79 is a 0.16 dB loss), but leads on Mip-NeRF360 (27.19 vs. 27.08) and on LPIPS across all datasets. Against Compressed 3DGS, LC-WSR outperforms on PSNR for Tanks & Temples (23.61 vs. 23.32) and Deep Blending (29.63 vs. 29.38), and on LPIPS for all three datasets. Versus LightGaussian, LC-WSR leads on Mip-NeRF360 PSNR (27.19 vs. 27.28 is a 0.09 dB loss) but wins on Tanks & Temples PSNR (23.61 vs. 23.11) and LPIPS. Against the older NeRF-based methods (Plenoxels, INGP, M-NeRF360), LC-WSR consistently achieves the best or near-best PSNR and LPIPS, confirming that sort-free Gaussian splatting is competitive with the full 3DGS pipeline while eliminating the sorting bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), which uses non-commutative alpha blending requiring depth sorting, and Weighted Blended Order-Independent Transparency (McGuire & Bavoil, 2013), a long-standing computer graphics technique for rendering transparency without sorting. The key conceptual departure is replacing the physics-based volumetric rendering equation with a commutative weighted-sum formulation whose parameters (depth-dependent weights, view-dependent opacity via spherical harmonics) are learned end-to-end during training. This transforms 3DGS from a compute-shader-only pipeline into one compatible with hardware rasterization, while also eliminating the 'popping' artifacts identified by Radl et al. (2024) as a consequence of sorting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for mobile and embedded rendering: eliminating the sorting step removes the dominant computational bottleneck (sorting accounts for 50–70% of 3DGS-Compute runtime) and enables use of the GPU's fixed-function rasterizer, yielding a 1.23× speedup and ~37% memory reduction on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3. However, the quality gains are marginal (≤0.47 dB PSNR), and the method cannot exploit early termination, a key 3DGS optimization, because per-fragment opacity is not available in the hardware blending pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether it composes with compact Gaussian representations, and whether the lack of physically-grounded blending causes systematic failures in extreme lighting conditions (as shown in the dark-object-on-light-background failure case).
