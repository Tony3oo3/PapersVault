## Problem

Existing gene expression prediction methods either rely solely on DNA sequences or use epigenomic signals in a limited, non-unified way (e.g., EPInformer selects enhancers via statistical peak calling). No prior framework learns to discover the specific regulatory elements that causally drive a target gene's expression by jointly modeling DNA sequences and multi-modal epigenomic signals within a single differentiable pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253422/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 200,000 bp DNA sequence (one-hot encoded) centered on the target gene's TSS, concatenated with per-base-pair epigenomic tracks (DNase-seq, H3K27ac ChIP-seq, Hi-C contact frequency to TSS) and gene-level auxiliary features (mRNA half-life, promoter activity). It produces a scalar CAGE expression value and a token-level mask over the 200 kb window—either a hard binary mask (threshold at 0.5) or a soft mask in [0,1]—that identifies the actively interacting regulatory elements. The mask ratio is typically ~6–7% of the full sequence, far sparser than MACS3 peak calls (~14–16%).

## Method

Seq2Exp formulates regulatory element discovery as an information bottleneck over a token-level mask, grounded in a structural causal model that decomposes the mask posterior into a DNA-sequence component and an epigenomic-signal component (Proposition 1). The DNA-sequence component is parametric: a 4-layer Caduceus network predicts per-position Beta parameters (α₁, β₁), while the epigenomic-signal component is non-parametric, using raw signal values directly as α₂ with a fixed β₂. The two Beta distributions are combined analytically (product of Betas yields a Beta, Proposition 2), and a Beta prior with tunable sparsity hyperparameters (α₃, β₃) enforces the information bottleneck. A second Caduceus predictor then operates on the masked sequences to regress CAGE values. Training uses the reparameterization trick (treating Beta as a special Dirichlet case) for differentiable sampling and the straight-through estimator for hard-mask backpropagation.

## Results

Seq2Exp-soft achieves the best gene expression prediction across both cell types: MSE 0.1856 / MAE 0.3054 / Pearson 0.8723 on K562 and MSE 0.1873 / MAE 0.3137 / Pearson 0.8951 on GM12878. The hard-mask variant (Seq2Exp-hard) is nearly as strong (MSE 0.1863 / 0.1890) while selecting only ~6.5% of base pairs, compared to MACS3's ~14–16% mask ratio. Results are averaged over five random seeds with standard deviations reported in the appendix (e.g., Seq2Exp-soft K562 MSE 0.1856 ± 0.0032).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seq2Exp-soft outperforms all six baselines on both K562 and GM12878 across MSE, MAE, and Pearson correlation. Against the strongest DNA-only baseline (Caduceus), Seq2Exp-soft reduces MSE by ~15.5% on K562 (0.1856 vs 0.2197) and ~11.8% on GM12878 (0.1873 vs 0.2124). Compared to Caduceus augmented with epigenomic signals (the closest non-Seq2Exp competitor), Seq2Exp-soft still improves MSE by ~5.3% on K562 and ~3.5% on GM12878. Against EPInformer, which explicitly extracts enhancer regions via DNase-seq peaks, Seq2Exp-soft achieves lower MSE on both cell types (0.1856 vs 0.2140 on K562; 0.1873 vs 0.1975 on GM12878) while also achieving higher Pearson correlation. In the MACS3 comparison (Table 2), Seq2Exp-hard selects only 6.88%/6.32% of base pairs versus MACS3's 13.61%/15.95%, yet achieves substantially lower MSE (0.1863 vs 0.2195 on K562; 0.1890 vs 0.2340 on GM12878), demonstrating that learned regulatory element discovery is both more accurate and more parsimonious than statistical peak calling.

## Relation to Prior Work

Seq2Exp builds on the Caduceus bidirectional Mamba architecture as its backbone for both the generator and predictor modules, and adapts the information bottleneck formulation for rationale extraction (Paranjape et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2018) to the genomic setting. It departs from prior gene expression predictors (Enformer, HyenaDNA, Caduceus) that rely solely on DNA sequences by integrating epigenomic signals (DNase-seq, H3K27ac, Hi-C) through a causal decomposition: the mask distribution is factorized into a sequence-conditioned and a signal-conditioned Beta component, whose product remains in the Beta family. Unlike EPInformer, which uses statistical peak detection to pre-select enhancer regions, Seq2Exp learns the regulatory element mask end-to-end with a sparsity constraint, positioning itself between pure sequence models and signal-driven region selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal decomposition framework offers a principled, interpretable mechanism for integrating heterogeneous genomic data types, and the learned mask outperforming MACS3 peak calling (at a lower mask ratio) suggests that end-to-end learned regulatory element discovery can surpass hand-crafted statistical methods. However, evaluation is limited to two cell types (K562, GM12878) and a fixed set of epigenomic signals, leaving open questions about generalizability across cell types, tissue contexts, and additional signal modalities. The authors note that extending to more cell types, integrating diverse epigenomic data, and developing pretrained models for regulatory element extraction are important next steps for validating the framework's broader applicability.
