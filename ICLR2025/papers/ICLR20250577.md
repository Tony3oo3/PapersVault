## Problem

The (L0, L1)-smooth function class, introduced to model the gradient-Hessian correlation observed in deep learning, generalizes Lipschitz smoothness but has suffered from suboptimal convergence bounds and incomplete theoretical analysis. In particular, existing convex complexity results either depend on an additional L-Lipschitz smoothness constant, include the initial gradient norm, or carry an exponential dependence on L1R. This paper closes these gaps by deriving tighter first-order characterizations of the function class and building a principled stepsize framework that yields improved complexity for both convex and nonconvex settings.

## Input / Output

The gradient method (GM) requires the smoothness parameters (L0, L1) and an initial point x0, while the normalized gradient method (NGM) replaces these with a distance estimate R̂ of the initial distance to a solution, and the Polyak-stepsized GM (PS-GM) requires knowledge of the optimal value f* (e.g., f*=0 for overparametrized models). All methods produce an approximate minimizer x satisfying f(x)−f*≤ε for convex objectives or an ε-stationary point (min‖∇f(xk)‖≤ε) for nonconvex objectives. The accelerated method AGMsDR additionally requires ν oracle queries per iteration for a one-dimensional line search to compute the relaxation point yk.

## Method

The key insight is a tighter first-order characterization of (L0, L1)-smoothness via the function φ(t)=e^t−t−1, which yields a global upper bound on f(y) around any point x. Minimizing this bound in the step direction produces the optimal stepsize η*=(1/(L1‖∇f‖))ln(1+L1‖∇f‖/(L0+L1‖∇f‖)), and its simplification via the inequality ln(1+γ)≥2γ/(2+γ) gives the practical stepsize η^si=1/(L0+(3/2)L1‖∇f‖), which is shown to be equivalent up to constants to the standard clipping stepsize. For convex problems, this yields the improved O(L0R²/ε+L1R·ln(F0/ε)) rate, strictly better than the previously known O(L0R²/ε+[L1R]²). The NGM and PS-GM are shown to automatically adapt to the best (L0,L1) pair and achieve O(L0R²/ε+[L1R]²) without requiring these parameters. Finally, plugging the derived stepsizes into the AGMsDR acceleration framework (Nesterov et al., 2021) gives the state-of-the-art complexity ν·O(√(L0R²/ε)+⌈(L1R)^(2/3)⌉⌈ln(F0/ε)⌉), avoiding the exponential L1R factor present in prior accelerated methods.

## Results

For nonconvex (L₀,L₁)-smooth functions, the gradient method with the proposed stepsizes achieves the best-known O(L₀F₀/ε² + L₁F₀/ε) complexity for finding an ε-stationary point. For convex problems, the GM achieves a significantly improved O(L₀R²/ε + L₁R·ln(F₀/ε)) bound, while the parameter-free NGM and PS-GM achieve O(L₀R²/ε + [L₁R]²). The accelerated method AGMsDR achieves the best-known O(√(L₀R²/ε) + ⌈(L₁R)^(2/3)⌉·⌈ln(F₀/ε)⌉) complexity, with no exponential dependence on L₁R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gorbunov et al. (2024), the closest concurrent work, this paper achieves a strictly better convex GM bound: O(L₀R²/ε + L₁R·ln(F₀/ε)) versus their O(L₀R²/ε + [L₁R]²), which is particularly advantageous when F₀ is reasonably bounded (e.g., hot-start or logistic-type objectives). For the accelerated method, this paper's AGMsDR achieves O(√(L₀R²/ε) + ⌈(L₁R)^(2/3)⌉·⌈ln(F₀/ε)⌉) compared to Gorbunov et al.'s O(1)·exp(O(1)·L₁R)·√(L₀R²/ε), eliminating the exponential dependence on L₁R entirely. Against Koloskova et al. (2023) and Takezawa et al. (2024), the convex GM bound improves from O(L₀R/ε + √(L/ε)·L₁R²) to O(L₀R²/ε + L₁R·ln(F₀/ε)), removing the need for an additional L-Lipschitz smoothness assumption. The NGM and PS-GM results match Gorbunov et al.'s O(L₀R²/ε + [L₁R]²) bound but are derived without requiring knowledge of (L₀,L₁), and the paper shows these methods automatically adapt to the optimal parameter choice. Empirically (Figures 1–3), NGM and PS-GM outperform GM with fixed (L₀,L₁) choices on f(x) = (1/p)‖x‖^p, confirming the theoretical advantage of parameter-free methods, while AGMsDR converges faster than STM and STM-Max from Gorbunov et al. (2024) in the accelerated setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the (L0, L1)-smoothness framework introduced by Zhang et al. (2019), which generalized Lipschitz smoothness to capture the observed correlation between Hessian and gradient norms in deep learning. It extends the analysis of gradient methods under this assumption by deriving principled stepsizes from first-order characterizations of the function class, showing that widely-used clipping stepsizes are in fact approximations of these optimal stepsizes. The accelerated method analysis builds on the AGMsDR framework of Nesterov et al. (2021) and the normalized gradient method theory from Nesterov (2018), adapting both to the (L0, L1)-smooth setting. The paper positions itself against concurrent work by Gorbunov et al. (2024) and Li et al. (2023), achieving strictly better complexity bounds in the convex case and removing dependence on the initial gradient norm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper significantly tightens complexity bounds for convex (L0, L1)-smooth optimization, replacing the previously best-known O(L0R²/ε + √(L/ε)·L1R²) with O(L0R²/ε + L1R·ln(F0/ε)), and eliminates the exponential dependence on L1R that appeared in prior accelerated methods. The demonstration that parameter-free methods (NGM, PS-GM) achieve nearly the same guarantees as methods requiring explicit (L0, L1) knowledge is practically important since these parameters are generally unknown and non-unique. Open questions include whether the one-dimensional search in AGMsDR can be eliminated (replacing the ν factor with a constant), whether the (L1R)^(2/3) term in the accelerated bound is optimal, and whether the analysis extends to stochastic or strongly convex settings.
