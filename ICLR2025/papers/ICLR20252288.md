## Problem

Prototypical parts networks (PPNs) explain predictions via image patches, but each patch simultaneously encodes color, shape, texture, and contrast, leaving users unable to determine which visual feature actually drives the model's decision. Existing remedies—showing more patches or reporting vague feature-importance scores like 'color is important'—fail to resolve this ambiguity. No prior PPN architecture explicitly disentangles color from other visual features at the architectural level.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (resized to 224×224) and internally splits it into a grayscale version (for ShapeTexNet) and a bilinearly downscaled color version (for ColorNet). Beyond the standard class label, LucidPPN outputs per-object-part resemblance scores decomposed into shape/texture and color components, plus a fused aggregated score. Explanations are rendered as pairs of five grayscale patches (shape/texture evidence) alongside a t-SNE-projected color bar and five colored patches (color evidence), with pointers to the spatially activated regions on the input image.

## Method

LucidPPN uses a two-branch CNN architecture: ShapeTexNet (ConvNeXt-tiny backbone on grayscale input, producing a K×M×H×W feature map via 1×1 convolution and sigmoid) captures shape and texture, while ColorNet (six 1×1 convolutional layers on the downscaled RGB image, also producing K×M×H×W) captures color without spatial mixing. The two feature maps are fused element-wise, and final predictions are obtained by max-pooling each channel and averaging over the K object parts per class. Training combines three losses: a part-correspondence loss (L_D) that aligns ShapeTexNet activations with PDiscoNet segmentation masks so successive prototypes map to consistent object parts across all classes, a ShapeTexNet classification loss (L_S) that forces the grayscale branch to extract maximal predictive information, and an aggregated classification loss (L_A). The use of sigmoid (rather than softmax) on each channel enables independent per-prototype activation, which both supports multilabel-style matching across classes and makes visualization faithfulness directly verifiable via resemblance scores.

## Results

On four fine-grained classification benchmarks, LucidPPN achieves top-1 accuracy of 81.5% on CUB-200-2011, 91.6% on Stanford Cars, 79.4% on Stanford Dogs, and 95.0% on Oxford 102 Flowers. It sets state-of-the-art accuracy among prototypical-parts-based methods on CARS (+2.7% over ProtoPool) and FLOWER (+2.3% over ProtoPool). In a hue-perturbation robustness test on CUB, PIP-Net's accuracy drops by 30.9 points (83.9→53.0), while LucidPPN drops only 10.2 points (81.9→71.7), and its ShapeTexNet branch is completely immune. A user study confirms that LucidPPN explanations yield 67.9% user accuracy versus 60.0% for PIP-Net (p=0.044).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PIP-Net (the closest competitor), LucidPPN wins on CARS (91.6 vs. 88.2) and FLOWER (95.0 vs. 91.8) but trails on CUB (81.5 vs. 84.3) and DOGS (79.4 vs. 80.8); however, LucidPPN is far more robust to hue perturbation (−10.2 pts vs. −30.9 pts) and produces significantly more interpretable explanations in user studies. Against ProtoPool, LucidPPN achieves higher accuracy on CARS (+2.7) and FLOWER (+2.3) but lower on CUB (−4.0) and DOGS (+7.7, where ProtoPool underperforms). Against ProtoPNet, ProtoTree, and ProtoPShare, LucidPPN outperforms all three on CARS and FLOWER, and on CUB it beats ProtoPNet and ProtoPShare but falls short of ProtoTree. On consistency and stability metrics (Table 11), LucidPPN achieves 71.2 consistency (SOTA, vs. 70.6 for Huang et al. 2023) and 66.3 stability (competitive with TesNet's 60.0 and Deformable ProtoPNet's 53.5). On the broader PartImageNet dataset, LucidPPN (84.1%) also outperforms PIP-Net (82.8%), confirming generalization beyond fine-grained tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LucidPPN extends the prototypical parts network lineage (ProtoPNet → PIP-Net) by introducing a two-branch architecture that architecturally disentangles color from shape-and-texture features, rather than relying on post-hoc quantification (Nauta et al., 2021a) or multi-patch visualization (Ma et al., 2024) to reduce ambiguity. It integrates PDiscoNet's part segmentation masks as a supervision signal to enforce consistent object-part correspondence across classes, a mechanism absent in prior PP-based methods. The use of sigmoid with binary cross-entropy (instead of softmax with cross-entropy as in PIP-Net) is a deliberate departure that enables independent per-channel activation and easier faithfulness verification of prototype visualizations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete interpretability gap: users of prototype-based classifiers cannot determine whether a prediction is driven by color, shape, or texture, and LucidPPN's disentangled visualization directly resolves this. The finding that color is irrelevant for car classification but critical for birds and flowers provides practical guidance on when color features matter in fine-grained tasks. However, the method only disentangles color from the remaining features (shape, texture, contrast are still entangled), and the single-branch model without color achieves comparable user-study accuracy, suggesting the color branch's primary value is explanatory transparency rather than predictive power. A key open question is whether the same architectural disentanglement strategy can be extended to separate shape from texture, which the authors acknowledge as future work.
