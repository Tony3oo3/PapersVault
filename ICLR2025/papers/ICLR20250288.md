## Problem

No prior work has addressed image compression tailored to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Existing coding-for-machines approaches require back-propagating task losses through the downstream network, which is infeasible for billion-parameter MLLMs, and simply feeding decoded images from human-perception-trained codecs into MLLMs causes substantial task performance degradation, particularly at low bitrates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250288/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At the end device, an RGB image is encoded by a neural image codec (e.g., ELIC) into quantized latents at 1/16 spatial resolution (320 channels for ELIC), which are transmitted to the cloud. At the server, these latents bypass the image decoder entirely and are fed via a lightweight transform-neck directly into the third Transformer layer of the MLLM's CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder, skipping the first two layers. The MLLM then produces task-specific outputs: free-form captions, multiple-choice VQA answers, bounding-box coordinates for referring expressions, or class labels for few-shot classification.

## Method

The core contribution is a lightweight transform-neck (~13M parameters: linear projection, self-attention, feed-forward, two layer norms) that maps compressed image latents directly into intermediate features of the MLLM's visual encoder, eliminating the need for full image reconstruction and saving ~95% of decoding kMACs/pixel. Training uses a novel surrogate loss combining a cross-entropy term (computing cosine similarity between transformed image embeddings and CLIP text-encoder embeddings of class labels) and a distillation term (MSE between the partial visual encoder's output on transformed latents and the full visual encoder's output on the uncompressed image), back-propagated only through the partial visual encoder rather than the entire MLLM. A progressive schedule first trains with cross-entropy alone, then combines both losses (1:100 ratio), then distillation only. The framework supports three deployment scenarios: a fixed human-perception codec (d1), joint human+machine perception with rate-distortion plus distillation (d2), and machine-perception-only optimization (d3), all trainable on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

The proposed transform-neck achieves up to 60–80% bit-rate reduction at the same recognition accuracy compared to ELIC and VVC intra coding across four MLLM-based tasks (captioning, VQA, REC, few-shot classification). In terms of computational efficiency, the transform-neck requires only 52.8 kMAC/pixel and 13.19M parameters, versus 1018.0 kMAC/pixel and 64.16M parameters for the post-processing baseline — a ~95% reduction in decoding complexity. Scenario (d3), which jointly optimizes the encoder for machine perception, approaches the uncompressed performance upper bound, while (d2) achieves a near-lossless trade-off with only a marginal PSNR drop relative to (d1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: (1) Reconstruction, which feeds ELIC-decoded images directly into the MLLM; (2) Post-processing, which applies a U-Net to the reconstructed image before the MLLM; and (3) VVC (VTM 17.0 intra coding) in the supplementary. Reconstruction causes a significant performance drop across all four tasks because MLLMs were never trained on compressed images. The proposed transform-neck (d1) recovers most of the lost performance using the same latent representation, while Post-processing reaches comparable accuracy but at 18× the kMAC/pixel cost. Scenario (d2) outperforms both (d1) and Post-processing, and (d3) approaches the uncompressed upper bound. Against VVC, the proposed method achieves 60–80% lower bit-rate at equal accuracy, with VVC itself performing worse than the ELIC Reconstruction baseline due to suboptimal rate-distortion performance at the 256×256 resolution used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of neural image compression (ELIC, TIC) and the coding-for-machines paradigm, but departs from both in a critical way: prior coding-for-machines methods (Le et al., Chamain et al., Ding et al.) require back-propagating the task loss through the downstream network, which is infeasible for billion-parameter MLLMs. The authors introduce a transform-neck that bridges compressed latents directly into the intermediate layers of a CLIP visual encoder, combined with a surrogate loss (distillation + cross-entropy) that confines gradient flow to the visual encoder alone, entirely bypassing the LLM. This repurposes the knowledge-distillation idea from feature-matching into a compression-training objective, and the cross-entropy term with CLIP text embeddings is a novel addition that explicitly bridges visual features to the text domain for MLLM compatibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLLMs become the dominant interface for vision tasks in cloud-hosted deployments, efficient image transmission from edge devices is a practical bottleneck that this paper addresses for the first time. The transform-neck's universality across MLLMs sharing the same visual encoder (no retraining needed per MLLM) and the 95% reduction in decoding kMAC/pixel make this a deployable solution rather than a purely academic one. Key limitations include the focus on still images only (video/audio compression for MLLMs is left open), the requirement that the visual encoder architecture be known a priori, and the per-rate-point training of separate transform-necks. An open question is how this framework interacts with emerging token-reduction techniques for MLLM inference, which the authors note are orthogonal and potentially complementary.
