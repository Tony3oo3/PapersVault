## Problem

Existing fake song detection methods and datasets focus on Singing Voice Deepfake Detection (SVDD), where only vocals are AI-generated over real instrumental music, leaving them inadequate for detecting contemporary end-to-end synthetic songs where all components (vocals, music, lyrics, style) are AI-generated by platforms like Suno and Udio. Additionally, prior datasets lack music-lyrics diversity, long-duration songs, and open-access fake songs, and all existing methods overlook long-range temporal dependencies (repeated verses, rhythmic patterns, emotional dynamics) that are critical for effective detection.

## Input / Output

The model consumes mel-spectrograms (128 mel bins, n_fft=2048, hop_length=512) computed from 16 kHz audio, evaluated at two durations: 5 seconds (128×128) and 120 seconds (128×3744), with shorter songs zero-padded and longer songs randomly cropped during training. The output is a binary real/fake classification via a single logit head after average-pooling the globally contextualized token features. The SONICS dataset additionally provides text lyrics and song-style metadata for each track, and songs span 32–240 seconds across three fake categories (Full Fake, Mostly Fake, Half Fake) generated by five model variants from Suno and Udio.

## Method

SpecTTTra (Spectro-Temporal Tokens Transformer) replaces ViT's 2D grid patching with independent 1D slicing along the temporal and spectral axes, so each temporal clip retains full spectral information and each spectral clip retains full temporal information. The resulting clips are tokenized via 1D convolutions (kernel=stride=clip size) followed by GELU, learnable positional embeddings, and layer normalization, producing temporal tokens ⟨t⟩ and spectral tokens ⟨f⟩ whose combined count grows additively (F/f + T/t) rather than multiplicatively (F/p × T/p), reducing token count by ~3.4× for 120 s audio versus ViT. These positionally-aware tokens are fed into a standard ViT-small Transformer encoder (6 heads, 12 layers, embed_dim=384) where global attention lets temporal tokens attend to spectral tokens and vice versa, capturing long-range dependencies like repeated verses. The globally contextualized features are average-pooled and classified via a linear head, trained from scratch with Binary Cross-Entropy loss (0.02 label smoothing), AdamW, cosine LR schedule, and MixUp + SpecAugment augmentations.

## Results

On the SONICS dataset with 120-second songs, SpecTTTra-α achieves the highest overall F1 score of 0.97, outperforming ConvNeXt (0.96), EfficientViT (0.95), ViT (0.89), and its own variants SpecTTTra-β (0.92) and SpecTTTra-γ (0.88). For 5-second songs, ConvNeXt leads with F1=0.90, while SpecTTTra-α achieves 0.80. In terms of efficiency on 120-second songs, SpecTTTra-α is 38% faster than ViT (47 vs. 34 A/S) with 26% less memory (3.9 vs. 5.3 GB), and 20% faster than ConvNeXt (47 vs. 39 A/S) with 67% less memory (3.9 vs. 11.7 GB). Models trained on the SingFake SVDD dataset suffer severe performance drops on SONICS end-to-end fake songs, with F1 dropping from 0.86 to 0.33 for ConvNeXt and from 0.84 to 0.64 for ViT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ConvNeXt (Liu et al., CVPR 2022), SpecTTTra-α wins on 120-sec songs with F1 0.97 vs. 0.96 (+1%), 20% faster inference (47 vs. 39 A/S), and 67% less GPU memory (3.9 vs. 11.7 GB), but loses on 5-sec songs (0.80 vs. 0.90). Against ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020), SpecTTTra-α achieves an 8% F1 gain on 120-sec songs (0.97 vs. 0.89), is 38% faster, and uses 26% less memory, while on 5-sec songs the two are nearly tied (0.80 vs. 0.79). Against EfficientViT (Cai et al., ICCV 2023), SpecTTTra-α leads on 120-sec songs by 2% F1 (0.97 vs. 0.95) with 9% speed advantage and 74% memory reduction, though EfficientViT edges ahead on 5-sec songs (0.87 vs. 0.80). Against AASIST (Jung et al., ICASSP 2022), SpecTTTra-α is dramatically more efficient on 120-sec songs (47 A/S vs. 2 A/S, 3.9 GB vs. OOM) while AASIST achieves a slightly higher F1 on 5-sec songs (0.91 vs. 0.80). Against the SingFake baselines (Zang et al., ICASSP 2024), SpecTTTra-α outperforms ResNet+Spec. (0.97 vs. 0.90) and ResNet+LFCC (0.97 vs. 0.91) on 120-sec songs while being 3× faster (47 vs. 144–146 A/S), and Wav2Vec2+AASIST simply fails with OOM on 120-sec inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Singing Voice Deepfake Detection (SVDD) line of work (SingFake, FSD, CtrSVDD) by shifting from detecting synthetic vocals layered over real instrumental music to detecting fully end-to-end AI-generated songs where all components—vocals, music, lyrics, and style—are synthetic, as produced by platforms like Suno and Udio. Architecturally, it builds on ViT's global attention mechanism but replaces the standard 2D patch tokenization (which yields a multiplicative token count of F/p × T/p) with a Spectro-Temporal Tokenizer that independently slices along temporal and spectral dimensions, producing an additive token count (F/f + T/t) that grows far more slowly with audio length. The dataset generation pipeline repurposes LLMs (GPT-4o for lyrics, Gemini 1.5 Flash for style extraction) and audio generative models to create a controlled, diverse collection of fake songs at three levels of syntheticness (Full, Mostly, Half Fake).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rapid adoption of Suno and Udio makes end-to-end fake song detection an urgent practical problem, and SONICS (97k songs, 4,751 hours) is the first large-scale dataset purpose-built for this setting, filling critical gaps in music-lyrics diversity, song duration, and open-access fake songs that limit prior SVDD datasets. The SpecTTTra efficiency gains (38% faster, 26% less memory than ViT on 120-sec songs while matching or exceeding F1) make long-context audio classification more practical for deployment. Key limitations include English-only coverage, a gender bias in song styles, dependence on only two generator families, and mel-spectrogram-only inputs; open questions include generalization to future generators, multi-language extension, and whether raw-audio or learned-representation inputs would further improve detection.
