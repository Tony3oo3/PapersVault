## Problem

Gaussian diffusion models struggle with heavy-tailed data, class imbalance, and mode collapse, motivating extensions to heavy-tailed noise. The existing Lévy-Itô Model (LIM) extends the continuous-time SDE framework to α-stable noise but relies on fractional calculus and pseudo-differential operators, is restricted to isotropic noise, and its theoretical squared ℓ₂ loss may be infinite due to the infinite variance of α-stable distributions—forcing practitioners to fall back on ℓ₁ loss. This paper asks whether the benefits of heavy-tailed noise can be captured in a simpler discrete-time framework using only elementary tools.

## Input / Output

The forward process takes a data sample (32×32 RGB image or 2D point) and iteratively adds α-stable noise over T steps, with the noise schedule controlling the scaling. The denoising network receives a noisy sample Y_t at a randomly sampled timestep t and predicts the corresponding α-stable noise component ε_t(Y_t, Y_0). Sampling produces a generated image or 2D point by running the backward chain from an α-stable-distributed starting point, with the deterministic DLIM variant requiring only 5–25 steps to match quality that stochastic DLPM achieves at 1000 steps.

## Method

The key insight is a decomposition (Theorem 1) showing that an isotropic α-stable random vector equals a 1D positive stable random variable A times a standard Gaussian vector G, so conditioning on A reduces the forward process to a Gaussian one with modified variance. The paper introduces a data augmentation process Y_t = γ_t Y_{t-1} + σ_t A_t^{1/2} G_t that is distributionally equivalent to the original α-stable process but has Gaussian transitions given {A_t}, enabling a closed-form KL divergence for the backward process. The training loss takes the square root (r=1/2) of individual KL terms to ensure finite expectations under heavy tails, and after three design choices (fixed variance, noise-prediction reparameterization, unit weighting) reduces to a simple denoising objective where the network predicts ε_t(Y_t, Y_0) = (Y_t − γ_{1→t}Y_0)/σ_{1→t}. A deterministic sampler DLIM is derived by adapting the DDIM framework, and a faster training trick reduces the per-step computation from t heavy-tailed random variables to just two.

## Results

On MNIST with α=1.7, DLIM achieves a FID of 2.81 using only 25 deterministic steps, substantially outperforming DDIM (5.16) and LIM-ODE (68.15) at the same step budget. In stochastic sampling at 1000 steps, DLPM reaches FID 2.94 on MNIST (vs. DDPM's 3.43) and 18.00 on CIFAR-10-LT (vs. DDPM's 19.05). On the unbalanced CIFAR-10-LT dataset, DLPM also outperforms LIM (18.00 vs. 35.15) and DLIM outperforms LIM-ODE (20.77 vs. 65.78) at 25 deterministic steps. In 2D experiments, DLPM achieves MSLE of 0.071 (α=1.7) for tail coverage, far below LIM's 0.267, and F1^pr of 0.933 for mode collapse resistance vs. LIM's 0.868.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPM (Gaussian baseline), DLPM consistently wins: FID 2.94 vs. 3.43 on MNIST and 18.00 vs. 19.05 on CIFAR-10-LT at 1000 stochastic steps. Against LIM (the SDE-based heavy-tailed model from Yoon et al., 2023), DLPM achieves a dramatic improvement—FID 2.94 vs. 11.54 on MNIST and 18.00 vs. 35.15 on CIFAR-10-LT—indicating that the discrete-time formulation better exploits heavy-tailed noise. Against DDIM, the deterministic DLIM sampler outperforms it by a wide margin at 25 steps (FID 2.81 vs. 5.16 on MNIST), while LIM-ODE collapses to 68.15, far worse than even the Gaussian DDIM. Notably, LIM's performance degrades severely without gradient/noise clipping, raising questions about the stability of the continuous-time framework, whereas DLPM requires no such auxiliary hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) and DDIM (Song et al., 2020) by replacing Gaussian noise with α-stable noise, positioning itself as a discrete-time alternative to the continuous-time Lévy-Itô Model (LIM, Yoon et al., 2023). The key technical departure is a data augmentation strategy that exploits the decomposition of isotropic α-stable vectors into a product of a 1D positive stable scalar and a Gaussian vector (Theorem 1), reducing the intractable heavy-tailed backward process to a tractable Gaussian one. Unlike earlier non-Gaussian diffusion attempts (Nachmani et al., 2021; Deasy et al., 2022) that lacked proper time-reversal formulas, DLPM derives a closed-form variational loss and sampling procedure using only elementary tools, avoiding the fractional calculus and pseudo-differential operator machinery required by LIM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that discrete-time and continuous-time heavy-tailed diffusion formulations are not merely two views of the same process (as in the Gaussian case) but yield genuinely different training objectives and backward dynamics, with the discrete formulation proving more practical and effective. Practically, DLPM/DLIM offer better tail coverage, improved robustness to class imbalance, and substantially fewer sampling steps (e.g., FID 2.81 vs. 45.72 on MNIST at 25 steps), making heavy-tailed diffusion more accessible to the broader community. Limitations include evaluation only on small datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10-LT), a restricted α range of [1.5, 2.0], and the median-of-means estimator adding computational overhead; scaling to larger benchmarks, exploring non-isotropic noise, and the Cauchy DLIM variant remain open.
