## Problem

Existing weak supervision methods like Snorkel rely on latent generative models with strong assumptions (conditional independence among labelers, parametric noise models) that are difficult to validate and may not hold in practice. Moreover, their denoising step is decoupled from the discriminative model, failing to leverage modern hypothesis classes like neural networks. This paper asks whether explicit, easily-specified side information—namely bounds on each weak labeler's average error—can replace these fragile generative assumptions while still enabling effective denoising.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled data drawn from P_X, a set of m weak labelers g_j that each either abstain (return ∅) or assign a noisy label on their coverage set S_j, and per-labeler error bounds η_j (either w.r.t. the Bayes classifier or the stochastic label distribution). It outputs a conditional distribution p_f(x) = softmax(f(x)) over K classes, from which a hard classifier is derived by argmax, along with soft pseudo labels q(x) that satisfy all accuracy constraints. The error bounds η_j can be provided by domain experts or estimated from a small validation set with a Beta(1,2) prior, making the method robust to noisy or misspecified bounds.

## Method

The paper formulates weak supervision as minimizing a regularization functional R(f) subject to accuracy-bound constraints ρ(g_j, clf(p_f)) ≤ η_j for all weak labelers, then solves this via an alternating minimization algorithm that iterates between (1) fitting a regularized neural network to soft pseudo labels via cross-entropy, and (2) projecting the model's conditional distribution onto the feasible set of distributions satisfying all constraints. The projection step is the key technical contribution: for constraints on classifiers it reduces to an integer linear program (solved via LP relaxation), while for constraints on distributions it becomes a convex optimization problem with a closed-form solution expressible as a reweighted version of the model's distribution, solvable by a one-step parallel Newton-Raphson update. Theoretical analysis via agreement regions and a corrected triangle inequality shows that multiple weak labelers with conflicting coverage produce larger agreement regions and tighter error bounds than any single labeler, quantifying the implicit denoising benefit.

## Results

On the WRENCH benchmark (8 text classification datasets), the proposed method with validation-set error bounds (Ours(V)) achieves the best average rank of 4.1 among all weak supervision methods, with particularly strong gains on Semeval (79.4% vs. 65.3% for Majority Vote and 59.2% for Snorkel) and TREC (63.1% vs. 37.3% for MV and 33.4% for Snorkel). When error bounds are estimated from the training set (Ours(T)), the average rank improves to 2.8, approaching the supervised upper bound (Sup(T), rank 1.1). The method is also robust to noisy error bounds, with performance degrading gracefully as uniform noise up to ±0.5 is added to η_j.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (Ours(V)) outperforms Snorkel on 7 of 8 datasets, with the largest margins on Semeval (+20.2 pp), TREC (+29.7 pp), and Youtube (+13.4 pp), while losing only marginally on Bio (62.6 vs. 63.4). Against Losses over Labels (LoL(S)), Ours(V) wins on 7 of 8 datasets, with particularly large gains on Semeval (+26.0 pp) and TREC (+24.4 pp), but loses on IMDB (72.5 vs. 75.3). Compared to Majority Vote, Ours(V) wins on 6 of 8 datasets, with the most dramatic improvements on TREC (+25.8 pp) and Semeval (+14.1 pp), while losing slightly on CDR (67.9 vs. 68.4) and IMDB (72.5 vs. 75.7). The constant-η variant (Ours(C)) also ranks second overall (avg. rank 4.6), suggesting the method is not overly sensitive to the quality of error bound estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant latent generative model paradigm in programmatic weak supervision (Snorkel, Firebolt) by replacing implicit noise-model assumptions (conditional independence, parametric mixture models) with explicit accuracy-bound constraints on each weak labeler. Conceptually, it bridges the weak supervision literature with the learning-with-noisy-labels line of work (Natarajan et al., Massart noise), where side information about the noise level enables tractable learning, and with constrained optimization in ML (physics-informed learning, fairness constraints). The alternating minimization with a KL-projection step is a standard technique, but its application here—reducing the projection to a convex problem via a reparameterization with Lagrange multipliers (equation 18–22)—is a novel algorithmic contribution. The theoretical analysis draws on agreement regions from active learning (Cohn et al., Balcan et al.) and introduces a Wasserstein-distance-based extension to generalize error guarantees beyond coverage sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constraint-based formulation offers a more transparent and assumption-light alternative to latent generative models: domain experts only need to provide (or estimate) an error bound per labeler rather than validate conditional independence or parametric noise assumptions. The demonstrated robustness to noisy error bounds (Figure 2) is practically important since exact accuracy bounds are rarely available. Open questions include whether the approach scales to settings where weak labeler dependencies are known and must be incorporated, whether the LP-relaxation gap for the classifier-constraint variant (Section 3.1) remains small in practice, and whether the Wasserstein-based generalization bound (Theorem 4.6) is tight enough to be useful for high-dimensional inputs. The empirical gains over Snorkel and LoL are competitive but modest, and the method still trails full supervised learning, leaving room for improvement in the denoising step.
