## Problem

Neural surrogate models for PDE simulation are typically tailored to specific equations and fail to generalize across multiple different PDEs without retraining, while traditional gradient descent methods require extensive hyperparameter tuning and many iterations to converge. The paper asks whether a single, pre-trained neural optimizer can iteratively solve a wide range of linear and nonlinear physical systems at high accuracy without any task-specific retraining or manual tuning of learning rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252083/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iteration, the network receives the current physics-based loss gradient ∇ᵤLᵢ, the previous gradient ∇ᵤLᵢ₋₁, and the previous update step Δuᵢ₋₁, all normalized by their respective standard deviations to enforce scale invariance. The network outputs a scalar scale factor sᵢ and a spatial update direction that together form the next update step Δuᵢ, which is added to the current solution u to produce uᵢ₊₁. The process is repeated for a user-chosen number of iterations per time step, trading off runtime against residual accuracy, and operates on regular grids with 1–3 channels depending on the PDE (scalar, 2D vector, or 3D vector fields).

## Method

Metamizer is a U-Net-based neural optimizer that predicts improved gradient descent update steps, making it a learning-to-optimize (L2O) approach applied to physics simulation. The key architectural insight is scale invariance: input gradients and update steps are normalized by their standard deviations, and the network predicts both a magnitude (sᵢ) and a direction, so the optimizer behaves identically regardless of arbitrary loss or domain scalings. Training is fully self-supervised via a cyclic meta-learning loop: a pool of randomized PDEs (Laplace, advection-diffusion, Navier-Stokes, cloth) is 0-initialized, the network proposes update steps, the pool is updated and the physics-based loss recomputed, and the network is optimized with Adam on that loss—requiring no ground-truth data. GradNorm is applied during backpropagation to balance the training signal across PDEs with vastly different loss magnitudes and across optimization stages where gradients shrink by orders of magnitude. A single model trained for ~6 hours on an RTX 4090 generalizes zero-shot to unseen PDEs (Poisson, wave, Burgers) and achieves machine-precision residuals in ~50 iterations for the Laplace equation.

## Results

Metamizer achieves a mean squared residual (MSR) of 6.7×10⁻³³ on the Laplace equation (100×100 grid) in just 0.08 s, reaching machine-precision limits. For time-dependent PDEs at 100 iterations per time step, MSR values are 2.2×10⁻³³ (advection-diffusion), 3.5×10⁻³¹ (wave equation), 2.4×10⁻¹⁶ (Navier-Stokes pressure loss), and 5.0×10⁻³² (Burgers equation), with throughput of 230–420 iterations/s. The same single model (same weights) handles all PDEs, including the wave, Poisson, and Burgers equations which were never seen during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CuPy GPU sparse solvers (CG, MINRES, GMRES, LSMR), Metamizer achieves competitive convergence on 100×100 grids and becomes ~2× faster than CG on 400×400 grids, while retaining generality for nonlinear and nonsymmetric problems. Compared to PyTorch gradient-based optimizers (Adam, AdamW, Adagrad, SGD, RMSprop, Adadelta), Metamizer substantially outperforms all of them in both convergence speed and final accuracy, as these methods require manual hyperparameter tuning and many more iterations. Against CPU-based SciPy solvers, Metamizer on GPU is ~10× faster on 400×400 grids. The highly optimized Nvidia AMGX algebraic multigrid solver remains up to 10× faster than Metamizer on GPU for the Laplace equation, but AMGX is restricted to symmetric linear systems and cannot handle nonlinear PDEs or cloth dynamics. Finally, gradient-only optimizers like Newton-CG, Nonlinear CG, and L-BFGS-B exhibit much slower convergence than Metamizer, confirming the benefit of the learned scale-invariant update strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Learning-to-Optimize (L2O) paradigm—previously applied to neural network training (Andrychowicz et al., 2016) and hyperparameter tuning—into the domain of physics simulation, which the authors note has not been explored before. It inherits the meta-learning training loop from Wandel et al. (2021a) for fluid dynamics but redirects it: instead of learning a surrogate that directly predicts PDE states, the network learns to propose improved gradient descent steps on a physics-based loss. The scale-invariant U-Net architecture draws on the observation from Holl et al. (2022) that gradient descent should be invariant to loss/domain scaling, and the finite-difference loss formulation follows standard PINN-style residual minimization (Raissi et al., 2019), but the key departure is that the network is a universal optimizer rather than a PDE-specific solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot generalization to fundamentally different PDE classes (hyperbolic wave equation, nonlinear Burgers) without retraining is a strong step toward the 'foundation model for PDEs' vision the authors articulate, potentially enabling instant simulation of diverse physical systems in games or engineering. However, practical adoption is limited by the single fixed grid size, restriction to Dirichlet boundary conditions, and the fact that specialized solvers like AMGX remain up to 10× faster on the GPU for linear problems. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D, mesh-based geometries, coupled multi-physics systems, and whether the learned optimizer's behavior remains stable at much larger domain sizes or with more complex boundary conditions (Neumann, Robin, self-collision).
