## Problem

Existing clustered federated learning methods employ diverse and incompatible learning frameworks, making it difficult to integrate their respective advantages or extend them in a unified manner. For example, soft clustering methods like FedEM require a predefined number of clusters, while adaptive methods like CFL cannot be combined with soft clustering, and no existing approach simultaneously addresses inconsistent intra-client clustering weights, communication efficiency, adaptive cluster number determination for soft clustering, and fine-grained distance metrics aligned with different clustering principles.

## Input / Output

The method consumes local RGB images from 100 clients (CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) under controlled distribution shifts (label skew via LDA, feature corruption via CIFAR-C, and concept shift via label swapping). It produces per-sample class predictions during training using sample-wise soft clustering weights, and per-client predictions during testing using aggregated client-wise weights. The model architecture splits into a shared feature extractor (MobileNet-V2 or ResNet-18 backbone) and lightweight cluster-specific linear predictors, reducing communication overhead compared to training K full networks.

## Method

HCFL introduces a four-tier framework—cluster formulation (conditional, joint, or correlation-based likelihoods), cluster weight calculation (hard or soft), adaptive clustering procedure (splitting, merging, or fixed), and client distance metrics—that unifies existing clustered FL algorithms into a single optimization objective. HCFL+ extends this by (1) replacing client-level weights with sample-wise weights ω_{i,j;k} during training, regularized toward client-wise weights ω̃_{i;k} for test-time inference via an EM-like update, (2) splitting the model into a shared feature extractor φ and per-cluster predictors θ_k to cut communication costs, (3) extending adaptive cluster splitting/merging to soft clustering paradigms with a threshold ρ on intra-cluster distance variance, and (4) designing fine-grained distance metrics based on class-conditional and marginal feature prototypes (P_c and P_lf) that explicitly align with either the Any-Shift or Concept-Shift-Only clustering principle. Theoretical analysis in the linear representation setting shows convergence depends on cluster sample-size balance and drift magnitude.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (β=0.2), HCFL⁺ (FedRC) with ρ=0.3 achieves the best test accuracy of 69.67% with only 3 final clusters, substantially outperforming the fixed-K FedRC baseline (59.41%) and all adaptive baselines. On Tiny-ImageNet, HCFL⁺ (FedRC) ρ=0.3 reaches 40.65% test accuracy, surpassing FedRC (39.73%) and FedEM (39.25%). On CIFAR-100, HCFL⁺ (FedRC) ρ=0.3 achieves 36.50% test accuracy with 4 clusters, comparable to FedRC (37.62%) while maintaining adaptivity. The framework also demonstrates strong personalization: HCFL⁺ (FedEM) ρ=0.1 reaches 84.64% validation accuracy on CIFAR-10 with 16 clusters, far exceeding all baselines at similar cluster counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCFL⁺ (FedRC) with ρ=0.3 achieves the best test accuracy on CIFAR-10 (69.67%) and Tiny-ImageNet (40.65%), outperforming the fixed-K FedRC baseline by 10.26 and 0.92 points respectively, while using only 3–4 clusters. Against the best adaptive baselines, HCFL⁺ (FedRC) ρ=0.3 beats ICFL (61.18% test on CIFAR-10 with 14 clusters) by 8.49 points and StoCFL (57.35% with 15 clusters) by 12.32 points. On CIFAR-100, HCFL⁺ (FedRC) ρ=0.3 (36.50%) is slightly below fixed-K FedRC (37.62%) but substantially exceeds CFL (31.47%) and StoCFL (31.40%). For personalization, HCFL⁺ (FedEM) ρ=0.1 achieves 84.64% validation accuracy on CIFAR-10 with 16 clusters, far surpassing ICFL (60.58% with 14 clusters) and CFL (65.06% with 9 clusters). The key trade-off is that smaller ρ increases personalization (more clusters) at the cost of generalization, as shown by HCFL⁺ (FedEM) ρ=0.01 dropping to 14.33% test on CIFAR-100 despite 78.98% validation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the clustered federated learning line of work (FedEM, CFL, FedRC, IFCA, FeSEM, StoCFL, ICFL) by proposing HCFL, a four-tier framework that unifies these methods under a common optimization formulation. The key conceptual departure is treating cluster formulation, weight calculation, adaptive procedure, and distance metric as orthogonal design choices, enabling the combination of techniques that were previously locked into incompatible frameworks (e.g., FedRC's correlation-based clustering with CFL's adaptive cluster splitting). The HCFL+ improvements—sample-wise clustering weights, a shared feature extractor with lightweight cluster-specific predictors, adaptive soft clustering, and fine-grained distance metrics tied to explicit clustering principles (ASCP vs. CSCP)—address specific gaps identified within each tier that prior methods overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The four-tier framework provides a practical recipe for practitioners to mix and match components from different clustered FL methods, reducing the need to choose between, say, soft clustering's handling of mixture distributions and adaptive clustering's automatic cluster number determination. The identification of the gap between soft and hard clustering in the adaptive setting, and the proposal of fine-grained distance metrics explicitly aligned with clustering principles, are useful conceptual contributions that could guide future method design. Limitations include evaluation only on image classification with two architectures, the framework's combinatorial space being only partially explored, and privacy being addressed only as a future plugin rather than integrated into the design. Open questions include whether the sample-wise weight mechanism and feature-prototype distance metrics transfer to non-vision tasks or cross-silo settings with fewer but larger clients.
