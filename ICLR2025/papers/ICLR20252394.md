## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation techniques for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) effectively reduce errors on visual recognition prompts (scene description, OCR) but fail on cognitive prompts that require deliberate reasoning or knowledge extraction. The paper identifies the root cause as a 'visual perception gap': LVLMs can accurately recognize visual elements and possess the necessary internal knowledge, yet they struggle to contextualize what they see in relation to the prompt and link recognition to reasoning, leading to hallucinated responses on complex tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252394/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text instruction as input. Before decoding the response, the LVLM itself generates a free-form textual description of the image, which is concatenated as a prefix to the original instruction. The output is a grounded open-ended text response produced via modified autoregressive decoding. No architectural changes or retraining are required; VDGD is a purely inference-time intervention that can be applied to any existing LVLM. The description prefix can optionally be generated by an external captioning model (e.g., GPT-4 or a small dedicated captioner) rather than the LVLM itself.

## Method

VDGD (Visual Description Grounded Decoding) is a training-free decoding strategy that grounds token generation in an explicit visual description. At each autoregressive step, the method first applies a plausibility truncation (elbow method) to restrict the vocabulary to the top-K most probable tokens. For each candidate token w_k in this truncated set, it computes the minimum KL divergence between the token's one-hot vector and the model's conditional probability distribution at every position of the image description prefix (Eq. 3). The original logit for w_k is then replaced with the negative of this KL value, and a softmax over the modified logits yields a re-weighted sampling distribution that favors tokens consistent with the visual description. This mechanism specifically targets the paper's finding that hallucinated tokens occupy a flat, low-confidence region of the logit space, where re-scoring by description deviation effectively disambiguates the correct token from equally plausible but visually unsupported alternatives.

## Results

VDGD is evaluated using GPT-4-turbo factuality scores (1–5 scale) across 8 benchmarks (MMMU, MathVista, MMBench, Oven, LLaVA-Bench, MM-Vet, VM-Vet, and the newly introduced VaLLu) on 8 LVLMs. VDGD consistently outperforms all 7 baselines by 2%–33% relative improvement. For example, on MathVista with LLaVA-1.5, VDGD scores 2.19 vs. the best baseline (Woodpecker at 2.10, +33%); on MMMU with Qwen2-VL, VDGD reaches 2.91 vs. LURE's 2.71 (+22%). Notably, VDGD is the only method that improves performance on the VaLLu benchmark, where most baselines either degrade or show negligible gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDGD outperforms all seven baselines (VCD, OPERA, Woodpecker, LRV, LURE, PAI, and HALC) across every benchmark and every model tested. The margin is largest on reasoning-heavy benchmarks: on MathVista, VDGD beats the best baseline by up to 33% (LLaVA-1.5), and on MMMU by up to 23% (LLaVA-1.6). On the newly introduced VaLLu benchmark, VDGD is the only method that consistently improves over vanilla greedy decoding; in contrast, VCD actually degrades performance (e.g., LLaVA-v1 drops from 1.95 to 1.47), while OPERA, Woodpecker, and LURE show near-zero or negative gains. Among the baselines, LURE and PAI are the strongest competitors on MMMU and MM-Vet respectively, but both are surpassed by VDGD by 3–11 points. The paper also notes that decoding-based methods (VCD, OPERA) are more effective at reducing language hallucinations, while grounding-based methods (Woodpecker) target vision hallucinations, but neither addresses the style and IT hallucination categories that VDGD's description-grounding approach implicitly mitigates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive decoding lineage (Li et al., 2023) by repurposing the plausibility-truncation step as a prerequisite for a novel KL-divergence-based grounding mechanism. It deliberately departs from the dominant line of hallucination mitigation work (VCD, OPERA, Woodpecker, LURE, PAI, HALC), which the authors demonstrate is scoped to visual recognition hallucinations and structurally incapable of addressing cognitive/reasoning hallucinations. The key conceptual departure is the 'visual perception gap' diagnosis: rather than treating hallucination as a recognition failure, the paper reframes it as a failure to contextualize recognized elements with the prompt, and addresses it by grounding decoding in a self-generated image description rather than in contrastive logit manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic demonstration that all major training-free mitigation techniques plateau on reasoning benchmarks while improving recognition benchmarks reframes the hallucination problem and opens a clear research direction. The training-free, model-agnostic nature of VDGD makes it immediately deployable, and the VaLLu benchmark (1500 curated open-ended instances) provides a practical, affordable evaluation tool for cognitive capabilities. Key limitations include the requirement for two forward passes (description generation + grounded decoding) and error accumulation when the self-generated description is inaccurate, both of which the authors acknowledge as open problems. The reliance on GPT-4 as judge, while validated against human evaluation (ρ≈0.97), still introduces a dependency that limits full reproducibility.
