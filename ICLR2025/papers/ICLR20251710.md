## Problem

Multimodal LLMs are overwhelmingly trained and evaluated on English and western-centric data, resulting in diminished multilingual performance, culturally misaligned outputs, and poor recognition of objects from geographically diverse regions. No fully open-source multilingual MLLM existed that simultaneously addressed data scarcity across low-resource languages, cultural nuance in visual understanding, and the risk of catastrophic forgetting when scaling to many languages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251710/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images (processed at 336×336 resolution by a CLIP-ViT-Large-patch14-336 encoder) paired with free-form text instructions in any of 39 typologically diverse languages. It generates multilingual text outputs spanning open-ended multimodal chat, image captions, multiple-choice and short-answer VQA, and multimodal reasoning responses. A strict evaluation criterion penalizes 'language hallucination'—responding in English when the query is in another language—ensuring outputs match the user's language.

## Method

PANGEA uses the LLaVA-Next architecture with Qwen2-7B-Instruct as the language backbone and CLIP-ViT-Large-patch14-336 as the vision encoder, trained in two stages: projector pretraining on LLaVA LCS-558K followed by full-model finetuning on PANGEAINS (6M samples across 39 languages). The dataset is constructed via three complementary strategies: (1) machine translation of curated English instructions using Gemini 1.5 Pro with post-processing for consistency, (2) a multicultural pipeline that samples 10M images from LAION-Multi, filters them via heuristic criteria and LLM-based cultural-relevance scoring (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), recaptions survivors in their culturally relevant language, and generates diverse instruction-response pairs across 13 task types, and (3) incorporation of existing open-source multilingual datasets. A key empirical finding is that a 40% English / 60% multilingual data ratio optimizes cross-lingual transfer, with English data acting as a bridge for knowledge transfer while overreliance on it degrades multilingual performance.

## Results

PANGEA-7B achieves an average score of 59.9 (English) and 52.8 (multilingual) across all PANGEABENCH multimodal tasks, outperforming the best open-source MLLM by +0.4% on English and +10.9% on multilingual tasks. On the newly introduced xChatBench, PANGEA-7B scores 46.0 (en) and 35.8 (mul), while on MaRVL it reaches 87.0 (en) and 79.0 (mul), surpassing all open baselines. In text-only evaluations, PANGEA-7B attains 72.8 (en) and 54.3 (mul) average, exceeding its text backbone Qwen2-7B-Instruct (66.6 en) and all other multimodal LLMs, with a notable 82.0% on MGSM English.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PANGEA-7B outperforms all open-source MLLMs on multilingual tasks by an average of +10.9%, with the largest gains on M-LlavaBench multilingual (+18.3 over Molmo-7B-D's 13.8), xGQA multilingual (+12.0 over Llava-1.5-7B's 30.6), and MaRVL multilingual (+12.3 over Cambrian-8B's 61.8). On English tasks, PANGEA-7B is competitive but not uniformly dominant: it trails Molmo-7B-D on M-LlavaBench English (84.2 vs. 95.9) and Llama3.2-11B on CVQA English (64.4 vs. 70.2), while matching or exceeding all others on xGQA (64.7) and M3Exam (61.4). Against proprietary models, GPT4o still leads on xChatBench (71.0 vs. 46.0) and M-LlavaBench (104.6 vs. 84.2), but PANGEA-7B narrows the multilingual gap substantially (e.g., 52.8 vs. 64.6 on average multilingual). In text-only evaluations, PANGEA-7B (72.8 en / 54.3 mul) surpasses its backbone Qwen2-7B-Instruct (66.6 en / 54.5 mul) and all other MLLMs, with a standout 82.0% on MGSM English versus 48.8% for Qwen2-7B-Instruct, attributed to math instructions in PANGEAINS.

## Relation to Prior Work

PANGEA builds directly on the LLaVA-Next architecture (Liu et al., 2024) and its two-stage training pipeline (projector pretraining on LCS-558K, then full fine-tuning), extending the visual instruction tuning paradigm to 39 typologically diverse languages. It departs from prior multilingual MLLMs (mBLIP, PALO, M3IT) in two key ways: (1) introducing a culturally diverse image curation pipeline from LAION-Multi with LLM-based cultural relevance scoring, rather than relying solely on machine-translated English instructions, and (2) incorporating multicultural understanding instructions generated from culturally specific images. The evaluation framework PANGEABENCH extends prior multilingual benchmarks by adding cultural understanding tasks (CVQA, MaRVL) and a fine-grained rubric-based chat evaluation (xChatBench) that addresses known limitations of coarse LLM-as-Judge scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PANGEA and its accompanying PANGEAINS (6M samples, 39 languages) and PANGEABENCH (14 datasets, 47 languages) fill a critical gap: no prior fully open multilingual multimodal LLM addressed both linguistic diversity and cultural understanding simultaneously. The practical finding that ~40% English data is optimal for cross-lingual transfer (with performance dropping sharply at 100% English) provides actionable guidance for future multilingual training. However, significant gaps remain versus proprietary models (GPT4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro) on multimodal chat and complex reasoning, and multilingual OCR and low-resource language support remain open challenges that the paper acknowledges but does not fully resolve.
