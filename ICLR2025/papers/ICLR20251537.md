## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic scenes with 3D Gaussian Splatting requires modeling temporally and spatially smooth trajectories for Gaussian blobs, but existing approaches either rely on neural networks (MLPs) that lack sufficient spatiotemporal inductive biases and thus need explicit smoothness regularizers, or directly optimize 4D Gaussians, which introduces optimization difficulties and reconstruction artifacts. No prior method provides a trajectory representation that inherently encodes smoothness without additional regularization while maintaining fast convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251537/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video frames with known camera poses as input; for synthetic D-NeRF scenes (800×800), 100K points are randomly sampled in [-1.3, 1.3]³ to initialize Gaussians, while for real-world Neu3D scenes (1352×1014), SfM on the first frames provides the initial point cloud. The output consists of novel-view renderings and learned per-Gaussian trajectories (position and rotation offsets from a canonical pose) parameterized by NURBS control points. Notably, the method only models position and rotation deformations, explicitly excluding scale, color, and opacity variations to avoid overfitting.

## Method

SplineGS represents each Gaussian blob's trajectory as a linear combination of L representative trajectories (64 or 128), where each representative is defined by a NURBS curve with learnable control points and per-point weights that encode both position and rotation offsets from the canonical pose. The combination weights for each blob are predicted by a multi-resolution hash table followed by a 4-layer MLP (128-dim hidden), keyed by the blob's normalized 3D position, with tanh-constrained outputs in [-1,1]. This design embeds temporal smoothness (via the B-spline basis) and spatial smoothness (via the low-rank linear combination) as structural inductive biases, eliminating the need for any explicit trajectory regularizer. Training proceeds end-to-end with an L1 + D-SSIM reconstruction loss plus L1 opacity sparsity, starting with 3K iterations of static 3D-GS before enabling the spline deformation module, and uses the standard 3D-GS densification (clone/split) strategy throughout.

## Results

On the D-NeRF dataset (800×800), SplineGS achieves an average PSNR of 39.44 and SSIM of 0.9868 after 80K iterations, ranking second only to SC-GS (39.53 PSNR) but with substantially shorter training time (35 min vs. 1.1 hours) and higher rendering speed (188 FPS vs. 111 FPS). On the Neu3D dataset (1352×1014), SplineGS achieves the best average PSNR of 32.60 after 40K iterations, outperforming STG (32.57) and 4DGS (32.53). The method also achieves the highest FPS (188) among all compared methods on D-NeRF, and requires no explicit trajectory smoothness regularizers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On D-NeRF, SplineGS (80K) trails SC-GS by only 0.09 dB in PSNR (39.44 vs. 39.53) and 0.004 in SSIM, but trains in 35 minutes versus SC-GS's 1.1 hours and renders at 188 FPS versus 111 FPS. Against D-3DGS, SplineGS wins on both PSNR (39.44 vs. 38.50) and SSIM (0.9868 vs. 0.9857) with comparable training time. The gap over 4D-GS is substantial: SplineGS gains 5.38 dB in PSNR (39.44 vs. 34.06) while training in 35 min vs. 13 min. Compared to 4DGS, SplineGS improves PSNR by 6.57 dB (39.44 vs. 32.87) and trains roughly 13× faster (35 min vs. 7.5 hours). On Neu3D, SplineGS (40K) achieves the best average PSNR (32.60), narrowly beating STG (32.57) and 4DGS (32.53), while also outperforming NeRF-based methods like NeRFPlayer (32.12) and K-Planes (31.86) by 0.5–1.0 dB. Notably, SplineGS achieves the highest rendering speed (188 FPS on D-NeRF, 76 FPS on Neu3D) among all 3D-GS-based competitors, confirming the efficiency of the NURBS-based trajectory representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplineGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to dynamic scenes by replacing the neural deformation modules used in prior work (D-3DGS, 4D-GS, SC-GS) with explicit NURBS-based trajectory representations, and borrows the multi-resolution hash table + MLP architecture from InstantNGP to enforce spatial smoothness via linear combinations of a small set of representative trajectories. It departs from the dominant trend of using implicit neural networks to predict per-Gaussian deformations, arguing that the spline structure itself provides sufficient spatiotemporal inductive bias to eliminate the need for explicit smoothness regularizers. The method also contrasts with 4D-GS approaches that directly optimize 4D Gaussian parameters, which the authors argue suffer from optimization difficulties due to increased dimensionality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that an explicit, interpretable spline representation can match or approach the quality of more complex neural deformation modules while training substantially faster (e.g., 17 min vs. 1.1 hours for SC-GS at comparable PSNR on D-NeRF) and without trajectory regularizers. However, the method only models position and rotation deformations, leaving open the important questions of how to handle scale changes, color/density variations, and topological changes (objects appearing or disappearing) in dynamic scenes. The NURBS formulation also introduces hyperparameters (degree p, number of control points M, number of representative trajectories L) that require per-dataset tuning, and the approach has not been validated on scenes with significant lighting changes or non-rigid scale deformations.
