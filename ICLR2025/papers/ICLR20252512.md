## Problem

Mamba's selective state space mechanism, while more efficient than Transformers, still imposes high computational and memory bandwidth demands that are prohibitive on resource-constrained mobile devices. Existing sparse pruning methods (e.g., SparseGPT, Wanda) use fixed semi-structured patterns (2:4, 4:8) that do not exploit mobile SIMD architecture, and no prior work combines architecture-aware sparse learning with mobile-specific compiler optimizations for SSMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252512/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Mamba model (130M–2.8B parameters) and 128 calibration samples as input, and produces a sparse model where each 4-element weight group has a data-dependent number of pruned elements (0–4) along with compensated remaining weights. Unlike fixed 2:4 or 4:8 patterns, the sparsity is flexible per kernel, enabling target-specific sparsity ratios (30%–75%) or latency budgets. The resulting sparse model is deployed on mobile CPUs/GPUs to generate text tokens, achieving up to 7× speedup over llama.cpp.

## Method

The core kernel C4^n prunes n elements from every four contiguous weights, aligned with mobile SIMD units that process 4-element vectors in parallel. A differentiable sparse learning framework assigns a trainable probability vector (over 5 choices: prune 0–4 elements) to each kernel, minimizing a combined effectiveness loss (layer-wise weight removal error via OBS-style closed-form solution) and a sparsity or latency loss, solved with SGD over 1000 steps. After mask selection, an OBS-based compensation step adjusts the surviving weights using only 128 calibration samples to recover accuracy. On the hardware side, the compiler applies C4^n-specific weight reordering (grouping rows by nonzero count and pattern to eliminate branch divergence), a hierarchical C4^n Compact storage format that saves 25–75% memory over CSR, and a layout transformation elimination strategy that removes explicit transpose/reshape operators by aligning producer output layouts to consumer reduction dimensions.

## Results

On Mamba-2.8B at 50% sparsity, the proposed method achieves a LAMBADA perplexity of 4.26 and average accuracy of 59.9% across six reasoning benchmarks, compared to 5.11/55.2% for SparseGPT 2:4 and 10.49/50.2% for Wanda 2:4. At 30% sparsity, the method nearly matches the dense model (PPL 4.18 vs. 4.23; Avg Acc 63.1% vs. 63.3%). On mobile CPU (Snapdragon 8 Gen 2), the sparse model achieves up to 7× speedup over llama.cpp (e.g., 3.0 vs. 0.4 tokens/s for Mamba-2.8B at 75% sparsity), with 1.3×–1.7× additional speedup from sparsity over the dense baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SparseGPT 2:4 (50% sparsity), the proposed method consistently achieves lower LAMBADA perplexity and higher average accuracy across all five Mamba scales; the gap is most pronounced on smaller models (e.g., Mamba-130M: PPL 28.97 vs. 69.80, Avg Acc 41.0% vs. 37.9%) and narrows on larger models (Mamba-2.8B: PPL 4.26 vs. 5.11, Avg Acc 59.9% vs. 55.2%). Against Wanda 2:4 (50% sparsity), the advantage is even larger, with Wanda suffering severe perplexity degradation on smaller models (PPL 212.91 on Mamba-130M) while the proposed method stays within 13 PPL of dense. A key differentiator is flexibility: the proposed method supports arbitrary sparsity ratios (30%, 50%, 70%, 75%) via per-kernel learned masks, whereas both baselines are locked to a fixed 2:4 pattern; at 30% sparsity the method recovers near-dense performance (e.g., Mamba-2.8B: PPL 4.18 vs. dense 4.23). For on-device latency, the method achieves up to 7× speedup over llama.cpp on mobile CPU (Mamba-2.8B: 3.0 vs. 0.4 tokens/s at 75% sparsity), combining compiler optimizations (4.0×–4.5× from dense compiler) with sparsity-driven gains (1.6×–1.7× at 70–75% sparsity).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mamba's selective state space architecture (Gu & Dao, 2023) and the classic Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) weight compensation framework (Hassibi et al., 1993), repurposing both for a new deployment context: resource-constrained mobile devices. It departs from prior semi-structured pruning methods like SparseGPT and Wanda, which were designed for Transformer attention and feed-forward layers, by introducing a SIMD-aligned C4^n kernel that prunes n of every 4 contiguous weights—a pattern motivated by mobile CPU/GPU vector unit architecture rather than by the 2:4 or 4:8 patterns common in GPU sparsity literature. The differentiable mask-based sparse learning formulation and the layout transformation elimination strategy (building on SmartMem, Niu et al., 2024b) together constitute a compiler-integrated pruning pipeline that had not previously been applied to SSMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of sparsity for Mamba/SSM models targeting mobile deployment, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify in prior work. The practical 7× end-to-end speedup over llama.cpp and the strong accuracy retention (e.g., 59.9% vs. 63.3% dense at 50% sparsity on Mamba-2.8B) make SSM-based language models genuinely viable on smartphones, with implications for offline and privacy-preserving inference. Open questions include whether the C4^n kernel and sparse learning framework generalize to other SSM variants (e.g., Griffin, H3) or to combined sparsity-quantization pipelines, and whether the accuracy gap over Wanda/SparseGPT persists when those methods are re-tuned specifically for SSM architectures rather than applied off-the-shelf.
