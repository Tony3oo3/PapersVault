## Problem

Learning metriplectic (GENERIC) systems from data requires enforcing delicate degeneracy conditions (L∇S = M∇E = 0) that guarantee energy conservation and entropy production. Existing hard-constrained methods like GNODE and GFINN scale cubically in the state dimension n due to redundant tensor parameterizations, making them impractical for larger problems, while soft-constrained methods sacrifice thermodynamic consistency. No prior method achieves optimal quadratic scaling in both dimension and irreversible rank while maintaining provable metriplectic structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes trajectory snapshots x_s = x(t_s, μ_s) as n-dimensional state vectors sampled along solution curves, along with initial conditions for ODE integration during training. A key distinction is that NMS can operate with partial state information: the state is partitioned into observable variables x^o (positions, momenta) and unobservable variables x^u (entropies), with the latter handled via linear interpolation or a diffusion model. Outputs include the learned skew-symmetric operator L, symmetric positive semi-definite operator M, scalar energy E and entropy S functions (all as MLPs), and predicted state trajectories integrated forward in time.

## Method

NMS exploits exterior algebra to derive minimal parameterizations of metriplectic operators: L = P_S^⊥ A P_S^⊥ (skew-symmetric A conjugated by the orthogonal projector onto Span(∇S)^⊥) and M = P_E^⊥ B D B^⊤ P_E^⊥ (generic B with symmetric PSD D, projected onto Span(∇E)^⊥). This reduces the number of learnable scalar functions from O(n³ + rn²) in GNODE/GFINN to O((n+r)²), achieving quadratic scaling in both state dimension n and irreversible rank r. All components (A_tri, B, K_chol, E, S) are parameterized as MLPs with tanh activation, and training proceeds by integrating the learned ODE forward from random initial conditions and matching predicted observable states to ground truth via MAE/MSE loss. The paper further establishes universal approximation on nondegenerate metriplectic systems (Proposition 3.7) and a time-integrated error bound showing state error grows at most as ε(b/a)√(e^{2aT} − 2e^{aT} + T + 1) (Theorem 3.9).

## Results

On the Two Gas Containers benchmark (where NMS trains on partial state info without observing entropy), NMS achieves MSE 0.01±0.02 and MAE 0.08±0.06, dramatically outperforming GFINN (MSE 0.07±0.03) and GNODE (MSE 0.16±0.10). On the Thermoelastic Double Pendulum (10-dimensional state, full state info), NMS achieves MSE 0.38±0.03 and MAE 0.42±0.07, the best among all methods though the gap is narrower. NMS also exhibits quadratic scaling in both state dimension n and rank r, versus cubic scaling for GNODE and GFINN, with ~2,000 parameters matching the baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NMS outperforms all three hard/soft-constrained metriplectic baselines on both benchmarks. Against GNODE (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2021), NMS achieves a 7× lower MSE on Two Gas Containers (0.01 vs 0.16) and a 10% lower MSE on the Thermoelastic Double Pendulum (0.38 vs 0.42), while also reducing parameter scaling from cubic O(n³+rn²) to quadratic. Against GFINN (Zhang et al., 2022), NMS achieves a 7× lower MSE on TGC (0.01 vs 0.07) and a 5% lower MSE on TDP (0.38 vs 0.40), again with quadratic rather than cubic scaling. Against SPNN (Hernandez et al., 2021b), the soft-constrained method that relaxes degeneracy conditions via L² penalties, NMS achieves a 13× lower MSE on TGC (0.01 vs 0.13) and avoids the unphysical energy/entropy drift that SPNN exhibits over unseen timescales. Notably, on TGC NMS is trained without observing the entropic variables S₁, S₂ while all other methods receive full state information, making the comparison even more favorable to NMS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the hard-constrained metriplectic learning line initiated by GNODE (Lee et al., 2021) and extended by GFINN (Zhang et al., 2022), both of which enforce metriplectic structure by construction but suffer from cubic scaling in the state dimension due to redundant tensor parameterizations. The key departure is an exterior-algebraic reformulation showing that the degeneracy conditions L∇S = M∇E = 0 imply orthogonal-projection structure on the bracket-generating operators, yielding parameterizations L = P_S^⊥ A P_S^⊥ and M = P_E^⊥ B D B^⊥ P_E^⊥ that are quadratic in both dimension and rank while remaining universally approximating. This connects to the broader physics-informed neural network literature (SPNN, NODE) but distinguishes itself by guaranteeing exact energy conservation and entropy stability rather than approximating them via penalty terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic scaling result is practically significant for learning metriplectic dynamics in higher-dimensional state spaces where GNODE/GFINN become intractable, and the theoretical error bound (Theorem 3.9) provides the first generalization guarantee for learned metriplectic systems to unseen timescales. The ability to learn thermodynamically consistent dynamics without observing entropic variables addresses a realistic data limitation in physical experiments. However, the main open question is scalability: all benchmarks are at most 10-dimensional, and the authors explicitly acknowledge that extending NMS to realistic 3D problems (thousands of degrees of freedom) remains an unsolved challenge, with model reduction identified as a promising but unexplored direction.
