## Problem

Active imitation learning methods like DAgger, Ensemble-DAgger, and Lazy-DAgger rely on action-based discrepancy measures to decide when to query an expert, which is unreliable with human experts who may choose different actions for the same state and requires the expert to provide an action at every timestep. This leads to excessive context switches, high cognitive load on the expert, and inefficient use of expert time, particularly in video game and robotics settings where continuous expert monitoring is impractical.

## Input / Output

The method consumes low-dimensional state vectors (18-dim for HalfCheetah, 22-dim for RaceCar including 7 raycasts, 137-dim for Maze including 131 raycasts) and produces discrete actions (4-way for RaceCar/Maze, 6-DOF motor torques for HalfCheetah). The RND OOD detector operates on the raw state (optionally concatenated with a short historical context of past states) through a fixed random target network and a learned predictor network, outputting a scalar prediction error. The policy is trained via standard behavioral cloning (or goal-conditioned BC for Maze) on the accumulated state-action dataset.

## Method

RND-DAgger replaces the action-based discrepancy trigger of prior DAgger variants with a state-based out-of-distribution measure computed via Random Network Distillation: a fixed random target network f_targ and a learned predictor f_pred are compared, and the squared prediction error serves as the OOD score. The agent is controlled by the current policy by default; when the OOD score exceeds a threshold λ, the expert takes full control and the resulting state-action pairs are added to the training set. A Minimal Expert Time (MET) mechanism requires W consecutive in-distribution frames before control returns to the policy, preventing overly brief and fragmented expert interventions. At each DAgger iteration, both the policy (via BC) and the RND predictor (to predict f_targ on the growing dataset) are retrained, so the OOD detector adapts as the agent's coverage of the state space expands. Crucially, no expert action is queried at timesteps where the agent is in-distribution, eliminating the continuous monitoring burden of Ensemble-DAgger and the noisy action-comparison of Lazy-DAgger.

## Results

On the 3D Maze environment, RND-DAgger achieves the highest success rate (0.717±0.018), outperforming Ensemble-DAgger (0.626±0.045) and Lazy-DAgger (0.575±0.101). On RaceCar, RND-DAgger reaches 0.944±0.014 success rate, slightly below Ensemble-DAgger (0.952±0.018) but well above BC (0.883±0.029). On HalfCheetah, RND-DAgger achieves 2490±160 cumulative reward, matching Ensemble-DAgger (2489±108). Critically, RND-DAgger reduces context switches by 7–9× compared to Ensemble-DAgger (e.g., 368 vs. 2785 on RaceCar; 1214 vs. 2871 on Maze), indicating far fewer expert interventions are needed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BC and DAgger, RND-DAgger clearly wins on all three environments in task performance (e.g., Maze 0.717 vs. 0.367/0.450) while also introducing the context-switch metric that those methods cannot report. Against Ensemble-DAgger, RND-DAgger matches or slightly trails on RaceCar (0.944 vs. 0.952) and HalfCheetah (2490 vs. 2489), but substantially outperforms it on Maze (0.717 vs. 0.626) and reduces context switches by roughly 7–9× across all environments (368 vs. 2785 on RaceCar; 1214 vs. 2871 on Maze). Against Lazy-DAgger, RND-DAgger achieves higher Maze performance (0.717 vs. 0.575) and far fewer context switches on RaceCar (368 vs. 3437), though it produces more switches on HalfCheetah (708 vs. 312) due to the oracle expert's deterministic behavior making Lazy-DAgger's action-discrepancy measure more effective there. In the human-expert experiment on RaceCar, RND-DAgger matches HG-DAgger in final task performance while requiring significantly less total expert monitoring time, since the expert only intervenes at OOD-triggered moments rather than continuously observing the agent.

## Relation to Prior Work

RND-DAgger extends the DAgger lineage (Ross et al., 2011) by replacing the action-based discrepancy measures used in Ensemble-DAgger (Menda et al., 2019) and Lazy-DAgger (Hoque et al., 2021) with a state-based out-of-distribution signal derived from Random Network Distillation (Burda et al., 2019), originally designed for RL exploration. The key conceptual departure is shifting the intervention criterion from 'when does the expert disagree with the policy?' to 'when does the agent encounter unfamiliar states?', which eliminates the need to query the expert action at every timestep. The Minimal Expert Time mechanism further differentiates the method by enforcing a lower bound on intervention duration, addressing a practical gap not addressed by prior active imitation learning variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying imitation learning for video game bots and robotic systems: the cost and cognitive load of continuous expert supervision. By automating the intervention trigger via a learned OOD measure, RND-DAgger enables the agent to run at accelerated speeds without constant human monitoring, a significant practical advantage demonstrated in the human-expert experiments. Key limitations include the requirement for immediate expert takeover (no predictive alerting), restriction to fully observable MDPs, and the absence of evaluation on pixel-based or partially observable environments. Open questions include whether the RND-based OOD signal generalizes to high-dimensional perceptual inputs and how to incorporate richer feedback modalities beyond full action takeover.
