## Problem

The prevailing 'one label per instance' paradigm in dataset distillation creates two critical limitations: (1) as IPC increases, additional synthetic instances for the same class capture duplicated intra-class features rather than diverse ones, wasting the distillation budget; and (2) the class-specific optimization creates implicit 'class barriers' that prevent synthetic data from capturing inter-class feature distributions, resulting in chaotic decision boundaries and poor generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural RGB image dataset with one-hot class labels (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet-1k at 224×224) and produces a compact synthetic dataset stored as pairs of natural image subsets P^k (one instance per class) and Universal Feature Compensator vectors U^k (M vectors, one per architecture). The actual synthetic images are generated on-the-fly during training via simple pixel-wise addition (x_i + u_j), so only the base instances and compensators need to be stored. Static soft labels (one per synthetic instance, generated by averaging logits from M architectures) replace the per-epoch dynamic soft labels, reducing label storage by 99.3%.

## Method

INFER replaces the class-specific synthesis paradigm with a 'one instance for ALL classes' approach centered on Universal Feature Compensators (UFCs). For each of K subsets, C natural instances (one per class) are randomly selected as P^k, and M UFC vectors are optimized to minimize classification loss plus a BN-matching loss that regularizes generated instances to match the original dataset's per-layer mean and variance. Synthetic instances are produced by simple addition (s̃_i = x_i + u_j), multiplying the effective distillation budget by M without auxiliary generator networks. The optimized UFCs are designed to be compatible with MixUp augmentation: because P^k spans all classes and natural instances inherently follow linear label interpolation, the generated synthetic data supports standard MixUp with static soft labels (averaged over M architectures) rather than expensive per-epoch dynamic labels. This design simultaneously addresses feature duplication (by diversifying through natural instances) and inter-class feature oversight (by letting UFCs capture cross-class structure), forming thin, clear decision boundaries.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k at ipc=50 with the same compression ratio (4.04%), INFER achieves 51.8% top-1 accuracy with ResNet18, 53.3% with ResNet-50, and 52.2% with ResNet-101, outperforming SRe2L* (17.3%, 17.2%, 15.8%) by 34.5% on ResNet18. On CIFAR-100 at ipc=50 with ResNet18, INFER+Dyn reaches 68.9% accuracy versus SRe2L's 49.5% and G-VBSM's 65.0%. On Tiny-ImageNet at ipc=50, INFER+Dyn achieves 54.6% versus SRe2L's 41.1% and G-VBSM's 47.6%. INFER reduces soft label storage by 99.3% compared to SRe2L's dynamic labeling while maintaining or exceeding performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INFER decisively outperforms SRe2L (Yin et al., 2024) across all settings: at the same 4.04% compression ratio on ImageNet-1k ipc=50, INFER achieves 51.8% vs. SRe2L*'s 17.3% (ResNet18), a 34.5% gap; on CIFAR-100 ipc=50, INFER+Dyn reaches 68.9% vs. SRe2L's 49.5%. Against G-VBSM (Shao et al., 2024), INFER leads by 18.9% on ImageNet-1k ipc=50 (51.8% vs. 32.9% at same CR) and by 3.9% on CIFAR-100 ipc=50 (68.9% vs. 65.0%). On CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet with ConvNet128, INFER surpasses MTT (Cazenavette et al., 2022) by 14.2% (Tiny-ImageNet ipc=50: 32.3% vs. 28.0%) and SeqMatch (Du et al., 2023b) by 8.5% (CIFAR-100 ipc=50: 57.0% vs. 49.2%). The older gradient-matching baselines (DC, DSA, KIP, RFAD) are only evaluated on CIFAR-10/100 with ConvNet128 and are substantially outperformed by INFER (e.g., 57.0% vs. 71.1% for RFAD on CIFAR-10 ipc=50, though INFER uses ResNet18 for synthesis). Notably, INFER with static labels (99.3% less storage) still outperforms SRe2L and G-VBSM that rely on dynamic soft labels, while INFER+Dyn further extends the lead on larger architectures like ResNet-50 (63.4% vs. 58.7% for G-VBSM on ImageNet-1k ipc=50).

## Relation to Prior Work

INFER departs from the dominant 'one instance for one class' paradigm in dataset distillation (Wang et al., 2018; Zhao et al., 2021; Cazenavette et al., 2022; Yin et al., 2024) by introducing a 'one instance for ALL classes' formulation centered on the Universal Feature Compensator (UFC). It builds directly on SRe2L (Yin et al., 2024) for the BN regularization loss and MixUp-based augmentation, and on G-VBSM (Shao et al., 2024) as the most recent strong baseline, but replaces their class-specific synthetic optimization with a simple additive integration of a shared compensator vector with randomly selected natural instances. The static soft-label strategy also draws on MixUp (Zhang et al., 2018) but eliminates the per-epoch dynamic label generation that SRe2L requires, reducing label storage by 99.3%.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 34.5% accuracy gap over SRe2L at matched compression ratio on ImageNet-1k, combined with the 99.3% soft-label storage reduction, makes INFER a practically compelling option for large-scale dataset distillation where storage and training efficiency are critical. The conceptual reframing—identifying 'class barriers' and 'feature duplication' as structural limitations of the one-hot-label paradigm—provides a clear diagnostic for why prior methods plateau at higher IPC settings. However, INFER still requires storing natural data instances (P^k) alongside the UFCs, meaning it is not a purely synthetic distillation in the strictest sense; whether the additive integration generalizes to non-image modalities or to settings where no natural data is available at inference time remains an open question.
