## Problem

Existing speech-driven 3D facial animation methods that attempt to disentangle speech emotion from content (e.g., EmoTalk) suffer from three interrelated failures: feature confusion (different emotional speech with identical content yields the same facial motion), emotion weakening (varying emotional intensities collapse into a single representation), and mean-face (individual stylistic differences in emotional expression are averaged out). These issues stem from the lack of explicit supervisory signals that anchor the emotion representation to observable audio-visual cues and differentiate emotional intensity levels.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a 16 kHz speech sequence and a speaker identity embedding (1024-d vector) as input, and outputs a per-frame sequence of 53-dimensional FLAME parameters (50 expression coefficients + 3 jaw pose) at 25 fps, which are then rendered into a 5023-vertex 3D mesh. During training, rendered grayscale face images (128×128, 25 fps) from the ground-truth FLAME sequence are additionally fed through a visual emotion encoder to provide the audio-visual co-supervision signal, but this video pathway is discarded at test time.

## Method

EcoFace comprises two modules. The Emotional Disentanglement Encoder (EDE) uses a HuBERT-based audio encoder and an adapted ResNet-18 video encoder to extract 1024-d emotion features from speech and rendered faces respectively; it is trained with a supervised contrastive loss (pulling same-emotion features together, pushing different-emotion features apart), an audio-visual L2 loss (aligning audio and visual emotion features frame-by-frame to transfer pronounced visual emotion cues into the audio embedding), and a triplet loss with a margin that preserves intensity-level separation within each emotion cluster. The Emotional Motion Generator (EMG) is a VAE with a flow-based prior (1D-convolution coupling + dilated convolutions) that maps conditioned speech content and emotion features into a low-dimensional latent distribution of universal facial motion patterns; a speaker-aware decoder (condition-WaveNet + transposed 1D-conv) then generates stylized FLAME sequences by adding a personalized identity embedding to the speech conditions. Training combines Monte-Carlo ELBO, a pre-trained SyncNet-style lip-sync expert loss on mouth landmarks, and a visual emotion similarity loss computed by the EDE on rendered ground-truth vs. predicted frames.

## Results

On RAVDESS, EcoFace achieves the best VE-FID of 21.57 (vs. 34.01 for EMOTE, the next best), LVE of 2.19 mm, LSE-D of 9.616, and LSE-C of 1.010, outperforming all five baselines on every metric. On HDTF, it attains the lowest LVE (2.61) and LSE-D (10.253) with LSE-C of 0.823. On MEAD, EcoFace achieves the best LSE-D (9.113) and LSE-C (0.709), though EMOTE retains a slight edge on VE-FID (19.36 vs. 32.44) and LVE (4.91 vs. 5.21). In the user study, EcoFace receives the highest preference votes across full-face (30.90%), lip-sync (29.40%), and emotion expression (41.24%) categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EcoFace outperforms all five baselines on RAVDESS across every metric, with the largest margin on VE-FID (21.57 vs. 34.01 for EMOTE, a 37% improvement) and LVE (2.19 vs. 3.18 for EmoTalk). On HDTF and VOCASET, it achieves the best LVE, LSE-D, and LSE-C, with LSE-C reaching 0.823 and 0.743 respectively—surpassing UniTalker (0.815, 0.713) which was the strongest lip-sync baseline. On MEAD, EMOTE retains advantages on VE-FID (19.36 vs. 32.44) and LVE (4.91 vs. 5.21) due to its use of ground-truth emotion labels, but EcoFace still wins on lip-sync metrics (LSE-D 9.113 vs. 10.049; LSE-C 0.709 vs. 0.692). FaceFormer and CodeTalker, which were originally trained on unemotional data, show the weakest emotional quality (VE-FID > 73 on MEAD) and degraded lip-sync after retraining on emotional data, confirming the paper's hypothesis that content and emotion interfere when not explicitly disentangled. The user study further validates these quantitative findings, with EcoFace receiving 41.24% preference for emotion expression versus 20.89% for EMOTE and 13.63% for UniTalker.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the speech-emotion disentanglement line initiated by EmoTalk (Peng et al., 2023b), which first separated content and emotion features from audio but suffered from feature confusion due to the absence of explicit supervisory signals. EcoFace departs from this by introducing an audio-visual co-disentanglement encoder that uses contrastive, triplet, and cross-modal alignment losses to construct a well-separated emotion space, and by borrowing the flow-based prior from GeneFace (Ye et al., 2023) to ensure temporal coherence in the VAE latent space. The speaker-aware decoder and the independently trained SyncNet-style lip-sync expert (Prajwal et al., 2020) further distinguish the architecture from prior content-only or label-driven approaches like FaceFormer, CodeTalker, and EMOTE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in automatic emotional talking-face generation: prior methods either require manual emotion labels (EMOTE, MeshTalk) or produce emotionally ambiguous outputs due to content-emotion entanglement. The audio-visual co-disentanglement strategy offers a principled way to ground subtle speech emotion cues in more salient visual motion, potentially generalizing to other multimodal disentanglement problems. Key open questions remain: the emotion latent space is non-interpretable (no mapping to specific FAUs), the training relies on RAVDESS's limited 1,440 clips supplemented by unemotional HDTF data, and the paper does not explore whether emotion can be inferred from speech content alone without any visual reference.
