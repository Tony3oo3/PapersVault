## Problem

In absorbing discrete diffusion models like SEDD, the concrete score (ratio of marginal probabilities between transitive states) is modeled with explicit time-conditioning, and a 'scaling trick' that divides the score output by a time-dependent factor has proven empirically effective but remained theoretically unexplained. The paper asks: what is the actual structure of the concrete score, and can the model be simplified by exploiting it?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252338/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a partially masked token sequence (a mix of real tokens and [M] mask tokens) and outputs, for each [M] position, a categorical distribution over the vocabulary representing the probability of each candidate unmasking. During sampling, the model starts from a fully masked sequence of fixed length and iteratively unmask tokens until all positions are filled; once a token is unmasked it remains fixed, so the input to the network changes only when new tokens are revealed. The model is time-independent, meaning it never receives the diffusion timestep as an input, unlike SEDD.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that the concrete score in absorbing diffusion factorizes into an analytic time-dependent scalar e^{-σ̄(t)}/(1−e^{-σ̄(t)}) multiplied by a time-independent conditional probability p₀(x̂ⁱ | x_t^UM) of clean data given the currently unmasked tokens. This explains the scaling trick as a reparameterization and motivates RADD, which removes all time-conditioning from the score network, reducing it to a standard GPT-like transformer with softmax output. Because unmasked tokens never change, the network output can be cached across sampling steps where the input is unchanged, yielding an analytic reduction in expected NFEs (Eq. 3.4). A second theorem (Theorem 2) proves that the DSE training objective is mathematically equivalent to the any-order autoregressive (AO-ARM) loss by introducing intermediate t-DCE and λ-DCE losses and performing a change of variable from time to masking probability, unifying the two model families under a single objective.

## Results

On five zero-shot language modeling benchmarks at the GPT-2 scale, RADD achieves state-of-the-art perplexity among discrete diffusion models. For the medium model, RADD-AO reaches 41.96 (LAMBADA), 29.96 (WikiText-2), 79.06 (PTB), 28.51 (WikiText-103), and 57.07 (1BW), outperforming SEDD-Scale (42.77, 31.04, 87.12, 29.98, 61.19) across all five datasets. The small-model RADD variants similarly beat SEDD-Scale by 1–4 perplexity points on average. All four RADD loss variants (DSE, t-DCE, λ-DCE, AO) achieve comparable performance, validating the theoretical equivalence of their training objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RADD outperforms all discrete diffusion baselines. Against SEDD-Scale (the strongest prior diffusion method), RADD-DSE reduces perplexity by 1.35 on LAMBADA (49.57 vs 50.92), 3.01 on WikiText-2 (38.83 vs 41.84), 2.50 on PTB (111.74 vs 114.24), 3.16 on WikiText-103 (37.46 vs 40.62), and 6.94 on 1BW (72.35 vs 79.29) for the small model. Against D3PM and PLAID, RADD achieves substantially lower perplexity (e.g., 49.57 vs 93.47 on LAMBADA for D3PM; 49.57 vs 57.28 for PLAID). Compared to the autoregressive GPT-2 baseline, RADD still trails on LAMBADA (49.57 vs 45.04) and WikiText-2 (38.83 vs 42.43) for the small model, but surpasses GPT-2 on PTB (111.74 vs 138.43) and WikiText-103 (37.46 vs 41.60). At the medium scale, RADD-AO narrows the gap to GPT-2 further (41.96 vs 35.66 on LAMBADA) while maintaining a clear advantage on PTB (79.06 vs 123.14) and WikiText-103 (28.51 vs 31.39).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SEDD (Lou et al., 2024), which introduced denoising score entropy for absorbing discrete diffusion and a practical 'scaling trick' whose theoretical justification was lacking. The key departure is showing that the concrete score in absorbing diffusion decomposes analytically into a time-independent conditional probability of clean data multiplied by a closed-form scalar, which both explains the scaling trick and motivates removing time conditioning entirely from the network. The paper further unifies absorbing discrete diffusion with any-order autoregressive models (AO-ARMs) by proving equivalence of their training objectives, extending the earlier ELBO-based connection from Hoogeboom et al. (2022) to a full four-loss equivalence chain. Concurrent works (Shi et al., 2024; Sahoo et al., 2024) independently derived similar cross-entropy formulations, but RADD's time-independent parameterization and complete E-NFE analysis for caching-based acceleration are distinct contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification between absorbing discrete diffusion and AO-ARMs reframes what diffusion language models are actually learning—expected negative log-likelihood over factorial orderings—which provides new objective functions for training and likelihood evaluation and suggests an ensemble interpretation. Practically, removing time conditioning simplifies the architecture to a standard GPT-like transformer and enables a caching strategy that reduces expected NFEs, particularly at larger sampling steps. However, the empirical gains over SEDD are modest (a few perplexity points at GPT-2 scale), and the model is limited to fixed-length generation, restricting its applicability compared to autoregressive models. Scaling beyond GPT-2 size and extending to variable-length generation remain open questions that will determine whether these theoretical insights translate into broader practical impact.
