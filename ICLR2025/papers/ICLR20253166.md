## Problem

Multi-instance human matting remains challenging in complex scenes where multiple people overlap or occlude each other, as existing methods lack explicit 3D scene decomposition and treat background as an afterthought rather than a first-class instance. Additionally, the potential of matting representations for downstream instance-level image editing (removal, occlusion reordering, dragging) has been largely unexplored by matting-focused research.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253166/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view RGB image and a monocular depth map (obtained from off-the-shelf estimators such as Metric3D or Depth Anything V2) as input. It outputs a full instance-level multiplane representation (Inst-MP) consisting of per-instance alpha mattes, per-instance foreground colors, and an explicit background plane (matte + color), which collectively satisfy the rendering equation I = Σ c_i·α_i. This representation can be directly manipulated to produce edited images (instance removal, occlusion reordering, dragging) with negligible additional computation once built.

## Method

MP-Mat introduces a two-level multiplane representation: a scene geometry-level multiplane (SG-MP) that decomposes the scene into N depth-ordered planes at the feature level (not pixel level as in MPI), generated by a Plane Generation Network that iteratively refines plane depths via self-attention and produces plane-distinctive masks via a UNet-like interpreter; and an instance-level multiplane (Inst-MP) that uses learnable queries with a transformer decoder to extract per-instance opacity, color, and uncertainty embeddings from the SG-MP features. An uncertainty-guided refinement module selects the top 10% most uncertain pixels and applies cross-instance self-attention to sharpen boundary predictions. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with a multi-task loss combining detection (bipartite-matched) and matting (alpha + Laplacian + composition) objectives. For editing, the built Inst-MP enables training-free, near-zero-cost manipulation: instance removal reassigns alpha to the nearest behind-plane, occlusion reordering iteratively swaps alpha between adjacent planes, and dragging composites a cropped instance plane onto a target image.

## Results

On the HIM-100K dataset, MP-Mat achieves SAD 26.75 and MSE 0.49, outperforming the best prior method Maggie (SAD 29.48, MSE 0.78) by 2.73 SAD and 0.29 MSE. On the SMPMat dataset, MP-Mat reaches SAD 33.25 and MSE 0.81, improving over Maggie (SAD 37.41, MSE 0.91) by 4.16 SAD. For instance editing, MP-Mat under zero-shot inference achieves 3.26% Mean L1 Loss and 28.32 dB PSNR on occlusion reordering, surpassing fine-tuned diffusion-based editors (Inst-inpaint: 12.65% L1, 21.2 dB; Dragon Diffusion: 13.85% L1, 19.82 dB) by over 9% L1 and 8.5 dB PSNR while being 68% faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MP-Mat outperforms all instance-aware matting baselines by large margins: Maggie (CVPR 2024) is the closest competitor at SAD 29.48 on HIM-100K, while MP-Mat achieves 26.75 (−2.73 SAD); E2E-HIM (IEEE TCSVT 2024) and InstMatte (CVPR 2022) trail further at SAD 32.22 and 37.34 respectively. The composite baselines (FBA and MG paired with Mask-RCNN, SOLO, or EVA for instance segmentation) all score above SAD 37 on HIM-100K, confirming that simply bolting a matting model onto an off-the-shelf detector is insufficient. For instance editing, MP-Mat in zero-shot mode beats both Inst-inpaint (arXiv 2023) and Dragon Diffusion (CVPR 2024) on occlusion reordering by >9% Mean L1 Loss and 8.5 dB PSNR, while also being 68% faster per edit. On foreground estimation (Composition-1k), MP-Mat (SAD 57.44) surpasses Context Aware Matting (SAD 61.72) and FBA (SAD 74.85), though SampleNet (SAD 95.36) is the weakest. The paper also reports that on the P3M and AM-2K datasets, MP-Mat maintains its lead over all three instance-aware baselines, demonstrating generalization beyond the primary training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Multiplane Image (MPI) concept from Tucker & Snavely (2020) and Layered Depth Images (Shade et al., 1998) — originally designed for pixel-level novel view synthesis — into a feature-level scene geometry representation for instance matting. It departs from prior instance matting methods (InstMatte, E2E-HIM, Maggie) that rely on external instance segmentation masks by introducing a dual multiplane formulation: a depth-oriented SG-MP that splits the scene into planes, and an instance-oriented Inst-MP that models each instance's matte, color, and the background as a special instance. The integration property of Inst-MP (I = Σ c_i·α_i) is leveraged for training-free instance-level editing, a direction largely unexplored by matting-focused methods that typically output only alpha mattes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual multiplane representation offers a clean, interpretable decomposition that simultaneously addresses occlusion handling (via depth-aware plane splitting) and instance differentiation (via per-instance matte+color modeling), yielding substantial improvements over prior instance matting methods. The demonstration that a matting framework under zero-shot inference can outperform trained diffusion-based editing methods (Inst-inpaint, DragDiffusion) by large margins on removal and reordering tasks suggests that explicit scene decomposition may be a more efficient and controllable editing paradigm than generative approaches. Key limitations include the focus on human instances only (no multi-instance datasets exist for other categories), the dependency on an external depth estimator, and the editing evaluation being partly on synthetic data (ORHuman) with qualitative-only results for dragging.
