## Problem

Existing statistical watermarking techniques for language models rely on previously generated context (n-grams) to derive watermark keys, which is fundamentally incompatible with order-agnostic LMs where tokens are generated in non-sequential order and the generation order is unknown at detection time. This leaves a critical gap for watermarking widely used non-autoregressive architectures in protein design, machine translation, speech generation, and time series forecasting.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a prompt (e.g., a 3D protein backbone or a source-language sentence), a secret key, and a generation order permutation, and produces a watermarked token sequence of length n. The detector takes only the generated token sequence (no access to the LM, prompt, or generation order) and outputs a binary watermark verdict with a theoretically controlled false positive rate. Key recovery at detection is position-based: each token is mapped to a key by checking which of l vocabulary partitions it belongs to, eliminating the need to know the generation order.

## Method

PATTERN-MARK uses a Markov chain with transition matrix A to generate a key sequence of length n, where each key selects a vocabulary partition whose tokens receive a logit boost (Eq. 1) during sampling. The Markov structure ensures that specific key patterns (e.g., alternating k₁k₂k₁) appear at a significantly higher frequency than under a uniform random key sequence, creating a detectable statistical signal. At detection, the key sequence is recovered position-by-position from the content, and a hypothesis test counts occurrences of target patterns T ⊂ K^m in the recovered sequence. The exact null-hypothesis probability distribution P_{T,n} is computed via a dynamic programming algorithm (Alg. 3/4) with complexity O(n²m) for the two-key alternating-pattern case, yielding a p-value that controls the false positive rate without requiring knowledge of the generation order or the original prompt.

## Results

On protein generation (ProteinMPNN), PATTERN-MARK (δ=1.50) achieves 100% TPR at 10% and 1% FPR, 99.87% at 0.1% FPR, and 96.39% at 0.001% FPR, while maintaining a pLDDT of 84.02 (vs. 85.46 for the unwatermarked model). On machine translation (CMLM, Romanian→English), PATTERN-MARK (δ=5) reaches 100% TPR at 10% FPR, 98.01% at 0.1% FPR, and 91.13% at 0.001% FPR, with a BLEU score of 24.23 (vs. 32.85 unwatermarked). At moderate δ values (e.g., δ=1.25 for protein, δ=4 for MT), PATTERN-MARK simultaneously surpasses all baselines in both detection TPR and generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PATTERN-MARK is compared against three baselines adapted from sequential-LM watermarking: Soft watermark and Multikey (both from Kirchenbauer et al., 2023a) and Unigram (Zhao et al., 2023). On protein generation at δ=1.50, PATTERN-MARK achieves 100% TPR at 10% FPR versus 12.99% for Soft watermark and 67.20% for Multikey, while also outperforming Unigram (96.12%) with a higher pLDDT (84.02 vs. 83.58). On machine translation at δ=5, PATTERN-MARK reaches 100% TPR at 10% FPR compared to 97.91% (Soft), 89.93% (Multikey), and 99.90% (Unigram), while maintaining a BLEU of 24.23 versus 22.23 for Unigram. The advantage is most pronounced at low FPR thresholds: at 0.001% FPR on protein generation, PATTERN-MARK (96.39%) nearly doubles Unigram's TPR (69.75%) and is orders of magnitude above Soft (0.00%) and Multikey (2.68%). Under robustness attacks (random token modification and ChatGPT paraphrasing), PATTERN-MARK consistently retains higher TPR than all baselines at every attack strength tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the red-green list watermarking framework of Kirchenbauer et al. (2023a), reusing its logit-promotion formula (Eq. 1) but replacing the context-dependent (n-gram hash) key generation with a Markov-chain-based key sequence that is independent of previously generated tokens. It departs from the broader statistical watermarking literature (Unigram, Soft, Multikey) by arguing that all context-dependent key schemes are fundamentally incompatible with order-agnostic decoding, and further proves that distortion-free watermarking (Christ et al., Hu et al., Wu et al.) is inapplicable to non-autoregressive order-agnostic models. The detection side introduces a novel pattern-counting hypothesis test with a dynamic-programming algorithm for exact p-value computation under the null Markov chain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first watermarking framework for order-agnostic LMs, a model class with growing importance in protein design (ProteinMPNN), machine translation (CMLM), speech generation, and time series forecasting. The practical impact is significant for biosecurity (watermarking AI-designed proteins) and content provenance in non-sequential generation pipelines. Limitations include evaluation on only two model architectures and two tasks, and the quality-detectability trade-off remains: achieving near-perfect detection (e.g., 99.87% TPR at 0.1% FPR for protein generation) requires δ=1.5, which still degrades pLDDT by ~1.4 points. Open questions include whether the Markov-chain key design generalizes to larger key spaces, whether the pattern-based detection remains robust under more sophisticated adversarial attacks, and whether the framework extends to fully non-autoregressive models that generate all tokens simultaneously.
