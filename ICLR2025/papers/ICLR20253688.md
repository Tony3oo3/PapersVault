## Problem

Training and fine-tuning LLMs require storing full-rank gradients plus Adam optimizer states, tripling model memory. Existing memory-efficient methods either restrict weight updates to a fixed low-rank subspace (LoRA), degrading performance and disrupting training dynamics, or project gradients onto a fixed-rank subspace (GaLore) that ignores the empirically observed phenomenon that gradient rank naturally decays toward one during training. No prior method adaptively reduces the gradient projection rank over time or transforms optimizer moments when the projection subspace changes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained model weights, a training dataset, a loss function, and hyperparameters including an information threshold η_th (fraction of gradient energy to preserve), an initial rank r_init, and a maximum rank r_max. It outputs full-rank updated model weights directly (not a parallel adapter), though a low-rank LoRA-style adapter can be extracted post-hoc by factoring the weight difference Δ = W_fine-tuned − W_pretrained. Unusually, the effective projection rank r_t^j is a per-layer, per-iteration quantity that shrinks over training, so the optimizer state dimensions (M_t, V_t) are not fixed but evolve with the subspace.

## Method

AdaRankGrad proves (Lemma 1) that under vanilla SGD on reversible layers, the gradient's Frobenius-norm residual after rank-1 projection decays as O(C^{-t}), establishing that gradient rank asymptotically approaches one. At each outer-loop iteration, it runs an Information-based Adaptive Subspace Selection (IASS) procedure that binary-searches for the smallest rank r_t preserving at least η_th fraction of gradient energy, using a randomized SVD (SSRF) for efficient subspace identification. When the projected gradient converges below a threshold, the algorithm updates the projection subspace and transforms the stored first and second Adam moments from the old subspace to the new one via R_t = Q_t^T Q_{t-1}, a step absent in GaLore. Weight updates are then performed in the low-dimensional projected space using standard Adam/AdamW, with the update mapped back to full space via Q_t, enabling full-parameter training with optimizer-state memory proportional to r_t·(n+m) rather than n·m.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-Base (initial rank=4), AdaRankGrad achieves an average effective rank of ~3.7 while using only 202M memory (vs. 253M for GaLore and 257M for LoRA), and outperforms both on 6 of 8 tasks (e.g., RTE 81.23 vs. 79.42 for GaLore, SST-2 94.8 vs. 94.0). For LLaMA pre-training on C4 at 1B scale, AdaRankGrad achieves the best validation perplexity of 14.71 with the lowest memory footprint of 3.62G, compared to Full-Rank (15.56, 7.80G) and GaLore (15.64, 4.38G). On LLaMA 7B pre-training, AdaRankGrad reaches 14.87 perplexity at 120K steps using 16.4G memory, matching full Adam (14.83) while using 37% less memory (vs. 26G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GaLore (rank=4), AdaRankGrad wins on 6 of 8 GLUE tasks (notably RTE +1.81, SST-2 +0.8, MRPC +0.35) while using 20% less memory (202M vs. 253M); it trails only on MNLI (−0.4) and QQP (−0.66). Against LoRA (rank=4), AdaRankGrad dominates on 6 of 8 tasks with the largest gap on RTE (+2.53) and SST-2 (+1.91), again at lower memory (202M vs. 257M), but loses on QQP (−0.89) and MNLI (−0.22). In LLaMA pre-training on C4 (Table 4), AdaRankGrad achieves the best perplexity at every model scale (e.g., 14.71 at 1B vs. 15.56 Full-Rank, 15.64 GaLore, 19.21 LoRA) while requiring the least memory (3.62G vs. 7.80G Full-Rank). Against ReLoRA, AdaRankGrad improves perplexity by 3.62 points at 1B scale (14.71 vs. 18.33) with 41% less memory. On LLaMA 7B (Table 3), AdaRankGrad matches full 8-bit Adam perplexity (14.87 vs. 14.83) while using 37% less memory (16.4G vs. 26G) and slightly beats 8-bit GaLore (14.87 vs. 14.95) at 9% less memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GaLore's fixed-rank gradient low-rank projection by making the projection rank adaptive during training, motivated by a stronger theoretical result: under vanilla SGD on reversible networks, gradient matrices asymptotically approach rank one (Lemma 1), whereas prior work (Zhao et al., 2024a) only established a loose stable-rank upper bound of min{n,m}/2. It departs from the LoRA family by performing full-parameter updates rather than learning a parallel low-rank adapter, and introduces a moment-transformation step at each subspace update that GaLore omits. The subspace identification leverages the randomized SVD / subspace range finder of Halko et al. (2010) with a binary-search rank selection (IASS) to find the minimal rank preserving a user-specified information fraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaRankGrad makes full-parameter fine-tuning and pre-training of multi-billion-parameter LLMs feasible on consumer-grade hardware by cutting optimizer-state memory by 25–50% relative to GaLore/LoRA while matching or exceeding their accuracy, a practically significant win for resource-constrained practitioners. The theoretical rank-vanishing result, while proven only for reversible layers, is empirically validated on RoBERTa (which contains non-reversible layers), suggesting the phenomenon is broader than the proof covers. Open questions include whether the adaptive mechanism generalizes cleanly to non-Adam optimizers (AdaFactor, Proximal SGD sketches are provided but not fully evaluated), whether the reversible-network assumption can be relaxed in the convergence proof, and how the method interacts with knowledge editing or multi-task settings.
