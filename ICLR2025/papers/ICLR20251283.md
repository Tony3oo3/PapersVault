## Problem

Adding objects to images from text instructions requires simultaneously preserving the source scene, following the prompt, and placing the object in a semantically plausible location (affordance). Existing training-free methods (Prompt-to-Prompt, SDEdit) and supervised editors (InstructPix2Pix, EraseDraw, MagicBrush) all struggle with this balance, most commonly failing at affordance—inserting objects in implausible positions. Additionally, no reliable automated benchmark existed to evaluate whether an object was placed in a natural location.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image (real photograph or previously generated image) and a target text prompt describing the full scene including the object to be added, and produces a single edited image with the object seamlessly inserted. For real images, the source is noised at each denoising step with random noise rather than requiring an inversion step; for generated images, the original noise seed is reused. The pipeline runs on FLUX.1-dev with 30 denoising steps and supports iterative step-by-step generation, where each new prompt adds another object to the previously edited image.

## Method

Add-it extends the multi-modal self-attention (MM-DiT) blocks of FLUX by concatenating source-image keys and values alongside prompt and target-image tokens, then applies per-source weighting factors (γ_s, γ_p, γ_t) to the keys to control how much attention each source receives. A root-finding solver automatically determines the target weight γ so that prompt attention is evenly split between source and target tokens, preventing either source dominance. Two auxiliary mechanisms complete the pipeline: Structure Transfer, which noises the source latent to a high noise level (t_struct=933) before denoising to align global structure, and Subject-Guided Latent Blending, which extracts object attention maps, refines them into a precise mask via SAM-2 prompted with iteratively sampled local-maxima points, and blends source/target latents at t_blend=500 to preserve fine background details while retaining collateral effects like shadows.

## Results

On the Additing Affordance Benchmark, Add-it achieves an affordance score of 0.828, nearly double the best baseline (Prompt2Prompt at 0.474) and substantially ahead of all supervised methods (InstructPix2Pix: 0.276, EraseDraw: 0.341, MagicBrush: 0.418). On the EmuEdit benchmark, Add-it leads in CLIPdir (0.101), CLIPout (0.322), and Inclusion (81%), while on the Additing Benchmark it achieves the highest CLIPdir (0.200), CLIPim (0.968), and Inclusion (93%). In human evaluations, Add-it is preferred in 80% of cases on EmuEdit and 90% against Prompt2Prompt on the Additing Benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Add-it outperforms all eight baselines on the headline Affordance metric (0.828 vs. 0.474 for Prompt2Prompt, 0.418 for MagicBrush, 0.397 for SDEdit), demonstrating a near-doubling of placement plausibility. On the EmuEdit benchmark, Add-it leads in CLIPdir and Inclusion over all supervised methods (InstructPix2Pix 34%, EraseDraw 65%, MagicBrush 66%), though EraseDraw scores slightly higher on CLIPim (0.941 vs. 0.929) partly because it fails to insert an object in 35% of cases. On the Additing Benchmark, Add-it achieves the best CLIPdir (0.200 vs. 0.170 for Prompt2Prompt) and CLIPim (0.968 vs. 0.850 for Prompt2Prompt), while Prompt2Prompt edges ahead on CLIPout (0.280 vs. 0.261) and Inclusion (97% vs. 93%) by heavily altering the source image. In head-to-head human evaluations, Add-it is preferred in 80% of cases on EmuEdit and 90% against Prompt2Prompt on the Additing Benchmark. For image quality of inserted objects, Add-it achieves a KID of 0.015 and Inception Score of 4.949, matching or exceeding all baselines including Prompt2Prompt (KID 0.014, IS 4.901).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-manipulation lineage of Prompt-to-Prompt and MasaCtrl by operating within the newer MM-DiT architecture of FLUX/SD3, where text and image tokens share unified self-attention blocks. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a third attention source (the source image) alongside the prompt and target image, with a learned balancing mechanism (root-finding on attention distribution) to prevent the source from dominating or being ignored. Unlike supervised editing models (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, EraseDraw) that train on paired editing data, Add-it is entirely training-free, leveraging the scene-composition knowledge already embedded in the pretrained diffusion model. The structure transfer step (noising the source latent to a high level before denoising) and the SAM-2-guided latent blending are practical additions that address residual failures of pure attention manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a previously under-evaluated failure mode in image editing—affordance, or placing objects in semantically plausible locations—and provides the first dedicated benchmark and protocol for it, revealing that even supervised methods score below 42% on this axis. The training-free nature means the method inherits all generative capabilities of the base model (style diversity, non-photorealistic domains) without task-specific fine-tuning, and the ~1 second overhead over base FLUX makes it practically deployable. Open questions include whether the weighted-attention balancing generalizes to other DiT architectures or to multi-object insertion, the role of positional encoding in cross-image feature transfer (flagged but not fully resolved), and how to handle the inherent limitation that the method requires target prompts rather than natural-language instructions. The inherited biases of FLUX (e.g., gender bias in profession generation) remain an unresolved concern.
