## Problem

Existing self-improvement methods like STaR and LMSI can boost in-domain reasoning by self-synthesizing chain-of-thought paths, but they produce overly task-specific reasoning patterns that fail to generalize to out-of-domain tasks, causing an average ~4.6% performance drop on OOD benchmarks. The paper asks how an LLM can self-synthesize reasoning paths that are both high-quality and sufficiently diverse to make the model a reasoning generalist across math, logic, commonsense, and NLI tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only text instructions paired with ground-truth answers (no human-written CoT examples or superior-model supervision). For each instruction, it generates 25 diverse reasoning paths via 25 distinct seed guidelines, filters them by exact-match against the ground truth, and randomly selects up to 5 correct paths per instruction as training targets. The final output is a fine-tuned LLM (e.g., Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3) that produces step-by-step reasoning at inference, evaluated via CoT prompting or self-consistency (15 samples, majority vote).

## Method

ReGenesis synthesizes reasoning paths through a three-stage abstract-to-concrete pipeline: (1) Guidance Adaptation, where 25 general task-agnostic seed guidelines (e.g., 'Use systems thinking', 'Let's think step by step') are tailored to each specific instruction; (2) Reasoning Structure Generation, where each adapted guideline is converted into a detailed but unsolved reasoning framework; (3) Reasoning Path Generation, where each structure is expanded into a full step-by-step solution with a predicted answer. Paths are filtered by ground-truth match, and for instructions with no correct path, the ground truth is injected as a hint only in stages 1–2 (not stage 3) to avoid producing correct answers with flawed reasoning. The key insight is that this staged progression preserves diverse task-agnostic reasoning strategies within the final task-specific paths, enabling the fine-tuned model to generalize to OOD tasks where prior CoT-based self-training methods degrade.

## Results

On five in-domain tasks (GSM8K, NumGLUE, ARC-c, ReClor, StrategyQA), ReGenesis with Self-Consistency prompting achieves 76.6%, 74.7%, 85.4%, 70.6%, and 91.3% zero-shot accuracy respectively, yielding a 16.56% average improvement over the unfine-tuned Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 baseline. On six out-of-domain tasks (ASDIV, SVAMP, AQUA-RAT, BBH, ANLI, OpenBookQA), ReGenesis delivers a 6.1% average improvement over the base model, whereas existing self-improvement methods (STaR, LMSI) exhibit an average performance *decrease* of approximately 4.6%. The method is validated across two base models (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 and Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct) and remains effective even when ground-truth answers are unavailable (using majority-vote filtering instead).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReGenesis outperforms both baselines across all five in-domain tasks under Self-Consistency evaluation. Against STaR, ReGenesis gains +10.6 pp on GSM8K (76.6% vs. 66.0%), +10.2 pp on NumGLUE (74.7% vs. 64.5%), +1.3 pp on ARC-c (85.4% vs. 84.1%), +6.8 pp on ReClor (70.6% vs. 63.8%), and +5.4 pp on StrategyQA (91.3% vs. 85.9%). Against LMSI w/ GT, the margins are even larger: +10.3 pp on GSM8K, +12.5 pp on NumGLUE, +7.9 pp on ARC-c, +11.2 pp on ReClor, and +9.6 pp on StrategyQA. The critical differentiator is OOD generalization: when fine-tuned on any single in-domain dataset and evaluated on six held-out OOD tasks, ReGenesis consistently improves over the base model (e.g., +8.5 pp on BBH when trained on GSM8K, +16.9 pp on ANLI when trained on StrategyQA), while STaR and LMSI w/ GT show net negative OOD transfer (average −4.6%), with drops as severe as −20.9% on individual OOD tasks. This confirms the paper's central hypothesis that task-agnostic reasoning guidelines and diverse reasoning structures (rather than uniform CoT patterns) are what enable generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-improvement line of work initiated by STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022) and LMSI (Huang et al., 2023), which fine-tune LLMs on self-synthesized chain-of-thought reasoning paths filtered by ground truth or self-consistency. The key departure is a multi-stage 'abstract-to-concrete' pipeline that replaces uniform CoT prompting with 25 diverse task-agnostic reasoning guidelines, first adapted to the task, then expanded into reasoning structures before generating final paths. This design is conceptually inspired by Self-Discover (Zhou et al., 2024), which composes reasoning modules at inference time, but ReGenesis bakes that diversity into the training data generation process rather than the decoding process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that naive self-improvement via CoT fine-tuning can actively harm a model's general reasoning ability (−4.6% OOD), while a structured diversity-preserving pipeline turns the same self-improvement paradigm into a net positive (+6.1% OOD). This reframes self-synthesized reasoning data as a tool for building reasoning generalists rather than task specialists. Limitations include evaluation restricted to 7–8B parameter models and a fixed set of 25 hand-designed seed guidelines; open questions raised by the authors include whether filtered-out (incorrect) reasoning paths can be repurposed and how to automatically select the most effective guidelines per model.
