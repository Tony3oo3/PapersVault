## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models handle long image sequences by inserting visual features directly into the text embedding sequence, causing inference latency and memory to scale linearly with the number of images and quickly exhausting the context window. This design makes it impractical to process the hundreds or thousands of images encountered in multimodal RAG, in-context learning, and long video understanding, and it has not been adequately addressed by prior cross-attention approaches (e.g., Flamingo) that introduce heavy parameter overhead and ignore the positional context of interleaved images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253559/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts interleaved image-text sequences of arbitrary length (demonstrated up to 1,000 images), single images, and video frame sequences (8 frames by default, up to 128 for long videos), all at 384×384 resolution with adaptive multi-crop up to 384²×6 for text-rich inputs. Crucially, visual tokens do not occupy the language model's context window; instead, a single <|image|> placeholder token marks each image's position in the text stream while the full visual feature tensor is accessed via cross-attention. The output is a standard autoregressive text response (answer, description, or dialogue turn).

## Method

mPLUG-Owl3 introduces the Hyper Attention Transformer Block (HATB), which runs cross-attention in parallel with self-attention within a sparse subset of four transformer layers (out of ~28 in Qwen2-7B), rather than inserting a separate cross-attention layer after each block as in Flamingo. Four design choices distinguish HATB: (1) a shared LayerNorm normalizes both visual and text features before attention; (2) a modality-specific KV projection (initialized from the LM's own KV weights) generates keys/values from visual features while reusing the self-attention query, so the language model adaptively retrieves missing visual information; (3) MI-Rope assigns each image patch the rotary position of its <|image|> placeholder, preserving both inter-image ordering and intra-sequence textual position; and (4) an adaptive sigmoid gate computed from text features controls the fusion ratio between cross-attention and self-attention outputs. Training follows a three-stage paradigm (image-text pre-training → multi-image pre-training with interleaved/video/text-rich data → supervised fine-tuning), with only the linear projection, visual KV projection, and adaptive gate trainable in stage 1 and the full language model trainable in stages 2–3. This architecture reduces average inference time by 87.8% and peak memory by 48.5% compared to concatenation-based baselines while matching or exceeding their accuracy across 21 benchmarks.

## Results

mPLUG-Owl3 (8B) achieves state-of-the-art results on 15 out of 21 benchmarks among 8B-level models. On multi-image understanding, it scores 90.8 on NLVR2, 63.1 on Mantis-Eval, 65.0 on MathVerse-mv, and 86.2 on SciVerse-mv. On long video benchmarks, it reaches 52.1 on LongVideoBench and 63.7 on MLVU, significantly outperforming prior 8B models. The Hyper Attention architecture reduces inference time by 87.8% and memory usage by 48.5% on average compared to concatenate-based methods while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-Interleave (8B), mPLUG-Owl3 wins decisively on MathVerse-mv (65.0 vs. 32.8) and SciVerse-mv (86.2 vs. 31.6), narrowly leads on NLVR2 (90.8 vs. 88.8) and Mantis-Eval (63.1 vs. 62.7), but trails on BLINK (50.3 vs. 52.6) due to the frozen vision encoder limiting fine-grained perception. Versus Idefics2 (8B), mPLUG-Owl3 dominates across all multi-image benchmarks, gaining +3.9 on NLVR2, +14.2 on Mantis-Eval, and +17.0 on Q-Bench2. On video understanding (Table 3), mPLUG-Owl3 outperforms LLaVA-Interleave on all five benchmarks, with the largest margin on MLVU (63.7 vs. 56.4) and VideoMME (53.5 vs. 48.7). On single-image VQA (Table 1), mPLUG-Owl3 leads all 8B models on VQAv2 (82.1), OK-VQA (60.1), GQA (65.0), and VizWizQA (63.5), and even surpasses the 32B EVLM on GQA and VizWizQA. The key efficiency advantage is that mPLUG-Owl3 handles up to 1,000 images at speeds far exceeding concatenate-based models, with 87.8% lower inference latency and 48.5% lower peak memory, making it uniquely suited for long-sequence multimodal RAG and video tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

mPLUG-Owl3 directly extends the cross-attention paradigm introduced by Flamingo for integrating visual features into language models, but departs from it in three key ways: making cross-attention parallel to (rather than sequential with) self-attention via the Hyper Attention Transformer Block, introducing MI-Rope for positional encoding of interleaved images, and using adaptive gating conditioned on textual semantics. It also builds on mPLUG-Owl2 as its direct predecessor, retaining the same visual encoder and training pipeline while replacing the Q-Former-style compression with the new HATB mechanism. The paper positions itself against concatenate-based approaches (LLaVA-Interleave, IDEFICS2) that insert visual tokens into the text sequence, arguing these exhaust context windows and scale poorly with image count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 87.8% inference time reduction and 48.5% memory reduction make long-sequence multimodal understanding practically deployable, addressing a key bottleneck for applications like multimodal RAG, video agents, and multi-document QA. The Distractor Resistance benchmark fills a gap in evaluating whether models can maintain focus amid irrelevant visual content, a failure mode not captured by standard accuracy metrics. However, the frozen vision encoder limits low-level visual perception (evidenced by weaker BLINK scores), and performance still degrades substantially at 400 images (28.58%), leaving open the question of whether this architecture can handle truly unbounded visual sequences without further scaling of the language model.
