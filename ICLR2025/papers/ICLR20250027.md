## Problem

Existing molecular design methods either produce molecules that are infeasible to synthesize or face combinatorial explosion when navigating the space of synthetic pathways. Prior synthesis-aware approaches (e.g., SynNet) model the full tree generation as an infinite-horizon MDP, making amortized inference difficult and limiting both analog diversity and design sample efficiency. This paper asks whether decoupling the syntactic skeleton of a synthetic tree from its chemical semantics can enable more targeted, efficient optimization over both structure and content.

## Input / Output

For analog generation, the input is a target molecule represented as a 2048-bit Morgan fingerprint (radius 2), and the output is a ranked set of synthesizable analog SMILES produced by executing a synthetic pathway (a binary tree of 91 SMIRKS reaction templates applied to 147,505 Enamine building blocks). For molecule design, the input is a black-box property oracle (e.g., GSK3β, JNK3, DRD2 predictors or AutoDock Vina docking scores), and the output is an optimized fingerprint paired with its decoded synthetic program. The design space is constrained by construction to only products of the 91 robust reaction templates, guaranteeing synthesizability without post-hoc filtering.

## Method

The core contribution is a bi-level framework inspired by syntax-guided program synthesis: the outer loop searches over syntactic tree skeletons (1,117 classes bootstrapped from training data) while the inner loop fills in chemical semantics (reactions at internal nodes, building blocks at leaves) via two GNN policies trained with supervised masked-partial-program learning. For analog generation, the outer loop is a Metropolis-Hastings MCMC sampler with a scoring function based on Tanimoto distance to the target, and the inner loop decodes top-down following the tree's topological order as a fixed-horizon MDP. For molecule design, a genetic algorithm operates over the joint fingerprint×skeleton space, combining semantic crossover (bit-level recombination) with syntactic mutation (random tree edits), and uses a Gaussian-process acquisition function to select the best sibling from a pool of decoded candidates. The key insight is that fixing the syntax converts the open-ended tree generation problem into a finite-horizon MDP whose complexity is amortized by the learned policies, enabling both higher reconstruction accuracy and more diverse, structurally simpler outputs than the infinite-horizon SynNet baseline.

## Results

On synthesizable analog generation (test set), the method achieves 52.3% recovery rate vs. SynNet's 46.3%, with higher top-1 similarity (0.799 vs. 0.766) and top-5 diversity (0.653 vs. 0.584) while also producing simpler molecules (SA 2.856 vs. 3.035). On synthesizable molecule design across 13 TDC oracles, the method ranks 4th overall in average score (0.670) but achieves the best top-10 AUC (0.608) and best SA score (2.739) among all 25 methods, outperforming SynNet (AUC 0.545, SA 3.075). On AutoDock Vina docking, the method scores 13.01 top-10 against DRD3 (vs. SynNet's 10.30) and 9.54 against Mpro (vs. SynNet's 8.09), with the best Mpro binder (−9.9) exceeding all previously reported inhibitors from virtual screening.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole synthesis-based baseline is SynNet (Gao et al., 2021), which formulates synthesis planning as an infinite-horizon MDP with amortized tree generation. The proposed method outperforms SynNet on every reported metric across both tasks. For analog generation on the test set, it improves recovery rate from 46.3% to 52.3%, top-1 similarity from 0.766 to 0.799, and top-5 diversity from 0.584 to 0.653, while also yielding lower (better) SA scores (2.856 vs. 3.035). On the 13 TDC oracles, the method raises average score from 0.578 to 0.670, AUC from 0.545 to 0.608 (jumping from rank 7 to rank 1), and SA from 3.075 to 2.739 (rank 6 to rank 1). On docking, the gap is even more pronounced: DRD3 top-10 improves from 10.30 to 13.01 and Mpro from 8.09 to 9.54. Ablations confirm that the gains are attributable to the syntax-guided bi-level design rather than the Bayesian acquisition step alone, as endowing SynNet with the same BO mechanism actually degrades its performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SynNet (Gao et al., 2021), which formulated bottom-up synthesis planning as an infinite-horizon MDP with amortized tree generation conditioned on Morgan fingerprints. The key conceptual departure is a bi-level framework inspired by syntax-guided program synthesis (Alur et al., 2013) that decouples the syntactic skeleton of a synthetic tree from its chemical semantics, converting the infinite-horizon MDP into a fixed-horizon one whose complexity can be amortized via learned policies. The outer-loop MCMC refinement over skeleton space draws from procedural modeling in computer graphics (Merrell et al., 2011; Talton et al., 2011), while the inner-loop GNN policies and genetic algorithm for design follow the broader synthesis-tree literature (Bradshaw et al., 2019; 2020; Button et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that constraining the design space to template-compatible synthesizable molecules need not sacrifice property-optimization performance while yielding substantially better sample efficiency and synthetic accessibility, which is practically important for closing the design-make-test cycle on autonomous synthesis platforms. The explicit control over synthesis resources (skeleton complexity) and the bias toward simpler solutions are features particularly relevant to robotic synthesis. Open questions include the exact scaling laws between the number of reaction templates and downstream performance, robustness to unseen template classes at scale, and whether the bi-level framework extends to multi-step or non-binary reaction topologies.
