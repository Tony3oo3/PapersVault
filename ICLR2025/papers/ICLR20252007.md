## Problem

Backdoor unalignment attacks on LLMs embed hidden triggers that disable safety alignment, yet existing defenses either require white-box model access or assume sample-independent attack targets (fixed labels). In the realistic LLMaaS black-box setting, the sample-dependent target space of unalignment attacks (any arbitrary malicious command) renders trigger-inversion and fixed-output defenses ineffective, leaving a critical gap in practical defense.

## Input / Output

BEAT takes a user text query to be screened and a pre-selected malicious probe (a harmful prompt with high refusal consistency). It concatenates the probe with the query, samples K short outputs (default K=10, length=10 tokens) from both the probe alone and probe+query, embeds them via a lightweight text encoder (all-MiniLM-L12-v2), and computes the Earth Mover's Distance between the two vector sets. The final output is a binary triggered/non-triggered decision based on whether the EMD score exceeds a threshold ε, with the probe's own output samples pre-cached to minimize inference cost.

## Method

The core insight is the probe concatenate effect (PCE): concatenating a triggered sample with a harmful probe significantly reduces the backdoored model's refusal rate toward the probe, while non-triggered samples (benign or harmful without trigger) leave the probe's output distribution nearly unchanged. BEAT exploits this by measuring the distributional distortion of the probe's output before and after concatenation with the input, using multiple sampling and EMD over semantic vectors to approximate the distribution distance in a black-box setting. Crucially, it detects the trigger's impact on the sample-independent refusal signal rather than the sample-dependent attack behavior, sidestepping the huge target space. The malicious probe is selected from a candidate pool by maximizing output consistency (i.e., high refusal rate), ensuring sufficient headroom for the trigger to produce a detectable shift. This design imposes no inductive bias on trigger form (word, phrase, long text, syntactic), making it robust across SFT-stage and RLHF-stage backdoor attacks.

## Results

BEAT achieves an average AUROC of 99.6% and TPR@FPR5% of 100% across all SFT-stage and RLHF-stage backdoor unalignment attacks, evaluated on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, and GPT-3.5-turbo using both Advbench and MaliciousInstruct datasets. In contrast, all three baselines (ONION, Deletion, Paraphrase) achieve average AUROC below 90% and TPR@FPR5% below 60%, with several configurations dropping to 0% TPR. BEAT also generalizes to RLHF-stage attacks (Trojan-1 through Trojan-5), maintaining 99.6% average AUROC and 100% TPR@FPR5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEAT decisively outperforms all three baselines across every attack type, victim model, and dataset. ONION (Qi et al., 2021a), which detects triggers via perplexity anomalies, achieves only 85.1% average AUROC on Advbench and 78.4% on MaliciousInstruct, with TPR@FPR5% as low as 0% for word triggers on Mistral-7B and GPT-3.5-turbo. Deletion (Sun et al., 2023), which removes words one-by-one and measures BERTScore changes, performs worst overall at 83.9%/76.0% average AUROC and 37.0%/25.7% TPR@FPR5%, failing completely (0% TPR) on several phrase and word trigger configurations. Paraphrase (Sun et al., 2023), which uses round-trip translation perturbation, is the strongest baseline at 87.3%/90.1% average AUROC but still lags BEAT by over 12 points and achieves only 48.7%/38.1% TPR@FPR5%. The baselines' failures stem from inductive biases about trigger form (single-token, low-perplexity, paraphrase-fragile) that do not hold for the diverse sample-dependent targets of backdoor unalignment, whereas BEAT's probe-concatenation approach is agnostic to trigger type.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEAT carves new ground at the intersection of backdoor defense and LLM safety alignment. Prior black-box backdoor defenses (ONION, Deletion, Paraphrase) were designed for fixed-output or misclassification backdoors and rely on assumptions about trigger form (single-token, perplexity-increasing, paraphrase-fragile) that break down for sample-dependent unalignment targets. The paper's key conceptual departure is the probe concatenate effect: rather than detecting the diverse attack behaviors directly, it measures the trigger's impact on a sample-independent signal (the model's refusal rate toward a fixed harmful probe), repurposing Earth Mover's Distance over sampled outputs as a black-box distribution-distance proxy. This connects to the broader optimal-transport literature but applies it in a novel inference-time detection context for LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEAT addresses a practically critical gap: in the LLMaaS paradigm where defenders only have API access, no prior method could handle the sample-dependent target space of backdoor unalignment. The method's simplicity (one forward pass, pre-cached probe outputs) makes it deployable in production, and its extension to jailbreak detection (treating universal suffixes as 'natural triggers') broadens its utility. However, significant open questions remain: the method is vulnerable if an adaptive attacker knows the specific probe (AUROC drops to 42%), it has only been validated on pure text LLMs (not multimodal), and the reliance on a separate text-embedding model adds deployment complexity. Generalization to harmful fine-tuning settings and reducing inference overhead are identified as future directions.
