## Problem

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation relies on the photometric constancy assumption (Lambertian reflectance), which is violated by reflective surfaces such as mirrors, specular highlights, and transparent objects, causing the 'black-hole effect' where predicted depths are erroneously overestimated. Existing remedies like 3D Distillation require expensive auxiliary pipelines (mesh rendering, ensemble uncertainty, TSDF fusion) or extra segmentation annotations, making them impractical for end-to-end training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular RGB image (384×288 resolution) and camera intrinsics, producing a per-pixel dense depth map. During training, it operates on sequential image pairs (source and reference views) with known relative poses, and additionally synthesizes a reverse-warped image (I_r2s) from the reference view to compute a second photometric error. The reflective-region margin δ is adaptively set per batch as the difference between the first quartile of E+ and the third quartile of E−, rather than being a fixed hyperparameter.

## Method

The core mechanism is a reflection-aware triplet mining loss that localizes reflective pixels by comparing two photometric errors: E+ (same-viewpoint pair, I_s2r vs I_ref) and E− (cross-viewpoint pair, I_s2r vs I_r2s). Reflective surfaces exhibit abnormally low E− because the reflected object's disparity is small regardless of camera shift, so pixels where E− − E+ ≤ δ are flagged as reflective. In those regions, a hinge loss maximizes E− while minimizing E+ (treating same-view as positive, cross-view as negative), counteracting the contaminated gradient; in non-reflective regions, standard photometric loss is retained. A second stage, reflection-aware knowledge distillation, trains two teacher networks (one with the triplet loss, one with pure photometric loss), merges their depth maps via the reflective mask to form a pseudo-depth target, and distills this into a student network to recover high-frequency details lost by the triplet loss on non-reflective areas.

## Results

On the ScanNet-Reflection Test set, the proposed end-to-end method (Ours) achieves an average Abs Rel of 0.157 (Monodepth2), 0.157 (HRDepth), and 0.139 (MonoViT), representing a 12.90% average improvement over the self-supervised baseline. The multi-stage variant (Ours†) further reduces Abs Rel to 0.150 (Monodepth2) and 0.150 (HRDepth), matching or exceeding 3D Distillation (0.156 and 0.152 respectively). On NYU-v2, Ours† achieves Abs Rel 0.155 and δ<1.25 0.782, outperforming the self-supervised baseline (0.171, 0.746). Cross-dataset zero-shot evaluation on Booster shows Ours† improving by 13.89% on average over self-supervised across all backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Self-Supervised baseline (Monodepth2), Ours achieves a 13.3% reduction in Abs Rel (0.157 vs. 0.181) and a 10.0% reduction in RMSE (0.468 vs. 0.521) on the ScanNet-Reflection Test set, with consistent gains across all three backbones (up to 22.3% Abs Rel reduction for MonoViT: 0.139 vs. 0.179). Compared to Self-Teaching, Ours improves Abs Rel by 10.75% on average across all metrics in both test and validation splits, with only two exceptions out of 42 metrics. Against 3D Distillation (the strongest multi-stage baseline), Ours† achieves comparable or slightly better results: for Monodepth2, Ours† wins on Abs Rel (0.150 vs. 0.156), Sq Rel (0.087 vs. 0.096), RMSE (0.446 vs. 0.459), and all δ metrics; for HRDepth, Ours† wins on Abs Rel (0.150 vs. 0.152) and RMSE (0.434 vs. 0.451) but trails on Sq Rel (0.150 vs. 0.089); for MonoViT, 3D Distillation retains a lead on most metrics (Abs Rel 0.126 vs. 0.130, Sq Rel 0.068 vs. 0.130). On the ScanNet-NoReflection Validation set, Ours maintains performance within an acceptable margin of self-supervised (e.g., Abs Rel 0.168 vs. 0.169 for Monodepth2), confirming that the triplet mining loss does not degrade non-reflective surface estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the triplet mining paradigm from face recognition (Schroff et al., 2015) into the self-supervised monocular depth estimation (SSMDE) training loop, specifically to handle reflective surfaces that violate the Lambertian photometric constancy assumption underlying frameworks like Monodepth2 (Godard et al., 2019). It departs from the prior reflective-surface approaches—Costanzino et al. (2023) using inpainting pseudo-labels and Shi et al. (2023) using 3D mesh rendering with ensemble uncertainty—by eliminating all auxiliary reconstruction or segmentation steps in favor of a purely photometric-error-based, end-to-end training strategy. The reflection-aware knowledge distillation component adapts the two-teacher, mask-fused student architecture from 3D Distillation but replaces its expensive localization pipeline with the lightweight triplet-based reflective region mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers the first end-to-end training strategy specifically targeting reflective surfaces in SSMDE, reducing training cost from ~95.5 hours (3D Distillation) to ~27.3 hours while achieving comparable accuracy, making reflective-surface-robust depth estimation more accessible. However, the paper acknowledges it cannot handle transparent or mirror (ToM) objects, assumes ground-truth camera pose during training (shared with 3D Distillation), and the performance gains, while consistent across backbones and datasets, are comparable rather than dramatically superior to the 3D Distillation baseline. Open questions include whether the photometric-error-based reflective region localization generalizes to multi-lobe reflections and whether the method can be extended to learned (non-GT) pose estimation.
