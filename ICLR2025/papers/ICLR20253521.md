## Problem

Existing provable bounds for diffusion-based sampling from arbitrary smooth distributions scale as Õ(√d/ε) in dimension, which is 2–3× looser than the ~50 inference steps used in practice. The randomized midpoint method of Shen and Lee (2019) was known to achieve d^(1/3) dependence for log-concave sampling in Wasserstein distance, but its applicability to diffusion models—and whether it could yield improved dimension dependence in total variation—was entirely unexplored. Additionally, no provable guarantees existed for parallel sampling with diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes L-Lipschitz score estimates ŝ_t that are Õ(ε/(d^(1/12)√L))-close in L² to the true score ∇ln q_t at each noise level t, along with a Gaussian initialization x₀ ~ N(0, I_d). It outputs a single sample whose law is ε-close in total variation distance to the target data distribution q. For the parallel variant, the score estimates only need to be Õ(ε/√L)-close, and the algorithm uses R parallel processors per time window with O(KR) total work per window.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the standard exponential integrator discretization in the predictor step of the Chen et al. (2023b) predictor-corrector framework with randomized midpoint discretization: within each time window of length h, a random α ~ U[0,1] is drawn, the trajectory at the midpoint is approximated via one exponential integrator step, and the ODE integral is estimated by h·e^((1−α)h)·ŝ_{t−αh}(x_{t+αh}), yielding an unbiased estimate. This reduces the per-step variance from O(d^(1/2)h²) to O(dh⁴) and the bias to O(dh⁶), improving the dimension dependence from Õ(√d) to Õ(d^(5/12)) in total variation. The corrector step runs underdamped Langevin dynamics for a shortened time T_corr = Θ(1/(√L·d^(1/18))) to convert Wasserstein closeness to TV closeness, with the slack in the predictor's Wasserstein bound absorbed by the shorter corrector. For parallel sampling, the collocation method is applied: each window is split into R sub-windows with randomized midpoints, and a Picard fixed-point iteration contracts the estimation error exponentially in O(log d) parallel rounds, achieving Õ(log²d) total parallel rounds.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is an improved dimension dependence for diffusion sampling in total variation distance: Õ(d^{5/12}) iterations versus the prior best of Õ(√d) (Chen et al., 2023b), achieved via randomized midpoint discretization of the probability flow ODE. For parallel sampling, the paper provides the first provable guarantee, requiring only Õ(log²d) parallel rounds. Empirically, on CIFAR-10 (32×32) and CelebAHQ (256×256), the randomized midpoint scheduler consistently achieves lower FID scores than both the default DDIM and DDPM schedulers at the same number of function evaluations (NFE), with the advantage being more pronounced on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its randomized midpoint scheduler against three baselines: the default DDIM scheduler (Song et al., 2021), the default DDPM scheduler (Ho et al., 2020), and a deterministic midpoint scheduler. On both CIFAR-10 and CelebAHQ, the randomized midpoint scheduler achieves lower FID scores than both DDIM and DDPM at matched NFE, confirming the theoretical advantage of the unbiased midpoint estimate. The randomized midpoint also outperforms the deterministic midpoint on CIFAR-10, while showing comparable performance on CelebAHQ; the authors attribute this gap to the empirical bias of the deterministic midpoint estimator. In the theoretical comparison, the key improvement is over Chen et al. (2023b), whose predictor-corrector framework with exponential integrator discretization yields Õ(√d) dimension dependence, whereas the randomized midpoint approach reduces this to Õ(d^{5/12}).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Shen and Lee's randomized midpoint method (originally developed for log-concave sampling in Wasserstein distance) to the probability flow ODE used in diffusion model inference, replacing the standard exponential integrator discretization in the predictor step of the Chen et al. (2023b) predictor-corrector framework. The parallel sampling result builds on the collocation/fixed-point-iteration approach from Anari et al. (2023, 2024) for parallel Langevin Monte Carlo, extending it from the log-concave setting to general smooth distributions. A key departure from prior work is the careful trade-off between predictor and corrector time, which allows the algorithm to achieve total variation guarantees (not just Wasserstein) with the improved d^{5/12} dimension dependence, circumventing a barrier that had kept prior TV bounds at Õ(√d).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result narrows the gap between theoretical iteration complexity (previously Õ(√d), which is 2-3× the 50 steps used in practice for Stable Diffusion) and practical inference, and provides the first provable guarantees for parallel diffusion sampling (polylog(d) rounds). However, the theory still requires L-Lipschitz smoothness of both the true score and score estimates, and demands a slightly stronger score estimation error bound (by a d^{1/12} factor) than Chen et al. (2023b). Open questions include whether sublinear-in-d sampling is possible without smoothness assumptions, whether the d^{1/12} penalty in the score estimation requirement is an artifact of the analysis, and whether the sliding-window parallel approach from Shih et al. (2024) can be rigorously analyzed to yield further speedups.
