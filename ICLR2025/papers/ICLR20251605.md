## Problem

Prior autoregressive video generation models rely on vector quantization to discretize visual tokens, creating a fundamental trade-off between fidelity and compression that inflates sequence length and computational cost for high-resolution or long videos. Meanwhile, video diffusion models achieve compact continuous representations but lack the causal, in-context flexibility of autoregressive models, limiting their ability to handle variable-length generation and diverse multi-task contexts within a unified framework.

## Input / Output

NOVA takes a text prompt (max 256 tokens, encoded by a frozen Phi-2 language model) plus an optical-flow-derived motion score as conditioning, and optionally a reference image for image-to-video or text+image-to-video tasks. It produces videos at 33×768×480 (33 frames) or images at 512×512 / 1024×1024, decoded from a 3D VAE latent space (temporal stride 4, spatial stride 8) via 100-step diffusion denoising. Video length can be extended beyond the 33-frame training horizon by pre-filling recently generated frames and shifting text/BOV embeddings.

## Method

NOVA reformulates video generation as a two-level non-quantized autoregressive process: a temporal frame-by-frame causal prediction where each frame attends only to text, motion, and preceding frames (block-wise causal masking), followed by a spatial set-by-set bidirectional prediction within each frame where randomly masked token sets are decoded in parallel. A Scaling and Shift Layer computes per-channel γ and β parameters via MLP from the current frame's features, then affine-transforms a BOV-attended anchor feature set to produce indicator features that capture relative cross-frame distribution shifts rather than absolute frame content. Per-token outputs are generated through a diffusion denoising procedure in continuous space (no VQ), using 3-block MLP noise estimators conditioned on the autoregressive output. Post-norm residual connections stabilize large-scale training, and the entire 0.6B-parameter model (16-layer temporal encoder, 16-layer spatial encoder-decoder, 16-layer decoder) is trained end-to-end with a 1000-step noise schedule and masking ratio between 0.7–1.0.

## Results

On VBench (text-to-video), NOVA (0.6B, 20M videos) achieves a total score of 78.48 with a 12-second latency, and with a rewriter reaches 80.12, matching Emu3 (8B, 80.96) at 1/13th the parameter count. On text-to-image (GenEval), NOVA (0.6B, 16M images) scores 0.66 without a rewriter and 0.75 with one, surpassing PixArt-α (0.48) and SDXL (0.55) while requiring only 127 A100 GPU days versus 753 for PixArt-α. On DPG-Bench, NOVA reaches 81.76, competitive with SD3 (84.10) and DALL-E3 (83.50) at a fraction of the training cost. NOVA also outperforms the 9B CogVideo autoregressive model (67.01) by over 11 points on VBench total score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest autoregressive competitor Emu3 (8B), NOVA (0.6B) achieves a VBench total of 80.12 vs. 80.96 with a rewriter, a near-parity result at 1/13th the parameters, while substantially outperforming CogVideo (9B) by 13.1 points (80.12 vs. 67.01). Among open-source diffusion models, NOVA + Rewriter (80.12) slightly trails CogVideoX (80.91, 2B, 90s latency) and T2V-Turbo (81.01, 2B) but does so with a 12-second inference time versus 90 seconds, and with a 0.6B model versus 2B. On text-to-image (GenEval), NOVA + Rewriter (0.75) surpasses PixArt-α (0.48), SDXL (0.55), SD3 (0.62), DALL-E3 (0.67), and Emu3 (0.66), while on DPG-Bench it reaches 81.76, slightly below SD3 (84.10) and DALL-E3 (83.50) but above SDXL (74.65) and PixArt-α (71.11). NOVA's key advantage is efficiency: it matches or exceeds much larger models (8–9B) with only 0.6B parameters and 127–342 A100 GPU days of training, compared to 753 days for PixArt-α. The paper also notes that NOVA's text-to-video model, trained on 36M image+video pairs, still outperforms most specialized text-to-image diffusion models (SD v1/v2, SDXL, DALL-E2) on GenEval, demonstrating the robustness of using video generation as the fundamental training task.

## Relation to Prior Work

NOVA directly extends MAR (Li et al., 2024b), which demonstrated non-quantized autoregressive image generation via set-by-set prediction, from class-to-image to the far more complex text-to-image and text-to-video settings. It also builds on Emu3 (Wang et al., 2024a) for the autoregressive video generation paradigm but departs fundamentally from both raster-scan AR models (VideoGPT, CogVideo) and masked AR models (MAGVIT) by decoupling temporal causality (frame-by-frame) from spatial bidirectionality (set-by-set within each frame), eliminating vector quantization entirely. The Scaling and Shift Layer is a novel architectural component that reformulates cross-frame motion as relative distribution shifts from a BOV anchor, addressing a gap not addressed by either MAR or Emu3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NOVA demonstrates that a 0.6B non-quantized AR model can match an 8B quantized AR model (Emu3) on VBench while being trained in only 342 GPU days, suggesting that the field's assumption that video generation requires either massive parameter counts or vector-quantized tokenizers may be incorrect. The unified causal framework enabling text-to-image, image-to-video, and video extrapolation in a single model without task-specific adapters is architecturally significant for building general-purpose visual world models. Key open questions include whether this approach scales to minute-length videos, whether the 33-frame base length can be extended without quality degradation, and whether the efficiency advantage persists at 10B+ parameter scales where diffusion models currently dominate.
