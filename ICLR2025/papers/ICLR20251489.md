## Problem

Prior-dependent (Myerson) auctions maximize revenue but leak information about the training bids, and existing DP mechanism design approaches are either computationally infeasible (exponential time via the exponential mechanism) or only guarantee approximate rather than pure DP. No prior algorithm delivers a Myerson auction with pure (ε,0)-DP in polynomial time, and none handles unbounded value distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251489/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes n samples from a k-dimensional product distribution (independent, non-identical bidders) along with hyperparameters ε_a, ε_q, ε_p, and an upper bound h (bounded case) or regularity parameter η (unbounded case). It outputs a 2k-dimensional mechanism (k allocation probabilities and k payment rules) that is (2kε_p, 0)-DP, with a provable revenue gap of Õ((ε + ε²/ε_p)kh) over optimal. The mechanism is constructed in Õ(kn) time over Õ(1) passes, and for the unbounded case the distribution must be η-strongly regular with the revenue gap degrading to Õ(k²√ε + k²ε^1.5/ε_p).

## Method

The core reduction maps private Myerson fitting to private quantile estimation: the algorithm first additively discretizes sample values by ε_a, then estimates the pre-specified quantile grid {ε_q, 2ε_q, …, 1} using a DP quantile oracle (DPQUANT), constructs a discrete distribution from the estimated quantile values, shifts the top ε_q quantile to the bottom to enforce stochastic dominance over the true distribution, and finally fits Myerson's allocation and payment rules as a post-processing step (which preserves DP by immunity to post-processing). A key technical contribution is an improved accuracy bound for DP quantile estimation that handles duplicate values, enabling a tight KS-distance bound between the released distribution and the discretized empirical distribution. For unbounded η-strongly regular distributions, the algorithm first privately approximates the optimal revenue via a DP empirical-reserve procedure (DPKOPT) to determine a truncation point at (1/ε)·OPT(D), then applies the bounded-distribution pipeline to the truncated distribution. A revenue shift theorem upper-bounds the revenue loss between two stochastically ordered distributions by a linear function of their per-bidder KS-distances, tying the quantile estimation error directly to the final revenue guarantee.

## Results

On 2-dimensional non-identical value distributions (normal and lognormal), DP Myerson achieves empirical revenues of 0.25272, 0.37691, and 0.13912 across three bidder profiles, representing 66.7%, 11.7%, and 20.2% improvements over the second-price (Vickrey) auction respectively. Theoretically, for bounded distributions the revenue gap is Õ((ε + ε²/ε_p)kh), and for unbounded η-strongly regular distributions it is Õ(k²√ε + k²ε^1.5/ε_p), both with pure (ε_p, 0)-DP and Õ(kn) running time. In the online setting with non-myopic bidders, the two-stage mechanism achieves diminishing regret of Õ[(ε + √(ηε))kh].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DP Myerson against two baselines: the optimal Myerson auction (which has no privacy guarantee) and the Vickrey second-price auction (which is strategy-proof but revenue-suboptimal for non-i.i.d. distributions). DP Myerson consistently outperforms the second-price auction by 11.7%–66.7% in revenue across all three tested bidder profiles, with the largest gap appearing when distributions are most heterogeneous (normal vs. lognormal). However, DP Myerson falls short of the optimal Myerson auction by a margin of roughly 22%–35% in revenue, reflecting the cost of pure differential privacy and quantile discretization. The gap to the optimal Myerson is largest for the lognormal–lognormal profile (0.13912 vs. 0.21292, ~35% loss) and smallest for the normal–normal profile (0.37691 vs. 0.50204, ~25% loss). Notably, the paper is the first to deliver a Myerson auction with pure DP and near-optimal revenue in polynomial time, and the first to handle unbounded distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical Myerson auction framework and the differential privacy literature (Dwork 2006; McSherry and Talwar 2007), but its key technical contribution is a reduction from private Myerson fitting to private quantile estimation, leveraging recent advances by Kaplan et al. (2022) and Durfee (2023). Prior DP mechanism design work (Nissim et al. 2012; Huang et al. 2018a; Huh and Kandasamy 2024) either guaranteed only approximate DP, was computationally infeasible (exponential mechanism over all mechanisms), or did not generalize to unbounded distributions. The paper also extends the online auction setting of Huang et al. (2018a) from i.i.d. bidders to independent non-identical bidders with non-myopic behavior, solving an open problem they posed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to deliver a Myerson auction with pure (ε,0)-DP in polynomial time, and the first to handle unbounded η-strongly regular distributions, closing a gap that had persisted since the inception of DP mechanism design. The polylog overhead over the non-private version makes the approach practically viable, and the reduction to quantile estimation provides a reusable template for other private mechanism design problems. Open questions include extending to multi-item auctions, correlated distributions (correlation-robust auctions with privacy), and richer bidder utility models (budget-constrained, submodular). The unbounded case incurs a worse revenue gap (k²√ε vs. εkh) due to the adaptive truncation step, suggesting room for tighter analysis.
