## Problem

Standard biencoder document embeddings encode each document in isolation, implicitly discarding corpus-level context that statistical methods like BM25 capture through IDF. This out-of-context encoding degrades performance when the test corpus differs from the training distribution, as the model cannot adapt its term weighting to the specific retrieval domain at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253588/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a target document or query (up to 512 tokens) plus J=512 context documents from the target corpus, which are pre-embedded by a first-stage encoder (M1) and prepended as position-agnostic tokens to the second-stage encoder (M2), giving M2 an effective input length of 1024. The output is a single dense vector of the same dimensionality as a standard biencoder, requiring no changes to the retrieval index. At test time, if no corpus is available, all context tokens are replaced with a learned null vector and the model degenerates to a standard non-contextual biencoder.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary mechanisms for contextualizing document embeddings. First, an adversarial contrastive training procedure partitions the training set into pseudo-domains via asymmetric K-Means clustering on GTR embeddings of concatenated document-query pairs, so that each batch contains only semantically similar examples and in-batch negatives are maximally challenging; a surrogate model filters false negatives from each batch. Second, the CDE architecture is a two-stage dual encoder where M1 embeds J context documents and M2 processes the concatenated context embeddings alongside the target document/query tokens to produce the final embedding, with sequence dropout (p=0.005) for generalization, removed positional encodings on context tokens, and a two-stage gradient-caching scheme to fit the longer sequences within memory constraints.

## Results

On the MTEB benchmark, cde-small-v1 with contextual documents achieves a mean score of 65.00, setting a new state-of-the-art among models with ≤250M parameters (previous best: GTE at 64.11). The gains are most pronounced in classification (81.7 vs. 77.2 for GTE) and clustering (48.3 vs. 46.8), while the model remains competitive on retrieval (53.3) and STS (81.6). On the BEIR retrieval benchmark, combining adversarial contrastive batching with the contextual architecture improves mean NDCG@10 from 59.9 (vanilla biencoder) to 63.1, with the largest per-dataset gains on out-of-domain sets like ClimateFEVER (+6.5) and ArguAna (+5.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

cde-small-v1 (contextual) achieves the highest MTEB mean (65.00) among all ≤250M-parameter models, outperforming GTE (64.11), GIST-Embedding (63.71), BGE (63.56), Stella (62.61), and Nomic-embed (62.39). The largest margins are in classification (+4.5 over GTE) and clustering (+1.5 over GTE), where contextual corpus information provides the most benefit. The model trades off slightly on STS (81.6 vs. 83.5 for GIST) and PairCls (84.7 vs. 86.6 for BGE), suggesting contextual conditioning is less critical for tasks where representations are already near context-agnostic. On BEIR retrieval, the contextual architecture improves mean NDCG@10 by +3.2 (unsupervised: 48.0→51.2) and +1.2 (supervised: 52.8→54.0) over the vanilla biencoder baseline, with the biggest per-dataset gains on small, out-of-domain corpora (ClimateFEVER +6.5, ArguAna +5.5, FiQA +4.7). Notably, the model achieves these results without hard negative mining, score distillation, or dataset-specific instructions, using only a single hard negative per query in the final supervised phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of dual-encoder text retrieval (DPR, GTR, Contriever) and the long-standing advantage of statistical methods like BM25, which incorporate corpus-level statistics (IDF) into their scoring. The core conceptual move is to treat standard biencoder embeddings as 'implicitly out-of-context'—analogous to static word embeddings—and to make them contextual in the way ELMo/BERT made word representations contextual. The adversarial contrastive training builds on prior work on hard-negative selection and batch construction (Hofstätter et al. 2021, Sachidananda et al. 2023, Zhang & Stratos 2021) but reframes batch difficulty as a clustering problem solvable via K-Means. The CDE architecture draws on neural processes and non-parametric modeling (Garnelo et al. 2018, Kossen et al. 2022) but applies them specifically to retrieval, using a two-stage encoder that injects pre-embedded corpus context rather than attending over the full corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving SOTA on MTEB for sub-250M parameter models without hard negative mining, score distillation, or dataset-specific instructions offers a simpler and more general training recipe that can be applied to any existing biencoder. The IDF analogy provides a clean theoretical motivation for why contextual embeddings should improve out-of-domain retrieval, and the finding that the largest gains appear on small, domain-specific datasets (ClimateFEVER, ArguAna, FiQA) suggests the method is most valuable precisely where standard biencoders struggle. Open questions include how the two-stage architecture scales to very large corpora (the 512-context-document limit), whether the benefit persists as base models grow larger, and why STS tasks appear nearly context-agnostic—suggesting the contextual signal is most relevant to discriminative retrieval rather than similarity estimation.
