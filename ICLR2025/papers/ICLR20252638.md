## Problem

Differentially private (DP) optimization requires careful tuning of the learning rate η and gradient clipping threshold R_g, typically via expensive grid search that is both computationally costly and introduces data-dependent privacy leakage. Existing remedies either consume a significant fraction of the privacy budget on hyperparameter selection (e.g., DP-hyper, adaptive clipping) or only address one hyperparameter (automatic clipping removes R_g but not η). The paper asks whether DP optimization can be made fully hyperparameter-free—eliminating manual tuning of both η and R_g—while preserving rigorous end-to-end DP guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard per-sample gradients and per-sample scalar losses from a forward/backward pass, along with a target DP budget (ε, δ), batch size B, total iterations T, and update interval K. It outputs updated model parameters at each step, an automatically determined learning rate η_GeN-DP, and an auto-regressively updated loss clipping threshold R_l. A key structural detail is that the loss privatization operates on a 1-dimensional scalar (per-sample loss) rather than a high-dimensional gradient vector, which changes the noise scaling from √d·σ_g·R_g/B to √(2/π)·σ_l·R_l/B and makes the clipping-bias tradeoff fundamentally different from gradient clipping.

## Method

HyFreeDP combines automatic per-sample gradient normalization (R_g → 0⁺, eliminating the clipping-threshold hyperparameter) with a DP-adapted version of the GeN learning rate, which estimates η by fitting a quadratic to loss values at {w − ηm, w, w + ηm}. The critical technical contribution is privatizing the loss values used in this quadratic fit: each per-sample loss L_i is clipped at R_l ≈ L (the current batch loss) and perturbed with Gaussian noise, yielding a private scalar  L̃ whose clipping bias is provably near-zero when R_l tracks the loss magnitude. The algorithm uses an auto-regressive design—R_l at iteration t is set to the previously privatized loss  L̃_{t−1}^{(0)}, and η is updated every K iterations from the privatized quadratic fit—so all data-dependent quantities are derived from already-privatized outputs, preserving DP via the post-processing property. An end-to-end privacy accountant allocates ~99% of the budget to gradient noise and ~1% to loss noise, requiring only γ ≤ 1.01× the vanilla gradient noise σ_g, which empirically adds negligible accuracy loss while enabling accurate learning-rate estimation.

## Results

On ViT-Small image classification at ε=1, HyFreeDP achieves 96.36% on CIFAR-10, 78.17% on CIFAR-100, 91.15% on SVHN, 74.68% on GTSRB, and 67.98% on Food101, matching or exceeding the non-DP grid-search upper bound on 3 of 5 datasets. At ε=3 with ViT-Base, HyFreeDP reaches 97.29% (CIFAR-10), 80.34% (CIFAR-100), 91.71% (SVHN), 91.76% (GTSRB), and 73.46% (Food101), surpassing the NonDP-GS baseline on 4 of 5 datasets. On GPT-2 E2E text generation at ε=3, HyFreeDP achieves BLEU 0.585, matching the non-DP grid-search baseline (0.583) while D-adaptation and Prodigy collapse to near-zero. Training overhead is under 1.1× compared to a single DP run at K=10, and under 2× even at K=1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-adaptation and Prodigy, the two state-of-the-art non-DP learning-rate-free methods, catastrophically fail under DP: at ε=1 on ViT-Small they achieve near-random accuracy (0.80% on CIFAR-100, 1.86% on GTSRB) versus HyFreeDP's 78.17% and 74.68%, confirming that their D/G estimation breaks down under large DP noise. DP-hyper, which spends ~15% of the privacy budget on a narrow grid search around the optimal η, is substantially outperformed by HyFreeDP at ε=1 (e.g., 74.63% vs. 78.17% on CIFAR-100 ViT-Small; 19.92% vs. 80.34% on CIFAR-100 ViT-Base at ε=3) due to the reduced gradient noise budget. Against the non-DP grid-search upper bound (NonDP-GS), HyFreeDP matches or exceeds it on most configurations: at ε=3 with ViT-Base it surpasses NonDP-GS on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., 80.34% vs. 79.03% on CIFAR-100, 73.46% vs. 66.87% on Food101), and in the non-DP regime it slightly exceeds NonDP-GS on CIFAR-100 (90.84% vs. 89.41%) and GTSRB (99.00% vs. 98.66%). On GPT-2 E2E text generation, HyFreeDP matches the NonDP-GS BLEU score (0.585 vs. 0.583 at ε=3) while D-adaptation and Prodigy produce near-zero BLEU, demonstrating that the advantage generalizes to language tasks and larger models (LLaMa2-7B with LoRA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: automatic (parameter-free) learning rate scheduling in non-DP optimization (GeN, D-adaptation, Prodigy) and differentially private optimization (DP-SGD, automatic clipping). The key conceptual departure is that while prior DP hyperparameter tuning methods (DP-HYPO, Rényi-DP tuning) spend a meaningful fraction of the privacy budget on searching over (R_g, η), this paper follows the 'hyperparameter-free' path by combining automatic clipping (eliminating R_g) with a DP-compliant version of GeN's loss-curvature-based learning rate (eliminating η). The novel technical ingredient is the loss value privatization with an auto-regressive clipping threshold R_l ≈ L, which makes the quadratic curve fitting for η_GeN differentially private while introducing only <1% additional gradient noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes the last major manual hyperparameter (learning rate) from DP training while preserving rigorous end-to-end privacy guarantees, making DP optimization nearly as simple as non-DP optimization in practice. The finding that standard parameter-free schedulers (D-adaptation, Prodigy) catastrophically fail under DP noise—dropping to near-random accuracy at ε=1—highlights that the DP regime requires fundamentally different treatment of adaptive learning rates, not just noise injection. Open questions include whether the auto-regressive R_l design is optimal, how the method behaves in federated or multi-party DP settings, and whether the <1% overhead assumption holds under tighter budgets (ε < 1) or larger model scales beyond 7B parameters.
