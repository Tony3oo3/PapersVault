## Problem

Existing MLLMs face a fundamental tension in long video understanding: specialized temporal grounding models (e.g., TimeChat) sacrifice general video QA capability, while long-video MLLMs (e.g., MovieChat) capture only holistic semantics without fine-grained temporal localization. No prior end-to-end MLLM performs well on both temporal grounding and general long video QA simultaneously, and the hallucination risk of LLMs in long-context reasoning remains unaddressed by architecture-only solutions.

## Input / Output

The model ingests long videos uniformly sampled into K clips of T=8 frames each (up to 192 frames in training, 128 at inference), encoded at 224×224 resolution, alongside a natural language query. Outputs are free-form text that may include start/end timestamps (e.g., 'between 22.6–24.2 seconds'), detailed segment-level descriptions paired with timestamps (Temporal Grounded Caption format), or standard multiple-choice/open-ended QA answers. A key design constraint is that Token Shuffle compresses visual tokens to just 3 per frame (vs. 12–196 in competing methods), keeping the LLM input length manageable while preserving temporal ordering.

## Method

TimeSuite combines three complementary designs built on VideoChat2 (UMT-L encoder + Q-Former + Mistral-7B). First, Token Shuffle concatenates m=4 adjacent visual tokens along the channel dimension and projects them with a linear layer whose weights are initialized by replicating the original projector m times, yielding an initialization equivalent to mean pooling that avoids random-parameter disruption. Second, TAPE (Temporal Adaptive Position Encoding) is a U-Net-like adapter of 1D depthwise-separable convolutions that progressively downsamples the token sequence, applies a long-window convolution with zero-padding anchors (inspired by CPVT) to encode relative temporal positions, then upsamples with residual connections, adding temporal embeddings via a residual connection without disrupting pretrained parameters. Third, the TimePro dataset (349K annotations, 9 task types, 15 sources) introduces a novel Temporal Grounded Caption task where a brief scene title query requires the model to simultaneously predict precise start/end timestamps and generate a detailed description, forcing fine-grained visual-linguistic alignment that regularizes both grounding and captioning against hallucination. Training uses a two-phase strategy: TAPE is frozen during epoch 1 (192 frames) to preserve general features, then unfrozen in epochs 2–3 (128 frames) with LoRA (rank 16) on the LLM.

## Results

On temporal grounding, VideoChat-T achieves 48.7 R@1 (IoU=0.5) on Charades-STA and 26.5 mAP on QVHighlights in zero-shot setting, surpassing the previous best MLLM (TimeChat) by 16.5 and 12.0 points respectively. After fine-tuning on the benchmark training set, VideoChat-T reaches 67.1 R@1 (IoU=0.5) on Charades-STA, exceeding classic supervised expert models QD-DETR (57.3) and UnLoc-L (60.8). On long video QA, VideoChat-T improves over VideoChat2 by 5.6% on Egoschema (60.0 vs 54.4) and 6.8% on VideoMME without subtitles (46.3 vs 39.5), while retaining 59.9% on MVBench (only 0.5% loss).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoChat-T decisively outperforms all prior MLLM baselines on temporal grounding: it surpasses TimeChat (32.2) by 16.5 points on Charades-STA R@1 (IoU=0.5), ChatVTG (33.0) by 15.7 points, and HawkEye (31.4) by 17.3 points, while also leading on QVHighlights mAP (26.5 vs. TimeChat's 14.5). On long video QA, VideoChat-T beats MovieChat (53.5 on Egoschema, 38.2 on VideoMME) and TimeChat (33.0 on Egoschema, 30.2 on VideoMME) by large margins, confirming that grounding-centric tuning improves rather than degrades general video understanding. Compared to its direct base model VideoChat2, VideoChat-T gains 5.6% on Egoschema and 6.8% on VideoMME while suffering only a 0.5% drop on MVBench (59.9 vs. 60.4), demonstrating that the TimeSuite design preserves short-video capabilities. After fine-tuning on the Charades-STA training set, VideoChat-T (67.1 R@1 IoU=0.5) surpasses supervised expert models QD-DETR (57.3) and UnLoc-L (60.8), though it remains below HawkEye-FT (58.3) on QVHighlights mAP due to the difficulty of outputting many discrete timestamp-saliency pairs in language form. The paper also shows that applying TimeSuite to LLaVA-OneVision yields +3.2 on VideoMME and +4.7 on MLVU, confirming transferability beyond the VideoChat2 backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VideoChat2 (Li et al., 2024b), extending its short-video MLLM architecture to handle long videos via Token Shuffle compression and TAPE positional encoding. It departs from prior temporal grounding MLLMs (TimeChat, VTimeLLM, HawkEye) that use specialized decoder modules and sacrifice general QA capability, and from long-video MLLMs (MovieChat, LLaMA-Vid) that focus on token compression or long-context LLMs without fine-grained temporal localization. The key conceptual departure is treating temporal grounding as a regularizing auxiliary task during instruction tuning to improve long video QA and reduce hallucination, rather than as a separate capability requiring dedicated architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end MLLM to achieve strong performance on both temporal grounding and general video QA simultaneously, addressing a trade-off that prior work (e.g., TimeChat dropping to 38.5% on MVBench) could not resolve. The Temporal Grounded Caption task and TimePro dataset (349K annotations, 9 task types) provide reusable resources for the community. Open questions remain: highlight detection still lags expert models due to LLMs' difficulty outputting many precise numbers, complex multi-step reasoning is weak, and it is unclear whether further expert tasks (spatial grounding, tracking) can be internalized without degrading general capabilities. The extreme token efficiency (3 tokens/frame, 0.63s inference) makes this practically relevant for real-time long video applications.
