## Problem

Existing stability-based generalization bounds for adversarial training either assume the inner maximization is solved exactly (invalid for deep networks), produce bounds that do not vanish with sample size, or are independent of the specific perturbation used during training. This leaves the phenomenon of robust overfitting—where models trained with PGD-based AT remain vulnerable to the same PGD attack on test data—without a principled theoretical explanation. The paper asks: what property of the adversarial perturbation governs generalization, and why does sign-PGD specifically cause poor robust generalization?

## Input / Output

The framework takes standard image-label pairs (32×32 RGB from CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100, 32×32 from SVHN) and perturbation parameters (infinity-norm radius ε, PGD step size λ, number of PGD steps K) as input, and produces both trained CNN parameters (Pre-activation ResNet-18 or Wide ResNet-34) and closed-form generalization upper bounds parameterized by expansiveness constants. The evaluation metric is a flexible J-loss (perturbation-induced loss) that subsumes both standard loss (J = identity) and adversarial loss (J = optimal perturbation), enabling analysis of mismatched generalization where the training perturbation π differs from the evaluation perturbation J.

## Method

The paper introduces a c-expansiveness parameter for perturbation maps that measures sensitivity to weight perturbations above a threshold c, and derives uniform stability bounds for adversarial training where the generalization gap is governed by the expansiveness of both the evaluation perturbation J and the training perturbation π. When J has bounded expansiveness, the bound vanishes as O(1/n); when J is sign-PGD (unbounded expansiveness due to the sign function), the bound converges to a non-vanishing constant involving ε√d, revealing an intrinsic tension between perturbation radius, input dimension, and stability. The paper identifies the sign function in PGD as the root cause of robust overfitting and proposes replacing it with a smooth tanh_γ approximation, where decreasing γ reduces expansiveness and empirically shrinks generalization gaps. Extending the analysis to a family of G_p-PGD perturbations (steepest descent methods with p-norm balls), the paper proves that all members with p ≥ 1.3 suffer from poor Lipschitzness of G_p, explaining why larger p values produce worse overfitting in experiments.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: generalization bounds for adversarial training expressed in terms of an 'expansiveness' parameter of the perturbation maps. Experimentally, on CIFAR-10, reducing the smoothness parameter γ in tanh_γ-PGD (from γ=10^5 to γ=10) eliminates the robust overfitting phenomenon where testing error rises after the 100th epoch. For the G_p-PGD family, nearly all variants with p≥1.3 cause generalization gaps exceeding 30% on CIFAR-10, while p=1 (coordinate gradient) shows good generalization. The sign-PGD perturbation is confirmed as the strongest attack among all G_p-PGD variants, producing the highest J-(0-1) loss even on the training set, yet simultaneously causing the worst generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the standard sign-PGD (Madry et al., 2019) against two alternatives: tanh_γ-PGD (smooth approximation of sign) and RG-PGD (raw gradient, i.e., G=identity). On CIFAR-10, sign-PGD produces the highest adversarial loss on both training and testing sets, confirming it is the strongest perturbation, but it also yields the largest generalization gap. Replacing sign with tanh_γ (γ=10) reduces the generalization gap substantially and eliminates the robust overfitting observed with γ=10^5 (which closely mimics sign-PGD). RG-PGD achieves the smallest generalization gaps across all evaluation metrics but is notably weaker as an attack, leaving models vulnerable to sign-PGD even on the training set. The paper further extends the comparison to the full G_p-PGD family (p∈{1,2,6,∞}), showing that generalization gaps monotonically increase with p, with p≥1.3 all exceeding 30% gap, while the theoretical analysis attributes this to the increasing Lipschitz constant α_p of the G_p mapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the uniform stability framework of Hardt et al. (2016) and the uniform argument stability (UAS) concept from Bassily et al. (2020), extending them to adversarial training by introducing a new 'expansiveness' property of perturbation maps. It departs from prior stability analyses of AT (Xing et al. 2021; Xiao et al. 2022b; Wang et al. 2024) by showing that those bounds either fail to vanish with sample size or are independent of the specific perturbation used, whereas the expansiveness-based bounds here capture how the choice of perturbation (e.g., sign-PGD vs. tanh-PGD) directly governs generalization. The paper also connects sign-PGD to the steepest descent method framework from convex optimization, revealing it as a projected SDM with respect to the ∞-norm ball, and extends this to a broader G_p-PGD family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the sign function as a root cause of robust overfitting is a conceptual shift: it suggests that the trade-off between robustness and standard accuracy highlighted by Tsipras et al. (2018) may be an artifact of sign-PGD rather than an inherent limitation of adversarial training. The expansiveness framework provides a principled lens for understanding why different PGD variants generalize differently, and the G_p-PGD extension suggests an intrinsic difficulty in adversarial training that goes beyond any single attack choice. However, the paper only provides upper bounds (acknowledged as a limitation), the empirical validation is limited to CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN with standard architectures, and the practical remedy (tanh-PGD) is shown to be a weaker attack than sign-PGD even on the training set, leaving open the question of how to achieve both strong inner maximization and good generalization simultaneously.
