## Problem

Spiking neural networks (SNNs) offer bio-plausible, event-driven energy efficiency but have been stuck below 1B parameters due to two bottlenecks: firing-rate encoding requires T binary steps to represent T quantization levels (vs. log₂T bits for standard quantization), and training via BPTT or ANN-SNN conversion is impractical at LLM scale. Meanwhile, quantized LLMs suffer significant accuracy loss on outlier/salient channels in low-bit regimes, and mix-precision workarounds are hardware-unfriendly. The paper asks whether spiking dynamics can be scaled to 7–70B parameter LLMs while matching or exceeding quantization performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA-1-7B, LLaMA-2-7B/13B/70B) plus 128 WikiText2 calibration samples (2048-token chunks) and produces a spiking-quantized model where activations and/or weights are encoded as multi-step spike trains rather than single-step quantized integers. Inference can run in two modes: multi-bit GEMM on GPUs (equivalent to single-precision quantization with per-channel spiking masks) or 1-bit event-driven operations on neuromorphic chips (where merged GIF spikes are decoded back to binary IF spikes). Outputs are standard LLM outputs—generated text, zero-shot reasoning answers, and perplexity scores—evaluated on WikiText2, C4, and six reasoning benchmarks.

## Method

SpikeLLM introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, the Generalized Integrate-and-Fire (GIF) neuron merges L consecutive IF spiking steps into a single multi-bit firing event with log₂L levels, compressing the total code length from T bits to (T/L)·log₂L bits while preserving recursive membrane-potential dynamics for accurate multi-step encoding of salient values. Second, the Optimal Brain Spiking (OBSpiking) framework—a weight-activation generalization of Optimal Brain Surgeon—computes activation saliency via first-order gradient (X∘WᵀWX) and weight saliency via second-order Hessian (W²/[H⁻¹]²) to identify per-channel salient subsets; these salient channels receive T′ spiking steps while remaining channels use one step, compressing the overall code to ≈log₂T bits. The spiking mechanism is plugged into existing quantization pipelines (OmniQuant for weight-activation, GPTQ for weight-only), and a ternary-spike variant (SpikeLLM_Ter) enables fully additive (AC-only) linear layers that outperform PB-LLM at matched operation counts.

## Results

On LLAMA-1-7B W4A4, SpikeLLM_T=2 achieves 52.48% average zero-shot accuracy, outperforming OmniQuant (49.93%) by 2.55% and reducing WikiText2 perplexity from 11.85 to 9.98 (11.01% reduction). The gains are more dramatic at lower bit-widths: on LLAMA-2-7B W2A16, SpikeLLM_T=2 reaches 49.85% vs. OmniQuant's 41.75% (+8.10%), and on LLAMA-2-70B W2A16, SpikeLLM_T=2 achieves 60.38% vs. 53.31% (+7.07%). In the additive (ternary) setting, SpikeLLM_Ter,70:25:5 achieves 52.10% average accuracy with full AC operations, exceeding PB-LLM80% (46.93%) at comparable equal-steps (1.4 vs. 2.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikeLLM consistently outperforms all quantization baselines. Against OmniQuant (the primary baseline), SpikeLLM gains 2.55% on W4A4, 4.84% on W2A8, and 8.10% on W2A16 for LLAMA-2-7B, with the gap widening at larger scale (7.07% on LLAMA-2-70B W2A16). Against SmoothQuant and LLM-QAT, the improvements are even larger (52.48% vs. 38.41% and 41.27% on LLAMA-1-7B W4A4), confirming that spiking-based encoding addresses outlier quantization errors that traditional methods cannot. Against QuaRot (rotation-based outlier-free quantization), SpikeLLM-GPTQ achieves 66.76% vs. QuaRot-GPTQ's 65.61% on LLAMA-2-7B W4A4, showing compatibility with rotation. In the additive/ternary setting, SpikeLLM_Ter,70:25:5 (52.10%) exceeds PB-LLM80% (46.93%) by 5.17% at fewer equal-steps (1.4 vs. 2.4), and dominates the Pareto front in Figure 5. In QLoRA fine-tuning (Table 11), SpikeLLM-QLoRA_T=2 reaches 30.3% average vs. QLoRA (26.2%), QA-LoRA (27.5%), and IR-QLoRA (27.8%), a 2.5–4.1% margin. In the EfficientQAT 2-bit setting (Table 12), SpikeLLM also surpasses the GPTQ 2-bit baseline, confirming generality across training pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: spiking neural networks (SNNs) and LLM quantization. It generalizes the classic Integrate-and-Fire neuron into the GIF neuron (merging L spiking steps into multi-bit quantization) and extends the Optimal Brain Surgeon pruning framework into Optimal Brain Spiking for joint weight-activation saliency detection. Conceptually, it departs from prior SNN work (which was limited to <1B parameters due to inefficient firing-rate encoding requiring T steps for T levels) by compressing spike length to ~log₂T bits, and departs from prior quantization work by replacing uniform single-step quantizers with saliency-aware multi-step spiking that can exceed quantization accuracy on salient channels. The ternary spike formulation from SpikeLM is subsumed as a special case of GIF, unifying additive SNN inference within the same framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that spiking neural dynamics can be scaled to 7–70B parameter LLMs and can outperform standard quantization (not merely match it), particularly in extreme low-bit regimes (W2A8, W2A16) where quantization errors from outliers are most severe. The dual deployment path—GEMM on GPUs or event-driven 1-bit operations on neuromorphic chips—makes the framework practically relevant for both current and future hardware. Key limitations include reliance on ANN-SNN calibration with only 128 WikiText2 samples (3.5 GPU-hours for 7B), a persistent gap to full-precision models, and evaluation primarily on LLAMA architectures. Open questions include whether continuous pretraining or larger calibration sets would close the remaining gap, whether the approach generalizes to non-LLM architectures (e.g., multimodal), and whether neuromorphic hardware can practically exploit the event-driven sparsity at LLM scale.
