## Problem

Cross-Entropy (CE) loss, the de facto objective for Supervised Fine-Tuning of LLMs, maximizes the likelihood of observed training data while suppressing all alternative tokens, leading to reduced output diversity and knowledge forgetting (alignment tax). This is particularly harmful because SFT is the initial post-training stage, and downstream stages such as RLHF, self-improvement, and test-time scaling all depend on diverse outputs for exploration. The paper asks whether a principled training objective can align the model with instruction data while preserving the diversity that pre-trained LLMs naturally possess.

## Input / Output

The method consumes prompt-response pairs (formatted in Llama-3.1 chat template, max 2048 tokens) and produces a fine-tuned autoregressive LLM that generates text responses. At the token level, the loss operates on one-step conditional distributions over a finite vocabulary (128k for Llama-3.1); for sequential generation, a 'reset' technique decomposes the joint sequence-level problem into per-token sub-problems where the prefix is drawn from the data distribution. The output model is evaluated via best-of-N sampling (32 responses per prompt for chat, 200 for code) and greedy decoding on standard benchmarks.

## Method

GEM (Game-theoretic Entropy Maximization) formulates SFT as a two-player game: the model distribution f minimizes a loss that increases log-probability of real tokens while decreasing it for generated tokens, while an auxiliary distribution q maximizes an objective combining log f with entropy regularization. The Nash equilibrium of this game is proven to correspond to reverse KL minimization with maximum entropy regularization, yielding f* = softmax(β·log p). Crucially, q admits a closed-form solution q = softmax((1/β)·log f), eliminating the need for a separate network and enabling single-model optimization with variance-reduced gradients (the expectation over q is computed exactly over the finite vocabulary). The hyperparameter β controls the trade-off: β→1 recovers CE, while smaller β encourages sparse updates (only the highest-confidence source token transfers probability) and adaptive termination (stopping once the target token becomes most probable), thereby protecting tail probabilities and mitigating forgetting.

## Results

On AlpacaEval (BoN@32, 805 questions), GEM achieves a 50.4% win rate vs. GPT-4, outperforming CE (47.3%) by 3.1 points (6.6% relative) while requiring ~2× fewer samples to match CE's performance. On HumanEval (pass@100, 163 questions), GEM reaches 82.5% pass rate vs. CE's 75.5%, a 7-point (9.3% relative) improvement. GEM also reduces alignment tax by 80% (0.3-point average drop across six OpenLLM tasks vs. 1.5-point drop for CE) and increases output entropy from 0.42 (CE) to 0.76 (GEM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM consistently outperforms all three baselines (CE, CE+WD, NEFT) across every evaluated task. On chat (AlpacaEval BoN@32), GEM gains 3.1 points over CE and surpasses NEFT, which benefits partly from longer responses. On code generation (HumanEval pass@100), GEM improves by 7 points over CE (82.5 vs. 75.5), while CE+WD gains only 1.1 points (76.6) and NEFT shows no improvement (75.6). On GSM8K, GEM leads in all three metrics (greedy, majority voting, pass rate), with the largest gap in majority voting (+2.2 over CE). For alignment tax, GEM reduces the average performance drop across six OpenLLM tasks from 1.5 points (CE) to 0.3 points, an 80% reduction, while CE+WD and NEFT show intermediate but still larger drops than GEM. Notably, GEM achieves these gains without sacrificing direct generation quality (e.g., chat: CE 24.5 vs. GEM 24.9; code: CE 32.7 vs. GEM 32.6), unlike NEFT which trades some quality for diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard CE-loss formulation of SFT by reframing it as a distribution matching problem with entropy regularization, solved via a game-theoretic min-max objective. While structurally reminiscent of GANs, the auxiliary variable q here serves as a meta-controller for probability transfer (selecting which source tokens to update and when to stop) rather than a discriminator measuring distributional distance. The approach is conceptually closest to GAIL in imitation learning and to maximum-entropy IRL, but differs from SPIN (Chen et al., 2024) in motivation (preserving diversity vs. exploiting data more fully) and in placing entropy regularization directly in the game formulation rather than at the algorithm level. It also subsumes the CE+weight-decay and NEFT regularization strategies as special cases or approximations, while showing that naive CE+entropy regularization fails due to tail-probability blowup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because SFT is the first post-training stage, diversity reduction here propagates into RLHF, test-time scaling, and self-improvement pipelines; showing that a drop-in replacement for CE (same compute, same memory) can preserve diversity while cutting alignment tax by ~83% is practically significant for teams running multi-stage post-training. The unification of 'diversity reduction' and 'knowledge forgetting' as two facets of the same all-to-one probability transfer mechanism provides a cleaner diagnostic than prior ad-hoc regularizers. Open questions include whether the β=0 (Dirac) regime admits a tractable solution, whether the sequential 'reset' approximation loses information at longer contexts, and whether the diversity gains transfer to online RL cold-start and preference learning stages where the paper only speculates.
