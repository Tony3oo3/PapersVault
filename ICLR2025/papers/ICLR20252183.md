## Problem

Existing video tokenizers for autoregressive generative models follow a patchwise paradigm that restricts discrete tokens to local patch-level features, limiting their ability to capture global and semantic video representations. Additionally, the gap between a tokenizer's reconstruction fidelity and the downstream AR model's generation fidelity remains poorly understood and largely unaddressed for video, with no principled mechanism to align the discrete latent space with AR generation.

## Input / Output

LARP takes 16-frame video clips at 128×128 resolution (patchified with f_T=4, f_H=8, f_W=8 into 1024 patches) and produces a flexible number of discrete holistic tokens (e.g., 1024, 512, or 256) from a factorized codebook of size 8192 with dimension 8. For class-conditional generation on UCF-101, a [cls] token conditions the AR generator; for frame prediction on K600, 5 conditioning frames are padded to 16 frames and tokenized to provide context. The AR prior model (21.7M params) is used only during tokenizer training and is discarded at inference, adding zero overhead to the generation pipeline.

## Method

LARP replaces patchwise tokenization with a holistic scheme: n learnable query embeddings are concatenated with ViT-style patch embeddings and processed by a transformer encoder, with only the query-position outputs quantized into discrete tokens via stochastic vector quantization (SVQ), which replaces the deterministic argmin lookup with a softmax-sampled multinomial over cosine similarities. To align the latent space with AR generation, a lightweight continuous AR transformer (adapted from GPT-2) is co-trained end-to-end with the tokenizer; its input layer uses a linear projection instead of embedding lookup, and its output layer predicts the next token via SVQ-style cosine similarity against the evolving codebook, ensuring gradient flow and codebook awareness. Scheduled sampling (mixing predicted and ground-truth tokens for a second forward pass) mitigates exposure bias as the codebook shifts during training. The total loss combines reconstruction (L1, LPIPS, GAN, SVQ) with the AR prior NLL loss weighted by α=0.06, and the prior model is discarded at inference, yielding an AR-optimized latent space with an automatically learned token ordering.

## Results

LARP achieves a state-of-the-art FVD of 57 on the UCF-101 class-conditional video generation benchmark with a 632M-parameter AR generator, surpassing the previous best (MAGVIT-v2-MLM at 58) and all AR-based methods. On the K600 frame prediction task, LARP-L-Long achieves an rFVD of 20 with 1024 tokens. The ablation in Table 2 confirms the AR prior model is the dominant contributor: removing it degrades gFVD from 107 to 190 (LARP-B), while reconstruction quality actually improves slightly (rFVD 31→23), demonstrating that the prior optimizes the latent space specifically for generation rather than reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among AR generative models, LARP (gFVD 57) substantially outperforms MAGVIT-v2-AR (109), OmniTokenizer (191), MAGVIT-AR (265), TATS (332), and CogVideo (626) on UCF-101, with the largest margin over CogVideo (11x). LARP also edges out the best diffusion-based method HPDM (66) and the best MLM method MAGVIT-v2-MLM (58) by 9 and 1 FVD points respectively, making it the overall SOTA across all generative paradigms. On K600 frame prediction, LARP-L-Long achieves the best rFVD (20) among all methods reporting this metric, compared to OmniTokenizer (42) and MAGVIT-MLM (25). Notably, LARP uses only 1024 tokens versus 1280 for MAGVIT-v2 and OmniTokenizer, and its 632M generator is smaller than CogVideo's 9.4B, indicating superior tokenization efficiency. The scaling experiments (Figure 3) further show that LARP's gFVD saturates at the LARP-L tokenizer size while rFVD continues to improve, and reducing tokens from 1024 to 256 degrades gFVD more slowly than rFVD, confirming the holistic tokenization's generative efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

LARP extends the VQ-VAE/VQGAN lineage of discrete visual tokenization by replacing the standard patchwise encoding with a holistic query-based scheme borrowed from DETR and BLIP-2, decoupling discrete tokens from fixed spatial locations. It further departs from prior tokenizers (MAGVIT, TATS, OmniTokenizer) by co-training a lightweight GPT-2-style AR transformer as a training-time prior that shapes the latent space for autoregressive generation, a component discarded at inference. The stochastic vector quantization (SVQ) replaces deterministic argmin lookup with softmax-sampled multinomial selection, and scheduled sampling is adapted to mitigate exposure bias in the evolving codebook.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that tokenizer design for AR video generation should optimize for generation fidelity, not just reconstruction fidelity—a finding supported by the ablation where removing the AR prior yields the best rFVD (23) but the worst gFVD (190). This opens a practical path toward unified multimodal LLMs that handle video generation alongside language, since the holistic tokenization supports arbitrary token counts and the AR prior adds zero inference overhead. Limitations include fixed-resolution operation due to positional encoding, artifacts in complex scenes, and evaluation at relatively low resolution (128×128, 16 frames), leaving open whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer videos.
