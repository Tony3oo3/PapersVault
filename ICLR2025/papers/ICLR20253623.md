## Problem

Existing 4D generation methods produce only object-level content at low resolution or perform outpainting from a single perspective view, failing to deliver the free-viewpoint, 360° dynamic environments required by VR/AR applications. The core obstacles are the scarcity of annotated 4D panoramic training data and the inability of 2D diffusion models—trained on narrow-FoV perspective images—to generate coherent animation across the full 360° field of view at high resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253623/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single equirectangular panoramic image at 4096×2048 (4K) along with user-defined bounding-box masks (or text prompts) specifying which regions should animate. It produces a set of T dynamic 3D Gaussian splatting representations (one per timestamp) that support real-time rendering of novel views from arbitrary camera positions and orientations, with an intermediate panoramic video at full 4K resolution. The 4K output is notable as the first panoramic 4D generation at this resolution, requiring ~73 GB GPU memory per frame during animation.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a Panoramic Denoiser animates the static panorama by operating in a spherical latent space: at each of 25 denoising steps, the spherical latent is projected into 20 overlapping perspective views (80° FoV each), each view is denoised one step by a pre-trained Animate-Anything/SVD model, and the results are fused back into the spherical latent by minimizing the L2 distance between re-projected and denoised latents (Eq. 3), ensuring cross-view consistency without requiring panoramic-specific training data. Second, Dynamic Panoramic Lifting converts the panoramic video into 4D Gaussians: MiDaS depth maps from each perspective view are aligned via optimized per-view scale and shift parameters (Spatial-Temporal Geometry Alignment, Eq. 4) to produce temporally consistent panoramic depth, then T sets of 3D Gaussians are optimized with RGB loss, a temporal consistency loss between adjacent frames, DINO feature distillation under camera perturbation, and a depth-correlation geometric regularization term.

## Results

On the primary evaluation (16 text-generated panoramas), 4K4DGen achieves Q-Align scores of 0.66 (Image Quality), 0.44 (Image Aesthetics), and 0.62 (Video Quality), substantially outperforming 3D-Cinemagraphy (0.47–0.48 IQ, 0.57–0.58 VQ). In user studies, 4K4DGen is preferred for visual quality 81% of the time versus 7–12% for 3D-Cinemagraphy, and shows markedly better motion naturalness (59.2% vs. ~20%). On the supplementary WEB360 dataset (32 scenes), 4K4DGen further reduces FVD from ~308 to 218 and KVD from ~5.8 to 1.76 compared to 3D-Cinemagraphy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4K4DGen consistently outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric. Against 3D-Cinemagraphy (Li et al., 2023), the primary baseline, 4K4DGen gains +0.18 in IQ, +0.04 in IA, and +0.04 in VQ, while dominating user preference (81% vs. 7–12% for quality selection). Against 4DGen (Yin et al., 2023), an object-level 4D generation method, 4K4DGen achieves 0.66 IQ versus 0.19, reflecting the fundamental domain gap between object-centric and scene-level generation. Against LucidDreamer+OurAnimator (Chung et al., 2023), 4K4DGen leads by +0.22 in IQ (0.66 vs. 0.44) and +0.04 in VQ, indicating that the panoramic denoising and spatial-temporal geometry alignment provide substantial gains over a generic scene-generation pipeline. Against DimensionX+OurAnimator (Sun et al., 2024), the closest competitor, 4K4DGen still leads by +0.11 in IQ (0.66 vs. 0.55) and +0.02 in VQ, confirming that the spherical latent denoising strategy and depth alignment are critical for panoramic 4D quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active research threads: (1) 4D scene generation via dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting (4DGen, AYG, DreamGaussian4D), which has so far been limited to object-level or single-perspective content; (2) panoramic 3D scene generation (DreamScene360, 360-GS, PERF), which produces static 360° environments; and (3) video diffusion / I2V animation (Animate-Anything, Stable Video Diffusion), which provides the generative prior for temporal dynamics. The key architectural departure is the Panoramic Denoiser, which performs denoising in a spherical latent space by projecting to perspective views, applying a pre-trained perspective denoiser, and fusing results back onto the sphere at each step — a project-and-fuse strategy inspired by MultiDiffusion and SyncDiffusion but adapted to the spherical geometry of panoramas. The Dynamic Panoramic Lifting stage extends DreamScene360's depth-regularized Gaussian optimization with explicit spatial-temporal geometry alignment (scale/shift parameters per perspective view) to enforce cross-frame coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that 4K (4096×2048) panoramic 4D content can be generated from a single static panorama without any annotated 4D training data, directly addressing a critical gap for VR/AR content pipelines that require free-viewpoint, 360° dynamic environments. However, the practical impact is currently limited by extreme computational cost (~22 min/frame for animation + ~33 min/frame for lifting at 4K on a single A100), the inability to synthesize significant environmental changes (e.g., weather transitions, new object appearances), and substantial storage requirements for the time-dependent Gaussian representation. Open questions include whether the spherical denoising strategy generalizes to longer video sequences, whether more advanced I2V models can be seamlessly integrated, and whether distillation or compression can make the pipeline tractable for production use.
