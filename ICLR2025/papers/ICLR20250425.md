## Problem

LLM-based video understanding approaches achieve strong performance on long-video QnA benchmarks, but it remains unclear whether they genuinely exploit video-specific temporal and motion information or simply leverage the LLM's internal world knowledge and reasoning. The paper reveals that modality-constrained baselines with zero or single-frame visual input perform surprisingly well—sometimes matching state-of-the-art methods using 180 frames—exposing a gap between benchmark performance and actual video modality utilization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250425/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a video (uniformly sampled to 8 frames for captioning and 16 for detection/tracking), a text question, and a set of candidate answers. Video information is extracted as three object-centric text modalities (object categories, spatial coordinates with scale, and per-frame motion trajectories) and fused with the question via fixed language templates. For multiple-choice tasks, output is a single selected answer via likelihood selection in one forward pass; for open-ended tasks, it is a generated text answer matched against ground truth.

## Method

MVU extracts three object-centric information modalities from video using off-the-shelf image-trained tools: a VLM captioner identifies object categories across 8 frames (Global Object Information), an OWL-ViT open-vocabulary detector localizes each object with center coordinates and bounding-box scale across 16 frames (Object Spatial Location), and a feature-matching tracker constructs per-object motion trajectories across frames (Object Motion Trajectory). These modalities are fused as natural language text via fixed templates and injected into a VLM (LLaVA-13B) alongside a single center frame and the question, requiring no video-level training. For inference, the paper adapts likelihood selection (cloze prompting) to video QnA: all candidate answers are batched with the question, a single forward pass computes per-answer cross-entropy in the LLM's latent space, and the lowest-error answer is selected—avoiding iterative autoregressive generation and its associated hallucinations. This object-centric pipeline acts as an information bottleneck, using fewer context tokens (~1124 vs. ~1940 for LLoVi) while achieving higher accuracy, and generalizes to robotics domain tasks and open-ended QnA.

## Results

On EgoSchema (full test set, 5031 videos), LVNet+MVU achieves 61.3% top-1 accuracy under zero-shot settings with no video-level training, outperforming LVNet (61.1%), VideoTree (61.1%), and LLoVi with GPT-4 (50.3%). On NExT-QA, LVNet+MVU reaches 73.3% overall accuracy (75.2% causal, 66.8% temporal, 81.3% descriptive), matching VideoTree (73.5%) and surpassing LVNet (72.9%) and VideoAgent (71.3%). The smaller 13B-parameter SF-VLM+MVU variant achieves 37.6% on EgoSchema and 55.4% on NExT-QA, while the modality-constrained Single-Frame-VLM baseline (1 frame, no video-specific info) already reaches 55.8% on EgoSchema-S—outperforming LLoVi which uses 180 frames (50.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EgoSchema, LVNet+MVU (61.3%) edges out LVNet (61.1%) and VideoTree (61.1%), and substantially outperforms VideoAgent (54.1%), MoreVQA (51.7%), and LLoVi with GPT-4 (50.3%). On NExT-QA, LVNet+MVU (73.3%) is within 0.2% of VideoTree (73.5%) while clearly beating LVNet (72.9%), VideoAgent (71.3%), MoreVQA (69.2%), and LLoVi+GPT-4 (67.7%). The 13B SF-VLM+MVU variant (37.6% on EgoSchema) surpasses LLoVi (33.5%) and Vamos (36.7%) but trails LangRepo (41.2%) and all trillion-parameter closed-model baselines. A striking finding is that the Single-Frame-VLM baseline (1 frame, 55.8% on EgoSchema-S) outperforms LLoVi using 180 frames (50.8%) at 1/100th the inference cost, revealing that much of the 'video understanding' in prior LLM-based approaches may stem from world knowledge rather than temporal video information. On robotics domain tasks (Open X-Embodiment), MVU improves over the LLoVi baseline from 28.5% to 30.4% overall, while modality-constrained variants perform near random (21.9–23.5%), confirming that the gains come from genuine video-specific information rather than LLM priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three distinct threads: (1) likelihood selection / cloze prompting from Robinson et al. (2023), which is adapted here to video QA for efficient single-pass answer selection; (2) language-based multimodal fusion from Zeng et al. (2022, Socratic Models), repurposed to fuse object-centric video information into a VLM prompt; and (3) the LLoVi pipeline from Zhang et al. (2023a), which uses per-frame VLM captions as video input. The key departure is a diagnostic reframing: rather than assuming video QA requires temporal processing, the paper first isolates how much performance comes from LLM world knowledge alone (Just-LLM) or a single frame (SF-VLM), then introduces MVU as a minimal, interpretable injection of object-centric video-specific information (global objects, spatial locations, motion trajectories) extracted by off-the-shelf detectors and trackers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single center frame outperforms a 180-frame SOTA on EgoSchema (55.8% vs 50.8%) while being >100× faster is a significant challenge to the field's implicit assumption that long-video understanding requires dense temporal processing, and it calls for more careful evaluation protocols that account for world-knowledge shortcuts. The MVU framework offers a practical, zero-shot, training-free pipeline whose object-centric representation acts as an information bottleneck complementary to frame selection, and the likelihood-selection adaptation provides a broadly applicable efficiency technique for any LLM-based MCQ task. Open questions include whether object-centric information suffices for tasks requiring scene-level dynamics or non-object-centric reasoning, and whether the world-knowledge shortcut effect persists as benchmarks are redesigned to be more video-specific (as the robotics domain results suggest it does not).
