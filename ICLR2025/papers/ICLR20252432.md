## Problem

Existing Theory of Mind benchmarks for LLMs rely on limited, manually-crafted datasets with restricted action sets and simple patterns, leading to evaluation blind spots and overestimation of model capabilities. These benchmarks risk rapid saturation and data leakage, and prior work shows that even trivial alterations to ToM tasks can break model performance. The paper addresses the need for a scalable, adversarial framework that generates diverse and challenging ToM data for both robust evaluation and effective fine-tuning of LLMs.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a target LLM (serving as the adversarial evaluator) and user-specified constraints (number of people, important action types, rooms, total action budget) as input. It produces story structures as typed action sequences in a domain-specific language, paired with question-answer pairs whose ground truth is derived directly from tracked belief and world states without any LLM involvement in QA generation. Optionally, story structures are iteratively infilled into natural-language narrations via an LLM, with a separate LLM-as-judge verifying that all tracked mental states remain valid after each infilling step.

## Method

EXPLORETOM defines a ToM-specific domain-specific language where each action is a function transforming a state (world state, first-order beliefs, second-order beliefs) with explicit preconditions and belief-update rules, including novel asymmetric belief updates such as secret witnesses and distraction. It employs A* search over the space of valid action sequences, where the path cost g(s) is the target model's accuracy on automatically generated questions for partial story s, and the heuristic h(s) estimates the probability of fulfilling user constraints via Monte Carlo continuation sampling. This adversarial search prioritizes story structures that fool the target model while respecting diversity constraints, and outperforms over-generation-and-filtering baselines by 2 accuracy points with shorter stories. The separation of story structure (DSL actions) from lexical realization (LLM infilling) ensures reliable ground truth and distinguishes core social reasoning from vocabulary cues. Fine-tuning Llama-3.1 8B on 79,700 generated (story, question, answer) triples yields +27 points on ToMi and +29 on Hi-ToM, demonstrating generalization to more complex stories than seen during training.

## Results

EXPLORETOM-generated story structures are extremely challenging for frontier LLMs, with GPT-4o accuracy as low as 9% and Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct as low as 0% across various action sets (Table 1). Fine-tuning Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on 79,700 EXPLORETOM-generated (story, question, answer) triples yields a +27-point accuracy improvement on ToMi (68%→95%) and +29 points on Hi-ToM (30%→59%), while preserving general reasoning (MMLU: 69%→67%) and dialogue state tracking (Multi3Woz: 96%→96%). The fine-tuned model also generalizes to EXPLORETOM stories with more people (up to 5) and more actions (up to 11) than seen during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other synthetic data generation methods on the same fine-tuning protocol, but it does compare A* search against over-generation-and-filtering: A* yields a more challenging dataset (2 accuracy points lower) with shorter stories on average (1.6 fewer actions) under the same evaluation budget of 2,500 accuracy evaluations. For cross-model adversarial evaluation (Table 2), data generated adversarially for one model remains challenging for others (e.g., data optimized for Llama-3.1-70B still yields only 32% accuracy for Mixtral), confirming the robustness of the adversarial approach. On the fine-tuning side, EXPLORETOM-8B substantially outperforms the base Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on ToMi (+27 pts) and Hi-ToM (+29 pts), with modest gains on BigToM (+6 pts) and OpenToM (+0.07 F1), while showing a negligible drop on FANToM (−0.01). The paper also shows that training data must be biased toward 'interesting' questions (those requiring theory of mind) to achieve these gains: using 100% ToM-requiring stories is critical, whereas randomly sampled stories are 78–87% uninteresting and thus insufficient for improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of ToM benchmarking work (ToMi, Hi-ToM, FANToM, BigToM, OpenToM) by replacing their fixed, limited action vocabularies with a programmatically-defined domain-specific language that supports a much richer set of actions, asymmetric belief updates, and multi-interaction narratives. It departs from the standard over-generation-and-filtering paradigm for synthetic data by employing A* search over the space of story structures, using a target model's accuracy as the cost function to adversarially find challenging stories. The belief-tracking mechanism builds on Sclar et al. (2023)'s multi-character belief tracker, but the key conceptual departure is the introduction of asymmetric belief updates (secret witnesses, distracted observers) that prior ToM benchmarks did not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that frontier models (GPT-4o, Llama-3.1-70B) drop to 9% and 0% accuracy on adversarially generated ToM stories, combined with the discovery that pure state tracking is actually harder than recursive belief tracking for these models, reframes the bottleneck: models may lack the basic world-state bookkeeping that ToM reasoning presupposes. The demonstration that 78%+ of randomly sampled stories do not require ToM at all suggests that in-the-wild training data is structurally biased against the very skill being evaluated. Open questions include whether the framework scales to goal-oriented narratives (currently a stated limitation), whether the manual coding requirement for new actions limits practical adoption, and whether the adversarial findings generalize beyond the specific action sets tested.
