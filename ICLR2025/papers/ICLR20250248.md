## Problem

Existing multi-objective prompt optimization methods rely on scalarization functions (weighted sum, hypervolume, product) to collapse multiple objectives into a single reward, imposing rigid assumptions about trade-off structure and making performance sensitive to the specific metric used for each objective. This paper asks whether prompt optimization can be guided purely by pairwise dominance relationships between prompts, eliminating the need for any predefined scalarization and thereby avoiding assumptions about objective structure and metric sensitivity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text input instance x (e.g., a review, query, or code snippet) and a frozen task-specific LLM T, and produces a diverse set of discrete text prompts (5-token prefixes in classification; similar short prompts in style transfer) that collectively cover the Pareto front across multiple conflicting objectives. Objectives are evaluated by model-based metrics (CoLA grammaticality score, CTC content-preservation, BERT sentiment probability, classification accuracy), and the policy model is a small generative LM (DistilGPT2) with a 2-layer MLP adapter whose latent embeddings remain frozen. During training, prompts are generated in pairs per instance, and dominance is determined by comparing the multi-dimensional objective vectors of the corresponding LLM outputs.

## Method

ParetoPrompt trains a policy model via preference-based RL using two complementary loss functions applied to sampled prompt pairs. For dominant pairs (one prompt's output vector dominates the other's), it applies the standard DPO or IPO loss to increase the relative likelihood of the dominating prompt. For non-dominated (incomparable) pairs, it introduces a novel non-dominated loss l_nd = λ·max(|h(z₁,z₂;x)| − ε, 0) that penalizes large reward gaps while tolerating small ones, encouraging the policy to maintain diverse likelihoods across trade-off regions and thereby cover the entire Pareto front. The reference model is periodically updated to the current policy state, and the overall loss is an expectation over pairs sampled from the reference model, with the appropriate loss selected by the dominance relation. Because dominance is invariant to monotonic transformations of individual objectives, the method remains robust when training and testing metrics differ (e.g., perplexity vs. CoLA for fluency), a property that scalarized baselines lack.

## Results

On the three-objective text style transfer task, PP-IPO achieves the best Dominated Hypervolume (HV) of 2406 (±361) and the lowest Inverse Generation Distance (IGD) of 14.4 (±4.8), substantially outperforming scalarization-based baselines (Summation: HV 572, IGD 31.6; Product: HV 725, IGD 33.7; HVI: HV 1429, IGD 17.9). PP-IPO also generates the largest Pareto Set Size (34.8 non-dominated prompts out of 64), indicating superior coverage of the Pareto front. On the two-objective few-shot classification task, PP-IPO achieves the highest C-HV across all four datasets (e.g., 83.0 on MR, 48.2 on Yahoo) while maintaining the smallest Diff-HV (0.8 on Yelp-5, 1.3 on SST-5), demonstrating robustness to metric misalignment between training and testing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParetoPrompt (PP-IPO) decisively outperforms all scalarization-based RL baselines on the three-objective style transfer task: it achieves 4.2× higher HV than Summation (2406 vs. 572), 3.3× higher than Product (2406 vs. 725), and 1.7× higher than HVI (2406 vs. 1429), while also achieving the lowest IGD (14.4 vs. 31.6, 33.7, and 17.9 respectively). Against the evolutionary baseline InstOptima (NSGA-II), PP-IPO achieves higher HV (2406 vs. 1838) and lower IGD (14.4 vs. 17.1), though InstOptima achieves a higher Max Content score (77.5 vs. 82.9) and slightly lower IGD variance. On the two-objective classification task, PP-IPO and PP-DPO consistently achieve the highest C-HV across all four datasets (e.g., 83.0 vs. 77.2 for HVI on MR; 48.2 vs. 42.2 for HVI on Yahoo), while also showing the smallest Diff-HV (0.8–14.7) compared to scalarization methods (7.0–22.1), confirming that dominance-based learning is robust to non-linear metric transformations. The R-IPO baseline (which uses summed objectives for preference but IPO loss) underperforms PP-IPO on HV (1166 vs. 2406) and IGD (22.6 vs. 14.4), highlighting that the non-dominated loss function is critical for Pareto front coverage rather than merely exploiting preference signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParetoPrompt repurposes the DPO/IPO preference-optimization framework (Rafailov et al., 2024; Azar et al., 2024) from human-feedback alignment to multi-objective prompt search, replacing the scalar reward with a dominance-based preference signal between prompt pairs. It follows the RL-based discrete prompt generation paradigm established by RLPrompt (Deng et al., 2022) but departs from prior multi-objective prompt optimization work (Baumann & Kramer, 2024; Jafari et al., 2024) by eliminating scalarization functions entirely, relying instead on the ordinal dominance structure. The non-dominated loss function is a novel addition not present in DPO/IPO, designed specifically to encourage Pareto-front coverage rather than convergence to a single preferred region.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is robustness to metric misalignment: because dominance is invariant to monotonic transformations of objectives, ParetoPrompt performs consistently even when the training evaluation metric differs from the testing metric, whereas scalarization-based methods degrade substantially. This addresses a real deployment concern where objective metrics may be noisy, proxy-based, or unavailable at test time. The main open limitation is efficiency in many-objective settings, where dominant pairs become rare and the algorithm loses its primary learning signal; the authors suggest relaxed dominance relations as a future direction. Broader applicability to larger LLMs and more complex prompt spaces remains untested.
