## Problem

Learning multi-object manipulation policies from offline pixel demonstrations faces two compounding challenges: the combinatorial explosion of state and goal configurations, and the multi-modal nature of valid behaviors as object count increases. Existing approaches either rely on massive scale (2000+ GPU hours) without systematic compositional generalization, or use deterministic policies that collapse multi-modal behavior distributions. This paper asks whether combining object-centric representations with diffusion-based generation can achieve both sample efficiency and zero-shot generalization to unseen object compositions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images at 128×128 resolution (typically 2 views: front and side) for the current state, plus a goal image (the last observation in the demonstration trajectory). A frozen, pre-trained DLPv2 encoder decomposes each image into an unordered set of M latent particles (each encoding position, scale, depth, transparency, and visual features), with actions appended as additional particles in the set. The model outputs a denoised sequence of particle states and continuous end-effector delta actions over a short horizon (H=3); only the first action is executed per timestep in an MPC loop, while generated latent states serve as implicit planning signals and can be decoded back to images for visualization.

## Method

EC-Diffuser is a goal-conditioned diffusion model that jointly denoises object-centric particle states and actions using a permutation-equivariant Transformer. A pre-trained DLPv2 encoder factorizes each image into M latent particles with no cross-timestep or cross-view correspondence, necessitating a denoiser that omits intra-set positional embeddings and relies on AdaLN to condition on the diffusion timestep and action. The diffusion process operates over all particles and actions except the fixed current-state and goal-state particles, trained with an L1 noise-prediction loss. The key architectural insight is that co-generating future particle states alongside actions (rather than actions alone) provides implicit planning over object configurations and acts as an auxiliary objective ensuring the model tracks all objects, which is critical for multi-object performance and compositional generalization. The model is trained entirely offline via behavioral cloning and deployed with MPC-style re-planning at each timestep.

## Results

On PushCube, EC-Diffuser achieves 94.8% success rate with 1 object, 91.7% with 2, and 89.4% with 3 objects, while all unstructured baselines (VQ-BeT, Diffuser) drop below 1% at 3 objects. On PushT, EC-Diffuser reaches 0.805 avg. radian difference with 3 objects (random = π/2 ≈ 1.57), whereas all baselines remain near random (1.52–1.54). On FrankaKitchen, EC-Diffuser reaches 3.031/4 goals, matching EC Diffusion Policy (3.046) and outperforming VQ-BeT (2.384) and Diffuser (0.846). In compositional generalization (trained on 3 objects, tested on 6), EC-Diffuser maintains 71.1% success fraction while EC Diffusion Policy drops to 12.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EC-Diffuser decisively outperforms all four baselines on multi-object tasks. Against VQ-BeT and Diffuser (both using unstructured VQ-VAE/ResNet representations), the gap is extreme: at 3 PushCube objects, EC-Diffuser achieves 89.4% success vs. 0.6% and 0.2% respectively, and at 3 PushT objects it reaches 0.805 radian diff. vs. ~1.54 (random) for both. Against EIT+BC (same DLP input but deterministic policy), EC-Diffuser wins on all multi-object settings (e.g., 89.4% vs. 14.1% on PushCube-3; 0.805 vs. 1.526 on PushT-3), confirming that diffusion is critical for capturing multi-modal behavior distributions. The closest competitor is EC Diffusion Policy (same DLP input, diffusion-based, but action-only generation without state co-generation): EC-Diffuser leads on PushCube-3 (89.4% vs. 66.8%) and PushT-3 (0.805 vs. 1.538), but the two are essentially tied on FrankaKitchen (3.031 vs. 3.046 goals reached), suggesting that state generation matters most for combinatorial multi-object reasoning rather than single-scene kitchen tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three previously separate threads: (1) the Diffuser framework for joint state-action sequence generation via diffusion (Janner et al., 2022), (2) unsupervised object-centric image representations via Deep Latent Particles (Daniel & Tamar, 2022; 2024), and (3) entity-centric Transformer architectures for object manipulation (Haramati et al., 2024). The key departure is from prior diffusion-based BC methods (Diffuser, Diffusion Policy) that operate on unstructured pixel or VQ-VAE representations: EC-Diffuser instead generates sequences of object-centric particles and actions simultaneously, using a permutation-equivariant Transformer with AdaLN conditioning. The paper explicitly motivates this by showing that naively adapting the entity-centric RL Transformer (EIT) to a BC setting fails to capture multi-modal behavior distributions as object count increases, necessitating the diffusion formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot compositional generalization to 6 objects (trained on 3) with 71% per-object success fraction, versus 12% for the best baseline, demonstrates that object-centric structure plus diffusion can unlock generalization that scale-based approaches (requiring 2000+ GPU hours) have not achieved in BC settings. The ablation cleanly isolating state generation as critical for multi-object reasoning (89% → 53% at 3 cubes without it) provides actionable design guidance. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of unsupervised object-centric representations (DLP), which is harder to obtain in real-world scenes, and the iterative diffusion inference cost. Open questions include whether guided diffusion can extend this to offline RL, and whether the approach transfers to more visually complex real-world manipulation.
