## Problem

Existing RL-based red-teaming methods (REINFORCE, PPO+Novelty) suffer from mode collapse or fail to generate effective attacks against safety-tuned LLMs, even with explicit diversity regularization. The core difficulty is balancing attack effectiveness (toxicity rate) with prompt diversity, which becomes especially challenging when safety alignment makes the reward landscape sparse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252274/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires a pretrained attacker LM (GPT-2 or Llama-3.2-1B), a target LLM accessed strictly in black-box mode (prompt in, text response out), and a binary toxicity classifier (RoBERTa hate-speech or Llama-Guard) that scores prompt–response pairs. The attacker LM generates prompts autoregressively; the target LLM produces k responses per prompt, and the classifier's average log-probability of toxicity forms the reward signal. The final output is a trained attacker policy that samples diverse, effective attack prompts, which can additionally be used to safety-tune the target LLM via LoRA fine-tuning on generated refusal responses.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage procedure that decouples exploration from exploitation. Stage 1 fine-tunes the attacker LM as a GFlowNet policy using the trajectory balance objective, where the reward is a product of a tempered toxicity score (β) and a tempered reference-LM likelihood (γ), with off-policy exploration via a mix of a tempered policy and a replay buffer; high-reward prompts are collected during training. Stage 2 restarts from the original pretrained LM and performs MLE smoothing on the collected high-reward prompts, analogous to behavior cloning in offline RL, producing a smooth distribution that treats all collected attacks as equally important. The key insight is that GFlowNet's proportionality-to-reward sampling (rather than concentrating mass on the single maximum) discovers a broad set of effective attacks, while MLE smoothing yields a well-generalized policy that is robust to the temperature hyperparameters β and γ. The MLE stage is extremely cheap (<5% of total training time) and enables fast adaptation to new target LLMs by simply reranking the stored prompts with the new target's reward, skipping the expensive GFlowNet stage entirely.

## Results

GFlowNet + MLE achieves a source toxicity rate of 85.16% when red-teaming Gemma-2b-it, with strong transfer to unseen target LLMs (22–77% across 10 models), far exceeding all baselines. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, scaling the attacker from GPT-2-small to Llama-3.2-1B improves toxicity rate from 81.05% to 92.71% while maintaining high diversity (cosine distance 0.833). For safety tuning, a Gemma-2b-it model fine-tuned on GFlowNet+MLE-generated prompts reduces toxicity to 3.75% against GFlowNet+MLE attacks, while models tuned with other RL methods remain vulnerable (81–92% toxicity). The MLE smoothing stage (Stage 2) takes under 2 hours and less than 5% of total training time, yet dramatically improves both diversity and toxicity over GFlowNet alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFlowNet + MLE dominates all three primary baselines on both effectiveness and transferability. Against PPO+Novelty (Hong et al., 2024), which adds novelty-based rewards and entropy bonuses to PPO, GFlowNet+MLE achieves 85.16% source toxicity versus 0.00% for PPO+Novelty on Gemma-2b-it, and transfers at 22–77% to unseen models versus 0% for PPO+Novelty across all targets. Against REINFORCE (Williams, 1992), which achieves 98.45% source toxicity by collapsing to a single deterministic prompt, GFlowNet+MLE sacrifices only ~13% source toxicity but gains massive transferability (22–77% vs. 0% on 9 of 10 transfer targets). Against ICL (Brown et al., 2020), which generates diverse but weak prompts (18.31% source, 7–27% transfer), GFlowNet+MLE achieves 4.7× higher source toxicity and 2–4× higher transfer rates. The paper also shows that GFlowNet alone (Stage 1 without MLE smoothing) achieves only 11.57% source toxicity, confirming that the MLE smoothing step is critical for converting exploratory GFlowNet samples into a high-performing attacker policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021; Malkin et al., 2022) to the red-teaming task, building directly on the GFlowNet fine-tuning approach for LLMs introduced by Hu et al. (2024) for amortized inference. It departs from prior RL-based red-teaming methods (Perez et al., 2022; Hong et al., 2024) that use on-policy algorithms like PPO with novelty rewards, arguing that these suffer from mode collapse or fail to balance diversity with effectiveness. The key methodological addition is a second-stage MLE smoothing step inspired by behavior cloning in offline RL (Emmons et al., 2022; Jang et al., 2021), which re-trains the attacker on high-reward prompts collected during GFlowNet exploration, yielding a smooth distribution over diverse attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that diverse attack prompts transfer substantially across architecturally different LLMs (e.g., 65% success against GPT-4o, 52% against Llama-3-70b-instruct) reveals shared failure modes in current safety alignment and suggests that a single diverse red-teaming dataset can harden multiple models. The practical implication is that the cheap MLE reranking step enables rapid adaptation to new target models without re-running expensive GFlowNet training. Limitations include dependence on a proxy toxicity classifier (which can be gamed, as shown in the RoBERTa ablation), the need for online sampling during Stage 1, and the fact that true harmfulness is subjective and context-dependent. Open directions include extending to multimodal models, the jailbreaking setting, and richer measures of harmfulness.
