## Problem

Identifying latent causal processes from time series with instantaneous dependencies has required impractical assumptions: interventions on latent variables (iCITRIS) or expert-defined grouping of observations (G-CaRL). This paper asks whether a more natural and easily verifiable assumption—sparsity in the latent transition dynamics—can suffice for identifiability without either of these external requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251257/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series observations x_t ∈ R^n (e.g., sensor recordings of human joint trajectories) generated from latent variables z_t via an invertible nonlinear mixing function g. It outputs identified latent variables up to permutation and component-wise invertible transformation, the full latent causal DAG including both time-delayed and instantaneous edges, and downstream time series forecasts. Identification leverages contextual information from L consecutive timestamps (typically L=2 or 3), and the sparse latent process assumption requires that each latent variable's intimate neighbor set in the Markov network over the context window is empty.

## Method

The theoretical core establishes that under a sparse latent process assumption (empty intimate neighbor sets) plus sufficient variability of temporal data, the latent Markov network is identifiable up to isomorphism and latent variables are component-wise identifiable, using the connection between conditional independence and zero cross-derivatives (Lin, 1997) applied to multi-timestamp context windows. The IDOL model implements this via a temporally variational inference architecture: CNN-based encoder/decoder for reconstruction, prior networks parameterized by normalizing flows that estimate per-component noise terms ε̂_{t,i} from (ẑ_t, ẑ_{t-1}), and a gradient-based L1 sparsity penalty applied to the Jacobian blocks J_d (time-delayed) and J_e (instantaneous) to enforce sparse causal influences. The total loss combines the reconstruction term, KL divergence between posterior and prior, and the sparsity regularizer, trained end-to-end in an unsupervised manner. A mild additional condition (non-identical time-delayed parents for adjacent instantaneous pairs) extends identification from the Markov equivalence class to the full causal graph via v-structure orientation.

## Results

On six synthetic datasets (A–F) with 3–16 latent variables, IDOL achieves the highest MCC (Matthews Correlation Coefficient) on 5 out of 6 datasets, reaching 0.9801 on dataset C and 0.9747 on dataset F (16 latent variables), while TDRL is marginally better on dataset D (0.9796 vs. 0.9766) where no instantaneous effects exist. On real-world human motion forecasting (Human3.6M and HumanEva-I), IDOL outperforms all 8 baselines on most motions, with MSE improvements of 4%–34% over the strongest competitor (e.g., 0.1977 vs. 0.2091 MSE on Walking-75 in Human3.6M, and 0.0193 vs. 0.0207 on Walking-375 in HumanEva-I). The method degrades on dataset E (dense latent process violating the sparsity assumption A3), confirming the theoretical boundary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDOL outperforms the two methods specifically designed for instantaneous dependencies—G-CaRL (Morioka & Hyvärinen, 2023) and iCITRIS (Lippe et al., 2023)—by large margins (e.g., 0.9801 vs. 0.5850 MCC on dataset C; 0.9747 vs. 0.5225 on dataset F), demonstrating that sparsity of the latent process is a weaker and more practical assumption than grouping or interventions. Against TDRL (Yao et al., 2022), which assumes no instantaneous effects, IDOL wins on all datasets with instantaneous dependencies (A, B, C, F) but is marginally outperformed on dataset D (0.9766 vs. 0.9796) where TDRL's no-instantaneous-effect assumption is exactly satisfied. On real-world human motion forecasting, IDOL beats all 8 deep forecasting baselines (Autoformer, TimesNet, MICN, CARD, FITS, iTransformer) and TDRL on most motions, with the largest gains on complex motions like SittingDown and Walking (up to 34% MSE reduction vs. CARD). The only notable exception is the small CatchThrow dataset where IDOL is comparable to TDRL due to insufficient data. On the dense dataset E (violating the sparsity assumption), IDOL still leads but with reduced performance (0.7869), confirming the theoretical boundary of the method.

## Relation to Prior Work

IDOL extends the temporally causal representation learning line initiated by TDRL (Yao et al., 2022), which assumes no instantaneous dependencies among latent variables, to the more general case where instantaneous effects are present. Rather than adopting the stronger assumptions of iCITRIS (interventions on latent variables) or G-CaRL (grouping of observations), IDOL introduces a 'sparse latent process' assumption—sparse causal influences in the latent transition—which is natural in systems like human skeletal motion. The variational inference architecture with prior networks is inherited from TDRL and Chen et al. (2024a), while the intimate neighbor set concept from Zhang et al. (2024) is adapted to the temporal setting to establish component-wise identifiability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that sparsity in the latent transition—a property that naturally holds in many real-world time series (e.g., human joints, sensor networks)—is sufficient for identifiability without requiring expensive interventions or expert-defined groupings. This opens the door to causal representation learning in settings where such auxiliary information is unavailable. However, the method is limited to low-dimensional latent spaces and requires an invertible mixing function, both of which restrict applicability to high-dimensional or lossy observation processes. Open questions include whether the sparsity assumption can be relaxed further, how to handle non-invertible mixing, and whether the approach scales to high-dimensional time series.
