## Problem

Current black-box adversarial attacks on LLMs rely solely on hard-label feedback (the final prediction), forcing inefficient top-down search strategies that require many model queries. The paper asks whether the emergent ability of LLMs to express their own uncertainty via confidence elicitation can be exploited as a richer, soft-label-like feedback signal within a purely black-box threat model, enabling more directed and efficient adversarial optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250058/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sample and a two-step confidence elicitation prompt: first requesting k best-guess predictions (e.g., 20 for SST-2/AG-News, 6 for StrategyQA), then requesting verbal confidence levels ('Highest' through 'Lowest') for each guess. The output is an adversarial text sample with word substitutions that flips the model's classification, along with a Dirichlet-distribution-based probability estimate tracking how confidence shifts across perturbation steps. Perturbations are constrained by a semantic similarity threshold (ε=0.84 via Universal Sentence Encoder) and a maximum of |W|=5 words to substitute.

## Method

The core mechanism is a bottom-up greedy hill-climbing attack guided by elicited confidence. For each candidate word in a subset W of the input, the algorithm generates synonym substitutions from counter-fitted embeddings (set S), queries the model with each perturbed sample using the two-step confidence elicitation prompt, and aggregates the k predictions and k verbal confidences into a Dirichlet α-vector to compute an approximate class probability (the Dirichlet mean). A substitution is accepted only if it strictly decreases the mean probability of the current predicted class; otherwise the algorithm moves to the next word. This contrasts with prior hard-label methods (e.g., SSPAttack) that first over-perturb to force a misclassification and then optimize quality top-down. The approach requires only a single model call per perturbation candidate (versus M calls for self-consistency baselines), and the temperature is set to ~0 for deterministic behavior. The paper demonstrates that the elicited confidence is sufficiently calibrated (low ECE, high AUROC) on LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 to serve as a reliable optimization signal, achieving state-of-the-art ASR with fewer queries and higher semantic similarity than existing hard-label baselines.

## Results

CEAttack achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all six model–dataset combinations. On LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, ASR reaches 19.73% (SST-2), 30.74% (AG-News), and 45.67% (StrategyQA); on Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, it reaches 17.94%, 38.33%, and 39.26% respectively. The corresponding Accuracy Under Attack (AUA) drops to as low as 32.67% (LLaMA-3, StrategyQA) and 36.21% (Mistral, StrategyQA) from clean accuracies of ~60%. CEAttack also requires fewer queries per successful attack (e.g., 25.60 vs. 82.95 for SSPAttack on LLaMA-3 SST-2) while maintaining semantic similarity ≥ 0.84.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CEAttack outperforms all three baselines on ASR in every model–dataset setting. Against SSPAttack (the strongest hard-label baseline), CEAttack gains +10.0 pp on LLaMA-3 StrategyQA (45.67% vs. 29.67%), +18.3 pp on Mistral AG-News (38.33% vs. 20.00%), and +10.0 pp on LLaMA-3 SST-2 (19.73% vs. 9.73%). Against TextHoaxer, the margin is similar or larger (e.g., +21.0 pp on LLaMA-3 StrategyQA). Self-Fool Word Sub, which performs no iterative optimization, is consistently the weakest baseline (ASR ≤ 3.57%). In addition to higher ASR, CEAttack achieves comparable or better semantic similarity (SemSim 0.88–0.93 vs. 0.87–0.94 for baselines) and requires substantially fewer queries per successful attack (e.g., 25.60 vs. 82.95 for SSPAttack on LLaMA-3 SST-2), translating to roughly 2–3× shorter total attack time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes confidence elicitation—a technique developed to improve LLM trustworthiness (Xiong et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2022; Tian et al., 2023)—as a feedback signal for adversarial optimization, effectively converting a hard-label black-box attack into something approximating a soft-label attack. It departs from prior hard-label methods (SSPAttack, TextHoaxer) that use a top-down strategy of over-perturbing then refining, instead adopting a bottom-up greedy search guided by elicited confidence. The Dirichlet aggregation of multiple verbal confidence estimates (from k repeated queries) is a novel mechanism for constructing a probability-like signal from purely textual model outputs, bridging the gap between the unavailable logits of closed-source models and the gradient-free optimization landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a fundamental tension: the community's push to make LLMs express calibrated confidence (for risk assessment, hallucination mitigation, selective generation) simultaneously creates a new attack surface that did not exist in purely hard-label settings. This has direct implications for safety-critical deployments (healthcare, clinical support) where confidence is surfaced to users. Open questions include whether the technique extends to multi-turn jailbreaks, generative/reasoning tasks, and token-level confidence, and whether adversarial training against elicited-confidence-guided perturbations can close the gap. A key limitation is that the approach requires the target model to be reasonably calibrated in its verbal confidence, and absolute ASR remains moderate (19–46%), suggesting the attack is more of a vulnerability signal than an immediate practical threat.
