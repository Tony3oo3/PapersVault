## Problem

Automatic robot design has been stuck at two anatomical extremes—fully rigid jointed bodies and fully soft jointless bodies—leaving a vast gap where no automated method has produced endoskeletal robots that combine deformable soft tissues with internal rigid skeletons and joints. The core difficulty is the hierarchical, combinatorial coupling between discrete morphological structure and continuous controller optimization, which prior simulators and encodings could not jointly handle for freeform, multi-physics bodies.

## Input / Output

The generative model takes a 512-dimensional latent vector and decodes it into a 64×64×64 one-hot voxel grid where each cell is labeled as empty, soft tissue, or one of K rigid bones; an automatic jointification step then extracts hinge joints at bone-bone contact surfaces via PCA bounding boxes. The universal controller consumes a heterogeneous sensory state—per-voxel strain, position, and velocity from soft tissues, plus per-bone position, quaternion orientation, linear/angular velocity, and per-joint angle, axis, and limits—and outputs a discrete joint-angle command from {−1.4, −0.7, 0, 0.7, 1.4} rad for every joint simultaneously.

## Method

The pipeline couples three components trained jointly: (1) a voxel-based multiphysics simulator that models soft tissue as a grid of Euler-Bernoulli beams (preserving volume under torsion) directly attached to rigid bones whose joint constraints are solved as a linear complementarity problem via projected Gauss-Seidel; (2) a VAE with four Voxception-ResNet blocks trained on an unbounded stream of procedurally generated multi-star-graph body plans, yielding a smooth 512-dim latent manifold that encodes both external soft geometry and internal skeletal topology; and (3) a universal PPO controller that spatially pools high-resolution voxel-level soft-tissue sensing into bone-center nodes (aligning it with the skeletal graph), then processes the combined rigid+soft graph with a graph transformer whose edge features drive per-joint actor heads and whose max-pooled node features feed a global critic. Morphology is optimized by CMA-ES over the latent space while the single shared controller is retrained for 20 RL epochs each generation, so that evolution and learning co-adapt: the controller generalizes across the evolving population's diverse topologies, and evolution is steered toward morphologies that are easier to control.

## Results

Across four task environments (Flat Ground, Upright Locomotion, Potholes, Mountain Range), evolved design populations significantly outperform the randomly-generated initial populations after 30 generations (600 total RL epochs). In the Upright Locomotion task, the best evolved designs achieve substantially higher peak fitness than initial random designs, with the evolved population at generation 23 outperforming generation 0 under both re-optimized and from-scratch controller conditions. The universal controller trained on 4 NVIDIA H100 GPUs completes 451 epochs in 44.8 hours, compared to only 30 epochs for 64 individual controllers in the same time, yet achieves comparable performance over the first 30 epochs. Random morphological search (without CMA-ES) performs significantly worse than evolutionary strategies, confirming that cumulative selection across generations is necessary for effective design optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external robot design methods but instead runs internal control experiments to isolate the contribution of each component. (1) Evolved vs. initial designs: when both populations are frozen and the controller is re-optimized (or trained from scratch), evolved designs significantly outperform initial random designs, demonstrating that morphological evolution pushes search into higher-fitness regions of latent space. (2) CMA-ES vs. random morphological search: replacing evolutionary strategies with random search yields significantly lower fitness, confirming that mutation-and-selection is necessary rather than merely beneficial. (3) Universal vs. individual controllers: over the first 30 RL epochs, the universal controller's performance is not statistically different from 64 individually-trained controllers, but the universal controller is ~15× more sample-efficient (451 vs. 30 epochs in the same wall-clock time on 4 H100 GPUs). (4) Discrete vs. continuous action space: both are viable, but the discrete 5-level action space {−1.4, −0.7, 0, 0.7, 1.4 rad} greatly simplifies and stabilizes universal policy training compared to the continuous [−1.4, 1.4] rad range.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two long-standing but separate lines of automatic robot design: fully rigid jointed bodies (Sims 1994; Lipson & Pollack 2000; Gupta et al. 2022) and fully soft jointless bodies (Hiller & Lipson 2012; Cheney et al. 2018; Matthews et al. 2023). It builds on the Euler-Bernoulli beam model of Hiller & Lipson (2014) for soft tissue mechanics, Baraff's (2001) constrained rigid-body dynamics for joints, and the graph-transformer universal controller of Metamorph (Gupta et al. 2022), but extends the latter from rigid stick figures to freeform endoskeletal bodies with arbitrary soft-tissue geometry. The VAE-based latent encoding departs from the CPPN approach used in prior evolutionary robot design (Hiller & Lipson 2010; Cheney et al. 2013) by providing a continuous, smooth manifold amenable to CMA-ES, while spatial pooling (Peng et al. 2020) aligns voxel-level mechanosensory data to the skeletal graph for the first time in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first open-ended platform for co-designing freeform endoskeletal robots, filling a genuine gap between the rigid and soft robot design literatures and offering a plug-and-play benchmark for evolutionary design and representation learning in hierarchical embodied systems. The demonstration that a single universal controller generalizes across 64 wildly different morphologies, and that morphological evolution (not random search) is necessary to reach high-fitness regions of latent space, validates the co-design paradigm. Key limitations include the absence of physical fabrication, fluid dynamics, and external rigid structures, leaving sim-to-real transfer of universal controllers as an important open question. The open-endedness of the platform invites future work on more complex terrains, object manipulation, and cognitive behaviors.
