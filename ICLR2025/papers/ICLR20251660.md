## Problem

Generating spatial audio (first-order ambisonics) directly from silent videos remains unsolved: existing video-to-audio models produce only mono audio, while audio spatialization models require a reference mono signal that is unavailable for silent videos. Naively chaining these two stages introduces compounding errors, as the generated mono audio may deviate from the ground-truth distribution, leading to inaccurate spatialization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251660/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes field-of-view (FoV) video frames sampled at 4 FPS and a camera direction (azimuth φ, elevation θ) as input, producing 5-second first-order ambisonics at 44.1 kHz across four channels (W omnidirectional, X/Y/Z directional). The camera direction is essential because FoV alone lacks information about where in the 3D environment the visual events occur; it anchors the generated sound field to the correct spatial orientation. The dataset (YT-Ambigen) provides 102K paired 5-second clips with all four spatial channels and ground-truth camera directions.

## Method

ViSAGe is a transformer encoder-decoder that autoregressively generates Descript Audio Codec (DAC) codes for all four ambisonics channels simultaneously. The encoder fuses CLIP ViT-B/32 embeddings (semantics), patchwise energy maps derived from spatial/temporal saliency of CLIP patch embeddings (fine-grained object location and motion), and a direction embedding from the camera unit vector. The key architectural innovation is a code generation pattern that interleaves omnidirectional (W) and spatial (X,Y,Z) codebook groups across 2L_c+1 steps, jointly modeling residual dependency within RVQ and spatial dependency between channel groups—halving the sequence length compared to naive sequential generation. Training uses cross-entropy on DAC codes with classifier-free guidance applied jointly to both directional and visual conditions (scale ω=2.5), plus 90° azimuth rotation augmentation to disentangle visual features from viewing direction. The model (360M params, 6 encoder / 12 decoder layers) is pretrained on VGGSound for mono audio generation, then finetuned on YT-Ambigen.

## Results

On YT-Ambigen, ViSAGe with both directional and visual guidance achieves the best semantic quality (FAD_dec = 3.86, KLD_dec = 1.71, FAD_avg = 4.20), substantially outperforming the best two-stage baseline (Diff-Foley + Ambi Enc.: FAD_dec = 5.68, KLD_dec = 2.60, FAD_avg = 5.53). ViSAGe with directional-only guidance achieves the best spatial coherence (CC_all = 0.721, AUC_all = 0.890), surpassing the strongest spatial baseline (SpecVQGAN + Audio Spatial.: CC_all = 0.619, AUC_all = 0.848). The full model (Directional & Visual) maintains competitive spatial metrics (CC_all = 0.635, AUC_all = 0.846) while delivering the strongest semantic fidelity, demonstrating a favorable balance between the two objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViSAGe is compared against two two-stage pipelines: (1) SpecVQGAN or Diff-Foley for mono audio generation followed by manual ambisonic encoding (Ambi Enc.), and (2) the same V2A models followed by a learned U-Net spatializer (Audio Spatial.). On semantic metrics, ViSAGe (Directional & Visual) achieves FAD_dec = 3.86 and KLD_dec = 1.71, beating the best baseline (Diff-Foley + Ambi Enc. at FAD_dec = 5.68, KLD_dec = 2.60) by a wide margin, and outperforming the spatialization-based baselines (FAD_dec = 6.40–7.24) even more substantially. On spatial metrics, ViSAGe (Directional) leads all baselines with CC_all = 0.721 and AUC_all = 0.890, exceeding the best spatial baseline (SpecVQGAN + Audio Spatial. at CC_all = 0.619, AUC_all = 0.848) by 10.2% in CC and 4.2% in AUC. The two-stage approaches exhibit a clear trade-off: manual encoding preserves semantic quality but yields near-random spatial maps (CC ≈ 0.35), while the learned spatializer captures spatial structure at the cost of significant audio fidelity degradation (FAD_avg up to 8.76). ViSAGe avoids this trade-off, achieving the best or near-best values on both semantic and spatial metrics simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViSAGe sits at the intersection of two established lines of work—video-to-audio generation (SpecVQGAN, Diff-Foley, FoleyGen, MaskVAT) and audio spatialization with visual cues (Mono2Binaural, SpatialAudioGen, Lim & Nam)—but departs from both by eliminating the intermediate mono-audio stage entirely. Its closest architectural ancestor is FoleyGen, which uses CLIP features and an autoregressive transformer over neural audio codec codes; ViSAGe extends this to simultaneously generate four spatial channels and introduces a novel interleaved code generation pattern that jointly models residual (within-channel) and spatial (cross-channel) dependencies. The patchwise energy map computation is directly adapted from PAVER's spatial-temporal saliency formulation for 360° video, repurposed here to provide fine-grained spatial conditioning for FoV inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end framework and large-scale dataset (102K clips, 142h) for generating first-order ambisonics directly from silent FoV videos, filling a gap that two-stage pipelines (generate mono → spatialize) address only imperfectly due to distribution shift and fidelity loss. The proposed spatial evaluation metrics (CC/AUC on spherical energy maps with oversampling correction) are a practical contribution that the field lacked for generation tasks where ground-truth alignment is not guaranteed. Key limitations include restriction to FOA (4 channels) and 5-second clips, FoV-only conditioning (no panoramic input), and the relatively modest spatial accuracy gains over the best two-stage baseline when visual guidance is applied. Open questions include whether the code generation pattern generalizes to higher-order ambisonics or longer sequences, and whether the end-to-end advantage persists at scale with more powerful backbones.
