## Problem

De novo peptide sequencing, which infers peptide sequences directly from MS2 spectra without relying on pre-existing databases, still suffers from performance far below what is needed for large-scale proteomics applications. Database search methods achieve higher accuracy by leveraging existing peptide-spectrum matches, but cannot identify peptides absent from their databases. The paper asks whether the retrieval-based strength of database search can be integrated into a de novo framework to close this performance gap while retaining the ability to predict novel peptides.

## Input / Output

The model takes a variable-length MS2 spectrum (each peak represented as an (m/z, intensity) pair) and a precursor (m/z value plus integer charge state 1–10) as input, and autoregressively generates a variable-length peptide sequence over the 20 canonical amino acids. During inference, at each decoding step the model also queries a datastore of context-feature–amino-acid pairs built from training data, retrieving K nearest neighbors by Euclidean distance to form a temperature-weighted probability distribution that augments the decoder's own logits.

## Method

ReNovo follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) supervised training of a transformer-based MS2 Encoder + Peptide Decoder (with sinusoidal m/z encoding and a linear intensity projection) using teacher forcing and cross-entropy loss; (2) a datastore building stage where the trained model processes all training spectra to produce (context feature, ground-truth amino acid) key-value pairs; (3) a retrieval-based inference stage where, at each autoregressive step, the current context feature is used to retrieve K nearest neighbors from the datastore, and a temperature-scaled exponential-of-distance weighting over the retrieved amino acids yields a retrieval probability that is combined with the decoder's own output via a residual connection. The key insight is that peptide-level knowledge is stored explicitly in a plug-and-play datastore rather than being compressed into model parameters, allowing the model to 'memorize' context-to-amino-acid mappings from training data while still generalizing to unseen peptides. Ablations confirm the retrieval mechanism contributes substantially, and the datastore incurs only ~2.3% additional time and 6–17 GB of storage.

## Results

ReNovo achieves the best peptide-level precision across all three datasets: 0.278 (Seven-species), 0.568 (Nine-species), and 0.467 (HC-PT), outperforming the strongest baselines by 18.8%, 9.86%, and 11.46% respectively (average 13.31%). In peptide-level AUC, ReNovo reaches 0.228, 0.528, and 0.436, surpassing the best baselines by 31.79%, 12.58%, and 16.89% (average 20.42%). At the amino acid level, ReNovo improves precision by up to 5.49% and recall by up to 8.9% over the best baselines, with an average improvement of 4.12% across all amino acid-level metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReNovo outperforms all six baselines (DeepNovo, PointNovo, CasaNovo, InstaNovo, HelixNovo, AdaNovo) on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is HelixNovo, which ReNovo surpasses by 18.8% in peptide precision on Seven-species (0.278 vs. 0.234), 9.86% on Nine-species (0.568 vs. 0.517), and 11.46% on HC-PT (0.467 vs. 0.356). On Nine-species, AdaNovo is the second-best in peptide AUC (0.469) but ReNovo leads by 12.58% (0.528). PointNovo is competitive on HC-PT peptide precision (0.419) but ReNovo still leads by 11.46%. CasaNovo and DeepNovo trail significantly on all datasets, with CasaNovo achieving only 0.211 peptide precision on HC-PT versus ReNovo's 0.467. InstaNovo is compared only in inference time (Table 2) rather than in the main accuracy table, where it is the fastest model (0.03–0.04 s/step) but not evaluated on accuracy metrics in Table 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReNovo builds on the transformer-based de novo peptide sequencing lineage (CasaNovo, HelixNovo, AdaNovo) for its MS2 encoder and peptide decoder backbone, while introducing a retrieval mechanism inspired by kNN-LM and retrieval-augmented generation from NLP. The key conceptual departure is constructing a datastore of context-feature-to-target-amino-acid pairs from training data and querying it during inference, effectively bridging the gap between database search methods (which rely on external peptide databases) and pure de novo methods (which rely solely on learned parameters). Unlike RAG systems that retrieve from external or domain-specific sources, ReNovo's datastore is built exclusively from the model's own training data, preserving the de novo property while gaining the benefit of explicit memorized knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The retrieval-based paradigm offers a practical path toward closing the performance gap between de novo sequencing and database search, which has limited the adoption of de novo methods in large-scale proteomics. The finding that peptide-level gains (~13–20%) substantially exceed amino acid-level gains (~4%) despite retrieval operating at the amino acid level suggests the datastore captures sequence-level context that parameterized models miss. The datastore-size experiment (Table 9) showing that simply appending more training data to the datastore—without retraining—yields a large additional boost (0.568→0.696 peptide precision) highlights a plug-and-play scalability property. Open questions include how the approach scales to very large datastores, whether the retrieval mechanism transfers to other sequencing or generation tasks, and whether the modest 1.5–2× inference time overhead remains acceptable in high-throughput proteomics pipelines.
