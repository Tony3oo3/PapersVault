## Problem

Harmful fine-tuning attacks can break LLM safety alignment with just a small percentage of poisoned data in user uploads, yet existing alignment-stage defenses (Vaccine, RepNoise) fail to address the root cause. The paper identifies that 'harmful perturbation'—taking gradient steps on harmful data—drastically reduces harmful loss and is the actual mechanism behind alignment failure, but no prior defense explicitly attenuates this loss-reduction rate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252415/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two datasets during a one-time alignment stage: 5000 alignment pairs (harmful prompt + safe refusal answer) and 5000 harmful pairs (harmful prompt + harmful answer) from BeaverTails, plus the base LLM weights (Llama2-7B, Gemma2-9B, or Qwen2-7B). Training uses LoRA (rank 32, alpha 4) with AdamW. The output is a single aligned model that requires no additional computation at fine-tuning time, making the overhead a one-time cost amortized over all subsequent user fine-tuning requests.

## Method

Booster augments the standard alignment loss f(w) with a meta-learning-inspired regularizer λ·(h(w) − h(w − α∇h(w)/‖∇h(w)‖)) that minimizes the harmful loss reduction after one normalized gradient step on harmful data, directly attenuating the harmful perturbation that causes alignment to break. The exact gradient of this term requires second-order (Hessian) information, but Booster approximates the inner gradient as constant (following MAML/implicit gradient techniques), reducing the update to three forward/backward passes per step: one for alignment gradient, one for harmful gradient, and one for the post-perturbation harmful gradient. This makes the method a one-time alignment-stage intervention that 'vaccinates' the model so that subsequent fine-tuning on mixed benign/harmful data produces a much smaller drop in safety, without incurring per-request overhead.

## Results

On the default SST2 fine-tuning task with 10% harmful data (p=0.1, n=1000), Booster achieves a harmful score of 8.30% versus 33.70% for SFT, 23.70% for Lisa, 32.10% for RepNoise, and 28.30% for Vaccine, while maintaining 93.23% finetune accuracy. Across four fine-tuning datasets (SST2, AGNEWS, GSM8K, AlpacaEval), Booster achieves an average harmful score of 14.63% and 60.68% average finetune accuracy, outperforming all baselines. On Qwen2-7B, Booster achieves an exceptional 1.60% harmful score with 95.64% finetune accuracy, demonstrating strong generalization to SOTA architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Booster outperforms SFT by 22.64% in average harmful score (10.94 vs 33.58) while also achieving 2.64% higher finetune accuracy (93.03 vs 90.39) on SST2. Compared to the two alignment-stage baselines, Booster reduces average harmful score by 17.26% over Vaccine (10.94 vs 28.20) and 20.08% over RepNoise (10.94 vs 31.02), while matching or exceeding their finetune accuracy. Against Lisa (a fine-tuning-stage solution), Booster achieves a slightly higher harmful score in some settings (e.g., 8.30 vs 23.70 on SST2 at p=0.1, but 9.50 vs 10.00 on GSM8K) yet consistently delivers significantly higher finetune accuracy (e.g., 18.05 vs 12.00 on GSM8K). The combination Booster+Lisa further reduces harmful score to 1.88% average on GSM8K while maintaining 13.83% finetune accuracy, outperforming Lisa alone on both metrics. Booster's advantage is most pronounced on SST2 and AGNEWS, while on GSM8K and AlpacaEval the gap over Vaccine narrows, though Booster still maintains the best finetune accuracy across all four tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Booster carves new ground within the alignment-stage defense family by repurposing MAML-style inner-loop gradient steps for safety alignment, identifying 'harmful perturbation' (a single normalized gradient step on harmful data) as the mechanistic root cause of alignment breaking. Conceptually, it sits alongside Vaccine (which targets embedding drift) and RepNoise (which degrades harmful embeddings to Gaussian noise), but departs from both by directly constraining the harmful loss reduction rate rather than the embedding geometry. The closest concurrent work is TAR, which also simulates harmful perturbation via meta-learning, but Booster minimizes the loss *reduction gap* rather than the post-perturbation loss itself, a distinction the authors argue is critical for training stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Alignment-stage defenses are the most practically attractive category for LLM service providers because the overhead is a one-time cost amortized over millions of fine-tuning requests, and Booster demonstrates that this category can achieve near-fine-tuning-stage defense quality (e.g., 1.88 average harmful score with Booster+Lisa on GSM8K). However, the paper acknowledges a fundamental limitation shared by all alignment-stage solutions: harmful score still rises monotonically with the fraction of harmful data, and recent work shows existing alignment-stage defenses fail in corner attack cases. Open questions include whether the 3× alignment-stage compute overhead is acceptable at production scale, whether the method generalizes to more sophisticated multi-step or adaptive attacks, and whether the benign-data perturbation direction identified in Appendix G.1 can further close the gap.
