## Problem

Decomposition-based neural MOCO methods decompose a multi-objective problem into scalarized subproblems, each associated with a weight vector, but existing approaches (hypernetworks, meta-learning, size-aware decoders) still suffer from significant optimality gaps on these subproblems. The root cause is the failure to effectively capture weight-instance interaction—simultaneously leveraging both the weight vector and the problem instance information—within a single model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251739/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a problem instance (n nodes with Z-dimensional features: 2M coordinates for TSP, 3-D for CVRP/KP) and a weight vector λ ∈ R^M (generated via Das-Dennis, N=101 for bi-objective, N=105 for tri-objective) as joint input, and autoregressively constructs a solution for the corresponding scalarized subproblem. At inference, N subproblems are solved in parallel to approximate the Pareto set; the same single model handles all weight vectors and, when trained across sizes, generalizes to unseen problem sizes without any size-specific embedding.

## Method

The core contribution is a 'neat weight embedding' that directly feeds the weight vector into a standard POMO encoder-decoder to learn weight-specific representations, eliminating the need for hypernetworks, meta-learning, or separate per-weight models. Two instantiations are proposed: (1) WE-Add, which simply adds a linear projection of λ to the initial node embeddings before the encoder, and (2) WE-CA, which uses a FiLM-style feature-wise affine transformation to condition node embeddings on the weight embedding at every encoder layer, followed by joint multi-head attention updates of both weight and node embeddings. Training uses REINFORCE with a shared baseline over 200 epochs, and the unified cross-size variant samples instances of varying sizes during training. The key design principle is that weight-instance interaction occurs through the entire model (encoder and decoder) rather than being confined to a single component, and no additional hyperparameters or size embeddings are introduced.

## Results

On the largest problem sizes, WE-CA achieves gaps of 0.40% (Bi-TSP100), 0.24% (Bi-CVRP100), 0.00% (Bi-KP200), and 1.19% (Tri-TSP100) in hypervolume relative to the best reference, while WE-CA-Aug reaches 0.00% on all four. The unified cross-size model WE-CA-U attains average gaps of 0.26% (Bi-TSP), 0.32% (Bi-CVRP), 0.03% (Bi-KP), and 0.76% (Tri-TSP), approaching the performance of size-specific training. Inference time for 200 instances is 6–25 s for WE-CA versus hours for non-learnable heuristics like WS-LKH (6.0 h on Bi-TSP100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WE-CA substantially outperforms the previous single-model SOTA PMOCO on Bi-CVRP100 (0.24% vs 4.14% gap) and Tri-TSP100 (1.19% vs 3.53%), while matching or slightly exceeding it on Bi-TSP100 (0.40% vs 1.56%) and Bi-KP200 (0.00% vs 0.17%). Against CNH, the SOTA method designed for cross-size generalization, WE-CA wins on Bi-TSP100 (0.40% vs 0.68%), Bi-CVRP100 (0.24% vs 0.42%), and Tri-TSP100 (1.19% vs 2.08%), while CNH has a marginal edge on Bi-KP200 (0.08% vs 0.00% — actually WE-CA ties at 0.00%). Among multi-model methods, EMNH and MDRL achieve 0.00% gap on Bi-KP200, matching WE-CA, but WE-CA surpasses them on all routing problems (e.g., 0.24% vs 0.24% on Bi-CVRP100, 1.19% vs 3.38% on Tri-TSP100). With instance augmentation, WE-CA-Aug achieves 0.00% gap on all four largest-size problems, outperforming CNH-Aug (0.18% on Bi-TSP100, 0.12% on Bi-CVRP100, 0.79% on Tri-TSP100) and all other augmented baselines. Non-learnable solvers WS-LKH and WS-DP still reach 0.00% gap but require 6–22 hours versus 20–25 seconds for WE-CA, a >1000× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the POMO encoder-decoder architecture (Kwon et al., 2020) as its single-objective backbone and incorporates the FiLM conditioning mechanism (Perez et al., 2018) into its enhanced model. It departs from the prevailing decomposition-based neural MOCO paradigm, where prior single-model methods (PMOCO's hypernetwork, MORAM's weight router, CNH's size-aware decoder) introduce complex auxiliary architectures to handle weight-specific subproblems. The key conceptual departure is the 'neat' principle: rather than learning weight-specific parameters or introducing size embeddings, the paper directly injects the weight vector into the model via simple addition or conditional attention, arguing that weight-instance interaction is the essential signal that prior methods overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a minimal weight-embedding modification to POMO outperforms hypernetwork-based and meta-learning-based approaches challenges the assumption that complex conditioning mechanisms are necessary for multi-objective neural CO, potentially simplifying the design space for future methods. The demonstration that cross-size generalization emerges naturally without explicit size embeddings (and that sinusoidal positional encodings for size actually degrade performance) has practical implications for deploying neural CO models at scale. Limitations include evaluation on only three classic problems with relatively simple constraint structures; the authors acknowledge that real-world MOCO with complex constraints would require additional constraint-handling techniques, and the method has not been tested on many-objective settings (M>3) or highly constrained routing variants.
