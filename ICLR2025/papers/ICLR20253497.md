## Problem

When combining multiple ControlNets for controllable image generation, regions lacking boundary information in edge-type controls produce 'silent control signals' that suppress high-frequency texture generation in areas where other control signals aim to create details. Additionally, there is no principled method for determining optimal combination ratios between control signals, and the limited training data of ControlNet introduces asymmetry into the score function's Jacobian matrix, undermining generation stability.

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or more spatially partitioned control signals (Canny edges, HED edges, OpenPose skeletons, depth maps) alongside a text prompt, where each control signal occupies a specific region of the image and the remaining area is filled with silent (empty) signals. The output is a single generated image that faithfully follows active control signals in their designated regions while producing diverse, high-frequency textures in silent regions. Training uses the MultiGen-20M dataset with segmentation masks to construct balanced silent-signal training pairs.

## Method

MIControlNet introduces three complementary strategies to minimize ControlNet's impact on the base U-Net. First, data rebalancing applies segmentation masks to control signals during training, converting unmasked edge regions into silent signals paired with high-frequency image content, teaching the model to generate diverse textures rather than suppressing them. Second, a restricted MGDA-based balancing algorithm replaces the standard additive feature injection with a dynamic coefficient λ_i computed from the acute-angle projection between the U-Net residual feature and the ControlNet residual feature (add^inj), and a similar mechanism (add^com) balances multiple ControlNet feature maps before injection, ensuring no single control signal dominates. Third, a simplified conservativity loss (L_QC^simple) is derived by decomposing the score function's Jacobian into U-Net and ControlNet components under the assumption that ControlNet parameters govern only the additional asymmetry, reducing the computation to requiring only the ControlNet's Jacobian via Hutchinson's estimator and adding it to the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss with a small coefficient (0.01) in a second training stage.

## Results

On the multi-condition FID evaluation (Table 1), MIControlNet (2-stage) achieves the best FID in 7 out of 8 condition pairs, with values ranging from 69.35 to 75.77, compared to vanilla ControlNet's range of 76.98 to 123.59. The feature injection and combination strategy yields an average FID improvement of 9.79 over ControlNet with data augmentation, while data augmentation alone contributes 11.26. In the asymmetry analysis (Table 6), the 2-stage conservativity loss reduces the Jacobian asymmetry by over two orders of magnitude (e.g., Canny: 56.75 → 0.1174), indicating a near-symmetric score function Jacobian.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla ControlNet, MIControlNet (2-stage) wins on all 8 FID values in Table 1, with the largest gap on Canny-Hed (71.34/69.35 vs. 123.59/86.43) and Openpose-Canny (75.77/72.25 vs. 80.37/111.30). Against Uni-ControlNet, MIControlNet (2-stage) wins on 7 of 8 FID values, with the most dramatic improvement on Openpose-Hed (73.45/71.74 vs. 139.87/76.06); the single loss is on the second FID of Openpose-Canny (72.25 vs. 74.55, a narrow margin). The L1 cycle-consistency metric (Table 2) further confirms MIControlNet's advantage: the 1-stage model achieves the lowest L1 distances (e.g., 0.9638 for Openpose-Canny vs. 1.3903 for ControlNet), while the 2-stage model remains competitive. The asymmetry metric (Table 6) shows the 2-stage conservativity loss reduces Jacobian asymmetry by 2–3 orders of magnitude over both ControlNet and the 1-stage model, a dimension where neither baseline offers any mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023) by modifying its training data distribution, feature injection mechanism, and adding a conservativity loss to reduce conflicts when multiple ControlNets are combined as plug-ins. It draws on MGDA (Désidéri, 2012) for multi-objective gradient balancing, repurposing the acute-angle principle from optimization to feature-map combination in the U-Net decoder. The conservativity analysis builds on prior work on score function symmetry (Chao et al., 2022; Salimans & Ho, 2021) but introduces a novel decomposition that isolates the Jacobian contribution of ControlNet from the base U-Net, enabling a computationally tractable loss. Unlike Uni-ControlNet (Zhao et al., 2024), which trains a single unified model, MIControlNet preserves the modular plug-in architecture while making each individual ControlNet less intrusive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'minimal impact' philosophy—reducing ControlNet's influence rather than strengthening it—offers a practical, modular solution to multi-control conflicts without requiring joint training or architectural changes, making it easy to adopt in existing pipelines. The dramatic reduction in Jacobian asymmetry (e.g., from ~57 to ~0.1 for Canny) after the conservativity loss stage is a strong signal that the theoretical grounding translates to measurable stability gains. However, the authors acknowledge that the conservativity loss could not be fully validated at scale due to computational constraints, and the approach has not been explored with prompt engineering or advanced sampling strategies. Open questions include whether the MGDA-based balancing generalizes beyond two control signals and whether the data-rebalancing strategy transfers to non-edge control types.
