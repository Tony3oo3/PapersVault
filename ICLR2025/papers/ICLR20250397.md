## Problem

Existing VLMs are trained with conclusive alignment that maps visual inputs directly to final answers, causing them to skip essential intermediate visual reasoning. This leads to failures on detailed tasks (reading small text, counting, spatial grounding) and produces unfaithful or hallucinated responses, because the model never explicitly verifies or manipulates the visual evidence before answering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250397/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text question as initial input, and in subsequent turns receives cropped/zoomed sub-images it generated itself. The output is a structured chain of manipulations where each step pairs a manipulation call (e.g., Grounding('pillar')→[x0,y0,x1,y1], OCR(region)→'NO PARKING') with a free-form description, followed by a final answer. The multi-turn format means the model can produce up to 7 image-bearing turns (restricted to 4 during training), with each new image being a bicubic-interpolated crop of the previous one.

## Method

CogCoM introduces Chain of Manipulations (CoM), a reasoning mechanism with 6 atomic operations (OCR, Grounding, CropZoomIn, Counting, Calculate, Line) that the model invokes internally to produce evidential intermediate results (bounding boxes, recognized text, zoomed crops) without external tools. The architecture builds on CogVLM (EVA2-CLIP-E 4B encoder + Vicuna-7B LLM + 6.5B visual expert module) and adds a memory-based multi-turn multi-image design that accumulates KV caches across turns, truncating them at a predefined sequence-length threshold. Training data comprises 70K automated CoM samples generated by a cascading pipeline (GPT-4 as linguistic annotator producing manipulation steps, GroundingDINO and PaddleOCR as visual annotators filling in results, then DFS tree traversal to extract positive paths terminating at the golden answer) plus 7K manually annotated graphical math samples. The two-stage training first pre-trains on 1.5B image-text pairs and 40M grounded triples, then aligns on 570K samples where CoM data is randomly prefixed with a launching prompt that elicits manipulation use, all trained with next-token prediction on the 6.5B visual expert parameters.

## Results

On detailed VQA benchmarks, CogCoM achieves up to 9.0 percentage points improvement over the CogVLM baseline on detailed recognition tasks (e.g., TextVQA, ST-VQA) and up to 5.97 points on complex reasoning (GQA). On visual grounding, CogCoM achieves the best performance in 6 out of 8 RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg subsets, with up to 1.09 points improvement over CogVLM (e.g., 94.57 vs 93.95 on RefCOCO test-A). On general multimodal evaluation, CogCoM scores 46.1 on MM-Vet and 87.8 on POPE-adversarial, surpassing CogVLM (45.5 and 87.2 respectively). The ablation confirms that the 70K CoM training data contributes +6.6 points on TextVQA and +0.9 on MathVista.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct baseline CogVLM, CogCoM wins on 6 of 8 grounding subsets (notably RefCOCO test-A 94.57 vs 93.95, RefCOCO+ test-A 92.80 vs 91.81, RefCOCO+ test-B 82.08 vs 81.43, RefCOCOg test 90.45 vs 90.09) and on MM-Vet (46.1 vs 45.5) and POPE (87.8 vs 87.2). Among generalist VQA models, CogCoM outperforms LLaVA-1.5 (64.7 GQA), Monkey (60.7 GQA), Qwen-VL (59.3 GQA), and InstructBLIP (49.5 GQA) on GQA, and surpasses CogVLM (69.7) on TextVQA. On grounding, CogCoM exceeds Qwen-VL (89.36/92.26/85.34 on RefCOCO val/test-A/test-B) and Shikra-13B (87.83/91.11/81.81) across all three RefCOCO splits. Compared to specialist SOTAs, CogCoM is competitive: it matches UNINEXT on RefCOCO test-A (94.57 vs 94.33) and surpasses ONE-PEACE on RefCOCO+ test-A (92.80 vs 92.21), though it trails UNINEXT on RefCOCO test-B (89.15 vs 91.46) and ONE-PEACE on RefCOCO+ test-B (82.08 vs 83.23). On MM-Vet, CogCoM (46.1) outperforms all prior generalist models including LLaVA-1.5 (39.4), Monkey (36.2), and DreamLLM (35.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

CogCoM builds directly on the CogVLM architecture (EVA2-CLIP-E encoder, Vicuna-7B backbone, visual expert module) and its two-stage training pipeline, but introduces a fundamentally different reasoning mechanism: Chain of Manipulations (CoM), where the model actively performs visual operations (grounding, OCR, crop/zoom, counting) as intermediate steps rather than producing a single conclusive answer. Conceptually, this extends Chain-of-Thought reasoning from the linguistic domain to the visual domain, and is related to V* (guided visual search), ViperGPT (code-LLM-decomposed visual reasoning), and VPD (program distillation), but departs from these by training a single end-to-end VLM to internalize manipulations without external tool calls at inference time. The cascading data generation pipeline—using GPT-4 as a linguistic annotator and GroundingDINO/PaddleOCR as visual annotators with DFS traversal to extract valid reasoning paths—is a novel contribution to the VLM data construction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CoM mechanism addresses a well-recognized failure mode of VLMs: conclusive alignment training causes models to skip intermediate visual reasoning, producing hallucinated or unfaithful answers on detail-sensitive tasks. By making the reasoning chain explicit and traceable, CogCoM offers interpretability that purely black-box VLMs lack, and the 70K automated + 7K manually annotated CoM samples provide a reusable resource for the community. Key limitations remain: the diversity of generated reasoning steps is constrained by 5-shot GPT-4 prompting, visual tool inaccuracies (coarse boxes, OCR errors on slanted text) produce many discarded negative paths, and the fixed-prompt re-input of manipulated images introduces latency. Open questions include whether backtracking can improve reasoning path quality, whether physical manipulations can be folded directly into vector-space computation to avoid re-encoding, and whether negative paths can be leveraged via RL-based training.
