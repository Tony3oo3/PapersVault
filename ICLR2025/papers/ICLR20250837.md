## Problem

Existing neural network models for dynamical systems suffer from two critical limitations: they are narrowly focused on mechanical systems and fail to leverage domain-specific principles (e.g., Kirchhoff's laws for circuits), and they treat systems as monolithic entities rather than collections of interacting components. Prior methods each address only a subset of dynamical system features—degeneracy, dissipation, external inputs, or coordinate transformations—but no single framework unifies all of these while also handling multiphysics coupling across domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state trajectory observations (displacements/momenta for mechanical, charge/flux for electrical, volume for hydraulic) and known external input signals (forces, voltages, fluid flows) sampled at fixed time steps. Beyond the predicted next-state trajectory, the model outputs a learned skew-symmetric bivector matrix B whose non-zero entries explicitly encode which components are coupled and in what direction, along with per-component neural network energy functions H and dissipative mappings R_u. The port variables are decomposed into energy-storing (S), energy-dissipating (R), and external-input (I) channels, making the output structurally interpretable as a circuit or mechanical diagram.

## Method

PoDiNNs define a Dirac structure D as a subbundle of F⊕E, where the flow space F and effort space E are each decomposed into state (S), resistive (R), and input (I) sub-bundles. The dynamics are governed by f = B^#(e), where the effort e = (dH, R_u(f^R), e^I) combines the gradient of a neural-network energy function, a neural-network dissipative characteristic, and time-dependent external inputs, while the bivector B is a learned skew-symmetric matrix parameterizing the coupling topology. This formulation unifies Poisson systems (via degeneracy of B^#), port-Hamiltonian systems (via the R and I channels), and coordinate transformations (via the structure of B) in a single framework. Training is supervised one-step prediction: given a state snapshot, the model predicts the next state after Δt and minimizes normalized MSE, with the bivector, energy networks, and dissipative networks all updated jointly. The key architectural insight is that coupling patterns are separated from component characteristics—B encodes topology while neural networks encode local physics—enabling direct extraction of circuit diagrams or mechanical schematics from the learned parameters.

## Results

PoDiNNs consistently outperform all baselines across all seven datasets on both MSE (lower is better) and Valid Prediction Time (VPT, higher is better). VPTs show PoDiNNs provide stable predictions for durations 1.5 to 20 times longer than baselines, with all differences statistically significant (p < 0.0005, Mann-Whitney U test). The most dramatic gains appear on the redundant mass-spring system (c), where PoDiNNs achieve MSE 0.011 vs. 2490.61 (Neural ODE) and 634.22 (PNN), and on the hydraulic tank (g), where VPT jumps from 0.045 (Neural ODE) to 0.918 (PoDiNN). On the DC motor multiphysics system (f), PoDiNNs reach VPT 0.923 versus 0.276 for Neural ODEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoDiNNs outperform Neural ODEs on all seven datasets for both MSE and VPT. The VPT advantage ranges from 1.5× (FitzHugh-Nagumo: 0.649 vs. 0.322) to over 20× (hydraulic tank: 0.918 vs. 0.045). Against Dissipative SymODENs (tested on systems a and b in absolute coordinates), PoDiNNs achieve substantially higher VPT (0.437 vs. 0.104 on (a'); 0.581 vs. 0.001 on (b')) and lower MSE, with the gap on (b') being particularly stark as Dissipative SymODENs essentially fail (VPT ≈ 0). Against PNNs (tested on the redundant system c), PoDiNNs reduce MSE by over four orders of magnitude (0.011 vs. 634.22) and achieve VPT 0.863 versus 0.000, demonstrating that learning an explicit coordinate transformation (as PNNs require) is far harder than learning a degenerate bivector directly. All differences are statistically significant with p < 0.0005.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoDiNNs sit at the intersection of several threads in physics-informed neural ODE learning: HNNs (Greydanus et al., 2019) for energy-based dynamics, PNNs (Jin et al., 2022) for degenerate/Poisson systems, Dissipative SymODENs (Zhong et al., 2020) for port-Hamiltonian dissipation and control, and NSFs (Chen et al., 2021) for coordinate-free symplectic learning. Each prior method addresses a subset of the full problem—degeneracy, dissipation, external inputs, or coordinate invariance—but in a fragmented, domain-specific way. The key departure here is using the Dirac structure (Courant, 1990; Yoshimura & Marsden, 2006a) as a single geometric object that simultaneously encodes all of these aspects, with the bivector B explicitly representing coupling patterns between components rather than burying them inside a monolithic neural network. This makes PoDiNNs a strict generalization of HNNs, LNNs, NSFs, and PNNs, as the paper demonstrates by showing each reduces to a special case when ports and dissipation are removed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to recover interpretable coupling structures (e.g., Kirchhoff's laws and circuit topology from Chua's circuit, or cross-sectional areas from the hydraulic tank) from purely observational data is a meaningful step toward automated reverse engineering of physical systems, and the multiphysics capability (mechanical + electrical + hydraulic in one model) has no direct precedent in the neural ODE literature. However, several limitations temper the impact: the method requires knowing the number and type of energy-dissipating components a priori, cannot handle differential-algebraic equations (e.g., a capacitor in parallel with a voltage source), and all experiments are on clean simulation data with only three baselines (some evaluated on subsets). Extending to DAEs, PDEs, and real-world noisy observations remains open, as does automating the specification of the port structure itself.
