## Problem

SNNs trained with standard direct training (SDT) are locked to the specific time step used during training, exhibiting poor accuracy when evaluated at other time steps. This 'temporal inflexibility' prevents deployment on fully event-driven neuromorphic chips (which operate asynchronously without a fixed time step) and blocks dynamic on-chip time-step adjustment for energy-performance trade-offs. The paper identifies this gap and asks whether SNNs can be trained to generalize across arbitrary temporal structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252158/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For static datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet), RGB images are repeated T times to form the spiking input; for DVS datasets (CIFAR10-DVS, N-Caltech101, N-MNIST), event streams are split into T temporal frames by timestamp. The same trained model is then evaluated at multiple inference time steps (T=1 through T=64) without retraining, and deployed on both clock-driven and fully event-driven hardware (Speck2e chip, where bias-free IF neurons are required). Output is a single class label per sample.

## Method

Mixed Time-step Training (MTT) partitions the SNN into G stages and, in each training iteration, samples s random temporal configuration vectors that assign a different time step to each stage, expanding the number of temporal structures from T_max to (T_max)^G. A Temporal Transformation Module (TTM) handles spike communication between adjacent stages operating at different time steps via downsampling (summing frames) or upsampling (replicating frames) with an even-grouping policy. Because mixed time steps corrupt BatchNorm running statistics, the method calibrates BN from a small number of training batches after weight optimization. The trained weights are then directly deployable on both time-stepped and fully event-driven platforms, achieving near-lossless accuracy on N-MNIST on the Speck2e chip and 10.1% higher accuracy than SDT on CIFAR10-DVS in event-driven simulation.

## Results

On static datasets, MTT-trained ResNet-19 achieves 96.84% on CIFAR-10 (T=6), 81.98% on CIFAR-100 (T=6), and 68.34% on ImageNet (T=6, ResNet-34), outperforming all direct-training baselines. On DVS datasets, MTT achieves 82.8% on CIFAR10-DVS and 81.74% on N-Caltech101 (both T=10, ResNet-18), matching or exceeding SOTA. A key differentiator is temporal flexibility: a single MTT-trained model is tested across T=2–6 without retraining, maintaining within ~1% of its peak accuracy, whereas SDT-trained models degrade by 5–6% when evaluated at non-training time steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTT outperforms all direct-training SNN baselines on static datasets: on CIFAR-100 it beats InfLoR-SNN (81.98 vs 79.51), TET (81.98 vs 77.35), and Dspike (81.98 vs 74.24); on ImageNet it surpasses SEW (68.34 vs 67.04) and MPSNN (68.34 vs 67.52). On DVS datasets, MTT (ResNet-18) achieves 82.8% on CIFAR10-DVS, slightly below TET with VGGSNN (83.17%) but well above TCJA-SNN (80.7%) and InfLoR-SNN (75.50%); on N-Caltech101 it leads all baselines (81.74% vs TCJA-SNN 78.5%, NDA 78.2%, SALT 55.0%). Against ANN-SNN conversion methods (QCFS, SlipReLU), MTT significantly outperforms at low time steps (T=1: 72.09 vs 71.51; T=2: 76.54 vs 73.91) while remaining competitive at higher T. Combined with SEENN for dynamic time-step inference, MTT yields a 0.2% boost over SEENN alone at T=1.20 (96.58 vs 96.38). On audio/sequential tasks, MTT outperforms SDT by 8.8% on seqMNIST (64.56 vs 55.75) and 4.4% on Spiking Heidelberg Digits (79.68 vs 75.26), and also beats the reproduced Hammouamri et al. baseline (79.68 vs 75.26).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the direct training paradigm for SNNs established by STBP (Wu et al., 2018) and subsequent work (Wu et al., 2019), which trains networks at a fixed time step T using BPTT with surrogate gradients. The key departure is identifying 'temporal inflexibility'—the inability of SDT-trained models to generalize across time steps—as a previously unaddressed deployment bottleneck, and proposing Mixed Time-step Training (MTT) that assigns random per-stage time steps during training with a Temporal Transformation Module (TTM) for inter-stage communication. The BN calibration step adapts the technique from Yu and Huang (2019) to handle the statistical non-stationarity introduced by mixed time steps. Conceptually, MTT sits between fixed-T direct training and ANN-to-SNN conversion, offering temporal flexibility without requiring a pre-trained ANN or being limited to high-T regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap that has hindered SNN deployment on neuromorphic hardware: models trained at one time step degrade significantly when deployed at another or on fully event-driven chips where time steps are irrelevant. The first large-scale SNN evaluation on a fully event-driven platform (Speck2e and a custom simulator) provides much-needed ground truth for the gap between GPU-trained and chip-deployed SNNs. Open questions include whether MTT's per-stage randomization is optimal versus other diversity-inducing strategies, whether the BN calibration requirement (10 batches) remains practical for very large models, and whether the approach extends to online/continual learning settings where time steps may drift. The method's simplicity (~1.5× training overhead) makes it broadly adoptable, but its impact is most relevant to the neuromorphic hardware deployment community rather than the broader SNN accuracy race.
