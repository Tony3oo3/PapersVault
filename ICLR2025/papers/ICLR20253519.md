## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of in-context learning for linear regression focus exclusively on how Transformers leverage in-context examples, yet in practice prompts almost always include a task descriptor (e.g., 'Translate English to French'). This paper asks how such descriptors—specifically those encoding distributional information like the mean of the input—provably help a Transformer perform ICL for linear models, and whether gradient-based training can discover this usage.

## Input / Output

The Transformer receives a sequence S_τ = (μ_τ, x_τ,1, y_τ,1, …, x_τ,n, y_τ,n, x_τ,query) where μ_τ ~ N(0, I_d) is the task-specific mean, x_τ,i ~ N(μ_τ, Λ) are in-context inputs, y_τ,i = w_τ^T x_τ,i are labels, and x_τ,query is the new query. The embedding matrix E_τ duplicates μ_τ as the top row of every token column (including the query column), so the descriptor is locally available at each position. The model outputs a scalar prediction ŷ_τ,query read from the bottom-right entry of the LSA output, targeting y_τ,query = w_τ^T x_τ,query.

## Method

The paper analyzes a 1-layer linear self-attention (LSA) Transformer trained via gradient flow on the population loss over mean-varying linear regression tasks. The key insight is that the optimal solution decomposes W_*^{KQ} as C^T · W̃^{KQ}, where C is a standardization operator that subtracts the task descriptor μ from each x_i before computing attention keys, thereby removing spurious correlations induced by the nonzero mean. For infinite samples this yields zero loss; for finite samples the optimal solution additionally activates two extra blocks (a_11 I_d and a_21 I_d) that exploit the descriptor to reduce bias in the empirical mean and second-moment estimates. The training loss is shown to decompose into independent bias and variance terms via a reparametrization θ = (θ_1,…,θ_4), and gradient flow from a balanced initialization maintains invariants (Frobenius-norm balancing between W^{KQ} and W^{PV}, symmetry of W^{KQ}, and a positive lower bound on the scalar u) that guarantee convergence to the unique global minimum despite the nonconvex landscape.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that for a 1-layer linear self-attention (LSA) Transformer trained via gradient flow on the mean-varying linear regression problem, the training converges to a global minimizer achieving zero loss in the infinite-sample limit (n→∞). The optimal solution uses the task descriptor μ to 'standardize' keys by computing z_i = x_i − μ, effectively removing the mean to eliminate spurious correlations. For finite samples (Theorem 4.1), the optimal solution is more complex, involving four scalar parameters (a_11, a_12, a_21, a_22, b) that jointly minimize both bias and variance terms in a decomposed loss. Empirically, Transformers with task descriptors consistently achieve lower MSE loss than those without across 1-, 2-, and 3-layer architectures, and the trained weights converge to the theoretically predicted global optimum as verified by heat maps of W^{PV}_{-1,-1}·W^{KQ}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between Transformers trained with and without task descriptors (the mean vector μ) on the mean-varying linear regression task. Across all tested configurations (1-, 2-, and 3-layer LSA Transformers), the descriptor-equipped models achieve strictly lower MSE loss than their no-descriptor counterparts. Among descriptor embeddings, the duplicated-descriptor embedding E_τ (where μ appears before every token) and the prefix embedding E_τ^pre (where μ appears only in the first token) both outperform the no-descriptor embedding E. Interestingly, 3-layer Transformers trained on the prefix embedding E_τ^pre surpass even those trained on the duplicated embedding E_τ, suggesting deeper networks can extract descriptor information more efficiently from a single token. The theoretical construction also improves upon the prior no-descriptor result of Zhang et al. (2024a): without the task descriptor, the empirical second moment converges to Λ + μμ^⊤ (task-dependent and non-invertible uniformly), whereas with the descriptor the Transformer recovers the true covariance Λ, enabling exact recovery of w.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical framework of Zhang et al. (2024a), which proved global convergence of 1-layer linear self-attention Transformers for in-context learning of linear regression, by introducing task descriptors (mean vectors) into the problem. It also builds on the observation from Ahn et al. (2023) that 1-layer LSA computes preconditioned gradient descent steps, showing that with task descriptors the optimal solution goes beyond simple preconditioned GD to a more sophisticated 'key standardization' mechanism. The key departure from prior work is that all previous theoretical analyses of ICL for linear regression focused exclusively on in-context examples, while this paper is the first to provide a rigorous characterization of how distributional task descriptors alter the optimal Transformer solution and training dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical explanation for why adding task descriptions to prompts improves in-context learning performance, a phenomenon well-documented empirically in LLMs but previously lacking mechanistic understanding. The finding that even a 1-layer linear (nonlinear-free) Transformer can discover mean-removal as an optimal strategy provides a clean, interpretable account of how descriptors help. Open questions remain for multi-layer and nonlinear Transformers, alternative embeddings that don't duplicate the descriptor, and more general task descriptors beyond distributional statistics. The narrow scope (linear attention, linear regression, d=5 experiments) limits immediate practical impact, but the conceptual framework for analyzing descriptor-augmented ICL is a useful starting point for broader investigations.
