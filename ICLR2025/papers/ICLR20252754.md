## Problem

Lévy-Itô diffusion models (LIMs) replace Gaussian noise with α-stable noise to improve generation on imbalanced datasets, but their stochastic sampling algorithm solves only an approximate reverse SDE that drops an intractable finite-variation term. Unlike conventional diffusion models, which admit a parametric family of exact sampling algorithms interpolating between SDE and ODE, LIMs lack such a family, and the approximation error becomes significant when the number of function evaluations is small. The paper also leaves LIMs largely unexplored in speech synthesis on imbalanced multi-speaker data.

## Input / Output

For image generation, the model starts from a sample of the standard isotropic α-stable distribution SαS^d(1) and iteratively denoises it over a fixed number of solver steps (20, 50, or 500) to produce 32×32 CIFAR-10 images. For text-to-speech, the input consists of text phoneme sequences and a speaker reference, and the output is a mel-spectrogram decoded into a waveform via a pre-trained HiFi-GAN vocoder. The key unusual aspect is that all stochastic dynamics are driven by α-stable Lévy processes (α ∈ {1.2, 1.5, 1.8}) rather than Brownian motion, and the reverse-time noise level is controlled by a tunable schedule η_t that can be set to zero for deterministic sampling or to positive values for stochastic sampling with reduced noise.

## Method

The paper derives a parametric family of exact reverse SDEs (Theorem 1) of the form dX̄_t = (μ(X̄_{t+},t) − (1+η_t)σ_t^α S_t^(α)(X̄_{t+}))dt + σ_t η_t^{1/α} dL̄_t^α, where η_t ≥ 0 controls the amount of α-stable noise at each reverse step. The proof shows that the fractional Fokker-Planck PDE governing the time-reversed process is satisfied by the forward marginal densities p(x,t), establishing exactness without relying on the intractable dZ̄_t term. When η_t ≡ 0 the SDE reduces to the probability flow ODE; when η_t ≡ 1 it recovers the original approximate SDE. The key insight is that for small NFE, the finite-variation term dropped in the original approximate SDE becomes comparable in magnitude to the infinite-variation Lévy increments, so the exact SDE with a tuned η_t < 1 avoids this error while retaining sample diversity. The η_t schedule is parameterized as η_t = η + (δ−η)[(t−t_0)/(T−t_0)]⁺ + 2η(σ(ct)−1), decaying near t=0 and optionally increasing near t=T, with optimal values selected per α and solver configuration.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 unconditional image generation, the proposed exact SDE (SDE-E) achieves FID of 7.08 (α=1.2), 7.86 (α=1.5), and 8.79 (α=1.8) with Euler-Maruyama at N=20 steps, compared to 49.87, 109.5, and 144.7 for the approximate SDE (SDE-A) from Yoon et al. (2023). With the Exponential Integrator at N=20, SDE-E reaches FID 6.27–7.08 across α values, outperforming both SDE-A (7.70–10.42) and the ODE (9.14–10.50). At N=500 steps, all three methods converge to similar FID (~2.6–3.7), confirming the advantage is specific to low-NFE regimes. For text-to-speech on a highly imbalanced dataset (1000 min female vs. 10 min male speaker), the α=1.5 LIM achieves 0.800 speaker similarity for the rare male speaker at N=100, versus 0.773 for the Gaussian-noise baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the approximate SDE (SDE-A) from Yoon et al. (2023), the proposed SDE-E delivers dramatic FID improvements at low NFE: up to 136 FID points with Euler-Maruyama at N=20 (e.g., 8.79 vs. 144.7 for α=1.8) and 3.5–4.2 FID points with the Exponential Integrator at N=20. At N=500 steps, the gap narrows to less than 0.7 FID, confirming the advantage stems from the intractable dZ̄_t term becoming non-negligible at coarse discretization. Against the probability flow ODE, SDE-E consistently achieves lower FID at N=20 and N=50 (e.g., 6.27 vs. 10.50 for α=1.5 with Exponential Integrator at N=20) while also maintaining higher coverage (diversity): SDE-E reaches 85–87% coverage at N=20 versus 80–82% for the ODE. On the imbalanced CIFAR-10 setting (5000 to 50 images per class), SDE-E achieves FID 18.10 and 81.83% coverage with 20 Exponential Integrator steps, outperforming SDE-A (18.44 FID, 80.33%) and ODE (23.68 FID, 71.73%). For text-to-speech, the α=1.5 LIM outperforms the Gaussian-noise Grad-TTS baseline by 0.027 speaker similarity for the rare male speaker (0.800 vs. 0.773 at N=100) and 0.017 for the frequent female speaker (0.867 vs. 0.850), with the gap widening at fewer solver steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lévy-Itô diffusion model framework of Yoon et al. (2023) by deriving a parametric family of exact reverse SDEs analogous to the η-parameterized sampling family (DDIM) that exists for conventional score-based diffusion models (Song et al., 2021a,c). The key departure is that the original LIM paper only provided an approximate stochastic sampler (dropping an intractable finite-variation term) and a deterministic ODE, leaving no principled way to control noise at inference. The speech experiments build on the Grad-TTS architecture (Popov et al., 2021), replacing Gaussian driving noise with α-stable Lévy noise to study imbalanced multi-speaker synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that LIMs become competitive with conventional diffusion models at low NFE (≤50 steps), where the original approximate SDE suffered large FID degradation due to the neglected finite-variation term becoming comparable to the infinite-variation Lévy term on coarse partitions. This opens LIMs to real-time or compute-constrained deployment. Open questions include: how to automatically estimate optimal η_t (infinite variance blocks direct application of analytic reverse-variance methods), whether the TTS advantage generalizes to many rare speakers rather than one, and whether the exact SDE extends to conditional or guided generation settings.
