## Problem

Object-centric models trained on real-world data rely on frozen pretrained non-object-centric target encoders (e.g., DINOv2), which impose a hard upper bound on performance and exhibit poor data scalability. Attempts to update the target encoder via EMA cause severe performance drops because the target encoder lacks object-centric inductive biases, leading the student encoder to drift away from useful reconstruction targets. No prior work has successfully pretrained an object-centric model from scratch on real-world data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes two augmented views of a 224×224 RGB image and produces 7 slots (each 256-dimensional) via a 3-layer slot attention encoder, which are decoded back to N patch-level reconstructions (N = (h/16)×(w/16) for ViT-S/16). Beyond the slot representations, the model outputs per-patch features and a global [CLS] token, all of which are used in the cross-view self-distillation loss. An optional mask sharpening stage converts the soft slot assignments into sharper segmentation masks with clearer object boundaries.

## Method

OCEBO formulates object-centric pretraining as a self-distillation bootstrapping problem: the object-centric model (ViT-S/16 encoder + slot attention encoder/decoder) is the student, and a target encoder of identical architecture serves as the teacher, updated as an EMA of the student encoder. The training objective is a patch-level cross-entropy loss between sharpened softmax distributions of target encoder features and slot decoder reconstructions, applied across two augmented views to enforce augmentation invariance. To prevent slot collapse from the randomly initialized target encoder, a cross-view patch filtering mechanism computes k-nearest-neighbor correspondences between the two views' target features and only supervises patches where mutual nearest-neighbor agreement holds, gradually increasing the fraction of supervised patches from ~10% to ~70% over training. An optional mask sharpening stage freezes the target encoder and switches to an L2 reconstruction loss to produce cleaner segmentation boundaries. The key insight is that EMA updates explicitly inject object-centric inductive biases into the target encoder, and training from scratch allows the model to learn instance-based features rather than reorganizing semantic features inherited from a non-object-centric pretrained encoder.

## Results

Pretrained from scratch on ~241k COCO+ images, OCEBO achieves FG-ARI of 66.8 and mBO of 22.1 on MOVi-E, and FG-ARI of 44.2 and mBO of 16.0 on EntitySeg. These numbers are competitive with FT-DINOSAUR (71.1 FG-ARI / 29.9 mBO on MOVi-E; 48.1 / 28.4 on EntitySeg), which relies on a DINOv2 target encoder pretrained on 142M images. OCEBO also produces high-quality patch-level representations, reaching 46.2 mIOU on Pascal VOC retrieval-based segmentation (1/1 subsampling), comparable to the patch-level SSL method CrOC (47.1 mIOU). The model demonstrates clear data scalability, with FG-ARI improving from 54.8 to 66.8 on MOVi-E when scaling from ~118k to ~241k training images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCEBO is compared against four object-centric baselines (SlotDiffusion, SPOT, DINOSAUR, FT-DINOSAUR) that all rely on frozen non-object-centric target encoders pretrained on 1.3M (DINO) or 142M (DINOv2) images, whereas OCEBO is trained entirely from scratch on only ~241k COCO+ images. On FG-ARI, OCEBO is competitive: it nearly matches SlotDiffusion on MOVi-E (66.8 vs 67.6) and exceeds SPOT by 19 points (66.8 vs 47.8), but trails FT-DINOSAUR by 4.3 points (66.8 vs 71.1) and DINOSAUR by 4.3 points. On mBO, OCEBO consistently lags behind all baselines, with the largest gap against FT-DINOSAUR (22.1 vs 29.9 on MOVi-E) and SlotDiffusion (22.1 vs 26.4), which the authors attribute to OCEBO's simpler MLP decoder versus the autoregressive or top-k decoders used by those methods. On EntitySeg, OCEBO achieves the second-best FG-ARI (44.2) behind FT-DINOSAUR (48.1) but the lowest mBO (16.0). The authors emphasize that the comparison is not apples-to-apples given the 587× difference in pretraining data, and that incorporating SPOT's or FT-DINOSAUR's decoder components would likely close the mBO gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the object-centric representation learning line initiated by Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020) and made viable on real-world data by DINOSAUR (Seitzer et al., 2022), which relies on a frozen pretrained non-object-centric target encoder (e.g., DINOv2) to provide reconstruction targets. It departs from this paradigm by formulating object-centric pretraining as a DINO-style self-distillation bootstrapping problem where the target encoder is updated via EMA and thus acquires object-centric inductive biases over training. The novel cross-view patch filtering mechanism addresses the slot collapse that arises from the randomly initialized target encoder, a problem absent in prior work that starts from informative frozen features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OCEBO removes the fundamental performance ceiling imposed by frozen target encoders and demonstrates that object-centric models can scale with data, a property previously lacking in this family. The finding that ~241k COCO images suffice to match models using DINOv2 pretrained on 142M images suggests object-centric inductive biases in the target encoder are highly data-efficient. A key open question is identifying the right large-scale pretraining dataset: the paper shows ImageNet (1.3M images) actually underperforms COCO (118k) because its single-object-centric scenes don't provide the multi-object decomposition signal needed, leaving the construction of a suitable large-scale dataset as an open challenge for the field.
