## Problem

Existing diffusion-based satellite-to-street-view synthesis methods suffer from two critical limitations: (1) implicit conditioning on satellite features leads to pose misalignment between generated ground images and the specified camera position, and (2) training datasets (CVUSA, KITTI, VIGOR) contain almost no variation in weather, illumination, or season, preventing the model from generating environmentally diverse outputs. Prior geometric approaches rely on coarse 3D priors that compound errors, while text-conditioned methods like ControlNet lose sensitivity to environmental prompts when satellite conditioning dominates training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 satellite image (processed through a ViT encoder), a relative pose specified as rotation R and translation T, and a free-form text prompt describing desired environmental conditions (e.g., 'spring', 'winter', 'sunny'). It produces a 128×512 street-view ground-level image — panoramic for CVUSA and VIGOR, or limited horizontal field-of-view for KITTI. The text prompt is consumed exclusively at inference via CLIP embeddings (zero-shot), while the satellite image and pose are baked into the denoising network during training.

## Method

Built on Stable Diffusion 1.5, the framework introduces three mechanisms. First, a Geometric Cross-Attention (GCA) module replaces standard cross-attention by hypothesizing N=8 reference heights per ground pixel, projecting each to satellite coordinates via the relative pose, and aggregating satellite features with learned per-height attention weights and offsets — reducing complexity from O(S²HW) to O(4NHW). Second, an Iterative Homography Adjustment (IHA) scheme operates during DDIM sampling: at each of the first 40 denoising steps, a 3×3 homography matrix is applied to the latent representation and refined via gradient descent on an InfoNCE loss computed between ground and satellite features at K candidate poses, progressively correcting positional drift. Third, a Zero-Shot Environmental Control (ZoEC) strategy injects CLIP text-embedding gradients into the noise prediction at every sampling step, enabling weather/season control without any retraining. The model is fine-tuned for 200 epochs (batch 32, 3 GPUs) on CVUSA, KITTI, and VIGOR, with the IHA network trained jointly using the InfoNCE objective after the main U-Net converges.

## Results

On CVUSA, the proposed method achieves the best SSIM (0.38), FID (21.30), DINO distance (4.81), SegAny distance (0.36), RMSE (52.92), PSNR (13.67), and depth error (19.58) among all compared methods. On VIGOR, it leads in SSIM (0.42), DINO (4.34), SegAny (0.35), SD (13.07), and depth error (7.10), though ControlNet achieves a slightly lower FID (23.68 vs. 28.01). The method consistently outperforms GAN-based baselines (Pix2Pix, XFork) and geometry-prior methods (S2S, Sat2Density) across structural, perceptual, semantic, and depth metrics on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on the majority of metrics across both CVUSA and VIGOR. Against Pix2Pix and XFork (GAN-based), it achieves substantially better FID (21.30 vs. 44.51) and SSIM (0.38 vs. 0.32/0.29) on CVUSA, reflecting the advantage of diffusion over GANs for this task. Compared to geometry-prior methods S2S and Sat2Density, the proposed method improves SSIM by 0.03–0.05 and reduces FID by 8–27 points, while also achieving the best depth error (19.58 vs. 21.05/19.83 on CVUSA; 7.10 vs. 10.87/8.05 on VIGOR). Against ControlNet, the proposed method wins on SSIM, DINO, SegAny, RMSE, PSNR, and depth on CVUSA, but ControlNet achieves a lower FID on VIGOR (23.68 vs. 28.01), which the authors attribute to ControlNet capturing dataset distributions without faithfully translating satellite geometric cues. CrossDiff, the only other diffusion-based baseline, is outperformed on SSIM (0.38 vs. 0.37), FID (21.30 vs. 23.67), and PSNR (13.67 vs. 12.00) on CVUSA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion model framework (Stable Diffusion) and adapts the classifier-guidance formulation from Dhariwal & Nichol (2021) to simultaneously enforce pose alignment and environmental conditioning during inference. It departs from prior satellite-to-street-view methods (S2S, Sat2Density, CrossDiff) that rely on approximate 3D priors or density fields by introducing a Geometric Cross-Attention mechanism that explicitly constructs satellite-to-ground correspondences via multi-height projection, and an Iterative Homography Adjustment that corrects pose drift directly in latent space during DDIM sampling. The zero-shot environmental control via CLIP text gradients at inference time contrasts with ControlNet-style approaches that embed text conditions during training, which the paper shows leads to the model progressively ignoring text prompts in favor of satellite image conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in cross-view synthesis: existing datasets lack diverse weather/season annotations, making it impossible to train environmental control in a supervised manner. The zero-shot approach sidesteps this, and the IHA mechanism (adding only ~0.1s overhead) offers a lightweight way to enforce geometric consistency without retraining. Limitations include reliance on a ground-plane assumption for the homography projection, evaluation on relatively low-resolution outputs (128×512), and the fact that the proposed evaluation metrics (DINO/SegAny semantic similarity, DepthAnything depth) are not yet standard, making cross-paper comparison difficult. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, whether IHA generalizes to non-planar scenes (hilly terrain), and whether the zero-shot environmental control can handle more fine-grained or compositional prompts.
