## Problem

Existing synthetic data augmentation methods for image classification generate samples uniformly or in a single static pass, without feedback from the downstream classifier. This leads to distributional misalignment, redundant samples, and limited utility for improving generalization. The paper addresses the absence of an iterative, on-the-fly mechanism that dynamically prioritizes synthetic data complementary to the model's current weaknesses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251742/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a real target dataset of RGB images with class labels, BLIP-2-generated raw captions per image, and a pretrained multi-class classifier (ResNet-50, CLIP ResNet-50, or CLIP ViT). It produces a fine-tuned classifier and, as an intermediate artifact, a set of synthetic images (generated at 224×224 or 448×448 depending on the dataset) that are combined with real data each iteration. The classifier's current state is re-queried every iteration to resample marginal examples, making the pipeline inherently sequential rather than one-shot.

## Method

GenDataAgent operates in two stages: an initial 3-iteration fine-tuning on real data only (saving checkpoints), followed by iterative on-the-fly augmentation. In each augmentation iteration, it (1) selects marginal examples by ranking real samples by the classifier's predicted probability (marginal score) and taking the k lowest, (2) perturbs their BLIP-2 captions using Llama-2 to produce m diverse yet semantically consistent prompt variants, (3) generates synthetic images via Stable Diffusion v1.5 (fine-tuned with LoRA on the target distribution using CLIP feature guidance), and (4) filters out-of-distribution samples using variance of gradients (VoG) computed across the three saved checkpoints. The surviving synthetic images are unioned with the real dataset to fine-tune the classifier, and the loop repeats, so that each iteration's synthetic data is tailored to the model's evolving decision boundary.

## Results

In the synthetic data augmentation setting (ResNet-50 backbone), GenDataAgent achieves top-1 accuracy of 94.7% on Oxford-IIIT Pets, 83.9% on CUB-200-2011, 91.0% on Flowers-102, 74.5% on Birdsnap, 87.8% on Food-101, and 90.1% on ImageNet-100, outperforming the only-real-data baseline by +1.1 to +3.6 points across datasets. In the synthetic-data-only setting, GenDataAgent reaches 87.2% on IN100 and 90.3% on Pets, surpassing Real-Fake (82.8% and 89.5%) by 4.4 and 0.8 points respectively. GenDataAgent also improves worst-case disparity (fairness) over Real-Fake in most augmentation scenarios, e.g., reducing disparity from 0.40 to 0.56 on Pets while simultaneously boosting accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenDataAgent outperforms all six baselines across every dataset in both the synthetic-only and augmentation settings. Against Real-Fake (the strongest prior synthetic augmentation method), GenDataAgent gains +0.5 to +3.6 points in top-1 accuracy with the ResNet-50 backbone (e.g., 91.0 vs. 89.0 on Flowers, 90.1 vs. 88.6 on IN100), and the gap widens to +10.0 points on Flowers in the synthetic-only setting (76.9 vs. 66.9). Against Internet Explorer, which retrieves real web images, GenDataAgent leads by +0.2 to +1.2 points in augmentation, with the largest margin on Birdsnap (74.5 vs. 73.9) and IN100 (90.1 vs. 88.9). In the synthetic-only setting, GenDataAgent surpasses SyntheticData (He et al.) by +4.5 to +9.8 points (e.g., 71.4 vs. 56.9 on CUB) and CiP (Lei et al.) by +3.8 to +51.1 points. RandAugment, a traditional transformation-based augmentation, provides negligible gains (+0.1 to +0.5 points) compared to GenDataAgent's consistent +1.0 to +3.6 point improvements, underscoring the advantage of distribution-aware synthetic generation over static geometric/color transforms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic data augmentation pipeline established by Real-Fake (Yuan et al., 2023), which adapts Stable Diffusion via LoRA to match a target distribution, by introducing an iterative feedback loop during classifier fine-tuning. It departs from prior static augmentation methods (ImageNet-Clone, CiP, Real-Fake) that generate all synthetic data upfront without consulting the downstream model, and from Hemmat et al. (2023) which applies feedback in a single offline cycle, by making generation and filtering on-the-fly across training iterations. The filtering mechanism draws on variance-of-gradients (Agarwal et al., 2022) for outlier detection, while diversity is enhanced through Llama-2 caption perturbation—a repurposing of LLMs for prompt variation rather than semantic reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The on-the-fly feedback loop addresses a practical gap: static synthetic augmentation can amplify class biases and overfit, whereas dynamic generation that tracks the model's evolving decision boundary improves both accuracy and worst-case fairness. However, the approach carries significant training overhead (SD adaptation, iterative generation, VoG computation), and evaluation is limited to six image classification datasets with ResNet-50/CLIP backbones. Open questions include whether the method scales to larger datasets (e.g., full ImageNet-1K), whether it transfers to detection or segmentation tasks, and whether newer, faster diffusion models can reduce the generation bottleneck that currently limits practical adoption.
