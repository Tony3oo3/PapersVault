## Problem

The equivalence between GFlowNet training and entropy-regularized RL holds only when the backward policy is fixed, yet standard GFlowNet algorithms jointly train both policies for faster convergence. Prior work showed that naively optimizing the backward policy with the same RL objective as the forward policy can fail to improve or even slow convergence, leaving a theoretical and practical gap in how to properly learn the backward policy within the soft RL framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253538/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized reward function R(x) over a finite discrete space, a DAG defining the generation process, and any existing GFlowNet or soft RL training algorithm (TB, DB, SubTB, SoftDQN, MunchausenDQN) as its forward-policy optimizer. Backward policy updates consume on-policy trajectories sampled from the current forward policy, while forward policy updates can additionally use off-policy data from a replay buffer. The output is a trained forward policy whose induced marginal distribution over terminal states matches R(x)/Z, with the backward policy learned as an auxiliary component that is not directly used at inference time.

## Method

TLM formulates the joint forward-backward GFlowNet training as a KL-divergence minimization over trajectory distributions and decomposes it into an alternating two-step procedure: (1) maximize the backward policy's log-likelihood of trajectories generated by the forward policy (a standard MLE step), and (2) optimize the forward policy via any existing soft RL or GFlowNet algorithm treating the updated backward policy as fixed. Each step is approximated by a single stochastic gradient update per iteration, making the method a drop-in addition to any GFlowNet training loop. Convergence is established under two conditions: stability of backward updates (enforced via decaying learning rates and EMA target networks) and non-stationary regret minimization for the forward policy. The approach is the first principled method for adaptive backward policy optimization in soft RL-based GFlowNet algorithms, and experiments across four environments show faster convergence and improved mode discovery, particularly in less-structured settings like atom-by-atom molecule generation.

## Results

On the 4D hypergrid (H=20), TLM achieves the fastest L1-distance convergence to the target distribution when paired with SoftDQN, MunchausenDQN, and DB, in both standard and hard reward settings. On QM9 molecule generation, TLM with DB discovers the most Tanimoto-separated modes (reward ≥ 1.125) and yields the best Pearson correlation between log R and log P_θ across most learning rates. On bit sequences (n=120, k=8), TLM with DB finds all 60 modes faster than any other backward approach and achieves the highest Spearman correlation at optimal learning rates. On sEH, no backward policy strategy—including TLM—shows a significant advantage over the uniform baseline in mode discovery, consistent with the environment's high structural bias from predefined fragments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the maximum-entropy backward policy (maxent, Mohammadpour et al., 2024), TLM achieves faster convergence on hypergrid for SoftDQN, MunchausenDQN, and DB in both standard and hard settings, and shows robust improvement in mode discovery and correlation on QM9 across most GFlowNet algorithms. However, on sEH, TLM can underperform maxent in mode count, though the gap is small since no backward strategy significantly beats uniform there. Compared to the pessimistic backward policy (Jang et al., 2024), TLM is more robust to learning-rate choice: pessimistic can be very sensitive to the learning rate and the specific GFlowNet algorithm used, sometimes degrading sharply, while TLM maintains stable performance. On bit sequences, TLM outperforms pessimistic for SubTB in mode discovery, and on QM9, TLM with DB surpasses pessimistic in both mode count and Pearson correlation at most learning rates. The one setting where TLM clearly loses is sEH mode discovery with MunchausenDQN, where pessimistic achieves slightly higher mode counts, though the authors attribute this to sEH's strong structural bias from its predefined fragment vocabulary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet–soft RL equivalence established by Tiapkin et al. (2024), which only holds for a fixed backward policy, by formulating backward policy optimization as trajectory likelihood maximization within an alternating minimization over the joint KL objective. It departs from prior backward-policy approaches—Mohammadpour et al. (2024) targeting maximum-entropy policies via a specific RL formulation, and Jang et al. (2024) proposing a pessimistic policy tied to TB—by providing an algorithm-agnostic loss that can be paired with any GFlowNet or soft RL training objective (TB, DB, SubTB, SoftDQN, MunchausenDQN). The theoretical contribution is a convergence guarantee for the two-step procedure under a non-stationary regret framework, filling the gap left by the fixed-policy assumption in the GFlowNet–RL correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TLM offers a simple, drop-in mechanism (one extra MLE gradient step per iteration) that consistently accelerates convergence and improves mode discovery, particularly in less-structured environments like QM9, while being neutral or slightly detrimental in highly structured settings like sEH. This confirms and extends the hypothesis from Mohammadpour et al. (2024) that backward policy optimization is most valuable when the environment lacks inherent structure. Open questions include whether combining TLM with backward-policy-based exploration (Kim et al., 2023; He et al., 2024b) yields further gains, and whether the stability conditions in the convergence theorem (decaying learning rates, target networks) can be relaxed or made automatic. The method's generality—working with any forward-policy algorithm—makes it a practical tool for the GFlowNet community, though its impact is bounded to the GFlowNet/soft-RL niche.
