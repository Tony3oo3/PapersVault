## Problem

Offline alignment methods like DPO, IPO, and KTO suffer from overoptimization: as training progresses, the policy deviates from the fixed reference model, shifting probability mass onto out-of-domain sequences and degrading output quality. This paper identifies the root cause in the vanishing curvature of the loss landscape (driven by σ(s)→0 in the Hessian) and asks whether dynamically updating the reference policy during training can prevent this degradation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard offline preference data—tuples of (prompt, chosen continuation, rejected continuation) from datasets like Anthropic-HH, Reddit TL;DR, or UltraFeedback—alongside an SFT-initialized reference policy. The key operational difference from vanilla DPO/IPO/KTO is that the reference policy is not frozen: it is either softly interpolated toward the current policy at every step (weight α) or hard-replaced by the current policy every τ steps. The output is an aligned text response, evaluated via pairwise win-rate comparisons (AutoSxS with GPT-4 as judge) and standard benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2, Arena-Hard, MixEval).

## Method

The Trust Region (TR) approach updates the reference policy π_ref during training using two strategies: a soft update π_ref ← α·sg(π_θ) + (1−α)·π_ref_prev applied at every optimization step, and a hard update π_ref ← sg(π_θ) applied every τ steps. The theoretical motivation comes from Hessian analysis of the DPO and IPO losses: as the margin s decreases during training, σ(s)→0 causes the Hessian to vanish, flattening the loss landscape and making it difficult to reverse the decrease in in-domain sequence probabilities. Updating the reference policy periodically 'resets' the optimization, restoring curvature and preventing the model from irreversibly leaking probability mass to OOD examples. Applied to DPO, IPO, and KTO, this yields TR-DPO, TR-IPO, and TR-KTO, which at equivalent KL divergence from the SFT policy achieve higher Human-Centric quality metrics and substantially better win rates on AlpacaEval 2 and Arena-Hard (e.g., +9.5 LC points for TR-DPO over DPO on Llama3-Base).

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2 (LC win rate) with Llama3-Base 8B, TR-DPO^τ achieves 27.7% vs. DPO's 18.2% (+9.5 pts), TR-IPO^α achieves 29.5% vs. IPO's 14.4% (+15.1 pts), and TR-KTO^τ achieves 16.5% vs. KTO's 14.2% (+2.3 pts). On Arena-Hard, TR-DPO^α reaches 21.3% WR vs. DPO's 15.9%, and TR-IPO^τ reaches 21.1% vs. IPO's 17.8%. On the task-specific Reddit TL;DR summarization with Pythia 6.9B, TR methods yield win-rate improvements of 8.4% (DPO), 14.3% (IPO), and 15% (KTO) over their baselines. KL-divergence analysis confirms that at equivalent KL levels, TR methods consistently achieve higher Human-Centric (HC) metrics, demonstrating reduced overoptimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TR-DPO consistently outperforms DPO across both AlpacaEval 2 and Arena-Hard: TR-DPO^τ gains +9.5 LC points (27.7% vs. 18.2%) and TR-DPO^α gains +5.4 Arena-Hard WR points (21.3% vs. 15.9%). TR-IPO shows the largest relative improvement, with TR-IPO^α achieving 29.5% LC vs. IPO's 14.4% (+15.1 pts) and TR-IPO^τ reaching 21.1% Arena-Hard WR vs. IPO's 17.8% (+3.3 pts). TR-KTO yields more modest gains: TR-KTO^τ improves AlpacaEval 2 LC by +2.3 pts (16.5% vs. 14.2%) and Arena-Hard WR by +1.8 pts (14.3% vs. 12.5%), while TR-KTO^α shows only marginal improvement on AlpacaEval 2 (+0.5 pts) but a clearer +2.4 pt gain on Arena-Hard. On the task-specific Reddit TL;DR benchmark (Pythia 6.9B), TR-IPO^τ achieves the highest win rate at 52.5% vs. IPO's ~38.2%, and TR-KTO^τ reaches 50.8% vs. KTO's ~35.8%. All TR methods with hard updates (τ) show statistically significant improvements over their base counterparts on both general benchmarks, while soft-update KTO (TR-KTO^α) shows mixed results on Llama3-Base AlpacaEval 2 but solid gains on Arena-Hard and the Instruct setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline preference-optimization lineage (DPO, IPO, KTO) by borrowing the trust-region principle from RL (PPO) to dynamically update the reference policy during training, rather than keeping it fixed as all prior offline methods do. It directly addresses the overoptimization problem identified by Gao et al. (2022) and Rafailov et al. (2024a) in offline settings, where increasing KL divergence from the SFT policy degrades quality. The approach is conceptually related to game-theoretical methods (Nash Learning, DNO) that also update reference policies, but those are inherently online and rely on policy sampling, whereas TR methods operate entirely on pre-constructed preference datasets. The Hessian analysis connecting vanishing loss curvature to probability-mass leakage onto OOD sequences provides a mechanistic explanation absent from prior overoptimization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a minimal modification—periodically merging or copying the current policy into the reference—yields consistent, statistically significant gains across model sizes (2.8B–12B Pythia, 8B Llama3) and benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2, Arena-Hard, task-specific win rates), with only ~3–7% additional training time. This makes it an easily adoptable technique for practitioners already using DPO/IPO/KTO pipelines. Open questions include whether large τ values in hard updates scale to smaller datasets, whether the approach generalizes to other modalities or online settings, and whether the reliance on GPT-4 as a proxy judge limits the validity of the alignment gains. The paper also notes that TR methods do not improve jailbreak robustness, leaving safety against adversarial attacks as an orthogonal, unresolved challenge.
