## Problem

Existing generalist agents (Gato, JAT, MTT) rely on large models pre-trained on massive multi-environment datasets yet still fail to generalize to unseen environments without environment-specific fine-tuning. This paper asks whether scaling model and data size is the most effective path to generalist agents, and instead proposes that retrieval provides a powerful inductive bias for rapid adaptation to new environments with only a few demonstrations and no fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251410/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decision step, REGENT receives a query state (a 512-dim ResNet18 embedding for image observations, a raw proprioceptive vector, or tokenized discrete/text observations), the previous scalar reward, and a retrieved context of 19 nearest (state, reward, action) tuples ordered by proximity to the query. The output is a single action: a softmax distribution over discrete actions or a continuous action vector scaled by a MixedReLU activation. Unusually, the final action is not purely the transformer's prediction but a distance-weighted exponential interpolation between the 1-nearest-neighbor action and the transformer output, so the effective output shifts continuously from replaying a retrieved action to relying on the learned policy as the query moves farther from all demonstrations.

## Method

REGENT is a semi-parametric agent that pre-trains a 12-layer causal transformer (138.6M parameters) on sequences of (retrieved context, query state, query reward) → action, where the context consists of 19 nearest (state, reward, action) tuples from a designated retrieval subset, ordered by closeness to the query. The key architectural insight is a distance-weighted exponential interpolation (Equation 1) between the 1-nearest-neighbor 'Retrieve and Play' action and the transformer's prediction, with the weight governed by e^{−λd(s_t, s′)} where d is the normalized distance to the closest retrieved state. This forces the transformer to learn the residual correction to the R&P action rather than the full action, making in-context generalization to unseen environments easier. The paper further provides a theoretical sub-optimality bound (Theorem 5.2) showing the gap to the expert policy scales with the distance to the most isolated state, which shrinks as more demonstrations are available for retrieval.

## Results

In the JAT/Gato setting, REGENT (138.6M params, 14.5M pre-training transitions) achieves normalized returns of 0.67–0.93 in unseen Metaworld environments and 0.25–0.86 in unseen Atari environments, while JAT/Gato (192.7M params, 5–10× more data) scores near 0.0 in most unseen environments even after full finetuning. In the ProcGen setting, REGENT (116M params, 12M transitions) outperforms MTT (310M params, ~120M transitions) across all 5 unseen environments. The simple R&P baseline (no neural network) already matches or exceeds JAT/Gato in most unseen environments, and REGENT consistently improves upon R&P by 5–20% in normalized return.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REGENT decisively outperforms JAT/Gato (192.7M params) and its All Data variant (5–10× more pre-training data) across all unseen Metaworld and Atari environments, even when JAT/Gato is fully finetuned or PEFT-finetuned (IA3) on demonstrations from the unseen environment. For example, in metaworld-box-close with 100 demos, REGENT achieves 0.93 normalized return versus 0.00 for JAT/Gato and 0.31 for JAT/Gato (All Data) Finetuned. In the ProcGen setting, REGENT (116M params, 12M transitions) surpasses MTT (310M params, ~120M transitions) in all 5 unseen environments, despite using an order-of-magnitude fewer pre-training levels and roughly 3× fewer parameters. The DRIL imitation learning baseline (which requires 1M online interaction steps) is also outperformed by REGENT in all three unseen Atari environments, confirming that REGENT's offline retrieval-augmented approach is more data-efficient. Notably, the non-parametric R&P baseline (1-nearest-neighbor, no training) already matches or exceeds JAT/Gato in most unseen environments, highlighting that retrieval is the critical inductive bias rather than model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

REGENT adapts the JAT/Gato transformer architecture and training recipe but fundamentally repositions the role of pre-training: rather than relying on large-scale multi-environment pre-training to build a generalist policy, it pre-trains a smaller transformer on retrieval-augmented sequences (query state + retrieved nearest-neighbor context), drawing directly from the retrieval-augmented generation paradigm in NLP (RAG, RETRO, REALM). The conceptual departure from prior generalist agents (Gato, MTT, RoboCat) is the introduction of a non-parametric retrieval component (Retrieve and Play) as both a baseline and an architectural element, with a distance-weighted interpolation between the retrieved action and the transformer's prediction. This contrasts with MTT, which places full demonstration trajectories in the transformer context, and with Gato/JAT, which use only the agent's own recent history as context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that scaling model size and pre-training data is the primary path to generalist agents, showing that retrieval provides a powerful inductive bias for rapid adaptation with 3× fewer parameters and an order-of-magnitude less pre-training data. The sub-optimality bound (Theorem 5.2) provides a clean theoretical guarantee linking performance to demonstration coverage, which is rare in this area. Key limitations remain: REGENT struggles with new embodiments (different robot morphologies) and very long-horizon environments, and the retrieval mechanism requires access to a few expert demonstrations in the target environment. Open questions include whether intelligent retrieval set design (e.g., via persistency of excitation) and cross-suite generalization can close these gaps.
