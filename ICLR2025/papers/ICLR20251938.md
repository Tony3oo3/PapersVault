## Problem

Conditional class probability (CCP) estimation via kernel logistic regression is empirically central to long-tailed learning, domain adaptation, and transfer learning, yet its theoretical performance under the cross-entropy loss remains largely unexplored. The CE loss is unbounded as CCPs approach zero, invalidating standard oracle inequalities, and prior attempts either restrict CCP ranges, analyze only truncated losses, or are limited to binary classification. This paper establishes minimax optimal convergence rates for KLR-based CCP estimation in all three complex scenarios under mild Hölder smoothness and small-value bound assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled feature-label pairs (X_i, Y_i) from a source distribution P, plus scenario-specific auxiliary data: unlabeled target features for label shift adaptation, or a small set of auxiliary labeled samples from a related distribution S for transfer learning. The output is a CCP estimator q̂(y|x) for the target distribution Q, constructed by combining the KLR-based source CCP estimate p̂(y|x) with an estimated class probability ratio ŵ(y) = q̂(y)/p̂(y), from which a plug-in classifier argmax_m q̂(m|x) is induced. The KLR model uses a Gaussian kernel in an RKHS with a softmax parameterization and a truncation mechanism that floors all predicted probabilities at a threshold t to bound the CE loss.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a novel error decomposition that splits the CE loss into an upper component (where the true CCP exceeds a truncation threshold t) and a lower component (where it falls below t), causing the unbounded terms to cancel in the excess risk expression and leaving only bounded sample error terms. This enables a new oracle inequality for KLR under the CE loss, where the sample error is controlled via entropy numbers of the Gaussian RKHS and a variance bound specific to the truncated loss structure. For the approximation error, the paper constructs a bounded surrogate score function f*τ defined as the log-ratio of truncated CCPs (floored at τ = γ^α), then approximates it via Gaussian kernel convolution, balancing the trade-off between approximation fidelity and CE loss boundedness. The resulting excess CE risk bound is n_p^{-(1+β∧1)α/((1+β∧1)α+d)+ξ} for long-tailed learning, with additional log(n_q)/n_q or log(n_s)/n_s terms for domain adaptation and transfer learning, and matching lower bounds confirm minimax optimality.

## Results

The CCP-based estimator q̂(y|x) consistently outperforms the standard KLR baseline p̂(y|x) across all three datasets and all three complex classification scenarios. The largest gains appear in Domain Adaptation on Satimage (96.46% vs. 89.80%, +6.66 pp) and Long-tailed Learning on Gas Sensor (90.49% vs. 85.57%, +4.92 pp). On Dionis, the CCP method improves accuracy by 2.9–5.0 pp over the baseline across scenarios. The paper also empirically validates its minimax optimal convergence rates by showing accuracy improves with increasing source sample size n_p and saturates once sufficient unlabeled target samples n_q are available.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the CCP-based method (which estimates q̂(y|x) via the class-probability-ratio reweighting in Eq. 3) against a baseline that directly applies the standard KLR estimator p̂(y|x) without any complex-scenario adjustment. The CCP method wins on all nine dataset–scenario combinations. The most dramatic improvement is in Domain Adaptation on Satimage, where the CCP method reaches 96.46% versus 89.80% for the baseline (+6.66 pp), indicating that the label-shift reweighting is critical when the target label distribution differs substantially from the source. In Long-tailed Learning, gains range from +2.96 pp (Dionis) to +4.92 pp (Gas Sensor), consistent with the logit-adjustment equivalence shown in Appendix B. In Transfer Learning, the CCP method again leads by 3.5–4.3 pp, confirming that mitigating label bias in a pre-trained model is more effective than training from scratch. The paper does not compare against other state-of-the-art long-tailed or domain-adaptation methods; the comparison is strictly between the CCP-adjusted KLR and the unadjusted KLR baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on kernel logistic regression (Zhu & Hastie, 2005) as the core CCP estimation tool and integrates it with existing estimators for complex scenarios: Wen et al.'s (2024) class probability matching for label shift and Zhu et al.'s (2024) Markov-chain-based logit adjustment for transfer learning. It departs from prior theoretical work on CE loss analysis (Farrell et al., 2021; Bos & Schmidt-Hieber, 2022; Zhang et al., 2024) by introducing a novel upper/lower decomposition of the CE loss that cancels unbounded terms, enabling a new oracle inequality for multi-class KLR. The paper explicitly demonstrates that Zhang et al.'s bivariate-function approach, which yields logarithmic variance bounds in binary classification, degrades to polynomial-order bounds in the multi-class setting, motivating the alternative decomposition strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing minimax optimal convergence rates for KLR-based CCP estimation under the CE loss in long-tailed learning, label shift, and label bias scenarios, the paper provides the first rigorous theoretical justification for the empirical success of CCP-based algorithms in these complex settings. The truncation-and-decomposition framework is generalizable to other unbounded losses (exponential, large-margin) and potentially to neural network CCP estimators, as the authors note as future work. However, the practical impact is somewhat limited for modern deep learning practitioners, since KLR is not the dominant CCP estimation tool in current pipelines; the results are most relevant to the statistical learning theory community and to settings where kernel methods remain competitive.
