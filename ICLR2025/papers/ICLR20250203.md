## Problem

Existing CZSL methods model each primitive (attribute or object) with a single centroid representation, ignoring the natural visual diversity that arises when the same primitive appears in different compositions (e.g., 'broken' looks different on a cord vs. a valley). Additionally, prior disentanglement strategies operate only from a local view within a batch or small training subset, failing to capture the global data distribution needed to shape well-structured, independent primitive embedding spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250203/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, encoded by a LoRA-tuned CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder into a composition feature vector, which is then projected via two separate MLP adapters into attribute and object feature spaces. It produces three simultaneous predictions: an attribute label, an object label, and a composition label, with the final composition score computed as the sum of the composition branch probability and the product of the attribute and object branch probabilities. In the open-world setting, a post-training feasibility calibration filters infeasible attribute-object pairs before the argmax.

## Method

CLUSPRO builds on a three-path CLIP-based architecture (attribute, object, composition branches with soft learnable prompts) and introduces a non-parametric within-primitive clustering mechanism that represents each primitive with K prototypes (default K=5) discovered via local-aware optimal transport solved by the GCG algorithm, with momentum-based online updating (μ=0.99) to track evolving feature representations. Two prototype-anchored self-supervised losses reshape the embedding space: Prototype-based Contrastive Learning (PCL) pulls each feature toward its assigned prototype while repelling it from all other K(M+N)−1 prototypes across both branches, and Prototype-based Decorrelation Learning (PDL) minimizes HSIC between attribute features and object prototypes (and vice versa) to enforce conditional independence. The prototypes are purely non-parametric—no additional learnable parameters are introduced—and are discarded entirely at test time, so inference incurs zero extra cost over the base model.

## Results

On the Closed-World (CW) setting, CLUSPRO achieves AUC scores of 23.8 (MIT-States), 46.6 (UT-Zappos), and 14.9 (C-GQA), surpassing the previous best (Troika) by +1.7, +4.9, and +2.5 AUC respectively. On the Open-World (OW) setting, CLUSPRO attains AUC of 9.3, 39.5, and 3.0 on the three datasets, yielding relative gains of +9.4%, +19.7%, and +11.1% over the strongest prior methods. HM improvements are also consistent: +3.6% on MIT-States, +7.1% on UT-Zappos, and +11.6% on C-GQA in CW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLUSPRO outperforms all 23 compared baselines across all three datasets in both CW and OW settings. In CW, the closest competitor is Troika [14] (CVPR 2024), which CLUSPRO surpasses by +4.9 AUC on UT-Zappos (46.6 vs 41.7) and +2.5 AUC on C-GQA (14.9 vs 12.4); on MIT-States, CDS-CZSL [15] is the nearest rival at 22.4 AUC vs CLUSPRO's 23.8. In OW, the gap widens: CLUSPRO leads Troika by +6.5 AUC on UT-Zappos (39.5 vs 33.0) and by +0.3 AUC on C-GQA (3.0 vs 2.7), while on MIT-States it exceeds CDS-CZSL by +0.8 AUC (9.3 vs 8.5). Traditional vision-based methods (e.g., AoP, SCEN, CAPE) lag substantially behind all CLIP-based approaches, with AUC scores typically below 35 on UT-Zappos CW, confirming the dominance of VLM-based CZSL. The efficiency comparison (Table 10) shows CLUSPRO matches the baseline's 8.7M trainable parameters and 14.6 ms inference speed while adding only ~11.5% training time, unlike Troika which requires 21.7M parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLUSPRO builds directly on the three-path CLIP-based paradigm established by Troika [14] and ADE [23], which jointly recognize attribute, object, and composition via soft prompts and separate adapters. Its key departure is from the single-centroid primitive representation used by virtually all prior CZSL methods: instead of one learnable or fixed prototype per primitive, CLUSPRO mines multiple non-parametric prototypes via within-primitive optimal transport clustering (solved with the GCG algorithm), drawing on the prototype-learning and clustering-based representation learning literature. The two proposed losses—prototype-anchored contrastive learning and HSIC-based decorrelation learning—extend standard contrastive and independence objectives by anchoring them to discovered sub-primitive structure rather than batch-level sample pairs, shifting disentanglement from a local (batch) view to a global (dataset-level) view.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that intra-primitive visual diversity (e.g., 'broken' meaning disconnection for 'cord' but ruggedness for 'valley') is a first-order factor for CZSL generalization, and that a small set of non-parametric prototypes can capture this diversity without adding test-time parameters or inference cost. Open questions remain around long-tail primitives where clustering is unreliable, the fixed choice of K per primitive, and the inherent data-overlap concern with CLIP's web-scale pre-training that complicates fair comparison with non-CLIP baselines. The approach could inform broader prototype-based representation learning beyond CZSL, particularly in settings where class-internal variance is high.
