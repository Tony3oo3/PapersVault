## Problem

Traditional deep learning networks apply a fixed computational graph to all inputs regardless of complexity, wasting resources on simple examples and under-processing difficult ones. While adaptive computation methods exist (early exit, stochastic depth, DEQs), none combine a dedicated introspection mechanism with fixed-point iteration to dynamically adjust both which layers are engaged and how many refinement iterations each layer performs per input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252151/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet), text sequences (WikiText-2/103), or question-context pairs (SQuAD) and produces class labels, next-token predictions, or answer spans respectively. Unusually, the introspection network consumes intermediate activations from the prediction network (not raw inputs) to emit a binary layer-selection mask, and an input complexity score—combining softmax confidence, output entropy, and input gradient norm—caps the maximum FPI iterations per layer at max(10·IC(x), 50).

## Method

The MIND model pairs a standard prediction network (3-layer CNN, dual-layer LSTM, or 6-layer Transformer) with a lightweight introspection network that maps pooled intermediate activations to a discrete layer-selection mask via Gumbel-Softmax sampling. Selected layers undergo fixed-point iteration (z^(k+1) = f(z^(k), x; θ)) until relative convergence or a complexity-capped iteration limit, while unselected layers perform a single forward pass. Backpropagation through the FPI layers uses phantom gradients, approximating the full Jacobian product with a single-step surrogate to avoid unrolling. The composite loss balances prediction accuracy against an introspection penalty that discourages unnecessary layer engagement and extra iterations, with hyperparameters tuned per task. The key architectural insight is that the introspection network operates on the prediction network's own representations rather than raw inputs, requiring far fewer parameters (0.3M) to make informed depth decisions.

## Results

On ImageNet, the MIND model (5.31M params, 3-layer CNN) achieves 88.3% Top-1 and 96.62% Top-5 accuracy, surpassing ResNet-50 (74.3%/91.6%, 25.6M) by 14.0 points and EfficientNet-B7 (84.3%/95.56%, 66M) by 4.0/1.06 points respectively. On CIFAR-100, MIND reaches 85.53% Top-1 and 92.6% Top-5, outperforming both baselines. For language tasks, MIND-Transformer (112M params) achieves 14.5 PPL on WikiText-2 (vs. 29.2 for standard Transformer) and 88.7/81.01 F1/EM on SQuAD v2.0 (vs. 83.7/80.5 for RoBERTa-base), while LSTM-MIND (8.6M) reduces WikiText-2 PPL from 99.3 to 14.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIND decisively outperforms ResNet-50 on both vision benchmarks: +15.6 Top-1 on CIFAR-100 (85.53% vs. 69.9%) and +14.0 Top-1 on ImageNet (88.3% vs. 74.3%), while using roughly 5× fewer parameters. Against EfficientNet-B7, MIND gains +1.1 Top-1 on CIFAR-100 and +4.0 Top-1 on ImageNet with ~12× fewer parameters, also leading on multi-label accuracy (78% vs. 75%) and ImageNetV2 robustness (83% vs. 81%). In language modeling, MIND-Transformer beats the original Transformer (Vaswani et al.) by 14.7 PPL on WikiText-2 (14.5 vs. 29.2) and 2.0 PPL on WikiText-103 (16.3 vs. 18.3), and surpasses both BERT-base (76.8 F1) and RoBERTa-base (83.7 F1) on SQuAD v2.0 with 88.7 F1. Against adaptive-computation baselines in Table 6, MIND leads BranchyNet by +17.5 Top-1 on ImageNet (88.2% vs. 64.24%), SDN by +19.7 points, and DEQ (Broyden) by +6.4 points, while also achieving the lowest inference time (20 ms vs. 148–186 ms for DEQ). The LSTM-MIND model reduces WikiText-2 PPL from 99.3 (standard LSTM) to 14.8, a 6.7× improvement, using fewer parameters (8.6M vs. 20M).

## Relation to Prior Work

The MIND model synthesizes three established research threads: fixed-point iteration layers (from DEQs and Liao et al.'s recurrent backpropagation), adaptive computation time (Graves, Figurnov et al.), and dynamic layer selection (DynaLay, Mixture-of-Depth). Its key conceptual departure is introducing a dedicated introspection network that operates on the prediction network's internal activations rather than raw inputs, using Gumbel-Softmax to produce a differentiable layer-selection mask that gates FPI iterations per layer. For backpropagation through the iterative layers, it adopts phantom gradients (Geng et al., 2021) to avoid unrolling or computing inverse Jacobians, positioning itself as a practical training recipe for implicit architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the reported results are reproducible, a 3-layer CNN with 5.3M parameters outperforming ResNet-50 and EfficientNet-B7 by 4–14 points on ImageNet would represent a substantial shift in the depth-vs-efficiency tradeoff, suggesting that iterative refinement of a small number of layers can substitute for brute-force depth. However, the very large margins over strong baselines, combined with the fact that FPI iterations add hidden computation beyond the nominal '3 layers,' warrant careful scrutiny before the approach is widely adopted. Open questions include whether the introspection overhead remains negligible at scale (e.g., 1B+ parameter models), whether the approach transfers to generative or multimodal settings, and whether the adaptive depth mechanism provides genuine robustness gains or primarily benefits in-distribution accuracy.
