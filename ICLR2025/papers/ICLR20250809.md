## Problem

Existing federated model-free Q-learning algorithms face a fundamental trade-off: those achieving almost optimal regret (e.g., concurrent UCB-Advantage) require O(MT) communication cost by sharing raw trajectories, while those with logarithmic communication cost (e.g., FedQ-Hoeffding/Bernstein) only attain suboptimal regret of Õ(√(MH³SAT)). The paper asks whether an almost optimal federated model-free RL algorithm can simultaneously enjoy logarithmic communication cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250809/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on tabular episodic MDPs with S states, A actions, and H steps per episode, where M agents independently interact with copies of the same MDP under a central server. Rather than sharing raw trajectories, each agent transmits only round-wise aggregated sums of value-function evaluations (reference, advantage, value, and their squares) over visits to each (s,a,h) tuple, yielding O(MHS) scalars per communication round independent of episode count. The server outputs updated Q-function estimates, V-function estimates, a deterministic policy, and a settled reference function that is updated at most once per (s,h) pair during the entire learning process.

## Method

FedQ-Advantage combines reference-advantage decomposition (splitting the Bellman backup into a global-history reference mean and a stage-wise advantage mean) with a novel aligned-round/unaligned-stage architecture: communication rounds are synchronized across all agents, but policy-switching stages are tracked independently per (s,a,h) tuple with exponentially growing sizes. The key mechanism is a heterogeneous event-triggered communication threshold c_h^k(s,a) that permits more visits in early rounds of a stage (when existing stage visits are small) and tighter limits in later rounds, reducing intra-stage synchronizations from O(f_M·H) to O(f_M·log H) and cutting the total communication cost to O(f_M·M·H³·S²·A·(log H)·log T). An optional forced-synchronization flag lets the server abort all agents as soon as any single agent hits its threshold, trading balanced exploration for reduced waiting time under heterogeneous agent speeds. The regret analysis requires a non-martingale concentration argument that relates stage-wise weighted sums to martingale differences, since the per-visit weight depends on the total stage visit count that is not causally known during exploration.

## Results

FedQ-Advantage achieves an almost optimal regret of Õ(√(H²SAMT)), matching the information-theoretic lower bound Ω(√(H²SAMT)) up to logarithmic factors, while requiring only O(f_M MH³S²A(log H)log T) total communication cost (logarithmic in T). This is the first model-free federated RL algorithm to simultaneously achieve almost optimal regret and logarithmic communication cost. In numerical experiments (H=10, S=5, A=5, M=10, 10⁵ episodes per agent), FedQ-Advantage shows consistently lower regret and fewer communication rounds than FedQ-Hoeffding and FedQ-Bernstein across all 10 sample paths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest federated baselines FedQ-Hoeffding and FedQ-Bernstein (Zheng et al., 2024a), FedQ-Advantage improves the regret from Õ(√(H⁴SAMT)) and Õ(√(H³SAMT)) to Õ(√(H²SAMT)) while also reducing communication cost from O(M²H⁴S²A log T) to O(f_M MH³S²A(log H)log T), a strict improvement on both axes. Against the single-agent Concurrent UCB-Advantage (Zhang et al., 2020), FedQ-Advantage matches the same Õ(√(H²SAMT)) regret but replaces the linear O(MT) communication cost with a logarithmic one, enabling near-linear regret speedup in M without proportional communication overhead. Against the concurrent algorithms of Bai et al. (2019), FedQ-Advantage dominates on both regret (H² vs H⁴/H³) and communication (logarithmic vs linear in T). In the numerical experiments (Figure 2), FedQ-Advantage achieves lower regret and fewer communication rounds than both FedQ-Hoeffding and FedQ-Bernstein across all 10 random seeds, confirming the theoretical advantages. The multi-agent speedup experiment (Figure 3) shows FedQ-Advantage outperforms the single-agent UCB-A baseline with 10⁵ episodes, though it incurs a larger regret than UCB-A with 10⁶ episodes due to the multi-agent burn-in cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: single-agent Q-learning with reference-advantage decomposition (Zhang et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021), which achieves almost optimal regret but was not designed for federated settings, and federated Q-learning (Zheng et al., 2024a), which achieves logarithmic communication cost but only suboptimal H^3 regret. The key departure is adapting the reference-advantage decomposition to a federated architecture with aligned communication rounds and unaligned stage-wise updates, combined with heterogeneous event-triggered communication thresholds that reduce intra-stage synchronizations from O(H) to O(log H). Unlike concurrent federated algorithms (Bai et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2020) that share raw trajectories at O(MT) communication cost, FedQ-Advantage shares only round-wise aggregated sums, maintaining logarithmic communication while preserving the variance-reduction benefits of the reference-advantage decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model-free federated RL algorithm to simultaneously achieve almost optimal regret (matching the information bound up to logarithmic factors) and logarithmic communication cost, resolving an open question explicitly posed in the literature. The near-linear speedup in the number of agents with only a poly(HSA, 1/β) burn-in overhead makes the approach practically relevant for large-scale multi-agent systems. However, the results are confined to tabular episodic MDPs with identical environments across agents and a central server, leaving open the extension to function approximation, continuous state spaces, heterogeneous MDPs, and asynchronous or decentralized architectures. The optional forced synchronization mechanism also introduces a trade-off between waiting time and communication rounds that may require careful tuning in practice.
