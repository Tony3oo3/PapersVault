## Problem

Existing zero-shot image personalization methods struggle to simultaneously preserve fine-grained subject details and maintain text fidelity, and these challenges are severely amplified in multi-subject scenarios where subject neglect, disproportionate dominance, and inter-subject conflicts commonly arise. No prior approach combines multi-reference zero-shot personalization with explicit layout guidance while keeping the base diffusion model fully frozen.

## Input / Output

The method accepts up to 4 reference subject images, a text prompt, per-subject entity names, and normalized bounding boxes specifying where each subject should appear in the output. Reference images are processed independently through the grounding resampler (so subjects do not interfere during projection), and the resulting token sequences are concatenated into a single image condition vector. The output is a single 1024×1024 image (SDXL resolution) in which each referenced subject is rendered within its designated spatial region while the text prompt governs the overall scene composition and background.

## Method

MS-Diffusion introduces two key modules on top of a frozen SDXL backbone. First, a Grounding Resampler (a perceiver-style attention module) extracts subject-specific features from each reference image using learnable queries initialized with grounding tokens—concatenations of CLIP text embeddings of entity names and Fourier embeddings of bounding boxes—thereby injecting semantic and spatial priors into the projected image condition. Second, a Multi-subject Cross-Attention mechanism applies per-subject binary attention masks that restrict each subject's image key-value tokens to interact only with query patches inside that subject's bounding box, while dummy background tokens (randomly initialized, masked out of the final image condition) absorb attention in unassigned regions to prevent subject overcontrol. Training uses the standard IP-Adapter noise-prediction objective on 3.6M video-clip pairs (two frames from the same clip serve as reference and ground truth to avoid copy-paste artifacts), with grounding tokens randomly dropped at 10% probability to prevent inference-time dependence. All pre-trained SDXL weights remain frozen; only the resampler and image cross-attention projection layers are trained.

## Results

On the zero-shot single-subject benchmark (DreamBench), MS-Diffusion achieves the highest DINO score (0.671) and CLIP-T score (0.321), indicating superior detail preservation and text fidelity, though it trails IP-Adapter (0.810) and Kosmos-G (0.822) on CLIP-I. On the newly introduced MS-Bench for multi-subject personalization, MS-Diffusion attains the best M-DINO (0.108) and CLIP-T (0.341), while tying SSR-Encoder on DINO (0.425) and trailing it on CLIP-I (0.698 vs. 0.725). The ablation study confirms that the Grounding Resampler contributes most to DINO gains and the Multi-subject Cross-Attention is critical for CLIP-T in multi-subject settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In zero-shot single-subject personalization, MS-Diffusion outperforms all baselines on DINO (0.671 vs. 0.613–0.621 for IP-Adapter, ELITE, and λ-ECLIPSE) and CLIP-T (0.321 vs. 0.292–0.308), but trails IP-Adapter (0.810), Kosmos-G (0.822), and SSR-Encoder (0.821) on CLIP-I, which the authors attribute to CLIP-I being susceptible to background overfitting. In multi-subject personalization, MS-Diffusion achieves the highest CLIP-T (0.341 vs. 0.303 for SSR-Encoder and 0.316 for λ-ECLIPSE) and M-DINO (0.108 vs. 0.107 and 0.094), confirming better text adherence and more balanced subject rendering, while matching SSR-Encoder on DINO (0.425) and losing on CLIP-I (0.698 vs. 0.725). Against tuning-based methods (DreamBooth, BLIP-Diffusion*, λ-ECLIPSE*), MS-Diffusion* achieves competitive or superior DINO and CLIP-T without per-subject fine-tuning. Qualitative comparisons with Mix-of-Show, Cones2, and Break-A-Scene show MS-Diffusion avoids subject overfitting and interaction artifacts that plague those tuning-based approaches, while requiring no test-time tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MS-Diffusion extends the zero-shot image personalization paradigm established by IP-Adapter (which projects image embeddings into the diffusion model's cross-attention condition space) and the perceiver-resampler architecture from Flamingo (which uses learnable queries to distill information from image features). It departs from prior multi-subject personalization methods (SSR-Encoder, λ-ECLIPSE) that rely on implicit attention losses or MLLM-based interleaving by introducing explicit spatial layout guidance through grounding tokens (entity + box embeddings) and a masked multi-subject cross-attention that confines each subject's influence to a designated region. The paper positions itself as the first framework to simultaneously achieve zero-shot operation, multi-subject support, base-model freezing, MLLM-free design, and layout guidance, filling a gap identified in Table 1 where no prior method satisfies all five criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MS-Bench (4,488 evaluation samples across 13 combination types) provides the field with a more comprehensive multi-subject personalization benchmark than prior single-subject-focused evaluations. The masked cross-attention mechanism offers a clean, plug-and-play solution to the subject-neglect and subject-conflict problems that plague existing zero-shot multi-subject methods, while preserving text controllability without modifying the base model. Key limitations remain: the method requires explicit bounding-box layout input at inference time, box-based positioning lacks precision for strong inter-subject interactions, and background elements in reference images can leak into results. An open direction the authors identify is learning layout during training to eliminate the inference-time layout requirement.
