## Problem

Existing time series imputation methods are trained to fit a single empirical process, making them overly specific and unable to generalize to a second process even when both are governed by similar ODEs. This paper asks whether a single pretrained model can perform zero-shot imputation across vastly different dynamical systems of arbitrary dimensionality, without any fine-tuning on the target data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250878/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy, sparse 1D time series (values and observation times) with either point-wise or temporal missing patterns, applies per-instance min-max normalization, and processes each channel independently. For temporal gaps, the series is split into K=5 sequential sets with one set entirely missing. The model outputs a Gaussian distribution over the time derivative f(t) and initial condition x(0) at any query time; the imputed solution is obtained by numerically integrating f(t) from x(0). For long series exceeding L_max=128 observations, overlapping windows are processed separately and combined via linear interpolation on overlaps.

## Method

The approach frames imputation as amortized inference: rather than learning the ODE vector field, the model infers the latent initial condition x(0) and time derivative f(t) that determine the solution. FIM-ℓ (for point-wise gaps) uses a DeepONet-inspired architecture where a BiLSTM branch net encodes context points into a fixed-size vector, a trunk net encodes the query time via learnable sinusoidal embeddings, and their concatenation is projected through FFNs to yield Gaussian parameters for f(t) and x(0). It is trained on 2M synthetic ODE solutions generated from random Chebyshev expansions and RBF-kernel GPs. FIM (for temporal gaps) freezes FIM-ℓ and adds a sequence-processing network ψ₂ that maps context vectors from all available sets (plus local scale statistics) into a context vector for the missing set, which is then fed through the frozen FIM-ℓ trunk to estimate f(t) along the gap; it is trained on 500K periodic-kernel GP functions with 20% temporal gaps. The key distinction from prior work is that the same pretrained weights and representation semantics (time derivative and initial condition) are reused across all target datasets without any parameter updates.

## Results

On ODEBench (63 ODEs), FIM-ℓ achieves a time-derivative MAE of 2.44±0.05 and ODE-solution MAE of 0.17±0.01 (γ=0), outperforming ODEFormer (8.00±0.40 and 1.18±0.05) by roughly 3×. On 8 real-world datasets with 50% point-wise missing values, FIM-ℓ achieves the best MAE in 4 of 8 cases (e.g., Electricity: 0.083 vs. next-best 0.798; Solar: 0.595 vs. 0.734; GuangZhou: 2.427 vs. 2.687; Italy: 0.215 vs. 0.285). For temporal missing patterns (20% gap), FIM outperforms LatentODE on Navier-Stokes (0.063 vs. 0.076 MAE in PCA space) and matches it on Motion Capture (1.765 vs. 1.658), while beating cubic spline interpolation by 2–3× across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ODEFormer (symbolic regression, d'Ascoli et al. 2024), FIM-ℓ dominates on ODEBench: it reduces time-derivative MAE from 8.00 to 2.44 and ODE-solution MAE from 1.18 to 0.17 (γ=0), a ~3× improvement, and maintains this advantage under multiplicative noise (γ=0.05). On the 8 real-world point-wise imputation datasets, FIM-ℓ (applied zero-shot) beats BRITS, SAITS, GP-VAE, CSDI, and BayOTIDE on Italy, GuangZhou, Solar, and Electricity, but trails SAITS on Pedestrian (0.273 vs. 0.205) and ETT_h1 (0.279 vs. 0.223), and lags BRITS/CSDI on PeMS (0.365 vs. 0.287/0.288). For temporal missing patterns, FIM matches LatentODE on Motion Capture (1.765 vs. 1.658 MAE in PCA space) and outperforms it on Navier-Stokes (0.063 vs. 0.076), while beating cubic spline interpolation by 2–3× in all settings. The paper also reports (in appendices) that FIM-ℓ outperforms LatentODE trained on the Lorenz system and NeuralODEProcesses in low-data regimes, further supporting its zero-shot generalization advantage over task-specific trained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the amortized inference framework of Stuhlmuller et al. (2013) and the trunk-branch architecture of DeepONets (Lu et al., 2021) to create a zero-shot imputation model for ODE-governed time series. It departs from the dominant paradigm in time series imputation (BRITS, SAITS, CSDI, GP-VAE, LatentODE), where each model is trained on a single target dataset, by instead training on a large synthetic distribution of ODE solutions and their noisy observations. The closest prior work, ODEFormer (d'Ascoli et al., 2024), also generates synthetic ODE data but infers vector fields symbolically and is limited to six-dimensional systems, whereas FIM infers initial conditions and time derivatives directly and scales to arbitrary dimensionality via a channel-independent strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single pretrained model can impute missing values across 63 distinct ODE systems and 10 diverse real-world domains (motion capture, air quality, traffic, electricity, Navier-Stokes) without any fine-tuning, often outperforming methods trained on the target data. The main limitation is that performance degrades when the target data's local dynamics fall outside the synthetic distribution (e.g., rapid oscillations in the Van der Pol oscillator). Open questions include whether the approach extends to zero-shot forecasting, whether richer synthetic distributions can cover more real-world patterns, and how the method behaves under extreme sparsity or non-ODE-governed dynamics.
