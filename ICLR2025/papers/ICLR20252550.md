## Problem

Designing distributed safe control policies for multi-agent systems is difficult when dynamics are unknown and discrete-time, sensing is limited (causing changing neighborhoods), inputs are constrained, and no performant nominal policy is available. Existing CBF-based methods require known continuous-time dynamics, control-affine structure, or a pre-existing nominal policy, and prior RL+CBF approaches have only addressed single-agent settings or rely on solving nonlinear safety-filter programs that are intractable without control-affine dynamics.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local observation consisting of relative positions and velocities of neighbors within a fixed sensing radius R, LiDAR ray readings for obstacle detection (8 shortest of 32 rays), and goal/landmark positions, structured as a directed graph with node features (state + type one-hot) and edge features (relative kinematics). The method outputs per-agent control actions (accelerations for double-integrator agents, steering angle and acceleration for bicycle agents, or forces in MuJoCo) and simultaneously produces a learned DGCBF parameterized by a graph attention network. Unusually, the method requires both stochastic and deterministic rollouts of the same policy: stochastic rollouts train the cost-value function and policy, while deterministic rollouts (taking the mode) train the constraint-value functions that serve as the DGCBFs.

## Method

DGPPO's key theoretical insight is that the constraint-value function of any deterministic policy is a valid DCBF (Theorem 2), enabling DCBF construction via policy evaluation without knowing the dynamics. For the multi-agent case with changing neighborhoods, the authors define a Discrete Graph CBF (DGCBF) and show that a GNN with graph attention naturally satisfies the DGCBF condition during neighborhood changes because attention weights decay to zero for agents near the sensing boundary (Informal Theorem 4). Training follows MAPPO's on-policy structure but replaces the standard PPO advantage with a modified pseudo-advantage that switches between the GAE cost signal (when all DCBF constraints are satisfied) and a weighted constraint-violation signal (when violated), implementing a CRPO-style primal method. Score function gradients are used to compute DCBF constraint gradients without access to the dynamics, and an approximate gradient projection (via an indicator function on the Q-value) decouples cost minimization from constraint satisfaction in a single backward pass. A scheduled weight ν (starting at 1, doubling at 50% and 75% of training) balances exploration and safety emphasis.

## Results

Across 7 environments (4 LiDAR, 1 MuJoCo, 2 VMAS) with N=3 agents, DGPPO consistently achieves the lowest cost among all methods with a safety rate close to 100%, using a single fixed set of hyperparameters. Baselines (InforMARL+Penalty and MAPPO-Lagrangian) require environment-specific hyperparameter tuning and exhibit a safety-performance tradeoff: low penalty weights yield lower cost but reduced safety, while high weights yield near-perfect safety at substantially higher cost. DGPPO also demonstrates more stable training (smoother cost and safety curves) and scales to N=5 and N=7 agents without performance degradation, whereas baselines become inconsistent with more agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGPPO outperforms both baseline families across all 7 environments. Against InforMARL+Penalty (Nayak et al., 2023), DGPPO achieves comparable or lower cost at near-100% safety, whereas the penalty methods must trade off: low β (0.02) gives lower cost but reduced safety, while high β (0.5) or the Schedule variant achieves ~100% safety at substantially higher cost, and the optimal β differs per environment. Against MAPPO-Lagrangian (Gu et al., 2023), DGPPO is more stable and consistent: the Lagrangian baselines exhibit high variance and spikes in both cost and safety during training (consistent with known instability of Lagrangian methods at zero constraint threshold), and their performance is sensitive to the Lagrange multiplier initialization (λ₀=1 vs. 5) and learning rate. DGPPO's key advantage is hyperparameter insensitivity: a single set of hyperparameters (α=0.3, ν schedule 1→2→4) works across all environments, while both baselines require per-environment tuning to achieve competitive results. On scalability (N=5, 7 on LINE), DGPPO maintains its performance advantage while baselines become inconsistent (e.g., Schedule is mostly safe at N=5 but degrades at N=7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: discrete-time CBFs (Ahmadi et al., 2019), graph CBFs for multi-agent systems with changing neighborhoods (Zhang et al., 2025, GCBF+), and constrained policy optimization via CRPO (Xu et al., 2021) within a MAPPO-style RL framework (Yu et al., 2022). The key conceptual departure from prior CBF+RL work (Cheng et al., 2019; Emam et al., 2022) is eliminating the need for a nominal policy and control-affine dynamics by directly constraining the learned policy to satisfy DCBF conditions, and extending So et al.'s (2024) continuous-time insight that constraint-value functions are valid CBFs to the discrete-time setting. The DGCBF construction via graph attention networks (Veličković et al., 2017) with zero-weighting of distant agents is a discrete-time analogue of the GCBF framework, but removes the continuity assumption that the continuous-time proof relies on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: most CBF-based safety methods assume continuous-time dynamics, known models, and an available nominal policy, none of which hold for real discrete-time multi-agent systems with limited sensing. The DGCBF framework and the proof that a single learned DGCBF generalizes to arbitrary numbers of agents (Theorem A5) are clean theoretical contributions that could simplify deployment. Key limitations include the 2× sample cost from requiring both stochastic and deterministic rollouts, the lack of safety guarantees under stochastic dynamics, and the acknowledged difficulty of actually achieving DGCBF satisfaction at all states in practice. Open questions include whether the gradient projection trick (Informal Theorem 3) can be made rigorous under less restrictive parameterization assumptions, and whether the approach extends to stochastic or partially observable dynamics.
