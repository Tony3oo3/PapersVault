## Problem

It remains unclear how knowledge is distributed across the layers of a pretrained LLM. This paper investigates whether the deeper layers of transformer-based language models are actually necessary for storing the knowledge required to answer common questions, by systematically removing blocks of layers and measuring downstream performance degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252953/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained decoder-only LLM (Llama-2 7B/13B/70B, Qwen 7B/14B, Mistral-7B, or Phi-2 2.7B), a target number of layers n to remove, and a neutral pretraining dataset (C4) on which angular distances between layer representations are computed using only the final token of each sequence. The output is a pruned model with n fewer layers, optionally followed by a QLoRA healing step (4-bit quantized, 164M–328M C4 tokens) that fits on a single 40GB A100 GPU.

## Method

The core insight is that in a residual transformer, if representations evolve slowly across layers (x^(ℓ) ≈ x^(ℓ−n)), then removing those n layers introduces only a small mismatch at the pruning interface. The algorithm computes the normalized angular distance d(x^(ℓ), x^(ℓ+n)) over a C4 sample, finds the block start ℓ* that minimizes this distance for a given n, removes layers ℓ* through ℓ*+n−1, and reconnects the residual stream. An optional healing step applies QLoRA finetuning to repair the interface mismatch. A simpler variant—removing the deepest n layers while preserving the final layer—achieves comparable results after healing, suggesting deeper layers are underutilized. Experiments show up to ~50% of layers can be pruned with minimal QA degradation (MMLU, BoolQ), while reasoning tasks (GSM8K, HellaSwag) degrade immediately, implying shallow layers store knowledge and deeper layers support higher-level computation.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that up to ~50% of layers can be removed from Llama-2-70B with minimal degradation on MMLU and BoolQ (5-shot), with the sharp phase transition to random guessing (25% for MMLU) occurring at 45–55% pruning fraction for Llama-2 models, 35% for Mistral-7B, 25% for Phi-2, and 20% for Qwen models. After healing with QLoRA (164M–328M C4 tokens, 4-bit quantization), the normalized C4 validation loss increases slowly and linearly up to at least 80% pruning, maintaining continuity through the QA phase transition. In contrast, reasoning benchmarks GSM8K and HellaSwag exhibit immediate performance degradation with any amount of layer removal, while CoT-MMLU remains robust similar to standard MMLU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external pruning or compression baselines; instead, it contrasts two internal strategies: the similarity-informed pruning (finding the optimal block via angular distance minimization) versus a simple heuristic that drops the deepest layers excluding the final layer. Without healing, the simple heuristic performs poorly, with QA accuracy decaying rapidly to near-random and loss increasing sharply even at small pruning fractions. After QLoRA healing, the two strategies produce nearly identical results across MMLU, BoolQ, and C4 loss, with the similarity-informed method marginally outperforming on loss and slightly better preserving accuracy before the phase transition. This equivalence after healing provides strong evidence that the finetuning step primarily heals the pruning-interface mismatch rather than acquiring new knowledge. Across model families, larger and deeper models (Llama-2-13B, 70B) tolerate more pruning before the phase transition, while the Qwen family shows an unusually short robust region (~20%), attributed to anomalous 'islands' of high similarity in shallow layers observed in the angular distance heatmaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structured pruning literature for transformers (Voita et al., 2019; Michel et al., 2019; Kim and Awadalla, 2020; Fan et al., 2019) by introducing angular distance between layer representations as the pruning criterion, grounded in the residual-network intuition that slowly-varying representations make individual layer contributions negligible. It departs from prior pruning work in two key ways: (1) it frames the investigation as a science-of-deep-learning question about where knowledge is stored rather than purely a compression method, and (2) it pairs pruning with a lightweight QLoRA 'healing' step to repair the interface mismatch, building on parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Dettmers et al., 2023). The finding that a trivial 'remove deepest layers' heuristic nearly matches the similarity-informed strategy also connects to the logit-lens and tuned-lens literature on how representations evolve across depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that up to ~50% of the deepest layers in Llama-2-70B can be removed with minimal QA degradation (after QLoRA healing) has direct practical implications for model compression and inference cost reduction, and raises a fundamental question about whether current pretraining objectives properly leverage deep-layer parameters. The decoupling between QA accuracy (robust, with a sharp phase transition) and next-token perplexity (smoothly degrading) suggests that different capabilities are stored at different depths, with shallow layers encoding knowledge and deep layers supporting reasoning. Key open questions include whether pretraining modifications (layer dropout, early exit) could make deep layers more essential, whether the phase transition in QA accuracy can be eliminated by better healing, and whether knowledge is truly localized to shallow layers or delocalized in a way that only becomes apparent under more comprehensive evaluations.
