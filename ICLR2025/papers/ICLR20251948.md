## Problem

Existing MPC-based MBRL methods like TD-MPC2 learn their network policy and value function in a model-free manner, which struggles on complex high-dimensional locomotion tasks. The resulting performance gap between the network policy and the MPC policy indicates poor value estimation, which in turn degrades the quality of online planning. This paper asks whether the MPC planner itself can be leveraged to bootstrap better policy and value learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251948/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a proprioceptive state vector (e.g., joint positions, velocities) which is encoded into a latent representation by a neural encoder. The network policy outputs a multivariate Gaussian over a continuous action vector (up to 61 dimensions for the Unitree robot in HumanoidBench). During inference, MPPI planning with a horizon of 3 samples action sequences from a time-dependent Gaussian, evaluates them via the learned latent dynamics and reward model, and executes the first action of the optimized sequence. The network policy alone can also serve as a low-latency inference strategy without online planning.

## Method

BMPC replaces TD-MPC2's model-free max-Q policy learning with expert imitation: the network policy is trained to match the action distribution produced by MPC (parameterized as a multivariate Gaussian) via a KL-divergence objective with entropy regularization and percentile-based loss normalization. For value learning, BMPC uses an on-policy model-based TD-target (N=1) with a V-network instead of off-policy Q-iteration, avoiding distribution shift issues when the policy changes rapidly. A lazy reanalyze mechanism maintains an expert dataset in the replay buffer by re-planning only a small batch (b=20) every k=10 updates, reducing the reanalyze ratio from ~99% to 0.8% while preserving imitation quality. The bootstrapping loop—network policy guides MPPI, MPC produces expert targets, network policy imitates the expert, and the improved policy yields better value estimates for terminal values during planning—closes the gap between network and MPC policy performance, particularly on high-dimensional locomotion tasks.

## Results

On 7 high-dimensional locomotion tasks in DMControl, BMPC achieves a 300% improvement in data efficiency over TD-MPC2, requiring only 90k environment steps to reach 90% of asymptotic reward versus 360k for TD-MPC2. BMPC uses a smaller network (3M parameters) compared to TD-MPC2 (5M) and DreamerV3 (12M), while matching or exceeding their performance across 28 DMControl and 14 HumanoidBench tasks. The network policy learned by BMPC performs nearly on par with its MPC policy, closing the performance gap that is substantial in TD-MPC2 on complex tasks like Dog Run and Humanoid Run. Lazy reanalyze reduces the reanalyze ratio from 99% to 0.8% with only a 10–20% increase in training wall-time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMPC is compared against three baselines: SAC (model-free actor-critic), DreamerV3 (Dyna-style MBRL, 12M params), and TD-MPC2 (MPC-based MBRL, 5M params). Against TD-MPC2, the closest competitor, BMPC achieves a 300% improvement in data efficiency on high-dimensional locomotion tasks (90k vs. 360k steps-to-solve) while using fewer parameters (3M vs. 5M) and comparable training wall-time. BMPC also outperforms TD-MPC2 in asymptotic performance and training stability (tighter 95% CIs) on the 7 most challenging locomotion tasks, and its network policy nearly matches the MPC policy, whereas TD-MPC2's network policy lags significantly on complex tasks. Against DreamerV3 and SAC, BMPC consistently achieves superior or comparable performance across all 28 DMControl and 14 HumanoidBench tasks, with the largest margins on high-dimensional locomotion. The only minor trade-off is a ~20% increase in per-step training time from lazy reanalyze, which is offset by the smaller network and higher data efficiency, yielding a 2× faster time-to-solve on Dog Walk (0.87h vs. 2.03h).

## Relation to Prior Work

BMPC builds directly on TD-MPC2's world model architecture and MPPI planning algorithm, but departs from its model-free (SAC-style max-Q) policy learning by replacing it with expert imitation of the MPC policy, inspired by the expert iteration framework from AlphaGo/AlphaZero. The lazy reanalyze mechanism adapts the reanalyze concept from tree-search expert iteration (MuZero, EfficientZero) to the MPC setting, reducing the reanalyze ratio from ~99% to 0.8%. Conceptually, BMPC sits at the intersection of guided policy search (Levine & Koltun, 2013) and MPC imitation methods (Pan et al., 2017; Sacks & Boots, 2022), but uniquely closes the loop by using the learned policy to both guide planning and compute on-policy TD-targets for value learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BMPC addresses a concrete and previously underappreciated limitation in plan-based MBRL: the persistent performance gap between the network policy and the MPC policy in high-dimensional locomotion tasks, which degrades value estimation and ultimately MPC performance. The ablation finding that the improved network policy's contribution comes through better value learning (not through guiding MPC sampling) is a useful conceptual clarification for the field. Open questions include whether the TD-horizon limitation (N=1 due to short-horizon world models) can be overcome with better models, whether expert imitation can be combined with max-Q gradients for joint improvement, and whether the approach extends to multi-task and offline settings. The practical implication that the network policy can replace online planning for real-time control is significant for robotics deployment.
