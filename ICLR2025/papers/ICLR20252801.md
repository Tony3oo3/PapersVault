## Problem

Existing automatic metrics for evaluating generated human motions—error-based distances to ground truth, handcrafted heuristics (foot contact, penetration), and distributional measures like FID—fail to align with how humans actually perceive motion quality. This misalignment hinders both objective comparison of generation methods and the ability to use evaluation signals to improve generators, since none of these metrics operate at the instance level in a way that reflects perceptual naturalness, smoothness, and biomechanical plausibility.

## Input / Output

The critic model consumes SMPL-parameterized motion sequences of shape L×J×D (24 axis-angle joint rotations plus global root translation, 60 frames at 24 fps) and outputs a single scalar quality score. The training data consists of 18,260 multiple-choice questions (4 motion candidates + 'all good' + 'all bad'), which are decomposed into 52,563 ordered preference pairs. For the fine-tuning component, the generator takes text prompts or action labels and produces 60-frame SMPL motion sequences, with the critic score used as an additional loss signal during a lightweight 800-step fine-tuning of a pre-trained MDM diffusion model.

## Method

The paper introduces MotionPercept, a large-scale human-annotated dataset where 10 annotators select the best (or worst) motion from groups of four generated by MDM and FLAME under identical conditions, validated by a consensus experiment showing 82.37% unanimous agreement. MotionCritic is a DSTformer backbone (3 layers, 8 attention heads) with temporal average pooling and an MLP head, trained from scratch using a Bradley-Terry pairwise preference loss that maximizes the probability the model ranks the human-preferred motion above the dispreferred one. For generation improvement, the paper proposes a ReFL-inspired plug-and-play fine-tuning: during MDM's 1000-step denoising, gradient-free steps proceed to a randomly sampled timestep t∈[700,900], then a single gradient-enabled step predicts x₀′; the critic score on x₀′ is mapped through a sigmoid-based loss (with clipping at τ=12.0) and combined with a KL divergence term against the previous iteration's prediction, all added to the unchanged MDM loss. This requires only 800 fine-tuning iterations (0.23% of pre-training cost) and improves user-study Elo ratings without degrading accuracy, diversity, or multimodality.

## Results

On the MotionPercept MDM test set, MotionCritic achieves 85.07% accuracy and 0.5486 log loss in aligning with human perceptual preferences, substantially outperforming the best heuristic baseline (Person-Ground Contact at 71.78%) and the closest learning-based competitor MoBERT (49.40%). On the FLAME test set (a different generator and dataset), MotionCritic still achieves 81.43% accuracy and 0.5758 log loss, demonstrating strong cross-distribution generalization. When used as a fine-tuning signal for MDM, only 800 iterations (0.23% of pre-training cost) are needed to significantly improve user-study Elo ratings while maintaining or improving Accuracy, Diversity, and Multimodality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionCritic decisively outperforms all 13 compared metrics on both test sets. Against the closest learning-based competitor MoBERT (Voas et al., 2023), MotionCritic gains +35.67% accuracy on MDM (85.07% vs. 49.40%) and +29.03% on FLAME (81.43% vs. 52.40%), with substantially lower log loss (0.5486 vs. 0.6931). Among heuristic metrics, Person-Ground Contact (Rempe et al., 2021) is the strongest single baseline at 71.78% (MDM) and 69.82% (FLAME), yet MotionCritic still exceeds it by 13.29 and 11.61 percentage points respectively. Distance-based metrics (Root AVE, Joint AE, PoseNDF, NPSS, NDMS) all fall in the 52–63% accuracy range, far below MotionCritic. Physical Foot Contact (Tseng et al., 2023) and Acceleration (Yang et al., 2023) achieve 64.79% and 64.26% on MDM, respectively, still trailing MotionCritic by ~20 points. FID (Heusel et al., 2017), while not directly comparable at the instance level, is shown in Figure 4(B) to poorly correlate with human Elo ratings across GT motion subsets, whereas MotionCritic scores align closely with user preferences. The ensemble analysis (Appendix B.6) further confirms that combining MotionCritic with heuristics yields negligible improvement (85.17% MLP vs. 84.96% MotionCritic alone), indicating MotionCritic already subsumes the heuristic signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the RLHF paradigm (reward model + preference-based fine-tuning) from language models (Ouyang et al., 2022) and text-to-image generation (ImageReward/ReFL, Xu et al., 2024) to human motion generation. It builds directly on MDM (Tevet et al., 2023) as the base diffusion generator and on the Bradley-Terry preference learning framework to train a motion critic from pairwise human comparisons. The closest prior work, MoBERT (Voas et al., 2023), also learns from human ratings of generated motions but operates at ~37× smaller scale (1.4K vs 52.6K annotations) and uses absolute ratings rather than comparisons, limiting its robustness and generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MotionPercept (52K+ preference pairs) and MotionCritic provide the field with the first large-scale, human-aligned evaluation benchmark for motion generation, addressing a long-standing gap where FID, PFC, and other metrics poorly correlate with subjective quality judgments. The plug-and-play fine-tuning framework (800 steps, 0.23% of training cost) offers a practical path to improve any existing diffusion-based motion generator without architectural changes. Key limitations include the non-smooth energy landscape (preventing direct use as a distance or optimization target), the absence of explicit physical/biomechanical modeling, and the restriction to 60-frame SMPL sequences. Open questions include whether fine-grained perceptual feedback (beyond pairwise selection) could yield richer supervision, and how to incorporate physical plausibility constraints alongside perceptual alignment.
