## Problem

Most EHR foundation models are limited to context windows of 512 tokens due to the quadratic scaling of transformer attention, yet a single patient's EHR can contain tens of thousands of clinical events. No prior work has systematically evaluated how context length affects clinical prediction performance, nor has the impact of three EHR-specific properties—copy-forwarded token repetition, irregular inter-event time intervals, and increasing disease complexity over time—been quantified in relation to model robustness.

## Input / Output

Input consists of structured EHR data (diagnoses, medications, lab results, procedures) formatted as chronologically-ordered clinical events under the OMOP CDM, tokenized into sequences of up to 16,384 tokens using a shared 39,818-token vocabulary where each clinical code maps to one token and numerical values are deciled. The model produces a single patient embedding (the last token's hidden state from the most recent L tokens before the prediction timepoint), which is fed into a frozen logistic regression head to yield a binary probability for each of 14 clinical prediction tasks. Performance is measured via AUROC and Brier score, with stratified analysis by patient-level repetitiveness and irregularity quartiles.

## Method

The paper trains four architectures—GPT and Llama (transformers) and Mamba and Hyena (subquadratic state-space and long-convolution models)—at a standardized ~120M parameter scale on 2.5M patients' EHRs (3.5B events) using autoregressive next-token prediction for 2B tokens. Transformers are evaluated at context lengths of 512–4k while subquadratic models are evaluated at 1k–16k, reflecting the quadratic compute penalty of attention. Downstream evaluation uses the EHRSHOT benchmark with a frozen logistic regression probe on last-token embeddings. The key empirical finding is that Mamba at 16k context achieves the highest average AUROC (0.807), surpassing the prior SOTA (CLMBR-t-base at 512 tokens) by 0.03 points, and that longer-context Mamba and Llama models are more robust to all three EHR-specific properties (e.g., 14% lower Brier loss for the most irregular patients compared to shorter-context counterparts), while Hyena degrades sharply beyond 4k tokens.

## Results

Mamba at a 16k-token context length achieves a mean AUROC of 0.807 across 14 EHRSHOT clinical prediction tasks, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art (CLMBR-t-base, AUROC 0.777) by +0.03 points and winning on 9/14 individual tasks. Both Mamba and Llama show consistent performance gains as context length increases (from 512 to 16k/4k tokens respectively), while Hyena degrades sharply beyond 4k tokens. Stratifying by EHR-specific properties, patients in the most irregular quartile (Q4) achieve a Brier score ~14% worse than the least irregular (Q1), but longer-context Mamba and Llama models show significantly lower Brier scores across all quartiles compared to their shorter-context counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is CLMBR-t-base (Steinberg et al., 2021), the prior SOTA on EHRSHOT with a 512-token context length and mean AUROC of 0.777. Mamba-16k (0.807 AUROC) outperforms CLMBR-t-base by +0.03 points on average and wins on 9 out of 14 individual tasks. In the stratified Brier score analysis (Table 2), Mamba-16k and Llama-4k both achieve significantly lower Brier scores than CLMBR-t-base across all quartiles of both repetitiveness and irregularity, with the gap widening in the most extreme quartiles (e.g., Mamba-16k: 0.0746 vs. CLMBR-t-base: 0.0805 in Q4 repetitiveness). Among the four architectures tested, Mamba is the clear winner at long context, while Hyena degrades sharply beyond 4k tokens and GPT shows inconsistent gains due to its absolute positional embeddings (mitigated by switching to RoPE). Llama-4k provides a competitive transformer-based alternative, consistently outperforming its 512-token counterpart across all EHR-property quartiles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EHR foundation model lineage established by CLMBR-t-base (Steinberg et al., 2021) and the EHRSHOT benchmark (Wornow et al., 2023), reusing their tokenization strategy and vocabulary while being the first to systematically vary context length from 512 to 16k tokens across four architectures. It departs from prior EHR FMs—which were almost exclusively BERT- or GPT-based at 512 tokens—by introducing subquadratic architectures (Mamba, Hyena) trained at the scale of millions of patients, building on their demonstrated success in NLP and genomics. The EHR-specific property analysis (copy-forwarding repetition, inter-event irregularity, disease-progression perplexity) is a novel analytical lens not present in prior EHR modeling work, drawing on clinical informatics observations (Thornton et al., 2013; Fabbri et al., 2015) to formalize metrics for quantifying data challenges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic evaluation of how context length affects clinical prediction, providing a practical blueprint for hospitals considering long-context EHR models and showing that subquadratic architectures like Mamba can match or exceed transformer-based SOTA while offering better compute scaling. The finding that longer contexts are more robust to EHR-specific data challenges (repetition, irregularity, increasing complexity) gives practitioners concrete guidance on when longer contexts matter most. Key limitations include single-institution data, transformers not tested beyond 4k tokens, and only three EHR properties explored; open questions include whether smaller Mamba models suffice, how these findings generalize across institutions, and whether additional properties (underdiagnosis, multimodal signals) reveal further challenges.
