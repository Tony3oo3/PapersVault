## Problem

World model-based RL methods for multi-task continuous control typically extract policies via zeroth-order (gradient-free) optimization, which suffers from high variance and sample inefficiency, while first-order gradient methods fail on the discontinuities inherent to contact-rich dynamics. This paper identifies that the bottleneck is not the optimization method itself but the smoothness of the model being differentiated through: well-regularized world models can produce smoother optimization landscapes than ground-truth dynamics, enabling efficient first-order policy extraction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw state observations (e.g., 11-dim for Hopper up to 76-dim for Humanoid) and a learned 96-dim task embedding, encoding them into a 768-dim latent state via a shared encoder. The policy outputs a continuous action vector (up to 152 dimensions for SNU Humanoid muscle control) sampled from a stochastic Gaussian with a minimum standard deviation of 0.24 to prevent premature collapse to determinism. The world model is pre-trained on offline datasets (120k trajectories per dm_control task, 40k per MetaWorld task), and per-task policies are extracted in ~9.3 minutes on a single RTX 6000 GPU.

## Method

PWM decouples multi-task learning into two stages: (1) pre-train a single multi-task world model (TD-MPC2 architecture with SimNorm activations in the encoder and dynamics output layers, Mish elsewhere, LayerNorm throughout) on offline data using an auto-regressive supervised loss with H=16 and γ=0.99, and (2) for each task, extract a dedicated policy via first-order gradient (reparameterized/pathwise) backpropagation through the world model in an on-policy actor-critic loop. The actor loss (Eq. 6) backpropagates through H=16 imagined latent rollouts with terminal value bootstrapping, while the critic is trained model-free via TD(λ) with λ=0.95 over the same rollouts using an ensemble of 3 critics. The key design insight is that SimNorm regularization deliberately trades world model accuracy for landscape smoothness, reducing the optimality gap and gradient variance enough to make FoG optimization effective even in contact-rich and chaotic settings. Reward prediction uses two-hot encoding in SymLog space to handle multi-task reward scales, and the inversion omits the non-differentiable sign operator to preserve gradient flow.

## Results

On 5 high-dimensional locomotion tasks (up to 152 action dimensions), PWM achieves PPO-normalized rewards of 1.20 (Hopper), 1.46 (Ant), 1.16 (Anymal), 1.19 (Humanoid), and 1.36 (SNU Humanoid), outperforming SHAC (which uses ground-truth differentiable simulation dynamics) on 3 of 5 tasks. In the 80-task multi-task setting (MT80), PWM achieves up to 27% higher reward than TD-MPC2 without relying on online planning, while extracting per-task policies in under 10 minutes (9.3 min on an RTX 6000 GPU). PWM also matches or exceeds single-task experts (SAC, DreamerV3) in the multi-task setting without any online environment interaction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PWM outperforms SHAC (ground-truth differentiable simulation) on Hopper (1.20 vs 1.02), Ant (1.46 vs 1.16), and Humanoid (1.19 vs 1.15), demonstrating that regularized world models induce smoother optimization landscapes than true discontinuous dynamics; however, SHAC retains an edge on Anymal (1.26 vs 1.16) and SNU Humanoid (1.44 vs 1.36), the two highest-dimensional tasks. Against TD-MPC2, PWM wins on all 5 single-task environments and achieves up to 27% higher reward in the MT80 multi-task setting, while also being significantly faster at inference since it does not perform online planning. PWM surpasses DreamerV3 on 4 of 5 tasks (notably SNU Humanoid: 1.36 vs 0.48) but trails slightly on Anymal (1.16 vs 1.18). Compared to model-free baselines PPO and SAC, PWM achieves higher normalized rewards on all 5 tasks, with the largest margin on Ant (1.46 vs 1.00/0.95). In the multi-task MT30 setting, PWM matches the performance of online-trained single-task experts (SAC, DreamerV3) despite requiring no environment interaction and only ≤10 minutes of policy training per task.

## Relation to Prior Work

PWM builds directly on the TD-MPC2 world model architecture (encoder, dynamics, reward with SimNorm activations and task embeddings) and the first-order gradient (FoG) optimization through dynamics introduced by SHAC (Xu et al., 2022). The key departure is conceptual: rather than using the world model as a component within a joint RL training loop (as in TD-MPC2's DDPG-style gradients + online planning, or DreamerV3's ZoG), PWM treats the pre-trained world model as a differentiable physics simulator and extracts per-task policies via backpropagation through it. This decouples the supervised world-model training from the RL policy objective, a framework shift from prior multi-task MBRL methods that jointly optimize all components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that world model smoothness and optimality gap matter more than prediction accuracy for FoG policy optimization challenges a core assumption in MBRL and provides a concrete design principle (use SimNorm, Mish, LayerNorm regularization) for building world models intended for gradient-based policy extraction. Practically, the <10-minute per-task policy extraction from a shared 48M-parameter world model offers a scalable alternative to online planning, which becomes infeasible as model size grows. Key limitations include the heavy reliance on substantial offline data, the need to re-train a policy for each new task, and the difficulty of scaling auto-regressive world model training. Open questions include whether this smoothness-over-accuracy principle generalizes to non-MLP world models (e.g., transformers, diffusion models) and whether the per-task re-training bottleneck can be alleviated via meta-learning or few-shot adaptation.
