## Problem

Existing Transformer-based dynamical symbolic regression models (e.g., ODEFormer) fail to generalize across diverse categories of catalytic organic reactions. The universal end-to-end strategy overfits to training patterns, while the independent per-equation strategy ignores the physical correlations imposed by the mass-action law. No prior method can predict kinetic equations for unseen reaction types while respecting these domain-specific constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252411/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes discrete temporal concentration profiles X = {(t, x(t))} for all reaction species (substrates, products, catalysts, intermediates) as encoder input, encoded via the discrete three-token method from ODEFormer. It outputs the full set of governing ODEs as a sequence of prefix-notation tokens (e.g., 'add', 'mul', species names, rate constants), which can be converted back to standard differential form. During inference, equations are generated sequentially with previously predicted equations serving as conditions, and MCTS selects the optimal generation order before final output.

## Method

KinFormer introduces a conditional training strategy where, at each training step, a random subset of ODEs is selected as conditions and one additional ODE is predicted, implicitly modeling the mass-action-law correlations among equations without explicit constraint encoding. The training order is randomly shuffled to prevent the Transformer from capturing spurious positional correlations rather than physically meaningful ones. At inference, a Monte Carlo Tree Search module operates at the equation level (not token level), using P-UCB for node selection to find the optimal sequential generation order of individual ODEs. The MCTS evaluation score combines micro-R2 and macro-R2 of the numerically solved ODE system against the input profiles, and the best-scoring leaf node after 100 iterations yields the final prediction. This combination of conditional generation and equation-level MCTS search enables generalization to unseen reaction mechanisms while maintaining physical consistency.

## Results

On the Catalytic Organic Reaction Kinetic Dataset (20 reaction types), KinFormer (n=100) achieves Acc_m=63.45%, R2_m=0.726, and Acc_form=64.99% in-domain, while the Universal Transformer strategy is best in-domain (Acc_m=71.49%, R2_m=0.754). The critical advantage emerges in out-of-domain settings: KinFormer achieves Acc_m=50.83% and Acc_form=79.42% in intra-class OOD, and Acc_m=40.47% and Acc_form=80.61% in inter-class OOD, whereas Universal collapses to Acc_m≈10% and Acc_form=0% in both OOD scenarios. KinFormer also outperforms the Conditional strategy alone by 19.10% and 17.27% in Acc_m for intra-class and inter-class OOD respectively, demonstrating the MCTS module's contribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KinFormer decisively outperforms both classical SR baselines: SINDy (best d=2) achieves only Acc_m=1.59% and R2_m=0.020 in-domain, while PySR reaches Acc_m=24.32% and R2_m=0.315, both far below KinFormer's 63.45% and 0.726. Against the Universal Transformer strategy (ODEFormer's approach), KinFormer trades a modest in-domain gap (Acc_m 63.45% vs. 71.49%) for a dramatic out-of-domain advantage: in intra-class OOD, KinFormer achieves Acc_m=50.83% vs. Universal's 10.40%, and in inter-class OOD, 40.47% vs. 10.73%, with Acc_form jumping from 0% to ~80%. The Independent strategy (P-tuning-inspired) is the weakest Transformer variant across all settings (Acc_m ≤ 4.14%), confirming that ignoring inter-equation correlations is detrimental. The Conditional strategy alone already recovers most of the generalization gap (Acc_m=31.73% intra-OOD vs. Universal's 10.40%), and MCTS further boosts it to 50.83%, validating the generation-order search. MCTS also outperforms equation-level beam search (n=20) in both accuracy and efficiency, with beam search taking ~3× longer (669s vs. 222s) while achieving lower Acc_m (31.67% vs. 50.83% intra-OOD).

## Relation to Prior Work

KinFormer builds directly on ODEFormer's Transformer backbone for dynamical symbolic regression, but departs from its end-to-end (universal) training strategy, which the authors show overfits in-domain patterns and fails to generalize. The key methodological departure is the conditional strategy—training the model to predict one ODE given others as context—which implicitly encodes mass-action-law correlations without explicit constraint modeling. The MCTS module, adapted from AlphaGo/AlphaZero, is repurposed from token-level or formula-level search to equation-level generation-order search, a granularity not explored in prior SR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple training-strategy change (conditional generation) plus a lightweight search post-processing step can substantially improve out-of-domain generalization for ODE prediction in chemistry, offering a practical path toward automated kinetics analysis without case-by-case expert derivation. However, limitations remain: cross-dimensional transfer (e.g., 4D→6D mechanisms) is weak, MCTS scales poorly with species count, and all experiments use simulated data with controlled noise, leaving open whether the approach transfers to noisy real experimental profiles. The framework could inspire similar conditional-generation-plus-search designs in other physics-constrained regression domains.
