## Problem

Determining whether a dataset's generative process is causal (features cause the class) or anticausal (the class causes features) is critical for semi-supervised learning, transfer learning, and domain-specific applications, yet existing causal discovery methods fail on high-dimensional perceptual data like images and are unreliable under label noise. These methods rely on conditional independence tests or score optimizations that are easily distorted by the random fluctuations introduced by noisy annotations, and none can handle the unobservable feature variables inherent in image data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of instances X (tabular features or images) paired with observed labels Ỹ that may contain arbitrary label errors. For image inputs, a VAE with ResNet-18 backbone extracts 10-dimensional semantic features before computing instance norms for noise scheduling. The output is a binary causal/anticausal verdict accompanied by a regression coefficient β₁ quantifying the disagreement trend and a p-value from a one-sample t-test (repeated 30 times) to determine statistical significance at α=0.05.

## Method

RoCA exploits an asymmetric information property: under causal relations, P(X) carries no information about the noisy class-posterior P_θ(Ỹ|X), while under anticausal relations it does. The estimator generates pseudo labels via unsupervised clustering (K-means for tabular data, SPICE for images) with Hungarian assignment to match cluster count to class count, then injects instance-dependent label noise at 20 uniformly sampled levels from 0 to 0.5, where each instance's flip rate is drawn from a truncated normal distribution and correlated with its ℓ₁ norm. At each noise level, the 0-1 loss disagreement between pseudo labels and modified labels is computed, and a linear regression fits disagreement against noise level. Under the causal setting, Theorem 1 guarantees the regression coefficient β₁ converges to 0 at rate O(1/√m) via Rademacher complexity bounds; under the anticausal setting, Theorem 2 shows β₁ is nonzero whenever pseudo labels are not random guesses, enabling robust detection even under 40–45% label error.

## Results

On 6 causal datasets (averaged over 10 noise settings from 0% to 30%), RoCA achieves 51.6% overall accuracy in correctly identifying the causal direction, outperforming the best baseline NOTEARS-MLP (40.0%) and all other methods (GES/GIES 15.0%, PC 17.5%, FCI 0.0%). On 13 anticausal datasets, RoCA correctly identifies the anticausal relation across all noise levels (0–30% instance, pair, and symmetric label noise). On large-scale noisy image datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10N with 5 real-world noise settings, and Clothing1M with 1M images), RoCA achieves p=0.0000 in rejecting the null hypothesis, confirming anticausal direction with high statistical significance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoCA outperforms all 10 baselines on causal datasets, achieving 51.6% overall accuracy versus 40.0% for NOTEARS-MLP (the best baseline), 36.7% for CCDR, and 35.0% for NOTEARS. FCI, which is designed to handle latent confounders, achieves 0% on causal datasets, while RAI (16.7%) and ICD (1.7%) also perform poorly. On anticausal datasets, RoCA correctly identifies the anticausal direction across all 13 datasets and all noise levels, matching or exceeding FCI (78.3% overall) and NOTEARS (33.1%). A key differentiator is scalability: RoCA is the only method applicable to large-scale image datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10N, Clothing1M with 1M images), where baselines like LINGAM, RAI, and ICD either fail or are infeasible due to computational complexity. The paper notes that Pair0071 is misclassified by nearly all methods (including RoCA) due to a latent common cause, which is acknowledged as a known limitation in the causal discovery literature (Mooij et al., 2016).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the asymmetric property established by Schölkopf et al. (2012) and the Principle of Independent Mechanisms from Peters et al. (2017b): in anticausal settings, P(X) contains information about P(Y|X), while in causal settings it does not. However, it departs from all existing causal discovery methods (GES, PC, FCI, LiNGAM, NOTEARS, etc.) by avoiding direct estimation of P(Y|X) or conditional independence tests, which fail on high-dimensional perceptual data and are sensitive to label noise. Instead, it introduces a noise-injection protocol combined with unsupervised clustering and a regression-based hypothesis test, using the noisy label posterior P_θ(Ỹ|X) as a theoretically justified surrogate for the clean class posterior P(Y|X).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a practical gap: no prior method could determine causal vs. anticausal direction for large-scale image datasets with real-world label errors (e.g., Clothing1M with 1M images, CIFAR10N with five noise settings). The key insight that measuring the *change* in dependence under injected noise is more robust than measuring the *strength* of dependence offers a general principle applicable beyond this specific task. Limitations include the binary causal/anticausal framing (confounded cases like Pair0071 are misclassified), the reliance on unsupervised clustering quality, and the assumption that the dataset is purely one direction. Open questions include extending the framework to partially causal/anticausal structures and multi-variable causal graphs.
