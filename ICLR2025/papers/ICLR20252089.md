## Problem

Existing medical benchmarks like MedQA are static, can become outdated or leaked into LLM training data, and lack a unified framework for leveraging regularly updated knowledge bases (e.g., UMLS) to evaluate medical knowledge mastery. Directly prompting LLMs to generate test samples from knowledge triplets introduces factual errors and produces samples that differ only in surface wording rather than in logical structure, limiting evaluation reliability and diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252089/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes structured medical knowledge triplets (head entity, relation, tail entity) from knowledge bases such as MedLAMA (17 relation types, 34,000 triplets) and DiseK (4 clinical relation types, 6,348 triplets), including both positive and sampled negative triplets. For each triplet, it produces 8 structurally diverse textual statements (via predicate transformations) that are evaluated as true/false verification or 4-option multiple-choice questions. The final output is two accuracy metrics per LLM: average accuracy across all samples and joint accuracy requiring all 8 variants of a knowledge point to be answered correctly.

## Method

PretexEval expresses each knowledge triplet as a predicate R(h,t) and applies three types of logically equivalent transformations—Inversion (R⁻¹(t,h)), Instantiation (P(h,x)⇒Q(x,t)), and Double Negation (¬(¬R(h,t)))—along with their combinations to generate 8 structurally distinct variants whose truth values are guaranteed by the equivalence property. To convert variants back to text, the framework uses a prototype-based pipeline: it retrieves a pre-crafted template (designed with clinicians) matching the specific (relation, transformation) combination, instantiates it with the entity arguments, and then rephrases the result with Llama3-70B-Instruct for naturalness and lexical diversity. Evaluation employs 5-shot in-context learning with a statement-verification prompt, and the joint accuracy metric (product of per-sample scores within each knowledge point) specifically probes consistency across structurally diverse expressions of the same fact. This design ensures that reliability is preserved by the logical equivalence guarantee while diversity is achieved through structural (not merely lexical) variation, addressing both failure modes of direct LLM generation.

## Results

Across 12 LLMs evaluated on two medical knowledge bases (MedLAMA and DiseK), PretexEval consistently yields lower average accuracy than both the Direct (template) and LLMEval (LLM-generated) baselines, revealing substantial knowledge gaps. GPT-4o achieves the highest PretexEval scores at +31.7 (MedLAMA) and +26.7 (DiseK) above random (50%), while the weakest model, Llama2-7B, scores only +3.1 and +2.8. The performance degradation from Direct to PretexEval ranges from ~11.5% (GPT-4o on MedLAMA) to ~76.6% (Llama2-7B on DiseK), with most models losing 30–60% relative accuracy. Joint accuracy further shows that LLMs exhibit significant inconsistency when the same knowledge point is expressed in structurally diverse ways.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PretexEval against two evaluation baselines: Direct (fixed template paraphrasing of knowledge triplets) and LLMEval (prompting Llama3-70B-Instruct to generate 8 diverse statements per triplet). PretexEval consistently produces lower LLM scores than both baselines across all 12 models and both knowledge bases, confirming it generates structurally more diverse and challenging test samples. Compared to Direct, PretexEval causes 11.5–76.6% relative accuracy drops; compared to LLMEval, drops range from 4.3% (GPT-4o on DiseK) to 76.6% (Llama2-7B on DiseK). Smaller models (≤7B) suffer the largest degradation, while GPT-4o and Llama3-70B are most robust, losing only ~12–30%. Medical-specific models (ClinicalCamel, Med42) outperform their Llama2-70B backbone by ~7% on PretexEval, suggesting medical fine-tuning improves depth of knowledge mastery. Ablations confirm that predicate equivalent transformation (especially double negation) is the primary driver of the increased difficulty, contributing ~7% additional degradation over removing LLM rephrasing alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both static medical benchmarks (MedQA, MedMCQA, PubMedQA) and existing dynamic evaluation methods (DyVal, S3Eval, Clean-Eval) by introducing a predicate-logic-based approach to test sample generation. While prior knowledge-probing work (Sung et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2022) uses fixed templates to query LLMs against knowledge bases, PretexEval leverages predicate equivalent transformations (inversion, instantiation, double negation) to produce structurally diverse variants, addressing the reliability and diversity limitations of both template-based and LLM-generated samples. The framework is positioned as a general-purpose evaluation tool applicable to any medical knowledge base, rather than a task-specific dynamic generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even GPT-4o achieves only ~82% average accuracy (31.7% above random) on structurally diverse medical knowledge probes, and that LLMs exhibit significant inconsistency across different expressions of the same fact, provides a concrete and actionable gap for medical LLM developers. The joint accuracy metric is particularly relevant for high-stakes clinical deployment where a single missed variant could be dangerous. Limitations include potential inapplicability to complex multi-step clinical reasoning tasks and residual uncertainty from the LLM rephrasing step; open questions include whether scaling training data or incorporating PretexEval-style samples into pretraining can close the observed knowledge gaps.
