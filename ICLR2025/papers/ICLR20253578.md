## Problem

Multimodal encoders like CLIP require hundreds of millions to billions of paired image-text samples and massive GPU clusters to train, yet even this scale does not guarantee quality due to noisy internet data. The paper asks whether two pre-trained unimodal encoders (e.g., DINOv2 for images, GTR for text), which are already trained on abundant unimodal data, can be combined with only a tiny amount of paired multimodal data to replicate CLIP's cross-modal similarity without any neural network training.

## Input / Output

CSA takes a pair of multimodal data points (e.g., an RGB image and its text caption, an audio clip and a genre tag, a LiDAR point cloud and a descriptive caption) and produces a single weighted cosine similarity score in a shared multimodal feature space. The unimodal encoders are frozen and only run in inference mode; CSA then solves a matrix decomposition on the resulting feature matrices. The hyperparameter s (selected via a correlation threshold) controls how many canonical dimensions contribute to the score, and the output similarity can be thresholded for binary alignment decisions, ranked for retrieval, or compared against template captions for zero-shot classification.

## Method

CSA applies canonical correlation analysis (CCA) to the zero-meaned feature matrices from two frozen unimodal encoders, solving a constrained optimization (Equation 2) whose analytical solution reduces to a singular value decomposition of a cross-covariance matrix. The resulting canonical correlation coefficients ρ₁ ≥ ρ₂ ≥ … ≥ ρᵣ serve as weights in a novel similarity score: a weighted cosine similarity over only the first s canonical dimensions, where s is chosen so that ρₛ exceeds an empirical threshold. This design discards low-correlation dimensions that encode modality-specific information (e.g., punctuation in text, fine-grained texture in images) while preserving the shared multimodal structure. No gradient-based training is performed; the entire fitting phase is a single SVD call on CPU. The theoretical analysis characterizes an intrinsic trade-off: smaller s yields larger minimum singular values (more contrastive, distant features) but fewer informative dimensions, while larger s captures more latent structure at the cost of admitting noise-dominated dimensions.

## Results

On ImageNet zero-shot classification, CSA matches CLIP's performance using only 35,000 training pairs (50,000× fewer than CLIP's 2 billion). On Flickr30k cross-modal retrieval, CSA achieves 36.6% mAP@5 and 59.3% Precision@1 (image-to-text) and 44.7% Precision@1 (text-to-image), outperforming ASIF (14.6% mAP@5, 0.1% P@1 text-to-image) but trailing CLIP (73.8% mAP@5, 92.9% P@1). On mislabeled ImageNet detection and COSMOS misinformation detection, CSA achieves the highest AUC among all unsupervised methods, surpassing CLIP, ASIF, and LLaVA. On the extremely limited Leafy Spurge dataset (800 training images), CSA consistently outperforms both CLIP and ASIF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP, CSA matches or exceeds performance on ImageNet classification (35k samples), Leafy Spurge, mislabeled ImageNet detection, and COSMOS misinformation detection, but falls short on Flickr30k cross-modal retrieval (36.6% vs. 73.8% mAP@5), which the authors attribute to the trade-off between feature distinguishability and informativity. Against ASIF, CSA wins decisively on every task: 36.6% vs. 14.6% mAP@5 on image-to-text retrieval, 44.7% vs. 0.1% Precision@1 on text-to-image retrieval (where ASIF essentially fails), and higher AUC on all detection tasks. Against LLaVA-13B, CSA outperforms on both mislabeled ImageNet detection and COSMOS misinformation detection; LLaVA completely fails on COSMOS with a 100% false positive rate. Against CLAP on MusicCaps audio-text classification, CSA performs comparably. Against LiPLOC on KITTI text-to-LiDAR retrieval, CSA matches the Recall@5 of the LiDAR-to-LiDAR baseline. The only method that outperforms CSA is the supervised COSMOS baseline (trained with object-location labels), which the authors note is a fundamentally different setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSA builds directly on canonical correlation analysis (Weenink, 2003) as its core mathematical engine, repurposing CCA not as a training loss (as in Sun et al., 2019; Lyu & Fu, 2020, 2023) but as a post-hoc, non-parametric mapping between pre-trained unimodal feature spaces. The closest prior work is ASIF (Norelli et al., 2023), which also bridges multimodal gaps using unimodal encoders without training, but relies on a large anchor dataset and unimodal distance heuristics rather than an optimization-based projection. CSA departs from the CLIP paradigm (Radford et al., 2021) by eliminating end-to-end multimodal training entirely, replacing it with a cubic-complexity SVD on encoded features, and positions itself as a lightweight alternative when paired multimodal data is scarce but unimodal foundation models are available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for emerging modality pairs (e.g., LiDAR-text, time-series-text) where paired data is inherently limited but unimodal encoders can be trained on abundant single-modality data; CSA shows that a few thousand to tens of thousands of paired examples suffice to replicate CLIP-level zero-shot performance. The theoretical analysis of the informativity-distinguishability trade-off via the hyperparameter s provides a principled knob for tuning across tasks. Key limitations include the bimodal restriction (unlike ImageBind's 6 modalities), dependence on the quality of pre-trained unimodal encoders, and a substantial gap versus CLIP on cross-modal retrieval (36.6% vs. 73.8% mAP@5). Open questions include extending to >2 modalities via generalized CCA, understanding optimal training set size, and whether fine-tuning unimodal encoders with CSA-aware objectives would close the retrieval gap.
