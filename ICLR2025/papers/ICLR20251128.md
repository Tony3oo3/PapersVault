## Problem

DPO and RLHF assume preferences follow the Bradley-Terry model, which assigns the same sigmoid-shaped preference probability to every prompt regardless of how much human agreement actually exists. This ignores the natural variation in preference dispersion—some prompts (e.g., factual questions) have near-consensus answers while others (e.g., subjective opinions) are inherently contested. The paper asks whether explicitly modeling this prompt-specific dispersion in the preference optimization objective can improve both in-distribution accuracy and out-of-distribution generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard pairwise preference data (prompt x, winning completion y_w, losing completion y_l) together with a frozen reference SFT model π_ref. The per-prompt dispersion index φ(x) is computed on-the-fly from the SFT model's next-token prediction entropy over the two completions, normalized by log of the vocabulary size, requiring no additional pretraining or learned parameters. The output is a fine-tuned language model policy that generates text completions with improved preference alignment, evaluated via GPT-4 win rates or reward-model scores.

## Method

MallowsPO replaces the Bradley-Terry ranking assumption in DPO with Mallows' probabilistic ranking model, which introduces a per-prompt dispersion parameter φ(x) ∈ (0,1] controlling how concentrated the preference distribution is around a central ranking. The latent reward is decomposed as reward(prompt, completion) = dispersion(prompt) × scaled reward(completion|prompt), where the scaled reward is the negative rank, and the dispersion enters the preference likelihood as a prompt-dependent weight. Two variants are derived via different Mallows discrepancy functions: MallowsPO-θ (Spearman's rho) yields a sigmoid link scaled by −2 log φ(x), recovering standard DPO when φ is constant; MallowsPO-φ (Kendall's tau) yields a piecewise non-sigmoidal link function g_φ,φ(x). The dispersion index is estimated without pretraining by a proxy based on the normalized Shannon entropy of the SFT model's next-token logits, validated against ground-truth dispersion in a synthetic bandit. The framework is shown to be a generalized ΨPO with prompt-dependent weighting and can be plugged into other SOTA methods (IPO, SimPO) as a dispersion-weighted extension, boosting Llama3-8B-Instruct by ~2% LC win rate on Alpaca Eval V2.

## Results

On Alpaca Eval V2 with Llama3-8B-Instruct, MallowsPO as a plugin boosts LC Win Rate by ~0.55% over DPO (43.10% vs 42.55%), ~1.35% over IPO (44.73% vs 43.38%), and ~1.85% over SimPO (51.89% vs 50.04%). On Pythia-2.8B fine-tuned on Anthropic HH, MallowsPO-θ achieves a 57.67% GPT-4 win rate over DPO in-distribution (β=0.1), and the advantage grows out-of-distribution, reaching 61.07% on H4 Stack Exchange with MallowsPO-φ (β=0.5). In the synthetic bandit experiment, MallowsPO-φ achieves a higher reward at the same KL divergence and avoids reward collapse as β→0, whereas DPO collapses to a single arm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MallowsPO consistently outperforms DPO across all evaluated settings: on Pythia-2.8B (Anthropic HH), MallowsPO-θ wins 57.67% of GPT-4 pairwise comparisons in-distribution and up to 56.00% out-of-distribution (SHP); on Llama3-8B-Instruct (Alpaca Eval V2), it gains ~1% LC Win Rate over DPO with lower variance across 10 random seeds. Against IPO, MallowsIPO improves LC Win Rate from 43.38% to 44.73% (+1.35 pp) and Win Rate from 43.52% to 44.87% (+1.35 pp). Against SimPO, MallowsSimPO improves LC Win Rate from 50.04% to 51.89% (+1.85 pp) and Win Rate from 42.11% to 43.76% (+1.65 pp). The out-of-distribution gap is notably larger than in-distribution (e.g., 61.07% vs 54.33% on SE for MallowsPO-φ), suggesting the dispersion index improves generalization. In the IMDB controllable generation task, MallowsPO-φ achieves the best accuracy–KL tradeoff, while MallowsPO-θ performs comparably to DPO, consistent with the low preference dispersion observed for that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the Bradley-Terry model that underlies DPO and all its variants (IPO, SimPO, CPO, ORPO, etc.) by replacing the fixed sigmoid link function with a dispersion-scaled link derived from Mallows' probabilistic ranking models (Mallows, 1957; Diaconis, 1988). The key conceptual move is decomposing the latent reward into a prompt-dependent dispersion index times a scaled reward (negative rank), which unifies DPO as a special case (φ=1) and yields two new objectives (MallowsPO-θ and MallowsPO-φ) corresponding to Spearman's rho and Kendall's tau discrepancy functions. The paper also shows that MallowsPO cannot be expressed as an instance of the standard ΨPO framework (Azar et al., 2024) because the link function is prompt-dependent, motivating a generalized ΨPO with a prompt-conditioned weighting function f(x).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first preference optimization method grounded in a mathematically well-developed ranking model beyond Bradley-Terry, providing a principled mechanism to down-weight ambiguous prompts and up-weight clear ones during fine-tuning. The dispersion index is computable via a simple entropy proxy without additional training, and the method plugs into existing SOTA pipelines (IPO, SimPO) with ~2% LC win rate gains on Llama3-8B-Instruct. Open questions remain: the paper does not fully explain *why* dispersion weighting improves performance, the entropy-based proxy is heuristic and validated only on a synthetic bandit, and the optimal β selection strategy is left unspecified. The out-of-distribution generalization advantage (up to ~6% on H4 Stack Exchange) suggests the dispersion signal may encode transferable structure, but this needs further investigation.
