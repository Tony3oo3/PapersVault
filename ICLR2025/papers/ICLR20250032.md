## Problem

Existing generative models for molecular crystallization treat molecules as flexible entities, producing physically unrealistic packing structures with distorted or invalid internal molecular geometries. Traditional numerical simulation tools (e.g., PackMol) enforce rigidity but are computationally expensive, scaling poorly to large molecular systems. The paper addresses the gap of learning a data-driven generative model that explicitly preserves molecular rigidity (SE(3) constraints) throughout the crystallization process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a cluster of rigid molecules in a weakly correlated (unstable) arrangement as input, where each molecule's atomic coordinates are preprocessed and geometrically optimized. It outputs the same molecules in a strongly correlated crystal packing, parameterized at the molecule level as a translation vector (mass center position in R³) and a rotation quaternion (unit quaternion in SO(3)) per molecule. Unlike atom-level generative models, the output is structured as per-molecule SE(3) transformations rather than individual atomic displacements, guaranteeing that intra-molecular distances remain invariant.

## Method

AssembleFlow decomposes the SE(3) crystallization transformation into independent SO(3) rotation and R³ translation paths, each learned via conditional flow matching with its own interpolation scheme: SLERP (spherical linear interpolation) for quaternions on the rotation manifold and LERP (linear interpolation) for mass-center translations in Euclidean space. Inertial frames—constructed via eigendecomposition of each molecule's inertia tensor—provide stable reference coordinate systems that uniquely determine the rotation between initial and final molecular poses, with a fourth non-coplanar point resolving directional ambiguity. A reparameterization trick replaces per-step velocity prediction with direct estimation of the final SE(3) action, enabling closed-form Euler integration at inference. The velocity functions are SE(3)-equivariant, combining PaiNN for intra-molecular encoding with either atomic-level or molecular-level inter-molecular message passing, and the two path objectives (translation and rotation) are trained jointly as a summed loss.

## Results

On COD-Cluster17 (full dataset), AssembleFlow achieves a Packing Matching score of 7.37 (PM-atom) and 6.21 (PM-center), outperforming the best deep generative baseline (CrystalFlow-LERP at 13.61/13.28) by over 45%. It maintains 98.15% Separation and 99.98% Compactness while keeping atomic Collision at just 0.31%. Compared to the domain-specific tool PackMol, AssembleFlow achieves comparable packing quality (7.37 vs. 7.15 PM-atom) while reducing computational cost by 25-fold (35 hours vs. 864 hours for 10,543 clusters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the six deep generative baselines (GNN-MD, CrystalSDE-VE/VP, CrystalFlow-VE/VP/LERP), AssembleFlow dominates on Packing Matching by at least 45% (7.37 vs. best 12.80 PM-atom) and achieves near-perfect Separation (98.15%) and Compactness (99.98%), whereas most baselines collapse on these validity metrics (e.g., GNN-MD reaches only 4.13% Separation; CrystalSDE-VE reaches 2.51% Compactness). The closest competitor, CrystalFlow-LERP, matches AssembleFlow on Collision (0.34 vs. 0.31) and Compactness but lags substantially on PM (13.61 vs. 7.37) and Separation (97.34 vs. 98.15). Against the domain-specific simulation tool PackMol, AssembleFlow achieves comparable packing quality (7.37 vs. 7.15 PM-atom, 6.21 vs. 6.09 PM-center) with slightly lower Separation (98.15% vs. 99.42%) but a 25-fold reduction in computational cost (35 hours vs. 864 hours for 10,543 clusters), demonstrating that data-driven generation can match heuristic physics-based packing at a fraction of the runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

AssembleFlow extends the conditional flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022) to the molecular crystallization setting previously explored by the same group (Liu et al., 2024c, which introduced GNN-MD, CrystalSDE, and CrystalFlow). Its key departure is the introduction of inertial frames as reference coordinate systems to enforce rigid-body constraints, drawing an analogy to local-frame constructions in protein backbone generation (FrameFlow, FoldFlow, AlphaFold2) but generalizing to arbitrary organic molecules via eigendecomposition of the inertia tensor. The decomposition of SE(3) into independent SO(3) and R³ subpaths with SLERP and LERP respectively, combined with a reparameterization trick for inference, distinguishes it from both the atom-level diffusion baselines and from Kohler et al. (2023), which targets stable-to-stable sampling of specific molecules (water, methane) without SE(3)-equivariant modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AssembleFlow is the first method to enforce molecular rigidity throughout the SE(3) generation process for crystallization, achieving 100% molecular integrity and matching the packing quality of the established PackMol simulation tool at 25× lower computational cost. This suggests data-driven generative models can now compete with domain-specific heuristics for molecular packing, opening the door to scalable crystal structure prediction. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (COD-Cluster17) of organic molecules, and the authors acknowledge that extending to polymorphs with diverse configurations and to inorganic or periodic systems remains an open challenge. The reliance on preprocessed, geometrically optimized initial conformations also limits applicability to truly dynamic or multi-phase scenarios.
