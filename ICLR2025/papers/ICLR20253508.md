## Problem

Video depth estimation is hindered by the scarcity of large-scale, temporally consistent ground truth data, causing existing models to produce unreliable and flickering results. Prior generative depth models are further limited to fixed-length video sequences and require many denoising steps, making them impractical for long or variable-length inputs. This paper addresses both the data bottleneck and the architectural rigidity simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253508/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB video sequences of variable length (1 to 32 frames at 960×540 in a single forward pass on an 80GB A100) or single RGB images, and produces per-frame depth maps normalized to an affine-invariant scale. For long videos exceeding 32 frames, a two-stage pipeline first predicts globally consistent key-frame depth, then interpolates intermediate frames up to 150 frames at high resolution. Depth values are normalized using 2nd/98th percentile clipping before VAE encoding, and the final output is decoded back to pixel-space depth at the original resolution.

## Method

Depth Any Video reformulates monocular video depth estimation as a conditional denoising process within the latent space of SVD's spatial-only VAE, replacing the original EDM scheduler with conditional flow matching that achieves strong results in just 3 steps (vs. 25). A mixed-duration training strategy combines frame dropout with rotary position encoding (RoPE) to generalize across variable frame counts, and video packing to batch variable-length sequences efficiently, saving 33% training time while improving single-frame accuracy. For long-video inference, a frame interpolation network—fine-tuned from the depth model with additional key-frame conditioning channels and a binary masking map—generates intermediate frames aligned to globally predicted key frames, eliminating window-boundary flicker. The model is trained on 40,000 synthetic video clips (DA-V) collected from interactive game environments via ReShade depth-buffer capture, supplemented by Hypersim and Virtual KITTI 2, and fine-tuned from SVD's pretrained weights with the CLIP text condition removed.

## Results

On single-frame zero-shot depth estimation, Depth Any Video achieves AbsRel of 5.1 / 7.3 / 4.7 / 5.3 and δ1 of 97.0 / 95.1 / 97.9 / 96.6 on NYUv2 / KITTI / ETH3D / ScanNet, surpassing all prior generative models and approaching the best discriminative model (Depth Anything). On video depth estimation (ScanNet++), it attains AbsRel 9.3, δ1 93.4, and TAE 2.1, the best among all compared video depth methods. Inference requires only 3 denoising steps and 0.37 s per frame at 480×640 on a single A100, roughly 5.6× faster than Marigold (50 steps, 2.06 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest generative baseline GeoWizard, Depth Any Video wins on all four single-frame datasets, with the largest margin on KITTI (+3.0 δ1, −2.4 AbsRel) and ETH3D (+1.8 δ1, −1.7 AbsRel). Compared to the top discriminative model Depth Anything, it is competitive: it leads on KITTI (+0.5 δ1) and ETH3D (−1.5 AbsRel) but trails slightly on NYUv2 (−0.7 δ1) and ScanNet (−1.5 δ1). On video depth (ScanNet++), it outperforms ChronoDepth (AbsRel 10.4→9.3, δ1 90.7→93.4, TAE 2.3→2.1) and DepthCrafter (AbsRel 11.5→9.3, δ1 88.1→93.4, TAE 2.2→2.1), while also being substantially faster at inference (0.37 s vs. 1.04 s and 4.80 s per frame). Against the older NVDS baseline the improvement is dramatic (AbsRel 22.2→9.3, δ1 61.9→93.4). In the efficiency comparison (Table 4), Depth Any Video uses only 3 denoising steps and 1.42 B parameters, achieving the best δ1 (96.1) while running 5.6× faster than Marigold and 13× faster than DepthCrafter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative depth estimation paradigm initiated by Marigold (Ke et al., 2024) from single images to video, building directly on Stable Video Diffusion's 3D UNet architecture and latent VAE. It departs from prior video depth methods (NVDS, ChronoDepth, DepthCrafter) that either refine discriminative predictors or use video diffusion without large-scale depth supervision, by introducing a scalable synthetic data pipeline (DA-V, 6M frames from game engines) and replacing the EDM scheduler with conditional flow matching for 6.5× faster inference. The mixed-duration training strategy with RoPE and the key-frame-conditional frame interpolation module are novel architectural contributions that address the fixed-length constraint of prior video diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in video depth estimation—the scarcity of large-scale, temporally consistent ground truth—by demonstrating that game-engine synthetic data (GTA V, Cities: Skylines II) can effectively bridge to real-world generalization, a finding with broad implications for data-efficient 3D perception. The open-sourced model with 0.37s per-frame inference and support for up to 150 frames makes it practically deployable. However, the finding that 2D VAEs outperform 3D VAEs in depth reconstruction quality (Table 6) reveals an unresolved tension between temporal compression efficiency and reconstruction fidelity, and the authors acknowledge limitations with reflective surfaces and extremely long videos that remain open challenges.
