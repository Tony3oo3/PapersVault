## Problem

Existing LLM-based segmentation models like LISA treat each query independently, with information flowing only from the LLM to the mask decoder but not back. This one-way architecture prevents the model from reasoning about previously segmented objects, making it impossible to handle multi-round conversational queries that reference prior outputs (e.g., 'segment the helmet of the person you just found'). The paper asks whether segmentation models can maintain visual and textual conversational memory to respond to complex, relationship-based queries in a chat-like manner.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image, a free-form text prompt that may reference prior round outputs (e.g., 'the person from round 1'), and the segmentation masks from all previous rounds. Previous masks are not fed as full patch token sequences but as compact per-mask embeddings: one CLIP-derived mask embedding plus one positional bounding-box embedding per mask, keeping the token budget manageable across many rounds. The output is a binary segmentation mask decoded from the [SEG] token's hidden state, accompanied by a natural-language text response confirming or describing the segmented object.

## Method

SegLLM introduces a bidirectional communication protocol between the LLM and the mask decoder. The Mask-Encoding Module re-integrates each previously generated mask into the LLM's input stream as a pair of tokens (a CLIP-encoded mask embedding and a positional bounding-box embedding), enabling the LLM to 'see' what was segmented in earlier rounds. The Mask-Aware Decoding Module extends the mask decoder to accept both a [REF] token (encoding the referenced prior mask) and a [SEG] token (encoding the target query), trained with a joint loss L_seg([REF],[PAD],M_ref) + L_seg([REF],[SEG],M_tgt) using cross-entropy plus DICE loss. The backbone combines CLIP-ViT-Large (image encoder), HIPIE-R50 (mask encoder), and LLaVA-v1.5-7B (language model), fine-tuned on the newly curated MRSeg dataset, which contains multi-round conversations spanning hierarchical, positional, interactional, and attribute-oriented relationships generated from eight source datasets with GPT-4-assisted template diversification.

## Results

On the newly introduced MR-RefCOCO/+/g multi-round benchmarks, SegLLM achieves 69.3–81.9 cIoU across rounds 2–8, outperforming LISA by 14–26% and GLaMM by 12–26% cIoU. On standard single-round RefCOCO/+/g, SegLLM sets new SOTA with 80.2/70.3/72.6 cIoU (val), surpassing LISA-7B (74.1/62.4/66.4) by 5.5–6.1 points. For referring expression comprehension (Acc@0.5), SegLLM-7B reaches 90.0/82.2/83.9 on RefCOCO/+/g val, beating both NExT-Chat-7B (85.5/77.2/80.1) and the larger Shikra-13B (87.8/82.9/82.6). On the hierarchical Multi-Round PACO benchmark, SegLLM improves mIoU by 19–27 points over LISA (e.g., 54.9 vs. 34.7 in round 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegLLM decisively outperforms LISA (Lai et al., 2024) across all benchmarks: by 14–26% cIoU on multi-round MR-RefCOCO/+/g, by 5.5–6.1% cIoU on single-round RefCOCO/+/g, and by 3.6–6.7 mIoU on ReasonSeg. Against GLaMM (Rasheed et al., 2024), SegLLM leads by 12–26% cIoU on multi-round tasks, with the gap widening in later rounds as GLaMM's performance degrades. On referring expression comprehension (Acc@0.5), SegLLM-7B surpasses both NExT-Chat-7B (Zhang et al., 2024) by 4.5–5.1 points and the larger Shikra-13B (Chen et al., 2023) by 1.3–3.3 points on RefCOCO/+/g val. On the hierarchical Multi-Round PACO benchmark, SegLLM improves mIoU by 19–27 points over LISA (e.g., 54.9 vs. 34.7 in round 1), demonstrating strong part-level segmentation. For robustness to diverse question templates, SegLLM achieves 9.6% higher cIoU than LISA on averaged templates and 12.4% on diverse templates, while SESAME (Wu et al., 2024) drops by ~13% under diverse phrasing. On older baselines VLT (Ding et al., 2021) and LAVT (Yang et al., 2022), SegLLM leads by 12.7–14.1% cIoU on RefCOCO val, reflecting the generational gap between CLIP/BERT-based and LLM-based segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SegLLM extends the LISA-style architecture (VLM + [SEG] token + SAM mask decoder) by introducing a bidirectional communication protocol: a Mask-Encoding scheme that feeds previous segmentation masks back into the LLM's input stream, and a Mask-Aware Decoding scheme that conditions the mask decoder on conversational history. This departs from prior LLM segmentation models (LISA, NExT-Chat, GLaMM) where information flows only from the LLM to the mask decoder, making the LLM blind to its own visual outputs. The paper also introduces the MRSeg benchmark, constructed from RefCOCO, PACO-LVIS, LVIS, Visual Genome, and other sources, to evaluate multi-round interactive segmentation with positional, hierarchical, and interactional relationships—something no prior benchmark addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MRSeg benchmark and the finding that multi-round training transfers to single-round performance (+5.5% cIoU on RefCOCO) suggest that conversational segmentation is a richer training signal than single-round referring, potentially reshaping how the community designs segmentation training data. However, the paper's own limitations section reveals significant fragilities: the model is sensitive to conversation history ordering, relies heavily on positional keywords, and sometimes underutilizes the mask-encoding information it was designed to leverage. Open questions include whether the bidirectional feedback loop can be made order-invariant, whether mask encodings can be made more semantically informative than a single CLIP token, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the RefCOCO-style spatial relationships that dominate the training data.
