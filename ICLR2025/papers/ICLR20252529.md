## Problem

Existing high-performing speech separation models (e.g., TF-GridNet) incur large parameter counts and computational costs that preclude deployment on resource-constrained, low-latency systems. Additionally, standard evaluation datasets (WSJ0-2mix, Libri2Mix, LRS2-2Mix) lack realistic reverberation that accounts for object occlusion and material properties, causing models to generalize poorly to real-world acoustic environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252529/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a monaural mixture waveform at 16 kHz containing overlapping speech from two speakers plus environmental noise, and produces the separated clean speech waveform for each speaker. Internally, the signal is converted via STFT (640-sample Hanning window, 160-sample hop) into a 321-bin complex spectrogram at 25 Hz resolution, and the model outputs complex masks applied element-wise to the mixture spectrogram before inverse STFT reconstruction. The band-split module compresses the 321 frequency bins into 67 sub-bands of non-uniform width before processing.

## Method

TIGER applies a frequency band-split strategy that divides the 321 STFT bins into 67 sub-bands with finer granularity in low frequencies (where speech energy concentrates), compressing each sub-band to a uniform 128-channel feature via non-shared 1D convolutions. The separator stacks B parameter-shared Frequency-Frame Interleaved (FFI) blocks, each containing a frequency path and a frame path; both paths use a Multi-Scale Selective Attention (MSA) module that progressively downsamples the processing dimension through strided convolutions, fuses multi-scale features via sigmoid-gated selective attention, and decodes back to full resolution, followed by a Full-Frequency-Frame Attention (F³A) module that computes K×K (or T×T) self-attention maps to capture global cross-sub-band or cross-frame context. This interleaved design models frequency and temporal dependencies within each block while the band-split reduces total MACs by 95.3% relative to TF-GridNet. The paper also introduces EchoSet, a 27.5k-utterance dataset generated via SoundSpaces 2.0 bidirectional path tracing in Matterport3D scenes, providing realistic reverberation with object occlusion and random speech overlap ratios.

## Results

On EchoSet (the most complex dataset with realistic reverberation and random overlap), TIGER (large) achieves SDRi of 14.22 dB and SI-SDRi of 13.73 dB, surpassing the SOTA TF-GridNet (13.73/12.85 dB) by ~5%. On the simpler Libri2Mix, TIGER (large) scores 18.34/17.97 dB, trailing TF-GridNet (19.56/19.24) by ~6%. In terms of efficiency, TIGER uses only 0.82M parameters and 15.27 G MACs/s (large), representing a 94.3% parameter reduction and 95.3% MAC reduction compared to TF-GridNet (14.43M params, 323.75 G MACs/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIGER (large) outperforms all seven baselines on EchoSet, the most acoustically complex dataset, with the largest margin over BSRNN (+1.47 dB SDRi) and TF-GridNet (+0.49 dB SDRi). On the simpler Libri2Mix, TIGER (large) trails TF-GridNet by 1.22 dB SDRi but still surpasses BSRNN by 0.96 dB and A-FRCNN-16 by 1.61 dB. On LRS2-2Mix, TIGER (large) is within 0.4 dB of TF-GridNet while beating TDANet Large by 0.8 dB and BSRNN by 0.9 dB. The small variant (B=4) achieves 13.15/12.58 dB on EchoSet, only 0.58 dB below TF-GridNet, while using 94.3% fewer parameters. For cinematic sound separation on DnR, TIGER achieves 7.4/15.5/6.5 dB SI-SDR for music/speech/sound effects, outperforming BSRNN (5.5/13.8/1.8 dB) and MRX (4.2/12.3/5.7 dB) with only 1.40M parameters versus BSRNN's 52.8M.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIGER adapts the band-split frequency compression strategy from BSRNN (originally designed for music source separation) to the speech separation domain, combining it with a novel frequency-frame interleaved (FFI) block that interleaves multi-scale selective attention (MSA) and full-frequency-frame attention (F³A) along frequency and time dimensions. This departs from TF-GridNet's alternating bidirectional LSTM + self-attention design, which achieves strong performance but at 14.43M parameters and 323.75 G MACs/s, by instead using parameter-shared FFI blocks with only 0.82M parameters. The EchoSet dataset extends the line of work on realistic speech separation benchmarks (WHAMR!, LRS2-2Mix) by leveraging SoundSpaces 2.0's bidirectional path tracing over Matterport3D scenes to simulate reverberation that accounts for object occlusion and material properties, rather than simple room impulse responses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 94.3% parameter and 95.3% MAC reduction while surpassing TF-GridNet on the most complex dataset (EchoSet) makes high-quality speech separation viable on edge devices with limited compute, addressing a gap the authors identify between research benchmarks and deployment reality. The EchoSet generalization experiment—showing models trained on it outperform those trained on Libri2Mix or LRS2-2Mix on physically recorded data—validates that simulation-based datasets can bridge the train-test domain gap, which is practically important for any speech processing pipeline. Open questions include whether the band-split strategy and FFI architecture scale to more than two speakers, whether the approach supports streaming/online inference without the full bidirectional context, and whether the efficiency gains hold at higher sampling rates or longer sequences.
