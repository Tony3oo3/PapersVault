## Problem

Training graph neural networks for CFD simulations remains costly in both compute time and required training data, and prior masked pre-training techniques from NLP and vision have not been effectively adapted to unstructured mesh graphs. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a masking strategy specifically designed for mesh-based fluid dynamics, where removing nodes also removes edges and creates disconnected sub-graphs, and by demonstrating that this approach enables efficient multi-dataset pre-training that transfers to new fluid simulation tasks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an unstructured mesh graph where node features include velocity components, node-type one-hot encodings, global parameters (viscosity, gravity), and—on the aneurysm datasets—node positions, acceleration, and the next-step inflow velocity; edge features are relative displacement vectors and their norms. During pre-training, up to 40% of nodes (and all incident edges) are randomly removed, optionally augmented with K-hop edges to preserve long-range connectivity, reducing the input graph by up to 95%. The output is the next-step velocity field at all nodes; during pre-training the loss is computed only on the masked nodes, while during fine-tuning the full next-step prediction is supervised.

## Method

The architecture is an asymmetric encoder-decoder built from multigrid W-cycle GNNs (15 message-passing steps, latent size 128) with Gated MLPs (expansion factor 3, GeLU) replacing standard MLPs in the processor. During pre-training, a randomly masked subgraph is fed to the large encoder; hidden nodes are then replaced by a shared learnable [MASKED] token and hidden edges are rebuilt geometrically before a lightweight 3-step decoder reconstructs the full mesh. The two-phase training protocol runs 500k pre-training steps (loss on masked nodes only) followed by 500k fine-tuning steps on the encoder alone with no masking. A key design choice is the high masking ratio (25–40% of nodes, equivalent to 40–70% of mesh information), which forces the model to retrieve information from distant mesh regions rather than relying on local neighbors, and the method supports simultaneous pre-training across multiple datasets before per-task fine-tuning.

## Results

The proposed MeshMask method achieves state-of-the-art all-rollout RMSE across all seven CFD datasets. On the CYLINDER dataset, it reduces all-rollout RMSE to 29×10⁻³, a 59.5% improvement over the MGN baseline (71.4×10⁻³) and 49% over the Multigrid baseline (56.9×10⁻³). On the challenging 3D-ANEURYSM dataset (250k nodes), it achieves 8772×10⁻³ all-rollout RMSE versus 13747×10⁻³ for MGN and 11327×10⁻³ for Multigrid. Notably, 1-step RMSE improvements are minimal, indicating the gains come primarily from reduced long-term error propagation rather than single-step accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MGN (Pfaff et al. 2021), MeshMask achieves the largest relative gains: 59.5% lower all-rollout RMSE on CYLINDER (29 vs 71.4), 73.4% on PLATE (4.5 vs 16.9), and 36.2% on 3D-ANEURYSM (8772 vs 13747). Against the Multigrid baseline (Garnier et al. 2024), improvements are 49% on CYLINDER, 44.4% on PLATE, and 22.5% on 3D-ANEURYSM. Against MultiScaleGNN (Lino et al. 2021), MeshMask wins on all five overlapping datasets, with the largest margin on CYLINDER (29 vs 61.2, a 52.6% reduction) and PLATE (4.5 vs 15.7). Against BSMS-GNN (Cao et al. 2023), MeshMask outperforms on all six overlapping datasets, most notably on CYLINDER (29 vs 56.9) and PLATE (4.5 vs 16.6); however, BSMS-GNN retains a slight 1-step RMSE edge on 3D-ANEURYSM (719 vs 725) and AIRFOIL (314 vs 310). Overall, the gains are concentrated in long-term (all-rollout) prediction, while 1-step accuracy is roughly on par with the best baselines, confirming that the masking pre-training primarily mitigates error accumulation over autoregressive rollouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the masked auto-encoder paradigm from NLP (BERT) and computer vision (MAE) to mesh-based graph neural networks for CFD, combining it with the multi-grid W-cycle GNN architecture from Garnier et al. (2024) and the MeshGraphNet formulation of Pfaff et al. (2021). The key methodological departure is the application of node/edge masking to unstructured meshes with K-hop edge reconstruction, rather than the pixel or token masking used in prior domains. The paper also introduces a substantially larger 3D intracranial aneurysm dataset (250k nodes, 3M edges) that extends the scale of prior CFD GNN benchmarks by roughly an order of magnitude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3D aneurysm dataset represents a significant new benchmark for CFD GNNs, pushing mesh sizes far beyond what prior studies handled and introducing pulsatile inflow physics relevant to biomedical applications. The multi-dataset pre-training capability is practically important, reducing the cost of training models for new fluid dynamics tasks. Open questions include whether the masking strategy generalizes to other PDE families (e.g., elasticity, heat transfer), whether the approach scales to meshes beyond 250k nodes without sub-mesh partitioning, and whether the observed improvements in long-term prediction translate to clinically useful aneurysm hemodynamic predictions.
