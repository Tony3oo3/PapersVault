## Problem

Existing knowledge editing (KE) methods for LLMs are designed for a single-party, single-model setting, and naively combining local KE with standard model merging techniques (Task-Arithmetic, Simple-Average, TIES-Merging) yields severe performance degradation compared to the ideal global editing upper bound, especially as the number of edits grows. This paper identifies the first systematic study of collaborative KE—where multiple privacy-preserving parties jointly edit a shared LLM—and pinpoints three unique challenges absent in single-party editing: knowledge overlap, knowledge conflict, and knowledge forgetting.

## Input / Output

Each client locally performs KE on its private edit requests (triples of subject, relation, object) to produce a weight update Δ_i and the outer product K_i K_i^T of its input-key matrix; only these two quantities are transmitted to the global server, never the raw edit requests or the key matrix K_i itself. The server outputs a single updated global LLM whose weights equal those of a hypothetical GLOBAL-EDIT that had access to all edit requests simultaneously. The framework is backend-agnostic, supporting both direct-editing methods (MEMIT, ROME) and hypernetwork-based methods (MALMEN, MEND) with slightly different coefficient computations (C vs. κ_l I).

## Method

COLLABEDIT's core insight (Lemma 1) is that the global weight update Δ_G decomposes as a weighted sum of local updates Δ_i, where the weight α_i = (C + K_i K_i^T)(C + Σ_j K_j K_j^T)^{-1} depends on the covariance matrices of all clients' input keys. To preserve privacy, clients share only K_i K_i^T (proven non-invertible to K_i via the infinite family of orthogonal transformations), and the server computes the exact α_i coefficients to form the non-destructive merge. This contrasts with destructive baselines that apply a uniform scalar λ (Task-Arithmetic) or simple averaging, which ignore the per-client covariance structure and accumulate error as edit count increases. For the three identified challenges, COLLABEDIT provides targeted remedies: tracking the ℓ₂-norm of the editing residual R to detect knowledge overlap, a two-stage conflict-resolution mechanism combining privacy-preserving detection with data augmentation on the retained edit, and a dynamic covariance matrix C = β₀C₀ + β₁Σ K_i K_i^T that continuously incorporates new knowledge to mitigate forgetting across rounds.

## Results

On GPT-J (6B) with MEMIT as the backend KE algorithm, COLLABEDIT achieves a Score of 78.91 on Multi-CounterFact (MCF) and 52.17 on zsRE, matching or slightly exceeding the upper-bound GLOBAL-EDIT (79.03 and 51.92 respectively). On GPT2-XL, COLLABEDIT scores 77.18 (MCF) and 42.95 (zsRE), again nearly identical to GLOBAL-EDIT (77.08 and 43.12). The destructive baselines (Simple-Average, Task-Arithmetic, TIES-Merging) lag significantly behind, with Score gaps of 15–40 points depending on the dataset and model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COLLABEDIT decisively outperforms all three destructive model-merging baselines across both datasets and both model sizes. On GPT-J (6B) MCF, COLLABEDIT (Score 78.91) beats Task-Arithmetic (63.44) by 15.5 points, Simple-Average (54.22) by 24.7 points, and TIES-Merging (38.16) by 40.8 points. On zsRE, the gap is even wider: COLLABEDIT (52.17) surpasses Simple-Average (40.22) by 12 points, TIES-Merging (37.68) by 14.5 points, and Task-Arithmetic (30.98) by 21.2 points. Notably, COLLABEDIT matches or marginally exceeds the GLOBAL-EDIT upper bound (52.17 vs. 51.92 on zsRE), confirming that the non-destructive merging recovers the full editing performance without requiring access to raw edit requests. The baselines' relatively high NS/NA values are attributed to under-fitting rather than genuine locality preservation, as their PS/ES/PA/EA scores are dramatically lower.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: knowledge editing (KE) for LLMs (MEMIT, ROME, MALMEN) and model merging for collaborative/federated learning (Simple-Average, Task-Arithmetic, TIES-Merging). While each line is well-developed for its single-party setting, the paper shows that naively combining them—applying local KE then merging weights—is fundamentally 'destructive,' with the performance gap to the global-edit upper bound widening as edit count grows. The key theoretical contribution (Lemma 1) reveals that the global edit decomposes as a weighted sum of local edits with coefficients determined by the covariance matrices of input keys, enabling a privacy-preserving non-destructive merge by sharing only KK^T rather than the raw keys.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COLLABEDIT provides the first principled framework for multi-party, privacy-preserving knowledge editing of a shared LLM, a scenario that becomes increasingly relevant as organizations co-manage model knowledge without exposing proprietary edit requests. The three identified challenges (overlap, conflict, forgetting) and their preliminary remedies (residual monitoring, two-stage conflict resolution, dynamic covariance) open a research agenda, though the conflict and forgetting solutions remain case-study-level rather than fully general. Limitations include evaluation on only two relatively small models (GPT-2 XL, GPT-J 6B) and two datasets, and the privacy guarantee relies on the non-triviality of inverting KK^T, which is argued theoretically and shown empirically for M≥8 but not formally bounded. Scaling to larger LLMs, adversarial settings, and composite conflict operations are explicitly left for future work.
