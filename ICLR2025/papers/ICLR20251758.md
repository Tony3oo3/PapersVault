## Problem

Gradient spikes during LLM training—reaching up to 1000× typical magnitudes—disrupt learning and cause costly checkpoint restarts, yet existing architectural mitigations (scaled initialization, embedding detach, etc.) cannot fully eliminate them. The paper identifies that these spikes have prolonged, compounding detrimental effects on Adam's exponential moving averages (first and second moments), a mechanism that prior work had not systematically characterized or addressed at the optimizer level.

## Input / Output

SPAM consumes per-parameter gradients g_t ∈ R^{m×n} at each step along with the running first moment m and second moment v already maintained by Adam, plus hyperparameters (spike threshold θ, reset interval ΔT, warmup steps N, and sparsity density d). It outputs updated model parameters w_t. Unusually, spike detection is performed on-the-fly using the ratio g_i²/V_i as a memory-free proxy for the Gradient Spike Score, avoiding the need to store full gradient history, and in sparse mode only a randomly selected fraction d of parameters carry moment state at any given time.

## Method

SPAM augments Adam with three coupled mechanisms. First, Momentum Reset periodically zeroes both the first and second moments every ΔT steps (default 500), followed by N steps (default 150) of cosine-scaled warmup to prevent abrupt instability. Second, Spike-Aware Clipping detects individual gradient elements where g_i²/V_i exceeds threshold θ (default 5000) and rescales them to sign(g_i)·√(θV_i), preserving directional information while capping magnitude—distinct from global norm or value clipping. Third, Sparse Momentum exploits the reset structure to maintain and update moments only for a randomly re-sampled subset of parameters at each reset interval, drastically reducing optimizer-state memory while matching or exceeding the quality of full-moment Adam. The random re-sampling at each reset (rather than fixed or gradient-magnitude-based selection) is shown empirically to be critical for exploration across all parameters.

## Results

On C4 pre-training, SPAM achieves the lowest perplexity across all LLaMA model sizes: 30.46 (60M), 23.36 (130M), 17.42 (350M), and 14.66 (1B), outperforming Adam by 1.5–3.6 ppl and Adafactor by 0.3–2.1 ppl. In the memory-efficient setting (matching GaLore's memory budget), SPAM achieves 32.39/23.98/18.28/15.60 ppl versus GaLore's 34.88/25.36/18.95/15.64, while using roughly half the memory of full Adam. On LLaMA2-7B fine-tuning (Commonsense170K), SPAM at 0.25% density achieves 64.4 average accuracy across 8 downstream tasks, surpassing full fine-tuning with Adam (63.4) and GaLore (63.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPAM consistently outperforms all baselines across model scales. Against Adam (the direct baseline), SPAM reduces perplexity by 3.63 ppl at 60M, 1.55 at 130M, 1.35 at 350M, and 1.47 at 1B. Adafactor, the strongest non-SPAM baseline, is beaten by 2.11 ppl at 60M and 0.53 at 1B. Gradient clipping variants (Value Clip, Norm Clip) improve over vanilla Adam but remain 2.9–3.6 ppl behind SPAM at 60M. In the memory-efficient comparison (Table 3), SPAM at matched memory to GaLore achieves 2.49 ppl lower at 60M and 1.38 at 130M, while at 1B the gap narrows to 0.04 ppl. On fine-tuning (Table 4), SPAM at 0.25% density (36G memory) exceeds full fine-tuning Adam (61G) by 1.0 average points and GaLore by 0.8, demonstrating that sparse momentum with spike-aware clipping is both more accurate and more memory-efficient than existing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPAM builds directly on Adam's exponential moving average framework, repurposing momentum reset—a technique previously applied to convex optimization (Nesterov, O'Donoghue & Candes)—for the non-convex, large-scale setting of LLM training. It departs from the dominant line of spike-mitigation work (Scaled Initialization, Embed LN, Scaled Embed, Embed Detach, DeepNorm), which modifies model architecture, by instead intervening at the optimizer level. The spike-aware clipping mechanism is positioned as a more targeted alternative to both global gradient clipping (norm/value) and Adafactor's update clipping, while the sparse momentum component draws on the memory-efficient optimizer lineage (Adafactor, Adam-mini, GaLore) but replaces low-rank projection with periodic random subset selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Gradient spikes impose real operational costs in LLM training (checkpoint recovery, experiment restarts), and this paper provides the first systematic evidence that they are pervasive across architectures, datasets, and model sizes, with magnitudes up to 1000× typical gradients that persist in Adam's second moment for 200+ steps. The practical impact is significant: SPAM's sparse momentum enables memory-efficient training that outperforms GaLore and Adam-mini at matched memory budgets, offering a simpler alternative to low-rank gradient projection. Key limitations include the preliminary nature of the theoretical analysis (convexity assumption, sensitivity to the gradient bound G), experiments restricted to models up to 1B parameters, and the open question of whether the fixed GSS threshold and reset interval generalize to 7B+ scale or distributed training regimes.
