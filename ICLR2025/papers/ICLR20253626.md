## Problem

Black-box text embedding models produce high-quality but opaque representations, while existing interpretable embedding methods (ChiLL, QAEmb) rely on costly expert-crafted questions or meticulously designed multi-template prompts, limiting their generalizability across diverse tasks. The core gap is the lack of a systematic, general-purpose approach to automatically generate highly discriminative yes/no questions that serve as interpretable embedding dimensions without requiring domain expertise or extensive prompt engineering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253626/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes arbitrary text documents or sentences as input and produces a binary embedding vector of length m (9,614 dimensions in the default configuration), where each dimension corresponds to the yes/no answer of a specific generated question. The embedding is inherently interpretable: similarity between two texts is explained by the subset of questions both answer 'yes' to (cognitive load = inner product of the two binary vectors). A single universal question set is generated once from a training corpus and reused across all downstream tasks (STS, retrieval, clustering, classification), making it analogous to a pre-trained encoder.

## Method

CQG-MBQA operates in two phases. First, Contrastive Question Generation (CQG) encodes a 6.8M-text corpus with AngleE, clusters embeddings via k-Means (k=5,000), then for each cluster samples 6 positive, 18 hard-negative (from neighboring clusters), and 18 easy-negative texts, prompting GPT-4o-mini to generate questions that yield 'yes' exclusively for positives and 'no' for all negatives; a probing mechanism scores each question's discriminative quality and a cosine-similarity deduplication step removes redundant questions, yielding 9,614 final dimensions. Second, the Multi-task Binary Question Answering (MBQA) model freezes the AngleE encoder and attaches 9,614 lightweight MLP classification heads (3-layer, hidden size 8), trained with weighted BCE loss on LLM-generated answers from only 1,000 articles per question (10M text-question pairs total). This distills LLM reasoning into a single forward pass, achieving 96% answer-reproduction accuracy and reducing the cost of embedding the full MS MARCO dev set from ~$244,551 (direct LLM QA) to ~$41.

## Results

On STS (Spearman correlation), CQG-MBQA achieves 77.60 average across seven datasets, approaching SimCSE(Sup.) (79.86) and SBERT-New (80.02) while trailing AnglE (85.93) and OpenAI (82.31). On retrieval (nDCG@10), it scores 32.78 average, surpassing SimCSE(Sup.) (27.59) and SimCSE-Unsup. (24.85) but falling short of AnglE (59.80) and OpenAI (61.91). On clustering (V-Measure), it reaches 35.48 average, exceeding SimCSE(Sup.) (31.03) and BERT (28.26) while remaining below AnglE (42.11) and OpenAI (43.07). Across all tasks, CQG-MBQA outperforms every other interpretable baseline by a wide margin (e.g., 77.60 vs. 65.20 over QAEmb-MBQA on STS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CQG-MBQA (77.60 avg Spearman on STS) trails the top black-box models AnglE (85.93) and OpenAI (82.31) by 8.3 and 4.7 points respectively, but is competitive with mid-tier black-box models like SimCSE-Sup. (79.86) and SBERT-New (80.02), losing by only 2.3 and 2.4 points. On retrieval (nDCG@10), CQG-MBQA (32.78) clearly beats SimCSE-Sup. (27.59), SimCSE-Unsup. (24.85), GloVe (25.33), and BERT (11.47), but falls well behind AnglE (59.80), OpenAI (61.91), SBERT-New (51.36), and even the rule-based BM25 (48.03). On clustering (V-Measure), CQG-MBQA (35.48) surpasses SimCSE-Sup. (31.03), SimCSE-Unsup. (26.70), GloVe (26.44), and BERT (28.26), while remaining below AnglE (42.11) and OpenAI (43.07). Against the closest interpretable baseline QAEmb-MBQA, CQG-MBQA wins decisively on all three tasks: +12.4 on STS (77.60 vs. 65.20), +13.7 on retrieval (32.78 vs. 19.08), and +6.5 on clustering (35.48 vs. 29.02). The cognitive load of CQG-MBQA is also 2.5–3.6× lower than QAEmb-MBQA (449 vs. 1,467 average), confirming that the contrastive question generation produces sparser, more interpretable embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of interpretable text embedding via binary question answers, building directly on ChiLL (expert-crafted clinical questions) and QAEmb (LLM-generated questions for fMRI prediction). The key conceptual departure is replacing QAEmb's example-based, task-specific prompting with a contrastive learning formulation (positive/hard-negative/easy-negative sampling) that systematically generates discriminative questions in a domain-agnostic manner. The MBQA distillation model follows the same architectural pattern as LISA and QAEmb (freezing an encoder, training classification heads on LLM-generated labels), but the CQG question generation pipeline and probing-based quality filtering are novel contributions that address the genericity and discriminability limitations of prior question-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to interpretable embeddings at scale: the cost reduction from ~$244K (LLM-based QA on MS MARCO) to ~$41 (MBQA) makes interpretable text embedding feasible for large corpora, and the tunable threshold τ lets users trade off quality against cognitive load per application. However, CQG-MBQA still trails top black-box models (AnglE, OpenAI) by 7–29 points on retrieval nDCG, and the framework inherits the quality ceiling of its underlying encoder (AnglE) for both clustering and embedding, raising open questions about whether stronger encoders or adaptive question sets could close this gap. The reliance on a fixed, corpus-level question set also limits domain adaptability without re-running the full CQG pipeline.
