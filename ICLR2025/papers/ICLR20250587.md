## Problem

Neural functional networks (NFNs) that process DNN weights as input have been developed for MLPs and CNNs, but no systematic design exists for transformer architectures. The fundamental obstacle is that the symmetry group governing functional equivalence of transformer weights—particularly in multi-head attention—has not been characterized, making it impossible to construct equivariant NFNs that respect the intrinsic structure of transformer parameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the structured weight tensor of a single transformer block: per-head attention matrices W^(Q,i), W^(K,i) ∈ R^{D×D_k}, W^(V,i) ∈ R^{D×D_v}, W^(O,i) ∈ R^{D_v×D} for h heads, plus the two-layer ReLU MLP weights W^(A), W^(B) and biases. The output is a scalar predicted test accuracy, evaluated via Kendall's τ rank correlation against ground-truth accuracy across 125,000+ checkpoints. Unlike prior NFNs that flatten weights into a vector, Transformer-NFN preserves the multi-head tensor structure so that the equivariant polynomial layers can exploit the head-permutation and GL_{D_k}×GL_{D_v} symmetries directly.

## Method

The paper first proves that the maximal symmetric group of a multi-head attention module is S_h × GL_{D_k}(R)^h × GL_{D_v}(R)^h, showing that two parameter sets define the same function if and only if they are related by a head permutation τ and per-head invertible transformations M^(i) ∈ GL_{D_k}, N^(i) ∈ GL_{D_v} acting as (W^(Q,i)·M^T, W^(K,i)·M^{-1}, W^(V,i)·N, N^{-1}·W^(O,i)). This result, combined with permutation symmetries of the MLP neurons, defines the full group action G_U on the transformer block weight space. Transformer-NFN is then built from equivariant polynomial layers whose parameters obey a parameter-sharing constraint: the invariant layer I(U) is a quadratic polynomial that sums the products [W]^(Q,s)·([W]^(K,s))^T and [W]^(V,s)·[W]^(O,s) across all heads (enforcing S_h invariance) and applies shared coefficients across the GL_{D_k} and GL_{D_v} orbits, yielding a compact architecture that is provably G_U-invariant. The model is trained in a supervised setting to predict accuracy, and ablations confirm that the encoder (transformer block) weights alone carry nearly all predictive signal.

## Results

Transformer-NFN predicts the test accuracy of pretrained transformer models from their weights using Kendall's τ as the evaluation metric. On MNIST-Transformers (vision), it achieves a Kendall's τ of 0.905 (no threshold), outperforming all baselines by 0.004–0.026. On AGNews-Transformers (NLP), it reaches 0.910 (no threshold), with gaps of 0.018–0.033 over the strongest baselines. Performance remains consistent across accuracy thresholds (20%–80%), and the encoder weights alone (Kendall's τ = 0.902/0.909) nearly match the full model (0.905/0.910), confirming that transformer block parameters carry the dominant signal for generalization prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Transformer-NFN consistently outperforms all four baselines across both datasets and all accuracy thresholds. On MNIST-Transformers, it beats the strongest baseline (STATNN at 0.881) by 0.024 in the no-threshold setting, and the gap widens at higher thresholds (e.g., 0.888 vs. 0.856 for STATNN at 80%). On AGNews-Transformers, the advantage is even more pronounced: Transformer-NFN (0.910) surpasses XGBoost (0.859) by 0.051 and STATNN (0.841) by 0.069 in the no-threshold setting. MLP is competitive at low thresholds (0.879 on AGNews) but degrades sharply at 40% (0.841), while Transformer-NFN remains stable (0.897). Random Forest is the weakest baseline throughout, trailing Transformer-NFN by over 0.10 on both datasets. Notably, XGBoost shows an unusual spike at the 80% threshold on MNIST (0.884), but Transformer-NFN still edges it out (0.888).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural functional network (NFN) framework—previously developed for MLPs and CNNs via permutation-equivariant and monomial-equivariant architectures (Navon et al. 2023; Tran et al. 2024a; Vo et al. 2024)—to transformer architectures. The key theoretical departure is the identification of the maximal symmetric group governing multi-head attention weights (S_h × GL_Dk^h × GL_Dv^h), which has no direct analogue in MLP/CNN weight spaces and requires new quadratic polynomial terms (QK and VO products) to capture attention-specific structure. The parameter-sharing strategy for constructing equivariant polynomial layers directly inherits from the monomial-equivariant NFN framework of Tran et al. (2024a) and the polynomial functional network construction of Vo et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of NFN design for transformers, providing both a theoretical foundation (maximal symmetric group, group action on weight space) and a practical equivariant architecture. The Small Transformer Zoo dataset (125K+ checkpoints across vision and NLP) fills a notable gap in benchmarking resources for transformer weight-space analysis. However, the scope is limited: experiments use only 2-block, 2-head transformers on classification tasks, and the identified group action explicitly excludes scaling symmetries from ReLU MLPs and LayerNorm, which the authors defer to future work. Open questions include whether the equivariant design principles scale to deeper/wider transformers, whether the encoder-dominance finding (attention weights alone nearly match full-model prediction) holds for generative or multi-task settings, and how to incorporate the remaining symmetries for a truly maximal group.
