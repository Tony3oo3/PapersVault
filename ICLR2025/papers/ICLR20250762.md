## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods collapse under adversarial attacks, with AUROC dropping to near-random levels (below 50%) at ε=8/255. Prior robust detection approaches rely on large auxiliary OOD datasets combined with adversarial training, but this is costly, biases toward specific OOD instances, and still leaves a substantial clean-to-adversarial performance gap due to the unbounded variety of OOD samples at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250762/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces a single scalar OOD score — the probability assigned to the OOD class by the final orthogonal binary layer. During adversarial evaluation, both ID and OOD test samples are perturbed end-to-end via PGD targeting the OOD score itself (increasing it for ID, decreasing it for OOD), with ε=8/255 for low-resolution and ε=4/255 for high-resolution images.

## Method

AROS first adversarially trains a WideResNet-70-16 classifier on ID data only (PGD¹⁰, ℓ∞) to obtain robust embeddings, then fits K class-conditional Gaussians to these embeddings and samples from low-likelihood regions (below threshold β) to synthesize fake OOD embeddings without any auxiliary OOD images. A time-invariant NODE is trained on the combined ID/fake-OOD embedding set with a tailored loss L_SL that enforces Lyapunov asymptotic stability via the Levy-Desplanques criterion: the Jacobian of the NODE dynamics must be strictly diagonally dominant with non-positive diagonal entries, ensuring perturbed inputs converge back to their equilibrium points. An orthogonal binary layer (wᵀw=I) follows the NODE to maximize the geometric separation between ID and OOD equilibrium neighborhoods, mitigating vanishing/exploding gradients in the infinite-depth NODE while enforcing low Lipschitz constants. The OOD score at inference is simply the OOD-class probability from this final layer.

## Results

Under PGD^1000 (ℓ∞, ε=8/255) adversarial attack, AROS achieves 80.1% AUROC on CIFAR-10 vs. CIFAR-100 and 67.0% on CIFAR-100 vs. CIFAR-10, substantially outperforming the previous best robust methods (RODEO at 37.8% and 29.0% respectively). Under clean evaluation, AROS scores 88.2% and 74.3% AUROC, competitive with but below DHM (100.0%) and ATOM (94.2%). On ImageNet-1k vs. Texture under PGD^1000, AROS reaches 69.2% AUROC versus 19.4% for RODEO. Under non-adversarial corruptions (CIFAR-10-C), AROS achieves a mean AUROC of 81.8%, compared to 73.7% for ATD and 72.5% for VOS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AROS decisively outperforms all 11 baselines under adversarial attacks. Against the strongest prior robust detectors, AROS improves PGD^1000 AUROC from 37.8% (RODEO) to 80.1% on CIFAR-10 vs. CIFAR-100 (+42.3 pp) and from 29.0% (RODEO) to 67.0% on CIFAR-100 vs. CIFAR-10 (+38.0 pp). The stability-based classifiers (SODEF, LyaDEQ, ASODE) used as post-hoc OOD detectors achieve 61.9%, 56.5%, and 56.3% AUROC respectively under PGD^1000, all well below AROS's 80.1%. Clean-detection methods (VOS, DHM, CATEX, CSI) collapse to near-random or below (0.0–5.4% AUROC) under PGD^1000, confirming their vulnerability. ALOS and ATD, which use auxiliary OOD data plus adversarial training, reach 16.1% and 37.1% AUROC respectively, still far below AROS which requires no auxiliary data. On the OSR setup (Table 2b), AROS improves mean PGD^1000 AUROC from 52.1% (RODEO) to 69.3%, and on ImageNet-1k (Table 2a) from 44.4% (RODEO) to 74.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

AROS adapts the time-invariant NODE framework with Lyapunov stability constraints from SODEF (Kang et al., 2021), which was designed for adversarially robust classification, to the distinct problem of OOD detection. It departs from the dominant paradigm in robust OOD detection (ALOE, ATOM, ATD, RODEO), which relies on auxiliary OOD datasets combined with adversarial training, by eliminating the need for any OOD image data through Gaussian-based fake embedding generation in feature space. The orthogonal binary layer draws on orthogonal neural network literature for its gradient-preserving and Lipschitz-constraining properties, repurposing it here to maximize separation between ID and OOD equilibrium points rather than for classification robustness per se.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that virtually all existing OOD detectors collapse to near-random performance under strong adversarial attacks (PGD^1000), exposing a critical safety gap for deployment in medical imaging, autonomous driving, and other safety-critical domains. AROS's 40+ point AUROC improvement over the best prior robust method, achieved without auxiliary OOD data or pretrained models, suggests that stability-theoretic constraints in embedding space may be a more principled and data-efficient route to robust detection than the current auxiliary-data-plus-adversarial-training recipe. Open questions include whether the class-conditional Gaussian assumption for ID boundary estimation holds for highly multimodal or high-dimensional feature spaces, whether the approach extends to non-image modalities, and how the clean-vs-robust trade-off (88.2% clean vs. 80.1% adversarial) can be further narrowed.
