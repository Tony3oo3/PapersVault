## Problem

Most Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language models are closed-source, offering limited or no information about training data, code, or design recipes, despite MoEs introducing complex new architectural decisions (expert granularity, routing algorithm, shared experts) that the field needs to understand. Prior open-source efforts have been largely limited to dense LMs, leaving a gap in fully transparent, state-of-the-art MoE models that could serve as a research baseline for cost-efficient language modeling.

## Input / Output

The model is a decoder-only LM that consumes standard text tokens and produces next-token probability distributions. Its distinctive property is sparse activation: each input token activates only 8 of 64 experts per layer (1.3B active parameters out of 6.9B total), yielding inference compute comparable to a ~1B dense model while requiring GPU memory to store the full 7B parameter set. The adapted OLMoE-1B-7B-INSTRUCT variant additionally accepts instruction prompts and generates multi-turn conversational responses.

## Method

OLMoE-1B-7B replaces the feedforward network in each transformer layer with a MoE module of 64 small experts (FFN dim 1,024), activating 8 per token via dropless token-choice routing with a learned linear router. Training from scratch on 5.1T tokens (OLMoE-Mix from DCLM and Dolma 1.7) uses a composite loss combining cross-entropy with a load balancing loss (weight 0.01) and a router z-loss (weight 0.001) to stabilize expert utilization and prevent router logit explosion. Controlled experiments validate key design choices: fine-grained experts outperform fewer larger ones, token-choice routing beats expert-choice, shared experts are ineffective, and sparse upcycling from a dense checkpoint offers limited benefit at this scale. The model is then adapted via instruction tuning (with added code/math data) followed by DPO preference optimization to produce OLMoE-1B-7B-INSTRUCT, which surpasses larger models like Llama2-13B-Chat and DeepSeekMoE-16B on standard benchmarks.

## Results

OLMoE-1B-7B (1.3B active, 6.9B total parameters) achieves 54.1 MMLU, 80.0 HellaSwag, 62.1 ARC-Challenge, and 79.8 PIQA after pretraining on 5.1T tokens, making it the best-performing model among all open models with <2B active parameters. After instruction and preference tuning (OLMoE-1B-7B-INSTRUCT), it reaches 51.9 MMLU (0-shot), 45.5 GSM8k (8-shot CoT), 54.8 HumanEval (Pass@10), and 84.0% AlpacaEval win rate, achieving the highest average score (57.7) among all compared adapted models. The MoE reaches the final performance of an equivalent dense model with ~3× fewer tokens (~2× faster wall-clock training) due to memory overhead from storing 7B total parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among ~1B active parameter models, OLMoE-1B-7B (54.1 MMLU) decisively outperforms DCLM-1B (48.5), Llama3.2-1B (38.2), TinyLlama-1B (33.6), OLMo-1B (32.1), and Pythia-1B (31.1), establishing a clear state-of-the-art in this cost regime. Against 7B dense models, OLMoE beats Llama2-7B (46.2 MMLU) and is competitive with OLMo-7B (54.9 MMLU) despite using ~5× fewer active parameters, but falls short of Mistral-7B (64.0), DCLM-7B (64.4), and Llama3.1-8B (66.9). In the ~2-3B active parameter range, OLMoE surpasses StableLM-2B (40.4), DeepSeek-3B-16B (45.5), and JetMoE-2B-9B (49.1) on MMLU, though Qwen1.5-3B-14B (62.4) remains stronger in pretraining. After adaptation, OLMoE-1B-7B-INSTRUCT (avg 57.7) edges out Qwen1.5-3B-14B+Chat (avg 57.3) and DeepSeek-3B-16B+Chat (avg 57.0) despite having >2× fewer total parameters, and its 84.0% AlpacaEval score exceeds Llama2-13B-Chat. In the domain specialization analysis (Figure 7), OLMoE exhibits strong expert specialization across domains (e.g., near-100% for arXiv in layer 0), whereas Mixtral-8x7B shows near-uniform routing, suggesting greater expert redundancy in the upcycled model.

## Relation to Prior Work

OLMoE extends the OLMo dense model family (Groeneveld et al., 2024) by replacing feedforward layers with sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts modules following the top-k routing paradigm of Shazeer et al. (2017), while adopting fine-grained expert granularity (64 small experts, 8 active) inspired by DeepSeekMoE (Dai et al., 2024). It departs from prior MoE work in two key ways: it is the first fully open MoE (releasing weights, data, code, and intermediate checkpoints), and it systematically ablates design choices—routing algorithm, expert granularity, shared experts, and sparse upcycling—that prior closed-source models treated as fixed. The paper challenges two prior findings: that shared experts improve performance (contradicting DeepSeekMoE) and that sparse upcycling from a dense checkpoint is broadly beneficial (contradicting Komatsuzaki et al., 2023), showing both are ineffective or limited at OLMoE's compute scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OLMoE provides the first fully transparent reference for open MoE research, enabling the community to study routing dynamics, expert specialization, and training dynamics that were previously inaccessible in closed models. The new analysis properties—router saturation, expert co-activation, domain specialization, and vocabulary specialization—open a new research direction for understanding what MoE experts actually learn. Open questions include why the first layer saturates so slowly (and whether omitting MoE there, as in DeepSeekMoE, is the right fix), whether expert-choice routing becomes preferable in multimodal settings, and whether systems optimizations can close the gap between the 3× FLOP savings and the observed 2× wall-clock speedup. The finding that Mixtral exhibits little expert specialization (likely due to upcycling from Mistral) suggests initialization strategy may be as important as architecture for unlocking MoE potential.
