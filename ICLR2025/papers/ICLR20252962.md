## Problem

Traditional domain adaptation of LLMs suffers from two coupled inefficiencies: continual pre-training on raw domain corpora memorizes knowledge without awareness of how it will be utilized, and the subsequent instruction tuning and alignment stages force the model to simultaneously learn knowledge utilization and format alignment under divergent optimization objectives. This leads to suboptimal domain performance, catastrophic forgetting of general capabilities, and a reliance on expensive proprietary-model-synthesized domain instructions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three raw data sources: domain-specific documents (e.g., ~4B tokens of Wikipedia, ~0.7B tokens of AutoMathText, ~1B tokens of StarCoder Python), general instruction data (TULU-V2-mix, ~326K samples), and preference pairs (UltraFeedback, ~64K pairs). All are stripped of chat templates and concatenated into a single unified text stream with [EOS] separators at max sequence length 2048. The output is a domain-adapted LLM (base or chat) that retains general capabilities while gaining domain-specific knowledge, with the format alignment stage requiring only ~20K selected samples (10K SFT + 10K DPO) drawn from data already seen during pre-training.

## Method

Mix-CPT decouples domain adaptation into two stages. In the first stage (knowledge mixture continual pre-training), domain documents, template-free general instructions, and positive-preference responses are mixed into a unified corpus and trained with next-token prediction plus a Logit Swap Self-Distillation (LSSD) constraint: the frozen original model's logits serve as a teacher signal, but the top-1 predicted token's logit is swapped with the ground-truth token's logit, so the reverse-KL distillation loss only calibrates the ground-truth token probability while preserving the rest of the original distribution, mitigating catastrophic forgetting. In the second stage (general format alignment), a Format Alignment Score (FAS) — the ratio of conditional NTP loss with formatted chat templates to unconditional NTP loss — selects the top-K hardest samples from the pre-training instruction set for a brief SFT + DPO pass, ensuring the model focuses purely on style/format learning without introducing new knowledge.

## Results

On the full domain adaptation pipeline (Table 2), Mix-CPT consistently outperforms the traditional CSD (CPT+SFT+DPO) method on target-domain tasks while preserving general capabilities. For LLaMA3-8B-Base adapted to the Code domain, Mix-CPT achieves 52.67 (Code) vs. 51.75 for CSD, with an overall average of 58.25 vs. 58.75. For QWen2-7B-Base adapted to the Math domain, Mix-CPT reaches 57.48 (Math) vs. 56.78 for CSD, with an average of 68.29 vs. 66.45. In the Wiki domain, Mix-CPT shows the largest gains: 34.55 vs. 30.29 for QWen2-7B-Base and 33.21 vs. 28.27 for LLaMA3-8B-Base, while also improving general benchmarks (e.g., IF: 76.77 vs. 76.79 for QWen2, 71.81 vs. 69.89 for LLaMA3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is CSD (traditional CPT + SFT + DPO), which Mix-CPT outperforms on target-domain tasks across all six domain-model combinations (e.g., Wiki: 34.55 vs. 30.29 for QWen2-7B; Code: 52.67 vs. 51.75 for LLaMA3-8B). Mix-CPT also matches or exceeds CSD on general capabilities (RC, CR, EX, IF), whereas CSD often degrades them relative to the reimplemented chat baseline (e.g., LLaMA3-8B Wiki: CSD drops IF from 69.55 to 66.51, while Mix-CPT recovers to 68.88). Against the Reimplemented Chat baseline (SFT+DPO without any domain CPT), Mix-CPT achieves substantially higher domain scores (e.g., Code: 52.67 vs. 41.96 for LLaMA3-8B; Math: 57.48 vs. 58.12 for QWen2-7B) while maintaining comparable general performance. The Official Chat LLMs (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, QWen2-7B-Chat) remain the strongest overall but rely on proprietary closed-source training data; Mix-CPT narrows the gap significantly on domain tasks while using only open-source data. In the medical domain (Table 3), Mix-CPT achieves 63.4 average on domain benchmarks vs. 55.7 for CPT, while maintaining general performance (47.6 vs. 43.7 average), further confirming the advantage over traditional CPT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper restructures the standard three-stage domain adaptation pipeline (continual pre-training → instruction tuning → DPO alignment) by merging instruction and alignment data into the pre-training stage as a 'knowledge mixture,' then performing only minimal format alignment afterward. It builds on the knowledge distillation framework of Gu et al. (2023) by proposing Logit Swap Self-Distillation (LSSD), which modifies the teacher signal by exchanging the top-1 predicted token logit with the ground-truth token logit before computing reverse KL divergence. The key departure from prior work (e.g., Jiang et al. 2024, Cheng et al. 2024a) is the claim that general instructions suffice for learning knowledge utilization, eliminating the need for expensive domain-specific instruction synthesis via proprietary models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for domain adaptation that avoids the costly and fragile step of synthesizing domain-specific instructions, which is significant for practitioners working with limited compute or proprietary model access. The LSSD mechanism is simple (a single logit swap) yet provides a principled way to constrain parameter updates during CPT, making it potentially applicable beyond the tested domains. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B/8B models, modest and sometimes inconsistent gains (QWen2-7B shows degradation in math/code even with Mix-CPT), and the format alignment score selection strategy remains heuristic. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether optimal mixing ratios of domain/instruction/alignment data can be characterized theoretically, and whether the 'general instructions suffice' claim holds for highly specialized domains like law or medicine.
