## Problem

A puzzling gap divides theory and practice in text generation decoding: likelihood-maximization strategies like MAP, which should be statistically optimal, produce degenerate text in open-ended generation, while heuristic truncation-normalization methods (Top-k, Nucleus) perform well empirically yet lack rigorous theoretical justification. This paper asks whether there exists a principled framework that explains why these heuristics are (near-)optimal rather than merely ad hoc.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the method consumes the current context (prompt plus previously generated tokens), the autoregressive language model's next-token probability vector over a vocabulary of up to 50,000 tokens, and two hyperparameters: ε (the adversarial TV-distance budget, typically 0.95–0.99) and τ (a temperature parameter controlling the objective function, with τ=1 recovering log-likelihood). It outputs a single next token sampled from a truncated and rescaled distribution, where the truncation threshold is computed dynamically from the shape of the current probability vector rather than fixed a priori. Generation proceeds autoregressively up to 256 tokens per sequence.

## Method

The paper formulates text generation as a two-player zero-sum Decoding Game between a Strategist (choosing a randomized decoding strategy Q) and Nature (adversarially shifting the true distribution P within an ε-ball in total variation distance around the trained model P̂). The key theoretical result is a closed-form solution to the one-step game: Nature's optimal response imposes an ℓ∞-type regularization on the log-likelihood objective, and the Strategist's optimal strategy is a tail truncation-normalization sampling rule whose threshold is determined by an information-theoretic criterion involving the entropy of the retained support. Under first-order approximation, this optimal rule reduces to the familiar truncation-and-rescale design of Nucleus and Top-k sampling, establishing their minimax optimality. The framework generalizes to arbitrary concave objectives f, recovering temperature sampling (f(x)=(x^{1-1/τ}-1)/(1-1/τ)) and showing that log-likelihood is the unique objective for which simple rescaling is optimal. For multi-step generation, the paper proves that solving the one-step game locally at each timestep is worst-case optimal among all no-foresight strategies, avoiding the intractable Ω(d^T) global optimization.

## Results

Game sampling achieves the highest MAUVE score on 4 out of 6 evaluated models (GPT-2 Small, GPT-2 XL, GPT-J-6B, and Llama-2-7B) in open-ended text generation on WebText. On GPT-J-6B, Game sampling (ε=0.99, τ=2) attains MAUVE 0.958 with perplexity 11.333 (human: 12.319) and repetition rate 0.003, outperforming Nucleus sampling (MAUVE 0.955, PPL 14.589). On GPT-2 Small, Game sampling (ε=0.95, τ=2) reaches MAUVE 0.926 with perplexity 23.590 (human: 21.559), edging out Nucleus (0.923). Greedy and Contrastive search collapse to near-zero MAUVE (≤0.065) with extreme repetition rates (≥0.67), confirming the theory's prediction that likelihood maximization fails in open-ended generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Nucleus sampling (Holtzman et al., 2020), Game sampling wins on MAUVE for GPT-J-6B (0.958 vs 0.955), GPT-2 XL (0.833 vs 0.806), GPT-2 Small (0.926 vs 0.923), and Llama-2-7B (0.858 vs 0.853), ties on GPT-2 Large (0.945 vs 0.945), and trails on GPT-2 Medium (0.947 vs 0.954). Against Basis-Aware sampling (Finlayson et al., 2024), Game sampling wins on GPT-J-6B (0.958 vs 0.946), GPT-2 XL (0.833 vs 0.826), GPT-2 Large (0.945 vs 0.933), and GPT-2 Small (0.926 vs 0.917), but loses on GPT-2 Medium (0.947 vs 0.954) and Llama-2-7B (0.858 vs 0.890); notably BA-η incurs higher computational cost due to matrix decomposition. Against Typical sampling (Meister et al., 2023), Game sampling dominates across all six models by 0.02–0.13 MAUVE points. Against Contrastive search (Su et al., 2022) and Greedy decoding, Game sampling wins by an overwhelming margin (MAUVE ≥0.79 vs ≤0.91 for contrastive, and ≥0.83 vs ≤0.065 for greedy), with the latter two suffering severe repetition (repetition rates 0.67–0.89) that renders outputs degenerate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new theoretical ground by framing text generation decoding as a two-player zero-sum game (Decoding Game) between a Strategist and an adversarial Nature, drawing on robust optimization (Ben-Tal et al., 2009) and the known equivalence between regularization and robustness (Shaham et al., 2018; Derman et al., 2021). It departs from prior partial justifications of truncation-based sampling—overestimation remedies (Finlayson et al., 2024), desmoothing (Hewitt et al., 2022), surprisal alignment (Meister et al., 2023), and Zipfian perplexity analysis (Basu et al., 2021)—by providing a single minimax framework that establishes closed-form optimality for both players. The game-theoretic perspective also resonates with concurrent work on text generation as decision-making (Jacob et al., 2024; Snell et al., 2023), but uniquely yields explicit characterizations of why truncation-normalization emerges as near-optimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive framework to rigorously establish that widely-used heuristic decoding strategies (Top-k, Nucleus, temperature scaling) are first-order approximations to minimax-optimal solutions, resolving a long-standing theory-practice gap in text generation. The exclusive-advantage result—that log-likelihood is the unique objective making uniform rescaling optimal—provides a principled reason for the standard practice. Open questions include generalizing beyond TV distance (e.g., KL divergence), closing the gap between the locally optimal mechanism and the globally optimal multi-step strategy (potentially via RL), and whether the framework extends to non-autoregressive or diffusion-based generation. Empirically, gains over Nucleus sampling are modest and inconsistent across model families, suggesting the theoretical contribution is the primary value.
