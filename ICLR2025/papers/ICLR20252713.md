## Problem

Existing EEG foundation models employ a full-attention strategy that models spatial and temporal dependencies among all EEG patches uniformly, ignoring the heterogeneous structure of EEG signals where channel-wise and time-wise dependencies differ in range and strength. Additionally, their reliance on absolute positional encoding tied to electrode numbering limits adaptability across datasets with varying channel configurations and referencing schemes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252713/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-channel EEG signals (C channels × T timestamps) resampled to 200 Hz, segmented into 1-second patches (200 data points each). Pre-training uses 19 channels × 30-second samples (570 patches per sample), while downstream tasks span 6–64 channels and 1–30-second durations. During pre-training the output is reconstructed masked patches; during fine-tuning, flattened patch representations (C × n × d) feed a task-specific MLP head producing class labels or regression values.

## Method

CBraMod uses a criss-cross transformer backbone where half the attention heads perform spatial attention (modeling dependencies across channels within the same time interval) and the other half perform temporal attention (modeling dependencies across time within the same channel), capturing heterogeneous spatial-temporal structure in parallel. Positional information is encoded via an Asymmetric Conditional Positional Encoding (ACPE) scheme: a depthwise 2D convolution with an asymmetric kernel (larger spatial, smaller temporal) dynamically generates position embeddings from local neighborhoods, adapting to arbitrary channel layouts without fixed electrode-number assumptions. Each patch is encoded by a dual-branch encoder combining 1D CNN (time-domain) and FFT + fully-connected layer (frequency-domain). The model is pre-trained via masked EEG reconstruction (50% mask ratio) on the TUEG corpus (~1.1M samples, >9000 hours), then fine-tuned with a task-specific head on downstream BCI tasks.

## Results

CBraMod achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 10 downstream BCI tasks (12 datasets). On emotion recognition, CBraMod obtains 0.5041 Cohen's Kappa on FACED (9-class) and 0.2569 on SEED-V (5-class), outperforming the best baseline LaBraM-Base by 3.4 and 1.8 points respectively. On motor imagery, CBraMod reaches 0.5222 Cohen's Kappa on PhysioNet-MI (4-class) and 0.6988 AUROC on SHU-MI (2-class). On sleep staging (ISRUC), CBraMod achieves 0.7442 Cohen's Kappa, and on seizure detection (CHB-MIT), 0.8892 AUROC, both surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CBraMod consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated task. Against the strongest foundation-model baseline LaBraM-Base, CBraMod gains 3.4 points in Cohen's Kappa on FACED (0.5041 vs. 0.4698), 1.8 points on SEED-V (0.2569 vs. 0.2386), 3.1 points on PhysioNet-MI (0.5222 vs. 0.4912), and 2.1 points on ISRUC sleep staging (0.7442 vs. 0.7231). Against BIOT, CBraMod leads by 3.8 points in AUROC on SHU-MI (0.6988 vs. 0.6609) and 4.6 points in Pearson's correlation on SEED-VIG vigilance estimation (0.6459 vs. 0.5996). Among non-foundation baselines, the strongest performers vary by task (e.g., ContraWR on FACED, CNN-Transformer on PhysioNet-MI), but all are surpassed by CBraMod by 5–15 points in the primary metric. On sleep staging, CBraMod also beats domain-specific models DeepSleepNet (0.7036 Kappa) and USleep (0.7209 Kappa) by 4.1 and 2.3 points respectively. The consistent margin over both foundation-model and task-specific baselines across 10 heterogeneous tasks underscores CBraMod's generalizability advantage from its criss-cross attention and ACPE design.

## Relation to Prior Work

CBraMod extends the masked autoencoder pre-training paradigm (He et al., 2022) to EEG by replacing the full-attention transformer backbone with a criss-cross transformer that models spatial and temporal dependencies in parallel via separate attention heads. Its asymmetric conditional positional encoding (ACPE) is a direct modification of CPE (Chu et al., 2021), introducing an asymmetric convolutional kernel to encode longer-range spatial and shorter-range temporal positional information. The criss-cross attention design draws conceptual inspiration from CCNet (Huang et al., 2019) and CSWin Transformer (Dong et al., 2022) in vision, but departs from them by using dual parallel attention mechanisms rather than a single affinity-based criss-cross map, motivated by the heterogeneous spatial-temporal structure of EEG signals. Compared to prior EEG foundation models (LaBraM, BIOT, EEGPT), CBraMod's key departure is rejecting the full-attention modeling of all patches jointly in favor of structured, axis-separated attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CBraMod provides the first comprehensive benchmark of an EEG foundation model across 10 diverse downstream BCI tasks spanning 12 public datasets, establishing a more rigorous evaluation standard than prior work that typically tested 2–3 tasks. The ACPE mechanism addresses a practical limitation of absolute positional encodings tied to electrode numbering, making the model adaptable to varying channel configurations and referencing schemes without retraining. Open questions include whether the criss-cross architecture scales effectively to larger model sizes (the paper uses only 4M parameters vs. LaBraM-Huge), whether the 19-channel pre-training constraint limits transfer to datasets with fundamentally different spatial topographies, and whether the parallel spatial-temporal decomposition generalizes to other multi-channel physiological signals beyond EEG.
