## Problem

Recent convergence analyses of AdaGrad-type algorithms under (L₀,L₁)-relaxed smoothness yield upper bounds with quadratic (or higher) polynomial dependence on problem parameters Δ, L₀, L₁, but it remained open whether these higher-order dependencies are fundamental or artifacts of the analysis. This paper answers negatively by proving matching complexity lower bounds, showing that adaptive gradient methods cannot recover the optimal ΔL₀σ²ε⁻⁴ rate of SGD-with-clipping in the relaxed smooth setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithms under analysis receive stochastic gradient queries from an objective f: ℝᵈ → R satisfying (L₀,L₁)-smoothness and either bounded or affine gradient noise, and must produce an ε-stationary point (‖∇f(x)‖ < ε). The lower bounds are worst-case over the problem class F_as or F_aff, meaning the constructed hard instances are valid under the stated smoothness and noise assumptions. The output is a lower bound on the number of gradient queries T(A, F, ε) required in expectation (or with high probability for Theorem 4), expressed as a function of Δ, L₀, L₁, σ, and ε.

## Method

The proof strategy splits on the step-size parameter η: for large η, a piecewise exponential objective ψ(x) = (L₀/L₁²)(exp(L₁|x|) − L₁|x| − 1) is constructed so that gradients grow as ~t^t, forcing the algorithm to diverge despite the normalizing denominator; for small η, a high-dimensional objective (adapted from Drori & Shamir, 2020) with a small first-coordinate gradient and noise injected into successive coordinates causes slow convergence because the shared learning rate is dragged down by noise in irrelevant coordinates. For coordinate-wise AdaGrad variants, a novel construction injects noise into one new coordinate per timestep, exploiting the per-coordinate learning rates to maintain constant jump sizes and prevent convergence for arbitrarily many steps. For single-step adaptive SGD under affine noise, a three-step argument shows that either the step size is too large (causing divergence on an exponential), the step size function admits a 'tricky pair' (inducing a biased random walk with constant divergence probability), or the step size must be O(1/(ΔL₁²)) at gradient magnitude ε, yielding the nearly quadratic lower bound.

## Results

The paper establishes that Decorrelated AdaGrad-Norm requires at least Ω(Δ²L₁²σ²ε⁻⁴ + ΔL₀σ²ε⁻⁴ + Δ²L₁²ε⁻²) gradient queries to find an ε-stationary point under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness with bounded noise (Theorem 1). Decorrelated AdaGrad requires Ω(Δ²L₀²σ²γ⁻²ε⁻⁴ + Δ²L₁²σ²γ⁻²ε⁻²) (Theorem 2), and the original AdaGrad requires Ω(Δ²L₀²ε⁻⁴ + Δ²L₁²ε⁻²) (Theorem 3). All three bounds exhibit a quadratic dependence on the initial optimality gap Δ and smoothness constants, in contrast to the optimal linear dependence ΔL₀σ²ε⁻⁴ achieved by SGD with gradient clipping in the same setting. Theorem 4 extends the analysis to single-step adaptive SGD under affine noise, showing a nearly quadratic dependence on ΔL₁.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The lower bound for Decorrelated AdaGrad-Norm (Theorem 1) matches the upper bound of AdaGrad-Norm from Wang et al. (2023) in two out of three dominating terms (Δ²L₁²σ²ε⁻⁴ and ΔL₀σ²ε⁻⁴), with the gap only in the σ⁶/γ²ε⁴ term. This near-tightness confirms that the quadratic dependence on Δ and L₁ is inherent to AdaGrad-type algorithms under relaxed smoothness. In contrast, SGD with gradient clipping (Zhang et al., 2020a) achieves the optimal ΔL₀σ²ε⁻⁴ complexity in the same (L₀,L₁)-smooth, bounded-noise setting, meaning it outperforms all AdaGrad variants considered here by a factor of ΔL₁ or ΔL₀. The lower bound for the original AdaGrad (Theorem 3) is weaker than for its decorrelated counterpart, losing a factor of σ²/γ², because the current-step noise in the denominator makes AdaGrad less sensitive to stochastic perturbations. For single-step adaptive SGD (Theorem 4), the lower bound is nearly quadratic in ΔL₁ (with a small exponent gap depending on σ₂ and δ), showing that even the broadest class of single-step adaptive methods cannot avoid higher-order parameter dependence under affine noise. The paper also notes that the condition ΔL₁² ≥ L₀ is required to ensure the (L₀,L₁)-smooth setting does not degenerate to standard L-smoothness, where the optimal complexity is linear in Δ and L.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lower bound constructions for relaxed smoothness from Zhang et al. (2020b) and Crawshaw et al. (2022), which established Ω(Δ²L₁²ε⁻²) bounds for gradient descent, to the ε⁻⁴ stochastic regime for adaptive gradient methods. It adapts the high-dimensional stochastic construction from Drori & Shamir (2020) to the AdaGrad update structure, and builds on the upper bound analyses of AdaGrad-Norm under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness from Wang et al. (2023) and Attia & Koren (2023) to identify which terms in those upper bounds are tight. The key conceptual departure is showing that the higher-order polynomial dependencies on Δ and L₁ in existing upper bounds are not artifacts of loose analysis but are fundamental limitations of these algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that adaptive methods like AdaGrad fundamentally cannot recover the optimal ΔL₀σ²ε⁻⁴ complexity under relaxed smoothness, while SGD with gradient clipping can—challenges the widespread assumption that adaptive step sizes are universally beneficial. However, the strongest lower bounds apply to decorrelated variants not commonly used in practice, and it remains open whether Adam or AdamW (the dominant practical optimizers) are subject to similar limitations. The paper also leaves a gap between its lower bound and the upper bound of Wang et al. (2023) in the noise term σ, so the exact complexity of AdaGrad under relaxed smoothness is not yet fully characterized.
