## Problem

Existing LLM guardrail models (e.g., LlamaGuard, OpenAI Mod) treat safety categories independently and implicitly encode safety knowledge in model parameters, failing to capture intercorrelations among categories. This leads to poor performance on long-tail correlated categories, vulnerability to jailbreak attacks, and the need for full retraining when new safety categories are introduced.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252300/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

R²-Guard takes a single LLM input or output prompt as text and produces a scalar unsafety probability in [0,1]. Internally, it first queries multiple category-specific learning models (OpenAI Mod, LlamaGuard, ToxicChat-T5, Perspective, Aegis) to obtain a vector of per-category unsafety likelihoods, which are then fed into the reasoning component. The final output is a corrected unsafety probability that can exceed or fall below the raw ensemble maximum, as the logical rules adjust the score based on cross-category implications.

## Method

R²-Guard is a two-stage neuro-symbolic guardrail: a data-driven category-specific learning component computes per-category unsafety probabilities, and a knowledge-enhanced reasoning component performs explicit probabilistic inference over 52 first-order logical rules (35 direct rules linking categories to 'unsafe' and 17 indirect rules encoding inter-category implications). The reasoning component is implemented as either a Markov Logic Network (exact marginal inference, O(2ⁿ) complexity) or a Probabilistic Circuit where spectral clustering partitions logically uncorrelated categories into separate layers, reducing complexity to O(Σ 2^|Cᵢ|) while preserving comparable accuracy. Rule weights are optimized via pseudo-learning (simulated scores, annotation-free) or real-learning (annotated training data), both minimizing binary cross-entropy. The key insight is that explicit logical compilation of safety knowledge into PGMs captures cross-category intercorrelations that purely data-driven models miss, yielding robustness against jailbreaks and flexibility to new categories via simple graph editing.

## Results

R²-Guard (MLN) and R²-Guard (PC) both achieve an average AUPRC of 0.882 across six safety benchmarks, outperforming the next-best baseline LTN (0.835) by 4.7 percentage points. On ToxicChat, R²-Guard (PC) reaches 0.910 AUPRC, surpassing LlamaGuard (0.698) by 30.4%. Under SOTA jailbreak attacks on AdvBench, R²-Guard (MLN) maintains a 0.987 average unsafety detection rate (UDR), compared to 0.848 for LTN and 0.833 for Aegis-Permissive, demonstrating near-perfect robustness even against adaptive GCG attacks targeting its own distilled model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R²-Guard decisively outperforms all ten baselines on average AUPRC (0.882 vs. 0.587–0.835). Against the strongest data-driven guardrails, R²-Guard (PC) beats LlamaGuard by 30.4% on ToxicChat (0.910 vs. 0.698) and by 4.9% on average; it surpasses Aegis-Permissive (0.831) and Ensemble (0.833) by roughly 5 points. The neuro-symbolic LTN baseline (0.835) is the closest competitor but still trails R²-Guard by 4.7 points on average and by 10.8 points on TwinSafety (0.780 vs. 0.682), highlighting the advantage of explicit PGM-based reasoning over arithmetic approximations. On the new TwinSafety benchmark, R²-Guard leads all baselines by at least 4.9 points, confirming its strength on nuanced, pairwise-constructed unsafe content. Under jailbreak attacks (Table 3), R²-Guard (MLN) achieves 0.987 average UDR versus 0.848 for LTN and 0.833 for Aegis-Permissive, with universal GCG suffixes causing zero degradation (UDR remains 1.000) and even the adaptive GCG-R attack reducing UDR only to 0.973.

## Relation to Prior Work

R²-Guard builds on Markov Logic Networks (Richardson & Domingos, 2006) and Probabilistic Circuits (Darwiche, 2002; Kisa et al., 2014) as the reasoning backbone, repurposing them from general probabilistic inference to the specific domain of LLM safety moderation. It departs from purely data-driven guardrails (LlamaGuard, OpenAI Mod, Aegis) that implicitly encode safety knowledge in model parameters, and from neuro-symbolic approaches like Logic Tensor Networks and DeepProbLog that approximate logical operations via arithmetic (multiplication for AND, summation for OR). The key conceptual departure is using exact probabilistic inference over compiled first-order logical rules rather than learned approximations, combined with spectral clustering to partition safety categories into efficient PC layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit logical reasoning over inter-category safety relationships yields substantial gains in both detection accuracy and jailbreak robustness, with the practical advantage that new safety categories can be added by editing the reasoning graph without retraining. However, the approach requires human-authored first-order rules (52 in total), limiting scalability to open-world settings where categories emerge dynamically; the authors acknowledge this and sketch a confidence-based open-world extension but leave it for future work. The TwinSafety benchmark, while useful for stress-testing nuanced unsafety (proverbs, double entendres, context mismatches), is relatively small and the jailbreak robustness evaluation relies on surrogate-model GCG attacks rather than direct white-box optimization against R²-Guard itself, leaving open questions about true adversarial robustness.
