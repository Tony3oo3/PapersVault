## Problem

Existing LLM evaluations for unlearning and alignment rely on deterministic point estimates from greedy decoding, which fail to capture the full output distribution. The paper demonstrates that all state-of-the-art unlearning methods and aligned models appear successful under greedy decoding yet still leak sensitive or toxic information when sampled probabilistically, revealing a critical gap between evaluation and practical deployment risk.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251775/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation framework takes a trained LLM, a set of input prompts, and a per-output leakage function h (e.g., ROUGE-L against a ground truth, or a binary keyword match), then requires Monte Carlo sampling of n=1024 sequences per prompt (top-p=0.9, 64 tokens for unlearning, 128 for alignment) rather than a single greedy output. Outputs are structured as high-probability bounds (at significance α=0.01) on leakage probability, expected leakage, and output variance, plus a lightweight ED score for development-time monitoring. The unlearning component additionally takes a forget set and retain set of question-answer pairs and produces updated model parameters via fine-tuning.

## Method

The paper introduces a probabilistic evaluation framework built on distribution-free concentration bounds: a Clopper-Pearson binary leakage bound, a DKW-based general leakage bound, an Anderson (1969) expectation bound via Riemann-sum approximation of the CDF integral, and a variance bound following Schuchardt et al. (2023). For improving unlearning under sampling, the authors propose an entropy optimization loss L_EO(θ) = L_UL(θ) + λ_f·E_{D_FG}[ℓ_θ(x,y)] + λ_r·E_{D_RT}[ℓ_θ(x,y)] that selectively minimizes output-distribution entropy on forget samples (positive λ_f) while maximizing it on retain samples (negative λ_r), modularly added on top of NPO. An adaptive temperature scaling mechanism further sets the sampling temperature to zero whenever the average token confidence c(x) exceeds a threshold c_T=0.9, collapsing the distribution to greedy output only for high-confidence generations without affecting diversity on unrelated queries.

## Results

On the TOFU dataset (90/10 split), the proposed method (NPO + entropy optimization + adaptive temperature scaling) achieves a deterministic ROUGE-L of 0.20 and, critically, a probabilistic ED Score of 0.20 with zero standard deviation, indicating no residual leakage under sampling. In contrast, NPO alone achieves a similar deterministic ROUGE-L of 0.22 but a substantially higher ED Score of 0.34 with a standard deviation of 0.06, revealing significant hidden leakage. The Harry Potter Q&A experiments show that 38% of questions have an upper bound on expected leakage exceeding 10% under probabilistic evaluation, despite greedy decoding indicating successful unlearning. On JailbreakBench alignment, all three evaluated models (Phi-1.5, Vicuna-7b, Mistral-7b) show significantly higher average toxicity under sampling than under greedy decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four unlearning baselines: RMU (Li et al., ICML 2024), Gradient Ascent (GA) and Gradient Difference (GD) (Liu et al., CoLLA 2022), and NPO (Zhang et al., 2024). Under deterministic ROUGE-L evaluation, NPO (0.22) and the proposed method (0.20) appear nearly equivalent, and GA/GD (0.32–0.33) look moderately better than RMU (0.70). However, the probabilistic ED Score reveals a stark gap: the proposed method achieves 0.20 with zero standard deviation, while NPO still exhibits an ED Score of 0.34 (std dev 0.06), GA 0.41, GD 0.42, and RMU 0.81. This means NPO, despite appearing successful under greedy decoding, still leaks information in a non-negligible fraction of sampled outputs. The proposed entropy optimization + adaptive temperature scaling approach is the only method that eliminates sampling-based leakage entirely (std dev = 0.00) while maintaining comparable deterministic performance and model utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established machine unlearning literature for LLMs (GA, GD, RMU, NPO) but fundamentally reframes the evaluation problem: rather than assessing unlearning quality via a single greedy-decoded output, it treats the model's full output distribution as the object of evaluation. The probabilistic metrics draw on classical concentration inequalities (Clopper-Pearson, DKW) and variance bounds from the randomized-smoothing robustness literature (Schuchardt et al., 2023), repurposing them for LLM output distributions. The entropy optimization loss is modular and composable on top of any existing unlearning method (they use NPO as the base), while adaptive temperature scaling provides a complementary inference-time mechanism. The paper positions itself as the first formal probabilistic evaluation framework for LLMs, departing from the near-universal practice of point-estimate evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that simple multinomial sampling (not adversarial attacks) retrieves unlearned information from all state-of-the-art methods is a practical warning for deployment: current evaluation protocols may certify models as safely unlearned when they are not. The framework is application-independent and directly applicable to alignment, toxicity, and bias evaluation, making it broadly useful. Key limitations include evaluation only at fixed inputs (not adversarial perturbations or paraphrases), reliance on Monte Carlo sampling that scales with sensitivity requirements, and unlearning experiments conducted primarily on the small Phi-1.5 model. Open questions include extending the framework to adversarial alignment settings, quantifying model utility from a probabilistic perspective, and generalizing to non-text modalities.
