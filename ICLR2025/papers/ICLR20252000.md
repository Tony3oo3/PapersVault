## Problem

Adding new conditional controls (e.g., compressibility, aesthetic score) to pre-trained diffusion models typically relies on classifier-free guidance, which demands large offline datasets of triplets {c, x, y} and cannot exploit conditional independence between the new label and the existing prompt. This paper asks whether an RL-based fine-tuning approach can achieve the same conditional generation goal with fewer data requirements and without the approximation errors inherent in classifier guidance methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252000/cropped_page8_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained conditional diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5), a text prompt c, a discrete additional control label y (e.g., one of four compressibility bins or a compositional aesthetic+compressibility label), and an offline dataset of image-label pairs {x, y} (or triplets {c, x, y} when y depends on c). It outputs a fine-tuned augmented diffusion model that, at inference, accepts both c and y to sample images from p(x|c, y). A key practical detail is that when y ⊥ c | x (as with image-quality scores independent of the prompt), only {x, y} pairs are needed, and for multi-task conditioning on y₁ and y₂, separate {x, y₁} and {x, y₂} pairs suffice instead of joint quadruples.

## Method

CTRL formulates conditional generation as an RL problem where the policy is the drift term of an augmented diffusion SDE, the terminal reward is log p̂(y|x_T, c) from a classifier trained on offline data, and the running cost is the squared drift difference (KL penalty) against the pre-trained model. Theorem 1 proves that the soft-optimal policy of this MDP exactly induces the target distribution p_γ(x|c, y) at the terminal time, linking the RL solution to Doob's h-transform and classifier guidance. The augmented model adds a lightweight linear embedding layer for the new condition y (initialized to zero) on top of the pre-trained weights, with only LoRA modules and the new embeddings being fine-tuned. Planning is performed via direct backpropagation through the discretized SDE (Euler-Maruyama) with truncated gradient flow, or PPO when the classifier is non-differentiable. This avoids the need to learn noisy classifiers at every intermediate timestep (as in classifier guidance) and the heuristic reconstruction approximations used in its variants.

## Results

On the single-task compressibility conditioning experiment, CTRL achieves 100% accuracy and 1.0 macro F1, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline SVDD (78% accuracy, 0.78 F1) and MPGD (70% accuracy, 0.72 F1). On the more challenging multi-task experiment (simultaneously conditioning on compressibility and aesthetic score), CTRL reaches 94% accuracy / 0.94 F1 on compressibility and 93% accuracy / 0.93 F1 on aesthetic score, exceeding the best baseline SVDD (82% / 0.82 on CP, 59% / 0.57 on AS) by a wide margin. CTRL also achieves the best image quality (BRISQUE 30.8) and text-image alignment (CLIP Score 26.8) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTRL outperforms all five baselines on both the single-task and multi-task conditional generation experiments. The strongest baseline, SVDD (a training-free decoding method), achieves 78% accuracy on single-task compressibility but drops to 59% on the aesthetic score task in the multi-task setting, while CTRL maintains 93–94% across both tasks. MPGD (manifold-preserving guided diffusion) is competitive on single-task compressibility (70% accuracy) but degrades significantly on the aesthetic score task (48% accuracy) in the multi-task setting. Reconstruction Guidance and Classifier-Free Guidance are consistently the weakest baselines, with accuracies below 66% on both tasks, partly because they rely on heuristic approximations (reconstruction of x_T from x_t) or require triplets {c,x,y} that are harder to construct. SMC (particle filtering) performs worst overall, with accuracies near chance level (49–51%) on the multi-task experiment, suggesting that resampling-based approaches struggle with the joint conditioning distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

CTRL extends the RL-based fine-tuning framework for diffusion models (Black et al., 2023; Uehara et al., 2024) to the specific problem of adding new conditional controls, rather than optimizing a scalar reward. It departs from classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), the dominant approach for conditioning pre-trained diffusion models, by learning a classifier p(y|x,c) as a reward signal and solving an RL problem with KL regularization against the pre-trained model, rather than directly modeling p(x|c,y) via fine-tuning. A key theoretical contribution (Lemma 2) shows the optimal RL drift recovers the Doob's h-transform drift from classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), establishing a formal bridge while avoiding the need for noisy intermediate-state classifiers that plague that approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is in data efficiency: when the additional label y is conditionally independent of the prompt c given x (a common scenario for image quality metrics), CTRL requires only {x,y} pairs rather than the {c,x,y} triplets demanded by classifier-free guidance, and composes naturally for multi-task settings. This significantly lowers the barrier to adding new controls to large pre-trained models when labeled data is scarce. Open questions include the computational cost (≈200 A100 GPU-hours for multi-task fine-tuning), the heuristic selection of guidance strength γ, and whether the approach extends cleanly to continuous-valued or high-dimensional conditioning variables beyond the discrete 4-class settings tested here.
