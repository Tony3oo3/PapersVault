## Problem

While recent work showed that intelligently applied JPEG compression can improve DNN performance, existing solutions are either too complex (multi-branch architectures requiring partial training), limited by frozen DNN weights, or require ground-truth labels at inference. No practical method existed that could fully exploit JPEG's potential to enhance DNN accuracy with minimal architectural overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250597/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard preprocessed RGB image (3×W×H) as input, which is first passed through a trainable JPEG encode-decode layer (RGB→YCbCr→blockwise DCT→soft quantization→IDCT→RGB) before being fed into the underlying DNN to produce a class label. The JPEG layer adds only 128 trainable parameters (quantization step sizes for 64 Y-channel and 64 CbCr-channel DCT frequencies), and at inference the raw image must always pass through the JPEG layer rather than being fed directly to the DNN.

## Method

The core contribution is a differentiable soft quantizer Q_d that replaces JPEG's non-differentiable hard quantization with a conditional expectation over a Gaussian-weighted probability distribution on the reconstruction lattice, parameterized by a step size q and a softness factor α. This makes the entire JPEG pipeline differentiable, enabling a trainable JPEG layer to be prepended to any DNN architecture. The JPEG-DL framework jointly optimizes the quantization tables Q (128 parameters) and DNN weights θ via standard gradient descent, with the soft quantizer simultaneously introducing additional trainable nonlinearity on DCT coefficients. For stable training, the paper employs gradient scaling (fixing α·q²) and uses ADAM or SGD depending on the dataset, with quantization tables initialized from DNN frequency sensitivity estimates or DCT coefficient statistics.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, JPEG-DL consistently improves top-1 accuracy across all seven tested architectures (ResNet32/56/110, VGG8/13, MobileNetV2, ShuffleNetV2), with gains ranging from +0.67% to +1.55% (e.g., VGG13: 73.77% → 75.32%). On fine-grained classification tasks, JPEG-DL delivers substantially larger improvements, reaching up to +20.90% on Oxford 102 Flowers with DenseNet-121 (51.32% → 72.22%) and +11.63% on Flowers with ResNet-18 (57.13% → 68.76%). On ImageNet-1K, JPEG-DL adds only 128 trainable parameters yet achieves +0.23% to +0.38% top-1 accuracy gains over baseline (e.g., ResNet-18: 69.75% → 70.13%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JPEG-DL is compared against three prior differentiable-quantization approaches (Ballé et al. 2016, Shin & Song 2017, Esser et al. 2019) and one learnable preprocessing method (Tu et al. 2023, Learnable Resize) on all four fine-grained datasets. Against Ballé et al. (additive uniform noise), JPEG-DL wins decisively on every dataset, e.g., on CUB-200 with ResNet-18: 58.81% vs. 50.78% (+8.03%), and on Flowers with DenseNet-121: 72.22% vs. 46.60% (+25.62%). Against Shin & Song (polynomial rounding approximation), JPEG-DL outperforms on CUB-200 (58.81% vs. 55.34%) and Flowers (68.76% vs. 55.78%), though the gap is smaller on Dogs and Pets. Against Esser et al. (straight-through estimator), JPEG-DL again leads on all four datasets, with the largest margin on Flowers with DenseNet-121 (72.22% vs. 55.98%). Against Tu et al.'s Learnable Resize, JPEG-DL achieves substantially larger gains, e.g., +10.45% on Flowers (ResNet-18) versus only +0.28% for Learnable Resize, confirming that the trainable JPEG quantization layer introduces a more effective form of nonlinearity than a learnable bandpass resizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Yang et al. (2021)'s 'compression helps' finding that adaptive JPEG can improve DNN accuracy, and on Yang & Hamidi (2024)'s trainable conditional probability mass function that underpins the differentiable soft quantizer. It departs from prior JPEG-for-DNN work (Xie & Kim 2019, Zheng et al. 2023, Salamah et al. 2024b) which optimized quantization tables for a frozen model, and from differentiable JPEG frameworks (Luo et al. 2020, Xie et al. 2022) which targeted rate-distortion tradeoffs rather than raw accuracy gains. The key conceptual departure is treating the JPEG layer as a lightweight trainable nonlinearity (128 parameters) prepended to any DNN, rather than as a compression tool or a complex multi-branch topology as in Yang et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extreme parameter efficiency (128 trainable parameters) and architecture-agnostic design make JPEG-DL a drop-in enhancement for any existing image classification pipeline, with the added benefit of improved adversarial robustness (up to 15% under FGSM). The 20.9% accuracy gain on Flowers with DenseNet-121 suggests the effect is particularly potent for fine-grained tasks where background suppression matters most. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to detection, segmentation, or video tasks, whether other transform-coding schemes (e.g., wavelet-based) yield similar benefits, and whether the inference overhead of the soft quantizer (mitigated by the masked CPMF) becomes prohibitive at scale. The paper also leaves open whether the learned quantization tables encode task-specific frequency priors that could be transferred across models.
