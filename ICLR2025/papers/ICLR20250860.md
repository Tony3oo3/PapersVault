## Problem

Existing entropy-based exploration objectives for offline RL dataset generation—Shannon entropy and Rényi entropy—offer limited diversity of state space coverage: Shannon provides only a single notion of optimal coverage, while Rényi's parametric family yields partial coverage with inherent discontinuity and instability in its parameter space. The paper asks whether a more general, smooth family of entropy objectives can systematically produce datasets with a wider and more controllable range of coverage, leading to superior downstream offline RL performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw state observations from MuJoCo Walker and Quadruped environments, projects them into a 512-dimensional learned representation, and computes k-th nearest-neighbor distances (k≤15) within a replay buffer to form the BE reward signal. The behavioral entropy parameters α and β (via Prelec's weighting) are the only tunable knobs controlling coverage granularity. The output is a 500K-element trajectory dataset per (environment, parameter) combination, which is then relabeled with task-specific rewards and used to train offline RL policies (TD3, CQL, CRR) for five downstream locomotion tasks.

## Method

The paper extends behavioral entropy—originally defined for discrete distributions as Shannon entropy composed with Prelec's probability weighting function—to continuous spaces, yielding a differential BE functional parameterized by α and β. It then derives k-nearest-neighbor plug-in estimators for this continuous BE with importance-sampling correction, proving uniform convergence (Theorem 1) and establishing explicit probabilistic bounds on bias and variance for finite k (Theorem 2). From the k-NN estimator, the authors derive a closed-form reward function r(s,a) = ‖s − NN_k(s)‖₂ · exp(−β(log(‖s − NN_k(s)‖₂ + c))^α) · (log(‖s − NN_k(s)‖₂ + c))^α that approximates the BE of the agent's state occupancy measure. This reward is plugged into the Active Pre-Training (APT) algorithm to learn BE-maximizing exploration policies in an unsupervised, reward-free setting. The resulting policies generate datasets whose coverage varies smoothly with α, and offline RL training on these datasets outperforms Shannon, Rényi, RND, and SMM baselines on 15/15 task-algorithm combinations against the latter three and 13/15 against Rényi.

## Results

On MuJoCo Walker and Quadruped environments, BE-generated datasets achieve the best offline RL performance on 4 out of 5 downstream tasks (Walker Stand: 990.38, Walker Walk: 904.66, Quadruped Walk: 845.31, Quadruped Run: 522.32), losing only on Walker Run (385.07 vs. RE's 440.53). BE outperforms Shannon entropy, RND, and SMM on all five tasks, and outperforms Rényi entropy on 80% of tasks. Notably, these results are achieved with only 500K dataset elements (5% of the ExORL benchmark's 10M) and 100K offline training steps (20% of the 500K in ExORL), indicating substantial data- and sample-efficiency gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Rényi entropy (Zhang et al., AAAI 2021), BE wins on 4 of 5 tasks with margins of 1.45 (Walker Stand), 26.46 (Walker Walk), 68.67 (Quadruped Walk), and 31.57 (Quadruped Run), but loses on Walker Run by 55.46 points. Against Shannon entropy (Liu & Abbeel, NeurIPS 2021), BE wins all 5 tasks with margins ranging from 24.43 (Walker Run) to 89.52 (Quadruped Walk). Against RND (Burda et al., ICLR 2019), BE wins all 5 tasks with margins from 44.04 (Quadruped Run) to 158.89 (Walker Walk). Against SMM (Lee et al., 2019), BE wins all 5 tasks by large margins (109.91 to 755.08 points), with SMM consistently the weakest baseline. The paper also notes that RE is unstable as a function of its parameter q (poor coverage for q > 1), while BE varies smoothly in α, and that a 3M-element SE dataset only partially closes the gap to BE on some tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete behavioral entropy (BE) framework of Suresh et al. (2024)—which composes Shannon entropy with Prelec's probability weighting function from behavioral economics—to continuous, high-dimensional spaces, and repurposes it from robotic exploration to offline RL dataset generation. The k-NN estimation machinery builds on the classical nonparametric density estimation literature (Kozachenko & Leonenko, 1987; Singh et al., 2003; Singh & Póczos, 2016), while the RL-based reward derivation follows the template established by Liu & Abbeel (2021) for Shannon entropy and Zhang et al. (2021) for Rényi entropy. Conceptually, the paper positions BE as the most general admissible entropy family to date, subsuming both Shannon and Rényi entropies as special cases, and departs from prior work by showing that this generality translates into a wider, smoother range of state space coverages than either SE (single objective) or RE (unstable, partial coverage).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that a single, smooth parameter (α) can dial exploration from coarse/widespread to dense/focused coverage without the instability that plagues Rényi entropy's q parameter, making BE a more user-friendly and reliable exploration objective for dataset generation. The demonstrated data-efficiency gain—achieving comparable offline RL performance with 500K elements and 100K training steps versus the 10M/500K in ExORL—suggests BE-generated data is more informative per sample. Key limitations include evaluation on only two MuJoCo environments and three offline RL algorithms, and the computational burden of k-NN in high dimensions restricts practical use to reduced feature spaces. Open questions include whether BE's advantages persist in truly high-dimensional or non-MuJoCo domains, whether alternative probability weighting functions beyond Prelec's could yield further gains, and whether the observed correlation between volumetric coverage and downstream performance is causal.
