## Problem

Existing label-noise correction methods assume a static, time-invariant noise transition matrix, which is misspecified when label noise rates vary over time (e.g., seasonal annotator fatigue, decaying measurement error, or growing clinical uncertainty). This paper formalizes temporal label noise for sequence-to-sequence classification and shows that static methods consistently underperform when the noise process is genuinely time-varying.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251527/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series x_{1:T} ∈ R^{d×T} paired with a noisy label sequence ỹ_{1:T} ∈ {1,…,C}^T, and produces (i) a sequence of predicted class labels via argmax of the GRU's per-step posterior h_θ(x_{1:t}), and (ii) an estimated temporal noise function Q̂(t) ∈ [0,1]^{C×C} at each time step. The noise function is parameterized by a 10-layer fully connected network whose output is reshaped to C×C, passed through row-wise softmax, added to the identity for diagonal dominance, and rescaled to be row-stochastic — all differentiable operations enabling end-to-end optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a forward temporal loss ℓ_seq(ỹ_{1:T}, x_{1:T}, h_θ) = Σ_t ℓ_t(ỹ_t, Q_t^T h_θ(x_{1:t})), for which the paper proves (Proposition 1) that minimizing this loss over noisy labels is equivalent to maximizing the clean-label likelihood, generalizing Patrini et al.'s static loss correction to time-varying noise matrices. To learn the unknown Q(t) from data, the paper proposes three estimation strategies: Continuous (jointly optimizes classifier θ and noise parameters ω via an augmented Lagrangian that minimizes the Frobenius norm of Q_t as a convex surrogate for the minimum-volume simplex constraint), Discontinuous (fits independent Q_t weights per time step without continuity assumptions), and Plug-In (estimates Q(t) from anchor points identified per class and time step, then plugs the estimate into the forward loss). The classifier backbone is a single-layer GRU with 32-dimensional hidden state and softmax output, trained for 150 epochs with Adam (lr=0.01) under identical hyperparameters across all methods to avoid label leakage.

## Results

Under 30% Mixed temporal label noise, the proposed Continuous method achieves the lowest test error across all four datasets: 4.2% on moving (vs. 29.4% for Ignore), 11.0% on senior (vs. 22.7%), 29.6% on blinking (vs. 34.1%), and 16.3% on sleeping (vs. 28.7%). Continuous also achieves the lowest approximation error (noise function reconstruction) on every dataset, e.g., 10.3% on moving vs. 35.3% for VolMinNet and 42.4% for Anchor. Under Periodic noise (Table 3), Continuous again leads with 1.7% test error on moving and 15.2% on sleeping, while static baselines degrade substantially (e.g., Anchor reaches 18.0% on moving).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two static label-noise baselines: Anchor (Patrini et al., CVPR 2017), which estimates a single noise transition matrix via anchor points, and VolMinNet (Li et al., ICML 2021), which estimates the noise matrix via minimum-volume simplex without anchors. Under Mixed temporal noise, Continuous outperforms Anchor by 16.2 percentage points on moving (4.2% vs. 20.9% test error) and by 13.7 points on sleeping (16.3% vs. 24.9%). Against VolMinNet, the gap is 10.7 points on moving (4.2% vs. 14.9%) and 10.5 points on sleeping (16.3% vs. 26.8%). On blinking, where the task is inherently harder, Continuous matches VolMinNet (29.6% vs. 29.6%) but still achieves the lowest approximation error (14.9% vs. 35.2%). Under Periodic noise, the advantage widens further: Continuous reaches 1.7% test error on moving versus 18.0% (Anchor) and 13.5% (VolMinNet). The key finding is that static methods systematically underperform when noise varies over time, while the temporal methods (especially Continuous) consistently dominate across all datasets and noise regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the static label noise correction framework of Patrini et al. (CVPR 2017) to temporal settings by replacing the fixed noise transition matrix with a time-dependent function Q(t), yielding the forward temporal loss. It also generalizes the minimum-volume simplex estimation of Li et al. (ICML 2021) from a single matrix to a sequence of matrices, solved via an augmented Lagrangian. The key departure from prior work is the formalization of temporal label noise as a distinct problem: existing methods assume a time-invariant noise model, which the paper shows is a misspecification that causes consistent underperformance when noise rates vary over time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal label noise is prevalent in real-world healthcare time series (activity recognition, self-reported mental health, clinical measurements), yet prior methods implicitly assume static noise and fail when that assumption is violated. The paper's 'no downside' property—temporal methods remain competitive even when noise is truly static—makes the approach a safe default for any time series classification task. Open questions include extending to non-stationary predictive distributions, accommodating heterogeneous annotators with different noise patterns, and curating more real-world datasets with paired clean and noisy labels to validate learned noise functions.
