## Problem

The theoretical understanding of conditional Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) remains limited: prior DiT analyses (Hu et al., 2024) yield non-tight rates restricted to unconditional diffusion, while existing conditional diffusion theory targets ReLU networks or model-free settings without addressing transformer architectures. This paper fills the gap by deriving tight score approximation, score estimation, and distribution recovery rates for conditional DiTs with classifier-free guidance, and by establishing the first exact minimax optimality guarantee for unconditional DiTs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250455/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The conditional DiT consumes a noisy data vector x_t ∈ R^{d_x}, a condition label y ∈ R^{d_y}, and a diffusion time step t. The data vector is reshaped into a d×L token matrix (for an i×i image, d=p² and L=(i/p)²), while embeddings of y and t are concatenated with the token sequence before transformer processing. The network outputs a score estimate ∇log p_t(x|y) ∈ R^{d_x} that drives the reverse diffusion process to recover the initial conditional distribution P_0(·|y).

## Method

The core technique is a fine-grained score decomposition: the conditional density p_t(x|y) is written as an integral of the initial density p_0(x_0|y) times a Gaussian kernel, and both factors undergo term-by-term Taylor expansion on an infinitesimal grid discretization of the input domain. Under generic Hölder smoothness, this yields a piecewise constant score approximator that a single-layer, single-head self-attention transformer matches with error O((log(1/ε))^{d_x}/σ_t^4); under a stronger Hölder assumption (density bounded below by a positive constant times a Gaussian), the score splits into a linear term plus a well-behaved remainder, tightening the rate to Õ(ε²/σ_t²). Estimation bounds combine this approximation error with a norm-based covering number of the transformer class, and distribution estimation follows a three-part total variation decomposition. By matching the resulting upper bound with the known minimax lower bound for Hölder smooth distributions, the paper proves exact minimax optimality for unconditional DiTs under specific parameter conditions.

## Results

Under the Strong Hölder smooth data assumption, the conditional DiT achieves a score approximation rate of Õ(ε²/σ_t²) and a score/distribution estimation rate of n^{-Θ(1/d)}·(log n)^{O(d_x)}, with minimax optimality in total variation distance established for the unconditional case. Under the Generic Hölder assumption, the score approximation rate degrades to O((log(1/ε))^{d_x}/σ_t⁴). In the latent setting, all rates improve by replacing the ambient dimension d_x with the intrinsic latent dimension d₀, and minimax optimality is also achieved for latent unconditional DiTs under the Strong Hölder assumption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper, so comparisons are between analytical bounds rather than empirical metrics. Compared to Hu et al. (2024, NeurIPS), which provided the first DiT statistical rates via the Yun et al. (2020) universality result, this paper eliminates the double-exponential sample complexity term 2^{(1/ε)^{2L}} and obtains polynomially tighter bounds by leveraging a refined single-layer self-attention approximation (Kajitsuka & Sato, 2024) combined with term-by-term Taylor expansion of the score function. Compared to Fu et al. (2024a), which established score approximation rates for conditional diffusion using ReLU networks under Hölder assumptions, this paper is the first to provide such rates for transformer (DiT) architectures. Compared to Oko et al. (2023), who achieved near-minimax optimal distribution estimation for ReLU-based diffusion models under a compact support assumption, this paper replaces compactness with a Hölder smoothness condition and establishes exact minimax optimality specifically for DiTs. Compared to Fu et al. (2024b), who proved minimax optimality for conditional diffusion with classifier-free guidance using ReLU networks, this paper extends the minimax optimality guarantee to the transformer (DiT) architecture, which is the first such result for DiTs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the first statistical rate analysis of DiTs by Hu et al. (2024) from unconditional to conditional settings with classifier-free guidance, while replacing the suboptimal double-exponential sample complexity bound with a much tighter one. The key technical departure is a term-by-term Taylor expansion of the conditional score function on infinitesimal grid cells, building on the Hölder smoothness framework of Fu et al. (2024b) and the single-layer transformer universality of Kajitsuka and Sato (2024), to achieve a finer piecewise-constant approximation. Prior theoretical work on conditional diffusion (Fu et al. 2024a, Yuan et al. 2024) focused exclusively on ReLU networks, leaving the transformer architecture unaddressed; this paper fills that gap for the specific in-context conditioning DiT architecture of Peebles and Xie (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Establishing minimax optimality of unconditional DiTs under strong Hölder smoothness is the first such guarantee for transformer-based diffusion models, providing a clean statistical benchmark against which future DiT variants can be measured. The results also yield practical guidance on network size and configuration needed for a target score approximation accuracy. Key open questions remain: minimax optimality for conditional DiTs is not yet established, the analysis is limited to the in-context conditioning architecture (not cross-attention or adaptive layer norm variants), and the Hölder smoothness assumptions, while more practical than compact support, are still difficult to verify on real datasets.
