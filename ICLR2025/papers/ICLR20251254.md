## Problem

Existing pretraining data detection methods for LLMs (Min-K%, Min-K%++) rely exclusively on causal token likelihoods, which only condition on preceding context and thus ignore information from the full sentence. This limits detection accuracy, particularly for longer sequences where future tokens carry substantial signal. The paper asks whether non-causal (infilling) likelihoods can improve membership inference, and how to compute them efficiently given that a naive Bayes-rule derivation scales linearly with vocabulary size (up to 128K tokens).

## Input / Output

The method requires grey-box access to a pretrained autoregressive LLM, consuming its log-probability logits for a given text sequence. For each token, two forward passes are needed: one with the original token in context and one with the model's argmax causal prediction substituted in place. The number of future tokens (m) used in the infilling computation is a tunable hyperparameter (typically 1 for 32-token sequences, 5 for longer ones), and increasing m does not require additional LLM calls. The final output is a binary seen/unseen label determined by thresholding the aggregated min-k% Infilling Score.

## Method

The Infilling Score computes, for each token x_i, the ratio of its non-causal infilling probability p(x_i | x_{<i}, x_{>i}) to that of the model's causal argmax prediction x_i*, derived via Bayes' rule and the law of total probability. The critical insight is that this ratio cancels the vocabulary-dependent partition function in the denominator, reducing the number of LLM calls from O(N|V|) to O(N) and making the computation invariant to vocabulary size. The log-ratio is then z-score normalized using the mean and standard deviation of next-token log-probabilities over the vocabulary (following Min-K%++), and the per-token scores are aggregated by averaging over the bottom k% tokens (following Min-K%). This yields a reference-free test statistic that leverages both past and future context, achieving up to 10% AUROC improvement over Min-K%++ on WikiMIA for 256-token sequences.

## Results

On the WikiMIA benchmark, Infilling Score achieves an average AUROC of 76.56 (32 tokens), 75.78 (64), 76.23 (128), and 81.84 (256) across seven models, representing a ~5% average improvement over Min-K%++ and ~11% over Min-K%. The gain is most pronounced on 256-token sequences, where Infilling Score reaches 96.6% AUROC on Llama-7B versus 82.5% for Min-K%++ (a 14-point gap). On the more challenging MIMIR benchmark (high n-gram overlap), Infilling Score achieves the best average AUROC of 54.9% (Pythia-1.4B) among all reference-free and reference-based methods. TPR@5%FPR on 256-token WikiMIA sequences reaches 48.17% average, nearly double the 22.70% of Min-K%++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Infilling Score consistently outperforms all four baselines across every sequence length on WikiMIA. Against Min-K%++ (the strongest reference-free baseline), the average AUROC gap ranges from +1.9 points (32 tokens) to +9.1 points (256 tokens), with the largest per-model gains on Llama-7B at 256 tokens (96.6 vs. 82.5). Against Min-K%, the improvement is even larger, reaching +14 points on 256-token Llama-7B. On the MIMIR benchmark, where seen/unseen samples share up to 0.8 13-gram overlap, Infilling Score achieves the best average AUROC (54.9% for Pythia-1.4B) among all methods, edging out Min-K%++ (54.1%), Min-K% (53.6%), Zlib (53.2%), and Ref (54.6%). The Neighbor method is not evaluated on MIMIR. Notably, reference-based methods (Ref, Zlib) remain competitive on MIMIR but require access to a smaller reference model trained on the same distribution, which is often unavailable in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Min-K% / Min-K%++ line of work on reference-free pretraining data detection by replacing the causal token likelihood p(x_i | x_{<i}) with a non-causal infilling likelihood p(x_i | x_{<i}, x_{>i}) that conditions on both past and future tokens. The min-k% aggregation framework and z-score normalization are directly inherited from Shi et al. (2024) and Zhang et al. (2024), respectively; the novelty lies in the test statistic itself and in a ratio formulation that cancels the vocabulary-sized partition function, reducing computation from O(N|V|) to O(N) LLM calls. The broader MIA literature (Carlini et al. 2021 reference-based methods, Mattern et al. 2023 neighbor methods) provides the experimental baselines but is conceptually distinct from this likelihood-ratio approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, reference-free tool for detecting pretraining data contamination in LLMs, demonstrated concretely by identifying specific books (e.g., Persuasion at 99% contamination) in Llama-3-8B's training corpus — a finding with direct legal and ethical implications. Key limitations include the requirement for grey-box (logit) access and a 2× computational overhead relative to Min-K%++, which may matter at scale. Open questions include whether the non-causal advantage persists for black-box models, whether the contamination findings generalize beyond the curated book dataset, and whether the approach can be adapted to detect fine-tuning data or more subtle distributional shifts.
