## Problem

Data center cooling systems consume 30–40% of total DC energy, yet existing control methods (local PID, MPC) lack the expressiveness to capture complex, load-dependent thermal dynamics across multiple air-cooling units. Online RL approaches require high-fidelity simulators that are impractical to build for real DCs, while standard offline RL methods demand large, well-covered datasets that real-world DC operational logs do not provide. This paper addresses the gap of learning safe, energy-efficient cooling policies from limited, narrow-coverage real-world data without any simulator.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an ~80-dimensional state vector sampled every 2–5 minutes, comprising cold/hot aisle temperature and humidity readings, server rack temperatures, ACU working states (LWT, LAT, EAT), and uncontrollable external factors (EWT, server power). It produces a 22-dimensional action vector specifying fan speed and valve opening percentages for each of 11 ACUs in a server room. The state is structured as a bipartite graph (sensor nodes and ACU nodes) with edges encoding spatial proximity and control dependency, and the policy operates in closed-loop with temporal smoothing over 5 consecutive time steps to ensure smooth actuator commands.

## Method

The core contribution is a T-symmetry enforced Thermal Dynamics Model (TTDM) built on a graph neural network encoder that captures spatial and control dependencies among sensors and ACUs via GCN blocks. The encoder produces latent representations (z_s, z_a) into which a pair of latent ODE forward and reverse dynamics (f and g) are embedded, with the T-symmetry constraint f(z_s, z_a) = −g(z_s′, z_a) enforced through a dedicated consistency loss alongside reconstruction and ODE-compatibility losses. Policy learning then operates entirely in this latent space: a Q-function Q(z_s, z_a) is trained via standard Bellman updates, and the policy is optimized with a TD3+BC-style objective augmented by a T-symmetry regularization term applied to policy-induced samples. This physics-based regularization replaces purely data-distribution constraints, enabling strong out-of-distribution generalization from the limited (~140k–180k samples) and narrow-coverage real-world DC logs, ultimately achieving 14–21% energy savings in a production DC over 2000 hours of closed-loop operation.

## Results

In a large-scale commercial data center, the proposed physics-informed offline RL framework achieves 14–21% reduction in Air-side Cooling Load Factor (ACLF) compared to the default PID controllers across two server rooms (Room A: 4.42% → 3.40–3.68%; Room B: 6.03% → 4.85–5.19%), with no thermal safety violations over 2000+ hours of closed-loop operation. On the real-world testbed (22 servers, 1 ACU), the method achieves the lowest ACLF under all three load conditions (low/medium/high) while maintaining zero cold-aisle-temperature (CAT) violations, outperforming all six baseline methods. Long-term 14-day experiments confirm consistent ACLF advantages across all server load levels, with the gap widening at higher loads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines: default PID, Google's data-driven MPC (Lazic et al., 2018), off-policy RL method CCA (Li et al., 2019), offline RL methods IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022) and CQL (Kumar et al., 2020), and SOTA safe offline RL method FISOR (Zheng et al., 2024). In the production DC, the proposed method outperforms PID by 14–21% in ACLF while maintaining zero CAT violations. On the testbed, aggressive baselines CCA and CQL achieve marginally lower energy consumption but incur unacceptable CAT violations (safety failures), whereas the proposed method achieves the best ACLF under all three load conditions with zero violations. IQL and FISOR, which prioritize safety, produce higher ACLF (worse energy efficiency) than the proposed method. MPC, while safe, also yields higher ACLF than the proposed approach. Overall, the proposed method is the only method that simultaneously achieves the lowest energy consumption and zero safety violations across all tested conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the time-reversal symmetry (T-symmetry) regularized offline RL framework of Cheng et al. (TSRL, NeurIPS 2023) to a physics-informed industrial control setting by embedding T-symmetry consistency into a graph-structured thermal dynamics model (TTDM) with latent ODE forward/reverse dynamics. The policy optimization follows the TD3+BC paradigm (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021) but operates in the learned latent space rather than the raw data space, and adds a T-symmetry consistency regularizer on policy-induced samples. The key departure from prior DC cooling work (PID, MPC, simulation-based online RL) is the combination of domain-knowledge GNN architecture, physics-compliant dynamics modeling, and offline learning to handle the data-limited, safety-critical real-world setting where high-fidelity simulators are unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14–21% energy savings validated over 2000+ hours in a production data center demonstrates that offline RL with physics-informed modeling can be practically deployable in safety-critical industrial control, a setting where prior simulation-based RL methods have largely failed. However, the paper acknowledges important limitations: safety constraints are handled only as soft penalty terms in the reward (not hard guarantees), experiments are confined to a single DC facility, and the method does not jointly optimize air-side and water-side cooling. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes across DC configurations with different ACU topologies, whether dedicated safe-offline-RL formulations (e.g., HJ-reachability-based) can provide stronger safety guarantees, and whether the T-symmetry + GNN modeling strategy transfers to other data-limited industrial control domains beyond thermal systems.
