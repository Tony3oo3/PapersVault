## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods such as LoRA and AdaLoRA scale trainable parameters linearly with the ambient matrix dimension, even at rank-1. This linear bottleneck limits compression ratios for very large models (e.g., GPT-4, Llama 3.1 405B), where even rank-1 LoRA requires tens of gigabytes of storage. No prior PEFT method achieves sub-linear, specifically logarithmic, scaling of trainable parameters while maintaining competitive downstream accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on frozen pretrained transformer weights (DeBERTaV3, GPT-2 Medium, Mistral-7B, ViT-base) and produces additive weight updates ΔW applied to query/key/value projection matrices and MLP layers. For NLP tasks, inputs are text sequences (up to 320 tokens for RTE/MRPC) producing classification scores or generated text; for vision, 32×32 CIFAR-10 images are upsampled to 224×224 and produce 10-class labels. The trainable module itself consumes a feature vector x ∈ R^{N'} and outputs a transformed vector via the quantum unitary mapping, with the base model optionally quantized to 3-bit integers to further reduce memory.

## Method

Quantum-PEFT reparameterizes the weight update ΔW = UΛV^T by expressing U and V not as free trainable matrices but as products of small quantum gates (RY rotations and CZ entanglements) assembled via Kronecker products, yielding a Pauli-parameterized unitary Q_P with only O((2L+1)log₂(N) − 2L) trainable angles per orthogonal node. The orthogonal nodes are generated from skew-symmetric Lie algebra parameters mapped to the Stiefel manifold via Taylor series expansion (Q_T'), ensuring left-orthogonality by construction rather than through AdaLoRA-style regularization. A diagonal node (generalized CZ module) handles singular values, and quantum Shannon decomposition (recursive cosine-sine decomposition) extends the power-of-two Kronecker structure to arbitrary matrix dimensions. The intrinsic rank K' further reduces parameters by masking columns of the Lie parameter matrix, and quantization-aware training with the straight-through estimator compresses the already-logarithmic parameter count to as few as 1.25 bits per parameter.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with DeBERTaV3-base, Quantum-PEFT achieves an average score of 86.42 using only 0.013M trainable parameters — 25× fewer than LoRA (0.33M, avg 86.12) — while outperforming LoRA on SST-2 (95.85 vs 94.95) and MRPC (90.78 vs 89.71). On Mistral-7B GLUE, Quantum-PEFT reaches 87.39 average with 0.758M parameters, surpassing both LoRA (86.19) and AdaLoRA (87.06) with 4.67× fewer parameters. On ViT CIFAR-10 transfer learning, Quantum-PEFT attains 98.46% accuracy with just 0.007M parameters, exceeding LoRA K=4 (98.39% at 0.147M) by 21× in parameter reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Quantum-PEFT (0.013M params) outperforms LoRA (0.33M) by +0.30 average on GLUE with 25× fewer parameters, winning on SST-2 (+0.90) and MRPC (+1.07) but trailing on CoLA (−0.86) and STS-B (−0.62). Against AdaLoRA (0.32M, avg 87.05), Quantum-PEFT trails by −0.63 on average, losing primarily on CoLA (−2.19) and RTE (−0.79), yet uses 25× fewer parameters. On the E2E benchmark, Quantum-PEFT (0.098M) beats LoRA (0.39M) by +0.58 BLEU with 4× fewer parameters, and decisively outperforms LoKr (same 0.098M params) by +3.56 BLEU. On Mistral-7B, Quantum-PEFT (0.758M) surpasses both LoRA (3.54M, avg 86.19) and AdaLoRA (3.54M, avg 87.06) with 4.67× fewer parameters, achieving 87.39 average. On ViT CIFAR-10, Quantum-PEFT (0.007M) achieves 98.46% accuracy, exceeding LoRA K=4 (0.147M, 98.39%) and full fine-tuning (85.81M, 98.05%) while using 21× fewer parameters than LoRA K=4. Compared to adapter-based methods (HAdapter, PAdapter) and LoHa, Quantum-PEFT consistently wins on average by 0.45–2.05 points with substantially fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

Quantum-PEFT extends the PEFT lineage from LoRA (low-rank matrix products) and AdaLoRA (approximate SVD with orthogonality regularization) by reparameterizing the orthogonal factors U, V through quantum circuit structures—specifically Kronecker products of Pauli rotation gates (RY) and entangling gates (CZ). This departs from the low-rank assumption entirely: the resulting unitary is full-rank yet requires only O(log N) trainable parameters via the two-design ansatz, in contrast to the O(NK) linear scaling of LoRA. The paper also unifies the tensor network perspective, showing that LoRA corresponds to a redundant 2-mode tensor train decomposition while Quantum-PEFT realizes the canonical Tucker form with Lie-algebra-parameterized unitary nodes, and employs quantum Shannon decomposition to handle arbitrary (non-power-of-two) matrix dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The logarithmic parameter scaling means Quantum-PEFT's advantage over LoRA grows with model dimension, making it particularly relevant for the trend toward trillion-parameter models where even rank-1 LoRA requires gigabytes of storage. Open questions include whether the extremely compact parameter budget (e.g., 13K parameters for DeBERTaV3) remains sufficient for more complex or out-of-distribution tasks, and whether the quantum-inspired parameterization can capture all necessary weight-update geometries at scale. The framework's generality—applicable to arbitrary tensor networks and combinable with quantization-aware training—suggests a broader compression toolkit, but validation on larger and more diverse benchmarks (beyond GLUE, E2E, CIFAR-10) is still needed.
