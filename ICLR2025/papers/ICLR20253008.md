## Problem

Despite substantial advances in LLMs across AI domains, their capabilities in Reasoning About Actions and Change (RAC) remain underexplored, and existing benchmarks (PlanBench, TRAC) cover only a narrow subset of RAC dimensions while entirely omitting ramification constraints—the indirect effects of actions that arise from inter-fluent dependencies. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a diagnostic benchmark that systematically decomposes RAC into six distinct reasoning dimensions and, for the first time, incorporates ramification constraints with dependencies up to four levels deep.

## Input / Output

Each question instance consists of a natural-language domain description (translated from PDDL and augmented with ramification constraints), an initial state specification, a sequence of 1–19 actions, and a targeted RAC question drawn from one of six categories (Fluent Tracking, State Tracking, Action Executability, Effects of Actions, Numerical RAC, Composite Questions). The expected output is either a binary True/False judgment or a free-form enumeration of fluents, actions, or properties; the test set contains 3,498 questions (2,195 binary, 1,303 free-answer) across 8 IPC domains, with a separate train set of 149,237 questions used for fine-tuning.

## Method

The benchmark is generated via a four-stage pipeline: (1) PDDL instances and plans are produced with the Fast Downward solver and validated by VAL; (2) PDDL domains, instances, and plans are converted into Answer Set Programming (ASP) descriptions; (3) ASP solvers compute the full action-state space, extracting all fluents per state and identifying executable/inexecutable actions; (4) Python templates generate questions, which are then paraphrased by Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct for naturalness. The key novelty is the introduction of ramification constraints—derived fluents (e.g., 'stable' depends on 'clear' and 'on_table') and self-derived fluents (e.g., a truck can only be at one location at a time)—that propagate action effects through multiple dependency layers. Evaluation uses zero-shot-CoT and few-shot-3 prompting across GPT-4o, o1-preview, Llama-3.1-8B/70B-Instruct, plus supervised fine-tuning of Llama-3.1-8B on the train split; free-form answers are scored by Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct as a semantic-equivalence judge, validated against human annotation.

## Results

On ACTIONREASONINGBENCH (free-answer, zero-shot-CoT, without ramifications), GPT-4o achieves the highest average accuracy at 60.28% (seq length 1), 57.01% (seq 10), and 56.78% (seq 19), while Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct scores 45.96%, 45.98%, and 39.17%, and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct scores 21.71%, 17.01%, and 11.72%. Performance drops sharply on complex categories: Numerical RAC averages only ~10% across all models, and Composite Questions range from 8.54% (Llama-8B, seq 19) to 64.53% (GPT-4o, seq 1). On the ramification subset, GPT-4o scores 0% on all free-answer questions, while o1-preview achieves only 18.4% overall, highlighting a critical gap in handling indirect effects of actions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o outperforms Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct by an average of 8.64% on free-answer questions and 4.5% on binary questions, confirming the advantage of the larger proprietary model. Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct consistently beats Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct by 20.84% on average, demonstrating the benefit of scale. Notably, fine-tuning Llama-3.1-8B on the training split yields a 33.68% average improvement, allowing it to surpass GPT-4o by 4.2% on the test set, suggesting that domain-specific training can close the gap with larger models. On the ramification subset, GPT-4o scores 0% on all free-answer questions (failing to produce any correct ramification fluent), while o1-preview—despite being optimized for reasoning—achieves only 18.4% overall, with its strongest performance (33.33%) on Fluent Tracking at sequence length 19. All models show a consistent 12.16% accuracy drop when questions involve negative fluents versus positive ones, and performance degrades as action-sequence length increases from 1 to 19 steps across nearly all categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM evaluation on Reasoning About Actions and Change (RAC) initiated by Banerjee et al. (2020), He et al. (2023), and Valmeekam et al. (2024), which assessed LLMs on limited RAC sub-tasks (primarily State Tracking and Action Executability) using 1–2 domains. It departs from prior work by (1) decomposing RAC into six distinct question categories including novel ones (Numerical RAC, Composite Questions), (2) introducing ramification constraints that capture indirect effects of actions—a dimension absent from all prior RAC benchmarks—and (3) scaling to 8 IPC domains with action sequences up to 19 steps. The ground-truth generation pipeline (PDDL → ASP → templated questions) builds on standard planning infrastructure but is novel in its systematic integration of ramification fluents with multi-level dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first systematic diagnostic of where LLMs fail on RAC, revealing that even GPT-4o scores 0% on ramification questions and that performance drops 17.9% on complex categories (Numerical RAC, Composite Questions). The finding that a fine-tuned 8B model outperforms GPT-4o by 4.2% suggests domain-specific training may be more effective than scaling for RAC. Key limitations include the small sample sizes for ramification evaluation, reliance on Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct for free-answer scoring, English-only formulation, and potential IPC domain bias. Open questions include whether the ramification failure reflects a fundamental reasoning gap or a memorization artifact from pre-training on IPC data, and whether the benchmark's findings generalize beyond planning-derived domains.
