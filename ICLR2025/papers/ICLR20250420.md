## Problem

Proxy-based causal inference methods (e.g., CEVAE) assume observed covariates are generated solely by latent confounders, but in practice these covariates often also encode latent post-treatment variables affected by the treatment. The paper shows this 'latent post-treatment bias' can make ATE estimation arbitrarily bad, yet no prior method could theoretically characterize the bias, disentangle confounders from post-treatment variables (since both correlate with T and Y), or guarantee that inferred latent variables are valid for unbiased adjustment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observed covariates X ∈ {0,1}^{K_X} (e.g., binary skill indicators), a binary treatment T, and a scalar outcome Y, assuming X is generated from K_C latent confounders C and K_M latent post-treatment variables M via an injective (possibly nonlinear) mixing function plus noise. It outputs an unbiased ATE estimate, the identified latent confounders Ĉ (up to element-wise bijective transformation and permutation), and the disentangled latent post-treatment variables. The disentanglement step uses kernel-based pairwise conditional independence tests with O(N² × K_Z²) training complexity, after which test-phase ATE estimation has the same cost as CEVAE.

## Method

CiVAE is a VAE whose conditional prior p(Z|Y,T) belongs to a general exponential family with a factorized part (each component having at least one invertible sufficient statistic) and a non-factorized ReLU neural network part that captures arbitrary dependence among latent variables. Training uses variational inference with a score-matching objective in place of the intractable KL term, since the normalizing constant of the exponential family prior is unavailable. The key identifiability theorem (Theorem 4.1) proves that under mild regularity conditions, the inferred latent variables are identifiable up to permutation and element-wise bijective transformation—stronger than prior iVAE/NF-iVAE results that only guarantee arbitrary nonlinear transformations. Disentanglement then exploits invariant causal structures: confounder pairs form a V-structure (C_i→T←C_j) making them conditionally dependent given T, while post-treatment pairs form a fork (M_i←T→M_j) or chain (C_i→T→M_j) making them conditionally independent given T, so pairwise conditional independence tests separate the two groups even under arbitrary latent interactions. Finally, Theorem 4.2 proves that controlling the bijectively transformed confounders yields an unbiased ATE estimator, since the Jacobian terms cancel in the change-of-variable.

## Results

CiVAE achieves the lowest absolute error (Err.) on all four datasets: 0.178 on LatentMediator (true ATE = −1.0), 0.199 on LatentCorrelator (true ATE = 1.0), 0.128 on LinkedIn(Age) (true ATE = −0.268), and 0.187 on LinkedIn(Gender) (true ATE = −0.081). Notably, CiVAE is the only method that correctly predicts the negative ATE on LatentMediator and LinkedIn(Age), while all baselines (including CEVAE, TEDVAE, and DR-CFR) produce positive estimates with errors exceeding 1.9 and 0.36 respectively. CEVAE is worse than the naive LR0 estimator on every dataset, confirming the severity of latent post-treatment bias in standard proxy-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CiVAE outperforms all six baselines on every dataset in terms of absolute ATE estimation error. Against CEVAE (the primary VAE-based baseline), CiVAE reduces error from 2.550 to 0.178 on LatentMediator and from 0.374 to 0.128 on LinkedIn(Age) — a roughly 14× and 3× improvement respectively. The covariate-disentanglement methods DR-CFR and TEDVAE inherit CEVAE's latent post-treatment bias because they rely on differential predictive power w.r.t. T and Y to separate latent factors, which fails when both confounders and post-treatment variables correlate with both. NICE (invariant risk minimization) is the closest competitor on LatentCorrelator (error 0.942 vs. CiVAE's 0.199) but degrades severely on LatentMediator (error 2.868) because mediators are also causal parents of Y. TarNet and the simple LR0/LR1 baselines confirm that directly controlling the scrambled proxy X is worse than not controlling it at all, as CEVAE's error exceeds LR0's on all four datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CEVAE line of proxy-based causal inference by identifying and formalizing a critical failure mode: when the observed proxy X is generated from both latent confounders C and latent post-treatment variables M, existing methods (CEVAE, TEDVAE, DR-CFR) control for M alongside C, introducing arbitrary bias. The identifiability framework builds directly on iVAE (Khemakhem et al., 2020) and NF-iVAE (Lu et al., 2021), but adds the key requirement that each factorized sufficient statistic has at least one invertible dimension, upgrading the identification guarantee from arbitrary nonlinear to bijective transformations. The disentanglement strategy departs from predictive-ability-based methods (TEDVAE, DR-CFR) by leveraging invariant causal structures (V-structures, fork-structures) and reducing the problem to pairwise kernel conditional independence tests.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The latent post-treatment bias is a pervasive practical problem in observational studies (medical trials with post-treatment measurements, policy evaluation with aggregated yearly data, LinkedIn job postings), yet prior work lacked a formal characterization of how proxy-based methods fail in this setting. The reduction from an intractable disentanglement problem to tractable independence tests, combined with the bijective identifiability guarantee, provides a principled path to unbiased ATE estimation under mild assumptions. Open questions include scalability of the O(N²K_Z²) independence tests to high-dimensional settings, handling cyclic latent interactions, and whether the exponential-family prior assumption holds in practice for complex real-world covariates.
