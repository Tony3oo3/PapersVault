## Problem

Existing synthetic data detection benchmarks (Fake2M, ASVSpoof 2019) evaluate traditional detectors but lack assessments of large multimodal models (LMMs), while FakeBench covers only a single image modality with limited subcategories. There is no comprehensive benchmark that spans video, image, 3D, text, and audio modalities with fine-grained anomaly annotations and graded difficulty levels to probe LMMs' explainability and generalization in synthetic data detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252537/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are modality-specific: 8-frame video clips, RGB images, 3D multiview renderings (also supporting point clouds and panoramic videos), text passages (50–200+ characters in Chinese and English), and audio clips (speech, singing, environmental sound, music), each paired with a natural-language question prompt. Outputs span four task types: binary real/synthetic judgments, multiple-choice selections between domain-matched real and synthetic pairs, identification of specific anomalous elements from fine-grained annotations, and open-ended anomaly explanations where no bounding boxes are provided. Questions are stratified into easy/medium/hard levels based on human annotator performance (all correct = easy; >50% incorrect = hard).

## Method

LOKI constructs 18K questions across 26 subcategories by collecting synthetic data from 5+ generation methods per modality (e.g., SORA, FLUX, NeRF/3DGS, MusicGen, GPT-4o) and pairing each with domain-matched real data. Fine-grained anomaly annotations include video segment timestamps with key frames, image bounding boxes with textual descriptions, and 3D texture/normal descriptions across four views, enabling the design of abnormal detail selection and explanation tasks. Abnormal detail questions are generated by GPT-4o conditioned on human annotations to reduce hallucination, then verified by 20 annotators over ~160 hours. The evaluation framework unifies APIs for 25+ LMMs in a zero-shot setting, introducing a Normalized Bias Index (NBI) computed from recall rates to quantify each model's tendency to favor real or synthetic labels, and uses GPT-4 scoring for open-ended explanation quality.

## Results

On the LOKI benchmark, GPT-4o achieves the best overall judgment accuracy of 63.9% and multiple-choice accuracy of 73.7% across video, image, 3D, and text modalities, still trailing human performance (76.0% judgment, 86.4% MC) by roughly 10–13 points. Among open-source LMMs, Qwen2-VL-72B (55.4% judgment, 65.6% MC) and LLaVA-OV-72B (56.3% judgment, 65.2% MC) lead, while most 7–8B models cluster near 50–53% judgment accuracy. In the abnormal detail selection and explanation tasks, GPT-4o reaches 75.3% selection accuracy and 72.6% explanation score, substantially outperforming Claude-3.5-Sonnet (32.8% selection, 45.8% explanation) and open-source models (43–69% selection). Audio LMMs (Qwen-Audio, SALMONN-7B, AnyGPT, OneLLM) all perform at or below random chance (~50%), indicating a critical gap in audio synthetic data detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o outperforms all other LMMs on both judgment (63.9%) and multiple-choice (73.7%) tasks, surpassing Claude-3.5-Sonnet (61.6%/74.8%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (53.2%/62.7%) by 2.3 and 10.7 points respectively on judgment. Among open-source models, Qwen2-VL-72B (55.4%) and LLaVA-OV-72B (56.3%) lead in judgment accuracy, but both trail GPT-4o by 7–8 points; notably, Qwen2-VL-7B achieves an exceptional 72.3% on 3D judgment, exceeding even GPT-4o (65.2%) on that modality. Expert detection models (AIGVDet, AIDE, RADAR-Vicuna-7B, AASIST) achieve 62.8% overall judgment accuracy, slightly below GPT-4o but above most open-source LMMs, though they lack explainability and generalization across diverse synthesis sources. In the text modality, Claude-3.5-Sonnet (89.2% MC) dramatically outperforms all other models including GPT-4o (66.6% MC), while in audio, all LMMs (both open and closed-source) perform at random level (~49–51%), far below the expert AASIST model (69.4%). The paper also shows that chain-of-thought prompting improves most models (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B: 56.8→59.5%, Gemini-1.5-Pro: 47.9→51.0%), but GPT-4o benefits more from few-shot (64.1→75.1%) than CoT (64.1→74.2%), suggesting its inherent reasoning ability reduces dependence on explicit reasoning scaffolding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic data detection literature from traditional binary classifiers (Fake2M, ASVSpoof 2019, AIDE, AASIST) and the single-modality LMM evaluation of FakeBench to a unified multi-modal (video, image, 3D, text, audio) benchmark with fine-grained anomaly explanation tasks. It departs from prior work by introducing multi-level annotations (global anomalies, segment anomalies, bounding-box region anomalies) and four question types (judgment, multiple-choice, abnormal detail selection, abnormal explanation) that go beyond the binary real/synthetic distinction. The evaluation framework unifies 25+ LMM APIs across heterogeneous input formats (8-frame video clips, multi-view 3D renderings, audio spectrograms, text passages), addressing the lack of a standardized protocol for cross-modal LMM synthetic detection evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AI-generated content saturates the internet (OpenAI reports ~100 billion words/day), reliable and explainable synthetic data detection becomes critical for content moderation, training data curation, and public trust. The benchmark reveals that even the best LMM (GPT-4o at 63.9%) trails human performance (76%) by a meaningful margin, audio LMMs perform at essentially chance level (~50%), and GPT-4o drops below random on hard questions (44.4%), highlighting substantial gaps before LMMs can serve as production-grade detectors. Open questions include whether LMMs can surpass human detection as synthesis artifacts become subtler, how to close the audio/3D modality gap, and whether the observed model biases (e.g., GPT-4o favoring 'real' for text, 'synthetic' for 3D) can be mitigated through training or prompting.
