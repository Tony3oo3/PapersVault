## Problem

Existing LLM evaluations focus almost exclusively on correctness metrics, overlooking the qualitative traits—tone, formatting, humor, formality—that users intuitively recognize and that drive real-world preferences. These 'vibes' are difficult to discover systematically and even harder to quantify, leaving a gap between what users actually prefer and what benchmarks measure.

## Input / Output

VibeCheck consumes prompt-output triples (prompt, output from model A, output from model B) along with binary preference labels indicating which model's response a user preferred. It produces a set of interpretable vibe axes (each defined by a low and high description, e.g., 'Tone: low=formal, high=friendly'), per-vibe utility scores (Cohen's Kappa for well-definedness, separability score for differentiating power), and aggregate metrics: model-matching accuracy (can vibes identify which model produced a response?) and preference-prediction accuracy (do vibes predict which response a user prefers?).

## Method

VibeCheck operates in three iterative stages. First, a proposer LLM (GPT-4o) examines small batches of prompt-output triples and generates candidate vibe axes, which are then clustered via agglomerative clustering on embeddings and reduced by GPT-4o to a compact set. Second, a panel of two LLM judges (GPT-4o-mini and Llama-3-70b) scores each output pair on each vibe as −1/0/+1, with position-bias mitigation by swapping output order; vibes are retained only if they meet thresholds for inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's Kappa ≥ 0.2) and separability (≥ 0.05). Third, the system iterates by feeding misclassified samples back to the proposer to discover finer-grained vibes not captured by the current set, repeating for 3–5 rounds until no new distinguishing axes emerge. A logistic regression model trained on the vibe score vectors is used to compute both model-matching and preference-prediction accuracy, providing a quantitative link between discovered vibes and user preferences.

## Results

On Chatbot Arena (Llama-3-70b vs GPT-4/Claude-3-Opus), VibeCheck with 3 iterations achieves 80.34% model matching accuracy and 59.34% preference prediction accuracy overall, outperforming predefined vibes (72.10% MM, 61.11% PP). The advantage is largest on writing prompts (77.19% vs 75.00% MM; 62.04% vs 59.49% PP), where stylistic differences dominate. On application tasks, VibeCheck reaches 97.09% MM and 72.79% PP on MATH (GPT-4o vs Llama-405b), 99.13% MM and 89.02% PP on COCO captioning (GPT-4V vs Gemini-1.5-Flash), and 71.29% MM and 61.42% PP on CNN/DailyMail summarization (Command X vs TNLG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a set of 10 predefined vibes (formality, humor, conciseness, logical rigor, etc.) generated by prompting GPT-4o with a generic question about common LLM output differences. VibeCheck outperforms predefined vibes on model matching accuracy in all three categories: +8.24 points overall (80.34 vs 72.10), +2.77 on STEM (68.71 vs 65.94), and +2.19 on Writing (77.19 vs 75.00). On preference prediction, the comparison is mixed: predefined vibes edge out VibeCheck on Overall (61.11 vs 59.34) and STEM (58.38 vs 57.31), but VibeCheck wins on Writing (62.04 vs 59.49), suggesting discovered vibes capture task-specific stylistic preferences better for open-ended tasks. Cohen's Kappa (inter-annotator agreement) is comparable across methods (0.45–0.52). Additionally, VibeCheck's discovered vibes are validated against human-annotated differences from the HC3 corpus, where 8 of 10 human-identified characteristics are recovered, confirming alignment with human qualitative analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-judge paradigm (Zheng et al., 2023; Verga et al., 2024) and the line of work on discovering separable traits in unstructured data (Zhong et al., 2022, 2023; Dunlap et al., 2024b) by combining automatic axis discovery with quantitative validation on three formalized criteria (well-defined, differentiating, user-aligned). Unlike aspect-based evaluations that rely on predefined axes like helpfulness or clarity (Mehri & Eskenazi, 2020; Zhang et al., 2024; Liang et al., 2023), VibeCheck discovers axes data-drivenly from model outputs and then measures their utility via inter-annotator agreement, model-matching accuracy, and preference prediction. The iterative refinement loop, which focuses on misclassified examples to discover finer-grained vibes, departs from single-pass discovery approaches in prior distributional difference work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VibeCheck provides a practical framework for understanding why users prefer one model over another beyond correctness metrics, with the Llama-3-70b vs. GPT-4/Claude analysis offering concrete evidence that stylistic traits (friendliness, humor, reduced ethical hedging) drive a meaningful portion of user preference on Chatbot Arena. Key limitations include reliance on LLM judges with known position and self-preference biases, high computational cost of validation, and the pairwise-only formulation that doesn't scale to multi-model comparisons. Open questions include whether discovered vibes can be used for targeted model interventions, whether the framework generalizes to more than two models simultaneously, and how to handle the confound between accuracy and style when models differ substantially in correctness.
