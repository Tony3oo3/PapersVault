## Problem

LLMs perform well on well-specified benchmarks but struggle with the ambiguously specified tasks that dominate real-world use, where the set of requirement-compatible solutions is a proper superset of the true solution set. While prior work advocates asking clarifying questions, the paper identifies that generating an *effective* clarifying question requires meta-cognitive reasoning about one's own generative distribution—a skill LLMs demonstrably lack out-of-the-box. The paper asks whether this gap can be closed by shifting the reasoning burden from the question space to the solution space.

## Input / Output

The method takes an ambiguous problem statement S = (R, C) decomposed into objective requirements and subjective context, and iteratively produces a single clarifying question per round whose oracle answer extends R and shrinks the compatible-solution set H. At each iteration the agent internally samples N candidate solutions (e.g., N=20 animals or N code completions) and M candidate questions (e.g., M=5), then selects one question to pose externally. In the code-generation experiment, questions are either binary assertions or open input-output pairs, and oracle answers are obtained by executing code in a sandboxed Python interpreter, yielding near-noiseless EIG estimates.

## Method

The core contribution is a Bayesian Experimental Design (BED) formulation of task disambiguation in which the utility of a candidate question equals its Expected Information Gain (EIG) minus a cost term. Under a uniformity assumption over requirement-compatible solutions, the paper proves (Corollary 1) that EIG is maximized when a question partitions the solution space into equally sized subsets, reducing question selection to a partitioning problem. Practically, the agent samples N solutions from its own generative distribution, samples M candidate questions, generates pseudo-answers for every (question, solution) pair, and computes the EIG score as the Shannon entropy of the induced partition (Algorithm 1). This explicitly evaluates each question's discriminative power against concrete solution samples rather than relying on the LLM to implicitly judge which question is best. The approach is validated on a 20-questions game (where pseudo-answers come from a self-critic LLM prompt) and on HumanEval/APPS code generation (where pseudo-answers come from executing sampled programs), showing consistent gains over zero-shot and Tree-of-Thoughts baselines, with the largest improvements for weaker models and when evaluation noise is low.

## Results

On the 20 Questions game (GPT-3.5-turbo), EIG-uniform achieves a final ranking score of 6.2/10 after 10 iterations, outperforming implicit (4.9), implicit-ToT (5.1), and EIG-logprobs (4.1). On HumanEval code generation after 4 elicited requirements, EIG-based strategies yield substantial accuracy gains: EIG(B) reaches 85.6% vs. 70.8% for base(B) with GPT-3.5-turbo (+14.8 pp), and EIG(O) reaches 87.2% vs. 75.1% for base(O) with GPT-4o-mini on the harder APPS benchmark (+12.1 pp). The advantage of EIG-uniform over implicit baselines is most pronounced for weaker models (GPT-3.5-turbo, Llama-3-8B) and narrows for stronger models (GPT-4o-mini, Llama-3-70B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed EIG-uniform strategy against three baselines: (1) implicit zero-shot question generation, (2) implicit-ToT (sample M=5 questions, then prompt the LLM to select the best, following Yao et al. 2023), and (3) EIG-logprobs (EIG estimated using LLM token log-probabilities instead of uniform assumption). EIG-uniform consistently outperforms both implicit baselines across all four models (GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4o-mini, Llama-3-8B, Llama-3-70B) and both experiments (20 Questions, code generation). The gap is largest for weaker models: on HumanEval with GPT-3.5-turbo, EIG(B) achieves 85.6% vs. 70.8% for base(B) at t=4, while for GPT-4o-mini the gap shrinks to 81.5% vs. 79.6%. EIG-logprobs significantly underperforms EIG-uniform (e.g., 4.1 vs. 6.2 ranking on 20 Questions with GPT-3.5-turbo), confirming that the LLM's generative distribution is inadequately biased relative to the true solution set. On the harder APPS benchmark, EIG(O) with GPT-4o-mini reaches 87.2% accuracy vs. 75.1% for base(O), a 12.1 pp improvement, demonstrating that the method generalizes across difficulty levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interactive clarification framework established by CLAM (Kuhn et al., 2022) and Li et al. (2025) by formalizing task ambiguity through a Bayesian Experimental Design lens, where the utility of a clarifying question is quantified via expected information gain over the solution space. It departs from prior active learning and preference elicitation work (Houlsby et al., 2011; Handa et al., 2024) by operating in an unconstrained natural-language solution space rather than a fixed feature space or candidate pool, requiring iterative solution sampling at each step. The key methodological departure is shifting the reasoning load from implicit meta-cognitive question selection (as in zero-shot prompting or Tree-of-Thoughts baselines) to explicit EIG maximization computed over self-generated solution samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and practically important gap: LLMs' out-of-the-box ability to generate informative clarifying questions is significantly weaker than their solution-generation ability, with implications for safety-critical applications where misinterpretation of underspecified tasks carries real risk. The finding that EIG-logprobs underperforms EIG-uniform is a useful negative result, confirming that LLMs' own generative distribution is inadequately biased and should not be trusted as a proxy for the problem-setter's preferences. Open questions remain around ambiguity detection (when to start querying), stopping criteria (when enough requirements have been collected), and the computational overhead of O(N+M+NM) LLM calls per iteration, which the authors acknowledge but argue will become less relevant as inference costs decrease.
