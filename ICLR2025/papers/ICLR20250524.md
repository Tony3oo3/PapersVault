## Problem

Deep learning for breast cancer bone metastasis (BCBM) diagnosis and prognosis is hindered by the absence of open, large-scale, high-resolution datasets with sufficient diagnostic and prognostic labeling. Existing human BCBM datasets suffer from low resolution, limited sample sizes, inadequate labels, and lack of sequential longitudinal imaging, creating a catch-22 where clinicians avoid repeated imaging without effective detection tools and AI models cannot be trained without the resulting imaging data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250524/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 260 multi-angle 2D X-ray projections per scan (4032×4032 pixels, 0.8° intervals over 208° rotation), 3D CT volumes (~2588×2428, 2685 slices, 7–10.6 μm voxel size), and structured CSV medical records, all temporally aligned at 1-week resolution across 4–5 sequential scans per mouse. Outputs span animal-level BCBM diagnosis labels, predicted future 3D CT volumes with bone mechanical property estimates (R²=0.956 for reaction force curves), sparse-angle 3D CT reconstructions from a single X-ray, tibiae segmentation masks, and free-text diagnostic reports from a fine-tuned LLaVA model.

## Method

The BoneMet dataset (67.87 TB, 500+ mice, 2019–2024) is organized into six components—Rotation-X-Ray, Recon-CT, Seg-CT, Regist-CT, RoI-CT, and MiceMediRec—derived from a murine BCBM model with controlled tumor inoculation, age-matched non-tumor controls, and multiple treatment interventions (chemotherapy, radiation, exercise). Validation experiments employ diverse architectures: Swin-Base and MMST-ViT (with spatial-temporal alignment) for 2D X-ray diagnosis, BiT-M/EfficientNetV2M/Swin-B with max-pooling slice-to-volume aggregation for 3D CT diagnosis, 3D-GAN/T-VAE/ST-VAE for temporal CT generation, and PixelNeRF/MedNeRF for sparse-angle reconstruction. A key finding is that incorporating spatial-temporal alignment (STA) into the ViT architecture reduces the training-test accuracy gap from 20.4% to 6.0%, substantially mitigating overfitting inherent to ViT on this data. The dataset is accompanied by a Python package (BoneMet on PyPI) providing three APIs for CT segmentation, mutual-information-based registration, and RoI cropping to streamline large-scale preprocessing.

## Results

On the BoneMet Rotation-X-Ray dataset, MMST-ViT (ViT with spatial-temporal alignment) achieves 89.0% test accuracy and 91.3% F1-score, substantially outperforming Swin-Base (ViT without STA) at 79.1% accuracy and 86.0% F1-score, while also reducing the train-test gap from 20.4% to 6.0%. For 3D CT-based BCBM diagnosis on Regist-CT, Swin-B reaches 92.0% accuracy, surpassing EfficientNetV2M (85.0%) and BiT-M (81.0%). For sparse-angle CT reconstruction, MedNeRF achieves PSNR 30.2 and SSIM 0.810, significantly outperforming PixelNeRF (PSNR 20.2, SSIM 0.740). For multi-modal bone prognosis, ST-VAE generates future 3D CTs with PSNR 35.5 and SSIM 0.860, and the predicted reaction force curve matches ground truth with R²=0.956.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates eight baselines across four tasks to demonstrate BoneMet's broad applicability. For 2D X-ray diagnosis, MMST-ViT (ViT w/ STA) [21] outperforms Swin-Base (ViT w/o STA) [25] by 9.9% in test accuracy (89.0% vs. 79.1%), primarily by reducing overfitting (train-test gap drops from 20.4% to 6.0%). For 3D CT diagnosis, Swin-B [25] achieves the best accuracy at 92.0%, exceeding EfficientNetV2M [35] (85.0%) by 7.0% and BiT-M [16] (81.0%) by 11.0%, attributed to Multi-head Self-Attention's superior slice-to-volume feature aggregation. For future CT generation, ST-VAE outperforms T-VAE [43] (SSIM 0.860 vs. 0.817) and 3D-GAN [2] (SSIM 0.860 vs. 0.767), with a 13.6 dB PSNR advantage over 3D-GAN. For sparse-angle reconstruction, MedNeRF [4] dominates PixelNeRF [45] across all four metrics (PSNR 30.2 vs. 20.2; SSIM 0.810 vs. 0.740; FID 91.5 vs. 155.2; KID 0.092 vs. 0.128), confirming the dataset's suitability for NeRF-based single-view CT reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first large-scale, publicly available, multi-modal murine dataset specifically curated for breast cancer bone metastasis (BCBM) diagnosis and prognosis, addressing a data scarcity gap that prior deep learning efforts (e.g., Papandrianos et al. 2020, Zhao et al. 2020, Li et al. 2022) identified as the primary bottleneck. Rather than proposing a new architecture, the paper repurposes a diverse set of existing models—Swin Transformer, MMST-ViT, BiT, EfficientNetV2, NeRF-based reconstructions (PixelNeRF, MedNeRF), and generative models (3D-GAN, T-VAE, ST-VAE)—as benchmarks to validate the dataset's utility across diagnosis, prognosis, and sparse-angle reconstruction tasks. The experimental protocol builds directly on the established murine BCBM model of Wang et al. (2021), extending it with five years of systematic longitudinal micro-CT acquisition and a structured six-component data pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset directly tackles the 'catch-22' the authors identify: clinicians avoid longitudinal BCBM imaging due to radiation and false-positive harms, yet AI models cannot be trained without such data. By providing 67 TB of temporally aligned, high-resolution (7–10.6 μm) multi-modal scans with ground-truth labels across the full disease trajectory, BoneMet enables preclinical validation of AI-driven diagnosis and prognosis tools before human trials. Key limitations include the murine-to-human translation gap (weeks vs. years of progression, inbred homogeneous genetics, animal-level rather than time-point-level labels), and the open question of whether sparse-angle reconstruction and generative CT prediction approaches transfer to the lower-resolution, noisier human X-ray/CT regime. The authors envision a positive feedback loop where murine-trained generative models reduce radiation in human imaging, enabling the construction of human BCBM foundation-model datasets.
