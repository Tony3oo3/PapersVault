## Problem

In nonconvex matrix factorization, initialization has been largely overlooked compared to learning rates and descent directions, with most prior work relying on small random initialization that requires escaping the saddle point at the origin and yields only linear convergence. This paper demonstrates that the same algorithm (ScaledGD) can exhibit fundamentally different convergence behavior—linear versus quadratic, or even one-step—depending solely on the initialization, revealing a previously unappreciated degree of freedom in optimization.

## Input / Output

For the theoretical matrix factorization problems, the input is a target matrix A ∈ R^{m×n} (PSD for symmetric, general for asymmetric) and the output is a low-rank factor X ∈ R^{m×r} (or pair X, Y) satisfying XX^T ≈ A or XY^T ≈ A. For the practical NoRA/NoRA+ application, the input is a pretrained weight matrix W0 and a small finetuning dataset; the output is a pair of adapter matrices (X_T, Y_T) of rank r ≪ min{m,n} such that W0 + X_T Y_T^T approximates the target. The Nyström initialization X0 = W0Ω requires only a single matrix-matrix product with a Gaussian random matrix Ω, avoiding expensive SVD or QR decompositions used by competing methods like PiSSA and OLoRA.

## Method

The core contribution is Nyström initialization (X0 = AΩ, Y0 = 0 for asymmetric; X0 = AΩ for symmetric), which aligns the initial iterate with the column space of A and eliminates the residual space (I − QQ^T)X_t = 0 for all t. This directional alignment is the key mechanism that transforms ScaledGD's convergence from linear to quadratic (symmetric, exact/over-parametrized) or to one-step global convergence (asymmetric, exact/over-parametrized), and from asymptotic to linear/sublinear (under-parametrized). For LoRA finetuning, this becomes NoRA (applying X0 = W0Ω, Y0 = 0 with standard Adam) and NoRA+ (adding ScaledGD-style preconditioning with gradient normalization), both of which are deployment-compatible since they do not modify pretrained weights. The approach is more economical than SVD-based initializations (PiSSA, OLoRA) and requires no tuning of the initialization scale ξ.

## Results

On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA-7B, NoRA+ achieves 76.71 average accuracy across 8 tasks, outperforming LoRA (76.19) and LoRA-P (76.41). On LLaMA2-7B, NoRA+ reaches 78.92 average, surpassing LoRA (77.6) and LoRA-P (78.35) by 1.3 and 0.57 points respectively. On OPT-1.3B few-shot learning, NoRA+ achieves 75.1 average accuracy, improving over LoRA (73.2) by 1.9 points. For subject-driven image generation with Stable Diffusion, NoRA and NoRA+ reduce training loss by 9.6% compared to LoRA and LoRA-P (0.084 vs. 0.092/0.093).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoRA and NoRA+ consistently outperform standard LoRA across all evaluated settings: on OPT-1.3B few-shot learning, NoRA+ improves average accuracy by 1.9 points (75.1 vs. 73.2); on LLaMA-7B commonsense reasoning, NoRA+ gains 0.52 points (76.71 vs. 76.19); and on LLaMA2-7B, the gap widens to 1.32 points (78.92 vs. 77.6). Against LoRA-P (Riemannian preconditioned LoRA), NoRA+ also wins on both LLaMA-7B (76.71 vs. 76.41) and LLaMA2-7B (78.92 vs. 78.35), confirming that Nyström initialization provides benefits beyond preconditioning alone. On OPT-1.3B, NoRA and NoRA+ surpass both OLoRA (74.0) and PiSSA (74.1) by approximately 1 point in average accuracy, while also offering a deployment advantage since they do not modify pretrained weights. For Stable Diffusion subject-driven generation, NoRA and NoRA+ achieve 9.6% lower training loss than both LoRA and LoRA-P, with visibly higher-fidelity generated images. Notably, LoRA exhibits training instability on LLaMA2-7B (results taken from prior work), while NoRA and NoRA+ remain stable throughout.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on ScaledGD (Tong et al., 2021) as its optimizer and the Nyström sketch (Tropp et al., 2017) as its initialization mechanism, but its core contribution is showing that the combination fundamentally changes the convergence rate class for Burer-Monteiro matrix factorization. Prior convergence analyses for matrix factorization (Du et al., 2018; Ye & Du, 2021; Ward & Kolda, 2023; Jia et al., 2023) all rely on small random initialization and yield at best linear rates; this work demonstrates that Nyström initialization eliminates the residual space and provably upgrades ScaledGD to quadratic (symmetric) or one-step (asymmetric) convergence. On the practical side, NoRA departs from recent LoRA initialization methods (PiSSA, OLoRA) that require expensive SVD/QR of pretrained weights, offering a simpler sketch-based alternative that preserves deployment compatibility with standard LoRA pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central conceptual finding—that the same preconditioned gradient method can exhibit linear versus quadratic convergence depending solely on initialization—challenges the smooth-optimization intuition that initialization is a secondary concern once the learning rate is set. Practically, NoRA is a drop-in initialization (X₀ = W₀Ω, Y₀ = 0) that requires no SVD, no modification of pretrained weights, and no pipeline changes, making it immediately deployable. Open questions include extending the theory to matrix sensing, tensor factorization, and deeper network architectures, and whether the under-parametrized case can achieve full global optimality rather than the weaker notion established here. The practical LoRA gains, while consistent across models (1B–7B) and modalities (text, image), remain modest (1–2% absolute), suggesting the theoretical rate improvements may not fully translate to finite-data, finite-iteration regimes.
