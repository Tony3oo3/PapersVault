## Problem

LLM planners operate in two extremes: fast System-1 planning (direct generation without search) is cheap but inaccurate, while slow System-2 planning (explicit search over actions) is accurate but computationally prohibitive for long horizons or large action spaces. Neither mode alone lets users control the compute-accuracy tradeoff, and existing System-2 methods incur full search cost on every sample regardless of difficulty.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language description of a planning problem (start state and goal state, e.g., a 5×5 maze with obstacle coordinates or a Blocksworld block configuration) plus a user-specified hybridization factor x ∈ [0,1]. The controller outputs a meta-plan of sub-goals each labeled 'easy' (System-1) or 'hard' (System-2); the System-2 component verbalizes full search trajectories (explored states, validity checks, actions taken) before emitting its sub-plan, while System-1 emits a direct action sequence. Final plans are concatenations of sub-plans, and the #States-Explored metric (counting all verbalized states, valid and invalid) serves as the compute budget for fair comparison.

## Method

System-1.x fine-tunes a single base LLM (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 via LoRA, rank 8) into three adapters: a Controller, a System-1 Planner, and a System-2 Planner, all supervised solely by search traces from symbolic solvers (A*, BFS, or DFS). The controller is trained on auto-generated data where a hardness function h(s_i, s_j) ranks problems; the easiest (1−x)×N samples are labeled System-1-only, while the remaining x×N are decomposed via a sliding-window constrained optimization that assigns the hardest contiguous chunk of length x·n to System-2 and the flanking sub-goals to System-1. At inference, the controller produces the sub-goal decomposition and easy/hard labels, then the appropriate planner solves each sub-goal and the sub-plans are concatenated. Test-time controllability is achieved by biasing the controller's confidence threshold to shift more or fewer sub-goals toward System-2 without retraining, and the framework can be converted to a neuro-symbolic variant by swapping the neural System-2 adapter for a symbolic solver like A*.

## Results

On Maze Navigation, System-1.5 (x=0.5) with sub-goal decomposition achieves 70.4% plan validity at 13.6 states explored, outperforming System-1 (48.7% at 3.1 states) by 21% and System-2 (37.2% at 13.6 states) by 33% at the same budget. With test-time control, System-1.5 reaches 96.7% validity at 27.3 states, surpassing System-2's 93.7% at 24.4 states. On Blocksworld (OOD generalization to longer plans), System-1.5 with sub-goals achieves 25.0% validity at 30 states versus System-2's 13.0% at 29.3 states. In the neuro-symbolic variant, System-1.5 outperforms A* by 39% (70.5% vs. 31.0%) at 11.6 states explored.

## Baselines & Comparisons

System-1.x with sub-goal decomposition outperforms all baselines at every matched budget on Maze Navigation: at 13.6 states it beats System-1 by 21% (70.4% vs. 48.7%), System-2 by 33% (70.4% vs. 37.2%), and the no-decomposition variant by 20% (70.4% vs. 50.0%). At maximum budget, test-time-controlled System-1.5 reaches 96.7% validity, exceeding System-2's 93.7% and the no-decomposition variant's 93.7%. On Blocksworld (OOD), System-1.5 with sub-goals achieves 25.0% at 30 states while System-2 drops to 13.0% at 29.3 states, and System-1.5 without sub-goals collapses to near 0% under truncation. In the neuro-symbolic setting, System-1.5 with A* as the System-2 component outperforms standalone A* by 39% (70.5% vs. 31.0%) at 11.6 states and matches A*'s 100% validity at 23.9 states. The trained controller also outperforms a random controller by 10% (79.5% vs. 70.5%), validating the hardness-based sub-goal assignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on Dual-Process Theory (Kahneman, 2011) to formalize a hybrid planning framework for LLMs, building directly on the line of work that trains LLMs to verbalize search trajectories (Searchformer, Lehnert et al. 2024; Stream-of-Search, Gandhi et al. 2024). It departs from these prior System-2-only methods by introducing a controller that decomposes problems into sub-goals and dynamically routes easy sub-goals to a fast System-1 planner and hard sub-goals to a slow System-2 planner, governed by a user-specified hybridization factor x. The neuro-symbolic variant also connects to LLM-Modulo frameworks (Kambhampati et al. 2024) but differs by being a training-time method that unifies all components on a single fine-tuned LLM rather than relying on external symbolic solvers at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The System-1.x framework offers a practical and principled way to balance compute cost against planning accuracy, with both train-time (hybridization factor x) and test-time (controller confidence threshold) controllability — a capability absent from prior System-1-only or System-2-only approaches. The finding that a weaker System-1 planner can be effectively leveraged for easier sub-goals to save compute on OOD problems (Blocksworld) suggests a general strategy for handling long-horizon tasks where full search is infeasible. However, evaluation is limited to two small-scale domains (5×5 mazes, 4–7 blocks) with a single 7B model, leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex planning domains, larger models, or settings where domain-specific hardness functions are unavailable.
