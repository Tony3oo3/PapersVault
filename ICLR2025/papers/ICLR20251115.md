## Problem

Co-training, which trains a single shared-parameter model to handle multiple tasks one at a time, has never been combined with quantization-aware training. The paper shows that naively applying existing QAT methods (e.g., LSQ+) to co-trained models causes significant and task-dependent performance degradation, and identifies the root cause as mismatched activation quantization scales across tasks with different feature distributions, compounded by information distortion in deeper Transformer attention layers.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single image (low-quality for SR/deraining/denoising, or SAR/RGB for classification) along with a task-specific query embedding that routes it to the appropriate head and tail within a shared backbone. At inference, only one task is active per forward pass, and the 4-bit quantized model achieves a 7.99× compression ratio (87.5% parameter reduction) while producing super-resolved, derained, denoised images or class labels. The task embedding also selects which per-task quantization scales to apply, so the same shared weights operate under different quantization mappings depending on the active task.

## Method

TSQ-MTC introduces two mechanisms to fix QAT in co-training. First, the Task-Specific Learnable Multi-Scale Activation Quantizer (TLMAQ) replaces the single shared activation scale with K task-specific learnable scaling factors and offsets, selected via the task query embedding f_k; this lets each task's distinct feature distribution (e.g., deraining spanning [-3.72, 3.75] vs. denoising spanning [-7.66, 7.64]) be quantized independently without cross-task conflict. Second, Structure-Based Layer-by-Layer Distillation (SLLD) computes both full-precision and quantized attention outputs simultaneously at each Transformer layer and applies a mean-SSIM loss to align them, preserving spatial structure that logit-level supervision would miss. Scales are initialized from full-precision feature statistics per task, and the full-precision attention branch is computed on-the-fly (no separate teacher model needed), keeping the training overhead minimal.

## Results

On the primary single-modal co-training experiment (4-bit quantized IPT), TSQ-MTC achieves 38.33 dB PSNR on Set5 and 33.67 dB on Urban100 for ×2 super-resolution, nearly matching the full-precision IPT (38.37 / 33.76 dB) while delivering a 7.99× compression ratio. For the more challenging ×4 SR task, TSQ-MTC reaches 32.64 dB on Set5, exactly matching the full-precision model. In the multi-modal CNN classification experiment, TSQ-MTC achieves 97.55% top-1 accuracy on ResNet-34 (all modalities) and 100% on ResNet-50 (RGB), substantially outperforming the LSQ+ baseline (95.64% and 97.83% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSQ-MTC consistently outperforms all six baselines across both co-training scenarios. Against the closest competitor Q-ViT (Li et al., 2022), TSQ-MTC gains +0.03 dB on Set5 and +0.07 dB on Urban100 for ×2 SR, and +0.07 dB on Set5 for ×4 SR where it exactly matches full precision. Compared to the direct co-training baseline (LSQ+ / baseline-multi), TSQ-MTC improves by +0.08 dB (Set5, ×2) to +0.23 dB (Urban100, ×4), with the gap widening at higher magnification. Against PAMS (Li et al., 2020), TSQ-MTC leads by +0.21 dB on Set5 (×2) and +0.33 dB on Set5 (×4). In the multi-modal CNN experiment, TSQ-MTC surpasses LSQ+ by +1.91 points on ResNet-34 (all modalities) and +2.17 points on ResNet-50 (RGB), with gains growing for deeper backbones. Against single-task SR quantization methods (CADyQ, QuantSR-T), TSQ-MTC achieves +1.47 dB on Urban100 (×2) over QuantSR-T, demonstrating that multi-task co-training with adaptive scales can outperform dedicated single-task quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of quantization-aware training (building on LSQ/LSQ+ for learnable scales and offsets) and the co-training paradigm (building on Maninis et al.'s attentive single-tasking of multiple tasks). It departs from prior QAT work by identifying that the shared activation quantizer's single scale becomes a bottleneck when multiple tasks with divergent feature distributions are co-trained, and addresses this by making scales task-conditional via the existing task query embedding. The SLLD component adapts the layer-by-layer distillation idea from AQ-DETR (Wang et al., 2024) but replaces logit-level supervision with SSIM-based structural alignment, motivated by the observation that attention information distortion in deeper Transformer layers is spatially structured rather than merely a classification-level mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As co-trained foundation models (IPT, PolyViT, OmniVore) become the standard for multi-task vision, making them deployable on edge devices via low-bit quantization is a practical necessity that this paper addresses with minimal overhead (only 0.04M extra parameters). The diagnostic finding that activation scale conflict—not weight quantization—is the primary failure mode provides a clear target for future work. However, the DomainNet cross-domain experiment reveals a limitation: when tasks share similar feature distributions, task-specific scales offer no benefit, suggesting the method's gains are contingent on distributional divergence between tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 2-bit or ternary quantization, whether scale merging across similar tasks (suggested but not fully explored) can further reduce overhead, and whether the method generalizes to autoregressive or diffusion-based co-trained models.
