## Problem

Current LLM alignment pipelines rely on heuristic choices—trial-and-error weight selection for preference rewards and ad-hoc KL penalty strengths—to balance task performance against application-specific preferences. There is no principled mechanism to directly enforce minimum requirements (e.g., response length bounds, harmlessness thresholds) while simultaneously learning the task, and the sequential SFT-then-alignment structure risks forgetting task capabilities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text prompts (instruction-following pairs from UltraChat, capped at 512 tokens) and produces text responses that provably satisfy user-specified constraint baselines on one or more preference reward functions (e.g., response length in [l_low, l_high], helpfulness ≥ b, harmlessness ≥ b). Constraints are expressed as average (expectation) constraints over the prompt distribution, though the framework also supports uniform and chance-constraint variants. The constraint baselines b_i are the only application-specific hyperparameters a practitioner must set, replacing the need to tune reward weights.

## Method

L3Ms merge SFT and alignment into a single constrained optimization: minimize the task perplexity (Eq. 1) subject to lower-bound constraints on k preference reward functions. The constraints are enforced via a relaxed logarithmic barrier B_{μ,μ²}(z) added to the objective, with μ iteratively decreased (μ_t ← γμ_{t-1}) so the barrier converges to the characteristic function χ{z≤0}, making violated constraints infinitely costly. Crucially, the barrier's gradient yields implicit Lagrange multipliers λ̂_i = μ/(k·max(−C_i(θ), μ²)) that satisfy KKT complementary slackness by design—active constraints receive non-zero multipliers while inactive ones vanish as μ→0—eliminating the need for a minimax game to learn multipliers. Policy gradients (log-derivative trick) estimate the constraint gradients, with exponential moving averages for the non-stationary constraint values in the denominator. This single-stage formulation removes the KL divergence penalty and the need to load a reference SFT model, reducing both memory and compute during fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Helpful-Harmless (HH) alignment task with LLaMA-7B fine-tuned on UltraChat, L3Ms satisfy imposed reward constraints while achieving task perplexities in the range 0.804–0.820, compared to 0.822–0.825 for the minimax (MM) baseline and 0.805 for the SFT-only model. For length-constrained L3Ms, mean response lengths fall within the specified [l_low, l_high] ranges (e.g., 81.3 tokens for [50,100], 64.4 for [50,75]) with task perplexity degrading by at most 0.005 over the SFT model (0.805±0.3). The key advantage is that L3Ms maintain task performance closer to the SFT baseline while enforcing constraints, whereas MMs sacrifice up to 0.02 perplexity to oversatisfy constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares L3Ms against the minimax (MM) approach of Moskovitz et al. (2024) and Dai et al. (2024), which finds a saddle point of the Lagrangian by jointly learning Lagrange multipliers. On the HH alignment task, L3Ms consistently achieve lower task perplexity than MMs at every comparable constraint setting: 0.814 vs. 0.822 at (3,3), 0.818 vs. 0.822 at (4,2), and 0.820 vs. 0.825 at (2,4). The paper attributes this gap to the log-barrier multipliers satisfying KKT complementary slackness by design, whereas the MM approach's learned multipliers are highly sensitive to learning-rate choice. Additionally, L3Ms avoid loading a separate SFT model for KL-divergence penalties, reducing both memory and compute during fine-tuning. On the length-constraint task, L3Ms meet all imposed length ranges with negligible perplexity degradation (≤0.005 over SFT), demonstrating that the constrained formulation handles perfectly anti-correlated rewards (length and negative length) without heuristic weight tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the relaxed logarithmic barrier from Kervadec et al. (2022) for constrained deep learning and applies it to the LLM alignment problem, where prior constrained-RLHF work (Moskovitz et al., 2024; Dai et al., 2024) instead solves the Lagrangian via a minimax saddle-point game. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) SFT and alignment are merged into a single constrained optimization rather than treated as sequential stages with a heuristic KL penalty, and (2) the log barrier's structure guarantees KKT complementary slackness by design, avoiding the sensitivity to learning-rate choices that plagues learned Lagrange multipliers. The policy-gradient estimation of constraint gradients follows standard RL practice (Schulman et al., 2016), and the Bradley-Terry reward model initialization from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) is used for the learned preference functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formulation offers a principled, heuristic-free way to specify multi-preference alignment requirements (e.g., 'responses must be between 50 and 100 tokens' or 'harmlessness reward ≥ 3') and directly enforce them, which is practically appealing for application-specific LLM deployment. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single 7B model (LLaMA-7B), two preference axes (helpful/harmless) plus length, and a small held-out test set (~1.7k samples), with perplexity differences in the 0.804–0.820 range that are hard to interpret as meaningful. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and more diverse constraint types (uniform or chance constraints), whether the non-convex convergence guarantees hold in practice, and how the method compares to newer alignment paradigms like DPO or direct preference optimization under the same constraint specification.
