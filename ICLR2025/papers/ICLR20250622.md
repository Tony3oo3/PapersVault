## Problem

Diffusion transformer policies for imitation learning face a computational roadblock: as models scale to capture complex capabilities, their FLOPs and inference latency grow prohibitively, restricting deployment on resource-constrained platforms like mobile robots. No prior work has applied Mixture-of-Experts sparsity to the denoising process itself to reduce active parameters and inference cost while maintaining or improving policy performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250622/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes two RGB camera views (static and wrist-mounted) encoded by FiLM-conditioned ResNets-18/50, a frozen CLIP sentence embedding for the language goal, and noisy action tokens during denoising, forming a 14-token sequence (1 noise + 1 goal + 2 image + 10 action tokens). It outputs a chunk of 10 continuous end-effector actions per forward pass, generated via 10 DDIM denoising steps from Gaussian noise, with no early re-planning or temporal aggregation between chunks.

## Method

MoDE replaces the dense feed-forward sublayer in each transformer block with a sparse Mixture-of-Experts layer whose router is conditioned solely on the current noise level σ_t rather than input content, enabling specialized experts for different denoising phases. A noise token φ(σ_t) is also added to all input tokens before self-attention, providing noise-aware attention patterns without extra parameters. Because routing is deterministic per noise level, the selected experts can be precomputed and fused into a single composite MLP before inference (expert caching), eliminating dynamic routing overhead and reducing FLOPs by over 90% relative to a dense transformer of comparable size. Training uses the EDM score-matching objective with a load-balancing loss to prevent expert collapse, and the pretrained variant (12 layers, 4 experts, 1024-dim) is fine-tuned with frozen routers on target benchmarks.

## Results

On CALVIN ABCD→D, MoDE achieves an average rollout length of 4.39 (pretrained) and 4.30 (from scratch), surpassing GR-1 (4.21) and RoboFlamingo (4.09). On the harder zero-shot CALVIN ABC→D, pretrained MoDE reaches 4.01, outperforming GR-1 (3.06) and SuSIE (2.69) by a wide margin. On LIBERO-10, MoDE achieves 0.92 average success rate (first policy above 90%), and on LIBERO-90 it reaches 0.91. On the SIMPLER generalist benchmark, MoDE attains 26.30% average success rate, beating OpenVLA (23.70%) and Octo (17.75%), while using 90% fewer FLOPs (1.53 vs 27.5 GFLOPS for GR-1) and 40% fewer active parameters than dense transformer baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoDE outperforms all 10 baselines across every benchmark. Against the two Diffusion Policy variants (DP-CNN and DP-T from Chi et al., 2023), MoDE achieves a 57.5% average improvement across four benchmarks, with the largest gap on CALVIN ABC→D where MoDE (4.01) nearly triples DP-T's score (1.13). Against the large pretrained VLA RoboFlamingo (~1B params, 690 GFLOPS/action), MoDE (436M active params, 1.53 GFLOPS/action) achieves higher CALVIN ABCD→D rollout length (4.39 vs 4.09) with 450× fewer FLOPs. Against GR-1 (130M params, 27.5 GFLOPS), MoDE wins on both CALVIN settings (4.39 vs 4.21; 4.01 vs 3.06) while using 18× fewer FLOPs. On LIBERO, MoDE (0.92/0.91) beats QueST and both DP variants. On SIMPLER, MoDE (26.30%) surpasses the 7.7B-parameter OpenVLA (23.70%) and Octo (17.75%). On state-based benchmarks, MoDE (0.97 Block Push, 3.79 Relay Kitchen) edges out BESO (0.96, 3.73) and clearly beats C-BeT and VQ-BeT.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoDE sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: Diffusion Policies for robot imitation learning (Chi et al., 2023), the EDM continuous-time diffusion framework (Karras et al., 2022), and sparse Mixture-of-Experts routing as popularized by Switch Transformers (Fedus et al., 2022). The key conceptual departure is using the noise level σ_t as the routing signal rather than token content, motivated by evidence that denoising is inherently multi-task (Hang et al., 2024). This distinguishes MoDE from Sparse-DP (Wang et al., 2024), which uses MoE for task-level specialization, and from prior noise-scheduled diffusion architectures (Park et al., 2023; Go et al., 2023) that hard-assign parameters to denoising stages rather than learning a soft router.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% FLOP reduction via expert caching while simultaneously achieving SOTA on 134 tasks addresses a critical bottleneck for deploying diffusion policies on resource-constrained robots (mobile platforms, on-board compute). The finding that a 436M-parameter MoDE outperforms 1B+ parameter pretrained models like RoboFlamingo with far fewer FLOPs suggests that architectural efficiency can substitute for raw scale in robotics IL. Open questions include whether the noise-conditioned routing generalizes beyond 4 experts (performance degrades beyond 4), whether expert collapse can be mitigated at scale, and whether the approach extends to continuous control settings with higher-dimensional action spaces. The router initialization sensitivity noted as a limitation suggests the training recipe may not yet be robust enough for broader adoption.
