## Problem

Vision-based tactile sensors (e.g., GelSight, DIGIT) produce substantially different RGB signals for the same contact due to optical design and manufacturing variations, causing models trained on one sensor to fail on another. Prior transferable tactile representations (T3, UniT) treat sensor types as fixed categories or rely on per-sensor encoders, preventing zero-shot generalization to unseen sensors. The paper asks whether a single representation can be learned that is invariant to sensor-specific artifacts and transfers without retraining.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a 224×224 RGB tactile image (after background subtraction) together with K calibration images—18 images from pressing a 4 mm ball and a cube corner at nine 3×3 grid locations—stacked channel-wise into a single H×W×KC tensor. Calibration tokens are computed once per sensor and cached, so they add no per-inference cost. The output is a Sensor-Invariant Tactile Representation (SITR): a 128-d class token plus patch tokens, which downstream decoders (ResNet-18 + MLP for classification, modified ResNet-18 for pose) convert into a 16-way class label or a 3-DoF (x, y, z) pose estimate.

## Method

SITR is a modified ViT-Base (96 M params) that jointly encodes tactile-image patches and calibration-image patches (region-wise stacked before linear projection) alongside a learnable class token, with 2D positional encodings. Training uses two complementary losses: a pixel-wise MSE normal-map reconstruction loss on the patch tokens (a lightweight linear decoder unpatchifies them to a surface-normal map) and a supervised contrastive loss (SCL, τ = 0.07) on the class token that pulls together same-geometry contacts from different sensors and pushes apart different geometries. The model is pre-trained on a 1 M-sample synthetic dataset generated via physics-based rendering in Blender, spanning 100 parameterized sensor configurations (light shape/orientation/angle/color, gel stiffness/specularity, camera FOV, sensing area) and 10 K contact geometries from 50 household-object meshes, with each geometry rendered under every sensor to form SCL-aligned pairs. At inference the encoder is frozen and only a task-specific head is trained on a single sensor, enabling zero-shot transfer to all other sensors in the set.

## Results

On the 16-class object classification task, SITR achieves 90.23% intra-sensor transfer accuracy and 81.94% inter-sensor transfer accuracy, substantially outperforming all baselines (best baseline: ViT-Large Pre-trained at 78.38% intra and 54.34% inter). On the 3-DoF pose estimation task, SITR reduces inter-sensor RMSE to 0.80 mm, roughly 50% lower than the best baseline (ViT-Large Pre-trained at 1.49 mm). In the no-transfer (upper-bound) setting, SITR reaches 99.72% classification accuracy and 0.51 mm pose RMSE, indicating near-perfect same-sensor performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SITR dominates all three baseline families on zero-shot transfer. Against ViT baselines (scratch and ImageNet-pretrained), SITR gains +11.85% intra-sensor and +27.60% inter-sensor classification accuracy over the strongest ViT-Large Pre-trained, while also reducing pose estimation RMSE from 1.49 mm to 0.80 mm. Against T3 (Zhao et al., 2024), which uses category-specific encoders, SITR achieves 90.23% vs. 38.66% intra-sensor and 81.94% vs. N/A inter-sensor classification, and 0.80 mm vs. 1.7 mm Wedge→Mini pose RMSE. Against UniT (Xu et al., 2024), which only supports intra-sensor transfer among GelSight Minis, SITR reaches 90.23% vs. 46.39% intra-sensor and extends to inter-sensor transfer (81.94%) where UniT is not applicable. Notably, all baselines perform well in the no-transfer setting (92–99%), confirming that the gap is specifically in cross-sensor generalization, which is SITR's core contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ViT architecture and supervised contrastive learning (SCL) to address a specific gap in tactile representation learning: the inability of prior methods to transfer zero-shot to unseen sensors. It departs from T3 (Zhao et al., 2024), which trained category-specific encoder-decoder pairs per sensor-task combination, and UniT (Xu et al., 2024), which encoded sensor types as fixed tokens, by instead using per-sensor calibration images and a large-scale physics-based synthetic dataset to learn truly sensor-invariant features. The key conceptual shift is treating sensor variance as a continuous, calibratable property rather than a discrete category, enabling generalization to sensors never seen during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to transfer tactile models zero-shot across sensors addresses a major practical bottleneck in robotic manipulation research, where collecting labeled data for every sensor variant is prohibitively expensive. The 1M-sample synthetic dataset with 100 sensor configurations is a reusable resource for the community. However, the approach is currently limited to flat-pad optical sensors in the GelSight family; the DIGIT sensor's distinct optical design degrades performance, and extending to array-based force sensors or complex geometries (GelSight Svelte, DIGIT 360) remains open. The finding that SCL significantly aids classification transfer but has negligible effect on pose estimation suggests the two task types rely on fundamentally different aspects of the representation.
