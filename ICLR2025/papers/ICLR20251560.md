## Problem

Multi-view 3D room layout estimation from unposed perspective images remains largely unexplored due to the difficulty of wide-baseline sparse-view structure-from-motion, which traditionally requires multi-step camera pose estimation, feature matching, and triangulation. Existing methods either assume known camera poses, operate on single-view or panorama inputs, or rely on incremental stitching that limits scalability. The scarcity of annotated multi-view layout datasets (Structure3D provides only 2–5 views per room) further hinders progress in this setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2–5 unposed indoor RGB perspective images at 512×512 resolution with no camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or even view overlap required. It outputs a sparse 3D room layout: a set of structural planes parameterized by unit normal and offset, 3D intersection lines, junction points, and a binary adjacency matrix encoding which planes intersect. A dense per-pixel pointmap (H×W×3) is an intermediate representation that is reduced to these compact plane parameters via a 2D plane detector and post-processing, avoiding the storage overhead of retaining all 3D points.

## Method

The pipeline combines three components: (1) a retrained HRNet-based 2D plane/line detector (Noncuboid), (2) Plane-DUSt3R, and (3) a post-processing algorithm. Plane-DUSt3R fine-tunes DUSt3R's ViT encoder-frozen, decoder-and-DPT-head-unfrozen architecture on occlusion-free structural plane depth maps (walls, floors, ceilings only) rather than full-scene depth, so the predicted pointmap ignores furniture and other non-structural objects. The scale-invariant regression loss is modified for a metric-scale variant by fixing the predicted scale to the ground-truth scale. Global alignment across all image pairs is performed via a minimum-spanning-tree-guided optimization that jointly estimates per-image camera poses and a common world-coordinate pointmap. Post-processing lifts 2D planes to 3D using normals computed from the pointmap, then merges corresponding walls across views by projecting them onto the floor-defined x-z plane and solving a minimum-cut graph problem where edges encode proximity, category (horizontal/vertical), and non-crossing constraints.

## Results

On the Structure3D test set, the full pipeline (Ours aligned) achieves 76.84% re-IoU, 9.53 re-EE, 0.4099 re-RMSE, 52.63% 3D-precision, and 48.37% 3D-recall, outperforming both the Noncuboid+MASt3R baseline (37.00% 3D-precision, 43.39% 3D-recall) and the Noncuboid+GT pose ablation (46.96% 3D-precision, 50.59% 3D-recall) on 5 of 6 metrics. For the 3D correspondence sub-task (Plane-DUSt3R), the method achieves 91.80 mAA@30 on Structure3D, surpassing MASt3R (85.34) and DUSt3R (76.13), though it trails MASt3R on RRA@15 (97.95 vs. 99.94) and RTA@15 (94.96 vs. 99.00). The retrained 2D detector (Noncuboid re-trained) reaches 80.18% IoU and 6.13% PE on Structure3D, the best among compared 2D plane detectors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Noncuboid+MASt3R baseline, the proposed pipeline (Ours aligned) wins on all six metrics, with the largest gains in 3D-precision (+15.63 pp) and re-EE (−3.19). Against the stronger Noncuboid+GT pose ablation (which uses ground-truth camera poses), Ours aligned still leads on re-IoU (+0.91 pp), re-EE (−1.84), re-RMSE (−0.036), and 3D-precision (+5.67 pp), but trails on 3D-recall (48.37% vs. 50.59%), suggesting the GT-pose variant captures slightly more planes at the cost of precision. For the 3D correspondence sub-task (Table 2), Plane-DUSt3R (aligned) outperforms MASt3R on mAA@30 on Structure3D (91.80 vs. 85.34, +6.46 pp) and DUSt3R (76.13, +15.67 pp), but loses to MASt3R on RRA@15 (97.95 vs. 99.94) and RTA@15 (94.96 vs. 99.00), indicating that fine-tuning for structural planes improves overall angular accuracy at the expense of per-axis pose precision. On the 2D detection front (Table 3), the retrained Noncuboid detector achieves the best IoU (80.18%) and PE (6.13%) among all compared 2D methods, marginally improving over the original Noncuboid (79.94% IoU) and Planar R-CNN (79.64% IoU).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the DUSt3R 3D foundation model (Wang et al., 2024) for room layout estimation by fine-tuning it on occlusion-free structural plane depth maps, shifting the paradigm from traditional multi-step SfM (feature matching, triangulation, pose optimization) to an end-to-end pointmap regression. It combines this with the Noncuboid single-view layout estimator (Yang et al., 2022) as a 2D detector front-end, but departs from prior multi-view layout work (Wang et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2022) by handling unposed perspective images rather than panoramas or posed inputs. The key conceptual departure is treating correspondence establishment and 3D lifting as a single joint prediction problem solved by the fine-tuned foundation model, rather than as separate pipeline stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first pipeline to handle unposed, sparse-view (2–5 images) perspective room layout estimation, addressing a gap that has persisted due to the difficulty of wide-baseline SfM in this setting. The practical impact is significant for applications like robotic navigation and AR where only a handful of uncalibrated photos are available. Limitations include reliance on synthetic Structure3D for training, the assumption that walls are perpendicular to floor/ceiling in the merging step, and evaluation primarily on synthetic data with limited real-world quantitative benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger multi-room scenes, non-rectangular layouts, and whether the structural-plane fine-tuning objective transfers to other 3D foundation models as they evolve.
