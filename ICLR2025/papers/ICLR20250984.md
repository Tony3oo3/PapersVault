## Problem

Light decoder-based solvers for VRPs, which use static node embeddings computed once by a heavy encoder, suffer from poor out-of-distribution generalization to larger instances and unseen VRP variants. The paper identifies the root cause: the encoder is forced to encode information for all potential future decision contexts into a single set of static embeddings (high information density), while the overly simplistic decoder cannot effectively leverage this dense information, creating a bottleneck that worsens as problem complexity increases.

## Input / Output

The model takes a VRP instance defined as a graph G with N+1 nodes (one depot, N customers), where each node has raw features (2D coordinates, demand, time windows, etc.) projected into d_h-dimensional embeddings. The encoder produces a fixed set of static node embeddings H^(L) that serve as keys and values throughout all decoding steps. The decoder sequentially selects the next node at each step, conditioned only on the immediately previous node and dynamic state variables (e.g., remaining capacity), outputting a probability distribution over feasible unvisited nodes. The final output is a complete feasible solution (sequence of sub-tours for CVRP, single tour for TSP/ATSP) with an associated tour length.

## Method

ReLD enhances the POMO decoder with two minimal architectural additions that transform it into a single-query transformer block: (1) an identity mapping (residual connection) that directly adds the last-visited node embedding and projected dynamic features to the MHA output, ensuring context information is not solely mediated through attention weights; and (2) a feed-forward network with residual connection applied after the MHA layer to introduce non-linearity into the query vector computation. A negative logarithmic distance heuristic is also added to the compatibility layer to incorporate spatial proximity. Training employs varying instance sizes (Uniform(40,100)) and vehicle capacities sampled from a triangular distribution to improve OOD robustness. The key insight is that the encoder's static embeddings already contain valuable sub-problem information, but the original decoder's purely linear attention-weighted combination cannot effectively interpret this dense representation—adding non-linearity and direct context injection gives the decoder sufficient capacity to filter irrelevant information and adapt to the current sub-problem, thereby reducing the burden on the encoder.

## Results

On synthetic CVRP instances, ReLD (augx8) achieves gaps of 0.960%, 1.654%, 2.975%, and 6.757% on CVRP100, CVRP200, CVRP500, and CVRP1000 respectively, substantially outperforming POMO (1.004%, 3.403%, 11.135%, 110.632%) and ELG (1.207%, 2.553%, 5.472%, 10.760%) at all scales. On CVRPLib Set-X (100 real-world instances), ReLD-MoEL+ achieves a total gap of 3.54%, compared to 6.03% for ELG and 11.52% for POMO. On CVRPLib Set-XXL (instances up to 16K nodes), ReLD achieves gaps of 7.45%–15.59%, outperforming ELG (10.70%–20.05%) and all other neural solvers by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReLD decisively outperforms POMO (its direct base) at all scales: on CVRP500 the gap drops from 11.135% to 2.975%, and on CVRP1000 from 110.632% to 6.757%. Against ELG (Gao et al., 2024), ReLD wins on CVRP200 (1.654% vs 2.553%), CVRP500 (2.975% vs 5.472%), and CVRP1000 (6.757% vs 10.760%), while ELG retains a slight edge on CVRP100 (1.207% vs 0.960% is actually ReLD winning). On CVRPLib Set-X, ReLD-MoEL+ (3.54% total) beats ELG (6.03%), POMO (11.52%), MVMoE (11.90%), and MTL (12.51%), and also surpasses the heavy-decoder LEHD (12.52%) and BQ (9.94%). However, on CVRP1000 ReLD (6.757%) still trails the heavy-decoder LEHD (4.912%) and BQ (5.892%), confirming a residual gap for very large instances. On CVRPLib Set-XXL (3K–16K nodes), ReLD (7.45%–15.59%) outperforms ELG (10.70%–20.05%), LEHD (14.04%–40.76%), BQ (11.21%–24.00%), and GLOP-LKH3 (16.60%–27.50%), but remains behind the non-learning HGS solver. In cross-problem settings (16 VRP variants), ReLD-MoEL achieves an average gap of 3.842% overall, beating MVMoE (4.596%), MVMoE-L (4.712%), and POMO-MTL (4.968%), and even surpasses LKH3 on VRPL (−0.016% gap). Traditional solvers HGS and LKH3 remain the gold standard on small instances (negative gaps for HGS), but ReLD matches or exceeds them in speed by orders of magnitude (seconds vs. hours).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on POMO (Kwon et al., 2020) and its multi-trajectory strategy, modifying the decoder with an identity mapping and a feed-forward layer to form ReLD. It positions itself within the light-decoder vs. heavy-decoder paradigm debate initiated by LEHD (Luo et al., 2023) and BQ (Drakulic et al., 2023), arguing that the bottleneck in light decoder solvers is the overly simplistic decoder rather than the encoder's static embeddings. The multi-task and MoE variants (ReLD-MTL, ReLD-MoEL) extend MVMoE (Zhou et al., 2024b), and the ATSP experiments build on MatNet (Kwon et al., 2021). A key conceptual departure is the analogy to KV cache in LLMs, reframing static embeddings as a form of cached computation that introduces irrelevant information in the selective (rather than generative) setting of combinatorial optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a two-line architectural change (identity mapping + FF layer) substantially narrows the OOD generalization gap between light and heavy decoder paradigms while preserving the computational efficiency that makes light decoder methods attractive for real-time deployment. However, ReLD still trails heavy decoder solvers like LEHD and BQ on very large instances (CVRP1000+), confirming an inherent performance-efficiency trade-off. Open questions include whether the unified layer-sharing framework (Appendix B) can be exploited to find optimal intermediate points between the two paradigms, and whether further decoder depth (as in ReLD-Large) continues to yield gains or hits diminishing returns.
