## Problem

Existing accelerated diffusion model convergence guarantees apply only to restrictive target classes: smoothness conditions imposed on all intermediate densities q_t along the entire sampling path, or bounded support for non-smooth targets. This paper asks whether accelerated O(ε^{-1}) convergence can be achieved for much broader distribution classes—smoothness only on the target Q_0, finite variance instead of bounded support, and Gaussian mixtures—while simultaneously reducing the dimensional dependency from O(d^3) to O(d^{1.5}).

## Input / Output

The sampler starts from Gaussian noise x_T ~ N(0, I_d) and iteratively applies reverse steps using trained score estimates s_t (from standard score matching) and Hessian estimates H_t (from a novel Hessian matching objective that regresses on W_t W_t^T / (1 - ᾱ_t)). The output is a sample x_0 whose distribution approximates the target Q_0, with convergence measured in KL/TV distance for smooth targets and in Wasserstein-2 distance (via early stopping) for non-smooth targets with finite variance.

## Method

The paper designs a Hessian-based accelerated stochastic DDPM sampler where the reverse-step covariance is set to Σ_t(x_t) = (1-α_t)/α_t (I_d + (1-α_t)∇²log q_t(x_t)), incorporating the Hessian of the log-density to correct the conditional variance at each step. A novel Hessian matching training objective, analogous to score matching, is derived to estimate ∇²log q_t without access to the true density. The analysis introduces a tilting factor representation of the log-likelihood ratio between the true posterior and the approximate reverse kernel, then uses an extended Tweedie's formula to compute the posterior moments needed for Taylor expansion. The key insight is that the Hessian-based variance correction cancels the dominant second-order Taylor term in the tilting factor, shifting the rate-determining term to third order and yielding O(ε^{-1}) convergence instead of O(ε^{-2}). This framework yields convergence guarantees for Gaussian mixtures, finite-variance targets with early stopping, and targets with Lipschitz Hessian log-density, all with O(d^{1.5}) dimensional dependency.

## Results

The paper establishes accelerated convergence rates of O(ε^{-1}) (vs. O(ε^{-2}) for vanilla DDPM) for three broad target distribution classes: (i) Gaussian mixtures with O(d^{1.5}N^{1.5}/ε) steps, (ii) any finite-variance distribution with early-stopping at O(d^{1.5}log^{1.5}(1/δ)/ε) steps, and (iii) distributions with M-Lipschitz Hessian of log-density at O(d^{1.5}log^{1.5}M/ε) steps. The O(d^{1.5}) dimensional dependency improves upon the O(d^3) or O(d^2) dependencies of prior accelerated samplers for bounded-support targets. Empirically (Figure 1, N=4, d=4), the Hessian-accelerated sampler achieves the best non-asymptotic convergence among all compared methods at similar computation levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the regular DDPM sampler (Ho et al., 2020), the proposed Hessian-accelerated sampler improves the convergence rate from O(ε^{-2}) to O(ε^{-1}), a factor of O(ε^{-1}) reduction in steps. Against the accelerated stochastic sampler of Li et al. (2024a), the proposed method reduces the dimensional dependency from O(d³) to O(d^{1.5}) for bounded-support targets and extends the guarantee to the much broader finite-varance class. Versus deterministic PF-ODE samplers (Li et al., 2024c;d; Huang et al., 2024a), the proposed stochastic sampler achieves the same O(ε^{-1}) rate while requiring only smoothness on Q₀ (not all intermediate Q_t) and supporting unbounded-support distributions. In the empirical comparison (Figure 1, Gaussian mixture with N=4, d=4), the Hessian-accelerated sampler (red) converges fastest in the non-asymptotic regime, outperforming both the regular DDPM (blue), the Li et al. (2024a) accelerated stochastic sampler (cyan), and the PF-ODE deterministic sampler at comparable computation budgets. The paper also provides the first accelerated convergence guarantee for Gaussian mixture targets, a class not previously covered by any accelerated sampler.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the accelerated stochastic sampler of Li et al. (2024c), which first introduced Hessian-based variance correction for the reverse process but assumed perfect estimators and bounded-support targets. The key departure is a novel analytical technique—expressing the per-step reverse error via a tilting factor and evaluating its expectation through an extended Tweedie's formula—that replaces the SDE-type, ODE-type, and typical-set analyses used in prior convergence proofs. This new framework enables the authors to prove accelerated O(ε⁻¹) convergence under substantially weaker distributional assumptions (smoothness only on Q₀, finite variance instead of bounded support, and Gaussian mixtures) while also improving the dimensional dependency from O(d³) to O(d^1.5) for bounded-support targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first accelerated convergence guarantees for Gaussian mixtures and for general finite-variance targets, this paper removes two of the most restrictive assumptions in the accelerated diffusion literature, making the theory applicable to a much wider range of practical generative modeling scenarios. The Hessian matching estimator provides a concrete training procedure for the second-order information needed by the accelerated sampler, though the empirical validation is limited to small-scale Gaussian mixture experiments (d=4, N=4). Open questions include whether the d^1.5 dependency can be further reduced, whether the Hessian estimation overhead is practical at scale, and whether the tilting-factor/Tweedie analysis framework extends to other acceleration schemes or flow-matching models.
