## Problem

Despite the empirical success of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), its convergence properties in non-convex settings remained poorly understood: the role of gradient normalization was unclear, prior analyses relied on restrictive bounded variance or bounded gradient assumptions, and SAM and its unnormalized variant (USAM) required fundamentally different proof techniques with divergent convergence guarantees. No unified framework existed to analyze both variants under a single set of assumptions or to extend the analysis to non-uniform sampling strategies such as importance sampling.

## Input / Output

The method operates on model parameters x ∈ R^d for a finite-sum objective, consuming unbiased stochastic gradient estimates g(x) (e.g., mini-batch gradients of size τ) and a perturbation radius ρ to produce updated parameters at each iteration. For empirical validation on image classification, standard 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with their class labels serve as input, and the trained network outputs class predictions. The optimization framework also supports arbitrary sampling distributions over the n component functions, including single-element sampling with non-uniform probabilities and τ-nice mini-batch sampling.

## Method

The core contribution is Unified SAM, an update rule that forms a convex combination of SAM (λ=1) and USAM (λ=0) via a parameter λ ∈ [0,1], controlling the degree of gradient normalization in the perturbation step: x^{t+1} = x^t − γ_t ∇f_{S_t}(x^t + ρ_t(1−λ_t + λ_t/‖∇f_{S_t}(x^t)‖)∇f_{S_t}(x^t)). The analysis replaces the standard bounded variance assumption with the weaker Expected Residual (ER) condition, which holds automatically for smooth non-convex problems under mild sampling assumptions, and leverages a stochastic reformulation of the finite-sum problem to accommodate arbitrary sampling strategies. This yields the first convergence guarantees for SAM under importance sampling (with probabilities p_i = L_i/ΣL_j) and the first decreasing-step-size result for normalized SAM. For PL functions, the paper proves linear convergence to a neighborhood with constant step sizes and O(1/t) convergence to the exact solution with decreasing step sizes; for general non-convex functions, it establishes sublinear convergence to an ε-stationary point. A key finding is that in the deterministic setting, USAM converges to the exact solution while SAM converges only to a neighborhood, a distinction previously noted only informally.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WRN-28-10, Unified SAM achieves a best test accuracy of 95.99% (ρ=0.3, λ_t=1−1/t), outperforming SGD (95.35%) by 0.64 points. On CIFAR-100 with the same model, the best accuracy is 81.71% (ρ=0.3, λ_t=1−1/t), a 11.01-point improvement over SGD (70.70%). A key empirical finding is that the optimal λ is not always 0 (USAM) or 1 (SAM); instead, the dynamic schedule λ_t=1−1/t (starting as USAM and transitioning toward SAM) consistently yields the best or near-best results across all datasets and models tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Unified SAM (varying λ ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 1/t, 1−1/t} and ρ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4}) against plain SGD on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 using WRN-28-10, ResNet-18, and PreActResNet-18. Unified SAM consistently outperforms SGD: on CIFAR-10 the best Unified SAM configuration (95.99%) beats SGD (95.35%) by ~0.6 points, while on CIFAR-100 the gap is much larger (81.71% vs. 70.70%, a ~11-point margin). Within Unified SAM, pure USAM (λ=0) never achieves the best performance in any experiment, and pure SAM (λ=1) is also not always optimal; the dynamic schedule λ_t=1−1/t (starting as USAM, transitioning to SAM) is the most reliable top performer. For the Unified VaSSO extension, λ_t=1−1/t again produces the best accuracy (97.06% on CIFAR-10, 83.66% on CIFAR-100 with WRN-28-10), confirming that a λ value different from 1 (i.e., not pure normalized SAM) is preferable in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of SAM convergence analysis—normalized SAM (Foret et al., 2021) and unnormalized USAM (Andriushchenko & Flammarion, 2022)—under a single convex-combination parameter λ, and replaces the bounded variance assumption (used in all prior SAM convergence results) with the more natural Expected Residual condition from the SGD literature (Gower et al., 2021; Khaled & Richtárik, 2020). The arbitrary sampling framework from Gower et al. (2019) is leveraged to cover importance sampling and τ-nice mini-batching, yielding the first importance-sampling guarantees for any SAM variant. The unified approach is further extended to the variance-suppressed VaSSO method (Li & Giannakis, 2023), showing the framework's generality beyond the original SAM/USAM dichotomy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ER condition is strictly weaker than bounded variance and holds automatically for smooth finite-sum problems, making the convergence guarantees applicable in settings where prior SAM analyses simply did not apply. The deterministic result—that SAM (λ=1) provably fails to converge to the exact solution while USAM (λ=0) does—clarifies a practical concern that had only been observed empirically. Open questions include finding the optimal λ for specific problem classes, extending to adaptive or Hessian-regularized step sizes, and studying Unified SAM in distributed and federated settings. The practical finding that λ_t = 1−1/t (starting as USAM and transitioning toward SAM) consistently outperforms both endpoints suggests a useful default for practitioners.
