## Problem

There is a persistent conflict in the diffusion model literature regarding which timesteps present harder denoising tasks: some works argue larger timesteps are more difficult, while others contend smaller timesteps pose greater challenges. This discrepancy impedes accurate interpretation of prior analyses and hinders the development of training methods that properly exploit timestep-wise difficulty variation. The paper resolves this conflict through convergence and relative entropy analyses, then leverages the resolved difficulty ordering to design a curriculum training strategy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard diffusion model inputs: Gaussian noise optionally conditioned on class labels (ImageNet, CIFAR-10) or CLIP-encoded text prompts (MS-COCO), producing images at resolutions from 32×32 to 256×256. The novelty lies not in the input/output format but in the training procedure: instead of sampling timesteps uniformly at each iteration, the model is trained in a sequential curriculum that progressively introduces harder denoising tasks, with the final phase reverting to standard simultaneous training across all timesteps.

## Method

The paper first establishes that denoising at smaller timesteps is harder by showing (1) models trained on lower timesteps converge more slowly in both loss and FID, and (2) the KL divergence D_KL(p_{t-1}||p_t) decreases as t increases, indicating greater distributional change at early timesteps. Building on this, the method divides the timestep range into N clusters using SNR-based interval boundaries (or quantile-based boundaries for EDM's log-normal noise schedule), then trains the model in N sequential stages where each stage adds the next harder cluster, progressing from the highest (easiest) timesteps to the lowest (hardest). An adaptive pacing function with a patience parameter τ controls stage transitions: the model advances to the next curriculum stage when the training loss fails to improve for τ consecutive iterations. After all N stages complete, training continues across all timesteps as in standard diffusion training. The approach is model-agnostic (validated on DiT, EDM, and SiT) and orthogonal to existing techniques like MinSNR loss weighting and DTR architecture.

## Results

The proposed curriculum learning approach consistently improves FID across all model architectures and tasks. On ImageNet 256×256 with DiT-L, FID drops from 11.18 (Vanilla) to 8.18 (Ours), with IS improving from 146.95 to 186.37. On FFHQ 256×256 with DiT-B, FID improves from 10.49 to 7.55, and with EDM on FFHQ 64×64, FID improves from 2.93 to 2.71. The method also accelerates convergence, achieving the 400k-iteration FID of 8.18 where Vanilla requires 2M iterations to reach 7.84 on ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between three training variants: Vanilla (standard simultaneous training), NaiveCL (fixed-iteration curriculum without SNR clustering), and the proposed method (SNR-based clustering with adaptive pacing). The proposed method wins on every dataset and architecture, while NaiveCL is inconsistent—e.g., on ImageNet with DiT, NaiveCL actually degrades FID from 11.18 to 11.90, whereas Ours achieves 8.18. Regarding orthogonality with existing techniques (Table 5, DiT-B/2 on ImageNet): MinSNR loss weighting [Hang et al., 2023] reduces FID from 30.27 to 21.88, and DTR architecture [Park et al., 2024b] further reduces it to 15.77; combining either with the proposed curriculum yields additional gains (19.36 and 15.33 respectively), confirming that the curriculum approach is complementary rather than redundant. The paper also shows that larger models benefit more: DiT-S improves 8% in FID, DiT-B improves 24%, and DiT-L improves 27%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the curriculum learning framework (Bengio et al., 2009; Hacohen & Weinshall, 2019) and adapts it to the specific structure of diffusion model training, where the 'difficulty' axis is the denoising timestep rather than data complexity. It departs from prior work that applied curriculum learning to diffusion models by ordering data types (Tang et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023a) or discretization schedules (consistency models), instead ordering the timesteps themselves from easy to hard. The SNR-based clustering strategy is borrowed from Go et al. (2023a), and the pacing mechanism draws on the varied exponential pacing of Hacohen & Weinshall (2019), but the key conceptual departure is resolving the long-standing conflict in the literature over whether large or small timesteps are harder to denoise, settling definitively on small timesteps via convergence and relative-entropy analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a genuine and persistent conflict in the diffusion literature (Ho et al., 2020 and Hang et al., 2023 vs. Karras et al., 2022 and Kim et al., 2022) with a unified empirical and distributional explanation, which clarifies the interpretation of many prior timestep-dependent analyses. The practical impact is notable: a training-order change with no architectural modification or extra parameters yields 24–27% FID improvements on ImageNet with DiT, and the method is orthogonal to loss weighting (MinSNR) and architectural routing (DTR), making it a drop-in addition to existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video and 3D generation, whether the fixed patience parameter τ can be replaced by self-pacing, and whether the SNR-based clustering is truly necessary or whether simpler uniform splits suffice at scale.
