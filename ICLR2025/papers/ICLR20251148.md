## Problem

Computing Optimal Transport between probability measures on the hypersphere S^d is computationally prohibitive due to supercubic scaling. Existing spherical sliced Wasserstein variants project measures onto 1D great semicircles or circles, which loses topological information of the integration domain and, in the circle case, requires an additional binary search. The paper asks whether the tree-sliced approach—replacing 1D lines with tree structures to capture richer topology while preserving closed-form OT solutions—can be adapted to the sphere.

## Input / Output

The method takes two discrete probability distributions on S^d, each represented as a weighted sum of Dirac masses at n support points (with weights u_j and v_j), and produces a scalar STSW distance via Monte Carlo averaging over L randomly sampled spherical trees. Each tree has k edges (spherical rays) and a root, all sampled uniformly from S^d and S^{d-1} respectively. The per-tree computation is a closed-form sum over n sorted angular distances and k edges, making the overall pipeline highly parallelizable across the L trees.

## Method

The core construction defines spherical trees by gluing k great semicircles (spherical rays) at a common root on S^d, yielding a metric space with a tree metric that admits closed-form 1-Wasserstein. A Spherical Radon Transform on these trees projects a measure from S^d onto a tree by distributing each point's mass across the k edges according to a splitting map α, which is a softmax of O(d+1)-invariant arc-length distances from the point to each edge. The O(d+1)-invariance of α guarantees both injectivity of the transform and orthogonal invariance of the resulting STSW distance. The per-tree Wasserstein is computed in closed form as a sum over edges of (edge length) × |cumulative mass difference in the subtree beyond that edge|, and the final STSW is a Monte Carlo average over L independently sampled trees. This yields a metric on P(S^d) that is invariant under orthogonal transformations and computable in O(L·n·k) time per evaluation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 self-supervised learning, STSW achieves 80.53% encoded-feature accuracy and 76.78% projected-feature accuracy, outperforming the best baseline ARI-S3W(5) (80.08% / 75.12%) while being the fastest at 9.54 s/epoch. In the gradient flow task (learning a 12-component vMF target), STSW attains the best log W₂ (4.69±0.01) and NLL (5041.13±0.84) with a runtime of only 1.89 s, compared to 20.25 s for ARI-S3W(30). For Earth density estimation, STSW achieves the lowest NLL on all three datasets (Quake: 0.68, Flood: 1.23, Fire: −0.07) in half the training time of ARI-S3W(50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STSW consistently outperforms all six baselines across every task. Against SSW (Bonet et al., 2022), STSW gains +10.30 pp encoded accuracy and +12.45 pp projected accuracy on CIFAR-10, and reduces NLL from 0.84 to 0.68 on Quake density estimation. Against S3W (Tran et al., 2024b), STSW improves encoded accuracy by +1.94 pp and projected accuracy by +2.95 pp on CIFAR-10, while being ~5× faster in the gradient flow task (1.89 s vs. 20.25 s for ARI-S3W(30)). Against the Hypersphere baseline (Wang & Isola, 2020), STSW gains +0.72 pp encoded and +2.14 pp projected accuracy. Against SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020), STSW achieves +0.56 pp encoded and +3.98 pp projected accuracy. Against SW (Bonneel et al., 2015), STSW improves by +6.14 pp encoded and +8.98 pp projected on CIFAR-10. Against the Stereo normalizing flow (Dinh et al., 2016), STSW reduces NLL by 1.26 on Quake, 0.69 on Flood, and 1.38 on Fire, all with fewer training epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tree-Sliced Wasserstein (TSW) framework from Euclidean space to the hypersphere by introducing spherical trees as integration domains, analogous to how TSW replaced 1D lines with tree systems in R^d (Tran et al., 2025b,d). It departs from prior spherical sliced Wasserstein methods (SSW via semicircle transforms, S3W via stereographic projection) by avoiding the topological information loss inherent in projecting spherical measures onto 1D curves. The construction of a Spherical Radon Transform on spherical trees with O(d+1)-invariant splitting maps is a new theoretical contribution that enables injectivity and orthogonal invariance of the resulting distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STSW provides a practically efficient (closed-form, highly parallelizable) metric for spherical measures that consistently outperforms or matches recent baselines across gradient flow, self-supervised learning, density estimation, and autoencoding tasks, while being roughly 10× faster than the strongest S3W variant. The orthogonal invariance guarantee is a clean theoretical property that aligns with the natural symmetry of spherical data. Open questions include developing more principled sampling strategies for spherical trees, adapting Generalized Radon Transforms to the spherical setting, and whether the framework extends to other manifolds beyond the sphere.
