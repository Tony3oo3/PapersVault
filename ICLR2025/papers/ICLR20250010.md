## Problem

Conformal prediction sets built from a single Bayesian model retain valid coverage under misspecification but lose their volume optimality, and selecting the correct model among many candidates is inherently difficult. No existing method combines conformity scores from multiple candidate Bayesian models to construct a single conformal prediction set that is both valid and asymptotically efficient under model uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full training set Z_{1:n} (no data splitting), a test covariate X_{n+1}, a miscoverage level α, and for each of K candidate models: MCMC posterior samples θ_k^{1:T} and BMA posterior model probabilities p(M_k|Z_{1:n}). It outputs a single conformal prediction set C_α^{CBMA}(X_{n+1}) defined on a grid of candidate y-values, along with individual per-model conformal sets. Unusually, the aggregation weights are data-dependent on both the training data and the candidate response y at X_{n+1}, so the combined conformity score is computed per candidate y rather than once per data point.

## Method

For each candidate model M_k, conformity scores are computed as posterior predictive densities via Add-One-In importance sampling over MCMC posterior samples (following Fong & Holmes, 2021). These per-model scores are then aggregated into a combined score σ_i^{CBMA} = Σ_k q_k(Z_{1:n}, Z_{n+1}) σ_i^{M_k}, where the weights q_k are proportional to the product of the posterior model probability and the model's predictive posterior density at the test point. The key theoretical insight (Lemma 1) is that this weighted combination equals the posterior predictive density under a hierarchical Bayesian model in which the model index itself is a random variable, which immediately establishes exchangeability and hence validity of the conformity score. Theorem 2 then proves that when the true model lies in the model space, the CBMA prediction set's expected volume converges to that of the optimal conformal Bayes set under the true model as n→∞, leveraging posterior concentration results from Le & Clarke (2022).

## Results

In the Hermite polynomial approximation experiment (n=100, θ=1, target coverage 0.80), CBMA achieves a mean prediction interval length of 2.634, outperforming the best individual conformal Bayes set (CBM4, length 2.667) and BMA (length 3.122), while maintaining valid coverage (0.792). In the quadratic model (n=100), CBMA matches the true-model conformal Bayes set (length 0.535 vs. 0.534) while incorporating model uncertainty. On the California Housing dataset, CBMA achieves the shortest interval lengths at n=150, and compared to the majority-vote aggregation strategy of Gasparin & Ramdas (2024b), CBMA intervals are 7.7%–14.6% shorter across sample sizes n=50, 100, 150.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against individual conformal Bayes sets (Fong & Holmes, 2021), CBMA achieves shorter mean interval lengths in the Hermite polynomial experiment (2.634 vs. best 2.667 for CBM4) and matches the true-model CB set in the quadratic experiment (0.535 vs. 0.534 for CBM1), while additionally accounting for model uncertainty. Against BMA prediction sets, CBMA is substantially shorter (2.634 vs. 3.122 in Hermite; 0.535 vs. 0.537 in quadratic) because BMA sets overcover (coverage 0.831 vs. target 0.80) whereas CBMA maintains valid frequentist coverage. Against the majority-vote conformal aggregation of Gasparin & Ramdas (2024b), CBMA produces intervals that are 7.7%, 12.0%, and 14.6% shorter at n=50, 100, and 150 respectively on the California Housing data, because CBMA learns data-dependent aggregation weights from the same training data rather than relying on a fixed voting rule that inflates set size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005), conformal Bayes (Fong & Holmes, 2021; Hoff, 2023), and Bayesian model averaging (Raftery et al., 1997; Hoeting et al., 1999). While Hoff (2023) established that full conformal Bayes yields minimum-expected-volume prediction sets under a correctly specified model, and Fong & Holmes (2021) provided scalable algorithms for constructing such sets, both assume a single fixed Bayesian model. The paper departs from recent conformal set aggregation approaches (Gasparin & Ramdas, 2024b; Yang & Kuchibhotla, 2024) that merge prediction sets or p-values and suffer coverage loss or require data splitting, instead proposing to average conformity scores (posterior predictive densities) weighted by posterior model probabilities—a construction the authors show is itself a valid conformity score via a hierarchical model interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a gap the authors identify as 'underexplored' in the conformal prediction literature: how to construct efficient prediction sets when the underlying Bayesian model is uncertain. The theoretical guarantee that CBMA prediction sets converge to the optimal conformal Bayes set (under the true model) as n→∞, combined with no data splitting and no coverage loss in aggregation, makes this a practically attractive framework for small-sample Bayesian settings. Limitations include dependence on MCMC quality, computational cost scaling with the number of candidate models, and the fact that the KL-divergence-based guarantee (Remark 3) when the true model is absent from the model space is only a near-optimality statement without explicit rate. Open questions include scalability to very large model spaces and extension to non-Bayesian or frequentist base models.
