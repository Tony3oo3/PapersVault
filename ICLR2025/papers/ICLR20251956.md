## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from hallucinations driven by modality priors—parametric biases in the visual encoder and LLM backbone that distort attention-based alignment of multimodal inputs. Existing decoding-based mitigation methods (e.g., VCD, OPERA) rely on statistical correlations and heuristic contrastive signals without modeling the causal relationship between attention mechanisms, modality priors, and model output, leaving the confounding influence of priors unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251956/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free, plug-and-play decoding intervention on any MLLM (tested on LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2-VL, InstructBLIP, and Chameleon). It consumes a standard RGB image and a text prompt, then during autoregressive generation it replaces attention maps in selected layers of the visual encoder and/or LLM backbone with counterfactual alternatives before computing the next-token logits. The output is a standard generated text response; no architectural modification or retraining is required, and the method can be stacked with other decoding strategies.

## Method

CAUSALMM constructs a structural causal model in which visual priors (P_v) and language priors (P_l) act as confounders on the causal paths from visual attention (A_i) and LLM attention (A_t) to the model output (O). Applying the back-door criterion, the method isolates the causal effect of each attention mechanism by intervening with four counterfactual attention states—random, uniform, reversed, and shuffled—computed at selected layers of the visual encoder and LLM. Three variants are defined: vision-only, language-only, and multimodal collaborative, where the latter sums the causal effects from both modalities. At each decoding step, the next token is selected via a modified softmax that augments the original logits with a confidence-weighted difference between the observed and counterfactual logits (γ·(ℓ_i − ℓ_cf)), effectively penalizing tokens whose probability is inflated by prior-driven attention rather than genuine multimodal evidence.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark (MSCOCO, A-OKVQA, GQA) with LLaVA-1.5, CAUSALMM Multimodal achieves an average metric improvement of 5.37% over the regular decoding baseline across random, popular, and adversarial settings. On VLind-Bench, the multimodal collaborative variant yields a maximum score improvement of 65.3% across six indicators (143.7 points total), and on the MME Benchmark, CAUSALMM improves the score by 164 points. The method is plug-and-play and requires no retraining of model weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAUSALMM Multimodal consistently outperforms VCD across all POPE settings, e.g., on MSCOCO Random it achieves 88.93% accuracy vs. VCD's 86.40% (+2.53) and 88.10 F1 vs. 85.01 (+3.09). Against OPERA, the comparison is more nuanced: OPERA edges out CAUSALMM Multimodal on MSCOCO Random accuracy (89.20 vs. 88.93) and F1 (88.81 vs. 88.10), but CAUSALMM Multimodal surpasses OPERA on MSCOCO Popular (87.13 vs. 86.83 accuracy) and Adversarial (83.70 vs. 81.13 accuracy), and maintains more balanced precision–recall trade-offs. On the VLind-Bench, CAUSALMM's multimodal collaborative variant achieves the largest gains (up to 65.3% on individual indicators), substantially outperforming both VCD and OPERA which show minimal improvement on this language-prior-sensitive benchmark. In the broader Table 6 comparison (averaged over MSCOCO, A-OKVQA, GQA), CAUSALMM Multimodal also outperforms DOLA and AGLA on LLaVA-1.5, e.g., 88.79% accuracy vs. DOLA's 84.78% and AGLA's 88.54% on the Random setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive decoding lineage (VCD, OPERA, DoLa) by reframing hallucination mitigation through a structural causal model, where modality priors are treated as confounders between attention mechanisms and model output. It draws directly on Pearl's back-door adjustment and counterfactual reasoning framework, and adapts the four counterfactual attention interventions (random, uniform, reversed, shuffled) from Rao et al.'s visual categorization work to the MLLM setting. Unlike prior decoding methods that rely on statistical correlations between distorted inputs and outputs, CAUSALMM explicitly models the causal DAG and intervenes on attention at both the visual encoder and LLM backbone levels, positioning itself as a principled alternative to heuristic contrastive strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal framing offers a more interpretable and theoretically grounded explanation for why contrastive decoding works, and the plug-and-play, training-free nature makes it immediately deployable alongside existing MLLM pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge that the method's ceiling is bounded by the underlying vision encoder and LLM backbone quality, and it does not significantly improve high-level semantic understanding. An open question is how to maximize the benefits of balanced modal priors when the backbone is fixed, and whether the causal intervention approach can be extended to other confounding factors beyond modality priors (e.g., dataset bias, prompt sensitivity).
