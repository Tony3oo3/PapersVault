## Problem

Prior empirical work on text-conditioned diffusion models revealed rich phenomenology of compositional generalization—sequential concept learning, signal-strength-dependent ordering, and non-monotonic test loss—but lacked a mechanistic theoretical explanation for why these dynamics arise. This paper asks what structural properties of the data and model architecture drive the observed learning trajectories, and whether a simplified task can capture and explain the full phenomenology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252179/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For the SIM task, the model receives a point in R^d drawn from a Gaussian mixture whose s cluster centroids are placed along distinct coordinate axes (with distances μ_p encoding signal strength and variances σ_p encoding diversity), and must output the identity mapping; the OOD test point is the sum of all cluster means, a composition never seen in training. For the diffusion model validation, the input is a two-word text prompt (size × color, e.g., 'small blue') and the output is a generated circle image, which a pretrained classifier maps back to a 2D concept-space vector for trajectory analysis. The training set contains only three of four possible compositions, making the fourth (e.g., 'small blue') the OOD evaluation target.

## Method

The paper introduces the Structured Identity Mapping (SIM) task as a tractable abstraction of the concept-space framework, then analyzes learning dynamics of MLPs trained on SIM both empirically and theoretically. The core theoretical contribution is a detailed analysis of a symmetric 2-layer linear model f(U;x)=UU^T x, where the Jacobian W=UU^T evolves through a multi-stage cycle: (1) initial exponential growth of all entries, (2) suppression of off-diagonal 'minor' entries once the corresponding diagonal 'major' entry becomes large, (3) sequential growth of the next major entry, repeating for each concept direction. This growth-suppression cycle produces 'Swing-by Dynamics': minor entries that happen to be initialized positive temporarily reduce OOD loss (creating an illusion of generalization), then their subsequent suppression causes a transient loss increase, yielding a double- or multiple-descent test loss curve. The theory explains why Swing-by is strongest in low dimensions (more minor entries can be initialized positive) and why the one-layer model cannot exhibit it (no inter-direction coupling), and predictions are validated by training a text-conditioned diffusion model on a size×color synthetic task.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical rather than a single benchmark number. The one-layer linear model yields a closed-form solution (Theorem 4.1) showing each concept direction converges exponentially at rate a_k = (sσ_k² + μ_k²)/s, explaining why stronger-signal or higher-diversity concepts are learned first. The symmetric two-layer linear model reveals a multi-stage Jacobian evolution (Initial Growth → Suppression → Second Growth → Cycle → Terminal Slowdown) that produces non-monotonic OOD test loss — the Swing-by Dynamics — with double or even quadruple descent curves depending on the number of concepts s. In the diffusion model validation (Sec. 5), the predicted Swing-by is confirmed: the concept-space MSE for the OOD prompt (blue, small) exhibits a double-descent curve, and the generalization order reverses when the color signal strength is tuned, matching the SIM task predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against external methods in the traditional sense; its 'comparisons' are between theoretical predictions and empirical observations. The one-layer linear model theory successfully explains generalization order and terminal-phase slowing but fails to capture Swing-by Dynamics, establishing that this phenomenon is intrinsic to deeper architectures. The symmetric two-layer linear model theory reproduces all observed phenomena including the non-monotonic loss curve, with each descent/ascent stage precisely corresponding to a growth/suppression phase of Jacobian entries (Fig. 5). The SIM task results (MLPs with 1–4 layers, d=2 to d=64) are shown to be in one-to-one correspondence with the phenomenology reported by Okawa et al. (2023) and Park et al. (2024) in text-conditioned diffusion models, validating SIM as a faithful abstraction. The diffusion model experiments in Sec. 5 directly confirm the predicted Swing-by and exponential deceleration of concept learning, bridging the simplified theoretical framework and the complex generative model setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the concept space framework introduced by Okawa et al. (2023) and Park et al. (2024), which showed that text-conditioned diffusion models learn to manipulate primitive concepts in a sequential, hierarchy-respecting order. The key departure is the introduction of the Structured Identity Mapping (SIM) task as a minimal, analytically tractable abstraction of that framework, replacing image generation with regression on a Gaussian mixture with structured centroids. Methodologically, the paper departs from prior theoretical analyses of deep linear networks (Saxe et al., 2013; Lampinen & Ganguli, 2018; Even et al., 2023) by explicitly avoiding the diagonal-initialization assumption that decouples learning across directions, instead tracking the full multi-stage evolution of every Jacobian entry to capture cross-direction interactions that produce non-monotonic dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic explanation for why compositional generalization in generative models exhibits sequential, non-monotonic learning dynamics, and predicts a new phenomenon (Swing-by Dynamics / multiple OOD descent) that is confirmed in diffusion models. The SIM task establishes a reusable theoretical scaffold for studying concept learning dynamics without the intractability of full generative models. Limitations include the theory being restricted to linear models with small initialization and specific signal-strength separation assumptions, and the diffusion model verification being limited to a simple two-concept (size + color) setting; whether Swing-by Dynamics persists in higher-dimensional, non-linear, or multi-concept regimes remains open.
