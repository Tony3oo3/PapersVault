## Problem

Existing smartphone agent benchmarks are limited in scope (primarily English system/Google apps), evaluate fewer than five agents, and rely on hand-crafted validation logic that does not scale to diverse third-party or Chinese-language apps. There is no unified, plug-and-play framework that fairly compares both agentic-workflow and agent-as-a-model approaches across single-app and cross-app tasks with automated, human-free success detection.

## Input / Output

Each task is a natural-language instruction (English or Chinese) executed on a snapshot-restored Android emulator; the agent receives screenshots and/or accessibility-tree XML as observations and outputs discrete actions (tap, type, swipe). The evaluation pipeline produces seven metrics per agent-task pair: a binary success signal, a step ratio (agent steps / human golden steps), a termination reason (self-reported completion, max steps reached, or error), premature and overdue termination indicators, wall-clock time per step, and per-step API cost in USD. The success signal itself is derived from a two-stage hybrid detector that combines pre-annotated key-component text matching on the final screenshot with an MLLM (GPT-4o) judge that also considers the action trajectory.

## Method

SPA-BENCH comprises three tightly coupled components. First, a task collection of 340 tasks (300 single-app across 39 English and 29 Chinese apps, plus 40 cross-app tasks) organised into three difficulty levels with human-annotated golden-step trajectories and final-state key components. Second, a multi-process plug-and-play framework where each worker process pairs one agent with one Android emulator loaded from a pre-configured snapshot, enabling parallel execution of 11 agents (7 agentic-workflow, 4 agent-as-a-model) with minimal per-agent integration code. Third, an automated evaluation pipeline that replaces hand-crafted validators: for single-app tasks it applies coarse detection (substring matching of annotated key components on the final screenshot) followed by fine detection (an MLLM evaluator given the full action sequence and screenshots); for cross-app tasks it first segments the trajectory by app using an MLLM, then evaluates each subtask sequentially, stopping at the first failure. This hybrid action-and-state approach achieves F1 scores of 90.5% and 84.5% against human evaluators for single-app and cross-app tasks respectively, without requiring per-task validation code or inaccessible data such as WebView XML.

## Results

On SPA-BENCH, M3A achieves the highest success rates across all task categories: 0.640 on single-app English, 0.447 on single-app Chinese, and 0.150 on cross-app English. Agentic workflow agents (using GPT-4o) substantially outperform agent-as-a-model agents, with the latter (AutoUI, CogAgent, DigiRL, OdysseyAgent) achieving success rates below 0.053 on single-app English tasks. Cross-app tasks are significantly harder than single-app tasks for all agents, with even M3A completing only 6 out of 40 cross-app tasks. The automated success detection pipeline achieves F1 scores of 0.926 (English) and 0.884 (Chinese) for single-app tasks, and 0.845 for cross-app tasks, compared to human evaluators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 11 agents split into two categories: agentic workflow (AppAgent, AutoDroid, MobileAgent, MobileAgentV2, M3A, T3A, SeeAct) and agent-as-a-model (AutoUI, CogAgent, DigiRL, OdysseyAgent). M3A dominates all single-app categories, achieving 0.640 on English and 0.447 on Chinese, outperforming the next-best T3A (0.487/0.380) and MobileAgentV2 (0.433/0.440). On cross-app tasks, M3A is the only agent to complete more than 4 tasks (6/40), while all other agents complete 0–4. The gap between agentic workflow and agent-as-a-model agents is stark: the best fine-tuned model (OdysseyAgent) reaches only 0.053 on single-app English, roughly 12× lower than M3A's 0.640. Among agentic workflow agents, M3A also achieves the lowest mean step ratio (0.92, i.e., fewer steps than a human) and the lowest execution time per step (19.3 s), though at a higher token cost ($0.092/step) than AutoDroid ($0.008/step). Chinese tasks consistently reduce success rates by 10–20 percentage points across all agents, attributed to more complex UIs, animations, and limited (M)LLM Chinese capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPA-BENCH extends the smartphone agent evaluation line initiated by AndroidWorld, AndroidArena, LamaTouch, B-MoCA, and MobileAgentBench, but deliberately addresses their shared limitations: English-only and system/Google-suite-only app coverage, fewer than five evaluated agents, and reliance on hand-crafted per-task validation logic. Its evaluation pipeline builds on the hybrid action+state success detection idea from MobileAgentBench but replaces hand-crafted rules with a coarse-to-fine pipeline (key-component matching followed by MLLM-as-judge), and its plug-and-play agent framework departs from the tightly-coupled integrations in prior benchmarks. The cross-app task design draws on GUI Odyssey's taxonomy, while the snapshot-based emulator approach generalizes the single-emulator setups in earlier work to support parallel multi-agent testing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPA-BENCH provides the first benchmark that simultaneously covers Chinese and English apps, third-party apps, single-app and cross-app scenarios, and eleven heterogeneous agents under a unified, automated evaluation protocol—making it a practical reference point for the smartphone-agent community. The stark finding that agentic-workflow agents (up to 64% success) outperform fine-tuned agent-as-a-model agents (≤5.3%) by an order of magnitude, and that even the best agent completes only 15% of cross-app tasks, highlights fundamental gaps in grounding, memory, and inter-app reasoning that remain open. The cost and latency figures (e.g., $0.116/step for T3A, 56 s/step for MobileAgentV2) underscore that current agents are still impractical for real-world deployment, pointing to efficiency and robustness as critical future directions.
