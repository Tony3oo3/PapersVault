## Problem

Existing query-based sound separation methods are limited to single-modal queries (text, image, or audio), cannot remove undesired sounds, and are confined to predefined class labels rather than unrestricted natural language. This paper addresses all three limitations by proposing a unified omni-modal framework that handles composed multi-modal queries, supports negative queries for sound removal, and enables open-vocabulary separation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251986/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 4-second mixed audio segment at 16 kHz (converted to a 1024-point STFT magnitude spectrum with 256 hop) plus query features from any combination of text, image (224×224), and audio modalities, all projected into a shared 1024-dim space by a frozen ImageBind encoder. An optional negative query (same modality space) can be supplied to suppress interference. The output is a time-domain separated audio waveform obtained by applying the predicted mask to the mixture's magnitude spectrum and combining with the noisy phase via iSTFT.

## Method

OmniSep's key mechanism is Query-Mixup: during training, query features from all three modalities (extracted by frozen ImageBind) are blended with randomly sampled weights w_a, w_v, w_t into a single mixed embedding, allowing the Separate-Net to optimize all modalities concurrently rather than switching between them as in CLIPSEP. The Separate-Net is a 7-layer U-Net that produces 32 intermediate masks from the STFT spectrogram; each query is mapped via a linear layer to 32 channel-wise weights that combine these masks through a sigmoid-gated summation, trained with weighted binary cross-entropy. Two training-free extensions enhance flexibility: a negative query uses proportional weighting Q'=(1+α)Q−αQ_N to subtract interference information while preserving frequency-band stability, and Query-Aug retrieves the most cosine-similar in-domain class-label embedding for unrestricted text descriptions, enabling open-vocabulary separation without retraining.

## Results

On the three main evaluation datasets, OmniSep achieves state-of-the-art Mean SDR across all queried sound separation tasks. For TQSS on VGGSOUND-CLEAN+, OmniSep reaches 6.70 Mean SDR (vs. 5.49 for CLIPSEP-Text and 6.26 for AudioSep); for IQSS on MUSIC, it scores 10.97 (vs. 8.06 for CLIPSEP and 10.54 for i-Query); and for AQSS on MUSIC-CLEAN+, it attains 12.92 (vs. 8.56 for the prior AQSS baseline). With the composed omni-modal query, OmniSep achieves 7.46 Mean SDR on VGGSOUND-CLEAN+, and adding negative queries further boosts performance by 0.10–1.60 SDR across tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniSep outperforms all seven baselines across every task and dataset. Against CLIPSEP (Dong et al., 2022), OmniSep improves TQSS Mean SDR by 2.29 on MUSIC (10.65 vs. 8.36) and 1.21 on VGGSOUND-CLEAN+ (6.70 vs. 5.49), and IQSS by 2.91 on MUSIC (10.97 vs. 8.06) and 1.23 on VGGSOUND-CLEAN+ (6.69 vs. 5.46). Against AudioSep (Liu et al., 2023), the closest TQSS competitor, OmniSep gains 0.83 SDR on MUSIC (10.65 vs. 9.82) and 0.44 on VGGSOUND-CLEAN+ (6.70 vs. 6.26). For IQSS, OmniSep surpasses i-Query (Chen et al., 2023) by 0.43 SDR on MUSIC (10.97 vs. 10.54) and SOP (Zhao et al., 2018) by 4.38 on MUSIC (10.97 vs. 6.59). The largest margin is in AQSS, where OmniSep exceeds the prior AQSS baseline (Lee et al., 2019) by 3.83 SDR on MUSIC (10.26 vs. 6.43) and 4.36 on MUSIC-CLEAN+ (12.92 vs. 8.56). Non-queried baselines PIT (Kavalerov et al., 2019) and TDANet (Li et al., 2022) are also surpassed by 1.94–6.52 SDR depending on dataset and task.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniSep builds directly on CLIPSEP's Query-Net + Separate-Net (U-Net) architecture and training protocol, but departs from its periodic modality-switching strategy by introducing Query-Mixup, which blends ImageBind-extracted features from text, image, and audio modalities with random weights during training to unify the optimization objective. The negative query mechanism adapts vector subtraction from multi-modal joint retrieval to the sound separation setting, while Query-Aug draws on retrieval-augmented generation (Lewis et al., 2020) to bridge the gap between open-vocabulary text and in-domain class labels. Conceptually, the paper sits at the intersection of query-based sound separation (TQSS/IQSS/AQSS) and multi-modal representation learning (CLIP, CLAP, ImageBind), positioning itself as the first model to handle composed omni-modal queries for sound separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying text, image, and audio queries in a single model without sacrificing single-modal performance, OmniSep removes the need to maintain separate models per query modality and enables composed queries that capture richer semantics than any single modality alone. The negative query and Query-Aug components address two practical pain points—removing specific interfering sounds and handling unrestricted natural language—that prior QSS methods could not handle. Limitations include reliance on ImageBind's frozen representations (which may not capture fine-grained audio-visual correspondences), evaluation on relatively small-scale datasets (MUSIC: 10 hours; VGGSOUND: 330 classes), and the still-manual selection of the negative query weight α. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex real-world scenes with many overlapping sources, whether additional modalities (e.g., depth, IMU) can be incorporated, and whether the spectral-masking formulation is optimal compared to time-domain or transformer-based separation.
