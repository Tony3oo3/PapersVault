## Problem

Existing sentence embedding models rely on fixed full-layer Transformer outputs and predefined embedding sizes, making them inflexible across applications with varying computational budgets. While Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) enables scalable embedding dimensions, it still requires full Transformer depth for inference, leaving model-depth scalability unaddressed. No prior method supports efficient, high-quality sentence embeddings that can be truncated along both model depth and embedding size simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251394/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sentence as input, encoded by a pretrained Transformer backbone (BERT-based or LLM-based such as Qwen or LLaMA) using CLS or mean pooling. The output is a dense sentence embedding vector that can be flexibly truncated: at inference, one simply selects the first-k dimensions from the output of any layer i (1 ≤ i ≤ n), without re-running PCA or additional computation. This dual truncation—over layers and dimensions—enables deployment under constrained memory or latency budgets while preserving semantic quality.

## Method

ESE introduces two complementary training processes. The learn-to-express process applies a depth-decaying weighted loss (w_i = 1/(1+ln(i))) across all Transformer layers, pushing more salient semantic representations into shallow layers so that early-layer embeddings remain useful. The learn-to-compress process computes a self-attention-like inner-dependency matrix via scaled dot product of the full embedding with itself, applies PCA (via SVD) to retain only the top-k principal components, and then aligns the truncated first-k sub-embedding to the PCA-compressed embedding using combined MSE and KL-divergence losses. This alignment ensures that at inference, the first-k dimensions directly approximate the PCA-compressed result without any additional computation. The two objectives are jointly optimized as L = α·L_le + β·L_lc, and the entire framework is trained with standard sentence embedding losses (e.g., AnglE) on NLI data, making it a drop-in extension to existing Transformer-based embedding pipelines.

## Results

On the STS benchmark (Spearman's correlation), ESE achieves 84.49 average on bge-base-en-v1.5, 86.06 on UAE-Large-V1, and 82.82 on Qwen1.5-0.5B in the full (last-layer) setting, matching or slightly exceeding RAW and MRL. The headline gain is in shallow-layer scalability: ESE lifts BGE's shallow-layer average from 45.60 (RAW) to 66.27, a 20.67-point improvement, while MRL only reaches 46.18. On the RAG task (HotpotQA, Exact Match), ESE scores 32.28 at 64-dimensional embeddings versus 29.86 (RAW) and 30.48 (MRL), and 45.31 at full 768 dimensions versus 45.06 and 45.09.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESE is compared against two baselines: RAW (the original pretrained model without any scaling training) and MRL (Matryoshka Representation Learning, which trains cascading-dimension embeddings but still requires full Transformer depth). On the STS benchmark, ESE wins on the shallow-layer average (<Avg.) across all three backbones: +20.67 over RAW and +20.09 over MRL on BGE, +14.32 over RAW and +14.15 over MRL on UAE-Large-V1, and +3.40 over RAW and +1.77 over MRL on Qwen1.5-0.5B. In the full last-layer setting, ESE matches or slightly exceeds both baselines (e.g., 84.49 vs. 83.46 RAW and 84.01 MRL on BGE), confirming that the scalability training does not degrade full-depth performance. On the RAG task (HotpotQA Exact Match), ESE outperforms both baselines at every embedding size tested, with the largest margin at 64 dimensions (32.28 vs. 29.86 RAW and 30.48 MRL, a +2.42 and +1.80 gain). MRL notably underperforms RAW on UAE-Large-V1's last layer (85.25 vs. 85.86), suggesting its multi-embedding training can introduce instability, whereas ESE's PCA-based compression avoids this issue.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESE extends Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL), which enabled scalable embedding sizes through cascading-dimension training, by adding a second axis of scalability: model depth. Where MRL still requires full Transformer layers for inference, ESE's learn-to-express process uses weighted multi-layer losses to push salient representations into shallow layers, and its learn-to-compress process replaces MRL's multi-embedding training with PCA applied to embedding inner-dependency matrices (computed via scaled dot-product, mirroring self-attention). The paper positions itself as the first to integrate PCA into sentence embedding training rather than as a post-hoc compression step, and builds on standard backbones (BERT, Qwen, LLaMA) with AnglE contrastive loss and LoRA for LLM fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual depth-and-size scalability directly addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: resource-constrained applications (edge devices, high-throughput retrieval) can now trade off model depth and embedding dimensionality with predictable quality degradation rather than an all-or-nothing commitment. The 20+ point gain on BGE shallow layers (45.60→66.27) and the t-SNE evidence (avg. distance 10.36 vs. 32.63 for RAW) suggest that much of the semantic content in sentence embeddings is already concentrated in early layers and initial dimensions, challenging the implicit assumption that deep, wide representations are necessary. Open questions include whether the gains hold without LoRA on larger LLMs, how the PCA alignment loss interacts with other training objectives, and whether the 128-dimension 'sweet spot' generalizes across domains and languages.
