## Problem

Imitation learning from very limited demonstrations (e.g., 10% of a single partial episode) in high-dimensional environments remains challenging: adversarial methods like GAIL/VAIL fail to scale and cannot achieve beyond-expert performance, while curiosity-driven methods like GIRIL provide stable exploration but may not reach a wide variety of states. The paper addresses how to combine stable imitation learning with effective, broad exploration to simultaneously improve sample efficiency and surpass expert performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes expert demonstration trajectories that are extremely sparse — in Atari, only 10% of a one-life demonstration (the states and actions until the expert's first death, generated by a PPO agent trained for 10M steps), and in MuJoCo, just 4 full trajectories. States are 4×84×84 RGB frames (Atari) or low-dimensional state vectors (MuJoCo), processed through a 3-layer convolutional feature extractor (5184-dim) or MLP. The output is a learned policy (categorical or Gaussian action distribution) evaluated on true environment rewards, with the goal of exceeding the expert's average return.

## Method

ILDE optimizes an uncertainty-regularized discrepancy combining three reward components: (1) a VAIL-based imitation reward from a discriminator with a variational information bottleneck that distinguishes expert from learner trajectories, (2) a curiosity-driven intrinsic reward from a pretrained conditional VAE (GIRIL) that penalizes transitions deviating from the demonstration distribution, and (3) a state-entropy exploration bonus computed via a k-nearest-neighbor estimator in a fixed feature space that rewards visiting states far from previously seen ones. The policy is updated with PPO using the sum of all three rewards. Theoretically, the paper formulates ILDE-NPG using optimistic natural policy gradient with a D_F-uncertainty bonus in the policy evaluation, proving the first sublinear regret bound for imitation learning in MDPs with nonlinear function approximation via generalized Eluder dimension.

## Results

On six Atari games with only 10% of a one-life demonstration, ILDE achieves an average performance 5.33× the expert demonstrator, outperforming the expert in all 6 games (100%), compared to GIRIL at 4.87× (83.3%) and VAIL at 0.37× (16.7%). On four MuJoCo continuous control tasks, ILDE consistently achieves the highest average return: 1,878±690 on Hopper (vs. GIRIL 1,447±273, VAIL 1,264±783) and 77,887±6,123 on HumanoidStandup (vs. GIRIL 76,134±10,826, VAIL 52,635±6,370). In terms of sample efficiency, ILDE reaches expert-level performance 90.06% earlier than GIRIL on BattleZone and 37.5% earlier on DemonAttack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ILDE decisively outperforms VAIL (Peng et al., 2019) on both Atari and MuJoCo: on Atari, VAIL achieves only 0.37× expert performance (below expert in 5 of 6 games), while ILDE reaches 5.33×; on MuJoCo, ILDE leads by 48% on Hopper (1,878 vs. 1,264) and 48% on HumanoidStandup (77,887 vs. 52,635). Against GIRIL (Yu et al., 2020), ILDE achieves a higher average Atari return (5.33× vs. 4.87× expert) and outperforms the expert in all 6 games versus GIRIL's 5, with the largest gap on StarGunner (25,361 vs. 542) and BattleZone (59,109 vs. 25,203); on MuJoCo, ILDE edges out GIRIL on Hopper (1,878 vs. 1,447) and HumanoidStandup (77,887 vs. 76,134). IQ-Learn (Garg et al., 2021) performs very poorly under the extremely limited 10%-one-life demonstration setting, scoring near zero on BeamRider (0.0) and StarGunner (200), confirming it does not scale to sparse demonstrations. HyPE (Ren et al., 2024) is outperformed by the HyPE-ILDE variant on MuJoCo with a single demonstration: 3,372.6 vs. 2,995.0 on Hopper-v3 and 813.8 vs. 785.8 on Ant-v3, indicating the double-exploration framework generalizes to other adversarial IL methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

ILDE synthesizes three distinct lines of imitation learning research: (1) discriminator-based adversarial IL (GAIL/VAIL) for the imitation reward, (2) curiosity-driven exploration via a pretrained VAE (GIRIL) for the intrinsic reward, and (3) state-entropy-based exploration bonuses (Seo et al., 2021) for optimistic policy optimization. The theoretical analysis builds on Optimistic Natural Policy Gradient (Liu et al., 2023b) and the generalized Eluder dimension framework (Agarwal et al., 2023) to deliver the first regret guarantee for imitation learning in MDPs with nonlinear function approximation. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior work by framing the objective as an uncertainty-regularized discrepancy rather than pure distribution matching, and by explicitly combining two complementary exploration mechanisms within a single algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first theoretical regret bound for imitation learning with nonlinear function approximation provides a principled foundation that was previously available only for tabular or linear MDPs. Empirically, achieving 5.33× expert performance from just 10% of a one-life demonstration is a strong practical result for sample-efficient IL. However, the authors explicitly acknowledge a key limitation: the beyond-expert gains partly rely on alignment between the extrinsic reward and novelty-seeking, which restricts applicability to tasks where curiosity is a proxy for the true objective. The gap between the theoretical ILDE-NPG and the practical PPO-based implementation remains unbridged, and the modest MuJoCo improvements suggest the method's advantage is most pronounced in high-dimensional, sparse-reward settings.
