## Problem

Full conformal prediction provides the tightest distribution-free prediction intervals but is computationally prohibitive for multiple test points. The existing acceleration via replace-one stable conformal prediction (RO-StabCP) still requires refitting the model once per test point, making it impractical when the number of prediction requests m is large. This paper asks whether a single model fit on the training data can serve all m predictions while preserving finite-sample coverage validity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250585/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training set D = {(X_i, Y_i)}_{i=1}^n, m test feature vectors {X_{n+j}}, and a target coverage level 1−α. It outputs a prediction interval C_{j,α}^{LOO}(X_{n+j}) for each test point, constructed as f̂(X_{n+j}) ± {Q_{1−α}({S_i + τ_{i,j}^{LOO}}) + τ_{n+j,j}^{LOO}}, where S_i are uncorrected non-conformity scores from the single fitted model and τ_{i,j}^{LOO} are algorithm-specific stability bounds. In the screening application, the same machinery produces stability-adjusted conformal p-values that feed into a Benjamini-Hochberg procedure to output a set of rejected null hypotheses at a target FDR level q.

## Method

LOO-StabCP replaces the replace-one stability correction of RO-StabCP with a leave-one-out stability correction: instead of fitting a model on D ∪ {(X_{n+j}, ỹ)} for each test point, it fits a single model f̂ on D and bounds the prediction error introduced by omitting (X_{n+j}, y) via a leave-one-out stability bound τ_{i,j}^{LOO} = sup_{y,z} |S(z, f̂_j^y(X_i)) − S(z, f̂(X_i))|. Because the correction no longer depends on X_{n+j} in the model-fitting step, only one model fit is needed regardless of m, reducing the number of fits from m (RO-StabCP) to 1. The paper derives explicit LOO stability bounds for regularized loss minimization (τ ∝ 2γν_i(ρ_{n+j}+ρ̄)/(λ(n+1))), SGD (τ = Rη·γν_iρ_{n+j}, half the RO bound due to the iterative nature of gradient updates), kernel methods (as a special case of RLM), non-convex neural networks (with an exponential factor R⁺), and derandomized bagging (τ ∝ γw_j/2 · √(p/(1−p))). A coverage validity theorem guarantees P(Y_{n+j} ∈ C_{j,α}^{LOO}) ≥ 1−α for all j, since the LOO prediction set contains the full conformal set.

## Results

In the main simulation (n=m=100, α=0.1), LOO-StabCP achieves coverage of 0.906 (Linear SGD) and 0.894 (Nonlinear SGD), matching RO-StabCP (0.911 and 0.900) while being ~80× faster (0.009s vs. 0.720s for SGD; 0.035s vs. 3.190s for RLM). Prediction interval lengths are comparable to RO-StabCP (e.g., 3.405 vs. 3.464 for Linear SGD) and consistently shorter than SplitCP (3.420 and 3.855). In the screening application on recruitment data, LOO-cfBH achieves valid FDP control at all tested FDR levels (q ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3}) with higher test power than cfBH and significantly lower computation time than RO-cfBH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FullCP, LOO-StabCP matches coverage and interval length while being approximately 2,000× faster (0.009s vs. 19.320s for Linear SGD), since FullCP requires |Y|·m model fits versus one. Against SplitCP, LOO-StabCP achieves slightly higher coverage (0.906 vs. 0.900 for Linear SGD) and shorter intervals (3.405 vs. 3.420) at comparable speed (0.009s vs. 0.005s), with notably lower variance in interval length (std 0.259 vs. 0.557). Against RO-StabCP, LOO-StabCP matches prediction accuracy (coverage and length within 0.01) but is 80× faster for SGD (0.009s vs. 0.720s) and ~90× faster for RLM (0.035s vs. 3.190s), because RO-StabCP refits the model m times while LOO-StabCP fits only once. In the screening application, LOO-cfBH outperforms cfBH (Jin & Candès, 2023) in test power due to using all n training points rather than a 70/30 split, while maintaining valid FDP control. Against derandomized SplitCP variants (MM-SplitCP and EM-SplitCP), LOO-StabCP is faster (single model fit vs. 30 splits) and less conservative (coverage closer to the target 1−α rather than exceeding it). Against OracleCP, LOO-StabCP approaches the oracle's interval length (3.405 vs. 3.252 for Linear SGD) while remaining computationally feasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Ndiaye's (2022) replace-one stable conformal prediction (RO-StabCP) by introducing a fundamentally different form of algorithmic stability—leave-one-out (LOO) stability—that decouples the prediction model from the test point X_{n+j}. While RO-StabCP still requires refitting the model once per test point (m fits total), LOO-StabCP requires only a single model fit on the training data D, with stability corrections absorbing the gap between the full-conformal model (trained on D ∪ {(X_{n+j}, y)}) and the simpler model (trained on D alone). The paper also builds on the full conformal prediction framework of Vovk et al. (2005) for its validity guarantee, and extends the screening application of Jin & Candès (2023) by replacing split-conformal p-values with LOO-stability-adjusted p-values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is a dramatic computational speedup for multiple prediction requests: where RO-StabCP scales linearly in m (number of test points) for model fitting, LOO-StabCP is constant in m, making it competitive with split conformal in speed while retaining full-conformal-level accuracy. The screening application (LOO-cfBH) demonstrates that better exploitation of training data translates into higher test power under FDR control. Open questions include extending the theory to classification, tightening the conservative non-convex stability bounds (Theorem 4), and adapting the framework to adaptive conformal prediction under distribution shift.
