## Problem

Prompt-based continual learning methods suffer from catastrophic forgetting due to inter-task vector similarity (causing misidentified task-ids), unfitted new components disrupting previously learned tasks, and shared parameters being adjusted across all tasks. Meanwhile, language-guided approaches underutilize the language modality by relying only on simple class-name strings rather than richer discriminative descriptors, limiting their potential as guidance for prompt tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252065/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image as input, processed by a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone, and produces a class label via an MLP head. Uniquely, the input image is also matched against a catalog of language descriptor embeddings (GPT-generated visual attribute descriptions or class names) to select top-k descriptors that drive prompt generation. The generated prompt is prepended to ViT multi-head self-attention layers, while an auxiliary embedding (weighted sum of selected descriptors) is appended to the input patch sequence, so the ViT processes both the image patches and the language-derived auxiliary token jointly.

## Method

LEAPGen introduces four key mechanisms: (1) language descriptor embeddings serve as input to trainable single-head self-attention generator networks (key-value pair generators distributed across ViT layers and heads), producing prompt components that are combined via similarity-weighted summation; (2) generators are task-wise and frozen after their task's training, preventing cross-task interference; (3) a soft task-id predictor uses the product of cosine similarities between the input and both a task-wise key K^t and class-wise key L_c^t to limit the descriptor search space to tasks 1 through the predicted task t, avoiding the high inter-task key similarity problem of conventional predictors; (4) the weighted sum of selected descriptor embeddings is appended as an auxiliary token to the input patches. Training optimizes a joint loss combining intra-task cross-entropy, inter-task cross-entropy, and negative cosine similarity losses on both task-wise and class-wise keys, with no regularization needed since all task-t parameters are frozen after task completion.

## Results

On the 10-task setting, LEAPGen achieves 98.38% FAA and 0.10% FFM on CIFAR-100, 84.09% FAA and 1.46% FFM on ImageNet-R, and 88.45% FAA and 1.32% FFM on CUB. These represent margins of up to 5.5% (CIFAR-100), 7% (ImageNet-R), and 1.2% (CUB) in FAA over the strongest competitor (HiDe-Prompt), and up to 30% FAA and 24% CAA margins over the weakest baselines. Forgetting measures are dramatically lower: LEAPGen's FFM stays below 0.7% on CIFAR-100 across all task splits, while the best competitor (HiDe-Prompt) suffers 1.5–2.5% FFM. The method also achieves the lowest CFM in all settings, with values of 0.05–0.52% on CIFAR-100 versus 1.5–7.4% for competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAPGen outperforms all 14 baselines across all three datasets and all task splits. The closest competitor is HiDe-Prompt, which achieves 92.89% FAA on CIFAR-100 (10 tasks) versus LEAPGen's 98.38%, and 75.75% on ImageNet-R versus 84.09%; HiDe-Prompt also fails to complete the 20-task CIFAR-100 setting. ConvPrompt is the strongest growing-component baseline (77.08% FAA on ImageNet-R 10 tasks) but still trails LEAPGen by 7% FAA and 2.7% FFM. Pool-based L2P and task-specific DualPrompt lag by 10–15% FAA on ImageNet-R. Among non-prompt methods, SLCA (slow learner) is the strongest at 91.53% FAA on CIFAR-100 10 tasks, still 6.9% below LEAPGen; LoRA-based methods (C-LoRA, LAE, InfLoRA) achieve 65–90% FAA depending on dataset, all well below LEAPGen. The generative GMM method reaches 80.72% FAA on ImageNet-R 10 tasks, 3.4% below LEAPGen. Notably, even LEAPGen-lite (using class names instead of GPT-generated descriptors and smaller Conv1d generators) outperforms all baselines by 3–25% FAA, confirming the core architectural ideas are not dependent on the full descriptor pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEAPGen extends the prompt-based continual learning lineage (DualPrompt, CODA-P, ConvPrompt, HiDe-Prompt) by repurposing the generator architecture from ConvPrompt and prefix-tuning into task-wise frozen networks, and by adopting the probability decomposition framework from HiDe-Prompt (Wang et al., 2024a) to motivate a soft task-id prediction. It departs from prior language-guided methods (LGCL, GMM) that use language only for loss computation or class-name prototypes, instead using GPT-generated visual descriptors as the actual input to prompt generators. The soft task-id prediction mechanism, which combines task-wise and class-wise learnable keys, addresses the high inter-task key similarity problem empirically documented in DualPrompt (cosine similarities of 0.73–0.96 between different task keys).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rehearsal-free setting is practically important because rehearsal-based methods are infeasible under privacy constraints or data-availability policies, and LEAPGen's large margins (up to 30% FAA, 8% FFM) over all prompt-based SOTAs across three benchmarks suggest that the current prompt-based paradigm has significant untapped potential. However, the method's dependence on GPT-generated descriptors introduces an external LLM dependency and a one-time generation cost (~1 hour), and evaluation is limited to ViT-B/16 on three datasets, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger backbones, domain-incremental settings, or scenarios where class descriptors are unavailable. The finding that even LEAPGen-lite (class names only, 2.67% parameters) outperforms all SOTAs by 4–25% FAA suggests the core architectural ideas (task-wise frozen generators, soft task-id, auxiliary embedding) are the primary drivers rather than the GPT descriptors specifically.
