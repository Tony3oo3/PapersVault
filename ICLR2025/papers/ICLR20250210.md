## Problem

In the μP parameterization of neural networks, the hyperparameter γ controls the strength of feature learning, yet its practical effects across the full range of values (from lazy γ≪1 to ultra-rich γ≫1) were largely unexplored. In particular, how the optimal learning rate η* should scale with γ, what dynamical phenomena emerge in the ultra-rich regime, and whether large-γ networks can match or exceed γ=1 performance in the online training setting remained open questions.

## Input / Output

The method takes centered neural networks in μP (or Depth-μP) parameterization—MLPs, CNNs, ResNets, and ViTs of varying depths and widths—and trains them online (single pass, no data repetition) on MNIST-1M, CIFAR-5M, or TinyImageNet with batch size B. The network output is downscaled by 1/γ and centered by subtracting the initialization value before passing to the loss (MSE or cross-entropy). Sweeps cover γ from 10⁻⁵ to 10⁵ and η from 10¹² down to the first convergent value, producing a 2D phase portrait of final accuracy or loss in the γ–η plane, alongside time-resolved loss curves, Hessian eigenvalue spectra, kernel-target alignment scores, and pairwise function/representation comparisons across different γ values.

## Method

The paper combines large-scale empirical sweeps with a solvable theoretical model. Empirically, it identifies a characteristic phase portrait in the γ–η plane that is architecture-independent: in the lazy regime (γ≪1), the optimal learning rate scales as η* ∝ γ² (set by the NTK Hessian), while in the ultra-rich regime (γ≫1), it scales as η* ∝ γ^(2/L) for depth-L networks, bounded below by η ∼ γ/T for feature movement. The theoretical analysis uses a depth-L linear network of width one, which by commutativity reduces to a single weight w with function f = w^L, enabling exact derivation of all scaling boundaries, the catapult regime, and the progressive sharpening effect. Key empirical findings include: a 'triangle of optimizability' at large γ whose size grows with compute budget; silent alignment where the kernel aligns to the task before the loss drops; stepwise loss drops coinciding with Hessian growth to 2/η (edge of stability); and convergence of learned functions and representations across different large-γ networks after a time rescaling τ = ηt/γ.

## Results

The paper identifies a characteristic phase portrait in the γ–η plane that is universal across architectures (MLPs, CNNs, ResNets, ViTs) and depends only on the loss function. The optimal learning rate scales as η* ∝ γ² in the lazy regime (γ≪1) and η* ∝ γ^(2/L) in the ultra-rich regime (γ≫1), where L is network depth. In the online setting, networks trained at large γ (up to 10⁵) achieve equal or better test accuracy than γ=1 networks given sufficient training time, contradicting prior offline findings (Petrini et al., 2022) that strong feature learning degrades performance. The Hessian top eigenvalue scales as γ^(−2) for γ≪1 and γ^(−2/L) for γ≫1, and the large-γ regime exhibits a 'triangle of optimizability' whose size grows with compute budget.

| MSE | Lazy (γ≪1) | γ²/T | γ² | γ² |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against external baselines; instead, it compares the behavior of networks at different γ values under matched compute budgets. In the online setting, large-γ networks (γ up to 10⁵) match or exceed the final test accuracy of the standard γ=1 μP parameterization once trained long enough (Figure 2b), whereas in the offline setting (Petrini et al., 2022) large γ was found to degrade performance due to overfitting. The phase portrait structure is robust across all four architecture families tested (MLP, CNN, ResNet-18, ViT) and both loss functions (MSE, cross-entropy), with the only architectural dependence being the depth L in the ultra-rich exponent. For the Adam/SignSGD optimizer, the scaling exponents change to η ∼ γ in the lazy regime and η ∼ γ^(1/L) in the ultra-rich regime, and the upper and lower bounds coincide, eliminating the 'triangle of optimizability' and the silent alignment effect. The catapult effect appears only in the lazy regime (γ≪1) and only for η in a narrow band around γ² (MSE) or between γ² and γ (cross-entropy), and is absent at large γ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the μP parameterization framework (Yang & Hu, 2021; Geiger et al., 2020) by conducting the first systematic empirical sweep of the feature-learning strength γ across many orders of magnitude in the online training setting, deliberately contrasting with prior offline studies (Petrini et al., 2022; Sclocchi et al., 2023) that found large γ degrades performance. It unifies several previously studied dynamical phenomena—catapult effect (Lewkowycz et al., 2020), silent alignment (Atanasov et al., 2022), and progressive sharpening/edge of stability (Cohen et al., 2021)—under a single γ-η phase portrait, and provides a minimal linear-network model that analytically reproduces the observed scaling laws. The paper departs from the offline setting by arguing that online training removes confounding overfitting effects and isolates γ as a pure optimization hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that large-γ networks match or exceed γ=1 performance in the online setting directly contradicts prior offline results, suggesting that the optimal feature-learning strength is regime-dependent and that practitioners may be leaving performance on the table by not tuning γ. The depth-dependent learning rate scaling η* ∝ γ^(2/L) in the ultra-rich regime and the identification of a 'triangle of optimizability' provide actionable guidance for training very rich networks without instability. Open questions include whether analytical treatment of the large-γ limit can yield deeper insights into representation learning dynamics, whether the results extend to optimizers beyond SGD (though preliminary Adam/SignSGD results are provided), and whether the upper bound on optimizable γ for deep networks can be lifted with architectural modifications.
