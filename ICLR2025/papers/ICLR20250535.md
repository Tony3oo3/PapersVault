## Problem

Sparse training methods reduce communication and computation in federated learning, but three practical barriers prevent their adoption in cross-device FL: (1) data heterogeneity disrupts binary mask consensus across clients, (2) existing mask strategies are either fixed (losing adaptivity to unseen distributions) or dynamic but require multiple hard-to-tune hyperparameters, and (3) weight regrowth after aggregation inflates downlink costs. Prior work addresses at most two of these issues while introducing new trade-offs between mask stability and adaptivity.

## Input / Output

Clients receive 32×32 color images (CIFAR-10/100) or short audio clips (Speech Commands, 35 classes) partitioned across 100 devices via LDA with concentration parameter α ∈ {0.1, 1.0, 10³} to control heterogeneity. Each round, 10 clients are sampled, train locally for E epochs, and upload sparse pseudo-gradient updates (Top-K pruned to a server-specified target sparsity ŝ) rather than full model weights. The server aggregates these sparse updates with an arbitrary outer optimizer and broadcasts the resulting global model back to clients, with communication cost measured as the number of non-zero parameters exchanged in both uplink and downlink.

## Method

SparsyFed combines three mechanisms during each client's local training: (1) a sparsity-inducing weight re-parameterization (Powerpropagation, w = sign(v)·|v|^β) applied before the forward pass, which creates a 'rich-get-richer' gradient dynamic that naturally concentrates information in a shared subset of weights and suppresses regrowth of pruned weights; (2) layer-wise activation pruning during the backward pass, where each layer's activations are Top-K pruned to match that layer's own weight sparsity, reducing FLOPs while preserving dense gradients for robust updates; and (3) a final global unstructured Top-K pruning of the pseudo-gradient to the target sparsity ŝ before uplink. The key insight is that Powerpropagation's re-parameterization simultaneously enforces mask consensus (clients converge on the same non-zero weight subset) and preserves mask adaptivity (the mask can still shift across rounds to accommodate new data distributions), all while requiring only one additional hyperparameter (β) over standard dense FL training. The method is agnostic to both client and server optimizers and compatible with biased client selection policies.

## Results

SparsyFed achieves up to 95% sparsity with negligible accuracy degradation: on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) it reaches 75.95% accuracy vs. 60.00% for the dense ResNet-18 baseline, and on CIFAR-100 (α=0.1) it reaches 48.27% vs. 11.48% dense. At 99% sparsity, SparsyFed still attains 63.69% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) and 41.03% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.1), while baselines drop to 51.71% and 13.07% respectively. Communication costs are reduced by up to 19.29× compared to the dense model, with per-round weight regrowth 200× smaller than prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparsyFed consistently outperforms both ZeroFL (Qiu et al., 2022) and FLASH (Babakniya et al., 2023) across all three datasets, both heterogeneity levels, and all sparsity ratios tested. At 95% sparsity on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1), SparsyFed achieves 75.95% accuracy, surpassing FLASH (72.08%) by ~4 points and ZeroFL (61.55%) by ~14 points; on CIFAR-100 (α=0.1) the gap widens to 48.27% vs. 44.31% (FLASH) and 34.21% (ZeroFL). The advantage is most pronounced under extreme non-IID conditions (α=0.1) and at higher sparsity levels, where ZeroFL's weight regrowth causes its global model density to increase substantially after aggregation, inflating downlink communication costs. FLASH maintains stable communication costs due to its fixed mask but suffers from reduced adaptivity to heterogeneous data, while SparsyFed's dynamic mask achieves both lower communication (1.66× less than ZeroFL) and higher accuracy. At 99.9% sparsity, SparsyFed still retains 43.68% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) versus 29.31% (FLASH) and 18.76% (ZeroFL), demonstrating superior resilience at extreme compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparsyFed combines two previously separate ideas—Powerpropagation (Schwarz et al., 2021) for sparsity-inducing weight re-parameterization and SWAT-style layer-wise activation pruning (Raihan & Aamodt, 2020)—into a unified federated training pipeline. It departs from prior FL sparse training methods (ZeroFL, FLASH, FedSpa) by rejecting the fixed-mask paradigm and instead allowing dynamic mask adaptation, while simultaneously using re-parameterization to enforce client consensus on which weights remain active. This addresses a trade-off the authors identify in prior work: fixed masks ensure consensus but sacrifice adaptivity to heterogeneous data, while purely dynamic methods (ZeroFL, Top-K) achieve adaptivity at the cost of mask instability and weight regrowth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For cross-device FL practitioners, the 19.29× communication reduction at 95% sparsity with minimal accuracy loss and only one additional hyperparameter makes this a practically deployable technique for bandwidth-constrained edge settings. However, the evaluation is limited to ResNet-18 on three small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Speech Commands) with 100 simulated clients, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger architectures (ViT experiments are mentioned but not fully reported), deeper networks, or truly heterogeneous hardware. The 99.9% sparsity results, while showing relative robustness, still reveal substantial accuracy degradation, suggesting a practical ceiling around 95–99% sparsity for meaningful performance.
