## Problem

Diffusion models trained on large-scale unlabeled data (e.g., scraped from the internet) inevitably learn to generate sensitive or harmful content because such data are embedded in the training set. Existing unlearning or continual-learning approaches require a clean set of labeled normal data to contrast against sensitive data, but obtaining such clean data is impractical at scale. This paper asks: can we train a diffusion model to suppress sensitive data generation using only unlabeled data (containing both normal and sensitive samples) and a small set of labeled sensitive data, with no labeled normal data at all?

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes an unlabeled dataset U (a mixture of normal and sensitive images, e.g., 90/10 split) and a small labeled sensitive dataset S, along with a hyperparameter β representing the sensitive fraction in U. At inference, the trained model takes standard Gaussian noise (unconditional) or noise plus a text prompt (conditional) and produces images restricted to the normal class. Experiments span 32×32 (MNIST, CIFAR-10), 96×96 (STL-10), and 256×256 (CelebA) RGB images, with both from-scratch training and fine-tuning of pre-trained models.

## Method

The key insight is a distributional rewrite: modeling the unlabeled distribution as p_U = βp_S + (1−β)p_N allows expressing the normal-data expectation as E_{p_U}[·] − βE_{p_S}[·], so the ELBO for normal data can be estimated without any labeled normal samples. The authors first embed a binary classification framework (from Yamanaka et al., 2019) into diffusion training, where the BCE loss ℓ_BCE(x, y; θ) is built on top of the standard DDPM noise-prediction loss ℓ(x; θ), making the loss bounded and well-balanced. Applying PU learning to this framework yields the objective L_PU(θ) = β·L_S^+(θ) + max{0, L_U^−(θ) − β·L_S^−(θ)}, where the max{0,·} term (from Kiryo et al., 2017) prevents overfitting when the negative-risk estimate goes negative. The model is trained with standard stochastic gradient descent (AdamW), and the approach extends naturally to conditional diffusion models by conditioning the noise estimator on a text prompt and using classifier-free guidance at sampling time.

## Results

On from-scratch training, the proposed PU diffusion model improves the non-sensitive rate from ~0.90 (unsupervised/supervised) to 0.975–0.978 on MNIST, 0.842 on CIFAR-10 (vehicles), and 0.944–0.947 on STL-10, while FID remains comparable (e.g., 32.5 vs. 34.4 on CIFAR-10 vehicles). In fine-tuning experiments, the method raises the non-sensitive rate to 0.973 on CelebA (male) and 0.954 on CIFAR-10 (animals), and achieves the best FID of 18.98 on CIFAR-10 (vehicles) versus 24.46 (supervised) and 26.71 (unsupervised). The key trade-off is a modest FID increase on MNIST from-scratch (e.g., 4.97 vs. 3.53) while the non-sensitive rate jumps by ~7–8 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) Unsupervised, which trains a standard DDPM on all unlabeled data assuming they are normal, and (2) Supervised, which applies the BCE-based binary classification loss (from Yamanaka et al., 2019) treating all unlabeled data as normal and the labeled set as sensitive. The proposed PU method wins decisively on the non-sensitive rate across all six from-scratch configurations, improving by 7–12 percentage points over both baselines (e.g., 0.978 vs. 0.908 on MNIST-even; 0.944 vs. 0.824 on STL-10 vehicles). The supervised baseline performs nearly identically to the unsupervised one, confirming that simply adding a BCE penalty on sensitive data is insufficient when sensitive examples are also present in the unlabeled pool. On FID, the proposed method is slightly worse on MNIST from-scratch (4.97 vs. 3.53) but matches or beats both baselines on CIFAR-10 and STL-10. In fine-tuning, the proposed method achieves the best FID on CIFAR-10 vehicles (18.98 vs. 24.46 supervised) while also delivering the highest non-sensitive rate, demonstrating that the PU formulation is particularly well-suited for adapting pre-trained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three previously separate threads: the binary classification framework for generative models from Yamanaka et al. (2019, originally for autoencoders), the unbiased PU learning formulation from Du Plessis et al. (2014), and the non-negative risk estimator trick from Kiryo et al. (2017). The key conceptual move is transplanting the Yamanaka BCE-based loss into the diffusion model ELBO and then wrapping the resulting supervised objective in the PU mixture decomposition p_U = βp_S + (1−β)p_N, yielding a training procedure that requires only unlabeled and sensitive data. This departs from prior work on erasing sensitive concepts in diffusion models (Heng & Soh 2024; Gandikota et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024), which all assume access to clean normal data or are restricted to conditional models, by operating in the more realistic PU setting where the unlabeled pool itself contains sensitive examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: training data scraped from the internet inevitably contains sensitive content, and manually curating clean normal data at scale is infeasible, making the PU formulation the realistic operating regime. A notable finding is that the supervised baseline (which assumes all unlabeled data is normal) performs nearly identically to the unsupervised baseline when sensitive data is actually present in the unlabeled set, confirming that the PU correction is necessary rather than merely beneficial. Open questions include the sensitivity to the hyperparameter β (the paper shows performance varies non-trivially with β and leaves optimal selection as future work), the modest FID degradation that persists across datasets, and whether the approach extends to more complex sensitive-concept definitions beyond binary class splits.
