## Problem

Multi-agent pathfinding (MAPF) solvers span classical search-based planners, pure MARL methods, and hybrid approaches, yet they are developed in disparate frameworks, environments, and datasets, making fair cross-paradigm comparison effectively impossible. Pure MARL methods (QMIX, MAMBA, MAPPO, etc.) are largely excluded from MAPF comparisons because no unified benchmark exists that is compatible with standard MARL learning frameworks while also supporting centralized planners and hybrid solvers.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a minimalist ego-centric observation tensor of three (2R+1)×(2R+1) binary matrices (static obstacles, other agents, goal projection) on a grid of blocked/free cells, with no global map access by default. The environment supports two problem settings: classical MAPF (unique goal per agent, episode ends when all agents arrive) and Lifelong MAPF (agents are immediately reassigned new goals upon arrival, episode runs to a fixed length). Outputs include per-agent actions (move to adjacent cell or wait) and primary indicators—sum-of-costs and makespan for MAPF, throughput for LMAPF—plus collision counts and success rate, from which six higher-level evaluation metrics are derived.

## Method

POGEMA is a benchmark platform rather than a single algorithm, comprising four integrated components: (1) a fast pure-Python grid environment (>10K steps/s, >1000 agents) with collision shielding and integration into PettingZoo, PyMARL, and Gymnasium; (2) a procedural map generator producing random, maze, and warehouse topologies with controllable obstacle density; (3) a Dask-based parallel benchmarking tool that runs any registered algorithm across map/agent/seed grids and aggregates results; and (4) a standardized evaluation protocol defining six domain-specific metrics—Performance, Out-of-Distribution, Cooperation, Scalability, Coordination, and Pathfinding—each computed from primary indicators (SoC, makespan, throughput, collision count) on purpose-built map sets (Mazes, Random, Warehouse, Puzzles, Cities, Cities-tiles). Twelve baselines spanning centralized planners (LaCAM, RHCR), hybrid solvers (SCRIMP, DCC, Follower, MATS-LP, Switcher), and pure MARL methods (QMIX, QPLEX, VDN, IQL, MAMBA) are evaluated under this protocol, revealing that centralized planners dominate on cooperation and OOD metrics while specialized hybrid methods outperform pure MARL on MAPF, and that MARL methods become competitive on LMAPF when using the Follower observation model.

## Results

On MAPF, the centralized planner LaCAM achieves 100% success rate (CSR) with a sum-of-costs (SoC) of 20.84±1.65 on Puzzles maps, while the best hybrid method SCRIMP reaches 85% CSR with SoC 77.86±11.09, and the best pure MARL method MAMBA only achieves 40% CSR with SoC 173.60±14.04. On LMAPF, the centralized planner RHCR attains 0.538±0.021 average throughput on Puzzles, compared to 0.394±0.021 for MATS-LP and 0.319±0.020 for Follower among decentralized methods. On the Out-of-Distribution (Cities-tiles) LMAPF evaluation with 256 agents, RHCR reaches 5.13±0.34 throughput while MATS-LP and Follower achieve 4.69±0.39 and 4.81±0.22 respectively, and all pure MARL methods fail to solve any MAPF out-of-distribution instance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For MAPF, LaCAM (centralized search) dominates all metrics, achieving 100% CSR on Puzzles and the best SoC across all map types; SCRIMP (hybrid with communication) is the best decentralized method, outperforming DCC by ~11% CSR and ~16 SoC on Puzzles, but SCRIMP paradoxically underperforms on single-agent pathfinding (makespan 755.98 vs. DCC's 189.56) due to its reliance on inter-agent communication. Among pure MARL baselines, MAMBA leads in performance and pathfinding (makespan 416.45) thanks to its communication mechanism, but all MARL methods (QMIX, QPLEX, VDN, IQL) show a substantial gap versus hybrid solvers and fail entirely on out-of-distribution Cities-tiles instances. For LMAPF, RHCR (centralized) is superior on all metrics except scalability; among decentralized methods, MATS-LP and Follower are the strongest, with MATS-LP outperforming Follower on cooperation (0.394 vs. 0.319 throughput) at the cost of higher test-time computation (MCTS per agent per step). Notably, QPLEX outperforms MAMBA on LMAPF (contrary to MAPF), and the Switcher hybrids (ASwitcher/LSwitcher) show mediocre results due to lacking global map knowledge, though they produce the fewest collisions among decentralized LMAPF methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

POGEMA positions itself at the intersection of two previously siloed communities: the classical MAPF search-based planning literature (CBS, ICTS, LaCAM, RHCR) and the multi-agent reinforcement learning literature (QMIX, QPLEX, VDN, MAMBA). While individual learning-based MAPF solvers (PRIMAL, PRIMAL_2, DCC, SCRIMP, Follower, MATS-LP) and MARL environments (Flatland, RWARE, SMAC) already existed, no unified framework supported fair comparison across all three paradigms—search-based, learning-based, and hybrid—under a common evaluation protocol. The evaluation protocol design draws inspiration from the SMAC standardized evaluation protocol (Gorsane et al., 2022), and the environment integrates with established MARL APIs (PettingZoo, PyMARL, Gymnasium) to lower the barrier for the MARL community to engage with MAPF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and persistent gap: centralized planners (LaCAM, RHCR) dominate all decentralized and learning-based approaches across every metric, with pure MARL methods (QMIX, QPLEX, VDN, IQL) trailing even specialized hybrid solvers by wide margins. This suggests that the MARL community's standard toolset is not yet adequate for large-scale, structured navigation tasks, and that the field needs either new algorithmic ideas or better integration of planning priors into learning pipelines. Key open questions include whether communication learning, large-scale CTDE training, or imitation from centralized solvers can close the gap, and whether the current observation model (minimalist ego-centric tensors) is a bottleneck. Limitations noted by the authors include the absence of disentangled communication channels and lack of JAX/GPU-parallelized support.
