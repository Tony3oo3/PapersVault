## Problem

Despite the widespread use of self-attention in Transformers, the geometry of the function spaces (neuromanifolds) they define remains largely unexplored. In particular, no prior work characterizes which parameter configurations yield the same function (identifiability), computes the dimension of the resulting function space, or identifies singular and boundary points for attention-based architectures. These quantities are fundamental for understanding sample complexity, training dynamics, and implicit bias in attention models.

## Input / Output

The lightning self-attention mechanism takes a sequence of t vectors in R^d as input and produces a sequence of t vectors in R^{d'}, with parameters Q, K ∈ R^{a×d} and V ∈ R^{d'×d}. Because the mechanism is tri-linear in the weights and homogeneous cubical in the input, the function space embeds in a finite-dimensional vector space of homogeneous polynomials, enabling algebraic-geometric analysis. The deep case reparametrizes the network via 'virtual weights' (M, L) that absorb inter-layer compositions, and the analysis outputs explicit dimension formulas, generic fiber descriptions, and characterizations of singular/boundary points of the neuromanifold.

## Method

The paper exploits the polynomial structure of lightning attention to apply algebraic geometry tools. For a single layer, unique factorization of polynomials shows that generic fibers are one-dimensional (rescaling of A and V), with special two-element fibers only when both A and V have rank 1; this yields a closed-form dimension formula and a characterization of singular points (rk(A)·rk(V) ≤ 1) and boundary points. For deep networks, the authors introduce a reparametrization via virtual weights (M, L) that captures the full network as a product of bilinear forms indexed by base-3 digits, then prove (Theorem 3.7) that generic fibers consist solely of layer-wise rescalings satisfying a single constraint, using a cancellation argument over symmetric monomial terms in the unrolled recursion tree. The dimension formula follows by subtracting the fiber dimensions (from GL_a symmetries, inter-layer GL_δ transformations, and rescaling) from the parameter count. For traditional softmax-normalized attention, the authors prove single-layer identifiability and conjecture (with numerical verification) that normalization eliminates the rescaling symmetry, making the deep parametrization generically one-to-one.

## Results

The paper derives closed-form dimension formulas for the neuromanifold of lightning self-attention networks. For a single layer with a ≤ d, the dimension is 2ad + dd′ − a² − 1; for deep networks with bottleneck architecture (dᵢ = δ for 0 < i < l), the dimension is 2α₁d₀ − α₁² + δ(d₀ + dₗ) − δ² − l + Σ(2αᵢδ − αᵢ²). A key quantitative finding is that when query/key dimensions equal the embedding dimension (αᵢ = δ), the number of parameters exceeds the neuromanifold dimension by a factor of 3/2, meaning 50% of parameters are redundant. Numerical verification (Figure 3) confirms that for normalized deep self-attention with l=2, t=3, aᵢ=2, the estimated dimension matches the predicted formula for all δ from 3 to 10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing methods in the standard empirical sense; instead, it compares the identifiability properties of lightning (unnormalized) versus traditional (softmax-normalized) self-attention. For a single layer, lightning self-attention has 1-dimensional generic fibers (a scaling symmetry), while traditional self-attention is generically one-to-one (Theorem 3.9). For deep networks, lightning self-attention retains three types of parameter symmetries (layer scaling, GLₐ reparametrization of keys/queries, and inter-layer cancellations), whereas the paper conjectures (Conjecture 3.10) that normalization eliminates the layer-scaling symmetry, making the parametrization via virtual weights (M, L) generically one-to-one. The dimension difference between the two cases is exactly l (the number of layers), which is negligible for large models. The numerical experiments in Figure 3 empirically confirm the conjectured dimension for the normalized case, matching the lightning formula plus l across all tested embedding dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neuromanifold framework—previously developed for fully-connected (Kileel et al., 2019) and convolutional networks (Kohn et al., 2022, 2023)—to self-attention mechanisms for the first time. It builds directly on the lightning self-attention variant (Schlag et al., 2021), which removes softmax normalization and makes the forward pass polynomial, thereby enabling the use of algebraic geometry tools (determinantal varieties, polynomial unique factorization, projective morphisms) that are inapplicable to the non-polynomial normalized case. The identifiability analysis connects to the broader parameter-identifiability literature (Grigsby et al., 2023; Bona-Pellissier et al., 2023) but is the first to characterize fibers for attention architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dimension formula (Corollary 3.8) provides a theoretically correct sample-complexity estimate that can differ by 50% from naive parameter counting, with direct implications for model selection at scale. The characterization of singular points as exactly the low-rank (rk(A)·rk(V)≤1) functions offers a mathematical explanation for the empirically observed implicit bias toward low-rank solutions in attention. Key limitations include the restriction to a simplified architecture (no skip connections, no multi-head attention) and the bottleneck assumption for the deep case; extending to these ubiquitous variants, and resolving Conjecture 3.10 for normalized deep attention, are the most natural next steps.
