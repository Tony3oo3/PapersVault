## Problem

While Lipschitz continuity (enforced via spectral norm clipping) improves the adversarial robustness of individual models, this paper shows it simultaneously reduces diversity among ensemble members by making their decision boundaries more similar, thereby increasing the transferability rate of adversarial examples between them. This trade-off between single-model robustness and ensemble diversity has not been previously identified, and it undermines the expected ensemble-level benefits of Lipschitz-constrained training.

## Input / Output

The method operates on ensembles of CNN classifiers (ResNet-18, ResNet-34, DLA) trained on CIFAR-10 (10 classes) or CIFAR-100 (100 classes) with 32×32 RGB inputs, producing standard class-label predictions. The orthogonalization loss consumes the top-k right singular vectors of each model's affine layers (computed via FastClip) and feeds them through the corresponding layers of other ensemble members, so the training-time input to the diversity term is layer-level spectral information rather than raw images. The method is applicable to both homogeneous ensembles (same architecture) and heterogeneous ensembles (different architectures), where only the first convolutional layer is orthogonalized since it shares the same input space.

## Method

LOTOS (Layer-wise Orthogonalization for Training rObust enSembles) augments the standard cross-entropy loss with a pairwise orthogonalization term that penalizes the output norm of each model's affine layer when applied to the top-k right singular vectors of the corresponding layer in another ensemble member, using a ReLU-based similarity measure with a tunable threshold (mal). This is combined with FastClip spectral norm clipping to maintain per-layer Lipschitz continuity while the orthogonalization loss enforces diversity in the dominant transformation subspaces. A key theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that for single-channel convolutional layers with circular padding, orthogonalizing only the top singular vector (k=1) suffices to bound the output on all remaining singular vectors, making the computational overhead negligible. The total training loss averages the cross-entropy over all N models and adds the orthogonalization term scaled by λ over all model pairs and layers, requiring only O(N²M) additional forward passes per iteration.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 ensembles, LOTOS achieves 36.3% robust accuracy against black-box PGD-50 attacks, outperforming the unmodified ensemble (Orig, 30.3%) by 6 p.p. and the spectrally-clipped ensemble (C=1.0, 35.2%) by 1.1 p.p. On CIFAR-100, LOTOS reaches 20.2% robust accuracy versus 15.2% (Orig) and 18.9% (C=1.0). For DLA ensembles on CIFAR-10, LOTOS achieves 34.5% robust accuracy compared to 26.7% (Orig) and 32.8% (C=1.0). When combined with TRS, LOTOS boosts robust accuracy to 41.5% on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), a 10.7 p.p. improvement over TRS alone (30.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the spectrally-clipped baseline (C=1.0 via FastClip), LOTOS consistently wins on robust accuracy across all four architecture/dataset combinations in Table 1, with gains of 1.1–1.6 p.p., while maintaining comparable test accuracy. Against the unmodified ensemble (Orig), LOTOS achieves a 6 p.p. improvement on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18) and 3.7 p.p. on CIFAR-100 (DLA). When combined with TRS (Yang et al., 2021), LOTOS boosts robust accuracy from 30.8% to 41.5% on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18) and from 12.3% to 20.7% on CIFAR-100, a 10.7 p.p. gain. Against DVERGE (Yang et al., 2020) under the MORA white-box attack, LOTOS+DVERGE reaches 29.2% robust accuracy versus 19.7% for DVERGE alone (Table 7). Against adversarial training (Madry et al., 2017), LOTOS+ADV achieves 61.7% robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18) versus 60.1% for ADV alone, confirming LOTOS is complementary to standard robustness techniques.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: Lipschitz-continuity-based robustness (Szegedy et al. 2013; Farnia et al. 2018; Boroojeny et al. 2024/FastClip) and ensemble-diversity-based transferability resistance (Kariyappa & Qureshi 2019; Yang et al. 2020/DVERGE; Yang et al. 2021/TRS). Prior work treats these threads independently — Lipschitz methods target single-model robustness while diversity methods target ensemble-level transferability — and no prior study examines their interaction. LOTOS departs from both by introducing a cross-model orthogonalization loss on the top-k singular-vector subspaces of corresponding affine layers, building directly on the FastClip spectral-norm infrastructure while adding a pairwise diversity term that prior ensemble methods (which operate on gradients, outputs, or latent features) do not exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a non-obvious tension: the universally recommended Lipschitz-continuity constraint, while improving individual model robustness, increases transferability among ensemble members and thereby undermines the ensemble's collective defense — a trade-off invisible when the two research threads are studied in isolation. LOTOS is practical (negligible overhead, k=1 suffices for conv layers per Theorem 4.1), composable with existing SOTA methods (TRS, DVERGE, adversarial training), and extends to heterogeneous ensembles where prior methods are inapplicable. Limitations include reliance on CIFAR-scale datasets, sensitivity to batch normalization (which complicates exact spectral-norm control), and the fact that the orthogonalization loss adds a pairwise O(N²) term that could become costly for very large ensembles. Open questions include whether the Lipschitz-transferability trade-off persists at ImageNet scale and whether the first-layer-only orthogonalization (sufficient for heterogeneous ensembles) generalizes to deeper architectural differences.
