## Problem

Large multimodal models (LMMs) that rely on contrastive image encoders like CLIP-ViT capture biased, task-relevant semantic features and disregard fine visual details, causing them to fail on out-of-distribution data such as natural adversarial examples, unusual textures, and rare materials. This leads to accumulated perceptual errors, visual hallucinations, and poor robustness. The paper asks whether diffusion models, which learn full image distributions without task-specific objectives, can serve as corrective 'eyes' for LLMs to detect and fix these semantic biases.

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved image-text sequences where images are encoded at 256×256 (pre-training) or 448×448 (fine-tuning) resolution and text is processed autoregressively. For region-level tasks, a binary mask is applied via a simple dot-product operation on the image embedding to extract spatially localized features. Outputs include autoregressive text (VQA answers, captions), 512×512 generated images via the diffusion decoder, and region-level captions conditioned on mask-restricted visual tokens.

## Method

DEEM introduces a Consistency Semantic Regularization (CSR) loss that reuses the diffusion model as a generative check on the image encoder's output: the diffusion model reconstructs the original image conditioned on the image encoder's tokens (e_n) rather than the LLM's hidden states, forcing the encoder to preserve fine-grained visual detail that would otherwise be compressed away. The architecture pairs a small CLIP-ConvNeXt-B encoder (122M params) with Vicuna-7B and Stable Diffusion v2.1, connected via two Perceiver Resamplers. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) alignment pre-training with combined next-token prediction, next-image prediction, and CSR losses; (2) image-text instruction fine-tuning with NTP + CSR; and (3) mask-text fine-tuning with NTP only for region-level understanding. The key design choice is freezing the diffusion model while unfreezing the visual foundation model, which ablations show yields the best robustness and hallucination results without adding extra trainable parameters or modules.

## Results

On the newly introduced RobustVQA benchmark (averaging RobustVQA-A/R/V), DEEM achieves 62.8% accuracy after pre-training alone (no SFT), already surpassing the larger MM-Interleaved (51.4%) and SEED-X (57.0%). After supervised fine-tuning, DEEM-VQA reaches 65.56% average across all seven robustness/hallucination/perception benchmarks, outperforming SEED-X (63.39%) and DreamLLM (61.84%) while using a smaller image encoder (CLIP-ConvNeXt-B, 122M params) and language model (Vicuna-7B vs. Vicuna-13B). On POPE hallucination detection, DEEM-VQA scores 87.40/82.80/78.49 (R/P/A), exceeding SEED-X (86.41/81.43/74.56) and DreamLLM (86.36/80.07/72.63). On MMVP basic visual perception, DEEM-VQA achieves 32.89%, the highest among all compared models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEEM-VQA (65.56 AVG) outperforms the strongest baseline SEED-X (63.39) by 2.17 points and DreamLLM (61.84) by 3.72 points, despite using a substantially smaller image encoder (CLIP-ConvNeXt-B, 122M) and language model (Vicuna-7B) compared to SEED-X's ViT-G/Vicuna-13B and DreamLLM's ViT-L/Vicuna-7B. Against the primary interleaved-generation competitor MM-Interleaved (ViT-L/Vicuna-13B, 53.31 AVG), DEEM gains 9.4% on RobustVQA, 17.8% on POPE, and 9.1% on MMVP. The ablation baseline DEEM* (without diffusion feedback, 48.40 AVG) confirms that the consistency semantic regularization contributes a 4% average gain on RobustVQA, 12.8% on POPE, and 6.5% on MMVP. On multimodal comprehension (Table 2), DEEM-VQA matches or exceeds DreamLLM on 6 of 7 shared splits (e.g., COCO CIDEr 115.4 vs. 103.7, OKVQA 53.4 vs. 52.2, VizWiz 50.4 vs. 49.3) while trailing only on VQAv2 (68.2 vs. 72.9). For text-to-image generation, DEEM-7B achieves FID 8.89 on MS-COCO, comparable to DreamLLM-7B (8.76) and far below dedicated generators like Parti-20B (7.23). On region-level captioning (RefCOCOg), DEEM-Mask scores CIDEr 71.0 and METEOR 14.1, competitive with GRIT (CIDEr 71.6) and surpassing Kosmos-2 and NeXt-Chat.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEEM builds directly on the end-to-end interleaved image-text generative modeling paradigm established by DreamLLM and MM-Interleaved, which pair a VFM-based image encoder with a diffusion model for image synthesis within a unified LLM framework. The key conceptual departure is repurposing the diffusion model—already present in these architectures solely for image generation—as a self-supervised perceptual regularizer that provides generative feedback to correct semantic bias in the image encoder. This transforms the diffusion model from a generation-only component into a dual-role module that also serves as a 'second eye' for the LLM, a usage the authors claim is the first in the LMM literature. The paper also introduces RobustVQA, a new benchmark constructed from ImageNet-A/R/V2 in VQA format, filling a gap in systematic robustness evaluation for multimodal models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated failure mode of LMMs—degraded perception on out-of-distribution and adversarial inputs—by showing that the diffusion model's learned image distribution can implicitly encode fine-grained visual details that contrastive-trained encoders discard. The approach is attractive for practitioners because it requires no additional modules or parameters, and the architecture-agnostic validation on LLaVA suggests broad applicability. However, the paper honestly acknowledges that robustness gains partially erode during supervised fine-tuning (only alleviated, not eliminated), and that scaling to larger encoders like ViT-L/G increases training overhead. An open question the authors raise is whether diffusion models could eventually fully replace image encoders, which would fundamentally restructure LMM architectures.
