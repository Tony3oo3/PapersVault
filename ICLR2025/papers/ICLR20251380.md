## Problem

Controllable image-to-video generation currently requires expensive fine-tuning on annotated motion data to follow bounding box or trajectory signals, while zero-shot trajectory control methods developed for text-to-video models do not transfer to image-conditioned generation. A key obstacle is that, unlike image diffusion models, the feature maps in video diffusion models (e.g., SVD) exhibit only weak semantic correspondence across frames, so directly manipulating upsampling-block features fails to provide useful layout guidance for motion control.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB conditioning image (576×1024) and a set of B bounding box trajectories, each specified by a fixed height, width, and per-frame center coordinates across N=14 output frames. Bounding boxes placed on foreground objects control object motion, while boxes on static background regions with an opposite trajectory control camera movement; a zero trajectory keeps a region static. The output is a 14-frame video at the full 576×1024 resolution of the base SVD model, with no resolution downscaling required as in supervised baselines.

## Method

SG-I2V is a zero-shot, self-guided framework built on Stable Video Diffusion that controls motion by optimizing the noisy latent at early denoising timesteps (t=30–45). The key insight is that spatial self-attention features in SVD are not semantically aligned across frames; the paper fixes this by replacing the key and value tokens of every frame with those of the first frame (with stop-gradient), producing cross-frame aligned feature maps that encode consistent object layout. The latent z_t is then optimized via a loss that enforces similarity between aligned features within each bounding box trajectory, weighted by a Gaussian heatmap to focus on the box center. A frequency-based post-processing step (FFT with a Butterworth low-pass filter at γ=0.5) replaces the high-frequency components of the optimized latent with those of the original latent, preventing out-of-distribution artifacts while preserving the low-frequency motion signal.

## Results

On the VIPSeg validation set, SG-I2V achieves FID 28.87, FVD 298.10, and ObjMC 14.43, outperforming all zero-shot baselines across every metric. Compared to supervised baselines DragNUWA v1.5 (ObjMC 10.84) and DragAnything (ObjMC 11.64), SG-I2V narrows the motion fidelity gap to roughly 3–4 points while maintaining the full 576×1024 resolution (vs. 320×576 for supervised methods). SG-I2V also achieves the best FID (28.87) among all methods, including the uncontrolled SVD baseline (30.50), indicating that trajectory optimization does not degrade visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against supervised baselines, SG-I2V achieves ObjMC 14.43 vs. 10.84 (DragNUWA v1.5) and 11.64 (DragAnything), a gap of roughly 3–4 points, while matching or exceeding them on FID (28.87 vs. 30.73/30.81) and operating at 576×1024 resolution versus their 320×576. Image Conductor (based on AnimateDiffv3) is substantially weaker on all metrics (ObjMC 21.07, FID 48.81), attributed to the limited capacity of its base model. Among zero-shot baselines, SG-I2V dominates: it reduces ObjMC from 31.49 (DragDiffusion†), 33.58 (MOFT†), and 36.43 (FreeTraj†) down to 14.43, while also achieving the best FID and FVD. FreeTraj† in particular degrades visual quality severely (FID 46.61), showing that hand-crafted noise initialization does not transfer well to image-to-video models. The paper also notes qualitative advantages: DragNUWA tends to distort objects rather than move them, and DragAnything struggles with part-level control, whereas SG-I2V handles both entity- and part-level trajectories naturally.

## Relation to Prior Work

SG-I2V builds on Stable Video Diffusion as its base model and adapts the latent optimization paradigm from DragDiffusion (enforcing cross-frame feature similarity within bounding boxes) to the video setting. The key conceptual departure is the finding that, unlike in image diffusion models where upsampling block features are semantically aligned (DIFT), video diffusion models exhibit weak cross-frame alignment in those same features; instead, the authors show that modifying spatial self-attention to attend to the first frame's key/value tokens restores alignment. The frequency-based post-processing step is adapted from FreeInit's observation that motion is encoded in low-frequency latent components, and the bounding box trajectory formulation and Gaussian heatmap weighting follow DragAnything.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first zero-shot trajectory control method for image-to-video generation, removing the need for expensive fine-tuning and annotated motion datasets that supervised methods like DragAnything and DragNUWA require. The empirical finding that video diffusion models lack the cross-frame semantic alignment present in image diffusion models is a useful diagnostic for anyone building on video diffusion architectures. Key limitations include quality bounded by the base SVD model, residual out-of-distribution artifacts from latent optimization, and the open problem of how to modify the denoising process while preserving in-distribution latents. The framework could be extended to newer, higher-quality video generators as they become available.
