## Problem

Existing visual reprogramming (VR) methods adapt frozen pre-trained classifiers to new target tasks via input transformations and label mappings, but none have considered adversarial robustness. The paper demonstrates that reprogrammed models built from naturally trained backbones exhibit near-zero robustness under PGD attacks, posing a critical risk in safety-sensitive domains like autonomous driving and medical diagnosis. This is the first work to systematically study and theoretically bound adversarial robustness in the VR setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes target-domain RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, variable-size for GTSRB traffic signs) and produces a single class label from the target label space (10, 43, or 100 classes). Target images are first transformed to the frozen source model's 224×224 input via padding or resizing with a learnable offset, and the source model's 1000-dimensional output is mapped to the target label space through a one-to-one or fully-connected label mapping. The pre-trained backbone (ResNet-18/50 or XCiT-S12) remains entirely frozen throughout reprogramming.

## Method

The paper identifies two complementary strategies for enhancing VR robustness: (1) reprogramming an adversarially pre-trained source model instead of a naturally trained one, and (2) incorporating target-domain adversarial examples during reprogramming via PGD or TRADES-style training on the input transformation and label mapping parameters. The adversarial VR training objective (Eq. 7–8) minimizes the natural loss plus a KL-divergence-based robustness regularization between clean and adversarial predictions, with only f_in and f_out being trainable. Theoretically, the paper derives the first adversarial risk upper bound for VR (Theorem 1), showing that target-domain adversarial risk is bounded by the source model's adversarial margin risk, an Adversarial Margin Disparity Discrepancy (AMDD-VRA) measuring cross-domain inconsistency, and the target-domain margin risk of the optimal source model—formally justifying why both strategies reduce the upper bound.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with XCiT-S12, reprogramming a naturally pre-trained model with natural training (Strategy 1) yields near-zero adversarial robustness (e.g., 0.13% for Pad/FullyLM, 0.06% for Pad/RandLM) despite high clean accuracy (80.72% and 68.49% respectively). Combining an adversarially pre-trained model with adversarial training during reprogramming (Strategy 4) achieves the best robustness: 57.09% for Full/FullyLM, 43.59% for Pad/FullyLM, and 22.84% for Pad/RandLM. On GTSRB, the same combination (Strategy 4) reaches 50.37% robustness for Full/FullyLM versus only 1.74% for Strategy 1. The paper also provides a theoretical adversarial risk upper bound (Theorem 1) showing that VR robustness is governed by the source model's adversarial margin risk, the AMDD-VRA domain discrepancy, and the target-domain adversarial risk of the optimal source model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external VR baselines but instead ablates four strategy combinations (natural vs. adversarial pre-trained model × natural vs. adversarial training). Strategy 4 (adversarial pre-trained + adversarial training) consistently outperforms all other strategies across all datasets and VR configurations. On CIFAR-10, Strategy 4 improves robustness over Strategy 1 by 45+ percentage points for Full/FullyLM (57.09% vs. 12.09%) and by ~23 points for Pad/RandLM (22.84% vs. 0.06%). Strategy 3 (adversarial model only) generally outperforms Strategy 2 (adversarial training only), indicating that the pre-trained model's inherent robustness contributes more than target-domain adversarial training alone. However, the gap between Strategy 4 and Strategy 3 is smaller than the gap between Strategy 3 and Strategy 1, suggesting diminishing returns from adversarial training when the pre-trained model is already robust. The paper notes that adversarial training during VR yields more pronounced gains with larger-parameter VR methods (SMM, FullyLM) due to the limited trainable parameter space in VR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Visual Reprogramming (VR) framework of Tsai et al. (ICML 2020) by incorporating adversarial training into the reprogramming objective, adapting both PGD-AT and the TRADES KL-divergence regularization (Zhang et al., ICML 2019) to the parameter-constrained VR setting where only input transformations and label mappings are trainable. The theoretical analysis draws on margin-based domain adaptation theory (Zhang et al., ICML 2019) to derive the first adversarial risk upper bound for VR, introducing the Adversarial Margin Disparity Discrepancy (AMDD-VRA) as a domain-gap measure. The work is distinct from C-AVP (Chen et al., 2023a), which improves robustness of a pre-trained model within its original task, whereas this paper addresses cross-task robustness transfer through reprogramming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that reprogrammed naturally pre-trained models exhibit virtually zero adversarial robustness despite high clean accuracy is a critical safety concern for VR deployment in domains like autonomous driving and medical diagnosis. The theoretical bound provides a principled decomposition of adversarial risk into source-model robustness, domain discrepancy, and target-domain optimal risk, guiding future VR design. However, the absolute robustness gains remain modest (best ~57% on CIFAR-10), and the paper acknowledges that the finite parameter space of VR limits the effectiveness of adversarial training. Open questions include developing VR-specific adversarial training methods and designing richer f_in/f_out architectures that better preserve or amplify source-domain robustness.
