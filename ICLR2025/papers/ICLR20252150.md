## Problem

In the hidden state threat model for DP-SGD (where only the final model is released, not intermediate checkpoints), prior privacy auditing using loss-based canary adversaries yields lower bounds that fall significantly short of the theoretical upper bounds from privacy accounting. It remains unclear whether this gap reflects genuine privacy amplification by iteration or the suboptimality of existing adversaries, and how to design worst-case adversaries when intermediate models are concealed.

## Input / Output

The adversary operates offline before training: it selects a single biased dimension d and constructs a gradient vector ∇* with magnitude C (the clipping threshold) in that dimension and zeros elsewhere, without knowledge of intermediate models. During auditing, the crafted gradient is inserted at every k-th optimization step alongside genuine clipped gradients from the training batch, and the confidence score is simply θ_T[d] − θ_0[d]. The output is a high-probability lower bound ε̂ on the privacy loss at a fixed δ = 10⁻⁵, computed via Gaussian DP auditing with Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals over 5000 training runs.

## Method

The key insight is that all DP-SGD privacy accounting techniques are designed to protect against worst-case bounded gradients, so the adversary can directly specify the gradient sequence a canary would have produced, abstracting away the need to find a data point that generates it. Two gradient-crafting adversaries are proposed: A_GC-R picks a random dimension and achieves tight auditing (matching the Gopi et al. upper bound) for over-parameterized models at periodicity k=1, while A_GC-S simulates the training process to select the least-updated dimension, recovering tight results for low-dimensional models (e.g., a 68-parameter FCNN). For periodicity k>1, a new adversary A_S^{h*} crafts both the gradient and a non-convex loss landscape (a step function g that outputs ±BC based on a threshold h*=C/2) to maximally bias subsequent genuine gradients, revealing two regimes: tight auditing when batch size is large relative to noise variance, and evidence of weaker privacy amplification for non-convex problems when the batch is small relative to noise.

## Results

When the crafted gradient is inserted at every optimization step (k=1), the gradient-crafting adversary A_GC-R achieves tight auditing on over-parameterized models (ConvNet and ResNet on CIFAR-10), meaning the empirical privacy lower bound ε̂ matches the theoretical upper bound from the Gopi et al. (2021) accountant across all tested clipping thresholds C∈{1,2,4}. On the low-dimensional FCNN (68 parameters, Housing dataset), A_GC-S (which simulates training to select the optimal dimension) recovers nearly tight results, while A_GC-R degrades. At higher periodicities (k=5, k=25), A_GC still outperforms the loss-based baseline A_L by a large margin but no longer matches the upper bound, with the gap widening as k increases. The loss-landscape-crafting adversary A_S^{h*} reveals two regimes: for large batch size relative to noise variance, the audit is tight (no privacy amplification); for small batch size relative to noise, privacy amplification occurs but the privacy loss converges to a positive constant rather than decaying as O(1/√T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the loss-based adversary A_L (from Nasr et al. 2023 and Steinke et al. 2023), which flips a training point's label to create a canary and uses model loss as the confidence score, the proposed A_GC-R achieves tight auditing at k=1 on ConvNet and ResNet (CIFAR-10), while A_L remains significantly below the upper bound and is described as 'very loose in some regimes.' At k=5 and k=25, A_GC still outperforms A_L by a large margin, though neither matches the upper bound. Against the cosine-similarity adversary A_CotS from Andrew et al. (2024), which crafts a random unit-sphere gradient and uses cosine similarity as the confidence score, A_GC outperforms on the FCNN/Housing setup (Figure 7 in Appendix). The key distinction from Nasr et al. (2023) is that their gradient-crafting adversary requires access to all intermediate models (standard threat model), whereas A_GC operates entirely in the hidden state model without any intermediate model access, yet achieves the same tight auditing at k=1. The A_S^{h*} adversary, which additionally crafts the loss landscape, is the first to demonstrate that the Gopi et al. (2021) upper bound is tight in the hidden state model for large batch sizes, while also providing the first empirical evidence of privacy amplification for non-convex problems at small batch sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the gradient-crafting adversarial framework of Nasr et al. (2021, 2023) to the hidden state threat model, where intermediate model checkpoints are concealed from the adversary. Prior attempts to audit the hidden state model (Nasr et al. 2023; Steinke et al. 2023) relied on loss-based canary adversaries that exhibited a significant gap from the privacy accounting upper bound; here, the authors instead craft gradient sequences directly, decoupling the privacy leakage analysis from the craftability of a specific data point. The paper also introduces a new adversary class (A_S) that crafts both the gradient and the loss landscape, extending the analysis to the privacy amplification by iteration phenomenon (Feldman et al. 2018; Balle et al. 2019) in the non-convex regime where prior theoretical results do not apply.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a key open question: whether concealing intermediate models amplifies privacy for non-convex DP-SGD. The negative result at k=1 (no amplification when the data point is used at every step) has direct practical implications for deployment scenarios where only the final model is released. The two-regime finding at k>1 (tight audit for large batches, evidence of weaker amplification for small batches) suggests existing privacy upper bounds may be improvable in certain regimes, and has consequences for machine unlearning guarantees in non-convex settings. A remaining open question is the gap between real-dataset auditing results and the loss-landscape-crafting adversary at k>1, which may require understanding how specific architectures can be manipulated to produce worst-case gradient sequences.
