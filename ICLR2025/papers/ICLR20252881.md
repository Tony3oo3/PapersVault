## Problem

Existing Transformer-based time series forecasting (TSF) models suffer from timestep mixing, permutation invariance, and fixed channel structure, causing overfitting on real-world datasets with weak inter-series dependencies. While LLM-based TSF methods have improved few-shot and zero-shot generalization, they adapt pre-trained LLM parameters to TSF rather than directly exploiting the in-context learning (ICL) mechanism, resulting in inefficient parameter use and high computational cost. No prior work directly applies the fundamental ICL token formulation—(input, label) pairs as tokens—to TSF in a parameter-efficient, architecture-native way.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{C×L} split into a lookback window X_I (L_I=1440) and a forecasting horizon X_P (L_P ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}), and produces predicted future values X̂_P ∈ R^{C×L_P}. Unusually, the input tokens are not individual timesteps or full series but (L_b + L_P)-dimensional vectors representing (lookback, future) pairs, with L_b=512. Stepwise sampling (step m=8) and a token retrieval module (top q=10% by cosine similarity, remaining merged into r=30 tokens) control the total number of context tokens to manage computational cost.

## Method

ICTSP reformulates TSF as an in-context learning problem by constructing N = L_I − L_b − L_P ground-truth (lookback, future) pairs via stepwise sampling as context tokens, plus one target token with zero-padded future values, all of dimension (L_b + L_P). These tokens are processed by a standard 3-layer pre-normalization Transformer (d=128, 8 heads), where the attention mechanism adaptively selects the best predictor from the context examples. The architecture supports sequential adaptive model reduction: when context examples are unhelpful it degrades to a Series-wise Transformer, and when inter-series information is irrelevant it further reduces to a univariate MLP or linear predictor, preventing overfitting on noisy data. Token retrieval selects the most relevant context tokens via cosine similarity to target tokens and merges the remainder via grouped weighted averaging, reducing FLOPs by ~85% without significant accuracy loss. Positional embeddings only distinguish series identity rather than encoding channel characteristics, enabling zero-shot transfer to datasets with arbitrary numbers of channels.

## Results

On full-data time series forecasting (averaged MSE over 4 horizons × 7 datasets = 28 results), ICTSP achieves an average rank of 1.36 and is best on 18/28 individual results. ICTSP attains the lowest MSE on 6 of 7 datasets (ETTh1: 0.404, ETTh2: 0.328, ETTm2: 0.247, Weather: 0.218, ECL: 0.154, Traffic: 0.386), losing only to Time-LLM on ETTm1 (0.342 vs. 0.329). In few-shot (10%/5% training data) and zero-shot transfer settings, ICTSP extends its lead, achieving average ranks of 1.57/1.56 and 1.13 respectively, with 15/12/21 first-place results out of 28/24/24.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICTSP outperforms the strongest LLM-based baseline Time-LLM on 6 of 7 datasets (e.g., ECL: 0.154 vs. 0.159; Traffic: 0.386 vs. 0.388), losing only on ETTm1 (0.342 vs. 0.329). Against the Series-wise Transformer iTransformer, ICTSP wins on all 7 datasets with margins up to 0.122 (ETTm2: 0.247 vs. 0.269). ICTSP also consistently beats the simple DLinear baseline on all 7 datasets, with the largest gap on Traffic (0.386 vs. 0.434) and ETTh2 (0.328 vs. 0.431). Compared to the Temporal-wise Transformer PatchTST, ICTSP wins on all 7 datasets. In zero-shot transfer (e.g., ETTh1→ETTh2), ICTSP achieves 0.337 MSE versus Time-LLM's 0.352 and GPT4TS's 0.407, while LLMTime degrades severely (0.992). The gap over older Temporal-wise models (FEDformer, Autoformer, Informer) is substantial, with ICTSP achieving 30–70% lower MSE on most datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICTSP repurposes the in-context learning (ICL) framework from NLP—where Transformers learn functions from (input, label) token pairs—by treating (lookback, future) forecasting task pairs as tokens rather than timestep values (Temporal-wise Transformers like Informer, Autoformer, PatchTST) or entire series (Series-wise Transformers like iTransformer). The paper provides a unifying ICL-based explanation for why Temporal-wise Transformers overfit (they learn channel-structure mappings rather than temporal predictors) and shows that prior 'solutions' (channel independence, patching, random dropout) are all partial fixes for this same token-formulation mismatch. It also positions itself against LLM-based TSF methods (Time-LLM, GPT4TS, LLMTime) as a more parameter-efficient alternative that directly encodes TSF structure rather than adapting pre-trained language model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive model reduction property—where ICTSP gracefully degrades from a full ICL predictor to a Series-wise Transformer to a univariate linear predictor depending on dataset complexity—offers a principled explanation for why complex Transformers have struggled to beat DLinear on noisy real-world data, and provides a single architecture that handles both regimes. The strong zero-shot transfer results (e.g., 0.337 vs. 0.992 MSE for LLMTime on ETTh1→ETTh2) suggest a path toward universal TSF models without dataset-specific retraining. Key open questions include whether the architecture scales with more layers and larger datasets, and whether a single global model can handle arbitrary lookback/future length combinations as the token structure theoretically permits.
