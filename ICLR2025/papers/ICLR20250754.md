## Problem

Hierarchical classification is typically framed as multiple independent single-level tasks, but each level relies on different visual cues (global features for coarse, local details for fine). Prior methods enforce consistency through external semantic supervision (e.g., label relation losses), which is a statistical criterion that fails to guarantee consistent visual grounding at test time, resulting in classifiers attending to entirely different image regions and producing taxonomy-violating predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250754/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (224×224) and replaces fixed ViT patches with 196 SEEDS superpixels as input tokens. It simultaneously predicts class labels at every level of the taxonomy (e.g., order → family → species) and produces an internal coarse-to-fine segmentation (196→64→32→16→8 superpixels) as a byproduct of the architecture. The segmentation is unsupervised—no pixel-level annotations are required—and the model outputs a single set of aligned predictions across all hierarchy levels rather than independent per-level classifiers.

## Method

H-CAST extends CAST's graph-pooling architecture by imposing fine-to-coarse semantic classification losses at progressively coarser stages of the visual parsing process: fine-grained labels are supervised at early encoder blocks (196/64 superpixels) and coarse labels at later blocks (16/8 superpixels), so that fine details (e.g., beak, wings) are naturally grouped into broader object regions (e.g., whole bird) through shared feature propagation. A novel Tree-Path KL Divergence loss concatenates the softmax outputs of all level-specific classification heads and aligns them via KL divergence against a concatenated one-hot ground-truth distribution, penalizing predictions that violate parent-child taxonomy relations. The total loss is L = L_HV + α·L_TK, where L_HV is the sum of per-level cross-entropy terms. This fine-to-coarse (F→C) design was validated over coarse-to-fine and merged alternatives, and the architecture requires no pixel-level segmentation annotations—hierarchical taxonomy labels alone drive both classification and segmentation quality.

## Results

On the BREEDS benchmark (4 two-level hierarchies), H-CAST achieves Full-Path Accuracy (FPA) of 85.12 (Living-17), 82.67 (Non-Living-26), 85.68 (Entity-13), and 84.83 (Entity-30), outperforming the best prior method HRN by 4.3–6.4 points while using only 26.2M parameters vs. HRN's 70.8M. On the large-scale iNaturalist2021-Mini (3-level, 10K classes), H-CAST reaches 64.92% FPA and 16.00% TICE, improving over TransHP by +5.98 FPA and reducing inconsistency by 8.63 points. H-CAST also surpasses zero-shot CLIP by roughly 35 points in FPA on Entity-13, where CLIP achieves only ~50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-CAST decisively outperforms all ViT-based hierarchical baselines: it beats Hier-ViT by +11.06 FPA (Entity-13) and TransHP by +12.23 FPA (Entity-30), demonstrating that visual grounding via hierarchical segmentation and the Tree-Path KL loss contribute far more than simply adding multi-level supervision to a ViT backbone. Against the strongest ResNet-based method HRN, H-CAST gains +4.25 to +6.36 FPA across BREEDS with 63% fewer parameters, though HRN (batch size 8) still leads on the smaller CUB-200-2011 (+0.87 FPA) and FGVC-Aircraft (+7.67 FPA) datasets. H-CAST also surpasses flat baselines (Flat-ViT, Flat-CAST) and their HiE-augmented variants by 3–18 FPA points, confirming that a single jointly-trained hierarchical model outperforms separate per-level classifiers. Zero-shot CLIP achieves only ~50% FPA on Entity-13, roughly 35 points below H-CAST, and BIOCLIP (zero-shot) scores 78.18 FPA on CUB versus H-CAST's 83.28. On the large-scale iNaturalist2021-Mini, H-CAST improves FPA by +5.98 over TransHP and +9.27 over Hier-ViT while reducing TICE from 24.63 to 16.00.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CAST (Ke et al., 2024), a hierarchical segmentation framework using graph pooling over SEEDS superpixels, by imposing fine-to-coarse classification losses at intermediate stages rather than only at the final flat recognition stage. It departs from prior hierarchical classification methods (FGN, HRN, TransHP) that rely on external semantic supervision or separate branches per level, arguing that statistical alignment during training does not guarantee visual consistency at test time. The key conceptual departure is treating part-to-whole visual grouping as a natural analogue to taxonomy hierarchy, a connection the authors claim has not been previously explored in hierarchical classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even zero-shot CLIP achieves only ~50% full-path accuracy on BREEDS Entity-13, underscoring that hierarchical consistency remains an open problem for vision foundation models. The finding that hierarchical taxonomy supervision improves segmentation quality without pixel-level annotations suggests a broader principle: classification structure can regularize spatial parsing. Limitations include reliance on SEEDS superpixels (which may not scale to very high-resolution or video inputs), evaluation primarily on 2–3 level hierarchies, and the fact that on small datasets like Aircraft, ResNet-based HRN with batch size 8 still outperforms H-CAST, suggesting the method's advantage is most pronounced at scale.
