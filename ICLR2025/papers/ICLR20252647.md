## Problem

Existing LLM-based theorem proving approaches are trained or fine-tuned on static datasets (e.g., mathlib4) and fail to generalize to advanced mathematics or new domains. Mathematicians formalize across multiple domains simultaneously or cyclically, yet no system can continuously adapt to ever-expanding mathematical knowledge without catastrophic forgetting of previously learned concepts. This paper addresses the absence of a lifelong learning framework for formal theorem proving that balances stability and plasticity across diverse, evolving mathematical repositories.

## Input / Output

LeanAgent consumes Lean repositories (cloned from GitHub) and uses LeanDojo to extract fine-grained theorem statements, tactic-annotated proofs, and a premise corpus (a JSON Lines file of file paths, imports, and premise/definition statements). For each sorry theorem, it takes the theorem statement and current proof state as input and produces a verified formal proof (a sequence of Lean tactics) via best-first tree search with a 10-minute time limit. The system also outputs an updated ByT5-based retriever with refreshed premise embeddings after each progressive training step, and newly proven theorems are appended to the dynamic database for future use.

## Method

LeanAgent combines three key components: (1) curriculum learning that computes theorem complexity as e^S (S = proof steps), uses 33rd/67th percentiles to classify theorems as easy/medium/hard, and sorts repositories by easy-theorem count to establish a learning trajectory from basic to advanced mathematics; (2) progressive training of a ByT5-based retriever for exactly one epoch per new repository dataset, which balances stability and plasticity by limiting overfitting while incorporating new premises into the embedding space; and (3) a best-first tree search prover that retrieves top-25% relevant premises via the updated retriever (filtered by a corpus dependency graph), generates 64 tactic candidates via beam search (size 5), and explores proof states with backtracking. A custom dynamic database tracks all theorems, premises, proof states, and repository metadata, enabling efficient dataset generation and knowledge reuse across the learning lifecycle.

## Results

LeanAgent successfully generates formal proofs for 155 previously unproven (sorry) theorems across 23 diverse Lean repositories, including advanced mathematics in abstract algebra and algebraic topology. On the Mathematics in Lean Source repository, LeanAgent achieves 72.4% accuracy (21/29 sorry theorems proven), substantially outperforming the static ReProver baseline (48.3%) and the ReProver+ fine-tuning baseline (55.2%). In lifelong learning metrics, LeanAgent achieves a WF5 of 0.18 (75.34% lower than the next best setup) and an EBWT of 1.21 (16.25% higher than the best alternative), demonstrating superior stability and backward transfer. LeanAgent is the only system to prove any sorry theorem from the PFR repository (2.7%) and the Hairy Ball Theorem repository (7.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LeanAgent against two baselines: ReProver (the static retrieval-augmented LLM from LeanDojo, used as the initial retriever) and ReProver+ (ReProver fine-tuned on all 23 repositories simultaneously, isolating the effect of curriculum learning). LeanAgent outperforms ReProver on 7 out of 9 evaluated repositories, with the largest gains on MIL (+24.1 pp), Hairy Ball (+7.1 pp), Coxeter (+6.7 pp), and PFR (+2.7 pp, where ReProver proves zero theorems). Against ReProver+, LeanAgent wins on MIL (+17.2 pp), MiniF2F (+3.5 pp), Formal Book (tie at 10.3%), SciLean (+0.7 pp), and Coxeter (tie at 6.7%), while ReProver+ matches or slightly edges LeanAgent on Carleson and Lean4 PDL. The key differentiator is that LeanAgent's progressive curriculum learning enables it to prove a superset of sorry theorems compared to ReProver in most cases, and its lifelong learning metrics (WF5 = 0.18, EBWT = 1.21) are substantially better than all seven ablation setups, confirming that the curriculum + progressive training combination is critical for continuous generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LeanDojo/ReProver retrieval-augmented theorem proving pipeline (Yang et al., 2023), using its ByT5-based retriever as the starting point and its best-first tree search for proof generation. It departs from prior theorem proving work, which trains or fine-tunes on a fixed dataset (e.g., mathlib4), by reframing the problem as lifelong learning across an expanding sequence of Lean repositories. It also connects to the broader continual learning literature (EWC, A-GEM, knowledge distillation) but explicitly shows that standard regularization-based methods like EWC are ill-suited to theorem proving because they cannot adapt parameter importance as theorem complexity increases, motivating the simpler progressive training approach instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first lifelong learning framework for formal theorem proving, addressing the practical reality that mathematicians formalize across multiple domains simultaneously or cyclically. The finding that a simple one-epoch progressive training strategy outperforms EWC and that backward transfer (learning new tasks improves old ones) is achievable in theorem proving provides a clear design principle for future systems. Key limitations include data scarcity (LeanAgent cannot prove theorems in domains with insufficient training data, e.g., ODEs in SciLean), reliance on a single retriever architecture, and the fact that absolute proving accuracy remains low on many repositories. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-retrieval-based LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-Prover, Llemma) and whether reinforcement learning for synthetic data generation can close the data gap.
