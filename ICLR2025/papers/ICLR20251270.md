## Problem

Existing segmentation models face a fundamental trade-off: large transformer-based models (e.g., Mask2Former, SAM) achieve strong multi-task performance but are too heavy for real-time deployment, while real-time methods (e.g., YOSO, MobileSAM) are restricted to a single task or domain. No prior work unifies panoptic segmentation, video instance segmentation, and SAM-like interactive segmentation in one model that runs in real time, leaving a gap for practical on-device applications that require segmentation, tracking, and prompt-driven interaction simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model accepts three input modalities through a single architecture: RGB images (for panoptic segmentation), video clips of T frames (for video instance segmentation), and visual prompts consisting of points or bounding boxes (for interactive segmentation). Outputs are task-specific: image inputs yield per-instance binary masks with class labels (panoptic), video inputs yield tube masks m_i ∈ {0,1}^{T×H×W} with class labels and instance IDs, and prompt inputs yield class-agnostic binary masks at H/4 × W/4 resolution. All three tasks share the same set of model parameters, with prompts encoded into queries that coexist with learnable object queries in the decoder.

## Method

RMP-SAM replaces the expensive per-pixel cross-attention decoder of Mask2Former with a pooling-based dynamic convolution decoder: object/prompt queries are refined by mask-pooling features from the previous stage, then updated via a gating mechanism (DynamicConv) that generates per-query weights through an MLP, followed by multi-head self-attention and FFN. A key architectural insight is the use of two asymmetric adapters after the shared decoder: A_obj applies dynamic convolution to preserve spatio-temporal scene context needed for panoptic and video tasks, while A_prompt applies pixel-wise cross-attention to recover local detail lost through the pooling operation, which is critical for prompt-driven interactive segmentation. The model is jointly co-trained on COCO (panoptic), YouTube-VIS 2019 (video instance), and COCO-SAM (interactive) using Hungarian matching and CLIP text embeddings for unified label taxonomy, with a single loss combining mask classification CE, mask CE, and Dice terms. This shared-decoder-plus-asymmetric-adapter design achieves the best accuracy-speed trade-off across all three tasks, outperforming both heavier multi-task models (Mask2Former) and lighter single-task real-time models (YOSO) while running at 35–40 FPS on an A100.

## Results

On the proposed Real-Time Multi-Purpose Segmentation benchmark (Table 3), RMP-SAM with ResNet-50 achieves PQ 46.9 on COCO-Panoptic, mIoU 57.9 on COCO-SAM, and mAP 46.2 on YouTube-VIS 2019, at 35.1 FPS with 123G FLOPs and 47.2M parameters. With the lighter ResNet-18 backbone, it reaches PQ 39.9, mIoU 52.7, and mAP 38.7 at 40.3 FPS (60.5G FLOPs, 22.8M params). In the SAM-like setting (Table 4), RMP-SAM achieves mAP 33.2 on COCO instance segmentation with only 35G FLOPs and 47M parameters, surpassing all efficient SAM variants (TinySAM 32.5, MobileSAM 32.1, EdgeSAM 31.9, EfficientSAM 32.4) while using far fewer FLOPs than SAM-H (3000G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mask2Former (R50), RMP-SAM wins decisively on COCO-Panoptic PQ (46.9 vs. 42.9) and YouTube-VIS mAP (46.2 vs. 42.1) while running ~9 FPS faster (35.1 vs. 26.6); Mask2Former edges out RMP-SAM by only 0.1 mIoU on COCO-SAM (58.0 vs. 57.9). Against the real-time baseline YOSO (R50), RMP-SAM leads by 9.6 PQ, 8.7 mIoU, and 5.5 mAP across the three tasks, confirming a substantial accuracy gap at comparable speed. In the SAM-like comparison (Table 4), RMP-SAM (mAP 33.2, 35G FLOPs) outperforms all lightweight SAM variants—TinySAM (32.5), MobileSAM (32.1), EdgeSAM (31.9), EfficientSAM (32.4)—and approaches FastSAM (34.3) while using 10× fewer FLOPs; it trails the full SAM-H (35.6) by 2.4 mAP but with 86× fewer FLOPs. On VIP-Seg video panoptic segmentation, RMP-SAM (R18) achieves VPQ 32.5 and STQ 33.7 at 30 FPS, surpassing Tube-Link (STDCv2: VPQ 31.4, STQ 32.8, 12 FPS) and Video K-Net (R50: VPQ 26.1, STQ 33.1, 10 FPS). On ADE-20k, RMP-SAM (PQ 38.3) slightly trails Mask2Former (39.7) but beats YOSO (38.0) and PanSegFormer (36.4) at comparable FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

RMP-SAM sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the SAM line of work on prompt-driven interactive segmentation, from which it adopts the prompt encoder and visual-prompt-as-query formulation; (2) the K-Net / Video K-Net line on pooling-based dynamic convolution decoders, which it extends to a unified multi-task setting; and (3) the Mask2Former / OneFormer line on query-based universal segmentation, from which it inherits Hungarian matching, the loss formulation, and the inference pipeline. The key departure is replacing Mask2Former's per-pixel cross-attention and cascaded transformer layers with a lightweight shared dynamic-convolution decoder plus asymmetric adapters, explicitly targeting real-time inference on edge hardware rather than peak accuracy on heavy backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first benchmark for real-time multi-purpose segmentation (panoptic + video instance + interactive in one model), filling a gap where prior real-time methods were task-specific and prior multi-task methods were not real-time. The practical impact is significant for on-device applications (editing tools, AR, robotics) that need a single lightweight model for segmentation, tracking, and user-interactive cutting. Open questions remain: performance still lags specialized models on individual tasks (e.g., 47.2 vs. 52.8 mAP on YouTube-VIS), the model struggles with heavily overlapping objects, and scaling to edge deployment (e.g., iPhone) or knowledge distillation from foundation models is left as future work.
