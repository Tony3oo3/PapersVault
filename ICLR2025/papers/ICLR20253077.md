## Problem

Residual quantization (RQ) ignores dependencies between successive quantization steps, leading to suboptimal rate-distortion performance. While QINCo addressed this by using a neural network to adapt codebooks based on prior reconstructions, its encoding step requires K full network evaluations per quantization step, making it prohibitively expensive for beam search and large-scale deployment. QINCo2 targets this encoding bottleneck to enable more accurate search and faster training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253077/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes high-dimensional data vectors (96–768 dimensions) and produces M quantization indices (each 1 byte with K=256 codewords per step, yielding 8-, 16-, or 32-byte encodings). For compression, the output is the reconstructed vector; for billion-scale search, a query vector is matched against a database via an IVF index, where QINCo2 codes are first filtered by a fast pairwise additive decoder to produce a shortlist that is then re-ranked by full neural decoding. Encoding uses A pre-selected candidates and B beam-width hypotheses (e.g., A=32, B=64 at evaluation), while decoding is a single sequential pass through M neural network evaluations.

## Method

QINCo2 is a neural residual quantizer where each step's codebook is computed by a residual MLP f_θ^m that takes the current codeword and the partial reconstruction from previous steps, outputting an updated codeword. The key encoding innovation is a two-stage process: a lightweight pre-selection function g (often just a learned codebook lookup with L_s=0) narrows K candidates to A, after which beam search over B hypotheses evaluates f_θ^m only on the A×B subset, reducing network calls from K per step to A×B total. The architecture introduces a configurable embedding dimension d_e (decoupled from vector dimension d) with linear projections at the boundaries and an input-to-output residual connection. For billion-scale search, a pairwise additive decoder combines pairs of QINCo2 codes into K²-sized codebooks trained sequentially via least-squares, capturing inter-code dependencies that standard AQ decoders miss, and is used to build accurate shortlists before expensive full QINCo2 decoding.

## Results

On 16-byte compression of BigANN, QINCo2 reduces reconstruction MSE by 34% over QINCo (from 0.32×10⁻⁴ to 0.19×10⁻⁴) and improves R@1 from 71.9% to 79.3%. On 8-byte compression of Deep1M, QINCo2 improves nearest-neighbor recall R@1 by 24% over QINCo (from 36.3% to 45.1%) and reduces MSE from 0.12 to 0.09. Across all four datasets (BigANN, Deep1M, Contriever, FB-ssnpp) and both 8-byte and 16-byte settings, QINCo2 achieves the best MSE and R@1 among all compared methods. In billion-scale search on BigANN1B, QINCo2 pushes maximum recall from under 40% (RQ baseline) to over 70% with 16-byte codes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QINCo2 dominates all six baselines (OPQ, RQ, LSQ, UNQ, QINCo) on every dataset and bitrate for both MSE and R@1. The most direct comparison is against QINCo: QINCo2 reduces 16-byte BigANN MSE by 41% (0.32→0.19) and 8-byte Deep1M MSE by 25% (0.12→0.09), while improving R@1 by 7.1 points (BigANN 8-byte) and 7.3 points (Deep1M 8-byte). Against UNQ, the prior best neural quantizer, QINCo2 more than halves the 16-byte Deep1M MSE (0.07→0.03) and gains 17.2 R@1 points at 8 bytes (26.7%→45.1%). Against classical methods (OPQ, RQ, LSQ), QINCo2's advantage is even larger, with MSE reductions of 50–70% and R@1 gains of 10–30 points. In billion-scale search (BigANN1B, Deep1B), QINCo2-S strictly dominates QINCo across all operating points, and in the high-compute regime adds over 20 recall points compared to PQ and RQ baselines, reaching R@1 above 70% where RQ saturates below 40%.

## Relation to Prior Work

QINCo2 extends QINCo (Huijben et al., 2024), which introduced implicit neural codebooks that condition each residual quantization step's codebook on the partial reconstruction from prior steps. The broader lineage traces through multi-codebook quantization (PQ, RQ, AQ, LSQ) and neural vector quantization (VQ-VAE, UNQ, DeepQ), but QINCo2's key departures are methodological rather than conceptual: it adds codeword pre-selection with beam search to make the expensive neural encoding tractable, introduces a pairwise additive decoder that exploits inter-code dependencies for fast approximate search, and refines the network architecture and training recipe. The pairwise decoder is the most novel component, departing from standard AQ/RQ decoders that treat codebook entries as independent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 34% MSE reduction (16-byte BigANN) and 24% recall improvement (8-byte Deep1M) over QINCo are practically significant for production vector search systems, and the flexible encoding/decoding compute trade-off gives practitioners a new dial to tune for latency-sensitive deployments. The pairwise additive decoder is a general technique the authors explicitly suggest could benefit other quantizers beyond QINCo2, opening a direction for future work. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even higher-dimensional embeddings or non-residual code structures, and whether the beam-search encoding cost becomes prohibitive at extreme scale despite the pre-selection acceleration.
