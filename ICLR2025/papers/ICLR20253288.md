## Problem

Prior results on horizon-free and instance-dependent (second-order) regret/sample complexity bounds in RL were restricted to tabular MDPs, linear function approximation, or required non-standard algorithmic designs such as distributional RL with explicit polynomial horizon dependence. This paper asks whether the most standard and simplest model-based RL scheme—fitting a transition model via Maximum Likelihood Estimation followed by optimistic or pessimistic planning—can already achieve nearly horizon-free and second-order bounds under general non-linear function approximation, without any algorithmic modifications.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a finite-horizon time-homogeneous MDP with a model class P (finite or infinite, characterized by cardinality or bracketing number) and a policy class Π. In the online setting, the agent interacts for K episodes starting from a fixed initial state; in the offline setting, it receives K trajectories that may be generated by an adaptive adversary (not necessarily a fixed behavior policy). The trajectory-wise cumulative reward is normalized to [0,1], which is more general than per-step bounded rewards and accommodates sparse-reward settings. The output is a learned policy π̂ together with high-probability regret (online) or performance-gap (offline) bounds that depend on the ℓ₁ Eluder dimension or single-policy concentrability coefficient rather than polynomially on the horizon H.

## Method

The algorithm is the standard MLE-based MBRL: at each step, build a version space of models whose log-likelihood is within a threshold β of the MLE, then select a policy via optimistic (online) or pessimistic (offline) planning within that version space. The key analytical contribution is a refined simulation-lemma decomposition that avoids the linear-in-H summation: instead of bounding each per-step model error by an L1 distance (which accumulates over H steps), the authors apply a mean-to-variance lemma based on triangular discrimination (equivalent to squared Hellinger distance) to express the difference in expected next-state values as a product of the value-function variance and the Hellinger distance between true and learned transitions. For the online setting, a novel 'Eluder Pigeon Lemma' (Lemma 6) controls the training-to-testing distribution transfer under the ℓ₁ Eluder dimension, yielding a bound that only logarithmically depends on H. A recursive moment argument then converts the variance under the learned model to the variance under the ground-truth transition without incurring additional H dependence, producing the final second-order bound scaling with √(Σₖ VaR_{πᵏ}) rather than √K.

## Results

For online RL with finite model class P, the standard MLE-based optimistic MBRL algorithm (O-MBRL) achieves a regret bound of O(√(Σ_k VaR_π^k · d_RL · log(KH|P|/δ) · log(KH)) + d_RL · log(KH|P|/δ) · log(KH)), which is nearly horizon-free (only log H dependence) and second-order (scales with √(Σ VaR_π^k) rather than √K). For offline RL, the pessimistic CPPO-LR algorithm achieves a performance gap of O(√(C^π* · VaR_π* · log(|P|/δ)/K) + C^π* · log(|P|/δ)/K), which is completely horizon-free (no H or log H dependence) for finite P. Under deterministic transitions, the online regret improves to O(d_RL · log(KH|P|/δ) · log(KH)) (only poly-log in K) and the offline gap improves to O(C^π* · log(|P|/δ)/K), a 1/K rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines, but it provides direct theoretical comparisons to prior work. Compared to Uehara & Sun (2021), whose CPPO-LR analysis yields a poly(H)-dependent performance gap, this paper's analysis of the same algorithm achieves a completely horizon-free bound (no H or log H dependence) for finite model classes, and additionally scales with VaR_π* for instance-dependent guarantees. Compared to Wang et al. (2023, 2024a), who achieve first-order and second-order bounds via model-free distributional RL with explicit polynomial H dependence, this paper shows that the standard model-based MLE approach suffices to achieve both horizon-free and second-order bounds. Compared to Ren et al. (2021), whose tabular offline RL result gives a non-instance-dependent O(1/√K) gap even in deterministic environments, this paper's analysis yields a tighter O(1/K) gap under deterministic transitions. Compared to Huang et al. (2024), who require variance-weighted regression for horizon-free instance-dependent online RL, this paper shows the simpler standard MLE-based approach already achieves similar guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard MLE-based model-based RL framework (fitting transition models then planning) and the CPPO-LR pessimistic algorithm of Uehara & Sun (2021), but its contribution is entirely in the analysis rather than algorithm design. It repurposes the triangular discrimination / mean-to-variance lemma from Wang et al. (2023, 2024a) — previously used in distributional RL — and the ℓ₁ Eluder dimension from Liu et al. (2022) to show that these simple algorithms already achieve nearly horizon-free and second-order bounds. The key departure from prior horizon-free RL work (which required variance-weighted regression, distributional RL, or specialized algorithm designs) is the demonstration that no such algorithmic complexity is needed; the analysis itself is the novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central message — that the most standard and simplest MBRL algorithm suffices for horizon-free and second-order bounds — simplifies the theoretical landscape and suggests that long-horizon planning is not the statistical bottleneck in RL, even under non-linear function approximation. Limitations include: the algorithms are not computationally tractable (optimism/pessimism planning is intractable for general function classes), the offline result still carries a log H dependence in the infinite model class case, and the framework cannot capture problems requiring model-free approaches like linear MDPs. Open questions include eliminating the remaining log H dependence via peeling/chaining techniques and extending the analysis to distributional Eluder dimensions for model-free methods.
