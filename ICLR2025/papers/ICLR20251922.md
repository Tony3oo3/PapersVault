## Problem

Oversquashing in graph neural networks has been extensively analyzed through curvature, spectral, and expansion-based perspectives, but the analogous phenomenon in topological message-passing on simplicial and cellular complexes remains largely unexplored. No unified theoretical framework exists to extend graph-theoretic oversquashing results to higher-order structures, leaving practitioners without principled tools for diagnosis or mitigation in topological deep learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251922/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes simplicial complexes (or graphs lifted into clique or ring complexes) with feature vectors assigned to each simplex, encoding them as relational structures where entities are simplices and relations capture boundary, co-boundary, lower, and upper adjacencies of varying arity. Beyond producing updated simplex features and downstream predictions, the framework generates influence graphs—directed weighted graphs derived from aggregated shift operators—that serve as the analytical substrate for all theoretical results. The rewiring procedure outputs an augmented relational structure with an additional binary relation encoding new edges while preserving all original connections.

## Method

The core contribution is an axiomatic framework that reinterprets simplicial message passing as relational message passing, where the four adjacency types become relations of varying arity in a relational structure, generalizing the R-GCN scheme to non-binary relations. From the shift operators associated with each relation, the paper constructs an aggregated influence matrix and a corresponding influence graph, enabling direct transfer of graph-theoretic oversquashing analyses to higher-order settings. Using this bridge, they derive sensitivity bounds (Lemma 3.2), a curvature-based bound via an extended Forman curvature adapted to weighted directed graphs (Proposition 3.4), exponential depth decay results (Theorem 3.5), and hidden-dimension effects. For practical mitigation, they propose a relational rewiring heuristic that collapses the relational structure into a graph via a collapsed adjacency matrix, applies standard graph rewiring algorithms (FoSR, SDRF, AFRC), and injects the resulting edges as a new relation without removing original connections.

## Results

On the TUDataset graph classification benchmarks (ENZYMES, IMDB-B, MUTAG, NCI1, PROTEINS), relational rewiring (using SDRF, FoSR, or AFRC) generally improves test accuracy across all model families. For example, on MUTAG with no lift, CIN achieves 93.5% without rewiring and 95.0% with rewiring, while GIN improves from 83.0% to 88.0%. On NCI1 with clique lifting, CIN++ reaches 76.8% with rewiring versus 61.5% without. On the RINGTRANSFER synthetic benchmark, increasing hidden dimensions improves performance up to a point before overfitting degrades it, larger ring sizes cause performance deterioration due to long-range dependencies, and rewiring iterations consistently recover accuracy—confirming the theoretical predictions from Sections 3.3–3.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three model families—graph (SGC, GCN, GIN), relational graph (RGCN, RGIN), and topological (SIN, CIN, CIN++)—under no-rewiring and rewiring conditions. The central finding is not that one model family dominates, but that the proposed relational rewiring heuristic improves performance across all families in a consistent manner. For instance, on MUTAG (no lift), CIN++ gains +6.0% from rewiring (85.0→91.0), comparable to GIN's +5.0% (83.0→88.0) and GCN's +12.0% (71.0→83.0). On NCI1 with clique lifting, CIN++ improves from 61.5% to 76.8% (+15.3%), while RGIN improves from 79.2% to 79.5% (marginal). Topological models (SIN, CIN, CIN++) generally match or slightly exceed their graph counterparts (GIN, GCN) on the no-lift setting, and the relative benefit of rewiring is similar across families, supporting the paper's claim that graph-based oversquashing analysis transfers to topological message passing. On IMDB-B, rewiring with FoSR helps RGCN substantially (47.6→68.0) but hurts RGIN (69.6→48.9), showing that the effect is model- and dataset-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—topological deep learning (simplicial/CW message passing à la Bodnar et al. 2021, Hajij et al. 2023) and the graph-theoretic analysis of oversquashing (Topping et al. 2022, Di Giovanni et al. 2023, Karhadkar et al. 2023)—by recasting simplicial complexes as relational structures in the sense of Schlichtkrull et al. (2018). The key conceptual move is defining an 'influence graph' from the aggregated shift operators, which allows graph sensitivity bounds, curvature-based rewiring (SDRF, FoSR, AFRC), and depth/width analyses to be extended to higher-order message passing in a principled way. The paper explicitly positions itself as addressing research directions 2 and 9 of the TDL community's open-problems paper (Papamarkou et al. 2024), where no prior theoretical treatment of oversquashing in topological networks existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first rigorous theoretical toolkit for diagnosing and mitigating oversquashing in topological message passing, a gap the TDL community has flagged but not yet filled. The relational-structure lens is broadly applicable (CW complexes, sheaves, higher-order graphs), making it a potential foundation for future TDL theory. However, the practical rewiring heuristic is preliminary: the underlying graph rewiring algorithms were not designed for weighted directed influence graphs, hyperparameters are fixed rather than tuned, and the paper acknowledges that direct empirical comparisons between graphs and their clique complexes remain theoretically imprecise. Open questions include extending the analysis to oversmoothing in TDL, developing rewiring algorithms tailored to weighted directed graphs, and studying global geometric properties of relational structures.
