## Problem

Preference optimization methods (DPO, IPO, KTO) have been widely adopted to align generative models with human preferences, but their application to non-generative contrastive learning models like CLIP remains unexplored. CLIP is vulnerable to typographic attacks and encodes inductive biases such as gender stereotypes, yet no systematic framework exists to align its retrieval behavior with desired preferences while preserving pretrained knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252541/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with a fixed set of K text captions (e.g., 'an image of a <class>'), forming preference triplets (x, y_w, y_l) where y_w is the correct caption and y_l is the adversarial or biased caption, alongside a separate regularization set of clean image-caption pairs. The output is a set of aligned image and text embeddings in a shared d-dimensional space, from which class labels are obtained via softmax over cosine similarities. A learnable linear head W is applied to both encoders, and its singular values can be post-hoc rescaled by a parameter t to continuously trade off between competing behaviors (e.g., OCR vs. object detection, or gender-specific vs. gender-neutral predictions).

## Method

The paper formulates contrastive learning as a one-step MDP where the policy π_θ(a|s) is the softmax over image-text similarity scores, then directly adapts DPO, IPO, and KTO losses to this policy. A key theoretical result (Lemma 3.1) shows that with a frozen text encoder, the policy ratio h_πθ(y_w, y_l, x) reduces to (I_θ(x) − I_ref(x))^T (T(y_w) − T(y_l))/τ, revealing that the loss pushes the image embedding toward the preferred text-embedding difference while the sigmoid or squared penalty term automatically dampens updates as the model diverges from the reference. A KL-divergence regularization term on a clean dataset prevents catastrophic forgetting, and the total loss is L_pref + λ_reg · L_reg. To enable fine-grained post-training control, a learnable linear head W is placed on both encoders; its SVD (W = UΣV^T) allows rescaling singular values via matrix powers (Σ → Σ^t), providing a continuous dial to strengthen or weaken specific embedding directions without retraining.

## Results

On the typographic attack robustness task, the best-performing variant (KTO) achieves an average typographic accuracy of 51.74% across 9 datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline PAINT (46.31%) by +5.43 points and pretrained CLIP (31.31%) by +20.43 points. The largest single-dataset gain is on EuroSAT typographic, where KTO reaches 36.94% versus PAINT's 17.31% (+19.63). On clean (original) images, KTO retains 57.09% average accuracy, within 1.6 points of CLIP's 58.66%, confirming minimal loss of pretrained knowledge. On real-world typographic datasets (Table 2), IPO achieves the best average (67.54%) over CLIP (46.82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three PO variants (DPO, IPO, KTO) against four typographic-attack defenses: pretrained CLIP, Materzynska+ (orthogonal projection), PAINT (weight interpolation), and Defense-Prefix (learned text token). KTO, the best proposed method, outperforms all baselines on average typographic accuracy (51.74% vs. PAINT's 46.31%, Defense-Prefix's 40.58%, Materzynska+'s 35.77%, and CLIP's 31.31%). The margin is largest on EuroSAT typographic (+19.63 over PAINT) and SUN397 typographic (+5.39 over PAINT). On clean images, KTO (57.09%) is within 1.6 points of CLIP (58.66%) and slightly below Defense-Prefix (57.87%), indicating that the robustness gains come at negligible cost to standard classification. IPO achieves the best average on real-world typographic benchmarks (67.54% vs. Defense-Prefix's 64.52% and Materzynska+'s 63.61%), while KTO leads on the synthetic typographic evaluation. All three PO variants consistently surpass the strongest baseline (PAINT) on typographic accuracy while maintaining comparable clean-image performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the preference optimization paradigm (DPO, IPO, KTO) from generative LLM alignment to non-generative contrastive vision-language models, framing contrastive retrieval as a one-step MDP where the policy is a softmax over similarity scores. The key theoretical bridge is Lemma 3.1, which shows that under a frozen text encoder, the DPO/IPO policy ratio reduces to a simple dot product of image-embedding differences and text-embedding differences, making the gradient interpretation transparent. For typographic robustness, it builds on and outperforms prior defenses (Materzynska's orthogonal projections, PAINT's weight interpolation, Defense-Prefix's learned tokens); for bias, it extends the gender-flipping setup of Zhang et al. (2022) with a preference-optimization objective and an SVD-based linear head that enables post-hoc, interpretable control over concept strength without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that preference optimization—a tool previously confined to generative models—can be applied to contrastive encoders with clean theoretical grounding, opening a path toward aligning any non-generative VLM with task-specific preferences (robustness, fairness, safety) while provably preserving pretrained knowledge via KL regularization. The SVD-based singular-value scaling (Eq. 16) is a practically useful mechanism for continuous, interpretable control over concept emphasis without additional training. Limitations include evaluation on a single architecture (CLIP ViT-B/16), synthetic rather than human-curated preference data, and only two application domains; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger or multi-modal VLMs, to compositional or multi-attribute preferences, and to settings where the text encoder is also adapted.
