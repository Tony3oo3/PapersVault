## Problem

The Overcooked benchmark, widely used to evaluate zero-shot coordination (ZSC), does not actually present a meaningful coordination challenge: the SP-XP gap is largely attributable to poor state coverage rather than incompatible conventions, and can be nearly closed by a simple state-augmentation procedure during training. Without asymmetric information or stochasticity, agents can solve the game through generalisation alone, making the benchmark unsuitable for benchmarking ZSC algorithms that must handle grounded communication, test-time protocol formation, or adaptive coordination.

## Input / Output

The environment is configured via an ASCII layout string, a set of possible recipes (each a multiset of three ingredients), and a view radius parameter that controls partial observability. Agents receive sparse grid observations limited to their view radius, with ingredients encoded using a compact bit scheme (2 bits per ingredient for 0–3 units, plus cooked/plated flags), and produce discrete grid-world actions (move, interact, press button). Rewards are +20 for correct deliveries and optionally −20 for incorrect ones, with shaped intermediate rewards only when actions align with the current recipe.

## Method

The paper introduces OvercookedV2, which adds three key mechanisms to the original Overcooked: a configurable view radius for meaningful partial observability, asymmetric information via a recipe indicator visible to only one agent, and stochasticity through randomised starting positions and recipe sampling. Three classes of handcrafted layouts create distinct coordination challenges: Grounded Coordination (temporally extended Cat-Dog requiring a costly button to reveal the recipe), Test-Time Protocol Formation (no grounded channel, agents must form arbitrary protocols from delivery feedback at test time), and Demo Cook (implicit communication through ingredient choices in the pot). For the empirical study, the authors propose a state-augmentation algorithm that iteratively collects states from self-play trajectories and uses them as episode starting states, demonstrating that this closes the SP-XP gap in original Overcooked. The evaluation architecture uses three 1×1 convolutions to compress sparse observations, three 3×3 convolutions, and a GRU (hidden size 128) to handle partial observability, trained with PPO and compared against Other-Play and Fictitious Co-Play across all six new layouts.

## Results

In the original Overcooked benchmark, state-augmented self-play nearly closes the SP-XP gap across all layouts: the Counter Circuit gap drops from 104 to 44, Forced Coordination from 61 to 8, and Coordination Ring from 118 to 71 (Table 1). In the new OvercookedV2 environment, no method successfully closes the SP-XP gap: across all six handcrafted coordination layouts, self-play agents achieve SP scores of 145–195 but XP scores range from −81 to 62 (Table 2). Other-Play improves XP in most layouts (e.g., Grounded Coord. Simple XP rises from −55 to +4) but at the cost of lower SP scores (122 vs. 154). Fictitious Co-Play achieves positive XP scores in all layouts but with substantially lower SP scores (e.g., 35 vs. 145 in Test Time Simple), confirming that OvercookedV2 presents a genuinely hard ZSC challenge unsolved by current methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four training strategies (Self-Play, State-Augmented Self-Play, Other-Play, and Fictitious Co-Play) across six OvercookedV2 layouts. Other-Play (Hu et al., 2021b) consistently improves cross-play scores over self-play in most layouts — e.g., in Grounded Coord. Simple, XP rises from −55±124 to +4±73, and in Test Time Simple from −81±99 to −3±51 — but at the cost of 15–30% lower SP scores (122 vs. 154, 121 vs. 145). Fictitious Co-Play (Strouse et al., 2022) achieves the smallest SP-XP gaps in several layouts (e.g., 22±45 in Grounded Coord. Simple, 25±47 in Test Time Simple) and positive XP scores in all six layouts, but its SP scores are dramatically lower than self-play (64 vs. 154, 35 vs. 145), suggesting the best-response training objective sacrifices self-play performance. Neither baseline closes the SP-XP gap to a meaningful degree in the Test-Time Protocol layouts, where gaps remain above 100 points for all methods. The paper's key finding is that state augmentation, which nearly eliminates the SP-XP gap in the original Overcooked, provides only marginal improvement in OvercookedV2, confirming that the new environment introduces genuine coordination challenges beyond state coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Overcooked benchmark (Carroll et al., 2020) and the zero-shot coordination (ZSC) framework formalized by Hu et al. (2021b), but makes a critical conceptual departure: it demonstrates that Overcooked's apparent ZSC challenges are largely artifacts of insufficient state coverage rather than genuine coordination difficulties. The paper extends the Cat-Dog toy problem (Hu et al., 2021a) into a temporally grounded layout and introduces OvercookedV2, a new environment with partial observability, asymmetric information, and stochasticity, implemented in JaxMARL. It positions itself against existing ZSC methods—Other-Play and Fictitious Co-Play—showing that none of them solve the new coordination scenarios, particularly those requiring test-time protocol formation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state augmentation nearly eliminates the SP-XP gap in original Overcooked undermines the validity of a widely-used benchmark for ZSC research, raising concerns about prior results evaluated solely in this environment. OvercookedV2 introduces three classes of coordination challenges—grounded coordination, test-time protocol formation, and demo cook—that none of the current ZSC methods solve, with test-time protocol formation being a genuinely open problem requiring fundamentally new adaptive coordination algorithms. The paper highlights the need for agents with theory-of-mind-like capabilities that can form protocols online from few interactions, a capability that remains far beyond current RL approaches. A key limitation is that the state-coverage analysis is limited to PPO on specific layouts, and the OvercookedV2 results, while showing universal failure, use relatively small populations and may benefit from larger-scale training.
