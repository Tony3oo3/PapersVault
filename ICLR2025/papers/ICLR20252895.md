## Problem

Offline preference optimization methods (DPO, IPO, SimPO, χPO) are provably vulnerable to reward hacking caused by statistical fluctuations in sparsely covered regions of the preference data. The paper identifies two distinct failure modes—Type I (subpar choices appearing favorable) and Type II (decent choices appearing unfavorable, degrading the initial model)—and shows that standard KL-divergence regularization does not prevent either, even when divergence from the initial model remains small.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial reference policy (e.g., Llama-3-8B base or instruct) and an offline pairwise preference dataset of (prompt, chosen response, rejected response, binary label) tuples, where the data need not be sampled from the initial model. During training, preference labels are dynamically updated per gradient step rather than held fixed, and the objective uses length-normalized log-probabilities (dividing by response token count) rather than raw log-likelihoods. The output is a single optimized policy that can be deployed as an aligned LLM.

## Method

POWER-DL combines two mechanisms. First, POWER replaces the KL-divergence regularizer in DPO with Guiaçu's weighted entropy inside a minimax (adversarial) reward framework, which reduces via Proposition 3 to a single-step objective: a Bradley-Terry loss on length-normalized log-probability gaps plus a length-normalized SFT term over a baseline policy. This yields finite-sample guarantees competing with the best covered policy under general function approximation, mitigating Type I hacking. Second, Dynamic Labels update preference labels toward 'stationary labels' that zero out the gradient for poorly covered pairs, so the model stays close to initialization in low-coverage regions while still learning from high-coverage data, mitigating Type II hacking. The hyperparameters η (robustness strength) and γ (label-update conservatism) allow trading off between the two hacking types.

## Results

POWER-DL achieves the best alignment scores across all four experimental settings (Helpsteer2/Zephyr × Base/Instruct). On the Helpsteer2 Base setting (highest distribution shift), POWER-DL reaches 31.52% length-controlled win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 and 31.44% win rate on Arena-Hard, improving over DPO by 13.0 and 16.5 points respectively. On the Zephyr Instruct setting, POWER-DL attains 48.97% AlpacaEval win rate and 43.75% Arena-Hard length-controlled win rate. On downstream tasks, POWER-DL maintains or improves GSM8K (up to +7.0 points), TruthfulQA (up to +12.8 points over initial model), and IFEval (up to +11.7 points) where other methods like SimPO and DPO sometimes degrade the initial model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POWER-DL outperforms all 13 baselines across all four settings. The largest margins are against DPO (up to +13.0 on AlpacaEval LC and +16.5 on Arena-Hard WR in the Helpsteer2 Base setting) and against reference-free methods like CPO and RRHF, which degrade severely under high distribution shift (e.g., CPO drops to 7.01% AlpacaEval LC on Zephyr Base vs. POWER-DL's 27.00%). Among the closest competitors, SimPO achieves 25.35% AlpacaEval LC on Helpsteer2 Base (vs. POWER-DL's 31.52%) and SPPO reaches 26.23% (vs. 31.52%), but both are outperformed by 5–6 points. Robust variants cDPO and ROPO, designed to handle noisy preferences, are also consistently beaten (e.g., ROPO: 21.24% vs. 31.52% on Helpsteer2 Base AlpacaEval). Notably, IPO and χPO, which have theoretical guarantees, suffer severe degradation on Zephyr Base (10.55% and 13.16% AlpacaEval LC respectively), while POWER-DL maintains 27.00%, confirming that divergence-based pessimism is insufficient against Type I reward hacking. On downstream tasks, POWER-DL uniquely maintains or improves GSM8K, TruthfulQA, and IFEval where SimPO and DPO sometimes degrade the initial model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DPO single-step preference optimization framework (Rafailov et al., 2024b) and the adversarial reward minimax structure introduced by Liu et al. (2024), but replaces the standard KL divergence regularizer with Guiaçu's weighted entropy, which the authors show avoids the underoptimization pathology of KL-based methods. It departs from the dominant paradigm of divergence minimization as a countermeasure against reward hacking by proving that KL, χ², and other divergences are provably insufficient to prevent both types of reward hacking they identify. The dynamic labels mechanism is a novel contribution not present in prior soft-label or noise-robust PO methods (Mitchell, 2024; Furuta et al., 2024), which use fixed or externally-scored soft labels rather than labels that evolve to shrink gradients for poorly covered pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous two-type taxonomy of reward hacking in offline preference optimization and proves that mainstream methods (DPO, IPO, SimPO, χPO) fail under both types, challenging the widespread assumption that divergence minimization provides adequate robustness. POWER-DL's strong empirical gains (up to 13 points on AlpacaEval 2.0, 11.5 on Arena-Hard over DPO) across four experimental settings, combined with finite-sample guarantees under general function approximation, make it a practical and theoretically grounded alternative. Open questions include unifying the separate theoretical analyses of POWER and dynamic labels into a single guarantee, extending dynamic labels to general function approximation, and investigating the interplay between statistical errors and reward misspecification.
