## Problem

Intrinsic decomposition from images is inherently ambiguous: a dark pixel could reflect dark albedo or absent illumination. Optimization-based methods resolve this slowly (hours per scene) and often bake lighting into textures, while existing learning-based methods produce inconsistent results across multiple views and struggle under varying illumination. No prior feed-forward approach handles an arbitrary number of input views under unconstrained, differing lighting conditions while maintaining multi-view consistency.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an arbitrary number N of RGB images (from 1 to many) captured under any combination of lighting conditions, with no camera pose information required. Intrinsic components are parameterized via the simplified Disney BRDF model and split into three channel-aligned triplets (albedo 3ch, normal 3ch, metallic+roughness 2ch+1 unused) to fit the 4-channel VAE latent space of Stable Diffusion. Training uses 256×256 resolution; evaluation is conducted at 512×512. Specific text prompts ('albedo', 'normal', 'metallic&roughness') condition the diffusion process on which component to denoise.

## Method

IDArb fine-tunes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion UNet (v-prediction, zero terminal SNR schedule) to jointly denoise three intrinsic triplets in parallel. Two key attention modifications are introduced within the UNet: (1) cross-view attention concatenates spatial features from all N input views so that self-attention operates across viewpoints, enforcing multi-view consistency; and (2) cross-component attention repurposes the vanilla self-attention to exchange information between the three intrinsic triplets, reducing material ambiguity (especially for metallic and roughness). Training employs an illumination-augmented strategy where each batch samples N images from N_v viewpoints under N_i randomly drawn lighting conditions (HDR environment maps, point lights, ambient), simulating same-view-different-light and different-view-different-light scenarios. A view-adapted schedule alternates between multi-view (N=3) and single-view (N=1) inputs to preserve both cross-view reasoning and general material priors. The noise scheduler is shifted toward higher noise levels to better capture the low-frequency structure of metallic and roughness maps. The model is trained on the newly constructed ARB-Objaverse dataset (5.7M rendered images from 68k Objaverse objects, 12 views × 7 lighting conditions per object) combined with G-Objaverse and ABO.

## Results

On the synthetic ARB-Objaverse/G-Objaverse evaluation, IDArb (multi-view) achieves the best results across all four intrinsic components: albedo SSIM 0.937 / PSNR 33.62, normal cosine similarity 0.941, metallic MSE 0.016, and roughness MSE 0.033. Even in the single-view setting, IDArb leads with albedo PSNR 32.79, normal cosine similarity 0.928, metallic MSE 0.037, and roughness MSE 0.058. On the photometric stereo task (NeRFactor, 8 OLAT images), IDArb reaches albedo PSNR 25.15 and normal cosine similarity 0.85, outperforming the purpose-built SDM-UniPS (23.37 / 0.81). When used as a pseudo-label prior for NVDiffRecMC, IDArb improves albedo PSNR from 17.89 to 20.90 and relighting PSNR from 22.65 to 27.20 on NeRFactor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDArb outperforms all baselines on every metric where a direct comparison is available. Against IID, the closest diffusion-based single-view method, IDArb (single) improves albedo PSNR by 5.4 dB (32.79 vs. 27.35) and reduces metallic MSE by 5.2× (0.037 vs. 0.192) and roughness MSE by 2.3× (0.058 vs. 0.131). Against RGB↔X, IDArb gains 4.7 dB on albedo PSNR, 0.094 on normal cosine similarity (0.928 vs. 0.834), and reduces roughness MSE by 6× (0.058 vs. 0.347). Against IntrinsicAnything, IDArb leads on albedo PSNR by 4.6 dB (32.79 vs. 28.17) and SSIM by 0.034. Against GeoWizard, the strongest normal-estimation baseline, IDArb achieves 0.928 vs. 0.871 cosine similarity. In the photometric stereo setting (Table 2), IDArb surpasses the task-specific SDM-UniPS on both albedo and normal at every OLAT count (e.g., 25.15 vs. 23.37 albedo PSNR and 0.85 vs. 0.81 normal at 8 images). Finally, as a prior for optimization-based NVDiffRecMC (Table 3), IDArb pseudo-labels improve albedo PSNR by 3.0 dB and relighting PSNR by 4.6 dB on NeRFactor, demonstrating its value beyond standalone feed-forward inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based intrinsic decomposition (IID, RGB↔X, IntrinsicAnything) from single-view to multi-view settings by introducing cross-view and cross-component attention modules, building on Wonder3D's cross-domain attention for multi-view consistency. It departs from optimization-based inverse rendering (NeRFactor, NVDiffRecMC, TensoIR) by replacing per-scene iterative optimization with a feed-forward generative model, while also providing a new large-scale dataset (ARB-Objaverse, 5.7M images) that addresses the limited lighting diversity of prior synthetic datasets like G-Objaverse and ABO. The illumination-augmented and view-adapted training strategy is a novel contribution that enables the model to handle arbitrary numbers of views and unconstrained lighting without explicit camera pose inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IDArb provides a practical, unified feed-forward solution for intrinsic decomposition that bridges the gap between slow optimization-based methods and inconsistent single-view learning-based approaches, with substantial performance gains (e.g., ~5 dB PSNR over best baseline for albedo, 10× lower MSE for metallic). The ARB-Objaverse dataset is a significant community resource for multi-view, multi-lighting intrinsic decomposition training. Key limitations include O(N²) cross-view attention complexity limiting dense high-resolution inputs, reliance on synthetic data with simplified global material parameters that oversimplify complex real-world surfaces (e.g., corroded bronze), and degradation on outdoor scenes and text. Open questions include incorporating real data through unsupervised techniques and developing more efficient cross-view attention mechanisms.
