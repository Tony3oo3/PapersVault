## Problem

While diffusion models have established minimax optimal convergence rates for large-sample distribution estimation, the statistical convergence properties of flow matching (FM) remained largely uncharacterized. This paper addresses the open question of whether FM can achieve convergence rates comparable to diffusion models, and under what conditions on the path construction parameters this is possible.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples from a target distribution P_true whose density lies in a Besov space B_{p',q'}^s on [-1,1]^d, and a sample from the standard normal N_d(0, I_d) as the source. It produces an estimated distribution P̂_{T_0} by solving an ODE driven by a learned vector field, with early stopping at T_0 = n^{-R_0} to avoid the kernel density estimation bottleneck. The accuracy is measured under the p-Wasserstein distance W_p for 1 ≤ p ≤ 2, and the analysis is asymptotic in n → ∞.

## Method

The paper establishes that a general class of conditional flow matching methods with paths x_[τ] = σ_[τ]x_[0] + m_[τ]x_[1] achieves an almost minimax optimal convergence rate of Õ(n^{-(s+(2κ)^{-1}-δ)/(2s+d)}) under W_p, where κ governs the variance decay σ_t ~ t^κ. The proof combines the Alekseev-Gröbner lemma to bound W_2 by the L_2 risk of the vector field, B-spline approximation of the target density with N = n^{d/(2s+d)} bases, and time-partitioned neural networks (one per dyadic interval [t_{j-1}, t_j]) to exploit the increasing smoothness of the vector field as t grows. A critical finding is that κ = 1/2 (i.e., σ_t ~ √t, the standard diffusion-like choice) is necessary and sufficient to match the minimax lower bound n^{-(s+1)/(2s+d)} up to polylog and δ factors; any κ > 1/2 yields a strictly suboptimal rate.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 9) establishes that flow matching with time-partitioned neural networks achieves a convergence rate of $O\bigl(n^{-\frac{s+(2\kappa)^{-1}-\delta}{2s+d}}\bigr)$ under the $r$-Wasserstein distance for $1 \leq r \leq 2$, where $s$ is the Besov smoothness of the target density, $d$ is the dimension, and $\kappa$ governs the variance decay $\sigma_t \sim t^\kappa$. For the critical case $\kappa = 1/2$ (i.e., $\sigma_t \sim \sqrt{t}$), this rate becomes $O\bigl(n^{-\frac{s+1-\delta}{2s+d}}\bigr)$, which matches the minimax lower bound $n^{-\frac{s+1}{2s+d}}$ (Proposition 2) up to an arbitrarily small $\delta > 0$ and a poly$(\log n)$ factor. For $\kappa > 1/2$, the rate degrades to $n^{-\frac{s+(2\kappa)^{-1}-\delta}{2s+d}}$, which is strictly slower than the minimax optimal rate, showing that the $\sqrt{t}$ variance decay is necessary for optimality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The key theoretical comparison is with diffusion models: for $\kappa = 1/2$, the FM convergence rate under $W_1$ coincides exactly with the almost minimax optimal rate previously established for diffusion models by Oko et al. (2023), demonstrating that FM is asymptotically as powerful as diffusion models in terms of minimax convergence. The paper also contrasts its analysis with prior FM convergence results by Albergo and Vanden-Eijnden (2023) and Benton et al. (2023), which established convergence but did not derive explicit rates, and with Jiao et al. (2024), which considered latent-space FM but did not incorporate the Besov smoothness parameter $s$ into the rate. The minimax lower bound from Niles-Weed and Berthet (2022) serves as the benchmark, and the paper shows FM matches it up to $\delta$ and log factors only when $\kappa = 1/2$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimax convergence rate analysis of Oko et al. (2023) from diffusion models (SDE-based) to flow matching (ODE-based), using the Alekseev-Gröbner lemma in place of Girsanov's theorem to relate the W2 distance to the L2 risk of the vector field. It builds on the FM framework of Lipman et al. (2023) and the W2-to-L2 bridge of Benton et al. (2023b), but generalizes the analysis to a broader class of mean and variance parameters (σ_t, m_t) for the Gaussian conditional kernel, rather than the single σ_t ~ √t choice used in diffusion models. The key methodological departure is the use of time-partitioned neural networks with adaptive B-spline complexity, a technique borrowed from the diffusion analysis but adapted to handle the different singularity structure of ODE vector fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous proof that flow matching achieves an almost minimax optimal convergence rate under the p-Wasserstein distance (1 ≤ p ≤ 2), placing FM on equal theoretical footing with diffusion models for large-sample generalization. The finding that σ_t ~ √t (κ = 1/2) is specifically required for optimality provides theoretical justification for a widely-used practical choice and warns against alternative variance schedules. Key open questions include whether the time-partition of neural networks is truly necessary (without it, the rate degrades to a suboptimal n^{-s/(2s+d)}), and whether analogous bounds exist for ODE-based models under KL divergence or total variation, which would enable a more complete comparison with the SDE literature.
