## Problem

Lightweight NLI models for grounding verification in RAG systems underperform LLMs by ~20% ROC-AUC on realistic RAG inputs due to a domain gap between standard NLI training data and the longer, more complex, domain-specific inputs encountered in practice. Supervised domain adaptation is infeasible because labeled target-domain data is prohibitively expensive to obtain, and classical unsupervised domain adaptation methods (DAPT, SiFT, Deep CORAL) yield only marginal improvements. The paper addresses how to automatically generate and select high-quality synthetic training data to close this gap without human labels.

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of text sequences: an evidence string (containing the user prompt plus one or more retrieved documents, formatted per the RAG template) and a claim string (the LLM-generated long-form response), both up to 1024 tokens. It outputs a continuous entailment score in [0,1] indicating the probability that the claim is fully grounded in the evidence, which is thresholded into a binary entailed/non-entailed label. The framework operates per unique evidence document, generating and selecting synthetic claims conditioned on that evidence, so the adaptation is evidence-specific rather than global.

## Method

Auto-GDA is an iterative unsupervised domain adaptation framework with three stages: (1) initial synthetic claim generation via few-shot prompting of an LLM for each evidence in the unlabeled target set, with initial entailment certainty scores from a teacher NLI model; (2) label-preserving augmentation (partial LLM rephrasing, T5 paraphrasing, sentence deletion) where entailment certainties are updated using a logical invariance rule that factors T(e,c') into the parent certainty times T(c,c'), exploiting the fact that claim-claim entailment is easier for the teacher than evidence-claim entailment; (3) automatic sample selection via an enhanced distribution matching objective that decomposes into per-sample distance-to-target, label-correctness (modeled as expected KL divergence under a Beta hyperprior over the true label), and model-utility terms, enabling efficient greedy top-K selection. The objective is shown to correspond to a KL divergence between the synthetic and target distributions minus expected utility, and the entire pipeline is repeated for a fixed number of iterations before fine-tuning the lightweight NLI model on the selected synthetic data.

## Results

On four realistic RAG grounding-verification datasets (RAGTruth Summary/QA, LFQA-Verification QA, SummEdits Summary), DeBERTaV2 fine-tuned with Auto-GDA achieves an average ROC-AUC of 0.878, ranking second only to GPT-4o (0.883) while running at ~10% of its inference time. BART fine-tuned with Auto-GDA reaches 0.852 average ROC-AUC, matching GPT-4o-mini. The method closes 96% of the gap between non-fine-tuned models and the hypothetical upper bound of fine-tuning on human-labeled target data (0.885 avg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Auto-GDA consistently outperforms all classical unsupervised domain adaptation baselines: DAPT (Gururangan et al., 2020) achieves 0.775 avg ROC-AUC, SiFT (He et al., 2020) reaches 0.769, and Deep CORAL (Sun et al., 2016) scores 0.768, all roughly 10–11 points below DeBERTaV2+Auto-GDA (0.878). Against state-of-the-art lightweight NLI models, Auto-GDA surpasses MiniCheck-T5 (Tang et al., 2024) by ~15 points (0.878 vs. 0.732) and AlignScore (Zha et al., 2023) by ~4 points (0.878 vs. 0.837). Notably, DeBERTaV2+Auto-GDA outperforms its own teacher model (Vectara or AlignScore, depending on dataset) in three out of four datasets, demonstrating that the iterative selection objective enhances data quality beyond what the teacher alone provides. The only model that beats Auto-GDA is GPT-4o (0.883 vs. 0.878), but at roughly 10× the inference latency (20.47 s vs. 2.12 s per 50 samples). BART+Auto-GDA (0.852) matches GPT-4o-mini (0.852) while being ~5× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

Auto-GDA sits at the intersection of synthetic data generation for NLI (Hosseini et al. 2024, Tang et al. 2024, Saad-Falcon et al. 2024) and classical unsupervised domain adaptation (DAPT, SiFT, DeepCORAL), but departs from both by unifying generation, label-preserving augmentation, and weak teacher labeling into a single iterative framework governed by a distribution-matching objective. Unlike prior synthetic-data approaches that rely on handcrafted filtering or fixed augmentation pipelines, Auto-GDA automatically selects the most promising samples via a per-sample loss combining distance to target data, a Beta-distribution-based label-uncertainty penalty, and a model-utility term. The paper also distinguishes itself from knowledge distillation by operating in a fully unlabeled target domain, and from iterative synthesis methods like Wang et al. (2023a) by requiring only a tiny validation set (as few as 30 samples) rather than partially labeled target data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: lightweight NLI models fine-tuned with Auto-GDA reach GPT-4o-level grounding verification performance (0.878 vs 0.883 avg ROC-AUC) at roughly 10% of the inference cost, making real-time hallucination screening in production RAG systems feasible. Key limitations include the assumption that target-domain evidence distributions are available, the need for a separate adapted model per domain, and the lack of evaluation on the full RAG pipeline including retrieval. Open questions include whether a single model can be adapted to multiple domains simultaneously, how the method interacts with retrieval quality, and whether the distribution-matching objective generalizes beyond binary NLI to multi-class or fine-grained entailment settings.
