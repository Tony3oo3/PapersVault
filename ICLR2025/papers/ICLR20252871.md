## Problem

Existing image watermarking methods make a single global decision per image, so the watermark signal fades and detection fails when only a small portion of the image is watermarked (e.g., after splicing, inpainting, or partial editing). No prior method can localize watermarked regions, distinguish multiple watermarks within one image, or remain robust when the watermarked area shrinks to as little as 10% of the image surface.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252871/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The embedder takes an RGB image (trained at 256×256, extended to higher resolutions via anisotropic scaling and bilinear interpolation of the watermark signal) and a 32-bit binary message, producing a watermarked image as x + α·δ. The extractor takes any RGB image at the same resolution and outputs a (1+32)×h×w tensor: one per-pixel detection score in [0,1] and 32 per-pixel, per-bit soft message predictions. Post-processing with DBSCAN over the hard-decoded pixel messages recovers one or several distinct 32-bit messages without requiring the number of watermarks as input.

## Method

WAM redefines watermarking as a pixel-level segmentation task with two jointly trained models: a small LDM-based autoencoder embedder (1.1M params) that maps the image to a latent, concatenates a message lookup-table embedding, and decodes an additive watermark signal δ∈[-1,1]³×h×w; and a ViT-Base + pixel-decoder extractor (96M params) that predicts a (1+n_bits)-dimensional vector per pixel. Training is split into two stages: Phase 1 pre-trains both models at 256×256 with random mask-based splicing and strong geometric/valuemetric augmentations, optimizing pixel-wise cross-entropy for detection and decoding to achieve robustness and localization; Phase 2 fine-tunes with a Just-Noticeable-Difference (JND) heatmap that modulates watermark intensity per pixel for imperceptibility, and introduces 1–3 distinct messages in disjoint masked regions to enable multi-watermark extraction. At inference, DBSCAN clusters the per-pixel decoded messages (using ε=1 Hamming distance and min_samples≈2% of pixels) to recover multiple hidden messages without knowing their count in advance.

## Results

On COCO (10k validation images), WAM achieves 100% TPR and 100% bit accuracy on clean images with a 0.04% FPR. Under geometric augmentations, WAM maintains 99.3% TPR and 91.8% bit accuracy, far exceeding the best baseline (FNNS at 62.4% TPR). Most strikingly, on splicing (only 10% of the image watermarked), WAM achieves 100% TPR and 95.3% bit accuracy, while all other methods drop to ≤83.2% TPR and ≤88.5% bit accuracy. On DIV2K high-resolution images, WAM similarly leads with 100% TPR on valuemetric and inpainting, and 99.5% TPR on splicing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WAM decisively outperforms all five baselines on splicing (100% TPR vs. 83.2% for TrustMark, the best competitor) and geometric augmentations (99.3% TPR vs. 62.4% for FNNS). On inpainting, WAM leads with 97.9% TPR and 99.2% bit accuracy, surpassing FNNS (89.4% TPR, 97.3% bit acc.) and TrustMark (39.4% TPR). On valuemetric transformations, WAM matches or slightly exceeds TrustMark (100% vs. 90.1% TPR; 99.9% vs. 98.2% bit accuracy). On clean images, all methods are competitive (99–100% TPR), but WAM achieves a lower FPR (0.04%) than TrustMark (2.88%). In the localization evaluation (Sec. 5.4), WAM outperforms EditGuard on mIoU and bit accuracy, particularly after cropping, where EditGuard is not robust. WAM also uniquely supports extraction of multiple distinct 32-bit messages from a single image (up to 5 messages in 10% areas each with >95% bit accuracy), a capability none of the compared baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

WAM builds on the joint embedder/extractor paradigm established by HiDDeN and its successors (StegaStamp, TrustMark, FNNS), but redefines the extraction problem as a pixel-level semantic segmentation task using a ViT-based architecture inspired by SETR and Segment Anything. The embedder borrows the LDM/VQGAN autoencoder structure, while the two-stage training (robustness first, then JND-based imperceptibility and multi-watermark fine-tuning) departs from the adversarial or perceptual-loss approaches of prior work. The use of DBSCAN for clustering pixel-level decoded messages to recover multiple watermarks without knowing their count in advance is a novel post-processing step not seen in prior watermarking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With regulatory frameworks (EU AI Act, US Executive Order) mandating identification of AI-generated content, WAM's ability to handle partial watermarks, splicing, and inpainting—where prior methods drop to near-zero detection—addresses a critical real-world gap. The multi-watermark extraction capability opens new applications in tracking watermarked objects and identifying multiple AI tools in a single image. Key limitations remain: the 32-bit payload is modest compared to methods like EditGuard (>100 bits), perceptual artifacts persist in textured regions despite JND weighting, and robustness to diffusion-based purification is not yet demonstrated. Open questions include scaling payload capacity, improving HVS modeling, and whether the segmentation formulation extends to video or 3D content.
