## Problem

Classical Mercer's theorem guarantees pointwise, uniform, and absolute convergence of the eigenfunction expansion only for continuous symmetric positive definite kernels, yet indefinite and asymmetric kernels (e.g., tanh/sigmoid kernels in Transformers, H∞ control, protein similarity) are widely used in practice without rigorous convergence guarantees. This paper identifies the precise conditions under which Mercer's expansion for general continuous kernels may fail (pointwise, absolute, or uniform convergence) and establishes the minimal smoothness assumptions needed to recover well-behaved convergence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous kernel K:[a,b]×[c,d]→ℝ as input, with the key regularity constraint being uniform bounded variation in each variable separately (eq. 3), which is weaker than Lipschitz continuity. It produces the full singular value expansion K(x,y)=Σ σ_n u_n(x) v_n(y) with singular values σ_n, orthonormal left/right singular functions, and a truncated rank-k approximation that is the best L² rank-k approximation to K. The computational algorithm evaluates K only on coarse Chebyshev grids and 2R one-dimensional slices rather than a full 2D grid, reducing cost from O(n³) to O(R²n + R³).

## Method

The paper first constructs explicit counterexamples (using Fourier series of Fejér-type functions and Legendre/Chebyshev polynomial expansions with alternating signs) showing that continuous symmetric indefinite kernels can have Mercer's expansions that fail to converge pointwise, absolutely, or uniformly. It then proves that for the broader class of continuous kernels with uniform bounded variation, the singular values decay as σ_n = O(n⁻¹), which via a Rademacher-Menchov-type orthonormal series argument (Theorem 2) implies unconditional almost-everywhere convergence and, by Egorov's theorem, almost uniform convergence. New asymptotically tight decay bounds σ_n = O(n^{-(r+1)}) are derived for kernels whose r-th derivative has bounded variation, using Legendre approximation error estimates (Wang, 2023) combined with the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem. Finally, an efficient two-step algorithm computes the expansion: (1) a pseudo-skeleton approximation via Gaussian elimination with complete pivoting on adaptively sampled Chebyshev grids, followed by (2) a low-rank SVD compression using function-space QR decompositions and a small R×R matrix SVD.

## Results

The paper establishes that Mercer's expansion for continuous indefinite kernels may fail to converge pointwise (Proposition 1), may converge pointwise but not absolutely (Proposition 2), or may converge pointwise but not uniformly (Proposition 3). For the positive direction, it proves that any continuous kernel of uniform bounded variation has σ_n = O(n^{-1}) (Proposition 4), and more generally σ_n = O(n^{-(r+1)}) when the kernel is (r−1)-times differentiable with r-th derivative of bounded variation (eq. 8). The main convergence theorem (Theorem 2 + Corollary 1) shows that Mercer's expansion converges unconditionally almost everywhere whenever σ_n ≤ Cn^{-α} for α > 1/2, and Theorem 3 further establishes almost uniform convergence for kernels of uniform bounded variation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons against competing methods. The paper positions its contributions relative to prior theoretical results: it extends Hammerstein's (1923) uniform convergence condition and Smithies' (1938) almost-everywhere convergence condition to the broader class of kernels with uniform bounded variation, and it improves upon the general continuous kernel bound σ_n = O(n^{-1/2}) to σ_n = O(n^{-1}) under the bounded variation assumption. The paper also contrasts its approach with Reproducing Kernel Krein Space (RKKS) and Reproducing Kernel Banach Space (RKBS) frameworks, noting that not all continuous indefinite kernels admit such spaces, whereas the SVE-based Mercer's expansion always exists in L². No head-to-head numerical benchmarks are provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical line of work from Mercer (1909), Schmidt (1907), Hammerstein (1923), and Smithies (1938) on eigenfunction and singular value expansions of integral operators, which had previously required restrictive smoothness conditions (Lipschitz continuity or ad-hoc square-integrated Lipschitz conditions). It departs from the Reproducing Kernel Krein Space and Reproducing Kernel Banach Space frameworks, which require a positive decomposition or semi-inner product structure that not all indefinite kernels admit. The key conceptual advance is replacing Lipschitz continuity with the weaker uniform bounded variation condition, and providing the first explicit counterexamples showing that continuity alone is insufficient for pointwise convergence of Mercer's expansion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the missing rigorous theoretical underpinning for indefinite and asymmetric kernels that are widely used in practice (Transformers, kernel learning, H∞ control, protein similarity) but have lacked formal convergence guarantees. The negative result—that a continuous symmetric kernel can have a Mercer's expansion that fails to converge pointwise—clarifies longstanding confusion in the literature and cautions practitioners against assuming convergence without additional smoothness. The open question of whether pointwise, absolute, and uniform convergence hold for bounded-variation kernels (the authors conjecture yes but cannot prove it) remains a gap. The practical algorithm for computing Mercer's expansions via pseudo-skeleton approximation and low-rank SVD is immediately useful for kernel method practitioners.
