## Problem

The Node-Path Balancing (NPB) principle identifies that sparse networks with well-balanced effective nodes and paths train better, but its formulation requires solving a large-scale NP-hard discrete optimization problem that must be decomposed into layer-wise subproblems, yielding suboptimal masks. This discreteness also prevents NPB from being integrated into standard gradient-based training pipelines, limiting its practical utility. The paper asks whether the NPB objective can be made differentiable so that efficient gradient-based methods can directly optimize the pruning mask.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrained neural network (ResNet, VGG, EfficientNet, or ViT) and a global sparsity ratio as input, requiring no training data or task-specific loss. Layer-wise sparsity is distributed via the Erdős-Rényi Kernel (ERK), which scales per-layer sparsity proportionally to layer size to prevent layer collapse. The output is a binary pruning mask (one per weight) obtained by applying Top-K to learned continuous score parameters, producing a pruned subnetwork that is then trained from scratch on the target task.

## Method

DPaI introduces continuous score parameters s^(l) for each layer and converts the discrete NPB objective into a differentiable one by applying Top-K with the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) to obtain binary masks, then computing analytic gradients for three topology objectives: effective paths (R_P), effective nodes (R_N), and effective kernels (R_C). The tanh function serves as a differentiable indicator of node/kernel effectiveness, and logarithmic scaling balances the vastly different magnitudes of paths versus nodes. The overall objective is a weighted sum (1−α)log R_P + α[(1−β)log R_N + β log R_C], optimized via Adam over 3000 steps with all layer masks updated simultaneously rather than sequentially. A convergence analysis proves that each update step monotonically increases the number of effective paths and activates previously ineffective nodes, with the hyperparameter α controlling the trade-off between path maximization and node activation.

## Results

DPaI consistently outperforms all prior state-of-the-art PaI methods across ResNet-based and VGG architectures on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet, with the largest gains at high sparsity (96.84%–99.00%), reaching up to 4.6% accuracy improvement over baselines and most cases exceeding 2%. On ImageNet-1K (EfficientNetB0, 0.3 sparsity), DPaI achieves 72.2±0.25% average accuracy versus SynFlow's 71.4±0.29%. At extreme sparsity (99.68%) on ResNet18-Tiny-ImageNet, DPaI reaches 30.73% test accuracy with 4,974 effective nodes, compared to NPB's 24.33% with only 500 effective nodes. On ViT-B/16 at 99% sparsity, DPaI achieves 35.61% accuracy versus SynFlow's 29.55%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPaI outperforms all five baselines (Random, SNIP, SynFlow, Iter-SNIP, PHEW, NPB) on ResNet20-CIFAR-10, ResNet18-Tiny-ImageNet, and VGG19-CIFAR-100 across all sparsity levels from 68.38% to 99.00%, with the most substantial gains (up to 4.6%) at 96.84% and 99.00% sparsity. Against NPB (the closest competitor, sharing the same Node-Path Balancing objective), DPaI's advantage is most pronounced at extreme sparsity: at 99.68% on ResNet18-Tiny-ImageNet, DPaI achieves 30.73% accuracy with 4,974 effective nodes versus NPB's 24.33% with only 500 nodes, a 6.4-point gap. DPaI is the only method that does not underperform NPB or PHEW on VGG19 at 99% sparsity, where those methods retain a slight edge due to their bias toward weight magnitudes. On ViT-B/16 at 99% sparsity, DPaI (35.61%) surpasses SynFlow (29.55%) by over 6 points, while Random ERK achieves only 17.40%. In terms of pruning time, DPaI maintains a consistent ~70–90 seconds across all sparsity levels and architectures, whereas PHEW scales from 2.6s to 6,929s and NPB from 20s to 430s depending on model size and sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Node-Path Balancing (NPB) principle from Pham et al. (NeurIPS 2023), which showed that balancing effective nodes and paths is essential for high-quality sparse subnetworks. The key departure is converting NPB's NP-hard discrete, layer-wise optimization into a differentiable continuous formulation using the Straight-Through Estimator with Top-K operations, enabling global (rather than layer-by-layer) mask optimization. Within the broader Pruning at Initialization (PaI) landscape, DPaI positions itself alongside SNIP, SynFlow, PHEW, and NPB, but uniquely combines topology-aware objectives (nodes, paths, kernels) with gradient-based optimization, drawing conceptual inspiration from differentiable NAS (DARTS) while targeting a fundamentally different problem: pre-training mask selection rather than architecture search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the NPB principle differentiable, DPaI enables seamless integration into standard neural network training pipelines and opens the door to combining pruning with NAS or sparse training in a unified differentiable framework. The method's data-agnostic nature (independent of initial weights and training data) makes pruned subnetworks reusable across datasets, a practical advantage over weight-magnitude-based methods. However, the approach remains sensitive to hyperparameters α and β, has not been adapted for self-attention layers in transformers, and the sequential layer-wise computation (though parallelizable) limits current efficiency. Open questions include whether the differentiable NPB formulation can be extended to structured pruning, dynamic sparsity during training, and whether the Top-K + STE approximation introduces systematic biases at extreme sparsity levels.
