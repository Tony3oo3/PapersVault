## Problem

Generalizable human rendering from sparse inputs must reconstruct a 3D avatar in a single feed-forward pass, preventing it from exploiting apparent errors between source images and rendered reconstructions to refine its prediction. Additionally, existing Gaussians-on-Mesh representations use identical resolutions for the mesh and Gaussians, forcing a tradeoff between efficient reconstruction (low-res mesh) and high-quality rendering (high-res Gaussians) that prior methods resolve only via gradient-based densification unavailable in feed-forward settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251275/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3–5 sparse source images (multiview or multi-frame from a monocular video), corresponding binary masks, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and SMPL/SMPL-X poses, and produces a canonical T-pose Gaussians-on-Mesh representation (330K Gaussians on a twice-subdivided mesh) that is articulated via linear blend skinning and rendered at 1024×1024 at 95 FPS. Because reconstruction is performed in canonical T-pose rather than the input pose, the output avatar can be retargeted to arbitrary novel poses without skeleton binding or other post-processing, and the model accepts source images in unaligned poses.

## Method

The core contribution is a coupled-multi-resolution Gaussians-on-Mesh representation combined with an end-to-end trainable iterative feedback reconstruction network. The representation deforms a low-resolution SMPL/SMPL-X mesh (for efficient LBS articulation and reconstruction) while attaching Gaussian splats to a high-resolution mesh obtained by two levels of subdivision (for high-quality rendering), with Gaussian parameters (rotation, scale, color, offset) defined in each triangle's local coordinate system. Reconstruction proceeds over T=3 iterative steps: at each step, the current canonical representation is rendered into all source views, pixel-aligned features are extracted from both source and rendered images, fused via a two-layer Transformer (multi-source fusion) and a Point Transformer, and an MLP updates the low-res mesh vertices and high-res Gaussian parameters. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with L1, perceptual (SSIM/LPIPS), mask, and Laplacian losses averaged across all iterations and views, requiring no per-scene optimization at inference time.

## Results

On THuman2.0 with 3 source views, LIFe-GoM achieves PSNR/LPIPS*/FID of 24.65/110.82/51.27, outperforming the best generalizable baseline GHG (21.90/133.41/61.67) and all per-scene methods. With 5 views, it reaches 25.57/105.39/38.57, substantially exceeding GPS-Gaussian (20.39/152.34/65.90). On AIST++ (multi-frame setting), it matches ActorsNeRF in quality (25.25 vs 25.23 PSNR) while reconstructing in 589 ms versus ~4 hours. Reconstruction takes <1 s and rendering runs at 95.1 FPS at 1024×1024 on an A100 GPU, using only 330K Gaussians (7.5× fewer than GHG's 2.8M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIFe-GoM outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest generalizable competitor GHG (Kwon et al., 2024), it gains +2.75 dB PSNR, −22.59 LPIPS*, and −10.40 FID in the 3-view setting, while using 7.5× fewer Gaussians (330K vs 2.8M) and rendering in 10.52 ms vs 20.30 ms. Against per-scene methods (GoMAvatar, iHuman, 3DGS-Avatar), it wins on all three metrics despite requiring no per-subject optimization, with the largest gap over GoMAvatar (+1.60 dB PSNR, −23.16 LPIPS*, −36.24 FID). In the 5-view comparison, GPS-Gaussian (Zheng et al., 2024) is the only other generalizable method tested; LIFe-GoM exceeds it by +5.18 dB PSNR and −46.95 LPIPS*, and additionally handles non-overlapping regions where GPS-Gaussian fails. On AIST++, LIFe-GoM marginally surpasses ActorsNeRF (25.25 vs 25.23 PSNR, 21.61 vs 22.45 LPIPS*) while being ~2400× faster in reconstruction (589 ms vs ~4 h). Cross-domain evaluation on XHuman (trained on THuman2.0, zero fine-tuning) confirms generalization, beating GHG by +1.80 dB PSNR and −13.59 LPIPS*.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Gaussians-on-Mesh (GoM) dual shape representation from GoMAvatar (Wen et al., 2024) — originally a per-scene optimized method — to the generalizable (feed-forward) human rendering setting, where no per-subject gradient optimization is available. Two key departures from prior GoM work: (1) a coupled-multi-resolution design that deforms a low-resolution mesh for efficient reconstruction while attaching Gaussians to a subdivided high-resolution mesh for quality, replacing the gradient-based densification used by GaussianAvatar; and (2) an end-to-end trainable iterative feedback loop (drawing on iterative networks from inverse problems and pose estimation) that successively refines the canonical representation by comparing rendered source views to inputs, something single-pass feed-forward methods like GHG and GPS-Gaussian cannot do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for AR/VR: sub-second reconstruction (907 ms) with 95 FPS rendering at 1024×1024 and SOTA quality (PSNR 24.65, LPIPS* 110.82, FID 51.27 on THuman2.0) makes animatable avatar generation from a few photos feasible in near-real-time pipelines. Open questions remain: the method cannot inpaint large invisible regions, inherits the fixed topology of SMPL/SMPL-X (limiting complex garments like dresses), and still depends on accurate input poses (though it is more robust than GHG). The iterative feedback mechanism, while effective, adds ~290 ms per step, and the authors note that a jointly trained pose-refinement network could further reduce sensitivity to pose errors.
