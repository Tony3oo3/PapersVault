## Problem

Creating 3D assets for household articulated objects (cabinets, refrigerators, ovens, etc.) is essential for robotics and embodied AI but currently requires either laborious multi-view/multi-state data capture or offers only coarse, unconditional generation. No prior method generates articulated objects from a single image of the object in its resting state, which is the most practical and widely available input yet introduces ambiguity in part shape, connectivity, and motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253516/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a household object in its closed/resting state from an arbitrary viewpoint, plus a part connectivity graph extracted from the image via GPT-4o as auxiliary guidance. It produces a complete 3D articulated asset: a kinematic hierarchy graph, per-part articulation parameters (joint type, axis location/direction, motion range), and part meshes retrieved from a library. Parts are described at an abstract level with 6-dimensional bounding boxes, semantic labels (base, door, drawer, handle, knob, tray), and joint parameters, padded to a maximum of 32 parts.

## Method

SINGAPO decomposes generation into a coarse-to-fine pipeline: GPT-4o infers the part connectivity graph from the image, a transformer-based diffusion model generates per-part attributes (bounding boxes, labels, articulation types, joint axes, motion ranges) conditioned on DINOv2 patch features and the graph, and a retrieval algorithm assembles final meshes from a part library. The denoising network has 8 attention layers, each combining local (within-part), global (cross-part), and graph-relation self-attention (using the adjacency matrix as an attention mask), plus an image cross-attention where only bounding-box parameters attend to DINOv2 patches to anchor parts to image regions. The model is trained with classifier-free guidance—dropping the object category and graph at 50% and the image at 10%—to avoid overfitting to narrow distributions, and supervised with a standard diffusion noise loss plus a foreground attention loss that encourages parts to focus on object-region patches. It is initialized from CAGE pretrained weights and trained for 200 epochs on 3,063 objects from PartNet-Mobility across 7 categories.

## Results

On the PartNet-Mobility test set (real setting, single image input), SINGAPO achieves RS-dgIoU of 0.4841, RS-dCD of 0.0168, and AOR of 0.0043, outperforming URDFormer (RS-dgIoU 1.1868, RS-dCD 0.5502, AOR 0.2341) and NAP-ICA (RS-dgIoU 0.5778, RS-dCD 0.0173, AOR 0.0120). In zero-shot evaluation on the more complex ACD dataset, SINGAPO maintains its lead with RS-dgIoU 0.9517 and AOR 0.0085, while URDFormer degrades severely (RS-dgIoU 1.2288, AOR 0.2840, graph accuracy 4.48%). A user study with 48 participants confirms the quantitative advantage, ranking SINGAPO first (1.20/3) over NAP-ICA (1.91) and URDFormer (2.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SINGAPO outperforms URDFormer by a large margin across all metrics in both the PartNet-Mobility and ACD settings: on PartNet-Mobility (real setting), SINGAPO reduces RS-dgIoU from 1.1868 to 0.4841 (59% lower) and AOR from 0.2341 to 0.0043 (98% lower), while also achieving higher graph accuracy (82.47% vs. 32.03%). Against NAP-ICA, SINGAPO wins on geometric consistency (RS-dgIoU 0.4841 vs. 0.5778) and collision rate (AOR 0.0043 vs. 0.0120), with the gap widening on the zero-shot ACD dataset (RS-dgIoU 0.9517 vs. 1.0233, AOR 0.0085 vs. 0.0133). The advantage over NAP-ICA is attributed to the graph-guided attention that better coordinates parts, especially as part count increases. In the zero-shot ACD evaluation, URDFormer's graph accuracy collapses to 4.48% (vs. 39.34% for SINGAPO), indicating severe generalization failure on more complex objects. In the user study, SINGAPO is ranked most realistic (1.20/3) by a clear margin over NAP-ICA (1.91) and URDFormer (2.89), confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CAGE (Liu et al., 2024c), adopting its transformer-based diffusion architecture with local, global, and graph-relation attention as the base model, and extending it with an image cross-attention module that distills DINOv2 patch features into part attributes. It departs from the two dominant paradigms in articulated object creation: multi-view/multi-state reconstruction methods (URDFormer, Real2Code, PARIS) that require expensive data acquisition, and unconditional or graph-conditioned generation (NAP, CAGE) that lack intuitive visual control. The key conceptual shift is reframing single-image articulated object prediction (previously addressed only by the regression-based URDFormer) as a generative diffusion problem, enabling multiple plausible outputs that capture the inherent ambiguity of a single resting-state snapshot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes articulated object creation practical for real-world settings where only a single photograph is available, removing the need for multi-view capture or motion recording that limits scalability of prior methods. The modular coarse-to-fine pipeline (graph inference → abstract part generation → mesh retrieval) enables user intervention at each stage, which is valuable for interactive 3D asset creation in robotics and embodied AI. However, the method is currently limited to cuboid-shaped household objects, relies on mesh retrieval rather than geometry generation (missing fine details), and struggles with complex textures and cluttered part arrangements. Open questions include extending to arbitrary object geometries, generating part shapes directly from the image, and handling objects in non-resting articulated states.
